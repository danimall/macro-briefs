**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MGIC Investment Corporation is the holding company for the firm that founded the modern U.S. private mortgage insurance industry. It sits inside a six-player oligopoly with ~19.4% market share, an A2/A-/A ratings profile, and a balance sheet carrying a $2.5B surplus over its GSE-mandated minimum capital. The core equity story is capital return: net income has been flat-to-declining ($865M in 2022 → $738M in 2025), yet diluted EPS rose 9% to $3.14 in 2025, driven almost entirely by a 24% reduction in share count over three years. In 2025 alone MGIC repurchased 30.1M shares for $782M and returned roughly $915M to shareholders. The company manufactures per-share growth from surplus capital rather than from organic revenue expansion.

The tension in the thesis is that reported earnings are flattered by cyclical tailwinds that are now fading. MGIC only reserves against loans once they become delinquent, so it holds no reserves against future losses on its $81.2B performing risk-in-force. For several years, home-price appreciation has cured delinquencies through third-party sales and suppressed claim severity, producing large favorable reserve releases ($155M in 2025, down from $212M in 2024). Both drivers are moderating: FHFA home-price appreciation slowed to 1.4% through November 2025 (from 4.8% in 2024), and claim severity climbed to 77.8% of exposure in Q4 2025 versus 66.3% a year earlier. Management explicitly warns net losses and LAE paid "will increase." The buyback program works while credit stays benign, but the surplus that funds it is also the cushion the company needs in a downturn: in a housing stress scenario, buybacks stop and capital gets trapped to cover rising PMIERs minimum-asset requirements on delinquent loans.

The stock trades at ~9.2x trailing earnings, near its 52-week high of $29.97, with essentially flat estimate revisions and analyst targets clustered tightly at $28-30 — below or at the current price. We rate MTG a HOLD on an income/total-return basis. The ~2.1% dividend plus a double-digit buyback yield delivers a durable shareholder return, but there is no identified catalyst for meaningful multiple expansion, and the fundamental trajectory (moderating releases, rising severity, decelerating HPA, a new market entrant) argues against paying up here.

## Company Overview

MGIC (Mortgage Guaranty Insurance Corporation) insures lenders against loss when borrowers default on low-down-payment (>80% LTV) residential mortgages. This coverage allows those loans to be sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, making PMI a structural enabler of the U.S. conforming mortgage market. Although the data label reads "Reinsurance," MGIC is a primary mortgage credit insurer that uses reinsurance (quota-share and excess-of-loss) as a capital-management lever rather than as its core business.

Scale as of FY2025: $303.1B primary insurance-in-force; $81.2B risk-in-force; $60.2B new insurance written (up from $55.7B in 2024); total revenue $1.21B. The company is licensed in all 50 states and domiciled in Wisconsin. Its competitive moat rests on very high barriers to entry: GSE PMIERs eligibility, state capital rules, ratings requirements, and entrenched lender relationships. Only six active private mortgage insurers operate in the U.S. (MGIC, Essent, Radian, Arch MI, Enact, National MI), and MGIC is the #1 or co-leader by share.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is exceptional but plateauing.** Net income trajectory: $865M (2022) → $713M (2023) → $763M (2024) → $738M (2025). Net margin is roughly 61%, ROE 14.3%, ROA 11.1%, with free cash flow near $852M (a 70% FCF margin). The 2025 dip versus 2024 reflects normalizing loss experience: losses incurred, net swung from a $14.9M benefit in 2024 to a $48.9M charge in 2025 as favorable prior-year reserve development moderated.

**EPS growth is a share-count story, not an earnings story.** Diluted EPS rose 9% to $3.14 from $2.89 despite lower net income, purely because diluted shares fell from ~311M in 2022 to ~235M in 2025. In 2025 MGIC repurchased 30.1M shares (12% of shares outstanding) for $782M and raised the dividend 14% to $0.56/share. Buybacks continued into 2026 (4.5M shares/$120.5M through February 20). This is the central value-creation mechanism.

**The balance sheet is a fortress.** Altman Z-score 3.34; debt/equity 0.29; a single debt instrument ($650M 5.25% Notes due 2028); equity of $5.15B; roughly $1.1B of cash and investments at the holding company. PMIERs Available Assets stand at $5.7B, a $2.5B excess over the Minimum Required Assets. Risk-to-capital is 10.0:1 against a 25:1 regulatory maximum, and the policyholder position sits $3.6B above the required minimum. This surplus funds the buyback program and would be consumed first in a credit downturn.

**Revenue quality signals are the key forward-looking concern.** Net premium yield fell to 32.2 bps (from 33.0), and the in-force portfolio yield dropped to 38.1 bps, with management guiding both to keep declining as older, higher-rate policies run off and are replaced by lower-priced new business. Net premiums earned were roughly flat. Investment income of ~$246M reflects a 4.0% pre-tax book yield on a $5.8B, 98%+ investment-grade fixed-income portfolio with 4.2-year duration, a genuine tailwind if rates stay higher-for-longer, which the current macro path supports.

**Credit quality of the current book is strong.** Delinquency rate 2.43% (up modestly from 2.40%), NIW weighted-average FICO 748, and 80% of RIF written after 2020. Persistency is elevated at 84.8% because most borrowers hold below-market mortgage rates and have no incentive to refinance, a rate-lock dynamic that supports in-force stability but also slows new-business origination.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine here. Revenue is essentially flat, net income is declining, and the premium yield is structurally bleeding as higher-priced legacy policies roll off. Estimate revisions confirm the plateau: current-year consensus of $3.07 is essentially unchanged over 90 days (revised -0.1%), and next-year $3.30 has been revised down 1.4%. There is no estimate-revision acceleration that would flag an improving story.

NIW rose to $60.2B in 2025 from $55.7B, and market share ticked up to 19.4% from 18.6%, both modest positives. But the industry backdrop is soft: PMI's share of the total mortgage insurance market fell to 38.0% from 41.1% as FHA (34.3%) and VA (26.8%, rising) took share on pricing and capital flexibility PMI cannot match. A new PMI market entrant intending to write business in 2026 — the first in years — adds a competitive-intensity headwind to already-thin yields.

The realistic per-share growth path is the buyback: at ~$800M/year of upstreamed dividends and a $2.5B surplus, MGIC can retire meaningful share count for several years if credit stays benign. On flat net income, that mechanically drives mid-single-digit to high-single-digit EPS growth. That engine is durable but contingent on the same benign-credit assumption that underpins everything else.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.65, MTG trades at ~9.2x trailing and forward earnings (nearly identical given flat estimates), a ~2.1% dividend yield, and roughly 1.22x book on $5.15B of equity against a $6.27B market cap. Against Reinsurance-labeled peers, MTG's 9.2x P/E is a premium to the peer median of 6.9x (SPNT 6.0x, RNR 4.9x, EG 6.9x, RGA 11.2x). But this peer set is a poor comparable: RNR and EG are property-catastrophe and diversified reinsurers with different cycle dynamics. The relevant comps are the PMI peers (Essent, Radian, Arch MI, Enact), whose valuation levels are not provided in the source data. On a franchise basis MGIC screens as fairly valued given its capital-return profile, neither obviously cheap nor expensive.

The valuation is not a hidden mispricing. The low trailing multiple reflects a legitimately cyclical earnings stream flattered by reserve releases that are moderating and a premium yield that is declining. Here a low P/E is a warning rather than a signal: the "E" is at a cyclically favorable point, and forward earnings on normalized loss experience (fewer releases, higher severity) would compress the effective earnings power. Book-value-per-share growth from buybacks provides intrinsic-value support, and the dividend plus double-digit buyback yield anchors total return, but neither justifies a higher multiple absent a catalyst.

Analyst targets reinforce the balanced picture: mean $28.80, median $29.00, range $28-30, all below or at the current price. On a stable, flat-estimate name like this, targets reflect a consensus that the stock is close to fair value. We do not treat proximity to consensus as a floor here because there is no rising-estimate momentum to make the targets lag reality.

## Competitive Landscape

MGIC operates in a stable six-player oligopoly with genuinely high barriers to entry. Its share and improving ratings (Moody's upgrade to A2 in 2025, S&P outlook to positive) reflect a durable franchise. Reinsurance program optimization — two 40% QSR transactions covering 2026-2027 NIW, a reduction in the 2022 QSR cede rate from 30% to 28%, and a new $324M ILN in January 2026 — demonstrates disciplined capital management that lowers PMIERs requirements and preserves buyback capacity.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-negative rather than improving. Pricing has commoditized via industry-wide risk-based pricing engines, so MGIC often cannot observe competitors' price changes until its own NIW mix shifts. Some peers carry lower-cost offshore reinsurance and tax structures. A new entrant planned for 2026 will pressure already-thin yields. And the broader mortgage-insurance market is ceding share to FHA/VA. MGIC is defending a strong position rather than extending a widening moat, which supports a mid-range competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**GSE dependence and privatization uncertainty is the defining structural risk.** The substantial majority of NIW covers GSE-purchased loans, and the GSEs set PMIERs capital rules, coverage levels, guarantee fees, and cancellation practices. FHFA/administration privatization efforts remain live but stalled: through mid-2026 the message was an IPO "on the table" but "no rush," and FHFA Director Pulte's expanded role (acting DNI) was read as reducing near-term IPO likelihood (FNMA/FMCC shares down 30%+ YTD). The direction is genuinely ambiguous: a private GSE might rely more on PMI credit enhancement, or it might change capital/coverage rules or expand credit-risk-transfer programs that compete with traditional PMI. This is a binary macro swing factor with no clear near-term resolution.

**Earnings carry hidden cyclicality.** MGIC reserves only against delinquent loans, holding nothing against performing RIF. Reserve sensitivity is ±$19M per 1-point change in claim rate and ±$7M per $1,000 change in severity. Years of favorable development ($155M-$404M annually) driven by home-price appreciation curing delinquencies have flattered reported earnings; both the HPA tailwind and pre-claim third-party sales are moderating, and severity is already climbing sharply. This is the most important negative trend a standard data pull would miss.

**Legacy tail and concentration.** The 2005-2008 vintages are only ~3% of remaining IIF but ~18% of the delinquency inventory (4,871 loans, averaging 14 months delinquent), a 17-year credit tail that persists. Customer concentration is meaningful (largest customer ~16% of NIW, top 10 ~32%), and ~59% of loans are serviced by thinner-liquidity non-bank servicers. MGIC also leans heavily on reinsurance for $2.8B of PMIERs capital credit; if the GSEs cut that credit or reinsurance becomes costlier, required capital rises.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.1% is rich against 23.5% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (31.1% near-term vs. 27.7% at six months), consistent with the market pricing a near-term event. Put/call skew is elevated at 15.9% and the put/call open-interest ratio is 1.37, indicating modest hedging demand and a mild bearish tilt. The P/C OI ratio fell sharply from 3.91 to 1.37 over the past month as the stock rose 9.2%, suggesting some prior downside positioning has unwound. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.7%. This confirms a two-sided, event-driven near-term setup rather than a directional conviction, but does not override the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$33-36).** Credit stays benign through 2026-2027, the higher-for-longer rate path lifts investment income above the current level, and MGIC continues retiring share count against flat net income, compounding EPS at high-single-digits. Ratings continue improving, GSE privatization resolves neutral-to-positive (a private GSE relying more on PMI), and the multiple re-rates modestly toward 11-12x on demonstrated capital-return durability. Book value per share grows steadily via buybacks below intrinsic value.

**Bear Case (~$21-24).** Home prices roll over as the affordability chain (elevated mortgage rates → weaker demand → falling values) plays out, delinquencies rise, and favorable reserve development flips to strengthening. Claim severity accelerates, forcing higher losses incurred. Buybacks halt as capital is trapped to meet rising PMIERs minimums on delinquent loans, removing the per-share growth engine. GSE privatization resolves adversely (changed capital/coverage rules or expanded CRT competition), and the new 2026 entrant pressures yields. The stock derates toward book value.

The realistic outcome sits between these: a durable, well-capitalized franchise generating steady total shareholder return but facing moderating tailwinds and no catalyst for meaningful multiple expansion. This is an income/total-return hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the dividend plus buyback yield and steady book-value-per-share accretion, not on price appreciation. Reassessment point: the next quarterly earnings report, with particular attention to loss trends, reserve development, and severity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision inflection: current-year or next-year EPS consensus turning positive by >3% on a quarter, signaling the market is underestimating buyback-driven accretion or stabilizing credit.
- A pullback toward book value (low-$20s / ~1.0x book) that restores asymmetric downside protection.
- Clear neutral-to-positive resolution of GSE privatization that preserves or increases PMI demand.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Favorable reserve development turning to net reserve strengthening for two consecutive quarters, confirming the credit cycle has turned.
- Claim severity accelerating materially above the ~78% level alongside a rising delinquency rate above ~3%.
- A GSE capital/coverage rule change or expanded credit-risk-transfer program that structurally reduces PMI demand.
- Suspension of the buyback program.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the upcoming quarterly print; the GSE privatization overhang is an ongoing monitor with no defined resolution date.

## Conclusion

MGIC is a high-quality, well-managed capital-return compounder trading at a fair ~9.2x earnings near its 52-week high. The fortress balance sheet, disciplined buyback, and improving ratings profile are genuine strengths that justify a solid financial-health score. But the equity thesis is fundamentally contingent on benign credit, and the leading indicators — decelerating home-price appreciation, moderating reserve releases, and sharply rising claim severity — are all trending the wrong way. There is no organic growth, estimates are flat, competition is intensifying with a new 2026 entrant, and the GSE privatization overhang is an unresolved binary. The valuation offers no hidden mispricing; the low trailing multiple reflects cyclically favorable earnings, not a bargain.

The score trajectory (5.9 → 6.7 → 6.1) captured the improving financial-health recognition and the fading valuation edge as the stock re-rated toward the top of its range. The current data confirms that pattern: financial health is a real anchor, but valuation and growth do not support a higher call. We maintain HOLD, classified as an income hold, and would look to add only on a pullback toward book value or a positive estimate-revision inflection. Investors already owning the shares are being paid a durable total-shareholder-return yield to wait; new money has no compelling entry at these levels.
