**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dana should be analyzed as a special situation rather than a conventional auto-parts supplier. Within roughly eighteen months the company will have executed two portfolio-defining transactions: the January 1, 2026 sale of its Off-Highway business to Allison for $2.66B (a $1.19B pre-tax gain), and a June 2026 Reverse Morris Trust agreement to combine with Eaton's Mobility business, a deal that values the Eaton unit at ~$5.1B, produces a combined enterprise worth roughly $10B including debt, and leaves Eaton shareholders owning at least 50.1% of the resulting company. The current DAN share is effectively a stub whose intrinsic value depends on the pro-forma economics of a combined entity that has not yet published audited financials and will not close until Q1 2027.

The operating story underneath the M&A is a genuine margin turnaround. Adjusted EBITDA on continuing operations nearly doubled from $317M in 2023 to $610M in 2025, and management guides to $750–850M for 2026 despite flat-to-declining revenue in a soft North American truck market (Class-8 production fell 23% in 2025). This is cost self-help rather than demand-driven growth: a $325M annualized cost-reduction program ($260M realized through 2025), lower premium freight, and cut EV/electrification spend. The balance sheet is materially healthier than mechanical screens suggest: post-divestiture debt is ~$1.26B against ~$2.0B equity with ~$1.6B liquidity and an undrawn revolver, a profile that a stale FY2025 10-K balance sheet (which fed a distressed-looking D/E of 8.2 and an Altman Z of 1.25) badly misrepresents.

The problem for a public-equity investor is that the operating improvement has been reframed by the Eaton combination into a deal-arbitrage question. The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $39.56 (late April) to $27.63, a ~30% drawdown, most of it after the June 11 deal announcement clarified that current Dana holders become minority owners. Consensus targets ($33 low / $46 high, $37.57 mean) sit well above the current price, but the sell-side sentiment trend is degrading and current-year EPS estimates have been cut 9.1% over 90 days. Without published pro-forma synergy math or a confirmed consideration mix, the risk/reward is two-sided. We rate DAN a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Dana (founded 1904, headquartered in Maumee, Ohio) is a Tier-1 global supplier of power-conveyance and energy-management systems for on-highway vehicles. Core franchises include drive systems (axles, driveshafts, transmissions, the legacy Spicer brand), sealing solutions (Victor Reinz), thermal management, electrodynamic/EV technologies, and aftermarket parts. Following a January 2025 realignment and the Off-Highway divestiture, the continuing business runs on two segments:

- **Light Vehicle Systems** — ~68% of continuing sales ($5.2B FY25), weighted toward full-frame trucks and SUVs where Dana content per vehicle is highest.
- **Commercial Vehicle Systems** — ~32% ($2.3B FY25), covering medium/heavy-duty truck and bus drivelines plus the aftermarket business.

Geographically, sales are roughly 60% North America and 40% rest of world, with ~26,900 employees across 24 countries. The competitive advantage rests on long-cycle OEM relationships, high content on rear-/all-wheel-drive light trucks (a favorable mix versus passenger cars), and recognized brands. Top-10 customers account for 76% of 2025 sales, a concentration that cuts both ways.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture must be read on continuing operations, with Off-Highway now in discontinued ops and prior periods recast.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 Guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7,613M | $7,734M | $7,500M | $7,300–7,700M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $317M | $395M | $610M | $750–850M |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 4.2% | 5.1% | 8.1% | ~10.6% (mid) |
| Adj. FCF | $(23)M | $81M | $331M | $250–350M |
| Net income to parent | $38M | $(57)M | $85M | — |

The margin mechanism is explicit cost-out rather than volume: material-cost recoveries, lower premium freight, and reduced EV spending, with $65M of the cost program targeted in 2026. Q1 2026 confirmed the trajectory: continuing-ops sales of $1,868M (+5% y/y, mostly FX and pricing), gross margin up 240bps to 9.0%, and adjusted EBITDA of $171M versus $93M (+84%). Segment EBITDA margins improved to 8.8% (Light Vehicle, from 5.6%) and 10.5% (Commercial Vehicle, from 7.2%).

The balance-sheet transformation is the single most important fact that standard screens miss. The FY2025 10-K balance sheet showed ~$3.2B gross debt against $840M equity, the source of the misleading D/E and Altman Z, but that snapshot predates the divestiture close. On January 1, 2026 the Allison sale delivered $2,563M net cash in Q1, which Dana used to redeem/tender ~$1.33B of senior notes and repay the $225M Term A and $390M revolver. The Q1 2026 balance sheet carries no revolver draw and ~$477M cash. Interest expense fell from $39M (Q1'25) to $22M (Q1'26). Any model anchored to the 10-K balance sheet understates financial health by an order of magnitude.

On capital return, the board authorized up to $1B in June 2025, then raised it to $2B through 2030 in February 2026. Through Q1 2026, ~$775M was spent to repurchase ~38.7M shares, cutting share count from ~145M to ~108M (a ~25% reduction) at prices generally below current levels, with ~$1.225B of authorization remaining. A modest dividend runs at ~$0.12/quarter (~1.7% yield). Q1 operating cash flow is seasonally negative on a working-capital build ($295M receivables consumption), which is normal for the industry and not a red flag; FCF is back-half weighted.

The earnings-surprise record is uneven: over the last four quarters, one beat, one in-line, and two misses, including a Q4'25 print of $0.08 versus a $0.37 estimate (-79%) and a modest Q1'26 miss (-4.4%). Visibility into quarterly EPS is limited, which argues against high confidence in near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Organic top-line growth is not the thesis. End markets are in a downcycle: NA Class-8 production fell 23% in 2025, Classes 5–7 also fell 23%, and light-truck production was roughly flat. The 2026 revenue guide implies flat-to-slightly-lower volumes offset by pricing and FX. Growth in EPS and margin, not revenue, is what the model delivers: the cost program plus lower EV spend drives the EBITDA guide, and a $950M net-new-business backlog for 2026–28 is meant to offset soft volumes as it converts.

Estimate revisions are mixed and worth parsing carefully. Near-term quarters are rising (current quarter +8.0% over 90 days to $0.69; next quarter +16.1% to $0.81), consistent with the margin-recovery story landing. But current-year EPS has been cut 9.1% over 90 days (to $2.44), and the sell-side recommendation mix has softened (strong-buys fell from 4 to 3, holds rose from 2 to 3). The divergence between rising short-dated quarters and a lower full-year number reflects both the timing of cost realization and the overhang of the Eaton transaction on how analysts model the continuing entity. This is a two-sided revision picture, not the clean, broad-based +30% 90-day revision profile that would flag a buy signal.

The dominant growth variable is the Eaton Mobility combination. Reuters, WSJ, and Bloomberg coverage frames it as a bet on resilient, higher-margin aftermarket demand and on-highway powertrain scale. If synergies and pro-forma earnings power materialize as management intends, the combined ~$10B entity could re-rate. But pro-forma financials and synergy targets were not available in filed SEC documents at research time, and the consideration mix for Dana shareholders beyond "Eaton holders ≥50.1%" is not confirmed. Growth durability therefore cannot be underwritten with the evidence currently available.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline multiples are unusable here. The trailing and forward P/E screen at -51x on negative reported EPS (-$0.52), distorted by non-cash charges and the accounting mechanics of the divestiture. On forward consensus of $2.44 for the current year and $3.71 for next year, DAN trades at ~11.3x current-year and ~7.4x next-year earnings.

Against peers, that looks cheap: the Automotive Parts & Equipment median P/E is 20.7 (PATK 22.0, VC 20.1, DORM 20.7, ADNT 30.9, LCII 11.2). But cheapness in this cohort is the signal to distrust. The valuation dimension has been a reliable value-trap warning across this sector; a low multiple in a cyclical, OEM-concentrated auto supplier during a truck downcycle typically reflects the cyclicality and structural uncertainty, not a mispricing. DAN's forward multiple is also not independent of the deal: the standalone continuing entity will not persist in its current form, so applying a peer multiple to standalone forward EPS produces a number of dubious relevance. The real valuation question, what the combined Dana+Eaton Mobility entity is worth on a per-share basis to current holders who become the minority, cannot be answered without the pro-forma economics.

Consensus targets ($33 low / $39 median / $46 high) imply 19–66% upside and would ordinarily be treated as floors on a cheap, improving stock. Here, the guardrails cut the other way: the sentiment trend is degrading, current-year estimates are falling, and the targets predate full clarity on deal terms. Treating them as reliable floors requires confidence in a standalone valuation framework that the transaction has made obsolete. We score valuation at 5.0: the apparent discount is real on paper but is not a clean asymmetric-upside setup, and the anti-predictive history of "cheap" auto-supplier multiples argues against rewarding it.

## Competitive Landscape

Dana competes among Tier-1 driveline and powertrain suppliers — American Axle, Meritor/Cummins, BorgWarner, Allison, and Eaton's mobility unit — in cyclical, concentrated end markets. Its structural edge is high content on rear-/all-wheel-drive light trucks and SUVs, deep OEM relationships, and the Spicer/Victor Reinz brands. The margin trajectory (Light Vehicle EBITDA to 8.8%, Commercial Vehicle to 10.5%) shows the business executing on cost, but that is operational efficiency rather than share gains; revenue is roughly flat, so there is no evidence of the company taking meaningful share.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The Eaton combination is a deliberate bulk-up in on-highway powertrain and aftermarket, where scale and less-cyclical revenue improve positioning. Against that, EV disappointment is materializing directly on Dana's income statement: a $56M charge in Q1 2026 and $36M in 2024 for cancelled EV programs, plus the buyout of Hydro-Québec's 45% stake in Dana TM4 for $190M to clear a JV overhang. Management frames EV as a content-per-vehicle opportunity (up to 3x on a dollar basis) but is simultaneously cutting EV/engineering spend to match slowing adoption, and the 10-K flags rising OEM vertical integration and more bidders. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the aftermarket/scale axis but pressured on the electrification axis, netting to a below-average score. Competitive-position scores in this framework have shown weak recent predictive value, so we weight this dimension modestly.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is elevated and concentrated around binary, non-priced events.

1. **Deal execution and terms (largest).** The Eaton RMT is large, complex, and subject to regulatory approval. Pro-forma financials, synergy targets, and the precise consideration mix are not in filed SEC documents. Deal failure, unfavorable terms, or a protracted regulatory review are all material downside vectors that the market cannot yet fully price.
2. **Customer concentration.** Top-10 customers are 76% of 2025 sales; loss of a high-content truck platform would be materially damaging.
3. **End-market cyclicality.** NA truck production is in a sharp downcycle; the growth bridge depends on converting the $950M backlog against soft volumes.
4. **Tariffs / trade policy.** Dana recovers most tariff costs from OEMs on a ~90-day lag (margin timing drag) and holds $73M of unapplied Section 232 credits as a contract liability. The February 2026 SCOTUS ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs creates un-booked, ambiguous refund potential in both directions.
5. **EV transition.** Recurring program-termination charges confirm the downside is already materializing.
6. **FX.** ~43–44% of sales are non-USD; a 5% euro move is a ~$115–135M sales swing, and Argentina remains hyperinflationary.
7. **Pension/OPEB.** Frozen U.S. plans with $125M unrecognized losses; manageable but present.

Set against these, the mitigants are meaningful: liquidity of ~$1.6B, a de-risked balance sheet, and pay metrics tied to the deleveraging/cost/return-of-capital plan. But the deal binary dominates, and it is not priced. Risk factors score 4.5.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 54.5% versus 42.8% one-year historical vol, rich by ~12 points, consistent with the market pricing elevated event uncertainty around the transaction. The term structure is flat (near 54.5% ≈ far 54.3%), meaning uncertainty is spread evenly across tenors rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst, appropriate for a deal that resolves over quarters. The put/call skew is balanced (0.1% at the front, though the 3-month tenor shows an 8.7% put skew), and put/call OI at 1.26 is modestly put-heavy. The implied ±12.1% move on the nearest expiry reflects a wide distribution of outcomes. IV has eased slightly (56.0% → 54.5%) as the price fell 8.7% since mid-June, indicating no acute panic but sustained two-sided uncertainty. This confirms the market is treating DAN as an unresolved special situation, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38–46).** The margin turnaround continues, 2026 EBITDA lands in the upper half of guidance, and the Eaton combination closes on terms that credit Dana holders with meaningful synergy value in a larger, less-cyclical, aftermarket-weighted entity. A re-rating toward the peer median on a stronger pro-forma earnings base, aided by the $2B buyback authorization, pushes the stock back toward and above the prior high. Consensus high target is $46.

**Bear Case ($20–24).** The truck downcycle deepens and backlog conversion disappoints; the Eaton deal faces regulatory delay, unfavorable terms, or collapses, stranding a standalone supplier at a mid-cycle-trough multiple. EV charges recur, tariff timing drags margins, and the minority-holder structure caps any control premium. A return toward the low-$20s (where the stock traded as recently as late 2025) is plausible, roughly 15–25% below current.

**Base Case ($27–30).** Margins hold near guidance, the deal progresses toward a Q1 2027 close with terms that are fair but not transformative to current holders, and the stock trades roughly in place while the market awaits pro-forma clarity. The absence of published synergy math and a degrading sentiment trend keep a lid on re-rating in the near term.

The thesis is balanced. The operating improvement and clean balance sheet support downside protection; the deal binary and stale valuation framework cap conviction on the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call. The operating turnaround is real and the balance sheet is far stronger than screens show, but the equity's value now depends on undisclosed pro-forma economics of a deal not closing until Q1 2027, and the recent drawdown reflects legitimate uncertainty about how current holders fare as the minority, not merely price volatility.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Publication of pro-forma financials and credible synergy targets for the combined entity that imply per-share value to current holders materially above the current price (e.g., pro-forma EBITDA and a defensible multiple supporting >$35).
- Confirmed 2026 EBITDA tracking to the upper half of guidance with current-year EPS revisions turning positive and the sentiment trend re-inflecting.
- Regulatory clearance signals that de-risk the Q1 2027 close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Eaton deal termination or a materially unfavorable renegotiation of terms for Dana holders.
- 2026 EBITDA guidance cut, or backlog conversion visibly failing against a deepening truck downcycle.
- A step-up in EV program charges or tariff-timing margin drag that pushes continuing-ops FCF below the $250M guide floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release and the eventual filing of pro-forma/transaction documents (S-4 or equivalent) for the Eaton combination, the first point at which the central valuation question becomes answerable. Interim quarterly prints are secondary to deal disclosure.

## Conclusion

Dana has done the hard work of a turnaround: it divested Off-Highway at a healthy price, deleveraged from ~$3.2B to ~$1.26B, nearly doubled adjusted EBITDA on cost self-help, and returned ~$775M to shareholders while cutting share count 25%. The financial health is meaningfully better than any screen keyed to the FY2025 balance sheet will show. But the equity is now a special situation whose payoff hinges on a Reverse Morris Trust combination with Eaton's Mobility business that makes current holders the minority, whose pro-forma economics are not yet public, and which does not close until Q1 2027. The stock's slide from its April high is a rational response to that structure, not an obvious mispricing. With forward multiples that look cheap only against a standalone frame the deal has rendered obsolete, degrading sell-side sentiment, falling current-year estimates, and an options market pricing wide two-sided uncertainty, the risk/reward is balanced. We recommend HOLD and would revisit once transaction documents make the pro-forma value to current shareholders assessable.
