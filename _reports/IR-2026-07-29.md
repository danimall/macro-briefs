**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ingersoll Rand is a diversified "flow creation" and life-science equipment manufacturer (compressors, pumps, vacuums, blowers, and biopharma containment) built through a serial-acquisition and decentralized-operating playbook ("IRX"). The business has genuine structural quality: a large installed base drives 36.5% of revenue from recurring aftermarket, capex is light (~1.8-2.0% of sales), adjusted EBITDA margins sit at 27.4%, and free cash flow ran $1.22B in 2025. The stock trades around $84.67 (recent $86.50), roughly 14% below its 52-week high of $100.96, giving a market cap near $33B.

The core problem with the investment case is the quality of growth. Headline revenue rose 5.7% to $7.65B in 2025 and 7.6% in Q1 2026, but those numbers are manufactured. Strip out acquisitions (+$419.9M) and FX (+$92.0M) and organic revenue fell $96.0M in 2025 (-1.8% in the core ITS segment); Q1 2026 organic revenue was roughly flat with ITS organic orders down 2.6%. The core cash engine's segment EBITDA margin dropped 130bps in 2025 and another 210bps in Q1 2026 to 26.7%. GAAP net income fell 31% to $581.4M, dragged by $273.4M of goodwill/intangible impairments tied to the 2024 ILC Dover acquisition plus a $127.1M equity-method loss. The company has filed an insurance claim on ILC Dover's reps and warranties, which implies IR believes the largest recent deal was materially misrepresented or fell short of diligence. The residual Life Sciences reporting unit carries only a 30% fair-value cushion, a flagged candidate for further write-downs.

At ~24x the FY26 consensus of $3.50 EPS, IR is not expensive against its 27.3x peer median, but it is not cheap either for a business with negative organic growth, deteriorating core-segment margins, and stable-to-slightly-negative estimate revisions. Analyst sentiment is degrading and the score has drifted lower across three prior reports (6.3 → 5.6 → 5.5). We maintain HOLD. The recurring-revenue moat, disciplined capital structure, and genuinely downside-aligned management compensation keep this out of AVOID territory; the stalled organic engine and impairment overhang keep it out of BUY. The variable that resolves the call is an organic-order re-acceleration, which is not yet in the data.

## Company Overview

Ingersoll Rand operates 60+ manufacturing facilities across six continents with ~21,000 employees and 90+ brands (Ingersoll Rand, Gardner Denver, Nash, CompAir, Elmo Rietschle, ILC Dover, Milton Roy, Seepex, ARO). It reports two segments:

- **Industrial Technologies & Services (ITS)** — ~79% of revenue ($6.06B in 2025). Air/gas compression, vacuum, blowers, fluid transfer. This is the cash engine, with ~41% aftermarket mix.
- **Precision & Science Technologies (PST)** — ~21% of revenue ($1.59B in 2025). Positive-displacement pumps, liquid handling, life-science single-use systems and containment (ILC Dover, Flexan), plus the space-suit/aerospace business.

The durable moat rests on four pillars: a large installed base where aftermarket revenue over a compressor's 10-12 year life typically exceeds the original sale price; mission-critical, low-cost-of-product/high-cost-of-failure positioning; scale leadership in a fragmented market; and the IRX operating model that drives margin discipline and M&A sourcing. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue. Principal competitors are Atlas Copco, IDEX, Kaeser, and Elgi in industrial; Dover, Graco, Sartorius, Thermo Fisher, and Watson-Marlow in PST.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** $5.92B (2022) → $6.88B (2023) → $7.24B (2024) → $7.65B (2025). The composition matters more than the growth rate: 2025 growth came from acquisitions and FX, offset by organic decline. ITS organic revenue fell 1.8% and ITS organic orders rose only 1.4%. Q1 2026 repeated the pattern: reported revenue +7.6% to $1,847.2M, but organic -0.4%, ITS organic revenue -1.6%, ITS orders -2.6%. The organic demand engine has stalled and growth is being sourced through acquisitions.

**Profitability.** GAAP net income fell from $838.6M (2024) to $581.4M (2025) despite higher revenue, driven by the impairments (Biopharma $170.3M, Aerospace & Defense $59.4M, tradename $36.1M, all ILC Dover-linked) and the equity-method loss. GAAP operating margin compressed from 18.0% to 15.0%. Adjusted EBITDA margin slipped 50bps to 27.4% and adjusted net income was flat ($1,348M vs $1,349M). The clearest fundamental signal is the ITS segment adjusted EBITDA margin: down 130bps to 28.9% in 2025 and a further 210bps to 26.7% in Q1 2026, attributed to unfavorable cost leverage on lower volume, product mix, input-cost inflation, and margin-dilutive one-for-one tariff pass-through pricing. PST margins moved the other way: 30.0% in 2025, 30.3% in Q1 2026.

**Cash flow.** FCF was $1,220M in 2025 (16% margin), essentially flat versus $1,247.6M in 2024. Operating cash flow ($1,356M) exceeds net income, a positive earnings-quality signal, and capex is minimal (PP&E only $930M). Q1 2026 FCF fell to $163.4M from $222.7M on working-capital use and higher taxes.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $4.78B, all fixed-rate senior notes (5.2%-5.7%, laddered 2027-2054, partly swapped to floating). Net leverage is modest at ~1.7x adjusted EBITDA; D/E 0.81; current ratio 2.06; $1.25B cash plus $2.6B undrawn revolver and a $2.6B CP program. The defining feature is intangibility: goodwill ($8.48B) plus intangibles ($4.24B) equal $12.7B, or 70% of total assets. The grey-zone Altman Z of 2.28 is a mechanical artifact of that goodwill-heavy structure depressing EBIT/assets and asset turnover, not a distress signal; a Piotroski 5/5 on available components confirms underlying health.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks quadrupled to $1,018M in 2025 (vs $261M in 2024) after the Board raised authorization by $1.0B in May 2025. The dividend is nominal ($31.8M, ~0.1% yield); IR is not an income name. M&A remains the primary capital use ($525M in 2025; $2.96B in 2024 for ILC Dover).

Financial health earns a middling score. The cash generation, light capex, and manageable leverage are genuine strengths, but the 70%-goodwill balance sheet with a demonstrated, still-active impairment risk and softening ITS margins prevent a strong reading.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has deteriorated materially over the last four quarters, and the data confirms rather than reverses the decline reflected in prior scoring (Growth Prospects fell from 7.5 to 5.0 across three reports). The distinction that matters: reported growth is positive, organic growth is negative. Acquisitions and FX carried the top line in both 2025 and Q1 2026 while the underlying business shrank. That is a structurally lower-quality growth profile than the headline suggests.

Estimate revisions corroborate the stall. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $3.50, down 0.4% over 90 days and flat over 30 days; the current-quarter estimate fell 3.6% over 90 days. There is no positive revision velocity here, nothing resembling the kind of upward move that would flag a re-rating candidate. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of adjusted EPS $3.45-$3.57 and revenue growth 2.5%-4.5%, with no net tariff impact expected. That guidance implies continued reliance on M&A and pricing to reach the top of a low-single-digit range while organic volume stays flat-to-negative.

PST/life science is the one bright spot, outgrowing and out-margining ITS and offering a secular counterweight (aging population, biopharma investment). But PST is only 21% of revenue and is itself the source of the ILC Dover impairment, so its contribution is offset by execution risk. The industrial backdrop of soft capex, uncertain capacity utilization, and tariff-driven cost inflation is a sector-wide headwind visible across the broader industrial complex, not an IR-specific problem, but it caps the odds of a near-term organic re-acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $84.67, IR trades at roughly 24.2x the FY26 consensus of $3.50 and 22.0x the FY27 estimate of $3.85. The reported trailing P/E of 58.6x is distorted by the impairment-depressed GAAP EPS of $1.45 and is not the relevant metric; the forward multiple on adjusted earnings is the right anchor.

Against the peer set (Dover 27.3x, Xylem 27.1x, Hubbell 27.7x, Otis 18.6x, with a 27.3x median), IR's ~24x forward looks like a modest discount. But that discount is justified, not a mispricing to exploit. Dover, Xylem, and Hubbell carry positive organic growth; IR does not. A business growing organically at negative-to-flat with core-segment margins compressing 130-210bps deserves to trade below peers with cleaner organic trajectories. The PEG is unfavorable: on ~5-8% forward adjusted EPS growth (largely M&A- and buyback-driven), 24x is not cheap.

There is no identifiable catalyst that would force a re-rating. Estimates are flat, the dividend yield offers no support, and cycle-adjusted earnings power is not meaningfully above the trailing adjusted level because the trailing period is not a cyclical trough but a period of manufactured growth masking organic softness. The valuation dimension scores below neutral: this is a full-price stock for a business whose underlying engine is stalled, and cheapness relative to peers reflects a real quality gap rather than a market error. The historical pattern in this environment is that "cheap-looking" multiples on decelerating businesses sort the wrong way; IR is not even cheap on an absolute basis.

The $80-$115 analyst target range (mean $93.46, median $92) frames modest upside to consensus, but with sentiment degrading and no revision momentum, those targets are not floors here; the setup lacks the rising-estimate profile that turns consensus into a springboard.

## Competitive Landscape

IR's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, which is why the trajectory reading matters more than the level. The aftermarket-dense installed base is a real moat and remains intact; recurring revenue at 36.5% of the total provides ballast even as new-equipment orders soften. The IRX operating system continues to drive PST margin expansion and disciplined-in-theory M&A. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, limiting concentration risk.

Against that, ITS organic orders were negative in Q1 2026, and margin compression in the core suggests either pricing pressure, adverse mix, or an inability to fully leverage a decentralized cost base on lower volumes. Atlas Copco remains a larger, better-capitalized competitor in the flagship compressor markets. The tariff pass-through dynamic of raising revenue without raising profit is a symptom of pricing power that is defensive rather than expansive. The ILC Dover impairment is direct evidence that the M&A engine, the primary source of "competitive expansion" in the thesis, has misfired on its largest recent bet. On balance the position is holding, not widening, which supports a mid-range score consistent with the prior report's downgrade from 7.5 to 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/intangible impairment (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** With 70% of assets in goodwill and intangibles built on a roll-up, IR is structurally exposed to write-downs. This already materialized in 2025. The most important disclosure in the filings is the Q1 2026 note that reps-and-warranties on the ILC Dover deal "were backed up by insurance, and we have filed a claim under that policy." That is IR effectively signaling it believes ILC Dover was materially misrepresented or underperformed diligence less than two years after a $2.96B purchase. The Life Sciences unit's 30% fair-value cushion (versus 85%+ elsewhere) means further impairment is plausible and not yet priced.

**Organic demand and cyclicality.** ITS posted negative organic revenue in 2025 and Q1 2026, with orders flat-to-negative. If the M&A cadence slows or valuations tighten, reported growth would converge toward the negative organic reality.

**Tariffs and trade policy.** One-for-one, margin-dilutive tariff pass-through raises the revenue denominator without profit; Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs raise input costs. With 57-58% of revenue non-US and heavy manufacturing in Germany, China, Italy, the UK, and India, the "no net tariff impact" 2026 guidance carries material uncertainty.

**FX translation.** ~54-57% of revenue is non-USD. FX was a +$92M tailwind in 2025 and +$72M in Q1 2026; a reversal would pressure reported results and expose the organic weakness.

**Leverage and interest cost.** Interest expense rose to $253.9M in 2025 (+$40.7M) post-ILC Dover financing. Leverage is manageable at ~1.7x with a 3.5x covenant (4.0x post-acquisition), but a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the acquisition-financed model.

**Governance note.** Insider activity is routine option-exercise-and-sell (CEO Reynal sold ~$13.8M Feb-Apr 2026 at $88-94), with no open-market purchases, a neutral-to-slightly-negative but unremarkable signal. Frequent Item 5.02 8-K filings (2025-12-17, 2025-07-15) warrant monitoring but appear to be board changes rather than abrupt C-suite departures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 43.4% versus 34.9% trailing realized, a rich 8.5pp spread, with the term structure in backwardation (43.4% at 1m falling to 33.5% at 6m); the market is pricing an imminent event, consistent with an approaching earnings print. Put/call skew is balanced (0.0%) and put/call volume (0.40) and OI (0.21) ratios are light, so there is no directional bearish tilt in the options positioning despite the elevated vol. Short interest is low at 4.1% of float (3.3 days to cover), confirming this is not a crowded bearish setup. The collapse in the recorded ATM IV since the prior snapshot (704% → 43.4%) is a thin-chain data artifact and non-informative. Net read: the market expects a move around earnings but is not positioned directionally against the stock; neutral, with the rich near-term IV arguing against buying premium into the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$105-115).** Industrial capex recovers in late 2026, ITS organic orders turn positive, and the core-segment margin compression reverses as volume leverage returns. The M&A pipeline continues to convert at accretive multiples, PST/life science compounds through the biopharma cycle, the ILC Dover insurance claim recovers a portion of the impairment, and the $1B+ buyback pace supports EPS toward $4+. On a recovered organic-growth profile the stock re-rates toward the 27x peer multiple on rising estimates. This is the analyst-high $115 scenario, requiring a genuine organic inflection that is not yet visible.

**Bear case (~$65-70).** Industrial demand stays soft into 2027, ITS margins compress further, and the Life Sciences unit takes another impairment against its thin 30% cushion. FX reverses from tailwind to headwind, tariff pass-through continues to dilute margins, and the market re-rates a negative-organic-growth serial acquirer to a discount. EPS stalls near $3.40-3.50 and the multiple compresses toward 19-20x, taking the stock back to its May 2026 low near $68. This is roughly 18-23% downside from current levels.

**Base case (~$88-92).** Reported growth stays in the guided 2.5-4.5% range on continued M&A and pricing, adjusted EPS lands near the $3.50-3.55 midpoint, organic remains flat-to-slightly-negative, and the stock holds a ~24-25x forward multiple with limited re-rating in either direction. This yields roughly $88-92, modest upside to consensus mean/median, consistent with a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print (expected late July 2026), where the ITS organic-order trend and margin trajectory will either confirm continued stall or signal inflection.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ITS organic orders turn positive for two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026), confirming the core demand engine has re-accelerated.
- Consensus FY26/FY27 EPS revisions turn positive (30-day and 90-day both rising), reversing the current flat-to-negative trend.
- A pullback toward the low-$70s that compresses the forward multiple below ~20x while fundamentals hold, materially improving the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second material impairment in the Life Sciences/ILC Dover units, or disclosure that the insurance claim is denied, confirming the M&A engine's largest bet is unrecoverable.
- ITS adjusted EBITDA margin compresses below 25%, indicating structural rather than cyclical erosion.
- FX or tariffs turn from neutral/tailwind to a quantified drag that forces a guidance cut below the $3.45 floor.

## Conclusion

Ingersoll Rand is a well-run, cash-generative industrial with a defensible aftermarket moat, light capital intensity, and a downside-aligned management team (the CEO's compensation-actually-paid swung to negative $30.2M in 2025 as the company underperformed its peer TSR for the first time in five years). Those qualities keep the stock out of AVOID territory.

They are offset by a stalled organic engine masked by acquisitions and FX, core-segment margin compression of 130-210bps, flat-to-negative estimate revisions, a full-price ~24x forward multiple that is only "cheap" against peers with better organic trajectories, and a live impairment overhang on the ILC Dover acquisition that IR itself is contesting through an insurance claim. The score has drifted down across three reports, and the current data confirms that decline rather than reversing it.

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** There is not enough evidence of organic inflection to justify a BUY, nor enough deterioration to justify a short or AVOID on a fundamentally healthy franchise. The call resolves on organic orders: a positive turn upgrades the name; a second impairment or sub-25% ITS margin downgrades it. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced around the mid-$80s.
