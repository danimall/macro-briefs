## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a concentrated Delaware Basin E&P operator trading at approximately 10x trailing and forward earnings, with proved reserve PV-10 ($8.24B) that, when compared to enterprise value (approximately $10.8B including net debt), suggests modest undervaluation of the company's unproved inventory and midstream assets, though PV-10 is not a direct measure of fair market value. The stock has rallied ~70% from its November 2025 lows near $37 as oil prices recovered and the company delivered four consecutive earnings beats, but it remains below its 52-week high. The core thesis rests on three converging catalysts: the Hugh Brinson Pipeline coming online in Q4 2026 (improving chronically depressed Waha gas realizations), reduced 2026 capex ($1.35-1.44B D/C/E vs. $1.53B in 2025) driving FCF expansion, and production volume growth compounding on prior Ameredev acquisition gains.

The primary risk is commodity price sensitivity — oil below $55/Bbl would stress unit economics, and the current geopolitical environment introduces meaningful near-term binary uncertainty on oil prices. Geographic concentration (98%+ Delaware Basin) and elevated post-acquisition leverage (Debt/EBITDA ~1.5x consolidated) add to the risk profile. However, the combination of operational momentum, clustered insider buying at $38-50, and a stock where enterprise value exceeds PV-10 by only a modest margin — leaving limited value assigned to midstream assets, unproved inventory, and commodity upside — makes this an attractive entry for investors willing to accept oil price volatility.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources is a mid-cap independent E&P company founded in 2003 by CEO Joseph Wm. Foran, listed on the NYSE in 2012. The company operates almost exclusively in the Delaware Basin portion of the Permian Basin across Southeast New Mexico and West Texas, with ~212,500 net acres of concentrated, operated acreage.

**Key operational metrics (FY2025):**
- Average production: 207,070 BOE/d (58% oil, 42% natural gas/NGLs), up 21% YoY
- 1,802 net identified drilling locations (~19.1 million net lateral feet)
- 51% ownership in San Mateo Midstream JV (720 MMcf/d gas processing, 475K Bbl/d water disposal)
- Average lateral length of 10,400 feet
- Adopted Simul-Frac/Trimul-Frac completion techniques

The company completed its $1.83B Ameredev acquisition in September 2024, which significantly expanded its Delaware Basin footprint and drove the production growth acceleration visible in 2024-2025 results.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Growth Runway

Matador's 1,802 net drilling locations at current development pace (~24-30 wells/quarter) provides 15+ years of inventory. The Ameredev acquisition in 2024 added substantial Wolfcamp and Bone Spring targets. Multi-zone development across 15+ horizons creates optionality to shift capital toward higher-return zones.

2026 production guidance of 209,500-215,000 BOE/d represents approximately 3% growth despite the capex reduction, as wells drilled in late 2025 continue to ramp.

### Hugh Brinson Pipeline Catalyst (Q4 2026)

Matador secured 500,000 MMBtu/day of capacity on Energy Transfer's Hugh Brinson Pipeline, expected online Q4 2026. This provides Gulf Coast gas market access, significantly improving gas realizations versus depressed Waha pricing that averaged -$2.96/MMBtu differential to Henry Hub in 2025 and widened to -$5.11/MMBtu by February 2026.

However, the uplift magnitude depends on the portion of MTDR's gas volumes currently exposed to Waha pricing. According to the 10-K, a significant portion of Delaware Basin gas is already sold at Houston Ship Channel pricing, approximately 4% of 2025 gas production came from the Haynesville (not Waha-exposed), and most Delaware gas is processed for NGLs, which reduces reported Waha exposure. While the pipeline is a meaningful positive catalyst for gas realizations, the annualized revenue uplift will depend on the specific volume of production currently disadvantaged by Waha pricing and the net transportation cost differential — a precise uplift figure requires assumptions not fully disclosed in public filings.

## Valuation Assessment

### Absolute Valuation

At $62.93 and approximately 8.8x estimated FY2026 EPS, MTDR trades at a discount to the broader E&P peer group. Adjusting for SM Energy (the most comparable pure-play Delaware Basin peer) at 5.5x, MTDR's multiple is modestly higher, reflecting its superior growth rate and midstream integration.

On EV/EBITDA: Enterprise value of approximately $10.8B (market cap plus total consolidated net debt) divided by ~$2.3B trailing Adjusted EBITDA = approximately 4.7x. On FY2026 EBITDA (estimated higher given volumes + potentially better gas realizations post-Hugh Brinson), this should compress modestly.

### Asset-Based Valuation

PV-10 of proved reserves: **$8.24B**; after-tax Standardized Measure: **$6.99B**. PV-10 is an asset-level, pre-tax reserve metric that the company explicitly states "is not an estimate of the fair market value" of its properties. PV-10 should be compared to enterprise value, not market capitalization. With enterprise value of approximately $10.8B (using market cap of ~$7.4-7.8B plus ~$3.4B consolidated net debt), EV modestly exceeds PV-10, suggesting the market assigns limited incremental value to:
- 5,295 gross (1,802 net) identified but unproved drilling locations
- San Mateo Midstream's equity value (noting its $883M of non-recourse borrowings and the 49% non-controlling interest held by Five Point)
- The Hugh Brinson Pipeline capacity commitment
- Any commodity upside above SEC pricing assumptions used in the PV-10 calculation

### San Mateo Midstream

The San Mateo JV (51% owned by MTDR) generated substantial distributions ($136.7M + $13M performance incentives to MTDR in 2025) and has significant infrastructure. However, any sum-of-the-parts valuation must account for San Mateo's $883M of non-recourse borrowings and the 49% non-controlling interest. Without disclosed standalone EBITDA, a precise equity valuation attributable to MTDR requires assumptions; management has indicated the midstream represents meaningful value beyond what is reflected in the upstream-focused PV-10 metric.

## Competitive Landscape

### Positioning Within Delaware Basin

Matador occupies a differentiated mid-cap position: large enough to execute multi-zone pad development efficiently (10,400 ft laterals, Simul-Frac completions), small enough to generate meaningful per-share production growth. The 85% average working interest in operated wells provides operational control and full exposure to returns.

The San Mateo midstream JV ensures flow assurance, generates third-party revenue, and provides operational flexibility that non-integrated operators lack. Processing capacity of 720 MMcf/d with expansion potential supports production growth without relying on third-party infrastructure.

### Relative to Peers

Against SM Energy (the closest pure-play comp), MTDR trades at a premium on P/E but offers: higher production growth, midstream integration, deeper inventory, and the Hugh Brinson catalyst. Against larger operators (EOG, FANG), MTDR offers higher per-share growth but lower capital market access and geographic diversification.

The competitive trajectory is positive: each quarter of execution extends the multi-zone development track record, the midstream JV is scaling, and the Ameredev integration appears complete.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Oil Price Sensitivity (High Impact, Moderate Probability):** With a breakeven around $55-60/Bbl for positive FCF and $40-45/Bbl for cash costs, MTDR can sustain current operations in most price environments. However, the stock's equity value is highly leveraged to oil prices given post-acquisition debt. The hedge book (described in Financial Analysis) provides downside protection but limits upside participation. Recent oil price volatility — with WTI trading in a wide range during early 2026 amid shifting geopolitical dynamics including reported U.S.-Iran discussions — introduces meaningful binary risk.

**2. Geographic Concentration (Moderate):** 98%+ single-basin exposure. Any regional regulatory action (New Mexico air permits, federal lands restriction, seismicity-related injection curtailments) would affect nearly 100% of operations.

**3. Leverage (Moderate, Declining):** Consolidated debt of approximately $3.4B with Debt/EBITDA at ~1.5x is manageable. The March 2026 refinancing eliminated near-term maturity risk, though at a 6.000% coupon that maintains elevated interest expense.

**4. Federal Lands Exposure (~33%):** BLM permitting delays and potential regulatory changes under future administrations create headline risk, though current political environment appears favorable (EPA rescinded Endangerment Finding Feb 2026 — favorable for oil/gas sector if upheld in court; OBBBA made bonus depreciation permanent).

**5. Key Man Risk:** CEO Foran (age 70+) founded the company and remains central to strategic direction. The dual Co-President structure suggests succession planning is underway.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources offers an attractive risk/reward setup: enterprise value only modestly exceeds proved reserve PV-10, insider buying at $38-50 confirms management's conviction in intrinsic value, production is growing approximately 3% on reduced capex, and a specific dated catalyst (Hugh Brinson Pipeline Q4 2026) should improve the single largest operational headwind (Waha gas pricing). The March 2026 refinancing has de-risked the balance sheet by eliminating near-term maturities.

The position requires acceptance of oil price volatility — geopolitical uncertainty creates near-term risk, and the 2026 hedge book limits both downside and upside capture. However, MTDR's low breakeven (~$40-45/Bbl cash costs), reduced 2026 capex, and 1,800+ location inventory provide resilience through price cycles. At approximately 8.8x forward earnings for a company growing production with a specific catalyst to improve suppressed gas realizations, the risk/reward favors ownership.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% portfolio allocation. Size conservatively given oil price binary; add on any post-earnings dip below $58 or on deal-collapse oil spike.
