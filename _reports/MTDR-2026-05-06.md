## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a mid-cap Delaware Basin E&P that has grown through disciplined organic development and two accretive acquisitions (Advance in 2023, Ameredev in 2024) into a major Delaware Basin producer. The company now generates 207,000+ BOE/d (58% oil) with a proved reserve PV-10 of $8.24B. The stock trades at approximately 10x trailing P/E and an estimated 8x forward P/E on FY2026 consensus estimates, with sell-side estimate revisions reportedly running strongly positive over recent months.

The investment case centers on three converging catalysts: (1) a free cash flow inflection driven by lower capex post-integration and rising production, with management guiding 2026 adjusted FCF of $1.1–1.2B (vs. $437M adjusted FCF attributable to shareholders in 2025); (2) the Hugh Brinson pipeline entering service in H2 2026, which is expected to provide access to historically more profitable Henry Hub markets and materially improve natural gas realizations from current depressed Waha pricing; and (3) active deleveraging ($350M repaid in Q1 alone) reducing the elevated post-acquisition leverage. At oil prices above the ~$65 WTI strip used in management's 2026 guidance, actual FCF could exceed guided levels.

The primary risks are commodity price cyclicality, geographic concentration in the Delaware Basin, and remaining leverage of ~1.5x net debt/EBITDA. The current geopolitical environment creates potential binary outcomes for oil prices. In a scenario where oil falls to $70 WTI, Matador's cash flows compress but the company should remain cash flow positive given its competitive cost structure.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources operates primarily in the Delaware Basin of southeastern New Mexico and West Texas, targeting the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations. The company maintains a vertically integrated model through San Mateo Midstream (51% owned), which provides natural gas processing (720 MMcf/day capacity), oil transportation (~120 miles of pipelines), and produced water disposal (475,000 Bbl/day). This midstream integration provides flow assurance and cost control in a basin where infrastructure constraints have caused severe pricing dislocations.

Key operational metrics at year-end 2025:
- Average daily production: 207,070 BOE/d (58% oil, 42% natural gas/NGLs)
- Proved reserves: 667 million BOE
- Net acreage: ~190,000+ acres in Delaware Basin
- 2026 D/C/E budget: $1.35–1.44B (down ~11% from 2025)
- Active rigs: 6 (down from 8 during Ameredev integration)

The company's operational footprint includes approximately 33% of acreage on federal BLM-administered lands, which introduces regulatory complexity but also provides access to premium rock quality in Eddy and Lea counties, New Mexico.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Dynamics:**

FY2025 total revenues of approximately $3.70B grew modestly YoY despite a 14% decline in realized oil prices ($64.99/bbl vs. $75.89/bbl), with 21% production growth more than offsetting price compression. Net income attributable to Matador shareholders declined to $759M from $885M, reflecting the lower price environment. Adjusted EBITDA attributable to Matador shareholders of $2.29B held flat YoY, demonstrating volume-driven earnings resilience.

**Cash Flow Profile:**

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026E |
|--------|------|------|-------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $2.25B | $2.43B | ~$2.5–2.7B |
| D/C/E Capex | $1.22B | $1.54B | $1.35–1.44B |
| Adjusted FCF (attributable to shareholders) | N/A* | $437M | $1.1–1.2B (guided) |

*2024 consolidated capital expenditures totaled approximately $3.91B, including the $1.83B Ameredev acquisition cash consideration, $2.08B in land/seismic acquisition expenditures, and $236M in midstream acquisition expenditures. D/C/E capex in 2024 was approximately $1.22B.

The adjusted FCF inflection from $437M in 2025 to guided $1.1–1.2B in 2026 is driven by lower capex (post-Ameredev integration), continued production growth, and expected improvement in gas realizations. At current oil prices above the strip used in guidance, actual 2026 FCF could exceed these levels.

**Balance Sheet & Deleveraging:**

Total debt stands at approximately $3.4B (Credit Agreement: $398M; San Mateo: $883M; Senior Notes: $2.12B). This represents approximately 1.5x net debt/adjusted EBITDA (using Matador-attributable adjusted EBITDA of $2.29B). In Q1 2026, Matador paid down $350M on its revolver and expects the revolving balance to reach zero by May 2026. The March 2026 refinancing issued $750M of 6.000% senior notes due 2034, retiring 2028 maturities and extending the average debt duration while saving $4.3M/year in interest.

The GAAP current ratio of 0.79 understates liquidity: approximately $1.80B in undrawn revolver capacity (after letters of credit) provides ample cushion. The Altman Z-Score of 1.6 is a mechanical artifact of high D&A relative to total assets — common for all E&P companies post-large-acquisition — and is not indicative of genuine financial distress given $2.4B in operating cash flow and active deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth Trajectory:**

Matador has compounded production at 25%+ CAGR over the past three years, driven by organic drilling efficiencies and the Ameredev bolt-on. FY2026 guidance calls for continued mid-single-digit growth with oil production raised to 123,000–125,000 Bbl/d (from 122,000–124,000) without any capex increase, reflecting improving capital efficiency.

The growth runway is supported by 800+ net engineered drilling locations added since January 2023 through the company's "brick-by-brick" land acquisition strategy. Finding and development costs declined from $10.98/BOE in 2024 to $10.34/BOE in 2025, demonstrating that inventory additions are occurring at attractive economics.

**Hugh Brinson Pipeline — The Key Catalyst:**

The single most impactful growth catalyst is the Hugh Brinson pipeline (operated by Energy Transfer), expected to begin flowing gas in Q3 2026 and be fully in service by Q4 2026. Matador has secured firm transportation capacity providing access to historically more profitable Henry Hub pricing at zero capital cost to Matador. Currently, the Waha basis discount averages approximately $5/MMBtu below Henry Hub, meaning Matador realized only $0.64/Mcf for natural gas in Q1 2026.

Management has disclosed that each $0.50/MMBtu improvement in realized natural gas pricing could add approximately $90M in annual revenue. At a $3–4/MMBtu improvement in realized pricing, the pipeline could contribute an estimated $540–720M in incremental annual revenue, though actual cash flow impact would be net of transportation fees and other costs. Even conservatively assuming a smaller volume allocation and $2.50/MMBtu net improvement, the revenue impact would be material.

**Estimate Revision Velocity:**

Sell-side consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS have reportedly risen significantly over recent months, reflecting both the improving oil price environment and analysts catching up to the company's improved capital efficiency profile. Forward P/E compression as estimates rise — while the stock price lags — creates potential for continued re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

**Absolute Valuation:**

At approximately $58–63 (recent trading range), Matador trades at:
- ~10x trailing EPS ($6.09)
- An estimated ~8x FY2026E consensus EPS
- Guided adjusted FCF yield of approximately 15–16% on 2026 estimates ($1.1–1.2B on ~$7.3B market cap)

**Reserve-Based Valuation (with important caveats):**

Matador's proved reserve PV-10 of $8.24B is a standardized SEC metric using year-end pricing. As the company explicitly notes in its filings, PV-10 is **not an estimate of fair market value** and differs from the GAAP standardized measure of discounted future net cash flows ($6.99B) because PV-10 excludes future income taxes. Comparing PV-10 to equity market cap alone ignores the company's approximately $3.4B in consolidated debt.

On an enterprise value basis (equity market cap of ~$7.3B plus net debt of ~$3.4B = ~$10.7B EV), PV-10 of $8.24B does not suggest the same degree of undervaluation as a simple market-cap comparison would imply. However, PV-10 also excludes the value of the midstream business. San Mateo generates approximately $280M in consolidated adjusted EBITDA; Matador's 51% share at 6–8x midstream multiples could be worth $850M–$1.14B. Taking the standardized measure of $6.99B plus midstream value of $850M–$1.14B yields $7.8–$8.1B in total asset value against an enterprise value of ~$10.7B — suggesting the stock is more fairly valued on this basis, though at a discount to PV-10-based estimates.

**Relative Valuation:**

Versus larger E&P peers that trade at higher multiples, Matador's discount has some justification from the elevated debt profile and smaller scale. However, given that: (a) leverage is actively declining, (b) guided FCF yield is among the highest in the peer group, (c) production growth is above-average, and (d) the Hugh Brinson pipeline provides a near-term earnings step-change, the discount appears excessive.

**Analyst Consensus:**

Mean target of $72.47 (median $72.00) with a range of $60–$107. 15 of 20 analysts rate BUY or Strong BUY; zero sells.

## Competitive Landscape

**Positioning Within Delaware Basin:**

Matador has established itself as a major Delaware Basin producer following the Ameredev acquisition. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Integrated midstream (San Mateo):** Flow assurance in a basin where pipe capacity constraints forced shut-ins across the industry. While Matador itself reported "significant market price difficulties for natural gas at the Waha hub" in Q4 2025, its dedicated gathering and processing infrastructure provides operational reliability and some insulation from the most severe spot-market dislocations.

2. **Well productivity outperformance:** Per Enverus data cited in filings, Matador's 12-month cumulative oil production per well improved 8% from 2021 to 2024 while the peer average declined 9%. This 17-percentage-point differential suggests superior geoscience targeting, completion design, or both.

3. **Operational innovation:** 90% diesel reduction through electric/hybrid frac fleets; field-produced natural gas for completions; AI integration for drilling optimization; development of 3.4-mile lateral wells on Ameredev acreage.

4. **Cost structure:** LOE of $5.63/BOE (2025) is competitive, providing substantial downside protection.

**Industry Context:**

The Delaware Basin is in a mature consolidation phase. M&A premiums have risen, making organic inventory addition through leasehold strategy (Matador's approach) increasingly cost-effective relative to corporate M&A. Well productivity degradation is an industry-wide challenge, making Matador's claimed improvement trend — if sustained — a meaningful differentiator.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):**
At ~88% oil revenue, Matador is fundamentally an oil price bet. The current environment is uncertain, with geopolitical developments (the company references "geopolitical events related to the war in Iran") creating potential binary outcomes for oil pricing. At $70 WTI (severe bear case), cash flows would compress materially but should remain positive given the competitive cost structure, though the specific breakeven figure has not been independently verified against a disclosed corporate-level definition.

**Waha Basis (MODERATE, IMPROVING):**
The $5+/MMBtu Waha discount eliminated virtually all natural gas profitability in Q1 2026. This is expected to improve materially with Hugh Brinson in-service H2 2026. Until then, each quarter of depressed gas prices costs approximately $50–75M in potential revenue.

**Leverage (MODERATE, DECLINING):**
Net debt/EBITDA of ~1.5x is manageable but limits flexibility in a severe downturn. The active deleveraging trajectory should bring this below 1.0x by year-end 2026 at current strip pricing.

**Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):**
98% Delaware Basin exposure creates single-basin risk (induced seismicity regulations, infrastructure failures, severe weather). The 33% federal lands exposure adds regulatory risk from potential BLM policy changes.

**Key Person Risk (LOW-MODERATE):**
CEO Foran is the founding architect. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges he cannot be quickly replaced and the company does not carry key-man insurance except for Foran. The recent CFO/COO promotions (April 2026) suggest succession planning is underway.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources offers a compelling combination of value (15–16% guided adjusted FCF yield), growth (mid-single-digit production CAGR with capital efficiency improving), and a specific near-term catalyst (Hugh Brinson pipeline expected to materially improve natural gas realizations). Forward P/E compression as estimates rise — while the stock price has lagged — places MTDR in the category where the market may be systematically underestimating normalized earnings power.

The primary risk is oil price direction, which is currently influenced by geopolitical developments referenced in company filings. The insider buying pattern ($38–56 range by CEO, CFO, and directors with no open-market selling observed) and broad sell-side consensus (zero sell recommendations) provide additional confidence. This is a well-run, domestically-focused Delaware Basin producer with improving capital efficiency, declining leverage, and a defined catalytic event in H2 2026 that the market has not fully priced.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2–3% portfolio sizing, acknowledging the binary oil exposure. Reduce position by 25% if oil falls below $70/bbl sustained, or increase sizing if Hugh Brinson confirmation de-risks the natural gas revenue catalyst.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation case is weaker than originally presented: (1) 2025 adjusted FCF attributable to shareholders was $437M (not $883M), reducing the demonstrated FCF track record; (2) PV-10 compared to enterprise value (not market cap alone) does not show a meaningful discount; (3) the sum-of-parts "25–29% discount" claim is not valid when properly accounting for ~$3.4B in debt. The guided 2026 FCF inflection to $1.1–1.2B remains the core thesis driver but is forward-looking and unverified.
