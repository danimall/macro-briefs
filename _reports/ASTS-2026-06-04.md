**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AST SpaceMobile is a pre-commercial satellite-infrastructure company attempting to build the first space-based cellular broadband network that connects directly to standard, unmodified smartphones. The technology is genuine, the partner roster is marquee (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, STC, Orange, Telefónica, Rakuten), and the company has begun to generate revenue. The problem is the gap between the achievement and the price. At ~$107.73, ASTS carries a market capitalization near $32 billion against $70.9M of FY2025 revenue that is dominated by one-time gateway hardware shipments and government milestones, not the recurring subscriber connectivity revenue the entire thesis depends on. This implies a price-to-sales multiple in the hundreds on a company that has not yet earned a dollar of recurring service revenue.

The financial trajectory makes the valuation harder to support. FY2025 net loss widened to $341.9M, capex rose to $1.06B (from $174M the prior year), and management has stated explicitly in its 10-K that it will need to raise "significant additional capital beyond the currently funded constellation." Share count grew roughly 37% in a single year (208M to 285M Class A shares), and the capital structure is layered with convertible notes and secured loans tied to the Ligado spectrum transaction. Estimate revisions are deeply negative across every period — current-year EPS estimates cut 70.8% in 90 days, next-year flipping from +$0.04 to −$0.65 — and the company has missed earnings in all four reported quarters, including a roughly 60% revenue miss in Q1 2026.

We rate ASTS AVOID. This is a binary, capital-markets-dependent, founder-controlled venture in which the bull case requires flawless execution across launch cadence, spectrum approval, capital raises, and the conversion of MOUs into recurring revenue, while the current price already discounts a successful outcome. The April 2026 loss of BlueBird 7 (failed to reach altitude, deorbited) demonstrates the dominant risk, and a strategic insider associated with Rakuten reportedly reducing its position by 30%+ into retail-driven sector euphoria is a meaningful cautionary signal.

## Company Overview

AST SpaceMobile builds large phased-array satellites ("BlueBirds") in low Earth orbit designed to communicate directly with ordinary mobile phones, eliminating cellular dead zones for the roughly 5 billion mobile subscribers globally and reaching the unconnected. The differentiating asset is the size of its unfolding antenna array; BlueBird 6, deployed in late 2025, is described as the largest commercial communications array antenna deployed in LEO, targeting peak speeds above 120 Mbps.

The business model is wholesale. AST does not sell to consumers directly; it partners with Mobile Network Operators (MNOs) who market the service to subscribers and provide access to their licensed terrestrial spectrum. A second leg is U.S. Government and defense work, including a $30M SDA "HALO Europa" Track 2 prime contract and a prime position on the Missile Defense Agency SHIELD program. The principal competitor in direct-to-device broadband is SpaceX Starlink (partnered with T-Mobile), which has in-house launch capacity and a vastly larger balance sheet.

The company is structurally controlled by founder, Chairman, and CEO Abel Avellan, who holds roughly 72% of voting power through Class C super-voting shares. AST is a Nasdaq "controlled company," does not maintain a majority-independent board or independent compensation committee, and its debt covenants require Avellan's continued presence as CEO.

The FY2025 revenue is real but low-quality for valuation purposes. Roughly $44.4M was one-time product/gateway hardware (15 ground gateways across five continents), with the rest from government milestones. Q4 2025 alone was $54.3M, gateway-driven. None of this is the recurring, high-margin connectivity revenue the thesis requires. Q1 2026 revenue of approximately $14.7M missed consensus near $36M by roughly 60%, with an EPS loss of $0.66; management attributed the shortfall to timing of ground-station shipments and government revenue recognition and reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $150M–$200M. The stock fell roughly 13% on the print.

The balance sheet currently provides runway. At 12/31/25, cash plus restricted cash was approximately $2.78B ($2.34B cash plus $0.44B restricted cash); pro forma liquidity including the February 2026 $1.075B convertible and ATM availability is cited near $3.9B. Total debt was approximately $2.3B at year-end and rises toward $3B-plus after the February raise. The capital structure is heavily convertible-note-based across multiple tranches (2032 4.25%, 2032 2.375%, 2036 2.00%, 2036 2.25%), plus the new 10-year 2.25% convert with an effective conversion price of $116.30. Management has equitized approximately $296.5M of 2032 notes ($250M + $46.5M) and deployed capped calls, but the direction of travel is dilutive.

The crux is capital intensity against liquidity. Per-satellite costs are $21–23M for 90+ Block 2 satellites, with early launches higher, and the plan calls for 45–60 satellites in orbit by end of 2026 at a cadence of roughly one launch every one-to-two months. Layer in $130M+ of remaining Ligado payments and secured-loan debt service, and the funding requirement dwarfs revenue for years. The 10-K is candid that capital "beyond the currently funded constellation size" is required, which makes additional dilution or debt essentially guaranteed. The 10-K also carries explicit going-concern language conditioned on continued capital access.

## Growth Analysis

The growth opportunity, if realized, is large. Direct-to-device broadband addresses a global subscriber base in the billions, and AST has assembled a broad MNO roster across multiple continents, more than $1.2B in aggregate contracted revenue commitments cited, and a $175M prepayment from STC under a 10-year deal. The government/defense leg adds a second revenue vector with credible early contracts.

The growth signals to weigh against that opportunity are negative. Estimate revisions are falling sharply across every horizon: current-quarter EPS revised down 39.5% in 90 days, current-year down 70.8%, and next-year flipping from a slightly positive $0.04 to −$0.65 (a 1,824% downward swing) as the timeline to profitability extends. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate inflection that would justify treating a high trailing multiple as cheap on forward earnings. Here forward estimates are deteriorating, and the company has no positive EPS in sight on consensus.

Revenue growth is real in magnitude ($4.4M to $70.9M) but its composition undermines its predictive value. The Q1 2026 miss demonstrates that guidance is driven by lumpy hardware shipments and government milestone timing rather than the recurring subscriber economics that would compound. Until in-orbit capacity reaches commercial scale and MOUs convert to definitive commercial agreements with subscriber-linked revenue, the growth thesis is an option on execution rather than a trend in the data.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation is the core of the AVOID. A ~$32B market cap on mostly one-time FY2025 revenue is a price/sales multiple in the hundreds, and the company is loss-making with negative and deteriorating forward EPS, so conventional earnings multiples are not meaningful. The cycle-adjusted valuation principle that rescues high trailing multiples on inflecting earnings does not apply here, because the relevant estimates are falling, not rising, and there is no path to positive EPS on current consensus.

The proper way to value ASTS is as a multi-year option on a successful constellation. On that basis, the current price already embeds substantial deployment, spectrum, and monetization success while ignoring the dilution required to fund it. Analyst price targets reflect this skepticism: the mean is $82.02 and median $80.00, both roughly 23–25% below the current price, with a high of $108.00 and a low of $41.20. The stock trades above the high end of the analyst range. Under the guardrail on premium-multiple names, analyst targets here are not a floor; the stock has run past even the most optimistic published target, and the consensus distribution sits materially below spot. The new convertible's effective conversion price of $116.30 is another reference point the market is testing.

The stock has appreciated 328% over the trailing year and ranged from $23.80 to $133.86. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to be bearish, but the appreciation has been driven substantially by sector sentiment (SpaceX IPO buzz, the launch of a 2x leveraged single-stock ETF) rather than by improving fundamentals, while estimates fell. That combination — price up, estimates sharply down — is a warning sign, not an opportunity. We score valuation 2.5.

## Competitive Landscape

AST's competitive edge is technical and partnership-based. Its large arrays are designed to deliver true broadband data directly to phones rather than messaging only, which differentiates it from Globalstar/Apple, Iridium, Lynk, and Skylo. Its MNO roster spanning AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, STC, Orange, Telefónica, CK Hutchison, Taiwan Mobile, Sunrise, and Rakuten is genuinely broad and gives it access to terrestrial spectrum across multiple regions.

The structural disadvantage is decisive over a long horizon. SpaceX Starlink, partnered with T-Mobile, has in-house launch capacity through SpaceX and a far larger balance sheet, while AST must purchase launches from third parties (the lost BlueBird 7 was on Blue Origin's New Glenn per news reports) and continuously tap capital markets to fund deployment. The industry's history is unforgiving of capital intensity — the 10-K itself cites OneWeb and Intelsat bankruptcies as examples of satellite businesses becoming capital traps when capacity outruns demand. We score competitive position 6.0, recognizing genuine technical leadership in large-array D2D against a better-capitalized rival and an unproven monetization model.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are large, specific, and partly already materializing.

- **Going-concern / perpetual capital dependence.** The 10-K explicitly warns failure to raise capital could trigger a going-concern qualification. The thesis rests on continuous access to capital markets at favorable terms, with dilution named as potentially "substantial."
- **Execution / launch failure — already realized.** BlueBird 7 failed to reach altitude and was deorbited in April 2026. The 2026 plan now hinges on a simultaneous mid-June launch of BB8/9/10, concentrating outcome on a single event.
- **Ligado spectrum transaction.** AST agreed to pay $550M total ($420M already paid October 2025; $100M due 3/31/2026; $15M+$15M at closing) for long-term access to up to 45 MHz of lower mid-band spectrum out of Ligado's Chapter 11. Closing is contingent on FCC approval and entangled in litigation: Inmarsat sued in NY State court (December 2025) and has appealed a Bankruptcy Court compliance order to the Delaware District Court. An adverse outcome could impair $420M already sunk and add secured leverage via a $550M Sound Point delayed-draw facility and a $420M UBS cash-collateralized loan.
- **Spectrum/regulatory dependence.** AST operates on terrestrial spectrum it does not own, dependent on MNO consent and a still-pending FCC Modification Application for full U.S. commercial service. Many MNO arrangements remain MOUs, not definitive commercial agreements.
- **Concentrated control and key-man covenant.** Avellan controls ~72% of voting power; debt covenants require him to remain CEO. Class A holders have limited governance protection.
- **Tax Receivable Agreement.** Obligates AST to pay 85% of certain tax savings, with change-of-control acceleration risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 133.5% sits rich to one-year historical vol of 104.0% (a 29.4-point spread, 81st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (133.5% near-term vs. 113.3% at six months), consistent with the market pricing the mid-June launch event as a binary catalyst. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±6.1%, widening to ±19.9% by mid-June. Skew is call-heavy (−6.9% near-term) and put/call volume (0.32) and OI (0.49) ratios are low, reflecting retail-driven upside speculation rather than hedging demand. Short interest is elevated at 17.6% of float (52.4M shares, 2.6 days to cover), so a positive launch outcome carries meaningful squeeze potential. The combination of rich IV, backwardation, and a binary event makes both directional exposures expensive and timing-sensitive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** AST executes the mid-June triple launch and subsequent cadence, reaches commercial constellation scale by end of 2026, closes the Ligado spectrum deal with FCC approval, secures the pending Modification Application, and converts its MNO MOUs into recurring subscriber-linked revenue. In that scenario the company becomes a global D2D broadband utility with a multi-billion-dollar revenue runway, and the current valuation eventually looks early. Bull-case scenarios from the most optimistic published targets and beyond imply prices well above current levels if the option pays off.

**Bear Case.** A launch or deployment failure (as already occurred with BB7), an adverse Inmarsat appeal impairing the Ligado deal, an FCC delay, or simply the dilution required to fund $1B+ annual capex compresses the equity value materially. With falling estimates, four consecutive misses, going-concern language, and a strategic insider reportedly cutting 30%+ of its stake into a retail-fueled rally, the path of least resistance is toward the analyst median ($80) or lower. A meaningful capital raise or launch setback could send shares toward the analyst low ($41.20), roughly 62% below current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would reconsider a constructive view on evidence that the option is being de-risked at a more reasonable price: (1) successful mid-June BB8/9/10 launches with the satellites reaching and maintaining commercial orbit; (2) FCC approval of both the Ligado spectrum transfer and the pending Modification Application, with the Inmarsat appeal resolved favorably; (3) conversion of MNO MOUs into definitive commercial agreements with disclosed recurring, subscriber-linked revenue and a credible path to positive operating cash flow; and (4) a valuation reset toward or below the analyst median ($80) or a level where price/forward sales reflects execution risk. Absent those, the position remains uninvestable on fundamentals.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — capital intensity, dilution, regulatory contingency, and the gap between valuation and recurring revenue — persist for multiple years. The first near-term catalyst is the mid-June 2026 launch sequence, which is a variance event, not a directional one, given the rich, backwardated options pricing.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated event is the mid-June 2026 simultaneous launch of BB8/9/10. A failure, comparable to the April BB7 loss, would remove the near-term deployment narrative and likely drive a sharp decline. The next earnings report and any capital raise (essentially guaranteed given $1B+ capex against a $150–200M revenue plan) are additional negative catalysts. The Inmarsat appeal in Delaware District Court and the pending FCC decisions are dated but uncertain in timing.

**Timing window.** The launch resolves within weeks. The financing and regulatory catalysts are spread over the coming quarters, with the next raise likely as cash burn accelerates through 2026.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest of 17.6% of float with only 2.6 days to cover is elevated and crowded enough to create meaningful squeeze risk into a positive launch outcome. The launch of a 2x leveraged single-stock ETF (ASTY) and heavy retail call-buying (P/C volume 0.32, call-heavy skew) amplify upside-squeeze mechanics on any good news.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis. Skew is call-heavy and put/call ratios are low, indicating speculative upside positioning rather than informed put demand. Near-term IV makes puts expensive, and backwardation means a short-vol or directional put position carries high theta and event premium into the June catalyst.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A failed launch or forced raise could plausibly push shares toward the analyst median ($80, roughly −26%) or the analyst low ($41.20, roughly −62%). But the upside risk on a successful triple launch into elevated short interest, a leveraged ETF, and call-heavy positioning is a squeeze that could exceed +30–50% in days. With a binary, near-dated catalyst that cuts both ways and expensive, event-rich options, the asymmetry does not clear the 2:1 reward/risk bar for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental thesis is sound, but the mechanics are hostile: a near-dated binary launch catalyst, elevated and crowded short interest with low days-to-cover, retail-driven call demand, a leveraged single-stock ETF feeding momentum, and expensive puts in backwardation. This is a name to avoid owning, not to short.

## Conclusion

AST SpaceMobile has accomplished real engineering and assembled a credible partner roster, but the equity is priced as if the multi-year option has already paid off. A ~$32B market cap rests on $70.9M of mostly one-time hardware revenue, a $341.9M net loss, $1B+ annual capex, guaranteed future dilution, falling estimates across every horizon, four consecutive earnings misses, a litigated spectrum deal contingent on FCC approval, a recent in-orbit satellite loss, and a strategic insider cutting 30%+ of its stake into retail euphoria. The stock trades above even the most optimistic analyst target, with the consensus median roughly a quarter below spot. There is no fundamental reason to own this at the current price, and the short side is unattractive given crowded positioning and a near-dated binary catalyst. We rate ASTS **AVOID** and would revisit only on successful deployment, regulatory clearance, recurring-revenue conversion, and a materially lower valuation.
