**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Worthington Enterprises is a Columbus-based designer and manufacturer of building and consumer products, and the surviving "RemainCo" from the December 2023 spin-off of Worthington Steel. The headline revenue collapse from ~$4.9B (FY2023) to $1.4B (FY2026) reflects the steel separation, not a business decline. On a clean post-separation basis, FY2026 net sales grew 20% (9% organic) to $1.4B, adjusted EPS rose 9% to $3.37, and free cash flow reached $170M at a 102% conversion rate. This is a smaller, higher-margin, cash-generative company than the legacy financials imply.

The near-term picture is more mixed. Q4 FY2026 (reported June 23) missed on both lines — revenue down ~3.7% versus consensus, adjusted EPS of $0.97 down ~6.7% — the second consecutive miss, and the stock fell roughly 10% on the print. Two drivers explain most of the shortfall: ClarkDietrich equity income collapsed ~$19M for the full year to $22M on weak non-residential construction, and an A2L refrigerant pre-buy created a tough prior-year comp (~$5M EBITDA headwind). Management frames both as cyclical/timing rather than structural, but the company has now missed two quarters running, and consensus estimates have been cut 4-7% across every forward window over 90 days. The market is right to demand that the "temporary" framing be validated in FY2027 before re-rating the stock.

We rate WOR a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The balance sheet is a genuine strength (net leverage <1.0x, $500M revolver undrawn, JVs throwing off ~$35M of dividends in Q4 alone), the wholly-owned building products base is de-risking away from JV dependence (segment EBITDA +62% to $100M, margin +220bps), and data-center liquid-cooling tanks are a small but fast-growing optionality. But a large, uncontrolled share of earnings flows through equity-method JVs, estimate momentum is negative, and at ~15x forward adjusted EPS the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap. We would upgrade on confirmed ClarkDietrich/non-resi recovery or a pullback into the high-$40s.

## Company Overview

Post-separation, Worthington operates two reporting segments:

- **Building Products (~65% of sales, the profit engine):** pressurized containment (LPG/propane and refrigerant cylinders under BernzOmatic/Balloon Time, water heating and expansion tanks under Amtrol), HVAC components (Elgen), metal roofing clips and retrofit systems (LSI), and two large equity-method joint ventures — **WAVE** (ceiling systems, JV with Armstrong World Industries) and **ClarkDietrich** (steel framing).
- **Consumer Products (~35% of sales):** tools (BernzOmatic torches, 150 years old), outdoor living (griddles, pizza ovens), and celebrations (Balloon Time helium kits). Sold primarily through mass merchandisers.

The defining structural feature is that a large portion of profitability arrives as equity income from unconsolidated JVs rather than consolidated revenue. In FY2026, wholly-owned building products EBITDA was $100M against total building products EBITDA of $240M, implying the WAVE and ClarkDietrich JVs contribute roughly $140M of segment EBITDA. Management acknowledged on the call that the company "probably aren't the easiest, most plain vanilla company to model." Strategically, the company is assembling a "building envelope" portfolio (roofing, HVAC, water/expansion tanks, framing, ceilings) via its Worthington Business System's three pillars: innovation, transformation (automation/AI/80-20), and acquisitions.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2026 full-year (ended May 31, 2026):**
- Net sales $1.4B, +20% YoY (9% organic; ~11% from the Elgen, LSI, Ragasco, and Hydrostat acquisitions)
- GAAP EPS $3.14 (vs. $1.92 prior year); adjusted EPS $3.37, +9%
- Adjusted EBITDA $296M, +12%
- Free cash flow $170M, 102% conversion on adjusted net earnings
- SG&A down 200bps as a percentage of sales

**Q4 FY2026:**
- Net sales $371M (+17%) vs. ~$385.7M consensus (−3.7%)
- Adjusted EPS $0.97 vs. $1.04 consensus (−6.7%), down from $1.06 a year earlier
- Adjusted EBITDA $83.5M (22.5% margin), roughly flat vs. $85.1M
- Gross margin 27.4% vs. 29.3%, compressed by LSI inventory step-up purchase accounting, unfavorable building products mix, and input cost inflation

The two Q4 drags — ClarkDietrich (~$7M in the quarter, called "a trough") and the A2L comp (expected to persist a couple more quarters before moderating by Q2 FY2027) — are demand-timing and cyclical items rather than demand-quality problems. The offset: wholly-owned building products EBITDA grew 62% to $100M with margins expanding 220bps to 11.7%, and consumer products grew sales 4% and EBITDA 10% (margin to 17.5%) despite tariffs.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:**
- Net debt $278M; net leverage <1.0x
- $500M revolving facility fully undrawn; debt matures September 2028
- Cash was drawn from ~$250M (May 2025) to ~$6M (Feb 2026) to fund M&A, since replenished by FCF
- JVs paid ~$35M of dividends in Q4 (90% of equity income); WAVE distributions have historically exceeded the carrying value of the investment
- Dividend raised 5% to $0.20/quarter (declared June 2026); ~$18M repurchases in Q4 (350K shares)
- Capex elevated on a facility modernization project (~$16M remaining, completing mid-FY2027), then normalizing

The financial-health picture is the strongest part of the story: high-quality earnings (FCF conversion above 100%), conservative leverage, ample liquidity, and a JV cash-flow engine that funds M&A without stressing the balance sheet. This supports a financial-health score of 6.8, consistent with the improving trajectory across recent reports.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth of 9% in FY2026 is solid for a building products company, and the acquisition cadence (LSI $206M, Elgen $91M, Ragasco $109M, Hydrostat $10M over ~24 months) has added roughly 11 points of top-line growth. Two structural tailwinds warrant monitoring:

1. **A2L refrigerant transition** — nearly all new residential HVAC equipment is now A2L, expanding the installed base and creating a future service/repair annuity for refrigerant cylinders. This is the same transition that created the tough near-term comp, so the headwind and the tailwind are two phases of one regulatory shift.

2. **Data-center liquid cooling** — WOR shipped ~$13M of ASME water tanks for liquid-cooling infrastructure in FY2026 and expects at least that much in Q1 FY2027 alone. This is genuine, capital-light (partner-manufacturing) optionality with multiple value streams (WAVE, ClarkDietrich, Elgen, and LSI also participate in data-center construction). The caveat is that the opportunity is early-stage and unquantified, with a ~2-year lag from data-center announcement to liquid-cooling revenue.

Against this, near-term earnings momentum is negative. Consensus has cut the current quarter 3.8% (30d) / 7.3% (90d), current year 4.2% / 6.8%, and next year 4.1% / 4.1%. This velocity would not trigger a strong BUY signal, and it must be respected. The two-quarter miss streak (−9.4% and −8.6% in two of the last four quarters) undercuts confidence in forward estimates. ClarkDietrich recovery is the single biggest swing factor: at a trough of $22M, non-resi normalization could add meaningful equity income, but the timing is outside management's control. Growth prospects score 5.4 — real drivers exist, but falling estimates and an uncontrolled JV base cap the score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $56.00 on adjusted EPS of $3.37, WOR trades at ~16.6x trailing adjusted / ~15.0x forward on the $3.73 current-year consensus. That is a discount to the Building Products peer median P/E of 23.7x, but the peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use here: RUN (5.9x) is a distressed solar name, MBC is loss-making (−583x), GFF trades at 99x, and BCC/ROCK sit at 18-24x. The cleaner reference is WOR's own quality: a sub-1x-leverage, 100%+ FCF-conversion compounder with ~17% consumer-products margins and a JV cash-flow annuity arguably deserves a mid-teens forward multiple, which is roughly where it trades.

The discount to peers is largely explained by three factors the market is pricing correctly: (1) earnings opacity from uncontrolled JV equity income; (2) back-to-back misses and negative estimate revisions; and (3) a governance structure (McConnell ~35% stake, 75% supermajority for change of control) that removes takeover-premium optionality. A below-peer multiple is not itself a buy signal, and cheapness without a catalyst has historically warned of a value trap. Here the catalysts (ClarkDietrich recovery, data-center scale-up) are real but unproven, and estimate momentum is against the stock.

Analyst targets bracket $50 (low) to $76 (high) with a $65.6 mean / $67 median. Because estimate revisions are falling, the target range is a plausible upside reference rather than a stale floor, and the negative revision trend argues those targets are more likely to drift down than up in the near term. On balance, the stock is fairly valued: modest upside to consensus, offset by the risk that FY2027 estimates keep falling if the "temporary" framing does not hold. Valuation scores 5.2.

## Competitive Landscape

WOR is deliberately consolidating a building-envelope portfolio, and the June 2026 board addition of Brad Southern (former Chair/CEO of Louisiana-Pacific) reinforces the strategic emphasis on residential building products and R&R. The wholly-owned segment's margin expansion suggests genuine operating improvement, and the consumer franchise (BernzOmatic, Balloon Time, outdoor living) holds durable shelf positions with mass merchandisers, including a new Balloon Time Mini placement in a majority of Walmart stores.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. WAVE is a strong, well-run ceilings JV; ClarkDietrich's weakness is sector-wide non-resi softness rather than share loss. Management argues its procurement scale is a share-gain lever in tight steel markets, which is plausible but unproven as a durable moat. Data-center liquid cooling positions WOR in a structurally growing adjacency, but it is small and early. The competitive-position score of 6.0 steps back modestly from the prior read (6.8), not on any deterioration, but because a more conservative anchor is warranted absent hard share-gain evidence.

## Risk Assessment

1. **JV earnings dependence and lack of control** — roughly $140M of segment EBITDA runs through WAVE and ClarkDietrich, which WOR does not control. ClarkDietrich's decline single-handedly dragged FY2026, tied to a non-resi market WOR cannot influence.
2. **Customer concentration** — one Consumer Products customer is ~12% of consolidated sales; de-shelving would be material.
3. **Commodity input volatility** — steel, aluminum, brass, freight, and diesel pressured FY2026 margins.
4. **Tariff/trade uncertainty** — Section 232 aluminum tariffs affect cylinder costs; a Feb 2026 IEEPA legal development created unresolved refund uncertainty.
5. **Acquisition integration and goodwill risk** — rapid M&A has built ~$499M goodwill and ~$327M intangibles; integration and impairment risk are elevated.
6. **Governance concentration** — McConnell's ~35% stake plus a 75% supermajority for change of control caps minority-shareholder control and takeover-premium optionality.
7. **End-market cyclicality** — both consumer discretionary (celebrations, outdoor living) and construction exposure, with non-resi weakness already visible.

Most of these are known and partially priced, but the JV opacity, back-to-back misses, and tariff uncertainty are live, unresolved items that could move the stock. Risk factors score 5.3.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.0% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical volatility (30.8%), so options are priced fairly with no elevated stress. The chain shows a balanced put/call skew (0.6%), a low put/call volume ratio (0.06), and a normal contango term structure (near-term 31.0% < long-term 35.9%). IV has fallen sharply since the June 22 pre-earnings snapshot (42.8% → 31.0%), consistent with post-event vol crush after the print. Short interest is low at 3.2% of float (2.6 days to cover). The options market is not signaling a directional catalyst or defensive positioning; it corroborates a fair-value, wait-and-see posture rather than either an imminent breakdown or a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$70-76):** ClarkDietrich recovers from its trough as non-resi construction normalizes, adding double-digit millions of equity income; wholly-owned building products continues 200bps+ annual margin expansion; the A2L comp rolls off by Q2 FY2027 and the refrigerant service annuity grows; data-center liquid-cooling tanks scale into a multi-year platform; and $170M+ of annual FCF funds accretive tuck-in M&A and buybacks. On a re-rating toward the low-20s forward multiple on a recovering ~$4.13 next-year EPS, the stock approaches the analyst high.

**Bear case (~$47-50):** The two-quarter miss streak extends into a third; ClarkDietrich stays depressed as non-resi weakness persists; tariff and commodity inflation keep gross margins compressed; estimate revisions continue falling; and integration of the M&A stack disappoints, raising goodwill-impairment risk. At ~13x a cut ~$3.60 EPS, the stock revisits its 52-week low of $45.01.

**Base case (~$60):** ClarkDietrich stabilizes but recovery is gradual; wholly-owned margins expand modestly; data-center revenue grows off a small base; FCF stays strong; and the stock re-rates only modestly as estimates find a floor. ~16x forward on ~$3.75 EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY2027 print (roughly late September 2026), which will test whether the A2L comp is moderating and whether ClarkDietrich has bottomed, followed by the Q2 FY2027 print where management guided the comp headwind to ease.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ClarkDietrich equity income inflects higher (a return toward $30M+ annualized run-rate) or non-resi construction shows a durable recovery.
- Estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive periods, reversing the current −4% to −7% trend.
- Data-center liquid-cooling revenue scales materially beyond the FY2026 base with quantified pipeline, or the stock pulls back into the high-$40s on unchanged fundamentals (improving risk/reward).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive quarterly miss with evidence the weakness is demand-quality rather than timing.
- Goodwill or intangible impairment on the recent acquisition stack.
- Gross margin compression accelerating beyond the LSI purchase-accounting and mix effects, signaling structural pricing loss.

## Conclusion

Worthington Enterprises is a well-financed, cash-generative building and consumer products compounder whose reported financials are widely misread because of the 2023 steel spin-off. The underlying business is improving across organic growth, wholly-owned margin expansion, FCF conversion, and leverage, and the strategic direction (fund wholly-owned building envelope M&A with JV cash flow) is coherent. But the investment case is held back by two consecutive misses, falling estimates, a large slug of uncontrolled JV earnings, and a governance structure that removes takeover optionality. After the 10% post-earnings decline, the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap.

We rate WOR a **HOLD (growth-hold)**. The 12-month growth thesis is real but unproven: it needs ClarkDietrich recovery, continued wholly-owned margin gains, and evidence that the data-center and A2L platforms scale. Own it for the cash-flow-funded compounding and the optionality, but wait for confirmation of the FY2027 recovery, or a better entry in the high-$40s, before treating it as a BUY.
