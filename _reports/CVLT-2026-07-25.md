**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commvault is a data-protection software vendor that has repositioned around cyber-resilience (ransomware and cyber-recovery, immutability, air-gap, and clean-room recovery) delivered through its unified Commvault Cloud platform across on-premise, hybrid, and SaaS environments. The company completed its transition from perpetual licenses to subscription, generating $1.18B in FY2026 revenue (+19% YoY) with a 20% free-cash-flow margin ($237M FCF). The investment case does not turn on solvency (screening ratios that flag "distress" are artifacts of buyback-depleted equity) but on growth quality.

The central controversy surfaced on January 27, 2026, when Q3 FY2026 results showed SaaS ARR growth decelerating from 71% to 40% YoY, a net-new-ARR miss, and a full-year guidance cut, triggering a ~31% single-day crash. The FY2026 10-K confirmed the trend: full-year SaaS ARR growth fell from 68% (FY25) to 42% (FY26), and SaaS net revenue retention slipped from 127% to 122%. A securities class action (class period April 29, 2025 – January 26, 2026) alleges management masked deteriorating ARR quality with heavy discounting and low-priced SaaS deals. The stock bottomed near $75 in late March 2026 and has since recovered to ~$142, helped by a June 2026 multiyear Microsoft partnership to deliver Commvault's cyber-resilience as a native ISV service on Azure.

At the current price the market is pricing a substantial recovery already. CVLT trades at roughly 27x forward EPS ($5.22 current-year consensus), a premium to the systems-software peer median of ~20x, while 30-day estimate revisions have rolled over slightly negative even as the 90-day trend remains positive. The prior report trajectory (AVOID → HOLD → HOLD, scores 4.7 → 6.1 → 5.7) reflects a business whose balance-sheet fears were overblown but whose growth durability remains unresolved. We maintain a HOLD. The next two quarterly ARR prints will resolve whether the SaaS deceleration is base-maturation (natural) or competitive price erosion (structural).

## Company Overview

Commvault sells cyber-resilience and data-protection software through three tiers: Operational Recovery (core backup/restore), Autonomous Recovery (DR orchestration/automation), and Cyber Recovery (threat detection, immutability, air-gap, clean-room recovery). Add-ons include Cleanroom Recovery, Threatscan, Air Gap Protect, Cloud Rewind, and Clumio Backtrack.

The FY2026 revenue mix reflects a completed subscription transition: term-based license $435M (37%), SaaS $333M (28%), customer support $320M (27%), perpetual $43M and declining (4%), and services $52M. Distribution is ~90% indirect (resellers, distributors, OEMs, cloud marketplaces) and ~10% direct, with heavy partner concentration: Partner A at 32% of revenue (down from 36% in FY24) and Partner B newly crossing 11%. The company runs ~3,300 employees (64% international, ~40% in India), holds >1,600 patents, and reports as a single segment; 47% of revenue is international.

Commvault competes primarily against Rubrik, Cohesity (post-Veritas merger), and Veeam. Several of these are cloud-native, better-funded, or (Rubrik) recently IPO'd with a security-led growth narrative. Commvault's differentiation rests on platform breadth and a unified data-management console, alongside the switching costs that favor incumbents in backup/recovery.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew from $784.6M (FY23) to $839.2M (FY24, +7%), $995.6M (FY25, +19%), and $1,183.7M (FY26, +19%). SaaS is the growth engine: FY26 SaaS revenue rose 52% to $333M, term-based license +18% to $435M, perpetual −22%. FY26 GAAP operating income was $74.0M (6.3% margin) and net income $70.7M (6.0% margin), compressed by ~$120M/yr of stock-based comp and $32.2M of FY26 restructuring. FY24 net income of $169M was inflated by an $85M valuation-allowance release, which is non-operational.

**Cash flow.** FY26 operating cash flow was $244.7M and free cash flow $237M (20% FCF margin), materially above GAAP net income and indicating high-quality earnings. Overall gross margin is ~81%; SaaS gross margin holds steady near ~64% as hosting/infrastructure cost scales with SaaS growth. The four-quarter EPS record (3 beats, 1 miss; +15.6%, +17.0%, −5.1%, +2.2%) shows a company that generally clears the bar but stumbled once, in the September 2025 quarter.

**Balance sheet.** The screening Altman Z-Score of 1.16 ("distress") and D/E of ~252x are artifacts, not distress signals. Stockholders' equity is a razor-thin $7.5M because years of aggressive buybacks drove cumulative retained earnings to −$1.47B, distorting equity-denominated ratios. Actual liquidity is strong: ~$900M cash, current ratio 1.95, and the only debt is $900M of 0% convertible senior notes due 2030 (issued September 2025). At a 0% coupon, cash interest cost is negligible, and the notes traded below par (~$794M fair value at 12/31/25). Piotroski F-Score is 5/5 on available signals. The genuine balance-sheet consideration is the 2030 maturity: $900M must be repaid in cash (or converted at ~$236.88), a future refinancing/dilution decision, not a near-term solvency risk.

**Capital allocation.** Commvault repurchased $446M of stock in FY26 (4.2M shares at avg $106.91), of which $117.7M was tied to the convert issuance. The convert essentially financed buybacks and capped calls, using near-free money to shrink the share count while trading away debt-free status. It also monetized its NJ headquarters via sale-leaseback (~$36M) in April 2025. Buybacks at ~$107 average look reasonable versus the current $142 and the March low near $75; the board continues to recommit $250M tranches.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth remains solid: revenue +19%, total ARR +21%. The problem is beneath the surface. SaaS ARR growth halved from 68% (FY25) to 42% (FY26), and SaaS NRR eroded from 127% to 122%. The securities suit and January guidance cut both center on the allegation that management won SaaS ARR through heavy discounting and cheap deals while describing "hyper-growth." The core diligence question is whether this deceleration reflects a maturing base (natural, and consistent with a still-healthy 122% NRR) or competitive price erosion that structurally lowers the growth ceiling.

Estimate revisions cut both ways. Over 90 days, consensus is up meaningfully: current-year EPS +8.0% to $5.22, next-year +6.4% to $6.02, current-quarter +6.1%, reflecting the post-crash recovery and Azure catalyst. But the 30-day trend has rolled over slightly negative across every window (current-year −0.5%, next-year −1.1%, next-quarter −0.7%). This is not the >30% upward revision velocity that would signal a clean inflection buy; it is a stabilizing-to-softening profile after a sharp reset. The Microsoft Azure native-ISV partnership (June 2026) is a distribution positive that could re-accelerate SaaS if it drives marketplace co-sell volume, but it is early and unproven in the numbers.

Cyber-resilience remains a structural demand tailwind: ransomware, regulatory recovery mandates, and immutability requirements are durable drivers. The question is Commvault's share of that spend against Rubrik and Cohesity, not whether the category grows.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$142.31, CVLT trades at roughly 27x current-year consensus EPS ($5.22) and ~24x next-year ($6.02). Against the systems-software peer median P/E of ~19.9 (PATH 17.6, QLYS 19.9, PRGS 16.2, ATEN 53.5, NABL negative), CVLT carries a premium. The trailing P/E of ~89x is not the right lens: trailing EPS of $1.58 is depressed by SBC and restructuring, and the FCF-based valuation is far more favorable. At $237M FCF against a ~$5.9B market cap, the FCF yield is ~4%, reasonable for a software compounder but not cheap.

The cycle-adjustment principles that would justify a high valuation score do not apply cleanly here. This is not a trough-earnings inflection with rapidly rising estimates: the 30-day revision trend is flat-to-negative, and the growth engine (SaaS) is decelerating, not accelerating. On growth-adjusted terms, ~27x forward for a business growing revenue ~19% with SaaS ARR growth cut in half is fair, not asymmetric. Analyst targets (low $100, mean ~$159, median ~$166, high $200) bracket the current price with modest upside to the mean; on a decelerating-growth name with an unresolved ARR-quality question, I would not treat the mean as a floor. The valuation prices in a recovery that is plausible but not yet confirmed by fundamentals. This warrants a below-average valuation score: the premium multiple is supported by FCF quality but not by an identifiable near-term catalyst for re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The data-protection market is consolidating and shifting to cloud-native/SaaS delivery, where Commvault is a fast-follower rather than a pioneer. Rubrik entered public markets with a security-led narrative and momentum; Cohesity, post-Veritas merger, is now a scaled competitor. Veeam remains a formidable competitor. Commvault's advantages are platform breadth, a large installed base with high switching costs, and its patent portfolio.

The competitive-position trajectory is the concern. The class-action allegations are consistent with real pricing pressure compressing growth quality even as headline revenue holds. A 122% SaaS NRR is still healthy but the direction (down from 127%) matters more than the level. The Microsoft Azure ISV partnership could deepen a durable hyperscaler channel, though it increases dependence on a partner that supplies infrastructure, co-sells, and could bundle competing data-protection. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding: incumbent strengths intact, but the growth-share battle against better-capitalized cloud-native rivals is going the wrong way at the margin.

## Risk Assessment

1. **ARR growth quality (the central risk).** The SaaS ARR deceleration and NRR erosion are the subject of active litigation. If they prove structural, the stock re-rates lower.
2. **Securities class action.** Class period April 29, 2025 – January 26, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline was July 17, 2026. Litigation cost and reputational overhang, with headline risk on future disclosures.
3. **Partner/customer concentration.** Partner A (32% of revenue, ~25% of receivables) plus Partner B (11%) = ~43% of revenue through non-exclusive, terminable agreements with partners that also sell competing products.
4. **Microsoft Azure triple dependence.** Azure hosts key SaaS offerings (supplier), co-sells (partner), and could compete (bundled data-protection). Any pricing, disruption, or competitive move is a direct risk.
5. **Convertible overhang.** $900M principal due 2030, conversion price ~$236.88 (capped-call cap ~$357.56). Refinancing/dilution decision ahead; the notes may also encourage short-selling.
6. **Finance-leadership churn at a bad time.** CFO seat rotated back to Gary Merrill in April 2026 (his second stint); the Q3 10-Q of the miss quarter was signed by the CAO as interim PFO. Combined with a new field-ops president and two FY26 restructurings (~$32M), this signals organizational turbulence during the growth stumble.

Many of these risks are now at least partially priced after the January crash and partial recovery; the balance-sheet "distress" fear was a false positive, which is why risk scoring has improved from 3.0 to 5.0 over prior reports. But the ARR-quality and concentration risks remain live and not fully resolved, capping the risk score at a neutral 5.0.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$185–200).** The SaaS deceleration is base-maturation, not structural erosion; NRR stabilizes near 120%+ and re-accelerates as the Microsoft Azure native-ISV channel drives marketplace co-sell volume. Cyber-resilience demand stays a durable tailwind, term-license and support revenue keep compounding mid-teens, and the 20% FCF margin plus aggressive buybacks (share count shrinking against a $900M convert at 0%) drive per-share value. Estimates resume rising, the multiple re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range, and the securities suit settles without material financial impact. This maps to the analyst high target of $200.

**Bear Case (~$95–105).** The SaaS deceleration proves structural: discounting masked weak underlying demand, NRR keeps sliding below 120%, and Rubrik/Cohesity take share in the cloud-native segment. Partner A concentration bites or Microsoft turns competitive. The securities suit produces a costly settlement, finance-leadership churn signals deeper operational issues, and estimate revisions turn decisively negative. The multiple compresses toward peer-median ~20x on lower forward EPS, mapping to the analyst low target of $100 and roughly 30% downside from current levels.

**Base Case (~$150).** SaaS growth stabilizes in the high-30s/low-40s, NRR holds near 120%, the Azure partnership contributes modestly, and FCF supports continued buybacks. The stock tracks earnings growth with a stable ~25x forward multiple, delivering mid-single-digit upside: approximately 25x on next-year $6.02 EPS = ~$150.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The balance-sheet fears that drove the earlier AVOID have been correctly reappraised, and the business generates genuine cash. But at ~27x forward on decelerating SaaS growth, with 30-day revisions rolling over and the ARR-quality question unresolved, neither the upside nor the downside is compelling enough for a directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SaaS ARR growth stabilizes or re-accelerates in the next two prints (a sequential reversal of the 68% → 42% trend), with NRR holding at or above 120%.
- 30-day estimate revisions turn positive again alongside evidence the Azure ISV partnership is contributing measurable marketplace/co-sell ARR.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s that restores meaningful upside to the analyst mean while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SaaS NRR falls below 118% or SaaS ARR growth decelerates below ~30% in the next print, confirming structural price erosion.
- Loss or material renegotiation of Partner A (32% of revenue), or a competitive move by Microsoft.
- A costly securities-suit development or renewed finance-leadership disruption, combined with negative estimate-revision velocity.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY2027 earnings print (consensus EPS ~$1.16, revenue ~$311M), where updated SaaS ARR and NRR metrics will be the decisive data.

## Conclusion

Commvault is a financially sound, cash-generative cyber-resilience software company whose solvency screening flags are false positives created by buyback-depleted equity. The genuine debate is growth quality: a January 2026 ARR miss, SaaS deceleration, eroding NRR, a securities class action, and finance-leadership churn, partially offset by a strong liquidity position, a 20% FCF margin, disciplined buybacks below current prices, and a new native-Azure Microsoft ISV partnership that has helped the stock recover from ~$75 to ~$142.

At ~27x forward EPS the market has already priced a meaningful recovery. The 90-day estimate revisions are positive but the 30-day trend has stalled, the SaaS growth engine is decelerating rather than inflecting, and the ARR-quality question is unresolved pending the next one to two prints. The information-technology sector is the one durable area where security-led selection has added alpha, which argues against a reflexive negative, but that edge requires a clear company-specific catalyst that CVLT does not yet offer. We maintain **HOLD**, with the next two ARR prints as the swing factor between a re-rating toward the analyst mean and a slide back toward peer-median multiples.
