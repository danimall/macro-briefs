## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy trades at $35.29, up 46% over the past year, driven primarily by the February 2026 announcement of its all-stock merger with Devon Energy (0.70 DVN shares per CTRA share). At current prices, the stock trades near its implied deal value — Devon at ~$50 implies ~$35 for CTRA — leaving minimal merger arbitrage spread for the regulatory approval, shareholder vote, and commodity price risks inherent in an all-stock transaction expected to close in Q2 2026.

The underlying business has improved materially: FY2025 revenue grew 34% to $7.3B, operating cash flow reached $4.0B, and the FME/Avant Delaware Basin acquisitions ($4.0B total) added meaningful oil production. Analyst estimates have surged, with current-year EPS revised up 24% in the past 30 days to $2.96. However, Coterra missed earnings estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, the acquired properties carry higher per-unit costs, and the company drew its cash balance from $2.0B to $98M to fund acquisitions. The pending merger effectively caps standalone upside while introducing deal-specific risks: antitrust scrutiny on a $58B combination creating the 2nd largest U.S. independent E&P, activist pressure from Kimmeridge, and full exposure to Devon's equity price in an all-stock structure.

The investment thesis is binary: either the Devon deal closes (CTRA tracks DVN at 0.70x) or it breaks (standalone CTRA reprices to fundamentals, likely $28-32 range given commodity exposure). With the deal priced in, the risk/reward does not support an incremental BUY. A HOLD reflects that the most likely path is deal closure at roughly current value, but the absence of meaningful spread and multiple identifiable risks prevent higher conviction.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy is a diversified U.S. E&P operating across three shale basins: the Delaware Basin (Permian), Marcellus Shale, and Anadarko Basin. Formed through the 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy, Coterra is unusual among peers for its balanced oil/gas production mix — approximately 154.6 MBbl/d oil, 2,978 MMcf/d gas, and 121.1 MBbl/d NGLs as of 9M 2025, totaling 772 MBoe/d.

In January 2025, Coterra closed two Delaware Basin acquisitions (FME for $2.5B and Avant for $1.5B), significantly expanding its Permian footprint. Thirteen months later, on February 2, 2026, Devon Energy and Coterra announced a definitive all-stock merger at a 0.70 exchange ratio, creating a combined entity with 1.6M+ Boe/d of production and 750,000+ net Delaware Basin acres. The deal is expected to close in Q2 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** FY2025 revenue of $7.29B represents a 34% recovery from the $5.46B trough in FY2024, though it remains well below the $9.51B peak in FY2022. Operating income recovered to $2.45B (33.6% margin) from $1.39B in 2024. Net income of $1.72B translates to approximately $2.24 EPS on ~759M diluted shares.

The FME/Avant acquisitions contributed $783M in revenue and $319M in net income through 9 months, but at a cost: direct operations expense per BOE increased 33% ($2.60 → $3.45), and all-in operating costs rose 11% to $18.09/BOE. This dilution in per-unit economics is partially offset by absolute production growth (oil volumes +44% YoY) but raises the company's breakeven price in a downturn.

**Balance Sheet:** The acquisition spree consumed most of the balance sheet cushion. Cash declined from $2.04B to $98M; total debt increased modestly to $3.92B. Debt-to-total-capital of 21% and investment-grade ratings provide comfort, but the current ratio compressed from 2.9x to 1.0x. A $2.0B undrawn revolving credit facility provides adequate liquidity, and $250M in notes due September 2026 should be manageable through either facility draws or the Devon merger closing first.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $4.0B in FY2025 comfortably funded $2.3B in capex, producing approximately $1.7B in free cash flow. The capital allocation priority shifted dramatically: share repurchases fell from $404M (9M 2024) to just $47M (9M 2025) as capital was directed toward acquisitions and debt reduction. The quarterly dividend was raised modestly to $0.22/share ($0.88 annualized, ~2.5% yield).

## Growth Analysis

Coterra's near-term growth trajectory is strong on an absolute basis. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up 51% over 90 days (from $0.62 to $0.93), and full-year 2026 estimates rose 33% to $2.96. This reflects both the contribution from acquired assets and improving natural gas realizations — Coterra's ~3.0 Bcf/d of gas production provides substantial leverage to rising gas prices, with realized gas prices up 50% YoY in Q3 2025.

However, several factors temper conviction in the growth trajectory:

**Earnings delivery has been poor.** Coterra missed estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, including a significant -20.6% miss in Q4 2025 ($0.39 actual vs. $0.49 estimate). This pattern undermines confidence that the aggressive upward revisions will be met.

**Growth beyond 2026 is flat.** Next-year (FY2027) EPS consensus is $2.96 — identical to FY2026, implying zero earnings growth. The 90-day revision for FY2027 is only +3.4%, suggesting analysts see the current commodity price environment as approximately peak for this production base.

**The merger subsumes standalone growth.** If the Devon deal closes in Q2 2026, Coterra's standalone operational trajectory becomes academic. Combined entity growth will depend on Devon's capital allocation framework, the ~$1B in projected synergies, and commodity price outcomes.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.29, CTRA trades at 16.0x trailing EPS ($2.24) and approximately 11.9x the FY2026 consensus of $2.96. The peer median P/E is 11.2x, so CTRA trades at a ~7% premium on forward earnings, worth flagging given the 3-of-4 miss record.

The analyst consensus target is $37.43 (median $37.00), implying only 6% upside from current levels. The target range of $28-$46 is wide, reflecting uncertainty around the merger and commodity prices. The stock sits 4% below its 52-week high of $36.88.

**Merger math provides the valuation anchor.** At the 0.70 exchange ratio, CTRA's value is mechanically tied to Devon's share price. DVN at $50 implies CTRA at $35; DVN at $55 implies $38.50; DVN at $45 implies $31.50. The current CTRA price of $35.29 implies the market is pricing DVN at approximately $50.41, which is where Devon actually trades. The merger spread is effectively zero, meaning the market assigns near-100% closure probability.

On standalone fundamentals (ex-merger), CTRA at 11.9x forward earnings with no EPS growth into FY2027, a 2.5% dividend yield, and investment-grade leverage is reasonably but not compellingly valued versus the 11.2x peer median. The Marcellus gas asset and basin diversification justify a modest premium, but the higher cost structure from recent acquisitions partially offsets this.

The prior score trajectory showed valuation improving from 6.5 to 7.0, which reflected the stock's pullback at the time. The subsequent 46% rally has largely closed that gap. At 16x trailing and approximately deal-value parity, the asymmetry that justified a 7.0 valuation score no longer exists.

## Competitive Landscape

Coterra occupies a strong competitive position within U.S. E&P, distinguished by its dual oil/gas production profile. The Marcellus Shale asset (~3.0 Bcf/d) provides leverage to LNG export demand growth that purely oil-weighted Permian peers lack, while the expanded Delaware Basin position (post-FME/Avant) gives meaningful oil production scale.

The company's $5.7B in long-term fixed-price natural gas contracts (over 13 years) provides unusual cash flow visibility for an E&P. Waha Basin negative pricing in 2025 highlighted both the risk and the value of basis hedging — Coterra's $36M in Waha basis swaps (locked at -$1.86 to -$2.05/MMBtu) provided partial insulation from the worst of Permian gas takeaway constraints.

The pending Devon merger would create the 2nd largest U.S. independent E&P (behind ConocoPhillips) with 750,000+ net Delaware Basin acres and 1.6M+ Boe/d of combined production. Scale advantages in procurement, midstream access, and operational efficiency should provide competitive benefits, though the Kimmeridge activist campaign suggests market skepticism about whether Devon's management will maximize these advantages.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger Execution (High Impact, Moderate Probability):** Regulatory review of the $58B combination creating the largest Delaware Basin operator could draw antitrust scrutiny. Shareholder votes are required from both sides, and initial market reaction was negative. Kimmeridge's activist campaign adds governance uncertainty. A deal break would likely send CTRA to $28-32, representing 11-21% downside.

**2. Commodity Price Exposure Through DVN (High Impact, Moderate Probability):** As an all-stock deal, CTRA's realized value fluctuates 1:1 with Devon's share price. Devon's exposure is more oil-weighted than standalone Coterra. In a scenario where oil prices decline significantly, Devon at $40-42 would imply CTRA at $28-29.

**3. Earnings Execution Risk (Moderate Impact, High Probability):** The miss record is concerning given aggressively rising estimates (+24% in 30 days). If Q1 2026 results disappoint again (report expected near-term), the stock could pull back 5-10% despite the merger backstop.

**4. EPA Enforcement Action (Low-Moderate Impact, Moderate Probability):** The DOJ-referred Clean Air Act enforcement action (ongoing since mid-2023) represents a tail risk. While management considers it immaterial, environmental penalties could complicate merger approval timelines or result in meaningful remediation costs.

**5. Acquired Property Cost Inflation (Moderate Impact, High Probability):** The FME/Avant properties' 33% higher direct operating costs per BOE are already materializing. Without merger synergies, this would compress standalone margins over time.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-42):** Devon's stock appreciates toward $55-60 on sustained elevated oil prices and $1B in merger synergies, pulling CTRA mechanically to $38.50-42.00 through the exchange ratio. The combined entity benefits from massive Delaware Basin scale, Marcellus gas leverage to LNG demand, and improved capital efficiency. Post-merger, the announced $5B buyback and 31% dividend increase support shareholder returns. Upside: 8-19%.

**Bear Case ($28-31):** The merger faces regulatory delays or conditions that reduce value. Alternatively, oil prices decline significantly, compressing Devon to $40-44 and implying CTRA at $28-31. On a standalone basis (deal break), CTRA's 11.9x forward multiple with zero FY2027 growth, the earnings miss record, and depleted cash balance would likely reprice toward $28-30. Downside: 12-21%.

**Base Case ($35-37):** Deal closes in Q2 2026 at approximately current implied value. Devon trades $49-53, implying CTRA at $34.30-37.10. Limited spread capture from current levels. The base case delivers the current dividend (2.5% annualized) plus modest price appreciation of 0-5%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Meaningful merger spread opens — CTRA trades at >5% discount to implied deal value (0.70 × DVN), creating an arbitrage opportunity with identified catalyst (shareholder vote date, regulatory clearance).
2. Deal breaks and CTRA reprices to $28-30 on standalone fundamentals, creating a value entry at ~9.5-10x forward earnings with the Marcellus gas asset undervalued and strong FCF generation.
3. Devon stock appreciates >10% on operational execution while CTRA lags (exchange ratio mispricing).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Regulatory challenge materializes — DOJ or FTC initiates second request or files suit, extending timeline beyond Q3 2026 and creating prolonged uncertainty.
2. Q1 2026 earnings miss by >15% combined with estimate revisions turning negative, suggesting the underlying business is deteriorating.
3. Oil prices fall below $55 WTI, compressing Devon's valuation and mechanically dragging CTRA toward $28-31.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (expected late April/early May 2026) and merger shareholder vote (expected Q2 2026) are the two critical windows. The merger vote date, once announced, will provide a definitive timeline for thesis resolution.

## Conclusion

Coterra Energy is a diversified E&P with an investment-grade balance sheet, strong FCF generation, and positive estimate momentum. At $35.29, the stock is trading at its merger-implied value with near-zero spread, meaning investors receive minimal compensation for real residual risks: regulatory review, all-stock price exposure, and a concerning earnings miss pattern. The prior BUY recommendation (at lower prices, when spread existed) was well-timed, but the 46% rally has compressed the upside/risk ratio.

The absence of merger spread eliminates the asymmetric return profile that would justify incremental capital commitment. If the deal closes, investors earn ~2.5% annualized dividend plus 0-5% price appreciation over 2-3 months — a low-single-digit return for carrying merger break risk (11-21% downside). If the deal breaks, standalone CTRA at 11.9x with zero FY2027 growth and a depleted cash position does not offer a margin of safety at current levels. The score reflects quality fundamentals and positive momentum, but the risk/reward at this price is symmetric at best. HOLD until either a meaningful spread opens or the deal resolves.
