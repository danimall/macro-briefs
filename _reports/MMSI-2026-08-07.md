## Executive Summary

Merit Medical delivered the print the bulls needed. Q2 2026 revenue of $418.8M grew 9.5% reported and 9.0% on an organic constant-currency basis, against +3.7% organic constant-currency in Q1 — a 530 basis point sequential swing. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.19 beat consensus of roughly $0.96 by approximately 24%, the fourth consecutive beat and the largest of the four. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $1.631–1.643B from $1.612–1.634B and non-GAAP EPS to $4.25–4.35 from $4.01–4.15. Consensus followed: current-year EPS estimates rose 5.7% over 90 days to $4.30, next-year 4.7% to $4.61. The stock has moved from $60.44 on May 6 to $87.69, recovering the full drawdown that followed the weak Q1 report and now sitting 7.4% below its 52-week high of $94.75.

Two findings temper the enthusiasm. First, the Q2 10-Q discloses approximately $6.9M of IEEPA tariff refunds recognized in cost of sales, equal to 1.65% of quarterly revenue, and this benefit is **not** excluded from Merit's non-GAAP measures. Stripping it out, Q2 non-GAAP operating margin was roughly 20.9% versus 21.2% in Q2 2025 — down about 27 basis points rather than the reported +140 — and H1 was approximately 20.4% versus 20.2%, up about 16 basis points. Against a 2021–2025 track record of roughly 148 basis points of annual non-GAAP operating margin expansion, the core margin engine is currently close to stalled. Second, the guidance raise looks like a pass-through of the quarter itself: the non-GAAP EPS midpoint rose $0.22 while the Q2 beat was about $0.23, and implied H2 revenue growth of +6.7% to +8.3% is below the +8.5% delivered in H1, despite the View Point acquisition contributing revenue for the first time and the C2 CryoBalloon assets annualizing.

The result is a good business at an unexceptional price. At $87.69 Merit trades at 20.4x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $4.30 and 19.0x the FY2027 consensus of $4.61, which implies a PEG of roughly 2.6 on next-year consensus earnings growth of 7.2%. Balance sheet quality is high — net debt/EBITDA of 0.97x, $448.7M of cash, a single 3.00% convertible maturing February 2029, and an undrawn revolver — and the therapeutic portfolio (cardiac therapies +25%, endoscopy +30% constant currency in H1) is compounding faster than the legacy consumables core. But the base case delivers roughly +11% against a bear case near -20%, and the market demonstrated in May that a single soft organic quarter is worth a 23% de-rating in this name. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the business has improved since the last review while the entry price has improved less than the business, and the score declines slightly on valuation arithmetic rather than on any fundamental deterioration.

## Company Overview

Merit Medical designs and manufactures single-use, proprietary devices for interventional, diagnostic and therapeutic procedures in cardiology, radiology, oncology, critical care and endoscopy. Founded in 1987 in Utah, the company operated eleven manufacturing facilities across roughly 43 countries of operation and employed roughly 7,500 people at year-end 2025, with a direct sales and clinical support organization exceeding 800.

The business is a breadth-of-line disposables platform rather than a single-franchise story. The FY2025 10-K states plainly that "no single patent, trademark or other intellectual property asset is of material importance," despite approximately 2,200 patents and applications. Competitive advantage rests on line breadth, custom procedural kitting to facility specification, vertical manufacturing integration (injection molding, extrusion, embolic bead production, an in-house silicon pressure sensor subsidiary), and rapid line extension. Merit self-reports US leadership in control syringes, radar localization, waste-disposal systems, embolic beads, tubing and manifolds; these are company assessments, not third-party share data.

Reporting changed in 2026. The FY2025 10-K disclosed two segments (cardiovascular $1,443.0M, +10.9%; endoscopy $72.9M, +33.0%). From Q1 2026 Merit reports a single segment on the basis that the new CEO manages the business consolidated, with revenue disclosed across two platforms and eleven product lines. Q2 2026: **Foundational $281.0M (+6% cc)** — Access $161.8M (+5%), OEM $48.3M (+12%), Vascular Intervention $41.7M (+18%), Procedural Solutions $27.9M (−12%); **Therapeutic $137.9M (+14% cc)** — Cardiac Therapies $28.5M (+22%), Vascular Intervention $34.4M (+5%), Oncology $25.8M (+7%), Endoscopy $23.6M (+29%), Renal Therapies $12.7M (−2%), OEM $12.8M (+31%). Geographically, the US was 60.2% of Q2 revenue and grew 11%; international was 39.8% and grew 7%. China, served through more than 500 distributors plus Merit clinical promotion staff, is the largest non-US market.

Founder Fred Lampropoulos resigned as CEO effective October 3, 2025 and as board chair effective January 4, 2026, ending approximately 38 years of founder leadership. Martha Aronson is President and CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year record.** Revenue compounded from $1,151.0M (2022) to $1,515.9M (2025), with growth of 9.2%, 7.9% and 11.8%. Operating income roughly doubled over the period, from $87.6M to $184.7M, and operating margin expanded from 7.6% to 12.2%. Non-GAAP operating margin, the company-selected compensation metric, marched 14.4% → 15.5% → 17.2% → 19.0% → 20.3% across 2021–2025.

**The FY2025 acceleration was substantially purchased.** MD&A attributes $35.3M of incremental cardiac intervention revenue to the Cook Medical lead-management assets (November 2024) and $11.9M to Biolife (May 2025); endoscopy's +33% was driven by EsophyX Z+ from EndoGastric Solutions (July 2024). Identified acquisition contribution of roughly $47M equals about 3.5 points of the 11.8% reported growth before EndoGastric carryover, implying high-single-digit organic constant-currency growth. Regional dispersion was wide: EMEA +15.3%, Rest of World +12.2%, Asia-Pacific only +2.3% — consistent with the China volume-based procurement pricing pressure the filings describe at length without quantifying.

**GAAP net margin fell in 2025 even as operating margin rose**, from 8.87% to 8.48%. Net income grew 6.8% against operating income growth of 18.6% because the effective tax rate moved 15.8% (2023) → 19.8% (2024) → 24.8% (2025) → 23.9% (H1 2026), and other expense worsened to $13.8M from $5.7M. SG&A also deleveraged 56 basis points to 30.03% of sales, consuming most of the 130 basis point gross margin gain. The tax normalization is a persistent EPS headwind that does not appear in the non-GAAP operating margin on which management is measured.

**Cash flow: strong 2025, softer H1 2026.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $297.4M on $128.5M of net income (2.3x conversion, helped by $123.2M of D&A and $43.5M of stock compensation), capex $81.7M, free cash flow $215.7M — 14.2% of sales and approximately 93% of implied FY2025 non-GAAP net income, a useful confirmation that the non-GAAP earnings are cash-backed at the annual level. H1 2026 reversed: operating cash flow $110.0M (−11%) while net income rose 27%; FCF $76.6M versus $89.1M (−14%). Working capital consumed $50.8M versus $29.0M, of which inventories absorbed $43.6M versus $11.7M. Inventories rose 12.1% to $374.1M against H1 revenue growth of 8.5%; days inventory on hand moved from roughly 157 to roughly 170. Receivables days were flat near 49, so the issue is inventory specifically. The most plausible reading is a deliberate buffer against tariff and supply-chain uncertainty plus launch support (OneMark, C2 CryoBalloon, Wrapsody); the alternative — stock accumulating in the declining lines, with Procedural Solutions −10% and Renal Therapies −9% constant currency in H1 — cannot be excluded, though obsolescence expense has been stable at $11.2M/$10.6M/$11.5M across 2025/2024/2023.

Do not annualize H1 FCF. FY2025 was H2-weighted, with H1 FCF of $89.1M representing 41.3% of the full year. Applying the same split as a sensitivity gives FY2026 FCF near $185M, a 3.5% yield on the current market cap; a naive doubling of H1 gives $153M (2.9%). Both ignore H2 View Point operating costs and 2026 capex guidance of $80–100M.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $448.7M ($65.5M offshore, $19.7M in China with transfer restrictions), current assets $1,112.2M against current liabilities $264.9M (current ratio 4.20x), total assets $2,863.6M, equity $1,679.8M. Debt is a single instrument: $747.5M of 3.00% convertible senior notes due February 1, 2029, carried at $736.3M. The credit agreement provides a $150M term facility plus up to a $700M revolver maturing June 6, 2028, undrawn with roughly $697M available. Covenants (total net leverage ≤5.0x, senior secured ≤3.0x, interest coverage ≥3.0x) leave very large headroom: on an FY2025 EBITDA proxy of $307.9M (operating income $184.7M + D&A $123.2M), gross leverage is 2.43x and **net leverage 0.97x**, with interest coverage of 7.0x and a cash coupon of only $22.4M annually against $7.65M of interest income earned in H1 2026 alone.

Two qualifications. First, goodwill of $539.8M plus intangibles of $612.0M equals $1,151.8M, or 40.2% of total assets and 68.6% of equity; tangible book value is roughly $528M. That is why ROE of 8.1% and ROA of 4.8% look pedestrian against 12.2% operating margins — a decade of purchased growth sits capitalized on the balance sheet, and it carries impairment risk if any acquired line disappoints. Second, the revolver matures June 2028, roughly eight months before the notes, so it cannot backstop the notes without being extended first. With $448.7M of cash and ~$185M of estimated annual FCF, this is a sequencing item rather than a solvency question.

**Convertible dilution, stated precisely.** The conversion rate of 11.5171 shares per $1,000 implies a conversion price of $86.83; the stock at $87.69 sits about 1.0% above it. Maximum contingent shares are 8,609,032, or 14.4% of the 59.71M outstanding — that is a maximum, not current diluted capitalization. At $87.69, aggregate conversion value of $754.9M exceeds $747.5M principal by $7.4M, which if share-settled is roughly 84,000 shares (about 0.14% of the count, and 0.14% of ownership transferred). Diluted EPS for Q2 2026 included zero shares from the notes because the average price was below the strike. At the $114.68 capped-call cap price, excess value would be about $239.8M, or roughly 2.09M shares (3.5% of the count) — an exposure the capped calls, purchased for $66.5M, are designed to offset. Merit's guidance embeds about $0.03 of if-converted dilution and ignores the capped-call offset, a conservative presentation. Merit may call the notes on or after February 7, 2027 if the stock holds ≥$112.88 for 20 of 30 trading days.

## Growth Analysis

**The 2026 intra-year swing is the central fact.** Using Merit's own constant-currency reconciliation dollars, organic constant-currency growth was +3.7% in Q1 ($363.3M versus $350.4M) and +9.0% in Q2 ($411.2M versus $377.2M). For a consumables business whose only meaningful seasonality is a softer Q3, a 530 basis point single-quarter swing is unusual. Management framed the Q2 improvement as "as expected" and called US growth of 10–11% "well ahead of our expectations." Two quarters is a small sample, and distributor ordering, the Q1 tariff-policy disruption, and the DualCap transition-services arrangement could each generate noise without signalling a change in underlying demand.

**Guidance quality.** The revenue midpoint rose from $1,623M to $1,637M (+0.86%) while the non-GAAP EPS midpoint rose from $4.08 to $4.30 (+5.4%). The Q2 beat versus non-GAAP consensus was roughly $0.23, so the full-year raise approximately equals the quarter. Implied H2 revenue of $830.3–842.3M against H2 2025 of roughly $778.1M is +6.7% to +8.3%, below the +8.5% H1 delivery, with View Point contributing revenue for the first time and C2 CryoBalloon annualizing. Consensus is aligned: Q3 $1.03 and Q4 $1.14 sum to $2.17 against H1 non-GAAP actuals of $2.13, essentially flat sequentially in aggregate. Roughly $0.087 of the Q2 beat was the after-tax tariff refund, so ex-refund Q2 non-GAAP EPS was about $1.10 — still a ~15% beat — and the $0.22 raise is about 2.5x the tariff benefit, so the raise is not merely a pass-through of that one item.

**The mix is bifurcating.** H1 2026 constant currency: Foundational +5% (Access +7%, Vascular Intervention +17%, OEM +1%, Procedural Solutions −10%) versus Therapeutic +11% (Cardiac Therapies +25%, Endoscopy +30%, Oncology +6%, Vascular Intervention +6%, Renal Therapies −9%, OEM −3%). Every one of the fastest-growing lines is acquisition-anchored: cardiac therapies by the Cook lead-management assets, endoscopy by EsophyX Z+ and C2 CryoBalloon. The legacy consumables core grows mid-single-digits. Sustaining roughly 7% total growth has required approximately $740M of acquisition spend from 2023 through H1 2026 — 2023 $134.5M, 2024 $320.2M, 2025 $144.8M, H1 2026 $93.0M cash plus $47.2M of deferred View Point obligations — against FY2025 FCF of $215.7M.

**Estimate revisions are positive but not inflectional.** Current-year consensus rose 5.7% and next-year 4.7% over 90 days, with the current quarter +1.7%. The one negative line is next quarter, revised −1.0% over both 30 and 90 days, into what is historically the seasonally softest quarter. This is healthy earnings momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that marks a genuine cyclical inflection, and it should be weighted accordingly.

**Identifiable catalysts over twelve months:** the Continued Growth Initiative program expires at the end of 2026, and Aronson has flagged a longer-term strategic plan "focused on identifying opportunities to better position the company for sustainable growth, enhanced profitability and value creation." The articulation of a successor framework — specifically whether it commits to continued annual operating margin expansion and defines organic versus acquired growth — is the clearest catalyst. Secondary: View Point's OneMark revenue starting (the 10-Q states there were no sales of the acquired products in Q2 or H1), and the February 2027 opening of the convertible call window.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit competes as a $5.2B company against Medtronic, Boston Scientific, BD, Abbott and Johnson & Johnson, plus focused specialists including Teleflex, Cook, Hologic, Argon, Olympus, Getinge, Terumo, Edwards and Stryker. The 10-K acknowledges that "many of our competitors are much larger than we are and have access to greater resources," and separately flags domestic manufacturers in China and Japan that benefit from local-supplier status — a dynamic that compounds the volume-based procurement pricing headwind.

The strategic response is coherent: avoid head-on competition in high-capital, high-clinical-evidence categories, aggregate accessory and access consumable positions where breadth of line, custom kitting and facility-level service create switching friction, then bolt on differentiated therapeutic devices via acquisition. The 10-K's own competitive-factor list — quality, price, product features, customer service, breadth of line, customer relationships — contains no clinical-superiority claim, which is an honest description of an accessory-led portfolio.

Trajectory is the relevant question. On the positive side, Therapeutic is compounding at 11% constant currency with cardiac therapies at 25% and endoscopy at 30%, Foundational Vascular Intervention grew 17% (apparently organic), and the CIA with the HHS Office of Inspector General — a five-year condition of the October 2020 DOJ settlement — expired in October 2025 with a formal completion letter received January 23, 2026, removing a compliance overhang and its associated costs. On the negative side, industry pricing dynamics all point one direction: GPO and IDN consolidation extracting concessions, public cost-containment, China VBP, cost-effectiveness-conditioned reimbursement. The four-year offset was manufacturing efficiency and mix, evidenced by the non-GAAP operating margin climb from 14.4% to 20.3%. Ex-tariff-refund, that offset is currently producing roughly zero. Until the next two prints show core margin expansion resuming, the competitive trajectory reads stable rather than improving, and the mix shift toward therapeutic lines does not by itself establish reduced concentration risk — Merit discloses no customer-concentration data in the reviewed material.

## Risk Assessment

**China volume-based procurement.** Structural, ongoing, unquantified. The 10-K states VBP impacts began in 2022 as decreased sales prices and revenue, have persisted, and are expected to continue in 2026, and that Merit cannot reliably forecast the effect. China is the largest non-US market; APAC grew 2.3% in FY2025 against EMEA's 15.3%. Foreign-currency-denominated sales were $510.3M, or 34% of FY2025 revenue, with the renminbi and euro the largest exposures.

**EU MDR transition.** Devices holding pre-May-2021 MDD certificates may remain on the EU market only until December 31, 2027 or 2028 depending on risk class, and the 10-K states directly that "there will be products that we will instead choose to discontinue or postpone introduction in the E.U." Merit does not disclose the revenue attributable to products it intends to discontinue. This lands on EMEA, currently the fastest-growing region. MDR compliance costs are running $1.5–2.1M per quarter and are excluded from non-GAAP despite being structurally recurring through 2028.

**Sterilization concentration.** Merit relies on a small number of third-party sterilizers and most products use ethylene oxide, with US and EU restrictions developing. For an overwhelmingly single-use portfolio, a sterilization capacity disruption is a revenue-stopping event; the filings offer no quantified contingency capacity.

**Tariffs cut both ways.** Merit imports from Mexico (Tijuana is its largest plant), Ireland and China. FY2026 guidance is explicitly stated to exclude the potential impact of trade actions implemented after July 30, 2026. The $6.9M refund is a one-time benefit; the underlying exposure remains live, and Merit notes it is not the importer of record for all affected imports, limiting further recovery.

**Leadership transition and controls.** Founder departure is complete and the succession risk is named in the company's own risk factors, with no key-man insurance on Aronson. The Q2 filing also surfaced a convertible-note administrative lapse carrying an approximately $5.1M cost; the mechanism and duration are not fully reconstructible from the evidence available and I treat it as an open verification gap rather than a thesis input, but it belongs on the controls watch list.

**Balance sheet composition.** Goodwill and intangibles at 40.2% of assets and 68.6% of equity create impairment exposure, most concentrated in the recently acquired lines. View Point's $116.4M of developed technology over a 12-year life adds roughly $9.7M annually of intangible amortization (about $0.12/share after tax) with no acquired-product sales recorded in Q2 or H1.

**New risk factor added in Q2 2026:** conflict among the United States, Israel and Iran and related geopolitical instability. The debt risk factor was also updated with revolver-capacity language and the word "accelerated" in respect of the convertible notes.

**Repricing risk on single prints.** The demonstrated behaviour of this stock — a 23% drawdown from February to May on one soft organic quarter, then a strong recovery on one strong one — means the market is pricing MMSI off individual quarterly organic prints. With H2 guided below H1 and Q3 seasonally softest, that mechanism is live in both directions.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($114, +30%)

Q1's +3.7% organic was the anomaly and Q2's +9.0% is the run-rate. US growth of 11% reflects genuine share capture in cardiac therapies and endoscopy rather than order timing, and OneMark begins contributing in H2 against a guide that assumes essentially nothing from it. Management, in its first full guidance cycle under a new CEO, set a beatable H2 bar, and two more beats of the magnitude delivered over the last four quarters (average surprise roughly 12%) take FY2026 non-GAAP EPS above $4.40 and FY2027 toward $4.80. The CGI successor framework, due as the current program expires at end-2026, commits to renewed operating margin expansion, restoring the 2021–2025 cadence and converting revenue growth into low-teens EPS growth. Balance sheet capacity is real — 0.97x net leverage, $448.7M cash, $697M undrawn revolver — leaving room for further tuck-in acquisitions of the type that have reliably added growth. Sentiment support: the CIA overhang is formally resolved, five C-suite officers bought stock in the open market at the May trough, and the sell side is revising targets up by 20%+ increments. Re-rating to 24x on $4.75 gives $114.

### Bear Case ($70, −20%)

The margin story is the thesis, and ex-tariff-refund it has stopped working. Core non-GAAP operating margin was down about 27 basis points in Q2 and up about 16 in H1, against roughly 148 basis points of annual expansion in 2021–2025. Without margin expansion, a 7% revenue grower produces 7–8% EPS growth, which does not support 20x. Growth itself is increasingly purchased: approximately $740M of acquisitions over three and a half years to sustain ~7% total growth, with the legacy consumables core at mid-single-digits, Procedural Solutions −10% and Renal Therapies −9%. The tax rate has normalized from 15.8% to roughly 24%, a headwind invisible in the compensation metric. H1 free cash flow fell 14% on a $43.6M inventory build that pushed days on hand from 157 to 170. Two undisclosed-magnitude structural drags sit in the 2027–2028 window: China VBP, which management says it cannot forecast, and the EU MDR discontinuation decision, which lands on the fastest-growing region. H2 guidance already implies deceleration versus H1, and Q3 is seasonally softest. A single print at 4–5% organic re-runs the February-to-May sequence: 16x on $4.40 gives $70, with the $60.44 May low as the tail.

### What Would Resolve It

The Q3 print (late October 2026) is a clean test on three axes: organic constant-currency growth versus the 6–8% band, non-GAAP operating margin excluding any further tariff items, and whether the H2 guide holds without leaning on one-offs. Whether management quantifies the EU MDR revenue at risk in the FY2026 10-K would materially narrow the bear case.

## Conclusion

Merit Medical is a well-financed, competently run mid-cap consumables franchise that just delivered its fourth consecutive earnings beat and raised full-year guidance on both revenue and EPS. Net leverage of 0.97x, $448.7M of cash, an undrawn $697M revolver, a single 3.00% convertible not due until 2029, and FY2025 free cash flow equal to roughly 93% of non-GAAP net income together describe a balance sheet that can absorb a bad year. The therapeutic platform is compounding at 11% constant currency with cardiac therapies and endoscopy in the mid-20s to 30s, and the CIA overhang from the 2020 DOJ settlement is formally closed.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade is arithmetic, not deterioration. Roughly all of the reported Q2 non-GAAP operating margin expansion traces to a $6.9M tariff refund that was not excluded from non-GAAP; ex-refund, core margin expansion in H1 was approximately 16 basis points against a four-year average near 148. The full-year EPS raise approximately equals the Q2 beat, leaving H2 revenue guided to grow more slowly than H1 despite two acquisitions beginning to contribute. Growth has required roughly $740M of acquisitions over three and a half years to sustain ~7%, and the resulting $1.15B of goodwill and intangibles equals 68.6% of equity. Against that, the stock has recovered from its May low to 20.4x guided FY2026 earnings and 19.0x FY2027 consensus, which leaves roughly +11% to a defensible base case against −20% to a bear case the market executed as recently as three months ago.

The five open-market purchases by C-suite officers in early May were a genuine signal at $60–63; they are not a signal at $87.69, and the company itself repurchased no shares at that trough despite holding $448.7M of cash — a capital-allocation observation that argues M&A will continue to absorb discretionary capital. Own the position through the Q3 print. Add in the mid-$70s or on evidence that core margin expansion, stripped of one-time credits, has resumed.
