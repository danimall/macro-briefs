**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Titan International is a global manufacturer of off-highway wheels, tires, wheel-and-tire assemblies, and undercarriage systems serving agricultural, earthmoving/construction, and consumer end markets. The company is in a pronounced cyclical down-cycle: operating income has collapsed roughly 90% from its 2022 peak of $206M to $21M in 2025, driven by large-ag OEM destocking, weak farm income, and high financing costs that suppress equipment demand. The headline 2025 net loss of $63.5M overstates operating deterioration; roughly $50M reflects a non-cash deferred-tax valuation allowance (domestic and Luxembourg), and the Q1 2026 loss is dominated by a $25.1M Jackson, TN plant-closure impairment. Underlying EBITDA runs near a $100M annual pace. This is a cyclical trough rather than a solvency crisis, with an Altman Z of 2.23 sitting in the grey zone rather than distress territory.

The investment question is whether the down-cycle is adequately discounted at $7.57. The stock has compressed ~35% from its February 2026 high of $11.70 and trades within ~18% of its 52-week low of $6.43, below every published sell-side target: the full panel carries 3 BUY / 0 HOLD / 0 SELL with a mean target of $11.75, a median of $12.00, and a low of $10.00. The only forward consensus estimate ($0.37 next-year EPS) is being revised upward (+8.8% over 30 days, +10.4% over 90 days), implying roughly 20x forward earnings, a reasonable trough multiple for a recovering cyclical.

Against that, the bear case is concrete and current: free cash flow was negative $24.6M in 2025, operating cash flow fell from $141M to $30M, net revolver availability is thin at $49.6M (with $145M of cash held abroad), a $400M 7.00% senior note matures in April 2028, and a multi-year selling overhang from American Industrial Partners (an effective S-3 shelf, plus a 3.04M-share sale in March 2026) caps the stock. With positives and negatives roughly offsetting and no dated catalyst in either direction, we rate TWI HOLD. We do not yet see enough conviction in the recovery timing to call it a buy, and the absence of a dividend or imminent solvency threat rules out both an income thesis and an avoid.

## Company Overview

Titan operates three reportable segments organized by end market:

- **Agricultural (~41% of sales, $741M in 2025):** Wheels and tires for tractors, combines, planters, and irrigation, sold to OEMs and the aftermarket under Goodyear Farm Tire (licensed), Titan Tire, Carlstar, ACES, and Voltyre-Prom brands. Titan makes the largest (54-inch) ag wheel in North America.
- **Earthmoving/Construction (~32%, $582M):** Wheels, tires, and undercarriage systems for mining, construction, military, and forestry equipment. Titan produces the largest (63-inch) E/C wheel globally.
- **Consumer (~28%, $506M):** Substantially built out by the February 2024 Carlstar acquisition (now "Titan Specialty"): powersports, outdoor power equipment, and high-speed trailers under Carlisle (licensed through 2033), ITP, Marastar, and Black Rock brands, plus Latin American truck tires and Russian light-truck tires.

The competitive differentiator is Titan's ability to deliver complete wheel-and-tire assemblies through a globally distributed manufacturing footprint, which management frames as a tariff-era advantage. Customer relationships are deep and multi-decade (Deere, CNH, AGCO, Caterpillar, Kubota, Volvo, Liebherr, Hitachi). A structural dependency runs through the brand licensing: Titan licenses the Goodyear and Carlisle names rather than owning them.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is a textbook cyclical down-cycle layered with acquisition cost absorption:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,169M | $1,822M | $1,846M | $1,828M |
| Gross margin | 16.6% | 16.8% | 14.0% | 13.9% |
| Operating income | $206M | $149M | $33M | $21M |
| Net income | $176M | $79M | ($5.6M) | ($63.5M) |
| Adj. EBITDA (co. measure) | $253M | $205M | $128M | $102M |

**Operating deleverage is the dominant feature.** Operating income fell on roughly a 16% revenue decline from peak, leverage that works powerfully in both directions. Gross margin compressed ~270bps as volumes dropped, concentrated in large ag. Carlstar's higher-margin aftermarket Consumer business (~20% segment margin) partly offsets the ag and E/C weakness.

**The 2025 net loss reads worse than the underlying business.** Income tax expense was $49.9M on a pre-tax loss of $11.3M (an effective rate of -442%) because management established deferred-tax valuation allowances domestically and in Luxembourg. This is a non-cash charge, but it carries a real signal: management does not have enough confidence in near-term U.S./Lux profitability to assume it can realize those deferred tax assets. Stripping the tax item, operations sit near breakeven at the pre-tax line and positive at the EBITDA line.

**Cash flow quality deteriorated sharply.** The 2024 operating cash flow figure was flattered by one-time working-capital harvesting and acquired Carlstar working capital that did not repeat, so the year-over-year drop overstates the deterioration somewhat, but the absolute FCF figure is the binding concern.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Total debt is ~$586M (Dec 2025), dominated by the $400M 7.00% senior secured notes due April 2028. Debt-to-equity is ~2.2x. Liquidity is adequate but tight: net revolver availability was $36.1M at YE2025, improving to $49.6M by March 2026. Cash was $203M (Dec) falling to $171M (Mar), but $145M is held abroad. A fixed-charge coverage covenant springs if availability falls below roughly $17M or 10% of the line cap; the company is not at that trigger but has limited cushion if the down-cycle extends. Capex is disciplined at $50–55M; covenants restrict dividends and repurchases.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $505M (+2.9% y/y), but the gain was entirely an FX tailwind (+3.7% from a stronger real and euro) masking continued ag and consumer volume declines. The reported net loss of $24.2M / ($0.38) included the $25.1M Jackson, TN restructuring/impairment; stripped of that charge, the quarter was roughly breakeven. The GAAP miss versus the $0.04 consensus drove shares down ~4% on the print. Management characterized revenue and adjusted EBITDA as "near the high end of guidance," and Q2 adjusted-EBITDA guidance of $25–30M (including a ~$3M Iran-conflict cost headwind) implies still-subdued but stable profitability.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative on a volume basis; the reported revenue stabilization is an FX artifact. The forward question is when the large-ag cycle troughs and inflects. The industry is bifurcated: large ag equipment is in a deep destocking trough, while small ag, aftermarket Consumer (Carlstar), and mining-driven E/C show resilience or improvement.

The earnings-estimate picture is mixed but tilting constructive at the forward node. The current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have been cut (current-quarter to -$0.04, down 188.9% over 90 days; next-quarter to $0.01, down 86.2%), reflecting the persistence of the trough. The next-year EPS estimate, which captures the recovery, has been revised *up*. This is the period that matters for a recovery thesis, and it is moving the right way, supported by a unanimous BUY sell-side panel.

The caveat: this is a forecast of a cyclical turn that has not yet shown in volumes. The earnings track record over the last four quarters (1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses, including a -100% surprise in Q1 2026) does not support high confidence in consensus delivery. The Jackson closure lowers the post-2026 cost base, which mechanically improves recovery-year operating leverage if volumes return. The magnitude and timing of the ag inflection remain the swing variable, with no dated catalyst confirming it.

The estimate-revision signal does not clear the threshold for a strong buy trigger: revenue is not growing (volumes are declining), so this does not match the "+30% revisions plus accelerating revenue" inflection profile. It is a constructive but unconfirmed forward estimate against a still-contracting top line.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here; the company is loss-making, with a reported EPS of -$1.00. The relevant frame is normalized/forward earnings power.

On the only forward estimate, the $0.37 next-year consensus implies ~20x forward earnings. For a recovering cyclical, that is a reasonable multiple, neither a screaming bargain nor stretched. On normalized mid-cycle EBITDA (~$100M run-rate at trough, with peak above $250M), enterprise value of roughly $0.9B (equity ~$487M plus ~$586M debt less ~$171M cash) is ~9x trough EBITDA and would compress meaningfully on any recovery. Against peers in Agricultural & Farm Machinery (median P/E 26.7x: LNN 21.0x, AGCO 10.7x, TTC 26.7x, CNH 33.4x, DE 32.2x), TWI's recovery-year multiple is within range, but the peers are larger, better-capitalized, and carry stronger balance sheets. TWI's leverage and thin liquidity warrant a discount, and one is embedded.

The sell-side panel is unanimously constructive: 3 BUY / 0 HOLD / 0 SELL across all four reporting periods, with a high of $13.00. The stock at $7.57 trades below *every* published target; even the most conservative ($10) sits ~32% above the current price. This is genuine disconfirming evidence for an outright bearish view and is one of the main reasons we are not rating this AVOID. On inflection cyclicals with rising forward estimates and sub-30x forward multiples, consensus targets tend to lag, and a stock trading below the entire panel with the forward estimate rising is more often mispriced low than high.

The counterweight is that these targets are predicated on a cyclical-recovery thesis that has not yet materialized in volumes, and operating deleverage has already shown it cuts both ways. We assign a valuation score of 5.0: modestly cheap on normalized earnings with a credible re-rating path, but not a high-conviction mispricing given the leverage, negative FCF, and the absence of a confirmed inflection. Cheapness alone, without a dated catalyst, is not a sufficient basis for a high score on a name where the recovery timing is genuinely uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

Titan holds a defensible position in a cyclical niche. It is a leading off-highway wheel/tire supplier with multi-decade OEM relationships and a structural advantage in delivering complete assemblies from regionally distributed plants, a real edge in a tariff-fragmented supply environment. The Carlstar acquisition diversified the mix toward higher-margin, less OEM-concentrated aftermarket Consumer revenue. The Jackson closure consolidates North American production into a lower-cost footprint.

The trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving. Risks to the moat: Titan competes against larger, better-capitalized global tire makers that periodically cut price; OEMs (Deere is ~10% of sales, top-10 are 33%) continually pressure suppliers and could in-source wheels and tires; and Titan licenses rather than owns the Goodyear and Carlisle brands, a dependency renewable on terms it does not fully control. We score competitive position 5.5: a defensible niche position holding share through a downturn, but without evidence of structural share gains that would justify a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

- **Cyclicality / large-ag downturn (primary risk):** Three cyclical end markets, with large ag in a pronounced trough. Operating deleverage means the earnings swing is amplified. This risk is well known and substantially priced after a ~35% drawdown.
- **Liquidity, not earnings, is the binding constraint:** Limited cushion if the down-cycle extends. Management explicitly lists "potential divestitures and unencumbered assets" as a liquidity lever, indicating options are being kept open.
- **2028 refinancing:** ~$570M of total debt matures that year. Two years out, this is not an imminent catalyst, but it is the pivotal forward event and will increasingly weigh on sentiment as it approaches, particularly in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
- **Persistent share supply (distinctive technical risk):** American Industrial Partners (legacy Carlstar owner) holds an effective S-3 shelf and sold shares at $8.95 in March 2026, still holding 8.88M. MHR/Rachesky exited (the $57.6M October 2024 buyback, options zeroed February 2026). This overhang helps explain the peer-lagging TSR (index 161 vs. peer 291) despite a defensible business, and it caps near-term upside.
- **Tariff regime uncertainty:** The April 2025 IEEPA tariffs were struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026, after which the administration moved to a 10%→15% global tariff. Open questions on cost pass-through, supply-chain disruption, and duty refunds. In-region manufacturing is the partial hedge.
- **Russia / Voltyre-Prom:** 64.3% ownership of a Volgograd asset (~7% of assets, 3–4% of sales) that is sanctions-exposed and hard to monetize.
- **Hyperinflation accounting:** Argentina and Turkey under ASC 830, generating ongoing FX/monetary losses.
- **Governance:** Two recent officer/director departures (8-Ks dated December 2025 and February 2026, Item 5.02), with succession detail not fully resolved. Disclosure controls were certified effective with no internal-control changes.

On balance, the risks are real but largely identified and substantially discounted at a price near 52-week lows. There is no single binary event with imminent asymmetric downside; the 2028 refi is the largest discrete risk and it is two years out. We score risk factors 4.5: below midpoint because of the liquidity tightness, persistent supply overhang, and leverage into a still-contracting top line, but not lower because no near-term catalyst threatens a step-change down.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 51.5% to 59.6% over the May 28–June 19 window while the stock gained 4.8%, indicating the market is pricing increasing uncertainty rather than complacency. Short interest is moderate at 5.2% of float with 3.8 days to cover; uncrowded, meaning informed investors are not heavily positioned for a decline, which is consistent with the two-sided fundamental setup rather than a one-way bear case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($11–13):** The large-ag cycle troughs in 2026 and inflects in 2027. Operating leverage works in reverse: on a lower post-Jackson cost base, recovering volumes restore EBITDA materially above the current ~$100M run-rate, the deferred-tax valuation allowance reverses, FCF turns positive, and the 2028 notes refinance on manageable terms. The stock re-rates toward the unanimous sell-side target band of $10–13. The Carlstar aftermarket and mining-driven E/C provide a higher-margin ballast that lifts through-cycle margins. This is the D.A. Davidson $13 / consensus $11.75 scenario.

**Bear case ($5–6):** The ag down-cycle extends through 2027, FCF stays negative, the revolver tightens toward the covenant trigger, and AIP continues distributing stock into a weak tape. The 2028 refinancing approaches with leverage still elevated and rates still high, forcing either a dilutive equity raise or a distressed refinancing premium. The non-cash valuation allowance proves prescient as U.S. profitability fails to recover. Operating deleverage compounds any volume disappointment.

The asymmetry is genuinely two-sided. The stock sits roughly midway between a bear case ~25% below and a base/bull case 30–70% above, with no dated catalyst to resolve the direction in either window.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD rated on uncertainty: positives and negatives roughly cancel, and we lack the evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of *volume*-driven (not FX-driven) revenue stabilization or growth in the Agricultural segment, confirming the cycle has troughed.
- Return to positive free cash flow on a trailing-twelve-month basis, restoring balance-sheet flexibility ahead of the 2028 maturity.
- Concrete progress on refinancing or extending the $400M 7.00% notes on terms that do not require material dilution, OR completion of the AIP share distribution removing the supply overhang.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, which is currently absent (only a single ~$30K buy in August 2025 against routine grants and a ~$2.9M director sale).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net revolver availability falling toward the ~$17M covenant-spring threshold, or a covenant breach.
- A further leg down in adjusted EBITDA guidance below the current $25–30M quarterly pace, signaling the trough is deepening rather than stabilizing.
- A dilutive equity raise announced to address liquidity or the 2028 maturity.
- Falling next-year EPS estimates (the current +10.4% 90-day revision reversing), which would remove the one constructive forward signal.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with the ag-volume trend and Q3 EBITDA guidance as the key data points. The 2028 refinancing window becomes the dominant variable through 2027.

## Conclusion

Titan International is a leveraged off-highway wheel/tire cyclical caught in a deep large-ag down-cycle, trading below every published analyst target on a depressed EBITDA run-rate. The business is defensible (a leading assembler with multi-decade OEM relationships and a diversifying aftermarket mix), and the situation is a cyclical trough rather than a solvency crisis, with a grey-zone Altman Z and breakeven underlying operations once non-cash tax and impairment charges are stripped out.

The case for owning it rests on a cheap normalized valuation (~20x a rising next-year consensus), a unanimous BUY sell-side panel pointing 32–72% higher, and lower post-Jackson breakeven costs that would amplify any recovery. The case against rests on negative free cash flow, tight liquidity, the 2028 refinancing overhang, and a persistent AIP/sponsor share-supply drag with no insider conviction buying to offset it. These forces roughly cancel, and there is no dated catalyst to break the tie in the next two to three quarters. We rate TWI HOLD pending evidence of a volume-driven cyclical inflection or material progress on the balance sheet, at which point the asymmetry would tilt toward the bull case the sell-side already expects.
