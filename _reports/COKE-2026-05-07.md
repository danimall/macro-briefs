## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola Consolidated is the largest Coca-Cola bottler in the United States, serving approximately 60 million consumers across 14 states through exclusive territorial distribution agreements. The company executed a $2.4 billion share repurchase of The Coca-Cola Company's entire stake in November 2025, reducing shares outstanding from 77 million to 57 million while nearly doubling total debt from $1.8B to $2.8B. This leveraged recapitalization created a stockholders' deficit of -$740 million and prompted S&P to revise its outlook to Negative.

The Q1 2026 earnings release (May 6, 2026) presented a mixed picture: adjusted volume grew 6.4% (the strongest in years), but adjusted net income declined 12.3% YoY due to $35 million in incremental aluminum tariff costs, wage investments, and approximately $100M in annualized interest expense from the recapitalization. The stock dropped 17-20% intraday on the report, settling near $210 — still up 79% from a year ago but down 15% from the 52-week high of $219.65.

At approximately 31x forward earnings, the stock trades at a premium to consumer staples peers (median 25.1x) without a commensurate growth differential. The combination of compressed margins, elevated leverage with a Negative credit outlook, and absolute governance control by one individual creates a risk profile that demands a wider margin of safety than the current valuation provides.

## Company Overview

Coca-Cola Consolidated operates 10 manufacturing plants and 60 distribution centers organized across four regions: Carolinas, Mid-Atlantic, Mid-South, and Mid-West. Approximately 85% of bottle/can sales volume consists of Coca-Cola Company products, with the remainder split between Monster Energy and Keurig Dr Pepper brands.

The business model centers on exclusive territorial distribution rights protected by the Soft Drink Interbrand Competition Act. These rights function as a near-monopoly within COKE's geography — no other bottler can distribute Coca-Cola branded products in its territory. This structural advantage is reinforced by the capital-intensive nature of DSD (direct store delivery) networks, creating high barriers to entry.

J. Frank Harrison III serves as Chairman and CEO, controlling approximately 78% of total voting power through Class B Common Stock. With The Coca-Cola Company's departure as a shareholder in November 2025, Harrison's control faces no institutional counterweight.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth**

| Year | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|------|---------|-----------|
| 2022 | $6.20B | — |
| 2023 | $6.65B | +7.3% |
| 2024 | $6.90B | +3.7% |
| 2025 | $7.23B | +4.8% |
| Q1 2026 | $1.85B | +8.5% adjusted |

Growth has been primarily pricing-driven through 2025 (physical case volume grew only 0.3% in FY2025). Q1 2026 marked a genuine acceleration: adjusted volume reached +6.4%, with Sparkling beverages up 5.3% and Still beverages up 10.2%. This contrasts with the near-flat volume of FY2025 and suggests either category-level recovery or meaningful share gains.

Key growth vectors:
- Energy drinks (Monster) and sports drinks (BODYARMOR, Powerade) growing above the portfolio average
- Zero-sugar variants stabilizing traditional Sparkling demand
- Alternative Routes to Market (ARTM) improving per-unit economics on lower-margin deliveries

**Management Guidance**

Mid-single-digit organic revenue growth and 7-8% comparable EPS growth for FY2026. Given Q1's 12.3% adjusted net income decline, achieving 7-8% EPS growth for the full year requires meaningful margin recovery in H2 — likely contingent on aluminum cost stabilization and operating leverage on higher volumes.

**Structural Growth Ceiling**

The US nonalcoholic beverage market grows 2-4% annually. COKE's territorial exclusivity ensures participation in that growth but limits the ability to grow beyond it absent acquisition or territory expansion. The company is fundamentally a distribution and logistics business tied to The Coca-Cola Company's brand portfolio and marketing decisions.

**Profitability Under Pressure**

Gross margin declined to 39.7% in FY2025 (down 20bps) and compressed further in Q1 2026 (down 70bps adjusted). The drivers:
- Aluminum tariffs: ~$35M incremental cost in Q1 2026 alone, with 54% of volume sold in cans
- Wage investments: Front-line wage increases effective Q3 2025, with labor representing ~2/3 of SD&A
- Interest expense: From $1.8M net in FY2024 to $42.7M in FY2025, trending toward $100M+ annualized

Adjusted net income declined 1.5% in FY2025 and 12.3% in Q1 2026, a concerning deceleration directly attributable to the recapitalization's interest burden and tariff exposure.

**Balance Sheet Transformation**

The November 2025 buyback fundamentally altered the capital structure:
- Total debt: $2.79B (vs. $1.79B in 2024)
- Cash: $282M (vs. $1.14B in 2024)
- Stockholders' equity: -$740M (vs. +$1.42B in 2024)
- Debt/EBITDA: ~2.4x (operating income $951M + D&A $219M = ~$1.17B EBITDA)
- S&P rating: BBB+ with Negative outlook

The company repaid $150M of its term loan in February 2026 and has $100M in senior notes due October 2026. The remaining $900M term loan matures December 2028. While 2.4x leverage is manageable for a stable cash flow business, the trajectory matters: the company went from a net cash position to materially leveraged in 12 months.

**Cash Flow Quality**

Operating cash flow of $931.9M (FY2025) and free cash flow of $619.6M (8.6% FCF margin) remain strong. Capex guidance of ~$300M for 2026 suggests continued FCF generation of $550-650M, sufficient to cover interest, dividends ($87M), and gradual deleveraging. However, there is limited capacity for additional shareholder returns or strategic investments while the balance sheet rebuilds.

**Contingent Consideration Liability**

A $717.9M liability representing sub-bottling payments to The Coca-Cola Company over approximately 40 years creates ongoing earnings volatility. Fair value adjustments produced $131.9M in non-cash expense in FY2025 and $53.4M in Q1 2026 alone. This liability increases as COKE's projected cash flows improve or when discount rates decline — a perverse dynamic where business strength generates P&L charges. Annual cash payments of $50-80M are an ongoing drag.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Acceleration — Real But Early**

The 6.4% adjusted volume growth in Q1 2026 is the most bullish data point in this report. Still beverages (energy drinks, enhanced water, protein) grew 10.2%, and even Sparkling beverages grew 5.3%. Whether this reflects category-level recovery or COKE-specific share gains requires at least one more quarter of data.

The structural growth ceiling remains: the US nonalcoholic beverage market grows 2-4% annually. COKE's territorial exclusivity ensures participation but limits upside absent acquisition or territory expansion.

Achieving management's 7-8% comparable EPS growth guidance for FY2026 requires meaningful margin recovery in H2, likely contingent on aluminum cost stabilization and operating leverage on higher volumes.

## Valuation Assessment

At $178.80, COKE trades at:
- ~31x trailing P/E (using $6.81 EPS per Phase 1 data)
- ~31x forward P/E (per Phase 1 data)
- ~17x EV/EBITDA (using EV of ~$12.6B [$10.1B market cap + $2.8B debt - $0.3B cash] over ~$1.17B EBITDA)

Against consumer staples peers (median P/E 25.1x), COKE trades at a 24% premium. This premium was arguably justified when the company had a net cash balance sheet; with leverage now at 2.4x and margins compressing, the justification weakens.

The stock has appreciated 79% over the past year, driven primarily by two events: the share count reduction from the November 2025 buyback (mechanically increasing per-share metrics) and the Q1 2026 volume acceleration. However, the May 6 earnings-day selloff of 17-20% suggests the market is now questioning whether volume strength can translate into earnings growth given the elevated cost structure.

A DCF approach using $600M FCF, 8% WACC, and 3% terminal growth implies fair value near $13-14B enterprise value, or roughly $185-195 per share equity value. The stock at $178.80 sits within this fair value range, though the embedded assumptions about margin recovery have not yet materialized.

## Competitive Landscape

COKE's competitive position is among the strongest in consumer staples:

1. **Territorial exclusivity**: No competitor can distribute Coca-Cola products in COKE's 14-state territory. This is a legal monopoly.
2. **Scale advantages**: 10 manufacturing plants, 60 distribution centers, and direct relationships with every major retailer create logistics moats impossible to replicate.
3. **Brand portfolio**: Access to Coca-Cola, Monster, Dr Pepper, BODYARMOR — the highest-demand brands in every retail refrigerator.

The primary competitive constraint is not from other bottlers but from The Coca-Cola Company itself, which unilaterally sets concentrate prices. COKE has "few limits" on its ability to negotiate these prices, creating structural margin pressure whenever The Coca-Cola Company decides to raise concentrate costs.

Customer concentration (Walmart 21% of volume, Kroger 15%) creates channel risk but is inherent to any large-scale distribution business and is well-understood by the market.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff/Input Cost Escalation (HIGH)**

$35M in incremental aluminum tariff costs in Q1 2026 annualizes to $140M+ — nearly 15% of adjusted net income. With 54% of volume in aluminum cans and elevated tariff uncertainty, this headwind may persist or worsen. Pricing power exists but operates with a lag and faces consumer elasticity constraints.

**2. Leverage and Credit Deterioration (MODERATE-HIGH)**

S&P's Negative outlook creates a defined downgrade risk. A one-notch downgrade to BBB would increase borrowing costs on future refinancing. The December 2028 term loan maturity ($900M) arrives in an uncertain rate environment. While current FCF provides coverage, margin compression reduces the buffer.

**3. Governance / Key-Man Risk (MODERATE)**

Harrison's 78% voting control means minority shareholders cannot influence capital allocation, executive compensation ($16.7M annually), or succession planning. The multiple C-suite departures/appointments throughout 2025 — including the naming of Matt Blickley as CFO & Chief Accounting Officer and Dave Katz as President & COO — suggest a succession transition is underway, but the timeline and ultimate structure remain opaque to outside investors.

**4. Contingent Consideration Volatility (MODERATE)**

The $718M liability will generate unpredictable non-cash charges for decades. In periods of declining interest rates or improving business performance, this liability increases and reduces reported earnings — creating a headwind precisely when fundamentals improve.

**5. SNAP Benefit Restrictions (LOW-MODERATE)**

Several states implementing restrictions on SNAP purchases of soft drinks and energy drinks. The affected demographics overlap with COKE's territory. Impact is difficult to quantify but represents a structural demand headwind for carbonated beverages in lower-income segments.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($240-260, +34% to +45%)**

Volume acceleration proves durable through H2 2026. Aluminum costs stabilize as tariff uncertainty resolves. Operating leverage on 6%+ volume growth drives margin expansion. Rapid deleveraging ($550-650M annual FCF vs. $2.5B net debt) restores balance sheet health within 18-24 months. S&P revises outlook to Stable. The reduced share count (57M vs. 77M pre-buyback) amplifies per-share earnings growth, and the stock re-rates toward 25x forward earnings on a normalized ~$10 EPS power in FY2027.

**Bear Case ($140-160, -22% to -11%)**

Tariff costs persist or escalate. Volume acceleration in Q1 proves one-time (calendar benefit, inventory fill). Wage inflation and interest expense continue compressing margins. S&P downgrades credit rating to BBB, increasing refinancing costs. Adjusted EPS stagnates at $6-7 while the stock de-rates from 31x toward 20-22x (in line with mature beverage/distribution peers with declining margins). The December 2028 term loan refinancing in a high-rate environment further pressures profitability.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating gross margin expansion alongside volume growth >3%, confirming pricing power and operating leverage
- S&P revising credit outlook back to Stable, removing the downgrade overhang
- Stock pullback to $150-160 range (24-26x forward) providing adequate margin of safety

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 volume growth decelerating below 2% (invalidating acceleration thesis)
- S&P executing a rating downgrade to BBB
- Gross margin declining below 37.5% (indicating inability to pass through input costs)

**Reassessment Timeline:**
- Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) — primary catalyst for determining whether Q1 volume acceleration is sustainable or anomalous
- October 2026 — $100M senior note maturity provides data point on refinancing capacity and terms

## Conclusion

Coca-Cola Consolidated possesses irreplaceable competitive advantages through its territorial distribution rights and scale infrastructure. The November 2025 buyback meaningfully reduced the share count at an attractive price (~$127/share vs. current ~$178.80). Q1 2026 volume acceleration is genuinely encouraging.

However, the current valuation at 31x forward earnings embeds optimistic assumptions about margin recovery that face concrete headwinds: aluminum tariffs ($140M+ annualized), wage investments, $100M+ in interest expense, and a Negative credit outlook. The Q1 adjusted net income decline of 12.3% directly contradicts the earnings growth embedded in the multiple. Until the company demonstrates that volume strength translates into earnings growth — plausibly two to three quarters away — the risk/reward at current prices is balanced rather than asymmetric.
