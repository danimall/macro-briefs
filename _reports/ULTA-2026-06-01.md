**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ulta Beauty is the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, operating 1,591 stores at fiscal 2025 year-end and generating $12.39B in net sales. The demand story has reaccelerated: comparable sales rose 5.4% in FY2025 (vs. +0.7% the prior year), with Q3 comps reaching +6.3% on positive ticket and transaction growth. Gross margin is stable-to-up at 39.1%. The problem is not the top line.

The problem sits below gross profit. Operating margin has fallen roughly 260 basis points over two years, from 15.0% in FY2023 to 12.4% in FY2025, driven entirely by SG&A deleverage rather than merchandise margin. SG&A rose to 26.6% of sales on higher incentive compensation, store labor, and strategic investments in loyalty, ERP/cloud software, supply chain, media, and international expansion. Net income has now declined for two consecutive years despite record revenue, from $1.24B to $1.29B to $1.15B, with the optics partly masked by a 13% share-count reduction funded by roughly $1B per year in buybacks. The Q4 FY2025 EPS miss ($8.01 vs. $8.18 estimate) and a guidance setup the market read as light triggered a sharp derating from the February high near $715.

At ~$509, ULTA trades around 18x forward EPS (consensus FY2026 ~$28.61), a meaningful discount to its historical multiple and below the peer specialty-retail cohort adjusted for franchise quality. That discount is defensible, and BofA's recent upgrade to Buy ($685 PT) frames the bull case as a "compounder at a discount." But the core debate, whether the margin compression is a recoverable investment cycle or a structural reset for a maturing U.S. retailer facing an intensifying Sephora rivalry, is unresolved in the data. Estimates are flat (not rising), insider activity is neutral-to-mildly negative, leadership has churned heavily through a transition year, and near-term options pricing is signaling acute event risk. This is a reasonable franchise at a reasonable price without the asymmetry that would justify a higher conviction call. We rate ULTA a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1990, Ulta broke down the traditional beauty channel silos (department stores for prestige, drugstores for mass, salons for professional hair care) with a one-stop model offering roughly 29,000–30,000 SKUs across mass and prestige cosmetics, skincare/wellness, haircare, and fragrance, plus full-service salons in nearly every store. The company operates as a single reportable segment.

Category mix as of Q3 FY2025: cosmetics 41%, skincare/wellness 24%, haircare 19%, fragrance 11%, services 3%, other 2%. The store base is 1,505 Ulta U.S. locations plus 86 Space NK stores in the UK and Ireland following the July 2025 acquisition.

The competitive moat rests on three pillars: the Ulta Beauty Rewards loyalty program (management's repeatedly cited crown jewel, generating proprietary first-party data and personalization), an omnichannel footprint including the "Ulta Beauty at Target" shop-in-shop royalty arrangement, and brand-partner relationships with exclusive and early-access products. The current strategy ("Ulta Beauty Unleashed") prioritizes core growth, scaling accretive newer businesses (wellness, retail media, international), and cost-structure streamlining.

## Financial Analysis

The two-year financial record contains a clear tension between accelerating revenue and compressing profitability.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $10.21B | $11.21B | $12.39B |
| Comparable sales | +5.7% | +0.7% | +5.4% |
| Gross margin | 39.1% | 38.8% | 39.1% |
| SG&A % of sales | 24.0% | 24.9% | 26.6% |
| Operating margin | 15.0% | 13.9% | 12.4% |
| Net income | $1.24B | $1.29B | $1.15B |
| Net margin | 12.2% | 11.5% | 9.3% |

FY2025 revenue grew 10.5% to $12.39B, driven by the comp recovery (+5.4%, comprising +3.3% ticket and +2.0% transactions), the Space NK acquisition, and new stores. Comps accelerated through the year, with Q3 reaching +6.3% versus +0.6% a year prior. The demand recovery is broad-based.

The margin compression is mechanically explained by SG&A, which grew 17.4% in FY2025. Gross margin held at 39.1%, helped by lower shrink and better merchandise margin. The SG&A increase reflects higher incentive comp, store payroll and benefits, strategic corporate investments (including cloud-software and ERP amortization), and store expenses. The result: operating margin down 260bps over two years and net income down for a second consecutive year despite record sales. EPS held up better than net income because share count fell from roughly 51.7M to 44.9M diluted over three years (~13%), a structural tailwind.

Cash generation remains solid. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.50B and free cash flow roughly $1.07B (8.6% FCF margin), with disciplined capex of $434.8M (guided ≤$450M for FY2026). New U.S. store economics run about $2.4M average investment per store.

The balance sheet is healthy but no longer pristine. The Altman Z-Score of 3.61 sits in the safe zone, the current ratio is 1.41, ROE is 41%, and ROA is 16.5%. Reported D/E of 1.5 is inflated by roughly $3.5B of operating lease liabilities consistent with an asset-light leased-store model; true financial leverage is low. The shift is from net-cash to net-borrower: Ulta drew on its revolver for the first time in years, with $551.7M outstanding at Q3 (including the Space NK facility, at roughly 6.4%) before paying down to $62.3M at year-end. Cash fell from $703M to $205M at Q3. The company now incurs net interest expense after years of net interest income, which is manageable but a genuine change in the financial profile.

Capital allocation has been buyback-centric: $898.5M in FY2025, $1.02B in FY2024, $1.01B in FY2023, against a $3.0B authorization (October 2024) with roughly $2.0B remaining at Q3. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed. The U.S. core is reaccelerating on comp recovery, and management guided FY2026 to roughly 6–7% sales growth with EPS growth up to ~11.4%, the EPS-ahead-of-sales spread implying modest margin stabilization plus buyback support. Three growth vectors beyond the maturing U.S. store base are now live:

1. **Retail media and advertising** — higher-margin "other revenue" leveraging Ulta's first-party loyalty data.
2. **Wellness** — a category extension within existing stores.
3. **International** — three new modes launched in a single year (Space NK in UK/Ireland, a Mexico JV, and a Middle East franchise).

The domestic new-store runway is narrowing with 1,500+ U.S. locations, which is why the international and remodel push matters. The Google Gemini integration positions Ulta for agentic commerce, though this is Ulta deploying Google's AI as a customer/partner rather than proprietary technology.

The estimate-revision data tempers enthusiasm. Consensus is stable rather than rising: current-quarter EPS revised -4.3% over 90 days, next-quarter -1.5%, with current-year and next-year revisions roughly flat (+0.4% and +1.3%). This is below the +30% revision velocity that signals an underestimated acceleration. The earnings track record is decent, with three beats and one miss across the last four quarters, but the most recent quarter was the miss (-2.1%), breaking the prior streak of 8–14% beats. That deceleration in beat magnitude reduces confidence in the conservative-guidance argument.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$509 on ~43.6M shares outstanding, ULTA carries a market cap of roughly $22.2B. On trailing EPS of $25.64 that is ~19.8x; on consensus FY2026 EPS of ~$28.61 it is ~17.8x forward. The stock has fallen roughly 29% from its February 2026 high of $714.97 and trades near the lower end of its 52-week range.

The bull case for valuation rests on three points: the forward multiple sits below the company's historical 22–25x range, below where a high-ROE specialty retailer with 5%+ comps and durable loyalty economics typically trades; the ~13% share-count reduction compounds EPS; and analyst targets (mean $678.88, median $700, low $475, high $810) sit well above the current price, with BofA recently upgrading to Buy at $685. On the surface this looks like a quality compounder at a discount.

The counter is that the discount is doing real work. The market is pricing a genuine uncertainty: whether the margin compression reverses (investment cycle) or persists (structural reset as the U.S. business matures and Sephora intensifies competition). At 18x forward, ULTA is not pricing in a margin recovery, but it is also not pricing in further deterioration. Estimates are flat, not rising, which means the analyst targets reflect a margin-stabilization assumption that has not yet been confirmed in the numbers. Net income has declined two years running. A low multiple alone does not establish mispricing; here the multiple is reasonable for the demonstrated trajectory rather than clearly too cheap. The asymmetry is roughly balanced: meaningful upside if margins stabilize and comps hold, meaningful downside if SG&A deleverage proves structural and comps soften into a discretionary-spending slowdown.

We score valuation 6.5, modestly attractive on absolute and relative multiples, but without the specific, falsifiable mispricing that would warrant a higher mark. The improvement from prior reports (6.0 → 6.5) reflects the lower price on roughly unchanged fundamentals, a better entry rather than a deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

Ulta says it continues to gain U.S. share, supported by its comp reacceleration against a beauty market that expanded through 2024 and into Q3 2025. The structural rival is Sephora, whose LVMH backing, luxury exclusives, loyalty base, and Sephora-at-Kohl's footprint represent persistent pressure; both retailers ran competing spring sale events in 2026. Ulta's differentiation is its blend of mass and prestige with a value/pricing edge and salon services that Sephora does not match at scale.

Additional pressure comes from Amazon and online marketplaces, mass merchants, and DTC/social-commerce brands. The 10-K explicitly describes "highly competitive markets with few barriers to entry." Vendor concentration is a structural vulnerability: the top 10 brand partners represented ~51% of net sales in FY2025 (down from 54%), with no long-term supply agreements. A key brand defecting to a competitor or going DTC would damage both revenue and the exclusivity advantage.

The competitive trajectory is best characterized as stable-to-slightly-pressured rather than clearly improving. Comps are accelerating, which argues for share gains, but the SG&A spend required to defend that share (loyalty investment, store labor, media) is itself the margin problem. We score competitive position 5.5, a market leader maintaining share but spending heavily to do so against a well-resourced rival.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin/SG&A deleverage** is the live, central risk. FY2026 guidance implies only modest recovery; if comps decelerate, the deleverage worsens and the "investment cycle" thesis collapses into "structural reset." Watch the SG&A ratio quarter-over-quarter as the primary tell.

**Discretionary demand.** Beauty is discretionary, and management explicitly flags inflation, tariffs, and a possible "reset of consumer spending habits." In a macro environment where mass-market and budget-constrained consumers are showing the first earnings-level cracks, a broader trade-down or pullback in beauty spending is a real risk to the comp recovery. Organized retail crime shrink has run above historical levels.

**Leadership churn.** A cluster of officer/director departures ran through 2025 (Item 5.02 filings in January, March, June, July, August, October, December). Kecia Steelman is a relatively new CEO (elevated from COO), and the CFO transitioned mid-year, with an interim CFO signing the Q3 10-Q before Christopher DelOrefice's arrival. Execution risk is elevated precisely when margin discipline matters most.

**Integration and international.** Space NK, the Mexico JV (already loss-making), and the Middle East franchise are all new in 2025. Roughly $382M of new goodwill from Space NK creates impairment optionality if integration disappoints. Limited international operating experience compounds the risk.

**Vendor concentration and competition** (covered above) round out the named risks. The balance sheet shift to net-borrower modestly raises financial risk but remains well within tolerance.

We score risk factors 5.3, slightly below the prior 5.5, reflecting the combination of unresolved margin trajectory, leadership churn during a transition year, and discretionary-demand exposure: none individually catastrophic, but cumulatively meaningful and not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 108.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 34.8%, rich by roughly 74 points, in the 96th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (108.9% near-term vs. 43.8% at six months), with the nearest expiry pricing a ±9.6% implied move. ATM IV jumped from 42.4% to 108.9% over three weeks while the price barely moved (-0.6%), and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.29 to 0.84. This pattern is consistent with the market pricing an imminent, binary catalyst. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.2%), so this is event-uncertainty pricing rather than a directional bearish bet. The elevated near-term IV argues against new option-buying-based entries and confirms that the near-term setup is high-variance rather than directional.

Short interest is modest at 5.3% of float (3.4 days to cover), indicating the bearish thesis is not crowded; informed investors are not heavily positioned short, which cuts both ways for a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The margin compression is investment-driven and recoverable. FY2026 guidance of EPS growth ahead of sales growth signals management expects SG&A leverage to return. The share-count reduction compounds EPS, retail media and international add higher-margin growth vectors, and the stock at ~18x forward is well below its historical multiple after a 29% drawdown. If margins stabilize and the multiple re-rates toward 22x on FY2026 EPS of ~$28.61, the stock approaches $630–700, consistent with the analyst mean ($679) and BofA's $685 target. Bull case ~$700 (+38%).

### Bear Case

The margin compression is a structural reset for a maturing U.S. retailer. SG&A deleverage persists, comps soften as discretionary spending slows, and the Sephora rivalry forces continued elevated investment. Net income declines a third year, the international bets underperform (Mexico JV losses, Space NK impairment), and the leadership churn signals deeper operational instability. Estimates fall and the multiple compresses toward 15x on flat-to-down EPS, taking the stock toward $420–440, near the 52-week low of $414. Bear case ~$430 (-16%).

The base case sits between: comps moderate but hold positive, margins stabilize without sharp recovery, EPS grows modestly on buyback support, and the stock re-rates partway. Base case ~$575.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The central variable (margin trajectory) resolves quarter by quarter, and the next earnings report is the natural reassessment point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SG&A as a percent of sales declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters, confirming the investment cycle is producing leverage.
- Forward EPS estimates resume rising (current-year revisions turn positive >+5% over 90 days) alongside maintained comps >4%.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by C-suite (currently absent), which would signal management conviction at the depressed price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps decelerate below +2% while SG&A deleverage continues, confirming a structural margin reset.
- A guidance cut, or a third consecutive year of net income decline, with operating margin falling below 11%.
- Space NK goodwill impairment or evidence the international expansion is materially loss-making at scale.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release, near-term given the front-month IV signaling an imminent event. Monitor the SG&A ratio and comp trend at each subsequent quarter.

## Conclusion

Ulta is a high-quality specialty retail franchise trading at a reasonable ~18x forward EPS after a 29% drawdown, with a real comp recovery and a healthy balance sheet. The lower price on roughly unchanged fundamentals improves the risk/reward versus prior reports. But the case for owning it now requires conviction on a question the data does not yet answer: whether the margin compression is a recoverable investment cycle or a structural reset for a maturing retailer in an intensifying competitive environment. Estimates are flat, not rising; net income has fallen two years running; leadership has churned through a transition year; insiders are not buying; and the options market is pricing an imminent high-variance catalyst. None of this is disqualifying, but together it removes the asymmetry that would justify a BUY. The risk/reward is roughly balanced around a base case of ~$575 against a bear case near $430 and a bull case near $700. We rate ULTA a HOLD and recommend waiting for the SG&A trajectory to clarify before committing capital in either direction.

This marks the fourth consecutive HOLD on ULTA. The thesis has been consistent (good demand, problematic margins, fair valuation), and nothing in the current data resolves the core uncertainty enough to change it. The bar for continuing an unchanged HOLD is appropriately higher after three prior calls, and the assessment remains that the evidence is insufficient for a stronger view.
