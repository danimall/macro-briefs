**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Old National Bancorp is the sixth-largest commercial bank headquartered in the Midwest, with $73.0 billion in assets following the May 2025 acquisition of Bremer Financial. The investment case rests on a single analytical point: the bank's headline FY2025 numbers, a 0.77% ROA quarter and a provision spike to $106.8M, were distorted by Bremer purchase accounting (a $75.6M CECL "Day 1" non-PCD provision and roughly $140M of cumulative merger charges), not by deterioration in the underlying business. Strip those out and the franchise re-accelerated cleanly: Q1 2026 delivered an 18.4% ROTCE, a 48.3% efficiency ratio, and $0.59 diluted EPS ($0.61 adjusted). On normalized metrics, this is a top-tier regional bank.

The market has largely figured this out. The stock has run roughly 15% off its 52-week low, trades at ~12.3x trailing and ~9x forward earnings, and sits about 15% below the consensus target mean of $27.90. The recovery from the deal-accounting trough is no longer a secret, and the path from here depends on whether the integration continues to compound tangible book value (it has, $12.54 to $13.93 over five quarters) without a credit air pocket. Against that, ONB carries two specific, dated risks: a $779.5M refinancing cliff in sub-4% non-owner-occupied CRE maturing within 18 months, and a CRE book that is 45% of loans with office ($2.82B, $53.3M nonaccrual) as the visible soft spot. Classified assets rose $112.7M QoQ to $3.22B even as nonaccruals fell, an early migration signal that precedes provision pressure.

We rate ONB a HOLD with a growth bias. The franchise is high-quality and management is executing, but the entry price no longer offers the asymmetry that would justify a higher-conviction call. We would become a buyer on a pullback toward tangible book (~$14, implying a price in the low-$20s on the current multiple) or on confirmation that classified-asset migration is stabilizing through the next two quarters.

## Company Overview

Old National operates a classic relationship-banking model: commercial and CRE lending funded by a granular, low-cost core deposit base, supplemented by wealth management, capital markets, and mortgage fee businesses. The bank runs 346 banking centers across the Midwest and Southeast. Its legal headquarters sits in Evansville, Indiana, but commercial and consumer operations are run from Chicago, a legacy of the 2022 First Midwest merger. Post-Bremer, the loan book is geographically led by Minnesota (21%), Illinois (17%), Indiana (11%), Wisconsin (9%), and Michigan (6%), with a growing Southeast footprint.

ONB is a serial acquirer: Anchor BanCorp (2016), First Midwest (2022), CapStar (2024), and Bremer (2025). The Bremer transaction, which closed May 1, 2025 for ~$1.35 billion (50.2M shares plus $314.6M cash), added $16.3B in assets, $11.1B in loans, and $12.9B in deposits, growing the bank by roughly 35% and establishing ONB as a dominant Upper-Midwest commercial bank with stable, agriculture- and commerce-driven markets in Minnesota and the Dakotas. Nearly every current financial line item must be read through this lens.

## Financial Analysis

The five-quarter earnings trajectory is the most important table in the filings:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income ($M) | 387.6 | 514.8 | 574.6 | 580.8 | 572.6 |
| Provision ($M) | 31.4 | 106.8 | 26.7 | 32.7 | 34.9 |
| Net income to common ($M) | 140.6 | 121.4 | 178.5 | 212.6 | 229.6 |
| Diluted EPS | $0.44 | $0.34 | $0.46 | $0.55 | $0.59 |
| ROA | 1.08% | 0.77% | 1.03% | 1.21% | 1.29% |
| ROTCE | 15.0% | 12.0% | 15.9% | 17.8% | 18.4% |
| NIM | 3.27% | 3.53% | 3.64% | 3.65% | 3.55% |
| Efficiency ratio | 53.7% | 55.8% | 58.8% | 51.6% | 48.3% |

The Q2 2025 trough is a deal-mechanics artifact, driven by the $75.6M CECL Day 1 provision (the accounting double-count that forces a new reserve through the P&L on acquired non-credit-deteriorated loans) plus $41.2M of merger charges. Adjusted EPS rose steadily throughout: $0.45, $0.53, $0.59, $0.62, $0.61. Any standardized data pull flagging "spiking provisions and collapsing ROA" would misread this company.

The latest quarter showed net income to common of $229.6M. NII was down $8.3M QoQ on lower asset yields. NIM was 3.55%, down 10bps QoQ but up 28bps YoY on Bremer accretion and repricing. Loans grew 8% annualized to $49.7B and deposits 4% annualized to $55.7B. Fee income is a meaningful 18% of revenue ($122.3M), led by wealth (+34% YoY) and capital markets (+145% YoY), diversification that softens the deposit-cost margin headwind facing the whole sector.

The balance sheet is well-capitalized: CET1 11.11%, total capital 13.71% (boosted by a January 2026 $450M sub-debt issuance), TCE/TA 7.67%. The main watch item is a $543M accumulated OCI loss, with roughly $1.1B in combined unrealized losses across AFS (~$702M) and HTM (~$392M) securities. These are rate-driven, not credit, and ONB has no intent or need to sell. Tangible book value per share has risen through the integration, which for a serial acquirer is the decisive test of whether deals create value. Operating cash flow was solid at $206M in Q1'26, and the bank repurchased $104M of stock in the quarter, its most aggressive buyback in the dataset, with $383.5M remaining through February 2027.

The balance sheet is essentially rate-neutral. A -100bp shock cuts two-year NII by only 1.16%; a +100bp lift adds only 0.70%. With Fed funds at 3.50–3.75%, that defensive posture limits the downside from a hawkish surprise but also caps the upside from cuts.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is solid but not spectacular. Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of 4–6% loan growth, consistent with the 8% annualized pace in Q1'26 (which includes some seasonal and pipeline effects). The fee businesses are the brighter spot, with wealth up 34% and capital markets up 145% YoY off a smaller base.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive. Current-year EPS consensus has edged up 0.6% over 30 days to $2.62; next-year consensus is $2.91, revised +1.5% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate has actually drifted down 1.1% over 90 days to $0.63. This is the profile of a normalizing post-deal franchise, not an accelerating one: the easy revision tailwind from Bremer accretion is largely behind the company, and forward growth now depends on organic loan demand and continued cost discipline. The earnings beat record (3 beats, 1 in-line over four quarters, with beats in the 1.5–3.6% range) supports confidence in consensus but does not point to large upside surprises.

The durable growth lever is the acquisition playbook. The $450M sub-debt raise plus a 13.71% total capital ratio gives ONB M&A firepower in a consolidating sector where scale increasingly dictates the ability to absorb technology and compliance costs. The track record of compounding TBV through deals is the cleanest evidence that the model works.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.85, ONB trades at ~12.3x trailing EPS of $1.79 and ~9.1x forward consensus of $2.62. Against the regional-bank peer set, this is a quality premium that is largely justified:

ONB trades above the peer median P/E of 10.2x, which is defensible given its 48.3% efficiency ratio (best-in-class), 18.4% normalized ROTCE, and diversified fee base. On forward earnings the gap narrows: ~9.1x forward is broadly in line with the cohort. The 2.4% dividend yield, with a conservative ~25% payout, is below the peer median.

The case against chasing here is that the stock is no longer cheap relative to its improving fundamentals. The recovery from the deal-accounting trough is now widely understood, and the multiple has expanded as the trough faded. Consensus targets (mean $27.90, median $28.00, low $25.00, high $30.00) imply ~17% upside to the mean, and the stock sits below even the low target of $25.00, suggesting the Street views the franchise favorably while the price has lagged. We treat that gap as real but not asymmetric: closing it requires continued clean integration and no CRE credit air pocket, and the analyst targets reflect the same recovery thesis the market is already pricing.

Cycle adjustment does not materially change the picture. This is not a trough-earnings stock; Q1'26 already reflects normalized ~18% ROTCE earnings power. The valuation is fair, with modest upside to consensus, but the margin of safety that would warrant a higher-conviction call is absent at this price. The cleaner entry is closer to tangible book ($13.93), which would put the stock in the low-$20s.

## Competitive Landscape

ONB competes in a consolidating regional-bank sector where scale is the dominant strategic variable. Its differentiation is a disciplined-acquirer playbook in attractive, less-saturated Midwest markets, paired with a low-cost, sticky deposit base. The Bremer deal gives it a credible claim as a dominant Upper-Midwest commercial bank, with exposure to stable Minnesota and Dakota economies that face more rational competition than coastal markets.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The efficiency ratio is genuinely top-tier and reflects the cost synergies that scale enables; the fee mix is more diversified than many similarly-sized peers. The principal competitive headwind is industry-wide: deposit-cost normalization and a mix shift toward interest-bearing deposits. Noninterest-bearing demand deposits declined $320M in Q1'26, the visible edge of that pressure and a margin headwind ONB shares with the sector.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on a higher rating, and several risks are specific, dated, and not fully priced.

**Bremer integration and purchase-accounting risk.** $1.9B of acquired loans were classified as purchased credit deteriorated, with ONB estimating $103.5M of contractual cash flows will not be collected. Net charge-offs on PCD loans jumped 244% YoY to $9.6M in Q1'26. Management states it is "impractical" to break out legacy Bremer's standalone results, which limits transparency into where credit is migrating. Fair-value and credit-mark assumptions on the largest deal in the bank's history could prove optimistic.

**CRE concentration and the refinancing cliff.** CRE is $22.2B, with concentration at 255% of Tier 1 plus ACL, high but below the 300% guideline. Office is $2.82B with $53.3M nonaccrual (the worst-performing category), and multifamily is $6.79B with $86.2M nonaccrual. The most concrete forward risk is the $779.5M of sub-4% non-owner-occupied CRE maturing within 18 months. As these reprice to current rates, office and multifamily borrowers could face debt-service-coverage stress, a dated catalyst that can pressure provisions in a specific quarter.

**Credit migration is occurring.** Criticized and classified assets rose to $3.22B from $3.11B in one quarter, and commercial "doubtful" loans grew. NCOs ticked up to 0.26% from 0.24%. Nonaccruals improved slightly to 1.03% of loans with ACL coverage of nonaccruals at a comfortable 112%, but the direction of classified assets, loans migrating into criticized buckets before becoming nonaccrual, is a leading indicator that warrants monitoring through the next two quarters.

**Interest-rate / AOCI risk.** The combined securities unrealized losses sit in equity; a renewed long-end rate spike would pressure TBV and could constrain capital flexibility.

These risks are why the recommendation stops at HOLD despite a high-quality franchise. They are quantified, dated, and capable of producing a specific quarter of provision pressure that the current valuation does not discount.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility compressed from 30.8% to 25.7% over the past week as the stock drifted down 2.6%. The falling IV indicates the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst or elevated near-term uncertainty, consistent with a post-earnings lull (last report 4/22/26) and no looming binary event. Short interest is 7.7% of float with 7.5 days to cover, which is moderate: uncrowded enough that there is no meaningful squeeze setup, but high enough to signal that some investors share the CRE-and-integration caution. Neither the IV nor the positioning argues strongly for or against the fundamental view; they are consistent with a fairly-valued bank between catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Integration continues cleanly, classified-asset migration stabilizes, and the bank sustains ~18% ROTCE and a sub-50% efficiency ratio. TBV compounds toward $15+, the office/CRE cliff reprices without material losses, and ONB uses its capital firepower for another accretive deal. The stock re-rates toward the consensus mean of $27.90–$30.00, roughly 17–26% upside, supported by continued buybacks near book value.

**Bear case.** The CRE refinancing cliff produces a wave of office/multifamily DSCR stress, classified assets continue migrating into nonaccrual, and provisions step up over two to three quarters. Combined with deposit-cost pressure compressing NIM and a renewed rate spike deepening the AOCI hole, normalized ROTCE falls back toward the low teens. The multiple compresses toward tangible book, taking the stock to roughly $19–20, about 16–20% downside.

The base case sits between these: continued solid execution with manageable credit migration, modest organic growth, and a stock that tracks earnings without significant multiple expansion. That is a fairly-valued, well-run bank, a hold rather than a buy at $23.85.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (expected late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to the trajectory of classified assets and the first repricing batches from the CRE maturity wall.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Classified assets decline or flatten for two consecutive quarters while NCOs stay below 0.30%, confirming the recent migration was noise rather than the leading edge of CRE stress.
- The stock pulls back toward tangible book (~$14/share area on price, i.e., low-$20s) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring the margin of safety.
- Next-year EPS revisions accelerate above +5% over 90 days alongside reaffirmed or raised loan-growth guidance, signaling an organic re-acceleration beyond Bremer accretion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material credit event in office/CRE: nonaccruals in the office book rising meaningfully above the current $53.3M, or a provision step-up well beyond the $30–35M quarterly run-rate.
- Evidence the Bremer credit marks were understated (PCD charge-offs continuing to accelerate, additional reserve builds tied to acquired loans).
- NIM compression accelerating below ~3.4% as the noninterest-bearing deposit mix shift continues, pressuring the earnings power that justifies the quality premium.

## Conclusion

Old National is a genuinely well-run regional bank that has executed its largest-ever integration without impairing tangible book value, the metric that matters most for a serial acquirer. Normalized profitability is top-tier, management is properly aligned through TSR/ROATCE-linked compensation and substantial share ownership, and capital return is conservative and well-covered. These are the attributes of a stock worth owning through the cycle.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: at $23.85 the stock trades at a quality premium to peers (above the 10.2x median P/E) with only ~17% upside to consensus, while carrying a dated, quantified CRE refinancing cliff, a 45%-of-loans CRE concentration with office as the visible soft spot, and classified assets that rose in a single quarter. That combination of a full-ish price plus a specific, near-term credit risk that the valuation does not discount is what prevents the upgrade. The franchise is improving and the prior trajectory (6.5 → 7.0) reflected real progress, but the gap between price and intrinsic value has narrowed to the point where the risk/reward is no longer asymmetric. We would upgrade on a pullback toward tangible book or on two quarters of stabilizing credit migration. For now, own it for the compounding rather than chasing it at this price.
