**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Travel + Leisure Co is a vertically integrated timeshare developer that sells vacation ownership interests, finances roughly half of those sales itself at a 14.6% weighted-average yield, securitizes the resulting receivables in non-recourse vehicles, and collects recurring property-management and exchange fees. The Vacation Ownership segment (~83% of revenue) is the growth engine; the Travel and Membership exchange business (~17%) is in structural decline. At $75.14, the stock has appreciated 52.7% over twelve months and trades within ~7% of its 52-week high. On adjusted forward earnings ($7.36 current-year consensus), the multiple is roughly 10.2x, a steep discount to the 30.8x peer median.

The investment case rests on a specific, identifiable mispricing: the $233M of resort-optimization charges taken in 2025 depressed GAAP net income (to $230M from $411M) while adjusted EPS rose to $6.34 from $5.75. Those charges are now converting into a 2026 margin tailwind, with Q1 2026 Vacation Ownership EBITDA up $32M YoY, partly from an $18M reduction in maintenance fees on unsold inventory. The CFO made an open-market purchase of 1,000 shares at $65.67 on April 23, the strongest insider signal available, and current-year estimates have risen 1.4% over 90 days. Goldman turned constructive on the timeshare sector on June 1, rating TNL a Buy.

The offset is concentrated in the consumer-credit book. The allowance for loan losses stands at $646M against $3.26B gross receivables (~19.8%), the Q1 provision rose to $100M, write-offs hit $117M, and management describes delinquencies as "remaining elevated over historical levels." With meaningful sub-700 FICO exposure, this subprime-adjacent receivable portfolio is the dominant downside vector. Combined with a 52% run that has erased the entry asymmetry and near-term options pricing acute event stress, I rate TNL a HOLD with a constructive lean. The fundamentals support the recent improvement in the underlying scores; the price does not yet support adding new capital.

## Company Overview

TNL (HQ: Orlando, FL) operates two segments. **Vacation Ownership** develops and sells timeshare interests, provides consumer financing on roughly half of sales, and manages resorts for homeowners' associations. This generated Q1 2026 revenue of $798M, up 5.7% YoY. **Travel and Membership** runs the RCI exchange network (the world's largest) and travel-club businesses, capital-light and high-margin, but generating Q1 revenue of $165M, down 8.3% YoY.

The business model captures economics at multiple points: the sale of the interest, the 14.6% loan yield on financed sales, the securitization spread, and recurring management and exchange fees. The recurring fee base and the fact that the majority of owners carry no loan provide resilience versus a pure-cyclical travel name. The company was spun out of Wyndham Worldwide and licenses the "Travel + Leisure" brand.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026:** Net revenue $961M (+2.9% YoY); net income $79M (+8%); diluted GAAP EPS $1.22 versus $1.07. Non-GAAP EPS came in near $1.45, roughly 10% above consensus, against in-line revenue. Adjusted EBITDA reached $225M (+11.4%), with Vacation Ownership EBITDA climbing to $191M from $159M. Gross VOI sales were $549M (+7.3%), tours rose 4.9%, and volume per guest rose 3.4% to $3,321 with a 69% owner-upgrade mix, a genuine sales-efficiency signal.

**FY2025 context:** GAAP net income fell on $233M of resort-optimization charges, while adjusted diluted EPS rose, confirming the GAAP decline was charge-driven rather than operational.

**Balance sheet:** Stockholders' equity is negative at -$1,023M. This is the cumulative effect of $7.03B of buybacks (164M treasury shares) against a smaller paid-in capital base; retained earnings remain positive at $2.45B. The deficit is a capital-return artifact rather than distress. Total debt is roughly $5.76B ($3.65B corporate + $2.11B non-recourse securitized). Covenants are comfortable: interest coverage 5.06x (minimum 2.0x), first-lien leverage 3.16x (maximum 4.25x). Ratings are Ba3 / BB- / BB+, all stable. Liquidity comprises $254M cash, $759M revolver availability, and $382M conduit capacity, with near-term maturities ($650M 6.625% notes due July 2026; $400M 6.00% notes due April 2027) manageable.

**Cash flow — the yellow flag.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $38M from $121M YoY, driven by an $84M inventory build and working-capital timing. The company returned $87M of buybacks plus $41M of dividends, so shareholder returns exceeded operating cash flow, funded by $177M of net revolver draws. Q1 is a seasonally heavy inventory and incentive quarter, so this is partly timing, but if delinquencies worsen, returns running ahead of cash generation becomes a discipline question.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is Vacation Ownership: gross VOI sales +7.3%, VPG +3.4%, tours +4.9%, and segment EBITDA up $32M YoY in Q1. The resort optimization payoff is the key incremental driver: by shedding 17 weak resorts through court-supervised HOA restructurings, TNL is removing maintenance-fee drag on unsold inventory, an $18M Q1 benefit that recurs. Management argues the timeshare value proposition strengthens during inflation as alternative lodging costs rise, a thesis the Q1 tour and VPG figures support.

The drag is structural. Travel and Membership revenue fell 8.3%, exchange transactions fell 12.1%, exchange membership declined 2.1%, and revenue per transaction fell 10.3% as mix shifts toward lower-value Travel Club. Management has flagged it may need to purchase inventory to supplement declining member-supplied exchange supply. This high-margin segment is shrinking and management has not yet articulated a fix.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year consensus rose to $7.36 from $7.26 over 90 days (+1.4%), with current-quarter and next-quarter estimates up 1.1% and 1.8% over 30 days. The earnings track record is solid: three beats in four quarters, with the most recent quarter +10.1%. These revisions are constructive but well below the +30% threshold that would mark a structural step-change; they confirm steady momentum, not acceleration. I weight growth as the most reliable forward dimension, and here it points to mid-single-digit revenue growth with margin uplift from optimization — a 6.3.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of ~21x is distorted upward by the 2025 GAAP charges. On adjusted/forward earnings the picture is materially cheaper: ~10.2x current-year and ~8.9x on $8.48 next-year. Against a peer median of 30.8x — HGV at 27.8x, WH at 30.8x, MTN at 31.2x, with VAC's GAAP P/E negative on its own charges — TNL trades at roughly a third of the cohort.

The discount is partially earned. TNL carries the highest credit-book risk in the group (subprime-adjacent receivables, elevated delinquencies), the negative-equity optic that trips screens, and a declining exchange segment. But the magnitude of the gap is hard to justify purely on those grounds when the business grew adjusted EBITDA 11% in Q1 and is unlocking a margin tailwind. A cheap multiple alone is not a buy signal; it requires an identifiable reason the market is wrong, and here there are three: the GAAP-to-adjusted distortion that screens miss, the optimization payoff now showing in segment EBITDA, and the CFO's open-market purchase. The analyst range of $74-$105 (mean $87) implies the consensus already sees ~13-37% upside.

I score valuation 6.8: genuinely cheap on forward earnings with identifiable catalysts, but capped below 7 because the credit overhang is a real reason for some of the discount, and because the 52% run has compressed the asymmetry from where it was at the prior report. The 3.1% dividend yield (raised ~7% to $0.60/quarter, with management targeting dividend growth at or above earnings growth) provides downside support.

## Competitive Landscape

TNL is one of two scaled pure-play timeshare developers, competing with Marriott Vacations Worldwide and Hilton Grand Vacations. Its competitive advantages are the vertically integrated financing model (loan yields captured in-house), the RCI exchange network (a genuine moat even as exchange volume declines), and a large recurring property-management fee base. The Vacation Ownership segment is gaining sales efficiency (VPG +3.4%, 69% upgrade mix), a positive trajectory signal. The exchange segment, however, is losing both members and transaction value, a deteriorating position in the smaller part of the business. On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the segment that matters most, which supports a 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer-credit deterioration (primary).** The allowance on gross receivables reflects a portfolio with meaningful sub-700 FICO exposure (~$487M in the 600-699 band, $166M below 600, $87M no-score). A consumer downturn hits both new sales and the existing book simultaneously. This is where downside surprises originate, not the balance sheet.

**Securitization dependence.** TNL must continuously securitize receivables (closed $325M at 5.11% in Q1, $950M in 2025). If markets seize or pools breach performance triggers, cash gets trapped as additional collateral. All pools are currently compliant, and the 2025 refinancings cut term-loan and revolver spreads, but this is a structural liquidity sensitivity.

**Resort optimization execution.** Court approvals for the 17-resort restructuring are pending, expected by year-end 2026, and failure is explicitly flagged as a forward risk. The HOA and member approvals are secured; the court gate is the remaining item.

**Exchange segment secular decline** and the **negative-equity optic** (benign in substance but trips Altman-style screens) round out the company-specific risks. The SEC matter — an agreement in principle to settle a loan-disclosure inquiry on a neither-admit-nor-deny basis with a $975,000 penalty — is small in dollars but thematically tied to the credit-quality sensitivity.

I score risk 5.2: the credit book is a real, partially-priced binary, and the cash-flow/buyback dynamic adds a discipline concern.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 75.8% versus 35.9% one-year historical vol, an extreme spread placing IV in the 83rd percentile of its chain, with steep backwardation (1-week 75.8% versus 6-month 34.9%). This prices imminent event stress: the nearest expiry sits one day out (June 18) with an implied ±3.5% move. Put/call skew is balanced (0.0%) and the OI ratio fell from 2.13 to 0.59 alongside the 21% price rise since mid-May, indicating put protection was unwound into the rally rather than fresh bearish positioning. The 6-month skew of -4.8% shows no structural collapse pricing. The market is not pricing a directional bet against the stock, but the rich near-term vol and backwardation argue against treating the current level as a clean entry. Short interest is moderate at 7.8% of float, 4.5 days to cover — neither a squeeze setup nor a confirmed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The optimization initiative completes court approval by year-end, locking in the maintenance-fee tailwind; Vacation Ownership continues mid-single-digit revenue growth with EBITDA leverage; the credit book holds without acceleration in write-offs; and the market re-rates TNL toward even a discounted fraction of the peer median. On $8.48 next-year EPS at ~14x, that is roughly $119; the analyst high sits at $105. Bull case ~$100-105 (+33-40%).

**Bear case.** Delinquencies accelerate, the provision steps up, new VOI sales slow as tour conversion weakens, and the negative-equity/cash-flow-below-returns dynamic forces buyback restraint. The optimization court approval slips. The stock de-rates toward the post-earnings April low. Bear case ~$60-62 (-18% to -20%).

**Base case.** Steady execution, optimization tailwind partially offsetting exchange decline, credit book stable but not improving, with the multiple staying compressed on the credit overhang. ~$82-85 over twelve months (+9-13%), supported by the 3.1% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) and the year-end 2026 court-approval milestone for the resort optimization plan are the two natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the CFO's $65-66 open-market buy level on unchanged fundamentals, restoring entry asymmetry.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-declining loan-loss provisions and flat-to-improving delinquency rates, removing the credit overhang.
- Court approval of the resort optimization plan, de-risking the 2026 EBITDA tailwind.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 loan-loss provision rising above ~$115M or write-offs accelerating beyond Q1's $117M pace, signaling consumer-credit deterioration.
- Gross VOI sales growth turning negative or VPG declining YoY, indicating demand rollover.
- Buyback suspension or covenant pressure as cash returns continue to outrun operating cash flow.

## Conclusion

TNL is a genuinely cheap stock on forward adjusted earnings (~10.2x versus a 30.8x peer median) with real, identifiable catalysts: a margin tailwind from the resort optimization plan, a CFO open-market purchase at $65.67, rising current-year estimates, and a sector that an external desk turned constructive on. The recent improvement in the underlying scores is justified by these developments. The trajectory from 5.6 to 6.2 to the current 6.15 reflects a business whose fundamentals are firming.

I stop at HOLD rather than BUY for three reasons. First, the subprime-adjacent receivable book ($646M allowance, elevated delinquencies) is the precise exposure to underwrite cautiously, and it is the dominant downside vector. Second, the 52% twelve-month run has erased the entry asymmetry that existed at the prior report; the stock is now ~7% off its high, not at the CFO's buy level. Third, near-term options price acute event stress that argues against deploying capital at the current level. This is a constructive growth_hold: existing holders are well-positioned for the optimization payoff and collect a 3.1% yield while waiting; new capital should wait for a pullback toward the low-$60s or for two quarters of clean delinquency data before treating the discount as a clear buy.
