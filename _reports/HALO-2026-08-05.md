## Executive Summary

Halozyme collects royalties, milestones and cost-plus API revenue from other companies' biologics franchises. Its rHuPH20 enzyme (ENHANZE) permits subcutaneous administration of high-volume biologics, and Halozyme funds none of the development, manufacturing, clinical or commercial cost of the ten approved partner products it earns on. The result is a ~57% adjusted operating margin on FY2025 revenue of $1,396.6M, capex under 1% of revenue, and $180.1M of operating cash flow in Q1 2026 against $150.0M of GAAP net income. Revenue grew 42.2% in Q1 2026 to $376.7M, with royalties up 43% and bulk rHuPH20 API sales up 172%. Management guides FY2026 to $1,710–1,810M revenue, $1,125–1,205M adjusted EBITDA and $7.75–8.25 non-GAAP diluted EPS after a January 28 raise that lifted the royalty line by roughly 25% in a single revision.

At $83.23, enterprise value is approximately $11.73B ($9.87B equity + $2.18B convertible principal − $0.32B cash and securities), or 10.1x 2026E adjusted EBITDA at the guidance midpoint and 8.3x the 2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $9.99. That is a conspicuously low multiple for a 25–30% grower with minimal capital intensity, and the reason is identifiable: an issued U.S. rHuPH20 patent expires in 2027, and Halozyme's contracts reduce royalty rates in a given country once rHuPH20 patent claims lapse there. The 10-Q states explicitly that Janssen's own DARZALEX subcutaneous formulation patents do not delay that reduction. DARZALEX royalties were 34.25% of Q1 2026 total revenue. The magnitude of the step-down is not disclosed in any filing, so every valuation of Halozyme past 2027 is an assumption about an undisclosed contractual term.

Three developments since the last review make that discount harder to argue against. In May 2026 the PTAB invalidated claims of two Halozyme modified-PH20 (MDASE) patents on written-description and enablement grounds, with twelve additional post-grant reviews pending, weakening the primary mechanism for extending economics past rHuPH20 and for taxing competing enzymes. Merck's Keytruda Qlex, co-formulated with berahyaluronidase alfa rather than rHuPH20, was approved in September 2025 and generated roughly $40M of 2025 sales, establishing that a large-cap pharma can bring a co-formulated SC biologic to market without Halozyme. And the company has disclosed that a Hypercon partner will decide by September 2026 whether to return a target, an outcome that could trigger a material interim impairment against roughly $853M of Elektrofi-related carrying value. Against a price 1.4% below the 52-week high and 4.2% below the $86.89 mean analyst target, this is a business worth owning at current levels but not one to add to at this price.

## Company Overview

Halozyme operates as a single reporting segment with four technology stacks:

**ENHANZE/rHuPH20** is the core franchise. The recombinant hyaluronidase degrades hyaluronan in the subcutaneous space, reducing resistance to bulk fluid flow and enabling SC delivery of biologics that otherwise require IV infusion (Herceptin SC in 2–5 minutes versus 30–90 minutes IV, DARZALEX FASPRO in 3–5 minutes versus multi-hour infusions). Halozyme earns upfronts, target nominations, development and sales milestones, bulk API supply revenue at cost plus a contractual markup, and average mid-single-digit royalties on partner net sales. Ten commercial products currently pay royalties: five Roche (Herceptin SC, MabThera SC, Phesgo, TECENTRIQ SC, OCREVUS SC), two Janssen (DARZALEX FASPRO, RYBREVANT FASPRO), and one each from Takeda (HYQVIA), argenx (VYVGART Hytrulo) and BMS (Opdivo Qvantig).

**Hypercon**, acquired via Elektrofi in November 2025 for $810.4M, hyperconcentrates biologics to reduce injection volume. Partners: Janssen, Lilly, argenx (pre-acquisition), plus Vertex (April 2026, $15M upfront) and Oruka (May 2026). **Surf Bio**, acquired December 2025 for $305M plus up to $100M of milestones, is a preclinical spray-dry/excipient approach targeting concentrations to 500 mg/mL. Both are supported by non-clinical data only; neither has an approved product. The **auto-injector business** from the 2022 Antares acquisition supplies Teva's generic epinephrine and teriparatide devices and, from December 2025, a Viatris selatogrel device. Proprietary products are Hylenex and XYOSTED.

The economic moat has three components. Patent coverage on rHuPH20 and modified PH20 enzymes is the piece under active attack. Two decades of regulatory validation across 100+ markets is durable but not exclusionary. Switching costs are the strongest leg: once a partner has co-formulated on rHuPH20, run pivotals and secured approvals across dozens of geographies, swapping enzymes requires new comparability work and filings to save a mid-single-digit royalty. None of the ten approved products has been reformulated away. That inertia protects the installed base; it does not protect new-target competition, where a partner selecting an enzyme today can shop.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2021 $443.3M → FY2022 $660.1M (+48.9%) → FY2023 $829.3M (+25.6%) → FY2024 $1,015.3M (+22.4%) → FY2025 $1,396.6M (+37.6%). The four-year CAGR is 33.2%, and growth accelerated in 2025 as a cohort of products launched 2020–2025 ramped simultaneously.

Q1 2026 revenue was $376.708M, up 42.2%. Royalties reached $240.681M (+43%), composed of DARZALEX $129.032M (+26%), VYVGART Hytrulo $46.283M (+119%), Phesgo $30.199M (+25%) and other $35.167M (+69%). Product sales were $130.424M (+67%), of which bulk rHuPH20 API was $73.497M (+172%). Collaborative revenue fell to $5.603M from $18.628M because the prior-year quarter contained a $15.0M sales milestone; this line is structurally lumpy.

**Margins compressed for identifiable mix reasons.** Q1 2026 operating income of $184.517M on $376.708M is a 49.0% margin, down from 53.4% a year earlier. Bulk API, priced at cost plus markup, rose to 19.5% of revenue from 10.2%, and royalties carry essentially no incremental cost, so mechanically a doubling of API share compresses the blend even with royalties compounding at 43%. Amortization rose 66% and R&D 73% on the Elektrofi and Surf Bio additions; SG&A rose 37% partly on patent litigation costs. Management's own 2026 guide names API as a growth driver, so this compression should be expected to persist. A less benign co-existing reading is that ~$60M of new Hypercon/Surf Bio operating investment plus litigation spend has structurally raised the cost base. Both explanations fit.

**FY2025 GAAP earnings were distorted.** Net income fell to $316.9M from $444.1M on 37.6% revenue growth because of a $284.887M acquired IPR&D charge for Surf Bio (asset acquisition, no alternative future use, expensed and not tax-deductible) and a $48.7M ATRS-1902 impairment. Adding those back gives roughly $640M of underlying earnings, up ~44%, and an adjusted operating margin near 57.5%. Two consequences follow. First, the Q4 2025 "EPS miss" of −110.7% ($2.24 estimate versus −$0.24 actual) compares a non-GAAP estimate against a GAAP actual carrying the IPR&D charge; it is a basis mismatch, not an operational miss. Second, any screen reading the XBRL R&D tag for FY2025 picks up $284.887M rather than the actual $81.49M of operating R&D, overstating R&D intensity by more than 3x.

**Cash conversion is high.** Q1 operating cash flow of $180.078M exceeded GAAP net income, supported by $29.512M intangible amortization, $16.637M SBC and $4.221M D&A. Capex was $3.741M, so free cash flow was approximately $176.3M. Working capital was a net ~$26M drag, partly offset by a $27.4M inventory release (finished goods $144.5M → $113.4M). Do not annualize: the inventory drawdown does not repeat indefinitely, API shipments are order-driven, and Q2 will include Vertex, GSK and Oruka upfronts. Receivables of $457.989M represent ~111 days of Q1 revenue, of which $298.116M is royalty receivable, a function of partners reporting 45–60 days after period end while Halozyme recognizes royalties on estimate. Management reports no material historical adjustments, but reported revenue does depend on estimation of partner sales.

**Capital structure: cheap, deliberately equity-light, with one timing issue.** Total convertible principal is $2,179.574M across four tranches with a blended cash coupon of 0.54% ($11.79M annually); Q1 interest expense was $5.508M against $184.5M of operating income. Against guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $1,125–1,205M and net debt of ~$1.86B, implied net leverage is 1.5–1.7x versus a 4.50x covenant maximum, and interest coverage is not a live question. The revolver is $750M undrawn to November 2030. Halozyme reported covenant compliance at 3/31/2026.

The screen-level Altman Z-Score of 1.51 and debt/equity of ~50.7x are artifacts of near-zero book equity ($48.8M at 12/31/2025, rising to $219.6M at 3/31/2026) created by retiring repurchased shares against retained earnings and charging roughly $280M of capped-call premiums to APIC. A business at 57% adjusted operating margins generating ~$700M of annual operating cash flow is not in solvency distress. The screen is, however, pointing at something real and mislabeled: $679.574M of 2027 and 2028 convertible principal is in the money at $83.23 and settles principal in **cash**. The 2027 notes ($209.575M, conversion price $77.17) become freely convertible September 1, 2026 and mature March 1, 2027; $208.743M has already been reclassified to current debt. Cash and securities are $318.6M. Coverage is adequate (cash plus the undrawn revolver plus ~$176M of quarterly free cash flow), but Halozyme will likely draw or refinance to retire the 2027s while executing $400M+ of buybacks. The November 2025 refinancing (raising $1.5B at 0.00% and 0.875% while retiring the majority of the 2027s and 2028s) demonstrates both access and that this issue was already largely pre-solved.

**Dilution is modest and often misstated.** Because principal is cash-settled, share issuance arises only from the conversion premium on in-the-money notes. At $83.23: 2027 notes contribute ~0.16M shares (conversion value 12.9576 × $83.23 = $1,078.44; premium $78.44; 0.9425 shares per note × 209,575 notes), 2028 notes ~2.70M (17.8517 × $83.23 = $1,485.79; premium $485.79; 5.8367 shares per note × 469,999 notes), 2031/2032 zero (conversion price $87.20 above spot). Total ~2.86M shares, 2.41% of the 118.613M outstanding, or 2.36% post-issuance ownership dilution. The 27.9M "anti-dilutive shares excluded" figure in the Q1 10-Q is a maximum-contingent envelope aggregating out-of-the-money options, unvested awards and if-converted notes under conditions that do not hold; reported Q1 diluted shares were 122,875K, of which only 1,723K came from converts.

## Growth Analysis

The FY2026 guide implies 23–30% total revenue growth and 30–35% royalty growth, with royalties crossing $1B a year earlier than previously projected. Management projects 15 partner programs in development by year-end 2026 and three or more new drug-delivery licensing agreements. Since the 10-K: a GSK global ENHANZE collaboration across multiple oncology targets including a first potential ADC application (May 2026); Vertex and Oruka Hypercon licenses; FDA approval of VYVGART Hytrulo with ENHANZE across all gMG serotypes; positive Takeda TAK-881 Phase 2/3 topline; EMA (CHMP) approval for DARZALEX SC self- or caregiver administration from the fifth dose, described as the first oncology injectable so approved in Europe; and a Takeda ENTYVIO ENHANZE agreement.

The composition of royalty growth matters. New products are ramping steeply (VYVGART +119%, other +69%) while Roche's oldest ENHANZE products (Herceptin SC and MabThera SC) are in price erosion, a drag management explicitly calls out. Non-DARZALEX royalties grew roughly 63% in Q1 while DARZALEX grew 26%. That composition is what supports 30–35% guided royalty growth, and it is also why concentration will fall with a lag rather than immediately.

**Two cautions temper the growth score.** First, guidance is materially back-half weighted. Royalty guidance of $1,130–1,170M against Q1 actual of $240.681M implies $296–310M per quarter for Q2–Q4, 23–29% above Q1. Total revenue guidance implies $444–478M per quarter, 18–27% above Q1. There are concrete reasons for the skew (Q1 payer and gross-to-net resets, order-driven API timing, and three upfront payments landing in Q2 or later), so this is a sensitivity rather than evidence of unachievability. It does mean the Q2 and Q3 prints carry more information than usual, and Q2 results are due within days.

Second, consensus estimates are flat, not rising: current quarter −0.1% over 90 days, next quarter −1.8%, current year +0.1%, next year +1.2%. There is no earnings-inflection revision signal here. Large upward 90-day revisions paired with accelerating revenue are absent; what exists is a stable consensus of $8.10 for 2026 and $9.99 for 2027 against a guidance midpoint of $8.00. Analysts are, if anything, marginally above the company for 2026 and are modeling ~23% EPS growth into 2027.

The durability question sits beyond the guidance window. Management asserts "durable revenue growth well into the 2040s," supported by co-formulation patents and Hypercon/Surf Bio IP into the mid-2040s. The counter-evidence is that the EPO Opposition Division revoked two Janssen DARZALEX SC co-formulation patents (March 2023, October 2024), so partner-side co-formulation IP is not a reliable backstop, and the hyperconcentration platforms have no approved products.

Peer comparison is close to uninformative: of five market-cap-proximate biotechnology peers, four have negative earnings (NUVL −20.4x, CYTK −10.1x, PTGX −61.3x, SMMT −8.2x) and only KRYS carries a positive multiple at 43.1x. Halozyme is a profitable, cash-generative royalty licensor inside a peer set of clinical-stage developers, so the "peer median P/E 43.1x" comparison overstates the discount. The more relevant benchmark is other royalty and milestone business models, which have de-rated broadly; part of Halozyme's low multiple may be a sector observation rather than a company-specific signal.

Two important caveats on the apparent cheapness. First, the headline multiples use non-GAAP denominators. Halozyme's non-GAAP EPS definition excludes share-based compensation ($16.637M in Q1 alone, roughly 4.4% of revenue), intangible amortization, debt-discount amortization and one-time charges, and excludes convertible dilution from the share count. SBC is a real cost to shareholders. On GAAP, the trailing multiple is 32.5x. Part of the gap between 10x and 32x is definitional rather than a durability discount.

Second, and decisively: the discount has a specific, rational cause. The 10-Q describes the royalty mechanic precisely: royalty terms run to the later of a specified period or expiry of the last valid claim of Halozyme's rHuPH20 patents or specified collaboration patents, and when no valid claim covers the product in a country, "the royalty rate is reduced" upon expiry of the rHuPH20 patents. An issued U.S. rHuPH20 patent expires in 2027; EU patents run into 2029. Janssen's DARZALEX SC formulation patents do not delay the reduction. No filing discloses the reduced rate, the per-partner terms or the per-country schedule. A 10x forward EBITDA multiple on a 25–30% grower is the market applying a terminal-value haircut to an undisclosed contractual term, and I cannot demonstrate the market is wrong about it because the input is not public.

Cheapness alone is not a buy case; it requires a specific catalyst or a demonstrable mispricing. The available near-term catalysts (Q2/Q3 prints validating the H2 step-up, $400M+ of 2026 buybacks, new licensing agreements) support the earnings power that is already in consensus. They do not resolve the terminal-value question. Meanwhile the two developments that bear directly on durability (PTAB invalidations, an approved competing hyaluronidase) both cut against it.

I therefore score valuation modestly below neutral at 5.7. Base case fair value is $93 (9.3x the 2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $9.99, just above the $90 median analyst target), implying +11.7% from $83.23. A cross-check on EV/EBITDA supports a slightly higher figure (10x an assumed ~$1.40B of 2027 adjusted EBITDA less ~$1.86B net debt is roughly $103/share), but that cross-check ignores the likelihood that the multiple compresses as the 2027 patent date approaches, which is why I anchor to the EPS-based number.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural backdrop, the industry's IV-to-SC conversion wave, remains intact and favorable. Converting an infused biologic compresses chair time from hours to minutes, shifts care out of infusion centers, and can create a separately patented presentation that defends franchise revenue as the IV form loses exclusivity. Halozyme is the most commercially validated enabling technology for that conversion.

The competitive position is nonetheless deteriorating at the margin, which is why I hold this dimension at 5.5 rather than restoring the 7.5 of earlier reviews.

**Halozyme no longer has the field to itself.** Keytruda Qlex, approved September 2025, is co-formulated with berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph, not rHuPH20, and generated roughly $40M of 2025 sales. Keytruda is one of the industry's largest franchises facing IV loss of exclusivity late this decade, so the SC conversion Halozyme is *not* participating in is very large. The originator of berahyaluronidase alfa is not confirmed from any source I would rely on; treat the competitor's identity as unverified and its existence as established.

**The enzyme-level barrier is patent-based, and the patents are losing.** Halozyme's response to Qlex was litigation asserting its MDASE portfolio. On May 12, 2026 the PTAB found claims of U.S. 11,952,600 unpatentable for lack of written description and enablement; on May 18 it reached the same conclusion on U.S. 12,152,262. Institution was denied on U.S. 12,371,685 via a bulk discretionary order without reaching the merits, so that patent's survival is procedural rather than a merits win. Twelve additional PGRs are instituted and awaiting decisions, and one district court case is live (D.N.J. 2:25-cv-03179). The §112 rationale is structural (the claims encompassed on the order of 10^63 modified polypeptides against roughly 6,000 disclosed single-replacement examples), which suggests read-through to family members sharing the specification. That read-through is a hypothesis, not a finding, and Final Written Decisions are appealable to the Federal Circuit.

Two offsets matter. Halozyme is the plaintiff seeking incremental royalties, not a defendant facing liability, so an adverse outcome means foregone upside rather than a cash outflow, consistent with Item 3 of the 10-K and Item 1 of the 10-Q stating no proceeding whose adverse outcome would be materially adverse, and with risk factors left unchanged in Q1 2026. And the current economics at stake are small (~$40M of Qlex sales). The strategic stake is large and long-dated; the near-term P&L stake is not.

**Concentration is flat, not improving.** DARZALEX was 34.6% of FY2025 revenue and 34.25% of Q1 2026 revenue, essentially unchanged, and 53.6% of Q1 royalties. True Janssen dependence is higher still, since Janssen also pays RYBREVANT FASPRO royalties and holds a Hypercon license. Mix is genuinely broadening, and mathematically sustained sub-total DARZALEX growth will reduce its share with a lag. But the diversification narrative and the concentration data currently point in different directions, and DARZALEX is precisely the product whose royalty step-down is explicitly not protected by Janssen's own patents. Partner termination rights compound this: ENHANZE collaborations may be terminated by the partner for any reason on 90 days' notice, and Chugai has already terminated its program.

**The offset is real.** Hypercon signed Vertex and Oruka *after* the acquisition, and GSK signed an ENHANZE collaboration in May 2026 including a first potential ADC application. The platform can win business under Halozyme's ownership, and management dates the hyperconcentration IP into the mid-2040s. That is the strongest argument for durability, and it rests on non-clinical data.

## Risk Assessment

**The 2027 royalty step-down (unquantified).** The single most consequential unmodeled variable. Mechanism disclosed, magnitude not. Applied against 34% of revenue with no partner-patent protection.

**September 2026 Elektrofi impairment trigger (dated, company-disclosed).** A Hypercon partner is evaluating returning a target, with a decision expected by September 2026; a return without a replacement of similar cash flows could produce a material interim impairment. Exposure at 3/31/2026: customer relationships $458.424M net (58% of the $810.4M Elektrofi price, underwritten on cash flows from Janssen, Lilly and argenx), Hypercon developed technology $224.335M, trade name $4.877M, Elektrofi goodwill $165.502M, approximately $853M. Purchase accounting remains preliminary. Any charge would be non-cash: it would not affect operating cash flow, the EBITDA-based net-leverage covenant, or the non-GAAP metrics management is compensated on. It would depress already-minimal book equity and degrade equity-based screens. The company disclosed the trigger proactively with a specific date.

**Twelve pending PGRs.** Sequential adverse decisions through late 2026 would further narrow the post-rHuPH20 patent option.

**Convertible cash settlement.** $679.6M of 2027/2028 principal in the money and cash-settleable; 2027 notes freely convertible September 1, 2026 against $318.6M of cash. Manageable with the $750M revolver, but it competes with $400M+ of intended buybacks.

**Single-source manufacturing.** Bulk rHuPH20 comes from two single-source CMOs, Avid Bioservices and Catalent Indiana. With API at ~19.5% of revenue and the enzyme embedded in ten approved products, an interruption hits product sales and partner royalties simultaneously.

**Drug pricing.** CMS's September 2025 final guidance preserved favorable fixed-combination treatment for IPAY 2028 but stated an intent to address the policy in rulemaking beginning with IPAY 2029. The favorable treatment is settled only through 2028. The May 2025 most-favored-nation Executive Order is a sector-wide overhang.

**Balance-sheet composition.** Goodwill plus intangibles are 57.4% of total assets against $219.6M of book equity. A large impairment would not breach covenants but would swing GAAP results sharply.

**Immunogenicity.** Anti-rHuPH20 antibodies detected in Takeda's HYQVIA trial and a former partner's Phase 2 study, with no associated adverse events per the 10-K. Monitorable.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.6% sits 10.8 points above 30.8% one-year realized volatility, with a 69th-percentile IV reading within the chain and a ±7.1% implied move on the August 21 tenor, consistent with an imminent Q2 print. Term structure is flat (41.6% near versus 41.9% at six months), so the market distributes uncertainty evenly rather than concentrating it in the event, which fits a business whose central question (the 2027 step-down) is long-dated rather than quarterly.

Positioning is call-tilted, not defensive: put/call volume ratio 0.07, put/call open interest 0.29, and skew of −5.8% at one month described as call-heavy. ATM IV also fell from 44.9% to 41.6% while the stock rose 7.4% since the mid-July snapshot, which is complacency rather than accumulating protection. That sits awkwardly against 14.5% short interest and 6.7 days to cover. The combination of heavy short base, call-heavy options, and price at the 52-week high is a squeeze-prone setup into the print, and it argues against initiating short exposure. It equally argues that options are not offering cheap protection ahead of the September Elektrofi decision. For a holder, IV rich to realized favors writing calls over buying puts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($115, +38.2%)

Royalties compound through the H2 step-up and 2026 lands at or above the $1,710–1,810M guide, with VYVGART Hytrulo's all-serotype label and DARZALEX self-administration in Europe adding to a base already growing 43%. The Hypercon partner retains its target or nominates a replacement, removing the September impairment overhang. Federal Circuit appeals or narrower family members preserve enough MDASE coverage to matter, and the pending twelve PGRs do not all fall. New licensing wins (GSK ADCs, three or more new agreements in 2026) establish that hyperconcentration IP running into the mid-2040s can carry economics past rHuPH20. Management executes the $1B buyback against a ~2.4% dilution envelope while net leverage stays near 1.5x at a 0.54% cash coupon. In that world the terminal-value haircut narrows and 11.5x 2027 EPS of $9.99 is defensible.

### Bear Case ($62, −25.5%)

The Hypercon partner returns its target and Halozyme takes an interim impairment against a meaningful portion of $853M of carrying value, pushing GAAP equity toward zero and validating the screens. Sequential PGR losses close off the modified-PH20 route, and one or more partners begin evaluating alternative hyaluronidases for new targets now that Merck has proven an alternative can clear FDA. The 2027 step-down proves steeper than assumed on DARZALEX, and because the magnitude was never disclosed, the market prices the worst plausible case. Q2 or Q3 fails to deliver the required 23–29% sequential royalty step-up, guidance is trimmed, and a stock at the 52-week high with 14.5% short interest and call-heavy positioning unwinds toward the $58.68 low. Roche's mature products keep eroding while the new cohort's ramp decelerates.

### Base Case ($93, +11.7%)

Guidance is met on the strength of Q2–Q4 upfronts (Vertex, GSK, Oruka), API shipments and label expansions; 2026 non-GAAP EPS lands near $8.00–8.10 and 2027 near consensus $9.99. The Hypercon target decision is resolved without a catastrophic charge or with a non-cash charge the market discounts as such. The 2027 converts are retired with revolver capacity, and buybacks absorb roughly 4–5M shares. The terminal-value discount persists, since nothing in the next twelve months resolves it, so the multiple stays in the 9–10x range on rising earnings, and the stock grinds toward the $90 median analyst target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a positive twelve-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the base case of +11.7% against a bear case of −25.5% does not clear the bar for new capital at a price 1.4% below the 52-week high and 4.2% below the mean target.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Q2/Q3 validation of the H2 step-up.** Royalties of $296M+ in a single quarter (Q2 or Q3) with FY2026 royalty guidance reaffirmed or raised, confirming the required 23–29% sequential step is delivering. Q2 results are due within days.
2. **Resolution of the September 2026 Hypercon target decision** with the partner retaining or replacing the target, or a disclosed non-cash impairment accompanied by reaffirmed guidance and no reduction in Hypercon partner count.
3. **Any disclosure that quantifies the post-2027 royalty step-down** (an investor-day slide, a call answer, or a filing amendment) showing a reduction of less than roughly 30% on the DARZALEX rate. This single input would justify a materially higher multiple.
4. **Price toward $70–72** on unchanged fundamentals, which would take the stock to roughly 7x 2027 consensus EPS and restore the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **A partner terminating an ENHANZE program on a material approved product**, or public evidence that a top-five partner is evaluating an alternative hyaluronidase for a new target.
2. **Adverse Final Written Decisions in the majority of the twelve pending PGRs**, closing off the modified-PH20 route, combined with dismissal or adverse ruling in the D.N.J. case.

## Conclusion

Halozyme is a high-quality cash generator: 57% adjusted operating margins, sub-1% capital intensity, 0.54% blended cash cost of debt, ten royalty-bearing products, and revenue growing 42% in the most recent quarter. It trades at roughly 10x forward EBITDA and 8.3x 2027 consensus earnings, which looks like a mispricing until you locate the reason. The reason is an undisclosed contractual royalty step-down triggered by a 2027 U.S. patent expiry, applied against a product that supplies 34% of revenue and whose partner's own formulation patents explicitly do not delay the reduction. Nothing in the last quarter improved that picture: the PTAB invalidated two of the patents that were supposed to extend economics past rHuPH20, twelve more reviews are pending, and a competing hyaluronidase is already commercial in one of the industry's largest franchises.

The offsetting evidence is real: GSK, Vertex and Oruka signed in the last four months, hyperconcentration IP dates to the mid-2040s, VYVGART is compounding at triple digits, and a $1B buyback is authorized against a ~2.4% dilution envelope. But the two nearest dated events are asymmetric to the downside: a September 2026 impairment decision against ~$853M of carrying value, and a Q2 print that must begin delivering a 23–29% sequential royalty step-up while implied volatility sits 11 points above realized and positioning is call-heavy at the 52-week high. Estimates are flat, not rising, so there is no inflection signal to lean on.

Own the compounding but do not pay the high of the year for a business whose terminal value depends on a contractual term the company has not disclosed. HOLD at $83.23, base case $93, with $70–72 the price at which the risk/reward becomes compelling and the H2 royalty step-up the evidence that would let me pay more.
