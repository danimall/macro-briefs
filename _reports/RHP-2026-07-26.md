**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ryman Hospitality Properties owns a differentiated portfolio of seven large-scale group and convention destination resorts (five Gaylord Hotels, two JW Marriott resorts) plus a ~70% controlling interest in Opry Entertainment Group (OEG), which houses the Grand Ole Opry, Ryman Auditorium, Ole Red, and a growing amphitheater and live-entertainment business. The convention-hotel format is genuinely defensible; few properties in the country combine 400,000+ square feet of contiguous exhibit space with the rooms, food-and-beverage, and self-contained amenities needed to host large national conventions, which gives RHP multi-year forward booking visibility that most lodging REITs lack.

The defining near-term development emerged through news in June 2026 rather than the last 10-Q. Management has confirmed an active strategic review of OEG, with Morgan Stanley engaged, roughly ten inbound interested parties over the past year, and a range of outcomes on the table including a minority-partner transaction, a partial or full sale, or spinning OEG into a stand-alone public company. Executive Chairman Colin Reed has framed this around the observation that "the investor in a REIT is very different to an investor in an entertainment business," a sum-of-the-parts case. The stock has risen roughly 34% year-to-date to a fresh 52-week high near $133, and the OEG-monetization narrative appears to be the main driver.

The problem is that this catalyst is now substantially priced. Underneath the headline +13.2% Q1 revenue growth (almost entirely from the Desert Ridge acquisition), same-store group room nights traveled fell 4.1%, same-store occupancy dropped two points, and growth was defended through rate rather than volume. Consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window, leverage is high (~$3.97B debt against ~$771M equity at a rising 6.4% blended rate), and the shares trade at ~35x trailing earnings near their high. We rate RHP a HOLD (growth hold). The OEG optionality is real and worth owning through, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced, and we would look for a better entry or clearer catalyst progression before adding.

## Company Overview

RHP is a REIT specializing in group-oriented, meetings-focused destination hotels — a niche within lodging REITs. The core portfolio comprises:

- **Five Gaylord Hotels** (Marriott-managed): Opryland (Nashville), Palms (Orlando), Texan (Dallas), National (Washington D.C./National Harbor), and Rockies (Denver), each with 400,000+ square feet of meeting and convention space.
- **Two JW Marriott resorts**: Hill Country (San Antonio, acquired 2023) and Desert Ridge (Phoenix, acquired June 2025 for ~$865M), each with 240,000+ square feet of meeting space.
- Two overflow hotels (Inn at Opryland, AC Hotel National Harbor). Total portfolio of ~11,869 rooms.
- **~70% controlling interest in Opry Entertainment Group** — the Entertainment segment: the Grand Ole Opry (roughly 100 years old), Ryman Auditorium, WSM-AM, the Ole Red venues (Blake Shelton), Category 10 (Luke Combs), Block 21 (Austin), Southern Entertainment (festivals), and newly managed amphitheaters (Ascend in Nashville, CCNB in South Carolina). Minority partners are Atairos/NBCUniversal via a 2022 transaction.

Marriott manages all hotels under base and incentive fee arrangements (2%–3.5% of gross revenue); RHP owns the real estate. The Gaylord "everything under one roof" model is largely without direct public-market peers, and the OEG brands are irreplaceable cultural assets that provide diversification atypical for a hotel REIT.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended 3/31/2026):**
- Total revenue **$664.6M, +13.2% YoY** (from $587.3M), driven almost entirely by Desert Ridge (+$73.9M) and ADR gains.
- Operating income **$137.8M (+18.7%)**; net income to common **$70.5M**; diluted EPS **$1.03** (vs $1.00).
- Adjusted FFO **$156.1M (+19.3%)**; Adjusted EBITDAre **$219.3M (+18.2%)**.
- Operating cash flow **$169.2M** (vs $98.2M) — a genuine strength.

The same-store picture is materially softer than the headline. Excluding Desert Ridge, occupancy fell 2.0 points to 67.7% and group room nights traveled declined 4.1%. Growth was rate-driven: same-store ADR +5.1% but same-store RevPAR only +2.1% and Total RevPAR +2.8%. Property-level results diverged sharply: Opryland (+16.5% revenue) and Palms (+10.5%) were strong, while Gaylord National fell 8.2% (occupancy -9.4 points, partly Winter Storm Fern and a weak D.C./National Harbor market), JW Marriott Hill Country fell 9.0%, and Gaylord Texan fell 3.5% on ongoing rooms renovation. Group mix slipped to 78% from 80%.

The forward-booking data is the offset the bulls lean on: net definite group room nights booked +18.1% same-store (+37.9% including Desert Ridge), with group room nights on the books for all future years +2.0% and ADR on those bookings +5.4% higher. The central debate is whether this converts into travel or reflects demand pull-forward amid macro caution.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$3.97B against total equity of ~$771M — very high leverage, though not abnormal for a levered hotel REIT. Debt is 88% fixed-rate after swaps at a 6.4% weighted-average rate, and interest expense rose 18.1% YoY to $64.1M as acquisition-related notes came online. The maturity ladder is well-termed, with nothing due until July 2028. Liquidity is ample: revolver capacity raised to $850M with ~$930M total revolver availability, plus $424M unrestricted cash at quarter-end. The net leverage covenant ceiling is 7.25x and the company was in compliance. Property and equipment net is ~$5.0B, and the company is running a heavy capex program (~$113.7M in Q1, full-year 2026 guided $235–335M) within a $1B+ multi-year investment plan through 2027.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.20 (from $1.15), roughly $76.2M per quarter, consistent with the REIT requirement to distribute at least 100% of taxable income. At the current price the yield is ~3.7%, comfortably covered by AFFO. The 2026 refinancing swapped $700M of 4.75% notes due 2027 for $700M of 5.75% notes due 2034, extending maturity but raising the coupon 100 bps and booking a $2.2M extinguishment loss. Combined with the $625M of 6.50% notes issued for Desert Ridge, the cost of debt is rising, a structural earnings headwind on this capital structure as debt reprices over time.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three components. First, the acquisition engine: Desert Ridge is the single biggest driver of YoY growth and is performing well (Q1'26 occupancy 73.0%, ADR $489.75, Total RevPAR $863.95 — the highest-rate asset in the portfolio). Second, the forward group bookings, which show demand being priced up (+5.4% ADR on the books) with room-night volume ahead of prior years. Third, OEG expansion: the group began managing Ascend and CCNB amphitheaters in early 2026, with Category 10 Las Vegas opening late 2026 and CityWalk Orlando plus a seventh Ole Red slated for late 2027.

Against this, the organic signal is decelerating. Same-store volume is declining while the business defends revenue through rate, which is characteristic of a late-cycle lodging environment. OEG operating income collapsed 58.8% YoY to $4.3M — largely a tough comp against the prior-year Opry 100th anniversary plus weather, but it underscores that the entertainment segment is lumpy and not a stable annuity.

Consensus estimates are moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS has been revised from $1.39 to $1.31 over 90 days (-5.8%), next-quarter from $0.66 to $0.60 (-9.1%), and next-year from $4.96 to $4.73 over 30 days (-4.5%, and -2.2% over 90 days). The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over four quarters, including a +26.5% beat in Q1'26 but an -8.9% miss the prior quarter. That combination of falling estimates and inconsistent delivery argues against extrapolating the strong forward-booking headline into confident 2026-2027 growth.

## Valuation Assessment

RHP trades at ~35x trailing earnings and a ~3.7% dividend yield at ~$133, near its 52-week high of $133.75. On a FFO basis the multiple is more reasonable — Q1 AFFO annualizes toward a mid-single-digit-teens P/AFFO, which is the metric that matters for a REIT — but even there the shares are no longer cheap after a 34% year-to-date advance.

Peer context: the Hotel & Resort REIT median P/E is ~25.1x with a 3.1% yield. RHP screens as expensive on trailing P/E, though the comparison is muddied by the fact that several peers carry negative or extreme multiples (PK -13.2x, PEB -22.3x, SHO 107.2x) that reflect their own earnings distortions. The cleaner read is that RHP commands a premium to the group, justified in part by its differentiated convention niche and its multi-year forward booking visibility, but that premium is now fully reflected.

The valuation case rests on two things. The OEG sum-of-the-parts unlock is the genuine upside argument: separating a live-entertainment business (which a growth-oriented buyer may value on entertainment multiples rather than a REIT cap rate) from the hotel real estate could surface value the market underweights in a REIT wrapper. But the stock has already re-rated on precisely this narrative; the shares moved from ~$94.65 to a high near $134 as the review became public. Much of the potential unlock is embedded in the current price, and the downside if the review stalls or Colin Reed resists a clean sale (his stated attachment to protecting the Opry brand is a real execution risk) is a re-rating back toward the low-to-mid-$100s.

Analyst targets bracket the current price closely — low $120, mean ~$131, median $130, high $149 — with the stock trading essentially at the mean. On a stock at ~35x trailing with falling estimates, we treat proximity to consensus as a signal that the easy re-rating has happened rather than as a floor. We would be more constructive on a pullback that restores asymmetry, or on concrete evidence the OEG transaction advances toward a signed structure. At current levels the valuation dimension scores below neutral: cheapness is not the issue, but neither is there a clear reason the market is underpricing the equity today.

## Competitive Landscape

RHP's moat is physical and durable. The Gaylord convention format — contiguous large-scale exhibit space plus rooms and self-contained amenities in a single property — faces extreme barriers to entry and has few direct public-market comparables. Group customers book multi-year in advance, which gives the business unusually good forward revenue visibility for lodging. The OEG brands, led by the century-old Grand Ole Opry, are irreplaceable and provide a diversification lever that pure hotel REITs cannot replicate.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the asset base (Desert Ridge added a top-rate property; the renovation program at Texan and Hill Country is a self-inflicted but temporary occupancy drag that should reverse). Management's relative TSR has outperformed the lodging-REIT peer set (cumulative TSR 160.06 vs peer 137.83 in 2025). The main structural dependence is on Marriott, which manages all hotels and whose operating execution RHP relies on entirely; the management agreements also impose debt limitations (75% LTV, 1.2x debt-service coverage on pooled hotels). Gaylord National in D.C. is a persistent soft spot (operating income -34.3%) with potential sensitivity to federal-government and association travel budgets, which bears watching given the current federal spending environment. On balance the competitive position is a strength — a defensible niche with improving asset quality — which is why this dimension scores above the portfolio's other lines.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Group-demand softening.** Same-store group room nights traveled fell 4.1% in Q1'26 with occupancy declines at three of seven hotels. The business is a bet on discretionary, cyclical corporate and association convention spending; management repeatedly flags geopolitical uncertainty, inflation, and trade policy as risks to group customers.
2. **High financial leverage.** Interest expense is rising and each refinancing at prevailing rates pressures earnings on a thin equity base. The Altman Z-Score screens in the distress zone (~0.9), which is expected for a levered hotel REIT but flags that leverage deserves scrutiny.
3. **Geographic and asset concentration.** Only seven core hotels; a weather event (Winter Storm Fern in January 2026) or regional weakness (Gaylord National) materially moves consolidated results.
4. **OEG review execution uncertainty.** The strategic review is a two-sided catalyst — a sum-of-the-parts unlock on the upside, but with real risk that the process stalls, produces a suboptimal structure, or is slowed by the Executive Chairman's stated attachment to the Opry brand. No transaction is signed.
5. **Renovation drag.** Ongoing rooms renovations at Texan and Hill Country are actively depressing occupancy — temporary but real near-term.
6. **Entertainment earnings volatility.** OEG operating income swung down 58.8% YoY, underscoring the lumpiness of the segment being shopped.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent, not defensive. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.0% against 24.2% trailing realized — modestly rich but at only the 18th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (1.6%), the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 29.0% below six-month 31.1%), and the implied one-month move is ±6.4%. Short interest is low at 4.8% of float with 2.9 days to cover. Nothing in the derivatives or positioning data signals informed bearish pressure ahead of a catalyst; the market is pricing an orderly path, consistent with a stock that has already re-rated on the OEG narrative rather than one bracing for a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$149, roughly +12% from current).** The OEG strategic review advances to a value-realizing transaction — a stand-alone spin or partial sale that surfaces entertainment-multiple value the market underweights inside the REIT. Forward group bookings convert into travel, restoring same-store volume growth. Desert Ridge continues to lead the portfolio, renovation drags at Texan and Hill Country reverse, and rate discipline sustains AFFO growth in the high teens. Interest-rate relief eases the refinancing headwind on the levered balance sheet.

**Bear case (~$105–110, roughly -17% to -21%).** The OEG review stalls or disappoints, and the ~34% YTD run partially unwinds. Same-store group demand keeps decelerating as macro caution and federal/association travel budgets weigh (Gaylord National extends its weakness), and the rate-led growth model runs out of room as occupancy erodes. Estimates keep falling, and the market re-rates a highly levered, thin-equity REIT with rising debt costs back toward peer multiples. Refinancing at higher coupons compresses AFFO per share.

**Base case (~$133–138, roughly flat to +4%).** The OEG review continues without a signed transaction near-term, keeping the optionality intact but unrealized. Same-store growth stays rate-driven and modest, Desert Ridge accretion supports headline growth, the dividend remains well-covered at ~3.7%, and the stock consolidates near its recent range as the market waits for either catalyst resolution or evidence that forward bookings convert.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is the OEG monetization catalyst plus a defensible convention-hotel franchise, but the catalyst is substantially priced and same-store fundamentals are decelerating, so the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric at ~$133.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (updated same-store group metrics and forward bookings) and any 8-K or press update on the OEG strategic review, the single largest swing factor for the equity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A signed or well-structured OEG transaction (spin terms, minority-partner deal, or sale) that credibly surfaces sum-of-the-parts value above the current implied valuation.
- Same-store group room nights traveled returning to positive YoY growth (converting the forward-booking strength), alongside a turn in estimate revisions from negative to positive.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s that restores asymmetry while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The OEG review terminated with no transaction, combined with the stock still trading near its high.
- Same-store group demand deteriorating further (occupancy declines widening beyond the two-point Q1 drop) with continued negative estimate revisions.
- A covenant or refinancing signal — leverage approaching the 7.25x ceiling, or a materially higher-cost refinancing that compresses AFFO per share.

**Next natural reassessment:** The upcoming earnings release and any interim update on the Morgan Stanley-led OEG process.

## Conclusion

RHP is a high-quality, differentiated lodging REIT with an irreplaceable entertainment franchise, and the active OEG strategic review is a legitimate value-realization catalyst that has driven a strong year-to-date run. That said, the equity now trades near its 52-week high at ~35x trailing earnings with the catalyst largely priced, estimates broadly falling, same-store group demand decelerating, and a highly levered balance sheet whose debt cost is rising. The forward-booking strength is encouraging but unproven until it converts to travel, and the OEG process carries genuine execution and timing uncertainty. We rate RHP a HOLD (growth hold): worth owning for the OEG optionality and the defensible niche, but not a name to add at the current price. We would become constructive on a pullback that restores asymmetry or on concrete progress toward a signed OEG structure.
