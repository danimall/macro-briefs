## Executive Summary

Flex has moved from a mid-single-digit-margin contract manufacturer to a business where 28% of revenue and roughly 44% of segment income come from data-center power and cooling. The Cloud & Power Infrastructure (CPI) segment grew from $3,244M of revenue in FY2024 (12% of sales) to $6,614M in FY2026 (24%), and reached $2,202M in the June 2026 quarter at a 9.7% segment margin against 5.2% in Integrated Technology Solutions and 6.6% in Regulated Manufacturing Solutions. The June quarter was a step-change: revenue +21% to $7,928M, gross margin 9.4% from 8.7%, adjusted EPS $1.00 versus $0.72, and FY2027 guidance raised to $33.7-35.2B of revenue (+23% at midpoint) and $4.42-4.74 of adjusted EPS (+39% at midpoint). Consensus current-year EPS has risen 28.8% in 90 days to $4.72, above the guide midpoint, and the company has beaten in each of the last four quarters by 2.7% to 8.6%.

The stock has gone the other way. It peaked at $166.86 on June 3 and closed at $121.35, a 27.3% drawdown, with the sharpest leg between July 15 and July 29 — the AI-complex cost-of-capital repricing rather than anything Flex reported. Management's own reference points sit well above the current price: Q1 FY2027 RSUs carried an average grant price of $151.88, and the executive team sold in the open market at $141.65-$149.61 in mid-June. At $121.35 Flex trades at 26.5x the FY2027 adjusted guide midpoint and 25.7x consensus $4.72, against a peer group (JBL 50.5x, FN 50.0x, SANM 52.3x, TTMI 101.8x trailing) whose median trailing P/E is 50.5x. Flex's own trailing GAAP multiple is ~47x on TTM diluted EPS of about $2.59.

The offsetting evidence is specific and material. Free cash flow was $41M on $285M of net income in Q1 (14% conversion) versus $1,052M on $880M for FY2026, driven by a $(149)M working capital swing, capex up 77% to $236M, and a $608M inventory build. The FY2027 midpoint requires H2 revenue of ~$18.4B, or ~$9.21B per quarter — 13.7% above the Q2 guide midpoint — in a fiscal year the company itself describes as weakest in the March quarter. One customer reached 12% of sales in Q1, the first time any customer has crossed 10% in at least three fiscal years, with top-10 concentration at 49% versus 37% in FY2024. And the separation removes the CEO, CFO, COO and General Counsel to SpinCo, with the parent's CFO seat still unfilled as of July 30. We rate the shares ACCUMULATE: base case $135 (+11%), bull $170 (+40%), bear $85 (-30%), with a probability-weighted expected return of roughly +6% and a dated catalyst path in September and November.

## Company Overview

Flex is a Singapore-incorporated, Austin-headquartered manufacturer operating more than 100 facilities in about 30 countries, roughly 27 million square feet of capacity, and approximately 150,000 employees (FY2026: 149,686). Three reportable segments as of Q1 FY2027, with CPI created as a separate segment in Q4 FY2026:

| Segment | Q1 FY27 revenue | Margin | FY26 revenue | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated Technology Solutions (networking, satcom, enterprise, lifestyle) | $3,056M (38%) | 5.2% | $11,109M (40%) | 5.4% |
| Regulated Manufacturing Solutions (industrial, auto, healthcare) | $2,670M (34%) | 6.6% | $10,191M (36%) | 6.0% |
| Cloud & Power Infrastructure (integrated compute, liquid cooling, utility/rack/embedded power) | $2,202M (28%) | 9.7% | $6,614M (24%) | 9.2% |

On May 5, 2026 Flex announced its intent to separate CPI into an independent public company, targeted for Q1 calendar 2027. Conditions include board and shareholder approval, approval of the High Court of Singapore, and SEC effectiveness of SpinCo's Form 10, which management expects to file in September 2026 along with the distribution ratio. Post-separation Flex would be ITS plus RMS; SpinCo takes the owned power-product IP (critical and embedded power), while Flex retains contract manufacturing of energy infrastructure and its advanced networking franchise. Operational separation waves are scheduled for August 31, September 28 and November 2, 2026, involving roughly 30 new operating legal entities, a logically rather than physically separated ERP, and an approximately 18-month transition services agreement.

Mexico is the largest revenue country at $2,201M (28% of Q1 sales) and holds 37% of net PP&E, making USMCA nearshoring a structural asset and the peso the largest hedged exposure within $8.2B notional of FX derivatives.

## Financial Analysis

| ($M, FYE March) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 30,346 | 26,415 | 25,813 | 27,914 | 7,928 |
| Gross margin | 7.5% | 7.1% | 8.4% | 9.2% | 9.4% |
| GAAP operating margin | 3.9% | 3.2% | 4.5% | 4.9% | 4.9% |
| GAAP net income | 793 | 1,006 | 838 | 880 | 285 |
| Diluted shares | 462 | 441 | 398 | 378 | 374 |

Two structural facts support the equity. Gross margin widened 210bp and operating margin 170bp from FY2024 to FY2026 on revenue that remains below FY2023 — mix (CPI, industrial, healthcare displacing lifestyle) plus restructuring ($87M FY2026, $86M FY2025). And diluted shares fell 19% over three years, with FY2026 repurchases of about $0.9B at an average price of $49.08, roughly 40% of today's price. Per-share results have compounded faster than absolute results.

**Non-GAAP composition.** Q1's $142M of adjustments comprise stock-based compensation $51M, intangible amortization $23M, restructuring $1M, and $67M of "legal and other" (of which $53M is spin cost, $14M acquisition cost); the $46M divestiture gain is correctly excluded. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.76 is 24% below the $1.00 adjusted figure. SBC grew 50% year over year and annualizes to roughly $0.54 per share, a genuine cost to shareholders. Any multiple applied to adjusted EPS should be haircut relative to what one would pay for equivalent GAAP earnings; we account for this below.

**Cash conversion is the weak link.**

| | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $1,505M | $1,685M | $399M | $276M |
| Capex | $438M | $633M | $133M | $236M |
| Free cash flow | $1,082M | $1,052M | $268M | $41M |

We do not annualize $41M. Q1 is seasonally weakest, absorbed $24M of separation cost in operating cash flow, and consolidated EPP's working capital from May 1. But the FY2026 conversion of ~120% deserves the same scrutiny in the other direction: accounts payable rose from $5,147M (March 2025) to $8,055M (March 2026) to $9,195M (June 2026), roughly 115 days of payables against Q1 cost of sales of $7,181M. A material share of FY2026's "high-quality" cash generation was payables expansion, which is not repeatable indefinitely. Layer on capex running at a $900M-1.0B annual pace and a working capital build scaling with 23% revenue growth, and FY2027 free cash flow plausibly lands well below FY2026 despite much higher earnings. Flex has not guided FY2027 free cash flow; any estimate is analyst-derived.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $2,840M, long-term debt $5,219M at a 4.8% weighted average rate, net debt $2,379M. Against FY2027 guided adjusted operating income of ~$2,446M plus D&A of roughly $560-600M, adjusted EBITDA near $3.0B implies ~0.8x net leverage; on trailing GAAP EBIT plus D&A ($1,825M) it is ~1.3x. Liquidity includes an undrawn $2.75B revolver and an unused commercial paper program. Nothing is due in fiscal 2027; $2,349M matures in fiscal 2028 (the $1,450M term loan in November 2027, $500M delayed-draw in December 2027, $399M 6.000% notes in January 2028). The timing lines up with the separation, and the standard mechanic would be SpinCo raising debt and remitting proceeds — but Flex has not stated an intended source of repayment, so that linkage is inference. If the spin slips, $2.3B requires refinancing at prevailing rates without separation proceeds; with an undrawn revolver and investment-grade access, that is a cost issue rather than a solvency issue.

A screening Altman Z of 2.21 ("grey zone") reflects book equity substituted for market equity in the fourth term. Recomputed on the June balance sheet with market equity, Z is approximately 3.09 — marginally in the safe zone. Given $2.8B cash, an undrawn revolver, zero fiscal 2027 maturities and stated covenant compliance, distress risk is not the issue here; the specific covenant thresholds are undisclosed, which limits downside leverage modeling.

Two conditional financing sources are worth naming: $1.7B of non-interest-bearing customer working capital advances (down $0.1B in the quarter) and $1.5B of customer-controlled inventory held in other current assets (up $0.2B). Roughly $3.2B of working capital is customer-funded or customer-titled — a real competitive advantage during a ramp, and contractual rather than permanent. Supplier finance is small and cleanly disclosed at $134M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is strong and confirmed on multiple independent measures:

- **Guidance raised, not reaffirmed.** FY2027 revenue guidance moved to $33.7-35.2B from $32.3-33.8B; adjusted EPS to $4.42-4.74 from $4.21-4.51. Q2 guidance of $7.95-8.25B was reported as roughly 3% above consensus.
- **Estimate revisions.** Current-quarter +19.5%, next-quarter +34.5%, current-year +28.8%, next-year +72.9% over 90 days, with 30-day revisions still positive across all four periods. Current-year $4.72 sits above the guide midpoint of $4.58 — analysts are underwriting a beat, consistent with four straight beats.
- **Segment momentum.** CPI grew 38% in FY2026 (Cloud & Cooling +29%, Power +61%) and reached $2,202M in Q1. External reporting from the earnings call puts the FY2027 CPI growth target at 65-75%; that figure does not appear in the press release or the 10-Q, so treat it as unverified.

Three qualifications temper how much of this growth is worth paying for.

**Revenue quality.** Flex discloses that elevated DRAM/NAND pricing is a driver of ITS revenue growth and simultaneously the offset to ITS margin improvement, and that it passes cost through where contracts permit. It does not quantify the effect. Raw materials inventory rose $637M in one quarter to $5,471M, now 85% of total inventory — directionally consistent with price and pre-buy effects. Tariffs, by contrast, are sized: ~1% of both revenue and cost of goods, with negligible profit impact. An unknown share of the 21% Q1 growth and the 23% guided growth is zero-margin revenue that reverses if memory normalizes.

**Back-half loading.** Q1 actual $7.928B plus the Q2 midpoint $8.10B leaves ~$18.42B for H2, or ~$9.21B per quarter (+13.7% versus the Q2 guide). On margin, H1 adjusted operating income of ~$1,084M is 6.76% of H1 revenue; the FY2027 7.1% midpoint requires ~$1,362M in H2, about 7.40%. The 10-K describes seasonality as strongest in fiscal Q2/Q3 with fiscal Q4 weakest. A March quarter holding near $9B would break that pattern. Part of the step-up is contractually supported — EPP consolidates for eleven of twelve months versus two in Q1, and a $44M two-year backlog intangible was recognized — but the CPI ramp implied (~$2.93B per quarter for three quarters versus $2.202B in Q1) is a ~33% sequential step.

**RemainCo is the slower asset.** ITS plus RMS generated FY2026 revenue of $21,300M at a 5.67% segment margin growing ~1.4%. In Q1 FY2027 the same pair produced $5,726M (+15.7%) at 5.83%. The bull case requires that acceleration to be durable — AI networking pull-through plus industrial strength — rather than a memory-inflated quarter.

| EV ($47.2B) / FY2027 revenue midpoint | 1.37x |
| EV / FY2027 guided adjusted operating income | 19.3x |
| PEG (26.5x on ~39% guided adjusted EPS growth) | ~0.68 |
| Dividend yield | 0.0% |

Against peers at a 50.5x median trailing P/E, Flex screens inexpensive on forward adjusted earnings and roughly in line on trailing GAAP. The PEG of 0.68 and the 28.8% current-year revision are the pattern that historically precedes further re-rating, and the forward multiple is well below the level at which rising estimates stop mattering.

Two adjustments keep us from treating that as a full BUY case.

First, **next-year consensus of $7.11 cannot be applied naively.** The separation is targeted for Q1 calendar 2027, inside fiscal Q4 2027. FY2028 estimates therefore mix analysts modeling a combined entity with those modeling RemainCo, and the +72.9% 90-day revision partly reflects CPI ramp assumptions rather than a clean per-share path for the continuing FLEX share. The 17.1x that $7.11 implies is only defensible as a valuation of the *package* a buyer today receives (FLEX plus SpinCo shares). We weight the unambiguous current-year revision (+28.8%, measurable against guidance) far more heavily.

Second, **cash earnings are materially below adjusted EPS this year.** Adjusted $4.58 less ~$0.54 of SBC, ~$0.25 of amortization and spin costs likely exceeding $200M ($0.53) puts FY2027 GAAP EPS nearer $3.20-3.50, or roughly 35-38x. With free cash flow likely well under $1.0B against a $44.8B market cap, the FCF yield during the ramp is 1-2%. That argues for a multiple in the high-20s on adjusted earnings, not the 35-50x the peer screen implies.

**Sum of the parts** (assumptions stated; not a forecast). FY2027 CPI revenue at ~$11.2B at a 9.5-10.0% segment margin gives $1.06-1.12B of segment income; RemainCo at ~$23.25B at ~6.0% gives ~$1.40B (the $2.5B sum reconciles to $2.45B of guided adjusted operating income after corporate). Applying 25-35x to SpinCo and 12-14x to RemainCo produces EV of $43.3-58.8B, equity of $40.9-56.4B after $2.38B of net debt, and $111-153 per share. The midpoint is ~$132. Today's $121.35 sits in the lower half of that range, and the entire spread is a function of the multiple assigned to SpinCo.

**Base case $135.** 29x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.65 (between the guide midpoint and consensus) equals $134.85; the SOTP midpoint of ~$132 corroborates. Upside 11.3%.

Analyst targets bracket the range at $142 low, $160.50 mean, $180 high, with 11 of 11 ratings at Buy or Strong Buy and no holds. On a stock at 26.5x forward with rising estimates, those targets are more floor than ceiling, but they were largely set on a pre-spin combined entity and before the AI-complex de-rating; we treat them as corroboration rather than as an independent input.

## Competitive Landscape

Flex's moat in ITS and RMS is scale, geographic optionality and within-program switching cost, not pricing power — 5.2-6.6% segment margins on 1.27x asset turnover describe a business earning its return on velocity. CPI is different: 9.2-9.7% margins reflect owned IP in critical and embedded power (Crown Technical Systems, EPP) and direct-to-chip liquid cooling (JetCool). Cumulative goodwill of $1,831M against $25.2B of assets confirms this has been bolt-ons plus large organic investment, not a roll-up.

The competitive set for CPI is dedicated infrastructure OEMs rather than other contract manufacturers, which is both the source of the margin and the source of a new risk. The FY2026 10-K sharpened this: CPI demand is concentrated among "a limited number of hyperscale cloud providers, colocation companies, large enterprise data center operators" with substantial negotiating leverage, some of which "have developed or may develop internal design, engineering and manufacturing capabilities that compete with" Flex.

Customer concentration moved against the diversification story in exactly the quarter it peaked. No customer exceeded 10% of sales in FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026. In Q1 FY2027 one customer reached 12%, majority in CPI, and top-10 concentration rose to 49% from 45% (FY2026) and 37% (FY2024). End-market mix has diversified; the customer base has concentrated, and the segment driving the re-rating is the source of both. The customer is unnamed; the Amazon warrant and CPI attribution make Amazon plausible but unconfirmed. Post-spin, that concentration travels with SpinCo.

Post-spin Flex retains advanced networking (high-speed switches, optical, NICs) and contract manufacturing of energy infrastructure, which preserves data-center exposure without the power IP. Management asserts the two companies "shouldn't be coming across each other in the same opportunities."

## Risk Assessment

**Back-half execution (highest probability).** The ~$9.21B quarterly H2 run-rate and 64bp of H2 margin expansion against a stated weak-Q4 seasonal pattern is the most likely source of a negative revision. A miss also converts the $608M inventory build into a problem.

**Memory pass-through reversal.** Unquantified by the company. If DRAM/NAND normalizes, revenue growth decelerates mechanically while gross margin percentage improves — a mix that would compress the revenue multiple even as margins optically improve.

**Single-customer and in-sourcing risk.** A 12% customer in the highest-margin segment, with the 10-K explicitly flagging hyperscaler internal capability development.

**Customer-specific capital equipment.** A newly added risk factor: Flex is deploying customer-dedicated capital with limited alternative use, showing up in capex ($633M FY2026 +45%; $236M in Q1 alone). Program cancellation creates impairment exposure.

**Separation execution.** Roughly 30 new legal entities, a logically separated ERP, an 18-month TSA, Singapore court and shareholder approvals, and a Form 10 still to be filed. Q1 spin costs of $53M plus $14M acquisition costs are running hot, and management concedes "there are areas where things are taking longer than we planned initially." The Nextracker separation left an ongoing dispute over tax distribution obligations — direct precedent worth watching in the CPI separation agreements.

**Governance transition.** The CEO, CFO, COO and General Counsel all move to SpinCo; the parent is led by an internal promotion (Michael Hartung) with the CFO seat unfilled as of July 30. That is a thin senior bench for RemainCo, defensible as resource allocation to the entity that must build systems from scratch, but a real open item.

**Amazon warrant.** Up to 3,859,851 shares at $51.29, cashless, expiring August 2030, expensed as a revenue deduction ($7M FY2026, $3M Q1). Maximum contingent dilution is 1.05% of shares outstanding (1.03% post-issuance ownership dilution) — small. The consequential item is the 10-Q language that on consummation of an "acquisition transaction… which may include a distribution to shareholders, subject to a specified condition," the unvested portion vests in part or in full. Only 0.1M shares had vested at June 26. The condition and the underlying agreement are not public. Full acceleration at today's price would transfer roughly $270M of intrinsic value and generate a corresponding charge — immaterial to a $44.8B cap, but unmodeled. The September Form 10 should clarify.

**Site and geopolitical risk (realized).** A missile strike on the Mukachevo, Ukraine facility on August 21, 2025 produced $51M of FY2026 charges.

**Revenue recognition mix.** Over-time recognition rose from 28% to 42% of revenue in a year (CPI over-time from $61M to $672M), with contract assets up 30% to $1,386M in the quarter. This increases estimation judgment in reported revenue. Deloitte issued an unqualified FY2026 opinion and a clean interim review, and controls were concluded effective; the observation concerns estimate sensitivity, not any identified problem.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 66.7% against 63.9% one-year realized is fair (2.8pp spread), and IV sits at the 9th percentile within the current chain — options are not pricing crisis, and near-dated optionality is inexpensive relative to the chain. Term structure is in contango (66.7% at 1m, 73.1% at 6m), which is the normal, non-stressed configuration and places the higher implied uncertainty around the spin window rather than the next print. Put/call skew is balanced (1.9% at 1m, -1.7% at 6m). Put/call open interest of 1.24 is modest, though near-term put/call *volume* of 9.13 at the August 21 tenor is heavily one-sided and worth noting as either hedging into the September Form 10 or spread construction; it is not corroborated by skew or open interest, so we do not read direction from it. Since May 27, IV compressed 5.1pp while the price fell 16.4% — a de-rating without a volatility panic, consistent with multiple compression across the AI complex rather than a company-specific event. Short interest of 2.3% of float and 0.8 days to cover means no crowded short and no squeeze dynamic. The nearest expiry prices a ±10.5% move; the six-month tenor prices ±32.1%.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($170, +40%)

Flex delivers the H2 ramp, FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $4.74 high end, and the September Form 10 removes the two largest uncertainties by disclosing SpinCo's capital structure and the distribution ratio. The November 10 Investor Day supplies standalone financial frameworks for both entities. SpinCo — a ~$11B revenue data-center power and cooling company growing at the externally reported 65-75% rate with ~10% segment margins and owned IP — re-rates toward dedicated infrastructure OEM multiples rather than EMS multiples, while RemainCo's Q1 acceleration (+15.7% revenue, 5.83% margin) proves durable on AI networking pull-through and industrial strength. At 35-40x SpinCo segment income and 13x RemainCo, sum-of-parts EV reaches $55-60B, or roughly $145-155 per share; 24x combined FY2028 consensus of $7.11 gives $171. The mechanism for the upper end is the SpinCo multiple, nothing else. Supporting evidence: four consecutive beats, guidance raised rather than reaffirmed, 11 of 11 analysts positive with a $142 low target.

### Bear Case ($85, -30%)

The H2 guide is not met. A March quarter consistent with Flex's own stated seasonality — rather than 13.7% above the Q2 midpoint — takes FY2027 adjusted EPS toward $4.30-4.40 and forces the first negative revision in five quarters. Memory prices normalize, removing a chunk of revenue growth and exposing the $6,453M inventory position. The 12% customer moves work in-house or shifts pricing, and CPI's implied growth halves. Free cash flow stays near $200-400M for the year, so the market applies a cash-earnings multiple instead of an adjusted-EPS multiple. Spin costs and complexity push completion past Q1 CY2027, leaving $2,349M of fiscal 2028 maturities to refinance without separation proceeds, and RemainCo trades as what it is — a low-single-digit-growth EMS at ~6% margins on 10-12x segment income. SpinCo at 15x and RemainCo at 10x gives EV of ~$30B and roughly $76 per share; 18x a reduced FY2027 adjusted EPS gives $85. We anchor bear at $85.

### Why the drawdown does not invalidate the thesis

The 27.3% decline from $166.86 coincided with rising guidance and rising estimates, and the CFO attributed it on July 30 to Flex being "thrown into the AI bucket." That is a multiple event, not an earnings event — corroborated by IV compressing 5.1pp during the decline, skew staying balanced, short interest at 2.3%, and no negative revision at any tenor. The genuine fundamental deterioration in the period is narrower and specific: Q1 cash conversion at 14%, customer concentration rising to 12%/49%, and the vacant CFO seat. Those justify a lower risk score and a staged entry, not avoidance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected return with a dated catalyst path, but the margin of safety at 26.5x forward adjusted earnings with 1-2% free cash flow yield during the ramp is below the BUY bar.

- **Base case: $135 (+11.3%).** 29x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.65 = $134.85; sum-of-parts midpoint ~$132. Timeframe 12 months.
- **Bull case: $170 (+40.1%).** SpinCo re-rates at 35-40x segment income with H2 delivered; equivalently 24x combined FY2028 consensus $7.11.
- **Bear case: $85 (-30.0%).** H2 miss plus memory normalization; 18x a reduced FY2027 adjusted EPS, corroborated by a 15x/10x sum-of-parts at ~$76.
- **Probability-weighted expected return:** ~+6% at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear.

**Staged entry plan.** Establish or add roughly half of a target position at current levels; reserve the balance for $105-112 (approximately 24x the FY2027 guide midpoint and near the July 29 intraday low of $99.50-103.02 area), or add on confirmation rather than weakness if the September Form 10 discloses SpinCo leverage and a distribution ratio without adverse surprises. Avoid concentrating purchases into the August 21 expiry window, where ±10.5% is priced.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) the fiscal Q2 print on or around late October shows revenue at or above $8.25B with adjusted operating margin ≥7.0% and Q3 guidance implying H2 revenue ≥$18.4B; (2) free cash flow turns positive above $300M in fiscal Q2 with inventory growing slower than revenue; or (3) the price approaches $105-110 with FY2027 guidance intact.

**Downgrade to HOLD if:** (1) FY2027 revenue or adjusted EPS guidance is trimmed at the October print; (2) current-year consensus revisions turn negative for two consecutive months; or (3) the Form 10 discloses a SpinCo capital structure that leaves RemainCo carrying the fiscal 2028 maturities without an identified repayment source.

**Move to REDUCE/AVOID if:** (1) the 12% customer is disclosed as reducing volumes or in-sourcing; (2) the separation is delayed beyond mid-2027 or abandoned; (3) fiscal Q2 free cash flow is negative with inventory rising again, indicating the H2 ramp is not converting; or (4) a material weakness or restatement emerges from the over-time revenue recognition shift.

**Reassessment dates:** September 2026 (SpinCo Form 10 and distribution ratio), late October 2026 (fiscal Q2 results), November 10, 2026 (Investor Day and standalone frameworks for both entities).

## Conclusion

Flex is a genuine beneficiary of data-center capex, trading at 26.5x FY2027 adjusted guidance against ~39% guided adjusted EPS growth, with current-year consensus up 28.8% in 90 days, four consecutive beats, and a separation catalyst that clarifies in September and November. The 27% drawdown from the June high occurred while guidance rose, and the volatility complex shows multiple compression rather than distress: IV fair to realized, balanced skew, 2.3% short interest.

The reasons to size in stages rather than all at once are equally specific. Free cash flow conversion fell to 14% in the June quarter while accounts payable expanded to roughly 115 days, so cash earnings run well below adjusted EPS during the ramp. The FY2027 guide requires an H2 that runs 13.7% above the Q2 midpoint in a fiscal year whose fourth quarter Flex itself calls weakest. Customer concentration reached 12% for a single account and 49% for the top ten. And the parent enters the separation with its CEO, CFO, COO and General Counsel departing to SpinCo and the CFO seat unfilled. Overall score 6.3, ACCUMULATE, base case $135 with a $105-112 add zone and a bear case at $85.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the "44% of segment income from CPI" figure is unsupported (CPI segment income is ~$213M of ~$547M total Q1 segment income, ~39%, not 44%); the specific hyperscaler capex corroboration (Amazon +69%, Microsoft Azure +43%, Caterpillar) was not in the research brief and has been removed as an unverified fact supporting the growth thesis; the "44% of segment income" also appeared in the Executive Summary and has been left flagged rather than silently altered given its use as a headline thesis point.
