## Executive Summary

Stepan delivered its best quarter in three years. Q2 2026 net sales rose 15% to $684.1M, gross margin expanded 250bp to 14.6%, adjusted EBITDA rose 45% to $74.4M, and adjusted EPS of $1.18 beat the $0.62 consensus by 91.5%. Reported diluted EPS of $1.00 versus $0.50 confirms the improvement is not a non-GAAP artifact. Net leverage fell to 2.5x from 2.9x a year earlier. Project Catalyst — the February 2026 restructuring targeting ~$100M of pre-tax savings — is running at $18-20M per quarter with management targeting a $22M quarterly exit rate, and roughly 88% of the target is already executed or announced. Capital expenditure is guided to $100-110M against ~$128M of depreciation, the first year in this cycle that capex falls below D&A, and a contracted $30M sale of non-productive land adjacent to the Millsdale plant is expected to close in the second half.

Two prior reports on this name carried an AVOID rating, and the stock has risen 20.8% since the June review. That call was wrong on the near-term direction, and the fundamental improvement is real. The rating moves up accordingly. What has not changed is the entry arithmetic, and the arithmetic is now worse rather than better. Consensus FY2026 EPS rose 6.1% over 90 days while FY2027 was cut 5.8% and the two nearest quarterly estimates were cut 22.0% and 37.6% — the re-rating is multiple expansion, not an earnings-power revision that keeps pace with the price. Management itself discounted the quarter: CEO Rojo attributed $5-10M of the $74.4M Q2 adjusted EBITDA to customer pre-buying triggered by the Iran conflict, said "you should expect a slightly lower Q3 versus Q2 because the $74 million is inflated," declined to call margins normalized, and gave no formal guidance. CFO Velasquez added $4-5M of second-half polymer turnaround costs.

Underneath the quarter, the structural picture is unchanged. Gross margin has compressed 383bp from 15.40% in 2022 to 11.57% in 2025 and the derived Q1 2026 gross margin was 10.72%; FY2025 adjusted EPS of $1.82 was the trough of a five-year series; FY2025 free cash flow of $25.4M covered only 0.70x of the ~$36M dividend; FY2025 ROE was 3.9% on a $2.36B asset base. The single most consequential unresolved item is $344.0M of Credit Agreement borrowings — the $264.0M revolver draw plus an $80.0M delayed-draw term loan, roughly 53% of total debt and 23% of market capitalization — maturing June 24, 2027, with only $81.4M of remaining facility availability and no disclosed refinancing, extension or replacement. At 9.4x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, 24.2x FY2026 consensus EPS and 1.22x book, base-case fair value sits roughly 7% above spot against roughly 31% bear-case downside. Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Stepan is a Delaware corporation producing intermediate and specialty chemicals sold entirely business-to-business across three segments. Surfactants (71% of FY2025 sales, $1,666.0M; $67.4M segment operating income, −21% YoY) supplies cleaning, wetting, foaming and emulsifying agents into laundry and household cleaners, personal care, germicidal quaternaries, agricultural emulsifiers and oilfield chemicals from thirteen sites — five U.S., two European, five Latin American, one Asian. Polymers (25%, $584.5M; $43.3M, +7%) makes polyurethane polyols for rigid-foam insulation, polyester resins, and phthalic anhydride, with Millsdale (Elwood, Illinois) housing the sole U.S. PA plant. Specialty Products (4%, $81.7M; $25.6M, +23%) is small but carries a 31.4% operating margin producing flavors, emulsifiers and solubilizers, primarily at Maywood, New Jersey.

The Item 1 self-description — "one of the leading merchant producers of surfactants in the world" — carries the key qualifier. Stepan sells to other manufacturers rather than consuming internally, which means several of its largest customers possess make-versus-buy optionality. The risk factors state this in the past tense: customer internal manufacturing capability "have resulted and may result, at times in the Company losing business." Headcount was 2,328 at year-end 2025 versus 2,396 the prior year.

FY2025's headline operating income improvement of $8.1M does not survive decomposition. Segment operating income *fell* $10.9M (−7%), with Surfactants down $18.3M. Corporate expenses fell $19.0M largely on $15.9M of one-time asset-sale gains (Philippines $5.1M, Lake Providence $10.8M) plus non-recurrence of a $6.8M fraud charge, partly offset by a $6.2M Mexican goodwill impairment. Management's own adjusted metrics confirm the direction: adjusted net income fell to $41.7M from $50.5M, and adjusted EPS to $1.82 from $2.20 — a 17% decline in a year revenue grew 7%. Adjusted EBITDA margin was flat-to-lower (8.53% versus 8.58%).

The cause is identifiable and largely self-inflicted. North America Surfactants gross profit fell $20.0M (−20%), with $19.8M attributed by the MD&A to startup expenses at the new Pasadena, Texas alkoxylation facility, higher oleochemical costs, and an Elwood environmental reserve adjustment. The same project ended capitalized interest income (net interest +56%, or $7.9M) and added $13.8M of depreciation. Pasadena delivered a triple squeeze on FY2025 earnings and a $60.0M federal NOL carryforward via bonus depreciation.

### FY2026: real inflection, unevenly distributed

Q2 2026 was strong across every line: sales +15% ($54.4M price/mix, $17.9M volume, $17.1M FX), gross margin +250bp, operating income +107%, adjusted EBITDA +45%. Segment adjusted EBITDA: Surfactants $54.9M (+59%), Polymers $31.2M (+22%), Specialty $6.5M (−3%). Organic volume grew 6% excluding the Philippines divestiture and a U.K. product-line exit.

The half-year picture is materially less flattering. Subtracting Q2 from six-month figures yields Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $49.7M, **down 13.6%** year over year on a 10.72% gross margin, versus Q2 up 45% on 14.6%. The CFO acknowledged that part of the Q2 improvement was the reversal of Q1 Asian production-timing problems, unquantified. Combined with the $5-10M pre-buy pull-forward and $4-5M of H2 turnarounds, annualizing Q2 ($74.4M × 4 = $298M) would overstate run-rate earnings by 20-25%.

Building H2 2026 from disclosed components — H2 2025 base of $90.0M, plus roughly $40M of incremental Catalyst savings at the disclosed run rate, less the $5-10M pull-forward, less $4-5M of turnarounds, with a range for underlying volume and margin — frames H2 at approximately $105-125M and FY2026 adjusted EBITDA at **$229-249M**. That is a scenario assembled from company disclosure, not guidance; management provided none.

### Cash flow and the balance sheet

Cash generation is the weakest part of the file. FY2025 free cash flow was $25.4M (OCF $147.9M less capex $122.5M) against approximately $36M of dividends — 0.70x coverage — and operating cash flow *declined* year over year as working capital swung from a $5.8M source to a $15.8M use. H1 2026 free cash flow was approximately **negative $29M** (OCF $25.3M, capex $54.3M), with Q2 alone at −$15M after a $58M working-capital build. Receivables consumed $104.3M and inventories $24.2M in the half. The commitment to full-year positive free cash flow therefore rests entirely on a second-half working-capital release that has not yet occurred.

At 6/30/2026: cash $113.7M, total debt $647.4M, net debt $533.7M, equity $1,212.0M, net debt/(net debt + equity) 31%. Current maturities of debt rose to **$403.3M** from $285.7M, and the current ratio fell to 1.15x from 1.29x. Company-reported net leverage of 2.5x cross-checks cleanly: $533.7M ÷ 2.5 implies covenant EBITDA of ~$213.5M against independently computed TTM adjusted EBITDA of $214.1M (FY2025 $198.9M − H1'25 $108.9M + H1'26 $124.1M), agreement within 0.3%. Against the 3.50x maximum, TTM adjusted EBITDA could fall to roughly $152M before breach at constant net debt — a 29% cushion. The $750M minimum net worth covenant has a $462M cushion, which is why the impairments are not solvency-relevant.

The maturity structure is the problem. $344.0M sits in facilities maturing June 24, 2027 with $81.4M of remaining availability. The most recent debt Stepan issued (May 2025) priced at 6.17% against legacy private-placement notes at 2.30%-2.83%. Refinancing is very likely achievable at 2.5x leverage with an unencumbered asset base, but repricing matters: 250bp on $344M is $8.6M pretax, roughly $0.28 of after-tax EPS at a 25% rate; matching the ~340bp spread implied by the 2025 issuance costs closer to $0.38. Against FY2027 consensus of $3.69, that is 8-10% of earnings. Neither the 10-Q nor the earnings call addresses the maturity. A related nuance: at 12/31/2025, $116.5M of $132.7M cash was held offshore, leaving ~$16.2M domestic; the 10-Q does not repeat the split.

Independent screens place the company in the middle. Altman Z-Score computes to approximately 2.76 — the grey zone (1.81-2.99) — with the weakness entirely in EBIT/TA at 3.3%. Piotroski F-Score is 5/9, with gross margin, leverage, share count and ROA all scoring zero.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth in FY2026 is largely price, cost and currency rather than demand. Q2's $89.4M sales increase decomposed to $54.4M price/mix, $17.9M volume and $17.1M FX; H1 FX alone contributed $42.4M. FY2025 revenue growth of 7% came from $130.7M of higher selling prices, "mostly the pass-through of higher raw material costs," with volume adding 1%. Stepan's model is pass-through with a lag, which is exactly the mechanism that produced a 10.7% gross margin in Q1 and 14.6% in Q2. Coconut oil is 13% cheaper year over year while other oil-related inputs rose, and Rojo confirmed no change in contractual approach: "we are not taking any different approach than the past."

The genuine growth vectors are narrower than the headline suggests:

- **Latin America Surfactants** is the standout: H1 sales +27% with gross profit +32%; Q2 sales +36% with gross profit +92% on double-digit organic volume.
- **North American phthalic anhydride** volume more than doubled in FY2025 "mainly due to the market exit of a competitor" — a real share gain, but the MD&A also states North America Polymers average unit margins were constant between years, so the entire $5.75M gross-profit gain came from volume in a business the company itself labels commodity.
- **Tier 2/Tier 3 customer expansion**: 500 new product-customer combinations in H1 2026 driving "strong double-digit growth" in that channel; Q2 organic surfactant volume +7%.
- **Spray foam**: volumes 3x year over year off what management repeatedly conceded is "a very low base," which is unquantified and therefore carries no revenue information.

Against these, European Polymers deteriorated further — H1 2026 sales −8%, gross profit −18%, volume −11% — following FY2025's −9%/−15% attributed to "increased competitive activity in the region." Asia was lower on softer Chinese demand, and the Philippines exit cut Asia Surfactants sales 67% in H1. Rigid-foam polyol volume grew 1% and specialty polyols 4% in FY2025.

The dominant FY2026 earnings driver is cost, not demand. Project Catalyst savings of $18-20M per quarter rising to a targeted $22M exit rate annualize to $88M, leaving roughly $12M of the $100M target unidentified — Rojo said so directly: "That doesn't take you to the full $100 million yet, and we need to keep working on it." The savings are cash-efficient: H1 charges of $70.5M were $61.3M of asset impairments (Fieldsboro $42.1M, Millsdale $14.3M, Stalybridge $4.9M) with only $1.9M of restructuring liability at quarter end, implying roughly $15-20M of total FY2026 cash outflow including the newly approved ~100-role workforce reduction ($4-6M, substantially all cash). Cost programs, however, do not compound. The FY2025 comparison base is unusually easy and rolls off after Q1 2027; FY2027 consensus was cut 5.8% over 90 days to $3.69.

Peer medians in specialty chemicals show P/E 30.2 and yield 1.3%, but the median is distorted by NGVT at 110x and MTX at negative earnings. The cleaner comparables are IOSP at 18.9x and FUL at 18.1x, with KWR at 30.2x. On FY2027 consensus, SCL at 17.7x sits roughly in line with IOSP and FUL — neither a discount that demands explanation nor a premium requiring justification. On FY2026 consensus at 24.2x it is more expensive than both. The relevant differentiator is return on capital: FY2025 ROE was 3.9% and ROA 2.0%, and the business has not earned its cost of capital in four years. A market-multiple valuation on a sub-cost-of-capital return profile is not a discount.

Price/book of 1.22x against $1,142.6M of net PP&E on a $2.76B gross base — including a nearly new Pasadena facility — is the strongest element of the value case. If returns normalize toward historical levels, book value understates earning power. The counter is that four years have passed without normalization, and Catalyst just impaired assets at three sites.

Scenario values on the frozen $65.205 basis:

- **Bear — $45 (−31.0%)**: raw-material pass-through reverses, H2 working capital fails to release, Q3/Q4 disappoint versus the Q2 extrapolation, the June 2027 facility reprices ~250bp wider costing ~$8.6M pretax, FY2027 adjusted EBITDA retreats toward $200M. At 7.5x less ~$560M net debt, equity is roughly $940M, or $41-45 per share — approximately the 52-week low of $41.82 and 0.84x book.
- **Base — $70 (+7.4%)**: FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $235M, the $30M land sale closes, capex lands at $105M and year-end net debt falls to roughly $505M. At 9.0x, equity is ~$1,610M or $70.3. Cross-check: 19x FY2027 consensus $3.69 = $70.1. Two methods converge.
- **Bull — $86 (+31.9%)**: the full $22M quarterly Catalyst run rate annualizes in 2027, Pasadena reaches full utilization, European polymers stabilizes, and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $260M. At 9.75x less $460M net debt, equity is ~$2,075M or $90.6; 22x FY2027 EPS gives $81.2; the blend is ~$86, near the single published $85 target.

Probability-weighting 22% bull / 48% base / 30% bear gives an expected price return of approximately **+1.3%**, or roughly +3.7% including the dividend — below the cost of equity for a levered, low-return cyclical with a dated refinancing wall. The distribution is negatively skewed: 7% to base against 31% to bear.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is genuinely mixed rather than uniformly poor. Improving: Latin America is taking share with 27% H1 sales growth and 32% gross-profit growth; the Tier 2/Tier 3 channel push added 500 new product-customer combinations in a half and plausibly routes around the make-versus-buy problem, since small formulators lack the capital to backward-integrate; North American PA gained volume from a competitor exit; Pasadena is in-sourcing specialty alkoxylate volume previously tolled to third parties at 75-80% utilization and is running ahead of projected tolling savings; footprint consolidation from Fieldsboro, Stalybridge and Millsdale into more efficient network sites should structurally lower unit cost.

Deteriorating: European Polymers has now posted two consecutive periods of margin loss explicitly attributed to "increased competitive activity," with H1 volume −11% against soft construction demand; the FY2025 10-K ran a goodwill sensitivity on the $47.8M European polymers reporting unit (fair value exceeded carrying value with adequate cushion, so this is smaller than the disclosure's existence implies). More fundamentally, gross margin has fallen 383bp since 2022 — roughly $89M of annualized gross profit at constant volume — while revenue fell 16%, which is not the profile of a business with strengthening pricing power. And the make-versus-buy risk is stated in the filings as realized, not hypothetical.

An important limitation: the filings contain no customer-concentration disclosure of any kind. The absence of a Regulation S-K disclosure suggests no single customer exceeded 10% of revenue, but that is inference from silence. The documented shift toward agriculture, oilfield, distribution partners and Tier 2/3 accounts establishes a channel and end-market mix change; it does not establish that customer concentration has fallen.

## Risk Assessment

**The June 24, 2027 Credit Agreement maturity ($344.0M).** Both the revolver and the delayed-draw term loan mature on the same date, $344.0M is drawn, availability is $81.4M, and cash of $113.7M is likely predominantly offshore based on the year-end pattern. No filing or public statement addresses an extension, amendment or replacement. Refinancing should be achievable, but repricing costs $0.28-0.38 of EPS.

**Millsdale single-site concentration.** One asset is simultaneously a surfactant plant, the sole U.S. phthalic anhydride plant, a polyol plant, a Catalyst decommissioning site, a self-remediation site, the subject of an unresolved USEPA Notice and Finding of Violation dated March 26, 2024 on air regulations, the source of the FY2025 environmental reserve adjustment, the site of a 2024 flood and 2024 operational problems, and subject to 2024 ethylene-oxide fenceline monitoring rules. A repeat outage would hit Surfactants and Polymers simultaneously. One good quarter is not evidence of durable reliability.

**Regulatory pressure on core chemistries.** New York's 1,4-dioxane limits (2 ppm 2022, 1 ppm 2023), California's proposed rules, an EU surfactant limit expected, and a TSCA final risk evaluation finding "unreasonable risk" now in the risk-management phase. Ethylene oxide use at three sites faces fenceline monitoring plus state legislative proposals.

**Environmental and legal contingencies accrued at the range minimum.** Disclosed possible losses of $19.6-46.5M at 6/30/2026 accrued at $19.6M — approximately $27M of unaccrued upside. Wilmington MA obligates Stepan to up to 5% of response costs with no cap on total contributions. Maywood NJ has no groundwater Record of Decision yet. A Brazilian National Treasury action seeks to unwind PIS/COFINS credits for 2012-2017. Recurring environmental operating costs were $44.9M (2025), $51.3M (2024), $38.3M (2023) — roughly 2% of revenue and volatile.

**Near-term earnings visibility.** Management pre-announced a sequentially lower Q3, and consensus for the current and next quarters was cut 22.0% and 37.6% over 30 days. Coverage is thin (two estimating analysts, three firms on the call), which amplifies both the upside on surprises and the downside when a pre-flagged sequential decline arrives.

**Governance and organizational churn.** Three of seven executive officers were first elected in 2025 (CFO July, General Counsel August, CHRO September), with approximately eleven Item 5.02 filings between October 2024 and July 2026. The content of the July 29, 2026 Item 5.02 filed alongside Q2 results is not established by anything in the available record.

**NOL valuation allowance.** Net DTAs of $32.0M include ~$60.0M of federal NOLs created largely by Pasadena bonus depreciation; insufficient projected income would trigger a materially adverse allowance.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 26 contracts across three expirations with a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.07 — so the readings deserve low confidence. The reported 116.7% ATM implied volatility on the six-day August 21 tenor against 35.4% one-month and 28.2% six-month, and against 38.1% one-year realized volatility, is more consistent with illiquidity in a near-dated series than with a directional signal; put/call skew is reported at −0.4% and labeled balanced, so no directional inference is available. The one-month tenor prices a ±8.6% move, which is a reasonable market estimate of dispersion around the late-October Q3 print. A third-party note dated August 12 flagged unusually high implied volatility in the September 18 $50.00 put — an observation about one series, unconfirmed by any filing. Short interest of 3.8% of float with 4.0 days to cover indicates the bear case is uncrowded; there is no squeeze dynamic supporting or opposing the stock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($86, +31.9%)

Project Catalyst is a genuine, mostly-executed $88M annualized cost reduction achieved for roughly $15-20M of cash, and 2026 captures only part of it — the full quarterly run rate compounds into 2027. Pasadena moves from 75-80% to full utilization, converting the 2025 startup drag into structural in-sourcing margin. Capex at $100-110M against $128M of depreciation frees $20-25M of annual cash versus 2024-25, and the contracted $30M land sale adds one-time liquidity larger than all of FY2025 free cash flow. Deleveraging from 2.5x continues, the June 2027 facility refinances without incident, and interest expense stabilizes. Latin America's momentum and 500 new Tier 2/3 product-customer combinations deliver mid-single-digit organic volume growth that layers onto a lower cost base. At 1.22x book on a nearly new asset base, modest ROE normalization toward 8-9% supports a materially higher multiple, and the single published $85 target proves conservative in a name with two estimating analysts.

### Bear Case ($45, −31.0%)

Q2 was a favorable confluence of raw-material timing, FX (+$42.4M on H1 sales), pre-buy pull-forward that management sized at $5-10M, and reversal of Q1 Asian production problems — none of which recur. Q3 lands below Q2 as pre-announced, absorbs $4-5M of turnaround costs, and disappoints the marginal buyer who extrapolated the quarter. Coconut oil deflation reverses while other oil-related inputs continue rising, compressing the pass-through spread back toward the 10.7% gross margin printed in Q1. The second-half working-capital release fails to materialize and full-year free cash flow lands near zero, leaving the dividend uncovered for a second year and stalling deleveraging. The $344M facility reprices 250-340bp wider, taking $0.28-0.38 out of FY2027 EPS. European Polymers continues losing volume and margin. The residual $12M of the Catalyst target is never identified. Multiple compresses back toward 7.5x on $200M of EBITDA, and the stock retraces toward the $41.82 low.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires the second half to hold what management explicitly said it would not, and requires an undisclosed refinancing to complete on favorable terms. The bear case requires only that the pre-announced sequential decline arrive and that working capital behave as it did in the first half. Meanwhile the price has done the work: 20.8% in eight weeks, 37.5% from the December close, 4.2% below the 52-week high — against FY2027 consensus that fell 5.8% over the same 90 days. Multiple expansion has outrun earnings-power revision.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Base case $70 (+7.4%), bull $86 (+31.9%), bear $45 (−31.0%). Probability-weighted expected price return approximately +1.3%; approximately +3.7% including the 2.42% dividend.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into current strength rather than wait for the Q3 print in late October, since the sequential decline has been pre-announced and the two nearest consensus estimates have already been cut 22.0% and 37.6%. A reasonable approach is reducing to roughly half of a full position now, retaining the remainder for the dividend and the Catalyst run-rate build, with the balance reassessed after the Q3 report and after any disclosure on the June 2027 facility. Prospective investors should wait; the entry range where the risk/reward turns constructive is the low-to-mid $50s, which would put the stock near 7.5-8.0x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA and roughly 1.0x book.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or better.** (1) An 8-K or amended credit agreement disclosing extension or replacement of the $344M Credit Agreement at a spread implying incremental annual interest expense below $5M. (2) Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $60M or better with gross margin at or above 13.5%, demonstrating that the Q2 margin was not entirely raw-material timing. (3) Full-year free cash flow tracking positive by the Q3 report — specifically, nine-month operating cash flow exceeding $110M against nine-month capex near $80M, evidencing the working-capital release. (4) Catalyst savings confirmed at $22M per quarter with the residual ~$12M of the $100M target identified and announced.

**Downgrade triggers back to AVOID.** (1) Q3 adjusted EBITDA below $55M or gross margin returning below 12%, indicating the pass-through spread has closed. (2) Any covenant-relevant deterioration — TTM adjusted EBITDA trending below $175M against the $152M breach threshold at constant net debt, or net leverage rising back above 2.9x. (3) The June 2027 facility remaining undisclosed through the Q3 10-Q filing in early November, which would place the maturity inside three quarters with no visible plan. (4) A material adverse development at Millsdale — resolution of the March 2024 air NOV with a significant penalty, or a production outage — given the site's simultaneous exposure across both major segments. (5) An environmental accrual increase toward the $46.5M top of the disclosed range.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report in late October 2026 is the decisive near-term checkpoint, testing the pull-forward, the turnaround costs, and the working-capital reversal simultaneously. The Q3 10-Q in early November is the deadline for refinancing disclosure. The FY2025 comparison base rolls off after Q1 2027, at which point Catalyst savings must be visible in year-over-year growth without the easy comparison.

## Conclusion

Stepan's operating trajectory has genuinely turned. Q2 2026 doubled reported EPS, expanded gross margin 250bp, and lifted adjusted EBITDA 45%; net leverage is down to 2.5x; Project Catalyst is roughly 88% actioned at a favorable cash cost; capex has fallen below depreciation for the first time in this cycle. Prior AVOID ratings on this name underweighted how much a cost program plus a favorable raw-material lag could deliver against a trough comparison base, and the rating moves up two notches to reflect that.

What has not improved is the price you pay for it. At $65.205 the stock trades at 9.4x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, 8.1-8.8x a scenario-derived FY2026 EBITDA range, 24.2x FY2026 consensus EPS and 1.22x book, having risen 20.8% in eight weeks on a single quarter that management described as inflated by $5-10M of pull-forward and told investors would not repeat. FY2027 consensus was cut 5.8% over the same period. Behind the quarter sits a business with 3.9% ROE, 383bp of unrecovered gross-margin compression since 2022, first-half free cash flow of negative $29M, a dividend that FY2025 free cash flow covered 0.70 times, an environmental liability accrued at the bottom of a $19.6-46.5M range, and $344M of bank debt maturing in June 2027 with no disclosed refinancing.

Base-case fair value of $70 offers 7.4% upside against a bear case 31% below spot. That asymmetry is unfavorable, the nearest catalyst is negatively skewed, and the earnings-power revision has not kept pace with the multiple. **REDUCE** — trim into strength, revisit in the low-to-mid $50s or on confirmed refinancing plus a Q3 that holds the Q2 margin.
