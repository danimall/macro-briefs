## Executive Summary

Columbia Banking System is a $66 billion-asset regional bank operating across the western United States following three transformative acquisitions since 2023. The company recently completed its integration of Pacific Premier Bancorp (August 2025) and is now generating strong operating metrics: Q1 2026 NIM of 3.96% (guiding above 4%), ROTCE of 13.88%, and executing an aggressive $700M share repurchase program. At $28.80, the stock trades at approximately 11x forward earnings with a 5.1% dividend yield, offering a combined total shareholder yield approaching 13-15% annually.

The primary tension is between strong capital return and improving operating performance on one hand, and significant CRE concentration (58% of loans) on the other — with 30-year Treasury yields above 5.1% and rising, CRE refinancing stress is a confirmed macro headwind for the sector through 2026. The Q1 2026 NPA jump from 0.30% to 0.40% (attributed to a single agricultural relationship) introduces early credit normalization signals. The stock's 5-year total shareholder return of ~100 vs. 149 for peers reflects the market's persistent skepticism about the acquisition-driven model.

The specific reason this does not warrant a BUY despite attractive yield metrics: CRE constitutes 58% of the loan book with 32% of CRE concentrated in Southern California, and the current macro regime (30Y at 5.12%, CRE distress at 12.07% ATH for CMBS) creates a testable, binary risk that credit migration accelerates in Q2-Q3 2026. If CRE non-accruals rise above 0.50% of the CRE portfolio (from current 0.19%), the ACL coverage ratio at 1.00% becomes inadequate and a reserve build would consume the EPS growth currently priced in.

## Company Overview

Columbia Banking System operates through Columbia Bank, an Oregon-chartered commercial bank with 362+ branches across nine western states. The franchise was assembled through three major acquisitions: the reverse merger with Umpqua Holdings (February 2023), and the acquisition of Pacific Premier Bancorp (August 2025). The company positions itself as "Community Banking at Scale," combining commercial lending, wealth management, HOA banking, equipment leasing (FinPac), and residential mortgage servicing ($7.8B portfolio).

Geographic footprint: #2 in Oregon (16.4% deposit share), #5 in Washington (7.8%), #11 in California (1.1%), with smaller positions in Idaho, Arizona, Nevada, Colorado, and Utah. The Pacific Premier acquisition significantly expanded the Southern California franchise and added a profitable HOA banking operation.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by three specific mechanisms rather than organic loan growth:

1. **NIM expansion:** The path from 3.96% to 4%+ is supported by continued deposit repricing (cost declining with rate cuts) and accretion of fair-value marks on Pacific Premier's acquired loan portfolio. If NIM reaches 4.10% by year-end, that alone adds ~$85M annualized NII versus Q1 2026 levels.

2. **Cost synergy realization:** Integration costs of ~$185M are substantially complete. Full run-rate savings expected by June 30, 2026. The efficiency ratio should decline toward the 45-48% range (from current ~50%) as duplicate costs roll off.

3. **Share count reduction:** At $150-200M quarterly buyback pace, the company is retiring approximately 5-7M shares per quarter (2% of float). Over 12 months, this mechanically adds $0.12-0.18 to EPS even with zero operating improvement.

Organic loan growth is modest — management is actively optimizing the balance sheet rather than growing aggressively. Total loans were $47.7B at Q1 end. The growth story is profitability per share, not top-line expansion.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive for the full year: current-year EPS revisions of +0.6% over 90 days, with slight near-term quarterly downward revisions (-0.8% to -1.1%). This does not trigger the "rapidly rising estimates" BUY signal — estimates are stable, not accelerating.

- 12.5x trailing EPS ($2.30)
- 9.4x FY2026E EPS ($3.06)
- 8.5x FY2027E EPS ($3.38)
- 1.52x tangible book value
- 5.1% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 10.0x. COLB at 9.4x forward sits slightly below median, despite above-average NIM trajectory and capital return intensity. Western Alliance (WAL) at 8.9x carries higher growth but also higher risk (deposit flight history in 2023). Cullen/Frost (CFR) at 13.4x commands a premium for its Texas franchise stability. The peer range suggests $29-$36 is reasonable depending on credit quality trajectory.

**Why the market applies a discount:** The 5-year cumulative TSR of ~100 vs. peer group 149 reflects persistent skepticism about acquisition-driven EPS growth. Investors question whether the integration actually creates value or merely buys revenue. The discount is partially justified — tangible book has been diluted by goodwill accumulation ($2.15B), and organic operating leverage has yet to be demonstrated independently.

**Total return analysis:** 5.1% dividend + 7-8% buyback yield + 10% EPS growth = potential 20%+ total return if credit quality holds. This is the bull case. The valuation is reasonable but requires credit quality stability to unlock — the market is rationally discounting the CRE risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Columbia occupies a defensible position as the largest community-oriented commercial bank in the Pacific Northwest. The 33% non-interest bearing deposit base (vs. industry average of ~20-25%) provides a structural cost-of-funds advantage worth approximately 50-60bps of NIM relative to peers.

**Competitive advantages:**
- Scale in Oregon/Washington markets enables full-service relationship banking that credit unions and sub-$10B community banks cannot replicate
- HOA banking (acquired via Pacific Premier) provides sticky, low-cost deposit gathering with high barriers to switching
- Wealth management cross-sell capability differentiates from pure-play commercial lenders
- Equipment leasing (FinPac) provides geographic diversification and higher-yielding assets

**Competitive risks:**
- JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo dominate the #1-3 positions in most western states and actively compete for middle-market commercial relationships
- Credit unions (tax-exempt) are explicitly cited as competitive threats for consumer and small business
- The California expansion via Pacific Premier puts COLB into a market where it lacks the brand recognition and density to compete on equal footing with larger players

The competitive position is stable-to-improving in core Pacific Northwest markets but unproven in Southern California. The trajectory deserves modest positive bias given NIM outperformance and deposit franchise quality.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Concentration in a Rising-Rate Environment (Primary Risk)**
CRE constitutes 58% of the $47.7B loan portfolio. With 30-year Treasury yields at 5.12% [unverified], CRE refinancing pressure will intensify through 2026. Office non-accruals rose from 0.05% to 0.11% in a single quarter. Southern California multifamily (21% of total loans) is particularly sensitive to cap rate compression at these yield levels.

**2. Credit Quality Migration**
NPAs jumped from 0.30% to 0.40% of assets in Q1 2026. While management attributed this to a single agricultural relationship, the broader pattern shows CRE non-accruals rising from $50M to $91M QoQ (+82%). The allowance for credit losses at 1.00% of loans is declining even as credit is migrating negatively — this creates reserve adequacy risk if deterioration accelerates.

**3. Integration Execution**
While substantially complete, the 20.8% employee turnover rate is elevated. Loss of Pacific Premier relationship bankers in Southern California could impair the acquired franchise's value. Full cost savings by June 2026 is management guidance, not yet achieved.

**4. Legal Contingencies**
The iCap Ponzi scheme lawsuit ($90M claims), MOVEit data breach class actions (429,000 customers affected), and demonstrated litigation costs ($55M settlement in Q1 2025) represent quantifiable tail risks.

**5. Macro Environment**
The tariff and trade policy environment creates uncertainty for COLB's western U.S. markets. The 10-K specifically identifies "the impact of proposed or imposed tariffs by the U.S. government and retaliatory tariffs" as a risk factor. Pacific Northwest/Western US economies are trade-dependent, and any economic slowdown could accelerate credit deterioration in the CRE-heavy portfolio.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-36, +15-25%):**
- NIM crosses 4% in Q2 2026 and expands toward 4.15-4.20% by Q4 as deposit repricing continues
- Credit quality stabilizes with no systemic CRE deterioration
- Full cost synergies achieved by June, driving efficiency ratio to sub-47%
- Aggressive buybacks ($600-800M annually) mechanically drive EPS toward $3.40-3.50
- Multiple re-rates from 9.4x toward peer median 10.0-10.5x on demonstrated execution
- Total return (including 5.1% yield): 20-30% over 12 months

**Bear Case ($22-24, -15-20%):**
- CRE credit cycle accelerates: office + retail non-accruals reach 2-3% of respective categories
- Reserve build of $100-150M (bringing ACL back toward 1.15-1.20%) reduces reported EPS by $0.25-0.35
- Southern California multifamily softens as cap rates decompress with higher long-term rates
- Integration dislocations lose key relationship bankers, impairing deposit franchise
- Stock re-rates to 0.9x TBV ($17) in severe scenario, or stays at 1.0-1.1x ($19-21) in moderate downturn

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 credit metrics stable or improving (NPAs ≤ 0.40%, net charge-offs ≤ 0.30%)
- NIM at or above 4.00% confirmed
- CRE non-accrual rate declining QoQ, demonstrating the Q1 jump was truly idiosyncratic
- Stock pulls back to $26-27 on market-wide selling without company-specific deterioration

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPAs exceed 0.60% of assets (indicating broader credit migration beyond single-name)
- ACL reserve build announced exceeding $50M in any quarter
- NIM contracts below 3.85% (indicating deposit franchise pressure or asset quality mix shift)
- Management reduces or suspends buyback program (signals capital preservation mode)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This is the first quarter where full integration synergies should be visible and where CRE credit trends from the rising-rate environment will be more apparent.

## Conclusion

Columbia Banking System presents an attractive total yield proposition (13-15% combined dividend + buyback) underpinned by improving NIM and completed integration synergies. The valuation at 9.4x forward earnings sits slightly below peer median despite above-average deposit franchise quality. However, the specific, falsifiable risk preventing a BUY rating is CRE concentration (58% of loans) in an environment where long-term rates are structurally pressuring refinancing capacity across the sector. The Q1 2026 NPA increase, while attributed to a single credit, occurred against a backdrop of rising CRE non-accruals (+82% QoQ) that warrants observation before adding exposure. The appropriate stance is to collect the 5.1% dividend yield while monitoring whether Q2 2026 confirms credit stability or reveals broader deterioration.
