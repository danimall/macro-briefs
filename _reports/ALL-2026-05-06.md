## Executive Summary

Allstate has completed one of the most impressive financial turnarounds in P&C insurance history, swinging from a $(1.3)B net loss in 2022 to $10.3B net income in 2025, with an 85.2 combined ratio that ranks among the best in class for large personal lines carriers. Q1 2026 results extended this trajectory: combined ratio of 82.0, adjusted EPS of $10.65 (beating consensus by approximately 46%), and trailing 12-month ROE of 48.4%.

At recent prices, the stock trades at an estimated 7.5–8.3x forward earnings, representing a discount to the peer group. Allstate has consistently beaten consensus by wide margins in recent quarters, with the Q1 2026 beat following a Q3 2025 beat of approximately 36% and a prior-quarter beat of approximately 79%. The earnings "surprise" is so consistent and large that consensus remains structurally behind reality.

The bear case rests on mean reversion: the 85.2 combined ratio benefited from $1.81B in reserve releases (unsustainable), new tariffs threaten claims cost inflation on auto parts and building materials, and catastrophe losses are inherently volatile. If ROE normalizes from 42% toward 18-22%, the current price-to-book multiple could compress. These are real risks, but at current valuations with $3.64B in remaining buyback authorization (approximately 6.9% of market cap as of March 31, 2026), the risk/reward remains asymmetric to the upside over a 12-month horizon.

## Company Overview

Allstate is one of the largest publicly held personal lines property and casualty insurers in the US, with 38.3 million policies in force across its Allstate Protection segment and approximately 173 million PIF in Protection Services (embedded consumer product plans). Written premiums totaled approximately $59.5B in 2025 across auto (66%), homeowners (28%), and other lines (6%).

The company distributes through exclusive agents, independent agents (via National General, acquired 2021), and a direct-to-consumer channel that grew new auto applications 32.9% in 2025. Allstate's "Transformative Growth" strategy is pivoting from margin repair (2022-2024) to policy count growth (2025+), supported by a $2.1B advertising budget — up 3.3x from 2023's $638M.

Strategic dispositions in 2025 (employer voluntary benefits for $888M gain; group health for $715M gain) simplified the business model to focus on P&C and embedded protection. Commercial lines are being intentionally run off. New homeowners policies in California and Florida are not being written due to regulatory constraints.

## Growth Analysis

Allstate is transitioning from a margin-repair story to a growth story. Key metrics:

- Auto PIF growth: +2.3% (2025), +2.6% (Q1 2026)
- Homeowners PIF growth: +2.5%
- New issued auto applications: +25.3% (2025)
- Direct channel applications: +32.9%
- Premiums earned growth: +5.5% in Q1 2026 (premiums written growth: +2.3%)

The $2.1B advertising investment is the leading indicator. Management is deliberately reinvesting pricing room into competitiveness ("affordability initiatives") while maintaining sub-86 combined ratios. This suggests confidence that profitable growth can co-exist with current margin levels.

The magnitude of recent beats suggests consensus estimates have lagged the company's actual execution trajectory.

The growth ceiling is real, however. Allstate cannot write new homeowners in California or Florida, limiting TAM in the two largest catastrophe-prone markets. Auto insurance growth is constrained by vehicle sales volumes and regulatory rate approval timelines. The Protection Services segment (~173M PIF) offers the highest growth optionality through embedded distribution partnerships, but its revenue contribution (~5%) limits near-term materiality.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current valuation:** Based on available data, Allstate trades at an estimated forward P/E in the range of approximately 7.5–8.3x, depending on the consensus EPS estimate and share price used. Yahoo Finance data shows a forward P/E of 8.29x.

**vs. Peers:** The stock trades at a discount to large-cap P&C peers including Travelers, Hartford Financial, and Cincinnati Financial, though the precise discount varies by data source and timing.

**vs. Book:** At a March 31, 2026 book value of $113.52/share, the stock trades at approximately 1.8–1.9x book at recent prices.

The discount to peers is difficult to justify given:
1. Allstate's combined ratio (85.2 in 2025, 82.0 in Q1 2026) is materially better than industry averages (mid-90s)
2. ROE (42.3%) substantially exceeds most large P&C carriers, though notable peers like Progressive also deliver strong returns (35.3% ROE in 2025)
3. Earnings momentum remains robust

The P/B ratio of approximately 1.8–1.9x is elevated relative to the 1.2-1.5x range typical for P&C carriers, but a 42% ROE justifies a premium to book. The Gordon Growth model (P/B = [ROE - g] / [r - g]) at a sustainable 20% ROE, 4% growth, and 10% cost of equity implies a 2.7x book — above the current level.

The key valuation question: what's normalized earnings power? If combined ratios revert from 85 to 93-95 (still profitable) and reserve releases normalize, EPS could fall to $18-22. At current prices, that's 10-12x normalized earnings — still reasonable relative to peers but not deeply cheap. The current price effectively requires ongoing sub-90 combined ratios to be justified.

## Competitive Landscape

**Pricing adequacy advantage:** Having repriced aggressively in 2022-2024, Allstate now has pricing room to invest in growth while competitors may still be raising rates. The 25.3% surge in new applications validates this competitive positioning.

**Telematics/data advantage (Arity):** Allstate's Arity platform processes driving data from connections across its network, providing proprietary risk selection advantages that compound over time. This is a durable moat in a data-driven business.

**Distribution evolution:** The three-channel strategy (exclusive agents + independent agents + direct) provides broader customer access than peers dependent on single channels.

**Scale in reinsurance:** The $1.23B reinsurance program and California wildfire recovery ($1.16B) demonstrate the benefits of scale in catastrophe management.

**Competitive threats:** Progressive (87.4 combined ratio, 35.3% ROE in 2025) and GEICO continue gaining market share in personal auto. State Farm remains dominant in exclusive agency distribution. Travelers reported a strong 89.9% combined ratio in 2025. The long-term risk from autonomous vehicles reducing accident frequency is real but 10+ years away from material impact.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff-Driven Claims Inflation (NEW — Material):**
Allstate's filings address tariffs as a material risk. The Q1 2026 10-Q notes the U.S. "implemented and continues to modify tariff measures" with potential effects including higher vehicle pricing, replacement parts costs, building material increases, and reduced auto applications. If average auto claims cost rises 5-8% due to tariffs, this could add 2-3 points to the combined ratio, eroding the margin cushion. This risk is partially quantifiable and creates a ceiling on current-quarter earnings expectations.

**Reserve Development Sustainability:**
$1.81B in favorable reserve releases contributed ~22.6% of 2025 underwriting income (14.1% of consolidated net income applicable to common shareholders). These releases reflect over-reserving during the 2022-2023 loss years. As those accident years mature, the pool of releasable reserves shrinks. Even neutral development (zero releases) would reduce EPS by roughly $5-6/share.

**Catastrophe Volatility:**
Q1 2026 cat losses fell 43.7% YoY, directly boosting results. The 10-year average catastrophe impact is 8.8 combined ratio points. A severe hurricane season or wildfire event could easily add $3-5B in losses. Reinsurance mitigates but doesn't eliminate this binary risk.

**Rate Adequacy Cycle Turn:**
Auto rate increases moderated to 3.5% in 2025 versus much higher levels in 2023-2024. If claims inflation accelerates (via tariffs or social inflation) while rate momentum fades, the combined ratio trough may be behind us.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 22.4% is at parity with 1-year realized volatility of 23.2% (spread: -0.7pp). The options market is pricing no unusual event risk. Put/call OI ratio of 1.07 is neutral. The term structure is in normal contango (22.4% near-term vs. 25.5% 6-month), suggesting no imminent stress expected but some uncertainty priced into the back end. IV compression from 33.0% on April 13 to 22.4% today (-10.6pp) reflects the Q1 earnings release removing uncertainty. No divergence between options positioning and fundamental thesis.

## Conclusion

Allstate at current prices represents a high-quality P&C franchise generating extraordinary returns at a discount to peers. The forward P/E discount, strong upward estimate revisions, and consistent earnings beats create a compelling setup. The transition from margin repair to profitable growth extends the earnings cycle.

The primary risk is that current earnings represent a cyclical peak, with tariff-driven claims inflation, reserve release exhaustion, and catastrophe volatility all capable of compressing returns toward historical norms. At approximately 1.8–1.9x book value, the stock requires ongoing superior execution to justify its premium to tangible assets.

The bear case requires simultaneous adverse developments across tariffs, reserves, and catastrophes, while the bull case has multiple independent paths to value realization through estimate revisions, buybacks, and multiple expansion. Maintain BUY with a 12-month target of $245.

The declining score trajectory (7.7 → 7.3) reflected legitimate risk escalation (tariffs, interim CFO) rather than fundamental deterioration. The Q1 2026 beat and continued momentum stabilize the thesis at current levels. The Risk Factors score remains at 5.5 due to the tariff overhang, which is a genuinely new and unresolved threat to claims costs that did not exist in prior evaluations.
