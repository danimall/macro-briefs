## Executive Summary

HCI Group is a Florida-focused property and casualty insurance holding company trading at ~6.6x trailing earnings and ~9.0x forward consensus ($16.66 FY2026E), a substantial discount to both the P&C peer median of 17.3x and its own earnings trajectory. The company earned $22.72 in diluted EPS in FY2025 — a year with zero catastrophe losses — generating $441M in free cash flow on $901M revenue. Its 82.5% stake in Exzeo (NYSE: XZO), an insurtech platform that IPO'd in November 2025 at a ~$1.9B valuation, creates a sum-of-parts dynamic in which the core insurance business is being ascribed minimal standalone value by the market.

A prior BUY recommendation at ~$167 in March 2026 has lost approximately 10.8%. Two material developments since that call warrant re-engagement: (1) the board authorized an $80M share repurchase program in March 2026, representing ~4% of market cap and signaling management's view of undervaluation, and (2) analyst estimates have reversed their downward trend with 30-day revisions of +5.8% for FY2026 and +11.3% for the next quarter, indicating positive earnings momentum after the post-hurricane-free normalization period. The stock's decline from $210.50 (52-week high) to $149.26 compresses the valuation further without a corresponding deterioration in the fundamental outlook. The primary risk remains concentrated Florida hurricane exposure, a binary seasonal factor that defines the stock's trading range each June through November.

## Company Overview

HCI Group operates primarily through its insurance subsidiaries HCPCI and TTIC, writing homeowners insurance in Florida. As of year-end 2025, the company had approximately 313,400 policies in force, up 15.5% from 271,300 a year prior. Growth has been driven predominantly by Citizens Property Insurance depopulation programs — HCI assumed 173,485 policies with $757M in annualized gross premiums from Citizens over 2023-2025.

The company operates five segments:

- **Insurance Operations** (core): $1.146B gross premiums earned, $822M net premiums earned in FY2025.
- **Exzeo** (~82.5% owned, publicly traded as XZO): Insurtech platform generating $217M revenue and $110M pretax income in FY2025 with a 54% adjusted EBITDA margin.
- **Reciprocal Exchange Operations**: Two exchanges (CORE and Tailrow) managed by HCI. $95.6M gross premiums; net income flows to exchange policyholders, not HCI shareholders.
- **Real Estate**: Commercial properties in Florida (marinas, retail, office).
- **Corporate/Other**: Holding company activities.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement Trajectory

| Net Combined Ratio | — | ~85% | ~85% | 56.3% |

Revenue has compounded at ~22% annually over 2022-2025. The FY2025 result was extraordinary: net income nearly tripled year-over-year, driven by zero catastrophe losses (versus $128M from Hurricanes Milton, Helene, and Debby in 2024) and structurally reduced claims litigation following Florida's 2022-2023 tort reform. The gross loss ratio fell to 19.6% from 34.6% in 2024.

The 56.3% net combined ratio is not sustainable in an average hurricane year, but it demonstrates the earnings power available when catastrophe losses are absent. In a "normal" year with moderate hurricane activity ($50-100M in cat losses), normalized EPS likely sits in the $14-18 range — still well above current consensus of $16.66 for FY2026.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow reached $444M in FY2025, up 34% from $332M in FY2024 and 92% from $231M in FY2023. Capital expenditures were minimal at $3.6M, producing $441M in free cash flow — a 48.9% FCF margin and 147% FCF-to-net-income conversion. This quality of cash generation reflects the capital-light nature of the insurance model combined with a benign loss year.

Capital allocation priorities are sensible: the $80M buyback at ~4% of market cap, a $1.60/share annual dividend (~1.1% yield), and minimal debt issuance. The convertible note conversion during 2025 eliminated $172.5M in debt and ~$4.2M in annual interest expense, modestly diluting shares but materially improving the balance sheet.

### Balance Sheet

Cash and equivalents totaled $1.21B at year-end 2025 (up from $532M a year prior), providing substantial liquidity. Long-term debt is minimal: $32.4M in promissory notes and $36M drawn on the revolving credit facility. The D/E ratio of 1.36 appears elevated but is typical for insurance companies where unearned premiums and loss reserves constitute the bulk of "liabilities." Stockholders' equity stands at $1.04B. The investment portfolio of $663M in fixed-maturity and equity securities generates meaningful investment income at current rate levels.

## Growth Analysis

HCI's growth has been powered by three engines:

1. **Citizens depopulation takeouts** — This has been the primary driver, but the opportunity is narrowing. Citizens' policy count has declined ~45% from its October 2023 peak of 1.41M to ~778K by mid-2025. HCI can continue to take policies, but the remaining pool is smaller and potentially lower quality (policyholders who have been harder to place).

2. **Organic premium growth** — Florida's population growth and rising insured values provide a base-rate growth tailwind. Premium rate adequacy has improved post-tort-reform, allowing carriers to write at reasonable loss ratios.

3. **Exzeo platform expansion** — The insurtech subsidiary added its sixth carrier client in Q4 2025. Managed premium reached $1.39B (+139% YoY). If Exzeo successfully scales third-party revenue beyond HCI affiliates, this becomes a high-margin, capital-light growth vector. However, current third-party revenue contribution remains early-stage.

Estimate revisions are constructive: current-year FY2026 consensus has risen 5.8% over the past 30 days (from $15.75 to $16.66), and next-quarter estimates increased 11.3% (from $4.50 to $5.01). This reversal from the 90-day downward trend (-2.1% FY2026, -5.8% current quarter) suggests the post-hurricane-free normalization has been processed and forward expectations are stabilizing at reasonable levels.

The discount to peers is wide. Part of it is justified by HCI's concentrated Florida hurricane exposure and small-cap size. But a 48-62% discount exceeds what binary weather risk alone warrants, particularly given:

**Sum-of-Parts Analysis:**
- HCI's 82.5% Exzeo stake: At IPO valuation (~$1.9B), this stake is worth ~$1.57B. Even at a 30% discount to IPO valuation (reflecting post-IPO uncertainty), the stake is worth ~$1.1B.
- HCI's total market cap: ~$1.99B.
- **Implied value of the core insurance business: $0.42B-$0.89B** — a business that generated $299M in net income and $441M in FCF in FY2025.

Even normalizing for hurricane losses, the core insurance business earning $150-200M annually is being valued at 2-6x earnings. This is anomalously cheap.

Analyst consensus targets range from $235 to $255, with a mean/median of $245 — representing 60-71% upside from current levels. Coverage is sparse (4 analysts: 1 Strong Buy, 2 Buy, 1 Hold), but consensus is unanimously above current pricing.

## Competitive Landscape

HCI operates in a structurally improved Florida insurance market. The 2022-2023 tort reform reduced Citizens' litigation rate from 18.6% (2018) to 10.6% (2024), directly benefiting loss ratios across the Florida homeowners market. Reinsurance costs are softening — Citizens expects lower reinsurance costs in 2026, a trend that benefits all Florida carriers' ceding ratios.

Competitive positioning is mixed:
- **Strength:** Proprietary Exzeo platform provides underwriting precision and operational efficiency that legacy-system competitors lack. The platform's IPO validates its technological differentiation.
- **Strength:** Deep Florida market knowledge from years of Citizens assumption programs.
- **Weakness:** No A.M. Best rating limits addressable market to Demotech-accepting mortgage companies — sufficient in Florida but constraining for national expansion.
- **Weakness:** Geographic concentration means HCI cannot diversify away from Florida hurricane risk the way national carriers can.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Hurricane Concentration Risk (HIGH)
This is the dominant risk factor. A Category 4+ hurricane striking the Tampa Bay or Miami metro area could produce insured losses far exceeding HCI's reinsurance coverage. The FY2022 loss from Hurricane Ian demonstrates this vulnerability. Each June-November, HCI is effectively a binary bet: no major landfall produces exceptional earnings; a direct hit produces significant losses or worse. The 2026 hurricane season forecasts are modestly favorable (potential El Niño suppression), but forecasts have limited predictive value for specific landfall risk.

### 2. Citizens Depopulation Exhaustion (MEDIUM)
With Citizens' policy count down 45% from peak, the organic takeout pipeline is narrowing. HCI's growth rate may decelerate if it cannot find alternative channels.

### 3. Loss Reserve Uncertainty (MEDIUM)
Loss reserves of $576.5M on a rapidly growing book create inherent uncertainty. A 20% adverse development would reduce equity by ~$81M (7.8%). HCI's book is relatively young (many policies assumed 2023-2025), meaning ultimate loss development is still uncertain.

### 4. Exzeo Customer Concentration (MEDIUM)
Exzeo derives substantially all revenue from HCI affiliates. The $1.9B IPO valuation assumes successful third-party expansion that has not yet materialized at scale. If third-party customer acquisition stalls, Exzeo's standalone valuation compresses, and the sum-of-parts thesis weakens.

### 5. Regulatory Rate Pressure (LOW-MEDIUM)
Citizens is recommending rate cuts for 2026. If regulators pressure private carriers to follow suit after a benign loss year, HCI's premium adequacy could be compressed. Florida's current regulatory posture is favorable, but this can change.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($225-255)
- 2026 hurricane season is benign (El Niño favorable), producing another strong earnings year ($18-22 EPS).
- Exzeo successfully signs 3-5 additional third-party carriers, validating independent revenue growth and supporting its market valuation.
- Sum-of-parts discount narrows as investors recognize the Exzeo stake value versus HCI's total market cap.
- Share repurchase program reduces float at depressed prices, driving per-share value accretion.
- Reinsurance costs decline, expanding net margins even in a normalized loss environment.
- 15-17x normalized earnings of $15-17 = $225-255.

### Bear Case ($100-120)
- Major hurricane strikes Tampa Bay or South Florida, producing $200M+ in net losses.
- Loss reserves develop adversely, adding $50-100M in prior-year charges.
- Exzeo fails to gain third-party traction, compressing its valuation to 5-8x revenue (~$1.0-1.7B), reducing HCI's sum-of-parts support.
- Regulatory rate pressure forces premium reductions in 2027.
- 6-8x trough earnings of $8-12 in a heavy cat year + negative sentiment = $100-120.

**Upside/downside:** +41% to base case, +67% to bull case, -20-30% to bear case. Risk/reward is approximately 1.5:1 to base case and 2.0:1+ to bull case.

## Addressing Prior BUY Performance

The prior BUY issued at ~$167 on March 16, 2026 has declined approximately 10.8%. Two material developments since that recommendation justify re-engagement rather than abandonment:

1. **$80M share repurchase authorization (March 2026):** This did not exist at the time of the prior call. Management is deploying capital to buy back stock at current prices — an explicit signal of their valuation assessment.

2. **Estimate revision reversal:** At the time of the prior BUY, 90-day estimate trends were negative (-2.1% FY2026, -5.8% current quarter). Over the subsequent 30 days, this reversed to positive. The negative estimate momentum that contributed to the stock's decline has turned.

The stock has also declined an additional 10.8%, compressing the forward P/E from ~10.5x to ~9.0x. The fundamental thesis (sum-of-parts discount, Florida tort reform tailwind, normalized earnings power) remains intact. The risk (hurricane exposure) is unchanged. The stock is cheaper, and two new positive data points support the thesis.

## Conclusion

HCI Group offers an asymmetric risk/reward profile driven by a persistent sum-of-parts discount, positive earnings revisions, and a structurally improved Florida insurance market. At ~9.0x forward earnings — a 48% discount to P&C peers — the stock prices in a level of catastrophe risk that exceeds actuarial expectations in any given year. The Exzeo stake provides a valuation floor that approaches HCI's entire market cap, leaving investors paying very little for a core insurance business generating $150-200M+ in normalized annual earnings.

The concentrated Florida hurricane exposure is real and must be sized accordingly — this is a position that should be at standard or modest weight, with investors prepared for 20-30% drawdowns in a major hurricane year. For investors willing to accept that binary seasonal risk, the current entry point at $149.26 offers 41% upside to a conservative base case of $210 and 67% upside to a bull case of $250.

**Recommendation: BUY**
