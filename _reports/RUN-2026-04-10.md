## Executive Summary

Sunrun is the largest US residential solar and battery storage provider, with 1.17 million customers and 8.4 GW of networked solar capacity under long-term (20-25 year) subscription agreements. The company has executed an aggressive pivot to storage-first installations (70% attachment rate), positioning its 130,000+ activated home batteries as a 650 MW distributed grid resource. Revenue grew 45% in FY2025 to $2.96B, and the company has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters by wide margins.

The fundamental problem is that Sunrun's business model requires continuous access to external capital — tax equity, non-recourse project debt, and fund investor contributions — to grow or even maintain operations. Operating cash flow was $(421M) in FY2025 while capital expenditures on solar systems totaled $2.5B. Interest expense of $997M consumed 34% of total revenue. The balance sheet carries 5.6x debt/equity and an Altman Z-Score of 0.46, placing it deep in the distress zone. While the project-finance structure ring-fences most debt from the parent, any disruption to tax equity or non-recourse lending markets would halt the business's ability to install new systems.

Three simultaneous headwinds compound the capital structure risk: (1) the OBBB legislation signed July 4, 2025, truncated the solar ITC safe harbor and eliminated the Section 25D residential credit effective January 1, 2026; (2) tariffs on Chinese battery cells and Southeast Asian solar modules ranging from 1.9% to 534.7% are inflating equipment costs; and (3) management guided FY2026 aggregate subscriber value down 11% and net value creation down 15%. The entire C-suite sold stock at ~$13.25 on April 6, 2026 — six days before today's price of $13.43. At 7.8x trailing earnings with these structural risks, the stock appears optically cheap but the earnings denominator is unreliable and likely to deteriorate.

## Company Overview

Sunrun was founded in 2007 and went public in 2015. The company's core business is installing solar panels and battery storage systems on residential rooftops, then selling the generated electricity to homeowners under 20-25 year customer agreements (leases or PPAs) at rates typically below local utility pricing. As of December 31, 2025, the company operated 1,165,686 customer agreements generating recurring monthly cash flows.

The financing model works as follows: Sunrun creates investment fund vehicles (59 active as of year-end 2025), contributes installed solar systems, and brings in tax equity partners who receive federal ITCs and depreciation benefits. The funds are consolidated as VIEs, with partner contributions flowing through as noncontrolling interests. Non-recourse project debt is layered at the fund level. The net effect is that Sunrun borrows heavily at the project level to fund system installations while receiving ongoing distributions from the customer payment streams.

Sunrun acquired Vivint Solar in 2020, expanding its customer base and geographic footprint. The $3.1B goodwill from that acquisition was fully impaired in Q4 2024 when the stock price decline triggered a quantitative impairment test — a direct acknowledgment that the transaction destroyed substantial shareholder value.

Geographic concentration is significant: over 45% of cumulative systems are in California. The company has been expanding into east coast markets and territories like Puerto Rico but remains heavily dependent on California's regulatory and grid economics.

### Revenue and Growth Trajectory

| Revenue | $2.32B | $2.26B | $2.04B | $2.96B |

FY2025 revenue acceleration was driven by two factors: (1) recurring customer agreement revenue grew 21% to $1.82B as the subscriber base expanded, and (2) energy systems and product sales surged 114% to $1.14B, boosted by a Q3 2025 transaction selling newly originated systems to a third-party investor. The latter is lumpy and non-recurring.

Customer additions weakened in Q4 2025 — 25,475 net additions vs. 30,709 in Q4 2024, a 17% decline. Management guided FY2026 aggregate subscriber value to a $5.0B midpoint (down from $5.6B in FY2025) and net value creation to $850M (down from $1.0B). The guidance decline reflects the loss of the Section 25D credit, tariff-driven cost inflation, and a deliberate decision to exit lower-margin partner and direct purchase channels.

### Profitability

GAAP profitability is distorted by the HLBV (hypothetical liquidation at book value) method of allocating income/losses to noncontrolling interests. In FY2025, net income attributable to common stockholders was $450M, but this was achieved by allocating $(1.46B) in losses to NCI partners. Operating income remained negative at $(126M), improved from $(3.7B) in FY2024 (which included the $3.1B goodwill impairment).

Key cost ratios improved: cost of customer agreements fell to 70% of related revenue (from 78% in FY2024), and cost of energy systems dropped to 68% (from 101%). Sales and marketing expense grew 15% to $709M, reflecting higher customer acquisition costs.

At 34% of total revenue, Sunrun's interest burden exceeds most real estate REITs and approaches the levels seen in distressed leveraged buyouts.

The business is a net cash consumer at every GAAP level. Management's preferred "Cash Generation" metric of $377M excludes non-recourse debt raises and fund investor contributions. This metric has some logic — project-level debt is ring-fenced from the parent — but it obscures the fundamental dependency: without continuous external capital infusions, the business stops growing.

Cash on hand was $823M as of year-end 2025. The company successfully repaid its 2026 convertible notes in February 2026, removing the nearest maturity overhang. Remaining convertible notes ($475M) mature March 2030.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $22.6B are dominated by $16B+ in solar energy systems. Total stockholders' equity is $3.1B against massive consolidated liabilities, producing a debt-to-equity ratio of 5.63x. Retained earnings are $(3.83B). The Altman Z-Score of 0.46 places the company deep in the distress zone (threshold: 1.81).

The Z-Score model was not designed for project-finance structures where most debt is non-recourse. However, the score correctly identifies the business's core vulnerability: any disruption to the tax equity or project debt markets would impair Sunrun's ability to install new systems.

Management values the existing subscriber portfolio at $21.1B in "Gross Earning Assets" discounted at 6%. At an 8% discount rate, this drops to $17.3B. These figures are highly sensitive to assumed discount rates, default rates, and renewal assumptions at contract end.

## Growth Analysis

### Secular Tailwinds

The residential solar market benefits from several structural drivers: rising retail electricity rates (US residential prices up 37% since 2020 with further increases projected), increasing grid instability from extreme weather and demand growth, and customer desire for energy independence and predictable costs. The AI-driven surge in data center electricity demand adds further strain on the grid, potentially enhancing the value proposition of distributed generation and storage.

Sunrun's storage-first strategy is well-timed. The 70% storage attachment rate creates a differentiated product (solar + battery vs. solar only) that performs better under net billing tariffs and provides grid services revenue.

### Near-Term Headwinds Outweigh Tailwinds

1. **OBBB Tax Credit Truncation**: The elimination of the Section 25D residential credit effective January 2026 removes the tax advantage for direct-purchase customers. The shortened 48E solar ITC safe harbor forces accelerated construction timelines. While the 48E storage ITC extends through 2033 (a relative positive for Sunrun's storage-first model), the net effect is contractionary.

2. **Tariff Cost Inflation**: AD/CVD rates up to 534.7% on Southeast Asian solar modules and significant tariffs on Chinese battery cells create enormous supply chain cost uncertainty. Sunrun is not a direct importer but absorbs these costs through its supply chain.

3. **Management-Guided 2026 Decline**: Aggregate subscriber value guided to $5.0B midpoint (-11% YoY), net value creation to $850M (-15%), and cash generation roughly flat at $350M midpoint. The company is deliberately shrinking lower-margin channels.

4. **Interest Rate Environment**: With rates likely to remain elevated (3-month Treasury above 3.5%, no cuts expected in 2026), Sunrun's cost of capital for new projects remains elevated. Higher rates reduce advance rates from fund investors and compress the spread between customer payment rates and financing costs.

Analyst estimates for FY2026 EPS have been revised downward sharply over 90 days — from $0.95 to $0.44 (a 53% cut) — though the 30-day trend shows a modest recovery from $0.37 to $0.44. FY2027 estimates have been cut similarly, from $1.54 to $0.65. The positive 30-day trend reflects stabilization after the post-earnings selloff rather than fundamental improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.43 with forward EPS of $0.44 (FY2026E) and $0.65 (FY2027E), Sunrun trades at approximately 30x FY2026 and 21x FY2027 forward earnings. The trailing P/E of 7.8x reflects the FY2025 GAAP net income figure of $1.70 EPS, which benefits from the HLBV allocation methodology and does not represent forward economics.

Peer building products companies trade at a median 20.8x P/E. Sunrun's FY2027 multiple of ~21x appears in-line with peers, but this comparison is misleading: Sunrun's earnings quality is substantially lower due to the HLBV methodology, negative free cash flow, and sensitivity to external financing markets. Peers like Boise Cascade and Trex generate positive free cash flow and carry modest leverage.

On a price-to-book basis, with $3.1B in equity and 234M shares outstanding, book value per share is approximately $13.22 — virtually at the current price. However, book value includes $16B+ in solar systems whose recoverable value depends on sustained customer payments over 20+ years.

Against a market cap of $3.1B and total enterprise debt of ~$13B+, even the lower Gross Earning Assets estimate ($17.3B at 8% discount) implies the assets cover liabilities — but only if customer payments arrive as projected over two decades, default rates remain below 1%, and renewal economics hold.

The analyst consensus target of $21.18 (median $23.00) implies 58-71% upside. However, with 29.4% short interest and a recent 35% post-earnings crash, the analyst community is divided: 13 analysts cover the stock with 3 Strong Buy, 10 Buy, 10 Hold, and 1 Strong Sell.

## Competitive Landscape

Sunrun's competitive advantages include:

**Scale**: At 1.17M customers and 8.4 GW, Sunrun has the largest residential solar fleet in the US. This provides procurement leverage, installation efficiency, and credibility with tax equity and debt investors.

**Grid Services Platform**: Grid services revenue is nascent but growing, and the company's ability to dispatch 130,000+ batteries during peak demand creates a resource that utilities and grid operators increasingly need.

**Storage-First Positioning**: The 70% storage attachment rate places Sunrun ahead of most competitors in adapting to post-NBT economics. Solar-only installers face existential pressure as credit timelines shorten.

**Switching Costs**: 20-25 year customer agreements create durable revenue streams. Customer churn is structurally limited once systems are installed.

**Competitive Threats**: The 10-K explicitly warns about new entrants paying "significantly higher turnkey prices and sales commissions than prevailing industry norms." Traditional utilities are developing their own solar and storage programs. Tesla Energy offers integrated solar/storage with strong brand recognition. Consumer loan providers enable direct purchases that bypass Sunrun's subscription model (though the 25D credit elimination partially closes this competitive avenue).

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Capital Market Access**: Sunrun requires $3B+ annually in external financing to sustain operations. Credit market stress from any source could halt installations.

2. **OBBB Implementation**: The solar ITC truncation and 25D elimination force a business model transition already compressing subscriber value. Prohibited Foreign Entity (PFE) rules could further disrupt supply chains for Chinese-manufactured components.

3. **Interest Rate Sensitivity**: Higher-for-longer rates compress every margin in the business: financing spreads, advance rates, and competitive pricing.

4. **California Concentration**: 45%+ of systems in one state. Wildfire risk, earthquake risk, regulatory changes (NBT already reduced solar-only economics permanently), and potential net metering litigation (AB942) all concentrated in the same geography.

5. **Tariff Volatility**: AD/CVD rates up to 534.7%, Section 232 investigations, and reciprocal tariffs create unpredictable equipment cost inflation. The supply chain from China through Southeast Asia is particularly exposed.

The call-heavy volume alongside very high short interest creates squeeze potential, but the fundamental picture does not support the long thesis at current levels.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: No single imminent dated catalyst. Next earnings are likely May/August 2026 (Q1 reporting). The structural deterioration is multi-vector: (1) each quarterly report will show the 2026 guidance decline materializing; (2) Q1 2026 was the first full quarter without the 25D credit, and subscriber additions likely declined further; (3) any disruption in the tax equity market (which correlates with broader credit stress) would be devastating. The tariff situation could worsen at any point with Section 232 decisions expected in 2026.

**Timing Window**: 3-6 months. The 2026 guidance decline plays out across the year, with Q1 2026 earnings (likely May 2026) providing the first data point.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk**: At 29.4% of float with 4.2 days to cover, this is an extremely crowded short. 55M shares short against 234M outstanding. The recent 150%+ rally from the $5.38 low demonstrates that squeeze dynamics are real and violent. Institutional call positioning (0.67 OI P/C) could amplify any short squeeze.

**Borrow and Options Signal**: The 156% near-term IV implies very high borrow costs for put-based shorts. The backwardated term structure means near-term options are extremely expensive relative to longer-dated alternatives.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward**: Bear case of $5-8 implies 40-63% downside from $13.43. Squeeze risk to $18-20 (where the stock traded for months in late 2025/early 2026) implies 34-49% upside risk. At roughly 1:1 risk/reward with 29.4% short interest, the mechanics are unfavorable.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT.** The bear thesis on fundamentals has merit — the business is over-leveraged, capital-dependent, facing regulatory headwinds, and guided lower by its own management. However, 29.4% short interest, call-heavy options positioning, and demonstrated squeeze vulnerability (stock rallied from $5.38 to $22.44 in 6 months) make this unsuitable as an active short. The crowding means the remaining shorts are the most convicted and have the highest carrying costs. Avoid owning the stock; do not short it.

## Conclusion

Sunrun holds a strategically important position in the US energy transition: the largest residential solar and storage fleet, a growing grid services platform, and long-term customer agreements providing revenue visibility. The storage-first pivot is the correct strategic response to the changing regulatory environment.

However, total dependency on external capital ($3.2B in financing inflows in FY2025 against $(421M) operating cash flow), 5.6x leverage with an Altman Z of 0.46, management-guided 2026 declines, the OBBB tax credit truncation, tariff-driven cost inflation, and universal C-suite selling at prices essentially equal to the current stock price collectively make this an inappropriate investment at $13.43. The trailing P/E of 7.8x is misleading because HLBV-allocated GAAP earnings do not represent sustainable free cash flow. On forward estimates of $0.44 (FY2026) and $0.65 (FY2027), the stock trades at 30x and 21x respectively — not cheap relative to the risk profile.

The 29.4% short interest prevents recommending this as an active short despite the fundamental weakness. The correct posture is to avoid owning RUN and revisit when the business demonstrates positive operating cash flow, short interest normalizes, and the OBBB-driven transition costs are fully absorbed.
