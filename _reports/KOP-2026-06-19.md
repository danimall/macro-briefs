**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Koppers Holdings is a vertically integrated specialty-materials company executing a deliberate three-year transformation toward a smaller, higher-margin, higher-FCF business. The headline metric most investors anchor on, FY25 revenue down 10.2% to $1,879.3M, overstates deterioration, because a meaningful portion of that decline is intentional: management is shrinking the low-margin Carbon Materials & Chemicals (CMC) segment and exited phthalic anhydride production. Adjusted EBITDA held essentially flat at ~$257M even as revenue fell $212.8M, with RUPS adjusted EBITDA margin expanding 8.7%→11.7% and CMC 7.4%→11.2%. The recent earnings record reinforces this: KOP beat consensus by +29.7% in Q1'26 and +16.8% in Q4'25, and Performance Chemicals (PC) rebounded sharply in Q1'26 (+17.5% sales, +28% EBITDA), undercutting the bear thesis that PC's 2025 share loss was permanent.

Against that improving operating picture sits a balance sheet that leaves little room for error. Total debt is $928.3M, net leverage is 3.3-3.4x (against a 4.75x covenant ceiling), and interest expense consumed $66.1M in FY25. CMC's Q1'26 EBITDA collapsed to $0.9M (1.0% margin), which crystallizes the segment's exposure to the secular decline in non-Asian coal-tar supply. Layer on a CFO transition executed mid-transformation, persistent five-year TSR underperformance (89.66 vs. a 154.36 peer group), CEO net selling with zero open-market insider buying, and a newly emerged copper-tariff hedge dislocation, and the negatives roughly offset the positives.

EV/EBITDA of approximately 6.9x on flat adjusted EBITDA, an entire published analyst target range ($48-$55) above the $43.73 spot, and a forward P/E of ~11.6x argue the stock is not expensive; leverage and CMC's secular squeeze argue it is not cheap enough to be a clear buy. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric, which defines a HOLD. I rate KOP HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the path to a BUY requires demonstrated deleveraging and confirmation the utility-pole tailwind is compounding; the path to AVOID requires CMC weakness to spread to RUPS/PC or a covenant problem to emerge.

## Company Overview

Koppers serves niche industrial end-markets through three segments:

- **Railroad & Utility Products and Services (RUPS)** — ~49% of FY25 sales ($926.8M). The #1 supplier of railroad crossties to North American Class I railroads (roughly 75% of NA railroad sales under long-term contracts; supplies all Class I railroads) and the #2 US utility-pole producer (serves 8 of the top 10 utilities), operating 18 wood-treating plants. Primary competitor: Stella-Jones.
- **Performance Chemicals (PC)** — ~29% of FY25 sales ($543.8M). Global leader in copper-based wood preservatives (MicroPro, FlamePro), supplying 10 of the 11 largest US lumber treaters, vertically integrated in copper (~30M lbs scrap copper purchased annually).
- **Carbon Materials & Chemicals (CMC)** — ~22% of FY25 sales ($408.7M). The largest global creosote supplier to the NA railroad industry, plus carbon pitch (aluminum/steel) and naphthalene. Primary competitor: Rain Carbon.

The structural moat is vertical integration: CMC's creosote supplies 100% of RUPS's NA creosote needs, and PC's CCA/DCOI feeds RUPS pole treating. This delivers security of supply and a logistics edge: crosstie plants sit directly on customers' rail lines. The clearest secular tailwind in the portfolio is utility-pole demand, driven by grid hardening, electrification, aging pole infrastructure, and data-center power build-out.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trend:**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,980.5 | 2,154.2 | 2,092.1 | 1,879.3 |
| Operating income ($M) | 137.7 | 195.2 | 148.2 | 167.8 |
| Net income ($M) | 63.4 | 89.2 | 52.4 | 56.0 |
| Adjusted EBITDA ($M) | 228.1 | 256.4 | 261.6 | 256.7 |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | 399.4 | 498.9 | 488.7 | 574.0 |

The defining feature of FY25 is the divergence between revenue and adjusted EBITDA. PC volume fell 16.5% on a market-share shift, and CMC fell 17.9% on ceased phthalic anhydride production and lower carbon-pitch prices. RUPS proved resilient (–1.7%). Two of three segments expanded margins, RUPS to 11.7% and CMC to 11.2%, while PC compressed from 21.9% to 18.9% on scrap-copper cost pressure. Operating income rose to $167.8M from $148.2M. This is the operating signature of deliberate revenue-shedding, not broad deterioration.

**Q1'26** shows the same two-sided pattern. Revenue was essentially flat YoY ($455.3M vs. $456.5M), and net income swung to +$7.1M from –$13.9M (the prior year carried a $29M pension settlement charge). PC rebounded (volumes +15%), directly countering the bear case that PC's 2025 share loss was structural. The concern was CMC, where EBITDA collapse dragged consolidated adjusted EBITDA down 11.2% to $49.3M and pushed cost of sales to 81% of sales (from 77%).

**Cash flow** improved materially. FY25 operating cash flow was $122.5M with FCF of $67.5M (a thin 3.6% FCF margin). Q1'26 OCF jumped to +$46.3M from –$22.7M a year earlier (the prior period was burdened by pension funding). Capex is guided to ~$55M for 2026.

**Leverage is the central issue.** Long-term debt is $914.3M, D/E ~2.29x. Interest coverage is 4.4x against a 2.0x minimum: in compliance with cushion, but interest expense absorbs meaningful cash flow. Altman Z of 2.26 sits in the grey zone. Liquidity is ~$386M (revolver plus cash), and substantially all assets are pledged as collateral.

**Capital allocation** is shareholder-friendly but draws on a stretched balance sheet. KOP authorized a $100M buyback in Feb 2025 and repurchased 580,418 shares at ~$37.7 in Q1'26, with ~$55.4M remaining. Share count is declining (20.66M→19.86M). Buying back stock at $37.7, below the current $43.73, was good capital allocation in hindsight, but it spent liquidity that could have serviced debt.

## Growth Analysis

The consolidated top line is contracting, but the relevant question is whether the businesses Koppers intends to keep are growing. The estimate-revision data supports a more constructive view than the headline. Current-quarter consensus was revised down 16.7% over 90 days and the full-year estimate down 4.8%, but this softness is concentrated in CMC, the ~22% segment being deliberately wound down (Stickney, IL coal-tar distillation closing by end-2026; phthalic anhydride already exited). Next-year EPS estimates were essentially unchanged (+0.1%). This is a business shedding a structurally challenged unit while the keeper segments hold, not one in broad-based estimate decline.

On the growth side of the ledger:

- **Utility poles** are the clearest secular tailwind. Demand is supported by grid hardening, electrification, aging pole infrastructure, and data-center power build-out, a demand theme corroborated across the broader power/grid-infrastructure complex. Koppers is the #2 US pole producer and is expanding in the midwest/west.
- **RUPS crosstie demand** is stable. The RTA estimates ~19.9M ties for 2026 (~13.3-13.4M Class I), flat YoY and within the historical 18-22M band.
- **PC** is tied to home repair/remodel (LIRA forecasting +1.6-2.9% in 2026), and the Q1'26 volume recovery suggests the 2025 share loss was at least partly cyclical.
- **CMC** is the deliberate scale-back. Coal-tar availability declines with non-Asian blast-furnace steel, and carbon-pitch demand for aluminum smelting has migrated to low-energy-cost regions where Koppers has less presence.

Growth prospects score modestly below neutral. The transformation is credible and one segment has a genuine secular tailwind, but consolidated revenue is shrinking, EBITDA is flat rather than growing, and accelerating estimate revisions are absent. There is no +30% revision velocity here that would signal a fundamental change.

## Valuation Assessment

KOP trades at a forward P/E of ~11.6x and roughly 6.9x EV/EBITDA. The Materials peer median P/E is 21.0x, though that comparison is noisy: several listed peers (SXC, SCL, TWI) carry negative or distorted multiples, so the sector is heterogeneous rather than a clean benchmark. On its own merits, ~6.9x EV/EBITDA on flat-but-stable adjusted EBITDA, with two segments expanding margins, is not a stretched multiple.

The published analyst targets sit entirely above spot: low $48, mean $51, median $50, high $55, implying ~10-26% upside if those targets are credible. Four consecutive months show 1 strong buy + 1 buy and zero holds or sells. I take this as evidence the stock is not obviously overpriced, but I weight it cautiously: this is a two-analyst coverage universe, thin enough that consensus can lag a fast-moving leveraged story in either direction.

I do not score valuation as a high-conviction "cheap" signal for two reasons. First, a compressed multiple on a 3.3-3.4x-levered cyclical with a secularly declining 22% segment can persist; leverage and CMC are real reasons the market discounts the equity, not a clear mispricing. Second, cheapness without an identified catalyst that forces re-rating tends to be a value trap rather than an entry. The catalyst here, deleveraging plus utility-pole margin compounding, is plausible but slow and not yet demonstrated in deleveraging (net leverage actually ticked up to 3.4x in Q1). The valuation supports the stock's downside but does not by itself create asymmetric upside. That nets to a roughly neutral valuation score: cheap enough to limit downside, not cheap enough relative to the leverage risk to be a buy.

## Competitive Landscape

Koppers holds defensible positions in its two keeper segments. RUPS operates in a duopoly-like structure with Stella-Jones, where Koppers' internal creosote supply and on-rail-line plant footprint create switching costs and logistics advantages. PC is the global leader in copper-based preservatives, supplying 10 of the 11 largest US lumber treaters, with vertical integration into copper. These are stable-to-improving positions: RUPS margins expanded in FY25, and PC's Q1'26 volume recovery suggests its 2025 share loss was not a permanent structural erosion.

CMC is the weak leg. As the largest global creosote supplier, it retains scale, but it faces a secular squeeze: declining non-Asian coal-tar supply and the migration of carbon-pitch demand to low-energy-cost smelting regions. The competitive position there is deteriorating, which is why management is shrinking it.

The unexplained item worth monitoring is the Florence, SC RUPS plant idling, attributed in the filing to "lower forecast demand from its largest customer." A specific customer relationship softening would be a more concerning signal than a routine capacity consolidation, and the filing does not clarify which it is. On balance, competitive position is stable, with two improving segments offsetting one declining one.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Leverage** is the dominant risk, explicitly flagged first in the 10-K. Variable-rate exposure and fully pledged assets amplify cyclicality and limit flexibility. In compliance with covenant cushion, but a downturn that compresses EBITDA narrows that cushion quickly.
2. **CMC secular decline.** Coal-tar availability shrinks with non-Asian metallurgical coke / blast-furnace steel. CMC's Q1'26 near-zero EBITDA shows how fragile the segment is to pitch pricing (down ~9% globally). The open question is whether Q1 was a quarter-specific trough or a structural step-down.
3. **Copper-tariff hedge dislocation.** July 2025 50% US tariffs on semi-finished/derivative copper products created a COMEX-LME premium gap that degrades PC's hedge effectiveness (hedges on LME, buys on COMEX). Estimated $4-8M pre-tax impact over the next 12 months.
4. **Environmental/legacy liabilities.** $10.2M environmental reserve, ~$13M environmental capex in 2026, numerous toxic-tort suits (coal-tar pitch, creosote, benzene). The Beazer East indemnity backstops pre-1988 contamination, but post-July 2019 untendered claims may flow back to Koppers, and Beazer's performance is not guaranteed.
5. **Goodwill impairment.** $329.4M goodwill with reporting-unit cushions narrowed to 24%-58% (from 22%-103%), sensitive to stock-price declines.
6. **Restructuring execution.** FY25 impairment/restructuring jumped to $51.9M (from $17.3M), with Vance/Florence/Stickney closures carrying overrun risk, alongside a multi-year ERP migration that introduces internal-control risk.
7. **Pension.** Post-retirement obligations exceed assets by $14.5M (81% funded); the US qualified plan was terminated at a ~$28-29M settlement cost in 2024-25.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is elevated at 72.9% versus 1-year HV of 37.9%, with backwardation (1-month 72.9% vs. 3-month 41.3%) and an elevated 46.0% put skew implying a ±12.1% near-term move. On its face this reads as imminent stress. I down-weight it heavily. The chain is thin (22 contracts across 4 expirations), and the snapshot includes a flagged data artifact (a reported 1256.5% IV reading) that renders the delta comparison non-informative. Elevated near-term IV ahead of an earnings print is mechanically routine and is not, on this evidence, independent fundamental corroboration of a bear case. Short interest is moderate at 7.3% of float (5.5 days to cover): neither a crowded short nor a contrarian setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$55, +26%)
The transformation delivers. Flat EBITDA on a shrinking revenue base proves margin discipline is structural, not transient. PC's Q1 recovery continues, RUPS margins keep expanding, and the utility-pole tailwind compounds revenue and mix. CMC stabilizes at a smaller, higher-margin footprint after the Stickney closure removes the worst drag. FCF improves, net leverage falls below 3.0x, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median, and the buyback at sub-$40 prices looks prescient. This is roughly the published high target of $55.

### Bear Case (~$32-34, -22% to -27%)
CMC's Q1 near-zero EBITDA proves structural, not a trough, and pitch/coal-tar pressure spreads. The "largest customer" demand drop at Florence signals a RUPS relationship softening rather than routine consolidation. Copper-tariff hedge dislocation pressures PC margins just as the volume recovery flatters the segment. A cyclical downturn compresses EBITDA, narrowing the covenant cushion and pressuring goodwill. Leverage forces management to choose between buybacks and deleveraging. The stock re-rates toward its 52-week low of $25 / low-$30s.

### Base Case (~$48-51)
The transformation grinds forward: margins hold, PC recovery sticks, utility poles provide modest growth, CMC remains a manageable drag, but deleveraging is slow and the multiple stays compressed on leverage concerns. The stock drifts toward the lower end of the analyst range, roughly $48-51, broadly in line with consensus targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the offsetting forces produce roughly symmetric risk/reward, and I lack the evidence for a confident directional call. Leverage plus CMC's secular decline are real negatives roughly offset by the RUPS pole tailwind, PC recovery, flat EBITDA, and improving FCF.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage falls below 3.0x on improving FCF, demonstrating the transformation is deleveraging the balance sheet (currently 3.4x and ticking up).
- CMC EBITDA stabilizes at a sustainable level (e.g., >$5M/quarter) post-Stickney closure, confirming Q1'26's level was a trough, not a step-down.
- Open-market insider buying emerges, or estimate revisions for RUPS/PC turn positive, confirming the keeper segments are inflecting.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CMC weakness spreads into RUPS or PC margins (e.g., consolidated adjusted EBITDA down >15% YoY for two consecutive quarters).
- Goodwill impairment charge or covenant-cushion erosion (net leverage approaching 4.0x).
- Confirmation that the Florence "largest customer" demand drop reflects a structural loss of a Class I relationship.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (~August 2026), specifically CMC's EBITDA trajectory, the net-leverage print, and PC margin durability under the copper-tariff dislocation. The new CFO's (Eric Brenner, started May 26) first full quarter and any change in capital-allocation tone are also key watch items.

## Conclusion

Koppers is a leveraged specialty-materials company in the middle of a credible margin-focused transformation. The bear framing of "cheap-but-deservedly-cheap, falling estimates, no catalyst" does not survive scrutiny: adjusted EBITDA is flat during deliberate revenue-shedding, two of three segments are expanding margins, PC inflected +28% EBITDA in Q1'26, the company beat its last two quarters by +29.7% and +16.8%, and the downward estimate revision is concentrated in the CMC unit it is intentionally shrinking. The entire published analyst target range sits above spot, and there is no asymmetric short setup (my own work puts reward/risk near 1:1).

The bull case is equally incomplete. Net leverage of 3.4x on $928M debt, CMC's structural coal-tar squeeze, a CFO transition mid-overhaul, persistent TSR underperformance (90 vs. 154), CEO net selling with no insider buying, and a copper-tariff hedge dislocation all cap the upside and leave little error margin. The estimate-revision picture is stable rather than accelerating, so the strongest forward buy signal is absent.

A name with symmetric risk/reward, an improving margin trajectory, recent earnings beats, and a bullish analyst backdrop, offset by genuine leverage and secular-decline risk, is a HOLD. I rate KOP **HOLD**. The path to a more constructive view runs through demonstrated deleveraging and CMC stabilization; the path to a negative view runs through CMC weakness spreading or a covenant problem emerging. Until one of those resolves, the stock is fairly valued at ~6.9x EV/EBITDA and roughly 11.6x forward earnings.
