## Executive Summary

SkyWest is the largest independent regional airline operator in the U.S., operating ~2,260 daily departures under capacity purchase agreements with United (44%), Delta (28%), American (19%), and Alaska (9%). The CPA model converts the airline into a quasi-utility: partners set pricing, bear fuel risk, and control scheduling, while SkyWest earns fixed fees for block hours and departures. This model produced $428M in net income on $4.1B revenue in FY2025, representing a complete recovery from the 2022-2023 pilot shortage that compressed earnings to $34M.

The stock has declined approximately 30% from its August 2025 high of $123.94 to approximately $84.82 (as of report date), driven by three factors: (1) normalizing growth as pilot recovery gains are fully captured, (2) new tariff risk on E175 aircraft imported from Brazil, and (3) negative estimate revisions. At 8x trailing earnings and approximately 7.6-7.8x forward FY2026 estimates, the market is pricing SKYW as though earnings will decline materially. Consensus targets range $95-$150, with median at $128, implying 51% upside. Downside is protected by contracted revenue streams, an accelerating buyback, and a fleet expansion program secured through 2032.

## Company Overview

SkyWest Airlines operates 487 aircraft in scheduled service (637 total fleet) across 240+ destinations. The fleet is dominated by the Embraer E175 (271 aircraft as of March 31, 2026), with CRJ-family aircraft (900/700/550/200) constituting the remainder. Revenue splits approximately 84% from capacity purchase agreements (fixed-fee, fuel-risk-free) and 16% from prorate/charter operations where SKYW bears more operational risk.

The business has two segments: SkyWest Airlines & SWC ($3.4B revenue, $263M pre-tax) and SkyWest Leasing ($643M revenue, $303M pre-tax). The leasing segment's profitability reflects the asset ownership structure where SKYW owns aircraft and leases them into the operating entity.

SkyWest Charter (SWC), formed in 2022, received DOT commuter authority in September 2025. The company is evaluating opportunities for EAS subsidized routes and scheduled service, though there is no assurance SWC will be awarded such markets or that demand will materialize. This represents meaningful optionality outside the traditional CPA framework.

The pilot shortage recovery drove block hours +14.7% in FY2025 on a nearly flat fleet (-5 aircraft). Q1'26 block hours grew only +3%, confirming utilization gains are exhausted. Future growth must come from fleet additions.

**Cash Flow:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was $940M (36% growth over $692M in FY2024). However, conventional free cash flow — accounting for aircraft and rotable spare purchases ($546M), buildings/ground equipment ($32M), and aircraft deposits ($75M), partially offset by asset-sale proceeds ($16M) — was approximately $300M–$380M depending on treatment of deposits. Net cash used in investing activities was $652M. While operating cash generation is strong, the $940M operating cash flow figure should not be confused with free cash flow available to shareholders. With $277M in 2026 aircraft commitments plus $550M in debt maturities, cash allocation will remain heavily directed toward fleet investment and debt service.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of $2.4B (down from $2.9B in 2022) is secured against aircraft assets. At YE2025, debt-to-equity was approximately 0.88x, with the capital structure at 53.4% equity / 46.6% debt — an improvement from 47.4% equity in 2024. Liquidity stands at $783M (cash + marketable securities + credit line).

**Capital Allocation:**

Management repurchased $85M in FY2025 and $75M in Q1'26 (at ~$96 average), with $138M remaining under authorization. The buyback is accelerating at lower prices — 783K shares in Q1'26 vs. 268K in Q4'25. No dividend since 2020 (CARES Act restriction, not yet reinstated). The board authorized a $250M buyback increase in May 2025.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is transitioning from recovery-driven (high-teens) to fleet-expansion-driven (mid-single-digits):

**Fleet Pipeline:**
- 69 total E175 delivery positions, including one Alaska E175 delivered in Q1 2026; 68 remaining firm purchase commitments as of March 31, 2026 (consisting of 8 United, 16 Delta, and 44 secured delivery positions for 2028-2032)
- 50 additional purchase rights beyond firm orders
- Company target: 300+ E175s by end of 2028 (vs. 271 currently in service)

**CRJ450 Program:** Converting retired CRJ200s to dual-class 41-seat aircraft with Starlink Wi-Fi for United, beginning Fall 2026. This creates incremental CPA revenue from sunk-cost assets with minimal capex.

**SWC Charter Expansion:** DOT commuter authority enables potential EAS subsidized routes and scheduled service. Currently 11 aircraft; potential to scale as CRJ200s exit mainline CPA flying.

**Consensus estimates (vendor-specific, as of report date):** FY2026 EPS approximately $10.91-$11.10 (management has guided to the "mid-$11" range), FY2027 EPS approximately $11.99. These reflect the transition to steady-state growth. Current-year estimates have seen negative revisions over recent months, primarily reflecting tariff-related cost concerns and Q1'26 operating income softness.

The peer median P/E is 22.1x (though this comparison is imperfect — SNCY at 22x is the only profitable comparable, while ALGT, JBLU are loss-making). Air Lease (AL) at 7.0x is the closest relevant comp — an asset-heavy aircraft financing model with contracted cash flows.

The market is pricing SKYW at a 65% discount to the profitable peer (SNCY) and at parity with Air Lease, which has lower growth. This implies the market expects earnings to decline from current levels, or views the tariff/growth-normalization risks as severely impairing future profitability.

**Why the market may be wrong:** SKYW's CPA model provides revenue visibility that no other publicly traded airline achieves. Individual aircraft contract expirations/removal dates extend as far as 2034, with United removal dates spanning 2026-2033, Delta 2026-2034, and American 2027-2032. Partners cannot easily replace SKYW's capacity — there is no other independent regional with 500+ aircraft and relationships with all four majors. The 8x multiple prices zero growth into perpetuity, yet fleet expansion alone provides 5-10% annual growth through 2028+.

The consensus price target median is $128 (51% upside) with a floor at $95 (12% upside). Five of six analysts rate the stock Buy.

## Competitive Landscape

SkyWest's competitive position rests on three structural advantages:

1. **Scale:** 500 aircraft across 240+ destinations with all four major U.S. carriers. No other independent regional approaches this breadth. Republic Airways (~200 aircraft) is the closest competitor.

2. **Diversification:** Serving all four majors simultaneously reduces concentration risk and provides negotiating leverage. If one partner reduces flying, SKYW can redeploy assets to others. Wholly-owned regionals (Endeavor/Delta, Envoy/PSA/American, Horizon/Alaska) lack this flexibility.

3. **Pilot Pipeline:** Scale enables superior pilot recruitment infrastructure, including pathway programs to major airlines. This was the critical bottleneck in 2022-2023 and SKYW's recovery demonstrates the advantage of being the largest recruiter.

**Structural Risk:** Scope clauses (76-seat maximum) cap the aircraft size available for regional operations. Majors' wholly-owned regionals (Endeavor, Envoy) could theoretically absorb more flying, reducing SKYW's addressable market. However, the January 2026 contract extensions (40 E175s with United, 13 with Delta) demonstrate partners continue choosing SKYW's services.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Impact on E175 Deliveries (HIGH — NEW):**
The 10-K identifies recently enacted tariffs as having a significant impact on the cost of aircraft parts and supplies sourced internationally, including non-U.S.-manufactured components used on E175 aircraft imported from Brazil. With 68 remaining firm orders (~$2.3B total commitment), tariff exposure is material, though the precise impact depends on which cost components tariffs apply to and whether contractual protections exist. As an illustrative sensitivity, a 10-15% increase on the full commitment value would imply $230-345M in added costs — though actual exposure may be lower if tariffs apply primarily to parts/components rather than full aircraft value. Pass-through in CPA negotiations is uncertain but likely given that major airlines need the capacity and SKYW is the only scaled delivery vehicle.

**Growth Normalization:**
Block hour growth decelerated from +14.7% (FY2025) to +3% (Q1'26). The pilot recovery story is complete. Mid-single-digit earnings growth is the realistic baseline.

**Estimate Revisions (Negative):**
Recent consensus revisions have been negative, reflecting higher pilot training costs, maintenance cost escalation (+32% in FY2025), and tariff uncertainty.

**Partner Concentration:**
72% of aircraft fly for United or Delta. Loss of either relationship would be material (though contract terms span years and replacement would take partners 3-5 years to execute independently).

**Maintenance Cost Escalation:**
Aircraft maintenance surged 32.4% in FY2025. Aging CRJ fleet drives this structurally higher. Partially mitigated by CRJ retirement/CRJ450 conversion, but remains a headwind through 2027-2028.

## Conclusion

SkyWest at 8x earnings represents a compelling risk/reward for a contracted infrastructure business classified as an airline. The CPA model insulates against the fuel/yield/demand volatility that defines traditional airline investing. The approximately 30% drawdown from highs is driven by legitimate concerns (growth normalization, tariff risk, falling estimates), but the valuation already discounts significant impairment that the business fundamentals do not support.

SKYW's contracted revenue from investment-grade counterparties (United, Delta, American, Alaska), with individual contract expirations/removal dates extending as far as 2034, combined with visible fleet expansion and an accelerating buyback at depressed prices, warrants a multiple closer to 10-11x. The negative estimate revisions and tariff overhang are real headwinds, but manageable within the CPA framework where cost pass-throughs are negotiable. SkyWest has surpassed consensus EPS estimates three times over the last four quarters, providing confidence that consensus remains conservative. At approximately $85, the downside is protected by a $70 floor (17% below) while upside to consensus median target ($128) offers 51%.

The primary risk to this thesis is tariff-driven margin compression on E175 deliveries that cannot be passed through to partners. This is testable: if Q2'26 guidance or commentary indicates inability to renegotiate CPAs for tariff absorption, the thesis weakens materially.
