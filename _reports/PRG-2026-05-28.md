**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

PROG Holdings operates the largest virtual lease-to-own platform in the United States through Progressive Leasing (~96% of 2025 revenue), supplemented by a fast-growing BNPL product (Four Technologies) and a newly acquired employee purchase program (Purchasing Power, closed January 2026 for $420M). The stock surged ~24% from late April following a Q1 2026 earnings beat ($1.24 vs. $0.80 estimate, +54.4% surprise) and now trades at $37.60, or approximately 8.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.65.

The valuation is optically cheap relative to peers (median P/E 18.3x) and consensus estimates are rising sharply (+29.3% over 90 days for FY2026). However, the business faces structural headwinds that justify a persistent discount: the core Progressive Leasing segment is in unit decline (GMV -8.6% in 2025), the company depends on brick-and-mortar furniture retailers that are going bankrupt sequentially (Big Lots 2024, American Signature 2025), and 54.8% of revenue comes from just three POS partners. Post-acquisition leverage at ~2.5x constrains financial flexibility during a period when the subprime consumer is under increasing stress from elevated cost of living, higher rates, and potential tariff-driven price increases.

The investment case hinges on whether the Purchasing Power acquisition and Four's continued growth can offset Progressive Leasing's decay fast enough to sustain earnings growth. At 8x forward earnings, some impairment is priced, but the path to the $45 median analyst target requires execution on integration, continued Four momentum, and no further major partner losses — all of which carry meaningful uncertainty.

## Company Overview

PROG Holdings was spun off from Aaron's Holdings in November 2020. The company provides alternative payment solutions to underserved consumers (near-prime/subprime) through four products:

- **Progressive Leasing** (~96% of 2025 revenue, ~$2.31B): Virtual point-of-sale lease-purchase platform integrated into ~24,000 third-party retail locations. Consumers lease merchandise (furniture, electronics, appliances, jewelry) with no credit needed. Typical lease term: ≤12 months. The company purchases merchandise from retailers, leases to consumers, and recognizes depreciation as the primary expense (~66% of revenue).

- **Four Technologies** (~3% of 2025 revenue, ~$74M): BNPL product offering four interest-free installments. GMV grew 144% YoY in 2025 to $737M. Turned profitable in 2025.

- **Purchasing Power** (acquired Jan 2, 2026): Employee benefit program allowing purchases via payroll deduction. Expected $680-730M revenue and $50-60M EBITDA in 2026.

- **MoneyApp**: Interest-free cash advance mobile app; not yet material.

The company sold its Vive Financial (revolving credit) segment in October 2025 for $143.9M to sharpen strategic focus.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
Consolidated revenue has been stagnant at ~$2.4B for four years (2022-2025). Progressive Leasing's underlying decline has been masked by Four's growth. The Purchasing Power acquisition creates an artificial step-change to $3.0-3.1B in 2026, but organic growth in the legacy business remains negative.

**Profitability:**
- Operating income: $207M in 2025 (8.6% margin), reflecting high depreciation loads inherent to the lease model
- Non-GAAP EPS: $3.51 in FY2025; guided $4.40-$4.80 for FY2026
- Q1 2026 EPS of $1.24 (non-GAAP) represented a 54.4% beat and implies the company is tracking toward the high end of annual guidance
- Four turned profitable in 2025 ($2.8M pretax, ~$10M adjusted EBITDA)

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow surged to $335M in 2025 (vs. $138.5M in 2024), driven by $154M reduction in lease merchandise purchases. This improvement reflects the shrinking portfolio — fewer new leases means less capital deployed, which boosts near-term cash flow but signals underlying demand weakness.

**Balance Sheet Post-Acquisition:**
- Recourse debt: $600M senior notes (6.00%, 2029) + $135M revolver draw + $125M term loan = ~$860M
- Non-recourse ABS: ~$339M (Purchasing Power securitization)
- Cash: ~$309M at YE 2025 (deployed for acquisition)
- Net leverage: ~2.5x, targeting 1.5-2.0x
- Altman Z-Score: 5.05 (safe zone)

**Capital Returns:**
Share count reduced 44.8% since spin-off ($1.12B in buybacks). However, buyback pace will decelerate given deleveraging priority. Dividend: $0.52/year (~1.4% yield).

## Growth Analysis

**Bear Case for Growth:** Progressive Leasing GMV declined 8.6% in 2025, and the portfolio entered 2026 9.4% smaller than a year ago. The company's primary retail partners are in structurally challenged categories (brick-and-mortar furniture at 58% of Progressive Leasing volume). Sequential partner bankruptcies (Big Lots removed ~$40M/quarter in GMV; American Signature bankrupt Nov 2025) create discrete GMV losses that are difficult to replace.

**Bull Case for Growth:**
1. Purchasing Power adds $680-730M revenue at higher margin (payroll deduction model has lower credit risk)
2. Four GMV growth of 144% in 2025, with unit economics inflecting positive
3. E-commerce penetration rising (23.3% of GMV in 2025, targeting 30%+ in 2026), which partially offsets brick-and-mortar decline
4. Cross-sell strategy generating $45M incremental leasing GMV (up from $23M prior year)
5. Bonus depreciation permanently extended under OBBBA creates structural tax benefit

**Net Assessment:** Organic growth in the core business is negative. Total company growth is acquisition-dependent. Four's momentum is genuine but starting from a small base ($74M revenue vs. $2.3B Progressive Leasing). The growth profile is that of a business managing decline in its core while building adjacent revenue streams — a challenging but not uncommon pattern in consumer finance.

FY2026 consensus EPS estimates rose from $3.60 to $4.65 over 90 days (+29.3%), primarily reflecting Purchasing Power accretion and Q1 beat momentum. FY2027 consensus is $5.33, implying 14.6% YoY growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.60, PRG trades at:
- 8.1x FY2026E EPS ($4.65)
- 7.1x FY2027E EPS ($5.33)
- ~12.4x trailing EPS ($3.04)

Peer comparison:
- Consumer finance peer median: 18.3x P/E (EZPW)
- ECPG (closest comp in debt buying/subprime): 6.2x
- WRLD (subprime lending): 23.0x

The wide dispersion among peers reflects different business models and credit quality profiles. PRG's 8.1x forward multiple sits between ECPG's 6.2x (pure debt collector) and EZPW's 18.3x (pawn/secured lending). The discount to WRLD (23x) and EZPW (18x) is partially justified by PRG's revenue stagnation and partner concentration risk.

**What the market is pricing:** At 8x forward, the market assigns low confidence to earnings durability — implying either (a) Progressive Leasing continues declining, offsetting acquisition gains, or (b) subprime consumer deterioration raises write-offs materially. The market is not pricing the full upside if integration succeeds and Four scales.

**Analyst targets:** Low $33, median $45, high $57. Current price sits 20% below median and 52% below high target. Six of eight covering analysts rate BUY/Strong Buy.

The valuation is undemanding on forward numbers, but the low multiple reflects real structural risks that could compress earnings below current estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

Progressive Leasing holds the dominant position in virtual lease-to-own by POS count (~24,000 locations) and GMV volume. Its moat consists of:
- Proprietary decisioning algorithms trained on extensive lease performance data
- Deep integrations with major retail POS systems (switching costs for partners)
- Scale advantages in merchandise purchasing and servicing

**Deterioration signals:**
- GMV declining 8.6% in 2025 (not gaining share)
- Competitor Katapult growing its retail partnerships aggressively
- Acima (owned by Rent-A-Center/Upbound Group) expanding in similar categories
- The addressable market itself is shrinking as brick-and-mortar furniture retail declines secularly

In BNPL, Four is a small player in a market dominated by Affirm, Klarna, and Afterpay. Its differentiation (targeting subprime/near-prime) is a niche that larger players may choose to enter or abandon depending on regulatory/credit environment.

Purchasing Power operates in a less competitive space (employer-sponsored purchase programs), but market size is limited.

**Net:** Competitive position is stable in the near term but secularly challenged. The core franchise is tied to a declining retail format.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **POS Partner Concentration (54.8%/top 3; 77.0%/top 10):** Loss of any single major partner would create an immediate, material revenue hole. Big Lots and American Signature bankruptcies already demonstrated this vulnerability. The remaining top partners operate in stressed categories.

2. **Subprime Consumer Deterioration:** PRG's core customer is increasingly stressed. Provision for lease write-offs at 7.5% of lease revenues could increase to the 8%+ range, compressing margins.

3. **Tariff-Driven Merchandise Price Increases:** Imported furniture and electronics face potential tariff escalation. Higher ticket prices reduce consumer willingness to lease, compressing GMV.

4. **Regulatory:** NY BNPL legislation (May 2025), 7-state AG coordinated inquiry (Dec 2025), and ongoing FTC compliance monitoring create persistent overhang. Material adverse regulatory action could impair Four's growth trajectory.

5. **Integration Execution:** $420M Purchasing Power acquisition at 2.5x leverage during economic uncertainty increases operational complexity and constrains flexibility if conditions deteriorate.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (54.2% near vs. 49.3% long-dated), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk rather than structural deterioration. Put/call OI ratio at 0.03 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting limited institutional hedging against downside. The implied move of ±11.8% on nearest expiry reflects elevated uncertainty. Combined with 12.1% short interest (elevated), the stock has both bullish options positioning and meaningful bearish equity positioning — suggesting conviction divergence between options and equity markets.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($45-57, +20% to +52%):**
- Purchasing Power integration succeeds, contributing full $50-60M EBITDA
- Four continues 100%+ GMV growth, reaching profitability at scale by 2027
- Progressive Leasing stabilizes via e-commerce shift (targeting 30%+ penetration)
- Buyback resumes once leverage reaches 2.0x target
- Bonus depreciation creates ongoing tax tailwind
- Multiple re-rates from 8x to 10-11x as earnings durability is demonstrated

**Bear Case ($25-28, -25% to -33%):**
- Another top-3 POS partner experiences financial distress (plausible given retail environment)
- Subprime consumer deterioration pushes write-offs above 8.5%, compressing margins
- Purchasing Power integration stumbles, EBITDA contribution disappoints ($30-40M vs. $50-60M guided)
- Regulatory action against BNPL operations constrains Four's growth
- Revenue misses force management to cut guidance; multiple compresses to 6x

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Progressive Leasing GMV turns positive YoY for two consecutive quarters
2. Stock pulls back to $30-32 (6.5-7x forward EPS) on macro weakness without fundamental deterioration
3. Purchasing Power demonstrates $55M+ EBITDA contribution over first 4 quarters

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Loss of a top-3 POS partner (announced departure or bankruptcy)
2. Write-off rate exceeds 9% of lease revenues for two consecutive quarters
3. FY2026 EPS guidance revised below $4.00

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This will provide the first clean quarter of Purchasing Power contribution data and updated consumer credit metrics heading into the seasonally weaker back half.

## Conclusion

PRG presents a genuinely cheap stock (8x forward earnings, 7x FY2027) with legitimate reasons for the discount. The estimate revision momentum (+29.3% on FY2026 over 90 days) and Q1 beat are positive, but they primarily reflect Purchasing Power accretion rather than organic improvement. The core business is in secular decline, partner concentration risk is extreme, and the subprime consumer is deteriorating.

At current prices, the stock offers reasonable upside to the $45 median target (20%) if execution proceeds as planned, but downside to the low-$20s if a major partner loss or consumer deterioration materializes. The risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric. HOLD with reassessment after Q2 results provide visibility on integration progress and consumer credit trends.
