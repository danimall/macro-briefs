**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Consolidated Edison is a pure-play regulated delivery utility whose earnings derive almost entirely from three New York subsidiaries: CECONY (~93% of assets, ~79-85% of net income), Orange & Rockland, and Con Edison Transmission. The business is a legal monopoly in one of the densest, wealthiest, and most supply-constrained service territories in North America, with revenue decoupling and pass-through fuel mechanisms that insulate it from volume and commodity swings. This is a low-beta, rate-base-growth business with a decades-long dividend-increase streak.

The central issue is that the standard regulated-utility formula (5-7% rate-base growth translating to 5-7% EPS growth) is being materially diluted at Con Edison by aggressive equity issuance. The company issued ~$776M of common stock in Q1 2026 on top of ~$1.3B in Q1 2025, lifting average share count roughly 4% YoY. Adjusted EPS has compounded steadily at ~5-6% ($4.39 in 2021 to $5.70 in 2025), but the Q1 2026 headline of GAAP EPS +13% YoY is misleading: roughly $0.37/share came from a one-time gain on the Mountain Valley Pipeline stake sale, and stripping that out, core CECONY net income actually declined $12M YoY ($745M to $733M) under the weight of dilution, higher O&M, and higher interest. An external adjusted read showed Q1 2026 EPS of ~$2.17 missing consensus by ~6.6%.

We rate ED a HOLD on income-hold rationale. The stock at $111.98 sits near the top of its 52-week range ($94.96-$116.23), trades at ~18.3x forward EPS (a discount to the 22.6x peer median), and offers a ~3.2% yield. That peer discount is not a mispricing to exploit — it reflects genuine below-median allowed-ROE outcomes, a contentious steam rate case, and several unquantified contingencies (weld-fraud prudence probe, a $1.03B tax regulatory asset earning no return, $1.35B of aged receivables). Consensus targets (mean $111.85, median $112.00) sit essentially at spot. There is no catalyst that argues for meaningful appreciation, and the downside is well-supported by the dividend and the defensive rate-base model. Hold for yield, not for capital gains.

## Company Overview

Con Edison is a New York holding company that has deliberately simplified itself into a regulated transmission-and-distribution pure play. The subsidiaries:

- **CECONY** provides electric service to ~3.7 million customers across NYC (excluding part of Queens) and most of Westchester, gas to ~1.1 million customers, and operates the largest steam distribution system in the United States (~16,975 MMlb/year to ~1,485 Manhattan customers). The steam franchise is a unique, high-barrier asset.
- **Orange & Rockland** serves ~0.3 million regulated electric customers in southeastern NY and northern NJ (~6% of assets).
- **Con Edison Transmission** holds FERC-regulated transmission investments, principally a ~45.7% interest in New York Transco (TOTS, NYES, and the developing Propel NY Energy project).

The competitive structure is a legal monopoly. Con Edison faces no direct competitors in its delivery franchises; its "competition" is the NYSPSC regulator and the political pressure over affordability. Delivery revenue is protected by decoupling mechanisms and weather-normalization clauses, and the utility earns no margin on commodity fuel, purchased power, or gas, which flow through to customers. Earnings are a function of approved rate base and allowed ROE.

The strategic direction is confirmed by the recent divestiture cadence: the MVP stake sale ($357.5M) completed in Q1 2026, an agreement to sell the Honeoye gas storage interest ($5M) in April 2026, and prior exits from Broken Bow II wind (January 2025) and the Crane solar tax-equity interest (October 2025). Con Edison is shedding non-utility investments to become a cleaner regulated T&D and transmission story.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** Total operating revenues were $5,095M, up 6.2% YoY from $4,798M, driven by rate increases and higher pass-through fuel/purchased power. Net income was $924M versus $791M, diluted EPS $2.54 versus $2.25. The headline growth overstates operating momentum. Approximately $134M of the MVP gain (the $0.37/share after-tax noted above) came at CET. Excluding it, core utility earnings were flat-to-down: CECONY net income declined pressured by equity-issuance dilution (-$0.08), higher O&M (-$0.08), and higher interest expense (-$0.03), only partly offset by rate-base growth (+$0.08). The external adjusted read of ~$2.17 EPS (missing consensus of ~$2.32, down 3.6% YoY) is the truer picture of the operating quarter.

**Earnings surprise track record.** The four-quarter record is mixed: 2 beats, 2 misses, with the two most recent quarters both missing (Q4 2025 -8.8%, Q1 2026 -6.9%). This softens confidence in near-term consensus and is consistent with core earnings running below plan on dilution and cost inflation.

**Multi-year trajectory.** Adjusted EPS: $4.39 (2021), $4.57 (2022), $5.07 (2023), $5.40 (2024), $5.70 (2025), a ~5-6% CAGR. Net income grew from $1.35B (2021) to $2.02B (2025). This is the steady compounding investors buy the name for.

**Balance sheet and capital intensity.** Total assets are $74.7B, net utility plant $56.1B and growing (+$0.7B in a single quarter). Long-term debt is $25.6B; the common-equity ratio improved to 50.0% at the parent / 49.3% at CECONY, above the ~48% assumed in rate cases — healthy for a utility. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.0 is not a distress signal; it is an artifact of applying a manufacturing bankruptcy model to an asset-heavy, regulated, decoupled utility.

**Cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow collapsed to $174M from $837M YoY. This looks alarming but is driven by working-capital timing — a $691M prepayment build, ~$348M of higher recoverable energy-cost deferrals, and slow AR recovery — rather than deteriorating economics. It nonetheless underscores the negative-free-cash-flow reality of the model: capex ran ~$1.17B in the quarter (~$4.7B+ annualized) and is funded by a mix of retained earnings, debt, and equity.

**Equity issuance is the structural drag.** The Q1 2026 issuance settled via forward-sale; average share count rose from ~350M to ~363M YoY. Rate-base growth is being financed heavily with equity, which mutes EPS growth and, in a higher-for-longer rate environment, raises the cost of the negative-FCF financing model. This is the single most important variable capping per-share returns and dividend-growth headroom.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.89/share from $0.85 in Q1 2026 (~$308M paid), continuing a decades-long increase streak. A new $3.5B revolving credit facility (March 2026, expires 2031, debt/total-cap covenant ≤0.65) replaced prior agreements, and a $500M term loan was repaid early — clean liquidity management.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is rate base. Net utility plant at $56B, an electrification/grid-modernization mandate, and NYISO-flagged NYC reliability needs through 2030 provide a long runway of recoverable capex. The mechanical translation is ~5-7% rate-base growth into ~5-7% EPS growth — before dilution.

The problem is the dilution wedge. With ~4% annual share-count growth funding the build, the per-share outcome lands at the low end of that range, or below it in quarters where O&M and interest inflation bite. Estimate revisions confirm the stall: current-year consensus is essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days ($6.11), next-year flat at $6.49, and next-quarter was cut 2.9% in the last 30 days. The only positive revision velocity is the current quarter (+8.8% over 90 days to $0.77), which is small and seasonal. There is no earnings-inflection signal here — the +30% revision threshold that would flag an accelerating fundamental turn is absent by an order of magnitude.

Growth optionality exists but is incremental: the Propel NY Energy transmission project (New York Transco share ~$2.2B, in-service ~2030, ~11.3% ROE) is a meaningful CET vehicle, and NYC reliability tightness supports continued transmission investment. Offsetting this, the decarbonization mandate threatens the gas and steam franchises long-term via declining volumes and stranded-asset risk, even though decoupling protects near-term revenue.

Net, this is a durable mid-single-digit compounder with per-share growth capped by financing choices. The growth thesis is real but unexciting, and the data does not support scoring it as an accelerator.

## Valuation Assessment

At $111.98, ED trades at ~18.3x forward EPS ($6.11 CY consensus) and offers a ~3.2% dividend yield. Against the Multi-Utilities peer set — PCG 13.0x, XEL 22.6x, AEE 19.5x, DTE 24.0x, CNP 26.1x, median 22.6x — Con Edison sits at a meaningful discount to the median multiple. On yield, ED is above the peer median of 2.8%.

The discount is not a mispricing to exploit. It is largely explained by fundamentals: below-median allowed-ROE outcomes (staff testimony for 9.3% ROE / 47.5% equity in the steam case versus CECONY's 9.9% / 48% request signals regulatory pushback), heavier equity dilution than most peers, and a cluster of unquantified contingencies. A cheap-looking multiple in this context is a fair reflection of lower-quality regulatory economics rather than an entry opportunity; treating the discount as a buy signal would be a value trap.

There is no cycle-adjustment argument to make: this is not a trough-earnings inflection stock. Trailing and forward P/E are both ~18.8x on stable, mid-single-digit earnings. The estimate revisions are flat, so analyst targets are not stale floors here; they are fair ceilings. The consensus mean target of $111.85 and median of $112.00 sit essentially at the current price, with the range spanning $94.00 to $130.00. The high target implies ~16% upside on a stretched-ROE scenario; the low implies ~16% downside on adverse rate/contingency outcomes. That symmetry, combined with a price near the 52-week high, argues for a valuation score below the neutral line.

## Competitive Landscape

Con Edison's competitive position is structurally strong and stable. It is a legal monopoly delivery utility in a service territory with no direct competition, protected by revenue decoupling and weather normalization. The steam system is the largest in the country and effectively irreplaceable. NYC's supply/reliability tightness (NYISO ordered certain generators to remain online through May 1, 2029) reinforces the case for continued rate-base investment.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-favorable rather than improving. On the positive side, the reliability squeeze and electrification mandate expand the recoverable capex base, and a March 2026 FERC ruling directing PJM to correct an over-allocation of regional transmission costs to CECONY is modestly favorable (refunds to CECONY electric customers, amount not yet estimable). On the negative side, the gas and steam franchises face long-term terminal risk from New York's electrification mandates, and affordability pressure is visibly translating into tougher ROE and revenue outcomes — the wide gap in the steam case is the clearest evidence. Peer TSR context is in-line: Con Edison's 5-year cumulative TSR of 164 slightly beat its peer group's 159.

The competitive moat is wide and permanent; the pressure comes from the regulator and political affordability dynamics rather than competitors, which is priced into the below-peer multiple.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter are company-specific and several are unquantified:

1. **Rate-case / allowed-ROE risk (central driver).** CECONY's electric and gas plan runs January 2026-December 2028. The contentious steam case (CECONY requested $66M at 9.9% ROE / 48% equity; DPS staff testified March 2026 for $18M at 9.3% ROE / 47.5% equity) is a live indicator of a tougher regulatory posture.

2. **Weld-fraud / main-integrity contingency.** Since 2023, CECONY discovered non-conforming gas and steam main welds, including third-party contractors substituting duplicate weld films. Two contractor employees were indicted for wire fraud (August 2025). An NYSDPS prudence investigation is active, and CECONY has agreed that $100M of gas revenue (2026-2028) is subject to refund pending resolution; the loss is "unable to estimate." This is a genuine, unresolved tail risk unique to CECONY.

3. **$1.03B income-tax regulatory asset earning no return.** From a 2018-initiated NYSPSC normalization audit; recovery is probable but a NYSPSC-ordered write-down would be a material, non-estimable loss.

4. **Aged accounts receivable.** CECONY customer AR of $3.12B includes $1.35B aged over 60 days, versus only $408M pre-COVID. This pressures liquidity and, if write-offs exceed rate allowances, is unrecoverable during the current rate term.

5. **Cost-of-capital sensitivity.** The negative-FCF, equity-and-debt-funded model is directly sensitive to the cost of capital; a higher-for-longer rate environment raises financing costs on a plan requiring continuous external capital. [unverified — no specific rate-path/hike-probability figure in the sources]

6. **Environmental liabilities.** Manufactured gas plant / Superfund exposure up to ~$3.5B (largely recoverable via regulatory assets), plus legacy asbestos and 2014 Manhattan explosion litigation (accrued $25M, insured).

7. **Unresolved leadership change.** An Item 5.02 officer/director departure was filed July 2, 2026, that could not be retrieved (SEC 403 error) or corroborated. A prior 5.02 was filed August 6, 2025. This is an open governance item to monitor.

Several of these — the weld-fraud contingency, the tax regulatory asset, and the aged AR — are unquantified and not obviously reflected in the current premium-to-book, discount-to-peer valuation. That is why we score risk factors below the neutral line despite the defensive nature of the underlying business.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($130).** Rate-base growth compounds at the upper end of the 5-7% range, the steam case and pending proceedings land closer to CECONY's requested ROE than to staff's, the weld-fraud and tax-asset contingencies resolve favorably or immaterially, equity-issuance cadence moderates as retained earnings scale, and a defensive rotation in a stagflationary macro pushes the multiple toward the peer median (22.6x). Propel NY and NYC reliability investment add incremental rate base at attractive ROEs. This is the ~16%-upside scenario embedded in the high analyst target.

**Bear case ($94).** Affordability pressure translates into persistently below-median allowed ROEs, the steam case anchors expectations lower, one or more of the unquantified contingencies (weld-fraud prudence disallowance, tax-asset write-down, AR write-offs above rate allowances) crystallizes into a material charge, continued heavy equity issuance keeps per-share growth suppressed, and a higher-for-longer rate path both raises financing costs and compresses the multiple. This is the ~16%-downside scenario at the low analyst target.

**Base case ($112-118).** Mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth net of dilution, a flat-to-slightly-expanding multiple, and the ~3.2% dividend deliver a total return in the mid-single digits — roughly the dividend plus modest EPS growth, with the multiple doing little work. This is where consensus sits and where the fundamentals point.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (income-hold). Reassessment timeline: the next earnings print and the resolution path of the steam rate case over the next two to three quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Steam and subsequent rate cases settle at allowed ROEs of 9.7%+ with equity ratios at or above request, signaling a constructive regulatory turn, combined with a moderation in equity-issuance pace that lifts per-share EPS growth back toward 6%+.
- A pullback toward the low-$90s (near the 52-week low), which would restore a meaningful yield cushion (~3.9%+) and asymmetric upside to consensus.
- Favorable resolution or immaterial outcome of the weld-fraud prudence probe and the $1.03B tax regulatory asset, removing two unquantified overhangs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material prudence disallowance in the weld-fraud matter or an NYSPSC-ordered write-down of the tax regulatory asset producing a charge above ~$0.50/share.
- AR write-offs exceeding rate allowances by a material margin, or a step-change in aged AR beyond the current $1.35B.
- A dividend-growth pause or coverage deterioration signaling the equity-funding model is straining the payout.
- Consensus current-year EPS revised down more than 5% on rate-case or cost outcomes.

## Conclusion

Consolidated Edison is a high-quality defensive holding: a monopoly regulated delivery utility in an irreplaceable service territory, with decoupled revenue, a Dividend Aristocrat streak, and a long runway of recoverable capex tied to NYC electrification and reliability needs. Owned for its ~3.2% yield and low-beta stability, it does its job in a stagflationary regime.

It is not a compelling appreciation vehicle at $111.98. Per-share earnings growth is structurally capped by heavy equity issuance, the two most recent quarters missed consensus, core CECONY earnings declined YoY once the one-time MVP gain is stripped out, and estimate revisions are flat. The below-peer multiple is justified by below-median allowed-ROE outcomes and a set of unquantified contingencies rather than being a mispricing to buy. Consensus targets sit at spot with a symmetric ~16% band in either direction, and the stock trades near its 52-week high. The score trajectory (5.7 → 5.7 → 5.2) has been declining, and the current data — flat revisions, two consecutive misses, a contentious steam case, and continued dilution — confirms rather than reverses that drift, though it does not warrant a downgrade to AVOID given the defensive floor and dividend support.

Recommendation: **HOLD** for income. Look to add on a pullback toward the low-$90s or on a genuinely constructive regulatory turn; trim into strength near the top of the analyst range ($125-130) where risk/reward turns unfavorable.
