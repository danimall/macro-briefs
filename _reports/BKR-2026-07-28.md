**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes is halfway through a deliberate transformation from an oilfield-services company priced on the oil cycle into an energy-technology company priced on gas infrastructure, LNG, and AI/data-center power demand. The two operating segments are moving in opposite directions, and the divergence is now large enough to redefine the equity. In FY2025, Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) revenue rose 10% and segment EBITDA rose 21% to $2.48B, while Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE) revenue fell 8% and EBITDA fell 9% to $2.62B. The two are now near-equal earnings contributors, a structural change from the company's historical oil-levered profile. The Q2 2026 print recorded IET orders of $7.1B (gas-technology equipment orders alone jumped to $4.9B from $0.8B a year earlier), IET RPO of $37.1B, and a raised multi-year IET orders target above $45B through 2028.

The complication is the balance sheet. To close the $13.6B all-cash Chart Industries acquisition on July 16, 2026, Baker Hughes tripled total debt from roughly $6.1B at year-end 2025 to $16.3B by June 2026, issued $6.5B of USD notes plus €3.0B, added two $1B term loans, and suspended buybacks entirely in the first half of 2026 (zero repurchased versus $384M in H1 2025). The equity thesis for the next 12–24 months turns on execution: synergy delivery, de-levering, and credit-rating maintenance, set against an OFSE business that management explicitly guides to "continued soft market conditions" through most of 2026.

At $60.59, the stock trades at roughly 20x forward EPS (consensus $2.85 for next year, $2.34 current year), a discount to the peer median of 27x but not a bargain given the leverage step-up and the fact that much of the IET re-rating is now reflected in price. This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The IET franchise deserves a higher multiple than oilfield services, estimate revisions are turning positive across all windows, and the earnings-beat record is unbroken; but the valuation is fair rather than asymmetric, and the Chart integration adds a layer of execution risk that argues for holding rather than adding at current levels.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates in over 120 countries with approximately 54,000 employees, organized into two reportable segments (with Chart Industries now forming a third following its July 2026 close):

- **Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE)** — the legacy business spanning well construction, completions/intervention/measurements, production solutions, and subsea/surface pressure systems. FY2025 revenue was roughly $14.3B (52% of total). It competes with Schlumberger, Halliburton, NOV, Weatherford, and TechnipFMC. This is a mature, cyclical, oil-price-driven business currently in decline.

- **Industrial & Energy Technology (IET)** — the growth engine, spanning gas technology equipment (aeroderivative and heavy-duty gas turbines, compressors), gas technology services (aftermarket contracts averaging over 10 years), industrial products and solutions, and climate technology (CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal). FY2025 revenue was roughly $13.4B (48%). It competes with Siemens Energy, Solar/Caterpillar, Mitsubishi Heavy, Sulzer, Flowserve, and Emerson.

The turbine and compression franchise stems from the 2017 GE Oil & Gas merger, and Baker Hughes continues to license turbine IP from GE Vernova and GE Aerospace, operating a 50/50 aeroderivative JV with GE Vernova. IET's core structural advantage is its installed base of turbines and compressors, which generates decades-long, high-margin service annuities.

The strategic identity is shifting toward an "industrialized energy solutions company," with a three-pillar strategy: transform the core (margins and cash), profitable growth (LNG, gas infrastructure, power generation, and data centers), and new energy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY revenue moved from $25.5B (2023) to $27.8B (2024) to $27.7B (2025) — roughly flat at the headline. The flat top line masks the divergence that matters: IET grew +$1.2B (+10%) while OFSE fell −$1.3B (−8%). H1 2026 continued the pattern with revenue flat at $13.3B (OFSE −6%, IET +7%), and IET segment EBITDA up 25% in the first half. Q2 IET EBITDA margin expanded toward 16%.

**Profitability and earnings quality.** FY2025 net income was $2.59B (−13% YoY), a decline driven by non-operating items rather than core deterioration: a $103M mark-to-market loss on equity securities in 2025 versus a $367M gain in 2024, plus transaction costs. GAAP net income is materially distorted by mark-to-market swings on equity holdings (chiefly a stake in ADNOC Drilling with fair value around $1.3B) and by disposition gains. H1 2026 net income of $1.62B (+46% YoY) was boosted by $697M of gains on business dispositions (the PSI sale to Crane and the Cactus JV). Segment EBITDA and adjusted EPS are the correct gauges of the underlying trend; on those measures the business is solidly profitable and improving on the IET side. Baker Hughes has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters, with the most recent surprise +26.2% ($0.64 vs. $0.51 estimated), lending confidence to forward estimates.

**Cash flow.** Cash generation is strong and improving: FY2025 operating cash flow of $3.81B and FCF of $2.54B (9.1% FCF margin), up from $3.33B/$3.06B in the prior period, with capex disciplined at under 5% of revenue. FCF conversion relative to segment earnings is healthy.

**Balance sheet — materially transformed.** At December 2025, total debt was $6.1B against $18.8B equity and $3.7B cash — a conservative structure. By June 2026, Baker Hughes had pre-funded Chart: cash rose to $15.7B, and long-term debt jumped from $5.4B to $15.5B. Post-close (July 16), net leverage steps up meaningfully. Pro-forma debt/equity is elevated relative to Baker Hughes's historically clean profile, though the company remains investment-grade and covenant-compliant.

A note on the Altman Z-Score of 1.73, which nominally flags "distress." This is a false signal for Baker Hughes. The Z-Score penalizes asset-heavy industrials with modest EBIT/assets and negative retained earnings, and Baker Hughes carries a −$3.25B retained-earnings deficit that is a legacy of the 2017 GE merger accounting and prior impairments, not current losses. With $2.5B of annual FCF, $15.7B of cash, investment-grade ratings, and no near-term liquidity stress, bankruptcy risk is not a genuine concern. The company's Piotroski score of 5/5 on measured signals is a better reflection of underlying financial health.

**Capital return.** The dividend is $0.23/quarter ($0.92 annualized, raised 2¢ in 2025), a modest ~1.6% yield. Buybacks were suspended in H1 2026 to preserve cash for Chart, with roughly $1.35B of authorization remaining. Management prioritizing de-risking the balance sheet over EPS-accretive buybacks is a rational signal, but it removes a per-share support that had been in place.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests almost entirely on IET, and the recent order data supports it. Q2 2026 IET orders, RPO ($37.1B, total company RPO $40.1B), and the $45B-through-2028 target are records. Management guided FY2026 revenue to approximately $27.35B.

Three durable secular tailwinds underpin IET:

1. **Structural LNG demand growth** (roughly 7% in 2025), where Baker Hughes supplies compression and turbine technology across the liquefaction chain. Chart deepens this molecule-handling franchise.
2. **Gas-fired power generation**, benefiting from the retirement of coal and the need for firm capacity to back intermittent renewables.
3. **AI/data-center power demand** — the newest and highest-multiple layer. Baker Hughes booked roughly $1B of data-center-related orders in 2025 and expects approximately $3B of data-center orders across 2025–2027. It is an equipment and technology supplier (gas turbines and compression) to power generators and data-center operators, not an AI developer, but the demand mechanism is real: AI compute build-out → surging electricity demand → gas-fired generation to meet baseload → turbine and compression orders. This positions Baker Hughes alongside Siemens Energy and GE Vernova, where the data-center-load thesis is now visible in hard order data across the power-equipment supply chain.

Aeroderivative and heavy-duty turbines are supply-constrained, with extending lead times, giving Baker Hughes pricing power and multi-year visibility. The estimate-revision trend confirms the momentum: current-year EPS estimates are up 1.5% over 30 days, next-quarter up 3.5%, and current-quarter up 4.8% — modest but positive across every 30-day window, though the 90-day trend had been negative (a period that captured the OFSE-driven softness before the IET orders inflection). This is steady, mid-single-digit forward momentum on a company already re-rating, not the +30% revision velocity that flags a deep-trough inflection.

The offset is OFSE, where management guides to soft conditions through 2026. Worldwide rig count fell from 2,087 (2023) to 1,818 (2025), Brent averaged $69 in 2025 versus $80+ prior, and OPEC+ returning idled supply is a structural overhang. Baker Hughes is managing this defensively — cost-out, footprint consolidation, and divestitures — reducing capital intensity and cyclical exposure rather than fighting the cycle. A temporary Q2 2026 Brent spike toward $102 on Middle East disruption was double-edged: it lifted prices but hurt Middle East upstream spending, Baker Hughes's largest OFSE region.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.59, Baker Hughes trades at approximately 20x forward EPS, against a peer median of 27x. The headline discount is real but partly justified. The company's earnings blend a growing, higher-multiple IET business with a declining, lower-multiple OFSE business, and the just-closed Chart deal has tripled leverage and paused buybacks, both of which suppress the multiple the market is willing to assign near-term.

Peer context: SLB trades at 24.7x, Halliburton at 21.9x, TechnipFMC at 27.0x, with NOV (85x) and Kodiak (89x) skewing the median upward on depressed earnings. On a like-for-like basis, Baker Hughes at 20x sits below the pure-play equipment/services peers, which is defensible given the leverage step-up but leaves modest room if the IET narrative continues to command a premium multiple.

The stock has re-rated substantially over the past year — up roughly 30% over the trailing twelve months and now sitting about 14% below its April 2026 52-week high of $70.41 after a June-July pullback. The prior appreciation is not itself a reason to be cautious; it was driven by fundamental improvement (IET orders, RPO records, disposition gains). But the current 20x forward multiple already embeds a meaningful portion of the IET re-rating. Cheapness on a peer-relative basis is offset by the OFSE drag on blended earnings quality and the near-term leverage overhang. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal; the market's discount to peers reflects genuine mix and balance-sheet considerations, not pure mispricing.

The mean analyst target of $70.32 (median $71.00) implies roughly 16% upside, with a range of $48–$85. Analyst targets on a stock at 20x forward with positive estimate revisions typically function as floors that get revised upward as consensus catches up to order momentum. But the near-term IV backwardation (37.5% one-month vs. 33.5% six-month) and the modest revision velocity temper the case for treating the target as a hard floor. Fair value sits in the mid-to-high $60s on base-case IET execution.

## Competitive Landscape

Baker Hughes's competitive position is improving on the trajectory that matters. On the IET side, it is gaining structural share of a supply-constrained, secularly-growing market. The installed base of turbines and compressors generates decades-long service annuities — a genuine moat with high switching costs and recurring, high-margin revenue. The record RPO and the raised orders target through 2028 confirm the franchise is winning share of the LNG, gas-power, and data-center power build-out ahead of Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy. The Chart acquisition deepens the LNG and molecule-handling franchise and adds recurring aftermarket revenue, further reinforcing the pivot away from pure oil-price beta.

On the OFSE side, competitive position is stable-to-defensive in a shrinking market. Baker Hughes is not losing share so much as managing a structurally softer oilfield cycle, and the divestiture strategy (PSI to Crane, the Cactus surface-pressure JV, Waygate pending to Hexagon) is deliberately high-grading the portfolio out of lower-margin, more-cyclical work.

The GE dependency is the central competitive vulnerability. Baker Hughes's turbine franchise depends on IP licenses and supply agreements with GE Vernova and GE Aerospace, plus the aero JV (which purchased $435M in H1 2026). Loss or non-performance would materially damage IET. This is a structural feature of the 2017 merger, not a new risk, but it caps how much moat credit the franchise can claim as fully owned.

Net trajectory: improving, driven by IET share gains in a re-rating end market, partially offset by a managed OFSE decline and a dependency on GE-licensed technology.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Chart integration and leverage (the dominant new risk).** The $13.6B all-cash deal — Baker Hughes's largest ever — was financed almost entirely with new debt. Integration, delivery of the targeted ~$325M annualized cost synergies, de-levering, and credit-rating maintenance are now the central execution story for the next 1–2 years. The 10-K added a dedicated Chart risk factor. Buybacks were paused to protect the balance sheet, signaling management sees this as the priority risk.

2. **OFSE cyclical downturn.** Management explicitly guides to soft oilfield conditions through most of 2026, with rig counts declining and Brent in a sub-$70 base case. OPEC+ spare capacity is a structural overhang. This drags blended earnings and offsets IET growth.

3. **GE dependency.** The turbine franchise's reliance on GE Vernova/GE Aerospace IP and supply, plus aeroderivative lead-time tightness, is a concentrated operational risk.

4. **Energy-transition ambiguity.** The 10-K reframed transition risk, now warning that a *slowdown* in the transition could strand clean-energy investments (CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal), a reversal from the prior "transition too fast" framing. Genuine uncertainty in new-energy demand.

5. **Tax-rate normalization.** The unusually low effective tax rate has been propped up by one-time valuation-allowance reversals ($308M in 2025, $664M in 2024). As these exhaust, the reported rate should rise toward statutory levels, pressuring GAAP EPS.

6. **Customer and geographic concentration.** ~16% of receivables from U.S. customers, managed Pemex exposure via CDS, and 82% of cash held ex-U.S. at year-end 2025 (Argentina currency controls).

The C3.ai securities class action naming Baker Hughes and CEO Simonelli was dismissed March 12, 2026, clearing one legal overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.5% is rich relative to one-year historical volatility of 32.4% (a 5.1-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (37.5% one-month versus 33.5% six-month) — the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the July 26 earnings event now passed and continued Chart-close digestion. Put/call skew is balanced (0.2%), and the put/call OI ratio dropped sharply from 1.28 to 0.60 over the past week as the stock rose 8.3% into and through the Q2 beat — a shift toward call-heavier positioning that confirms rather than contradicts the constructive read on the print. The implied ±8.0% near-term move reflects normal event uncertainty rather than an asymmetric downside bet. Options positioning does not challenge the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($75–85):** IET orders momentum sustains, the >$45B through-2028 target proves conservative, and data-center power orders scale toward and beyond the $3B 2025–2027 target. Chart integration delivers synergies on schedule, de-levering proceeds faster than expected, credit ratings hold, and buybacks resume in 2027. The market fully re-rates Baker Hughes as a gas-infrastructure/AI-power equipment franchise at a 24–27x multiple in line with peers, on next-year EPS of $2.85+ rising further. OFSE stabilizes as OPEC+ discipline firms oil prices.

**Bear case ($48–50):** OFSE weakness deepens as oil stays soft, dragging blended earnings. Chart integration disappoints — synergy shortfalls, dis-synergies, or a credit-rating downgrade forcing higher financing costs — while the leverage from a tripled debt load limits financial flexibility. Data-center order enthusiasm proves front-loaded or gets absorbed into a broader AI-capex deceleration. Tax-rate normalization pressures GAAP EPS, and the multiple compresses back toward the low end of the services peer range on execution doubts.

**Base case (~$68):** IET continues to deliver record orders and RPO growth, OFSE stabilizes at a soft level, Chart integrates without major surprise, and the stock re-rates modestly as consensus targets drift upward. This yields roughly $68 fair value — 22x forward EPS of $3.10, reflecting continued IET-driven mix improvement and steady de-levering.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)**

**Base case target: $68** — 22x forward EPS of ~$3.10, reflecting continued IET mix improvement and de-levering over 12–18 months. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios above.

**Bull case target: $80** — full peer-multiple re-rating on sustained order momentum and clean Chart integration.

**Bear case target: $49** — approximately 19% downside from current $60.59, on OFSE deepening plus Chart integration/leverage stress.

**Upside/downside from current ($60.59):** +12% to base case, +32% to bull case, −19% to bear case.

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to the Chart integration milestones, quarterly IET order prints, and the de-levering trajectory.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- IET orders sustain above the quarterly run-rate implied by the >$45B through-2028 target for two consecutive quarters, with data-center orders tracking above the $3B 2025–2027 pace.
- Net leverage falls back below ~1.5x pro-forma with buybacks resuming, confirming Chart is de-risked ahead of schedule.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $50s that widens the risk/reward while fundamentals stay intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit-rating downgrade or a material Chart synergy shortfall disclosed on subsequent calls.
- OFSE EBITDA decline accelerating beyond the guided soft-market range with no offsetting IET strength.
- IET orders reversing below prior-year levels for two consecutive quarters, signaling the data-center/LNG demand thesis is faltering.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 2026), which will give the first clean look at Chart's contribution as the new third segment and an updated de-levering trajectory.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes is a transformation story with supporting data: record IET orders, a raised multi-year target, and a data-center power demand layer that reframes the company as a partial beneficiary of the AI-electrification build-out. The Q2 beat-and-raise and the Chart close are catalysts for the pivot away from oil-price beta. The score trajectory has drifted lower over the past six months (from 6.8 in February to 6.2 in June) as growth, management, and financial-health dimensions all softened, and the current analysis largely confirms that: the underlying franchise is improving, but the Chart-driven leverage step-up and buyback suspension have degraded the financial-health and management dimensions, and the multiple already reflects much of the IET re-rating.

The stock is a growth-oriented HOLD at $60.59. The IET franchise merits a premium to oilfield services, estimate revisions are turning positive, and the earnings-beat record is intact; but at 20x forward with tripled debt and integration risk overhanging the next 12–24 months, the risk/reward is fair rather than asymmetric. A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s, or clear evidence of on-schedule de-levering and synergy capture, would strengthen the case for adding. This HOLD is warranted on the merits: the growth thesis is confirmed, but the entry point and balance-sheet transition argue for patience rather than accumulation at current levels.
