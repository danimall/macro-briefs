## Executive Summary

American Tower is the world's largest independent tower REIT with ~131,600 owned towers across 19 countries plus 30 data center facilities via CoreSite. The stock has declined approximately 20% over the past year, underperforming REIT peers by 26 percentage points, creating a valuation opportunity at 16.4x guided 2026 AFFO — meaningfully below its historical 18-25x range. Three catalysts support a re-rating thesis: (1) DISH Network's ~$200M revenue headwind is fully de-risked in guidance, making any legal recovery incremental upside; (2) CoreSite's data center business is delivering 17%+ revenue growth with a substantial development pipeline; (3) management initiated its first-ever share buyback ($2B authorized, $565M executed) while a director purchased $495K of stock on the open market in March 2026.

The central concern is the $37.4B debt load in a sustained high-rate environment, with $3.4B maturing in 2026 and $4.7B in 2027 at materially higher refinancing rates. The AT&T Mexico arbitration ($300M annual revenue at stake, August 2026 hearing) adds a near-term binary risk. However, $54B in contracted non-cancellable lease revenue, 83% U.S. tower gross margins, and AFFO payout coverage at 62% provide substantial cushioning. At current levels, the risk/reward skews favorably for a 12-18 month holding period.

## Company Overview

American Tower operates the largest global portfolio of communications infrastructure assets, leasing space on wireless towers and in data centers under long-term, non-cancellable contracts with built-in annual escalators (~3% fixed domestically, CPI-linked internationally). The business model generates extremely high incremental margins — each additional tenant on an existing tower costs almost nothing to serve but generates full lease revenue.

**Revenue Mix (2025):**
- U.S. & Canada: 49% of property revenue (~42,000 towers)
- Latin America: 15% (~47,000 sites)
- Africa/APAC: 13% (~27,800 sites)
- Europe: 9% (~32,500 sites)
- Data Centers: 10% (30 CoreSite facilities, ~3.7M net rentable sq ft)

**Customer Concentration:** T-Mobile (18%), AT&T (17%), Verizon (14%), Telefónica (10%) — 59% of revenue from four customers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $10.71B (2022) → $11.14B (2023) → $10.13B (2024) → $10.64B (2025). The 2024 dip reflects the India divestiture (~$911M annual revenue eliminated) and FX headwinds, not organic weakness. Underlying tenant billings growth was ~4% in 2025 and guided to continue at mid-single digits.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA of $7.13B in 2025 (~67% margin). U.S. tower gross margins at 83%. AFFO from continuing operations grew ~10% YoY to $5.04B ($10.76/share). 2026 AFFO/share guided at $10.99 (+2.1% headline, but ~5% normalized ex-DISH churn).

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $5.46B; capex of $1.72B; free cash flow ~$3.78B. Distributions of $3.16B represent a 62% AFFO payout ratio — well-covered with room for both continued dividend growth and buybacks.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $37.4B; net debt/EBITDA ~4.9x (within the company's 3-5x target and below covenant limit of 6.0x). Total liquidity of $11.1B ($9.6B undrawn facilities + $1.5B cash). The current ratio of 0.40 looks concerning but is distorted by $3.4B in current-year debt maturities that will be refinanced from ample liquidity. New debt issuances in 2025 carried 4.7%-5.35% coupons versus maturing 1.3%-2.95% notes — the cost of capital is rising but manageable given the earnings growth profile.

**Capital allocation shift:** Management initiated a $2B buyback program in Q4 2025, repurchasing $565M through April 2026. This is unprecedented for AMT and signals conviction that shares are undervalued relative to both organic growth and potential M&A returns.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (2026-2027):**
- U.S. organic tenant billings growth of ~5% (ex-DISH), driven by 5G mid-band densification
- CoreSite data center cash revenue growth of 17%+ with 200 MW added to development pipeline
- Africa/APAC growth of ~11% on mobile penetration
- Europe growth of ~4% with CPI-linked escalators
- Potential DISH site re-leasing (currently excluded from guidance)

**Medium-term growth algorithm:** Management targets "mid to upper single digit AFFO per share growth" over the long term, driven by: (1) 4-5% organic growth from existing towers, (2) data center expansion, (3) margin improvement (200-300 bps by 2030 via ground rent reduction), (4) buybacks at current valuations providing 1-2% accretion.

**CoreSite specifically:** The data center business is positioned at the intersection of AI/cloud demand and interconnection-rich, multi-tenant colocation — a model that commands premium pricing versus hyperscale single-tenant builds. The 14% revenue growth in 2025 accelerated to 17%+ in Q1 2026. As ecosystem density increases within CoreSite facilities, pricing power is expanding. Management describes reaching an "interconnection inflection" where each additional participant increases the value of the platform for existing tenants.

**Headwinds:** DISH churn (~$200M, already de-risked in guidance); Brazil elevated churn in 2026 (pulled forward from 2027); FX volatility on international operations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $180.16 and $10.99 guided 2026 AFFO/share:
- **P/AFFO:** 16.4x
- **Dividend yield:** 3.8%
- **Total return profile:** 3.8% yield + mid-to-high single digit AFFO growth = 10-13% annual total return potential

**Historical context:** AMT's P/AFFO has ranged from 18-25x over the past five years. The current 16.4x compression is driven by: (1) higher rates compressing all REIT valuations, (2) DISH uncertainty (now resolved), (3) underperformance creating forced selling/rebalancing.

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 51.8x (Equinix 79x, Digital Realty 52x, Realty Income 54x) makes AMT's 28.7x trailing P/E look reasonable. On the more relevant AFFO metric, Equinix trades at ~28x, CCI at ~16x (with its own operational issues), and SBA at ~20x. AMT at 16.4x is at the low end despite having the most diversified growth profile and strongest credit rating.

**Sum-of-parts consideration:** CoreSite alone, at data center REIT multiples (25-30x EBITDA), could be worth $12-16B against AMT's total enterprise value of ~$121B. This implies the core tower business is valued at a significant discount to standalone tower comps.

**Why the market may be wrong:** The DISH overhang depressed the multiple through 2025, but management has now completely de-risked it. The stock's 26-point underperformance versus REIT peers seems disproportionate to the actual earnings impact (~2% of revenue). As this de-risking becomes recognized and CoreSite growth accelerates, the multiple should normalize toward 18-20x AFFO, implying $198-$220 per share.

## Competitive Landscape

**Tower moat intact:** Zoning and permitting barriers, long-term leases with high switching costs (equipment relocation costs $100-300K per site), and the "toll road" revenue model remain durable. U.S. carriers collectively spend $30B+ annually on network capex, and mid-band 5G densification requires additional equipment on existing sites — there is no viable alternative to macro towers for wide-area coverage.

**Data center differentiation:** CoreSite operates an interconnection-focused, multi-tenant model that differs from hyperscale builds (Digital Realty). This positions it well for AI inferencing (latency-sensitive), hybrid cloud workloads, and "neo cloud" GPU-as-a-Service — applications that benefit from proximity to multiple networks and cloud on-ramps simultaneously. Equinix operates a similar interconnection-dense model and is a direct competitor in this space.

**Portfolio simplification:** The systematic exit from volatile emerging markets (India, Poland, South Africa fiber, Bangladesh under review) and redeployment of capital into developed markets improves earnings quality and should eventually warrant a more predictable growth premium.

**Competitive risks:** Crown Castle's potential strategic changes under new leadership, SBA's domestic focus, and the entry of infrastructure-focused private equity (Brookfield's purchase of AMT's India assets) create competitive pressure for M&A. Small cells and Open RAN remain theoretical threats but have not materially displaced macro towers in any market.

## Risk Assessment

**High severity:**
1. **AT&T Mexico arbitration (August 2026):** $300M annual revenue at stake. An adverse ruling could set negative precedent for international lease negotiations. Probability of material adverse outcome: 20-30%.
2. **Refinancing cost escalation:** $3.4B due 2026, $4.7B in 2027, $7.5B in 2028. In a sustained 30Y >5% environment, each 100bps higher refinancing cost on the total $37.4B portfolio represents ~$374M in incremental annual interest expense (~$0.80/share AFFO drag).
3. **Customer consolidation risk:** If a major carrier (particularly in international markets) undergoes financial stress or merger, churn could exceed the 2% historical baseline.

**Moderate severity:**
4. **Foreign currency volatility:** $809M FX loss in 2025 versus $308M gain in 2024. Not hedged materially. Creates P&L noise that can obscure underlying operational progress.
5. **Data center execution:** CoreSite expansion faces power availability constraints, community opposition, and longer stabilization periods. Competition from larger players (EQIX, DLR) for hyperscaler workloads.
6. **Technology displacement:** Satellites (LEO constellations), RAN sharing, and AI-optimized network efficiency could reduce long-term tower equipment demand. Management dismisses this but the 10-20 year trajectory is uncertain.

**Low severity:**
7. **DISH litigation outcome:** Fully de-risked in guidance; negative outcome means status quo, positive outcome means upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210-240):**
- AFFO grows at 6-8% annually as CoreSite accelerates and DISH headwind fully laps
- Multiple re-rates to 19-22x AFFO as market recognizes de-risking and data center value
- AT&T Mexico resolved favorably; Brazil churn lower than feared
- Interest rates moderate, compressing the REIT valuation discount
- Sum-of-parts recognition drives potential CoreSite spinoff premium
- Buybacks provide 1-2% annual accretion at current valuations

**Bear Case ($150-160):**
- 30Y rates sustain above 5.5%, compressing REIT multiples further
- AT&T Mexico arbitration results in adverse ruling, impairing ~3% of revenue
- Data center expansion faces execution delays or pricing competition
- Additional carrier churn beyond DISH (e.g., international market stress)
- FX deterioration in LatAm/Africa accelerates
- Stock trades down to 14-15x AFFO

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $210 (19x $10.99 2026 AFFO + dividend collection). This represents the low end of historical multiple normalization as DISH de-risking is recognized and CoreSite growth continues.

**Bull case target:** $240 (22x $10.99, achievable if rates moderate and CoreSite growth accelerates further toward $1B+ revenue).

**Bear case target:** $155 (14x AFFO if rates spike and multiple compresses further). This represents ~14% downside from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: AT&T Mexico arbitration resolution (August 2026), Q2/Q3 2026 earnings showing ex-DISH organic growth trajectory, potential Fed rate posture changes.

**Upside/downside:** +17% to base case, +33% to bull case, -14% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.2:1 to base case, 2.4:1 to bull case — favorable asymmetry.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. AT&T Mexico arbitration results in >$100M annual revenue loss AND sets precedent for other international markets
2. 2026 organic tenant billings growth (ex-DISH) declines below 3% for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural demand weakness
3. Net leverage exceeds 5.5x EBITDA, signaling balance sheet stress

## Conclusion

American Tower at $180 and 16.4x AFFO represents a quality infrastructure compounder trading at a meaningful discount to historical multiples and REIT peers. The DISH de-risking removes the primary overhang that drove the 26-point relative underperformance. CoreSite provides genuine AI/data center optionality that is underappreciated in the current price (data center peers trade at 25-30x EBITDA for similar growth). Management's initiation of buybacks and a director's open-market purchase both confirm the internal view that shares are undervalued.

The primary risks — leverage in a high-rate environment and AT&T Mexico arbitration — are real but quantifiable. The contracted revenue base, high U.S. gross margins, and covered AFFO payout ratio provide substantial margin of safety. At 3.8% yield plus mid-to-high single digit AFFO growth, the total return profile is attractive for a 12-18 month holding period with identifiable catalysts for multiple normalization.
