**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kilroy Realty is a self-managed REIT that owns roughly 17.1 million square feet of modern office and life-science space concentrated in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Seattle, San Diego, and Austin, plus a large entitled land bank (nine sites, ~6.0M sq ft of potential density, including the ~2.3M sq ft San Francisco Flower Mart). The investment case rests on three facts that pull in different directions: the balance sheet is sound and the ~5.6% dividend is comfortably covered by FFO; the underlying operations are eroding at the per-share level (FFO/share has declined from $4.77 in 2022 to ~$4.20 in 2025, with negative second-generation leasing spreads); and the stock trades at a meaningful discount to management's own implied NAV, which the company is exploiting through accretive buybacks. None of these facts has changed enough since the prior review to move off HOLD.

The headline financials look worse than the operations. Q1 2026 showed a $(0.16) EPS net loss, but that was driven by a $61.8M non-cash impairment on two Los Angeles residential assets that were then sold in April 2026 for $202M gross. FFO of $108.8M and operating cash flow of $150.7M remained positive and covered the $0.54 quarterly dividend. The occupancy drop to 77.6% is also largely mechanical — it reflects adding the barely-leased KOP 2 life-science development to the stabilized count; excluding KOP 2, occupancy was ~81.4% and same-property NOI grew 3.2%. The genuine problems are subtler: leases rolled at ‑10.6% GAAP / ‑16.8% cash because in-place rents still sit above current market, and KOP 2 ($1.2B invested, 44% leased) is now dragging NOI as it stopped capitalizing costs.

We continue to rate KRC a HOLD (income-oriented). The score has drifted up (4.6 → 5.1) as the market has come to appreciate that the net loss is optical and the balance sheet is fine, and we mark it 5.05. The stock has rallied from a ~$27.36 low to ~$38.35, so the deepest-discount entry is gone. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of a dated catalyst to close the NAV gap combined with two structurally weak core markets (SF/LA) and a multi-year FFO/share decline that has not yet inflected. What keeps it above AVOID is the covered yield, the discount to NAV, disciplined capital recycling, and optionality on a Bay Area tech-demand recovery.

## Company Overview

Kilroy operates as a single reportable segment through its operating partnership (KRC owns ~99%). As of March 31, 2026, the stabilized portfolio was 123 office/life-science buildings totaling ~17.1M rentable square feet, plus the 608-unit One Paseo residential property in San Diego. The tenant base is heavily concentrated: roughly 51% of annualized base rent comes from technology tenants, ~19% from life science/healthcare, and the top 20 tenants represent ~53.7% of ABR. Geography is 100% West Coast plus Austin, with the two largest exposures — SF Bay Area and Los Angeles — being the slowest-recovering major office markets in the country.

The differentiators are asset quality (newer, amenitized, sustainability-leading buildings targeted at knowledge/creative tenants), a fixed-rate and staggered debt book, an undrawn $1.1B revolver, and a valuable entitled land bank. The strategic pivot under CEO Angela Aman (who succeeded founder John Kilroy in early 2024) is explicit capital recycling: prune non-core and residential assets, redeploy into pre-leased development and buybacks. This is a more disciplined posture than the founder-era development-heavy model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI.** Q1 2026 total revenue was $270.1M, essentially flat versus $270.8M a year earlier (‑0.3%). (The standardized ~$19M XBRL "revenue" figure is a parser misclassification and is disregarded.) NOI was $178.4M, down 1.0% year over year — dispositions cost $10.4M and the KOP 2 re/development drag cost $2.7M, partly offset by +3.2% same-property NOI and $6.0M of acquisition contribution.

**Earnings quality.** The Q1 2026 net loss of $(19.3)M / $(0.16) EPS versus +$39.0M / $0.33 a year ago is dominated by the residential impairment on the Columbia Square Living and Jardine assets, partly offset by $23.5M of gains on operating-property sales. The relevant REIT earnings metric, FFO, fell to $108.8M from $122.3M (‑11%). Full-year 2025 FFO-as-adjusted per share was ~$4.20, down from $4.59 (2024) and $4.77 (2022) — a multi-year per-share erosion that is the core fundamental concern.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was healthy and rising, $150.7M in Q1 2026 versus $136.9M. Total debt is ~$4.62B, 95.7% fixed-rate at a weighted-average effective rate of ~4.5%, with only 4.3% variable and the $1.1B revolver fully undrawn. The company is in compliance with all covenants. Interest expense rose 23.6% ($31.1M → $38.5M), but this is misleading: it is largely because capitalized interest fell $6.6M as KOP 2 stabilized; gross interest expense was up only 1.6%. Liquidity is $192.9M cash plus the $1.1B revolver. Near-term maturities of ~$600M in the remainder of 2026 (including the $200M term loan due October 2026, extendable 12 months; the $50M Series A notes were already repaid at par in April 2026) are manageable against that liquidity and the staggered ladder.

**Dividend coverage.** The dividend was held flat at $0.54/quarter ($2.16 annualized), a ~5.6% yield at $38.35. FFO of ~$0.90+/quarter comfortably covers it. As is normal for the sector, the distribution has historically exceeded taxable income, giving it a return-of-capital character, which is not a coverage concern on an FFO basis.

The financial-health picture is a strong balance sheet layered over eroding per-share earnings. That combination supports a mid-range score rather than a high one: resilience is real, but capital structure efficiency and per-share earnings power are both being tested by negative rollover spreads. We score financial health 5.5, consistent with the prior review, and note that this dimension is our most reliable discriminator — a mid-range read here is a genuine constraint on the overall rating.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the story, and the estimate-revision data requires careful interpretation. Consensus current-year EPS was revised from $0.34 to $0.70 over 90 days (+105%), and next-year from $0.31 to $0.50 (+61%). Under our framework, +30%+ revisions are normally a strong BUY signal. Here they are not, for two reasons. First, these are GAAP EPS estimates on a REIT where GAAP earnings are dominated by non-cash impairments and lumpy disposition gains — the revisions largely reflect the market repricing the optical net loss (impairments now behind the company after the residential sales) rather than a genuine operating inflection. Second, the analyst count is thin (one to four analysts per line), so the percentage moves are mechanically large off a tiny base. FFO/share, the metric that actually matters, is still in multi-year decline.

The operating growth drivers are mixed. Same-property NOI growth of 3.2% shows the in-place portfolio has pricing traction. But second-generation leasing spreads were ‑10.6% GAAP / ‑16.8% cash, meaning every rollover of below-market-expiring space repriced lower — in-place rents remain above current market. Leases on space vacant ≤12 months were positive (+19.2% GAAP), so newly-marketed space is being absorbed at market, but the blended figure is negative and will remain a headwind until in-place rents converge with market or occupancy tightens enough to reverse the trend.

The forward levers are the development pipeline and a potential Bay Area demand recovery. The 1900 Broadway JV in Redwood City (251,000 sq ft, 58% pre-leased, ~97% KRC-owned, 2027 start / 2030 delivery) is a rare pre-leased commitment in a difficult market and a sign of disciplined underwriting. The larger land bank (Flower Mart) is real optionality but requires substantial capital with uncertain lease-up and long timelines. The externally-promoted "AI tailwind" narrative — that an AI-driven tech resurgence reignites Bay Area office/life-science demand — is a legitimate optionality factor but should be treated as sentiment, not a fundamental driver: Kilroy is a landlord to tech and AI tenants, not an AI operator, and any benefit is indirect and speculative. We score growth prospects 4.8, essentially flat with the prior 5.0, reflecting real same-property NOI traction offset by negative rollover spreads and the fact that the headline revision surge is a GAAP artifact rather than an FFO inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.35 the stock trades at a trailing P/E of ~20.9 on EPS of $2.32, but the P/E metric is close to meaningless here given the impairment noise. On the relevant metric, the dividend yield and the discount to NAV are the anchors. Management's own Q1 capitalization table used a $28.21 reference close to derive total market cap; the shares have since moved to $38.35, and the company repurchased 2.36M shares at ~$30.80 in Q1 (implying management viewed that level as well below intrinsic value). The current price sits between analyst targets of $28 low and $51 high, with mean and median both at $38.50 — essentially at consensus, which for a REIT trading below NAV is not the "target is a floor" situation that applies to cheap high-growth names. Here the consensus reflects the tension between the NAV discount and the operating headwinds.

The valuation case is that KRC trades below replacement cost and below its own implied NAV, with a covered yield providing carry while the discount persists. The case against is that a NAV discount can persist indefinitely in a sector where cap rates remain elevated and where the two largest markets (SF/LA) are the weakest-recovering; there is no dated catalyst to force the gap closed. Our valuation framework treats "cheap" with skepticism — a discounted multiple is only a buy signal when there is an identifiable reason the market is wrong. Here the reason (NAV discount + accretive buybacks + Bay Area recovery optionality) is real but not near-term. We score valuation 5.0: fairly priced for the risk, with asymmetric upside gated on a market recovery that has no fixed timeline.

## Competitive Landscape

Kilroy competes with West Coast office/life-science owners including Boston Properties (BXP), Alexandria (ARE) in life science, and Hudson Pacific. Its relative advantages are asset quality (newer, sustainable, amenitized buildings that win tenant preference in a flight-to-quality environment), a fixed-rate staggered debt book, an undrawn revolver, and the entitled land bank. Its structural disadvantage is concentration in the two weakest-recovering markets, with 51% tech-tenant exposure and top-20 tenants at ~53.7% of ABR.

The trajectory is roughly stable to slightly negative rather than improving. Same-property NOI growth of 3.2% and flight-to-quality tenant preference argue the modern portfolio is holding share within its markets. But negative rollover spreads, SF Bay Area occupancy at 75.2% and LA at 74.8%, and a multi-year TSR lag versus the peer group (cumulative TSR 84.29 vs peer 126.71, with the gap widening) indicate the competitive position has not yet turned. We score competitive position 4.7 — the assets are high quality, but the market backdrop and rollover economics are keeping trajectory flat rather than improving, and our calibration flags competitive-position as a low-reliability dimension not to over-weight.

## Risk Assessment

The principal idiosyncratic risks not fully priced:

1. **Negative releasing spreads.** ‑10.6% GAAP / ‑16.8% cash on Q1 second-generation leases means the largest lease-rollover risk is dilution, not vacancy. This is a slow, quantifiable drag that will persist until in-place rents converge with market.

2. **Development lease-up.** KOP 2 is now a NOI drag; the broader pipeline requires substantial capital with uncertain absorption. 1900 Broadway (58% pre-leased) is well-underwritten, but the Flower Mart is long-dated and speculative.

3. **Concentration.** 100% West Coast + Austin, ~51% tech ABR, top-20 at ~53.7% — highly levered to tech hiring, return-to-office, and California regulatory/tax risk (Prop 13 reassessment). This is partly priced but the reversal risk is real.

4. **Refinancing.** The 2026 maturities (including the extendable $200M October term loan) are manageable against liquidity, but future development funding and any credit downgrade would raise cost of capital. The June 17, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03) discloses a new material financing obligation whose details warrant follow-up.

5. **Environmental/seismic.** $72.2M accrued environmental remediation liability tied to development sites, plus West Coast seismic exposure.

Offsetting these: the impairment cycle on residential appears largely behind the company (those assets are sold), the balance sheet is 95.7% fixed-rate, and known risks are substantially priced given the NAV discount. We score risk factors 4.8 — improved from the prior 3.5/4.7 as the impairment overhang cleared, but capped by the negative-spread drag, concentration, and the unexamined June financing 8-K.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 50.7% is rich versus 28.6% one-year historical vol (a 22.1pp spread), sits at the 57th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.7% near-term vs 35.1% at six months) — the market is pricing imminent stress around the July 27 Q2 earnings date, with a ±11.6% implied move on the nearest expiry. The 7.9% put/call skew signals elevated near-term put demand, and 11.1% short interest (5.8 days to cover) confirms institutional bearish positioning. This divergence between a covered-yield income thesis and elevated hedging demand is a caution flag rather than a thesis-changer: it argues for patience on entry and for treating the July 27 print as a binary. The elevated IV also means options are an expensive way to express a bullish view here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The optical net loss and mechanical occupancy drop have been misread by the market. Ex-KOP 2 occupancy is ~81.4%, same-property NOI grew 3.2%, FFO covers the dividend, and the balance sheet is sound with an undrawn revolver. Management is buying back stock at a steep discount to NAV, funded by disposition proceeds rather than leverage — accretive capital allocation. A Bay Area tech/AI-driven demand recovery reignites leasing, occupancy and rents inflect, the NAV discount closes, and FFO/share re-inflects. Bull target ~$48–51 (approaching the high analyst target and a partial NAV-gap close).

**Bear case.** SF/LA office recovery stalls, negative rollover spreads compound, development lease-up disappoints, and the NAV discount persists or widens as cap rates stay elevated. FFO/share continues its multi-year decline, and the yield becomes a value trap rather than carry. Bear target ~$28 (the low analyst target and near the 52-week low of $27.36), roughly ‑27% from current.

**Base case.** The portfolio grinds sideways: same-property NOI positive but offset by negative rollovers and development drag, dividend maintained, buybacks continue below NAV, and the stock delivers its yield plus modest appreciation as the impairment overhang fades. Base target ~$40–42, roughly flat-to-modestly-positive from current plus the yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income orientation. The reassessment horizon is 12 months, anchored to the July 27, 2026 Q2 print and subsequent quarterly leasing/occupancy data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Second-generation leasing spreads turn positive (from ‑10.6% GAAP), signaling in-place rents have converged with market and the rollover drag is over.
- SF Bay Area and/or LA occupancy inflect upward on a sequential basis for two consecutive quarters, confirming a demand recovery rather than stabilization.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s (near management's ~$30.80 buyback level and closer to implied NAV) that restores a clearer margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FFO/share decline accelerates or dividend coverage on an FFO basis deteriorates toward the point of a cut.
- The June 2026 financing obligation or the October term-loan refinancing proves materially dilutive or covenant-tightening.
- A large tech/life-science tenant among the top 20 (~53.7% of ABR) fails to renew or downsizes significantly, deepening the vacancy problem.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the July 27, 2026 Q2 earnings release, with the elevated near-term IV and backwardation confirming this is the market's focal event.

## Conclusion

Kilroy is a well-capitalized, high-quality office/life-science REIT whose reported financials look worse than its operations. The Q1 net loss was an optical impairment, the occupancy drop was mechanical, and the dividend is genuinely covered by FFO. Management's pivot to professional leadership with accretive buybacks below NAV, disciplined capital recycling, and a rare pre-leased development JV is constructive. Against that, FFO/share is in multi-year decline, second-generation leases still roll negative, and the two largest markets are the weakest-recovering office markets in the country, with no dated catalyst to close the NAV discount.

We rate KRC a HOLD for income. Own it for the covered yield and the optionality on a Bay Area tech-demand recovery, but recognize the deepest-discount entry has passed (the stock is up ~40% from its low) and that near-term appreciation depends on a market recovery whose timeline is uncertain. The July 27 earnings print, which the options market is pricing as a binary, is the next decision point. Better risk/reward would come from a pullback toward the low-$30s or from confirmed positive leasing spreads.
