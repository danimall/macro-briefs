## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes is undergoing a fundamental transformation from a cyclically-exposed oilfield services company into a diversified energy technology platform. The Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) segment grew 14% YoY in Q1 2026, posted record orders of $4.9B (+54% YoY), and now carries a $32.4B remaining performance obligation (RPO) backlog. The company's gas turbine, LNG, and data center power capabilities position it at the intersection of three structural growth vectors: global LNG expansion, AI-driven power demand, and energy transition infrastructure.

The investment case is complicated by three factors: (1) the pending $13.6B all-cash Chart Industries acquisition, with EU regulatory decision due by June 26, 2026, which will roughly double the company's debt load; (2) consensus EPS estimates falling 11-22% over the past 90 days as OFSE weakness persists and analysts incorporate acquisition financing costs; and (3) the stock's 80% appreciation over 12 months, which has already re-rated BKR from a legacy oilfield services multiple toward an energy technology premium. At 21x forward P/E on $2.30 consensus FY26 EPS (or 25.4x on the $2.60 trailing EPS figure), the stock is priced for successful execution on Chart integration and continued IET momentum — leaving limited margin of safety if either falters.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates through two segments with divergent trajectories:

**Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE):** $14.3B revenue in FY2025 (-8% YoY), providing well construction, completions, production solutions, and subsea systems. This legacy business is tied to global rig counts, which fell from 2,087 in 2023 to 1,818 in 2025. Management expects continued weakness through most of 2026.

**Industrial & Energy Technology (IET):** $13.4B revenue in FY2025 (+10% YoY), encompassing gas turbines/compressors for LNG facilities, aftermarket services, industrial inspection (Waygate), condition monitoring (Bently Nevada), and climate technology solutions (CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal). This segment generated 18.5% EBITDA margins in 2025 (up from 16.8% in 2024) and is the company's strategic focus.

Post-Chart acquisition, IET plus Chart could represent 55-60% of total revenue, repositioning Baker Hughes as an energy technology company.

The financial profile is solid on a standalone basis — strong and improving FCF, expanding IET margins, and manageable leverage. The critical variable is post-Chart leverage, which could push total debt above $18-19B and net debt/EBITDA above 3x, requiring 2-3 years of disciplined deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

Baker Hughes has three distinct growth drivers:

**1. LNG Infrastructure (Proven, Large):**
The company is the world's largest independent LNG equipment provider. IET's record $4.9B quarterly orders in Q1 2026 were driven by QatarEnergy LNG North Field West and other major projects. The $32.4B RPO provides visibility through decade-end. Global LNG demand grew ~7% in 2025, and multiple FIDs are expected through 2027-2028.

**2. Data Center Power (Emerging, Structural):**
$1B in data center-related orders in 2025, targeting $3B cumulative by 2027. The collaboration with hyperscalers on AI-enabled power optimization validates the positioning. Gas turbines are the fastest path to baseload power for hyperscaler facilities. This revenue stream didn't exist two years ago.

**3. Climate Technology Solutions (Early, High-Growth):**
$1.6B in CTS orders in 2025, up 71% YoY. Includes CCUS, hydrogen, and geothermal technologies. Still small relative to total revenue but growing rapidly with strong policy tailwinds.

**Offset: OFSE Decline.**
Global rig counts are falling. Management expects weakness through 2026. OFSE revenue declined 7% in Q1 2026 with Middle East disruptions adding pressure. This segment is being managed for cash flow, not growth.

**Estimate Trends:**
Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 22.4% over 90 days (from $0.61 to $0.47), and current-year estimates are down 11.1% ($2.58 to $2.30). This reflects: (1) OFSE weakness persisting longer than expected, (2) integration of Chart financing costs into models, and (3) Middle East operational disruptions. The negative revision trajectory is a material concern — while IET fundamentals are accelerating, the consolidated P&L is under pressure from the declining segment and deal-related costs.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.06, BKR trades at:
- 25.4x trailing P/E (on $2.60 reported EPS)
- 28.7x forward P/E on current-year consensus of $2.30
- 23.8x on next-year consensus of $2.78
- ~13.7x EV/EBITDA (using $4.8B EBITDA and ~$66B EV pre-Chart)

**Peer Comparison:**
The peer median P/E is 17.5x (PSX, OKE, VLO). BKR's premium is justified by: (1) superior growth trajectory (IET growing double-digits vs. flat/declining peers), (2) higher-quality revenue mix (long-term service contracts, technology licensing), and (3) structural positioning in LNG/data center growth vectors. However, the premium has already expanded significantly — the stock traded at 11-13x earnings a year ago.

**Vs. Technology/Industrial Comps:**
If BKR is re-rated as an energy technology company (comparable to Siemens Energy, Emerson, Flowserve), 20-25x forward earnings is reasonable. But this re-rating has largely already occurred with the 80% stock appreciation over 12 months.

**The specific concern preventing a BUY:** At 28.7x current-year consensus (which is falling), the stock requires both successful Chart integration AND continued IET acceleration to justify the multiple. If Chart integration stumbles (dis-synergies, delayed closure, regulatory concessions), or if the 22% current-quarter estimate decline signals broader earnings deterioration, the downside is 15-20% to $53-56 (where the stock traded pre-Q1 beat). The risk/reward at this price is asymmetric to the downside for a 6-12 month holding period given the integration overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

**IET Competitive Position: Strong and Improving.**
Baker Hughes holds a unique market position as the only company combining LNG compression/turbine expertise with broad industrial technology (inspection, monitoring, flow control) and emerging energy transition capabilities. The Chart acquisition would extend this moat into cryogenic processing, heat exchange, and industrial gas technologies.

Key competitive advantages:
- Largest independent LNG equipment provider globally
- $32.4B RPO creating switching cost barriers (13+ year average service contracts)
- Cross-licensing and supply agreements with GE Vernova and GE Aerospace for gas turbine technology
- First-mover in data center gas turbine solutions
- Installed base of gas turbines generating recurring service revenue

**OFSE Competitive Position: Stable but Pressured.**
Third-largest globally behind SLB and Halliburton. The segment faces pricing pressure in a declining rig-count environment, but maintains technology differentiation in areas like directional drilling, chemicals, and artificial lift. Management's strategy is to optimize OFSE for cash flow while investing incrementally in digital/automation capabilities.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Chart Industries Acquisition (HIGH — Binary Near-Term Event):**
The EU regulatory decision by June 26, 2026 is the most significant near-term catalyst/risk. If approved without material remedies, the stock likely holds $64-70. If blocked or requiring significant divestitures, the stock could pull back to $55-58 (pre-deal anticipation levels). Post-close, integration execution over 18-24 months determines whether synergy targets ($325M) are achievable.

**2. Falling Estimates (MODERATE-HIGH):**
The negative revision momentum contradicts the stock's premium multiple. While partially explained by one-time factors (Middle East disruption, deal costs), if Q2 2026 earnings miss or guidance is cut, multiple compression risk is real.

**3. Post-Acquisition Leverage (MODERATE-HIGH):**
In a rising-rate environment, the carrying cost of $18-19B in debt is material. Deleveraging requires 2-3 years of $2.5B+ annual FCF dedicated to debt reduction, limiting buyback capacity and flexibility.

**4. OFSE Structural Decline (MODERATE):**
If oil prices remain under pressure, OFSE could face even sharper declines. Conversely, if energy prices spike, upstream capex may respond with a lag, providing eventual support.

**5. GE Vernova/Aerospace Supply Dependency (MODERATE):**
The company's core IET turbine business relies on licensing/supply from GE Vernova and the aeroderivative JV with GE Vernova. Any disruption to this relationship would be material.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes possesses a genuinely differentiated strategic position: an energy technology company with structural exposure to LNG infrastructure, data center power demand, and energy transition, supported by a $32.4B backlog and expanding margins. The IET segment's Q1 2026 performance validates the transformation thesis.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because at 25.4x trailing / 28.7x current-year consensus, the stock prices in successful execution on a $13.6B leveraged acquisition that hasn't yet received regulatory approval, while near-term estimates are falling. The Chart acquisition introduces binary risk (EU decision June 26), material leverage risk, and integration distraction risk — all occurring simultaneously with OFSE weakness that is compressing reported earnings. The 80% appreciation over 12 months has captured much of the re-rating from oilfield services to energy technology; the next leg requires proof of execution on Chart integration and estimate stabilization.

For investors with a 2-3 year horizon willing to accept the integration risk, a pullback to $55-58 (15-20% below current) would create an attractive entry point. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced — the growth thesis is real but adequately reflected in the multiple.
