**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Pediatrix Medical Group has completed a meaningful operational turnaround, exiting non-core office-based practices in 2023-2024 and emerging as a leaner, higher-margin business focused on hospital-based neonatal, maternal-fetal, and pediatric subspecialty care. The restructuring, which generated significant impairment and restructuring charges across 2023-2024 (including $178M in impairment and $64M in restructuring costs in 2024 alone), obscured a business that grew adjusted EBITDA from $200M (2023) to $276M (2025) and now generates $256M in annual free cash flow on a $1.7B market cap—a 15% FCF yield.

At 10.5x forward earnings and approximately 7.0-7.4x EV/EBITDA, the stock is priced at a discount to healthcare services peers. The market appears to be applying a discount for Medicaid reform risk (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), yet Q1 2026 results showed no obvious adverse payor-mix deterioration attributable to subsidy lapse or Medicaid reform, though patient volumes were modestly negative. With estimates rising (+4.1% over 90 days for FY2026), $166M remaining on a buyback authorization (~9.6% of market cap), and the Q1 2026 earnings beat, the risk/reward profile skews positive.

The specific reason the market may be wrong: investors are pricing the Medicaid overhang as if it were already reducing volumes and deteriorating payor mix, when Q1 2026 data does not yet confirm this. The company's patient population (pregnant women and infants) has strong Medicaid protection floors across all states where Pediatrix operates. If the feared volume decline and payor-mix deterioration do not materialize through H2 2026, the stock should re-rate toward 12-14x earnings ($27-$33).

## Company Overview

Pediatrix Medical Group is the only publicly-traded national-scale provider of hospital-based physician services in neonatal, maternal-fetal, and pediatric subspecialty care. The company employs approximately 2,295 affiliated physicians across 37 states, staffing and managing clinical activities at over 400 hospitals.

The company is focused primarily on hospital-based neonatal and maternal-fetal subspecialty care, though it also retains office-based maternal-fetal medicine practices, newborn hearing screening, and other pediatric subspecialty services following its 2024 strategic pivot.

Revenue is derived from fee-for-service billings (70% contracted managed care, 24% government programs, 4% other, 2% private-pay) supplemented by administrative fees from hospitals (~14% of net revenue). The business model is capital-light: physicians perform clinical work at hospital facilities, while Pediatrix manages billing, compliance, scheduling, recruitment, and quality oversight.

Geographic concentration is notable: Texas accounts for 32% of net revenue; the top 5 states represent approximately 64%. The company operates the largest newborn hearing screening program in the U.S. (793,000+ babies annually at 340 hospitals) and maintains a clinical data warehouse spanning 2.1M patients and 37M patient days.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**

Full-year 2025 revenue of $1.91B reflected a 4.9% decline from 2024's $2.01B, entirely driven by practice dispositions. Same-unit revenue grew 6.2% (5.7% pricing, 0.5% volume), demonstrating strong underlying organic performance.

The profitability transformation:
- Adjusted EBITDA: $200M (2023) → $224M (2024) → $276M (2025)
- Adjusted EPS: $1.26 (2023) → $1.51 (2024) → $2.04 (2025)
- Operating margin: improved to 10.9% in 2025 from negative GAAP territory in 2023-2024
- FCF: $256M in 2025 (13.4% margin), up from approximately $195M in 2024

**Q1 2026 Performance:**

Revenue of $476M beat consensus. Adjusted EPS of $0.44 exceeded estimates (the magnitude of beat varies by consensus source, ranging from approximately 13-19%). Same-unit revenue grew ~3%, with 4%+ pricing gains partially offset by ~1% NICU day volume decline and a 1.6% decline in same-unit patient volumes. Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $280-$300M, implying 1.4-8.7% growth over 2025.

**Balance Sheet:**

Cash of $375M at year-end 2025 (declining to approximately $206M at March 31, 2026 after seasonal Q1 usage). Total debt of $597M ($400M senior notes at 5.375% due 2030 + $197M Term A). Net leverage of approximately 1.3x adjusted EBITDA is conservative for a healthcare services company. DSO improved to 42.8 days from 47.6 days, reflecting RCM transformation benefits. Current ratio of 1.66 at December 31, 2025 (declining to approximately 1.33 at March 31, 2026 due to seasonal cash usage) provides adequate liquidity.

The credit facility (revolver + Term A) matures February 2027. With strong cash generation and low leverage, refinancing should proceed without difficulty, though at potentially higher market rates.

**Capital Allocation:**

Share repurchases totaled $87M in 2025 (4.3M shares) plus $21M in Q1 2026 (1M shares). Actual shares outstanding were approximately 82.1M as of March 31, 2026, down from approximately 84M. The remaining $166M authorization represents 9.6% of current market cap. At current prices, the buyback is highly accretive (buying at 10.5x earnings vs. generating 15% FCF yield).

## Growth Analysis

Pediatrix's growth profile is modest but steadily improving:

**Revenue Growth Drivers:**
- Pricing: 4%+ annually from improved collections (RCM transformation), administrative fee increases, payer mix optimization, and patient acuity
- Volume: Modestly negative near-term (NICU days -1% in Q1 2026) due to secular birth rate decline
- Tuck-in acquisitions: $23M deployed in 2025, performing "better than initial projections"
- Teleservices expansion: Management describes "major opportunity" in remote OB and neonatal telehealth

**Estimate Trajectory:**
- FY2026 consensus: $2.28 EPS (+11.8% over 2025's $2.04)
- FY2027 consensus: $2.36 EPS (+3.5% over 2026)
- 90-day revision: FY2026 estimates up 4.1%, FY2027 up 2.5%

The growth rate is moderate—mid-to-high single digits on earnings—constrained by the secular headwind of declining U.S. birth rates and the volume-limited nature of hospital-based subspecialty care. However, earnings growth compounds with aggressive share repurchases (5-6% annualized reduction) and potential margin expansion as RCM benefits continue.

Management warned that the "hockey stick" improvement in collections will moderate in H2 2026 as year-over-year comparisons lap. This likely caps organic revenue growth in the 3-5% range going forward.

## Valuation Assessment

Current valuation metrics:
- Forward P/E: 10.5x (on $2.05 trailing EPS per Phase 1 data; or 9.4x on consensus FY2026E of $2.28)
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 7.0-7.4x on 2026 guidance midpoint ($290M), using ~82.1M shares outstanding at $21.54 ($1.77B market cap), approximately $591M in total debt, and $206-$329M in cash and short-term investments at March 31, 2026
- FCF yield: ~15% (using 2025 FCF of $256M on $1.73B market cap)
- P/FCF: ~6.7x

Peer comparison suggests undervaluation, though direct comparisons are difficult given Pediatrix's unique business mix (hospital-based physician staffing, neonatal/MFM focus, high Medicaid exposure, No Surprises Act constraints). Selected healthcare services companies (ADUS at 17.3x, PGNY at 33.3x) trade at significantly higher multiples, though differences in business model, growth profile, and risk exposure limit direct comparability. Even applying a substantial Medicaid risk discount, Pediatrix's multiple implies the market is pricing in either a meaningful earnings decline or permanent value-trap status.

The mispricing thesis: the market is applying a Medicaid/ACA subsidy lapse discount as if volume declines and payor-mix deterioration were already occurring. Through Q1 2026, results showed no obvious adverse payor-mix deterioration attributable to the subsidy lapse, though the observation window remains short (~3 months). If this pattern persists through Q2-Q3 2026 reporting, the discount should partially close.

At 12x forward earnings, the stock prices at $27.36 on FY2026 consensus. At 14x, the stock prices at $31.92.

Analyst consensus targets range from $19.00 to $27.00, with a mean of $23.17 and median of $23.50. Given the predominance of HOLD ratings (5 of 7 analysts), these targets likely represent conservative, risk-adjusted fair value rather than upside scenarios.

## Competitive Landscape

Pediatrix operates in a highly fragmented market with structural advantages:

**Competitive Moats:**
1. National scale in a subspecialty requiring rare physician credentials (~7,200 board-certified neonatologists nationwide)
2. Clinical data warehouse (37M patient days) enabling outcomes research, quality benchmarking, and value-based contracting—unmatched in the specialty
3. Recruitment infrastructure that smaller groups cannot replicate
4. Multi-decade hospital relationships with major health systems
5. Capital-light, high-FCF model (13.4% FCF margin)

**Competitive Trajectory:** Improving. The 2024 strategic pivot removed dilutive, low-quality revenue streams and sharpened focus on the moated core business. New clinical leadership hires from Stanford and University of Colorado signal investment in quality differentiation. The "Pediatrix Partners" clinician ownership program aligns physician incentives with firm performance.

**Threats:**
- Hospital systems employing physicians directly (long-standing risk, not accelerating)
- Private equity-backed competitors with capital to acquire practices
- Telehealth platforms potentially reducing the need for on-site specialists (though NICU care inherently requires physical presence)
- Antitrust scrutiny of healthcare consolidation

The competitive position is stable-to-improving. Scale advantages compound as RCM improvements and data-driven care models become differentiators in hospital partner discussions.

## Risk Assessment

**Medicaid Reform (High Impact, Moderate Probability):**
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) includes health provisions that CBO/CRS estimates will reduce federal outlays by approximately $1.1T over FY2025-2034 and increase the uninsured by approximately 10.0M in FY2034. Within that, Medicaid-specific provisions are estimated to reduce federal outlays by approximately $990B and increase the uninsured by 7.5M in FY2034. With 24% of net revenue (and 53% of gross billings) from government programs, this is the single largest risk. Mitigant: pregnant women and infants are covered at or below 133% FPL in all states where Pediatrix operates, and these populations are politically difficult to cut. Timing: Impact would be gradual (2027-2034 CBO projection window), not immediate.

**ACA Subsidy Lapse (Moderate Impact, Moderate Probability):**
Enhanced premium tax credits expired 12/31/25. Risk is that formerly-insured patients shift to Medicaid or become uninsured. If impact emerges in H2 2026-2027, it would likely appear as adverse payer mix rather than volume decline (pregnant women still need OB/neonatal care regardless of insurance status).

**Birth Rate Secular Decline (Moderate Impact, High Probability):**
U.S. births declined structurally over the past decade. This is a persistent headwind limiting volume growth to flat-to-slightly-negative, requiring pricing and share gains to drive revenue growth.

**Refinancing (Low Impact, High Probability):**
Credit facility matures February 2027. With $200M+ in cash, $275M annual operating cash flow, and 1.3x leverage, this is manageable but may result in modestly higher interest expense.

**Geographic Concentration (Moderate Impact, Low Probability):**
Texas at 32% of revenue creates event risk (regulatory, storm, Medicaid rate changes). Diversification across 37 states partially mitigates.

## Conclusion

Pediatrix Medical Group is a fundamentally improved business trading at a discount due to Medicaid reform anxiety that has not yet produced observable adverse payor-mix deterioration. The 15% FCF yield, aggressive capital return program, and rising analyst estimates create asymmetric upside if the feared volume decline does not materialize. The specific, falsifiable thesis: if Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings continue to show stable volumes and no payer mix deterioration, the stock should re-rate 15-25% higher as the Medicaid discount partially unwinds.

The primary risk preventing a higher-conviction call is that Medicaid reform implementation timelines could extend into 2027-2028, maintaining the overhang for longer than the 12-month investment horizon. Additionally, the modest top-line growth rate (~3-5% organic) limits the upside ceiling in the absence of multiple expansion. At 10.5x earnings with 15% FCF yield and management actively shrinking the share count, the downside is well-cushioned and the setup favors patient capital.

Rating: **BUY** with 2% position sizing appropriate given the concentrated risk factor (Medicaid) and moderate growth profile.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** EV/EBITDA is approximately 7.0-7.4x (not 6.1x as originally stated), Q1 patient volumes were modestly negative (-1.6%), and the post-lapse observation window is only ~3 months—these corrections reduce but do not eliminate the valuation and "no impact" arguments supporting the recommendation.
