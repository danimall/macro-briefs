## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy trades at $257, down 38% from its October 2025 high of $413 and 15.4% below where it was when previously rated BUY on May 11. The fundamental investment case (largest U.S. nuclear fleet serving accelerating data center demand at rising capacity prices) has not deteriorated. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.74 beat estimates by 5.7%, and full-year consensus of $11.72 implies 22x forward P/E, a modest premium to traditional utility peers but well below prior trading ranges for this company.

The problem is execution risk and macro timing. The FERC ruling declaring PJM's co-location tariff "unjust and unreasonable" directly threatens a pillar of CEG's premium valuation: the ability to sign hyperscaler PPAs at generation sites. Simultaneously, the Calpine acquisition added ~$12.6B of assumed debt into a rising rate environment, raising financing costs and compressing the multiple the market assigns to leveraged infrastructure. Management reaffirmed full-year guidance but the midpoint disappointed relative to buy-side expectations, triggering a post-earnings decline.

The stock warrants a HOLD with a growth orientation. The structural thesis (nuclear baseload scarcity, irreplaceable asset base, capacity price surge) is multi-year and intact. But the prior BUY has failed, the near-term catalyst calendar is uncertain (FERC resolution timing unclear, Crane restart subject to NRC/PJM), and the options market is pricing elevated stress (58% IV vs 45.5% HV, OI P/C ratio of 3.73). Upgrading back to BUY requires either: (a) FERC clarity on co-location economics, (b) visible Calpine deleveraging progress (LS Power divestiture completion), or (c) further price decline to $230-240 where forward P/E compresses to <20x.

## Company Overview

Constellation Energy is the world's largest private-sector power producer following the January 2026 Calpine acquisition, operating approximately 55 GW of generation capacity. The fleet includes 22 GW of nuclear power (14 stations, 25 units), ~23 GW of natural gas (Calpine), 730 MW of geothermal, and 800 MW of battery storage. The company serves 2.5 million customer accounts including three-quarters of the Fortune 100, holding 32%+ market share in C&I direct energy supply.

The nuclear fleet operates at a 94.7% capacity factor (vs. 91% industry average) with average refueling outages of 22 days (vs. 33-38 industry). These assets are functionally irreplaceable — no new commercial nuclear has been constructed in the U.S. in decades, and existing licenses are being extended to 80 years. The Crane Clean Energy Center (TMI-1 restart) represents the first attempted restart of a previously shuttered reactor, backed by a 20-year Microsoft PPA and a $1B DOE loan guarantee.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue was $25.5B with adjusted operating EPS of $9.39 (up 8.3% from $8.67 in FY2024). GAAP net income declined from $3.75B to $2.32B, but this was driven by nuclear PTC phase-outs — a positive signal that market prices exceeded the $26/MWh gross receipts threshold, meaning the fleet earned more from market revenues than the PTC replaced.

Q1 2026 revenue surged 64% YoY to $11.12B (Calpine consolidation) with adjusted EPS of $2.74, beating consensus by 5.7%. Full-year 2026 guidance implies $11.72 EPS at the midpoint, representing 25% growth from FY2025's $9.39.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $4.24B in FY2025, with FCF of $1.29B after $2.95B in capital expenditure. The company has guided $5.7B capex for FY2026 (including Crane restart, nuclear uprates, and co-location infrastructure), declining to $4.7B in FY2027. A $13B cumulative cash flow outlook supports the growth plan.

**Balance Sheet:** Post-Calpine, total debt is approximately $20B+ (pre-existing $7.25B long-term + $1.65B short-term + $12.6B assumed Calpine debt + $2.75B new senior unsecured notes). Credit ratings remain investment grade (BBB+ / Baa1). The planned $5B gas asset divestiture to LS Power will reduce leverage materially. Nuclear decommissioning AROs of $12.9B are a long-dated obligation offset by dedicated trust funds.

**Debt service in a rising-rate environment:** With structural upward pressure on long-end yields, CEG's ~$20B+ post-Calpine debt stack faces refinancing headwinds. The approximately $1.5B of legacy Calpine corporate debt maturing March 2028 is the nearest test.

## Growth Analysis

**Multi-year demand tailwinds are confirmed and quantified:**

1. **PJM Capacity Prices:** PJM West up 247% ($52→$180/MW-day), ComEd up 445% ($31→$170/MW-day). These auction results lock in 1-3 years forward. Each $10/MW-day increase across CEG's ~25 GW PJM-exposed fleet adds approximately $90M in annual revenue.

2. **Electricity Price Surge:** 22-65% increases across all regions in 2025. Data center demand (10-50x more energy-intensive per sq ft than typical commercial buildings) is structural, not cyclical.

3. **Nuclear Baseload Scarcity:** As coal retires and intermittent renewables cannot serve 24/7 load, nuclear becomes the only existing technology offering clean baseload at scale. This creates a scarcity premium that is increasing.

4. **Specific Growth Projects:** Crane restart (~835 MW, 2027 target), nuclear uprates across the fleet, Meta PPA (Clinton, June 2027 start), Calpine synergies.

**Growth rate trajectory:** Consensus projects $11.72 EPS in FY2026 and $13.57 in FY2027, implying 16% compound growth. Estimate revisions over 90 days are modestly positive (+2.0% for current year, +0.8% for next year) — stable rather than accelerating.

**Calpine adds optionality:** The gas fleet provides peaking capacity that complements nuclear baseload, the geothermal assets (Geysers) are effectively renewable with 90%+ capacity factors, and the retail platform adds 62 TWh/year of contracted load. Management expects the deal to be accretive in FY2026.

## Valuation Assessment

At $257, CEG trades at approximately 22x forward FY2026 EPS of $11.72 and 19x FY2027 EPS of $13.57. This compares to:

- Peer median P/E: 23.9x (DUK 19.2x, SO 23.9x, AEP 19.5x, ETR 28.9x, VST 24.8x)
- CEG's 52-week high implied ~35x forward earnings
- Analyst consensus target: $366 mean, $380 median (42-48% upside)

The valuation is reasonable relative to peers, particularly given CEG's superior growth rate (16% compound vs. utility-average 5-7%). However, peers offer 2.3-3.4% dividend yields versus CEG's 0.6%, meaning investors are paying for growth that must materialize. The PEG ratio on forward estimates is approximately 1.4x (22x P/E / 16% growth), which is not stretched but also not clearly cheap.

The key valuation question is whether CEG deserves a traditional utility multiple (18-20x) or a growth infrastructure multiple (25-30x). At $257, the market is pricing it closer to the utility end of the range, implicitly discounting the data center/nuclear premium thesis. If the co-location thesis remains intact, a 25x multiple on $11.72 = $293 (14% upside); on $13.57 FY2027 EPS = $339 (32% upside). If the thesis impairs, traditional utility at 20x $11.72 = $234 (9% downside).

The analyst consensus low of $272 is only 6% above current price, suggesting even bears see limited downside. The high target of $441 implies a return to peak multiples which seems unlikely near-term given the regulatory uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is strengthening. The company holds three advantages no competitor can replicate in the medium term:

1. **Nuclear fleet scale:** 22 GW of nuclear cannot be built by competitors at any price within a decade. License extensions to 80 years make this a multi-generational asset base.

2. **24/7 clean energy capability:** Microsoft, Meta, and other hyperscalers require carbon-free baseload that wind/solar cannot provide. Only nuclear and geothermal meet this requirement at scale.

3. **Largest C&I customer platform:** 32%+ market share in competitive retail energy, 77% renewal rates, 2.5M accounts. This creates a distribution advantage for new products (behind-the-meter solutions, on-site generation, energy management).

The Calpine acquisition extended this into natural gas peaking (complementary to nuclear baseload) and Texas (ERCOT market access). The combined entity operates at a scale and technology diversity that no other U.S. power company matches.

**Competitive threats remain distant:** SMRs (NuScale, TerraPower) are 8-12 years from commercial deployment at scale. CCUS-enabled gas is uneconomic without significant carbon pricing. New market entrants would need $50B+ and a decade to build comparable nuclear infrastructure.

## Risk Assessment

**FERC/Regulatory (High Impact, Medium Probability):** The FERC ruling on PJM co-location tariffs is the most significant near-term risk. If the resolution restricts behind-the-meter co-location economics, it could undermine a core component of CEG's hyperscaler value proposition. However, the underlying demand for 24/7 clean energy remains regardless of tariff structure — the question is whether CEG captures it through co-location or traditional PPAs. Downside estimate: 5-10% of embedded premium if co-location economics materially impair.

**Calpine Integration (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** $22B deal, cultural integration of gas operations with nuclear safety culture. The $5B divestiture to LS Power is required for antitrust compliance — any delay or renegotiation would keep leverage elevated longer than planned.

**Crane Restart (Medium Impact, Low-Medium Probability):** First-of-a-kind nuclear restart with NRC review, FERC interconnection, and PJM queue challenges. A 12-month delay would not kill the thesis but would defer cash flows and potentially trigger Microsoft contract penalties.

**Macro/Duration (High Impact, Active):** At ~$20B+ post-Calpine debt, CEG faces structural refinancing headwinds in the current rate environment. This is a persistent headwind, not a transient one.

**Nuclear Fuel Supply:** Russian LEU ban effective Aug 2024 with retaliatory export restrictions. CEG consuming 29% of capex budget on fuel procurement as buffer. Multi-year contracts mitigate near-term risk, but Western enrichment capacity constraints are a 3-5 year overhang.

## Addressing the Prior BUY Failure

The May 11 BUY at a score of 6.6 has lost 15.4%. The fundamental thesis (nuclear scarcity + data center demand) was correct on the demand side but misjudged two factors:

1. **The FERC co-location ruling's signaling effect was underweighted.** The ruling created uncertainty about the specific mechanism through which CEG would monetize hyperscaler demand, even though the underlying demand is real.

2. **The guidance midpoint disappointment was a near-term de-rating catalyst.** Despite the Q1 beat, management's full-year midpoint slightly below Street expectations signaled that Calpine synergies may take longer to materialize than the market expected.

3. **Rising long-term yields compress multiples for leveraged infrastructure more than previously modeled.** This is structural, not transient.

These factors explain the decline without indicating fundamental impairment. The thesis remains valid on a 12-18 month basis but lacked a near-term catalyst to overcome the headwinds. The correct current posture is HOLD rather than repeat BUY, because the catalyst that would re-rate the stock (FERC resolution, deleveraging, or Crane progress) has not yet materialized.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy at $257 offers compelling long-term value: a 22x forward multiple on 16% EPS growth with irreplaceable nuclear assets at the center of the data center buildout. The competitive position is improving (nuclear scarcity deepening, capacity prices surging), management execution has been strong (734% TSR since separation), and the Calpine acquisition creates legitimate optionality.

The stock has earned a HOLD rather than BUY because: (1) the prior BUY failed and no new positive catalyst has emerged to overcome the headwinds that caused the decline; (2) FERC regulatory uncertainty on co-location directly threatens the mechanism through which the nuclear premium monetizes; (3) the options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress; and (4) the rising rate environment structurally pressures leveraged infrastructure equities. Patient capital should hold for the thesis to play out over 12-18 months while monitoring FERC proceedings and Calpine integration progress for upgrade signals.
