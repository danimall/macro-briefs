## Executive Summary

Deutsche Bank's operating recovery is real and now five years long. H1 2026 net revenues were €17.2bn against a full-year ambition of "around €33bn," post-tax profit was a record €4.1bn, post-tax RoTE was 11.9%, the cost/income ratio was 60.9%, and CET1 finished the half at 13.9%. The 29 July print delivered €8.48bn of Q2 revenue (+8.7% YoY), profit before tax of €2.68bn (+10.7%), and a new €500m buyback, the first DB has executed out of current-year earnings, with ECB sign-off obtained before announcement. The shares rose roughly 6% on the release and have recovered from a $28.12 March low to within 5.9% of the January high.

Most of the equity-market reward for this recovery has already been paid. The EURO STOXX Banks index has doubled in two years; DB no longer trades at a deep discount to tangible book. Tangible book value per share was not disclosed in any source available for this review, but bounding it off the disclosed 11.9% H1 RoTE gives a range of roughly 0.9x–1.2x P/TBV depending on whether the return numerator is stated before or after AT1 coupons. A bank earning 11-12% on tangible equity at approximately 1x tangible book is fairly priced, not mispriced. Against that, USD consensus EPS has been cut 9.9% in 30 days and 12.4% in 90 days for the current year, and 11.8%/12.0% for next year, on coverage of only one to two contributing analysts, so the signal is directionally consistent but statistically thin.

The composition of the Q2 beat matters more than its size. Investment Bank revenue grew 18.5% to €3.19bn (37.6% of group revenue, my calculation from disclosed segment figures) on rates and credit flow trading, with FIC *financing* revenue "essentially flat." Corporate Bank, the highest-return division at 16.4% RoTE and a 62% cost/income ratio, grew 1%. Corporate & Other contributed €65m that management said would revert. Reconstructing from the disclosed H1 (+5%) and Q2 (+9%) growth rates puts Q1 2026 growth at roughly +1.6% YoY, so one quarter did nearly all of the half's work, and it did so in the most cyclical line. The FY ambition of €33bn implies H2 revenue of about €15.8bn, roughly 8% below H1. Our recommendation is HOLD, anchored on the distribution stream rather than on near-term appreciation.

## Company Overview

Deutsche Bank is Germany's largest bank and the only European-headquartered universal bank combining a globally significant fixed-income trading and financing platform with a mass-market German retail and corporate deposit franchise. Management's strategic frame, the "Global Hausbank," is literal: DB positions as primary house bank to German and European corporates (cash management, trade finance, hedging, lending, capital-markets access) with a European wealth and asset-gathering business layered on top. It is not attempting to match US bulge-bracket breadth.

Four reported divisions, Q2 2026:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Investment Bank | €3.19bn | +18.5% | 37.6% | not separately disclosed |
| Private Bank | €2.57bn | +8.2% | 30.3% | RoTE 11% (≈13% ex-India), CIR 70% (≈66% ex) |
| Corporate Bank | €1.91bn | +1.0% | 22.5% | RoTE 16.4%, CIR 62% |

Scale indicators from the 29 July call: assets under management €1.92 trillion (+16% YoY), of which DWS is "almost €1.2 trillion" and Private Bank client assets "almost €850 billion"; 19 million German Private Bank customers; loans +4% and deposits +7% YoY. Record H1 net inflows of €56bn across Private Bank and Asset Management, including a record €25bn in Q2 Asset Management flows skewed to passive (Xtrackers).

One structural note that governs everything below: DB is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and 6-K under IFRS. It files no 10-K, no DEF 14A, and its insiders file no Form 4s. The FY2025 20-F (filed 12 March 2026) and the November 2025 Investor Deep Dive materials were not accessible for this review. Consequently there is no verified FY2025 audited statement set, no disclosed tangible book value per share, no litigation provision figure, no SREP/MDA capital requirement, and no insider or compensation record. The evidence base here is issuer commentary from the 29 July earnings call corroborated by two independent reporters. Those gaps are priced into the scores, particularly Financial Health and Risk Factors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profit.** Q2: revenue described as the 20th consecutive quarter of year-on-year revenue growth; profit attributable to shareholders €1.64bn (+10.5%); noninterest expenses €5.34bn (+7.7%); provision for credit losses €460m (+8.7%, implying a Q2 2025 base of roughly €423m).

**A definitional trap on the "record."** DB's own headline reads "record second-quarter post-tax profit of €1.9 billion." That is total post-tax profit before AT1 coupons and non-controlling interests. On the attributable-to-common measure, Q2 (€1.64bn) was *below* Q1 2026 (€1.912bn). Independent reporting characterised the quarter as "near-record." The presentation is defensible, since the "second-quarter" qualifier is in the headline, but it means the €1.9bn figure must never be compared to attributable-basis consensus.

This resolves an apparent contradiction in the data. Per-ADR Q2 EPS printed at $0.57 against a $0.66 estimate, a 14% miss, while attributable profit grew 10.5%. The precise split between AT1 coupons and non-controlling interests that reconciles the per-ADR figure to the €1.64bn attributable profit cannot be derived from the available sources, so the exact bridge is `[unverified — actual: N/A]`; directionally, a per-share number well below the attributable-profit trend is consistent with DB's AT1 coupon and non-controlling-interest deductions. The "miss" therefore appears to be substantially a basis and per-share artefact rather than a straightforward operating shortfall. It does not explain away the estimate revisions discussed below.

**Trailing earnings and multiples.** Summing the four disclosed quarterly ADR EPS actuals ($0.96 + $0.74 + $0.97 + $0.57) gives $3.24, against a provider-reported trailing EPS of $3.44, a modest discrepancy likely from FX translation timing. At $38.06 that is 11.7x–11.8x trailing. On the euro side, H1 attributable earnings of €3.552bn (Q1 €1.912bn + Q2 €1.64bn) equal roughly €1.90 per share; doubling gives €3.80 and a P/E of about 8.9x on a €63.0bn market cap (converting at the USD/EUR ≈ 1.138 implied by the euro-to-dollar conversions in same-day reporting). That doubling is a mechanical sensitivity, not a forecast: Q1 is seasonally the strongest markets quarter and the FY revenue ambition implies a weaker H2, so a normalized €6.0–6.5bn full-year attributable figure gives 9.7x–10.5x.

**Net interest income — two figures, one guidance basis.** Managed NII "across the key banking book segments and other funding" was €3.6bn in Q2, and full-year guidance on that basis was nudged to "slightly exceed" the prior €14bn. Total IFRS net interest income for the quarter, including trading-related interest, was reported at €4,549m. These are not comparable and neither is wrong; trading interest is offset in trading and other income. Any model must use the €14bn+ banking-book basis and must not annualize €4,549m.

The forward mechanic disclosed here is the most important in the release: "benefits from recent rate decisions will become more pronounced in 2027 and 2028, reflecting our structural hedging approach." A structural-hedge rollover tailwind is largely contractual rather than discretionary, and it underwrites part of the 2028 target independent of policy relief. The specific rate decisions were not identified, so magnitude is unverified.

**Costs.** Q2 noninterest expense grew 7.7-8% against 8.7% revenue growth, positive jaws but barely. Management's reconciliation: roughly €100m of one-off cost from the India exit, absence of prior-year litigation provision releases, and higher fixed and performance-related compensation. Excluding the prior-year releases and India, cost growth was 4%. Separately, roughly €200m of incremental investment (including just under €100m of severance) was offset by roughly €200m of efficiencies. The forward statement, "we expect positive jaws to become increasingly visible in the next 2 years," paired with guidance that Private Bank growth-hiring and investment costs rise in H2, points to H2 cost/income deteriorating from the 60.9% H1 level. The cost story is a 2027-28 event, with the offset that management retains flexibility on investment pacing.

**Credit.** The €460m PCL contains two offsetting quality-of-earnings items: roughly 10bp of the quarter's provisions came from a planned exit of certain nonperforming CRE exposures, described as capital-accretive once executed, and a portion of management overlays was released. Excluding the CRE actions, CRE provisions would have fallen quarter-on-quarter. Underlying performance was said to support a normalized ~30bp provision rate through 2028, with full-year underlying PCL expected to fall slightly year-on-year. The true underlying trend cannot be isolated from available disclosure; paying to remove nonperforming CRE while freeing capital is materially better than provisions rising on credit migration.

**Capital.** CET1 13.9% at 30 June, up 11bp sequentially but down from 14.2% a year earlier; leverage ratio 4.5% versus 4.7%. The Q2 CET1 bridge: +45bp net income after AT1 coupons, –27bp distribution deduction (the 60% payout accrual), +11bp other. RWA rose €5bn ex-FX on loan and commitment growth plus guaranteed funds in Asset Management, with model calibration effects inside "other." Management plans new significant-risk-transfer platforms in H2 2026 "to create additional capital capacity."

Distributions: a €1bn buyback is largely complete, and a new €500m buyback has supervisory approval for H2 execution, funded from 2026 net profit. The CFO noted it has no incremental CET1 impact because it is fully covered by capital already deducted through the payout accrual; this is execution of pre-reserved capital, not fresh headroom. A 60% payout ratio is committed starting with the 2026 financials.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth engines with materially different quality:

**Annuity-like and improving.** Record H1 net inflows of €56bn, AUM +16% YoY to €1.92 trillion, deposits +7%, loans +4%, Private Bank revenue +8.2%. German private pension reform plus the EU Savings and Investment Union would push household savings from deposits into investment products, and DB captures fee migration on both distribution (19 million German customers) and manufacturing (DWS). This leg is genuinely less cyclical than trading and is the most credible part of the growth case.

**Cyclical and currently dominant.** The Q2 acceleration was Investment Bank-led on rates and credit flow trading, with credit helped by "a strong environment for distressed product trading" and a non-repeat of prior-year volatility. FIC financing, the annuity-like part of the IB, was essentially flat. Management's own read is that this environment supports H2 markets revenue relative to the conservative-looking €33bn ambition, with the CFO citing "IBCM pipeline visibility for the second half of 2026" pointing to "further significant year-on-year revenue growth." It is not a base to extrapolate at 9%.

**Stalled where returns are highest.** Corporate Bank grew 1%, with Institutional Client Services down 6% on non-recurring prior-year benefits and FX. This is the 16.4% RoTE, 62% CIR division, and it is not currently the growth engine. Management says headwinds are diminishing and points to 5% sequential growth.

**The estimate revisions are the disconfirming evidence and must be weighted.** USD consensus for the current year has fallen from $4.37 (90 days ago) to $4.25 (30 days) to $3.83, a 12.4% cut; next year from $5.43 to $5.42 to $4.78, a 12.0% cut. Revisions accelerated in the most recent 30 days (-9.9% current year, -11.8% next year), which is the more concerning pattern. Two caveats: only one to two analysts contribute, so the sample is thin and mechanically jumpy; and USD-denominated ADR estimates carry FX translation, which can move estimates without any change to euro earnings power. The direction is consistent across all four forecast periods, and it is the most plausible explanation for a quantitative Sell rank being published on the same day as an earnings beat. Whatever the mix of FX and fundamentals, the market is not being handed rising forward numbers to re-rate against.

**The 2028 target.** Management frames >13% RoTE as a "floor," explicitly "dependent on the successful execution of our strategy." From 11.9% in H1 2026 that is a gap of at least 110bp. Of the four stated upside levers — German fiscal expansion (34-point plan, infrastructure and defence spending), AI-driven productivity, the EU Savings and Investment Union, and a more level regulatory playing field (CRR3 transitional relief, temporary FRTB relief possibly from January 2027, macroprudential simplification) — only AI and organisational simplification are company-controlled, and neither was quantified. The structural-hedge NII rollover is the one non-discretionary support. Model >13% as an execution-plus-policy scenario, not as guidance.

The P/TBV band is the crux, and its width is a verification gap rather than analytical vagueness. Using the €4.1bn H1 post-tax profit as the RoTE numerator implies average tangible equity of about €68.9bn and P/TBV of roughly 0.91x. Using attributable profit after an estimated ~€0.4bn of half-year AT1 coupons implies about €52.9bn and roughly 1.19x. Either way, DB has ceased to be a deep sub-book special situation. A bank earning 11-12% on tangible equity, with a cost of equity plausibly in the 10-12% range for a FIC-heavy European universal bank, is worth approximately tangible book. That is where it trades.

Peer context reinforces the point. In the same quarter UBS grew net profit 17% to $2.80bn and Barclays grew attributable income 36% to £2.26bn, versus DB's 10.5%. UBS earned roughly €2.46bn attributable on a smaller balance sheet than DB's €1.64bn, reflecting a higher-multiple wealth mix. DB is improving from a lower return base and was not fastest in its cohort this quarter. The sector-wide re-rating from 0.3-0.4x book to approximately 1x book has already occurred; from here returns must come from earnings growth and distributions.

Two independent quantitative models put fair value near or slightly below spot (one at €31.80, roughly $36.2, against a base case of €35.6bn revenue and €7.6bn earnings by 2029). One published fundamental view argues the discount is unwarranted. Sell-side positioning is one Buy and one Hold, stable for three months, with the mean target $40.50, 6.4% above spot, and the low target $38.00 essentially at spot. On a stock with rising estimates, consensus targets are lagging indicators worth discounting; here estimates are being cut, so the targets carry more weight, and they bracket the price tightly.

The distribution stream, not the multiple, is the valuation support: 2.28% declared dividend plus 2.4% of market cap in buybacks executed or approved for 2026, with a committed 60% payout that could take total annual distributions toward 6-7% if the payout base and earnings hold. That is a respectable absolute return floor, not asymmetric upside.

Valuation score 5.5: cheap on absolute earnings multiples, fair on the metric that governs bank equity value, with no identified catalyst for the market to be wrong and falling estimates working against the multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

DB's defensible position is the combination no other European bank has: a top-tier global FIC franchise funded by a mass German retail and corporate deposit base. That yields a funding advantage in trading and cyclical upside when rate and credit volatility is elevated. The price is a structurally higher cost/income ratio (63% in Q2) than a monoline wealth manager and a capital-intensive trading book exposed to FRTB.

Evidence of trajectory:

- **FIC share.** Management asserts share expansion "based on disclosures from peers so far." No independent league-table data was obtained; treat as an issuer claim.
- **German incumbency.** Corporate Bank at 16.4% RoTE and 62% CIR demonstrates what the franchise earns where incumbency is genuine. It is also DB's most differentiated exposure to German fiscal expansion versus wealth-centric UBS or UK/US-centric Barclays.
- **Asset gathering.** €56bn H1 inflows, €25bn record Q2 in Asset Management, skewed to passive. This is share capture in a competitive, fee-compressing product set, with volume growth partly offset by mix.
- **Transaction banking credential.** An appointment as RMB Clearing Bank for Europe was announced 10 August 2026. Headline only; appointing authority, scope and economics unverified. Assign no value.
- **Mix direction.** Management's statement that non-IB businesses contribute more than 60% of revenue is true at a point in time but describes a mix moving *toward* the Investment Bank, since IB grew 18.5% while the other three divisions grew 1.0%, 4.3% and 8.2%. Incremental growth is currently cyclical.

Net: modestly improving trajectory, best-in-class in one product (FIC) and one geography (German corporate), structurally mid-pack on returns and efficiency, and not the fastest improver in its peer group. Score 5.9.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate — acknowledged, managed, unsized.** The Q2 provision included a planned exit of nonperforming CRE positions at roughly 10bp of cost, described as capital-accretive once executed; the IB CRE book was "broadly flat"; ex-actions, CRE provisions fell sequentially. What cannot be established from available disclosure is the absolute size of the book, its US/Europe and office/other split, NPL ratio, or coverage. One independent model names US CRE and rising provisions as the key near-term swing factor. A maturing problem being paid down is a better setup than an accelerating one, but the residual tail cannot be sized.

**Litigation — the largest unquantified item.** The only evidence obtained is indirect and telling: Q2 cost growth was inflated by the *absence* of prior-year litigation provision releases, at both group and Corporate Bank level. That establishes DB still carries a provision stock large enough that its release meaningfully moves quarterly results. Current provision levels, pending matters and any 2026 regulatory actions are unverified. For this institution specifically, that is the most important gap in the evidence base.

**Cyclical revenue concentration.** The growth rate depends on flow trading in favourable rates and distressed-credit conditions, plus €65m of Corporate & Other that management said reverts. Compensation ratchets up with IB revenue, so the cost base rises on cyclical income.

**Capital drift and synthetic relief.** CET1 fell 14.2% → 13.9% and leverage 4.7% → 4.5% year-on-year while RWA grew €5bn ex-FX in a single quarter, including model recalibration effects. The stated answer includes new SRT platforms. SRT is legitimate and widely used, but it is priced by third-party investors, so capacity can close in a stress, and it converts capital relief into ongoing cost. A plan that funds growth with SRT while paying out 60% of earnings has less slack than 13.9% suggests. Without the SREP/MDA requirement, headroom above requirement cannot be quantified.

**Policy dependence.** Three of four stated 2028 upside levers sit outside DB's control. Management hedged appropriately ("the speed and exact shape of change is hard to predict"), but if Brussels delivers less CRR3 and FRTB relief than assumed, the RWA and capital math tightens.

**German and European macro.** Nineteen million German retail customers and a German-corporate Corporate Bank make DB a levered play on the German economy: upside if the 34-point plan and defence/infrastructure spending land, downside on both revenue and credit if they do not. Management presents the German fiscal program as an upside driver; the offsetting downside is that a German fiscal or industrial disappointment would hit DB's revenue and credit simultaneously.

**Governance blind spot.** No compensation disclosure, no board composition data, no insider-transaction record, all structural for a 20-F filer, but it means the alignment question cannot be answered. Management repeatedly invokes Shareholder Value Added as its screening metric (the India exit is "SVA positive upon closing"); whether SVA is embedded in incentive design is unverified. Rising performance-related compensation alongside an IB-led revenue surge is the specific item that disclosure would resolve.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term positioning is complacent and cheap. The 10-day ATM implied volatility is 18.2% against 33.0% one-year realized, a 14.9-point discount, in the 2nd percentile of the current chain, with a ±2.6% implied move, balanced 1.2% put/call skew, and a put/call volume ratio of 0.06. Short interest is 0.2% of float at 1.4 days to cover, so there is no bearish positioning to squeeze and no options-market corroboration of a near-term downside catalyst.

The term structure is in steep contango: 18.2% at 10 days, 31.6% at 38 days (±8.6% implied move, covering the 28 October Q3 print), 37.7% at 157 days (±21.0%). The six-month tenor carries a raw put/call skew of –12.7% against 0.3% at three months and 1.2% near-dated, with zero put volume at that tenor. Read this as event risk being priced into 2027 rather than into the next month: the market sees a quiet late summer and material uncertainty around whatever resolves in the first half of next year, plausibly the FRTB/CRR3 legislative package, the H2 cost inflection, and the credit cycle. For a holder, cheap near-dated volatility makes protection inexpensive relative to realized vol; it is also consistent with a fundamental view that the next reassessment point is October, not August.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $47 (+23%)

The €33bn revenue ambition proves conservative because it was set before the Q2 FIC surge and before management observed that "IBCM pipeline visibility for the second half of 2026 points to further significant year-on-year revenue growth." The structural-hedge rollover delivers a largely contractual NII tailwind into 2027-28 on top of a full-year managed NII figure already guided above €14bn. Efficiency programmes convert to visible positive jaws in 2027, closing the 110bp gap from 11.9% RoTE toward the >13% floor, with CRR3/FRTB relief and German fiscal spending as free options. The 60% payout compounds through a shrinking share count. On $4.75-5.00 of normalized ADR earnings at 9.5-10x, or 1.2x tangible book, that is $47.

### Bear Case — $29 (–24%)

Corporate Bank revenue stagnation extends and FIC flow revenue reverts as rate and distressed-credit conditions normalize, while the compensation ratchet from 2026 stays in the cost base. H2 cost/income deteriorates from 60.9% as guided investment lands, and the positive-jaws promise slips beyond 2027. CRE and a litigation matter both surface in the same year, with no provision cushion left after the overlay releases. RWA inflation from model recalibration plus 60% payout takes CET1 toward the low end of the operating range, SRT capacity prices poorly in a stress, and the buyback pauses. At 0.75x tangible book, or 7.5x depressed earnings, that is $29, near the $28.12 March low.

### Base Case — $40.50 (+6.4%)

H2 revenue comes in near the implied €15.8bn, H2 cost/income deteriorates modestly from H1, full-year attributable earnings land in the €6.0-6.5bn range, and RoTE holds around 11%. The 60% payout is honoured, delivering 4.7-6.5% in cash and buybacks. Estimate cuts stabilize as analysts reset to a non-repeating FIC base. The stock holds approximately 1.0-1.05x tangible book, which is the analyst mean target of $40.50 and roughly 9.5x a normalized $4.20-4.30 of ADR earnings.

Probability-weighting at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +2.5% price return plus a 2.3% dividend, or about 5% expected total return over twelve months. That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted outcome, and the -24% bear tail is wider than the +6.4% base upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income-anchored).** Existing holders are being paid a committed 60% of earnings — 2.28% in dividend plus 2.4% of market cap in buybacks declared or approved for 2026 — to wait for the 2027-28 structural-hedge NII tailwind and the deferred cost jaws. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 1x tangible book with consensus being cut 12%.

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the first hard checkpoint at Q3 2026 results on 28 October 2026.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. **Estimate revisions turn.** Current-year and next-year USD consensus stop falling and post a positive 30-day revision, on coverage of at least two analysts. This is the single most important trigger; it would confirm the Q2 strength is being modelled forward rather than dismissed.
2. **Cost inflection evidence at Q3.** Group cost/income at or below 62% in Q3 with year-on-year cost growth below revenue growth on an unadjusted basis, demonstrating the jaws are arriving in 2026 rather than 2027-28.
3. **Price at $33.50 or below on unchanged fundamentals**, which would restore roughly 0.85x the midpoint tangible-book estimate and take the distribution yield toward 7%.
4. **Verified tangible book value per share below $36** (i.e., confirmed P/TBV under 1.06x) in the FY2026 20-F or an interim disclosure, resolving the largest valuation gap in the bull's favour.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of the following:**
1. **CET1 falls below 13.5%** or the leverage ratio below 4.4%, or the H2 €500m buyback is deferred, any of which would indicate the payout and growth plan are competing for the same capital.
2. **A CRE or litigation charge exceeding €500m in a single quarter**, or full-year underlying PCL guidance revised from "reduce slightly year-on-year" to an increase.
3. **Investment Bank revenue declines year-on-year in Q3 or Q4** while group cost/income exceeds 65%, confirming the bear sequence of cyclical revenue reverting into a ratcheted cost base.
4. **The 2028 >13% RoTE floor is withdrawn, re-based, or made explicitly conditional on regulatory relief** at any investor event.

**Reassessment timeline:** 28 October 2026 (Q3 results, with the 38-day option tenor pricing a ±8.6% move around it), then the FY2026 release in late January 2027 and the 20-F in March 2027, which should finally supply verified tangible book, litigation provisions and CRE composition. The first-half-2027 option tenor is where the market has placed its uncertainty; that is also when FRTB/CRR3 legislative outcomes and the cost-jaws promise become checkable.

## Conclusion

Deutsche Bank has done what it said it would do since 2019: twenty consecutive quarters of year-on-year revenue growth, an H1 2026 cost/income ratio of 60.9%, post-tax RoTE of 11.9%, CET1 at 13.9% with ECB approval to buy back stock out of current-year earnings, and a 60% payout committed from the 2026 financials. Disclosure practice has improved: pre-announcing a soft provision line eight weeks ahead of the print, hedging the 2028 target as a floor conditional on execution, and obtaining supervisory sign-off before announcing capital return all reduce the surprise premium this institution historically carried.

The equity has been paid for that. At $38.06 the ADR sits at roughly 1x a bounded tangible-book estimate on an 11-12% return, at the analyst low target of $38.00 and 6.4% below the $40.50 mean, in a sector whose index has doubled in two years. The Q2 acceleration was Investment Bank flow-trading led while the highest-return division grew 1%; the full-year ambition implies H2 revenue about 8% below H1; H2 cost/income is guided to deteriorate; and USD consensus for both the current and next year has been cut roughly 12% in 90 days, with revisions accelerating downward in the last 30 days. Set against that, CRE exposure, litigation provisions, tangible book per share and capital headroom above requirement could not be verified from available disclosure, gaps that argue for a discount to conviction, not a premium.

The holding case is the distribution: 4.7% of market cap already declared or approved for 2026, potentially 6-7% if the 60% payout is fully honoured, while a contractual structural-hedge tailwind builds into 2027-28. That justifies owning the shares. It does not justify buying them at 1x book with estimates falling and a -24% bear case twice the size of the base-case upside. **HOLD**, reassess on 28 October.

- Rationale: Fair value at ~1.0-1.05x the bounded tangible-book estimate and ~9.5x normalized ADR earnings, coinciding with the analyst mean target; no margin of safety at spot.

**Thesis:** DB has delivered twenty quarters of revenue growth, 60.9% H1 cost/income, 11.9% RoTE and CET1 13.9% with ECB-approved in-year buybacks. The equity has re-rated with the European bank complex from deep sub-book to about tangible book, so future return must come from earnings and a committed 60% payout rather than multiple expansion. Q2 upside was Investment Bank flow-trading led while the highest-return division grew 1%, and the FY ambition implies H2 revenue ~8% below H1.

**Variant view:** Consensus reads the Q2 beat as momentum; the more defensible read is that the beat was cyclical and per-share, with forward estimates cut ~12% in 90 days on thin coverage. The real earnings step-up is the largely contractual structural-hedge NII rollover in 2027-28, which is a waiting game, not a 12-month catalyst.

### Catalysts

- **High — 2026-10-28:** Q3 2026 results on 28 October test whether cost/income improves and IB revenue holds; options price a ~8.6% move at that tenor.
- **Medium — H2 2026:** Execution of the ECB-approved EUR500m buyback plus the 60% payout committed from the 2026 financials, taking total distributions toward 6-7%.
- **Medium — 2027-2028:** Structural-hedge NII rollover management says becomes more pronounced in 2027-28, on managed NII already guided above EUR14bn.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **Next two quarters:** CET1 falls below 13.5% or leverage below 4.4%, or the EUR500m H2 buyback is deferred, signalling payout and growth competing for capital.
- **Any quarter:** A CRE or litigation charge above EUR500m in one quarter, or full-year underlying PCL guidance revised from a slight decline to an increase.
- **Q3-Q4 2026:** Investment Bank revenue declines year-on-year in Q3 or Q4 while group cost/income exceeds 65%.
