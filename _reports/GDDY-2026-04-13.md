## Executive Summary

GoDaddy has declined 53% from its 52-week high of $193.55 to $82.25, driven by a Q4 FY2025 earnings report that beat on current results but disappointed with FY2026 guidance of ~6% revenue growth (deceleration from 8.3%). The stock now trades at ~12x forward earnings and carries a 14.3% free cash flow yield — optically cheap for a recurring-revenue technology business generating $1.6B in annual FCF on an $11B market cap.

The valuation is attractive, but the risks are structural rather than cyclical. GoDaddy's customer base is shrinking (20.4M from 21.0M two years ago), domain registrations are secularly declining, and the company's own 10-K now devotes expanded language to the risk that AI platforms may reduce demand for traditional domains, hosting, and website building tools. Management guided to slower growth and attributed it partly to slower AI adoption — which cuts both ways, since GoDaddy's own Airo initiative has not yet demonstrated sufficient traction to offset the risk that competitors or platform-level AI solutions render traditional web presence services obsolete. With 37x debt-to-equity, an Altman Z-Score of 0.96, and aggressive buybacks executed at roughly double the current share price, financial flexibility is limited if business deterioration accelerates.

A prior BUY recommendation at approximately $89 has lost ~10%. The guidance deceleration and the absence of any insider buying despite a 53% drawdown represent material negative developments since that call. The current price embeds significant pessimism, but the thesis for recovery requires either a re-acceleration in growth or sustained FCF generation at current levels — neither of which is guaranteed given the competitive and secular headwinds. The appropriate stance is HOLD until either growth stabilizes above guidance or valuation compresses further to provide a wider margin of safety.

## Company Overview

GoDaddy is the world's largest web presence and commerce platform for small businesses, serving approximately 20.4 million customers with ~80,800 domains under management. Revenue splits across two segments: **Core Platform** (61.8% of FY2025 revenue, $3.06B) encompassing domain registrations, renewals, aftermarket sales, hosting, and security; and **Applications & Commerce** (38.2%, $1.89B) covering website builders, commerce solutions, GoDaddy Payments, merchant cash advances, and Microsoft 365 reselling.

The business model is subscription-based with high retention: 89%+ of FY2025 revenue came from prior-year customers, and retention rates reach 90% for customers of three or more years. ARPU has expanded from $203 in FY2023 to $242 in FY2025, reflecting successful upselling into higher-value products. The company is headquartered in Tempe, Arizona and trades on the NYSE.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

GoDaddy's revenue trajectory shows modest acceleration followed by guided deceleration:

| Year | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|------|---------|-----------|
| FY2022 | $4.09B | — |
| FY2023 | $4.25B | 4.0% |
| FY2024 | $4.57B | 7.5% |
| FY2025 | $4.95B | 8.3% |
| FY2026E (guided) | $5.24B | ~6.0% |

The A&C segment drove the improvement, growing 14.3% in FY2025, while Core Platform contributed 4.9%. FY2026 guidance of $5.195B–$5.275B implies a step-down to ~6% growth. Management attributed the deceleration to slower adoption of AI-related tools and weaker customer acquisition, suggesting the company's growth engine is losing momentum at the exact moment AI investment is supposed to be a differentiator.

### Profitability — Genuinely Strong Operating Leverage

Operating margins expanded from 12.2% (FY2022) to 22.8% (FY2025). NEBITDA margins reached 32.0%, up from 26.7% in FY2023. Operating income grew 26.2% on 8.3% revenue growth in FY2025 — evidence of meaningful cost discipline. Technology & development, customer care, and G&A all declined as a percentage of revenue. The cloud migration to AWS is reducing depreciation from fully-depreciated legacy data center assets.

Net income requires careful reading: FY2023 included a $971.8M tax benefit from deferred tax asset recognition, and FY2024 included a $267.4M benefit from a corporate restructuring. Normalized earnings power is better measured through NEBITDA ($1.59B in FY2025) and free cash flow ($1.58B).

### Cash Flow — The Core of the Bull Case

FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.60B (+24.2% YoY), and free cash flow was $1.58B, representing a 31.8% FCF margin. Capital expenditures were only $23.9M, making the business exceptionally capital-light. Management guided FY2026 FCF to $1.8B, implying continued improvement.

At the current market cap of approximately $10.6B (133M shares × $79.29), the stock carries a **14.9% trailing FCF yield** and a **17.0% forward FCF yield** on the $1.8B FY2026 guide. These are compelling numbers for a recurring-revenue technology business.

### Balance Sheet — Real Vulnerability

The headline leverage metrics are extreme: 37.4x debt-to-equity, 0.61x current ratio, $3.77B long-term debt against $215M equity, and an accumulated deficit of $(2.79B). The Altman Z-Score of 0.96 sits in the distress zone.

Some of this is mechanical: $2.2B+ in deferred revenue (prepaid domain registrations) inflates current liabilities, and aggressive buybacks funded partly by debt have driven retained earnings deeply negative. The business generates $1.6B in OCF against ~$151M in annual interest expense, providing >10x interest coverage.

However, the leverage is not purely cosmetic. The company has $3.77B in debt, and the buyback program consumed $1.59B in FY2025 alone — essentially returning 100% of FCF to shareholders while carrying nearly 4x revenue in debt. If business deterioration accelerates or refinancing conditions tighten (rates are at 3.64% and potentially heading higher), the debt load constrains management's ability to invest in the AI transition that the business needs.

### Capital Allocation — Questionable Timing

GoDaddy repurchased $1.59B in stock in FY2025 and executed two ASR programs totaling $767M in Q1 2025, settling at approximately $176/share. The stock now trades at $79.29 — meaning the company spent $767M at prices roughly 2.2x the current level. A $3.0B new repurchase authorization was approved in April 2025, with $2.17B remaining as of December 2025.

Buybacks at $176 when the stock is at $79 represent real value destruction from the perspective of current shareholders. The math is straightforward: $767M would buy approximately 9.7M shares at today's price versus the ~4.4M shares purchased at $176. The per-share FCF accretion from buybacks is roughly halved relative to what it would have been at current prices.

## Growth Analysis

### ARPU Expansion vs. Customer Base Contraction

The growth model rests on extracting more revenue per customer rather than growing the customer base. ARPU has risen 19% over two years ($203→$242), but total customers declined 3% (21.0M→20.4M). Domains under management also declined from 83.6K to 80.8K. This strategy works as long as upsell headroom remains, but it has inherent limits and becomes more fragile as the installed base shrinks.

### AI: Opportunity and Threat Simultaneously

GoDaddy's Airo platform is its primary growth catalyst, offering agentic AI tools for domain search, logo creation, and website building. However, the FY2026 guidance miss was explicitly attributed to "slower adoption of AI-related tools," suggesting the market is not yet embracing these offerings at the expected rate.

The larger risk is that AI disrupts the entire value proposition. If small businesses can establish digital presence through AI chatbots, social media integrations, and conversational commerce without dedicated websites and domain names, GoDaddy's addressable market contracts. The 10-K's expanded AI risk language — acknowledging that "demand for our website building tools, hosting products, domain names or related services could decline" — is significant because companies do not add legal risk disclosures speculatively.

### Estimate Revisions — Negative Drift

Current-year EPS estimates have declined 2.8% over 90 days ($7.16→$6.96) and are flat over 30 days. Next-year estimates show a modest +1.0% increase over 90 days. The absence of positive revision momentum, combined with the guidance deceleration, suggests the market has not yet found a bottom in expectations. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.96 implies ~12x forward earnings — cheap, but only if estimates don't continue declining.

## Competitive Landscape

GoDaddy competes across multiple fragmented markets: domain registration (Namecheap, regional registrars), website building (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress), commerce (Shopify, Square), and email productivity (Microsoft 365 reselling). Its primary moats are:

1. **Scale**: 20.4M customers and 80.8K domains create distribution advantages and data for AI training.
2. **Switching costs**: Domains are registered at GoDaddy, and migrating involves technical friction and downtime risk.
3. **Brand recognition**: GoDaddy's brand awareness among SMBs is well-established, supported by the "Guides" customer care team (driving ~9% of bookings).

The moat is narrowing. The closure of Google Domains (with its domain portfolio transferred to Squarespace) removed one competitor but benefited Squarespace more than GoDaddy. AI tools from Wix, Shopify, and larger platforms (Google, Meta) are lowering the technical barriers to creating a web presence, reducing GoDaddy's value-add as a simplification layer for non-technical users. The .CO registry loss, while financially immaterial, signals competitive pressure in core infrastructure.

The critical dependency on AWS for cloud infrastructure and Microsoft for email productivity creates concentration risk. The 10-K identifies both as essential partners whose terms changes could disrupt operations.

## Risk Assessment

### Structural Risks
1. **AI disruption of core demand**: If AI platforms reduce the need for traditional domains, hosting, and website builders, the Core Platform segment (62% of revenue) faces secular decline, consistent with the company's own expanded 10-K risk language.
2. **Customer base contraction**: 3% decline over two years with no reversal in sight. ARPU growth can offset this for a period, but a shrinking installed base limits the compounding effect.
3. **Domain secular decline**: Social media, apps, and AI chat interfaces are alternatives to traditional web addresses.

### Financial Risks
4. **Extreme leverage**: The $3.77B debt load is serviceable at current FCF levels but constrains investment capacity and creates refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment.
5. **Buyback value destruction**: $767M spent at $176/share in Q1 2025, now worth ~$340M at market prices. Ongoing repurchases at depressed prices partially offset this, but the capital allocation track record includes a significant recent error.

### Regulatory/Legal Risks
6. **FTC 20-year consent order**: Ongoing compliance costs and reputational overhang from cybersecurity failures.
7. **Data breach history**: Multiple past incidents acknowledged in risk factors, with the FTC order confirming inadequate security practices.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.4% is 12.1 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 36.3%, indicating the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 48.4% > long-term 45.9%), consistent with imminent event/stress pricing. The 1-month expiry shows 13.9% put skew — meaningful demand for downside protection. The put/call OI ratio of 0.31 is relatively low, suggesting positioning is not heavily bearish in outstanding contracts, but the near-term elevated skew warrants caution. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.0%, or roughly ±$3.30 from current levels. Short interest at 5.2% of float is moderate — enough informed skepticism to register, not enough to create squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($120–$140)
- FCF generation remains robust at $1.8B+ (FY2026 guide), and aggressive buybacks at $80-90 per share are dramatically more accretive than the $176/share purchases. At 15x forward FCF, fair value exceeds $140.
- A&C segment continues growing at 12-15%, eventually reaching 45%+ of revenue, shifting the growth profile upward and supporting multiple expansion.
- Airo AI traction accelerates in H2 2026, reversing the deceleration narrative and re-engaging growth investors.
- The FTC order is managed without material financial impact, and the compliance program strengthens the brand's security positioning.

### Bear Case ($55–$65)
- Growth continues decelerating below 5% as AI tools from larger platforms erode demand for traditional web presence services.
- Customer base decline accelerates from 3% to 5%+ annually as SMBs shift to social-first or AI-native commerce platforms.
- Leverage becomes problematic: $3.77B debt requires refinancing in a higher-rate environment (current refinancing would be at 5.5%+ vs. existing weighted average), compressing FCF by $50-100M.
- Estimate revisions turn sharply negative, pushing forward P/E below 10x on reduced earnings.
- Management continues buybacks at unattractive prices, further eroding per-share value.

### Why the Prior BUY Failed and What Has Changed

The prior BUY recommendation at approximately $89 was issued February 13, 2026 and has lost ~10%. The material changes since that call: (1) Q4 FY2025 results revealed FY2026 guidance of ~6% growth, a deceleration from 8.3% in FY2025 — the growth trajectory moved downward rather than stabilizing; (2) management attributed the slowdown to AI adoption headwinds, which undermines the Airo-as-catalyst thesis; (3) the stock dropped an additional 15% on the guidance miss, indicating the market repriced the growth trajectory more severely than the prior BUY anticipated; (4) no insider buying has materialized despite the 53% drawdown — CEO Bhutani and CFO McCaffrey both sold shares at $89 in March 2026, the same price as the prior BUY entry. The absence of insider conviction at current levels is a meaningful negative signal.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Revenue growth re-acceleration**: FY2026 quarterly results showing sequential improvement toward 7%+ growth (specifically A&C segment sustaining 14%+ and Core Platform stabilizing at 5%+).
2. **Customer base stabilization**: Two consecutive quarters of flat-to-positive net customer additions, indicating the contraction has bottomed.
3. **Price decline to $65-70**: At that level, the FCF yield approaches 20%+, providing sufficient margin of safety to absorb further estimate deterioration.
4. **Meaningful insider buying**: Open-market purchases by C-suite executives totaling $500K+ would signal management conviction in the turnaround.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Revenue growth below 4%** for two consecutive quarters, indicating the business has lost organic growth momentum.
2. **FCF decline below $1.4B** (15%+ miss to guidance), suggesting the operating leverage story is reversing.
3. **Customer base decline accelerating to >5% annually**, indicating the secular risk thesis is materializing faster than expected.
4. **Debt refinancing at spreads implying credit deterioration** (>350bps above SOFR).

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural catalyst is Q1 FY2026 earnings (expected May 2026), which will provide the first read on whether FY2026 guidance is achievable or at risk. A full reassessment should follow that report.

## Conclusion

GoDaddy presents a genuine tension between a compelling valuation (12x forward earnings, ~15% FCF yield, 70% discount to peer median P/E) and real structural headwinds (AI disruption risk, shrinking customer base, decelerating growth, extreme leverage). The stock has repriced from a growth multiple to a value multiple; the question is whether the value is sustainable or whether the business is on a secular decline curve.

The FCF generation is authentic: $1.6B trailing, $1.8B guided, on a capital-light model with high customer retention. At $79.29, the market is assigning minimal value to the growth optionality in A&C and Airo AI. If either of those initiatives gains traction, the stock is significantly undervalued. But the guidance deceleration, insider selling, customer base contraction, and expanded AI risk disclosures in the 10-K all argue against high conviction in a near-term recovery.

Given the prior BUY at $89 has not worked, the growth trajectory has worsened since that call, and no insiders are buying at these levels, maintaining a BUY here requires ignoring the evidence that the thesis has degraded. The appropriate stance is HOLD, with a clear path to BUY if growth re-accelerates or the stock declines another 15-20% to provide a wider margin of safety against the structural risks. The company's FCF generation provides a durable floor, but the ceiling depends on factors (AI traction, customer acquisition recovery) that have moved in the wrong direction over the past two quarters.
