## Executive Summary

Columbia Sportswear trades at $57.75, representing a 15.7% discount to the 52-week high of $68.50 and a forward P/E of approximately 14.4-16.3x on management's FY2026 guidance of $3.55-$4.00 EPS. The company delivered a Q1 2026 earnings beat ($0.65 vs. $0.36 expected) driven by exceptional international performance (EMEA +35%), but the U.S. business declined 10% and faces compounding headwinds from tariff-driven price increases and weakening consumer spending.

Columbia has a pristine balance sheet ($791M cash, zero debt), is buying back stock aggressively ($150M in Q1 2026 alone, ~5% of market cap), and is seeing its international business accelerate. Against this, the U.S. segment — 58% of revenue — is in structural decline, operating margins have halved from 11.3% (2022) to 6.1% (2025), and the brand faces increasing competitive pressure from both premium outdoor and athleisure categories.

The 12-month thesis hinges on whether international growth plus cost savings can drive the guided EPS improvement ($3.55-$4.00 vs. $3.24 trailing) while the ACCELERATE brand strategy begins to stabilize U.S. trends. Estimate revisions are moving positively (+16.3% over 90 days for current year), and the buyback provides mechanical EPS support. This is a turnaround story with credible catalysts but material execution risk.

## Company Overview

Columbia Sportswear designs, markets, and distributes outdoor and active lifestyle products under four brands: Columbia (flagship, ~87% of revenue), SOREL (fashion/functional footwear), Mountain Hardwear (technical outdoor), and prAna (yoga/climbing). Products are sold in 115 countries through a 52% wholesale / 48% DTC split. The company operated 173 U.S. retail stores at year-end 2025.

Geographic revenue breakdown: U.S. (~58%), LAAP (~18%), EMEA (~17%), Canada (~7%). The founding Boyle family controls >50% of outstanding shares through direct and indirect holdings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Effectively flat for four years, oscillating around $3.4B. FY2025 revenue of $3.40B represented +0.9% growth. Q1 2026 revenue of $779M was flat YoY. Management guides 1-3% growth for FY2026.

**Margin Deterioration:**
- Operating margin: 11.3% (2022) → 8.9% (2023) → 8.0% (2024) → 6.1% (2025)
- Operating income: $393M (2022) → $207M (2025), a 47% decline on flat revenue
- The driver is SG&A deleverage: SG&A/sales expanded 660 bps from 37.6% to 44.2% over three years
- Gross margins have been stable (49.4% → 50.5%), confirming this is a spending problem

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash + short-term investments: $791M
- Total debt: $0 (undrawn $500M credit facility available)
- Current ratio: ~2.6x
- Altman Z-Score: 3.18 (safe zone)
- Inventory units down 11% YoY (healthy composition)

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $283M (down from $491M due to working capital timing)
- FCF: $217M (6.4% margin on revenue)
- CapEx: $66M (modest)

**Capital Return:**
- Q1 2026 buyback: $150M (~5% of market cap in one quarter)
- Share count declined from 62.8M (2022) to 54.7M (2025) — 13% reduction
- Quarterly dividend: $0.30/share (~2.1% yield)
- Policy: return at least 40% of FCF to shareholders

**Q1 2026 Beat Mechanics:** EPS of $0.65 vs. $0.36 expected (80.7% beat). EMEA surged 35%, LAAP grew, while U.S. declined 10%. The beat was driven by international acceleration and better-than-expected gross margins despite tariff headwinds.

## Growth Analysis

**The International Story:**
- EMEA: +13% in FY2025, +35% in Q1 2026
- LAAP: +9% in FY2025
- China showing "continued positive outdoor category trends"
- Europe outdoor market demonstrating strength

**The U.S. Problem:**
- U.S. revenue: -4% in FY2025, -10% in Q1 2026
- Brand perception weakening among younger consumers
- Athletic/athleisure brands (On Running, Hoka, lululemon) capturing the casualization trend
- High-single-digit price increases from tariffs risk further demand destruction

**ACCELERATE Strategy Progress (Early Indicators):**
- "Engineered for Whatever" brand platform launched
- Amaze Puff product attracting younger consumers
- Bahama shirt (30th anniversary) double-digit growth
- Dry Tortuga Boot sales more than tripled in Q1
- Clearance store closures (28 → 8) protecting brand equity

**Estimate Revisions:**
- Current year: +13.1% (30d), +16.3% (90d)
- Next year: +10.5% (30d), +16.8% (90d)
- Broad positive momentum across all time periods

The growth thesis is credible but narrow: international acceleration must offset domestic decline long enough for ACCELERATE to gain traction. The estimate revisions are meaningful but below the +30% threshold that would signal high conviction. FY2026 guidance of $3.55-$4.00 represents 10-23% EPS growth, though much of this comes from buyback math rather than organic operating improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $57.75 with FY2026 consensus estimate of $3.85 EPS, COLM trades at ~15.0x forward earnings. The peer median P/E is 19.6x, but the peer set is heterogeneous (PVH at 158.6x is an outlier; KTB at 13.7x and CRI at 13.4x are more comparable value-oriented apparel names).

Against value-oriented peers (KTB, CRI, GIII), COLM's ~15x forward P/E is a slight premium, partially justified by the stronger balance sheet and international growth trajectory. The 2.1% dividend yield matches or slightly exceeds peers.

On an FCF yield basis: $217M FCF on ~$3.0B market cap = 7.2% FCF yield. This is reasonable but not compelling given the declining margin trajectory.

The buyback at $150M/quarter creates mechanical EPS accretion of ~2-3% annually at current prices, providing a floor under earnings growth even if operations stagnate.

The market is pricing the U.S. structural decline risk but may be underweighting the international acceleration. At $57.75, the stock is 16% below the post-Q1-beat peak of $67.91 (Feb 4, 2026), suggesting initial earnings enthusiasm has faded as investors refocus on tariff/consumer risks.

## Competitive Landscape

Columbia occupies a difficult middle position: functional outdoor gear at accessible price points, squeezed from above (Arc'teryx, Patagonia capturing premium/style) and from adjacent categories (On Running, Hoka, lululemon moving into outdoor). The company explicitly acknowledges in its 10-K that "athletic, athleisure, and emerging outdoor brands have capitalized on the casualization and style trend."

**Weakening Indicators:**
- U.S. revenue declining while outdoor participation grows globally
- prAna and Mountain Hardwear both received impairment charges ($29M combined)
- Market share losses to both premium and athleisure competitors

**Stabilizing Indicators:**
- International markets receptive to the brand (EMEA +35% suggests brand equity intact outside U.S.)
- New products (Amaze Puff, Dry Tortuga) showing traction with younger demographics
- Gross margin stability (50.5%) suggests pricing power has not yet eroded

The competitive position is deteriorating in the U.S. but stable-to-improving internationally. The core risk is that U.S. brand erosion eventually infects international markets as global fashion trends converge.

## Risk Assessment

**1. U.S. Structural Decline (HIGH):** The 10% Q1 decline is accelerating from -4% in FY2025. If the ACCELERATE strategy fails to stabilize the domestic business by late 2026, the bull case collapses. International growth cannot fully offset indefinite domestic decline at 58% of revenue.

**2. Tariff/Consumer Headwinds (HIGH):** ~$50M in tariff costs paid in 2025 (representing ~25% of operating income). New Section 122 tariffs took effect February 2026. High-single-digit consumer price increases in an environment of weakening consumer confidence create meaningful demand elasticity risk.

**3. Operating Margin Recovery Uncertainty (MODERATE-HIGH):** The Profit Improvement Program delivered $150M+ in savings, but these are being reinvested in brand building rather than flowing to margins. Management must demonstrate operating leverage, but the timeline is unclear.

**4. Family Control / Succession (MODERATE):** Tim Boyle, 76+, has led since 1988. >50% family control. The 10-K notes potential estate tax selling pressure. No clear succession timeline communicated.

**5. Weather Dependence:** Outerwear-focused brand structurally exposed to warming trends.

**6. Short Interest (ELEVATED):** 10.9% of float short with 5 days to cover. This signals meaningful institutional skepticism about the turnaround thesis but also provides squeeze potential on positive catalysts.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing COLM as relatively calm despite elevated fundamental uncertainty. Near-term IV of 36.2% is below 1-year historical vol of 39.4% (a -3.2% spread), and IV percentile is just 13% within the current chain. The term structure is in normal contango (36.2% near-term vs. 40.6% 6-month). The put/call volume ratio of 2.00 suggests recent hedging activity, but OI P/C of 0.05 indicates minimal structural put positioning. The options market is not pricing an imminent catalyst, consistent with a stock in a holding pattern between earnings reports.

The cheap vol environment means protective puts are relatively inexpensive for downside hedging around tariff escalation or consumer weakness catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($68-72, +18-25%):**
- ACCELERATE strategy gains visible U.S. traction by H2 2026
- International growth sustains 15-20%+ trajectory
- Tariff recovery of ~$50M flows to operating income
- FY2026 EPS hits high end of guidance ($4.00) or above
- Buyback continues at $150M/quarter pace, reducing share count by 8-10%
- Multiple re-rates from ~15x to ~18x as margin recovery becomes visible

**Bear Case ($42-47, -19-27%):**
- U.S. decline accelerates beyond -10% as tariff-driven price increases meet weakening consumer spending
- Consumer spending deterioration hits outdoor discretionary hard
- ACCELERATE strategy spending fails to generate returns; SG&A continues rising
- Tariff escalation beyond current levels
- FY2026 EPS lands at low end ($3.55) or below
- Stock de-rates to 12-13x forward (matching deep-value apparel peers CRI/KTB)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. U.S. segment returns to flat or positive growth for one full quarter, demonstrating ACCELERATE strategy stabilization
2. Operating margin shows sequential improvement for two consecutive quarters, reaching >8% on a trailing basis
3. Stock pulls back to $48-50 range (12.5x forward on midpoint guidance) providing margin of safety

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. decline accelerates beyond -12% for two consecutive quarters
2. FY2026 guidance cut below $3.55 EPS
3. International growth decelerates below +10% on a blended basis (suggesting the brand issue is globalizing)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, likely July/August). The Q2 result will show whether tariff-driven price increases are destroying demand or being absorbed. Secondary reassessment at Q3 (the critical fall/winter booking season).

## Conclusion

Columbia Sportswear presents a viable 12-month growth thesis predicated on three specific catalysts: (1) FY2026 EPS of $3.55-$4.00 (10-23% growth over FY2025's $3.24), supported by rising consensus estimates; (2) continued international acceleration offsetting domestic pressure; and (3) $600M+ annualized buyback reducing share count 8-10%, creating mechanical EPS accretion regardless of operating performance.

The balance sheet limits downside, and the 2.1% dividend provides modest income while waiting. At ~15x forward earnings with a 7.2% FCF yield, the stock is not expensive but neither cheap enough to compensate for the substantial execution risk on the U.S. turnaround and the hostile tariff/consumer environment.

The position warrants holding for investors already exposed, with the Q2 2026 earnings report as the critical test. New capital should wait for either (a) demonstrated U.S. stabilization or (b) a pullback to the $48-50 range that would provide more adequate compensation for the risks.
