**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vishay Intertechnology sits at the intersection of a real cyclical recovery and a stock price that has already discounted much of it. After two consecutive loss years (2024 net income –$31M, 2025 –$9M), the company posted a clean turn in Q1 2026: revenue of $839.2M (+17.3% YoY), book-to-bill of 1.34 (strongest in five quarters), backlog of $1.59B (up ~42% YoY to 5.7 months of coverage), gross margin recovering to 21.0% from 19.0% a year earlier, and a return to net profitability at $7.2M. Management guided Q2 2026 to $875–905M revenue and 22% gross margin, framing the "Vishay 3.0" capacity-expansion strategy as beginning to pay off. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 46.8% over 90 days, and current-quarter and next-quarter revisions of +15.9% and +28.2% confirm the earnings momentum is broad-based.

The problem for new capital is price. VSH rallied roughly 300% during 2026, from the ~$12 low in November 2025 to a June high of $69.47, before pulling back to $38.19. That move captured the trough-to-recovery re-rating almost in full. At the current price the stock trades within the analyst target band (low $28, mean/median $34, high $40), and against forward estimates of $0.75 (2026) and $1.54 (2027) it prices at roughly 51x and 25x forward earnings on thin, first-inning recovery numbers. The trailing P/E is meaningless (the trailing twelve months carry a small net loss), so forward and normalized power must anchor the analysis; even on normalized mid-cycle earnings power, the asymmetry that existed at $12–20 is gone.

Layered on top are genuine capital-structure and governance frictions: the $750M of 2.25% convertible notes due 2030 became convertible in July 2026 after the rally cleared the $30.16 × 130% trigger, introducing dilution/cash-settlement overhang and a ~8.6% single-day drop; free cash flow is negative through a $400–440M 2026 capex peak; substantially all cash sits offshore while the U.S. parent bears debt service and buybacks; and the Zandman family controls ~44% of voting power through a dual-class structure. This is a HOLD: a defensible cyclical operating story with no valuation edge at the current price and several unresolved overhangs. The prior four AVOID calls reflected the pre-rally setup; the operating turn now justifies moving off AVOID, while the run-up removes the case for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Vishay is one of the world's largest and broadest manufacturers of discrete semiconductors (MOSFETs, diodes, optoelectronics) and passive electronic components (resistors, inductors, capacitors). Founded in 1962, it operates six reportable segments across roughly 22,600 employees and derives ~76% of revenue from outside the United States. Its components are low-cost-per-unit parts designed into virtually every electronic system, which gives the company enormous catalog breadth but limited pricing power per SKU.

End-market exposure is diversified: industrial (~36%), automotive (~34%), military/aerospace (~11%), plus medical, computing, telecom, and consumer. No single customer exceeds 10% of consolidated revenue, though the top 30 customers together represent ~74%. Vishay competes against larger, better-capitalized players (Infineon, STMicroelectronics, onsemi, Nexperia, TDK/EPCOS, Rohm, Panasonic on the discrete side, and Murata, Yageo/KEMET, TDK among passive specialists). Its competitive edge is breadth of catalog, reliability and mil-spec qualification, and long-standing automotive/industrial design-ins rather than technology leadership or scale economics.

The strategic thrust, "Vishay 3.0," pairs a customer-first commercial model with aggressive capacity expansion (Newport UK wafer fab, Itzehoe Germany 12-inch fab, Mexico power inductors/resistors, Taiwan/Turin diodes) intended to let the company "reliably scale with customers" and capture share in high-margin products (SiC, power MOSFETs, inductors) tied to e-mobility, AI datacenter power, industrial automation, and connectivity.

## Financial Analysis

The financial narrative is a deep cyclical trough followed by a Q1 2026 turn.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$3.5B | ~$3.4B | $2.94B | $3.07B | $839.2M (+17.3% YoY) |
| Net income | $429M | $324M | –$31M | –$9M | +$7.2M |
| Gross margin | ~30% | ~28% | ~19% | ~19% | 21.0% |

Gross margin fell from ~30% at the 2022 peak to ~19% in 2024–25 as revenue declined and heavy new-capacity depreciation and underabsorption hit reported profitability. The recovery is now visible in the sequential data: 19.0% (Q1'25) → 19.6% (Q4'25) → 21.0% (Q1'26), with Q2 2026 guided to 22.0% ±50bps. Operating income improved sequentially over the past three quarters ($0.8M → $14.8M → $22.1M). The book-to-bill of 1.34 (1.47 in semiconductors, 1.23 in passives) and a backlog that grew to $1.59B / 5.7 months from $1.12B a year earlier are the strongest leading indicators, consistent with an early-cycle recovery rather than merely easy comparisons.

Capital intensity is extreme and near a peak. Capex was ~$273M in 2025 and is guided to $400–440M in 2026, roughly half of it for the Itzehoe 12-inch wafer fab. Q1'26 capex was $110.7M versus $61.6M a year prior. That spend keeps free cash flow negative through the investment cycle: –$46.9M in Q1'26 (–$45.2M in Q1'25), though Q4'25 was positive at +$54.9M. Depreciation and amortization run ~$58M per quarter, and EBITDA margin is recovering (7.6% → 8.8% → 9.3%).

The balance sheet is adequate but stretched at the point of maximum investment. Cash of $479M against total debt of ~$983M ($750M convertible notes due 2030 plus $250M drawn revolver) yields net leverage of ~2.3x against a 3.25x covenant, with interest coverage of ~9.7x. Equity is ~$2.08B, and goodwill is only $180M, limiting impairment risk. Two liquidity nuances warrant attention. First, substantially all cash sits in non-U.S. subsidiaries (only ~$13.6M in the U.S. at year-end 2025), yet the U.S. parent funds dividends, buybacks, debt service, and Newport fab funding. Second, the company is drawing on an accounts-receivable securitization program (~$125M of ~$150M capacity) to help fund the capex ramp, a sign that internal cash generation is stretched during the build-out. The $0.10/quarter dividend (~1.0% yield) continues alongside opportunistic buybacks.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is the strongest single element of the thesis. Revenue accelerated to +17.3% YoY in Q1 2026 off a depressed base, with continued sequential growth guided for Q2. The estimate-revision data reinforces the turn:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | $0.14 | $0.12 | +15.9% |
| Next Quarter | $0.25 | $0.20 | +28.2% |
| Current Year | $0.75 | $0.51 | +46.8% |
| Next Year | $1.54 | $1.01 | +53.0% |

The combination of revenue growth >17% and current-year EPS revisions of +46.8% over 90 days is a meaningful earnings-momentum signal, the kind that typically precedes further consensus upgrades on genuine inflection stocks. That signal is at full strength when the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates, but VSH has already re-rated ~300% and now trades at roughly 51x the 2026 estimate and ~25x the 2027 estimate. At those multiples, rising estimates are partially, though not fully, discounted. The 2027 forward multiple of ~25x on +53% next-year revisions leaves some room if the recovery compounds, which is what keeps this out of AVOID territory; the 51x current-year multiple caps the near-term upside.

Structural demand drivers are real: automotive electrification, AI datacenter power delivery, industrial automation, and 5G/connectivity. The industrial and automotive end markets (together ~70% of revenue) are early-cycle beneficiaries of the components upturn. The durability question is whether the recovery is broad and sustained or a restocking bounce. Book-to-bill above 1.0 across both semiconductors and passives, plus rising backlog, argue for the former. The offset is that ASPs are still declining (~–1.1% QoQ in Q1'26), so margin recovery depends on mix and capacity absorption rather than price, a leverage source that can reverse quickly if the industry adds supply into the same upcycle.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are not usable here, since the trailing period spans a net loss and produces a nonsensical multiple. On forward earnings the picture is a stock priced for the recovery to continue rather than to surprise, trading at ~51x the $0.75 2026 consensus and ~25x the $1.54 2027 consensus.

Peer comparison is genuinely heterogeneous and offers little clean anchor. The electronic-components peer set shows P/Es of 18.9x (BDC), 55.4x (KN), negative earnings at Rogers and Littelfuse, and 85x at Corning, a peer median of 55.4x that reflects a group of companies at various points in their own earnings cycles, not a coherent multiple to apply. The peer data confirms that no peer trades at a valuation that would suggest VSH is materially cheap on forward numbers; the sector is broadly at cyclical-trough or early-recovery earnings that inflate reported P/Es across the board.

The most relevant framing is normalized earnings power. Vishay generated $429M net income at the 2022 peak (roughly $3/share) and –$31M at the trough. Mid-cycle earnings power somewhere in the $1.50–2.50 range is plausible if the Vishay 3.0 capacity converts to high-margin revenue and gross margin climbs toward the mid-20s%. On $2.00 of mid-cycle EPS, $38 is ~19x, reasonable but not cheap for a mid-cap cyclical competing against larger rivals, with a governance discount and no pricing power. The stock sits within the analyst target band ($28 low, $34 mean, $40 high). On an inflection stock with a reasonable multiple, analyst targets often lag reality and act as floors; here the current-year multiple of 51x is above the "reasonable" threshold, so the targets are more likely fair markers than stale floors. The upside from consensus targets is limited (~+5% to the high) and the downside to the low target is ~–27%.

The valuation score reflects a two-sided setup with no identifiable mispricing at the current price. Cheapness is not the case; the stock is fairly-to-fully valued on the recovery it has already delivered. Applying appropriate skepticism to any "cheap on 2027 estimates" argument (2027 numbers assume a clean two-year ramp that the capex/execution risk could disrupt) keeps this at a 4.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Vishay's competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving at the operating level but structurally challenged. It is a mid-cap competing against giants: Infineon and onsemi dwarf it in R&D and capital resources, and most former peers have been absorbed by larger, better-funded players. Its moat is catalog breadth, reliability, mil-spec qualifications, and design-in stickiness in automotive and industrial, not technology leadership.

The Vishay 3.0 bet is that pre-built capacity lets the company grow faster than end markets by serving lead-time-sensitive customers when demand scales. The risk embedded in that thesis is that larger rivals are adding capacity into the same upcycle, which could compress the very pricing power the expansion is meant to capture, a concern given that ASPs are still declining even as volumes recover. The inductors segment (~27% operating margin) is a genuine bright spot and the clearest evidence of mix-driven improvement, and the MOSFET segment turned operating-profitable (+0.8%) after –6.1% a year earlier. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 77.3 versus a peer group at 268.2 shows that Vishay has structurally underperformed across the full cycle; the 2026 rally is a catch-up, not evidence of durable competitive gains. Competitive position is stable at best, with improving segment margins offset by persistent price erosion and scale disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and several are not fully discounted.

**Capacity-expansion execution and cyclical timing (the central risk).** Vishay is adding large capacity into and through a downturn. Management explicitly warns of construction delays, cost overruns, equipment qualification issues, poor yields, and "excess capacity during periods when demand recedes, resulting in inefficient use of capital." The entire thesis hinges on the upcycle arriving and holding before the new fixed-cost base becomes a drag.

**Negative free cash flow through peak capex.** With capex guided to $400–440M against recovering-but-thin EBITDA, FCF will likely stay negative near-term. Reliance on the revolver and receivable securitization, combined with the U.S.-parent liquidity mismatch, creates financing pressure if the recovery stalls.

**Convertible-note conversion overhang (newly live).** The $750M of 2.25% notes due 2030 (conversion price $30.16) became convertible in July 2026 after the stock cleared the 130% threshold. Conversion could dilute shareholders or force cash outlays the cash-poor U.S. parent may struggle to fund, with cross-default links to the credit facility. This directly caused an ~8.6% single-day drop and remains an active overhang so long as the stock trades above the trigger.

**Geographic and policy exposure.** 55 years of manufacturing in Israel is explicitly flagged against Middle East conflict risk; 2025 U.S. tariffs and retaliation add cost and demand uncertainty.

**Governance discount.** Dual-class control (Class B at 10 votes each, Zandman family ~44.4% of total voting power), a classified board, blank-check preferred, and Section 203 defenses entrench the family and prevent public shareholders from capturing a control premium. A CEO pay increase to ~$10.3M in 2025 during a loss year, layered on 5-year TSR that badly trailed peers, is a persistent structural discount factor.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing high uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 103.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 62.2%, rich by ~41 points, indicating the market expects large moves and is charging a premium for that. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±23.3%. The term structure is in contango (near-term 103.6% below long-term 108.7%), a normal-to-complacent shape rather than acute near-term stress. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.3% near-term, steepening to -6.9% at 6 months), and put/call volume (0.58) and OI (0.69) ratios are not extreme. The most telling change is the collapse in IV from 218.5% to 103.6% alongside the price falling from $64.61 to $38.21; the acute post-rally, post-convertible-conversion volatility has drained as the stock settled ~41% off the June high. Short interest at 12.5% of float (2.0 days to cover) reflects meaningful institutional bearishness but is not crowded, and the low days-to-cover means little squeeze fuel. The market prices genuine two-sided uncertainty rather than a clean directional setup, consistent with the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55–65).** The Q1 2026 turn proves durable. Book-to-bill stays above 1.0, backlog continues to build, and gross margin climbs from 22% toward the mid-to-high 20s% as the Newport/Itzehoe/Mexico capacity ramps and absorption improves. The 2027 estimate of $1.54 proves conservative and normalized earnings power moves toward $2.50+. Estimate revisions continue upward, consensus targets get raised, and the market re-rates the stock to ~25x mid-cycle earnings. AI datacenter power and automotive electrification provide multi-year secular volume growth on top of the cyclical recovery. This is the outcome the +53% next-year revision trend is pointing toward.

**Bear Case ($24–28).** The recovery is a restocking bounce that fades in H2 2026. ASP declines accelerate as Vishay and larger rivals bring capacity online into the same upcycle, capping gross margin below management's targets. The capex program leaves FCF negative into a demand stall, forcing heavier revolver and receivable-securitization reliance while the U.S. parent struggles with offshore-trapped cash. The 2030 convertibles dilute or drain cash. The stock reverts toward the low analyst target of $28 or below, a ~27%+ decline from current levels, as the market re-prices the run-up on unproven earnings.

The base case sits between these: a moderate, real recovery that the current price largely already reflects, leaving a roughly symmetric risk/reward at $38.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: a genuine operating turn without a valuation edge or clear directional catalyst at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$30s or below (near the low analyst target and the convertible trigger zone), which would restore asymmetry against normalized mid-cycle earnings power.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin expansion beyond guidance (toward 24%+) with book-to-bill sustained above 1.1, confirming the Vishay 3.0 capacity is converting to durable high-margin revenue rather than a restocking bounce.
- Resolution of the convertible overhang (settlement structure clarified, capped-call protection confirmed, or notes refinanced) that removes the dilution/cash-drain uncertainty.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Book-to-bill falling back below 1.0 or backlog contracting for two consecutive quarters, signaling the recovery was a restock.
- Gross margin stalling or reversing below ~21% while capex remains at the $400–440M peak, confirming excess-capacity underabsorption.
- Any covenant pressure (net leverage approaching 3.0x against the 3.25x covenant) or a forced draw that signals the U.S.-parent liquidity mismatch is binding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which will test the $875–905M revenue and 22% gross margin guidance and reveal whether book-to-bill and backlog momentum held into the summer. The trajectory of estimate revisions over the next 60–90 days is the interim tell.

## Conclusion

Vishay is a legitimate cyclical-inflection and self-help capacity story, and the prior string of AVOID ratings (appropriate when the stock traded at $12–20 and the recovery was unproven) no longer fits now that Q1 2026 delivered a clear operating turn. Those signals justify moving off AVOID.

What prevents a BUY is that the market has already done most of the re-rating. The 2026 rally captured the trough-to-recovery move, and the stock now trades within the analyst target band on ~51x current-year and ~25x next-year forward earnings, fairly-to-fully valued on the recovery it has delivered, with no identifiable mispricing. Against that priced-in recovery sit several unresolved frictions: the live convertible-note conversion overhang, negative free cash flow through the capex peak, an offshore-cash/U.S.-parent liquidity mismatch increasingly funded by receivable securitization, persistent ASP declines that make margin gains dependent on absorption rather than price, and an entrenched dual-class governance structure with a 5-year TSR that badly trailed peers. The result is a two-sided setup with roughly symmetric risk/reward: meaningful upside if the recovery compounds toward mid-cycle earnings, meaningful downside if it proves to be a restocking bounce. **Recommendation: HOLD.** Reassess at Q2 earnings; upgrade on a pullback to the low-$30s or confirmed multi-quarter margin expansion, downgrade if book-to-bill or margin momentum reverses.
