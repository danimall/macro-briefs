**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carlyle is one of the world's largest alternative asset managers, running ~$477B in AUM across Global Private Equity, Global Credit, and AlpInvest (secondaries/co-investment). The business is functioning: FY2025 fee-related earnings (FRE) hit a record $1,236M (+12%), distributable earnings reached $1,691M (+11%), fund management fees rose 10% to $2,397M, and the firm returned ~$0.9B to shareholders while carrying "A–" credit ratings. Reported GAAP screens that flag distress (deeply negative consolidated operating cash flow, weak Altman-Z) are artifacts of consolidating VIEs, CLOs, and Fortitude-related vehicles; company-only operating cash flow was positive ~$1,089M. The correct lens on this company is segment-level FRE/DE, not raw consolidated GAAP.

The problem is not the balance sheet but the earnings trajectory and the mix underneath it. GAAP revenue fell 12% as performance allocations dropped 39% in a sluggish exit environment, and the legacy buyout engine is contracting: GPE distributable earnings fell 7% on smaller successor funds (CEP V, CRP IX, CIEP II raising less than predecessors) and fee step-downs. The offset is real (Global Credit DE +27% and AlpInvest DE +67%), but consensus is not buying durability yet: current-year EPS estimates have been cut 13.3% over 90 days to $3.72, next-quarter cut 9.7%, and the firm has missed the last four quarters (Q1'26 by 8.4%). The stock is down ~27% over the trailing year and sits ~35% below its 52-week high, consistent with the sector-wide derating of listed alternatives managers on rate and redemption-gate fears.

At ~$45.40 the shares trade near ~21x trailing EPS ($2.18) but ~12.2x current-year consensus ($3.72) and ~8.9x next-year ($5.09), cheap-looking on forward numbers that are still falling. The forward multiple is only attractive if the estimate cuts stabilize and Credit/AlpInvest growth converts into carried-interest realizations as the exit window reopens. Absent open-market insider buying (co-founder Rubenstein sold $23M in March; only routine grants otherwise) and with the alternatives complex being repriced on higher-for-longer rates, we rate CG a HOLD. The Q2 print on August 5 is the natural reassessment point.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1987 and headquartered in Washington, DC, Carlyle manages ~$477B in AUM (up 8% YoY) across 27 offices, with ~2,500 employees and 3,200+ carry-fund investors in 87 countries. Three segments define the franchise:

- **Global Private Equity (GPE):** ~$163.5B AUM / ~$101.4B fee-earning. Buyout, growth, real estate, infrastructure, and energy (NGP). The legacy franchise and the current soft spot.
- **Global Credit:** ~$211.3B AUM / ~$169.5B fee-earning. The largest and fastest-growing segment — direct lending, opportunistic/liquid credit, CLOs, aviation finance, and the Fortitude insurance relationship. Roughly quadrupled over five years.
- **Carlyle AlpInvest:** ~$102B AUM / ~$66B fee-earning. Secondaries, co-investment, and fund-of-funds behind an information wall.

The economic model has three legs: recurring management fees (durable), performance allocations/carried interest (lumpy, realization-dependent), and principal investment income on Carlyle's own balance-sheet capital. Roughly 60–70% of carry is allocated to employees; the GP typically commits ~1% of fund capital.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP (FY2025 vs FY2024):** Total revenue fell 12% to $4,779.8M, driven entirely by performance allocations dropping 39% to $1,222.5M as the exit environment stayed sluggish. Net income to common fell 21% to $808.7M. The durable base held up: fund management fees rose 10% to $2,396.6M.

**Non-GAAP (the metrics management and the Street steer by):**
- **FRE: $1,236.2M, +12%** — a record, with a steady five-year climb ($598M in 2021 → $834M → $859M → $1,105M → $1,236M).
- **DE: $1,691.2M, +11%.** By segment: GPE $890.8M (–7%), Global Credit $481.0M (+27%), AlpInvest $319.4M (+67%). The growth is in Credit and AlpInvest; the legacy PE engine is shrinking.
- **Net accrued performance revenues (unrealized carry): ~$2,859M** — stored value awaiting a better exit environment.

**Capital allocation and balance sheet:** Returned ~$0.9B in 2025 (~$505M dividends at $1.40/yr + ~$400M buybacks, 7.5M shares), with a new $2.0B buyback authorization announced February 2026. The firm issued $800M of 5.05% senior notes due 2035 (September 2025); total notes ~$1.875B principal, ~$3.0B total debt, with a $1.0B undrawn revolver extended to 2030. S&P and Fitch affirmed "A–" stable in Q4 2025. Effective tax rate ~18.5%.

**The critical accounting caveat:** Consolidated GAAP operating cash flow is deeply negative (~–$3.3B) and standardized bankruptcy screens flag distress. These are artifacts of consolidating VIEs, CLOs, and Fortitude-related vehicles, not real distress. Company-only assets (~$16.5B) are far below consolidated (~$29.1B). Any screen applied to CG must use segment/DE/FRE metrics.

**Earnings quality is shifting toward the durable.** With performance allocations down but FRE up, the revenue mix is tilting toward recurring management fees — improving earnings quality even as headline GAAP revenue falls. This is the constructive read on an otherwise weak-looking top line.

**Earnings momentum is negative, however.** The firm missed all four of the last four quarters (Q1'26 –8.4%, Q4'25 –2.1%, Q3'25 –7.6%, Q2'25 –0.6%). Consensus current-year EPS has been cut from $4.29 to $3.72 over 90 days, next-quarter from $1.19 to $1.08. Next-year holds up better ($5.09, only –4.3% over 90 days), reflecting expectations of an exit-market recovery. Financial health scores well because the franchise is genuinely cash-generative and appropriately levered for an asset manager, but the estimate trend caps enthusiasm.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is bifurcated, and this is the central analytical tension. The two engines, Global Credit and AlpInvest, are working, driven by the private-credit/insurance land-grab and rising demand for secondaries in a liquidity-constrained LP environment. AUM grew 8% to $477B and record inflows continued, with the Global Wealth build-out (>$7B evergreen inflows, ~$18B wealth AUM, 45,000+ investors) a genuine new vector. FRE growth argues that, in aggregate, the growth segments are outrunning the fade of the flagship.

The drag is structural, not merely cyclical. GPE management fees fell ~3%, FRE ~6%, DE ~7%, driven by successor buyout funds raising *less* than predecessors — a durable concern for the highest-margin carried-interest engine. The 10-K is candid that cumulative LP contributions have exceeded distributions by ~$550B industry-wide since 2020, and net LP distributions only turned positive in Q2 2025 for the first time in 18 quarters. This liquidity logjam directly throttles both realized carry and future fundraising, even with M&A volume up 44% to $5.1T in 2025.

The unrealized carry balance and the Medline monetization template (Carlyle sold ~26.1M Medline shares at $41 for ~$1.07B in March 2026 post-IPO) illustrate the upside if the exit window reopens, though that upside is contingent on markets Carlyle does not control. Growth prospects score in the middle: the durable fee base is compounding, but consensus estimates are falling, the buyout flagship is under-scaling, and Carlyle is a follower rather than a pace-setter in the two structural growth themes (insurance capital and retail democratization). We mark growth down from the prior 5.5 read given the sustained negative estimate revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing multiple is inflated by the collapse in performance allocations — a depressed-carry period, not a fair earnings base — so forward metrics are the right anchor.

Against peers in Asset Management & Custody Banks (median P/E 24.3, median yield 3.1%), CG's forward multiple screens cheap. But this peer set (BEN, IVZ, SEIC, AMG) is largely traditional long-only managers, not alternatives peers; the more relevant comparison is the alternatives complex (Apollo, Ares, Blackstone), which has derated sector-wide on rate fears and redemption-gate risk. Within that cohort, CG's ~12x forward is a discount justified by its weaker growth profile (buyout under-scaling, smaller insurance platform than Apollo/KKR) and its heavier reliance on lumpy carry.

The valuation is not a clear mispricing in either direction. The forward multiple is only attractive if the estimate cuts stabilize, and they have not yet, with current-year estimates down another 8.9% in the last 30 days. Applying the equity discipline that cheap-looking multiples on falling estimates are frequently value traps rather than buy signals, we do not reward the low forward P/E. A specific catalyst is required: an exit-market reopening that converts the accrued carry into realized DE, or confirmation that Credit/AlpInvest growth durably offsets the PE fade. The ~3.2% dividend yield (covered by DE) provides some support but is close to the peer median. The $2.0B buyback authorization at these prices is accretive but is not, on its own, a re-rating catalyst against a falling estimate backdrop.

Analyst targets (mean $55.6, median $56, low $44, high $70) imply ~22–23% upside to the mean, but sentiment is stable-to-softening (a StrongBuy downgraded to Buy recently) and the low target sits essentially at the current price. We treat the targets as a wide band, not a floor, because the estimate revisions underneath them are still negative.

## Competitive Landscape

Carlyle competes with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, and Brookfield, plus traditional managers building private-credit and wealth platforms. The 10-K acknowledges Carlyle competes primarily on performance, not fees, and that industry fee/carry levels are under pressure (LPs negotiating lower management fees, fee holidays, and invested-vs-committed capital bases).

Two structural dynamics dominate, and Carlyle is a participant rather than a leader in both:

1. **The private-credit/insurance land-grab.** Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, and Brookfield have used captive insurance balance sheets to scale permanent, fee-generating AUM. Carlyle's version — the Fortitude relationship (~$24.6B under IMAs, ~$80B insurance AUM under advisory, up 50% since 2022) — is smaller than peers' captive insurers, and the strategic-advisory fee adjusts with Fortitude's profitability, adding volatility and concentration.

2. **The retail/wealth "democratization" wave.** The August 2025 Executive Order opening 401(k) access to alternatives is a potentially large but fiercely contested opportunity requiring heavy distribution investment.

Carlyle's competitive trajectory is the weak dimension. Its strongest legacy franchise (buyout) is under-scaling versus predecessor funds, and it is a follower in the two themes driving the sector's re-rating. Global Credit and AlpInvest are gaining, but Carlyle is not widening a moat relative to Apollo or Blackstone in the areas that matter most for permanent, fee-earning AUM. Trajectory, not level, drives this score, and the trajectory in the flagship is negative.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that matter for CG:

1. **Structural weakness in the flagship PE franchise** — smaller successor funds shrinking the highest-margin carry base. Durable, not purely cyclical.
2. **The industry liquidity logjam** — ~$550B cumulative LP contribution-over-distribution gap since 2020; exits still sluggish despite strong M&A. Throttles carry realization and fundraising.
3. **Fee compression** across the industry as LPs negotiate and wealth-distribution costs rise.
4. **Fortitude concentration and conflicts** — controlling economic interest (10.5% indirect stake) with a profitability-linked advisory fee, drawing rising insurance-regulator scrutiny of alt managers.
5. **Key-person risk** — dependence on Schwartz, co-founders, and Co-Presidents, with fund-doc key-person clauses that can suspend investment periods.
6. **Equity-comp dilution** — large ongoing RSU grants (13.2M in 2024, 8.1M in 2025, 5.8M in Feb 2026), with 2026 comp expense guided higher.
7. **Regulatory** — heavy SEC PE focus, AIFMD II (effective April 2026), SFDR/CSRD.

The alternatives-complex derating adds a sector overlay: managers with evergreen/redemption-exposed vehicles face gate risk, and higher-for-longer rates compress the equity multiples of financing-heavy asset managers. CG's exposure here is real but less acute than pure evergreen-retail-flagship names, since the bulk of its fee base is tied to committed capital. Many of these risks are known and partly priced after the ~27% drawdown, so we score risk in the middle rather than at the low end: the drawdown has done real work de-risking the entry, but the negative estimate trajectory and the unresolved durability of the PE flagship prevent a higher mark.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.1% is rich versus 36.4% one-year historical vol (spread +11.6%, 79th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (48.1% near-term vs 40.2% at six months) — the market is pricing imminent stress around the August 5 earnings print, with an implied move of ±10.8% on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (–1.1%), so this is event-uncertainty pricing rather than a directional bearish tilt. IV has fallen since the prior snapshot (50.3% → 48.1%) as the stock recovered 8.7% off its June low. For a fundamental holder, the elevated near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors waiting for the post-print vol crush, consistent with the HOLD stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60–70):** The exit environment reopens (M&A already +44%, LP distributions turned positive Q2 2025), converting the accrued carry into realized DE and re-igniting fundraising. Global Credit and AlpInvest continue compounding, the Global Wealth build-out scales into the 401(k) democratization opportunity, FRE growth sustains above 10%, and the forward multiple re-rates toward the alternatives cohort as next-year EPS ($5.09) proves conservative. The $2.0B buyback shrinks the share count into recovering earnings. Analyst high target $70.

**Bear Case ($35–40):** The PE flagship continues under-scaling with successor funds shrinking, the liquidity logjam persists into 2027, fee compression accelerates, and higher-for-longer rates keep the alternatives complex derated. Estimate cuts continue, the firm extends its four-quarter miss streak, and carry realizations disappoint. Bear ~$36–40, roughly 12–21% below current price.

**Base Case (~$52):** FRE grows mid-teens, Credit/AlpInvest offset the GPE fade, carry realizations recover partially but not fully, and the stock re-rates modestly toward ~10x a stabilizing next-year estimate as the exit window gradually improves. Roughly 15% upside plus the ~3.2% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings on August 5 is the natural checkpoint — it tests whether the four-quarter miss streak breaks, whether estimate cuts stabilize, and whether carry realizations (post-Medline template) are recovering.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window, OR a Q2 beat that ends the miss streak.
- Realized performance revenue / carry realizations inflect upward as the exit market reopens, converting the accrued balance into DE.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s / high-$30s (bear-case zone) with fundamentals intact, which would restore asymmetric upside to the mean target.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- GPE distributable earnings decline accelerates beyond the –7% pace, or successor buyout funds are confirmed to raise materially below predecessors, signaling durable flagship impairment.
- Next-year EPS estimates roll over materially (the currently-resilient $5.09 breaking down), removing the forward-valuation support.
- A dividend or buyback cut, or a Fortitude-related impairment/conflict event.
- Clustered C-suite open-market selling (beyond routine grants/diversification).

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Carlyle is a healthy, cash-generative franchise wrapped in a misleading GAAP picture: the distress screens are consolidation artifacts, and the real business (record FRE, +10% management fees, positive company-only cash flow, "A–" ratings, ~$0.9B returned) is sound. But the investment case is genuinely two-sided: the growth segments are outrunning a structurally fading buyout flagship, and the market is not yet convinced that dynamic is durable, as evidenced by falling estimates across every window and four straight misses. At ~12x forward on estimates that are still being cut, the shares are cheap-looking but carry value-trap risk: the low multiple reflects the exit-market logjam and PE under-scaling, not an obvious mispricing.

The score trajectory (5.6 → 5.8 over four reports, all HOLDs) has been drifting up on improving financial-health and risk-factor reads, but the sustained negative estimate revisions and the deteriorating growth-segment mix argue against extending that improvement this cycle; we mark growth prospects down. With no open-market insider buying, an alternatives complex being repriced on rates, and near-term options pricing event stress into the August print, the disciplined stance is to wait for the Q2 result and for estimate cuts to stabilize before committing capital. The ~3.2% covered dividend and the discount to the mean analyst target provide a floor, but not a catalyst. HOLD, reassess August 5.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing P/E was overstated as ~30x (actual ~21x on EPS $2.18), modestly reducing the "expensive on trailing / cheap on forward" contrast; the ~3.2% dividend yield is at/near the peer median rather than below it, marginally strengthening the yield-support argument.
