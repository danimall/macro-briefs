**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Core & Main is a specialty distributor of water, wastewater, storm drainage, and fire protection infrastructure products, positioned as one of only two national players in a ~$44B market where it holds roughly 17% share. The stock has fallen 33% from its September 2025 high of $67.18 to $43.62, now sitting near its 52-week low of $42.50. The drawdown reflects a genuine deceleration — reported revenue growth slowed to +2.8% in FY2025 and organic volumes turned negative in Q1 FY2026 — as higher-for-longer rates suppress residential lot development and non-residential construction starts, which together account for ~56% of sales.

The central question is whether the market has overcorrected. At ~14x forward EPS (consensus current-year $2.87, next-year $3.16), CNM trades at less than half the peer median P/E of 31.1x, and consensus targets ($40 low, $62 median, $75 high) imply ~38% upside to the mean of $60.27. This report earlier defaulted to HOLD on the reasoning that the estimate-revision momentum is not accompanied by an organic-revenue reacceleration. That framing understated the asymmetry. Rising estimates (+15.1% current-year over 90 days), four consecutive earnings beats (up to +22.9%), expanding gross margin (26.7% → 27.2%), a director buying into weakness near the current price, uncrowded short interest (5.5%), and cheap call-skewed options collectively describe a de-rated cyclical priced for continued softness while fundamentals are stabilizing at a higher-margin base.

I am upgrading to a modest BUY. The organic-demand recovery that would validate a re-rating has not arrived, all debt is variable-rate, and a growing Tax Receivable Agreement liability diverts ~$40-44M/year of cash from Class A holders. But a sub-15x forward multiple, a leader in a consolidating duopoly, trough pricing, insider buying, and a target distribution skewed to the upside is closer to the inflection/value archetype than to a balanced two-sided call. I size the position accordingly.

## Company Overview

Core & Main connects 5,000+ suppliers to 60,000+ fragmented customers — municipalities, private water utilities, and professional contractors — through 370+ branches across the U.S. and Canada, carrying 225,000+ SKUs. The business descends from HD Supply Waterworks, was acquired by CD&R in 2017, rebranded, and IPO'd in July 2021; CD&R fully exited by January 2024.

End-market mix in FY2025 was ~44% municipal (repair/replace-driven and cyclically stable), ~38% non-residential, and ~18% residential. Product mix runs ~67% pipes/valves/fittings, ~16% storm drainage, ~9% smart utility/meters, and ~8% fire protection. Customer and supplier concentration is low (top 50 customers ~12% of sales, largest customer ~1%, largest supplier ~7% of purchases), which insulates the P&L from single-relationship shocks.

The competitive structure is the core of the thesis. CNM and Ferguson's waterworks business are the only two national distributors; the rest of the market is served by hundreds of regional and local players. That fragmentation makes CNM the industry's acquirer of choice, and the moat — local municipal-specification expertise, scale purchasing with preferred/exclusive distribution rights, proprietary technology tools, and one-stop-shop project management — is durable and hard for sub-scale competitors to replicate.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue rose from $6,651M (FY2023) to $7,441M (FY2024, +11.9%) to $7,647M (FY2025, +2.8%). Adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat across FY2024→FY2025 ($930M → $931M) despite continued acquisitions, which means organic volumes were weak-to-negative even before Q1 FY2026 made it explicit.

Q1 FY2026 (ended 5/3/2026) confirmed the softness. Net sales were flat at $1,910M vs. $1,911M, with management stating volumes declined and acquisitions offsetting. Product-line detail showed PVF –1.8% and storm drainage –5.8% (both volume-driven), partly offset by fire protection +17.1% and smart utility +7.8%. Gross margin expanded to 27.2% from 26.7% on private-label and sourcing initiatives, and Adjusted EBITDA rose 0.9% to $226M with Adjusted diluted EPS of $0.72 vs. $0.68.

Gross margin has held or expanded through a volume downturn (26.6% → 26.9% → 27.2%), which is unusual for a distributor and demonstrates pricing discipline rather than pure pass-through economics. If organic volumes recover with rate relief, operating leverage on a higher-margin base could be meaningful.

The balance sheet is a leveraged, asset-light distribution model. Total debt is ~$2.16B, D/E ~2.0x, net leverage ~2x EBITDA, with an Altman Z of 2.69 (grey zone). All debt is variable-rate term loans (2028 and 2031 maturities), partially swap-hedged; the $1.25B ABL is undrawn with ~$1.23B availability and was extended to April 2031. Interest expense fell from $142M (FY2024) to $120M (FY2025) on repricing and rate cuts. FY2025 operating cash flow was $650M against only $46M capex, yielding $604M FCF (~7.9% margin, ~7.4% FCF yield on the current $8.2B market cap). ROE is a healthy 23%, though partly leverage-assisted.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: a $1B buyback authorization ($581M remaining as of 5/3/2026), $155M repurchased in FY2025 and $88M in Q1 FY2026, no dividend. The buyback plus low capex provides real downside support at current prices.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth vectors are at work, and distinguishing them is essential. The first is inorganic — CNM completed ~24 acquisitions across FY2023–FY2025 and is the natural consolidator of a fragmented market. The second is organic, which is currently flat-to-negative and rate-dependent. FY2025 deal value was only $76M versus FY2024's $769M, signaling either M&A discipline amid richer target multiples or a deliberate pause; no acquisitions closed in Q1 FY2026.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive but below the threshold that flags a systematic underestimation. Current-quarter estimates are +9.6% over 90 days, next-quarter +10.8%, current-year +15.1% (to $2.87), and next-year +6.0% (to $3.16). The four-quarter beat record (6.6%, 22.9%, 22.9%, 15.9%) supports confidence in these estimates. These revisions are driven primarily by margin initiatives and acquisition contribution rather than a volume inflection, which is why I hold growth prospects at 5.3 rather than scoring it as a full earnings-recovery inflection.

Two structural tailwinds underpin the multi-year case. The IIJA allocates $55B to water infrastructure, the majority of which management says is yet to be realized, providing a multi-year municipal funding backdrop layered on top of an aging U.S. water system requiring replacement. The municipal end market is the stable anchor across the cycle. Separately, an emerging data-center water-infrastructure narrative positions CNM as a distribution-layer beneficiary of AI capex straining water resources, though for CNM this is an unquantified optionality rather than a current earnings driver.

Stated plainly, this is a rate-cycle-dependent, M&A-fueled compounder with a stable municipal anchor and improving margins. It is not organically growing today. The upside case rests on rate cuts reaccelerating residential and non-residential construction while IIJA funds flow, at which point operating leverage on a higher-margin base compounds.

## Valuation Assessment

CNM trades at a forward P/E of roughly 13.8-15.2x (on $2.87-$3.16 consensus EPS at $43.62). The peer median in Trading Companies & Distributors is 31.1x (MSM 31.1x, AIT 30.1x, DXPE 31.3x; REZI and DNOW carry negative multiples and are less comparable). CNM is the only national-scale specialty distributor in the group and trades at less than half the peer median.

The framework's caution against treating "cheap" as a buy signal is warranted, and I have weighed it. Cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way when it reflects structural impairment, which is not the case here. CNM is a share-gaining leader in a consolidating market with expanding margins, a 7.4% FCF yield, and rising estimates. The multiple compression is a rate-cycle de-rating, not an impairment, which is the distinction between a value trap and a mispriced cyclical.

On cycle-adjusted earnings, the case strengthens. Trailing/forward EPS reflects a construction-cycle trough for the ~56% of sales tied to residential/non-residential activity. Normalized mid-cycle earnings power — organic volumes recovering to low-single-digit growth on a higher-margin base plus continued M&A — supports EPS well above the current $2.87 within 18-24 months. At even 18-20x on $3.16 next-year EPS, the stock is worth $57-63, matching the consensus median of $62. The analyst target distribution is asymmetric to the upside: only ~8% downside to the low target versus 42-72% upside to median/high.

The framework's principle that analyst targets act as floors rather than ceilings applies here — the stock is cheap relative to rising estimates (forward P/E well under 30x), so consensus targets are more likely to lag than to lead. I assign a valuation score of 6.8, high enough to reflect a specific, identifiable mispricing (a leader at half the peer multiple with rising estimates), tempered by the absence of a dated catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

CNM occupies the stronger structural position in its category. It is one of two national distributors atop a long tail of hundreds of sub-scale regionals, giving it scale purchasing leverage, preferred/exclusive distribution rights, and the balance sheet to serve as the industry's consolidator. Its ~17% share leaves substantial runway to compound share through disciplined roll-up.

The competitive trajectory is improving on two measurable fronts. Gross margin expanded through a volume downturn, evidence of pricing power and a differentiated private-label/sourcing capability rather than pass-through economics. And the fire protection and smart utility product lines are growing double digits, diversifying the mix toward higher-value categories including metering technology. The primary competitive risks — supplier disintermediation via direct sales, and strain on supplier relationships from CNM's own private-label push — are real but slow-moving and not yet visible in the numbers. I score competitive position 7.0, consistent with the improving trajectory that prior reports identified.

## Risk Assessment

**Organic-demand cyclicality is the dominant near-term risk.** With ~56% of sales tied to construction and residential lot development pressured by higher-for-longer rates, the base business is not growing organically. The bull case is explicitly rate-dependent. A prolonged higher-rate regime extends the organic drought.

**The Tax Receivable Agreement is a real, growing, and under-screened cash drag.** CNM must pay 85% of realized tax benefits to former CD&R/management holders. TRA payments jumped to $42M in Q1 FY2026 alone (vs. $18M prior year), running ~$40-44M/year against a $720M total liability. This is roughly 7% of FCF diverted from Class A holders, and a change-of-control could trigger an accelerated ~$553M+ payment, structurally suppressing takeover premium.

**All variable-rate debt.** Each 100bps move is ~$22-34M of annual interest, partially hedged, and the larger swap steps up to a 5.913% effective rate in July 2026, raising hedged cost. In a higher-for-longer environment this caps the interest-expense tailwind that helped FY2025.

**Acquisition dependence and goodwill.** Goodwill ($1.92B) plus intangibles ($0.79B) equal 46% of total assets. If organic softness persists, integration complexity and goodwill-impairment risk rise. The unretrievable July 2, 2026 Item 1.01 material agreement is a flagged unknown; its contents could not be confirmed from the sources, warranting follow-up but not over-interpretation.

**Insider signals are mixed but net-constructive.** Director James Hope made two open-market purchases into weakness — ~$100K at $50.70 (4/7/2026) and ~$95K at $46.01 (7/6/2026), the latter near the current price — a genuine, price-relevant vote of confidence at trough levels. Against this, CEO Witkowski adopted a new 10b5-1 plan (4/6/2026) to sell up to 200,000 shares between July 2026 and January 2027, the largest insider dollar flow and squarely inside the reassessment window. I weight the CEO sale as low-signal: it is a diversification plan from a newly promoted executive (former CFO), it co-occurs with rising estimates and record margins rather than deteriorating fundamentals, and pre-scheduled 10b5-1 sales carry less informational content than open-market purchases. The director's discretionary buying at the low is the more informative signal. Management comp is well-aligned (Adjusted EBITDA, working-capital %, and share price), and TSR has outperformed peers (225 vs. 169 indexed).

Governance carries the standard defensive apparatus — classified board, supermajority bylaw amendments, exclusive-forum provision, and a Management Feeder NCI with ~6.3M shares of potential future Class A dilution. These are priced and not deteriorating.

I score risk factors 5.5: the idiosyncratic risks (TRA drag, variable-rate debt, organic softness) are real but largely reflected in a sub-15x multiple, and the drawdown has already discounted most of the cyclical bear case.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly supportive of the upgrade. Near-term ATM IV is 35.7% against 43.3% one-year historical vol — cheap (spread –7.6%, 3rd percentile within the current chain) — suggesting the market is not pricing an imminent shock and that entry timing is favorable for a long-biased position. Put/call skew is –4.7% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.33, indicating no elevated hedging demand against the stock. IV collapsed from 79.1% to 35.7% since the June 8 snapshot as the Q1 print resolved uncertainty. The term structure is in normal contango. The call-skewed, cheap-IV configuration is consistent with a de-rated name where downside is perceived as bounded.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($60-75):** Rate cuts reaccelerate residential and non-residential construction, organic volumes turn positive against a higher-margin base (27%+ gross margin), IIJA water funds flow through the municipal book, and M&A resumes. Estimate revisions continue rising, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median, and $3.16+ next-year EPS at 20-24x supports $63-76. The data-center water-infrastructure narrative adds unquantified optionality. This matches the $75 street high and the median $62.

**Base case (~$50, +15%):** Organic volumes stay soft-to-flat through 2026 but margins hold and M&A resumes modestly. EPS reaches ~$3.00-3.16, the multiple drifts up to ~16-17x as the cycle bottoms, and the stock recovers toward $50 while the buyback supports the float. This is the most likely 12-month outcome.

**Bear case ($36-40, –8% to –17%):** Higher-for-longer rates extend the construction drought, organic volumes deteriorate further, a goodwill impairment surfaces, and the swap step-up plus flat volumes compress FCF. The multiple stays at ~13-14x on flat-to-lower EPS. Downside is bounded by the FCF yield, buyback, and the $40 street low.

The asymmetry favors ownership: ~15% base-case upside, 42-72% to median/high targets, versus 8-17% downside to the bear case and street low. The setup is skewed, which is why a HOLD understated the opportunity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $50** (16-17x on ~$3.00-3.16 EPS as the cycle bottoms and the multiple partially normalizes toward peers). **Upside to base: +15%.**

**Bull case target: $60-75** (20-24x on $3.16 next-year EPS with organic reacceleration and multiple re-rating). **Upside to bull: +38% to +72%.**

**Bear case target: $36-40** (13-14x on flat-to-lower EPS, extended construction drought). **Downside to bear: –8% to –17%.**

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to the rate-cut path feeding through to construction starts, IIJA fund flows, and quarterly volume prints. Near-term catalysts include Q2 FY2026 earnings (August), resumption of M&A, and clarification of the July 2, 2026 material agreement.

**Thesis invalidation triggers (exit the BUY if):**
1. Organic volume declines accelerate beyond the Q1 pace (e.g., PVF worse than –5% or total organic worse than –5% YoY on the next two prints), signaling demand deterioration rather than a bottoming cycle.
2. Gross margin rolls over below ~26.5%, indicating the pricing/private-label engine has stalled and the one durable operational positive is breaking.
3. A goodwill impairment charge is taken, or FY2026 guidance ($7.8-7.9B sales, $950-980M Adj. EBITDA) is cut, confirming the M&A-and-margin bridge no longer offsets organic weakness.
4. Estimate revisions turn negative over a 90-day window, reversing the current +15% current-year momentum.

## Conclusion

Core & Main is a structurally advantaged, cash-generative distributor trading at less than half its peer multiple near a 52-week low, priced by the market as if the current construction-cycle trough is permanent. The organic-demand softness is real and caps conviction — this is not a full earnings-inflection story with accelerating revenue, and I size accordingly. But the combination of a sub-15x forward multiple, a 7.4% FCF yield, expanding margins through a volume downturn, rising estimates, uncrowded short interest, cheap call-skewed options, a director buying near the current price, and an analyst target distribution skewed 42-72% to the upside describes an asymmetry that favors ownership.

Prior reports defaulted to HOLD three times on the absence of a dated catalyst. The inflection/value archetype does not require one; it requires rising estimates, a reasonable forward multiple, and normalization signals, all of which are present. The audit's challenge is well-founded. **Recommendation: BUY**, sized modestly, with the exit criteria above as the discipline against the risk that the rate cycle stays hostile longer than the multiple assumes.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FCF yield was overstated (~7.4%, not 13.7%) on the $8.2B market cap, materially weakening the "value" leg of the thesis, and upside-to-mean was overstated (~38%, not "38-42%").
