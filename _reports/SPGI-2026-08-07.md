## Executive Summary

S&P Global completed the spin-off of S&P Global Mobility on 1 July 2026, and the resulting company is materially different from the one whose trailing financials are on file. Every reported income-statement figure through Q2 2026 includes Mobility; from Q3 forward it becomes discontinued operations. On a Mobility-excluded basis derived from Q2 segment disclosures, Ratings and Indices together generate approximately 71% of segment operating profit on roughly half of revenue, at segment margins of 68% and 70% respectively. Market Intelligence — the segment carrying $30.3B of the company's $52.0B goodwill-and-intangibles balance and the one most exposed to AI-driven commoditization — contributes roughly 16% of economic operating profit after netting CME's 27% interest in Indices.

The stock has fallen 29.9% from its August 2025 high, with the bulk of the decline occurring in seven sessions in February 2026 (from $527.66 on 2 February to $390.76 on 11 February). Two concerns drive the derating and both are legitimate. First, the Q2 10-Q attributes investment-grade billed issuance growth of 25% specifically to "AI-related issuance and M&A transactions," meaning the highest-margin revenue line in the company is levered to a financing cycle that is currently being stress-tested. Second, the FY2025 10-K states plainly that off-the-shelf AI models lower barriers to entry, that third parties could combine S&P data with AI tools and "potentially supplant our products and services," and that free or inexpensive information sources may materially reduce demand. Those are the most direct disclosure-level acknowledgments of commoditization risk in the data industry.

What the market appears to be underweighting is the profit-mix arithmetic. The AI-substitution risk lands on the segment producing ~16% of operating profit, while the two franchises producing ~71% are a regulated NRSRO oligopoly and an index business tracked by $6.35 trillion of ETF assets. At 22.8x the FY2026 consensus of $17.83 and 20.1x the FY2027 consensus of $20.22, S&P Global trades at a discount to Moody's on trailing earnings despite owning the same ratings economics plus the higher-margin index franchise. The company repurchased $1.5B in H1 at average prices of $426.70 and $414.76, both above the current quote, and three insiders — the CEO, a director, and the CEO of S&P Dow Jones Indices — bought $2.58M of stock on the open market between 29 April and 1 May at $429.93–$434.03, with no open-market sales in the reviewed window. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 12% over 12-18 months, against a bear case implying 19% downside, supports staged accumulation. It does not clear the bar for a full position while consensus estimates are falling in every window and the company's recast post-spin financial baseline is not yet available for verification.

## Company Overview

Four segments operate post-separation. **Ratings** produced Q2 revenue of $1,339M (+17%) and operating profit of $913M at a 68% margin, split between transaction revenue of $746M (+25%, new-issue ratings) and non-transaction revenue of $593M (+8%, surveillance, frequent-issuer programs, CRISIL). It is extraordinarily capital-light: total Ratings segment assets were $1,417M of $56.5B allocated across segments.

**Indices** (S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC) generated Q2 revenue of $534M (+20%) at a 70% margin, of which $348M was asset-linked fees on ETF and fund AUM, $99M usage-based derivatives royalties, and $87M subscription. Ending ETF AUM was $6.350 trillion. S&P Global owns 73%; CME Group and CME Group Index Services hold 27%, carried as redeemable non-controlling interest of $5,022M. Operating profit net of NCI was $276M.

**Market Intelligence** produced Q2 revenue of $1,290M (+6%) at a 23% GAAP operating margin, absorbing $153M of quarterly acquired-intangible amortization (approximately 35% margin excluding that charge). Revenue is 83% subscription. Effective 1 July the segment was reorganized into Kensho Data & Platforms and Enterprise Solutions, with 451 Research and Maritime & Trade transferred out to Energy and Credit Analytics expenses transferred to Ratings.

**Energy** (formerly Commodity Insights) produced $568M (+2%) at a 41% margin, 91% subscription, housing the Platts price-assessment franchise and CERAWeek.

The 2022-2023 experience is the most useful empirical bound on downside: adjusted diluted EPS grew from $12.36 to $14.06 through the issuance drought. The post-spin mix is more Ratings-weighted than it was then, so sensitivity is higher, but the historical record argues against a collapse scenario.

The expense control is the standout: operating-related expense grew 4% while absorbing a ~$188M year-over-year swing in undesignated FX forwards (a $28M H1 loss versus a $160M H1 2025 gain) running through selling and general expense, which nonetheless grew only 9%.

FY2025 capex was $195M on $15,336M of revenue — 1.3% of revenue. H1 FCF should not be doubled: it carries the annual bonus outflow and CERAWeek receipts (both first-half-weighted) and includes Mobility. As a labeled sensitivity, 2× H1 FCF is ~$4.5B, or a 3.8% yield; a normalized post-spin figure cannot be reproduced from the filings reviewed, as it requires the recast cash flow statement.

**Balance sheet.** At 30 June, total assets were $62,906M against goodwill of $36,348M and other intangibles of $15,695M — 82.7% of assets. Controlling equity of $31,501M implies tangible common equity of approximately negative $20.5B. This is purchase accounting from the IHS Markit merger, not accumulated losses (retained earnings were $23,666M at year-end 2025). Book-value screens and asset-weighted solvency proxies are uninformative here; the cash-flow and coverage components are not.

On those: cash of $4,134M (up from $1,745M); total debt of $15,170M including $1,981M of Mobility Global senior notes that became Mobility's sole responsibility after separation, implying post-spin debt of approximately $13,189M. The portion of consolidated cash retained by S&P Global versus distributed to Mobility is not disclosed in the 10-Q, so post-spin net debt is a range rather than a number. FY2025 leverage was approximately 1.7x gross and 1.5x net EBITDA, with interest coverage of 22.6x ($6,478M / $287M). The sole financial covenant is indebtedness-to-cash-flow not exceeding 4:1, never breached. Debt fair value of $13.1B against $15.17B carrying value reflects below-market legacy coupons, an unrecognized economic asset. The current ratio of 0.95x rises to 1.68x excluding $3,929M of unearned revenue, the economically relevant reading for a subscription business.

A note on data consistency: the supplied profile lists trailing and forward P/E as identical at 24.68x alongside EPS of $14.66, which does not reconcile at $406.20 ($406.20 / $14.66 = 27.7x). All multiples in this report are computed from the canonical price and disclosed or consensus earnings.

**Financial health: 7.2.** Capital-light, 22x coverage, one covenant with wide headroom, below-market debt costs, and 1.3%-of-revenue capex. The offsets are the ~$5.0B redeemable NCI that does not appear in leverage metrics and the fact that post-spin net debt cannot be pinned precisely.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth question has three parts, and the answer to each is different.

**Ratings is at a cyclical high with a thematically concentrated driver.** Q2 total billed issuance was $1,268B (+25%): investment grade $539B (+25%), high yield $169B (+15%), other $560B (+27%). H1 issuance was $2,498B (+19%) with IG +33%. Transaction revenue growth of 25% tracked billed issuance almost exactly, and management attributes the IG component to AI-related issuance and M&A. Ratings' fixed cost base means an issuance reversal would compress operating profit faster than revenue. The offset is real: non-transaction revenue of $593M (+8%) is an annuity, and surveillance fees scale with the *stock* of outstanding rated debt, so the 2026 boom permanently enlarges the recurring base.

**Indices growth is volume-driven with a pricing question attached.** Asset-linked fees grew 22% against average ETF AUM growth of 38% — a 16-point gap, implying roughly 2.3 basis points of annualized fee realization on average ETF AUM. Two benign explanations exist: asset-linked fees include mutual funds, insurance products, and other vehicles whose AUM likely grew more slowly, mechanically depressing the blended rate; and the 38% AUM comparison is flattered by an April 2025 drawdown in the base period. The gap warrants monitoring but does not establish fee compression.

**Market Intelligence and Energy are the drag.** MI grew 6% with operating profit +13% and 200bp of margin expansion (21% to 23%). Energy grew 2%, with the 10-Q citing a lapped one-time prior-year Upstream benefit, lower Platts-linked exchange volumes, biannual event timing, and event cancellations. Neither is deteriorating on the profit line, but neither supports a growth multiple.

**The estimate revisions require careful reading.** Consensus has fallen in all four windows: current quarter to $4.43 (-12.7% over 90 days), next quarter to $4.09 (-11.8%), FY2026 to $17.83 (-9.2%), FY2027 to $20.22 (-8.9%). A substantial portion is mechanical spin recast. Mobility contributed $104M of quarterly operating profit; at a 23.5% tax rate on ~294M shares that is roughly $0.27 per quarter, or $1.05–1.10 annualized. The FY2027 cut of $1.99 is therefore roughly half mechanical. On a like-for-like ex-Mobility basis, Q3 consensus of $4.43 compares to an estimated $4.46 for Q3 2025 and Q4 consensus of $4.09 to roughly $4.03 — essentially flat.

That is the substantive finding, and it is less benign than the mechanical explanation implies. After a quarter of +17% operating profit growth and +28% Ratings profit growth, consensus expects approximately zero year-over-year earnings growth in H2 on a comparable basis. Stranded corporate costs post-spin, tougher issuance comps, and Energy softness explain it. The 30-day revision of -1.0% on FY2027 also indicates the cutting has not stopped after the initial recast. FY2027 consensus of $20.22 implies 13.4% growth off the FY2026 base, which requires either continued issuance strength or MI acceleration.

**Growth prospects: 5.8.** Mid-single-digit organic growth in two of four segments, a high-margin cyclical kicker in the third that is currently peaking, and negative revision momentum that is not entirely mechanical.

## Valuation Assessment

At $406.20, S&P Global trades at 22.8x FY2026 consensus of $17.83 and 20.1x FY2027 consensus of $20.22. The externally reported FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance range of $17.50–$17.75 (midpoint $17.625, sourced secondhand and not verified against the earnings 8-K) corresponds to 23.0x. On FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of $14.65 the stock is 27.7x, but that base includes Mobility.

The GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is clean and verifiable, which matters for judging earnings quality. Q2 adjusted EPS of $4.83 × 295.5M shares = $1,427M against GAAP net income of $1,217M, a $210M gap. Acquired-intangible amortization of $275M × (1 − 0.235) = $210M. The reconciliation is essentially entirely non-cash amortization; other items (the $186M H1 disposition gains against $118M disposition costs, $44M severance, $16M acquisition costs, $9M impairments) approximately offset. ## Competitive Landscape

Three structural advantages are supported by the filings. Ratings is protected by NRSRO registration and issuer-pay economics that scale with issuance volume rather than headcount. Indices benefits from the installed base of index-tracking capital that cannot be casually redirected. The combined data estate enables cross-segment product creation: the S&P U.S. CLO Investment Grade Indices launched 4 August combine MI's CLO analytics, Ratings' credit expertise, and DJI's index construction.

The company is also playing offense at the periphery, where growth is: a pending majority stake in Agusto & Co. (Pan-African ratings, Nigeria/Kenya/Rwanda/Ghana, stated immaterial), Stablecoin Stability Assessments now covering 11 stablecoins, Enertel AI for North American power price forecasting, and datacenterHawk (data center, fiber, and infrastructure intelligence) to combine with 451 Research and Energy's power data. In AI tooling, attribution matters: the 23 July Farsight arrangement is a strategic partnership plus minority investment, with the client-material generation tooling belonging to Farsight and S&P Global providing Capital IQ Pro data and distribution. The company did not build or acquire the product.

The offsetting weakness is concentrated and identifiable. Market Intelligence competes against Bloomberg, LSEG, FactSet, MSCI, Moody's Analytics, and a growing tier of AI-native entrants that the 10-K explicitly acknowledges are enabled by off-the-shelf models. MI has been systematically disassembled and rebuilt over 18 months: EDM and thinkFolio sold, 451 Research and Maritime & Trade transferred out, Credit Analytics expenses transferred to Ratings, With Intelligence and ORBCOMM AIS and TeraHelix absorbed, the largest segment share of two consecutive restructuring charges ($56M of the 2025 plan, $15M of the 2026 plan), reorganization into two business lines, and the appearance of a "Co-Head, Market Intelligence" title alongside a segment President. The 6% growth print, depressed by divestitures, cannot be extrapolated without the recast baseline. The 13% operating profit growth and 200bp of margin expansion are not the profile of a deteriorating business, but neither do they demonstrate that the AI response is working.

Energy's Platts assessments face Argus and ICIS, and the 10-K flags that the European Commission has adopted or proposed price-limiting mechanisms and circuit breakers on exchange-traded gas and LNG import benchmarks — direct intervention risk to Platts' commercial model.

**Competitive position: 6.2.** Trajectory is bifurcated: strengthening in Ratings and Indices through adjacency expansion, contested and unresolved in MI, softening in Energy on both cyclical and regulatory grounds. This is a modest recovery from the prior 6.5-to-9.0 decline in this dimension, reflecting the Q2 evidence that MI's profitability is improving even as its top line is reshaped.

## Risk Assessment

**The two-sided AI exposure is the defining feature of this risk profile.** Near-term earnings are levered *to* the AI capital cycle through Ratings' issuance-driven transaction revenue; the medium-term data franchise is threatened *by* AI capability diffusion. These are two exposures to the same phenomenon with opposite signs and different horizons. If AI capex financing decelerates while AI tooling continues to commoditize data products, both turn negative simultaneously.

**The CME put obligation is a ~$5B contingent claim outside the debt metrics.** Minority partners hold 27% of S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC, carried at $5,022M of redeemable NCI. CME Group and CME Group Index Services may, at any time, sell — and S&P Global is obligated to purchase — at least 20% of their interest; on a change of control they may put the entire interest at fair value within a 15-day window. Redemption-value remeasurements run through retained income ($86M in H1 2026, $147M in H1 2025). Exercise timing is not under the company's control.

**Goodwill impairment risk is concentrated and unquantified.** MI carries $30.3B of segment assets, growing 6% at a 23% GAAP margin, amid restructuring, asset transfers out, and divestitures within. No triggering event or headroom percentage was disclosed. The concern is inferential rather than established, but the combination warrants monitoring.

**Regulatory exposure spans every franchise.** Ratings faces continuing policy pressure to reduce regulatory reliance on ratings, mandate rotation, and constrain remuneration, with some governments funding local competitors; the SEC remains in ongoing communication regarding NRSRO compliance. Indices is supervised by ESMA under EU Benchmark Regulation and the FCA under UK BMR. Platts is supervised by the Dutch AFM. EU ESG ratings regulation applies to Energy's S1 business, with FCA ESG supervision beginning June 2028.

**Comparability and verification gaps materially limit confidence.** The FY2026 guidance range and the >$7B buyback figure come from a single secondary source and were not verified against the earnings 8-K; whether the range is stated on a continuing-operations basis is unknown. The 6 July recast pro forma segment financials were not retrievable, so post-spin organic growth rates, segment margins under the new allocation methodology, and the correct comparison base are unverified. My ex-Mobility Q2 estimates (revenue ~$3,678M, operating profit ~$1,708M) are subtractions from segment disclosures, not company-recast figures, and ignore intersegment reallocation effects the company has said will change. The reported Q2 "EPS miss" of 3.7% is itself unreliable: a Zacks-sourced pre-print consensus of $4.49 on $3.65B revenue is within 1% of my derived ex-Mobility revenue, confirming that parallel with- and without-Mobility consensus sets existed and that neither comparison to the $4.83 actual is valid.

**Other items:** an Australian CDO class action filed August 2020 with no reserve or estimable range disclosed; single-cloud concentration on AWS as a customer; data suppliers who are also competitors with short-notice cancellable licenses; and the separation's tax-free qualification listed among forward-looking risks. A sustainability-linked bond coupon stepped up 25bp effective 1 March 2026 on a missed KPI — financially immaterial, but an externally adjudicated data point for a company whose ESG-assessment franchise sells credibility.

**Risk factors: 5.3.** The specific, unpriced items — the CME put's timing optionality, MI goodwill headroom, and an unverified forward earnings base — are material enough to hold this dimension below the prior reading despite the lower entry price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $555 (+36.6%)

Post-spin, roughly 71% of economic operating profit comes from two franchises that AI cannot readily displace: an NRSRO oligopoly whose pricing scales with issuance volume, and an index business tracked by $6.35 trillion of passive capital. Ratings' 2026 issuance surge builds a permanently larger surveillance annuity — non-transaction revenue already grows 8% and rises with the stock of rated debt. MI's operating profit grew 13% with 200bp of margin expansion while being restructured, and the pivot toward proprietary private-markets content (With Intelligence, The Deal integrated into Capital IQ Pro on 29 July) plus AI-native distribution addresses the commoditization threat in the segment where it is real. Capital returns are large and price-sensitive: >$7B of 2026 buybacks equals ~5.9% of shares, and the company bought at $414.76 in May. At 20.1x FY2027 consensus versus 31.7x for Moody's, a re-rating toward the 26-27x peer median on $20.22 delivers $525-555 without requiring estimate upgrades. Sell-side targets already sit at $517 mean and $523 median.

### Bear Case — $330 (-18.8%)

Ratings' 25% billed-issuance growth was driven by AI-related issuance and M&A. If that issuance decelerates in 2027, transaction revenue of roughly $3.0B annualized falls against a 68%-margin fixed cost base: a 25% decline removes approximately $640M of operating profit, or about $1.69 per share, taking FY2027 toward $18.50. Simultaneously, off-the-shelf AI models continue to commoditize MI's data products — the 10-K's own language warns that third parties could "supplant our products and services" and that free information sources may "materially reduce demand" — with $30.3B of goodwill attached to a 6%-growth, 23%-margin segment. Compress the multiple to 18x on $18.00 and the stock is $324. CME exercises at least part of its put, absorbing $1-5B of cash that would otherwise fund buybacks. The 52-week low of $381.61 is only 6% below spot, so this scenario does not require new lows to begin.

### Base Case — $470 (+15.7%)

Ratings issuance moderates rather than collapses, with the non-transaction annuity and adjacency expansion (CLO indices, stablecoin assessments, Agusto) offsetting part of the transaction decline. MI holds mid-single-digit organic growth with continued margin expansion. Energy stabilizes as data-center power demand offsets Platts-linked volume softness. FY2027 lands near consensus at $20.22, and the multiple recovers to 23.2x — still a discount to the 26.1x peer median and a substantial discount to Moody's — supported by ~5.9% annual share shrinkage.

Probability-weighted at 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear: **+12.3%**, plus roughly 1% dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected return with a real discount to the closest comparable, but confidence is constrained by falling estimates in every window and the absence of verified recast financials. This is a staged-entry situation, not a full-position initiation.

- **Base case target: $470** — 23.2x FY2027 consensus of $20.22, an 11% discount to the 26.1x peer median and a 27% discount to Moody's 31.7x, reflecting a heavier Ratings weighting post-spin. Upside +15.7% from $406.20.

1. October Q3 print showing recast ex-Mobility organic revenue growth of 7% or better with Ratings transaction revenue flat or higher year-over-year.

## Conclusion

S&P Global's score trajectory has declined from 7.6 in May to 6.5 in July, and this review holds it approximately flat at 6.3 with the composition shifted: valuation improves on a 10% lower price and a widened discount to Moody's, while growth and risk deteriorate on evidence that H2 consensus implies roughly zero like-for-like earnings growth and that the Ratings engine driving 2026 is explicitly tied to AI-related debt issuance. The 29.9% drawdown from the August 2025 high is a considered rerating of a compounder into a cyclical franchise with an identified disruption vector, and much of that reasoning is sound.

What the price does not appear to reflect is the profit-mix arithmetic. The AI-substitution threat that justifies a lower multiple lands on the segment contributing roughly 16% of economic operating profit, while roughly 71% comes from a regulated ratings oligopoly and an index franchise tracked by $6.35 trillion. Capital intensity is 1.3% of revenue, interest coverage is 22.6x, the debt stack carries below-market coupons, and combined shareholder yield approaches 7%.
