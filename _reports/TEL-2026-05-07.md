## Executive Summary

TE Connectivity is the world's largest publicly traded connector company, positioned at the intersection of AI infrastructure buildout, grid modernization, and transportation electrification. The company reported record Q2 FY2026 results on April 22: $4.74B revenue (+15% YoY), $2.73 adjusted EPS (+24% YoY), and record orders of $5.3B (book-to-bill 1.12). The stock has declined approximately 14% from its pre-earnings peak of $252.56, creating a compelling entry. At approximately $216 (recent close), TEL trades at roughly 19.2x FY2026E EPS of $11.24, a discount to Amphenol and to its own historical average during periods of accelerating growth. This discount is partially justified by TEL's automotive exposure (~41% of revenue with -4% organic decline), but the market is underpricing the structural mix shift toward Industrial Solutions, which now generates 21.8% adjusted operating margins and is growing 46% organically in its Digital Data Networks (DDN) business.

The fundamental thesis centers on three drivers: (1) DDN/AI-related revenue trajectory from $1.16B in FY2023 to management's expectation of $2.3B–$2.4B in AI revenue for FY2026, with management raising the AI outlook by $150M; (2) expanding margins as Industrial Solutions approaches 50% of revenue; and (3) disciplined capital return ($2.16B in FY2025 via dividends and buybacks, with a fresh 10% dividend increase). The primary risk is automotive cyclicality in Western markets and the binary outcome around AI capex sustainability, though the 1.12 book-to-bill supports near-term demand visibility.

## Company Overview

TE Connectivity designs and manufactures connectivity and sensor solutions across two segments: Transportation Solutions (54% of FY2025 revenue, dominated by automotive) and Industrial Solutions (46%, encompassing AI/data centers, energy, aerospace/defense, and factory automation). The company operates in ~130 countries with 93,000 employees and addresses a ~$200B total addressable market with approximately 9% share. Revenue is diversified by customer and by currency (57% invoiced in non-USD, primarily EUR and RMB).

The company's reorganization from three segments to two in FY2025 clarified its strategic positioning: Transportation captures the electrification content opportunity (EV content per vehicle is 2-3x ICE), while Industrial Solutions captures digital infrastructure spending. The critical dynamic is the mix shift: Industrial grew from 40% of revenue in FY2023/24 to 46% in FY2025 and is trending toward 50%, carrying higher margins and faster growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** After two years of modest declines (FY2023 $16.03B, FY2024 $15.85B), TE returned to growth in FY2025 at $17.26B (+8.9% reported, +6.4% organic). Q2 FY2026 revenue of $4.74B (+15% reported) puts the company on pace for ~$19B+ in FY2026.

**Margin expansion:** Gross margins improved from 31.5% (FY2023) to 35.2% (FY2025). Operating margins expanded from 14.4% to 18.6% over the same period. Q2 FY2026 adjusted operating margin hit 21.7%, reflecting both Industrial mix shift and operational leverage. Industrial Solutions segment adjusted operating margins improved from 12.8% (FY2023) to 21.8% (Q2 FY2026), a nearly 900bps improvement driven by DDN revenue scaling.

**Cash generation:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.14B (+19% YoY) on $17.26B revenue (24% conversion rate). Free cash flow was $3.20B (18.6% FCF margin), best-in-class for industrial technology companies. FCF comfortably covers the $2.16B in total capital returns.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt rose to $5.69B from $4.20B, driven by the Richards Manufacturing acquisition ($2.3B). Current ratio is 1.56x. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.4x. The $3.0B revolving credit facility (entered February 2026, maturing February 2031) is undrawn, with compliance well within the 3.75x leverage covenant.

**Altman Z-Score of 2.56** places TEL in the "grey zone," but this is mechanical — driven by acquisition-related debt deliberately taken on to fund a strategic purchase with immediate revenue contribution. FCF coverage of interest expense is >8x.

## Growth Analysis

TE's growth profile splits into two distinct trajectories:

**AI/Data Center (DDN):** DDN revenue trajectory of $1.16B (FY2023) → $1.27B (FY2024) → $2.21B (FY2025). Management has guided AI revenue specifically to $2.3B–$2.4B for FY2026, with the outlook raised by $150M during Q2. Q2 FY2026 DDN sales were $714M (+46% organic) with orders up 60%+ and record backlog. TE supplies high-speed interconnects, power connectors, and thermal solutions that are mission-critical for GPU clusters. Sustained hyperscaler spending confirms the demand environment at the component level.

**Energy:** +60% reported growth in Q2 FY2026 ($445M in sales), though organic growth was approximately 11% with the balance driven by acquisition contributions (primarily Richards Manufacturing). Grid modernization spending (renewables integration, grid resilience) is a multi-decade theme. The Richards acquisition ($2.3B) positions TE for electrical distribution market opportunity.

**Automotive:** -4% organic in Q2 FY2026. Per TE's 10-Q, organic automotive sales declined 7.9% in the Americas and 6.1% in Asia-Pacific, partially offset by 1.5% growth in EMEA. Long-term, EV content per vehicle at 2-3x ICE provides structural growth even with flat unit production, and China's EV market remains a positive content story despite near-term regional weakness.

**Factory Automation:** Growing 3.5% in FY2025 after a -19% destocking. Early-stage recovery with further upside as industrial activity normalizes.

Estimate revisions are positive: FY2026 EPS consensus moved from $11.05 (90 days ago) to $11.24 (+1.7%), with FY2027 at $12.62 (+2.3%). These are modest but directionally positive. The two-quarter beat record at +0.2% and +5.6% suggests consensus is catchable.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $216, TEL trades at:
- ~19.2x FY2026E EPS ($11.24)
- ~17.1x FY2027E EPS ($12.62)
- ~15x EV/EBITDA (estimated on ~$4.5B EBITDA)
- 1.5% dividend yield (recently raised 10%)
- ~5% FCF yield on market cap

Compared to Amphenol (APH), TEL trades at a meaningful discount despite growing DDN/AI revenue at a similar or faster clip and having comparable margin trajectory in Industrial Solutions. Relative to broader electronic component and connector peers (JBL forward P/E ~23x, FLEX ~19x, SANM ~15x), TEL's multiple is in a similar range but with superior margin expansion potential and a more diversified secular growth portfolio. The discount to APH reflects TEL's automotive drag but appears excessive given the accelerating Industrial mix shift.

The analyst consensus target of approximately $264 (mean) implies roughly 22% upside from recent levels. The current price is near the low end of the analyst target range. With 13 of 19 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and positive estimate revisions, the risk/reward is favorable.

## Competitive Landscape

TE holds a wide moat based on three factors:

1. **Switching costs:** Custom and semi-custom connector designs are embedded into customer product architectures during the design phase. Once qualified, switching requires re-qualification, testing, and production line changes, creating high retention.

2. **Portfolio breadth:** Few competitors match TE's simultaneous presence in automotive, AI/data center, energy, aerospace, and industrial automation with integrated solutions. Amphenol comes closest with broad exposure across communications, aerospace/defense, industrial, and automotive markets, but TE's automotive scale and energy infrastructure position provide differentiation.

3. **Scale and manufacturing proximity:** 93,000 employees across global facilities allow production proximity to customers, reducing lead times. 19 China manufacturing sites serve the world's largest EV market directly.

The competitive position is improving: Industrial Solutions' margin expansion from 12.8% to 21.8% reflects pricing power and operating leverage in AI interconnects, where TE is designing in early with hyperscalers. The segment reorganization simplified go-to-market, and the Richards acquisition expanded energy infrastructure addressable market.

Risk to competitive position: Co-packaged optics (CPO) technology could reduce demand for traditional high-speed copper interconnects in future data center architectures. This is a 3-5 year risk, not immediate — current GPU cluster architectures still rely heavily on traditional connectors, and TE is investing in next-gen solutions.

## Risk Assessment

**Automotive cyclicality (HIGH):** 41% revenue exposure to automotive. Western and Asia-Pacific production declining organically. If global auto production enters recession (-5% to -10%), TEL's Transportation segment would face ~$400-600M revenue headwind. Partially mitigated by EV content growth over time.

**AI capex cycle dependency (MEDIUM-HIGH):** DDN revenue heading toward $2.3B–$2.4B creates meaningful concentration. If hyperscaler capex cycles moderate (Google, Microsoft, Meta pull back), TE's fastest-growing segment decelerates. The 1.12 book-to-bill and 60%+ order growth provide near-term buffer, but this is an inherently cyclical investment category.

**Tariff and trade exposure (MEDIUM-HIGH):** 19 manufacturing sites in China; ~25% of sales to Chinese customers. U.S.-China tariff escalation creates both demand risk (Chinese OEMs may re-source) and cost risk (tariffs on imports to the U.S.). Management states mitigation through pricing and sourcing changes is largely effective, but a full trade war would be material.

**Integration and leverage (MEDIUM):** $5.69B total debt post-Richards acquisition. $852M due in FY2026. Cash flow coverage is comfortable (>8x interest), and the $3.0B revolver is undrawn, but leverage has doubled from 2 years ago. If automotive and AI simultaneously weaken, debt service becomes a tighter constraint.

**Tax uncertainty (LOW-MEDIUM):** OECD global minimum tax effective FY2025. FY2025 effective rate was 42.5% (inflated by valuation allowance adjustments). Normalized rate likely 15-18% going forward, but legislative changes could increase this.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-280, +20-30%):**
- DDN/AI revenue exceeds management's $2.3B–$2.4B AI guidance as hyperscaler capex accelerates further
- Industrial Solutions reaches 50% of revenue by FY2027, dragging portfolio operating margin above 22%
- Automotive stabilizes on EV content growth and Western production bottoming
- Multiple re-rates from ~19x toward 22-24x as the market recognizes the structural mix shift
- FY2027 EPS of $13-14 on accelerating AI + auto recovery × 22x = $286-308

**Bear Case ($165-180, -17-24%):**
- AI capex cycle moderates as hyperscalers face earnings pressure; DDN growth decelerates to 15-20%
- Automotive enters deeper recession (-8-10% global production); Transportation margin contracts
- Tariff escalation creates margin headwinds in China operations
- FY2026 EPS misses consensus ($10.50 vs. $11.24) → multiple compresses to 16x = $168

**Base Case ($240-260, +11-20%):**
- FY2026 EPS of $11.24 achieves consensus, FY2027 of $12.62
- Market re-rates gradually as Industrial mix shift becomes evident in reported results
- Multiple moves from ~19x to 20-21x on improved growth perception
- 20.5x × $12.62 FY2027E = $259

## Conclusion

TE Connectivity offers structural growth exposure (AI, energy, EV content) with industrial cash flow characteristics (18.6% FCF margin, 10% dividend growth) at a reasonable valuation (~19x current-year EPS, ~17x forward). The decline from $252.56, driven by market concern about automotive weakness, has created an entry near the low end of analyst target ranges. The primary risks (auto cyclicality, AI capex sustainability, tariffs) are real but partially mitigated by diversification, FCF coverage, and management's demonstrated ability to navigate through cycles. Position at 2-3% of portfolio.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation discount argument is narrower than originally presented: TEL's forward P/E (~19x) is comparable to several electronic component peers rather than trading at a deep discount to a 44.8x peer median. Additionally, AI revenue as specifically defined by management ($2.3B–$2.4B FY2026E) is materially below the broader DDN revenue figures used in the original bull case, which reduces the magnitude of the AI growth story though does not negate the directional thesis.
