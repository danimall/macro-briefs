## Executive Summary

EMCOR Group is executing at the highest level in its history, with Q1 2026 delivering $4.63B in revenue (+19.7% YoY), EPS of $6.84 (+30% YoY), and remaining performance obligations (RPO) reaching $15.62B — a 33% year-over-year increase that provides strong near-term revenue visibility. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $18.5B–$19.25B in revenue and $28.25–$29.75 in EPS. The company is the clear beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout, with network & communications revenue growing approximately 47% (electrical) and 86% (mechanical) in Q1.

The stock has appreciated 114% over the past year, trading at ~$944 and ~32.6x forward earnings on midpoint guidance. While this is a premium for a construction services company in absolute terms, it represents a discount to direct peers Comfort Systems (~42.5x forward) and Quanta Services (~41x forward). The growth trajectory, conservatively capitalized balance sheet, accelerating backlog, and structural positioning in data center electrical/cooling infrastructure justify a BUY recommendation, though the valuation score reflects limited margin of safety should the data center spending cycle decelerate.

EMCOR's financial conservatism (no direct borrowings outstanding, $916M cash, $1.23B available revolver capacity) provides substantial downside protection, while the $15.6B backlog with 78% expected to convert within 12 months ensures strong revenue visibility into early 2027.

## Company Overview

EMCOR Group operates approximately 100 specialty construction and facilities services subsidiaries across the United States, employing ~44,000 people. The company exited its UK operations in December 2025, becoming a pure domestic play. Four segments comprise the business:

- **U.S. Electrical Construction & Facilities Services** (30% of revenue, $5.07B): Power systems, lighting, communications infrastructure, data center electrical. FY2025 operating margin: 12.1%.
- **U.S. Mechanical Construction & Facilities Services** (42%, $7.05B): HVAC, plumbing, fire protection, clean-room systems, cooling infrastructure. FY2025 operating margin: 12.8%.
- **U.S. Building Services** (18%, $3.12B): Facilities maintenance, HVAC retrofits, building automation. FY2025 operating margin: 6.0%.
- **U.S. Industrial Services** (7%, $1.27B): Refinery turnarounds, petrochemical maintenance. FY2025 operating margin: 2.0%.

The company's competitive moat centers on scale-derived bonding capacity (essential for large projects), a decentralized operating model preserving local relationships, and technical capabilities in virtual design/construction and prefabrication that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

## Growth Analysis

Three structural growth vectors are driving EMCOR's acceleration:

**1. Data center construction super-cycle.** Network & communications revenue grew approximately 47% in electrical and 86% in mechanical in Q1 2026. EMCOR provides both the electrical power distribution and mechanical cooling systems — the two highest-complexity, highest-value subsystems in data centers. The emergence of liquid cooling for AI workloads adds a technically demanding service line where EMCOR's engineering capabilities create differentiation. Major U.S. technology firms have reportedly committed up to $650–725B in combined 2026 capex primarily for AI data-center infrastructure, suggesting this tailwind has multi-year duration.

**2. Geographic and capability expansion through M&A.** The Miller Electric acquisition ($877M, Feb 2025) delivered approximately $1.09B in incremental revenue in its first year (exceeding the expected $805M). This expands EMCOR's presence in Southeast and Texas markets — the highest-growth regions for data center and industrial construction. Management's acquisition pipeline remains "good," focused on electrical, fire protection, and building controls.

**3. Reshoring and industrial construction.** While semiconductor fab construction from CHIPS Act is transitioning past peak, food processing, renewable energy, and traditional manufacturing projects are replacing it. EMCOR's diversified end-market exposure prevents single-sector dependence.

The 2026 EPS guidance range of $28.25–$29.75 represents 0–6% growth over 2025's GAAP EPS of $28.19 (which included the UK sale gain). Adjusting for the gain, 2025 non-GAAP EPS was $25.87, implying underlying growth of approximately 9–15% — consistent with the revenue growth trajectory and secular demand tailwinds. Analyst consensus for FY2027 at $32.43 (revisions up 7.8% over 90 days) implies continued 10-11% earnings growth.

- 29.1x on FY2027 consensus ($32.43)
- ~2.8% FCF yield (on trailing $1.19B FCF and ~$42B market cap)

EMCOR trades at an approximately 23% discount to Comfort Systems (the most comparable peer in specialty mechanical/electrical construction) and a roughly 21% discount to Quanta Services on forward P/E. Given EMCOR's superior financial health (no direct borrowings vs. peers with leverage), comparable growth rates, and 33% backlog growth, this discount appears difficult to justify.

However, the absolute multiple of 32x is elevated for a construction services company by historical standards. EMCOR itself traded at 12-15x forward earnings as recently as 2022-2023. The re-rating from "construction contractor" to "AI infrastructure play" is partially complete but defensible given the structural demand shift. The risk is that a multiple contraction from 32x to 25x (still above historical but below current) on $29 EPS would imply $725 — a 23% drawdown from current levels.

The valuation reflects the company's quality and growth trajectory accurately, with upside dependent on continued execution and potential multiple expansion toward peers.

## Competitive Landscape

**Revenue growth outpacing industry:** Revenue growing 17-20% (including approximately 17% organically in Q1 2026) significantly exceeds industry estimates of specialty construction market growth, suggesting EMCOR is gaining share — though precise market-share quantification is difficult in this fragmented industry.

**Technical differentiation:** Data center construction requires integrated electrical and mechanical capabilities that few contractors can provide at scale. EMCOR's combination of power distribution expertise and advanced cooling systems (including liquid cooling for AI data centers) positions it as a preferred partner for hyperscalers.

**Financial barrier to entry:** Surety bonding capacity for large projects requires strong balance sheets. EMCOR's bonding capacity and financial strength represent meaningful competitive advantages in pursuing the largest projects. The company's 10-K identifies bonding ability as a key competitive factor.

**Decentralized model preserves relationships:** The ~100 subsidiary structure maintains local market knowledge and customer relationships while leveraging corporate-scale financial resources, technology platforms, and safety programs.

The primary competitive risk is that other large contractors (Quanta, MasTec) move more aggressively into data center construction. However, EMCOR's existing customer relationships, integrated capabilities, and proven execution provide strong incumbent advantages.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Data center concentration (HIGH).** Network & communications is now the dominant growth driver. Any deceleration in hyperscaler capex would directly impact EMCOR's growth rate. The company's 10-K explicitly flags this risk. Mitigant: hyperscaler capex commitments provide multi-year visibility, and EMCOR's diversified end markets (healthcare, education, water/wastewater) provide floor revenue.

**2. Margin normalization (MODERATE-HIGH).** Q1 2026 consolidated operating margin of 8.7% vs. 10.1% for FY2025 suggests peak margins may be behind. Q1 2026 construction segment margins ranged from 10.9% (mechanical) to 12.1% (electrical), below FY2025 levels of 12.8% and 12.1% respectively. Negative project revisions trending upward ($29M → $66M → $86M over 2023-2025) signal potential execution challenges as the company grows rapidly in new geographies. Management explicitly guides investors away from anchoring on peak margins.

**3. Tariff and material cost risk (MODERATE).** Steel prices rose 12% in 2025 on tariffs. Fixed-price contracts expose EMCOR to cost overruns if material prices spike during project execution. Mitigant: shift toward GMP/cost-plus structures and short project durations limit exposure.

**4. Labor constraint (MODERATE).** CEO identified "field leadership" as the primary capacity constraint. 62% unionized workforce with $726M in multiemployer pension contributions (up 44% in two years) creates cost escalation pressure. Skilled labor scarcity in hot markets could constrain growth or compress margins.

**5. Acquisition integration risk (MODERATE).** Miller Electric ($877M) is the largest acquisition in history. Currently generating 80bps margin drag from intangible amortization. Successful integration is assumed in forward estimates.

## Conclusion

EMCOR Group warrants a BUY recommendation based on: (1) accelerating structural demand validated by 33% backlog growth, (2) a valuation discount to closest peer FIX of approximately 23% on forward P/E despite comparable fundamentals, (3) a conservatively capitalized balance sheet providing both offensive (M&A) and defensive (downturn protection) optionality, and (4) management with demonstrated execution capability (247% EPS growth over 3 years, approximately 581% cumulative TSR over 5 years vs. approximately 90% for the peer group).

The primary constraint on conviction is the absolute valuation level (32x forward) and concentration risk in data center spending. The 5.5 valuation score reflects this tension: the stock trades at a moderate discount to peers on forward P/E but is expensive relative to its own history and the construction sector broadly. If the data center cycle extends (which the backlog growth strongly suggests), the stock re-rates toward peers at $1,050-1,100. If the cycle pauses, the floor at $700-750 represents meaningful downside but remains above the 52-week low given underlying business quality.

Position sizing should reflect the elevated absolute valuation: 2-3% portfolio weight is appropriate, with additions on any pullback toward $850-870 where the risk/reward improves materially.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The peer valuation discount is materially smaller than originally presented (~23% vs. FIX on forward P/E, not 35–65%), which narrows the relative-cheapness argument supporting the BUY at current levels.
