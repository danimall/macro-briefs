## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is the largest independent U.S. refiner by capacity (~3.0 million bpd), currently operating in a geopolitical environment that is mechanically widening crack spreads. The stock has rallied 79% from its May 2025 low of ~$137 to $253 as Hormuz disruption, elevated Brent pricing (recently trading in the $114–$120 range, with some analysts discussing $125+ scenarios), and tight U.S. refining supply have driven margin expansion. Q4 2025 EPS of $4.07 beat consensus by approximately 49%, and 2026 full-year estimates have risen significantly in recent months (consensus approximately $21.49 per an April 15 source), reflecting expectations for sustained elevated cracks through the year.

The investment thesis centers on three pillars: (1) structural supply tightness in U.S. refining (no major new capacity, closures continuing) amplified by geopolitical disruption that MPC is positioned to capture via export capability and sour crude processing; (2) embedded MPLX value (~$34.6B in MPC's stake, generating $6.75B fee-based EBITDA) providing cash flow stability through commodity cycles; and (3) an aggressive capital return program that has reduced shares outstanding significantly over three years. At approximately 11.8x 2026E EPS, MPC trades at a discount to downstream refining peers, even after the recent rally.

The primary risk is crack spread normalization — every $1/bbl decline in margins reduces EBITDA by approximately $1.1B. A diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict would compress margins rapidly. California regulatory overhang, management turnover, and the cyclical nature of refining earnings temper conviction. Position sizing should reflect this cyclicality.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates 13 refineries across three U.S. regions (Gulf Coast: 1,248 mbpcd; Mid-Continent: 1,186 mbpcd; West Coast: 552 mbpcd), processing approximately 2,986 thousand barrels per calendar day (often rounded to ~3.0 million bpcd) of crude oil into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and other refined products. The company distributes fuel through ~7,882 Marathon-branded and ~1,162 ARCO direct dealer outlets across 40 states.

MPC's corporate structure includes three segments: Refining & Marketing (core operations), Midstream (via ~64% ownership of MPLX LP), and Renewable Diesel (Dickinson ND facility plus 50% stake in Martinez Renewables JV). MPLX provides natural gas gathering, processing, NGL fractionation, crude/product transportation, and terminal services primarily in the Permian, Marcellus, and Utica basins.

The company was spun off from Marathon Oil in 2011, acquired Andeavor in 2018, and sold Speedway convenience stores in 2021 for $21.4B. Proceeds from the Speedway sale have funded the industry's largest buyback program ($55.67B cumulative since 2012).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** FY2025 revenue of $132.7B was down from $138.9B (2024) and $177.5B (2022) due to lower crude prices (WTI averaged $64.73 in 2025 vs. $75.76 in 2024). However, operating income improved to $8.3B from $6.8B, and EPS grew 31% to $13.22 as throughput volumes increased 67 mbpd and crack spreads widened modestly. R&M margin per barrel was $16.87 in 2025 vs. $16.01 in 2024.

**Cash Flow Generation:** Operating cash flow was $8.25B in FY2025, with $3.49B in capital expenditures yielding $4.77B in free cash flow. FCF yield at current market cap is approximately 6.4%. MPLX distributed $2.56B to MPC in 2025, providing a stable cash flow stream independent of crack spreads.

**Balance Sheet Structure:** Consolidated debt of $33.3B appears elevated, but $26.0B resides at MPLX (non-recourse to MPC and its subsidiaries other than MPLX and MPLX GP LLC). Non-MPLX debt is approximately $7.3B before MPC-only cash of ~$1.5B. R&M segment adjusted EBITDA of $6.14B does not represent standalone MPC EBITDA, as it excludes corporate costs, renewable diesel losses, and MPLX distributions received at the parent level. MPC-level liquidity is $6.5B ($5.0B undrawn revolver plus $1.5B cash). Credit ratings are BBB/Baa2 (stable) at both entities.

**Capital Return:** The share count has declined from approximately 407M weighted average basic shares (2023) to 305M (2025) — a 25% reduction over two years. Management has stated cumulative repurchases since 2012 total $55.67B. In 2025, MPC repurchased 21M shares at an average price of $163.64 ($3.399B cash paid for shares). The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.00/share in January 2026; total declared dividends were $3.73/share in 2025 vs. $3.385/share in 2024. Only $4.38B remains under the current authorization, though reauthorization is likely.

**Earnings Sensitivity:** The critical variable is the crack spread. Approximately $1/bbl change equates to roughly $1.1B in annual EBITDA impact (based on ~2.99 million bpd throughput annualized). Current 2026 consensus EPS (approximately $21.49 per April 15 sources) implies blended cracks near $20-22/bbl — achievable in the current geopolitical environment but above the 2024-2025 average of $14-17/bbl.

## Growth Analysis

MPC's growth trajectory is unusual for a refiner in that volume growth is limited (capacity is essentially fixed), but EPS growth is being driven by three mechanisms:

1. **Crack Spread Expansion:** Current estimates embed $20+/bbl margins for 2026, up from $14.89 in 2025. The Iran-Hormuz disruption has widened diesel cracks materially. Recent upward EPS revisions for FY2026 reflect this dynamic.

2. **Share Reduction:** Continued buybacks at even reduced pace ($3-4B/year) remove another 5-7% of shares annually at current prices, amplifying per-share metrics.

3. **MPLX Organic Growth:** The 2025 acquisition spree (Northwind $2.4B, BANGL $703M + up to $275M earnout, Whiptail $235M) expanded Permian-to-Gulf Coast infrastructure. MPLX EBITDA grew 3.1% organically in 2025 and should accelerate as acquired assets ramp.

Estimate revisions have been strongly positive for FY2026, though individual quarterly estimates remain volatile. MPC reported Q1 2026 results on May 5, 2026. The immediately following quarters are expected to benefit from a strong driving season combined with elevated crack spreads.

## Valuation Assessment

At $253, MPC trades at approximately 11.8x 2026E EPS (~$21.49 per April 15 consensus). Among downstream refining peers, valuations vary widely: VLO and PSX represent the closest comparables, though multiples are highly sensitive to whether current-year earnings are trough, mid-cycle, or peak-cycle. MPC's forward multiple appears moderate relative to refining peers on current estimates.

The key question is whether $21-23/share EPS is sustainable or represents a cyclical peak. Arguments for sustainability:

- U.S. refining capacity additions near zero through decade-end (EIA data confirms operable capacity essentially flat year-over-year, with no major new greenfield projects)
- Geopolitical disruption extending into 2026
- MPLX provides $6.75B in stable EBITDA regardless of crack direction
- Export capability allows MPC to capture global margin arbitrage

Arguments against sustainability:
- A diplomatic resolution would compress cracks rapidly toward $14-15/bbl
- 2023 margins ($23/bbl) normalized to $16-17 within 12 months
- At $14/bbl normalized cracks, EPS would be closer to $12-14

**MPLX Embedded Value:** MPC's approximately 647M MPLX units had a market value of $34.55B based on the December 31, 2025 closing unit price of $53.37. Against MPC's ~$74.5B market cap, this implies the market values the refining business at approximately $40B, or roughly 6.5x normalized refining EBITDA. This is not cheap but not demanding for the largest U.S. refiner with export capability. However, MPLX is consolidated by MPC, and monetizing the stake would involve significant tax, control, liquidity, and structural considerations — it is a substantial embedded asset and cash-flow source, but not a clean "valuation floor."

**FCF Yield:** 6.4% at current price, declining if cracks normalize but supported by MPLX distributions ($2.56B annually).

## Competitive Landscape

MPC holds structural advantages over both domestic and international peers:

**Scale and Integration:** Largest U.S. independent refiner. Inter-refinery logistics via MPLX pipelines reduce transportation costs and enable crude optimization across the system.

**Crude Slate Flexibility:** ~50% sour crude processing capability allows MPC to capture heavy/sour discounts (currently $3-5/bbl for Maya/WCS vs. WTI). This is particularly valuable when light-heavy differentials widen during supply disruptions.

**Export Infrastructure:** 401 mbpd export capability (11% of throughput) enables margin capture when U.S. cracks are below international levels. With Brent elevated and U.S. cracks widening, export economics are highly favorable.

**Midstream Vertical Integration:** MPLX provides integrated logistics under long-term, fee-based commercial agreements (MPC paid $4.03B in fees to MPLX in 2025), while MPLX generates external fee revenue as well. This structure insulates a meaningful portion of consolidated earnings from refining cyclicality.

MPC is gaining relative positioning as smaller, less complex refiners (PBF, DINO) face margin compression and potential rationalization. The acquisition of Andeavor's complex refining assets in 2018 proved well-timed, adding West Coast capacity that benefits from California's structural supply deficit.

## Risk Assessment

**Crack Spread Mean-Reversion (HIGH):** The single largest risk. Current consensus embeds $20+/bbl margins vs. a 5-year average closer to $16-17/bbl. A $5/bbl compression would reduce annual EPS by approximately $5-6. Probability of sustained $20+ cracks depends heavily on geopolitical duration.

**Geopolitical De-escalation:** A diplomatic breakthrough on Iran would rapidly compress oil prices and crack spreads toward $12-14/bbl. This would take EPS to ~$10-12 and the stock to $150-170. The probability of such a resolution is inherently difficult to quantify, but it represents the primary binary downside risk.

**California Regulatory (MODERATE):** SB X1-2 authorizes margin caps, though the CEC voted not to pursue for at least 5 years. The Los Angeles refinery (365 mbpcd, 12% of capacity) faces ongoing regulatory uncertainty. California labor negotiations are also unresolved (24-hour notice contracts).

**Management Transition (MODERATE):** CEO, CFO, and EVP Refining all changed within 18 months. The new team lacks a track record at MPC specifically, though credentials are strong (Mannen was prior CFO; Khoury from Danaher/GE). Strategy continuity appears intact given the capital return program has been maintained.

**Buyback Authorization Depletion:** Only $4.38B remains, covering roughly one more year at the current pace. Reauthorization is likely but introduces governance event risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($290-330, +15% to +30%)

Sustained Hormuz disruption through H2 2026 keeps crack spreads at $22-25/bbl. Full-year 2026 EPS reaches $25-28. Multiple re-rates to 12-13x as market recognizes structural refining tightness. MPLX organic growth adds incremental value. Buyback reauthorization at Q2 earnings. Stock approaches prior cycle peak multiples.

### Bear Case ($170-190, -25% to -33%)

Diplomatic resolution compresses oil to $85-95 and crack spreads to $12-14/bbl. 2026 EPS reverts to $12-14. Market applies 12-13x trough multiple. California regulatory action accelerates. New management team underperforms capital allocation. Stock returns to late-2025 levels.

### Base Case ($265-280, +5% to +11%)

Cracks average $18-20/bbl for FY2026 as Hormuz disruption persists but partially normalizes. EPS of $20-22. Market applies 12-13x multiple given cyclical uncertainty. MPLX stable performance. Continued buybacks at reduced pace ($2-3B). Dividend maintained/increased.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $275 — approximately 12.5x ~$22 FY2026E EPS (supported by MPLX embedded value providing downside support).

**Bull case target:** $310 — 12x $26 EPS on sustained elevated cracks, or multiple re-rating to 14x on $22 EPS.

**Bear case target:** $180 — 13x $14 EPS on diplomatic resolution + crack normalization. This represents a -29% decline from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 6-9 months. The thesis resolves through: (1) Q1/Q2 2026 earnings demonstrating elevated cracks flowing through to results; (2) geopolitical trajectory clarifying durability of Hormuz disruption; (3) summer driving season demand data.

**Upside/downside from current ($253):** Base case +9%, Bull case +23%, Bear case -29%. Risk/reward ratio at base case is modest but the bull case provides 2.4:1 reward:risk vs. base-to-bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Blended crack spread falls below $14/bbl for 30+ consecutive days (indicates margin normalization regardless of geopolitical environment)
2. Diplomatic resolution announced with verified Hormuz reopening and shipping insurance normalization
3. Management signals buyback pause or cuts dividend — would indicate internal cash flow deterioration not visible in public data
4. California enacts enforceable margin caps affecting >10% of MPC capacity

## Conclusion

MPC is a structural beneficiary of the current geopolitical-energy environment. The combination of widening crack spreads, U.S. refining supply tightness, and aggressive share reduction creates a favorable setup at ~12x earnings with estimates rising. The stock has rallied substantially and currently trades near the analyst consensus mean target (approximately $245 per April 15 data).

Position sizing should reflect the inherent cyclicality — cracks can compress rapidly on geopolitical de-escalation. A 2-3% portfolio weight is appropriate, with willingness to add on pullbacks toward $220-230 (where crack sensitivity and MPLX support provide better risk/reward). Maintain the position while geopolitical conditions support elevated margins, with disciplined adherence to the invalidation triggers above.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Several forward-looking inputs (FY2026 EPS consensus, Brent pricing level, geopolitical probability estimates, and peer valuation comparisons) could not be fully validated at the figures used in the scenario/target-price analysis; the base-case target may be modestly overstated if consensus EPS is closer to $21.49 rather than $22.68.
