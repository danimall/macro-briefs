**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pebblebrook Hotel Trust is a gateway-market lodging REIT that owns 44 hotels and 11,052 rooms concentrated in supply-constrained coastal urban and resort markets: Southern Florida, San Diego, San Francisco, Boston, Los Angeles, Portland, and Washington D.C. The investment story has two intertwined components: a genuine operating recovery confirmed in Q1 2026 (same-property RevPAR +11.8%, comparable Hotel EBITDA +27.6%, AFFO of $0.32/share), and a capital-allocation program in which management is funding buybacks of deeply discounted common stock and preferreds with proceeds from hotel sales. The CEO and a director have been buying open-market shares across a wide price range, alongside a corporate buyback executed near $11–$12. That alignment is among the strongest insider signals in the sector.

The complication is that the stock has already responded. PEB closed at $18.33 on June 17, up 93% from its 52-week low of $9.28 and within reach of its $19.60 high. The buybacks executed at $11–$12 were well-timed; the discount-to-NAV that drove the thesis has compressed materially. Q1's headline RevPAR gain was flattered by easy comps (the lapping of wildfire-depressed Los Angeles and a San Francisco Super Bowl boost), and management itself has stated caution toward the remainder of 2026 "given an increasingly uncertain macroeconomic environment." The $350M convertible due December 2026 is the discrete near-term watch item, though it is covered by roughly $846M of liquidity.

We rate PEB a HOLD. The business is real, the management alignment is exceptional, and the supply backdrop in its markets is a multi-year tailwind. But after a near-double, the asymmetry that made this a discount-to-NAV opportunity has largely been realized, consensus price targets ($15.00 median, $19.50 high) now bracket the current price, and short interest at 18.7% of float signals that sophisticated investors remain skeptical of the durability of the recovery. We would re-engage on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $14s or on evidence that the H2 demand picture holds rather than fades.

## Company Overview

Pebblebrook is an internally managed lodging REIT formed in 2009. Its portfolio is heavily weighted toward independent and lifestyle upper-upscale full-service hotels: properties such as 1 Hotel San Francisco, Viceroy Santa Monica, The Liberty Boston, Margaritaville Hollywood Beach, Jekyll Island Club, and Estancia La Jolla. The footprint is a deliberate barbell of high-barrier coastal urban markets and leisure resort destinations.

As a REIT, Pebblebrook cannot operate its hotels directly. It leases them to a taxable REIT subsidiary (PHL), which engages third-party managers. The company is a co-founder and majority owner of the Curator Hotel & Resort Collection, an owner-centric soft-brand platform for independent lifestyle hotels. Curator gives PEB access to brand-scale economics (distribution, procurement, loyalty) while preserving the property independence that differentiates its assets from branded competitors. This is a genuine structural differentiator versus most hotel REITs.

Management's core thesis is that supply growth in its gateway markets is structurally constrained by high development costs, regulatory barriers, and scarce sites, which should support above-average long-term RevPAR and ADR recovery. The corresponding vulnerability is heavy exposure to slow-recovering urban and convention demand (San Francisco, Washington D.C.) and to idiosyncratic shocks; the LA wildfires and Gulf Coast hurricanes both materially shaped recent results.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and operating trend.** Annual revenue has grown modestly: $1.39B (2022) → $1.42B (2023) → $1.45B (2024) → $1.48B (2025), roughly +1.5% in 2025, partly muted by dispositions. The company has posted GAAP net losses every year from 2022 through 2025, including a $65.8M loss in 2025. These losses are driven primarily by ~$228M of annual depreciation and recurring impairments ($48.9M in 2025, $48.1M in 2024, $81.8M in 2023). Depreciation is a normal feature of a real-estate-heavy REIT; the impairments are more telling, as they reflect genuine markdowns on hotels being sold below carrying value.

The meaningful operating metric is AFFO. AFFO to common was $187.4M in 2025, down from $204.3M in 2024. That decline occurred even as revenue rose, for two reasons: the lapping of large hurricane business-interruption insurance gains ($23.8M in 2024 vs. $12.7M in 2025), and higher property taxes and wages. The insurance gains flattered prior-year FFO and will not recur, which both understates 2025's organic improvement and removes a tailwind going forward.

**Q1 2026 inflection.** The first quarter showed a clear operating turn. Total revenue of $345.7M was up 7.9% YoY; same-property RevPAR rose 11.8% to $215.78; reported Hotel EBITDA of $82.2M was up 35%. AFFO of $0.32/share beat expectations and operating cash flow rose to $84.1M from $50.3M. The strength was led by San Francisco (+41% revenue) and Los Angeles (+23%), both rebounding off depressed 2025 levels, plus San Diego. Washington D.C. was the notable drag (−18%), lapping the 2025 inauguration and reflecting government-related demand softness. A portion of the San Francisco and Los Angeles strength is event-driven and comp-driven (the Super Bowl and the wildfire-depressed prior year), which limits how much of the +11.8% should be extrapolated.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is approximately $2.1B face. The Altman Z-Score of 1.48 nominally flags a "distress zone," but in isolation this is misleading: it is an artifact of REIT accounting (low EBIT relative to assets because of depreciation, plus negative GAAP net income) rather than a liquidity crunch. PEB is in compliance with all covenants, holds roughly $846M of liquidity (cash plus an essentially undrawn $650M revolver), and has termed out its debt. Only ~1.8% of debt is genuinely floating after $865M of interest-rate swaps. In February 2026, the company extended its $360M term loan from 2027 to 2031 and added a $90M delayed-draw option. The two converts are the $350M 1.75% due December 2026 (conversion price ~$25.47, deeply out-of-the-money) and the $400M 1.625% due 2030 (conversion ~$15.89, now in-the-money with shares near $18). The December 2026 convert is the key near-term maturity, but with current liquidity and the delayed-draw term loan, repayment or refinancing looks manageable rather than threatening.

**Capital allocation.** The central financial story is asset sales funding buybacks of discounted equity. In 2025, PEB sold two hotels for $116M and repurchased 6.3M common shares at ~$11.37 ($71.4M) plus $10.1M of preferreds at ~$18.95. In Q1 2026 it repurchased 0.5M shares, with $145.1M remaining on the October-2025 $150M authorization and $74.1M of preferred authorization. Share count fell from ~130M in 2022 to ~113M today. The common dividend has been cut to a token $0.01/quarter, a deliberate choice to redirect capital toward buybacks at a discount to NAV rather than a sign of distress. The buybacks at $11–$12 now look well-timed given the rally to $18.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the durability of the gateway-urban recovery layered on resilient leisure demand. San Francisco and Los Angeles are recovering off depressed bases, and PEB's low-new-supply markets provide a genuine multi-year RevPAR tailwind as occupancy and rate normalize. The Q1 2026 results confirm that the recovery is real, and full-year guidance was raised.

Estimate revisions are modestly constructive. Next-year EPS has been revised from −$0.51 (90 days ago) to −$0.36, a +30.1% improvement, and the current-year estimate ticked up +3.5% over 90 days. The next-quarter estimate, however, was cut sharply (−80% on a single-analyst basis to $0.01), consistent with management's own H2 caution and the fading of the easy Q1 comps. The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats and one in-line in the last four quarters, against a large Q3 2025 miss driven by the wildfire and seasonal dynamics. We do not treat this as a clean +30% inflection-buy signal, because the headline RevPAR gain was partly event- and comp-driven, the insurance tailwinds are rolling off, and the single-analyst forward-quarter estimate was cut.

The structural offsets are meaningful. Washington D.C. faces persistent government-related softness and was hurt by the Q3 2025 government shutdown. San Francisco and D.C. remain below pre-pandemic EBITDA. The 10-Q also flags potential changes to U.S. visa and inbound-travel policy as a demand risk, relevant given PEB's gateway-market and international-traveler exposure. Growth is positive and improving but uneven, with a credible path to deceleration in the back half of 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

PEB trades near its 52-week high. Trailing and forward P/E are negative and uninformative for a REIT with depreciation-driven GAAP losses; the relevant frame is AFFO and discount-to-NAV. On 2025 AFFO of $187.4M against ~113M shares, the stock trades at roughly 11x trailing AFFO. The discount-to-book, cited near 22% earlier in the year, has compressed substantially after the rally.

Within the hotel REIT peer set, the comparison is muddied by negative or distorted earnings multiples: SHO at 107x, DRH at 25x, XHR at 28x, PK at −13x, INN at −23x (peer median P/E 27.5x). PEB's near-zero dividend yield (0.2%) sits well below the peer median of 3.1%, reflecting the buyback-over-dividend choice rather than weaker cash generation. The cleaner read is that PEB has historically traded at a discount to NAV and to better-recovered peers; that discount was the source of the thesis, and it has now largely closed.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price: $15.00 median, $15.70 mean, $19.50 high, $12.00 low. With the stock at $18.33, it sits above the mean and median and just below the high. We are cautious about treating a cheap-looking multiple as a buy signal here, because the conditions that justified the discount (uneven urban recovery, event-driven Q1 strength, the rolling-off of insurance gains, and a token dividend) are still present. The asymmetric upside that existed at $11–$12 has been substantially harvested. We assign a valuation score reflecting fair value rather than clear mispricing in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

PEB competes with gateway-focused lodging REITs including Host, Park, RLJ, Xenia, DiamondRock, and Sunstone. Its differentiation is threefold: the highest concentration in independent and lifestyle upper-upscale assets in the peer group, ownership of the Curator soft-brand platform, and a deliberately barbelled urban-plus-coastal-resort footprint. The low-new-supply characteristic of its markets is a real competitive advantage that should support pricing power as demand recovers.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the operating side, with Q1 2026 confirming share recovery in San Francisco and Los Angeles. The counterweight is cumulative TSR of 61.0 versus a peer-group TSR of 126.8 since the pay-versus-performance base year. PEB has materially lagged its hotel-REIT peer group, which is the context behind the buy-the-discount thesis: the company is cheap relative to peers because it has underperformed them, in part owing to its heavier exposure to the slowest-recovering urban markets. The competitive position is genuine but not dominant, and the recovery narrows rather than eliminates the gap.

## Risk Assessment

**Urban and convention dependence.** San Francisco and Washington D.C. remain structurally below pre-pandemic EBITDA, with D.C. exposed to government demand weakness and shutdown risk.

**Geographic and climate concentration.** Florida and California coastal exposure carries ongoing catastrophe and insurance-cost risk. The 2025 results were materially shaped by early-2025 LA wildfires and prior Hurricanes Helene and Milton (LaPlaya Beach Resort closure). Insurance settlements flattered recent FFO and will not recur.

**Recurring impairments.** A $7.7M impairment again in Q1 2026, on top of $48.9M in 2025, indicates the disposition program is partly forced-value realization rather than purely opportunistic recycling.

**Refinancing.** The December 2026 convert is the discrete near-term item; it is covered by liquidity, but the REIT's 90% payout structure limits retained earnings and keeps the company reliant on capital markets and asset sales.

**Capital structure seniority.** A large preferred stack (~$677M liquidation preference, ~$46M annual preferred dividends, plus Series Z OP units) sits ahead of common equity, which matters in downside scenarios. The token common dividend means PEB is not currently an income vehicle; the return thesis is NAV-discount closure plus operating recovery.

**Key-man risk.** Heavy dependence on Chairman/CEO Jon Bortz.

### Options Market Signal

Listed options activity is thin and we have limited delta detail. The directional read is the +28.3% price move from $14.72 to $18.88 between the two snapshots, consistent with the post-earnings rally to a 52-week high. The more informative positioning signal is the 18.7% short interest with 6.2 days to cover. That is elevated and indicates persistent institutional skepticism about the recovery's durability, a divergence against the bullish insider activity. Heavy shorting plus a near-double creates two-sided risk: a short squeeze on continued operating beats, or a sharp reversal if H2 demand fades as management has warned.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The operating recovery is real and has runway. San Francisco and Los Angeles are recovering off depressed bases in low-supply markets, supporting multi-year RevPAR growth. Management is recycling capital from hotel sales into buybacks of stock and preferreds at a discount to NAV, and the CEO and a director are buying open-market shares with their own money across a wide price range. The 2030 convert is now in-the-money, the debt is termed out to 2031, and liquidity covers the 2026 maturity. As the NAV discount continues to close and AFFO compounds, the stock re-rates toward and through prior highs. Bull case ~$23, roughly +25% from current.

### Bear Case

The Q1 2026 RevPAR gain was substantially driven by easy LA wildfire comps and a one-time San Francisco Super Bowl boost, and management's own H2 caution proves correct as macro uncertainty, weak D.C. government demand, and softening inbound travel weigh on the back half. Insurance gains that flattered prior FFO do not recur. The stock has already nearly doubled, harvesting most of the discount-to-NAV upside, and the 18.7% short interest reflects investors who expect the recovery to stall. A demand disappointment or a credit-market wobble around the December convert pressures the stock back toward the low teens. Bear case ~$13, roughly −29% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** After a 93% move off the lows, the discount-to-NAV asymmetry that defined the original thesis has largely been realized, and the path forward depends on whether the urban recovery sustains or fades against management's stated caution. We lack sufficient conviction for a stronger call in either direction at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $14s (back to a meaningful discount-to-NAV), with fundamentals intact.
- Q2/Q3 2026 same-property RevPAR holding above ~5% YoY on a clean, comp-adjusted basis (i.e., excluding the LA wildfire and Super Bowl effects), demonstrating the recovery is broad rather than event-driven.
- Successful repayment or refinancing of the December 2026 convert on favorable terms, removing the near-term maturity overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- H2 2026 same-property RevPAR turning negative YoY, confirming the bear demand thesis.
- A covenant breach, a forced equity raise, or a deterioration in the liquidity position below comfortable coverage of the 2026 convert.
- Cessation of insider buying combined with a resumption of clustered insider selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (Q2 2026, expected late July), which will test the durability of the recovery against the easier-comp tailwinds that boosted Q1.

## Conclusion

Pebblebrook is a well-run gateway lodging REIT with a coherent capital-allocation program, an exceptional insider-alignment signal, and a confirmed operating inflection. The issue is price: the stock has nearly doubled to a 52-week high, the NAV discount that anchored the bull case has compressed, consensus targets now bracket the current price, and management itself is cautious on the back half of 2026 given comp dynamics and macro uncertainty. The 18.7% short interest underscores that the recovery's durability is genuinely contested rather than settled. We rate PEB a HOLD and would look to re-engage on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $14s or on clean evidence that H2 demand holds. The downside is buffered by management buying its own discounted equity; the upside is constrained by how much of the recovery is already in the price.
