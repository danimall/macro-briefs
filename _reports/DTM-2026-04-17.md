## Executive Summary

DT Midstream is a focused natural gas midstream operator with an integrated pipeline and gathering asset base spanning the Haynesville Shale, Appalachian Basin, and Midwest corridor. The company has delivered strong operational execution since its 2021 spinoff from DTE Energy, generating a cumulative TSR of 348% versus 206% for peers. Adjusted EBITDA grew at an 11% CAGR from 2022-2025, reaching $1.138B, and the company achieved investment-grade ratings from all three major agencies during 2025.

Two secular demand themes underpin the growth thesis: (1) expanding U.S. LNG export capacity pulling incremental volumes through DTM's LEAP pipeline system, and (2) surging Midwest data center construction driving gas-fired power demand through Guardian, Vector, and NEXUS pipelines. The Guardian G3 expansion ($850-930M, 537 MMcf/d, late 2028 in-service) backed by 20-year utility contracts is the near-term growth catalyst. Haynesville gathering volumes hit a record 2.04 Bcf/d in Q3 2025.

The core problem is valuation. At ~31x trailing earnings, DTM trades at 2.3x its peer median P/E of 13.7x, with a dividend yield of 2.7% versus the peer median of 4.4%. This premium is partially justified by DTM's superior margin profile (49% operating margin), longer-duration contract structure, and higher growth visibility. But the premium also embeds considerable execution risk on multi-year capital projects, while masking real structural vulnerabilities: 45% revenue concentration in a single customer (Expand Energy), a Gathering segment in secular decline, and falling equity method investee earnings. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised down 2.6% over 90 days, and next-year estimates are down 4.3%. The stock at $132 sits almost exactly at the analyst consensus mean target of $142, leaving limited upside to consensus.

## Company Overview

DT Midstream was spun off from DTE Energy in July 2021 and operates through two segments:

**Pipeline (~55% of revenue, ~84% of net income):** Interstate pipelines (Guardian, Midwestern, Viking — acquired December 2024 for $1.2B), intrastate pipelines, storage (Washington 10 Storage Complex), and equity method investments in Vector, Millennium, and NEXUS. The LEAP pipeline connects Haynesville production to Gulf Coast LNG terminals.

**Gathering (~45% of revenue, ~16% of net income):** Blue Union (Haynesville), Susquehanna, Appalachia, Ohio Utica, and Tioga gathering systems, plus Clean Fuels Gathering (acquired July 2024) for methane capture and carbon credit generation.

The dual-basin strategy connecting Haynesville and Appalachia to Midwest, Northeast, and Gulf Coast demand centers provides geographic diversification, though the customer base is heavily concentrated.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew 27% YoY to $1.243B in 2025, driven primarily by the Midwest Pipeline Acquisition adding $212M, plus $31M from new LEAP contracts and $18M from Blue Union. Net income rose 25% to $441M ($4.30 EPS). Adjusted EBITDA of $1.138B exceeded the raised guidance range midpoint.

**Margins:** Operating margin of 49.4% and net margin of 36.5% are exceptional, reflecting fee-based, fixed-charge contract structures with minimal commodity exposure. These margins are meaningfully above midstream sector averages.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $867M (up 14% YoY) covers $426M of capex, yielding $441M of free cash flow. Dividends of $324M are comfortably covered at a 74% payout ratio on FCF. Distributable cash flow through 9 months annualizes to ~$890M+.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $3.324B represents 1.1x D/E — manageable for midstream. No debt matures until 2029 ($1.1B), and ~$1B in available liquidity provides ample cushion. Investment-grade credit ratings from Moody's, S&P, and Fitch were all achieved during 2024-2025. The Altman Z-Score of 1.53 is mechanically misleading for capital-intensive infrastructure; the Piotroski score of 5/5 better reflects actual financial condition.

**Segment Divergence:** The Pipeline segment revenue jumped 55% to $687M in 2025 with the acquisition. The Gathering segment, however, is deteriorating: net income declined 33% over two years ($106M → $78M → $71M), with Susquehanna volumes down $22M and Appalachia down $9M in 2025. Blue Union growth partially offsets but does not fully compensate. This divergence is structural, not cyclical.

**Equity Method Earnings:** Declining from $177M (2023) → $162M (2024) → $138M (2025) as joint ventures (Millennium, NEXUS, Vector) carry higher interest expense from debt issuances used to return capital. This was a deliberate capital allocation trade-off but creates a persistent reported earnings headwind.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Demand Catalysts:** Two growth vectors support DTM's medium-term outlook:

1. *LNG Export Expansion:* LEAP pipeline capacity approaching 2.1 Bcf/d directly connects Haynesville production to Gulf Coast LNG terminals. LEAP Phase 4 came online early and on budget in Q3 2025. The current administration's lift of the LNG export authorization pause accelerates terminal approvals and incremental throughput demand.

2. *Data Center/AI Power Demand:* Midwest data center construction (Microsoft/Foxconn near Milwaukee, Meta $800M near Toledo) requires massive gas-fired power. Guardian G3 adds 537 MMcf/d capacity, backed by 20-year utility contracts with five utilities. Vector's potential 400 MMcf/d westbound expansion and NEXUS expansion serve this same demand.

**Quantified Growth Path:** 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.155-1.225B implies ~6% growth at midpoint. Consensus EPS of $4.70 for FY2026 and $5.07 for FY2027 implies ~9% and ~8% growth, respectively. Guardian G3 is the primary 2027-2028 growth driver.

**Estimate Revisions — Negative:** Current-year EPS estimates have been revised down from $4.82 to $4.70 over 90 days (-2.6%). Next-year estimates declined from $5.29 to $5.07 (-4.3%). These are meaningful negative revisions for a stock trading at 31x earnings and undermine the growth premium narrative.

**Earnings Consistency:** The 4-quarter track record shows 2 beats and 2 misses, with the most recent quarter (Q4 2025) missing by 8.7%. This is a below-average hit rate that reduces confidence in forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

DTM trades at ~31x trailing P/E versus a peer median of 13.7x — a 126% premium. The dividend yield of 2.7% compares unfavorably to the peer median of 4.4%. On a forward basis using consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.70, DTM trades at ~28x forward earnings.

**Justifications for a premium:** Superior margin profile, longer-duration contracted cash flows, investment-grade credit, two secular growth catalysts (LNG + data centers), and strong management track record.

**Problems with the premium at this level:** (1) The premium requires near-perfect execution on Guardian G3, Vector expansion, and continued LNG volume growth. (2) Estimates are being revised downward. (3) Gathering segment structural decline reduces the quality of consolidated earnings growth. (4) 45% customer concentration in Expand Energy is not being compensated. (5) At $132, the stock trades within 5% of the analyst consensus median target of $140.50 and above the consensus mean of $141.57.

Applying a more generous premium of 22-24x to FY2027 EPS of $5.07 yields a range of $111-$122 — below current price. To justify $132, the market needs to apply ~26x FY2027 EPS, which requires sustained high-single-digit growth beyond 2027 and no execution missteps.

The analyst price target range of $111-$165 brackets the stock, with the consensus mean at $141.57 offering approximately 7% upside. With 2 sell ratings among 15 analysts, there is meaningful disagreement about fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

DTM operates in a competitive midstream landscape but holds differentiated positions:

- **LEAP corridor:** Competes with Williams, Energy Transfer, Momentum Midstream, and WhiteWater Midstream for incremental Haynesville-to-Gulf Coast volumes. Management acknowledges "competitive tension" but LEAP's existing capacity and customer relationships provide incumbency advantages.

- **Midwest pipelines:** Guardian G3's 20-year utility contracts create a significant moat. Vector's Michigan-to-Chicago route faces limited direct competition for the specific westbound capacity being contemplated.

- **Scale disadvantage:** At $13.5B market cap, DTM is significantly smaller than Williams ($70B+), Enterprise Products ($65B+), or Energy Transfer ($55B+). Scale matters for capital allocation flexibility and negotiating leverage, though DTM's focused footprint partially compensates.

- **Contract quality:** DTM's heavy use of demand charges, minimum volume commitments, and long-term firm service agreements provides revenue predictability superior to many midstream peers that retain meaningful commodity volume/price exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (Critical):** Expand Energy accounts for approximately 45% of operating revenues. This is extreme concentration for a $13.5B enterprise. Expand Energy was formed from the Chesapeake Energy/Southwestern Energy merger, and any future M&A, production strategy shifts, or financial stress would directly and materially impact DTM. Mitigants exist (long-term contracts with MVCs, Expand Energy's scale in DTM's core basins) but they do not eliminate the tail risk.

**Gathering Segment Decline:** Susquehanna and Appalachia volumes declining. Unless Blue Union growth accelerates materially, the Gathering segment will continue to compress consolidated margins and growth rates.

**Execution Risk:** Guardian G3, Vector potential expansion, and LEAP Phase 4+ are large capital commitments requiring FERC permitting in an "increasingly challenging" regulatory environment with "heightened opposition and permitting scrutiny." Delays or cost overruns would directly impact projected EBITDA growth.

**Cross-Border/Tariff Risk:** Vector Pipeline crosses the U.S./Canada border. The 10-K specifically identifies tariffs on cross-border gas trade as a risk factor.

**Clean Fuels Tax Credit Uncertainty:** Clean Fuels Gathering economics depend on federal tax credits that may not materialize. The 10-K warns treated gas "may not qualify" and legislative changes "could lower or eliminate" these credits.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With FOMC unanimously on hold at 3.64% and rate cuts effectively off the table for 2026, DTM's borrowing costs remain elevated. The $3.3B debt load generates ~$110M in annual interest expense. Any rate increases would compress margins further.

## Conclusion

DT Midstream is a high-quality midstream operator with legitimate secular growth catalysts, a strong balance sheet, and exceptional margins. The management team has delivered outstanding shareholder returns since the 2021 spinoff. The LNG export and data center power demand themes are real and durable.

At $132, the stock prices in substantial growth execution at a 126% premium to peer median P/E, while estimate momentum is negative and the Gathering segment is in structural decline. The 45% revenue concentration in Expand Energy is an underappreciated tail risk. The risk/reward profile for new capital is unattractive at current levels. Investors already holding DTM should maintain positions given the positive growth trajectory, but new purchases should wait for either a price correction to the $115-120 range or a reversal of the negative estimate revision trend.
