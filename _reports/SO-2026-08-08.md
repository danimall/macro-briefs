## Executive Summary

Southern Company is midway through the largest construction program in its history, and the earnings mechanics of that program are now the central analytical issue. Reported first-half 2026 diluted EPS rose 11.5% to $2.23, but consolidated operating income rose 0.5% — from $3,774M to $3,794M. The gap sits entirely below the operating line: the effective tax rate fell 700 basis points to 14.1%, AFUDC-equity rose $95M to $248M as construction work in progress grew from $10.5B to $11.9B, and equity-method earnings rose $93M including a $59M single-quarter swing in Southern Holdings venture-capital fund marks. Grossing net income back up at the disclosed tax rates implies pretax income grew about 5%, meaning roughly $0.16 of the $0.23 EPS increase traces to the tax line rather than to utility operations. None of this is an accounting concern — AFUDC and production tax credits are the legitimate early-cycle form of return on regulated capital — but it is a reason not to extrapolate 11.5% growth.

The reason operating income is flat is structural and disclosed: base rates are frozen across three jurisdictions at once. Georgia Power's Alternate Rate Plan was extended through December 31, 2028 with no base-rate adjustments except storm costs. Alabama Power's December 2025 Consent Order holds rates stable through 2027, and the Alabama legislature then hard-wired that outcome by enacting the "Power to the People Act" on April 2, 2026, barring increases to base rates in place on October 1, 2026 before January 1, 2029. Southern has bought multi-year rate certainty and political cover for a very large build; the cost is that 2027-2028 earnings must come from riders, trackers and capitalized AFUDC while Georgia Power spends $10.1B in 2026 alone against a 9.50% ROE band floor and no reset until 2029.

Against that, the funding side improved materially. On February 20, 2026 Georgia Power and Alabama Power entered DOE loan guarantee agreements supporting up to ~$22.4B and ~$4.1B of Federal Financing Bank term loans at Treasury plus 37.5bp, covering up to 80% of eligible project costs with principal amortization beginning 2049-2055. Georgia Power drew ~$1.0B in March 2026 at 5.041% maturing December 2055 — thirty-year money at a level competitive with where its three-year and five-year public notes priced in May (4.60%-4.85%). This is a differentiated advantage. It is also contingent: if recoverable eligible project costs fall below 95% of advances when tested, prepayment is mandatory. At 20.4x FY2026 adjusted guidance midpoint, 2.7x book and a 3.19% yield, the shares price the growth story without pricing the freeze-period earned-return compression. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Southern is a holding company whose earnings come predominantly from state-regulated, vertically integrated utilities in the Southeast: Alabama Power (1,563K retail electric customers), Georgia Power (2,834K) and Mississippi Power (193K), totalling 4,590K electric customers. Southern Company Gas serves 4,416K gas customers across Illinois (Nicor, 2,294K), Georgia (Atlanta Gas Light, 1,733K), Virginia and Chattanooga, plus SouthStar retail marketing and a 50% interest in Southern Natural Gas. Southern Power operates 12,648 MW of contracted merchant generation with ~12-year average remaining PPA duration. Roughly 9.0 million total customers, 29,800 employees.

FY2025 generation mix: gas 51%, coal 20%, nuclear 19%, wind/solar/other 8%, hydro 2%, on 191 billion KWh generated. Nuclear was 36% of Georgia Power's generation, and the NRC approved subsequent license renewal for Plant Hatch Units 1 and 2 on June 12, 2026, extending operation to 2054 and 2058. Vogtle 3 and 4 are licensed to 2062-2063. For hyperscale customers pursuing 24/7 carbon-free supply, eight nuclear units at high capacity factors is a differentiated product.

One qualification on the "regulated monopoly" framing deserves attention because it bears directly on the data-center thesis: Georgia's Territorial Electric Service Act of 1973 permits new customers outside 1973 municipal limits with connected load of 900 KW or more to choose their supplier once, for the life of the premises. Hyperscale data centers are exactly such customers. Georgia Power competes with EMCs and municipal systems at siting for its largest new loads.

The tax-rate decline has three disclosed drivers, and they are not equivalent in durability. The Georgia Power state valuation-allowance charge ($126M in 2025, as Georgia credits were expected to expire unused) is a prior-year comparison artifact — 2026 simply lacks the headwind, and it does not recur as a benefit. Southern Power's production tax credits are durable and paid for: following the December 31, 2025 purchase of the noncontrolling Class A interests in the SP Wind tax-equity partnership for ~$282M, Southern retains 100% of the credits, and Southern Power's effective tax benefit rate went from (7.0)% to (85.1)%. Georgia Power's nuclear PTC amortization is different in character — the economics are largely flowing to customers, with Alabama Power deferring $180M of 2024 nuclear PTCs as a regulatory liability for 2027 cost of service and Georgia Power agreeing in the May 28, 2026 storm stipulation to apply $77M of 2024-2025 nuclear PTCs for customer benefit. The GAAP tax-line benefit and the economic benefit diverge there.

AFUDC-equity growth (+62% in 1H 2026, following +52.2% in FY2025) is the arithmetic consequence of CWIP rising $1,340M in six months. This is regulator-sanctioned and normal, but it means a growing share of reported EPS is non-cash and contingent on the plant ultimately entering rate base at recoverable cost. Venture-capital fund marks should not be capitalized into any growth rate at all.

Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.03 against $0.79. The reported $1.13 versus $1.01 "beat" is an adjusted-versus-adjusted comparison, with revenue missing. Management guided full-year adjusted EPS to "near or at the top" of the $4.50-$4.60 range. The earnings track record — three beats and one miss over four quarters, average beat 3.0% — supports reasonable confidence in that guidance without implying much upside beyond it.

Negative free cash flow at a regulated utility in a build cycle is structural, not distress — the capital converts into rate base earning a return. It does mean the external funding treadmill is permanent while the program runs. Cash taxes paid in 1H 2026 were only $146M, a genuine support to operating cash flow.

At June 30, 2026: total debt $75,582M (up from $72,591M at year-end), cash $2,984M, total equity $42,342M including $2,772M noncontrolling, book value per share $34.40. Debt/total capitalization 64.1%; net debt/EBITDA ~5.35x on TTM EBITDA of ~$13.6B; interest coverage 2.41x in 1H 2026. Committed bank facilities of $8.92B were $8.909B undrawn, with maturities extended into 2031 during 2026.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.81 that appears on screens should not be read as a solvency signal. The model penalizes high leverage, negative working capital and low asset turnover — all designed features of a rate-regulated monopoly with cost-recovery mechanisms and 0.19 asset turnover. The genuine leverage concern is narrower: 64% debt/cap and 5.35x leave limited headroom for a company that must keep issuing, and the 10-K discloses that a downgrade to BB+/Ba1 or below would trigger up to $3,723M of collateral postings (Georgia Power $2,430M, Southern Power $1,422M). That is a scenario, not a forecast, but it is the mechanism by which a funding problem would become an operating problem.

An unrecognized asset sits on the liability side: long-term debt carried at ~$72.7B had a fair value of ~$68.0B at June 30, 2026 — the existing stack is worth ~6.5% less than book because of below-market coupons. It is eroding. Southern redeemed $1.25B of 4.00% junior subordinated notes in January 2026 and issued $1.3B of 6.00% junior subordinated notes in March — roughly $26M/year of incremental pre-tax interest on that swap alone. Southern Company Gas Capital issued $500M at 6.05% in May. Georgia Power's 2026 issues priced at 4.60%-4.85% against maturing 3.25% paper. This refinancing step-up is a persistent drag that partially offsets rate-base growth.

Equity issuance is running hard. Shares outstanding reached 1,150.4M at June 30, up ~31.1M (+2.8%) in six months, with $2,596M of common stock proceeds from settling 27.7M shares under 2024 ATM forwards. A new 50-million-share ATM launched in June 2026, of which ~7.56M is contracted, leaving ~42.4M available — 3.7% of current shares, or 3.6% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully used. A further 11,483,995 shares sit under unsettled forwards at $88-$97.58 with settlement dates through mid-2028 (1.0% of shares; 0.99% ownership dilution). Diluted weighted-average shares already reflect forwards, converts and equity units at 1,141M.

The financing pattern itself is worth flagging. Within roughly two years Southern has issued Series 2024A convertibles, Series 2025A convertibles, November 2025 equity units (NYSE: SOMN), and on August 3, 2026 announced $650M of converts due December 2027 plus $1.5B due September 2029, with proceeds partly repurchasing the 2024A and 2025A notes. Conversions may be settled in cash, stock or a mix, so dilution is at Southern's election. The most likely reading is that management is minimizing near-term share count and EPS drag while funding a program consuming $6.6B of property additions against $4.3B of operating cash flow in six months, with hybrids earning partial rating-agency equity credit. An equally plausible reading is opportunistic pricing in a receptive convert market. Either way it adds embedded optionality a headline debt/equity ratio misses, and the principal amounts of the 2024A/2025A converts and the equity units are not established in what was reviewed, so total contingent dilution cannot be sized.

## Growth Analysis

The demand evidence is real and specific. Since 2023 the traditional electric operating companies have contracted ~9 GW of large load, each contract above 100 MW of maximum annual load, with service beginning through 2028. On December 19, 2025 the Georgia PSC certified 9,885 MW of resources — 7,999 MW from RFP (6,804 MW Georgia Power-owned) with CODs 2028-2030, a 930 MW 20-year non-affiliate PPA, five 25-year PPAs totalling 646 MW, and a 260 MW battery. Georgia Power-owned projects plus transmission total ~$16.7B excluding AFUDC. On June 9, 2026 Georgia Power issued an RFP for a further 2,000-6,000 MW with CODs 2032-2033. Georgia Power had recorded only $4.4B of capital costs at June 30 against $19.5B certified, leaving ~$15.1B of already-approved spend.

Reuters and MarketWatch confirmed on July 22, 2026 that Georgia Power signed a 25-year power supply agreement to serve OpenAI's planned data center in Effingham County, including one of the country's largest single-site demand-response commitments. Georgia Power is the supplier and earns a regulated return on the generation and delivery infrastructure; OpenAI is the retail customer and owns the data center. The widely circulated 3.2 GW figure appears in secondary coverage only and is not confirmed in tier-1 reporting or SEC filings.

The constraint on translating that into per-share earnings growth is threefold.

First, the rate freezes. With base rates fixed in Georgia through 2028 and in Alabama by statute through 2029, growth between now and the 2029 resets must come from riders, trackers and capitalized AFUDC. That is precisely what the 1H 2026 composition shows. Georgia Power's authorized band runs 9.50%-11.90% around a 10.50% set point on a 56% equity ratio; spending $10.1B in 2026 with no base-rate reset creates regulatory lag pressure toward the band floor. (The ROE-lag conclusion is an inference from the freeze terms and the band, not a company disclosure.)

Second, the earnings from the new load are contractually shared. In the December 2025 certification stipulation Georgia Power agreed to file its next base rate case such that incremental large-load revenue provides downward pressure of at least $556 million per year, levelized, for 2029-2031. If the load underdelivers, Georgia Power owns the shortfall, and the natural resolution is lower earned returns rather than higher residential bills.

Third, affordability politics are compressing the recovery Southern actually receives. The approved May 28, 2026 storm stipulation delivered ~$187M/year against ~$300M/year requested. On the same date the PSC approved a 12.9% (~$394M annual) fuel rate decrease effective June 1 — after Georgia Power had filed three notifications (March, April, May 2026) that its under-recovered fuel balance exceeded $200M without proposing an increase.

Estimate revisions confirm a plateau rather than an inflection: current-year consensus $4.58 is +0.3% over 90 days, next year $4.92 is +0.1%, while the current quarter is −1.3% and next quarter −8.5%. There is no estimate-revision buy signal here. Reconstructing 2025 adjusted EPS from GAAP $3.92 by adding back the parent extinguishment charge (~$0.18) and the Nicor disallowance (~$0.04) gives ~$4.14, so 2026 guidance of $4.50-$4.60 implies roughly 9-11% adjusted growth — but 2027 consensus of $4.92 implies only 7.4% off the 2026 midpoint, and much of the 2026 step-up is the non-repeating tax comparison plus the passing Southern Power repowering trough (~$200M of accelerated depreciation left in 2026, ~$100M in 2027, from ~$290M recognized in 1H 2026).

## Competitive Landscape

Southern operates in the most favorable geography in U.S. utilities. Georgia Power's weather-adjusted commercial sales rose 4.1% in FY2025, explicitly attributed to data centers, and its regulator certified nearly 10 GW in a single order. Real estate analysts cited in regional reporting describe Georgia as the fastest-growing U.S. market for new data center construction. Alabama and Mississippi are pursuing similar agreements but, per the same reporting, not at Georgia's pace — the concentration visible in the filings is geographic as well as by end-market.

Three differentiators stand out. The DOE/FFB facilities are the most distinctive: ~$26.5B of thirty-year, back-ended, Treasury+37.5bp financing covering up to 80% of eligible project costs is not something most utilities have, and it directly reduces the equity-issuance burden. Nuclear scale — eight units, Hatch relicensed to 2054/2058, Vogtle to 2062/2063 — is a differentiated product for 24/7 clean-power buyers. And Georgia's 10.50% ROE set point on a 56% equity ratio sits at the favorable end of the industry.

Two areas detract. The gas segment is a drag: Southern Company Gas earned $732M in 2025, down 1.1%, carries the portfolio's lowest authorized ROE (9.60% at Nicor on a 50% equity ratio), has taken disallowances in two of the last three Illinois rate cases ($47M after tax in Q4 2025 on $120M of excluded capital; $44M in Q4 2023 on $127M), and holds essentially all of the group's $5.2B of goodwill ($5.0B at the gas segment). Illinois is running a "future of natural gas" proceeding with a final report due by end-2026 — the most plausible identified path to a material goodwill impairment, though no impairment indicator is reported today. There has been partial relief: on June 16, 2026 the Illinois Appellate Court found the ICC had not sufficiently supported the $43M 2023 disallowance and remanded, and a Supreme Court petition on capital structure awaits a decision around September 30, 2026.

Separately, the RPO figure of ~$5,925M at June 30 (Southern Power $4,686M) is not total backlog and should not be used as a proxy for the data-center opportunity. It excludes regulated retail tariff revenue and volumetric PPA consideration, so the ~9 GW of large-load contracts and the OpenAI agreement are essentially absent from it.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center concentration with explicit unrecovered-capital language.** The 10-K states that demand "significantly exceeds recent experience," that "the majority of this demand is driven by the power needs and projected power needs of data centers," and that "if anticipated demand growth does not materialize, the traditional electric operating companies could experience unrecovered capital investments made to serve expected load." Southern is also pre-contracting fuel: new long-dated purchase commitments added since December 31, 2025 for the Barry 5 conversion and Georgia Power construction total roughly $9.0B (2027 $136M, 2028 $762M, 2029-2030 $751M each, thereafter $6,571M). Mitigants are real — minimum bills, minimum durations, early-termination payments, financial security, customer construction contributions, and OpenAI's demand-response commitment — and the 10-K notes they "may not fully protect" against all risks. The shift toward commercial revenue (1H 2026 commercial +2.8%, residential −1.9%) increases concentration in one end-market rather than diversifying it.

**Legislated rate freeze as precedent.** Alabama's Power to the People Act converts an affordability negotiation into statute. Legislated freezes are harder to unwind than PSC settlements, and the precedent is not confined to 2029. This is the risk I weight most heavily as underpriced.

**DOE facility contingency.** The mandatory prepayment test — advances exceeding 105% of rate-recoverable eligible project costs, tested on the third anniversary of the availability period's end — hard-wires disallowance risk into the capital structure. Given Nicor's two consecutive Illinois disallowances and simultaneous freeze regimes in Georgia and Alabama, the linkage is not theoretical. Only ~$1.0B of ~$26.5B has been drawn; the facility is a ceiling, and advances require ongoing investment-grade confirmation plus Davis-Bacon and Cargo Preference compliance.

**Coal-ash litigation reopened.** All of Alabama Power's CCR asset retirement obligations assume closure-in-place. On May 18, 2026 the Eleventh Circuit overturned dismissal of the Mobile Baykeeper action on Plant Barry and remanded; a separate Coosa Riverkeeper suit challenges Gadsden. A ruling compelling closure-by-removal would raise AROs above the $653M of 2026 budgeted CCR spending, with recovery subject to PSC approval. A reversal of dismissal is procedural, not a merits ruling, and Alabama Power's Environmental Accounting Order permits regulatory-asset treatment for certain costs.

**Nuclear-worker wage antitrust.** A July 2025 D. Md. purported class action against all U.S. commercial nuclear operators including Southern alleges suppression of nuclear worker compensation since May 2003 with treble damages sought; Southern Nuclear was added in November 2025, motion to dismiss filed December 2025. The 10-Q states an adverse outcome "could have a material impact." No reserve or exposure estimate is disclosed.

**Emissions goal set aside.** The 10-K states it will be "extremely challenging" to meet the 2030 goal; reductions slipped from 49% to 47%, coal's generation share rose from 18% to 20%, and retirements were deferred across Barry 5, Gaston 1-4 (to 2034), Scherer 3 (to at least 2035), and Daniel/Watson. Near term this reduces stranded-asset risk (~$307M of Barry 5 book value returned to plant in service in Q1 2026), supported by a favorable federal posture — EPA's proposed Section 111 repeal, the February 2026 endangerment-finding repeal, Paris withdrawal. Every one of those actions is reversible and litigable, and EPA's May 18, 2026 proposed leachate rule could still "result in significant compliance costs."

**Other.** A standalone shareholder-activism risk factor appears in the FY2025 10-K (whether newly added is unverified, and no activist position was identified). Litigation seeking decertification of at least 757 MW of the 9,885 MW is pending in Fulton County Superior Court.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110)

Nearly 10 GW of certified generation plus ~$16.7B of approved Georgia projects and ~$15.1B of remaining certified spend convert into rate base at a 10.50% ROE set point on a 56% equity ratio, funded disproportionately by thirty-year Treasury+37.5bp DOE-guaranteed debt rather than equity at 2.7x book. Load from ~9 GW of signed large-load contracts, including a 25-year OpenAI agreement with demand-response, arrives on schedule and validates the $556M/year downward-pressure commitment without eroding earned returns. Southern Power's repowering drag ends after 2027 with 100% of PTCs retained post-buyout, adding a tailwind. Hatch's relicensing to 2054/2058 makes Southern a preferred counterparty for 24/7 clean power. The 2029 rate resets in Georgia and Alabama capture the accumulated investment at once, and the stock re-rates toward 22x on ~$5.00 of adjusted EPS.

### Bear Case ($80)

Base rates are frozen in three jurisdictions while Georgia Power spends $10.1B in 2026, pressing earned ROE toward the 9.50% band floor and leaving reported growth dependent on non-cash AFUDC and a tax rate that cannot fall another 700bp. The long end keeps rising, compressing utility multiples toward 17x while the refinancing step-up (4.00% to 6.00% on $1.3B of hybrids; 3.25% to 4.60-4.85% at Georgia Power) erodes the value of the below-market debt stack. Serial convert, equity-unit and ATM issuance dilutes per-share growth — 2.8% share growth in six months plus 3.7% of remaining ATM capacity. Data-center load underdelivers against ~$9.0B of pre-contracted fuel obligations and the $556M commitment, and Georgia Power absorbs the shortfall. Illinois's end-2026 gas decarbonization report threatens the $5.0B of gas-segment goodwill, and the Eleventh Circuit remand forces closure-by-removal cost above budgeted CCR spending.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fourth consecutive; income-driven). The prior three HOLDs at overall scores of 6.1-6.5 rested largely on the data-center load thesis, which remains intact and is now corroborated by a confirmed 25-year OpenAI agreement and the DOE facilities. What has changed since the June 18 assessment is fundamental rather than price-related: the Alabama statutory rate freeze (enacted April 2, 2026) removes a jurisdiction's rate mechanism through 2029; 1H 2026 operating income was flat while EPS rose 11.5% on tax, AFUDC and venture-capital marks; the Eleventh Circuit reopened the Barry coal-ash case on May 18; and near-quarter estimates are being cut (−8.5% for next quarter). The overall score declines to 5.5 on that evidence, with growth prospects marked down from 7.0 to 6.0 to reflect where the earnings are coming from, and financial health held at 5.5 as the DOE facility offsets rising leverage (debt $72.6B → $75.6B in six months).

## Conclusion

Southern owns the best-positioned regulated franchise for the data-center load cycle and has secured a funding advantage — ~$26.5B of DOE-guaranteed thirty-year capacity at Treasury plus 37.5bp — that most peers cannot match. Those are durable positives, and they justify a premium to a slow-growth regulated average. They do not justify 20.4x forward earnings and 2.70x book at a moment when base rates are frozen across three jurisdictions (one by statute through 2029), consolidated operating income grew 0.5% in the first half, and roughly two-thirds of the reported EPS increase traces to a tax rate that cannot repeat the move. Add ~$9.0B of pre-contracted fuel obligations against load that has not yet arrived, a $556M/year contractual commitment to route large-load revenue into other customers' bills, and a $5.0B gas-goodwill exposure to an Illinois proceeding concluding this year, and the probability-weighted 12-month return of roughly 7% — of which the 3.19% dividend is the reliable half — is approximately fair rather than compelling.

Own it for the covered dividend and the 2029 reset optionality. Add on price, not on the story: $84-86 restores the margin of safety this business model requires while it funds a $15.9B annual program with negative free cash flow.
