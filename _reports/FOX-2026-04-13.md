## Executive Summary

Fox Corporation is a deliberately lean media company focused on the two content categories most resistant to cord-cutting: live sports and news. After the 2019 asset sale to Disney stripped out entertainment and studios, Fox retained Fox News (the #1 rated cable news network), a portfolio of sports rights anchored by the NFL, and the fast-growing Tubi AVOD service. The result is a high-margin, high-cyclicality business that generates extraordinary cash flow in "on" years (Super Bowl + elections) and meaningful but diminished cash flow in "off" years.

At $56.25, FOX trades at ~13x trailing EPS of $4.91, which reflects the peak FY2025 cycle boosted by Super Bowl LIX and ~$550M in political advertising revenue. The upcoming FY2026 (ending June 2026) will lack both tailwinds, creating a well-telegraphed earnings trough. The stock has already declined 18% from its January 2026 high of $68.17, partially discounting this reversion. The critical analytical question is whether the current price adequately reflects the trough, and what the normalized earning power of the business is worth.

The answer tilts positive. Fox holds $5.4B in cash against $6.6B in debt (net debt ~$1.2B), investment-grade credit ratings, and generated $3.0B in FCF in FY2025. Even at a conservatively normalized ~$1.5B FCF (stripping out the Super Bowl and political cycle), the stock trades at ~8.4x normalized FCF. The $5.4B cash position — 43% of market cap — provides a floor through either M&A optionality, accelerated buybacks, or a Smartmatic litigation reserve. Four consecutive earnings beats averaging 36% surprise support confidence in management's ability to exceed conservative guidance.

## Company Overview

Fox Corporation operates through two reportable segments:

**Cable Network Programming (43% of FY2025 revenue, $6.9B):** Fox News, Fox Business, Fox Sports 1/2, and associated digital properties. This segment generates 44% EBITDA margins ($3.0B) and is the cash flow engine. Fox News has maintained #1 cable news ratings for over two decades, providing pricing power with MVPDs.

**Television (57% of FY2025 revenue, $9.3B):** The FOX broadcast network, 29 full-power TV stations (including 11 duopolies), Tubi (free ad-supported streaming), and production companies. EBITDA margin of ~10% ($945M), up 87% YoY in FY2025 driven by Super Bowl and political advertising.

Revenue mix is approximately 47% affiliate fees ($7.7B), 42% advertising ($6.9B), and 11% other ($1.8B). The business is almost entirely U.S.-focused. FOX One, the company's new DTC streaming service, launched in August 2025 at $19.99/month, bundling the full Fox portfolio. Management deliberately set "modest" subscriber expectations — this is a defensive cord-cutting hedge with limited growth ambitions.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are complicated by the cyclical overlay. Stripping out the Super Bowl/election cycle, organic growth drivers include:

**Affiliate fee escalation:** $7.7B in FY2025, growing at ~5% despite declining MVPD subscriber counts. Rate increases per subscriber added ~$790M while subscriber losses cost ~$460M. The net $330M tailwind is durable for 3-5 years but narrowing as the subscriber base erodes. Eventually, rate increases cannot compensate for unit losses.

**Tubi AVOD growth:** Tubi contributed meaningfully to the 14% increase in digital advertising revenue in FY2025. As a leading AVOD platform, Tubi has reached scale with limited incremental content investment. Digital ad revenue is the highest-growth organic revenue line.

**FOX One DTC:** Launched August 2025 at $19.99/month. Management set deliberately low subscriber expectations. At this price point with no exclusive content, the addressable market is limited to cord-cutters who specifically want Fox News and Fox Sports — a loyal but finite audience. FOX One is unlikely to move the needle materially in FY2026-2027.

**Political cycle recurrence:** The 2026 midterm elections will provide a partial political advertising tailwind — likely $200-300M, less than the $550M from the 2024 presidential cycle but still incremental.

The core challenge: Fox's organic growth rate, ex-cyclical events, is likely in the low-to-mid single digits. Affiliate fee escalation provides the floor, but secular MVPD subscriber decline is the ceiling. Growth above GDP requires either acquisitions or a successful DTC transition, neither of which is assured.

## Competitive Landscape

Fox occupies a differentiated position in the media landscape. After divesting entertainment assets to Disney, the company's competitive advantages are concentrated in two areas:

**Fox News dominance:** #1 cable news network for over two decades. The brand loyalty of Fox News viewers creates affiliate fee pricing power that exceeds what ratings alone would justify — MVPDs cannot afford to lose the Fox News audience. This is a genuine moat, though it's tied to the ideological/demographic profile of the viewership, which skews older and is subject to generational risk.

**Live sports portfolio:** NFL (NFC package through 2033), college football, MLB, NASCAR, FIFA World Cup 2026. Live sports is the last reliable appointment viewing. The NFL contract through 2033 provides long-term revenue visibility but also locks in escalating rights costs.

**Tubi's AVOD position:** A leading free streaming platform, benefiting from the content Fox acquired cheaply and the natural promotion through Fox's broadcast and cable channels. Tubi operates at low marginal cost and is reaching meaningful scale.

**Competitive threats:** Amazon's acquisition of Thursday Night Football, Apple's MLS deal, and YouTube's growing share of sports viewership demonstrate that tech platforms with deeper pockets are competing for the same live content. ESPN's standalone streaming launch at $29.99/month is the most direct competitive threat to FOX One.

## Risk Assessment

### Secular Cord-Cutting (CRITICAL)
MVPD subscriber losses are accelerating — Fox's own 10-K acknowledges this explicitly. While rate escalation has more than offset subscriber losses to date, the math becomes progressively harder. If subscriber losses accelerate from ~5% annually to 7-8%, rate increases would need to correspondingly accelerate, which MVPDs may resist. The tipping point where rate increases can no longer compensate is likely 3-5 years away.

### FY2026 Earnings Cliff (HIGH — BUT PRICED)
The ~$1.4B cyclical revenue tailwind evaporates in FY2026. The stock's 18% decline from January highs partially reflects this. Consensus appears to be building in a trough year, and Fox's earnings surprise track record suggests the actual trough may be less severe than feared.

### Smartmatic Litigation (HIGH)
The Dominion settlement cost $800M. Smartmatic's claims could be comparable or larger. An adverse outcome of $500M-1B would be material but absorbable given the balance sheet. A larger judgment (>$1B) would meaningfully impair the cash position and potentially force debt issuance.

### Sports Rights Cost Escalation (MODERATE-HIGH)
Sports programming costs rose ~$1B in FY2025. The NFL contract through 2033 provides visibility but at escalating rates. Each renewal cycle brings more bidders (tech platforms), driving costs higher. If rights costs grow faster than advertising and affiliate revenue, margins compress.

### Murdoch Family Governance (MODERATE)
Dual-class structure with 43.39% family voting control limits minority shareholder influence. Ongoing buybacks push Murdoch voting power toward the 44% cap. The CEO (Lachlan Murdoch) simultaneously chairs News Corp — a potential conflict of interest.

### C-Band Spectrum Risk (NEW)
FCC-mandated auction of C-Band spectrum within two years could disrupt Fox's ability to deliver programming to affiliates and MVPDs. This is a low-probability, high-impact risk that requires monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 49.5% is sharply above 1-year historical volatility of 26.9% — a 22.6pp spread indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (49.5% near-term vs. 31.6% at 3 months), consistent with imminent event pricing. This likely reflects broader market stress (naval blockade, oil above $100, credit concerns) rather than Fox-specific catalysts, as the company just reported earnings in February. The call-heavy skew (-7.6% at 1-month) suggests institutional positioning leans constructive despite elevated vol.

The disconnect between call-heavy options skew and high implied volatility indicates options participants are cautiously bullish on direction but hedging against macro tail risks. This is consistent with the fundamental thesis of a stock that is cheap on normalized earnings but exposed to macro uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($68-72)
- Normalized earnings power is higher than consensus models because: (1) Tubi digital ad revenue growth accelerates, (2) affiliate rate increases continue to outpace subscriber losses, (3) FY2027 benefits from the next Super Bowl cycle (if Fox hosts) or FIFA World Cup 2026 viewership
- The $5.4B cash position is deployed into an accretive acquisition (regional sports networks, additional stations, or a content library) that creates meaningful synergy with the existing portfolio
- FOX One exceeds "modest" subscriber expectations and provides incremental recurring revenue
- Smartmatic litigation settles for a manageable amount ($300-500M)
- Buyback acceleration at current depressed prices reduces share count by 8-10%, providing meaningful per-share earnings lift
- 15x mid-cycle EPS of ~$3.75 + $19.50/share excess cash = $76; discounting for execution risk = $68-72

### Bear Case ($40-44)
- MVPD subscriber losses accelerate to 8-10% annually, overwhelming rate increases and causing affiliate fee revenue to decline in absolute terms
- Smartmatic litigation results in a judgment exceeding $1.5B, depleting the cash reserve and requiring debt issuance
- Sports rights renewal costs escalate beyond advertising growth, compressing Television segment margins below 5%
- FOX One fails to gain traction and Fox News viewership demographics skew further toward older cohorts, creating a secular audience decline
- Recession reduces advertising spend by 15-20%, compounding the FY2026 cyclical trough
- 10x trough EPS of ~$2.50 = $25 + some cash benefit = $40-44

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $65** — derived from 12x mid-cycle EPS of ~$3.75 ($45) + $19.50/share in excess cash above $1B operating reserve. This implies 16% upside from the current $56.25.

**Bull case target: $72** — 15x mid-cycle EPS + excess cash deployment into an accretive transaction or accelerated buybacks.

**Bear case target: $42** — 10x trough EPS + reduced cash (post-Smartmatic). Implies 25% downside.

**Upside/downside from current:** +16% to base case, +28% to bull case, -25% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of ~1.1:1 to base case, improving to ~1.6:1 including bull case probability weighting.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: (1) FY2026 Q3 earnings (spring 2026) — first full off-cycle quarter comparison that will set the trough; (2) Smartmatic litigation developments (ongoing); (3) announcement of cash deployment strategy (M&A, special dividend, or accelerated buyback); (4) FOX One subscriber metrics at the 6-month and 12-month marks.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Affiliate fee revenue declines in absolute terms (not just growth deceleration) in any quarter — would signal the cord-cutting tipping point has arrived
2. Smartmatic judgment or settlement exceeding $1.5B — would consume the cash advantage that underpins the valuation
3. Loss of a major sports rights contract (NFL, college football) without replacement — would structurally impair the Television segment's competitive position

## Conclusion

Fox Corporation at $56.25 is cheap, obscured by cyclical noise. The market is focused on the FY2026 earnings cliff — which is real and predictable — but is underweighting the balance sheet strength, the durability of the Fox News franchise, and the consecutive earnings beats that suggest management consistently underpromises.

The risks are real: secular cord-cutting is a structural headwind with a 3-5 year timeline before it becomes critical, Smartmatic litigation has an unknowable but potentially material outcome, and sports rights inflation erodes operating leverage. These are the reasons the stock is cheap.

At 13x trailing earnings with an investment-grade balance sheet, the downside appears well-cushioned. The 1% dividend provides minimal income support, but the combination of ongoing buybacks and potential M&A deployment of the cash hoard creates catalysts for re-rating. FOX trades at a substantial discount to its own cash-adjusted earning power on normalized metrics. BUY with a $65 base case target over 12-18 months.
