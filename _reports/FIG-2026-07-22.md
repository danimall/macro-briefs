**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Figma is a collaborative, browser-based platform for designing and building digital products that IPO'd in August 2025 at $33, spiked to $124.63 intraday, and has since collapsed to roughly $21.93 — down about 83% from peak and 52% in the first half of 2026. The decline does not reflect operational deterioration. The underlying business accelerated over the same window: fiscal 2025 revenue grew 41% to about $1.06B, Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 46% year-over-year to $333.4M, net dollar retention rose to 139% from 132%, and the company generated $88.6M of free cash flow in the quarter against a debt-free, ~$1.64B liquidity position. The market is repricing whether a seat-based SaaS pricing model survives an AI transition in which fewer people can produce more output, and whether AI inference costs structurally compress what were 91% gross margins.

The evidence cuts both ways, which is why this remains a hold rather than a conviction call in either direction. On the bull side, the sell-off looks overdone relative to fundamentals, and sell-side sentiment has turned positive on the dip (BofA reinstated Buy at $30, Citi initiated Buy at $36, Wells Fargo Overweight at $36), with consensus targets clustering near $30-37 versus a ~$22 price. On the bear side, gross margin fell 12 points to 79% in a single year on ~$33.7M of higher AI inference/hosting costs, management expanded its AI-disruption risk language in the latest 10-Q, and the March 2026 enforcement of AI credit limits already produced disclosed customer backlash and reduced usage. Layered on top is a live technical overhang: a final lock-up tranche of roughly 77.7M shares releases around Q2 earnings/August 31, against a small float, with short interest at 44.4%.

The headline GAAP loss (–$142.4M in Q1, a –41% operating margin, –$3.71 trailing EPS) is almost entirely IPO-triggered stock-based compensation ($169M in the quarter) and is misleading as a measure of business health — non-GAAP operating income was $52.1M (16% margin) and the company is cash-generative. The genuine analytical tension is not solvency; it is whether Figma's platform expansion and AI monetization outrun the margin erosion and seat-model risk the market is now pricing. That question is unresolved, and the upcoming Q2 print plus lock-up expiry are the near-term catalysts that will move it.

## Company Overview

Figma pioneered multiplayer, browser-based design in 2015 using WebGL and has since built an end-to-end "idea-to-product" suite: FigJam (whiteboarding, 2021), Dev Mode (developer handoff, 2023), Figma Slides (2024), and a doubling of the portfolio in 2025 with Figma Make (prompt-to-prototype), Figma Sites (design-and-publish), Figma Buzz (marketing assets), and Figma Draw (vector editing). It acquired Payload CMS and Weavy (now "Figma Weave," a multi-model AI editing canvas) and launched its own MCP server, allowing third-party AI agents to inspect and modify Figma files and generate code.

The commercial model is seat-based SaaS across a free-to-enterprise tier ladder, sold both bottoms-up (viral/organic adoption) and through a direct enterprise sales force. In 2025, Figma introduced AI credits across seats and, in March 2026, began enforcing credit limits with pay-as-you-go billing. The company operates as a single segment, is hosted entirely on AWS, and derives a majority of revenue internationally ($178.4M international vs $155.1M US in Q1). Co-founder and CEO Dylan Field controls approximately 72.3% of voting power through a dual-class structure, so minority holders have limited influence and no takeover-premium optionality.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is strong and consistent.** Q1 FY2026 revenue accelerated modestly against FY2025's +41%. Net dollar retention of 139% is best-in-class and rising. Customers above $10K ARR reached 15,218 (+37%) and above $100K ARR reached 1,525 (+48%), with no single customer above 10% of revenue. Deferred revenue was $627.7M and RPO $682.3M, giving strong forward visibility, with roughly 72% of revenue recurring off the prior deferred balance.

**The GAAP loss is an accounting artifact; margin erosion is the real signal.** The Q1 net loss (–$0.27/share) is almost entirely IPO-triggered SBC, with recognition having begun at the August 2025 IPO. A year earlier, pre-IPO, Figma posted +$44.9M of GAAP net income. The trend that matters is gross margin: it compressed from 91% (Q1'25) to 79% (Q1'26), with cost of revenue up 253% YoY on the higher AI inference and hosting costs. Management warns margins will remain under pressure and describes AI economics as "volatile and difficult to predict." This is the single most important financial metric to track: it is the mechanism through which the AI-disruption thesis becomes real in the numbers.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are strong.** Q1 operating cash flow was $97.3M (flat YoY despite absorbing a $56.1M bonus payout), and FCF margin fell to 27% from 41% on AI costs and investment. The company holds roughly $1.64B in liquidity ($405.7M cash plus $1.23B marketable securities), carries no debt, and has an undrawn $500M revolver (2030 maturity, easily-met $100M minimum-liquidity covenant). The –$1.59B accumulated deficit is SBC/IPO-driven, not operational burn. Any bankruptcy-risk screen flagging this company is a false negative distorted by SBC-driven negative EBIT.

One idiosyncrasy: Figma holds Bitcoin ($11.8M), a Bitcoin ETF ($57M), and USDC ($15.7M), which produced roughly $20M of unrealized/remeasurement losses in Q1. The charter also authorizes "Blockchain common stock," which introduces earnings noise but is immaterial to the thesis.

**Earnings track record supports estimate confidence.** Figma has beaten consensus in all four reported quarters (by 11%, 114%, 21%, and 56%), which lends credibility to the forward EPS path. Estimate revisions are mixed: current-year EPS is up 17.6% over 90 days (to $0.27) and next-year up 14.1% (to $0.33), but both have drifted down slightly over the trailing 30 days (–2.5% and –3.1%), and next-quarter estimates fell 4.6%. The 90-day trend is positive; the 30-day trend is flat-to-negative, so the momentum signal is not clean.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth with expanding net dollar retention is the strongest part of the story and would, in a different sentiment environment, command a premium multiple. Platform expansion is broadening the addressable market beyond core design: Make, Sites, Buzz, Weave, and the Payload CMS acquisition move Figma toward a product-building platform rather than a single-purpose design tool. The RPO and deferred revenue provide visibility, and the enterprise cohort ($100K+ ARR customers +48%) is expanding faster than the base, indicating land-and-expand is working.

The growth debate is entirely about durability under AI. The bull framing, echoed by BofA, Citi, Wells Fargo, JPMorgan, and RBC, is that AI increases the need for a shared enterprise platform to organize, govern, and ship AI-assisted work, and that AI credits monetize incremental usage without breaking the subscription core. Citi named Figma a key AI beneficiary in July 2026. The bear framing is that AI lets fewer people produce more, compressing seat counts, while auto-conversion of files and design systems lowers switching costs and agent-mediated workflows shift value outside Figma's UI.

The March 2026 enforcement of AI credit limits provides the first real-world data point, and it is mixed: it demonstrates a monetization lever, but it also produced disclosed elevated support volume, public dissatisfaction, and reduced usage by some customers. This is the pricing-transition risk materializing. Growth prospects are genuinely good in the near term but carry an unresolved structural question that caps conviction.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here — it is negative on SBC-driven GAAP losses. On non-GAAP economics, the picture is more nuanced. At ~$22 and roughly $9.5B market cap, backing out net liquidity gives an enterprise value near $7.9B, against a revenue run-rate approaching $1.33B (annualizing Q1) — roughly 6x forward revenue for a company growing 46% with 139% NDR. That is not expensive for the growth rate if the growth is durable; it is a full multiple if AI compresses seats and margins.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use: application-software comparables show a median P/E of 86 (GRAB), with several names negative (FROG –150, CHYM –5.7, TTAN –46.8) — the group is a mix of profitable and unprofitable growth stories, so a clean multiple comparison isn't available. The compressed valuation reflects a structural debate about the business model's survival, which is the value-trap pattern to treat with skepticism rather than a "cheap" signal.

Sell-side targets cluster at $30 (BofA), $36 (Citi, Wells Fargo), with a $38 high and a $22 low, mean $30.6. The targets sit meaningfully above price, and the sell-side frames the sell-off as overdone. I would not treat these as guaranteed floors, because the entire debate is whether the forward estimates that underpin them survive the AI transition — if seat monetization deteriorates, both the estimates and the targets move down together.

The honest valuation read is symmetric. If the seat-model-survives thesis proves out, re-rating toward the low-$30s is plausible on the sell-side math. If AI genuinely compresses seats and margins, ~6x forward revenue on decelerating, lower-margin growth could still be too high. That two-sided distribution supports a mid-range valuation score rather than a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

Figma retains a category-defining brand, strong network effects (multiplayer files, Community content, plugin ecosystem), and deep enterprise penetration. Its competitive trajectory has become genuinely uncertain, which is why the competitive-position assessment has been marked down from earlier reports (7.5 to 5.0 over the trajectory window). The threat is a fast-growing cohort of AI-native "prompt-to-product" entrants plus well-capitalized model providers who can bundle or subsidize AI functionality, not a legacy competitor gaining share.

The company's own MCP server, which lets third-party AI agents act through a single authenticated seat, cuts both ways: it positions Figma in the agentic workflow but also creates a channel that could erode per-seat monetization. Platform expansion (Make, Sites, Weave) is a credible offensive response that widens the moat if it works, but it is unproven at scale. Figma is a live referendum on whether vertical, seat-based SaaS survives the AI transition, and its moat is being tested rather than clearly widening or collapsing. That justifies a neutral score with a cautious lean.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile here is above-average and not fully priced, which is why this dimension scores low.

1. **AI seat-model erosion (the core bear thesis).** Management materially expanded this risk language in the Q1 10-Q, conceding a question about its pricing model in its own filings. This is a binary, hard-to-handicap risk.

2. **Gross margin compression, already underway.** Management won't commit to where margin stabilizes. Non-cancelable hosting/AI commitments exceed $465.9M, plus a new $50M AI-services commitment (May 2026).

3. **Pricing backlash, materialized.** The March 2026 credit-limit enforcement produced disclosed support-volume spikes, social-media dissatisfaction, and reduced usage.

4. **Anthropic/government dependency.** Figma built government AI features on Claude; the U.S. federal government flagged Anthropic as a supply-chain risk and directed agencies to cease Claude use (litigation ongoing). This directly threatens FedRAMP-driven public-sector expansion.

5. **Lock-up supply overhang.** The final tranche releases at the earlier of two trading days after Q2 earnings or August 31, 2026, against a small float — a concrete, dated technical pressure.

6. **OFAC voluntary self-disclosure** for inadvertent access from sanctioned jurisdictions, still under review (no loss accrued).

7. **Governance.** ~72.3% founder voting control means no takeover-premium optionality and no minority-holder recourse, given the terminated $20B Adobe bid.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 110.9% is rich versus 88.4% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (110.9% near vs 85.9% at six months), consistent with the Q2-earnings/lock-up catalyst window. ATM IV jumped 31.9 points (79.0% to 110.9%) over the past month as the stock rose 12.6%, indicating rising uncertainty rather than complacency. Near-term skew is call-heavy (–3.8% at the 1-week tenor, put/call volume ratio 0.41), and the single unusual-activity print was a bullish near-dated $22 call at 6.4x volume/OI, so near-term options positioning leans constructive. That is a mild caution for shorts and a mildly supportive tell for the dip-buyers. Short interest of 44.4% of float (4.4 days to cover) creates meaningful squeeze potential on any positive surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($32-38):** Q2 confirms continued 40%+ growth and stable-to-rising NDR; AI credit monetization proves accretive rather than dilutive to net revenue; gross margin stabilizes near 78-80% as inference costs scale sublinearly; the lock-up clears without a lasting overhang; and the seat-model-survives thesis gains evidence. Sell-side re-rates toward the low-to-mid $30s, and the short base amplifies the move. This maps to the high end of sell-side targets ($36-38).

**Bear Case ($12-15):** AI credit-limit backlash spreads into seat contraction and slowing NDR; gross margin continues to erode below 79%; the final lock-up tranche floods a thin float; and the market concludes seat-based design SaaS is structurally impaired. At ~6x forward revenue on decelerating, lower-margin growth, the multiple compresses further, and estimates and targets move down in tandem. Roughly 30-45% downside from current levels.

**Base Case (~$24):** Growth stays strong but the AI question remains unresolved through the next one to two quarters; margin pressure persists but does not accelerate; the lock-up creates volatility but not a permanent re-rating; and the stock oscillates in the low-to-mid $20s as the market waits for evidence. This is the most likely near-term outcome given the two-sided evidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business is strong enough that outright avoidance understates the fundamental quality, but the AI seat-model question, margin trajectory, and lock-up overhang are unresolved binary risks that prevent a BUY. The distribution of outcomes is two-sided, and I lack sufficient evidence to lean confidently either way.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 FY2026 earnings report (expected August, coinciding with the final lock-up trigger) is the decisive near-term catalyst. It will provide the first full quarter of data on the AI credit-limit enforcement's effect on seats and NDR, and on whether gross margin stabilizes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or improves (≥79%) in Q2 while revenue growth holds above 40%, indicating AI inference costs are scaling sublinearly.
- Net dollar retention holds at or above ~135% with no material seat contraction attributable to the March credit-limit changes, demonstrating AI monetization is accretive.
- The final lock-up clears without a sustained price break below the low-$20s, removing the supply overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin falls below ~76% or management guides to further structural compression with no stabilization path.
- NDR declines below ~130% or the company discloses accelerating seat/usage attrition from the pricing changes.
- Q2 revenue growth decelerates below ~35% YoY, signaling the AI-obsolescence thesis is materializing in the top line.

## Conclusion

Figma is a high-quality growth business (46% revenue growth, 139% NDR, $89M quarterly FCF, debt-free) trading at a valuation reset by a genuine, filing-acknowledged question about whether seat-based design SaaS survives the AI transition. The ~83% decline from peak has moved the risk/reward toward balanced rather than clearly favorable: the sell-side sees the sell-off as overdone and clusters targets at $30-37, but the gross-margin erosion (91% to 79% in a year), the disclosed pricing-change backlash, and the imminent lock-up supply are real, near-term risks that the market is right to weigh. The setup is a hold: the next earnings report and lock-up resolution, not the current price, should drive the next decision. The high short interest and call-heavy near-term options positioning suggest asymmetric upside on any positive surprise, but I would want evidence on margin stabilization and seat durability before upgrading.
