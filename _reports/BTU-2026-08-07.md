## Executive Summary

Peabody spent three years positioning for a metallurgical coal price recovery. In the first half of 2026 that recovery arrived — premium hard coking coal averaged $238/t in Q2, up 29% for the half — and Peabody's Seaborne Metallurgical segment lost money at the unit level. Revenue per ton rose 29% year over year to $148.04; cost per ton rose 30% to $155.08, producing a negative $7.04/ton margin. The reason is Centurion, the Queensland underground longwall that was supposed to be the growth engine: mechanical and electrical failures during commissioning constrained cutting speeds, which created roof-condition problems in a rock fault zone, which forced management to raise full-year Seaborne Met cost guidance by roughly $10/ton on July 29.

Simultaneously, the Powder River Basin posted a negative unit margin for the first time in the periods reviewed (revenue $13.63/ton against cost $14.06/ton) on volumes down 18%. Consolidated Q2 revenue rose 12.7% to $1,003.2M while Adjusted EBITDA fell 74% to $24.0M, and the GAAP loss of $0.74 per share came in 75.9% below GAAP consensus. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut from +$2.45 ninety days ago to -$0.01, a revision of a magnitude that removes the forward-earnings anchor entirely. Free cash flow was negative $77.7M in FY2025 and approximately negative $115.2M in H1 2026.

Against that, the June 2026 balance sheet work was the best executed quarter in the sector: a $250M 0.50% 2031 convertible placed at a 32.5% premium retired $241.2M of 3.25% 2028 notes and roughly 6.1M shares of potential dilution; the revolver was upsized to $400M and extended to June 2030; and A$700M of Australian surety commitments from Liberty Mutual and Swiss Re replaced fully cash-collateralized programs, releasing $384.3M of restricted cash. Peabody now holds roughly $180M of net cash, $959.1M of total liquidity, and has no debt maturity before March 2028. That resilience is real, and it is why this is not a solvency story. It is also not enough. Anglo American's June 5 Statement of Claim quantifies damages at $755.0M — $6.19 per share, 25.9% of market capitalization — carried with no accrual, no estimable range, and a final hearing 13 months out in September 2027. A company with negative free cash flow, widening operating losses, and an unreserved contingent claim of that size does not offer adequate compensation at 0.90x book. **AVOID.**

## Company Overview

Peabody operates 17 active mining operations (16 at 12/31/2025, plus Centurion's transition to production) across four reportable segments. FY2025 shape:

| Seaborne Thermal | $908.5M | 15.4Mt | $222.2M | $14.42 |

| Powder River Basin | $1,153.0M | 84.5Mt | $175.8M | $2.08 |

The structural asymmetry the company discloses directly: the two seaborne segments generated ~20% of volume but ~51% of coal-supply-agreement revenue, while the two U.S. thermal segments generated ~80% of volume for ~49% of revenue. Seaborne contributed 53.0% of pre-corporate mining EBITDA ($278.6M of $525.8M). Peabody is a thin-margin domestic utility supplier attached to a high-price-beta export business, and the export business is where the earnings variance lives.

Reserves are ~2.0 billion tons proven and probable. North Antelope Rochelle alone produced 65.0Mt in 2025 — 54% of consolidated output. Long-term supply agreements covered 87% of FY2025 volume (down from 90% and 92% in the prior two years), but within seaborne, 50% of 2025 volume sat under contracts of less than one year. The reported 238Mt sales backlog at 1/1/2026 (up from 153Mt) includes agreements subject to price reopeners and extensions; it is a tonnage commitment, not priced revenue, and should not be capitalized as such.

Five largest customers were 25% of FY2025 coal-supply revenue; the largest single customer contributed ~$291M (~8%) under contracts expiring in 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**The multi-year trajectory is unambiguous deterioration in earnings power on a stable asset base.**

| Operating cash flow | — | — | $606.5M | $333.7M |
| Capex | — | — | — | $411.4M |

Revenue fell 21.9% from FY2023 to FY2025. Adjusted EBITDA fell 47.8% in FY2025 alone. FY2025 free cash flow was negative $77.7M. The FY2025 operating loss contains no impairment charges — this is genuine margin compression plus $78.9M of expensed costs for the terminated Anglo American acquisition, of which $46.7M was bridge-loan commitment and duration fees. That is $78.9M of cash cost for a transaction that never closed and now carries a $755M counterclaim.

Segment margin decay FY2024 → FY2025: Seaborne Met EBITDA/ton fell from $22.19 to $6.57 (-70.4%); Other U.S. Thermal EBITDA fell from $150.8M to $71.4M. The one offset was PRB, where EBITDA rose from $138.6M to $175.8M, helped by roughly $19M realized from the OBBBA federal royalty reduction — though cost pass-through provisions recycled part of that benefit back to customers as lower revenue.

**H1 2026 accelerated the decay.** Revenue $1,976.5M (+8.2%) against Adjusted EBITDA of $106.5M versus $237.3M — a 55% decline on higher revenue. Q2 was worse than Q1 on every line: operating loss widened from -$44.2M to -$105.4M; net loss to common from -$32.4M to -$90.6M; Adjusted EBITDA from $82.5M to $24.0M. Within Q2, Corporate & Other swung from +$12.5M to -$30.9M, a $43.4M year-over-year deterioration against a $24.0M consolidated quarter that the disclosures do not attribute to a specific driver. On a quarter that small, an unexplained line of that size is material.

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $28.6M against $143.8M of PP&E additions, implying free cash flow of approximately -$115.2M. Inventories rose from $383.2M to $440.3M. Coal working capital and PRB volumes are strongly seasonal and management has guided to a materially stronger second half, so this should not be annualized, but it is the second consecutive period of cash consumption.

**The capital structure, by contrast, improved materially.** At 6/30/2026: cash $526.3M; total equity $3,297.9M ($3,248.6M attributable to PEC); gross debt $346.5M (2028 converts $78.8M, 2031 converts $250.0M, finance leases $17.7M). Net cash of roughly +$179.8M. Total liquidity $959.1M ($526.3M cash + $341.8M revolver availability + $91.0M A/R securitization availability), up from $942.1M at year-end.

Three June transactions drove this. The convertible exchange retired $241.2M of 3.25% 2028 notes (conversion ~$19.09) for $386.8M of consideration, funded by $250M of 0.50% 2031 notes struck at ~$38.32 plus cash, with $16.7M spent on capped calls (cap $50.6095) and $17.2M of induced conversion expense. Potential dilution fell from ~16.8M to ~10.7M shares — 8.7% of the 121.9M outstanding, or 8.0% on a post-issuance ownership basis. Net cash out above proceeds was ~$136.8M plus $16.7M of capped calls, or roughly $25.16 per share of dilution retired against a then-prevailing $28.92 VWAP, plus a ~$6.6M annual coupon saving. On that arithmetic it was accretive. The caveats: the capped call does not reduce GAAP diluted shares and only settles above $38.32; the 10.7M is maximum contingent dilution, and reported diluted EPS currently excludes 14.4M shares as anti-dilutive because of the loss position.

The revolver went from $320M to $400M with maturity extended from January 2028 to June 2030 at SOFR + 3.25–4.00%. The A$700M Australian surety facilities with Liberty Mutual and Swiss Re replaced fully cash-collateralized programs, cutting restricted cash and collateral from $844.1M to $459.8M. That release is a change in collateral form, not obligation: off-balance-sheet surety bonds rose from $997.2M to $1,276.4M, obligations supported net rose from $1,054.6M to $1,332.4M, and the asset retirement obligation was flat at $754.3M. Peabody now pays premiums to two insurers who guarantee reclamation performance, secured by liens on Australian assets, and inherits renewal risk in a market where the 10-K itself notes financial counterparties are exiting coal.

Despite the $384.3M collateral release, unrestricted cash still fell $49.0M over the half — the freed cash went to the convertible repurchase, $143.8M of capex, capped calls, issuance costs, $18.3M of dividends, and $7.7M of NCI distributions against $28.6M of operating cash flow.

Mechanical distress screens are unhelpful here. A properly specified Altman Z on FY2025 inputs computes to approximately 1.86 (working capital/TA 0.1234; retained earnings/TA 0.2335; EBIT/TA -0.0138; market equity/total liabilities 1.277; sales/TA 0.665), essentially at the 1.81 threshold, and roughly 1.9 on 6/30/2026 balances. The model's leverage term penalizes total liabilities without crediting cash, and Peabody's liabilities are dominated by long-dated non-financial obligations — $754.3M of ARO plus ~$1.74B of legacy benefit and reclamation outflows spread over decades ($110M in 2026, $90M in 2027, $85M in 2028, $1.3B+ thereafter). The score correctly flags that returns on assets have collapsed; it materially overstates near-term insolvency risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests entirely on Centurion plus a met price recovery, and Centurion is failing to convert.

Centurion began development in 2023, produced first development coal in June 2024, and commenced full-scale longwall production in February 2026. The Q2 10-Q discloses that during initial longwall commissioning the mine encountered mechanical and electrical issues which, once resolved, had left "constrained cutting speeds" that created "temporary roof condition challenges" still being worked at quarter end. The earnings release attributes this to a limited rock fault zone.

**FY2026 guidance is severely back-end loaded.** Reconciling the July 29 revised guidance against H1 actuals:

- **PRB**: FY 82.0–88.0Mt; H1 actual 37.6Mt → H2 implied 44.4–50.4Mt versus 44.9Mt in H2 2025. Q3 is guided at 22Mt, leaving Q4 needing 22.4–28.4Mt. Cost guidance of $12.00–12.50/ton against 80.8Mt already priced at $13.65 leaves a $1.15–1.65/ton margin. H1 delivered $16.6M of PRB EBITDA against an implied full-year $95–145M.
- **Seaborne Met**: FY 8.8–10.3Mt; H1 actual 4.5Mt; Q3 guided 1.9–2.1Mt (reduced for a Metropolitan longwall move and an expected Black Warrior River lock outage at Shoal Creek) → Q4 implied 2.2–3.9Mt against a Q2 that was already the strongest recent quarter at 2.5Mt. Segment EBITDA was -$24.0M in H1.

Building the H2 arithmetic from the Q3 guidance: PRB at 22Mt with $13.60 revenue and $11.75–12.25 cost gives $30–41M; Other U.S. Thermal at 3.7Mt with $58.20 revenue and $45–49 cost gives $34–49M; Seaborne Met at 2.0Mt at 70–75% of a PHCC now at $214 (~$150–160/ton) against $130–140 cost gives $20–60M; Seaborne Thermal at 3.0Mt with $52–57 cost plausibly $40–50M; Corporate at -$25 to -$31M. That frames Q3 at roughly $95–175M and a full year around $350–400M of Adjusted EBITDA — below FY2025's $454.9M and 55%+ below FY2024's $871.7M. Against $340M of guided capex and $65M of ARO cash spend, FY2026 free cash flow lands roughly flat to negative for a second consecutive year, before the $36.6M dividend.

**The demand backdrop has turned against the thermal book.** The FY2025 10-K described a supportive 2025: U.S. electricity demand +2%, stockpiles down 20Mt, retirement deferrals, AI/data-center narrative. The Q2 10-Q reverses it: H1 2026 U.S. electricity demand rose under 1%, coal generation *fell*, coal's share dropped from ~16% to ~14% while wind and solar reached 22%, and U.S. coal inventories rose almost 10Mt versus year-end. The cause is straightforward substitution — Henry Hub averaged $3.62/mmBtu in 2025 versus $2.41 in 2024, which is why 2025 PRB volumes were decent, and gas prices fell in H1 2026.

This is the most important dissonance in the file: U.S. coal policy is the most favorable in a decade — OBBBA royalty relief (~$19M realized in FY2025), metallurgical coal added to Section 45X at 2.5% of production costs (~$5M/year), BLM opening up to 13.1 million acres for lease, EPA's February 2026 rescission of the endangerment finding and repeal of subsequent GHG standards, extended effluent-limitation deadlines — and coal burn still declined. Regulatory relief did not translate into demand because the marginal fuel got cheaper and renewables grew. That materially discounts the weight the "AI/data-center demand" narrative deserves in the thermal thesis.

**The met price strength was supply-driven and is unwinding.** The 10-Q attributes H1 strength to a Shanxi Province mine accident that triggered Chinese safety inspections and production suspensions, tightening domestic Chinese coking coal and pulling in seaborne imports, while Indian demand moderated. PHCC stood at $243.50 on 6/30 and $214.00 by 8/3. Peabody sells at ~70–80% of the index FOB Australia, so a $29.50 index decline removes roughly $21–24/ton of realization at a segment whose Q2 cost was $155.08/ton.

Estimate revisions confirm the picture: current-quarter consensus is down 91.2% over 30 days and 93.1% over 90; current-year down from $2.45 to -$0.01; next year at $3.74, down 5.3% over 30 days on three analysts. The forward earnings anchor did not shift; it evaporated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.52 with 121.9M shares, market capitalization is $2,867M. Adding $346.5M of gross debt and deducting $526.3M of cash gives an enterprise value of approximately $2,687M.

- **EV / TTM Adjusted EBITDA ($324.1M): 8.3x.** Including the $754.3M ARO as debt-like, 10.6x.
- **EV / FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA: 6.0x.** EV / FY2024: 3.1x.
- **Price / book: 0.90x** ($23.52 against $26.65 book value per share).
- **Forward P/E on next-year consensus $3.74: 6.3x** — on an estimate from three analysts covering a year after a current-year estimate collapsed 100% in 90 days.
- **Dividend: $0.30 annualized, 1.28% yield**, ~$36.6M annual cash cost.
- $469.6M remains under the $1.0B repurchase authorization (16.4% of market cap), with **zero open-market repurchases in Q2 2026**.

The peer set supplied offers little relative signal — no coal comparables, and three of five peers carry negative trailing P/Es. The more useful test is what the current price implies. Coal producers with declining reserve life and large reclamation obligations have generally cleared at 3.5–5.5x EV/EBITDA. At 5.5x, today's $2,687M enterprise value implies roughly $490M of sustainable EBITDA. My H2 build points to $350–400M for FY2026 and requires Centurion to reach full run-rate plus PRB normalization to approach $490M. The equity already discounts a recovery beyond what the guidance delivers.

The 0.90x book multiple is the cheapness that draws attention, and it is the weakest leg of the bull case. Book value is being eroded by losses (retained earnings fell from $1,355.9M at year-end to $1,214.4M at 6/30/2026), the balance sheet carries $1,469.2M of valuation allowance against $1,616.5M of gross deferred tax assets — an explicit statement that management does not expect to earn into U.S. tax attributes — and 25.9% of market capitalization sits exposed to an unreserved claim. Book value on assets whose earnings power has halved twice in two years is not a floor.

Analyst targets run $26.50 low, $30.50 median, $36.00 high, with a 4 buy / 2 hold split unchanged for four months. Every target sits above spot, which is unusual and, given Benchmark's July 30 target cut on production issues and the -100% current-year revision, suggests the target set is stale rather than that it represents a floor. On a stock where consensus earnings just went to zero, targets last refreshed against a $2.45 EPS assumption carry little information.

**Base case $23.50** — roughly spot, applying ~5.5x to a normalized $500M EBITDA net of the cash position, which is what the market is already paying for. **Bull case $34** — Centurion reaches 4Mt+ at competitive cost, PHCC holds above $230, Anglo is dismissed or settles cheaply, and the buyback resumes into the $469.6M authorization; roughly 8.5x FY2027 EPS of $4.00 or 5x on $800M of EBITDA. **Bear case $16** — Centurion continues to disappoint, PHCC settles near $180, PRB Q4 volumes miss on a mild winter, and Anglo produces a material accrual; approximately 4x on $350M of EBITDA, near the $15.67 52-week low.

Probability-weighting bear 38% / base 40% / bull 22% gives an expected return of roughly -2%: modest upside skew in magnitude, unfavorable in probability, and with a 32% drawdown in the adverse case. That does not clear a HOLD bar, let alone ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

**Thermal.** Peabody's PRB position is structurally advantaged within a structurally shrinking market. North Antelope Rochelle is the largest single mine in the basin at 65.0Mt, weighted-average quality ~8,730 BTU. But the economics are thin to the point of fragility: guided 2026 unit margin of $1.15–1.65/ton on ~85Mt. A 10% volume shortfall or a $0.50/ton cost overrun eliminates the segment's contribution, and Q2 demonstrated exactly that when mild weather and extended plant maintenance outages produced -$0.43/ton. Management notes it kept the equipment fleet fully utilized removing overburden in advance of "higher expected volumes" in H2, pulling cost forward against a volume recovery that has not yet materialized. Roughly 3 GW of U.S. coal capacity retired in 2025 and U.S. coal capacity is down ~46% since 2010.

**Metallurgical.** Peabody competes against BHP, Glencore, Anglo American, Stanmore, Whitehaven, Yancoal, and Core Natural Resources on quality, delivered cost, service, and reliability of supply. Reliability of supply is precisely the attribute Centurion's commissioning has called into question, at the moment Peabody needed to establish it with new customers. Anglo American is simultaneously a named competitor and the arbitration counterparty. Longer-term, electric arc furnace steelmaking removes coking coal from the input mix, NSW's Coal Industry 2026–50 policy precludes new standalone greenfield mines in the state (constraining reserve replacement at Wilpinjong, Wambo, and Metropolitan), Queensland's progressive royalty tiers remain (20%/30%/40% above A$175/225/300 per tonne), Australia's Safeguard Mechanism requires 4.9% annual baseline declines to 2030, and June 2024 NGER amendments push open-cut mines onto fugitive-methane methods the 10-K expects will increase Queensland Safeguard liabilities by an amount not yet quantified. "Same job same pay" labour-hire orders already cover Coppabella and Metropolitan and have raised operating cost.

**Two genuine competitive assets.** First, capital markets access. The 10-K states plainly that banks, insurers, and large investors have curtailed relationships with fossil-fuel companies, adversely affecting coal producers' liquidity. Securing A$700M of surety commitments from Liberty Mutual and Swiss Re, upsizing and extending the revolver to 2030, and placing $250M of 0.50% converts at a 32.5% premium — all inside one month — is a demonstration of access most peers could not match. Second, safety: a FY2025 global incidence rate of 0.71 per 200,000 hours, a company record for the second consecutive year and 76% better than the 2024 U.S. industry average of 2.96. That matters commercially and legally given Queensland and NSW industrial manslaughter statutes.

Neither asset offsets the trajectory. Competitive position is deteriorating on the metrics that matter: unit margins, cost guidance direction, share of U.S. generation, and demonstrated operational reliability at the growth asset.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Anglo American arbitration — $755M unreserved, 25.9% of market cap.** Purchase agreements were signed 11/25/2024 for Anglo's steelmaking coal assets. An ignition event at Moranbah North on 3/31/2025 led Peabody to issue a material adverse change notice and terminate on 8/19/2025. Anglo initiated ICC arbitration in London on 9/23/2025 and, on 6/5/2026, filed a Statement of Claim quantifying damages at approximately $755.0M — $628.4M as the claimed difference in the assets' value between signing and termination, the remainder costs and interest. Peabody disputes liability, calls the calculation "fundamentally flawed," discloses that a loss is "reasonably possible," and states it cannot estimate a range. Final hearing: September 2027. That is $6.19 per share of unreserved, unquantifiable exposure resolving beyond any reasonable forecast horizon, and it is the reason a net-cash balance sheet should not be treated as unencumbered — it plausibly explains why buyback authorization went unused in a quarter when the stock traded below book. In fairness, a claim amount is an adversary's assertion rather than an expected value, and Anglo's valuation-differential methodology requires establishing that no MAC occurred across a period when met prices themselves moved substantially.

**2. Securities class action.** Filed 6/25/2026 in the Eastern District of Missouri on behalf of purchasers from 10/14/2024 through 5/4/2026, alleging §10(b) and §20(a) violations regarding statements about the ability to resume longwall production at Centurion. The class period ends the day before the Q1 2026 release, indicating plaintiffs treat that release as the corrective disclosure. No loss estimate.

**3. Guidance concentration risk.** Q4 must deliver 22.4–28.4Mt of PRB volume and 2.2–3.9Mt of Seaborne Met against a Q2 that produced 16.4Mt and 2.5Mt respectively. Both depend on conditions outside management's control (winter weather, roof conditions, Black Warrior River lock outages).

**4. Reclamation and surety dependence.** U.S. bonding requirements $878.6M and Australian $346.1M at 12/31/2025 against GAAP ARO of $754.9M; $1,276.4M of surety bonds outstanding at 6/30/2026 supported by ~$355M of restricted cash. Self-bonding is not permitted in Australia. The June shift to third-party sureties improved liquidity but created renewal dependence on insurers underwriting coal reclamation.

**5. Fixed obligations independent of volume.** Take-or-pay rail and port commitments of ~$1.0B with terms up to 19 years (~$113M due 2026, $115M in 2027). Legacy benefit and reclamation outflows of ~$110M in 2026 declining to $65–90M annually through 2030 with $1,318M thereafter. Black lung excise tax expense was $54.6M in FY2025, with a flagged possibility of ~$40M of additional security.

**6. Input and rate exposure.** 95–105M gallons of diesel over the next twelve months; a $10/bbl crude move is ~$24M of annual fuel cost. AUD exposure of ~$220–230M per $0.10 move, hedged to limit the adverse case to ~$178M. A 1pp decline in rates costs ~$9M of interest income, a reminder that a meaningful slice of current pretax income is cash yield rather than operations.

**7. Deferred tax and disclosure gaps.** $1,469.2M valuation allowance against $1,616.5M of gross DTAs. FY2025 recorded $8.8M of tax expense on a pretax loss; H1 2026 recorded a $53.0M benefit — most plausibly Australian losses generating recognizable benefits while U.S. losses do not, though that is inference. Both the 10-K and 10-Q retain a risk factor about a "significant stockholder" whose interests could conflict with other stakeholders; the holder was not identifiable within the research budget and should not be treated as established either way.

**8. Q3 optics.** Effective 12/31/2026, Peabody will discontinue a medical reimbursement benefit for certain employees and retirees, reducing a ~$21.6M net obligation in Q3 2026. That is a non-cash gain that will flatter Q3 GAAP results. Readers should strip it before judging whether the H2 recovery is real.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($34, +45%)

Q2 was the trough. Management framed it as such, and the mechanics support the framing: PRB volumes were suppressed by mild weather and plant maintenance while the fleet pre-stripped overburden, so Q3's guided 22Mt at $11.75–12.25/ton cost restores a positive $1.35–1.85/ton margin on 34% more volume. Centurion's rock fault zone is by definition finite; once through it, cutting speeds normalize and Seaborne Met unit costs fall toward the $130 low end against realizations of $150–160/ton at even a $214 PHCC. Seaborne Thermal is already performing — $52.1M of Q2 EBITDA on 17% lower volume at $95.87/ton export realization, with costs at the low end of guidance. The balance sheet is the cleanest in the sector: net cash, $959.1M liquidity, no maturity before March 2028, a revolver to 2030, and A$700M of surety capacity that most coal peers could not source. If Anglo is dismissed or settles for a fraction of $755M, $469.6M of buyback authorization against a $2.87B market cap becomes available at 0.90x book, and FY2027 consensus of $3.74 puts the stock at 6.3x. Policy is the most supportive in a decade, and any winter that tightens gas reverses the H1 substitution.

### Bear Case ($16, -32%)

The met price recovery already happened and Peabody lost money on it. That is the falsifiable fact the bull case must overcome. The supply shock that lifted PHCC 29% — a Shanxi mine accident triggering Chinese inspections — has begun unwinding, taking the index from $243.50 to $214.00 in five weeks, which removes $21–24/ton of realization from a segment whose Q2 cost was $155.08. U.S. thermal demand is deteriorating under the most coal-favorable policy in a decade, with coal generation down, share falling from 16% to 14%, and inventories up 10Mt: the substitution ratio, not regulation, sets coal burn, and gas got cheaper. PRB's guided $1.15–1.65/ton margin has no absorption capacity for volume or cost variance, and Q4 must deliver 22.4–28.4Mt on weather Peabody does not control. FY2026 free cash flow is likely negative for a second year even if guidance is met. Above all, $755M of unreserved claim equal to 25.9% of market cap sits unresolved until September 2027, freezing the buyback and capping the multiple. Consensus current-year EPS went from +$2.45 to -$0.01 in 90 days; three of the last four quarters missed, two of them by more than 50%. At 8.3x TTM EBITDA (10.6x including ARO), the stock is not compensating for any of it.

**Re-entry criteria.** Any of the following would move the rating to HOLD or better:

**Timeline.** The headwinds should be expected to persist through at least the February 2027 full-year report. ## Conclusion

Peabody has done impressive financial engineering into a deteriorating operating result. The June 2026 quarter delivered a convertible refinancing that retired roughly 6.1M shares of dilution at an implied $25.16 per share, a revolver upsized to $400M and extended to 2030, and A$700M of Australian surety capacity that released $384.3M of restricted cash — in a market where the company's own 10-K notes that banks and insurers are exiting coal. Net cash of roughly $180M, $959.1M of liquidity, and no maturity before March 2028 mean this is not a distress situation, whatever a mechanically computed Altman Z of ~1.9 implies.

But the operating business moved the other way at the exact moment it needed to convert. Q2 revenue rose 12.7% while Adjusted EBITDA fell 74%, with Seaborne Met at -$7.04/ton and the Powder River Basin at -$0.43/ton simultaneously. Free cash flow was -$77.7M in FY2025 and approximately -$115.2M in H1 2026, and my reconstruction of guided H2 economics points to a second consecutive negative free cash flow year even if guidance is met. Consensus current-year EPS fell from +$2.45 to -$0.01 in ninety days. The met price strength that drove H1 realizations came from a Chinese supply disruption that is unwinding, with PHCC down from $243.50 to $214.00. U.S. thermal demand declined in H1 under the most favorable coal policy in a decade, which should discount how much weight the data-center demand narrative deserves.

Layered on top is a $755M quantified arbitration claim — $6.19 per share, 25.9% of market capitalization — carried with no accrual, no estimable range, and a hearing thirteen months out, which appears to be freezing $469.6M of buyback authorization at a share price below book. At $23.52, an enterprise value of $2,687M is 8.3x trailing Adjusted EBITDA (10.6x including the $754.3M asset retirement obligation) and implies roughly $490M of sustainable EBITDA against a FY2026 trajectory nearer $350–400M. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately -2% with a 32% drawdown in the adverse case and no dated resolution of the largest overhang inside a year.

The 0.90x book multiple is the attraction, and it is the wrong reason to own this. Book value is eroding through losses, $1,469.2M of valuation allowance says management does not expect to earn into U.S. tax attributes, and a quarter of the equity value is exposed to an unreserved claim. **AVOID.** Revisit at $17–18, or on two consecutive quarters of positive Seaborne Met unit margin, or on Anglo resolution.
