## Executive Summary

Allison Transmission has become a different company in eight months. The January 1, 2026 all-cash purchase of Dana's Off-Highway Drive & Motion Systems business for a final $2,628M doubled revenue, tripled headcount, added roughly 46 plants across 22 countries — and cut the consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin from 37.5% in FY2025 to 25.8% in the first half of 2026. Both facts are central. The acquired business earns a 14.6% segment adjusted EBITDA margin against the legacy transmission franchise's 37.3%, so a business that used to screen as an unusually high-margin niche monopolist now screens as a diversified mid-margin industrial. The multiple compressed accordingly.

The relevant question is whether the compression went too far. At $121.70 the shares trade at 9.1x FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA, 12.3x the $9.89 consensus current-year EPS, 10.9x the $11.20 next-year figure, and an 8.0% adjusted free cash flow yield on guided FCF of $805M. The peer median trailing P/E in construction machinery and heavy transportation equipment is 24.1x (Oshkosh 17.8x, GATX 17.6x, Rush 24.1x, Federal Signal 27.6x, Terex 40.2x). Three catalysts are dated rather than speculative: roughly $140M of genuinely non-recurring purchase-accounting and integration charges roll off after 2026, mechanically lifting GAAP earnings; deleveraging from 2.44x net debt/EBITDA toward roughly 2.0x within twelve months at guided cash generation would free the $1,125M of remaining buyback authorization (11% of market capitalization); and the $120M synergy target equals about 7.9% of guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA. Q2 delivered record transmission-segment sales of $860M, record adjusted FCF of $281M, a 4.1% EPS beat, and a guidance raise across every metric.

The counterweights are real enough to keep this short of a full position. Legacy transmission gross margin fell from 49.5% to 46.2% year over year in Q2 — gross profit declined $6M on $46M more revenue — because tariff and raw-material inflation is arriving faster than the six-to-twelve-month contractual pass-through recovers it. Tangible book equity is approximately negative $2,440M after $4,419M of goodwill and intangibles. Purchase accounting remains preliminary, with measurement-period adjustments still moving through the balance sheet and the acquired unit carved out of internal-control attestation for a full year. And the cyclical leg of the growth story — construction, material handling, mining and especially agriculture, which management says "has yet to inflect positively" — sits directly under an uncertain policy rate environment. We rate ALSN **ACCUMULATE** with a $148 twelve-month base case (+21.6%), a $180 bull case and a $94 bear case (-22.8%), for a probability-weighted expected return in the mid-teens.

## Company Overview

Allison, founded in 1915 and headquartered in Indianapolis, is the largest manufacturer of fully automatic transmissions for medium- and heavy-duty commercial vehicles and for medium- and heavy-tactical U.S. defense vehicles. Products reach end users through roughly 1,500 independent distributor and dealer locations.

FY2025 revenue of $3,010M split as North America On-Highway $1,540M (51%), Outside North America On-Highway $507M (17%), Global Off-Highway legacy $53M (2%), Defense $267M (9%) and Service Parts/Support Equipment/Other $643M (21%). North America generated roughly 76% of the total.

The moat has an unusual mechanism. Allison markets to fleet operators and municipalities, who then specify Allison by name in vehicles ordered from OEMs. That specification pull-through is a structural defense against OEM vertical integration in a way that ordinary component supply is not. Reinforcing it: OEMs representing over 90% of North American on-highway unit volume participate in three-to-five-year long-term agreements with defined commodity-cost sharing; Allison deliberately avoids the Class 8 line-haul tractor market where cheaper manuals and automated manuals suffice, insulating it from the freight cycle in favor of vocational demand (refuse, fire, utility, school bus, construction); and it supplies substantially all propulsion for U.S. medium- and heavy-tactical wheeled vehicles plus the Abrams, M10 Booker and related tracked platforms, a relationship dating to the 1940s.

One qualification worth carrying: many key patents are licensed from General Motors, not owned. The license is perpetual, royalty-free and exclusive within defined vocational weight classes, but GM controls maintenance and enforcement, and the 10-K states that a number of the licensed patents expire in the next few years.

The acquired Off-Highway business supplies axles, gearboxes, transmissions and motion-control systems into construction, agriculture, mining, material handling and industrial equipment. It moves Allison from selling a component to selling systems, raising content per machine — into end markets where Caterpillar, Komatsu and Volvo build their own transmissions. The 10-K states the point plainly: "we now face competition in these expanded end markets."

## Financial Analysis

**Pre-acquisition base.** Revenue ran $2,769M (2022), $3,035M (2023), $3,225M (2024), $3,010M (2025) — a 2.8% three-year CAGR. This was never a compounder; it was a high-margin annuity with cyclical volume. FY2025 was a down year (revenue -6.7%, net income -14.8% to $623M), but gross margin *expanded* to a period-high 48.6% during the decline, which is the cleanest single evidence of pricing discipline in the file. Operating margin slipped to 29.2% as SG&A absorbed roughly $48M of transaction costs and R&D fell $26M to $174M on reduced electrification spending. FY2025 generated $836M of operating cash flow, $175M of capex and $661M of adjusted FCF.

**Post-acquisition H1 2026.** Net sales $2,972M versus $1,580M; gross margin 31.0% versus 49.4%; GAAP net income $293M versus $387M (-24%); adjusted diluted EPS $5.30 versus $4.88 (+8.6%); adjusted EBITDA $766M versus $609M; adjusted FCF $384M versus $308M. Segment adjusted EBITDA: Transmission $594M on $1,593M (37.3%), Off-Highway $202M on $1,379M (14.6%), Central Group Function -$30M.

The GAAP decline decomposes cleanly: interest expense net rose $72M ($43M to $115M), purchase accounting added roughly $135M of pre-tax charges (inventory step-up $63M, step-up depreciation $31M, incremental amortization ~$41M), a marketable-securities mark swung about $17M unfavorably, and Central Group costs rose $19M. None of that reflects operating deterioration in the acquired asset; it reflects mandatory acquisition accounting plus a levered balance sheet.

**The sequential trajectory is the most useful signal.** Deriving Q1 from H1 minus Q2: consolidated sales $1,406M then $1,566M; Off-Highway segment operating profit -$21M then +$47M; Off-Highway adjusted EBITDA $98M then $104M; adjusted FCF $103M then $281M. Transmission-segment sales moved from -4.3% year over year in Q1 to +5.7% in Q2, and North America On-Highway from roughly -13.8% to +3.1%. Defense grew 60% in H1 to $186M. The legacy business turned mid-year and the acquired business crossed into GAAP profitability in its second quarter of ownership.

**FY2026 guidance, raised August 3:** net sales $5,800-6,000M, GAAP net income $600-700M, adjusted EBITDA $1,465-1,575M, operating cash flow $1,025-1,125M, capex $260-280M, adjusted FCF $745-865M. Embedded: roughly $140M of one-time pre-tax separation/integration/restructuring expense including a ~$75M inventory step-up, roughly $50M of additional step-up depreciation, interest expense $210-220M, amortization $80M. Guidance has progressed from $5,575-5,925M in February to the current $5,800-6,000M, a ~$150M midpoint increase.

Implied H2 revenue of $2,828-3,028M against H1's $2,972M looks flat, which is consistent with the 10-K's disclosure that North American truck production is first-half weighted because of July and December plant shutdowns. We anchor valuation on guidance rather than the H1 run rate for that reason.

**Balance sheet.** Cash fell from $1,495M to $399M; total debt rose to $4,114M net of deferred financing costs; net debt $3,715M; total assets $8,717M including $2,812M goodwill and $1,607M net intangibles; stockholders' equity $1,979M. Net debt/FY26E adjusted EBITDA is 2.44x. Adjusted EBITDA/interest expense is 7.1x. Company-reported first-lien net leverage is 1.01x against a 5.50x covenant maximum that is only tested when the revolver carries a quarter-end balance, which it does not — the $150M balance was retired in Q2, leaving $995M available. The earliest maturity is $400M of 4.75% notes in October 2027. The committed $2,000M bridge facility was never drawn. Ratings: Ba1/BB+ corporate with investment-grade term loans.

Two genuine quality-of-balance-sheet concerns. Goodwill and intangibles of $4,419M equal 51% of total assets and 223% of book equity, implying tangible book equity of roughly negative $2,440M. And Off-Highway H1 capex of $20M ran below its estimated base depreciation of roughly $29M (total segment depreciation $60M less $31M of step-up), a 0.69x ratio, versus 4.0% of sales at the legacy segment against Off-Highway's 1.5%. First-year carve-out capital approval processes are frequently back-half weighted, and FY26 guidance includes roughly $30M of one-time integration capex, so this is a hypothesis rather than a finding — but if the acquired asset base is being under-replaced, current free cash flow is flattered and catch-up spending is deferred, not avoided.

**Valuation arithmetic at $121.70.** EV/FY26E adjusted EBITDA 9.1x. GAAP P/E 15.7x on $650M/84M diluted shares. Adjusted P/E 11.8x on roughly $10.32 of adjusted EPS (GAAP $650M plus $275M of pre-tax add-backs taxed at ~21%). On a stricter basis that adds back only the roughly $140M of genuinely non-recurring items — treating $80M of acquired-intangible amortization, $50M of step-up depreciation and $30M of stock compensation as real economic costs — adjusted EPS is about $9.06, or 13.4x. Consensus at $9.89 sits between the two, which is where it belongs. Adjusted FCF yield is 8.0% on equity and 5.8% on EV; excluding the roughly $55M of one-time cash outlays, normalized FCF of about $860M is an 8.6% equity yield. The dividend of $1.16 annualized yields 0.95% and consumes only about 12% of guided FCF.

We note a data inconsistency: the profile feed reports both trailing and forward P/E at 18.87 against stated EPS of $7.33, which does not reconcile at $121.70 ($121.70/$7.33 = 16.6x). All multiples in this report are computed from guidance, consensus and the filings rather than from that field.

## Growth Analysis

The FY2026 revenue near-doubling is entirely inorganic and tells you nothing about growth quality. Three organic legs matter.

**Defense (+60% in H1 to $186M) is the highest-quality leg.** It is long-cycle, high-barrier, aftermarket-rich (Abrams sustainment parts kits), fielded in roughly 120 countries, and largely uncorrelated with the truck and construction cycles. The 10-K describes increasing supply into international tracked programs — armored fighting vehicles, heavy tracked artillery, main battle tanks — which maps onto the multi-front demand environment. The specific "major contract wins" referenced in Q2 call coverage were not verified in a filing; the 60% revenue growth was.

**Off-Highway cyclical recovery is corroborated by management but incomplete.** Management describes Construction & Material Handling and Mining demand as "rebounding from trough levels," with H1 splits of Construction & Material Handling $476M, Agriculture $306M, Service Parts/Specialty/Other $304M, Industrial $189M, Mining $104M. Agriculture, roughly 22% of Off-Highway H1 revenue, has not inflected. That is both the largest remaining upside option and the reason the recovery leg is not yet proven.

**Legacy on-highway is stabilizing, not accelerating.** Q2's +5.7% segment growth and +3.1% North America On-Highway growth reversed a weak Q1, but Outside North America On-Highway declined 5% in H1 and the decade-old thesis that manual-to-automatic conversion arrives outside North America remains unrealized — the 10-K concedes "there can be no assurance that adoption of automatic transmissions will increase."

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus rose 4.6% over 90 days to $9.89 and next-year 0.6% to $11.20, with both essentially flat over 30 days and the current quarter trimmed 2.7%. The aggregate feed label of "estimates broadly falling" overstates it: the pattern is upward revision through the spring on the guidance-raise path, now flat, with a current-quarter trim consistent with disclosed second-half seasonality. This is stabilization, not the +30% ninety-day inflection that would constitute a standalone buy signal. Consensus implies about 13% EPS growth in 2027, sourced from step-up roll-off, synergy capture and interest reduction rather than volume.

Against these, the terminal risk is electrification eroding the aftermarket annuity — the highest-margin 21% of legacy revenue. Currently that threat is receding: Allison impaired electrified-product assets in FY2025 and another $2M in Q2 2026, cut R&D from $200M to $174M, and its Jing-Jin Electric stake fell from $24M to $16M. Near-term favorable, but it means capital already deployed into eGen products is earning below plan.

## Valuation Assessment

The gap to peers is wide and the reasons for part of it are identifiable. At 12.3x current-year consensus versus a 24.1x peer median trailing multiple, ALSN carries roughly a 50% discount. Adjusting for quality, the appropriate comparators are Oshkosh at 17.8x and GATX at 17.6x rather than Terex at 40.2x; ALSN still trades at a 30%+ discount to those.

What justifies part of the discount: 2.8% pre-deal revenue CAGR; a consolidated margin structure now roughly 1,170bps below the legacy business; 2.44x net leverage against peers with lighter balance sheets; negative tangible book equity; and an acquired earnings base whose mid-cycle power has not been observed under Allison ownership.

What the market appears to be underweighting:

1. **The step-up roll-off is mechanical.** Roughly $140M of pre-tax one-time charges in FY26 — inventory step-up $75M, acquisition expenses ~$40M, restructuring ~$25M — do not repeat. On the guidance midpoint of $650M GAAP net income, that is roughly $1.30 of after-tax EPS returning in 2027 without any operational improvement.
2. **The acquisition multiple.** $2,628M against annualized H1 Off-Highway adjusted EBITDA of $404M is roughly 6.5x, and roughly 5.0x with the full $120M synergy target. Only 29% of the price became goodwill; $867M went to PP&E and $876M to identifiable intangibles. Management bought a cyclical asset it describes as rebounding from trough. The multiple annualizes one seasonal half-year and excludes any Central Group allocation, so treat it as a sensitivity rather than a precise figure — but even at 8x the price would have been defensible.
3. **Capital-return optionality.** At $805M of guided adjusted FCF, roughly $96M of dividends and modest buybacks, Allison can retire $600M+ of debt annually, reaching roughly 2.0x net leverage within twelve months. The remaining $1,125M authorization is 11% of market capitalization.

**Base case $148.** Applying 9.5x to FY27E adjusted EBITDA of $1,625M (FY26 midpoint $1,520M plus partial synergy capture and modest volume) gives $15,438M of EV; less net debt of roughly $3,150M after a year of paydown equals $12,288M, or $149 per share on 82.5M shares. Cross-check: 13.2x the $11.20 next-year consensus is $148. That is +21.6% from $121.70 and 6.1% above the $139.40 analyst mean.

**Bull case $180.** 10.5x on $1,725M of FY27 adjusted EBITDA (agriculture inflects, full $120M synergies, legacy margin recovers as tariff pass-through catches up) less $2,950M net debt, on a share count reduced by resumed repurchase, approaches the $185 analyst high. +47.9%.

**Bear case $94.** 8.0x on $1,425M (industrial recession, agriculture stays trough, legacy gross margin compression persists, integration costs overrun) less $3,550M net debt gives roughly $95. -22.8%, and 10% below the $105 analyst low.

Probability-weighted at 55/20/25 the expected return is about +15.8% plus a 0.95% dividend. That is a positive expected return with moderate confidence and a modest margin of safety — the shares are 11.6% below the April 27 high of $137.62 and 60% above the October 30 low of $76.01. It supports staged accumulation rather than a full initiation.

## Competitive Landscape

Allison faces three distinct rival classes. Independent transmission makers: ZF, Driventic GmbH, Shaanxi Fast Gear, Ford, BAE Systems in transit hybrids. Vertically integrated OEMs: pervasive in Western Europe and South America, and in off-highway Caterpillar, Komatsu and Volvo each build the automatics in their own mining trucks. Alternative technology: automated manuals, manuals, and electric architectures that may not require a transmission at all. In off-highway energy, electric fracturing structurally erodes the legacy FracTran franchise.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving. Supporting stability: specification pull-through, LTAs over 90% of North American volume, near-sole-source defense positions, a growing installed base feeding a $643M aftermarket, and content-per-machine expansion from component to system supply. Working against it: disclosed top-five customer concentration flat at 52% (2025), 55% (2024), 52% (2023) with Daimler at roughly 18%, PACCAR 11% and Traton 10%; the 10-K's explicit acknowledgment that customers "manufacture or could in the future manufacture transmissions"; supplier concentration with roughly 40 single-source suppliers accounting for 75% of component spend and cost recovery lagging six to twelve months; GM-licensed patents beginning to expire; and new competition in the expanded off-highway markets.

One point deserves discipline. Segment and geographic mix diversified sharply — Off-Highway was 45% of Q2 sales, and roughly 14 manufacturing countries were added. But all disclosed customer-concentration data is pre-close FY2025, and it was flat to worse across three years. Mix diversification is not verified customer diversification. Off-highway OEM markets are themselves concentrated among Caterpillar, Deere, Komatsu, CNH and Volvo, so one concentration may simply replace another. The FY2026 10-K will settle it.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration.** The FY2025 10-K added an entirely new risk section covering integration cost, integration failure, failure to realize anticipated benefits, and managing expanded operations. The scale is meaningful: roughly 8,000 employees added of whom only about 8% are U.S.-based, 46 plants, 22 countries, 68% of acquired U.S. employees unionized. Management's disclosure-controls evaluation for Q2 excluded Allison Off-Highway under the newly-acquired-business accommodation, and the FY2026 ICFR report will also exclude it — a roughly $2.8B-revenue unit will operate a full year without auditor-attested internal control opinion.

**Legacy margin compression.** Transmission-segment Q2 gross margin of 46.2% ($397M on $860M) versus 49.5% for all-legacy Q2 2025 means gross profit fell $6M on $46M more revenue. Allocating Central Group's Q2 adjusted-EBITDA drag pro rata by sales suggests roughly 270bps of segment EBITDA compression. Management attributes it to unfavorable direct material costs and higher manufacturing expense partly offset by price. Given the disclosed six-to-twelve-month recovery lag, this should mean-revert if input costs stabilize — but the 2025-to-2026 comparison is not clean because the Central Group carve-out changed the cost base, and part of the decline may be mix as defense grew 57%.

**Impairment and preliminary accounting.** $4,419M of goodwill and intangibles against $1,979M of book equity, with goodwill not yet allocated to reporting units and the measurement period running to January 1, 2027. Adjustments already recorded (PP&E +$24M, intangibles -$54M, goodwill -$8M) show the figures are still moving, and FY26 net income guidance is expressly subject to completion of purchase accounting.

**Cyclicality and macro.** Agriculture, mining and construction are explicitly named as high-cyclicality end markets, and they now represent roughly 45% of revenue. A higher-for-longer interest-rate environment would suppress equipment financing and purchase decisions in these end markets.

**Labor and FX.** The UAW Local 933 agreement expires November 2027 — dated and concrete, covering 43% of legacy employees. FX exposure escalated with the acquisition; Allison holds $1,000M of euro net-investment cross-currency swaps, and H1 accumulated other comprehensive loss widened from -$56M to -$134M, mostly on $106M of translation.

**Electrification.** The 10-K is candid that increased electric adoption would reduce demand for transmissions and, over time, related service parts. Currently retreating, but this is the terminal-value question.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 95 contracts across four expirations — so read it with caution. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.0% against 29.9% one-year realized is rich, with backwardation (40.0% at ten days versus 35.6% at six months) and a 10.3% near-term put skew. That combination normally signals imminent event risk, but earnings already cleared on August 3, which weakens the interpretation. Positioning cuts the other way: put/call volume ratio 0.07 and put/call open interest 0.28 are heavily call-tilted, and the six-month skew inverts to -4.2%. Short interest is 6.6% of float at 2.5 days to cover — modest, uncrowded, no squeeze dynamic. The honest read is thin-market noise in near-dated quotes with longer-dated positioning mildly constructive; nothing here challenges the fundamental case, and the rich near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting a reasonable adjunct for existing holders rather than a warning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($180, +47.9%)

Allison bought a cyclical asset at roughly 6.5x trough EBITDA, in cash, with no dilution, and financed it without drawing the bridge. Agriculture — 22% of Off-Highway H1 revenue and explicitly not yet inflected — recovers alongside construction, mining and material handling, which are already rebounding. The $120M synergy target lands, adding 7.9% to EBITDA. Off-Highway's Q1-to-Q2 operating profit swing from -$21M to +$47M continues as purchase-accounting charges roll off, and legacy transmission gross margin recovers the 270-330bps lost to tariffs as contractual pass-through catches up. Defense compounds at a double-digit rate on international tracked programs. Net leverage reaches the target by mid-2027, the $1,125M authorization is deployed, and the multiple re-rates from 9.1x toward the 10-11x range appropriate for a diversified industrial generating an 8% FCF yield. FY27 adjusted EBITDA of $1,725M at 10.5x on a reduced share count supports the $185 high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case ($94, -22.8%)

Off-highway construction, agriculture and mining demand stalls as higher-for-longer rates suppress equipment financing; the "rebound from trough" proves a false start, and the 14.6% acquired margin turns out to be near the top of its range rather than depressed. Legacy gross margin compression persists because tariff costs recur faster than annual LTA resets recover them, and the mix shift toward lower-margin off-highway compounds it. Integration costs exceed the $140M plan and the measurement period produces an impairment against $4,419M of goodwill and intangibles carried at 223% of book equity. Off-Highway capex catches up from 1.5% of sales toward the legacy 4.0%, cutting free cash flow by $100M+ and stalling deleveraging at 2.4x with buybacks still suspended. FY27 adjusted EBITDA of $1,425M at 8.0x on unchanged net debt yields roughly $95, 10% below the $105 analyst low.

**Which case the evidence currently favors.** The Q2 print is the strongest disconfirming evidence for the bear case: record segment sales, record adjusted FCF, an EPS beat, a guidance raise on every metric, the revolver fully repaid, and the acquired segment crossing into GAAP profit in quarter two. The strongest disconfirming evidence for the bull case is the legacy gross profit decline on higher revenue — the highest-quality part of the business is losing margin to input costs, and the pass-through lag means at least two more quarters before that resolves. On balance the base case dominates, which is why this is ACCUMULATE rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $148 (+21.6% from $121.70).** 9.5x FY27E adjusted EBITDA of $1,625M = $15,438M EV, less roughly $3,150M net debt after a year of paydown = $12,288M ÷ 82.5M shares = $149. Cross-checked at 13.2x the $11.20 next-year consensus = $148.

**Bull case target: $180 (+47.9%).** 10.5x on $1,725M FY27E adjusted EBITDA less $2,950M net debt, with share count reduced by resumed repurchase.

**Bear case target: $94 (-22.8%).** 8.0x on $1,425M FY27E adjusted EBITDA less $3,550M net debt.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to the Q3 print in late October 2026, the FY2026 10-K in February 2027 (which should disclose combined-company customer concentration, full-year segment capex, and completed purchase accounting), and the leverage-target milestone expected during 2027.

**Staged entry plan.** Establish a half position at current levels. Add on either (a) a decline into the $105-112 range, which would put the shares below 10x next-year consensus and near a 9.5% normalized FCF yield, or (b) confirmation in the Q3 report that legacy transmission gross margin has stabilized sequentially. Avoid adding into strength above $135 absent evidence that agriculture has inflected.

**Upgrade to BUY on any two of:** legacy Transmission-segment gross margin recovering 150bps or more sequentially; Off-Highway segment adjusted EBITDA margin above 16% for two consecutive quarters; agriculture end-market revenue growing year over year; or announcement of a resumed buyback pace above $100M per quarter alongside net leverage at or below 2.0x.

**Downgrade to HOLD on:** FY2026 adjusted FCF guidance cut below $745M; Off-Highway capex guidance for FY2027 above 3.5% of segment sales, which would confirm deferred maintenance capital; or two consecutive quarters of Off-Highway sequential revenue decline.

**Thesis invalidation triggers — exit on any one:** (1) a goodwill or intangible impairment charge exceeding $300M, which would confirm the acquisition was mispriced; (2) net leverage rising above 3.0x on either weaker EBITDA or incremental borrowing; (3) loss or material insourcing announcement from Daimler, PACCAR or Traton, which together represented roughly 39% of FY2025 sales; (4) FY2027 guidance implying adjusted EBITDA below $1,450M, which would mean neither synergies nor cycle recovery are arriving.

## Conclusion

Allison converted itself in one transaction from a high-margin niche franchise into a diversified mid-margin industrial, and the market repriced the margin structure without fully repricing the assets acquired. At 9.1x guided EV/EBITDA, 12.3x current-year consensus and an 8.0% adjusted free cash flow yield against a 24.1x peer median, the discount is wider than the structural differences justify — particularly with three catalysts that are mechanical rather than hopeful: purchase-accounting roll-off worth roughly $1.30 of 2027 EPS, deleveraging that frees an 11%-of-market-cap buyback authorization, and a $120M synergy target on a $1,520M EBITDA base.

We are not paying up for it. The legacy franchise is losing gross margin to tariff and material inflation faster than contracts recover it; tangible book equity is approximately negative $2.44B; purchase accounting is preliminary with the acquired unit outside internal-control attestation for a year; nearly half of revenue now sits in end markets levered to the interest-rate path; and management, while well aligned on the evidence — CEO compensation actually paid was negative $423,908 in 2025, five-year TSR of 246 against a peer index of 122, an all-cash deal with zero dilution — has not bought a share in the open market this year and has reduced repurchases 74% to fund deleveraging.

**ACCUMULATE.** Base case $148, bull $180, bear $94, expected return in the mid-teens over twelve to eighteen months. Build the position in stages, add on the $105-112 range or on confirmed legacy margin stabilization, and reassess at the October print.
