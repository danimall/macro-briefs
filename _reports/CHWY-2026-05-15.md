## Executive Summary

Chewy is the largest online-only pet retailer in the U.S., generating $12.6B in revenue with an 83.3% Autoship (subscription) attachment rate that provides unusual revenue predictability for a consumer discretionary name. The business has undergone a meaningful profitability transformation: adjusted EBITDA margins expanded from 3.3% to 5.7% over three fiscal years, and free cash flow reached $562M in FY2025. At $21.48, the stock trades at approximately 11% FCF yield on a ~$5.1B market cap.

The stock has declined 56% from its 52-week high of $48.62, driven by specific headwinds: (1) China tariff exposure on private brands and hardgoods (~15% of revenue), (2) a deteriorating consumer spending environment, (3) insider selling by the CEO, CFO, and General Counsel with zero open-market purchases, and (4) a controlled-company governance structure where BC Partners holds >80% of votes while systematically liquidating its position. The business is high-quality, but the current risk configuration — combining macro consumer stress with tariff uncertainty and governance concerns — prevents a high-conviction BUY despite attractive headline valuation metrics.

## Company Overview

Chewy operates as a pure-play online pet retailer offering ~190,000 products from ~4,000 third-party brands plus proprietary labels (Frisco, American Journey, Tylee's, Vibeful). Revenue breaks down as: Consumables 69.7% ($8.78B), Pet Health & Specialty 15.7% ($1.98B), Hardgoods 11.4% ($1.44B), and Other 3.2% ($407M).

The company's primary competitive advantages are: (1) Autoship subscription at 83.3% penetration (up from 76.2% two years ago), creating subscription-like revenue visibility; (2) the #1 pet pharmacy in America with ~20,000 veterinary practices enrolled in PracticeHub (~50% of U.S. vet clinics); (3) overnight delivery to >80% of the U.S. population; and (4) 18 Chewy Vet Care clinics representing a nascent physical healthcare expansion. The February 2026 acquisition of SmartPak Equine for $175M extends the specialty animal vertical.

BC Partners (through Buddy Chester Sub LLC) controls >40% of shares and >80% of voting power via a dual-class structure, which excludes CHWY from FTSE Russell indices and limits passive fund flows.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** FY2025 revenue was $12.60B, up 6.2% (8.3% excluding a 53rd-week comp in prior year). Active customers reached 21.16M (+4.9% YoY) with net sales per active customer of $595 (+4.9%). Revenue growth is steady but unspectacular — mid-single-digit topline in a market growing ~4% annually.

**Margins:** Gross margin expanded to 29.8% (from 28.4% two years ago), driven by sponsored ads revenue and consumables mix improvement. Operating margin reached 2.0% (from -0.2% in FY2023). Adjusted EBITDA margin hit 5.7%, growing 26% on 6% revenue growth — demonstrating operating leverage. However, GAAP operating margin of 2.0% leaves limited buffer against cost headwinds.

**Cash Flow:** Free cash flow of $562M in FY2025, up from $343M in FY2023, represents a 64% increase over two years. Operating cash flow was $692M with only $129M capex (declining from $143M). The negative working capital model (trade payables exceed inventory by $355M) accelerates cash conversion.

**Balance Sheet:** $860M cash, no debt drawn on an $800M ABL facility (extended to 2030). Working capital is structurally negative — the 0.88 current ratio reflects the e-commerce payment terms advantage, not liquidity stress. The Altman Z-Score of 4.21 indicates low bankruptcy risk.

**Capital Allocation:** $1.2B in share repurchases over FY2024-2025, reducing shares outstanding from 429M to 414M. The company repurchased shares from BC Partners at prices ranging from $28.49 to $41.75 — all well above today's $21.48. This provides a data point on management's own assessment of intrinsic value, though it also served to facilitate BC Partners' exit.

**SBC Dilution:** $311M annually (2.4% of revenue). GAAP EPS of $0.52 vs. adjusted EPS of $1.27 — the $0.75 gap is almost entirely SBC. On a per-share basis, SBC represents approximately $0.75 in annual value transfer, which must be considered in any honest earnings-based valuation.

## Growth Analysis

Topline growth of 6-8% is modest for a company trading at its multiple. The growth drivers are:

1. **Autoship penetration deepening** (83.3% → potentially 85%+), improving retention and lifetime value
2. **Pet health expansion** (pharmacy, clinics) — 18 clinics today with potential for hundreds over 5+ years
3. **Sponsored ads platform** contributing high-margin revenue (following Amazon/Instacart monetization playbooks)
4. **SmartPak Equine** extending into specialty animals

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year EPS consensus of $1.62 is up 4.0% over 90 days, and next year's $1.97 is up 4.5%. These are constructive but far below the 30%+ threshold that would signal a strong inflection.

The pet industry grew 12% during 2008-2010, but Chewy's 56% decline suggests investors are applying a discretionary discount despite the necessity-adjacent nature of pet food.

Growth headwinds include: (1) online pet penetration at 41% (up from 24% in 2019) leaves less easy-share-gain runway; (2) active customer growth of 4.9% suggests acquisition is slowing; (3) the vet clinic expansion requires capital and years to scale.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.48, Chewy trades at:
- 41x trailing GAAP P/E ($0.52 EPS)
- ~13x current-year adjusted EPS ($1.62 consensus)
- ~11x next-year adjusted EPS ($1.97)
- ~11% FCF yield ($562M / $5.1B market cap)
- 0.41x EV/Revenue

Relative to peers: the peer median P/E of 19.7x is below Chewy's GAAP 41x but above its adjusted 13x. The stock is cheaper than Ulta (19.7x) and Winmark (32.5x) on a GAAP basis when adjusted for SBC, but more expensive than BBWI (6.1x) and TSCO (15.1x). The peer comparison is complicated by Chewy's SBC intensity.

The FCF yield of ~11% is genuinely attractive for a business growing mid-single-digits with margin expansion potential. However, this yield exists because of significant uncertainty — it reflects real risks (tariffs, governance, consumer environment) that could compress the numerator.

The company repurchased shares from BC Partners at $28.49-$41.75 over the past two fiscal years. The current price of $21.48 sits 25% below the lowest of those repurchase prices, suggesting either the company overpaid or the stock is meaningfully undervalued by management's own assessment.

Analyst consensus target is $41.05 (median $41.00), with a range of $28-$50. Twenty of 25 analysts rate BUY/Strong Buy with zero sells. This bullish consensus has not prevented the stock from declining 56%.

## Competitive Landscape

Chewy's 83%+ Autoship participation rate is the single strongest competitive moat in online pet retail. No competitor approaches this level of subscription lock-in. The pet pharmacy/vet clinic strategy creates healthcare switching costs that pure product retailers (Amazon, Walmart) cannot easily replicate.

Competitive threats exist: Amazon continues to expand pet consumables with Subscribe & Save; Walmart's omnichannel + price advantage; and veterinarian resistance to Chewy's pharmacy (explicitly cited as an ongoing headwind in the 10-K). The company acknowledges that competitors "may leverage AI more effectively" — a non-trivial risk for customer acquisition costs over time.

The trajectory is constructive: Autoship penetration rising, health segment growing faster than total, and advertising revenue creating new margin streams. Market share appears stable-to-growing.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Risk (HIGH):** The 10-K explicitly states that private brand and non-consumable suppliers are concentrated in China. With U.S.-China tariffs potentially escalating further, the $1.44B hardgoods business and private label margins face direct pressure. Consumables (70% of revenue) are largely domestic-sourced, providing partial insulation.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH):** While pet spending has historically been resilient, Chewy's stock behavior suggests the market is treating it as fully discretionary. Hardgoods and premium products are vulnerable to trade-down behavior.

**Governance (MEDIUM-HIGH):** BC Partners' >80% voting control with no fiduciary duty to minorities, controlled-company exemptions from NYSE requirements, dual-class exclusion from indices, and systematic liquidation ($950M+ sold back to the company) create a governance discount that is likely permanent until BC Partners fully exits.

**Insider Activity (MEDIUM):** CEO sold $2.2M in shares in May 2026 at $25.60. CFO and General Counsel also sold. Zero open-market purchases observed. While these appear to be routine post-vesting dispositions, the absence of any insider buying during a 56% drawdown is striking for a stock trading well below the company's own repurchase prices.

**Margin Vulnerability (MEDIUM):** GAAP operating margin of 2.0% provides minimal cushion. A combination of tariff costs, competitive pricing pressure, and higher input costs could compress margins back toward breakeven.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 161.5% vs. 45.6% historical volatility (115.9pp spread) with severe backwardation (161.5% near-term vs. 64.5% at 6-month) indicates the market expects an imminent catalyst — likely related to tariff implementation or macro deterioration. Put/call OI ratio surged from 0.63 to 1.59 over the past week while the stock dropped 10.1%, confirming institutional put buying is intensifying. The 15.9% put skew and 1.98 volume P/C ratio reinforce that sophisticated market participants are hedging or positioning for further downside. This does not override the fundamental case but flags that the near-term setup is adversarial to longs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35-42, +63% to +95%):**
- Pet spending proves recession-resistant as historically observed
- Tariff impact limited to 10-15% of revenue; pricing power absorbs most of the cost
- Adjusted EBITDA margins expand to 7%+ by FY2027 via ads revenue and operating leverage
- FCF reaches $700M+, supporting continued buybacks
- BC Partners exit completes, removing governance discount and enabling index inclusion
- Vet clinic expansion reaches 50+ locations, validating the healthcare strategy
- Valuation re-rates from 13x to 18-20x adjusted earnings

**Bear Case ($14-17, -20% to -35%):**
- Tariffs force 200-300bps gross margin compression on affected categories
- Consumer spending weakens further; pet owners trade down from premium to value
- Revenue growth decelerates to 2-3%; active customer growth stalls
- BC Partners accelerates secondary sales at discount prices
- Operating margins compress back to 1% or below
- The market applies a fair-value multiple of 10-12x adjusted EPS on reduced earnings power

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence that tariff impact is contained — e.g., next quarterly earnings showing gross margin stable at 29.5%+ despite tariff implementation
2. Insider open-market purchases by C-suite at current levels (~$21)
3. Consumer spending data stabilization

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Gross margin compression below 28% for two consecutive quarters
2. Active customer count declining YoY
3. BC Partners conducting secondary offerings at discount to market (currently they've only sold back to the company)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q1 FY2026, likely June 2026). The tariff impact and consumer spending trajectory should both be more visible by then.

## Conclusion

Chewy is a high-quality business trading at a valuation that appears attractive on several metrics (11% FCF yield, 13x adjusted earnings, 0.41x revenue). The 83%+ Autoship flywheel provides genuine differentiation, and the healthcare expansion creates long-term optionality. The company has repurchased shares at prices 33-94% above today's level, suggesting substantial management conviction in higher intrinsic value.

However, the 56% decline reflects concrete risks. China tariff risk directly threatens margins on 15%+ of revenue, governance is structurally weak, and insiders are selling rather than buying. The options market is pricing severe near-term stress (161% IV vs. 46% HV, put-heavy positioning). The previous BUY recommendation at $23.67 (May 7) has seen a further 9% decline to $21.48, and the fundamental picture has not improved.

HOLD. The business merits ownership at the right price and right time, but the current combination of macro headwinds, tariff uncertainty, governance concerns, and adversarial options positioning creates sufficient near-term risk to prevent a high-conviction entry. Wait for either fundamental confirmation (tariff-resilient margins in next earnings) or insider conviction (open-market purchases).
