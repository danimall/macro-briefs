**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Freeport-McMoRan is one of the world's largest copper producers and the dominant integrated U.S. copper franchise, supplying roughly 70% of domestic refined copper. The investment case is a tug-of-war between two forces. On one side, record metal prices (Q1 realized copper $5.78/lb, +30% YoY; gold $4,889/oz, +59%) are generating strong cash flow and a $0.7B insurance settlement flattered the headline quarter. On the other, the Grasberg Block Cave in Indonesia is operating at 40-50% of capacity following a fatal September 2025 mud rush, and the full-restart timeline has slipped from "end-2027" in the February 10-K to "early 2028" per management on May 8.

The holdable thesis is the 2027 volume recovery. Q1 PTFI copper sales fell to 82M lbs from 290M lbs a year earlier; the entire revenue beat came from price, not volume. Consensus reflects this cleanly: Current-Year EPS estimates have been cut 11.1% over 90 days (to $2.63) while Next-Year estimates have risen 6.0% (to $3.73). The market is pricing 2026 as a volume hole and 2027 as the recovery. If Grasberg ramps from ~3.08B lbs in 2026 toward higher volumes in 2027-2028 against a copper market that the outage itself is tightening, earnings power steps up materially. That recovery is concrete and datable, which is what makes this a hold rather than a sell.

The reasons it is not a buy are equally concrete. At $67, FCX trades at roughly 26x the rising $2.63 Current-Year and ~18x the $3.73 Next-Year estimate, a premium to copper peer Newmont (14.3x) and at the upper end of the broader Materials cohort. A forward multiple in the high teens to mid-twenties on a name whose nearest-term guidance is deteriorating leaves little margin if the restart slips again or copper retraces. Insiders sold into the $60s with zero open-market buying, and the restart timeline has now slipped twice in six weeks. The risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

FCX operates four mining divisions plus downstream processing. The Indonesian asset (PTFI, 48.76%-owned and FCX-managed) holds the Grasberg minerals district in Central Papua, one of the world's largest and lowest-cost copper-gold deposits, producing ~1.7B lbs copper and ~1.3M oz gold annually at normal rates. The gold by-product credit at Grasberg is central to FCX's cost structure. The U.S. division comprises seven mines (Morenci, Bagdad, Safford/Lone Star, Sierrita, Miami, Chino, Tyrone), lower grade but stable and strategically valuable given U.S. copper policy. South America adds Cerro Verde (Peru, 55.08%) and El Abra (Chile, 51%). Molybdenum comes from Climax and Henderson in Colorado. Downstream assets include Atlantic Copper (Spain), U.S. Rod & Refining, and PTFI's new Indonesian smelter.

The structural competitive advantages are genuine: long-lived ore bodies (Morenci 80+ year reserve life), low-cost gold by-product economics at Grasberg, and the only large integrated U.S. copper position, increasingly valuable as Section 232 tariffs and domestic-content mandates reshape the U.S. copper supply chain. Leadership transitioned to CEO Kathleen Quirk (former CFO), with Richard Adkerson remaining Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (10-Q filed 5/8/2026):** Revenue of $6.23B rose 8.8% YoY from $5.73B despite a collapse in Indonesian volumes; the entire gain was price-driven. Net income to common was $881M ($0.61 diluted) versus $352M ($0.24). The headline is materially flattered by a $0.7B PTFI mud-rush insurance settlement gain (collected April 2026), partly offset by $499M of idle-facility and restoration charges. Stripping the insurance gain, underlying earnings reflect a 72% YoY collapse in PTFI copper sales salvaged by record prices. Operating cash flow was $1.5B against capex of $0.97B.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026):** Total debt of $9.4B carries a weighted-average 5.2% rate, ~8-year duration, fixed. Cash is $3.7B. Net debt is $2.4B excluding $3.2B of PTFI smelter project debt, comfortably inside the $3-4B net-debt target. No senior note maturities in 2026; $1.3B in 2027. Liquidity is ample ($3.0B undrawn FCX revolver plus subsidiary facilities). The capital structure is appropriate for a cyclical commodity producer and provides resilience through the volume trough.

**FY2026 guidance (revised down at Q1):** Copper sales cut to ~3.08B lbs from 3.4B; gold to 650k oz from 800k. Operating cash flow guided to ~$8.7B against capex of $4.3B ($3.0B major projects, mostly Grasberg and Bagdad). Idle-facility/restoration costs of $1.3B for 2026. Consolidated unit net cash cost guided to $1.95/lb, though Q2 cost guidance ticked up to ~$2.24/lb on diesel/sulfur/sulfuric acid inflation linked to Middle East conflict.

**Earnings quality and surprise record:** FCX has beaten consensus four straight quarters (Q1 2026 +23.1%, Q4 2025 +62.0%, Q3 +21.4%, Q2 +17.8%). The beats are real but concentrated in the price line, which is the least controllable variable. FCF conversion is healthy, and capital allocation is disciplined: a base + variable dividend ($0.15/qtr), a $5B buyback with $2.9B remaining, and Q1 repurchases at $54.25, below the current $67 price. Net income attributable to noncontrolling interests is large (~$2B guided for 2026), a structural feature of partial PTFI/Cerro Verde/El Abra ownership that consumes a meaningful share of consolidated economics.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct legs separated by the Grasberg recovery curve. Near-term (2026) is contraction: copper volumes down ~10% on guidance, gold down ~19%, with the underground Block Cave milling 4,400 t/day in Q1 versus 93,600 a year earlier. The 2027-2028 leg is recovery: as wet drawpoints are remediated and chute/ore-loading modifications complete, PTFI is guided to ~65% of capacity in 2H26, ~80% by mid-2027, and approaching full only by end-2027 to early 2028.

The estimate-revision pattern captures this precisely: the market is marking down the trough and marking up the rebound. The 90-day Next-Year revision is positive but modest (+6.0%), well short of the velocity that signals a genuine fundamental shift, and the most recent 30-day Next-Year revision is slightly negative (-1.4%). I treat the recovery as a credible catalyst, not as an accelerating shift.

Longer-dated organic optionality is real but price-dependent and beyond a 12-month horizon: a Bagdad doubling (~$3.5B, needs ~$4.00/lb incentive, decision 2H26), El Abra mill expansion, Safford/Lone Star, and Kucing Liar (~8B lbs Cu / 8M oz Au, first production ~2030). Leaching/technology initiatives target ~300M lbs/yr of incremental low-cost copper in 2026. The macro backdrop of electrification, data-center/AI power demand, and urbanization supports structurally higher copper prices, and Grasberg's own outage is tightening an already-strained market alongside a global sulfuric-acid shortage.

## Valuation Assessment

At $67.15 and a ~$96.5B market cap, FCX trades at ~26x the $2.63 Current-Year consensus and ~18x the $3.73 Next-Year estimate. Against the Materials peer median P/E of 29.2, FCX does not look expensive on a headline basis, but the relevant comparison is copper peer Newmont at 14.3x, a substantial premium to its closest commodity comparable. The broader peer set (Sherwin-Williams 29.2x, Ecolab 34.6x, Air Products 29.4x) is a heterogeneous mix of specialty chemicals and building products that are poor reads for a copper-gold miner's cyclical earnings.

The cycle-adjustment argument cuts both ways. The trailing multiple is inflated by the Grasberg volume trough, which argues for using the Next-Year ~18x figure. But that 18x rests on copper holding near record levels (all-time LME highs of ~$6.28 in January) and on a ramp that has slipped twice in six weeks. Each $0.10/lb copper move is ~$220M of annual operating cash flow; each $100/oz gold is ~$50M. The Next-Year estimate is therefore a leveraged bet on both volume recovery and sustained record prices. Paying ~18x forward for a doubly-leveraged commodity recovery, where the operational leg is actively deteriorating, is full rather than cheap. Analyst targets (mean $67.95, median $69.00, high $77, low $31) bracket the current price almost exactly, with the wide low target reflecting genuine disagreement on the downside. The stock sits essentially at consensus mean: neither the floor case nor a clear discount applies, because the nearest-term fundamentals are getting worse, not better. I score valuation 4.5; the multiple prices in a clean recovery that is not yet de-risked.

## Competitive Landscape

FCX's competitive position is strong and improving on the policy axis. It is the only large integrated U.S. copper producer, which positions it as a structural beneficiary of the 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished/derivative copper (Aug 2025), a potential refined-copper tariff (15% in 2027 → 30% in 2028 under review), and domestic-sale mandates rising to 40% by 2029. Import-dependent competitors face a cost disadvantage FCX largely avoids. The ore bodies are world-class and long-lived, and the gold by-product credit at Grasberg gives a cost edge few peers can match.

The trajectory is mixed. The 2042 license-extension MOU secures Grasberg's resource life but dilutes FCX's PTFI economic interest from 48.76% to ~37% beginning in 2042, a real long-term value transfer to the Indonesian government, still subject to definitive agreements. The 5-year TSR of 208.6 lagged the peer group's 330.6, indicating relative execution has trailed even as the asset base remained elite. The competitive moat is wide; the share-of-value trajectory is being eroded at the margin by jurisdictional terms. I score competitive position 7.0; the franchise quality and U.S. policy tailwind outweigh the long-dated dilution.

## Risk Assessment

**Grasberg restart slippage (primary risk):** The full-restart timeline moved from end-2027 (10-K) to "approaching full by end-2027" (Q1 10-Q) to early 2028 (Wenas, May 8) within roughly six weeks. Management flags forecasts as "inherently more variable." This is a fast-deteriorating, recurring-revision situation; the most recent disclosures supersede the optimistic 10-K, and each further slip directly compresses the Next-Year earnings base that the valuation depends on.

**Commodity price reversal:** Earnings are propped by record copper and gold. A price retracement would compound the volume shortfall, and the leverage is large ($220M OCF per $0.10/lb copper).

**Indonesia concentration:** PTFI is the single largest asset in a jurisdiction with a history of export-license disputes, export duties, and resource nationalism. The 2042 dilution and a pending 2022 tax assessment (received April 10, 2026, disputed by FCX) add to the regulatory load.

**Resolved overhang:** The SEC declined FCPA enforcement on the PT Smelting matter (March 17, 2026), clearing the primary U.S. regulatory risk, though DOJ and non-U.S. aspects remain open.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 61.5% versus 48.0% one-year historical vol (rich, 75th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (61.5% near-term vs 52.1% six-month), so the market expects an imminent move and views uncertainty as front-loaded. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.6%) and put/call OI is 0.59 (call-tilted), so the positioning is not directionally bearish; it reflects event risk around upcoming Grasberg ramp data and copper prices rather than a one-sided downside bet. IV rose 3.1pp as the stock climbed 10.3% since early May, consistent with rising uncertainty alongside the rally. The rich, backwardated structure argues against chasing the stock here and supports a patient stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$77, +15%):** Grasberg ramps on the revised schedule, copper holds near record levels on AI/electrification demand tightened further by FCX's own outage, and 2027 volumes step higher. Next-Year EPS proves conservative, and the U.S. tariff/domestic-content regime delivers a structural margin premium the market underweights. At ~20x a rising forward number, the stock reaches the high end of analyst targets.

**Bear case (~$48-50, -26%):** The restart slips again past early 2028, copper retraces 15-20% from records, and the leveraged downside hits both volume and price simultaneously. Idle-facility costs persist above the $1.3B 2026 guide, and the ~18x Next-Year multiple compresses toward Newmont's 14x as the recovery thesis loses credibility. The $31 low analyst target marks the tail scenario of a deep copper downcycle.

**Base case (~$65-70, roughly flat):** Grasberg recovers broadly on the revised path with occasional minor slips, copper consolidates near current levels, and earnings rebuild toward the Next-Year estimate. The stock tracks consensus targets as the recovery delivers about in line with what is already priced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will provide the next Grasberg ramp data point and updated cost/volume guidance. The early-2028 full-restart claim should be tested against actual 2H26 capacity ramp (target ~65%).

**Upgrade triggers (to a constructive lean, capped by sector constraints):**
- Grasberg restart timeline holds or improves across two consecutive quarterly updates, with 2H26 capacity reaching the ~65% guide on schedule.
- Next-Year EPS revisions re-accelerate over 90 days alongside confirmed volume recovery, materially de-risking the $3.73 base.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s (~14-15x Next-Year EPS, near Q1 buyback levels of $54.25) that restores asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A third consecutive slip in the Grasberg full-restart timeline beyond early 2028, or capacity failing to reach ~65% by year-end 2026.
- Copper falling more than 20% from current levels while the volume recovery remains incomplete, the double-squeeze scenario.
- Net debt breaching the $3-4B target as idle costs and capex outrun cash flow.

## Conclusion

FCX is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis worth holding for is the Grasberg volume recovery into 2027, captured in the divergence between Next-Year and Current-Year EPS revisions, against a copper market structurally tightened by electrification and the outage itself. The franchise is elite, the balance sheet resilient, and the U.S. tariff position a genuine differentiator. That is a real, datable catalyst.

The reasons this is not a buy keep the recommendation in HOLD territory. The restart timeline has slipped twice in six weeks; the ~18x Next-Year and ~26x Current-Year multiple already prices a clean recovery; insiders sold into the $60s with zero open-market buying; and the forward number is a doubly-leveraged bet on both volume return and sustained record prices. The risk/reward is balanced, with a base case near current levels, ~15% bull-case upside, and ~26% bear-case downside. The call is to hold the franchise for the 2027 recovery while monitoring the Grasberg ramp closely, and to look for a better entry on a pullback toward the low-$50s where the asymmetry would improve.
