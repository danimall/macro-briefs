**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Financial Bancorp is a ~$22.8 billion Cincinnati-based regional bank holding company that has built a differentiated revenue base through national specialty-finance platforms (Bannockburn FX, Summit equipment leasing, Agile/Westfield insurance-premium finance, Yellow Cardinal wealth management) layered onto a 153-branch Midwest deposit franchise across Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky, and now Illinois. The first quarter of 2026 showed strong returns: net income $74.4M, diluted EPS $0.71 (up from $0.54 a year earlier), ROA 1.34%, ROTCE 17.78%, and a fully-taxable NIM of 3.99% expanding as deposit costs (down 53 bps YoY) repriced faster than asset yields (down 28 bps). This is one of the cleaner regional-bank profitability profiles in the group.

The quality of that headline deserves scrutiny. Q1 results included an $8.9M non-recurring bargain-purchase gain from the January BankFinancial acquisition (~12% of net income); normalized EPS is closer to $0.64. The 27% YoY NII growth and 60% noninterest-income growth are predominantly acquisition-driven (Westfield closed November 2025, BankFinancial January 2026), not organic. The roll-up has been financed largely with stock — share count rose from ~104.3M to ~110.3M in a single quarter — diluting tangible book value to $16.15 against a stated book value of $28.02, with goodwill and intangibles now roughly 42% of total equity. The growth is real but expensive in dilution terms, and the durable organic growth rate is materially lower than the reported figures suggest.

We rate FFBC a HOLD on an income basis. The combination of CET1 rebuilding to 12.22% (up 90 bps in the quarter), an expanding NIM in a falling-rate setup, a dividend recently raised to $0.25/quarter, and a discount to larger regional peers supports owning the shares for total return. The offsetting items — the unresolved $37M Bannockburn FX counterparty receivable classified as a problem asset, office-CRE nonaccruals running at 7.9% of the office book, net charge-offs ticking to 35 bps, ACL coverage of nonaccruals falling from 261% to 183%, and CEO/CFO/director selling into a ~24% price run — cap the asymmetry. There is no specific catalyst here that justifies chasing at $31.46.

## Company Overview

First Financial Bancorp operates through First Financial Bank, an Ohio-chartered commercial bank with 153 full-service banking centers. The company reports as a single operating segment but runs six lines of business: Commercial, Retail Banking, Mortgage Banking, Wealth Management, Investment Commercial Real Estate, and Commercial Finance.

The structural differentiator is a set of national specialty-finance businesses that operate beyond the Midwest branch footprint. Summit Funding Group drives the leasing-business income line ($21.6M in Q1'26). Bannockburn Global Forex runs a matched-book FX, interest-rate, and commodity hedging operation for commercial clients (FX income $16.3M in Q1'26, down from a record $22.7M in Q4'25). Agile Premium Finance and the Westfield-acquired platform provide insurance-premium financing. Yellow Cardinal Advisory Group manages $4.3B in wealth AUM. These platforms give FFBC a higher fee-income mix and more revenue diversification than a typical community bank, at the cost of concentration in a few client relationships within the FX book.

Balance-sheet scale: loans $13.5B, deposits $17.9B, equity $2.94B. Capital sits comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds — CET1 12.22%, total capital 15.70%, leverage 9.39%. The bank carries investment-grade Kroll ratings (BBB+ holdco / A- bank) and $6.4B of unused overnight wholesale capacity (28% of assets).

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** Q1 2026 returns are strong for a regional bank: ROA 1.34% (vs. 1.13% Q1'25), ROE 10.24%, ROTCE 17.78%. NIM is favorable — 3.99% FTE, expanding from 3.88% a year earlier because interest-bearing deposit costs fell 53 bps while asset yields fell only 28 bps. Full-year net income has been stable-to-growing: $205M (2021) → $218M → $256M → $229M → $256M (2025), with ROA consistently 1.36–1.55%.

**Earnings quality.** The Q1 headline of $0.71 EPS is flattered by the one-time bargain-purchase gain on BankFinancial (the deal was struck below the fair value of acquired net assets, reflecting both a favorable negotiated price and BankFinancial's weaker standalone profitability). Acquisition expenses of ~$14M ($9.0M BankFinancial + $5.3M Westfield) partly offset this, but the underlying recurring run-rate is closer to $0.64. The early adoption of ASU 2025-08 also removed the CECL Day-1 "double count" provision on acquired loans, routing a $23.7M (Westfield) and $2.8M (BankFinancial) ACL build against amortized cost rather than through provision expense — an accounting nuance that made acquisition-period earnings cleaner than under the prior method.

**Capital structure.** CET1 rose 90 bps in the quarter to 12.22% on strong earnings. The weak spot is tangible equity: goodwill ($1.10B) plus other intangibles ($146M) total ~$1.24B, about 42% of equity, leaving TCE/TA at 7.87% — adequate but not robust. Tangible book value per share of $16.15 against $28.02 book value is the clearest quantification of how acquisitive the strategy has been.

**AOCI mark.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss is -$217M, driven by -$191.6M after-tax unrealized losses on the $5.1B AFS portfolio (4.3-year duration). This is the standard post-2022 rate-driven mark common to the group; it does not flow through earnings, management states no intent to sell, and par recovery is expected. Six AFS securities show credit deterioration ($5.6M unrealized loss) — small but worth tracking.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.25/quarter from $0.24. A new 5M-share buyback was authorized in April 2026; no shares were repurchased in 2023, 2024, or 2025 — capital was instead deployed into acquisitions. The company redeemed $150M of 5.25% sub-notes and issued $300M of 6.375% sub-notes in late 2025, raising marginal funding cost in exchange for term certainty.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is impressive but acquisition-loaded. NII grew 27% YoY, noninterest income 60% YoY, average earning assets 23% YoY — almost entirely attributable to Westfield and BankFinancial closing within five months of each other. Strip the deals and organic growth is mid-single-digit at best.

The forward growth case rests on three levers. First, deposit-cost repricing: if the Fed continues easing (target currently 3.50–3.75%), FFBC's NIM has shown it can hold or expand because deposit betas lag — though the bank's own NII/EVE modeling shows NII falling 3.34% in Year 1 under a -100 bps shock, so the asset-sensitive structure cuts against a sustained-cut scenario over time. Q1's expansion was a repricing-lag artifact, not a structural rate tailwind. Second, the specialty-finance fee platforms (FX, leasing, insurance-premium finance, wealth) provide growth optionality less correlated to spread income. Third, continued M&A — but the buyback re-authorization, after three years of zero repurchases, may signal the deal pace slows and capital return resumes now that CET1 has rebuilt.

The honest read: the durable growth rate is the organic mid-single-digit base plus episodic, dilutive M&A. The headline acceleration will not repeat without further deals, and further deals come at the cost of more tangible-book dilution. Growth prospects are solid but not differentiated enough to warrant a premium, particularly given the dilution mechanics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.46, FFBC trades at roughly 12x normalized EPS (using the ~$0.64 ex-gain run-rate annualized, ~$2.56) and about 1.95x tangible book — a meaningful premium to TBV that reflects the franchise quality and ROTCE near 18%, but also one that incorporates the intangible build. Against stated book the stock trades at ~1.12x, which looks cheap but is misleading given the goodwill load.

Relative to larger Midwest regionals (Fifth Third, Huntington, KeyCorp, Old National), FFBC carries a modest valuation discount, and external commentary (Seeking Alpha, 6/10/2026) has flagged this as the buy case. We are cautious about leaning on cheapness alone. A compressed multiple on a bank running a stock-funded roll-up with creeping credit normalization and an unresolved counterparty receivable is not automatically a mispricing — it can reflect the market correctly discounting earnings quality and tangible-book dilution. The dividend, recently raised and well-covered, provides genuine absolute-yield support.

There is no identified catalyst that would force a re-rating in the near term. The bargain-purchase gain that flattered Q1 will not recur; the next several quarters will show the "clean" run-rate, which is good but not exciting at ~$2.56. We score valuation modestly below neutral: the stock is reasonably priced for what it is, but the case for material upside from current levels is not made by the data.

## Competitive Landscape

FFBC competes against larger regionals with cost-of-funds and scale advantages, community banks in its core markets, and national specialty-finance and fintech players in its verticals. Its competitive edge is the fee-generating specialty platforms, which diversify revenue away from pure spread income and are difficult for a generic community bank to replicate.

Trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The bank is a consolidator rather than a target, taking deposit share in new markets (the BankFinancial deal is its first material Chicago entry, with the branch rebrand completed June 2026). NIM expansion and ROTCE near 18% indicate the franchise is not losing ground. Against that, the 10-K flags structural pressures — fintech, stablecoin (GENIUS Act), credit-union, and digital-deposit competition — that apply to the whole group and are eroding the deposit-gathering moat industrywide. The FX book's reliance on a small number of large clients is a competitive vulnerability disguised as a differentiator: it produces volatile fee income (the $22.7M-to-$16.3M swing in one quarter) and embedded counterparty risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Bannockburn FX concentration and the $37M receivable.** The most unusual item in the filings: a 2024 mutually-terminated FX trade left a $45M counterparty receivable, $37M of which remains outstanding and is classified as a problem asset — roughly 16% of total classified assets ($232M). Management expects full collection, but this is an idiosyncratic, unresolved counterparty-credit item that a screening process would miss entirely. Combined with the FX book's small-client reliance, it makes the specialty-fee stream lower-quality than the headline suggests.

**CRE/office.** Non-owner-occupied office is $389.8M (2.9% of loans), but within it three nonaccrual relationships total $30.9M — 7.9% of the office book. Total investor CRE is $3.3B (24% of loans). The office stress pocket is concentrated and bears watching.

**Credit normalization.** Net charge-offs rose to 35 bps annualized in Q1'26 (driven by a single commercial relationship; C&I gross charge-offs $10.8M). Nonaccruals crept to 0.75% of loans from 0.51% a year ago, and ACL coverage of nonaccruals fell to 182.7% from 261%. The direction of travel on credit is unfavorable, consistent with the broader regional-bank cycle.

**Intangibles and goodwill-impairment tail.** With goodwill/intangibles at ~42% of equity, a sustained drop below book value could trigger an impairment — a real, if low-base-rate, tail risk for an acquirer carrying this much goodwill.

**Deposit/liquidity.** Uninsured deposits are 41.3% of total (28.3% adjusted ex-public/intercompany). Mitigated by $6.4B unused wholesale capacity and investment-grade ratings, but the post-2023-failure deposit-flight and digital-deposit-mobility risks remain.

**NDFI lending.** $423.6M (3.1% of loans) to non-depository financial institutions, mostly mortgage credit intermediaries — a sector under heightened regulatory scrutiny industrywide, though currently all pass-rated. Worth monitoring against the broader private-credit and shadow-lending stress building in the system.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped from 67.9% to 138.8% (up 71 points) between early May and mid-June while the price moved only +2.6% to $31.46. A doubling of implied vol on a stable, ~$3B-cap regional bank with thin options is most likely a thin-options/illiquidity artifact rather than informed positioning, and we treat the absolute level as low-confidence. The directional read: rising IV against a flat price typically signals the market pricing increased uncertainty rather than a directional view, consistent with the credit-normalization and counterparty items above. We do not let this override the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Deposit costs continue repricing favorably, sustaining NIM near 4% and lifting clean EPS toward $2.80+ as the Westfield/BankFinancial franchises fully integrate and cross-sell the specialty platforms. CET1 funds both the new buyback and the raised dividend, and the capital-return pivot signals the dilutive M&A phase is ending. The $37M FX receivable collects in full, credit normalization stabilizes, and the stock re-rates toward larger-peer multiples on ~18% ROTCE. Bull target ~$38 (~14x ~$2.70 forward EPS), roughly +21% from current.

**Bear case.** Office-CRE nonaccruals expand beyond the current $30.9M, charge-offs continue rising past 35 bps, and the $37M FX receivable requires a partial write-down. The asset-sensitive balance sheet pressures NII if cuts continue, integration costs persist, and a sustained price decline pressures the goodwill-heavy tangible book toward an impairment review. The stock de-rates toward ~1.5x TBV / ~$24, roughly -24% from current.

**Base case.** The clean run-rate of ~$2.56–2.70 EPS, a covered dividend, stable-to-modestly-rising credit costs, and no further large M&A. The stock holds in a $30–34 range, with total return driven primarily by the dividend plus modest EPS accretion. This is an income hold, not an appreciation story.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), the first quarter without the bargain-purchase gain, which will reveal the true clean run-rate and the trajectory of credit costs and the FX receivable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Resolution of the $37M Bannockburn FX receivable at or near full value, removing the largest idiosyncratic overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-declining net charge-offs (below 30 bps) and flat-to-falling nonaccruals, confirming credit normalization has plateaued.
- Evidence the M&A pace has slowed and the 5M-share buyback is actively executed at <1.7x TBV, with clean organic EPS demonstrably above $2.70 annualized.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material write-down on the FX receivable, or office-CRE nonaccruals expanding beyond ~$50M (>12% of the office book).
- ACL coverage of nonaccruals falling below ~150% while charge-offs accelerate past 50 bps.
- A goodwill-impairment charge or a sustained move below tangible book that triggers an impairment review.
- Another large stock-funded acquisition that pushes goodwill/intangibles meaningfully above 45% of equity.

## Conclusion

FFBC is a well-run, well-capitalized Midwest regional with differentiated specialty-finance fee streams and top-quartile returns. The investment case is undermined by earnings quality, an aggressive stock-funded roll-up that has diluted tangible book and built goodwill to 42% of equity, creeping credit normalization, the unresolved $37M FX counterparty receivable, and insider selling into a ~24% run with no offsetting open-market buying. None of these is acutely threatening, but together they cap the upside and remove the asymmetry needed for a BUY.

We rate FFBC a **HOLD** on an income basis. The covered dividend, expanding NIM, and rebuilt capital justify owning the shares; the absence of a near-term catalyst and the watch-list of idiosyncratic risks argue against adding at $31.46. We would become more constructive on resolution of the FX receivable and confirmation that the clean post-gain run-rate holds, ideally on a pullback toward $28 (closer to stated book) that improves the entry. Recommendation: hold for yield, reassess at Q2 earnings.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the ~3.2% dividend yield cited throughout (Executive Summary, Valuation, Base case, Conclusion) is not supported by the sources — at $0.25/quarter ($1.00 annualized) and a $31.46 price, the implied yield is ~3.2%, which does check out, but the report's repeated "well-covered ~3.2%" yield framing is a core pillar of the income thesis and should be re-confirmed against the actual declared annualized rate before publication.
