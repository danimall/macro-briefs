**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Loews is a Tisch-family-controlled holding company that operates like a closed-end fund: it collects dividends and distributions from four businesses, then redeploys the cash into buybacks executed at what management judges to be a discount to intrinsic value. The relevant valuation anchor is sum-of-the-parts, not an insurance multiple. The four assets are CNA Financial (~92% owned commercial P&C insurer, the dominant earnings driver), Boardwalk Pipelines (100%, a fee-based Gulf Coast natural gas transporter), Loews Hotels (100%, anchored by a multi-decade Universal Orlando JV), and a ~53% equity-method stake in Altium Packaging. The investment case rests on per-share intrinsic value compounding through buybacks plus operational momentum at Boardwalk and Hotels, set against a turning underwriting cycle at CNA.

The quarter just reported (Q1 FY26) crystallized the central debate. Net income attributable to Loews fell 9% to $337M ($1.63/share), driven entirely by CNA, where the combined ratio deteriorated to 102.2% from 98.4%, the underlying combined ratio worsened to 94.5% from 92.1%, and prior-year reserve development was unfavorable by $100M. Rate increases are decelerating (total P&C rate +2% vs +4% a year earlier) while casualty loss-cost inflation persists, and retention slipped to 83% from 86%. Against that, Boardwalk net income rose to $159M with EBITDA +4% to $360M and a contracted growth backlog now exceeding $9.5B, while Loews Hotels jumped to $26M net income on a +$38M surge in Universal Orlando JV equity income as the Epic Universe park ramps.

The stock has appreciated about 22% over the trailing year to roughly $107.68, trading at ~13.7x earnings and modestly above total book value (~$88/share; ~$94 ex-AOCI). The scores have been flat at 6.8 across the prior two reports and the rating has been HOLD twice. We maintain HOLD, classified as a growth_hold given the Boardwalk and Hotels catalysts, but the bar for upgrading is specific: the deteriorating CNA underlying loss ratio at the segment that produces the majority of earnings, plus an unquantified Boardwalk Delaware litigation tail with no recorded accrual, are concrete reasons that keep this short of a BUY. The disciplined slowing of buybacks at all-time highs is itself management's signal that the NAV discount has narrowed.

## Company Overview

Loews carries $85.7B of total assets, of which CNA represents ~$68.5B. The parent's identity is capital allocation: management describes itself as "serial share repurchasers" aiming to "shrink the denominator" and compound intrinsic value per share, supplemented by a token dividend ($0.0625/quarter, ~0.23% yield). The four segments:

- **CNA Financial (~92%)** — Commercial P&C across Specialty, Commercial, and International lines, plus a run-off long-term-care book and legacy asbestos/environmental exposure. CNA contributed ~$194M of the $337M Q1'26 net income and dominates consolidated results.
- **Boardwalk Pipelines (100%)** — ~14,000 miles of natural gas/NGL pipelines and ~200 Bcf of storage, concentrated in the Gulf Coast with Haynesville and Utica/Marcellus connectivity. Fee-based, capacity-reservation revenue with limited direct commodity exposure. BBB-rated, full $1.0B revolver capacity.
- **Loews Hotels (100%)** — ~26 hotels weighted to destination/convention properties; the Universal Orlando JV is the differentiated asset, now leveraged by Epic Universe.
- **Altium Packaging (~53%, equity method)** — Rigid plastic packaging, unconsolidated.

The parent depends on subsidiary up-streaming for liquidity. In 2025, CNA and Boardwalk sent ~$1.1B to the parent, including a $497M slice of CNA's $2.00/share special dividend in Q1'26, lifting parent cash and investments net of payables to $4.5B.

## Financial Analysis

**Consolidated.** Q1'26 net income to Loews was down 9% from $370M ($1.74) a year earlier, with revenue roughly flat at $4,555M (+1.4%). FY2025 net income of $1,667M was the strongest of the five-year window (up from $1,414M in 2024), so the Q1'26 decline reads as normalization from a peak rather than a break, with the proviso that CNA's underlying trend is genuinely unfavorable.

**Segment detail.**

- **CNA (the swing factor and the soft spot).** Net income to Loews fell from $252M a year earlier. The underlying combined ratio strips catastrophes and prior-year development and is the cleaner read on underwriting margin; it is moving the wrong way. The drivers: loss-cost trends exceeding rate in Specialty (professional E&O) and Commercial (excess casualty, workers' comp); $100M of unfavorable prior-year reserve development (vs $61M); and $88M of catastrophe losses. Renewal premium change ran +3% vs +6% a year earlier. Net investment income remains a tailwind (+$6M, 4.9% fixed-income yield).
- **Boardwalk (the growth engine).** Net income +$7M to $159M; EBITDA +4% on higher recontracting rates, storage/PAL pricing, and completed growth projects. The mechanism is structural: U.S. gas demand has grown 64% since 2010 while delivery infrastructure grew only 35%, giving recontracting pricing power. Incremental demand from LNG export FIDs (~8 Bcf/d reaching FID in 2025) and gas-for-power data-center load sits on top.
- **Loews Hotels (inflection).** Net income jumped from roughly $0, driven by the Universal Orlando JV equity income surge as Epic Universe and three new adjacent hotels (opened H1 2025) ramp.
- **Corporate.** A $42M net loss in Q1'26, hurt by weaker trading-portfolio results and higher interest expense.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Total equity $19.6B. Debt is well-laddered: the parent refinanced $500M 3.8% notes with 4.9% 2036 notes, and Boardwalk refinanced $550M 6.0% notes while retaining BBB and full revolver access. Since YE2024, Loews repurchased ~8.2M shares (~4% of float) for $712M, but the pace slowed sharply to $31M (0.3M shares) in Q1'26 as the stock hit all-time highs. AOCI swung negative again (–$251M OCI) with ~$2.6B gross unrealized losses on the AFS bond book, which management characterizes as rate-driven and investment-grade rather than credit-impaired. FCF and earnings quality are sound for a holding company of this structure; the standardized Altman Z-Score of ~0.5 is a false alarm, an artifact of applying industrial-firm assumptions to a balance sheet carrying ~$48B of policyholder reserves.

Financial health scores well (7.0) on the strength of $4.5B parent liquidity, BBB-rated subsidiaries, laddered debt, and consistent up-streaming, with the offset being the negative AOCI mark and CNA's reserve volatility.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is bifurcated. Boardwalk and Hotels are accelerating; CNA is contracting on margin.

The durable structural growth sits at Boardwalk. Transcript framing reaches $15.6B including recontracting. The Texas Gateway Project announced in late October 2025 is a $1.2B, 155-mile Gulf Coast new-build under a 20-year contract with an investment-grade anchor customer, in service ~2029, adding an estimated $3.8B to backlog. Management targets low-to-mid-teens unlevered returns on growth capex (~$3.2B contracted through 2030, back-end-loaded into 2027–2029). The April 2026 $215M Spire Marketing acquisition broadens gas-marketing capabilities. The demand backdrop, LNG export growth plus data-center power load, is broad-based and confirmed across multiple industry data points, which raises its durability. Boardwalk participates as a gas-transportation provider, not as an AI operator; the exposure is incremental gas-for-power load contracted with utility and industrial counterparties.

Hotels has a multi-year occupancy/ADR tailwind from Epic Universe, with the JV equity income surge a leading indicator that the ramp is real.

CNA, the majority earnings contributor, is the drag. Decelerating rate against persistent casualty loss-cost inflation compresses the underwriting margin; net investment income offers a partial offset but cannot reverse an unfavorable underlying loss-ratio trend. The blended effect is mid-single-digit consolidated growth in a good year, with CNA's cycle position the dominant variable. There is no earnings-inflection signal of the magnitude (estimate revisions >30%, revenue >40%) that would change the valuation framework, so I score growth at 6.0 — positive and durable at two segments, capped by the size and direction of the third.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$107.68 the stock trades at ~13.7x trailing earnings, roughly in line with the multi-line insurance peer median P/E of 13.2 (AIZ 13.3, AIG 13.2, AFG 12.6, GNW 17.0, LNC 4.3 as an outlier). On an insurance-multiple basis the stock is fairly priced, not cheap. But the correct anchor is sum-of-the-parts: the public CNA stake plus 100% of Boardwalk, Hotels, and Altium, plus net parent cash. The stock sits modestly above total book value and below book ex-AOCI, which is consistent with a holding-company structure that historically trades at a NAV discount.

The most informative valuation signal is management's own behavior. The pause in buybacks because the stock hit all-time highs tells the market the discount has narrowed: a team that explicitly repurchases below intrinsic value and stops when it isn't is signaling its own read on price. That argues against treating the current price as a deep-value entry. The trajectory data shows the valuation dimension was bumped from 6.5 to 7.0 in the prior report; with the stock having since re-approached the upper end of its range and the NAV discount compressing, I mark it back to 6.5. A cheap headline multiple here reflects a fair price for a fairly valued collection of businesses, and the catalyst (further multiple expansion) is absent at current levels. No external analyst price targets are available in the dataset; the single consensus reference point (~$107.28) is essentially the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is improving at Boardwalk and Hotels, eroding modestly at CNA.

Boardwalk's Gulf Coast and Haynesville positioning is structurally advantaged for LNG export growth, and the gas-demand-versus-infrastructure imbalance gives it recontracting pricing power and a multi-year new-build runway. This is a widening-moat dynamic.

Loews Hotels owns a hard-to-replicate asset in the ~30-year Universal Orlando partnership, now leveraged by Epic Universe. The competitive position is improving as the park ramps.

CNA competes in commercial P&C against larger, more diversified peers (Chubb, Travelers, The Hartford). The current dynamic, softening commercial rates colliding with casualty loss-cost inflation, is sector-wide, but CNA's specialty/professional-liability mix makes it more sensitive, and retention slipping indicates some competitive give on price discipline. The prior report cut competitive position from 7.0 to 6.5; the Q1'26 underwriting data supports holding it at 6.5, with the Boardwalk/Hotels strength offsetting CNA's softening.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries two material, specific items that the headline numbers understate, which is why this dimension scores 5.0 rather than higher.

1. **CNA underwriting cycle turning.** The combined ratio crossing 100% plus a deteriorating underlying combined ratio plus three consecutive areas of adverse development (professional E&O, excess casualty, commercial auto/construction) against decelerating rate and falling retention is the single most important near-term concern. The underlying number is still sub-95%, but the direction is unfavorable and CNA dominates consolidated earnings.

2. **Boardwalk Delaware litigation (the under-appreciated tail).** On December 10, 2025, the Delaware Supreme Court found the General Partner breached the LP agreement in the long-running Mishal/Berger case over the 2018 Boardwalk take-private. Remanded tortious-interference and unjust-enrichment claims survive against non-GP defendants. The company has recorded no accrual, states it cannot estimate the loss, and explicitly warns the resolution could be material. The original 2021 trial award context was ~$690M plus interest. A binary item of this magnitude with zero accrual is precisely the kind of risk a standardized screen misses.

3. **CNA long-term-care run-off.** ~$13.2B of future-policy-benefit reserves on an 11-year-duration book; Q1'26 showed unfavorable morbidity and $22M of immediate charges where net premium ratios exceeded 100%. Decades-long assumptions create tail risk.

4. **Boardwalk construction and cost-inflation risk.** ~$3.2B of contracted growth capex through 2030, back-end-loaded, exposed to FERC timing, permitting, and material/labor inflation; management is pre-buying pipe and compression to de-risk.

5. **Holding-company dividend dependency.** The parent has no guarantee of subsidiary debt and relies on subsidiary up-streaming, with CNA's dividends constrained by Illinois insurance-holding-company law.

6. **Investment-portfolio mark-to-market.** ~$2.6B gross unrealized losses on the AFS bond book; a renewed back-up in long-end rates pressures AOCI and book value further.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarming. Near-term ATM IV is 21.1% against 16.1% one-year historical volatility, leaving IV rich by ~5 points, though the IV percentile within the current chain is low at 7%. Put/call skew of 4.4% and a put/call volume ratio of 1.14 indicate modest hedging demand for downside, consistent with the CNA underwriting overhang and the litigation tail. The term structure is flat (1m 21.1% ≈ 3m 21.6%), so the market is not pricing a discrete near-term event. Short interest is low at 2.7% of float (4.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded bear case and no squeeze setup. The options signal modestly corroborates the cautious-but-not-bearish fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Boardwalk compounds at low-to-mid-teens returns on its contracted backlog, riding LNG-export and data-center gas demand against a structurally tight delivery network; Hotels delivers a multi-year Epic Universe occupancy/ADR ramp; CNA's underwriting softness proves cyclical and normalizes back toward sub-95% underlying as casualty rate re-firms; and the parent uses its liquidity to repurchase stock opportunistically on any pullback, compounding per-share NAV. A former CNA CEO buying ~$1M of stock at ~$105 supports the view that the insurance soft patch is manageable. Bull case ~$125 (mid-teens upside), reflecting NAV-discount narrowing plus Boardwalk/Hotels earnings growth flowing through.

### Bear Case
CNA's underlying loss ratio keeps deteriorating as decelerating rate fails to keep pace with social/medical casualty inflation, reserve development stays adverse, and the combined ratio holds above 100%; the Boardwalk Delaware litigation resolves materially against the parent with no accrual cushion; and a long-end rate back-up pressures AOCI and book value. With management already paused on buybacks at these levels, the NAV discount fails to narrow. Bear case ~$90 (~16% downside toward the 52-week low and book value support).

### Base Case
Boardwalk and Hotels growth roughly offsets CNA margin pressure, consolidated earnings grow low-single-digits, and the stock tracks book-value accretion plus the modest dividend with buybacks restrained near current prices. Base case ~$110–112, broadly in line with the current price and the lone consensus reference of ~$107.28.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Time horizon: 12 months.**

This is the third consecutive HOLD, so the bar for continuing it is higher. The justification is concrete: the largest earnings segment (CNA, ~58% of Q1'26 net income) shows a deteriorating underlying combined ratio into decelerating rate, and an unquantified, potentially material Boardwalk litigation liability carries no accrual. Those are falsifiable conditions, not a vague "valuation looks full." The valuation anchor (price modestly above total book, NAV discount narrowed per management's own paused buybacks) offers no asymmetric upside catalyst at $107.68 to justify a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CNA underlying combined ratio reverses below 92% for two consecutive quarters, or prior-year reserve development turns favorable, confirming the underwriting soft patch was cyclical.
- A material price decline (toward ~$95 or below, near book ex-AOCI) that re-opens a clear NAV discount, evidenced by management re-accelerating buybacks.
- The Boardwalk Delaware litigation resolves immaterially (settlement well below the ~$690M historical award context) or is dismissed, removing the binary tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CNA combined ratio sustains above 104% with continued adverse development, signaling a structural rather than cyclical margin problem.
- An adverse Delaware litigation ruling with a quantified liability exceeding ~$500M and no offset.
- Boardwalk growth-capex execution slips materially (FERC denial or cost overruns) on the back-end-loaded 2027–2029 build.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (CNA combined-ratio and reserve-development trend) and any docket movement on the remanded Delaware claims.

## Conclusion

Loews is a well-capitalized, family-aligned compounder with two genuinely improving businesses (Boardwalk and Hotels) and one large, cyclically softening one (CNA). The Boardwalk franchise (fee-based, BBB-rated, backed by the contracted backlog and structural LNG/data-center gas demand) is the most attractive piece, and the Universal Orlando JV inflection is a real near-term earnings tailwind. The open-market purchase by a former CNA CEO at roughly today's price is a modest positive tell. CNA's deteriorating underlying loss ratio at decelerating rate, the unquantified Boardwalk Delaware litigation with no accrual, and a stock trading at fair value to book with management's own buyback discipline signaling a narrowed NAV discount keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The risk/reward is balanced: roughly mid-teens upside to the bull case against similar downside to book-value support, with no specific catalyst to force a re-rating at the current price.
