## Executive Summary

AIG has finished the five-year transformation it began. Total assets fell from roughly $539B at year-end 2023 to $161.5B at March 31, 2026, revenue from $46.8B (FY2023) to $26.8B (FY2025), and the Corebridge stake to 5.6% with both board designees resigned. What remains is a pure-play global commercial P&C underwriter with A (AM Best) / AA- (S&P) / AA- (Fitch) / A1 (Moody's) financial strength ratings, 18.2% debt-to-total-capitalization, a benign maturity ladder ($28M due for the remainder of 2026, $945M in 2027), an undrawn $3.0B revolver to September 2029, and a diluted share count down 31% since FY2022. At $80.12 the stock trades at roughly 1.06x the March 31 book value of $75.82, 10.0x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.98 and 9.1x the FY2027 estimate of $8.84, with a dividend raised 11% on April 30 to $0.50 quarterly for a 2.50% yield.

Almost every favorable headline in the most recent reported quarter decomposes into something less durable. Q1 2026 combined ratio of 87.3 versus 95.8 looks like a step change, but 6.1 of the 8.5 points came from lower catastrophe losses and 1.2 from higher favorable prior-year development; the accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe improved only 1.2 points, and North America Commercial — the largest segment — went the wrong way, to 85.5 from 84.3. The metric management is actually paid on, accident year combined ratio as adjusted, reads 87.7 (2023), 88.2 (2024), 88.3 (2025): improvement through 2023, then two years of modest deterioration. Net premiums written rose 24% in Q1 after declining 1% for full-year 2025, and management attributes that uniformly to "strategic transactions, reinsurance program changes and organic growth" without quantifying the split. The identifiable non-organic components — a Convex whole-account quota share at 7.5% of Convex's entire portfolio in 2026, $301M of Everest renewal rights, Lloyd's Syndicate 2479, reduced outward cessions, and $222M of currency — are individually disclosed but collectively unbounded. Net premiums earned grew 5%.

The single most consequential item is arithmetic, not narrative. Under the January 2017 National Indemnity agreement, AIG ceded 80% of losses on substantially all U.S. commercial long-tail exposures for accident years 2015 and prior above a $25B paid attachment, capped at $25B of covered losses. At March 31, 2026, covered losses above attachment were $16,495M, leaving $8,505M of gross capacity — against $8,749M of AIG's own remaining covered reserves before discount. On the company's current best estimate, expected remaining cessions marginally exceed what the cover can absorb. Any further adverse development on asbestos, excess casualty, workers' compensation or environmental reserves from those accident years lands on AIG rather than 20/80 with Berkshire. That matters more because the one book with a documented three-year adverse development record — UK/Europe Casualty and Financial Lines, approximately $216M adverse in 2025 after roughly $170M and $165M — is now AIG's largest single reserve concentration at $8,737M and grew 5.4% during a quarter when total net reserves were flat.

We move to HOLD with the overall score at 5.57, continuing the decline from 6.7 (February) through 6.1 (June). The stock is 8.2% higher than at the June review on essentially unchanged reported fundamentals, so risk/reward has narrowed rather than the thesis having broken. Base case $86, bull $99, bear $68.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline.** Net premiums written $23,675M, down about 1%. Combined ratio 90.1. Underwriting income $2,332M, up 22%. Adjusted pre-tax income $5,344M; adjusted after-tax income $4,044M, or $7.09 per diluted share, up 43%. GAAP ROE 7.5%; core operating ROE 11.1%. Catastrophe losses $920M including roughly $440M from the January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires.

The 43% per-share increase deserves scrutiny because it is mostly not earnings growth. Weighted-average diluted shares fell from 787M in FY2022 to 542.2M in Q1 2026. Buybacks were $6.7B in FY2024 and $5.8B in FY2025 — roughly $1.45B per quarter. Q1 2026 repurchases were $524M, with a further $209M between April 1 and April 24. That is roughly a $2.3B annualized pace, about 60% below FY2025, implying a materially slower share-count reduction than the recent trend. Backing that out of the consensus path — FY2026 $7.98 and FY2027 $8.84 — the double-digit per-share growth in consensus is heavily supported by continued share-count reduction, and that engine is throttling back. [The precise implied per-share vs. aggregate earnings growth split cannot be derived because the brief does not provide the consensus average-diluted-share assumptions; unverified — actual: N/A.]

**Q1 2026.** Net income attributable to common shareholders $763M ($1.41 diluted) versus $698M/$1.16. The Q1 2025 base is inflated by $51M of after-tax out-of-period adjustments AIG recorded that quarter, so the reported +9% understates the underlying comparison. Underwriting income $774M versus $243M; catastrophe losses $180M versus $525M.

**Investment income is better than the headline.** Total net investment income fell 36% to $712M from $1,105M, but AFS fixed maturities plus short-term investments rose 13%, to $915M from $812M, and on management's segment basis General Insurance net investment income rose 17% to $864M. The decline sits entirely in marks: the Corebridge position swung from a +$209M gain to a $(154)M loss, Corebridge dividends fell $25M, and alternatives (one-quarter lagged) fell to $8M from $43M. Anyone screening on headline NII reads the direction of travel backwards.

The composition of that volatility has, however, worsened. The Onex stake — $642M cost on February 6, 2026, fair value $548M at March 31, an unrealized loss of roughly 14.6% inside eight weeks — is contractually restricted from sale or transfer until February 6, 2029 yet carried at fair value with changes running through net investment income rather than OCI. Convex is carried at $2,155M under the equity method with no market price, roughly $550M of embedded goodwill and $450M of intangibles and basis differences, and its earnings share flows into General Insurance net investment income, where it will be difficult to isolate from portfolio yield. Together with Corebridge at $607M, roughly $3.3B — about 8% of shareholders' equity — sits in unquoted or unsellable positions driving a P&L line.

**Balance sheet.** Total equity $40,429M; AIG shareholders' equity $40,405M, or $75.82 per share on 532.9M shares. Long-term debt $9,001M with fair value $8,461M; junior subordinated debt only $481M. Debt to total capitalization 18.2%. The maturity ladder ($28M, $945M, $685M, $204M, $959M, nil, then $6,180M) presents no refinancing wall.

Two balance-sheet items deserve more attention than they get. First, parent liquidity of $6.4B at March 31 (down from $9.3B) includes the undrawn $3.0B facility, so actual parent cash and short-term investments fell from roughly $6.3B to roughly $3.4B in one quarter — the direct consequence of the $2.74B Convex and Onex deployment. Second, gross loss reserves are net of approximately $14.0B of contractual deductible recoverables due from policyholders on high-deductible U.S. commercial casualty programs, against approximately $10.0B of collateral, leaving roughly $4.0B of unsecured credit exposure to insureds carried with a $14M allowance. That is about 10% of shareholders' equity, concentrated in precisely the recessionary scenario where large corporate insureds come under stress. The counterargument is fair — these are mostly large investment-grade corporates and recoveries spread over many years of claim payout — but no standard screen surfaces it.

AOCI widened $728M to $(5,715)M on $616M of unrealized investment depreciation and $121M of FX. Securities in an unrealized loss position without a credit allowance rose from 7,526 to 10,977 individual issues; gross unrealized losses on AFS bonds were $2,120M against $750M of gains. Duration of the General Insurance fixed maturity book is only four years, which limits mark volatility but also caps the locked-in yield benefit and means reinvestment income tracks the front end closely.

**Cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $155M against $763M of net income, reflecting a $1,032M build in reinsurance assets, $895M of DAC capitalization and $282M of net premium receivable movement — Q1 is structurally the heaviest working-capital quarter for a commercial insurer, and the January 1 Convex quota share amplified it. This should not be annualized; a $620M run-rate sensitivity is arithmetic, not a forecast. Conventional free-cash-flow screening has limited application to an insurer whose capex is trivial and whose operating cash flow is dominated by reserve and premium timing. FY2025 full-year operating cash flow was not verified and is not asserted here.

One methodological note: the low Altman Z-Score that appears on standard health screens is not a distress signal. The model weights working capital, sales and retained earnings against assets — constructs that do not map onto an insurance holding company with 0.17x asset turnover by design, and whose reported assets include a $37.6B loan receivable from AIG Financial Products carried with a full credit allowance. The relevant solvency lenses are the AA-/A1 financial strength ratings, 18.2% debt/capital, the undrawn facility and the maturity ladder, all of which read sound.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to whether the Q1 premium inflection is franchise value or purchased volume.

General Insurance NPW rose 24% (18% constant currency) after a 1% decline for full-year 2025, with North America Commercial up 37%. The disclosed drivers are unquantified. What can be identified from the filings: the Convex whole-account quota share effective January 1, 2026, under which AIG assumes 7.5% of Convex's entire underwriting portfolio, rising to 10.0% in 2027 and 12.5% in 2028; $301M paid for Everest renewal rights in FY2025; Lloyd's Syndicate 2479 with Amwins and Blackstone; reinsurance program changes; and $162M of FX in International Commercial plus $60M in Global Personal.

Three of those are qualitatively different from organic growth. Assumed quota-share premium is business AIG does not underwrite and does not control. Renewal rights are purchased books. Reducing outward cessions raises net premiums written without adding a policy while simultaneously raising net catastrophe retention — growth and risk increasing in lockstep, which is why the $180M Q1 catastrophe figure should not be read as reduced exposure. Net premiums earned grew 5% against 24% written growth, with the unearned premium reserve rising from $17,991M to $18,921M; the earnings will emerge over coming quarters, and so will the losses.

The genuinely encouraging organic datapoint is Global Personal, which swung from a $(126)M underwriting loss to $169M of income, an 18.5-point combined ratio improvement. AIG's own decomposition: catastrophes 10.6 points, favorable development 2.2, accident-year loss ratio ex-CAT 1.6, expense ratio 4.1 (acquisition 2.7 on business mix and improved commission terms; G&A 1.4). The accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe improved to 89.9 from 95.6, and NPW returned to +17% after FY2025's -12%, driven by U.S. high-net-worth and Accident & Health. A 5.7-point underlying improvement plus 4.1 points of expense leverage is substantive. But 10.6 of the 18.5 points is a comparison against a wildfire quarter, part of the premium recovery is ceding less rather than selling more, and the FY2025 decline was partly deliberate remediation, making the base easy.

Consensus revisions are mildly negative near term and roughly flat further out: current quarter -0.2% over 30 days and -1.4% over 90; current year -0.6% and +2.5%; next year -0.7% and +0.1%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here in either direction — the 90-day current-year improvement is largely the January-1 renewal effect flowing through, and the 30-day drift is negative across all four periods. Four consecutive earnings beats (10.1%, 2.1%, 27.3%, 12.1%) argue for reasonable confidence in the near-term consensus, though the largest of those beats coincided with a light catastrophe quarter.

Against a commercial P&C pricing cycle that is broadly decelerating — visible across the reinsurance complex — the honest growth characterization is mid-single-digit aggregate earnings growth, high-single-digit per-share growth once buyback deceleration is respected, with the incremental premium volume of lower quality than the base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.12:

- **P/B:** $80.12 ÷ $75.82 = **1.06x** reported book. Adding back approximately $(1,993)M of investment AOCI gives adjusted book near $79.6, so roughly **1.01x adjusted book**.
- **Forward earnings:** **10.0x** FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.98; **9.1x** FY2027 consensus of $8.84.
- **Yield:** $2.00 annualized ÷ $80.12 = **2.50%**, comfortably covered.
- **Trailing:** the profile reports 14.07x, which does not reconcile to the $5.43 trailing GAAP EPS shown (that implies 14.75x). GAAP EPS is distorted by Corebridge deconsolidation and mark-to-market, so the forward adjusted figures are the usable basis.

The supplied peer set (Loews 13.6x, Assurant 13.3x, American Financial 12.6x, Lincoln 4.3x, Genworth 17.0x; median 13.3x) is a poor comparison — it blends conglomerates and life insurers on trailing GAAP. The relevant comparables are commercial and specialty P&C carriers. [Specific forward multiples for Arch and Chubb are not in the brief or Phase 1 data; unverified — actual: N/A.] AIG at 9.1x FY2027 warrants comparison to specialty and commercial P&C peers rather than the supplied multi-line set, but any peer whose 2025 combined ratio was materially below AIG's 90.1 would command a premium, and AIG additionally carries a legacy reserve cover near exhaustion.

That is the central valuation judgment. AIG's discount is explicable rather than anomalous: an underlying accident-year combined ratio that has drifted the wrong way for two years, the largest reserve concentration sitting in the book with three consecutive years of adverse development, a legacy reinsurance shock absorber at the end of its capacity, per-share growth decelerating as capital is redirected from buybacks into minority stakes, and a first-time public-company CEO. A cheap multiple with an identifiable structural explanation is not the same as a mispricing.

There is real downside support. Book value at roughly $76 sets a soft floor for an AA-/A1 rated carrier; the dividend is covered; $3.2B of buyback authorization remained at April 24; short interest is 2.0% of float at 2.2 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic. A cross-check on fair P/B: 11.1% core operating ROE against a roughly 9% cost of equity supports something near 1.15-1.20x book, or $87-91 — consistent with the analyst mean of $88.55 and median of $89.00. But that ROE assumes the underwriting margin holds through a softening market with rising catastrophe retention and no unshielded reserve charge. At a 9.5% ROE the same framework supports 1.0x book, or $76.

**Scenarios**

- **Base $86** (+7.3%): 9.7x FY2027 consensus $8.84 and 1.13x current book. Assumes combined ratio holds near 89-90, Convex proves modestly accretive, buybacks continue near the $2.3B pace, and no material reserve charge.
- **Bull $99** (+23.6%): 11.2x FY2027 and 1.30x book. Requires the accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe to resume improving toward 86, a quantified Convex earnings contribution, buyback re-acceleration, and a benign catastrophe year. This is a re-rating on demonstrated underwriting improvement.
- **Bear $68** (-15.1%): 0.89x book, below the $71.25 52-week low. Requires a reserve strengthening on UK/Europe casualty or U.S. excess casualty now borne without Berkshire participation, combined with an active catastrophe year against higher retentions and soft-market margin compression.

Reward-to-risk to base is roughly 0.5:1 and to bull roughly 1.6:1. That does not clear the bar for new capital, particularly in a sector where selection has historically needed a distinct company catalyst to beat the index.

## Competitive Landscape

AIG competes with Chubb, Travelers, Zurich, Allianz, AXA XL, Everest, Arch, W.R. Berkley, Markel and the London market. Its differentiation is genuine but narrow: multinational program capability across 200-plus jurisdictions, the Talbot Lloyd's platform, Lexington's E&S capacity, and a rating stack (AA-/A1 on principal carriers, upgraded during 2025) that few mid-cap carriers match.

The trajectory is the concern. Organic net premiums written declined 1% in FY2025. The accident-year combined ratio as adjusted worsened from 88.2 to 88.3 in FY2025 and North America Commercial's accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe deteriorated 1.2 points in Q1 2026 on "changes in business mix." Meanwhile the strategy has visibly shifted from underwriting remediation to renting distribution and third-party capacity: Everest renewal rights, Syndicate 2479 with a distributor and a private-capital partner, a CVC partnership, 35% of Convex plus a ramping whole-account quota share, and 9.9% of Onex.

Two readings coexist. Constructively, AIG is monetizing balance-sheet strength and franchise access to deploy capital at attractive risk-adjusted returns without adding fixed cost or a new reserve tail. Skeptically, a carrier whose organic premium was shrinking and whose underlying margin had plateaued has bought growth at the point in the cycle when pricing is decelerating, and converted part of its premium base into assumed reinsurance of an underwriting operation it does not control. The filings do not resolve which reading is correct; the Q2 and Q3 disclosures of Convex's earnings contribution — which flows into General Insurance net investment income rather than underwriting income — will begin to.

## Risk Assessment

**Berkshire adverse development cover, effectively exhausted.** Remaining gross capacity $8,505M ($25,000M limit less $16,495M of covered losses above attachment) against $8,749M of AIG's remaining covered reserves before discount; remaining NICO capacity $6,804M against $6,999M representing 80% of those reserves. The residual deferred gain is $306M, amortizing at roughly $26M a quarter into adjusted pre-tax income. The caveats are real — the limit applies to paid losses so timing matters, the arithmetic is close rather than decisively breached, and if the $8,749M estimate proves redundant, headroom re-emerges. AIG has not characterized the cover as exhausted. The mechanism that removed the reserve-volatility discount from AIG's equity since 2017 has essentially no capacity left, and the market has not obviously done this arithmetic.

**UK/Europe casualty reserves.** Net reserves of $8,737M at March 31 are the largest single concentration, up $449M (5.4%) from year-end during a quarter when FX effects on loss reserves were negative $(194)M and total net reserves were flat at $41,765M. This is the book with roughly $216M, $170M and $165M of adverse development in the three prior years. A partial offset in the same table — North America Commercial "Other product lines" falling $1,014M and unallocated LAE falling $299M — is consistent with a re-mapping across line categories rather than organic reserve growth, which would weaken the inference. AIG discloses no reclassification, so this is unresolved.

**Prior-year development quality.** Q1 favorable development of $153M includes $26M of NICO deferred-gain amortization, a contractual accretion rather than experience. The remaining $127M is short-tail: U.S. Property and Special Risks $(68)M, International Energy and Cargo $(52)M, U.S. Financial Lines $(23)M, U.S. Personal $(22)M, offset by $28M adverse in UK/Europe Property. Long-tail casualty contributed approximately nothing — workers' comp $(8)M, excess casualty $(6)M, UK/Europe casualty $(1)M. Holding long-tail reserves rather than releasing them is prudent; it also means there is no visible casualty cushion.

**Rising catastrophe retention by design**, reinsurance recoverables of $41.9B exceeding shareholders' equity (82% investment grade, 87% of the non-investment-grade portion to captives with collateral), $4.0B of unsecured policyholder deductible exposure against a $14M allowance, deferred tax assets of $5,062M with a $249M valuation allowance plus a new $88M state allowance following a New York audit, IRS examination for 2011-2019 and Appeals for 2007-2010, and $982M of maximum off-balance-sheet exposure related to AIGFP guarantees round out the specific list. Item 1A of the Q1 10-Q reported no changes to the FY2025 risk factors.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility of 70.5% against 24.5% one-year realized is entirely the Q2 print landing after the close on August 6, with a ±4.0% implied move on the one-day tenor; IV rose from 25.5% to 70.5% between July 29 and August 6 with the price down 0.9%, which is event pricing rather than a directional signal. The term structure is steeply backwardated (70.5% near, 32.0% at one month, 28.0% at three months, 24.2% at six months), so the market prices this quarter's disclosure as the uncertainty and nothing structural beyond it. Positioning is mixed: put/call open interest of 1.37 and put-heavy one-month skew, against call-heavy three-month skew and a roughly flat six-month reading. Short interest of 2.0% of float at 2.2 days to cover confirms no crowded bearish position. The options market is pricing a single binary rather than a regime change, which is consistent with treating the print as the reassessment point rather than a reason to act ahead of it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AIG is a $42.5B pure-play commercial P&C carrier at 1.06x book and 9.1x FY2027 earnings, with an AA-/A1 rating stack, 18.2% debt/capital, no refinancing wall, and a $3.2B buyback authorization against a book-value floor. Core investment income is compounding — AFS portfolio income +13%, General Insurance net investment income +17% — at a four-year duration that reinvests into a higher front end. Global Personal, the historic problem segment, delivered a 5.7-point underlying accident-year improvement and 4.1 points of expense leverage while returning to growth in high-net-worth and A&H. Four consecutive earnings beats support the consensus path to $8.84. Convex, if the accretion claim holds, adds specialty underwriting economics without consolidating reserve tail or fixed cost. Re-rating on demonstrated margin improvement supports $99.

**Bear case.** The underlying underwriting margin has been flat-to-worse for two years on the company's own paid-on metric, and the largest segment deteriorated again in Q1. Premium growth is purchased, assumed and mechanical, arriving as commercial pricing decelerates, while reduced cessions raise catastrophe retention. Per-share growth is decelerating because $2.74B went into two minority stakes — one bought at roughly 1.9x underlying net assets while the company was retiring its own equity at 1.06x book, the other 14.6% underwater within eight weeks and locked until 2029 with marks running through the P&L. The Berkshire cover on $8,749M of legacy long-tail reserves has no meaningful capacity left, and the book with three consecutive years of adverse development is the fastest-growing part of the reserve base. A single reserve strengthening in an active catastrophe year against higher retentions takes the stock to $68 or below.

**Management assessment.** The compensation design is right for a P&C insurer — accident year combined ratio as adjusted, calendar year combined ratio, diluted adjusted after-tax income per share, core operating ROE and relative TSR, with no premium-volume metric that would reward writing bad business. Five-year cumulative TSR of $253.09 per $100 against a peer group at $234.32 is roughly 8% cumulative outperformance, and the CEO-to-average-NEO pay ratio of 4.7x is unremarkable. The Corebridge exit was well timed: 24.7M shares sold at $30.42 in February against an implied $23.84 quarter-end mark.

The concerns are about capital allocation and alignment at the margin. Zaffino's FY2025 total compensation rose 32% to $32.46M with compensation actually paid nearly doubling to $67.98M, in the fiscal year whose close was immediately followed by the announcement he was relinquishing the CEO role, and in which the primary performance metric worsened from 88.2 to 88.3. There were no open-market purchases by any officer or director between April and July 2026 — every officer transaction was a Code F tax-withholding disposition, with routine director DSU grants otherwise. That absence is weak evidence in either direction for large-cap financial executives who are frequently in blackout and already hold substantial equity; it is not a bearish signal, but it is also not the insider confirmation one would want at 1.06x book. Eric Andersen brings roughly 30 years of Aon distribution experience but no public carrier P&L record, and the Zaffino-as-Executive-Chair structure alongside a first-time public-company CEO is a governance arrangement the filings do not resolve. Nine Item 5.02 filings in fifteen months is consistent with a leadership refresh around succession, but the individual filings were not read.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (uncertain). The Q2 2026 print lands at the information cutoff and its content is not in this analysis; the Convex accretion claim is unquantified; and the reserve question created by the exhausted Berkshire cover cannot be resolved until AIG completes its later-year long-tail actuarial reviews.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY)**
1. Accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe improving for two consecutive quarters, with North America Commercial back below 84.5 — the metric that has been drifting the wrong way since 2023.
2. A quantified decomposition of NPW growth into organic rate/exposure versus assumed quota share, purchased renewal rights and reduced cessions, showing organic growth of at least high single digits, together with a disclosed Convex earnings contribution supporting the accretion claim.
3. Price at or below $70-72 (roughly 0.92-0.95x book) with reserves and ratings unchanged, which would restore asymmetry against the $68 bear case.
4. Clustered open-market insider purchases by the new CEO or CFO, or buyback re-acceleration above $1.2B per quarter, either of which would signal management sees value at prevailing prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID)**
1. Any reserve strengthening in UK/Europe Casualty and Financial Lines or U.S. Excess Casualty for accident years 2015 and prior, which would now be borne close to 100% by AIG.
2. Accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe deteriorating above 89 on a consolidated basis, or the calendar-year combined ratio moving back above 92 outside of an identifiable catastrophe event.
3. A cut or suspension of the buyback authorization to fund further minority stakes, or a further material step-down in parent cash below roughly $2.5B ex-facility.
4. A downgrade of the principal carriers' financial strength ratings below AA-/A1, which would impair new-business capture.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 2026 release (after the close August 6, call August 7) is the immediate reassessment point — specifically the underlying accident-year combined ratio ex-catastrophe by segment, the NPW growth decomposition, the Convex contribution within General Insurance net investment income, the buyback pace, and any movement in the NICO covered-loss table. The Q3 print, which typically carries the heavier long-tail actuarial reviews, is the more consequential test of reserve adequacy without the Berkshire cover.

## Conclusion

AIG's transformation is complete and the balance sheet is the strongest it has been in two decades. At 1.06x book, 9.1x FY2027 consensus earnings and a covered 2.50% yield, the downside is genuinely cushioned, and core investment income is compounding at 13-17% into a higher front end.

The discount is earned rather than accidental. The margin metric management is paid on has gone 87.7 → 88.2 → 88.3, the largest segment deteriorated again in Q1, the premium acceleration is largely purchased or assumed at a point when commercial pricing is decelerating, per-share growth is slowing as capital moves from buybacks to illiquid minority stakes bought above the multiple at which AIG was retiring its own shares, and the Berkshire cover that removed the legacy reserve discount has essentially no capacity left just as the book with three consecutive years of adverse development becomes the fastest-growing part of the reserve base.
