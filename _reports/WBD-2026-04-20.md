## Executive Summary

Warner Bros. Discovery is a $68B media conglomerate trading at $27.43, approximately 13% below the $31/share all-cash offer from Paramount Skydance (PSKY) announced on February 27, 2026. The merger spread exists for defensible reasons: the DOJ has issued antitrust subpoenas, the UK CMA is opening a formal investigation, and Democratic senators are demanding FCC review of $24B in Gulf sovereign wealth fund financing. The shareholder vote is scheduled for April 23, 2026, and is expected to pass, but regulatory clearance remains uncertain with a final deadline of March-June 2027.

The standalone business is deeply challenged. Total debt is $32.8B (including a $15B secured bridge loan maturing June 2027), credit ratings were downgraded to junk by all three agencies in 2025, and the Altman Z-Score sits at 1.25 — firmly in the distress zone. Revenue has declined from $41.3B in 2023 to $37.3B in 2025, driven by structural erosion in linear TV (domestic subscribers down 9%, audiences down 25%). The 2025 net income of $727M was entirely attributable to a $2.96B gain on debt extinguishment; absent that gain, the company posted a loss. Insiders sold aggressively in March 2026, with the CEO liquidating $113M and the Chief Revenue & Strategy Officer selling his entire position to zero shares.

This is a binary merger-arbitrage situation. For merger-arb specialists, the 13% spread may be attractive depending on their assessment of regulatory outcomes. For fundamental equity investors, the standalone risk profile makes this an avoid.

## Company Overview

Warner Bros. Discovery was formed in April 2022 through the merger of Discovery, Inc. and AT&T's WarnerMedia business. It operates three segments:

**Streaming ($10.9B revenue, 2025):** Max and discovery+ with 131.6M subscribers (up 13% YoY). The segment reached $1.37B in Adjusted EBITDA (up from $677M) but still posts a GAAP operating loss of ($264M) after depreciation, amortization, and impairments. Global ARPU is declining at 11% ex-FX as growth tilts toward lower-value international and wholesale markets.

**Studios ($12.6B revenue, 2025):** Warner Bros. film, DC Studios, television, games, and consumer products. Had a strong theatrical year with seven consecutive $40M+ domestic openings. Adjusted EBITDA reached $2.55B (up 54% YoY).

**Global Linear Networks ($17.7B revenue, 2025):** CNN, TNT Sports, Discovery Channel, HGTV, Food Network, and others. This is the largest segment by revenue and EBITDA ($6.4B) but in steep structural decline — revenue fell 12% YoY, and management acknowledged "declines in linear subscribers are expected to continue." A $9.1B goodwill impairment was recorded in 2024.

The company holds world-class IP including the DC Universe (Superman, Batman), Harry Potter/Wizarding World, Game of Thrones, and Looney Tunes, plus 2026-2032 Olympic Games rights.

### Profitability

The headline 2025 net income of $727M requires immediate adjustment. WBD recorded a $2.96B gain on debt extinguishment from its tender offers, without which the company would have posted a net loss of approximately ($2.2B). Operating income of $744M similarly benefits from various one-time items. Adjusted EBITDA of $8.74B declined 3% YoY despite improvements in Streaming (+$693M) and Studios (+$898M), because Linear EBITDA fell $1.74B (21%).

### Balance Sheet

Total debt: $32.8B, consisting of $15.0B in a secured Bridge Loan and $17.8B in senior notes. Cash on hand: $4.6B. Net debt: ~$28.3B. Debt-to-equity ratio: 1.75x. The Bridge Loan is secured by substantially all personal property assets and matures the earlier of June 30, 2027 or completion of the Separation Transaction.

All three credit agencies downgraded WBD to junk in 2025: BB+ (S&P), Ba1 (Moody's), BB+ (Fitch). The Altman Z-Score of 1.25 places the company in the distress zone (below 1.81 threshold). Total contractual obligations amount to $73.4B, including $32.8B debt principal, $10.7B interest, $19.7B content commitments, and $4.7B operating leases.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow declined from $5.4B in 2024 to $4.3B in 2025 (down $1.1B). CapEx was $1.2B, yielding free cash flow of approximately $3.1B. Cash interest payments consumed $2.3B and cash taxes $1.9B. The FCF/Net Income ratio is artificially high because net income was depressed by non-cash charges in prior years and inflated by the debt extinguishment gain in 2025.

### Streaming ARPU Compression

Global ARPU fell to $6.92 from $7.76 (down 11% ex-FX). Domestic ARPU declined to $10.79 from $11.89 (down 9%). Subscriber growth of 13% is being more than offset on a per-unit revenue basis by the mix shift toward lower-value international and wholesale distribution. Revenue growth will continue to lag subscriber growth, requiring accelerating subscriber additions just to maintain flat streaming revenue.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. Streaming subscriber growth (13% YoY to 131.6M) and Studios performance (54% EBITDA growth in 2025) represent genuine positive momentum. However:

1. **Linear decline overwhelms growth segments.** Linear Networks generated $6.4B in EBITDA — more than Streaming and Studios combined. Its 21% EBITDA decline ($1.74B) exceeded the combined improvement in Streaming and Studios ($1.59B). The math worsens as linear continues to contract.

2. **Streaming profitability is nascent.** While Adjusted EBITDA improved, the streaming segment still reports a GAAP operating loss. Sustainable profitability requires either ARPU stabilization or continued rapid subscriber growth at declining marginal cost.

3. **Studios are cyclical.** The 2025 theatrical slate was exceptionally strong. Film results are lumpy, and a weak slate year could reverse the segment's gains rapidly.

4. **No organic growth in consolidated revenue.** Revenue fell 5% in both 2024 and 2025. There is no visible path to consolidated organic revenue growth without the merger.

Estimate revisions show the company expected to report losses in both the current quarter ($-0.09) and next quarter ($-0.07), with full-year losses in both FY2026 ($-0.08) and FY2027 ($-0.00). The earnings surprise track record is poor: 3 misses in 4 quarters, with only the Q2 2025 quarter (which likely benefited from the debt extinguishment gain and Studios performance) showing a beat.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- IP portfolio (DC, Harry Potter, HBO prestige brand) is world-class and cannot be replicated
- 131.6M streaming subscribers represents meaningful scale — 4th largest globally
- Olympic Games rights through 2032 provide a long-duration sports anchor
- Seven consecutive $40M+ domestic openings in 2025 demonstrates Studios execution capability

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Streaming competes against Netflix (~300M+ subs), Disney+ (~150M+), Amazon, and Apple — all with deeper balance sheets
- Lost NBA rights; replaced but not fully compensated
- Linear TV decline is industry-wide and accelerating
- Junk credit rating constrains competitive investment in content and technology
- $19.7B in content purchase obligations vs. declining revenue creates margin pressure

The PSKY merger, if completed, would create the largest combined media company with unmatched content breadth (HBO + CBS + Paramount+ + Nickelodeon + DC + Harry Potter + NFL + Olympics + March Madness). Scale economics could improve competitive positioning. Without the merger, WBD faces a grinding standalone fight against better-capitalized competitors while managing a declining linear business and massive debt load.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Merger Failure (Probability: 20-30%)

Combining two of Hollywood's Big Five studios has obvious antitrust implications. If the deal fails:

- WBD may owe PSKY a $3.0B termination fee plus up to $1.5B reimbursement
- The company returns to standalone operations with junk credit and a $15B bridge loan due June 2027
- Stock would likely trade back toward $10-15, representing 45-64% downside
- If PSKY walks due to regulatory failure, PSKY owes WBD a $7.0B reverse termination fee — partially offsetting the damage

### Secondary Risks

**Bridge Loan Refinancing:** The $15B secured bridge loan matures June 2027. If the merger doesn't close before then, WBD must refinance at junk credit ratings. Given the current rate environment (FOMC unanimously holding at 3.64%), refinancing costs would be substantially higher than current rates.

**Linear Acceleration:** If cord-cutting accelerates beyond the current 9% subscriber decline rate, EBITDA erosion could overwhelm streaming gains faster than expected.

**ARPU Deterioration:** Continued global ARPU compression undermines the streaming growth narrative. If ARPU falls faster than subscriber growth, streaming revenue could plateau or decline.

**Content Execution Risk:** A weaker 2026-2027 theatrical slate would directly impact Studios EBITDA and streaming content pipeline.

## Investment Thesis

Gulf sovereign financing proceeds without FCC intervention. The ticking fee adds $0.25-0.50 if closing extends into late 2026 or early 2027. Annualized return of 17-26% depending on timing.

**Timing window:** 3-9 months. The deal deadline is March 4, 2027 (extendable to June 4, 2027), but regulatory signals should emerge well before then. The shareholder vote on April 23 is expected to pass and is not the short catalyst.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 2.7% SI with 2.1 days to cover. Very light — the short is uncrowded, providing room for a position but also suggesting informed investors are not positioning heavily for deal failure.

**Borrow and options signal:** The put/call OI ratio (3.11) and volume ratio (10.53) indicate institutional hedging. IV is cheap near-term but elevated at 1-month (47.6%), reflecting the vote catalyst. The merger pin effect makes the stock relatively stable near $27-28 until a regulatory catalyst forces a reprice.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the deal breaks, target $12-15 (55-45% downside from $27.43). If the deal closes, $31 (13% upside). Risk/reward: 3.5-4.2x downside vs. upside on a probability-weighted basis assuming 25% deal-break probability. Crude expected value: (25% × 50% downside) = 12.5% expected gain vs. (75% × 13% upside) = 9.75% expected loss. Net expected value of the short is modestly positive but the asymmetry is unfavorable because 75% probability of deal closure means the short loses most of the time.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The deal-break probability (~20-30%) is too low to justify an active short against a 75% probability of losing 13% to the deal close. The trade has positive expected value on paper but a poor Sharpe ratio due to the high probability of being wrong. Merger-arb specialists may express bearish views through puts spanning the regulatory decision window rather than outright short stock. For fundamental investors, avoid.

## Conclusion

WBD is a binary merger-arbitrage situation with a 13% spread to the $31 PSKY deal price. The spread exists because regulatory risk is real: DOJ antitrust subpoenas, UK CMA investigation, and potential FCC scrutiny of $24B in Gulf sovereign financing. The standalone business is deeply problematic — declining revenue, junk credit ratings, $32.8B in debt, a $15B bridge loan due June 2027, ARPU compression in streaming, and structural linear TV decline. The 2025 "profitability" was manufactured by a $2.96B one-time gain. Insiders sold aggressively post-announcement.

The analyst consensus overwhelmingly rates WBD as Hold (17 of 21 analysts), reflecting the merger-arb nature of the position. Current ratings represent a significant deterioration from three months ago, when the distribution was 1 Strong Buy / 7 Buy / 12 Hold / 1 Sell — suggesting growing concern about regulatory risk or standalone fundamentals.

The risk/reward is asymmetric in the wrong direction for equity investors: 13% upside if the deal closes vs. 45-64% downside if it fails. Merger-arb specialists may find the spread attractive based on their assessment of regulatory probabilities, but that is a specialized trade requiring portfolio-level hedging. For a general equity portfolio, avoid.
