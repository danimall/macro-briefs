**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OUTFRONT Media is a pure-play U.S. out-of-home (OOH) advertising REIT built around an irreplaceable billboard footprint in the largest U.S. markets, paired with a transit franchise dominated by the New York MTA. The investment story has shifted materially over the past four quarters. The MTA franchise, which generated $484M of cumulative impairments through 2024 and was the company's signature liability, has reversed into a contributor: full-year Transit Adjusted OIBDA rose from $8.3M to $43.1M, Q1 2026 Transit Adjusted OIBDA improved from -$14.2M to -$1.4M, and management expects to begin recouping sunk MTA deployment costs in 2026. That reversal, combined with restructuring savings and a digital revenue mix that now generates roughly 4-5x static-board revenue, drove a Q1 2026 inflection: revenue +10% to $429.6M, operating income $55.9M (from $13.9M), and Adjusted OIBDA +56% to $100.4M.

The stock has nearly doubled over the trailing twelve months (from ~$15.73 to ~$31.13) as this turnaround played out, and estimate revisions confirm the fundamental improvement: current-quarter EPS estimates are up +35.6% over 90 days and next-year estimates up +17.4%. The difficulty is that price has tracked the improvement closely. At ~29.5x trailing earnings and ~3.8% yield, OUT trades at a premium to the Other Specialized REIT peer median (P/E 18.3, yield 5.8%) and roughly in line with billboard-pure peer Lamar (28.1x). The asymmetric upside the turnaround once offered has largely been realized in the share price.

We rate OUT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis is specific: continued AFFO compounding driven by Transit/MTA recovery, MTA cost recoupment beginning this year, restructuring-driven SG&A leverage, and a new CEO repositioning the business toward programmatic/ad-tech OOH. These are identifiable catalysts that should support the current level and modest appreciation. The thesis is capped by 6.44x debt/equity in a higher-for-longer rate environment, flat billboard organic growth with continued LA erosion, management's own hedged 2026 liquidity language, and net insider selling into strength. We do not see the asymmetry that would justify a BUY at this price, but the fundamental trajectory and yield support are sufficient to hold rather than avoid.

## Company Overview

OUTFRONT operates one of the largest U.S. OOH advertising portfolios, competing nationally with Lamar, Clear Channel Outdoor, JCDecaux, and Intersection. The business runs two segments:

- **Billboard (~76% of revenue, $1,391M in 2025)** — the core asset. Adjusted OIBDA margin ~38%, ~38,240 displays across ~120 markets including the 25 largest DMAs, anchored by NYC, LA, and SF. The structural moat is scarcity: ~75% of U.S. billboards are "legal nonconforming," grandfathered structures that cannot be rebuilt under current zoning. OUTFRONT owns the structures and permits while leasing ~19,100 underlying land sites from ~17,500 landlords.
- **Transit (~24% of revenue, $431M in 2025)** — lower-margin (~10% Adjusted OIBDA), dominated by the New York MTA, which represents 61% of transit revenue. Most transit contracts are won via competitive municipal bids and are terminable for convenience, with the MTA agreement an exception.

Strategy centers on digitization (digital was $649M of 2025 revenue, earning 4-5x static-board rates), yield management, and an emerging push into programmatic/ad-tech OOH. The company sold its Canadian business in June 2024 for C$410M, sharpening the U.S.-only focus. New CEO Nick Brien, formerly CEO of Dentsu Americas and IPG Mediabrands, is positioning the company as a "tech-enabled in-real-life marketing platform."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been structurally flat.** Reported revenue moved from $1,820.6M (2023) to $1,830.9M (2024) to $1,831.7M (2025), with organic growth of +2% in 2025. Underneath the flat top line is a mix shift: Transit grew +12% on the MTA recovery while Billboard declined ~1% on "lost billboards," concentrated in Los Angeles, a headwind management expects to persist into the first half of 2026.

**Cash profitability is improving.** Adjusted OIBDA rose to $499.3M (27.3% margin, up from 25.4%), AFFO rose +10% to $337.7M, and FCF was $218.8M (OCF $307.6M less $88.8M capex). The headline GAAP earnings line is distorted by non-operating items: a -$430M net loss in 2023 (driven by $466M of MTA impairments plus $48M Transit goodwill impairment), $258M net income in 2024 (inflated by a $161M Canada-sale gain), and "clean" net income of $147M in 2025.

**Q1 2026 caveats.** Two items temper the headline growth: Q1 included $13.5M of one-time condemnation proceeds, and the comparison flattered against a weak Q1 2025. Normalize for the condemnation item before extrapolating the +56% Adjusted OIBDA growth.

**The balance sheet is the defining constraint.** $2.6B of debt against ~$710M equity yields a 6.44 debt/equity ratio, with retained earnings of -$1.91B reflecting cumulative dividends and impairments. The Altman Z-Score of 0.8 sits in the "distress zone," but that figure is distorted by REIT mechanics: thin equity, mortgage-like debt, and negative retained earnings from mandatory distributions. The covenant picture is materially more reassuring: Consolidated Total Leverage of 4.4x against a 6.5x covenant max, and Net Secured Leverage of 1.5x against 4.5x. The maturity schedule was termed out in a September 2025 refinancing: nothing due in 2026, $650M in 2027, $1.0B in 2029-2030, and $950M in 2031+, with a term loan to 2032 and revolver to 2030.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is held at $0.30/quarter ($1.20/yr, ~$210M annually), well covered by AFFO. The Series A preferred fully converted to common in November 2025. A $300M ATM program sits largely unused ($232.5M capacity), functioning as a liquidity backstop. The genuine caution flag is management's repeated, hedged language in both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q that liquidity "may be adversely affected if cash on hand and operating cash flows decrease in 2026," unusual for a stable REIT and worth monitoring.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, including a 19.4% beat in Q3 2025 and a $0.11 actual vs. $0.00 estimate in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three mechanisms with varying durability.

First, the **Transit/MTA recovery** is the most concrete. With MTA revenue now exceeding management's internal model, no new impairments were taken in 2025 or Q1 2026, and the company expects to begin recouping sunk MTA costs in 2026 by reclassifying intangibles back to prepaid. Of ~$630M deployed into the MTA, only $33.9M has been recouped, so the recoupment runway is long if ridership and ad demand hold.

Second, **digital conversion** continues to drive mix and yield. Each conversion lifts revenue per display without proportional capex once installed, supporting the margin trajectory.

Third, the **ad-tech pivot** under new CEO Nick Brien is the most speculative lever. The February 2026 AdQuick deal licenses an OOH "sales cloud" for $17M/year on a three-year non-cancellable term plus up to $20M of equity investment. If programmatic adoption accelerates yield, this could break the multi-year revenue plateau and support a multiple re-rating. It also adds ~$17M/year of fixed cost to a flat top line, so the payoff is binary on execution.

The estimate revision data confirms the fundamental momentum: current-quarter EPS up +25.5% over 30 days and +35.6% over 90 days; next-year up +10.7% over 30 days and +17.4% over 90 days. The 90-day current-quarter revision exceeds the +30% threshold that typically signals a genuine inflection. The qualifier is that this revision velocity is coming off a depressed trough (the MTA-impaired base) and is partly mechanical recovery rather than structural acceleration; the billboard core remains flat-to-down. We weight the revision signal positively for the next 12 months but do not treat it as evidence of a durable high-growth rate, because the largest driver (Transit normalization) is finite.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$31.13 and ~29.5x trailing earnings, OUT is not cheap on headline metrics, and the cycle-adjustment argument is weaker here than for a true earnings-trough story. On forward estimates, current-year EPS of $1.33 and next-year EPS of $1.39 imply forward multiples of ~23.4x and ~22.4x, still full for a flat-revenue REIT, though more defensible given the AFFO trajectory. On AFFO, $337.7M against a ~$5.5B market cap implies ~16x P/AFFO, a more reasonable figure for the asset quality.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. OUT's 29.5x P/E sits well above the Other Specialized REIT median and its 3.8% yield well below, suggesting the market already credits the turnaround. The most relevant single comp is billboard-pure Lamar at 28.1x and 4.2% yield: OUT trades roughly in line with Lamar on P/E but at a lower yield, and Lamar carries a higher-quality, billboard-heavy mix without OUTFRONT's transit drag. The other peers (EPR 18.3x/6.3%, FCPT 22.4x/5.8%, GLPI 15.1x/6.8%) are net-lease and gaming REITs with more stable cash flows and higher yields, making them poor structural comps but useful reminders of where REIT capital can earn yield with less leverage.

Analyst price targets bracket $32 (low) to $38 (high), with a $37.5 median, implying ~3% to ~22% upside. On inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, consensus targets often lag reality. OUT partially qualifies, with estimates rising and a forward multiple below 30x, but the revision velocity is concentrated in finite Transit normalization rather than a durable growth re-rating, so we treat the target band as a fair guide rather than a conservative floor.

We assign a below-neutral valuation score. The multiple is full relative to peers and to the flat organic growth profile, the bulk of the easy re-rating from the MTA scare has occurred, and the yield no longer compensates for the leverage. A compressed multiple here would reflect the leverage and flat core, not a value trap, but neither is there a clear mispricing to exploit at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

OUTFRONT's competitive position is the strongest part of the story and is improving at the margin. The legal-nonconforming billboard inventory is a durable structural moat; these structures cannot be replicated under current zoning, conferring scarcity value on the footprint in NYC, LA, and SF. No single advertiser exceeds 2% of revenue, so concentration risk is industry-level (entertainment 18%, retail 11%, legal 10%) and geographic (NY + LA), not client-specific.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The MTA franchise turnaround improves the transit competitive position after years of value destruction, and digital conversion strengthens yield versus static-only local operators. Against this, billboard erosion in LA and the structurally lower-margin, capital-intensive, terminable-for-convenience nature of municipal transit contracts weigh on the franchise relative to billboard-pure Lamar. The enterprise/national revenue mix is also shrinking (44%→37% in Q1), meaning local/regional advertising is carrying current growth, a watch item if national brand budgets soften in an H2 consumer slowdown.

OOH retains a structural advantage as one of the few "un-skippable, un-blockable" media formats, and the programmatic adoption curve favors scaled operators with measurement and attribution capabilities. OUTFRONT's ad-tech pivot is an attempt to capture that, though execution is unproven. On balance, the moat is real and the trajectory modestly positive, supporting an above-neutral competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and financing dependence.** The REIT model must distribute ≥90% of taxable income, leaving minimal retained cash. The September 2025 refinancing removed near-term maturity risk, but the higher-for-longer rate environment raises refinancing costs on the $650M 2027 maturity and beyond. Management's repeated hedged language about 2026 liquidity uncertainty is the most direct internal caution signal.

**MTA franchise asymmetry.** The single largest swing factor. The narrative has flipped from disaster to tailwind, but ~$630M was deployed with only $33.9M recouped, and ~$30-40M/year of MTA spend continues. A reversal in NYC ridership or transit ad demand would reintroduce impairment risk on the remaining intangibles.

**Goodwill concentration.** $2.0B of goodwill (~38% of assets) sits entirely in Billboard. The 2025 qualitative test passed comfortably, but any sustained billboard downturn could trigger impairment.

**Cyclicality and consumer exposure.** Pure ad-spend exposure ties revenue to discretionary marketing budgets. With national brand mix already shrinking, an ad-spend slowdown is a tangible near-term risk.

**Execution risk on the ad-tech pivot.** The AdQuick arrangement adds fixed cost plus up to $20M equity to a flat-revenue base; the payoff depends on programmatic adoption that has not yet materialized in the numbers.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term ATM IV of 35.4% is modestly rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.6%, with an IV percentile of just 8% within the current chain; the market is not pricing an imminent binary event. The put/call skew of 16.4% at the 1-month tenor reflects elevated put demand, suggesting some downside hedging into the near term, though this flips to -6.9% skew at the 3-month tenor, where uncertainty is more balanced. The flat term structure (near 35.4% ≈ far 37.4%) indicates evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a dated catalyst. The reported jump in ATM IV to 282.6% in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. The implied ±10.8% move and 4.2% short interest (2.6 days to cover) point to a stock with manageable squeeze risk and no extreme positioning in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$38, +22%).** The MTA recovery proves durable, cost recoupment accelerates beyond 2026, and Transit Adjusted OIBDA continues to compound toward historical levels. Digital conversion and restructuring SG&A savings drive AFFO higher, and the AdQuick/programmatic pivot begins to break the billboard plateau, supporting a multiple re-rating toward Lamar-plus levels. In this scenario AFFO grows double-digits and the dividend rises, with the stock re-rating on improving cash-flow quality.

**Bear Case (~$23, -26%).** A consumer-driven ad-spend slowdown pressures the flat billboard core while LA erosion persists. The MTA recovery stalls or reverses, reintroducing impairment risk. Higher-for-longer rates raise refinancing costs into the 2027 maturity, the ad-tech pivot adds fixed cost without yield benefit, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (~18x), de-rating the stock as the leverage becomes more visible in a slowing environment.

**Base Case (~$33, +6%).** Transit recovery continues at a decelerating pace, billboard stays roughly flat, AFFO grows mid-to-high single digits, and the dividend is maintained. The stock holds near current levels with modest appreciation as estimate revisions stabilize and the multiple stays full but does not compress.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is continued AFFO compounding from the Transit/MTA recovery, MTA cost recoupment beginning in 2026, restructuring-driven SG&A leverage, and digital conversion: a specific, dated set of catalysts that justify holding for appreciation rather than only for yield.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), where we will look for normalized (ex-condemnation) Adjusted OIBDA growth, confirmation that MTA recoupment has begun, and billboard organic trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Billboard organic revenue returns to positive growth (LA erosion stabilizes) alongside continued Transit recovery, breaking the multi-year top-line plateau.
- Forward P/AFFO compresses below ~14x on continued AFFO growth — a pullback to the high-$20s on unchanged fundamentals would restore the asymmetry.
- Confirmed evidence the AdQuick/programmatic initiative is generating incremental yield rather than only fixed cost.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any new MTA-related impairment or a reversal in Transit Adjusted OIBDA, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- Consolidated Total Leverage rising toward 5.5x+ (against the 6.5x covenant) or a dividend cut, indicating financing stress materializing per management's hedged liquidity language.
- Billboard revenue declining >3% YoY as a national ad-spend slowdown takes hold.

## Conclusion

OUTFRONT pairs an irreplaceable U.S. billboard portfolio and a genuinely recovering transit franchise with a heavily levered REIT balance sheet and a flat organic-growth core. The fundamental trajectory is positive: the MTA story has flipped from the company's largest liability to a tailwind, Q1 2026 marked a clear inflection, and estimate revisions confirm the improvement. The constraint is that the share price has nearly doubled in twelve months and now captures most of that improvement: OUT trades at a premium to its peer group with full forward multiples, the easy MTA re-rating largely behind it, and net insider selling into the rally. The leverage is real even if the distress-zone Z-Score is a REIT-mechanics artifact, and management's own hedged 2026 liquidity language warrants attention.

We rate OUT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The recovery catalysts and dividend coverage support holding through the next 12 months, with a base case of modest appreciation toward $33. We would become more constructive on a pullback to the high-$20s that restores asymmetry, or on evidence the billboard core has resumed growth. We would turn negative on any MTA reversal, leverage deterioration, or a billboard slowdown driven by softening ad budgets.
