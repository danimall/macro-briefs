## Executive Summary

UBS has done what it said it would do. Three years after the government-brokered Credit Suisse acquisition roughly doubled its balance sheet, the integration is substantially complete: all clients migrated, more than 90% of legacy applications retired, more than 90% of expected cost synergies realized, $12.6bn of cumulative gross cost reductions delivered, and the Non-core & Legacy run-off unit reduced to a $52M quarterly pre-tax loss. Q2 2026 delivered group revenue of $13.3bn (+16% YoY), net profit attributable to shareholders of $2.8bn (+17% YoY), a 70% cost/income ratio, a 14.4% CET1 ratio and record invested assets of $7.3 trillion. The wealth franchise is gathering assets ($36bn of net new assets in the quarter, $73bn in H1) and converting them into contracted revenue: recurring net fee income +11% to $3.7bn, and the "My Way" discretionary solution up more than 75% YoY to above $40bn.

The problem is the price and the composition of the earnings being capitalized at it. At $53.76 the stock trades at 18.3x trailing twelve-month EPS of $2.94, 22.8x FY2025 reported EPS of $2.36, 1.85x book and 2.00x the $26.89 tangible book value per share UBS reported at June 30, 2026, against a trailing return on tangible equity of 11.5%. Paying 2.0x tangible book for 11.5% ROTE prices in a substantial and durable improvement in returns. A Gordon-growth cross-check makes the embedded assumption explicit: at a 3.5% growth rate and a 9% cost of equity, 2.0x tangible book requires roughly 14% sustainable ROTE. The market is already paying for the bull case.

Two things make that uncomfortable. First, both engines of recent earnings growth are running out. Only about $0.9bn of the ~$13.5bn gross savings target remains for two quarters, versus $1.1bn delivered in Q2 alone; from 2027 growth must come from franchise operating leverage rather than integration. And the Q2 beat leaned on the Investment Bank, where pre-tax profit more than doubled to $1.2bn at a pre-tax RoE above 23% on Equities +53% and Capital Markets +55%, growth rates that are not a run rate. Second, the Swiss parliamentary vote on capital reform, which would require UBS to fully capitalize foreign subsidiaries with CET1 and is cited at $20-26bn of incremental capital, was still unresolved at the information cutoff. Shares reached 17-year highs in December 2025 on optimism about a favourable compromise and are now 2.5% below the July 15 high of $55.15. The compromise is priced; the strict outcome is not. The mean analyst target of $55.03 is 2.4% above spot and the median of $52.50 is below it. Trim.

## Company Overview

UBS Group AG is Switzerland's sole remaining globally systemically important bank and, on disclosed invested assets, the largest pure-play global wealth manager. Total assets were $1,707bn at June 30, 2026, up from $1,104bn at end-2022 before the Credit Suisse acquisition.

| Global Wealth Management | 25,960 | 24,516 | +5.9% | 52.4% |
| Investment Bank | 12,340 | 10,948 | +12.7% | 24.9% |
| Personal & Corporate Banking | 9,154 | 9,334 | −1.9% | 18.5% |
| Asset Management | 3,156 | 3,182 | −0.8% | 6.4% |

The sum of segment lines exceeds reported group revenue by $525M in FY2025, most plausibly credit loss expense or consolidation eliminations treated differently in the standardized series; segment shares should be read as approximate. The shape is roughly half fee-based wealth management, a quarter capital markets and advisory, a fifth Swiss domestic banking, and a small asset manager.

The structural feature that drives everything else in this report is a ~$1.7tn balance sheet against Swiss nominal GDP of roughly $0.9-1.0tn (approximate; not sourced from a primary statistical release), a ratio near 1.7-1.9x. That arithmetic is the root of the Swiss capital debate and of a single-jurisdiction political exposure that has no analogue at JPMorgan or HSBC.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings history, read correctly.** Revenue moved $35,541M (FY2021) → $34,534M → $39,797M → $48,060M → $49,049M (FY2025), with TTM through June 2026 at $52,396M. Net income ran $7,457M → $7,630M → $27,366M → $5,085M → $7,767M, with TTM $9,520M and diluted EPS $2.06 → $2.25 → $8.30 → $1.52 → $2.36 → $2.94 TTM.

FY2023's $27.4bn is not operating earnings; it is dominated by the one-off gain on acquiring Credit Suisse below the fair value of its net assets. (The commonly cited magnitude, approximately $29bn, could not be verified against the primary filing and should be treated as an open item.) The economically meaningful read: standalone UBS earned roughly $7.5bn a year in 2021-22; the combined entity earned $5.1bn in 2024 absorbing peak integration cost, $7.8bn in 2025, and is running at $9.5bn on a trailing basis. Three years after roughly doubling the balance sheet, UBS has just cleared its pre-merger standalone profit level.

FY2025's headline +2.1% revenue growth understates the underlying picture. Excluding Non-core & Legacy revenue, which fell from $1,605M to $154M as the run-off portfolio was deliberately wound down, revenue grew from $46,455M to $48,895M, or +5.3%. NCL wind-down is capital-releasing by design and should not be read as revenue weakness.

**Q2 2026.** Revenue $13.3bn (+16%), reported net profit $2.8bn (+17.2% vs $2.39bn), diluted EPS $0.87, reported pre-tax profit ~$3.6bn (implying a 22.2% effective tax rate), underlying pre-tax profit cited at $3.9bn (+45%), cost/income 70%, credit loss expense $121M at a 7bp cost of risk, credit-impaired exposures ~1% of loans.

**A consensus nuance.** The widely reported "17% beat" is measured against a company-compiled analyst poll of $2.39bn net profit, a figure that coincidentally equals the prior-year quarter, meaning that poll modelled flat YoY profit. The broader third-party EPS consensus was $0.93 for the quarter; UBS reported $0.87, a 6.3% miss. Both statements are arithmetically true against their respective baselines. The four-quarter surprise record against third-party consensus is +40.4% (Q3'25), −31.5% (Q4'25), +5.2% (Q1'26), −6.3% (Q2'26): two beats, two misses, and high dispersion. That argues for lower confidence in point forward estimates than the headline "beat" framing implies, particularly with only 1-3 contributing analysts in the current revision set.

**Segment quality.** Global Wealth Management pre-tax profit rose 38% to $2.0bn with 7pp of positive operating jaws, recurring net fee income +11% to $3.7bn, and transaction-based income +23% to $1.5bn (a twelfth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth in that line). The Investment Bank delivered revenue +31% to $3.7bn with pre-tax profit more than doubling to $1.2bn and pre-tax RoE above 23%, driven by Capital Markets +55%, Equities +53% and Global Banking +33%, while fixed income, rates and currencies fell 21% and Advisory fell 5%. Asset Management revenue was −2% (management attributes this to the prior O'Connor disposal; +5% excluding disposals) with pre-tax profit +9% to $237M. Regionally, APAC group pre-tax profit doubled and Americas rose 85%.

Two readings of the IB result are available and the evidence does not separate them. One is cycle: equity issuance and trading conditions in H1 2026 were unusually favourable, and 53-55% growth rates reverse. The other is share gain: UBS absorbed Credit Suisse's equities and prime franchise, and Global Banking +33% is more consistent with wallet capture than with market beta. Both are probably partly true. What matters for a buyer at 18x trailing is that the Investment Bank now supplies a larger share of a multiple that assumes durability, and management has said it is reallocating balance sheet from fixed income toward equities, increasing rather than reducing that exposure.

**The cost program is nearly spent.** Cumulative gross savings were $12.6bn at end-Q2 against a year-end target of approximately $13.5bn, leaving roughly $0.9bn for two quarters versus $1.1bn delivered in Q2 alone. Gross savings are also not net cost reduction: reported Q2 expenses were lifted by compensation, litigation and currency, and the retained benefit is visible in the 70% cost/income ratio rather than in the $12.6bn figure. An investor comparing "$13.5bn of savings" to a roughly $35bn expense base would materially overstate the margin actually captured. Offsetting this, the roll-off of integration expense itself is a distinct forward tailwind: ~$750M is guided for H2 2026, split evenly between quarters, with residual amounts in 2027.

**Capital and the distribution mechanics.** CET1 was 14.4% at June 30 against a stated ~14% operating target. Management said the new $3bn buyback accrual lowered the CET1 ratio by about 60bps while CET1 capital fell $0.8bn net over the quarter. Working backward, $3.0bn ÷ 0.006 implies risk-weighted assets near $500bn and CET1 capital near $72bn; a $550bn RWA base would give roughly $79bn. On the $500bn estimate, the 0.4pp buffer above target equals about $2.0bn of excess CET1, less than the $3bn program. The buyback is therefore a claim on forward earnings and RWA release rather than a distribution of an existing stockpile, consistent with management's "subject to performance and regulatory considerations" language. These RWA and CET1 figures are derived, not verified from a primary filing, and carry roughly a ±10% band; the direction of the conclusion is unchanged across that band.

**Tangible book per share is falling.** Common equity declined from $90,213M at December 31, 2025 to $89,165M at June 30, 2026 despite substantial first-half profit: retained earnings +$3,264M, additional paid-in capital −$3,299M (buyback share cancellations), AOCI −$1,111M, treasury stock +$105M, common stock −$6M. Management's framing was direct: $3.4bn of planned dividends and repurchases more than offset total comprehensive income, taking TBVPS down 2% sequentially to $26.89. Repurchasing stock at 2.0x tangible book is TBVPS-dilutive by construction. Part of the decline is AOCI mark-to-market and FX translation that can reverse, and the annual Swiss dividend falls in H1 so the half-year figure is seasonally exaggerated. The character of the equity case is nonetheless a distribution story, not a book-value compounding story.

**A caution on cash-flow screens.** Standardized operating cash flow reads $86,068M (FY2023), $3,279M (FY2024), $21,851M (FY2025), $9,016M TTM. For a deposit-funded bank these figures capture changes in trading assets, loans, deposits and repo balances: funding and balance-sheet decisions, not earnings quality. Do not annualize the TTM figure or compute a P/FCF multiple. The relevant measures are net profit, the CET1 trajectory and distribution capacity.

**A further data caution.** Net loans moved $639.7bn → $580.0bn → $653.8bn across FY2023-25. A 9.3% contraction followed by a 12.7% expansion in a mortgage-and-Lombard-heavy book is implausible economically; USD/CHF translation of a large franc-denominated book is the likely driver, with a partially offsetting move in "other earning assets" ($169.5bn → $154.3bn → $179.5bn) suggesting possible reclassification. USD-reported balance-sheet growth rates for UBS should not be read as organic volume growth.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is wealth management, and it is working. Invested assets reached a record $7.3tn; GWM net new assets were $36bn in the quarter and $73bn in H1; recurring net fee income grew 11%, faster than assets; and discretionary mandate growth (My Way +75% YoY to above $40bn) converts episodic transaction revenue into contracted fees, which is the highest-quality growth available in the business. Management guides GWM net interest income to grow roughly 10% in 2026 versus 2025.

Against that, three of four operating segments are flat or shrinking on a reported basis. Personal & Corporate Banking revenue fell 1.9% in FY2025 with net interest income guided flat to slightly up in Q3 2026, constrained by Swiss policy rates outside UBS's control. Asset Management revenue fell 2%. The Investment Bank is growing fastest and is the least repeatable. Non-core & Legacy has already delivered its revenue drag.

Consensus, thin as it is, has current-year EPS at $3.37 (revised +1.8% over 30 days, two analysts) and next-year at $4.08 (−0.4% over 30 days, three analysts). Next-year $4.08 implies roughly $12.5bn of net income, about 31% above the $9.52bn trailing figure. That is achievable only if the H1 2026 run rate largely holds while integration expense rolls off, which requires the Investment Bank not to mean-revert. There is no estimate-revision inflection here: revisions are broadly flat, so the strongest forward-looking growth signal available is muted.

The character of the earnings story changes in 2027. Through 2026 growth has been manufactured from cost extraction and portfolio wind-down, both of which are self-determined and highly visible. From 2027 it must come from revenue and franchise operating leverage in a business where the largest segment grew 5.9% last year and the second-largest just posted cycle-peak returns.

Three cross-checks all land in the same place.

**Returns versus the multiple.** Paying 2.00x tangible book for 11.5% trailing ROTE gives an earnings yield on the purchase price of 5.75%. Management's ~17% underlying return on CET1 capital for H1 2026 is the bull anchor, but that figure adds back integration costs and is measured against a CET1 base smaller than tangible equity, so it is structurally higher than ROTE and cannot be compared directly with a P/TBV multiple.

**Implied sustainable returns.** Using P/TBV = (ROTE − g) ÷ (COE − g): at 14% ROTE, 3.5% growth and a 9% cost of equity, fair value is 1.91x TBV, or about $52.50 on a forward TBVPS near $27.50. Getting to 2.30x requires roughly 16% ROTE at the same 9% COE. A Swiss G-SIB with a balance sheet at 1.7-1.9x national GDP facing legislated capital increases deserves a higher COE, not a lower one. The current multiple already embeds close to the best plausible return outcome.

**Analyst reference points.** The consensus mean target is $55.03 (+2.4% from $53.76), the median is $52.50 (−2.3%), the high is $62.60 (+16.4%) and the low is $50.00 (−7.0%). Recommendation mix has been static for four months at one buy and two holds. Analyst targets can be treated as lagging floors when a stock is cheap against rising estimates; neither condition applies here, since the multiple is above the peer-implied fair range on reported returns and estimates are flat.

The one legitimately supportive number is 13.2x FY2027 consensus. If $4.08 is delivered, a 15x multiple gives $61 and the stock is undervalued today. That is the bull case, and it depends on the Investment Bank holding a cycle-peak return while the capital reform lands on the lenient side. Both may happen. Neither is discounted at a margin of safety.

**Base case: $55.** Constructed two ways for consistency: 2.0x an estimated forward TBVPS of ~$27.50, and roughly 14.5x normalized EPS of ~$3.80 (between FY2026 consensus $3.37 and FY2027 $4.08, haircutting IB peak revenue while crediting integration-cost roll-off). That is +2.3% from spot, +4.3% including the dividend.

**Bull case: $63.** Parliament adopts the 70-80% threshold or the 50% AT1-substitution compromise, the buyback runs to completion and is extended, IB share gain proves real, ROTE settles near 15-16% and the multiple expands to ~2.3x TBV. +17.2%. Consistent with the $62.60 analyst high.

**Bear case: $42.** The strict full-CET1 deduction is enacted, requiring ~$2.9-3.7bn of annual capital retention over a seven-year phase-in (roughly the entire current buyback), while Equities and Capital Markets revenue normalizes 25-30% and the multiple compresses to ~1.5x TBV. −21.9%. The stock traded $36.41-$38.83 in March 2026 while the strict version was live news, so this is a stress-tested level rather than a hypothetical.

Probability-weighting 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear gives roughly +0.7% price return, about +2.7% including the dividend, materially below a 9-10% cost of equity for a bank of this profile, with the bear case 1.3x the magnitude of the bull.

## Competitive Landscape

The wealth franchise is improving on trajectory, which is what matters. $36bn of quarterly net new assets is asset gathering, not market appreciation; fee income growing faster than assets indicates pricing and mix discipline; and the shift into discretionary mandates raises revenue durability. Twelve consecutive quarters of double-digit transaction-income growth in GWM suggests broad-based client activity rather than a single trading window.

The Swiss domestic position is structurally stronger post-2023 with Credit Suisse absorbed, though this is an inference from market structure rather than a claim UBS makes. It has not yet translated into P&C revenue growth, which fell 1.9% in FY2025 under Swiss rate compression.

In the Investment Bank, Capital Markets +55%, Equities +53% and Global Banking +33% exceed what a normal backdrop produces, supporting a share-gain reading alongside the cyclical one. FICC's 21% decline is the clear relative weakness, and reallocating balance sheet away from it toward equities is a directional bet rather than a neutral optimization.

Two qualifications. First, a shifting revenue mix, with the Investment Bank rising from 22.8% of FY2024 segment revenue to 24.9% in FY2025, tells us about mix, not about client or counterparty diversification; no client-concentration data was available, and faster IB growth is plausibly concentration into a more cyclical and more counterparty-exposed business. Second, US wealth management adviser attrition, which management acknowledged as continuing and industry-wide with an expected normalization around 2026 and a conceded lag in client inflows. That candour sits awkwardly beside Americas GWM pre-tax profit up 47% on record revenue: adviser-led businesses lose assets after producers leave, not simultaneously. Group net new assets remain positive, so there is no evidence of net asset loss yet.

The decisive competitive dynamic is regulatory. UBS's wealth position is strong and improving while its regulatory cost of capital relative to US and UK peers may worsen by a legislated, non-market amount. Those two forces act on the same equity in opposite directions, and only one of them is scheduled.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Swiss capital reform — an unresolved, near-binary, thesis-defining event.** On April 22, 2026 the Federal Council submitted final proposals amending the Banking Act and Capital Adequacy Ordinance, requiring UBS to fully capitalize foreign subsidiaries with CET1 by deducting those investments from the CET1 buffer. Reported incremental requirements are $20-26bn, with a phase-in starting at 65% over seven years. Compromise options under parliamentary discussion include meeting up to 50% with Additional Tier 1 instruments or reducing the foreign-subsidiary threshold to 70-80%. The decision slipped from May to June to August 2026 and remained unresolved at the information cutoff. Against an estimated ~$72bn CET1 base, $20-26bn is a 28-36% increase, or roughly $2.9-3.7bn a year of retention over seven years, approximately the entire current buyback program. The mitigating case deserves genuine weight: on an H1 2026 run rate UBS may have $11-12bn of annualized earnings capacity, and further NCL RWA release could fund retention while leaving $6-8bn for distributions; the SNB stated on July 2, 2026 that UBS already meets the proposed requirements, though UBS publicly disputed that assessment the same day (the substance of the rebuttal is unconfirmed, and the disagreement is probably definitional, current versus fully phased-in requirements). The asymmetry is what matters: shares hit 17-year highs in December 2025 on compromise optimism and are 2.5% below the July high, so a lenient outcome is largely in the price while the strict outcome is not.

**2. Legacy Credit Suisse litigation — unquantified.** Reported Q2 expenses were lifted partly by litigation. No information on the size or status of legacy provisions or contingent liabilities could be obtained; targeted searches on AT1-related claims returned nothing, and the 20-F provisions note was inaccessible (SEC Archives returned HTTP 403 on repeated attempts). This is an evidence gap, not a finding that exposure is small.

**3. Investment Bank cycle concentration.** A pre-tax RoE above 23% is a peak-type return. If capital-markets conditions normalize, a meaningful share of the earnings supporting an 18x trailing multiple reverses, and management is increasing rather than reducing equities exposure.

**4. Credit normalization from a floor.** A 7bp cost of risk and ~1% credit-impaired exposures are exceptionally benign. On a $663bn loan book, normalization toward a through-cycle 25-35bps would add roughly $1.2-1.9bn of annual credit cost against $9.5bn of trailing net income. Guidance is for P&C credit loss expense of ~CHF 75M per quarter in H2 2026 and below CHF 300M for the year.

**5. Single-jurisdiction political concentration.** A balance sheet at 1.7-1.9x Swiss GDP creates recurring political exposure with no peer analogue. There is no evidence of a concrete redomiciliation plan, and any such claim should be treated as unverified.

**6. FX translation.** UBS reports in USD with a substantial CHF cost base and loan book. Franc strength inflates both the reported balance sheet and reported expenses; the ADR embeds unhedged CHF/USD exposure. This is a structural feature of the security rather than a manageable risk.

**7. Deposit behaviour.** Deposits were $784.8bn at June 30, marginally below $788.4bn at December 31 (−0.4%). LCR 177% and NSFR 115% are comfortable. The relevant risk is the demonstrated speed at which a modern deposit franchise can run, which is the reason UBS owns Credit Suisse.

**8. Reconciliation and governance gaps.** The cited $3.9bn underlying Q2 pre-tax profit does not reconcile cleanly with a reported ~$3.6bn PBT plus $352M of revenue adjustments and $645M of integration expense; at least one secondary summary is imprecise. As a foreign private issuer UBS files no DEF 14A, and no compensation, board-composition or related-party data was obtained, so governance alignment is unexamined. Section 16 exemption for FPI officers means no insider-transaction signal exists in either direction; the Form 4 records that appear under the UBS ticker are UBS reporting as a 10% owner of third-party securities and must not be read as insider dealing in UBS shares.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 26.8% against 26.0% one-year realized (effectively fair) and sits at only the 12th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (26.8% at 10 days, 27.8% at 38 days, 30.7% at 101 days), the normal, unstressed shape. The nearest expiry prices a ±3.9% move.

The market is not pricing a violent near-term binary despite a parliamentary vote described as imminent. Two readings are available: participants expect a compromise or a further procedural delay, or near-term protection has become complacent. The one signal pointing the other way is near-term put/call skew of 7.9%, characterized as elevated put demand, alongside a 1.24 put/call volume ratio at the 10-day tenor, hedging concentrated precisely where the event risk sits, while the 6-month skew of −1.0% and 0.38 put/call volume ratio show no longer-dated fear. Short interest is negligible at 0.2% of float (6.2M shares, 3.3 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic. Cheap near-term volatility at the 12th percentile makes protection inexpensive for holders who want to retain exposure through the vote rather than sell it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($63, +17.2%)

Parliament adopts the 70-80% threshold or the 50% AT1-substitution option, removing the overhang at a cost UBS can absorb from ordinary earnings; the SNB's July 2 assessment proves the better guide and the "$20-26bn hit" framing collapses into a narrative problem. The $3bn buyback completes by mid-2027 and is extended, dividends continue growing at the mid-teens rate accrued (DPS has gone $0.55 → $0.70 → $0.90 → $1.10 across FY2022-25), and the combined distribution yield of ~3.9% re-rates the equity. Integration expense of ~$750M in H2 2026 and residual 2027 amounts roll off, adding to reported earnings independently of gross synergies. The Investment Bank's 53-55% growth proves to be share capture from the absorbed Credit Suisse equities and prime franchise rather than market beta, so returns hold near 23% pre-tax RoE. GWM compounds at $70bn+ of annual net new assets with fee income growing faster than assets, ROTE settles at 15-16%, and FY2027 EPS reaches the $4.08 consensus. At 15x that gives $61; at 2.3x a forward TBVPS near $27.50 gives $63.

### Bear Case ($42, −21.9%)

The strict full-CET1 deduction is enacted, requiring roughly $2.9-3.7bn of annual capital retention against an excess CET1 cushion of only about $2.0bn above the ~14% target. The buyback is cut or suspended, removing the 1.82% buyback yield and the primary support for a 2.0x TBV multiple in a business earning 11.5% reported ROTE. Simultaneously, capital-markets conditions normalize and Equities and Capital Markets revenue falls 25-30%, taking the Investment Bank's contribution back toward FY2024 levels; the cost-synergy tailwind is gone, with only ~$0.9bn of the target remaining, so there is no offset. Credit normalizes from 7bps toward 25bps, adding ~$1.2bn of annual cost. US adviser attrition shows up in net new assets with the usual lag. The multiple compresses to 1.5x tangible book, below the Gordon-implied fair value at 14% ROTE, 3.5% growth and a 10% Swiss-adjusted cost of equity, giving roughly $41-42, above the 52-week low of $36.30 and consistent with where the shares actually traded in March 2026 when the strict proposal was live.

### What Tips the Balance

The franchise is good and getting better. The entry price is not. Base case sits 2.3% above spot, the mean analyst target 2.4% above and the median 2.3% below, and the probability-weighted total return is roughly +2.7% against a required return near 9-10%. The bear case is 1.3x the magnitude of the bull. There is no estimate-revision momentum to argue that consensus is stale, no insider signal available in either direction, and a scheduled binary within weeks whose favourable resolution is already reflected in a multiple that reached 17-year highs on exactly that expectation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Base case $55 (+2.3%), bull $63 (+17.2%), bear $42 (−21.9%). Horizon 6-12 months, anchored to the Swiss parliamentary vote and the Q3 2026 report.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the vote: the stock is 2.5% below its 52-week high, the median target is below spot, and near-term implied volatility at the 12th percentile of the chain means the market is charging little for the event. Reduce roughly one-third to one-half of the position now. For holders who want to retain franchise exposure through the vote, cheap near-term protection is the more capital-efficient alternative to a full exit, given a ±3.9% implied move on the nearest expiry against a bear case 22% below spot. Retain a core position: this is a high-quality wealth franchise, not an impaired business.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE** (any two of the following):
1. Parliament enacts the 70-80% threshold or the 50% AT1-substitution compromise **and** UBS reaffirms the $3bn buyback with completion by Q2 2027 and CET1 held near 14%.
2. Reported ROTE (not underlying RoCET1) reaches 13.5% or above for two consecutive quarters, validating the returns implied by a 2.0x TBV multiple.
3. Tangible book value per share stops declining: a Q3 or Q4 2026 print at or above $26.89 with distributions covered by comprehensive income.
4. A price near $46-47, which is roughly 1.7x forward tangible book and 11.5x FY2027 consensus, restoring a genuine margin of safety.
5. FY2027 consensus EPS revised above $4.08 by three or more contributors alongside two consecutive quarters of GWM net new assets above $35bn.

**Avoid triggers** (full thesis impairment):
1. Strict full-CET1 deduction enacted without AT1 substitution or threshold relief, combined with an explicit reduction, suspension or non-renewal of the buyback.
2. Investment Bank revenue down more than 25% YoY in any quarter without an offsetting GWM acceleration, confirming the cyclical rather than share-gain reading.

## Conclusion

UBS has executed the largest bank integration in a generation and emerged with a stronger franchise: $7.3tn of invested assets, a wealth business gathering $70bn+ a year with fee income outgrowing assets, a structurally improved Swiss domestic position, a 14.4% CET1 ratio, 177% LCR and a cost/income ratio of 70% that would have looked unattainable in 2023. Nothing in the current evidence suggests the business is deteriorating.

The recommendation rests on price and sequencing. At $53.76 the stock trades at 2.00x the tangible book value per share UBS itself reported at June 30, against 11.5% trailing return on tangible equity and a tangible book per share that is falling because distributions exceed comprehensive income. The two engines that produced recent earnings growth — roughly $12.6bn of realized cost synergies with only ~$0.9bn remaining, and an Investment Bank earning above 23% pre-tax RoE on Equities and Capital Markets growth of 53-55% — are both near their limits. The $3bn buyback exceeds the estimated ~$2.0bn of excess CET1 above target and depends on forward earnings and RWA release. And the Swiss parliamentary decision on a $20-26bn capital increase, the single largest determinant of distribution capacity, remained unresolved with the favourable compromise already reflected in a multiple that reached 17-year highs on that expectation.

Base case $55 against a spot of $53.76 leaves no margin of safety; the mean analyst target is 2.4% above and the median 2.3% below; the probability-weighted total return of roughly 2.7% falls short of a fair return for a G-SIB with an unresolved statutory capital question. Trim into strength, keep a core holding in the wealth franchise, and re-engage on either a resolved capital regime with the buyback intact or a price near $46-47.
