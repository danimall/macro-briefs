## Executive Summary

SpaceX is now a three-segment company — Space (Falcon/Starship/Dragon), Connectivity (Starlink), and AI (Grok, X, and rented GPU capacity) — following the February 2, 2026 common-control merger with xAI and the June 2026 IPO that raised $85,675M net. The first periodic report, the 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, shows revenue of $7,814M (+91.9% year over year) against a $541M net loss, a $2,086M half-year operating loss, and first-half free cash flow of negative $25,010M. Connectivity is the only profit engine, generating $1,656M of Q2 operating income (38.6% margin) on $1,367M of capex. The AI segment produced a $1,257M operating loss and consumed $15,828M of capex against $2,561M of revenue — a 6.2x capex-to-revenue ratio.

The first analytical task is fixing the share count. The 10-Q balance sheet shows 7,607M Class A and 5,569M Class B shares outstanding, totaling 13,176M. The market-capitalization field supplied by data vendors (~$1.06tn) reflects only the listed Class A count and omits roughly $741bn of Class B economic value at the current price. At $133.11, correct equity value is 13,176M × $133.11 = **$1,753,857M**, and with derived net cash of $60,497M ($100,009M of cash and marketable securities less $39,512M of debt and finance leases), **enterprise value is approximately $1,693,360M**. Every multiple built from the vendor figure is understated by roughly 40%.

On that corrected basis the stock trades at 54.2x annualized Q2 revenue, 119.7x annualized Q2 Adjusted EBITDA, and 107x the $1.24 next-year consensus EPS. A useful sanity check: Connectivity's Q2 segment Adjusted EBITDA of $2,597M annualizes to $10.4bn, which at a generous 25x supports roughly $260bn of enterprise value, leaving approximately **$1.43tn of the current EV attributed to Space plus AI** — the two segments that together lost $1,799M at the operating line and burned $17.0bn of capex in a single quarter. Growth is genuine and the balance sheet is strong enough to preclude near-term solvency risk, but the price already discounts execution across three unproven vectors simultaneously, and the dilution pipeline has negative convexity. We rate the shares REDUCE.

## Company Overview

**Space** designs, manufactures and launches reusable vehicles. Revenue splits between Launch Services (point-in-time recognition on payload deployment) and Launch & Development (up to 14-year government programs, cost-to-cost over time). Launches of the company's own Starlink satellites generate no inter-segment revenue; those costs are capitalized into PP&E, so reported Space revenue captures only third-party activity and understates operational output.

**Connectivity** is Starlink: over 10,200 satellites serving 167 countries and markets, 12.0M subscribers (from 6.0M), and monthly ARPU of $66 (from $85). A "subscriber" is a unique service line, not a household or a person — the company says so explicitly, and multiple lines can sit in one household.

**AI** is the former xAI/X business: Grok, the X platform, subscriptions, data licensing, API access, and, new in 2026, third-party GPU capacity rental. Installed nameplate compute draw was 1.4 GW at June 30 versus 0.4 GW a year earlier. Nameplate draw measures installed GPU power draw, not consumption or utilization, and excludes cooling and facility overhead.

Q2 revenue mix: Connectivity $4,291M (54.9%), AI $2,561M (32.8%), Space $962M (12.3%). Because the xAI merger was accounted for as a common-control transaction, prior-period comparatives are retrospectively recast, so year-over-year growth is like-for-like.

The AI segment shows $1,146M of positive Adjusted EBITDA and a $1,257M operating loss. The entire $2.4bn bridge is D&A ($1,885M, up 132% year over year) plus SBC ($516M). For a business whose depreciable base is compounding at this rate, the excluded D&A is a recurring replacement cost on a fleet with a short economic life, not a legacy sunk charge. Adjusted EBITDA is a structurally poor economic proxy here, and it is the metric on which the equity is most often defended.

Space deteriorated on a half-year basis: segment Adjusted EBITDA of $(556)M versus $+131M a year earlier, as Space R&D rose 64.6% to $2,006M while customer launches fell from 21 to 17 and Starship flights fell from three to one.

### Cash flow and capital intensity

First-half operating cash flow was +$3,466M (from +$351M). Investing was −$34,487M; financing +$100,291M. Derived free cash flow: $3,466M − $28,476M of PP&E purchases = **−$25,010M**, or −$25,886M including capitalized interest and intangible payments.

Two disclosures make accrual capital intensity worse than the cash line. $3,921M of PP&E was financed through "other financings" (non-cash; zero in the prior-year period), and $5,513M of PP&E purchases sat in accounts payable and accrued expenses at June 30 versus $4,260M a year earlier. Q2-only capex was $18,369M per the segment table — 39% above the $13,220M FactSet consensus, and the number that moved the stock on August 4 despite every revenue line beating.

We are not annualizing quarterly cash flow: Q2 OCF is not separately disclosed, Q4 is the seasonal peak for X advertising, and first-half working capital swung heavily (deferred revenue +$2,169M, receivables −$2,003M). As a labeled sensitivity, if Q2 OCF approximated $2.5–3.0bn, Q2 free cash flow was roughly −$15.4bn to −$15.9bn. At that pace, the $100.0bn liquidity stack funds approximately **six quarters** before new capital is required, against $22.2bn of non-cancelable commitments falling in 2027 alone and a stated plan to go from 1.4 GW installed to more than 2 GW by end-2026 and roughly 10 GW by end-2027.

### Balance sheet

Cash and equivalents $93,522M plus marketable securities $6,487M = $100,009M, plus $830M restricted cash and $1,098M of digital assets. Total debt principal $38,433M plus $1,079M of finance leases = $39,512M. Derived net cash **$60,497M**. The maturity ladder is remote: $944M remaining in 2026, $2,402M in 2027, $2,867M in 2028, $3,422M in 2029, $3,597M in 2030, $25,201M thereafter, with management stating no material principal due until July 15, 2031. The revolver was upsized to $5,000M undrawn (matures May 2031) with a Consolidated Leverage Ratio covenant of ≤3.75x; compliant at quarter-end. Shareholders' equity rose from $2,573M to $127,224M on IPO proceeds, preferred conversion and SBC.

The March 2026 refinancing is the period's most concrete, verifiable value creation. Roughly $17,460M of legacy X and xAI debt bearing SOFR+6.50% to 12.50% was extinguished and replaced with $25,000M of unsecured SpaceX Notes at a 5.855% weighted-average coupon and 11.7-year weighted-average maturity. Assuming Term SOFR near 4.3%, the extinguished stack cost roughly $1.88bn annually versus roughly $1.02bn at the new coupon — approximately **$0.86bn of annualized pre-tax savings against $1,164M of one-time prepayment penalties, a payback near 16 months**. The offsetting datapoint: at June 30 the notes carried at $24,852M against a fair value of $24,697M, trading slightly below carrying value six weeks after issuance. Credit investors have not marked the improvement up.

Two disclosed covenant breaches occurred and were cured — the xAI acquisition triggered technical default under the credit facility on assumed subsidiary debt (waived March 2, 2026), and a March amendment waived certain specified defaults. The benign reading is transaction-sequencing error in a fast reorganization; the less benign reading is that headroom was thin pre-refinancing. Both are resolved.

One structural item deserves weight in the financial-health assessment. A failed sale-leaseback with **Valor Equity Partners**, whose founder and CEO Antonio Gracias sits on the SpaceX board, carried $2,039M current plus $11,290M non-current — **$13,329M, or 34.7% of total debt principal** — up from $4,507M at year-end. Interest expense to Valor was $327M in Q2, which annualizes to a high single-digit to low-double-digit rate on a balance that grew within the quarter. Valor has been a long-term investor and GPU-equipment financing is scarce, so a fast related-party source is commercially explicable; "failed sale-leaseback" is a lease-classification outcome and carries no implication of impropriety. Without a proxy statement, we cannot confirm independent committee review.

**Financial health: 5.5.** $60.5bn net cash, a 2031 maturity wall and a $5bn undrawn revolver are genuinely resilient. Against that: −$25bn of half-year FCF, roughly six quarters of runway against a spending plan that requires far more than the current pace, $9.4bn of capital intensity sitting off the cash-flow line in payables and non-cash financings, and a third of the debt stack owed to a director-affiliated vehicle at roughly 10%.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 91.9% year over year is not in question, and it is broad within Connectivity and AI. Subscribers doubled to 12.0M. Connectivity's Enterprise & Government line reached $1,806M in Q2, +108% year over year, and is now 42% of segment revenue — the faster-growing and stickier half. The June 2026 price increase and $10 monthly kit rental fee barely affected the Q2 average and provide a Q3 tailwind. ARPU fell 22.4% to $66 but was flat sequentially, and management attributes the decline to international expansion and lower-priced plans; total consumer revenue rose $764M in the quarter, so the volume-for-price trade is working on the revenue line.

The AI segment is where growth and risk are both concentrated. The Anthropic arrangement began in May, Google around June, and the Reflection AI contract ($150M per month through 2029) began July 1 and contributed nothing to Q2. Management disclosed an additional $6.7bn of cloud services revenue contracted in the first weeks of Q3, ramping from October. That is real forward momentum.

Three cautions temper the growth score. First, **Space revenue declined 1.9% in the first half** while segment R&D rose 64.6%, mass to orbit fell to 1,041t from 1,102t, and only one Starship flew versus three. Flight 13 on July 24 (a Q3 event) achieved next-generation Starlink deployment, an in-space relight and an on-target splashdown, but the Super Heavy booster made a hard splashdown after engines failed to relight and the flight remained suborbital. Starship is the stated enabler of next-generation Starlink capacity, mobile V2 satellites and eventual orbital compute, so slippage propagates into both other segments.

Second, the **$100bn exit-2026 ARR target** is an unaudited non-GAAP management figure that explicitly assumes a contribution from Cursor, an acquisition that has not closed. Q2 revenue annualizes to $31.3bn. Getting to $100bn of ARR by year-end implies more than a tripling in two quarters, part of it from an unclosed deal. Treat it as an aspiration, not a forecast.

Third, the **estimate-revision data is artifactual**. All 90-day-prior estimates read $0.00 because coverage did not exist pre-IPO; the "+86.5% next-year revision" measures initiation, not upgrade momentum. The 30-day moves (current quarter $0.06 → $0.15; next year $0.66 → $1.24) are genuine post-print upgrades and directionally supportive. But the current-quarter consensus of $0.15 implies roughly $1,976M of net income on ~13.2bn shares, a $2.5bn swing from Q2's $541M loss in one quarter, with D&A still compounding. Either the estimates are non-GAAP, or they embed a very fast margin inflection. We do not treat them as a reliable anchor.

**Growth prospects: 8.0.** The rate of growth and the breadth across Connectivity and AI justify a high score. The deductions are Space's absolute decline, ARPU compression, the non-credible ARR headline, and the fact that the fastest-growing revenue rests on the shortest contracts.

Annualization cuts both ways. Upward: AI infrastructure revenue ramped intra-quarter, Reflection contributes nothing to Q2, the Starlink price increase landed in June, X advertising peaks in Q4, and $6.7bn of incremental cloud revenue was contracted in July. Downward: Adjusted EBITDA excludes $831M of quarterly SBC and $2,848M of quarterly D&A in a business where depreciation is the dominant real cost.

The sum-of-parts cross-check is the most decision-relevant number in this report. Connectivity's $2,597M of Q2 segment Adjusted EBITDA annualizes to $10.4bn; at 25x — a generous multiple for a capital-intensive telecom asset, even a growing one — that supports roughly $260bn of enterprise value. The residual **$1.43tn is what the market pays for Space plus AI**, which together lost $1,799M at the operating line and absorbed $17.0bn of capex in the quarter. To justify that residual, Starship must work, orbital compute must become a real market, and the AI cloud business must convert 90-day contracts into a durable franchise against AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle and CoreWeave.

Dilution compounds the entry problem, and one leg has the wrong sign. Pending equity issuance at $133.11:

- **Cursor** (Anysphere): consideration is Class A stock at a fixed implied Cursor equity value of $60bn, with the share count set by the 7-day VWAP before closing. At $133.11 that is **450.8M shares — 3.42% of existing shares, 3.31% post-issuance ownership dilution**. Because the value is fixed and the count floats, a lower SPCX price at closing produces *more* shares. At $100, Cursor requires roughly 600M shares. This is negative convexity for holders in a drawdown.
- **EchoStar spectrum**: the equity leg is a fixed 261.8M Class A shares struck at $42.40. At $133.11 those shares are worth $34,850M against $11,100M credited — a **$23.75bn increase in economic cost** since signing. Add the up-to-$8.5bn debt payoff and the Spectrum Credit Agreement support payments ($1,241M in 2026, $828M in 2027) and all-in economic cost is roughly $45–46bn against a $19.6bn headline. The licenses currently sit in Spectrum Business Trust 2025-1; SpaceX does not yet own them and closing is expected November 30, 2027.
- **Mesh Optical** closed July 6 for ~3.8M shares.

Combined pending issuance: 716.4M shares, 5.44% of existing, 5.16% post-issuance ownership dilution — before considering the 1,331M performance-award pool discussed below.

Analyst dispersion is the widest we can recall on a mega-cap: low $62, high $800, mean $231.40, median $200.00 — a 12.9x range. That dispersion is itself information: there is no consensus valuation framework, only competing terminal-value narratives. Morningstar carries a bearish published view and Phillip Capital carries $75 and projects negative cash flow through at least 2030. Morgan Stanley is Overweight. At >100x forward earnings, analyst targets are not independent confirmation of the price — the high targets and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation.

**Valuation: 3.0.** Growth is exceptional, which is why this is not a 2. But 54x annualized revenue on a GAAP-loss-making entity, with $1.43tn implied for two loss-making segments and a dilution structure that worsens as the price falls, offers no margin of safety at $133.11.

## Competitive Landscape

**Connectivity: moat widening.** No competitor is close to 10,200 satellites, 12M service lines and 167 markets. Amazon's Kuiper, Blue Origin, Viasat, AT&T and T-Mobile are named among more than 20 competitors in the prospectus; several are also launch customers, an inherent structural conflict. The EchoStar spectrum, once closed, plus mobile partnerships launched in Q2 with SoftBank, NTT DoCoMo and Spark New Zealand, position Starlink for direct-to-cell — though management's own timeline puts standalone-service satellites in 2027 and first Starlink Mobile customers at end-2027. This is the segment where competitive trajectory is unambiguously positive.

**Space: dominance intact, monetization deliberately constrained.** Internal launches held flat at 60 in the first half while customer launches fell from 21 to 17. Press reporting (CNBC, unconfirmed by any filing) says SpaceX has stopped taking launch reservations beyond late 2028/early 2029. If accurate, manifest capacity is being reserved for internal Starlink and future AI-constellation deployment, capping third-party Space revenue regardless of demand. That reading makes the revenue decline strategic; a capacity- or reliability-constrained reading is also consistent with the data, and the disclosures do not distinguish.

**AI: contested and the weakest link.** SpaceX has entered a market dominated by four hyperscalers plus neoclouds. Three named counterparties are all **buyers of SpaceX capacity**: Anthropic (Colossus 1, from May), Google (reported ~$920M per month, unconfirmed by filing), and Reflection AI ($150M per month from July 1, Colossus 2 GB300 capacity). Nvidia is the exclusive GPU supplier, a shareholder via the converted xAI stake, and a co-design partner on the "Starmind AI1" satellite compute payload — a supplier and partner, not a customer.

A dissenting industry read deserves weight. Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman stated publicly in July that SpaceX/xAI moved into rented compute because processors were under-utilized, Grok having drawn less usage than expected. That is a competitor's characterization, not a company statement, but it is internally coherent and implies very different terminal margins than a deliberate strategic entry — and a different reading of whether 90-day cancellable contracts are a bridge or a business.

The circularity web is unusually dense: Nvidia is supplier, shareholder and co-designer; Tesla converted a ~$2bn xAI investment into SpaceX stock and separately sold SpaceX $295M of Megapacks in Q2; Cursor is simultaneously a compute customer under exclusivity obligations and an acquisition target; and Terafab, a Texas chip fab with Tesla and Intel partners projected at up to $119bn of buildout per public hearing notices, appears nowhere in the $27,955M of disclosed non-cancelable commitments.

**Competitive position: 7.0.** Connectivity's trajectory is excellent and Space's franchise is unmatched. The AI segment, which carries most of the incremental valuation, is a new entrant against entrenched incumbents with an unresolved question about why it entered.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Revenue duration versus asset life.** The single Item 1A supplement to the prospectus risk factors states that a significant portion of AI infrastructure revenue is concentrated in a small number of customers and that cloud services agreements, after an initial ramp period, "may be terminated by either party upon 90 days' notice." Note 3 discloses **Customer B at 19.5% of consolidated Q2 revenue** in the AI segment — roughly $1,524M, or approximately 95% of the sequential increase in AI infrastructure revenue — funding a segment that spent $15.8bn of capex in the quarter. Asset lives are years; the revenue commitment is one quarter. The mitigating argument: termination rights are mutual, GPU capacity is scarce, and $6.7bn of additional cloud revenue was contracted in the first weeks of Q3. Customer B's identity is not disclosed.

**2. Concentration worsened while mix broadened.** Customer A (all three segments) was 18.3% of Q2 revenue versus 16.7%; Customer B 19.5% versus under 10%. Combined top-two: **37.8% versus roughly 16.7%+** a year earlier. Revenue mix diversified across segments; counterparty dependence more than doubled. The concentration table, not the mix table, governs revenue durability.

**3. Announced deal values exceed total company backlog.** Note 3 reports total backlog of **$47,461M** across all three segments (56% recognizable within a year), of which $14,286M is already deferred revenue. Media aggregation of the Anthropic, Google and Reflection deals reaches "over $76 billion through 2029." $76bn cannot fit inside $47.5bn of backlog that must also hold NASA/DoD launch contracts and Starlink enterprise commitments. Either the aggregate overstates enforceable commitments or these contracts fail the company's "enforceable agreement" test — most plausibly because of the mutual 90-day termination right. Do not treat the $76bn as contracted revenue.

**4. Legal exposure includes novel AI liability.** A $354M accrual sits against a statement that reasonably possible losses "may be material or exceed its estimated ranges." Grok image-generation class actions (N.D. Cal., January and March 2026) allege facilitation of nonconsensual explicit imagery including content sexualizing children, seeking punitive damages and disgorgement; a Baltimore action names **Space Exploration Technologies Corp. itself** as defendant. Loss is not estimable. A NAACP Clean Air Act suit seeks to enjoin the mobile gas turbines powering COLOSSUS II — an adverse ruling would directly threaten power to a facility underpinning cloud revenue. The EU imposed a EUR 120M DSA fine naming Musk personally (under appeal). A $172M patent judgment (damages plus prejudgment interest) is on cross-appeal.

**5. Contingent equity with undisclosed terms.** Note 13 excludes **1,331M share-based awards** from diluted EPS because performance and market conditions were not met, versus 13.5M a year earlier. At $133.11 that is $177bn of notional value — **10.10% of existing shares, 9.17% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully earned**. The 10-Q does not disclose the vesting conditions, who holds the awards, or the hurdles. This belongs strictly in probability-weighted scenarios, not in current fully diluted capitalization, but a pool of this size appearing without disclosed terms is a governance gap of the first order. It is the highest-priority open item and requires the prospectus or first proxy to resolve.

**6. Governance.** No DEF 14A exists, so there is no independent disclosure of board or committee independence, audit-committee expertise, or compensation benchmarking. Musk holds approximately 35–36% economics and **61–62% voting power** through the 10-vote Class B. As a newly public company, no management ICFR assessment or auditor attestation has been furnished; those arrive with the first 10-K. Disclosure controls were concluded effective at June 30.

**7. Supply schedule.** The first major lock-up expired August 6 and coincided with a 10–12% rally rather than the predicted supply shock, plausibly because the drawdown from $225.64 to ~$113 had already discounted it and because the CFO and President elected extended lock-ups (Johnsen's 10b5-1 plan does not begin sales until 2027; Shotwell's covers 585,605 shares). Offsetting: Valor-affiliated entities adopted a plan on June 12 for distribution to LPs/GPs of up to **225,857,490 Class A shares**, expiring September 30, 2027. One month of price action is weak evidence about a multi-quarter release schedule.

**8. Digital assets.** 18,712 Bitcoin at $661M cost carried at $1,098M, down from $1,637M at year-end — a $539M first-half unrealized loss through other income. Roughly $1.1bn of non-operating earnings volatility unrelated to the business.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 96.1% sits below one-year realized volatility of 105.3% (−9.1% spread), so options are marginally cheap versus delivered movement, and the term structure is in backwardation (96.1% at one week versus 77.4% at six months) — the market prices near-term event risk decaying into a still-extraordinary long-run 77% baseline. Near-dated skew is call-heavy (−7.1% at one week) with put/call volume of 0.81, and the unusual-activity table shows heavy August call buying at $158 (+13.5%) and September calls at $170 (+22.5%) alongside comparable put volume at $131–$138. The six-month tenor flips: put/call volume ratio 1.37 with −4.9% skew, and put/call open interest across the chain is 1.16. The read is speculative near-term upside positioning layered over longer-dated hedging. Short interest is only 2.7% of float with 1.3 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short setup and no squeeze support for the bull case. Nothing in the options data contradicts a REDUCE; the backwardation and the ±20.2% three-month implied move confirm that outcome dispersion is extreme, which argues against holding a full position through the next two prints.

**Risk factors: 2.5.** The stack of unpriced idiosyncratic risk — 19.5% of revenue on 90-day paper funding a 6.2x capex ratio, unquantifiable AI liability litigation naming the parent, an injunction risk to data-center power, a 1,331M-share award pool with undisclosed terms, no proxy, no ICFR attestation, and a 12.9x analyst target range — is as concentrated as we see in a company of this size.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($215, +61.5%)

The $100bn ARR target proves broadly right and the AI cloud business converts short contracts into a durable franchise on capacity scarcity and immediacy pricing. Connectivity compounds toward $20bn+ of annual Adjusted EBITDA as Enterprise & Government (+108%) scales, the June price increase and kit rental fee lift ARPU sequentially, and EchoStar spectrum plus mobile V2 satellites open direct-to-cell in 2027 against a subscriber base of tens of millions. Starship achieves tower catch and orbital flight, cutting Starlink capacity cost per bit and eventually enabling orbital compute — which New Street frames as potentially the biggest opportunity, albeit next-decade. The CFO's sub-one-year compute payback claim holds for marginal cohorts, capex converts to revenue faster than depreciation compounds, and the company reaches self-funding by late 2027 without another equity raise. In that world $215 is roughly 20x an eventual $140bn of revenue — expensive but not absurd, and Nasdaq-100 flows plus scarcity of exposure support the multiple.

### Bear Case ($65, −51%)

A single anchor customer exercises its 90-day termination right, or two renewals price down as hyperscaler capacity comes online, and AI revenue growth stalls while $1,885M of quarterly D&A and $629M of quarterly interest remain. Free cash flow stays near −$15bn per quarter, the six-quarter runway forces a capital raise into a falling market, and Cursor's floating share count issues 600M+ shares at a depressed price. Starship slips again after the Flight 13 booster failure, delaying next-generation Starlink capacity and mobile V2, which pushes direct-to-cell revenue beyond 2028. An adverse ruling in the NAACP turbine case interrupts COLOSSUS II power; the Grok image-generation suits survive dismissal and disgorgement becomes a live remedy. Connectivity alone at 25x supports roughly $260bn of enterprise value, and even generously valuing Space at $250bn and AI at $350bn produces a sub-$900bn equity value — $65 per share, consistent with Morningstar's published bearish view and Phillip Capital's $75 target.

### Base Case ($115, −13.6%)

Revenue reaches roughly $60–65bn in 2027 as AI cloud grows but at lower prices, Connectivity compounds, and Space stays flat pending Starship. GAAP profitability arrives later than the $1.24 next-year consensus implies. Free cash flow improves but remains negative through 2027, requiring at least one incremental financing. Cursor and Spectrum add roughly 5% ownership dilution; some portion of the 1,331M award pool vests. At $115 the equity is $1.52tn and EV approximately $1.46tn — about 23x 2027 revenue. That remains a premium multiple for a business with one profitable segment, which is why the base case sits below the current price rather than above it.

Probability-weighted (25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear): $125, or **−6.1% from $133.11**, with a 150-point spread between bull and bear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Expected return is modestly negative with severely unfavorable asymmetry — 51% downside against 61% upside at less than half the probability weight — and the entry offers no margin of safety at 54x annualized revenue.

**De-risking plan.** Trim to a starter or half position now rather than around a catalyst; the next two prints (early November for Q3, late February/March for the first 10-K) are both two-sided with a three-month implied move of ±20.2%, and the Cursor closing in Q3 issues shares at whatever price prevails. Holders wanting residual exposure should size it so that a move to $65 is tolerable. We would not short: 2.7% short interest with 1.3 days to cover offers no crowding cushion, near-dated positioning is call-tilted, and 96% implied volatility makes the carry punitive against a company with $60.5bn of net cash and no maturity wall until 2031.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE** (any two would move us):
1. Two consecutive quarters in which AI infrastructure revenue grows sequentially *and* the top-two customer concentration in Note 3 falls below 30% of consolidated revenue — evidence the 90-day contracts are renewing and broadening.
2. Quarterly capex flattening below roughly $15bn with free cash flow improving toward −$5bn or better, demonstrating that the compute buildout has a defined ceiling rather than an escalating one.
3. Disclosure of the 1,331M performance-award vesting conditions (prospectus recovery or first proxy) showing hurdles that are demanding and shareholder-aligned, together with a first DEF 14A establishing independent committee review of the Valor financing.
4. Price at or below $95 — roughly $1.25tn equity, $1.19tn EV, 38x annualized Q2 revenue — where the bull case begins to carry an adequate margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Any 8-K or disclosure of a material cloud-contract termination or non-renewal, or Customer B concentration falling sharply without offsetting new contracts.
2. An equity issuance for general corporate purposes (as distinct from acquisition consideration), which would confirm the six-quarter runway math is binding earlier than the plan assumes.
3. An adverse preliminary-injunction ruling in the NAACP Clean Air Act case interrupting COLOSSUS II power, or denial of dismissal in the Grok image-generation actions with disgorgement preserved as a remedy.
4. A third Starship flight failing to achieve orbital insertion or booster recovery, which would push next-generation Starlink capacity and mobile V2 into 2028.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next hard checkpoints: the Cursor closing (expected Q3 2026, share count set on the 7-day VWAP), the Q3 10-Q in early November — the first quarter with a full ~13.2bn share denominator, the first month of Reflection AI revenue, and the first read on whether Q2's $18.4bn capex was a peak — and the first 10-K, which brings the ICFR assessment and, with a proxy, the governance and award disclosures currently missing.

## Conclusion

SpaceX owns one of the best assets in global telecommunications and the most capable launch franchise ever built, and it is growing revenue at 92% year over year. Those facts are not in dispute. The question is price. At $133.11 on the correct 13,176M-share count, the market pays $1.75tn of equity value and $1.69tn of enterprise value, of which roughly $1.43tn is attributable to two segments that lost $1,799M at the operating line and consumed $17.0bn of capex in a single quarter. Nearly 20% of consolidated revenue sits on contracts terminable in 90 days while the assets those contracts fund depreciate over years. Free cash flow was −$25.0bn in the first half; the liquidity stack covers roughly six quarters at that pace against a stated plan to grow installed compute sevenfold. The pending Cursor consideration issues more shares the further the stock falls, the EchoStar equity leg has already cost $23.8bn more than credited, and 1,331M performance shares exist with vesting terms the company has not disclosed.

None of that makes the business bad or the equity uninvestable at some price. It makes $133.11 the wrong entry. Reduce exposure and revisit near $95, or on evidence that AI cloud revenue is renewing and broadening while capex finds a ceiling.
