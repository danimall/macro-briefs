**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Regal Rexnord is two years into a deliberate portfolio transformation: a $5.1B Altra acquisition (2023) that levered the balance sheet to fund a higher-margin motion-control and power-transmission franchise, followed by the $444M divestiture of the commoditized Industrial Systems motors business (2024) and a rapid deleveraging program. Three straight years of declining revenue ($6.25B → $6.03B → $5.93B) is largely a divestiture and FX artifact. Organic growth has turned positive (+0.8% FY25, +1.6% organic in Q1 2026), and the Automation & Motion Control segment grew +15.3% in Q1 (+12.1% organic) on data-center power-management demand. Total backlog jumped 49% YoY to $2.54B, driven by $735M of data-center orders booked in 2025 that convert to revenue largely in 2027.

That is the bull case, and it is credible. The problem is the price. RRX has run 55% over twelve months, from the high-$130s to $216, and now trades at roughly 50x trailing and ~20x forward consensus EPS ($10.63 current-year). For an industrial compounding mid-single-digit organic with margins still in the mid-teens operating range, that forward multiple is full rather than cheap, and the near-term estimate revisions are stable-to-slightly-negative (current-year EPS revised -0.3% over 90 days; current quarter -3.2%). The data-center catalyst is real but back-end-loaded into 2027, so 2026 reported numbers will understate booked momentum, and the stock is being asked to carry valuation on a story that does not show up in the P&L for another 18 months.

We rate RRX a HOLD (growth_hold). The deleveraging is working (interest expense -12.6%, $709M repaid in 2025), the secular thesis in AMC is intact, and a multi-year refinancing tailwind sits in the filings. But a CEO handoff in progress, residential HVAC cyclicality dragging PES (-8.6% in Q1), a thin tangible-equity cushion, and a fully-valued forward multiple together argue against chasing the stock here. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$170s, where forward valuation compresses to a level that prices the execution and transition risk.

## Company Overview

Regal Rexnord designs and manufactures products that power, transmit, and control motion: electric motors, air-moving subsystems, engineered power-transmission components, and factory/precision automation. The company is headquartered in Milwaukee with roughly 28,700 employees, about 20,000 of them outside the U.S. (Mexico 8,100, China 3,600, India 3,500). It operates three segments:

- **Industrial Powertrain Solutions (IPS)** — the largest at ~$2.59B FY25 sales (44% of revenue) and the highest-margin (42.1% gross): bearings, couplings, gearboxes, clutches, brakes, integrated powertrains for general industrial, metals/mining, energy, and commercial HVAC end markets.
- **Power Efficiency Solutions (PES)** — ~$1.65B (28%): fractional-to-5HP motors, drives, fans/blowers, air-moving subsystems, heavily tied to residential and commercial HVAC. The most cyclical and seasonal segment; lowest gross margin (29.4%) but improving.
- **Automation & Motion Control (AMC)** — ~$1.69B (28%): conveyor/automation subsystems, aerospace components, precision servo motors, and power-management products including automatic transfer switches, paralleling switchgear, and modular "E-Pods" for data centers. This is the secular-growth engine.

The current structure reflects the 2021 Rexnord PMC merger, the 2023 Altra acquisition, and the 2024 Industrial Systems divestiture. Strategy centers on 80/20 prioritization, the Regal Rexnord Business System (RBS), powertrain cross-selling, and deleveraging. No single customer exceeds 10% of sales, and roughly 40% of sales flow through distribution channels, providing aftermarket durability that dampens cyclicality.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Reported FY25 revenue declined to $5.93B, but the decline is dominated by the Industrial Systems divestiture (-2.8% in 2025) and FX rather than core deterioration. Organic sales grew +0.8% in 2025 and the trajectory inflected in Q1 2026: net sales +4.3% (organic +1.6%), with AMC +15.3% (+12.1% organic), IPS +5.8% on general-industrial strength, and PES -8.6% on residential HVAC weakness.

Profitability is structurally improving. Operating margin reached 11.5% FY25 versus 10.4% FY24; gross margin expanded to 37.4% from 36.3% on Altra synergy capture and PES restructuring. Net income recovered to $279.5M (FY25) from $196.2M (FY24) and a -$57.4M loss in 2023, the Altra integration year that carried impairments. Q1 2026 diluted EPS was $0.96 versus $0.86, though the comparison is flattered by a tax rate that fell to 15.9% from 21.2% on a discrete stock-option benefit; on a normalized tax basis the EPS gain would be smaller.

**Cash flow and deleveraging.** FY25 operating cash flow was $990.8M (up $381M YoY), with FCF near $893M and a ~15% FCF margin. The improvement requires a caveat: much of the OCF jump came from selling receivables under a new $400M securitization facility, a one-time working-capital pull-forward rather than pure organic conversion. Trade receivables dropped from $843M to $524M largely for this reason, and Q1 2026 OCF fell to $14.9M (versus $102M prior year) on the inverse working-capital build. Underwrite normalized FCF below the $893M FY25 print.

Deleveraging is the central financial story and it is progressing. The company repaid $709M of net debt in 2025; interest expense fell 12.6% to $349M from $400M; long-term debt declined to $4.76B from $5.45B (2024) and $6.38B (2023). The $1.1B of 6.05% 2026 senior notes that matured in February were refinanced via an $850M floating-rate term facility drawn at ~4.8%, lowering go-forward interest cost. Remaining senior notes ($1.25B due 2028 at 6.05%, $1.1B due 2030 at 6.30%, $1.25B due 2033 at 6.40%) carry high coupons issued at peak-rate Altra financing, creating a multi-year structural interest-expense tailwind as they mature or as ratings improve with continued deleveraging.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets of $13.9B include $6.6B of goodwill and $3.4B of intangibles — roughly 72% of assets are intangible, a direct legacy of the Altra and Rexnord deals. Tangible book is thin and D/E sits around 2.0x. The current ratio is healthy at 2.1–2.2x, the company was in covenant compliance as of Q1 2026, and it carries $1.3B of undrawn revolver capacity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.7 reads as "distress zone," but that is a false positive driven mechanically by the goodwill-heavy roll-up structure and leverage, not by liquidity or operational weakness; a Piotroski score of 5/5 (positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF > NI) confirms sound underlying fundamentals. The right reading is a levered but operationally healthy business with a thin tangible-equity cushion that is sensitive to goodwill impairment if demand or synergy capture disappoints. The AMC unit had three of five tested reporting units exceed carrying value by less than 10% — a thin cushion worth monitoring, and PES already carries $200.4M of cumulative goodwill impairment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests almost entirely on AMC and the data-center power-management franchise. The $735M of data-center orders booked in 2025 — automatic transfer switches, paralleling switchgear, E-Pods, and associated thermal/air-moving content — drove total backlog from $1,707M (Dec 2024) to $2,536M (Dec 2025), up 49%. That backlog is a quantified forward catalyst sitting in the filings.

RRX is a credible picks-and-shovels beneficiary of data-center electrification at the power-management layer.

The offset is PES, where residential HVAC weakness drove organic sales -10.3% in Q1 2026, tied to the housing and replacement cycle. PES recovery timing is uncertain and may remain a drag through 2026. IPS, the largest and highest-margin segment, grew +5.8% in Q1 on general-industrial strength, a steady but unspectacular contributor.

Consensus reflects a forward EPS path: current-year $10.63, next-year $13.52. The revision trend is the issue. Over 90 days, current-year EPS was revised -0.3% and the current quarter -3.2%, while next-year was revised +2.3% — consistent with the 2027 data-center conversion narrative but not with near-term acceleration. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here; revisions are essentially flat. The earnings beat record is unremarkable (three beats, one miss over four quarters, with surprises in the ±2% range), implying limited consensus mis-estimation in either direction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $216, RRX trades at roughly 50x trailing EPS ($4.20) and ~20x forward consensus EPS. The trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because the trailing period carried integration drag and a depressed EPS base, so forward is the right anchor. On forward earnings, ~20x for a business compounding mid-single-digit organic with margins in the mid-teens operating range is a full multiple, not a discount.

Peer comparison is unhelpfully noisy. The provided Electrical Components & Equipment cohort — Generac (80x), Vicor (100x), Powell (57x), Nextpower (31x), Acuity (21x), peer median 57x — spans wildly different business models and growth profiles. RRX at ~20x forward looks cheap against that median, but the median is dominated by hyper-growth and specialty names whose growth rates dwarf RRX's. The relevant comparison is to broad-line diversified industrials, against which RRX's ~20x forward is roughly market-to-modest-premium for its growth and leverage profile. We do not give RRX a thematic re-rating pass on valuation: the data-center demand is real, but RRX delivers it through a levered, mid-single-digit-organic vehicle, not a pure-play growth franchise.

The estimate-revision picture removes the "analyst targets are floors" argument. That principle applies to inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates and cheap forward multiples; here, near-term estimates are flat-to-slightly-down and the forward multiple is full. Analyst targets (mean $252, median $249, range $225–$300) imply 16–17% upside to the mean, but those targets lean heavily on 2027 data-center conversion.

A cheap-looking headline multiple is a poor reason to buy on its own, and that caution applies here in reverse: the trailing P/E understates value while the forward multiple is fair, leaving no obvious mispricing to exploit. The refinancing tailwind (lower coupons as 6%+ notes mature) is a real but slow EPS lever already partly visible in falling interest expense. We score valuation 4.5 — not a value trap, but not asymmetric upside at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

RRX competes in fragmented IPS and AMC markets where most rivals offer narrow product lines, and in a highly competitive global PES/motor market against European, Japanese, Chinese, and Indian motor makers. Its differentiation is scale and breadth: being the one-stop vendor for customers consolidating power-transmission and motion spend, and selling integrated powertrains (motor plus transmission components) that narrow competitors cannot easily match. The ~40% distribution mix supports a less-cyclical aftermarket revenue base.

The competitive trajectory is improving where it matters. AMC's power-management franchise is gaining traction in data-center electrification, evidenced by the order book and backlog growth, and the company is positioned in a structurally growing end market. The CEO hire of Aamir Paul, a 12-year Schneider Electric power-management veteran, signals that the board intends to lean further into this space. Against this, PES faces structural competition from low-cost global motor makers and near-term cyclical weakness. On balance, the competitive position is stable-to-improving, with AMC the clear bright spot and PES the laggard — a 6.5 reflecting genuine but uneven trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and intangible concentration.** $4.8B of debt and 72% intangible assets leave a thin tangible-equity cushion. The thin goodwill-impairment headroom in three AMC reporting units (under 10% over carrying value) is a specific, monitorable risk should demand or synergy capture disappoint.

**Rare-earth magnet supply.** China export controls are cited in the FY25 MD&A as a current, active constraint already limiting AMC's ability to serve medical and defense markets — a present operational headwind, not a hypothetical.

**Tariffs.** Major manufacturing in Mexico, China, and India exposes RRX to trade policy, including India's August 2025 reciprocal/penalty tariffs. Offsetting this is unrecorded optionality from the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized; RRX, as importer of record, may be due refunds it has not booked, a possible non-operating upside surprise.

**HVAC cyclicality.** PES organic sales fell -10.3% in Q1 2026 on residential HVAC weakness, and the broader housing and consumer environment could prolong the drag.

**Order conversion.** The FY25 10-K expanded its order-volatility risk language, emphasizing that purchase-order values can differ from realized revenue through cancellations, modifications, and deferrals. This matters directly to the large data-center backlog: management is signaling that order dollars are not guaranteed to convert dollar-for-dollar.

**CEO transition.** The Pinkham-to-Paul handoff (effective no later than July 1, 2026) is explicitly flagged as a risk factor; execution risk during the transition is real, and the prior CEO's tenure underperformed peers on TSR (cumulative 125.69 versus peer 191.48 indexed).

**Legacy litigation.** A 2007-acquired subsidiary faces claims that its sub-fractional motors (made through 2004) caused fires in third-party ventilation units; CPSC-regulated, with uncertain remediation cost.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 53.5% sits modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 47.2% (a 6.3% rich spread), with IV in only the 24th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing an extreme near-term event. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.3% near-term), and the term structure is in normal contango (53.5% near versus 56.9% long-term), a complacent rather than fearful structure. The put/call volume ratio of 7.75 looks alarming in isolation but pairs with a far more moderate put/call OI ratio of 1.62, suggesting tactical hedging rather than structural bearish positioning. The implied ±10.7% move over the nearest expiry frames the realistic near-term swing. The options market is neutral-to-slightly-cautious, consistent with a fully-valued stock awaiting a back-end-loaded catalyst, and does not contradict the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($265–$300).** Data-center orders convert cleanly in 2027, AMC sustains low-double-digit organic growth, IPS holds mid-single digits, and PES recovers as the HVAC cycle turns. Continued deleveraging plus refinancing of the 6%+ notes drives a multi-year interest-expense tailwind, lifting EPS toward and beyond the $13.52 next-year consensus. The Schneider-veteran CEO accelerates the power-management strategy, and an IEEPA tariff refund delivers a one-time upside surprise. The stock re-rates toward 22–24x on rising next-year earnings.

**Bear case ($150–$170, -22% to -31% from current).** Residential HVAC weakness deepens and lingers, data-center order conversion slips or disappoints relative to booked values (the expanded PO-risk language proves prescient), and a general-industrial slowdown pressures IPS. Thin goodwill-impairment headroom in AMC reporting units triggers a write-down, and the CEO transition introduces execution friction. The forward multiple compresses to ~16x on flat-to-down earnings.

**Base case ($230–$245).** Organic growth holds in the low-to-mid single digits, AMC carries the mix, PES stabilizes without a sharp recovery, and deleveraging plus refinancing deliver steady EPS progression toward the low-$11 range for 2026. The stock holds its current multiple and tracks earnings growth, with the 2027 data-center conversion providing a forward catalyst that supports a gradual re-rating but does not show up in reported numbers until late in the horizon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive but the entry price is unattractive.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$170s, where forward P/E compresses to ~16–17x and prices the execution/transition risk while preserving the 2027 data-center optionality.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity — current-year and current-quarter EPS revised upward by more than 5% over 90 days — confirming the AMC inflection is reaching the P&L ahead of 2027.
- Evidence of clean data-center order conversion (booked-to-billed visibility for the backlog) plus a PES organic-growth inflection back to positive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment in the thin-cushion AMC reporting units, or covenant pressure from a demand downturn.
- Data-center order cancellations or material deferrals that validate the expanded PO-risk language.
- A guidance cut tied to the CEO transition, or sustained PES organic declines worse than -10% extending through 2026.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release and the CEO transition completion by July 1, 2026, followed by visibility into 2027 data-center revenue conversion in subsequent quarters.

## Conclusion

Regal Rexnord is executing a credible transformation: deleveraging is real and accelerating, AMC's data-center power-management franchise is a genuine secular beneficiary with a quantified order book, and a multi-year refinancing tailwind sits embedded in the filings. The "declining revenue" headline is a divestiture optical illusion, and the Altman Z-Score "distress" flag is a false positive driven by goodwill-heavy roll-up accounting. The fundamentals are improving, and the score trajectory (4.8 → 5.5) reflects that.

But the stock has run 55% over twelve months to a full ~20x forward multiple, the primary catalyst is back-end-loaded into 2027, near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, PES is a cyclical drag, and a CEO handoff is in progress with no offsetting insider buying. The risk/reward at $216 is roughly balanced — meaningful upside if 2027 conversion delivers, comparable downside if HVAC weakness lingers or order conversion disappoints. We rate RRX a HOLD and would look to accumulate in the mid-$170s, where the forward valuation provides the margin of safety the current price lacks.
