**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tradeweb operates one of the highest-quality franchises in financial-market infrastructure: a debt-free electronic-marketplace network with entrenched #1 or top positions in U.S. Treasuries, TBA mortgages, global rate swaps, European government bonds, and U.S. institutional money-market funds, generating ~55% free-cash-flow margins. FY2025 was its 26th consecutive year of record revenue, with the top line up 18.9% to $2,052.4M and adjusted EBITDA margin at 54.0%. On recurring revenue mix, capital structure, and cash generation, Tradeweb scores near the top of its cohort.

The complication is that the reported earnings power overstates the underlying business. GAAP net income rose 61.7% to $921.5M, but $270.9M of that came from realized and unrealized gains on Canton Coin digital-asset holdings booked through "Other income." Adjusted net income grew 18.7% and adjusted EPS was $3.47 (versus $3.78 GAAP). Any valuation keyed to the GAAP number materially overstates earnings power and will be exposed when crypto marks reverse in a weak quarter. Per-million variable fees fell 10.2% year-over-year in Q4 as volume mix shifted toward lower-fee short-tenor swaps, and the U.S. cash-credit franchise is holding rather than gaining share against MarketAxess.

The stock has fallen ~34% from its $147.49 52-week high to ~$98, a magnitude that requires a specific thesis about what sellers are pricing. The decline is consistent with three identifiable concerns: fee-rate compression outpacing volume growth, skepticism about crypto-inflated GAAP earnings, and negative near-term estimate revisions (current-quarter consensus down 3.1% over 30 days). C-suite executives sold at $120–$126 in March–April 2026 and there has been no open-market buying at ~$100. At ~24x adjusted EPS for a business compounding adjusted earnings at high-teens, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. HOLD with a bullish lean.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1996 and public since April 2019, Tradeweb operates global electronic marketplaces across fixed income, rates, credit, equities, and money markets, serving 3,000+ clients across 85+ countries with average daily notional volume of roughly $2.6 trillion. The business is organized into four client sectors:

- **Institutional** — $1,275.5M (62% of revenue), +23.1% YoY. The core engine: asset managers, hedge funds, insurers, central banks.
- **Wholesale** — $400.8M, +3.9%. Inter-dealer brokerage (Dealerweb), including the 2021 Nasdaq eSpeed/NFI assets.
- **Retail** — $146.5M, +2.3%. Tradeweb Direct, serving financial advisors.
- **Corporates** — $95.9M, +121.8%. Added through the August 2024 ICD acquisition (~$860M), serving corporate-treasury money-market investing.

Revenue is 82.8% transaction fees/commissions and 15.9% subscription (including ~$93.2M of LSEG market-data fees). Clients are gradually migrating toward subscription and minimum-fee-floor pricing, which makes revenue more recurring and less purely volume-sensitive. Fixed revenue grew 18.1% in 2025.

The reported market cap of ~$11.7B reflects only the Class A float; on a fully diluted basis (~235M shares including LSEG/insider super-voting stock), implied equity value is closer to $23–24B. LSEG controls ~89.9% of voting power.

GAAP net income's jump is largely an artifact of the Canton Coin gains recorded in "Other income, net." In Q4 alone, a $205.4M crypto gain drove net margin to a non-credible 70.4%. Stripping this out, adjusted net income grew ~19% — the correct read on the underlying franchise. Valuation should anchor to adjusted EPS, not GAAP. The trailing P/E of ~24x on GAAP EPS is mechanically flattered; on adjusted EPS the trailing multiple is closer to ~28x, and on FY2026 consensus of ~$4.05 the forward multiple is ~24x.

The balance sheet is pristine: $2.08B cash, zero debt, a fully undrawn $500M revolver, $6.5B equity, and an Altman Z-Score around 5.6. For a marketplace operator, carrying no leverage with ~55% FCF margins is appropriate: this is the working capital and clearing-collateral flexibility a self-clearing market operator needs, not idle cash-hoarding. The asset base is goodwill-heavy ($3.15B goodwill plus ~$1.15B intangibles from acquisitions), which carries impairment risk if growth stalls but no refinancing risk.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: the dividend was raised 16.7% to $0.14/quarter, a new $500M buyback was authorized for 2026, and $105.9M was repurchased in 2025 at an average of $107.29 — above the current ~$98 price, showing that even disciplined buybacks can be poorly timed near highs.

The earnings-surprise record has weakened: across the last four quarters, one beat, one in-line, and two misses, including a 2.1% miss in Q1 2026 (EPS $1.08 vs. $1.10 expected). This is a deterioration from the consistent beat cadence the franchise historically delivered.

## Growth Analysis

Volume momentum remains strong. Q4 2025 ADV was $2.8T (+23.3%) with records in European government bonds, swaps/swaptions ≥1Y, U.S. ETFs, and global repo. January 2026 ADV reached $3.1T with double-digit YoY growth across rates, credit, and money markets. The secular electronification of fixed income — which still lags equities and FX in electronic penetration, especially in credit — is a durable multi-year tailwind, and Tradeweb is a primary beneficiary.

The qualifier is the gap between volume and revenue. Q4 volume grew 23% but revenue grew 12.5%, because per-million variable fees fell 10.2% YoY ($2.04 vs. $2.28), driven heavily by a mix shift toward lower-fee short-tenor swaps. Excluding that mix effect the decline was a milder 3.2%, but cash-credit fees per million fell 14.3%. Volume growth is real; its translation into revenue is being diluted by mix and price.

Credit, where the largest structural growth opportunity sits, is the slowest-growing major asset class at +6.3% in 2025. In U.S. high-grade credit, Tradeweb held 18.3% fully-electronic TRACE share (flat YoY) and 25.7% total share (down 110 bps). U.S. high-yield fully-electronic share was 8.1% (up 20 bps). The franchise is holding but not gaining decisive ground against MarketAxess in U.S. cash credit; its outperformance is concentrated in rates, money markets, and international.

Estimate revisions confirm decelerating near-term momentum. Current-quarter consensus fell 3.1% over 30 days, cut to $0.95 from $0.98. Full-year and next-year estimates are roughly flat over 30 days (FY26 $4.05, FY27 $4.57) despite a positive 90-day reading (+1.9% and +2.4%), so the recent direction is a stall. This is the opposite of the +30%/90-day pattern that would justify a momentum BUY. The franchise compounds adjusted earnings at high-teens with broad-based volume tailwinds, but negative near-term revisions cap the growth score.

## Competitive Landscape

Tradeweb's moat is built on two-sided liquidity network effects (dealers plus buy-side), deep integration into client order/execution-management systems and back offices, breadth across asset classes and geographies, and regulatory/compliance tooling. These are durable advantages in rates, mortgages, European governments, and money markets, where the franchise is dominant.

The competitive risk is concentrated and specific. Bloomberg and ICE are the most dangerous rivals because they bundle trading platforms with deep data and analytics relationships across virtually all market participants. MarketAxess is the credit-trading specialist where Tradeweb is holding rather than gaining. Trumid, the exchanges (CME, Nasdaq), other IDBs (TP ICAP, BGC), and EMS/OMS providers round out a crowded field. The flat-to-declining U.S. credit share is the clearest data point that the competitive position in the highest-opportunity vertical is stable rather than improving, which determines this dimension's score.

The pricing-model shift toward subscriptions and fee floors increases revenue durability and partially decouples revenue from volume, which offsets some of the per-million fee compression.

## Risk Assessment

**Rates concentration.** ~53% of revenue is the Rates asset class. A prolonged low-volatility, low-volume rates regime would disproportionately hurt Tradeweb. The current macro environment of elevated rate-path uncertainty is a tailwind, but this is a concentrated exposure.

**Canton Coin earnings volatility.** This is the most distinctive new risk. Tradeweb earns Canton Coins as a network validator and marks its 1.6B coin holdings ($242.7M) to fair value through earnings every quarter. With the coin now spot-traded (since November 2025) and self-custodied, this injects swings into both revenue (validator rewards) and net income (mark-to-market). The $270.9M FY2025 gain can reverse, converting a steady fee-based compounder into a partially crypto-exposed P&L. The strategic logic of positioning for tokenized/on-chain institutional markets is credible, but it raises reported-earnings volatility.

**U.S. Treasury central-clearing mandate.** SEC-mandated central clearing (cash Treasuries by December 2026, repo by mid-2027) could reshape trading economics and clearing flows in Tradeweb's largest cash product. A dated regulatory event in the company's biggest franchise warrants monitoring.

**Dealer/client concentration with no long-term contracts.** Liquidity depends on a limited set of dealer clients; most liquidity-takers trade transaction-by-transaction with no contractual lock-in. Industry consolidation increases pricing leverage against Tradeweb.

**LSEG control and Up-C/TRA structure.** LSEG controls voting power via super-voting shares and can block any change of control; Tradeweb is a "controlled company" exempt from majority-independent-board requirements. A $336.5M Tax Receivable Agreement funnels 50% of tax savings to insiders over ~15 years and accelerates on a change of control, making Tradeweb a less attractive acquisition target. A May 20, 2026 8-K (Items 3.03/5.03/5.07) was filed covering material modification of securityholder rights, bylaw amendments, and a shareholder vote; the underlying document was not read in the research process and warrants follow-up before relying on its specific contents.

**FX.** 42% of revenue is international (mainly GBP, EUR); FX swung G&A by ~$27.5M, producing a $17.1M loss in 2025 versus a $10.4M gain in 2024.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress without a strong directional tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 35.3% versus 28.1% historical volatility (rich, +7.2pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (35.3% near-term vs. 31.6% long-term), consistent with an upcoming catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.1% and put/call volume ratio is 0.79, so the market is pricing event uncertainty (next earnings, the Treasury-clearing timeline, crypto marks) rather than directional collapse. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.8%. Short interest is low at 2.7% of float with 2.2 days to cover, so this is not a crowded short and there is no squeeze setup. The 6-month tenor shows a negative skew (-3.3%, call-tilted), suggesting some longer-dated optimism, which fits the bullish-lean HOLD framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (to ~$140–$155):** Volume growth (low-20s% ADV) continues, fee-rate compression stabilizes as the swaps mix normalizes, and credit share resumes gaining against MarketAxess. The subscription/fee-floor shift increases revenue durability, the market normalizes out the crypto noise and re-rates on clean ~19% adjusted-EPS growth, and the $500M buyback supports the share count. At ~30x FY27 adjusted EPS of ~$4.57, this implies ~$137; at the high end with multiple expansion, the $197 analyst high is reachable. This aligns with consensus mean/median targets of $129–$136.

**Bear case (to ~$80–$85, roughly -15% to -18%):** Per-million fee compression persists and outpaces volume growth, holding revenue growth in the low double digits while volumes grow 20%+. A weak rates-volatility quarter combines with a reversal of Canton Coin marks to produce a sharp GAAP earnings decline and a confidence shock. Credit share continues to erode against MarketAxess. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the analyst target range; at ~18x on FY26 EPS, this implies ~$73. The Treasury-clearing mandate disrupts economics in the largest franchise.

The base case sits between these: a high-quality compounder de-rated on real but manageable concerns, trading at a fair mid-20s forward multiple with balanced risk/reward and no clean catalyst to force the upside before fee-rate trends and crypto-earnings volatility resolve.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (bullish lean), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Per-million variable fee rates stabilize or inflect positive for two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026), confirming that volume growth converts to revenue growth — watch the Q2 2026 print (late July) and Q3 (late October).
2. Current-quarter and current-year estimate revisions turn positive again (reversing the -3.1%/30-day current-quarter cut), signaling the near-term stall has ended.
3. A pullback toward the low-$90s (near the $94.25 52-week low) that compresses the forward multiple toward ~22x on FY26 EPS while fundamentals hold — at that level the risk/reward becomes asymmetric.
4. Clustered open-market insider buying at current levels, which would directly counter the March–April selling at $120–$126.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of revenue growth decelerating below 8% YoY while ADV stays above 15% — confirming structural fee compression.
2. A material reversal of Canton Coin marks driving a GAAP loss quarter combined with a guidance cut.
3. Confirmed U.S. credit share losses (fully-electronic TRACE share falling below ~17%) showing competitive erosion in the key growth vertical.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), which will show whether fee compression stabilized and how the swaps-mix dynamic evolved. The December 2026 Treasury central-clearing implementation is the key dated regulatory milestone.

## Conclusion

Tradeweb is a genuinely high-quality, debt-free electronic-marketplace franchise with dominant positions across rates, mortgages, European governments, and money markets, ~55% FCF margins, and a durable secular electronification tailwind. Those strengths justify a premium multiple and underpin the bullish lean. The reasons not to upgrade to BUY are specific and checkable: FY2025 GAAP EPS is inflated by the Canton Coin mark, with clean adjusted EPS growth of ~19%; per-million fees fell, diluting strong volume growth into more modest revenue growth; the U.S. cash-credit franchise is holding rather than gaining share; near-term estimate revisions are negative; executives sold with no buying at $100; and the LSEG voting-control structure plus the TRA caps strategic optionality. At ~24x FY26 consensus the valuation is fair, not asymmetric, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating before fee-rate and crypto-earnings dynamics resolve. HOLD, with accumulation warranted toward the low-$90s or on confirmed fee-rate stabilization over the next two prints.
