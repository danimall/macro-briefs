**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Columbia Banking System is the holding company for Columbia Bank, a ~$67B-asset western-U.S. regional built through a serial merger strategy: the 2023 Umpqua combination roughly doubled the franchise, and the August 2025 all-stock acquisition of Pacific Premier Bancorp (~$2.4B) added $11.4B in loans, $14.5B in deposits, and a valuable HOA/custodial-trust deposit franchise. The bank now operates across nine western states with a "business bank of choice" positioning, low-cost sticky funding, and a diversified commercial footprint. The core operating story in 2026 is favorable at the margin: net interest margin has expanded to 3.96% on falling deposit costs and a deliberate shift away from brokered/wholesale funding, and fee income (custodial trust drove trust revenue +200% YoY in Q1) is improving.

The central analytical tension is that rising net income has coincided with falling EPS. FY2025 net income rose to $550M from $534M, but diluted EPS declined to $2.30 from $2.55 because the PPBI stock issuance tripled the share count over three years (78M shares in 2022 to ~290M in Q1 2026). The combined franchise must now generate enough incremental earnings plus merger synergies to prove the strategy EPS-accretive, and Q1 2026's $0.66 (up from $0.41 YoY but down from $0.72 sequentially) leaves the outcome unresolved, particularly with one-time Q4 tailwinds fading.

Layered on top is a fresh, idiosyncratic credit crack: non-performing assets jumped 32% in one quarter driven by a single agricultural relationship, plus heavy CRE/multifamily concentration (~58% of loans, with Southern California ~32% of CRE).

Against that, the shares offer a genuine income profile: a 4.5% dividend yield (63% payout, well-covered), a $400M remaining buyback authorization after $200M repurchased in Q1 at ~$30.74, and an undemanding multiple. At $32.85, near the $33.68 52-week high, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate COLB a HOLD (income hold), with an upgrade path tied to synergy delivery (full run-rate from Q3 2026) and stabilizing credit, and a downgrade path tied to the agricultural credit spreading or a broader CRE normalization.

## Company Overview

Columbia Bank is an Oregon-chartered commercial and consumer bank offering retail/commercial banking, SBA lending, institutional/corporate banking, equipment leasing (via Financial Pacific Leasing), and wealth management. It reports as a single operating segment and competes as a "super-community" regional against Zions, Western Alliance, East West, Banner, Glacier, and the money-center banks.

The company's identity is defined by consolidation-driven scale. The February 2023 Umpqua merger produced the current Columbia Bank brand at ~$50B assets. The August 31, 2025 PPBI acquisition (0.9150 COLB shares per PPBI share) added a custodial-trust and HOA-banking franchise that brings structurally low-cost, sticky deposits. The strategic logic is defensible: rising regulatory and technology costs favor scale, and consolidation gives COLB enough size to compete for commercial relationships across a broad western footprint. The execution question is whether serial share issuance has diluted shareholders faster than the acquired earnings and synergies can compensate.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin.** FY2025 net interest income reached $2.0B (+17% YoY) with tax-equivalent NIM improving to 3.83% from 3.57%, driven by 75bps of Fed cuts lowering deposit costs and a funding-mix shift away from brokered/wholesale money. Q1 2026, the first full quarter as the combined company, produced $192M net income with NIM at 3.96%. The sequential NIM decline from Q4 2025's 4.06% looks worse than it is: Q4 was inflated by a one-time $12M PPBI time-deposit premium amortization benefit that concluded 12/31/2025, plus a $5M accelerated loan payoff. The cleaner run-rate NIM is ~3.96%, which is genuine expansion rather than deterioration.

**Returns.** ROA of 0.97%, ROE 8.98%, and ROTCE 12.51% (FY2025) are respectable but below the mid-teens the bank targets and well below 2024's 15.31% ROTCE, reflecting the drag from the enlarged equity base and merger noise. Intangible amortization runs ~$41M/quarter as a persistent EPS headwind from serial M&A ($1.5B goodwill plus $671M other intangibles).

**Capital and funding.** The balance sheet is solidly capitalized: CET1 of 11.6% and total risk-based capital of 13.4% (Q1 2026), TCE/TA of 8.63%, comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. Funding is the underappreciated strength: 32-33% non-interest-bearing deposits, core deposits of $50.2B, brokered deposits reduced to just 3% of the base, and a loan/deposit ratio of 89%. Uninsured deposits are ~39% of total, but total available liquidity of $27.1B equals ~129% of uninsured deposits, which is not a deposit-flight-risk profile. The AFS securities book carries ~$410M gross unrealized losses (mostly rate-driven MBS/CMO, flowing to a $291M AOCI loss), modest against $7.7B equity and unlike the SVB-type mismatch.

**Balance-sheet optimization.** Management is intentionally holding balances flat while improving quality: total assets declined to $66.0B from $66.8B, deposits down $722M via deliberate brokered reduction, and loans flat as transactional runoff is replaced by relationship commercial lending. This is a margin-and-quality strategy rather than a growth strategy, and it should support NIM as wholesale funding is shed.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.37/quarter in November 2025 (63% FY2025 payout, well-covered), and a $700M buyback was authorized in October 2025 with $200M repurchased in Q1 2026 (~6.5M shares at avg $30.74). Repurchases are being funded by the Bank upstreaming capital via buying back Bank stock, preserving Bank retained earnings, a technically clean approach. The 4.5% dividend yield anchors the income case.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is muted by design. With loans deliberately held flat and the balance sheet shrinking to optimize funding, revenue growth in 2026 depends primarily on NIM expansion and merger synergy realization rather than volume. Cost synergies from PPBI are expected in full by 6/30/2026, with full run-rate benefit from Q3 2026 against a $185M total merger expense budget, the key near-term earnings lever. Fee income is a bright spot: the custodial-trust franchise drove financial-services/trust revenue +200% YoY in Q1 2026, diversifying away from spread income.

Earnings momentum, however, is drifting modestly negative in the estimates. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $3.05 (down 0.4% over 90 days), next-year at $3.38 (down 0.9%), and the current quarter at $0.73 (down 1.4%). None of these declines are severe, but the trend is negative rather than the accelerating positive revisions that would signal a fundamental turn. Zacks Q2 2026 consensus of ~$0.73 EPS implies roughly –4% YoY, consistent with a market view that synergy benefits offset a normalizing credit-and-margin backdrop. The earnings-surprise record is a genuine positive, four consecutive beats (20.7%, 12.5%, 12.5%, 3.0%), but the shrinking magnitude of the beats (from ~21% to ~3%) suggests estimate quality is catching up to reality.

The durable growth thesis is EPS accretion from PPBI: if synergies land and credit stabilizes, the combined franchise should demonstrate mid-single-digit EPS growth off a more efficient cost base with a wider margin. That is a plausible but not-yet-proven path, which is why we weight growth as a moderate rather than a strong dimension.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.85, COLB trades at ~13x trailing EPS ($2.30 trailing) and offers a 4.5% dividend yield; on the $3.05 current-year consensus, the forward multiple is ~10.8x. On price-to-tangible-book, with TCE/TA at 8.63% and ~$7.7B equity against $1.5B goodwill plus $671M intangibles, the stock trades at a premium to tangible book that reflects the franchise value of its sticky deposit base.

The analyst target set frames the range: consensus mean $33.64, median $34.00, high $38.00, low $29.00. With the stock at $32.85, the mean target implies only ~2-3% upside, and the stock sits near the 52-week high. We do not treat proximity to consensus targets as a hard ceiling in general, but the guardrail applies here: estimate revisions are drifting negative, growth is muted by design, and there is no identified catalyst driving the market to systematically re-rate the multiple higher. Consensus targets are a reasonable anchor rather than a stale floor.

We are deliberately skeptical of rewarding the "cheap" ~13x trailing multiple in isolation. A low P/E on a bank with 58% CRE concentration, a fresh agricultural credit problem, and negative estimate momentum is the value-trap profile that punishes a naive cheapness call. The multiple is fairly priced for a well-capitalized regional with a genuine funding advantage but limited near-term growth and above-average credit and legal risk. The dividend yield provides real support, but the absence of a specific mispricing catalyst caps the valuation score in the middle of the range. The clearer opportunity would emerge on a pullback that widens the yield or on evidence that synergies drive a demonstrable EPS re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

COLB's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The PPBI deal broadened the western footprint and added fee-generating custodial-trust and HOA-banking businesses, and management is credited with strengthening the funding mix. The non-interest-bearing deposit base and the sticky HOA/custodial deposits give the bank a structural funding-cost edge that peers with more rate-sensitive funding lack. As COLB continues shedding brokered deposits and below-market transactional loans in favor of relationship commercial credit, the underlying deposit franchise quality improves.

The offset is a persistent total-shareholder-return underperformance versus peers. The 2026 proxy shows cumulative five-year TSR of 99.85 versus a peer group at 149.15, a material, widening gap since 2021. The market has rewarded COLB's M&A-heavy strategy less than it has rewarded peers, reflecting investor skepticism about serial dilution and integration risk. Competitive position is improving at the operational/franchise level (funding, fee mix, scale) while the equity-market scorecard has lagged. The net is a solidly positioned mid-cap regional whose moat is its deposit base rather than superior returns, which supports a moderate competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE and multifamily concentration.** CRE is $27.6B, with real-estate-related loans ~75% of the book. Post-PPBI the mix skews toward multifamily (24% of total loans, ~$11.4B) and Southern California (32% of CRE). Multifamily carries rent-control/tax-policy and refinancing exposure; office is a smaller-than-feared ~7% (mostly suburban, ~53% non-owner-occupied) but remains a watch item. This is the dominant structural risk and is only partially priced.

**Credit normalization and the agricultural crack.** NPAs jumped 32% sequentially, from $200M (0.30% of assets) at YE2025 to $264M (0.40%) at Q1 2026, driven primarily by a single agricultural relationship: agriculture non-accruals more than doubled from $22M to $49M, and commercial net charge-offs rose $15M sequentially on the same relationship. ACL is $478M (1.00% of loans), but non-accrual coverage fell from 418% to 256%. Management frames this as idiosyncratic and consistent with normalization; against ~$1B of agriculture exposure, this is the single most concrete new negative and the key item to track through Q2/Q3. The bank's own CECL sensitivity shows a Moody's S2 (25% downside) scenario would raise modeled losses ~1.4x (~$147M), a meaningful earnings swing if the macro deteriorates.

**Litigation.** Two unusual items stand out: the iCap Ponzi-scheme aiding-and-abetting suit (co-trustees sued the Bank on behalf of 488 investors, ~$90M claim, broader investor claims cited ~$290M), and the MOVEit data breach (~429,000 customers, consolidated into MDL No. 3083). A separate $55M class-action settlement was already accrued/funded in Q1 2025. This is a somewhat elevated legal/reputational profile for a bank this size.

**Integration and rate risk.** Systems conversion and nine branch consolidations completed in Q1 2026; the risk is synergy underdelivery or budget overrun. The balance sheet is modestly liability-sensitive (EVE down ~4.4% for +200bps), so a renewed rise in rates pressures economic value even as near-term NIM benefits from prior deposit-cost reductions.

**Governance.** Compensation is modestly aligned (CEO total comp $7.71M, metrics on Operating PPNR/ROTCE/TSR), but rising CEO comp-actually-paid against persistent TSR underperformance versus peers is a genuine alignment concern. Standard bankruptcy screens (Altman Z ~0.25) are meaningless for a well-capitalized bank and should be disregarded.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows no near-term stress. ATM implied volatility collapsed from a distorted 388.9% reading on June 18 to 27.8% on July 22 (the prior print was almost certainly a thin-options data artifact and non-informative), and the put/call open-interest ratio fell to 0.09, indicating minimal defensive positioning. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (2.8 days to cover), consistent with a market that views COLB as neither a crowded long nor a bearish target. The benign options complexion supports the view that the risks here are slow-burning fundamentals (credit, synergy delivery) rather than an imminent binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($38).** Merger synergies land in full from Q3 2026, driving demonstrable EPS accretion off a leaner cost base; NIM holds near 3.96% or expands as brokered funding is fully shed; the agricultural non-accrual proves genuinely idiosyncratic and credit normalizes without spreading; the custodial-trust fee engine continues compounding; and the $400M remaining buyback plus 4.5% yield deliver a total return that finally closes some of the peer TSR gap. Consensus EPS re-rates toward $3.40+ and the multiple holds, supporting ~$38. This tracks the analyst high of $38.

**Bear case ($26-28, roughly –15% to –21%).** The agricultural credit is the leading edge of broader CRE/multifamily normalization; NPAs continue climbing and provisions rise, pressuring EPS; the Moody's S2-type scenario materializes and adds ~$147M of modeled losses; integration synergies underdeliver; and the iCap litigation produces a material adverse outcome. In that case the multiple compresses toward tangible book and the dividend, while covered, no longer supports the price, taking shares to ~$26-28.

The base case sits between: synergies deliver modestly, credit normalizes without a systemic break, EPS lands near the $3.05 current-year / $3.38 next-year consensus, and the stock trades in line with its dividend-supported multiple around $33-34. The income component (4.5% yield) is the durable return; capital appreciation depends on catalysts that are plausible but unproven.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income orientation over a 12-month horizon, anchored to the Q3 2026 synergy run-rate milestone and the Q2/Q3 2026 credit prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 confirmation that the agricultural non-accrual is contained (NPAs stabilize at or below ~0.40% of assets, non-accrual coverage rebuilds toward 300%+), removing the credit overhang.
- Full synergy run-rate demonstrated from Q3 2026 with EPS revisions turning positive (current-year consensus moving above $3.10 rather than drifting down), confirming EPS accretion from PPBI.
- A pullback toward the high-$20s that widens the dividend yield above ~5% and restores a clearer margin of safety on tangible book.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Agricultural non-accruals spreading into broader commercial/CRE normalization — NPAs rising above ~0.60% of assets, or provision expense materially outpacing charge-offs on new relationships.
- A negative CECL model revision or a Moody's-scenario shift that forces a step-up in the allowance and cuts EPS estimates by double digits.
- A materially adverse development in the iCap litigation, or synergy underdelivery/overrun that pushes efficiency ratios the wrong way.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), with the more important read at Q3 2026 when full synergy run-rate should be visible and the agricultural credit trend confirmed or refuted.

## Conclusion

Columbia Banking System is a well-capitalized western-U.S. regional with a genuine, underappreciated funding advantage (32-33% non-interest-bearing deposits, HOA and custodial-trust franchises), an expanding core NIM (~3.96%), and a shareholder-friendly capital-return program (4.5% yield, $400M buyback remaining). Those attributes support ownership for income. The thesis is capped by four things: PPBI dilution that turned rising net income into falling EPS and left the accretion case unproven; a fresh idiosyncratic credit crack in agriculture that must be watched; heavy CRE/multifamily concentration; and estimate revisions drifting modestly negative with the stock near its 52-week high and only ~2-3% below consensus mean.

The score trajectory (5.8 → 6.6 → 6.1) reflects this push-pull: financial health and competitive position have genuinely improved on funding-mix and franchise gains, while valuation has compressed as the shares appreciated into a fully-priced range and growth momentum flattened. The current data confirms a stable, income-quality regional rather than a mispriced re-rating candidate. We maintain HOLD (income hold). The valuation is fair, not cheap-with-a-catalyst; a low multiple on a bank with these concentrations and a new credit problem is a reason to require evidence before rewarding cheapness. Own it for the yield and the synergy optionality, and use the upgrade/downgrade triggers above to convert this into a stronger call when the credit and synergy questions resolve.
