**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ePlus is now a pure-play IT solutions provider following the June 2025 divestiture of its domestic financing business. FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) was a record year on the surface: net sales rose 22.1% to $2.44B, operating income jumped 67% to $166.1M, and diluted continuing EPS climbed 64% to $4.71. The growth was narrow. Telecom/media/entertainment net sales grew 58.8% to $720.6M, and Verizon alone accounted for 24% of total net sales (up from 17% the prior year) and 36% of trade receivables (up from 17%). Of the $442M total sales increase, $267M came from this single vertical. The 22% number is heavily concentration-driven.

Management's own forward read confirms the concern. FY2027 guidance is mid-single-digit growth across net sales, gross profit, and adjusted EBITDA — a deceleration from +22% sales and +50% EBITDA. Consensus estimates have followed: next-year EPS estimates have been cut 20.7% over 90 days and 13.4% over the last 30 days, and current-year estimates are down 7.9% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, which works directly against the single most reliable forward indicator in this business. The earnings growth that justified prior optimism has reversed into a guided plateau.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong — $410.8M cash, no meaningful traditional long-term debt, current ratio 2.24, Altman Z of 3.89 — and the negative FY2026 operating cash flow of -$116.2M is a working-capital artifact of the revenue surge rather than an earnings-quality problem. At ~$80.81, the stock trades at roughly 17x trailing continuing EPS and ~15x forward, which is fair but not compelling given decelerating growth and concentration risk. The recommendation is HOLD. The thesis has shifted from a growth story with operating leverage to a value-stabilization story whose central variable, Verizon's order cadence, is outside the company's control.

## Company Overview

ePlus, in business since 1990, is a U.S.-based value-added reseller (VAR) and IT solutions provider. It resells third-party hardware and software from vendors including Cisco, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Dell, NetApp, Palo Alto Networks, AWS, and CrowdStrike, and wraps those products in professional and managed services across AI, cloud, data center, security, networking, and collaboration. It serves roughly 4,200 middle-market and large-enterprise customers, plus state/local/education accounts.

Following the June 30, 2025 sale of its domestic financing business to Marlin Leasing (PEAC Solutions), the company reorganized into three segments: Product (~81% of net sales), Professional Services (~11%), and Managed Services (~8%). A growing share of subscription/as-a-service revenue is recognized net (only the margin is booked as revenue), which is why gross billings of $3.84B materially exceed reported net sales of $2.44B. This compresses reported top-line growth optics while improving recurring-revenue quality and blended gross margin.

ePlus is a channel partner and customer of the technology vendors it resells; it does not own or develop the underlying products. Its competitive differentiation comes from vendor certifications (it is the only North American partner with NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD specialization plus DGX-Ready Managed Services status), engineering talent, configuration/logistics scale, an AI Experience Center, and a strong NPS of 74. The moat is real but modest relative to vendors selling direct and to larger resellers like CDW, Insight, SHI, WWT, and Presidio.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Product revenue grew 23.8%, Professional Services 19.4% (aided by the Bailiwick acquisition), and Managed Services 10.6%. Gross profit rose 20.3% to $616.1M, with gross margin slipping 40 bps to 25.2% on product mix and a higher proportion of large-enterprise deals at competitive pricing. Operating margin expanded 180 bps to 6.8% — opex rose only 9% against 22% sales growth, with headcount essentially flat at ~2,148. Net earnings from continuing operations were $124.1M (+62%); non-GAAP EPS was $5.39. Adjusted EBITDA was $204.8M (+49.5%), an 8.4% margin.

**Balance sheet.** Strong and conservative. Equity of $1.07B, debt/equity 0.68 (predominantly floor-plan working-capital financing rather than true leverage). Altman Z of 3.89 sits in the safe zone.

**Cash flow.** FY2026 operating cash flow was -$116.2M (FCF -$120.7M), versus +$302M the prior year. This is a working-capital build, not a deterioration in earnings quality. AR rose to $668M (DSO 70 days from 66), inventory rose to $201M (DIO 28 days, double the prior 14), and AP fell. The cash conversion cycle jumped to 51 days from 29. Total cash still rose to $411M because the financing-business sale delivered ~$164M of investing proceeds. A 22% revenue surge concentrated in large Q4 enterprise shipments and Verizon receivables consumed working capital. If revenue growth slows as guided, the working capital should reverse and cash should rebuild in FY2027. The caveat is that a meaningful portion of FY2026 "earnings" currently sits in receivables concentrated in one customer at 36% of the trade-receivable balance.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one in-line over four quarters, including a 58.7% surprise in Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended Sept 2025) and 40.7% in Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended Dec 2025). The most recent quarter (March 2026) came in exactly in-line at $1.00. The beat magnitude was driven by the same concentration dynamic now decelerating, so the past beat record offers limited forward comfort.

## Growth Analysis

The growth narrative has inverted. FY2026's sales growth was front-loaded into the telecom vertical, almost entirely Verizon. Strip that out and underlying growth was substantially more pedestrian. Management's FY2027 guidance is the explicit acknowledgment that the FY2026 dynamic does not repeat.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Current-quarter consensus is down 10.6% over 90 days. The acceleration of the cut — a steeper 30-day revision than the prior trend on next-year — is the opposite of the positive-revision-plus-revenue-acceleration pattern that would support a constructive growth call. Negative revision velocity of this magnitude is among the more reliable predictors of continued relative underperformance, and it directly caps the Growth Prospects score.

The durable demand driver is AI infrastructure: management cites a strong AI pipeline, infrastructure modernization, and security (gross billings +23% to $842M, 22% of total). ePlus's NVIDIA DGX credentials are a genuine differentiator in mid-market and enterprise AI buildouts, and the broader AI-infrastructure demand environment remains intact across the buildout chain. Two factors temper the AI tailwind near-term: a worldwide memory-chip shortage that management flagged will extend lead times and raise prices "for at least the next few quarters," and the reality that as a reseller ePlus captures thin margins on the product layer where AI hardware sits. The AI theme supports the medium-term demand floor; it does not offset the FY2027 plateau.

## Valuation Assessment

ePlus trades at ~17.2x trailing continuing EPS and ~15.2x the $5.33 current-year consensus. On non-GAAP EPS of $5.39, the trailing multiple is ~15x. This is a fair multiple for a debt-light, cash-generative IT solutions business, but it is not a mispricing.

The single analyst target on file sits at $111, implying ~37% upside. Analyst targets on rising-estimate stocks act as floors rather than ceilings, but that logic does not apply here: estimates are falling sharply, and the $111 target may predate the FY2027 deceleration guidance and the cut to next-year consensus. It is a thin (2-analyst) coverage base, and the more probable adjustment is downward toward the falling estimate base.

A "cheap" trailing multiple is not, on its own, a buy signal, and in the current regime, cheapness without an independent re-rating catalyst has tended to sort the wrong way. ePlus lacks a clear catalyst: FY2027 is guided to a plateau, the working-capital reversal merely restores cash that was always going to come back, and the Verizon concentration is a risk overhang rather than an upside driver. The dividend (recently raised 8% to $0.27/quarter, ~1.4% yield) and ~$30.6M of FY2026 buybacks provide modest support but do not anchor a re-rating. Against the IT solutions peer set (CDW, Insight, SHI), ePlus trades roughly in line on a forward basis; it is neither a discount worth chasing nor a premium worth fading. The valuation score reflects a fairly priced asset with no asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

The VAR/IT-solutions market is competitive and consolidating, with structural pressure from hyperscaler cloud marketplaces, direct-marketing resellers, and vendors selling direct. ePlus competes against larger players with broader scale. The industry shift from upfront product sales toward subscription/consumption and net-basis revenue compresses reported net sales growth while improving recurring-revenue quality — a genuine headwind to top-line optics that the company shares with peers.

On trajectory, the picture is mixed. Vendor concentration is improving: Cisco fell from 44% of net sales in FY2024 to 29% in FY2026, a healthy diversification. The AI/DGX credentials are a real and differentiating capability in a high-demand niche. Against that, customer concentration worsened materially, and product gross margin compressed (Q4 product margin 22.2% vs. 24.7% prior year) as the company won larger, lower-margin enterprise deals. Management frames this as a "land and expand" opportunity, but the cross-sell offset remains unproven. The competitive position is stable rather than improving: the moat is intact but not widening, and the margin trajectory is slightly negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Verizon concentration is the central risk and it is intensifying.** Contracts are non-exclusive, carry no volume commitments, and are terminable on 30 days' notice. A Verizon pullback would hit both revenue and cash collection disproportionately, and the receivable concentration adds a collection-risk dimension that did not exist a year ago.

**Vendor and distributor concentration.** Cisco at 29% of sales (improving), with NetApp/HPE/Juniper/Dell/Arista collectively 23–28%; top-three distributors (Ingram Micro, Arrow, TD SYNNEX) exceed 30% of product purchases.

**Memory/AI supply chain.** The worldwide memory-chip shortage was explicitly cited as a reason for conservative FY2027 guidance.

**Financial-statement revision.** The 10-K discloses a revision of FY2024 and FY2025 statements to correct misstatements management deemed immaterial (Note 2). This is not a formal restatement, but given the complexity of the discontinued-operations accounting around the divestiture, it is a yellow flag warranting attention.

**Post-divestiture diversification.** Selling the financing business removes a revenue stream and may increase working-capital and earnings volatility. PEAC contingent consideration is performance-based and already took a ~$4.2M downward fair-value adjustment.

**WFCDF facility dependence.** The $500M working-capital/AP facility is terminable by either party on 90 days' notice.

These risks are not fully priced. The Verizon receivable concentration in particular is a binary-flavored exposure — concentrated, recently worsened, and on short-notice contracts — that the ~17x multiple does not appear to discount. This drives the Risk Factors score below the midpoint.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning leans cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 37.2% sits modestly rich versus 34.0% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.2% near-term vs. 33.9% six-month), which signals the market is pricing near-term stress. Put/call skew of 21.8% at the front month indicates elevated put demand, though the overall put/call OI ratio of 0.46 is not alarming. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±7.6%. Elevated near-term put demand combined with backwardation is consistent with the cautious fundamental read and argues against a constructive entry here. It does not point to an imminent collapse (short interest is a benign 5.0% of float, 5.2 days to cover), but it confirms that the options market is not pricing a positive catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95–105).** Verizon orders prove durable and the "land and expand" enterprise strategy converts large-deal wins into recurring services revenue at improving margins. Working capital normalizes in FY2027, restoring strong free cash flow and demonstrating the earnings were high-quality. AI-infrastructure demand and the NVIDIA DGX credentials drive product growth above the conservative mid-single-digit guide, and the memory shortage eases. The debt-light cash position funds accretive M&A or accelerated buybacks. Re-rating toward 18–20x on $5.50+ EPS gets the stock to the mid-$90s to low-$100s.

**Bear Case ($60–65).** Verizon reduces or shifts spend on 30 days' notice, removing the bulk of FY2026's growth and exposing thin underlying organic momentum. The 36%-of-receivables concentration creates a collection scare. Estimate cuts continue, the stock de-rates toward 12–13x on a falling estimate base, and product-margin compression from large-enterprise mix persists without the promised cross-sell offset. This puts the stock back near its 52-week low of $62.11.

**Base Case ($80–85).** FY2027 plays out roughly as guided: mid-single-digit growth, working capital reverses to restore cash, dividend and buyback continue, but no catalyst forces a re-rating. The stock trades sideways around 15–16x a stabilizing ~$5.40 EPS. This is the most probable path and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction over the next 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive: next-year consensus stops falling and revises up over two consecutive monthly reads, signaling the FY2027 plateau is conservative.
- Verizon receivable concentration declines below ~25% of trade receivables alongside evidence of customer diversification (no single new customer replacing the concentration), reducing the central risk.
- Operating cash flow turns clearly positive in the first 1–2 quarters of FY2027, confirming the working-capital reversal, combined with a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s that restores valuation asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Verizon spend reduction or contract change disclosed in an 8-K or earnings call, or a receivable-collection issue.
- FY2027 quarterly results showing organic (ex-Verizon) revenue growth turning negative.
- Any escalation of the Note 2 revision into a formal restatement, or new auditor/control concerns.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY2027 earnings report (expected late July/early August 2026), which will provide the first read on whether working capital reverses, whether the Verizon receivable concentration moderates, and whether mid-single-digit guidance holds.

## Conclusion

ePlus is a well-run, debt-light IT solutions business with a clean balance sheet, a maturing capital-allocation program (initiated and raised dividend, ongoing buybacks), and genuine AI-infrastructure credentials. The trajectory of the scores — from 7.0 in April to 6.7 in late May, with Growth Prospects falling from 7.8 to 7.0 — reflects a real change in the fundamentals, not just price action, and the latest data confirms rather than reverses that decline. The FY2026 growth was disproportionately Verizon-driven, customer concentration worsened, management guided FY2027 to a sharp deceleration, and consensus estimates are being cut at an accelerating pace. The negative options-implied sentiment (backwardation, elevated put skew) corroborates the cautious read.

At ~$80.81 and ~17x trailing/~15x forward earnings, the stock is fairly valued with no identifiable re-rating catalyst, leaving symmetric-to-slightly-negative risk/reward. The negative-FCF screen flag is benign on inspection, but the core conclusion holds: the easy growth is behind, the durable driver (AI infrastructure) is offset near-term by a memory shortage and reseller margin economics, and the dominant variable (Verizon) is outside management's control. **HOLD.** The bar for a BUY is positive estimate revisions or a meaningful pullback that restores asymmetry; the bar for an AVOID is concrete Verizon deterioration. Reassess at Q1 FY2027 results.
