**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CTS Corporation is a 130-year-old designer and manufacturer of sensors, connectivity components, and actuators built around its "Sense, Connect, Move" framework. The investment story over the past three years has been a deliberate, executed pivot away from transportation (down from 55% of revenue in 2023 to ~43% in Q1 2026) toward higher-margin, stickier medical, aerospace/defense, and industrial end markets, which now total 57% of revenue. That mix shift is showing up cleanly in the numbers: gross margin has expanded from 34.7% in 2023 to 39.5% in Q1 2026, operating margin from 13.6% to 15.8%, and revenue has re-accelerated to +10.7% YoY in the most recent quarter after two years of decline. The balance sheet is strong: Altman Z of 6.39, D/E of 0.19, a current ratio of 2.3, and FCF of $86.4M on a 16% FCF margin. There is little to debate on financial resilience.

The debate is on valuation and signal. The stock has risen roughly 62% over the trailing twelve months, from the low $40s to $67, while analyst consensus price targets (admittedly thin for a $1.9B name) sit at $58, below the current price. CTS now trades at ~28x trailing and forward earnings, a discount to the EMS peer median (52x), but those peers are not clean comparables; CTS is an engineered-components business, not a low-margin contract manufacturer, and its absolute multiple is full for a company guiding to mid-single-digit revenue growth and ~$2.45–2.55 adjusted EPS in FY2026. The most actionable governance signal is that the CEO sold ~$8.5M and the CFO ~$1.3M of stock in early 2026 into the recovery, with no offsetting open-market buying.

We rate CTS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The fundamental turnaround is real and the diversification is structurally sound, which supports continued ownership for investors already positioned. But at $67 the asymmetry that existed in the low $40s has been arbitraged away: forward multiple full, consensus targets below price, insiders monetizing, and the defense leg (a load-bearing piece of the bull case) showing a soft 0.75 book-to-bill. We would add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s, where the forward multiple compresses toward 21–22x.

## Company Overview

CTS (founded 1896, incorporated in Indiana 1929, headquartered in Lisle, Illinois) employs ~3,492 people and operates manufacturing across North America, Asia (China, Philippines, Taiwan), Europe (Czech Republic, Denmark, Poland), and Mexico. It reports as a single operating segment and sells highly engineered, application-specific components to OEMs and tier-one suppliers across four end markets.

The company competes on custom-engineered, design-in solutions rather than commodity parts. Approximately 86% of 2025 sales were generated through its own sales engineers (reflecting embedded design relationships), with only ~7% flowing through distributors. CTS holds ~285 patents. Management characterizes the company as a niche player whose competitors are often larger and more diversified, but no single competitor overlaps across all of CTS's product lines.

The strategic core is diversification away from transportation cyclicality and EV/ICE transition risk. End-market mix has shifted as follows:

| End market | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transportation | 55% | 49% | 43% | ~43% |
| Industrial | 24% | 23% | 26% | ~27% |
| Medical | 12% | 14% | 16% | ~18% |
| Aerospace & Defense | 9% | 14% | 15% | ~13% |

The pivot has been driven both organically and through bolt-on M&A: TEWA temperature sensors, Ferroperm piezoceramics, Maglab, and most importantly SyQwest, a sonar/underwater-acoustics defense business acquired in July 2024 for ~$122M. The diversified end markets (industrial + medical + A&D) now stand at 57% of revenue, up from 45% in 2023.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The trajectory has been choppy but is re-accelerating. Revenue fell from $586.9M (2022) to $550.4M (2023) to $515.8M (2024) as commercial-vehicle softness and Chinese OEMs taking share from CTS's Western transportation customers weighed on the largest end market. 2025 returned to growth at $541.3M (+5.2%), with diversified markets up 16.3% offsetting a 6.6% transportation decline. Q1 2026 revenue was $139.2M, with diversified markets +17.5% and transportation back to modest growth (+2.9%). Overall book-to-bill was 1.1, with Medical at 1.20 and Industrial at 1.29, but A&D at a soft 0.75.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded from 34.7% (2023) to 36.4% (2024) to 38.4% (2025) to 39.5% in Q1 2026 (+250bps YoY). Operating margin moved from 13.6% to 15.8% over the same span; adjusted EBITDA margin runs ~23%. The drivers are mix (medical/defense/industrial carry higher margins than transportation) plus operational efficiency. This is genuine operating leverage rather than one-off cost cutting, and it is the single most credible piece of the bull case.

**Cash flow quality is high.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $102.1M and FCF $86.4M, with OCF/net income of ~156%, above the 100% threshold that signals high-quality earnings. Capex is light at ~$16M/year (~3% of sales), consistent with an asset-light engineered-products model rather than capital-intensive contract EMS.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Cash sits at $82–91M against total debt of only $57.5M (YE25) / $62.5M (Q1'26) on a newly refinanced $300M revolver (Nov 2025, matures 2030), leaving a roughly net-cash position. Shareholder returns are buyback-led: $56.2M of treasury purchases in 2025 versus only $4.75M in dividends (a token $0.04/quarter, ~0.2% yield). A new $100M buyback was authorized in November 2025, with ~$81.7M remaining at Q1'26. Share count has fallen from ~32M (2022) to ~28.6M, supporting per-share metrics. Goodwill ($209M) plus intangibles ($149M) total ~46% of assets, reflecting the acquisition-driven model; the 2025 goodwill test passed with no impairments to date.

**Guidance.** FY2026 revenue $560–580M; adjusted diluted EPS $2.45–2.55 (raised after Q1); effective tax rate 21–23%; assumes light-vehicle production slightly down. Management's track record supports the guidance: 3 beats and 1 miss in the last four quarters, including an 18.1% beat in Q1 2026 ($0.62 actual vs. $0.53 estimate).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs. First, **margin expansion**, the clearest and least speculative leg, reflecting an irreversible mix shift toward higher-value products. Second, **diversified-market momentum**: medical (ultrasound imaging/therapeutics via Ferroperm piezo) and industrial (temperature sensing in heat pumps, pool/spa, appliances) are both running book-to-bill well above 1.0. Third, **defense ramp** via SyQwest, which contributed $22.3M in 2025, though this leg is the most vulnerable on program-funding timing.

Estimate revisions are supportive without being explosive. Current-year EPS consensus has crept up to $2.43 (from $2.40 ninety days ago, +1.2%), and next-year EPS has been revised up more meaningfully to $2.89 (from $2.60, +11.2% over 90 days, +6.6% over the last 30 days). The next-year revision is the strongest forward signal in the data and implies ~16% EPS growth from the FY2026 guide midpoint. This does not meet the threshold of a high-conviction signal (the +30% revision velocity that historically precedes sustained re-rating), but it confirms positive earnings momentum and a market gradually catching up to the margin story. We weight it as a modest positive to growth, not a standalone buy trigger; coverage is thin (one estimating analyst), which reduces the signal's robustness.

The structural growth ceiling is real: management guides FY2026 to mid-single-digit revenue growth, with transportation tied to global light-vehicle production that is guided slightly down. The growth story is fundamentally a margin-and-mix story layered on a low-single-digit organic top line, with future step-changes dependent on continued M&A.

## Valuation Assessment

At $67, on the more bullish next-year consensus of $2.89 the forward multiple is ~23x. The peer-comparison table shows an EMS group median P/E of 52x (BHE 94.6x, IPGP 163.6x, PLXS 42.1x, SANM 52.3x, FN 50.0x), against which CTS looks cheap, but this comparison is misleading. CTS is an engineered-components designer with design-in customer relationships, structurally different from low-margin contract manufacturers; several of those peer multiples are inflated by depressed trough earnings. The relevant question is the absolute multiple for CTS's own growth and quality profile, not a discount to a heterogeneous and partly distressed peer set.

On that basis, ~28x for a business guiding to mid-single-digit revenue growth and high-teens EPS growth (driven substantially by margin mix and buyback, both of which have finite runway) is a full multiple. The thesis that compelled ownership at $42, a margin-inflecting business trading at a lower multiple with consensus targets above price, has largely played out. Consensus price targets at $58 sit roughly 13% below the current price, signaling that on the existing estimate base the stock has overshot near-term fair value. We treat analyst targets as floors only when a stock looks cheap relative to rising estimates; here the stock trades above target with a full multiple, so the "targets are stale" logic does not apply.

We assign a valuation score of 4.5. The compression from where the stock used to trade is not a value-trap structural impairment (fundamentals are genuinely improving), but at the current price there is no identifiable mispricing to exploit. A cheap-on-peers screen reading has tended to mark a full price rather than an opportunity. The asymmetry would re-emerge in the low-to-mid $50s.

## Competitive Landscape

CTS occupies a defensible niche in engineered electronic components, distinct from commodity EMS. Its moat rests on design-in relationships, application-specific customization, and a ~285-patent portfolio. The diversification strategy mirrors a broader trend of automotive-heavy component makers moving into defense, medical, and industrial to escape transportation cyclicality, and CTS's execution is visibly working in the margin and book-to-bill data.

The competitive trajectory is improving on balance. Medical (Ferroperm piezoceramics into ultrasound) and defense (SyQwest underwater acoustics) are higher-margin, stickier businesses with multi-year program visibility, and CTS reported new defense customer wins (secure comms/anti-jamming), an underwater hull-penetrator contract, a large non-invasive aesthetics ultrasound award, and a North American OEM accelerometer win. Against this, the transportation franchise faces a genuine secular headwind: Cummins fell from 15.0% of sales in 2023 to 8.4% in 2025, partly the intended diversification but also reflecting real volume loss in commercial vehicles and Chinese OEMs taking share from CTS's Western customers. Toyota remains the only >10% customer at 11.2%.

CTS is a beneficiary of the broader sensor/connectivity content-growth trend, but it is a small player whose customers can cancel or defer orders (orders are volume estimates, not firm commitments). The competitive position is improving in trajectory, with the caveat that defense, the leg the diversification narrative leans on hardest, showed a soft 0.75 book-to-bill this quarter on funding timing.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration in transportation.** Toyota (11.2%) and the collapsing Cummins relationship (15.0% → 8.4%) illustrate both the diversification progress and the volume risk inside the largest end market. Orders are non-binding volume estimates.

**Environmental / EPA Superfund liability.** CTS is a potentially responsible party at multiple contaminated sites, including two National Priorities List Superfund sites (Asheville, NC and Mountain View, CA). The Asheville matter reached an agreement-in-principle on March 16, 2026 to settle CTS's share at ~$6.67M, which effectively caps the largest single exposure, but total undiscounted remediation reserves are $16.4M and rose sharply in 2025 (a $5.5M environmental charge hit SG&A). The joint-and-several nature of Superfund liability means periodic charges will recur and depress GAAP results versus adjusted figures; watch the GAAP-vs-adjusted EPS gap.

**Rare-earth / China supply chain.** CTS uses REEs, semiconductors, conductive inks, and silver/platinum pastes from limited suppliers, with China the predominant REE producer. Chinese export restrictions are a tangible risk to certain product lines, and CTS also manufactures in China.

**Tariffs and input inflation.** Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs, broader US/China/Mexico trade policy, and precious-metals inflation are pressuring costs; the new ~$2.6M platinum position in PP&E underscores precious-metals input exposure.

**Defense funding cyclicality.** A&D revenue depends partly on U.S. Government funding subject to termination-for-convenience and budget timing; the 0.75 book-to-bill is the near-term manifestation.

**Acquisition integration and goodwill.** With ~46% of assets in goodwill/intangibles, a downturn could trigger impairment, and the strategy depends on continued successful M&A.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.1% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 34.1% (a 5.1% spread), and the term structure is in mild backwardation (39.1% near-term vs. 36.3% at six months), indicating the market is pricing some near-term event risk. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-6.5% at one month), which leans bullish rather than fearful; there is no elevated put demand to flag against our HOLD. Short interest is negligible at 2.3% of float (1.8 days to cover), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor crowded bearish positioning. The implied one-month move is ~9.0% per the options chain, consistent with normal earnings-window uncertainty. The options market reflects a name that has run hard and where the market sees two-sided near-term risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($80–85).** The margin-expansion story has further to run as the mix continues shifting toward medical and defense; FY2026 adjusted EPS comes in at the top of guidance ($2.55) and next-year consensus of $2.89 proves conservative. Defense funding normalizes and book-to-bill recovers above 1.0; medical and industrial sustain double-digit growth. A well-priced bolt-on acquisition, funded by the near-net-cash balance sheet and covenant-light $300M revolver, adds a step-change to the top line. The market re-rates the engineered-components franchise toward 28–29x on $2.89, getting to ~$82.

**Bear case ($48–52, roughly -22% to -28%).** Transportation drag intensifies as Chinese OEMs take more share from CTS's Western customers and global light-vehicle production weakens; defense book-to-bill stays soft on funding delays; a recurring environmental charge widens the GAAP-vs-adjusted gap and dents reported results. The full ~28x multiple compresses toward 18–20x as growth proves to be mid-single-digit organic dressed up by buyback and mix. On $2.45 EPS at 20x, the stock returns to the low $50s.

**Base case ($62–66).** Mid-single-digit revenue growth, continued (decelerating) margin expansion, FY2026 EPS at ~$2.50 and FY2027 around $2.80, with the multiple holding in the 23–24x range. The stock trades sideways-to-modestly-lower from $67 over the next twelve months absent a value-accretive acquisition or a defense-funding recovery. This is the most likely outcome and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July/early August), with the A&D book-to-bill recovery and any M&A announcement as the key forward catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s (forward P/E compressing to ~21–22x), restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- A&D book-to-bill recovering above 1.0 for two consecutive quarters, validating the defense leg of the diversification thesis.
- A value-accretive bolt-on acquisition (closing at a reasonable multiple in medical/defense) that adds a credible step-change to the top line, combined with next-year EPS revisions accelerating beyond +15%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Transportation revenue declining more than mid-single digits YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling accelerating Western-OEM share loss rather than managed diversification.
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, indicating an M&A misstep given the 46%-of-assets intangible base.
- Gross margin rolling over (declining sequentially for two quarters), which would break the core margin-expansion thesis.
- Further clustered, sizable insider selling without offsetting buying.

## Conclusion

CTS has executed a genuine, well-documented turnaround: margins at multi-year highs, revenue re-accelerating, FCF conversion above 150% of net income, and a strong balance sheet that positions the company for opportunistic M&A. The diversification away from transportation into higher-margin medical, defense, and industrial markets is structurally sound and visible in the numbers, and it has earned the stock its 62% twelve-month re-rating.

The problem is price. At $67 and ~28x forward earnings, with consensus targets below the current price, top-two executives monetizing the recovery, and the defense leg of the bull case flashing a soft 0.75 book-to-bill, the asymmetry that existed in the low $40s is gone. The next-year estimate revisions are supportive but not at the magnitude that justifies chasing a full multiple, and external coverage is thin. This is a high-quality business at a fair-to-full price. We rate CTS a HOLD (growth-hold), and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s or on confirmation that the defense ramp and another value-accretive deal can extend the growth runway.
