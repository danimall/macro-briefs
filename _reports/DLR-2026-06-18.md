**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Digital Realty Trust is the world's second-largest data center REIT, operating 309 consolidated data centers (~2,408 MW of white-space IT load) plus a substantial unconsolidated JV portfolio. The company sits squarely inside the structural AI/cloud infrastructure build-out, and the Q1 2026 results confirm the demand is real: revenue grew 16.2% YoY to $1.635B, diluted FFO/share rose 19% to $1.99, bookings hit a record $707M, lease backlog reached ~$1.8B, and management raised 2026 Core FFO guidance to $8.00–$8.10/share. Capacity under development jumped ~52% quarter-over-quarter to 1,169 MW at an estimated 11.4% stabilized yield, with 61% already pre-leased, which de-risks the build versus a speculative one.

The central tension is how that growth is funded. DLR raised FFO/share 19% while issuing more than $1.3B of equity in roughly four months ($875M in Q1 at an average $178.36, plus $435M after quarter-end at $181.21). As a REIT distributing nearly all taxable income, the company cannot self-fund a $2.8–3.3B remaining-2026 development program; per-share growth is mathematically contingent on development yields (~11.4%) comfortably exceeding the cost of equity. The equity is currently being issued at a high multiple (>28x forward AFFO), which makes the issuance accretive today but leaves the model acutely sensitive to any de-rating of the stock. The same hyperscaler customers driving demand also represent the concentration risk: the largest customer is ~11% of revenue, and a hyperscaler capex pause (the "AI oversupply" debate now circulating) would hit DLR's strongest growth engine and its top tenants at the same time.

We rate DLR a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The business quality, demand visibility, and balance sheet are all sound, and we expect continued mid-teens FFO growth. What prevents a BUY is the combination of a stretched valuation (49.9x trailing P/E, ~23.4x forward Core FFO, ~28x forward AFFO), consensus current-year EPS estimates that have fallen 17.8% over 90 days, and an equity-funded growth model whose accretion reverses if the multiple compresses. The risk/reward at $187 is balanced.

## Company Overview

Digital Realty operates a global UPREIT structure: Digital Realty Trust, Inc. owns ~98.2% of the operating partnership, which holds substantially all assets. The platform addresses two demand vectors. The hyperscale/large segment (>1 MW deployments) serves cloud and AI customers under long terms; Q1 2026 new >1MW leases carried a 13.0-year weighted-average term. The colocation and interconnection segment (0–1 MW plus the PlatformDIGITAL/ServiceFabric products) is the higher-margin, stickier enterprise and network-dense business that supports pricing power and retention.

The footprint is global, with 48.7% of Q1 2026 revenue from outside the U.S. The largest metros by annualized rent are Northern Virginia (20.0%), Frankfurt (6.4%), Chicago (6.4%), Singapore (5.3%), London (5.3%) and Amsterdam (4.8%). The competitive moat rests on global scale, a land-and-power bank exceeding 5 GW of buildable capacity (58% in 100MW+ sites), carrier/cloud-neutral interconnection, an investment-grade balance sheet, and a capital-recycling apparatus (JVs with Blackstone, GI Partners, Mitsubishi, TPG, Realty Income, and the new Digital Realty DC Partners NA Fund) that lets DLR originate and operate assets while holding only a minority stake.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was a record quarter. Rental and services revenue was up 15.4% and fee income up 68%. Stabilized rental revenue rose 10.0% while non-stabilized rental revenue surged 39.7%, reflecting the development pipeline coming online in Northern Virginia, Paris and Frankfurt, plus FX tailwinds from the Euro, GBP and SGD. Net income available to common was $169.1M ($0.46 diluted EPS), up from $99.8M. FFO reached $700.4M, up from $1.67/share a year earlier. Operating cash flow grew 33% YoY to $532M.

The balance sheet is conservative for a capital-intensive REIT. Total debt is ~$18.1B, but debt/total enterprise value is only 21.7%, and EBITDA/interest coverage is 4.6x. The debt is 93.4% fixed-rate (including swaps) at a weighted-average rate of just 2.85%, with a ~4.7-year weighted-average maturity and a well-laddered schedule. Covenant headroom is comfortable: total debt/assets of 33–39% against a 60% cap, and unencumbered assets/unsecured debt of 274–301% against a 150% minimum. Cash stands at $2.4B with $3.7B available on revolvers. About 65% of debt is Euro-denominated, serving as a natural hedge against European net assets.

The Altman Z-Score will read "distressed" (~1.4). This is a false signal for a REIT: low asset turnover and depreciation-heavy real estate mechanically depress the Z-Score. The actual leverage and coverage metrics are investment-grade.

The earnings-surprise record requires careful reading. The trailing four quarters show a 658.5% beat in Q2 2025 (a $2.94 actual vs. $0.39 estimate, distorted by gains on asset contributions), then two GAAP-EPS misses (-55.1% in Q3, -25.0% in Q4) and an in-line Q1 2026. GAAP EPS is a poor lens for a data center REIT because gains/losses on JV contributions and FX translation swing it materially quarter-to-quarter. FFO/share is the operating metric that matters, and on that basis the trajectory and the raised Core FFO guide are positive.

The capital allocation watch-item is the heart of the thesis. Development capex is partly funded by continuous ATM equity issuance, which lifted share count to ~351M. Strong FFO/share growth is being achieved while steadily issuing equity, so per-share accretion is entirely dependent on the spread between development yields and the cost of equity remaining wide. At today's multiple that spread is healthy; it narrows quickly if the stock de-rates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth setup is the strongest part of the case. Three forward indicators corroborate durable demand: record bookings, a backlog rolling into 2027–2028, and the development pipeline up ~52% QoQ with 61% pre-leased at long lease terms. Pre-leasing at this level materially reduces lease-up and vacancy risk relative to a speculative build and gives visibility into 2028 cash flows.

The sector tailwind is broad and confirmed rather than a single-company story. U.S. hyperscalers have committed an estimated $650–700B to data-center buildouts, and data-center construction has surpassed office investment. The binding constraint across the industry is power and grid interconnection, not capital or demand, which favors incumbents holding secured, buildable land-and-power. DLR's >5 GW bank is increasingly the scarce moat.

The capital-recycling strategy is now core. DLR increasingly originates and operates assets while retaining only ~20% (the DC Partners Fund holds >$3B; DLR owns 20% plus <2% direct), earning management and incentive fees. This raises ROIC and reduces balance-sheet risk, but it means reported consolidated revenue understates the platform DLR manages, and it makes equity-method JV income (and Ascenty-related FX) a larger, lumpier component of earnings.

The growth cautionary flag is in the estimate revisions. Consensus current-year EPS has fallen 17.8% over 90 days (to $2.97 from $3.61) and next-year by 15.9% (to $2.86 from $3.41). These are GAAP-EPS estimates, not Core FFO, so they overstate the deterioration; management simultaneously raised the Core FFO guide. But the downward GAAP revisions are a reminder that the bottom line is increasingly sensitive to JV gains/losses, FX, and the dilutive drag of perpetual equity issuance. We score Growth Prospects 7.5, reflecting strong leasing momentum tempered by the equity-funded character of per-share growth and falling GAAP consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

On the operating metric that matters, the raised guide implies ~23.4x forward Core FFO at $187, and roughly 28x forward AFFO per recent third-party analysis. The dividend yield is 2.56%.

Against Real Estate peers, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 33.5x and median yield is 3.7%. DLR's GAAP P/E sits above the median, and its yield sits well below it. The closer comparables are growth-oriented infrastructure REITs: Realty Income at 50.7x, American Tower at 30.5x, Public Storage at 33.5x. On a forward-FFO basis DLR is not an outlier within the data center/infrastructure cohort, and a premium is defensible given the AI demand backdrop, the land-and-power moat, and mid-teens FFO growth. The premium is full, not modest.

The cycle-adjustment argument does not rescue the valuation here. The cases where a high trailing multiple is misleading involve trough earnings with rapidly rising estimates (+30% revisions, accelerating revenue from depressed levels). DLR is the opposite profile: a high-quality compounder already at a premium forward multiple, with GAAP consensus estimates falling, not rising. At >28x forward AFFO with a 2.56% yield, the market is already pricing continued execution; the absolute multiple offers little margin of safety. A specific third-party valuation pegged the common around 10% above fair value.

The analyst-target framework supports a balanced read. The consensus mean target is $218.72 (median $220), implying ~17% upside, with a high of $250 and a low of $180. The guardrail on treating targets as floors applies only to cheap-relative-to-growth stocks (forward P/E <30x or PEG <1.5). DLR is not in that bucket at ~28x forward AFFO, so the consensus target is more likely to reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price than to be an independent floor. We score Valuation 4.5: the multiple is full versus fundamentals, and there is no identified catalyst for a re-rating beyond continued in-line execution that is already priced.

## Competitive Landscape

DLR's primary peer is Equinix, which is more interconnection-heavy. It also competes against private and hyperscaler-owned capacity and a rising tide of PE-backed platforms (Blackstone, Vantage, QTS-type vehicles, and neocloud operators). The most important competitive dynamic is the flood of private capital into the asset class (Blackstone's $1.75B data-center REIT IPO in May 2026 is emblematic), which is compressing cap rates and crowding out single-asset M&A. Management has signaled it expects acquisitions to be a smaller part of growth as private buyers with lower return hurdles dominate, which is why DLR is pivoting toward development at ~11.4% yields.

DLR's position is improving on the moat that matters most. With power and grid interconnection as the binding industry constraint, the >5 GW secured land-and-power bank (58% in 100MW+ sites) is a widening advantage that newer entrants cannot easily replicate, particularly in supply-constrained Northern Virginia. The June 2026 ServiceFabric MCP launch (an AI-native interconnection/control protocol across 800+ data centers) extends the interconnection platform toward "Private AI" workloads and reinforces the stickier, higher-margin part of the business. We score Competitive Position 7.0, reflecting a strengthening structural position offset by intensifying capital competition on the acquisition side.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration cutting both ways.** The top-20 customers are roughly half of recurring revenue. Many of those anchors are the same hyperscalers whose AI capex is driving DLR's demand, so demand and concentration risk are the same exposure. A hyperscaler capex pause would simultaneously hit the strongest growth engine and the top tenant base. This is the single most important unpriced tail risk, tied to whether AI infrastructure demand decelerates.

**Equity-funding dependency.** With distributions at ~100% of taxable income, DLR structurally relies on ATM equity and debt. The model is accretive only while the share price keeps the cost of equity below ~11.4% development yields. A meaningful de-rating or a higher-for-longer rate path (which raises the cost of both equity and refinancing) would impair the development math. Management has noted distributions may at times need revolver funding.

**Geographic concentration.** Northern Virginia is 20% of rent, where power availability, grid constraints and local permitting/community opposition are recurring gating factors.

**AI power-density/obsolescence.** AI workloads demand far higher power density and cooling; existing facilities may need costly retrofits, and rapid technology shifts could strand capacity.

**FX translation.** ~Half of revenue and ~65% of debt are non-USD; Q1 2026 saw a $100M negative FX swing in OCI.

**Teraco put.** The Rollover Shareholders' put on the remaining Teraco interest became exercisable February 1, 2026 (two-year window), settleable in cash or stock — a ~$1.6B contingent dilution/liquidity item to monitor.

A modestly favorable note: the prior SEC Division of Enforcement inquiry into cybersecurity disclosures concluded with no enforcement action in late 2025, and the 10-Q reports no material legal proceedings. The 10-Q also states the FY2025 risk factors continue to apply with no new additions.

We score Risk Factors 5.0. The concentration-plus-equity-dependency combination is a genuine asymmetric downside that is only partly priced, and the options market is signaling near-term stress.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing acute near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV is 144.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 22.9%, a 121.5pp spread that places IV at the 90th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (144.5% near-term vs. 32.4% long-term), which the market uses to price imminent, dated stress. The put/call skew is elevated at 11.1%, the put/call volume ratio is 1.69, and the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 1.57 to 2.15 over the past month. The ATM IV jumped 103.7pp in a month on a flat (+0.3%) price. The extreme front-week IV is almost certainly distorted by a thin near-dated expiry, but the broader signal of rising put demand and a 2.15 OI ratio is a real divergence worth flagging against a constructive fundamental view. It argues against adding here and supports a HOLD over a BUY. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float), so this is hedging/positioning, not a structural bear bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($230–$250).** AI demand stays robust, hyperscaler capex does not pause, and the pipeline converts to stabilized cash flow at ~11.4% yields. Core FFO compounds in the mid-teens, the fee-income/capital-recycling engine scales, and the stock holds a premium multiple. At ~28–30x forward AFFO on a rising AFFO base, the stock reaches the $250 high target. The power-and-land moat keeps widening as the industry's binding constraint stays power, not capital.

**Bear Case ($150–$165).** A hyperscaler capex pause or AI-oversupply realization hits bookings and pressures the top tenants at once. A higher-for-longer rate path lifts the cost of equity above development yields, breaking the accretion math and forcing slower growth or dilutive issuance at a lower price. The multiple compresses toward the high-end of where it traded in late 2025 (the stock printed a 52-week low of $146.23 in December). At ~20–22x a flat-to-lower forward Core FFO, the stock falls to the $150–$165 range, roughly -12% to -20% from current levels, a drawdown the December trough demonstrates is realistic.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and the next two bookings/backlog prints.**

This is a growth-oriented hold: we expect continued mid-teens Core FFO growth and modest appreciation toward the consensus mean of ~$218, but the full valuation and the equity-funding sensitivity cap the asymmetry. The specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY is that DLR funds its per-share growth by issuing equity at >28x forward AFFO while GAAP consensus EPS has fallen 17.8% over 90 days, and the accretion that justifies the premium reverses if the stock de-rates, a self-reinforcing risk that the AI-capex-pause debate could trigger. The options market corroborates this with a put/call OI ratio of 2.15 and a 121pp IV-over-HV spread. That is a concrete valuation-plus-funding concern, not a generic "valuation appears full" objection.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback toward $160–$170 (high-single-digit to low-double-digit below current) that compresses the forward AFFO multiple toward ~24x while bookings/backlog remain intact — widening the development-yield-to-cost-of-equity spread.
2. Two consecutive quarters of record bookings plus rising GAAP consensus estimates (reversing the -17.8% 90-day revision), confirming demand is accelerating rather than plateauing.
3. Evidence the company can fund the development pipeline with less reliance on common-equity ATM issuance (e.g., scaled JV/fund capital recycling covering a larger share of capex), reducing the de-rating sensitivity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A hyperscaler capex pause or a major anchor non-renewal/loss that visibly cuts bookings below ~$400M/quarter.
2. A sustained share-price decline that pushes the cost of equity above ~11.4% development yields while DLR continues issuing stock — i.e., dilutive issuance below intrinsic value.
3. A Core FFO guidance cut, a dividend-coverage strain forcing revolver-funded distributions, or a forced cash settlement of the ~$1.6B Teraco put at an inopportune time.

## Conclusion

Digital Realty is a high-quality, structurally advantaged data center REIT executing well into the strongest secular demand backdrop in the sector's history. Record bookings, a raised Core FFO guide, a 61%-pre-leased 1,169 MW pipeline, and a >5 GW land-and-power moat all argue for continued mid-teens FFO growth. The balance sheet is investment-grade despite the misleading Z-Score.

The reason to hold rather than buy is precise: per-share growth is funded by perpetual equity issuance at a premium multiple, and that accretion only works while the stock holds its valuation. With GAAP consensus EPS falling 17.8% over 90 days, an options market pricing near-term stress, and the AI-capex-pause risk that would hit demand and tenant concentration in tandem, the current price offers a balanced rather than asymmetric setup. We would become buyers on a pullback toward $160–$170 or on confirmation that demand is re-accelerating with rising estimates. HOLD at $187.
