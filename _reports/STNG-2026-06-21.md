## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is one of the largest publicly listed product-tanker owners, operating a modern fleet of LR2, MR, and Handymax vessels predominantly through Scorpio pools and the spot market. The commercial model gives the company very high operating leverage to time-charter-equivalent (TCE) rates, which is the dominant driver of every fundamental in this report. Q1 2026 was strong, with net income of $216.3M, GAAP EPS of $4.32, and adjusted EPS of $3.02, and the company has now beaten consensus for four consecutive quarters. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised from $5.89 ninety days ago to $12.68 today, a +115% move that reflects how the market is repricing the strength of the 2025–early-2026 rate environment.

The problem is what comes next. On June 18, management disclosed that spot and pool rates eroded in Q2 2026 versus Q1, reversing an early-May trading update that had implied daily earnings roughly doubling sequentially. This is the single most decision-relevant data point in the file: the company's own guidance whipsawed from "rates ~2x Q1" to "uncertain product carrier sector" within six weeks. Product-tanker rates are driven by ton-mile demand, and a meaningful slice of the recent strength came from Red Sea/Suez diversions and Russia-sanction-induced rerouting of refined-product flows. A normalization of these trade routes would shorten voyages, cut ton-miles, and pressure rates further. That same de-escalation is broadly positive for risk assets but specifically negative for the trade-disruption premium embedded in tanker TCEs.

At $80.58, near the 52-week high of $87.39 and up 82% over the trailing year, STNG trades at roughly 7.9x trailing EPS and a much lower multiple on the elevated current-year estimate. That looks cheap on the surface, but a single-digit P/E is the normal signature of a cyclical at or near peak earnings; the market is discounting mean reversion, not mispricing the stock. The balance-sheet repair and buyback story are real positives, and the four-beat track record argues against fading near-term momentum aggressively. We rate STNG HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the bull and bear cases are close to symmetric, and the path of Q2/Q3 rates plus the evolution of the trade-disruption picture will determine which one wins.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers (incorporated in the Marshall Islands, headquartered in Monaco, NYSE: STNG) owns and operates a large fleet of product tankers that carry refined petroleum products (gasoline, diesel/gasoil, jet fuel, and naphtha) rather than crude. The fleet spans LR2 (~115k dwt), MR (~50k dwt), and Handymax vessels, and the company has historically marketed itself as the largest publicly listed pure-play product-tanker owner by vessel count.

The commercial model matters more than the fleet count. Vessels are operated largely through Scorpio pools (managed by a related Scorpio group entity) and on the spot market, with a minority on time charters. This maximizes utilization and rate capture during strong markets but leaves earnings almost fully exposed to spot TCE rates, which can move materially quarter to quarter. The related-party structure, with commercial pool management and technical management both provided by Scorpio group affiliates associated with the Lolli-Ghetti family, is a standing governance feature that warrants ongoing scrutiny but is well understood by the market.

A methodological note: STNG is a foreign private issuer that files Form 20-F (annual) and Form 6-K (interim), not 10-K/10-Q/8-K. Audited FY2025 financials and the current fleet/charter/debt schedule sit in the 20-F filed 2026-03-20 and the Q1 6-K filed 2026-05-05, neither of which could be retrieved directly for this report. Several balance-sheet specifics below are therefore inferred from sector knowledge and the verified earnings data rather than read line-by-line.

## Financial Analysis

The verified data points are strong but backward-looking:

- **Q1 2026:** Net income $216.3M, GAAP EPS $4.32, adjusted EPS $3.02 (the gap reflects non-recurring gains). On ~45.8M shares outstanding, this is a high-return quarter.
- **Q4 2025:** Revenue $241.4M, +25.6% YoY, ahead of estimates.
- **Trailing EPS:** $7.03, implying a 7.9x trailing P/E at the current price.
- **Earnings surprise record:** Four straight beats — Q2'25 +26.7%, Q3'25 +8.8%, Q4'25 +0.7%, Q1'26 +16.6%. The cadence supports confidence that management guides conservatively and that consensus estimates tend to lag actual rate capture.

The capital structure is the constructive part of the story. Through the 2022–2024 upcycle, STNG generated very large free cash flow and directed it toward aggressive debt reduction and share buybacks, materially de-leveraging from the heavy 2019–2020 debt load. A cleaned-up balance sheet is what allows a shipping company to survive the down leg of the cycle without dilutive equity raises or distressed asset sales, and it is the foundation of the financial-health score. The 2.2% dividend yield is modest; total shareholder return in this name has historically come through buybacks funded by cyclical cash flow, not the regular dividend.

The forward concern is concrete. Management's June 18 disclosure points to a sequential earnings decline in Q2 and confirms the violent quarter-to-quarter volatility inherent to a spot-exposed pool operator. The $216M Q1 print should not be annualized.

## Growth Analysis

"Growth" is the wrong frame for a cyclical shipping company; the relevant question is where we sit in the rate cycle. The estimate-revision data is striking and pulls in two directions:

- Current quarter: $5.57, +94.4% over 30 days, +300.2% over 90 days
- Next quarter: $2.10, +24.7% over 30 days, +116.7% over 90 days
- Current year: $12.68, +28.4% over 30 days, +115.3% over 90 days
- Next year: $6.41, +2.0% over 30 days, +33.4% over 90 days

The headline revision velocity clears the threshold that usually signals a strong continuation. But two things qualify it heavily here. First, these revisions are catching up to a rate environment that has already strengthened and that management is now telling us is softening in real time; consensus is revising up into a backward-looking strength while the forward setup deteriorates. Second, the term structure of the estimates is itself the tell: the next-year figure ($6.41) is roughly half the current-year figure ($12.68), and the next-quarter estimate ($2.10) is well below the current-quarter ($5.57). Consensus is explicitly modeling mean reversion off a peak. Rising estimates that simultaneously embed a forward decline are not the same signal as a stock with a rising forward curve.

The growth thesis, such as it is, rests on ton-mile demand staying elevated: sustained Red Sea diversions, durable Russia-sanction reroutings, and a refinery-dislocation pattern that keeps product flows long-haul. A normalization of these disrupted routes works against precisely this. A return to shorter, undisrupted voyages would be a net negative for product-tanker ton-miles even as it is a positive for the broader market. We score growth prospects at 5.0; the near-term revision momentum is real, but it is peak-cycle momentum with a declining forward curve and an identifiable demand headwind, not durable secular growth.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the Energy peer set (median P/E ~11.8), STNG screens cheap. The peer comparables, however, are a mixed bag of oilfield services and E&P names (HP, CRGY, WHD, OII, CRK) that share little with a product-tanker operator, so the comparison is of limited use. The more relevant peers (Torm, Hafnia, Ardmore, International Seaways) also trade at low single-digit-to-high-single-digit multiples on peak earnings, which is the structural signature of the sub-sector.

A low P/E on a cyclical at or near a rate peak is not a mispricing; it is the market correctly discounting that current earnings are unsustainable. The cleaner valuation anchor for shipping is price-to-NAV (the value of the fleet net of debt) and mid-cycle, not peak, EPS. On peak 2026 earnings the stock looks like 6x; on a normalized mid-cycle number closer to the $6.41 next-year estimate it looks more like 12–13x, in line with where the market values the group through-cycle. Neither figure points to a clear asymmetry at the current price.

The 82% trailing-twelve-month gain is not itself a reason to be cautious; it tracked a genuine fundamental rate upcycle. But the combination of a peak-cycle multiple, a management-disclosed rate softening, and a normalization scenario that compresses the demand driver leaves the risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetrically attractive. We score valuation at 5.0. There is no specific catalyst that would force the market to re-rate the stock higher from here; if anything, the next earnings print is more likely to confirm the Q2 softening than to surprise to the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG's competitive position is solid and broadly stable. Its scale, fleet modernity (eco/scrubber-fitted tonnage), and pool efficiency give it better-than-average rate capture and utilization, and the Scorpio commercial platform is a genuine operational advantage in matching cargoes to vessels. Against Torm, Hafnia, Ardmore, and International Seaways, STNG competes credibly on fleet quality and scale.

The trajectory, rather than the level, is what caps the score. The entire product-tanker complex benefited from a trade-disruption windfall that inflated ton-miles industry-wide; STNG's relative position within the group is not changing materially, but the absolute tailwind is at risk of fading. IMO decarbonization rules (CII/EEXI, future carbon pricing) add a capital-and-compliance overhang on older tonnage over the medium term. We score competitive position at 5.5: a strong operator in a sub-sector whose structural demand support may be normalizing, with no evidence of share gains or moat-widening that would justify a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is rate cyclicality, and it is partly idiosyncratic to the current moment rather than a permanent sector truism. The specific, near-dated risk is the management-disclosed Q2 rate erosion, which makes a sequential earnings decline likely and introduces the possibility that consensus estimates, currently revising sharply higher, reverse over the next one to two quarters.

The second-order risk is the normalization of disrupted trade routes. A durable normalization shortens voyages and reduces ton-mile demand, directly pressuring product-tanker TCEs. This is a two-sided dynamic: continued or intensified trade disruption would extend the rate strength, while a normalization compresses it. The macro event that most of the market is rooting for is specifically bearish for this stock's earnings driver. Additional standing risks include capital intensity and refinancing exposure to rates, related-party governance through the Scorpio group, IMO regulatory compliance costs, and the reduced disclosure cadence of a foreign private issuer (20-F/6-K rather than 10-Q).

We score risk factors at 4.5. The combination of peak-cycle earnings, a disclosed near-term rate softening, and a two-sided geopolitical dynamic that the consensus estimate trend has not yet incorporated represents material downside that is not fully reflected in a stock near its 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Product-tanker rates re-accelerate in H2 2026 on continued refinery dislocation and sustained trade-route disruption; trade routes stay disrupted (Red Sea diversions and Russia reroutes persist), keeping ton-miles elevated. The four-beat track record and current-year estimate revision prove consensus has been chronically behind the rate reality, and the stock re-rates as full-year EPS lands near or above $12.68. The de-leveraged balance sheet funds continued buybacks at a sub-7x multiple, accreting per-share value. Bull case target ~$100–120, in line with the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case

The Q2 rate softening extends into H2; a durable normalization of disrupted trade routes shortens voyages and compresses ton-mile demand across the product-tanker complex. Consensus estimates that revised sharply higher reverse, and the stock de-rates as the market marks down forward earnings toward the $6.41 next-year figure or below. A cyclical with a single-digit peak multiple compresses on both lower earnings and lower price-to-NAV. Bear case target ~$58–62, roughly 23–28% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting a genuinely balanced setup rather than a confident directional view.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 spot and pool TCE rates stabilize or re-accelerate, confirming the rate softening was temporary, with current-year EPS estimates holding above $11.
- A decisive continuation or intensification of trade-route disruption that locks in elevated ton-mile demand into 2027.
- A pullback to the high-$50s/low-$60s that restores a clear margin of safety relative to mid-cycle earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The Q2 print confirms sequential earnings decline and management guides Q3 lower, accompanied by consensus current-year estimates beginning to revise down.
- A durable, sustained normalization of disrupted trade routes, removing the ton-mile premium with no offsetting refinery dislocation.
- Evidence of renewed leverage build (debt-funded fleet expansion at peak asset values) or dilutive equity issuance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural reassessment point, alongside the evolving trade-route picture. The fundamentals have not deteriorated catastrophically, but the asymmetry that justified a higher score earlier has narrowed as the stock re-approached its peak and the forward rate signal turned softer.

## Conclusion

Scorpio Tankers is a well-run, de-leveraged product-tanker operator with a strong recent earnings record and rapidly rising current-year estimates. It is also a deeply cyclical stock trading near its 52-week high, on a peak-cycle multiple, into a management-disclosed Q2 rate softening and a potential normalization of disrupted trade routes whose resolution would be specifically bearish for its ton-mile demand driver. The current-year estimate revision is real momentum, but it is peak-cycle momentum with a declining forward curve and an identifiable demand headwind.

The bull and bear cases are close to symmetric: roughly 24–49% upside in a sustained-disruption scenario against 23–28% downside in a normalization scenario, with the next earnings print and the trade-route outcome as the swing factors. We do not see enough asymmetry to recommend buying near the highs, nor enough fundamental deterioration to recommend avoiding a profitable, balance-sheet-strong operator with four consecutive beats. We rate STNG **HOLD (uncertain)** and would reassess at the Q2 print, looking for either rate stabilization (upgrade) or a confirmed sequential decline with falling estimates (downgrade).
