**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KBR is a global provider of engineering, technology, and logistics services organized into two segments: Mission Technology Solutions (MTS, defense/intelligence/space/civilian government, ~72% of revenue) and Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS, energy process-technology licensing and advisory, ~28%). At $36.93 the stock trades ~29% below its 52-week high of $52.23, at roughly 10.7x trailing and forward earnings, a steep discount to the diversified-support-services peer median near 37x, though that peer set is heterogeneous and the more relevant government-services comps (SAIC at ~13x) sit closer to KBR. The central event is a planned tax-free spin-off of MTS into a standalone public company ("SpinCo"), now firmed to January 4, 2027, designed to separate a lower-margin government-services business from higher-margin technology licensing and unlock a sum-of-the-parts re-rating.

The bull-bear tension is unusually clean. On the positive side: a $17.3 billion backlog (plus ~$5.9–6.3 billion of priced options), major new awards ($8 billion NSF Antarctica ceiling, NASIC IDIQ, Golden Dome positioning), a favorable multi-year defense-spending backdrop, healthy underlying free cash flow (the screen-level negative FCF is a discontinued-operations artifact; real continuing-ops operating cash flow was $557 million in FY2025), and a May 2026 cluster of open-market insider buys by the CFO and three directors at ~$30–32, below today's price. On the negative side: Q1 FY2026 revenue fell 5% and EPS declined to $0.80 from $0.88 on reduced European command activity, consensus estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative, TSR has lagged the peer group by ~75 points, and a January 7, 2026 Executive Order gives the Secretary of War authority to seek limits on KBR's dividends and buybacks if it judges contract performance inadequate, an idiosyncratic capital-return threat.

We rate KBR a HOLD with a constructive lean (growth_hold). The spin-off is a legitimate value-unlock catalyst and the insider buying is a strong signal, but the catalyst is roughly six months out, execution-dependent, and near-term operating momentum has softened. Accumulate on weakness toward the insider-buy zone rather than chase at current levels. The score trajectory has declined from 6.5 (March) to 5.8 (June), driven by revenue deceleration and multiple compression; our current 5.95 reflects stabilization at the lower end as the insider signal and spin timeline offset weaker growth.

## Company Overview

KBR emerged from its Halliburton roots into a diversified government-and-energy services contractor with ~36,000 employees. Following a FY2025 segment realignment, the business runs two reportable segments:

**Mission Technology Solutions (MTS)** — ~72% of revenue ($5.58 billion FY2025). Full-lifecycle defense, intelligence, space, aviation, and civilian-agency support: systems engineering, C5ISR, cyber, space domain awareness, prototyping, and digital/AI integration. Key customers include the U.S. Army, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Missile Defense Agency, NRO, NGA, NASA, plus U.K. MoD and Australian defense. Predominantly cost-reimbursable and time-and-materials work with modest margins but reliable cash conversion.

**Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS)** — ~28% of revenue ($2.21 billion FY2025). Anchored by 85+ proprietary process technologies (ammonia/syngas, petrochemicals, clean refining, circular economy) with high-margin licensing (K-GreeN green ammonia, Hydro-PRT plastics recycling, PureSAF sustainable aviation fuel), plus energy-transition advisory and engineering. STS carries higher margins than MTS and is boosted by equity earnings from LNG-project joint ventures.

Customer concentration is significant: U.S. government represents 57% of consolidated revenue, U.K. government another 9%. The model is asset-light (PP&E only $232 million), which supports favorable cash conversion but also means goodwill and intangibles (~$3.4 billion, 54% of assets) dominate the balance sheet. The strategic thesis is that separating MTS and STS lets each be valued on its own terms — defense-services multiples for SpinCo, higher-margin technology-licensing multiples for STS — releasing a conglomerate discount that the ~75-point TSR gap versus peers arguably reflects.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** $6.56 billion (FY2022) → $6.96 billion (FY2023) → $7.71 billion (FY2024) → $7.79 billion (FY2025). Growth decelerated to +1% in FY2025 as the LinQuest acquisition tailwind was offset by declining European command and space activity. Q1 FY2026 revenue fell 5% year-over-year to $1,923 million on reduced contingency activity in the European command, the clearest near-term negative in the data.

**Profitability.** FY2025 operating income was $778 million (+18%), net income to KBR $415 million. Segment operating income: MTS $463 million (+12%), STS $477 million (+18%, aided by LNG equity earnings). Net margin ~5.9%, ROE ~30.5%, ROA ~7%. The FY2023 headline net loss (-$265 million) was driven by one-time items (a $144 million legacy legal settlement and $494 million of convertible-note charges), not operational deterioration.

**Cash flow — correcting the screen.** Standardized data pulls show negative operating cash flow (-$33 million) and negative FCF (-$75 million), which would flag an earnings-quality alarm. This is a data-stitching artifact that conflates discontinued operations. The actual 10-K shows continuing-operations operating cash flow of $557 million in FY2025 (up from $450 million) against $458 million of net income from continuing operations, a healthy ~1.2x conversion. Capex is minimal (~$42 million), so real free cash flow is solidly positive at roughly $500 million+, implying an FCF yield above 10% at the current market cap. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow rose to $110 million from $91 million. The Altman Z-Score of 1.74 ("distress zone") is likewise misleading for an asset-light services contractor generating 30% ROE; the score penalizes high asset turnover and leverage in a way that overstates credit risk here.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$2.6 billion (Term Loan A $977 million, Term Loan B $980 million, $250 million 4.75% Senior Notes due 2028, $395 million revolver). Cash was $380 million (April 2026). Book D/E of 3.37x looks high, but the covenant net-leverage ratio (max 4.00x) is in compliance. Interest expense was $158 million in FY2025, with ~$1.8 billion of debt fixed-rate after swaps. The U.K. frozen pension is in an ~$85–95 million surplus with no near-term funding required, a positive versus many industrial peers. The mechanics of the spin matter: SpinCo will raise debt and distribute cash to KBR to reduce RemainCo leverage, so the deal is deleveraging for the surviving entity.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend held at $0.165/quarter (~1.85% yield). $427 million remains on the buyback authorization, but repurchases slowed sharply in FY2026 (only $4 million withhold-to-cover in Q1 versus $156 million in Q1 FY2025) as cash is conserved ahead of the spin. KBR contributed $115 million to the BRIS JV in Q1 FY2026 to fund a welding/turnaround-services acquisition (STS expansion) and made further investments in Mura Technology (~17% stake, plastics recycling).

**Earnings surprise track record.** Four consecutive beats (Q2 FY2025 +1.9%, Q3 +5.7%, Q4 +3.3%, Q1 FY2026 +4.6%) suggest management guidance is conservative and forward estimates are dependable. This supports confidence in the ~$3.97 current-year consensus EPS even as headline revenue softens.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is the weakest part of the story. Revenue decelerated to +1% in FY2025 and turned negative (-5%) in Q1 FY2026 as European command contingency activity wound down and the government shutdown (October–November 2025) delayed awards, execution, and payments. Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-quarter consensus is $0.90 (unchanged over 30 days, -2.5% over 90 days), current-year $3.97 (+0.3% / -0.2%), next-year $4.14 (-0.2% / -1.4%). There is no positive revision velocity here, and the +30% inflection signal that would justify aggressive re-rating is absent.

The offsetting positives are structural and catalyst-driven rather than showing up in trailing growth. Backlog rose to $17.3 billion (April 2026) from $16.9 billion, plus ~$5.9–6.3 billion of priced options, providing multi-year revenue visibility. New awards are meaningful: an $8 billion-ceiling NSF Antarctic Science & Engineering Support Contract (June 2026), a NASIC multiple-award IDIQ (July 2026), and positioning for the Golden Dome missile-defense program. The macro backdrop is favorable: FY2026 defense appropriations of $839 billion, NDAA up to $901 billion, and a proposed FY2027 defense budget of $1.5 trillion (+44%). The DoW's acquisition strategy favoring OEM-independent capability integrators plays to KBR's positioning, and NATO/allied defense-spending commitments (5%-of-GDP-by-2035 pledge, U.K. and Australian increases) support MTS demand internationally.

The critical caveat is that federal procurement is being reshaped by DOGE efficiency pressure, fixed-price mandates, and shutdowns, which redistribute value away from civilian/non-defense discretionary work toward defense/intel/space. KBR's 57% U.S. government exposure is a two-sided variable: defense/intel/space is favored, but any civilian-agency de-scoping is a headwind. The growth thesis for the next 12 months rests less on organic acceleration than on the spin-off unlocking value and on backlog conversion resuming after the shutdown-driven air pocket.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.93, KBR trades at ~10.7x trailing and forward earnings and ~8.9x on next-year consensus EPS of $4.14. Against the diversified-support-services peer median P/E of 36.8x, KBR looks deeply discounted, but that median is distorted by low-quality comparables (Openlane at a negative multiple, UNF at 37x, AMTM at 36.8x). The cleaner government-services comparable is SAIC at ~12.9x, which puts KBR roughly in line to slightly cheap. Amentum, another direct peer, sits 46% below its 52-week high, indicating the whole federal-services subsector is being derated on procurement-reform and shutdown fears; KBR is participating in that repricing rather than being uniquely cheap.

The valuation case rests on three legs. First, the sum-of-the-parts argument: STS (higher-margin technology licensing with proprietary IP) should command a premium multiple standalone, while SpinCo (defense/government services) is valued at government-services multiples; the conglomerate discount that suppressed the blended multiple should narrow post-separation. Second, the absolute FCF support: ~$500 million+ of real free cash flow against a $4.7 billion market cap is an FCF yield above 10%, which provides downside support even absent re-rating. Third, cycle position: at ~10.7x on stable-to-growing earnings with a favorable defense-spending backdrop, the multiple embeds substantial pessimism.

We temper the score for three reasons. Cheapness in this sector has been an anti-predictive value-trap signal: the federal-services derating (BAH, AMTM) shows that a low multiple can persist or compress further absent a catalyst. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, so there is no earnings momentum to force a re-rating. And the spin-off, while a genuine catalyst, is six months out and execution-dependent, so the sum-of-the-parts value is not yet crystallized. Analyst targets (mean $45.86, median $45.00, low $36.00, high $60.00) imply meaningful upside from consensus, but the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, so we treat these as aspirational rather than confirmed. A valuation score of 6.0 reflects an inexpensive stock with real downside support and a value-unlock catalyst, discounted for the value-trap risk that has characterized the subsector.

## Competitive Landscape

MTS competes with Leidos, SAIC, Booz Allen, CACI, Amentum, Parsons, and Jacobs' government unit in defense-services and government-IT. KBR's differentiation is its positioning as an OEM-independent capability integrator, which aligns with the DoW's November 2025 "Transforming the Warfighting Acquisition System" strategy favoring vendors that can integrate across manufacturers. The NSF Antarctica win and NASIC IDIQ confirm the company remains competitive on large recompetes, though the stock's decline even on the Antarctica award announcement signals the market is more focused on near-term revenue softness and procurement-reform risk than on backlog wins.

STS occupies a more defensible niche: proprietary process-technology licensing (85+ technologies) with high margins and IP moats, supported by energy-security and decarbonization spending. The LNG-JV equity earnings are lumpy but have been favorable (+$52 million estimate change in Q1 FY2026). STS is the higher-quality half of the company and the primary beneficiary of the re-rating thesis post-spin.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly gaining share. Revenue is not growing faster than the defense-services industry, and the European command decline reflects contract roll-off rather than competitive loss. The score of 6.0 reflects a solid, defensible position in both segments without evidence of accelerating share gains — level is good, trajectory is neutral. The competitive_position dimension has historically carried a mildly negative recent signal in our framework, so we weight it modestly and lean on financial health and the catalyst instead.

## Risk Assessment

**Government-spending and budget dependence.** With 57% of revenue from the U.S. government, KBR is exposed to continuing resolutions, shutdowns, DOGE-era staff reductions, and the FAR overhaul. This is partially offset by the strong multi-year defense-spending backdrop, but the civilian-agency portion is directly threatened by budget-efficiency reviews.

**Unusual KBR-specific regulatory risk — January 7, 2026 Executive Order.** The order permits the Secretary of War to seek limits on KBR's ability to pay dividends or repurchase shares if it judges the company has underperformed or under-invested on U.S. government contract performance. This is a rare, company-relevant capital-return constraint not seen at most peers and directly threatens the shareholder-return leg of the thesis. It is the single most idiosyncratic risk in the file and is not obviously priced.

**Spin-off execution risk.** The separation carries stranded-cost, tax-free-qualification, financing-dependency, and IT/ERP-disentanglement risks. SpinCo must raise the intended debt to fund the cash distribution to RemainCo; a disrupted debt market would complicate the deleveraging mechanic. A failed or delayed spin removes the primary catalyst.

**Contract and estimate risk.** Cost-to-cost revenue recognition on complex fixed-price and PFI contracts creates catch-up-charge exposure; $98 million of backlog is in loss positions. LNG-JV estimate changes cut both ways.

**Legal and contingency.** FKTC (Kuwait) — a lower court ruled against KBR for $41 million in March 2026 (appealed, no accrual taken). LogCAP III closeout continues, and DCAA cost-disallowance exposure ($39 million accrued) persists.

**Goodwill impairment.** The ~$3.4 billion goodwill/intangible base, concentrated in the LinQuest and Infrastar acquisitions, is an impairment-exposure item if the stock and segment margins stay depressed.

We score risk 5.0. The business is not fundamentally fragile (real cash flow is healthy, leverage is covenant-compliant), but the combination of a novel dividend/buyback-restriction EO, a binary spin-off execution, procurement-reform exposure, and pending litigation creates enough asymmetric, not-fully-priced downside to keep this below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.9% is rich versus 32.7% one-year historical volatility (an 11.2-point spread), and the term structure is flat (near 43.9% ≈ far 42.1%), indicating the market is pricing evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a single imminent binary. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.6% near-term), and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 2.47 to 1.34 over the past month as the stock rose 9.6%, a meaningful reduction in bearish positioning that corroborates improving sentiment off the May lows. The 6-month tenor shows a modestly negative skew (-5.1%), consistent with hedging into the spin timeline. Rich IV argues against paying up for calls; a patient accumulator is better served scaling in on weakness than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50–60).** The spin-off closes on schedule, the market values STS on higher-margin technology-licensing multiples and SpinCo on defense-services multiples, and the sum-of-the-parts exceeds the current blended value, narrowing the TSR gap versus peers. Backlog converts as shutdown-driven delays clear, the favorable FY2027 defense budget drives MTS growth, STS licensing and LNG equity earnings compound, and the RemainCo deleveraging (via SpinCo's cash distribution) improves the credit profile. The May 2026 insider cluster proves prescient. High-end analyst target of $60 implies ~62% upside.

**Bear Case ($26–29).** The procurement-reform derating deepens: DOGE efficiency cuts and fixed-price mandates de-scope civilian-agency work, the January 2026 EO forces a dividend/buyback constraint, and revenue continues to decline into FY2027. The spin-off slips or SpinCo struggles to raise debt on acceptable terms, removing the catalyst. Goodwill impairment on the $3.4 billion base hits reported earnings. The stock revisits the low-$30s / high-$20s, a ~22–30% drawdown. This is the value-trap scenario the sector's cheapness has repeatedly punished.

## Conclusion

KBR presents a well-defined event-driven setup: a cheap, high-cash-flow government-and-energy services contractor into a tax-free spin-off designed to unlock a sum-of-the-parts re-rating, with a high-conviction insider-buying cluster endorsing the value case. The screen-level cash-flow "red flag" is a discontinued-operations artifact; the underlying business generates $557 million of continuing-ops operating cash flow at ~30% ROE.

The offsetting reality is that near-term operating momentum has softened — Q1 revenue -5%, EPS declining, flat-to-negative estimate revisions — and the sector faces a genuine procurement-reform derating that has punished cheap federal-services names (Amentum 46% off its high). The January 2026 Executive Order threatening dividend/buyback restrictions is a novel, not-fully-priced idiosyncratic risk. The catalyst is real but roughly six months out and execution-dependent, and cheapness alone has been an unreliable buy signal in this subsector.

We rate KBR a HOLD with a constructive lean. The insider signal and the spin timeline warrant a positive bias, but the absence of earnings momentum and the binary nature of the catalyst argue against maximum conviction at current levels. Accumulate on weakness toward the $30–32 insider-buy zone; upgrade to BUY on positive estimate revisions or de-risked spin financing. The overall score of 5.95 sits essentially flat versus the prior 5.8, reflecting stabilization after the March-to-June decline as the insider buying and firmed spin timeline offset the weaker growth trajectory.
