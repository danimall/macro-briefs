## Executive Summary

Verisign is the sole registry operator for .com and .net, a contractually granted utility franchise that converted $1,656.6M of FY2025 revenue into $1,121.0M of operating income (67.7% margin) and $1,068.3M of free cash flow with 928 employees. The financial architecture is close to unique: negative working capital by design, 15.5x interest coverage on H1 2026 operating income, maximum equity dilution disclosed at under 1.0%, and a share count down 16.3% in three and a half years. The reported stockholders' deficit of −$2,254.1M is the arithmetic residue of $16.19B of cumulative buybacks charged against additional paid-in capital, and the 0.49 current ratio reflects $1,084.8M of deferred revenue that is discharged by operating the registry rather than by paying cash. Neither is a solvency signal.

Two developments give the next eighteen months more definition than usual. First, the .com registry agreement permits price increases only in the final four years of each six-year period, and the current period began October 26, 2024 — meaning the +6.4% FY2025 and +6.3% H1 2026 revenue growth was achieved almost entirely on volume, with the first available increase (+6.9%, $10.26 to $10.97) taking effect November 1, 2026. Management guided that roughly half of that increase lands in 2027 revenue, which on a 168M .com base assumption (the split is not disclosed) is approximately $60M, or 3.4% of FY2026E revenue and about 5% of operating income, flowing at near-full incremental margin. Second, the decade-long .web litigation with Altanovo resolved and .web was delegated on July 22, 2026 — the first TLD in the portfolio with unrestricted wholesale pricing, no Cooperative Agreement oversight, premium-name monetization rights, and unrestricted channel marketing. It carries zero registrations today, cannot reach general availability before late 2026 at the earliest, and is excluded from FY2026 guidance.

The obstacle is price. At $290.02 the stock trades at 30.3x current-year consensus EPS of $9.56, 27.0x next-year $10.76, 23.1x EV to FY2026E operating income, and a 4.0% FCF yield, for a business guiding to 5.6% revenue growth. Consensus FY2027 of $10.76 decomposes sensibly — roughly 5 points from the November price increase, 4 to 5 from volume, 3 from buyback — which raises confidence in the estimate but means upside depends on the multiple holding. It did not hold six months ago: on February 6, 2026, following a 4.3% EPS miss, the stock fell from $242.62 to an intraday $208.86, roughly 22x forward. That episode defines the bear case and is the reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Company Overview

Verisign's customers are ICANN-accredited registrars, not registrants. Registrars pay a per-annual-registration wholesale fee and set retail pricing themselves; Verisign remits $0.2575 per annual .com registration to ICANN and $0.75 per .net registration. The company therefore sits one layer removed from end demand, with limited visibility into retail pricing, marketing emphasis, or churn drivers — a structural feature that matters for interpreting the current unit surge.

The asset base: .com under an ICANN registry agreement through November 30, 2030 plus a Cooperative Agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce (auto-renewed November 30, 2024 for a six-year term); .net through July 1, 2029; .name, transliterated IDN gTLDs, .cc, back-end services for .edu (all immaterial); Root Zone Maintainer under an agreement running to October 20, 2032; two of the thirteen global root servers; and now .web. Infrastructure comprises three data centers in Virginia and Delaware plus more than 200 points of presence, processing hundreds of billions of queries daily with 29 consecutive years of 100% .com/.net resolution availability as of July 2026.

The moat is incumbency plus contract, not technology. Roughly 179M registered names embed switching costs in every link, email address, and search index entry pointing at them. The contract cuts both ways: Verisign discloses that pricing, bundling, marketing, distribution, and new-service restrictions apply to .com/.net but not to ccTLDs or other gTLDs, "and therefore may create a competitive disadvantage."

Depreciation fell from $17.2M to $13.0M in H1 because prior-year capex was abnormally low; that tailwind reverses.

**Cash generation and the seasonality caveat.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,091.1M against $22.8M capex, giving $1,068.3M of free cash flow — a 64.5% FCF margin. H1 2026 OCF was $504.0M with $26.0M capex, or $478.0M of H1 FCF. Collections are back-half weighted: H1 2025 OCF represented 45.3% of the full year. Applying identical seasonality to H1 2026 implies FY2026 OCF of approximately $1,112M; less guided capex of $55–65M gives roughly **$1,050M of FY2026E free cash flow**. That is a seasonality-adjusted sensitivity, not a forecast, and two timing items sit inside it: FY2025 cash taxes benefited from restored immediate R&D expensing, and H1 2026 benefited from the absence of the final TCJA transition-tax installment paid in 2025.

**Capex is stepping up 2.4x to 2.9x, for a disclosed reason.** Guidance of $55–65M against $22.8M actual reflects AI-driven hardware inflation. The FY2025 10-K names it directly: demand for "servers, chips, memory and other technology, caused by new demand from AI companies, is increasing our costs." Management confirmed server and memory pricing has had "a meaningful impact," that spend was pulled forward from 2027 "to avoid price increases that we know are coming," and that it competes with data-center buildout for the same components. At roughly 3.5% of revenue this cannot threaten a 68% margin, but it is a real cost channel in a business contractually barred from raising .com price until November 1.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $1,802.8M, total liabilities $4,056.9M, stockholders' deficit −$2,254.1M. The H1 roll-forward reconciles exactly: −2,154.2 + 431.0 net income − 426.8 buybacks − 147.8 dividends + 39.0 SBC + 8.5 ESPP − 3.7 excise tax − 0.1 OCI = −2,254.1. Of $1,899.1M current liabilities, $1,084.8M is deferred revenue and $549.3M was the 2017 notes reclassified on an irrevocable redemption notice and already cash-funded (redeemed July 20). Residual accounts payable and accruals of $265.0M are covered 4.2x by $1,122.3M of current assets. Standard distress screens do not apply here — with book equity negative by construction, two of five Altman Z components floor at zero and the score is carried by ratios inflated by a $1.8B asset base.

Post-refinancing debt is $1.80B: $750M at 2.70% (2031), $550M at 5.10% (July 2031, issued June 26, 2026), $500M at 5.25% (2032). Weighted average coupon 4.14%; $200M revolver undrawn to 2028. Two observations. The June refinancing raised that tranche's coupon from 4.75% to 5.10%, ending a long run of coupon reduction. And 72% of the stack ($1.30B) now matures in 2031, including the 2.70% paper — refinancing that at ~5.1% would add roughly $18M of annual pretax interest, about 1.5% of FY2026E operating income. Pro forma liquidity after the July redemption is approximately $485M and pro forma net debt approximately $1.32B, or 1.1x FY2026E operating income.

The dividend, initiated April 2025, was raised 5.2% to $0.81 quarterly in February 2026 — $3.24 annualized, a 1.12% yield at $290.02, roughly $293M per year.

Financial health scores 8.0. The negatives are second-order: refinancing into higher rates, a 2031 maturity cluster, and a payout above FCF that depends on continued leverage capacity.

## Growth Analysis

The domain base inflected: 172.7M (Dec-2023) → 169.0M (Dec-2024, −2%) → 173.5M (Dec-2025, +2.6%) → 176.1M (Mar-2026) → **179.1M (Jun-2026, +5.1% YoY)**, a 3.05M net add in Q2 alone. New registrations rose from 37.4M (FY2024) to 41.7M (FY2025), with quarterly units up 14% in Q1 2026 and 21% in Q2 2026 (12.7M vs 10.4M). The blended renewal rate improved from 72.2% (Q3-2024) to 75.4% (Q3-2025), and from 75.5% (Q1-2025) to 76.3% (Q1-2026).

Management's cohort disclosure is the most useful modeling input available: first-time renewal runs in the "mid-40% range" and has been stable for several quarters; previously-renewed names renew in the "mid-80% range." A wave of new registrations therefore mechanically dilutes the blended rate as it reaches first renewal, and the CFO guided explicitly to "a little bit of a tick down… in the remainder of this year and then possibly into next year." That is a known, disclosed headwind to 2027 volume, and it is why the Q2 2026 unit strength cannot be extrapolated linearly.

Three explanations compete for the unit surge, with different terminal values. Secular: AI content and site-creation tools lowering the friction of establishing a web presence, plus a credibility premium on owning an identity in an AI-mediated search environment — the Q2 MD&A newly cites "the evolution of AI tools used in content and website creation" as a positive driver, reversing the FY2025 10-K's treatment of AI as purely a threat. Cyclical: registrars rotating marketing spend "from ARPU back to customer acquisition," which reverses. Pull-forward: demand ahead of the November 1 increase, which Bidzos addressed qualitatively — "You can't say that that's not a factor… We just don't see it as anything coming close to a material factor" — without quantification. Management declined to decompose the drivers. The stable mid-40% first-time renewal rate is genuine evidence against a low-quality cohort, but it does not yet cover the large H1 2026 cohorts, which report first renewals in H1 2027.

**Price is the more visible 2027 driver.** The +6.9% .com increase on November 1 produces roughly $119M of gross annualized uplift on a 168M .com base assumption; management guided about 50% recognized in 2027, or approximately $60M — 3.4% of FY2026E revenue and about 5% of operating income at near-full incremental margin (the ICANN fee is fixed per registration). After tax and on a declining share count that is roughly $0.50 of 2027 EPS. Separately, .net permits 10% annual increases through June 2029 and has been unchanged at $10.91 since February 2024 — an unpulled lever with no explanation in the filings.

FY2026 guidance: revenue $1.745–1.755B, operating income $1.185–1.195B, interest and non-operating expense $59–65M, capex $55–65M, tax rate 22–25%. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year $9.56 (+1.1% over 90 days), next year $10.76 (+4.3% over 90 days, +3.9% over 30). The 30-day acceleration in the out-year estimate reflects .web delegation and the guidance raise. This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection; it is a stable compounder with a mild cycle upturn.

The earnings record tempers confidence in tight consensus: three misses and one beat over four quarters (−1.1%, −4.3%, +2.1%, −1.4%). Verisign guides narrowly and has been landing slightly under.

Growth prospects score 6.0 — mid-single-digit revenue, low-teens EPS with buyback assist, one dated pricing catalyst, one unquantified option.

## Competitive Landscape

Verisign competes with more than 1,000 TLDs, and Bidzos noted that "many of those are growing at rates much faster than dot com" while "compared to that market, com is a very low-priced TLD." Both statements build the public record required if Verisign ever petitions Commerce to remove .com pricing restrictions — an option it holds and has never exercised. They also concede that .com's advantage is share and durability, not growth rate.

The substitution set that matters is domain-versus-no-domain: social identities, marketplace storefronts, app-based access bypassing the address bar, third-level domains, and blockchain namespaces outside the authoritative root. Verisign's counter is governance — ICANN coordination produces globally unique, stable, secure identifiers that alternative namespaces lack.

.web changes the portfolio qualitatively. Governed by ICANN's Base Registry Agreement and outside the Cooperative Agreement, it carries complete wholesale pricing flexibility subject only to six-month notice, the right to sell premium names (prohibited in .com and .net), and unrestricted channel marketing. The launch sequence is 90 days of security testing, then a minimum 30-day trademark sunrise, then an optional Limited Registration Period offering .com holders matching .web names, with general availability "late this year or very, very early next year." Registry integration requires no incremental build, and 2026 marketing spend is characterized as immaterial. The $145.2M "deposits to acquire intangible assets" on the balance sheet reclassifies to an acquired intangible in Q3 2026; no impairment was ever taken across a decade of litigation. The base rate for new gTLD launches is poor, .web starts at zero registrations, ratable recognition defers any P&L effect, and the settlement terms with Altanovo are confidential.

Geographic mix is improving in quality — EMEA +10% and U.S. +6% in Q2 versus APAC +4%, with "Other" declining 6% — and management notes renewal quality is stronger in the U.S. and EMEA. Two disciplines apply: revenue is attributed to the registrar's country of domicile, which the 10-Q warns may differ from where registrants sit, and geographic mix says nothing about channel concentration. All registrations still flow through registrars, and no registrar-level concentration figures are disclosed. The planned new gTLD round creates a specific reflexivity: the registrar promotional focus currently driving 21% new-unit growth can rotate to new offerings and reduce Verisign's shelf prominence.

Competitive position scores 7.0. The level is exceptional; the trajectory is mixed — units and renewal rates improving, .web added, against unresolved AI direction, unchanged channel dependence, and a guided renewal tick-down.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-contract concentration.** Substantially all revenue derives from the .com Cooperative Agreement plus registry agreements for .com and .net. Presumptive renewal is real but conditional. One clause is worth noting: if certain terms diverge from those generally in effect at the five largest gTLDs, renewal must be on terms rendering them similar. Since .com's pricing restrictions are stricter than peers', that clause is more plausibly a latent benefit on pricing, but it cuts both ways on non-pricing terms.

**Pricing authority is political.** Commerce approval is required for changes to .com pricing, vertical-integration provisions, security specifications, renewal conditions, and Whois. The 10-K anticipates hostility, citing publicity campaigns, governmental scrutiny, and ICANN accountability challenges that "even when unmeritorious and/or unsuccessful, could have a material adverse effect." The Cooperative Agreement next auto-renews November 30, 2030 absent written notice 120 days prior — a dated binary well outside a twelve-month horizon.

**Renewal-rate dilution** is the near-term risk with the clearest mechanism: mid-40% first-time renewal on large 2026 cohorts against mid-80% on seasoned names, with management already guiding blended rates lower into 2027.

**Registration quality.** Registrants buying for resale are exposed to secondary-market pricing, and those monetizing via advertising are exposed to changes in how Google, Baidu, and Bing compensate placement — disclosed as having "adversely affected, and may continue to adversely affect" that segment of demand.

**China and APAC.** Demand declined substantially in 2023–2024 on regulatory mandates; MIIT licenses are required per TLD, and failure to renew could suspend registry services in China.

**Cyber and infrastructure, largely uninsured.** Verisign states it maintains no specific reserves for security breaches, cyber-attacks, or DDoS, that insurance "may be inadequate," and that it carries neither insurance nor reserves for data-center interruptions. Root Zone Maintainer errors could expose it to tort claims without assured indemnification. Data centers are concentrated in one geographic region. The 29-year uptime record is itself the franchise asset; a material failure would impair both renewal positioning and the reputational premium.

**Succession.** Bidzos, 70, holds Executive Chairman, President, and CEO simultaneously. The executive roster contains four people, three aged 62 or older, with no President, COO, or divisional head beneath him. The 10-K asserts strategic talent reviews and succession planning occur on a regular basis, including potential successors for both emergency and long-term succession, but names no one. Six separate Item 5.02 8-K filings between February and November 2025 is a dense cadence for a four-person executive suite.

**AI is genuinely two-sided.** The FY2025 risk factors treat it as a threat (alternative namespaces, changed navigation behavior, transformational applications "that cannot be anticipated fully," accelerated attacks, hardware cost inflation); the Q2 MD&A cites it as a demand positive. The net sign is the largest swing factor in any terminal-value assumption, and it is unresolved.

Risk factors score 6.0. Most exposures are long-dated, disclosed, and understood; the near-term renewal dilution and the demonstrated fragility of the multiple are the live items.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation is the strong side. Buybacks executed at $252.42 (FY2025) and $252.51 (H1 2026) averages, both below the current price; a 16.3% share-count reduction in three and a half years; maximum RSU dilution disclosed under 1.0%; a dividend initiated in 2025 and raised 5.2%; and a stated, verified policy of returning more than 100% of free cash flow. The June refinancing and July redemption were executed cleanly, and the .web litigation was resolved after a decade without ever impairing the $145.2M deposit.

Alignment is the weak side. Five-year total shareholder return indexed to 113 against a peer group at 258 — a 145-point gap — while CEO total compensation rose 53.5% from $10.12M (2021) to $15.54M (2025), with 2025 compensation actually paid of $24.21M. The disclosed performance measures are operating income, operating income CAGR, revenue, and relative TSR versus the S&P 500 rather than the technology peer group used in the TSR table. Three of four are absolute operating metrics that a sole-source registry with contractual escalators and 68% margins delivers with high reliability; operating income rose every year from 2021 to 2025 including the years the stock was flat. A defensible counterargument exists — for a price-capped franchise, operating metrics are what management controls, and the TSR gap partly reflects multiple compression from an elevated 2020 base — but the magnitude of the gap is not disputed.

Insider activity is one-directional. Bidzos sold on a weekly cadence from June 2 through July 28 in laddered tranches across $246–$302, reducing holdings from 441,439 to 415,939 shares (−5.8%, roughly $6.9M gross). The General Counsel sold in five tranches; the CFO sold 500 shares. The mechanical regularity, corroborated by the 10-Q Item 5 statement that no plans were adopted, terminated, or modified in the quarter, indicates pre-existing Rule 10b5-1 arrangements that predate the .web resolution. Routine plan sales are not a bearish signal. But no executive officer made an open-market purchase during a period that included a decade-defining catalyst and a year-to-date advance the stock had gained over 15%, and Director Armstrong's fractional 13.807 and 2.312 share acquisitions are consistent with dividend reinvestment rather than conviction buying.

Management quality scores 5.8 — competent operators and disciplined allocators, weighed against a compensation structure benchmarked away from shareholder outcomes and an unhedged, unnamed succession path.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $355 (+22.4%)

Unit growth proves secular rather than cyclical: AI tooling genuinely expands the addressable web-presence market, H1 2026 cohorts renew at or above the mid-40% first-time band when they cycle in H1 2027, and the domain base compounds 4–5%. The November .com increase adds roughly 5 points to 2027 operating income at near-full margin, followed by a second available increase in 2027 and a third in 2028 under the same six-year window. .web reaches general availability in early 2027 and, with unrestricted pricing and premium-name inventory, contributes a visible revenue line by 2028 at higher unit economics than .com. The unpulled .net lever (10% annual authority) provides additional latent pricing. Buybacks continue at 3% annually against under 1% dilution. The multiple holds at 30–33x on FY2027 EPS of $10.76–11.00, supported by a 4% FCF yield with escalating price authority. That is the analyst high of $355.

### Bear Case — $232 (−20.0%)

The blended renewal rate declines through 2026 and 2027 as mid-40% first-time cohorts dominate the mix, and unit growth decelerates toward 2% as registrars rotate marketing to new-round gTLDs. Part of the Q2 21% new-unit strength proves to have been pull-forward ahead of November 1, producing a visible Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 deceleration that management cannot cleanly attribute. .web launches into 1,000+ incumbent alternatives from zero registrations and contributes nothing material through 2028, as most new gTLDs have. FY2027 EPS lands near $10.30 rather than $10.76 — still growth, but below the estimate that supports the current multiple. Given three misses in the last four quarters and the February 2026 precedent of an intraday decline to $208.86 on a 4.3% miss, the multiple compresses toward 22.5x. AI hardware inflation keeps capex elevated and the 2031 maturity concentration adds $18M of annual interest at refinancing.

The bear case does not require the franchise to break. It requires only that the multiple normalize on a modest estimate shortfall, which has already happened once inside the last six months.

3. A material resolution failure or security incident breaking the uptime record, which would impair both renewal positioning and the premium the market pays for the franchise.
4. An unexplained CEO transition without a named internal successor, given the concentration of contract-negotiation relationships in one executive.

**Reassessment point: the Q3 2026 report in late October**, which will carry the first post-increase unit commentary, the .web intangible reclassification, and initial FY2027 framing.

Nothing in the current data reverses either. The .web resolution is a genuine positive that arrived in July, and the Q2 volume acceleration is real; both are already in the price, which has recovered from $261.58 pre-earnings to $290.02 and sits 7.2% below the May high of $312.48.

The investable question is narrow. Verisign will almost certainly earn between $10.30 and $11.00 in FY2027, and the components — roughly 5 points of price, 4 to 5 of volume, 3 of buyback — are individually verifiable. At 27x that number, the stock returns what the multiple allows. The multiple has proven unstable on modest misses, and management has missed three of the last four quarters while guiding the blended renewal rate lower into 2027. Rich near-term option premium with no scheduled company catalyst reinforces the case for patience rather than for adding.

Own it if you own it — the franchise, the 4.0% FCF yield, the 4.4% total return run-rate, and the November pricing lever justify holding through the cycle. New capital should wait for either $255 or confirmation that the 2026 registration cohorts renew in line with history.
