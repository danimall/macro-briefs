## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas producer operating exclusively in the Marcellus Shale with direct operating costs of $0.12/mcfe — among the lowest in the U.S. E&P sector. The company's financial trajectory has improved materially over the past 12 months: natural gas prices recovered 62% YoY (driven by LNG export demand), driving a 179% increase in net income through the first nine months of FY2025 and enabling $850M in combined debt reduction, share buybacks, and dividends. Total debt has declined from $1.71B to $1.23B, and $839M in buyback authorization remains outstanding (8.7% of market cap).

The stock trades at ~10.5x consensus FY2026 EPS ($4.02), a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 17.0x. Estimate revisions are strongly positive: current-quarter EPS estimates rose 35% over 90 days, and full-year estimates rose 14.5%. Three of the last four quarters beat consensus. The thesis depends on sustained natural gas prices above $3.00/mcf; at the company's all-in cost of ~$2.00/mcfe, margin elasticity to gas prices is high in both directions. Given the favorable estimate trajectory, improving balance sheet, large buyback capacity, and structural LNG demand tailwinds, the risk/reward at current levels warrants a buy.

The principal risk is concentration: 100% of production comes from a single basin, ~69% of revenue is natural gas, and the stock's 52-week range of $32.08–$48.31 reflects the commodity-driven volatility inherent in the business. A return to sub-$2.50/mcf gas prices would compress free cash flow toward zero and invalidate the thesis.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates as a single-segment independent E&P company focused on the Marcellus Shale in southwestern Pennsylvania. Production mix is approximately 69% natural gas, 30% NGLs, and 1% condensate/oil. The company produced 2.21 Bcfe/d in the first nine months of 2025, up 2% YoY — a maintenance-level capital program rather than a growth strategy. Proved reserves at year-end 2024 were approximately 64% natural gas and 35% NGLs.

The business model combines low direct operating costs with high midstream/transportation costs ($1.51/mcfe), reflecting Appalachia's geography and pipeline infrastructure requirements. Revenue is highly leveraged to Henry Hub natural gas prices, with realized prices discounted by Appalachian basis differentials averaging -$0.37/mcf in 9M 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Recovery.** FY2024 revenue was $2.35B, down from $5.34B at the FY2022 commodity peak. The trajectory reversed in FY2025: natural gas revenue surged 75% YoY in the first nine months ($1.25B vs. $715M) as realized gas prices rose 72% to $3.02/mcf. Total net income for 9M 2025 reached $479M vs. $171M in the prior-year period (+179%). Diluted EPS was $1.99 vs. $0.70.

**Cash Flow Generation.** Operating cash flow was $914M through 9M 2025 (+26% YoY) against $487M in capital expenditures, producing approximately $427M in free cash flow. Hedging settlements contributed $98M (down from $363M as rising prices reduced hedge value). The free cash flow margin is highly elastic — at $3.50/mcf gas, FCF is robust; at $2.50/mcf, it approaches zero.

**Balance Sheet Improvement.** Total debt declined to $1.23B from $1.71B at year-end 2024, primarily through the retirement of $608.7M in 4.875% senior notes. Remaining debt consists of $600M in 8.25% notes due 2029 and $500M in 4.75% notes due 2030. The credit facility was amended in October 2025, extending maturity to 2030 with $2.0B in commitments and $1.2B in available liquidity. The company maintains virtually zero cash, deploying all excess to debt reduction and buybacks.

**Cost Structure.** Direct operating costs remain exceptionally low at $0.12/mcfe. All-in cash costs (ex-DD&A) are approximately $1.97/mcfe. The dominant cost component is transportation/gathering/processing at $1.51/mcfe (44% of realized revenue before hedges). Interest expense declined 13% YoY as debt was reduced. An ongoing divestiture contract obligation from the 2020 North Louisiana sale adds approximately $90–95M in annual cash costs through 2030.

**Capital Returns.** The company repurchased $176.6M (4.9M shares) in 9M 2025 at prices between $34–37/share, with $839.5M in remaining buyback authorization. The quarterly dividend was increased 12.5% to $0.09/share ($0.36 annualized, ~0.9% yield). Combined debt reduction, buybacks, and dividends totaled ~$850M through three quarters.

## Growth Analysis

Range Resources is executing a maintenance capital program with ~2% production growth. The growth thesis is driven by price realization, not volume expansion. Three structural factors support higher natural gas prices:

1. **LNG export demand.** New U.S. LNG export facilities are absorbing incremental Appalachian gas supply. Management specifically attributes the gas price recovery to "increased exports from new LNG export facilities" and views this as structural.

2. **Power demand growth.** Data center and AI-related electricity demand is increasing natural gas consumption for power generation.

3. **Supply discipline.** Management notes "moderated reinvestment rates and inventory exhaustion" limiting supply response from competing basins.

Estimate revisions quantify the earnings momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates rose 35.1% over 90 days (from $0.94 to $1.27), and full-year FY2026 estimates rose 14.5% (from $3.51 to $4.02). FY2027 estimates stand at $4.53, implying 13% EPS growth. With 24–25 analysts covering the name, the revision breadth adds confidence to the direction.

The company's NGL exposure (~30% of production) provides some diversification but introduces oil price sensitivity. NGL revenue declined 2% in 9M 2025 despite volume growth, as NGL prices fell with oil.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.27, RRC trades at approximately 10.5x consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.02 and 9.3x FY2027 EPS of $4.53. This compares to a peer median P/E of 17.0x (Antero Resources 17.0x, Comstock Resources 17.4x). The wide dispersion among peers (SM Energy at 3.3x, Chord Energy at 125.5x) reflects varying production mixes and commodity exposures, but gas-weighted peers AR and CRK both trade at 17.0x+.

The discount to gas-weighted peers is partially explained by RRC's high midstream cost burden, which structurally compresses margins relative to producers with lower gathering costs. However, the magnitude of the discount (38% below peer median) appears excessive given: (1) the strongly rising estimate trajectory, (2) the buyback authorization that should mechanically support the stock, and (3) the 8.25% notes that are refinanceable at lower rates, potentially unlocking $20–25M in annual interest savings.

The analyst consensus median target is $46.00 (9% upside from current), with a range of $33.00–$59.00. Jefferies recently raised its target to $42 while maintaining a Hold rating. The consensus distribution skews heavily toward Hold (17 of 24 analysts), with 5 Buy-equivalent and 2 Strong Sell.

On a cash flow basis, annualizing the 9M 2025 FCF of $427M implies ~$570M full-year, or roughly a 6% FCF yield at current market cap ($9.7B). At peer-average multiples, the stock would trade in the $50–55 range.

## Competitive Landscape

Range operates in a competitive but geologically differentiated market. The Marcellus Shale is the largest natural gas producing basin in the U.S., and RRC is one of its largest pure-play producers alongside EQT Corporation and the combined Chesapeake/Southwestern entity.

**Strengths:** Direct operating costs of $0.12/mcfe are essentially best-in-class. The company's NGLs-rich acreage in southwestern Pennsylvania provides revenue diversification within the gas stream. Diversified sales outlets mitigate Appalachian basis risk.

**Weaknesses:** The midstream cost burden is structural and contractual, extending years into the future. This compresses realized margins and limits the company's competitive position relative to producers in basins with lower infrastructure costs. Single-basin concentration means no geographic diversification. The $299M divestiture obligation from the 2020 North Louisiana sale is an ongoing cash drain with no production benefit.

**Industry Dynamics:** The Iran conflict and associated Hormuz disruption have elevated global energy prices, though RRC's gas-weighted production mix limits direct benefit from oil price spikes. More relevant is the structural increase in LNG export demand pulling Appalachian gas into international markets at higher netback prices.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Natural Gas Price Reversal (HIGH).** At all-in costs of ~$2.00/mcfe, gas prices at $2.50/mcf produce thin margins and sub-$3.00 gas approaches cash flow breakeven. The company generated $1.18B in net income at FY2022 peak prices but only $266M in the trough FY2024. A return to $2.00/mcf gas (the 2024 average) would reduce annual FCF to approximately zero.

**2. Single-Basin Concentration (MODERATE).** Any regional regulatory action, pipeline constraint, or geological issue affects 100% of output. Management's decision to let some leases expire rather than drill introduces incremental impairment risk ($16.3M in 9M 2025).

**3. Appalachian Basis Widening (MODERATE).** The differential between Appalachian gas and Henry Hub was -$0.37/mcf in 9M 2025, widening from -$0.33 in the prior year. Basis hedges extend through 2029 but cover only a portion of production.

**4. Midstream Contractual Rigidity (MODERATE).** Transportation/gathering/processing costs are largely fixed contractual commitments. In a low-price environment, these costs cannot be reduced proportionally.

**5. Geopolitical/Trade Policy (LOW-MODERATE).** The 10-Q explicitly identifies tariff impacts on energy demand as a monitored risk. Current geopolitical tensions have supported energy prices broadly, but a sudden resolution could pressure gas prices. Conversely, escalation benefits RRC's revenue but may impair NGL realizations if oil-linked components decline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50–55, 18–30% upside).** Natural gas prices sustain above $3.50/mcf through 2026–2027, driven by LNG export demand growth and power sector consumption. EPS reaches $4.50+ in FY2027. The company executes on the remaining buyback authorization, reducing share count by 5–8%. The 8.25% notes due 2029 are refinanced at 5–6%, saving $15–20M annually. Re-rating toward peer median P/E of 17x on $4.50 EPS implies $50+. The accumulated deficit is eliminated, potentially enabling further dividend increases.

**Bear Case ($30–33, 22–29% downside).** Natural gas prices revert to $2.00–2.50/mcf as LNG demand disappoints or supply growth exceeds expectations. NGL prices decline with oil. FCF approaches zero, buyback pace slows materially. Appalachian basis differentials widen as new supply overwhelms pipeline capacity. The stock returns to the $32–33 trading range seen in August 2025 and January 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $48.** Derived from 12x FY2027 EPS of $4.02 = $48.24, approximately where the stock traded in late March 2026 when gas price optimism peaked. Alternatively, ~10.6x FY2027 EPS of $4.53 produces a similar level. This represents 14% upside from $42.27.

**Bull case target: $54.** 12x FY2027 EPS of $4.53 = $54.36 (28% upside). Requires sustained gas above $3.50 and buyback execution.

**Bear case target: $32.** ~8x reduced EPS estimate of ~$2.00 in a gas price reversal scenario (24% downside). Near the 52-week low of $32.08.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q1 FY2026 earnings (late April/May 2026), summer cooling demand for power generation (June–August), LNG export facility ramp updates, and any refinancing of the 8.25% notes.

**Upside/downside:** +14% to base case, +28% to bull case, -24% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio approximately 1.8:1 on base case, improving to 2.6:1 on upside scenario weighting.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Henry Hub natural gas settles below $2.50/mcf for any consecutive 30-day period.
2. FY2026 EPS estimate revisions turn negative (30-day revisions below zero), which would signal the positive momentum thesis is broken.
3. Management pauses the buyback program, indicating deteriorating internal confidence in the stock's value.

## Conclusion

Range Resources offers an attractive entry into the structural natural gas demand thesis at a valuation discount to gas-weighted peers. The 38% discount to peer median P/E is not fully explained by midstream cost structure differences, particularly given the strongly positive estimate revision trajectory. The aggressive capital return program provides a mechanical bid under the stock, and balance sheet improvement reduces financial risk. Rising estimates, discounted valuation, buyback support, and structural LNG demand tailwinds create favorable risk/reward, with the primary risk being a reversion in natural gas prices that would compress the narrow margin structure. At 10.5x forward earnings with 14.5% estimate growth over 90 days, the stock is buyable.
