## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab's FY2025 results mark a decisive recovery from the 2022-2023 cash sorting crisis. Revenue grew 22% to $23.9B, net income surged 49% to $8.9B, and diluted EPS jumped 56% to $4.65, driven primarily by a $44.8B (90%) paydown of expensive supplemental funding that compressed net interest margin during the crisis. Pre-tax profit margin expanded nearly 900bps to 47.9%. The remaining $5.1B in supplemental funding will roll off through 2026, providing a visible and largely mechanical earnings tailwind.

The company custodies $11.9T in client assets across 38.5M accounts, gathered $519B in core net new assets (+42% YoY) in 2025, and has completed the Ameritrade integration, realizing ~$2.5B in combined cost synergies. At ~$97, the stock trades at roughly 16.3x consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.95, a moderate premium to the peer median P/E of 17.4x but justified by Schwab's superior organic growth rate, expanding margins, and the mechanical nature of the remaining NIM expansion. Estimate revisions are positive across all periods (+5% current quarter over 90 days, +4.6% current year), confirming the earnings trajectory.

The primary risks are interest rate sensitivity (49% of revenue is net interest income), $11B in residual unrealized securities losses (AOCI), and elevated margin lending ($112B, +34% YoY) that creates exposure to equity market corrections. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (IV 36.7% vs. 23.1% HV, backwardated term structure), which warrants monitoring but appears related to macro volatility rather than company-specific stress.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is the largest publicly traded U.S. retail brokerage by client assets ($11.9T), operating as a savings and loan holding company through two segments: Investor Services (~79% of revenue, serving individual retail investors) and Advisor Services (~21%, serving ~7,000+ RIAs). Revenue is diversified across net interest income (49%), asset management fees (27%), trading revenue (17%), bank deposit account fees (4%), and other (3%).

The company completed the integration of TD Ameritrade's ~17M accounts and $1.9T in assets by year-end 2024, and TD Bank fully exited its 10.1% ownership stake in February 2025, eliminating a governance and liquidity overhang. Schwab is expanding its product suite with the Forge acquisition (~$660M, providing private market securities access) and Schwab Crypto (Bitcoin/Ethereum spot trading launching H1 2026).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | YoY Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|------------|
| Revenue | $18.8B | $19.6B | $23.9B | +22% |
| Net Income | $5.1B | $5.9B | $8.9B | +49% |
| Diluted EPS | $2.54 | $2.99 | $4.65 | +56% |
| Pre-tax Margin | 33.9% | 39.2% | 47.9% | +870bps |

The earnings acceleration is driven by two identifiable mechanisms:

1. **Supplemental funding elimination:** High-cost FHLB borrowings and brokered CDs declined from $49.9B to $5.1B during 2025, reducing interest expense by 41% ($6.4B → $3.75B). Each dollar of supplemental funding removed converts directly to pre-tax earnings because the replacement funding (organic client cash deposits returning to sweep) carries near-zero cost.

2. **Operating leverage on fixed-cost base:** Total expenses ex-interest grew just 5% against 22% revenue growth. The Ameritrade integration delivered ~$2.5B in run-rate synergies, and management guides 5.5-6.5% expense growth for 2026.

### Net Interest Revenue — The Primary Earnings Driver

Net interest revenue grew 28% to $11.75B. Net interest margin expanded from 2.12% to 2.74%. The remaining $5.1B in supplemental funding, expected to roll off through 2026, provides a visible NIM expansion path. Client cash trends have normalized: bank sweep deposits and payables to brokerage clients increased $36.6B (+12%) during 2025, reversing the cash sorting dynamic.

Margin lending reached $112.3B (+34% YoY), yielding approximately 6.44%. This is highly profitable but introduces cyclical risk (discussed below).

### Balance Sheet and Capital

Free cash flow was $8.76B. Tier 1 leverage ratio stood at 9.3%, well above the 4% regulatory minimum. Inclusive of AOCI (unrealized losses), the adjusted Tier 1 leverage ratio was 7.1%, above management's 6.75-7.0% target.

AOCI improved to -$11.0B from -$14.8B a year earlier and -$18.1B two years ago, driven by securities portfolio runoff (~$40B/year contraction pace) and lower market rates. The AFS+HTM portfolio has 3.9-year effective duration, meaning ongoing natural amortization.

Capital returns in 2025 totaled approximately $11.8B: $7.3B in buybacks, $2.5B in preferred redemption, and $2.0B in dividends. A new $20B buyback authorization was established in July 2025, with $14.5B remaining. A 19% dividend increase was declared in January 2026.

### Earnings Beat/Miss Record

Three beats in four quarters, with the Q4 2025 miss marginal (-2.3%, $1.39 actual vs. $1.42 estimate). The miss appears isolated rather than trend-breaking given positive estimate revisions across all forward periods.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab's competitive position is among the strongest in U.S. financial services. At $11.9T in client assets, only Fidelity (~$14T+, private) is larger in retail brokerage. The scale advantages are self-reinforcing: larger asset base → lower per-unit costs → more competitive pricing → more asset gathering.

Specific moats include:
- **Scale economics:** 38.5M accounts and $11.9T in assets provide unmatched operating leverage
- **Ameritrade integration complete:** $2.5B in synergies fully realized, no remaining execution risk
- **RIA custody:** ~7,000+ RIA relationships create a sticky institutional client base with high switching costs
- **Brand and trust:** Schwab Crypto launch leverages brand trust into new asset classes where trust is scarce
- **Regulatory moat:** Savings and loan holding company status provides access to low-cost deposit funding unavailable to pure brokerages

The SEC's withdrawal of the Order Competition Rule in June 2025, along with the DOL fiduciary rule withdrawal in November 2025, removed two significant regulatory threats to Schwab's order flow revenue ($1.93B in 2025).

## Risk Assessment

### Interest Rate Sensitivity
A 100bp rate decline would reduce NIM by approximately 4.4%. In the current macro environment (FOMC minutes showing hike openness, core PCE at 3.1% and rising), the near-term risk is more likely rate stability or increase, which would be neutral-to-positive for Schwab. A recession-driven cutting cycle remains the primary downside scenario.

### AOCI / Unrealized Losses
The $11.0B in unrealized losses has improved from the $18.1B peak but remains a meaningful drag on tangible book value. If Basel III endgame rules mandate AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital, it would constrain growth and capital returns. The 3.9-year portfolio duration means natural amortization, but the timeline is years, not quarters.

### Margin Lending Concentration
A sharp equity market drawdown would trigger margin calls, potential defaults, and rapid deleveraging. The 10-K specifically flags increased market liquidity risk.

### Macro Environment
The stagflationary macro regime documented in the world model creates a complex backdrop: higher rates support NIM but hurt client portfolios and reduce risk appetite. Consumer contraction could reduce new account formation. Credit cascade risk (55-65% probability per world model) would be negative for financial stocks broadly, though Schwab's deposit-funded model is more insulated than leveraged alternatives managers.

### Options Market Signal
ATM IV of 36.7% versus 23.1% historical volatility represents a 13.6pp richness. The term structure is backwardated (near-term 36.7% > 6-month 26.1%), consistent with the market pricing an imminent stress event or macro catalyst rather than structural company-specific risk. Put/call OI ratio of 1.07 shows slight put dominance.

Unusual activity centers on upside calls: $99 and $102 strike calls for April 17 with volume/OI ratios of 18.9x and 5.3x respectively. This suggests some participants are positioning for near-term upside, directionally consistent with the fundamental thesis.

The elevated IV means protective puts are expensive, but the backwardation pattern typically reflects a catalyst window that resolves within weeks rather than a structural concern.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- **Mechanical earnings growth from supplemental funding elimination:** The remaining $5.1B rolls off through 2026, expanding NIM with near-certainty. This requires no new business wins, no market appreciation, and no management creativity.
- **Operating leverage:** 5.5-6.5% expense growth against 15-20%+ revenue growth creates margin expansion through FY2027.
- **Organic asset gathering at 5.1%** on a $11.9T base compounds all revenue lines higher.
- **Product expansion optionality:** Schwab Crypto and Forge are not in estimates and address growing client demand categories.
- **Capital return:** $14.5B remaining buyback authorization + 19% dividend increase provide shareholder returns during the earnings ramp.
- **Consensus target $118 implies 22% upside** from current levels.

### Bear Case
- **Rate sensitivity:** A 200bp+ cutting cycle would compress NIM meaningfully, particularly as the supplemental funding tailwind has already largely played through.
- **Equity market correction:** Margin lending at $112B creates reflexive risk — market decline → margin calls → forced selling → deeper decline. Client asset values would also decline, compressing fee revenue.
- **Valuation premium vs. peers:** At 16.3x forward, the premium could compress if growth decelerates faster than expected.
- **AOCI regulatory risk:** Basel III endgame rules mandating AOCI inclusion would constrain capital returns.
- **Crypto execution risk:** New product category with operational, regulatory, and reputational risks.

## Conclusion

Schwab is a high-quality franchise in the middle of a multi-year earnings recovery driven by identifiable, mechanical tailwinds. The supplemental funding paydown, NIM expansion, and operating leverage on the integrated Ameritrade platform provide earnings visibility through FY2027. At 16.3x FY2026 consensus with positive estimate revisions across all periods, the valuation is reasonable for a company delivering 28% EPS growth with structural competitive advantages.

The risks — rate sensitivity, margin lending concentration, and AOCI legacy — are well-understood and manageable at current levels. The macro regime (rates likely stable-to-higher) is supportive of the NIM thesis in the near term.

With 19 of 22 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy, consensus target of $118 (22% upside), and accelerating estimate revisions, the fundamental setup is favorable. The elevated near-term options IV reflects macro uncertainty rather than company-specific stress and should normalize as the catalyst window passes.

**Recommendation: BUY** at current levels (~$97), with the expectation of earnings-driven appreciation toward the $110-120 range over 12 months as the supplemental funding elimination completes and NIM expansion flows through to reported results.
