**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Fuel Gas is a vertically integrated Appalachian gas company spanning upstream E&P (Seneca Resources), FERC-regulated interstate pipelines and storage, and a regulated LDC serving western New York and northwestern Pennsylvania, despite its defensive gas utility sector label. The distinction matters because roughly 64% of first-half FY2026 net income ($276.1M of $429.3M) came from the commodity-exposed Integrated Upstream and Gathering segment. NFG's 64% YoY earnings jump is primarily a natural-gas-price story (realized after-hedge price of $3.16/Mcf vs. $2.74) layered on a prior-year comparison flattered by a $103.6M after-tax ceiling-test impairment, rather than a rate-base compounding story. Investors treating this as a utility-like income play are mismeasuring the cyclicality.

The forward thesis now pivots on the $2.62B CenterPoint Ohio (Vectren) acquisition announced October 20, 2025. The deal roughly doubles NFG's regulated gas utility rate base, tilts the earnings mix toward stable regulated income, and diversifies regulatory exposure away from hostile New York decarbonization policy into a pro-gas Ohio jurisdiction. That strategic logic is sound. The execution is unfinished: only ~$338M of the ~$2.62B purchase price is permanently financed, the structure includes a $1.2B seller note at 6.5%, PUCO approval is not secured (expected Q4 calendar 2026), and management has suspended its buyback to preserve balance-sheet capacity. Consensus estimates have drifted down 5-6% over 90 days across current and next fiscal year, and the stock trades ~15% below its 52-week high at $82.49.

This is a balanced setup. The ~2.7% dividend yield (a "Dividend King" with comfortable coverage), the current ceiling-test cushion, and a moderate 0.37 debt-to-cap ratio support a floor. The deal-financing overhang, negative revision momentum, and the commodity leverage embedded in the largest segment cap the upside until permanent financing structure and regulatory clearance resolve. We rate NFG HOLD, downgrading in line with the declining score trajectory; the deterioration is in estimate revisions and unresolved deal risk rather than in the price weakness itself.

## Company Overview

National Fuel Gas operates across the full natural gas value chain in the Appalachian Basin through three reportable segments, all sharing a common western NY/Pennsylvania footprint that allows coordinated management and infrastructure:

1. **Integrated Upstream and Gathering** (Seneca Resources + Midstream) — Marcellus/Utica shale gas production plus the gathering systems (Trout Run, Tioga) that feed it. This is the unregulated, commodity-price-exposed core, and NFG merged its former separate E&P and Gathering segments here in Q4 FY2025. It contributed $276.1M of 1H FY2026 net income.
2. **Pipeline and Storage** (Supply Corporation + Empire Pipeline) — FERC-regulated interstate transportation and storage on a straight-fixed-variable rate design; ~$62.8M 1H FY2026 net income.
3. **Utility** (Distribution Corporation) — NYPSC- and PaPUC-regulated LDC; ~$99.4M 1H FY2026 net income.

The integrated model is the differentiator: Seneca produces gas, Supply Corp and Empire transport it (including affiliate precedent agreements such as 190,000 Dth/day for the Tioga Pathway Project), and Distribution Corp delivers it. The regulated pipeline/storage and utility segments provide rate-base-driven stability that partially offsets upstream volatility. The market-cap-proximity peer set (UGI, SWX, NJR, OGS, SR) captures the LDC comparison but not the upstream commodity exposure, which is the reason NFG's earnings profile diverges from pure-play gas utilities.

## Financial Analysis

The research brief correctly flags a data-extraction artifact: screening tools reported implausible leverage (~$6.2M long-term debt, D/E 0.06, Altman Z 11.75). The actual balance sheet at 3/31/2026 shows ~$2.38B long-term debt (net of current portion) plus a $300M current portion — roughly $2.68B total. All figures below use primary-source 10-Q/XBRL data.

**Revenue and earnings (1H FY2026):**
- Revenue: $1,509.9M, +18% YoY from $1,279.4M.
- Net income: $429.3M vs. $261.3M, +64%. Diluted EPS $4.58 vs. $2.86.
- The prior-year comparison is flattered by a $141.8M pre-tax ($103.6M after-tax) non-cash impairment in Q1 FY2025. Roughly $64M of the $168M YoY improvement is the impairment non-repeat. Underlying growth is real but less dramatic than the headline.

The earnings mechanism is straightforward: higher realized gas prices ($3.16/Mcf after hedging vs. $2.74; $3.33 pre-hedge vs. $2.64) on roughly flat-to-up production (211.2 Bcf, +8 Bcf). The 12-month average Henry Hub used in the ceiling test was $3.72/MMBtu.

**Cash flow and balance sheet (3/31/2026):**
- Operating cash flow 1H FY2026: $657.3M, +39% YoY.
- Capex 1H FY2026: $498.3M, heavily upstream-weighted ($194.6M Utica, $68.4M Marcellus; $188.9M developing PUD reserves).
- Total assets $9.13B; total debt ~$2.68B; shareholders' equity $3.82B.
- Credit-agreement debt-to-cap: 0.37 vs. a 0.65 covenant limit — headroom of ~$5.26B before breach. Embedded long-term debt cost 4.81%.
- Dividend: $0.535/quarter ($2.14 annualized), raised from $0.515, comfortably covered.

Pre-deal, this is a healthy balance sheet with strong cash generation. The important caveat: the CenterPoint deal will add meaningful financing (potentially $2.28B+ before equity offsets), so the current 0.37 debt-to-cap does not represent the post-close structure. Earnings quality is decent — the FCF picture is complicated by heavy growth capex, but operating cash conversion is strong and the dividend is not stretched.

**Earnings surprise track record** supports moderate confidence in near-term estimates: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, though the most recent (Q2 FY2026, March) was a 4.0% miss ($2.71 vs. $2.82 estimate), the first negative surprise in the sequence. This aligns with the softening estimate revisions.

## Growth Analysis

Growth breaks into two components with different durability.

The near-term earnings surge is a gas-price cyclical, not a structural compounding. This matters for scoring because commodity-driven earnings mean-revert. The favorable Henry Hub environment is supporting both upstream earnings and the ceiling-test cushion, but a price reversal cuts the largest segment directly.

The structural growth story is the CenterPoint Ohio acquisition and the pipeline growth pipeline. CenterPoint roughly doubles the regulated utility rate base, a genuine step-change in the stable earnings base if it closes and integrates cleanly. The regulated growth projects are concrete and filing-grounded:
- **Tioga Pathway** (~$101M, 190,000 Dth/day), construction started Feb 2026, in-service late 2026.
- **Shippingport Lateral** (~$57M, 205,000 Dth/day), serving a gas plant that feeds a co-located data center in Beaver County, PA; construction started March 2026. This is NFG's tangible link to the AI/data-center power-demand theme.
- **Line N System Upgrade** (~$93M, 94,000 Dth/day), precedent agreement April 2026, in-service late 2028.

Rate cases in flight add incremental regulated revenue: a NY 3-year plan (9.7% ROE, +$15.8M revenue requirement FY2026), a PA case (+$19.7M, suspended to Oct 2026), and a Supply Corp FERC Section 4 case filed April 30, 2026 seeking ~$404M cost of service (+$95M).

The problem is that estimate revisions are moving the wrong way. Current-year EPS consensus fell from $7.78 (90d ago) to $7.37, and next-year from $8.25 to $7.78. The current-quarter estimate dropped 12.2% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum — the opposite of the +30% inflection signal that would justify a growth-oriented upgrade. The falling revisions reflect gas-price normalization expectations and deal-related dilution. Given the commodity-cyclical nature of the largest segment and the downward revision trend, growth prospects score below the sector-average level despite the strong headline growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.49, NFG trades at ~11.2x trailing EPS ($5.68) and ~11.2x consensus current-year EPS. Against the Gas Utilities peer median P/E of 16.1x, NFG looks cheap. The discount is largely justified, and cheapness here should be read as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent a specific catalyst.

The reasons the discount is warranted:
- ~64% of earnings come from a commodity-exposed segment that the market correctly assigns a lower multiple than regulated LDC earnings. UGI (P/E 12.0), the closest structural comparable given its own non-regulated exposure, trades near NFG. Pure regulated LDCs (SWX 26.7x, OGS 17.0x, NJR 16.1x) command higher multiples precisely because their earnings are more stable.
- Estimate revisions are falling, which typically precedes multiple compression, not expansion.
- The CenterPoint deal financing is unresolved, injecting uncertainty into the forward earnings base.

The bull-case valuation argument rests on the deal closing and the earnings mix shifting toward regulated income, which would justify a higher multiple over time. That re-rating is contingent on execution that hasn't happened yet. Analyst targets (low $87, mean/median $98.5, high $110) imply upside, but on a stock where estimates are being revised down, those targets are stale and likely to follow estimates lower before they lead.

The dividend yield provides some absolute support, but it sits below the peer median yield of 3.5%, so income buyers can find better yield elsewhere in the group. On a cycle-adjusted basis, if gas prices normalize below the current strip, the upstream segment's contribution compresses and the trailing multiple on normalized earnings looks less cheap than the headline suggests. We score valuation as neutral — cheap on the surface, but appropriately so, with no independent catalyst to argue the market is mispricing it today.

## Competitive Landscape

NFG competes across three arenas. In upstream it competes with larger Appalachian producers (EQT, Range, Antero, Coterra) but is smaller and integrated, which limits scale advantages. In pipelines it interconnects with Transco and Tennessee Gas Pipeline and holds regulated franchise positions. In the utility segment it is a regional monopoly LDC.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the integrated model creates captive throughput (affiliate precedent agreements), and the Shippingport Lateral gives NFG under-construction exposure to the data-center/gas-power demand theme that a plain LDC screen misses. The CenterPoint pivot diversifies regulatory exposure into a jurisdiction management describes as a "constructive regulatory and political environment supportive of natural gas."

On the negative side, New York's CLCPA (40% GHG reduction by 2030, 85% by 2050), the building-electrification ban (effective 12/31/2025, currently in litigation/suspended pending appeal), and the December 2025 rule requiring new residential gas applicants to pay the first 100 feet of service installation cost (starting 12/19/2026) directly threaten NY utility customer growth and terminal value. This is a structural headwind on a meaningful portion of the regulated base. The Ohio expansion is the strategic response, but until it closes, NFG's regulated growth is exposed to the most aggressive decarbonization regime in the country. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the deal logic but with an unresolved regulatory drag, warranting a middling score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully priced, which is the primary reason the overall score sits below the sector-neutral line.

1. **CenterPoint Ohio execution and financing (dominant near-term risk).** The structure relies on the $1.2B seller note and a term-loan/bridge facility (SOFR + 1.125%-1.75%). A rating downgrade or adverse market conditions before permanent financing is executed raises deal cost. NFG added this as a specific forward-looking risk factor. This is a binary regulatory event with financing tail risk.

2. **Full-cost ceiling-test impairment risk (structural).** NFG has taken $797M cumulative after-tax ceiling-test impairments since July 2018. At 3/31/2026 the ceiling exceeded book value by ~$1.6B, but a $0.25/MMBtu gas-price decline erodes that cushion materially. This is a recurring downside not present in pure LDCs.

3. **Commodity-price exposure.** The largest earnings segment is fully exposed to gas prices. Hedging (374.1 Bcf of swaps/collars, up to ~5 years) dampens but does not eliminate the volatility, and the current earnings surge is largely a price story that mean-reverts.

4. **New York decarbonization/terminal-value risk.** Detailed above — a structural jurisdictional headwind on regulated utility growth.

5. **Interest-rate/financing-cost risk.** The 6.5% seller note and SOFR-linked facilities mean rising rates raise the cost of the permanent financing not yet executed.

6. **Governance ambiguity.** A December 10, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K (officer/director change) and an apparently recent CFO transition (T.J. Silverstein now signs the 10-Q) are unconfirmed as to whether they reflect a planned transition. The December 17, 2025 private placement of 4,402,513 shares at $79.50 ($338.4M net) increased share count ~5% and was modestly dilutive at a slight discount to the current price.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 29.2% vs. 1-year historical volatility of 19.7% — rich by ~9.5 points, indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (consistent with the July 29 earnings date and unresolved deal financing). The term structure is in backwardation (29.2% near-term vs. 24.9% at six months), which signals the market expects imminent event stress and then normalization. Put/call skew is balanced (0.2% near-term), so there is no strong directional bearish tilt in the options. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±7.1%. Rich near-term IV argues against paying up for the stock ahead of the print; a post-earnings IV crush could offer a better entry. Short interest is moderate at 6.2% of float (7.0 days to cover) — no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Gas prices hold near the current strip, sustaining upstream earnings and the ceiling-test cushion. The CenterPoint Ohio deal closes cleanly in Q4 calendar 2026 with permanent financing skewed toward equity (management has said it may reduce the term loan "possibly to zero"), roughly doubling the regulated rate base and shifting the earnings mix toward stable, higher-multiple income. Rate cases (NY, PA, FERC) add incremental regulated revenue, and the Shippingport data-center lateral plus the broader gas-power demand theme provide organic pipeline growth. The market re-rates NFG closer to the regulated peer median as the earnings mix improves. Combined with the dividend, this supports a move toward the analyst high of $110 (~33% upside).

### Bear Case
Gas prices normalize below the current strip, cutting the upstream segment and narrowing the ceiling-test cushion toward another non-cash impairment. The CenterPoint deal financing lands debt-heavy (leaving the seller note in place longer or drawing the term loan), pressuring credit ratings and raising interest expense while NY decarbonization rules continue to erode the utility's terminal value. Estimate revisions continue lower, and the multiple compresses toward UGI's ~12x on falling normalized earnings. Downside to the low-to-mid $60s (~20-25% below current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is balanced, not asymmetric. The commodity leverage, falling estimate revisions, and unresolved deal financing offset the strategic logic and the yield floor. This is neither a confident income hold (yield below peer median) nor a growth hold (revisions negative); the honest classification is uncertain, pending resolution of the deal and the gas-price environment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CenterPoint permanent financing executed with an equity-heavy split (term loan materially reduced), removing the financing overhang, alongside constructive PUCO progress.
- Estimate-revision inflection: current-year consensus stops falling and turns positive over two consecutive readings.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (near the 52-week low of $75.17) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- PUCO rejection or a materially adverse condition on the CenterPoint deal, or a credit-rating downgrade tied to deal financing.
- A sustained gas-price decline (Henry Hub below ~$2.75) that reopens ceiling-test impairment risk and cuts the upstream earnings base.
- A NY regulatory ruling that accelerates the electrification/terminal-value erosion beyond current expectations.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release on July 29, 2026, followed by the CenterPoint PUCO review progress and permanent financing announcements through Q4 calendar 2026 (expected close). The deal resolution is the single most important catalyst; the thesis should be revisited concretely at close.

## Conclusion

National Fuel Gas is a cyclically-exposed integrated gas company wearing a utility label, whose recent earnings strength is primarily a gas-price story and whose forward trajectory hinges on the CenterPoint Ohio acquisition. The strategic case for the deal is strong (it doubles the regulated rate base and diversifies away from hostile New York regulation), but the execution risk is live, only ~13% of the purchase price is permanently financed, and the structure carries the seller note and PUCO approval risk. Consensus estimates are falling, the most recent quarter missed for the first time in the sequence, and the ~11x trailing multiple is cheap for reasons the market is pricing correctly rather than a mispricing to exploit.

The dividend with comfortable coverage, moderate leverage, and current ceiling-test cushion establish a reasonable floor. The balanced risk/reward, negative revision momentum, and unresolved deal overhang do not support a BUY at $82.49. This continues the declining score trajectory (7.0 → 6.7 → 6.0 → 5.5) and confirms the prior downgrade to HOLD rather than reversing it. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s, an equity-weighted deal financing that removes the overhang, or an estimate-revision inflection; on current data, HOLD is the appropriate call.
