**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BrightSpring Health Services is a diversified home- and community-based healthcare platform that has undergone two simultaneous transformations over the past year: a profitability turnaround and a governance cleanup. Net income from continuing operations rose from $20.5M in 2024 to $189.1M in 2025, and reached $74.3M in Q1 2026 alone (versus $9.2M a year earlier). Revenue grew 28% to $12.9B in 2025 and 25.6% to $3.61B in Q1 2026, driven overwhelmingly by the Infusion & Specialty Pharmacy business, which grew 35.5% in the quarter. Net leverage fell from 4.16x to 2.27x, aided by the $835M sale of Community Living. The stock has re-rated accordingly, rising roughly 250% over twelve months from a $19.01 low to ~$71.

The central analytical tension is that this exceptional top-line and EBITDA growth sits on razor-thin net margins (roughly 0.8-1.5%) in a business fighting a large, quantified drug-pricing undertow. Management has sized the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) impact at ~$200M in specialty/infusion (>$400M gross including brand-to-generic conversion) plus ~$175M in home & community pharmacy. That BrightSpring can still guide to 23-28% adjusted EBITDA growth against that headwind is impressive, but it also means the durability of the earnings turnaround depends entirely on reimbursement stability and continued limited-distribution drug (LDD) launches out-running the pricing drag.

We rate BTSG a HOLD (growth_hold). The growth thesis is real and estimate revisions are rising (+9.2% on current-year EPS over 90 days), which supports continued momentum. But the valuation is stretched on any near-term earnings measure, the informed seller (KKR) exited entirely at $40-58 — well below today's $71 — and the options market is pricing an imminent ±14% move. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the improving fundamentals justify maintaining the call, but they do not yet justify upgrading into a valuation that has run ahead of the sponsor's own exit price.

## Company Overview

BrightSpring serves high-need, medically complex, and aging populations across all 50 states, touching approximately 475,000 patients daily through more than 12,400 clinicians. The business runs on two segments:

**Pharmacy Solutions (~88% of revenue)** is the growth engine and splits into two very different sub-businesses:
- *Infusion & Specialty Pharmacy* is the dominant driver, growing ~43% in 2025 and +35.5% in Q1 2026 to $2.64B. It is anchored by a portfolio of 149 limited-distribution drugs (24 launched in 2025), a genuine competitive advantage: manufacturers grant exclusive or preferred dispensing access, and BrightSpring earns manufacturer service fees plus dispensing margin.
- *Home & Community Pharmacy (PharMerica)* serves long-term care, assisted living, and behavioral pharmacy. It is currently declining (-9.2% in Q1 2026) due to IRA pricing, a large bankrupt customer unwind, and the exit of uneconomic contracts.

**Provider Services (~12% of revenue)** includes Home Health, Hospice, Rehab, and Personal Care. It carries higher segment EBITDA margins (~16%) and is steadier. Home Health census surged +52% YoY to ~46,000 following the acquisition of ~110 branches from Amedisys/LHC (a UnitedHealth entity).

The revenue engine is a price/mix story rather than a volume story. Revenue per script rose ~27% to ~$296 on specialty mix shift, even as script counts fell 1.4%. This matters for the risk profile: growth is being manufactured through higher-value drugs, not more patients, which makes the business more sensitive to the specialty reimbursement environment specifically.

## Financial Analysis

The defining financial feature is the combination of large scale, high growth, and very thin margins.

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$10.1B | $12.9B (+28%) | $3.61B (+25.6%) |
| Adj. EBITDA | ~$461M | $618M (+34%) | $189.8M (+44.8%) |
| Net income (cont. ops) | $20.5M | $189.1M | $74.3M (vs $9.2M) |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | ~4.6% | 4.8% | ~5.3% |
| Operating cash flow | — | $490M | strong |

Gross margin runs ~13% and net margin ~0.8-1.5%. On this base, every reimbursement, mix, or cost shift moves the bottom line materially: a 100-200bp change in gross margin is the difference between a good year and a bad one. This is the structural reality that both the bull and bear cases must reconcile.

The balance sheet has been materially de-risked. Net leverage fell to 2.99x at year-end 2025 and to 2.27x at Q1 2026, aided by $889M of cash from the Community Living sale. The Altman Z-Score of ~3.02 sits in the safe zone. Debt is ~$2.56B of term loan (SOFR+2.50%, effectively ~6.2%, maturing February 2031), of which $1.5B is swapped to fixed. The remaining ~$1.1B is variable, so a 1% rate rise costs roughly $10.1M of net income — meaningful against a ~$189M net income base but not existential, and ~$715M of divestiture proceeds is earmarked for further debt paydown. Given the distribution business model, this leverage level is appropriate and now sits below the typical range for a recently PE-owned healthcare services roll-up.

Capital allocation has been disciplined: no dividend, $60M of buyback in Q1 2026 (~1.46M shares repurchased at $40.96 alongside the KKR secondary, an efficient use of cash near the sponsor-exit price), and bolt-on M&A as the primary growth lever. One dilution item to watch: Tangible Equity Units mandatorily convert into ~26-31M shares around February 1, 2027, roughly 13-16% dilution on the ~193M share base.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in the near-term estimate path: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with a 23.3% beat in Q1 2026 and a 41.6% beat in Q3 2025. The single miss (Q4 2025, -7.6%) is a reminder that on thin margins the quarters can be lumpy.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance calls for revenue of $14.45-15.0B (+12-16%) and adjusted EBITDA of $760-790M (+23-28%). EBITDA growing faster than revenue implies continued margin expansion and operating leverage, a favorable signal, though management itself described the guidance as "somewhat fluid" with "multiple moving parts."

Estimate revisions are rising across every tenor: current-year EPS is up 9.2% over 90 days to $1.70, next-year up 9.2% to $2.14, with 30-day revisions modestly positive (+1.2%). This is a positive earnings-momentum signal, and estimate revisions are among the more reliable forward indicators. The +9.2% pace, while healthy, sits below the +30% threshold that flags a growth acceleration under-appreciated by consensus. The market has largely caught up to this story: all 19 analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy, and the mean target ($76) is only ~7% above the current price.

The durability question is the specialty pharmacy engine. Growth here is a price/mix story running against the quantified IRA drag, and it depends on the pace of new LDD launches (24 in 2025) continuing to more than offset both negotiated Medicare Part D price cuts (first 10 prices took effect January 1, 2026) and brand-to-generic conversion. The Home Health census surge from the Amedisys/LHC branches adds volume, but those branches currently run high-single-digit margins and are dilutive near-term, with accretion timing described as "uncertain."

## Valuation Assessment

BTSG trades at ~89x trailing EPS ($0.48) and ~42x current-year consensus EPS ($1.70), falling to ~33x next-year ($2.14). Against a Health Care Services peer set (DVA 19.6x, CHE 23.0x, LH 23.5x, DGX 22.5x; median ~23x), BTSG carries a large premium. Some premium is warranted — those peers grow low-single-digits while BTSG is compounding EBITDA in the mid-20s — but the gap is wide even on a growth-adjusted basis. On next-year EPS the PEG is roughly 1.4-1.5x (33x forward against ~23% EBITDA growth), which is at the high end of reasonable.

The cycle-adjusted valuation guidance argues for looking through trough trailing earnings, and we do: this is a ~33-42x forward business with a genuine profitability turnaround behind it, not an 89x business. That reframing is why we do not score valuation below 4. But the forward multiple is still full, not cheap, and the stock is not at a trough; it is at essentially a 52-week high after a 250% run.

Three factors keep us cautious rather than constructive on price:

1. **The informed seller exited well below current levels.** KKR sold ~19.7M shares at $40.96 in March and ~14.7M at $58.45 in June, and its Form 4 entities now report zero. The most informed holder of this business, with full access to the guidance and the IRA modeling, chose to exit entirely at prices 17-42% below today's $71. That does not prove the stock is overvalued, but it removes any argument that insiders see deep value at current levels.

2. **Analyst targets do not function as a floor here.** The stock trades at ~$71 against a mean target of $76 and a high of $90. The guidance for treating targets as floors applies to cheap, inflecting stocks (<30x forward, PEG <1.5). BTSG is at the boundary (33x forward, PEG ~1.4), so the targets are better read as fairly-valued markers than as stale lowballs. The low target of $49 (31% below spot) reflects genuine downside if the IRA transition disappoints.

3. **The options market is pricing near-term stress.** Near-term ATM IV of 57.1% sits well above 1-year historical vol of 39.5% and in backwardation (near-term > long-term), with an implied ±14.4% move on the nearest expiry. The market is pricing a binary event, most likely the next earnings print.

On balance, valuation offers no asymmetric upside at $71; the reward to the mean target (~7%) is roughly matched by the risk to the low target (-31%).

## Competitive Landscape

BrightSpring operates in large, fragmented, secularly-growing markets: aging demographics, care shifting to lower-cost home and community settings, and specialty-drug proliferation. Its competitive advantages are real: the 149-product LDD specialty portfolio (manufacturer-granted preferred access), national scale and payor relationships, and an integrated pharmacy-plus-provider model.

The structural constraint is that BrightSpring competes against vertically integrated behemoths — UnitedHealth/Optum, CVS Health, and Cigna/Express Scripts — that own PBMs, payors, and pharmacies and can command superior drug-purchasing economics. This is a durable margin ceiling that the thin net margin already reflects. In Provider Services, low barriers to entry in non-CON states and MCO consolidation pressure pricing. The value-based-care shift (home-health value-based purchasing now in all 50 states, Medicare Advantage penetration above 50%) favors low-cost home providers but concentrates negotiating power in fewer, larger payors.

Trajectory rather than level is what matters for scoring. The competitive position is improving at the margin — the LDD portfolio is expanding, the Community Living divestiture sharpened the payor mix, and the Amedisys branch acquisition grew Home Health census — but the specialty engine's dependence on manufacturer relationships and the vertically-integrated competitive set cap the upside. We score competitive position at 6.3: improving, but not a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

Several BTSG-specific risks are material and not fully de-risked:

1. **IRA / drug-pricing headwinds are the primary issue.** The first 10 negotiated Medicare Part D prices took effect January 1, 2026, a live, ongoing drag that the LDD launch cadence must continually out-run. If launches slow or negotiated pricing broadens faster than expected, the thin-margin math turns unfavorable quickly.

2. **PBM and reimbursement dependency.** Contracts are often terminable on short notice, and reimbursement is set by AWP/WAC benchmarks outside BTSG's control. Federal PBM reform became law in February 2026 with uncertain impact. Roughly half of revenue is government-payor, exposed to rate cuts and the 2% sequestration running through 2030.

3. **Active DOJ/DEA investigation of Embrace Hospice** — potential False Claims Act and Controlled Substances Act exposure over hospice services alleged to be "not reasonable and medically necessary," plus ongoing qui tam actions. Hospice medical-necessity is a known enforcement hotspot that has produced large industry settlements. This is a genuine binary overhang that could result in a settlement or corporate integrity agreement.

4. **TEU dilution** of ~26-31M shares in February 2027, roughly 13-16% of the current base.

5. **Acquisition integration risk** on the low-margin, near-term-dilutive Amedisys branches, with uncertain accretion timing, plus the customer-bankruptcy unwind pressuring home & community scripts through ~Q3 2026.

On the positive side, the KKR exit resolves the long-standing controlled-company overhang, and the deleveraging removes a solvency concern that was live a year ago. Net-net, the idiosyncratic risk set is meaningful and includes at least two live binary items (the Embrace investigation, the IRA transition trajectory) that are only partially priced. We score risk at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term caution consistent with the pending-earnings binary. Near-term ATM IV of 57.1% is rich versus 1-year HV of 39.5% (a 17.6pp spread) and sits at the 78th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (57.1% near-term vs 49.5% at six months), which the market uses to price imminent event risk. IV has risen 7.9pp since the prior snapshot as the stock climbed 13%, so the market is pricing more uncertainty even as price advanced. The put/call skew is negative (-5.1%, call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.48, so despite the 10.1% short interest, positioning is not aggressively bearish — the elevated vol is event-driven rather than directional. The implied ±14.4% move on the nearest expiry is a strong argument for waiting for the next print before adding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90)
The specialty/infusion engine continues to out-launch the IRA drag, EBITDA lands at or above the top of the $760-790M guide (+28%), and margin expansion proves the operating-leverage story durable. Deleveraging continues past 2.27x, bolt-on M&A compounds, and the Home Health branches inflect to margin accretion in 2027. Consensus revises next-year EPS toward $2.30+, and the stock holds a ~40x forward multiple on rising estimates, reaching the analyst high of ~$90 (+27%). Resolution of the KKR overhang broadens the shareholder base and supports a higher stable multiple.

### Bear Case ($49)
The IRA transition proves worse than modeled — negotiated Part D pricing broadens, LDD launches slow, or generic conversion accelerates — and the thin-margin math compresses EBITDA growth into the mid-teens or below. The Embrace Hospice investigation produces a material settlement or corporate integrity agreement. The TEU conversion dilutes 2027 EPS. The stock de-rates toward peer multiples on decelerating growth, falling to the analyst low of ~$49 (-31%). The KKR exit at $40-58 looks prescient in hindsight.

### Base Case ($76)
EBITDA lands in the middle of guidance (~$775M, +25%), estimate revisions continue at the current single-digit pace, and the stock holds roughly its current forward multiple as growth normalizes. Fair value converges to the consensus mean of ~$76 (+7%), with the risk skewed to the downside given the pending IRA transition and the rich near-term options positioning.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold), 12-month horizon.**

This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the improving fundamentals (financial health from 5.5 to 6.8, valuation from 4.0 to 5.0, risk from 4.5 to 5.5 across prior reports) confirm the upward score trajectory. We do not upgrade to BUY because the price has run ahead of a thesis that has itself strengthened. The stock is up ~250% and now trades above the price at which the most informed holder exited entirely, with near-term options pricing a ±14% event move. The risk/reward at $71 is balanced, not asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A clean Q2 2026 print that demonstrates the specialty engine is out-running the IRA drag, with adjusted EBITDA tracking to the upper half of the $760-790M guide, combined with 90-day EPS revisions re-accelerating toward +20%+.
- A pullback to the low-$50s to low-$60s (near the KKR June exit at $58.45), which would restore asymmetry versus the ~$76 mean and ~$90 high targets.
- Favorable resolution or de-scoping of the Embrace Hospice investigation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Guidance cut on IRA pricing or a specialty margin compression that pushes adjusted EBITDA growth below ~15%.
- A material adverse development in the Embrace DOJ/DEA matter (settlement above ~$100M or a corporate integrity agreement).
- Estimate revisions turning negative over a 90-day window alongside a Home Health integration that fails to inflect toward accretion.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings release (following the May 1 print, likely late July / early August) is the natural checkpoint. It resolves the near-term options-implied event and provides the first read on whether the back-half-weighted 2026 guidance is on track through the IRA transition.

## Conclusion

BrightSpring has executed a genuine turnaround: a profitability recovery in net income, a de-risked balance sheet, a resolved sponsor overhang, and a differentiated specialty-pharmacy moat compounding EBITDA in the mid-20s despite the quantified IRA headwind. Estimate revisions are rising and the analyst community is unanimously constructive. These are the ingredients of a durable growth story, and they justify holding the position.

They do not, at ~$71, justify buying more. The stock trades at a full ~33x forward / ~42x current-year multiple after a 250% run, the informed seller exited entirely 17-42% below the current price, analyst targets offer only ~7% to the mean, and the options market is pricing near-term stress. The thin-margin business model amplifies every reimbursement shock, and the IRA transition plus the Embrace Hospice investigation are live binaries only partially reflected in price. We maintain HOLD with a base-case fair value of ~$76, and would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$50s to low-$60s or on a clean quarter that proves the specialty engine can durably out-run the pricing drag.
