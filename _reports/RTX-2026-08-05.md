## Executive Summary

RTX's second quarter of 2026 was the strongest operating print of this cycle. Revenue of $24,708M grew 14.5% reported and 16.3% organically, adjusted segment operating profit rose 18% to $3.2B with all three segments expanding margins year over year, and adjusted EPS of $1.89 beat the $1.68 consensus by 12.6% — the fourth consecutive beat, with prior surprises of 16.1%, 4.6% and 19.5%. Backlog reached a record $289B, up 22% year over year, with Raytheon booking $19.9B in the quarter alone for a 2.42x book-to-bill and a 1.77x trailing-twelve-month ratio. Management raised full-year guidance across every line: adjusted sales to $95-96B from $92.5-93.5B, organic growth to 8-9% from 5-6%, adjusted EPS to $7.10-7.25 from $6.70-6.90, and the Raytheon operating-profit growth target to $575-650M from $275-375M. Both rating agencies moved RTX to positive outlook during 2026 (Moody's Baa1 in February, S&P BBB+ in May) as gross debt fell from $41.3B to $37.4B.

The problem is price. The shares traded at $193.39 in mid-July, jumped roughly 7% on the July 23 print, and closed at $217.93 — 2.2% below the 52-week high of $222.82 and up 38.5% over twelve months. At $217.93 the stock carries 30.4x the guided FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $7.175, 27.8x the FY2027 consensus of $7.83, 38.4x trailing-twelve-month GAAP EPS of $5.68, and a 2.94% free-cash-flow yield on guided FY2026 FCF of $8.625B. Consensus estimates rose 5.0% (current year) and 3.7% (next year) over 90 days — genuine positive momentum, but an order of magnitude below the revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection.

Two structural facts compress the return math further. RTX has effectively stopped repurchasing stock — roughly $50M in FY2025, zero in H1 2026, with only $615M of authorization remaining and weighted-average basic shares up 0.75% year over year — so per-share growth receives no help from buybacks and the dividend yields 1.34%. And guided FCF growth of about 8.6% lags guided adjusted EPS growth of roughly 14-17%, with the implied H2 2026 FCF of ~$4.44B sitting far below H2 2025's $7.22B on working-capital build. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on RTX, but the reasoning has inverted: prior holds reflected execution uncertainty around powder metal and Raytheon margins, both of which are resolving favorably. The current hold is about entry price against a base case of roughly 5-6% upside and a bear case of roughly -20%.

## Company Overview

RTX operates three segments of roughly comparable scale. **Collins Aerospace** ($8.2B Q2 sales) supplies aircraft systems and aftermarket services spanning electrical power, environmental control, avionics, nacelles, landing systems and cabin interiors, plus defense and human-spaceflight content; H1 segment margin was 16.5%. **Pratt & Whitney** ($8.9B) builds and services large commercial, military and small engines; H1 margin 8.5%. **Raytheon** ($8.3B) is the effectors and integrated air-and-missile-defense business; H1 margin 12.4%.

Two franchises anchor the durability of the business. The F135 is the sole propulsion system for all three F-35 variants including partner-nation and foreign-military-sale aircraft, and passed one million engine flight hours in 2025. The PW1000G geared turbofan powers more than 2,600 aircraft across 90-plus operators on the A320neo family, A220 (exclusively GTF-powered) and Embraer E2; the GTF Advantage received FAA and EASA certification in 2025, with entry into service expected later in 2026 and full production cutover in 2028. On the defense side, Patriot/GEM-T, LTAMDS, NASAMS, SM-3/SM-6, Tomahawk, AMRAAM, AIM-9X, ESSM and SPY-6 are combat-validated and named in the FY2027 budget request's increases.

The customer mix is frequently misread. U.S. government sales fell from 46% of net sales in 2023 to 38% in 2025, but the dollar amount rose from $31,628M to $33,279M, and the disclosed figure excludes foreign military sales transacted through the U.S. government. International sales grew from $29,440M to $41,312M over the same period, and Raytheon's backlog is now 48% international, up four points year over year. Absolute exposure to U.S. appropriations, FAR/DFARS termination rights, CAS audits and defense-contractor executive orders is at an all-time high in dollar terms even as the percentage falls.

Backlog equals remaining performance obligations for this issuer, an unusually clean definition. Of the 12/31/2025 RPO, roughly 25% converts to revenue within twelve months, and roughly 45% relates to Pratt long-term aftermarket agreements realized over periods up to 20 years. The headline ~3x backlog-to-revenue ratio is not near-term visibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Annual trajectory.** Revenue compounded 9.7% from $67,074M (2022) to $88,603M (2025). Operating margin expanded 240 basis points in 2025 alone, from 8.10% to 10.50%, with operating income up 42.2% on 9.7% revenue growth. GAAP diluted EPS went $3.50 → $2.23 → $3.55 → $4.96 across 2022-2025; the 2023 trough reflects the Powder Metal Matter revenue reduction, which makes 2023 unusable as a comparison base for either growth or margin.

**Q2/H1 2026.** H1 revenue of $46,784M grew 11.7% reported and 13.3% organically — reported understates the underlying rate because H1 absorbed a $761M net-divestiture drag from the 2025 actuation/flight-control and Simmonds sales. H1 operating margin reached 11.47% from 9.98%, and GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations rose to $3.08 from $2.36. The non-GAAP bridge reconciles cleanly: Q2 GAAP $1.57 + $0.27 acquisition-accounting amortization + $0.05 restructuring and other = $1.89 adjusted. That $0.27 quarterly amortization is a permanent consequence of 2020 purchase accounting and annualizes near $1.08/share, so GAAP and non-GAAP series must be compared on matching bases.

**Cash flow, with the necessary adjustment.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $5,402M rose $3,639M year over year, but the 10-Q discloses that factoring activity accounted for $1.5B of that increase. Factoring accelerates collection timing within operating cash flow; it is not incremental earnings. Management separately attributed strength to catch-up on engine deliveries and collections delayed by the four-week Pratt work stoppage in Q2 2025, and to international customer advances at Raytheon on newly booked awards. Both are timing benefits.

Trailing-twelve-month figures derived as FY2025 less H1 2025 plus H1 2026: revenue $93,500M, operating profit $10,485M (11.21% margin), GAAP diluted EPS $5.68, operating cash flow $14,206M, capex $2,799M, free cash flow $11,407M. **The TTM FCF figure should not be treated as a run-rate.** It embeds the factoring step-up, the work-stoppage catch-up and the international advances. Removing only the disclosed factoring delta yields roughly $9.9B. RTX's own FY2026 guidance of $8.5-8.75B implies substantially less, and I weight the guidance. Historically RTX generated cash in the back half — H1 2025 FCF of $720M was 9% of the FY2025 total — and in 2026 that pattern has inverted, which is precisely why the H2 guide looks weak.

The arithmetic: guided FY2026 midpoint $8.625B less H1 actual $4,187M leaves ~$4.44B implied for H2 2026, against H2 2025 actual of $7,220M. The CFO's explanation is that working capital flipped from expected slight tailwind to headwind because three additional points of organic growth require inventory build for H2 2026 and early 2027. Notably, RTX raised only the bottom of the FCF range (+$250M) while leaving the top at $8.75B, despite a $2.9B second quarter — the clearest tell that management sees the cash conversion as tighter than the earnings raise implies. FY2026 FCF also absorbs approximately $0.7B of powder-metal customer compensation cash; adjusting for that, underlying generation is roughly $9.2-9.5B.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt fell to $37,383M at 6/30/2026 from $41.3B in 2024, against ~$8.3B cash for net debt of ~$29.1B and roughly 1.96x net debt to TTM EBITDA. Goodwill of $53,343M plus roughly $32B of intangibles totals ~$85B against $65,245M of equity, implying tangible common equity near negative $20B. That is a mechanical artifact of the 2020 stock-for-stock combination rather than value destruction, but it is not costless: it constrains covenant headroom in a downturn and makes RTX more sensitive to a goodwill-impairment headline than a tangibly capitalized peer. The current ratio of 1.03x is thin in absolute terms, though typical for aerospace and defense where customer advances sit in current liabilities against an off-balance-sheet RPO. Recomputed Altman Z from filing inputs is approximately 2.90 — grey zone, driven by 0.52x asset turnover and negligible working capital rather than by earnings weakness or leverage. Against Baa1/BBB+ with positive outlooks and ~2x leverage, I do not read that as a solvency signal.

Two earnings-quality items deserve naming. The H1 effective tax rate of 16.3% benefits from foreign-derived-income provisions of the 2025 tax act — real, but policy-dependent. And $703M of H1 non-service pension income is non-operating, driven by return and discount-rate assumptions rather than segment execution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has strengthened materially since the prior review, and it rests on three distinct legs.

**Defense bookings.** H1 2026 defense bookings were $37B against $21B in H1 2025. Q2 Raytheon awards included over $5B of GEM-T Patriot effectors — including the first domestic GEM-T production order in more than 30 years — over $4B of classified awards, $1.8B of AMRAAM, $1.1B of AIM-9X and roughly $800M of LTAMDS. International bookings exceeded $10B in H1, more than double year over year, with over $7B from European customers. This is the multi-front demand structure showing up in signed orders rather than in commentary, and Raytheon's backlog at $86B and 48% international supports the pricing and productivity improvement management described.

**Margin quality at Raytheon.** The CFO disclosed that Raytheon saw only ~$20M of productivity favorability in Q2 and ~$40M year to date. For a cost-to-complete business, margin expansion driven by base program economics rather than cumulative-catch-up EAC adjustments is materially higher-quality earnings. Raytheon hit 12.6% in Q2 with mid-12% guided for the balance of the year, and the operating-profit growth guide was roughly doubled — the single largest revision in the release.

**The commercial aftermarket cycle.** Commercial aftermarket is guided to low double-digit growth, commercial OE mid-to-high single digit, defense high single digit. GTF MRO output rose over 40% year over year (1,100 shop visits, +43%) on a 23% reduction in turnaround time with work scope 14 points heavier, and PW1100 AOGs are down 25% year to date. The V2500 fleet remains young with many engines pre-first or pre-second shop visit, and retirements are low.

Three offsets keep this from being a 8+ growth score. First, deceleration is explicit: the CFO guided H2 organic growth to "somewhere in the 5% range" against 13% in H1, driven by comparison effects (the Q2 2025 work stoppage caught up in H2 2025, the Q3 2025 F135 Lot 18 definitization pulling capitalized cost into revenue, and an already-strong Raytheon H2 2025). Second, Pratt commercial OE fell 8% in Q2 despite deliveries rising 15% and is now guided down low single digits, because RTX is deliberately allocating material to the MRO network to drive AOGs down. That is economically rational — aftermarket margins on narrowbody engines far exceed OE — but Pratt's growth is currently being driven by servicing a problem fleet, and OE installed-base capture is being deferred. Third, and most telling, management deliberately did *not* raise Collins commercial aftermarket guidance, "staying fairly prudent... as we continue to monitor the macro situation," even while raising Pratt aftermarket. That caution constitutes management's own flag on where cycle risk sits.

The largest unquantified upside is five signed DoD munitions framework agreements that carry significant ten-year demand and are explicitly **not in the $289B backlog**, pending conversion to definitive contracts. That conversion depends on appropriations and multiyear procurement authority. FY2026 appropriations were enacted February 3, 2026, removing shutdown risk for the fiscal year; the FY2027 request released April 21, 2026 totals roughly $1.5T with named increases for Tomahawk, LTAMDS and Standard Missile, and a June 24 supplemental added roughly $67B for defense including ~$21B for munitions replenishment.

## Competitive Landscape

The evidence for franchise durability is concrete. F135 is the exclusive powerplant for every F-35 variant worldwide. The A220 is exclusively GTF-powered. The GTF installed base spans 2,600-plus aircraft. Patriot/GEM-T, AMRAAM, AIM-9X, SM-3/SM-6, Tomahawk, NASAMS and LTAMDS carry combat-demonstrated intercept records that RTX cited as validation, and the FY2027 request names three of those lines for increases — a claim supported by budget documents rather than assertion. Collins was downselected for mission autonomy software on the USAF Collaborative Combat Aircraft program. Raytheon more than doubled year-over-year output across critical munitions in H1 and more than doubled Coyote counter-UAS production.

Three counterweights keep the trajectory judgment at "improving on defense, mixed on commercial engines." The 10-K explicitly names aftermarket erosion risk — customers buying parts from suppliers other than the OEM, and competitors offering discounts, guarantees and financing participation to win aftermarket work, which is why RTX carries roughly $13B of gross commercial aerospace financing commitments. It also names new defense entrants with different technology approaches and business models, and government bid processes demanding expanded IP disclosure. And Item 1 discloses that third-party collaboration participants hold 13% to 49% aggregate interests in Pratt-directed engine programs, so headline GTF fleet statistics overstate RTX's economic capture of the installed-base annuity.

The Pratt OE/aftermarket allocation choice is the clearest live competitive tension: prioritizing material flow to the 15-shop MRO network to hit consistent ≤90-day turnaround protects customer relationships and the aftermarket margin pool, at the cost of deferred OE deliveries and installed-base share capture on the neo. Management still expects a record number of GTF engine deliveries this year, and GTF Advantage — roughly double the time on wing, fully interchangeable with the installed fleet — is the mechanism by which the powder-metal episode converts from liability to competitive reset. Entry into service later in 2026 is the milestone to watch.

## Risk Assessment

**Cost Accounting Standards claims (~$6B gross demanded, largely unaccrued).** Contracting-officer claims against Pratt over IR&D cost allocation total approximately $1.7B plus ~$1.6B interest (2007-March 2019) and ~$1.1B plus $461M interest (April 2019-December 2023), with collaborator-parts claims adding roughly $723M plus $542M interest across three lines. A December 5, 2025 Federal Circuit opinion reversed in part in the government's favor on the enforceability of a 2006 agreement provision and remanded. RTX believes the claims lack merit and has not accrued the demanded amounts — the correct treatment when a loss is not probable and estimable. A full adverse outcome would be roughly 2% of market capitalization; the greater risk is prolonged uncertainty and precedent affecting future IR&D allocation. This is the largest contingency in the filings with the least visible resolution path.

**The January 7, 2026 Executive Order.** RTX's own 10-K discloses an EO permitting the Secretary of War to limit corporate distributions, share repurchases and executive compensation incentives at defense contractors during periods of program underperformance, and warns it could adversely affect the stock price. Practical scope, trigger conditions and implementing regulations are unverified. This intersects directly with the buyback question below and represents a live, unquantified constraint on the capital-return lever for the entire prime cohort.

**Compliance overhang running to 2029.** The DOJ deferred prosecution agreements (October 2024) carry a three-year cooperation term running from the engagement of an independent monitor, which the Q2 10-Q confirms occurred in April 2026 — extending obligations to roughly April 2029. Breach is grounds for prosecution on the underlying charges at DOJ's sole discretion. The State Department consent agreement carries $33M due by August 29, 2026, with ~$218M accrued at 6/30/2026 for that plus other voluntarily disclosed export matters.

**Powder metal, SEC investigation and litigation.** The customer-compensation accrual fell from $0.7B to $0.4B with ~$0.7B of FY2026 cash outflow, and operational metrics are improving, but the 10-K states AOG levels remain elevated through 2026 and explicitly leaves open that other engine models contain affected powder metal. SEC subpoenas regarding 2023 powder-metal disclosures remain outstanding; the related securities class action was dismissed September 12, 2025 but is on appeal to the Second Circuit, and Delaware derivative suits are pending.

**Contract and mix risks.** The $2.8B F135 Lot 18/19 award is an undefinitized contract action, and the 10-K notes the government can unilaterally definitize — real pricing risk on RTX's most important military engine program. Roughly $14B notional of offset obligations, ~$13B of gross commercial aerospace financing commitments and ~$4.1B of letters of credit and surety bonds are conditional claims on future cash that should not be added to net debt but are not nothing. Tariffs are two-sided: RTX paid ~$0.5B of IEEPA tariffs that the Supreme Court ruled unauthorized in February 2026, with a refund portal open since April 20 but only immaterial amounts received and a government appeal filed June 2; replacement tariffs under other authorities are in place and unquantified.

**Commercial cycle exposure.** The Collins aftermarket line that management declined to raise — flagged as macro-sensitive — is the most exposed part of the P&L. A commercial aftermarket disappointment is the mechanism most likely to produce the bear case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $265 (+21.6%)

Defense bookings momentum sustains: the five munitions framework agreements convert to definitive contracts on FY2027 appropriations and multiyear procurement authority, adding backlog not currently reflected in the $289B figure. Raytheon holds mid-12%+ margins on base program economics with 48%-international mix supporting pricing, and the doubled profit guide proves conservative. GTF Advantage enters service on schedule in late 2026, roughly doubling time on wing and converting the powder-metal episode into a competitive reset that lifts Pratt margin from 8.5% toward low double digits. The 2027 cash bridge delivers: ~$0.7B of powder-metal compensation cash rolls off, the ~$0.5B IEEPA refund is recovered, and the H2 2026 inventory build converts to 2027 revenue and margin, taking FCF toward $11-12B and the yield above 3.5% on a rising base. Consensus FY2027 drifts from $7.83 toward $8.80 and 30x supports $265. Continued deleveraging earns actual ratings upgrades from the current positive outlooks.

### Bear Case — $175 (-19.7%)

The commercial aftermarket cycle turns before defense fully offsets it. Airline profitability compresses, retirements accelerate, shop-visit deferrals begin, and the Collins aftermarket line management pointedly refused to raise decelerates from low double digits toward flat. Pratt's OE deferral costs installed-base share on the neo while Advantage cutover slips past 2028. H2 2026 FCF lands at the low end or below, and the working-capital build proves to be more than timing. The January 2026 Executive Order or the DPA monitor produces a constraint that keeps buybacks suspended into 2027 while share count keeps rising, so the multiple compresses toward the defense-prime cohort at 24x — $175 on FY2026 consensus of $7.25. An adverse CAS ruling on remand adds a $2-4B cash claim, or a commercial downturn triggers a goodwill impairment headline against $85B of goodwill and intangibles on $65B of equity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fifth consecutive). Classification: growth hold.** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and specific — defense bookings conversion, GTF Advantage EIS, and the 2027 cash bridge — but base-case upside of 5.5% against 19.7% downside does not justify new capital at 30x forward with a rising share count.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) is the natural checkpoint. It is the first print inside the guided H2 deceleration and the first read on whether the implied ~$4.44B H2 free cash flow is timing or deterioration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Price at or below $185-195** with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $7.83 — that compresses forward P/E to 24-25x and lifts the guided FCF yield above 3.3%, restoring a two-sided asymmetry. (A move to $190 on unchanged estimates would be an improvement in risk/reward, not a thesis break.)
2. **Definitive contract conversion of two or more of the five DoD munitions framework agreements** with disclosed dollar value entering backlog — this is the largest identified upside currently outside reported figures and is not in consensus.
3. **H2 2026 free cash flow at or above $5.5B** with the working-capital build reversing, plus reinstatement of a meaningful repurchase authorization (>$5B) — that would demonstrate the cash conversion concern was timing and restore the per-share lever.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Collins commercial aftermarket growth decelerating below mid-single-digit** in any quarter, or explicit guidance reduction on that line — this is the segment management itself flagged as macro-sensitive and the mechanism of the bear case.
2. **FY2026 free cash flow guided or delivered below $8.0B**, or FY2027 FCF guidance below $9.0B when initiated — that would confirm structural conversion deterioration rather than inventory timing.
3. **An adverse CAS ruling on remand requiring accrual above $1.5B**, or a formal action under the January 2026 Executive Order restricting distributions.

For existing holders: hold the position. For new capital: wait for $185-195, or for framework-agreement conversion to be quantified in backlog.

## Conclusion

RTX's operating performance has improved on every dimension I track since the prior review. Organic growth of 16.3% in Q2, a record $289B backlog up 22%, a 2.42x quarterly and 1.77x trailing book-to-bill at Raytheon, margin expansion in all three segments, a guidance raise that roughly doubled the Raytheon profit target, four consecutive earnings beats averaging 13%, gross debt down $3.9B with both agencies at positive outlook — this is a company executing well into a demand environment that the FY2027 budget request and $10B of H1 international bookings confirm is durable. Growth prospects rise to 7.0 from 6.5 on that evidence, and the competitive position at 7.0 is supported by F135 exclusivity, A220 exclusivity and named budget line increases.

The reason this remains a HOLD, and why the overall score ticks down to 5.95 from 6.1, is entirely in the last two columns of the valuation table and in capital allocation. The shares re-rated roughly 15% in six weeks against a 5% consensus estimate raise, leaving 30.4x guided FY2026 adjusted earnings, a 2.94% guided free-cash-flow yield, a PEG near 3.5 on FY2027 consensus growth, and a 1.34% dividend as the entire cash return. Buybacks are zero with $615M of authorization left and share count rising 0.75%, so per-share compounding must come from operations and multiple maintenance alone. Guided free-cash-flow growth of 8.6% lags guided earnings growth of 14-17%, and the implied second-half cash flow of ~$4.44B against $7.22B a year earlier is the number that will be litigated in October. Against roughly $6B of unaccrued CAS claims with an adverse December 2025 procedural development, an unverified-scope executive order over defense-contractor distributions, an SEC investigation and DPA obligations running to 2029, the risk-factor dimension does not support paying a growth multiple.

Own RTX for the bookings cycle and the 2027 cash bridge. Add at $185-195, or on quantified framework-agreement conversion.
