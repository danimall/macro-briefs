## Executive Summary

Toro Company is a diversified professional and residential outdoor equipment manufacturer trading at $95.28, roughly 28x trailing EPS of $3.17 and 21x consensus FY2026E EPS of $4.53. The company's Professional segment (80% of revenue, 19.4% EBIT margin) possesses a durable competitive position in golf equipment/irrigation, where Toro is the only integrated provider, and benefits from secular infrastructure spending through its Ditch Witch underground construction franchise. Free cash flow generation is strong at $578M (12.8% FCF margin, 146% conversion), and the balance sheet is adequate with $1.24B in total liquidity and investment-grade ratings.

The core problem is that revenue has been flat near $4.5B for four consecutive years, the Residential segment (19% of revenue) posted a 14% decline in FY2025 with margin compressing to 4.2%, and management guides for further Residential declines in FY2026. The $81.1M full impairment of the Spartan trade name confirms a meaningful acquisition failure. Management has underperformed its own peer group by 76 percentage points on a 5-year TSR basis while maintaining $8M+ in annual CEO compensation. No insiders have made open-market purchases despite the stock trading near multi-year lows through much of 2025.

At 28x trailing earnings and ~21x forward, TTC commands a significant premium to the agricultural/farm machinery peer median of 19.4x, which requires visible earnings acceleration to justify. The AMP productivity initiative ($125M+ target savings by FY2027) and Tornado acquisition provide potential catalysts, but current valuation already embeds optimistic assumptions. The risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Toro manufactures and markets outdoor maintenance equipment across two primary segments. The Professional segment ($3.62B, 80.3% of FY2025 sales) serves golf courses, landscape contractors, underground construction markets, snow/ice management, and agricultural irrigation. Key brands include Toro, Ditch Witch, eXmark, BOSS, Ventrac, and now Tornado Infrastructure Equipment. The Residential segment ($858M, 19.0% of sales) sells walk and riding mowers, snow throwers, and yard tools through home centers, mass retailers, and dealers. Products are distributed through 150+ distributors in 125+ countries. International sales accounted for 19.5% of revenue in FY2025.

The company employs approximately 9,227 people and operates manufacturing facilities primarily in the U.S., with additional operations in Mexico, the UK, and other locations. A joint venture with Huntington National Bank (Red Iron) provides critical floor plan financing to Toro's dealer network, financing roughly $2.7B in receivables annually.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue has plateaued at approximately $4.5B over four fiscal years (FY2022-FY2025), with FY2025 posting a -1.6% decline to $4.51B. The Professional segment grew modestly at +1.9% to $3.62B, while Residential declined 14.0% to $858M. Backlog fell from $1.2B to $0.8B, which management characterizes as normalization from post-pandemic overhangs rather than demand destruction.

FY2026 guidance calls for 2-5% net sales growth overall: Professional mid-single-digit growth, Residential low-to-mid single-digit decline. At the midpoint, this implies roughly $4.66B in revenue, still below FY2022 levels on a real basis given cumulative inflation.

### Margins and Profitability

GAAP operating margin of 9.1% in FY2025 was depressed by the $81.1M Spartan impairment and $48.1M in AMP restructuring charges. Adjusted operating margin was 12.0%, down modestly from 12.2%.

The segment-level picture is bifurcated:
- **Professional EBIT margin improved to 19.4% from 18.0%**, driven by price realization, productivity gains, and cost savings. This suggests the AMP initiative is gaining traction in the higher-margin business.
- **Residential EBIT margin collapsed to 4.2% from 7.9%**, nearly halving due to volume deleverage, higher material costs, and inventory adjustments. At current volumes, Residential is barely profitable.

GAAP EPS was $3.17 (vs. $4.01 prior year). Adjusted EPS was $4.20 (vs. $4.17), reflecting the impact of one-time charges. Consensus FY2026E EPS of $4.53 implies roughly 8% adjusted EPS growth, which requires continued Professional margin expansion and AMP savings to offset Residential drag.

### Cash Flow Quality

Free cash flow of $578M on $316M of GAAP net income represents 183% conversion, or 146% of adjusted net income. Operating cash flow of $662M benefited from $118M in inventory reduction and improved working capital management. Capital expenditures of $84M (1.9% of revenue) are modest for an industrial manufacturer.

Total shareholder returns of $441M (dividends + buybacks) consumed 76% of FCF, leaving headroom for debt reduction or further acquisitions.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt of approximately $922M in long-term obligations against $341M in cash yields net debt of ~$581M, or roughly 1.0x net debt/adjusted EBITDA. The revolver ($898M available) provides ample liquidity. No principal maturities until FY2027. The Altman Z-Score of 2.83 sits in the "grey zone" but close to the 2.99 safe threshold. Investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa1, stable) reflect adequate credit quality.

The Tornado acquisition (CAD $279M, ~$200M USD) was funded with revolver borrowings and a new $200M 5.27% senior notes issuance. This modestly increases leverage but remains within manageable bounds.

## Growth Analysis

The Professional segment has clear positive catalysts: (1) infrastructure spending tailwinds benefiting Ditch Witch through fiber optic, broadband, and utility replacement projects; (2) the Tornado acquisition adding hydrovac excavation capabilities; (3) continued golf and grounds equipment demand supported by Toro's unique integrated offering; and (4) the AMP productivity initiative targeting $125M+ in run-rate savings by FY2027, up from $86.2M achieved cumulatively.

The Residential segment faces structural headwinds: record-low consumer confidence, intense competition from both incumbent and new electric-focused brands (Ego, Greenworks), the California electrification mandate requiring investment in battery platforms, and tariff-driven input cost inflation. Management's guidance for continued Residential declines suggests no near-term recovery.

Estimate revisions provide a modestly positive signal. Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimates rose 2.4% over the past 90 days to $4.53. The most recent quarter (Q1 FY2026) showed a 5.6% upward revision in the last 30 days. Next-year estimates are more mixed, with 90-day revisions of -1.4% to $5.08. The Q1 FY2026 earnings beat of 13.1% ($0.74 vs. $0.65 expected) was the strongest surprise in the four-quarter track record, potentially signaling improving operational momentum.

The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (13.1%, 3.6%, 0.8%, 0.6%) shows improving magnitude, which typically correlates with positive estimate momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.28, TTC trades at:
- 28.2x trailing GAAP EPS ($3.17)
- ~22.7x trailing adjusted EPS ($4.20)
- ~21.0x FY2026E EPS ($4.53)
- ~18.8x FY2027E EPS ($5.08)
- ~16.5x FCF ($578M / $9.24B market cap)
- ~1.66% dividend yield

Against the agricultural/farm machinery peer median P/E of 19.4x, TTC commands a meaningful premium. This premium is partially justified by Toro's higher-margin Professional segment, stronger FCF conversion, and diversified end-market exposure compared to pure-play agricultural equipment makers like AGCO (11.8x). However, the premium has expanded while top-line growth has stalled and GAAP profitability has declined.

Applying 21x to FY2026E adjusted EPS of $4.53 yields a fair value of ~$95, essentially equal to the current price. To reach the analyst consensus median target of $111, the market would need to assign ~24.5x forward earnings, a premium that requires visible acceleration in either revenue growth or margin expansion beyond current guidance.

The stock has rallied 44% from its April 2025 low of $65.28, suggesting much of the recovery from the 2025 downturn is already priced. The current price sits 9% below the 52-week high of $105.19.

## Competitive Landscape

Toro's competitive position varies significantly by business line:

**Golf Equipment & Irrigation (strong moat):** Toro is the only company offering both equipment and irrigation systems for golf courses, creating high switching costs and bundled procurement advantages. This near-duopoly position (with Deere) in golf equipment provides predictable, high-margin revenue.

**Underground Construction (solid position):** Ditch Witch is a well-established brand competing with Vermeer in a market benefiting from fiber optic and utility infrastructure deployment. The Tornado acquisition extends the product line into hydrovac excavation. Infrastructure spending provides a multi-year secular tailwind.

**Landscape Contractor (moderate position):** The Spartan full impairment reveals that expansion via acquisition has proven difficult. Competition from Deere, Husqvarna, and Honda is intense. eXmark remains a strong brand, but the Spartan failure indicates limits to market share gains.

**Residential (weak position):** Low barriers to entry, intense price competition, and growing disruption from battery-electric entrants (Ego, Greenworks) challenge profitability. The California electrification mandate requires capex investment while potentially cannibalizing existing gas-powered product margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff and Input Cost Risk:** The 10-K explicitly flags tariffs as a current and expected ongoing cost headwind. Toro sources commodities globally (steel, aluminum, engines, with Chinese engines facing antidumping petitions). Management acknowledges it may not fully offset tariff impacts through pricing and sourcing adjustments. Professional customers can absorb price increases more readily than Residential consumers, making the margin squeeze asymmetric in a rising-tariff environment with consumer spending weakness.

**Residential Deterioration Risk:** Record-low consumer confidence, elevated inflation, and tariff-driven cost increases create conditions where Residential margins could approach breakeven. A further 10% volume decline on the current 4.2% margin would likely push the segment into losses.

**Acquisition Integration Risk:** The Tornado acquisition adds integration complexity ($200M+ deal, new market adjacency). The Spartan experience demonstrates that Toro's acquisition track record is imperfect. The 10-K contains extensive new risk language about Tornado integration challenges.

**Weather Dependence:** Drought reduces mowing equipment demand; low snowfall impacts BOSS snow/ice management. Climate unpredictability creates revenue volatility.

**Dealer Financing Risk:** The Red Iron JV and floor plan financing arrangements fund ~$3.4B in dealer inventory. If credit conditions tighten or dealer financial health deteriorates, Toro faces reduced sell-through and potential repurchase obligations (up to $36.5M contingent).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 27.6% sits nearly in line with the 26.7% realized volatility; options are fairly priced with minimal risk premium. IV percentile at 7% (within the current chain) indicates the market sees relatively low uncertainty near-term. The put/call skew at 3.6% for the nearest expiry shows modestly elevated put demand, consistent with lingering concerns about Residential macro headwinds and tariff exposure. The flat term structure (27.6% at both 1-month and 6-month tenors) suggests no specific event-driven risk is being priced beyond the normal earnings cadence. Put/call OI ratio of 0.09 is extremely call-heavy, indicating institutional positioning skews toward upside participation. Overall, options markets are pricing modest uncertainty at levels consistent with a mid-cycle industrial name.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$115-120)
- AMP initiative delivers $125M+ in run-rate savings by FY2027, driving adjusted EPS toward $5.50+
- Tornado acquisition integrates successfully, adding $100M+ in revenue with 15%+ EBIT margins
- Infrastructure spending accelerates (fiber, broadband, utility replacement), driving Ditch Witch and underground construction above expectations
- Residential stabilizes as channel destocking completes, and electric product launches gain traction
- Professional segment continues margin expansion toward 21-22%
- Valuation re-rates to 22-24x forward EPS on demonstrated growth

### Bear Case (~$72-75)
- Residential deterioration accelerates as consumer spending weakens further under tariff and inflation pressure
- Tariff costs prove larger than manageable, compressing gross margins by 150-200bps
- Tornado integration stumbles (execution risk in a new market adjacency)
- Professional segment growth stalls as golf/landscape construction customers defer spending
- Market de-rates industrial multiples amid recession concerns
- Stock reverts to 15-16x depressed forward earnings

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Two consecutive quarters of positive total revenue growth exceeding 4% YoY**, confirming that the top-line plateau is breaking
2. **Residential segment EBIT margin returning above 6%**, demonstrating that volume deleverage has troughed
3. **Pullback to $80-85** (17.5-18.5x FY2026E EPS), improving risk/reward without requiring fundamental improvement

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Residential segment posts an operating loss in any quarter**, signaling the segment is structurally unprofitable
2. **FY2026 revenue guidance cut below 0% growth** on an earnings call
3. **Another material impairment or acquisition write-down**, indicating systematic capital allocation failures

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 earnings report (expected June 2026), which captures peak spring season demand and provides the clearest read on Professional momentum and Residential trajectory.

## Conclusion

Toro is a quality industrial franchise with a strong Professional segment, excellent free cash flow generation, and a durable competitive moat in golf and underground construction. These attributes underpin a floor on the business's long-term value. However, at ~21x forward earnings, the stock prices in a recovery that depends on: (1) AMP savings materializing on schedule, (2) the Tornado acquisition executing well, and (3) Residential at minimum stabilizing, all while tariff and consumer spending headwinds remain active. Revenue has been flat for four years, the Spartan impairment signals imperfect capital allocation, and management has underperformed its peer group by 76 percentage points on a 5-year TSR basis without meaningful insider buying. The 44% rally from April 2025 lows has compressed the risk/reward. HOLD, with a reassessment at Q2 FY2026 earnings or on a pullback to the mid-$80s.
