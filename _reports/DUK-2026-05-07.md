## Executive Summary

Duke Energy is one of the largest regulated utility holding companies in the United States, serving 8.7 million electric and 1.6 million gas customers across six states. The company sits at the intersection of two powerful forces: unprecedented data center-driven load growth (7.6 GW executed agreements, another 7.8 GW in high-confidence pipeline) and a massive $103B five-year capital plan that requires sustained regulatory approval to earn adequate returns. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.93 (+9.7% YoY) beat consensus by 2.2%, and management reaffirmed full-year guidance of $6.55-$6.80.

The investment case hinges on whether Duke can execute its capital plan at allowed returns without triggering regulatory backlash. The proposed ~15% rate increase in North Carolina has drawn significant public opposition, with evidentiary hearings scheduled for Q3 2026. At 19.3x forward earnings, DUK trades at a 33% discount to the 28.9x peer median — but peers like CEG (43.6x) and VST (72.6x) carry substantially different risk profiles as competitive generators exposed to merchant power markets. Against traditional regulated utility peers like SO (23.9x) and AEP (19.6x), Duke's valuation is appropriate for its growth rate but doesn't yet offer asymmetric upside given the near-term regulatory uncertainty.

The 3.3% dividend yield provides reasonable income support, and the 5-7% long-term EPS growth rate (targeting "top-half" from 2028) is among the strongest for large-cap regulated utilities. However, the path to realizing that growth runs through rate case outcomes, $9B+ annual debt issuance in an elevated rate environment, and successful execution of the largest utility capex program in U.S. history.

## Company Overview

Duke Energy operates primarily through two segments: Electric Utilities and Infrastructure (EU&I, ~90% of segment income) and Gas Utilities and Infrastructure (GU&I). The company's generation fleet comprises approximately 55,700 MW of proportional net capacity including 11 nuclear reactors, natural gas, coal (being retired), hydro, and renewables.

The geographic footprint spans the Carolinas, Florida, Indiana, Ohio, and Kentucky — with the Carolinas and Florida representing the fastest-growing territories. Duke won 87 economic development projects in 2025 totaling $30 billion in new capital investment, including Amazon's $10 billion data center campus in North Carolina.

Three strategic transactions are in progress: (1) sale of Piedmont's Tennessee operations to Spire for $2.48B, (2) a $6B minority investment for 19.7% of Duke Energy Florida, and (3) a proposed merger of Duke Energy Progress into Duke Energy Carolinas (FERC-approved January 2026, targeted closing January 1, 2027, with estimated $2.3B in customer savings through 2040).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
Duke has delivered consistent EPS growth: $5.41 (2022) → $5.56 (2023) → $5.90 (2024) → $6.31 (2025), representing a 5.3% CAGR. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.93 vs. $1.76 prior year (+9.7%) indicates continued momentum. Full-year 2026 guidance of $6.55-$6.80 implies 3.8-7.8% growth from the $6.31 reported in 2025.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Long-term debt: $80.1B
- Short-term debt: $2.6B
- Shareholders' equity: $51.8B
- Debt-to-equity: 3.78x
- Debt-to-capitalization: ~63% (vs. 65% covenant limit)
- Credit ratings: BBB+/Baa2 at parent, A-level at operating subsidiaries, all stable outlooks

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $12.33B (flat YoY despite higher earnings due to storm cost timing)
- Capital expenditures: $14.0B (2025), escalating to $17.75B (2026), $19.5B (2027), $21.2B (2028)
- Free cash flow: -$1.69B

The negative FCF is structural for a utility in heavy investment mode and will persist for years. Duke funds the gap through debt issuance (~$9B/year planned), the Florida minority investment ($6B), Piedmont Tennessee sale ($2.48B), IRA tax credit monetization ($3.1B through 2028), and planned equity ($10B through 2030).

**Operating Efficiency:**
- Operating margin: 29.7%
- Net margin: 17.5%
- ROE: 9.8% (consistent with regulated allowed returns of 10-10.5%)

**Dividend:**
The $4.16/share annualized dividend yields 3.3% at current prices with a ~62% payout ratio on 2026 guidance midpoint. Duke has paid dividends for 99 consecutive years with ~2% annual growth. The payout ratio provides adequate coverage given the regulated earnings profile.

## Growth Analysis

Duke's growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Data Center Demand (Primary Catalyst):**
7.6 GW of executed data center agreements with hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta), with an additional 7.8 GW in high-confidence pipeline — totaling 15.4 GW. Duke added 2.7 GW of new agreements in Q1 2026 alone. Contract structures include minimum demand provisions, credit support, refundable capital advances, and termination charges — materially de-risking stranded asset exposure.

**2. Service Territory Demographics:**
The Carolinas and Florida are among the fastest-growing states in the U.S. by population and economic activity. This organic load growth compounds with data center demand to create a multi-year investment runway.

**3. Capital Plan at 5-7% EPS Growth:**
The $103B five-year capital plan underpins 5-7% adjusted EPS growth through 2030, with management targeting the "top-half" from 2028 onward as data center load materializes. FY2027 consensus EPS of $7.17 implies 7% growth from current year.

**Growth Risks:**
- Regulatory approval of rate increases is required for capital recovery
- AI capex cycle deceleration could slow data center construction timelines
- Rising construction costs (labor, materials, tariffs) could pressure earned returns

## Valuation Assessment

At $124.48, DUK trades at:
- Forward P/E: 19.3x (on $6.45 midpoint) to 18.6x (on 2026 consensus $6.70)
- P/E on FY2027 consensus ($7.17): 17.4x
- Dividend yield: 3.3%
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 12-13x (given ~$97B market cap + $83B debt - $2B cash)

**Peer Comparison:**
Duke's 19.3x forward P/E sits at a significant discount to the 28.9x peer median, but this comparison is misleading. CEG (43.6x) and VST (72.6x) are competitive/merchant generators benefiting from nuclear premium and energy trading — fundamentally different risk profiles. Against true regulated peers: SO trades at 23.9x and AEP at 19.6x.

Duke's discount to Southern Company (SO) — the most comparable peer at ~$104B market cap — reflects Duke's higher leverage (3.78x D/E vs. SO's lower ratio), the regulatory risk concentrated in North Carolina, and Duke's more aggressive capex trajectory creating execution uncertainty.

**Is the Market Wrong?**
The 19.3x multiple on a 5-7% grower with 3.3% yield is not obviously mispriced. With the 30-year Treasury above 5%, the ERP challenge for utilities is real: a utility stock yielding 3.3% with 5-7% EPS growth offers a total return expectation of 8-10%, which provides only modest premium to risk-free rates. The discount to SO (23.9x) is approximately 19% — this would close if (a) the NC rate case receives constructive treatment, and (b) the data center pipeline continues converting to revenue. At SO's multiple, DUK would trade at ~$160.

**Analyst Consensus:**

## Competitive Landscape

**Territory Advantage:** The Carolinas are emerging as a premier data center hub due to low existing power costs, temperate climate, fiber connectivity, and business-friendly state policy. This is a durable structural advantage — data centers, once built, don't relocate.

**Scale Advantage:** The $103B capital plan creates procurement economies and workforce capabilities that smaller utilities cannot match. Duke's nuclear fleet (11 reactors) provides clean baseload that hyperscalers value for corporate sustainability commitments.

**Regulatory Framework Evolution:** New legislation in Ohio (HB15), South Carolina (Act 41), and North Carolina (SB266) modernizes cost recovery mechanisms, creating more favorable investment environments. The proposed Carolinas merger also strengthens the regulatory relationship by demonstrating efficiency commitment.

**Competitive Threats:** Limited in the regulated context — Duke holds exclusive service territories. The primary competitive dynamic is between Duke and regulators/ratepayers over the pace and cost of new investment.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Risk (HIGH — Near-term Binary):** The NCUC rate case hearings scheduled for Q3 2026 represent a binary event for the earnings growth trajectory. A constructive outcome (rate increase approved within 10-15% of request) validates the capital plan. A hostile outcome (rate increase materially reduced or denied) would force capex deferrals and downward EPS revisions. Public testimony has been emotionally charged, with ratepayers describing hardship. The political dynamics of "ordinary customers subsidizing data centers" pose an underappreciated risk.

**Balance Sheet Leverage (MODERATE):** At 63% debt-to-capitalization against a 65% covenant limit, headroom is thin. The Florida minority investment and Piedmont sale are structurally important to maintaining investment-grade credit metrics. Failure to complete either transaction would likely require accelerated equity issuance (dilutive) or capex curtailment (growth-impairing).

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):** With $9B+ in annual debt issuance and 30-year Treasury above 5%, Duke's cost of new debt is elevated. Each 100 bps increase in rates adds approximately $64M in pretax interest expense on floating exposures, with larger impacts on new fixed-rate issuance rolling through over time. These costs ultimately flow into rate requests, compounding the affordability challenge.

**Execution Risk (MODERATE):** Delivering $17-21B annual capex without significant cost overruns, permitting delays, or construction defects across natural gas generation, transmission, distribution, nuclear extensions, and renewables simultaneously is unprecedented for any utility.

**Weather/Climate (MODERATE):** The 2024 hurricane season (Debby, Helene, Milton) caused multi-billion dollar damage. While storm cost recovery mechanisms exist and securitization provides funding, the frequency and severity of tropical weather events appears to be increasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($140-146, +12-17%):**
- NC rate case receives constructive treatment (rate increase approved at 12-15% vs. 15% requested)
- Data center pipeline converts at 80%+ rate; Q3/Q4 2026 announcements of additional hyperscaler contracts
- Florida minority investment and Piedmont Tennessee sale close on schedule, maintaining credit metrics
- Interest rates moderate (30Y to 4.5%), reducing utility sector discount to bonds
- Multiple re-rates toward SO's 23.9x → stock reaches $145-160

**Bear Case ($111-115, -8-11%):**
- NC regulators materially reduce rate increase (approved at <10%), signaling structural underrecovery
- Data center demand growth decelerates as AI capex cycle moderates
- 30-year Treasury remains >5%, compressing utility multiples further
- One or both strategic transactions delayed/restructured, forcing dilutive equity raise
- Stock retests May 2025 lows of $111-113

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. NCUC rate case outcome in Q3 2026 approves ≥80% of requested rate increase, confirming regulatory support for the capital plan
2. Stock pulls back to $115-118 range (18x FY2026 guidance midpoint), creating a more favorable entry point with 3.5%+ yield
3. Completion of both the Florida minority investment AND Piedmont Tennessee sale, de-risking the balance sheet

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NCUC approves <60% of requested rate increase or imposes significant conditions on data center cost allocation
2. Debt-to-capitalization exceeds 65%, triggering covenant concerns or credit rating downgrades
3. Data center executed agreements fall below 7 GW (indicating hyperscaler contract attrition)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q3 2026 (following NCUC evidentiary hearings and rate case decision, expected late summer/fall 2026). Secondary reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings for updated data center pipeline metrics.

**Specific risk preventing BUY recommendation:** The Q3 2026 NCUC rate case represents a clearly identifiable regulatory risk where significant public opposition to a 15% rate increase has materialized. Until this is resolved, the stock's P/E discount to SO (19%) is justified by the binary nature of the outcome. The 63% debt-to-capitalization ratio (vs. 65% covenant) leaves insufficient margin for error if the rate case disappoints, as Duke would need to either accelerate equity issuance (diluting the 5-7% EPS growth) or curtail capex (removing the growth catalyst). This is falsifiable: if the NCUC approves ≥80% of the rate request, the discount should compress and the stock would warrant a BUY upgrade.

## Conclusion

Duke Energy is a well-positioned regulated utility with one of the strongest demand growth stories in the sector, supported by hyperscaler data center commitments and favorable demographics in its service territories. The 5-7% EPS growth trajectory, 3.3% dividend yield, and 19.3x forward multiple create an attractive total return profile for income-oriented investors.

However, the near-term regulatory uncertainty (North Carolina rate case, Q3 2026), thin balance sheet headroom (2pp to covenant limits), and the unprecedented execution demands of a $103B capital program prevent a higher-conviction call at current prices. The stock is appropriately valued for its risk profile pending resolution of these catalysts. Income investors should maintain positions for the yield and structural growth exposure; new capital deployment should wait for either price weakness ($115-118) or regulatory clarity (post-Q3 2026 NCUC decision).
