**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FWONK is a different company than the ticker's history suggests. After a multi-year unwind of Liberty's tracking-stock complex — splitting off the Atlanta Braves (2023), SiriusXM (2024), and Liberty Live/Live Nation (December 2025) — the Series C shares now represent direct common equity in a pure-play global motorsports operator. The company holds two crown-jewel assets: 100% of Formula 1 (exclusive commercial rights to the FIA World Championship through 2110) and ~84% of MotoGP (exclusive rights through 2060, acquired July 2025 for ~$3.66B). This is an asset-light, high-margin rights business that collects contractually escalating media, promotion, and sponsorship revenue without owning or paying the competing teams and drivers.

The reported financials are distorted and require care. GAAP revenue appears to collapse from $12.5B (2023) to $3.7B (2024), and 2024 shows a $2.1B net loss — both artifacts of divesting SiriusXM and Live Nation as discontinued operations. The underlying business is growing double digits: the F1 segment grew revenue +13.5% to $3,873M in 2025 with Adjusted OIBDA up ~20% to $946M, and MotoGP grew pro-forma revenue +13.9% (+8.6% constant currency). Consolidated FY2025 free cash flow was $789M on a ~17.6% FCF margin. Any screen keying off trailing GAAP EPS (-$0.13) or trailing revenue trends will badly misjudge this equity.

The investment question is whether the sum-of-the-parts discount closes. Morgan Stanley — which named FWONK its Top Pick in US Media & Entertainment in May 2026 (PT $117) — argues the stock trades ~25% below the aggregate value of the 10-11 F1 teams, meaning the market values the league that collects escalating rights from all of them at less than its franchisees, with MotoGP thrown in for free. The media-rights re-pricing catalyst is live and validated: the new five-year Apple US deal at ~$140M/year is a step-up of roughly 55-65% from ESPN's prior ~$85-90M, and F1's ~50 broadcast agreements roll on 3-5 year cycles. We rate FWONK BUY, with the Malone control overhang and unproven MotoGP integration as the primary offsets.

## Company Overview

Formula 1 monetizes the sport through three primary streams: media rights (~31% of F1 revenue), race promotion (~27%), and sponsorship (~22%), plus hospitality (Paddock Club), freight/logistics, the F2/F3/F1 Academy feeder series, and the self-promoted Las Vegas Grand Prix. The 2025 calendar ran 24 events across 21 countries. The fanbase is roughly 800M and growing at a low-double-digit rate, skewing young and affluent, a demographic advertisers pay a premium to reach. F1's US expansion — Netflix's *Drive to Survive*, the Miami and Las Vegas Grands Prix, the *F1* feature film (Apple/Warner Bros.), and now Apple distribution — converted a once-niche European property into a fast-growing US franchise.

MotoGP follows a similar model but is more media-rights-weighted (~41% of revenue) and more Europe-centric (concentrated in Spain and Italy). Liberty acquired ~84% of it in July 2025, funded partly by a $2.5B distribution drawn up from F1's senior loan facilities plus cash on hand.

The structural transformation is central to the thesis. As of December 15, 2025, Liberty Formula One common stock ceased to be a tracking stock and became direct equity in an operating holding company. This is why the sell-side describes the setup as "own the sport, not a team." F1 and MotoGP are singular, unreplicable global sporting IP with century-scale governing-body agreements, contractually escalating multi-year revenue, and no direct payroll for the competing teams.

## Financial Analysis

**Clean operating view (continuing operations):**

- **F1 segment (2025 vs 2024):** Revenue $3,873M vs $3,411M (+13.5%); Adjusted OIBDA $946M vs $791M (+19.6%); operating income $632M vs $492M. Growth was broad-based: media rights up on contractual escalators, F1 TV subscriber growth, and one-time revenue tied to the *F1* film; race promotion up on escalators; sponsorship up on new and existing partners.
- **MotoGP (pro-forma FY2025):** Revenue $573M (+13.9% reported, +8.6% constant currency); Adjusted OIBDA $201M. Only six months (Jul-Dec) consolidated into FWONK actuals, contributing $325M revenue and $117M Adjusted OIBDA.
- **Consolidated FY2025:** Revenue $4,482M; operating income $577M; net income $555M; Adjusted OIBDA $1,068M; operating cash flow $908M; free cash flow $789M.

The margin structure requires precise understanding. F1's largest cost is team payments (the Prize Fund), which rose to $1,400M from $1,266M plus a one-time $50M Concorde incentive payment to teams for signing the 2026-2030 agreement. Because the Prize Fund is a fixed percentage of F1's own "Prize Fund Adjusted EBIT," team payments scale with F1's success: the more F1 earns, the more flows to teams. This caps incremental operating leverage by design, but it also aligns the teams with F1's commercial growth and de-risks defection.

**Balance sheet and leverage:**
- Total assets $15.4B; stockholders' equity $7.76B. Goodwill is $7.0B (F1 $3.96B + MotoGP $3.07B) and intangibles $5.1B — roughly 79% of assets are goodwill/intangibles, standard for two acquisition-accounted rights businesses.
- Long-term debt $5.05B, but only ~$499M is corporate-level (mainly the 2.25% Convertible Senior Notes due 2027). The bulk sits at the F1 and MotoGP subsidiary level, non-recourse and ring-fenced by restricted-payment/leverage covenants. The company and both subsidiaries are in full covenant compliance.
- Cash $1.05B (F1 $539M / MotoGP $197M / Corporate $319M). The restricted-payment leverage tests can gate Liberty's access to subsidiary cash when leverage is elevated, a structural liquidity nuance rather than a distress signal.
- The Altman Z-Score of 1.82 lands in the grey zone but is misleading here: it is driven by the intangible-heavy denominator inherent to a rights business, not by cash-flow distress. Piotroski scores 5/5 on available signals; the current ratio is 1.46; operating cash flow exceeds net income.

Margins run ~12.9% operating, ~13.3% net, and ~17.6% FCF, the cash-generative quality the bull thesis leans on. The company pays no dividend and does not intend to; an authorized buyback exists but capital return is not the story here.

**Earnings quality and estimate context.** The quarterly beat/miss record is noisy because Q1 is seasonally weakest (the calendar runs March-December) and the 2025 quarters straddle the restructuring. The Q2 2025 print (128% surprise) and Q1 2025 (163% surprise) reflect one-time items and the low bar of trough quarters. Current-quarter estimates were cut sharply over 90 days (-59%), a seasonal-mix artifact rather than deterioration: full-year 2026 estimates rose +9.4% over the same window and next-year estimates rose +1.6%. Consensus for the current year sits at $1.79 and next year at $2.13, both edging higher. The revision picture is best read as stable-to-improving on the durable annual figures.

## Growth Analysis

Two independent growth engines are compounding. The first is media-rights re-pricing. The Apple US deal validates the thesis that live-sports rights values continue to rise in a fragmented streaming world. F1 owns roughly 50 broadcast agreements that roll on 3-5 year cycles; each renewal is an opportunity to re-price against the Apple template. F1 is the licensor and Apple the licensee: Apple pays F1, not the reverse. Management reported no fan backlash from the ESPN-to-Apple switch and rising US viewership, which de-risks future US renewals.

The second engine is fan-base and demographic expansion. The ~800M fanbase growing ~12% year-over-year drives all three revenue lines: more viewers support higher media rights, more attendance supports promotion escalators, and a young affluent audience supports sponsorship pricing. F1 TV/VideoPass DTC subscriber growth adds a directly-owned, higher-margin revenue layer.

MotoGP adds a third leg with its own escalator structure and a European base that is less penetrated on the commercial side than F1's. Integration synergies are unproven, but the pro-forma +13.9% revenue growth and $201M Adjusted OIBDA give a credible floor.

The 2026 season adds structural tailwinds: an 11th team (Cadillac), new engine suppliers (Audi and Honda enter, Renault exits), and the 2026-2030 Concorde Agreement that locks team participation through 2030. Rising rights values, DTC subscriber growth, sponsorship digitization, and the F1/MotoGP dual-property base give the growth thesis higher durability than a single-company story.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here given the restructuring and the mid-year MotoGP consolidation. On forward earnings, the ~$1.79 current-year and $2.13 next-year consensus put FWONK at ~54x and ~46x P/E — optically high, but earnings multiples are the wrong lens for a rights business where OIBDA and free cash flow are the operative measures. The ~$789M FY2025 free cash flow against a ~$21.8B market cap is a ~3.6% FCF yield, which understates the picture because it captures only six months of MotoGP and reflects a year with elevated one-time team and integration costs.

The sum-of-the-parts framework is the crux. Morgan Stanley's ~25%-discount-to-teams argument is the specific mispricing thesis: the market values the league collecting escalating rights from all franchisees at less than the franchisees themselves, with MotoGP effectively free. F1 teams have transacted at increasingly rich valuations, and the league's cash flows are more diversified and contractually protected than any single team's. If that gap even partially closes as the de-conglomeration is digested and MotoGP contribution annualizes, there is meaningful upside.

Peer comparison is thin and imperfect: the "Entertainment" comp set (ROKU at ~90x, SIRI at ~12x, the Liberty Live tracking stubs at negative multiples) is heterogeneous and not directly relevant. The better comparables are other scarce live-sports IP holders (TKO for UFC/WWE, Live Nation), which trade on forward OIBDA/FCF multiples that support a re-rating case for a growing, moat-protected rights owner.

We score valuation a 6.5 rather than higher for two reasons. First, the SOTP discount is a real catalyst but its closure is not dated or guaranteed. Second, the Malone control overhang and non-voting Series C structure justify a persistent governance discount that caps how far the gap closes absent a strategic event. The analyst consensus target range ($97 low, $135 high, $119 median) implies upside from current levels, and on an inflecting business with rising annual estimates, those targets are more floor than ceiling.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is among the widest in media. F1 holds exclusive commercial rights until 2110 under the "100-Year Agreements"; MotoGP holds exclusive rights until 2060. These are singular, unreplicable global sporting IP — there is no substitute Formula 1, and a rival series would have to poach teams, drivers, circuits, and a century of brand equity simultaneously. The asset-light structure means F1 captures rights economics without payroll or capital tied up in the competing teams.

The competitive trajectory is improving. F1 is gaining share of the US sports/entertainment wallet through the Netflix/Miami/Vegas/film flywheel, and the Apple deal converts that engagement into a durable rights uplift. Sponsorship is growing off new and digital partners. MotoGP adds a second scarce property. Against all live sports and entertainment for viewership and sponsor dollars, F1's unpredictability, communal viewing, and premium demographic make it a preferred advertiser property.

The structural threats are genuine but bounded: a rival series poaching teams (low probability given the century-long FIA agreement and the 2026-2030 Concorde lock-up), EV/ICE societal shifts affecting motorsport relevance (a slow, manageable adaptation), and piracy of pay-TV feeds. None of these are near-term share-loss vectors. We score competitive position an 8.0 — one of the strongest moats in the coverage universe, with an improving trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Malone control overhang (the sharpened governance risk).** John Malone controls ~49% of voting power via ~97% of the Series B super-voting shares. The FY2025 10-K discloses that Malone resigned from the board, and as a result no further Exchange Agreement swaps will occur to cap his voting at the 49% target, meaning his voting power could exceed a majority over time. Series C holders have no voting rights at all. Combined with a classified board, supermajority provisions, blank-check preferred, and overlapping directors across the Liberty complex, this heightens the controlled-company discount and complicates any takeover. It does not change Series C economics, but it is a genuine, recently-modified risk.

**MotoGP integration and valuation risk.** Acquired mid-2025 for €3.12B, the purchase price allocation is still preliminary and created $3.07B of goodwill. Synergies, retention, and margin expansion are unproven; the IRTA rider-team agreement expires end-2026 and is being renegotiated for 2027-2031, with risk of less favorable terms.

**Existential agreement risk (low probability, catastrophic).** Termination of F1's FIA license (available on unpermitted change of control, material breach, insolvency, or interference with FIA rights) could force F1 to discontinue operations; the same applies to MotoGP under the FIM Agreement. These are tail risks, but they are the reason the business carries a governance/structural discount.

**FIA/FIM as uncontrollable counterparty.** The governing bodies prioritize sporting and safety interests over commercial ones and can raise team costs, alter regulations, or block calendar/circuit changes against F1's commercial interest.

**Team leverage.** The 2026 Concorde Commercial Agreement runs only through 2030; teams hold consent rights (70% needed for >24 events or <8 European/North American races). The shared-margin structure is by design.

**Tax-leakage risk.** The SiriusXM and Liberty Live split-offs relied on opinions of counsel, not IRS private letter rulings. A Section 355(e) change-of-control event within the plan window could trigger significant corporate tax, constraining M&A flexibility and deterring a takeover.

**Event and popularity risk.** Popularity is the entire thesis; a scandal, fatal accident, or rival series could impair it. Events have been cancelled for flooding, geopolitics, and promoter failures, and insurance generally does not cover cancellation.

**Insider posture.** There is no open-market buying signal. The large December 2025 block transactions across insiders were mechanical dispositions tied to the Liberty Live split-off, not sentiment. Genuinely discretionary trades were all sells — Chase Carey exercised options and sold ~100k shares near $90 in May 2026 and similar in December 2025, CLO Renee Wilm sold ~11,600 shares at $90.09 in June 2026, plus routine tax-withholding sales. This is routine option-monetization, not a bearish tell against improving estimates, but it removes any positive management-alignment signal, the primary reason we score management quality a 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress: near-term ATM IV of 32.1% sits rich to 24.6% one-year historical vol, the term structure is in backwardation (32.1% near vs 29.1% long), and the near-term put/call skew is elevated at 9.7%. This aligns with the August 6 Q2 earnings catalyst and an implied ±7.4% move. The longer-dated tenors show negative skew (call-leaning at 3m and 6m), and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 7.10 to 0.19 since early June alongside an 11.4% price gain — the earlier bearish positioning has largely unwound. Short interest is modest at 3.7% of float (3.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The market is hedging the imminent print, not signaling a structural bearish thesis. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry or writing puts around the event rather than chasing pre-print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($135):** The SOTP discount to team values closes as the de-conglomeration is fully digested and MotoGP annualizes into consolidated results. Media-rights renewals across the ~50-agreement portfolio re-price toward the Apple template, driving mid-teens OIBDA growth. Fan base compounds at ~12%, sponsorship digitizes, and DTC subscribers scale. MotoGP integration delivers synergies and margin expansion. Sell-side targets migrate toward and beyond $135 as consensus catches up to a clean, growing, moat-protected rights compounder.

**Bear case ($78, ~20% downside):** The Malone control overhang deepens into majority voting control, entrenching the governance discount permanently. MotoGP integration disappoints and the IRTA renegotiation lands on unfavorable terms. A media-rights renewal cycle underwhelms, or a popularity shock (scandal, safety incident, rival series) impairs the franchise. The SOTP discount persists because the market applies a durable controlled-company haircut, and shares drift back toward the low-$80s where they traded through the first half of 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $118.** This is anchored on the consensus mean ($118) and median ($119), which we treat as a reasonable floor for an inflecting business given rising annual estimates and a validated re-pricing catalyst. It implies ~22% upside from $97.05. Applying a modest closure of the SOTP discount to a business generating ~$789M-plus of growing free cash flow, with MotoGP contribution annualizing and mid-teens F1 OIBDA growth, supports a re-rating toward this level over 12-18 months.

**Bull case target: $135** (+39% from current), matching the top of the analyst range if the SOTP gap closes materially and rights re-pricing accelerates.

**Bear case target: $78** (-20% from current), if the governance discount entrenches and integration or a renewal cycle disappoints.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, tied to the August 6, 2026 Q2 print (first clean full-quarter with MotoGP consolidated), subsequent media-rights renewal announcements, and the 2027-2031 IRTA outcome.

**Upside/downside from current ($97.05):** +22% to base, +39% to bull, -20% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. F1 segment Adjusted OIBDA growth decelerates below high-single digits on a full-year basis, signaling the escalator/re-pricing engine is stalling.
2. The 2027-2031 IRTA agreement (MotoGP) is signed on materially less favorable economics, or MotoGP posts a goodwill impairment.
3. A media-rights renewal (particularly a major territory) prices flat or down versus the prior contract, contradicting the Apple re-pricing template.
4. A Section 355(e) tax event or a Malone-driven strategic action that impairs Series C economics or removes takeover optionality.

## Conclusion

FWONK is a clean, single-thesis equity for the first time in over a decade: exclusive commercial rights to two scarce global motorsports properties, an asset-light rights model, double-digit revenue growth, ~17-18% free-cash-flow margins, and a validated media-rights re-pricing catalyst in the Apple US deal. The moat is among the widest in media, the growth is broad-based and confirmed across multiple revenue lines, and the sum-of-the-parts discount to team values offers identifiable upside as the de-conglomeration is digested.

The offsets keep this from being a maximum-conviction call: the Malone control overhang is a recently-sharpened governance risk with Series C holders holding no votes, MotoGP integration is unproven, and management's discretionary insider activity is net selling. The near-term options market is pricing stress into the August print, arguing for patience on entry. On balance, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside on a moat-protected compounder with rising estimates and a specific mispricing thesis. We rate FWONK **BUY** with a base case of $118 (~22% upside) over 12-18 months, and would accumulate on weakness toward the low-$90s or post-earnings IV compression.
