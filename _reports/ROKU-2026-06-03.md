**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Roku has reached durable, operating-driven profitability after years of market doubt. FY2025 delivered the company's first full-year GAAP net profit (+$88.4M, versus a -$129.4M loss in FY2024 and a -$709.6M loss in 2023), and Q1 2026 confirmed the trend is accelerating. Net income swung to +$85.7M (diluted EPS $0.57) from a -$27.4M loss a year earlier, a $113M YoY improvement in a single quarter, driven by 22% revenue growth against roughly flat operating expenses and a decline in stock-based compensation. Adjusted EBITDA rose 165% to $148.4M. This is genuine operating leverage, not a one-time gain. With ~$2.38B in cash and short-term investments, no funded debt, and $538.8M of trailing free cash flow (+81% YoY), the balance sheet is strong, and management has initiated the company's first-ever buyback ($400M authorization).

The complication is that the market has already absorbed much of this. The stock is up 74% over the trailing twelve months to ~$127, near its 52-week high of $133.46, and trades at roughly 95x trailing earnings and ~36x next-year consensus EPS of $3.53. The central operating tension the bull case must resolve is visible in Roku's own Q1 disclosure: advertising revenue grew 27% on a +59% surge in video impressions but a -14% decline in average price per impression. Roku is monetizing more inventory at lower prices. Whether that reflects healthy share gains and mix shift (the bull reading) or eroding pricing power in an oversupplied CTV ad market dominated by Amazon's DSP and The Trade Desk (the bear reading) is the question that determines whether the multiple is justified.

We rate ROKU a HOLD with a constructive growth lean. The profitability turn is real and the new three-segment disclosure (Advertising, Subscriptions, Devices) materially improves visibility. But after a 74% run, the risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric: the bull case requires CPM stabilization and continued operating leverage, while the bear case rests on margin compression in both advertising (CPM) and subscriptions (owned-content costs) plus intensifying platform competition. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the $95-105 zone or on confirmation that ad CPMs have stabilized.

## Company Overview

Roku operates the leading independent TV streaming platform in the United States, built on a razor-and-blades model. It sells streaming players and Roku-made TVs at or below cost to expand its installed base of Streaming Households, then monetizes the resulting engagement through advertising and subscriptions. The Roku TV OS is also licensed (generally royalty-free) to third-party TV OEMs, extending reach without hardware cost.

Effective Q1 2026, Roku reorganized reporting from two segments (Platform/Devices) into three (Advertising, Subscriptions, and Devices), which for the first time exposes the economics of the two monetization engines separately:

- **Advertising: $612.7M (49% of revenue)**, gross margin ~61%
- **Subscriptions: $518.5M (42%)**, gross margin ~41%
- **Devices: $117.6M (9%)**, gross margin -16%, a deliberate loss-leader

Key owned assets include The Roku Channel (an AVOD/FAST service that supplies Roku's primary owned ad inventory), plus two newer owned subscription services: Frndly TV (acquired May 2025 for $169.8M) and Howdy (an internally launched ~$2.99/month service, August 2025). Roku is the U.S. market-share leader in streaming devices and TV OS but competes against Amazon (Fire TV), Google (Android TV/Google TV), Apple, the Comcast/Charter Xumo JV, and a vertically-integrating Walmart, which acquired Vizio and is folding Vizio's OS into its own products.

## Financial Analysis

The profitability turn is the dominant story, and it is operating-driven rather than financial-engineering.

**Income statement (Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025):**
- Total revenue $1,248.9M, +22% YoY
- Advertising +27%; Subscriptions +30%; Devices -16% (intentional)
- Total gross profit $564.9M, +27%; gross margin expanded to 45% from 43%
- Operating income +$51.8M, versus a -$57.7M loss
- Adjusted EBITDA $148.4M, +165% from $56.0M
- Operating expenses essentially flat (+2%) on 22% revenue growth; stock-based comp declined to $78.7M from $95.5M

The combination of flat opex and falling SBC on 22% top-line growth is the mechanical source of the earnings swing, and it is the strongest evidence that this is durable leverage rather than a transient print.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- TTM free cash flow $538.8M, +81% from $298.4M
- Cash + short-term investments ~$2.38B ($1.65B cash + $730M short-term)
- No funded debt; undrawn $300M revolver; only $39.5M letters of credit outstanding
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow $199.1M

**Capital allocation:** The $400M buyback authorization (August 2025 through December 2026) is the company's first-ever capital return. Roku repurchased $100M (1.01M shares at an average $98.53) in Q1 2026, with $150M remaining at quarter-end. Buying roughly 1% of the float per quarter at $98 — below the current $127 — was accretive and signals a maturing capital-return posture from a formerly cash-burning growth company.

**Tax catalyst:** Roku carries a full valuation allowance against U.S. net deferred tax assets but states there is "a reasonable possibility" of reversing it within twelve months given current and anticipated earnings. A reversal would produce a large one-time non-cash tax benefit and signal durable profitability. We flag it as a 2026 catalyst, but it would inflate GAAP net income optically and should not be confused with operating improvement.

**Earnings track record:** Four consecutive beats, by wide margins (71%, 88%, 83%, 144% over the last four quarters). The beats reflect both conservative guidance and genuine acceleration. This warrants elevated confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 22% with both monetization engines growing faster than the blended rate (Advertising +27%, Subscriptions +30%) is a healthy profile, and the secular tailwind is intact: ad dollars continue migrating from linear TV to connected TV, and Roku sits at the aggregation point.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been revised up +41% over both 30 and 90 days; current-year EPS is up +18.6% (30d) and +23.6% (90d) to $2.41; next-year EPS is up to $3.53. The current-quarter revision velocity is high, but I would temper the strongest interpretation of the +30% revision rule here. That signal is most powerful for inflection stocks that remain cheap relative to the improving estimates. Roku trades at ~36x next-year EPS and ~53x current-year — not at a trough multiple. The revisions support the growth thesis but do not by themselves make the stock cheap, and one segment of the curve (next-quarter EPS, -2.2%/30d) is flat-to-down, suggesting the near-term earnings path is not uniformly accelerating.

The crux of the growth-quality question is advertising. The +59% impression growth against a -14% CPM decline means Roku is winning volume while ceding price. Management attributes this to product and country mix — more programmatic, more international, lower-CPM inventory growing fastest. If CPMs stabilize as the mix matures and Roku's first-party data and self-serve Ads Manager scale, the volume engine compounds into durable advertising profit. If CPM erosion persists, advertising revenue growth decelerates as the impression base saturates, and the bull case loses its primary engine. This is the single metric to watch over the next two prints.

Subscriptions present a different trade-off. Revenue grew 30% but cost of revenue rose 50%, compressing gross margin to 41% from 49% as owned services (Frndly, Howdy) carry direct content and licensing costs that Roku never bore as a pure toll-taker. The pivot builds "Roku-billed" relationships and top-line, but it converts a high-margin distribution business into a lower-margin, content-risk-bearing one.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$127, Roku carries a market cap of ~$16B against trailing EPS of $0.59 (~95x trailing). On forward estimates the picture is less extreme: ~$2.41 current-year EPS (~53x) and ~$3.53 next-year (~36x). Backing out ~$2.4B net cash (~$18/share), the enterprise trades at roughly $109/share, or ~31x next-year EPS — still a growth multiple, not a value multiple.

The analyst price-target distribution (low $95, mean $145, median $150, high $170) sits above the current price, implying ~14-18% upside to consensus mean/median. On a high-momentum stock with rising estimates and a forward P/E below 40x, I would not treat consensus targets as a hard ceiling — analysts typically chase rising estimates upward. But neither is this a sub-30x forward, sub-1.5 PEG setup where the estimate-revision signal overwhelms the multiple. With next-year EPS growth implied at ~46% ($2.41 → $3.53), the PEG is roughly 0.8 on next-year growth, which looks reasonable. The tension is that the ~46% EPS growth is partly mechanical (operating leverage off a low base) and depends on advertising holding its growth rate while CPMs are falling. Pay roughly 36x next-year EPS for that and you are pricing in continued flawless leverage with no CPM-driven deceleration.

I score valuation a 5.5 — not stretched enough to AVOID given the cash backing and growth, but not offering the asymmetric mispricing required to justify chasing after a 74% run. The market is paying a fair price for a confirmed inflection. The asymmetric entry was at $72-98, not $127.

## Competitive Landscape

Roku's independent-platform position is both its strength and its structural vulnerability. As a neutral aggregator unattached to any single content owner, it is a credible neutral party for streaming services and advertisers. But it lacks the content flywheel of an Amazon or Disney and the ad-tech and first-party-data scale of Amazon and Google.

The competitive pressure is intensifying on multiple fronts. Walmart's acquisition of Vizio puts a rival OS inside the largest U.S. retail channel, and Walmart simultaneously remains one of Roku's device-distribution partners, so a key channel is now also a vertical competitor. Amazon's DSP and first-party retail purchase data are formidable in the same CTV ad budgets Roku is competing for, and the CPM decline is at least partly a symptom of this competition plus industry-wide inventory expansion (every SVOD now sells an ad tier). On content, the newly emphasized disclosure that the top three streaming services (excluding The Roku Channel) account for nearly half of all streaming hours, combined with Roku's lack of access to YouTube ad inventory, highlights both engagement concentration and a meaningful inventory gap.

Roku's counter-strategy — Roku Ads Manager (self-serve/SMB), programmatic partnerships, owned subscriptions, and leveraging home-screen and first-party viewing data — is coherent but unproven as a durable pricing-power offset. Trajectory is mixed: share leadership and scale are improving, but pricing power is under visible pressure. I score competitive position a 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Advertising pricing power (the central risk).** The +59% impressions / -14% CPM split is the crux of the bear case. Persistent CPM erosion would convert a 27%-growth advertising engine into a decelerating one as the impression base saturates. Monitor over the next two prints.

2. **Subscriptions margin compression.** Cost of revenue +50% vs. revenue +30%; gross margin down to 41% from 49% as owned content (Frndly, Howdy) adds direct costs and content-acquisition risk.

3. **Customer and channel concentration.** "Customer J" (an unnamed advertising counterparty, likely a major DSP) was 11% of total Q1 revenue; Customers J and B were each 13% of net receivables. On Devices, Amazon, Best Buy, Target, and Walmart together represented 86% of segment revenue — heavy retail dependence, with Walmart now also a competitor.

4. **Governance.** Founder Anthony Wood is Chairman, CEO, and President, controlling a majority of voting power via 10-votes/share Class B stock while owning only ~11.5% economically. CFO Dan Jedda also serves as COO. The proxy explicitly notes the company "did not use any financial performance measures" in its pay-vs-performance disclosure. This entrenches management and limits Class A holders' influence.

5. **Tariff exposure on Devices.** Components (memory chips, SoCs from sole-source suppliers) sourced from China, Taiwan, Mexico, Vietnam, and Thailand pressure the already-negative Devices margin.

These are largely company-specific risks that are partially but not fully priced. The cash position and confirmed profitability mitigate financial risk, but the binary on CPM and the governance overhang keep the risk score at 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 56.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 44.6% (an 11.7-point spread, 73rd percentile within the chain), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty rather than complacency. Put/call skew is balanced (+2.5%) and the put/call volume (0.55) and OI (0.48) ratios are call-tilted, so positioning is not bearish. The term structure is flat (near 56.3% ≈ far 54.5%) with no concentrated event hump, consistent with uncertainty distributed evenly rather than a single dated catalyst. The implied ±24.5% six-month move and a -4.0% six-month put skew suggest the market sees a wider, slightly downward-tilted distribution further out. None of this contradicts the fundamental view; it reinforces that the stock is priced for two-sided outcomes, supporting a HOLD rather than a chase.

### Insider Signal

Heavy, broad-based executive selling occurred March-May 2026 — Wood, Collier (President, Roku Media), Jedda (CFO/COO), and Fuchsberg (President, Subscriptions) — but essentially all of it is option-exercise-and-sell or pre-planned 10b5-1 activity (Wood adopted a new 10b5-1 plan in February 2026 for 402,000 shares). Co-occurring with record results and rapidly rising estimates, this reads as routine diversification into strength rather than a distress signal. That said, the complete absence of open-market buying, combined with the volume of selling near 52-week highs, removes any insider tailwind from the bull case and contributes to the Management Quality score of 5.0.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$160-170)

Roku has crossed into durable profitability with strong operating leverage that compounds as advertising and subscriptions scale on a near-flat cost base. CPMs stabilize as international/programmatic mix matures and first-party data plus Ads Manager build pricing power; the +59% impression engine converts into accelerating advertising profit. The deferred-tax-asset valuation allowance reverses, signaling sustained profitability. Continued buybacks shrink the float. On ~$4.20+ EPS in FY27 at ~38-40x, the stock reaches $160-170, matching the Street high.

### Bear Case (~$90-95)

CPM erosion persists as CTV inventory oversupply and Amazon/Google ad-tech scale commoditize Roku's impressions; advertising growth decelerates as the base saturates. Subscriptions margin keeps compressing under owned-content costs. Walmart/Vizio and Fire TV erode device and OS share. Multiple compresses toward 25x on ~$3.50 EPS as the growth premium fades, taking the stock to $90-95 (roughly the analyst low and the buyback-support zone).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment timeline: next two quarterly prints (Q2 2026, late July; Q3 2026, late October), where CPM and segment margins resolve the central tension.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Advertising CPM (average price per impression) stabilizes or returns to growth in the next one to two prints while impression volume growth stays double-digit.
- A pullback toward $95-105 that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged or improving fundamentals.
- Deferred-tax-asset valuation allowance reversal accompanied by continued operating-income expansion (not just the one-time benefit).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CPM decline accelerates beyond -14% YoY or advertising revenue growth decelerates below ~15% YoY.
- Subscriptions gross margin compresses below ~38% or operating income reverses on rising opex.
- Material device/OS share loss to Walmart/Vizio or Fire TV that pressures Streaming Household growth.

## Conclusion

Roku has converted a perpetual cash-burner into a profitable, free-cash-flow-generative platform with a strong balance sheet and a maturing capital-return program. The FY2025 first profit and the Q1 2026 acceleration are operating-driven and well-evidenced. The stock has re-rated to reflect that.

At ~36x next-year EPS, the market is paying a fair price for a confirmed inflection, leaving risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. Whether advertising pricing power stabilizes or continues to erode sits between the bull and bear cases and will be answered in the next two prints. The cash backing, four-quarter beat streak, and rising estimates support a constructive stance; the CPM trajectory, subscriptions-margin trade-off, intensifying competition, and governance concentration argue against chasing here. We rate ROKU a HOLD with a growth lean and would become buyers on CPM stabilization or a pullback toward $95-105.
