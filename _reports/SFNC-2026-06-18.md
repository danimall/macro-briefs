**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Simmons First National is a ~$24.7B-asset Mid-South regional bank whose headline 2025 GAAP loss masks a deliberate balance-sheet repositioning that has materially improved go-forward earnings power. The reported trailing EPS of -$2.96 and the resulting negative trailing P/E are accounting artifacts of a one-time $625.6M after-tax securities loss taken in Q3 2025, not operating deterioration. Management reclassified ~$3.59B of held-to-maturity securities to available-for-sale, sold ~$3.16B (amortized cost) of low-yielding paper, and used the proceeds, alongside a $327.4M equity raise and $325M of subordinated notes, to retire high-cost wholesale and brokered funding. The payoff is visible: net interest margin (FTE) expanded 89 bps year-over-year to 3.84% in Q1 2026, net interest income (FTE) grew 17.9%, and quarterly EPS recovered to $0.47 from $0.26 a year earlier.

The forward case rests on this margin recovery proving durable. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 7.1% over 90 days for the current year ($1.93 → $2.07) and 6.6% for next year ($2.06 → $2.19), a positive-momentum signal. Management guided to the high end of 9-11% NII growth for 2026 and is willing to let CET1 drift from 11.58% toward ~10.5%, implying capital return through the new $175M buyback. The stock has recovered ~20% over the trailing year and now trades at $21.71, near its 52-week high of $22.62 and at roughly 10.5x the current-year consensus.

The constraints keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Credit is migrating in the wrong direction (nonaccruals up to $141.2M from $111.8M, construction & development nonaccrual more than doubling to $39.4M); a specific IRS risk could recharacterize the repositioning loss with material tax consequences; the CEO transition from the Makris family to Jay Brogdon is incomplete; the bank is now modestly liability-sensitive to Fed cuts (-2.08% NII on a 100bps cut); and parent-level dividend capacity is constrained by regulatory approval requirements. The valuation no longer offers asymmetric upside after the recovery; most of the margin re-rating is priced. We would add toward tangible-book support, not at the high.

## Company Overview

Simmons First National Corporation is the parent of Simmons Bank, an Arkansas state-chartered institution operating since 1903 and headquartered in Pine Bluff. As of March 31, 2026 the company held ~$24.7B in total assets and operated ~221 financial centers across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. It is managed as a single reportable segment (community and commercial banking), with wealth management and insurance reported under "Other."

SFNC has grown primarily through acquisition, which shows up in a heavy intangibles load: $1.32B of goodwill plus $81M of other intangibles. The result is a wide gap between stated book value per share ($23.70) and tangible book value per share ($14.03). At $21.71, the stock trades below stated book but at roughly 1.55x tangible book.

The loan portfolio of $17.93B is real-estate-concentrated: ~78% real estate (commercial real estate $8.76B, construction & development $2.62B, single-family residential $2.57B), ~16% commercial and agricultural, ~5% other (mortgage warehouse, municipal), and ~1.5% consumer and credit card. Deposits totaled $20.20B with core deposits at 83.5% and brokered deposits of $1.91B.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 securities repositioning is the central event. The mechanics: a Q3 2025 reclassification of ~$3.59B HTM securities to AFS, followed by the sale of ~$3.16B (amortized cost) of low-yielding AFS holdings, producing a pretax loss of ~$801.5M and an after-tax loss of $625.6M at the realized 21.95% tax rate. This drove a FY2025 GAAP net loss (net income of approximately -$379.6M per the proxy's pay-versus-performance table), while adjusted earnings remained positive throughout. The trailing EPS of -$2.96 and negative trailing P/E in the data set are direct consequences and should not be read as ongoing performance.

The trade was financed to protect capital. SFNC raised $327.4M net through an 18.65M-share offering at $18.50 on July 23, 2025 (lifting share count from ~125.8M to ~145M) and issued $325M of 6.25% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes due 2035 (Tier 2 capital). Proceeds retired high-cost wholesale, brokered, and public-fund deposits plus FHLB advances. The mechanism is straightforward: selling sub-2% legacy securities and using the cash to extinguish 4-5%+ wholesale funding lifts the net interest margin even though it crystallizes the embedded loss.

The margin result validates the trade. NIM (FTE) reached 3.84% in Q1 2026 versus 2.95% a year earlier (+89 bps) and 3.81% in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 net income was $68.5M ($0.47 diluted EPS) versus $32.4M ($0.26) in Q1 2025, though down sequentially from $78.1M ($0.54) in Q4 2025. Annualizing the Q1 run-rate gives roughly $1.88, against the rising current-year consensus of $2.07.

Capital and liquidity are sound: CET1 11.58%, Tier 1 leverage 10.14%, total risk-based 14.36%, TCE/TA 8.74%. The dividend is $0.2150/quarter (+1% YoY, ~3.9% yield), and a $175M buyback was authorized in January 2026 (unused in Q1).

The lingering balance-sheet drag is the remaining AFS book: $397.9M of gross unrealized losses (AOCI of -$314.4M), with 82.6% of AFS holdings in an unrealized loss position. These are rate-driven, not credit-driven, and are added back for regulatory capital, but they continue to suppress GAAP equity until rates fall or the securities mature.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 missing by 1.8% ($0.47 vs $0.48 estimate). This argues for moderate rather than high confidence in the upper end of guidance.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is margin- and balance-sheet-led, not volume-led, though loan growth is contributing. Loans grew $440.7M QoQ in Q1 2026 (growth in CRE, C&I, and mortgage warehouse), and the commercial pipeline stood at $1.56B. CEO Brogdon framed the strategy around multi-year "quality growth."

The estimate-revision trend is the strongest forward signal here. Current-year consensus rose from $1.93 to $2.07 over 90 days (+7.1%), next-year from $2.06 to $2.19 (+6.6%), with 30-day revisions still positive (+2.7% and +3.2%). This consistent, broad-based upward revision is positive earnings momentum that has not yet fully re-rated the multiple. It does not reach the +30% threshold that would flag a high-conviction BUY signal; this is a steady recovery.

The durability question turns on rates. After deleveraging the balance sheet and paying down funding, SFNC is now modestly liability-sensitive to cuts: a -100bps move reduces NII by ~2.08% (-3.70% at -200bps), versus only +0.97% on a +100bps move. In a 2026 easing scenario, part of the 89bps margin gain reverses as asset yields reprice faster than the remaining deposit base. The asset-sensitive tailwind that drove the recovery has flipped to a modest headwind if the Fed cuts.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are uninformative given the one-time loss, so valuation must be done on forward and book metrics. At $21.71 against current-year consensus EPS of $2.07, SFNC trades at ~10.5x forward earnings, a discount to the regional-bank peer median P/E of 15.9x (BOH 15.9x, CBU 15.9x, BANC 16.0x, PRK 16.3x), though closer to the cheaper PFS at 9.9x. The dividend yield of ~3.9% sits above the peer median of 2.9%.

The forward P/E discount is real but partly earned. The peer comparison must be adjusted for: (1) the goodwill-heavy balance sheet, which makes SFNC's 1.55x tangible-book valuation less of a bargain than the sub-book stated valuation implies; (2) credit migration that peers are not all showing; (3) the IRS tax-recharacterization tail; and (4) asymmetric downside to rate cuts. A modest discount to peers is warranted, not a 30%+ gap.

Analyst price targets bracket the stock at $23.00 low to $25.00 high (mean $23.57, median $23.00), implying ~6-15% upside from current levels. These are useful as a sanity check rather than as floors; SFNC is not the cheap-relative-to-rapidly-rising-estimates profile where consensus targets reliably lag. The estimate revisions are positive but moderate, and the stock already sits near its 52-week high.

The valuation score reflects a key discipline: a compressed multiple alone is not a buy signal, and "cheap" regional banks with credit migration and rate asymmetry have a poor track record of converting that cheapness into outperformance. The market is mispricing some of the improved earnings power (TSR of 105 lagged the peer group's 153 in FY2025), but a meaningful portion of the recovery is now in the price after the ~20% trailing-year move. The re-rating case requires two-to-three more quarters of clean margin and stable credit before the discount closes. We assign a modestly below-neutral valuation score: the discount exists but is largely justified, and there is no asymmetric upside at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

SFNC competes against national, regional, and community banks, credit unions, fintechs, and nonbank lenders across its six-state Mid-South and Great Plains footprint. The 2025 repositioning is a standard post-2022 regional-bank maneuver: crystallize legacy securities losses, redeploy into higher-yielding assets and cheaper funding, accept a one-time GAAP hit for structurally higher forward NIM. The 89bps margin expansion confirms SFNC executed the trade competently.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly widening. The bank is growing loans faster than the funding base and reported a $1.56B pipeline, but it operates in a fragmented, competitive deposit market with no obvious structural moat beyond a long-established Arkansas franchise and acquisition-built scale. The TSR underperformance (105 vs 153 peer) suggests the market views the franchise as a middling regional rather than a share-gainer. There is no evidence of accelerating share capture or a widening moat; equally, there is no evidence of erosion. This is a competent operator in a commoditized business, which supports a neutral competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration is the most monitorable near-term risk.** Total nonaccruals rose to $141.2M from $111.8M, with construction & development nonaccrual jumping to $39.4M from $17.5M, driven largely by a single $26.9M (described as well-collateralized) construction relationship. NPLs reached 0.79% of loans (up from 0.64%). With 78% of the book in real estate and $2.62B in C&D, this concentration is the live credit vector. The ACL/loans ratio of 1.28% and ACL/NPLs coverage of 162% provide a cushion, and annualized net charge-offs of 0.21% remain low, but the direction of nonaccruals warrants close watching against the broader CRE stress backdrop.

**The IRS tax-recharacterization tail is the cleanest idiosyncratic risk.** Management's own 10-K flags that the IRS could reject ordinary-loss treatment on the repositioning loss, which "would have a material adverse effect" through additional taxes. This is specific, SFNC-only, and not obviously priced.

**Rate asymmetry tilts downside.** After deleveraging, NII is more exposed to Fed cuts (-2.08% on -100bps) than to hikes (+0.97% on +100bps). An easing cycle erodes part of the margin recovery that drives the bull case.

**Parent liquidity is constrained.** Simmons Bank had only $133.6M of undivided profits at March 31, 2026, none upstreamable to the parent without regulatory approval, and management admits recently needing such approval. This pressures the parent's ability to fund the dividend and buyback.

**Other risks:** geographic concentration in six states including New Madrid Seismic Zone exposure; $1.32B goodwill carrying impairment risk (none indicated); newly expanded AI/fraud risk disclosures; and a prior-year $4.3M commercial deposit-fraud event.

### Options Market Signal

The reported ATM implied volatility jump from 147.1% to 402.1% is almost certainly a thin-options data artifact for a small-cap regional bank rather than a genuine signal, and should be treated as non-informative. The put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.00 to 0.29, which is modest and not indicative of unusual bearish positioning. Short interest is low at 4.6% of float (5.3 days to cover), confirming this is not a crowded short and that informed investors are not aggressively positioned against the stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($25-27):** The repositioning proves durable: NIM holds near 3.84% or expands further, 2026 NII growth hits the high end of guidance, and credit migration stabilizes around the single identified construction relationship rather than broadening. Loan growth runs off the $1.56B pipeline, the $175M buyback supports per-share metrics, and the CEO transition executes cleanly. Consensus estimates continue rising toward $2.20+ for 2027, and the forward P/E re-rates from 10.5x toward the low-teens peer range. At 12.5x $2.19 next-year EPS, the stock reaches ~$27.

**Bear Case ($16-17):** Credit deteriorates beyond the single construction loan as CRE/C&D stress broadens, forcing higher provisions that consume the margin gains. The Fed begins cutting, eroding the now-liability-sensitive NIM. The IRS challenges the loss treatment, triggering a material tax charge. The stock de-rates toward tangible book ($14.03) plus a thin premium, in the $16-17 range, a ~22% decline.

**Base Case ($23):** Margin holds, NII grows ~10%, credit migrates modestly but charge-offs stay contained, the buyback runs, and the stock re-rates partway toward peers at ~11x current-year EPS. This aligns with the analyst median of $23.00 and implies ~6% upside plus the ~3.9% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-declining nonaccruals (back below ~$120M) confirming the C&D migration was idiosyncratic, combined with NIM holding ≥3.80%.
- A pullback toward tangible-book support ($16-18) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Resolution of the IRS tax-treatment question in SFNC's favor, removing the tail risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Nonaccruals rising above ~$180M or net charge-offs accelerating above ~0.50% annualized, signaling broadening CRE/C&D credit problems.
- A reserve build or guidance cut that reverses the positive estimate-revision trend.
- An IRS recharacterization of the repositioning loss producing a material tax charge.
- Sequential NIM compression below ~3.60% as a Fed easing cycle erodes the margin gain.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected mid-July). Monitor the credit-migration trend and NIM trajectory at each print, and track Fed policy given the liability-sensitive profile.

## Conclusion

SFNC is a competently managed regional bank that executed a sensible balance-sheet repositioning, trading a one-time loss for structurally higher margin and earnings power. The Q1 2026 recovery, NIM expansion, and 90-day consensus revisions are genuine positives that the market has only partly rewarded, leaving a ~10.5x forward multiple against a 15.9x peer median. The forward growth thesis is real but moderate, supported by rising estimates and a $1.56B pipeline.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of asymmetric upside at the current price plus three real overhangs: credit migration in the C&D book, the IRS tax-recharacterization tail, and the now-liability-sensitive rate profile that turns a Fed easing cycle into a headwind. The stock sits near its 52-week high after the recovery, the dividend is supported but parent-liquidity-constrained, and the CEO transition is unfinished. The growth-hold thesis is to own the recovery for the dividend and the modest re-rating toward peers, while waiting for either a pullback toward tangible book or two clean quarters of credit data to justify adding. We rate SFNC **HOLD** with a $23 base-case target and would upgrade on weakness or on confirmation that the credit migration was contained.
