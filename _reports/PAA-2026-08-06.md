## Executive Summary

Plains All American has done what it said it would do. The C$5.15B (~US$3.75B) sale of Plains Midstream Canada ULC to Keyera closed in May 2026 — confirmed by PAA's 8-K under Items 1.02/2.01 on 2026-05-12 and independently by Keyera's own June 15 business update — with expected net proceeds of roughly $3.3B earmarked against the $1,099M term loan and $1,086M of commercial paper. Applying those proceeds to total debt of $11,376M takes simple leverage from approximately 3.95x attributable Adjusted EBITDA to roughly 2.8-3.1x, and retires the two highest-cost, floating-rate tranches on the balance sheet. At a blended ~4.5%, that is $96-149M of annual interest saved against roughly $1.85B of distributable cash available to common units — a 5-8% accretion to per-unit cash flow that requires no incremental barrel to materialize.

The operating business is also growing. Permian crude pipeline tariff volumes, including PAA's proportionate share of equity-method systems, reached 7,774 kbpd in Q1 2026, up 13% from 6,869 kbpd; total tariff volumes rose 10% to 10,039 kbpd. Management raised FY2026 attributable Adjusted EBITDA guidance by $130M to $2.88B on the May 8 call, then raised growth capital guidance to $400-450M from ~$350M on June 15. Q1 operating income rose 13.8% to $405M even as GAAP net income attributable to PAA fell 65.7% to $152M — the latter driven almost entirely by a $(103)M discontinued-operations loss on Canadian tax charges and asset-sale losses, plus the absence of a prior-year $31M non-monetary gain.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of price and rate risk. The units are up 30.5% over twelve months and sit 7.4% below the 52-week high of $25.03; consensus mean target of $24.47 implies 5.6% price upside. Annual consensus EPS has moved the wrong way recently — current-year down 3.6% and next-year down 2.6% over 30 days, even though next-year is up 7.6% over 90 days. And the FY2025 10-K names the specific mechanism by which 13% volume growth need not become revenue growth: third-party shippers "generally do not have long-term contractual commitments to ship crude oil on our pipelines," the Permian and Eagle Ford are characterized as overbuilt, and leveraged competitors "may be motivated to reduce transportation rates to levels approaching variable operating costs, without regard to whether they are generating an acceptable return." Distribution coverage is already being deliberately compressed — 1.46x in Q1 2026 versus 1.73x a year earlier — as management raised the per-unit rate 9.9% into a quarter of 6.7% lower DCF available to common.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on PAA, with the composite score drifting from 5.9 (June 3) to 5.5. The direction is defensible: financial health has genuinely improved on the confirmed close and quantified interest savings, while growth and risk have deteriorated as the shrinking asset base, flat-to-negative annual revisions, and a $40M/quarter higher interest run-rate work through the numbers. The units are worth holding for the covered yield. They are not worth chasing at $23.17.

## Company Overview

PAA is a Delaware master limited partnership formed in 1998 that gathers, transports, stores, and markets crude oil and NGLs. Following the Canadian reorganization, continuing operations run on two reportable segments — Crude Oil and NGL — with the divested Canadian NGL business in discontinued operations.

The Crude Oil segment is the business. It produced $12,548M of segment revenue (including intersegment) and $582M of segment Adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026. The retained U.S. NGL stub posted $(7)M of segment Adjusted EBITDA versus $(5)M a year earlier — negative in what is seasonally the strongest quarter for frac-spread economics. Post-divestiture, PAA is functionally a Permian-anchored crude oil midstream company with a small loss-making NGL appendage, which is a narrower business than the two-segment presentation implies.

Three structural features matter more than the asset list.

**Minority interests are large.** PAA consolidates but does not own outright its most important growth assets: 65% of the Plains Oryx Permian Basin JV, 70% of Cactus II, 67% of Red River. Noncontrolling interests carried $3,219M of partners' capital at 3/31/26, absorbed $122M of Q1 Adjusted EBITDA, and took $103M of cash distributions in the quarter. Consolidated headline metrics overstate what accrues to unitholders by roughly 14-15% at the EBITDA line.

**A merchant book sits on top of the pipelines.** Product sales of $12,026M against $11,493M of purchases in Q1 is a ~4.4% gross spread on an enormous gross-up — roughly $50B of annualized revenue against ~$3.37B of total Adjusted EBITDA. Commodity derivative gains in earnings swung from $(3)M in Q1 2025 to $+89M in Q1 2026. The "toll road" framing that appears in retail coverage is only half right: the tariff business is a toll road, the supply-and-logistics book is not.

**Three-tier MLP governance.** The non-economic GP interest sits with PAA GP LLC, whose sole member is Plains AAP, L.P. (holder of ~233.0M PAA common units, roughly 31% of common plus Series A combined); Plains GP Holdings (PAGP) owns ~85% of the LP interest in AAP. PAA reimburses all direct and indirect expenses of the Plains Entities other than PAGP income taxes, with the GP exercising sole discretion over the amount. Officers are employed by GP LLC, not by PAA.

The GAAP headline is noise; the operating line is the signal. Operating income up 13.8% against a 65.7% decline in net income attributable to PAA is reconciled by the discontinued-operations loss and the absence of a prior-year non-monetary gain. Likewise, Adjusted Free Cash Flow "improving" from $(308)M to $+82M is not organic — acquisition spending fell from $624M to $88M.

**Interest expense is the real deterioration.** Net interest rose 31.5% and cash interest paid rose 43% ($183M vs $128M), the direct cost of a 2025 acquisition program funded with $3.0B of new senior notes and commercial paper. This is precisely what the divestiture proceeds address.

**Debt composition (3/31/26):** $11,376M total — senior notes net $9,120M (fair value ~$8.9B, i.e. trading below carrying value as rates rose), commercial paper $1,086M, term loan net $1,099M at a 4.8% weighted average, other $71M. Variable-rate exposure was ~$2,185M at an average 4.4%. The Series B preferred floats at 3M SOFR + 4.11% + 0.26161%, with a disclosed ±$8M annual sensitivity per 100bp.

Note for anyone screening this name: standardized data returns "long-term debt" of $1,578M, which is only the "Other long-term debt, net" caption, and equity/assets of 0.0 from partnership-capital tagging. Screened leverage understates PAA by roughly 7x and equity-based distress models are unusable here. The debt footnote itself is complete and clear.

**Deleveraging arithmetic.** $11,376M ÷ $2.88B = ~3.95x. Applying ~$3.3B of net proceeds: ~$8,076M ÷ $2.88B = ~2.8x, or ~3.1x if the retained-business EBITDA base is closer to $2.6B ex-Canada. The divestiture is worth 0.8-1.2 turns. PAA's covenant-defined ratio will differ from this arithmetic; treat it as directional.

**Distribution and coverage.** The declared quarterly rate is $0.4175 ($1.67 annualized, 7.21% at $23.17), reconstructed from cash-flow totals: Q1 2026 paid $295M ÷ 706M units = $0.4178 versus Q1 2025 $267M ÷ 704M = $0.3793, a 9.9% increase. Coverage: $431M ÷ $295M = 1.46x, down from $462M ÷ $267M = 1.73x. On a full-year basis, roughly $1.85B available to common against ~$1,179M of distributions is ~1.57x. Coverage at 1.46-1.57x is comfortable by midstream standards; the direction is what to watch, because management raised the rate into declining DCF and is relying on the interest saving to backfill.

**Liquidity tightened.** Total liquidity of $1,779M at 3/31/26, down from over $2.0B at year-end; the working capital deficit widened to $380M from $198M. Gross commercial paper borrowings were $26.5B against $26.4B of repayments in Q1, versus $12.9B/$12.8B a year earlier — the CP book churned at double the prior-year rate as higher crude prices inflated working capital (receivables +$1,223M, payables +$1,472M sequentially). The net balance is modest and the gross-up is largely price-driven rather than collection deterioration, but the 10-K explicitly ties CP access and supplier trade credit to maintaining investment-grade ratings. That is a self-reinforcing dependency worth respecting in a stress scenario.

One further cash item: the pre-closing contribution of Canadian crude assets to newly formed Plains Canada Liquid Pipelines ULC created $216M of current income tax expense with an approximately offsetting $215M deferred benefit, plus $77M of deferred tax expense in discontinued operations. Cash taxes paid in Q1 were only $28M, so the $216M is largely a future obligation. It should be absorbed in the ~$3.3B "net proceeds after taxes and expenses" figure, but a reader watching only the tax line would see almost nothing.

**Financial health: 6.4.** Investment grade from all three agencies, 1.5x coverage, a confirmed 0.8-1.2 turn deleveraging with quantified interest savings, offset by a widened working capital deficit, lower liquidity, doubled CP churn, a $216M future cash tax, and a debt load whose absolute size (3.95x pre-paydown, ~2.8x post) leaves no room for a coverage-plus-leverage double miss.

## Growth Analysis

Volumetric growth is real and confirmed in the filings. Permian tariff volumes +13%, total +10%, with secondary systems materially smaller and mixed (South Texas/Eagle Ford 514 kbpd, Mid-Continent 475 kbpd, "Other" 1,276 kbpd and down 3%). Roughly half of 2026 investment capital is directed to Permian JV assets, which means PAA funds the growth but shares 35% of the resulting EBITDA and cash with the minority partner.

Guidance moved twice in five weeks: FY2026 attributable Adjusted EBITDA midpoint raised $130M to $2.88B (NGL timing, Cactus III optimization, captured crude opportunities), then growth capex raised to $400-450M from ~$350M with maintenance held at ~$185M. A capex raise on an improving macro read is a genuine forward signal, though it also defers free cash flow.

Consensus tells a more restrained story. Current-year EPS of $1.73 and next-year $1.93 imply 11.6% earnings growth, but a large share of that step-up is the mechanical interest saving rather than incremental operating income. Revision direction is mixed and net negative at the annual level: current quarter +16.9% over 30 days and +18.7% over 90 days, but current-year -3.6%/-0.6% and next-year -2.6%/+7.6%. The near-quarter upgrade against annual downgrades is consistent with timing shifts and a strong crude tape in the quarter rather than a durable upgrade cycle. This is not the >30% revision inflection that would justify overriding valuation discipline.

The structural cap on growth is rate rather than volume. The 10-K's own language — overbuilt basins, competitors pricing toward variable cost, no long-term commitments from most third-party shippers — describes the mechanism by which throughput records coexist with flat or falling per-barrel economics as legacy long-haul contracts signed in a tighter market roll to current terms. Two second-order constraints reinforce this: Permian crude growth is hostage to someone else's natural gas takeaway capex, because associated gas must find a home for oil wells to flow; and a 2017 Tenth Circuit ruling bars condemnation of tribal fractional allotment interests, a hard ceiling on route flexibility in certain corridors.

Remaining performance obligations of $3,374M ($481M rest-of-2026, $579M 2027, $482M 2028, and $1,263M thereafter) should not be read as backlog. The disclosure explicitly excludes legacy shippers without MVCs, acreage dedications, buy/sell arrangements, short-term contracts, ASC 842 leases, ASC 815 derivatives, and — importantly — MVCs on joint-venture pipeline systems, which is where half the 2026 capital is going. Low RPO is not evidence of weak commercial support, but it does mean the contracted-revenue disclosure covers a narrow slice of a ~$50B revenue base.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Strong volumes and a raised guide against a shrinking asset base, negative annual revision drift, a loss-making retained NGL stub, and rate risk that could neutralize volume gains.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported trailing and "forward" P/E of 20.87x are the same number and both rest on trailing GAAP EPS of $1.116 — depressed by the $(103)M discontinued-operations loss and a $43M charge against income attributable to common from the Series A repurchase above carrying value. Neither is a usable forward multiple.

On consensus, $23.17 is **13.4x FY2026 ($1.73) and 12.0x FY2027 ($1.93)**.

**Enterprise value (derived):** common 705.5M × $23.17 = $16,347M, plus Series A carrying value $1,249M, Series B $787M, total debt $11,376M, less cash $171M, plus NCI at carrying value $3,219M = **~$32.8B**. Against total Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $3.37B (attributable $2.88B plus ~$490M annualized NCI share using Q1's $122M) that is **~9.7x**. Applying ~$3.3B of proceeds to debt takes EV to ~$29.5B, or **~8.7-9.2x** depending on the retained-business EBITDA base. NCI is at carrying rather than market value, so this is an approximation.

**On distributable cash:** ~$1.85B available to common on 706M units is $2.62/unit; adding ~$120M of mid-range interest saving gives ~$2.79/unit. At $23.17 that is 8.8x current and 8.3-8.8x pro forma DCF per unit — the middle of the historical large-cap MLP band of roughly 7-11x.

**Peer context.** The supplied comparables are heterogeneous: OVV 19.2x, PR 22.4x, APA 8.6x and DINO 10.6x are E&Ps and a refiner with fundamentally different earnings duration; only DTM at 31.1x is a midstream comparable, and it is a gas-focused, fully contracted business that legitimately commands a premium to a partnership with a merchant book. PAA's yield of 7.2% is 4.6 points above the 2.6% peer median, which reflects the MLP structure and payout policy rather than distress. The peer table cannot settle PAA's multiple; the DCF-yield frame is more informative.

**Is the market wrong?** Partly, and not by much. The identifiable catalyst is quantified: $96-149M of annual interest saving, worth 5-8% of DCF available to common, mechanically supporting continued high-single-digit distribution growth even with flat EBITDA. That is a real, non-speculative source of per-unit value. But it is also visible — UBS reiterated Buy with a $25 target explicitly on the back of the June capex and macro disclosure, and the units have already re-rated 30.5% over twelve months to within 7.4% of the high. The consensus mean of $24.47 implies 5.6% price upside; the median of $25.00 implies 7.9%. On a stock at 12-13x forward with modest organic growth, analyst targets are not a stale floor — they are roughly where the DCF math lands.

Against that, quality of earnings argues for a discount rather than a premium to a pure toll road: a 4.4% gross spread on $50B of annualized product sales, a $92M quarter-over-quarter swing in derivative results, long-term inventory carried at $89.55/bbl versus $57.20 a year earlier, and a derivative book running net short 9.5 MMbbl through December 2029.

**Valuation: 5.4.** Not expensive, with genuine yield support and a quantified accretion catalyst, but no meaningful margin of safety at 8.3-8.8x DCF within 8% of the high and 5.6% below the consensus mean.

## Competitive Landscape

PAA's advantages are scale and integration in the Permian — gathering into long-haul into terminals — and investment-grade ratings that unlock open trade credit from suppliers and support contango/storage merchant activity. Both are real and difficult to replicate.

The disclosed vulnerability is commercial rather than physical. Most third-party shippers have no long-term commitment, with an exception carved out for certain more recently constructed long-haul assets. In a basin the company itself calls overbuilt, alongside competitors it says may price toward variable cost, that structure transmits basin-level capacity surplus straight into PAA's realized tariffs at renewal. Volume share can rise while rate per barrel falls. This is the single dynamic most likely to be missed by anyone reading only the throughput statistics.

The Keyera transaction gives one modest positive read on competitive positioning: Keyera called the acquisition transformative, built a 2029 growth outlook around it, and is defending it before the Competition Tribunal. That is consistent with PAA having achieved a good price for a business it decided was non-core.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Durable scale in the right basin, stable-to-improving volume share, offset by weak contractual protection at the rate line and a retained NGL segment that loses money at segment-EBITDA level in its best seasonal quarter.

## Risk Assessment

**Recontracting into an overbuilt Permian (highest-consequence, specifically disclosed).** The mechanism is described above. This is the risk least reflected in a 7.2% yield supported by 1.5x coverage, because it would show up gradually in realized tariffs rather than as a discrete event.

**Post-closing antitrust challenge.** Canadian Press reporting dated 2026-07-16 states that Canada's Competition Bureau has challenged the completed C$5.15B Keyera purchase before the Competition Tribunal, with the parties arguing for dismissal with costs. Stated literally: Keyera is the purchaser and now owns the assets; PAA is the seller and has the cash. Tribunal remedies run principally against the acquirer, so PAA's residual exposure depends on the share purchase agreement's representations, warranties, and indemnities, which have not been read. This is externally reported and absent from the Q1 10-Q (filed 2026-05-08, predating it). Do not treat PAA's exposure as established in either direction.

**Merchant and control risk.** The 10-K carries a risk factor on risk-policy insufficiency and unauthorized trading, noting controls may not detect all violations "particularly if deception, collusion or other intentional misconduct is involved." The derivative book at 3/31/26 was multi-directional: net long 11.4 MMbbl through June 2026, net short time spread 2.9 MMbbl, net crude basis spread 1.6 MMbbl through March 2027, net short 9.5 MMbbl through December 2029; $130M of fair value with ±$20M sensitivity to a 10% price move. NYMEX ranged $56-$105 with a $73 average in Q1 2026 versus $66-$80 and $71 a year earlier. That range is more likely to widen than narrow.

**Uninsured cyber.** Two disclosures read together: PAA self-insures and "does not carry insurance specifically for cybersecurity events," and available insurance markets have contracted with premiums and deductibles up substantially, in part because "we have experienced several incidents in the past." For a physical pipeline operator with a documented incident history, this is an uninsured tail most peers do not disclose in these terms.

**Environmental.** Line 901 is substantially behind the company — ~$870M aggregate estimated cost with only ~$23M of remaining undiscounted liability, remediation complete, no costs recognized in Q1 2026 or 2025, and one remaining "stigma" claim from a landowner on an adjacent pipeline with no trial date. That is a decade-long overhang nearly extinguished. Open: the California State Fire Marshall investigation of the March 2025 Line 48 release (~125 barrels, ~$15M estimated cleanup, $12M incurred, $10M self-insured retention) is not complete and penalties are expressly possible. Other environmental reserves $74M.

**Structural and dilution.** GP removal requires 66⅔% while AAP holds ~31%; a 20% voting cutback applies; the board is classified; a limited call right exists at 80% ownership; units may be issued without unitholder approval. The 58,411,908 Series A preferred units were antidilutive in Q1 and excluded; on conversion, units rise from ~706M to ~764M — an 8.3% increase in units and 7.6% ownership dilution to existing holders. Contingent, not current.

**Reporting items to resolve.** Total Q1 Adjusted EBITDA of $852M exceeds the sum of continuing-operations reportable segments ($582M + $(7)M = $575M) by $277M, which is difficult to square with discontinued operations reporting only $294M of revenue and a $(28)M pre-tax loss. Whether the $2.88B FY2026 guide is on a retained-business basis materially affects every multiple derived from it. Additionally, two officer-change 8-Ks (2026-05-13 under Items 5.02 and 7.01; 2026-06-02 under Item 5.02) remain unread, as does a 2026-06-17 filing under Items 1.01/1.02/2.03 that plausibly relates to credit-facility resizing after the term loan's mandatory prepayment. A senior transition is possible and unverified — Chiang signed the 10-Q as CEO on May 8, five days before the first 5.02.

Finally, the reported earnings-surprise series (Q1 2026 $0.39 actual versus $0.41 estimate, -5.1%; Q4 2025 $0.40 versus $0.41, -3.3%) does not reconcile to GAAP diluted EPS of $0.14 total or $0.29 continuing. The basis is unlabeled on both sides. Two small misses following two beats is at least directionally consistent with the annual revision drift, but the magnitude should not be relied upon.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 54.8% against 19.7% realized, with a 22.0% put/call skew that the data source characterizes as elevated put demand, is drawn from a one-day-to-expiry contract and should be read as quoting mechanics rather than a vol regime change. The term structure beyond that expiry says something different: 20.4% at one month, 19.9% at three, 17.4% at six — at or below realized volatility — with skew of -5.7%, +1.0%, and -4.6% and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.19. Low put open interest and sub-realized longer-dated vol indicate very little hedging demand and no priced catalyst. The IV jump from 22.1% to 54.8% between snapshots compares a longer-tenor quote to a one-day quote and is not evidence of new stress. Short interest of 2.1% of float with 6.6 days to cover confirms an uncrowded, unhedged position. Options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; it mainly says the market expects no imminent shock, which is a mild caution for anyone assuming the recontracting risk is already discounted.

**Risk factors: 5.0.** Multiple specific, unpriced tails — recontracting economics, an uninsured cyber exposure, a live Tribunal proceeding of unquantified residual relevance, an open Fire Marshall investigation, and an active multi-year derivative book — against genuine offsets in Line 901's near-resolution and investment-grade access.

## Management Quality

The team is long-tenured and stable: Chiang (Chairman/President/CEO), Swanson (CFO), Chandler (COO), Goebel (CCO), McGee (GC), Herbold (CAO) — the same six names in Form 4 filings back to 2023. Compensation is delivered overwhelmingly in phantom units, and executives hold large absolute positions in the common units: Chiang ~1.19M units (~$27M at $23.17), Swanson ~602K, McGee ~606K, Goebel ~519K, Chandler ~494K. That is alignment with the security public investors own, not with a separate GP economic interest. The August 2025 vesting cycle struck at $17.78, so retention economics have improved ~30%.

Capital allocation over the past eighteen months reads as coherent portfolio management: acquire Permian exposure (Cactus III in Q4 2025, Cheyenne in Q1 2025, Ironwood in January 2025) funded with $3.0B of senior notes, then sell a non-core Canadian NGL business at a price the buyer defends before a competition tribunal, then apply proceeds to the most expensive debt. Guidance was raised twice in five weeks. The deal-contingent C$4.5B CAD forward worked in PAA's favor, carrying a $61M asset and a $53M Q1 gain.

The negatives are specific rather than general. There are **no open-market insider purchases by any officer or director from 2023 through June 2026** — essentially all Form 4 activity is grants, phantom exercises, and Code F tax withholding. The only recent open-market sale was director Ziemba's 2,346 units at $21.95 on 2026-06-29, taking his reported holding to zero; the $51,495 is immaterial and the zeroing is more consistent with a board departure or cleanup than a value view. MLP insiders typically accumulate through grants, so the absence of buying is weak evidence, but it does not corroborate management conviction at $23 either.

Two items carry more weight. The January 2025 repurchase of 12,678,560 Series A preferred units from director-affiliated holder Gary Petersen at $26.25 ($332.8M) was executed above carrying value, producing a **$43M charge against income attributable to common unitholders**. It simplified the capital structure, cut quarterly Series A distributions from $46M to $36M, and removed 12.7M units of potential dilution — plausibly a fair mutual exit — but it was a related-party transaction at a premium that cost common holders $43M of reported income, and the conflicts-committee process was not reviewed. Separately, $1,317M of related-party notes receivable and $1,317M of notes payable are presented net at zero under a right of offset, with $23M of interest income and $23M of expense each netted from non-GAAP metrics. The earnings effect is genuinely nil and the presentation appears defensible; the practical point is that $1.3B of gross related-party exposure is invisible on the face of the balance sheet, and the most plausible explanations (tax structuring or jurisdictional cash management around the Canadian reorganization) are inferences, not established facts.

Also: $190M of repurchase authorization remains unused, with nothing bought in Q1 2026 or Q1 2025. Prioritizing deleveraging over buybacks at 3.95x leverage is the right call; it does mean the only capital return is the distribution.

**Management quality: 5.3.** Aligned holdings and a coherent deleveraging sequence, offset by zero open-market conviction buying, a $43M related-party charge borne by common holders, a distribution raised ahead of DCF, and two unresolved officer-change filings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $28 (+21% price, ~28% total return)

Permian volumes compound at high single digits as basin activity holds; long-haul contracts roll at flat-to-better rates because PAA's integrated gathering-to-long-haul position makes it the low-cost path to market rather than a marginal capacity holder. The full ~$3.3B goes against the term loan and commercial paper, saving ~$149M annually and taking leverage to ~2.8x — comfortably inside target — which frees the distribution to grow 8-10% per year toward $1.85-1.90 by 2028. Higher crude and wider differentials keep the merchant book earning above trend. Coverage rebuilds toward 1.7x, the market re-rates a de-levered pure-play Permian crude franchise to 10x DCF per unit (~$2.79 pro forma), giving $28. Total return in the high twenties with the distribution.

### Bear Case — $18 (-22%)

Recontracting bites: legacy long-haul tariffs reset lower as overbuilt competitors price toward variable cost, so 10% volume growth converts to flat or declining segment EBITDA. Crude normalizes toward the $60s, compressing the merchant spread, marking down the 3,484 kbbl of inventory carried at $89.55/bbl, and reversing the derivative tailwind that swung $92M in a single quarter. FY2026 attributable EBITDA lands below the $2.88B guide on a retained-business basis. Coverage falls toward 1.2x, the distribution is held flat, and the units de-rate to 6.5x DCF per unit — roughly $18, near the January 2026 level and above the 52-week low of $15.685.

### Base Case — $25 (+7.9% price, ~15% total return with distribution)

Volumes grow, rates are roughly flat, EBITDA lands near guidance, and the interest saving of ~$120M lifts DCF per unit from $2.62 toward $2.79. At 9x pro forma DCF per unit that is $25.1, which coincides with the analyst median of $25.00 and sits between the mean $24.47 and UBS's $25. The distribution grows to roughly $1.78 by mid-2027 with coverage holding near 1.5x.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Reassessment: the Q2 2026 report, due imminently — the first quarter reflecting the post-divestiture capital structure.**

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price below $20.50** (approximately 7.3x pro forma DCF per unit, an 8.1% distribution yield, and the analyst low target) with coverage confirmed at or above 1.5x. That restores roughly 22% upside to the $25 base case plus the yield.
2. **Confirmed post-closing balance sheet at or below 3.0x** with the term loan retired and commercial paper reduced below $400M, plus explicit disclosure that FY2026 guidance of $2.88B is on a retained-business basis. This would remove the largest verification gap in the earnings base and validate the interest-saving arithmetic.
3. **Evidence that long-haul Permian recontracting is being completed at flat or better rates** — segment revenue per barrel stable or rising over two consecutive quarters despite volume growth. This is the specific fact that would convert the toll-road framing from a label into a demonstrated economic property.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Distribution coverage below 1.25x for two consecutive quarters**, or a distribution held flat while growth capex remains at $400-450M — which would signal the deleveraging accretion is being consumed rather than accruing to unitholders.
2. **Crude Oil segment Adjusted EBITDA declining year over year while tariff volumes rise**, the direct signature of rate compression. Q1's $582M is the base.
3. **A ratings agency placing PAA on negative outlook**, given the 10-K's explicit link between investment-grade status, commercial paper access, and supplier trade credit for the merchant book. With $26.5B of gross quarterly CP borrowings, a funding-access impairment would be immediate rather than gradual.

**Monitoring items with no scoring weight until resolved:** the contents of the 2026-05-13 and 2026-06-02 Item 5.02 filings (possible senior transition); the 2026-06-17 Items 1.01/1.02/2.03 filing (probable facility resizing); the $277M reconciliation gap between total and segment Adjusted EBITDA; and Cactus III earnout terms, which should not enter any net-debt calculation until the maximum consideration and performance conditions are known.

## Conclusion

PAA is a competently run, investment-grade Permian crude midstream partnership that has just executed a clean deleveraging transaction and is paying a 7.2% distribution covered roughly 1.5x. The interest saving from applying ~$3.3B against the term loan and commercial paper — $96-149M annually, 5-8% of distributable cash to common — is a mechanical, non-speculative source of per-unit accretion that supports continued high-single-digit distribution growth even if EBITDA goes nowhere. Volumes are growing 10-13%, guidance has been raised twice, and the Line 901 overhang that shaped PAA's cost of capital for a decade is nearly extinguished.

The reasons not to add here are equally concrete. At $23.17 the units trade 8.3-8.8x pro forma DCF per unit and 12.0x next-year consensus EPS, 7.4% below the 52-week high after a 30.5% twelve-month advance, and 5.6% below the consensus mean of $24.47 — an entry point that offers 15% total return in the base case against 22% downside in the bear case, closer to 1:1 than the asymmetry a new position warrants. Annual consensus EPS has drifted down over 30 days. Coverage is being deliberately compressed, from 1.73x to 1.46x, as management raises the distribution ahead of DCF. And the company's own 10-K names the mechanism that could hollow out the volume story: no long-term shipper commitments on most legacy assets, overbuilt basins, and competitors motivated to price toward variable cost. Layer on an uninsured cyber tail, a live Tribunal proceeding whose relevance to PAA is undetermined, an open California investigation, a multi-year merchant derivative book, and two unread officer-change filings, and there are more unresolved binaries here than a 7% yield compensates for at the current multiple.

Hold the units for the covered distribution and the deleveraging arithmetic. Add on price weakness toward $20.50, or on evidence that long-haul recontracting is clearing at flat-to-better rates. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD and the composite has drifted from 5.9 to 5.5 across that period; the trajectory reflects a genuinely improved balance sheet offset by a narrower asset base, softening annual revisions, and a higher price against unchanged rate risk.
