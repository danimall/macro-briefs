## Executive Summary

AMD delivered a decisive Q1 2026 beat ($10.25B revenue, +38% YoY; non-GAAP EPS of $1.37 vs. $1.29 consensus) with Data Center revenue accelerating to +57% YoY at $5.8B. The stock has appreciated significantly over the past year, driven by the OpenAI 6GW partnership (October 2025), Meta 6GW deal (February 2026), and consistent execution on the MI-series GPU ramp. Q2 guidance of ~$11.2B exceeded Street expectations.

The multi-generation purchase agreements with OpenAI and Meta — each structured as 6GW plans with binding initial 1GW commitments and performance-based warrant milestones for the remaining ramp — provide meaningful forward demand signals for a semiconductor company. However, meaningful risks persist: export control escalation could strand inventory and foreclose China revenue, potential warrant dilution of up to approximately 19.6% of outstanding shares (320 million total warrant shares across both deals), and a customer concentration profile that creates dependency on hyperscaler capex cycles.

The stock warrants a BUY at current levels based on the combination of accelerating estimate revisions, structural demand confirmation from multiple hyperscalers, and a forward P/E that remains reasonable relative to the growth rate. The post-earnings move prices in the Q1 beat but not the full implications of the Meta/OpenAI ramps through FY2027-2028.

## Company Overview

AMD operates as a fabless semiconductor company across three segments: Data Center (48% of FY2025 revenue), Client & Gaming (42%), and Embedded (10%). The company designs and sells x86 server/client CPUs (EPYC, Ryzen), data center GPU accelerators (Instinct MI-series), networking solutions (Pensando), gaming/console SoCs, and embedded processors.

Under CEO Lisa Su since 2014, AMD has executed one of the most successful turnarounds in semiconductor history, evolving from a struggling CPU maker to a credible multi-product competitor across AI accelerators, server CPUs, and networking. The company relies on TSMC for advanced node manufacturing and competes primarily with NVIDIA in data center GPUs and Intel in x86 CPUs.

The FY2025-2026 period marks AMD's transition from credible alternative to committed platform partner for hyperscale AI infrastructure, anchored by the OpenAI and Meta multi-gigawatt purchase agreements.

## Growth Analysis

1. **MI450 GPU ramp** — the first product under the OpenAI deal's binding "first gigawatt" commitment. Revenue contribution likely begins mid-to-late FY2026.
2. **Meta deployment** — full ramp contingent on purchase milestones and warrant vesting conditions.

4. **Embedded recovery** — Q1 showed +6% growth after two years of declines; industrial destocking appears complete.
5. **EPYC server CPU gains** — inferencing and agentic AI drive increasing demand for high-performance CPUs and accelerators, supporting continued EPYC growth.

**Estimate Revisions Confirm Momentum:**
- Consensus EPS estimates have risen meaningfully over the past 90 days, reflecting the Q1 beat and raised guidance
- The revision velocity on FY2027 estimates is a strong forward indicator, implying substantial EPS growth from FY2026 to FY2027

**Revenue Visibility:** SEC filings disclose that the binding initial purchase commitment is 1GW for each customer, with the broader 6GW ramps tied to purchase milestones and warrant vesting conditions. Lisa Su's "tens of billions" in data center revenue commentary implies $20B+ annual Data Center segment by FY2027-2028 (vs. $16.6B in FY2025 and a $23B+ Q1 annualized run rate already).

**Structural Tailwinds:**
- Hyperscaler capex commitments validated across multiple quarters
- Supply diversification imperative — even hyperscalers entirely satisfied with NVIDIA architecturally want a second source for negotiating leverage and supply security

AMD's forward multiple compares to peers growing significantly slower. The key question is whether consensus EPS growth projections are achievable — given the announced multi-generation agreements from OpenAI and Meta, the path is clearer than for most semiconductor growth stories.

**Why the Market Could Be Wrong (Bull Case):**
- Consensus EPS estimates could still be conservative if MI450 ramp exceeds expectations and Helios gains traction
- The OpenAI/Meta commitments likely haven't been fully modeled by all analysts given the recent timing
- Warrants create unique customer lock-in that's difficult to value conventionally

**Why the Premium Could Contract (Bear Case):**
- The stock requires consistent 50%+ EPS growth for multiple years — any stumble gets punished severely at these multiples
- Export control escalation could remove China revenue entirely (undisclosed % of Data Center currently)
- High trailing P/E creates headline risk for generalist funds with valuation screens

**Valuation Verdict:** AMD is expensive on trailing metrics but more reasonably priced on a growth-adjusted forward basis given 60%+ expected EPS growth. The market has not fully priced in the multi-year demand commitments from major hyperscalers. The growth rate supports the multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

**vs. NVIDIA (Primary Competition):**
AMD remains a distant #2 in data center GPUs by revenue — NVIDIA's quarterly data center revenue exceeds AMD's annual figure. However, AMD's competitive trajectory is improving:
- OpenAI and Meta deals represent two of the most demanding AI customers validating AMD hardware
- ROCm software ecosystem is maturing (though still lags CUDA)
- Helios rack-scale competes directly with Grace Blackwell NVL72
- The warrant model creates switching costs that partially neutralize CUDA's software moat

**vs. Intel:**
Intel is in early-stage recovery but remains fundamentally weaker in both execution and design. The AMD-Intel collaboration on x86 AI extensions signals the real competitive threat is custom silicon (Broadcom, Google TPU, Amazon Trainium), not each other. AMD continues gaining server CPU share.

**vs. Custom Silicon (Broadcom, Marvell, Google TPU):**
Hyperscalers increasingly develop custom ASICs for specific workloads. AMD counters with rack-scale flexibility (Helios), broad compute portfolio (CPU + GPU + networking in one vendor), and proven general-purpose architecture. The Meta deal specifically calls for "AI-optimized CPUs" alongside GPUs, demonstrating AMD's multi-product advantage.

**Competitive Position Score Rationale:** AMD is gaining share in both data center GPUs and CPUs simultaneously, with validated customer commitments from two of the most important AI infrastructure builders. The trajectory is strongly positive even if absolute market share remains well behind NVIDIA.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Export Controls (HIGH — Potentially Binary):**
The U.S. is drafting regulations requiring approval for nearly all global AI accelerator shipments. Commerce Department staffing collapse creates multi-month delays. AMD already took $440M net inventory charges on MI308 in FY2025 (gross charge of ~$800M, partially reversed after licenses were granted). CFO's refusal to clarify sequential Data Center growth ex-China is concerning. If comprehensive controls materialize, AMD could face material revenue loss — the company's 67% international revenue exposure and undisclosed China-specific AI GPU revenue suggest significant potential impact.

**2. Warrant Dilution (MODERATE — Quantifiable):**
Both the OpenAI and Meta warrants each cover up to 160 million shares at $0.01/share, for a combined 320 million shares (approximately 19.6% of current shares outstanding). Vesting is milestone-based and contingent on purchase commitments scaling to 6GW plus stock-price and technical/commercial conditions. Dilution only fully triggers if AMD wins the business, but the potential magnitude is substantial.

**3. Customer Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH):**
AMD's FY2025 10-K states no customer accounted for at least 10% of consolidated revenue in FY2025 or FY2024. However, with OpenAI and Meta each signed to multi-GW GPU agreements, forward Data Center GPU revenue concentration is likely increasing materially. Any pullback in hyperscaler capex (recession scenario, AI monetization disappointment) would disproportionately hit AMD's growth narrative.

**4. Execution Risk — MI450 and Helios:**
The OpenAI deal's "first gigawatt" depends on MI450 shipping on time and performing competitively. AMD has limited track record shipping at this scale in GPUs. Any delay or performance shortfall could shift commitments.

**5. Inventory Exposure:**
$7.9B inventory + $12.2B purchase commitments create meaningful downside if demand weakens or export controls expand.

## Conclusion

AMD is expensive on trailing metrics but reasonably valued on forward estimates that carry meaningful demand support from announced multi-generation hyperscaler agreements. The combination of estimate revisions, 57% Data Center growth acceleration, and multi-GW agreements from OpenAI and Meta creates a growth profile that justifies a premium multiple. The forward P/E of approximately 41x with 60%+ expected EPS growth offers a growth-adjusted valuation that remains attractive for the semiconductor sector.

The BUY recommendation reflects the weight of evidence: announced demand commitments from OpenAI and Meta (with binding initial 1GW each and conditional 6GW ramps), accelerating estimate revisions, and a strengthening competitive position. The risks — export controls, warrant dilution, customer concentration, and execution at unprecedented GPU scale — are real but either partially priced (China uncertainty) or spread over multi-year timelines (dilution). The stock warrants a 2-3% portfolio allocation with the understanding that volatility will remain elevated (72% near-term IV) and that export control headlines could create significant drawdown windows that represent buying opportunities absent fundamental deterioration.
