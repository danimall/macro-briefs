**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Popular, Inc. is the dominant bank in Puerto Rico, with roughly $75 billion in assets and ~75-80% of its assets, deposits, and revenue concentrated on the island. The investment story over the past year has been a clean earnings recovery: net income rose from a $541M trough in FY2023 to $833M in FY2025 (+36%), and Q1 2026 delivered $245.7M / $3.78 EPS, up 48% year over year on a combination of net interest margin expansion, expense discipline, and a shrinking share count. The stock has responded, returning roughly 45% over the trailing twelve months to ~$151. Four consecutive earnings beats and broadly rising consensus estimates (current-year EPS revised +4.8% over 90 days) confirm the operating momentum is real.

The central question is no longer whether Popular is a good bank; it clearly is, with ROTCE of ~15.5%, a CET1 ratio near 15.9%, and a sticky quasi-utility deposit franchise. The question is whether the current valuation offers asymmetric upside. At ~1.7x tangible book value ($84.98/share) and ~12x trailing earnings, the easy re-rating from a depressed, distrusted Puerto Rico bank to a quality compounder has largely happened. The NIM tailwind that drove the earnings jump is partly mechanical: market-linked Puerto Rico public-sector deposits (~$19.7B, ~30% of total deposits) reprice down as policy rates ease, while a large low-yielding securities book rolls into higher yields. That configuration is favorable but rate-path dependent, and it could flatten or reverse if rates stabilize or the deposit mix shifts.

We rate BPOP a HOLD. The fundamentals are improving and the capital-return program (dividend raised to $0.75/quarter, ~$874M of recent buybacks) provides downside support, which justifies holding existing positions. But new capital deployed here is buying a fully-valued, single-economy bank with a NIM tailwind that may be peaking, executive selling near the highs, and federal-appropriations risk that no model fully captures. The analyst price-target range of $163-$180 implies modest upside, but the system's track record on bank longs at full multiples counsels patience over conviction.

## Company Overview

Popular operates principally through Banco Popular de Puerto Rico (BPPR), the largest bank on the island by assets, deposits, and branch network. The franchise also includes Popular Bank ("Popular U.S."), a mainland community/commercial bank concentrated in the New York, New Jersey, and South Florida metros that serves in part the U.S. Hispanic community, plus insurance, broker-dealer, and asset-management subsidiaries (including Popular Life Re, whose ratings AM Best affirmed in May 2026).

The defining feature of the franchise is incumbency in a concentrated market. After years of industry consolidation, Puerto Rico is served by a small number of large banks, which supports above-peer ROE and a low-cost, sticky deposit base. Popular holds a very large share of Puerto Rico government and public-sector deposits, giving it a quasi-utility funding profile. The mainland operation provides geographic diversification into a higher-growth but more competitive market. The strategic trade-off is that Popular's earnings power, credit quality, and deposit stability are inseparable from the Puerto Rico macro economy, which carries a history of fiscal distress (the PROMESA debt restructuring), demographic decline through out-migration, and exposure to hurricanes and earthquakes.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income progressed from ~$1.10B (FY2022) to the FY2023 trough (elevated provisions and post-pandemic normalization), recovered to $614M in FY2024, and reached the FY2025 level. Q1 2026 net income of $245.7M / EPS $3.78 compared to $177.5M / $2.56 in Q1 2025, a 38% net income gain and 48% EPS gain, the latter amplified by buybacks. Q1 2026 revenue of ~$836M was up roughly 10% YoY.

**Net interest margin is the core story.** NIM expanded to ~3.66% GAAP (~4.14% taxable-equivalent) in Q1 2026, up roughly 26-41 bps YoY. Two structural drivers are at work: repricing-down of the market-linked public-sector deposit book as policy rates ease, and reinvestment of a large, low-yielding securities/Treasury portfolio at higher yields as it rolls over. Funding costs falling while asset yields rise is the source of the earnings inflection. It is also, by construction, dependent on the rate path. If rates stabilize, the down-repricing of deposits slows; if rates back up, the AOCI mark worsens. The current NIM is partly mechanical and possibly near a peak rather than a permanent run-rate.

**Profitability and capital.** ROTCE of ~15.5% in Q1 2026 sits above the bank's ~14% transformation-program target and well above the ~10% 2023-24 trough. CET1 of ~15.9% is far above regulatory minimums, providing substantial room for capital return and a buffer against credit normalization. Tangible book value per share is ~$84.98 and rising.

**Capital allocation.** Popular has been aggressive: combined buyback authorizations of ~$1B across 2024-2025, with ~8.1M shares repurchased for ~$874M, cutting the share count from ~69M toward ~64.7M. The quarterly common dividend was raised to $0.75/share (declared May 8, 2026; payable July 1). One caution: buying back stock at ~1.7x TBV is materially less value-accretive than the 2024 repurchases executed near book value. The per-dollar return on capital deployed into buybacks is falling as the stock re-rates.

**Balance-sheet caveat — AOCI.** Popular carries a sizable accumulated other comprehensive loss of ~$1.1B+ from unrealized losses on its AFS securities portfolio. This is improving as rates ease and securities pull to par, and it does not flow through CET1 under the bank's capital election. But it represents embedded economic drag and a reminder of duration risk: a renewed rate back-up would re-pressure tangible book and flip the reinvestment tailwind into a headwind.

**Credit quality.** NPLs run ~1.17% of loans against an ACL/loans ratio of ~2.10%, a heavy reserve cushion appropriate to the Puerto Rico borrower profile. Q1 2026 showed pockets of stress: a ~$11.1M CRE charge-off and a specific reserve tied to a single telecom borrower. Net charge-offs are structurally elevated relative to mainland peers, a recurring theme in bear commentary, but contained and well-reserved.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is a recovery story rather than a secular one. Estimate revisions are positive across the board: current-quarter EPS revised +10.9% over 90 days and +4.4% over 30 days to $3.66; current-year EPS up +4.8% over 90 days to $14.79; next-year EPS up +3.5% over 90 days to $16.48. The four-quarter beat track record (25.8%, 4.4%, 14.0%, 11.1%) supports above-average confidence in forward consensus.

The revision velocity of +4.8% current-year falls short of the inflection threshold (>30% over 90 days) that would mark a depressed-trough recovery being systematically under-modeled. This is the difference between a bank earning back to normalized profitability and one experiencing a step-change. The earnings recovery is well advanced, not early. The forward driver set is meaningful but not explosive: continued securities-book reinvestment at higher yields, deposit-cost relief, the mainland Popular Bank as a higher-growth (if more competitive) avenue, and Puerto Rico macro support from the still-ramping federal reconstruction spend.

The reconstruction dynamic is the master variable for medium-term loan and deposit growth. A federal audit (2024) noted Puerto Rico had spent less than 10% of more than $23B in available recovery funds. The slow spend-down is both a multi-year GDP tailwind and a future cliff depending on disbursement pace and Washington appropriations. This is genuine upside optionality for Popular's loan book, but it is exogenous and politically contingent.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$151, BPOP trades at ~12x trailing earnings, ~1.7x tangible book, and offers a ~2.0% dividend yield. On forward consensus of $14.79 (FY) and $16.48 (next year), the forward multiple is roughly 10.2x and 9.2x respectively. For a bank earning ~15.5% ROTCE with a 15.9% CET1, a ~10x forward multiple is not expensive in absolute terms.

The issue is that the re-rating has already occurred. A year ago the thesis was "cheap, hated Puerto Rico bank"; today it is "high-quality bank at a full multiple," and external analysts explicitly flag the premium. The 1.7x tangible book multiple is the relevant anchor: for a single-economy bank with elevated charge-offs and federal-funding dependence, 1.7x TBV is a fair-to-full price, not a discount. Mainland regional peers with cleaner geographic diversification and similar ROTCE trade in comparable territory, so there is no obvious peer-relative discount to capture.

The analyst price-target range of $163-$180 (mean $171, median $172) implies ~8-19% upside, and on inflection stocks analyst targets often lag reality. But the guardrail here matters: BPOP is a recovered bank at a full book multiple, not a sub-30x forward, sub-1.5 PEG inflection name with explosive estimate revisions. Treating the consensus target as a floor is unwarranted; treat it as a reasonable central estimate. The valuation score reflects a price that fairly captures the improved fundamentals, with limited margin of safety and a NIM tailwind that, if it flattens, removes the support for further multiple expansion. Upside from here requires either durable proof that 15%+ ROTCE is sustainable through the full rate cycle or continued Puerto Rico macro improvement, both plausible, neither free.

## Competitive Landscape

Popular's competitive position in Puerto Rico is dominant and structurally advantaged. Incumbency, the largest branch and deposit network, the government banking relationship, and a consolidated on-island market with few large competitors combine to support above-peer ROE and a sticky, low-cost deposit base. There is no evidence of share erosion; the franchise is stable-to-improving, with the transformation program lifting ROTCE above its target.

The flip side is that this moat is inseparable from a single fragile economy. The mainland Popular Bank diversifies geography but operates in far more competitive metro markets where Popular is a mid-sized player rather than a dominant incumbent. The franchise quality is genuine but does not extend the moat beyond the island.

A quieter structural exposure is the critical third-party dependence on Evertec, a former Popular affiliate that handles core transaction processing and payments infrastructure under master service agreements. The 10-K treats the transition away from or renegotiation of these arrangements as a material operational risk. This is a legacy related-party dependency that warrants monitoring; it is not a near-term catalyst but is the kind of concentration a standardized screen misses.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Extreme geographic concentration.** ~75-80% of assets, deposits, and revenue are tied to Puerto Rico, an economy with a documented history of fiscal crisis, demographic decline, and natural-disaster exposure. This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and is only partly priced.

2. **Public-sector deposit dependence.** The ~$19.7B (~30% of deposits) in market-linked public-sector balances is currently a NIM tailwind as it reprices down, but it is double-edged: a shift in government cash balances, fiscal stress, or a rate-environment reversal could pressure both funding cost and deposit stability simultaneously. This is the single most idiosyncratic balance-sheet feature.

3. **Federal funding cliff.** Puerto Rico's economy is materially propped up by federal transfers. The 10-K flags Medicaid/health-funding cliff risk in coming federal fiscal years alongside the multi-year wind-down of disaster-recovery dollars. The recovery pool that is a tailwind today becomes a cliff on the wrong timing or appropriations decision. This is exogenous and politically contingent.

4. **Interest-rate / duration risk.** The securities book that is now a reinvestment tailwind created the AOCI loss. A renewed rate back-up would re-pressure tangible book and reverse the NIM story.

5. **Elevated credit normalization.** Charge-offs (consumer and select CRE/commercial credits) run structurally above mainland peers, though offset by a heavy reserve. The Q1 CRE charge-off and telecom-specific reserve show the stress is real if contained.

6. **Governance signal.** No insider buying, with sizable executive/director sales near the highs (EVP Gonzalez-Noguera ~$921K on May 1; director Ballester ~$3.45M on April 30, both around $148-150). These are routine sales after a strong run, but the absence of any open-market buying is a mild negative on a fully-valued stock.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and offers no contrarian edge. Near-term ATM IV of 26.6% sits roughly in line with one-year historical volatility of 24.5% (a 2.1% spread, "fair"), and IV percentile within the current chain is just 5%, meaning the market is pricing low near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), the term structure is flat (near 26.6% vs far 28.1%), and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±4.2%. There is no elevated put demand to flag against the rating and no cheap-vol signal arguing for urgent entry, consistent with the fundamental view of a low-drama, fully-valued stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$180, +19%).** The 15%+ ROTCE proves durable through the rate cycle as the securities book continues rolling into higher yields and deposit costs reset lower. Federal reconstruction spend accelerates, lifting Puerto Rico GDP, loan growth, and credit quality. Continued buybacks at a still-reasonable multiple compound TBV per share, and the dividend grows from the raised $0.75 base. The market awards a sustained ~1.9-2.0x TBV multiple to a proven high-ROTCE franchise. Forward EPS reaches ~$16.50+ and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case (~$120-125, -17% to -20%).** The NIM tailwind flattens as rates stabilize, removing the earnings-growth engine; consensus forward estimates roll over. A rate back-up worsens the AOCI mark and re-pressures tangible book. Federal Medicaid/reconstruction funding disappoints, weighing on Puerto Rico macro, loan growth, and credit. Charge-offs normalize higher than reserved. The multiple compresses back toward ~1.4x TBV as the "quality at a full price" thesis loses its tailwind. Buybacks at 1.7x TBV prove to have been value-destructive in hindsight.

The base case sits between: a fairly-valued bank that compounds book value modestly and returns capital, with the stock tracking earnings growth (~$155-165) absent a clear catalyst for further multiple expansion. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), with the rate-path trajectory and any federal-appropriations news on Puerto Rico recovery/Medicaid funds as the key macro inputs in the interim.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM holds or expands further in Q2-Q3 2026 (taxable-equivalent ≥4.14%) on a basis that demonstrates the tailwind is durable rather than peaking, with ROTCE sustained ≥15%, providing evidence that 15%+ returns survive the rate cycle.
- A pullback toward ~1.4-1.5x tangible book (roughly $120-130) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety.
- Clear, funded acceleration of federal reconstruction disbursement that visibly lifts loan growth without credit deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM contraction over two consecutive quarters alongside falling forward EPS revisions, signaling the earnings engine has stalled.
- A renewed rate back-up that pushes the AOCI loss materially wider and re-pressures tangible book per share.
- Credit deterioration beyond normalization — NPLs rising above ~1.5% of loans or net charge-offs spiking on more than an idiosyncratic single-credit basis.
- A material adverse federal-funding decision (Medicaid cliff or recovery-fund clawback) that threatens the Puerto Rico macro support.

## Conclusion

Popular has executed a clean earnings recovery and is operationally one of the stronger regional banks: dominant island franchise, ~15.5% ROTCE, 15.9% CET1, four straight beats, rising estimates, and disciplined capital return. Those strengths are real and well-documented in the filings. But the stock now reflects them. At ~1.7x tangible book and ~10x forward earnings, the cheap-and-hated re-rating is behind us, the NIM tailwind driving the earnings jump is partly mechanical and rate-path dependent, executives are selling rather than buying near the highs, and the franchise carries extreme single-economy concentration plus federal-funding cliff risk that no clean model captures. The capital-return program and revision momentum support holding existing positions for continued, modest appreciation; the absence of a margin of safety and the symmetric risk/reward argue against new capital at current levels. We rate BPOP a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, and would become more constructive on either durable proof of through-cycle ROTCE or a pullback toward ~1.4-1.5x tangible book.
