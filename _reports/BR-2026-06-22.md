**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Broadridge runs the back-office plumbing of global capital markets: proxy distribution and voting, post-trade processing, wealth-management platforms, and customer communications. It is the kind of recurring-revenue, switching-cost-heavy infrastructure franchise that normally commands a premium multiple and rarely surprises investors. The surprise this cycle is the share price: BR has fallen from a 52-week high of $271.91 (August 2025) to $137.60, a 49% drawdown, and now trades at roughly 14x forward consensus EPS, a multiple it has not seen in over a decade and a near-50% discount to its own recent norms.

The drawdown reflects more than market noise. The operating business is still compounding: Q3 FY26 revenue rose 8%, recurring revenue grew 6% constant-currency, adjusted EPS rose 11%, and management raised its FY26 recurring-revenue and adjusted-EPS guidance. The problem sits in the bookings engine. Closed sales fell from $341.8M (FY24) to $287.9M (FY25), management cut FY26 closed-sales guidance to $240–290M from $290–330M, and the 9-month FY26 figure of $147M was down 16% year over year. Because recurring revenue lags bookings, two consecutive years of declining closed sales plus explicit commentary about "lengthening sales cycles" is a forward headwind to the 7–9% recurring-growth algorithm the entire bull case rests on. Closed sales is also a CEO compensation metric, which makes the guidance cut credible, since management is disclosing against its own pay.

A second issue clouds the headline numbers: 9-month FY26 GAAP net income jumped 56%, but roughly $238M of that came from Canton Coin mark-to-market and a related digital-asset transaction. Stripping those out, adjusted EPS grew a respectable but far less dramatic 16%. The right anchor is the adjusted figures. This is a quality business at a genuinely attractive absolute valuation, but with a slowing growth signal and no obvious catalyst to reverse the de-rating in the next two quarters. We rate the stock HOLD, with a clear upgrade path if closed sales stabilize.

## Company Overview

Broadridge operates two segments. **Investor Communication Solutions (ICS)** is roughly 74% of revenue ($5.1B of $6.89B in FY25) and is the crown jewel. Broadridge handles the vast majority of U.S. beneficial-shareholder proxy distribution and voting, processing 900M+ equity positions and 7B+ investor communications in FY25. Reimbursement rates for proxy distribution are set under NYSE/SRO rules, which gives Broadridge a quasi-monopoly position that is structurally very hard to dislodge, and a regulatory dependency that is the company's single largest idiosyncratic risk. Sub-lines include regulatory communications, data-driven fund solutions, issuer services, and customer communications (digital and print).

**Global Technology & Operations (GTO)** is roughly 26% of revenue ($1.78B in FY25). It provides SaaS and processing platforms for Capital Markets (post-trade, NYFIX routing, the Itiviti-derived trading and connectivity stack) and Wealth & Investment Management (the "Wealth in Focus" omnichannel platform). Broadridge processes more than $15T in average daily fixed-income and equity trades and serves 21 of the 25 largest primary dealers and the 15 largest U.S. wealth providers.

The competitive position rests on entrenched regulatory positioning in proxy, deep integration into client back offices, and network effects across issuers, brokers, and asset managers. The company also has a genuine early-mover position in tokenization: its Distributed Ledger Repo (DLR) platform tokenized $354B/day in Q3 FY26, up 392% year over year, and powered the first on-chain U.S. public-company proxy vote. This is real institutional volume rather than a pilot, though it remains a small contributor to revenue today.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The top line has compounded steadily: $5.71B (FY22) → $6.06B (FY23) → $6.51B (FY24) → $6.89B (FY25, +6%). The 9-month FY26 figure of $5.26B is up 8%, with Q3 FY26 revenue of $1.95B also up 8%. Recurring revenue, the highest-quality stream, reached $4.51B in FY25 (+7%) and is guided to at least 7% constant-currency growth in FY26, raised from "higher end of 5–7%."

**Margins.** GAAP operating margin expanded from 13.6% (FY21) to 17.3% (FY25); adjusted operating margin rose from 18.1% to 20.5% over the same span. Management targets 50+ bps of annual adjusted margin expansion and has consistently delivered it, helped by the margin-accretive shift to digital communications (91% of proxy comms now digitized, reducing paper and postage).

**Earnings — read the adjusted figures.** FY25 GAAP net earnings were $839.5M; adjusted EPS was $8.55 versus $7.73 in FY24. The 9-month FY26 GAAP net earnings of $726.2M (+56% YoY) include $238.3M of digital-asset gains from Canton Coin mark-to-market and the Canton Strategic Holdings transaction. Stripping those out, adjusted EPS was $5.81 versus $5.00 (+16%), strong but well short of the headline GAAP EPS of $6.18 (+57%). FY26 adjusted EPS growth is guided to 10–12%. The earnings-surprise record is solid (3 beats and 1 small miss over the last four quarters, with double-digit beats in the September and December 2025 quarters), supporting confidence in the adjusted-EPS algorithm.

**Cash flow.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1.17B and free cash flow was $1.06B, a 90% FCF conversion rate. 9-month FY26 FCF rose to $590.9M from $393.2M, with FY26 conversion guided to 100%+. This is a genuinely cash-generative business.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is roughly $3.25B against equity of roughly $2.66B (D/E ~2.2x), with a current ratio near 0.98 and an Altman Z-score around 1.96 (grey zone). The sub-1 current ratio and grey-zone Z-score are structural artifacts of an asset-light recurring-revenue model, where goodwill, intangibles, and deferred client-conversion costs make up roughly 67% of the $8.5B asset base; they are not distress signals. The real watch item is goodwill of roughly $3.6B sitting atop a debt-funded, acquisition-heavy strategy. A $750M term loan was added in August 2025, plus further obligations in May 2026, to fund M&A. Impairment risk rises if any large acquisition underperforms in a soft-bookings environment.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend has risen every year for roughly 18 years: $2.30 (FY21) → $3.52 (FY25) → $3.90 guided for FY26 (an ~11% raise). 9-month FY26 returns were $248M in dividends and $475M in net buybacks. The forward dividend yield at the current price is roughly 2.8%, comfortably covered by FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided, and growth is the dimension where the evidence sorts winners from losers most reliably, so it warrants the most scrutiny here.

On the positive side, recurring revenue is compounding at 7%+, adjusted EPS at 10–12%, and management raised both during a quarter when the stock was falling. Margin expansion is steady and structural. The digital-communications transition has a long runway, the wealth-platform modernization is gaining its first Canadian client, and the DLR tokenization volume growth provides real optionality the market is assigning little value to.

On the negative side sits the most important non-obvious signal in the company: closed sales have declined two consecutive years, FY26 guidance was cut by roughly $50M at the midpoint, and 9-month FY26 closed sales fell 16%. Management attributed this to lengthening sales cycles. Recurring revenue reflects past bookings, so today's recurring strength is a lagging indicator; the bookings slowdown is the leading one. If closed sales remain depressed, the 7–9% recurring-growth algorithm decelerates in FY27–FY28. Estimate revisions confirm the muted picture: current-year and next-year EPS estimates are essentially flat over 90 days (+0.4% and +0.6%), with the current quarter trimmed slightly. There is no estimate-revision tailwind to lean on, and not the +30% revision that would force a buy. Given the slowing bookings engine and the absence of acceleration in the estimates, we score growth prospects at the lower end of the company's historical range.

## Valuation Assessment

At $137.60 the stock trades at roughly 14x forward consensus EPS (FY26 ~$9.54, FY27 ~$10.41). Against the peer median P/E of 18.2x in Data Processing & Outsourced Services, BR is at a discount, though that peer set is heterogeneous (Genpact 9.8x, Maximus 9.3x, CSG 35.9x, Concentrix negative), so the median is not a precise anchor. More telling is BR's own history: the stock spent most of the past several years at a meaningfully higher multiple, and the current 14x is a level it has not carried in over a decade. The de-rating has compressed the multiple roughly in line with, arguably below, its high-single-digit-to-low-double-digit earnings growth.

Two cautions temper an outright cheap call. First, a low multiple on this name is the market's verdict on the closed-sales/sales-cycle problem, not an oversight; a cheap headline multiple should be treated as a value-trap warning until a catalyst confirms the discount is wrong. Second, the consensus price target range of $165–255 (mean ~$206.5) implies roughly 20–85% upside, but those targets were cut roughly 13% on the sales-cycle concern and may continue drifting lower if closed sales do not stabilize. We do not treat that target range as a floor here, because this is a de-rating story driven by a real fundamental signal, not a high-momentum stock with rising estimates.

On balance, the absolute valuation is attractive: a moat-protected compounder generating 100%+ FCF conversion, raising its dividend ~11%, and growing adjusted EPS 10–12% at 14x earnings is not expensive. But the asymmetry is capped by the lack of a near-term re-rating catalyst. We score valuation a notch above neutral: cheap enough to support the stock, not cheap enough to override the growth signal.

## Competitive Landscape

The proxy franchise within ICS faces little direct competition because of its regulatory structure; it is effectively a regulated quasi-monopoly with extremely high switching costs. That franchise is stable-to-improving as digitization lifts margins. In GTO, Broadridge competes against SS&C, FIS, and Fiserv, plus in-house broker systems, and the competitive trajectory there is steadier rather than share-gaining.

The competitive trajectory overall is stable, with two upside vectors the market underweights: the DLR tokenization platform ($354B/day, +392% growth) and the wealth-platform modernization. The offsetting drag is the lengthening sales cycle, which suggests Broadridge is finding it harder to close new mandates, whether due to client caution, financial-services consolidation reducing the addressable client count, or competitive friction. We score competitive position firmly above neutral on the durable moat, but confirm rather than over-weight it given the bookings softness; this dimension's recent predictive signal has weakened to roughly half its all-time strength, so it should not carry the thesis alone.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Regulatory dependency on proxy economics (most material, idiosyncratic).** A large share of ICS revenue depends on NYSE/SEC-governed proxy fees. Any SRO rule change to fee structures or default-delivery rules could pressure distribution revenue. This is structural and hard for a generic screen to capture.
2. **Slowing bookings.** Two years of declining closed sales and a guidance cut citing lengthening sales cycles is the central forward-growth risk; it is partly but not fully priced.
3. **Customer concentration.** The largest client is ~7% of revenue, and deferred client-conversion costs (~10% of assets) are concentrated in one client. A loss or renegotiation would be material.
4. **Digital-asset volatility (new in FY26).** Canton Coin holdings (~1.5B coins, fair value ~$218M vs ~$11.5M cost) are now marked to market through earnings; a $28M unrealized loss on the related warrants already illustrates the downside. This injects equity-style volatility into a stock investors buy for stability. Note the attribution: Canton Coin and DLR are Broadridge's own assets; Canton Strategic Holdings (CNTN) is a separate third-party vehicle in which Broadridge holds warrants, not a subsidiary.
5. **Goodwill impairment** given ~$3.6B goodwill on a debt-funded acquisition strategy, with integration risk elevated in a soft-bookings environment.
6. **Key-vendor reliance** on Kyndryl (ITSA to Dec 2031, ~$400M committed) and AWS.
7. **CFO transition.** Ashima Ghei is signing as CFO; 8-Ks show officer/director changes, worth monitoring for continuity, with no current sign of distress.

We score risk factors below neutral because the closed-sales signal and the new digital-asset volatility are real and only partially priced, and because the proxy-regulatory dependency is an underappreciated structural exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm given the magnitude of the price decline. Near-term ATM IV is 31.9% versus 25.8% historical volatility, modestly rich, with the IV percentile at just 2% within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5% near-term, turning mildly negative further out), and the implied one-month move is ±6.9%. The put/call OI ratio fell from 5.93 to 2.07 as the stock dropped from $147 to $135, and ATM IV declined 2.9pp: the market is not pricing an imminent catastrophe or a heavily defensive posture. The high put/call volume ratio (20x) is more consistent with hedging and income-overwriting than panic, given the falling IV and OI. The options market sees uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors rather than a binary near-term event, consistent with a slow-grind de-rating rather than a crisis. Short interest is low (3.3% of float, 2 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($200–230)
The closed-sales slowdown proves cyclical rather than structural: sales cycles normalize as clients regain confidence, and bookings re-accelerate toward the FY24 level. Recurring revenue continues compounding at 7%+, adjusted EPS at 10–12%, and the multiple re-rates from 14x back toward its prior elevated levels as the bookings concern fades. The DLR tokenization optionality starts to be valued explicitly. At a re-rated multiple on FY27 adjusted EPS of roughly $10.40, the stock reaches the low-$200s; if re-rating runs further with bookings recovery, the high-$220s toward the consensus mean of ~$206 is reachable. Dividend growth (~11% raises) and 100%+ FCF conversion pay the investor to wait.

### Bear Case ($110–125)
Lengthening sales cycles persist or worsen, closed sales decline a third year, and the recurring-revenue algorithm decelerates below 7% in FY27. The multiple stays compressed at 12–13x on flat-to-falling estimates, and a goodwill impairment on an underperforming acquisition (CQG, Acolin) or a Canton Coin mark-down adds a negative GAAP surprise. At 13x a stalled ~$9.50 EPS, the stock sits around $123; a sharper deceleration takes it toward $110, roughly 10–20% below the current price.

### Base Case ($150–165)
Bookings stabilize but do not sharply re-accelerate; recurring revenue holds at 6–7% and adjusted EPS grows ~10%. The multiple recovers modestly from 14x to ~15–16x as the worst fears fade, supported by the dividend and buyback. At ~15.5x FY27 EPS of ~$10.40, the base case is roughly $160, about 16% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q4/FY26 earnings release (early-to-mid August 2026), which will report full-year closed sales against the cut guidance and set the FY27 algorithm. This is the decisive data point for the thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Closed sales stabilize or re-accelerate: a FY26 print at or above the high end of the $240–290M range, or a sequential improvement in quarterly bookings with commentary that sales cycles are shortening.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+3% or more over 90 days) alongside reaffirmed 7%+ recurring-revenue growth.
- Multiple re-rating begins from 14x with the bookings concern visibly resolved.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive year of declining closed sales, or FY26 closed sales below the $240M floor.
- Recurring-revenue growth guidance cut below 7% constant-currency for FY27.
- A material goodwill impairment or a large Canton Coin/warrant mark-down that signals capital-allocation missteps.

## Conclusion

Broadridge is a high-quality, moat-protected infrastructure compounder now trading at a genuinely cheap absolute valuation: roughly 14x forward earnings, a ~2.8% well-covered dividend yield, 100%+ FCF conversion, and an 18-year dividend-growth record. The 49% drawdown has reset the multiple to levels not seen in over a decade, and the recurring-revenue franchise remains intact and still compounding.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the de-rating reflects a real fundamental signal, not a mispricing the market overlooked. Two consecutive years of declining closed sales, a guidance cut, and explicit commentary about lengthening sales cycles point to a slowing bookings engine that, given the lag between bookings and recurring revenue, is a forward headwind to the growth algorithm the bull case depends on. Estimate revisions are flat, there is no near-term re-rating catalyst, no open-market insider buying to signal conviction, and the headline GAAP earnings strength is largely a reversible crypto gain. The move from BUY to HOLD over the last four reports reflects this deterioration in the growth signal even as the valuation has become more attractive.

The risk/reward at $137.60 is reasonably balanced: roughly 16% upside to a $160 base case, 45–67% to the bull case if bookings recover, against 10–20% downside in the bear case. We would become buyers on confirmation that closed sales have stabilized, most likely at the August FY26 print, and would step away if bookings decline a third year. The franchise quality and valuation support holding, but the slowing growth engine and lack of a catalyst keep us from a confident buy.
