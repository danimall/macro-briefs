**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lamar Advertising is one of North America's largest out-of-home (OOH) advertising operators, running ~360,800 displays across 45 U.S. states and Canada. The business rests on genuine structural advantages: regulatory barriers to new billboard construction (Highway Beautification Act, local zoning), the largest digital LED billboard network in the U.S., a near-monopoly in state logo-sign contracts (24 of 28 privatized programs), and a small/mid-market focus where Lamar holds dominant local share. This is a durable, cash-generative REIT — FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $1.058B at a ~47% margin, AFFO of $846.7M, and ~$864M of operating cash flow — with a well-laddered debt maturity profile and a ~4% dividend.

The operating story is intact and accelerating. Q1 2026 revenue rose 4.5% YoY (national demand "particularly" strong per management), Adjusted EBITDA +7.7%, and AFFO/share +7.5% to $1.72. Free cash flow of $152.4M in the quarter was up 25.8% YoY. The GAAP net-income "decline" of 26.9% in Q1 2026 is an optical artifact — the prior-year period contained a ~$67.7M one-time gain from selling the Vistar Media stake. Anyone anchoring on reported net income will misread both the 2025 spike (inflated by the Vistar gain and a $136.6M D&A reduction) and the Q1 2026 print. The clean metric is AFFO/share, which is compounding in the mid-to-high single digits.

The debate is about the price, not the business. The stock has appreciated ~28% over the trailing year to $159.46, sits near its 52-week high of $164.38, and trades close to the consensus mean target of $158.60 with a median of $160.00. On roughly $6.85–$7.00 of 2026 AFFO/share (annualizing the $1.72 Q1 run-rate), the current price implies ~23x AFFO — reasonable for the quality but no longer cheap, and offering limited room for further multiple expansion. We rate LAMR a HOLD (growth_hold). The total-return case is real (mid-to-high-single-digit AFFO growth plus ~4% yield), but it is a compounding thesis, not a re-rating thesis, and the risk/reward at current prices is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Lamar has operated under its name since 1902, went public in 1996, converted to a REIT in 2014, and adopted an UPREIT structure in 2022 (enabling tax-deferred property contributions in exchange for operating-partnership units, the mechanism used for the 2025 Verde Outdoor deal). The business breaks into three segments:

- **Billboard (~89% of revenue, $2.01B):** ~159,300 billboard faces including ~5,500 digital LED billboards, the largest U.S. digital billboard network. Bulletins are 77% of billboard revenue; digital generates ~33% of billboard revenue from just ~3.5% of face count, reflecting the high revenue density of LED conversion.
- **Logo signs (4%, $89M):** The largest U.S. provider, operating 24 of 28 privatized state logo contracts (highway exit gas/food/lodging signs) plus 15 TODS tourist-sign programs, a near-monopoly niche.
- **Transit (7%, $163M):** Bus/shelter/bench/airport displays in 80+ markets, held in a Taxable REIT Subsidiary.

The competitive moat has several distinct sources. Regulatory barriers restrict new billboard supply, protecting incumbents' inventory and pricing. Lamar's small/mid-market focus differentiates it from Clear Channel Outdoor and OUTFRONT, which skew toward large-metro/transit assets. Its local revenue mix (~79% of outdoor revenue) is stickier and more diversified than national-ad-dependent peers, supported by ~1,000 local account executives and regional managers averaging 34 years of tenure. Underpinning the network is a hard-to-replicate real estate base: Lamar owns ~11,200 land parcels beneath its displays.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily at a low-single-digit organic pace: $2.03B (2022) → $2.11B (2023) → $2.21B (2024) → $2.27B (2025, +2.7% reported, +2.1% acquisition-adjusted). Q1 2026 accelerated to $528.0M (+3.9% acquisition-adjusted), which management frames as trending toward the top end of full-year AFFO/share guidance.

On the cash-flow metrics that matter for a REIT: FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA carried a ~47% margin, and AFFO grew +3.4%. Q1 2026 diluted AFFO/share reached $1.72, with AFFO up 8.0%, a step-up from the FY2025 pace. Capex is disciplined at $180.8M (2025) and ~$186M guided for 2026, heavily weighted toward digital ($90.9M), the highest-return growth channel.

GAAP net income requires normalization. The 2025 figure (~$587–593M) was inflated by a $75.9M gain on the Vistar Media stake sale and a $136.6M drop in D&A from a 2024 asset-retirement-obligation cost revision. Conversely, Q1 2026 net income fell purely because Q1 2025 carried the Vistar gain. The clean read is the AFFO trajectory — mid-single-digit in 2025, accelerating to +8% in early 2026.

On the balance sheet, total debt was ~$3.42B at end-2025, rising to ~$3.50B at Q1 2026, with net-debt/EBITDA around 3.2x — moderate for the OOH sector and well inside the 4.5x secured-debt covenant. Standardized screens flag an Altman Z-Score of 2.74 (grey zone) and a current ratio of 0.58, but these are structural REIT artifacts rather than distress signals. The working-capital deficit (~$334M in 2025) is driven by the $250M A/R securitization balance and $232M of current operating-lease liabilities offset by long-term right-of-use assets; negative retained earnings (–$1.1B) reflect cumulative REIT distributions, not accumulated losses.

The debt maturity ladder is clean. Only $0.4M matures in 2026. In late 2025 Lamar issued $400M of 5⅜% Senior Notes due 2033 and repriced/extended $700M of Term B loans to 2032 (SOFR +1.50%). The next meaningful item is the $600M 3¾% Notes due February 2028, which carry a springing-maturity clause on the revolver. Liquidity of ~$701–807M (cash plus revolver availability) is adequate.

Interest expense fell in 2025 to $160.4M on lower short rates. The quiet risk is cost-of-debt creep: new issuance at 5⅜% sits well above the legacy sub-4% notes, so as the 3¾% 2028 notes roll into higher-coupon paper, interest costs rise structurally and dampen AFFO/share growth. This is the marginal headwind that keeps growth in the mid-single digits rather than pushing it higher.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is straightforward and reasonably durable: low-single-digit organic revenue growth, augmented by digital conversion (which multiplies revenue per structure), disciplined tuck-in M&A (50+ deals for ~$191M in 2025 plus the $147.6M Verde Outdoor UPREIT contribution), and a modest share-count reduction from buybacks. The result is mid-to-high-single-digit AFFO/share growth, currently at the higher end of that range.

Estimate revisions are mixed but net-positive. Current-year consensus EPS sits near $6.06, down slightly over 90 days (–0.4%) and down –0.8% over 30 days, but with next-year estimates revised up +5.0% over 30 days to $6.39. The earnings surprise record is uneven — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with the Q1 2026 beat (+18.2%) following two Q3/Q4 2025 misses (–11.2%, –9.7%). The revision magnitude does not approach the +30% thresholds that flag a systematic underestimation; this is a steady compounder, not a trough-to-recovery story.

Structural tailwinds support durability. OOH is one of the few legacy media channels growing, benefiting as advertisers seek attention outside crowded digital feeds; it is immune to ad-blocking and cord-cutting. Programmatic DOOH remains only ~2% of Lamar's outdoor business but is a management-flagged growth vector. The digital-conversion runway is the clearest lever — with only ~3.5% of faces digital today, incremental LED deployment continues to raise revenue density. Tenant diversification is a defensive plus: no single advertiser exceeds 2% of billboard revenue, with a broad category mix (Services 19%, Health Care 10%, Restaurants 9%, Retail 8%, Automotive 8%).

The offset is that this is a mature, GDP-plus business. Advertising is cyclical, and management explicitly notes OOH "may be impacted more negatively than the economy as a whole" in downturns, and Q1 2026 forward-looking language newly cites tariff/trade and geopolitical demand risks.

## Valuation Assessment

At $159.46, LAMR trades at ~29x trailing GAAP earnings (a distorted number given the D&A and gain artifacts) and, more relevantly, ~23x annualized 2026 AFFO or ~26x on consensus current-year EPS of ~$6.06. The dividend yield is ~4.0% (dividend of $6.45 in 2025, guided to at least $6.40 in 2026). This is a full, quality-appropriate valuation, not a cheap one.

Peer context reinforces the point. Among Other Specialized REITs, the peer median P/E is ~18.3x, so LAMR trades at a clear premium. Its closest direct comparable, OUTFRONT Media (the other scaled OOH pure-play), trades at ~29.0x with a 4.0% yield, essentially in line with LAMR. Gaming/net-lease REITs (GLPI 15.1x/6.8%, EPR 18.3x/6.3%, FCPT 22.4x/5.8%) trade cheaper on higher yields, but are structurally lower-growth. Lamar's premium to the broad specialized-REIT median is justified by its superior moat, digital growth engine, and best-in-class local franchise, but that premium already embeds the quality.

The valuation dimension scores below neutral because the current price offers no identifiable mispricing. The stock is neither a cyclical trough (AFFO is compounding steadily, not inflecting) nor a beaten-down value trap. It sits near its 52-week high, near the consensus mean target ($158.60) and just below median ($160.00), with a target range of $150–$170. On a high-multiple, stable-growth compounder trading at analyst consensus, targets reflect the same steady-growth extrapolation the price already embeds and are not independent evidence of hidden upside. A cheap-looking screen would be misleading here; the honest read is that quality is fairly priced.

The realistic total-return math frames the call: ~mid-to-high-single-digit AFFO growth + ~4% yield = a high-single-digit-to-low-double-digit annual total return if the multiple holds, with the multiple already at the high end of its historical range.

## Competitive Landscape

Lamar's competitive position is stable-to-improving, which supports an above-neutral competitive-position score. The scaled OOH rivals — Clear Channel Outdoor and OUTFRONT Media — are more metro/transit-weighted, leaving Lamar's small/mid-market dominance and logo-sign near-monopoly largely uncontested in its core. The land-ownership base and the digital billboard network are structural advantages that widen over time as digital conversion proceeds.

The broader competition is for advertising dollars against digital/search/social, streaming/CTV, radio, and print. OOH's counter-pitch (un-skippable reach, immunity to ad-blocking and cord-cutting, and improving measurement/programmatic capabilities) is holding up, and the channel is one of the few legacy media formats gaining share. Five-year TSR of 198 versus a peer-group 161 confirms Lamar has executed and re-rated better than its comparables.

Two watch items on trajectory: the Vistar Media sale (February 2025) monetized Lamar's programmatic-DOOH equity stake at a large gain but ceded strategic ownership in the ad-tech layer, meaning Lamar may increasingly rent programmatic economics from third parties. And 7 of 25 logo contracts are up for renewal/expiration in 2026 — these are competitively re-bid with meaningful start-up costs, a modest annual risk to the near-monopoly.

## Risk Assessment

The most material risks are financial and macro rather than idiosyncratic-binary:

1. **Cost-of-debt creep.** With $3.5B of debt and refinancing occurring at 5⅜% versus legacy sub-4% coupons, AFFO/share growth faces a structural headwind as the $600M 3¾% 2028 notes roll. This is a primary drag on the growth algorithm and a direct sensitivity for a leveraged REIT.
2. **Ad-spend cyclicality.** OOH demand is highly cyclical and management flags it as potentially more downturn-sensitive than the broad economy. With newly disclosed tariff/geopolitical demand risks, a demand air pocket would compress the AFFO trajectory the current multiple assumes.
3. **Reilly family super-voting control (~63% of votes, ~15% economic).** This dual-class structure delivers alignment and long-term stewardship (validated by TSR outperformance) but eliminates any realistic takeover premium and concentrates decision-making with limited external accountability.
4. **Digital deployment dependency.** Digital is the main growth lever (most of capex, 33% of billboard revenue) but faces potential new driver-safety/aesthetic regulation and component-cost/supply risk.
5. **Contract renewal (logo/transit) and permitting/regulatory risk** (HBA, nonconforming-billboard amortization, eminent domain, content restrictions), plus self-insured catastrophe exposure on structures.
6. **New AI risk factor** in the 10-K, reflecting increasing operational AI adoption and the evolving programmatic/measurement tech layer; more a disclosure evolution than a quantified near-term threat.

Most of these are either priced or structural rather than acute binary catalysts, which keeps the risk-factor score modestly below neutral rather than in the danger zone. The screening flags (Altman Z 2.74, current ratio 0.58) are REIT artifacts, not distress; the correct read is a robust cash generator with a clean maturity ladder.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is close to neutral with a mild caution tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 30.8% is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 22.6% (an 8.2pp spread, 61st percentile within the chain), indicating the market prices somewhat elevated uncertainty, consistent with a stock near highs. The put/call skew is balanced at +2.3% and the term structure is flat (near 30.8% ≈ far 29.7%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded. The put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.75 to 1.09 since early June even as the stock climbed 6.6%, a modest hedging signal but not a strong bearish tell. Short interest is moderate at 9.2% of float (5.4 days to cover), not a crowded short. Net, the options market neither confirms strong conviction upside nor flags acute downside; it is consistent with a fairly-valued compounder near its range top.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($185–$195):** OOH demand stays resilient through any macro softness, national advertising continues the Q1 2026 strength, digital conversion and programmatic DOOH accelerate AFFO/share growth toward the high end of the range (~8%+), and the multiple holds at ~24–25x forward AFFO. Continued disciplined tuck-in M&A via the UPREIT structure and $400M buyback authorization add per-share leverage. A ~4% and growing dividend plus high-single-digit AFFO growth compounds to a low-teens total return, and the quality franchise sustains its premium multiple.

**Bear case ($120–$130, roughly –18% to –25% from current):** A cyclical ad-spend downturn (tariffs, recession) compresses organic growth toward zero or negative; cost-of-debt creep as the 2028 notes roll dampens AFFO/share; the multiple de-rates from ~23x toward the specialized-REIT peer median (~18x) as the growth premium erodes. Even in this scenario the ~4% dividend and clean balance sheet limit downside, given this is a quality REIT rather than a distressed one, but the current premium multiple is the vulnerable point.

**Base case ($165–$172):** AFFO/share grows mid-to-high single digits, the dividend rises modestly, and the multiple stays roughly flat near current levels. Total return is driven by the ~4% yield plus earnings growth, landing modestly above current price over 12 months — a good compounder with limited multiple-expansion room.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The score trajectory has improved steadily (5.6 → 6.2 → 6.1, now 5.8) with the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the Financial Health improvement (5.5 → 6.8 across prior reports, reflecting the recognition that screening flags are REIT artifacts and the maturity ladder is clean) is genuine. But four consecutive HOLDs raise the bar for continuation, and nothing in the current data warrants an upgrade: the business is executing well while the price has caught up to fair value. The quality is real and the compounding is intact, but the price offers balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) A pullback to ~$135–$140 (approaching ~20x forward AFFO), restoring a meaningful margin of safety; (2) AFFO/share growth sustaining above ~8% for two consecutive quarters alongside accelerating programmatic-DOOH monetization; (3) 90-day AFFO estimate revisions turning decisively positive (>+5%) rather than flat-to-negative.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) Two consecutive quarters of negative organic (acquisition-adjusted) revenue growth signaling a cyclical rollover; (2) net-debt/EBITDA rising toward 4.0x or a disorderly 2028-notes refinancing at materially higher spreads; (3) a dividend cut or freeze, or AFFO/share growth stalling below ~2% while the multiple stays elevated.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly AFFO print (August 2026) and full-year 2026 AFFO/share guidance are the natural checkpoints, along with progress on the 7 logo-contract renewals due in 2026 and any early read on the 2028-notes refinancing path.

## Conclusion

Lamar is a high-quality, structurally advantaged outdoor-advertising REIT with a durable moat, the largest U.S. digital billboard network, a near-monopoly logo franchise, and accelerating AFFO/share growth. The balance sheet is sound once REIT-specific artifacts are set aside, and the ~4% dividend is well-covered. This is a HOLD rather than a BUY entirely because of price: the stock has re-rated ~28% over the trailing year to sit near its 52-week high, at ~23x forward AFFO and near consensus targets, leaving mid-to-high-single-digit AFFO growth plus the yield as the realistic total-return engine with little room for multiple expansion. Own it for the compounding, add on a pullback toward the mid-$130s, and monitor cost-of-debt creep and ad-spend cyclicality as the two variables most likely to move the thesis.
