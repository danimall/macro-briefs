## Executive Summary

Medpace is a pure-play clinical CRO generating exceptional free cash flow ($682M in FY2025, 27% FCF margin) with zero financial debt and a business model that collects payment nearly two months before performing services. The company grew revenue 26.5% YoY in Q1 2026 and has beaten EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters by an average of 7%.

However, the forward picture is deteriorating. Net book-to-bill fell to 0.88 in Q1 2026. Backlog cancellations hit their highest point in over a year for the second consecutive quarter. Longer-dated backlog has declined for six consecutive quarters, and RFPs are down both sequentially and year-over-year. CEO Troendle explicitly acknowledged uncertainty about the growth profile "both now and six months from now." The stock dropped ~23% overnight on the Q1 2026 earnings release (April 22 to April 23) despite beating EPS estimates, reflecting the market's focus on deteriorating forward indicators.

At ~26x forward earnings with negative estimate revision momentum (-3.3% on next year over 30 days) and a pending securities fraud class action, the risk/reward is balanced. The business quality is high, but the specific risk of a prolonged cancellation cycle tied to biotech funding pressures warrants a neutral stance until visibility improves.

## Company Overview

Medpace is a full-service Phase I-IV clinical Contract Research Organization headquartered in Cincinnati, with approximately 6,200 employees operating across 46 countries. The company differentiates through:

- **Pure-play focus:** Exclusively clinical trial execution — no consulting, commercial, or data analytics services
- **Small/mid-cap biotech specialization:** 82% of FY2025 revenue from small biopharma companies; no large pharma strategic partnerships
- **Capital-light model:** Capex at 1.24% of revenue; proprietary ClinTrak clinical trial management system
- **Therapeutic expertise:** Oncology, metabolic disease, cardiology, CNS, and antiviral/anti-infective areas

The company competes against IQVIA, ICON, PPD (Thermo Fisher), and Fortrea, but occupies a distinct niche by refusing large pharma master service agreements. This strategy produces higher margins but exposes MEDP to biotech funding cyclicality.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated between current performance and forward indicators:

**Positive (current):**
- Q1 2026 revenue +26.5% YoY
- Q1 2026 EPS $4.28 vs. $3.96 estimate (+8.1% beat)
- Four consecutive quarterly beats averaging 7% surprise
- FY2025 net new awards of $2.65B (+19% vs. FY2024)

**Negative (forward-looking):**
- Q1 2026 book-to-bill: 0.88 (below 1.0 = backlog shrinking)
- Q4 2025 book-to-bill: 1.04 vs. guided 1.15
- Cancellations at highest point in over a year (two consecutive quarters of record cancellations)
- Longer-dated backlog declined for 6 consecutive quarters
- RFPs down sequentially AND year-over-year
- Backlog at $2.9B (end Q1 2026, down from $3.03B at end FY2025)

CEO Troendle's Q1 2026 commentary was cautious: "Our growth profile both now and six months from now in terms of sequential growth is a real question." He stated improvement requires "either cancellations to abate or gross awards, new notifications and a bigger pipeline" — neither of which he was willing to forecast.

The cancellations are concentrated in oncology ("products not working") and cardiovascular, with metabolic disease having driven the initial Q4 2025 deterioration. This reflects the fundamental binary nature of drug development for small biotech sponsors.

**Estimate revisions confirm deceleration:** Current year EPS estimates have fallen 1.5% over 30 days and next year estimates are down 3.3% over 30 days. The 90-day revisions are modestly positive (+3.3% current year), but the negative 30-day direction indicates Wall Street is beginning to price in forward weakness.

## Valuation Assessment

At $421, MEDP trades at:
- Forward P/E: ~26.4x (on $15.94 current year consensus; or ~24.7x on $17.06 FY estimate)
- Trailing P/E: ~26.4x (on $15.28 TTM EPS)
- FCF yield: ~5.7% (on FY2025 FCF of $682M / $12B market cap)
- EV/EBITDA: ~21x (on FY2025 EBITDA of $558M, zero net debt)

**Peer comparison:** The available peers trade at P/E 25.7x (Illumina) to 45.0x (Bio-Rad), with AVTR and AZTA in losses. MEDP's 26x is reasonable relative to Illumina but well below Bio-Rad's 45x. MEDP has superior growth (+26.5% vs. ILMN's low single digits) and far superior FCF conversion, suggesting the current multiple is fair-to-modest given high business quality.

**The problem:** At 26x with negative estimate momentum and a sub-1.0 book-to-bill, the multiple reflects a growth business but the leading indicators suggest growth may be decelerating. If revenue growth slows to 10-12% (which the backlog trajectory implies for late 2026/2027), a 26x forward P/E becomes expensive relative to the delivered growth rate. A PEG >2.0 on a decelerating growth profile is not compelling.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $443 (5% upside), median $456 (8% upside), range $329-$495. This is a tight range with limited consensus upside, consistent with the majority Hold ratings (8 of 13 analysts).

The valuation is not demanding on an absolute basis for a high-quality capital-light business, but it requires sustained high-teens growth to justify — which is precisely what the forward indicators are calling into question.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Only major CRO exclusively focused on small/mid-cap biotech (others chase large pharma partnerships)
- 21%+ operating margins among the highest in the CRO space
- Proprietary ClinTrak system creates operational stickiness
- Negative working capital position (customer prepayment) funds operations without external capital
- 5-year TSR of 403% vs. 99% for peer group demonstrates execution superiority

**Vulnerabilities:**
- 82% customer concentration in small biotech = directly correlated to biotech funding cycles
- When clients get acquired by large pharma, Medpace is "generally cut out on future work"
- 30-day cancellation provisions give customers easy exit
- CEO Troendle's measured skepticism on AI ("net cost for 2+ years") could become a competitive disadvantage if rivals achieve meaningful automation gains

The competitive moat depends on execution quality and therapeutic expertise rather than structural barriers to entry. The deliberate avoidance of large pharma protects margins but constrains the addressable market and creates binary exposure to biotech funding conditions.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks (in order of materiality):**

1. **Cancellation spiral (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** If the sub-1.0 book-to-bill persists for 2-3 more quarters, revenue growth could decelerate sharply by late 2026/early 2027 as the current backlog is consumed without adequate replenishment.

2. **Biotech funding dependency (MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact):** With 82% of revenue from small biotech, a sustained contraction in venture/public market biotech funding would compress MEDP's entire addressable market simultaneously. The current macro environment (high rates, tight capital markets) is not favorable for small biotech fundraising.

3. **Securities fraud class action (MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact):** Class period April 22, 2025 – February 9, 2026. The chronology of repeated 1.15 book-to-bill guidance affirmation while cancellations built creates non-trivial legal exposure. Lead plaintiff deadline June 8, 2026.

4. **Key-man risk (LOW probability, HIGH impact):** Founder-CEO Troendle (~19% ownership) now absorbing presidential duties after Geiger's departure. No obvious succession depth. Related-party transactions noted in risk factors.

5. **M&A headwind (ONGOING):** Consolidation of small biotech clients by large pharma systematically removes customers from MEDP's addressable market.

## Conclusion

Medpace is a high-quality business facing meaningful uncertainty about forward growth. The capital-light model, zero debt, 27% FCF margin, and negative working capital dynamics make this among the best-positioned CROs structurally. However, the backlog deterioration is real and accelerating: a 0.88 book-to-bill means the company is consuming its growth runway faster than it can replenish it.

At ~26x forward earnings with negative 30-day estimate revisions, a pending securities fraud lawsuit, and a departed president, the stock requires patience rather than conviction. The analyst community agrees: 8 of 13 rate it Hold with a mean target only 5% above current price.

The stock is a BUY below $370 (providing meaningful margin of safety on ~22x a potentially reduced earnings path) or above $520 (which would require confirmed backlog improvement justifying sustained 20%+ growth). At $420, the outcome depends entirely on whether cancellations normalize — and CEO Troendle himself cannot predict that outcome.
