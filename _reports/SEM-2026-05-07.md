## Executive Summary

Select Medical Holdings is a $2.0B post-acute care operator that has entered a definitive agreement to be taken private at $16.50/share by a consortium led by co-founder Robert Ortenzio, Senior EVP Martin Jackson, and Welsh Carson Anderson & Stowe (WCAS). The stock currently trades at $16.43-$16.44, representing a 0.4% spread to the deal price. The transaction is expected to close mid-2026, subject to majority-of-the-minority shareholder approval and regulatory clearances.

The narrow deal spread reflects high market confidence in closure. Committed debt financing from J.P. Morgan and Wells Fargo, the consortium's deep familiarity with the business (WCAS was the original 1997 PE sponsor), and insider equity rollovers all point toward consummation. Q1 2026 results missed EPS estimates by 24%, but management maintained full-year guidance and the deal remains on track; the stock hasn't budged from the $16.25-$16.45 trading band established in early March.

This is a deal-arb situation. The stock has traded in an extremely tight range (daily high-low spreads of $0.01-$0.05) since the merger announcement, confirming the market views this as a closed transaction. Upside is capped at $16.50; downside in a deal-break scenario would likely be $13-$14 based on the unaffected price and recent earnings trajectory. The recommendation is HOLD for existing shareholders pending deal close.

## Company Overview

Select Medical operates across three segments in post-acute healthcare services:

- **Critical Illness Recovery Hospitals (45% of revenue):** 104 long-term acute care hospitals (LTCHs) across 28 states, treating patients recovering from critical illness with average stays of 31 days.
- **Rehabilitation Hospitals (24% of revenue):** 38 inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRFs) in 15 states, providing intensive physical medicine with 14-day average stays.
- **Outpatient Rehabilitation (24% of revenue):** 1,917 clinics in 39 states — the largest U.S. operator by facility count.
- **Other (7%):** Corporate services, employee leasing, minority investments.

The company completed a tax-free spin-off of its Concentra occupational health subsidiary in November 2024, simplifying the business to pure post-acute care and reducing total debt from $3.6B to $1.8B.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
- 2025 revenue (continuing ops): $5.45B, up 5.1% YoY from $5.19B
- Adjusted EBITDA: $493M (9.0% margin), down 3.4% from $510M in 2024
- Net income from continuing operations: $215M, up 65% YoY
- Q1 2026 revenue: $1.42B (+5% YoY), but Adjusted EPS missed at $0.35 vs. $0.46 estimate (-24%)

**Segment divergence is pronounced:** Rehabilitation hospitals delivered 21.6% EBITDA margins with 16.1% revenue growth in 2025 and 13.4% EBITDA growth. Critical illness recovery margins fell from 12.3% to 10.7% on cost pressures. Outpatient margins deteriorated from 9.4% (2023) to 7.0% (2025), a persistent and concerning trend driven by personnel cost inflation against flat ~$100 revenue per visit.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $1.83B; leverage ratio 3.67x (covenant max 7.0x)
- Cash: Only $26.5M, with $469M revolver availability
- D/E: 2.24x
- Altman Z-Score: 1.81 (distress boundary — typical for leveraged healthcare operators)
- Goodwill: $2.36B (40% of total assets)

**Cash Flow:**
- 2025 operating cash flow: $346M (down from 2024, reflecting Concentra separation and capex ramp)
- 2025 FCF: $117M after elevated capex for rehab hospital expansion ($113M vs. $54M in 2024)
- Stock buybacks: $96.5M at average $15.13/share in 2025

**2026 Guidance (maintained post-Q1 miss):** Revenue $5.6B-$5.8B, Adjusted EBITDA $520M-$540M.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are mixed but largely irrelevant to the near-term investment thesis given the pending acquisition:

**Positive:**
- Rehabilitation hospitals adding 275 beds through 2027, with capex more than doubled
- Revenue per patient day growing steadily (LTCH: +2.4%, IRF: +5.9%)
- Aging demographics providing structural demand tailwind
- Commercial payor mix shifting favorably (51.6% of revenue in 2025, up from 49.3% in 2023)

**Negative:**
- CMS fixed-loss outlier threshold nearly doubled since FY2023 ($38,518 → $78,936 for FY2026), structurally compressing LTCH economics
- Outpatient margin erosion from 9.4% to 7.0% in two years with no visibility on stabilization
- OBBBA Medicaid cuts ($1T over 10 years) creating long-term reimbursement uncertainty
- Q1 2026 EPS miss (-24%) followed by Q4 2025 miss (-31%) — two consecutive misses after two beats

Estimate revisions reflect this deterioration: current-year EPS consensus has fallen from $1.31 to $1.23 over 90 days (-6.1%), and next-year from $1.50 to $1.37 (-8.5%).

## Valuation Assessment

**Take-Private Context:** At $16.50/share, the deal implies:
- EV/EBITDA: ~7.9x trailing (2025 EBITDA $493M, EV ~$3.9B), ~7.2-7.5x forward (2026 guidance midpoint)
- Forward P/E: ~13.4x on current-year consensus ($1.23), ~12.0x on next-year ($1.37)
- 18% premium to unaffected price (November 24, 2025), 25% premium to 90-day VWAP

This valuation is reasonable for a healthcare services company with mixed growth characteristics but is far from generous. Comparable OPCH trades at 16.0x earnings; NHC at 22.1x. The peer median P/E is 51.8x, though this is skewed by ASTH (76.9x) and USPH (51.8x), companies with different growth profiles.

**Current trading at $16.43-$16.44** leaves essentially no upside (0.4% to deal price). The stock is purely a deal-closing bet at this point. In a break scenario, fair value would likely be $13-$14 based on the pre-announcement trading range and deteriorating earnings trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Select Medical holds leading positions by facility count in all three segments. Key competitive dynamics:

- **LTCHs:** Consolidation trend benefits scale operators. ScionHealth is the primary competitor. Barriers to entry are high (Medicare certification, specialized staffing). However, CMS payment methodology is structurally challenging and potential unified post-acute payment (IMPACT Act) could further pressure the segment.
- **Rehabilitation Hospitals:** Encompass Health is the largest IRF operator. Select's joint venture strategy with major health systems provides referral pipelines and reduces capital intensity. This is the strongest competitive position.
- **Outpatient:** Highly fragmented market (ATI, Athletico, USPH, Upstream). Select is largest by count but faces intense labor competition in a commodity-like service.

The company's scale provides centralized operational advantages (billing, compliance, IT, purchasing) and its rehabilitation hospital JV model offers a differentiated growth approach.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risk for current shareholders is deal-break:**
1. **Shareholder vote risk (LOW):** Majority-of-the-minority approval required. Multiple law firms have launched investigations, but this is standard for going-private transactions. No competing bid has emerged. Insiders rolling over ~11.8% of equity provides alignment.
2. **Regulatory clearance (LOW):** No obvious antitrust issues in these fragmented markets. Healthcare regulatory approvals are routine for horizontal acquisitions without significant market overlap.
3. **Financing risk (LOW):** Committed financing in place. Existing debt remains, limiting new capital required.
4. **Downside in break scenario (MODERATE):** Stock likely falls to $13-$14, representing 14-21% downside against 0.4% upside.

**Operational risks (relevant to break scenario):**
- CMS outlier payment compression is structural and worsening
- Two consecutive earnings misses signal accelerating margin pressure
- Cash position of only $27M provides minimal buffer
- Labor cost inflation showing no signs of abating in outpatient segment

**Short Interest:** 5.2% of float with 1.4 days to cover, consistent with market confidence in deal closure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** Deal closes as expected mid-2026. Shareholders receive $16.50/share, capturing 0.4% return plus any dividends. If the deal breaks and fundamentals improve (CMS methodology corrects, rehab expansion drives EBITDA), the stock could trade at 14-15x forward earnings ($17-$19).

**Bear Case:** Deal breaks due to shareholder vote failure or unforeseen regulatory issue. Stock falls to $13-$14 on deteriorating earnings trajectory. Further LTCH outlier compression and outpatient margin erosion push EPS below current consensus. Without the deal premium, the stock is a $12-$14 security.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For existing holders:**
- **Base case:** Hold through deal closure (mid-2026), receive $16.50/share.
- **Timeline:** 1-3 months to close based on announced expectations.
- **Upside/downside:** +0.4% upside to deal price; -14% to -21% downside in break scenario.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** Not applicable — the deal caps upside. Only relevant if stock dipped below $15.50 on temporary market dislocation while deal remained on track, creating a 6%+ spread.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any credible indication of deal-break (financing withdrawal, regulatory block, shareholder group opposing vote)
- Stock breaking below $16.00, which would signal the market pricing in closure uncertainty

**Reassessment timeline:** Upon shareholder vote announcement and any regulatory filings. Expected resolution mid-2026.

## Conclusion

The stock trades within pennies of the $16.50 deal price, reflecting near-certainty of closure. The committed financing, insider participation (including the co-founder leading the consortium), and lack of regulatory barriers all support high closure probability. There is minimal analytical value in fundamental scoring of a company about to be taken private; the only question is deal risk vs. deal spread, and the 0.4% spread accurately reflects the low break probability.

For existing shareholders, the correct action is to hold through closure. For new capital, there is insufficient spread to justify the position given deal-break tail risk.
