**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is the world's leading provider of offshore-energy and government search-and-rescue (SAR) helicopter services, operating roughly 214–216 aircraft across 15 countries under 12 Air Operator Certificates. The company has executed a genuine fundamental turnaround: revenue grew from $1.19B (2022) to $1.49B (2025) while net income swung from a $15.8M loss in 2022 to a $129.1M profit in 2025. The business now sits at a capital-cycle pivot: the heavy government-contract aircraft investment phase is complete, capex is falling, the balance sheet was refinanced in January 2026 at a lower rate with no near-term maturities, and management initiated the company's first-ever dividend alongside a $125M buyback authorization. This is an attractive late-stage recovery story trading at only ~10.7x trailing earnings.

The complication is that the forward earnings picture has deteriorated meaningfully even as the long-term structural story holds. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut ~22% over 90 days ($4.97 to $3.91), and the last two quarters missed estimates badly (-56.5% in the March quarter, -31.7% in the December quarter) after two large beats earlier in 2025. The 2026 bull case depends almost entirely on a Government Services margin recovery: operating income in that segment collapsed 75.9% in 2025 to $5.1M on contract-transition costs, and management guides it to roughly triple to $70–80M as the IRCG and UKSAR2G transitions complete. That is an execution-and-timing story rather than a demand story, and the market discounted it: VTOL fell ~12% over two sessions following a Q1 print that beat on revenue and affirmed full-year guidance.

We rate VTOL a HOLD. The valuation is reasonable and the structural tailwinds (tight offshore helicopter supply, ~half of offshore contracts repricing to leading-edge rates in 2026, decade-long government SAR backlog) are real. But negative estimate momentum, two consecutive misses, insider selling into the highs with no open-market buying, and the market's negative reaction to affirmed guidance leave the risk/reward balanced. We would need confirmation that Government margins are inflecting on schedule and that estimate revisions stabilize before moving to a more constructive stance.

## Company Overview

Bristow provides vertical-flight aviation services across three segments:

- **Offshore Energy Services (~66–67% of revenue):** Transports personnel to and from offshore production platforms and drilling rigs across Europe (North Sea), the Americas (US Gulf, Brazil, Trinidad, Suriname), and Africa (Nigeria). This book is production-weighted rather than exploration-weighted, which makes it materially less sensitive to short-term oil price swings than the broader oilfield services group.
- **Government Services (~25–26%, growing):** Outsourced SAR for the UK (UKSAR/UKSAR2G), Ireland (IRCG), Netherlands, Dutch Caribbean, and the Falklands. These are 10+ year contracts with high-credit-quality government counterparties, the company's strategic growth engine and the source of multi-year revenue visibility.
- **Other Services (~8%):** Regional fixed-wing operations (Airnorth in Australia), Humberside Airport in the UK, and dry-leasing.

The company has 75+ years of operating history and ~3,627 employees (951 pilots, 904 engineers). Its principal competitors are CHC Group, NHV Group, Omni Helicopters, and PHI. The offshore helicopter industry is consolidated, with few certified relevant aircraft models, few qualified operators, and 24-month-plus new-build lead times, characteristics that raise barriers to entry and support the current pricing cycle.

The current corporate entity traces to Era Group's reverse merger with "Old Bristow," which filed Chapter 11 in 2019 and emerged later that year. This is background context, not a sign of current distress.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue rose 5.3% to $1.49B in 2025, the third consecutive year of growth. Net income went from losses in 2022 (-$15.8M) and 2023 (-$6.8M) to +$94.8M in 2024 and +$129.1M in 2025. 2025 operating margin was 10.7%, net margin 8.7%, ROE 12.2%, and ROA 5.6%. Trailing EPS is $4.32.

**Segment dynamics.** The profit story diverges sharply by segment:

- **Offshore Energy is the engine.** 2025 operating income rose 25.3% to $165.6M, with margin expanding from 14% to 17%. The drivers were legacy contracts resetting to leading-edge rates, near-100% effective utilization, and roughly $34M of vendor credits that lowered repairs and maintenance costs.
- **Government Services margin collapsed.** Operating income fell 75.9% to $5.1M (margin from 6% to 1%) on transition costs for the new IRCG and UKSAR2G contracts: subcontractor costs up $28.2M, plus higher personnel and amortization. Management characterizes this as a timing issue and guides 2026 Government Adjusted Operating Income to $70–80M as transitions complete (IRCG fully live February 2026; UKSAR2G transition concluding late 2026).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $198.4M; capex fell to $142M (from $255M in 2024 as the government-contract investment cycle ended); free cash flow was $56.4M. Liquidity stood at ~$347M at year-end 2025 and ~$393.6M ($342M cash) at March 2026. Net debt was ~$432M at March 2026, with D/E of 1.18x. The Altman Z-Score of 2.19 sits in the "grey zone," reflecting the leverage normal to capital-intensive aviation rather than distress; Piotroski signals are uniformly positive.

**Earnings quality caveats.** 2025 results benefited from a $22.5M FX gain and ~$34M of vendor credits. FX gains are noise (83% of revenue is non-US, primarily GBP-denominated), and vendor credits, while recurring in this industry, are lumpy. Adjusted EBITDA, which strips FX out, was ~$247M LTM, the cleaner metric to anchor on. Q1 2026 free cash flow was negative (-$12.6M) on seasonal working capital, which is normal for the quarter but worth flagging.

**Capital allocation pivot.** With the government-contract capex cycle largely complete, Bristow has shifted to shareholder returns: it initiated its first-ever quarterly dividend in Q1 2026 ($0.50/year annualized, ~1.2% yield) and has $121M remaining on a $125M buyback authorization. It also paid down $40M of UKSAR debt in 2025 and, in January 2026, closed $500M of 6.750% Senior Secured Notes due 2033 to redeem the 6.875% Notes due 2028, extending the ABL to 2031 and cutting pricing 25bps. The result is no material near-term maturities at a lower blended rate.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars, with materially different durability:

**1. Offshore rate repricing (highest-confidence near-term driver).** Offshore helicopter supply is structurally tight: limited new deliveries since the 2014–2021 downturn, near-100% effective utilization on medium/super-medium/heavy aircraft, fewer OEMs, and 24-month-plus lead times. Roughly half of offshore contracts reset to leading-edge rates in 2026, a tangible revenue tailwind. The offset is a mid-cycle activity plateau that management concedes will persist through much of 2026, with a pickup expected in 2H26 and beyond.

**2. Government margin recovery (highest-swing-factor, execution-dependent).** This is the single biggest variable in 2026. If IRCG/UKSAR2G transitions conclude on schedule and subcontractor costs roll off, Government Adjusted Operating Income roughly triples to the guided range. This is a timing-and-execution story rather than a demand story. The 10-K explicitly warns that FX strength and aircraft-availability penalties "could offset a portion" of the benefit, a qualifier that can compress the upside.

**3. AAM/eVTOL optionality (long-dated, capital-light).** Bristow has signed deposit/launch agreements with BETA Technologies, Electra.aero (5 EL9 hybrid-electric aircraft, 2029–2030 delivery slots), and Vertical Aerospace plus Skyports (UK air-taxi, ~2029). Bristow does not own or develop these aircraft; it is the operator/launch customer leveraging its AOCs and certification expertise. All commitments are deposit-only and refundable if certification deadlines slip. This should be valued as a long-dated call option, not a core earnings driver.

The growth picture must be read against the estimate-revision data, which has turned decisively negative. Current-year consensus EPS was cut 22.5% over 90 days; current-quarter estimates were cut 39.6%. Next-year EPS was cut 16.2% over 30 days. This is the opposite of the positive-revision velocity that would support a higher growth score. Combined with two consecutive large misses, it signals that the market's confidence in the 2026 recovery timing has weakened, even if the structural backlog is intact.

## Valuation Assessment

VTOL trades at ~10.7x trailing earnings and ~10.7x forward earnings on a stable EPS base near $4.32. Against the company's own near-term Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $295–325M, the ~$1.24B market cap and ~$432M net debt imply an EV of roughly $1.67B, or ~5.4x the midpoint of guided EBITDA, a reasonable multiple for a consolidated leader with a long-duration government backlog and a maturing capital-return profile.

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The named oilfield-equipment-and-services comparables (HLX at 100x, RES at 75.6x, CLB at 22.1x, INVX at 37.3x, AESI negative) are a heterogeneous group dominated by depressed-earnings or trough-cycle multiples, and the median P/E of 75.6 is not a meaningful benchmark for a business that earns money through stable production-transport and government SAR contracts rather than exploration-driven services. Bristow's recurring, contract-backed revenue stream warrants a different lens than commodity-levered service peers, and its single-digit multiple looks low precisely because the market is uncertain whether 2025's earnings level is sustainable through the Government transition drag.

Analyst price targets (mean $62, median $60, range $56–70) sit far above the current $41.16, implying ~46–51% upside. We treat these with caution. On a stock where estimates are falling 20%+ in 90 days, target distributions tend to lag reality on the downside; the targets predate the most recent cuts and the negative reaction to Q1. The valuation is supportive rather than asymmetric: the stock is cheap if 2026 EBITDA lands at the guided midpoint and Government margins inflect, but the recent miss pattern raises the probability that earnings come in at the low end of guidance, in which case the current multiple is fair rather than cheap.

We score Valuation a 6.0, modestly above neutral. The price is not stretched, and there is a credible mispricing case if the recovery executes, but falling estimates and the lack of a near-term positive catalyst prevent a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow is one of the largest operators in each of its core markets (North Sea, US Gulf, Brazil, Nigeria) and a leader in outsourced government SAR. The competitive position is durable and arguably improving on the government side, where Bristow has locked in 10+ year contracts that provide visibility "into the middle of the next decade." Incumbency, its "Target Zero" safety record, and entrenched customer relationships are advantages in an industry where certification and operating-certificate barriers are high and new-build aircraft lead times exceed two years.

The offsetting concerns are concentration and basin maturity. The top 10 customers represent 63% of revenue and the top 3 represent 36%; the UK SAR contract alone is ~19% of revenue and can be terminated at will by the UK Department for Transport, subject to a specified cancellation fee. Heavy reliance on the North Sea and US Gulf, both mature, declining E&P regions, caps the structural growth of the offshore book even as near-term rate repricing helps.

One management-disclosed metric tempers the competitive read: five-year total shareholder return of 139.1 (indexed) has lagged the peer group at 181.7. The business turnaround is recent, and the stock has not kept pace with peers over the measurement window. We score Competitive Position a 6.8, a leader with improving government positioning, offset by customer concentration and mature-basin exposure.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are concentrated and specific rather than diffuse sector risk:

1. **S92 / S76D fleet supply chain (most acute operational risk).** The S92 is ~29% of the fleet (61 of 216 aircraft); the 10-K explicitly cites significant parts-delivery delays forcing groundings and lost opportunities. Management is transitioning out of the S76D fleet by early 2027, and the forward-looking risk language newly added "AW189" to the parts-delay concern, a fresh item versus prior filings.

2. **Customer and contract concentration.** Top 3 customers at 36% of revenue; the at-will-terminable UK SAR contract at ~19%.

3. **Government budget / appropriation risk.** 26% of revenue depends on government funding; Q1 2026 disclosures newly emphasize potential US government shutdown effects.

4. **FX exposure.** 83% of revenue is non-US (primarily GBP). FX swings move both translated results and the "Other income" line, adding meaningful noise to GAAP earnings.

5. **Execution risk on the Government transition.** The entire 2026 earnings-recovery thesis depends on subcontractor and transition costs rolling off on schedule. Any overrun, or aircraft-availability penalties, directly compresses the guided Government Adjusted Operating Income.

6. **Labor.** ~60% of employees are unionized, with multiple collective bargaining agreements expired in late 2025 or expiring in 2026 (UK engineers, Nigeria, Norway, Brazil).

7. **Foreign-ownership voting cap.** FAA citizenship rules cap collective non-US voting power at 25%, a structural overhang on liquidity and market value.

The negative-estimate-revision momentum and two-quarter miss streak are the most immediate market risks. The market reacted negatively to an affirmed-guidance Q1, suggesting much of the 2026 recovery may already be discounted, which limits upside if execution merely meets rather than exceeds expectations.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 73.2% against 33.3% one-year historical volatility (a 39.9pp premium), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (73.2% at 1m versus 41.3% at 6m). ATM IV jumped from 37.8% to 80.8% over the roughly two weeks into early June while the price was roughly flat, indicating the market is bracing for a near-term catalyst (the implied move at the nearest expiry is ±17%, easing to ±12.9% at the 3m tenor). The skew is call-heavy (-28.3% put/call skew, P/C OI ratio 0.20), which is unusual for a stock with negative estimate momentum and suggests positioning is tilted toward upside or that hedging demand is concentrated on the call side. We read the elevated near-term IV primarily as event-premium ahead of a catalyst rather than directional information, consistent with the backwardation collapsing to 41.3% by the six-month tenor. This reinforces that the next print is binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$56–62).** Government Services margins inflect on schedule as IRCG and UKSAR2G transitions complete, lifting Government Adjusted Operating Income to the guided $70–80M; ~half of offshore contracts reprice to leading-edge rates, expanding Offshore margins further; 2026 revenue and EBITDA land at the guided midpoint or above ($1.58–1.69B / $295–325M); free cash flow recovers as capex stays low; and the buyback plus dividend signal capital-cycle maturity. On normalized FY27 earnings power approaching the prior $5+ EPS consensus, a 10–12x multiple supports a mid-to-high $50s valuation, with analyst targets at $60+. The eVTOL optionality provides a free long-dated call.

**Bear Case (~$30–33).** Government transition costs overrun or aircraft-availability penalties bite; the mid-cycle offshore activity plateau persists longer than 2H26; FX reverses the 2025 tailwind; and 2026 earnings come in at or below the low end of guidance. With estimates already falling 22% in 90 days and two consecutive large misses, a further guide-down would compress the multiple toward 7–8x on a lower EPS base, retesting the low-$30s (near the 52-week low of $29.26).

**Base Case (~$42–46).** Modest Government margin improvement, partial offshore repricing benefit, and full-year results near the low-to-mid point of guidance leave EPS in the high-$3 to low-$4 range. At ~10–11x, the stock holds roughly its current level, with the dividend and buyback providing a floor and offsetting modest multiple risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (fiscal Q2 2026, expected early August 2026), which will provide the first clean read on whether Government transition costs are rolling off and whether estimate revisions stabilize.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Government Services Adjusted Operating Income tracking toward the $70–80M annual guide (i.e., a quarterly run-rate inflection) in the Q2 or Q3 print, confirming the transition-cost roll-off.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions stabilize or turn positive after the recent 22% cut, ideally alongside an in-line-or-better quarter.
- Offshore segment showing the leading-edge rate repricing flowing through to margin expansion above the 17% achieved in 2025.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive earnings miss or a downward revision to the affirmed FY2026 revenue/EBITDA guidance.
- Evidence that S92/AW189 parts shortages or S76D transition costs are forcing material aircraft groundings that impair utilization or trigger availability penalties.
- Free cash flow turning persistently negative outside of seasonal Q1 effects, or a leverage increase that threatens the buyback/dividend.

## Conclusion

Bristow has executed a real operational turnaround, refinanced its balance sheet cleanly, and pivoted to shareholder returns from a position of structural strength in a consolidated, supply-tight industry. The long-term thesis (production-weighted offshore exposure, a decade-long government SAR backlog, and capital-light eVTOL optionality) is intact and not richly valued at ~10.7x earnings.

The near-term setup is the constraint. The 2026 earnings-recovery story depends on execution-and-timing in Government Services, consensus EPS has been cut sharply across every horizon, the last two quarters missed badly, insiders have sold into the highs with no offsetting open-market buying, and the market reacted negatively to a beat-and-affirm Q1. These are not signs of fundamental impairment, but they indicate that the recovery is contingent and that much of it may already be priced. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate VTOL a HOLD and would revisit on confirmation that Government margins are inflecting on schedule and that estimate revisions have bottomed. The declining score trajectory (6.9 to 6.5 to 6.0) reflects this deterioration in forward earnings momentum, not a change in the structural quality of the franchise.
