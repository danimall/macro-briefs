## Executive Summary

Hope Bancorp is a $1.6 billion market cap regional bank headquartered in Los Angeles, operating through Bank of Hope with $18.5 billion in total assets. The company is in the early stages of a multi-year transformation from a Korean-American community bank into the largest multicultural commercial banking platform in the United States, driven by two acquisitions: Territorial Bancorp (closed April 2025, adding Hawaii operations) and the pending SMBC Manubank Commercial Banking Unit (expected 2H 2026, adding $2.5B in loans and $2.7B in deposits).

The stock trades at $12.41, a 9.5% discount to tangible book value of $13.71/share, with trailing EPS of $0.49 (heavily impacted by $51.8M in notable items). Adjusted 2025 EPS was $0.89, and management projects >20% EPS accretion from SMBC in 2027. NIM has inflected positively (2.90% in Q1 2026, up 36bps YoY), criticized loans are declining, and the collaboration agreement with SMBC provides a unique referral channel with no peer equivalent. Against this: CRE concentration at 58% of loans (heavily Southern California), mixed earnings beat/miss history (2 in-line, 2 misses over the last 4 quarters), near-term estimate revisions trending negative, and dual integration execution risk.

The thesis is growth-oriented: the stock is cheap relative to what it could earn in 2027 ($1.60 consensus), but the path is laden with execution challenges and macro headwinds. At 7.8x consensus 2027 EPS, the valuation offers meaningful upside if management delivers. The risk is that CRE credit deterioration or integration missteps delay or prevent the expected profitability improvement.

## Company Overview

Hope Bancorp operates Bank of Hope across 74 branches in 12 states plus a representative office in Seoul, South Korea. The loan book ($14.6B) is dominated by commercial real estate (58%), followed by C&I (25%) and residential mortgage (17%). The bank holds National Preferred Lender status for SBA loans and has historically served Korean-American business communities, expanding into broader multicultural banking.

The company's strategic direction has shifted materially since 2024. The Territorial Bancorp acquisition (April 2025) added 29 branches in Hawaii and $1.07B in residential mortgages, diversifying away from CRE. The pending SMBC Manubank acquisition will add a Japanese Banking Division, creating a Japanese-Korean multicultural banking platform. SMBC's collaboration agreement provides ongoing referrals of Japanese mid-market corporate clients, a structural competitive advantage no other regional bank possesses.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability trajectory is the core story.** GAAP net income collapsed from $218M (2022) to $62M (2025), but this decline is misleading. 2025 included $51.8M in after-tax notable items: merger costs ($21.5M pre-tax), securities portfolio repositioning losses ($38.9M), and a California tax law change. Adjusted EPS was $0.89 in 2025 versus $0.85 in 2024, demonstrating that underlying profitability actually improved modestly.

Q1 2026 showed further improvement: net income of $30M ($0.23/share), up 40% YoY, with pre-provision net revenue up 43%. NIM expanded to 2.90% from 2.54% a year earlier, driven by deposit cost reduction (time deposit repricing as CDs mature at lower rates). Management expects an additional 5-7bps of deposit cost improvement per quarter.

**Capital position is strong.** CET1 at 12.27% provides substantial buffer above regulatory minimums. Tangible book value of $13.71/share exceeds the current stock price. The company initiated share repurchases in Q1 2026 (~$7M), which is modestly encouraging given the discount to book value.

**Credit quality is the primary fundamental concern.** NPAs nearly tripled from $45.5M (2023) to $136.1M (2025), driven by CRE loan downgrades. However, the trajectory appears to have inflected: criticized loans declined 7% sequentially and 28% YoY by Q1 2026. Net charge-offs of 0.20% are manageable. The ACL coverage ratio of 1.07% looks thin relative to the $136M in NPAs, though specific reserves on individually evaluated loans increased to $15.2M.

**Free cash flow of $151M in 2025** (operating CF $165M less $13M capex) comfortably supports the $72M annual dividend burden. Liquidity is adequate: $2.2B liquid assets and $5.95B in available FHLB/FRB borrowing capacity.

**Efficiency ratio at 78.13% (GAAP) / 68.6% (adjusted)** remains elevated versus high-performing regional bank peers (typically 55-62%). This reflects the cost structure of operating 74 branches pre-rationalization and the incremental overhead from Territorial integration. Improvement toward 60-62% is plausible over 2-3 years with SMBC scale benefits but is unproven.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is primarily acquisition-driven rather than organic:

**SMBC Manubank (2H 2026 close):** Adds approximately $2.5B in loans and $2.7B in deposits. The deposit mix is highly attractive (22% non-interest bearing, only 3% time deposits), which should improve overall funding costs. Management projects >20% EPS accretion in 2027 and ROTCE improvement to ~12%. At 12% ROTCE on a ~$14 TBV base, implied EPS would be approximately $1.65-1.70, consistent with the $1.60 consensus for 2027.

**NIM expansion:** The trajectory from 2.55% (2024) to 2.76% (2025) to 2.90% (Q1 2026) should continue as time deposits reprice lower. Each 10bps of NIM expansion on $18.5B in assets generates approximately $18.5M in pre-tax income, or roughly $0.11/share after tax.

**Organic loan growth:** Management targets >20% total loan growth for FY2026, though much of this is SMBC-related. Underlying organic growth has been modest, constrained by management's deliberate CRE concentration management.

**Near-term headwinds exist.** Estimate revisions are negative: current quarter EPS estimate has fallen 4.4% over 90 days, and next quarter is down 11%. This suggests the market is not yet pricing in the transformation benefits and may be concerned about interim credit costs or integration delays. The $1.60 next-year consensus (up 15.9% over 90 days) reflects the SMBC accretion expectation.

## Valuation Assessment

At $12.41, HOPE trades at:
- 0.91x tangible book value ($13.71)
- 22.9x trailing EPS ($0.49 — heavily depressed by notable items)
- 13.9x adjusted 2025 EPS ($0.89)
- 11.2x current-year consensus EPS ($1.11)
- 7.8x next-year consensus EPS ($1.60)

Peer medians: P/E 14.0x, dividend yield 2.8%. HOPE's 4.4% dividend yield provides a premium to peers, and the 7.8x forward (2027) P/E is cheap relative to both the peer median and the growth trajectory.

The discount to tangible book value is significant. Banks earning their cost of equity (roughly 10-12% ROTCE for community/regional banks) typically trade at 1.0-1.3x TBV. If SMBC accretion delivers as projected and ROTCE reaches 12%, a re-rating to 1.1-1.2x TBV implies $15-16.50 per share, 21-33% upside.

The market's skepticism is rational given: (a) recent GAAP earnings collapse, (b) rising NPAs, (c) execution risk on simultaneous integrations, and (d) tariff sensitivity of the customer base. But the magnitude of the discount implies that even modest execution success would generate meaningful returns.

**Key valuation risk:** If CRE credit losses accelerate or the SMBC deal encounters regulatory delays, the 2027 earnings estimate becomes unreliable and the stock could trade to 0.75-0.80x TBV ($10.30-11.00).

## Competitive Landscape

Hope Bancorp occupies a defensible niche as the largest multicultural regional bank in the U.S. Its primary competitive advantages are:

1. **Cultural affinity:** Deep relationships with Korean-American and, post-SMBC, Japanese business communities provide a customer acquisition channel that large money-center banks cannot replicate efficiently.

2. **SMBC collaboration agreement:** The referral partnership with one of the world's top-5 banks for Japanese mid-market corporate clients has no precedent among U.S. regional banks. This creates ongoing lead generation at minimal cost.

3. **SBA National Preferred Lender status:** Provides access to guaranteed government loan programs with gain-on-sale revenue.

**Competitive weaknesses include:**
- Scale disadvantage versus East West Bancorp ($75B assets, >1% ROA), the closest direct competitor
- Efficiency ratio significantly above peers, suggesting the cost base has not kept pace with revenue trajectory
- CRE concentration limits growth in a regulatory environment increasingly scrutinizing such exposures

The trajectory of competitive position is modestly improving: the SMBC deal broadens the addressable market and adds a referral channel that did not previously exist.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (High Impact, Medium Probability):** 54% of CRE is in Southern California. While weighted average LTV of 46% provides cushion, persistently high long-term rates create refinancing stress for borrowers. Office exposure is modest ($332M, 4% of CRE), but multi-tenant retail and hospitality segments face occupancy pressure in a consumer slowdown.

**Dual Integration Execution (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** Territorial (closed April 2025) is still being integrated while SMBC (expected 2H 2026) will layer additional complexity. Integrating a Japanese Banking Division into a Korean-American banking platform is unprecedented. Cultural, systems, and personnel challenges could delay expense synergies or cause customer attrition.

**Tariff/Trade Policy Risk (Medium Impact, Elevated Probability):** The bank's customer base is disproportionately exposed to U.S.-Asia trade flows. Tariff uncertainty (even post-Supreme Court ruling) could impair borrowers' ability to service debt, particularly in import/export businesses.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** The bank is modestly asset-sensitive in a falling rate environment (42% variable rate loans). If rates rise from here, NIM expansion could stall and variable-rate borrowers could face payment stress.

**Regulatory Risk:** Post-SMBC, CRE concentration will be approximately 320% of capital, well above the 300% threshold that triggers enhanced regulatory scrutiny. Management will need to actively manage this ratio, potentially constraining the most profitable lending segment.

**Catalyst risks:** No identifiable binary events could materially de-rate the stock beyond continued credit deterioration. The SMBC regulatory approval process is the key near-term catalyst (positive if approved smoothly by 2H 2026).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($15.00-16.50, 21-33% upside):**
- SMBC deal closes on time in 2H 2026, delivering >20% EPS accretion in 2027
- NIM continues expanding toward 3.10%+ as deposit costs normalize
- ROTCE reaches 12%, justifying 1.1-1.2x TBV valuation
- Criticized loans continue declining, releasing reserves to earnings
- SMBC collaboration agreement generates meaningful incremental loan originations
- Math: 12x forward 2027 EPS of $1.60 = $19.20; 1.1x TBV of ~$14 (post-dilution) = $15.40

**Bear Case ($10.00-10.50, 15-19% downside):**
- CRE credit deterioration accelerates; NCOs rise to 0.40-0.50%, requiring $40-60M in additional provision expense
- SMBC integration delays or cost overruns reduce accretion from >20% to <10%
- Tariff-related stress impairs the Asian trade-linked customer base
- Stock de-rates to 0.75x TBV on profitability concerns
- Math: 0.75x TBV of $13.71 = $10.28

**Base Case ($13.50-14.50, 9-17% upside):**
- SMBC closes as planned; EPS reaches $1.40-1.50 in 2027 (slightly below $1.60 consensus due to integration costs)
- NIM stabilizes at 2.95-3.00%
- Credit remains elevated but manageable (NCOs 0.25-0.30%)
- Stock re-rates to 1.0-1.05x TBV
- Math: 1.0x TBV of $13.71 = $13.71; 10x 2027 EPS of $1.45 = $14.50

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. The SMBC deal close (2H 2026) and first full quarter of combined operations (likely Q1 2027) are the key milestones for thesis validation.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock declines below $11.00 (0.80x TBV), providing 30%+ upside to base case with limited incremental fundamental deterioration
2. Q2 2026 results show NIM above 2.95% AND criticized loans continue declining sequentially
3. SMBC regulatory approval received with clear closing date, removing key execution uncertainty

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 0.40% in any quarter, signaling CRE credit deterioration beyond management control
2. SMBC deal encounters material regulatory delay (pushed to 2027) or restructured terms that reduce accretion below 10%
3. Deposit outflows exceed 5% in a quarter, suggesting confidence loss in customer base

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July 2026) and SMBC regulatory approval timeline update.

## Conclusion

Hope Bancorp presents a credible transformation thesis at an undemanding valuation (0.91x TBV, 7.8x 2027 consensus EPS, 4.4% dividend yield). The SMBC Manubank acquisition creates a strategically unique multicultural banking platform with a referral agreement from a top-5 global bank. NIM is expanding, criticized loans are declining, and capital ratios provide meaningful buffer.

However, the near-term earnings profile remains weak, estimate revisions are negative on a quarterly basis, the earnings beat track record is poor (2 in-line, 2 misses over the last four quarters), and dual integration execution risk is real. CRE concentration in Southern California creates vulnerability to a broader real estate correction at precisely the time that commercial real estate refinancing stress is elevated nationally. No insiders are buying stock at these levels.

The risk-reward at current prices is modestly favorable but lacks a near-term catalyst sufficient to overcome the execution uncertainty. The 4.4% dividend yield provides adequate compensation for holding through the integration period. This is a growth-hold: the 12-18 month thesis is positive, but conviction is insufficient for an outright BUY given the mixed fundamental signals and macro headwinds facing CRE-heavy regional banks.
