## Executive Summary

Ameriprise Financial is a $40.9 billion diversified financial services company managing $1.66 trillion in assets across wealth management, asset management, and retirement/protection solutions. The business is increasingly dominated by its Advice & Wealth Management (AWM) segment, which contributed ~70% of adjusted operating earnings in 2025 and benefits from structural growth in fee-based advisory wrap accounts ($650B). The company has compounded adjusted operating EPS at ~12% annually over recent years and returned $29.7B cumulatively in share repurchases against a current market cap of $40.9B.

At $446 per share (~12.3x forward earnings), AMP trades at a 21% discount to the 15.6x peer median despite generating a 52.8% adjusted operating ROE and maintaining 4 consecutive quarters of earnings beats. The discount partially reflects legitimate concerns: high equity market sensitivity ($375M pretax income hit per 10% equity decline), persistent asset management outflows ($32.5B in 9M 2025), and legacy variable annuity/LTC tail risks. However, the structured variable annuity (SVA) growth engine, aggressive buyback program ($4.5B authorization through June 2027), and the ongoing advisor recruitment strategy provide concrete earnings support through a challenging macro backdrop. The stock has corrected 19% from its February 2026 high of $550, creating a more favorable entry point.

## Company Overview

Ameriprise operates four segments:

**Advice & Wealth Management (AWM)** — ~70% of earnings. Approximately 10,000 financial advisors providing financial planning, investment advisory, and brokerage services. Wrap account assets of $650B generate recurring advisory fees. Average revenue per advisor has reached a record $1.1M, and the company recruited 336 experienced advisors in 2025 without acquisition spending.

**Asset Management** — Operates as Columbia Threadneedle Investments, managing $674.7B in AUM. Serves retail and institutional clients globally (U.S., U.K., Europe). Experiencing persistent net outflows ($32.5B in 9M 2025 vs. $21.3B in 9M 2024) as the active-to-passive shift continues, partially offset by market appreciation.

**Retirement & Protection Solutions** — Variable annuities ($91B account balances), structured variable annuities ($20.3B, doubled in two years), fixed annuities, and life/disability insurance through RiverSource Life. SVAs are the fastest-growing product line.

**Corporate & Other** — Closed blocks of long-term care insurance and fixed annuities, plus corporate overhead. Runs at ~$300M+ annual loss.

FY2025 adjusted operating EPS grew 12% year-over-year to $39.34, within management's 12-15% target range. GAAP EPS of $36.28 grew faster due to favorable mark-to-market swings on non-traditional long-duration products. The most recent quarter (Q4 2025, ending December) delivered $10.83 EPS vs. $10.43 consensus, a 3.9% beat.

Net income margin reached 26.9% in FY2025, and the adjusted operating ROE stands at 52.8%. The high ROE reflects both genuine operational efficiency and the mechanical effect of a small equity base ($6.45B stockholders' equity) compressed by cumulative buybacks.

### Cash Flow and Capital Return

Free cash flow was $8.16B in FY2025 (after $162M capex), supporting one of the most aggressive capital return programs in financial services. Through 9M 2025, the company returned ~$2.5B via $2.05B in buybacks (at ~$513 average price) and $461M in dividends. Outstanding shares declined from ~100M to ~93M over the past year.

The board authorized $4.5B in additional repurchases through June 2027, with $3.5B remaining as of September 2025. At the current $446 share price, that authorization represents approximately 8.6% of the market cap. Combined with the 1.4% dividend yield, the total shareholder yield is approximately 9-10% annualized.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $190.9B include $80.7B in separate account assets (offset by equal liabilities), making the "operating" balance sheet substantially smaller than the headline figure. Key metrics:

- **Stockholders' equity:** $6.45B (up from $5.23B at year-end 2024)
- **AOCI:** $(969M), improving from $(1.91B) at year-end 2024
- **Long-term debt:** $3.08B (well-laddered: 2026, 2028, 2032, 2033, 2035)
- **Cash and equivalents:** $9.57B
- **Debt-to-equity:** ~48%
- **Investment portfolio quality:** 99% investment grade, 70% AAA/AA rated
- **Available liquidity:** $2.5B parent company + $1B undrawn revolver + $11.7B FHLB capacity + ~$9.3B Fed borrowing capacity

The February 2025 issuance of $750M in 5.20% notes due 2035 (used to repay $500M of 3.0% notes at maturity in April 2025) modestly increased leverage but extended the maturity profile. Overall, the balance sheet is conservatively managed with ample liquidity buffers.

## Growth Analysis

### Positive Growth Vectors

**1. Wrap Account Asset Growth:** Fee-based advisory assets of $650B generate recurring revenue at approximately 1% annual fee rates. Even in flat markets, organic advisor recruitment and existing client asset aggregation drive mid-single-digit growth.

**2. Structured Variable Annuity (SVA) Expansion:** SVA account balances doubled from $10.7B to $20.3B in two years, with $2.8B in quarterly deposits. These products offer index-linked returns with downside buffers and carry higher margins than legacy VA products with guaranteed living benefits.

**3. Advisor Recruitment:** 336 experienced advisors joined in 2025 with record $1.1M average revenue per advisor. The company achieves this through upfront transition loans ($1.55B outstanding) rather than acquisitions, preserving capital efficiency.

**4. Share Count Reduction:** The ~7% annual share count reduction mechanically increases EPS by approximately that amount, independent of operating earnings growth.

### Growth Constraints

**1. Asset Management Outflows:** Columbia Threadneedle experienced $32.5B in net outflows in 9M 2025, up from $21.3B in the prior year. This is a structural headwind driven by active-to-passive migration and the loss of a large institutional client. While fund performance remains solid (65-91% of assets in top-half over various periods), industry trends are unfavorable.

**2. AWM Margin Compression:** AWM pretax adjusted operating margin declined from 30.3% (9M 2024) to 29.0% (9M 2025), reflecting lower spread income as certificate balances declined $3.1B and yields compressed. The shift from spread to fee-based income is strategically positive but creates near-term margin pressure.

**3. Estimate Revisions Turning Negative:** Consensus current-year EPS has been revised down 1.6% over 30 days (from $43.15 to $42.48), and next-quarter estimates fell 1.4%. The 90-day trend remains positive (+2.0% current year, +3.5% next year), but the directional shift is notable given deteriorating equity markets and macro uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameriprise occupies a distinct position in the wealth management/financial services landscape. Its integrated model combining proprietary investment products (Columbia Threadneedle funds, RiverSource annuities) with an advisor distribution network creates higher revenue per advisor and stronger client retention than pure-platform models like LPL Financial.

Key competitive dynamics:

**Advisor economics:** Ameriprise's upfront transition loans ($1.55B outstanding) function as golden handcuffs, creating switching costs for recruited advisors. The 336 experienced advisors recruited in 2025 represent organic growth without M&A premiums.

**Product integration:** The ability to manufacture and distribute proprietary products (funds, annuities, insurance) captures a larger share of the client relationship than broker-dealers relying entirely on third-party products. This explains the ROE premium.

**SVA differentiation:** The structured variable annuity product line positions Ameriprise in the fastest-growing segment of the annuity market. SVAs appeal to clients seeking equity participation with buffer protection, and Ameriprise's embedded manufacturing capability (RiverSource Life) provides structural margin advantages.

**Asset management headwinds:** Columbia Threadneedle faces the same active-to-passive migration pressuring all traditional active managers. The segment contributes only ~20% of operating earnings and is partially offset by Ameriprise's advisors directing flows into Columbia Threadneedle products through the integrated model.

## Risk Assessment

### Equity Market Sensitivity (HIGH)
This is the dominant risk. A 10% equity market decline reduces pretax income by an estimated $548M ($375M adjusted operating), approximately 10% of annual earnings. With recession probability elevated, SPY IV at 28%, and consumer sentiment at record lows, the probability of a >10% equity drawdown over the next 12 months is meaningful. The stock's 19% decline from its February high already partially reflects this risk, but further equity deterioration would compress both AUM-linked revenue and the stock's multiple.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)
A 100bps rate increase adds $471M pretax; conversely, rate cuts would be modestly negative. With hike probability at 35-45%, this risk is contained. However, the higher-for-longer rate stance has caused $3.1B in certificate product attrition as clients seek better alternatives.

### Legacy Liability Tail Risks (MODERATE)
Variable annuity guaranteed living benefits ($2.1B net amount at risk) and long-term care insurance ($5.3B net liability, $2.7B reinsured through Genworth) create non-trivial tail risk. The Genworth reinsurance counterparty exposure is a specific concern; management's statements about "credit protections in rehabilitation or insolvency proceedings" acknowledge this is a live issue.

### Asset Management Structural Decline (MODERATE)
Persistent outflows from Columbia Threadneedle reflect the secular active-to-passive shift. This is partially mitigated by the segment's declining share of total earnings, but a sustained acceleration in outflows could force restructuring charges or impair the franchise value.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term IV of 32.2% is 6.8 percentage points above historical vol of 25.4%, indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The backwardated term structure (32.2% near-term vs. 28.4% at 6-month) and elevated put/call volume ratio of 8.50 confirm institutional hedging activity. Put/call skew of 10.4% in the front week is elevated. The OI P/C ratio of 1.50 indicates meaningful but not extreme put accumulation. This positioning is consistent with a financial stock exposed to equity market stress and credit concerns. The options market is pricing sector-wide hedging against macro deterioration that would compress AUM and fee revenue, rather than company-specific distress.

- SVA product growth continues at $2.5B+ quarterly run rate, reaching $30B+ in account balances by year-end 2026

## Conclusion

Ameriprise Financial offers a combination of attributes rarely found together: a growing, high-quality wealth management franchise, aggressive capital return ($29.7B cumulative buybacks), a 52.8% adjusted operating ROE, and a valuation at a 21% discount to peers. The stock's 19% correction from its February high reflects legitimate macro concerns: equity market sensitivity is the dominant risk, and the deteriorating consumer/geopolitical environment creates real downside scenarios.

However, the correction appears to overstate the probability of a severe outcome. Fee-based advisory revenue from $650B in wrap assets provides a recurring base, the SVA growth engine adds $2.5B+ quarterly in new deposits, and the 7% annual share count reduction mechanically boosts per-share earnings. At 10.5x current-year and 9.7x next-year consensus estimates, the stock is priced for a meaningful earnings decline that would require a prolonged equity bear market to materialize.

The negative near-term estimate revisions (-1.6% over 30 days) and elevated options market hedging warrant attention, but 90-day revision trends remain positive (+2.0% current year, +3.5% next year), and the 4/4 quarterly beat record supports confidence in the consensus floor. BUY with a $530 base case target, with the $4.5B buyback program providing ongoing support and limiting downside duration.
