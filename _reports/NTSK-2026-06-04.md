**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Netskope is a cloud-native cybersecurity vendor competing in the Secure Access Service Edge (SASE) and Security Service Edge (SSE) market, which IPO'd in September 2025 at $19 and now trades at ~$12.40 after a roughly 56% peak-to-trough decline from its $27.99 high. The drawdown has been driven by a combination of post-lockup share supply (lockup expired March 13, 2026; share count roughly doubled to ~400M as preferred converted), an unfinished path to GAAP profitability, and a market that met a beat-and-raise quarter with a sharp selloff. The operating business underneath the GAAP noise is improving: revenue grew 28% YoY in Q1 FY2027 to $201.6M, ARR reached $845M (+29%), net retention improved to 116%, and non-GAAP operating margin has compressed losses from -56% (FY24) to -16% (FY26). The company turned operating-cash-flow positive (+$38.1M) and free-cash-flow positive (+$12.4M) for the first time in FY26.

The investment question is whether the ~56% drawdown reflects fundamental deterioration or multiple compression on an otherwise improving business. The evidence points to the latter. Estimate revisions are positive over 90 days (current-year EPS +9.8%, next-year +12.0%), the company has beaten on the bottom line in its two reported quarters as a public company, and all 18 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with the lowest target ($14) sitting above the current price. Three genuine overhangs prevent a constructive call: a convertible note structure (~$529M combined principal-plus-accrued) that accrues toward a punitive 13.5-14% compounded return and is deeply out of the money at the current price, meaning likely cash repayment; substantial dilution and continued post-IPO stock-comp expense; and a just-announced CFO retirement during the company's first public year.

The bull case (~$18-20) and bear case (~$7-8) bracket the current price with roughly symmetric risk/reward. Combined with an improving operating trajectory offset by a real but multi-year capital-structure overhang and decelerating growth, this is a two-sided setup. We rate the stock HOLD.

## Company Overview

Netskope, founded in 2012 and led by co-founder/CEO/Chairman Sanjay Beri, sells a unified cloud-security platform, Netskope One, that bundles 25+ products across three domains. Security products include CASB, Next-Gen Secure Web Gateway, DLP, Zero Trust Network Access, threat protection, SSPM/DSPM, an enterprise browser, and a new AI Security suite (AI Gateway, AI Guardrails, Agentic Broker, AI Red Teaming). Networking products include Firewall-as-a-Service, SD-WAN, IoT/OT security, and DNS-as-a-Service. Analytics include UEBA, Digital Experience Management, and Data Lineage.

The platform runs on NewEdge, a proprietary private cloud of 125+ full-compute edge data centers across 80 regions. Netskope owns and operates this network rather than reselling hyperscaler infrastructure, which provides single-pass inline inspection, latency guarantees, and data-sovereignty localization. The company holds ~270 patents (50+ in AI/ML). As of the latest reporting, Netskope serves 4,733 customers (+21% YoY), approximately one-third of the Fortune 100, with 1,531 accounts at ARR above $100K representing 86% of total ARR. Subscription revenue is ~99% of the total. Fiscal year ends January 31.

The company is a credible challenger to Zscaler and Palo Alto Networks in SSE/SASE convergence, a category at the center of the enterprise security spend cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:**
- FY2024: $406.9M
- FY2025: $538.3M (+32%)
- FY2026: $709.0M (+32%)
- Q1 FY2027 (reported June 3, 2026): $201.6M (+28% YoY); ARR $845M (+29%); net new ARR $34M in the quarter

Growth is decelerating in a controlled fashion: 32% (FY26) → 28% (Q1 FY27) → ~24% guided for FY27, consistent with management's own disclosed warning that growth rates will decline. Net retention improved to 116% from 113%, and roughly 71% of FY26 growth came from existing-customer expansion, a healthy quality signal for a subscription model.

**Profitability and the GAAP/non-GAAP wedge:**
- Gross margin: 68% GAAP / 75% non-GAAP, and rising
- GAAP net loss FY2026: -$679.4M (vs. -$354.5M FY25), distorted by ~$516M of stock-based comp triggered by IPO liquidity-vesting (a step-up event) plus a ~$34M convertible-note fair-value loss
- Non-GAAP operating margin: -16%, improved from -34% (FY25) and -56% (FY24)
- Accumulated deficit: $2.6B

The GAAP loss substantially overstates the operating reality. The non-GAAP operating margin progression from -56% to -16% over three years is the more accurate read on the business, and the underlying unit economics (75% non-GAAP gross margin, 116% NRR) are improving each year. Q1 FY27 GAAP net loss was -$116.5M (-$0.29/share) against a non-GAAP loss of -$0.06/share, again driven by the post-IPO RSU/PSU expense overhang that will persist for several quarters.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — the genuine bright spot:**
- Operating cash flow turned positive at +$38.1M in FY26 (from -$110.7M)
- Free cash flow +$12.4M (2% margin), the first cash-positive year
- Liquidity: ~$1.2B cash plus marketable securities post-IPO ($992M net proceeds)

The Altman Z-score of -0.07 ("distress") and debt/equity of 8.11 are misleading. Both are artifacts of SBC-driven GAAP losses and low book equity ($194.5M) sitting beneath convertible-note liabilities. Near-term insolvency is not the risk: ~$1.2B of liquidity against the convertible structure provides roughly 2.3x coverage of combined principal-plus-accrued (~$528.8M) today, and the nearest convertible maturity is 2028. The genuine balance-sheet concern is the structure and economics of the convertibles, not solvency over the next 12-24 months.

## Growth Analysis

Netskope is a direct beneficiary of two durable secular demand drivers: the SASE/SSE convergence cycle as enterprises retire appliance-based legacy security, and the emerging "secure AI adoption" category (real-time coaching on data entered into ChatGPT/Gemini, AI Gateway, agentic security). The AI security angle is a legitimate tailwind and aligns with the broader bifurcation we see across enterprise software, where infrastructure and security-layer vendors with platform breadth are outperforming application-layer SaaS facing displacement.

Forward estimate revisions are positive and improving over the 90-day window: current-quarter EPS -6.9% (modestly negative), but current-year +9.8% and next-year +12.0%, with current-period revisions flat over 30 days. The combination of revenue growth above 28% and rising full-year estimates is constructive, though the magnitude of revision does not reach the threshold that would, on its own, signal aggressive accumulation, and the company is not at a trough — it is decelerating from a higher base. This is a quality compounder gradually turning the corner on profitability, decelerating from a higher base rather than recovering from a depressed level.

The earnings track record supports modest confidence in forward estimates: the company beat on the bottom line in both reported quarters (39.6% and 16.0% surprises on small absolute numbers), and management raised the FY27 outlook to ~24% growth alongside the Q1 beat. The key growth risk is durability of net new ARR ($34M in Q1) against intensifying competition from far larger rivals with bundling power.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$12.40 and ~400M shares, market cap is approximately $5.0B (the snapshot $2.3B figure reflects the pre-conversion share count and is stale). Net of ~$1.2B cash less ~$529M convertibles, enterprise value is roughly $4.3B against FY27 revenue tracking toward ~$880M (implied by ~24% growth on the FY26 $709.0M base), implying EV/sales of ~4.9x on current-year revenue. On any reasonable construction, the stock trades at a discount to high-growth security peers (Zscaler and CrowdStrike command meaningfully higher revenue multiples), reflecting the dilution overhang, the convertible liability, and the unfinished GAAP profitability path.

Trailing and forward P/E are not meaningful given GAAP losses, and we do not penalize the stock for a negative multiple driven by non-cash SBC. The relevant frame is the EV/sales discount versus peers adjusted for slower growth and the capital-structure overhang. The discount is partly justified — Netskope grows slower than Zscaler did at comparable scale and carries a compounding convertible — but it is not so steep as to be obviously mispriced in either direction.

On analyst targets: the consensus mean ($17.89) and median ($17.00) sit ~37-44% above spot, and even the lowest target ($14.00) is above the current price, with 18 of 18 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy. We treat these as supportive but not decisive — analyst targets on a stock at a reasonable multiple with positive revisions tend to lag, but the bear-case downside to ~$7-8 (a re-rating to a deeper discount on convert-servicing fears or a growth disappointment) is real and roughly symmetric to the upside. That symmetry is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Netskope occupies one of cybersecurity's most strategically important and contested categories. Its NewEdge owned-network and single-platform architecture are genuine technical advantages over appliance-legacy incumbents and stitched-together platform vendors, and its single-pass inline inspection plus data-sovereignty localization differentiate it on enterprise procurement criteria. The >$100K ARR cohort (86% of ARR) indicates a real enterprise franchise rather than a low-end book.

The competitive threat is structural. Netskope competes against Palo Alto Networks, Cisco, Fortinet, and Broadcom — all of which can bundle SSE/SASE into existing footprints — and against the SSE pure-play leader Zscaler. AI security risks becoming a feature war won on distribution rather than architecture, which would disadvantage a sub-scale challenger. Channel concentration (top 5 partners = 35% of revenue) and a heavy offshore footprint (67% of employees abroad, including 29.5% in India and 8% in Taiwan) add operational and geopolitical exposure. On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving — share is being gained in a growing category — but the company is the challenger, not the leader, and the resource asymmetry caps the multiple.

## Risk Assessment

**Convertible PIK toggle notes — the most underappreciated risk.** The structure consists of $401M (2028 notes, 3.75% coupon, conversion ~$23.75) plus $75M (2029 notes, 3.00%, conversion ~$24.70). At ~$12.40, both tranches are deeply out of the money against conversion prices near $24, which means cash repayment is the likely path. The economics are punitive: the 2028 notes accrue toward a 14%/yr compounded return (potentially approaching ~$913M owed at maturity), the 2029 notes at 13.5% (~$145M), and change-of-control repurchase rights run 140-170% of principal. The PIK toggle preserves cash near-term but compounds the liability. This is a real, escalating claim against the ~$1.2B cash balance over a multi-year horizon. It is not a near-term solvency risk — coverage of combined principal-plus-accrued is roughly 2.3x and the nearest maturity is 2028 — but it materially constrains capital flexibility and is the single most important item a standard screen misses.

**Dilution and stock comp.** Share count roughly doubled from ~214M (Jan 2026) to ~400M (Q1 FY27) as preferred converted and the lockup expired (3/13/26). Post-IPO RSU/PSU expense will continue to depress GAAP results for several quarters, and the supply overhang has been a primary driver of the price decline.

**Growth deceleration into intense competition.** 32% → ~24% guided growth against rivals with larger budgets and bundling power.

**Governance.** Multi-class structure gives Class B holders 20 votes/share and 97.3% of voting power; insiders/5%+ holders control ~61.6%. Class A holders have minimal influence, and the structure may exclude the stock from some indices. Emerging-growth-company status means reduced disclosure and no external ICFR attestation yet.

**CFO succession.** The June 3, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) disclosed that CFO Andrew Del Matto plans to retire — a notable transition for a newly public company, and one to monitor for successor quality and continuity.

**Insider activity.** CEO Beri sold $14M+ and CFO Del Matto $3-4M of Class A in Dec 2025-Jan 2026 at ~$17 (largely RSU-settlement-related). No open-market purchases have occurred on the way down to ~$10-12. We weight this as low-signal given the RSU mechanics and the contradicting positive estimate revisions, but the absence of insider conviction at depressed levels warrants monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$18-20, +45-60%).** The operating inflection continues: non-GAAP operating margin keeps improving toward breakeven, free cash flow scales, and the convert-servicing concern fades as cash generation proves out. NRR holds at/above 116%, AI security becomes a meaningful net-new ARR driver, and the EV/sales multiple re-rates toward the security peer group as profitability visibility improves. Analyst targets (mean $17.89) prove to be the floor rather than the ceiling, and dilution overhang clears as post-lockup supply is absorbed.

**Bear Case (~$7-8, -35-44%).** Growth decelerates faster than the ~24% guide as larger rivals bundle SSE into existing footprints, net new ARR disappoints, and the market re-prices the convertible liability as a structural constraint on capital returns. GAAP losses persist longer than expected, the CFO transition introduces execution friction, and continued share supply plus weak insider conviction keep a lid on the multiple. A re-rating to a deeper EV/sales discount drives the stock back toward post-IPO lows.

The two scenarios bracket the current price roughly symmetrically. The operating trajectory is genuinely improving, the balance sheet is liquid, and the decline is largely valuation/lockup-driven rather than a reflection of fundamental deterioration, which argues against an AVOID. But the convertible overhang, dilution, decelerating growth, and management transition are real enough to prevent a BUY. We rate the stock HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY27, expected early September 2026), with the CFO successor announcement as an interim monitoring point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Non-GAAP operating margin improves to better than -8% (from -16%) with free cash flow margin sustained above 5%, demonstrating a credible path to GAAP profitability and convert servicing from operations.
2. Net new ARR re-accelerates (Q2 net new ARR above the Q1 $34M run-rate) and management holds or raises the ~24% FY27 growth guide, confirming the AI security tailwind is converting to bookings.
3. A high-quality CFO successor is named with continuity, and forward estimate revisions extend the +9.8%/+12.0% positive trend into the next reporting cycle.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below ~18% YoY or net new ARR contracts, signaling competitive displacement rather than orderly deceleration.
2. Operating cash flow turns negative again or management signals it will issue equity/raise debt to fund convert repayment, confirming the capital-structure constraint is binding.
3. NRR falls below 110% or gross margin compresses, indicating erosion of the core unit economics.

## Conclusion

Netskope is a competitively legitimate SASE/SSE challenger showing a real operating inflection that the market has discounted heavily on post-lockup supply, dilution, and skepticism about the path to GAAP profitability. The ~56% drawdown is largely valuation and supply driven, not evidence of fundamental deterioration; estimate revisions are positive, the company has beaten on the bottom line in both public quarters, and all 18 covering analysts rate it Buy with the lowest target above spot.

The offsetting concerns are substantive: a convertible structure that accrues at 13.5-14%/yr toward a potential ~$1B+ cash claim by 2028 maturity, ongoing dilution, decelerating growth into a competitive field of much larger rivals, and a CFO transition. These do not constitute structural impairment — cash coverage of combined principal-plus-accrued is roughly 2.3x, the nearest maturity is two years out, and the business is cash-generative — but they do cap the upside and create genuine downside symmetry. With a bull case near $18-20 and a bear case near $7-8 bracketing the current price, and an operating trajectory improving against a multi-year capital-structure overhang, the call is **HOLD**. We would turn constructive on confirmation that margin expansion and net new ARR re-acceleration are converting the operating inflection into a clean profitability and deleveraging path.
