**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Apellis is no longer a standalone investable equity. On March 31, 2026, Biogen (BIIB) agreed to acquire the company for approximately $5.6 billion, structured as $41.00 per share in cash plus a $2.00 per share contingent value right (CVR) tied to future sales milestones. Reuters, CNBC, and multiple other outlets confirm the deal has closed and Biogen has begun integrating the business, pausing or discontinuing most of Apellis's early-stage research programs and trimming R&D headcount while retaining the two approved commercial products, Syfovre and Empaveli. The relevant analysis is a completed-M&A and CVR-valuation exercise rather than a forward discounted-cash-flow equity thesis.

The acquisition capped a rapid commercial ramp. Revenue scaled roughly 13x in three years, from $75 million in 2022 to about $1.0 billion in 2025, and the company crossed into GAAP profitability for the first time in FY2025 (net income of +$22 million, operating income of +$55 million, free cash flow of +$45 million) after accumulating a deficit of roughly $3.0 billion during its pre-commercial years. Biogen paid a premium reported at roughly 137% to the pre-announcement price to capture two de-risked, revenue-generating rare-disease assets across four indications, with the C3 glomerulopathy kidney indication cited by Biogen's management as the strategic driver.

For any residual holder, the only remaining source of upside beyond the $41 cash is the $2 CVR, which pays only if specified sales milestones (most likely tied to Empaveli's kidney franchise) are achieved. Because the definitive merger agreement's CVR triggers could not be independently verified and the deal has already closed at a fixed cash price, we rate the situation HOLD with an uncertain rationale. There is no equity-appreciation case remaining; the cash consideration is locked, and the CVR is a binary, milestone-dependent claim.

## Company Overview

Apellis is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company built on complement-cascade (C3) inhibition through its lead molecule, pegcetacoplan, marketed under two brands. SYFOVRE (intravitreal pegcetacoplan) treats geographic atrophy secondary to age-related macular degeneration and was the first FDA-approved therapy in that large retinal-ophthalmology market. EMPAVELI (systemic pegcetacoplan) treats paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) and, per Biogen's deal rationale, has expanded into rare kidney disease, specifically C3 glomerulopathy (C3G) and IC-MPGN.

The company's competitive moat rested on first-mover status in complement biology across both geographic atrophy and complement-mediated kidney and hematologic diseases. That moat, along with the run-rate revenue base of roughly $1 billion, was the acquisition prize. Biogen's cull of the discovery pipeline within about a month of closing confirms the deal value sat in the two approved commercial products, not the platform.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, Apellis exhibited strong revenue growth and had just reached the operating leverage that turns commercial biotechs profitable. Forward growth would have depended on continued Syfovre uptake in geographic atrophy against Astellas's Izervay, the Empaveli PNH franchise, and the expansion into C3G/IC-MPGN kidney disease. Positive five-year GALE extension data from November 2025 (Syfovre delaying GA progression by roughly 1.5 years) supported the long-term efficacy narrative.

That standalone growth story ended with the close of the Biogen transaction. Biogen has paused or discontinued most Apellis research programs, so any long-term upside now rests narrowly on the two approved assets inside Biogen's portfolio. For an APLS holder, there is no forward growth exposure remaining beyond the milestone conditions embedded in the CVR. The growth score reflects a business that was genuinely inflecting but whose forward trajectory is now internalized within an acquirer.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard equity valuation metrics do not apply. The company has been acquired at a fixed cash price of $41.00 per share, with a $2.00 CVR representing the only variable component. There is no forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, or peer-multiple comparison that changes the value of a share held at deal close, because the cash consideration is contractually fixed.

The relevant valuation question is the probability-weighted value of the $2 CVR. If we assign a placeholder 40–60% probability to achieving the milestones (a range that cannot be tightened without the definitive merger agreement's specific triggers), the expected CVR value is roughly $0.80–$1.20, implying a fair total value near $41.80–$42.20 per share. This is a fixed cash payout plus a modest, binary milestone claim. We score valuation as neutral because the situation offers neither mispricing to exploit nor a value trap to avoid; the price is set by the merger terms.

The prior-period revenue ramp and profitability inflection were what allowed Biogen to justify the reported ~137% premium. The market priced the two commercial assets rather than the discovery platform, which Biogen validated by culling the pipeline post-close.

## Competitive Landscape

In geographic atrophy, Apellis's Syfovre competes head-to-head with Astellas's Izervay (avacincaptad pegol), the two first-approved GA therapies, both complement-pathway drugs. Competition has pressured Syfovre's growth trajectory and pricing, compounded by the 2023 retinal vasculitis safety events that damaged the franchise's reputation. In PNH and rare kidney disease, Empaveli competes in complement-mediated indications, with the C3G/IC-MPGN kidney opportunity representing the explicit strategic rationale for Biogen, which sought rare-disease growth to offset declining legacy multiple-sclerosis revenue.

The company's competitive position was reasonable but not dominant: first-mover advantage in complement biology, offset by a credible direct competitor in its largest market and a durable safety overhang in the GA franchise. Inside Biogen, the assets gain the distribution and balance-sheet support of a larger rare-disease operator, but standalone competitive dynamics no longer accrue to APLS holders.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is now deal-and-CVR risk. The cash portion is locked, so downside on the cash consideration is negligible. The variable risk sits entirely in the $2 CVR: if the specified sales milestones (likely tied to Empaveli/kidney) are missed, the CVR pays zero. Because the precise triggers could not be verified against the definitive merger agreement, the CVR's probability of payment is genuinely uncertain.

Underlying commercial risks that feed CVR outcomes include the Syfovre safety overhang from the 2023 retinal vasculitis events, intensifying GA competition from Izervay, and the durability of thin newly established profitability. Biogen's pipeline cull removes long-term optionality but does not directly affect the CVR, which is tied to the retained commercial products.

Governance risk is low. The proxy data show pay-for-performance alignment: CEO Cedric Francois's "compensation actually paid" swung to –$1.09 million in 2024 (from +$14.3 million in 2023) as the equity-heavy package penalized management during the safety-driven drawdown, with a modest 2.7x CEO-to-median pay ratio and no related-party red flags.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($43+):** The CVR milestones are achieved in full, delivering the additional $2.00 per share on top of the $41.00 cash. This requires Empaveli's kidney franchise (C3G/IC-MPGN) to hit the specified sales thresholds under Biogen's commercialization. Total realized value approaches $43.

**Bear Case ($41):** The CVR milestones are missed and the contingent payment lapses to zero. Holders realize only the $41.00 cash consideration. Because the cash is contractually fixed, this represents the floor, not a loss of principal below the deal price.

The asymmetry here is narrow and fully bounded by the merger terms: a fixed $41 floor and a $2 milestone-contingent upside. No scenario re-rates the equity on fundamental business performance, because the equity no longer trades as a going concern.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Because APLS is a closed-deal situation, standard price-target framing is replaced by deal-terms framing.

- **Base case value: ~$41.80–$42.20** — the $41.00 fixed cash plus a probability-weighted CVR of roughly $0.80–$1.20, assuming a 40–60% milestone-achievement probability. This cannot be tightened without the definitive CVR triggers.
- **Bull case value: $43.00** — full CVR payment on milestone achievement.
- **Bear case value: $41.00** — CVR expires worthless; cash floor realized.

**Timeframe:** The cash consideration is realized at deal close (already occurred per June 2026 reporting). The CVR resolves over the milestone measurement period, likely 2027 and beyond, tied to Empaveli/kidney sales trajectories under Biogen.

**Upgrade triggers (to a positive view on the residual CVR):** Confirmation from the definitive merger agreement that milestone thresholds are achievable at current or trending Empaveli kidney run-rates; Biogen commentary indicating C3G/IC-MPGN uptake ahead of plan.

**Downgrade triggers:** Biogen guidance or disclosures indicating the CVR milestones are unlikely to be met; deterioration in Empaveli kidney launch metrics; any dispute over CVR mechanics.

**Reassessment timeline:** Biogen's periodic disclosures on the acquired rare-disease franchise, particularly any commentary on C3G/IC-MPGN sales relative to the CVR thresholds.

## Conclusion

Apellis is a completed acquisition, not an active equity. Biogen has bought the company, the deal has closed, and integration (including a pipeline cull) is underway. The business genuinely inflected, and that inflection is what commanded the reported premium and what makes the assets attractive to Biogen.

For any residual holder, the cash is locked and the only remaining variable is the $2 CVR, whose payment depends on milestone triggers we could not independently verify. This is an uncertain HOLD: there is no fundamental appreciation case left, no downside below the $41 cash floor, and a modest, binary contingent upside. Anyone still holding should verify the precise CVR milestone terms against the definitive merger agreement (the exhibit to the March 31, 2026 8-K) before making any final decision.
