## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is an ethanol production company that owns majority stakes in two ethanol plants (One Earth Energy and NuGen Energy) and a 10.3% interest in Big River Resources. The stock has appreciated ~120% over the past 12 months, accelerating sharply after Q4 FY2025 earnings on March 26, 2026, when the company reported $2.50 in diluted EPS, a record. The headline number, however, obscures a critical dependency: $28.1M in Section 45Z Clean Fuel Production Tax Credits converted what would have been a $6.5M tax expense into a $6.5M tax benefit. Stripping out the 45Z credits, normalized net income was approximately $55M, roughly flat with FY2024's $58.2M.

The balance sheet is exceptionally strong: $375.8M in cash and short-term investments against zero bank debt, representing ~27% of the current $1.4B market cap. The One Earth capacity expansion from 150M to 200M gallons per year is nearing completion and should provide meaningful volume growth in FY2026. But the company operates a commodity spread business with limited hedging ability (~4 months forward), faces material regulatory and tax credit uncertainty, and trades at 17.4x trailing earnings that embed a non-recurring tax benefit. With no analyst coverage to provide external price discovery, the stock's recent surge reflects thin-float momentum rather than institutional re-rating.

## Company Overview

REX operates through two consolidated ethanol production entities, One Earth Energy (76.1% owned, Gibson City, IL) and NuGen Energy (99.7% owned, Marion, SD), plus a 10.3% equity-method investment in Big River Resources (four plants across Iowa, Illinois, and Wisconsin). Total effective ownership represents approximately 294 million gallons of annual ethanol shipping capacity across six facilities.

Revenue is concentrated in ethanol (77.5% of FY2025 revenue), with dried distillers grains (13.6%), distillers corn oil (8.1%), and modified distillers grains (0.8%) as co-products. The business model is fundamentally a corn-to-ethanol crush spread: profitability depends on the differential between ethanol/by-product selling prices and corn input costs, which represent ~74% of cost of sales.

The company is headquartered in Dayton, Ohio, and completed a 2-for-1 stock split on September 15, 2025.

**Revenue and Profitability:**

Revenue declined 23% from FY2023 to FY2024 as ethanol prices normalized post-pandemic, then grew 1% in FY2025. Gross margins improved over this period as corn prices fell, expanding from 11.8% to 14.4%. The 43% jump in net income from FY2024 to FY2025 was almost entirely attributable to the $28.1M 45Z tax credit, which swung the effective tax rate from a normal ~24% to negative 7.3%.

**Normalized earnings:** Removing the 45Z credit impact, FY2025 net income was approximately $55M, or ~$1.65/share, flat with FY2024. The "record EPS" narrative requires ongoing 45Z credit recognition at similar or higher levels.

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $117.8M in FY2025 (up from $64.2M in FY2024)
- Free cash flow: $49.4M after $68.4M in capex (primarily the One Earth expansion)
- FCF margin: 7.6%

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash and short-term investments: $375.8M
- Total debt: $3.2M (minor lease obligations)
- Current ratio: 5.9x
- Working capital: $372.5M

Zero financial leverage provides substantial downside protection and optionality. However, $336.5M of the cash sits at the subsidiary level (One Earth and NuGen), not at the parent, which limits deployment flexibility somewhat.

**Capital Allocation:** REX repurchased 1.65M shares for $32.9M in FY2025 at an average price of approximately $20/share (pre-split adjusted). An additional 2.36M shares remain authorized for repurchase (~$100M at current prices). The buyback at ~$20 was well-timed given the subsequent doubling, but the company's stated strategy of "investigating various uses of excess cash" lacks specificity.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:** Over the last four quarters, REX beat consensus three times and was in-line once. The most recent Q4 beat was massive (+123% vs. estimate), though the consensus figure appeared anomalously low, likely not incorporating the 45Z credit. Either analyst coverage is sparse enough that estimates are unreliable, or the commodity business creates genuine forecasting difficulty — likely both.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts:**
1. **One Earth capacity expansion** from 150M to 200M gallons/year (33% increase) is nearing completion. Testing and commissioning expected in FY2026. This provides real volume growth, though the value of incremental gallons depends entirely on crush spreads at the time of production.

2. **45Z tax credit runway** extended through 2029 by OBBBA legislation (July 2025). The legislation also removed the indirect land-use change penalty for crop feedstocks, which is favorable for corn ethanol's carbon intensity scoring.

3. **Export demand:** U.S. ethanol exports hit a record 2.2 billion gallons in 2025, supported by ample corn supply (16.75B bushels forecasted, up 12%).

**Structural growth constraints:**
- U.S. ethanol production capacity is ~18.5B gallons versus the 15.0B gallon RFS mandate — 19% structural overcapacity
- The E10 "blend wall" limits domestic demand growth without meaningful E15/E85 adoption expansion
- Year-round E15 depends on annual emergency waivers, not permanent regulatory approval
- EV adoption poses a multi-decade structural headwind to gasoline (and ethanol) demand

**Carbon capture and sequestration (CCS):** REX has invested $58.9M in CCS at One Earth, with a total budget of $220-230M. CCS would lower carbon intensity scores and maximize 45Z/45Q credits. However, the EPA Class VI permit has been pushed to Q3 2026. Illinois imposed a moratorium on CO2 pipeline construction certificates until July 1, 2026 or federal PHMSA standards are finalized. The ICC dismissed REX's pipeline application. NuGen's CCS route depends on Summit Carbon Solutions, whose South Dakota/North Dakota pipeline faces eminent domain bans and voided storage permits.

CCS is the company's most important long-term competitive differentiator and its most significant execution risk. Failure would leave One Earth with higher CI scores than competitors who achieve sequestration, reducing credit eligibility over time.

## Valuation Assessment

REX trades at 17.4x trailing EPS of $2.50 and 17.4x forward P/E. Against the peer median P/E of 15.0x (DINO being the most relevant clean comparable), REX commands a modest premium.

Adjusting for the non-recurring nature of the 45Z credit benefit: on normalized FY2025 earnings of ~$1.65/share, the stock trades at ~25.6x, a substantial premium for a commodity processing business with no dividend yield.

**Cash-adjusted valuation:** Enterprise value is approximately $1.0B ($1.39B market cap less $376M cash). Against normalized net income of ~$55M, the EV/earnings ratio is ~18x. This is reasonable if 45Z credits persist at current levels, but provides minimal margin of safety if credits are reduced or eliminated.

**Bull case valuation:** If One Earth expansion adds 50M gallons/year at $0.20/gallon crush margin, that's $10M incremental pre-tax income. Combined with sustained 45Z credits at $25-30M/year, total net income could reach $90-100M, supporting $2.80-3.00/share EPS and a $45-55 stock price at 16-18x.

**Bear case valuation:** If crush spreads compress to FY2024 levels and 45Z credits are delayed/reduced, normalized EPS could fall to $1.30-1.50/share. At 12-14x, the stock would trade at $16-21, though the cash floor (~$11.40/share) limits absolute downside.

REX pays no dividend (0% yield vs. 3.4% peer median), which reduces its appeal in a higher-for-longer rate environment where risk-free yields exceed 3.5%.

## Competitive Landscape

REX operates in a structurally challenged industry. The U.S. ethanol market has excess capacity, regulated demand ceilings, and increasing competition from integrated grain processors (ADM, Bunge) and oil companies that have entered ethanol production.

REX's competitive advantages are its balance sheet and operational discipline (22 consecutive profitable quarters). These provide resilience but not pricing power.

The emerging competitive axis is carbon intensity scoring. Plants that achieve CCS will earn higher 45Z credits and potentially access premium pricing in low-carbon fuel standard markets (California, Oregon). If REX's CCS project succeeds, it would be well-positioned. If CCS fails while competitors succeed, REX would face a meaningful competitive disadvantage in the credits that now drive the majority of its earnings outperformance.

The company's concentration in ethanol, explicitly acknowledged in filings as a risk ("our business is not diversified"), means it has no offset when the crush spread compresses.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Spread Volatility (Critical).** A 10% adverse move in ethanol prices alone would reduce pre-tax income by approximately $47M — more than half of annual pre-tax income. The ~4-month forward hedging window is insufficient to protect against sustained spread compression. This is the dominant risk and is inherent to the business model.

**2. 45Z Tax Credit Dependency (High).** Treasury regulations remain proposed, not finalized. Energy Attribute Certificate costs could increase. Prevailing wage/apprenticeship compliance requirements add operational burden. Any regulatory tightening of CI score requirements would directly reduce credit eligibility.

**3. CCS Permitting and Execution (High).** $220-230M total commitment with $166M spent. EPA permits delayed to Q3 2026. Illinois pipeline moratorium effective until July 1, 2026. Summit Carbon Solutions route for NuGen severely compromised by eminent domain bans and voided storage permits. Write-off risk if projects fail.

**4. IRS Audit Exposure (Material).** $82.7M in refined coal ($58.2M) and R&E ($24.5M) tax credits under IRS challenge, with the IRS signaling denial. This represents roughly one full year of net income. Unrecognized tax benefits total ~$19M.

**5. Trade/Tariff Risk (Moderate).** 36% of U.S. ethanol exports go to Canada; 20% of distillers grains exports go to Mexico. Retaliatory tariffs in the current trade environment could materially impact demand and pricing.

**6. EPA Small Refinery Exemptions (Moderate).** In August 2025, EPA granted 63 full and 77 partial exemptions, effectively reducing mandated ethanol blending. This directly undermines the demand floor for REX's product.

**7. EV Structural Headwind (Long-term).** Multiple states and countries have ICE vehicle bans targeting 2035. Ethanol demand is structurally linked to gasoline consumption.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:**
- One Earth expansion adds 33% capacity, operational in FY2026
- 45Z credits sustained at $25-30M/year through 2029, with potential upside if CCS achieved
- Record corn supply keeps input costs low, supporting crush spreads
- $376M cash provides acquisition optionality or sustained buybacks
- EPS reaches $2.80-3.00 in FY2026-2027; stock re-rates to $50-55

**Bear Case:**
- Crush spreads compress as ethanol capacity exceeds demand mandates
- 45Z credit regulations tightened or delayed, reducing benefit to $10-15M/year
- CCS project permitting fails, leading to write-offs and competitive disadvantage
- IRS prevails on $82.7M in tax credits, creating one-time charge
- Tariff retaliation from Canada/Mexico reduces export demand
- Normalized earnings revert to $1.30-1.65/share; stock corrects to $20-25

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**

2. EPA finalizes Class VI permit for One Earth CCS, de-risking the largest capital project
3. Treasury finalizes 45Z regulations confirming current CI score methodology and EAC eligibility

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q1 FY2026 earnings show crush spread compression returning EPS to sub-$0.50/quarter run rate
2. CCS project faces additional permitting denials or cost overruns exceeding $250M total budget
3. IRS audit resolution adverse, with >$50M in tax credit disallowances

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely June 2026 for Q1 FY2026 ended April 30). EPA CCS draft permit expected May 2026. Illinois pipeline moratorium review July 1, 2026.

## Conclusion

REX has genuine strengths: an impregnable balance sheet, a capacity expansion nearing completion, and a multi-year 45Z credit runway. But the stock has more than doubled in 12 months, with the most recent 15% single-day surge on March 26 driven by earnings whose quality is questionable (half the "record" EPS came from a tax credit). At $42-44, the market is pricing the bull case — sustained 45Z credits, successful CCS execution, favorable crush spreads — while the bear case scenarios are numerous and individually material.

The commodity spread nature of the business means quarterly earnings can swing violently and unpredictably. With no analyst coverage providing external discipline on expectations, no dividend yield in a high-rate world, and significant execution risk on the CCS project that underpins the long-term competitive thesis, the risk-reward is unfavorable at current levels. The stock is a HOLD. Multiple binary outcomes (45Z regulations, CCS permitting, IRS audit, tariff policy) will resolve over the next 6-12 months, and insufficient evidence exists for a stronger directional call until they do.
