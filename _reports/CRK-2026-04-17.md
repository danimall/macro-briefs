## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is the largest pure-play Haynesville natural gas producer, with 7.0 Tcfe in proved reserves and ~1.2 Bcf/day production concentrated in North Louisiana and East Texas. The company is majority-controlled (~71%) by Jerry Jones, who has invested over $1 billion to fund an aggressive pivot from legacy Haynesville to the deeper, less-proven Western Haynesville shale. Revenue recovered from $1.25B (FY2024) to $2.22B (FY2025), driven almost entirely by a 60% increase in realized gas prices ($3.17/Mcf vs. $1.98). Net income of $420M included a $292M asset sale gain, meaning recurring earnings were closer to $130M.

The core problem is that Comstock is spending far more than it generates. FY2025 capex of $1.35B exceeded operating cash flow of $900M by 50%, producing negative free cash flow of approximately $450M. FY2026 guidance calls for $1.4–1.5B in development/exploration capex plus $100–150M in midstream, plus a $440M cash payment to buy out Quantum Capital's midstream interest in 1H 2026. Total 2026 outflows could approach $2B against uncertain cash generation, with $3.07B in existing debt and only $24M in cash. The Altman Z-Score sits at 1.48 (distress zone), the current ratio is 0.49, and interest expense consumes 25% of operating income.

The long-term thesis — Gulf Coast LNG demand, data center power needs via the NextEra partnership, and decades of drilling inventory — has merit but requires years of execution, sustained high gas prices, and continued access to external capital. With 20.8% short interest, an options market in backwardation pricing near-term stress, and analyst consensus skewed bearish (10 Hold, 3 Sell, 1 Strong Sell vs. 1 Buy), the risk-reward is unfavorable at current levels.

## Company Overview

Comstock Resources operates as an independent natural gas exploration and production company focused on the Haynesville and Bossier shale formations. The company holds interests in 1,724 producing wells (959.7 net), operating 1,074 of them across its core acreage in North Louisiana and East Texas. CRK operates 99% of its proved reserve base, giving it strong operational control.

**Key operational characteristics:**
- **Near-100% natural gas exposure** — no meaningful oil or NGL diversification
- **7.0 Tcfe proved reserves** with PV-10 of $4.5B (SEC prices); only 41% developed, leaving a massive 4.2 Tcf proved undeveloped reserve base
- **Massive drilling inventory:** 1,848 locations in legacy Haynesville (886 net) + 3,343 locations in Western Haynesville (2,561 net)
- **Company-owned midstream** through Pinnacle Gas Services LLC
- **Geographic advantage:** Proximity to Gulf Coast LNG terminals, petrochemical facilities, and Texas power generation markets provides a $0.20–0.50/Mcf premium over Appalachian gas
- **Key customers:** Enterprise Products (18%), Venture Global LNG (11%), Shell Energy (10%)

The company is executing a strategic pivot from legacy Haynesville to the deeper Western Haynesville, where well depths reach 19,200 feet and completion conditions are more extreme. Only 30 Western Haynesville wells had been turned to sales through end of 2025.

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue volatility is extreme. The swing from $3.63B to $1.25B to $2.22B over three years is driven almost entirely by natural gas spot prices. FY2025 net income of $420M included a $292M gain from the Shelby Trough and Cotton Valley divestitures, meaning core operating earnings were approximately $128M — roughly $0.44/diluted share after adjusting for the one-time gain.

Operating cost efficiency is genuinely strong: $0.27/Mcfe lease operating, $0.37/Mcfe gathering and transportation, and total lifting costs of ~$0.73/Mcfe rank among the lowest in the Haynesville. Operating margin was 29.1% and net margin 18.9%, though both figures are inflated by the asset sale gain.

The gas services segment generated $500M in revenue against $516M in expenses — a net loss of $16M. This segment functions as a cost center that trades third-party gas to utilize excess transport capacity.

### Cash Flow and Capital Spending

This is where the thesis breaks down quantitatively:

- **FY2025 Operating Cash Flow:** $900M
- **FY2025 Capex:** $1.35B
- **FY2025 Free Cash Flow:** approximately **($450M)**

The funding gap will be bridged by revolver draws, pushing utilization from $260M toward $700M+ by mid-2026.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Production Outlook

Production fell 15% in FY2025 (450 Bcf vs. 528 Bcf in FY2024) despite a 24% increase in capex. Management attributes this to the deliberate shift from legacy to Western Haynesville drilling, where new wells take longer to develop and initial production rates are still being characterized. Declining production while capex rises demands careful monitoring — it could reflect the early phase of a value-creating investment or capital destruction in an unproven formation.

### Western Haynesville Thesis

The Western Haynesville represents CRK's primary growth vector. Key parameters:
- **3,343 drilling locations** (2,561 net) — decades of inventory
- **Only 30 wells turned to sales** through end of 2025 — still a delineation-stage play
- **Extreme well depths** (up to 19,200 feet) create higher drilling and completion costs
- **Estimated future PUD development costs:** $4.7B (up from $0.9B a year ago due to reserve additions at higher price decks)

The PUD reserve base is highly price-sensitive. Proved undeveloped reserves swung from 4.2 Tcf (2022) to 1.0 Tcf (2024) to 4.2 Tcf (2025), driven almost entirely by gas price changes affecting economic thresholds. A return to sub-$2.50 gas would likely de-book a significant portion of these reserves.

### NextEra Power Generation Hub

The March 2026 announcement of a $16B, 5.2 GW natural gas-fired power generation facility in Anderson County, Texas, with Comstock as the dedicated gas supplier (~1 Bcf/day by 2031), is the most significant potential growth catalyst. This would essentially double CRK's current dedicated demand and provide long-term contracted volume.

However, the project remains subject to "negotiation and execution of definitive documents" and NextEra's completion of construction and commissioning. A $16B, multi-GW power facility requires extensive permitting (air quality, water, grid interconnection), construction timelines measured in years, and sustained demand from data center operators to justify the investment. The probability of this project delivering 1 Bcf/day by 2031 as described should be substantially discounted.

### LNG Demand Structural Tailwind

Growing Gulf Coast LNG export capacity (Venture Global, Sabine Pass expansion, Golden Pass) creates structural incremental demand for Haynesville gas. CRK's geographic proximity provides a real cost advantage. Haynesville rig count has more than doubled year-over-year to 52 rigs, reflecting industry confidence in this demand thesis. The risk is that supply growth matches or exceeds demand growth, keeping gas prices range-bound.

## Competitive Landscape

Comstock is the largest independent pure-play Haynesville operator, which gives it scale advantages in the basin including preferred contractor relationships, midstream ownership, and geological knowledge. Proximity to Gulf Coast demand centers is a durable competitive advantage over Appalachian producers.

Competitive threats include:
- **Expand Energy** (the combined Chesapeake/Southwestern entity, the largest US gas producer) expanding Haynesville operations
- **Aethon Energy and BPX Energy** as significant private operators in the basin
- **Supply growth outpacing demand growth** as Haynesville rig count doubles — this could cap gas price upside
- **Western Haynesville specifically is less proven** than legacy Haynesville, and CRK is essentially the pioneer operator, bearing the geological and engineering risk of proving the play

CRK's owned midstream infrastructure (Pinnacle) provides cost advantages and operational flexibility, but the $440M Quantum buyout represents the price of consolidating this asset.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Natural Gas Price Collapse:** A return to $2.00–2.50/Mcf gas (which occurred as recently as 2024) would render the current capex program unsustainable, force production curtailments, potentially trigger PUD reserve de-bookings, and put covenant compliance at risk. There is no hedging program disclosed, and the company's FY2024 operating loss demonstrates what happens at low gas prices.

2. **Persistent Negative Free Cash Flow:** The growth strategy requires $1.4–1.5B annually in capex for the foreseeable future. With OCF in the $0.9–1.1B range at current gas prices, the company will continue accumulating debt or requiring equity infusions, creating a funding treadmill where production growth must exceed cost of capital to create shareholder value.

3. **Balance Sheet Fragility:** The leverage and liquidity metrics leave minimal margin for error. The Quantum buyout in 1H 2026 will further strain liquidity. If gas prices soften while capex remains elevated, the company could face liquidity pressure by late 2026 or 2027.

4. **Western Haynesville Execution Risk:** Only 30 wells have been turned to sales. Extreme depths, high pressures, and temperatures create operational risks absent in legacy Haynesville. If well productivity disappoints or costs escalate, the $4.7B in future PUD development costs could prove uneconomic.

5. **Controlling Shareholder / Governance:** Jerry Jones's 71% ownership concentrates decision-making. The board has only 3 independent directors — one of the smallest for any ~$5B public company. CEO Allison has served since 1988 (37 years). Minority shareholders have essentially no governance leverage.

6. **NextEra Project Uncertainty:** The power hub involves a multi-year, highly complex infrastructure buildout subject to permitting, construction, and counterparty execution risks. Investors pricing this into CRK's current valuation are accepting substantial execution risk.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (66.6% near-term vs. 63.2% at 6-month), indicating the market expects a near-term volatility event. Combined with 20.8% short interest (8.9 days to cover), the options and short data indicate informed participants see meaningful downside risk, with sufficient crowding to create periodic short-squeeze rallies that are then sold.

- Western Haynesville well performance disappoints, with higher costs and/or lower productivity than legacy Haynesville
- NextEra project stalls or is cancelled due to permitting, financing, or demand shortfalls
- Continued negative FCF forces additional debt accumulation, pushing leverage to unsustainable levels
- The Quantum buyout + elevated capex strain liquidity, requiring a dilutive equity raise at depressed prices
- PUD reserves are de-booked as in 2023–2024, reducing the reserve base and NAV

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Gas price trajectory through summer 2026. If Henry Hub retreats below $3.00/Mcf, CRK's FCF deficit widens, the Quantum buyout becomes increasingly dilutive, and the company may need to cut capex or raise equity. Next quarter estimates have been revised down ~30% over the last 30 days. The next catalyst window is Q2 2026 earnings (likely August) and winter 2026 gas strip pricing by October.

**Timing window:** 3–6 months. Gas prices are seasonal — summer typically brings lower demand and prices, which could pressure CRK heading into Q2/Q3 earnings. The Quantum buyout completion in 1H 2026 will be the next liquidity event to watch.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 20.8% of float with 8.9 days to cover, this is a crowded short. Periodic squeeze rallies are likely (the stock moved from $14.65 to $31.17 between August 2025 and June 2025 before collapsing back to $15 and rallying again). Short sellers face significant carrying cost and squeeze risk, particularly around any gas price spikes or NextEra project updates.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV (66.6%) is rich to historical (57.3%), increasing the cost of put-based expressions. The backwardated term structure and 3-month put/call volume ratio of 2.43 confirm institutional bearish positioning. Borrow costs are likely elevated given the short interest level.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $10–12 implies 30–40% downside from $17. Upside risk on a squeeze or gas price spike to $25–28 implies 47–65% upside. Risk/reward is approximately 0.6:1 to 0.9:1 — unfavorable for a short given the squeeze risk, commodity volatility, and Jerry Jones's willingness to inject capital.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is valid — persistent negative FCF, high leverage, single-commodity exposure, and unproven growth play — but the short interest level creates unacceptable squeeze risk. The stock's history shows violent rallies on gas price spikes or headline catalysts (it nearly doubled from August to November 2025). Jerry Jones's 71% ownership means a motivated, deep-pocketed insider backstops the equity. Avoid ownership rather than initiate a short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Valuation compression to 8–9x forward earnings (approximately $8–10 per share), providing adequate margin of safety for commodity and execution risk
2. Demonstrated positive free cash flow for two consecutive quarters, confirming the capex program is generating returns in excess of investment
3. Western Haynesville well data showing productivity and cost metrics competitive with or superior to legacy Haynesville (published in quarterly 10-Q supplements)

**Timeline:** The negative FCF condition is likely to persist through at least FY2027 given guided capex levels. Western Haynesville delineation results should become clearer over the next 4–6 quarters as the well count grows from 30 toward 60+. The NextEra project will provide clarity on its progression (or lack thereof) within 12–18 months.

## Conclusion

Comstock Resources presents a speculative commodity bet wrapped in a growth story that requires sustained high gas prices, successful execution of an unproven formation, and continued access to external capital to fund a multi-billion-dollar development program. The fundamentals today show deeply negative free cash flow, distress-zone leverage metrics, declining production despite rising capex, and extreme revenue volatility from single-commodity exposure. The long-term catalysts (LNG demand, data center power, NextEra partnership) are real but multi-year in nature and subject to substantial execution risk.

At ~12.7x forward P/E with zero dividend yield and negative FCF, CRK trades at a peer-median multiple without delivering peer-median capital returns. Analyst consensus (10 Hold, 3 Sell, 1 Strong Sell, 1 Buy) confirms the bearish lean. The risk-reward is asymmetrically negative: the company needs sustained favorable gas prices and successful Western Haynesville execution to justify the current valuation, while a return to 2024-level gas prices could create genuine financial distress. AVOID.
