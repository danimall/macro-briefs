**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The operative fact about Air Lease Corporation overrides any conventional equity analysis: the company was acquired in a take-private transaction that **closed on April 8, 2026**. A consortium led by Sumitomo Corporation, SMBC Aviation Capital, Apollo-managed funds, and Brookfield cashed out Class A common stockholders and folded the business into a Dublin-based holding company now operating as Sumisho Air Lease Corporation DAC. The publicly-traded AL common stock has been extinguished and converted to cash consideration. Any report framed as an ongoing public-equity investment case is moot.

The underlying business was healthy at the time of the deal. Air Lease was one of the largest aircraft leasing companies globally, running an asset-intensive operating-lease model: order new narrowbody and widebody aircraft from Airbus and Boeing at scale, lease them to airlines worldwide under long-term contracts, and manage fleet age and residual value through disciplined aircraft sales. As of December 31, 2025 the fleet comprised 490 owned and 45 managed aircraft plus a forward orderbook of 218+ aircraft. Revenue grew steadily from roughly $2.0B in 2020 to $2.7B in 2024, and Q4 2025 EPS of $2.20 beat consensus of $1.46 by a wide margin. The company carried an investment-grade balance sheet, and the successor entity received an A- issuer/senior unsecured rating from KBRA in March 2026.

The recommendation is **AVOID**, but the rationale is unusual: this is a statement that the security no longer exists as an investable instrument rather than a judgment on business quality or valuation. The take-private closed at approximately $7.4B equity value (roughly $28.2B enterprise value including assumed and refinanced debt net of cash). Stockholders approved the deal December 18, 2025; final regulatory approval came March 30–31, 2026; and the merger completed April 8, 2026, with the orderbook transferred to SMBC Aviation Capital. There is nothing left to buy, hold, or short in the common stock.

## Company Overview

Air Lease Corporation was founded by Steven Udvar-Házy, the aircraft-leasing pioneer who previously built ILFC, and was historically headquartered in Los Angeles. Its model matched long-lived depreciating assets (modern, fuel-efficient commercial jets) against long-term lease contracts with global airline customers, funded predominantly with low-cost unsecured debt enabled by an investment-grade rating.

The strategic core of the franchise was its forward orderbook. By committing to delivery slots and pricing years in advance, Air Lease locked in capacity that became increasingly valuable as Boeing and Airbus production delays constrained the supply of new aircraft. Tight OEM supply supported both lease rates and residual values, and the orderbook represented delivery positions at pre-inflation pricing. That orderbook was a central rationale for the acquisition; it was carved out and transferred to SMBC Aviation Capital, bringing SMBC's combined Airbus/Boeing commitments to approximately 420 aircraft.

Historical competitive advantages included scale and OEM relationships providing priority delivery positions, a young and in-demand fleet, broad geographic and airline diversification, and the funding-cost advantage of an investment-grade balance sheet. These attributes made the company an attractive consolidation target rather than a distressed seller.

## Financial Analysis

The structured data feed for this name is unreliable. The XBRL entity already reads "SUMISHO AIR LEASE CORP" (the post-merger successor), and the revenue line pulled a sub-component of roughly $245–331M rather than total revenue near $2.7B. Automated distress screens returned "insufficient data" and do not map cleanly onto a leasing balance sheet, where inventory is near zero, the asset base is leased aircraft, and high leverage is deliberate and normal. The accurate financial picture was recovered from the proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosures:

| Metric | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total Revenue | $2.02B | $2.09B | $2.32B | $2.68B | $2.73B |
| Net Income | $516M | $437M | ($97M) | $615M | $428M |

Revenue compounded at roughly 7–8% annually from 2020 to 2024, driven by fleet expansion. The ($97M) net loss in 2022 reflected write-downs on aircraft stranded in Russia after the Ukraine invasion, a sector-wide event for lessors and a one-time hit; profitability rebounded to $615M in 2023. Net income then declined to $428M in 2024 despite higher revenue, indicating margin pressure from higher interest expense in a rising-rate environment and depreciation on a growing fleet.

The balance sheet carried total assets of approximately $32.9B, long-term debt of roughly $19.7–20.2B, and stockholders' equity near $8.5B. Flight equipment net was roughly $34–36B. Interest expense (~$780–840M) is the dominant cost line, so funding cost and credit rating are the central drivers of the model. Operating cash flow ran near $1.7B, with the economically meaningful "capex" being aircraft purchases financed via debt. This was a financially healthy, investment-grade lessor at the point of acquisition.

The earnings-surprise record over the four quarters preceding the close showed three beats and one miss. The Phase 1 vendor data (which differs from the Zacks/Nasdaq figures cited above) records a Q4 2025 EPS beat of 23.9% ($1.51 actual vs. $1.22 estimate) and an anomalous Q2 2025 print (a +317% surprise, reflecting deal-related and gains-on-sale distortions rather than recurring operating strength). None of this is investable prospectively, since the equity has been retired.

## Growth Analysis

The historical growth thesis rested on fleet expansion into a supply-constrained OEM environment. Steady revenue growth and a large orderbook of delivery slots at favorable pricing gave the business a visible multi-year runway, and the tight supply of new aircraft supported lease rates and residual values. That growth optionality is what the acquiring consortium paid for; the orderbook was extracted and moved to SMBC Aviation Capital rather than left to a standalone public company.

For a public-equity investor, forward growth is not accessible. The common stock no longer participates in any of it. The growth score reflects the absence of a claim on the future cash flows of the enterprise rather than any deterioration in the underlying business.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is a settled question. The cash consideration has been paid, and the stock rose on the September 1, 2025 deal announcement. There is no live multiple to compare against peers such as AerCap or BOC Aviation, because the shares have been extinguished at a fixed cash price.

Historically the company traded at a discount to book on the depressed post-Russia comparisons and then re-rated as earnings recovered; the deal delivered a premium to a stock that had modestly underperformed its peer group through 2024. The cheapness the market saw in AL before the announcement was resolved through the acquisition: the catalyst was a buyout, and the equity mispricing was closed by cash consideration rather than by market re-rating. There is no asymmetric upside remaining in a security that has already been cashed out.

## Competitive Landscape

Air Lease operated in a consolidating, capital-intensive global aircraft leasing industry alongside AerCap, SMBC Aviation Capital, Avolon, and BOC Aviation. The industry backdrop favored lessors: Boeing and Airbus production constraints kept aircraft supply tight, supporting lease rates and residual values. The merger reflected strategic consolidation, combining Air Lease's modern fleet and orderbook with SMBC's servicing scale, backed by deep-pocketed sponsors (Sumitomo, Apollo, Brookfield) that signal intent to lower funding costs and scale further.

The competitive position of the underlying assets was sound. The franchise now sits inside a larger private platform, and the competitive dynamics accrue to the successor entity's creditors and owners, not to former public shareholders.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant "risk" from the perspective of a public-equity report is that the security is gone. There is no residual common stock to own, so ordinary business risks (interest-rate and funding spreads, residual-value swings, OEM delivery timing, airline credit concentration, geopolitical expropriation of the kind demonstrated by the 2022 Russia write-offs) are relevant only to the private successor's debt holders.

The material risk for anyone still nominally referencing the AL ticker is data integrity. The structured feeds continue to surface stale and mislabeled information: the entity name already reads as the successor, and the revenue field is understated by roughly an order of magnitude. Automated screens will produce misleading readings on this name. Any position or valuation built on that data would be built on a security that no longer trades.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** There is no bull case for the common stock. The historical bull case, a growing investment-grade lessor with a valuable orderbook capturing tight-supply lease economics, was realized and monetized through the September 2025 take-private at a premium. Holders who owned the stock into the deal received cash.

**Bear Case:** There is no bear case for the common stock either, because there is no residual equity exposure to decline. For the private successor's creditors, the relevant risks are funding-spread compression, residual-value impairment, and airline defaults, but these do not touch former shareholders.

The only actionable conclusion is that the security is no longer investable. This is a completed deal, not an open situation.

## Conclusion

Air Lease Corporation was a well-run, investment-grade aircraft lessor with a valuable forward orderbook and a one-off 2022 Russia-driven loss well behind it. That business quality is why the consortium paid roughly $7.4B in equity value to take it private, cashing out Class A shares and transferring the orderbook to SMBC Aviation Capital.

The recommendation is **AVOID** because the common stock is no longer an investable security. This is a factual statement that the equity has been retired at a fixed cash price rather than a comment on the merits of the underlying franchise, which was sound. No forward-looking public-equity investment case exists. The scores below reflect the reality of a defunct security carried on stale data feeds rather than a live equity opportunity; the trajectory of improving scores across prior reports (3.5 → 6.3 → 6.0) captured the deal-premium re-rating, but that process concluded with the cash-out and cannot continue.
