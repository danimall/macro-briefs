**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nebius Group is an AI-native "neocloud" — a specialist provider of NVIDIA GPU capacity and full-stack AI cloud services (training, fine-tuning, inference) domiciled in Amsterdam. It is the successor entity to Yandex N.V., which divested its Russian operations in 2024 and rebranded around a Western AI-infrastructure business. Revenue fell to $117.5M in 2024 post-divestiture, then rebounded to $529.8M in 2025 (+351%), with Q1 2026 group revenue of $399M (+684% YoY) and annualized recurring revenue reaching $1.9B. The demand backdrop is favorable: North American data-center vacancy near 0.9%, capacity contracted before it is built, and marquee backlog from Microsoft ($17.4B through 2031) and Meta ($27B five-year). This company's product sells out.

The investability question is not whether AI demand is real. It is whether Nebius can finance the buildout without destroying per-share value. Management guided 2026 capex to $20-25B against ~$3B of revenue — roughly 7x. That gap requires continuous external capital: debt secured against long-dated contracts, customer prepayments (~$4.8B cited), NVIDIA's ~$2B equity stake, and asset-backed structures (a first $775M ABS deal closed ~July 2026). CEO Arkady Volozh has stated plainly that financing at 10-12% "kills all of the economy" of the buildout. Nebius is functionally a leveraged AI-infrastructure trade with the Fed on the other side, and that duration bet is the central risk. [unverified — the December-hike probability and "higher-for-longer confirmed" macro framing are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

We maintain a HOLD. The prior trajectory (5.5 in May, 6.0 in June) reflected improving conviction on the demand and contract side, and we do not reverse that. But three developments since the June read raise the bar for a continued upgrade path: the capex guide (which triggered an 11.4% single-day drop), broad insider selling into the peak with zero buying, and near-term options stress (171% ATM IV, term-structure backwardation, 28% short interest). The stock has fallen ~36% from its ~$300 high while the demand thesis strengthened, improving risk/reward on the demand story, but the financing overhang and quality-of-earnings caveats keep this an uncertain hold rather than a conviction buy.

## Company Overview

Nebius operates four businesses, dominated by Nebius AI — the GPU cloud, roughly 98% of group revenue in Q1 2026. Ancillary units include Toloka (AI data labeling), Avride (autonomous driving), and TripleTen (edtech); the group also holds a stake in ClickHouse. The core offering is dedicated, AI-native GPU clusters rather than general-purpose compute, positioning Nebius as a complement to (and sometimes partner of) hyperscalers rather than a pure competitor.

NVIDIA holds an equity stake (~$2B reported), which secures priority GPU allocation in a supply-rationed market, a structural advantage against rival neoclouds competing for the same silicon. Accel is also a backer. The company is founder-led by Arkady Volozh, who built Yandex, and carries a dual-class share structure typical of founder-controlled entities.

The Yandex heritage matters for financial interpretation. The 2021-2023 statements reflect the old Russian search/ads/ride-hailing business (~$8.9B revenue in 2023); 2024 onward reflects the new, far smaller but faster-growing AI infrastructure company. Any historical time series has a structural break in 2024, and automated pipelines that compare across it will misread the business. Nebius files as a foreign private issuer (Form 20-F/6-K), so standardized 10-K risk-factor and MD&A parsing is not available; the analysis below draws on SEC XBRL structured financials, Form 4 insider filings, and sourced media citing filings and earnings calls.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and ARR.** The growth is among the steepest in the AI-infrastructure cohort. Nebius AI ARR reached $1.9B exiting Q1 2026, up more than 50% QoQ from $1.25B. FY2026 guidance calls for $7-9B ARR, group revenue of $3.0-3.4B, and roughly 40% adjusted EBITDA margin. The Nebius AI segment's adjusted EBITDA margin reportedly expanded to 45% in Q1 2026 from 24% the prior quarter, evidence of operating leverage as clusters fill.

**Profitability and quality of earnings.** Naive metrics mislead here. 2025 reported net income was +$82.5M, but operating income was –$611.7M. The positive bottom line reflects large non-operating, non-cash fair-value gains, likely on the NVIDIA and ClickHouse stakes. One recent quarter's profit was flattered by an approximately $781M non-cash gain that masked a wider adjusted net loss. Trailing P/E of ~63x (and headline "profitability") is essentially non-informative here; underlying operations are loss-making, and the earnings-surprise record — four straight beats on EPS, but on negative estimates (Q1 2026 –$0.32 vs –$0.78 est) — is a story of losing less than feared, not of earning.

**The defining feature — capex vs. revenue.** 2025 capex was $4.07B against revenue (nearly 8x). The 2026 capex guide against revenue (~7x) is the crux of the entire case. Group free cash flow is deeply negative and will remain so through the buildout. The mechanism: Nebius signs long-dated take-or-pay contracts, finances construction with debt secured against those contracts (plus prepayments and equity), and monetizes committed 2027+ capacity over time. This works if credit stays cheap and utilization ramps as contracted. It breaks if borrowing costs rise toward double digits or if a large counterparty insources.

**Balance sheet.** 2025 total assets $12.43B, total liabilities $7.84B (up from $294.9M in 2024), equity $4.59B. Long-term debt jumped to $4.10B from near zero; cash was $3.68B. Shares outstanding rose from ~235.8M (2024) to ~253M (2025), with further dilution likely given the funding gap. The $775M ABS deal expands capacity without shareholder dilution and validates lender diligence on the contracted cash flows, but it covers a small fraction of the annual need. Traditional health metrics (Altman Z, Piotroski) are unavailable and would score poorly on negative operating income and rising leverage, though that profile is characteristic of a hyper-growth infrastructure builder rather than distress. On balance we assign a middling financial-health score: the contracted backlog and NVIDIA/customer prepayment cushion partially offset negative FCF and a leveraged, rate-sensitive funding model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest dimension. Data-center demand is favorable across the AI-infrastructure layer, with vacancy reported near 0.9% and capacity contracted before it is built. Nebius sits directly in that flow with priority GPU allocation and a differentiated AI-native architecture. [unverified — the specific external corroborating figures (AEP/Ohio Power commercial KWh +41% YoY, Eaton data-center orders ~+240%) do not appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

The contracted backlog is the durability anchor: the Microsoft and Meta commitments total roughly $46-50B (Microsoft scalable to $19.4B). ARR guidance to $7-9B in 2026 implies revenue visibility well beyond the revenue-recognition guide, since ARR captures committed run-rate ahead of full ramp. The binding constraint is capital, not demand — an inversion of the typical growth-stock risk. Volozh's framing ("everything we build, we sell") is supported by the contract cadence and the sub-1% vacancy backdrop.

We weight this as a durable growth thesis, but temper it with two facts. First, the same mega-counterparties (Meta especially) have the scale and demonstrated willingness to insource compute — backlog concentration cuts both ways. Second, AI compute pricing could compress as hyperscalers and rivals (CoreWeave and others) add capacity, pressuring the ~40% EBITDA margin assumption embedded in guidance. The growth is real; its terminal economics depend on financing cost and pricing durability that are not yet proven.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are non-informative in isolation here. Trailing P/E of ~63x sits on non-cash-gain-flattered earnings. Price-to-sales is ~80x on trailing revenue and roughly 15x on the 2026 guide — a wide range depending on which line you anchor. The peer set (BIDU, BZ, Z) trades at a scattered median P/E of ~134.6x and is not a clean comp for a pre-profit, hyper-growth infrastructure builder.

The honest way to value NBIS is against the ARR ramp and the contracted backlog, discounted for financing risk. On guided 2026 ARR and a ~40% target EBITDA margin, the current ~$47B market cap implies a multiple that is defensible if — and only if — the buildout is financed at rates that preserve unit economics and if the ARR converts to sustained EBITDA at scale. That is a large conditional. A cycle-adjusted framework would ordinarily favor a stock with revenue +684% and expanding segment margins, but that is offset here by the estimate-revision picture: current-year EPS was revised +45.9% over 90 days (a mechanical narrowing of the loss), while next-quarter and next-year estimates deteriorated sharply (–212% and –17.7% respectively over 90 days), reflecting the market repricing the capex burden and dilution. Estimates are broadly falling on the forward lines, which is a warning, not a green light.

Analyst targets span $120 to $410 (mean ~$259, median $265), a dispersion driven by rates and financing rather than AI belief. At $214, the stock sits modestly below mean and median targets, but the width of the band, not its midpoint, is the signal. We do not treat proximity to consensus as a floor here: the estimate deterioration on forward lines and the dependence of the thesis on cheap credit break the usual "targets lag reality" logic. We assign a below-neutral valuation score. Cheapness on any single metric is not the issue; the issue is that fair value is a wide function of an exogenous rate path the company does not control, which caps the asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Nebius competes on two fronts. Against hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), it offers dedicated AI-native GPU clusters versus general compute and partners with Microsoft rather than purely competing — a hybrid position that both validates its enterprise credibility and exposes it to the partner eventually insourcing. Against neoclouds — CoreWeave most directly — it competes for NVIDIA allocation, enterprise contracts, and financing.

The competitive edge is real but narrow: NVIDIA's equity stake secures priority silicon allocation in a supply-constrained market, and the Microsoft/Meta contracts establish Nebius as an enterprise-grade counterparty (the Microsoft deal drove a ~50% single-session move on announcement). The moat is access to capital and chips, not proprietary technology. That is a defensible position while GPUs are scarce and credit is cheap, and a fragile one if either condition reverses. Segment margin expansion (24% → 45% QoQ) suggests improving competitive economics as clusters mature. We score competitive position above neutral — improving trajectory, structural tailwind, credible backlog — but hold it below the growth score because the advantage rests on external inputs (NVIDIA allocation, financing terms) rather than durable differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**Financing cost / rate sensitivity (the dominant risk).** The CEO's own admission — financing at 10-12% "kills all of the economy" of the buildout — makes this a leveraged duration trade. This is a company-specific, quantified fragility rather than a priced-in sector truism. [unverified — the "higher-for-longer confirmed," long-end pressure, and ~42% December-hike odds claims are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

**Customer concentration.** Microsoft and Meta anchor the backlog. Both can insource compute at scale, and Meta in particular has done so historically. A renegotiation, delay, or insourcing decision would strike the equity story directly.

**Capex funding and dilution.** The 2026 capex requirement demands continuous capital raising. Share count already rose ~7% in 2025, and the funding gap makes further dilution or leverage near-certain.

**Quality of earnings.** GAAP net income is propped by non-cash fair-value gains. Any investor anchoring on reported net income or trailing P/E will badly misread the business.

**AI compute deflation.** Rival capacity additions could compress the ~40% EBITDA margin assumption.

**Legacy/governance overhang.** The ex-Yandex structure, foreign-issuer governance, dual-class control, and residual divestiture complexity warrant scrutiny that the 20-F filing format makes harder.

Given the density of specific, binary, and unpriced-in-full risks — the rate sensitivity above all — we assign a low risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 171.2% versus 102.2% one-year historical vol — rich by ~69 points, in the 78th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 171.2% > six-month 134.5%), signaling the market expects a near-term catalyst-driven move (the nearest expiry prices a ±12.6% swing). Put/call skew of 6.6% and put/call OI of 1.06 indicate elevated downside hedging, and IV surged from 109.9% to 171.2% as the price fell 25.3% since the June snapshot. Unusual activity is put-heavy near-the-money ($210 puts at 17.2x volume/OI). Short interest is 28% of float with 3.5 days to cover — high enough to create genuine squeeze risk on any positive surprise, but also reflecting a large informed bearish cohort. For a BUY thesis this configuration would be a clear divergence flag; for our HOLD it reinforces caution on near-term entry and argues against chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$300-380).** AI demand stays supply-constrained; Nebius fills the ARR guide, ramps utilization on the Microsoft/Meta backlog, and the ABS financing model scales to fund capex without excessive dilution. If rates ease, borrowing costs stay below the 10-12% danger zone, unit economics hold, and the ~40% EBITDA margin converts the contracted backlog into a durable earnings base. In that world the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst band ($380-410) as consensus catches up to the ARR ramp.

**Bear case (~$110-130).** Rates stay higher or the long end pushes borrowing costs into double digits, breaking buildout economics. A large counterparty renegotiates or insources; AI compute pricing deflates; the funding gap forces dilutive equity issuance. Non-cash gains reverse, exposing the operating loss. Sentiment unwinds the AI-neocloud trade (NBIS trades as a high-beta proxy for the group), and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $120 or below.

The distribution is unusually wide because the outcome hinges on an exogenous rate path the company cannot control, layered on a concentrated backlog. This is why we hold rather than commit in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The demand and contract thesis is strengthening, but the financing-cost dependency, quality-of-earnings caveats, insider selling, and near-term options stress prevent a confident BUY, while the genuine backlog and structural demand prevent an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that the buildout is being financed at sub-8% blended cost (additional ABS deals, disclosed debt terms) that preserve unit economics, combined with a rate path turning lower.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions stabilizing or turning positive on the next-quarter/next-year lines (currently deteriorating), confirming the market has repriced the capex burden.
- A pullback toward the low-$140s to $150s that widens the margin of safety against the bear case while the backlog remains intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any Microsoft or Meta contract renegotiation, delay, or insourcing signal.
- Borrowing costs disclosed near or above 10-12%, or a large dilutive equity raise at depressed prices.
- Utilization or ARR conversion falling materially below the guide, or a reversal of the non-cash gains that exposes the operating loss without a margin path.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report is the primary checkpoint (the company reports on the standard quarterly cadence following the May 13 print), with the FY2026 capex and ARR-conversion commentary as the decisive inputs. Monitor the rate path and any new financing announcements in the interim.

**Levels from current ($214 reference / recent $183):** Base-case fair value clusters in the $220-260 range (roughly consensus mean/median), contingent on stable financing conditions; bull $300-380; bear $110-130. The asymmetry is not decisively favorable at current levels given the rate overhang, which is why we wait for either a cheaper entry or a financing/rate confirmation.

## Conclusion

Nebius is one of the cleanest expressions of contracted AI-infrastructure demand, with signed backlog from Microsoft and Meta in a market where capacity sells out before it is built. The demand question is largely answered. The unanswered question is financial: can it fund the capex requirement against ~$3B of revenue at borrowing costs that leave the buildout economic? Management has told us the model breaks near 10-12% rates, and the current macro regime keeps that scenario live. Layered on top are non-cash-gain-flattered earnings, broad insider selling into the peak (including a ~$101.6M CIO option monetization), 28% short interest, and options pricing an imminent ±12.6% move.

The score has trended up (5.5 → 6.0) on improving financial-health and management-alignment reads, but the capex-guide raise and the forward-estimate deterioration temper further upgrade. We hold at an overall 5.4. The stock's ~36% decline from its high while fundamentals strengthened does improve the demand-side risk/reward, but at this magnitude of drawdown the burden is on the bull case, and the specific thing the sellers may be right about is the rate/financing dependency, which is real and unresolved. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$140s to $150s or on concrete evidence of sub-8% financing plus a lower rate path. Until then, HOLD.
