## Executive Summary

Grocery Outlet is a differentiated extreme-value retailer working through its second consecutive restructuring in eighteen months, with two goodwill impairments booked in consecutive quarters totaling $307.0M and $475.8M of goodwill still carried against $807.1M of book equity. The operating diagnosis is narrow and well documented in the filings: traffic is growing (+1.6% in FY2025, +2.1% in Q1 FY2026) while average transaction size is falling and the decline is accelerating (-1.1%, then -3.1%). Management has named the cause — the post-ERP push to keep commodity staples in stock displaced the opportunistic, high-excitement inventory that drives basket adds — and has guided the remedy to be margin-dilutive: promotional investment that "adversely impacted gross margin in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and which we expect will continue to adversely impact gross margin for the remainder of fiscal 2026." Q1 FY2026 gross margin fell 80bp to 29.6% while SG&A rose to 29.8% of sales, so SG&A now exceeds gross margin, and adjusted EPS fell to $0.05 from $0.13.

The balance sheet is not the binding constraint. Net funded debt of $431.0M against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $245.5M implies roughly 1.76x total net leverage versus a 3.25x covenant maximum, meaning adjusted EBITDA would need to fall about 46% before a leverage-test breach; interest coverage is roughly 8x against a 1.75x minimum; liquidity is $59.0M cash plus $174.9M of undrawn revolver. This is why we move from the prior AVOID rating to REDUCE. The distress-tail framing that AVOID implies is not supported by the covenant and liquidity math, and the 33.5% short interest with 8.6 days to cover makes an actively bearish position unattractive on mechanics.

We cannot support ownership at $9.835. The stock has moved from $5.655 in March to $9.835 today, capturing most of the dislocation the CEO and a director bought into at an average $6.03 and $6.37 respectively. At today's price the equity is 19.7x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $0.50 and 15.6x FY2027 consensus of $0.63, above the $8.38 consensus mean and $9.00 median targets, on a franchise whose adjusted EPS has gone $1.07 (FY2023) → $0.77 → $0.76 → $0.05 in the most recent quarter, and whose FY2026 free cash flow is plausibly negative once $170M of capex and $49-60M of Optimization Plan lease-exit cash are funded. Base case $8.50, bear $5.75, bull $14.00; probability-weighted return roughly -8%. Existing holders should use strength to trim ahead of the Q2 print that the options market is pricing at ±16% within nine days.

## Company Overview

Grocery Outlet operated 570 stores across 16 states at fiscal year-end January 3, 2026, falling to 549 by April 4, 2026 after the first tranche of Optimization Plan closures. Sales floors average roughly 13,600 square feet; 284 of 570 stores (50%) are in California, along with four company-operated distribution centers and the Emeryville headquarters. Distribution runs through nine primary DCs — four self-operated for dry goods, five third-party operated for cold, produce and dry.

Two structural features define the model. On the buy side, a centralized team acquires excess and opportunistic inventory from order cancellations, manufacturer overruns, packaging changes and approaching sell-by dates. The 10-K discloses that all inventory is acquired via purchase orders with no long-term supplier contracts specifying quantity or price. Basket pricing is described as roughly 40% below conventional grocers and 20% below discount retailers; "WOW!" deals run 40-70% below conventional retail. Private label is nascent — approximately 485 SKUs, first introduced in Q3 FY2024.

On the sell side, 506 of 549 stores are operated by Independent Operators. Grocery Outlet leases and builds every store, owns the fixtures, pays rent and occupancy, retains ownership of merchandise on consignment until sale, and recognizes all sales proceeds as its own revenue ($1.11B of Q1's $1.166B came from IO stores). It pays the IO a commission of generally 50% of store-level gross profit ($171.0M in Q1) and lends the operator startup capital via IO Notes bearing 4.00%-9.95%. The IO hires and pays all store labor, utilities and card-processing fees. Either party may terminate on 75 days' notice. IOs are variable interest entities but are not consolidated; maximum ASC 810 exposure is the note and receivable balance of $84.4M gross at April 4, 2026.

The economic consequence is a P&L that looks like a full retailer's on the revenue line and a franchisor's on the cost line: variable IO commission (14.7% of Q1 sales) replaces fixed store payroll, but occupancy is fixed and sits with the company. Rent is therefore the cost that breaks a weak store, which is why the Optimization Plan is a lease-exit exercise and why $109.8M of long-lived asset impairment landed on stores subsequently designated for closure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is square footage, and the square footage is now shrinking.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 (52wk) | $3,969.5M | +10.9% | +7.5% | 468 |
| FY2024 (52wk) | $4,371.5M | +10.1% | +2.7% | 533 |
| FY2025 (53wk) | $4,688.8M | +7.3% | +0.5% | 570 |
| Q1 FY2026 (13wk) | $1,166.4M | +3.6% | -1.0% | 549 |

FY2025's headline +7.3% is flattered by a 53rd week worth $82.4M; ex-week growth was roughly +5.4%, essentially all of it new stores. FY2026 openings are guided to 30-33 net new (excluding closures) against 42 in FY2025 and 67 in FY2024, while 36 stores plus one distribution center exit — 27 closed in Q1, the remaining 9 in Q2.

**The margin picture separates into a non-cash cliff and a genuine erosion beneath it.** FY2025 GAAP results carried $113.8M of long-lived asset impairment, $45.9M of restructuring and $149.0M of goodwill impairment, producing an operating loss of $(221.7)M and net loss of $(224.9)M, or $(2.30) per diluted share. Q1 FY2026 added $158.0M of goodwill impairment — from an interim test triggered specifically by the decline in the share price — plus $18.2M of restructuring, for a net loss of $(180.3)M, or $(1.83).

Strip the charges and the trend is still down. Adjusted EBITDA of $254.3M in FY2025 was a five-year record, but adjusted EBITDA margin compressed from 6.36% in FY2023 to 5.42% in FY2024 and held flat at 5.42% in FY2025. Adjusted EPS went $1.07 → $0.77 → $0.76, then $0.05 in Q1 FY2026 versus $0.13, a 64.6% decline in adjusted net income to $4.6M. In Q1, gross margin of 29.6% (50bp of the 80bp decline from closure-store markdowns, the balance promotional investment) fell below SG&A of 29.8%. Adjusted EBITDA of $43.1M was down 16.9%.

**Leverage and liquidity.** At April 4, 2026: cash $59.0M, funded debt $490.0M gross ($270.0M term loan, $220.0M revolver), all floating at approximately 6%, all maturing February 21, 2028. Total assets $2,958.0M, liabilities $2,150.9M, equity $807.1M — down from $1,197.4M at FYE2024, roughly $390M of book equity destroyed in five quarters, essentially all impairment-driven, with retained earnings flipping to a $(23.8)M accumulated deficit. Operating lease liabilities of $1,352.5M against $1,122.3M of ROU assets leave a $230M excess, the accounting footprint of previously impaired store assets. Current ratio 1.28x, down from 1.37x.

Net funded debt of $431.0M against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $245.5M ($254.3M FY2025 less $51.9M Q1 FY2025 plus $43.1M Q1 FY2026) implies 1.76x against a 3.25x covenant; break-even adjusted EBITDA at today's debt is approximately $132.6M. That headroom calculation is ours, not the certified covenant test — the credit agreement's "Consolidated EBITDA" is a defined term permitting add-backs beyond reported adjusted EBITDA — but the company affirms compliance at both January 3 and April 4, 2026. On a lease-adjusted basis the picture is tighter: net debt plus lease liabilities of roughly $1,784M against EBITDAR of approximately $415M (adjusted EBITDA plus $169.8M of FY2026 lease payments) is roughly 4.3x, normal for a grocer but not a balance sheet with room for error.

**Cash flow is the item most likely to be mis-modeled.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $222.1M against $198.3M of capex — free cash flow of roughly $23.8M, a 0.5% margin. The $110.2M year-over-year OCF improvement came substantially from working-capital timing (inventory management, trade payables, accrued liabilities) rather than earnings; the three-year OCF range of $112M (FY2024) → $303M (FY2023) → $222M (FY2025) on a stable revenue base means any single year is low-confidence for trend purposes.

For FY2026, management has guided capex to approximately $170M net of tenant improvement allowances. The Optimization Plan's lease exits are expected to require $49-60M of cash, largely offset in the P&L by a $48-52M non-cash ROU and lease-liability write-off, which is why the *net* charge estimate is only $1-8M, an arithmetically misleading figure for anyone reading it as a cash indicator. Only $0.176M of restructuring liability was accrued at April 4, so nearly all of that cash is still ahead. Add roughly $3M of operator-termination cash. If OCF lands at $180-210M, free cash flow after capex and restructuring cash is approximately **negative $22M to negative $53M**, funded from the same revolver that is already $220M drawn and matures with the term loan. Reinstated 100% bonus depreciation under OBBBA should help cash taxes at the margin; a favorable payables cycle could keep FCF near breakeven. Neither changes the direction.

## Growth Analysis

The comp decomposition is the whole argument. FY2025: transactions +1.6%, average transaction size -1.1%. Q1 FY2026: transactions +2.1%, average transaction size -3.1%. Customers are arriving in growing numbers and spending less per visit, with the per-visit decline widening by 200bp in a single year.

Two explanations fit, and the disclosed data does not separate them. The first is self-inflicted: management's own MD&A states that "our recent focus on improving in-stocks and ensuring the availability of everyday commodity staples adversely impacted our ability to deliver high-quality opportunistic product and the perception of our value leadership." If that is dominant, re-acquiring opportunistic inventory and promoting it should restore ticket at the cost of gross margin through FY2026, which is what management has guided.

The second is external: a financially stressed low-income cohort buying less per trip regardless of assortment. Approximately 9% of FY2025 net sales were EBT payments, and the 10-K discloses that the Q4 FY2025 federal shutdown disrupted benefit disbursement and negatively affected EBT sales. If the external driver dominates, the promotional investment buys little comp recovery and simply resets margin lower.

Consensus splits the difference: FY2026 EPS of $0.50 (revised +1.0% over 90 days) and FY2027 of $0.63 (+2.4%), implying a 26% earnings recovery next year. Near-term estimates are moving the other way — the current quarter has been cut 18.6% over 90 days to $0.12, next quarter -4.8%. The pattern is a stable out-year recovery assumption sitting on top of deteriorating near-term visibility, which is the configuration most vulnerable to a second-half guide-down. The closure of 36 loss-making stores should be mechanically accretive to FY2027 unit economics; against that, 31 executed leases for stores not yet possessed carry $206.5M of undiscounted future payments through 2045, partially offsetting closure savings.

The strategic pivot to company-operating certain FY2026 openings "for an uncertain time period" before transitioning to an IO is a departure from historical practice that the 10-K prices: considerable resources, a significant increase in employees, and direct exposure to store-level employment, labor and general liability claims previously absorbed by IOs, plus greater union-organizing exposure. It also implies the IO recruitment funnel is not producing enough qualified operators for the opening plan, a dependency Item 1A calls out and one corroborated by the state of the IO loan book.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is a relative price gap, and the filings state that it is narrowing. The FY2025 10-K names **Aldi, Lidl and WinCo** as having "moved into, or increased their presence in, our geographic and product markets," and carries as a standalone Recent Trends item: "During the last few years we have observed an increase in promotional and pricing activities from key competitors, putting further pressure on our relative value proposition." Customers most often name Walmart and Safeway as alternates.

Grocery Outlet's structural response is constrained in three ways it has disclosed itself. It downshifted FY2025-26 distribution investment from capital-intensive projects to lower-cost distribution centers, acknowledging this "could hinder our ability to compete with companies that have invested in multi-temperature or automated facilities." Its roughly 485 private-label SKUs sit against hard discounters built on private label, while Item 1A separately flags consumer movement toward private label and away from name brands as a threat, because the entire pitch is name brands at discount prices. And it has no traditional loyalty program (only a personalization app) and no owned e-commerce, relying on three third-party delivery partners, while noting competitors have "established, long-standing mobile apps and personalized marketing."

The supply side carries a subtler and more durable threat. Grocery Outlet's inventory is a function of its suppliers' operational imprecision. Item 1A states that "to the extent that some of our suppliers are better able to manage their inventory levels and reduce the amount of their excess inventory, the amount of over-stock and short-dated products available to us could also be materially reduced," and adds that as the store base grows, "our ability to secure opportunistic products in sufficient quantities may become more difficult." CPG demand-planning improvement is a structural headwind, not a cyclical one, and it is a rare diseconomy of scale: the model gets harder to source as it gets bigger.

The IO loan book is the clearest available read on store-level economics, and it is strained. The allowance against IO notes and receivables went $18.5M (March 2025) → $27.5M (January 2026) → $35.4M (April 2026), reaching 42.0% of the $84.4M gross exposure. The Q1 provision was $18.0M versus $3.3M a year earlier, though $15.5M of that was explicitly tied to Operator Agreement Terminations for stores the company chose to close; excluding it, the provision was roughly $2.5M, below the prior-year quarter. Operators in the Temporary Commission Adjustment Program, for those "who require assistance in meeting their working capital needs," held 48.0% of note balances at April 4, improved from 50.6% at January 3, at a subsidized 4.00% rate versus the 9.95% standard. The 10-Q states no notes were past due or on non-accrual, then notes TCAP participants are excluded from those measures, which limits what the reassurance establishes. On balance this is elevated, disclosed and largely reserved IO stress rather than a hidden credit event, but a real constraint on recruiting the operators needed for 30-33 openings.

## Risk Assessment

**Reflexive goodwill impairment.** $475.8M of net goodwill remains against $807.1M of equity, 59% of book. The impairment test uses equally weighted DCF and guideline-public-company approaches with the implied control premium versus market capitalization as a reasonableness check; the Q1 charge was triggered by the share-price decline itself. Both the 10-K and 10-Q warn that if cash flows fall short or WACC rises, "the fair value may further decrease resulting in an incremental material goodwill impairment." This is non-cash, excluded from covenant math, and does not affect the cash outflows that matter, but a third charge would push equity toward $650M and reinforce the negative narrative into a refinancing.

**Refinancing.** $463.75M due February 21, 2028, floating rate, roughly 20 months out, at a company currently generating GAAP losses with a shrinking store count and probable negative free cash flow this year. Liquidity is adequate through 2027; the risk is arriving at the negotiation having consumed rather than built cash.

**Litigation, unaccrued and escalating.** Six concurrent matters: the 2025 ERP-related class action (motion to dismiss fully briefed, submitted on the briefs after the March 19, 2026 hearing was vacated), a stayed 2025 derivative suit, two derivative suits filed in March 2026 (one dismissed without prejudice April 17), a further derivative suit filed April 13, and the most significant, a **2026 class action filed March 16 in N.D. Cal. naming the company and certain of its *current* officers**, alleging misstatements regarding growth strategy, financial performance and "the effectiveness of its restructuring plan." No accrual has been recorded and no range is estimable. Securities suits follow price declines mechanically and filing establishes nothing about merit, but the shift from legacy officers/ERP to current officers/restructuring effectiveness constrains how freely management can characterize the turnaround.

**Governance and continuity.** Twelve Item 5.02 officer or director change filings between December 2023 and June 2026, including a CFO transition inside the last quarter — Christopher M. Miller signed the Q1 10-Q on May 13, 2026, and Form 4s identify Ian Daniel Ferry as EVP, CFO and Treasurer by June 15, 2026. The 8-K text is unread (fetch returned HTTP 403), so cause and terms are unconfirmed. The 10-K itself concedes the churn: external searches for "several new executive officers, including a new Chief Financial Officer and a new President and Chief Executive Officer," with interim officers serving in the interim. Related-party leases — 14 stores and one warehouse from entities in which Chairman Eric Lindberg or family hold interests, $49.3M of lease liability and $1.9M of Q1 rent — are small and disclosed but represent a live conflict while the company negotiates third-party landlord exits on 36 leases.

**Concentration.** 50% of stores and four DCs in California, with 2025 Southern California fires cited as having affected certain stores. Approximately 9% of sales via EBT, exposed to federal benefit disruption. Tariff whipsaw is disclosed and unquantified: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking certain IEEPA tariffs, followed by the administration invoking tariffs under other authorities, with exposure across imported fresh meat, general merchandise and construction materials. IO reclassification under AB-5 or joint-employer theories remains a longstanding structural tail, and the company-operated pivot increases direct labor exposure at the margin.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 119.3% against 54.7% one-year realized — a 64.6pp spread — with the term structure in steep backwardation (119.3% at 9 days, 83.7% at 37 days, 67.8% at 156 days) and a ±16.0% implied move on the August 21 expiry. That configuration prices an imminent, dated event, almost certainly the Q2 FY2026 report (Q1 was released May 13 for a quarter ended April 4; Q2 ended in early July). Put/call volume of 2.11 and the data provider's read of the 11.0% skew as elevated put demand indicate near-term flow is hedging or positioning for downside.

Standing open interest points the other way: a put/call OI ratio of 0.07 is heavily call-tilted, consistent with longer-dated speculation on a squeeze or turnaround given 21.4M shares short (33.5% of float, 8.6 days to cover). The two signals together describe a stock where near-term event risk is being hedged with puts while the structural bet in open interest is upside, a two-sided setup that argues against both owning into the print and shorting it. We place no weight on the prior snapshot's 1,572.9% ATM IV reading, which appears to be a data artifact. Rich near-term volatility is unfavorable entry timing for buyers and expensive protection for holders; trimming into strength is the cheaper way to reduce exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($14.00, +42%).** Traffic is positive and accelerating, so the brand remains relevant; the problem is what customers put in the basket, and management has identified it. Rebuilding opportunistic assortment and promoting it restores ticket over two to three quarters; removing the 36 closed stores plus $109.8M of associated impaired assets lifts FY2027 unit economics. Covenant headroom of roughly 46% of EBITDA and $234M of liquidity buy time. At 15.6x FY2027 consensus and 5.7x EV/adjusted EBITDA, modest normalization to $0.90 of adjusted EPS by FY2028 supports $13-14. The CEO bought 398,905 shares for $2.41M at an average $6.03 and a director bought 325,000 for $2.07M at $6.37, both in the two weeks after the FY2025 loss and Optimization Plan were disclosed, with the CEO plausibly holding visibility into the quarter that produced the second impairment. A 33.5% short base with 8.6 days to cover means any comp inflection produces disproportionate upside.

**Bear case ($5.75, -41.5%).** The ticket decline is a low-income consumer problem rather than an assortment problem: 9% EBT exposure, disclosed shutdown-driven benefit disruption, and pressure concentrated in the lowest-income tier all point that way. In that case the promotional investment permanently resets gross margin lower for no comp benefit, and FY2026 exits with adjusted EPS near $0.45 and free cash flow negative $40M. A third goodwill charge, mechanically linked to the share price through the market-approach input, drives equity below $650M. The company enters the February 2028 refinancing of $463.75M having consumed cash with negative comps, and the competitive squeeze — Aldi/Lidl/WinCo on price, CPG demand-planning discipline on supply, a distribution network management concedes may not compete with automated facilities — is structural rather than cyclical. Six concurrent legal matters with no accrual and a CFO transition inside the last quarter cap the multiple regardless of operations.

**Our view.** The bear risk is not solvency and the bull case is not implausible; the equity is simply not purchasable at 17% above the consensus mean target with negative near-term estimate revisions, a negative FCF year in progress and a ±16% event nine days out. The insider buying is the most credible evidence in the file, and it was executed roughly 39% below today's price. That dislocation has been arbitraged.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $8.50 (-13.6%), bull $14.00 (+42%), bear $5.75 (-41.5%); probability-weighted expected return approximately -8% over 12 months.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into strength ahead of the Q2 FY2026 print rather than after it, because near-term implied volatility of 119.3% makes options protection expensive and a ±16% move on a $973M market cap is not a risk worth carrying for a -8% expected return. Reduce to a residual position no larger than a third of a full weight, sized to survive a squeeze rather than to express conviction. Do not short: 33.5% of float short with 8.6 days to cover and call-tilted open interest is the wrong mechanics regardless of the fundamental view.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD.** (1) Comparable sales return to positive with average transaction size decline narrowing to better than -1.0% for two consecutive quarters; (2) gross margin stabilizes at or above 29.6% sequentially, indicating the promotional investment has annualized rather than deepened; (3) reported free cash flow for FY2026 lands positive after the $49-60M of lease-exit cash, demonstrating the capex reduction to $170M is real.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE.** All three of the above plus a price near $7.00 or below — approximately 11x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS — or a refinancing of the February 2028 term loan and revolver completed at a spread and tenor that removes the 2028 event from the equity story.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) FY2026 adjusted EBITDA tracking below approximately $200M, which would cut covenant headroom from 46% to roughly 34% and make the refinancing conversation materially harder; (2) a third goodwill impairment accompanied by a guidance reduction, indicating the DCF input and not just the market-cap input has deteriorated; (3) revolver drawings advancing beyond roughly $300M, or (4) an adverse ruling in the 2025 class action that establishes a quantifiable liability alongside the 2026 action naming current officers.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 FY2026 report, expected within days given the nine-day expiry priced at ±16%, is the immediate checkpoint; the specific items to read are comparable-sales decomposition (traffic versus ticket), gross margin sequential trend, whether a third goodwill test was triggered, and the cash outflow recognized against the $49-60M lease-exit estimate. The next structural checkpoint is Q4 FY2026 (reported approximately March 2027), when the closure program completes and the FY2027 opening cadence and any refinancing plan should be disclosed.

## Conclusion

Grocery Outlet's problem is legible and management has named it: the post-ERP focus on commodity in-stocks displaced the opportunistic buying that generates basket adds, and the fix runs through gross margin. Traffic of +2.1% against ticket of -3.1% in Q1 FY2026 means the brand still draws customers it can no longer monetize at the prior rate. Whether that is repairable assortment damage or an unfixable low-income consumer is the central open question, and the disclosed data does not settle it.

What is settled is the price. At $9.835 the equity trades at 19.7x FY2026 and 15.6x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, 5.7x EV/adjusted EBITDA with a negative free cash flow yield, and 17% above the $8.38 consensus mean target, on an adjusted EPS series that has gone $1.07 → $0.77 → $0.76 → $0.05 in the latest quarter. The balance sheet buys time — 1.76x net leverage against a 3.25x covenant, $234M of liquidity, nothing due until February 2028 — which is why we move off AVOID after three consecutive such ratings. It does not create value. Our overall score of 3.8 reflects marginally weaker operating evidence than the prior review (negative comps, a second impairment, adjusted net income down 64.6%) offset by better-understood solvency; the rating improves one notch because the distress-tail framing was too severe and shorting into a 33.5% short base is not a trade we would recommend. Holders should trim into strength. Prospective buyers should wait for either the comp inflection or a price near $7.00.
