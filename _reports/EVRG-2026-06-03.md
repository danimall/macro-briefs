**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Evergy is a vertically integrated, rate-base-driven regulated electric utility serving roughly 1.7 million customers across Kansas and Missouri through three regulated subsidiaries, with about 15,800 MW of owned generating capacity anchored by the 94%-owned Wolf Creek nuclear plant. The investment story has shifted materially over the past year: after a long stretch of "muted" 3-4% earnings growth, management is now guiding to 6-8%+ adjusted EPS CAGR through 2030, accelerating above 8% from 2028-2030. The driver is a $21.6B 2026-2030 capital plan (roughly double the historical run rate), underpinned by approximately 1,900 MW of contracted data-center load and a supportive regulatory and legislative backdrop in both states.

The most important de-risking feature is structural rather than rhetorical. Evergy's Large Load Power Service tariffs, approved in both Kansas and Missouri in November 2025, require an 80% minimum monthly demand charge, two years of collateral, and material termination fees on new load above 75 MW over 12-year terms. This shifts much of the "stranded asset if hyperscalers don't show up" risk onto the customers themselves (Meta and Google are building in the territory), addressing the single biggest objection to the capex thesis. Missouri SB 4 (CWIP in rate base, PISA extended to 2035) and Kansas HB 2107 (wildfire liability caps) further improve cash recovery timing and limit tail liabilities.

The valuation is where the case loses asymmetry. At $81.35 the stock trades at roughly 19x the FY26 consensus of about $4.25, slightly below the electric utility peer median of 21.7x, and roughly 10% below the analyst mean target of $90.63. The dividend yields about 3.4%, below the peer median of 4.0%. The funding plan requires approximately $3.3B of equity issuance through 2030, a real per-share dilution headwind that offsets part of the rate-base growth. The stock has appreciated 22% over the trailing year and sits within 5% of its 52-week high. This is a quality compounding utility at a fair price, which supports a hold with a bullish lean rather than a buy.

## Company Overview

Evergy (incorporated 2017, headquartered in Kansas City) operates as a single reported segment through three regulated integrated electric utilities: Evergy Kansas Central (the largest, ~$3.06B revenue and $551M net income in 2025), Evergy Metro (Missouri and Kansas, ~$1.92B revenue and $320M net income), and Evergy Missouri West. Generation is "all-of-the-above": coal, the 1985-vintage Wolf Creek nuclear unit, natural gas, wind, and a growing solar/storage book, supplemented by renewable PPAs. The company is a member of the SPP regional transmission organization.

Earnings power is set by what the Kansas Corporation Commission, the Missouri Public Service Commission, and FERC allow Evergy to charge. The company's current rate cases are filed at a 10.5% requested ROE on a 52% equity capital structure. This is a textbook regulated-monopoly model where returns are formula-driven and growth comes from expanding the rate base on which the allowed return is earned.

The defining external dynamic is the data-center and AI-driven load surge hitting the Kansas-Missouri corridor. Evergy is the electricity provider to these facilities, not the owner. Meta is building near Kansas City in Evergy territory, and Google announced a $15B Missouri infrastructure investment with a New Florence data center whose Capacity Commitment Framework is embedded in MPSC-approved tariffs through partnerships with both Ameren Missouri and Evergy (a unanimous Missouri PSC settlement on November 24, 2025 was signed by Evergy Metro and Evergy Missouri West alongside Google, Ameren, and others). These hyperscalers are customers and counterparties.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue rose from $5.51B (2023) to $5.85B (2024) to $5.96B (2025). A meaningful portion is pass-through (fuel and purchased power recovered through cost-recovery mechanisms), so management's preferred metric is utility gross margin, which grew from $4.00B (2024) to $4.11B (2025). GAAP net income was $731M (2023), $874M (2024), and $856M (2025), a modest decline. Diluted EPS fell from $3.79 (2024) to $3.66 (2025); adjusted EPS edged up from $3.81 to $3.83.

The 2025 GAAP EPS dip is explainable and largely non-recurring. It was driven by higher O&M, depreciation, and interest expense, plus $48.7M of losses and impairments on non-regulated early-stage clean-energy investments (now being divested) and roughly $0.05/share of convertible-note dilution. New retail rates (Missouri West in January 2025, Kansas Central in October 2025) and higher transmission revenue partly offset these. The clean-energy losses are a one-time cleanup of legacy mistakes and should not recur.

**Profitability and cash flow.** Operating margin was 25.7%, net margin 14.6%, ROE 8.5%, ROA 2.6% — all typical for a regulated utility. Operating cash flow is stable and rising: $1.98B (2023), $1.98B (2024), $2.05B (2025). The critical point is that capex now vastly exceeds operating cash flow: $2.80B in 2025 (up from $2.34B), placing the company in a heavy free-cash-flow-negative investment phase funded by external capital.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets are $33.9B, net PP&E $26.3B, stockholders' equity $10.2B. D/E is 3.32; the capital structure ex-short-term debt runs roughly 43% equity / 57% long-term debt. The current ratio of 0.49 is structurally normal for a utility relying on commercial paper. Screening metrics flag distress — Altman Z of 0.86 and Piotroski 2/9 — but these are artifacts of the regulated-utility model (high leverage, low asset turnover of 0.18, negative working capital) and do not signal bankruptcy risk. Credit ratings are solidly investment grade (Evergy BBB+/Baa2 stable; operating subsidiaries A-/BBB+), and cash flows are predictable with regulatory cost recovery. The relevant financial risk is financing cost and equity dilution, not solvency.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend payout target is 50-60%, yielding about 3.4% at the current price. The $21.6B 2026-2030 capex plan ramps annual spend to roughly $4.0-4.8B, funded by approximately $12.3B of debt issuance (including ~$3.9B of refinancing) and approximately $3.3B of equity (a $1.2B ATM with forward sales is in place). Management has been disciplined on dilution at the margins: it repurchased $244.1M of the $1.4B 4.50% convertibles in January/February 2026 for $308.6M (a premium reflecting in-the-money conversion), leaving $1,156M outstanding.

**Earnings delivery.** The recent surprise record is mixed: a 4.6% beat in Q1 2026, but misses of 27.3% (Q4 2025), 3.2% (Q3 2025), and 1.4% (Q2 2025). Quarterly utility EPS is heavily weather-driven, so the Q4 miss is less alarming on an annual adjusted basis. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.69 beat the $0.66 consensus despite mild weather, and FY26 adjusted EPS guidance was maintained at $4.14-4.34 (consensus ~$4.24). The annual delivery track record is more reliable than the noisy quarterly cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The growth inflection is genuine and supported by multiple confirming pieces of evidence rather than a single-quarter story. Management has stepped up from low-single-digit growth to a 6-8%+ adjusted EPS CAGR through 2030, with explicit acceleration above 8% in 2028-2030. The mechanism is straightforward: the $21.6B capex plan expands the rate base, and the allowed ROE (10.5% requested) on that base drives earnings, with growth front-loaded into generation and transmission tied to data-center load.

The load itself is contracting, not hoped-for. In February 2026 Evergy signed energy service agreements with multiple large-load data-center customers totaling approximately 1,900 MW of projected peak steady-state load (two new projects plus two expansions), with service beginning 2026-2028. The capex plan includes roughly $9.3B of new generation: two ~705 MW combined-cycle gas plants (Sumner County ~2029, Reno County ~2030) and a 440 MW Missouri West simple-cycle plant (2030), plus solar projects (Kansas Sky 159 MW delayed by litigation, Sunflower Sky, Foxtrot).

Recovery economics are improving. Missouri SB 4 (April 2025) allows CWIP in rate base for new gas plants in lieu of AFUDC and extends PISA through 2035, materially improving cash recovery timing on the generation build. Rate cases are progressing: the Kansas Central 2025 case settled at +$128M (vs. $196M requested) with rates effective October 2025, and the Evergy Metro 2026 case was filed in February requesting +$140M at 10.5% ROE/52% equity, with new rates targeted for January 2027.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising — current-year and next-year consensus are essentially flat over 30 and 90 days, with a modest -4.0% trim to the current quarter offset by a +1.1% lift to next quarter. This is not an accelerating-revision profile that would mark a strong technical buy signal; consensus already reflects the growth guidance. The growth thesis is durable and broad-based, but the market is not systematically underpricing it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.35 Evergy trades at roughly 19x the FY26 consensus of about $4.25 and roughly 17.9x the FY27 consensus near $4.55. Against the electric utility peer set, this is fair to slightly cheap: peer median P/E is 21.7x (LNT 22.5x, PPL 21.7x, FE 25.2x), though the group spans EIX at 7.6x (litigation/wildfire overhang) to ES at 14.6x. Evergy's dividend yield of 3.4% sits below the peer median of 4.0%, reflecting its lower payout and faster growth profile rather than a premium quality discount.

A growth-adjusted view is more favorable than a static P/E. A regulated utility guiding to 6-8%+ EPS growth, accelerating above 8% later in the plan, trading at 19x forward is reasonable relative to slower-growing peers at similar multiples. The implied PEG is around 2.5-3.0, which is unremarkable for utilities but better than several peers when adjusted for Evergy's superior growth trajectory. The analyst range runs $80 (low) to $99 (high), with a mean of $90.63 and median of $90.00, placing the stock roughly 10-11% below consensus mean and only marginally above the low. BTIG's $99 target (Buy) frames the 6-8% outlook as "reasonable and possibly conservative" if additional large-load deals land.

The valuation does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a higher score. The stock has risen 22% over the trailing year and trades within 5% of its 52-week high of $85.27, so the easy re-rating off the prior muted-growth multiple has largely happened. The ~$3.3B equity issuance through 2030 is a genuine per-share dilution headwind that partially offsets rate-base growth. The fair conclusion is a stock priced approximately at intrinsic value with a modest cushion to consensus targets, not a clear mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Competition in this business is regulatory and locational rather than commercial. Evergy holds a regulated monopoly in its service territory, so the relevant questions are whether it can win its share of regional hyperscaler demand and whether regulators continue to permit constructive recovery.

On the first question, the benchmark is in-state peer Ameren, which is pursuing the same theme more aggressively (2.2 GW of signed ESAs out of 3.4 GW of construction agreements, 5+ GW of new generation planned). Evergy's ~1,900 MW of signed ESAs is meaningful but smaller, and the Google CCF framework is shared between Ameren and Evergy in Missouri. Evergy is a credible participant in the regional load build but not the dominant capture vehicle; the trajectory is improving as pipeline converts to binding agreements.

On the regulatory question, the trajectory is favorable. The LLPS tariffs (80% minimum demand charge, 2-year collateral, termination fees, 12-year terms) are enshrined in both states' settlements and represent a structural moat against demand risk. SB 4 and HB 2107 reflect a supportive legislative environment. The countervailing pressure is affordability: local reporting (Kansas City Star, March 2026) shows residents fear data centers will raise bills, and PISA recovery is capped (1.5%/yr in Kansas, 2.5%/yr in Missouri) — a tripling of capex could test those caps and leave some costs unrecovered. Net, the competitive position is stable-to-improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Large-load demand materialization.** The entire capex thesis, including the $9.3B generation build, is predicated on data-center load arriving and being sustained. The 10-K explicitly warns of material adverse effects if demand does not occur or sustain as projected. The LLPS tariffs substantially mitigate this by shifting stranded-asset risk onto the hyperscalers, but the demand-growth assumption remains the central swing variable.

**Construction execution.** Evergy committed to a single power-island equipment vendor and made equipment purchases ahead of regulatory approval for the gas plants, creating cancellation-penalty and stranded-cost exposure if approvals or load fail.

**Regulatory lag and PISA caps.** A capex ramp this large risks breaching the annual PISA recovery caps, leaving deferred costs unrecovered against affordability limits regulators are sensitive to.

**Financing and dilution.** With $12.3B of planned debt issuance and $3.3B of equity, both interest-rate moves and share count materially affect per-share earnings. Interest expense already rose $53M in 2025. Refinancing roughly $3.9B and issuing new debt is a real cost headwind, partially offset by the long-duration, formula-driven nature of utility cash flows.

**Concentration risks.** Wolf Creek is a single 40-year-old nuclear unit with a sole fuel-fabrication supplier (previously in Chapter 11); an extended outage could trigger rate-base exclusion. Operations are geographically concentrated in Kansas and Missouri. Wildfire liability is partially mitigated by HB 2107 but not eliminated.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 21.1% against 15.8% trailing realized vol, modestly rich (5.3% spread) but at only the 10th percentile of the current chain. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.5%, and the term structure is flat (21.1% near vs. 21.8% far). Put/call skew is call-heavy (-7.5% near-term), and put/call OI is a low 0.38, indicating no meaningful downside hedging demand. The options market is not pricing a binary catalyst or elevated downside risk; positioning is benign-to-mildly-constructive, consistent with the fundamental view. The recent rise in P/C OI from 0.12 to 0.38 is off a tiny base and not informative.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-99).** Large-load demand materializes faster than guided, additional ESAs are signed beyond the current ~1,900 MW, and the LLPS tariff structure proves the demand is firm. Rate cases settle constructively (the Metro +$140M case lands favorably for January 2027 rates), SB 4's CWIP-in-rate-base mechanism accelerates cash recovery on the gas build, and EPS growth tracks toward the upper end of the 6-8% range with acceleration above 8% confirmed for 2028-2030. The stock re-rates toward the peer-high multiple and the BTIG $99 target, roughly 22% above current levels. This requires the data-center pipeline to convert and regulators to remain supportive on affordability.

**Bear Case ($65-68).** Data-center load disappoints or slips, the gas-plant equipment commitments turn into stranded costs, and PISA caps bind, leaving capex unrecovered. Rising rates lift financing costs while the $3.3B equity program dilutes per-share growth more than the rate base adds. The stock de-rates toward the low end of the utility group and back toward its 52-week low of $64.70, roughly 19% below current levels. A Wolf Creek outage or an adverse rate-case outcome would accelerate this.

The base case sits between these: EPS grows in the 6-7% range, rate cases settle near precedent, dilution offsets some of the rate-base lift, and the stock compounds at high-single-digits plus the 3.4% dividend toward the high-$80s over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a bullish lean (growth_hold). The growth inflection is real and the structural de-risking via LLPS tariffs is the strongest part of the case, but at ~19x forward and within 5% of the 52-week high, the risk/reward is constructive rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (forward P/E compressing below ~17x), which would restore asymmetric upside to the $90+ target range.
- Additional binding large-load ESAs materially above the current ~1,900 MW, confirming the demand pipeline converts and supporting the >8% acceleration earlier than 2028.
- Constructive resolution of the Evergy Metro +$140M rate case at or near the 10.5% ROE / 52% equity request with January 2027 rates intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cancellation or material delay of signed data-center ESAs, or a public hyperscaler pullback in the territory.
- A rate-case outcome that cuts allowed ROE below ~9.5% or signals regulators turning adverse on affordability grounds (binding PISA caps leaving costs unrecovered).
- A material Wolf Creek outage triggering rate-base exclusion, or a credit-rating downgrade below BBB- threatening the financing plan.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the next quarterly earnings release and FY26 guidance reaffirmation, plus the Evergy Metro rate-case progress through 2026 toward January 2027 rates.

## Conclusion

Evergy is a higher-quality regulated utility at a credible growth inflection, transitioning from low-single-digit to 6-8%+ EPS growth on the back of a $21.6B capex plan and ~1,900 MW of contracted data-center load. The decisive feature is that the large-load tariffs (80% minimum demand charges, collateral, termination fees) shift demand risk onto the hyperscalers themselves, and legislative tailwinds (SB 4 CWIP, PISA to 2035) improve recovery economics. Management is cleaning up legacy non-regulated losses and managing convertible dilution sensibly, and the pay-for-performance structure has demonstrated genuine downside flex.

The constraint is price. At 19x forward, slightly below peers and roughly 10% below the analyst mean target, with a 3.4% yield below the peer median and a $3.3B equity issuance overhang, the stock offers a constructive but not asymmetric setup. The fundamentals support owning it; the entry point does not compel adding aggressively here. The recommendation is HOLD with a bullish lean — a quality rate-base compounder worth holding for the growth and income, with a BUY upgrade warranted on a pullback toward the mid-$70s or on confirmation of additional large-load contracts.
