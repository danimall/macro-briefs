## Executive Summary

Integer Holdings ceased to be a fundamental equity story on August 2, 2026, when it signed a definitive merger agreement with Armstrong Parent, Inc. and Armstrong Bidco, Inc., affiliates of Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co., to be acquired for **$127.00 per share in cash**. Equity value at that price is $4.317bn (33,992,048 shares × $127.00); the company states enterprise value of approximately $5.7bn. The stock closed at $125.27, a gross spread of **$1.73, or 1.38%**. Integer withdrew its 2026 outlook and cancelled its August 6 earnings call. The remaining questions are arithmetic and probability, not competitive strategy.

The arithmetic does not favor holding. Management has been reported as expecting closing by the end of 2026 — roughly five months from the reference date — which annualizes the 1.38% spread at **3.3%**. Three-month and nine-month closings produce 5.5% and 1.8% annualized respectively. Against a policy rate of 3.50-3.75% and Treasury bills yielding roughly 4%, the deal spread offers **no excess return over cash, plus a break tail**. Solving for indifference: if a break returns the stock to the pre-strategic-review anchor of $88 (the price at which March 31, 2026 insider tax-withholding was struck), downside is $37.27 and the market is pricing a **95.6% probability of consummation**; at a $95 break price the implied probability is 94.6%. A signed, all-cash deal with committed equity *and* debt financing, no financing condition, and a reverse termination fee ($307M) at 1.99x the company fee ($154M) plausibly deserves a high probability, but 95% is near the practical ceiling for a transaction still requiring HSR clearance plus antitrust and foreign-direct-investment approvals across a manufacturing footprint spanning Ireland, Mexico, Malaysia, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic and Uruguay. Probability-weighting at 95% close and a $92 break midpoint produces an expected return of approximately **-0.4%** before financing costs.

What sits behind the deal is a business that got worse in the quarter it was sold. Q2 2026 organic sales fell 1.5%, gross margin compressed 280bp to 24.3%, GAAP operating income fell 41.8%, and GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations dropped to $0.69 from $1.04. Net total debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA rose to 3.2x from roughly 2.7x six months earlier. Inventories rose $34.3M against falling sales. A $5.9M equipment impairment was recorded after revised cash-flow projections on specific manufacturing assets. The widely reported "beat" was against non-GAAP consensus (adjusted EPS $1.60 versus $1.55), assisted by a negative 1.3% effective tax rate and a newly created ERP-implementation add-back ($4.9M in the quarter, $8.3M in the half). That deterioration is nearly irrelevant to the deal case and central to the break case, which is why the asymmetry is unattractive. We move to **REDUCE** on materially changed information rather than on price action.

## Company Overview

Integer is one of the largest medical device contract development and manufacturing organizations globally, supplying components, sub-assemblies and finished devices to medtech OEMs from its Plano, Texas headquarters under the legacy Greatbatch Medical and Lake Region Medical brands. It reports as a single segment with three disclosed product lines. FY2025 revenue of $1,853.6M (+8.0%) split into Cardio & Vascular $1,107.1M (+16.6%), Cardiac Rhythm Management & Neuromodulation $668.8M (+1.2%), and Other Markets $77.8M (-26.9%, a deliberately exited Portable Medical line with final sales expected during 2026).

Approximately 11,000 associates operate plants in the U.S., Mexico, Ireland, Malaysia, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic and Uruguay. Firm backlog was approximately $675M at December 31, 2025, with the majority expected to ship within a year (the company's own definition of firm orders, not an ASC 606 remaining-performance-obligation figure).

The structural feature that governs everything is customer concentration: Abbott, Boston Scientific and Medtronic each exceeded 10% of revenue and together were approximately 49% of FY2025 sales, rising to 52% in Q2 2026 and 1H 2026 (from 51% in the prior-year periods). Many supply agreements contain no minimum purchase requirements. The 10-K discloses that customers have previously insourced production and implemented supplier diversification, and that Integer has reduced prices for some customers and expects continued pressure.

Strategy over the last three years has been a coatings roll-up (Pulse Technologies, Jan 2024, $138.5M; Precision Coating/Katahdin, Jan 2025, $153.5M; VSi Parylene, Feb 2025, $24.0M; and Biocoat, Dec 2025, $15.0M) moving up the value chain toward surface technologies that are harder for OEMs to insource. The 2025 acquisitions contributed $58.7M to C&V revenue, roughly 3.4 points of the reported 8.0% consolidated growth, implying organic growth of approximately 4.6%.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trend.** Revenue grew from $1,376.1M (FY2022) to $1,853.6M (FY2025), a 10.4% three-year CAGR. Operating income rose from $121.3M to $221.3M, but operating margin slipped 20bp in FY2025 to 11.9% from 12.1% despite 8% revenue growth. Gross margin improved modestly to 27.0% from 26.7%.

**The FY2025 net income anomaly resolves below the operating line.** Operating income of $221.3M less interest expense of $43.2M and other loss of $53.2M (of which $46.7M is a non-cash induced-conversion expense from the March 2025 exchange of $383.7M of 2028 converts) produced pretax income of $125.4M, an 18.0% tax rate, and continuing-operations net income of $102.8M ($2.89 diluted, down from $3.40). This is an accounting charge, not operating deterioration. It does depress FY2025 ROE (5.9%), ROA (3.0%) and the EBIT-dependent terms of any screen, and it is why the Altman Z-Score of 2.29 (grey zone) should be read with the intangible-heavy asset base (goodwill $1,110.9M plus intangibles $825.4M equals 57% of total assets) and 0.54x asset turnover as the dominant explanatory factors rather than as a distress signal.

**Cash conversion is adequate; the asset base is maturing.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $196.1M was 1.9x GAAP net income, supported by $130.7M of D&A. Capex fell for a third consecutive year to $91.0M from $105.4M and $119.9M, producing FY2025 free cash flow of $105.1M (5.7% of revenue). Depreciation now materially exceeds capex, with FY2026 capex guided to $95-105M. Two readings are consistent with the data: the heavy capacity-build phase is complete and conversion should structurally improve, or reinvestment is being throttled during a demand shortfall. The $5.9M Q2 impairment on manufacturing equipment "following revised expectations and cash flow projections regarding the future use of these assets" is circumstantial support for the second. R&D fell 22% from $63.8M (FY2023) to $49.5M (FY2025), or from roughly 4.0% to 2.7% of sales, which is consistent with either reading. A recurring non-recourse receivables sale program ($228.7M in FY2025, $231.0M in FY2024) is stable in size but is a real component of the working-capital picture.

**Leverage moved the wrong way.** At July 3, 2026: revolver $50.0M, Term Loan A $91.0M, 2028 converts $116.284M (2.125%, $87.20 conversion price), 2030 converts $1,000.0M (1.875%, $150.96 conversion price), total principal $1,257.3M against cash of $21.4M, for company-defined net total debt of $1,235.9M. Net debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA was **3.2x**, up from approximately 2.7x at December 31, 2025, driven by EBITDA compression rather than new borrowing. Operating income to interest expense was 5.1x in FY2025 ($221.3M / $43.2M); the credit-agreement coverage of 13.6x flatters, because the coupons on $1.116bn of converts are only 1.875-2.125%. Ninety-two percent of debt was fixed at year-end 2025, up from 50%.

**Q2 2026 in detail.** Sales $464.1M, -2.6% reported and -1.5% organic; C&V -2.3%, CRM&N +1.0%, Other Markets -42.8%. Gross margin 24.3% versus 27.1%. Operating income $34.5M versus $59.3M. Adjusted EBITDA $94.9M versus $99.0M (-4.1%). Adjusted diluted EPS $1.60 versus $1.55. For 1H 2026: sales $903.7M (-1.1% reported, -0.1% organic), gross margin 24.6% versus 27.3%, adjusted EBITDA $180.0M versus $190.5M. The 1H 2026 period contained 184 days against 178; six extra days flatter the reported comparison, so the underlying organic trend is modestly worse than the headline.

The gap between adjusted EPS (+3.2%) and GAAP EPS (-34%) widened materially in 2026. Q2 add-backs and unusual items included ERP implementation costs of $4.9M (a new adjustment category in 2026), the $5.9M impairment, activist costs $0.6M ($3.8M in 1H), strategic review $1.3M, securities defense $1.8M, cyber incident $0.7M, and leadership transition $0.1M. The quarter's effective tax rate was negative 1.3% including $3.7M of discrete benefits, against 18.0% for FY2025. ERP exclusion is a defensible convention many issuers follow, and discrete tax items are ordinary course, but the composition means adjusted EPS growth overstates operating momentum by a wide margin this year.

**Financial health score: 5.0.** No covenant or refinancing crisis (nearest maturity is $116.3M of 2028 converts, in the money at the deal price and puttable at par on a fundamental change), OCF nearly 2x net income, current ratio 3.32. Offsetting: 3.2x and rising leverage into compressing gross margin, $21.4M of cash against $1.26bn of principal, 57% intangibles that would impair quickly if the demand story worsened, and a Z-Score in the grey zone.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis broke in 2026, and the company said so before anyone else did. The FY2025 10-K states: "In 2026, we expect sales growth to be impacted by lower sales related to three new products due to lower than anticipated market adoption. We believe the magnitude of these changes on multiple products at the same time is highly unusual." The Q2 10-Q identifies them by category (two new electrophysiology products within C&V and one neuromodulation product within CRM&N) without naming customers or programs.

Corroborating evidence of severity: the Precision Coating earn-out was not met (2025 revenue growth milestone failed, no additional consideration paid); the VSi contingent consideration was written down to a $1.2M fair-value benefit because achieving revenue thresholds is now assessed as unlikely; inventories rose $34.3M against declining sales; and $5.9M of manufacturing equipment was impaired.

The mechanism matters for durability. Integer's revenue is levered to its OEM customers' launch curves. Electrophysiology is currently the most competitively turbulent area in cardiology, and a component supplier positioned on a losing platform loses multi-year program revenue through no fault of its own execution. That is inference: the filings name neither the customers nor the competing technologies, and a general end-market adoption slowdown across all three programs is equally consistent with the disclosure. Either way, recovery is not within Integer's control and is not obviously a 2027 event.

**Estimate revisions are technically positive and practically moot.** Current-year consensus of $6.14 rose 1.0% over 30 days and 0.3% over 90; next year $6.87, +0.9% and +0.2%. These are adjusted figures from 9-10 analysts, and they are too small to constitute a genuine signal. The company has withdrawn its 2026 outlook, so consensus is now anchored to nothing management will confirm. Forward growth accrues to KKR, not to public holders.

Offsetting positives worth naming: C&V grew 16.6% in FY2025 and remains the structurally advantaged line at 61% of Q2 sales; CRM&N returned to +1.0% growth in Q2; the coatings platforms address exactly the insourcing vulnerability that caps pricing power; and Costa Rica emerged as an 11-12% ship-to geography in 2026, reflecting customers' final-assembly relocations that Integer followed.

**Growth prospects score: 4.0**, continuing the decline from 5.8 to 4.5 across prior reviews. Organic revenue is negative, gross margin is compressing 280bp, R&D dollars are down 22% over two years, guidance is withdrawn, and the merger's interim operating covenants prohibit the tuck-in M&A that supplied roughly 3.4 points of FY2025 growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At the deal price, Integer is being taken out at approximately **14.8x trailing adjusted EBITDA** and **3.08x FY2025 revenue**. The EBITDA figure is derived: $1,235.9M net total debt divided by the disclosed 3.2x leverage implies trailing adjusted EBITDA of $386.2M, and $5,700M EV divided by that gives 14.8x. The company did not publish a trailing adjusted EBITDA figure, and the credit-agreement definition (roughly 2.9x on the same debt) differs, so this multiple should be treated as approximate to within about ±5%. On the same basis, the pre-deal March 2026 price of roughly $88 implied an EV of approximately $4.29bn and 11.1x; the deal delivered roughly 3.7 turns of multiple uplift.

The bridge from equity to enterprise value reconciles: $4.317bn equity plus $1,235.9M net total debt, approximately $51M of finance lease liabilities, $7.0M of contingent consideration and roughly $66M of RSU/PSU cash-out at $127 sums to approximately $5.68bn, within rounding of the stated $5.7bn.

**Reported multiples require a basis warning.** The profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 34.22, which reconciles to $2.89 of GAAP continuing-operations EPS at a price near $98.90, a stale price. At the $125.27 close, ITGR trades at **43.3x trailing GAAP continuing-operations EPS** and **20.4x** the $6.14 current-year adjusted consensus. The deal price of $127 is 20.7x adjusted 2026. Peer medians (P/E 34.0 across LIVN, TFX, TMDX, UFPT, LMAT, with TFX at 4,212x rendering the median unstable) are not informative for a deal-pinned security.

**The decision-critical arithmetic is the spread, not the multiple.**

**Is a bump plausible?** Three facts argue against: the strategic review was publicly announced April 30 and ran roughly thirteen weeks, so the asset was openly shopped; the July 31 Wall Street Journal leak gave rivals two additional days of public notice before signing; and the $154M company termination fee is only 3.57% of equity value, well inside the range that would deter a serious topper. **Valuation score: 3.2.** There is no identified mispricing to exploit. Maximum upside is 1.4% absent a topping bid for which no evidence exists; downside is 24-30%. The consensus median target of $127.00 is the deal price, and the low of $95.00 approximates a standalone/break estimate; analyst targets here are mechanical, not independent confirmation of anything.

## Competitive Landscape

Integer's own framing is that medical outsourcing is "highly fragmented amongst several hundred companies" with few competitors offering comparable scope. Scaled comparables include Nordson MEDICAL, Freudenberg Medical, Cirtec Medical and Viant, alongside OEMs' in-house capacity. Integer competes on breadth, quality, on-time delivery and increasingly on providing a complete supply-chain solution.

The ceiling on that position is the customer set. Abbott, Boston Scientific and Medtronic are three of the most capable manufacturers in the world, and the 10-K discloses that they have previously insourced production and pursued supplier diversification. Combined with disclosed ongoing pressure for price reductions, this explains why a business compounding revenue near 10% has never sustained gross margin above roughly 27%, and why margin fell to 24.3% the moment volume softened.

One inference to resist: the customer mix rotated within the top three (one customer moved from 14% to 18% of sales, another from 21% to 17%), but aggregate top-three concentration went from 51% to 52%. Rotation among the same three buyers is not diversification. Letter designations may not be consistent across periods, so the individual movements are indicative; the total is the reliable figure, and it edged up.

The coatings roll-up is a genuine strategic response: parylene, hydrophilic, fluoropolymer and anodic coatings are harder to insource than machined components and carry higher differentiation. But at roughly $331M of aggregate consideration across four deals it has not yet shifted consolidated gross margin, and the merger's interim covenants suspend further acquisitions until closing.

**Competitive position score: 4.5**, continuing the decline from 6.5. Trajectory is what matters: concentration flat-to-worse, gross margin down 280bp, three simultaneous new-program shortfalls, R&D dollars down 22%, and an M&A engine now contractually paused.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-break risk dominates.** The 10-Q added an entirely new "Risks Related to the Merger" section disclosing that the stock price currently reflects an assumption of consummation, that pursuit of the merger could disrupt customer, supplier and employee relationships, and that **interim operating covenants restrict Integer's ability to enter contracts, acquire or dispose of assets, enter new lines of business, incur indebtedness, and make capital expenditures**. A break in, say, Q1 2027 would return to public markets a company operationally frozen for two to three quarters precisely while its new-product demand was disappointing.

Closing conditions are: majority shareholder approval; no prohibiting order; HSR expiration plus approvals under certain other antitrust and foreign-direct-investment laws; covenant compliance in all material respects; **no Company Material Adverse Effect since August 2, 2026**; and representation accuracy subject to customary qualifiers. The FDI element is the least-analyzed risk: the jurisdictions are not named in the filing, and whether KKR holds portfolio companies creating a horizontal overlap with a medical device CDMO (an HSR timing risk) was not verified. The MAE condition sits against a business whose GAAP operating income just fell 41.8%; further acceleration would create argument space that does not exist in a stable-fundamentals deal.

Deal-certainty terms are strong on the other side: no financing condition, committed equity *and* debt, a $307M reverse fee at 1.99x the $154M company fee, and a May 2, 2027 outside date providing nine months of runway. The convertible notes' fundamental-change mechanics (holders may put at par plus accrued interest, and a make-whole may increase the conversion rate) are a real cash call inside KKR's financing plan but are contemplated within the $5.7bn EV.

**Standalone risks defining the break case:** pricing concessions against 24-27% gross margin; customer insourcing and dual-sourcing, both disclosed as having occurred; healthcare consolidation increasing buyer power; tariff exposure on steel and copper and on imports from Mexico and Ireland, where long-lived assets are $50.7M and $152.9M respectively; single-site manufacturing concentration with regulatory barriers to transfer; single-sourced platinum, gold, titanium, nitinol, lithium, palladium, iridium and tantalum; $1.9bn of goodwill and intangibles (57% of assets, $735.1M definite-lived) last tested before the Q2 deterioration; and EU MDR transition deadlines running through 2028.

**Litigation.** The December 2025 securities class action was **dismissed June 18, 2026** (materially favorable), but plaintiff appealed July 22. Q2 defense costs were $1.8M. The merger-related law-firm "investigations" announced August 3-7 are routine for essentially every announced deal and, absent a filed injunctive complaint, carry minimal closing weight; standard resolution is supplemental proxy disclosure.

**Cyber.** $0.7M of Q2 costs related to "a cyber incident" with no further detail, no materiality characterization, and no identified Item 1.05 filing. Appears contained; scope unverified.

### Options Market Signal

The options chain confirms that the market treats this as a closed question. Near-term ATM implied volatility collapsed from **71.6% to 3.6%** between the July 24 and August 8 snapshots while the price rose 26.6% to $125.27, the classic deal-pinning signature. IV sits in the 5th percentile of its own chain against 51.4% one-year realized volatility, and the twelve-day implied move is ±3.0%, wider than the $1.73 spread. Term structure is in contango (3.6% at one month rising to 6.8% at six months), which is the correct shape: near-term outcomes are pinned, and the residual uncertainty lives further out where the antitrust and FDI timeline resolves. The 5.6% one-month and 8.6% six-month put/call skew readings are reported as elevated put demand, but with zero recorded put/call volume and open-interest ratios, they carry little information; only 87 contracts exist across four expirations.

Short interest of 15.2% of float (4,352,974 shares, 8.9 days to cover) looks bearish on its face but is more plausibly mechanical. The 2028 converts are deep in the money at an $87.20 conversion price against a $127 deal, with fair value at 124% of par, and convertible arbitrage delta hedging routinely produces short stock of this magnitude. Reading 15.2% as directional conviction against a signed all-cash deal would be a mistake; equally, it removes the squeeze upside that sometimes cushions a deal break.

The analyst recommendation migration (strong buy 2→0, buy 5→1, hold 4→9) and the "degrading sentiment" flag are also mechanical post-announcement repositioning to the deal price, not fundamental judgments.

**Risk factors score: 4.0.** A binary event with a 24-30% left tail, an MAE condition against deteriorating operations, unverified antitrust overlap, and effectively no compensating upside. The prior improvement from 3.5 to 4.5 reflected the securities-suit dismissal; the deal now reintroduces a different, larger binary.

## Management Quality

The strongest evidence is capital allocation into the drawdown. Integer repurchased 698,356 shares for $50.0M in FY2025 (average approximately $71.60) and 589,605 shares via a February-April 2026 accelerated repurchase at a VWAP-less-discount of $84.80, roughly 1.29M shares (about 3.7% of the pre-buyback count) retired at an average well below the eventual $127 deal price. Repurchases were suspended once the sale process went live, which is correct sequencing, leaving $100M of the $200M authorization unused. Judged ex post this was accretive; judged ex ante it deployed $50M into stock rather than debt reduction with leverage heading from 2.7x to 3.2x and a multi-product demand shortfall about to be disclosed.

Pay-for-performance alignment functioned. Indexed TSR fell from 163.22 to 96.60 during 2025, a 40.8% single-year decline against a peer group up 6.8%, leaving five-year cumulative TSR at -3.4% versus peers +16.3%. CEO "compensation actually paid" was **negative $5,377,560** in 2025 against $8,671,332 granted, versus positive $25.2M and $23.6M in the two prior years. The equity-heavy structure delivered genuine downside participation. Management's own 2026 PRSU Monte Carlo inputs raised expected volatility to 37% from 33% and the illiquidity discount to 10.98% from 8.78%, reflecting internally recognized higher risk entering the year.

Offsetting: leadership turnover has been extensive (Payman Khales became CEO October 24, 2025; a new general counsel, quality head, C&V president and operations head all arrived in 2025-2026); the prior CEO's separation involved approximately $4.3M of equity that would otherwise have been forfeited, exchanged for a claims release and extended restrictive covenants; change-of-control agreements and cash retention letters for the CEO and other executives were papered in June-July 2026 immediately before signing (standard practice, but worth naming); and **no officer or director made an open-market purchase between February and July 2026** through a $63-$88 drawdown. The absence of buying is weak evidence given blackout constraints during an active strategic review and pending litigation. The single open-market sale was 825 shares at $85.00 by the corporate controller on March 13.

The forecasting failure that produced three simultaneous new-product shortfalls, an inventory build against falling sales, and an equipment impairment is a management outcome, not just a market outcome. **Management quality score: 5.5** — capital allocation and the negotiated exit were shareholder-constructive; operating execution and demand planning were not.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $132-135 (+5.4% to +7.8%)

A topping bid emerges before the shareholder vote. The structure permits it: a fiduciary out for a Superior Proposal, a company fee of only 3.57% of equity value, and a deal price still 12% below the $144.36 high disclosed for the January 2025-February 2026 window. Plaintiff firms are circulating price-adequacy notices, and private equity is actively bidding medtech (American Industrial Partners/Avanos at $1.27bn; Blackstone-TPG/Hologic at $18.3bn). If C&V stabilizes and the three programs recover under private ownership, the 14.8x EBITDA exit will look cheap in hindsight, but that value accrues to KKR, not to the public holder.

Probability: low. The asset was publicly shopped for thirteen weeks, the July 31 leak provided additional notice, and no rival had surfaced.

### Base Case — $127.00 (+1.38%)

The deal closes as signed. Committed equity and debt with no financing condition, a reverse fee at nearly 2x the company fee, an activist with two board seats supporting the outcome, and no obvious horizontal overlap make consummation the overwhelmingly likely path. Timing spans a five-to-nine-month range: management's reported year-end 2026 expectation is the central case, but the only filing-verified deadline is the May 2, 2027 outside date. Annualized return: 1.8% to 5.5%, centered near 3.3%, below the risk-free rate.

### Bear Case — $88-95 (-24.2% to -29.8%)

The deal breaks. Plausible mechanisms: an HSR second request or FDI review extending past the outside date; an MAE assertion if Q3-Q4 deterioration accelerates from an already-negative organic base; or credit-market disruption impairing the debt commitment, in which case the $307M reverse fee (equal to about $9.03 per share) partially cushions but does not offset. The break floor is arguably below the $88 anchor: the company would return to market having been operationally frozen under interim covenants, with a suspended M&A engine, withdrawn guidance, 3.2x leverage, compressing gross margin, and a 12-month tail on the $154M company fee complicating a quick replacement transaction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** The distinction from HOLD is arithmetic rather than judgmental. HOLD implies approximately fair risk-adjusted return from the current price; here the expected return over the guided timeline is roughly zero to slightly negative *before* accounting for the opportunity cost of cash yielding about 4%, and the payoff distribution is capped at +1.4% against a -24% to -30% tail. That is unfavorable downside asymmetry with a sub-riskless expected return, which is the definition of REDUCE.

**De-risking plan.** Sell into the market now at $125.27 rather than carry the position three to nine months for the residual $1.73. Holders with tax-lot considerations that make near-term realization costly can retain exposure, since the base case still pays $127 in cash, but new capital has no reason to enter and no scaled position should be maintained as an equity allocation. If the position must be held, it belongs in a dedicated arbitrage sleeve sized to tolerate a 30% single-name loss, not in a fundamental portfolio.

**Base/bull/bear from $125.27:** base $127.00 (+1.4%); bull $132-135 (+5.4% to +7.8%); bear $88-95 (-29.8% to -24.2%). Timeframe: shareholder vote and regulatory clearance expected within five months per company statements, with a hard backstop at the May 2, 2027 outside date.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. The spread widens to more than 4-5% annualized on a shortened timeline — for example, price below approximately $121-122 *after* HSR clearance and expiration of the FDI review periods, which would pay adequately for residual vote risk.
2. A credible competing proposal is publicly disclosed or the merger proxy's "Background of the Merger" reveals a competitive process that materially raises the probability of a bump.
3. HSR clearance plus all named foreign approvals obtained with the shareholder vote scheduled, compressing the timeline to under 60 days at a spread above 1.0% (annualizing above 6%).

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A second request under HSR, or any FDI review disclosed as extended, pushing expected close beyond Q1 2027 while the spread remains inside 2%.
2. Any Company MAE assertion, or Q3 2026 results showing organic decline worse than -3% with gross margin below 24%, which would create MAE argument space against an already-impaired base.
3. Disclosure of a KKR portfolio overlap with medical device contract manufacturing that creates a substantive antitrust theory.

**Reassessment timeline.** The preliminary merger proxy (Form DEFM14A) is the next substantive disclosure; it will contain the process background, the fairness opinion and financial projections, and the named regulatory jurisdictions. Secondary checkpoints: HSR waiting-period expiration, the shareholder vote, and Q3 2026 results if reported before closing.

## Conclusion

Integer's equity has been converted into a short-dated cash claim with a large left tail. The business KKR is buying — a scaled medtech CDMO with 52% top-three customer concentration, 24.3% gross margin, negative organic growth, 3.2x leverage and three simultaneously disappointing new-product programs — justifies the multiple uplift from 11.1x to 14.8x EBITDA only from a sponsor's vantage point, with control, a five-to-seven-year horizon and the ability to reset the cost base. Public holders receive $127.00 in cash, 1.38% above the current price, on a timeline that annualizes to roughly 3.3% at management's reported year-end expectation.

At that spread the market is pricing a 94.6-95.6% probability of consummation depending on the assumed break price. The deal terms (no financing condition, committed equity and debt, a reverse fee at 1.99x the company fee, an activist-influenced board supporting the outcome) support a high probability. They do not support a *higher* probability than the market has already assigned, and the remaining conditions include HSR plus unnamed foreign antitrust and direct-investment approvals across at least six manufacturing jurisdictions, together with an MAE condition sitting on top of a business whose GAAP operating income just fell 41.8%.

Expected return net of the risk-free alternative is negative. **REDUCE.** Sell into the market, redeploy into Treasury bills at comparable or better yield without the break tail, and revisit only if the spread widens materially after regulatory clearance or a competing proposal surfaces.
