## Executive Summary

COPT Defense Properties is a specialized REIT positioned at the intersection of two structural growth drivers: U.S. defense spending acceleration and hyperscale data center demand. CDP's tenant base requires on-site presence for classified work, creating a demand dynamic that has produced 95.5% Defense/IT occupancy and mid-single-digit annual FFO/share growth through a period where peer total shareholder returns averaged 81 vs. CDP's 132.

As of early 2026, CDP trades at approximately 11.3x forward FFO ($2.76 midpoint guidance) with a dividend yield of approximately 4.1% covered at under 50% of FFO. This represents a material discount to the growth characteristics of the business: FFO has compounded from $2.29 (2021) to $2.72 (2025), a ~4.4% CAGR with recent years near 6%, and visibility to $2.75-$2.77 in 2026 is supported by same-property NOI growth of 3.6-4.1%, data center shell development at 8.5% yields, and a development pipeline that as of Q1 2026 totals approximately $508M and is 73% pre-leased. With the FY2026 National Defense Budget request at approximately $962B for DoD (+13.4% over FY2025) and emerging missile defense initiatives potentially catalyzing new demand at Redstone Arsenal and Fort Meade, CDP's growth trajectory has improving visibility that the current multiple does not reflect.

The primary investment risks include 2026 lease expirations — which were 19.3% of ARR as of year-end 2025 but have been materially reduced to approximately 9.8% of total ARR following strong Q1 2026 renewal activity, including a 953,000 sq. ft. full renewal of CDP's U.S. Government campus near Lackland AFB — and dependence on a single Fortune 100 data center customer (11.3% of ARR). Both are manageable: CDP has maintained 75%+ defense tenant retention for 10 consecutive years, and management committed $201M to new investments in Q1 2026, raising full-year investment-activity guidance to a $290M midpoint.

## Company Overview

COPT Defense Properties operates 207 total properties encompassing 25.1 million square feet, including a core Defense/IT Portfolio of 201 properties totaling 23.2 million square feet, concentrated around key U.S. Government defense installations including Fort Meade/NSA, Northern Virginia defense corridors, Lackland Air Force Base, and Redstone Arsenal. The company's business model centers on owning and developing properties where physical proximity to defense missions creates non-discretionary demand for space — tenants cannot simply relocate when their work requires daily access to classified facilities.

The portfolio breaks into two segments: the core Defense/IT Portfolio (95.5% occupied, serving USG agencies and contractors) and a small "Other" segment (6 properties, 2.0M sq ft, 76.6% occupied) targeted for eventual sale. A growing data center shell business (8.4% of ARR, 100% occupied) develops triple-net shell buildings for hyperscale cloud operators in Northern Virginia — one of the world's densest data center markets.

CDP controls approximately 990 acres of Defense/IT land capable of supporting approximately 10.4 million square feet of future development, providing a multi-year growth runway without external land acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & NOI:** Real estate revenues grew 6.5% to $721.8M in 2025, with same-property cash NOI up 4.1%. Net income increased 10.8% to $159.5M. The headline total revenue growth of only 1.4% masks the real estate strength — construction contract revenue (a lower-margin pass-through business) declined, distorting the top line.

**FFO Growth:** Diluted FFO per share of $2.72 in 2025 represents the fifth consecutive year of growth: $2.29 → $2.36 → $2.42 → $2.57 → $2.72. This ~4.4% CAGR, with recent years near 6%, compares favorably to peers trading at significantly higher multiples for similar growth. Management raised 2026 guidance to a $2.76 midpoint after Q1 results.

**Balance Sheet:** Investment-grade rated by all three agencies with stable/positive outlooks (Moody's upgraded to Baa2 Stable post-Q1 2026). Weighted average interest rate of 3.4% as of year-end 2025, though new issuances (4.50% October 2025 notes) are at higher rates, creating a $0.09/share headwind in 2026. No significant maturities until 2028 after the March 2026 payoff. As of year-end 2025, the company carried no variable-rate debt exposure (including swaps); however, $200M in swaps expired in February 2026, and as of Q1 2026 approximately 85% of debt was subject to fixed rates with the weighted average effective rate rising to 3.8%.

**Liquidity:** $275M cash (as of 12/31/25) plus $746M revolver capacity plus $104M development facility provides ample runway for the $135-175M development spend planned in 2026.

**Dividend Coverage:** The 2025 dividend was $1.22/share, with the Q1 2026 quarterly dividend increased to $0.32 (annualized $1.28). The 2025 FFO payout ratio of 44.6% provides exceptional coverage, with the forward annualized payout on guidance of approximately 46% — still leaving significant retained cash for investment.

**Development Returns:** As of Q1 2026, CDP's active development pipeline consists of seven properties totaling 1.033M sq. ft. with an estimated total investment of approximately $508M, 73% pre-leased, targeting 8.5% cash yields on cost. This spread over the cost of capital (~4.5-5.0%) creates genuine value through development rather than acquisition.

## Growth Analysis

CDP's growth derives from three distinct engines operating simultaneously:

**1. Same-Property Growth (2.5-4.1% annually):** Driven by contractual rent escalators (typically 2.5-3.0% annually) and occupancy gains in the non-strategic "Other" segment. The Defense/IT segment maintains 95.5% occupancy with limited upside from further gains but stable contribution from escalators and re-leasing spreads.

**2. Data Center Shell Development:** This sub-segment has grown from 5.8% to 8.4% of ARR in two years with 100% occupancy and long-term triple-net leases. CDP's 33 data center shells (6.3M sq. ft.) in Northern Virginia, developed for a Fortune 100 cloud-computing customer, provide multi-year visibility. Development yields of 8.5% on pre-leased shells represent the highest-margin growth channel.

**3. Defense Mission-Driven Demand:** The FY2026 National Defense Budget request of approximately $962B for DoD (+13.4% over FY2025), along with the FY2026 NDAA signed into law in December 2025, directly supports demand for space near installations. Emerging programs — including missile defense initiatives such as the approximately $24.4B in integrated air and missile defense funding in the 2025 reconciliation law, Redstone Arsenal expansion, and cyber/intelligence facility upgrades — represent potential tailwinds that could translate into measurable leasing demand. CDP committed $201M to two new investments in Q1 2026, and management increased the midpoint of full-year 2026 investment-activity guidance to $290M.

**Forward Visibility:** The combination of 73% pre-leased development (as of Q1 2026), contractual escalators, and defense budget commitments provides above-average earnings visibility for a real estate company. FFO growth of 3-5% annually is the relevant metric given REIT accounting dynamics.

## Valuation Assessment

As of early 2026, CDP trades at approximately:
- **11.3x forward FFO** ($2.76 midpoint guidance)
- **~4.1% dividend yield** (annualized $1.28) with under 50% payout ratio
- **Modest discount to 52-week highs**

**Peer Comparison:** The peer benchmark data shows median office REIT P/E of 104.4x and median yield of 6.3%. These comparisons are misleading because CDP's peers (KRC, SLG, HIW, DEI) face secular remote work headwinds, declining occupancy, and negative leasing spreads. CDP's consistent FFO growth, 95.5% Defense/IT occupancy, and defense budget tailwinds justify a premium to distressed office peers.

A more appropriate comp set would be net-lease REITs with similar growth profiles (VICI, NNN) trading at 14-16x FFO, or specialized REITs with government tenants. At 14x FFO on $2.76 guidance, CDP would trade at $38.64 — aligning with the high end of analyst targets.

**Analyst Targets:** Based on available market data, consensus points to approximately $35-36 with a range of $33-$38, reflecting 12-14x FFO — still below where the growth profile warrants.

**Dividend Support:** The approximately 4.1% yield provides meaningful downside support. If the stock declined to match peer yields of 6.3%, that would imply ~$20 — but this scenario requires assuming CDP faces the same occupancy/demand headwinds as traditional office, which its Defense/IT portfolio demonstrably does not.

## Competitive Landscape

CDP's competitive position is strengthening through three mechanisms:

**Security-Mandated Switching Costs:** SCIF build-outs cost millions and require lengthy government approval processes for relocation. Once established, tenants face 12-24 month timelines and significant capital expenditure to move. This explains the 79.3% Defense/IT retention rate maintained for 10+ consecutive years above 75%.

**Supply Constraints:** CDP is typically the dominant or sole commercial landlord in its business parks adjacent to defense installations. New construction requires proximity to the same installations and equivalent security infrastructure — barriers that limit competitive entry.

**Dual Growth Channels:** The data center shell business diversifies beyond pure defense office while leveraging the same Northern Virginia land bank. This provides a growth engine less dependent on federal budget cycles.

**Versus Traditional Office REITs:** Classified/cleared defense work cannot be performed remotely, eliminating the primary structural headwind facing peers. SLG trades at negative P/E, KRC at 27% below its 52-week high, and DEI at 35% below its high. CDP's relative outperformance (132 TSR vs. 81 peer average) quantifies this differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**1. 2026 Lease Expiration Concentration (MEDIUM, DECLINING):** As of year-end 2025, 19.3% of ARR ($140.3M) was scheduled to expire in 2026, with the majority being USG leases whose renewals were delayed by the 2025 government shutdown. Q1 2026 materially de-risked this concern: CDP completed a 953,000 sq. ft. full renewal of its USG campus near Lackland AFB, and remaining 2026 Defense/IT expirations declined to $70.6M (10.6% of Defense/IT ARR), with total remaining 2026 expirations of approximately $72.5M (~9.8% of total ARR). Management expects to renew "virtually all" remaining USG leases, and 10+ years of 75%+ retention history supports this expectation.

**Falsifiable test:** If Q2 2026 results show Defense/IT occupancy declining below 93% (from 95.5%), or if management acknowledges delays in USG renewal processing, the thesis requires reassessment.

**2. Data Center Customer Concentration (MEDIUM):** A single customer at 11.3% of ARR and growing creates binary risk if that customer shifts development allocation. However: (a) all existing leases are long-term with escalators, (b) Northern Virginia remains supply-constrained for data center power/land, (c) CDP's pre-development model keeps capex manageable, and (d) the customer has taken 33 shells over multiple years demonstrating sustained demand.

**3. DOGE/Defense Budget Uncertainty (LOW-MEDIUM):** The Zacks upgrade to #2 (Buy) in March 2026 and the FY2026 defense budget at ~$962B suggest this risk has been overpriced since JP Morgan's March 2025 downgrade to $29 target. CDP's tenants serve core missions (cybersecurity, missile defense, intelligence) that are bipartisan priorities. The risk is more applicable to non-mission-critical contractors than to CDP's tenant base.

**4. Rate Environment (LOW-MEDIUM):** With development yields at 8.5% vs. ~4.5-5.0% cost of capital, CDP's development economics remain attractive. Refinancing risk is limited given no significant maturities until 2028. The $0.09/share FFO headwind from higher financing costs in 2026 is already in guidance. The expiration of $200M in interest rate swaps in February 2026 introduced some variable-rate exposure; as of Q1 2026, 85% of debt is at fixed rates.

**5. Short Interest:** Short interest is elevated but not extreme, likely reflecting broader office REIT sector shorts rather than CDP-specific bearish positioning. If fundamental execution continues (2026 lease renewals proceeding), short covering could provide incremental upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36-38, +15-22%):** USG lease renewals continue proceeding on schedule through 2026, Defense/IT occupancy maintains 95%+, data center shell development continues at pace, and defense budget tailwinds accelerate leasing demand. The stock re-rates from 11.3x to 13-14x FFO as investors recognize CDP's differentiation from traditional office. FFO grows to $2.90+ in 2027.

**Bear Case ($27-28, -10-13%):** Remaining 2026 USG lease renewals are delayed further or retention drops below 75%, DOGE initiatives reduce contractor space needs, the data center customer slows development pace, and rising rates compress REIT valuations broadly. FFO growth stalls at $2.72-2.76 through 2027.

**Base Case ($34-35, +9-12%):** Lease renewals continue proceeding (2026 wall largely resolved after Q1), occupancy holds 94-95%, FFO achieves $2.76 guidance, and the multiple modestly expands to 12.5x as execution de-risks remaining concerns.

## Conclusion

COPT Defense Properties offers a differentiated growth profile within a structurally challenged sector, trading at a valuation (11.3x FFO) that inadequately reflects its competitive advantages. The security-mandated nature of its tenant demand, consistent mid-single-digit FFO growth, development pipeline at 73% pre-leased (as of Q1 2026), and defense budget tailwinds create a different risk/reward profile than traditional office peers. With analyst consensus pointing to approximately $35-36 (double-digit upside), a well-covered ~4.1% dividend yield providing income support, and specific catalysts in continued USG lease renewals and data center development through 2026, CDP offers asymmetric upside at current prices. The 2026 lease expiration concentration — the primary near-term risk at year-end 2025 — was materially de-risked in Q1 2026, and 10+ years of 75%+ retention history supports a favorable resolution of the remainder.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The defense-budget/Golden Dome catalyst claims are inferred macro tailwinds rather than confirmed company-cited demand drivers from Q1 2026 SEC-filed materials; if these tailwinds do not materialize in CDP-specific leasing, the bull case upside may be more limited.
