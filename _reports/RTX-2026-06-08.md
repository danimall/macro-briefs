**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX is the largest pure-play aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B FY2025), operating across three segments (Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, and Raytheon) with a record $268B backlog providing roughly three years of revenue visibility. The investment story is a margin recovery: consolidated operating margin doubled from 5.2% in 2023 (depressed by the $2.9B GTF Powder Metal charge) to 10.5% in 2025, all three segments are expanding margins, and the highest-margin revenue stream, commercial aftermarket, grew 21% YoY in early 2025. The company has beaten consensus EPS for four straight quarters, including a 16.1% beat in Q1 2026.

The problem is price. At $178-181 the stock trades at roughly 26x current-year adjusted EPS (~$6.91 consensus) and ~24x next-year (~$7.58). That is below the headline 34x trailing GAAP P/E, which is suppressed by ~$1.15/share of recurring merger amortization, but it still embeds the margin-expansion thesis substantially. Estimate revisions have stalled: current-quarter and next-quarter consensus moved essentially zero over 30 days, and next-quarter is down 2.7% over 90 days, which means the rapid-revision tailwind that would justify chasing the stock is absent. The analyst tally is degrading: sell/strong-sell ratings appeared in the trailing record where there were none, and the stock has pulled back ~16% from its $214.50 high while the sector trades broadly higher.

Two RTX-specific overhangs that an automated screen would understate keep this a hold rather than a buy. First, a January 2026 executive order grants the Secretary of War authority to restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive incentive compensation at defense contractors judged to be underperforming on U.S. government contracts, a novel threat to the capital-return thesis that RTX itself flags as potentially adverse to the share price. Second, the company is operating under two active Deferred Prosecution Agreements, an SEC order, and an ITAR consent agreement through approximately 2027, where a compliance breach risks suspension or debarment from federal contracting. Neither is a near-term catalyst, but both cap the asymmetry that a BUY at this multiple would require.

## Company Overview

RTX was formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies' aerospace businesses with Raytheon Company. It employs roughly 185,000 people and is headquartered in Arlington, Virginia. The three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** (FY2025 sales $30.2B, 16.3% operating margin): avionics, interiors, mechanical and electrical systems for commercial and defense aircraft. Highest-margin segment.
- **Pratt & Whitney** (FY2025 sales $32.9B, +17% YoY, 7.9% operating margin): commercial and military aircraft engines, including the GTF/PW1100 for the A320neo and the F135 for the F-35. Largest segment, lowest margin, recovering from the GTF crisis.
- **Raytheon** (FY2025 sales $28.0B, 11.5% operating margin): missiles, integrated air and missile defense (Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS, AMRAAM, SM-3, SPY-6 radar), and sensors. Roughly 75% of revenue is sensors and effectors.

The competitive moat rests on entrenched sole- and dual-source positions on installed platforms (engines, avionics, and missile systems) that generate decades of high-margin aftermarket and reorder revenue. The blend of long-cycle defense backlog and shorter-cycle commercial aftermarket gives the business unusually high revenue visibility. The $268B backlog was up from $218B at the prior year-end, split $161B commercial and $107B defense.

2025 revenue grew 9.7% with $8.9B of organic growth led by Pratt & Whitney (+$4.8B), Collins (+$2.6B), and Raytheon (+$1.7B). Adjusted EPS rose ~10% to $6.29 and free cash flow jumped $3.4B to $7.94B. The 2023 trough reflected the GTF Powder Metal charge; 2024 carried roughly $1.5B of legal and contract charges from the DOJ/SEC resolution and a Raytheon contract termination. 2025 benefited from the absence of those items plus operating leverage as commercial aftermarket accelerated.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Cash of $7.4B against total debt of $37.9B, down from $41.3B after ~$3.4B of repayment in 2025. Debt-to-total-capital improved to 36% from 40%. Interest expense was $1.75B at a ~4.5% average rate. Operating cash flow conversion is healthy at $10.6B against $6.7B GAAP net income.

Goodwill and intangibles of ~$85B represent roughly half of total assets, a legacy of the merger that produces ~$2.0B/year of acquisition-amortization (~$1.15/share). This is why the trailing GAAP P/E of ~34x materially overstates the operating multiple; on adjusted EPS the figure is closer to 26x current-year. The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 ("distress zone") is a measurement artifact driven by the goodwill-heavy asset base, not genuine financial stress. RTX is investment-grade (Moody's Baa1/stable; S&P BBB+ with a positive outlook), with a $5B undrawn revolver and 56 consecutive years of dividend payments. The Z-Score should be disregarded for a scaled defense prime with this cash generation and backlog.

Capital allocation has narrowed to dividend-led. The quarterly dividend is $0.68 (~1.6% yield). Buybacks have effectively stopped: only $50M in 2025 versus $12.9B in 2023, when RTX executed a $10B accelerated share repurchase at an average $94.28 (incurring a $261M cash penalty on tranche 2 as the stock ran). Only ~$0.6B remains on the 2023 authorization. A new buyback authorization at today's price would be additive, but is now also subject to the executive-order constraint discussed below.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs, all reasonably well-supported:

1. **Commercial aftermarket ramp.** Aftermarket grew 21% YoY in early 2025. Record air traffic, constrained OEM production at Airbus and Boeing, and an aging in-service fleet support spares and maintenance demand, RTX's highest-margin revenue. As GTF-affected aircraft return to service through 2026-2027, the GTF aftermarket should ramp.

2. **Defense budget tailwind.** The July 2025 reconciliation act added $156.2B to defense, including $24.4B for the "Golden Dome" missile-defense shield and $25.4B for munitions and supply-chain resiliency, both squarely in Raytheon's strengths (Patriot, LTAMDS, SM-3, sensors). Golden Dome funding grew a further $3.2B in May 2026. 2025 defense bookings were strong (Raytheon $40B, Pratt $9B including $2.9B F135 production, Collins $12B), and 2026 contract wins include a $1.02B NASAMS FMS for Kuwait and a $515M SPY-6 Navy radar award.

3. **Margin expansion.** Jefferies (Buy, $220 PT, June 2026) projects consolidated operating margin rising from 11.5% in 2025 to 14-15% by 2028, with EPS reaching ~$10.45 by 2028 versus ~$8.40 consensus, driven by the GTF aftermarket ramp, Pratt OE pricing, and Collins operational gains.

The 2026 guidance of 5-6% organic sales growth and adjusted EPS of roughly $6.40-6.55 is solid but not explosive. Current-year estimates are up 1.5% over 90 days and next-year up 0.9%, positive but modest. This is a mid-single-digit grower with a credible path to low-double-digit EPS growth as GTF clears, not a stock showing the estimate-revision velocity that would force a re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$180, RTX trades at ~26x current-year adjusted EPS ($6.91) and ~24x next-year ($7.58). Peer P/Es in aerospace and defense are heterogeneous: GD 21.8x, LMT 25.4x, GE Aerospace 40.7x, HWM 58.5x, Boeing 86.3x (the latter two distorted by depressed or recovering earnings). On a pure-defense basis RTX sits between GD/LMT and the commercial-exposed names, which is defensible given its commercial aftermarket leverage: that exposure deserves a premium to pure defense primes but a discount to the highest-multiple aero names.

The question is whether the multiple offers asymmetric upside. The bull math is straightforward: if Jefferies' ~$10.45 2028 EPS proves out, even a conservative 20x exit produces ~$210 and a 23-24x exit produces $240+, matching the high end of the analyst range. But that requires three years of flawless margin execution against a multiple that already embeds the recovery. The bear math is equally clean: at 24x next-year EPS the stock is priced for continued beats, and any GTF residual charge, a fixed-price overrun, or an executive-order capital-return restriction compresses the multiple toward LMT/GD's 22-25x on flat-to-lower estimates, implying $150-165.

Analyst targets (low $180, mean $216, median $220, high $242) sit above the current price, but the sentiment trend is degrading. The guidance against treating prior appreciation as a valuation input applies, but so does the guidance that estimate revisions, not price, drive the re-rating signal, and revisions have stalled. At a premium-to-pure-defense multiple with flat near-term revisions and a degrading tally, the valuation is full rather than mispriced. This warrants a 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX competes with Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, GE Aerospace, Boeing, Airbus, Safran, BAE, and L3Harris across its segments. Its differentiation is breadth: it touches engines, avionics, and missiles simultaneously, with deep aftermarket annuities on each. Industry dynamics are favorable on both sides of the business: commercial aerospace is in a multi-year recovery driving record aftermarket demand against constrained OEM production, and global defense budgets are rising amid the Middle East, Ukraine, and Indo-Pacific tensions, with the U.S. reconciliation act prioritizing missile defense and munitions.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: segment margins are expanding across all three businesses, RTX has pruned non-core assets (Collins actuation, Simmonds Precision, Raytheon CIS, Goodrich Hoist & Winch) to sharpen focus, and ~75% of Raytheon sits in sensors/effectors, a market growing ~8% annually. The principal competitive risk is execution on the GTF fleet recovery and on fixed-price development programs, where RTX has a recent record of charges. On balance, the franchise is widening its moat in defense and recovering position in commercial engines, supporting a 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

The RTX-specific risks materially outweigh generic sector risk, which is already priced:

1. **GTF / PW1100 Powder Metal.** The defining operational overhang. A powder-metal defect forced accelerated inspections and removals of the A320neo PW1100 fleet, triggering a charge of RTX's 51% share. Aircraft-on-ground levels remain elevated through 2026. The customer-compensation accrual fell from $1.7B to $0.7B, with ~$0.7B of cash outflow expected in 2026. Residual risk: other engine models contain affected powder metal, and material findings could increase charges.

2. **January 2026 Executive Order on distributions.** The presidential EO allows the Secretary of War to restrict RTX's dividends, buybacks, and executive incentive compensation during periods of judged contract underperformance. RTX explicitly warns this "could adversely affect the market price of our common stock." This directly threatens the dividend-and-buyback component of the thesis.

3. **DOJ/SEC compliance overhang.** RTX entered two Deferred Prosecution Agreements, an SEC administrative order, an FCA settlement, and an ITAR consent agreement in October 2024, resolving FCPA bribery and defective-pricing charges on legacy Raytheon contracts. A three-year monitorship and external ITAR audit run through ~2027. Any breach risks criminal prosecution and potential suspension or debarment from federal contracting.

4. **Fixed-price/development contract risk.** EAC adjustments were a net -$386M drag in 2025 (improving from -$648M in 2023), but the structural exposure to overruns on FFP/FPI and development contracts persists.

5. **China sanctions and tariffs.** China has sanctioned the Raytheon business over Taiwan arms sales; new U.S. tariffs since February 2025 pressure the supply chain and fixed-price margins.

6. **Single-source materials.** Dependence on foreign sources for cobalt, titanium, rare earths, rhenium, nickel, and microelectronics, with explicit China/Russia exposure.

The combination of a live capital-return restriction risk and an active criminal-deferral overhang, neither fully priced, supports a Risk Factors score of 5.0.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($220-242):** GTF aircraft-on-ground levels normalize through 2026, freeing the high-margin GTF aftermarket to ramp in 2027+. Consolidated operating margin expands toward 14-15% by 2028 (Jefferies), driving EPS to ~$10+ versus ~$8.40 consensus. Golden Dome and the reconciliation defense add convert into multi-year Raytheon awards. A new buyback authorization at sub-$200 prices augments capital return. At ~$10 EPS and 22-24x, the stock reaches $220-242, matching the analyst high.

**Bear Case ($150-165):** A residual GTF or fixed-price charge resets EPS, or the January 2026 executive order restricts dividends and buybacks, removing the capital-return support. The multiple compresses toward pure-defense peers (GD 21.8x, LMT 25.4x) on flat or lower estimates, implying $150-165, roughly 8-17% below the current price.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit organic growth, gradual margin recovery, and a multiple that holds near current levels as the stalled revision trend prevents a near-term re-rating. That produces a stock that grinds with earnings, with the GTF clearing as the genuine 2027+ catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**: the 12-month thesis is constructive (GTF clearing into an aftermarket ramp, record backlog, defense budget tailwind) but the valuation is full and the near-term catalysts are not 60-day events, so the risk/reward does not support a BUY at ~$180.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>5% on current-year) alongside a Q2/Q3 2026 print confirming GTF AOG levels declining ahead of plan.
- A new, sizeable buyback authorization announced AND confirmation that the January 2026 executive order will not restrict RTX's capital return.
- A pullback toward $150-160 (~20x next-year EPS), which would restore the asymmetry the current multiple lacks.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new GTF Powder Metal charge on additional engine models, or a fixed-price development overrun that resets EPS guidance lower.
- A DPA/ITAR breach or monitorship finding that raises debarment risk.
- The executive order being invoked to restrict RTX's dividend or buyback.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the company has reported through Q1 2026; the next print is the natural checkpoint), and any development on the executive order or the compliance monitorship expected to conclude ~2027.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality franchise in a genuine margin recovery: operating margin doubled over two years, all three segments are expanding, the commercial aftermarket is accelerating, and the backlog provides exceptional visibility. The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters. These are the attributes of a stock worth owning through the cycle.

What prevents a BUY is the combination of a full valuation (~24-26x adjusted EPS, a premium to pure-defense peers), stalled estimate revisions, a degrading analyst tally, and two novel, unpriced RTX-specific risks: the January 2026 executive order that could legally restrict the dividend and buyback that anchor the capital-return case, and the active DOJ/SEC DPAs and ITAR consent agreement through ~2027 where a breach risks debarment. The 16% pullback from the high has not created a mispricing: fundamentals are roughly unchanged, the multiple still embeds the recovery, and the catalysts that would force a re-rating (GTF aftermarket ramp, margin expansion to 14-15%) are 2027+ events, not 60-day ones. The score sits at 5.95, below the threshold that would warrant a high-conviction long, and the call is **HOLD** with a constructive 12-month bias. The cleaner entry is on a pullback toward $150-160 or on confirmation that the executive order will not constrain capital return.
