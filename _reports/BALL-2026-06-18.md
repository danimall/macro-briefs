**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ball Corporation is the world's largest producer of aluminum beverage cans, holding the #1 share in all three of its core regions (North & Central America ~36%, EMEA ~39%, South America ~46%). Following the February 2024 sale of its aerospace business to BAE Systems for ~$5.6B, Ball is a pure-play aluminum packaging company operating in a consolidated oligopoly with high switching costs and ~80% of volume under aluminum cost pass-through provisions. The investment debate is narrow and specific: whether the secular "substrate shift" toward aluminum (away from PET plastic and glass) accelerates enough to drive organic volume growth above the current ~1% pace, and whether a near-complete C-suite reset can close a persistent shareholder-return gap.

At $57.72, the stock trades at 12.8x forward earnings (consensus FY26 EPS $4.00) against a consensus target range of $60-77 (mean $70.79). That gap, combined with positive estimate revisions (current-year +0.9% over 30 days), a favorably resolved U.S. Customs tariff matter, and 2026 FCF guidance above $900M supporting ~$800M of shareholder returns, supports holding the stock with a 12-month re-rating thesis. The Q1 2026 print reinforced the tension: adjusted EPS of $0.94 beat the $0.84 consensus and comparable diluted EPS rose 22%, but gross margin compressed to 17.9% from 19.5% and global volumes grew only ~1%. The stock fell roughly 10.5% in the month after that beat, signaling the market is focused on margin pressure and modest volume rather than the headline EPS.

The constraints on conviction are real. The buyback-driven EPS tailwind is decelerating sharply ($1.71B in 2024 → $1.32B in 2025 → ~$600M guided for 2026) as cash pivots toward bolt-on M&A, which weakens the algebra behind the company's stated ">10% EPS growth per annum" target. Margin compression is running ahead of pass-through, and the five-year TSR of 60 versus a peer group at 100 underscores the execution gap the new team must close. The setup is constructive enough to hold for the income and the re-rating optionality, but the fading buyback support and lack of an earnings inflection of the magnitude that would justify a BUY keep this a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1880 and headquartered in Westminster, Colorado, Ball operates roughly 16,000 employees across three reportable beverage-packaging segments plus a small Personal & Home Care unit:

- **Beverage Packaging, North & Central America** — ~48% of 2025 sales; ~50B cans shipped; #1 of 5 producers; 17 US plants plus Canada/Mexico.
- **Beverage Packaging, EMEA** — ~30% of sales; ~38B cans; #1 of 4.
- **Beverage Packaging, South America** — ~16% of sales; ~20B cans; #1 of 4. Highest-margin segment at ~15%.
- **Other / Personal & Home Care** — extruded aerosol containers, reclosable bottles, aluminum slugs; <5% of sales.

The business model is capital-intensive with continuous-run plants, long-term multi-year supply contracts with multinational beverage customers (Coca-Cola, AB InBev and similar), and aluminum pass-through provisions covering ~80% of volume. This structure insulates net earnings from aluminum price swings over time, but both sales and COGS move with LME aluminum prices, which distorts the reported revenue trend. The strategic thesis is the substrate shift to aluminum on recyclability and sustainability grounds (cans run ~74% recycled content; aluminum is infinitely recyclable), reinforced by deposit-return schemes, plastic restrictions, and PFAS bans across multiple jurisdictions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** On a continuing-operations basis, revenue moved from $12.06B (2023) to $11.80B (2024) to $13.16B (2025, +11.5%). The 2024 dip reflects the aerospace business moving to discontinued operations, not organic weakness. The 2025 increase decomposes into roughly +$713M volume, +$579M price/mix (largely aluminum pass-through), and +$177M FX. A large share of the "growth" is aluminum-price pass-through that flows proportionally into both sales and COGS.

**Profitability.** 2025 net earnings from continuing operations were $912M, a 7% net margin (2024's reported $4.0B was inflated by the aerospace disposal gain). Segment comparable operating margins are healthy and stable: NCA ~12%, EMEA ~12%, South America ~15%. The emerging concern is consolidated margin compression: COGS rose to 80% of sales in 2025 from 79% in 2024, and reached 82% in Q1 2026 versus 80% a year earlier. Costs are rising faster than they can be passed through, reflecting both timing lags and non-pass-through inflation. Packaging-sector peers reported similar Q1 margin pressure, suggesting part of this is industry-wide cost inflation rather than purely company-specific.

**Cash flow.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.26B, recovering from an anomalous $115M in 2024 (depressed by an $830M cash tax bill on the aerospace sale, of which $766M was paid in 2024). FCF was ~$788M in 2025. Management guides 2026 capex ~$600M and shareholder returns split roughly $600M buybacks + ~$210M dividends. The dividend (1.37% yield) is well covered by FCF. Q1 is seasonally cash-negative on working-capital build (operating cash flow of -$777M in Q1 2026), so the annual figure is the relevant benchmark.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was $7.01B at year-end 2025, rising to $7.86B at Q1 2026 on the Benepack acquisition (assumed debt) and a revolver draw. Debt/equity is ~2.6x, which is high on a book basis largely because aggressive buybacks have shrunk equity ($7.3B of treasury stock). The most restrictive covenant (net leverage ≤5.0x, tightening to 4.5x as of Q1 2026) is comfortably met, and there is no near-term maturity wall — facilities run to November 2030. 2025 was an active refinancing year (issued €850M 4.25% '32 and $750M 5.50% '33 notes; redeemed higher-coupon 2026/2028 notes), which terms out the debt profile.

A note on the Altman Z-Score of 1.33 ("distress zone"): this is a false distress signal. The score penalizes Ball's deliberately leveraged, buyback-driven structure (low book equity) rather than any genuine fragility. The company generates ~$900M+ FCF, is covenant-compliant, retains investment-grade market access, and faces no liquidity issue. The standardized screen misreads financial engineering as financial weakness. That said, the leverage is real and does limit flexibility, and roughly half the share-count reduction story is now slowing.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algebra has three components, and two of them are weakening.

**Organic volume.** This is the heart of the bull case and the area of greatest uncertainty. Global volumes grew ~1% in Q1 2026. The substrate-shift thesis — cans gaining share from plastic and glass on sustainability and regulation — is real and durable, but it is playing out gradually after the 2022-2023 overcapacity and destocking cycle. Low-single-digit volume growth is the realistic base case; an acceleration to mid-single digits would meaningfully change the earnings trajectory but is not yet visible in the data.

**Price/mix and pass-through.** This inflates reported revenue without adding proportional earnings, since aluminum cost moves through both sides of the P&L. It is not a true growth driver and can reverse if aluminum prices normalize.

**Buyback-driven EPS.** Share count fell from ~314M in 2023 to ~266M by May 2026 (~15% reduction), a major historical contributor to EPS growth. This tailwind is fading as cash redirects to bolt-on M&A (Florida Can Manufacturing $160M, Alucan Entec, Benepack 80% stake for $94M). The deceleration directly pressures the company's stated ">10% EPS growth" goal, which historically leaned heavily on share reduction.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not signaling an earnings inflection: current-year EPS $4.00 (+0.9% over 30 days, +0.7% over 90 days), next-year $4.52 (+0.6% over 30 days, flat over 90 days). The current-quarter estimate has drifted down 2.0% over 30 days to $0.98. This is a stable-to-slightly-improving picture, not the >30% revision velocity that would mark a trough-to-recovery inflection. The earnings beat track record is mixed (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss over the last four quarters; the most recent beat was 8.8%), supporting moderate confidence in consensus rather than a case for systematic upside.

## Valuation Assessment

At $57.72, Ball trades at 12.8x forward earnings (FY26 EPS $4.00) and 16.8x trailing. Against the consensus target range of $60 (low) to $77 (high), with a mean of $70.79 and median of $71.50, the implied upside to the mean is ~23%. Even the low target of $60 sits above the current price. This is the core of the constructive case: a stable, market-leading business in a consolidated oligopoly trading below where the sell-side anchors fair value.

The valuation discount is partly earned, and "cheap" should not be read as automatically attractive. The forward multiple compression reflects three genuine concerns the market is pricing: margin compression (gross margin down ~160bps), the fading buyback tailwind that weakens the EPS-growth narrative, and a five-year TSR that has lagged peers by 40 points. A low multiple on a business with decelerating EPS support and ~1% volume growth is a fair multiple for a steady but slow compounder facing transition, not a deep mispricing.

Where the valuation case holds up: forward P/E of 12.8x is reasonable for a #1-share oligopolist with ~$900M FCF, a covered dividend, and a resolved tariff overhang. The PEG is not flattering given low organic growth, but on a normalized basis — adjusting for the ~$463M non-cash U.K. pension settlement charge expected in H2 2026 that will dent GAAP optically — the cash-earnings power is more durable than the headline implies. The valuation supports a HOLD with re-rating optionality if volume accelerates or new management demonstrates margin recovery; it does not support a BUY, because the catalysts for re-rating are not yet in hand and the sector offers weak selection edge.

## Competitive Landscape

The aluminum beverage-can industry is a consolidated global oligopoly: Ball, Crown Holdings, Ardagh/Trivium, and Canpack are the main players, with four to five producers per region. Ball holds #1 share in all three core regions, defended by scale, capital intensity (continuous-run plants are expensive to build and operate), high switching costs, and long-term contracts. This is a stable, wide-moat structure.

The competitive trajectory is steady rather than improving. Ball is not visibly gaining share against its main can competitors; the growth question is about the substrate shift (cans vs. plastic/glass), where the entire industry benefits together. Substitution risk runs the other way too — PET plastic in US/European CSDs and water, and glass beer bottles in Brazil, remain real competitive pressures. The Q1 margin compression appears partly sector-wide, which both limits the company-specific blame and removes any near-term differentiation. The bolt-on M&A modestly expands the footprint but does not change the competitive structure. Competitive position is solid and stable, which supports a moderate-to-good score, but the trajectory is not improving in a way that would warrant a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume stagnation masked by pass-through.** Ball is now almost entirely a beverage-can company (narrow product range). If the substrate shift fails to accelerate, the growth story stalls at ~1% volume, and reported revenue growth would be revealed as largely aluminum-price pass-through with no earnings benefit.

**Margin/pass-through timing risk.** Some Americas, Europe, and Asia contracts do not allow full pass-through, leaving Ball reliant on derivatives. Timing lags compressed gross margin in late 2025/Q1 2026. Sustained aluminum inflation outrunning pass-through directly hurts profitability.

**U.S. Customs tariff matter — RESOLVED FAVORABLY.** A September 2025 CBP challenge to Ball's tariff classification on certain aluminum imports was flagged in the February 2026 10-K as unresolved with possible material loss and no reserve. The Q1 2026 10-Q (May 2026) states the matter "is now concluded" with no liability recorded. This removes a meaningful overhang. Broader aluminum tariff/trade-action risk remains.

**U.K. pension buy-out settlement.** A ~$463M non-cash settlement charge is expected in H2 2026 (release of accumulated OCI pension losses). Optically large for GAAP earnings but cash-neutral and de-risking. Analysts should normalize for it.

**Geographic/EM concentration and FX.** ~53% of sales are non-US, with exposure to euro, Brazilian real, Argentine peso, Egyptian pound, and Turkish lira. Brazil tax assessments are ongoing, and geopolitical exposure (Russia/Ukraine on EMEA, US-Iran cited in Q1) adds tail risk.

**Customer concentration.** A small number of large beverage customers; loss of one would be material, mitigated by long-term contracts.

**Leverage.** $7.9B debt limits flexibility; variable-rate revolver/term loan (~4.67%) is exposed to rates, though the maturity profile is termed out to 2030.

**Related-party complexity with ORG Technology.** Ball bought Benepack from ORG (ORG retains 20%) and separately holds $99M of equity-linked notes referencing ORG's own stock (Level 3, marked-to-market; -$14M in Q1 2026, fell from $101M to $87M). This concentrates counterparty/market exposure to a single partner and warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Options data through mid-May showed ATM IV rising from 21.9% to 25.5% even as the price fell 5.6%, with the put/call open-interest ratio jumping from 0.83 to 1.29. That combination of rising IV and increasing put demand alongside a falling price reflects defensive positioning into and after the Q1 print, consistent with the market's focus on margin pressure. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (2.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The options signal is mildly cautious but not extreme, and IV at 25.5% is not pricing a binary event; it supports the view that the market sees a steady business with modest downside concern rather than an acute risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($70-77)
The substrate shift accelerates from ~1% toward mid-single-digit volume growth as deposit-return schemes and plastic restrictions broaden. New management restores gross margin toward 19%+ as pass-through timing normalizes and bolt-on integration delivers. FCF holds above $900M, the dividend grows, and even a reduced ~$600M buyback supports EPS toward the >10% growth target. The forward multiple re-rates from 12.8x toward the mid-teens as the EPS story regains credibility, driving the stock to the $70-77 consensus range. The favorably resolved tariff matter and de-risking pension settlement clear overhangs.

### Bear Case ($48-52)
Volume stays stuck at ~1%, revealing that most reported growth is aluminum pass-through with no earnings leverage. Margin compression persists as non-pass-through inflation outruns contracts, pushing gross margin below 18%. The buyback deceleration to ~$600M removes the EPS crutch, and the >10% EPS growth target proves unreachable, leading to estimate cuts. The new management team takes multiple quarters to demonstrate traction, and the TSR gap versus peers persists. The multiple holds at ~12x on flat EPS, taking the stock back toward its 52-week low near $48-52.

The base case sits between these: low-single-digit volume growth, gradual margin stabilization, ~$4.00-4.52 EPS, and a stock that re-rates modestly toward the low end of the analyst range over 12 months as the tariff and pension overhangs clear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)**

This is a growth-oriented hold, not an income-only hold. The 12-month thesis is a re-rating from 12.8x toward the mid-teens forward multiple as new management stabilizes margins and the volume recovery becomes visible, supported by a resolved tariff overhang, modestly positive estimate revisions, and a price that sits ~23% below the consensus mean target with the low target ($60) above the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin recovery to ≥19% for two consecutive quarters, confirming pass-through normalization
- Organic volume growth accelerating above 3% YoY, validating the substrate-shift thesis
- Estimate revisions turning sharply positive (current-year EPS revised up >5% over 90 days) alongside the volume acceleration

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin falling below 17% for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural pass-through failure
- Management cutting the FY26 EPS-growth outlook or the dividend
- Net leverage breaching 4.0x, signaling balance-sheet stress as the covenant tightens to 4.5x
- Volume turning negative YoY, invalidating the secular bull case

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) and the H2 2026 U.K. pension settlement charge. The Q2 print is the key checkpoint on whether margin pressure is stabilizing or worsening.

## Conclusion

Ball Corporation is a high-quality, market-leading business in a consolidated oligopoly, trading at a reasonable forward multiple with a covered dividend, strong FCF, a favorably resolved tariff overhang, and meaningful upside to the consensus mean target. Those factors justify holding the stock. The constraints are equally clear: organic volume growth of ~1%, gross-margin compression, a decelerating buyback-driven EPS tailwind, and a five-year TSR that has lagged peers and prompted a near-complete C-suite reset. The score trajectory (6.5 → 5.7 → 5.7) reflects this real fundamental softening in management transition and the fading buyback algebra, not merely price action; the recent decline in management quality and financial-health scores tracks genuine developments (leadership turnover amid TSR underperformance, rising leverage to $7.86B).

The HOLD rests on a specific 12-month catalyst path: margin stabilization under new management plus a visible substrate-shift volume recovery driving a multiple re-rating toward the low end of the analyst range. That path is plausible but not yet evidenced, and the fading buyback support means the EPS-growth target is at risk. Until margin recovery and volume acceleration appear in the data, the risk/reward is balanced — enough to hold for the re-rating optionality and the income, not enough to commit fresh capital at a BUY rating.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: The Q1 2026 adjusted-EPS beat is consistently described as "$0.94 vs. $0.84 consensus," but Phase 1 earnings-surprise data shows the actual estimate was $0.86 (an 8.8% surprise, not the larger beat the $0.84 figure implies); the magnitude of the "beat" narrative supporting the re-rating thesis is modestly overstated.
