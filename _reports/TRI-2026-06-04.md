**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Thomson Reuters is a global professional-information and workflow-software company organized around three high-growth segments (Legal, Corporates, Tax/Audit/Accounting) plus Reuters News and a structurally declining Global Print business. Full-year 2025 organic revenue grew 7%, accelerating to 8% in Q1 2026, with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 42.2% in the most recent quarter and free cash flow tracking toward roughly $2.1B in 2026. This is a recurring-subscription, mission-critical-workflow business with high switching costs, a fortress balance sheet, and management guiding to roughly 100 basis points of annual margin expansion through 2028.

The defining feature of this name is the disconnect between operating performance and the share price. The stock has fallen approximately 57% from mid-2025 levels near $200 to roughly $83, with a 52-week range of $78.60 to $221.84. That decline did not occur because the business deteriorated; organic growth accelerated and margins expanded across the same window. The decline reflects a re-rating driven by a single, genuinely unresolved question: does generative AI expand Thomson Reuters' addressable market by shifting professional-services spend toward technology, or does it commoditize legal and tax research and erode the company's pricing power and retention? The bull and bear cases rest on the identical fact pattern. GenAI products now represent roughly 30% of legal annual contract value, up from 24% two quarters earlier, which proves AI monetization is real but does not yet prove whether the net effect on the moat is positive or negative.

A 57% drawdown on improving fundamentals would normally argue for an aggressive buy on improving risk/reward. This resolves to a HOLD rather than a BUY because of three things: the AI-disruption outcome is binary and not yet falsifiable in either direction, near-term consensus estimates are falling (current-quarter EPS cut 6.1% over 90 days), and the options market is pricing imminent stress through a backwardated term structure and elevated put demand. The asymmetry has improved meaningfully versus six months ago, but conviction on either side of the AI debate is not yet supportable from the data.

## Company Overview

Thomson Reuters serves professional markets through proprietary, authoritative content repositories layered with workflow software. The "Big 3" segments are the engine. Legal Professionals (Westlaw, the CoCounsel GenAI legal assistant, and the ongoing Westlaw Advantage upgrade cycle) is the largest and most strategically important. Corporates sells legal, tax, and risk-workflow tools into corporate departments. Tax, Audit & Accounting is the fastest-growing Big 3 segment, posting 11% organic growth in Q4 2025. Reuters News operates as a news agency and increasingly monetizes content licensing, including GenAI training-data licensing. Global Print is the legacy physical-products business in structural decline (−5% to −6%).

The competitive thesis centers on what management calls "fiduciary-grade AI": deterministic, verifiable, source-grounded AI outputs built on top of proprietary legal, tax, and regulatory source-of-truth data that general-purpose probabilistic large language models cannot replicate. The company is model-agnostic, currently using Anthropic models for its agents and partnering commercially with Microsoft, while building a proprietary Thomson legal LLM as a cost-and-performance hedge. The CoCounsel products and the proprietary legal LLM are owned by Thomson Reuters; Anthropic and Microsoft are vendor and partner relationships, not the owners of the differentiated IP.

A note on filing status: Thomson Reuters is a Canadian company and an SEC foreign private issuer. It files Form 40-F (annual, latest 2026-03-05) and Form 6-K (interim), not 10-K/10-Q/8-K. Standard XBRL and financial-health screening tools return no data, and the figures in this report are reconstructed from the company's Q4/FY2025 and Q1 2026 earnings disclosures. Two material items remain unconfirmed from primary filings: the current size and value of the company's London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) equity stake (a legacy of the 2018 Refinitiv sale), and the Woodbridge Company controlling-shareholder ownership stake. Both are material to the investment case and should be verified against the 40-F.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is high quality. FY2025 delivered 7% full-year organic revenue growth, adjusted EBITDA of $2.9B (+6% YoY) at a 39.2% margin (+100 bps), adjusted EPS of $3.92 (versus $3.77 prior year), and free cash flow of $1.95B, which beat the company's ~$1.9B guidance.

Q1 2026 accelerated. Revenue was $2,087M, up 10% reported and 8% organic. Adjusted EBITDA was $881M at a 42.2% margin. Big 3 organic growth was 9%, with Legal ex-government up 11%. Net income was $459M. The quarterly earnings record is consistent: three beats and one in-line result over the trailing four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 1.8%. These are modest beats, indicating estimates are well-calibrated rather than systematically low.

The 2026 guidance, reaffirmed (not raised) at the Q1 call, calls for organic revenue growth of 7.5%–8.0%, Big 3 organic around 9.5% (Legal +8–9%, Corporates +9–11%, Tax +11–13%), EBITDA margin around 40% (+100 bps), free cash flow around $2.1B, an effective tax rate of roughly 19%, and capex around 8% of revenue. Reaffirming rather than raising guidance after an 8% organic quarter suggests credibility and a degree of conservatism. The margin-expansion roadmap of roughly 100 bps per year through 2028 has an operational backbone: internal AI deployment (over 85% of employees on the internal "OpenArena" platform, ~300 use cases, call-center handle time down 15%, first-call resolution up 10%, Westlaw US content delivery speed up 25%) plus roughly $20M of 2026 severance tied to workflow automation. This is real cost takeout, not pure mix shift.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and supported by capacity. The company cites roughly $9–11B of capital capacity through 2028. In 2025 it completed a $1B buyback, raised the dividend 10% to $2.62/share, and deployed roughly $850M across four bolt-on acquisitions. In Q1 2026 it completed a $605M return of capital with a roughly 2% share-count reduction and again raised the dividend 10%. The plan is to return roughly 75% of FCF to shareholders in 2026. Interest expense guidance was nudged up by $30M (to $180–190M) because of the pace of capital returns, a manageable consequence. The undeployed M&A capacity plus the unquantified LSEG stake represent both a downside cushion and an upside optionality lever that a simple screen would miss.

This is a financially healthy company. The genuine debate is valuation and AI-disruption risk, not solvency. Financial Health scores 7.5.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest part of the current fundamental picture and is accelerating, which is unusual for a name down 57%. Organic growth moved from 7% (FY2025) to 8% (Q1 2026). The Big 3 segments are compounding at roughly 9% organic, with Tax/Audit/Accounting at the high end (11–13% guided). GenAI products are ramping rapidly inside the revenue base: roughly 30% of legal annual contract value in Q1 2026, up from 28% of total ACV in Q4 2025. Westlaw Advantage is adopting faster than the two prior upgrade cycles. The next-generation CoCounsel Legal launch is scheduled for Q3 2026, a concrete product catalyst.

The offsetting growth dynamics are real but contained. Government legal growth slowed to roughly 5% on contract cancellations and downgrades, a headwind into 2026 with reacceleration expected only late in the year as comps lap. Global Print continues to decline 5–6%, a shrinking but persistent drag. One-time GenAI content-licensing revenue at Reuters fell from $33M (2024) to $13M (2025), creating an optical comparison headwind. Management identifies the gating factor for AI-driven TAM expansion as customer change management within firms rather than the technology, which means the AI revenue ramp is real but slower than the underlying technology would allow.

There is a tension worth flagging: the operating data shows acceleration, but consensus estimates are moving the other way. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 5.7% over 30 days and 6.1% over 90 days; current-year and next-year estimates each slipped about 1%. The near-term cuts are likely a combination of government softness, the licensing comp, and LLM cost normalization expected in H2 2026 (management calls current LLM costs "small," with normalization ahead). Falling near-term estimates against accelerating organic revenue is a contradiction the next two prints will resolve. Growth Prospects scores 6.5 — strong and durable, but held back by the falling near-term revisions and the unresolved question of whether AI is a net tailwind or headwind to the long-run trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline valuation requires care because the data is internally inconsistent. The profile reports a trailing P/E of 24.1 on $3.34 EPS at $85.42. But the company reported FY2025 adjusted EPS of $3.92 and consensus current-year EPS sits at $4.43 with next-year at $5.06. On consensus forward earnings, the stock at ~$83 trades at roughly 18.8x current-year and 16.5x next-year adjusted EPS. That is a material de-rating for a business compounding revenue at 8% organic with expanding margins and ~$2.1B of FCF.

The FCF yield is attractive: roughly $2.1B of FCF on a ~$36–37B market cap is about 5.7%, with ~75% of that returned to shareholders.

The reason the market assigns this discount is the AI-disruption thesis. The bear case is that GenAI commoditizes the legal and tax research that underpins Westlaw's pricing power, allowing firms to build or buy cheaper substitutes and eroding retention. If that thesis is correct, today's 16.5x forward multiple is the early stage of a structural derating toward a declining-franchise multiple. If the bull thesis is correct (AI expands TAM and the authoritative-content moat grounds fiduciary-grade AI), then 16.5x forward on an accelerating compounder is a clear mispricing with substantial re-rating upside.

Because the outcome is genuinely binary and not yet resolved, I cannot score valuation as a high-conviction cheap. The forward multiple is undemanding relative to growth and quality, but the discount exists for a reason that is real and unsettled. Analyst targets (low $97, median $120, mean $125, high $186) all sit above the current ~$83 price, implying roughly 16%–123% upside, but those targets were largely set during a higher-price regime and have a wide dispersion reflecting the same binary debate. Valuation scores 6.0 — cheap on forward fundamentals if the franchise holds, fairly-to-fully valued if the AI bear case proves out.

## Competitive Landscape

Thomson Reuters' moat has historically been among the widest in professional information: proprietary, authoritative, regulated-domain content (legal, tax, regulatory source-of-truth data) embedded in mission-critical workflows with very high switching costs. The strategic question is whether generative AI strengthens or weakens that moat.

The bull view: the proprietary content becomes the grounding layer that makes AI outputs trustworthy enough for high-stakes professional use, a "source of truth" that general-purpose LLMs lack. Management reports "green shoots" in retention improvement and frames AI as shifting law-firm spend from real estate and headcount toward technology, expanding rather than cannibalizing the TAM. The proprietary Thomson legal LLM reportedly outperforms frontier models on specific tasks and serves as both a cost hedge against rising third-party LLM costs and a differentiator versus pure LLM resellers.

The bear view is the mirror image: if AI commoditizes research, the content advantage matters less, and firms gain leverage to substitute cheaper tools, pressuring price and retention. A May 2026 Seeking Alpha note maintained a Hold specifically on the argument that AI workflow risk persists and that strong Q1 results do not disprove the bear case. The data so far is consistent with both stories: GenAI at 30% of legal ACV and rising proves adoption and monetization but does not yet prove whether net pricing power is rising or falling.

The competitive position level is high (entrenched leader, embedded workflows), but the trajectory is the open question, and the market is currently betting that the trajectory is at risk. Until retention and net-pricing data clarify the direction, I score Competitive Position 5.5 — a strong incumbent facing a real, unpriced-direction structural shift, where the trajectory is genuinely undetermined rather than clearly improving or deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is AI disruption to the core research franchise. This is a specific, idiosyncratic, and currently unresolved swing factor that is actively driving the share price. Its eventual resolution will likely determine whether the next move is back toward $120+ or down toward a structurally lower multiple.

Secondary risks: LLM cost inflation as AI offerings scale (management expects normalization in H2 2026, mitigated by the model-agnostic strategy and proprietary LLM); government legal softness; structural print decline; customer change-management bottlenecks slowing the AI revenue ramp; M&A execution risk given the capital capacity and management's own flag that software-sector valuation volatility complicates larger deals; and minor FX/rate sensitivity. Governance is a standing consideration: as a controlled company, the Woodbridge Company (Thomson family) holding entrenches a controlling shareholder, which can both stabilize long-term strategy and limit minority-shareholder influence and activist optionality. The controlling stake should be confirmed in the management information circular.

The estimate-revision trend is a near-term warning: consensus is broadly falling, signaling negative near-term earnings momentum even as organic revenue accelerates. This divergence increases the risk of a disappointing near-term print.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 59.9% versus 43.0% trailing realized volatility, a rich 16.9-point spread, with the term structure in backwardation (59.9% near-term versus 55.3% at six months). That configuration indicates the market expects an event-driven move soon, consistent with the next earnings print and the unresolved AI debate. Put/call skew is elevated at 8.8% and the put/call open-interest ratio is 2.78, both reflecting meaningful downside hedging demand. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±9.7%. Short interest is moderate at 12.8M shares and 6.1 days to cover — neither a crowded short nor a clear squeeze setup. The options signal does not override the fundamentals, but it confirms that sophisticated positioning is braced for a binary, near-term resolution. Risk Factors scores 5.0: the AI-disruption catalyst is specific and unpriced-in-direction, near-term estimates are falling, and the options market is flagging event risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Thomson Reuters is a recurring-revenue compounder accelerating to 8% organic growth with 42.2% EBITDA margins, ~$2.1B FCF, and a ~16.5x next-year P/E that is a discount to its own history. The 57% drawdown was a sentiment-and-multiple event, not a fundamental one. If the authoritative-content moat proves to be the indispensable grounding layer for fiduciary-grade AI — and the 30%-and-rising GenAI share of legal ACV, faster Westlaw Advantage adoption, and "green shoots" in retention suggest it might — then AI expands the TAM and the stock re-rates on rising earnings. A re-rating to ~22x on ~$5.06 next-year EPS implies roughly $111, with a more optimistic re-rating plus the proprietary-LLM margin lever and LSEG-stake optionality supporting the analyst high near $186. Bull case: ~$150–185.

**Bear Case.** GenAI commoditizes legal and tax research. Retention and net pricing erode as firms substitute cheaper tools, organic growth decelerates, and the franchise re-rates toward a structurally declining-business multiple. Near-term estimate cuts and government softness are early evidence the deceleration risk is real, and the backwardated, put-skewed options market signals informed positioning braced for downside. In this scenario the forward multiple compresses toward 12–14x on flat-to-declining earnings, implying ~$60–70. Bear case: roughly −16% to −28% from current levels.

Both cases rest on the same fact pattern. That is why this is a HOLD rather than a high-conviction directional call: the determining variable (the net direction of AI's effect on the moat) is not yet falsifiable from the available data.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale — the operating quality and improved valuation are real, but the central swing factor is genuinely undetermined and the near-term estimate trend is negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters showing GenAI share of legal ACV continuing to climb (above ~33–35%) *with* stable-to-improving Big 3 retention and net pricing — direct evidence the moat is expanding rather than eroding.
- Near-term EPS estimate revisions inflecting positive (current-quarter and current-year revisions turning up), confirming the organic acceleration is flowing through to the bottom line rather than being offset by LLM cost inflation.
- A successful Q3 2026 CoCounsel Legal launch with quantified adoption metrics and evidence of incremental ACV, validating the TAM-expansion thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Big 3 organic growth decelerating below ~6% or any disclosed deterioration in Westlaw retention — the first hard evidence the bear case is materializing.
- GenAI ACV growth stalling while pricing pressure emerges, indicating commoditization is winning.
- A large, dilutive, or strategically defensive acquisition that signals management is buying growth to offset organic erosion.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings print (Q2 2026, likely early August 2026) is the primary near-term checkpoint, with the Q3 2026 CoCounsel Legal launch as the key product catalyst. The AI-direction debate should become materially clearer over the next two to three quarters as the GenAI-ACV trajectory and retention data accumulate.

## Conclusion

Thomson Reuters is a high-quality, recurring-revenue compounder with accelerating organic growth, expanding margins, robust free cash flow, a fortress balance sheet with substantial undeployed capacity, and a shareholder-friendly capital-return program. On forward earnings it trades at a discount to its own history, and the ~57% drawdown from mid-2025 occurred against improving, not deteriorating, fundamentals, which on its face argues for a constructive stance.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the entire valuation gap is explained by a single, binary, and currently unresolved question about whether generative AI expands or cannibalizes the company's legal and tax research moat. The same 30%-of-legal-ACV GenAI datapoint feeds both the bull and bear cases. Near-term consensus estimates are falling, and the options market is pricing imminent event risk through a backwardated, put-skewed term structure. The risk/reward has genuinely improved versus six months ago, and a patient long-term investor could reasonably accumulate here on the franchise quality and forward FCF yield. But the determining variable is not yet falsifiable, near-term momentum is negative, and conviction on either side of the AI debate is not supportable from the current data. I recommend holding existing positions and watching the next two prints and the Q3 CoCounsel launch for the data that will settle the direction. Overall score: 6.15.
