## Executive Summary

Micron Technology is in the middle of the most extreme earnings inflection in semiconductor history. The company reported $23.9B in revenue for fiscal Q2 2026 (ended February 2026) at 74% gross margins, up from a $5.8B net loss just 10 quarters earlier. Management has guided Q3 FY2026 at $33.5B revenue with ~81% gross margins and non-GAAP EPS of $19.15 ± $0.40, implying annualized revenue approaching $134B. This transformation is driven by AI-related memory demand — specifically HBM, high-density DRAM, and data center SSDs — which CEO Mehrotra characterizes as "structural, not cyclical" with meaningful new supply not arriving until 2028.

The investment case rests on two pillars: (1) the earnings trajectory is accelerating, with FY2027 consensus EPS estimates of $102.74 having risen 131% in the past 90 days alone, and (2) valuation on forward earnings remains reasonable at ~14.8x current fiscal year (FY2026E) and ~8.4x next fiscal year (FY2027E) consensus, despite the stock's 9x appreciation from May 2025 lows. The stock trades at a discount to peers on forward earnings while delivering superior growth. The critical risk is cyclical reversion — memory has historically been brutally cyclical, and the current pricing environment depends on AI capex continuing to accelerate. Taiwan manufacturing concentration adds a low-probability but catastrophic geopolitical tail risk.

At $864 (current price per Phase 1 data), the stock has pulled back ~5% from its 52-week high of $867. With estimates still rising and the supply-demand imbalance confirmed by multiple data points (inventory running lean, 50-67% demand fulfillment, ASPs up 60-80% QoQ), the fundamental inflection remains intact.

## Company Overview

Micron Technology is the sole U.S.-headquartered memory and storage semiconductor manufacturer, producing DRAM (79% of Q2 FY2026 revenue), NAND (21%), and NOR memory products. The company reorganized into four business units in late FY2025:

- **Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU):** $13.5B FY2025 revenue (up 623% from FY2023), serving hyperscale cloud and HBM for data center AI workloads
- **Core Data Center Business Unit (CDBU):** $7.2B FY2025, mid-tier cloud and enterprise
- **Mobile and Client Business Unit (MCBU):** $11.9B FY2025, mobile and PC
- **Automotive and Embedded Business Unit (AEBU):** $4.8B FY2025, automotive/industrial

The company operates in an oligopoly (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron control over 90% of DRAM production) and is currently shipping leading-edge 1-gamma DRAM (using EUV lithography), HBM3E 12-high stacks, and G9 NAND. HBM4 samples have been delivered to multiple key customers, with volume production targeted for CY2026.

These revision magnitudes are extraordinary — FY2027 estimates have more than doubled in 90 days. This is the strongest estimate revision signal I've observed in the current market, and it's occurring with 31 analysts contributing to the consensus (not a thin-coverage situation).

The earnings beat pattern reinforces confidence: 4 consecutive beats, with Q2 FY2026 delivering non-GAAP EPS of $12.20 versus prior consensus estimates (the beat magnitude varies by source, with some data providers showing a 27%+ surprise and others showing an even larger beat depending on the consensus cut used). Management has consistently under-guided; however, Q3 FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $19.15 ± $0.40 is itself the company's explicit outlook — any upside beyond this range would require revenue/margin outperformance above the guided midpoint or more favorable tax/share-count assumptions. Given management's track record of conservative guidance, a beat remains plausible but should not be treated as mechanical.

### Growth Drivers

1. **HBM demand:** Sold out through 2026 per management. Each AI accelerator generation requires more HBM (H100: 80GB → H200: 141GB → B200: 192GB → next-gen: 288GB+).

2. **DRAM content growth per server:** AI training clusters require 4-8x more memory than traditional servers. Inference at scale requires similar memory densification.

3. **ASP expansion:** DRAM ASPs rose mid-60% QoQ in Q2 — unusual because unit volumes also grew (mid-single digits). Both price AND volume expanding simultaneously indicates demand well ahead of supply.

4. **Supply constraint duration:** CEO states meaningful new capacity doesn't arrive until 2028 (Boise fab first wafers mid-2027, NY fab 2030+). The Singapore NAND expansion operates in H2 2028.

5. **Mix shift toward premium products:** HBM, HBM4, and high-density server DRAM carry margins significantly above conventional DRAM. As mix continues shifting toward AI, blended margins expand even without further ASP increases in conventional products.

### Growth Risk: Cyclical Reversion

The counter-argument is straightforward: memory has always been cyclical. The 10-K discloses DRAM ASP swings of +40% to -40% in recent years. If AI capex slows, hyperscalers reduce orders, or Samsung resolves yield issues and floods the market, the current pricing environment could collapse rapidly given the industry's fixed-cost structure.

However, three structural differences from prior cycles merit consideration: (1) HBM requires significantly more wafers and cleanroom space per bit than conventional DRAM (per Micron's 10-Q disclosure), meaning capacity cannot easily shift to oversupply, (2) the industry consolidated from 5+ players to 3, reducing the prisoner's dilemma dynamic, and (3) AI demand growth rates (each generation requiring more memory) create compound demand that prior cycles never had.

### Why the Market May Be Undervaluing the Duration of Earnings Power

The market is pricing Micron as if the current margin environment is temporary and peak-cycle. The specific reason this may be incorrect: HBM requires significantly more wafer area and cleanroom space per bit than conventional DRAM (per Micron's SEC filings), which means the industry's effective capacity measured in usable bits has structurally declined as production mix shifts toward HBM. This is a supply-architecture argument that persists as long as HBM remains a significant share of DRAM production.

Additionally, at $58.29 FY2026 consensus and $102.74 FY2027 consensus, even if estimates plateau or are modestly cut 10-15%, the stock at 14.8x current-year earnings with 76% growth to next year remains cheap relative to the semiconductor peer group.

### Valuation Constraints

Whether consensus estimates — which doubled in 90 days — have fully caught up to reality is uncertain. ## Competitive Landscape

### Market Structure

The DRAM market operates as a tight oligopoly:
- Samsung: ~38-40% market share (but struggling with HBM yields)
- SK hynix: ~30-32% (HBM leader currently)
- Micron: ~25-27% (gaining share in HBM, sole U.S. producer)

### Micron's Competitive Trajectory — Improving

1. **Samsung's HBM yield problems** are Micron's gain. Multiple industry reports indicate Samsung's HBM3E yields remain below target, creating an opening for Micron to capture share in NVIDIA's supply chain.

2. **Only U.S. memory manufacturer:** This provides a structural advantage in an era of reshoring, CHIPS Act funding, and potential tariff protection. The January 2026 tariff action on certain advanced computing chips (targeting specific AI accelerators like NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X) signals a policy direction that could expand to benefit domestic semiconductor production more broadly, though the current tariff is narrow and does not directly apply to memory products.

3. **Technology leadership:** 1-gamma node (first EUV DRAM in production), competitive HBM3E 12-high, and HBM4 samples ahead of some competitors.

4. **Vertical SSD integration:** Proprietary controllers + firmware + NAND = higher-margin, stickier data center SSD products vs. commodity flash.

### Competitive Risks

- SK hynix maintains HBM leadership and deeper NVIDIA qualification
- Samsung could resolve yield issues and flood market with previously constrained capacity
- Chinese competitors (CXMT) expanding in legacy DRAM — not HBM-competitive but could pressure non-AI segments
- YMTC aggressively expanding in NAND markets

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Cyclical Reversion (HIGH PROBABILITY over 3-5 year horizon, LOWER over 12-18 months)**
Memory margins above 60% have historically been unsustainable for more than 4-6 quarters. The current cycle is unprecedented in magnitude, but reversion to 40-50% gross margins over 2-3 years would roughly halve EPS from peak levels. Supply additions arrive 2027-2028. The next 12-18 months appear structurally protected.

**2. AI Capex Slowdown (MODERATE, 25-35% probability within 12 months)**
If hyperscaler capex budgets are cut, HBM orders could slow. However, the pipeline (NVIDIA Blackwell ramp, next-gen Rubin, AMD MI400, Google TPU) extends through 2027 with committed orders. Near-term risk is low; medium-term risk is moderate.

**3. Taiwan Geopolitical (LOW probability, CATASTROPHIC impact)**
Majority of DRAM production in Taiwan. This is a tail risk that's difficult to hedge directly.

**4. Regulatory/Antitrust Scrutiny (NEW RISK)**
The Q2 FY2026 10-Q adds explicit language about supply allocation potentially attracting "government and regulatory focus" that could "limit or severely restrict" ability to sell into certain end markets. This is new disclosure language signaling management awareness of potential scrutiny of pricing practices during the shortage.

**5. Valuation Compression**
At $860+, the stock prices considerable future growth. Any disappointment in Q3 guidance execution or commentary about demand normalization could trigger 15-25% drawdowns given elevated expectations.

## Conclusion

Micron presents a BUY at current levels, driven by the strongest earnings momentum in the semiconductor industry. The combination of 90-day estimate revisions exceeding +70% for the current year and +131% for next year, a current fiscal year P/E of 14.8x on FY2026E and 8.4x on FY2027E consensus, consistent earnings beats (4 consecutive), and validated supply constraints (inventory at multi-year lows relative to revenue) constitutes a clear inflection-stage opportunity.

The scoring trajectory shows improvement from 7.0 to 7.3-7.5 over the past month, driven by competitive position strengthening (Samsung struggles, U.S. policy tailwinds) and valuation becoming incrementally more reasonable as estimates catch up. The primary constraint on a higher overall score is valuation uncertainty: at $864, the stock prices significant forward growth, and memory's historical cyclicality means reversion risk is non-trivial over a 2-3 year horizon. However, over the next 12 months, supply-demand dynamics appear structurally favorable, estimates are still rising, and management has guided above prior consensus.

The 3.3% short interest, call-dominated options flow, and broad analyst consensus (39 buy/strong-buy vs. 1 sell) confirm this is a momentum/inflection trade where the fundamental support is genuine. Position sizing should reflect the elevated volatility (110% near-term IV) and potential for 20-30% drawdowns on any demand narrative shift.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The Q3 EPS guidance of $19.15 is Micron's explicit midpoint (not a conservative consensus to be mechanically exceeded), the consensus mean price target ($613) is well below the current stock price, and the tariff tailwind cited is narrower than presented — these factors collectively increase the risk that the valuation case is more dependent on continued estimate revisions and FY2027 earnings durability than the report's framing suggests.
