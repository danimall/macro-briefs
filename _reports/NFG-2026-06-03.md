**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Fuel Gas is an integrated Appalachian natural gas company that owns the molecule from wellhead to burner tip across three segments: Integrated Upstream & Gathering (Seneca Resources plus midstream), FERC-regulated Pipeline & Storage, and a regulated New York/Pennsylvania distribution utility. The structure is genuinely differentiated, and the stock is cheap on headline metrics: at $76.44 it trades at roughly 10x the midpoint of FY2026 guidance ($7.45–$7.75 EPS), versus a gas-utility peer median P/E of 16.5. The company is a Dividend King, carries conservative leverage (debt-to-cap 0.37 against a 0.65 covenant), and is in the middle of a $2.62B acquisition of CenterPoint's Ohio gas utility that would double its regulated rate base.

The tension is that nearly every component of the bull case is contingent. The acquisition requires PUCO approval in a jurisdiction NFG has never operated under, with closing not expected until Q4 calendar 2026. Roughly 60% of consolidated net income comes from gas production, which exposes the company to commodity-price swings and full-cost ceiling-test impairments; the Dec. 2024 quarter carried a $141.8M pre-tax write-down, a recent reminder that the tail risk is live. Near-term estimates are falling, not rising: current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 13.7% over 90 days and current-year EPS 4.0%. The stock has declined ~21% from its March 2026 high of $97.06 and now sits near its prior 52-week low, with the Q2 FY2026 print (adjusted EPS $2.71, a 4.0% miss) triggering a ~7.4% drop.

We rate NFG HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The valuation is attractive and the rate-base doubling is real, but the combination of a binary, un-closed regulatory transaction, negative near-term estimate momentum, commodity dependence, and structurally hostile New York decarbonization policy keeps this below our conviction threshold for a long. The path to upgrade is concrete and monitorable: PUCO approval and a turn in estimate revisions, neither yet in hand.

## Company Overview

NFG operates around a single Appalachian Basin footprint (western New York, northwestern Pennsylvania, and the Marcellus/Utica shale) across three reportable segments:

1. **Integrated Upstream & Gathering** — Seneca Resources (E&P) plus Midstream gathering. The largest and most variable profit driver. In Q2 FY2026 it produced ~102 Bcf and generated $152M of the $247.7M consolidated net income (~61%). NFG merged its former separate E&P and Gathering segments into this single segment in Q4 FY2025, a presentation change that complicates comparison with older models.

2. **Pipeline & Storage** — Supply Corporation and Empire Pipeline, FERC-regulated interstate transport and storage. Straight fixed-variable rate design insulates revenue from volume swings. ~$31.6M Q2 net income.

3. **Utility** — Distribution Corporation, the regulated LDC serving western NY and northwestern PA. ~$65.3M Q2 net income.

The strategic logic is vertical coordination: produce Appalachian gas, gather it, transport it, and distribute it, capturing margin across the chain. The standardized "gas utility" classification understates this, but it also means NFG is not a pure regulated utility: its earnings carry meaningful commodity beta that peers like One Gas or Spire do not.

## Financial Analysis

The H1 FY2026 earnings improvement is partly real and partly optical. Net income rose to $429.3M from $261.3M in H1 FY2025 (+64%), diluted EPS to $4.58 from $2.86. A large share of the year-over-year swing is the absence of the prior-year impairment: H1 FY2025 carried a $141.8M ($103.6M after-tax) write-down (a $108.3M full-cost ceiling-test charge plus ~$24.5M of water-disposal asset impairment) in the Dec. 2024 quarter. Stripping that out, the underlying improvement was driven by higher realized gas prices: the after-hedging realized price rose to $3.16/Mcf in H1 FY26 from $2.74, and Q2 weighted-average after-hedging price was $3.45 vs. $2.94.

Cash generation is healthy. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $657.3M (up from $473.9M), comfortably funding $498M of capex. The balance sheet is conservatively levered: embedded long-term debt cost is 4.81%, long-term debt is $2.38B, and debt-to-cap of 0.37 sits well below the 0.65 covenant ceiling; the company notes it could add ~$5.26B of debt before breaching. This matters for the acquisition, which the company can finance without straining covenants.

Two items temper the picture. First, hedging is currently a drag: with 374.1 Bcf hedged, the Q2 pre-hedge price of $3.92 versus the $3.45 after-hedge realization means NFG gave up ~$0.47/Mcf in a rising market. The hedge book cushioned the FY2025 downturn but caps upside now. Second, the full-cost ceiling cushion was ~$1.6B after-tax at 3/31/26 (at a $3.72 12-month Henry Hub average); a $0.25/MMBtu drop would shrink it to ~$1.2B. Impairment is remote at current prices but, as Dec. 2024 demonstrated, not theoretical.

Capital allocation reflects acquisition priorities. The dividend was raised to $0.535/quarter ($2.14 annualized), but the $200M buyback authorized in March 2024 has been suspended to preserve capital for the CenterPoint deal, and the company completed a $338M equity raise in December 2025.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth story is the CenterPoint Ohio acquisition: Vectren Energy Delivery of Ohio, announced Oct. 20, 2025. It would diversify the regulated mix into Ohio, a more gas-friendly jurisdiction. The deal is structured as $1.42B cash plus a $1.2B seller promissory note (6.5%, 364-day), with financing materially de-risked: the December equity placement reduced the term loan to $1.08B, the revolver was upsized to $1.3B in March 2026, and the seller note can be excluded from the covenant calculation if defeased.

A second, quieter growth vector is gas-fired power and data-center demand showing up in the pipeline segment. The Shippingport Lateral (~$57M, supporting a co-located data center, 100% contracted, in-service late 2026), Line N System Upgrade (~$93M, 100% incremental capacity contracted, in-service late 2028), and Tioga Pathway (~$101M, 190,000 Dth/d, in-service late 2026) are firm-transportation contracts that monetize the footprint. Rate cases add incremental revenue: a NY three-year plan (+$15.8M FY2026, +$12.7M FY2027), a PA filing for +$19.7M (suspended to Oct. 2026), and a FERC Section 4 case filed April 30, 2026 seeking ~$95M for Supply Corporation.

Against this, near-term estimate momentum is negative, which is the most important counterweight to the structural story. Current-quarter consensus EPS has fallen to $1.48, next-quarter 10.2%, and current-year 4.0%. The earnings revision picture here is the inverse of the strong-buy signal pattern: estimates are broadly declining, which weighs against treating the cheap multiple as a clean inflection trade. The acquisition and pipeline projects support a credible multi-year rate-base growth thesis, but the next several quarters carry negative momentum tied to weather normalization and the financing overhang.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.44 and a forward P/E of ~10.3x, NFG trades at a substantial discount to the gas-utility peer median P/E of 16.5x. UGI sits at 12.1x, NJR 16.3x, Spire 16.5x, One Gas 17.4x, and Southwest Gas 26.1x. NFG's 2.8% dividend yield is below the peer median of 3.5%, but the discount on earnings is the relevant signal.

The discount is partly justified and partly opportunity. It is justified because ~60% of NFG's earnings come from commodity-exposed E&P, which warrants a lower multiple than a pure regulated LDC, and because the New York policy environment is structurally worse than for utilities in supportive states. It is opportunity to the extent the market is also discounting deal/financing/regulatory uncertainty that will resolve over the next 12 months: KeyBanc's April 2026 Overweight initiation with a $110 price target frames exactly this "under-the-radar integrated gas" thesis.

We do not treat this as a clean cycle-adjusted-trough situation. Estimates are falling rather than rising, so the cheap multiple is not the signature of an earnings inflection the market is missing; it more closely reflects genuine uncertainty about a transaction that has not closed and a commodity input the company does not control. On normalized regulated rate base post-acquisition, there is a credible path to re-rating toward the low end of the peer band, but that requires the deal to close on acceptable terms. The analyst target range ($93 low, $110 high, $102 median) implies meaningful upside, but those targets are predicated on deal completion. We assign a moderate valuation score: the discount is real, but it is a discount for identifiable, unresolved risks rather than a mispricing the market is simply overlooking.

## Competitive Landscape

NFG's vertically integrated footprint is a durable structural advantage that the gas-utility label understates. Owning production, gathering, transport, and distribution across one geography lets it coordinate capital and capture margin along the chain. The pipeline segment's straight fixed-variable rate design provides stable, volume-insulated cash flow, and the data-center/power-demand contracts (Shippingport, Line N) are a non-obvious growth source for what is usually a sleepy regulated business.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The Northeast decarbonization push, concentrated in New York, directly attacks new-customer growth: the CLCPA mandates 40% GHG cuts by 2030 and 85% by 2050, the 2023 new-building fossil-fuel-equipment ban (effective Dec. 31, 2025, currently in litigation/suspended), and December 2025 legislation requiring new residential gas applicants to pay the first 100 feet of hook-up cost (effective Dec. 2026). These are structurally worse than peers face in supportive states. The CenterPoint Ohio acquisition is in part a deliberate pivot away from this exposure toward a constructive Ohio regulatory environment. The trajectory is improving on the regulated-diversification front but eroding on the NY residential-growth front, a wash that supports a stable-to-modestly-positive competitive read rather than a clear share-gainer thesis.

## Risk Assessment

**CenterPoint Ohio regulatory approval (binary, dominant).** Closing is contingent on PUCO approval, a body NFG has never operated under. Failure to close, or onerous merger conditions, would remove the central growth pillar and likely the multiple-rerating thesis.

**Commodity exposure and ceiling-test impairment.** ~60% of earnings come from gas production. The ceiling cushion is comfortable at current prices, but the Dec. 2024 impairment shows the tail is live in a downturn. A sustained price decline would compress both earnings and the cushion simultaneously.

**New York policy hostility.** Structurally worse than peers; directly attacks new-customer growth and is the company's most idiosyncratic structural risk.

**Hedging drag.** The 374 Bcf hedge book caps upside in a rising-price environment, currently costing ~$0.47/Mcf.

**Estimate momentum.** Falling consensus across all near-term periods signals deteriorating visibility into the next several quarters, even as the multi-year story stays intact.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 30.6% against 19.9% historical volatility (rich by 10.7pp), and the term structure is in backwardation (30.6% near-term vs. 23.4% at six months), pricing an imminent event consistent with the June timeframe and post-earnings digestion. The put/call volume ratio of 3.00 is heavy, though put/call OI of 0.69 is balanced and the skew is roughly neutral (2.6%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.6%. The collapse in P/C OI from 3.86 to 0.69 over the past month, alongside a 5% price decline and a 6.6pp IV rise, suggests prior hedges were unwound while fresh near-term uncertainty was priced. The signal is elevated near-term uncertainty rather than a directional informed bet, consistent with the un-resolved acquisition and falling estimates rather than an imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** PUCO approves the CenterPoint deal on acceptable terms in Q4 2026, doubling the regulated rate base and shifting the mix toward gas-friendly Ohio. Gas prices hold above $3.50, keeping Seneca's realized economics healthy and the ceiling cushion intact. Data-center and power-demand contracts (Shippingport, Line N, Tioga) ramp on schedule, adding firm fee-based revenue. Estimates stabilize and turn, the market re-rates toward the low end of the peer P/E band, and the stock closes the gap toward analyst targets of $93–$110.

**Bear case.** PUCO imposes onerous conditions or the deal slips/fails, removing the growth pillar and the financing rationale (with a $338M raise and suspended buyback already spent). Gas prices fall toward $3.00, compressing E&P earnings and shrinking the ceiling cushion toward the impairment zone. New York policy accelerates, pressuring residential growth. Estimates continue falling. The stock de-rates toward $63–$66 (8.5x a lower forward EPS), roughly 14–18% downside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural reassessment is the FQ3 FY2026 earnings print (roughly late July/early August 2026) and any PUCO procedural milestones on the CenterPoint review.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PUCO approval of the CenterPoint acquisition on acceptable terms, or clear procedural signals that approval is likely on a defined timeline.
- A turn in estimate revisions: current-year EPS revisions returning to flat-to-positive over a 30-day window, reversing the current -3.7%/30d trend.
- Henry Hub sustaining above $3.50 12-month average, preserving Seneca economics and the ceiling cushion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- PUCO rejection or materially onerous merger conditions, or deal termination.
- A full-cost ceiling-test impairment in any quarter, signaling commodity stress has breached the cushion.
- Current-year EPS estimates falling another 8%+ from the current $7.49, or a second consecutive earnings miss.

## Conclusion

NFG offers a genuine valuation discount (~10x forward EPS versus a 16.5x peer median) on an integrated gas business that is doubling its regulated rate base via a conservatively financed acquisition, with a credible secondary tailwind from gas-fired power and data-center demand. The score reflects a business with sound finances and a coherent multi-year strategy.

What prevents a BUY is specific and falsifiable. The central growth pillar (CenterPoint Ohio) is contingent on PUCO approval that has not been granted, in a jurisdiction the company has never operated under, with closing not expected until Q4 2026. Near-term estimates are falling, not rising. Roughly 60% of earnings depend on a gas price the company does not control, with a live (if currently remote) impairment risk demonstrated as recently as Dec. 2024. The options market is pricing near-term stress, and the stock trades near its prior 52-week low. The cheap multiple is a discount for these identifiable, unresolved risks rather than a mispricing the market is simply overlooking. We rate NFG HOLD (growth-hold) and would upgrade on PUCO approval combined with a turn in estimate revisions, both monitorable, neither yet in hand.
