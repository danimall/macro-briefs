## Executive Summary

Rio Tinto's first half of 2026 was the best six months the company has produced in four years, and the composition of the result matters more than the headline. Consolidated sales revenue rose 15% to $31,028m, underlying EBITDA rose 28% to $14,826m, underlying earnings rose 43% to $6,851m, and free cash flow rose 75% to $3,834m. Underlying EBITDA margin expanded 4.8 percentage points to 47.8%. The incremental EBITDA margin on the revenue increase was 78.9% ($3,279m of EBITDA on $4,155m of revenue), the signature of price-led earnings growth through a high-fixed-cost asset base rather than volume or cost-driven growth. Management reported copper, aluminium and lithium together contributing more than half of underlying EBITDA for the first time. External coverage attributed the copper and aluminium strength to data-centre and electrification demand — an attribution Rio itself does not make, describing the drivers as "favourable commodity prices" plus "accelerating productivity."

The valuation question reduces to two multiples that cannot both be right. At a derived market capitalisation near $166bn (≈1,626m plc + Ltd shares at $101.91), RIO trades at 13.8x trailing-twelve-month EPS of $7.38, 12.1x annualised H1 underlying EPS of $8.43, and 6.1x annualised H1 EBITDA on an enterprise value near $180bn. Against trailing free cash flow on a broad capex definition, the same equity trades near 30x. The gap is capex: spending has risen from 11.6% of revenue in FY2021 to 21.9% on a trailing basis, and free cash flow has fallen four consecutive years from $17,961m to $4,497m. Buying RIO here is underwriting project delivery (Simandou, three Pilbara replacement mines for first ore in 2027, Oyu Tolgoi's underground ramp, and lithium from 61-64kt LCE in 2026 to a ~200ktpa capacity target by 2028) on top of commodity prices.

We rate the shares HOLD. The dividend is real and formula-driven (211.0c interim at a stated 50% payout, +43% year on year), leverage is comfortable at 0.47x net debt to annualised H1 EBITDA, and net debt fell $301m in the half while funding $5,037m of capital investment and the FY2025 final dividend. But at $101.91 the base case sits roughly 6% above spot, the analyst mean is $105.85 and median $107.50, and the bear case — copper and aluminium reverting toward mid-cycle while Simandou volumes arrive into a cooling Chinese steel market — carries more than 20% downside. Full-year dividends at the current formula consume a substantial share of annualised free cash flow at peak prices, and the designed bridge (a $5-10bn cash-release programme, footnoted "subject to market conditions and execution") has not been executed. This is a fairly priced, cyclically extended equity with adequate income support, not an asymmetric entry.

## Company Overview

Rio Tinto is a dual-listed diversified miner (Rio Tinto plc, London; Rio Tinto Limited, Australia) operating across five value chains:

- **Iron ore.** The Pilbara system in Western Australia (H1 2026 production 162.3Mt on a 100% basis, the highest first half since the 2018 record), Iron Ore Company of Canada (2026 sales guidance 15-18Mt), and Simandou in Guinea (2026 sales guidance 5-10Mt). Group iron ore sales guidance is 343-366Mt, of which Pilbara is 323-338Mt.
- **Aluminium.** Integrated bauxite→alumina→primary metal (H1: bauxite 28.5Mt, alumina 4.0Mt, aluminium 1.68Mt), with FY guidance of 58-61Mt, 7.6-8.0Mt and 3.25-3.45Mt respectively.
- **Copper.** Oyu Tolgoi (H1 production +31% year on year), Kennecott, an Escondida interest, and growth options at Resolution and Winu. H1 consolidated copper 442kt against FY guidance of 800-870kt.
- **Lithium.** Built primarily through the Arcadium acquisition completed 6 March 2025 — Fénix, Sal de Vida, Rincon. 2026 guidance 61-64kt LCE against a ~200ktpa capacity target for 2028.
- **Minerals** (titanium dioxide, borates, diamonds), not separately quantified in the interim disclosures.

The competitive assets are the ones that matter in a price-taking industry: very large, long-life, low-cost orebodies with owned rail, port and power, plus the only genuinely new high-grade seaborne iron ore basin in a generation. Management's own framing of the edge in H1 2026 was operational rather than geological: a Management Operating System launched 1 July 2026 and a productivity programme running at a $1.3bn annualised rate with $870m banked year to date against a $1.8bn exit-rate target for end-2026. Those are two different measures; the company distinguishes them, and it has not "delivered $1.8bn of savings."

Commodity mix has demonstrably shifted: iron ore fell from 62.3% of segment revenue in FY2021 to 49.4% on a trailing basis while copper rose from 12.3% to 26.1% and aluminium from 20.0% to 30.3%. End-market concentration is a separate question and is unverified: Simandou ore undergoes final crushing in China, and Guinean volumes are being directed at Chinese steel mills. No customer- or country-concentration disclosure was retrievable. Treat commodity diversification as improved and China exposure as unknown.

As a foreign private issuer Rio files a 20-F and furnishes 6-Ks, and is exempt from Section 16 and proxy requirements. There are no Forms 4 and no DEF 14A. Insider-activity and proxy-alignment data are unavailable as a matter of filing regime, not as a signal. Governance should be treated as unassessed rather than clean.

**Margin expansion is real, and it is price-driven.** EBITDA margin of 47.8% versus 43.0% is a large move for a half. The mechanism is visible in the incremental margin: $3,279m of additional EBITDA on $4,155m of additional revenue, or 78.9%. In a business where mine, rail, port and smelter costs are largely fixed within a half, that ratio indicates the revenue increase came predominantly from realised prices rather than tonnes. Confirmation sits in the production data: Q2 copper output fell 7% year on year and 7% quarter on quarter despite Oyu Tolgoi's +31% first half, and full-year copper guidance of 800-870kt implies a second half of 358-428kt against H1's 442kt.

**Earnings quality did not deteriorate.** Operating cash flow to underlying EBITDA was 61.9% versus 60.0% a year earlier. Cash conversion tracked EBITDA growth closely; there is no accrual or working-capital tell in the interim figures.

**The free-cash-flow definition changed, and the comparative appears to have been restated.** Rio refined its FCF definition to include Rio Tinto share of capital investment from H1 2026. Reconciling: OCF less RT share of capital investment gives $4,136m for H1 2026 against reported $3,834m (residual −$302m), and $2,420m for H1 2025 against reported $2,185m (residual −$235m). The residual is similar in both halves, which is consistent with a restated comparative and with the +75% growth being genuine rather than definitional. The alternative (two different bases producing coincidentally similar residuals, or a line item that scales with capex such as lease principal) cannot be excluded without reading the formal reconciliation, which was not accessible.

Three observations drive the financial-health score.

**Capex intensity has roughly doubled and free cash flow has fallen four straight years.** Capex went from 11.6% of revenue in FY2021 to 21.9% trailing; FCF fell 75% from FY2021 to FY2025 even though FY2025 revenue exceeded FY2023 and FY2024. This is deliberate growth spending (Simandou, Pilbara replacement mines, Oyu Tolgoi underground, Rincon/Arcadium), not operational failure, but part of the decline is also cost inflation and softer iron ore realisations, and the aggregator series cannot separate the two. The aggregator's TTM capex of $13,548m is materially wider than Rio's own "share of capital investment" of $5,037m in H1 (roughly $10bn annualised). The two definitions must not be mixed; on Rio's basis, FCF conversion looks considerably better.

**Net debt roughly tripled in calendar 2025**, from $4,941m to $14,071m on the aggregator basis, against Rio's own issuer-verified $14,362m at 31 December 2025. The build reconciles approximately to the Arcadium cash consideration plus a dividend that exceeded free cash flow: FY2025 DPS of 402c on ~1,626m shares implies roughly $6.5bn of dividends against $4,497m of FCF, a ~$2.0bn shortfall. The Arcadium consideration was not verified in this work, so the split is approximate.

**Leverage is comfortable now.** Net debt of $14,061m against annualised H1 EBITDA of $29,652m is 0.47x. That figure is a sensitivity rather than a forecast: full-year iron ore sales guidance of 343-366Mt against H1 actual of 164.5Mt implies an H2 of 178.5-201.5Mt, which flatters the second half on volume, while copper/aluminium pricing may not persist. Even assuming H2 EBITDA 20% below H1, leverage would be about 0.53x. Cash and investments of ~$9.5bn sit against total debt of ~$22.9bn.

**The real constraint is the coverage arithmetic, not the leverage ratio.** Annualising the H1 interim on Rio's own FCF definition gives roughly $7.7bn of free cash flow. Annualising the interim DPS at 211c on ~1,626m shares gives roughly $6.9bn of distributions. Coverage of about 1.1x, at what look like peak realisations, with capital investment running near $10bn a year on Rio's own definition. That leaves essentially nothing for debt reduction or buybacks unless prices rise further or the $5-10bn cash-release programme executes. Financial health is strong on the balance sheet and tight on the cash-flow waterfall, which is why the score sits at 6.5 rather than 8.

**Note on the reported P/E field.** The quote feed shows trailing and forward P/E of 13.69 on EPS of $6.084, which multiplies to $83.3 rather than the $101.91 close — the multiple is stale or paired with FY2025 EPS at an earlier price. On the close: 16.8x FY2025 EPS of $6.08, 13.8x TTM $7.38, 12.1x annualised H1 underlying $8.43.

## Growth Analysis

There are three distinct growth engines, with different confidence levels.

**Volume growth is contracted and dated, and the near-term contribution is small.** Simandou achieved first high-grade sales in April 2026, with mine and port more than 75% complete and rail commissioning in Q1 2026; 2026 sales guidance is only 5-10Mt against group iron ore sales of 343-366Mt. Three Pilbara replacement mines are on budget for first ore in 2027 — replacement, not incremental. Oyu Tolgoi's underground ramp delivered +31% in H1 but was more than offset elsewhere in copper, leaving consolidated copper volumes falling. Lithium first production at Fénix 1B and Sal de Vida came in ahead of plan, but 2026 guidance of 61-64kt LCE against ~200ktpa of 2028 capacity requires roughly a tripling on an asset base acquired in March 2025.

**Productivity is being delivered and is measurable.** $870m banked year to date, $1.3bn annualised run-rate achieved, $1.8bn targeted by year-end, with a 2030 framework of roughly 3% copper-equivalent production uplift and ~4% CAGR reduction in operating unit costs from a 2024 baseline. This is the most credible part of the growth case because it is management-controlled and the disclosure separates flow from exit rate.

**Price is the largest 2026 driver and the least durable.** Copper C1 net unit cost guidance was cut to 30-50c/lb from 65-75c/lb, roughly a 43% reduction at the midpoints. C1 net of by-products falls mechanically when gold and molybdenum credits rise, and gold is at a structural bid in the current reserve-asset environment. Part of that cut is likely price-driven rather than efficiency-driven; Rio did not disaggregate. If the external attribution of copper and aluminium strength to data-centre and electrification demand is correct, the earnings base inherits the duration risk of the AI capex cycle, and copper growth projects at Resolution and Winu have decade-long lead times that do not match a multi-year demand pulse.

The demand backdrop is mixed in ways that matter. Chinese steel demand is described as cooling, which caps the iron ore side [Seeking Alpha, 9 Jun 2026]. Against that, external coverage attributes the copper and aluminium strength to data-centre and electrification demand [Reuters, 29-30 Jul 2026; Forbes, 28 Jul 2026], which supports those chains, though Rio itself does not make that attribution. [Broader macro claims about Chinese PMI, Politburo stimulus and hyperscaler capex prints are unverified in the sources — unverified]

Growth prospects score 6.2: the pipeline is real and dated, but 2026's growth was overwhelmingly price, one of the two volume engines (copper) is shrinking, and the largest volume addition (Simandou) works against Rio's own dominant revenue line.

Both P/E readings are sensitivities, not forecasts. Share count is derived (6,851 ÷ 4.214 = 1,626m) and assumes 1:1 ADR-to-plc equivalence with comparable USD pricing across the two lines; independent reporting of a ~$160bn market cap at a lower late-July price is consistent.

**The 12-14x earnings versus 22-30x free cash flow spread is the whole valuation debate.** The earnings multiple is undemanding for a tier-1 diversified miner. The cash-flow multiple is not. Reconciling them requires believing capex has peaked, which the project calendar supports directionally (Simandou >75% complete, Pilbara replacement mines first ore 2027, Rincon toward 200ktpa by 2028) but which nothing in the disclosures guarantees on timing or cost.

**The more important discipline is what earnings the multiple is applied to.** Twelve times peak-cycle earnings is not the same as twelve times mid-cycle earnings. H1 delivered a 47.8% EBITDA margin with a 78.9% incremental margin on price. If copper and aluminium realisations revert 20% toward mid-cycle while iron ore softens as Simandou volumes arrive, underlying EPS does not hold near $8.40. A cheap-looking multiple on a cyclical peak is the classic value trap in this sector, and commodity producers as a group have not rewarded low-multiple entries without a company-specific driver.

**What is genuinely supportive.** The 4.1-4.5% yield is formula-based (40-60% policy band, 50% interim payout) and covered by current-year cash flow. Leverage at 0.47x gives balance-sheet optionality. The productivity programme is a controllable earnings driver worth up to $1.8bn of exit-rate benefit against annualised EBITDA near $29.7bn. A $5-10bn cash release, if executed, would fund the capex tail without touching the dividend.

**What the market is already pricing.** The stock has advanced 64% over twelve months and sits 9.5% below its May high. The analyst mean of $105.85 implies +3.9% and the median $107.50 implies +5.5%; the distribution runs 1 strong buy, 4 buy, 3 hold, 1 strong sell, with the strong sell newly reappearing. On a stock at 12-14x with an improving recommendation trend one would normally treat consensus targets as lagging, but the earnings base here is price-driven and cyclically extended, which is exactly the condition where a target range clustered within 6% of spot deserves to be read at face value rather than as a floor.

Valuation scores 5.4: fair rather than mispriced, with the yield doing most of the work.

## Competitive Landscape

Rio's position is improving on mix and mixed on trajectory within each chain.

**Iron ore.** Rio is simultaneously the marginal new high-grade supplier and a ~330Mt incumbent. On 4 June 2026, iron ore fell to a two-month low on a reported surge in Guinean exports — a single-day, weakly-sourced attribution, but directionally the structural concern: Simandou's 5-10Mt in 2026 is immaterial to earnings while any benchmark effect applies to the entire Pilbara book. The offsetting argument is that Simandou's high-grade product may occupy a premium "green steel" segment distinct from Pilbara mid-grade fines. Simandou is also a four-block, multi-party project with shared rail and port through La Compagnie du TransGuinéen, alongside a Chinalco-led consortium, Winning Consortium Simandou with Baowu, and the Government of Guinea. In May 2026 more than 3,000 workers struck at the Baowu-led site seeking pay parity with Rio's SimFer operation. Because infrastructure is shared, disruption originating on blocks Rio does not control can constrain SimFer's evacuation capacity.

**Copper.** The strategic direction is right, as the sector is being repriced on electrification and data-centre exposure, but Rio's consolidated copper volumes fell 7% year on year in Q2, and full-year guidance embeds no recovery from the Q2 run rate. Cost leadership improved sharply on a C1-net basis, with an unresolved question about how much is by-product price credit.

**Aluminium.** The Australian smelter position now rests on an A$2bn/10-year Queensland government package for Boyne, built on A$7.5bn of underwritten renewable energy and storage arrangements with developers. The plain reading is that the Queensland portfolio is not self-sustaining at prevailing power prices without public support, and that Rio has taken on long-dated offtake credit obligations. The counter-reading is that industrial policy has created a barrier to entry and secured long-dated volumes. Either way, holders of Rio's aluminium chain are partly underwriting an energy-policy outcome.

**Business-model gap versus the closest peer.** Glencore's H1 2026 earnings rose 86%, driven by commodity trading amid Middle East conflict volatility. Rio has no marketing engine of comparable scale, which is the industrial logic behind a combination; per reporting, the January 2026 talks collapsed over leadership and control rather than valuation (this rests on tier-3 aggregated UK press and is unconfirmed by any Rio or Glencore statement). A standstill on Rio approaching Glencore reportedly expired in early August 2026; Reuters' sources indicate no near-term revival, with the CEO focused on cost cuts and asset sales. Glencore's planned ASX secondary listing is read by some Australian analysts as facilitating future M&A. The M&A optionality is two-sided: a value-accretive combination or a very large integration risk on a business whose governance and incentive structures are unassessable from US filings.

Competitive position scores 6.2: improving mix, tier-1 assets, but self-cannibalisation risk in the largest earnings block and a shrinking copper volume base.

## Risk Assessment

**Peak-cycle earnings extrapolation (highest-impact).** The 78.9% incremental EBITDA margin locates 2026's earnings growth in price. The dividend formula applies 50% to those earnings. A 20% mean reversion in copper and aluminium realisations, with iron ore softening, takes FY2027 underlying EPS materially below the annualised H1 run rate and takes the dividend with it.

**Pilbara approvals conditionality.** Guidance carries an explicit qualifier that Pilbara iron ore volumes remain subject to the timing of approvals for planned mining areas and heritage clearances. That caveat attaches to 323-338Mt of guided sales, the single largest earnings block in the group.

**Simandou execution and shared-infrastructure dependency.** Multi-party ownership, co-developed rail and port, live labour disputes on blocks Rio does not control, and a fatality at the site in H1 2026. Ownership percentages, nameplate capacity, and Rio's share of remaining capex were not verifiable.

**Lithium ramp and carrying value.** A tripling of output from 61-64kt LCE to ~200ktpa capacity by 2028 on an asset base acquired in March 2025, near the top of the lithium cycle. Lithium's standalone EBITDA contribution is not disaggregated, and whether any impairment was booked in FY2025 could not be confirmed.

**Dividend-versus-capex tension.** FY2025 distributions of roughly $6.5bn exceeded FY2025 FCF of $4,497m. The designed bridge — $5-10bn of cash release through portfolio management and infrastructure mechanisms, with ~$5bn targeted by end-2026 — is footnoted as subject to market conditions and execution, and nothing has been executed.

**Estimate delivery.** Four consecutive quarters of misses against consensus EPS: −11.2%, −1.4%, −3.0% and −1.7% (June 2026: $4.21 actual against $4.29). The magnitude is shrinking, which is constructive, but the direction has been consistent. Note the conflict in the H1 characterisation: underlying earnings of $6,851m beat a Visible Alpha consensus of $6,610m by 3.6%, while the aggregated EPS series shows a 1.7% miss. Different vendors and bases; this should not be described as a clean beat in either direction.

**Governance is unassessed.** No Forms 4, no proxy, remuneration and board detail inside a report that could not be parsed. Compensation quantum, incentive metrics, board composition and related-party transactions are unknown. This is a filing-regime consequence and carries no directional information, but it removes a dimension of evidence that would normally support a higher management score.

**Decarbonisation target credibility.** H1 Scope 1+2 emissions of 15.9Mt CO2e represent a 14% reduction against a 2018 baseline, against a 50%-by-2030 target that Rio states depends on third-party project delivery and commercial discussions, "neither of which can be guaranteed by that date," with the baseline itself changing over time. Achieving 14% of a 50% target with ~4.5 years left implies roughly 2.5x the trailing pace.

**Macro overlay.** [Specific claims about US 30-year yields at a 2007 high, Chinese PMI contraction, and gold displacing Treasuries as the top reserve asset are not traceable to the research brief — unverified] The demand backdrop is mixed: cooling Chinese steel demand caps the iron ore side, while external commentary links copper and aluminium strength to data-centre and electrification demand.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.4% against 29.9% one-year realised is modestly rich (spread 4.5%, 61st percentile within the chain), with the term structure in backwardation — 34.4% at nine days against 29.8% at three months and 29.9% at six months. The nine-day tenor prices a ±4.8% move and carries a 6.7% put-side skew, while the three- and six-month tenors show the opposite sign convention and put/call volume ratios of 2.01 and 2.49 respectively. Two readings are consistent with the data. First, the near-term backwardation and put skew do not map to a company event — the next scheduled disclosure is Q3 production in October, with annual results in February — so they more plausibly reflect the interim dividend timing (211c is ~2.1% of the price) plus macro event risk. Second, the heavier put volume at three and six months against call-tilted open interest suggests hedging of a cyclically extended position rather than directional bearishness. Short interest of 0.9% of float with 3.3 days to cover confirms there is no crowded bear case. Options are not contradicting the fundamental view, but the market is paying up for near-term protection rather than upside, which argues against paying up for entry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $128 (+26%)

Copper and aluminium realisations hold at or above H1 levels as data-centre and electrification demand proves durable; the productivity programme reaches the $1.8bn exit rate and delivers the flagged ~4% CAGR unit-cost reduction; Simandou ramps toward nameplate without further shared-infrastructure disruption and its high-grade product prices in a premium segment rather than cannibalising Pilbara; $5bn of cash release executes by end-2026, funding the capex tail and allowing the payout to move toward the top of the 40-60% band. FY2027 underlying EPS reaches roughly $9.00 and the capex peak visibly rolls off, closing the earnings-to-FCF multiple gap. At 14.5x that is $130; the highest published target is $125. Optionality from a Glencore combination on terms favourable to Rio sits on top and is not in the base case.

### Bear Case — $78 (−23%)

The AI-linked bid under copper and aluminium proves to be a capex pulse rather than a demand regime; realisations revert 20-25% toward mid-cycle. Chinese steel demand continues cooling while Simandou volumes and Guinean exports add high-grade supply, pressuring the benchmark against a ~330Mt Pilbara book. Copper volumes stay below the H1 run rate, and the C1 cost improvement partially unwinds as gold and moly credits normalise. Capex stays near $10bn+ on Rio's own definition while asset sales slip on market conditions; the 50% payout is applied to lower earnings and the dividend falls 25-35%. FY2027 underlying EPS near $6.20 at 11.5x-12.5x is $71-78. The stock traded near $62 twelve months ago on comparable asset quality and a weaker mix, so $78 is not a stress floor.

### Base Case — $108 (+6%)

H2 iron ore volumes rise per guidance (implied 178.5-201.5Mt against H1's 164.5Mt) while copper volumes stay flat-to-lower per implied guidance of 358-428kt. Copper and aluminium prices ease modestly from H1 levels; productivity contributes $400-600m of incremental H2 EBITDA. FY2026 underlying EPS lands near $8.00-8.40. Applying 13x to $8.20 gives $107, with roughly $4.30-4.60 of full-year dividends. Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 50% / bear 25% gives roughly +4% of price return plus ~4.3% of yield, or a high-single-digit total expected return with wide dispersion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** The rationale is income. The dominant, reliable component of expected return is a 4.1-4.5% formula-driven dividend covered by current cash flow, on a balance sheet at 0.47x net debt to annualised EBITDA. The equity-appreciation component is only about 6% to base case, against 23% downside if the price cycle turns, with no margin of safety at 12x annualised peak earnings. Existing holders should hold; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $101.91.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. **Executed cash release.** Announcement and closing of $3bn+ of the $5-10bn programme at disclosed proceeds, converting the funding bridge from intention to cash. Combined with a price below $92 this would restore asymmetry.
2. **Capex peak confirmed with FCF inflection.** FY2026 results (February 2027) showing full-year capital investment guidance for 2027 at or below the 2026 level on Rio's own definition, with FCF covering the dividend by 1.4x or better.
3. **Copper volume stabilisation.** Q3 or Q4 production showing consolidated copper at or above 220kt per quarter, confirming that Oyu Tolgoi's ramp is no longer being offset elsewhere.
4. **Price.** A decline to $85-90 (roughly 10-11x annualised H1 underlying EPS, ~5% yield on the current formula) with fundamentals intact would be an accumulate zone.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. **Pilbara guidance cut on approvals or heritage timing**, or full-year iron ore sales tracking below 343Mt.
2. **Realisation reversal.** Two consecutive months of copper and aluminium prices 15%+ below H1 average realisations, which would break the earnings base the dividend formula is applied to.
3. **Dividend below formula, or payout above the band.** A final dividend implying a full-year payout above 60% of underlying earnings, or a stated deviation from the 40-60% policy, would signal the cash-flow waterfall has broken.
4. **A Glencore transaction on control terms unfavourable to Rio**, or any large equity-funded combination — integration risk on an unassessable governance base is not something we would underwrite at this multiple.
5. **Lithium impairment** against the Arcadium carrying value, or 2027 lithium guidance implying the ~200ktpa 2028 target has slipped.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are Q3 2026 production (mid-October 2026), any 6-K disclosing an asset-sale or infrastructure transaction, and FY2026 annual results with the 20-F (February 2027). The 20-F is also the point at which the FY2025 underlying income statement, any FY2025 impairment, the Simandou ownership and capital detail, and the FCF reconciliation should be verified — all of which are currently gaps.

---

## Conclusion

Rio Tinto is a better-diversified, better-run company than it was two years ago, and H1 2026 proved it in the numbers: 47.8% EBITDA margin, 17% ROCE, $870m of banked productivity, net debt down $301m while funding $5bn of capital investment and the prior-year final dividend. Copper, aluminium and lithium exceeding half of underlying EBITDA is a genuine structural change in commodity mix, even though it says nothing verified about China end-market exposure.

The problem is entry, not quality. Roughly 79 cents of every incremental revenue dollar dropped to EBITDA, which places the earnings growth in price rather than tonnes, and the dividend formula applies 50% to those price-driven earnings. Twelve times annualised peak EPS with capex at 21.9% of revenue, a full-year payout consuming a substantial share of annualised free cash flow, an unexecuted $5-10bn asset-sale bridge, four consecutive quarters of small consensus misses, and a consensus target range clustered within 6% of spot describe a fairly valued cyclical with a well-covered current-year dividend and a wide distribution of 2027 outcomes, not a mispricing.

Hold for the yield and the tier-1 asset base. Add on evidence — executed cash release, a confirmed capex roll-off with FCF covering the dividend by 1.4x, or a price in the $85-90 range — rather than on momentum.
