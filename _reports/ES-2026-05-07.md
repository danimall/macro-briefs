## Executive Summary

Eversource Energy trades at $68.52 with a 4.7% dividend yield and a forward P/E of ~15x on 2026 guided earnings of $4.57-$4.72 — a meaningful discount to the electric utility peer median P/E of 22.7x. The discount is partially justified: the company just cut 2026 guidance by ~5% following FERC Opinion No. 594, which reduced the New England Transmission Owners' base ROE by 100 bps to 9.57%. The stock carries a $448M contingent liability from Revolution Wind with no visibility on final exposure, has missed earnings estimates in 3 of its last 4 quarters, and faces negative estimate revision momentum across all forecast periods.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a 4.7% yield that is above the peer median and recently increased, (2) a $26.5B regulated capex program that grows rate base 61% by 2030 (supporting 5-7% long-term EPS CAGR guidance), and (3) upside optionality from FERC resolution — Eversource has filed for a replacement ROE of 11.39% that, if approved, would materially exceed the prior 10.57%. The bear case is equally concrete: FERC refunds could total hundreds of millions, Revolution Wind losses continue escalating without Eversource's control, and credit rating agencies maintain negative outlooks with the company financing its dividend entirely through external capital markets (FCF was -$45M in 2025).

This is a yield-oriented hold at current levels. The 4.7% dividend provides adequate compensation while regulatory and legal outcomes develop over the next 12-18 months. The stock requires either a FERC resolution (upside) or a price pullback to the $60-62 range to warrant accumulation.

## Company Overview

Eversource is New England's largest energy delivery company, serving 4.6 million electric, natural gas, and water customers across Connecticut, Massachusetts, and New Hampshire. After exiting offshore wind development in 2024 and pending sale of its Aquarion water business (~$2.4B), the company is transitioning to a pure-play regulated T&D utility.

Operating segments (2025 net income):
- Electric Distribution: $667M
- Electric Transmission: $777M (FERC-regulated, now impacted by ROE ruling)
- Natural Gas Distribution: $361M
- Water Distribution: $44M (pending divestiture)

The transmission segment is the highest-margin, lowest-risk business, which makes the FERC ROE ruling particularly material. At each 10 bps change in base ROE, annual after-tax earnings shift by approximately $7M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue grew 13.8% to $13.55B, driven primarily by pass-through energy cost recovery mechanisms (which don't flow to earnings). Non-GAAP EPS grew from $4.34 (2023) to $4.57 (2024) to $4.76 (2025) — a ~4.7% CAGR. Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS was $1.73 vs. $1.50 year-ago, beating the $1.64 consensus estimate by 5.4%.

**Guidance Cut:** Management reduced 2026 EPS guidance to $4.57-$4.72 from the prior $4.80-$4.95, reflecting ~$0.20/share impact from FERC's lower allowed ROE and the loss of Aquarion earnings post-sale. The midpoint ($4.65) becomes the new 5-7% long-term growth base through 2030.

**Cash Flow:** OCF was $4.11B in 2025, but capex consumed all of it ($4.16B), producing FCF of -$45M. The $1.09B annual dividend is entirely funded through debt and equity issuance. This is sustainable only under continued capital market access and constructive regulatory outcomes.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 3.94x is high even for utilities. Interest expense rose 83% from $680M (2022) to $1.24B (2025). Fixed-rate debt payments total $11.7B through maturity. Moody's and Fitch both maintain negative outlooks. Shares outstanding grew ~7% over three years (from 350M in 2023 to 376M in Q1 2026) through ATM issuances.

**Earnings Consistency:** The 3-of-4 miss record (recent quarters) reflects operational challenges including a significant Q3 2025 miss (-15.9%) related to the Revolution Wind charge. The Q1 2026 beat is encouraging but a single data point.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth:** The $26.5B capex plan (2026-2030) grows rate base from $30.6B to $49.3B — a 61% increase. Major programs include Greater Cambridge Energy ($1.84B), natural gas infrastructure replacement ($6.8B), and transmission expansion ($7.2B). These are almost entirely regulated investments earning allowed returns.

**EPS Growth Path:** Long-term guidance of 5-7% CAGR from a $4.65 base implies $5.88-$6.53 EPS by 2030. Current consensus for FY2027 is $4.99, representing 7.3% growth from the 2026 midpoint — consistent with guidance.

**Headwinds to Growth:** Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current year down 5.8% over 90 days, next year down 5.0%. This reflects the FERC ruling impact and signals the market hasn't fully digested the new growth trajectory. Until revisions stabilize, the earnings growth story lacks confirmation.

**Structural Tailwinds:** Data center load growth in New England, building electrification mandates, and ISO-NE capacity market tightness support long-term electricity demand. However, these are industry-wide rather than Eversource-specific advantages.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.52, ES trades at 15.0x trailing EPS ($4.56) and ~14.7x the 2026 guided midpoint ($4.65). Against the peer median of 22.7x, this represents a 35% discount. The discount reflects three specific concerns: (1) FERC ROE uncertainty, (2) Revolution Wind contingent liability, and (3) recent guidance cut.

**Is the discount excessive?** Partially. If FERC issues are resolved favorably (Eversource wins in court or secures a replacement ROE near 11%), the stock deserves a multiple closer to 17-18x on normalized earnings — implying $80-85. If FERC's ruling stands and Revolution Wind exposure grows further, 14-15x on depressed earnings ($4.50-4.60) suggests $63-69 fair value.

The consensus median analyst target of $73.50 implies ~7% upside, with a range of $54-$87. The wide spread ($33 between low and high) quantifies genuine uncertainty about regulatory outcomes.

**Dividend Yield Support:** The 4.7% yield exceeds the peer median (3.4%) by 130 bps and provides a valuation floor for income-oriented investors. At the current payout ratio (~68% of guided 2026 midpoint), the dividend is well-covered on an earnings basis despite the negative FCF dynamic.

## Competitive Landscape

Eversource holds natural monopoly positions in its three-state service territory, providing fundamental stability. The transmission segment benefits from FERC cost-of-service ratemaking, though FERC's push for competitive bidding on new projects introduces longer-term competitive risk.

Key competitive dynamics:
- ISO-NE's Longer-Term Transmission Planning may award future projects to non-incumbents
- Connecticut PBR framework introduction (pending PURA implementation) creates uncertainty about allowed returns vs. traditional cost-of-service
- Regional energy costs among highest in the nation — political pressure on rates could constrain future investment recovery

The company's position is stable but not improving. The offshore wind exit removed a source of competitive differentiation (and losses), while the FERC ruling specifically reduced returns on the company's highest-quality asset class.

## Risk Assessment

**FERC ROE Ruling (High Impact, Binary):** The 100 bps reduction and potential cumulative refunds (~$1.5B across all NETOs) represent the most material near-term risk. Multiple legal vectors are in play (FERC rehearing, Section 205 filing, federal court challenge filed April 14, 2026), creating a 12-36 month resolution timeline. The Q1 2026 charge of $43.9M covers only the first 15-month complaint period; additional refund charges are likely.

**Revolution Wind (Medium-High Impact, Escalating):** The $448M contingent liability grew from $365M in one year. The project faces stop-work orders, tariff impacts, and ITC qualification risk. Eversource has no operational control. Management explicitly states additional material losses are "reasonably possible." The OBBBA's July 2026 construction-commencement deadline and December 2027 in-service deadline create hard time constraints.

**Financing Risk (Medium Impact, Structural):** With FCF at -$45M and $5.1-5.5B annual capex needs, continuous access to debt and equity markets is essential. Negative credit outlooks and ~7% equity dilution over three years indicate this is a binding constraint. A credit downgrade would increase borrowing costs on the company's substantial total debt.

**Regulatory Risk (Medium Impact, Ongoing):** Four new PURA commissioners in Connecticut, performance-based regulation transition, and storm cost prudency reviews ($2.06B pending) all create ongoing regulatory uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-85, +17-24%):** FERC Section 205 filing is approved with a prospective ROE near 11% (above prior 10.57%). Revolution Wind contingent liability stabilizes or Ørsted absorbs cost overruns. Aquarion sale closes, reducing parent debt by ~$2B. Rate base growth materializes at guidance (5-7% EPS CAGR). At 17x a normalized $4.80-$5.00 EPS, stock reaches $82-85. Timeline: 12-18 months.

**Bear Case ($55-60, -12-20%):** FERC ruling upheld on appeal with full refund obligation (hundreds of millions). Revolution Wind ITC loss triggers additional material charge ($200M+). Credit downgrade forces equity dilution at depressed prices. At 12-13x depressed $4.40-$4.50 EPS, stock falls to $55-58.

**Base Case ($72-75, +5-10%):** FERC resolution takes 12-18 months with partial outcome (prospective ROE of 10.0-10.5%, refund compromise). Revolution Wind stays at current accrual levels. Aquarion closes as planned. At 15.5x $4.65, stock gravitates toward $72.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price decline to $60-62 (yield >5.0%, P/E <13x) providing adequate margin of safety for regulatory risks
2. FERC grants rehearing or signals favorable disposition on the Section 205 filing (ROE ≥10.5% prospective)
3. Two consecutive quarterly beats with positive estimate revision momentum (30-day revisions turning positive for current and next year)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Moody's or Fitch downgrades to BBB- (investment grade floor) — would impair capital market access
2. Revolution Wind contingent liability exceeds $700M (indicating ITC loss or major additional cost overrun)
3. 2026 EPS guidance cut below $4.40 (implying structural earnings power below what the dividend requires)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026), which will provide the first full quarter under the new FERC ROE and initial Aquarion sale proceeds impact. The Section 205 filing response from FERC (likely late 2026) is the key catalyst.

## Conclusion

Eversource is a yield vehicle with regulatory optionality. The 4.7% dividend, paid by New England's largest regulated utility with a 61% rate base growth trajectory, justifies a HOLD rather than an AVOID despite the sub-6.0 overall score. The FERC ROE resolution represents a genuine binary catalyst where Eversource's filed replacement ROE (11.39%) would, if approved, actually exceed the prior authorized return — creating upside that doesn't exist for most utility peers.

However, the negative estimate revision trend (-2.9% current year, -2.4% next year over 30 days), 3-of-4 quarterly misses, Revolution Wind liability escalation, and credit rating pressure prevent a constructive stance beyond income holding. At 15x forward with identifiable risks that could compress both the multiple and the earnings stream, there is insufficient margin of safety for capital appreciation confidence.

Hold for the 4.7% yield while monitoring FERC resolution and estimate stabilization as upgrade conditions.
