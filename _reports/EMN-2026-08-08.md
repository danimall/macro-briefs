## Executive Summary

Eastman delivered its first earnings beat in four quarters on July 30 — adjusted EPS of $1.97 against a $1.80 non-GAAP consensus — and the shares have risen 8.4% from $67.95 pre-print to $73.50. The segment composition of that beat is the entire analytical question. Chemical Intermediates, the commodity olefins-derivative and plasticizer business, swung to +$58M of adjusted EBIT from −$30M a year earlier on 39% sales growth (volume/mix +24%, price +14%). Management's own attribution in the 10-Q and press release is "tightening market conditions due to the ongoing Middle East conflict" plus supply disruptions. Consolidated operating-segment adjusted EBIT rose only $29M ($354M vs $325M) — less than CI's $88M swing — because Advanced Materials EBIT fell 10% and Additives & Functional Products fell 1%. Fibers, historically the highest-margin franchise, fell 56% to $36M. Management then guided CI to "modestly decline as the impact of supply chain disruptions lessens" and consolidated Q3 adjusted EPS merely "to approach" $1.97.

Behind the improved headline, the underlying trajectory has not turned. First-half adjusted EPS is $3.06 versus $3.51, down 13%; first-half adjusted EBIT is down 11%. Roughly $40M of pre-tax H1 benefit is either non-repeating ($24M of IEEPA tariff refunds recognized in cost of sales after the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling) or non-cash (a ~$9M-per-quarter prior service credit from a 2025 retiree benefit plan amendment), and neither is excluded from "adjusted" figures. Most tellingly, management raised the earnings outlook while cutting operating cash flow guidance to approach $900M from approaching FY2025's $970M, citing receivables pressure. Accounts receivable rose from $737M to $1,097M in six months; the H1 receivables cash use was $369M against $183M a year earlier; and the factoring add-back grew to $406M from $346M, so economic days-sales-outstanding is nearer 54 days than the 40 the reported balance implies.

The balance sheet constrains everything else. Net debt rose ~$305M in the first half to $4,526M, about 3.26x LTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,387M. The $1.5B revolver was amended in December 2025 to strip sustainability-linked pricing and again in February 2026 to extend maturity to 2031 and "temporarily adjust the maximum leverage ratio covenant through the fiscal quarter ending June 30, 2027 in the event of further macroeconomic uncertainty impacting operating results." Eastman was in compliance at both 12/31/25 and 6/30/26, holds $691M of cash against an undrawn revolver, and has no commercial paper outstanding — so this is not distress. It is, however, consistent with the observed capital allocation: capex cut to ~$400M, a token 1.2% dividend increase to $0.84 quarterly, and zero share repurchases in the first half (treasury shares unchanged at 109,891,531) despite buybacks still being listed as a 2026 priority. We move to REDUCE: at 10.6x FY2027 consensus with a 4.56% yield the stock is not expensive, but the forward multiple is inflated by a windfall management says fades, and the risk/reward from $73.50 is skewed against owners.

## Company Overview

Eastman is a Kingsport, Tennessee specialty materials producer, public since 1993, operating 36 manufacturing sites plus four manufacturing joint ventures across 12 countries with roughly 13,000 employees. The business is organized around four feedstock streams — polyester; cellulosic biopolymer and acetyl (gasification-based, with high-sulfur coal a key input); alkylamines; and olefins — rather than around end markets. That vertical integration at a small number of very large sites produces genuine cost advantage and equally genuine operating leverage in both directions. Raw materials and energy were roughly 45% of total cost of operations in 2025, and 55% of revenue was generated outside the US and Canada.

Four reportable segments (FY2025 sales / adjusted EBIT): Advanced Materials $2,880M / $349M (interlayers, performance films, specialty plastics; transportation and building end markets); Additives & Functional Products $2,880M / $516M (care and coatings additives, functional amines, Therminol and Skydrol specialty fluids); Chemical Intermediates $1,925M / −$38M; and Fibers $1,050M / $285M (acetate tow, Naia textiles, nonwovens). Corporate and growth initiatives absorbed −$182M, reconciling to $930M of total adjusted EBIT.

Customer concentration is low at the corporate level — top 100 customers ≈60% of FY2025 sales, none ≥10% — but within Fibers the ten largest customers represent roughly 65% of segment revenue, a concentrated book of global and regional cigarette producers. The strategic differentiator is molecular recycling: the Kingsport methanolysis plant, described by the company as the world's largest polyester molecular recycling facility, produced roughly 2.5x more in 2025 than 2024, and circular platform revenue doubled in H1 2026 (reported growth of more than $100M, split between specialty and rPET).

## Financial Analysis

**Three years of top-line contraction.** Revenue has fallen from $10,580M (2022) to $9,210M, $9,382M and $8,752M — down 17.3% from the peak. The FY2025 7% decline decomposed as volume/mix −6%, price −1%, FX flat, so this is a volume problem. Adjusted EBIT, the measure Eastman's compensation committee selects, has fallen from $1,635M (2021) to $930M (2025), a 43% decline. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $4.10 against $7.67; adjusted EPS $5.42 against $7.89.

**A data-quality note.** An automated screen returned FY2025 operating margin of −1.0% and EBIT/assets of −0.006. Rebuilding from the income statement — revenue $8,752M less COGS $6,908M less SG&A $658M less R&D $255M = +$931M, against reported GAAP EBIT of $776M (8.9% of sales) after non-core charges — those figures are XBRL tagging artifacts, not economic facts. Correcting the EBIT term in an Altman Z calculation (working capital/TA 0.0668; retained earnings/TA 0.680; EBIT/TA 0.0522; market equity/liabilities ≈0.95; sales/TA 0.589) produces Z ≈ 2.4, still in the grey zone and close to the screen's 2.67. The conclusion — neither safe nor distressed — is unchanged.

**The Q2 print and its quality.** Sales $2,513M (+10% YoY, +15% sequentially); GAAP EBIT $311M (+40%); adjusted EBIT $320M (+16%); adjusted EPS $1.97 (+23%). Sequential adjusted EBIT margin improved 350bp on 8% higher volume/mix. Set against that: H1 adjusted EBIT $520M vs $586M, H1 adjusted EPS $3.06 vs $3.51. The half-year is the more reliable read of run-rate, and it is down.

**Cash conversion is the sharpest tension.** Q2 operating cash flow was $224M, *below* Q2 2025's $233M, despite GAAP EBIT rising 40%. H1 OCF was $87M against $203M of capex, producing roughly −$113M of H1 free cash flow while $192M of dividends were paid, funded by the Q1 issuance of $600M of 4.5% notes due 2031. This should not be annualized: Eastman discloses that operating cash flow is structurally H2-weighted, and FY2025's $970M against H1 2025's $66M implies $904M of H2 2025 OCF, or 93% of the year. H1 2026 OCF was actually better than H1 2025. The material new information is not the seasonal shape but the guidance cut — OCF now "to approach $900 million," roughly $70M below the prior framing, attributed specifically to receivables.

**Derived leverage and coverage.** LTM adjusted EBIT = $930M − $586M + $520M = $864M; LTM D&A ≈ $523M; LTM adjusted EBITDA ≈ $1,387M. Net debt $4,526M / $1,387M = 3.26x. EV = $8,400M + $5,217M − $691M = $12,926M, or 9.3x LTM adjusted EBITDA. Interest coverage ≈ 4.1x. Near-term maturities — €-denominated 1.875% notes due November 2026 ($569M) and 7.6% debentures due February 2027 ($196M) — are manageable against $691M of cash plus a fully undrawn $1.5B revolver.

**Capital returns.** The dividend is $3.36 annualized, a 4.56% yield at $73.50, costing roughly $384M. FY2025 coverage from free cash flow was $424M/$381M ≈ 1.11x. On FY2026 guidance ($900M OCF less $400M capex) coverage improves to roughly 1.30x, leaving about $116M for buybacks *and* deleveraging combined — which is why buybacks are zero. Cumulative repurchases under the 2021 authorization total 13,032,926 shares for $1.2B, an average of ~$92.08 against today's $73.50, roughly 20% underwater in aggregate. The 2025 tranche (1,420,768 shares for $100M ≈ $70.38) was executed below the current price, so recent execution was better than the cumulative record.

**Other balance-sheet items.** Goodwill and intangibles of $4,585M equal 75% of $6,096M of Eastman stockholders' equity. The environmental remediation reserve is $321M within a disclosed range of $287M–$524M, accrued at the low end, leaving ~$237M unreserved; environmental cash expenditures were $339M in 2025, $307M in 2024, $314M in 2023. A supplier finance program carried $138M of confirmed obligations at 6/30/26, up from $110M. Roughly 3,987,014 options were excluded as antidilutive in Q2 (up from 3,264,994) because strikes exceed market — not current dilution, but if the stock recovered above those strikes the incremental ~4.0M shares equals ~3.5% of shares outstanding, leaving existing holders with ~96.6% of the company.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, and each has qualifications.

**Cost reduction.** Management reaffirmed $125M–$150M of savings net of inflation as "on track" and states it can "significantly improve earnings in 2026 versus 2025." Building from actuals: H1 $3.06 + Q3 guided ~$1.97 + a seasonally weaker unguided Q4 of ~$1.30–1.50 gives roughly $6.33–6.53, consistent with consensus of $6.39. That is +17% on FY2025's $5.42. The caveat is that this path requires the CI spread benefit to persist through Q4 at reduced levels; full reversion to CI's FY2025 loss-making run rate would take it materially lower.

**Circular economy.** Kingsport methanolysis is operating at scale, H1 circular revenue doubled, and the reported plan to debottleneck to 130% of capacity is inherently more capital-efficient than greenfield expansion. But the DOE terminated its award for the Longview, Texas PET recycling decarbonization project on May 29, 2025; Eastman has appealed, outcome unresolved. Capex is cut to ~$400M and described as "maintenance capital and limited growth capital for projects already in progress," and the CEO's reference to "a capital-efficient set of options to serve the packaging market" is consistent with a pivot away from a second full plant. The Other segment still absorbed $178M of growth-initiative losses in FY2025, improving to −$72M in H1 2026 from −$103M.

**Segment mix.** AFP was the only FY2025 grower (+5% adjusted EBIT) and is now the largest contributor at $516M, 46% of operating-segment adjusted EBIT. Its stability derives from cost-pass-through contracts, which dampen both downside and upside. Fibers serves cigarette filters, an end market in structural volume decline in developed regions; Eastman's response — long-term variable pricing and repurposing tow capacity into Naia textiles — is sensible but does not create growth.

**Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call.** Current-year consensus is $6.39 (+1.2% over 30 days, +0.1% over 90 days); next-year $6.93 (−0.1%/−1.1%); next quarter $1.39, revised down 5.4% in 30 days and 8.3% in 90 days. Characterizing this as "broadly rising" overstates it: the aggregate is flat, and the nearest quarter is deteriorating. A large positive ninety-day revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection is nowhere present. The earnings record — one beat and three misses in four quarters, including −40.6% in Q1 2026 and −30.0% in Q4 2025 — argues for modest confidence in consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.50: trailing GAAP EPS $3.84 → 19.1x; LTM adjusted EPS $4.97 → 14.8x; FY2026 consensus $6.39 → 11.5x; FY2027 consensus $6.93 → 10.6x; EV/LTM adjusted EBITDA 9.3x; dividend yield 4.56%.

Against peers, the picture is less compelling than the forward multiple suggests. The stated peer median P/E of 33.9 is distorted by Element Solutions at 69.9 and Westlake at −6.9. The cleanest structural comparables are NewMarket at 19.2x and Axalta at 19.1x — essentially identical to Eastman's 19.1x trailing GAAP. Eastman's 4.56% yield versus a 0.8% peer median is the widest gap in the group, and a yield that far above the cohort is a market judgment about growth and payout durability as much as a value signal.

**The central valuation issue is what earnings base to capitalize.** Q2 CI adjusted EBIT of +$58M compares with −$30M a year prior and −$38M for all of FY2025. If CI reverts toward breakeven as the supply disruption abates — which management guides toward, beginning in Q3 — the annualized removal is plausibly $150M–$200M of EBIT, roughly $0.90–$1.20 of after-tax EPS. Layer in the ~$0.18 of pre-tax EPS from non-repeating IEEPA tariff refunds. Underlying earnings power ex-windfall is therefore closer to $5.20–$5.50, near FY2025's $5.42, which puts the stock at 13.4–14.1x normalized rather than 11.5x forward. That is a fair multiple for a 3.3x-levered cyclical with a declining top line — not a mispricing.

Sell-side targets are uniformly above spot: low $74.00, mean $81.60, median $80.00, high $100.00, with a stable 3 strong buy / 6 buy / 6 hold distribution unchanged over four months. The mean implies 11.0% price upside. Analyst targets deserve weight as corroboration where a stock is cheap against rising estimates; here estimates are flat to falling, so the targets read as fair-value anchors rather than stale floors.

**Scenario construction (all from $73.50):**

- **Base $78 (+6.1%).** FY2027 adjusted EPS of roughly $6.40 — below the $6.93 consensus to reflect partial CI normalization, offset by full-year cost savings and Fibers volume recovery — at 12.2x. Plus 4.6% dividend, total ~10.7%.
- **Bull $97 (+32.0%).** Sustained transit disruption keeps CI spreads wide, Fibers tow pricing stabilizes and volumes normalize, cost savings land at the high end, working capital releases and buybacks resume. Approximately $7.20 of EPS at 13.5x. This is essentially the sell-side high of $100.
- **Bear $54 (−26.5%).** Middle East de-escalation removes the CI benefit, Fibers contract pricing proves a structural reset, performance-films goodwill is impaired, and covenant headroom is consumed. Roughly $4.70–$5.00 of EPS at 11x, near the 52-week low of $56.11.

Weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +4.4% price and ~8.4% total return; a 30% bear weight — defensible given the Middle East reversal is a live and specifically identified catalyst — gives roughly +1.5% price and ~5.4% total return. Against a required return near 10-11% for a levered cyclical whose peer beta median is 1.28, both fall short, and the asymmetry (−26.5% bear against +6.1% base) is unfavorable.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory, not level, is what matters here, and it is mixed to negative outside AFP.

**AFP is where the moat holds.** It grew adjusted EBIT 5% in FY2025 — the only segment to grow — and held Q2 EBIT roughly flat at a 18.7% margin, with growth cited in water treatment, medical/pharma and electronics. Cost-pass-through contracting explains the stability. Specialty fluids face few named competitors (Dow, ExxonMobil), and Skydrol serves a qualification-gated aviation market.

**Advanced Materials and Chemical Intermediates are commoditizing at the margin.** Saflex interlayers compete not only with Sekisui and Kuraray but with Kingboard/Fo Gang and Chang Chun. The FY2025 CI collapse was attributed to "competitive pressure in Asia" ($121M of lower selling prices plus higher raws), and EMEA and APAC revenue each fell 10% against a 3% decline in US/Canada. Eastman's marketed advantage is shifting from technology differentiation toward regional supply security — "significant and advantaged integrated U.S.-based assets" — and Q2 regional data support the reframing: US/Canada sales +20%, EMEA +3%, APAC −1%. Performance films competes against XPEL, 3M and Saint-Gobain in the consumer-discretionary auto aftermarket.

**Fibers is the unresolved question.** Adjusted EBIT margin has gone 34.4% (FY2024) → 27.1% (FY2025) → 14.8% (Q2 2026), and Fibers' share of operating-segment adjusted EBIT has fallen from 30.1% to 10.2%. FY2025 drivers were destocking, "industry capacity share adjustments," and tariff-hit textiles. Q2 2026 adds "modestly lower acetate tow contract pricing." Volume destocking is cyclical; contract price concessions in a four-competitor oligopoly (Celanese, Cerdia, Daicel, Jinan Acetate) with ten customers at ~65% of segment revenue are harder to reverse. We could not corroborate what "industry capacity share adjustments" means — whether Eastman lost share, a competitor added capacity, or the industry rationalized. Management's Q3 guide for higher Fibers earnings "with customers increasing purchases to meet their annual contracts" is a genuine mitigant implying intra-year timing rather than permanent demand loss. This distinction is the single most decision-relevant open item, and it is unresolved.

The IP position is broad but explicitly not concentrated: over 700 active US patents, 1,600 foreign patents, 4,500 trademarks, and the company states its business "is not substantially dependent upon any one particular patent."

## Risk Assessment

**Chemical Intermediates reversal.** The Q2 improvement is exogenous. The working case is sustained Middle East supply disruption, but a de-escalation would remove Eastman's CI benefit. Note the two-sided exposure: the conflict widens CI spreads while raising Fibers logistics costs, so de-escalation removes the benefit while only partly relieving the cost.

**Performance-films goodwill.** The 10-K discloses that fair value significantly exceeded carrying value for all quantitatively tested reporting units *except* performance films, which carries $812M of goodwill (≈13.3% of stockholders' equity). Independent 50bp sensitivities — lower long-term growth, higher WACC — each still cleared, so a cushion exists, but it is narrow, and the exposure is to a consumer-discretionary auto aftermarket. An impairment would be non-cash and would not affect adjusted EBIT or an EBITDA-defined covenant, but would reduce book equity at a moment when leverage tests already carry negotiated temporary relief.

**Covenant relief through mid-2027.** Eastman is in compliance and liquid. But obtaining prospective headroom against "further macroeconomic uncertainty impacting operating results" indicates the company and its lenders modeled scenarios in which the existing maximum leverage ratio could be pressured. It is defensible, cheap insurance bundled with a maturity extension — and it also constrains the dividend/buyback/deleveraging trilemma, which is the observed behavior.

**Dividend coverage.** $384M annual cost against $424M FY2025 FCF (1.11x) and ~$500M FY2026E FCF (1.30x). Coverage is adequate on guidance but leaves little cushion. The 1.2% increase signals the board's own read.

**Environmental and legacy.** Reserve of $321M within a $287M–$524M range, accrued at the low end; ~$237M unreserved. Persistent cash drain of $307M–$339M annually, of which $65M–$80M is capex. Non-environmental asset retirement obligations add $57M.

**Feedstock and tax.** Raw materials and energy ≈45% of cost of operations, with the cellulosic/acetyl stream dependent on wood pulp, methanol and high-sulfur coal. The FY2025 adjusted effective tax rate of 13% was aided by reserve releases; the Q2 2026 GAAP rate jumped to 28% from 17% under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and $731M of deferred tax valuation allowances are outstanding.

**Disclosure lag.** Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q reports no changes from the 10-K, so the risk disclosure has not been updated to reflect either the Fibers deterioration or the covenant amendment as standalone risks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term positioning is complacent, which sharpens the case for trimming rather than adding. ATM IV collapsed from 45.2% on July 30 to 31.9% post-print, against 33.8% one-year realized — a fair-to-slightly-cheap spread and a 13th-percentile reading within the current chain. The nearest expiry prices a ±5.1% move with balanced skew (−0.0%) and a put/call volume ratio of 0.77, while put/call open interest fell from 0.26 to 0.06, indicating almost no standing put protection after the event. Term structure is in contango — 31.9% at one month rising to 37.0% at December — and the December tenor carries a −2.5% skew with a 2.81 put/call volume ratio, so what hedging demand exists is concentrated in the longer tenor. Short interest is unremarkable at 6.3% of float and 4.1 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction. The read: the options market is pricing no near-term event risk into a quarter where management has already guided the primary earnings driver to decline, and the cost of downside protection into December is where the market locates the risk. That divergence supports reducing exposure at spot rather than waiting.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($97, +32%)

Cost savings of $125M–$150M net of inflation are real, structural and only partly in the H1 numbers, and they land against a revenue base that has already absorbed three years of volume decline. AFP's cost-pass-through franchise generates $516M of stable EBIT — 46% of operating-segment profit — with growth in water treatment, medical and electronics. Fibers' Q2 shortfall proves to be intra-year contract timing plus operating deleverage, both reversible, exactly as management's Q3 guide implies, and tow pricing stabilizes after a "modest" reset. Advanced Materials improves on price-cost and utilization. CI holds a portion of its benefit because management attributes part of the gain to genuinely higher volume availability from company-specific capacity and reliability improvements, not price alone. Working capital releases in H2, delivering ~$900M of OCF and ~$500M of FCF, leverage falls back toward 3.0x, and buybacks resume below the $92.08 cumulative average cost. Circular platform revenue, having doubled in H1, compounds off a debottlenecked Kingsport at lower capital intensity than a second plant. On roughly $7.20 of FY2027 EPS at 13.5x, plus a 4.6% yield, the shares reach the sell-side high near $100.

### Bear Case ($54, −27%)

Middle East supply disruption normalizes, CI reverts to the loss-making run rate that produced −$38M of FY2025 EBIT, and $150M–$200M of annualized EBIT disappears. The $24M IEEPA benefit does not repeat. Fibers' contract pricing proves a structural reset in a four-competitor oligopoly serving a secularly declining end market, permanently impairing what was a 34%-margin business. Performance-films goodwill of $812M is impaired, cutting book equity ~13% and consuming covenant headroom that already required temporary relief through Q2 2027. Receivables pressure persists rather than reversing, OCF undershoots $900M, and FCF fails to cover the $384M dividend, forcing a choice between the payout and deleveraging at 3.3x. Earnings settle near $4.70–$5.00 at 11x. The 52-week low of $56.11 is the reference point, and a dividend freeze or cut would take it lower.

### What Would Change Our Mind

The bull case requires evidence we do not yet have: two consecutive quarters of Fibers margin recovery with tow pricing described as stable, and specialty-segment EBIT growth that does not depend on commodity spreads. Both are testable at the October print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Horizon: 6–12 months, with the October 2026 Q3 report the first decision point.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the Q3 print. The shares are up 8.4% since July 30 on the segment management has already guided lower, and the December options tenor shows where hedging demand sits. For income-oriented holders, retaining a partial position for the 4.56% yield is defensible given 1.30x guided FCF coverage; we would not carry a full-weight position through a quarter in which the primary earnings driver is guided to decline and next-quarter consensus has fallen 5.4% in 30 days. Prospective buyers should wait — a price in the low $60s (roughly 11x normalized $5.60 earnings, a ~5.4% yield) would restore the margin of safety that $73.50 does not offer.

**Base case target: $78 (+6.1% price, ~10.7% total return with dividend).** FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$6.40 — below the $6.93 consensus to reflect partial CI normalization, above the ex-windfall run rate to credit cost savings and Fibers volume recovery — at 12.2x.

**Bull case: $97 (+32%).** Bear case: **$54 (−26.5%)**.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. Fibers adjusted EBIT margin recovering above 22% for two consecutive quarters with management characterizing acetate tow contract pricing as stable — this resolves the structural-versus-cyclical question.
2. H2 2026 operating cash flow delivering ~$813M (to reach the ~$900M full-year guide from H1's $87M) with the receivables balance declining and the factoring add-back falling below $346M.
3. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA below 3.0x with buybacks resumed, and the credit facility's temporary leverage covenant adjustment allowed to lapse rather than being extended.
4. Advanced Materials and AFP together growing adjusted EBIT year over year, demonstrating specialty earnings independent of commodity spreads.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A performance-films goodwill impairment charge against the $812M balance, particularly if accompanied by any change in covenant terms.
2. Sustained Middle East de-escalation coinciding with CI adjusted EBIT returning below breakeven, without offsetting Fibers and AM recovery.
3. FY2026 operating cash flow guided or reported below $850M, which would put FCF near or below the $384M dividend.
4. Any further amendment extending or widening the leverage covenant relief beyond Q2 2027, or a credit rating outlook change.
5. Fibers adjusted EBIT margin below 14% in Q3 despite management's guide for higher Fibers earnings on annual contract purchases.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report in late October is the decisive test: it will show whether Fibers recovered on contract timing as guided, how much of the CI benefit persisted, whether the H2 working-capital release materialized, and whether buybacks resumed. The November 2026 €569M maturity is a secondary checkpoint on refinancing cost.

## Conclusion

This is our fourth consecutive rating on Eastman and the third consecutive decline in the composite score (5.3 → 4.7 → 4.4 → 4.5, essentially flat this cycle). The Q2 beat is the first genuinely positive data point in a year, and it is right to mark the earnings-improvement confirmation and reaffirmed cost savings as incremental positives. But the beat's composition undermines its signal value: CI's $88M swing exceeded the entire $29M consolidated operating-segment improvement, management attributes it to Middle East supply tightness, and management guides it to fade beginning in Q3. Both specialty segments' EBIT declined. First-half adjusted EPS remains down 13%. Roughly $40M of pre-tax H1 benefit is non-repeating or non-cash and sits inside "adjusted" figures. Earnings guidance went up while cash flow guidance went down, and the gap is receivables.

The valuation looks supportive at 11.5x FY2026 and 10.6x FY2027 consensus with a 4.56% yield, and every sell-side target sits above spot. On normalized earnings power of roughly $5.20–$5.50 ex-windfall, the multiple is 13–14x — fair for a 3.3x-levered cyclical with a three-year revenue decline, a covenant temporarily loosened through mid-2027, $812M of goodwill the 10-K itself flags as the sole reporting unit without a comfortable impairment cushion, no insider open-market buying with the stock 12% off its 52-week high, and a Fibers franchise whose margin has halved with an admitted contract price concession we cannot yet classify as cyclical or structural.

Expected total return of roughly 5–9% against a required return near 10–11%, with a base case of +6% versus a bear case of −27%, does not justify full-weight ownership. REDUCE. Existing holders should trim into the post-print strength and retain only what the dividend justifies; prospective buyers should wait for either the low $60s or two quarters of evidence that Fibers and the specialty segments — not the commodity book — are carrying the earnings.
