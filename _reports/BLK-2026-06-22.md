**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BlackRock manages $13.9 trillion of assets as of March 31, 2026, up from $11.6 trillion a year earlier, and remains the structural winner in the two highest-growth corners of asset management: index ETFs and embedded technology. The investment case rests on a deliberate mix shift: management is using three large acquisitions (GIP in infrastructure, Preqin in private-markets data, and HPS in private credit) to move the revenue base toward higher-fee, stickier private assets and recurring technology revenue, insulating the franchise from index fee compression. Q1 2026 revenue rose 27% YoY to $6.70B, as-adjusted operating margin expanded to 44.5%, and the firm has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters.

The complication is that the headline numbers require careful translation. GAAP diluted EPS of $14.06 (+46%) was inflated by a $549M non-cash gain on contingent-consideration revaluation, a gain that exists precisely because BLK's share price fell during the quarter, lowering the value of stock- and unit-settled earnout liabilities. The operative figure is as-adjusted EPS of $12.53 (+11%). The same acquisitions that drive the growth story are also a dilution engine: roughly 7.6M Subco Units already sit in the diluted count, with up to ~13.8M additional potential shares from HPS and 4–5M from GIP earnouts, meaning the active buyback is running partly to offset acquisition issuance rather than to shrink the share count.

At ~$1,053, BLK trades at roughly 20x as-adjusted forward EPS (consensus current-year ~$53.17, next-year ~$60.72), a discount to the alternatives-manager peer set (Blackstone 31x, KKR 33x, Apollo 85x) but a premium to the custody banks (BNY 17x, State Street 17x). The stock has been range-bound to lower over the past year, down ~14% from its October high of $1,219.94, while estimates have drifted modestly lower (current-year EPS revised -2.0% over 90 days). We rate BLK a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the franchise quality and mix-shift thesis are intact, but the combination of earnout-driven dilution, a live antitrust case, and stalled estimate revisions argues against chasing the name at current levels rather than accumulating on weakness.

## Company Overview

BlackRock is the world's largest investment manager, operating as a single asset-management segment serving institutional and retail clients across 100+ countries with ~25,400 employees. The platform comprises:

- **iShares ETFs** — the growth engine, $5.49T AUM, roughly 40% of fee revenue and ~$2.4B/quarter of base fees. ETFs drove $132B of net inflows in Q1 2026 and $551B over the trailing twelve months.
- **Index/institutional** — large but low-fee; $3.56T of institutional index AUM that is in persistent net outflow (-$108B TTM), concentrated in low-margin equity and fixed income.
- **Active strategies** — equity, fixed income, multi-asset (LifePath target-date a notable winner).
- **Private markets & alternatives** — the strategic pivot: $429B alternatives AUM, of which $320B is private markets.
- **Technology** — Aladdin, eFront, Preqin, and Cachematrix generate ~$530M/quarter (~$2.1B+ annualized) of high-margin recurring revenue, growing ~22% YoY.

The business is a fee-on-AUM model: roughly 92% of fees are AUM-based, so revenue is heavily levered to market levels and net flows. The moat rests on scale economics in indexing/ETFs, the Aladdin risk-management platform (an industry standard with high switching costs because it is embedded in clients' operational workflows), and a fiduciary-only model with no proprietary trading conflicting with clients.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (vs. Q1 2025):**
- Total revenue $6.70B, +27% YoY, driven by market beta on average AUM, organic base-fee growth, ~$230M of HPS fees, and a full quarter of Preqin (~$65M).
- GAAP operating income $2.81B; GAAP operating margin 42.0% (vs. 32.2%). As-adjusted margin 44.5% (vs. 43.2%), the cleaner read after stripping non-cash acquisition items.
- GAAP diluted EPS $14.06 (+46%); as-adjusted EPS $12.53 (+11%).

The GAAP/as-adjusted gap is the single most important interpretive point on this name. The $549M positive swing from contingent-consideration revaluation flattered GAAP EPS because BLK's stock fell during the quarter, reducing the value of stock- and unit-settled GIP and HPS earnout liabilities. In a quarter where the stock rises, this line reverses into an expense. Any screen keying off GAAP EPS will be systematically misled; the as-adjusted $12.53 is the operative number.

Revenue quality is improving at the margin. Performance fees jumped to $272M (from $60M), led by private markets/HPS, which is higher-quality but lumpier. Q1 operating cash flow was seasonally negative (-$980M GAAP, +$583M excluding consolidated investment products) due to payment of prior-year incentive comp, which is normal seasonality rather than deterioration.

The balance sheet is strong: net debt-to-EBITDA below 1.0x against a 3.5x covenant cap, $12.85B of long-term debt at low fixed coupons, $9.4B accessible cash, and a $6.3B undrawn revolver (upsized from $5.9B and extended to 2031 in March 2026). Book value/share is $364.87.

One screening caveat deserves explicit mention because it is a recurring trap on this issuer: any Altman Z-Score flagging BLK as "distressed" is a false negative. The reported ~$170B balance sheet is inflated by ~$65B of pass-through separate-account assets (UK pension structures) and ~$10B of consolidated investment products, both with equal-and-offsetting liabilities and zero economic interest to BLK. The economically relevant balance sheet is closer to $100B, and leverage is trivial. We score financial health accordingly, weighting the firm's resilience and capital structure rather than the mis-applied manufacturing-firm metric.

## Growth Analysis

Headline AUM growth of $13.9T (from $11.6T) overstates organic momentum because it captures both market beta and acquisition consolidation. Q1 2026 itself illustrates the beta dependence: $130B of inflows was more than offset by $216B of net market depreciation and $50B of FX drag, cutting AUM by $147B sequentially. Underneath the headline, growth is bifurcating in a way that is central to the thesis:

- **Thriving:** iShares ETFs (+$551B TTM), private markets (now 12% of base fees on ~2% of AUM, generating $232M of Q1's $272M private-markets performance fees), and technology services (+22% YoY). Digital-asset ETPs are now a real line item at $60.7B AUM with +$32B net inflows TTM, a genuine but volatile new vector.
- **Bleeding:** legacy institutional index AUM (-$108B TTM, including a single client's $52B partial redemption in Q2 2025), facing fee compression.

The deliberate logic is to shift mix toward higher-fee, stickier, performance-fee-bearing assets, accepting outflows in the low-fee book. This is a sound multi-year strategy and the durable component of the growth case. The risk is that BlackRock is buying into private credit and infrastructure late in the cycle and at full valuations, with the integration and fundraising-milestone execution still unproven.

The estimate-revision picture is the reason we do not classify this as a BUY. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised -2.0% over 90 days and is flat over 30 days; next-year -1.8%/+0.1%. This is stable-to-modestly-negative, not the +30% revision velocity that would signal a systematic underestimate of an earnings inflection. Growth here is durable and high-quality but not accelerating, which is the correct framing for an asset manager whose revenue tracks broad market levels. The four-quarter beat streak (7.5%, 6.7%, 1.7%, 10.3%) supports reasonable confidence in the consensus path, but the recent quarters' beats are partly market-beta surprises rather than structural acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,053 and as-adjusted forward EPS of ~$53.17 (current year) and ~$60.72 (next year), BLK trades at roughly 19.8x current-year and ~17.3x next-year as-adjusted earnings. The trailing P/E of 26.4x reflects GAAP earnings distorted by acquisition accounting and is not the right anchor.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. BLK is materially cheaper than the alternatives managers it is now competing with in private markets (Blackstone at 31x, KKR at 33x, Apollo at 85x; peer-median P/E 31x). That gap partly reflects business-model differences (the alternatives names carry more incentive-fee leverage and more redemption-gate risk), but it also suggests the market is not paying BlackRock a private-markets premium for the GIP/HPS pivot. BLK trades at a premium to the custody banks (BNY 17x, State Street 17x), justified by superior growth and margin.

The valuation is reasonable rather than asymmetric. The consensus targets (low $1,140, mean $1,246, median $1,250, high $1,393) imply roughly 8–32% upside, and the recommendation mix is constructive (4 strong-buy, 10 buy, 3 hold, with a recent shift toward more conviction). We are cautious about treating those targets as a floor: estimates have drifted lower, not higher, over 90 days, which is the condition under which consensus targets are stale on the downside, not the up. A ~20x forward multiple on an 11%-growing earnings stream (PEG ~1.8) is fair, not cheap. The buyback complicates per-share math: at $810M of repurchases against acquisition-related issuance, net share count is rising, so the multiple is not being mechanically supported by shrinkage. We score valuation a 6.0: defensible support and a peer discount, but no specific mispricing catalyst, and recent cycles caution against rewarding a "looks cheap vs. peers" read without an independent driver.

## Competitive Landscape

BlackRock's competitive position is strong and largely improving, with one structural headwind. In passive/ETFs it leads Vanguard and State Street, and iShares continues to take share ($551B TTM inflows). Aladdin remains an industry standard with genuine switching costs. The technology franchise, now bolstered by Preqin's private-markets data, is widening into the fastest-growing fee pool in the industry at ~22% YoY recurring growth.

The pivot into private markets repositions BlackRock from incumbent to challenger against Apollo, Blackstone, KKR, Ares, and Brookfield. This is the area of greatest uncertainty: BlackRock is buying its way in (GIP, HPS) rather than building organically, and it is doing so as the private-credit cycle matures. The strategic rationale is sound, since private markets carry far higher fee rates and produce the bulk of performance fees, but execution against fundraising and fee milestones is unproven, and the entry valuations were full.

The one clear deterioration is the institutional index book, where fee compression and concentration (a single $52B redemption) erode a low-margin base. Management is consciously trading this for higher-quality mix, which is the right call, but it leaves the franchise's organic flow profile more concentrated and lumpy. We trim competitive position from the franchise-peak readings of prior reports to 7.5, reflecting a still-wide moat in ETFs/technology offset by challenger status in private markets and a fragile low-fee base.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Acquisition integration and earnout/dilution overhang (dominant new risk).** Combined contingent consideration on the balance sheet is $7.9B (GIP $4.3B + HPS $3.4B), settleable in millions of shares and Subco Units. Integration failure would put ~$35.3B of goodwill and ~$27.7B of intangibles at impairment risk.

2. **Index fee compression and institutional outflows.** The institutional index outflows are explicitly concentrated in low-fee offerings, with single-client concentration risk demonstrated by the $52B redemption.

3. **Market beta dependence.** ~92% of fees are AUM-based. A sustained equity drawdown compresses revenue directly, as Q1's market depreciation showed.

4. **Antitrust/political litigation.** The 13-state AG suit (E.D. Texas) alleging coal-supply suppression via index holdings largely survived a motion to dismiss in 2025, with four states adding consumer-protection claims. This is a novel theory of liability for passive ownership with potential industry-wide implications, and it is a live tail risk for an index manager.

5. **Private-credit cyclicality.** The HPS bet ($165B AUM added) increases exposure to a late-cycle private-credit market; a credit downturn would hit fundraising, the now-material performance-fee line, and the HPS earnout valuation simultaneously.

We score risk factors a 5.5. These are real, partly idiosyncratic, and not fully priced, particularly the dilution mechanics (which the GAAP accounting obscures) and the antitrust case. None is acutely binary in the next two quarters, which keeps the score from going lower.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.9% sits above 1-year HV of 26.1% (a 6.8pp rich spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 32.9% > 6-month 26.8%), indicating the market is pricing some imminent event risk. Put/call skew is balanced (2.5%) and the put/call OI ratio is benign at 0.72, so this is elevated near-term uncertainty rather than a directional bearish signal. IV has collapsed sharply from 47.5% to 32.9% since late May, suggesting a prior stress event has resolved. The 6-month skew is mildly negative (-2.1%), consistent with modest downside hedging into a wider-uncertainty window. On balance, options positioning is neutral-to-slightly-cautious and does not contradict a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,250–1,393).** The mix shift works: iShares and private markets compound, technology revenue scales toward $2.5B+ annualized, performance fees become a durable contributor, and rising markets lift the AUM toll road. As-adjusted EPS reaches the high-$50s to low-$60s, and the market awards a private-markets premium that narrows the gap to Blackstone/KKR multiples. The earnout dilution is absorbed by earnings growth, and the antitrust case is dismissed or settled immaterially.

**Bear case (~$880–900).** A sustained equity/credit drawdown compresses AUM-based revenue; private-credit fundraising stalls and HPS underperforms its milestones, triggering goodwill/intangible impairment; institutional outflows accelerate beyond the low-fee book; and an adverse antitrust ruling forces costly changes to index-ownership practices. EPS stalls in the low-$50s and the multiple compresses toward custody-bank levels. This is roughly a 15–17% decline from current levels.

The base case sits between: as-adjusted EPS of ~$53 in 2026 growing to ~$60 in 2027, a ~20x multiple holding, and a fair value of ~$1,150–1,180.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (mid-July), with particular attention to private-markets fee-paying AUM growth, HPS milestone progress, and the trajectory of institutional index outflows.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision velocity turns positive (current-year as-adjusted EPS revised up >2% over 90 days), confirming the mix shift is accelerating earnings rather than treading market beta.
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $900s (~10–14% lower) that restores a clear discount to fair value while fundamentals remain intact.
- Resolution of the 13-state antitrust case on immaterial terms, removing the most novel tail risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse antitrust ruling that forces structural changes to index-ownership practices or imposes material liability.
- Evidence of HPS/GIP underperformance against fundraising/fee milestones that points toward goodwill/intangible impairment.
- Acceleration of institutional outflows beyond the low-fee book into core ETF or active franchises, signaling the mix shift is failing.

## Conclusion

BlackRock is a high-quality franchise executing a coherent and largely successful pivot away from the structurally challenged low-fee index business and toward higher-fee private markets and technology. The financials are strong once translated out of acquisition-accounting noise, the balance sheet is conservatively levered, and the four-quarter beat streak supports confidence in the consensus path. At ~20x as-adjusted forward earnings, the valuation is reasonable and carries a defensible discount to alternatives-manager peers.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of an independent re-rating catalyst combined with three live frictions: earnout-driven dilution that the GAAP numbers obscure, a novel antitrust case that has survived dismissal, and estimate revisions that are flat-to-modestly-negative rather than accelerating. We would become buyers on weakness toward the low-to-mid $900s or on a positive turn in revision velocity, and we would step aside on an adverse antitrust ruling or evidence of acquisition underperformance. For now, BlackRock is worth holding for its compounding, high-quality franchise and its constructive 12-month setup, with the recognition that the easy multiple expansion has already happened and the next leg requires the private-markets bet to prove out.
