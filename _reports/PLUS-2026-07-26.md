**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ePlus is a mid-tier IT value-added reseller and solutions integrator that just completed a structural transformation: on June 30, 2025 it sold its domestic financing business to PEAC Solutions for ~$180M, leaving a pure-play technology solutions company across three segments (Product, Professional Services, Managed Services). The reshaped business posted a record FY2026 — net sales up 22.1% to $2,442.5M, operating income up 66.7% to $166.1M, and diluted EPS from continuing operations of $4.71 — with genuine operating leverage as the operating margin expanded roughly 200bps. It is an Information Technology name riding the AI-infrastructure demand wave, the sector where selection has historically added the most alpha.

The problem is that the quality of that growth is questionable and the forward trajectory is decelerating. The 22% top-line surge was disproportionately driven by a single fast-growing customer/vertical — Telecom/Media/Entertainment (Verizon-led) grew 58.8% to $720.6M, now ~29.5% of net sales and ~36% of trade accounts receivable. That same relationship largely explains the ballooning working capital: accounts receivable rose 31.4% to $667.8M and inventory rose 66.8% to $200.9M, driving nine-month operating cash flow to negative $223.4M versus +$186.6M a year earlier. Management's own FY2027 guidance of only mid-single-digit growth signals it does not expect the FY2026 surge to recur. Consensus estimates are falling across every window (next-year EPS revised down 20.7% over 90 days), and the current-quarter estimate has been cut 10.6% over 90 days.

The offsetting strength is a clean, essentially debt-free balance sheet ($410.8M cash, no funded long-term debt, interest-free floor-plan working-capital financing) that provides real downside cushion, plus a reasonable ~19x trailing/forward multiple and a growing dividend. This is a hold. The concentration risk and cash-flow divergence are material and specific; the balance sheet and modest valuation prevent it from being an AVOID. The score has declined steadily from 7.0 (April) to the mid-5s, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend: decelerating guidance and falling estimates now dominate the picture.

## Company Overview

ePlus (founded 1990, Herndon VA; fiscal year ends March 31) resells hardware, third-party software, and maintenance/subscriptions from thousands of manufacturers and layers professional and managed services on top. Core solution areas are networking, cloud/data center, security, collaboration, and increasingly AI infrastructure and AI advisory. It serves middle-market to large enterprises plus state/local government and education (SLED), primarily in the US, with smaller UK/EU/India/Singapore operations and ~2,150 employees.

The central strategic event was the June 30, 2025 divestiture of the domestic financing business. That segment had historically generated ~$28–44M of stable, high-margin, counter-cyclical gross profit per year. Selling it in exchange for ~$180.1M cash ($156.7M net of cash transferred, plus up to ~$13M in contingent earn-outs, already being written down toward $12.4M) converts ePlus into a cleaner, faster-growing, but more cyclical pure-play IT solutions provider. All prior periods have been recast to present financing as discontinued operations.

Competitively, ePlus is mid-tier scale in a fragmented market against CDW (~$21B revenue), Insight Enterprises, WWT, SHI, and Presidio. At ~$2.4B in revenue it is a fraction of CDW's size, so it competes on solution depth, engineering certifications, and customer intimacy rather than procurement scale. Its AI offerings are consulting, integration, and managed services layered on third-party vendor technology (Cisco, hyperscalers), not proprietary models.

## Financial Analysis

FY2026 (year ended March 31, 2026, continuing operations) was a strong year on the income statement:

- **Net sales +22.1% to $2,442.5M** (Product +23.8% to $1,979.3M; Services +15.6% to $462.9M)
- **Gross billings +17.0% to a record $3,838.5M** (net sales run below gross billings because much maintenance/subscription revenue is recognized on a net/agent basis)
- **Gross profit +20.3% to $616.1M; gross margin 25.2%** (down from 25.6% on product mix)
- **Operating income +66.7% to $166.1M**, operating margin ~6.8% (+~200bps) — meaningful operating leverage
- **Net earnings from continuing ops +62.4% to $124.1M; diluted EPS $4.71** (Non-GAAP $5.39)
- **Adjusted EBITDA +49.5% to $204.8M** (8.4% margin)

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Cash was $410.8M at March 2026 against total equity of $1,069.0M, with no funded long-term debt. Working capital is financed through a Wells Fargo (WFCDF) floor-plan facility ($500M limit, $119.7M drawn, interest-free when paid within 45–60 days) and an undrawn $200M revolver; the parent guaranty is only $10.5M. Goodwill sits at $202.9M and intangibles at $61.3M. For a thin-margin distributor, this leverage profile is atypically resilient and provides downside cushion and capital-return optionality.

The clear financial concern is cash-flow quality. Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was **negative $223.4M** (continuing ops) against +$186.6M in the prior-year period. The swing was driven by a $183.7M build in accounts receivable (AR-trade +31.4% YoY to $667.8M) and a $120.3M build in inventory (+66.8% to $200.9M). The cash conversion cycle deteriorated to 41 days at December 2025 from 32 days, with days-in-inventory rising from 13 to 22 on longer customer delivery schedules and projects-in-process. The ~$180M divestiture inflow largely masked this — cash grew only ~$21M for the year despite the sale, because working capital consumed most of the proceeds.

This build is plausibly benign — project timing on large enterprise/telecom work plus AI-driven memory pre-buys — but it is exactly the kind of divergence a headline earnings screen would miss. It could equally reflect revenue pull-forward or extended payment terms on the concentrated telecom business. The divergence between record GAAP earnings and deeply negative nine-month operating cash flow is the single most important item to diligence.

The earnings surprise record has weakened. The last four quarters were three beats and one in-line, but the beats were front-loaded: +58.7% (Sep 2025) and +40.7% (Dec 2025) gave way to exactly in-line ($1.00 vs $1.00) in March 2026. The deceleration in surprise magnitude tracks the deceleration in the underlying business.

## Growth Analysis

Reported FY2026 growth was strong but narrow. The Verizon-led TME vertical (up 58.8% to $720.6M, now ~29.5% of net sales from ~23% a year earlier) largely explains the 22% consolidated growth, the operating leverage, and the AR/inventory buildup. SLED revenue fell 7.4%, a reminder that growth is concentrated in commercial verticals exposed to government-budget cyclicality elsewhere.

The FY2027 guidance of mid-single-digit growth in net sales, gross profit, and Adjusted EBITDA is a sharp step-down from ~20%+. This implicitly concedes that FY2026 benefited from unusual, potentially non-recurring tailwinds (an AI/telecom project surge and possible pull-forward). ePlus tends toward conservative guidance, but the magnitude of the deceleration is too large to dismiss as sandbagging.

Consensus revisions corroborate the caution. Over the past 90 days, current-year EPS has been cut 7.9% to $5.33, and next-year EPS has been cut 20.7% to $5.80. The 30-day trend shows the same direction (next-year down another 13.4%). This is falling earnings momentum, not the positive revision velocity that would flag an upturn. Managed services (recurring, 3–5 year contracts) grew ~10.6% and remains the strategic margin-stabilization priority, but it is not large enough to offset a slowdown in the lumpy product/telecom business. The AI-infrastructure demand theme is real and broad-based at the sector level, but ePlus captures it as an integrator on third-party platforms, and the demand it did capture drove the concentration and cash-flow problems rather than a durable, diversified growth engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $89.02, ePlus trades at ~18.9x trailing/forward EPS ($4.71). The sole covering analyst carries a $111 target (~25% upside), but with only one estimate this is a thin data point rather than a robust consensus.

Against Technology Distributor peers (median P/E 19.3), ePlus is roughly in line: CNXN 21.0x, NSIT 19.3x, SCSC 15.0x, AVT 34.6x, ARW 15.9x. There is no clear discount to argue mispricing, and no premium the market is obviously over-paying either. The name screens as fairly valued for a distributor with above-average balance-sheet quality but decelerating growth.

The cycle-adjusted-valuation framework does not rescue the multiple here. That framework applies to stocks with rising estimates and accelerating revenue; ePlus is the opposite case, with revenue guided to decelerate to mid-single digits and estimates falling 8–21% across windows. Trailing EPS of $4.71 may in fact overstate normalized earnings power if the FY2026 telecom surge partly reverses. On the falling next-year estimate of $5.80 the stock is ~15.3x, which looks cheaper but rests on estimates that have themselves been cut and could be cut further. A low multiple is not by itself a reason to own the stock; cheapness on a decelerating, concentration-exposed distributor is a value-trap warning absent a specific re-rating catalyst, and there isn't one in view. The ~1.2% dividend yield (raised 8% to $0.27/quarter) provides modest support but no valuation floor. I score valuation slightly below neutral: fairly valued rather than mispriced, with the balance sheet the only clear argument for the low end of the range.

## Competitive Landscape

ePlus occupies a defensible but unremarkable middle-market niche. Its services-led, engineering-certified model differentiates it from pure procurement players, and its Cisco relationship (now ~29% of net sales, down from a historical 34–44%) has diversified in a healthy direction. The Bailiwick acquisition (~$124.9M, August 2024) added multi-site retail/hospitality services and drove professional-services growth, albeit at lower margins. Managed services is the strategic push toward recurring, higher-quality revenue.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. Scale disadvantage versus CDW/Insight is structural, product gross margin is thin (~22.2% in Q4) and mix-sensitive, and a material margin lever — vendor incentives (which added ~140bps to product margin in Q3) — is controlled by vendors, not ePlus. The industry shift toward cloud/consumption models pushes more revenue to net/agent recognition, suppressing reported sales and pressuring margin mix. The AI-integration positioning (including the new Private AI Infrastructure Managed Service) is a credible participation vector but does not confer a durable moat, since the underlying platforms belong to others. Net, ePlus is holding its position, not widening it.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer/end-market concentration (the standout risk).** TME at ~36% of trade AR (per the FY2025 10-K disclosure of a single customer, Verizon, rising from ~17%) is a rapid, outsized dependence. A pullback would hit revenue, earnings, and the elevated receivable simultaneously. This is the swing factor for the whole thesis.

**Cash-flow/working-capital risk.** If growth decelerates as guided, the working-capital build could reverse into a cash release (favorable); if it reflects pull-forward or lengthening terms on the concentrated telecom book, it is a warning.

**Vendor concentration (Cisco ~29%)** and dependence on discretionary vendor incentives that can compress product margin.

**Loss of the WFCDF floor-plan facility** (terminable on 90 days' notice) would materially disrupt working-capital financing.

**Loss of the financing segment's earnings stabilizer** leaves ePlus fully exposed to lumpy, project-driven IT cycles.

**Governance/accounting items to verify.** The FY2025 "immaterial misstatement correction" (FY2026 10-K Note 2) and the unread July 6, 2026 Item 5.02 officer/director filing (amid a pattern of recurring 5.02 filings and new director appearances — Lutz, Portegello) suggest a possible board/management change that could not be verified from the provided sources. These are monitor-items, not confirmed negatives, but they add uncertainty.

I score risk factors below neutral because the concentration and cash-flow divergence are specific, quantified, and only partly reflected in a mid-teens/high-teens multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 44.2% versus 34.5% trailing realized — rich by ~9.7 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (44.2% at 1 month falling to 39.8% at 6 months), which indicates the market is pricing near-term event stress. IV has risen 7 points since late June as the stock climbed ~10%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced near-term (0.3%) but the 6-month tenor shows a notable -5.8% put skew, and short interest is moderate at 6.2% of float (5.1 days to cover). The picture is consistent with a market bracing for an event (likely the next earnings print) rather than a strong directional bet. For a prospective buyer, rich near-term IV argues against chasing at $89 and favors waiting for post-print volatility compression.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105–115).** The telecom/AI-infrastructure relationship proves durable rather than a one-time surge; managed services continues compounding toward recurring, higher-quality revenue; the working-capital build reverses into a cash release as project timing normalizes, restoring FCF quality; and the debt-free balance sheet funds continued buybacks and dividend growth. On this path, mid-single-digit growth is a conservative floor that management beats, estimates stabilize and turn up, and the stock re-rates toward the peer-high multiple (~$111 analyst target implies ~25% upside). The pure-play narrative earns a cleaner multiple than the old financing-encumbered structure.

**Bear Case ($62–70).** The Verizon-led surge partly reverses in FY2027; concentration bites revenue, earnings, and the receivable simultaneously; the inventory build turns into a writedown or margin pressure as AI-supply timing shifts; and estimates keep falling. Normalized EPS proves closer to the mid-$4s than the reported $4.71, and the stock de-rates toward the low end of the peer range and its 52-week low ($62.11), a ~22–30% decline. The loss of the financing stabilizer amplifies the cyclical drawdown.

**Base Case (~$88–95).** Growth decelerates to management's mid-single-digit guide, estimates find a floor near the revised $5.33 current-year figure, working capital partially normalizes, and the balance sheet supports the price. The stock churns near current levels on a ~17–18x forward multiple, with the dividend and buyback providing modest support. This is the most likely path, and it is why the name is a hold rather than a buy or an avoid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q1 FY2027 earnings report (expected around late August/early September 2026, following the March-31 fiscal year), which will show whether the FY2026 telecom surge is repeating or reversing and whether working capital is releasing cash. The rich near-term IV and backwardation confirm the market views that print as the key catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating cash flow turns clearly positive as AR/inventory normalize, restoring FCF conversion above ~80%, confirming the working-capital build was timing rather than pull-forward.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year EPS revised up rather than down) alongside evidence the TME/AI relationship is broadening beyond a single customer.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s that widens the discount to peer multiples while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q1 FY2027 shows TME revenue declining sequentially/YoY, confirming the surge was non-recurring and pressuring the elevated receivable.
- An inventory writedown, material gross-margin compression below ~24%, or a further large downward estimate revision on next-year EPS (below ~$5.00).
- Confirmation that the July 2026 Item 5.02 filing and recurring governance changes represent a disorderly management/board transition, or escalation of the FY2025 misstatement matter beyond "immaterial."

## Conclusion

ePlus is a well-capitalized, cleanly financed IT solutions provider that delivered a record FY2026, but the record was built disproportionately on one fast-growing customer relationship whose durability management itself is signaling not to extrapolate. The FY2027 mid-single-digit guide, the downward estimate revisions across every window, the negative nine-month operating cash flow, and the telecom concentration collectively describe decelerating, lower-quality growth rather than an upturn. Against that, the debt-free balance sheet, ~$411M cash, interest-free working-capital financing, and a fair (~19x) multiple with a growing dividend provide a genuine floor.

The score has declined steadily from 7.0 in April to the mid-5s, driven by falling growth-prospects and valuation marks, and the current data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it. I maintain a **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: the balance sheet and modest valuation prevent an AVOID, but the concentration risk, cash-flow divergence, and negative earnings momentum prevent a BUY. The right posture is to wait for the next earnings print to resolve whether the telecom surge and working-capital build are durable and reversible respectively, and to prefer entry on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s rather than chasing at $89 into rich near-term implied volatility.
