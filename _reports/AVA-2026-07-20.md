**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avista Corp is a small-cap ($3.5B market cap), hydro-advantaged regulated electric and natural gas utility serving eastern Washington, northern Idaho, and parts of Oregon and Alaska. The investment case rests on a standard regulated-utility franchise: a legal monopoly service territory, decoupling mechanisms that pass most commodity risk to customers, and a long-term plan targeting ~5% earnings CAGR and ~3% dividend growth. At $41.77, the stock yields ~4.7% and trades at ~16.6x trailing/forward earnings, a discount to the multi-utility peer median of ~21x. The dividend is well-covered and the balance sheet is investment-grade. This is an income vehicle with limited near-term appreciation potential.

The tension in the story is per-share growth. Avista is in a heavy capital-investment cycle, with base capex escalating from ~$615M (2026) toward $800M (2028) and ~$710M (2030), funded with a mix of new long-term debt (~$230M in 2026) and equity issuance (~$90M ATM in 2026). Free cash flow is structurally negative and share count has risen from 80.0M to 82.6M over the past year. That dilution, combined with a persistent, disclosed power-supply-cost drag (the ERM mechanism, baked into 2026 guidance as a $(0.10)/share hit that the WUTC declined to modify), caps how much of the utility's ~5% earnings growth converts to per-share value. Avista's indexed total shareholder return trailed its peer group (121 vs. 145) at year-end 2025 despite steady net-income growth.

The single most important swing factor is the ~500 MW data-center load Avista confirmed in June 2026, a customer equal to more than half of all power used in Spokane County, which management has quantified as ~$350M of incremental capex (a hypothetical 12% capital CAGR versus the 5% base plan). That optionality is now impaired: Avista voluntarily paused the request on June 12, the Spokane City Council imposed a one-year moratorium on new data centers on June 23, and community protests followed. The upside is real but not bankable, and the timeline has slipped by at least a year. We rate AVA a HOLD for income, with the September 2026 Washington rate-case hearings and the data-center resolution as the catalysts to watch.

## Company Overview

Avista operates through three parts. **Avista Utilities**, an operating division rather than a subsidiary, generates ~98% of net income and serves ~427,000 electric and ~385,000 natural gas retail customers across Washington, Idaho, Oregon, and Montana. Its generation is hydro-heavy, which is both a cost and carbon advantage and a source of weather/streamflow variability. **AEL&P** (Alaska Electric Light & Power) is a small, ring-fenced utility serving Juneau (~$4M quarterly net income), isolated from any other grid, with a single hydro project (Snettisham) providing roughly two-thirds of its generation, a concentrated operational risk. **Avista Capital** holds a small portfolio of illiquid private-equity investments (~$149M carrying value) that inject quarter-to-quarter earnings noise (a $3M loss in Q1'25 swung to a $1M gain in Q1'26).

The economic moat is the regulated-utility standard: a monopoly service territory with cost recovery through rate cases, supported by decoupling mechanisms in Washington, Idaho, and Oregon plus power and gas cost-adjustment mechanisms (ERM, PCA, PGA) that pass most commodity risk to customers over time. Moat durability is high; the value question is entirely about the constructiveness of regulatory outcomes and the per-share arithmetic of a dilutive capex cycle.

## Financial Analysis

Net income has grown steadily: $147M (2021), $155M (2022), $171M (2023), $180M (2024), and $193M (2025). Company-selected EPS rose from $2.10 to $2.76 over the same window; GAAP diluted EPS for 2025 was $2.38, with non-GAAP utility EPS of $2.55. Q1 2026 net income of $92M ($1.11 diluted) was up ~16% from $79M ($0.98) in Q1'25, driven by rate-case revenue and the swing to investment gains at "Other." Q1 is seasonally the strongest quarter due to winter heating, so the growth rate should not be annualized.

The underlying utility is steadier than headline revenue implies. Q1'26 revenue fell $47M YoY (to $570M from $617M), but that is almost entirely commodity pass-through and decoupling noise. Electric utility margin was roughly flat (~$234M) and gas margin was up $5M (~$115M). Investors should anchor on utility margin and net income, not GAAP revenue.

The balance sheet is typical for a capital-intensive utility. Total assets are $8.4B, total debt $3.29B versus equity $2.78B, for debt/cap of ~54% against a 65% covenant ceiling, leveraged but comfortable and normal for the sector. Operating cash flow was $179M in Q1'26 (vs. $184M), while Q1 capex alone was $150M. Liquidity is adequate but not abundant: $156M available under the $500M revolver plus LC facility (with $385M drawn, high utilization), plus $25M at AEL&P.

The defining financial characteristic is structurally negative free cash flow. The 2026 plan funds the capex ramp with new long-term debt and ATM equity, producing ongoing shareholder dilution. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the cost of that debt-funded model rises and the equity issuance dilutes per-share growth. The dividend ($0.493/quarter, $41M paid in Q1'26) is well-covered against ~$2.60 current-year EPS, and management targets bringing the payout into a 60–70% band, so dividend-cut risk is low.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: over the last four quarters, two beats and two misses, including a Q1'26 beat of 6.2% but a Q4'25 miss of 14.6% and a Q2'25 miss of 44.5% (though on a small seasonal base). This reflects weather and power-cost variability, not chronic guidance problems, but it argues against high confidence in quarterly precision.

## Growth Analysis

The base-plan growth algorithm is ~5% earnings CAGR and ~3% dividend CAGR, driven by rate-base investment. That is a credible but unexceptional trajectory, and the per-share conversion is diluted by ATM equity issuance. Estimate revisions confirm the low ceiling: current-year consensus EPS of $2.60 has moved just +0.8% over 90 days and is flat over 30 days; next-year consensus of $2.79 is flat across both windows. The larger positive revisions in near-term quarterly estimates (current quarter +14.6%, next quarter +26.9% over 90 days) are off small seasonal bases and reflect timing, not a fundamental change. The overall revision trend is flat-to-slightly-negative; there is no earnings-momentum tailwind here.

The genuine growth lever is data-center load. This is the AI/data-center demand theme that is reshaping regional resource adequacy across the sector, and for a utility Avista's size a single 500 MW customer is disproportionately impactful. The opportunity is currently stalled: Avista paused the request "to align with government agencies and stakeholders," the Spokane City Council imposed the moratorium, and protesters gathered at HQ on June 29. The pause is prudent (protecting existing ratepayers from cost misallocation and grid-adequacy risk), but it removes the near-term growth catalyst and defers any rate-base upside by at least a year.

Constructive rate-case outcomes elsewhere support the base plan: Idaho settled in August 2025 (+$20M electric, 6.3%, ROE 9.6%) and Oregon in May 2025 (+$4M, 5.0%, ROE 9.5%). The pending 2026 Washington multi-year rate plan requests a $111M electric increase for Rate Year 1 (2027), a 13.9% ask, plus step-ups through 2030 and new mechanisms, including a revised baseline power-supply calculation intended to fix the ERM drag. A constructive WUTC decision (hearings September 2026, decision expected late 2026) is the credible route to accelerating earnings growth without the data center.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.77, AVA trades at ~16.6x current-year consensus EPS ($2.60) and ~15.0x next-year ($2.79), a meaningful discount to the multi-utility peer median P/E of ~21.1x (NWE 25.5, MDU 22.7, BKH 18.5, OGE 21.1, PNW 19.3). On yield, its ~4.7% exceeds the peer median of ~3.6%. The discount is a rational reflection of small-cap status, hydro/weather earnings variability, wildfire tail risk, high revolver utilization, and structural equity dilution that dampens per-share growth. The persistent TSR underperformance is the market's verdict on these factors, and there is no identified catalyst to close the gap in the near term.

We treat the cheapness as a warning against expecting re-rating, not as a buy signal. A compressed multiple on a utility with negative FCF, ongoing dilution, and a stalled growth catalyst is more likely a durable feature than a temporary anomaly. The independent catalyst that could justify a higher score, a bankable data-center rate-base expansion or a clean, constructive Washington rate order, is either paused or unresolved.

The stock has recovered to $41.77 (up ~11% over the trailing year and below the 52-week high of $43.50 after dipping to ~$39.40 on the data-center pause), so the current entry offers roughly the analyst-consensus level (mean $41.83, median $41.00) with limited asymmetry. Analyst positioning is unenthusiastic: 1 buy, 5 holds, 1 strong sell (one strong-sell was removed over the past three months but a strong-sell rating is again present in the latest three periods). The high target of $50 embeds a favorable data-center/rate-case scenario; the low of $37 reflects the dilution-and-drag base case. Fair value clusters near current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Avista's competitive position is stable-to-marginally-favorable. Its hydro-heavy generation base is a structural cost and carbon advantage in a decarbonizing region, and the Washington multi-year rate-plan framework with decoupling and balancing accounts is generally supportive. The AI/data-center load theme is a sector-wide tailwind for rate-base growth, and Avista is directly exposed.

Offsetting these, the regulatory environment is tightening at the margin. The WUTC declined to modify the ERM in the 2024 order and has flagged a Washington overearnings refund provision, indicating regulators are not rubber-stamping. Oregon's HB 3179 (2025) tightens rate-case timing (no residential increases Nov 1–Mar 31; 18-month gaps between ROE-based increases). The Pacific Northwest wholesale market is, by Avista's own disclosure, "strained during regional events" and may not reliably supply peak needs without new generation and transmission, which is the double edge of the data-center opportunity. Relative to larger utilities navigating the same data-center demand theme (from AEP to Dominion), Avista's data-center thesis is smaller in absolute terms, higher-variance for its size, and currently blocked by a local moratorium. The trajectory is roughly stable, warranting a score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is regulatory lag on power-supply cost recovery. Management explicitly expects net power-supply costs to exceed base-rate levels through 2025–2026, within the 90%/10% customer/company sharing band. The 2026 Washington MYRP's proposed baseline recalculation is the remedy, and the September 2026 hearings are the key event; an adverse or reduced outcome directly hits earnings.

Wildfire liability is the catastrophic tail risk. Avista equipment could ignite (or be alleged to ignite) wildfires, creating potentially uninsured liability. The 2023 Orofino, Idaho fire is an active but small matter (one $0.2M suppression claim, no negligence found). The company carries a ~$27M wildfire resiliency regulatory asset and a balancing account, but the tail is unquantifiable.

Hydro and weather dependence drives quarter-to-quarter volatility: Q1'26 had milder weather (heating degree days 90% of normal), cutting residential/commercial usage 10–15%, largely offset by decoupling but with cash-timing lag. Financing risk is elevated by the negative-FCF, debt-plus-equity funding model in a higher-rate environment; long-term debt fair value sits well below carrying value (Level 3 debt marked ~79 cents), and rating maintenance is critical for collateral posting. Additional contingencies include the Rathdrum, Idaho gas-explosion lawsuits, a FERC transmission-planning complaint, and Oregon Climate Protection Plan litigation. The Colstrip coal-plant ownership transfer to NorthWestern (effective Jan 1, 2026) is earnings-neutral and largely resolved, with a ~$34M regulatory asset remaining.

Two governance items warrant flagging. There is no open-market C-suite buying (routine grants and minor sales/gifts only), so the strongest bullish insider signal is absent. And a July 2, 2026 Item 5.02 filing (officer/director departure/election) could not be retrieved and has no news coverage; it is an unresolved item that could be routine or could involve a senior executive. Combined with the absence of insider conviction and the persistent TSR lag, this keeps our management score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral with a note of caution. Near-term ATM IV is 26.6% versus 19.2% trailing realized vol, a modestly rich 7.4-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (26.6% near-term vs. 23.9% at six months), consistent with the market pricing imminent event stress, most plausibly the data-center/moratorium resolution and rate-case developments. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.17, so there is no aggressive downside hedging. The implied ±7.5% one-month move is large for a utility, reflecting binary regulatory/load-decision risk. The reported prior-snapshot IV of 264.2% collapsing to 26.6% is a stale thin-options artifact and is treated as non-informative; the current 26.6% read is the credible one. Net: the options market is not confirming a directional bearish thesis, but it is pricing more uncertainty than usual for a name of this profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$50, roughly +20%):** The Washington MYRP is resolved constructively in late 2026, restoring power-supply cost recovery and lifting the ROE trajectory. The Spokane moratorium expires or is worked around, and the ~500 MW data-center load advances on terms that protect existing ratepayers, adding ~$350M of capex and lifting the capital CAGR toward 12%. Rate-base growth accelerates above the 5% base plan, the market awards Avista a peer-closer multiple (~19–20x), and the yield plus growth delivers a mid-teens total return.

**Bear Case (~$37, roughly -11%):** The WUTC delivers a reduced rate outcome, the ERM drag persists, and the data center never materializes (moratorium extended, community opposition hardens, or the developer walks). Continued ATM dilution and higher financing costs erode per-share growth below the 5% plan, weather variability produces another soft year, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range. The ~4.7% yield provides a floor, limiting downside.

**Base Case (~$43, roughly +3% plus ~4.7% yield):** Avista delivers its 5%/3% earnings/dividend plan with modest per-share dilution, the Washington rate case lands in a workmanlike middle, and the data center remains optionality rather than a bankable driver through 2026. Total return is dominated by the dividend, with the stock oscillating around consensus fair value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Own AVA for its well-covered ~4.7% yield and defensive characteristics, not for appreciation. The score trajectory has been declining (5.6 in May → 5.3 in June, now ~5.05), driven by the data-center pause and the moratorium, plus flat-to-negative estimate revisions and the persistent dilution. That decline reflects genuine fundamental developments (a stalled growth catalyst), not price action; the stock has recovered from ~$39.40 to $41.77 since the June lows.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The Spokane moratorium lifts or the ~500 MW data-center deal advances on ratepayer-protective terms, with management reaffirming the incremental ~$350M capex / ~12% capital CAGR path.
- A constructive Washington MYRP order (September 2026 hearings, decision expected late 2026) that meaningfully restores power-supply cost recovery and supports an ROE at or above 9.6%.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus moving above ~$2.70) alongside stabilization or reduction of ATM equity issuance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Washington rate order that entrenches the ERM drag and pushes the ROE trajectory lower, combined with accelerating equity dilution.
- A material wildfire liability event or a governance surprise from the unresolved July 2026 Item 5.02 filing.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (payout rising well above the 60–70% target range on missed earnings).

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are the Q3 2026 earnings release, the September 2026 Washington rate-case hearings, and the June 2027 expiration window of the Spokane data-center moratorium.

## Conclusion

Avista is a competent, hydro-advantaged small-cap utility offering a well-covered ~4.7% yield and a credible but modest 5%/3% earnings/dividend growth plan. Its discount to peers (~16.6x versus ~21x) is rational rather than an opportunity: structurally negative free cash flow funded with ongoing equity dilution caps per-share growth, the ERM power-supply-cost drag is a persistent headwind, and the ~500 MW data-center load is paused by a Spokane City Council moratorium and community opposition, deferring any upside by at least a year. Analyst positioning (1 buy, 5 holds, 1 strong sell) and flat estimate revisions confirm the absence of near-term momentum, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal conviction. This is an income hold: collect the dividend, watch the September 2026 Washington rate case and the data-center resolution, and reassess if either catalyst turns decisively favorable. We do not see an asymmetric appreciation case at $41.77.
