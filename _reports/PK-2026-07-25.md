**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Park Hotels & Resorts is the second-largest U.S. lodging REIT, a Hilton spin-off holding interests in 34 hotels (~23,000 rooms) concentrated in the luxury and upper-upscale segment. The company is midway through a defensive restructuring: shed 13 remaining Non-Core hotels, reinvest in Core renovations, and deleverage a balance sheet that carries ~$3.8B of debt at a 5.2% weighted rate. The FY2025 GAAP loss of –$283M is a paper event driven by a $318M non-cash impairment tied to the accelerated Non-Core disposition decision. The cash-flow metrics that matter for a REIT held up: Nareit FFO $1.47/sh, Adjusted FFO $1.97/sh, Adjusted EBITDA $609M, operating cash flow $398M.

The most important development since prior reviews is the full resolution of the San Francisco overhang. PK walked away from the $725M non-recourse SF Mortgage Loan (Hilton SF Union Square + Parc 55, ~2,945 rooms) that had been in default since 2023, and the receiver sold both assets in November 2025. That structurally removes a money-losing, high-leverage EBITDA drag and improves portfolio quality. Paired with a September 2025 refinancing that expanded the revolver to $1B and added an $800M delayed-draw term loan to pre-fund the 2026 maturities, the company enters 2026 with a materially cleaner setup than its trailing GAAP numbers imply. Q1 2026 delivered RevPAR +5.5% YoY (ex-Royal Palm), and management raised full-year RevPAR guidance to 0.5%–2.5% with Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $587M–$617M.

The stock has re-rated ~32% from the July 2025 base near $11 to $14.74, with the June 2026 momentum consistent with a lodging-demand recovery thesis. The re-rating has already occurred as sentiment normalized. What remains is a leveraged, geographically concentrated REIT executing a slow, defensive plan with a ~$1.4B 2026 refinancing still to complete and no acquisition growth engine. The ~6.6% dividend on a well-covered ~50% AFFO payout is the durable return; the appreciation case depends on refinancing and disposition execution that is credible but not yet delivered. We rate PK a HOLD on income.

## Company Overview

PK was spun off from Hilton in January 2017 and does not operate its own hotels; third-party managers, primarily Hilton, run the properties to preserve REIT status. The portfolio is anchored by iconic, high-barrier assets: New York Hilton Midtown, Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki, Hilton Waikoloa Village, Waldorf Astoria Orlando, Signia by Hilton Orlando Bonnet Creek, Casa Marina Key West, and Caribe Hilton in Puerto Rico, plus assets in D.C., Chicago, Boston, New Orleans and Denver. More than 96% of Core rooms sit in the luxury/upper-upscale segment.

The company has reorganized into three reporting buckets: Core hotels (~90% of Hotel Adjusted EBITDA), Non-Core hotels (13 remaining, being sold), and unconsolidated JVs. The strategic thesis is a pure-play premium REIT funded by capital recycling: selling low-growth Non-Core assets and reinvesting in Core renovations. This is a defensive posture; PK has not acquired a hotel since the 2019 Chesapeake Lodging deal. The business is heavily tied to the Hilton brand family and the Hilton Honors loyalty program.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has drifted lower: $2,698M (2023) → $2,599M (2024) → $2,541M (2025), a 2.2% decline in 2025 driven by Hawaii softness (labor-strike recovery plus renovation disruption), the Royal Palm South Beach Miami closing for a full renovation in May 2025, and Non-Core dispositions. GAAP net income swung to –$283M in 2025 from +$212M in 2024, with operating income going from +$391M to –$33M. Both swings are almost entirely the non-cash impairment on nine Non-Core hotels tied to the accelerated decision to sell them.

The REIT-relevant cash metrics tell a steadier story, holding versus $1.91 FFO and $2.06 Adjusted FFO in 2024, and $652M Adjusted EBITDA. On the balance sheet, 95% of debt is fixed. Total assets fell from $9.16B to $7.7B on asset sales and impairments. Liquidity is adequate: $232M cash, the undrawn revolver, and the undrawn delayed-draw term loan. The Altman Z of 1.29 flags "distress," but this is largely an artifact of impairment-driven negative EBIT and the high fixed-asset leverage normal for hotel REITs; this is a leveraged-but-liquid REIT mid-restructuring, not a bankruptcy candidate. Screens penalizing PK on the GAAP loss and the Z-score are reading a strategic write-down as operational failure.

Capital allocation in 2025: $296M renovation-heavy capex, $280M dividends, $45M buybacks (3.5M shares). Share count has shrunk steadily from 228M (2022) to ~199M–201M. The forward P/E of –13.4 is meaningless given the impairment-driven loss; the stock should be evaluated on AFFO, where PK trades at roughly 7.5x AFFO.

The earnings-surprise record is noisy and dominated by non-cash items: the –$1.04 Q4 2025 print reflects the impairment, while Q1 2026 came in at $0.06 vs. a $0.01 estimate. This pattern is not a reliable read on underlying trajectory because GAAP EPS for a REIT mid-disposition is distorted by impairments and gains on derecognition. The FFO/AFFO series is the cleaner gauge, and it is holding.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on three embedded drivers rather than top-line acquisition growth. First, RevPAR recovery, with management citing the 2026 event calendar — the World Cup and the U.S. 250th anniversary — and recovering group demand. Second, the renovation ramp: Bonnet Creek and Casa Marina renovations are already delivering (F&B +11–13%), and Royal Palm reopens June 2026 after driving ~$56M of accelerated depreciation in 2025, providing a 2026 earnings tailwind. Third, the SF exit removes a persistent EBITDA drag, so 2026 Adjusted EBITDA against a cleaner portfolio is a higher-quality base than the headline decline suggests.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and does not clear the high bar for a growth inflection. Current-year EPS estimates rose +12.0% over 30 days to $0.52 but are down 4.6% over 90 days; next-year is roughly flat (+4.4% 30d, –2.8% 90d). The current-quarter estimate is down 21.4% over 90 days and next-quarter down 76.2%. This is not the +30%-over-90-days revision velocity that signals a durable earnings inflection; it reflects a stabilizing but not accelerating outlook, complicated by dispositions that shrink the earnings base. The growth story is a cyclical lodging recovery layered on renovation ROI, capped by a strategy that trims assets rather than adding them. Per-share AFFO growth is constrained because dispositions offset the recovery, and the payout has already reset far below prior elevated levels.

## Valuation Assessment

On AFFO the stock trades at ~7.5x (2025) or roughly 7x against the implied 2026 EBITDA guidance midpoint, a discount that reflects genuine concerns: leverage, concentration, slow growth, and a five-year TSR of 84.35 versus a peer group at 137.83. The dividend yield of ~6.6% is well above the hotel-REIT peer median of ~3.1% and is covered at a ~50% AFFO payout, giving the yield real support.

Against peers, PK's trailing P/E is not comparable (negative on the impairment), so the relevant frame is yield and AFFO multiple. Peer P/Es range widely (DRH 25.1, SHO 107.2, XHR 27.5, APLE 22.3, PEB –22.3), reflecting a heterogeneous group where trailing GAAP earnings are heavily distorted by depreciation and one-offs. APLE, the closest yield comparable at 6.1%, trades at a market cap roughly 1.3x PK's. PK's discount to the group on yield-adjusted terms is defensible given higher leverage and concentration, but it is not deeply anomalous.

A compressed multiple here is not a standalone buy signal, and the sector's raw prints are mostly beta rather than selection edge. The specific catalyst that could re-rate the stock — successful completion of the ~$1.4B 2026 refinancing plus disposition execution at favorable prices — is credible but unproven, and much of the "clean-up year is over" optimism is already in a stock that has moved 32% off its base. Analyst targets bracket the current price: mean $14.59, median $14.00, low $11.00, high $20.00. With the stock at $14.74, consensus implies roughly flat-to-modest upside to the mean and material upside only in the bull scenario. On a premium/discount basis the valuation is fair rather than mispriced, which supports a HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

PK's differentiation is scale and a concentrated set of irreplaceable, high-RevPAR urban and resort assets in high-barrier markets. It competes with Host, RLJ, Pebblebrook, DiamondRock and Sunstone for capital and assets, with major chains and OTAs on distribution, and with Airbnb-type supply on the leisure margin. The competitive trajectory is mixed: the SF exit and Non-Core disposals structurally upgrade portfolio quality toward the luxury Core, while the five-year TSR underperformance versus peers signals the market's persistent skepticism about concentration, leverage, and the lack of external growth.

The portfolio's quality is real but its trajectory is one of shrinking-to-improve rather than gaining share. Renovation-driven ROI is the stated growth engine given the acquisition drought, and early results (F&B growth at renovated properties) are encouraging. The 2026 setup — group demand recovery, marquee events, Royal Palm reopening — is a cyclical tailwind that lifts the whole group rather than a PK-specific share gain. We view competitive position as stable, with a portfolio-quality improvement offset by defensive strategy and lagging relative returns.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant near-term risk is the ~$1.4B of mortgage loans maturing in 2026 (no significant maturities until Q4 2026). Management plans to repay via the $800M delayed-draw term loan plus Non-Core sale proceeds, which reduces but does not eliminate refinancing risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Second, disposition execution: selling all 13 Non-Core hotels on favorable terms is central to deleveraging, and real estate is illiquid with timing/price uncertainty plus REIT tax frictions and franchise-transfer restrictions.

Geographic concentration is structural: Florida, Hawaii, Chicago, NYC, New Orleans and Boston represent >69% of rooms, with Florida and Hawaii alone ~36% of rooms and >39% of 2025 revenue — heavy exposure to hurricanes, climate risk, and single-market demand shocks. Hawaii has been a persistent soft spot (occupancy down 3.2–7.4 pts). Additional risks include Hilton brand dependence, union/labor exposure that limits cost flexibility in downturns, and active ground-lease litigation (Hilton Salt Lake City Center, DoubleTree San Diego–Mission Valley, DoubleTree Durango), with two Non-Core hotels surrendered to a ground lessor at lease expiration on 12/31/2025. As a leisure-transient-heavy REIT, PK is more exposed to consumer discretionary weakness than business-travel-heavy peers.

The very high short interest of 25.4% of float (36.8M shares, 6.5 days to cover) is a two-sided signal: it reflects genuine skepticism about leverage and concentration, but it also creates squeeze potential on positive refinancing/disposition news, which likely contributed to the June momentum.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.2% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.2% (a 10.1pp spread), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty rather than complacency. The put/call skew of 19.8% shows elevated put demand, consistent with hedging around the refinancing timeline and the high short base, though the flat term structure (near 41.2% ≈ far 41.4%) suggests uncertainty is spread across the year rather than concentrated on a single dated event. The implied ±9.0% move at the nearest expiry and the low put/call volume ratio (0.23) argue against imminent directional positioning. IV rose 3.0pp since the prior snapshot as the stock climbed 4.2%, and the P/C OI ratio fell to 0.20, a modestly constructive shift in positioning. Net read: the options market is neither confirming a strong bullish thesis nor flagging acute downside; it is pricing a leveraged REIT with a known refinancing overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The 2025 GAAP loss was a strategic clean-up, not operational deterioration. With the SF drag removed and the balance sheet pre-funded via the delayed-draw term loan, a successful 2026 refinancing plus favorable Non-Core sales could deleverage the REIT and close the persistent discount to peers. Layered on a cyclical RevPAR recovery, the Royal Palm reopening, and renovation ROI, AFFO could inflect and support a re-rating toward the high end of analyst targets. The high short interest amplifies upside on positive execution. Bull case ~$20 (analyst high), roughly +36% from current.

**Bear Case.** Refinancing $1.4B in a higher-rate environment lifts interest costs; Non-Core sales close slowly or at soft prices; Hawaii and leisure-transient demand weaken into a consumer slowdown; and the defensive strategy keeps per-share AFFO flat while the dividend, though covered, offers no growth. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the low end of the analyst range near $11, roughly –25% from current.

**Base Case.** RevPAR recovers within guidance, the refinancing completes without disruption using the pre-funded term loan, and Non-Core sales proceed at reasonable prices. AFFO stabilizes near $1.90–$2.00 and the yield anchors the return. The stock holds roughly in the $14–$15 area with the dividend as the primary return, consistent with the median and mean targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The durable return is the dividend, well-covered at a ~50% AFFO payout; capital appreciation depends on execution catalysts that are credible but not yet delivered.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Completion of the ~$1.4B 2026 refinancing at rates at or below the current 5.2% weighted cost, materially de-risking the balance sheet.
- Two or more Non-Core dispositions closed at prices at or above carrying value, demonstrating disposition execution and net deleveraging.
- Full-year 2026 RevPAR tracking to the upper half of the guide with 90-day EPS/AFFO estimate revisions turning positive, signaling a genuine earnings inflection rather than stabilization.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Refinancing terms materially above the current weighted cost or a slipped 2026 maturity, indicating financing stress.
- A dividend cut or AFFO payout rising toward or above 100% on deteriorating cash flow.
- RevPAR turning negative on a Hawaii/leisure demand shock, or a Non-Core sale at a distressed price signaling weak asset demand.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any 8-K on refinancing execution or Non-Core sale closings. The Q4 2026 maturity window is the natural checkpoint on the refinancing thesis.

## Conclusion

PK has resolved the San Francisco default and enters 2026 with a cleaner, higher-quality luxury portfolio, a pre-funded refinancing bridge, and a covered dividend. The SF resolution and RevPAR recovery are real, and the earlier distress read that the impairment made superficially worse than reality has eased. But the improvement has substantially played out in a stock up 32% off its base, the growth strategy is defensive and per-share-dilutive on dispositions, the ~$1.4B refinancing remains to be executed, and Florida/Hawaii concentration plus leisure-transient exposure sit against a soft macro consumer backdrop. Estimate revisions are stabilizing rather than inflecting, and the valuation is fair rather than mispriced. The yield is the reliable return, and the appreciation case hinges on execution catalysts that would warrant an upgrade only once demonstrated. We rate PK a HOLD on income, with a clear path to BUY on confirmed refinancing and disposition progress.
