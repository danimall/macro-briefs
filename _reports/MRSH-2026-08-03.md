## Executive Summary

Marsh is one of three global insurance brokers with the scale to intermediate the entire risk-transfer chain, and its financial architecture is genuinely high quality: FY2025 revenue of $26,981M, operating income of $6,223M (23.1% margin), free cash flow of $5,001M, operating cash flow at 127% of net income, and return on average equity near 29% once the balance sheet is corrected for the fiduciary-cash gross-up that distorts standard screens. Credit ratings are A−/A3/A−, all stable, with 6.5x interest coverage and a newly enlarged $4.25B revolver undrawn to June 2031.

The problem is the earnings bridge. FY2025 revenue grew 10.3% and pretax income grew 1.1%. Interest expense rose $260M to $960M as the $7.75B McGriff acquisition carried its first full year of financing and purchase-intangible cost; fiduciary interest income fell $94M to $403M; Thrive restructuring charges began. Underlying (organic) revenue growth is now running 4% for the first half of 2026, with management guiding full-year underlying growth "similar to 2025." Guy Carpenter's revenue declined 2% in Q2 as its property catastrophe rate-on-line index fell 16% at mid-year — steeper than the 12% decline at January 1 and, per management, the sharpest year-over-year move since the index was created. Property catastrophe is roughly half of Guy Carpenter's book.

Against that, the second quarter was better than the first: underlying growth accelerated to 5%, GAAP diluted EPS rose 7% to $2.63, Marsh Management Consulting grew 13% underlying, Consulting segment margins expanded (19.3% vs 19.2% in Q2; 19.9% vs 19.5% for the half), and Guy Carpenter posted record first-half new business with double-digit growth in cat bonds, sidecars and capital advisory — the classic broker hedge as traditional reinsurance pricing falls. Consensus has 2026 adjusted EPS at $10.45 and 2027 at $11.41, both revised slightly higher over 30 and 90 days. The shares have risen roughly 21% from the June 3 low of $156.60 and now sit at 18.1x 2026 and 16.6x 2027 consensus. That is cheap versus the 24-28x this franchise commanded in 2023-2024, and roughly fair versus a 4% organic grower facing an accelerating pricing downcycle, a September litigation trial, and a goodwill test whose last quantitative baseline predates $6B of added goodwill. Fourth consecutive HOLD.

## Company Overview

Marsh operates in 130 countries with more than 95,000 employees across two reporting segments:

**Risk and Insurance Services (~66% of revenue; $9,874M of $15,001M in 1H26)** comprises Marsh Risk — retail broking, program management, risk consulting and analytics, including the Marsh McLennan Agency (MMA) U.S. middle-market roll-up platform — and Guy Carpenter, the reinsurance intermediary.

**Consulting (~34%)** comprises Mercer (health, wealth including delegated/OCIO asset management, and career) and Marsh Management Consulting (ex-Oliver Wyman).

The economics matter for how the balance sheet should be read. Marsh takes no underwriting risk and is not a principal to insurance contracts; $18.0B of uncollected premiums and claims at June 30, 2026 (versus $14.6B at year-end 2025) sit off balance sheet. Separately, $12.2B of client cash held in a fiduciary capacity is grossed up on the balance sheet against an exactly offsetting liability. Marsh keeps the interest — $403M in FY2025 — but the principal is client and insurer money and is explicitly not a source of corporate liquidity. Any screen that treats the $59.7B asset base or the 1.1x current ratio as operating capital produces a misleading answer.

### Cash flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $5,292M against $291M of capex, producing $5,001M of free cash flow — an 18.5% FCF margin and 127% conversion of net income. On ~482M current diluted shares that is roughly $10.38 per share, a **5.5% free cash flow yield** at $189.69. This is the single strongest support under the valuation.

First-half 2026 OCF of $835M (versus $1,049M) should not be annualized. Annual incentive compensation is paid in Q1 — accrued compensation fell $1,319M in 1H26 — and roughly 80% of FY2025 OCF arrived in the second half. The 1H26 shortfall is explained by a larger receivables build (−$1,365M vs −$921M), partly offset by +$521M of other liabilities that plausibly includes the non-cash Greensill accrual. If 2H26 repeats 2H25's seasonality, FY2026 OCF lands near $5.1B; a more conservative assumption on receivables reversal puts it at $4.8-5.0B. Net receivables of $8,943M at June 30 versus a reconstructed ~$8,077M a year earlier implies roughly +11% growth against 1H revenue +6.9% — a monitoring flag, blurred by FX and acquisition effects, not established deterioration.

### Leverage and balance sheet

Total debt of $19,537M at June 30, 2026 is flat versus year-end and below the $19,947M peak at December 2024. Equity is $15,433M; corporate cash $1,700M (of which ~$588M is restricted). Using FY2025 operating income plus D&A and intangible amortization (~$7.14B of EBITDA), gross leverage is 2.74x and net leverage approximately 2.5x, with 6.5x interest coverage. The maturity ladder is well spread — $2,793M inside three years, $13,827M beyond five — and the June 2026 refinancing replaced the $3.5B revolver with $4.25B extended to 2031, undrawn.

Two structural qualifications. First, goodwill of $24,352M is 41% of total assets and 158% of book equity; with $4.5B of other intangibles, tangible equity is roughly **negative $13.5B**. That is normal for a serial acquirer of people businesses, but it means there is no asset cushion and the entire equity value rests on client-relationship durability (acquired relationships are amortized over a weighted-average 11.6 years for 2026 vintages). Second, standard distress screens misfire here: removing the $11.5B of fiduciary cash and its identical offsetting liability lifts the Altman Z-score from ~2.8 (grey) to ~3.6 (safe), and correctly computed ROE on average FY2025 equity of $14,425M is 28.8%, not the ~7% a naïve screen produces. Neither Z-score deserves much weight against A−/A3 ratings, 6.5x coverage and 127% cash conversion.

**Financial Health: 7.0.** Genuine cash generation, investment-grade credit, ample liquidity and a repaired U.K. pension (all four sections in surplus, no deficit funding required before 2029 at the earliest, ~$200M/year of non-cash benefit credit). Held below 8 by post-McGriff leverage that doubled long-term debt versus 2023, $960M of annual interest now consuming most incremental operating income, deeply negative tangible equity, and first-half capital returns of $2.39B bridged with $1,024M of commercial paper — seasonally explicable but not repeatable if 2H cash disappoints.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has 2026 adjusted EPS at $10.45 (+0.5% over 30 days, +0.6% over 90) and 2027 at $11.41 (+0.8% on both windows). Estimates are drifting up, but nowhere near the magnitude that signals an inflection — this is a stable, modestly improving revision profile, not an earnings acceleration.

The bridge from 4% underlying revenue growth to ~9% adjusted EPS growth is checkable. On $27B of revenue, 4% underlying growth at roughly a 25% incremental margin contributes ~$275M of operating income; Thrive contributes incremental savings on the way to ~$400M annualized (~$0.64/share after tax at a 23.6% rate); FX at June 30 spot would add ~$85M to full-year net operating income; fiduciary interest income subtracts roughly $50M at current rates. Net operating income growth of ~6%, flat interest expense, and a 2.8% annual reduction in share count gets to ~9%. Consensus is achievable without heroic assumptions, which is why estimates are creeping higher rather than falling.

What limits the growth score is the composition. The reinsurance broking engine is in the teeth of a downcycle whose slope is still steepening: property cat rate-on-line −16% at mid-year versus −12% at January 1, Florida June 1 renewals repricing 15-20% lower, and property cat representing half of Guy Carpenter's book. Because brokerage revenue is largely a percentage of premium, falling rates compress revenue on unchanged or growing exposure. Guy Carpenter's flat first half was achieved *despite* record new business and headcount growth for five consecutive years — an indication of how much rate is subtracting. The January 1, 2027 renewal season is the next read on whether the decline is decelerating.

Three offsets are real. Marsh Management Consulting at +13% underlying in Q2 is uncorrelated with insurance pricing and added CR3 Partners (turnaround/distressed advisory) in June — an explicitly countercyclical capability. Guy Carpenter's alternative-capital franchise placed 20 catastrophe bonds for $5B of limit in the first half, a company record against a $61B market, converting soft traditional pricing into structuring fees. And on fiduciary interest income, company sensitivity puts a 29bp move at ~$20M annually; the direction of policy is worth roughly $20-40M of high-margin revenue over a year either way. Most sell-side models embed continued decay.

**Growth Prospects: 5.0**, unchanged from the prior review. Q2's acceleration to 5% underlying and slightly rising estimates confirm stabilization rather than reversal of the decline from 7.5 in February.

## Valuation Assessment

Reconcile the multiples first, because the supplied data conflicts. The profile reports forward P/E identical to trailing P/E at 23.22x, which cannot both be true given a $10.45 consensus. Trailing twelve-month GAAP diluted EPS is $8.18 ($8.42 − $5.23 + $4.99), giving a trailing P/E of **23.2x** — the figure the data provider is actually reporting. That trailing multiple is inflated by the Greensill charge; adding back $425M pretax (~$0.67/share after tax) gives ~21.4x on adjusted-for-litigation trailing GAAP earnings.

The peer trailing set is heterogeneous (AJG's 35.7x reflects its own accounting distortions), so the cleaner statements are: MRSH's trailing GAAP multiple carries a premium to the peer median that is partly a one-time charge; its forward multiple of 18.1x is a meaningful de-rating from the 24-28x range this franchise held in 2023-2024; and every large broker has de-rated together, which tells you the market is repricing the pricing cycle rather than singling Marsh out. MRSH also offers the highest dividend yield in the group at 2.09% ($0.99 quarterly, raised 10% in July after absorbing the litigation charge) and has fallen the least from its high — meaning it has already recovered most of what it lost.

Three supports for the current price: a 5.5% free cash flow yield; a 2.8% annual reduction in share count with $4.2B remaining on a $6B authorization; and management's own revealed price opinion — $1.5B repurchased in the first half at an average of $166.73, with directors granted stock at $159.28 on June 1. Today's price is 14% above that grant and 14% above the average buyback.

That last point cuts both ways and is the crux of the valuation call. Six weeks ago, at ~$162, the shares were 14.2x 2027 consensus with a 6.4% FCF yield. At $189.69 they are 16.6x and 5.5%. The fundamentals improved modestly (Q2 underlying +5%, estimates +0.8%); the price improved roughly 17% off that level. Prospective return compressed by more than fundamentals improved.

**Base case $208** = 18.2x 2027 consensus $11.41, roughly the current forward multiple applied one year out, implying +9.7% price and ~11.8% total return with the dividend. Analyst mean target is $205.52 and median $201, consistent with this. Applying 19x gives $217; 17x gives $194.

**Valuation: 5.5.** Not stretched, not compelling. A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong; here the market has priced a soft-market broker at a soft-market multiple, and the de-rating is explained by a verifiable fact — 1.1% pretax growth on 10.3% revenue growth — rather than sentiment. I am not paying a premium for cheapness that has a cause.

## Competitive Landscape

Marsh's scale advantages are documented rather than asserted: 130 countries, 95,000+ employees, the largest property catastrophe book in the reinsurance market (per management), and MMA's serial middle-market roll-up (17 of 20 FY2025 acquisitions landed in RIS, mostly MMA). Client relationships carried at $7,055M of gross cost and amortized over 11.6 years encode management's own estimate of relationship durability. There is no material customer concentration disclosure, consistent with a highly fragmented client base.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is mixed. Q2 underlying growth of 5% is roughly in line with WTW's reported 5% organic — Marsh is holding share, not taking it, though "underlying" and "organic" are company-defined non-GAAP measures and not strictly comparable. RIS margins are compressing (30.6% from 31.2% in Q2; FY2025 saw 28.4%→26.8%) while Consulting margins expand. The largest property cat book is an asset in a hardening market and a liability in the steepest softening on record.

Two competitive questions are unresolved. The rebrand retires the Oliver Wyman name precisely as that unit becomes the fastest-growing part of the company — in strategy consulting, brand is a recruiting and pricing asset, and Oliver Wyman competed for talent against McKinsey, BCG and Bain on an independent identity. Thirteen percent Q2 growth is not the signature of a damaged franchise, and the company retained Mercer and Guy Carpenter brands through a transition period, so this is a monitoring item rather than an observed problem. Separately, the industry is investing hard in AI-enabled risk analytics (Aon launched an AI Risk Diagnostic; Verisk acquired a catastrophe-intelligence firm), and Marsh's answer is Business Client Services, which centralizes operations, technology, data and AI investment firmwide. Whether centralized AI investment in a people-based intermediary compresses cost or compresses the value of intermediation itself is not answerable from current disclosure.

**Competitive Position: 6.0**, down modestly from 6.5. Share is stable, the alternative-capital franchise is genuinely counter-cyclical, but the largest business line is shrinking on price and the segment margin mix has inverted.

## Risk Assessment

**Greensill — the dated binary.** Marsh Ltd. brokered trade credit insurance for Greensill Capital from 2014. Applicants in the Australian omnibus proceedings have collectively claimed ~$5B plus interest and costs; Credit Suisse funds added Marsh in November 2023 claiming ~$2B. White Oak settled in May 2025 and was "recovered through the Company's E&O insurance." Greensill Bank AG, which had claimed ~$3B, settled in June 2026. Marsh recorded a **gross liability of $425M in Q1 2026** covering the Greensill Bank settlement plus its best estimate of remaining Credit Suisse claims. **Trial is scheduled for September 2026.** The company warns it "may record additional charges in future periods."

Two features make this the highest-probability source of a near-term surprise in either direction. The accrual is described as gross, and the filing's explicit E&O recovery language for White Oak is absent here — so whether an offsetting recoverable exists is unknown. And the trial falls one quarter after the balance-sheet date. Context on magnitude: $425M is roughly 10% of one year's net income and 8.5% of FY2025 FCF. A doubling would be a quarter's earnings event, not a solvency event.

**Goodwill test.** Marsh performed only a qualitative assessment in 3Q25, relying on headroom established by its **2023** quantitative assessment. That baseline predates roughly $6B of McGriff goodwill and a ~23% decline in average traded price. RIS carries $19.6B of the $24.4B goodwill and is the unit with compressing margins. A qualitative test is permissible under ASC 350 and aggregate headroom against a $90.5B market cap is very large, so there is no evidence of impairment; the methodological staleness raises the probability of a quantitative refresh at the 3Q26 test date. Any write-down would be non-cash but would test the market's willingness to look through it.

**Pricing cycle.** Sector-wide and partly priced, but Marsh's oversized property cat book gives it above-average exposure to the specific line falling fastest.

**Fiduciary interest decay.** Down $94M to $403M in FY2025, $173M in 1H26 versus $202M. High-margin revenue that flows almost entirely to operating income.

**U.K. tax.** The U.K. authority issued closure notices for 2016-2020 disallowing certain interest deductions; Marsh has appealed and resolution "may take several years." The assessed amount is not disclosed and gross unrecognized tax benefits are only $112M firmwide, which does not necessarily bound this item. Structurally live because essentially all external debt sits at the U.S. parent while proceeds finance foreign operations.

**Unquantified legacy.** From the 1998 Sedgwick acquisition, Marsh retains a River Thames policy guarantee and an evergreen letter of credit for English & American policies incepting 1980-1983, against which it "anticipates that additional claimants may seek to recover." Decades-old run-off, unquantified.

**FX.** Roughly 52% of revenue is non-USD; a 10% same-direction move in EUR/GBP/AUD/CAD shifts full-year net operating income by ~$130M. First-half translation reduced net equity by $184M.

**Management and governance.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed in five months (February 25, March 10, and two on April 14), and their texts could not be retrieved — the specific personnel changes are unverified. What is verifiable: CFO Mark McGivney's role was expanded to COO and CFO effective April 15, 2026, and Martin South now carries the title Chief Client Officer rather than leading Marsh broking. Combined with the rebrand, Thrive and the new Business Client Services organization, this is consistent with deliberate centralization; it is equally consistent with ordinary succession, and I draw no performance inference.

**Short interest** is 1.4% of float with 2.6 days to cover — no crowded bearish positioning, and no squeeze dynamic either way.

**Risk Factors: 5.0.** A specific, dated litigation binary inside one quarter, a goodwill test with a stale baseline at the next test date, an unquantified tax assessment, and no asset cushion behind negative tangible equity. Partially offset by the modest absolute size of the litigation accrual relative to annual cash flow.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 27.7% against 26.0% one-year realized is fair — 1.7 points of spread, 43rd percentile within the current chain. The informative feature is the term structure: backwardation from 27.7% at 17 days to 25.2% at 45 days to 22.1% at 164 days, with the September 18 tenor pricing a **7.7% implied move and a +2.9% put skew** while the six-month tenor shows −3.7%. The September expiry brackets the Greensill trial, and the positioning is consistent with hedging that specific event rather than a directional bet on the business. Put/call open interest rose from 0.55 to 0.85 while the price fell 4.0% from the July 29 high of $197.59, and ATM IV rose 5.2 points over the same window — protection being bought after a rally, into a known catalyst. Nothing here challenges the fundamental view; it corroborates treating September as a two-sided event and argues against adding immediately ahead of it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $238 (+25%)

Underlying growth of 5% in Q2 marks the trough of the reinsurance downcycle, and January 1, 2027 renewals show rate declines decelerating from −16%. Thrive delivers the full $400M annualized savings — $239M of the $500M cost budget is already incurred, so charges are behind and savings ahead — lifting RIS margins back toward 28%+ in 2027. Stabilization of the $403M fiduciary interest line instead of erosion. The September trial resolves at or below the $425M accrual, removing the overhang and possibly revealing an E&O recovery. Marsh Management Consulting sustains low-double-digit growth, Consulting rises toward 36-37% of revenue, and the market pays 21x on $11.41 of 2027 EPS as the mix de-risks and the buyback compounds a 2.8% annual share-count reduction against $4.2B of remaining authorization. That is roughly $238, essentially the top of the analyst range.

### Bear Case — $158 (−17%)

Property cat rate declines steepen again at January 1, 2027 (the index has already accelerated from −12% to −16%), and Guy Carpenter's revenue declines mid-single digits for a full year. Marsh Risk decelerates below 4% as primary premium rates follow reinsurance down and MMA's roll-up faces the same pricing headwind. The September trial produces an incremental charge above the accrual, and the 3Q26 goodwill refresh — the first quantitative test since 2023, now covering $19.6B of RIS goodwill against compressing margins — produces a write-down. Thrive's total cost exceeds $500M while savings arrive later than guided, and 2027 consensus falls to ~$10.90. The multiple compresses toward WTW's ~15x, giving roughly $158 — modestly above the 52-week low of $156.60.

### Base Case — $208 (+10% price, ~12% total return)

18.2x 2027 consensus $11.41. Underlying growth holds at 4-5%, Thrive delivers most of $400M, buybacks continue at ~$2.5-3.0B annually, the September trial resolves within a range the market can absorb, and the multiple stays where it is. Total return of roughly 12% including the 2.09% dividend, with a bull/bear span of +25%/−17%. Base-to-bear reward:risk is approximately 0.6:1; bull-to-bear is 1.5:1. Neither supports adding at this price, and both support holding a quality compounder that generates $5B of cash and shrinks its share count.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment: Q3 2026 earnings (late October), which will report the September trial outcome and the 3Q goodwill test.**

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $168-172** (≤15x 2027 consensus $11.41, ≥6.0% FCF yield) with underlying growth still ≥4% — this reconstitutes the asymmetry that existed in June and matches management's own Q2 buyback zone.
2. **Guy Carpenter underlying revenue returns to positive growth** for two consecutive quarters, or the property cat rate-on-line index decline moderates to single digits at the January 1, 2027 renewal. This removes the largest drag on the growth score.
3. **Greensill resolved at or below the $425M accrual with no incremental charge**, combined with reaffirmed Thrive savings of $400M and 2027 consensus holding above $11.40.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **An incremental Greensill charge above $300M**, or disclosure that the $425M gross accrual carries no E&O recovery and the Credit Suisse exposure is being re-estimated upward.
2. **A goodwill impairment at the 3Q26 quantitative test**, particularly in RIS — this would confirm that acquired client relationships are attriting faster than the 11.6-year amortization assumption.
3. **Underlying revenue growth below 3% for two consecutive quarters** with Marsh Risk (not just Guy Carpenter) decelerating, or 2027 consensus falling below $10.90 — the point at which the EPS bridge from Thrive and buybacks stops offsetting the pricing cycle.
4. Net leverage above 3.0x, or a buyback pause funded by commercial paper outside the normal first-half seasonal pattern.

**Score trajectory note.** Prior reviews scored 7.5 (February), 6.4 (May) and 6.0 (June), all HOLD; this report is 5.7 and a fourth HOLD. The decline no longer reflects further fundamental deterioration — Q2 underlying growth accelerated to 5% from 4%, Consulting margins expanded, consensus rose slightly, and the dividend was raised 10% after a $425M charge. It reflects a price advance from the June 3 low that compressed the 2027 multiple from ~14.2x to 16.6x and the FCF yield from ~6.4% to 5.5%, plus a September litigation trial and a goodwill test now inside the next 90 days. Growth (5.0) and management quality (5.5) are held flat, competitive position eased to 6.0 on the RIS margin inversion, and financial health is held at 7.0. A repeated HOLD at a higher price on similar fundamentals is a weaker holding than the same call at $162 — which is precisely why the upgrade triggers are set at price levels, not just at fundamental milestones.

## Conclusion

Marsh is a high-quality intermediary — no underwriting risk, 5.5% free cash flow yield, 127% cash conversion, ~29% return on equity, A−/A3 credit, a shrinking share count and a 2.1% dividend just raised 10%. It is also a business whose largest engine is contracting on price in the steepest reinsurance softening its own index has recorded in 25 years, whose $7.75B acquisition proved roughly EPS-neutral in its first full year while adding $260M of annual interest cost, and whose forward earnings depend on a $400M cost program and a 2.8% annual buyback to convert 4% revenue growth into 9% EPS growth.

At 18.1x 2026 and 16.6x 2027 consensus, that trade-off is fairly struck. Management's capital allocation deserves credit — $1.5B repurchased in the first half at $166.73 average, into the trough — but the shares now sit 14% above that level, and the September Greensill trial plus the first quantitative goodwill test since 2023 both land before the next full data point. The options market is pricing a 7.7% move through September with elevated put skew, which is the right way to think about the next ninety days.

Hold for the ~12% total return to a $208 base case and the compounding from buybacks and Thrive savings. Add at $168-172, or on confirmation that Guy Carpenter's revenue has turned. Do not add here.
