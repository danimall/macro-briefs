**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hasbro presents one of the more misleading screens in consumer discretionary. The trailing P/E reads -53x and FY2025 shows a $322.4M net loss, but both figures are driven by a single $1,021.9M non-cash goodwill impairment in the Consumer Products (toy) segment. Strip the impairment out and the business generated $893.2M of operating cash flow and $829.9M of free cash flow (17.7% margin) in a "loss" year, with management guiding to a 24-25% adjusted operating margin and $1.40-1.45B of adjusted EBITDA for FY2026. The business that emerges is a high-margin, increasingly capital-light games-and-licensing operation carried by MAGIC: THE GATHERING (MTG), which grew revenue ~$641.5M in 2025 inside a Wizards of the Coast (WOTC) segment running a 46% operating margin.

The investment question is not solvency. It is whether MTG's growth and moat are durable enough to justify holding through a leveraged balance sheet, a live cybersecurity incident, and a structurally declining toy unit. On forward fundamentals the setup is constructive: full-year consensus EPS has risen to $6.01 (FY26) and $6.41 (FY27), up 5.8% and 2.5% over 90 days, placing the stock at roughly 14x forward earnings versus a 15.5x peer median. The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters by 28-66%. The analyst low target of $85 sits above the current $83.90, and the median of $115 implies ~19x forward — material re-rating potential if the impairment distortion clears the trailing screen and MTG momentum holds.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concrete: company-level growth is guided at only +3-5% constant currency because the toy unit (Nerf, Beyblade, Transformers, licensed Marvel/Star Wars) is shrinking and now subsidized by one franchise; the balance sheet carries $3.28B of debt against a thin 10.2% equity-to-assets ratio after the impairment; a contained-but-unresolved cyber incident will dent Q2 consumer-products numbers (the current-quarter EPS estimate fell 9.9% over 30 days and 13.1% over 90 days); and the proxy shows a governance flag — $35.3M of CEO comp "actually paid" in a net-loss year against five-year TSR that lags peers. We rate HAS a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: forward earnings power and MTG's moat support the position, but concentration, leverage, and cyber overhang cap the asymmetry below a buy threshold.

## Company Overview

Hasbro is a century-old games and IP company that has deliberately reshaped itself away from toy manufacturing toward higher-margin owned-IP games and third-party licensing. It reports three segments:

- **Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming (WOTC)** — MAGIC: THE GATHERING and DUNGEONS & DRAGONS, plus digital licensing (Monopoly Go! via Scopely; Baldur's Gate 3 via Larian) and internally developed titles (Exodus, Warlock, both slated for 2027). FY2025 revenue $2.19B, +44.7%, at a 46% operating margin.
- **Consumer Products (toys)** — Nerf, Play-Doh, Transformers, Peppa Pig, Beyblade, plus licensed Marvel and Star Wars. FY2025 revenue $2.44B, -4.2%, swinging to a $942.6M operating loss on the goodwill impairment.
- **Entertainment** — a $76.8M stub after the eOne Film & TV divestiture, with content spend cut more than 90%.

The "Playing to Win" framework buckets brands into Grow ($3.48B, +24.4% — MTG, Monopoly, Play-Doh, Marvel, D&D), Optimize ($698M, -4.6% — Transformers, Star Wars, Peppa), and Reinvent ($524M, -13.7% — Nerf, Beyblade, Power Rangers). The strategic logic is sound: MTG is a structurally advantaged owned-IP collectible game with network effects, a deep secondary market, and a "Universes Beyond" licensing flywheel (Final Fantasy, Spider-Man, Avatar crossovers), while the "Partner Scale" model out-licenses IP to outside developers and collects royalties without funding the studios. Monopoly Go! contributed $168.0M of licensing revenue in 2025 as Scopely's product, not Hasbro's.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue was $4.70B in FY2025, up 13.7% from $4.14B, reversing a multi-year decline from $5.86B (2022) to $5.00B (2023) to $4.14B (2024). The rebound is entirely a WOTC/MTG story — MTG alone added $641.5M while toys contracted again.

GAAP operating profit collapsed to $11.1M (0.2% margin) from $690M (16.7%) in 2024 because of the non-cash impairment in Consumer Products. The cash statement tells a different story: operating cash flow rose to $893.2M and free cash flow was $829.9M, a 17.7% margin in the headline-loss year. Earnings adjusted for non-cash items reached $1.21B. The impairment does not touch cash generation.

The balance sheet is the genuine concern. Total long-term debt is $3.28B against $776.6M cash plus $105.4M short-term investments. The Altman Z-Score of 1.22 sits in the distress zone, but this is largely mechanical: the impairment eroded equity to 10.2% of assets and pushed reported D/E to 8.82x. This is not a liquidity event. The $1.25B revolver is fully available and was refinanced in February 2026 with maturity extended to 2031, all debt is fixed-rate, covenants are in compliance, and FY2026 capex guidance of $250M is comfortably covered by free cash flow.

Capital allocation is a watch item. Dividends consumed $392.5M in 2025, roughly 47% of FCF, covered about 2x but a real claim on cash given the leverage and a GAAP-loss year. Debt repurchases were modest ($119.9M of 2026/2027 notes) and buybacks minimal. Product development expense rose to $385.6M (8.2% of revenue), reflecting capitalized digital-game costs that carry write-off risk if Exodus or Warlock underperform at their 2027 launches.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats, including +65.8% (Q2 2025), +56.5% (Q4 2025), and +28.3% (Q1 2026), almost entirely MTG-driven.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is bifurcated and concentrated. WOTC is the engine — MTG growing 40%+ at 46% margins, with the Universes Beyond cadence and digital-licensing royalties extending the franchise's reach. Against this, the toy unit is in structural decline (Reinvent brands -13.7%, Optimize -4.6%), and Entertainment is a stub after the eOne exit.

At the company level, FY2026 guidance is +3-5% revenue growth on a constant-currency basis. That is modest top-line growth carried by margin and mix, not volume. The estimate-revision picture reflects this split clearly: full-year FY26 consensus EPS rose +3.8% over 30 days, while the current-quarter estimate fell to $1.12. The near-term cut is the cyber incident's effect on Consumer Products order processing and shipping; management expects most delayed Q2 shipments to recover in 2H 2026.

This is a credible but not explosive growth thesis. The positive full-year revisions plus four large consecutive beats argue consensus is still catching up to MTG's earnings power, but the absence of revenue acceleration at the company level and the single-franchise dependency keep this below the profile (revenue >40% with broad-based expansion) that would warrant a higher growth score. The growth is real, durable, high-margin, and narrow.

## Competitive Landscape

MTG is the differentiator. It functions less like a toy and more like a game-publisher/collectibles network — owned IP, network effects, a robust secondary market, and pricing power, all at ~46% segment margins. The Universes Beyond strategy converts external franchises into MTG sets that pull in new collectors, and the Partner Scale licensing model (Scopely, Larian) generates third-party-funded royalty revenue without studio capex. This is a structurally advantaged position improving in trajectory.

The other side of the ledger is deteriorating. The toy business faces secular headwinds — short product cycles, competition from screens for kids' attention, retailer concentration, and tariff-inflated China/Vietnam sourcing — and Hasbro's Reinvent and Optimize brands are shrinking. The "Aging Up" pivot toward adult collectors and gamers is the right strategic response and distinguishes Hasbro from pure-play toymakers like Mattel, but it leaves the company increasingly dependent on one franchise. Customer concentration compounds this: the top five customers are ~35% of revenue, with Amazon at 11% and Walmart at 9%, and no long-term purchase commitments.

The high-value segment is widening its moat while the low-value segment erodes, with the company's fortunes converging on MTG.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are live and not fully reflected in the screen:

1. **Single-franchise concentration.** With MTG driving essentially all growth, the 10-K now explicitly names "franchise fatigue / oversaturation" as a risk — directly relevant given the aggressive Universes Beyond release cadence. Any deceleration in MTG hits the entire equity thesis.
2. **Cybersecurity incident (live).** Disclosed April 1, 2026, the unauthorized network access forced systems offline and delayed the Q1 10-Q. As of the April 23 update access was contained and MTG shipments continued, but Consumer Products Q2 revenue and operating profit will be impacted by order/shipping/invoicing delays plus advisory costs. The scope of any data exfiltration or ransomware remains unconfirmed in Hasbro's filings.
3. **Leverage on thin equity.** The Z-Score of 1.22 overstates distress but underscores limited cushion. Refinancing risk is mitigated (revolver to 2031, fixed-rate, covenant-compliant), but the dividend's ~47% FCF claim leaves less room for error.
4. **Tariffs (with offsetting optionality).** ~$44.9M of tariff cost hit COGS in 2025, and forward tariff assumptions drove the toy impairment. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling against IEEPA tariffs opens a possible 2026 refund tailwind on duties paid since April 2025, though refund mechanics remain legally unsettled.
5. **Governance/alignment.** The CEO comp "actually paid" in a net-loss year stands against five-year TSR (108.23) that lags the peer group (151.09). A January 2026 executive departure and the Boston HQ relocation add execution/talent-retention friction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 22.1% against 28.8% one-year historical volatility — implied vol is cheap (-6.7% spread), in the 1st percentile of its own chain. Near-term put/call skew of 3.6% shows mild put demand, but the put/call volume ratio is very low at 0.10 and the term structure is in normal contango (22.1% near-term vs. 34.7% at six months). IV has fallen 12.5 points (from 34.6% to 22.1%) since the May 19 snapshot as the stock declined 10.5%, indicating resolved event risk around the Q1 print rather than building fear. The 7.5% short interest with 4.5 days to cover is moderate. The options complex is consistent with a market that views the cyber overhang as contained and is not positioning for a sharp downside break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The GAAP loss is an accounting artifact; the real business throws off $830M of FCF and runs at 24-25% adjusted margins on the strength of a wide-moat MTG franchise growing 40%+. Full-year estimates are rising, the company has beaten four straight quarters by wide margins, and at ~14x forward (below peers) with a covered 3.4% dividend the stock is cheap on normalized earnings power. Cyber-delayed Q2 shipments recover in 2H, the IEEPA tariff refund provides upside not in numbers, and re-rating toward the peer multiple plus MTG growth carries the stock toward the analyst median of $115 (+37%).

**Bear Case.** MTG growth proves cyclical or hits Universes Beyond fatigue, removing the one engine; the toy business continues to erode and triggers further impairment; the cyber incident reveals material data loss or larger remediation cost; and the leveraged balance sheet (thin equity, ~47% FCF dividend) forces a dividend reassessment. In that scenario the multiple compresses and the stock retraces toward the 52-week low region around $67-70 (-17% to -20%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FY2026 Q2 print (the delayed 10-Q and cyber-impact quantification), then Q3, over a 6-12 month horizon. The cyber-scope confirmation and any FY2026 guidance revision are the key near-term inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A second consecutive quarter of MTG-led adjusted EPS beats with FY2026 guidance raised above the current +3-5% revenue / 24-25% margin frame, AND forward EPS estimates continuing to revise higher (FY27 above $6.50).
- Cyber incident formally closed with no material data-loss liability, removing the overhang and confirming the 2H shipment recovery.
- A confirmed IEEPA tariff refund of material size, or a credible dividend-plus-deleveraging plan that lifts the equity cushion. Note: sector positioning would still warrant caution even on an upgrade.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- MTG/WOTC revenue growth decelerating to single digits or a sequential decline, signaling franchise fatigue.
- A further goodwill impairment in Consumer Products, or a dividend cut indicating balance-sheet stress.
- Disclosure of material data exfiltration/ransomware liability, or remediation cost materially above current expectations.
- Full-year EPS estimates rolling over (FY26 consensus falling below $5.70).

## Conclusion

Hasbro is a cash-generative, MTG-anchored business whose GAAP results look distressed. The net loss and negative trailing P/E are functions of a non-cash toy impairment, not operating distress. On forward earnings the stock is reasonably valued at ~14x rising consensus, below the 15.5x peer median, with a 3.4% dividend covered ~2x and an analyst low target above the current price. The specific 12-month growth thesis is intact: continued MTG-led beats (four straight, 28-66% surprises), positive full-year estimate revisions, cyber-impacted Q2 shipments recovering in 2H, and re-rating as the impairment distortion clears the trailing screen, with potential tariff-refund upside on top.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a higher-conviction call is the combination of single-franchise concentration (now an explicit 10-K risk), a leveraged balance sheet with thin equity, a live cyber incident of unconfirmed scope, modest company-level growth guided at +3-5%, and a governance flag in the proxy. The risk/reward is positive but not asymmetric — base-case fair value in the mid-$90s to ~$100 against a bear case near $67-70. We rate HAS a **HOLD (growth_hold)**: hold for the MTG-driven earnings power and re-rating optionality, and reassess at the next earnings checkpoint as the cyber scope and FY2026 trajectory resolve.
