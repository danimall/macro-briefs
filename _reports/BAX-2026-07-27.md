**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Baxter International is a smaller, less-diversified medical-technology company following the 2023 sale of BioPharma Solutions and the January 2025 sale of its Kidney Care franchise (Vantive) to Carlyle for $3.8B. The intended result, a leaner "pure-play" hospital-products company with a deleveraged balance sheet, has not materialized on the profitability side. Continuing-operations revenue optically grew 6% to $11.24B in FY2025, but roughly half of that came from a low-margin manufacturing-supply agreement selling products back to the divested Vantive business. Underlying organic growth was around 3% for the year and turned negative (-1%) in Q1 2026, with all three operating segments posting operating-income declines.

The financial picture is weak and, on the trajectory data, deteriorating. GAAP gross margin fell from 37.5% in 2024 to 30.1% in 2025, with a further ~5-point year-over-year contraction in Q1 2026. Baxter has reported net losses in three of the last four years (FY2025 net loss of $957M, EPS -$1.87), carries ~$9.46B of debt against ~$6.0B of equity, and sits one notch above junk at S&P BBB- / Moody's Baa3 with the outlook reversed back to Negative in Q1 2026. The Altman Z-Score of 1.52 places the company in the distress zone. Management amended (loosened) the revolver's net-leverage covenant in November 2025 and slashed the dividend from $0.17 to $0.01 per quarter to accelerate deleveraging.

We reaffirm AVOID. The consistent AVOID history across four prior reports, with a declining score trajectory (4.5 in February to 3.4 in July), reflects a thesis that new data continues to confirm rather than reverse. The flagship Novum IQ large-volume pump remains under a US/Canada distribution hold from three FDA Class I recalls with no clear timeline, estimate revisions are broadly negative, there is no open-market insider buying, and the CFO seat is filled only on an interim basis during a CEO transition. There is genuine turnaround optionality if the Novum hold lifts and tariffs ease against a depressed margin base, but the balance sheet leaves little room for error and no dated catalyst supports entry today.

## Company Overview

Baxter (~37,500 employees, products sold in 100+ countries) now operates in three reporting segments:

- **Medical Products & Therapies (~$5.3B, ~47% of sales)** — sterile IV solutions, infusion pumps and systems, administration sets, parenteral nutrition, and Advanced Surgery hemostats/sealants. This is the highest-margin segment (~18% operating margin) and includes Baxter's leading US position in IV fluids.
- **Healthcare Systems & Technologies (~$3.1B, ~27%)** — smart beds, patient monitoring/diagnostics, respiratory devices, and OR integration, largely the 2021 Hillrom acquisition plus the Welch Allyn brand. Contains the troubled Front Line Care reporting unit.
- **Pharmaceuticals (~$2.5B, ~22%)** — specialty injectables, inhaled anesthetics, and drug compounding.

The competitive base rests on scale in essential hospital consumables, a broad product catalog, and long-term GPO/IDN contracts (~$8.87B of remaining performance obligations). Management explicitly acknowledges in the 10-K that the divestitures have left the company "more vulnerable to changing market conditions and increased volatility." The current market capitalization is roughly $11.7B at a $22.40 share price, down ~22% over the trailing year and well below the $29.35 52-week high.

## Financial Analysis

The financial-health dimension is the clearest and most reliable discriminator here, and it points down.

**Losses and margin compression.** The FY2025 net loss followed losses of $649M in FY2024 and $2.43B in FY2022; the only recent positive year (FY2023, $2.66B) was driven by divestiture gains, not operations. Even excluding special items, gross margin dropped ~4.2 points on tariffs, unfavorable manufacturing variances, adverse product mix, and the dilutive Vantive MSA. Q1 2026 ex-items gross margin fell a further ~5.0 points year-over-year.

**Special items are large and recurring, not one-off.** FY2025 carried over $1.0B in special items, including a second consecutive year of Front Line Care goodwill impairment ($485M in 2025, $425M in 2024), a $290M Welch Allyn trade-name impairment, Hurricane Helene costs, and Novum recall reserves. The pattern of repeated impairments on the Hillrom/Welch Allyn assets indicates the ~$12B 2021 acquisition is quietly underperforming, and management warns that a further 5% fair-value decline in Front Line Care would trigger ~$160M more impairment.

**Cash flow is positive but modest against the debt load.** Continuing-operations operating cash flow was $951M in 2025 and $213M in Q1 2026, against capex of ~$513M. That leaves limited free cash flow to service the debt, fund R&D, and rebuild the balance sheet simultaneously, a constraint management itself flags as limiting growth investment.

**Leverage and credit.** Debt/equity is ~2.3x. Divestiture proceeds repaid $3.81B of debt in 2025 (with $2.0B refinanced via new notes). The current ratio of 2.3x is adequate and near-term maturities are modest, but the ratings outlook reversal in Q1 2026 comes after a brief Q4 2025 stabilization. The revolver's maximum net-leverage covenant was amended for the quarters through Q3 2026, an admission of covenant pressure. The Altman Z sits in the distress zone, driven by near-zero EBIT/assets and thin equity/assets.

**Dividend.** The dividend cut removes Baxter from income-oriented mandates and confirms a defensive posture rather than a stable-compounder profile.

## Growth Analysis

Reported top-line growth overstates the health of the continuing business. Of the ~6 points of 2025 continuing-ops revenue growth, roughly 3 came from the Vantive MSA — selling products back to the divested kidney-care business at low margin.

Estimate revisions confirm negative momentum. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 16.9% over 90 days (to $0.37), current-year EPS is roughly flat at $1.92, and next-year EPS has been trimmed to $2.01. This is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would flag an inflection; the estimate trend is broadly falling. The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, including a -18.7% miss in Q4 2025 — which does not support high confidence in forward estimates.

There are genuine growth pockets: Advanced Surgery (+9–13%) and Drug Compounding (+10–30%) are expanding. But these are outweighed by structural headwinds. The Novum IQ large-volume pump distribution hold means no meaningful Novum LVP sales while it persists, ceding share to competitors in a strategically important category. Injectables & Anesthesia declined ~10% in Q1 2026 on generic pricing competition. And management's language that post-Hurricane Helene fluid-conservation practices "appear to have reset demand levels" in core IV solutions suggests a lasting demand hit rather than a temporary disruption.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here — the company is loss-making on a GAAP basis (trailing EPS -$1.75). On adjusted forward earnings, the stock trades at roughly 11.7x the current-year consensus and ~11.1x the next-year figure. That looks superficially cheap for a medtech company, but it is precisely the value-trap configuration that warrants skepticism: a compressed multiple attached to falling estimates, a distressed balance sheet, and no identifiable catalyst to re-rate the equity.

The lessons of this scoring system are directly relevant: a "cheap" multiple has been reliably anti-predictive, and cheapness sorts the wrong way absent an independent catalyst. Baxter does not clear that bar. The Novum hold has no timeline, the tariff drag is live and unquantified (no refund receivable booked), and the Front Line Care assets carry ongoing impairment risk. The analyst target dispersion — low $17, median $20, mean ~$22, high $40 — is unusually wide, reflecting deep disagreement about whether the turnaround takes hold. The median target sits below the current price, and the guidance from this system on treating targets as floors explicitly does not apply to distressed names with falling estimates.

A DCF is fragile given the negative-organic, margin-compressing trajectory and heavy debt service. If margins stabilize and the Novum hold lifts, there is meaningful operating leverage on the depressed base, which is the entire bull case, but that scenario is not being demonstrated in current results. We score valuation at 4.0: cheap on a headline forward multiple, but the discount reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Baxter competes across fragmented medtech and pharma niches against larger, better-capitalized rivals: BD (Alaris) and ICU Medical in infusion pumps; Stryker, GE HealthCare, and Baxter's own competitors in beds and monitoring; and a crowded generics field in injectables. Several dynamics are eroding position:

- **GPO/IDN consolidation** pressures pricing and can cause sudden revenue loss on a lost award.
- **The Novum hold hands share to competitors** while Baxter steers customers to its older Spectrum IQ pump. Multi-year uncertainty risks permanent share loss in a category Baxter had positioned as a growth driver.
- **Regulatory chemical phase-outs** (DEHP, desflurane in the EEA by 2026, PVC/PFAS scrutiny) threaten portions of the portfolio.
- **International tender-based procurement** compresses margins.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating rather than stable, which is the relevant measure. Baxter retains a durable position in IV solutions and hospital consumables, but the demand reset in that franchise and the pump hold offset the moat. Competitive position scores 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by specific, largely unpriced-catalyst risks rather than generic sector risk:

1. **Novum IQ pump — three FDA Class I recalls (2025).** US/Canada distribution halted July 2025 with no clear timeline; ~$105M reserved. Has spawned securities class actions and derivative suits.
2. **Leverage as a strategic constraint.** Management states indebtedness limits R&D, capex, and growth investment; it has not yet reached its net-leverage target (goal end of 2026). Failure risks a downgrade below investment grade.
3. **Structural IV-demand reset** post-Helene, described by management as potentially permanent.
4. **Tariffs.** An ongoing, unquantified margin drag; Section 232 pharma/device investigation and Section 122 duties create refund uncertainty, with no receivable booked.
5. **Front Line Care impairment risk.** $1.51B goodwill and $390M Welch Allyn trade name remain at risk; a 5% fair-value decline triggers ~$160M more.
6. **IRS transfer-pricing dispute** (Costa Rica/Puerto Rico) — ~$280M reserved, NOPA received Q1 2026. A US valuation allowance on deferred tax assets signals doubt about near-term US profitability.
7. **Litigation stack:** securities class actions, derivative suits, DOJ CIDs (Hillrom respiratory/FCA; a December 2025 CID on IV flexible containers), ethylene oxide claims, and Hillrom antitrust litigation.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 103.3% is rich against 49.8% one-year historical vol, sits at the 83rd percentile of the chain, and jumped 64.7 points (from 38.6% to 103.3%) in the week to July 27. The term structure is in steep backwardation (103.3% near-term vs 43.7% at six months), with a ±9.2% implied move on the nearest expiry, consistent with the Q2 earnings date. Put/call skew is balanced (1.7%) and put/call OI is 0.49, so the positioning is not directionally one-sided; the market is pricing a large binary move around the print without a clear bearish lean. For a fundamental AVOID, this argues against establishing a short into the event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** A new operationally-focused CEO (Andrew Hider, elected July 2025) with fresh, recovery-aligned incentives runs a leaner portfolio through the "Baxter Growth and Performance System." Deleveraging is underway with $3.81B repaid; the India Warning Letter is closing; and Advanced Surgery and Drug Compounding are growing double digits. If the Novum hold lifts and tariffs ease, operating leverage on the depressed margin base could drive a sharp adjusted-EPS recovery and multiple re-rating toward the $40 high analyst target. Bull case ~$32–40.

**Bear Case.** Organic growth stays negative, margins fail to recover, the Novum hold persists, Front Line Care takes another impairment, and leverage pressure forces a downgrade below investment grade. Further estimate cuts and a distress-zone balance sheet drive the stock toward the low analyst target of $17 or below. Bear case ~$15–17.

The base case sits in between: a slow, execution-heavy turnaround with the stock rangebound in the high-teens to low-$20s as investors wait for proof the Novum hold clears and margins stabilize. Given negative organic growth, falling estimates, no insider conviction buying, and an interim-CFO vacancy during a CEO transition, the risk/reward does not support ownership.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The nearest dated event is the Q2 2026 earnings report (implied by the July options backwardation, roughly late July/early August). A further miss or Novum-hold extension could drive the stock lower, but the direction is not asymmetrically bearish — beats on Advanced Surgery/Drug Compounding or a Novum timeline could equally spark a relief rally. There is no dated debt cliff, regulatory ruling, or contract loss with a clear negative resolution date.

**Timing window.** The earnings catalyst resolves within weeks, but the broader turnaround thesis (leverage, margins, Novum) is a multi-quarter-to-multi-year story with no discrete trigger, which is poor for a carry-cost-bearing short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 11.3% of float with 5.8 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme; enough institutional bearishness that the easy downside may be priced, without the >25% level that would make it a crowded short.

**Borrow and options signal.** The rich near-term IV makes protective/directional put strategies expensive and confirms the market prices a large binary move. Balanced skew and put/call OI of 0.49 show positioning is not confirming a one-sided bearish thesis, a warning sign for shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear outcome takes the stock to ~$17 (-24% from $22.40); a squeeze or Novum-hold-lift relief rally could push it toward $28–32 (+25% to +43%). That asymmetry is unfavorable — closer to 1:1 than the 2:1 required — with expensive optionality and a live turnaround narrative.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental thesis is intact, but there is no dated negative catalyst, the reward/risk is roughly symmetric, near-term IV is prohibitively expensive, and turnaround optionality plus moderate short interest create meaningful squeeze risk. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view to constructive):**
- **Novum IQ hold lifts** with a clear US distribution restart timeline, removing the largest revenue and reputational overhang.
- **Organic growth returns to positive** for two consecutive quarters alongside gross-margin stabilization (ex-items gross margin flat-to-up year-over-year).
- **Estimate-revision inflection** — 90-day consensus EPS revisions turn positive, reversing the current -16.9% current-quarter trend.
- **Credit stabilization** — outlook returned to Stable and demonstrable progress toward the end-2026 net-leverage target, plus a permanent CFO appointment.
- **Open-market insider buying** by the new CEO or CFO would be a meaningful confidence signal currently absent.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — Novum hold, tariff drag, IV-demand reset, and leverage — are expected to persist through at least the end of 2026, the deadline management has set for hitting its net-leverage target. We expect no clean fundamental inflection before then.

**Downgrade to more negative view** if: a further Front Line Care impairment is booked, credit is downgraded below investment grade, or organic growth deteriorates further alongside continued covenant pressure.

## Conclusion

Baxter is a distressed post-divestiture turnaround rather than a stable medtech compounder. The evidence is consistent: net losses in three of four years, gross margin down to 30.1%, organic growth negative in Q1 2026, ~$9.46B of debt against a Negative credit outlook one notch above junk, an Altman Z of 1.52 in the distress zone, a dividend cut to a token penny, a loosened revolver covenant, an interim CFO, and no open-market insider buying. The flagship Novum pump remains under an open-ended distribution hold. The stock is cheap on a headline forward multiple (~11.7x), but that discount reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing, and the estimate trend is falling — the classic value-trap configuration.

We reiterate **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.6, consistent with the four prior AVOID calls and the declining score trajectory. There is a real turnaround option embedded in the depressed margin base if Novum reopens and tariffs ease, but that scenario is unproven in current results and unsupported by any dated catalyst. The stock is not a short either — symmetric risk/reward, prohibitively expensive near-term options, and moderate short interest with squeeze potential make it a poor active position. Wait for the Novum hold to lift, organic growth to turn positive, and estimate revisions to inflect before revisiting.
