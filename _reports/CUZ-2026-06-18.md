**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cousins Properties is a self-managed Sun Belt office REIT owning 22.0 million square feet of Class A "lifestyle office" concentrated in Austin, Atlanta, Charlotte, Tampa, Phoenix, Dallas, and Nashville. The investment case rests on a bifurcation thesis: newer, amenity-rich trophy office in growing Sun Belt cities takes share from commodity stock while the broader office sector remains distressed. The data supports this. FY2025 FFO grew 5.6% to $2.84/share, same-property NOI was positive (+2.4% straight-line), and second-generation cash re-leasing spreads accelerated to +15.2% in Q1 2026 from +3.5% for the full prior year. The balance sheet is investment-grade, 87% fixed-rate, conservatively laddered, and the dividend is covered at roughly 2.2x by FFO.

Management has executed an unusually disciplined capital cycle: issuing forward equity at ~$30.44 in 2025, then repurchasing 3.9M shares at ~$23.36 in Q1 2026, while recycling out of non-core assets (Harborview, One Eleven Congress) into trophy buildings (300 South Tryon, The Link). The April 2026 credit facility recast (revolver upsized to $1.2B, extended to 2031, with lower SOFR spreads) de-risks the near-term liquidity and refinancing picture that has crippled less-capitalized office peers.

The constraints are concrete. Interest expense rose 30% in FY2025 and 23% in Q1 2026 as the company refinances 3.8–4.1% legacy debt into roughly 5% coupons, and the higher-for-longer rate path keeps that drag intact. Current-year EPS estimates have been cut sharply (the lone covering analyst's FY estimate fell from $0.39 to $0.09 over 90 days, partly reflecting the One Eleven Congress impairment running through GAAP). Austin is ~35% of office NOI and 53% tech, a single-market/single-sector concentration that would be the first casualty of a tech employment downturn. At ~$28, with a 4.5% yield and a peer-median valuation, the stock is fairly priced rather than mispriced. We rate CUZ a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias.

## Company Overview

Cousins Properties (Georgia corporation, NYSE: CUZ) operates as a fully integrated REIT through Cousins Properties LP (>99% owned), with 351 full-time employees as of YE2025. The portfolio is 22.0 million square feet of office plus ~974,000 square feet of other space across seven Sun Belt markets. Geographic NOI is dominated by Austin (~35% of office NOI) and Atlanta (~31%), with Charlotte (~11%), Phoenix (~8%), Tampa (~7.5%), and Dallas (~5%) filling out the balance.

The strategy is straightforward: own the newest, best-located, most-amenitized buildings in growing Sun Belt cities, maintain low leverage so the company can buy when the cycle dislocates, and let the flight-to-quality dynamic within office concentrate tenant demand into premium space. The differentiation shows up in operating metrics (positive same-store NOI, strong re-leasing spreads, and investment-grade capital access) at a time when much of the office sector is capital-starved and posting negative absorption.

## Financial Analysis

For a REIT, FFO is the operative metric, not GAAP net income. FY2025 FFO was $478.4M, or $2.84/share, up from $2.69 in 2024 and $2.62 in 2023. Per-share FFO growth despite a rising share count is a real positive in an office sector where many peers have seen FFO erode. Q1 2026 FFO was $0.73/share versus $0.74 a year earlier — essentially flat, with rising interest expense and dilution timing offsetting operating growth.

Revenue rose 15.7% to $993.8M in FY2025, driven mostly by acquisitions (Non-Same-Property NOI nearly quintupled). Organic growth was modest but positive: same-property NOI +0.9% cash in FY2025, and +1.7% (consolidated plus JV share) in Q1 2026. The standout operating signal is leasing economics. Second-generation cash rents jumped to +28.7% straight-line in Q1 2026. If sustained, that points to genuine pricing power in CUZ's submarkets. Occupancy was 90.7% leased at YE2025 (down from 91.6%), with the gap between leased and economically occupied (88.3% in Q4'25) reflecting signed-but-not-commenced leases — a forward NOI tailwind.

The balance sheet is the company's clearest competitive asset. Total debt was ~$3.77B at Q1 2026 (up from $3.34B at YE2025 after the 300 South Tryon acquisition), with a weighted-average maturity of ~4.1 years. Management states its leverage metrics rank among the strongest among public office REITs, and the company holds investment-grade ratings. About $793M was available on the recast $1.2B facility at Q1 2026. The dividend is conservative: $1.28 annualized against $2.84 FFO is a ~45% payout, leaving substantial retained cash for reinvestment.

A note on the headline Altman Z-Score of 1.53 ("distress"): this is a false signal for an asset-heavy REIT. The score penalizes low asset turnover, minimal working capital, and low EBIT/assets — all structural features of the real estate model, where value sits in depreciated property and earnings flow through as FFO rather than GAAP EBIT. CUZ is investment-grade, covenant-compliant, and dividend-covered. Similarly, the trailing GAAP loss (Q1 2026 net loss of $(0.15)/share) is a non-cash artifact of the $36.6M One Eleven Congress impairment, not an operating deterioration. Any P/E-based read on this stock is meaningless.

The genuine financial headwind is the cost of debt. CUZ is refinancing legacy 3.78–4.09% private notes into new issuance at 4.875–5.875% (the February 2026 $500M notes priced at a 5.001% yield). Leverage level is not the problem; the marginal cost of capital is. The April 2026 facility recast — lower SOFR spreads (72.5bps over SOFR) and a maturity extension to 2031 — partially mitigates this, but in a higher-for-longer environment the refinancing drag persists.

## Growth Analysis

Forward FFO growth has three levers: organic re-leasing, lease commencement of signed space, and accretive capital deployment. The re-leasing acceleration is the most encouraging, and the leased-versus-occupied gap embeds a built-in tailwind as signed leases commence. The buyback at ~$23.36 is accretive to per-share FFO at a discount to estimated NAV.

Working against these is the sharp downward revision in consensus EPS estimates (a -77.2% revision over 90 days). Much of that reflects the impairment flowing through GAAP rather than an FFO collapse, but the direction is unambiguous — earnings momentum on a reported basis is negative, and that matters for the multiple. The earnings surprise record is mixed (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss over four quarters), so confidence in the consensus path should be moderate.

The thematic backdrop reinforces caution. A higher-for-longer rate path weighs on commercial real estate financing and tenant expansion. Austin's tech concentration intersects directly with any tech-sector employment weakness. Office remains a structurally challenged sector, which argues for tempering optimism on the growth durability claim. We score growth prospects at 5.5 — positive organic signals offset by rate-driven FFO drag and falling consensus estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

CUZ trades at ~$28 (recently $27.71) with a market cap of ~$4.65B and a 4.5% dividend yield. The negative reported P/E is uninformative. On FFO, the stock trades around 9.7–10x FY2025 FFO — a discount to where higher-quality office traded pre-2022, but appropriate given the rate environment and sector overhang.

Against Office REIT peers, the picture is balanced rather than cheap. Peer-median P/E is 25.0 and median yield 4.8%; CUZ's yield sits modestly below median, while its balance sheet quality is above median. Kilroy (5.6% yield, -15% 52-week discount), Highwoods (6.6% yield), and SL Green (negative P/E, -23% discount) all offer either higher yield or deeper discounts, though with weaker balance sheets or coastal exposure. CUZ is the highest-quality name in the group and is priced accordingly — there is no obvious mispricing to exploit.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price: low $24, mean $30.25, median $31, high $34. With the stock at ~$28, the consensus mean implies ~7% upside plus the yield. The setup does not present asymmetric upside; it is a fairly valued, dividend-supported holding. We assign a valuation score of 5.5 — the absolute yield and balance-sheet quality provide support, but the cheapness here historically marks fairly-priced quality rather than a value-trap or an actionable discount, and there is no near-term catalyst to force a re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

CUZ holds the highest-quality balance sheet among Sun Belt-focused office REITs (peer set includes Highwoods, Piedmont, plus private equity and pension fund bidders for assets). Its differentiation is showing in the data: positive same-store NOI, strongly positive re-leasing spreads, and investment-grade capital access while many office peers face refinancing stress. The flight-to-quality bifurcation is supported by 2.1M square feet of leasing in 2025 and continued acquisition opportunism (300 South Tryon, The Link, Vantage South End).

The competitive trajectory is improving within a structurally challenged industry. CUZ's TSR (95.72 cumulative) modestly beat its office peer group (81.12) over the measurement period. Whether CUZ is a durable structural winner or the best house in a bad neighborhood is unresolved. The One Eleven Congress impairment and the slight occupancy dip are reminders that even trophy Sun Belt assets are not immune to office demand erosion. We score competitive position at 6.0 — improving relative trajectory, strong quality differentiation, tempered by sector-wide secular risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** Top-20 tenants represent 38.6% of annualized rent; the largest single tenant is 8.9%. Austin is 53.1% technology; Charlotte is 19.2% banking/financials; Tampa 25.0% biotech/health sciences. A tech employment downturn would hit the largest market hardest.

**Interest-rate / refinancing.** Roughly $500M of term loans and several mortgages mature in 2026; the company is terming out at higher coupons. Rising debt cost is the single biggest drag on FFO growth. The facility recast mitigates near-term liquidity but does not reverse the higher marginal cost.

**Impairments.** FY2025 booked $14.3M; Q1 2026 added the One Eleven Congress impairment tied to an agreed below-carry sale (closing expected Q3 2026). This signals that even within CUZ's portfolio, some assets are being marked to clearing prices.

**Neuhoff JV (Nashville).** The 50%-owned development is still leasing up; its $251M construction loan (CUZ share $125.6M) matures September 2026 with one 12-month extension option. CUZ loaned its partner $19.6M (SOFR+625bps) to fund the partner's equity, indicating a capital-constrained partner. JV losses widened to $(8.2)M in FY2025. Small relative to the whole, but a concentrated development/JV risk.

**Governance / takeover.** A 3.9% ownership-limitation charter provision is anti-takeover and could block a control premium. Say-on-pay passed with ~9% opposition — modest pushback.

Idiosyncratic risk is moderate and partly priced (the impairment is already in the stock; the rate drag is well understood). The concentration risk is the element least fully priced given the otherwise clean financials. We score risk factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 26.6% against 26.1% trailing realized — fairly priced, with IV at only the 9th percentile of the current chain. The notable feature is a 34.1% put/call skew (elevated put demand) consistent with the 10.9% short interest and Resolution Capital trimming ~$62M. The term structure is in contango (long-dated IV 35.7% versus near-term 26.6%), a normal, non-stressed shape. The extreme 1111% ATM IV reading in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is disregarded. Net read: the options market reflects mild bearish positioning rather than imminent event risk, which aligns with a HOLD stance rather than a high-conviction directional call.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Sun Belt flight-to-quality bifurcation is real and accelerating — re-leasing spreads demonstrate pricing power that most office owners cannot match. The signed-but-not-commenced lease pipeline commences, lifting economic occupancy and NOI. Management's sell-high/buy-low capital cycle (forward equity at $30.44, buybacks at $23.36, $410M still authorized) compounds per-share FFO. If rates ease, the refinancing drag inverts to a tailwind and the multiple re-rates toward the low-$30s and beyond. Bull case ~$34 (the analyst high), roughly +23% from $27.71 plus yield.

**Bear case.** A tech employment downturn hits Austin disproportionately; occupancy slips further; same-store NOI turns negative. The higher-for-longer rate path keeps refinancing accretive-to-dilutive, and additional asset sales trigger further impairments. Office secular demand erosion proves the "best house in a bad neighborhood" thesis correct. Bear case toward the analyst low of ~$24, roughly -13% from current.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced — moderate upside to base/bull, moderate downside to bear — with the 4.5% yield providing carry while the thesis develops. That is the profile of a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias and a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of re-leasing spreads sustained above +10% cash, confirming durable pricing power.
- A clear move lower in the marginal cost of debt (refinancing at coupons below maturing debt) or a sustained move lower in the long end, removing the FFO drag.
- FFO/share guidance or consensus revised upward, reversing the current downtrend, ideally with the stock still below ~$26.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-property NOI turns negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling the bifurcation thesis is failing in CUZ's own portfolio.
- A material Austin/tech tenant loss or occupancy decline below ~86% economic.
- Dividend coverage deteriorating toward ~1.3x FFO, or further large impairments signaling broad portfolio markdowns.
- Neuhoff JV construction loan refinancing stress at the September 2026 maturity beyond the extension option.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the Q3 2026 closing of the One Eleven Congress sale; the September 2026 Neuhoff loan maturity is the next discrete event to monitor.

## Conclusion

Cousins Properties is the best-capitalized, best-managed name in a structurally challenged sector, and the data confirms genuine operating improvement: FFO/share growth, positive same-store NOI, accelerating re-leasing spreads, and disciplined countercyclical capital allocation. The score trajectory (4.3 → 5.7 → 5.75) reflects that improvement, and the latest data confirms rather than reverses the trend. But the stock at ~$28 is fairly priced against peers and against analyst targets, the cost-of-debt headwind persists in a higher-for-longer rate regime, current-year estimates are being cut, and the Austin/tech concentration is the under-appreciated structural vulnerability. The setup lacks the asymmetric upside or specific catalyst required for a BUY, and the quality and dividend coverage rule out an AVOID. We rate CUZ a HOLD (growth_hold), reassessing on the next earnings print and any change in the rate environment.
