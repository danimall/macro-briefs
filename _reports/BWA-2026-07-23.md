**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BorgWarner is a global Tier-1 automotive propulsion supplier whose reported GAAP earnings have collapsed 71% from their 2022 peak, but the collapse is overwhelmingly an artifact of ~$1.27B in non-cash goodwill and asset impairments taken across FY2024–2025 against its loss-making EV businesses. Strip those out and the underlying franchise is stable-to-improving: segment adjusted operating income rose to $1,798M (12.6% margin) in FY25 from $1,696M in FY24, gross margin held near 18.7% and expanded 100bps to 19.2% in Q1'26, and free cash flow was a healthy ~$1.18B (8.2% margin). The company's "balanced portfolio" (roughly 82% Foundational ICE/hybrid revenue funding an 18% eProducts book that is being restructured) has been vindicated by the slower-than-expected EV transition, since the profitable ICE segments (turbos, drivetrain, timing chains) are the cash engine while the EV drag is shrinking.

The problem for a new investor is timing and price. The stock ran from a 52-week low of $34.27 (July 2025) to a high of $78.82 (June 2026) before settling near $64, an 82% move over twelve months that has taken the trailing GAAP P/E to ~38x and the forward P/E to roughly 12x on $5.18 consensus current-year EPS. The deep-value re-rating that drove that move (the market recognizing that impairments obscured a cash-generative business trading at ~11x depressed earnings) has largely completed. What remains is a "show me" question about growth: management guides 2026 total sales to decline year-over-year ex-FX, dragged by Battery Energy Systems; underlying Q1'26 volume was down ~3% with the EPS beat flattered by FX and tariff recoveries; and consensus estimate revisions are flat-to-negative across every window.

We rate BWA a HOLD. The balance sheet is sound, the cash generation is real, and the multiple is not stretched on a normalized basis, which limits downside. But there is no near-term earnings-growth catalyst, insiders have been uniform sellers into the run-up (including the CEO), and the risk/reward from ~$64 is roughly symmetric rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

BorgWarner (Delaware, incorporated 1987) is a Tier-1 propulsion supplier selling to nearly every major global OEM. FY2025 revenue was $14.3B, with ~84% generated outside the U.S., ~82% light-vehicle exposure, and ~21% of revenue attributable to China. The company runs four reportable segments:

| Segment | FY25 Sales | FY25 Adj. Op. Margin | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turbos & Thermal Technologies | $5,772M | 15.2% | Foundational (ICE) cash engine |
| Drivetrain & Morse Systems | $5,654M | 18.4% | Foundational cash engine |
| PowerDrive Systems (inverters, eMotors, iDMs) | $2,347M | (3.5)% | EV, loss-making, improving |
| Battery Energy Systems | $590M | (6.6)% | EV, loss-making, shrinking |

The strategic frame is a "balanced portfolio": ~82% of revenue is Foundational (ICE/hybrid), ~18% is eProducts. Management explicitly positions the ICE book as a hedge against regional EV adoption volatility. Customer concentration is meaningful: Volkswagen (13%) and Ford (12%), with the top-10 customers at 71% of sales. Light-vehicle turbochargers are the single largest product line (~21% of sales). The moat rests on scale, ~5,000 patents, four decades of automotive software experience relevant to integrated drive modules, and deep OEM relationships. Principal competitors are Bosch, Denso, Garrett Motion, Schaeffler, Valeo, Magna Powertrain, and increasingly CATL.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings understate operating reality.** Net income to BWA fell from $944M (2022) to $625M (2023), $338M (2024), and $277M (2025); diluted EPS from continuing operations was $1.28 in 2025 versus $1.63 in 2024. The driver is impairments: $624M in FY2025 (including a $410M Q4 Battery Energy Systems goodwill charge) and $646M in FY2024 (mostly PowerDrive and Battery). Goodwill has fallen from $3.4B (2022) to $2.0B, with PowerDrive goodwill now fully impaired. Non-comparable items reduced FY25 EPS by $3.63. A screener keying on falling net income or the grey-zone Altman Z (2.27) would misread the company: the Z-score is depressed by impairment-driven negative EBIT/assets, not by operational distress.

**Underlying operations are stable-to-improving.** Segment adjusted operating income rose in both FY25 and Q1'26. Q1'26 revenue was $3,533M (+1% YoY), gross margin expanded 100bps to 19.2%, operating income was $336M (9.5% margin) versus $237M, and diluted EPS jumped to $1.16 from $0.72. Both loss-making EV segments narrowed losses sharply: Battery Energy Systems from -14.7% to -2.0% after the charging-business exit, PowerDrive from -7.7% to -6.1%.

**Quality-of-earnings caveat on Q1'26.** The EPS jump was flattered by a soft prior-year base (Q1'25 carried $39M impairment plus $26M charging-exit charges), a $167M FX tailwind, and ~$32M of tariff recoveries. Underlying volume was actually negative (-$181M, market production down ~3%). The improvement from cost-out and restructuring is real, but partly optical.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are solid.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1,648M; capex fell to $469M (3.3% of sales, down from 4.8% as eProduct investment moderates), yielding FCF of $1,179M (8.2% margin). Liquidity is ~$4.1B (Q1'26: $2.1B cash + $2.0B undrawn revolver). Long-term debt is ~$3.9B, all fixed-rate and well-laddered, with only $5M due through end-2026 and the first meaningful maturity a $1.1B 2.65% note due July 2027. Ratings are Baa1/BBB+/BBB+, all stable. Net interest expense is only ~$39M on ~$14B of revenue, so interest coverage is comfortable. The current ratio is 2.07; D/E of ~1.5 is elevated partly because impairments reduced equity, not because operating leverage is stressed.

**Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and well-funded.** Buybacks totaled $508M in FY25 plus $150M in Q1'26 (average repurchase price ~$52–59), reducing the share count from 235M (2022) to ~205M weighted in Q1'26. A new $1B authorization runs through 2028 with $450M remaining. The quarterly dividend was raised from $0.11 to $0.17 during 2025 (current yield ~1.1%). Dividend and buyback are comfortably covered by FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the thesis, and it is the dimension that has deteriorated in this company's recent history (declining from 6.0 to 5.0 across prior reports). New data confirms rather than reverses that trend.

Management guides 2026 total sales to decline year-over-year ex-FX, with global production expected flat-to-down modestly and Battery Energy Systems the explicit drag. Consensus estimate revisions are flat-to-negative: current-quarter +0.4% over 30 days but -1.2% over 90 days; next-quarter -0.4%/-0.7%; current-year -0.3% over 30 days (+0.2% over 90 days); next-year -0.7%/-0.1%. This is well below the >30% positive revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection; it reflects a business at a mature, low-single-digit growth ceiling in a cyclical, price-down industry.

Two forward levers deserve monitoring. First, the ~$300M of power-generation industrial-solutions revenue management expects in 2027 is a genuine diversification beyond automotive, likely leveraging thermal and power-electronics capabilities into data-center and grid backup demand. If it scales, it could re-rate the multiple, but at ~2% of revenue it is not yet material. Second, the eProduct portfolio (iDMs, inverters) is the long-term growth vector as ICE content eventually declines, though whether BWA can win enough content at acceptable margins without further capital destruction is unresolved. The recent narrowing of EV segment losses is encouraging on margins; the ~3% underlying volume decline is discouraging on top-line trajectory.

The earnings-beat track record is a point in favor of forward estimates: four consecutive beats (Q2'25 +10.6%, Q3'25 +5.8%, Q4'25 +12.7%, Q1'26 +4.9%). But recent beats have been driven by cost-out, FX, and tariff recoveries rather than volume, which limits how much they should raise confidence in a durable growth reacceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$64, BWA trades at ~38x trailing GAAP EPS ($1.28, impairment-distorted) and roughly 12x forward on $5.18 current-year consensus. The forward multiple is the relevant one, and it is not demanding relative to the ~8% FCF margin and the buyback support. But it sits above the automotive parts peer median.

Against the peer median trailing P/E of 14.5x, BWA's trailing multiple looks stretched, but that comparison is misleading because BWA's trailing earnings are impairment-suppressed while peers like ALV, LEA, and GNTX report cleaner numbers. On a normalized/forward basis, BWA at ~12x forward is roughly in line with the group, neither cheap nor expensive.

The critical valuation point is that the mispricing that existed in early 2026 has been arbitraged away. When the stock traded at ~$35–52, it was a genuine value opportunity: a cash-generative business valued as though the EV impairments reflected operating failure. The run to $78.82 and settling at ~$64 captured most of that re-rating. The most bullish external analyst (Seeking Alpha) downgraded from Strong Buy to Buy because the stock had already risen 43%, and BofA's Neutral $78 target frames the stock as fairly valued rather than a bargain. Analyst consensus targets (mean $76.87, median $78, low $48, high $95) imply modest upside to the median but do not act as a floor here because the stock is no longer cheap relative to a decelerating growth profile.

Consistent with the discipline of not rewarding cheapness that lacks a catalyst, we assign valuation a below-average score. The multiple is not stretched, but there is no identified reason the market is materially underpricing the stock at current levels. The unquantified tariff refund optionality (below) is the one asymmetric upside not in the numbers, but it is too uncertain to anchor a valuation case.

## Competitive Landscape

BorgWarner operates in a mature, cyclical, mid-teens-margin Tier-1 supplier industry facing three simultaneous pressures: a bumpy, region-divergent EV transition; structural annual OEM price-downs; and rising OEM in-sourcing, especially by Chinese domestic brands. Its differentiated position is the profitable, cash-generative Foundational franchise that funds EV R&D and buybacks while the EV market matures, the opposite bet from pure-play EV suppliers, and one the current environment has rewarded.

Competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. The Foundational segments are holding share and margin (Drivetrain at 18.4% adjusted margin is genuinely strong for the industry), but the eProduct segments face intense pricing pressure and dual-sourcing by customers; the Q4'25 Battery impairment was directly attributed to "dual sourcing actions by certain customers, pricing pressures from competitors and EV adoption delays in North America." A European customer cyber-shutdown dented FY25 Drivetrain sales, a reminder of concentration exposure. Whether BWA converts iDM and inverter content wins into acceptable-margin growth as ICE content structurally declines is unresolved. On balance the position is stable with a modest structural headwind, not a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

The most consequential BWA-specific risks, drawn from the 10-K Item 1A (no material risk-factor changes in the Q1'26 10-Q):

1. **EV transition timing and stranded investment.** The Battery Energy Systems reporting unit's carrying value now approximately equals fair value (zero headroom), making it highly sensitive to any further deterioration. Further EV demand disappointment could trigger additional impairments and losses. The counterpoint is that management has already written EV goodwill down to near-fair-value and is aggressively cutting losses, which reduces the incremental downside from here.

2. **Tariffs and trade policy.** BWA imported ~$918M into the U.S. in 2025 (68% Mexico, 9% Canada, 6% Korea); FY25 tariff expense added ~$108M to COGS, of which only ~$80M was recovered via customer agreements. USMCA renewal is due in 2026. Offsetting this, the Q1'26 10-Q discloses that the U.S. Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA-based tariffs on Feb 20, 2026, and BWA is pursuing refund recovery, an unquantified asymmetric potential cash upside.

3. **OEM pricing pressure and customer concentration.** Annual price-downs are structural, and cost recovery of inflation/commodities is "less than 100% and often delayed." Management flags higher commodity volatility in 2026 than 2025.

4. **Goodwill impairment sensitivity.** A 14% WACC is used with small headroom on remaining units after prior acquisitions.

5. **PHINIA spin-off tail risk.** A VAT/tax-matters dispute with the 2023-spun PHINIA settled in October 2025 (PHINIA paying $78M; BWA booked a net $40M charge), but residual indemnity and tax-free-status risk remains.

Two recent Item 5.02 8-Ks (April 30 and May 28, 2026) indicate director/officer changes that were not fully retrievable, a governance thread worth confirming.

Many of these risks are already priced given the 82% run and the compressed forward multiple, which is why we score risk near the middle rather than low. The zero-headroom Battery unit and the higher-commodity-volatility guidance are the live downside catalysts; the tariff refund is the live upside catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is flashing near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV is 53.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.9% (rich by ~14 points, at the 87th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (53.1% near-term versus 50.6% at six months), which the market uses to price imminent stress. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.8%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%), and put/call volume is light (0.21), so the elevated IV reflects general uncertainty rather than a directional bearish bet. The put/call OI ratio has risen sharply from 0.27 to 0.85 since mid-June while the stock fell 14% from ~$74.68 to ~$63.98, so hedging demand has increased on the way down. For a HOLD, the read is that the market prices meaningful two-way risk into the next earnings print; the rich IV also argues against paying up for near-term optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85–95).** The market continues to recognize that impairments obscured a cash-generative business. Foundational margins hold near mid-to-high teens, EV segment losses continue narrowing toward breakeven, the power-generation leg scales and re-rates the multiple toward a diversified-industrial comp, the IEEPA tariff refund delivers a lump-sum cash benefit, and continued buybacks on a $13B cap compound EPS. A forward multiple of ~16x on ~$5.82 next-year EPS supports the low-$90s; the high analyst target of $95 sits here.

**Bear Case ($48–52, roughly -20% to -25%).** 2026 revenue declines ex-FX as guided, global auto production weakens, commodity volatility compresses margins, the zero-headroom Battery unit takes another impairment, USMCA renewal raises structural tariff costs, and the EV content-win thesis fails to convert to acceptable-margin growth. Estimate revisions turn firmly negative and the multiple compresses back toward the ~10–11x forward the stock carried before the re-rating. The low analyst target of $48 sits here.

**Base Case (~$65–70).** The business generates ~$5.18 in current-year EPS and ~$5.82 next year, grows revenue at low-single-digits on FX and cost-out rather than volume, keeps buying back stock, and holds a ~12–13x forward multiple. Modest upside from continued repurchase support and optional tariff refund, capped by decelerating top-line growth and uniform insider selling. This clusters near the current price and reaches the mean/median analyst targets of ~$77 only if the power-gen and EV-margin stories begin to prove out.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next checkpoint is the Q2'26 earnings release (roughly early August 2026), followed by confirmation of the Item 5.02 governance changes and any update on the IEEPA tariff refund.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive (>+3% over 90 days) alongside evidence of positive underlying volume growth, not just FX/cost-out.
- Battery Energy Systems and PowerDrive reach or approach breakeven, removing the impairment overhang, and management raises the 2027 power-generation revenue outlook above ~$300M with committed contracts.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores an asymmetric forward multiple (~10x) with the cash-generation and buyback support intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further Battery Energy Systems goodwill impairment (the unit has zero headroom), or expansion of impairment to additional units.
- 2026 revenue guidance cut beyond the current flat-to-down ex-FX framing, or Q2'26 EPS miss after four consecutive beats.
- USMCA renewal outcome that materially raises structural tariff costs without offsetting customer recovery.

## Conclusion

BorgWarner is a well-financed, cash-generative Tier-1 supplier whose GAAP earnings badly understate operating reality, and the market has already corrected that misreading as the stock rose 82% over twelve months and the deep-value thesis played out. What remains is a mature business at a fair (not cheap) forward multiple, guiding revenue to decline ex-FX in 2026, with flat-to-negative estimate revisions and uniform insider selling into the run-up, including the CEO's 29,000-share sale at $67.31. The balance sheet and FCF limit downside, and the tariff-refund optionality plus the nascent power-generation leg provide genuine (if unquantified) upside. But there is no near-term earnings-growth catalyst and the risk/reward from ~$64 is roughly symmetric.

We rate BWA a **HOLD** (uncertain rationale). The fundamentals are sound enough to own for the buyback support and FCF yield, but the compelling entry point has passed and the growth story has not yet proven it can offset ICE decline. We would turn constructive on evidence of positive underlying volume, EV segment breakeven, or a pullback toward the low-$50s; we would turn negative on another Battery impairment, a guidance cut, or an earnings miss. This is our view against a Consumer Discretionary / auto-parts backdrop that carries a structural alpha headwind and an H2 consumer-demand cliff, which reinforces our reluctance to reward the stock above HOLD absent a company-specific growth catalyst.
