## Executive Summary

Kinetik is a Delaware Basin–focused, integrated midstream C-corp with 2.4 Bcf/d of cryogenic processing capacity, ~4,200 miles of gas gathering pipe, and equity interests in the two long-haul systems that move its molecules to the Gulf Coast (55.5% of Permian Highway Pipeline, 33% of Shin Oak). The Q2 2026 print, released August 5, was the strongest in company history: Adjusted EBITDA $280.8M (+15.6% YoY), operating income $134.0M (+73%), and full-year guidance raised to $1.04–1.10B from a February midpoint roughly 7% lower. Capital commitments corroborate the demand story: Kings Landing II reached FID in May 2026 (~$260M, capacity to 2.7 Bcf/d, completion pulled forward to mid-2028), the ECCC pipeline entered service, and the board authorized long-lead equipment for a plant beyond KLII.

Two things prevent that from being a buy case. First, valuation. Kinetik is an Up-C: 162.375M total shares and units existed at 6/30/2026 (78.9M Class A plus 83.4M exchangeable Class C), not the ~73.6M Class A in the market-data feed. On the complete count, equity value is $7,851M, enterprise value $11,791M, and the multiple is **11.0x FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA at the $1,070M guidance midpoint** — mid-range for Permian midstream, with no identifiable discount to exploit. Second, the funding arithmetic. The $3.24 annualized dividend costs ~$526M a year against reported 1H26 free cash flow (post-growth-capex) of $206.6M; on a cash-statement basis, 1H26 operating cash flow of $341.5M less $197.4M of capex left $144.1M against ~$263M of distributions, and net debt rose $125.9M (the two reconcile within ~$7M). Capex guidance was simultaneously raised ~14% to ~$560M. Leverage holds at 3.84x because EBITDA is growing, not because the payout is self-funded.

The near-term swing factor is legal, not operational. Kinetik and affiliates are defendants in two consolidated suits by Energy Transfer GC NGL Product Services over two long-term NGL purchase agreements; a bench trial concluded in April 2026 in the Texas Business Court and a decision is pending, with only "immaterial reserves" accrued and **specific performance among the remedies sought**. Disclosed litigation costs added back to Adjusted EBITDA escalated from $6.1M (FY2024) to $19.7M (FY2025) to $17.0M in 1H26 alone. Near-term implied volatility of 51.8% against 35.5% realized, in backwardation, two weeks after earnings, is consistent with a market pricing an unresolved event. Own it for the 6.70% yield and the 2027–2028 volume ramp; do not add here.

## Company Overview

Operations sit in two segments. **Midstream Logistics** (>99% of consolidated revenue) comprises gas gathering and processing (eight complexes, >2.4 Bcf/d cryogenic, >825,000 hp compression), crude gathering and stabilization (~290 miles, 90,000 Bbl of storage feeding Plains All American takeaway), and produced-water gathering and disposal (~370 miles, ~580,000 Bbl/d permitted). **Pipeline Transportation** holds the two equity-method investments — PHP (carrying value $1,579.9M at 3/31/26, ~2.65 Bcf/d to Gulf Coast/Mexico, operated by Kinder Morgan) and Breviloba/Shin Oak ($411.7M, operated by Enterprise) — plus wholly owned in-basin pipes including Delaware Link (~1.0 Bcf/d to Waha) and the newly completed ECCC line connecting Eddy County, NM to Culberson County, TX.

The Up-C structure matters for every per-share calculation. Kinetik Holdings Inc. owns a minority LP interest in Kinetik Holdings LP that has risen from ~40% (12/31/25) to ~48.6% (6/30/26) purely because Class C holders keep exchanging units 1-for-1 into Class A. Class A rose 68.8M → 78.9M in one quarter while Class C fell 93.6M → 83.4M; total count was essentially unchanged at 162.375M. There is no ownership dilution from this process, only float expansion.

A related artifact: total book equity fell from $(565.4)M at 12/31/25 to $(1,669.2)M at 3/31/26, driven by a $1,280.5M "change in redemption value of noncontrolling interest" that marks the redeemable Common Units to the Class A market price against APIC and accumulated deficit. Redeemable NCI rose $3,495.8M → $4,511.4M over the quarter, implying per-unit values of ~$35.83 and ~$48.22. When the share price rises, book equity falls. Any Altman Z-Score, debt/equity, ROE or book-value-per-share output for KNTK is meaningless. This does not excuse cash-flow, leverage or coverage analysis, which is where the real questions sit.

An accounting nuance with commercial teeth: Kinetik owns 55.5% of PHP's economics but accounts for it under the equity method with Kinder Morgan as operator. Whatever the specific negotiated consent rights, the practical consequence is that Kinetik cannot unilaterally direct PHP distributions, expansions or capital calls; PHP plus Breviloba contributed roughly $308.7M of proportionate EMI EBITDA to LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $1,026.7M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and reported earnings are poor guides here.** FY2025 revenue was $1,764.4M (+19%), but operating income *fell* 8% to $164.9M as cost of sales rose 27%, operating expenses 38% (Durango/Kings Landing start-up), and D&A 18% to $382.6M. FY2025 net income including NCI of $525.9M was dominated by a $415.4M gain on the EPIC Crude sale; FY2024 similarly carried an $89.8M GCX gain. Two consecutive years of asset-sale-driven GAAP earnings explain the erratic surprise record below.

| Quarter | Est. EPS | Actual EPS | Surprise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 | $0.24 | $0.64 | +163.6% |
| Q1 2026 | $0.21 | $(0.07) | −133.5% |
| Q4 2025 | $0.29 | $2.16 | +644.6% |
| Q3 2025 | $0.34 | $0.03 | −91.2% |

A ±100%+ swing every quarter reflects asset-sale gains, derivative marks (net $(37.0)M fair value at 3/31/26 swinging to a $49.6M unrealized gain by Q2) and Up-C noncontrolling-interest allocation. Neither the beats nor the misses carry forward-looking information, and the reported trailing P/E of 19.9x on $2.63 of EPS is an artifact of the EPIC gain.

**Adjusted EBITDA is the usable series.** Q1 2026 was flat at $251.2M despite revenue falling 8% (a mix effect — principal-basis gas service fees netted inside cost of sales grew to $102.3M from $62.2M while gathered volumes rose 5%). Q2 2026 delivered $280.8M, but the composition warrants attention: Midstream Logistics EBITDA rose 35% to $204.8M on flat processed volumes of 1.74 Bcf/d, attributed to NGL recoveries, condensate yields, optimization and favorable commodity prices and spreads, while Pipeline Transportation fell 14% to $83.0M on the EPIC divestiture. Product revenue rose 58% YoY; service revenue fell 23%. The upside was margin- and spread-driven rather than throughput-driven, which is lower-persistence, and management appears to agree, guiding Q3 to $260–270M and Q4 to $270–280M, both below the quarter just delivered.

**The dividend funding gap is the central financial fact.** Kinetik reports a Dividend Coverage Ratio of 1.41x for 1H26 ($375.8M of distributable cash flow against ~$263M declared), a standard midstream measure computed before growth capex. On the company's own free-cash-flow definition (DCF less growth capex), 1H26 was $206.6M against ~$263M, or **0.79x**. The cash statement is tighter and it ties out:

| 1H 2026 | $M |
|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 341.5 |
| Capital expenditures (net) | (197.4) |
| Cash flow after capex | 144.1 |
| Dividends/distributions paid | (~263.0) |
| **Shortfall** | **(~118.9)** |
| Actual net debt increase ($3,814.2M → $3,940.2M) | **+125.9** |

Roll that forward on guidance: EBITDA $1,070M, of which ~$310M is proportionate EMI EBITDA that arrives as distributions (~$260M annualized based on $130.2M of 1H26 returns on invested capital), gives ~$1,020M of cash-basis EBITDA; less ~$215M of interest (1H26 interest expense $107.5M) and ~$560M of capex leaves ~$245M against a ~$526M dividend. That implies roughly $250–280M of incremental net debt across FY2026, with leverage staying near 3.84–3.93x only because EBITDA is growing ~15%. FY2025 was the same pattern at larger scale: operating cash flow $604.1M less $529.7M of capital spend left $74.4M against $500.1M of dividends plus $176.0M of buybacks, bridged by $504.2M of EPIC proceeds and a $250M notes add-on.

**Capital structure.** Total debt $3,948.0M against $7.8M of cash at 6/30/26: $225M A/R securitization (fully drawn, limit *reduced* from $250M in March 2026, scheduled termination 3/30/2027), ~$523M drawn on a $1,600M revolver (2030), a $1,150M unsecured term loan (May 2028), $1,050M of 6.625% notes (December 2028) and $1,000M of 5.875% notes (2030). Approximately $2.2B, or 56% of total debt, matures in 2028. Revolver draws have climbed $453M → $468M → ~$523M across the last three balance-sheet dates. Reported liquidity of $1,072M is genuine but is now almost entirely revolver capacity.

Interest coverage is roughly 4.8x on total LTM Adjusted EBITDA ($1,026.7M against ~$215M annualized interest) and ~3.3x on the $718M of wholly consolidated EBITDA (stripping EMI contribution entirely); the latter is a stress lens, not a headline metric, but it frames why the market assigns a 6.7% yield. Floating-rate debt was ~$1.9B at 6/30/26 against $1.0B notional of swaps fixed at 3.06%–3.42%. Two swaps mature 12/31/2026 and five on 12/31/2027, and the 10-Q quantifies ~$18.1M of annual interest sensitivity per 100bp on unswapped floating debt. With the fed funds target at 3.50%–3.75%, the roll-off is a 2027–2028 earnings headwind rather than a benefit.

**Score: 5.0.** Leverage is inside midstream norms and covenant compliance is intact, EBITDA is growing, and the negative-equity signal is noise. Against that: the payout is not self-funded after growth capex, capex guidance rose 14%, the securitization facility was downsized and exhausted, revolver reliance is rising, favorable hedges roll off just before a $2.2B maturity wall, and cash on hand is $7.8M. The prior improvement to 5.5 was driven by the leverage print; the composition has deteriorated at the margin since.

## Growth Analysis

The operating growth case is credible and backed by capital commitments rather than commentary alone:

- **Q4 2026 processed-volume exit rate of nearly 2.2 Bcf/d, ~20% exit-to-exit** growth, embedded in guidance.
- **Kings Landing II** FID'd May 2026, ~$260M, lifting system capacity to 2.7 Bcf/d and Delaware North sour-gas processing above 700 MMcf/d, completion pulled forward to mid-2028; processing, amine and residue compression equipment already purchased.
- **Kings Landing AGI/sour-conversion** in service by YE2026 (permits approved, drilling underway).
- **ECCC** in service, with right-of-way procurement started for a 2027 expansion.
- **Diamond Volt**, 40 MW of behind-the-meter generation at Diamond Cryo, Q2 2027 — self-generation in a basin where grid power constrains compression.
- **Firm incremental Gulf Coast netback residue market access commencing 2027**, plus new residue and NGL transportation agreements for Delaware North (counterparties unnamed).
- Board approval of long-lead equipment for a plant *beyond* KLII.

The demand backdrop supports it. Permian gas takeaway is tightening against LNG export growth and data-center load, the same electricity-demand dynamic driving power-sector capex elsewhere. Guidance assumes Waha gas averages negative $(0.26)/MMBtu for 2026; Kinetik disclosed ~250 MMcf/d of price-related processed-volume shut-ins in Q2, easing to an assumed ~25 MMcf/d average in 2H26. Negative in-basin pricing cuts both ways: producers curtail, but processors with residue takeaway to Gulf Coast markets capture wider spreads. Kinetik's PHP equity and 2027 firm netback access are direct expressions of that arbitrage, and are the clearest form of its integration advantage.

The counterweight is that EBITDA growth is not reaching per-share earnings. Consensus EPS revisions:

| Period | Current | 90d ago | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.23 | $0.40 | −41.2% |
| Next quarter | $0.29 | $0.46 | −37.7% |
| Current year | $1.27 | $1.28 | −0.7% |
| **Next year** | **$1.61** | **$2.01** | **−19.8%** |

Only 2–3 analysts contribute, and GAAP EPS for this structure is noisy, so I discount these more than I would for a clean C-corp. But a 19.8% cut to FY2027 EPS while FY2026 EBITDA guidance rose 7% is internally coherent: D&A on new plants, ~$215M of interest rising as debt grows, and hedge roll-off absorb the EBITDA gains before they reach the Class A line. Investors are being paid the growth in distributions financed partly by debt, not in per-share earnings.

**Score: 5.3**, up from 4.5. The volume exit rate, KLII pull-forward and secured 2027 netback capacity are new, verifiable improvements. The offset is that the Q2 beat was margin-driven, management guides sequentially lower, and the earnings-per-share transmission is negative.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik describes itself as the fourth-largest gas processor in both the Delaware Basin and the Permian overall — genuine scale (2.4 Bcf/d going to 2.7 Bcf/d) behind materially larger integrated players. The differentiation is vertical: wellhead gathering through processing, then equity ownership in the two long-haul systems that move residue gas (PHP) and NGLs (Shin Oak) to the Gulf Coast, allowing Kinetik to offer producers Gulf Coast netback pricing rather than Waha-indexed pricing in a basin where Waha has repeatedly priced negative. That is a real commercial advantage, and the newly secured firm 2027 netback capacity extends it.

Two trajectory concerns temper the score. First, revenue quality is shifting the wrong way: service (fee-based) revenue fell 23% YoY in Q2 while product (commodity-linked) revenue rose 58%; product revenue was $490.8M of the $581.4M quarter and $1,307.2M of FY2025's $1,764.4M. Second, contractual underpinning is thin. The Q1 10-Q shows Remaining Performance Obligations of only **$428.9M** through "thereafter," and the filing explicitly limits that table to contracts with fixed pricing and fixed volume terms including MVCs. Against ~$2.0B+ of annualized revenue, that indicates the great majority of Kinetik's revenue rests on acreage dedications with variable volumes rather than minimum-volume commitments. MVC deficiency revenue was $2.0M in Q1 2026. This is not backlog and should not be read as one.

Kinetik's position in the basin is worth stating literally: Kinder Morgan operates PHP where Kinetik holds 55.5% of the economics; Enterprise operates Shin Oak where it holds 33%; Plains operates crude takeaway into which its gathering system connects; and Energy Transfer's NGL marketing subsidiary is its litigation adversary under two long-term NGL purchase agreements. It is simultaneously co-investor, shipper, supplier and defendant across the Permian's largest operators.

**Score: 5.5.** Capacity and integration are improving; fee-based revenue share and contractual coverage are not.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Energy Transfer NGL litigation — unreserved, pending now, with specific performance sought.** Two consolidated suits (filed 2021 and 2025) by Energy Transfer GC NGL Product Services, formerly Lone Star NGL, alleging breach of contract and related torts under two long-term NGL purchase agreements. Remedies sought include monetary damages, declaratory relief and specific performance. A bench trial was held in late March–April 2026 in the Texas Business Court and the matter is pending. Kinetik has accrued only "immaterial reserves." Q1 G&A rose $6.6M YoY entirely because legal fees rose $9.0M. Two features make this more than a reserve question: the escalating spend and the addition of a second suit against further subsidiaries indicate a contested, high-stakes dispute, and a specific-performance remedy could alter NGL commercial economics on an ongoing basis rather than producing a single charge. The counter-reading is equally legitimate: the immaterial accrual reflects management's and counsel's ASC 450 judgment that a material loss is not probable and reasonably estimable, which may simply be correct. No evidence on the merits is available and no external reporting on the trial was located. This is a live binary of unquantified size.

**2. Dividend funded partly with debt against a rising commitment stack.** Covered above. The commitment stack is growing: KLII, the Kings Landing AGI well, Diamond Volt, ECCC expansion right-of-way, and long-lead equipment for a plant beyond KLII. The FY2025 10-K states the company "does not expect to significantly increase the amount of stock repurchases until gross debt is reduced below certain thresholds," and there were zero repurchases in Q1 2026, only 91,252 shares in Q4 2025, with $318.2M of authorization unused. Buybacks are effectively suspended by leverage while the dividend is maintained on borrowings.

**3. Commodity and Waha basis exposure, bidirectional.** Guidance assumes Waha at $(0.26)/MMBtu, WTI $78.65, HSC gas $2.83, composite NGLs $0.62/gal, on 7/28/2026 forwards. Roughly 250 MMcf/d of price-related shut-ins occurred in Q2. My read that negative Waha widened Kinetik's processing spread is an inference from the disclosed drivers, not a company statement; an alternative reading attributes the Q2 margin gain predominantly to recovery/yield improvements and optimization.

**4. 2028 maturity concentration and hedge roll-off.** ~$2.2B due in 2028 against a legacy 6.625% coupon; the A/R facility's scheduled termination is March 2027; five of seven swaps expire 12/31/2027. Total debt fair value ($3.88B at 3/31/26) sits close to principal, so credit markets are not pricing stress, but a higher-for-longer rate path applies directly to a 3.84x-levered, capex-heavy payer.

**5. Sponsor supply overhang.** I Squared Capital has been liquidating methodically: 4.0M shares at $44.85 on 2026-02-26 ($179.4M, the day the 10-K was filed), 1,071,107 shares across eight April transactions at $48.01–$50.86 (~$53.2M), and further 1.5M-unit conversions on 4/6 and 7/29. Residual holding is ~17.5M share-equivalents, ~10.8% of the 162.375M total. Because each sale follows a 1-for-1 exchange, there is no ownership dilution; the effect is float expansion and price pressure. Sponsor exits are fund-life driven and carry limited fundamental information; the April sales occurred above the February block, inconsistent with distressed liquidation.

**6. Other quantified items.** A $14.0M accrued liability for excess-emission civil penalties at gas plants and compressor stations acquired with Durango; a Winter Storm Uri receivable of ~$11.6M with no allowance recorded; a PDC Permian earn-out capped at $60.5M through 2029, never triggered and not accrued; a $96.0M EPIC earn-out now contingent on a board Kinetik no longer sits behind.

**7. Unverified customer concentration.** The 10-K risk factors address dependence on a limited number of customers, but named-customer revenue percentages are not in evidence. I explicitly do not infer diversification from the 2026 revenue-mix shift. The thin RPO disclosure means most volumes are dedication-based and volumetric.

**8. New macro language.** The Q1 10-Q added forward-looking risk language absent from the 10-K, citing the Iran conflict, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and related energy-market disruption — management's own characterization of a new exposure. Kinetik is gas-weighted and domestic, so the direct channel is modest; the indirect channel runs through rates and NGL/condensate pricing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 51.8% against 35.5% one-year realized volatility is a 16.3-point premium, and the term structure is in backwardation (51.8% at 14 days, 43.6% at 42 days, 36.2% at 133 days). ATM IV rose from 39.3% to 51.5% between July 19 and August 6 while the price fell 3.8%. Because Q2 earnings have already been reported, the elevated front-end vol is pricing something other than the print. The pending Energy Transfer verdict is the most plausible candidate, and the ±10.6% implied move on the nearest expiry is roughly the magnitude one would expect from an adverse ruling with damages plus specific performance. Skew is reported as call-heavy (−9.0%), and volume/OI put-call ratios are unpopulated in the chain (22 contracts across three expirations, so liquidity is thin and these readings are low-confidence). The options market expects a large move without a directional put bias, and selling near-dated premium is expensive relative to realized vol for a reason. Short interest of 10% of float with 5 days to cover is moderate — neither a crowded bear position nor a vote of confidence.

**Score: 4.0.** An unreserved binary with a pending decision and an ongoing-economics remedy, layered on rising leverage, thin contractual coverage, unverified customer concentration and a $2.2B refinancing wall, is more idiosyncratic risk than an 11x multiple and a 6.7% yield compensate for.

## Management Quality

The alignment evidence is strong. CEO Jamie Welch holds ~3.79M Class A shares; FY2025 "compensation actually paid" was **negative $58.2M** against positive $95.2M in FY2024 — a $153M swing that reflects the mark-to-market of his equity through a year in which the proxy's five-year TSR index shows KNTK at 206 versus a peer group at 347, implying a roughly −32% FY2025 total return. He absorbed that personally. Total FY2025 compensation was $6.16M; NEO average $2.30M; CEO-to-NEO ratio 2.7x. Performance metrics — Adjusted EBITDA, Free Cash Flow and Net Debt/EBITDA — are well matched to the company's actual pressure points.

Capital allocation is more mixed. Selling EPIC Crude at $504.2M for a $415.4M gain and GCX at $524.4M for an $89.8M gain were good monetizations; Barilla Draw ($175.5M) and Durango (~$341.2M plus a $75M earn-out, $9.9M settled) built the Delaware North position that underpins KLII. But the revealed priority ranking places a debt-funded dividend above both deleveraging and buybacks — repurchases are explicitly suspended until gross debt falls below undisclosed thresholds, with $318.2M of authorization idle. That is a defensible midstream choice; it is not a shareholder-return optimum at 3.84x leverage with capex rising.

Two governance items deserve flagging. There is **no separate CFO signature** on the filings: Welch signs as Principal Executive Officer and Steven Stellato, EVP and Chief Accounting/Chief Administrative Officer, signs as both Principal Financial Officer and Principal Accounting Officer. For an entity with ~$11.8B EV, 3.84x leverage and a $2.2B 2028 wall, that is thin, though Stellato may functionally perform the role under another title and Welch is a former Energy Transfer CFO. Whether a titled CFO position exists, is vacant, or was eliminated is unconfirmed. Separately, Blackstone and I Squared together hold a majority of voting shares with director-designation rights; Blackstone's current position and any recent sales are not in evidence.

There were **no open-market insider purchases** in the ~18 months of Form 4 data reviewed. The only non-sponsor dispositions were small March 2026 sales by four officers at $46.92 two days after grants, consistent with tax withholding.

**Score: 5.0.** Real equity alignment and sensible incentive metrics, offset by a capital-allocation hierarchy that funds distributions with debt, an incomplete senior-finance signatory structure, and no insider buying into a stock that fell ~32% last year.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $64 (+32%)

Volumes inflect as guided: Q4 2026 exit rate near 2.2 Bcf/d, curtailments continue easing, and the AGI project plus ECCC expansion lift 2027 EBITDA to ~$1,250M. The 2027 firm Gulf Coast netback commitments let Kinetik keep capturing residue spreads while in-basin Waha stays weak, and processing margins hold nearer Q2's level than management's conservative guide. Kings Landing II delivers on the pulled-forward mid-2028 date within its ~$260M budget, taking capacity to 2.7 Bcf/d into a Permian gas market tightening on LNG and data-center load. Growth capex rolls off after 2028, dividend coverage on a post-growth-capex basis crosses 1.0x, leverage falls below 3.5x, and buybacks resume against $318.2M of authorization. The Energy Transfer suits resolve without material damages, removing ~$35M of annualized legal drag and an overhang worth a multiple point. At 11.8x $1,250M less ~$4.0B of net debt, that is ~$66; the analyst high of $64 is a reasonable ceiling for a 12-month horizon.

### Bear Case — $36 (−26%)

The court rules against Kinetik with material damages and orders specific performance under the NGL purchase agreements, forcing volume deliveries on unfavorable terms and permanently compressing NGL marketing economics, an outcome for which nothing is accrued. Waha basis stays deeply negative, producer shut-ins re-widen beyond the assumed 25 MMcf/d, and the Q2 spread tailwind reverses, holding 2027 EBITDA near $1,000–1,020M. Capex overruns on KLII and the plant beyond it, the payout stays at $3.24, and net debt drifts toward $4.2B, taking leverage above 4.1x just as five swaps roll off at end-2027 into a fed funds rate that has not fallen — every 100bp costs ~$18.1M. Refinancing $2.2B in 2028 against a 6.625% legacy coupon becomes the story, the multiple compresses toward 10x, and the market begins discounting a distribution cut. At 10x $1,020M less $4.2B of net debt, equity is ~$37; the 52-week low of $31.33 sets the tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD — income rationale.** The reason to own KNTK is the $3.24 distribution (6.70% at $48.35), covered 1.41x on distributable cash flow and supported by a volume ramp that should improve post-growth-capex coverage from 2027 as KLII and the AGI project convert capital into EBITDA.
