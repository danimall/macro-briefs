## Executive Summary

Sprinklr's Q1 FY2027 income statement contains the single most important fact in the file: revenue rose $13.98M year over year (+6.8%) while gross profit rose $0.16M (+0.1%). Incremental gross margin was 1.2%. Subscription gross margin has fallen from 82.7% (FY2024) to 80.4% (FY2025) to 76.4% (FY2026) to 73.9% (Q1 FY2027), and management states in the 10-Q that it expects gross margin "to decline in the near term due to higher data and hosting costs, coupled with higher service delivery costs." For a software company, growth that does not convert into gross profit dollars is not growth that a revenue multiple should capitalize.

Beneath a headline that shows GAAP operating income swinging from -$1.755M to +$10.609M, the underlying trend is negative. Adjusting both periods for restructuring (a $16.313M charge in Q1 FY2026 versus a -$0.654M credit in Q1 FY2027), underlying GAAP operating income fell from $14.558M to $9.955M, a 31.6% decline. Non-GAAP operating income fell 13.6% to $31.735M (14.5% margin from 17.9%), and company-defined free cash flow fell 18.4% to $65.815M. Growth composition is also deteriorating: US revenue declined 4.9%, the "Other" region (Asia including the Middle East) grew 86.3% and accounted for 98.0% of the entire consolidated revenue increase, a first-ever >10% customer appeared at 10.4% of revenue "as a result of the timing of revenue recognition," total customer count fell roughly 13% to 1,677, and current remaining performance obligations grew about 1% while reported revenue grew 7.6%.

The offset is real and should not be dismissed. Sprinklr carries no debt, $442.8M of cash and short-dated marketable securities (27.6% of total equity value), and generates $130-150M of normalized annual free cash flow. On the correctly counted share base — 233.98M Class A plus Class B shares, not the 133.1M Class A figure that produces the widely quoted $913M market cap — enterprise value is approximately $1.16B, or 1.36x FY2026 revenue and roughly 8.0x FY2026 non-GAAP operating income. Those multiples are low. But the stock has advanced 27% since July 3 ($5.41) and 22.9% since June 1 on no fundamental development we can verify, and now trades above the analyst median target of $6.75 and above the analyst low of $6.00. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately +4% against a bear case 34% below spot. We move to REDUCE from HOLD: the rating change reflects a 27% higher price against modestly softer operating results, not a new discovery about the business.

## Company Overview

Sprinklr sells a "Unified Customer Experience Management" platform organized into four suites — Social, Insights, Marketing, and Service — that consolidates marketing, advertising, market research, customer care and engagement functions. Founded 2009, headquartered in New York, operating through 21 subsidiaries, serving customers in more than 90 countries across more than 150 languages. Fiscal year ends January 31; FY2026 ended January 31, 2026 and Q1 FY2027 ended April 30, 2026.

Revenue splits 89% subscription / 11% professional services. Subscription contracts run predominantly two to three years and are recognized ratably. The company reports as a single segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker evaluating consolidated net income, so no suite-level margin data is disclosed — whether Sprinklr Service is profitable cannot be determined from filings.

The structural feature that defines the economics is disclosed in the risk factors verbatim: social listening functionality "is dependent on the application programming interfaces ('APIs') of different third-party channel providers (e.g., X, Meta, Snap, etc.)," and if those providers alter "API capabilities, technical or legal limitations, or monetization models, that could have a negative impact on our overall value proposition." The company relies on negotiated agreements with these providers that are "subject to termination in certain circumstances" with no assurance of renewal on favorable terms.

## Financial Analysis

### Gross profit has stopped growing

| Q1 (3 mo. ended Apr 30) | FY2027 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscription revenue | $194.789M | $184.127M | +5.8% |
| Professional services revenue | $24.690M | $21.373M | +15.5% |
| **Total revenue** | **$219.479M** | **$205.500M** | **+6.8%** |
| Cost of revenue — subscription | $50.854M | $42.186M | +20.5% |
| Cost of revenue — professional services | $25.594M | $20.445M | +25.2% |
| **Gross profit** | **$143.031M** | **$142.869M** | **+0.1%** |

Total GAAP gross margin fell 4.3 points, from 69.5% to 65.2%. Management attributes the subscription portion to a $5.7M increase in third-party cloud, data and network infrastructure costs "partially attributable to increased customer demand, as well as higher rates from our third-party providers," plus $1.7M of personnel costs. The phrase "higher rates" points at price rather than volume.

Two competing explanations exist and the disclosure does not resolve them, because the filing bundles cloud, data and network into one line. If platform data providers have repriced access and Sprinklr lacks the leverage to pass it through, the margin reset is permanent. If the increase is predominantly generative-AI inference compute, it is a discretionary investment that could moderate as industry inference costs fall. One independent secondary note (Seeking Alpha, July 15, 2026) attributes the compression to "AI investments," which favors the second reading, but that source is a single unverified opinion. This ambiguity is the largest open question in the fundamental case, and it cuts both ways.

What is not ambiguous is the cost floor: non-cancelable minimum guaranteed purchase commitments for data, hosting and software total $117.2M through fiscal 2029, of which $70.9M falls due within twelve months of January 31, 2026, against FY2026 subscription cost of revenue of $178.6M.

Professional services now destroys gross profit. Segment gross margin moved from roughly +4% to -3.7% ($24.690M revenue against $25.594M cost), driven by a $3.2M increase in subcontractor costs and "higher delivery costs associated with complex implementations." Because services grew 15.5% versus subscription's 5.8%, the faster-growing line is the loss-making one, which flatters headline revenue growth while degrading mix.

### Balance sheet: no solvency question, and a screening trap

At April 30, 2026: cash $163.333M plus marketable securities $279.475M (all contractual maturities under twelve months, US government/agency and high-grade corporate paper) = $442.808M. No revolver, no term loan, no convertible notes; interest expense does not appear on the income statement. Current ratio 1.43. Total stockholders' equity $488.460M against total liabilities $573.387M. Operating leases only, $43.826M of lease liabilities at an 8.42% discount rate.

A standard screen returns "debt-to-equity 1.03" for this company. That figure reconciles exactly to total liabilities ÷ equity at January 31, 2026 ($612.418M ÷ $592.643M), and $433.163M of those liabilities — 70.7% — is deferred revenue, which is customer prepayment and a funding source rather than a refinancing claim. Treating 1.03 as leverage would materially misprice the risk here. Financial health scores 6.5 on that basis, held below 7 by the margin trajectory, the committed cost base, and declining cash generation rather than by any solvency concern.

### Cash flow: strong, seasonal, and declining

Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow was $70.376M (from $83.776M) and company-defined free cash flow $65.815M (from $80.701M), -18.4%. Q1 is structurally the strongest collection quarter — the company states billings concentrate in Q4 and collections in the first half — and Q1 operating cash flow included an $81.646M decrease in accounts receivable. Annualizing produces a nonsense number. Stripping working capital, Q1 cash earnings were roughly $37.5M (net income $4.181M plus ~$33.3M of non-cash charges), and FY2026 full-year operating cash flow was approximately $159.2M (18.6% of revenue). A defensible normalized free cash flow range is $130-150M.

Two quality caveats. Capitalized costs to obtain customer contracts stood at $183.8M at April 30, 2026 (from $173.0M at January 31), roughly 21% of annual revenue on the balance sheet, with amortization up 50% year over year to $18.0M — the drag is now arriving in the P&L. And effective February 1, 2026 the amortization period for these costs was extended from five to six years, reducing sales and marketing expense by $0.5M in the quarter, about 4.7% of GAAP operating income. The stated justification (improved retention) is defensible given net dollar expansion rose from 101.8% to 103.5%, since the relevant benefit period is dollar-weighted. We treat it as a small non-operational tailwind to monitor.

### The tax line distorts GAAP earnings in both directions

Q1 FY2027 pretax income was $16.298M against a $12.117M provision — a 74.3% effective rate — driven by a $3.8M discrete charge for non-deductible stock-based compensation, $1.2M of withholding tax, and $2.3M of uncertain tax position changes at non-US entities. FY2026 ran approximately 65.7%.

This means the apparent FY2025→FY2026 earnings collapse (GAAP net income $121.609M → $22.905M, -81%) is not operational: non-GAAP operating income rose from $89.788M to $146.235M over the same period, and FY2025 net income was inflated by a non-recurring deferred-tax valuation-allowance release. Much of the current tax drag is non-cash — cash taxes paid in Q1 were only $5.374M against the $12.117M provision, with the non-current deferred tax asset drawing down from $70.400M to $64.281M. At a similar consumption rate the DTA is substantially exhausted within roughly ten quarters, after which the drag converts to cash and free cash flow compresses by an amount plausibly in the mid-tens of millions annually. That is a directional flag, not a schedule; new deductible differences arise each period and consumption is not linear.

The narrow conclusion: noisy net income invalidates earnings-based screens for this name, but it does not touch the gross margin trend, the flat gross profit dollars, or the cRPO deceleration, which are independently measured and independently negative.

Professional services growth is attributed to "implementation services provided in connection with large-scale enterprise projects." These facts are consistent with one large Asia/Middle East engagement having become the dominant incremental growth driver, but no filing names the customer, its geography, or its contract value, and management's own language ("timing of revenue recognition") signals a lumpy recognition event rather than a durable 10% run-rate account. We treat the single-large-contract reading as an unconfirmed hypothesis. What is established is the two-part fact that US revenue is shrinking and essentially all incremental revenue came from one reported region.

Forward indicators are weaker than reported revenue. RPO at January 31, 2026 was $986.5M against $987.7M a year earlier (-0.1%); cRPO was $618.8M against $612.5M (+1.0%). At April 30, 2026 RPO was $1,038.3M and cRPO $627.1M. Applying the FY2026 realized coverage ratio (FY2026 revenue $857.2M ÷ opening cRPO $612.5M = 1.40x) to the January 2026 cRPO yields roughly $866M of FY2027 revenue, about +1.0%. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast — the coverage ratio depends on in-year booking velocity and Q1 actual growth of 6.8% ran well ahead of it. But contracted revenue growing ~1% while reported revenue grew 7.6% is the standard signature of deceleration not yet visible in the P&L.

Customer count fell from approximately 1,930 to 1,677 (-13%) and customers above $1M of subscription revenue from 149 to 141 (-5%), while net dollar expansion improved from 101.8% to 103.5%. Both measures are real and they are not contradictory: shedding small logos while expanding large ones raises dollar-weighted expansion. But improving NDE does not establish diversification, and the concentration data is unambiguously worse.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than supportive: current-quarter consensus was cut 14.4% over both 30 and 90 days to $0.10, while current-year rose 1.9% to $0.49 and next-year 0.5% to $0.54. Growth prospects score 3.5 — the earnings base is roughly flat, gross profit dollars are flat, and forward contracted revenue implies low single digits.

## Competitive Landscape

Independent evaluators place Sprinklr among leaders in its founding franchises: Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader for Social Media Management and Listening (July 2026) and for Voice of the Customer Platforms (March 2026), plus ISG "Exemplary" in its 2026 CXM Buyers Guide (June 2026). Those are company-issued press releases reporting third-party evaluations, but they are genuine external validation of product position in Social and Insights.

The record where Sprinklr expanded is weaker. The 10-K concedes "difficulties with managing the implementation of certain larger Contact Center as a Service ('CCaaS') projects, which resulted in increased customer dissatisfaction, loss of certain customers, and a delay in recognizing revenue." The now-dismissed securities litigation alleged the company was concentrating resources on Sprinklr Service without disclosing the associated risks. Contact center is where the incremental TAM sits and where the best-capitalized competitors operate.

The core competitive problem is positional. Upstream, the platforms that supply the data underpinning Sprinklr's listening differentiation are repricing access and the 10-K warns that changes to their monetization models could damage the "overall value proposition." Downstream, management cites "non-renewals and reductions in contract size, particularly among customers adjusting budgets or deferring investments." Subscription cost of revenue up 20.5% on 5.8% subscription revenue growth is what a price-taker on both sides looks like in the financials. Nine points of subscription gross margin lost in nine quarters is the quantitative expression.

On AI, the filings disclose that Sprinklr's capability "leverage[s] existing and widely available technologies, such as those owned by Microsoft Azure, OpenAI, or alternative large language model providers." Sprinklr is a paying customer of those model and cloud providers. It owns the application layer, workflow and data architecture; it licenses the foundation-model capability. "AI-native" is a product-architecture claim, not a proprietary-model claim, and AI functionality flows through cost of revenue. Compounding this, "certain of our customer agreements... in some cases, prohibit[] the use of AI or generative AI in our products," capping the monetizable surface at some accounts.

One question we could not resolve: whether social-data licensing costs rose sector-wide in 2025-26. If universal, Sprinklr's relative position is intact and pricing eventually resets industry-wide. If Sprinklr is disproportionately exposed because listening is a larger share of its mix, the margin loss is idiosyncratic and permanent. Competitive position scores 4.0 on trajectory, not level.

## Risk Assessment

**Correlated Middle East exposure — the most specific and least priced risk.** A risk factor new to the FY2026/Q1 FY2027 filings states verbatim: "in connection with the 2026 Iran conflict, multiple availability zones within a cloud service provider's facilities in the United Arab Emirates on which we relied were damaged by Iranian drone strikes, resulting in the inaccessibility and potential loss of certain customer data." The company relocated data and implemented backups but "cannot provide assurance that these locations (including back up locations) will not also be adversely impacted, or that data will not be destroyed." Incremental costs were immaterial through April 30, 2026, but future costs "may be significant," and the company specifically flags reputational harm "particularly with regulated or public-sector customers." The region generating 98% of incremental revenue is the region where infrastructure was destroyed and customer data may be permanently lost, with no reserve established against contractual or data-protection liability.

**Data-agreement renewal risk.** The FY2026 10-K discloses a dated expiry for the X data agreement around December 31, 2026; we flag the exact counterparty-date pairing as requiring verification, but the general renewal-and-repricing risk is verbatim-confirmed in the Q1 FY2027 10-Q, which also warns that acquisition of data providers by competitors could impair access.

**Growth-quality concentration.** US revenue -4.9%; first-ever >10% customer on recognition timing; customer count -13%; cRPO +1% against revenue +7.6%; the fastest-growing revenue line carrying negative gross margin.

**Governance and the drift of control.** Class B carries ten votes. At April 30, 2026 Class B holders held 43.2% of shares and 88.4% of voting power; Hellman & Friedman controlled approximately 49.5% of combined voting power, up from ~48.9% at January 31. The 10-Q states the mechanism directly: "as a result of the combined effect of recent conversions of Class B common stock and corresponding sales by our former Chief Executive Officer and founder and our stock repurchase programs," H&F's stake rose, and future transfers or conversions "may result in H&F controlling greater than 50% of the combined voting power." Buybacks retire one-vote Class A shares, mechanically raising the weight of the ten-vote block. This is an arithmetic consequence rather than evidence of intent, and a $200M repurchase at 1.3x EV/sales is defensible on its own merits. But public Class A holders bear the outcome: majority control transferring without a control premium, reinforced by a classified board, no action by written consent, no cumulative voting, a 66⅔% supermajority for certain charter amendments, and exclusive Delaware forum provisions. The same structure creates plausible take-private optionality; searches returned no reporting of a strategic review, so it is a structural observation and not a catalyst.

**Tax and DTA.** 74.3% effective rate with non-deductible SBC a recurring driver; expected future exposure to OECD Pillar Two; NOL usage potentially capped by a Section 382 ownership change, which large buybacks, Class B conversions and H&F's rising stake all make more live.

**Management churn and insider behavior.** Thirteen Item 5.02 filings between February 5, 2024 and April 29, 2026 — roughly one leadership change every ten weeks — spanning the founder-to-Rory-Read CEO transition. On March 15, 2026 the company granted Read 2,101,575 shares (~$12.2M of grant-date value) in the same quarter non-GAAP operating income fell 13.6%. Insider sales on March 16 at a uniform $5.85 and June 16 at a uniform $5.30 across seven and seven insiders respectively are characteristic of sell-to-cover tax withholding rather than discretionary bearish selling, and Read retained 3,562,844 shares — we do not read these as a signal. What is notable is the absence: no open-market purchase (code P) appears among the 31 most recent Form 4 transactions, including at multi-year lows, while the company deployed $125M of shareholder cash into the same stock at $5.84. NEO average total compensation roughly doubled to $7,256,823 in FY2026 while cumulative TSR stood at 36 against a $100 IPO baseline and a peer group at 51; the extraction of that pay comparison is not clean like-for-like and should be re-derived, but the direction is clear. Compensation anchors on non-GAAP operating income, the metric that improved 63% in FY2026 largely via a 12% workforce reduction. Management quality scores 4.0.

**Risks that improved.** *In re Sprinklr Securities Litigation* (S.D.N.Y.) was dismissed March 31, 2026 and with prejudice April 24, 2026 after plaintiff declined to amend; the consolidated derivative action was dismissed May 6, 2026 without prejudice. No provision existed at April 30, 2026. Disclosure controls were concluded effective with no change in ICFR during the quarter.

**Unresolved items.** A July 1, 2026 8-K filed under Item 7.01 (Regulation FD) with exhibits could not be retrieved. The stock closed at $5.41 on July 3 and is now $6.86, a ~27% move with no verified driver. Candidates include the July 8 Gartner Leader placement, a broad small-cap software re-rating, or an investor communication. Separately, a Seeking Alpha headline dated July 15 ("Slashed Growth Outlook Leaves Shares Deeply Discounted") implies FY2027 guidance was reduced at or after the June 3 print, but the body was inaccessible and we could not confirm it. Both the 8-K content and the rally driver should be treated as unknown, which is itself a reason not to add exposure at the top of the move.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility eased from 77.1% (June 1) to 75.2% (August 9) while the stock rose 22.9%. Falling implied volatility into a 23% rally indicates the options market is not pricing a resolving binary — the uncertainty premium is being sold rather than bid, which is more consistent with a positioning-driven re-rating than with an approaching disclosed catalyst. At 75%, absolute implied volatility remains high for a $1.6B software name, so the market still prices wide outcomes. The put/call open-interest ratio reading of 0.00 (from 1.68) is almost certainly a feed artifact and we place no weight on it. Short interest at 13.0% of float and 3.6 days to cover creates two-sided risk: enough crowding to fuel a squeeze on any positive disclosure, not enough to make the short side crowded in the technical sense. Options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; it argues against paying up here. Risk factors score 4.0.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $10.00 (+46%).** Gross margin compression proves to be AI-inference investment rather than permanent data repricing, and stabilizes at or above 74% subscription margin as inference costs fall industry-wide. The Asia/Middle East engagement is durable and renews, converting the 10.4% recognition-timing customer into recurring run-rate revenue and validating the 86% regional growth. Net dollar expansion continues rising above 103.5% while the shed small-logo base stops depressing count. The residual $75M of buyback executes, the ASR settles favorably, and the share count declines. At 2.0x EV/sales on ~$900M of revenue plus $443M of net cash, the equity is worth roughly $9.60, with the H&F control trajectory adding take-private optionality above that. Gartner leadership in Social/Insights supports a premium to the current 1.36x.

**Bear case — $4.50 (-34%).** Subscription gross margin follows management's guidance to roughly 70%, removing ~$31M of gross profit. The 10.4% customer does not recur, the "Other" region reverts, and consolidated revenue goes flat to slightly negative as the 4.9% US decline broadens. Non-GAAP operating income falls to roughly $100M; at 7x plus net cash the equity is worth about $4.80, and a UAE-related contractual or data-protection liability, or a failure to renew the X data agreement around year-end, takes it to $4.25-4.50. The 52-week low of $4.715 sits inside this range, so the bear case is not hypothetical territory.

**Base case — $7.00 (+2%).** Revenue grows 1-3% to roughly $870-880M as cRPO implies; subscription gross margin drifts down another 1-2 points; non-GAAP operating income settles near $120-125M; free cash flow normalizes at $120-130M before the DTA drag begins converting to cash. At 9x forward non-GAAP operating income or 10x forward free cash flow plus $443M of net cash, fair value is $6.60-7.05.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives $7.13, approximately +4% from $6.86. For a name at 75% implied volatility with a -34% bear case and no identifiable near-term catalyst, that is a less-than-fair expected risk-adjusted return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $7.00 (+2%). Bull $10.00 (+46%). Bear $4.50 (-34%).**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength. The stock has advanced 27% in five weeks on an unverified driver, sits above the analyst median target of $6.75 and above the analyst low of $6.00, and 13.4M options are exercisable at a $6.50 weighted average strike — now in the money and a source of supply. Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized for the cash-and-buyback floor (net cash of $1.89/share plus $75M of remaining authorization limits how far the equity can fall on multiple compression alone), rather than exit entirely. Prospective investors should wait. Do not short: no dated catalyst, 13% short interest with only 3.6 days to cover, a controlling holder that could take the company private, and $442.8M of net cash all argue against it.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) Subscription gross margin flat or up sequentially for two consecutive quarters — Q2 FY2027 (reporting approximately early September 2026) is the first test, and management's own guidance says this should not happen, so a stabilization print would be genuinely new information. (2) Q2 disclosure confirming the >10% customer is a recurring run-rate account rather than a recognition-timing artifact, with the "Other" region sustaining growth above 40%. (3) cRPO growth accelerating above 5% year over year, which would break the ~1% forward-contracted-revenue signal. (4) A price near $5.50-5.75, which would restore roughly 20% upside to the base case and re-establish a margin of safety. (5) Any open-market insider purchase by the CEO or CFO.

Full reassessment at the Q2 print.

## Conclusion

Sprinklr's operating problem is stated in its own income statement and its own guidance: revenue grew 6.8% and gross profit 0.1%, subscription gross margin has fallen roughly nine points in nine quarters, and management expects further decline. Non-GAAP operating income fell 13.6%, underlying GAAP operating income (adjusted for restructuring in both periods) fell 31.6%, and free cash flow fell 18.4%. Forward contracted revenue grew about 1% while reported revenue grew 7.6%. Growth composition worsened: the US shrank 4.9% while one region supplied 98% of the consolidated increase and one customer crossed 10% of revenue on recognition timing, in a region where a relied-upon UAE data center was destroyed and customer data may be permanently lost.

The offsets are substantive — $442.8M of net cash with no debt, 1.36x EV/sales, roughly 8x EV/non-GAAP operating income, $75M of remaining buyback authorization, Gartner leadership in Social and Insights, and securities litigation dismissed with prejudice. They establish a floor. They do not establish upside from $6.86, because that floor was already there at $5.41 five weeks ago, and the 27% intervening move has consumed the margin of safety without any verifiable change in the fundamentals. Adjusted for roughly $80M of annual stock-based compensation and the deferred-tax asset that converts a non-cash tax drag into cash within roughly ten quarters, the multiple is not the bargain the headline metrics suggest.

Our prior view moved from AVOID to HOLD as the cash-and-buyback floor became clear. It now moves to REDUCE for the mirror-image reason: a higher price against softer operating results. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +4% against a -34% bear case, with no dated catalyst and 75% implied volatility, does not justify holding a full position. Trim into strength; revisit at $5.50-5.75 or on two consecutive quarters of stable subscription gross margin.
