## Executive Summary

Intel's June-2026 quarter was the best operating result the company has produced in five years. Revenue grew 25.4% to $16,128M — the fastest rate since 2011 — gross margin recovered to 40.4% from 27.5%, R&D fell 8.6% as restructuring completed, and operating income swung from -$3,176M to +$1,796M, a $4,972M year-over-year improvement. Consensus has responded forcefully: current-year EPS has been marked up from $1.09 to $1.52 (+39.7% over 90 days) and next-year from $1.52 to $2.05 (+35.5%), with the 30-day revisions running nearly as large as the 90-day figures. Four consecutive quarters of beats, all by wide margins, support confidence in those estimates.

The composition of the recovery is where the case weakens. Client revenue rose on ASP +27% against volume -8% (H1: +22% / -10%); server revenue rose on ASP +48% and volume +9%. Management states plainly that "market demand exceeded our available product supply," citing industry-wide substrate and memory constraints. Intel is monetizing component scarcity created elsewhere in the supply chain. Management guides client constraints to ease in H2 2026 while industry-wide constraints persist into next year. Intel Foundry, the reason the balance sheet carries $105.7B of net PP&E, generated $5,765M of revenue in Q2 of which $5,472M was intersegment and only $293M external — and $181M of that external figure was Altera, a related party in which Intel retains a 49% stake. Non-related-party external foundry revenue was roughly $112M, about 0.7% of consolidated revenue. The FY2025 10-K states Intel "has been unsuccessful to date in securing any significant external foundry customers for any of our nodes."

At $101.06 the stock trades at 66.5x current-year and 49.3x next-year consensus, ~9.3x EV/TTM revenue, and 5.8x the $17.36 book value. The prior review was written with the stock near $141; the 28% decline since, against a 38% upward revision to current-year estimates, materially improves risk/reward and is the reason our valuation score rises. It does not make the shares cheap. A sum-of-parts anchored on Intel Products' segment earnings, net of Foundry's losses and unallocated corporate cost, lands closer to $50-60 per share; the gap to spot is Foundry option value and the sovereign strategic premium attached to a U.S. government shareholder. We can neither dismiss that option nor underwrite it from the segment data. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Intel reports two segments plus a residual, and the split is the investment case.

**Intel Products** — CCPG (renamed in 2026 from Client Computing Group to Client Computing and Physical AI Group) plus DCAI (Data Center and AI) — generated $15,139M of Q2 2026 revenue at $4,817M of operating profit, a 31.8% segment margin. This is a healthy franchise built on the x86 installed base and software ecosystem.

**Intel Foundry** — process technology, fabs, and advanced packaging — generated $5,765M of revenue (95% intersegment) and lost $2,089M, a -36.2% operating margin. H1 Foundry loss narrowed to $4,526M from $5,488M, but the 10-Q attributes roughly $1.8B of that improvement to the absence of prior-year impairment and accelerated-depreciation charges, partly offset by ~$340M of lower Q2 product profit from a higher-cost Intel 18A wafer mix. Underlying node economics deteriorated as 18A ramped.

**All Other** — principally Mobileye and IMS Nanofabrication — contributed $701M of revenue and $230M of operating income.

Structural exposures: three customers were 43% of FY2025 revenue (45% in 2024, 40% in 2023 — flat to worse); 70% of revenue is non-U.S.; China billings were 24% of total revenue. Only TSMC and Samsung can supply Intel's own leading-edge outsourced needs, and Intel discloses no long-term contract with TSMC. ASML is the sole EUV supplier.

## Financial Analysis

**The four-year revenue decline ended in 2026.** Revenue fell from $63,054M (FY2022) to $52,853M (FY2025), a 16.2% cumulative decline, while the SOX index roughly tripled. FY2025 operating loss narrowed to -$2,214M from -$11,678M and net loss to -$267M from -$18,756M, but the FY2025 bottom line was substantially non-operating: pretax income was +$1,557M against a -$2,214M operating loss, a $3,771M swing from asset monetization (Altera), equity gains, and interest income. Net income attributable to Intel was approximately breakeven at roughly -$98M on a retained-earnings roll-forward derivation. The 98% effective tax rate reflects a full domestic valuation allowance: U.S. losses generate no deferred benefit while foreign profits are taxed.

**GAAP earnings are currently uninterpretable.** Q2 2026 operating income was +$1,796M; net loss attributable to Intel was -$11,033M. The bridge is a single line — "interest and other, net" of -$12,576M, containing a $12.5B (Q2) / $13.6B (H1) fair-value loss on the Escrowed Shares derivative issued in connection with the August 22, 2025 Commerce Department agreement. The liability grew from $2.7B to $15.6B, and the 10-Q attributes the loss to "an increase in our stock price." The full charge was added back in operating cash flow. Excluding it, Q2 net income attributable to Intel was approximately +$1,467M; non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 beat a $0.22 non-GAAP consensus. A rising share price mechanically produces larger reported GAAP losses and lower book equity. The correct treatment is to exclude the mark from earnings while counting the shares in the capitalization. Do not ignore the instrument, because it is settled in Intel stock and the economic cost of the CHIPS-to-equity conversion genuinely rose with the price.

**Equity fell $26,739M in two quarters** — from $114,281M to $87,542M. Drivers reconcile as: -$14,761M H1 net loss attributable to Intel, -$13,545M charged directly to capital in excess of par for the Ireland SCIP buyout, plus smaller items. Book value per share is $17.36; the stock trades at 5.8x.

**Leverage is manageable; capital cost is not cheap.** Total debt $50,537M against $29,727M of cash and short-term investments gives net debt of $20,810M. TTM revenue is ~$57,032M, TTM EBITDA ~$11,358M (~$15,258M excluding the $3.9B Q1 Mobileye goodwill impairment). That is gross debt/EBITDA ~4.4x (~3.3x ex-impairment) and net debt/EBITDA ~1.8x (~1.4x ex-impairment). Liquidity is genuine: an undrawn $7.0B revolver to February 2029, a new $3.0B three-year facility, a $10B commercial paper program with nothing outstanding, and roughly $8.8B of refundable AMIC tax receivables. Against that, Intel has suffered multiple credit downgrades, and the June 2026 issuance priced expensively at the long end — $1.8B at 6.13% due 2056 and $500M at 6.20% due 2066 alongside $1.0B at 4.65% (2031) and $2.2B at 5.30% (2036). A capital-intensive issuer paying 6%+ for 30-year money is structurally disadvantaged versus fab peers funding from operating cash flow.

**Cash flow improved, but do not annualize H1.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $8,102M versus $2,863M. Capex appears in two places: $6,192M of PP&E additions in investing plus $1,423M in financing (extended-payment-terms arrangements). H1 free cash flow is therefore +$1,910M on the narrow definition and roughly +$487M including the financing-classified additions — against FY2025 FCF of -$4,949M. Three quality caveats: H1 OCF includes $1.7B of customer deposits received under new long-term supply arrangements; Intel did not factor receivables in H1 2026 versus $1.6B sold in H1 2025 (a genuine improvement in cash-flow quality that understates the underlying YoY gain); and cash taxes fell to $904M from $1,793M. Management explicitly warns of "corresponding detriment to cash flow in future periods as these actions cease."

The decisive forward item is FY2026 capex guided above $20B against $7.6B of H1 cash capex across both classifications — implying an H2 step-up of roughly $12B. As a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast: if H2 operating cash flow merely repeats H1's $8.1B while capex runs at ~$12.5B, FY2026 FCF is approximately **-$4B**, before offsets from AMIC receivable refunds and Arizona SCIP partner contributions ($4,082M contributed in H1).

**One cash item bypasses every conventional FCF calculation.** On April 8, 2026 Intel reacquired Apollo's 49% interest in the Fab 34 Ireland joint venture for $14.2B cash, eliminating $142M of non-controlling interest and a $532M derivative liability, with the residual $13.5B charged directly to capital in excess of par. The outflow sits in financing as "partner distributions." It never touched the income statement, never touched the FCF calculation, and consumed more cash than Intel's entire H1 operating cash flow. The strategic logic may be sound — Fab 34 runs Intel 4 and Intel 3, the nodes now enjoying the ASP surge — but any 2026 cash-burn analysis starting from OCF minus capex materially understates cash deployed.

A note on screening output: an Altman Z-Score of 1.48 ("distress") circulates from substituting book equity for market equity. Recomputed properly on the June 27, 2026 balance sheet with a ~$505B market capitalization, Z ≈ 3.7 ("safe"). The score is carried almost entirely by the market-equity term, since TTM operating income of -$77M contributes essentially nothing. Halve the equity value and Z falls to roughly 2.2, the grey/distress band. Solvency risk is not imminent, but the screen's comfort is reflexively price-dependent rather than earnings-derived.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision pattern here is as strong as any in the coverage universe: current quarter $0.39 from $0.26 (+47.6% over 90 days), next quarter $0.43 from $0.30 (+41.0%), current year $1.52 from $1.09 (+39.7%), next year $2.05 from $1.52 (+35.5%), with 30 to 40 analysts contributing. Revisions of this magnitude paired with 25.4% revenue growth normally precede further re-rating as consensus catches up. We weight it heavily in the growth score and it is the single strongest reason this is not an AVOID.

We do not, however, treat it as a BUY trigger, for two reasons the data itself supplies. First, the multiple. The revision signal is most reliable when the stock is cheap against the improving estimates — forward P/E below roughly 30x or PEG below 1.5. Intel trades at 49.3x next-year consensus. Beats are already the expectation at that multiple; the asymmetry around any disappointment is unfavorable.

Second, the composition. This is a pricing recovery inside a shortage. Client ASP +27% with units -8% means Intel is extracting more revenue from a shrinking installed-base refresh, not winning sockets. Server is better — ASP +48% and volume +9% on hyperscaler demand — and DCAI grew from 31% to 39% of product revenue year over year with a ~40% segment margin. But management attributes ASP gains mostly to premium mix "with demand-based pricing actions contributing to a lesser extent," and guides client constraints to ease in H2. When substrate and memory availability normalizes, the pricing component of the recovery reverses. Mix shift toward AI PCs and higher-core-count server SKUs is plausibly durable; scarcity rent is not.

Forward-demand evidence is thin but new and real. Intel entered long-term customer arrangements in Q1 2026 with $1.7B of deposits received in Q2 and carries $1.2B of long-term customer deposits to secure future supply. Intel does not define these as backlog and neither should we — they evidence that some customers are paying cash to lock supply.

The Foundry growth story remains prospective. The highest-signal change between filings: the January 2026 10-K said Intel *may pause or discontinue* Intel 14A; the July 2026 10-Q states "we committed to completing development of Intel 14A," with expansion projects underway and "continued progress towards meeting performance and design milestones for potential significant customers to evaluate Intel 14A." Intel would not commit that capital without improved confidence. But the 10-Q hedges exactly where it matters — scale and pace "dictated by the amount of committed demand... from our Intel products roadmap **and** design wins with potential significant external customers." The only named external foundry customer to date, Fortinet, is on **Intel 4**, not a leading-edge node. Reports of 14A risk production pulled to 2027 and Synopsys EDA certification (both late July 2026) are secondary and not filing-confirmed. So is Terafab: neither the FY2025 10-K nor the Q2 2026 10-Q mentions Terafab, Tesla, SpaceX, or xAI, and Tesla/SpaceX confirming a $16.8B Texas first phase on August 6 confirms the project, not Intel's economics in it. The reported Apple relationship rests on a presidential statement neither company has confirmed.

But quality-adjusted comparisons cut against Intel. TSMC operates at ~68% gross margins with a genuine external customer book; Intel at 49x forward, 34.8% FY2025 gross margin, a -36% margin foundry, and a data-center franchise that AMD has passed is expensive relative to better-positioned names in the same theme.

Two anchors bracket the range. On earnings: annualizing Intel Products' Q2 segment operating income gives $19.3B, but Foundry's annualized drag is roughly -$8.4B and unallocated corporate cost runs ~$6B/year. On the other side, if Foundry converts into a $30B+ revenue third-party business at even half of TSMC's margins, the fab base alone supports a valuation multiples of today's price. Analyst targets: mean $115.17, median $110, low $74, high $200 — a $126 spread across the range. On a stock at 49x forward, targets are not independent confirmation; they reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price. We take the low ($74) as a plausible earnings-anchored anchor and the high ($200) as the foundry-succeeds case.

The prior review was written at ~$141. Estimates have since risen ~38% and the price has fallen 28%, compressing the forward multiple from roughly 69x to 49x on today's consensus. That is a genuine improvement in risk/reward and the sole reason the valuation score rises to 3.8 from 3.0. What the sellers appear to be discounting — and we cannot say they are wrong — is that the beat was price-led, that the 14A commitment produced no named leading-edge customer, that FY2026 free cash flow will likely be negative, and that the SOX index entered a bear market in mid-July.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory, not level, is what matters, and the trajectory is mixed with the negative side concentrated in the market that determines value creation this cycle.

**Data center.** AMD reported June-2026 quarter data-center revenue of $6.7B, +107%, against Intel DCAI's $6.262B, +59%. Segment scopes are not identical — AMD's includes Instinct accelerators, Intel's includes network, edge and ASICs — so this is directional rather than a precise share statistic, but AMD has passed Intel on that measure, and AMD guides data-center revenue to more than double in 2027 on multi-year hyperscaler agreements. Nvidia's $75.2B quarterly data-center revenue frames the accelerator market Intel has effectively exited: Gaudi inventory charges were $922M in 2024 and $375M in 2025, and the 10-K concedes the effort "was unsuccessful."

**Client.** Volumes down 8% in the quarter and 10% in H1. Intel retains the x86 installed base and software ecosystem, which is a durable moat, but it is not gaining share.

**Foundry.** TSMC runs ~68% gross margins on a record ~$40B quarter. Intel Foundry runs -36% operating margin on roughly $112M of non-related-party external revenue. The gap is not narrowing on any disclosed metric. What Intel does have, and it is scarce: the only U.S.-headquartered leading-edge logic manufacturing at scale, advanced packaging capability that both the Google IPU co-design and the Terafab framing emphasize, the U.S. government as shareholder and defense-chip customer, and a captive internal customer large enough to fund node development without external anchors. That last point is why the 18A ramp is possible at all, and also why Foundry margins are compressed, since internal wafers are transfer-priced.

Intel released its first 18A products in high-volume production at the start of 2026 and moved 18A-P into risk production in June 2026. That is the first credible leading-edge node execution in years and deserves credit. It is not yet monetized externally.

## Risk Assessment

**The 14A binary, described by the company as near-irreversible.** The FY2025 10-K states that failure to secure a significant external 14A customer may lead Intel to pause or discontinue 14A *and subsequent leading-edge nodes*, with consequences including dependence on TSMC (no long-term contract), impairments against a >$100B PP&E base whose "substantial majority" relates to foundry, likely discontinuation of the Ohio fabs, loss of eligibility for and possible repayment of government incentives, and SCIP penalty payments. It adds that such a decision "may be effectively irreversible." This dominates the file.

**Uninsured geopolitical exposure to a named target.** The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses that following the February 28, 2026 U.S./Israel strikes on Iran, Iran published a list of U.S. companies with Middle East operations whose facilities it would target, "with Intel being near the top of that list." It further discloses that "a significant portion of our current and anticipated future revenues are generated from products on Intel 7 manufactured at our fabrication facility in Israel," that Intel carries **no business-interruption insurance for war or political violence**, and that Israeli PP&E is self-insured. Iranian strikes on Qatari energy fields have created a global helium shortage, helium being essential to semiconductor manufacturing. This is a live, uninsured, high-severity tail attached to a specific named fab and node. The disclosure does not quantify Intel 7's revenue share or the replaceability of that capacity.

**Litigation with large tails.** VLSI: ~$1.0B accrued, and in April 2026 the Federal Circuit **reversed and remanded** the N.D. Cal. case Intel had won on all eight patents; a Texas case had a $1.5B infringement finding affirmed with damages vacated and remanded; a third carries a $949M jury award without final judgment. EireOg has filed 11 complaints since April 2024 against Intel's customers (Intel indemnifies AWS, Cisco, Dell, HPE, IBM, Lenovo, Oracle) alleging damages in excess of $2.0B, though Intel won a no-infringement verdict in the April 2026 Cisco trial. The EC fine stands at $308M accrued, on appeal.

**Governance and structure.** A March 2026 derivative suit (Delaware Chancery, removed to D. Del.) names Intel's directors, the Commerce Department, and Secretary Lutnick, alleging breach of fiduciary duty in approving the August 2025 Warrant and Common Stock Agreement and **seeking invalidation of the agreement**; motions to dismiss were filed in May 2026. The 10-K itself warns the arrangement "could be deemed unauthorized, void or voidable," that it eliminated Intel's contractual rights to future CHIPS grants, and that government ownership may deter strategic transactions. Separately, the 241M DOC warrant shares at a $20.00 strike become exercisable only if Intel ceases to own at least 51% of Foundry — a structural obstacle to the foundry-separation path many bulls treat as the value unlock.

**Dilution, properly framed.** Shares went 4,330M (Dec 2024) → 4,994M (Dec 2025) → 5,043M (Jun 2026), +16.5% over eighteen months. 143M Escrowed Shares remain unreleased (71M already in basic EPS, 71M contingent); if unreleased at period end, half go to DOC for no consideration and half are cancelled. The 143M equals 2.8% of current shares — existing holders retain ~97.2% of their proportional stake, taking the count to 5,186M and market capitalization to ~$524B. A maximum contingent case including the warrant takes the count to ~5,497M, an 8.3% ownership dilution.

**Concentration.** Three customers at 43% of revenue and China billings at 24% into an escalating U.S.-China technology conflict. DCAI's rise from 31% to 39% of product revenue is a *mix* shift and does not establish customer diversification; the concentration disclosure has moved from 40% (2023) to 43% (2025).

**$19.8B of Arizona SCIP PP&E** sits on the balance sheet but is disclosed as unavailable to Intel — usable only to settle VIE obligations — and Intel owes volume-related damages if it fails to operate Arizona at minimum production levels.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 92.4% against 77.5% one-year realized is rich by 14.8 points, with IV at the 86th percentile of the current chain and the term structure in **backwardation** (92.4% near versus 78.3% long-dated). The market is pricing near-term stress and charging a premium for it, which makes hedging expensive rather than opportune. Put/call volume ratios rise monotonically with tenor — 0.42 at one week, 0.85 at one month, 1.58 at three months, 2.50 at six months — indicating protection being accumulated out to the November expiry rather than positioned for the next print. Nearest-expiry skew is described as balanced; we do not infer direction from the longer-dated skew values without a defined sign convention. Unusual activity is concentrated in near-dated calls around the $101 strike (15,830 contracts against 730 open interest at $101 for August 10; 17,300 against 1,649 for August 7) alongside a 16.5x volume/OI print in the $102 August 7 put — two-way positioning around spot rather than a directional tell.

Short interest is only 2.4% of float at 1.0 day to cover. There is no bear consensus in the shares and no squeeze fuel. That cuts both ways: the downside case is uncrowded, but informed capital is not expressing it either.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $160 (+58%)

The supply-constrained pricing environment persists into 2027 as management indicates, server volumes keep growing on hyperscaler demand, and premium mix proves structural rather than cyclical. FY2027 EPS reaches $2.50-3.00 against the $2.05 consensus as 18A yields mature and the higher-cost wafer drag reverses. Intel announces a named significant external customer on 14A — the Synopsys EDA certification and the pull-in of risk production to 2027 are ecosystem prerequisites being met — and Foundry's loss trajectory inflects toward breakeven, at which point the >$100B PP&E base is revalued from a liability into an asset. The U.S. government shareholding converts from a dilution and litigation overhang into demonstrated commercial support, with defense and possibly Apple volume following. Estimate revisions of the current magnitude continue, and the multiple holds because the market re-underwrites Foundry as a business rather than an option. $160 is roughly 78x consensus FY2027 or 40x a $4.00 normalized 2028 figure.

### Bear Case — $60 (-41%)

Substrate and memory availability normalizes through 2027, the pricing tailwind reverses, and client volumes — already -10% in H1 — keep declining while AMD compounds data-center share off a base that already exceeds DCAI. FY2026 free cash flow prints around -$4B as capex steps up above $20B, and the balance sheet absorbs another year of negative FCF at 6%+ marginal long-end funding cost. No significant external 14A customer is named; Intel either pauses the node or funds it from internal demand alone, and the market re-anchors on earnings rather than option value. At 29x consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.05, the stock is $60. A tail path — an impairment against foundry PP&E, an adverse VLSI or EireOg outcome, invalidation of the DOC agreement, or disruption at the Israeli fab against which Intel carries no war-risk insurance — takes it materially below that.

### Base Case — $105 (+4%)

Consensus estimates prove roughly right at $1.52 and $2.05, the pricing environment fades gradually rather than abruptly, Foundry's loss narrows without a leading-edge external win being announced in the next twelve months, and the multiple drifts down as earnings rise, leaving the price approximately where it is. A probability-weighted blend of the earnings-anchored ($70) and option-value ($150-160) cases lands near $105.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** We lack sufficient evidence to underwrite either direction. The estimate-revision and earnings-momentum evidence is too strong to call for underperformance; the multiple and the segment data are too weak to underwrite appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (guided to approximately $0.38 non-GAAP EPS on $15.8-16.8B of revenue, per the July 23 release) in late October, and any 8-K naming an external 14A customer. The six-month options tenor prices a ±34.5% move, so the market expects resolution inside that window.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. A **named significant external customer on 14A or 18A** disclosed in an Intel filing or press release — not a co-design engagement, not a related party, not an older node. The Fortinet win on Intel 4 does not qualify.
2. **Q3/Q4 client unit volumes returning to growth** with gross margin held at or above 40%, demonstrating the recovery has a volume leg rather than only a pricing leg.
3. **Price below $75** (roughly 37x FY2027 consensus and near the analyst low), which would restore asymmetry against our $60 bear anchor.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 10%** from $2.05, or a Q3 miss against the ~$0.38 guide — which would break the four-quarter beat streak that currently anchors confidence in estimates.
2. **Client and server ASP growth decelerating below +10%** while volumes remain negative, confirming the recovery was scarcity rent.
3. **Any impairment charge against foundry PP&E**, a disclosed decision to pause 14A, or an adverse ruling requiring repayment of government incentives.
4. **Physical disruption to the Israeli fab operations**, which Intel discloses is uninsured for war and political violence.

**Position management for existing holders:** the shares are up roughly 400% over twelve months and 29% off the June 30 high of $142.35. We would not add at $101 and would trim into any move above $130 absent a named leading-edge foundry customer, since at that price the earnings-anchored downside gap widens to more than 50%.

## Conclusion

Intel is a genuinely improving business trading at a price that requires more improvement than the disclosures support. The operating turnaround is real and measurable: 25.4% revenue growth, 12.9 points of gross-margin recovery, a $5.0B year-over-year operating swing, four consecutive large beats, and 35-40% upward estimate revisions over 90 days. Those are the strongest forward indicators available and they rule out an AVOID.

But the recovery runs on ASPs rather than units in client, Foundry loses 36 cents on every revenue dollar and has roughly $112M of non-related-party external revenue, AMD's data-center segment has passed DCAI, FY2026 free cash flow is likely negative against capex above $20B, and the company discloses being named near the top of an Iranian target list with a significant portion of revenue produced at an uninsured Israeli fab. At 49x next-year consensus, 9.2x EV/revenue and 5.8x book, the equity prices Foundry as a business rather than an option, and the segment data does not yet show one.

The 28% decline from the June high against a 38% upward estimate revision has genuinely improved risk/reward, which is why our valuation and overall scores tick higher rather than lower. It has not created a margin of safety. Overall score 4.5/10. **HOLD.**
