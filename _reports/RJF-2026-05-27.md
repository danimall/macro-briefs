**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Raymond James Financial is a well-capitalized, diversified financial services firm with $1.87 trillion in client assets under administration and a 20%+ return on tangible common equity. The business model vertically integrates advisory, banking, asset management, and investment banking, generating durable earnings power. At $152/share (12.8x forward FY2026E EPS of $11.87), the valuation is undemanding relative to the quality of the franchise.

However, the near-term earnings trajectory is deteriorating. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised downward across all forecast periods over the past 30 days (-1.9% current year, -2.5% next year), and the earnings beat record is uneven (2-for-4 quarters, including a material -8.4% miss). The combination of NII headwinds from rate cuts, rising compensation costs from revenue mix shift, an approaching $100B asset regulatory threshold, and unresolved SEC inquiry into cash sweep practices creates multiple concurrent headwinds without a clear near-term catalyst for re-rating. The stock warrants a hold pending evidence that revenue growth can overcome margin compression.

## Company Overview

Raymond James Financial operates through five segments: Private Client Group (PCG, the core advisory platform), Capital Markets (investment banking and fixed income), Asset Management, Bank, and Other. The firm employs approximately 19,500 associates and serves clients through 8,943 financial advisors — split between employees (3,878) and independent contractors (5,065). Its proprietary "AdvisorChoice" model, which allows advisors to affiliate in whichever capacity suits their practice, is a genuine competitive differentiator for recruiting.

The firm's fiscal year ends September 30. Revenue of $14.07B in FY2025 grew 10% YoY, driven primarily by asset management fees and recovering investment banking activity. Net income was $2.13B (diluted EPS $10.30). The stock currently trades at approximately 2.3x tangible book value.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Composition and Trajectory:** FY2025 revenues of $14.07B grew 10% on the back of: (1) PCG asset management fees +14% to $5.98B (largest contributor), (2) Capital Markets investment banking +26% to $1.04B, and (3) brokerage +6-11%. The headwind was combined NII + RJBDP fees declining 4% to $2.63B as short-term rates fell.

This revenue mix shift is structurally important. Fee-based advisory revenue has higher associated compensation costs (~64.5% comp ratio, up from 62.8% two years ago), while NII has minimal direct compensation cost. Result: revenue grew 10% but pre-tax income grew only 3%. This reflects the ongoing secular shift toward fee-based accounts (now 60.5% of AUA, up from 56.9% in FY2023), and the dynamic is persistent rather than temporary.

**Profitability Metrics:**
- Pre-tax margin: 19.3% (down from 20.6% prior year)
- ROTCE: 20.6% — top-quartile for diversified financial services
- FCF: $2.25B (23.4% FCF margin)
- EPS diluted: $10.30 (+6% YoY, aided by $1.1B in buybacks)

**Balance Sheet:** Total assets $88.23B. Tier 1 leverage ratio 13.1%, total capital ratio 24.1% — well above regulatory minimums. Long-term debt increased to $3.52B following the $1.5B September 2025 senior note issuance (4.90% 10-year + 5.65% 30-year). Corporate cash of $3.67B provides substantial deployment capacity. Credit ratings A-/A3 with stable outlook.

**Capital Return:** $1.1B in buybacks at an average price of $148/share in FY2025 (7.4M shares retired). Dividend yield approximately 1.4%. Combined capital return exceeded 70% of net income.

**Key Financial Concern:** The $52B loan portfolio includes $7.84B in CRE (5% office), and nonperforming loans ticked up to $186M (0.36% of loans). Net charge-offs are low at 0.08%, but in a declining-rate environment with CRE stress intensifying, the reserve adequacy buffer ($170M potential increase under management's "downside case") deserves monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

**Positives:**
- Record client AUA of $1.87T in April 2026 (+22% YoY, +6% MoM)
- Fee-based AUA growing faster than total AUA (28% YoY vs. 22%) — reflects deepening client relationships
- Securities-based lending portfolio grew from $14.5B to $19.8B over three years — high-quality, low-risk growth engine
- Capital Markets recovered from -$91M pre-tax (FY2023) to +$146M (FY2025) — still has normalization upside
- Recruiting investment up 22% in Q1 FY2026 — forward indicator of advisor headcount growth

**Negatives:**
- Net new asset growth decelerated from 7.7% (FY2023) to 3.8% (FY2025), though Q1 FY2026 showed rebound to 8% annualized
- NII faces continued pressure from rate cuts; management's rate sensitivity shows -$80M per 100bp decline
- Consensus EPS estimates declining across all periods
- The revenue-to-earnings growth conversion ratio is poor due to comp ratio expansion

**Forward Estimates:** FY2026E EPS $11.87 (13 analysts), FY2027E $13.38. These imply 15% and 13% growth respectively — reasonable but estimates are moving in the wrong direction, and the 2-for-4 earnings track record undermines confidence in delivery against consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

At $152, RJF trades at:
- 12.8x FY2026E EPS ($11.87)
- 11.4x FY2027E EPS ($13.38)
- ~2.3x tangible book value
- ~14.8x trailing EPS ($10.30)

**Peer Context:** Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley both trade at 18.2x trailing earnings; Schwab at 17.9x. RJF's discount is structural — it is a smaller, less diversified franchise with less brand recognition in institutional markets. The discount reflects RJF's positioning as a mid-tier wealth management platform with limited international scale.

The median analyst target of $172 implies 13% upside from current levels. The low target of $147 is near the current price, suggesting limited downside risk is priced in.

**Fair Value Range:** At 14-15x FY2026E EPS ($11.87), fair value sits at $166-178. The current price ($152) offers modest upside to the midpoint ($172), but with estimates declining, the "E" in the multiple is a moving target. If FY2026 EPS comes in at $11.50 (a modest miss), 14x implies $161 — only 6% upside, insufficient to compensate for the multi-dimensional risk profile.

## Competitive Landscape

RJF occupies a defensible niche: large enough to offer a full-service platform (banking, lending, research, investment banking) but small enough to maintain a culture-driven, advisor-centric model that wirehouses struggle to replicate. The AdvisorChoice model is a genuine recruiting advantage.

However, competitive intensity is increasing. LPL Financial is aggressively acquiring advisor teams. PE-backed platforms (Cetera, Advisor Group) are offering rich transition deals. The departure of "primarily one large branch" in Q1 FY2025 — significant enough for management to disclose — demonstrates that large advisor relationships create concentration risk.

The secular shift toward fee-based accounts is a tailwind for the business model but is shared by all competitors. RJF's differentiation is cultural (long-tenured advisors, no forced protocols) rather than economic (pricing or technology superiority).

Net new asset growth deceleration (7.7% → 5.5% → 3.8%) is a potential warning that competitive moats may be narrowing, though the Q1 FY2026 rebound to 8% provides some counter-evidence.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cash Sweep Regulatory/Litigation Risk:** An SEC Division of Enforcement inquiry (since August 2024) and two consolidated class action lawsuits target cash sweep practices. RJBDP fees from third-party banks were $486M in FY2025. Any regulatory mandate to increase client yields would directly compress this revenue stream — potentially $50-100M annually depending on the magnitude of yield enhancement required. This is an industry-wide risk (Oppenheimer recently settled its own cash sweep case), which limits its stock-specific impact but creates earnings uncertainty.

**2. $100B Asset Threshold:** At $88.23B and growing ~6% annually, RJF will likely trigger Category IV enhanced prudential standards by FY2027. The AOCI opt-out elimination is the most material consequence — unrealized losses on AFS securities (currently excluded from regulatory capital) would flow through, potentially pressuring capital ratios in rising-rate environments. Compliance cost increase likely $30-50M annually.

**3. NII Headwinds:** Combined NII + RJBDP fees already declined 4% in FY2025. With the Fed having cut rates, the sensitivity table shows further pressure. Management's guidance of "unfavorable impact in fiscal 2026" is explicit.

**4. Estimate Momentum Negative:** All forecast periods show declining estimates over both 30-day and 90-day windows. This removes one of the strongest arguments for a directional call.

**5. Short Interest:** At 5.2% of float with 6.1 days to cover — modestly elevated, consistent with skepticism about near-term earnings trajectory.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 25.8% is only marginally above 1-year historical vol of 24.2% — the options market is not pricing exceptional uncertainty. IV percentile at 6% suggests the stock is near its lowest implied volatility level, which can indicate complacency or simply a lack of catalysts. The 3-month put/call volume ratio of 2.29 indicates meaningful institutional put buying at the July expiry — consistent with hedging around fiscal Q3 results or regulatory developments. The elevated near-term put skew (3.6%) confirms a slight bearish lean but is not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-185, 15-22% upside):**
- Record AUA continues to grow at 8%+ annualized, driving fee revenue acceleration
- Investment banking activity sustains its recovery into FY2026, providing incremental operating leverage
- Cash sweep litigation resolves with an industry-wide framework that limits financial impact
- New CEO Shoukry deploys the $3.67B cash position into accretive acquisitions (GreensLedge as template)
- 14-15x $12.50 FY2026E EPS (revised up) = $175-188

**Bear Case ($125-135, 11-18% downside):**
- Rate cuts accelerate, NII declines 10-15% YoY, overwhelming fee revenue growth
- SEC cash sweep resolution requires material yield enhancement, compressing RJBDP revenue $75-100M
- Recruiting investment fails to generate proportional advisor headcount growth; NNA growth stalls below 4%
- Credit losses in CRE portfolio materialize; reserves increase $100M+
- 11-12x $11.00 FY2026E EPS (revised down) = $121-132

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats >5%, demonstrating that fee revenue growth is outpacing comp ratio expansion
2. Cash sweep SEC inquiry resolved with financial impact <$50M annually
3. Stock price declines below $135 (11.5x FY2026E EPS) without fundamental deterioration — creating genuine valuation asymmetry

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026E EPS consensus falls below $11.00 (indicating structural margin compression)
2. Net new asset growth decelerates below 3% for two consecutive quarters
3. $100B asset threshold crossed with AOCI unrealized losses >$500M, creating capital ratio pressure

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next fiscal Q3 earnings (July 2026) will provide data on NII trajectory, NNA growth sustainability, and compensation ratio direction. The SEC inquiry resolution timeline is uncertain but could materialize by calendar year-end 2026.

## Conclusion

Raymond James is a high-quality franchise (20%+ ROTCE, strong capital, diversified revenue) trading at a reasonable but not compelling valuation (12.8x forward earnings). The core business — growing fee-based advisory revenue on expanding client assets — is fundamentally healthy. However, the earnings trajectory is mixed: revenue growth is strong but doesn't convert efficiently to bottom-line growth due to rising compensation costs and declining NII. Estimate revisions are negative across all periods. The approaching $100B regulatory threshold and unresolved cash sweep litigation add near-term uncertainty without obvious positive catalysts to offset them.

The prior trajectory decline from BUY (7.2) to HOLD (6.5) reflected the emerging headwinds. Today's data — continued negative estimate revisions, uneven earnings beats, and stock price 14% below the 52-week high without visible catalysts for recovery — confirms the HOLD stance. This is a stock to own when estimate momentum turns positive or when the price creates genuine margin of safety below $135.
