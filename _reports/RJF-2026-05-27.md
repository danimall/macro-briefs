**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Raymond James Financial is a conservatively managed, diversified financial services firm with $1.67 trillion in client assets under administration, generating $15.6B in annual revenue and $2.25B in free cash flow. The stock trades at 12.8x forward FY2026 EPS — a 30% discount to its peer median P/E of 18.2x — while delivering 17.7% return on common equity and maintaining capital ratios (Tier 1 leverage 13.1%, total capital 24.1%) well above regulatory minimums.

The discount exists for identifiable reasons: declining net interest income as rate cuts compress spreads on $52B of bank loans and the RJBDP sweep program, SEC enforcement inquiry into cash sweep practices (with three active class actions), a compensation ratio rising toward 65% as revenue mix shifts away from low-comp NII, and decelerating organic net new asset growth (3.8% vs. 5.5% prior year). Estimates are moving lower — current-year EPS revised down 3.0% over 30 days, next year down 2.5%.

The 30% P/E discount to peers is real, but closing it requires either rate stabilization (which reverses NII headwinds and eases compensation ratio pressure) or sustained IB/advisory fee momentum strong enough to offset the NII drag. The Q2 FY2026 earnings beat (+13.4% revenue growth, record quarterly revenues) demonstrates the offset mechanism works, but the estimate revision trend is still negative. This is a high-quality franchise trading at a discount for genuine near-term reasons rather than a mispricing where the market is clearly wrong about something specific.

## Company Overview

Raymond James operates five segments: Private Client Group (PCG, ~8,943 financial advisors, $1.67T AUA), Capital Markets (investment banking, institutional sales/trading), Asset Management ($274.9B financial AUM), Bank ($52B in loans across two bank subsidiaries), and Other (corporate, private equity). The firm differentiates through its hybrid model — offering employee, independent contractor, and custodial/RIA channels — combined with an integrated bank providing securities-based lending. The company is headquartered in St. Petersburg, Florida, and carries A-/A3 credit ratings (stable outlook from all three agencies).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** FY2025 net revenues reached $14.07B (+10% YoY), with diluted EPS of $10.30 (+6% YoY). The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, ended March 2026) delivered record revenue of $3.86B (+13.4% YoY) and EPS of $2.83, beating consensus of $2.80 by 1%.

**Revenue Mix — The Core Dynamic:** The business is experiencing a meaningful mix shift:
- Asset management fees grew 14% (recurring, market-linked)
- Investment banking revenues surged 26% (M&A +20%, debt underwriting +57%)
- Combined NII + RJBDP third-party fees declined 4% ($2.63B vs. $2.74B)

This matters because NII revenue carries near-zero compensation costs (margin ~80%+), while advisory and brokerage revenues carry 60-75% compensation loads. The mix shift mechanically compresses pre-tax margins even as absolute revenue grows.

**Capital Position:** Total capital ratio of 24.1% provides substantial excess capital above the 10.5% well-capitalized threshold. Credit quality remains excellent: nonperforming loans at 0.36% of total, net charge-offs just 8 bps. The bank loan provision fell to $37M from $45M YoY.

**Cash Flow & Returns:** Operating cash flow of $2.43B, capex of $188M, yielding $2.25B FCF (23.4% margin). The firm repurchased 7.4M shares for $1.1B at an average of $148/share in FY2025, reducing diluted shares from 216.9M (FY2023) to 206.6M. Current trailing P/FCF is approximately 13.2x — attractive for a business of this quality.

**Balance Sheet Composition:** Securities-based lending ($19.8B, 38% of total loans) is the fastest-growing and lowest-risk asset category, collateralized by marketable securities with daily LTV monitoring and near-zero charge-offs. CRE exposure at ~15% of total loans (with office at 5% of corporate loans) is manageable given geographic diversification and low observed charge-offs (11 bps).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Headwinds (H2 FY2026):** Management explicitly guided that NII + RJBDP third-party fees will decline further if rates continue lower. The sensitivity: a 100 bps rate decline reduces Bank NII by approximately $74M. With the current macro environment (potential rate hikes rather than cuts), this headwind may actually stabilize or reverse — but estimate revisions haven't yet incorporated this scenario.

**Structural Growth Drivers:**
1. Fee-based AUA now 60.5% of total (up from 56.9% in FY2023), providing annuity-like market-linked revenue
2. Investment banking recovery from FY2023 trough ($613M → $1.04B in FY2025) with further runway if M&A activity continues improving
3. Securities-based lending compound growth (average SBL grew from $15B to $17.7B in one year, now $19.8B on-balance-sheet)
4. Advisor recruiting/retention maintaining network effects

**Deceleration Signal:** Net new asset growth at 3.8% (FY2025) vs. 5.5% (FY2024) vs. 7.7% (FY2023) represents a clear deceleration, though management commentary on Q2 FY2026 emphasized "robust recruiting." The departure of one large independent contractor branch contributed, but competitive intensity from LPL, PE-backed RIA aggregators, and wirehouse payouts is structural.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-quarter EPS estimates declined from $3.01 to $2.92 over 30 days (-3.0%), and next-year estimates from $13.73 to $13.38 (-2.5%). This negative revision momentum is the primary quantitative signal preventing a more constructive stance. The 90-day revisions are also negative, suggesting a sustained trend rather than noise.

## Valuation Assessment

At $152.13, RJF trades at:
- 12.8x forward FY2026 EPS ($11.87)
- 11.4x forward FY2027 EPS ($13.38)
- ~1.2x tangible book value (estimated ~$125 per share)
- 14.8x trailing EPS ($10.30)
- 13.2x trailing FCF

Against peer medians of 18.2x P/E (GS, MS, SCHW), the discount is 30%. This is wider than the historical 10-15% discount typically assigned to RJF's smaller scale and lower IB contribution. However, the peers also face rate sensitivity (Schwab's bank NII similarly under pressure), so the discount may partly reflect RJF-specific factors: the cash sweep litigation, compensation ratio trajectory, and NNA deceleration.

The analyst consensus target of $174.77 (median $172) implies 15% upside from current levels. The low target of $147 is only 3% below the current price, while the high target of $198 implies 30% upside.

**Why the market may be right (partially):** At 12.8x forward earnings, the market is pricing in continued estimate revisions downward. If NII headwinds intensify (rates cut further) and compensation ratio pressure extends (to 66%+), EPS could compress toward $11 — putting the stock at ~14x normalized earnings, not a clear discount.

**Why the market may be wrong (partially):** If rates stabilize or rise (the current macro lean toward potential hikes), NII headwinds reverse, compensation ratio pressure eases, and the IB recovery continues, forward EPS could reach $14+ — putting the stock at less than 11x, genuinely cheap for a 17.7% ROCE franchise.

## Competitive Landscape

Raymond James occupies a defensible niche as the largest independent broker-dealer, positioned between wirehouses (Morgan Stanley, Merrill) and pure-play platforms (LPL). Its integrated model (advisor network + bank + capital markets + asset management) creates synergies competitors cannot easily replicate:

- The bank provides SBL directly to advisor clients, generating $19.8B in near-zero-loss loans
- The RJBDP sweep program provides low-cost deposit funding
- Capital markets provide IPO/secondary allocations to advisors' clients
- Asset management provides managed solutions within the advisory platform

**Competitive threats:** LPL's aggressive recruiting, PE-backed RIA aggregators, and fintech entrants all pressure the independent channel. However, RJF's conservative culture, succession planning, and multi-channel flexibility (employee, IC, and custodial) provide differentiation that manifests in above-average advisor retention.

The fee-based revenue shift (60.5% of AUA now in advisory accounts) aligns with industry trends toward recurring revenue models, though average fee rates face compression pressure from passive investment adoption and competitive pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MATERIAL):** The combined NII + RJBDP revenue ($2.63B, ~19% of net revenues) is directly sensitive to rate levels. In a rate-cut scenario, this revenue declines 3-5% per year; in a rate-hike scenario, it increases. The current macro debate about potential hikes vs. cuts creates genuine uncertainty here.

**2. Cash Sweep Litigation/Regulatory Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Three class actions + SEC inquiry into industry-wide sweep practices. Settlement exposure is difficult to quantify — LPL recently disclosed $50M+ in related costs. For RJF, reasonable estimate: $50-200M total resolution cost (2-9% of annual net income). The structural risk is larger: if sweep economics permanently compress, the annuity-like revenue from RJBDP fees could decline 10-30% over time.

**3. $100B Asset Threshold (2-3 year timeline):** At $88.2B growing 6%+ annually, RJF crosses the Category IV threshold around FY2028. Enhanced prudential standards would require capital planning, stress testing, and potentially AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital. The September 2025 $1.5B debt raise appears to pre-position for this.

**4. NNA Growth Deceleration (MODERATE):** If organic growth continues slowing toward 2-3%, the market valuation framework shifts from "growth compounder" to "mature financial" — warranting lower multiples.

**5. CRE/Credit Cycle Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** At 15% of loans (with office at 5% of corporate), losses to date are minimal. A severe credit downturn could generate $200-400M in additional provisions — material but not existential given $12.5B equity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-185, +15-22%):** Rate stabilization (or hikes) reverses NII headwinds, investment banking recovery accelerates as M&A pipelines convert, NNA growth re-accelerates to 5%+, and cash sweep litigation resolves at manageable cost (<$100M). EPS reaches $13-14 in FY2027, and the multiple re-rates to 13-14x as rate concerns dissipate. Share buybacks at $1B+/year provide floor support.

**Bear Case ($125-135, -11-18%):** Rates cut further, NII declines 8-10%, compensation ratio exceeds 66%, cash sweep settlements reach $200M+, and NNA growth stalls at 2%. EPS compresses to $10-10.50, multiple contracts to 12x on "mature financial" reclassification.

**Base Case ($165-172, +9-13%):** Rate environment stabilizes, IB revenue sustains but doesn't reaccelerate, NNA growth partially recovers to 4-4.5%, cash sweep litigation resolves at $75-125M. FY2027 EPS of $12.50-13.00 at 13x = $163-169.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive months (demonstrating NII headwind has stabilized)
2. NNA growth rate recovers above 5% in consecutive quarterly reports
3. Stock declines below $139 (52-week low area) on non-fundamental market stress, providing 10%+ discount to even bear-case valuation

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Cash sweep litigation results in settlement/ruling exceeding $300M or structural requirement to raise client sweep rates by 100bps+
2. Compensation ratio exceeds 67% for two consecutive quarters with no offsetting revenue acceleration
3. Q3 FY2026 earnings miss consensus by >5% AND management lowers full-year guidance

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (Q3 FY2026, likely late July 2026) is the natural catalyst. June FOMC (June 16-17) will provide rate path clarity that directly impacts the NII outlook.

## Conclusion

Raymond James is a well-run financial services franchise trading at a genuine discount to peers (12.8x vs. 18.2x median). The business generates attractive returns (17.7% ROCE, $2.25B FCF), maintains conservative capital levels, and has structural competitive advantages through its integrated advisor-bank-capital markets platform.

The discount exists for specific, measurable reasons: negative estimate revision momentum, rate-sensitive revenue headwinds, cash sweep litigation overhang, and decelerating organic growth. Closing this discount requires external catalyst confirmation — either rate stabilization, litigation resolution, or sustained IB/NNA acceleration. None of these are imminent or guaranteed.

The specific blockers preventing a BUY rating at a 6.5 overall score: (1) negative estimate revisions in both current and next year across 30-day and 90-day windows, (2) a 2-for-4 earnings beat/miss record suggesting inconsistent execution visibility, and (3) zero insider buying despite the stock trading near buyback execution levels ($148 avg). These three factors together indicate that while the valuation looks attractive on paper, the fundamental trajectory has not yet confirmed a turning point. The prior BUY recommendation (February at $157) has seen the stock decline 3% and estimates decline further — the thesis was premature, not wrong, but the timing evidence says "wait for confirmation." HOLD with growth thesis intact; upgrade on estimate stabilization or further price weakness to $138.
