**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rayonier is now one of the largest publicly traded timberland owners in the United States following its merger of equals with PotlatchDeltic, which closed January 30, 2026. The combined company holds roughly 4.1 million acres of timberland (3.2M in the U.S. South, ~930K in the Northwest), six sawmills and a plywood mill, and a real-estate/land-based-solutions layer spanning HBU land sales, solar leases, and carbon capture. The transaction doubled the share count (from ~161M to ~301M) and added a new business line, commodity lumber and plywood manufacturing, that carries a different risk profile than the legacy timber/land REIT. Headline GAAP results are distorted right now: Q1 2026 showed a $12.5M net loss driven by $70.4M of one-time merger costs partly offset by a $40.3M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release. The cleaner read is pro-forma full-quarter net income of $47.9M ($0.16 EPS) and Adjusted EBITDA of $94.1M.

The investment tension is straightforward. The stock trades below a reasonable sum-of-the-parts estimate of its timberland and mill assets, and the balance sheet is genuinely strong: 28% debt-to-capital, 7.4x interest coverage, and 100% of $1.61B in variable-rate debt swapped to fixed at a blended effective rate near 2.3% after Farm Credit patronage. Against that, Q1 Adjusted CAD of $62.7M annualizes below the ~$316M dividend obligation, meaning the ~4.8% yield currently leans on lumpy Real Estate EBITDA ($46.2M in Q1) and expected synergies rather than steady timber cash flow. Southern pine pulpwood remains structurally soft on paper-mill closures, and lumber pricing was moderating by mid-April even as tariffs support the domestic market.

We rate RYN a HOLD on an income basis. The below-NAV valuation and strong balance sheet limit downside, but per-share growth is capped by dilution and dividend coverage that requires either synergy delivery or firmer timber/lumber pricing to become comfortable. Consensus EPS estimates have been mixed over the past 30 days: the current quarter edged up slightly while next-quarter and full-year estimates drifted marginally lower, there is no open-market insider buying to lean on, and integration of a business whose contributed assets represent 59% of the total is unproven. Prior reports have held at HOLD three times; nothing in the current data warrants changing that, though the fundamentals have stabilized rather than deteriorated.

## Company Overview

Rayonier operates as a "land resources" REIT structured as an UPREIT (assets held in Rayonier, L.P., with RYN as sole general partner at a 99.4% interest). Post-merger, it runs four segments:

- **Southern Timber** — the largest EBITDA contributor (~$45.5M in Q1'26), harvesting pine sawtimber and pulpwood across eight Southern states.
- **Northwest Timber** — renamed from Pacific Northwest to reflect the added Idaho acreage; a meaningful portion of Idaho sawlogs is indexed to lumber prices on a roughly four-week lag, importing direct lumber-price exposure into what was formerly a pure stumpage business.
- **Wood Products** (new, from PotlatchDeltic) — six sawmills plus an industrial plywood mill (AR, ID, MI, MN), with 1,155 MMBF of lumber and 150 MMSF of plywood capacity.
- **Real Estate** — HBU land sales, rural land, master-planned developments (Wildlight/FL, Heartwood/GA, Chenal Valley/AR), plus land-based solutions (carbon capture, solar leases).

The competitive foundation is scale, geographic diversification across the two most productive U.S. timber regions, vertical integration (own logs feeding own mills), a growing HBU/real-estate optionality layer, and low-cost Farm Credit System debt with patronage refunds. The company is relocating its headquarters to Buckhead, Atlanta (lease signed June 2026), consolidating legacy Rayonier (Wildlight, FL) and PotlatchDeltic (Spokane, WA) operations.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 headline vs. underlying.** Revenue was $276.8M against $82.9M in Q1'25, reflecting the addition of Wood Products (+$108.5M) and expanded timber/real estate volumes. The GAAP net loss is not a useful gauge of earnings power — it embeds the $70.4M of merger/integration costs and a $1.2M inventory step-up charge, partly offset by the $40.3M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release tied to expected utilization of TRS NOLs. Adjusted EBITDA of $94.1M (vs. $27.1M) is the cleaner run-rate. Most representative is the pro-forma full-quarter combined view (as if the merger had closed 1/1/25): revenue of $350.2M.

**Segment Adjusted EBITDA (Q1'26):** Southern Timber $45.5M, Real Estate $46.2M, Northwest Timber $8.6M, Wood Products $6.8M, Corporate ($11.8M). Real Estate was the standout, driven by 7,695 acres sold including a 2,226-acre solar-developer sale at $10,100/acre. This lumpy Real Estate line is not a stable base against which to size a fixed dividend.

**Balance sheet and capital structure (3/31/26).** Cash of $681.7M; total debt of $2.06B (up from $1.05B on ~$1.04B of assumed PotlatchDeltic term loans); total assets of $7.75B; equity of $5.33B. Net debt/enterprise value is 18%, conservative for the sector. Covenant headroom is large: EBITDA/interest at 7.4x (minimum 2.5x) and debt/(net worth + debt) at 30% (maximum 65%).

Interest-rate risk has been effectively neutralized. All variable-rate debt is 100% swapped to fixed, and the weighted-average effective rate is roughly 2.3% after swaps and Farm Credit patronage ($14.8M received in Q1). A $450M tranche of 2.75% Senior Notes matures in 2031. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a real and under-appreciated cost advantage versus peers who face rising financing costs, and it directly supports the Financial Health score.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1 CAD was $90.2M. The quarterly dividend of $0.26/share annualizes to roughly $316M. The company repurchased 1.48M shares in Q1 at an average $20.98 (below current NAV estimates and near current price), with $198.4M remaining on the $300M authorization. Capex is guided to $104–108M for 2026. The $200M 2016 term loan was repaid at April 2026 maturity with cash on hand.

On the Altman Z-Score, a distress-zone reading is a false positive for RYN. The model penalizes asset-heavy land holders because timberland is carried at historical/stepped-up cost far below market value, and REITs distribute most earnings. The reality is investment-grade credit, minimal leverage, and rich asset backing.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is more asset-transformation than organic acceleration. The merger created scale, diversification, and a manufacturing profit pool, but it also doubled the share count, so per-share metrics grow slowly. The 2026 operating outlook is mixed: Southern harvest of 12.1–12.6M tons with pricing expected lower than legacy Rayonier on geographic mix; Northwest harvest of 2.0–2.3M tons with pricing expected higher on the Idaho contribution; and Wood Products at roughly 1.1B board feet for eleven months. The real-estate pipeline is characterized as strong.

The earnings-surprise record is good: three beats and one in-line over the trailing four reported quarters, including a large Q1'26 beat that reflects merger accounting more than operating momentum. The estimate-revision picture is the more decisive forward signal, and it is soft. Current-quarter estimates were revised down 7.9% over 90 days (though up 4.2% over the last 30 days), and next-quarter and next-year estimates edged lower over the last 30 days. The current-year and next-year figures did rise 13.0% and 4.2% respectively over 90 days, but that reflects merger consolidation lifting the absolute earnings base rather than an underlying acceleration. This is not the >30% upward revision velocity that flags a genuine trough-earnings turn; the near-term trend is flat-to-negative.

Land-based solutions (solar leases, carbon capture) and HBU development provide a higher-margin, less-cyclical optionality layer that should compound over time, and synergy realization from the merger is a real earnings lever if management executes. Neither is a near-term growth catalyst that reprices the stock, and the growth thesis is capped by dilution and by soft timber/lumber prices in the core segments.

## Valuation Assessment

RYN trades at $21.58, roughly 21% below its 52-week high of $27.34 and near the low end of its range. Trailing P/E of ~62x is not a meaningful metric here — GAAP EPS is distorted by merger accounting, and timber REITs are properly valued on NAV, CAD/FFO yield, and EBITDA multiples rather than P/E. The key valuation anchor is that the stock trades below a reasonable sum-of-the-parts estimate of timberland, mills, and development land. Timberland carried far below market value on the balance sheet, combined with the below-NAV market price, supports the downside.

Against the peer set, the P/E comparison is not informative given the sector heterogeneity (peer medians of 24.1x P/E and 3.1% yield span REITs with very different asset bases and no true timber comps in this list). More useful: RYN's ~4.8% dividend yield sits above the ~3.1% peer median, which reflects both the below-NAV price and the market's discount for tight coverage. Analyst targets bracket the current price with a low of $22.00 (essentially spot), a median of $24.50, and a high of $32.00, implying modest consensus upside to roughly $24–25 with an asset-value tail toward the low $30s if timber/lumber pricing firms or synergies surprise.

We assign valuation a middling score. The below-NAV setup and yield support argue against a value-trap penalty, but the absence of a dated catalyst, the tight dividend coverage, and the fact that a low headline multiple here is partly structural (asset-heavy, distribution-mandated REIT) keep this from being an asymmetric-upside call. The market is discounting the stock for coverage and integration uncertainty, and cheapness alone, absent a catalyst, does not merit a high valuation score. The current price offers a reasonable entry for an income-oriented holder given the asset backing.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger creates one of the largest publicly traded timberland owners in the U.S., competing directly with Weyerhaeuser and other timber REITs. Vertical integration (own logs feeding own mills through intersegment sales) is a scale advantage, but it also imports commodity lumber-manufacturing cyclicality that the legacy REIT did not carry. The geographic diversification across the U.S. South and Northwest spans the two most productive domestic timber regions and smooths regional pricing swings; Idaho's lumber-indexed sawlogs give the Northwest segment direct lumber-price upside when the market is firm.

Near-term industry conditions are two-sided. Lumber prices were supported in early 2026 by the 10% Section 232 duty (effective October 2025), elevated Canadian duties, capacity curtailments, and a weaker dollar, a favorable backdrop for the new Wood Products segment, though management flagged moderation in some products by mid-April. Southern pulpwood pricing remains structurally soft due to paper and containerboard mill closures that have reduced regional demand. The real-estate and land-based-solutions optionality (solar, carbon capture, HBU) is a differentiator that adds a higher-margin, less-cyclical value layer and positions the company for structural land-monetization trends. Competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on scale, offset by the cyclical manufacturing exposure just added.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration execution.** PotlatchDeltic represents 59% of total assets, and its internal controls are being integrated with the possibility of exclusion from ICFR assessment through FY2026. Realizing projected cost synergies, retaining key personnel, and maintaining the investment-grade rating through a materially larger and more complex organization is the dominant near-term risk. The dual transformation of exiting New Zealand (77% JV interest sold for $710M in June 2025) and doubling the share count via merger within roughly seven months makes historical financials essentially non-comparable and elevates comparability/execution risk.

**Dividend coverage.** Adjusted CAD annualizes below the dividend, and coverage currently leans on lumpy Real Estate EBITDA. Management explicitly reserves discretion over the dividend. This is the central watch item; a soft real-estate quarter combined with weak timber pricing could pressure coverage and force either a synergy-dependent bridge or a payout reassessment.

**New commodity exposure.** Wood Products is capital-intensive, high-fixed-cost, and price-volatile, subject to substitute products and cyclical housing/repair-remodel demand. This shifts the company's risk profile from a stumpage-and-land REIT to one with meaningful manufacturing beta.

**Policy and pricing.** Executive Orders 14223 and 14225 aim to expand federal timber harvesting, particularly in Idaho/the West, which could depress regional prices if implemented at scale. The AR7 softwood-lumber duty review (final in H2'26) could lower Canadian duties and pressure domestic prices, partly reversing the current tariff support.

**Other.** An assumed underfunded PotlatchDeltic pension (qualified plan 90.9% funded — $181M assets vs. $197M PBO — plus $33.9M nonqualified PBO and $14M OPEB, with ~$11.8M of 2026 cash contributions); no insurance on standing timber (fire, hurricane, pest, disease); and Port Gamble, WA environmental remediation (~$8.9M, 15–20 year monitoring). REIT-qualification and prohibited-transactions tax risks are structural and well-managed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a mildly cautious signal. Near-term ATM IV of 27.5% is roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 26.6% (fair), but the put/call skew is elevated at 5.9% and the put/call open-interest ratio has jumped from 0.51 to 2.56 over the past month, with a put/call volume ratio of 2.50. That is a meaningful shift toward downside hedging or bearish positioning. ATM IV also rose 5.0 points (22.5% → 27.5%) even as the price fell only 0.9%, indicating the market is pricing in more uncertainty. The term structure is in contango (near-term 27.5% below long-term 34.4%), which is normal, and short interest is modest at 4.3% of float (5.0 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish trade. For an income-oriented HOLD, the elevated put demand argues for patience rather than aggressive accumulation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($28–32).** Merger synergies land ahead of plan, lumber and Southern timber pricing firm on tariff support and a housing recovery, and Adjusted CAD comfortably re-covers the dividend. Land-based solutions (solar, carbon) and HBU sales accelerate, closing the below-NAV gap. Investment-grade rating holds, buybacks continue below NAV, and the market re-rates the combined entity toward sum-of-the-parts. The 2.3% effective debt cost becomes a durable competitive edge as peers refinance at higher rates.

**Bear Case ($17–18).** Integration proves messier than guided, synergies slip, and lumber prices moderate (AR7 lowers Canadian duties) while Southern pulpwood stays soft on mill closures. Real Estate has a lumpy weak year, Adjusted CAD undershoots, and the dividend is reassessed. Federal timber-supply expansion pressures Northwest pricing. The stock derates toward a coverage-adjusted yield in the low $17s, an ~18% decline from current levels.

**Base Case (~$24).** The company executes a competent but unspectacular integration, timber/lumber pricing stays range-bound, and the dividend is maintained but tightly covered, bridged by synergy delivery and real-estate cadence. The stock drifts toward consensus median of ~$24.50 over 12 months, delivering the ~4.8% yield plus modest capital appreciation as coverage stabilizes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (first full combined quarter, key for synergy and coverage evidence), the H2'26 AR7 duty review outcome, and progress on the investment-grade rating and integration milestones.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted CAD demonstrably re-covering the annualized dividend (payout ratio moving below ~90% on a normalized basis) across two consecutive quarters, reducing reliance on lumpy Real Estate EBITDA.
- Quantified synergy realization confirmed on schedule combined with firming Northwest/lumber pricing, and positive estimate-revision velocity (rather than the current flat-to-negative 30-day trend).
- Open-market insider buying by the CEO or CFO at or below current prices — currently absent, and its appearance would be a meaningful conviction signal given the below-NAV setup.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or explicit signal of one, or Adjusted CAD deteriorating below ~$55M/quarter with no offsetting real-estate pipeline.
- Loss of the investment-grade rating or evidence of material integration failure (synergy shortfall, key-personnel departures, ICFR deficiencies surfacing).
- A structural pricing break: AR7 materially lowering Canadian duties plus federal timber-supply expansion depressing Northwest realizations.

## Conclusion

Rayonier is a below-NAV income holding backed by 4.1 million acres of timberland, a conservatively levered and fully rate-hedged balance sheet, and a manufacturing/real-estate profit layer that adds diversification. The strengths are real: 28% debt-to-capital, 7.4x interest coverage, and a ~2.3% effective debt cost that few peers can match in the current rate environment, all supporting a Financial Health score that has improved materially over the past year. The offsetting reality is a ~4.8% dividend only tightly covered by adjusted cash flow, unproven integration of a business whose contributed assets represent 59% of the total, soft Southern pulpwood and moderating lumber pricing, and mixed consensus estimate revisions. There is no open-market insider buying to lean on, and the options market has tilted toward downside hedging.

The score trajectory (4.8 → 5.9 → 5.8) reflects genuine balance-sheet and risk improvement, and the current data confirms stabilization rather than deterioration; the recent price weakness is not accompanied by fundamental decline. Per-share growth is capped by dilution and by dividend coverage that needs synergy delivery or firmer pricing to become comfortable. We maintain HOLD on an income basis. The asset backing and yield limit downside; the absence of a dated catalyst and tight coverage limit upside. Own it for the yield and the below-NAV optionality, and reassess after Q2 gives the first clean look at the combined company's cash generation.
