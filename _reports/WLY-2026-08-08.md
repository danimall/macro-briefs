**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wiley closed fiscal 2026 (year ended April 30, 2026) with flat revenue of $1,676.5M, adjusted operating income up 18% to $296.2M, adjusted EBITDA margin up 220bp to 26.2%, operating cash flow up 29% to $260.5M, and free cash flow less product development up 55% to $195.3M. Management guided FY2027 to adjusted EPS of $4.60-$5.05 (from $4.19), an adjusted EBITDA margin of 26.5%-27.5%, and free cash flow of $205M. On the total share base of approximately 51.0M (Class A plus Class B), the stock at $52.33 trades at 10.8x the FY2027 adjusted EPS midpoint of $4.825, roughly 7.7x EV/FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of about $487M, and a 7.7% yield on the guided free-cash-flow figure. The supplied publishing peer set carries a median P/E of 31.8x.

The gap is real, and so are its causes. Consolidated revenue was flat as reported and down 1% at constant currency; the only growth is in Research (+4% cc), while Learning — a third of revenue — declined 7% cc with both Academic and Professional shrinking. Margin gains came predominantly from corporate cost removal: of the $42.0M increase in adjusted EBITDA, $23.0M came from reducing the unallocated Corporate drag. GAAP diluted EPS of $4.16 is not comparable to the prior year — a valuation-allowance release contributed approximately $1.16 of the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge, so normalized GAAP EPS is closer to $3.00 and the quoted 12.9x trailing multiple (which the data feed also reports as the "forward" multiple, indicating it is not an estimate-based figure) overstates cheapness on a reported basis.

Two dated items constrain the rating. First, the $1.115B revolver and $200M term loan A both mature in November 2027, and the June 1, 2026 Emerald acquisition (£337.5M / ~$452M, funded with cash and revolver) pushed estimated pro forma net debt to roughly $1,060M against $608M at year-end — a refinancing of nearly the whole capital structure must be executed within about 15 months. Second, the FY2026 10-K carries an elevated risk factor on reductions in US federal research funding, and a second consecutive federal budget proposal targets deep science-agency cuts; Research growth depends on article volume and library/consortium budgets funded by exactly those grants. At $52.33 — 8.9% below the July 29 high of $57.45 and 84% above the February low of $28.38 — the base case of $61 leaves roughly 17% upside against 24% downside. That supports holding, not adding.

## Company Overview

Wiley is a scholarly and professional publisher with 85% of FY2026 revenue from digital products and 48% described by the company as recurring. Approximately 49% of revenue is earned outside the US; the currency mix is roughly 51% USD, 29% GBP, 12% EUR. Headcount is about 4,500. The multi-year divestiture program (University Services, Wiley Edge, CrossKnowledge) is complete — the Held-for-Sale segment reported zero revenue in FY2026 against $17.4M in FY2025.

**Research: $1,129.9M (67% of revenue), 33.2% adjusted EBITDA margin, +5% reported / +4% cc.** Over 1,900 journals across four Research Publishing revenue models (subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access, licensing) plus Research Solutions ($164.2M) covering advertising, pharma knowledge hubs, recruitment platforms, spectral databases and the Atypon hosting platform, which serves roughly 2,000 publishers and societies. Research is 96% digital. A structural feature deserves emphasis: approximately 46% of Journal Subscriptions revenue derives from publication rights owned by professional societies and partners (American Cancer Society, American Heart Association, American Geophysical Union, German Chemical Society among them) and published under long-term contract.

**Learning: $546.6M (33%), 38.0% adjusted EBITDA margin, -7% reported and cc.** Academic ($318.8M: textbooks, WileyPLUS, zyBooks) fell 4%; Professional ($227.8M: Dummies, Sybex, Everything DiSC assessments) fell 9%. Learning is 62% digital. Wiley owns no printing facilities and has outsourced US book distribution to Cengage.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality.** FY2026 operating income rose $55.5M (+25%) to $276.9M on flat revenue — genuine operating improvement. GAAP net income of $221.6M and diluted EPS of $4.16 are a different matter: the GAAP effective tax rate was -3.0% (a $6.5M net benefit on $215.1M pretax) versus +41.1% in FY2025, driven by a roughly $70.0M reduction in the US valuation allowance, of which $58.3M flowed only through the GAAP provision. The company's own reconciliation shows tax items contributing -$1.16 to the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge. Adjusted EPS was $4.19 on a 24.4% adjusted tax rate. Any screen keyed to GAAP net income reads FY2026 as a step-change that operations only partly justify.

**Where the margin came from.** Operating and administrative expense fell to $895.9M from $947.4M (-5% reported, -7% cc). Adjusted unallocated Corporate expense fell 15% (16% cc) to $152.8M from $179.9M, and 22% cc in Q4 alone. Cost of sales was flat at $431.5M and rose about 1% cc ex-divestitures, attributed partly to royalties on AI licence revenue derived from third-party content. Segment tables imply roughly $428M of total shared-service cost (allocated $167.6M Research, $107.9M Learning, plus $152.8M unallocated) — about 25.5% of revenue. That remaining pool is why the FY2027 margin guide of 26.5%-27.5% is plausible; the Global Restructuring Program was expanded again in Q1 FY2027.

The same fact is a caution. Cumulative restructuring charges reached $156.0M through January 31, 2026 against a roughly $120M annualized savings target (~$110M realized in FY2026) — a 1.3-year payback, economically sound, but the program has been initiated, expanded, extended and expanded again across FY2023 through FY2027 while $19.2M of FY2026 charges ($0.27 of adjusted EPS) sat outside adjusted results. Four consecutive years of "one-time" costs is closer to a recurring cost of doing business.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $260.5M (+29%) against capex of $51.2M and product development of $14.0M produced $195.3M of free cash flow less product development, +55%. Receivables rose 6.9% to $244.2M on flat revenue, consistent with management's comment about late renewal signings; Q4-based DSO deteriorated modestly from 47.0 to 49.6 days. Note the seasonality the 10-K discloses: operating cash flow is a use of cash in the first half because journal and transformational-agreement collections arrive at the start of H2. Nine-month FY2026 OCF was $103.3M, meaning roughly 60% of the year's cash landed in Q4. No single quarter here should be annualized.

**Balance sheet.** Equity of $848.2M on total assets of $2,591.8M; cash of $75.6M with approximately 96% held outside the US. Debt fell $120.3M to about $683.7M; net debt of $608M and 1.4x net leverage improved from $714M and 1.8x, aided by the $119.5M University Services settlement received in June 2025. Interest expense fell to $43.8M from $52.5M; the weighted-average rate was 5.27% at January 31, 2026, with $450M of notional swaps and roughly $357.8M of unhedged floating debt (a 1% move is ~$2.7M). Working capital is negative $359.3M (current ratio 0.54) because $451.4M of contract liabilities sit in current liabilities — float, not distress, for a prepaid-subscription publisher. A screen-derived Altman Z of 0.83 is not credible: recomputing from FY2026 inputs gives approximately 2.5-2.7, the grey zone, and even that penalizes the deferred-revenue structure.

Two balance-sheet items matter more than the leverage level. Goodwill ($1,132.4M) plus intangibles ($579.0M) equal 66% of assets against $848.2M of equity, leaving tangible book value of roughly -$863M; FY2024 recorded $108.4M of goodwill impairments, so the history is not clean, and Emerald adds materially to both lines. And the maturity concentration: $671.2M of the year-end $683.7M falls due in years two to three, with both facilities maturing November 2027. A May 15, 2026 amendment added $300M of incremental term commitments, taking total available lines to $1,590.5M, with roughly $606.9M unused at year-end. Coverage is comfortable — approximately 7.5x on FY2027E EBITDA of $487M against estimated pro forma interest near $65M — so this is a refinancing-pricing question rather than a solvency question.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026 returned a record $174M: $74.4M of dividends and $100.1M of buybacks. Repurchases averaged $35.08 (including 1,094,535 shares in Q3 FY2026 at an average of $32.00), versus $44.16 in FY2025 — management bought into the decline, and those shares are marked roughly 49% higher today. Weighted-average basic shares fell 2.9% to 52.466M. The dividend was raised to $1.42 annualized, a 32nd consecutive increase, costing roughly $72M — about 35% of the FY2027 FCF guide. $207.4M of the $250M authorization remains, but FY2027 FCF of $205M less $72M of dividends leaves roughly $133M for buybacks and deleveraging combined ahead of the refinancing.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is earnings growth on a flat revenue base. FY2027 organic revenue guidance is low-to-mid single digit (Research mid-single digit), with Emerald adding $78M over eleven months — roughly 4.7% of inorganic revenue. Decomposing the adjusted EPS guide: the $0.635 midpoint increase over FY2026's $4.19 includes only about $0.10 from Emerald, with the balance from incremental organic EBITDA plus continued overhead removal plus a lower share count, net of higher interest and an adjusted tax rate around 24-25%. The guide is achievable primarily because the cost base is still fat, not because demand is inflecting.

Research is the genuine engine. Open-access article output grew approximately 25% year over year (28% in Q3 FY2026), and gold open access was cited as the principal driver of Research Publishing growth. The industry's migration from pay-to-read to pay-to-publish makes Wiley a volume beneficiary, and the 2024 NIH Public Access Policy effective July 1, 2025 plausibly accelerates conversion of formerly free subscription-route publication into paid APC transactions. That mechanism is externally reported rather than company-confirmed and cuts both ways: higher APC costs against flat-to-lower grant budgets squeeze the same customer, and author/reviewer backlash plus the possibility of funder caps on allowable publication costs would compress APC pricing directly.

Learning is the drag and shows no stabilization: -7% cc for the year, -7% cc in Q4, with Academic -5% cc and Professional -10% cc. Contributing factors cited include retail channel softness (specifically inventory reductions at an online retailer in Q3), weaker consumer and corporate spending, and lower AI licensing. McGraw Hill reported in the same week that most of its gains came from digital products and student AI-tool usage, which suggests part of the courseware decline is Wiley-specific rather than uniformly sectoral.

**AI licensing needs to be read carefully.** FY2026 AI licence revenue was $49.1M, up 23% from $40.0M, with lifetime AI revenue above $110M. But $19.4M of the FY2026 figure relates to content Wiley licenses from other publishers, on which it pays a royalty — an agency-style pass-through with materially lower margin. Own-content AI revenue is therefore approximately $29.7M, or 1.8% of consolidated revenue. By segment, Research AI licensing rose to $33.1M from roughly $11M while Learning fell to $16.0M from roughly $29M. Deriving Q4 as full-year minus nine-month figures gives roughly $6.8M against roughly $10M in the prior-year Q4, a decline of about 32%. Prior-year comparatives are disclosed only as approximations, so this carries ±$1M of imprecision, and AI licensing is contractually lumpy — a single large non-repeating FY2025 Learning deal produces exactly this pattern without demand deterioration. Management's disclosure of 19 corporate customers by vertical plus four LLM developers is consistent with a mix shift from one-time training licences toward recurring application licences. This is the single most testable item over the next two prints.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive — current year $4.80 (+1.1% over 90 days), next year $5.30 (+3.9%) — but from a single contributing analyst. That is directional confirmation, not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. The earnings track record is adequate rather than strong: two beats, one in-line and one miss over four quarters, with fiscal Q4 landing exactly on the $1.67 estimate.

Including approximately 8.8M Class B shares (Q4 FY2026 weighted-average basic shares were 51,109K), total equity value is approximately $2.67B — 21% higher, and the correct basis for enterprise-value and yield work.

Against the supplied peer median P/E of 31.8x (IAC 24.5x, SCHL 18.0x, NWS 38.2x, NWSA 33.4x, NYT 31.8x), and against the larger STM comparables RELX, Informa and Wolters Kluwer, Wiley is cheap by any framing. The question is whether the discount is earned.

Much of it is. Consolidated revenue is flat, a third of revenue is in secular decline, margin expansion is cost-led with finite runway, tangible book is negative $863M, leverage roughly doubled to an estimated 2.2-2.4x into a November 2027 maturity wall, and 46% of Journal Subscriptions revenue rests on rights the company does not own. The company's own proxy discloses five-year TSR of 137.1 against a self-selected peer group at 205.4, with the gap widening every year since FY2022. That is the record of a business the market has repeatedly declined to re-rate.

Some of the discount looks excessive. A 48%-recurring, 85%-digital publisher generating a 7.7% free-cash-flow yield with roughly 7.5x interest coverage and a 32-year dividend growth record does not belong at 10.8x forward earnings if the FY2027 guide is delivered. Applying 12.5x to the $4.825 midpoint gives $60.3; cross-checking at 8.5x EV/EBITDA on $487M less $1,060M of net debt over 51.0M shares gives $60.4. I set the base case at $61, which is also 11.5x the single-analyst FY2028 estimate of $5.30. The bull case at 14x the $5.05 upper end, or 9.5x EV/EBITDA, gives $71. The bear case — adjusted EPS of $4.40 on a worsening Learning decline or enacted federal science cuts, at 9x, cross-checked at 7.0x EV/EBITDA — gives $40.

From $52.33 that is +16.6% to base, +35.7% to bull, -23.6% to bear. Weighting 55/20/25 gives a probability-weighted return near +10%. The lone published target of $68 implies 14.1x the FY2027 midpoint; with a single contributor, it is weak independent corroboration.

Two adjustments worth flagging. Emerald was acquired at roughly 5.3x annualized revenue ($452M against ~$85M), and reverse-engineering the guided $0.10 of accretion through estimated incremental interest implies something near 14-15x EV/EBITDA — roughly double the 7.7x at which Wiley's own equity trades. Every input except the $452M price and the $78M/$0.10 guidance is an estimate, but if directionally right, Wiley deployed 17% of its equity value at twice its own multiple for 2% of first-year accretion. Separately, the Anthropic copyright settlement ($1.5B fund, preliminary approval September 25, 2025) is genuine unrecognized optionality — Wiley confirms its content is among the pirated works and has not determined its share. It is not modelable and is excluded from all figures above.

## Competitive Landscape

The 2025 Clarivate Journal Citation Reports place Wiley in a top-three citation position with 8% of titles, 8% of articles and 10% of citations; 1,740 Wiley journals were included, ranking first in 17 categories with 269 top-ten category placements. That is credible third-party evidence of tier-one scale and simultaneously of sub-scale — Wiley is roughly a quarter to a third the size of Elsevier, with Springer Nature, Taylor & Francis and Wolters Kluwer also larger or comparable in segments. Emerald adds 480+ journals, 8,000 books and 3,000 business cases in economics, business, finance and social sciences.

Trajectory differs sharply by segment. Research is improving: mid-single-digit constant-currency growth, ~25% open-access output growth, Research AI licensing up roughly 3x, and control of the Atypon hosting platform and the ReX submission/peer-review system, which give Wiley infrastructure positions beyond its own content. In an environment where model developers need licensed, authoritative corpora, owning a large STM back catalogue is a durable asset — one of the few places where AI capex converts into third-party revenue rather than cost.

Learning is deteriorating: both lines shrinking, print retail contracting, used and rental substitution intact, and a competitor reporting share gains from digital and AI-enabled courseware in the same reporting week. Structurally, the 46% society-owned share of Journal Subscriptions revenue and the roughly 19% of consolidated revenue intermediated by subscription agents mean a meaningful portion of the franchise is contracted rather than owned. Net trajectory is modestly improving on mix, held back by a shrinking third and by scale disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Federal research funding.** The FY2026 10-K elevates this risk explicitly, noting proposed budget reductions, administrative actions on grants and litigation, while acknowledging Congress has not enacted the largest proposed cuts. A second consecutive federal budget proposal targets deep science-agency reductions. Both the APC-paying capacity behind open-access volume and institutional consortium budgets depend on that funding. This is the largest undiscounted fundamental risk, and the FY2027 appropriations outcome is the resolution point.

**November 2027 refinancing.** Nearly the entire drawn balance — now roughly $1,060M pro forma — matures with both facilities in November 2027. Coverage is adequate and $1,590.5M of lines exist, so this is about price. Every 100bp on $1.06B is roughly $10.6M pretax, about 3-4% of FY2027 net income.

**AI as engine and threat.** Wiley licenses content to model developers while the same content is ingested without licence; the 10-K warns AI could cannibalize existing products and complicates detection of unauthorized use. The majority of EU AI Act obligations apply from August 2026.

**Concentration.** Approximately 31% of articles published in calendar 2025 included China-based authors — a supply-side dependency invisible in a geographic revenue table. Subscription agents represent about 19% of revenue. Top-10 book customers are ~12% of revenue but ~20% of April receivables (~36% at January 31).

**Accounting and asset risk.** Goodwill and intangibles are 66% of assets against negative tangible book; FY2026 and FY2025 impairment tests were qualitative-only, following $108.4M of FY2024 impairments. Emerald purchase accounting is unread — no post-acquisition financial statements were available, so the pro forma net debt, leverage and interest figures here are constructions.

**Governance.** Five-year TSR one-third below the self-selected peer group; disclosed incentive measures are Adjusted Revenue, Adjusted Operating Income and Adjusted EBITDA, with no per-share, return-on-capital, cash-flow or TSR measure among them. For a company that has just deployed $452M on an acquisition at an estimated multiple twice its own, revenue-and-EBITDA-anchored incentives create at least a theoretical bias toward acquisitive scale; the plan design was not read in full, and these are standard sector metrics, so this is an observation rather than proof of misalignment. Offsetting: buybacks executed at a $35.08 average, a 32nd consecutive dividend increase, ~$110M of realized cost savings, and two credible external hires into the AI/Research build (from Microsoft/AWS/RELX and from Clarivate/Norstella).

**Divestiture consideration.** Three of four post-divestiture streams impaired: the Wiley Edge earnout written from $15.0M to zero, the CrossKnowledge earnout from $1.8M to zero, plus small receivable write-offs. The $15.2M Inspirit Seller Note was amended in November 2025 to cease interest accrual from January 5, 2026 in exchange for a contingent 120% make-whole on exit — a structure consistent with a borrower under strain, though also with a negotiated recapitalization ahead of a planned exit. Immaterial in size; informative about counterparty quality.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 48.7% against 36.0% trailing realized — rich by 12.8 points — with the term structure in backwardation (48.7% at 12 days, 51.2% at 40 days, 39.3% at 131 days). The 40-day tenor spans the likely early-September Q1 FY2027 report, the first with Emerald consolidated and the first post-acquisition balance sheet; a ±14.5% implied move to that expiry is consistent with a genuine two-sided event. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call volume 0.27, put/call open interest 0.05, skew reported as call-tilted. That absence of put demand does not corroborate the 12.6% short interest and 5.1 days to cover, which implies shorts are expressed in stock rather than options and leaves squeeze mechanics live on any upside surprise. Confidence in these readings is low — only 35 contracts exist across three expirations, so the volatility surface is thin and should not carry weight against fundamentals. The practical takeaway: the market is paying up for near-term optionality into the September print, which argues against paying for premium now and in favor of letting the print resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($71, +36%)

Research compounds at mid-single-digit constant currency on open-access volume growth near 25%, with Emerald adding scale and content for AI licensing. The remaining ~$428M shared-cost pool, plus the Q1 FY2027 program expansion, delivers the upper end of the 26.5%-27.5% margin band, producing FY2027 adjusted EPS at or above $5.05 and adjusted EBITDA near $496M. Learning stabilizes toward flat as the retail destocking annualizes. Own-content AI licence revenue reclassifies from lumpy one-time training deals toward disclosed recurring application licences, and the Anthropic claim delivers an unmodeled cash inflow. The November 2027 facilities are refinanced or extended at a rate at or below the current 5.27% weighted average, removing the maturity overhang. With flat-to-modest revenue growth, 27%+ margins and ~$220M+ of free cash flow, the multiple re-rates to 14x adjusted EPS — still a large discount to the 31.8x peer median and to RELX.

### Bear Case ($40, -24%)

Enacted FY2027 federal appropriations cut NIH and NSF budgets materially. Grant contraction reduces APC-paying capacity and library consortium budgets simultaneously, and Research constant-currency growth decelerates below 2%. Learning's decline persists at 6-8%, so consolidated organic revenue turns negative and the cost-cut runway is exhausted after five years — FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $4.40. Emerald integration costs and the FCF dilution run above the guided $15M while capex rises to $80M, so free cash flow stalls near $195M and buyback capacity is absorbed by deleveraging ahead of the refinancing. The November 2027 refinancing prices 100-150bp wider, costing $10-16M pretax annually. AI licence revenue confirms the Q4 pattern of decline, removing the growth narrative, and the multiple compresses to 9x. A society-partner non-renewal within the 46% of Journal Subscriptions revenue would push the outcome below this level.

### Trajectory Context

The score path — 6.5 (May, BUY) → 5.8 (June 1) → 6.1 (June 17) → 5.9 now — reflects a specific sequence rather than drift. At the June 17 report the stock was $45.42 with the FY2027 guide already public (released June 16). Since then fundamentals are unchanged: no new operating data, no guidance revision, mildly positive single-analyst revisions. What has changed is that the price is 15% higher and Emerald has closed, roughly doubling net leverage into the maturity wall. Both changes compress risk/reward on an identical fundamental base, which is why the valuation and financial-health scores hold rather than improve. This is a third consecutive HOLD, and the justification has shifted: in June the call was "cheap, wait for the guide to be tested"; now it is "the guide is credible and 15% of the gap to fair value has been captured, with the two gating items — Learning stabilization and the refinancing — both unresolved."

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the margin of safety at $52.33 does not support new capital.

- **Base case: $61** — 12.5x the FY2027 adjusted EPS midpoint of $4.825; cross-checked at 8.5x EV/adjusted EBITDA of $487M less ~$1,060M pro forma net debt over 51.0M shares ($60.4). Upside +16.6%.
- **Bull case: $71** — 14x the $5.05 guidance upper end; cross-checked at 9.5x EV/EBITDA on $496M. Upside +35.7%.
- **Bear case: $40** — 9x adjusted EPS of $4.40; cross-checked at 7.0x EV/EBITDA on $450M. Downside -23.6%.
- **Probability-weighted return (55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear): approximately +10%** over 12 months.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, gated by the early-September 2026 Q1 FY2027 print, the FY2027 federal appropriations outcome, and refinancing execution ahead of November 2027.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Price returns to $44-46 with the FY2027 guide intact, restoring 30%+ upside to the $61 base.
2. Two consecutive quarters with Learning constant-currency revenue flat or better while Research holds ≥5% cc — the single change that would justify a 14x multiple.
3. The November 2027 facilities are refinanced or extended at a weighted rate at or below approximately 5.5%, removing the maturity concentration.
4. Own-content AI licence revenue (excluding the third-party pass-through) exceeds roughly $45M annualized with a disclosed recurring share.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance is cut below $4.60 or the margin guide below 26.5%.
2. Learning's constant-currency decline worsens beyond -7%, or Research cc growth falls below +2%.
3. Enacted FY2027 appropriations cut NIH/NSF materially and library renewal pricing or APC volumes show contraction.
4. Pro forma net leverage exceeds 3.0x, or refinancing prices above roughly 6.5%.
5. A marquee society partner within the 46% of Journal Subscriptions revenue signals non-renewal.

**Reassessment:** the Q1 FY2027 report in early September 2026 — first Emerald purchase accounting, first post-close debt balance, first Q4-comparable AI licensing disclosure, and a seasonally negative cash-flow quarter that should not be extrapolated. Do not pay for optionality ahead of it: near-term implied volatility at 48.7% against 36.0% realized prices a ±14.5% move to the September expiry.

## Conclusion

Wiley has executed the operational plan. Flat revenue with $55.5M more operating income, 220bp of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, 29% higher operating cash flow, net debt down $106M, a record $174M returned including buybacks at a $35.08 average, and FY2027 guidance implying 10-21% adjusted EPS growth constitute a credible turn in profitability and cash conversion. At 10.8x the FY2027 adjusted EPS midpoint, 7.7x EV/EBITDA and a 7.7% guided free-cash-flow yield with a 2.7% dividend raised for 32 consecutive years, the valuation is undemanding against a publishing peer median of 31.8x.

The discount is not purely a mispricing. Consolidated revenue has not grown; a third of it is shrinking 7%; margin expansion is overhead-led with the program extended for a fourth consecutive year; GAAP earnings are flattered by roughly $1.16 of tax benefit; tangible book is negative $863M; the acquisition of Emerald deployed 17% of equity value at an estimated multiple twice the company's own for 2% of first-year accretion; and nearly the entire capital structure must be refinanced by November 2027. Five-year TSR one-third below the company's own peer group is the market's recorded verdict on this combination.

At $52.33, +17% to base against -24% to bear is a fair-to-modestly-positive expected return without adequate asymmetry. Existing holders should stay invested — the FY2027 guide is achievable and the cost runway is real. New capital should wait for either the mid-$40s or for evidence on the two items that would change the multiple: Learning stabilizing, and the November 2027 refinancing being taken off the table at an acceptable rate.
