**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Franklin Resources, operating as Franklin Templeton, is one of the largest publicly traded independent active asset managers, with $1,661.2 billion of AUM as of September 30, 2025. The investment debate reduces to a single fact: the firm suffered $84.8 billion of net outflows in FY2025 ($97.4B in long-term net outflows), and $141.9 billion of that bleeding came from one affiliate, Western Asset Management (WAM), whose former co-CIO Ken Leech was indicted for trade cherry-picking. Ex-WAM, the rest of Franklin was net positive on long-term flows. The entire bull case rests on whether WAM stabilizes now that the DOJ has closed its criminal probe of the firm without charges (December 15, 2025).

The stock has re-rated 42% off its 52-week low of $21.06 to $31.02, driven by two developments: the DOJ resolution removing the most acute tail risk, and two consecutive earnings beats of 27.7% and 25.2% in the FY2026 quarters. Current-year EPS estimates have risen 6.8% over 90 days. This is a genuine relief from a depressed base, and it justifies not being short the stock. But the underlying franchise is in secular decline: adjusted EPS has fallen three straight years ($2.60 → $2.39 → $2.22), the effective fee rate is eroding (41.1bps → 40.5bps), and the dividend consumed essentially all of GAAP net income last year, with retained earnings actually falling.

I rate BEN a HOLD on income grounds. The 4.3% dividend yield is covered on a cash basis (~$912M FCF vs ~$688M paid), the DOJ overhang has lifted, and the stock trades at roughly consensus fair value ($31.02 vs median target $31.00). I cannot construct a credible 12-month appreciation thesis because the growth engine is running in reverse and the re-rating has already captured most of the relief. The honest classification is a yield-supported hold, not a buy. Bear case downside ($24, -23%) is meaningful enough that this is not a stock to add to here.

## Company Overview

Franklin operates a multi-affiliate ("multi-SIM") federation of specialist investment managers, each with its own brand: Western Asset, ClearBridge, Brandywine Global, Clarion Partners, Benefit Street Partners, Lexington Partners, Royce, Martin Currie, Putnam, K2, and the legacy Franklin/Templeton/Legg Mason franchises. This structure was assembled through acquisition, most consequentially Legg Mason (2020, ~$4.5B), then Lexington and Putnam (closed January 2024), and Apera (closed October 2025). The company reports as a single segment and derives 84% of revenue from sponsored funds.

The asset base of $1,661.2B breaks down as equity $686.2B (41%), fixed income $438.7B (26%), alternative $263.9B (16%), multi-asset $193.9B (12%), and cash management $78.5B (5%). The mix has shifted materially toward equity (40% of average AUM, up from 31% in FY2023), which carries lower fees than the fixed-income business it is replacing, creating a mechanical fee-rate headwind.

Franklin sits in the uncomfortable middle of the industry: too large and active to compete on cost with BlackRock and Vanguard's passive scale, and sub-scale relative to those giants in ETFs. Its strategic response is a pivot to higher-fee, stickier alternatives (Lexington secondaries, Clarion real estate, Benefit Street/Apera private credit) and vehicle innovation (Canvas SMAs, ETFs, tokenized money-market funds, a spot-Bitcoin ETF). The problem is arithmetic: the traditional active core, especially WAM, is bleeding faster than alternatives can grow.

## Financial Analysis

The defining financial fact is that revenue is holding while profitability has collapsed.

| Metric ($M) | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating revenue | 8,275 | 7,849 | 8,478 | 8,771 |
| Operating income | 1,774 | 1,102 | 408 | 604 |
| Operating margin | 21.4% | 14.0% | 4.8% | 6.9% |
| Net income (to BEN) | 1,292 | 883 | 465 | 525 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $1.72 | $0.85 | $0.91 |
| Adjusted EPS | — | $2.60 | $2.39 | $2.22 |
| Adjusted operating margin | — | 29.9% | 26.1% | 24.5% |

Several things stand out. First, the 3% FY2025 revenue increase was driven by one additional quarter of Putnam consolidation and equity-market appreciation, not net inflows. Revenue growth here is acquisition arithmetic, not organic franchise health.

Second, the gap between GAAP EPS ($0.91) and adjusted EPS ($2.22) is enormous, driven by $406.5M of intangible amortization, $226.6M of intangible impairments, and $162.4M of acquisition-related retention comp in FY2025. The amortization and impairment are non-cash, so adjusted earnings better reflect cash-generating capacity. But these are recurring features of a roll-up model, and the adjusted trend is what matters: down three straight years, with adjusted operating margin compressing ~540bps over two years. The market is right not to value this stock on $0.91 GAAP EPS; it is also right not to celebrate $2.22 adjusted EPS when the trajectory is declining.

Third, the effective fee rate fell from 41.1bps to 40.5bps, a direct consequence of the mix shift toward lower-fee equity and the loss of higher-fee fixed-income AUM at WAM. Performance fees ($474M in FY2025) provided an offset but are volatile and not a stable base.

Cash flow remains sound. Operating cash flow was $1,066M and FCF roughly $912M (FCF margin ~10.4%). Liquid assets of $5.65B comfortably cover parent debt of $2.36B, which the company reduced from $3.05B in FY2023 by repaying $400M of senior notes and a $300M revolver draw. Leverage is well within the 3.25x net-leverage covenant.

On capital allocation, the dividend was raised to $1.28/share (~$688M declared), which consumed essentially all of GAAP net income; retained earnings fell from $11,928M to $11,516M. Buybacks were modest at $240M. Management has explicitly signaled it will prioritize reinvestment and seed capital over buybacks. The dividend is safe on a cash basis but offers little cushion if AUM or markets reverse.

Two data-integrity notes for context. The Altman Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress") is a false signal: the model is not designed for asset managers and penalizes their low asset turnover and large goodwill/intangible base. The consolidated balance sheet is inflated by ~$12.3B of Consolidated Investment Products (mostly CLOs) for which BEN bears no recourse beyond its direct equity stake; these should be stripped out for any leverage analysis. Underlying solvency is not in question.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the case for holding weakens. The growth engine is producing net outflows. WAM alone accounts for the bulk of the firm's long-term net outflows, meaning the firm ex-WAM was net positive, the "two Franklins" framing. The entire forward growth question is whether WAM's bleeding stabilizes.

The constructive signals are real but should not be overweighted. EPS estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year consensus rose to $2.75, and next-year to $2.96 (+1.2%). Those revisions, however, do not represent the kind of turnaround that drives a sustained re-rating: the current-quarter estimate is down 3.6% over 90 days, and the rising full-year numbers partly reflect cost discipline and market appreciation lifting the AUM base rather than a return to organic inflows. The beats came against estimates that had been cut hard ($0.56 estimate vs $0.71 actual), so the magnitude flatters more than it informs.

Durability of growth requires net inflows to turn positive at the firm level, which requires WAM to stop bleeding. PwC's Critical Audit Matter disclosure embeds WAM AUM growth assumptions of just 2.5–3.1%, and a 50bp cut would add ~$21M of further impairment. The alternatives pivot (Lexington, Clarion, BSP, Apera) is the right strategic direction and is generating positive flows, but it is too small to offset the active core's contraction in the near term. I do not see a path to positive organic firm-level flows over the next 12 months that would re-rate the multiple upward. The growth score of 4.5 reflects a franchise where revenue is propped by acquisitions and markets while the underlying flow trend is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.02, BEN trades at ~14x adjusted EPS of $2.22 and ~23.7x trailing GAAP EPS. On forward consensus, the stock is at roughly 11x current-year adjusted estimates ($2.75) — optically cheap, but the right anchor for a declining-adjusted-EPS franchise is closer to the trailing figure.

Peer comparison is messy because the cohort is heterogeneous. T. Rowe Price, the cleanest active-manager comparable, trades at 11.2x with a 5.0% yield and is itself fighting outflows. Invesco shows negative trailing P/E. The alternative managers (Ares 59.7x, Blue Owl 86.2x) trade on entirely different growth and fee-permanence profiles and are not relevant comps for BEN's traditional active base. Against TROW, BEN's ~14x adjusted multiple and 4.3% yield look reasonable but not cheap; both are value-trap candidates in a structurally challenged segment.

The analyst distribution is informative: mean target $30.86, median $31.00, against a current price of $31.02. The stock is trading precisely at consensus fair value, with the spread from low ($24) to high ($37) capturing the WAM-binary outcome. The consensus has converged on roughly today's price after the relief re-rating, which has already captured the DOJ resolution and the earnings beats.

I score valuation 5.0. There is no specific mispricing I can identify: the multiple reflects a declining-earnings franchise with a covered dividend, fairly priced. The yield provides downside support, but the absence of organic growth caps upside. A low forward multiple is not sufficient when the adjusted earnings stream is in secular decline.

## Competitive Landscape

Franklin's competitive position is deteriorating, which is why I score it 4.0. The 10-K is unusually candid about the secular pressures: fee compression, the active-to-passive migration, client consolidation of manager relationships, and the rise of low-cost digital distribution. Franklin is losing share in its core active franchises; WAM's collapse is the extreme case, but the broader active fixed-income and core equity complex faces the same migration.

The strategic pivot to alternatives and private markets is correct and is where the moat is most defensible (Lexington's secondaries franchise and Clarion's real estate carry stickier, higher-fee mandates). But alternatives are 16% of AUM and cannot yet offset the active core's contraction. The Canvas SMA platform and ETF lineup (including tokenized funds) are forward-looking, but Franklin remains sub-scale in passive against BlackRock and Vanguard, where the cost advantage is structural and widening.

The trajectory matters more than the level. A mid-tier active manager losing share in its largest businesses while its growth segments are too small to compensate scores low on competitive position, even though the absolute franchise (brand, distribution relationships, $1.66T AUM) remains substantial.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk has improved. The DOJ decision to close the WAM cherry-picking probe without charges against the firm removes the most acute tail risk and corresponds to BEN's December 15 and 17 8-Ks. The CFTC had already closed its investigation in June 2025. This is the single most important development since the scandal broke and is the principal reason the stock is not an AVOID.

Residual risks remain material:
- **Civil litigation tail:** A securities class action (Western PA Electrical Employees Insurance Trust Fund, July 2025) names Franklin, WAM, and Leech; Leech still faces individual criminal charges.
- **Further WAM impairments:** The intangible is down to $450M after two years of write-downs ($389.2M FY2024, $200M FY2025). PwC's CAM disclosure shows continued outflows would trigger more.
- **Structural fee/mix erosion:** Industry-wide but acute here given the active core's exposure.
- **Acquisition/earnout drag:** Contingent payments of up to €125M (Apera) and $375M (Putnam) plus recurring amortization and retention comp.
- **Other legal:** India credit-fund matter (~$64M SEBI penalties/disgorgement plus launch ban, under appeal); 401(k) self-dealing ERISA suit (July 2025); GDPR exposure.
- **Dividend-trap risk:** The payout consumes all GAAP income; an AUM/market reversal would pressure coverage.

I score risk 5.0: the DOJ resolution removes a binary downside, but the civil tail, impairment risk, and structural fee pressure are not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced-to-mildly-cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 32.9% is rich versus 26.7% historical vol (a 6.2pp spread), and IV rose 5.4pp since early May, indicating the market is pricing modestly elevated uncertainty into upcoming catalysts. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.7%, and the 6-month skew is mildly negative (-2.8%), so there is no strong directional bet either way. The flat term structure (near 32.9% ≈ far 31.3%) suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single event. The implied ±5.9% near-term move and rising P/C OI ratio (0.09 → 0.16) are consistent with the next earnings print being the key swing factor. The richness of IV argues against paying up for upside exposure here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($37, +19%):** WAM outflows stabilize materially post-DOJ resolution, firm-level net flows turn neutral-to-positive as the alternatives franchise (Lexington, Clarion, BSP, Apera) grows and the active core stops bleeding. Adjusted EPS stabilizes near $2.75+ on cost discipline and a rising AUM base, the effective fee rate floors out as the mix shift matures, and the market re-rates the stock toward 13–14x forward adjusted earnings on improved flow visibility. The 4.3% dividend plus modest multiple expansion delivers a high-teens total return.

**Bear Case ($24, -23%):** WAM outflows persist beyond the firm's 2.5–3.1% AUM growth assumptions, triggering further impairments and continued firm-level net outflows. Fee-rate erosion accelerates as the equity mix shift and passive migration grind on, adjusted EPS slides below $2.20, and the dividend's lack of GAAP cushion forces a buyback halt and raises questions about future dividend growth. The civil class action produces a settlement overhang. The stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $24 and a ~5.5% yield.

**Base Case ($31, flat):** WAM bleeding slows but does not reverse, firm-level flows stay modestly negative, adjusted EPS holds in the $2.20–$2.75 range, and the stock trades sideways near consensus fair value with the 4.3% dividend providing the total return. The DOJ relief is already in the price; the next leg requires evidence of organic flow recovery that I do not expect within 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The thesis is that the dividend is covered and the DOJ overhang has lifted, providing downside support around the analyst low, while the absence of organic growth caps upside near consensus. The total-return expectation is roughly the dividend yield, not capital appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of firm-level positive long-term net flows, confirming WAM stabilization, OR WAM long-term outflows decelerating below ~$15B/quarter.
- Adjusted EPS revisions turning decisively positive (next-year consensus above $3.05) driven by flow recovery rather than market appreciation.
- Effective fee rate stabilizing or rising above 40.5bps for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive year of WAM intangible impairment, or firm-level long-term outflows accelerating beyond FY2025's $97.4B pace.
- Dividend coverage on a cash basis deteriorating (FCF falling below the ~$688M payout) or any signal of a dividend cut.
- An adverse securities class-action development materially increasing the contingent liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the FY2026 Q3 print), with particular focus on the WAM flow trajectory in the first full quarters after the DOJ resolution.

## Conclusion

Franklin Resources is a structurally challenged active manager whose most acute risk, the DOJ criminal probe of Western Asset, has been resolved favorably, prompting a justified 42% relief rally. The dividend is covered on a cash basis and yields 4.3%, and the stock trades at consensus fair value. Those facts support holding the shares for income.

What is missing is a growth thesis. Adjusted EPS has declined three straight years, the firm is running net outflows driven by an affiliate whose franchise damage may not be fully repaired, fee rates are eroding, and the dividend consumes all GAAP income with no buyback cushion. The re-rating has already captured the available relief, and bear-case downside to $24 (-23%) is real. I rate BEN a HOLD on income grounds, classified as an income hold rather than a growth hold, with the recommendation to revisit upon evidence of stabilized firm-level flows. This is a stock to own for the dividend if already held, not one to add to here.
