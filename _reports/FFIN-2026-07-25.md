**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Financial Bankshares is a Texas-only community bank holding company that consistently ranks among the most profitable regional banks in the country. The operating metrics are elite: Q1 2026 return on assets of 1.89%, an efficiency ratio of 44.98%, CET1 of 20.23%, and a loan-to-deposit ratio around 62%. Net interest margin is expanding (3.86% tax-equivalent, up 12bps YoY) as the bank deploys a low-cost, granular deposit base (1.47% cost of deposits, ~26% noninterest-bearing) into higher-yielding assets while Fed cuts lower funding costs. Earnings quality is high: Q1 operating cash flow of $101.6M exceeded reported net income, and provisions are back to normalized levels after a one-off 2025 fraud event.

The investment debate is about the price rather than the business. FFIN trades at roughly 17x forward EPS ($2.05 current-year consensus against a $34.68 price) versus a regional-bank peer median near 11x, and offers only a ~2.5% dividend yield versus a peer median of 1.9%. The company has historically commanded this premium on the strength of its operating record, but its cumulative shareholder return has lagged its peer group (TSR 85.1 vs 109.39), indicating a multiple that has been slowly compressing rather than expanding. Overlaying that is a $367.5M pre-tax ($290M net-of-tax) unrealized loss in the available-for-sale securities book, which depresses tangible book value even though the bank's AOCI opt-out keeps it out of regulatory capital.

The new element in the story is a completed April 2026 acquisition (confirmed via 8-K Item 2.01, though the target and terms were not independently verifiable), which explains why management has hoarded capital rather than executing its live 5-million-share buyback authorization. It is a strategic pivot toward inorganic growth after years of purely organic expansion, and it is both the key forward catalyst and the key new risk. The trajectory of scores across prior reports (5.6 → 5.9 → 6.0) reflects improving fundamentals partially offset by a de-rated valuation dimension; we continue that pattern with a HOLD, classified as a growth hold: the franchise compounds, but the entry price does not offer asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

First Financial Bankshares (Abilene, Texas) operates First Financial Bank across 79 Texas locations concentrated in central-west Texas, the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, and southeast Texas. It reports a single operating segment: Texas community banking. Its wealth unit, rebranded to First Financial Wealth Management in July 2026, manages roughly $11.9B in trust assets (+9.7% YoY) and generates the largest recurring slice of fee income.

A note on identity is warranted because it materially affects data quality: FFIN (Abilene, TX) is routinely confused with First Financial Bancorp (FFBC, Cincinnati, OH), a different company. Automated screens that pull parent-holding-company XBRL data show only ~$1B in "total assets" and produce meaningless Altman Z-Scores and debt-to-equity ratios for a bank whose consolidated balance sheet is $15.4B and whose "debt" is largely customer deposits. All figures here reflect the consolidated Texas entity.

The franchise's differentiation is operating excellence and conservatism rather than growth velocity. Total assets are $15.4B; loans held-for-investment are $8.29B (growing ~1.6% QoQ); deposits are $13.25B. The bank runs unusually liquidity-rich, with a ~62% loan-to-deposit ratio and $4B+ of contingent liquidity (FHLB $2.31B, Fed discount window $1.87B, plus lines).

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 income statement shows continued momentum:

| Net income | $71.5M | $61.3M | +16.6% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.50 | $0.43 | +16% |
| Net interest income | $134.8M | $118.8M | +13.5% |
| NIM (tax-equiv.) | 3.86% | 3.74% | +12bps |
| ROA | 1.89% | 1.78% | improving |
| ROE | 14.83% | 15.12% | modestly lower (equity grew) |
| Efficiency ratio | 44.98% | 46.36% | improving |
| Provision | $2.3M | $3.5M | normalized |

The earnings driver is NIM expansion. Deposit growth is being deployed into higher-yielding loans and securities, while interest-bearing liability cost fell roughly 20bps as the Fed cut rates (target 3.50–3.75%). The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (+200bps rate shock → +1.6% NII), so continued Fed cuts are a modest headwind to spread income, but they support the securities book and reduce the AOCI drag. A higher-for-longer rate path in the current macro regime is a mild positive for NII but a continued weight on tangible book.

Capital allocation is the tell. The dividend is $0.19/quarter, a ~38% payout that sits below both the company's 40–50% policy and its 55% covenant cap, implying room to raise. No buybacks have been executed against a 5-million-share authorization. Combined with fortress ratios (Total RBC 21.42%, CET1 20.23%, Tier 1 leverage 12.58%), this is deliberate dry powder that the April 2026 acquisition now partly explains.

Earnings quality is strong. Operating cash flow of $101.6M in Q1 exceeded net income; charge-offs and provisions are low (NCOs 0.02%, NPAs 0.35% of assets). The earnings-surprise record is mixed but not alarming: over the last four quarters, two beats (+3.8%, +4.6%) and two misses (-2.4%, -24.8%), the large miss being the Q3 2025 fraud-driven quarter. Estimate revisions are broadly positive but modest: current-year consensus up +0.3% over 90 days to $2.05, next-year up +1.4% to $2.16.

## Growth Analysis

FFIN's organic growth engine is above-average population and economic expansion in Texas, driving mid-single-digit loan and deposit growth (loans HFI +1.6% QoQ, trust AUM +9.7% YoY). This is durable but not explosive. The bank's conservatism, its defining trait, is also a governor on growth: a 62% loan-to-deposit ratio means it is leaving balance-sheet leverage on the table in exchange for resilience.

The step-change growth lever is now M&A. The completed April 2026 acquisition marks a departure from years of purely organic expansion and, if it becomes a repeatable capability, is the most credible path to accelerating EPS beyond the mid-single-digit organic rate. Consolidation among Texas community banks gives a well-capitalized acquirer with a premium currency structural advantage. The risk is that the target and terms were not independently confirmable, so integration economics and the deployment of the hoarded capital cannot yet be assessed. Because the deal closed after the March 31 balance-sheet date, none of it is reflected in Q1 financials (intangibles were flat at $313.6M).

Consistent with the equity-selection framework, we do not treat modest positive estimate revisions as an inflection signal; this is not a stock recovering from a trough with accelerating revenue. Growth prospects are solid and improving (the dimension has risen from 5.0 to ~5.8 across recent reports), but the growth thesis rests on the still-unproven M&A pivot rather than an accelerating organic curve.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the constraint on this recommendation, and it is where our score has de-rated most (from 6.0 to 4.5 in the prior report, held near there now). FFIN trades at ~17x forward EPS against a Regional Banks peer median of 11.3x:

| SFBS | 15.0 | 1.9% | -12% |
| **FFIN** | **~17** | **2.5%** | **-9%** |

FFIN carries a ~50% premium to the peer median P/E. The quality genuinely justifies a premium: an ROA of 1.89% and an efficiency ratio under 45% place it in the top decile of the group, and SFBS (a comparably high-quality Texas name) also trades rich at 15x. But the relevant question is whether the premium can expand, and the evidence says no: cumulative shareholder return has lagged the peer group, indicating the multiple has been drifting lower, not higher. The dividend yield offers some absolute support and beats the peer median, but it is not high enough to anchor a value case on its own.

We resist reflexively rewarding "cheapness" here, but the opposite problem applies. FFIN is a premium-quality name at a premium price with a slowly compressing multiple and a tangible-book overhang. The AOCI loss depresses tangible book value and is the most plausible explanation for the de-rating. There is no earnings inflection that would justify treating trailing multiples as understated, and at ~17x forward with mid-single-digit organic growth, the PEG is unfavorable. Analyst targets frame the setup precisely: consensus mean and median both sit at $37.00 (range $35–$39), roughly 1–12% above the current price. On a stable-growth, premium-multiple name, those targets are a reasonable ceiling estimate rather than a stale floor. The valuation score of 4.8 reflects a fairly-to-slightly-richly valued stock with no identifiable catalyst to force the multiple higher.

## Competitive Landscape

FFIN's competitive position within Texas community banking is strong and stable-to-improving. Its moat is operational: a low-cost, granular, sticky deposit base (26% noninterest-bearing, uninsured deposits only ~30% of total and well-collateralized by contingent liquidity) that most competitors cannot replicate, and a cost structure that lets it out-earn larger rivals on the same assets. The wealth-management franchise adds recurring, capital-light fee income that diversifies away from spread reliance.

The improving margin trajectory (efficiency ratio down 138bps) is direct evidence the competitive position is not eroding. Against larger consolidating banks in DFW and statewide, FFIN competes on relationship banking and conservatism rather than price or scale. The main structural challenge is that its very conservatism caps market-share gains: a 62% loan-to-deposit ratio means it grows more slowly than aggressive peers by choice. The competitive-position score of 6.5 reflects a durable, well-defended franchise with a stable-to-slightly-improving trajectory rather than one taking rapid share.

## Risk Assessment

**AOCI / interest-rate risk (largest quantifiable risk).** The $5.67B AFS portfolio carries the unrealized loss at a 5.55-year duration, most correlated to the 5-year UST. A +100bp move in the 5-year would add ~$271M of unrealized loss. Mitigants are meaningful: the AOCI opt-out keeps this out of regulatory capital, there are no HTM securities (full transparency), and fortress ratios absorb the mark. But it is the swing factor for tangible book and the likely driver of the multiple de-rating.

**Concentration.** 100% Texas, with direct exposure to oil & gas, cattle/agriculture, wind energy, and severe-weather cycles. CRE is 23.6% of loans (non-owner-occupied only 9.97%), manageable but subject to reset risk on low-rate vintages repricing.

**Operational/fraud risk.** A single-borrower fraud caused a $21.55M loss in 2025, more than doubling FY25 provisions and spiking NCOs to 0.29%. Q1 2026 confirms full normalization, so this appears contained, but it exposed a control gap at a bank whose thesis rests on conservatism. A residual elevated C&I allowance ($20.7M) is the fingerprint.

**Acquisition/integration risk.** The April 2026 deal is now live and its terms are unconfirmed. Integration missteps or an overpriced deal would be the most likely source of a negative surprise, and the market cannot yet assess the economics.

**CEO transition.** F. Scott Dueser (49-year veteran) moved to Executive Chairman; David W. Bailey became President & CEO. This is an orderly insider succession, continuity-positive, but Dueser defined the conservative culture, so execution is worth monitoring.

Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, there was no material change to the 10-K risk factors. The risk score of 5.5 reflects a low-binary-risk business with two idiosyncratic swing factors (AOCI, the unverified acquisition) that are partially but not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that fundamentals do not obviously support. Near-term ATM IV is 64.0% against 1-year historical vol of 25.6%, richly priced, with the term structure in backwardation (64.0% 1-month vs 27.8% 6-month) and an implied move of ±12.5% into the nearest expiry. ATM IV jumped from 40.2% to 64.0% over the past month while the stock rose ~4.6%. This spike likely reflects positioning and lingering uncertainty about the acquisition's terms rather than a directional bearish view; put/call skew is essentially balanced at -1.2%. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that any near-term entry faces elevated implied volatility, and a longer-horizon owner need not pay into that premium.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($39–$42).** The M&A pivot proves repeatable and accretive, deploying the hoarded capital into EPS-accretive Texas consolidation while maintaining top-decile ROA. Fed cuts continue, lifting the securities book and shrinking the AOCI drag, which relieves the tangible-book overhang and allows the multiple to re-rate back toward its historical premium. Organic Texas growth stays mid-single-digit, the dividend is raised (payout has room from 38%), and NIM holds near 3.86%. At ~19x $2.16 next-year EPS, the stock reaches ~$41.

**Bear Case ($28–$30).** A higher-for-longer rate path or a 5-year UST backup deepens the AOCI loss and further compresses tangible book, extending the multiple de-rating that has already caused FFIN to lag peers. The acquisition disappoints on price or integration, Texas commodity/CRE cycles pressure credit, and the premium multiple converges toward the peer median. At ~13x $2.16, the stock falls to ~$28, roughly -19% from current.

**Base Case ($36–$37).** Steady mid-single-digit EPS growth, NIM roughly stable, AOCI slowly improving as rates ease, and the acquisition modestly accretive without transforming the growth rate. The multiple holds near 17x on continued quality recognition but does not expand. This lands around $36–$37, consistent with the consensus mean/median of $37.00.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, classified as a **growth hold**: the underlying franchise compounds book value and earnings at a mid-single-digit-plus rate with elite returns, and the M&A pivot offers optionality, but the current price does not offer asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$30s / high-$20s (≤~14x forward EPS), where the quality premium becomes cheap enough to offer asymmetric upside.
- Confirmation that the April 2026 acquisition is EPS-accretive with disciplined pricing (verified terms + accretion guidance), signaling a repeatable inorganic growth engine.
- A sustained decline in the 5-year UST that materially shrinks the AOCI loss and rebuilds tangible book, removing the de-rating driver.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material rate backup that pushes the AFS unrealized loss well beyond $500M pre-tax and forces a tangible-capital conversation.
- Evidence the acquisition is overpriced or dilutive, or a second operational/fraud loss indicating the 2025 event was not idiosyncratic.
- Deterioration in credit (NPAs rising back above ~0.75% of assets, provisions re-accelerating) tied to Texas commodity or CRE cycles.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release, which should show a full quarter of the acquisition's contribution and allow proper assessment of its economics. Verification of the acquisition's target and terms via the 8-K exhibit should occur sooner.

## Conclusion

First Financial Bankshares is one of the highest-quality regional banks available: top-decile ROA and efficiency, fortress capital, a conservative and liquidity-rich balance sheet, expanding margins, and high-quality cash-generative earnings. The financial-health improvement across our recent reports (6.5 → 7.5) is fully earned. The problem is that all of this is well known and priced: at ~17x forward with a ~50% premium to the peer median P/E, a ~2.5% yield, a multiple that has been compressing, and the net AOCI overhang on tangible book, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The M&A pivot is a genuine new catalyst, but its terms are unconfirmed and its accretion unproven.

We maintain **HOLD**. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and we have raised the bar accordingly: the fundamentals continue to improve, but the valuation and tangible-book overhang justify continuing the call rather than upgrading. Own FFIN for the compounding franchise and rising dividend; wait for a pullback toward the low-$30s or confirmation that the acquisition creates value before treating it as a BUY.
