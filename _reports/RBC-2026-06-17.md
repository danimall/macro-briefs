**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RBC Bearings is one of the higher-quality industrial franchises in the public market: a sole-source supplier of certified precision bearings and engineered components whose products get designed into aircraft, submarines, and missile platforms during multi-year development cycles, then earn recurring OEM and aftermarket revenue for the life of the platform. FY26 (ended March 28, 2026) delivered revenue of $1,871M (+14.3%), net income of $288M (+23%), and free cash flow of $343M (18.3% FCF margin), while long-term debt fell from $918M to $702M. The business is accelerating, the moat is real, and the capital-allocation playbook (pay down acquisition debt, then redeploy into distressed mechanical-products tuck-ins) is disciplined and repeatable.

The problem is price. At $621, RBC trades at 68x trailing EPS and roughly 43x current-year consensus ($14.36) for ~14% forward EPS growth (FY27 consensus $14.36 vs FY26 $9.09 reflects the trailing-to-forward step; the consensus FY27→FY28 step of $14.36→$16.39 is ~14%), a PEG near 3.1. Earnings are growing steadily, not recovering from a cyclical trough, so the forward multiple is the correct lens and it is a premium one. The market's reaction to the May 15 Q4 print is the tell: shares fell roughly 7% intraday on an 8% EPS beat and a guidance raise. When a stock sells off on good news, the multiple already embeds perfection, and the asymmetry tilts toward downside on any program-timing slip.

We rate RBC a HOLD with a growth bias. The 12-month thesis is positive — submarine revenue targeted to double over 24-36 months, ~40% of commercial OEM long-term agreements repricing into post-inflation rates by January 2027, term-loan retirement freeing capital for the next M&A leg — and we would own it. At 43x forward we cannot underwrite a BUY: the entry offers no margin of safety, and the same backlog concentration that drives the growth (marine + VACCO = ~$1.7B of a $2.3B book) is the lever that derates the stock if government build rates slip a quarter or two.

## Company Overview

RBC manufactures highly engineered precision bearings, gearing, and engineered components, deliberately positioned at the high-complexity end of the market where engineering content creates pricing power and switching barriers. The company operates 65 facilities (44 manufacturing) across 11 countries with 5,816 employees, organized into two segments:

- **Industrial (58% of FY26 sales, $1,082.9M, 47.0% gross margin):** Bearings, Dodge-branded gearing, and motion-control components serving construction, mining, aggregates/cement, food & beverage, warehousing/logistics, semiconductor, power gen, and rail. Customers include Caterpillar, Komatsu, Halliburton, and distributors Motion Industries and Applied Industrial.
- **Aerospace & Defense (42% of FY26 sales, $788.0M, 40.7% gross margin, accelerating):** Bearings and components for commercial and defense aircraft, marine (submarines), missiles/guided munitions, and space. Customers include Boeing, Airbus, Newport News Shipbuilding, Lockheed, Northrop, RTX, the DOD, Blue Origin, SpaceX, and Rocket Lab.

The economic engine is the product-approval dynamic. A&D bearings are designed-in during a platform's 3-6 year development cycle and certified by the OEM, FAA (via PMAs), or DOD, frequently making RBC the sole or primary supplier for that platform's production life. This generates long-tailed, recurring revenue with limited risk of competitive displacement. Competitors vary by product line (SKF, Timken, Regal Rexnord, New Hampshire Ball Bearings, NORD) but rarely compete head-to-head across RBC's full certified portfolio.

The multi-year trend is a high-quality, accelerating compounder:

| Free cash flow | — | — | ~$244M | $343M |

The dominant capital-allocation story is deleveraging. Total debt has fallen from ~$1.8B at the Dodge acquisition (FY2022) to $875.5M at FY26, cutting interest expense from $79M (FY24) to $50M (FY26). Management expects to fully retire the term loan by November 2026. RBC pays no dividend and has never executed its $100M buyback authorization — all excess cash services debt. The October 2024 conversion of the 5% Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred eliminated ~$12.4M of annual preferred dividends, a tailwind to common EPS in FY26.

FCF conversion was 119% in FY26, flattered by working-capital timing; the ~73% Q4 figure is a more normalized quarterly read. The margin-mix dynamic: Industrial (47% gross) carries higher margin than A&D (40.7%), so faster A&D growth dilutes consolidated gross margin near-term — yet A&D margins are themselves expanding (44.2% adjusted in Q4 vs ~41-43% trailing) on volume leverage and new contract pricing. FY27 guidance calls for consolidated adjusted gross margin of 45.25-45.5%, roughly 50bps of expansion.

The balance sheet is sound: current ratio 2.18, D/E 0.52, in compliance with the max 4.5x net-leverage covenant. The Altman Z of 2.85 ("grey zone") is an artifact of the goodwill/intangible-heavy, acquisition-financed balance sheet (~$2.0B goodwill + intangibles, 40%+ of assets), not a genuine distress signal given $416M operating cash flow and active debt paydown. We do not over-weight it.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a confluence of A&D secular tailwinds, with backlog the clearest evidence: it grew from $0.9B (FY25) to $2.3B (FY26).

- **Marine/submarine** is the standout. Management is capacity-"taxed" and targeting a doubling of marine revenue over 24-36 months, driven by the Virginia- and Columbia-class build-out plus fleet spares. This explains the $1.1B marine backlog.
- **Missiles/munitions** — content across Patriot, GMLRS, Tomahawk, Standard Missile, Javelin, Meteor, the Hellfire replacement, and a hypersonics program. Missile revenue exceeded $45M in FY26, with both volume and content-share gains targeted. DOD multiyear missile agreements imply substantial volume growth over the next 5-7 years.
- **Space** — revenue grew from $4M (2021) to >$70M (FY26, including $30M from VACCO), serving legacy primes and new-space (SpaceX, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab). RBC is a parts supplier into these programs, not a developer; Starship-related contribution is currently modest.
- **Commercial aerospace** — riding elevated Boeing/Airbus build rates; management guides >15% growth in FY27.
- **Industrial inflection** — modest but broadening across aggregates, semiconductors, food/beverage, and warehousing. Management links aggregates strength (~17-20% of Industrial) to AI data-center construction build-out.

Two structural tailwinds sit beneath reported numbers. First, ~40% of commercial OEM long-term agreements have not yet repriced into post-inflation rates and reset by January 2027 — a probable margin tailwind. Second, the VACCO acquisition (closed July 18, 2025; $83.9M of FY26 sales, $0.6B backlog) materially boosts missile and space content. VACCO's Q4 46%+ gross margin is not a run-rate; management normalized it to ~mid-30s%.

Earnings momentum supports the trajectory: four straight beats (8.0%, 5.2%, 4.3%, 2.5%), and consensus estimates have risen +3.0% (current year) and +3.0% (next year) over 90 days. This is positive, steady revision momentum — not the +30%-over-90-days signal that would justify overriding a premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the BUY case breaks. At $621:

- **Trailing P/E:** 68x on $9.09 EPS.
- **Forward P/E:** ~43x on FY27 consensus $14.36; ~38x on FY28 $16.39.
- **PEG:** ~3.1 on ~14% forward EPS growth (FY27 $14.36 → FY28 $16.39).

The cycle-adjustment principle applies only partially here. RBC is not at a cyclical earnings trough where trailing P/E misleads — earnings are growing steadily, so the forward multiple is the correct anchor, and ~43x forward for growth in the mid-teens is a premium, not a mispricing.

Against peers, the dispersion is wide but RBC sits at the top: ITT 32.9x, Crane 37.4x, Lincoln Electric 26.6x, Graco 24.2x, Mueller 17.7x, peer median 26.6x. RBC's ~43x forward (and 68x trailing) commands a meaningful premium to the entire set. A premium is defensible given the sole-source moat, the marine ramp, and the deleveraging flywheel, but the magnitude leaves no margin of safety. Analyst targets bracket the current price: mean $616, median $640, high $680, low $490. The stock trades essentially at the consensus mean, and on a name at 43x forward, consensus targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of upside.

The single most informative data point is the May 15 reaction: shares fell roughly 7% intraday (from $612 to $569) despite an 8% EPS beat and a guidance raise. When a quality compounder sells off on a beat, the multiple already prices perfection. That is the valuation concern that prevents a BUY: at this multiple, beats are expected and produce no re-rating, while any program-timing slip (the backlog is ~$1.7B concentrated in lumpy, government-build-rate-dependent marine + VACCO) derates the stock hard. Valuation scores low (4.0) because the entry price offers asymmetric downside, not because the business is impaired.

## Competitive Landscape

RBC's competitive position is among the strongest in the data set and improving. The certified, designed-in, sole-source model is a structural moat: once a bearing is qualified into a submarine or missile platform, displacement is rare for the platform's production life. Backlog growth, four consecutive beats, and expanding A&D margins all confirm the company is gaining content share, not merely riding volume. Customer concentration is improving — top-10 customers fell to 35% of sales (FY26) from 44% (FY25), aided by VACCO and broadening defense programs.

The M&A engine reinforces the moat: 30 acquisitions since 1992, with a specific, repeatable playbook — mechanical-products companies serving RBC's existing customer base, ideally distressed and geographically convenient, that RBC can fix and margin-enhance. Dodge and VACCO both exceeded expectations. Term-loan retirement reloads the balance sheet for the next leg of this strategy. We score competitive position 8.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely move the stock, beyond sector boilerplate:

1. **Backlog concentration and program timing.** The backlog is ~$1.7B marine + VACCO. This is genuine multi-year defense demand, but it is program-driven, lumpy, and subject to government inspection/build-rate timing that shifts revenue between quarters. At 43x forward, a one- or two-quarter slip is a derating catalyst.
2. **Goodwill/intangible concentration.** ~$2.0B (40%+ of assets) from Dodge and VACCO. Reporting-unit headroom exists (≥38.8%), but a demand reversal could trigger impairment.
3. **Tariffs / steel.** Steel is the largest raw input, subject to Section 232 tariffs. Many contracts are fixed-price with a lag before surcharges pass through; management reports no material impact "to date" but flags high-alloy steel and titanium availability as watch items.
4. **LTA repricing — two-sided.** The January 2027 reprice is a likely margin tailwind but a risk if customers push back.
5. **CEO key-person risk.** Heavy reliance on long-tenured Chairman/President/CEO Dr. Michael J. Hartnett, with no publicly disclosed succession plan — the most under-addressed governance issue.
6. **Interest-rate re-exposure.** The interest-rate swap expired December 30, 2025 with no replacement decided, leaving modest floating-rate exposure on remaining debt.

We score risk factors 5.5 — moderate idiosyncratic risk, with program-timing and goodwill concentration as the items not fully priced relative to a premium multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation (near-term ATM IV 36.0% vs long-term 29.8%), signaling the market is pricing near-term event risk. IV is rich vs 1-year historical volatility (36.0% vs 26.2%, a 9.9pp spread), and IV sits at the 66th percentile of its chain. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%) and the put/call OI ratio is low (0.20), so there is no structural bearish positioning — the elevated near-term IV reflects event-hedging more than a directional bet. IV has collapsed from 63.4% (May 12, pre-earnings) to 36.0%, confirming the post-earnings vol resolution. Short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float (1.6 days to cover) — no squeeze dynamics, and no informed short positioning to corroborate a bearish view. The options market reads as neutral-with-event-caution, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($720-760):** Marine revenue doubles on schedule, missile content-share gains compound, the January 2027 LTA reprice lifts commercial margins ~100bps, term-loan retirement funds an accretive distressed tuck-in, and consolidated growth holds in the high teens. Apply ~45x to FY28 EPS approaching ~$17 and the stock reaches the mid-$700s. This requires the backlog to convert cleanly and the premium multiple to hold.

**Bear Case ($465-500):** A government build-rate slip pushes marine revenue right by two to three quarters, an LTA reprice disappoints, or a broader industrial slowdown trims the cycle-sensitive 58% of revenue. With the multiple this stretched, a modest growth miss compresses the forward P/E from ~43x toward the high-20s peer median, taking the stock to ~$465-500 (the low end of analyst targets), a 21-25% drawdown. The May 15 reaction shows how quickly the market punishes anything short of perfection here.

**Base Case ($640-660):** Growth tracks guidance (low-to-mid-teens revenue, ~50bps gross margin expansion), FY27 EPS lands near $14.36, and the multiple holds roughly flat as deleveraging completes. That yields ~$640-660 over 12 months, ~3-6% upside from current — positive but not asymmetric enough to underwrite a BUY at this entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 print (revenue guided $500-510M, +14.7-17%), then the January 2027 LTA reprice and the November 2026 term-loan retirement.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$520-540 (sub-37x forward), which would restore a margin of safety against the moat and growth — the most likely path to a BUY given fundamentals are intact.
- Estimate revisions accelerating to +10%+ over 90 days (vs the current +3%), signaling the marine/missile ramp is beating consensus and the multiple is being underwritten by faster growth.
- Confirmed marine revenue inflection (a quarter showing the doubling trajectory ahead of plan) that re-rates the durability of the growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Marine/VACCO backlog conversion slipping — two consecutive quarters of A&D revenue below guidance on program timing.
- Goodwill impairment or reporting-unit headroom compressing materially.
- Gross margin reversing below ~44% on steel/tariff pass-through failure or LTA pushback.

## Conclusion

RBC Bearings is a genuinely excellent business: a sole-source precision-bearings franchise with a widening A&D moat, a backlog led by a national-priority submarine ramp, a margin tailwind from the January 2027 LTA reprice, and a deleveraging-to-EPS flywheel that frees capital for a disciplined M&A engine. The score trajectory (5.9 → 6.6) reflects improving financial health, competitive position, and management quality, and the new data confirms that trend.

What it does not offer at $621 is an attractive entry. At ~43x forward EPS for growth in the mid-teens (PEG ~3.1), and trading at the top of its peer set, the stock prices flawless execution. The May 15 sell-off on an 8% beat is the concrete, falsifiable evidence: the market already demands perfection, so beats produce no re-rating while any slip in the lumpy, government-build-rate-dependent backlog derates the stock toward the low-$500s. The valuation is the specific concern that prevents a BUY: an observed asymmetry where downside (-21% to bear) exceeds realistic 12-month upside (~3-6% to base).

We rate RBC a HOLD (growth_hold). We would own quality at the right price and would upgrade on a pullback toward $520-540 or on estimate-revision acceleration that re-underwrites the multiple. For now, the business qualifies as a buy and the stock does not.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward EPS growth (FY27→FY28 consensus) is ~14%, not ~18%, raising the PEG to ~3.1 (from ~2.4) — a less attractive growth-adjusted valuation that strengthens, not weakens, the HOLD case.
