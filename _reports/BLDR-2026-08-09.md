## Executive Summary

Builders FirstSource is the largest U.S. supplier and manufacturer of building materials to professional homebuilders, with roughly 565 locations across 43 states, low customer concentration (top 10 customers at 14% of FY2025 sales), and two structural differentiators in Ready-Frame pre-cut framing packages and the Paradigm design-software subsidiary. The franchise quality is not in dispute. The problem is arithmetic: a debt load assembled when Adjusted EBITDA ran $2.3-4.4B now sits against FY2026 guidance of $1.0-1.2B.

Three developments since our last review argue for reducing exposure rather than holding. First, the July 30 Q2 print delivered a third consecutive adjusted-EPS miss ($1.17 versus $1.29 consensus) and a guidance reduction that lowered management's own housing-starts assumptions; consensus responded by cutting current-quarter EPS 39.2%, current-year 26.4%, and next-year 26.0% over thirty days. Our July upgrade to HOLD rested on estimate cuts decelerating, and that condition no longer holds. Second, derived net leverage moved from 2.69x at December 31, 2025 to approximately 4.11x on the FY2026 guidance midpoint, roughly 85% of the move coming from the collapsing denominator rather than new borrowing. H1 2026 income from operations of $145M did not cover H1 net interest expense of $151M. Third, the stock is 5.9% higher than at our last review ($70.55) despite those cuts, so the compression in expected return is not a price-action artifact.

We are not calling distress. Liquidity is $1.56B of excess revolver availability against a springing covenant that only activates below $180.1M, the earliest bond maturity is 2030, and the high-coupon notes trade at or above par. FY2026 free cash flow is guided to $400-500M, a 5.0-6.3% yield, and the OBBBA cash-tax benefit (H1 cash taxes of $5.9M versus $62.0M) is a durable support. At 4x leverage with 1.7x quarterly interest coverage, the equity behaves as a levered option on a housing recovery. Our probability-weighted 12-month value is $72-73 against a $74.69 basis, with no dividend to carry the wait. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

BLDR sells to builders and contractors, not consumers. FY2025 revenue of $15,191M split roughly evenly across four categories: specialty building products and services ($4,068M, +4.1%), lumber and lumber sheet goods ($3,876M, −9.2%), windows/doors/millwork ($3,836M, −9.5%), and manufactured products ($3,411M, −14.4%). Value-added products — manufactured components plus windows, doors and millwork — were 47.7% of sales and are the economic core of the business: trusses and wall panels require local plants, engineering and design capability that a pure distributor cannot replicate quickly.

The company operates in 48 of the top 50 CBSAs by single-family permits, employs roughly 28,000 people including ~2,700 sales representatives, and is under 1% unionized. Concentration is low on both sides of the ledger: largest customer 4% of sales, largest supplier 8% of materials purchases. That structure means no single builder pullback can impair the business, and equally that BLDR must win each job on price and service.

In Q2 2026 management collapsed three operating divisions (East, Central, West) into two, reduced the footprint from ~585 to ~565 locations, and reassigned goodwill across the new reporting units. Peter Jackson is CEO, promoted from CFO; Pete Beckmann is CFO. A COO succession is in progress, with Form 4s showing Michael Hiller filing as "COO-Designate" on June 4 and Stephen Herron as COO on June 5.

## Financial Analysis

The cyclical unwind is severe and the operating leverage is working against the company at roughly 2:1.

| $M | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 22,726 | 17,097 | 16,400 | 15,191 | 7,150 |
| Gross margin | 34.1% | 35.2% | 32.8% | 30.4% | 28.2% |
| SG&A % of sales | 17.5% | 22.4% | 23.1% | 25.2% | 26.2% |
| Income from operations | 3,770 | 2,176 | 1,595 | 786 | 145 |
| Operating margin | 16.6% | 12.7% | 9.7% | 5.2% | 2.0% |
| Net interest expense | 198 | 192 | 208 | 274 | 151 |
| Net income (loss) | 2,749 | 1,541 | 1,078 | 435 | (51) |
| Company-reported Adj. EBITDA | 4,377 | 2,899 | 2,331 | 1,584 | — |

From the FY2022 Adjusted EBITDA peak of $4,377M to the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $1,100M, EBITDA falls 74.9% while revenue falls 36.6%. Gross margin has compressed roughly 710 basis points from the FY2023 peak, and management guides FY2026 gross margin to 27.5-28.5% against an H1 actual of 28.2%, so no back-half recovery is forecast.

**The cost line is the more troubling item.** SG&A dollars were essentially flat in FY2025 ($3,830M versus $3,788M) on 7.4% lower revenue, and in Q2 2026 SG&A fell only 3.0% against an 8.8% sales decline. Management realized roughly $28M of productivity savings in Q2 against a full-year target of $50-70M. On a ~$3.8B base that target is 1.3-1.8% of SG&A, which does not offset the deleverage. This is the practical reason revenue guidance of $14.0-14.8B produces EBITDA of $1.0-1.2B rather than something closer to $1.4B.

**Margin attribution matters and is not resolvable from disclosure.** Q2 2026 gross margin fell 260bp against a 7.0% core organic volume decline and 2.7% commodity deflation. Commodity deflation is roughly dollar-neutral for a distributor but dilutive to percentage margin. The residual points somewhere between volume deleverage in the high-fixed-cost truss and panel plants and genuine price competition. The mix evidence cuts toward deleverage — the least differentiated category (specialty) grew 4.1% in FY2025 while the highest-margin category (manufactured) fell 14.4% — but FY2025 acquisitions added 4.6% to total sales, so the organic specialty trend may be materially weaker than reported. We treat the split as unresolved.

**Interest coverage, not liquidity, is the pressure point.** Income from operations divided by net interest expense was 2.87x in FY2025, 1.69x in Q2 2026, and 0.96x for H1 2026. Cash interest paid rose 17.2% to $146.8M in H1, reflecting the 2025 issuance of $750M at 6.750%. FY2026 guidance implies interest of $280-290M against $1.0-1.2B of EBITDA.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt at June 30 was $4,632.4M principal ($4,591.3M net of discount and issuance costs) against cash of $65.7M, for derived net debt of $4,525.6M. Net debt to guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA is 4.11x at the midpoint, ranging 3.77x to 4.53x across the guidance band. The company's credit-agreement definition almost certainly includes add-backs that would produce a lower reported figure; we did not obtain BLDR's own stated ratio and treat 4.11x as an analyst estimate. Six months earlier the comparable calculation was 2.69x, and roughly 85% of the move is denominator.

The offsetting facts are real. Excess revolver availability was $1,559.8M against a springing fixed-charge test that activates only below $180.1M. The revolver matures May 2030; the earliest bond maturity is 2030. The 4.25% 2032 notes at 93 of par reflect coupon, not credit — every high-coupon issue trades at or above par. Tangible book value is deeply negative (derived −$1,193M; goodwill alone is 36.6% of assets), which is the arithmetic consequence of a decade of debt-funded acquisition and means book equity offers no downside support.

**Cash flow quality is deteriorating, and seasonality must be respected.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $155.5M versus $473.4M, with capex of $84.8M versus $188.7M, for derived H1 FCF of ~$70.7M versus ~$284.7M. We will not annualize this: BLDR builds working capital in H1 and releases in H2, and H1 receivables consumed $236.1M and inventories $170.8M against a $318.2M payables increase. Management guides FY2026 FCF to $400-500M, requiring an H2 release of $330-430M. As a sensitivity rather than a forecast, an H2 release that under-delivers by a third puts FY2026 FCF near $290-330M, a 3.6-4.1% yield; on the guided range the yield is 5.0-6.3%.

**Two items require correction of screening-model readings.** The Q2 GAAP net loss of $(3.9)M reflects a discrete $43.9M income tax charge from an IRS settlement on prior-year R&D expense treatment (effective tax rate 107.4%) with no current-period cash payment, because the company had previously remitted excess cash tax. That charge is non-cash and does not touch Adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, or covenants. It also does not rescue the underlying trend: Q2 pretax income of $52.4M on $3,862.5M of sales is a 1.36% pretax margin, and H1 pretax income was $(5.5)M. Separately, the reported trailing P/E of 80.4x implies TTM GAAP EPS near $0.93, which is inconsistent with the $3.89 EPS field (FY2025 GAAP diluted). We anchor all multiples on consensus adjusted EPS and enterprise value instead.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to underwrite over the next four quarters, and the estimate trend is the single most damaging datapoint in the file.

| Period | Current Est. | 30d Ago | 90d Ago | 30d Rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $1.07 | $1.77 | $1.78 | −39.2% |
| Next quarter | $0.64 | $0.98 | $0.98 | −35.2% |
| Current year | $3.16 | $4.29 | $4.31 | −26.4% |
| Next year | $4.47 | $6.04 | $6.06 | −26.0% |

Revisions of this magnitude in a single month, concentrated entirely in the post-print window, are the opposite of the inflection signal that would support a constructive call. Combined with three consecutive misses (−11.6%, −28.4%, −9.5%), forward-estimate confidence is low and the direction of surprise risk is negative.

The demand backdrop is the binding constraint. Q2 2026 Census data in the 10-Q shows single-family starts of 253K, down 4.2% year over year. Management's FY2026 guidance now assumes single-family starts down mid-to-high single digits, multi-family down mid-single digits, and repair-and-remodel down 1% within its geographies, a reduction from the roughly flat assumption embedded in the 10-K filed five months earlier. Q2 core organic declines were single-family −8.1%, multi-family −9.7%, and R&R −1.8%, with single-family contributing roughly two-thirds of the total organic decline.

Consensus embeds a modest 2027 recovery. Backing into it: FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.47 on ~106M shares, grossed for a 23% tax rate and adding back ~$275M of interest and ~$310M of tangible D&A, implies Adjusted EBITDA near $1.2B, about 15% above the FY2026 midpoint. The same build on FY2026 consensus of $3.16 implies ~$1.04B, at the low end of guidance. Consensus is therefore not aggressive, but it does require starts to stop declining. With the 30-year mortgage elevated and mortgage application demand soft, a policy rate cut would not automatically deliver the long-end relief housing needs. That mechanism — a policy cut need not translate into a lower mortgage rate — is the specific reason we do not underwrite a 2027 inflection at current prices.

The structural growth lever is also impaired. H1 2026 acquisition spend was ~$31.0M versus $891.9M in H1 2025, a 97% reduction, and capex guidance was cut from $250-300M to $175-225M. Capex now runs at roughly 35% of guided D&A of $580-610M. Against tangible D&A of ~$314M the ratio is a defensible 64% for plants running below capacity, but it removes the acquisition-plus-value-added-conversion engine that produced most of BLDR's historical growth.

|---|---|

| EV / FY2025 Adj. EBITDA ($1,584M) | 7.9x |
| EV / FY2024 Adj. EBITDA ($2,331M) | 5.4x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus adj. EPS $3.16 | 23.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus adj. EPS $4.47 | 16.7x |
| FCF yield on guided $400-500M | 5.0-6.3% |
| Dividend yield | 0.0% |
| Peer median P/E (SSD, AAON, AOS, AWI, WTS) | 22.7x |

BLDR is expensive on current earnings and cheap only on mid-cycle earnings. At 23.6x FY2026 consensus it trades at a premium to the building-products peer median of 22.7x while delivering falling estimates and 4x leverage against peers with cleaner balance sheets and, in AOS's and AWI's case, materially lower multiples. The 16.7x on FY2027 consensus is the fairest current-cycle marker and is not a bargain for a business whose forward EBITDA is 47% below the FY2024 level.

Two adjustments make the multiple worse than it looks. Adjusted EPS excludes roughly $281M of 2026 intangible amortization, or approximately $2.00 per share after tax. That amortization represents customer relationships acquired for cash and should not be treated as free. On a fully-loaded basis FY2027 earnings power is closer to $2.50 per share, and 17x that figure is $43. We do not use that as the base case, because a distribution roll-up's maintenance economics genuinely differ from its amortization schedule, but it defines why we will not pay a premium multiple on the adjusted number.

Second, the FY2025 FCF figure of $853M (10.7% on today's cap) circulating in screens is not repeatable. It was substantially a working-capital release in a declining-revenue year, and H1 2026 reversed it.

**Scenario construction.**

*Base — $76 (+1.8%).* Starts stabilize but do not recover; FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$1.2B in line with the consensus-implied build; net debt reduced to ~$4.2B by mid-2027 from guided FCF. At 9.5x EV/EBITDA that yields $68; at 17x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS it yields $76; the analyst mean of $80.71 and median of $81.00 sit modestly above. We take $76, which reconciles the EPS approach with the enterprise-value approach and sits essentially at spot.

*Bull — $103 (+38%).* Mortgage rates fall materially in 2027, single-family starts recover toward 1.05M, and mid-cycle Adjusted EBITDA rebuilds to ~$1.9B (below FY2024's $2.33B, reflecting permanently lower gross margin than 2022-23). At 8.0-8.5x EV/EBITDA and net debt reduced to ~$3.8B, equity is $109-118 in 2028, discounted back roughly 18 months at 12%. The 48% share-count reduction since 2021 makes this recovery per-share leverage genuine.

*Bear — $50 (−33%).* Single-family starts decline again in 2027 on a 7%+ mortgage rate; gross margin holds near 27% but EBITDA slips to ~$875M; derived leverage approaches 5.2x; the mandatory December 31, 2026 quantitative goodwill test, run against a forward base roughly 30% below the December 2025 test, produces an impairment. At 10-11x EV/EBITDA on $875M less ~$4.7B net debt, equity is $38-46; we set $50 to reflect that the market typically applies a higher multiple through a trough. A tail below $40 exists if impairment coincides with covenant proximity.

Probability-weighting 20% bull / 45% base / 35% bear gives $72.3, or −3.2% from the $74.69 basis with no dividend. That is the arithmetic behind REDUCE rather than HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which matters more than the absolute position.

BLDR's moat components are real: Ready-Frame pre-cut framing packages that compress builder cycle time; Paradigm, a wholly owned drafting, estimating and design-software subsidiary that embeds BLDR into the builder's specification workflow before an order exists; local truss and panel manufacturing that a pure distributor cannot replicate without capital and engineering; and diversification with the largest customer at 4% of sales.

Against that, the 10-K itself names the threat: Home Depot, through its SRS Distribution and GMS acquisitions, and Lowe's, through Foundation Building Materials, are "repositioning themselves to gain market share in the Pro Segment." A management team naming two specific competitors by acquisition vehicle in its risk factors is unusual. Home Depot agreed to acquire GMS for approximately $5.5B and has established an "Office of Pro Acceleration" targeting what it sizes as a $700B Pro market; Lowe's agreed to acquire FBM for approximately $8.8B in cash.

The overlap is partial. GMS is drywall, ceilings and steel framing; FBM is interior products and stucco; SRS is roofing, landscape and pool. None manufactures roof trusses, floor trusses or wall panels at scale, and none has a Ready-Frame equivalent. BLDR's head-to-head competitors remain U.S. LBM, 84 Lumber, Carter Lumber and the truss specialists. But roughly half of BLDR's revenue — specialty products plus lumber and sheet goods, 50.8% of FY2025 sales — is directly contestable by a well-capitalized specialty distributor, and a unified Pro fulfillment platform can bid aggressively on the commodity half to capture account relationships. Whether that is already showing up in BLDR's gross margin cannot be separated from volume deleverage using disclosed data.

The asymmetry that concerns us most is timing. The big boxes have committed roughly $14B combined to Pro-channel M&A across 2024-2026 while BLDR's acquisition spend fell 97% year over year, its footprint shrank by ~20 locations, and its capex guidance was cut. BLDR's historic competitive weapon was buying regional dealers at attractive prices and layering value-added manufacturing onto them. That weapon is unavailable at 4x leverage, and it is unavailable precisely when regional assets are cheapest.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment (highest unpriced risk).** $4,150M of goodwill equals 36.6% of total assets, and goodwill plus intangibles is 130% of book equity. The December 31, 2025 test was quantitative and concluded fair values were "substantially in excess" of carrying amounts. In Q2 2026, following the three-to-two division realignment, the company performed qualitative assessments both before and after reassignment. That sequence is standard interim practice under ASC 350 and we draw no inference about motive. What matters is that the next mandatory annual quantitative test, at December 31, 2026, will run against a forward EBITDA base roughly 30% below the one used a year earlier. This is a dated, identifiable binary.

**ERP implementation.** The migration decision was made in 2022; as of the FY2025 10-K only two pilot markets plus centralized finance and financial reporting had been implemented, with "several years of deployment" remaining. Amortization of capitalized cloud costs began this year ($10.3M in H1, versus zero prior year) against $95.7M of capitalized balances. The 10-K flags potential adverse effects on internal control effectiveness. Disclosure controls were concluded effective at June 30 with no material ICFR changes.

**Unresolved Item 5.02 8-K filed August 7, 2026.** Confirmed to exist but not retrievable. Filed one week after the guidance cut. Could be routine (formalizing the signalled COO transition, the new CHRO's arrangements, a director election) or a senior departure. We assign no direction to it and flag it as an open item.

**Legacy legal tail.** Known and threatened construction-defect claims and asbestos personal-injury suits from acquired businesses, with a reasonable possibility of loss disclosed as not estimable. No screening model captures this.

**Commodity conditioning.** FY2026 FCF guidance is explicitly conditioned on average lumber at $390-410/mbf. Deflation subtracted 2.7% from Q2 sales and compresses reported percentage margins even when dollar margins hold, making margin percentage a noisy signal in both directions.

**Risks that are already priced.** The housing cycle itself is well understood — starts data is public and monthly, and the stock is 50.5% below its 52-week high of $151.03. We do not double-count sector cyclicality.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 59.4% against 50.1% one-year realized is rich by 9.3 points, and the term structure is flat (59.4% near versus 58.2% far), so the market is pricing a wide distribution with no concentration around a single near-dated catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at −1.8% near-term, which does not corroborate a directional bearish view. Open-interest put/call has risen from 1.25 to 1.50 since July 30, and the six-month tenor carries a put/call volume ratio of 2.01 against 0.47 at one month. That combination of balanced near-dated skew with longer-dated put accumulation reads as institutional hedging of a 2027 recovery failure rather than a bet on an imminent break. The nearest expiry prices a ±9.1% move on a name with an 8.0% short interest and 3.1 days to cover, so positioning is neither crowded nor a squeeze constraint. Rich IV against balanced skew argues against buying downside protection here and in favor of reducing the underlying position directly.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($103, +38%)

Housing is in its fourth year of recession and BLDR is the highest-quality asset in the group. The share count has fallen 34% since FY2022 and 48% since 2021, so a mid-cycle recovery to ~$1.9B of Adjusted EBITDA converts into materially higher per-share value than in 2022. Credit markets are unworried: the high-coupon bonds trade at or above par, liquidity is $1.56B, and there is no maturity before 2030, so the company can wait. The OBBBA permanence of 100% bonus depreciation and domestic R&D expensing is a durable cash-tax tailwind already visible in H1 cash taxes of $5.9M versus $62.0M. Management is behaving rationally at the trough — buybacks halted, capex cut, footprint consolidated — which preserves the balance sheet capacity that a recovery requires. The stock rose 11.6% after the guidance cut against a 4.1% S&P move, suggesting the trough was substantially discounted, and a value manager (GoodHaven) added to the position in H1. If the long end normalizes and the 30-year mortgage falls, pent-up single-family demand releases into a supplier base that has taken out capacity, and both volume and margin recover faster than consensus.

### Bear Case ($50, −33%)

Consensus EPS has been cut 26-40% in thirty days and the company has missed three consecutive quarters, so forward estimates carry negative momentum and low reliability. Derived leverage doubled to ~4.1x in six months and H1 operating income of $145M did not cover H1 interest of $151M. Management's own guidance assumes no gross-margin recovery in H2 and further declines in single-family and multi-family starts. Productivity savings of $50-70M are 1.3-1.8% of SG&A and cannot offset the deleverage. The path to lower mortgage rates may be obstructed if the long end stays elevated, so a policy cut may not translate into cheaper mortgages. Home Depot and Lowe's are deploying $14B into the Pro channel while BLDR's M&A spend has fallen 97%, ceding the consolidation window. A further decline in 2027 starts pushes leverage above 5x and makes the December 2026 quantitative goodwill test the mechanism by which the downturn becomes visible in the accounts.

### Trajectory and what changed

Prior reviews scored 4.6 → 4.9 → 4.4 → 4.4 → 4.3 → 4.9, moving from AVOID to a single HOLD on July 24 at $70.55. That upgrade rested on the pace of estimate cuts decelerating and on the then-current next-quarter consensus implying a sequential rebound. Both conditions no longer hold: next-quarter consensus is now $1.07 for the current quarter, and the ninety-day revision is −39.6%. We are lowering the score and the rating while the price is 5.9% *higher* than at the prior review, which is the opposite of penalizing price action. The specific fundamental deteriorations are the guidance reduction, the third consecutive miss, the leverage move to ~4.1x, and H1 interest coverage below 1.0x. Competitive position, growth prospects and management quality all continue their multi-report decline for reasons visible in the filings rather than in the tape.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength rather than immediately at the low. The stock has rallied 12.4% off the July 31 close of $66.44 and 14.7% off the 52-week low of $65.10; the $76-82 band, which contains our $76 base case, the $80.71 analyst mean and the $81.00 median, is the appropriate exit zone. Reduce to a residual position of no more than one-third of a full weight ahead of the Q3 print (late October 2026) and the December 31, 2026 annual goodwill test. Do not use options for downside protection here: near-term IV at 59.4% against 50.1% realized makes protection expensive relative to selling the underlying.

**Scenario targets from the $74.69 basis.**
- Base: **$76** (+1.8%) — 17x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.47, cross-checked at 9.5x EV/EBITDA on FY2027E $1.2B less ~$4.2B net debt.
- Bull: **$103** (+38%) — mid-cycle Adjusted EBITDA ~$1.9B in 2028 at 8.0-8.5x, net debt ~$3.8B, discounted 18 months at 12%.
- Bear: **$50** (−33%) — FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA ~$875M, leverage ~5.2x, goodwill impairment; tail below $40 if impairment coincides with covenant proximity.
- Probability-weighted (20/45/35): **$72.3, −3.2%**, with no dividend.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two).** (1) Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising forward consensus EPS, specifically FY2027 consensus holding at or above $4.47. (2) Company-reported net leverage at or below 3.5x with quarterly interest coverage restored above 2.5x. (3) Reported gross margin at or above 28.5%, the top of guidance, for two consecutive quarters, confirming the compression is deleverage rather than price. (4) A 30-year mortgage rate sustained meaningfully lower, which would change the demand mechanism directly.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE.** All of the above plus resumption of counter-cyclical M&A at $150M+ per half-year, evidencing restored balance-sheet capacity, or a price in the $58-62 range where the base case offers 25-30% upside against a bear case near $50.

**Avoid triggers.** (1) A goodwill impairment charge at the December 31, 2026 test, particularly if accompanied by revolver borrowings above $500M. (2) FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance below $1.0B when issued in February 2027. (3) Excess revolver availability falling below $600M, which would put the $180.1M springing covenant within a plausible two-quarter path. (4) Disclosure of a material weakness tied to the ERP migration.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next scheduled checkpoints are the Q3 2026 print in late October (watch H2 working-capital release against the implied $330-430M requirement, gross margin versus the 27.5-28.5% guide, and whether buybacks resume against the untouched $500M authorization), the FY2026 10-K and annual goodwill test in February 2027, and the contents of the August 7 Item 5.02 8-K when it becomes retrievable.

## Conclusion

BLDR is a good business at the wrong point in its capital cycle. The franchise assets — Ready-Frame, Paradigm, local truss and panel manufacturing, 4% largest-customer concentration — are genuine and will matter when starts recover. The 48% share-count reduction since 2021 means recovery leverage per share is real. Credit markets are not signalling distress, liquidity is ample, and there is no maturity wall before 2030.

None of that resolves the near-term arithmetic. Consensus EPS has been cut 26-40% across every forward period in thirty days, the company has missed three consecutive quarters, derived net leverage has roughly doubled to 4.1x in six months, H1 operating income did not cover H1 interest, and management's own guidance forecasts no gross-margin recovery. At $74.69 the shares trade at 23.6x FY2026 and 16.7x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, a premium to a peer median of 22.7x on the current year and no discount worth paying on the out year, for a company carrying 4x leverage into a downturn its two largest new competitors are using to consolidate. Our probability-weighted value is $72-73 with no dividend to carry the wait, and the mechanism that would deliver the bull case is lower mortgage rates.

Existing holders should trim into the $76-82 band and retain no more than a residual position through the October print and the December goodwill test. Prospective buyers should wait for either $58-62, where the base case offers a genuine margin of safety, or hard evidence that forward estimates have stopped falling. **REDUCE.**
