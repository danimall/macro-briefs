## Executive Summary

Sonic Automotive is a $1.7B market cap franchised dealership operator. The company's EchoPark used-vehicle segment has crossed into profitability after years of investment, generating $49.2M in EBITDA in FY2025 and hitting record results in Q1 2026. Fixed operations (service/parts) continues to grow at 51% gross margins. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.62 beat consensus of $1.41 by 14.9%, and management authorized a new $500M buyback program.

Against these positives sits a set of material risks: $173.8M in franchise asset impairment charges in FY2025 (signaling overpayment on acquisitions), new vehicle GPU compression of 9% with further tariff-driven deterioration expected in H2 2026, leverage of 5.59x debt/equity, and 22.6% short interest — one of the highest readings in the consumer discretionary sector. The stock has rallied 35% from its February 2026 lows near $54 to ~$80, driven by the Q1 beat and EchoPark momentum, but options markets are pricing imminent stress (52.3% near-term IV in backwardation vs. 39.6% HV).

The fundamental picture: one improving segment (EchoPark) and one deteriorating segment (franchise GPU), with the near-term macro environment (tariffs, elevated rates compressing affordability, potential consumer weakness) creating asymmetric downside risk to the franchise business that generates 85% of revenue.

## Company Overview

Sonic Automotive operates 111 franchised dealerships (134 franchises across 24 brands, focused on luxury and mid-line imports), 18 EchoPark used-vehicle retail locations, and 14 powersports stores. Revenue in FY2025 was $15.2B: Franchised Dealerships at $12.9B (85%), EchoPark at $2.1B (14%), and Powersports at $203M (1%).

The company is controlled by the Smith family through a dual-class share structure (Class B shares carry 10 votes each), meaning minority shareholders have no governance influence. CEO David Bruton Smith also serves as Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:** FY2025 revenue grew 6.5% to $15.2B, primarily from acquisitions ($440M deployed). However, GAAP operating income fell 20% to $368M due to $173.8M in impairment charges. Adjusted EBITDA grew 10% to $615M, providing a cleaner view of underlying operations. Net income of $119M compares to $216M in FY2024, reflecting both impairment and a tax rate normalization from 15.7% to 31.3%.

**Cash Flow:** Free cash flow of $417.5M ($567M operating less $150M capex) represents roughly 24% of market cap. This is the company's most compelling financial attribute.

**Balance Sheet:** The 5.59x D/E ratio requires context — auto dealers carry significant floor plan debt ($1.9B) that is self-liquidating as vehicles sell. Excluding floor plan, the ~$1.6B in long-term notes has maturities concentrated in 2026-2031. Total liquidity is $701.6M. Altman Z-Score of 3.15 sits just above the safe threshold.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue of $3.69B (+1% YoY). Adjusted EPS of $1.62 beat consensus of $1.41 by 14.9%. The beat was driven by record gross profit and record EchoPark contribution.

**One-time items distorting comparisons:** The $40M CDK cyber insurance recovery in FY2025 won't recur. The 2024 tax rate of 15.7% was artificially low. Both factors mean FY2025 adjusted metrics still overstate run-rate profitability.

## Growth Analysis

**EchoPark inflection (positive):** The segment went from years of losses to $28.1M segment income and $49.2M EBITDA in FY2025. Combined used vehicle + F&I GPU of $3,484 grew 15% YoY, demonstrating viable unit economics. Management plans to resume geographic expansion in late 2026, targeting 90% U.S. population coverage and eventually 1M+ units annually. If this trajectory holds, EchoPark could contribute $150-200M in EBITDA within 3-4 years.

**Fixed Operations (positive):** Revenue grew 6% same-store with 8% gross profit growth. Warranty revenue surged 20%. This $1.03B gross profit (51% margins) business is structurally advantaged — it requires OEM authorization, has recurring demand, and is less cyclical than vehicle sales. Sonic is investing in technician hiring to expand capacity.

**New Vehicle GPU compression (negative):** Same-store new vehicle gross profit per unit fell 9% to $3,094 in FY2025, and management guided $2,700-3,000 for 2026 — further deterioration. Industry inventory normalization and increased price competition, particularly on EVs, are structural headwinds. Tariffs on imported vehicles (Sonic's brand mix skews luxury/import) could exacerbate compression or reduce volume.

**Estimate trajectory (mixed):** Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 5% over 90 days (from $7.02 to $6.67), though they've stabilized and ticked up 1.6% over 30 days post-Q1 beat. This is not the strong positive revision momentum that characterizes high-conviction BUY candidates. At $6.67 current-year and $7.33 next-year consensus, the stock trades at ~12x current and ~11x next year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.13, SAH trades at roughly 12x FY2026E EPS ($6.67) and 11x FY2027E ($7.33). The trailing P/E of 25.4x reflects depressed GAAP earnings due to the impairment charge.

**Peer comparison:** The most relevant direct peers are Asbury Automotive (ABG) at 7.0x and Group 1 Automotive (GPI) at 13.3x. SAH's ~12x forward P/E sits between these, which seems fair given EchoPark optionality (justifying a premium to ABG) but also higher execution risk and leverage (justifying a discount to GPI). The peer median of 21.9x reflects different business models (PHIN, AAP) and isn't directly comparable.

**FCF yield:** At $417.5M FCF vs. $1.75B market cap, the FCF yield is approximately 24%. This is attractive on the surface, but auto dealer FCF is volatile — it depends heavily on floor plan changes and working capital. Normalized FCF is likely $250-300M (14-17% yield), which is still substantial.

**Valuation summary:** The 12x multiple on earnings that may face tariff-driven deterioration in H2 2026 is reasonable but not obviously mispriced. The stock has already rallied 35% from its February lows, partially pricing in the Q1 beat and EchoPark trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonic's three-segment model (franchise + EchoPark + powersports) is unique among public auto retailers. AutoNation, Penske, Lithia, Asbury, and Group 1 are all primarily franchise operators. Carvana occupies the online-only used car space but at vastly different scale economics.

**Franchise competitive position (stable/declining):** Sonic's luxury/import brand mix exposes it more to tariff risk than peers weighted toward domestic brands. New vehicle GPU compression is industry-wide, but Sonic's import concentration creates above-average vulnerability. The $173.8M impairment signals that some recently acquired franchises are worth less than purchase price.

**EchoPark competitive position (improving):** After rightsizing in 2023-2024 (closing underperforming stores), EchoPark has found unit economics. The combined GPU of $3,484 at 18 locations provides a proof-of-concept for broader expansion. The key competitive question is whether EchoPark can scale profitably — Carvana's history shows how quickly used-car retail expansion can destroy value.

**Fixed Operations moat:** Service and parts represent a genuine competitive moat. Franchise authorization, technician expertise, and OEM diagnostic tools create barriers. This segment's 51% gross margins and counter-cyclical demand characteristics provide earnings stability that the market may not fully credit.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (HIGH):** Sonic's luxury/import brand concentration makes it disproportionately vulnerable to auto tariffs. Management's GPU guidance of $2,700-3,000 (vs. $3,094 in FY2025) already incorporates some compression, but the range is wide enough to suggest uncertainty. If tariffs reduce volume, the fixed-cost base in franchise operations amplifies margin impact.

**Short interest (HIGH — 22.6%):** This is a very elevated reading for a company with a $1.75B market cap. Days to cover of 7.7 means any sustained buying pressure could trigger a squeeze, but the elevated short interest also reflects informed skepticism about the company's ability to maintain earnings in a tariff/recession environment. Short sellers are typically right about deteriorating fundamentals more often than they're right about timing.

**Leverage and refinancing:** $1.6B in long-term debt with maturities concentrated 2026-2031 means refinancing in a high-rate environment. If rates don't decline materially, interest costs could rise significantly on rollover.

**EchoPark execution risk:** The plan to expand to 90% U.S. population coverage is ambitious. The 2023-2024 store closures demonstrate that expansion can go wrong. Capital deployment into EchoPark competes with debt reduction and buybacks.

**One-time item normalization:** FY2025 benefited from $40M in CDK insurance proceeds and FY2024 from a $31M tax adjustment. Neither recurs. Base earnings power is lower than reported.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term risk: 52.3% ATM IV vs. 39.6% historical volatility (12.7pp rich), with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 52.3% vs. 6-month 34.5%). Put skew of 5.4% confirms elevated put demand. The market is pricing a ±6.2% move in the next week — roughly $5/share. Combined with 22.6% short interest, this indicates that sophisticated participants see meaningful binary risk ahead, potentially related to tariff implementation timing or early consumer demand data for imported vehicles.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-100):**
- EchoPark continues its profitability inflection, with expansion resuming and segment EBITDA growing toward $80-100M by FY2027
- Fixed Operations growth at 6-8% annually compounds into a structurally advantaged earnings base
- $500M buyback authorization reduces share count by 15-20% over 2-3 years
- Tariff impact proves manageable (luxury buyers less price-sensitive; volume holds)
- Stock re-rates toward peer multiples (13-14x forward = $87-100)
- Paul Rusnak's $5.8M open-market purchase at $60 proves prescient

**Bear Case ($55-60):**
- Tariffs compress new vehicle GPU below $2,700 and reduce volume 5-10%
- Consumer weakness (elevated rates) disproportionately hits big-ticket auto purchases
- EchoPark expansion consumes capital without scaling profitably (Carvana-like dynamic)
- Additional franchise impairment charges in FY2026 as recently acquired assets underperform
- Short thesis proves correct: earnings power is $4.50-5.50, not $6.50-7.50, putting fair value at 10-12x = $45-66

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 results (likely late July) confirm new vehicle GPU stabilizing at or above $2,800 with volume holding despite tariffs
2. EchoPark expansion announcement includes specific store count targets with demonstrated path to profitability at each new location
3. Stock pulls back to $65-70 range on broader consumer discretionary weakness while estimates remain stable

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Next quarter shows new vehicle GPU below $2,700 with volume declining >5% YoY
2. EchoPark profitability reverses or management announces expansion delays beyond late 2026
3. Additional franchise impairment charges >$50M indicating systemic overpayment problem

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) is the natural next assessment point, when tariff impacts on volume and margin will be directly observable in results.

## Conclusion

Sonic Automotive combines a genuine operating improvement (EchoPark profitability, Fixed Operations growth) with elevated macro/industry risk (tariffs on imported vehicles, consumer affordability pressure, GPU normalization). The valuation at ~12x forward earnings is reasonable but not compellingly cheap given the headwinds, and the stock has already rallied 35% from its lows.

The 22.6% short interest and backwardated options term structure indicate that informed participants see material downside risk. Paul Rusnak's $5.8M open-market purchase at $60 provides a strong contrarian signal, but the CEO and President have not been buyers. The consumer discretionary sector — particularly auto retail — faces an unusually uncertain H2 2026 given tariff implementation, and Sonic's import-brand concentration amplifies this exposure relative to peers.

I rate SAH a **HOLD** at current levels. The EchoPark inflection is genuine and creates asymmetric upside optionality over a 2-3 year horizon, but the near-term earnings trajectory is uncertain, the current price reflects much of the Q1 beat, and the macro environment for big-ticket consumer purchases is deteriorating. Waiting for tariff impact clarity in Q2 results before establishing a position is the appropriate risk-management approach.
