**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NOV is the largest independent equipment and technology provider to the global energy industry — the "Switzerland" of oilfield equipment that arms service companies, national oil companies, drilling contractors, and shipyards rather than competing vertically with them. The business carries a conservative, investment-grade balance sheet (debt-to-cap ~24%, current ratio 2.42), a large OEM installed base generating recurring aftermarket revenue, and a backlog tilted toward the offshore/international markets management views as the marginal barrel. The franchise quality is real.

The 2025 results, however, were poor: revenue fell 1.4% to $8.74B while net income collapsed 77% to $145M and operating income dropped 44% to $494M. The headline collapse overstates operational deterioration — roughly two-thirds is attributable to a tax effective rate jumping to 59.7% (valuation-allowance build against foreign tax credits, unfavorable foreign mix, nondeductible goodwill impairment) and to ~$183M of one-time charges (impairments, severance, royalty-receivable discount). Adjusted EBITDA fell only 7% to $1.03B, and free cash flow held at $876M, a 10% FCF margin. The cleaner operating story is a divergence: Energy Equipment grew (offshore +9%, adjusted profit +16%) while Energy Products & Services contracted (margin from 11.5% to 7.0%) on weak North American land activity and tariff/inflation pressure.

The reason to remain cautious rather than constructive is forward-looking. Backlog has declined three consecutive periods ($4.43B → $4.34B → $4.23B), Q4'25 book-to-bill ran 73% and Q1'26 80% (drawing down faster than replenishing), Q1'26 operating cash flow turned negative (-$26M), the company has missed EPS estimates four straight quarters (the last two by 57% and 68%), and consensus current-year EPS estimates have fallen 21% over 90 days. The bullish thesis hinges on a Middle East-driven upstream capex recovery that has not yet shown up in orders. At 18.26 the stock trades near where management has been buying back, with a supportive dividend, but without an identifiable catalyst to reverse the negative estimate momentum. This is a hold on franchise and balance-sheet quality, with no asymmetric upside today.

## Company Overview

NOV (formerly National Oilwell Varco) operates through two roughly equal segments. **Energy Products and Services** (~$3.98B, 45% of revenue) is the shorter-cycle, transactional business: drill bits, downhole and completion tools, drill pipe, artificial lift, tubular inspection and coating, solids control, and the Max digital platform. It is more tied to drilling activity, particularly North American land. **Energy Equipment** (~$4.93B, 55%) is the longer-cycle capital-equipment and aftermarket franchise: land rigs, offshore drilling packages, intervention and stimulation systems, marine and construction (including wind-installation vessels), process systems, subsea flexible pipe, and FPSO turret/mooring systems. This segment is backlog-driven and heavily offshore and international.

The competitive differentiation rests on scale in global procurement and manufacturing, an OEM installed base that creates sticky aftermarket and spare-parts revenue, fleet standardization, a capital-light model, and a conservative balance sheet that reassures customers committing to multi-year equipment programs. The business operates in 57 countries from roughly 503 locations with ~31,600 employees, and roughly 66% of revenue is international. Adjacent optionality includes geothermal, offshore wind installation, carbon capture, and a nascent advanced-nuclear supply-chain effort.

A leadership transition is in progress and worth flagging for any model reading structured data. The proxy still lists long-time CEO Clay Williams, but Form 4s and multiple Item 5.02 8-Ks confirm Jose A. Bayardo (former CFO) is now Chairman, President & CEO, with Rodney C. Reed as CFO. Bayardo led the Q4'25 and Q1'26 earnings calls.

## Financial Analysis

**Capital structure is genuinely conservative.** Total debt is roughly flat at ~$1.7B, debt-to-cap is ~24%, D/E is 0.79, and the current ratio is 2.42. The $1.5B revolver (undrawn, expandable to $2.5B) was extended to September 2030. Altman Z sits at 2.82 (grey zone); the solvency question is mild, and the issue is profitability and returns rather than balance-sheet risk.

**The 2025 earnings bridge:**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 7.24 | 8.58 | 8.87 | 8.74 (-1.4%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 264 | 651 | 876 | 494 (-43.6%) |
| Net income ($M) | 155 | 993* | 635 | 145 (-77%) |
| Adj. EBITDA ($M) | — | — | 1,110 | 1,029 (-7%) |
| Diluted shares (M) | 394 | 397 | 396 | 375 |

*2023 net income included a ~$373M valuation-allowance release.

Three drivers explain the net-income drop from $635M to $145M. First, the effective tax rate jumped to 59.7% from 23.6%, driven by a valuation-allowance increase ($266M → $352M) against foreign tax-credit carryforwards and foreign DTAs, plus unfavorable foreign earnings mix and nondeductible goodwill impairment — the single largest swing. Second, pre-tax "Other Items" swung ~$292M unfavorably, from a +$109M net credit in 2024 (a $130M Pole Products divestiture gain) to a -$183M charge in 2025 (goodwill/long-lived impairment $70M including $40M Renewables; severance/facility closures $89M; $24M royalty-receivable timing discount; Russia deconsolidation $5M). Third, margin compression from Section 232 steel tariffs and inflation hit Energy Products & Services hardest.

**Cash generation quality is the offset.** Operating cash flow was $1.25B in 2025 and FCF $876M, materially exceeding GAAP net income — a quality signal that supports the view that the GAAP collapse overstates operational damage. The franchise returned $190M in dividends and $315M in buybacks (22.8M shares) in 2025, with share count falling from 396M to 375M.

**Q1'26 showed continued deterioration.** Revenue fell 2.4% YoY to $2.05B, net income dropped to $19M from $73M, diluted EPS was $0.05 versus $0.19, and operating profit fell to $47M from $152M. Adjusted EBITDA was $177M (8.6% margin) versus $252M. Operating cash flow turned negative at -$26M (versus +$135M) on an inventory build and lower accruals. The Strait of Hormuz disruption added delivery delays, higher freight, and aftermarket softness (aftermarket -12%). The effective tax rate remained elevated at 42.9%.

Cash flow quality and conservative leverage support a financial-health score above the sector trough, but the negative Q1 operating cash flow, structurally elevated and volatile tax rate, and four straight EPS misses keep the dimension below the level that the balance sheet alone would suggest.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest part of the story and the dimension carrying the most reliable predictive weight. Three signals all point the same direction.

**Backlog is eroding.** Energy Equipment backlog declined three consecutive periods: $4.43B (YE24) → $4.34B (YE25) → $4.23B (Q1'26). Book-to-bill ran 73% in Q4'25 (orders $532M versus $757M a year earlier) and 80% in Q1'26. Backlog is being drawn down faster than replenished, the clearest forward warning available for a backlog-driven business.

**Estimate revisions are negative and broad.** Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 28.6% over 90 days, current-year fell 21.0%, and next-quarter fell 8.8%. Only next-year estimates are marginally positive (+1.4%). Revenue is flat-to-down rather than accelerating, and estimates are falling rather than rising.

**The earnings track record undermines confidence in consensus.** NOV has missed EPS four straight quarters, with the two most recent misses at 56.6% and 68.1%. A company that consistently misses warrants lower confidence in forward estimates, not higher.

The bull narrative — offshore and international as the marginal barrel, deepwater and gas for LNG and data-center power demand, digitalization — is structurally coherent, and the backlog mix (58% offshore, 94% international) is positioned for it. Adjusted Energy Equipment profit grew 16% in 2025, confirming the offshore franchise is healthier than the consolidated numbers. But these are medium-term theses; the near-term operating reality is softening rig counts, a North American land market in decline, and orders that have not yet inflected. Management's $100M annualized cost-out program for 2026 is a defensive response to expectations of slightly lower upstream spending, not a growth lever.

## Valuation Assessment

NOV trades at ~$18.26 with a market cap of ~$6.6B. Trailing P/E of 75.7x is meaningless at a trough, and because this is a cyclical trough driven by tax and charges rather than a clean earnings inflection, the cycle-adjusted case is weaker than it would be for a name with rising estimates. On the consensus current-year EPS of $0.79 the forward P/E is ~23x; on next-year $1.27 it is ~14.4x. The $0.79 figure has fallen 21% in 90 days and the company has missed four straight quarters, so the forward multiple sits on estimates with poor reliability.

Against peers in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, the median P/E is 30.8x (AROC 19.6, WFRD 15.8, KGS 88.7, LBRT 30.8, WHD 55.0). NOV's forward multiples screen reasonable to cheap versus that dispersed set, but the peer group is heterogeneous and several peers (KGS, WHD) carry their own elevated multiples. The cleaner anchor is FCF: $876M of 2025 free cash flow against a $6.6B market cap is a ~13% FCF yield, the strongest element of the valuation case. The dividend yield of ~1.9% (recently raised to $0.09/quarter) adds modest absolute-yield support.

The analyst price-target range is $17 low to $26 high, mean $21.80, median $22.00 — implying ~19% upside to the mean. These targets sit alongside falling estimates and a "fairly valued" tone from Seeking Alpha coverage, so they do not function as a floor-style signal in the way they would for inflection names with rising estimates. Cheapness here is the kind that has historically marked value traps absent an independent catalyst, and the clearest near-term catalyst — a Middle East-driven order recovery — has not materialized in the backlog. There is genuine optionality in tariff refund claims (the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs, with NOV refund claims filed but no benefit booked), partly offset by replacement tariffs. The valuation is balanced: supported by FCF and yield, undermined by negative estimate momentum and the absence of a re-rating trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

NOV's competitive position is stable-to-improving in trajectory even as the cyclical backdrop softens. Its identity as the independent OEM that supplies the entire industry — rather than competing as an integrated service company against SLB, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes — drives end-market fragmentation, limits customer concentration, and supports a sticky aftermarket revenue stream from a large installed base.

The Energy Equipment segment is gaining where it matters for the structural thesis: offshore revenue grew 9% and international 4% in 2025, with the backlog migrating to 94% international and 58% offshore. That mix positions the franchise for the offshore-as-marginal-barrel and gas-for-power demand themes. The five-year TSR (123) has lagged the peer group (182), reflecting that the structural positioning has not yet translated into shareholder returns. The technology paradox is a genuine long-term competitive headwind: efficiency gains can reduce equipment demand over time as fewer rigs produce the same output. On balance the moat is intact and the offshore franchise is strengthening, but the North American product business is exposed to a declining land market and tariff-driven margin pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Tax and valuation-allowance fragility** is the single largest near-term earnings risk and it is non-operational. The $352M valuation allowance and elevated effective tax rate could grow further, as the company warns that additional taxable-income shortfalls "may require an additional valuation allowance." Open disputes include the IRS 2017 restructuring (~$48M), Canada 2016–18 (~$31M), and Denmark transfer pricing ($51M paid, under appeal).

**Backlog erosion and cyclicality** remain the core operating risk. Demand keys directly off rig counts and oil/gas capex; sub-100% book-to-bill means backlog is being consumed. Contracts carry cost-overrun, fixed-price, and liquidated-damages exposure.

**Royalty litigation** is an underappreciated binary. A ~$137M net royalty receivable (Grant Prideco leaching patents against Schlumberger, Halliburton, Baker Hughes and others) rests on Federal Circuit appeals after adverse September/October 2025 district-court rulings. An unfavorable outcome could force further write-downs.

**Geopolitical concentration** cuts both ways. With 66% of revenue and 94% of backlog international, the Strait of Hormuz disruption is currently a margin headwind (delivery delays, freight, aftermarket softness) but a potential medium-term capex tailwind. The Q1'26 10-Q data point of WTI at $94.40 on April 24, 2026 — up 31% from the Q1 average — alongside a 10% North American rig-count decline illustrates that the oil-price move reflects a supply shock, not demand strength, so it does not automatically translate into orders.

Additional items: $1.6B goodwill with $40M Renewables impairment already taken (further triggers possible in a downturn); Russia deconsolidation with a pending sale and potential severance/litigation exposure; tariff uncertainty after the Supreme Court ruling, with replacement tariffs introduced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 40.6% sits roughly fair to the 38.8% one-year historical vol (1.8pp spread), but the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 40.6% above long-term 38.1%) and the put/call skew is elevated at 17.9%, indicating demand for downside protection. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.8%. Since the prior snapshot, IV ticked down slightly (41.6% → 40.6%) while price fell 11.1% and the put/call OI ratio rose (0.17 → 0.25). Combined with 15.4% short interest of float and 7.2 days to cover, positioning is meaningfully bearish, consistent with the falling-estimate, missing-earnings fundamental picture rather than contradicting it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$26, +42%).** A Middle East-driven energy-security capex cycle reignites international and offshore orders, reversing the book-to-bill below 100%. The tax drag normalizes as foreign earnings mix improves and valuation allowances reverse. Tariff refund claims convert to cash. The $100M cost-out program lifts margins as activity recovers. With backlog inflecting, the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range on its ~13% FCF yield.

**Bear case (~$14, -23%).** Backlog continues to draw down as orders fail to inflect, North American land weakens further, and the Strait disruption persists as a margin headwind without the offsetting capex recovery. The tax rate stays structurally elevated, requiring additional valuation allowances. The Federal Circuit upholds the adverse royalty rulings, forcing a write-down of the ~$137M receivable. Negative operating cash flow in Q1'26 proves to be demand softness rather than working-capital timing. Estimates continue falling and the multiple compresses on trough earnings with no catalyst.

**Base case (~$19–20).** The franchise muddles through a soft cycle with FCF and dividend support and continued buybacks near current levels, but no order inflection and persistent tax drag cap appreciation. Estimates stabilize at depressed levels and the stock trades in a range around the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The franchise quality, conservative balance sheet, FCF yield, and capital return argue against an AVOID, while three consecutive backlog declines, four straight earnings misses, falling estimates, and negative Q1 operating cash flow argue against a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill recovers above 100% for two consecutive quarters, halting and reversing the backlog decline.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive, signaling the order/capex inflection has begun.
- The effective tax rate normalizes below ~30% with no further valuation-allowance build, or tariff refund claims convert to booked cash benefit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss with continued backlog erosion below ~$4.0B.
- Operating cash flow remains negative for a second consecutive quarter, confirming demand softness rather than timing.
- An adverse Federal Circuit ruling forcing a material write-down of the royalty receivable, combined with a goodwill-impairment trigger.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings is the natural checkpoint, with the backlog/book-to-bill figure and tax-rate guidance the key data points. The Federal Circuit royalty appeal and any tariff-refund developments are additional dated catalysts to monitor.

## Conclusion

NOV is a high-quality, conservatively financed franchise trading near a cyclical trough where the GAAP earnings collapse materially overstates operational damage. The offshore/international Energy Equipment franchise is genuinely strengthening, and management's capital-return discipline (raised dividend, continued buybacks at $16–20, revolver extension) signals confidence in the cyclical-recovery thesis. These are reasons not to sell.

What prevents a constructive call is the forward evidence: backlog has declined three consecutive periods, book-to-bill remains below 100%, estimates continue falling, the company has missed four straight quarters, and Q1'26 operating cash flow turned negative. The bull thesis depends on a Middle East-driven upstream capex recovery that has not yet appeared in orders, and the elevated, volatile tax rate is a persistent cash and EPS headwind unrelated to operations. The balanced valuation is the classic value-trap profile that requires an independent catalyst to break. Until book-to-bill inflects above 100% and estimate revisions turn positive, the risk/reward is symmetric. HOLD on franchise and balance-sheet quality, not for appreciation.
