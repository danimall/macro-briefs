## Executive Summary

Insperity is a professional employer organization whose economics reduce to a single spread: the price it charges small and mid-sized clients for a bundled benefits-and-HR package versus the medical claims and workers' compensation losses it actually incurs. In FY2025 that spread inverted. Benefit cost per covered employee rose 9.2% while realized pricing rose approximately 3%, and on a large benefits cost base the six-point gap took gross profit from $1,052M to $900M, adjusted EBITDA from $270M to $131M, and GAAP EPS to negative $0.19. Worksite employee (WSEE) volume was not the cause; average WSEEs grew 0.9% and revenue grew 4%.

Management's response has been the correct one for an underwriting business: reprice at renewal even at the cost of volume, cut the retained claims limit from $1 million to $500,000 for 2026, take a $9M restructuring charge, and reduce sales, service and support headcount 6%. The evidence that it is working is real. Benefit cost trend moderated from +9.2% for FY2025 to +5% in Q2 2026. H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA reached $139M, up 4%. FY2026 guidance of $185-225M adjusted EBITDA narrowed with the midpoint edging up from the February range of $170-230M. The CEO bought $15.5M of stock in the open market between March and June, with the largest and highest-priced tranche executed last, and the CFO, general counsel and two directors bought alongside him.

The problem is price. The equity has risen roughly 180% from its 52-week low of $18.57 and sits about 9% below its 52-week high of $57.22. At $51.98 it trades at 11.3x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA (a measure that adds back stock compensation and cash SaaS implementation spend), 24.1x guided adjusted EPS, and roughly 50x guided GAAP EPS. Consensus for next year has been cut 6.7% over 90 days to $2.70, so the multiple is not compressing against rising estimates. The WSEE base is guided to shrink 1.0-1.6%. The dividend costs $91.6M against guided adjusted EPS of $2.155 (111% payout) and guided GAAP EPS near $1.04 (roughly 232%), and it coexists with borrowings that rose from $370M to $419M, an incremental $50M Q2 draw for working capital, adjusted (corporate) cash of $95M, book equity of $61M, and a 2025 covenant amendment that excluded dividends from the interest coverage test and raised the maximum permitted leverage ratio. Probability-weighting a $57 base, $70 bull and $30 bear produces a 12-month total return of roughly 6-10% including the dividend, against a bear case 42% below spot. That asymmetry does not support owning the position at this price.

## Company Overview

Insperity co-employs the workforces of small and mid-sized businesses. It becomes the employer of record for payroll, employment tax, benefits and workers' compensation while the client directs day-to-day work, and it bills a bundled service fee covering WSEE payroll, payroll taxes, health and welfare benefits, workers' compensation coverage and HR services. FY2025 revenue of $6,812M is reported gross of WSEE payroll, which is why the 13.2% gross margin ($900M ÷ $6,812M) is not a margin in any conventional sense. The meaningful unit is gross profit per WSEE per month, which was $237 in Q2 2026, down 1% year over year and down roughly 15% from a derived FY2024 level near $285.

Approximately 85% of health plan costs run through an arrangement with UnitedHealthcare under which Insperity retains claims risk per covered participant up to an annually elected Selected Claims Limit. Pricing is set at enrollment or renewal; claims accrue continuously. A substantial part of reported profit is therefore an underwriting spread with an annual repricing cycle and a one-carrier concentration.

Offerings span bundled PEO products (Workforce Optimization and the lower-priced Select Edition tier, roughly 16% of WSEEs) plus non-PEO payroll (HRCore), retirement and insurance services. Distribution runs through a direct salesforce of Business Performance Advisors and channel referrals. The stated growth vector is HRScale, a full-service SMB HR solution jointly developed and marketed with Workday, under which Insperity licenses Workday SaaS and pays implementation costs ($20M in FY2025, $29M in FY2024) while retaining the client relationship, service obligation and billing.

## Financial Analysis

### The FY2025 break and its mechanism

| $M unless noted | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | — | ~6,550 | 6,812 (+4%) |
| Gross profit | — | 1,052 | 900 (−14%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 354 | 270 | 131 (−51%) |
| Net income | 171 | 91 | (7) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | (0.19) |
| Avg. WSEEs | — | ~307,300 | 310,089 (+0.9%) |

The adjusted EBITDA series is independently corroborated by the Compensation Committee's certified LTIP figures in the proxy. The causal chain is explicit in management's own MD&A: benefit cost per covered employee +9.2%, realized pricing approximately +3%, no variable offset inside cost of services, so the spread miss flowed directly to gross profit. Two secondary drags compounded it: Workday/HRScale investment largely expensed in FY2025, and a fading workers' compensation reserve-release tailwind discussed under Risk.

### Cash flow and the pass-through artifact

FY2025 operating cash flow of approximately negative $278M and a decline in total cash from $1,039M to $642M look like a solvency event on a screen. The dominant driver disclosed in the 10-K is the early-2025 distribution of roughly $440M of Employee Retention Credit funds Insperity held on behalf of clients at year-end 2024. Those were client funds in transit; receiving them inflated 2024 and remitting them deflated 2025. Similarly, book equity of $46M (12/31/25) and $61M (6/30/26) against $6.8B of revenue makes debt/equity and Altman-style readings arbitrarily severe, because two decades of buybacks and a dividend exceeding recent earnings have left equity near zero by design while total assets and liabilities are dominated by client payroll float and insurance reserves.

The correct liquidity measure is adjusted cash, excluding client-related funds: $57M at 12/31/2025, $134M a year earlier, and $95M at 6/30/2026.

### Capital structure

The $750M committed revolver had $370M drawn at year-end 2025 and $419M of borrowings plus $1M of letters of credit at 6/30/2026, leaving $330M unused, maturing December 15, 2028, secured by the stock of the captive insurance subsidiary with subsidiary guarantees. The facility was amended during 2025 to exclude dividends from the interest coverage ratio covenant and to increase the maximum permitted leverage ratio; the company reports compliance at 6/30/2026. Two 8-Ks (November 3 and December 16, 2025, the latter with Item 2.03) are consistent with that amendment activity, though the amendment exhibit itself was not read and the specific covenant levels and current headroom are unverified.

The reasonable inference is that the original package would have been strained at FY2025 EBITDA. The counter-inference is equally available: lenders routinely modernize definitions, and carving a long-standing dividend out of a coverage test may simply codify how lenders already viewed it. Either way, the practical constraint is the same. With $419M drawn, $95M of adjusted cash and $61M of book equity, Insperity has limited capacity to absorb a second FY2025 without cutting the dividend or renegotiating again.

### 2026: recovery with a back-half skew

Q2 2026: revenue $1,686M (+2%); average WSEEs 305,764 (−1%); gross profit $217M (−3%) at $237 per WSEE per month; benefit cost per covered employee +5%; operating expenses −8%; net income $4M; adjusted EBITDA $36M (+13%). H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA $139M (+4%).

FY2026 guidance: adjusted EBITDA $185-225M, adjusted EPS $1.88-2.43, GAAP EPS approximately $0.76-1.31, average WSEEs 305,000-307,000 (implying −1.0% to −1.6%). Q3 guidance is $14-41M adjusted EBITDA.

The seasonality arithmetic matters. The $205M midpoint less $139M of H1 actual implies H2 2026 of roughly $66M, with the guided range implying $46-86M. At the Q3 midpoint of $27.5M, implied Q4 is roughly $38.5M. Front-loaded payroll-tax gross profit makes an H2 step-down normal; H1 2025 adjusted EBITDA was $134M against FY2025 of $131M, implying H2 2025 near negative $3M. Guided H2 2026 of ~$66M therefore requires a roughly $69M year-over-year swing that has not yet been demonstrated in a reported quarter. The election to lower the Selected Claims Limit to $500,000 for 2026 reinforces the skew: its cost is recognized evenly while the recovery benefit builds through the year.

At the low end of guidance the dividend is essentially fully absorbed.

## Growth Analysis

There is an earnings recovery here, and there is very little growth.

Revenue grew 2% in Q2 on a 1% WSEE decline, meaning growth is pricing and wage inflation. Guidance embeds a full-year WSEE decline. Gross profit fell 3% and gross profit per WSEE fell 1%, so the unit economics are stabilizing rather than expanding. Client retention improved from 81% to 83%, which cuts against a pure attrition story and is consistent with existing clients simply employing fewer people. The 10-Q attributes WSEE declines "in part" to margin recovery efforts without decomposing the balance, and Q4 2025 commentary referenced persistently weak client net hiring. That decomposition is decision-relevant: repricing-driven attrition ends when repricing completes, employment-driven shrinkage does not.

Middle-market WSEEs grew 10% year over year to 29% of the total. That is mix shift, not diversification, and it is arithmetically consistent with faster attrition in small accounts. Middle-market and Select Edition accounts also carry lower gross profit per WSEE, so a favorable mix narrative and falling gross profit per WSEE can coexist.

HRScale is the stated multi-year vector. Third-party coverage of the Q2 call reports nearly 8,000 sold worksite employees, roughly 2.6% of the base, but that figure is unconfirmed in any filing or company release, "sold" is not "onboarded" or "revenue-generating," and no HRScale revenue or gross profit contribution has been disclosed. It cannot carry a growth thesis today.

Estimate revisions are mixed and, on the horizon that matters, negative. Current-year consensus rose 2.9% over 30 days and 1.6% over 90 days to $2.11, tracking the guidance narrowing. Next-quarter consensus jumped 113.5% off a low $0.12 base, which reflects the Q3 guidance bridge rather than an inflection. Next-year consensus fell 3.5% over 30 days and 6.7% over 90 days to $2.70. Current-quarter consensus fell 9.1%. The estimate-revision pattern that supports buying inflections (large upward revisions against a low forward multiple) is absent here; the out-year is being marked down while the multiple has expanded.

The earnings-surprise record is inconsistent: +5.9% and +5.1% in the last two quarters against −27.5% and −188.9% in the two before. Two beats and two misses in four quarters argue for moderate rather than high confidence in the H2 bridge.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer medians in HR and employment services are a P/E of 25.4 and a 2.6% yield (MAN 25.4, KFY 16.1, RHI 37.2, FA 165.3, UPWK 12.1). NSP at 24.6x current-year consensus sits at the peer median while shrinking its unit base, and at 19.3x next-year consensus on estimates that are being cut. Neither reading identifies a mispricing.

The bull framing is EV/EBITDA on normalized earnings power. Adjusted EBITDA was $354M in 2023 and $270M in 2024 against $205M guided for 2026. If the benefit spread normalizes toward historical levels, $250-280M is plausible by 2027-2028, and 11x on $270M less roughly $250M of net debt implies about $70 per share. That case requires two things the filings do not yet support: that 2023's $354M is a valid baseline rather than a period of depressed post-pandemic medical utilization plus $33M of workers' compensation reserve releases, and that the WSEE base stops shrinking so that normalization applies to a stable denominator.

The base case is more modest. FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $2.70 at 19-21x gives $51-57. An EV/EBITDA route of $240M × 10.5x less ~$300M net debt gives roughly $58. A 4.5% dividend yield floor, if the dividend holds, supports about $53. Triangulating, base fair value is **$57**, roughly 10% above spot, with the 4.62% yield taking a 12-month total return to roughly 14% if the base case lands.

Bear case: a second year of benefit-trend miss, or demand-driven WSEE decline that accelerates, returns adjusted EBITDA toward $140M and forces a dividend decision. At 9x on $140M less $400M of net debt the equity is worth roughly $22, and a dividend cut would remove the yield-based floor that currently underpins the multiple. The stock traded at $18.57 five months ago on exactly this fear set. **Bear $30**, 42% below spot.

Probability-weighting base 55% / bull 20% / bear 25% gives $52.85 plus $2.40 of dividends, a 6.3% total return. Shifting to base 55% / bull 25% / bear 20% gives 10.1%. Against 66% trailing realized volatility and a 42% downside tail, that expected return is below fair. Spot is also 0.4% above the $51.75 analyst mean, with the recommendation distribution at one buy, three holds and one strong sell, and a target range of $38-61. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap, accelerating stories with rising estimates; this is a median-multiple story with falling out-year estimates, so the mean target is better read as a fair-value marker than as a launch pad.

The valuation score of 4.5 reflects the absence of an identifiable mispricing rather than an objection to prior appreciation. If the stock were at $36 with the same guidance, the same arithmetic would produce a materially different score.

## Competitive Landscape

Direct PEO competition comes from TriNet plus the PEO divisions of ADP and Paychex. The asymmetry FY2025 exposed is structural: ADP and Paychex can absorb a benefit-cost shock inside a much larger diversified P&L, while for Insperity PEO gross profit is the entire P&L. A six-point spread miss cost Insperity half its adjusted EBITDA and its GAAP profitability; the same claims trend inside a diversified HCM business is a segment footnote.

The competitive trajectory is currently negative on the metrics that matter. The WSEE base is contracting. Business Performance Advisor, service and support headcount is down 6%, which reduces the capacity to rebuild an installed base that is acquired one salesforce relationship at a time, and to distribute HRScale. Gross profit per WSEE at $237 is roughly 15% below the derived FY2024 level near $285. Retention improved two points, which is a genuine offset and suggests the repricing is being absorbed by existing clients rather than driving them out.

Whether the 2025 shock was industry-wide or Insperity-specific is unresolved, and it is the most important open external question. No verified TriNet, ADP TotalSource or Paychex PEO benefit-trend datapoint was obtained. Insperity's own filings describe elevated claims utilization and cost per covered employee, a claims-trend phenomenon rather than a pricing failure confined to one cohort. If it was cyclical, the appropriate multiple is a trough multiple and the normalization case is credible. If Insperity underwrote worse than peers, the appropriate multiple is permanently lower. Buying the equity at 11.3x on the assumption that it was cyclical, without the peer datapoint, is paying for an answer that is not yet available.

## Risk Assessment

**Benefit-cost underwriting risk (dominant).** FY2025 proved a single year's spread miss can erase more than half of adjusted EBITDA, with approximately 85% of health plan costs concentrated in one carrier arrangement. Mitigations are real (Selected Claims Limit down to $500,000; aggressive renewal repricing) but repricing works with a lag and costs volume.

**Workers' compensation reserve fade.** Favorable prior-year development contributed $29M (2025), $32M (2024) and $33M (2023), roughly 3.2% of FY2025 gross profit. Q2 2026 development was $3M against $8M, YTD $7M against $14M. Simultaneously current-period workers' compensation cost rose 35% year over year to 0.32% of non-bonus payroll from 0.23%. A multi-year tailwind is compressing while accident-year severity rises. Reserve development is lumpy and two quarters do not establish a trend, but the direction of both series is unfavorable and is obscured by the benefit-trend narrative.

**Covenant and liquidity headroom.** $419M drawn, $95M adjusted cash, $61M book equity, December 2028 maturity, and a 2025 amendment that excluded dividends from the interest coverage covenant and raised the leverage cap. A repeat of 2025 forces a harder dividend decision.

**Working capital and insurance funding timing.** The program-cost versus premiums-paid position swung from an $18M liability at 12/31/2025 to a $40M prepaid asset at 6/30/2026, a $58M cash absorption caused by carriers setting 2026 funding rates off 2025 claims experience. Insperity drew $50M on the revolver in Q2 for working capital. This is a P&L non-event that converts thin corporate cash into revolver dependence.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is a policy commitment rather than a residual: $91.6M annually, 111% of guided adjusted EPS, roughly 232% of guided GAAP EPS, with zero buyback-program repurchases in H1 2026 and an incremental revolver draw in the same period. Halting the buyback is the rational first lever and evidence of discipline; the sequencing nonetheless means the next lever is the dividend.

**Guidance shape.** FY2026 requires an H2 adjusted EBITDA swing of roughly +$69M year over year, concentrated in Q4 (implied ~$38.5M). Two of the last four quarters missed consensus, one badly.

**Governance.** Combined Chair/CEO since 1989. The 2025 annual cash bonus paid entirely from a 30%-weighted, internally scored Workday milestone (34.7 of 40 points, 189% modifier) after adjusted EBITDAIC, WSEE growth and HRCore growth all scored zero, producing a 56.7% blended payout and $1.02M of CEO cash bonus. The Compensation Committee that designed and scored that metric includes a director who was Workday's Vice-Chairman until May 2024 and joined the board three months after the partnership announcement; the board deems him independent and cites HCM expertise. Set against this, the equity-side alignment worked forcefully: the 2023 LTIP paid 12.75% of target with relative TSR at the 6th percentile paying zero, and both the 2024 and 2025 LTIP EBITDA tranches paid zero. Compensation actually paid to the CEO was approximately $3.0M in 2025 and $0.08M in 2024 against reported grant-date totals of $16.81M and $9.37M, the 2025 jump driven by a one-time $6.76M special PSU whose lowest vesting rung requires a $105 30-day average price (roughly 103% above spot) for 10% vesting and $225 for full vesting through July 2030.

**Insider pledging.** Sarvadi had 250,000 shares pledged as loan collateral at April 6, 2026 (approximately 17.0% of beneficial holdings), with board pre-authorization to pledge up to 35% after January 31, 2027; Rawson has 75,000 pledged (33.1%). Pledged stock introduces a forced-sale channel in a severe drawdown. Whether any of the 2026 purchases were financed with borrowings secured by company stock is unresolved and no disclosure links them.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (41 contracts across four expirations) and a prior snapshot recorded an implausible 699.8% ATM IV, so these readings carry low confidence. Near-term ATM IV of 81.3% on a six-day expiry, implying a ±12.1% move with no scheduled catalyst following the July 29 print, is most plausibly a thin-chain artifact rather than information. The more usable tenors show one-month IV of 62.9% against 66.4% trailing realized volatility (roughly in line) and six-month IV of 57.4%, with the term structure in backwardation. Put/call open interest of 0.19 is call-tilted, which does not corroborate a bearish options positioning read; the one-month skew of +3.1% is characterized as elevated put demand while the six-month skew is −4.7%, and the sign convention makes directional inference across tenors unreliable. Short interest of 11.0% of float at five days to cover is elevated and creates squeeze risk on any upside surprise, which is one reason to trim rather than to position bearishly. The options data does not change the fundamental conclusion; it does argue against paying for near-dated protection at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case ($70, +35%)

The FY2025 shock was an industry-wide claims-trend event, so the spread mean-reverts. Repricing plus the $500,000 Selected Claims Limit plus an 8% opex reduction restores adjusted EBITDA toward $270M by 2027-2028, close to the 2024 level, on a base that stabilizes once repricing-driven attrition completes. Retention at 83% and middle-market WSEEs up 10% show the up-market pivot working. HRScale converts sold WSEEs into a durable higher-attach offering, and the Workday-powered product defends against HCM-plus-PEO bundles. At 11x on $270M less $250M of net debt the equity is worth roughly $70, and the 4.6% yield pays while waiting. The CEO's $15.5M of escalating open-market purchases, with the largest tranche last at $34.05, is the most credible corroboration available that the insiders with the claims data see normalization.

### Bear case ($30, −42%)

The 2023 baseline of $354M was flattered by depressed post-pandemic medical utilization and $33M of workers' compensation reserve releases, so normalization stops well short of it. Workers' compensation development compresses toward zero while accident-year cost rises 35%, adding a quiet second margin headwind. WSEE decline proves demand-driven rather than repricing-driven, so the denominator keeps shrinking, and a 6%-smaller salesforce cannot rebuild it. A single adverse claims year returns adjusted EBITDA to roughly $140M against $419M of borrowings, $95M of adjusted cash and $61M of book equity, forcing a dividend cut that removes the yield floor and re-rates the equity toward the 52-week low. HRScale never scales; the $20M of annual Workday SaaS cost remains an expense without a matching revenue line.

### Balance of evidence

The mechanism of recovery is management-controlled and verifiably improving, which is why this is not an AVOID. The constraint is that the market has already repriced from "is the dividend and covenant package safe" to "how much pre-2025 earnings power returns." The stock is up 43% since the prior assessment at $36.22 while the guidance midpoint improved by roughly $5M. That combination degrades risk/reward regardless of the operational direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now into strength, at or near the analyst mean of $51.75 and 9% below the 52-week high. For holders with a full position, reduce to a one-third residual to retain optionality on the FY2027 normalization case and to avoid being squeezed by 11% short interest. Do not add. The October Q3 print (Q3 guidance $14-41M adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS −$0.09 to $0.41) is the natural next decision point, and the February FY2027 guide is the decisive one.

**Scenario targets from the $51.98 basis:**
- Base **$57** (+9.7%; 21x FY2027 consensus $2.70, cross-checked against 10.5x $240M adjusted EBITDA less ~$300M net debt), total return ~14% with the dividend
- Bull **$70** (+34.7%; 11x $270M normalized adjusted EBITDA less ~$250M net debt)
- Bear **$30** (−42.3%; 9x $140M adjusted EBITDA less ~$400M net debt, plus multiple compression on a dividend cut)
- Probability-weighted 12-month total return: **+6% to +10%**

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) A reported quarter demonstrating the H2 bridge — Q3 adjusted EBITDA at or above the $27.5M guidance midpoint with benefit cost per covered employee at or below +5%. (2) Average WSEEs flat or growing sequentially for two consecutive quarters, confirming that attrition was repricing-driven and has ended. (3) A verified peer or third-party benefit-trend datapoint showing 2025 was industry-wide rather than Insperity-specific. (4) Price back toward the mid-$30s, where the base case implies 50%+ upside against the same bear case.

**Avoid triggers.** (1) Benefit cost per covered employee re-accelerating above +7% in any quarter. (2) FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $185M, or Q4 tracking below $30M. (3) Revolver borrowings above $475M, or a further covenant amendment. (4) A dividend reduction, or disclosure that the buyback remains suspended into 2027 while borrowings rise. (5) Workers' compensation favorable development below $5M for full-year 2026 with current-period cost above 0.35% of non-bonus payroll.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (late October) for the bridge; the FY2025 10-K analogue and FY2027 initial guidance (February 2027) for the normalization question; the 2026 annual meeting vote results and any amendment exhibit filing for the unresolved governance and covenant items.

## Conclusion

Insperity's trajectory has genuinely improved since the prior review. Benefit cost trend halved from +9.2% to +5%, H1 adjusted EBITDA grew 4%, the guidance midpoint edged up, adjusted cash rose from $57M to $95M, and the CEO put $15.5M of his own money into the stock in escalating tranches with the CFO and three others alongside him. Score dimensions reflect that: management quality at 6.0 is the highest reading in this file, and financial health holds at 4.5 rather than declining.

What has changed more than the fundamentals is the price. At $51.98 the equity trades at 11.3x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA on a definition that excludes stock compensation and cash SaaS spend, 24.1x guided adjusted EPS, roughly 50x guided GAAP EPS, and 19.3x a next-year consensus that has been cut 6.7% in 90 days. The worksite-employee base is guided to shrink. The dividend costs 111% of guided adjusted EPS and coexists with borrowings up $49M since year-end, a $50M working-capital draw, and a covenant package amended in the borrower's favor. The FY2026 recovery requires a $69M second-half swing that has not yet appeared in a reported quarter, and the workers' compensation reserve tailwind that flattered 2023-2025 by roughly 3% of gross profit annually is compressing while accident-year cost rises 35%.

A 6-10% probability-weighted total return with a 42% downside tail, on a stock sitting at the analyst mean with one buy and one strong sell against three holds, is less than fair compensation for the underwriting risk being carried. **REDUCE.** The stock becomes interesting again in the mid-$30s, or on a reported quarter that converts the second-half guidance bridge into evidence.
