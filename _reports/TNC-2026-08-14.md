## Executive Summary

Tennant is a 156-year-old maker of mechanized floor-cleaning equipment with a genuine channel asset — the largest direct sales and service organization in its niche, 40,000-plus customers, 11 manufacturing sites — that is currently being run through a botched enterprise-systems migration. The November 2025 North American ERP go-live cost the company three weeks of machine order entry and parts shipping capability, forced a two-week manufacturing shutdown in January 2026 for a physical inventory, and has not stabilized. The FY2025 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, projected normalization "by mid-year." The Q2 10-Q, filed August 6, states that targeted productivity gains "have taken longer to realize than expected." Management has separately described a systemic defect in which the system transmitted incorrect demand signals to suppliers, with manual purchase orders as the interim workaround.

The financial consequences are now measurable rather than prospective. H1 2026 operating income fell to $20.8M (3.3% margin) from $50.2M (8.2%); gross margin compressed 300bp to 38.8%; operating cash flow swung from +$22.1M to **-$26.2M**, and free cash flow to **-$34.7M**. Primary working capital (receivables plus inventory less payables) rose $47.5M, or 14.5%, on H1 revenue growth of 2.2%. Total debt went from $199.5M at December 2024 to **$358.9M** at June 30, 2026, and debt-to-capital from 24.3% to 40.1%, funding roughly $148M of buybacks at an average $71.98 — above today's price — plus $32.8M of dividends against approximately $9M of cumulative free cash flow over six quarters.

The stock has fallen 24.5% from its June 25 high of $91.93 and 20.7% over twelve months, which invites a trough-valuation argument: 8.6-9.4x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, 12.4x next-year consensus EPS of $5.62, and 67x trailing GAAP EPS of roughly $1.03 that is plainly depressed. We are not persuaded, for three reasons that are specific rather than atmospheric. First, current-year consensus has been cut 24.8% in 90 days and next-year 11.8%, so the price decline has been matched or exceeded by estimate cuts on the near leg — forward multiples have not meaningfully compressed. Second, the residual FY2026 guide requires H2 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $105.5M against about $57M in H1, a step-change management has already failed to deliver once this year, with Q3 gross margin guided roughly flat against Q2's 39.5% — meaning almost the entire improvement sits in Q4. Third, leverage is 3.24x-3.64x on reconstructible EBITDA against a 3.75x covenant, and the credit-agreement EBITDA definition is not reproduced in the filings, so headroom cannot be established. Our probability-weighted value is approximately $69, essentially the current price, against a bear case at $54 and specific dated catalysts on the downside in the next ninety days.

## Company Overview

Tennant designs and sells ride-on and walk-behind scrubbers, sweepers and carpet extractors, plus aftermarket parts, consumables, service, financing and asset management. Brands span premium (Tennant, Nobles) and mid-tier (IPC, Gaomei, Rongen, Alfa), a deliberate architecture intended to defend against price entry. Differentiated technologies include ec-H2O NanoClean detergent-free cleaning, IRIS asset management, ReadySpace carpet cleaning, and an expanding autonomous (ROVR) line. Four geographic operating segments — North America, Latin America, EMEA, APAC — aggregate into **one reportable segment**, with the CEO as CODM evaluating net income and gross profit. Tennant therefore does not disclose regional profitability, which matters directly to the EMEA analysis below.

Q2 2026 revenue of $324.0M split $216.5M direct (flat year over year) and $107.5M to distributors (+5.3%). By line: equipment $193.8M (-1.6%), parts and consumables $68.1M (-2.0%), service and other $62.1M (+19.2%). The 2.0% decline in parts and consumables — 3.0% for H1, $132.7M versus $136.8M — deserves emphasis. This is the installed-base annuity that underwrites the moat, and it contracted. Management attributes the shortfall to fulfillment constraints rather than demand, which is credible given the ERP-driven loss of parts inventory visibility, but it is the aftermarket line, not the equipment line, that historically justifies a premium multiple for this business.

Segment aggregation also means the 19.2% growth in "service and other" cannot be independently decomposed. Management has acknowledged that autonomy subscription revenue more than doubled "partly due to changed revenue recognition" under an enterprise license arrangement with Brain Corp, and Tennant separately amended its AMR contracts effective March 1, 2026 so that customers receive full rights to embedded autonomy software on delivery, moving that revenue from ratable recognition to point of sale. Deferred revenue fell from $32.4M to $29.6M over the half while amounts recognized into sales rose to $13.6M from $9.3M. The commercial change is substantive and the accounting follows appropriately, but reported robotics and service growth in 2026 is not a clean volume-and-price measure, and no organic-versus-timing split is disclosed.

Backing Q2 out of H1 gives a Q1 2026 with $297.9M of sales, **$4.9M of operating income (1.6% margin)** and $0.2M of net income — corroborated independently by the equity statement. Q1 was a break-even quarter.

The cash reversal is the central fact. Operating cash flow deteriorated $48.3M year over year, driven by receivables (-$31.1M use versus -$2.4M), inventories (-$18.3M versus -$8.3M), payables (-$12.7M versus -$6.2M) and other items (-$13.3M). Receivables rose 11.5% to $286.3M while H1 revenue grew 2.2%. Free cash flow was -$34.7M against +$11.3M a year earlier.

We deliberately do not annualize this. Tennant's business is back-half weighted, quarterly revenue ranges from 22% to 28% of the year by the company's own disclosure, and management indicated Q2 operating cash flow returned to roughly +$5M after a Q1 outflow near -$31M. As a labeled sensitivity: if H2 2026 replicated H2 2025's operating cash generation of $42.9M, FY2026 operating cash flow would be roughly +$17M, versus $65.0M in 2025 and $89.7M in 2024. That is the plausible good outcome, and it is still a two-thirds decline from two years ago.

### Balance sheet and the covenant question

|  | Dec-24 | Dec-25 | Jun-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | $99.8M | $106.4M | $76.9M |

Debt is almost entirely revolver: $357.5M drawn on a $650M senior secured facility maturing August 7, 2029, with $289.4M unused. Weighted average cost 5.2%, roughly 4.2% after $120M notional of swaps fixed at 3.443%. No near-term maturity wall, which is the strongest single element of the credit profile.

Covenants: indebtedness/EBITDA ≤ 3.75x; EBITDA/interest ≥ 3.00x; and a restriction on dividends and repurchases above 2.50x leverage, capped at the greater of 10% of consolidated total assets (roughly $127M) or $100M annually. Compliance was certified at June 30.

Reconstructing leverage: TTM operating income = FY2025 $68.3M − H1 2025 $50.2M + H1 2026 $20.8M = **$38.9M**; TTM D&A approximately **$59.8M**; TTM GAAP EBITDA approximately **$98.7M**. That gives 3.64x. Adding back share-based compensation (~$8M) and restructuring (~$4M) gives 3.24x. On a company-style adjusted basis (roughly $137M) it is 2.62x. Certified compliance mechanically implies credit-agreement EBITDA above $95.7M. **Headroom therefore ranges from about 0.11x to about 1.13x depending on the definition, and the credit agreement's permitted add-backs are not reproduced in the filings.** This is an unresolved verification gap, and neither endpoint should be treated as established. What is established: leverage has roughly doubled as a ratio in eighteen months, and it did so to fund buybacks and dividends rather than growth capex. Interest coverage, at roughly 6x GAAP EBITDA to interest, remains comfortable.

Recomputing Altman on the same basis as a prior FY2025-based reading (book equity substituted for retained earnings and market value) moves it from 2.62 to approximately **2.38**; the classic formulation with market equity gives roughly 2.94. Both sit in or near the grey zone. Neither indicates imminent distress; neither is reassuring.

### Capital allocation

Reconstructing January 2025 through June 2026: sources were $38.8M of operating cash flow less $30.2M of capex, or **$8.6M of free cash flow**. Uses were $148.2M of buybacks (2,058,768 shares), $32.8M of dividends, and roughly $10.8M of distributor tuck-ins — **$191.8M**. The $183.2M gap was funded by $159.4M of incremental debt and a $22.9M cash drawdown; the reconciliation closes within about $1M. Average repurchase price was **$71.98**, roughly 3.8% above the current $69.41. Share count fell 9.55%, from 18,849,456 to 17,049,303 — real and permanent, but purchased with balance-sheet capacity rather than earnings.

We do not infer imprudence from price outcome alone. FY2025 repurchases at $79.08 preceded full visibility on the ERP failure, and the Q1 2026 tranche at roughly $63.70 will look astute if the recovery lands. But the sequencing is unfavorable: the board authorized an additional 2,000,000 shares on April 29, 2026, and Q2 repurchases were then essentially nil (5,515 shares, mostly tax withholding), while the stock traded $78.81-$86.50. That is consistent with either leverage discipline after the Q1 cash burn or price discipline; both readings imply the earlier, more expensive purchases were the less disciplined ones.

The dividend has been paid 81 consecutive years and raised 54, now $0.31 quarterly or $1.24 annualized — a **1.79% yield** costing roughly $21.1M. Coverage was 49% of FY2025 free cash flow. In H1 2026, $10.8M of dividends were paid against -$34.7M of free cash flow and funded in cash terms by borrowing. The covenant permits well above the current payout even at elevated leverage, so this is not a near-term solvency constraint; it is a watch item on cash durability, and the streak creates an institutional commitment that could keep cash flowing out through a prolonged trough.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue line is not the problem. FY2026 sales guidance was **raised** to $1.27-1.31B from $1.24-1.28B, implying +5.5-9% growth with organic +3.5-7%. Orders rose 6.6% to $339M with June up 11%, and backlog reached $127M at quarter end, up $18M sequentially and $50M year to date. The 10-Q corroborates qualitatively: backlog "remained elevated, reflecting increased demand and future-ship orders that outpaced material availability and supplier responsiveness." Demand is not the binding constraint; conversion is.

Robotics is the genuine growth vector. Revenue of roughly $31M in Q2 (+37%) and $58M in H1 (+56%) against a FY2026 target of $130-145M and a stated $250M objective for 2028 — a 43.3% CAGR from FY2025's roughly $85M, or 34.9% from the FY2026 midpoint. R&D rose to 3.9% of sales from 3.1%, a dedicated SVP Robotics role was created in January 2026, roughly 120 people are assigned, ten launches are committed over two years, and the Savers/Value Village deployment expanded from pilot to 250 units. That last item is the most persuasive evidence for the moat thesis: the expansion was attributed to demonstrated operational support across varying store layouts, which is precisely what a large direct service organization provides and a low-cost entrant does not.

Two qualifications. The autonomy stack is licensed from Brain Corp, in which Tennant holds roughly 12% economic and 18% voting interest with board representation and equity-method accounting from December 9, 2025. Tennant is the licensee and payer; Brain Corp is the licensor. No exclusivity is disclosed, so we cannot say whether the same stack is available to competing OEMs, and Brain Corp is loss-making — $0.5M of equity-method losses flowed through in Q2 on a three-month lag. Second, some portion of 2026 robotics and autonomy growth reflects the March 1 contract amendment and the Brain Corp agreement change rather than unit volume, and the split is not disclosed. Discount the headline growth rate modestly.

Against this, earnings growth has gone the other way. Consensus for the current year fell from $5.12 to **$3.85** (-24.8% over 90 days) and next year from $6.38 to **$5.62** (-11.8%). The current quarter is down 33.4% over 90 days. The earnings track record is 1 beat and 3 misses in four quarters, with the June quarter missing by 38.2%. Rising revenue guidance alongside falling earnings guidance is the signature of a cost and conversion problem, and it argues against treating estimate momentum as supportive here.

The critical arithmetic is the H2 requirement. Implied H2 revenue at the guidance midpoint is **$668.1M**, or +12.3% against H2 2025's $594.9M. Implied H2 GAAP diluted EPS is **$1.70-2.35**, or 3.8x-5.2x the entire first half's $0.45. Implied H2 adjusted EBITDA is roughly **$105.5M** versus about $57M in H1 — a 15.8% margin against 9.2%, requiring approximately a 49% sequential increase from Q2's reported $35.3M. Management has told investors Q3 gross margin will be roughly flat versus Q2, which places nearly the entire step-change in Q4. Seasonality supports some of this (Q4 sits at the high end of the 22-28% quarterly range), and backlog plus new launches support more. But the guide has no cushion, and it has already been cut once this year on exactly these issues.

The problem with the cheap-multiple case runs through the adjusted numbers on which it rests. Either way, $167.4M should not be treated as an operating run rate, and an investor paying 15.6-18.3x "adjusted" earnings is paying for a wedge that is only partly traceable to a filed reconciliation.

One unpriced positive: following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize tariff imposition, Tennant filed refund claims for previously paid IEEPA tariffs during Q2. As of June 30 the claims were unapproved and no benefit was recognized. This is an unrecognized contingent asset of undisclosed size — genuine optionality outside guidance. It should not be sized or treated as probable, and Tennant continues absorbing tariff cost under replacement authority.

Analyst targets are $77 low, $87.75 mean, $90.50 median, $93 high across three analysts, with the recommendation split unchanged at 2 buy / 1 hold for four consecutive months. Every target sits above the current price, and the mean implies 26% upside. Given the 24.8% current-year estimate cut and the stock's 24% decline, these targets appear to predate or not reflect the August 5 revision. On a stock with falling estimates and repeated misses, consensus targets do not function as a floor; treat them as stale rather than as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names no competitors and rests its market-leadership claim on "sponsored market studies," which is management's characterization rather than verified fact. The structure described is a fragmented niche with large multinationals plus regional and local participants differentiated in part by degree of automation.

Trajectory is what matters, and it diverges sharply by region. North America appears to be strengthening: management cites higher-than-forecast industrial equipment demand, positive net pricing, and robotics traction, with consolidated price realization of +3.0% in Q2. EMEA is deteriorating: organic sales fell 2.8% in Q2 and 1.0% in H1, with margin pressure attributed in the 10-Q to "competitive price concessions, volume deleverage, and unfavorable mix," and management describing **negative net pricing**. Management has separately identified low-end competition from Chinese and Asian manufacturers in commercial walk-behind products, which the filed language corroborates in effect. APAC organic fell 10.6% in Q2 on cautious capital spending and distributor overstock, though at 6.4% of revenue the earnings impact is contained.

The two distributor buy-ins (R4Y, September 2025, $3.6M; Clean Machine, February 2026, $7.7M) contributed +2.3% to Q2 EMEA sales — flattering reported EMEA growth while organic declines, and adding goodwill to the reporting unit with the thinnest impairment cushion.

The most important competitive datapoint is the 3.0% H1 decline in parts and consumables. The moat is the service and channel annuity, not the machine. Management attributes the decline to fulfillment, which is plausible and reversible. But until that line returns to growth, the recurring-revenue quality argument is unproven, and a mix shift toward "service and other" that is partly driven by a recognition change does not substitute for it.

The plausible synthesis: Tennant is winning the automation transition in North America where its service network is a real barrier, while losing the commodity end in Europe where the mid-tier brand architecture is being tested by price entry. The alternative — that EMEA pricing pressure is cyclical destocking that reverses — is defensible but unproven, and the goodwill test in October will force the issue into reported numbers.

## Risk Assessment

**ERP defect (dominant, company-specific).** The 10-K's mid-year normalization timeline has slipped at least one quarter. A defect that corrupts supplier demand signaling is a data and design problem rather than a training problem, and it plausibly links the parts shortages, the $47.5M working-capital build, and the negative operating cash flow into a single chain. Offsetting: management concluded disclosure controls were effective at June 30 and reported no material change in internal control over financial reporting, and no material weakness has been disclosed — a meaningful absence of a worse signal. The "systemic issue" characterization is single-sourced from a secondary summary of the earnings call and is not verified in a filing.

**EMEA goodwill impairment (dated, quantified).** At the October 1, 2025 test Tennant bypassed the qualitative screen and ran a quantitative test for EMEA, finding fair value exceeded carrying value by only **$36.8M, or 7.9%**, on **$172.6M** of allocated goodwill — 83% of the $208.6M total. The company explicitly warned of impairment risk if results miss projections or assumptions deteriorate. EMEA has since deteriorated on both volume and price. A high-single-digit reduction in forecast cash flows or a modest discount-rate increase could eliminate the cushion at the October 1, 2026 test. To be precise about consequences: an impairment would be non-cash, would not affect operating cash flow, and is customarily excluded from credit-agreement and company-adjusted EBITDA. It would reduce GAAP EPS and book equity (against $535.2M total equity and roughly $278M tangible book), push the equity-based Altman inputs lower, and constitute an admission that the EMEA acquisition history is worth less than carried.

**Covenant headroom uncertainty.** Discussed above: 0.11x to 1.13x depending on the EBITDA definition, unresolvable from the filings. Not a predicted breach — an unquantifiable risk that would ordinarily be quantifiable.

**Guidance concentration.** The H2 ramp requires a 49% sequential increase in adjusted EBITDA from Q2, with Q3 gross margin guided flat, placing nearly all of it in Q4.

**CFO transition.** Fay West gave notice July 30, 2026, with a successor expected by Q1 2027; management characterized the decision as personal and unrelated to performance, and a five-year-plus tenure retirement is ordinary. We do not infer a negative signal from the departure itself. We do note that continuity of financial leadership is now an execution risk through ERP remediation, the back-half ramp and the October goodwill test, and that incoming CFOs frequently revisit estimates and non-GAAP presentation.

**Litigation.** The Oxygenator Water Technologies matter carries a $20.9M accrual after a November 2024 adverse jury verdict and a September 2025 post-trial enhancement, both sides appealed, stayed by a $20.3M supersedeas bond with no cash collateral posted, non-injunctive. At 1.8% of market capitalization this is modest and contained.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 62.0% against 41.3% one-year realized, with the curve in backwardation (62.0% at one week versus 28.2% at six months) and put/call open interest of 6.07, reads as stress positioning. Two cautions temper the reading. The chain contains only 45 contracts across four expirations, so open-interest ratios on a small-cap this thin are weak evidence, and the 62% near-term print sits on a six-day tenor after earnings have already passed, which is more consistent with illiquidity than with a known catalyst. The more usable observation is the far end: six-month implied volatility of 28.2% sits well below one-year realized of 41.3%, so longer-dated protection is priced cheaply relative to how this stock has actually traded. Short interest at 9.2% of float with 7.3 days to cover is elevated for a small industrial and creates squeeze risk on any clean Q3 print — a reason we recommend trimming rather than shorting.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+26.8%)

The ERP problem is transitional and largely solved by mid-Q3 as management indicates; parts availability recovers, the $47.5M working-capital build reverses into cash, and H2 operating cash flow returns toward $40-45M. Backlog of $127M, up $50M year to date, plus orders +6.6% and June +11%, converts as supplier responsiveness improves, delivering the guided $668M H2 revenue. Q4 seasonality plus the X2 ROVR and X16 SWEEP launches produce the required margin step-up. FY2027 consensus of $5.62 proves achievable; at 15.5x that is $87. Robotics compounds toward the $250M 2028 objective at over 10% of sales with better mix. Leverage falls back below 2.5x on cash generation, restoring buyback capacity at prices below the $71.98 historical average. EMEA passes the October test, aided by euro strength raising both carrying value and euro cash flows and by acquired distributor earnings. Tariff refund claims are approved, adding unguided cash. Three directors bought roughly $1.65M in the open market at three separate price points, including 9,500 shares at $67.34-$68.18 six days after the drawdown, which is a real vote on value near current levels.

### Bear Case — $54 (-22.2%)

The supplier demand-signal defect requires a software remediation that extends into Q4, parts and consumables stay in decline, and gross margin holds near 39% rather than recovering toward 42%. H2 adjusted EBITDA lands at $85-90M rather than $105M, FY2026 adjusted EPS prints $3.20-3.50, and FY2027 consensus resets from $5.62 toward $4.50. Working capital does not release; full-year operating cash flow is near break-even, leaving the $21.1M dividend and interest funded by revolver and leverage at or above 3.5x on reconstructible EBITDA. The October 1 EMEA test produces a partial write-down of the $172.6M goodwill balance, cutting GAAP EPS and book equity and, more importantly, validating the structural rather than cyclical reading of European price competition. At 12x $4.50 the stock is $54; a further multiple derating on covenant concern takes it lower. The CFO seat is vacant through the remediation.

### What the market may be missing, in both directions

At a 24.5% drawdown from the high, the burden of proof runs both ways, and the honest answer is that this is not primarily a mispricing story. The sellers are responding to a specific, disclosed fact pattern: a 38.2% earnings miss, an 11% adjusted EBITDA guidance cut, negative first-half free cash flow, and doubled leverage. Those are real. What the sellers may be underweighting is that revenue guidance went **up**, backlog rose $50M, and the constraint is conversion rather than demand — meaning the earnings power is deferred rather than destroyed. What the buyers appear to be underweighting is that the current-year estimate cut (-24.8%) exceeded the price decline, so the forward multiple has barely compressed on the near leg; that the recovery requires a Q4-loaded step-change from a management team that has already missed once on these issues; and that the October goodwill test and the covenant definition are both live, dated, unresolved items.

## Conclusion

Tennant's score trajectory (6.1 in May, 5.2 in June, 4.64 now) reflects confirmation rather than reaction. The prior two HOLDs were predicated on a transitional ERP disruption resolving by mid-2026 and on a balance sheet that could absorb the trough. The Q2 filing and guidance revision falsify the first assumption — normalization has slipped at least a quarter and the defect is described as systemic — and materially weaken the second, with debt-to-capital at 40.1%, first-half free cash flow of -$34.7M, and covenant headroom that cannot be established from the filings. A third consecutive HOLD would require evidence that has not arrived.

The valuation is genuinely undemanding on guided adjusted numbers (8.6-9.4x EV/EBITDA, 12.4x next-year consensus), and we do not treat the 24.5% drawdown as itself evidence against the stock. But cheap multiples on adjusted earnings whose $1.65-per-share wedge is only one-third traceable to filed reconciliations, resting on a Q4-loaded ramp from a team that has missed once already this year, do not constitute margin of safety. Expected return is approximately flat including the dividend, the two clearest dated catalysts in the next ninety days (the October 1 goodwill test and the early-November Q3 print) skew negative, and the offsetting positives — raised sales guidance, $127M backlog, robotics at a 35-43% growth trajectory, director buying near current levels, and an unsized tariff refund claim — are real but not yet visible in cash.

Reduce exposure into strength. Revisit on the Q3 print with positive operating cash flow and a recovering aftermarket line.
