*Formerly New York Mortgage Trust (NYMT), renamed September 3, 2025. Internally-managed mortgage REIT, Nasdaq: ADAM.*

---

## Executive Summary

Adamas Trust is an internally-managed hybrid mortgage REIT that spent 2023–2025 repositioning away from credit-heavy, price-sensitive multi-family JV equity toward liquid Agency RMBS and short-duration, high-coupon business-purpose loans. The July 2025 acquisition of the remaining half of Constructive Loans, its now-wholly-owned origination platform, vertically integrated a captive source of higher-yielding assets. FY2025 delivered a headline swing to profitability: net income to common of $101.1M ($1.12 EPS) versus a $103.8M loss in 2024, alongside the second consecutive dividend increase (to $0.27/quarter for Q2 2026).

The turnaround is real but partly optical. The $205M year-over-year earnings swing is dominated by $217.4M of non-cash unrealized gains as rates fell in late 2025, not operating improvement alone. Because the company applies the fair-value option to nearly all assets and liabilities, GAAP net income whipsaws with rates (EPS went from -$1.14 to +$1.12 in a single year). The more durable signal is Earnings Available for Distribution of $0.89/share, up 141%, reflecting a genuine widening of net interest spread (0.74% → 1.33% → 1.46%) as the portfolio rotated into business-purpose loans. Book value stabilized at $9.60 GAAP / $10.63 adjusted after years of erosion.

The offsetting concerns are material. Recourse leverage jumped from 3.0x to 5.0x in one year, magnifying sensitivity to margin calls and spread widening. New unsecured financing carries 9.125%–9.875% coupons against a 6.36% asset yield, compressing corporate-level economics. A residual multi-family credit tail persists (28.6% of the mezzanine book on non-accrual, one investment zeroed). The equity carries a ~$1.41B accumulated deficit, a legacy of the 2022–2023 losses and years of paying dividends above earnings. We rate ADAM a HOLD for income: the ~12%+ yield is now covered by EAD, but the discount to book reflects justified market skepticism about a levered model that works when rates fall and reverses sharply if they rise.

## Company Overview

Adamas Trust invests across residential real estate credit and capital markets, earning the spread between yields on its assets and the cost of financing them. As of December 31, 2025, the company reports two segments:

**Investment Portfolio** — the core mREIT engine, a ~$10.5B book comprising:
- Agency RMBS (~63%) — government-guaranteed, liquid, financed with short-term repo. This is the primary growth vehicle since 2023.
- Residential loans and credit assets (~35%) — business-purpose loans (~21% of total), performing/re-performing/non-performing loans, non-Agency RMBS, and first-loss subordinated securities in Freddie Mac-sponsored "Consolidated SLST" securitizations (economic interest limited to ~$151.5M of retained securities despite full GAAP consolidation of underlying loans).
- Multi-family — wound down to a residual cross-collateralized mezzanine book (~$136M net equity, 9 properties, 92.9% occupancy).

**Constructive** — a business-purpose loan originator (rental and bridge loans for residential investors) operating in 48 states. Adamas bought the remaining 50% on July 15, 2025 and now fully consolidates it. Constructive originated ~$1.8B in 2025 ($864.9M post-acquisition), generating $26.6M of mortgage banking income in H2 2025.

The strategic logic is vertical integration: rather than competing for higher-yielding business-purpose loans in the open market, Adamas now originates them internally through a captive, scalable platform. This is the central bet of the current thesis. Leadership is CEO Jason Serrano, President Nicholas Mah, and CFO Kristine Nario-Eng; former NYMT CEO Steven Mumma remains on the board.

**The GAAP swing is rate-driven, not operating-driven.** 2025 recognized $217.4M of net unrealized gains (versus $90.5M of losses in 2024) as three Fed cuts (75bp) lifted asset values, partly offset by $82.4M of unrealized swap losses and $65.4M of realized losses. With the fair-value option applied across nearly the entire balance sheet, reported earnings will continue to swing with rates. The right anchor for run-rate power is EAD, whose 141% jump reflects the portfolio mix shift and lower financing costs.

**Net interest spread has widened steadily** as the book rotated into higher-yielding business-purpose loans. Net interest income nearly doubled to $149.3M.

**Book value stabilized** at $9.60 (from $9.28) after entering 2024 at $11.31. The longer arc is less flattering: the accumulated deficit reflects the 2022–2023 losses and years of paying dividends above earnings.

**Leverage rose sharply.** Company Recourse Leverage went 3.0x → 5.0x; Portfolio Recourse Leverage 2.9x → 4.7x. Total assets grew 37% to $12.6B. The mechanism: the Agency RMBS buildout is financed with short-term repo, Constructive was consolidated, and unsecured notes were issued. Roughly 70% of debt (ex-mortgages/SLST CDOs) is subject to mark-to-market margin calls, 61% collateralized by Agency RMBS. The 10-K repeatedly cites the March 2020 COVID precedent, when margin calls forced fire sales and a dividend suspension.

**Corporate financing is expensive.** New senior unsecured notes carry 9.125%–9.875% coupons (2030) and 9.25% (2031); the company redeemed cheaper 5.75% 2026 notes in February 2026. Subordinated debentures run 7.85%. Preferred dividends are ~$47.9M/year on $559.6M liquidation preference. Raising unsecured debt near 9%+ against a 6.36% asset yield is negative carry at the corporate level: these notes provide liquidity flexibility, not accretive spread, and the Corporate/Other segment posted a -$126M net loss to common.

**Dividend trajectory turned positive** after prior cuts ($1.60 → $1.20 → $0.80), rising to $0.23/quarter in H2 2025 (+15%) and $0.27/quarter for Q2 2026. Year-end yield was ~12.6%. EAD does not fully cover the forward run-rate payout — annualizing the $0.27/quarter Q2 2026 dividend implies $1.08, above EAD, though it exceeds the trailing $0.86 paid in 2025 — and the re-acceleration signals management confidence in coverage, the key input for an income holder.

The financial-health score reflects a levered model operating as designed (a 7.85x asset/equity ratio is normal for a mortgage REIT, and a standardized Altman Z-Score of 0.27 is meaningless here) offset by the leverage step-up, expensive incremental funding, short-term repo dependence, and a residual credit tail.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a function of spread capture and portfolio scale, not organic revenue compounding. The 2025 record investment activity — portfolio +42% to $10.5B, $6.1B of acquisitions ($4.1B Agency RMBS, $1.7B business-purpose loans), four securitizations netting ~$945.5M — demonstrates the platform can deploy capital. The EAD trajectory (-$0.08 → $0.37 → $0.89) is the most credible growth signal, driven by the mix shift.

The genuine growth lever is Constructive. Full consolidation gives Adamas a captive origination engine feeding proprietary, higher-yielding assets rather than buying them at market. If the platform scales without straining reps-and-warranties reserves, it can widen spread durably. The offset is that most of Constructive's output is sold rather than retained, introducing repurchase liability and G&A drag (salaries/benefits rose $18M YoY, largely from Constructive).

Industry tailwinds cited in the MD&A support near-term book value and levered economics: three late-2025 Fed cuts, a steepening 2s10s curve (+71bp at year-end), and FHFA raising the GSEs' Agency RMBS holding caps in January 2026. Headwinds are equally present: subdued expectations for further cuts, persistent inflation, a cooling labor market, and falling apartment rents (-1.7% in Q4 2025) pressuring residual multi-family. Growth is real but rate-regime dependent, which caps the score.

## Valuation Assessment

ADAM trades at a persistent ~30% discount to adjusted book value ($6.50–$8.40 trading range versus $10.63 adjusted / $9.60 GAAP). For a mortgage REIT, price-to-book is the primary lens, and a discount this wide typically signals one of two things: the market doubts the reported book value will hold, or it demands a higher hurdle return given leverage and financing costs.

The bull framing is that the stock offers a yield now covered by EAD, plus a book-value discount that could compress if the repositioning proves durable. The bear framing is that the discount is warranted: a company with the accumulated deficit, 5.0x recourse leverage, negative corporate-level carry on 9%+ notes, and a fair-value book that inflated on falling rates could see book value give back if rates rise or spreads widen. The March 2020 precedent is the tail the discount prices.

Relative to larger, better-capitalized peers (AGNC, Annaly, Rithm, Redwood, MFA), ADAM's discount reflects its smaller scale, higher corporate funding cost, and less-proven hybrid model. Analyst coverage is thin; no formal consensus price target set is available in the reviewed sources. We do not treat the book-value discount as a clear buy signal; in the mortgage REIT space, persistent sub-book pricing is often a durable feature of lower-quality or higher-leverage names, not a temporary mispricing. The valuation supports an income hold: you are paid ~12% to wait, but appreciation to book requires the market to re-rate the model's durability, for which there is not yet sufficient evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

Adamas competes in a crowded mREIT field against larger, better-capitalized players for both Agency RMBS and residential credit. Its differentiation is the hybrid Agency + residential-credit + captive-origination (Constructive) model, which few pure-play Agency REITs replicate. The vertical integration is the moat thesis: proprietary origination of higher-yielding business-purpose loans should, if executed, deliver spread the open market does not offer.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin — net interest spread widened three years running, and the Constructive acquisition is a genuine structural change rather than incremental portfolio churn. But the position is not yet clearly advantaged: scale is a fraction of the leaders, corporate funding is more expensive, and the origination platform is unproven through a rate up-cycle or a credit stress. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving, warranting a middling score. Per our own calibration, competitive position has weakened as a standalone predictor, so we weight it lightly and lean on the spread trajectory as corroboration.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Interest-rate and spread risk amplified by leverage.** At 5.0x recourse leverage with ~70% of debt subject to margin calls, small moves in asset values or spreads are magnified. The March 2020 precedent (forced sales, dividend suspension) is the acute scenario.
2. **GAAP earnings volatility from fair-value accounting.** EPS swung -$1.14 to +$1.12 in one year purely on rate marks. This whipsaws sentiment and complicates valuation; the 2025 "profit" is heavily inflated by non-cash gains.
3. **Constructive integration and origination risk (new in the 2025 10-K).** Multi-state consumer-lending regulatory exposure, representation-and-warranty repurchase liability on sold loans, reserve-adequacy risk, and structurally higher G&A.
4. **Multi-family credit tail.** One preferred equity investment written to zero (3.0% of mezzanine book); 28.6% of the mezzanine portfolio on non-accrual; several DSCRs below 1.0x.
5. **Floating-rate preferred cost escalation.** Series E converted to floating in January 2025; Series F converts October 2026, Series D October 2027. Management warns floating rates "may materially exceed" prior fixed rates, pressuring distributable cash.
6. **GSE conservatorship/privatization uncertainty.** With 63% in Agency RMBS, any change to the Fannie/Freddie guarantee structure directly affects the core holdings' value and financing.
7. **Short-term funding dependence.** $6.75B of repo/warehouse is largely 30–90 day, exposing the company to rollover and haircut risk in any dislocation.
8. **Geographic concentration** (CA, FL, TX, NY, NJ) and key-person dependence on Serrano and Mah.

The risk score is low because several of these are specific, quantified, and asymmetric: the leverage step-up and the fair-value book are the swing factors, and a rate reversal would hit both simultaneously. These are not fully priced beyond the book-value discount.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The repositioning is durable. EAD of $0.89 (+141%) and a widening spread (1.46%) reflect a structurally better portfolio mix. Constructive scales as a captive origination engine, locking in proprietary spread. The ~30% discount to adjusted book compresses as the market gains confidence, and the ~12%+ yield — rising for two consecutive quarters — delivers total return even without full re-rating. In a falling or stable-rate regime, book value holds and levered economics remain favorable. Upside toward adjusted book ($10.63) plus the yield.

**Bear Case.** The 2025 profit was rate-driven; a rate reversal reverses the $217M of unrealized gains, and 5.0x leverage magnifies book-value erosion. Repo markets tighten, margin calls force sales (the March 2020 script), and the dividend is cut again, as it was three times before. The multi-family tail widens, Constructive's reps-and-warranties reserves prove inadequate, and 9%+ corporate financing bleeds the Corporate/Other segment. Book value gives back toward $8 or below, and the discount persists because it is warranted. Downside toward the low end of the recent range ($6.50) or worse in a stress scenario.

The evidence — improving EAD against a levered, rate-dependent model with a poor long-run book-value record — is genuinely two-sided, which supports a hold rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis rests on a ~12%+ yield largely covered by distributable earnings, not on expected price appreciation. Own it for the income while monitoring the leverage and credit tells.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Recourse leverage stabilizes or declines below ~4.5x while EAD holds above $0.85, demonstrating the spread is durable without ratcheting risk.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising adjusted book value with the multi-family non-accrual percentage declining, confirming the credit tail is contained.
- Meaningful open-market insider buying or a material step-up in buybacks at the current ~30% discount to book, signaling management conviction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A rate up-move that drives a book-value decline exceeding ~8% quarter-over-quarter, exposing the leverage risk.
- Any dividend cut, repo haircut widening, or margin-call disclosure — the March 2020 script re-emerging.
- Constructive reps-and-warranties reserve additions or origination-related charges that impair the vertical-integration thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment at the Q2 2026 earnings release (following the 4/29/2026 Q1 print), with particular attention to EAD, adjusted book value, leverage ratios, and dividend coverage commentary. The October 2026 Series F preferred conversion to floating is a further monitoring point for distributable-cash pressure.

## Conclusion

Adamas Trust has executed a genuine strategic reset — rotating into liquid Agency RMBS and high-coupon business-purpose loans, integrating Constructive as a captive originator, and re-establishing improved dividend coverage. EAD of $0.89 (+141%) and a widening spread are credible operating improvements, and the ~12%+ yield is substantially supported. But the headline GAAP profit is heavily rate-mark-driven, recourse leverage rose sharply toward 5.0x in a single year, incremental corporate financing runs at negative carry, and a residual multi-family credit tail persists — all against a long-run record of ~$1.41B in accumulated deficits and repeated dividend cuts. The persistent ~30% discount to adjusted book is a warranted reflection of these risks rather than an obvious mispricing. We rate ADAM a HOLD for income: collect the yield, but do not underwrite appreciation until the leverage stabilizes and the repositioning proves durable through a rate cycle it has not yet been tested against.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report repeatedly asserts the yield is fully "covered by EAD," but EAD of $0.89 is below the annualized forward payout implied by the $0.27/quarter Q2 2026 dividend ($1.08); the coverage claim is only true against the trailing 2025 dividend of $0.86, which softens the central income-hold thesis.
