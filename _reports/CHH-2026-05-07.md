## Executive Summary

Choice Hotels International operates a franchise-heavy hotel platform with 7,575 properties globally, concentrated in the economy-through-upper-midscale segments. The business model collects 5-6% royalty fees from independent hotel owners, generating high margins and recurring revenue in normal conditions. However, the company is now facing simultaneous deterioration across its three most critical operating metrics: U.S. RevPAR declined 3.0% in 2025 (accelerating to -7.6% in Q4), net U.S. room count contracted 2.9% (a reversal of prior growth), and operating cash flow fell $49M year-over-year.

The financial architecture compounds these operational challenges. Total debt stands at $1.91B against stockholders' equity of just $181M (10.6x leverage), interest expense has risen 43% since 2023 to $91M, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.16 places the company statistically in the distress zone. Management characterizes Q1 2026 as a turning point based on a 72% surge in franchise agreements awarded and 5-year-high openings, but the Q1 EPS miss of -19.7% versus consensus and continued system-wide RevPAR weakness undercut this narrative.

With 32.5% short interest, options in steep backwardation (near-term IV of 53.4% vs. 34.8% HV), and analyst consensus skewing negative (5 sells/strong sells vs. 3 buys/strong buys among 16 analysts), the market is pricing in further deterioration. The extended stay growth strategy (WoodSpring, Everhome) provides a legitimate long-term offset to legacy brand attrition, but at current trajectory, it requires 3-5 years to meaningfully shift the revenue mix. The balance sheet may not comfortably provide that time if consumer discretionary spending weakens further.

## Company Overview

Choice Hotels International is the second-largest hotel franchisor in the U.S. economy/midscale segment (behind Wyndham). The company licenses 22 brand names including Comfort Inn, Quality Inn, WoodSpring Suites, Cambria Hotels, and recently acquired Radisson Americas brands. Revenue derives primarily from franchise royalty fees (5-6% of gross room revenue), marketing/reservation system fees, and partnership services (credit card programs, vendor programs).

The company directly owns 17 hotels, primarily Cambria and Everhome Suites properties used to seed new brand development, creating a hybrid model that contradicts the pure "asset-light" positioning. Total invested capital in owned hotels and development exceeds $667M, representing 23% of total assets.

Key strategic priorities include: (1) growing the extended stay portfolio (WoodSpring + Everhome), which currently represents ~56,000 rooms; (2) moving upscale through Cambria Hotels (~10,200 rooms); (3) expanding internationally (now in 50 countries); and (4) improving the effective royalty rate by replacing low-rate economy properties with higher-rate new construction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Deceleration:**

Revenue growth has compressed from 10.1% (2023) to 2.6% (2024) to 0.8% (2025). The most concerning underlying trend is U.S. royalty fees declining $14.9M in 2025, representing a contraction in the company's primary revenue stream.

**RevPAR Trajectory:**

| Period | RevPAR | YoY Change |
|--------|--------|------------|
| FY 2023 | $55.19 | — |
| FY 2024 | $54.54 | -1.2% |
| FY 2025 | $52.85 | -3.0% |
| Q4 2025 | — | -7.6% |

The Q4 acceleration is critical. Both ADR (-1.6%) and occupancy (-80bps) declining simultaneously is unusual outside of recessionary conditions. Extended stay, the supposed growth engine, saw occupancy drop 210bps while RevPAR was flat.

**Cash Flow Deterioration:**

Operating cash flow declined from $319M (2024) to $270M (2025), a $49M drop driven by lower core operating income, higher interest payments, and working capital pressure. Capital expenditures for development remain elevated ($107M hotel investments + $94M equity investments in affiliates + $83M franchise acquisition costs = ~$284M of investment spending). After dividends ($54M) and buybacks ($126M), FCF generation is minimal.

**Leverage:**

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Total Debt | ~$1.5B | ~$1.7B | $1.91B |
| Interest Expense | $63.8M | $87.1M | $91.1M |
| D/E Ratio | ~8x | ~12x | 10.6x |
| Altman Z-Score | — | — | 1.16 |

The leverage trajectory is moving in the wrong direction. While franchisors typically operate with high leverage due to predictable cash flows, "predictable" requires stable room count and RevPAR, both of which are now declining.

**Q1 2026 Miss:**

EPS of $1.07 versus $1.33 consensus represents a -19.7% miss, the largest negative surprise in recent history. GAAP net income was just $20.3M ($0.44/share). This follows a -4.7% miss in Q3 2025 and an essentially in-line Q2 2025.

## Growth Analysis

**The Extended Stay Thesis:**

Management's growth strategy centers on extended stay brands, particularly WoodSpring Suites (economy) and Everhome Suites (midscale). The math:

- WoodSpring added ~28 properties in 2025 (+3,330 rooms)
- Everhome grew from 7 to 25 properties (+2,071 rooms)
- Combined net addition: ~5,400 rooms

Against this, legacy brands lost:
- Quality Inn: -5,112 rooms
- Econo Lodge: -2,870 rooms
- Comfort: -2,659 rooms
- Radisson/Park Inn: -4,932 rooms
- Total legacy losses: ~-14,760 rooms (net)

Extended stay additions cover roughly one-third of legacy attrition. For the system to reach positive net room growth, either legacy terminations must slow dramatically or extended stay openings must triple. Management's turning-point claim rests on the 72% increase in franchise agreements awarded in Q1 2026, but agreements do not translate to openings for 12-24 months.

**International Expansion:**

International royalty fees grew $11.5M (39%) to $41.3M in 2025, partially offsetting domestic weakness. The Choice Hotels Canada acquisition (July 2025) brought full ownership of Canadian properties. However, international revenues remain a small fraction of total franchise fees, too small to compensate for domestic contraction.

**2026 Guidance:**

Management guided adjusted EBITDA of $632-647M and adjusted EPS of $6.92-$7.14. Consensus for FY2026 is $7.20 EPS, implying modest upside to the midpoint of guidance. However, the Q1 miss and RevPAR trends suggest the low end of guidance is more realistic.

## Valuation Assessment

At $105.17, CHH trades at approximately 14.6x the $7.20 FY2026 consensus EPS, a substantial discount to the peer median P/E of 26.4x.

This discount reflects:

1. **Declining earnings quality** — 2025 net income was inflated by a $100M one-time gain; adjusted core income actually declined
2. **Deteriorating operating metrics** — RevPAR and room count both shrinking
3. **Higher leverage risk premium** — 10.6x D/E and Altman Z-Score in distress territory justify a discounted multiple
4. **Negative earnings revisions trajectory over 90 days for the current quarter** (-0.3%), despite marginal 30-day improvements (+3.5%)

The closest comparable is Wyndham Hotels (WH) at 33.1x, but Wyndham is growing its domestic system while Choice is shrinking. The valuation gap accurately prices the fundamental divergence.

At $7.20 consensus EPS and a 14-15x multiple (appropriate for a franchise company with contracting operations and high leverage), fair value ranges from $101-108. Current price of $105.17 sits near the middle of this range with limited upside absent a genuine inflection in room growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Choice Hotels occupies a structurally challenged position. Its core brands (Quality Inn, Comfort Inn, Econo Lodge) compete in the most commoditized segments of lodging where:

- Brand loyalty is weakest (price-driven demand)
- Properties are oldest (average age significantly above upscale segments)
- OTA disintermediation is most powerful (guests search by price, not brand)
- Franchisee capital for renovation is scarcest

The competitive position is **deteriorating** in the core segments. Room count declines across Quality (-5,112), Econo Lodge (-2,870), and Comfort (-2,659) reflect property owners either exiting the system (non-renewals, brand switching) or the underlying hotels reaching end of economic life.

The extended stay segment represents a genuinely improving competitive position. WoodSpring's value-engineered construction model and 69%+ occupancy rates are competitive advantages. But this segment is also attracting significant capital from Hilton (Home2 Suites), Marriott (TownePlace Suites), and IHG (Candlewood/Staybridge). The addressable market is growing, but so is competition.

The AI/OTA disintermediation risk flagged in the 10-K is particularly relevant for economy/midscale brands where guests have minimal brand attachment. If AI booking assistants optimize purely on price/location, Choice's brand value proposition (consistency, loyalty program) erodes further.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **System contraction accelerating** — If legacy brand attrition continues at 2025 rates (-14,760 rooms/year) without extended stay growth tripling, the franchise fee base continues eroding. Each lost room removes ~$2,600 in annual royalty revenue (at $52.85 RevPAR × 5.14% effective royalty rate).

2. **Consumer discretionary downturn** — In a weakening consumer environment, leisure travel demand in economy/midscale segments is vulnerable to further contraction. The Q4 2025 RevPAR decline of -7.6% may represent early-stage demand destruction.

3. **Refinancing risk** — $400M in 3.70% notes mature in 2029, and the $1B revolver expires in 2029. If operating metrics continue deteriorating, refinancing at elevated rates (5.85% was the 2024 issuance) on a potentially larger debt base creates a leverage spiral.

4. **Franchisee credit deterioration** — The $9.2M increase in credit loss provisions and the $47.1M reimbursable system deficit signal financial stress among hotel owners. Franchisee distress leads to property closures, non-renewals, and deferred maintenance that damages brand perception.

5. **Capital allocation in a declining environment** — Management guided $175-225M in buybacks for 2026 despite declining operating cash flow and stretched leverage. Returning capital when the balance sheet is deteriorating and operations are contracting is aggressive.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. ATM IV of 53.4% (vs. 34.8% HV) represents an 18.6pp premium, implying a 7.0% move over the next week. The term structure is in steep backwardation (53.4% near-term vs. 35.8% 6-month), indicating the market sees imminent risk rather than long-dated uncertainty. This aligns with the post-earnings selloff (stock fell from $117.36 to sub-$100 on the April 30 miss) and continued positioning for further downside.

The 32.5% short interest with 12.8 days to cover confirms broad institutional bearishness. This creates theoretical squeeze risk, but the underlying fundamental deterioration provides sufficient cover for shorts to maintain positions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-132):**
- Management's recovery thesis proves correct: net U.S. rooms growth turns positive in H2 2026 as extended stay openings accelerate (the 72% surge in agreements translates to openings within 12-18 months)
- RevPAR stabilizes as consumer confidence improves, potentially aided by lower energy costs
- The 14x multiple re-rates toward 16-17x as growth visibility improves
- Short squeeze: 32.5% SI creates violent upside on any positive catalyst

**Bear Case ($75-87):**

- Legacy brand attrition accelerates; room count falls below 490,000 U.S.
- Operating cash flow falls below $230M, constraining capital allocation and potentially triggering covenant pressure (currently 2.86x vs. 4.5x limit, but declining EBITDA erodes the buffer)
- Multiple compresses to 11-12x on recession fears

**Base Case ($95-108):**
- RevPAR declines moderate to -2-3% in 2026 as lagged Q1 weakness flows through
- Net room count stabilizes near flat as extended stay growth accelerates but doesn't fully offset legacy losses
- EPS comes in at $6.90-7.20 (low end of guidance to consensus)
- Multiple remains range-bound at 13-15x

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July/early August). If RevPAR declines persist at the Q4 rate through summer travel season, the recovery narrative collapses entirely.

**Timing window:** 2-3 months. Summer travel season data will either validate or invalidate the RevPAR stabilization thesis. If June/July hotel data shows continued weakness, the stock likely tests the December 2025 lows ($84).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 32.5% SI with 12.8 days to cover is extremely elevated. This creates material squeeze risk on any positive surprise. However, the high SI also means many sophisticated investors independently reached the same bearish conclusion, which validates the thesis. At these levels, any short position requires strict risk management.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options confirm the bearish thesis with steep backwardation, elevated put skew (5.2%), and IV 18.6pp rich to HV, all aligning with institutional positioning for further downside. Borrow costs are likely elevated given the SI level.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $75-87 (19-29% downside from $105.17). Upside risk to $120-125 on squeeze or positive fundamental surprise (14-19% upside). Risk/reward approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 depending on entry and scenario weights. Squeeze risk at current SI levels is the primary constraint.

**Verdict:** **CROWDED SHORT** — The bearish thesis is correct and well-supported by fundamentals, but 32.5% short interest with 12.8 days to cover creates substantial squeeze risk that makes initiating new shorts at current levels unattractive. The stock has already fallen 23% from its 52-week high. The optimal short entry was $130+; at $105, much of the fundamental deterioration is reflected. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria (for potential future BUY):**
- U.S. net room count growth turns positive for two consecutive quarters (confirmed stabilization)
- RevPAR returns to positive YoY growth, demonstrating demand recovery
- Total debt reduced below $1.5B or leverage ratio demonstrably declining
- Stock trades below $80 (11x FY2026 EPS), creating genuine valuation support

**Timeline:** The headwinds are likely to persist for 12-18 months. The extended stay ramp requires at least 2-3 more years to reach sufficient scale (100,000+ rooms) to dominate the overall system growth math. Until Q2-Q3 2026 data confirms or denies the recovery thesis, fundamental uncertainty remains elevated.

## Conclusion

Choice Hotels International faces a convergence of structural and cyclical headwinds: a shrinking domestic hotel system, accelerating RevPAR declines, extreme leverage, and a Q1 2026 earnings miss that undermined management credibility on the recovery timeline. The extended stay growth strategy is directionally correct but insufficiently scaled to offset legacy brand attrition within the next 12-18 months.

At 14-15x forward earnings, the stock appears fairly valued for a franchise company with contracting operations. The discount to peers is earned, not anomalous.

In the current macro environment, with consumer discretionary spending under pressure and leisure travel particularly exposed to budget constraints, the probability-weighted path for a midscale hotel franchisor tilts negative. The balance sheet provides limited cushion for further deterioration.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The thesis would change if: (1) two consecutive quarters of positive U.S. net room growth confirmed the recovery, (2) RevPAR returned to positive territory, or (3) the stock declined to $80 or below where the valuation provides meaningful margin of safety against continued fundamental weakness.
