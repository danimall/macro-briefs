## Executive Summary

Concentra Group Holdings is the largest occupational health services provider in the United States, operating 628 stand-alone centers and 411 onsite clinics across 41 states, treating approximately 1 in 4 work-related injuries nationally. The company was spun off from Select Medical (SEM) in July 2024 and completed its first full fiscal year as a public entity with $2.16B in revenue (+14% YoY) and $432M Adjusted EBITDA (+15% YoY).

The stock trades at $22.83, representing ~10.3x EV/EBITDA and approximately 15.7x forward P/E on FY2026E EPS of $1.45. Covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with price targets in the $27-$31 range. The discount appears driven by two factors: (1) structural leverage inherited from the spin-off (net debt/EBITDA of 3.4x), and (2) open DOJ and California Department of Insurance investigations, as well as an unsealed California qui tam lawsuit, relating to physical therapy billing practices. Neither factor, in our assessment, justifies the magnitude of the current discount given Concentra's dominant market position, predictable revenue base (98% of top-100 clients retained for 10+ years), and $198M annual free cash flow supporting active deleveraging.

The risk-reward is asymmetric at current levels. The downside scenario — where investigations result in material penalties and growth stalls — suggests a floor around $18-19. The base case, driven by continued organic growth (~5-7%), tuck-in acquisitions, and gradual multiple re-rating toward healthcare services peers, supports $27-29 within 12 months.

## Company Overview

Concentra Group Holdings Parent, Inc. provides occupational health services to approximately 200,000 employer clients (including 100% of Fortune 100 companies). The business model is built around three service pillars:

1. **Workers' Compensation Injury Care** (46% of visits, 65% of center revenue): Treatment of workplace injuries under state-regulated fee schedules. Concentra's value proposition is 25% lower average total claim costs and 65 fewer lost workdays per claim versus non-Concentra treatment.

2. **Employer Services** (52% of visits, 33% of center revenue): Pre-employment physicals, drug testing, compliance-related examinations. This is employer-paid, with high customer retention rates.

3. **Consumer/Urgent Care** (2% of visits): Opportunistic walk-in business that utilizes excess capacity.

Medicare/Medicaid exposure is less than 1% of revenue, insulating Concentra from the federal reimbursement risk that dominates most healthcare services companies. Revenue concentration is minimal — no single customer exceeds 3% of total revenue.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $2.163B grew 13.9% YoY, driven by:
- Patient visit volume up 7.3% to 13.5M annual visits (7.7% VPD growth)
- Revenue per visit up 4.3% to $147.42 (workers' comp visits up 5.3% to $210.15)
- Acquisition contributions from Nova (67 centers, March 2025) and Pivot (240+ onsite clinics, June 2025)

Adjusted EBITDA of $432M represents a 20.0% margin, expanding from 19.8% in FY2024. Cost of services improved to 71.7% of revenue (from 72.2%) due to staffing efficiencies. G&A was elevated at 9.4% (from 8.2%) on one-time separation/acquisition costs that will normalize.

### Cash Flow Generation

Operating cash flow of $279M and free cash flow of $198M (9.1% FCF margin) demonstrate the cash-generative nature of the business. Capital intensity is low at 3.8% of revenue ($82M capex), typical of clinic-based healthcare services.

### Balance Sheet — The Structural Overhang

Total debt of ~$1.596B ($943M term loan + $650M senior notes + $3.5M other debt) results in:
- Net debt/EBITDA: 3.4x (well within 6.5x covenant)
- Debt/Equity: approximately 4.0x (based on year-end 2025 total stockholders' equity of ~$401M)
- Altman Z-Score: estimated in the distress zone — though this metric is distorted by spin-off capital structures, goodwill-heavy balance sheets, and lease-intensive service businesses, and should not be interpreted as indicating operational distress given the company's 3.4x covenant leverage, long-dated maturities, and strong cash flow generation.
- Piotroski F-Score: strong operational health indicators across profitability, leverage improvement, and operating efficiency dimensions.

The leverage is entirely a product of the spin-off mechanics: Select Medical extracted ~$1.5B via dividend prior to separation, replacing parent equity with third-party debt. Maturities are long-dated (revolver 2029, term loan 2031, notes 2032) with $600M in interest rate hedges through February 2028. Liquidity stands at $507M ($80M cash + $428M revolver availability, net of $22M in outstanding letters of credit).

Management is actively deleveraging: $85M voluntary revolver paydown completed by year-end 2025. FCF of ~$198M/year, less ~$32M dividends and any acquisitions, is available for debt reduction. Management's 2026 guidance targets net leverage of 3.0x or below, implying meaningful debt paydown within the year.

### Quarterly Earnings Pattern

Q4 2025 delivered a 22% EPS beat ($0.28 vs. $0.23 consensus). The four-quarter trailing pattern shows 2 beats, 1 in-line, and 1 miss. The Q2 2025 miss coincided with acquisition integration costs and likely represents a one-time disruption rather than a structural issue.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers (Mid-Single Digits)

- **Employment base:** U.S. non-farm payrolls at approximately 159.5M as of December 2025, +6% vs. 2019, though employment growth slowed materially during 2025 (payrolls rose by 584,000 in 2025 versus 2.0 million in 2024). Each employed worker represents potential visit volume.
- **Workplace injury trends:** While Concentra's own visit volumes grew 7.3% in 2025, broader BLS data show private-industry nonfatal workplace injuries and illnesses declined 3.1% in 2024 to a total recordable case rate of 2.3 per 100 FTE workers — the lowest in the series back to 2003. Concentra's volume growth has been driven by market share gains and acquisitions rather than a broad industry injury tailwind. An aging workforce may generate more severe, longer-recovery injuries over time.
- **Pricing:** Workers' comp revenue per visit increased 5.3% in FY2025, driven by state fee schedule reimbursement increases and employer services rate increases.
- **Onsite expansion:** Pivot acquisition tripled onsite clinics (157→411). Addressable onsite market is ~$17B. Penetration is very early-stage.

### Inorganic Growth (Accretive Acquisitions)

The occupational health market is highly fragmented — Concentra's 628 centers represent the largest national footprint by a wide margin according to company disclosures. The company has completed 200+ acquisitions since inception. The 2025 deployment of $333M across Nova and Pivot demonstrates the acceleration enabled by public-market access.

FY2026 guidance of $2.25-2.35B implies 4-9% growth. Consensus sits at $2.32B. Given the conservative guidance history (Q4 beat) and acquisition pipeline, the upper end of guidance appears achievable.

### Estimate Revision Trajectory

Current-year EPS estimates show slight positive momentum over 30 days (+0.6% to $1.45) but modest negative revision over 90 days (-2.7% from $1.50). The estimate trajectory is stabilizing after post-IPO adjustments, and the Q4 beat suggests upward pressure at next reporting.

## Valuation Assessment

- Market cap: $2.93B
- Enterprise value (with ~$1.52B net debt): ~$4.44B
- EV/EBITDA: 10.3x (on FY2025 $432M)
- Trailing P/E: approximately 17.6x (on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.30); approximately 16.7x on adjusted EPS of $1.37
- Forward P/E: 15.7x (on FY2026E $1.45)
- FCF yield: 6.7% ($198M / $2.93B)
- Dividend yield: 1.1%

### Relative Valuation

Concentra's forward P/E of 15.7x represents a meaningful discount to healthcare services peers. The closest comparable by business model and valuation — Addus Homecare (ADUS) at 17.8x — operates in home health with lower margins and less dominant positioning. Pediatrix Medical (MD) at 10.8x is structurally impaired by physician practice management headwinds.

A more appropriate comp set for Concentra's growth profile and market position would likely be valued at 20-25x forward earnings, though the peer set is imperfect given Concentra's unique occupational-health focus, post-spin leverage, and legal overhang. At 20x FY2026E EPS of $1.45, the stock would trade at $29.

### Analyst Consensus

RBC Capital (Feb 2026) highlighted the "inherently stable" business model with a $31 target. JPMorgan initiated with Overweight and a $31 target citing favorable outpatient positioning.

The upside to analyst targets represents meaningful asymmetry when combined with the 1.1% dividend yield and active buyback program ($80M remaining authorization through December 2027).

### Why the Market Is Mispricing This Stock

The discount exists for three identifiable reasons:
1. **Spin-off neglect:** Small-cap healthcare spins from larger parents typically trade at a discount for 12-18 months as the investor base transitions. CON's $2.9B market cap is too small for many generalist healthcare funds.
2. **Leverage optics:** The ~4.0x D/E and distress-zone Z-Score flag in quantitative screens as "distressed," causing systematic selling by risk-managed strategies. The operational reality (3.4x net leverage, long-dated maturities, $198M FCF) contradicts this classification.
3. **Investigation overhang:** DOJ and California probes, plus an unsealed California qui tam lawsuit, create uncertainty that precludes institutional buying until resolution.

## Competitive Landscape

Concentra's competitive advantages are structural:

1. **Scale:** 628 centers in 85 of 100 largest MSAs. 64% of U.S. employers have a location within 12 miles. The occupational health market is highly fragmented beyond Concentra, consisting of independent practices, regional players, and hospital-affiliated clinics.
2. **Network effects:** Workers' comp carriers route claims to Concentra because of demonstrated outcomes (25% lower costs, 65 fewer lost days). More volume produces better data, which produces better outcomes, which drives more carrier referrals.
3. **Regulatory moat:** Navigating 38+ state fee schedules, occupational health licensure requirements, and workers' comp carrier networks creates meaningful barriers to entry.
4. **Switching costs:** 98% of top-100 customers retained for 10+ years. Employer integration (onsite clinics, compliance programs, drug testing protocols) creates operational stickiness.
5. **Data/outcomes:** 45+ years of occupational health data across 13.5M annual visits enables evidence-based treatment protocols that drive better outcomes and lower costs.

The primary competitive risk is the secular decline in workplace injuries as automation reduces physical labor. This is a 10-20 year gradual headwind partially offset by an aging workforce generating more severe injuries.

## Risk Assessment

### DOJ, California Investigations & Qui Tam Lawsuits (HIGH)

Concentra faces multiple legal proceedings related to physical therapy billing:

- **DOJ investigation** (since 2021): The DOJ Civil Division, Fraud Section is investigating billing for physical therapy services. Select Medical received the initial inquiry letter; in 2022-2023, DOJ sought and Concentra produced additional data/documents relating to physical therapy services furnished by Concentra. A related qui tam lawsuit was unsealed in May 2024 after the U.S. declined to intervene; that lawsuit names Select Medical entities and does not name Concentra as a defendant. However, DOJ's request for Concentra-specific data means the risk to Concentra is not zero.

- **California Department of Insurance investigation** (since February 5, 2024): Under the California Insurance Frauds Prevention Act, relating to billing and coding for physical therapy claims submitted to commercial insurers and workers' compensation carriers in California. This subpoena was received approximately one month after Select announced its intention to separate Concentra on January 3, 2024.

- **California qui tam lawsuit** (unsealed November 10, 2025): A separate qui tam action under the California Insurance Fraud Prevention Act, brought by a whistleblower. The California Department of Insurance declined to intervene. The complaint alleges that certain physical-therapy referral guidelines led to false/fraudulent reimbursement claims and also alleges retaliation. This represents an active, unsealed legal proceeding against the company.

The 2025 10-K states the company is unable to predict the timing or outcome of these matters. The filing does not identify a specific accrued liability for these proceedings.

**Speculative risk quantification:** If physical therapy represents ~15-20% of center revenue (~$200-260M annually), and investigations cover multiple years of billing, potential resolution could range widely depending on scope, findings, and forum. We estimate, speculatively, that exposure could range from immaterial (if practices are deemed compliant) to $50-150M+ in a pessimistic scenario — but this range is an analyst assumption, not a validated estimate. The filings do not provide sufficient information to bound this risk with confidence. A nationwide DOJ action, if it were to name Concentra, could be larger. Potential outcomes also include a Corporate Integrity Agreement that could constrain operations.

### Leverage Risk (MODERATE-HIGH)

A recession-driven employment decline of 5%+ would compress EBITDA toward $370-380M, pushing leverage above 4.0x. In a severe scenario (10% employment decline sustained for 4+ quarters), leverage could approach 4.5-5.0x — still within covenant but limiting financial flexibility for acquisitions or capital returns.

### Data Breach Litigation (MODERATE)

The PJ&A breach affecting nearly 4M patients has generated multiple lawsuits. Settlement costs are unpredictable but typically range from $5-20 per affected individual in healthcare breach cases, implying potential exposure of $20-80M. Insurance coverage likely offsets a portion.

### Macro Sensitivity (MODERATE)

Workers' comp volume is directly tied to employment levels. In a recession, non-farm payrolls could decline 3-5%, translating to roughly proportional visit volume decline. Additionally, BLS data show that workplace injury/illness incidence rates continue their long-term structural decline (2024 total recordable case rate was the lowest since 2003), which limits the organic volume tailwind from injury trends specifically. However, even during the 2008-2009 recession, Concentra maintained profitability — the business model is defensive relative to discretionary healthcare.

## Conclusion

Concentra Group Holdings represents a well-positioned occupational health platform trading at a meaningful discount to intrinsic value due to structural factors (spin-off neglect, leverage optics, investigation overhang) rather than operational deterioration. The company grew revenue 14% with expanding EBITDA margins, generated $198M in FCF, and beat Q4 earnings by 22%. At 15.7x FY2026E earnings with 6.7% FCF yield, a dominant market position (1 in 4 U.S. work injuries), and analyst targets in the high-$20s to low-$30s, the asymmetry favors a long position.

The primary risks — billing investigations and structural leverage — are real but manageable in our base case. The investigation exposure remains difficult to quantify with confidence given limited disclosure in filings; our speculative estimate of $50-150M in a pessimistic scenario should be weighed against the $4.4B enterprise value and $198M annual FCF, but investors should recognize this range is an assumption rather than a validated estimate. The leverage, while optically elevated at ~4.0x D/E, is serviced comfortably at 3.4x net leverage with long-dated maturities and active paydown from $200M+ annual FCF, with management targeting 3.0x or below in 2026.

We rate CON **BUY** with a 12-month target of $29, representing 27% upside. Position sizing should reflect the investigation overhang and limited liquidity (average daily volume ~850K shares / ~$19M daily turnover). A 2% portfolio allocation is appropriate.

These corrections weaken two elements of the original thesis (the lockup-expiry catalyst and the bounded legal-risk argument) without, in our view, invalidating the core BUY recommendation based on operational performance and valuation.
