## Executive Summary

Darling Ingredients has moved from the bottom of its cycle to near the top of it in two quarters. Fiscal 2025 — the period covered by the most recent 10-K — produced GAAP net income of $62.8M on $6,135.9M of revenue, with Fuel Ingredients segment operating income of $3.4M against $197.2M a year earlier. Q2 FY2026, reported July 30, produced net income attributable to Darling of $387.3M, GAAP diluted EPS of $2.41 against $0.08, segment operating income of $555.2M against $75.9M, and combined Adjusted EBITDA (core plus Darling's 50% share of Diamond Green Diesel) of $741.7M against $249.5M. First-half combined Adjusted EBITDA of $1,148.6M already exceeds all of fiscal 2025's $1,026.4M. Current-year EPS estimates are up 49.5% in 90 days to $6.66, and current-quarter estimates are up 43.8% in 30 days.

The mechanism is identifiable and almost entirely external. EPA's Final RFS "Set 2" rule, published April 1, 2026 and effective June 15, raised the biomass-based diesel obligation from 5.36 billion RINs in 2025 to 9.07 billion in 2026 including small-refinery-exemption reallocation — roughly a 69% step-up in mandated demand for the fuel DGD makes. IRS Notice 2026-41 (July 10, 2026, retroactive to January 1) set the 2026 on-road 45Z rate at $1.09/gallon. Higher RIN and credit values bid up domestic low-carbon-intensity feedstock, which lifted Darling's Feed segment (bleachable fancy tallow +51.3%, pet-food poultry meal +65.8%), while DGD's converter spread expanded despite paying those higher feedstock costs. Darling sits on both ends of that chain. The Feed sales bridge shows the character of the move precisely: of the $213.0M Q2 increase, $209.6M came from finished-product price and $4.0M from volume, with raw material processed of 3.08 million metric tons described as "consistent" with the prior year.

Two things keep this at HOLD rather than an upgrade despite a forward multiple near 10x against a peer median of 21.1x. First, earnings quality: Darling booked $457.4M of DGD equity income in H1 while receiving $279.7M of DGD distributions and paying in $190.6M of capital contributions — net cash of roughly $89M — and then lent DGD another $100M under the partner facility after quarter-end. DGD's own "total other current assets" rose from $1,199.2M to $2,175.2M in six months, plausibly dominated by unmonetized 45Z receivables. Second, durability: consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.75 sits 14% below the FY2026 figure of $6.66, because the renewable-diesel RIN equivalence value drops from 1.7 to 1.5 on January 1, 2027 (roughly 12% fewer RINs per gallon) and the 45Z SAF rate was cut to $1.00/gallon nominal for post-2025 production. The market is applying a low multiple to earnings it does not believe are the run rate, and on the evidence available that skepticism is reasonable rather than a mispricing.

## Company Overview

Darling collects and converts animal by-products, used cooking oil, trap grease, bakery residuals, blood, hides, and bones into fats, protein meals, collagen and gelatin, natural casings, crude heparin, fertilizer, biogas, and — through DGD — renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel. It operates more than 260 locations across five continents in three reported segments.

**Feed Ingredients** ($1,149.5M Q2 net sales, 66.7% of total; $150.7M segment operating income) is the rendering core: North America, Europe (Sonac), South America (FASA), plus UCO collection, bakery residuals, blood plasma, hides, and grease trap services. **Food Ingredients** ($408.5M; $74.9M) is collagen and gelatin (Rousselot, Gelnex), natural casings, heparin, and edible fats. **Fuel Ingredients** contributes only $166.1M of consolidated sales but $366.8M of segment operating income, because it houses Darling's 50% equity-method interest in DGD — a 50/50 joint venture with Valero formed in 2011, operating St. Charles, Louisiana and Port Arthur, Texas with roughly 1.2 billion gallons of annual capacity, about half of Port Arthur's 470M gallons upgradeable to SAF.

The moat is physical rather than brand-based: collection routes, a rail fleet, an Iowa Mississippi-River transload facility, and indoor UCO collection units placed in restaurants in exchange for extended contracts. Route density and permitted-facility scarcity are the barriers. DGD is now vertically integrated into that network: Darling collects fats, processes them, and rails refined fats to DGD as feedstock, isolating UCO to preserve low-CI identity for credit qualification.

The counterparty structure deserves precision. Darling *sells* feedstock to DGD (DGD is the customer, Darling the supplier) under a 2011 Raw Material Agreement that offers fats at market prices but does **not** obligate DGD to purchase. Darling and Valero each own 50%, each guarantee proportionate shares of certain terminaling obligations (up to ~$50M each at IMTT, ~$160M each at GT Logistics declining over 20 years, no liability recorded), and each committed $100M to a $200M partner revolver extended to June 2029. DGD's own $400M CoBank revolver is non-recourse to the partners.

## Financial Analysis

**The trough and the recovery.** Net income attributable to Darling fell four straight years: $737.7M (FY2022) → $647.7M (FY2023) → $278.9M (FY2024) → $62.8M (FY2025). Segment operating income fell from roughly $1.03B in FY2022 to $273.4M in FY2025, down 41.6% year over year. Fiscal 2025 was a 53-week year, with the extra week contributing $122.1M of sales and $9.8M of operating income. DGD standalone posted a net loss of $68.5M for calendar 2025 on 1.003 billion gallons, with Darling's share of DGD Adjusted EBITDA of $103.7M ($0.21/gallon), a figure flattered by a favorable $140.1M lower-of-cost-or-market inventory reversal.

A useful detail from that period: the *core* business improved through the trough. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA excluding DGD was $922.6M versus $789.9M in FY2024, up 16.8%. The entire consolidated deterioration sat in the JV.

Food Ingredients recognized $18.5M of net tariff recoveries following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA-based tariffs were unlawful and CBP's subsequent refund process; that single item accounts for roughly 57% of the segment's $32.3M operating income increase and most of its 940bp margin expansion. Acquisition and integration costs of $13.2M in the quarter ($18.2M in H1, versus $4.9M) run the other way.

**Cash conversion is the analytical crux.** H1 operating cash flow was $686.5M versus $394.8M. But reconcile the JV literally: $462.2M of equity income from DGD and other unconsolidated entities removed as non-cash; $281.7M of distributions received (of which $279.7M DGD); $190.6M of capital contributions paid in. Net cash from DGD in H1 was therefore roughly $89M against $457.4M of recognized DGD income — and after quarter-end DGD drew $200M on the partner facility, $100M of it from Darling. The reason is visible on DGD's balance sheet, where other current assets rose ~$976M in six months. Some of that is ordinary working capital on a 144% revenue increase; the attribution to 45Z receivables is an inference the 10-Q does not confirm, since it does not disaggregate. Either way, the partners are funding a working-capital gap that reported earnings do not show.

The August 11 agreement to sell approximately $150M of DGD-generated production tax credits to a corporate buyer, with proceeds expected by end of Q3 subject to funding conditions, is the monetization channel. Transfer-market pricing appears to have improved: discount and broker fees consumed $29.1M of $236.5M of DGD Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 (12.3%), versus a derived ~$14.2M on $354.4M of credits recorded in H1 FY2026 (~4%). That derivation rests on backing fees out of two disclosed subtotals against ambiguous footnote language, so treat it as indicative.

Tax expense is largely non-cash: H1 income tax expense of $149.3M against $116.0M of deferred taxes added back and cash taxes paid of $49.1M; the deferred tax liability rose from $240.6M to $366.4M.

**Balance sheet (July 4, 2026).** Total debt $3,947.7M against $160.7M of cash — net debt $3,787.0M. Composition: revolver $615.8M, Term A $891.0M (2031), 6% notes due 2030 $1,000M, **5.25% notes due April 2027 $500M**, 4.5% euro notes due 2032 (€750M / $857.7M), other $100.7M. Revolver availability $1,308.0M of a $2.0B facility; covenant compliant. Current ratio 1.56x; total debt/equity 0.75x.

Derived leverage: net debt/LTM combined Adjusted EBITDA ($1,729.7M) ≈ **2.19x**; net debt/LTM core-only ($1,134.2M) ≈ **3.34x**. Interest expense of $109.6M in H1 (~$220M annualized) covers roughly 5.2x on core EBITDA. Roughly $1.5B of the stack is floating (Term A at SOFR+1.625% = 5.269%), hedged by only $300M notional of swaps at 3.420% running into Q2 2027. The 10-Q states plainly that the company "is highly leveraged" and that "at the date of this report, the Company has not made a decision whether it will refinance or repay the 5.25% Notes at maturity."

One reconciliation flag: management's stated $223M net debt reduction cannot be tied to the balance sheets. Comparing January 3 to July 4, net debt fell from $3,848.8M to $3,787.0M — about $62M — of which $28.8M came from FX translation of euro debt rather than repayment. The $223M figure is most plausibly a single-quarter measure and should be treated as issuer-stated over an unspecified interval.

**Data conflict noted:** the supplied profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 16.54 alongside EPS of $0.39, which are mutually inconsistent ($65.16/$0.39 = 167x). Annualized H1 GAAP diluted EPS of $3.24 gives $6.48 and ~10.1x; current-year consensus of $6.66 gives 9.8x. I use the consensus and derived figures throughout.

**Score note.** Financial health moves up modestly from the prior 5.0 to 5.6: leverage at 2.19x combined and $1.31B of revolver availability are genuinely better than at the trough, and H1 operating cash flow nearly doubled. What caps it is the deferred cash conversion at the JV, $1.5B of largely unhedged floating debt, an undecided $500M 2027 maturity, and goodwill at 48% of book equity.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue line grew 16.4% in Q2, but the composition matters more than the rate. Feed volume was flat — 3.08 million metric tons "consistent" year over year, +0.5% for six months — and 98% of that segment's revenue growth came from finished-product price. North American slaughter throughput caps volume; there is no organic unit-growth engine in the core business. Earnings are a levered call on fat and protein prices, which are in turn levered to renewable-fuel credit values, against what MD&A describes as "a substantial amount of fixed operating costs."

Management guided Q3 core Ingredients Adjusted EBITDA to $325–340M, up from the $260–275M range guided for Q2, and indicated DGD production intent of approximately 335 million gallons for the quarter. That last figure annualizes to roughly 1.34 billion gallons against ~1.2 billion of nameplate capacity, which implies either very high utilization plus inventory movement or a broader definition; confirm it against the company's supplemental disclosure.

The inorganic pipeline is more interesting than the organic one. Three transactions in six months are filing-verified: the June 2, 2026 acquisition of UPI Bovinos NewCo in Brazil (three rendering plants bought out of bankruptcy for ~$122.2M, of which $70.9M — 58% — was allocated to goodwill), the sale of most of the U.S. grease trap business to a Waste Resource Management subsidiary ($84.1M received July 21), and the agreed sale of CTH natural casings to Van Hessen for ~€58.0M expected to close by year-end. Neither disposal was treated as discontinued operations.

The most consequential pending item is the Tessenderlo collagen joint venture (definitive agreement December 10, 2025). Both parties contribute their collagen and gelatin segments into NewCo Collagen LLC, with Darling holding 85% and Tessenderlo 15%, subject to regulatory approvals. Read literally, Darling is consolidating a larger collagen platform and paying with a minority stake rather than cash. Given Darling's own claim of roughly 30% of global collagen output, adding a second sizable producer raises a genuine antitrust review question that the filings do not address, and no purchase-price allocation exists.

Against that, the black soldier fly venture (EnviroFlight) took a ~$29.2M strategic realignment charge in FY2025, part of $58.0M total restructuring. The long-touted alternative-protein growth option has been materially written down, with capital redeployed toward conventional rendering density and collagen scale.

Consensus captures the shape of this well: current-year EPS $6.66, next-year $5.75. Estimates are rising sharply (+49.5% and +18.2% over 90 days respectively) but the *level* declines year over year. The estimate-revision magnitude is striking; the revenue growth accompanying it (16.4%, price-driven, flat volume) is not the accelerating-unit-demand pattern that makes such revisions self-reinforcing. Growth prospects score 5.5.

The normalized figure is built as follows: core FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1,255–1,290M (H1 $608.2M plus ~$332M at the Q3 guidance midpoint plus ~$320M for Q4), paired with a DGD contribution well below the Q2 run rate — $200–250M per quarter of Darling's share rather than $389M. The 6.1x annualized-H1 figure is a sensitivity, not a forecast; it assumes the Q2 credit-spread environment persists and ignores DGD turnaround timing and rendering seasonality.

At 9.8x current-year against a peer median of 21.1x, the surface case is that DAR is half-priced. The discount is explicable rather than anomalous. First, the earnings are policy-derived and non-contractual: the 45Z rate is set by IRS notice, RVOs by EPA rulemaking, and RIN prices by a market the administration was reported on July 29 to be considering intervening in via a compliance-deadline extension "amid high renewable credit costs." Second, the 2027 mechanics are already legislated against the company: the RD equivalence value falls from 1.7 to 1.5 on January 1, 2027, cutting RINs generated per gallon by roughly 12% unless producers successfully petition for higher values, and the 45Z SAF rate fell to $1.00/gallon nominal for post-2025 production. Third, a meaningful share of reported profit is non-cash equity income whose cash conversion is deferred and discounted, and a portion of Feed segment profit is timing-dependent — Darling eliminated $104.7M of Q2 sales to defer $29.8M of its own share of profit on feedstock sitting in DGD inventory.

Against those, three specific reasons the market may be too pessimistic. The RFS Set 2 rule locks elevated obligations for both 2026 and 2027 (total renewable fuel 26.81 and 27.02 billion RINs versus 22.33 in 2025), which is two years of visible mandated demand rather than a spot condition. The $1.0B repurchase authorization refreshed August 5 would retire ~15.4M shares, 9.8% of the count, at current prices, credible given $1.31B of revolver capacity and ~$150M of credit-sale proceeds inbound, though the prior $500M program saw only $34.7M of FY2025 utilization. And 45Z's North America feedstock requirement from 2026 structurally disadvantages competitors reliant on imported Chinese and Southeast Asian UCO.

Netting those, base-case fair value lands near **$70**: 12.0x next-year consensus of $5.75 gives $69, and 8.5x on $1.75B of normalized combined EBITDA gives $14.875B EV less $3.787B net debt = $11.09B equity, or $70.3 per share. That is +7.4% from $65.16.

Analyst targets frame the entry: low $65.00, mean $78.58, median $79.00, high $90.00, with 11 of 12 ratings at Buy or Strong Buy. The stock trades at the exact bottom of that range and 1.3% below its 52-week high of $66.02. Consensus targets often function as floors on cheap inflection stocks; here the multiple is low but the earnings base is at a cyclical peak with legislated 2027 headwinds, which is a different configuration. Valuation scores 5.5 — up from the prior 4.5 because the estimate reset genuinely compressed the forward multiple, but not higher, because a low multiple on peak policy-driven earnings with deferred cash conversion is the classic pattern where cheapness fails to convert into return.

## Competitive Landscape

Darling's position is strong on the axes that matter and structurally improving on one of them. The company states it processes roughly 15% of the world's animal agricultural by-products and produces roughly 30% of the world's collagen; these are issuer claims, not audited studies. In rendering, the principal U.S. competitors are Baker Commodities and Sanimax, with the industry consolidated substantially after Darling's 2022 Valley Proteins and FASA acquisitions. In collagen and gelatin, the set is Gelita, Nitta, Weishardt, and Tessenderlo — the last converting from rival to 15% partner. In renewable fuels, DGD competes with Neste, Chevron REG, Phillips 66 Rodeo, Marathon/Neste Martinez, and Vertex.

Two regulatory changes shift relative position in Darling's favor. The 45Z requirement, effective 2026, that eligible transportation fuel be derived exclusively from feedstock produced or grown in the U.S., Mexico, or Canada disqualifies imported UCO that had been flooding Gulf Coast plants. And EPA has deferred rather than abandoned the Import RIN Reduction, which would halve RINs for imported fuel and for domestic fuel made from foreign feedstock, now reconsidered for 2028 — a forward option on domestic feedstock scarcity value. Darling's collection network is domestic; Brazil, however, is not in the qualifying set, which raises an unresolved question about how FASA and Bovinos volumes are routed and about the strategic logic of the Brazil acquisition.

Two offsets sit inside the same integration. The equivalence-value cut to 1.5 for both RD and renewable jet removes RD's prior structural advantage over SAF — plausibly helpful for Port Arthur's SAF upgrade but a direct headwind to the base renewable diesel book. More fundamentally, the configuration that produced Q2 — feedstock prices up 51% while the converter's spread also expanded — is the same configuration that reversed violently in FY2024–25, when credit values compressed and Fuel Ingredients operating income fell to $3.4M. Integration amplifies the upside and the downside symmetrically; it is a leveraged policy position rather than diversification.

Customer concentration moved the wrong way. Sales to DGD were 18% of the total in H1 FY2025 and 20% in H1 FY2026; 20% in Q2 FY2025 and 24% in Q2 FY2026. FY2025 top-10 customers were roughly 40% of product sales. Secondary commentary framing Darling as "less dependent on traditional rendering economics" describes segment mix, not counterparty exposure. Darling's largest customer is a related party it half-owns, lends to, and partially guarantees — four distinct legal capacities toward one counterparty. Competitive position holds at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Policy is the earnings driver and it is not contractual.** MD&A attributes the Fuel improvement to production volumes, production tax credits at DGD, RIN values following EPA's 2026-27 rulemaking, and diesel prices. Each is a policy or commodity variable. The FY2025 10-K also flags EPA's February 12, 2026 repeal of the 2009 endangerment finding, which sits awkwardly alongside the same agency's aggressively pro-biofuel RFS rulemaking and, if it survives challenge, could weaken the architecture supporting federal GHG regulation.

**Credit monetization is a separate risk from credit earning.** DGD recorded $354.4M of production tax credits in H1 as a reduction of cost of sales; those hit equity income immediately while cash arrives later at a discount, subject to buyer demand. The August 11 release explicitly names "failure to satisfy closing conditions" as a risk on the $150M sale.

**No control over the JV.** Darling cannot unilaterally set DGD's distributions, capex, or turnaround schedule. DGD1 was idled during FY2025 and three catalyst turnarounds occurred. Valero reports DGD's results first, so Darling's disclosure cadence is partly determined by its partner.

**Goodwill.** $2,554.2M — 23% of assets and 48% of Darling's $5,245.7M of equity, plus $822.0M of intangibles. The FY2025 10-K disclosed one of six reporting units with fair value only 20–30% above carrying value and $563.3M of goodwill; the unit was not named. A charge there would hit GAAP net income, equity, and book-value multiples but not Adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, or covenant compliance.

**Leverage plus the 2027 decision.** $500M of 5.25% notes mature April 15, 2027 with no stated refinance-or-repay decision; long-term classification depends on continued revolver availability. Only $300M of ~$1.5B floating exposure is hedged.

**Other specific items.** Multiemployer pension withdrawal liabilities on two U.S. plans that "could be material," with five other plans in critical/red zone and $3.3M accrued. The Lower Passaic River consent decree is on appeal and Occidental's CERCLA contribution suit against 100+ defendants including Darling remains live; total insurance/regulatory/environmental/litigation reserves are $92.9M. Animal-disease exposure (avian influenza, ASF, BSE, PED) can remove regional raw material or close export markets, with China a real revenue line ($74.8M in Q2). The Bovinos acquisition is excluded from management's FY2026 internal-control assessment. An out-of-period Food segment inventory adjustment of approximately $25.1M appears in the FY2025 MD&A and warrants re-verification. Six Item 5.02 filings between September 2025 and February 2026 were not read; whether any involved an executive departure or succession planning is unresolved, which matters with a 23-year incumbent CEO.

Risk factors score 4.0 — the count of specific, unpriced binaries here (2027 credit regime, goodwill unit, 2027 maturity, Tessenderlo antitrust, pension withdrawal, credit-transfer market) is unusually high.

Together this indicates no bearish institutional positioning, near-term hedging into the Q3 print, and enough implied movement that a patient buyer has a reasonable chance of a materially better entry within a quarter.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+35.1%)

Elevated RVOs are locked through 2027 and 45Z runs to 2029. If RIN values hold near current levels and DGD's petition path or SAF mix offsets the equivalence-value cut, FY2027 EPS holds near $6.75 rather than falling to $5.75. The 45Z North America feedstock rule permanently removes imported-UCO competitors from the U.S. pool, giving Darling's domestic collection network scarcity value that compounds if the Import RIN Reduction lands in 2028. The $1.0B buyback retires ~9.8% of shares, lifting per-share earnings on a shrinking count. Q3 confirms cash conversion as the $150M credit sale funds and DGD distributions exceed contributions, closing the GAAP-versus-cash gap that currently caps the multiple. Tessenderlo closes and consolidates a larger collagen platform for a 15% minority stake rather than cash. At 13x $6.75 the stock is $88, in line with the $90 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $42 (-35.5%)

RIN prices fall under political pressure — the administration was already weighing a compliance-deadline extension in July because of credit costs — while the equivalence-value cut removes 12% of RINs per gallon from January 2027 and the SAF rate cut bites. DGD's share of combined EBITDA reverts toward $150M per year rather than $389M per quarter, taking combined EBITDA to ~$1.35B. At 7.5x that is $10.1B EV less ~$3.9B of net debt, or roughly $39 per share; some support from the core business and buyback puts the floor near $42. The credit-transfer market tightens, DGD's receivable build continues, and partner capital calls consume the cash earmarked for repurchases and the April 2027 maturity. The 20–30%-cushion reporting unit takes a several-hundred-million-dollar goodwill charge. Feed volume stays flat, removing any offset, and Food's 36.3% gross margin normalizes as the $18.5M tariff recovery does not repeat.

### Probability-Weighted View

Base $70 at 55%, bull $88 at 22%, bear $42 at 23% produces an expected return near +3.6%. That is approximately fair from the current price, with dispersion of roughly ±35% around it — a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 FY2026 report (late October/early November 2026), the first period in which the $150M credit sale, the Q3 core EBITDA guide, and DGD's ~335 million gallon production intent can all be tested against cash.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Cash conversion closes.** Q3 shows DGD distributions received exceeding capital contributions plus partner-loan advances, with the $150M credit sale funded and DGD's other current assets flat or declining from $2,175.2M. This is the single most important item; it would convert a ~10x forward multiple from suspect to real.
2. **Price at or below $55** (roughly 9.6x next-year consensus, 16% below current) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore a margin of safety against the $42 bear case.
3. **Buyback execution at scale** — repurchases running above $150M per quarter against the $1.0B authorization, alongside a resolved plan for the $500M April 2027 notes.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **FY2027 consensus falls below $4.50** (versus $5.75 today) on RIN compression, an unsuccessful equivalence-value petition, or 45Z rate changes — that would put the stock above 14x on declining earnings.
2. **DGD requires a further partner capital call or loan draw beyond the $100M already advanced post-quarter** without a corresponding increase in distributions, indicating the credit receivable build is structural rather than timing.
3. **Goodwill impairment at the 20–30%-cushion reporting unit**, or leverage on core-only EBITDA rising above 4.0x, or a decision to fund the April 2027 maturity from the revolver at current floating rates.
4. Loss or material reduction of 45Z eligibility for a significant share of DGD's feedstock, including any adverse clarification on Brazilian tallow routing.

**Existing holders:** maintain exposure. The combination of a 9.8x current-year multiple, a two-year legislated demand mandate, and a 9.8%-of-float repurchase authorization is enough to hold through the Q3 print. **Prospective buyers:** wait.
