**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Energizer Holdings is a heavily levered consumer-staples cash generator carrying ~$3.34B of debt against ~$173M of book equity. The investment question is whether modest EBITDA stability plus deliberate debt paydown can re-rate an equity stub trading at roughly 7x adjusted earnings while organic volume erodes; the operating business itself survives, generating steady cash through mature battery and auto-care franchises. The two prior reports on this name carried AVOID at declining scores (4.4 → 4.2), built around a bear-case "retest of the $15.75 52-week low." That call has been overtaken by price action and by the actions of informed insiders.

The stock troughed at $15.75 on 2026-05-20 and has since risen ~27% to ~$20, coinciding with a recovery in operating cash flow, a raised full-year profit outlook (Q2 FY26 reported May 5), and continued accumulation by a 5%+ holder. The CEO and a director bought stock on the open market at $17.11 and $17.40 — above where the prior bear case wanted to add. A bearish view here is substantially priced: the analyst consensus median target is $19 versus a current ~$20, the multiple is ~7x, and the yield is 5.9% and covered. The genuine bearish pillars (organic volume down ~5.5% in Q2 FY26, earnings quality propped up by a $47.6M non-cash tariff refund and ~$55-65M/yr of contingent production credits, and zero equity cushion) are real, but they are the reasons the stock trades where it does, not new information that the market has yet to absorb.

I am moving the recommendation to HOLD, classified as an income hold. The distribution of outcomes is two-sided: a deleveraging-plus-volume-stabilization path supports a re-rating toward the high-$20s; a continued volume bleed and policy clawback of the tax/tariff items pressures the equity back toward the mid-teens. With the base case roughly flat-to-modestly-higher around the $19-21 range and a covered ~5.9% yield paying the carry, the asymmetry no longer justifies an active avoid, but the leverage and structural volume erosion keep it well short of a buy.

## Company Overview

Energizer was spun off from Edgewell Personal Care in 2015 and operates two segments:

- **Batteries & Lights (~79% of sales, ~$2.33B FY25):** Energizer, Eveready, Rayovac, and Varta (Latin America/Asia Pacific) brands across alkaline, lithium, carbon zinc, rechargeable, hearing-aid and specialty batteries, plus flashlights and lanterns. Segment profit ~$542M (~23% margin).
- **Auto Care (~21% of sales, ~$620M FY25):** Armor All, STP, A/C PRO, and appearance/fragrance brands. Segment profit ~$106M; structurally the higher-growth, higher-margin franchise, though it weakened sharply in H1 FY26 (segment profit −32%).

The competitive base rests on long-standing brand equity (2,900+ battery trademarks) and broad retail distribution across mass, club, dollar, drug, and e-commerce channels. Walmart is the only customer above 10% of sales, at 12.8% of FY25 revenue. The categories are mature and competitive, pressured by private label, hard discounters, e-commerce price transparency, and the slow secular shift toward built-in/rechargeable batteries in devices. Management characterizes long-term primary-battery category volume as flat to slightly positive. Fiscal year ends September 30; roughly 40% of sales are foreign.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature is the capital structure. Total debt of ~$3.34B sits against ~$173M of equity (D/E ~26x), with goodwill ($1.05B) and intangibles (~$1.0B) exceeding book equity roughly 12x. The Altman Z-Score of 0.96 reads "distress zone," but that score is mechanically depressed by thin equity-to-assets for a business with stable cash flow; it overstates true bankruptcy probability while correctly flagging that there is no equity buffer. Any sizable goodwill/intangible impairment or sustained cash-flow shock would translate directly into the equity value.

Revenue is roughly flat-to-declining organically. FY25 was $2.95B (+2.3% reported, +0.7% organic). H1 FY26 deteriorated materially: Q2 organic sales fell 5.5% (volume −6.1%) and six-month organic fell 4.9%, driven by softer U.S. consumer demand in both segments, a battery-order timing shift tied to plastic-free packaging conversion, and a slow auto-care season start.

Reported profitability is heavily adjusted and noisy. FY25 GAAP net income of $239M ($3.32 EPS) was inflated by a one-time $78M retroactive 45X production tax credit; adjusted EPS was $3.52. H1 FY26 GAAP net income collapsed to $6.7M (from $50.6M) on restructuring, a $26.1M UK pension settlement loss, and lower volume, but adjusted EPS held at $1.25 (vs. $1.35). Q2 FY26 adjusted gross margin spiked to 44.4%, substantially the $47.6M IEEPA tariff refund booked in COGS, a non-cash, contingent benefit recorded as a long-term receivable rather than cash collected this year. Stripping the policy items, the underlying business is flat-to-declining with eroding volume.

Cash flow is the more reassuring part of the story. FY25 operating cash flow fell to $147M (from $429.6M) on a working-capital build for tariff mitigation and plastic-free inventory, leaving FCF of only $63.2M (~2% margin), thin against ~$154M of annual interest and ~$87M of dividends. H1 FY26 OCF recovered to $147.8M as inventory unwound, which is the more representative run-rate. Capital allocation has shifted sensibly: the dividend held at $0.30/quarter (~$87M/yr), buybacks pulled back sharply from $89.7M in FY25 to $4.5M in H1 FY26, and ~$94M went to term-loan paydown. Liquidity is adequate — $172.5M cash plus a $492.4M undrawn revolver, covenant compliant, with nearest large maturities pushed to 2028/2029/2032/2033. About $764M of debt is variable-rate, partly hedged by a $500M SOFR swap at 1.042% expiring December 2027; that swap roll-off is a future interest-cost risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine here at present. H1 FY26 organic declines reflect demand softness, not merely order timing, and the Auto Care segment — the relative growth franchise — saw profit fall 32% in H1. Management called Q3 FY26 a "potential inflection" toward returning to growth, which is the central swing variable for the thesis. That framing is unproven; the burden of proof sits with management.

Estimate revisions are mixed and net negative on the forward quarters. The current quarter has been cut 11.8% over 30 days and the next quarter 11.9%, while the full-year estimate was revised up 5.5% over 30 days (to $3.50) and next-year cut 3.0% (to $3.65). The full-year uptick is largely the mechanical flow-through of the Q2 tariff-refund benefit rather than evidence of accelerating demand. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is a slow-grower with policy-driven noise in the reported line.

The earnings surprise record is reasonable (three beats, one miss over four quarters, including a 102.8% beat in the March 2026 quarter), but the magnitude of the recent beats was inflated by non-operating items. The durable growth case is the deleveraging story: ~$3.4B of debt against a ~$1.5B equity cap means that steady EBITDA plus consistent debt paydown and eventual offshore-cash repatriation can compound equity value even with flat revenue. That mechanical de-risking is real but requires volume to stop falling. As a Consumer Staples slow-grower, the growth prospects score is appropriately low.

## Valuation Assessment

ENR trades at ~7.1x trailing/forward earnings on $3.32-3.50 EPS, against a Household Products peer median P/E of 19.0x. The discount is partly earned — peers like WD-40 (38x) and Interparfums (19x) are higher-quality, lower-leverage compounders, and Edgewell screens at a negative multiple on impaired earnings. ENR's ~26x leverage and structurally declining volume justify a meaningful discount to the median. The question is whether a 7x multiple plus a covered 5.9% yield over-discounts the situation.

The market has already priced the bear case. The consensus median target is $19 versus current ~$20, the mean is $20.67, with a range of $18 low to $28 high. The stock sits ~34% below its 52-week high of $30.29 and has round-tripped off its $15.75 low. The bearish pillars — volume erosion, low earnings quality, no equity cushion — are exactly why the multiple compressed from the high-20s. An active AVOID at this level would be issuing a view the market has substantially absorbed.

On a sum-of-cash-flows basis, ~$140-150M of normalized FCF (using the H1 FY26 OCF recovery rate net of ~$30M capex) against a ~$1.5B cap is a ~9-10% FCF yield, which supports the dividend and the deleveraging path. I do not treat the 7x multiple as a high-conviction buy signal — a low multiple on a levered slow-grower with falling forward-quarter estimates is the textbook value-trap profile, and cheapness alone has been an unreliable predictor. But cheapness combined with a covered yield, adequate liquidity, and informed insider buying above the current price is enough to support a HOLD rather than an avoid. Valuation scores a 5.5: cheap relative to peers and partly de-risked by the yield, but lacking a clean re-rating catalyst given the volume trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Energizer holds a defensible but not improving position. Its moat is brand equity and shelf-space breadth in mature categories, which provides pricing resilience but no organic growth. The trajectory is the concern: private-label and hard-discounter share gains, e-commerce price transparency, and the secular migration to built-in/rechargeable batteries (rechargeable hearing aids being a clear example) gradually erode primary-battery volume. The H1 FY26 volume declines are consistent with a franchise holding share at best while the category drifts.

Auto Care (Armor All, STP) is the relative innovation engine, with launches like the Armor All Podium Series, but it proved cyclical and seasonal with a sharp H1 FY26 profit decline. Walmart concentration at 12.8% is a manageable but real channel dependency, compounded by an ongoing Walmart antitrust price-fixing class action (consolidated, no accrual taken). The competitive position is stable-to-slowly-deteriorating, warranting a 4.5: level is adequate, but the trajectory offers no positive surprise the market is missing.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage with no equity cushion** is the dominant risk. Annual interest of ~$154M against thin FCF means any sustained cash-flow deterioration or impairment pressures the equity disproportionately. The SOFR swap roll-off in December 2027 raises future interest cost.

**Volume erosion** is the operating risk that matters most. The H1 FY26 organic declines need to stabilize; management's Q3 "inflection" call is the near-term test. A continued ~5% organic bleed would compress EBITDA and slow the deleveraging thesis.

**Earnings quality and policy contingency.** The FY25 production-credit catch-up and the ~$64.3M IEEPA tariff refund (only partly recognized, recorded as an uncollected long-term receivable) are material to reported profit and politically/regulatorily contingent. The U.S. promptly replaced IEEPA tariffs with a new 10% Section 122 global tariff, so cost pressure persists even as the refund is litigated and collected over uncertain timing. The 45X battery production credits (~$55-65M/yr) face legislative risk after OBBBA already modified the IRA.

**Impairment risk.** The Rayovac trade name fair value exceeds carrying value ($337M) by only ~17%; a 50bp discount-rate move implies ~$16.7M of additional impairment, on top of FY24's $110.6M of Rayovac/Varta write-downs. With goodwill and intangibles dwarfing equity, impairments hit the equity value directly.

**Governance/FX.** A May 5, 2026 8-K Item 5.02 flagged an officer departure; the CFO (John Drabik) remained in place per the 10-Q signature, and the specific executive is unconfirmed. About 40% of sales are foreign, with Argentina and Egypt under highly-inflationary accounting and ~97% of cash held offshore, repatriation friction that also represents latent deleveraging optionality if repatriated efficiently.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.7% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 51.6% (a −7.0% spread), so options are pricing relatively cheap near-term vol at the 23rd percentile of the current chain. Front-month put/call skew is balanced at 2.5% and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.43, indicating no acute near-term bearish positioning. The term structure is in contango (44.7% near vs. 48.7% long-term), a normal/complacent configuration, though the 3-month tenor shows a notably negative skew (−14.0%) and elevated put/call volume (2.56), suggesting some hedging concentrated around the next earnings window. The reported "ATM IV 89.6% → 838.1%" delta is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Short interest at 10.5% of float with 5.6 days to cover reflects meaningful institutional bearish positioning, but it is not at squeeze-prone extremes. Net, the options market is not flashing an imminent directional catalyst, consistent with a two-sided HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$26-28)
Volume stabilizes in H2 FY26 as management's "inflection" materializes, the auto-care season normalizes, and the IEEPA refund converts to cash. Steady FCF of ~$140-150M funds continued term-loan paydown, gradually transferring enterprise value from debtholders to the equity stub. A levered slow-grower that re-rates even one to two turns of EBITDA (toward a still-cheap 9-10x earnings) on a stabilized volume picture reaches the high-$20s, validated by the CEO/director open-market buys at ~$17 and a 5%+ holder accumulating through $22. The covered yield pays investors to wait.

### Bear Case (~$15)
Organic volume continues to decline ~5%, the policy tailwinds (45X credits, IEEPA refund) are clawed back or slip, and the new Section 122 tariff plus the SOFR swap roll-off compress margins and FCF. A Rayovac/Varta impairment erodes the already-thin equity, and the multiple compresses back toward the May low as the deleveraging thesis stalls. With no equity cushion, the downside to the mid-teens is real, roughly −25% from current.

### Base Case (~$19-21)
Volume bleeds modestly but the business generates enough FCF to hold the dividend and chip at debt. The equity oscillates around the $19 median target, with the yield providing most of the total return. This is the most probable outcome and the reason the call is a HOLD: the distribution is two-sided and roughly symmetric around the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: the next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY26 earnings report, which management has framed as a "potential inflection." This is the cleanest test of whether organic volume stabilizes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic sales return to flat-or-positive growth for two consecutive quarters (vs. the −5.5% Q2 FY26 reading), confirming the "inflection."
- Net debt reduction of $150M+ over the next four quarters with FCF holding above ~$130M, demonstrating the deleveraging path is funded by operations rather than financial engineering.
- IEEPA refund converts to cash and 45X credits are confirmed durable, removing the earnings-quality overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic volume decline accelerates beyond ~6-8%, or a guidance cut to adjusted EPS below ~$3.20.
- A material impairment (Rayovac/Varta) that further erodes equity, or covenant pressure emerging from FCF deterioration.
- Dividend coverage breaks down (FCF falling sustainably below the ~$87M dividend + interest burden), forcing a payout cut.

The income hold rests on the ~5.9% yield being covered by stabilized H1 FY26-rate operating cash flow; if that coverage erodes, the thesis breaks.

## Conclusion

The prior AVOID rating was anchored to a bear case that the stock has already tested and recovered from. The CEO, the director, and a 5%+ holder are informed parties signaling value above the current price. The bearish pillars are real, but at ~7x earnings, a 5.9% covered yield, a median analyst target roughly equal to the current price, and a two-sided outcome distribution, the bearish view is substantially discounted. This is the profile of a HOLD rather than an avoid: insufficient conviction either way, balanced risk/reward, and a yield paying the carry while the volume-stabilization question resolves.

I rate ENR a HOLD (income hold) with an overall score of 4.8. The leverage and structural volume erosion preclude a buy; the discounted valuation, adequate liquidity, deleveraging pivot, and credible insider buying preclude an avoid. The swing factor is whether management's Q3 "inflection" delivers volume stabilization.
