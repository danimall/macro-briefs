**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TransUnion is one of three U.S. consumer credit bureaus, a structural oligopoly with an irreplaceable multi-decade data asset, high switching costs, and regulatory entrenchment. The business is re-accelerating: Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.7% year-over-year to $1.246B, led by U.S. Financial Services (+24%, mortgage the standout) and consolidation of Trans Union de Mexico. At the same time, the stock has fallen roughly 22% from its 52-week high to $76.51 and has badly lagged its peer set on total shareholder return (cumulative TSR of 88.6 versus a peer-group 194.2 per the proxy). That gap between re-accelerating fundamentals and a de-rated multiple is the central debate.

The de-rating is not without cause. Headline mortgage revenue growth is inflated by FICO score-royalty pass-through, a low-margin revenue stream that mechanically compressed adjusted EBITDA margin ~100bps to 35.2% in Q1'26. FICO's aggressive wholesale price increases and its new Mortgage Direct License Program introduce both a margin squeeze and a channel-disintermediation risk. Offsetting this, VantageScore 4.0 (co-owned by the three bureaus) is now an FHFA-approved mortgage score, creating two-sided optionality on where the mortgage-scoring profit pool ultimately lands. The multi-year technology transformation (~$373M, complete end-2025) should lift free cash flow as capex normalizes, and net leverage has fallen from ~3.6x to ~2.8x.

We rate TRU a HOLD (growth_hold) at an overall score of 5.9. At ~16x forward EPS versus a peer median near 26.5x, the stock is priced for the FICO margin overhang and housing-cycle risk to persist. The valuation is supportive and the business quality is high, but two things keep us from a BUY: estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than the accelerating upgrades that mark a clean turn, and there is no insider buying into the decline. We prefer to see organic ex-royalty margin stabilization and a positive revision turn before upgrading.

## Company Overview

TransUnion sells credit data, analytics, and decisioning tools rather than a physical product, which explains its "Research & Consulting Services" classification even though it functions as a data/information-services business. It operates two reportable segments:

- **U.S. Markets (~78% of revenue)**, disaggregated into Financial Services (~40% of U.S. revenue, ~$500M in Q1'26 — credit reporting and marketing for banks, card issuers, auto, mortgage, and fintech; contains the highly cyclical mortgage line), Emerging Verticals (insurance, telecom, tenant/employment screening, collections, public sector — more diversified and less rate-sensitive), and Consumer Interactive (direct-to-consumer credit monitoring and B2B breach services).
- **International (~22%)** — Canada, Latin America (now including a controlling stake in Trans Union de Mexico), UK, Africa, India, and Asia Pacific.

The competitive moat rests on proprietary historical data, network effects from data furnishing, regulatory entrenchment (lenders effectively must pull bureau data), and high switching costs. Management has unified its data and AI stack on a "OneTru" platform following the completed multi-year cloud migration.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and segment mix.** Revenue has grown from ~$3.7B to ~$4.58B over recent years, split between organic growth and the Trans Union de Mexico consolidation. U.S. Markets grew 13.8%, led by Financial Services +24% (mortgage on both price and volume). Emerging Verticals grew 6.3%, Consumer Interactive was roughly flat (+1.3%, softening paid-credit demand), and International grew 13.1% (Mexico plus Monevo added ~9.6 points; India -10.5% and Asia Pacific -18.2% are current weak spots).

**Margins and the mortgage paradox.** Consolidated adjusted EBITDA rose 10.3% to $437.9M, but the adjusted EBITDA margin fell ~100bps to 35.2%, and U.S. Markets margin fell 80bps. Management attributes this directly to FICO mortgage royalty pass-through — revenue that carries little to no margin and mechanically dilutes the reported ratio. The practical implication: headline mortgage revenue growth overstates the economic contribution, and investors should track organic ex-royalty growth as the honest measure.

**GAAP distortion.** Q1'26 GAAP net income of $397M (+167% YoY) is not representative. It was inflated by a $225.5M non-taxable gain on the step-acquisition of Trans Union de Mexico and a resulting 6.5% effective tax rate. Adjusted diluted EPS of $1.18 (+12.5%) is the clean figure. The prior-year comparison is also flattered because Q1'25 benefited from a $56M legal-accrual reversal following the dismissal-with-prejudice of an older consent-order lawsuit.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** TTM adjusted EBITDA is ~$1.76B. Total debt is $5.6B, essentially all variable rate with ~68.5% hedged via swaps, in compliance with all covenants (max 5.5x senior secured net leverage). The nearest maturity is a $104M Term Loan B-5 due November 2026, which is immaterial. Capex is ~5-6% of revenue and declining now that the technology transformation is complete, which should lift free cash flow. The company drew $520M on its revolver to fund the ~$660M cash portion of the Mexico deal, temporarily slowing buybacks; $688M remains on the $1.0B authorization, and the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.125/share.

The Altman Z-Score "distress" flag of ~1.7 is a false positive. It is driven by a goodwill/intangible-heavy balance sheet ($5.8B goodwill + $3.6B intangibles) inherent to a private-equity-legacy data company, not operational stress. Mid-30s% EBITDA margins, positive FCF, and covenant headroom all indicate a healthy, de-levering business.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (surprises of +6.5%, +3.9%, +4.5%, +7.9%), which supports confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth setup is solid rather than explosive. Consensus and management framing point to ~10-11% revenue growth and ~12% EPS growth in 2026. Growth drivers are: mortgage price/volume recovery (cyclical, rate-dependent), the Mexico and Monevo consolidations extending the "own the local bureau" playbook into under-penetrated emerging-credit markets, and a free-cash-flow inflection as the ~$373M technology capex cycle ends and redundant costs roll off.

The estimate-revision picture is the key restraint. Current-year EPS consensus of $4.78 is flat over 30 days and down only 0.2% over 90 days; next-year $5.59 is essentially unchanged. Current-quarter estimates are down 2.2% over 90 days. This is stable, not accelerating — the pattern of a durable compounder, not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a clean turn worth chasing. We are treating growth prospects as durable and mid-teens-to-double-digit, but not as a signal strong enough to override the two-sided competitive and cyclical risks.

The cyclical caveat matters. Mortgage and auto volumes are tied directly to rates: elevated 30-year mortgage rates continue to suppress housing, and management notes renewed inflation/energy pressure from the February 2026 Middle East conflict. Current strength is partly price-driven; a volume reversal is a real risk to the growth trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

TRU trades at ~16x forward EPS ($4.78 current-year consensus against $76.51) and ~20.5x trailing. The peer median in Research & Consulting Services sits at 26.5x, with Equifax at 28.1x and Verisk at 27.8x. TransUnion's discount to its most-direct comparables (EFX, VRSK) is roughly 40%. On an information-services business with double-digit revenue growth, mid-30s% EBITDA margins, an oligopoly position, and improving free cash flow, that discount is wide.

The discount has an explanation: the FICO royalty margin squeeze, mortgage cyclicality, and materially worse relative TSR. The question for the valuation score is whether the market is over-discounting. On balance we see the stock as reasonably-to-attractively priced but not a clear mispricing:

- **Supportive:** ~16x forward for a quality compounder growing EPS ~12% is a sub-1.5x PEG; the FCF inflection from the completed tech cycle is not yet in trailing numbers; analyst mean target of ~$90 (median $90, high $108, low $72) implies ~18% upside and the low target sits just below the current price, suggesting limited downside on the sell-side view. Consensus recommendation skews positive (5 Strong Buy, 11 Buy, 6 Hold, no sells).
- **Restraining:** the discount is at least partly justified by a genuine, ongoing margin headwind, and consensus estimates are not rising. A cheap multiple against a real structural overhang is a value-trap warning sign; the burden is on identifying a specific catalyst (VantageScore share gains, ex-royalty margin recovery) rather than relying on cheapness alone. The drawdown from the high, on re-accelerating fundamentals, is a constructive signal only if the FICO overhang stabilizes, which has not yet happened.

We score valuation 6.0: the discount is attractive and the downside appears bounded, but the absence of a triggered catalyst and the flat revision trend prevent a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

TransUnion sits inside a stable three-bureau oligopoly, but the competitive action is in mortgage scoring, not bureau data:

- **FICO** dominates score provision and is aggressively raising wholesale mortgage-score prices and pursuing direct licensing via its new Mortgage Direct License Program. This extracts value from the bureaus, compresses their margins, and raises the tail risk that FICO bypasses the bureaus entirely.
- **VantageScore** (co-owned by the three bureaus, including TRU) is positioning VantageScore 4.0 as a lower-cost, alternative-data challenger, now enabled by FHFA approval for mortgages. A July 2026 VantageScore study explicitly markets 4.0 against "obsolete" FICO models, evidence the bureaus are pushing to reclaim score economics.
- The **bi-merge vs. tri-merge** question at FHFA/Fannie/Freddie is the swing factor. A shift from three-bureau to two-bureau pulls could commoditize the third pull (a direct volume risk to whichever bureau is dropped); combined with VantageScore adoption, however, it could shift the profit pool back toward the bureaus.

The competitive-position score has declined in this trajectory (from 7.5 to 6.3 prior, now 5.8) as the FICO royalty squeeze has become concrete in the P&L and the scoring dynamics have crystallized into live risk. The moat in the core data asset is intact and durable; the trajectory of the score-economics profit pool is the source of downgrade pressure. Internationally, the consolidation of local bureaus (Mexico, UK marketplace) is a modest offsetting positive, capturing faster-growing emerging-credit markets against U.S. maturity.

## Risk Assessment

1. **FICO mortgage-royalty squeeze (most distinctive near-term issue).** Rising FICO wholesale prices inflate revenue while compressing EBITDA margin. Direct licensing adds disintermediation risk. This is the primary reason the multiple is compressed.
2. **Mortgage-scoring shift (VantageScore/FHFA).** Two-sided: potential upside if bureau-owned VantageScore gains share versus FICO; downside if bi-merge reduces per-transaction bureau pulls.
3. **CFPB dispute-handling matter (paused, not resolved).** A March 2024 NORA letter alleged FCRA violations; the CFPB obtained authority in July 2024 to pursue injunctive relief and penalties, but engagement is currently paused amid CFPB leadership changes. This is an asymmetric tail risk not reserved beyond the $71.8M general legal accrual, and a future administration could revive it.
4. **Rate/housing cyclicality.** Mortgage and auto volumes are rate-sensitive; current strength is partly price-driven and vulnerable to a volume reversal, with renewed inflation pressure a live macro risk.
5. **Data security/privacy litigation.** Perpetual breach and FCRA/privacy exposure as a custodian of sensitive data.
6. **Leverage/rate exposure.** Essentially all debt is variable; ~31.5% is unhedged, giving material interest-rate sensitivity.

We score risk factors 5.3: the core business is stable, but the combination of a live margin squeeze, a dormant-but-unresolved CFPB matter, and genuine two-sided scoring/regulatory risk represents meaningful idiosyncratic uncertainty not fully priced away.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 50.5% is rich versus 40.9% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.5% near-term vs 42.8% six-month), signaling an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. Near-term put/call skew of 3.0% shows elevated put demand, the put/call volume ratio is 2.00, and the P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.41 to 0.98 since late June as the stock rallied 12.5%. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11%. The market is hedging downside into the next report, consistent with the FICO-margin and mortgage-volume concerns embedded in the de-rating. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for post-print vol compression.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$105-108).** The FICO-margin overhang proves manageable and organic ex-royalty growth remains double-digit; the completed technology cycle drives a visible free-cash-flow and margin recovery; VantageScore 4.0 gains meaningful mortgage share, shifting score economics back toward the bureaus; deleveraging continues toward ~2.5x and buybacks resume in force. On ~$5.60 FY next-year EPS and a re-rating toward ~19x (still a discount to peers), the stock reaches the ~$105-108 range, matching the sell-side high.

**Bear Case (~$62, ~-19%).** Mortgage volumes reverse as rates stay higher-for-longer, FICO price increases keep compressing margin, a bi-merge shift reduces bureau pulls, the CFPB matter is revived under a future administration, and estimate revisions turn negative. The multiple stays compressed at ~13x on a lower earnings base, taking the stock to the low-$60s (near the 52-week low and below the analyst low target of $72).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $88.** ~17-18x on next-year consensus of ~$5.59, moderated for the persistent FICO overhang: ~$5.10-5.20 on a blended forward basis at ~17x ≈ $88. This sits between the bear ($62) and bull ($105-108) scenarios and implies ~15% upside from $76.51. It aligns with the analyst mean/median near $90.

- **Bull case:** $105-108 (+37-41%).
- **Bear case:** $62 (-19%).
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to the next 2-3 earnings prints and FHFA/VantageScore mortgage-scoring policy developments.
- **Upside/downside:** ~+15% to base, ~+37-41% to bull, ~-19% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic ex-royalty EBITDA margin stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the FICO squeeze is contained.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+3% or more over 90 days).
- Concrete evidence of VantageScore 4.0 mortgage-share gains, or a favorable resolution of the bi-merge/tri-merge question.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of decelerating organic revenue growth (below ~6%) with continued margin compression.
- Revival of the CFPB enforcement action with a quantified penalty materially above the $71.8M accrual.
- A confirmed bi-merge mandate that removes bureau pulls, combined with negative estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (implied by the options-priced catalyst), and any FHFA/GSE ruling on mortgage-scoring methodology.

## Conclusion

TransUnion is a high-quality, deleveraging oligopoly business re-accelerating to double-digit revenue growth, trading at a ~40% discount to its closest comparables. The discount reflects a real, ongoing FICO royalty margin squeeze and genuine mortgage-cycle and scoring-policy risk rather than sentiment. The bull case rests on the margin overhang proving manageable, a post-transformation FCF recovery, and VantageScore optionality; the bear case rests on rate-driven volume reversal, continued margin erosion, and a revived regulatory matter.

We maintain HOLD (growth_hold) at an overall score of 5.9, confirming the recent trajectory. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately high: what keeps us from upgrading is that estimate revisions are flat, there is no insider buying into the drawdown, and the FICO margin dynamic remains an active headwind rather than a stabilizing one. The valuation and business quality argue for owning it; the lack of a triggered catalyst and the flat revision trend argue against paying up for it today. We would upgrade on evidence of organic ex-royalty margin stabilization or a positive revision turn, and we would use rich near-term implied volatility to be patient on entry rather than chasing into the next print.
