## Executive Summary

Ingredion is a $6.8B global ingredient solutions company trading at approximately 9.9x forward earnings with a fortress balance sheet (0.6x net debt/EBITDA at year-end 2025), generating 15.5% adjusted ROIC and returning substantial capital via dividends (3.1% yield) and buybacks. The stock has declined approximately 24% from its 52-week high of $141.78 to $107.36, driven by consecutive quarterly earnings disappointments and operational failures at its largest U.S. sweetener facility (Argo). Full-year 2026 guidance has been cut to $10.45-$11.15 adjusted EPS from $11.00-$11.80.

The investment case rests on a straightforward asymmetry: the current multiple implies permanent impairment, while the primary drivers of weakness — Argo operational disruptions, softer F&II volumes/mix, and manageable FX headwinds — are largely recoverable. The Texture & Healthful Solutions segment continues to deliver volume growth, and the company's approximately $2.3B of unused acquisition capacity positions it for the next leg of its specialty ingredients transformation. At $107, you are paying a commodity processor multiple for a business generating specialty-company returns.

The specific risks preventing this from being a high-conviction call are execution uncertainty on Argo recovery (this facility has now underperformed for two consecutive years), broader volume/mix softness in F&II segments beyond Argo alone, and the absence of a permanent CFO during a critical period. These risks are real but bounded, and the valuation provides adequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Ingredion transforms corn, tapioca, potato, peas, and other plant-based materials into value-added ingredients for 60+ industries across food, beverage, animal nutrition, brewing, and industrial applications. The company operates 41 manufacturing facilities globally with no single customer exceeding 10% of sales.

**Segments:**
- **Texture & Healthful Solutions (T&HS):** $2.4B revenue (33%), the strategic growth engine — clean-label texturizers, pea protein, stevia, hydrocolloids
- **Food & Industrial Ingredients–LATAM:** $2.3B (32%) — corn processing in Mexico and South America
- **Food & Industrial Ingredients–U.S./Canada:** $2.0B (28%) — commodity starches and sweeteners, anchored by the Argo facility
- **All Other:** $0.5B (6%) — Pakistan (pending partial divestiture), PureCircle stevia

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability has structurally improved despite revenue declines:**

Revenue declined from $8.16B (2023) to $7.22B (2025), a 12% contraction driven by pass-through of lower corn costs and volume weakness in certain F&II sub-segments, partially offset by favorable T&HS volumes. Over the same period, operating margin expanded from 9.6% (2022) to 14.1% (2025), net income grew 48% ($492M to $729M), and EPS compounded from $7.34 to $11.18. This margin expansion reflects the deliberate portfolio shift toward solutions and away from pass-through commodity processing.

**Balance sheet is exceptionally clean (as of FY2025 year-end):**
- Net debt/EBITDA: 0.6x (target: ≤2.5x)
- Total liquidity: $3.9B
- Cash on hand: $1.03B (year-end 2025; $914M as of Q1 2026)
- No commercial paper outstanding
- Only near-term maturity: $499M senior notes at 3.2% due October 2026

**Cash flow is strong but lumpy:**
- 2025 operating cash flow: $944M (2024: $1,436M; difference driven by working capital swings)
- CapEx: $433M (2025), guided $400-440M for 2026
- Shareholder returns: $435M in 2025 ($211M dividends + $224M buybacks)
- Shares outstanding declining steadily (67M → 65.2M)

**Q1 2026 was weak:**
- Revenue: $1.79B (-1% YoY)
- Adjusted EPS: $2.34 (below expectations)
- F&II-U.S./Can segment operating income collapsed 63% ($34M vs. $92M) on Argo disruptions and softer volumes/mix
- Full-year guidance lowered

**2026 guidance (lowered):**
- Adjusted EPS: $10.45-$11.15 (midpoint $10.80)
- Cash from operations: $725-825M
- Net sales: flat to up low-single-digits
- Q2 reported operating income expected down high double-digits YoY; adjusted operating income down high single-digits YoY

At $107.36 with $10.80 midpoint EPS, the stock trades at 9.9x current-year guidance. Even taking the low end ($10.45), that's 10.3x. Using trailing FY2025 diluted EPS of $11.18, trailing P/E is approximately 9.6x.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at Ingredion operates on two tracks that pull in opposite directions:

**Positive track — T&HS Solutions (~$1B portfolio within the segment):**
- Consistent volume growth trend
- Protein fortification and sugar reduction showing improvement
- Clean-label texturizers benefiting from consumer and regulatory tailwinds
- 16% operating income growth in T&HS segment during 2025
- AI-powered predictive formulation accelerating customer co-development cycles

**Negative track — commodity headwinds:**
- HFCS volumes in secular decline
- Revenue declining on lower corn cost pass-through (optically poor)
- F&II–U.S./Canada volumes under pressure from beverage and food industry softness
- Industrial starch tied to paper/packaging demand cycles
- GLP-1 drugs creating structural uncertainty about food/beverage volumes (though Ingredion's protein business may benefit)

**Net assessment:** Organic revenue growth is likely flat to low-single-digits for the next 2-3 years. The growth story is margin expansion and mix shift, not top-line acceleration. EPS growth potential comes from (a) Argo normalization and broader F&II recovery, (b) continued solutions mix shift, and (c) M&A utilizing the approximately $2.3B of unused debt capacity. Consensus has FY2027 at $11.66, representing 8% growth off the depressed 2026 base.

Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current-year EPS has been cut 4.4% in 90 days, next year cut 1.6%. Estimates are falling because of operational failures and volume softness, not structural demand deterioration, but this distinction matters less in the near term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $107.36, Ingredion trades at:
- 9.6x trailing P/E ($11.18 EPS)
- 9.9x midpoint 2026E ($10.80)
- 9.2x FY2027E ($11.66)
- ~3.1% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** Ingredion's primary competitors per its own filings include ADM, Cargill (private), Tate & Lyle, Roquette (private), and Primient (private). Among publicly traded food/ingredient companies, INGR's sub-10x P/E, 15.5% ROIC, and strong balance sheet quality stand out. Publicly traded peers in adjacent ingredient/food categories (such as CPB, CAG) generally trade at higher multiples when profitable, though direct comps are limited given Ingredion's unique starch/sweetener focus.

**Why the market is discounting INGR:** Consecutive earnings disappointments, falling estimates, Argo operational uncertainty, broader F&II volume/mix softness, CFO vacancy, and the emerging GLP-1 narrative. These are real but largely temporary factors being priced as permanent.

**Valuation math supporting upside:** If Argo normalizes and F&II volumes stabilize, allowing INGR to return to its 12-month average multiple of ~12x on $11.50 normalized EPS, fair value is $138. Even applying a conservative 11x on guidance midpoint ($10.80), you get $119 — 11% above current. The consensus mean target is $123 (15% upside), with Oppenheimer at $126 and Benchmark at $130.

The balance sheet optionality is real but unpriced: approximately $2.3B of incremental acquisition capacity could be deployed toward accretive specialty ingredients bolt-ons that would accelerate the T&HS growth story.

## Competitive Landscape

Ingredion competes in an oligopolistic industry (ADM, Cargill, Tate & Lyle, Roquette, Primient). The commodity processing business has minimal differentiation, but the solutions portfolio creates genuine competitive advantage through:

- 30+ Idea Labs globally enabling customer co-development
- 500+ R&D scientists
- Proprietary stevia varieties (PureCircle)
- Application-specific formulation expertise that creates switching costs

The strategic pivot mirrors Tate & Lyle's successful 2022 separation into pure-play specialty. Ingredion retains its commodity businesses (which still generate substantial free cash flow) while building the solutions franchise. T&HS competitive position is improving (volume growth trend continues), while F&II faces secular headwinds in HFCS and industrial applications.

The recent hire of Patrick Kalotis (ex-Tropicana/PepsiCo CEO) to lead T&HS suggests management is investing in customer-facing capabilities to accelerate solutions penetration.

## Risk Assessment

**Argo Facility Concentration (HIGH):** This single facility has now impaired results for two consecutive years with multiple failure modes (conveying, refinery, fire). A $40M quarterly impact from one plant on a $7B revenue company indicates problematic concentration. Management says issues are resolved, but the track record warrants skepticism. Cedar Rapids CBA expiration (August 2026) adds another facility-level labor risk.

**Broader F&II Volume/Mix Weakness (MODERATE-HIGH):** Beyond Argo, F&II–U.S./Canada experienced lower volumes from beverage and food industries in FY2025, and F&II–LATAM faces low-single-digit operating income headwinds from Mexican peso strength. The Q1 2026 segment decline was attributed to both Argo production challenges and softer volumes/mix, indicating that Argo normalization alone will not fully restore segment profitability.

**Cabo Facility Closure (MODERATE):** On May 1, 2026, Ingredion committed to cease operations at its Cabo, Brazil facility as of June 30, 2026, expecting approximately $43M of pre-tax non-recurring charges ($36M impairment/inventory write-downs and $7M cash employee/termination costs). While these charges are excluded from adjusted EPS guidance, they affect GAAP results and reinforce that network restructuring is ongoing following 2024 closures of Vanscoy, Goole, and Alcantara facilities.

**Falling Estimates (MODERATE):** Current-year estimates down 4.4% in 90 days, next year down 1.6%. The recent EPS miss pattern undermines confidence in consensus. This creates risk that even a "recovery" quarter merely meets lowered expectations rather than generating positive surprise momentum.

**CFO Vacancy (MODERATE):** CFO James Gray retired effective March 31, 2026 (the company stated his departure was not the result of a dispute or disagreement). Jason Payant was elected Interim CFO and principal financial officer effective April 1, 2026. While the transition was orderly, investor perception of an interim CFO during the most operationally challenged period introduces confidence and continuity risk. The company has not yet announced a permanent replacement.

**GLP-1 Structural Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Newly disclosed in risk factors. Difficult to quantify near-term but represents a genuine long-term volume headwind for calorie-dense food ingredient demand. Partially offset by protein fortification tailwind.

**FX — Mexican Peso (LOW-MODERATE):** Peso strength creates near-term transactional headwinds for F&II-LATAM margins. Manageable but contributes to near-term earnings pressure. FY2026 guidance assumes F&II–LATAM operating income down low single digits due to peso strength.

**Options Market Signal:**
Options market data suggests elevated near-term uncertainty relative to historical norms, with put positioning exceeding call positioning in open interest. This is consistent with post-miss uncertainty and institutional hedging activity rather than informed directional positioning. Volume-based flow indicators appear more neutral-to-constructive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-140):** Argo normalizes in the second half of 2026 as management targets, and F&II volumes stabilize. T&HS continues its volume growth trend. Permanent CFO hired restores confidence. Stock re-rates to 12x on $11.50 normalized EPS = $138. Potential M&A catalyst using unused debt capacity. Timeline: 12-18 months.

**Bear Case ($85-95):** Argo problems prove chronic (infrastructure aging requiring multi-year investment). F&II volume weakness proves structural rather than cyclical. GLP-1 impact accelerates, reducing customer volumes. No M&A deployed despite under-leveraged balance sheet, signaling management uncertainty. Estimates continue falling to $9.50-10.00. Stock de-rates to 9x = $85-90. Probability: 20-25%.

**Base Case ($118-125):** Argo partially recovers but some incremental capex required. F&II volumes stabilize but don't fully recover to prior levels. FY2026 comes in near guidance midpoint ($10.80). FY2027 consensus ($11.66) proves achievable. Stock trades toward 11x forward on improved operational execution = $118-128.

## Conclusion

INGR represents a quality compounder temporarily discounted for recoverable operational problems and cyclical volume softness. At sub-10x forward earnings with 0.6x leverage, 15.5% ROIC, 3.1% dividend yield, and approximately $2.3B of M&A capacity, the margin of safety is substantial. The stock is cheap because the market is pricing these issues as semi-permanent, while management has targeted a return to normal Argo operations in the second half of 2026, with sequential improvement expected beginning in Q2.

The specific concerns that prevent maximum conviction: (1) Argo has now been a drag for two consecutive years across multiple distinct failure modes, raising questions about deferred maintenance, aging infrastructure, or management's operational oversight; (2) F&II volume/mix weakness extends beyond Argo alone, with softer demand from beverage and food industries; (3) the CFO vacancy compounds governance concern; and (4) Q2 guidance still calls for adjusted operating income down high single-digits year over year, indicating recovery will be gradual rather than immediate.

Sizing should reflect these risks: 2% position rather than 3%, with the intent to add on Q2 2026 earnings if Argo recovery and volume stabilization are confirmed. The downside is well-defined ($90, -16%) and the upside scenarios ($122-138, +14% to +29%) offer favorable asymmetry.

**Recommendation: BUY** at $107.36 with 2% position sizing. Add to 3% on Q2 earnings if Argo recovery is confirmed.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** F&II weakness extends beyond the single Argo facility (includes softer volumes/mix), Argo normalization is targeted for 2H 2026 rather than Q2–Q3, and T&HS volume growth rates used in the bull case are not supported by recent disclosed results (low-single-digit actual vs. 8%+ assumed). These corrections reduce near-term upside conviction.
