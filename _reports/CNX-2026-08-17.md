## Executive Summary

CNX is an Appalachian pure-play gas producer running an explicit per-share-value strategy: it does not grow volumes, does not pay a dividend, hedges roughly 80% of near-term production, and converts the resulting cash into share repurchases. The design is coherent and the incentive structure supports it — compensation is measured on Adjusted Free Cash Flow per Share, free cash flow, relative TSR, absolute stock price, and methane intensity, with no production or reserve-growth metric anywhere in the plan. The balance sheet has been engineered to support the strategy through a soft price environment: after the February 2026 note exchange and the May 2026 convertible settlement, there is no bond maturity until the CNX Midstream 4.75% notes in April 2030, the revolver runs to May 2029 with $1,216M undrawn, and roughly 89% of debt is fixed-rate so a 100bp move costs roughly $2M pre-tax annually.

The problem is that the operating engine underneath is contracting while consuming more capital. Q2 2026 volumes were 151.5 Bcfe against 167.6 Bcfe a year earlier, down 9.6%. FY2026 guidance of 605-620 Bcfe sits below the 629.0 Bcfe delivered in 2025. Capital spending guidance of $556-586M against that volume base implies $0.932/Mcfe versus approximately $0.787/Mcfe in FY2025, an 18.4% increase in capital cost per unit produced while output declines. Management explicitly denied inflation as a cause, attributing the pattern to field timing across a program of only 34 turn-in-lines. That explanation is plausible — 34 TILs is genuinely lumpy — but it is unproven, and a mix shift toward higher-cost Utica development at roughly $1,700/ft is an equally consistent reading. On July 30 management cut FY2026 adjusted EBITDAX guidance to $1,265-1,315M from $1,310-1,360M and FCF to approximately $525M from $550M, in the same release that produced a 26-29% non-GAAP EPS beat.

Our scoring trajectory on CNX has declined from 6.1 in mid-May to 5.1 in late July across four consecutive HOLDs. The current evidence confirms rather than reverses that direction: the guidance cut is new, the environmental-attribute decline has extended, and the convertible settlement reset the share count. The offset is that the stock has fallen 18.1% from its March 6 high of $43.62, so the entry price has absorbed part of the deterioration. Net expected return is modestly positive but thin, and several binaries remain unpriced. We maintain HOLD, and specify below what tips the rating to REDUCE or to ACCUMULATE.

## Company Overview

CNX holds 9,662 Bcfe of proved reserves at 12/31/2025 — 89.5% natural gas, 72.2% proved developed, 99.1% operated — implying a 15.4-year reserve/production ratio on FY2025 volumes. Roughly 94% of production comes from the Marcellus and Utica segment and 6% from legacy Virginia coalbed methane. The acreage position is unusually large: 3,971,342 total net acres, with approximately 4.9 million gross unproved acres held by production or held in fee. That matters more than the headline: CNX faces almost no lease-expiry pressure, which is what makes a "drill only what clears the hurdle" posture credible rather than promotional.

The company owns roughly 2,600 miles of gathering pipeline and substantially all gathering serving its Pennsylvania and West Virginia production, capturing midstream margin in-house. Lifting costs were $0.15/Mcfe in 2025 with only 390 employees — extremely thin overhead for a 629 Bcfe producer. Long-haul takeaway runs on Texas Eastern, Columbia Gas Transmission, and Eastern Gas Transmission & Storage; midstream is held partly through CNX Midstream Partners, whose $400M of 4.75% notes and $600M revolver CNX does not guarantee, which is why covenant-basis net debt (approximately $1,747M) sits well below consolidated net debt.

A smaller "environmental attributes" business captures coal mine methane and sells Pennsylvania Alternative Energy Credits plus, prospectively, federal 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credits.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings are close to uninformative.** The proxy's own five-year table shows GAAP net income of -$498.6M (2021), -$142.1M (2022), +$1,720.7M (2023), -$90.5M (2024), +$633.2M (2025) against Adjusted FCF/share of $2.48, $4.14, $1.98, $2.22, $4.53. Net income and cash generation moved in opposite directions in three of five years. The reported trailing P/E of 5.28x on $3.98 diluted EPS is measuring the derivative book, not the business, and should not be used.

H1 2026 illustrates the point. Total revenue and other operating income was $1,405.1M against $1,044.8M, and net income $551.1M against $234.8M — but the swing factor was a derivative gain of +$180.3M versus -$107.1M, a $287M revenue-line move, of which the unrealized component was +$356.5M while cash settlements were a $176.2M outflow. Tax-effecting the unrealized mark at the blended 17.2% rate gives an approximate ex-mark H1 figure near $256M, or roughly $1.65/diluted share. That is an approximation, not a reported number.

**Unit economics improved despite falling volumes**, because hedges flipped within the half:

| Per Mcfe (non-GAAP, 10-Q) | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-hedge gas realization ($/Mcf) | $2.40 | $2.84 | $3.58 | $3.22 |
| Cash hedge settlement ($/Mcf) | +$0.33 | -$0.23 | -$0.64 | -$0.50 |
| Sales incl. cash settlements | $2.87 | $2.68 | $3.08 | $2.82 |
| Production costs | $1.73 | $1.67 | $1.73 | $1.67 |
| **Production margin** | **$1.14** | $1.01 | **$1.35** | $1.15 |

Production costs rose 3.6% per unit on lower volumes — modest negative operating leverage — and shale transportation, gathering and compression rose from $0.51 to $0.61/Mcfe in Q2, up 19.6%.

The hedge book's two-sided character is best shown by reconstructing Q1. H1 gas revenue was $985.5M on 275,210 MMcf; Q2 was $326.9M on 136,268 MMcf. Q1 therefore realized approximately $4.74/Mcf on 138,942 MMcf. But H1 cash hedge settlements cost $176.2M while Q2 gained $45.4M, implying Q1 settlements cost roughly $221.6M, or about $1.60/Mcf. The hedge book surrendered roughly a third of the cold-weather windfall and then added $0.33/Mcf back in a weak Q2. It is a volatility damper working in both directions, not a one-way giveaway.

**Cash flow and the buyback.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $557.0M against $498.1M, but it was helped by a $110.5M receivables release versus $9.8M a year earlier — a working-capital tailwind that will not repeat. Simple H1 FCF was $557.0M − $311.9M capex = $245.1M. Repurchases were $254.4M per the cash flow statement (7,181,455 shares at an average $35.66), or approximately 104% of H1 FCF. The monthly cadence shows genuine discipline: 1,135,802 shares in April at $38.57, 1,936,819 in May at $35.78, 2,662,821 in June at $33.41 — 2.3x the April tranche as the price fell. CEO Alan Shepard confirmed willingness to outspend FCF "if the margin of safety is wide."

**Balance sheet (6/30/2026 vs 12/31/2025).** Total face debt fell to $2,235.3M from $2,429.4M; cash is only $8.6M, so liquidity is revolver-dependent ($155.7M drawn, $1,216.4M unused, $2.4B borrowing base reaffirmed May 13, 2026). Current ratio improved to 0.72 from 0.44 as the converts left current liabilities and the current derivative liability fell from $377.9M to $174.9M; working capital remains negative at approximately -$186.5M. Net debt/equity is 46%; net debt to guided 2026 EBITDAX midpoint is 1.73x; covenant-basis leverage is roughly 1.4x against a 3.50x maximum. Interest coverage against annualized H1 interest of approximately $159M is about 8.1x.

Off-balance-sheet, firm transportation and processing purchase obligations total $1,300.9M — a fixed cost regardless of volumes, though unutilized firm transportation fees fell to $3M in H1 2026 from $18M. Surety bonds and letters of credit total $339.6M. Uncertain tax positions stand at $131.3M, all of which would affect the effective rate if recognized. Cash income taxes paid in H1 were $5.8M against $114.5M of book tax expense, reflecting approximately $0.7B federal and $1.2B state NOLs — a real but depleting cash-flow enhancement.

One correction to standardized data: widely circulated screens show FY2025 revenue near $5,786M and long-term debt near $25M. Actual FY2025 total revenue and other operating income was $2,239M and net long-term debt was $2,213M; the $25M figure appears to capture the finance-lease line. Any distress score built on those inputs is meaningless. Recomputing Altman Z on the 6/30/2026 balance sheet gives approximately 1.84 (against approximately 1.47 at 12/31/2025), with the entire improvement attributable to the current-liability shift rather than earnings. Z-scores systematically penalize capital-intensive E&Ps that run negative working capital and asset turnover near 0.25; we do not read 1.84 as a distress signal here.

## Growth Analysis

Conventional growth is negative and the capital required to sustain it is rising.

- Q2 2026 volumes 151.5 Bcfe, -9.6% YoY; H1 303.8 Bcfe, -3.7%
- FY2026 guidance 605-620 Bcfe versus 629.0 Bcfe actual — a guided 1.4-3.8% decline
- Net development wells drilled: 30.8 (2023) → 25.7 (2024) → 18.9 (2025)
- Only 34 turn-in-lines planned for all of 2026, with 12-13 arriving in Q3 from a single Marcellus pad
- H1 2026 capex $311.9M versus $245.0M, +27.3%, on 3.7% lower volumes
- Implied capital intensity $0.932/Mcfe in 2026 versus $0.787/Mcfe in 2025, +18.4%

Three explanations compete and cannot be separated from two data points: program lumpiness across 34 TILs, mix shift toward Utica at approximately $1,700/ft (2026 also includes the first of three annual $16M payments for the *right* to acquire Utica rights beneath the legacy Apex footprint — an option premium, roughly 2.8% of the capex midpoint, not productive capital), or a durable base-decline burden requiring more capital to hold a smaller volume base. The third reading would be a structural margin issue. This is the single most important item to track over the next two to four quarters.

The low-carbon growth leg is weakening on the revenue line. Environmental attribute revenue was $95M in FY2024, $78M in FY2025, and $29.4M in H1 2026 against $43.3M in H1 2025, down 32.6%, with the 10-Q attributing the fall to both lower volumes sold and lower prices. Management assumes this line is flat at roughly $50M going forward while targeting a combined approximately $90M annual run rate including 45Z from 2027. The gap between a two-year downtrend and a flat forward assumption is the weakest link in that target. Attribute sales are lumpy, so annualizing H1 (~$59M) is a sensitivity rather than a forecast.

The 45Z piece is more concrete and structurally under-appreciated in EBITDA-based models. Treasury's GREET refinement raised CNX's expected annual monetization to approximately $40M beginning 2027; a roughly $30M sale closed in early July 2026 and lands in Q3 cash flow. The CFO stated plainly that this flows through the income tax expense line and is not visible in EBITDA. Anyone adding $90M to EBITDA double-counts; anyone valuing CNX purely on EV/EBITDAX misses roughly $40M of recurring cash, worth approximately $400M at a 10% yield, or 7.5% of market capitalization. Final Treasury guidance is expected in 2H 2026, so this is policy-dependent, not contracted.

Per-share growth is the actual mechanism. On guided FCF of $525M and no dividend, full deployment retires roughly 9-10% of shares annually at current prices, with $2,171.9M of authorization remaining — 40.8% of market capitalization.

Estimate revisions are split rather than confirming an inflection: current quarter +23.6% over 30 days, current year +9.4% over 30 days and +5.9% over 90 days, but **next year -5.1% over 30 days and -9.0% over 90 days**. Four consecutive beats (18.1%, 27.0%, 94.8%, 47.6% on non-GAAP bases) support near-term confidence; the out-year cut is the signal that matters for a multi-year holding, and it does not meet the threshold that would justify treating this as an earnings-inflection story.

Peer comparables in the E&P screen are not informative: the median P/E of 17.0x spans CRC at -9.7x, CRGY at 79.3x, MGY at 11.3x and CRK at 7.7x, reflecting oil-weighted mixes and mark-distorted earnings rather than a coherent cohort. The more usable anchors are internal. EV/PV-10 of 1.11x means the market pays an 11% premium to the PV-10 of proved reserves struck at an SEC average Henry Hub of $3.387/MMBtu — assigning limited value to unproved inventory, the midstream asset beyond proved reserves, and the attribute franchise. That is neither a distressed valuation nor a cheap one.

The decisive constraint on the upside is the hedge book. As of 7/8/2026, 231.3 Bcf of 2H 2026 is hedged at $2.74/Mcf, 402.4 Bcf of 2027 at $3.31, and 220.9 Bcf of 2028 at $3.23. Against management's own assumed open-volume realization of approximately $3.00 for 2026 ($3.64 NYMEX less $0.64 basis), the 2H26 hedges cost roughly $60M of forgone revenue (231.3 Bcf × $0.26). More importantly, 402.4 Bcf against a roughly 612 Bcfe base means about two-thirds of 2027 is pre-sold at $3.31, and 2028 hedges were *increased* from 186.5 to 220.9 Bcf between January and July 2026. The common criticism that CNX hedged away the data-center upside is half right: the 2027-28 strikes are not punitive relative to the assumed 2026 open realization, but participation in a *spike* is capped. The company's disclosed sensitivity is large and symmetric — a 10% rise in forward gas prices reduces derivative fair value by $315M. We could not obtain a current forward curve, so whether $3.31 for 2027 sits above or below market is unverified.

Where does this leave asymmetry? At 5.86x EV/EBITDAX with the multiple held flat and $1,300M of 2027 EBITDAX, equity value is roughly unchanged at today's share count. The path to a higher share price runs almost entirely through share-count reduction: holding the multiple and retiring 8-9% of shares gets to roughly $39. That is real, mechanical, and modest. It is not a mispricing, and we score valuation at 5.0 accordingly. A compressed multiple on cyclical cash flow, absent an identified catalyst for re-rating, is fair value rather than opportunity — particularly in a sector where cheap-looking E&Ps have repeatedly failed to close the gap.

Consensus reflects the same read: 1 buy, 9 holds, 1 sell, 1 strong sell; mean target $37.73 (+5.6%), median $37.00, range $32-48. The analyst-targets-as-floors logic does not apply here, because next-year estimates are being cut, not raised.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX's structural advantages are real: fee/HBP acreage that removes drilling obligation, owned gathering that internalizes midstream margin, $0.15/Mcfe lifting costs, and 390 employees. What it lacks is the thing the Appalachian demand thesis actually monetizes. EQT is converting rising power demand into *contracted* offtake — power plants, data centers, pipeline access, LNG. CNX sells through wholesale marketing desks at index-linked prices with a $(0.64)/MMBtu regional differential assumption and has disclosed no behind-the-meter power arrangement, LNG supply agreement, or data-center contract in any filing reviewed. It participates in the demand boom only through commodity price, and only after hedges roll.

Trajectory indicators are pointed the wrong way. Liquids uplift fell from $0.17/Mcfe in 2024 to $0.05/Mcfe in 2025, halving the diversification benefit. Unit transportation, gathering and compression rose 19.6% in Q2. The CBM segment posted a *negative* $0.64/Mcf field margin in Q2 with $3.89/Mcf production costs against a $3.25/Mcf realization and a $6M pretax loss, and its economics only work when attribute revenue — booked in "Other," not CBM — is strong. Customer concentration worsened on the measure that matters: single-largest-customer share rose from 12.0% (Citadel, H1 2025) to 19.2% (NRG Business Marketing, H1 2026), with DTE Energy Trading at 10.7%, roughly 29.9% combined. The count of >10% names fell, but that reflects one counterparty growing while another exited, not diversification. All three are marketing desks rather than end users, so the exposure is credit and settlement rather than lost market access.

The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows CNX cumulative TSR indexed at 340.8 at FY2025 against a peer index of 531.8, with underperformance in all five disclosed years and the gap widening in 2025 (CNX +1.0 index points, peers +50.1). Competitive position scores 4.5 on a deteriorating trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**CBM impairment.** $886.0M of segment assets and 812.6 Bcfe of reserves against a negative Q2 field margin, with $473.0M of accumulated goodwill impairment already on the books. This is our inference, not a company disclosure; the counter-reading is that CBM is long-lived and low-decline and recovers with AEC pricing plus 45Z.

**Unidentified February 18, 2026 material agreement.** An 8-K carrying Item 1.01 plus Item 7.01 and Item 9.01, distinct from the February 26 notes indenture, could not be retrieved. A material contract with an accompanying press release exists and its content is unknown. This is the one gap that could be materially value-relevant, and we make no assumption about it.

**45Z policy dependence.** The approximately $40M annual credit stream from 2027 is contingent on final Treasury guidance expected in 2H 2026 and on the statutory credit life, neither verified.

**Basis exposure.** 14 Bcf of 2026 hedged volumes remain exposed to regional basis, and basis-only hedges extend to just 24.5 Bcf in 2029. Appalachian basis is the region's structural weakness and CNX is materially unhedged on it beyond 2028.

**Legacy coal dependency.** Core Natural Resources indemnifies specified coal liabilities and has reimbursed all UMWA 1974 Plan settlement payments plus interest through 6/30/26. The UMWA 1992 Benefit Plan case (approximately 2,159 retirees) remains open with no accrual. The dependency is on Core's creditworthiness through a coal cycle. Separately, the Buchanan mine identified on the call as CNX's methane source is, on external context we could not verify in filings, Core-operated — if so, the 45Z franchise depends on a third party's mine operating rates.

**Permitting.** Cecil Township setback ordinance and a pending setback rulemaking petition before the Pennsylvania EQB directly constrain drillable inventory underpinning PV-10.

**Reporting quality.** Management's marketed FCF/share of approximately $3.41 was cited against 141.5M shares as of April 15, 2026 — a count that predates the May 1 convertible settlement. The figure does not reconcile ($525M ÷ 141.5M = $3.71), and on the 149.0M shares actually outstanding at 6/30/26 the range is $3.24-3.52 depending on the FCF definition. This may be a transcript error rather than a disclosure error, but the primary deck should be verified before using the metric.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 35.1% against 28.6% one-year realized vol is rich (+6.5pp), and the term structure is backwardated (35.1% at one week versus 30.1% at six months), which the data source reads as pricing imminent stress rather than a directional view. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.56 on July 30 to 8.00 on August 17 while ATM IV fell 6.9pp and the price rose 3.6% — a large build in put open interest alongside cheapening near-dated vol. The one-month tenor carries a +3.6% put/call skew that the source flags as elevated put demand; the three- and six-month skew values are negative and we do not assign a direction to them absent a stated sign convention. Put/call *volume* at 0.68 is call-tilted, so the OI build is not being sustained by current flow.

Short interest is 14.3% of float (15.17M shares, 8.5 days to cover). That is meaningful bearish institutional positioning and it cuts both ways: it corroborates the skeptical fundamental read while creating squeeze risk on any gas rally or unexpected 45Z clarity. Combined with the guidance cut and rich near-dated vol, the options and short data confirm rather than challenge a fair-value HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $48 (+34.4%)

Gas strengthens into 2027 on Appalachian power and data-center demand; the roughly 210 Bcf of unhedged 2027 volumes realize $4.00+, adding roughly $200M to EBITDAX; final Treasury guidance confirms approximately $40M of annual 45Z monetization and the market begins crediting it as recurring cash outside EBITDA; the 2026 capital-intensity spike proves to be TIL timing and 2027 capex falls back toward $0.80/Mcfe; and management deploys the full $2.17B authorization, retiring 9-10% of shares annually. On $1,500M of EBITDAX at 6.5x with the share count down to roughly 138M, equity value approaches $48-50 — consistent with the high analyst target. The undeveloped acreage and midstream asset, currently valued near zero at 1.11x EV/PV-10, begin to carry credit.

### Bear Case — $27 (-24.4%)

The 18.4% rise in capital cost per unit proves structural rather than timing, meaning sustaining approximately 610 Bcfe now requires roughly $570M annually with base decline worsening; environmental-attribute revenue continues its two-year decline toward $45M, and the flat forward assumption in the $90M target fails; final Treasury guidance disappoints on 45Z; CBM takes an impairment against $886M of segment assets; and 2027 open-volume realizations fall to $2.50 with basis widening. On $1,100M of EBITDAX at 5.5x with net debt drifting to $2.3B as buybacks outrun FCF, equity value is roughly $26-27, near the $27.72 52-week low and below the $32 low analyst target.

### Base Case — $39 (+9.2%)

2027 EBITDAX holds near $1,300M as improved hedge strikes ($3.31 versus $2.74) offset modestly lower volumes; the multiple holds at 5.8-5.9x; roughly $500M of FCF plus $40M of 45Z cash funds retirement of 8-9% of shares; net debt is broadly flat. Equity value of approximately $5,320M across roughly 137M shares gives $38.8. The entire return is share-count arithmetic, not re-rating.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear: +4.1% + 6.9% − 8.5% ≈ **+2.5%**.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** — approximately fair risk-adjusted return, at the HOLD/REDUCE boundary. Existing holders are paid roughly a 10% FCF yield with the buyback compounding per-share value and no maturity risk before 2030. New capital has no urgent entry signal: base-case upside of 9.2% against 24.4% bear-case downside is unfavorable asymmetry, and the mechanism generating the base case (share retirement) requires no thesis validation but also produces no re-rating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which will contain the approximately $30M July 45Z monetization in cash flow, the Q3-heavy capex and the 12-13 Marcellus TILs — the first genuine test of the timing-versus-structural capital-intensity question. Final Treasury 45Z guidance in 2H 2026 is the second dated catalyst.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Q3 capital intensity reverting toward $0.80/Mcfe with FY2026 volumes tracking the upper half of 605-620 Bcfe — evidence the 18.4% increase was program timing.
2. Final Treasury 45Z guidance confirming approximately $40M of annual monetization, plus attribute revenue stabilizing at or above $15M per quarter (halting the $95M → $78M → ~$59M annualized decline).
3. Price toward $30-31 with fundamentals unchanged, which would take the FCF yield above 11.5% and EV/EBITDAX below 5.4x, restoring margin of safety.
4. Disclosure of a contracted power, data-center, or LNG offtake arrangement that converts CNX from an index price-taker into a contracted supplier.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. FY2026 volumes guided or tracking below 605 Bcfe, or FY2027 capex guided above $600M — either would confirm the structural capital-intensity reading.
2. A CBM impairment charge, or Q3 CBM field margin remaining negative with attribute revenue below $12M for the quarter.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $3.50 (from $3.90 today, already cut 9.0% in 90 days), or a second consecutive EBITDAX guidance reduction.
4. Buybacks continuing above 110% of FCF while the revolver draw rises above $400M — outspending FCF is defensible at a wide margin of safety, not at 5.9x EV/EBITDAX with falling out-year estimates.

**Downgrade to AVOID** if the unidentified February 18, 2026 material agreement proves to be a significant contingent obligation, or if covenant-basis leverage moves above 2.5x on a falling EBITDAX base.

## Conclusion

CNX runs one of the more intellectually honest capital-allocation frameworks in E&P: no growth mandate, no dividend, compensation tied to FCF per share and relative TSR rather than production, a board that includes the author of the standard text on per-share-value capital allocation, and a buyback cadence that demonstrably bought more stock as the price fell (2.66M shares in June at $33.41 against 1.14M in April at $38.57). The balance sheet has been deliberately cleared of near-term maturities to let that framework operate through a soft price environment.

The framework is being applied to a shrinking asset with rising capital requirements. Volumes fell 9.6% year over year in Q2 and are guided lower for the full year; capital cost per unit is up 18.4%; the revenue-line half of the low-carbon story has declined two years running; guidance was cut alongside a large non-GAAP beat; the single-largest-customer share nearly doubled; and about two-thirds of 2027 production is pre-sold at $3.31, so the Appalachian demand thesis management calls "tremendous" reaches shareholders only through the share count. Five consecutive years of relative TSR underperformance suggest the market has been pricing this trade-off, not missing it. Separately, roughly $781M of repurchases across FY2025 and H1 2026 left the share count essentially where it stood at the end of 2024, because the May 2026 convertible settlement issued 12.64M net shares — a sunk consequence of a 2020 financing decision that is now permanently behind the company, but one that should temper enthusiasm for the per-share narrative's historical delivery.

At $35.72 the stock offers a roughly 9.9% FCF yield, 5.9x EV/EBITDAX, 1.11x EV/PV-10, and a mechanical path to roughly $39 through share retirement, against a credible $27 downside if capital intensity proves structural. That is a fair price with thin asymmetry and several unpriced binaries. HOLD, with the October print and final Treasury 45Z guidance as the decision points.
