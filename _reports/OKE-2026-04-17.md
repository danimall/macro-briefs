## Executive Summary

ONEOK has transformed itself over the past two years through approximately $19B in acquisitions (Magellan, EnLink, Medallion, Delaware Basin JV) from a primarily NGL-focused midstream operator into one of North America's largest integrated infrastructure platforms spanning 60,000 miles of pipeline. Adjusted EBITDA grew 53% from $5.2B (2023) to $8.0B (2025), and the company now operates a well-diversified four-segment business with approximately 90% fee-based earnings.

The cost of this transformation has been substantial. Total debt tripled from $12.7B to $34.0B, interest expense doubled to $1.8B annually, and diluted EPS in 2025 ($5.42) was actually lower than 2023 ($5.48) despite the massive EBITDA expansion. At 15.7x earnings, OKE trades at a 44% discount to the peer median P/E of 28.2x, which reflects both the opportunity (cheap entry if integration succeeds) and the risk (leverage and execution). The 5.1% dividend yield is well-covered by OCF at a 46% payout ratio, but FCF after capex barely covers the distribution, leaving growth spending funded by debt.

This is a hold-for-income position. The valuation discount and yield are attractive, but the leverage burden ($34B debt, net debt/EBITDA ~4.2x), mixed earnings delivery (2 beats and 2 misses in 4 quarters), and disappointing 2026 EBITDA guidance (~2.7% growth) argue against upgrading to BUY until tangible deleveraging materializes.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates four reportable segments from its Tulsa, Oklahoma headquarters:

- **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing** (27% of EBITDA): 22,600 miles of gathering pipelines, 7.2 Bcf/d processing capacity across the Williston, Powder River, Mid-Continent, and Permian Basins. Over 4.7 million dedicated acres.
- **Natural Gas Liquids** (35% of EBITDA): 14,900 miles of gathering/distribution pipelines, 1.2 MMBbl/d fractionation capacity, 40 MMBbl storage, eight terminals connecting supply basins to Conway, KS and Mont Belvieu, TX.
- **Natural Gas Pipelines** (11% of EBITDA): 8,300 miles of intrastate/interstate pipelines, 74 Bcf of working gas storage. Includes ownership of the Henry Hub through Sabine Pipeline.
- **Refined Products & Crude** (27% of EBITDA): 9,800 miles of refined products pipelines (one of the longest common carrier systems in the U.S.), 2,100 miles crude gathering, 53 terminals, 100 MMBbl storage.

The company serves over 320 customers across North America, with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue. The integrated platform connects wellhead supply to refining/export demand through a continuous asset chain.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue reached $33.6B in 2025, up 55% from $21.7B in 2024, driven primarily by full-year contributions from acquired assets. Operating income of $5.7B represents a 17% operating margin, consistent with midstream economics where throughput volumes drive results. Adjusted EBITDA of $8.0B implies a 24% EBITDA margin.

The critical disconnect lies in per-share economics. Despite EBITDA growing 53% from 2023 to 2025, diluted EPS declined from $5.48 to $5.42. The culprits are straightforward: share count expansion (41M shares issued for EnLink alone, plus 4.9M for the Delaware Basin JV) and interest expense doubling from $866M to $1.783B. The acquisitions are accretive to total enterprise earnings but neutral-to-dilutive per share, at least initially.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow of $5.6B in 2025 is strong in absolute terms. Subtracting $3.2B in capex yields $2.4B in free cash flow. Dividends consumed $2.6B. FCF coverage of the dividend is thus slightly negative (-$200M), meaning the dividend is funded by OCF but growth capex is funded by debt. This is typical for midstream companies in an expansion phase, but it means deleveraging will be slow absent EBITDA growth exceeding capex.

### Balance Sheet

This is the primary area of concern:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total Debt | $34.0B |
| Stockholders' Equity | $22.5B |
| Debt/Equity | 2.96x |
| Net Debt/EBITDA | ~4.2x |
| Interest Expense (2025) | $1.783B |
| Cash on Hand | $78M |
| Current Ratio | 0.71 |
| Credit Ratings | BBB/Baa2/BBB (Stable) |

Net debt/EBITDA of ~4.2x is at the high end for investment-grade midstream. The company has access to a $3.5B revolving credit facility (expires Feb 2030) and a $3.5B commercial paper program, providing adequate liquidity. But the minimal cash balance ($78M) and sub-1.0 current ratio mean ONEOK is entirely dependent on capital market access. In 2025, the company issued $3.0B in new notes and retired $3.1B, effectively refinancing maturing debt at higher rates (4.95%–6.25% on August 2025 issuance). With rates likely elevated through 2026, each refinancing cycle incrementally increases interest expense.

Goodwill of $8.1B and intangibles of $2.9B represent $11B in acquisition-related assets that could face impairment risk if commodity volumes or asset utilization fall below acquisition assumptions.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Outlook

2026 EBITDA guidance of ~$8.1B represents only 2.7% growth over 2025, which disappointed the market. After spending ~$19B on acquisitions, investors expected more immediate per-share accretion. EPS estimates reflect this: the current-year consensus of $5.68 implies 4.8% growth, and next-year at $6.14 implies 8.1% growth. These are modest figures for a company carrying this much leverage.

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story. Over the past 30 days, estimates have ticked up modestly (+1.9% for current quarter, +0.4% for current year). Over 90 days, however, estimates have been cut meaningfully — next-year EPS revised down 4.3% from $6.42 to $6.14, and current-year down 4.1% from $5.92 to $5.68. The short-term stabilization is encouraging, but the medium-term direction has been negative.

### Growth Project Pipeline

ONEOK has $3B+ in identified growth projects:

- **Bighorn Processing Plant** (Permian): $365M, mid-2027
- **Medford Fractionator Rebuild**: $485M, 2026-2027
- **Texas City LPG Export Terminal** (JV with MPLX): $700M OKE share, early 2028
- **MBTC Pipeline**: $280M, early 2028
- **Eiger Express Pipeline** (JV): $350M, mid-2028
- **Greater Denver Pipeline Expansion**: $480M, mid-2026

These projects target the correct growth vectors: Permian Basin processing, NGL fractionation, and LPG export capacity. The Texas City export terminal positions ONEOK in the growing international LPG market. The 10-K also flags data center natural gas demand as an emerging pipeline growth driver.

The risk is execution. ONEOK is simultaneously integrating four major acquisitions, running $2.7–$3.2B in annual capex, and managing $34B in debt. Construction cost overruns, material cost inflation from tariffs (explicitly flagged in the 10-K), and the possibility that producer drilling activity slows would all pressure project returns.

### Earnings Delivery

The four-quarter earnings track record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses, including a significant -17.5% miss in Q1 2025 (EPS of $1.04 vs. $1.26 expected). Q4 2025 revenue missed by 4.5% while EPS beat by 4.7%, suggesting cost management offset lower-than-expected volumes. This inconsistency warrants caution in relying on forward estimates.

OKE trades at 15.7x trailing earnings (also the forward P/E per the data), compared to the peer median of 28.2x:

| KMI | 23.2x | 3.7% | -9% |
| **OKE** | **15.7x** | **5.1%** | **-14%** |

Three explanations for the discount:

1. **Leverage**: At 4.2x net debt/EBITDA, OKE carries materially more debt than peers, which should compress the equity multiple.
2. **EPS dilution from acquisitions**: Per-share growth has lagged total enterprise growth, undermining multiple expansion.
3. **Execution uncertainty**: Integrating four acquisitions simultaneously creates operational risk that the market discounts.

If ONEOK successfully deleverages toward 3.5x and delivers $6.14 in EPS (next-year consensus), a re-rating toward 18–20x P/E would yield a stock price of $110–$123. At current multiples and current estimates, the stock is fairly valued in the low-$80s. The yield exceeds every peer except KMI, providing income support while waiting for potential multiple expansion.

Analyst consensus targets range from $74 (low) to $113 (high) with a median of $91 — approximately 10% upside from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK now ranks among the top 5 midstream operators in North America alongside Enterprise Products Partners (EPD), Energy Transfer (ET), Williams Companies (WMB), and Kinder Morgan (KMI). The acquisition strategy has created several competitive advantages:

**Scale and integration**: The 60,000-mile pipeline network connecting wellhead to export dock provides optimization opportunities that standalone G&P or NGL operators cannot replicate. A molecule entering ONEOK's system at the Permian wellhead can be gathered, processed, fractionated, transported, stored, and exported entirely on ONEOK infrastructure.

**Strategic asset positioning**: Ownership of the Henry Hub (via Sabine Pipeline), the Medford fractionation complex, and the planned Texas City LPG export terminal creates exposure to structural demand growth from LNG exports and international LPG demand.

**Fee-based earnings**: The ~90% fee-based mix reduces volatility relative to peers with higher POP (percentage-of-proceeds) contract exposure.

**Permian concentration**: Through Medallion, EnLink, the Delaware Basin JV, and the new Bighorn plant, ONEOK has assembled a significant Permian processing and gathering footprint targeting the most active drilling basin in the country.

The primary competitive vulnerability is the leverage profile. EPD operates at ~3.0x debt/EBITDA and trades at a premium. If ONEOK can converge toward EPD's leverage profile, the valuation gap should narrow.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Leverage and refinancing risk**: Any credit downgrade below BBB would restrict commercial paper access and increase borrowing costs. A 50 bps increase across the debt stack would add ~$170M in annual interest expense.

2. **Integration execution**: Processing plant utilization fell to 78% in 2025 from 84% in 2024, suggesting either capacity addition outpaced demand or integration friction reduced efficiency.

3. **Volume risk from producer activity**: If Permian drilling activity slows (plausible given oil below $83 on ceasefire dynamics), ONEOK's G&P throughput volumes decline, directly impacting fee revenue. The dedicated acreage position provides some protection through minimum volume commitments, but these vary by contract.

4. **Interest rate environment**: With the FOMC unanimously on hold and rate cut probability in low single digits, each debt maturity that refinances at current rates creates incremental interest expense pressure.

5. **Tariff impact on construction costs**: The 10-K explicitly added tariff risk as a new factor. With $3B+ in projects under construction, material cost increases from tariffs directly affect project returns and timelines.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 114% (1-day expiry) is noise from approaching expiration. The more meaningful readings are 30.4% at 1-month and 26.6–26.8% at 3-month and 6-month tenors. Historical volatility is 26.7%, so options pricing is essentially at-the-money relative to realized vol once the expiry distortion is stripped out. Put/call OI ratio of 0.57 is call-skewed, suggesting modestly bullish positioning. The balanced skew (0.1%) and call-heavy volume ratio (0.62 P/C) do not signal elevated stress. The steep backwardation in the term structure is entirely a function of the 1-day expiry mechanics and should be disregarded.

### Short Interest

Short interest at 4.8% of float with 4.9 days to cover is moderate — elevated enough to indicate some bearish positioning but far from crowded. This is consistent with a stock where the thesis is debatable rather than decisively positive or negative.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($100–$113, 18–33% upside)

ONEOK executes on integration, delivering $200–$400M in synergies from EnLink/Medallion. EBITDA reaches $8.5–$9.0B by 2027 as growth projects come online (Bighorn, Texas City, Medford). Deleveraging to 3.5x net debt/EBITDA triggers a re-rating from 15.7x to 18–20x earnings on $6.14+ EPS. The dividend provides downside support and is raised 3–5% annually. Permian production growth sustains volume throughput. The data center demand thesis materializes, driving incremental natural gas pipeline utilization. At 18x $6.14 EPS, the stock reaches ~$110.

### Bear Case ($64–$70, 15–22% downside)

Integration stumbles compress margins. Permian drilling slows as oil prices stabilize in the $75–$85 range, reducing throughput volumes below acquisition assumptions. Interest expense continues rising as debt matures and refinances at higher rates. Goodwill impairment charges become necessary. The dividend is maintained but not raised, and the stock re-tests the November 2025 lows near $64. Credit rating agencies place the outlook on negative watch, further compressing the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Net debt/EBITDA falls below 3.8x on a trailing basis (current: ~4.2x)
2. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats >3%
3. Stock trades below $75 (improving risk/reward to >20% upside to base case)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Credit rating downgraded below BBB- by any major agency
2. Dividend cut or suspension
3. 2026 EBITDA guidance reduced below $7.5B
4. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 4.5x

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q1 2026, expected late April/early May 2026). Key items to monitor: segment EBITDA contribution from EnLink/Medallion on a same-store basis, capex run rate, and any updates to deleveraging timeline.

## Conclusion

ONEOK is a well-positioned midstream infrastructure company that has taken on substantial leverage risk to build scale. The dividend yield, P/E discount to peers, and diversified asset base support a reasonable income hold. However, the debt load, mixed recent earnings delivery, and modest 2026 growth guidance prevent a BUY recommendation at current levels. The stock requires demonstrated deleveraging and improved per-share earnings delivery before the valuation discount can close. Hold for income; reassess after Q1 2026 earnings.
