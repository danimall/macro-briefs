**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dominion Energy has ceased to trade as a standalone regulated utility. In May 2026, NextEra Energy (NEE) agreed to acquire the company in an all-stock merger valued at approximately $66.8 billion in equity (roughly $420 billion combined enterprise value), with NextEra as the buyer and its CEO John Ketchum slated to lead the combined entity. Regulatory applications were filed with the Virginia State Corporation Commission around July 15, 2026, and the transaction requires FERC, NRC, HSR, and shareholder approvals before an expected close near mid-2027. From this point, D is a merger-arbitrage and regulatory-approval instrument rather than a fundamental utility equity, and the analysis must weight deal-completion probability, the fixed exchange ratio, and NextEra's own valuation more heavily than Dominion's own earnings trajectory.

The stock closed the recent period at $70.27 (quoted intraday at $71.47), roughly 21% above where it traded a year ago and within about 2% of its 52-week high of $72.99. Most of that appreciation is the acquisition premium, crystallized in a single session on May 18, 2026, when the stock gapped from the low $60s to the high $60s on 40 million shares. At the current level against FY2025 EPS of $3.45, D carries a trailing P/E near 20.7x, a premium to the multi-utility peer median of ~19.5x that is difficult to justify on standalone regulated cash flows and reflects the deal spread and NEE's stock value.

The underlying business is a de-risked, regulated-electric pure-play with ~95% of earnings from state-regulated operations and a premier growth runway underneath the world's densest data-center market in Virginia. It also carries stretched leverage (D/E 2.83), structurally negative free cash flow (–$7.3 billion in FY2025), ongoing equity dilution, a deliberately flat dividend, a Moody's negative outlook, and a genuine tail risk in the Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind (CVOW) project. On balance, the remaining spread to the deal outcome is thin relative to a year-long approval process facing organized Virginia political opposition. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Dominion is a Richmond, Virginia-based regulated electric utility serving approximately 4.1 million customers across Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina. Following a major 2023–2024 portfolio overhaul (divesting all gas-distribution utilities — East Ohio, Questar, PSNC — to Enbridge for ~$9.3 billion in cash plus ~$4.9 billion of assumed debt, and selling its Cove Point LNG stake to Berkshire Hathaway Energy for $3.3 billion), the company transformed from a sprawling multi-utility into a focused regulated-electric operator. That simplification is what made it a clean acquisition target.

The business runs across three segments plus corporate:
- **Dominion Energy Virginia** — the crown jewel, with roughly $2,325 million in 2025 net income serving 2.8 million customers, situated atop "Data Center Alley" in Loudoun County.
- **Dominion Energy South Carolina** — electric and gas, ~$535 million net income.
- **Contracted Energy** — Millstone nuclear in Connecticut plus nonregulated solar and RNG, ~$438 million net income.
- **Corporate & Other** — approximately ($300 million).

The strategic asset is location: Dominion owns the grid under the epicenter of U.S. AI and data-center demand growth. That is the explicit rationale for NextEra's bid — acquiring the Virginia franchise as an "AI infrastructure prize."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $16.5 billion, up 14% from $14.46 billion, driven by Virginia non-fuel riders (+$764 million), fuel pass-throughs, a South Carolina rate case, and load growth. Net income was $2,998 million (+47% YoY), with diluted EPS of $3.45 (operating EPS $3.42). Q1 2026 operating EPS was $0.95, up 2%, and FY2026 guidance was affirmed at $3.45–$3.69 (midpoint $3.57). The earnings track record is solid: three beats and one in-line result over the last four quarters, including a 9.5% beat in Q3 2025 and an 8.6% beat in Q2 2025.

**Cash flow and capital plan.** Operating cash flow was $5.36 billion against capex of $12.64 billion, producing free cash flow of –$7.28 billion. This deeply negative FCF is structural rather than distress; it reflects one of the largest utility capital programs in the country. The 2026–2030 capital plan totals $64.7 billion ($55.8 billion at Virginia), funding CVOW, dispatchable gas, solar, storage, and transmission to serve data-center load.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets were $115.9 billion against $29.1 billion of equity, with D/E of 2.83 and a current ratio of 0.77. The Altman Z-score of 0.81 nominally signals "distress," but this is a false positive: the metric is calibrated for manufacturers and misfires on a rate-base-heavy regulated utility running deliberately negative FCF. Debt/cap of 53.5% sits comfortably inside the 67.5% covenant, and a Piotroski score of 4/5 alongside investment-grade ratings indicates a company that is stretched but not distressed. Credit ratings as of February 2026 were Moody's Baa2 (Negative outlook), S&P BBB+ (Stable), and Fitch BBB+ (Stable). The Moody's negative outlook reflects funding strain from the capex program.

**Financing dependence and dilution.** For 2026, Dominion plans $6.0–9.5 billion of long-term debt issuance and $1.6–1.8 billion of equity (ATM/forwards). Share count rose from 852 million to 880 million, with forwards struck at roughly $55–61 per share. The dividend was held flat at $2.67/share with no buybacks, signaling capital discipline appropriate to the funding demand. At the current price, the dividend yields ~3.75%.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, Dominion's growth story is among the best-positioned in the regulated space. Data centers represent 28% of Virginia Power sales, and PJM projects 5.4% annual DOM-zone load growth. The $64.7 billion capital plan converts that load into rate base, and Virginia's biennial framework supports an expected ROE near 9.8%. The Q1 2026 result included favorable contributions from the 2025 Biennial Review (+$105 million) and rider equity returns (+$84 million).

Estimate revisions are mixed but not directionally negative. The current-year consensus of $3.59 is essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days, and next year's $3.82 is stable. The current-quarter estimate was cut sharply (–16.2% over 30 days to $0.68), but this reflects quarterly timing and seasonality rather than a structural downgrade; next-quarter estimates rose +4.3%. There is no evidence of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection, so the estimate-revision signal is neutral here.

The critical qualifier: with the NextEra merger pending, Dominion's standalone growth trajectory matters less than deal economics. In an all-stock transaction, holders will receive NEE shares at a fixed exchange ratio, so the forward growth that matters is a blend of the combined company's prospects and the standalone reversion value if the deal breaks. Virginia's data-center load growth is now as much a regulatory variable in the merger review as it is an earnings driver, given the ~25% rate increases already in play and rising political scrutiny over who pays.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$71.5, D trades at roughly 20.7x trailing FY2025 EPS and ~20.8x forward, a premium to the multi-utility peer median P/E of 19.5x (Sempra 31.1x, Xcel 22.6x, Ameren 19.5x, ConEd 17.9x, PG&E 13.0x). Its 3.75% dividend yield exceeds the 2.9% peer median. On standalone regulated fundamentals — high leverage, negative FCF, flat dividend, and a capped ~9.8% ROE — the premium multiple is not organically justified. It exists because of the acquisition premium.

The relevant valuation math is now the merger spread. The stock has drifted up toward $71–72 as the market prices deal-completion probability. Analyst price targets bracket the situation tightly: low $67, high $79, mean $71, median $70.5 — the mean and median sit essentially at the current price. This is characteristic of a merger-arb stock where the market has already discounted most of the expected outcome. The high target ($79) reflects a higher-probability close and/or NEE stock appreciation; the low ($67) approximates the standalone reversion floor if regulatory approval falters.

The asymmetry is unattractive. Upside to the mean/median target is roughly flat-to-negative, upside to the high target is ~11% and gated by a ~12-month approval process, and the downside on a deal break reverts toward the pre-announcement mid-$60s — a ~10% loss — with additional risk that a contested Virginia review extracts concessions that impair the combined economics. We do not apply cycle-adjusted or inflection logic here because Dominion is neither at a trough nor showing the >30% revision velocity that would warrant it. The multiple is stretched precisely because of the deal, and a "cheap on peers" argument does not exist. Valuation scores low.

## Competitive Landscape

Dominion operates regulated monopolies with no retail competition in its core territories, with limited transmission competition under FERC Order 1000 and expanding transmission reach via the Valley Link JV with AEP and FirstEnergy. Its competitive position is anchored by the grid underneath the largest concentration of data-center demand in the world. This is a structurally advantaged franchise, and the trajectory is improving as AI-driven load growth expands the rate-base opportunity.

The counterweight is that this concentration is also a growing liability. The 28% data-center share of Virginia Power sales is fueling a political backlash: rate increases, cross-class subsidy fights, and a new GS-5 tariff requiring users above 25 MW to post $1.5 million/MW collateral on 14-year contracts from January 2027. That backlash directly shapes the merger's regulatory approval terms and future cost recovery. The competitive position is strong and improving on load, which supports an above-average score, but the regulatory/political overlay caps how much of that advantage flows through to shareholders.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger execution / regulatory risk (paramount).** Virginia SCC, FERC, NRC, and shareholder approvals are pending, against vocal public and political opposition in Virginia over "consolidation of power" and customer bills. A deal break reverts D to standalone valuation, likely in the mid-$60s. Timeline extends to roughly mid-2027, meaning the position carries a year of approval risk before resolution.

**CVOW offshore wind.** The 2.6 GW project has escalated to ~$11.5 billion from an $8 billion estimate. Under Virginia's cost-sharing bands, Dominion cannot recover overruns between $11.3 billion and $13.7 billion; it has already booked $258 million (2025) and $103 million (2024) in non-recoverable charges. Add tariff exposure (~$0.6 billion) and federal political interference (December 2025 stop-work order; January 2026 injunction allowing resumption; an un-quantified February 2026 Supreme Court tariff ruling). First power was achieved March 2026; COD targets mid-2027. Ketchum has publicly endorsed finishing the project, reducing but not eliminating this tail.

**Capital-markets dependence.** Executing the capital plan hinges on continued access to debt and equity at reasonable cost. Moody's is already Negative; a downgrade would raise financing costs on a model that is structurally negative-FCF and dilutive in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Regulatory earnings caps.** The Virginia biennial framework caps ROE near 9.8% with 85–100% earnings-sharing above authorized levels, limiting upside even in a strong load environment.

**Other.** Nuclear, coastal weather/hurricane exposure, cyber, and partnership risk (Stonepeak on CVOW; AEP/FirstEnergy on Valley Link).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is priced for a quiet, event-gated situation rather than an imminent catalyst. Near-term ATM IV is 22.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 20.6% — fair, with a 1.4-point spread and a 23rd-percentile IV reading. The term structure is in contango (22.0% near vs. 24.7% at six months), which is normal and consistent with a merger whose resolution sits ~12 months out. The put/call skew of 4.9% at the front month shows modestly elevated put demand, and the six-month tenor flips to a –3.3% skew, indicating the market prices more two-sided (or deal-optimistic) longer-dated outcomes. The implied ±4.6% move at the nearest expiry is muted. Short interest is low at 3.6% of float (4.3 days to cover). Nothing in options positioning contradicts a HOLD; the market is treating D as a slow-burn approval trade, not a near-term binary.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
NextEra completes the acquisition on schedule near mid-2027. Regulatory reviews clear with manageable concessions, Virginia's data-center load growth is confirmed as the strategic prize, CVOW reaches COD without further material non-recoverable charges, and NEE stock holds or appreciates, dragging the fixed-exchange-ratio value of D toward the $79 high target (~11% upside). Even absent full deal upside, the standalone franchise compounds mid-single-digit EPS on a premier rate-base runway.

### Bear Case
Virginia political opposition forces onerous merger conditions or the deal is denied outright. D reverts to standalone valuation in the mid-$60s (~10% downside), where the stretched leverage, negative FCF, dilution, flat dividend, and CVOW cost band reassert as the primary drivers. A Moody's downgrade or an adverse CVOW cost/tariff outcome compounds the reversion. The stock is currently pricing a high-probability, clean close; any friction removes the premium.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The stock is a merger-arbitrage situation trading near the mean/median analyst target, with thin remaining spread against a year of approval risk and organized political opposition.

**Reassessment timeline:** Key milestones are the Virginia SCC procedural schedule and rulings, FERC/NRC/HSR determinations, and the shareholder vote, spread across late 2026 through mid-2027. The next natural reassessment points are quarterly earnings and any material regulatory filing or ruling on the merger.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable Virginia SCC procedural ruling or FERC/NRC clearance that materially raises deal-completion probability, widening the spread to the $79 high target.
- The stock pulls back toward the mid-$60s on transient regulatory noise while the deal remains on track, restoring an asymmetric spread.
- Confirmation of CVOW COD near mid-2027 with no further material non-recoverable charges, de-risking the largest project-specific tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Virginia SCC or FERC signals rejection or demands conditions that impair the combined economics; deal-break probability rises materially.
- Standalone reversion becomes the base case (deal formally terminated), exposing the stretched leverage and CVOW band without the acquisition premium.
- A Moody's downgrade from Baa2 or an adverse CVOW cost/tariff ruling that expands non-recoverable charges beyond the $11.3–13.7 billion band already flagged.

## Conclusion

Dominion is a fundamentally sound regulated-electric pure-play with the single best data-center-driven growth runway among U.S. utilities, offset by stretched leverage, structurally negative free cash flow, ongoing dilution, a flat dividend, a Moody's negative outlook, and a genuine CVOW tail risk. The standalone case is now secondary. The pending NextEra all-stock acquisition dominates, and at ~$71.5 the stock has already captured the bulk of the deal premium, trading at the mean/median analyst target with the high target ($79) offering ~11% gated upside and a deal break implying ~10% downside toward the mid-$60s. That risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-unfavorable, resolving over a ~12-month regulatory gauntlet facing organized Virginia opposition. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the current price does not offer the spread to warrant chasing, nor does the fundamental picture warrant avoidance. Hold, monitor the SCC/FERC/NRC approval path, and reassess on a pullback toward the reversion floor or a decisive regulatory clearance.
