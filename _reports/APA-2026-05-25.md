## Executive Summary

APA Corporation has rallied 132% from its 52-week low of $16.15 to $38.80, driven almost entirely by elevated oil prices stemming from Hormuz Strait disruption. The company's forward earnings estimates have been revised up 187% over 90 days (FY26 from $2.15 to $6.19), placing the stock at 6.3x forward P/E — a meaningful discount to peer median of 13.1x. This discount reflects legitimate structural concerns: an $881M–$1.2B legacy decommissioning liability in the Gulf of America, a North Sea operation winding down under 78% effective tax rates, historical five-year TSR underperformance (197 vs. 295 peer group), and binary commodity price sensitivity.

The bull case centers on three elements: (1) current valuation compression relative to dramatically improved earnings power, (2) Suriname GranMorgu development providing transformational optionality with a highly favorable TotalEnergies carry structure, and (3) aggressive debt reduction ($1.55B in 2025) improving balance sheet flexibility. The bear case is that the entire earnings uplift is commodity-price-driven, APA has no structural competitive advantage within E&P, and if oil normalizes below $70 (a 25-30% probability within 60 days on potential Hormuz resolution), the earnings revision cycle reverses quickly. With consensus tilted heavily toward HOLD (15 of 27 analysts) and recent insider selling rather than buying, the risk/reward at $38.80 is approximately balanced.

## Company Overview

APA Corporation operates through its wholly-owned subsidiary Apache Corporation across four geographic segments. The United States (Permian Basin) accounts for 62% of production and 74% of proved reserves. Egypt contributes 31% of production through 7.5 million gross acres in the Western Desert under a Merged Concession Agreement with EGPC, with Sinopec holding a one-third noncontrolling interest. The North Sea (7% of production) is being wound down and will cease operations before 2030 due to the U.K. Energy Profits Levy. Suriname represents a pre-production development asset targeting first oil in 2028.

Total proved reserves stand at 1.056 billion boe (71% liquids). Production averaged approximately 464,000 boe/d in 2025. The company completed its Callon Petroleum acquisition in April 2024 ($4.5B all-stock) and has since rationalized the combined portfolio through non-core divestitures and a shift to fewer, higher-productivity Permian rigs.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** APA generated $7.23B in oil and gas revenue in 2025 (down 12% from $8.20B in 2024 on lower realized prices), but net income rose to $1.43B from $0.80B due to the absence of $1.1B in prior-year impairments. EPS of $3.99 reflects underlying operational improvement despite the price headwind. Average realized oil was $66.92/bbl, down 14% YoY.

**Cash Flow & Capital Discipline:** Operating cash flow reached $4.55B (+26% vs. 2024). Against $2.74B in upstream capex, free cash flow was approximately $1.8B. The company allocated this toward $2.25B in debt repayment, $360M in dividends, and $280M in buybacks — a credible capital allocation framework prioritizing balance sheet repair post-Callon.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:** Total debt fell from $6.04B to $4.49B in 2025, a $1.55B reduction. Net debt-to-equity improved to 0.65x. Available credit facilities total $4.02B. The current ratio of 0.82 is below 1.0 but standard for E&P companies with committed revolving facilities.

**Cost Reduction:** Lifting costs fell 13% to $8.86/boe. The company achieved $350M in annualized savings in 2025 and targets $450M by year-end 2026. This structural efficiency gain persists regardless of commodity prices.

**Q1 2026 Signals:** U.S. oil realized at $72.50/bbl (reflecting elevated Brent), international at $85.70/bbl. However, U.S. natural gas at negative $0.35/Mcf required curtailment of 88 MMcf/d. Gas marketing arbitrage generated $244M in net gains, partially offsetting the basis crisis.

**Earnings Trajectory:** The stock beat consensus by 17-80% in each of the last four quarters. Current quarter (Q2 2026) estimates sit at $2.00, up 309% in 90 days. Full-year 2026 consensus is $6.19 (up from $2.15 ninety days ago). Full-year 2027 is $4.14 (implying oil price normalization). The entire earnings revision cycle is commodity-price-driven.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**
- Permian Basin: High-graded to ~5 rigs on core Midland/Delaware acreage. Maintaining production with fewer rigs demonstrates capital efficiency improvement. Reserve replacement via extensions/discoveries (100 MMboe in 2025) and positive revisions (175 MMboe) exceed production.
- Egypt: New gas sales agreement (minimum $2.65/MMBtu) improves economics for gas exploration on 7.5 million acres. This is a multi-year growth vector if gas discoveries materialize.
- Alaska: Successful flow test (2,700 b/d) provides early-stage optionality. Requires further appraisal.

**Suriname — Transformational Optionality:** The GranMorgu FPSO (220,000 b/d capacity) targets first oil in 2028. The carry structure is exceptionally favorable: TotalEnergies pays 87.5% of the first $10B in capex. APA's net cost for its 50% working interest is approximately $1.3B on the first $10B of spend, plus it receives a $75M payment at first oil. At $70+ oil, this could contribute $1–2B+ in annual EBITDA from 2028-2029 onward. This optionality is not yet reflected in production or earnings but represents a genuine step-change if executed successfully.

**Production Guidance:** 2026 upstream capex is guided at ~$2.1B (down from $2.74B in 2025), suggesting capital efficiency improvement rather than production growth. APA is in a harvest/optimize mode in existing basins while developing Suriname for medium-term growth.

**Limitations:** Without Suriname, APA's organic growth profile is maintenance-level at best. The Permian rig reduction (from 7 to 5) prioritizes returns over volume growth. Egypt production is mature. North Sea is declining. The growth story is Suriname-dependent for the 2028+ timeframe.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.80, APA trades at:
- 6.3x FY26 consensus EPS of $6.19
- 9.4x FY27 consensus EPS of $4.14 (reflecting assumed oil price normalization)
- Trailing P/E of 9.7x on $3.99 TTM EPS
- Estimated ~3.5x EV/EBITDA on 2026 estimates

This represents a significant discount to peer median P/E of 13.1x. The discount is persistent — APA has traded cheap to peers for five years, with TSR of 197 vs. 295 for its peer group. The discount reflects: (1) GoA decommissioning liability ($2.50+/share accrued), (2) North Sea wind-down costs, (3) complex international mix with Egypt counterparty risk, (4) historical capital allocation missteps under the Apache legacy brand.

**Why the market may be wrong (bull case):** If FY26 EPS of $6.19 materializes (requiring oil to stay elevated through H2), the stock at 6.3x is genuinely cheap even accounting for the structural discount. Adding Suriname optionality (potentially worth $5-10/share in NPV at $70+ oil) and continued debt reduction provides a path to $45-50.

**Why the market may be right (bear case):** FY27 consensus of $4.14 implies the market expects oil to normalize. At a "fair" 9-10x on normalized $4.14 EPS, fair value is $37-41 — approximately where the stock trades today. The discount to peers may be permanent and justified by the liability/complexity overhang.

**Critical dependency:** The estimate revision signal (+309% in 90 days) is among the strongest possible. However, this is entirely commodity-driven, not company-specific operational outperformance. If Hormuz resolves and oil declines to $85-95 Brent, FY26 estimates compress toward $4.50-5.00, and the stock at 8-9x those numbers is $38-45 — roughly current levels. The appreciation from $16 to $39 has already priced in the commodity uplift.

## Competitive Landscape

APA occupies a specific niche: mid-cap E&P with international diversification and a Permian core. Its competitive position is mediocre relative to peers:

- **vs. EOG/FANG (pure Permian):** APA lacks the concentrated basin quality and returns profile that commands premium multiples. Its Permian position is solid but not best-in-class.
- **vs. COP/DVN (diversified E&P):** Smaller scale, higher complexity, weaker historical returns.
- **vs. Pure international (Hess/Murphy):** Egypt operations are mature; Suriname is shared with TotalEnergies (who operates) reducing differentiation.

The company's competitive position is neither improving nor deteriorating in a structural sense. The Callon integration added Permian scale, and portfolio rationalization (exiting New Mexico, selling non-core) has improved focus. But the Permian peer group operates with lower breakevens and higher returns on capital.

**One genuine competitive edge:** The gas marketing business generated $244M in net gains in Q1 2026 by arbitraging Waha basis differentials through APA's 750 MMBtu/d of firm pipeline capacity. This is a real, albeit volatile, source of differentiated income.

## Risk Assessment

**Gulf of America Decommissioning ($881M–$1.2B+ accrued):** APA's most distinctive risk. BSEE has issued orders requiring Apache to decommission legacy assets sold to Fieldwood (which went bankrupt twice). Annual cash outflows have been $200-275M. The total could exceed current accruals if "clear seabed" standards are applied. At $2.50+/share, this represents permanent value destruction with uncertain timing and magnitude.

**Binary Oil Price Exposure:** Current estimates assume elevated oil ($100+ Brent). A Hormuz resolution scenario (25-30% probability within 60 days) would bring Brent to $85-95, compressing FY26 EPS by 20-30%. Below $55 WTI, APA's capital program and shareholder returns are impaired.

**North Sea Wind-Down:** Decommissioning an entire offshore basin under 78% effective tax rates while maintaining safety and integrity will consume cash for years. The P&L contribution is negligible but the cash drain may be substantial.

**Egypt Counterparty Risk:** EGPC represents ~15% of revenue. Egypt experienced FX shortages and payment delays historically, with past-due receivables only "nearly eliminated" in Q3 2025. Political instability risk persists.

**Suriname Execution (2028):** $10.5B deepwater FPSO development in a frontier basin. While the carry structure limits APA's capital exposure, operational delays or cost overruns could defer the transformational earnings contribution.

**Insider Activity:** Recent insider selling (Director Bob at $36.23 in March, EVP Maddox at $40.04 in May) with no open-market purchases. Given the stock's rally from $16 to $40, this likely reflects profit-taking after 100%+ appreciation, but is directionally unhelpful.
