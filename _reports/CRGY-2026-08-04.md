## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy is a three-basin onshore U.S. oil-weighted E&P (Eagle Ford, Permian, Uinta) plus a minerals and royalty portfolio, externally managed by a KKR affiliate, that has more than doubled production through acquisition since 2023 — from 149 MBoe/d (2023) to 335 MBoe/d in Q2 2026. The August 3 second-quarter print was the strongest in the company's history: revenue $1,395.0M (+55% year over year), Adjusted EBITDAX $797.9M, operating cash flow $706.8M, levered free cash flow $417.7M, adjusted EPS $0.69. Management raised 2026 production guidance to 327-335 MBoe/d, cut adjusted opex guidance to $11.00-12.00/Boe and production tax guidance to 5.0-6.0% of commodity revenue, and tripled the Permian synergy target to $250-300M with roughly $190M captured. The balance sheet was materially repaired in the same window: revolver drawn to zero, $259M of 7.75% 2029 notes redeemed at par in July, net leverage 1.6x, liquidity ~$2.2B, earliest maturity now the February 2029 minerals term loan.

Three offsets keep this from being a buy at $11.43. First, the quarter was price-assisted and the price did not reach the equity: realized oil of $96.61/Bbl pre-hedge became $73.33 post-hedge, a $23.28/Bbl give-back worth roughly $297M in the quarter against 21.8 MMBbl of 2026 WTI swaps struck at $65.07. Second, natural gas realizations collapsed to $0.52/Mcf (working-interest basis $0.46) on Permian basis widening after the Vital merger — gas is 35.6% of Boe volumes and 2.4% of revenue, which makes every Boe-denominated metric for this company (reserves, EV/Boe, cost per Boe) overweight a stream that currently earns almost nothing. Third, the structure: $78.5M of annual cash Manager compensation (2.1% of market cap), unvested Manager performance units of 19.674M shares (6.00% of outstanding, 8.69% if measured at target) whose target grows mechanically with every share issued, no common-holder vote for directors, no say-on-pay, and change-of-control impediments (Series I Preferred veto, 100% PSU acceleration, a termination fee of 3× trailing Manager economics) that remove the takeover floor a 3x EBITDAX asset would normally carry.

The prior score trajectory ran 5.5 (May 1) to 4.5 (Jul 19). This report marks it up modestly to 4.85 on the Q2 operating and financing evidence, not the 6.3% price gain since July 19. The countervailing datapoint is that consensus has cut next-quarter EPS 12.5%, current-year 6.4% and next-year 5.7% over the last 30 days. That divergence is the thesis in one line: hedges, Waha basis and a 29% PDP decline rate mean a higher oil price in 2026 does not reach this equity. **HOLD.** Base case $13.25 (+16%), bull $19.00 (+66%), bear $7.25 (−37%).

## Company Overview

At 12/31/2025 Crescent held 1,019 thousand net working-interest acres plus 443 thousand net mineral acres, 975.5 MMBoe of proved reserves (61% liquids, up from 709.3 MMBoe a year earlier on the Vital merger), PV-10 of $8,633M and Standardized Measure of $7,756M at SEC prices of $65.34/Bbl and $3.39/MMBtu. Roughly 96% of net acreage is held by production, with only 8 thousand net undeveloped acres expiring in 2026, so lease-expiry pressure is effectively absent — a genuine structural positive for a company that buys rather than explores.

Two features distinguish it from a conventional E&P and both bear on valuation. Operations are run by ~1,066 Crescent employees, but the entire senior executive team is supplied by KKR Energy Assets Manager LLC under a Management Agreement. The CEO (David Rockecharlie) and CFO (Brandi Kendall) receive zero direct compensation from Crescent; the 10-K states the company cannot compute a CEO pay ratio for that reason. Separately, a KKR affiliate holds the non-economic Series I Preferred Stock, which appoints the entire board and carries approval rights over incremental debt above 10% of outstanding indebtedness, significant equity raises, mergers, CEO removal and liquidation. Crescent is a controlled company under NYSE rules and has never solicited a director-election proxy or a say-on-pay vote.

The acquisition record is dense: Western Eagle Ford (2023, ~$828M), SilverBow (July 2024), Central Eagle Ford (Oct 2024, $156.0M), Ridgemar (Jan 2025, $807.2M cash plus 5.5M shares plus up to $170M earn-out), Vital Energy (Dec 15, 2025, 73.3M shares; derived transaction EV ≈ $3,020M), and $355.3M of Eagle Ford minerals in Q1 2026. Purchaser concentration rose year over year: Shell Trading 21.4%, ConocoPhillips 13.5%, Enterprise Products 11.7% — 46.6% of FY2025 revenue from three purchasers versus 40.2% from two in FY2024. These are buyers of Crescent's hydrocarbons, and management's position that production is fungible across regional purchasers is reasonable; the point worth carrying is that concentration among named >10% purchasers increased despite the basin-mix shift.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (audited).** Revenue $3,579.8M (+22%), income from operations $229.3M (+5%), net income attributable to Crescent $132.9M versus a $114.6M loss, diluted Class A EPS $0.54. Adjusted EBITDAX $2,065.6M (+29%), levered free cash flow $856.1M (+36%), operating cash flow $1,680.2M (+37%), development capex $903.2M. Volume growth of 29% carried the year against a 13% decline in realized oil ($62.21 vs $71.14/Bbl). Impairments were $254.6M, following $161.5M (2024) and $153.5M (2023) — a recurring feature under the successful-efforts model here, and one that speaks to acquisition underwriting.

**Q2 and 1H 2026.** Q2 revenue $1,395.0M against $898.0M; production 335 MBoe/d versus 263; Adjusted EBITDAX $797.9M versus $513.9M; LFCF $417.7M versus $170.9M. 1H26 Adjusted EBITDAX $1,487.7M and LFCF $609.5M, on $668.8M of development capital.

GAAP net income is close to uninformative quarter to quarter for this company. The Q1 2026 derivative loss of roughly $706.6M (derived: 1H26 loss of $524.7M less the Q2 gain of $181.9M) produced a $419.2M Q1 GAAP loss, which reversed into $493.7M of Q2 GAAP income. The feed-supplied trailing and forward P/E of −11.06 is inconsistent with the same feed's positive $0.54 trailing EPS and should be disregarded; on consensus the stock is 5.0x current-year ($2.29) and 5.0x next-year ($2.28).

**Balance sheet, and it has genuinely improved.** Cash rose from $10.2M to $264.9M; long-term debt carrying value fell from $5,524.1M to $5,166.0M; the revolver went from $772.0M drawn to zero; net debt is $4,901M at 1.6x credit-agreement Adjusted EBITDAX. That covenant EBITDAX of $3,137M is acquisition-annualized rather than trailing GAAP-derived, which normally warrants suspicion, but 1H26 Adjusted EBITDAX annualized is $2,975M, giving 1.65x. The covenant definition is not doing meaningful work. Interest coverage on Q2 Adjusted EBITDAX against $95.6M of cash interest is 8.3x.

Reconstructing the post-July stack (9.750% 2030 $302.4M; 7.875% 2032 $1,000M; 7.625% 2032 $1,100M; 7.375% 2033 $1,000M; 8.375% 2034 $600M; 2.75% converts 2031 $690M; minerals facility ~$275.5M) gives ~$4,968M of principal and roughly $353M of coupon, plus ~$7.5M of commitment fees on the undrawn $2.0B revolver ≈ $360M/yr. Annualizing 1H26 cash interest of $196.2M implies $392M, so the run-rate is falling. Cash taxes are minimal — $10.0M paid in FY2025, $1.2M of current tax in 1H26 against $87.6M of total tax expense — because of restored 100% bonus depreciation and an EBITDA-based §163(j) limitation; the Vital merger also brought a $695.3M deferred tax asset, subject to Section 382 limits.

Two balance-sheet flags. Working capital is negative $64.7M (current ratio 0.94x) against a revolver minimum-current-ratio covenant; the reported 1.48x at 12/31/2025 was inflated by $725.7M of restricted acquisition cash and was 0.90x excluding it. And fixed commitments are substantial: $808.7M of firm transportation/gathering ($181.7M in 2026), $204.7M of electricity through 2029, $151.6M of Manager compensation through December 2027, up to $170M of Ridgemar earn-out, and $2.4B of estimated future development cost on 199.2 MMBoe of remaining PUDs ($820M of it in 2026).

The February 2026 minerals silo deserves a note: fourteen minerals subsidiaries were released from revolver and senior-note guarantees and became guarantors of a new $1.0B Crescent Minerals and Royalties facility ($365M drawn at 6.612%). For equity holders this unlocks incremental capacity against a high-margin asset; it also means minerals cash flow now services its own debt ahead of the corporate stack.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth is impressive and almost entirely inorganic. Production compounded 149 → 201 → 260 → 335 MBoe/d. The mechanism is stock- and debt-funded consolidation, and stock funding carries an embedded cost: each of the five Manager PSU tranches targets 2% of Class A shares outstanding *at settlement*, so every issuance enlarges the award. The 10-K quantifies it — a 140.8M-share increase in 2025 added ~9.8M target shares and $146.5M of expense (roughly 7% of shares issued); a 95.5M-share increase in 2024 added ~9.5M target shares and $121.8M (roughly 10%).

Organic momentum is the weaker part of the story, and the raised guidance obscures it. 1H26 actual production was 338 MBoe/d (341 in Q1 over 90 days, 335 in Q2 over 91). At the 331 MBoe/d guidance midpoint, full-year volume of 120,815 MBoe implies 2H26 of ~324 MBoe/d; at the 335 top end, ~332 MBoe/d. Development capital is unchanged at $1,325-1,425M with $668.8M already spent, leaving roughly $656-756M for a second half guided flat-to-down on volumes. That is consistent with the 10-K's own disclosure of a **29% estimated 2026 PDP decline rate** — against marketed five- and ten-year average PDP declines of ~12% and ~8% — a direct consequence of adding Permian shale volumes. Completion timing is lumpy (32 gross wells online in Q2) and guidance may be conservative; a 2H26 print at or above 335 MBoe/d would refute the decline read, and that is the single cleanest test available in the next two quarters.

Earnings growth, as consensus sees it, is absent: current-year $2.29 and next-year $2.28. The 30-day revision pattern is uniformly negative (−2.1% current quarter, −12.5% next quarter, −6.4% current year, −5.7% next year) despite three consecutive adjusted beats of 41%, 52% and 17%. The reconciliation is straightforward and is the most important analytical point in this report: 2026 oil is swapped at $65.07 on 21.8 MMBbl, Permian gas realizes near zero, and the Ridgemar earn-out pays $15M per quarter above $70 WTI and another $15M above $75, so the earn-out is a cash cost that rises exactly when hedges are suppressing realized upside. A higher crude price in 2026 improves the reported quarter through unhedged barrels at the margin while doing little for full-year earnings power.

The two genuine growth levers are the minerals business and 2027 hedge roll-off. Minerals volumes reached 13 MBoe/d from 6, realizing $51.45/Boe against $45.38 for working interest, at $4.26/Boe of operating expense versus $13.76 — $49.4M of Adjusted EBITDAX and $44.1M of LFCF in the quarter, an ~89% conversion rate. And 2027 is far less hedged: 3.3 MMBbl of swaps at $61.07 plus 3.65 MMBbl of counterparty-exercisable swaptions at $75.00. If a $90-plus crude regime persists, 2027 is where it reaches the equity.

Growth Prospects: **4.5**.

Peer medians in the group supplied (CRK, MGY, CRC, CNX, TALO) sit at a 6.2x P/E with two of five carrying negative trailing earnings — a heterogeneous set. On forward adjusted EPS, Crescent at 5.0x is a modest discount to that median, not a dramatic one.

**Normalized sensitivity (derived, not a forecast).** Holding Q2 volumes and costs flat and setting WTI at $65 (near the $65.34 SEC deck, above the $56.91-61.14 five-year forward range the 10-K cites): oil revenue falls ~$350M; NGLs at a 20% haircut −$25M; oil hedges swing from a ~$297M realized loss to roughly neutral, +$237M on the derivative loss embedded in Q2 EBITDAX; production taxes +$21M. Normalized quarterly Adjusted EBITDAX ≈ $680M, or ~$2,720M annualized. Deducting $360M of cash interest, $1,375M of development capital and ~$5M of current tax leaves **~$980M of normalized levered free cash flow, a 26% yield on the $3,775M market cap** at 3.2x EV/EBITDAX. The $158.6M dividend consumes 16% of that. Reinvestment at $1,375M ÷ $3,137M = 44% (or 51% on the normalized figure) brackets the company's claimed ~45% historical discipline.

That is a large number and it is the strongest bull argument. Three things stop it from producing a high valuation score.

First, the free cash flow is not reaching per-share value. Crescent repurchased 4.076M shares at an average $8.21 in FY2025 but **zero shares in 1H26 at $11-13**, with $336M of a $400M authorization remaining. Record cash went to the balance sheet — net debt fell roughly $610M in 1H26, which in a 1.30x net-debt-to-market-cap structure is a real transfer to equity, but it arrives as balance-sheet value rather than share count.

Second, the discount has an identified, durable cause rather than a mispricing. Manager cash compensation of $78.5M equals 2.1% of market capitalization annually, in perpetuity, indexed upward by 1.5% of net proceeds from future equity issuances. Unvested Manager PSU target shares were 19.674M (6.00% of outstanding; 94.3% post-issuance ownership retention), and the equity-plan table discloses that at target performance as of 12/31/2025, 28,508,063 shares would have been earned (8.69% of outstanding; 8.0% ownership dilution). The March 2026 $690M 2.75% converts at a $14.89 strike are a separate 46.34M shares (14.0% of existing stock, 12.3% post-issuance dilution), economically offset between $14.89 and $22.48 by a $56.6M capped call that GAAP if-converted accounting does not reflect. Add a $2.4B PUD development obligation and 2.1% of market cap in annual fees and the 40-50% multiple discount to less-encumbered peers is explained.

Third, and specific to a cheap E&P, there is no takeover floor. Any merger requires Series I Preferred approval; unearned Manager PSUs vest at 100% of target on a change of control; and terminating the Management Agreement without cause requires two-thirds of independent directors plus a fee of 3× the sum of average annual Manager Compensation and the average vested Incentive Compensation over the trailing 24 months. An asset at 1.15x PD PV-10 would ordinarily attract a bid; here the mechanism that would close the discount is contractually blocked.

One further caution on PV-10 as an anchor: the 10-K itself notes that SEC oil pricing of $65.34 sits *above* the forward curve while $3.39 gas sits *below* it, so 1.15x EV/PD PV-10 cannot be read as precisely "115% of PDP."

Valuation: **5.5**. Cheap on cash flow, with the cheapness largely accounted for by quantified structural claims on that cash flow, and with no catalyst that forces the market to re-rate on a defined timeline.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent's stated edge is capital allocation, not geology. The 10-K is candid about the constraint: "we compete with other companies that have greater resources than us, particularly following recent consolidation… these companies may be able to pay more for oil and natural gas properties." As buyer-of-choice for sub-scale operators, returns depend on continuing to source assets below intrinsic value.

The Vital integration is the best current evidence, and it is favorable: ~$3.0B of enterprise value for assets carrying $2.35B of fair-valued oil and gas properties plus a $695.3M (§382-limited) deferred tax asset, with the synergy target lifted to $250-300M against ~$190M captured. Tripling a synergy target within eight months of closing is an unusually strong early record. Against it, the 10-K's own forward-looking language names "the risk that the Permian Acquisition may not be accretive, and may be dilutive, to Crescent's earnings per share," and FY2025 revisions of previous estimates were −62.7 MMBoe, including 47.4 MMBoe from removal of PUD inventory and −34.2 MMBoe of PUD revisions tied partly to "revisions to PUD locations on acquired assets." Removing acquired PUD inventory within a year of acquiring it is a data point on underwriting quality.

The cost trajectory is real but partly compositional. Operating expense fell from $16.31/Boe to $13.38/Boe year over year; adjusted opex ex-production-taxes fell from $12.40 to $10.95, roughly 9% below the prior guidance midpoint. Some is synergy and scale. Some is arithmetic: the Boe denominator rose 27%, and the divested midstream gross-up removed roughly $33M of quarterly revenue and $25M of quarterly expense. Both explanations are operating.

Positioning has clearly deteriorated in gas. Q2 realized gas was $0.52/Mcf against $2.71 a year earlier, which the 10-Q attributes to lower index pricing and "a decrease in our price differentials due to the Vital Energy Merger and resulting differentials in the Permian Basin." FY2025 Permian gas realized $0.84/Mcf against $3.05 in the Eagle Ford. Waha fixed-index swaps (55.5 Bcf at $2.41 for 2026; 43.8 Bcf at $2.69 for 2027) and basis swaps at −$0.43 lift post-settlement realizations to $1.74/Mcf, but the physical market is punitive and the merger made the exposure worse. Uinta oil realized $52.96/Bbl in FY2025 against $64.34 in the Eagle Ford, an ~$11 discount on rail/truck-dependent takeaway.

Competitive Position: **5.0** — improving on cost and synergy capture, deteriorating on realized-price mix.

## Risk Assessment

**Gas realizations (highest-magnitude unpriced item).** At $0.52/Mcf, 35.6% of Boe volumes generate 2.4% of revenue. Even on the reserve-report deck, gas is 38.9% of proved Boe but only 15.5% of $32,853M of future cash inflows. Further Waha basis widening pushes a third of the production base toward cash-negative on a marginal basis.

**Hedge-and-earn-out asymmetry.** In Q2, high prices cost $23.28/Bbl of realized oil (~$297M) while triggering Ridgemar earn-out payments ($18.7M of financing outflow in 1H26, plus $46.6M of 1H26 transaction/nonrecurring expense described as primarily earn-out related). Up to $120M is payable in 2026 and $50M in 2027. The mirror image is real downside protection through 2026 at $65.07.

**Second-half volume risk.** Guidance implies ~324-332 MBoe/d against 338 in 1H26, on roughly flat capital, with a 29% PDP decline rate. A 2H26 miss would validate the concern that the acquired Permian base declines faster than the program replaces it.

**Governance and dilution.** No director election, no say-on-pay, corporate-opportunity renunciation, a Manager obligated to allocate only "at least 70%" of upstream opportunities to Crescent, related-party payables to KKR funds up from $1.2M to $25.1M, and $11.9M of 2025 plugging-and-abandonment funding for Chama, an equity-method investee in which the Chairman holds ~17.5%. Contingent Manager dilution of 6.0-8.7%; if achievement exceeds the 18,954,130 shares reserved, the excess settles **in cash**, converting a high-performance outcome into a claim on free cash flow.

**Working capital and borrowing base.** 0.94x current ratio against a covenant minimum (the covenant's treatment of revolver availability as a current asset is unverified), plus borrowing-base redetermination risk now across two facilities.

**Insider and holder flows.** PT Independence transferred 36,813,628 shares to Liberty Mutual Foundation on 12/16/2025 (code G); the Foundation then sold 32,600,000 at $12.33 on 5/7/2026, retaining 4,213,628. Director Marcus Rowland sold 40,000 at $13.25 on 5/6/2026. No open-market purchases by executives or directors appear in the 30 most recent Form 4 transactions.

**Recurring impairments** ($254.6M / $161.5M / $153.5M over three years) and Section 382 limits on the $695.3M deferred tax asset round out the list.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 67.1% against 50.4% one-year realized volatility (94th percentile within the chain), with the term structure in backwardation (67.1% at 16 days, 60.8% at 44, 52.0% at 135) and a 9.4% put skew at the one-month tenor. IV expanded 13.3 points (53.9% → 67.1%) between July 19 and August 4 while the stock rose 6.3%. Because earnings were already released on August 3, this is not earnings hedging — the market is paying up for near-dated protection on a high-beta, 14.8%-short crude proxy. The ±12.0% implied move on the nearest expiry is a reasonable estimate of near-term dispersion. Put/call open interest of 0.22 is call-heavy in aggregate, so the protection buying is recent and near-dated rather than structural. Practical read: this is a poor moment to pay up for exposure and a poor moment to sell downside; short interest of 14.8% with 6.5 days to cover means an upside oil surprise squeezes hard in both directions.

Risk Factors: **4.0**.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($19.00)

Normalized levered free cash flow of ~$980M against a $3,775M market cap is a 26% yield, generated at an EV of 1.15x proved-developed PV-10 with net leverage of 1.6x, an undrawn $2.0B revolver and no maturity before February 2029. Synergy capture at $190M of a $250-300M target implies $60-110M of incremental annualized EBITDAX still to land, against unchanged capital. 2027 hedging is thin — 3.3 MMBbl of swaps at $61.07 plus 3.65 MMBbl of counterparty swaptions at $75.00 — so if crude sustains a $90-plus level, 2027 realized prices flow through largely unimpeded, and consensus at $2.28 next year gives no credit for that. The minerals business converts 89% of EBITDAX to free cash flow at $4.26/Boe of operating expense and is scaling. Deleveraging in a 1.30x net-debt/market-cap structure is high-torque for equity: the $610M of 1H26 net debt reduction is roughly 16% of market cap. Resuming the $336M remaining authorization at $11 would retire ~8.9% of shares. Analyst high target $19.00; mean $15.93.

### Bear Case ($7.25)

WTI normalizes toward $65 in 2027 as OPEC+ spare capacity returns, arriving precisely when Crescent's protective 2026 swaps have rolled off. Waha basis stays punitive, leaving a third of volumes uneconomic and forcing gas curtailment or negative-margin production. 2H26 volumes come in at 324 MBoe/d on flat capital, confirming that the 29% PDP decline rate outruns the program. EBITDAX normalizes toward $2,200-2,300M; at 3.0-3.2x and net debt of ~$4,950M, equity is $1,600-2,400M, or $4.85-7.30 per share. On the way down, the Manager fee of $78.5M continues, the PSU expense continues, converts remain outstanding at a $14.89 strike, and the Series I veto plus the 3× termination fee prevent a takeover from establishing a floor. The 52-week low of $7.68 becomes the reference rather than the exception.

### Base Case ($13.25)

Crude holds in the $90s through 2026 without an outsized tail; Crescent generates $500-600M of 2H26 levered free cash flow, most of it applied to debt; 2H26 volumes land at 328-334 MBoe/d; synergies reach the low end of $250M. Normalized EBITDAX of $2,720-2,850M at 3.4x EV/EBITDAX with net debt at ~$4,350M gives equity of $4,900-5,350M, or $14.8-16.2 — but the multiple has not held above 3.2x for this structure, so I weight toward the conservative end. **Base case $13.25**: 3.3x on $2,800M of normalized EBITDAX less $4,900M of net debt, plus the deleveraging accrual, ÷ 330.25M shares. That is +16% from $11.43, against −37% to the bear case, for a reward/risk ratio below 0.5 at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold_rationale: uncertain). Existing holders have a covered 4.20% dividend, a repaired balance sheet and a 2027 hedge roll-off option. New capital should not pay $11.43 with base-case upside of 16% against bear-case downside of 37%.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November) is the decisive checkpoint, because it is the first quarter in which the second-half volume question resolves.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **2H26 production at or above 335 MBoe/d on capital at or below $756M**, refuting the implied decline and demonstrating that the acquired Permian base is being replaced at guided capital.
2. **Buyback resumption at scale** — deployment of at least $150M of the remaining $336M authorization below $12, which converts record free cash flow into per-share value rather than balance-sheet value.
3. **Price at $8.75-9.25** with fundamentals intact. At $9.00, market cap is $2,972M, EV is $7,873M, normalized EV/EBITDAX falls to 2.9x and the normalized free-cash-flow yield rises to 33%; base case upside becomes ~47% against bear-case downside of ~19%, a materially better than 2:1 setup.
4. **Working-interest gas realizations recovering above $1.50/Mcf pre-hedge**, evidencing Permian basis relief on roughly a third of volumes.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **2H26 production below 320 MBoe/d**, or 2027 guidance implying a decline on maintenance-level capital.
2. **Another large stock-funded acquisition** that expands the Manager PSU target by 7-10% of shares issued without disclosed synergies exceeding the combined fee-plus-dilution cost.
3. **Net leverage back above 2.0x**, or any revolver redraw to fund capital or the earn-out, which would signal that the free-cash-flow narrative depends on the price spike.
4. **A current-ratio covenant waiver or amendment**, given the 0.94x actual reading.
5. **Consensus current-year EPS cut below $2.00** (from $2.29), which would break the forward-multiple support that currently underpins the HOLD.

## Conclusion

The score moves from 4.5 to 4.85 because the August 3 evidence is better than what was available in July: record Adjusted EBITDAX and levered free cash flow, the revolver at zero, the 2029 notes retired, net leverage at 1.6x, opex and production-tax guidance cut, and a Permian synergy target tripled with two-thirds already captured.
