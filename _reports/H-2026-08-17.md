## Executive Summary

Hyatt has finished the transition it began a decade ago. As of June 30, 2026 the company operates 1,559 hotels and all-inclusive resorts with 377,886 rooms, of which only 22 hotels (7,928 rooms) and 6 all-inclusive resorts (1,262 rooms) are owned or leased. Owned all-inclusive rooms fell 84.3% year over year while managed all-inclusive rooms rose 9.9%, the mechanical result of the Playa acquisition and the subsequent ~$2.0bn sale of the 15-resort portfolio with management contracts retained on 13 of 14 properties sold to Tortuga Resorts. Management & Franchising now generates $530M of H1 2026 segment Adjusted EBITDA, 83% of the $636M pre-overhead segment total, up from 75.5% a year earlier.

That fee engine grew 11.7% in the first half. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA grew 2.8%, to $563M. The gap is two identifiable holes: the Distribution segment (ALG Vacations plus Mr & Mrs Smith) fell 39.1% at the EBITDA line on $499M of revenue (-13.4%) as Mexico security incidents and Jamaica weakness cut booking volumes, and Middle East/Africa comparable RevPAR fell 28.3% in Q2 with occupancy down 18.1 points. Management raised FY2026 system-wide RevPAR guidance to 3.5%-4.5% on July 30 while leaving the FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA outlook unchanged at $1,155M-$1,205M. That combination of better revenue guidance and unchanged profit guidance is the clearest single statement in the disclosure record about where the fee upside is going.

The valuation question turns on a data point that is easy to get wrong. Hyatt has 41,600,929 Class A and 52,635,807 Class B shares (10-Q cover page, July 24, 2026), Class B being 1:1 convertible and economically identical. Total equity value at $180.98 is therefore $17,055M, not the $7,573M that a Class-A-only count produces. Net debt of $3,675M ($4,281M total debt less $537M cash and $69M short-term investments) puts enterprise value at $20,730M ex-noncontrolling interests, or 17.6x the FY2026 EBITDA guidance midpoint of $1,180M. On next-year consensus EPS of $4.86 the shares trade at 37.2x. Neither multiple is distressed and neither is cheap. We rate the shares HOLD with a $197 base case, a fifth consecutive hold, and we are explicit below about why the incremental evidence this quarter argues for slightly lower scores on growth and financial health rather than an upgrade.

## Company Overview

Hyatt operates in three segments. **Management & Franchising** collects base management fees ($124M in Q2, +10.2%), incentive management fees ($64M, +2.6%), and franchise and other fees ($136M, +8.1%), for gross fees of $324M (+7.8%) in the quarter and $657M (+8.2%) in the half. Net fees were $307M in Q2 after $(17)M of contra revenue, which is the amortization of capitalized key money. **Owned & Leased** is now 28 properties and $50M of H1 EBITDA, down 20.0% on dispositions rather than operations (comparable owned & leased RevPAR rose 8.8% in Q2). **Distribution** is ALG Vacations and Mr & Mrs Smith.

The portfolio splits into 1,405 hotels/319,925 rooms (+5.2% rooms) and 154 all-inclusive resorts/57,961 rooms (-2.8% rooms), plus 1,242 Mr & Mrs Smith properties, 22 Hyatt Vacation Club properties and 44 branded residences. Franchised rooms grew 7.1% year over year against slower growth in managed hotel rooms. Total system rooms grew 3.9%. The executed pipeline was approximately 830 hotels and 148,000 rooms at December 31, 2025, roughly 46% of the current hotel room base, the highest pipeline-to-base ratio among the large branded platforms. An updated June 30, 2026 pipeline figure was not disclosed in the 10-Q.

International operations were about 30% of FY2025 revenue but roughly 55% of system-wide rooms, which tells you the non-US footprint is disproportionately fee-based. It also explains why regional geopolitics moves Hyatt's numbers more than it moves US-weighted peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings are noisy and the noise has a specific cause.** FY2025 revenue was $7,101M with Adjusted EBITDA of $1,159M (+5.8%), yet net income attributable to Hyatt was $(52)M. The bridge: interest expense nearly doubled to $317M from $180M on Playa-related borrowings, pre-tax income was only $81M after $105M of H1 transaction costs, and the company still recorded a $130M tax provision, producing a net loss from a positive pre-tax result. Effective tax rates were 78.1% in H1 2025 and 37.2% in H1 2026, reflecting global mix, $973M of non-deductible Playa goodwill, and open disputes. H1 2026 net income attributable was $148M against $17M, as transaction costs fell to $24M from $105M.

The supplied trailing EPS of $(0.55) is a GAAP figure inconsistent with the supplied P/E of 211.59; neither is usable. Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.14 on $110M of net income implies roughly 96M diluted shares, which independently corroborates the ~94M total share count rather than the 41.6M Class A count.

**Leverage is moderate and liquidity is ample.** Total debt of $4,281M carries a 5.3% weighted-average rate over a ~4-year weighted-average maturity across nine tranches laddered 2027-2035. The revolver is undrawn with $1,497M available; total liquidity is $2,103M. Net debt/FY2026E EBITDA is 3.11x and FY2025 EBITDA/interest coverage was 3.66x. Current maturities of long-term debt jumped to $605M from $6M at year-end, which is the $600M 5.750% notes crossing inside twelve months rather than incremental borrowing. Working capital is negative $(1,250)M, driven by $1,465M of current contract liabilities (loyalty and deferred-package obligations). For a franchisor collecting cash ahead of service delivery this is a structural feature of the model, and screening tools that penalize it (one third-party Altman Z of 0.99 appears to substitute book for market equity in the fourth term; recomputing with market equity gives roughly 1.85) misread the business.

**Cash conversion is improving but the improvement is smaller than headline EBITDA suggests.** H1 operating cash flow was $150M against $86M, with capex of $45M against $74M. That $105M half-year figure should not be annualized: it absorbed $(311)M of working-capital changes, was helped by a $194M increase in loyalty deferred revenue, and reflects Hyatt's seasonally weakest quarter. A cleaner sensitivity: H1 Adjusted EBITDA $563M less cash interest $117M, cash taxes $134M, capex $45M and key money payments $77M leaves $190M, versus $149M on the same construction a year earlier, a 28% improvement.

The key money line is the item that matters most for earnings quality. Hyatt paid $77M of key money in H1, up 38% from $56M. Capitalized key money assets rose to $1,160M from $1,095M at year-end. Key money is amortized as contra revenue (up 14.6%), so it depresses reported future fees while consuming cash today, and **Adjusted EBITDA does not deduct the cash outflow.** Alongside it: nonaccrual financing receivables rose to $172M from $142M in six months, and $126M of the $134M of "deferred fee arrangements" (fees Hyatt agreed to let owners pay later) is on nonaccrual, primarily more than 90 days past due, against a total allowance of only $51M on $550M of financing receivables. The separate receivables credit-loss allowance is $81M versus $62M at January 1, 2025.

The mechanism is straightforward: Hyatt is funding net rooms growth partly with balance sheet (key money, owner loans, deferred fees, and $171M of maximum performance-guarantee exposure, $119M recorded), none of which reduces Adjusted EBITDA. The counter-argument is legitimate: these sums are modest against $7.1bn of revenue and $2.1bn of liquidity, rising key money is industry-wide (the 10-K attributes contract terms to "terms offered by our competitors"), and six months of nonaccrual data could reflect a handful of properties in renovation or ownership transition. The filings do not disaggregate by owner. We treat it as a real but not yet quantified drag on the quality of the fee multiple, and it is the primary reason our financial health score sits at 5.6 rather than the 5.8 of the prior review.

## Growth Analysis

**Core RevPAR is strong with two acute regional holes.** Q2 comparable system-wide hotel RevPAR was $158.70, +5.9% in constant currency, on 73.2% occupancy (+0.6pt) and ADR of $216.81 (+5.0%). By region: Asia-Pacific ex-China +10.3%, Americas ex-US +9.5%, Greater China +7.2%, United States +6.7%, Europe +4.5%, Middle East & Africa -28.3%. Greater China at +7.2% runs against the weak-China read prevailing across the lodging group. MEA at -28.3% with occupancy down 18.1 points is direct transmission from the Middle East conflict into Hyatt's P&L; the H1 figure of -14.5% shows the deterioration concentrated in Q2, meaning the second-half comparison depends on whether regional transit and travel normalize.

**Forward indicators are constructive on the US.** Group booking pace for July-December 2026 at comparable full-service managed US hotels is +5.7% versus the same 2025 period. Management attributed part of the US strength to premium leisure, group travel and the FIFA World Cup (secondary reporting of call commentary, not a quantified filing disclosure). The World Cup is a 2026-only tailwind, which matters for how one reads 2027 US comparisons.

**Estimate revisions are mixed with negative near-term momentum.** Current-quarter consensus fell 15.3% in 30 days to $0.80 and next-quarter fell 6.2% to $0.99, while current-year consensus rose 0.6% in 30 days and 3.3% in 90 days to $3.60, and next-year fell 1.2% in 30 days but is up 2.9% over 90 days to $4.86. Read together: the annual anchors are roughly stable while the near-term quarters have been marked down, consistent with Distribution and MEA weakness landing in Q3 rather than a change in the full-year view. This is a modest downward drift at the quarterly level, not the +30% revision velocity that signals an underappreciated turn.

The earnings track record is three beats in four quarters, including a 20.6% Q2 beat ($1.12 versus $0.93) and a 187.6% Q4 2025 beat, against a 155.8% Q3 2025 miss. Wide dispersion around consensus is characteristic of a company whose reported EPS is swung by transaction costs, disposition gains and an erratic effective tax rate, and it argues for anchoring on EBITDA and fee growth rather than on quarterly EPS.

**The medium-term growth arithmetic.** Consensus next-year EPS of $4.86 against $3.60 this year implies 35% growth. Most of that is mechanical rather than operational: transaction costs falling toward zero (Q2 2026 $8M versus Q2 2025 $82M), a normalizing tax rate off 37-40%, and buyback-driven share reduction against a $1,531M remaining authorization equal to 9.0% of equity value. Underlying EBITDA growth is guided at roughly 2-4% on the headline comparison, though the comparison is imperfect: Hyatt narrowed its Adjusted EBITDA definition in 2026 to exclude its pro rata share of unconsolidated venture EBITDA and recast prior periods (H1 2025 recast to $547M), while the FY2025 reported $1,159M was computed on the old, broader basis. True underlying growth is therefore somewhat above the headline, but the recast full-year 2025 figure was not disclosed, so the gap cannot be quantified.

Growth score of 5.8 (from 6.2): the fee engine's 11-12% compounding is genuine and the pipeline supports it, but two segments are actively subtracting, the near-term quarters are being cut, and the raised RevPAR guidance did not translate into raised profit guidance.

## Competitive Landscape

Hyatt is the smallest of the four major US-listed global branded platforms by rooms, and the 10-K concedes the point twice: competitors are "larger than we are based on the number of properties or rooms" and have "significantly more members participating in their loyalty programs." The stated differentiation is luxury/upper-upscale and lifestyle brand density, World of Hyatt engagement, and category leadership in managed all-inclusive resorts following Apple Leisure Group (2021), Bahia Principe (2024) and Playa (2025).

Two structural observations cut against the simple asset-light bull case.

First, **brand growth has been almost entirely acquisitive**: ALG, Dream Hotel Group, Mr & Mrs Smith, Standard International, Bahia Principe, Playa in five years. Goodwill of $3,451M plus net intangibles of $2,121M equals $5,572M, 39.9% of $13,976M total assets and 69% more than the $3,306M of stockholders' equity. The 10-K flags that impairment triggers include "potential or actual terminations of underlying management and hotel services agreements," and H1 2026 already carried $26M of asset impairments tied largely to such terminations, against $14M a year earlier.

Second, **a rising fee share of EBITDA is not the same as diversification.** The mix shift establishes that earnings are less real-estate-intensive. It does not establish counterparty diversification, and the transaction that produced most of the shift concentrated 13 all-inclusive management contracts with a single new owner group, Tortuga Resorts. Hyatt does not disclose owner-level concentration. The retained contracts' fee rates, duration and any guarantees extended to Tortuga are not disclosed at a level permitting verification, so our estimate that Hyatt converted roughly $611M of net cash (purchase consideration $1,533M plus $1,078M term-loan repayment, less ~$2,000M of portfolio proceeds of which $200M was preferred equity, before up to $143M contingent and ~$79M of 2025 transaction costs) into brands plus 13 long-term contracts should be read as a favorable-looking trade whose quality is unverified.

**The cost of competing is rising, and it is visible in the financials rather than only in the risk factors.** Key money payments +38%, contra revenue +14.6%, capitalized key money $1,160M, and the 10-K's own statement that "future arrangements with these third parties may be less favorable to us, depending on the terms offered by our competitors." Franchising outgrowing management (+7.1% franchised rooms) improves capital intensity but weakens brand-standard control, which the 10-K also concedes.

**The AI distribution risk is unusually specific.** The FY2025 10-K names "large language models ('LLMs') like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and others" as potential alternative distribution channels and warns that answer-ranking "may also result in our properties receiving less attention." Hyatt is the most exposed of the large brands to direct-channel erosion because it has the smallest loyalty base. This is not yet measurable in the numbers, and we do not score it as a current impairment, but it is a live structural question for a company whose value is fee capture on demand it does not own.

Competitive position 5.8: trajectory positive on mix and pipeline, tempered by scale disadvantage, rising contract-acquisition costs, and unverified counterparty concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. IRS World of Hyatt loyalty-program case — $387M, live and unresolved.** The Seventh Circuit vacated and remanded the Tax Court decision on April 22, 2026, agreeing with certain Hyatt arguments. The IRS petitioned for panel rehearing and rehearing en banc on July 8, 2026. If ultimately resolved consistent with the Tax Court's prior opinion, Hyatt estimates a $387M payment for 2012-2026 including $71M of interest net of federal benefit — 2.3% of equity value and roughly 33% of one year's Adjusted EBITDA. Unrecognized tax benefits fell to $470M from $503M and the ETR-affecting portion to $171M from $244M during H1, which is consistent with an improved assessed position, though the filing does not attribute the change and statute lapses could equally explain it. Ancillary exposures: $40M of recorded Mexican tax liabilities, $9M Mexican VAT, $20M India service tax, and $102M of tax indemnifications to hotel purchasers including a $33M assessment received July 13, 2026.

**2. Owner credit and the cash cost of fee growth.** Covered above. The specific concern is that reported Adjusted EBITDA can overstate distributable cash when a franchisor is funding growth with key money, owner loans, deferred fees and performance guarantees. Aggregate guarantee liabilities carried at fair value are approximately $215M, with $750M of conditional lending/investment commitments, $163M of surety bonds, $114M of letters of credit and $150M of captive-insurance reserves.

**3. Geopolitical concentration.** With roughly 55% of rooms outside the US, Hyatt has the highest relative sensitivity of the major brands. Continued regional disruption is already running through the P&L, and it is the single item most likely to keep FY2026 EBITDA at the low end of guidance even with RevPAR guidance raised.

**4. Distribution segment.** ALG Vacations has external causes Hyatt cannot fix. Distribution/destination-management deferred revenue fell $179M from year-end to $464M, though part of that is seasonal conversion of H1 bookings into H2 travel and should not be read as a pure demand signal. Management has not addressed in any disclosure we can verify whether a tour operator belongs inside a company whose fee business compounds at 11-12%.

**5. Consumer transmission.** Hyatt's luxury and group skew has insulated it so far (+6.7% US RevPAR, +5.7% group pace), and premium leisure has been the most resilient segment industry-wide. The risk is a possible weakening of US discretionary travel in second-half 2027, not the next two quarters, and it is why we hold the bear case at $130 rather than something milder. [unverified — specific macro figures on mortgage rates, payroll trends, and jet fuel costs are not in the provided sources]

**6. Governance, float and insider behavior.** Thomas Pritzker resigned as Executive Chairman and left the board in February 2026 following disclosure of his association with Jeffrey Epstein; the transition is corroborated by an 8-K dated 2026-02-17 with Item 5.02 and by the Q2 10-Q identifying Mark Hoplamazian as Chairman, President and CEO. Control is unchanged: the Pritzker family holds 54.7% of economics and roughly 89.0% of votes under a voting agreement that expires only when more than 75% of fully diluted shares are held by non-family interests. A second, much smaller voting agreement (~4.0% of voting power) is described in the 10-K as expiring when Thomas Pritzker is no longer chairman; whether it terminated is unconfirmed and immaterial to control.

The disclosed sell-down schedule permits up to 14,964,952 family shares in the November 5, 2025 - November 4, 2026 window — 36% of the Class A float — with 8,434,635 shares additionally sellable under the May 2023 shelf. Observed activity is a small fraction of that ceiling (~132,000 shares), but Class B-to-Class A conversions jumped to 387,666 in H1 2026 from 19,001 in H1 2025, and a Certificate of Retirement filed July 29, 2026 retired 495,666 converted shares. Conversion is the mechanical precursor to sale; it is suggestive, not conclusive.

Insider activity across the 25 most recent Form 4 filings shows no open-market purchases. CEO Hoplamazian sold approximately 120,000 shares on June 18 and 22 at $195.96-$205.23 (~$23.9M), cutting his direct stake roughly 25% to 356,089 shares; Pritzker trusts sold ~120,000 Class B shares on June 2 at ~$187-189 (~$22.4M); four other officers and two directors sold at $170-188, including CFO Bottarini's 1,825 shares on August 3 at $170.37 and director McMillan taking his holding to zero. Whether these were under Rule 10b5-1 plans is unknown, which materially limits the signal, and multi-year vesting mechanically produces June sales at many companies. The observation we do weight: **the issuer bought approximately 840,249 shares in Q1 2026 (~$136M) at prices averaging well below $165, then bought zero in April and May and only $12M in June at $191.65.** The company and its insiders were both non-buyers above roughly $185. That is price discipline on the buyback and is a mild positive on capital allocation, with the caveats that blackout windows around the February resignation and May annual meeting, the $605M maturity moving current, and the $750M of conditional commitments all offer alternative explanations.

**7. Compensation design.** CEO FY2025 total compensation rose 61% to $26,699,762 in a year of $(52)M GAAP net income, with "compensation actually paid" of $36.2M and NEO average compensation of $5.36M. The three performance measures are Adjusted Compensation EBITDA, TSR, and Net Rooms Growth. Cumulative TSR of 218.56 versus a peer index of 207.15 supports the outcome on the metrics chosen. The gap is that none of the three is a per-share, GAAP, or return-on-capital measure. For a company that has spent five years buying brands with debt, the absence of any capital-efficiency or leverage-adjusted metric in the incentive architecture is the most consequential governance issue we identify, and it is the main reason the management quality score sits at 5.0 despite genuinely disciplined buyback execution.

**8. Central reservation system migration.** The 10-K warns of possible "delays or system interruptions in connection with the migration over the course of 2026." A CRS cutover failure at a hotel company is a revenue event.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.5% sits slightly below one-year realized volatility of 33.8% and in the 13th percentile of the current chain, having collapsed from 44.1% on July 23 through the July 30 print. The term structure is flat (31.5% near, 31.0% far), so the market prices uncertainty evenly across tenors with no identified near-term catalyst. Positioning is mixed and should not be over-read: the put/call volume ratio of 3.83 and +5.6% near-term put skew indicate active downside hedging into the three-day expiry, while the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.50 is call-tilted and skew turns negative at three and six months (-1.1%, -3.0%). The OI ratio fell from 1.06 to 0.50 since July 23, meaning standing protection was reduced through the print rather than added. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±2.7%; the three-month tenor prices ±13.6%.

Reported short interest of 27.8% of float on 4,543,005 shares and 5.7 days to cover requires care. Against 94.2M total shares that position is 4.8%; against the 41.6M Class A count it is 10.9%. The 27.8% figure implies a float denominator near 16.3M shares, reflecting how little of Class A is genuinely tradable after institutional and affiliate holdings. The practical read is that the absolute short position is moderate but the tradable float is thin, so squeeze mechanics on good news are real and downside conviction among shorts is less extreme than the headline percentage suggests.

Risk factors score 4.8, unchanged: the Seventh Circuit vacatur genuinely improved the largest discrete contingency, and that offset is balanced by newly visible MEA and Distribution deterioration plus rising owner nonaccruals.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($232, +28%)

Management & Franchising compounds at 11-12% on a pipeline equal to 46% of the existing hotel room base, the highest relative development runway among the majors, while Owned & Leased is now too small to matter. Two temporary drags reverse: Mexico security disruption normalizes and ALG Vacations recovers from a -39% EBITDA base, and MEA occupancy retraces from 50.5% toward the high-60s as regional transit stabilizes. On that path consolidated EBITDA reaches roughly $1,340M in FY2027 and the market applies the mid-to-high-teens multiple that fully asset-light lodging platforms command, at the upper end because Hyatt's fee growth rate is faster. Net leverage falls below 2.5x on retained cash, and the $1,531M remaining authorization (9.0% of equity value) is deployed at prices management has demonstrated it treats as attached to intrinsic value. Group pace at +5.7% and Greater China at +7.2% show the demand base is broader than the consensus lodging narrative assumes. Reported EPS ramps mechanically to $4.86 and beyond as transaction costs vanish and the effective tax rate normalizes off 37-40%.

### Bear Case ($130, -28%)

The 17.6x forward EV/EBITDA multiple already prices the asset-light conversion, and the cash cost of sustaining fee growth is rising faster than the fees. Key money payments +38%, contra revenue +14.6%, $1,160M of capitalized key money, $126M of deferred fees on nonaccrual, nonaccrual receivables +21% in six months, $171M of performance guarantees and $750M of conditional lending commitments together mean Adjusted EBITDA overstates distributable cash by a widening margin. The IRS pays out $387M. US RevPAR decelerates in 2027 against a World Cup comparison; MEA does not recover; ALG stays impaired. FY2027 EBITDA of $1,080M at a 14.5x multiple, plus wider net debt, puts the shares near the 52-week low. The float overhang is structural: up to 14.96M family shares are eligible for sale in the current window against a 41.6M Class A count, with the annual cap waivable by unanimous independent-director vote, and Class B-to-A conversions rose more than twentyfold in H1 2026.

### Which case the evidence supports

The bull case is directionally correct about the fee engine and the bear case is correct about the multiple. What decides the outcome is whether the two drags are cyclical or structural, and management's decision to raise RevPAR guidance while leaving EBITDA guidance unchanged is a statement that at least in 2026, the fee upside is being consumed. That is the reason we hold rather than accumulate at $180.98.

- **Bear case $130** — 14.5x on $1,080M with a $387M IRS outflow. **-28.2%.**

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**

## Conclusion

Hyatt is a better business than it was three years ago. The owned-real-estate rump is down to 28 properties, Management & Franchising is 83% of pre-overhead segment EBITDA and compounding at 11-12%, the pipeline is the largest relative to base among the major brands, and the Playa round-trip converted roughly $611M of net cash into brands plus 13 long-term all-inclusive management contracts. Leverage of 3.11x net with an undrawn $1.5bn revolver and $2.1bn of liquidity is comfortable, and the remaining $1,531M authorization equals 9.0% of equity value with a demonstrated record of buying below $165 and standing down above $190.

None of that is mispriced at $180.98. Correcting the widely mis-stated market capitalization to $17,055M puts the shares at 17.6x FY2026 guided EV/EBITDA and 37x next-year consensus EPS, inside the asset-light lodging band, on guidance that was left unchanged even as RevPAR guidance was raised. Against that, three specific items argue for patience rather than accumulation: the cash cost of fee growth is rising faster than the fees (key money +38%, $126M of deferred fees on nonaccrual, nonaccrual receivables +21% in six months), a $387M IRS contingency is favorably positioned but not resolved, and the Class A float carries a disclosed multi-year family sell-down ceiling equal to 36% of shares in the current window.

Overall score 5.4, a marginal step down from 5.5 in July, driven by the near-term estimate cuts and the maintained EBITDA guide rather than by the price decline from the June high; that decline modestly improved risk/reward and is reflected in the base-case upside. Fifth consecutive HOLD, at a base case of $197 against a $130 bear. Own it for the fee-growth path; wait for $150-158, Distribution stabilization, or MEA normalization before adding.
