**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Equifax is one of three U.S. consumer credit bureaus and the clear leader in income/employment verification through The Work Number, a proprietary database of ~209M active records fed by 4M+ employer payroll relationships at no cost to Equifax. That database anchors Workforce Solutions, the company's highest-margin segment (FY25 operating margin 44.2%, Q1 2026 45.3%) and roughly 42-43% of revenue. The investment debate concerns whether the Q1 2026 results — revenue +14% YoY, EPS +34% — represent a durable growth acceleration or a flattered print that fades into a softer 2026.

The evidence points to "flattered." Management guides 2026 U.S. mortgage origination activity "slightly below" 2025, and the Q1 USIS mortgage strength came largely from tri-merge royalty pricing pass-through (USIS mortgage margin fell ~90bps even as volumes rose) rather than durable volume. International growth carried a +7% FX tailwind that swung from a -2% headwind a year earlier and will not repeat. EPS was further helped by a buyback-reduced share count. Consensus has read the same signals: full-year EPS estimates have drifted down (-1.0% over 90 days for current year, -1.4% for next year), current-quarter down -2.6%. The stock fell ~7.7% on the print despite the headline beat and now sits at $165.79, down ~40% from its $275.91 high.

The capital-allocation pivot is the cleanest part of the bull case: after zero open-market buybacks in 2023-2024, the board authorized $3B in April 2025, executed $927M in 2025 and $260M in Q1 2026, and more than tripled the dividend in stages to $0.56/quarter, funded by a 22% jump in operating cash flow as cloud-transformation capex rolls off. Against that, Workforce Solutions — the entire margin engine — is the simultaneous target of an active CFPB data-accuracy investigation (three CIDs) and an antitrust class action filed May 2024. Falling estimates, an absent insider-buy signal, and a binary regulatory overhang on the crown jewel keep this a HOLD. The franchise quality and ~19x forward multiple are not cheap enough relative to decelerating estimates to warrant a BUY, and the FCF profile and moat are too durable for an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Equifax operates as a global data, analytics and technology company across 20+ countries, organized in three segments:

- **Workforce Solutions (~42-43% of revenue).** The Work Number income/employment verification database (~209M active records, 813M total) plus the lower-growth Employer Services unit (unemployment claims, I-9, ACA, tax credits). This is the most defensible asset: employers contribute payroll data for free, creating a data network competitors struggle to replicate at scale.
- **U.S. Information Solutions (~34-37% of revenue).** Traditional credit reporting, scores, mortgage tri-merge reports, fraud/identity and B2B/B2C consumer solutions. FY25 margin 22.9%.
- **International (~22-23% of revenue).** Latin America (including 2023 Boa Vista Serviços in Brazil), Europe (UK/Spain), Asia Pacific (Australia/NZ/India) and Canada. FY25 margin 12.9%, the lowest of the three.

Revenue is well diversified, with the largest single client at ~3% of revenue. The multi-year cloud migration is largely complete, which management positions as a durable cost and product-velocity advantage, with AI/agentic capabilities layered on a single data fabric.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 $5.27B → FY2024 $5.68B (+8%) → FY2025 $6.07B (+7%). Q1 2026 revenue was $1.65B, with all three segments accelerating: USIS +21% (Online Information +24%), Workforce Solutions +10% (Verification +14%), International +11% (+4% local currency, +7% FX). The headline growth rate is the highest in years, but the composition matters: a meaningful share came from mortgage royalty pricing pass-through and a non-repeating FX swing.

**Profitability.** FY25 operating margin 18.0% (down ~30bps YoY on higher royalty/data-share costs and software amortization); net income $660.3M (+9%); diluted EPS $5.32 (+10%). Q1 2026 operating margin expanded to 17.5% from 16.4%, and EPS rose 34% to $1.42 on operating leverage plus a lower diluted share count (120.8M vs 125.1M).

**Cash flow.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1.62B, up 22% YoY and well above net income, reflecting high-quality earnings. Capex is declining as the cloud transformation winds down ($481M FY25 vs $601M FY23), driving an FCF inflection (~$1.1B+ FY25 FCF). This is the structural support for the new capital-return regime.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt ~$5.3B at Q1 2026 against only $183M cash; net leverage ~3.0x EBITDA versus a 3.75x covenant max. Roughly 82% fixed-rate. The screening-tool Z-Score (223) and ROA (892%) are XBRL artifacts and should be disregarded; real total-debt/equity is ~1.15x ($5.3B / $4.6B equity). This is an investment-grade but not conservatively levered balance sheet. Commercial paper rose to $958.5M at Q1 2026 from $762M at YE25, and an 8-K filed 2026-04-24 (Items 1.01 + 2.03) indicates a new material financing obligation, most likely a senior-notes issuance to term out CP ahead of the 3.25% notes due June 2026. The exact coupon and interest-cost impact warrant confirmation.

**Capital allocation.** Share count is down from 124M to 119.3M, and the dividend more than tripled in stages ($0.39 → $0.50 → $0.56/quarter), signaling management confidence that the heavy-capex era is over.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters (Q1 2026 +8.3%, Q4 2025 +0.8%, Q3 2025 +4.5%, Q2 2025 +3.2%). The beat magnitude is modest and decelerating in some quarters, which argues for confidence in consensus levels but not for large upside surprises.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed. The prior reports already flagged Growth Prospects declining from 6.5 to 6.0; new data continues that direction.

The forward signal that carries the most weight is estimate revisions, and they are negative across every horizon: current quarter $2.20 (-1.0% 30d, -2.6% 90d), next quarter $2.27 (-1.2% 30d, -2.0% 90d), current year $8.60 (-1.0% 90d), next year $10.28 (-1.4% 90d). This is broad-based downward drift, not a single estimate moving. The strong-revision BUY signal that flags acceleration stocks (rising estimates >30% over 90 days plus accelerating revenue) is absent here; estimates are falling, which is the opposite condition.

The deceleration is structurally grounded, not just sentiment. Three of Q1's growth drivers are non-durable:

1. **Mortgage pricing, not volume.** USIS mortgage growth was royalty pass-through. Pricing-led growth is less repeatable than volume-led growth, and the mortgage mechanism is straightforward: higher long-end yields → higher mortgage rates → lower origination volume → lower tri-merge report demand.
2. **FX tailwind.** The +7% International FX benefit will not recur at that magnitude.
3. **Buyback-driven EPS.** Share-count reduction is real but is a return-of-capital lever, not organic growth.

What remains durable is Verification Services within Workforce Solutions (+14% in Q1), the genuine secular growth engine, as more verification use cases (employment, income, government benefits) attach to a database that compounds as more employers contribute. That keeps the growth score above the floor. The next-12-month picture is one of single-digit organic growth with falling consensus, not a re-acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $165.79 against TTM EPS of $5.32, the trailing P/E is ~31x; on the current-year consensus of $8.60 the forward P/E is ~19.3x, and on next-year $10.28 it is ~16.1x. The dividend yield is ~1.35%.

Peer medians in the Industrials Research & Consulting cohort sit at ~28.2x P/E (WWD 41.9x, LUV 28.2x, VLTO 21.2x, EXPD 25.5x, CHRW 36.2x). Equifax's forward multiple is below this median, which is the basis of the value-stock framing in Zacks' 2026-05-14 coverage. The cleaner comparison set is the information-services duopoly peers (Experian, TransUnion) and other recurring-data franchises, against which Equifax has historically commanded a premium that has compressed sharply with the ~40% drawdown.

The valuation is reasonable, not asymmetric. Two considerations cap the score. First, the forward multiple is computed off estimates that are themselves drifting down; a ~19x multiple on a $8.60 number that is sliding is less attractive than it appears, and if mortgage softens as guided, the realized number could land below consensus. Second, the proxy's own disclosure shows 5-year cumulative TSR of 116.7 versus a peer group at 169.3, a quantified admission of relative underperformance that argues against assuming a quick re-rating. The drawdown improves risk/reward versus the $275 high, but at ~19x forward on decelerating estimates with a binary regulatory overhang, the upside is not the kind of mispricing that supports a BUY. Analyst targets (low $195, median $224, high $265) imply meaningful upside, but those targets have lagged the stock down and embed the same growth-durability question the market is debating.

## Competitive Landscape

Equifax sits in a regulated U.S. credit-bureau oligopoly where it both competes with and licenses to Experian and TransUnion via tri-merge mortgage reports, creating pricing interdependence with shared exposure to regulatory changes in mortgage underwriting practices. Its differentiated edge over the duopoly is Workforce Solutions/The Work Number, where it is the clear U.S. leader in income/employment verification. The data network effect (more employer contributors → more records → more verification use cases → more revenue → more incentive to onboard employers) is the strongest moat in the portfolio.

Two caveats temper the competitive score. Management candidly describes the verification market as "highly competitive with low barriers to entry," citing free/low-cost consumer data and "obtain-verification-directly-from-employer" alternatives. The segment also faces an antitrust class action (filed May 2024) that directly challenges the franchise's pricing power. The completed cloud migration is a genuine product-velocity and cost advantage, but it is a catch-up to where the data-services industry is heading rather than a durable structural lead. The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on Verification share gains, offset by regulatory and legal pressure on the same asset, netting to a 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is heavier than for a typical data compounder, and several risks are specific and unpriced rather than sector truisms.

1. **Workforce Solutions regulatory/legal bullseye.** The 45%-margin engine is simultaneously the target of an active CFPB data-accuracy and dispute-handling investigation (three CIDs: July 2023, March 2024, Aug 2024) and the antitrust class action. An adverse outcome on either would strike the most valuable part of the company. This is the single most important risk to monitor and is binary in nature.
2. **Litigation accruals.** $30M accrued in Q4 2025 for four consolidated class actions on credit-file inquiry disputes (settlement in principle Jan 2026), plus ongoing FCRA litigation that management says has "increased substantially." A $15M CFPB settlement and a $13.5M UK FCA settlement (2017-breach-related) cleared in Q1 2025.
3. **Cybersecurity.** The 2017 breach remains a live reference point; Equifax operates under the 2019 Consumer Settlement with binding security commitments. Near-complete migration of data to third-party cloud hosting introduces new attack surface. A second material incident would be catastrophic to a trust-dependent franchise.
4. **Mortgage-cycle sensitivity.** Pricing-led mortgage growth is less durable than volume; management guides softer 2026 activity.
5. **Asia Pacific goodwill.** Management flagged the reporting unit's fair-value cushion as "greater than 10%" — relatively thin — and "at risk of a possible future impairment" at the Dec 2026 annual test. Non-cash but an earnings-optics risk.
6. **FX translation.** ~23% international revenue, generally unhedged; the +7% Q1 tailwind can reverse.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.1% against 1-year realized vol of 35.5% — rich by ~5.6pp, with IV up 4.6pp since early May even as the stock fell 2.9%, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next event window. The 1-month put/call skew is elevated at +11.0% (put demand), though this is concentrated in the near tenor and flattens to slightly negative at 3-6 months, consistent with hedging around a specific near-term catalyst rather than a structural bearish bet. The implied ±7.3% move on the nearest expiry is sizable. The P/C OI ratio fell from 1.08 to 0.55 over the past month, which softens the bearish read somewhat. The options market is signaling near-term event risk and modest downside hedging, which aligns with the falling-estimate, soft-guidance backdrop and supports a cautious posture rather than adding conviction in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$224-265, +35% to +60%).** The cloud-transformation capex cycle is over, FCF is inflecting (FY25 OCF +22%), and the capital-return pivot ($3B buyback, tripled dividend) is structurally shareholder-friendly. Verification Services compounds at low-double-digits independent of the mortgage cycle. The ~40% drawdown overcorrected a high-quality oligopolistic data franchise to ~16-19x forward EPS — below peers and well below its own history. As mortgage normalizes and CFPB/antitrust matters resolve without material damage, the multiple re-rates back toward 25-28x and estimates resume rising. Analyst median target $224 (~35% upside); high $265.

**Bear Case (~$130-140, -16% to -22%).** Q1 was flattered by mortgage royalty pricing, an FX swing, and buybacks — none repeatable. Management's own guide for softer 2026 mortgage activity drives consensus EPS below the current $8.60, and the stock de-rates further as the value-stock thesis fails. The CFPB accuracy probe or the antitrust class action produces an adverse outcome on Workforce Solutions pricing power, structurally impairing the 45%-margin engine. Asia Pacific goodwill impairs at the Dec 2026 test. At ~16x a cut estimate, the stock trades toward $130-140.

The base case sits between: single-digit organic growth, estimates stabilizing near current levels, regulatory matters grinding on without near-term resolution, and the multiple holding around 19-22x forward — a stock that compounds modestly with the dividend but lacks a catalyst to close the gap to consensus targets in the next 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality and FCF inflection are real, but the near-term growth picture is decelerating, estimates are falling, the crown-jewel segment carries a binary regulatory overhang, and there is no insider-buy signal to anchor a contrarian BUY. The drawdown improves risk/reward but not to the point of asymmetry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Consensus EPS estimates inflect upward (current-year estimate rising rather than falling over a 60-90 day window), confirming the deceleration thesis is wrong.
2. Favorable resolution or clear narrowing of the CFPB Workforce Solutions investigation and/or the antitrust class action, removing the binary overhang on the margin engine.
3. Verification Services organic growth re-accelerating to mid-teens with USIS mortgage volume (not pricing) turning positive, or a pullback toward $145-150 (~14-15x forward) that restores asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse CFPB or antitrust outcome that materially constrains Workforce Solutions pricing power.
2. A guidance cut to full-year revenue or margin, or current-year EPS estimate falling below ~$8.30.
3. Any cybersecurity incident, or covenant pressure if leverage rises toward 3.5x EBITDA.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report (Q2 2026, expected ~July 2026), which will test the durability of mortgage and FX-driven growth and reveal whether estimate drift stabilizes. Also monitor the Dec 2026 annual goodwill test for Asia Pacific.

## Conclusion

Equifax is a high-quality, moaty data franchise trading at a reasonable rather than cheap forward multiple, with a genuine FCF inflection and a shareholder-friendly capital-return pivot working in its favor. Those positives are offset by a decelerating organic growth profile that consensus is steadily marking down, a Q1 print whose strength leaned on non-repeating pricing and FX effects, and a binary regulatory/legal overhang on the segment that drives the company's margins. The ~40% drawdown has removed the most obvious overvaluation but has not created an asymmetric entry, and the absence of any open-market insider buying alongside falling estimates argues against fading the decline aggressively. The score holds at 5.6, consistent with the flat 6.1 → 5.6 trajectory, and the recommendation remains HOLD pending either an estimate inflection or resolution of the Workforce Solutions regulatory matters.
