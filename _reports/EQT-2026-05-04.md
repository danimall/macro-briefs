## Executive Summary

EQT Corporation is the largest natural gas producer in the United States, operating an integrated upstream-midstream platform across 2.3 million gross acres in the Appalachian Basin with 28 Tcfe of proved reserves. The July 2024 Equitrans Midstream merger has fundamentally reshaped the company's economics: third-party gathering expense dropped 75% (from $0.35/Mcfe to $0.08/Mcfe), FY2025 free cash flow reached approximately $2.5–2.9B (up from $573M), and the integrated model now provides stable midstream cash flows as a ballast against gas price volatility.

At $58.66, the stock trades at approximately 17.7x trailing EPS ($3.31) and approximately 12.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.85, which represents a modest discount to the E&P peer median P/E of 15.9x. The discount is partially warranted by EQT's pure-play natural gas exposure (vs. oil-weighted peers) and substantial debt load, but it undervalues the structural demand growth from LNG exports (approximately 6.5 MTPA contracted as of FY2025, with potential expansion to ~7.5 MTPA), data center power requirements (management estimates 2-3 Bcf/day from announced projects), and the MVP Boost/Southgate expansions that will deliver gas directly into supply-constrained Southeast markets by mid-2028.

The primary risk is commodity price sensitivity — EQT is 93% natural gas with declining hedge coverage into 2027. Current natural gas prices (~$3.40-3.50 Henry Hub) support strong economics, but a sustained return to $2.00 gas would severely impair FCF. The elevated geopolitical environment and energy supply disruptions provide near-term support for gas prices, though commodity prices remain volatile and beyond EQT's control. EQT's Appalachian focus makes it an indirect beneficiary of oil supply disruption (via gas-for-power substitution) rather than a direct one.

## Company Overview

EQT Corporation operates three segments — Upstream (production), Gathering (~2,945 miles of pipeline infrastructure), and Transmission (MVP Mainline and associated pipelines) — all concentrated in Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and Ohio. The company produced 2,382 Bcfe in FY2025 (~6.5 Bcf/day), making it the single largest natural gas producer in the country.

The business model centers on "combo-development" — synchronized multi-pad drilling that maximizes capital efficiency and reduces per-unit costs. EQT's LOE of $0.09/Mcfe is among the lowest in the industry, and vertical integration eliminates ~$580M annually in third-party gathering fees post-Equitrans merger.

Key strategic assets include:
- 4.3 Bcf/day of firm pipeline takeaway capacity
- 1.29 Bcf/day on the Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP Mainline), completed June 2024
- Approximately 6.5 MTPA in LNG offtake/tolling agreements as of FY2025 (4.5 MTPA offtake + up to 2.0 MTPA tolling), with some capacity contingent on underlying projects reaching final investment decisions. An additional 1 MTPA agreement was announced in April 2026, potentially bringing the total to ~7.5 MTPA.
- 30+ year drilling inventory (~4,000 gross undeveloped locations)

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** FY2025 total operating revenues reached $8.64B, up approximately 64% from $5.27B in FY2024, driven by higher realized prices ($3.19/Mcfe vs. $2.74/Mcfe), 7% volume growth from reduced curtailments and the Olympus Energy acquisition, and full-year consolidation of midstream revenues. Net income surged to $2.04B from $231M, and diluted EPS reached $3.31 vs. $0.45.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $5.13B (+81% YoY) and free cash flow of approximately $2.5–2.9B (depending on methodology; EQT reports consolidated FCF of $2.95B and FCF attributable to EQT of $2.50B) demonstrate the leverage inherent in a low-cost producer during favorable pricing. FCF yield on the current $37B market cap is approximately 6.8–8.0% depending on definition, which is attractive for a company still in active deleveraging mode.

**Balance Sheet:** As of Q1 2026, EQT has reduced total debt to approximately $6.0B (down from $7.8B at year-end 2025) and net debt to $5.7B, rapidly approaching management's $5.0B long-term debt target. Stockholders' equity of $23.8B at FY2025 year-end yields a debt/equity ratio of approximately 0.33x based on total debt. The company retired $1.4B of senior notes in 2025 and continues to prioritize deleveraging. Investment-grade ratings (BBB-/Baa3) are maintained with stable outlook. Current ratio of 0.76x indicates reliance on the revolving credit facility for short-term liquidity — normal for the industry but a constraint during severe downturns.

**Q1 2026 Results:** Revenue of $3.38B (+94% YoY from $1.74B) with adjusted EPS of $2.33, beating consensus of $2.10 by 11%. This marks four consecutive quarterly beats with an average surprise of +15%.

**Capital Allocation:** FY2026 capex guided at $2.65-2.85B (of which $580-640M is growth). Dividends of ~$390M ($0.66/share annualized, 1.1% yield) and the $2B buyback authorization are supplemented by the OBBBA tax deferral benefit that improves near-term FCF. The midstream JV obligation (~$355M/year to Class B unitholder) is a meaningful drain — approximately $3.41B remaining.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term headwinds:** Current quarter (Q2 2026) estimates have fallen 10.3% in 30 days and 20.2% in 90 days, reflecting seasonal gas price weakness and post-winter demand normalization. Next quarter estimates show similar pattern (-13.8% / -24.9%). These near-term revisions reflect the seasonal reality of natural gas pricing and are not indicative of structural deterioration.

**Full-year estimates rising:** FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.85 has risen 3.6% in 30 days and 17.4% in 90 days. This divergence between near-term and full-year revisions confirms the market expects a constructive second half driven by summer cooling demand, storage dynamics, and elevated baseline prices.

**Structural demand catalysts:**
1. **Data centers:** Management estimates 2-3 Bcf/day of demand growth from projects already announced or partnered, with potential for 8-10 Bcf/day in broader Appalachian demand from data centers and power generation. EQT's MVP pipeline delivers directly into Northern Virginia and Southeast markets where this demand concentrates.
2. **LNG exports:** As of FY2025, EQT has contracted approximately 6.5 MTPA (4.5 MTPA offtake + up to 2.0 MTPA tolling), with an additional 1 MTPA announced in April 2026. These are 20-year contracts commencing 2030-2031 that represent a substantial improvement in revenue quality and duration. However, EQT has disclosed that approximately 1.0 MTPA of offtake and the 2.0 MTPA tolling commitment depend on underlying export projects that have not yet reached final investment decisions and may be delayed, reduced, or not occur.
3. **MVP Boost/Southgate:** MVP Boost is a 600 MMcf/d expansion (upsized from 500 MMcf/d), expected mid-2028, pending FERC approval (application filed October 2025). MVP Southgate (31 miles, 0.55 Bcf/d) received FERC amended certificate approval in December 2025 and authorization to proceed with Virginia facilities construction in March 2026, with remaining regulatory approvals still needed. Both projects are expected in-service by mid-2028.
4. **Coal retirements:** Southeast utilities retiring coal plants creates baseload gas demand — structural and non-cyclical.

**Volume guidance:** FY2026 sales volumes guided at 2,275-2,375 Bcfe (slight decline from 2,382 Bcfe due to NEPA divestitures). The volume story inflects upward once MVP Boost comes online in 2028.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.66, EQT trades at approximately:
- 17.7x trailing EPS ($3.31)
- 12.1x FY2026E EPS ($4.85)
- ~9.5x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($5.9B)
- Approximately 6.8–8.0% FCF yield on FY2025 FCF (depending on definition used)

**Peer comparison:** The E&P peer median P/E is 15.9x. EQT's ~12x forward P/E represents a 24% discount. This discount is partially warranted: EQT is pure natural gas (lower and more volatile commodity value than oil) and has the midstream JV cash drain. However, peers like Expand Energy (EXE) at 7.6x P/E and Devon (DVN) at 12.2x are more directly comparable on a risk-adjusted basis. Against these gas-weighted/hybrid peers, EQT's valuation is in-line to slightly cheap given its superior scale, integration benefits, and growth pipeline. The balance sheet has improved materially: Q1 2026 total debt of $6.0B is rapidly approaching the $5.0B target, reducing leverage-related discount.

**PV-10 analysis:** SEC PV-10 of $25.6B (using 12-month average pricing) vs. $37.1B market cap implies the market prices in ~$11.5B of optionality above proved reserve value. This premium is justified by data center demand, LNG contracts, and MVP expansion — but requires these catalysts to materialize.

**Analyst consensus:** According to available data, the mean analyst target is approximately $68–70, with estimates ranging from the high-$40s to the mid-$70s. Approximately 69–76% of covering analysts rate Buy/Strong Buy.

The valuation is not deeply cheap on an absolute basis, but it offers reasonable asymmetry when incorporating the 2028+ demand inflection from MVP Boost and LNG contracts that are not yet flowing revenue.

## Competitive Landscape

EQT's competitive position is strengthening on multiple fronts:

**Cost advantage:** LOE of $0.09/Mcfe is the lowest among public Appalachian producers. Post-Equitrans integration, total unit costs have declined materially, creating breakeven economics that remain profitable even at sub-$2.50 Henry Hub gas.

**Scale:** At 6.5 Bcf/day, EQT produces 2-3x any individual Appalachian peer. This scale enables infrastructure optimization (combo-development across large contiguous acreage positions), negotiating leverage on services/transport, and the ability to commit to long-term LNG contracts that smaller producers cannot.

**Infrastructure moat:** Ownership of the MVP Mainline and associated gathering systems eliminates counterparty risk and captures midstream margin. Competitors (Range Resources, Antero, Coterra in Marcellus) must pay third-party transport fees that EQT avoids.

**Market access diversification:** 49% of volumes reach markets outside Appalachia (Gulf Coast, Midwest, East Coast, Northeast/Canadian markets), reducing basis discount exposure relative to peers trapped in local markets.

**Competitive threats:** Expand Energy (Chesapeake + Southwestern merger) creates a scaled gas-focused competitor, though concentrated in Haynesville/Marcellus rather than integrated. Range Resources and Antero compete in Appalachia but lack midstream integration. LNG export competition from Permian associated gas and Haynesville production is real but limited by takeaway capacity.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Natural Gas Price Exposure (HIGH):** EQT's earnings are highly leveraged to Henry Hub pricing. A $0.50/MMBtu decline in average realized prices reduces annual revenue by approximately $1.2B. Hedge coverage declines sharply: Q1 2026 at 2.5 Bcf/d hedged, declining to 1.2-1.4 Bcf/d in Q2-Q4, and only 0.1 Bcf/d in Q1 2027. If gas prices return to the $2.00-2.25 range seen in 2024, FCF would be severely impaired and deleveraging would stall. Basis risk (Appalachian discount to Henry Hub was -$0.48/Mcf in 2025) remains persistent.

**2. Regulatory/Construction Risk:** MVP Mainline cost ~$10B and faced years of legal challenges before completion (June 2024). MVP Boost (FERC application filed October 2025, pending approval) faces similar regulatory risk. MVP Southgate has made meaningful regulatory progress (FERC certificate amendment approved December 2025, Virginia construction authorization March 2026) but still requires remaining approvals. Environmental opposition, ESA challenges, and state/local permitting could delay or prevent projects.

**3. Debt Load:** While substantially reduced to $6.0B after Q1 2026 (from $7.8B at year-end 2025), debt remains elevated at lowest investment-grade ratings. Management states it is "quickly approaching" the $5.0B target, but a commodity downturn that impairs cash flow could still threaten ratings.

**4. Geographic Concentration:** 100% Appalachian Basin exposure creates vulnerability to regional events (regulatory changes in PA/WV, pipeline moratoriums, subsidence risk).

**5. Securities Class Action Litigation:** EQT's securities class action was settled for $167.5M, with court approval received November 4, 2025, and payment completed in Q3 2025 (partially offset by approximately $16M in insurance recoveries). While the primary settlement has been resolved, other litigation matters remain ongoing.

**6. Midstream JV Obligation:** ~$355M/year committed to Class B unitholder with $3.41B remaining — constrains shareholder returns for approximately 9-10 years.

## Conclusion

EQT Corporation represents a high-quality, structurally advantaged natural gas producer trading at a reasonable valuation with multiple identified catalysts for re-rating over the next 12-18 months. The Equitrans integration has delivered material cost savings, the data center/LNG demand thesis is beginning to crystallize, and the 4-for-4 earnings beat streak demonstrates operational execution.

The primary risk — commodity price sensitivity with declining hedge coverage — is genuine and binary. The current energy environment (geopolitical supply disruption, power demand growth, coal retirement) supports near-term natural gas prices above the $2.00-2.50 seen in 2023-2024, though commodity prices remain inherently volatile and unpredictable. At ~12x forward earnings with a ~7% FCF yield and mid-to-high-teens percentage upside to consensus, the risk/reward favors ownership for investors with a 12-18 month horizon and tolerance for commodity volatility.

Maintain BUY with 2-3% position sizing. The stock's 14% pullback from its March $68.24 high provides a reasonable entry point, though commodity-sensitive positions should be sized conservatively given the broader macro vol regime change.
