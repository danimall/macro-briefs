**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scotts Miracle-Gro is the largest marketer of branded consumer lawn-and-garden products in North America, anchored by Scotts Turf Builder, Miracle-Gro, Ortho, and Tomcat. GICS files it under "Materials / Fertilizers & Agricultural Chemicals," but the operating reality is a brand-driven consumer products company with shelf dominance at Home Depot and Lowe's. The investment story over the past two years has been a deleveraging turnaround: gross margin recovered from 18.5% in FY2023 to 30.6% in FY2025, the company returned to GAAP profitability ($145.2M net income, $2.47 diluted EPS), and net leverage fell from 4.10x at 9/30/25 to 3.71x by 3/28/26. The April 2026 divestiture of the loss-making Hawthorne cannabis-cultivation segment removes a multi-year drag and refocuses the company on a defensible core.

The bear case is grounded in balance-sheet fragility and earnings quality rather than operating weakness. Shareholders' equity is negative (−$286.5M at 3/28/26), the credit rating is sub-investment-grade (S&P B+, Moody's B1), and reported operating cash flow is materially flattered by a $750M receivables-sale facility ($654M derecognized) and a supplier-finance program; normalized free cash flow and net debt are worse than headline figures suggest. Customer concentration is extreme (Home Depot plus Lowe's = 52% of FY25 net sales), the Roundup marketing agreement that drives a meaningful slice of earnings is tied to Bayer's troubled glyphosate franchise, and a securities class action alleging inventory/sales misstatements survived a motion to dismiss in April 2026.

We rate SMG a HOLD on an income basis. The dividend yields roughly 3.6% and is covered. Operating momentum is genuine, but the near-term signals are mixed-to-soft: consensus current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 13.2% over 90 days, the analyst sentiment trend is degrading (one downgrade to hold within the last three months), and Stifel flagged slower May sales heading into the seasonally critical spring quarter. With the stock mid-range against its 52-week band and no identifiable near-term re-rating catalyst, the yield is the durable return. We would not chase the stock for appreciation here, and the materials-sector backdrop offers no thematic tailwind.

## Company Overview

SMG traces to 1868 and operates one streamlined U.S. Consumer segment representing roughly 88% of sales. Three revenue pillars sit within it. First, owned brands (Turf Builder, Miracle-Gro, Ortho, Tomcat) with shelf dominance and pricing power. Second, the Roundup marketing agreement, under which SMG is the exclusive U.S. marketing and distribution agent for Bayer/Monsanto's consumer Roundup, earning a commission of 50% of Program EBIT plus reimbursements; the net commission jumped to $53.6M in Q2 FY26 from $36.6M a year earlier, a meaningful H1 tailwind. Third, Bonnie Plants, a 50% live-plants joint venture with Alabama Farmers Cooperative.

The Hawthorne exit is the year's strategic pivot. The indoor/hydroponic segment, once a $1B+ growth engine tied to cannabis cultivation, collapsed with the cannabis oversupply glut to roughly $166M in FY25 and was divested April 8, 2026, reclassified as discontinued operations. The disposal structure is unusual: SMG transferred consideration (Vireo Growth shares, FLUENT shares) into entities labeled "Good Dog Holdings" and "Bad Dog Holdings," controlled by a strategic partner but consolidated by SMG as the primary beneficiary through promissory notes carrying call options to reacquire the exposure and counterparty put options. The effect is that SMG sold the cannabis exposure for accounting and regulatory purposes while retaining 10-year reacquisition optionality. The CEO's son, Christopher Hagedorn, was nominated to Vireo's board. This complex, related-party-adjacent arrangement warrants scrutiny.

The company is family-controlled. Hagedorn Partnership L.P. owns roughly 23% and effectively controls board elections; James Hagedorn is Chairman and CEO, with multiple Hagedorns inside the company.

## Financial Analysis

The margin recovery is the headline. Gross margin moved 18.5% (FY23) → 23.9% (FY24) → 30.6% (FY25), driven by lower material and freight costs, restructuring roll-off, favorable mix, and the Hawthorne exit. Revenue has drifted down over the period (FY22 $3,924M → FY25 $3,413M) as post-COVID gardening demand normalized, but the profitability turn is clear: net income swung from −$380.1M (FY23) and −$34.9M (FY24) to +$145.2M (FY25).

Q2 FY2026 (ended 3/28/26) confirmed momentum: continuing-ops net sales rose 5.0% to $1,459.5M, gross margin reached 41.8% (from 39.0%), continuing-ops EPS hit $4.46 (from $3.78), and U.S. Consumer Segment Profit grew 11.5%. Six-month continuing EPS was $3.65 versus $2.64. Deleveraging is working in parallel: interest coverage rose 4.78x → 5.62x, interest expense down roughly 15-19% YoY on lower balances and rates, with a weighted-average rate near 4.9% including swaps. The Seventh Amended & Restated Credit Agreement (11/21/2025) right-sized the facility to $2.0B and extended it to 2030, and the board authorized a $500M buyback in December 2025 (none executed as of 3/28/26).

Two structural cautions temper the picture. First, shareholders' equity is negative (−$357.5M at 9/30/25), the legacy of cumulative losses plus historical buybacks and dividends. This drives an Altman Z-Score of 1.31 in the "distress zone." The deficit is a capital-structure artifact rather than an operating-distress signal, since operations are cash-generative, but it does cap balance-sheet resilience and keeps the company reliant on a secured revolver, with $250M of 5.250% notes maturing December 2026. Second, reported operating cash flow is flattered by the up-to-$750M Master Receivables Purchase Agreement (facility expiring 9/1/26 with rollover risk) and a supplier-finance program. Cash on hand was thin at $6.2M, with seasonal working-capital builds debt-funded. These factors anchor the Financial Health score at 4.5: strong margins and a real deleveraging trajectory, offset by negative equity, sub-IG credit, off-balance-sheet financing tools, and a 2026 maturity to manage.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is margin-led earnings recovery on a flat-to-declining top line, not unit expansion. Revenue has been gently shrinking, and the durable drivers ahead are margin normalization (already substantially achieved) plus deleveraging that converts operating income into faster EPS growth. The Roundup commission has been a genuine H1 tailwind, but it is a function of Bayer's program economics rather than SMG's own demand generation.

The near-term forward signals are soft. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised down 13.2% over 90 days (and 2.0% in the last 30), the next quarter is off 11.5% over 90 days, and next-year EPS is down 3.7% over the same window. Current-year EPS is roughly flat (+1.1% over 90 days). The earnings revision trend is broadly negative, the opposite of the +30% inflection signal that would force a forward-multiple re-rating thesis. Stifel's flag of "slower May sales" matters because more than 75% of sales fall in fiscal Q2-Q3 (spring); a soft spring is hard to recover in a single fiscal year. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance on June 3, which limits downside surprise but does not provide an upside catalyst.

The earnings beat track record is constructive (3 beats in 4 quarters, including an 11.5% beat in the March 2026 quarter), so consensus has tended to be conservative. But falling estimates plus seasonal demand softness keep the growth setup balanced rather than accelerating. We score Growth Prospects 5.0.

## Valuation Assessment

SMG trades at roughly $68.40 (Phase 1 mark; the price history closes near $63.49), a market cap near $4.0B, on a forward P/E near 18.2x and a dividend yield around 3.6-4.1%. Against fertilizer-and-agricultural-chemical peers, the headline comparison flatters SMG: peer median P/E is 40.7x (CF at 9.6x, MOS at 148.8x, CTVA at 40.7x, FMC negative), so SMG looks cheaper than the median. But this peer set is heterogeneous and the wrong reference frame: SMG is a branded consumer products company, and the relevant comparables are consumer-staples names trading mid-to-high teens, where SMG sits roughly in line rather than at a discount.

The forward multiple is not stretched, but neither is there a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. Estimates are falling, not rising, so the cycle-adjusted-earnings argument for treating trailing multiples as misleadingly high does not apply: earnings are recovering but consensus is being trimmed. Analyst targets (low $63, mean $72.67, median $73.50, high $80) bracket the current price with modest upside to the mean, but the sentiment trend is degrading and one analyst recently moved to hold, which means treating consensus targets as floors is not warranted here. Cheapness on its own has been an unreliable buy signal in this regime; a low multiple without a rising-estimate or catalyst overlay tends to mark a value trap rather than an opportunity. The yield provides downside support but not asymmetric upside. We score Valuation 5.0: fairly priced, with the income yield as the floor and falling estimates as the cap.

## Competitive Landscape

SMG's competitive position is the strongest dimension and remains stable-to-improving. The company holds category leadership across lawn fertilizer/seed (Turf Builder), soils and plant food (Miracle-Gro), and pest/weed control (Ortho, Tomcat), backed by brand equity, shelf relationships, supply-chain scale, and innovation. The mature, consolidated retail channel (Home Depot/Lowe's dominance) is a double-edged feature: it is hard for challengers to replicate SMG's shelf presence, but it also concentrates SMG's fortunes in two customers.

The Hawthorne exit is a net positive for competitive focus, removing a costly misadventure into cannabis-cultivation adjacency that diluted management attention and capital. Volume re-acceleration across soils, grass seed, fertilizer, and controls in FY25-H1 FY26 suggests the core franchise is regaining footing after the post-COVID normalization. Primary competitors (Central Garden & Pet, Spectrum Brands, Kellogg Garden, Lebanon Seaboard, private label) have not materially eroded SMG's leadership. We hold Competitive Position at 6.5, a notch below the prior 7.0 read given the customer-concentration vulnerability and the absence of evidence that SMG is actively widening its share lead rather than defending it.

## Risk Assessment

Several company-specific risks are not fully washed out by valuation. Customer concentration is the largest: Home Depot plus Lowe's equal 52% of FY25 net sales and 25% of receivables, with no long-term contracts. Any destocking, shelf-space, or relationship change is highly material and could reverse quickly. Roundup dependence is the second: a substantial earnings source that Bayer can terminate if Program EBIT falls below $50M or on a Roundup sale/change of control (termination fee $175-375M), tying SMG to Bayer's ongoing glyphosate litigation and strategic review. Third, the live securities class action (S.D. Ohio, class period 5/5/21-8/1/23, alleging inventory/sales misstatements) survived a motion to dismiss in April 2026 and now proceeds, a financial and reputational tail risk directly tied to the write-downs that drove the FY22-23 losses. Fourth, the negative-equity/sub-IG balance sheet and the receivables-facility rollover (uncommitted, expiring 9/1/26) leave little cushion. Fifth, governance: ~23% Hagedorn family control alongside chronic five-year TSR underperformance (43.9 indexed versus peer 117.4) limits minority holders' ability to force change. Seasonality (>75% of sales in spring) compounds the near-term Stifel "slower May sales" flag. These factors anchor Risk Factors at 4.5: multiple specific, partially-priced catalysts rather than diffuse sector risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is roughly neutral and does not contradict the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV is 40.9% versus 34.1% one-year historical vol, modestly rich (6.8pp spread), but at the 8th percentile within its own chain, so the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. Put/call skew is balanced (1.1% at 1m), put/call volume ratio is light at 0.41, and the term structure is flat (40.9% near ≈ 40.0% far), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a single catalyst. IV has fallen 4.2pp since late May even as the stock rose ~11.9%, and the P/C OI ratio eased from 0.96 to 0.78, a modest reduction in defensive positioning. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.4%. Separately, short interest at 12.1% of float (5.2 days to cover) reflects elevated institutional skepticism, consistent with the balance-sheet and concentration concerns rather than an imminent squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The turnaround is real and underappreciated by the "distress-zone" Z-Score screen. Gross margin at decade highs, a clean Hawthorne exit, a fresh $2.0B credit facility extended to 2030, and a new $500M buyback authorization all point to recovery. The Roundup commission is surging, the dividend is covered, and the consumer-staples-like franchise is defensible. As leverage falls below 4.0x, unrestricted capital return resumes and EPS compounds faster than revenue. Re-rating toward the analyst mean ($72.67) and high ($80) is the bull path, implying roughly $73-80 if margin durability and buyback execution surprise positively.

**Bear case.** Negative book equity, sub-IG credit, and receivables-financing-flattered cash flow mean the balance sheet has little cushion if a weak spring or a Home Depot/Lowe's destocking event hits the 52%-concentrated revenue base. Estimates are falling, the securities litigation proceeds, the Roundup lever ties SMG to Bayer's troubled franchise, and family control with chronic TSR underperformance offers no governance pressure valve. A soft Q3, a receivables-facility renewal problem, or a Roundup disruption pulls the stock toward the low analyst target ($63) and potentially the low-to-mid $50s, near the 52-week low of $52.

Our base case is a fairly-valued stock supported by yield, with the margin/deleveraging story largely recognized and the near-term estimate trend soft. Base-case fair value sits around $66-70, roughly in line with the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income basis, with a 12-month reassessment cadence tied to the fiscal-year cycle (FY ends September 30) and the spring selling season.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage falling below 3.5x with buyback execution beginning, demonstrating that capital return has resumed at sensible prices.
- A reversal in the estimate revision trend (current-quarter and next-year consensus turning positive) alongside Q3 (spring) sales confirming demand has held despite the May softness.
- Clean renewal/extension of the receivables facility and refinancing of the December 2026 notes without straining liquidity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A meaningful Q3 FY26 sales miss confirming the "slower May sales" flag, indicating a lost spring season that the fiscal year cannot recover.
- An adverse development in the securities litigation, a receivables-facility non-renewal, or any disruption to the Roundup agreement (Program EBIT deterioration or Bayer strategic action).
- A dividend cut, which would remove the core reason to hold.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY26 earnings release (spring-season results) and the announced Investor Day, where capital-allocation priorities and FY26/FY27 framing will clarify the appreciation case.

## Conclusion

SMG is a genuine deleveraging turnaround in a defensible, mis-categorized consumer franchise, with decade-high margins, a clean Hawthorne exit, and a covered ~3.6% dividend. Those positives are largely recognized in an 18x forward multiple that sits in line with consumer-staples peers rather than at a discount. Against them stand negative book equity, sub-IG credit, cash flow flattered by off-balance-sheet receivables financing, 52% customer concentration, Roundup/Bayer dependence, live securities litigation, and family-control governance with chronic TSR underperformance. With consensus estimates falling, analyst sentiment degrading, and a seasonally soft May flagged into the critical spring quarter, the income yield is the durable return and there is no near-term catalyst to drive re-rating. We rate SMG HOLD (income), and would revisit on resumed buyback execution, a turn in the estimate trend, and confirmation that the spring season held.
