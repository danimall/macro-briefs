**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Banc of California is two-and-a-half years into the digestion of its November 2023 acquisition of PacWest Bancorp, a transaction that combined a smaller, healthier franchise with a much larger troubled one, backstopped by Warburg Pincus and Centerbridge capital. The operating turnaround is real and quantifiable: Q1 2026 pre-tax pre-provision income rose 28% YoY to $105.6M, net interest margin expanded to 3.24% (+16 bps YoY) as Fed cuts repriced deposits faster than assets, the efficiency ratio improved to 61.0% from 66.35% a year earlier, and management is returning capital through a raised dividend, an active buyback, and the simplification of the post-merger capital structure (full NVCE conversion, Warburg buy-down). On the operating-leverage line, this looks like competent execution of a normalization plan.

The complication is credit. Beneath contained headline metrics, the first quarter showed a clear adverse shift: special-mention loans jumped $230M QoQ to $688.7M (1.83% to 2.78% of loans), accruing loans 30-89 days past due surged from $93M to $235M, and both moves concentrated in residential construction and land. Nonaccruals rose to $185.7M on a single $28.8M venture-capital credit, allowance-to-nonaccrual coverage thinned to 149% from 176%, and the quarterly provision ($9.8M) ran below net charge-offs ($13.8M), shrinking the absolute allowance. Management characterizes the migrations as isolated and well-collateralized, but a balance sheet that is genuinely de-risking does not usually see criticized loans climb $270M in a single quarter. The Chief Credit Officer's open-market selling in late April and May, against this backdrop, is a legitimate flag.

At $20.74, BANC trades at roughly 1.05x book and 1.17x tangible book, with a forward P/E near 16.5x on a current-year consensus of $1.69 that has drifted slightly lower over 90 days. The valuation is undemanding relative to the franchise quality and the embedded NIM upside, but it is not cheap enough to ignore the credit risk, and the catalyst that resolves the central uncertainty — Q2 credit data — is one quarter away. We rate the stock HOLD, recognizing that the next two earnings prints will determine whether this is a value re-rating toward $24+ or a credit cycle that forces provisioning to step up and compress EPS.

## Company Overview

Banc of California, Inc. is the holding company for Banc of California, a California state-chartered, Fed-member commercial bank with approximately $34.7B in assets as of March 31, 2026, operating 79 full-service branches concentrated in Southern California plus regional offices in Denver and Durham, North Carolina. The current entity is the product of the 2023 merger of equals in which legacy Banc of California acquired the larger PacWest Bancorp. Management runs the company as a single Commercial Banking segment and positions it as a relationship-based business bank serving small, middle-market, and venture-backed companies.

The franchise's differentiated niches include venture banking, NDFI and warehouse lending, treasury management, the BancEdge payments platform, and SmartStreet, the HOA/community-association banking business that generates a large pool of low-cost, earnings-credit-rate-bearing deposits. The deposit base is a genuine asset: noninterest-bearing deposits are 29% of total, a level most regional peers would envy and a meaningful source of NIM resilience.

The investment story is a post-merger normalization: remix the balance sheet toward higher-yielding commercial production, run off lower-yielding fixed-rate assets, expand margin as deposit costs fall, drive the efficiency ratio lower, and return capital while the structure simplifies. The first phase of that plan is delivering. The open question is whether the credit book — heavy in California real estate and venture lending — cooperates.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings momentum is positive and broad.** Q1 2026 net income to common was $62.0M, diluted EPS $0.39, up 50% from $0.26 a year earlier (Q4'25 was a stronger $0.42). Total revenue of $286.9M grew 8% YoY. The cleanest read on operating leverage is pre-tax pre-provision income, which strips out provisioning and tax noise and shows the core engine improving.

**Margin is the driver.** NIM rose 16 bps YoY and 4 bps QoQ, driven almost entirely by falling deposit costs — cost of total deposits fell to 1.78% from 2.12% a year earlier as Fed cuts repriced the liability side faster than assets. New loan production is yielding 6.65% against a portfolio yield near 5.74%, which embeds further upside as the book turns over. Returns are recovering but still mid-pack: ROAA 0.86%, ROAE 8.22%, ROATCE 9.91%. The efficiency ratio reflects genuine cost discipline post-merger.

**The balance sheet is sound.** Loans held for investment of $24.8B declined modestly QoQ as $2.28B of payoffs and paydowns outran $2.12B of production. Deposits of $27.3B fell $521M QoQ. Capital is robust: consolidated CET1 10.18%, Total capital 16.55%, with Bank-level CET1 at 13.50% — comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. Book value is $19.80/share and tangible book $17.77/share, both up roughly 9-10% YoY.

**Two caveats deserve attention.** First, $143.3M of after-tax AFS unrealized losses sit in AOCI, with another $127.2M of HTM unrealized losses off the balance sheet, a mark-to-market overhang common to the sector but real if the bank ever needs to liquidate securities. Second, a $656.8M net deferred tax asset (of which $315.7M is disallowed from regulatory capital) depends on sustained profitability for full realization. Neither is a solvency concern, but both temper the quality of the reported capital position.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and informative: 3 beats and 1 large miss over four quarters. The Q2 2025 miss (-55.2%, $0.12 actual vs $0.27 estimate) was a merger-integration distortion; the subsequent three quarters beat by 2-16%, with the most recent (Q1'26) a narrow 2% beat. The trajectory is toward in-line delivery as the merger noise fades, which argues for taking current consensus at roughly face value rather than building in a beat cushion.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is normalization-driven, not organic balance-sheet expansion. Loans are flat-to-down as the company runs off legacy PacWest assets and the higher-cost funding that came with them. The earnings growth comes from three sources: NIM expansion (deposit-cost tailwind plus production-yield remix), efficiency gains (operating leverage as merged-cost synergies mature), and the mechanical lift from a shrinking share count via buybacks.

Consensus reflects this maturing-turnaround arithmetic. Current-year EPS is pegged at $1.69, rising to $2.05 next year — implying roughly 21% EPS growth driven largely by continued margin expansion and capital return rather than asset growth. Estimate revisions are stable-to-slightly-negative: current-quarter estimates are down 0.2% over 30 days and 3.1% over 90 days, with full-year estimates down 1.4% over 90 days. This is the opposite of an accelerating-revision signal. The growth thesis is intact but losing momentum at the margin, which is consistent with a turnaround that has captured the easy gains.

Two operational items support the forward picture. The $385M redemption of legacy PacWest 3.25% sub notes on May 1, 2026, pre-empted a floating-rate reset to SOFR+252, removing a funding-cost headwind. The pending sale of the $1.35B SFR mortgage-servicing-rights portfolio (settling late May 2026) funds that redemption and continues the de-risking remix. Both are sensible liability-management moves that protect, rather than expand, the earnings base.

The growth durability question is ultimately a credit question. If provisioning stays near current run-rates, the NIM-and-efficiency story carries EPS toward the $2.05 next-year number. If construction/land migration forces provisions materially higher, the growth thesis stalls because incremental PPNR gets absorbed by credit costs. We score growth prospects a 6.0 — credible and positive, but maturing and conditional on credit.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.74, BANC trades at 16.5x trailing/forward EPS of $1.11, 1.05x book ($19.80), and 1.17x tangible book ($17.77). Against the regional-bank peer set provided (BOH 15.9x, CBU 15.9x, PRK 16.3x, FFBC 11.2x; peer median 15.9x), BANC sits modestly above the median P/E. The forward P/E on the $1.69 current-year consensus is 12.3x and on the $2.05 next-year number is 10.1x — those forward figures look cheap, but they bake in the NIM-expansion and capital-return story that is the entire bull case.

The honest read is that the valuation is fair, not asymmetric. The case for a higher multiple rests on tangible book accreting 9-10% annually, ROATCE approaching 10% and rising, and a clean low-cost deposit franchise that should support a premium to peers as the turnaround completes. The case against rests on the credit deterioration: at 1.17x tangible book with criticized loans rising $270M in a quarter and coverage thinning, the market is right to demand a discount until the construction/land migration resolves. A bank trading slightly above peer median P/E does not offer a margin of safety against a credit-cost step-up.

A low or moderate multiple is not by itself a buy signal, and "cheap" regional-bank screens have repeatedly sorted the wrong way when credit was the swing variable. BANC is the textbook case: the forward multiple looks attractive precisely because the market is pricing some probability that consensus estimates are too high if provisioning rises. The analyst price target band of $20.00 low to $24.00 high (mean $22.68, median $23.00) puts the stock near the bottom of the range, which on a stock with rising estimates would argue the targets are stale floors; but estimates here are drifting down, so the targets are better read as a fair-value band than as a floor. We score valuation 5.5: undemanding but not mispriced, with the discount appropriately reflecting credit uncertainty rather than a buying opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Banc of California competes against larger regional and money-center banks with greater scale and higher lending limits, alongside credit unions and non-bank/fintech lenders. Its competitive edge is relationship banking in attractive California commercial niches and a strong low-cost deposit franchise (29% noninterest-bearing), which gives it a structural funding advantage that becomes more valuable as rates normalize. The SmartStreet HOA franchise and venture-banking relationships are genuine differentiators that larger generalist banks struggle to replicate.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The NIM expansion and efficiency gains indicate the merged franchise is becoming more competitive on profitability, and the structure simplification (NVCE wind-down, Warburg buy-down) removes overhangs that distinguished it negatively from peers. The deposit franchise is the durable asset.

The offsetting factor is concentration. Sixty-three percent of loans are real estate with 70% of real-estate collateral in California, plus a $5.1B NDFI book (20.4% of loans) and a $2.3B venture-lending book (9%). These are areas of heightened regulatory scrutiny, and they are precisely where competitors with more diversified footprints face less idiosyncratic risk. The franchise is not losing share, but its competitive position is tethered to a credit profile that is concentrated by geography and by borrower type. We score competitive position 6.0, holding roughly at the prior level — the deposit franchise and turnaround execution support a stable-to-improving read, but concentration caps the upside.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration is the central, specific, and timely risk.** The headline metrics are still contained — nonaccruals at 0.75% of loans, net charge-offs of 0.23% annualized — but the direction reversed sharply in Q1'26:

- Special-mention loans jumped $230M QoQ to $688.7M (1.83% → 2.78% of loans), driven by +$352M in residential construction and land.
- Accruing loans 30-89 days past due surged from $93M to $235M (+$142M), again concentrated in residential construction/land (+$114M).
- Nonaccruals rose to $185.7M on a single $28.8M venture-capital loan.
- Allowance-to-nonaccrual coverage thinned to 148.9% from 176.25%, and the Q1 provision of $9.8M ran below net charge-offs of $13.8M, shrinking the absolute allowance.
- Criticized loans (classified + special mention) climbed to $1.53B from $1.26B.

This is the falsifiable swing variable. If Q2 shows the construction/land migration stabilizing or curing, the bull case is intact and the stock should re-rate. If it spreads, provisioning steps up, EPS compresses, and the forward multiple that looks cheap today proves to have been pricing real deterioration.

**Concentration risk** compounds the credit watch: 70% California real-estate collateral exposes the book to CA-specific economic, earthquake, and wildfire risk, and the venture/NDFI exposure carries structurally higher loss-given-stress profiles. **DTA realization** ($656.8M net) and **holding-company reliance on Bank dividends** (FRB approval required given the $180M accumulated deficit) are secondary risks that matter only if earnings deteriorate materially. Roughly 39% of loans are adjustable-rate, and earnings are modestly liability-sensitive via ECR costs on ~$3.8B of HOA deposits.

Capital is not the risk. With Bank-level CET1 of 13.5% and $14.2B of available liquidity against $7.8B of uninsured deposits, any generic distress screen is misleading for this institution. The genuine exposure is to the earnings line through credit costs, not to solvency.

**Insider signal is mildly negative.** There has been no open-market buying. The Chief Credit Officer made open-market sales of roughly 31,000 shares (14,988 at $19.06 and ~20,800 at $18.52) in late April and May 2026. Given that credit is the precise swing factor for the thesis, the credit officer trimming — even if routine diversification — is worth flagging rather than dismissing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution without panic. Near-term ATM IV of 32.0% sits fair-to-slightly-rich against 1-year historical volatility of 29.6%, and the term structure is in backwardation (32.0% near-term vs 27.1% at six months), indicating the market expects elevated volatility around the next earnings print and then normalization. Put/call skew is balanced (2.3% near-term), and put/call volume and OI ratios are negligible, so there is no concentrated bearish positioning. The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 4.09 a month ago to roughly zero, and ATM IV fell 5.3 points while the price rose 8.6%, consistent with hedges being lifted into the rally rather than fresh downside bets. The implied move into the July expiry is ±7.0%. The market is pricing an imminent, contained binary around Q2 earnings and credit, which aligns with our view that the next print is the decisive catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Deposit costs continue to fall as the Fed eases, NIM expands toward the mid-3.30s, the production-yield remix lifts the asset side, and the efficiency ratio grinds below 60%. The Q1 credit migration proves isolated — construction/land credits cure or resolve on collateral, the single venture nonaccrual is worked out, and provisioning normalizes. PPNR growth flows to the bottom line, EPS tracks toward the $2.05 next-year consensus, tangible book compounds at ~10%, and buybacks at roughly 1x book accrete value. The stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range, ~$24 (+16% from current).

### Bear Case
The residential construction/land migration is the leading edge of a CA real-estate credit cycle. Special-mention and early-delinquency loans roll into nonaccrual, charge-offs accelerate, and provisioning steps up sharply to rebuild the thinning allowance. The venture book takes additional hits as VC funding tightens. EPS estimates, already drifting down, get cut harder, and the multiple compresses on a regional bank with 70% CA real-estate concentration. The stock falls toward tangible book or below, ~$17-18 (-13% to -18%).

The asymmetry is roughly balanced, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The franchise quality and turnaround execution are real, the credit deterioration is real, and the data that resolves it arrives in one quarter.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The operating turnaround and the credit deterioration roughly offset at the current 1.05x book / 1.17x tangible book valuation. We do not have sufficient evidence to make a confident directional call ahead of the Q2 credit print.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July). This is the decisive data point — it will show whether the construction/land migration stabilized or spread.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2'26 special-mention and 30-89 day delinquency balances stabilize or decline from the Q1 peaks ($688.7M and $235.1M), confirming the migration was isolated.
- Allowance-to-nonaccrual coverage rebuilds toward 170%+ with provisioning that at least matches charge-offs.
- NIM continues expanding (toward 3.30%+) with the deposit-cost tailwind intact, and full-year estimate revisions turn positive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2'26 criticized loans rise further (above $1.6B) with construction/land migration spreading into nonaccrual and a provision materially above charge-offs.
- A guidance cut or a 90-day estimate-revision decline exceeding 5%.
- Additional clustered insider selling, or any deferral of the buyback to preserve capital against credit losses.

## Conclusion

Banc of California presents a competently executed post-merger normalization — 28% PPNR growth, NIM expansion on a structurally strong deposit franchise, efficiency gains, and disciplined capital return and structure simplification. Those positives are genuine and visible in the numbers. They are offset by a clear adverse credit shift in Q1'26 that the headline metrics partially mask: $270M of criticized-loan growth and $142M of new early-stage delinquencies concentrated in California residential construction and land, with allowance coverage thinning and provisioning running below charge-offs. The Chief Credit Officer's open-market selling into this migration is a fair flag.

At 1.05x book and a forward P/E around 12x on consensus that is drifting slightly lower, the valuation is undemanding but appropriately discounted for the credit uncertainty rather than offering asymmetric upside. The score trajectory has improved (6.0 → 6.6 over the prior two reports), but the Q1 credit data is the first piece of evidence that meaningfully challenges the improving trend, and we hold the overall score at 5.85 to reflect that the turnaround thesis and the credit risk are now roughly balanced. The right posture is to wait one quarter: the Q2 credit print resolves the central question, and the options market is pricing exactly that contained binary. We rate BANC HOLD.
