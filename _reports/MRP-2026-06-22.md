**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Millrose Properties is a first-of-its-kind, publicly traded land-banking REIT spun off from Lennar in February 2025. Despite its "Single-Family Residential REIT" data-feed classification, it owns no rental homes. It buys entitled residential land, funds horizontal development, and grants homebuilders options to repurchase finished homesites on fixed takedown schedules, earning recurring monthly option fees as its core revenue. As of year-end 2025 it held 142,139 homesites across 933 communities in 30 states, with roughly $8.9B of homesites under option and $16.1B of estimated future takedown proceeds, a long-duration forward revenue indicator. FY2025, its first full operating year, produced $600.5M of revenue, an 81% operating margin, GAAP EPS of $2.44, and AFFO of $2.58/share that fully covers an escalating dividend now running near $3.00 annualized (~10.5% yield).

The investment case rests on three legs: a high, currently-covered yield; a low-leverage, recyclable-capital model that management describes as "perpetual"; and a recent cluster of credible insider buying. CEO Darren Richman purchased 235,000 shares (~$6.4M) in the open market on May 11, 2026 at roughly $27.31, with the COO, General Counsel, and a director also buying. This is the strongest type of insider signal and directly addresses the external-management alignment critique. The counterweight is structural risk: Lennar generated 88% of FY2025 option fee revenue, and although Lennar exited its ~20% equity stake in November 2025, it retained extraordinary "Founder's Rights" plus the Miller Family's ~43% voting control via Class B shares. Lennar can materially influence Millrose without share-price exposure.

We rate MRP a HOLD on an income basis. The ~10.5% yield is the durable return, fully covered by AFFO, and the insider buying lends credibility to the floor near $27. But the model has not been stress-tested through a genuine housing break, the concentration and governance structure cap the achievable multiple, and management has confirmed the equity is effectively gated from accretive issuance at current prices, limiting near-term growth and removing the most plausible re-rating catalyst. We hold for yield, not appreciation.

## Company Overview

Millrose operates a "homesite option platform." It acquires and holds entitled residential land, funds development up to predetermined budgets, and grants builders options to buy back finished homesites at pre-negotiated prices on set takedown schedules. Builders pay a non-refundable option deposit, recurring monthly option fees, and the takedown price when they exercise. Builders perform all development and bear cost overruns through completion and cost guarantees; Millrose funds but does not build. Capital recycles continuously as takedown proceeds fund new acquisitions, distinguishing it from traditional private land banks that return capital to investors after a fund life.

Approximately 96% of the portfolio is "pooled" under Multiparty Cross Agreements that create cross-termination penalties, raising the cost to a builder of walking away from a weak community. The portfolio carries a weighted-average yield of 9.2% (8.5% on the Lennar Master Program Agreement, 11.0% on non-Lennar agreements), a weighted-average remaining life around 3.0 years, and weighted-average maturity near 57 months.

Millrose has zero employees. It is managed entirely by Kennedy Lewis Land and Residential Advisors (KL), an affiliate of Kennedy Lewis (>$33B AUM), which is paid 1.25% of Tangible Assets per annum and covers most operating expenses. The fee is levied on assets regardless of per-share performance.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 is the first operating year, so pre-spin "Predecessor" periods reflect only allocated Lennar SG&A and are not comparable. For the year: operating income of $486.1M, net income of $379.9M ($404.8M attributable to common after pre-spin adjustments), and revenue split between $571.0M of option fees and $29.5M of development loan income. AFFO is the relevant earnings anchor; headline operating cash flow of $3.67B is dominated by gross takedown proceeds (capital recycling) against $5.7B of investing outflows for acquisitions, and overstates distributable earnings.

The balance sheet is conservatively capitalized for the asset-heavy model. Total assets were $9.26B against equity of $5.86B (63% of assets) and long-term debt of $2.11B, for a D/E of 0.58 and debt-to-capital under 30%, inside both the self-imposed 33% ceiling and Lennar's mandated 1:1 D/E consent limit. Cash was thin at $35M, but the company holds roughly $1.2–1.5B of revolver/term availability. During 2025 it termed out bridge financing with $1.25B of 6.375% notes due 2030 and $750M of 6.25% notes due 2032, repaying a $1.0B delayed-draw facility and revolver draws. In March 2026 it added a $500M delayed-draw term loan, bringing unsecured capacity to roughly $1.8B.

Q1 2026 confirmed the run-rate: net income of $122.9M, AFFO of $125.9M ($0.76/share), invested capital up to ~$8.7B with 31% now non-Lennar, expansion to 17 builder counterparties, ~$1.5B liquidity, debt-to-capital under 30%, and no option terminations. The Q1 AFFO of $0.76 fully covered the $0.75 quarterly dividend. Altman Z of 2.15 sits in the grey zone but is mechanically depressed by the low-turnover, asset-heavy structure rather than signaling distress; Piotroski scores 4/4 on available signals.

The financial health picture is solid but not pristine: strong margins and low leverage, offset by minimal cash, debt-funded growth at high-6% coupons in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and AFFO coverage that is adequate rather than abundant. ROE of 6.8% and ROA of 4.3% are modest, consistent with a low-leverage first year. We score financial health 6.8.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two engines and one constraint. The first engine is portfolio expansion through new builder relationships (15 counterparties at year-end 2025, 17 by Q1 2026) and M&A (the ~$859M Rausch acquisition of ~25,000 homesites and $494.5M funding for New Home Co.'s Landsea acquisition). The second is the yield-mix tailwind: as diversification proceeds (31% of capital outside Lennar and rising), blended portfolio yield should drift upward toward the 11.0% non-Lennar rate. The 57-month weighted maturity provides multi-year revenue visibility, supported by builders making 3–5 year land commitments for 2028–29 communities.

The constraint is capital. Management has explicitly stated it is funding growth with debt "until the equity markets become accommodating," confirming the stock trades below the level at which they would issue accretively. With debt-to-capital currently under 30% but with the 33% ceiling in view, the runway for debt-funded growth is finite, and accretive equity issuance to compound the platform is gated by the share price. Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year EPS consensus moved -0.6% over 90 days and next-year +3.2%, with no inflection signal. This is a steady compounder constrained by its own cost of capital. We score growth prospects 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

MRP trades at roughly 10.2x trailing earnings and ~9.4x on AFFO (against ~$3.04 annualized Q1 run-rate). On headline P/E, that is a steep discount to single-family residential REIT peers (AMH 26.8x, ELS 32.1x, INVH 31.0x; peer median ~31x). The peer comparison is largely uninformative because those companies own rental homes and earn rents, while MRP earns option fees on land — different cash-flow profiles, different risk. The relevant lens is the yield (high and currently covered) and the AFFO multiple relative to the model's risks.

The discount is partly justified rather than purely a mispricing. The Lennar concentration, the externally-managed conflict structure, the dual-class governance that limits index inclusion, and the unproven downcycle behavior all warrant a structural haircut. Analyst targets sit at $35–40 (mean $38.6, median $40), implying 23–41% upside, and the small covering group (5 analysts, all Buy/Strong Buy) is constructive. But these targets pre-date the recent softening and assume a re-rating toward a yield/spread the market has declined to award for over a year. We treat the targets as aspirational rather than as a floor here, because the gating of equity issuance signals the company itself does not view the current price as sufficient to fund growth, a tell that the market's skepticism is not obviously wrong.

The yield does provide genuine support: a covered distribution is a meaningful absolute return that limits downside absent a dividend cut. A "cheap" multiple is not by itself a buy signal; it requires a catalyst the market is missing, and the clearest near-term catalyst (accretive equity-funded growth) is unavailable at this price. We score valuation 6.5 — supported by yield and AFFO coverage, capped by the absence of a re-rating trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

Millrose claims to be the only publicly traded entity engaging in large-scale recycled-capital land banking, competing primarily against private funds (Blackstone among them) in a niche with high operational barriers. Its structural advantage is permanent, low-cost, perpetual capital, which it has leveraged into an M&A-partner role. The homebuilding industry's pivot toward asset-light, land-light, manufacturing-style velocity models is a real and durable tailwind: builders cannot grow community count while shrinking their balance sheets without a capital partner.

The competitive position is improving on diversification and protected by the pooling structure that raises walkaway costs. The offsetting reality is that the franchise's economics are heavily anchored to Lennar, whose Applicable Rate Adjustment Right caps Millrose's pricing flexibility (Lennar's rate auto-matches any lower rate offered to others), and whose Founder's Rights preserve outsized influence. The moat is genuine but narrow, and the trajectory positive but early. We score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on the rating.

**Counterparty concentration.** Lennar produced 88% of FY2025 option fee revenue (85% under the Master Program Agreement alone) and is under no obligation to provide future business or maintain exclusivity. A Lennar bankruptcy could render core agreements unenforceable and, in the company's own language, "could force [Millrose] into bankruptcy." Diversification is improving but remains early.

**Founder's Rights overhang.** Lennar retains Management Succession Consent, Enforcement Rights (can halt all monthly option payments within a 10-day cure period), a Capital Priority Right, the Applicable Rate Adjustment Right, a Pause Period Right (can pause takedowns up to six months and cut option payments to 50% in downturns), and a 1:1 D/E consent right. Combined with the Miller Family's ~43% voting power via Class B shares, control and economics are decoupled: Lennar can shape Millrose without share-price exposure.

**Untested downcycle model.** The entire thesis rests on builders electing to exercise options. In a severe downturn they can forfeit deposits and walk, and Millrose, with no building expertise at KL, would be left holding land. Zero terminations through ~two years of soft conditions is encouraging but is not a 2008-style stress test.

**External-management conflicts.** KL advised both Lennar and Millrose in structuring the spin and negotiated its own non-arm's-length fee on Tangible Assets, incentivizing asset growth over per-share returns. KL runs competing land-banking vehicles under a rotational Allocation Policy and can terminate on 60 days' notice.

**Other.** ~50% of portfolio value sits in CA/FL/TX (catastrophe and regional-softness exposure); no independent appraisals or full Phase I environmental reports on certain spin/Rausch assets (CERCLA liability tail); first 404(b) auditor attestation begins with FY2026; and the dividend may be paid up to ~80% in stock, a quiet lever that could dilute the cash yield.

These are specific, partially binary risks not fully neutralized by the current valuation. We score risk factors 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

A genuinely novel, capital-efficient platform with a covered ~10.5% yield, a 9.2% (and rising) portfolio yield, and a multi-year revenue runway captured by $16.1B of future takedown proceeds. Diversification away from Lennar (31% and climbing) structurally lifts blended yield via the 11% non-Lennar agreements. The May 2026 insider cluster — a ~$6.4M CEO open-market buy plus COO, GC, and director purchases near $27 — signals management sees the equity as cheap and rebuts the external-manager alignment critique. If housing stabilizes and the equity re-rates toward an 8% yield, the stock approaches analyst targets of $35–40 (23–41% upside) while paying a double-digit distribution along the way.

### Bear Case

A Lennar shock — bankruptcy, a strategic shift back to other land banks, or aggressive use of Founder's Rights — impairs 85–88% of revenue with limited recourse. A true housing downturn triggers the first option terminations, exposing a model with no building capability. The dividend, only adequately covered, gets trimmed (or paid partly in stock), removing the yield support. Debt-funded growth into the 33% ceiling at 6%+ coupons compresses AFFO in a higher-for-longer rate path, and the equity stays gated below issuance levels, capping growth indefinitely. Downside toward the low-to-mid $20s (a ~12% yield) is plausible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (early August), then the FY2026 10-K and first 404(b) attestation.**

This is an income hold: the AFFO-covered yield is the return we are underwriting, not appreciation. The franchise is too concentrated and the model too untested through a downturn to assign a confident appreciation thesis, and management's own gating of equity issuance signals the most likely re-rating catalyst is unavailable at current prices.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Non-Lennar capital share crosses ~40% with blended portfolio yield trending toward 9.5%+, materially reducing single-counterparty dependence.
- The equity re-rates to a level where management resumes accretive issuance, or AFFO coverage of the dividend widens to >1.2x with continued zero terminations.
- A definitive narrowing or sunset of Lennar's Founder's Rights / Miller Family control that improves governance and reopens index-inclusion potential.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any reported option terminations of scale, or evidence of builder walkaways in soft markets.
- A dividend cut, a shift to substantial stock-paid dividends, or AFFO coverage falling below 1.0x.
- Material adverse exercise of Lennar's Enforcement, Pause Period, or Capital Priority rights; or a Lennar credit deterioration.
- Debt-to-capital pressing the 33% ceiling without a corresponding equity window.

**Reassessment:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August) for counterparty count, non-Lennar mix, termination status, AFFO coverage, and leverage trajectory.

## Conclusion

Millrose is a well-structured, genuinely differentiated land-banking platform offering a high, currently-covered yield, conservative leverage, and a credible insider-buying signal that anchors a floor near $27. The score trajectory (6.4 → 7.1 → 6.7) reflects improving financial-health and competitive-position reads as the first operating year delivered; we hold the overall lower at 6.1, reflecting the binding risk profile rather than any fundamental deterioration, and the price decline since prior reports reflects market caution on concentration and the gated equity, not a weakening of the underlying franchise. What caps the rating is structural: the Lennar revenue concentration, retained Founder's Rights and Miller Family voting control despite Lennar's equity exit, an externally-managed conflict structure, and a model unproven through a true downturn. We rate MRP a HOLD for income. Investors comfortable with the concentration and governance can collect a covered double-digit yield while the diversification story plays out; we would not chase it for appreciation until non-Lennar mix and AFFO coverage demonstrably broaden, or governance overhangs ease.

No derived-metric error materially changes the HOLD thesis.
