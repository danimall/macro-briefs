**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG is a pure-play owner-operator of Very Large Gas Carriers (VLGCs) that transport propane and butane on long-haul seaborne routes, principally US Gulf-to-Asia and Middle East-to-Asia. The company emerged from a FY2025 earnings trough into a strong FY2026 recovery: revenue rebounded to $482M from $353M, net income roughly doubled to $194M, and time-charter-equivalent (TCE) rates climbed to $52,238/day from $39,778. The most recent quarter (Q3 FY26) showed revenue +48.7% YoY and EPS +122% on a double tailwind of rising Baltic LPG index rates and falling bunker fuel cost, which the scrubber-heavy fleet converts into incremental TCE.

The complication is that a meaningful portion of this strength is geopolitically sourced. Dorian's own 10-K cites an April 2026 partial US blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, which lengthened voyages and tightened vessel availability, inflating spot rates. Pareto analysts (via TradeWinds, June 16) expect a Hormuz "deal" to end what they call an "extraordinary" VLGC rates run. Peer BW LPG's Q1 CY2026 commentary corroborates a strong but explicitly inefficiency-driven market ("trading inefficiencies and a shortage of available vessels"). Layered onto this is a large newbuild orderbook—roughly 124 VLGCs through 2029, including ~78 ammonia carriers that can be redeployed into LPG trade. The trailing earnings power and the rising dividend cadence therefore overstate sustainable mid-cycle economics, and the estimate-revision surge (+80.5% on the current year) reflects analysts catching up to a peak rather than identifying durable growth.

We rate LPG a HOLD on an income basis. The investment case rests on three durable supports: a low-leverage balance sheet (D/E ~0.6, all covenants in compliance), roughly $608M of fleet market value above carrying cost providing genuine NAV and covenant protection, and a shareholder-friendly variable dividend (~7.5% trailing yield) backed by disciplined capital allocation. What is missing is asymmetric upside: at ~$40 the stock trades near the level where insiders other than the founder-CEO have been net sellers, and the forward earnings stream is geared entirely to a spot market that is most likely past its peak.

## Company Overview

Dorian LPG operates a fleet of 27 VLGCs (ships over 80,000 cbm), with 21 owned—mostly 2015-built fuel-efficient "ECO" vessels from Hyundai/Hanwha—and 6 time-chartered-in dual-fuel Panamax-beam vessels with purchase options. Average fleet age is roughly 10 years, younger than the global VLGC fleet, and 16 vessels carry exhaust scrubbers. A new 93,000 cbm dual-fuel VLGC/ammonia carrier (the *Areion*) was delivered in Q1 CY2026, providing optionality on a future seaborne ammonia trade.

The commercial model is the defining feature: roughly 99% of revenue flows through the Helios LPG Pool, a 50/50 joint venture with MOL Energia (a Mitsui O.S.K. Lines subsidiary). Vessels trade spot, on contracts of affreightment, or short time charters, leaving Dorian's earnings almost entirely exposed to the volatile VLGC spot market with essentially no contracted backlog. Helios is treated as a related-party variable interest entity that Dorian does not consolidate, since power is shared and neither party is the primary beneficiary.

Customers, accessed directly or through Helios, include ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, Equinor, TotalEnergies, Sunoco, and traders such as Glencore, Vitol, and Itochu, plus Asian importers. No single charterer exceeds 10% of revenue. The company is a Marshall Islands corporation headquartered in Stamford, CT, founded by executives who have operated in LPG shipping since 2002. Its principal pure-play competitor is the larger BW LPG.

## Financial Analysis

The business is highly cyclical, with revenue and earnings tracking the VLGC spot market:

| FY (Mar 31) | Revenue | Net Income | TCE/day | Adj EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $561M | $307M | $62,129 | $417M |
| FY2025 | $353M | $90M | $39,778 | $206M |
| FY2026 | $482M | $194M | $52,238 | $305M |

FY2024-to-FY2025 demonstrated the downside: net income fell from $307M to $90M, a 71% decline, in a single year on a TCE drop from ~$62K to ~$40K. FY2026 recovered most of that ground. Operating margins are exceptional for an industrial—roughly 44% operating and ~41% net in FY2026—which is characteristic of an asset-heavy shipping business at the top of its rate cycle rather than a structural feature.

The latest reported quarter (Q3 FY26, ended 12/31/2025) showed net income of $47.2M and TCE of $50,333/day. The mechanism is straightforward: the Baltic LPG index rose to roughly $67.8/mt from $55.7, while VLSFO bunker fuel fell to ~$452/mt from $570, a combination that benefits scrubber-equipped vessels disproportionately because they burn cheaper high-sulfur fuel and capture the spread.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Cash was approximately $294M at December 2025, total debt roughly $513M (mostly fixed-rate Japanese sale-leaseback financings plus one SOFR facility ~80% swapped at 2.85%), and equity ~$1.09B. The current ratio is ~2.4 and debt-to-equity ~0.6, conservative for capital-intensive shipping, where 1.5x to 2x leverage is common. Return on equity runs ~17% and ROA ~10% on FY2026's elevated earnings. Operating cash flow was ~$128M over nine months.

Aggregate fleet market value exceeded carrying value by approximately $608M at 3/31/2026. This is a real cushion: it protects against impairment, supports the minimum vessel-value-to-loan covenants, and the May 2026 sale of the 2015-built *Cobra* for ~$82M validates that asset marks are well above book. For a company with an Altman Z-Score in the grey zone (~2.6, typical for the sector), this NAV buffer is the central downside support.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and underscores low visibility: over the last four quarters, two beats (Q4 FY26 +27.5%, Q2 FY26 +1.2%) and two misses (Q3 FY26 -7.4%, Q1 FY26 -61.4%). The 61% Q1 miss in particular shows how quickly spot-rate assumptions can break.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data is dramatic on its face: current-quarter EPS revised +64.9% over 30 days and +117.0% over 90 days; next-quarter +137.4%; current year +80.5% over 90 days; next year +88.5% over 90 days. Under most circumstances revision velocity of this magnitude combined with revenue growth would be a strong forward signal. Here it requires careful interpretation.

These revisions reflect analysts marking estimates up to a rate environment that is, by the consensus of the shipping research community, near or past its peak. The current-year estimate jumped to $6.29 while the next-year estimate sits at $3.01: analysts themselves are forecasting EPS to roughly halve as the rate spike normalizes. That is a peak-and-fade profile. The +30% revision signal that typically precedes re-rating applies to stocks recovering from a trough, not to a cyclical at the top of its cycle where the same analysts model a roughly 52% decline next year.

The forward growth picture is therefore bimodal and rate-dependent. If Hormuz disruption persists, FY2027 approximates FY2026. If a Hormuz deal normalizes the Strait and pulls voyage lengths and vessel-supply tightness back toward baseline, rates fall and the next-year estimate of ~$3.01 becomes the operative number. The structural demand backdrop—US shale-driven LPG export growth feeding Chinese, Indian, and Southeast Asian petrochemical and heating demand—supports long-term ton-mile growth, but this is offset by the ~124-vessel orderbook. The ~78 ammonia carriers (VLACs) in that orderbook can be redeployed into LPG trade, as can ethane carriers, adding latent supply that caps mid-decade rate upside if LPG demand growth does not keep pace.

Growth prospects score modestly because the durable, normalized earnings trajectory is flat-to-declining off a peak, not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

LPG trades at ~8.7x trailing earnings (EPS $4.54) and offers a ~7.5% dividend yield. Against the peer median P/E of 24.9 for Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, the stock looks cheap. That comparison is misleading: the listed peers (KNTK, AM, DTM, VNOM) are midstream pipeline and gathering businesses with contracted, fee-based cash flows that justify higher multiples. The relevant comparable is the tanker/gas-carrier cohort, where INSW trades at ~7.5x, and cyclical shipping names structurally carry low multiples precisely because trailing earnings represent peak-cycle output.

The cycle-adjusted view is what matters. On the current-year estimate of $6.29, the stock trades at ~6.4x; on the next-year estimate of $3.01, it trades at ~13.3x. The next-year figure is the more honest anchor because it embeds the rate normalization that analysts and management both expect. At 13x normalized-toward-trough earnings, the stock is fairly priced, not cheap. The low trailing P/E is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal: the multiple is depressed because the market correctly discounts that the $4.54 trailing and $6.29 forward figures are not repeatable.

The genuine valuation support is asset-based, not earnings-based. Net asset value, with the fleet-value cushion on a ~$1.09B equity base, implies a book-plus value approaching the current market capitalization of ~$1.7B, leaving limited NAV upside but meaningful downside protection. The ~7.5% yield provides a real return floor, though it is explicitly variable and will be cut in a downturn, as it effectively was in FY2025.

Analyst price targets cluster between $45 (low) and $55 (high), with a mean of $51.2 and median of $51.0, implying ~12-37% upside from $40.19. These targets are anchored to the elevated current-year estimate and would likely come down if Hormuz normalizes. We do not treat them as floors here precisely because the underlying earnings stream is at a cyclical peak rather than a depressed trough; on a peak-multiple cyclical, analyst targets reflect the same rate optimism embedded in the stock and are vulnerable to the same normalization. Valuation scores middling: cheap on trailing, fair on normalized, with asset value as the binding support.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian's competitive position is solid but not improving in a way the market hasn't priced. Its modern, fuel-efficient, scrubber-heavy fleet (16 of 27 vessels) lowers fuel cost and raises TCE when high-/low-sulfur fuel spreads widen, a genuine cost edge over older tonnage. In-house commercial and technical management plus scale within the Helios Pool (sharing capacity with MOL) round out the moat. The dual-fuel/ammonia-capable newbuild positions the company for an eventual seaborne ammonia trade.

The constraints are structural. Dorian is materially smaller than BW LPG, the dominant pure-play, and it is fully spot-exposed with no time-charter backlog to smooth earnings. Its commercial dependence on the Helios JV concentrates revenue through a single related-party channel. The ammonia optionality cuts both ways: the same VLAC newbuilds that represent Dorian's long-dated opportunity are the near-term oversupply threat, since VLACs can spill into LPG trade. On trajectory—the dimension that matters most for scoring competitive position—Dorian is roughly stable: it is not gaining share against BW LPG, and its cost advantage is durable but already understood by the market. We score this dimension at the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely move this stock, beyond sector boilerplate:

1. **Spot-rate cyclicality with no backlog.** With ~99% of capacity in the spot-exposed Helios Pool and no fixed charters, earnings can halve year-over-year, as FY24-to-FY25 already demonstrated (-71% net income). The variable dividend is directly geared to the Baltic LPG index.

2. **Hormuz normalization as a downside catalyst.** This is the central near-term idiosyncratic risk and it is the inverse of the broader market's preference. A Hormuz reopening—broadly bullish for risk assets and disinflationary—is specifically bearish for Dorian, because the disruption is what lengthened voyages and tightened vessel supply to inflate rates. A verified, held reopening pulls VLGC TCEs down from elevated 2026 levels.

3. **Newbuild oversupply.** The ~124-vessel orderbook, including the ~78 VLACs that can be redeployed into LPG, threatens the supply/demand balance later this decade.

4. **Route and policy costs.** Panama Canal restrictions and tolls raise costs on the core US Gulf-to-Asia route, often unreimbursed. USTR Section 301 fees on Chinese-built/operated vessels and the Feb 2026 US Maritime Action Plan proposing a port fee on non-US-built vessels create exposure, since most of Dorian's fleet is Korean/Japanese-built.

5. **Covenant-value linkage.** A rate downturn deflates vessel values and could pressure minimum value-to-loan covenants, mitigated currently by the NAV cushion but not eliminated.

6. **Governance.** Combined Chairman/President/CEO role, classified board, blank-check preferred, and anti-takeover provisions are entrenchment features that could suppress any control premium.

These are specific, not generic, and several point to material downside the trailing earnings do not capture. Risk scores below the midpoint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market does not corroborate acute near-term bearishness. ATM IV at 43.4% is roughly fair to historical vol (41.5%, spread +1.9%), and IV has fallen sharply since early June (54.0% → 43.4%), suggesting resolved uncertainty around the May earnings event. Put/call skew is call-heavy (-6.4%) and the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.33: positioning is not defensively tilted. The one caution is term-structure backwardation (near-term 43.4% > long-term 38.9%), which prices some imminent stress, consistent with the live Hormuz/de-escalation binary that could swing rates either way. Net read: the market is not pricing a collapse, but it is pricing a wider-than-normal near-term range (±9.0% implied move) around an unresolved geopolitical catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

VLGC rates remain elevated because Hormuz disruption persists or because the structural US-export ton-mile growth and a vessel-shortage on the US-Far East trade keep the market tight even as some normalization occurs. Dorian's scrubber-heavy modern fleet continues to capture the fuel spread, FY2027 EPS approximates the $6.29 current-year run-rate rather than collapsing to ~$3, and the variable dividend stays in the $3-4/share annual range, yielding 8-10% at the current price. The NAV cushion and ongoing fleet renewal (selling 2015 tonnage at premiums to book) compound book value. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $55, roughly 37% upside.

### Bear Case

A verified Hormuz reopening normalizes voyage lengths and vessel availability, the "extraordinary" rates run that Pareto flags ends, and the newbuild orderbook begins to bite. FY2027 EPS reverts toward the ~$3.01 next-year estimate, the variable dividend is cut (as in FY2025), and the stock de-rates toward NAV. With the fleet-value cushion on $1.09B equity, downside is cushioned, but the equity could trade toward the high-$20s to low-$30s—the level at which the CEO bought and the rate cycle last troughed—implying 20-30% downside from $40.

### Base Case

Rates ease from the 2026 peak but do not collapse; FY2027 lands between the elevated current-year and depressed next-year estimates. The stock holds in the high-$30s to low-$40s, supported by NAV and the variable yield, with total return driven mostly by dividends rather than capital appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income basis). Reassessment timeline: next quarterly earnings and any verified Strait of Hormuz reopening, whichever comes first.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A clear signal that VLGC rate strength is structural rather than Hormuz-driven—for example, the next-year consensus EPS estimate revising upward from ~$3.01 toward $5+ on durable US-export ton-mile demand rather than disruption.
- A pullback toward the high-$20s/low-$30s that restores NAV-based asymmetry, particularly if accompanied by renewed CEO open-market buying.
- Conversion of any meaningful portion of the fleet to fixed time charters at attractive rates, reducing spot dependence and improving earnings visibility.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A covenant breach or a fleet-value impairment that erodes the NAV cushion (would require a severe and sustained rate downturn).
- A dividend cut paired with falling vessel values that pressures the balance sheet.
- Newbuild deliveries clearly tipping the market into structural oversupply with no demand offset.

**Reassessment anchors:** the next earnings release (TCE trajectory and dividend declaration), and any verified, held Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of sustained commercial transit), which would be the single most important input to forward rates.

## Conclusion

Dorian LPG is a well-managed, conservatively financed, modern-fleet pure-play VLGC operator. The FY2026 recovery was real, but a material portion of it reflects a Hormuz-driven rate spike that the shipping research community and the company's own cautious capital-allocation tone suggest will fade, against a building newbuild orderbook. The estimate-revision surge looks impressive but tracks a peak that analysts themselves model as halving next year. The trailing 8.7x P/E is a value-trap signal here, not a bargain, because the earnings underneath it are at a cyclical high.

The reasons to hold are genuine and defensible: a strong balance sheet, the asset value above book providing downside protection, and a variable dividend yielding ~7.5%. The reasons not to chase are equally clear: the stock sits near the level where the C-suite (excluding the founder-CEO who bought the bottom at $27.30) sold into the rally, the forward earnings stream is fully spot-exposed and most likely past its peak, and the most-watched geopolitical catalyst, a Hormuz reopening, happens to be specifically bearish for this name. Hold for the yield and the asset value; do not underwrite appreciation from current levels. The June score reset from 6.8 to the high-5s reflects the recognition that the recent boom is geopolitically borrowed rather than structurally earned, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that view.
