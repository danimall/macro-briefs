## Executive Summary

International Seaways is a pure-play tanker company operating approximately 64 vessels with 82% spot market exposure, positioned at the intersection of structural geopolitical disruption and cyclical rate strength. The company reported its strongest quarter since Q4 2022 in Q1 2026 with $286M net income ($3.90 adjusted EPS vs. $2.48 consensus), driven by VLCC rates exceeding $100K/day as Hormuz disruptions, Russian rerouting, and Houthi attacks extended ton-mile demand across all vessel classes.

Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up 195% in the past 30 days (from $1.25 to $3.70), with full-year 2026 estimates surging 153% to $14.79. At $85/share and ~5.7x forward P/E, the stock trades at a steep discount to peer median P/E of 24.9x, despite superior balance sheet metrics (D/E 0.32, current ratio 3.71, ~$918M total liquidity as of Q1 2026) and a 5-year TSR of 471% vs. 196% for peers. The January 2026 acquisition of 100% of the Tankers International commercial manager adds a modest recurring fee-income stream — a useful but relatively small complement to INSW's core rate-driven earnings.

The primary risk is the stock's extreme sensitivity to geopolitical resolution: a normalization of shipping routes (Hormuz reopening, Suez transit resumption, Russian oil sanctions relaxation) would compress rates 40-60% from current levels. The tanker orderbook, which grew by an additional 22.5M dwt in Q1 2026 alone, adds medium-term supply risk. However, with EIA reporting the Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed through late May 2026, with April Brent averaging $117/b, the rate environment remains structurally supportive through at least H2 2026.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates a diversified tanker fleet across two segments: Crude Tankers (12 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes — 52% of TCE revenue) and Product Carriers (1 LR2, 7 LR1s, 33 MRs — 48% of TCE revenue). The fleet's weighted-average age is 10.9 years, with active renewal through sales of older vessels and delivery of dual-fuel LNG-ready LR1 newbuilds (with remaining deliveries expected by September 2026). Following seven vessel sales in Q1 2026 and one delivery, the operating fleet stood at approximately 64 vessels at quarter-end.

The company participates in six major commercial pools and in January 2026 acquired the remaining 50% stake in TUKA for $10M, gaining 100% ownership of the commercial manager for Tankers International Limited (TIL) — a leading VLCC pool. INSW has expanded TI into a new Suezmax pool, migrating its own Suezmaxes and attracting third-party tonnage. While the acquisition is strategically useful for scheduling control and pool optimization, management has indicated the consolidation will add only a few million dollars per quarter in incremental earnings — modest relative to the company's core rate-driven income.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 revenue of $843M and net income of $309M reflected a weaker product carrier market (MR rates fell from $31K to $24K/day) offset by strengthening crude tanker rates. Q4 2025 saw VLCC rates spike to $75,566/day, and Q1 2026 produced the company's strongest quarter since Q4 2022: $286M net income ($5.75 diluted EPS reported, $3.90 adjusted). However, Q1 2026 GAAP results included $88.2M in vessel-sale gains and a $3.9M holding gain from the TUKA transaction. Adjusted net income of $194M and adjusted EBITDA of $243.6M better represent recurring earnings power.

**Balance Sheet Strength:** The balance sheet is exceptionally strong by shipping standards:
- Net debt to capital: 16.5% (down from 22.2% in 2024)
- D/E ratio: 0.32
- Current ratio: 3.71
- Total liquidity (as of March 31, 2026): $917.9M ($376.8M cash and short-term investments + $541.1M undrawn revolvers)
- 31 unencumbered vessels (at year-end 2025)
- Altman Z-Score: 3.79 (safe zone)

**Cash Flow & Capital Returns:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $380M funded $426M in fleet investment and $145M in dividends. Q1 2026 cash from operating activities was $141.1M, supplemented by $222.8M in vessel-sale proceeds. Since 2020, the company has returned over $1.0B to shareholders. The Q1 2026 dividend of $4.55/share (combined regular + supplemental) was described by management as reflecting an 85% payout ratio on adjusted net income plus an additional discretionary component — representing the largest quarterly dividend in company history. At $3.90 adjusted EPS, the $4.55 dividend represents approximately 117% of adjusted earnings, indicating the discretionary component meaningfully exceeded the 85% framework.

**Debt Profile:** The September 2025 issuance of $250M senior unsecured bonds at 7.125% (maturing September 2030) and August 2025 ECA credit facility at SOFR+125bps for newbuilds reflect disciplined capital structure management. No near-term refinancing cliffs exist.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is driven by three structural forces:

**1. Ton-mile demand expansion:** Hormuz disruptions, Cape of Good Hope rerouting (Houthi attacks), and Russian oil trade reorientation have collectively added 15-25% to average voyage distances. With EIA reporting Brent averaged $117/b in April 2026 and Middle East shut-ins of approximately 10.5M b/d, these drivers are intensifying rather than normalizing.

**2. TI pool ownership:** The transition from pool participant to pool operator/owner creates a recurring, though relatively modest, fee-income stream from third-party pool participants regardless of rate volatility. The Suezmax pool expansion demonstrates the platform's scalability, though the near-term contribution is expected to be only a few million dollars per quarter.

**3. Fleet renewal positioning for IMO 2050:** Dual-fuel LNG-ready LR1 newbuilds position INSW ahead of tightening emissions regulations (EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime). As older non-compliant tonnage retires, modern fleet operators capture premium rates.

Estimate revisions confirm the acceleration: current-quarter EPS revised up 195% in 30 days, full-year 2026 up 153% (from $5.83 to $14.79). The 4-quarter beat streak with increasing magnitude (5.6% → 21.6% → 25.4% → 57.1%) demonstrates systematic analyst underestimation of rate sensitivity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $85/share, INSW trades at:
- Forward P/E: 5.7x (on $14.79 current-year estimate)
- Trailing P/E: 13.6x (on FY2025 $6.23 EPS — which underrepresents current earnings power)
- P/E on annualized Q1 2026 adjusted net income (~$194M × 4 = $776M, or ~$15.60/share): ~5.4x
- EV/EBITDA (FY2025): ~8.8x
- Peer median P/E: 24.9x

The discount to peers reflects the market's correct assumption that tanker rates are cyclical and current earnings represent above-mid-cycle levels. However, the discount appears excessive given: (a) the geopolitical backdrop provides structural rate support with EIA assuming Hormuz remains effectively closed through late May and only gradual resumption thereafter, (b) the balance sheet can sustain dividends through moderate rate downturns, and (c) estimates are still rising — suggesting even the current consensus underestimates the earnings trajectory.

On normalized mid-cycle VLCC rates of $45-55K/day (vs. current $100K+), INSW would earn approximately $6-8/share, implying a mid-cycle P/E of 10.6-14.2x — reasonable but not demanding for a company with this balance sheet quality and competitive position.

The 6 consensus analyst ratings (1 Strong Buy, 5 Buy, 0 Hold/Sell) with a median target of $91.50 and high target of $120 suggest 7-41% upside from current levels. Given the magnitude and velocity of estimate revisions (+195% in 30 days), these targets likely lag reality.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW is differentiated from pure-play peers (Frontline, DHT, Euronav, Scorpio, Torm) by:

1. **Fleet diversification:** Exposure to both crude (VLCC, Suezmax, Aframax) and products (LR1, MR) markets provides natural hedging between segments and reduces single-rate-class dependency.

2. **Pool ownership:** No other U.S.-listed tanker company owns the commercial management of a major pool. The TI acquisition gives INSW influence over vessel scheduling, rate optimization, and third-party fee income — though the near-term earnings contribution remains modest relative to core operations.

3. **Balance sheet superiority:** D/E of 0.32 and significant unencumbered fleet compares favorably to peers, many of whom carry higher leverage. This provides optionality for opportunistic acquisitions in downturns.

4. **Modern fleet composition:** Active renewal program (vessels sold at peak asset values, newbuilds contracted with dual-fuel capability) positions for regulatory compliance advantages.

The primary competitive risk is the expanding global tanker orderbook, which grew by an additional 22.5M dwt in Q1 2026 (on top of 23.6M dwt growth in 2025). Shipyard capacity constraints (competing with bulk carriers, containers, LNG carriers) and limited dry-dock availability may slow actual deliveries. The orderbook creates a 2027-2028 supply headwind that constrains the stock's ability to sustain peak multiples indefinitely.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Cyclicality (HIGH):** The fundamental risk. At 82% spot exposure, INSW's earnings swing dramatically with rates. The current rate environment reflects exceptional geopolitical conditions. If all three disruptions normalized simultaneously (Hormuz, Suez, Russia), rates could fall 50-60% from current levels, compressing EPS toward $3-5/share and the stock toward $40-55.

**Geopolitical Resolution (MODERATE-LOW near-term, HIGH medium-term):** EIA's May 2026 STEO assumes Hormuz remains effectively closed through late May, with traffic gradually resuming in June and shipments returning toward pre-conflict levels later in 2026. This provides near-term rate support but creates cliff risk if resolution occurs unexpectedly or ahead of EIA assumptions.

**Orderbook Supply (MODERATE, 18-24 month horizon):** The tanker orderbook grew by 22.5M dwt in Q1 2026, implying accelerating fleet growth through 2027-2028. If ton-mile demand growth fails to keep pace (i.e., geopolitical normalization + trade route shortening), rates face structural pressure.

**Potential Equity Dilution (MODERATE):** On May 11, 2026, INSW filed an 8-K disclosing an Equity Distribution Agreement for an at-the-market (ATM) offering of up to $200M of common stock, replacing a prior $100M ATM under which no shares had been sold. While no shares have been sold to date, the facility represents potential dilution of approximately 4-5% at current prices if fully utilized.

**U.S.-China Port Fees (MODERATE):** 13 China-built vessels exposed (4 below the 55K dwt minimum threshold). One-year suspension (from November 2025) expires in late 2026. The fee structure (the higher of $18 per net ton, rising to $33, or container-based fees, assessed up to five times per vessel per year) could result in material costs per affected vessel depending on U.S. port-call frequency. The actual impact depends heavily on net tonnage, call frequency, exemptions, and charter pass-through terms.

**MR Impairment (LOW):** $43.3M aggregate carrying value excess across 9 MRs. Not material at current earnings levels but indicates downside vulnerability in the product carrier segment.

**Governance:** In April 2026, INSW extended its shareholder rights plan (poison pill) through April 2029, increasing the purchase price from $50 to $95 per right. This reflects standard anti-takeover governance rather than any unusual restructuring activity.

## Conclusion

INSW represents a high-conviction expression of the current geopolitical oil supply disruption within the tanker sub-sector. The combination of extreme estimate revision velocity (+195% current quarter), consistent beats with accelerating magnitude, fortress balance sheet, and strategic vertical integration through TI pool ownership creates a compelling risk/reward at 5.7x forward earnings. The stock has appreciated significantly over the past year, but this is fundamentally driven — Q1 2026 adjusted net income ($194M) represented an annualized pace well above FY2025 full-year results ($309M).

The primary risk — geopolitical resolution collapsing rates — has limited near-term probability based on EIA's assumption of continued Hormuz closure through late May with only gradual resumption beginning in June. The orderbook risk is real and accelerating. Position sizing should reflect the binary nature of geopolitical exposure: 2-3% of portfolio is appropriate given the extreme upside/downside asymmetry if the conflict arc changes.
