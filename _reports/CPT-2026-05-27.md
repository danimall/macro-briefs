## Executive Summary

Camden Property Trust is a $11.2B multifamily REIT owning 174 apartment communities (59,416 homes) concentrated in Sunbelt markets. The company generates ~$1B in annual NOI with investment-grade credit ratings (A-/A3/A-), well-laddered debt maturities, and a 61% FFO payout ratio on its $4.24/share annual dividend.

The core issue is timing: Camden sits at the intersection of a maturing Sunbelt supply wave and a rising-rate environment. Q1 2026 same-store NOI declined 0.7% — the first negative print in the available data — while interest expense grew 10.6% YoY. Management has responded with an extraordinary $694M buyback program (7-8% of shares retired), effectively leveraging the balance sheet to buy back stock at ~$105-107/share. This bet requires same-store growth to reaccelerate as supply moderates through 2026-2027.

At $108.81, the stock trades at ~30x trailing earnings but a more relevant ~15.8x Core FFO and ~18.4x Core AFFO. The 3.9% dividend yield and visible cash flow provide downside support, but growth visibility is poor — consensus EPS estimates have been revised down 10-30% across all forward periods. The stock is a hold for income-oriented investors waiting for the supply cycle to turn.

## Company Overview

Camden Property Trust was formed in 1993 and is headquartered in Houston, Texas. The company is a self-managed, vertically integrated apartment REIT — it develops, constructs, acquires, operates, and manages multifamily communities. Key geographic exposures include Texas (~27% of assets), Florida (~18%), the Southeast corridor (~23%), Washington D.C. metro (~12%), and Western markets (~19%).

The business model features short-duration leases (~14-month average), providing both upside market exposure when rents rise and downside exposure when conditions soften. Camden employs approximately 1,640 people and has appeared on Fortune's 100 Best Companies to Work For list for 18 consecutive years (#18 ranking).

A strategic shift is underway: Camden is exiting Southern California (~8.4% of assets) via 1031 exchanges, redeploying capital to higher-growth Sunbelt markets with better long-term demographic tailwinds.

Revenue growth has decelerated materially. Core FFO per share has grown at roughly 1% annually over the past three years, reflecting the supply headwind in Camden's Sunbelt markets.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt stands at $4.25B against equity of $4.10B (debt/equity ~1.04x). The weighted average interest rate is 4.1% with a 5.4-year weighted average maturity. Interest coverage has declined from 6.9x (2024) to 6.6x (2025) to 6.0x (Q1 2026 annualized). This trajectory reflects both rising interest expense from the $600M in new 4.90% notes issued in February 2026 and flattening NOI.

Approximately 90% of properties are unencumbered, providing substantial secured borrowing capacity as a fallback. All three investment-grade ratings maintain stable outlooks.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow was $827M in FY2025 (~$148M/quarter). Free cash flow was ~$795M. The dividend costs ~$450M annually, leaving ~$345M for reinvestment, development, and buybacks before external capital. The company has funded its buyback program through a combination of dispositions ($452M since start of 2025), new debt ($600M), and retained cash flow.

**Capital Allocation:**

The defining feature of Camden's recent capital allocation is the $694M buyback program executed over 16 months. Share count declined from ~110.2M (weighted, FY2025) to ~106.5M (Q1 2026 weighted) — a 3.4% reduction in that single quarter, with additional purchases in April 2026. The average buyback price of ~$105-107 is slightly below the current $108.81 price.

This is a leveraged bet on NAV accretion. If same-store growth reaccelerates as supply moderates, the per-share FFO impact is amplified by the lower share count. If growth stagnates and rates stay elevated, Camden will have added debt to fund buybacks near breakeven levels.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are currently impaired:

1. **Same-store revenue growth decelerated to 0.2%** in Q1 2026 from 0.8% in FY2025. Expenses grew 1.9%, producing negative same-store NOI growth of -0.7%.

2. **Estimate revisions are broadly negative.** Current year EPS estimates have been cut ~10% over 90 days, and next year estimates are down ~30% over 90 days. This is a negative momentum signal.

3. **The supply cycle thesis:** Management argues that multifamily starts peaked in 2023-2024 and deliveries are peaking in 2025-2026. If true, the competitive pressure from new supply should moderate in late 2026 and 2027, allowing rent growth to reaccelerate. This is the bull thesis, but it requires patience and is not yet visible in operating results.

4. **Development pipeline:** Camden has $492M in active development (1,162 homes) and $491M in pipeline (932 homes). These projects deliver in 2026-2028 and represent modest incremental growth.

5. **California exit:** Selling ~$1.5-2B in California assets and redeploying to higher-growth Sunbelt markets could improve long-term growth rates, but creates a near-term FFO drag as reinvestment timing is lumpy.

Core FFO/share CAGR over the past four years was approximately 1.4% (from $6.52 to $6.88). Forward visibility suggests flat-to-slightly-down FFO in 2026 before potential reacceleration in 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

Camden competes with AvalonBay (AVB), Equity Residential (EQR), Mid-America Apartment (MAA), UDR, and Essex (ESS) among public REITs, plus numerous private operators. Several dynamics define the competitive position:

**Relative Performance:** Camden has underperformed its peer REIT index over the measurement period in its proxy disclosure (TSR of 130.26 vs. peer group's 137.83). This persistent underperformance suggests the market views Camden's portfolio quality or execution as slightly below the top-tier names.

**Sunbelt Concentration:** Camden's heavy Sunbelt exposure is both a long-term demographic advantage and a near-term liability. Markets like Austin, Nashville, Charlotte, and Phoenix have experienced the most acute supply additions. Camden's same-store deceleration is partly geographic.

**Competitive Differentiation:** Camden's employer brand (Fortune #18), self-managed structure, and development capabilities provide modest operational advantages. However, apartment management is not a high-moat business — properties compete on location, amenities, and price.

**California Exit:** By disposing of slower-growth California assets and redeploying, Camden is concentrating further in high-supply markets. This increases the operating leverage to a supply recovery but also increases portfolio concentration risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Sunbelt supply cycle takes longer to clear (Probability: 35-40%).** If new deliveries remain elevated through late 2027 rather than moderating in H2 2026, same-store growth stays near zero and FFO/share declines.

2. **Rising rates compress interest coverage further.** With $664M in near-term maturities (as of Q1 2026), refinancing will likely occur at higher rates than legacy debt. Coverage could continue to decline if NOI growth doesn't reaccelerate.

3. **RealPage antitrust — federal/DOJ case ($50-200M additional exposure).** The $53M class action settlement is done, but DOJ involvement and prospective business practice commitments could limit future revenue management optimization.

4. **Rent control legislation.** Multiple jurisdictions in Camden's Sunbelt markets are considering rent stabilization — a structural cap on growth if implemented.

5. **Buyback timing risk.** $694M deployed at ~$105-107/share; if NAV declines or capital is needed for distressed opportunities, this leverage-funded buyback could prove ill-timed.

**Mitigants:**
- Investment-grade ratings with stable outlooks
- 90% unencumbered properties = substantial secured borrowing capacity
- Well-covered dividend (61% payout)
- Short lease duration means rapid participation in any market recovery
- Strong demographic tailwinds in core markets

## Investment Thesis

- Multifamily supply peaks mid-2026 as projected; rent growth reaccelerates to 2-3% by H2 2026 / H1 2027
- Same-store NOI growth returns to 3-4% range, driving Core FFO/share toward $7.20-7.50
- Share count reduction from buybacks amplifies per-share recovery
- California exits close at favorable cap rates; redeployment generates incremental returns

- Stock rerated to 16-17x Core FFO = $115-127

**Bear Case ($88-95, -13-19% downside):**
- Supply wave extends into 2027; same-store NOI continues to decline
- Interest rates remain elevated or rise further, compressing REIT valuations
- Interest coverage approaches 5x; rating agencies put Camden on negative outlook
- RealPage DOJ settlement exceeds $100M with ongoing business practice restrictions
- Consumer weakness drives move-outs and concessions; occupancy declines
- Stock derated to 13-14x Core FFO on declining estimates = $88-95

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Same-store revenue growth ≥1.5% sustained for two consecutive quarters (indicating supply absorption)
- Core FFO/share estimates revised upward for 2 consecutive months
- Stock trades below $100 (implied cap rate compression to entry-attractive levels)

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI declines ≥2% for two consecutive quarters
- Interest coverage falls below 5.0x
- Debt/equity exceeds 1.3x or rating agency action (negative watch)
- RealPage DOJ settlement exceeds $150M with material operational restrictions

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) will provide the critical data point on whether same-store growth is stabilizing or continuing to deteriorate.

## Conclusion

Camden Property Trust is a well-managed, investment-grade apartment REIT navigating a supply-heavy period in its core Sunbelt markets. The 3.9% dividend yield is well-covered, the balance sheet is adequate despite aggressive buybacks, and management's strategic repositioning (California exit, share repurchases) could create value if the supply cycle turns as expected.

However, growth is absent today — same-store NOI is declining, estimates are being cut across all forward periods, and interest expense is rising. The stock trades near the midpoint of a $95-$134 analyst target range with a median consensus implying only ~4.3% upside.

Recommendation: collect the 3.9% dividend while waiting for the supply cycle to turn. The supply thesis has credible support (starts have peaked), but the translation to improved same-store results lags by 2-3 quarters at minimum. Until Q2/Q3 2026 data confirms the turn, there is no catalyst to drive re-rating. Maintain HOLD.
