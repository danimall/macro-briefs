## Executive Summary

News Corp presents a sum-of-parts undervaluation case. The company's 61.4% stake in ASX-listed REA Group (Australia's dominant real estate portal) represents a significant portion of News Corp's approximately $16B combined equity market cap, implying the market assigns a steep discount to Dow Jones (with $588M segment EBITDA), HarperCollins ($296M EBITDA), the Move/Realtor.com platform, and net cash.

The post-Foxtel News Corp is a materially cleaner business. The April 2025 divestiture removed a capital-intensive, declining cable TV operation and generated substantial proceeds. The remaining portfolio — professional information (Dow Jones Risk & Compliance growing 15%), digital real estate, premium content brands, and book publishing — generates $1.4B in segment EBITDA and $571M in free cash flow. AI content licensing deals with OpenAI and Meta represent an emerging revenue stream that partially offsets traffic cannibalization risk.

The primary thesis impediment is the Murdoch family's controlling position and dual-class structure, which cap re-rating potential and explain the persistent conglomerate discount. However, approximately $1.0B in remaining buyback capacity provides a mechanism for value realization that partially circumvents governance overhang. With forward EPS estimates rising (+5.4% over 90 days for FY2027) and 3 of 4 recent quarters beating estimates, the fundamental trajectory supports the position.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates five segments following the Foxtel divestiture:

- **Dow Jones** (28% of revenue, $2.33B): Wall Street Journal (4.5M digital subscribers), professional information products (Risk & Compliance, Factiva, Dow Jones Energy), Barron's, MarketWatch. 82% digital revenue mix.
- **Digital Real Estate** (21%, $1.80B): 61.4% of REA Group (dominant Australian platform with 4x traffic vs. competitors) and 80% of Move (Realtor.com).
- **Book Publishing** (25%, $2.15B): HarperCollins, world's #2 consumer publisher. 120+ imprints, 15 countries.
- **News Media** (26%, $2.17B): News Corp Australia, News UK (The Sun, The Times), New York Post, talkSPORT, Sky News Australia.

Total FY2025 revenue from continuing operations: $8.45B. Segment EBITDA: $1.415B (+14% YoY).

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** The headline trailing P/E of 39x is distorted by GAAP charges that mask underlying profitability. Segment EBITDA of $1.415B represents a 16.7% margin, expanding from 15.0% in FY2024. GAAP operating income is depressed by $459M in D&A (much from acquired intangibles) and $132M in restructuring charges. After these charges and equity losses, operating income before interest/other/tax was approximately $809M, representing an operating margin of roughly 9.6%. Net income from continuing operations was $648M (+71% YoY); normalized earnings power is approximately $1.08/share for FY2026E, growing to $1.25 in FY2027E.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow from continuing operations: $978M (+9% YoY). Free cash flow: $571M (6.8% FCF margin, approximately 3.6% FCF yield on the ~$16B combined market cap). The FCF-to-net-income conversion is strong despite restructuring-impacted GAAP margins.

**Balance Sheet:** As of December 31, 2025, cash was approximately $2.05B vs. $1.95B in borrowings, yielding net cash of approximately $100M. Approximately $321M of cash was held at REA Group and is not readily accessible to News Corp unless REA declares a dividend. Current ratio 1.84. $750M undrawn revolver. Debt-to-equity is approximately 0.21x on a borrowings-to-total-equity basis, reflecting the significant equity base ($9.4B). Near-term maturities are manageable — only $25M due in fiscal 2026, though $450M matures in fiscal 2027 and term loan/revolver facilities mature March 2027 unless extended. Goodwill of $4.37B (40% of assets) is the primary risk to book value integrity — particularly if U.S. real estate weakness triggers Move impairment.

**Capital Return:** As of December 31, 2025, approximately $1.04B in combined buyback authorization remained (after $266M repurchased in H1 FY2026), representing roughly 6-7% of the ~$16B combined market cap. At continued repurchase rates, this materially accretes EPS on a 2-3 year horizon. These programs have no expiration date but can be modified, suspended, or discontinued at any time.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth from continuing operations is modest at +2% YoY, but this masks a bifurcated portfolio:

**Growing segments (12-18% EBITDA growth):**
- Dow Jones Risk & Compliance: +15% revenue
- Dow Jones Energy: +11% revenue
- REA Group: +18% segment EBITDA, driven by pricing power in Australian property listings
- HarperCollins: +10% EBITDA on backlist stability
- Move/Realtor.com: Q2 FY2026 showed recovery with revenue +10% and lead volumes +13% YoY

**Challenged segments:**
- News Media: revenue -4%, though EBITDA improved +15% on cost restructuring
- Book Publishing digital sales: decreased 4% in H1 FY2026, with digital representing approximately 20% of consumer revenue

**AI content licensing** represents an incremental growth vector. News Corp has announced deals with OpenAI (reported at approximately $250M over five years, including cash and technology credits, though official financial terms were not publicly disclosed) and Meta (a multi-year agreement, though specific annual financial terms have not been officially confirmed). Additional negotiations with other platforms are ongoing per company disclosures, though specific deals with Google, Apple, or Amazon have not been announced.

Consensus estimates show FY2026E EPS of $1.08 and FY2027E of $1.25 — representing 16% earnings growth. Estimates have risen +4.3% and +5.4% over 90 days respectively. This is steady mid-teens earnings compounding driven by mix shift toward higher-margin professional information and digital real estate.

## Valuation Assessment

The core investment case is sum-of-parts:

| Component | Estimated Value |
|-----------|----------------|
| REA Group stake (61.4% of ~A$20.7B market cap, converted to USD) | ~$8.0-8.5B |
| Dow Jones (15x $588M EBITDA) | ~$8.8B |
| HarperCollins (10x $296M EBITDA) | ~$3.0B |
| News Media (6x $153M EBITDA) | ~$0.9B |
| Move/Realtor.com | ~$1.5-2.0B |
| Net cash | ~$0.1B |
| Less: Corporate overhead (8x $223M) | -$1.8B |
| **Estimated NAV** | **~$21-22B** |
| **Per share (Class A + B, ~558M shares)** | **~$37-40** |

Against this, the combined Class A + B market cap is approximately $16B. The NWS Class B shares trade at $30.23. Class B shares carry one vote per share, while Class A shares have voting rights only in limited circumstances. The current price represents a 20-30% discount to conservative SOTP.

On earnings multiples: 24x FY2027E EPS of $1.25 is in line with the peer median P/E of 24.2x, while the company offers significantly more asset value. The NYT trades at 40x with lower asset backing.

The conglomerate discount is real and structural (driven by Murdoch control/dual-class), but the magnitude appears excessive given the cleaner post-Foxtel portfolio and buyback capacity.

## Competitive Landscape

**REA Group** maintains a near-monopoly position in Australian residential real estate with 4x traffic vs. the nearest competitor. This is a structural advantage with pricing power — REA has consistently raised listing fees without losing market share. The withdrawn Rightmove bid signals intent to build global scale.

**Dow Jones professional information** competes with RELX, S&P Global, and Thomson Reuters in data/compliance. The Risk & Compliance product (15% growth) and specialized energy data create recurring-revenue moats in niches where customer switching costs are high.

**HarperCollins** benefits from scale as #2 globally, with backlist providing predictable cash flow.

The business mix is shifting toward segments with structural advantages (REA Group, Dow Jones professional products) and away from structurally challenged operations (News Media print, Foxtel).

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Traffic Cannibalization (Moderate-High):** Generative AI chatbots trained on News Corp content can reduce direct traffic to digital properties. The Sun's traffic decline from platform algorithm changes demonstrates this vulnerability. Licensing revenue partially offsets but may not fully compensate for lost advertising and subscription revenue if AI answers replace visits.

**2. U.S. Housing Market Weakness (Moderate):** Move/Realtor.com depends on transaction volumes. While Q2 FY2026 showed recovery (lead volumes +13% YoY, revenue +10%), the segment remains sensitive to elevated mortgage rates and affordability constraints. NAR rule changes on cooperative compensation create additional uncertainty.

**3. Murdoch Family Governance (Structural):** Dual-class structure prevents activist pressure or acquisition premium realization. Lachlan Murdoch's dual role as News Corp Chair and FOX CEO creates inherent conflicts. This is the primary reason the conglomerate discount persists and may never fully close.

**4. Goodwill Impairment Risk:** $6.3B in goodwill + intangibles. Any write-down at Move (due to housing weakness) would mechanically reduce book value, though it wouldn't affect cash flow.

**5. Short Interest (Approximately 14% of float):** Elevated short positioning suggests institutional skepticism. The short thesis likely centers on earnings quality concerns (one-time gains flattering results) and structural media headwinds. Any positive catalyst could trigger short covering.

**6. Print Revenue Decline:** Structurally permanent. The mitigation is cost restructuring and digital transition, but News Media segment economics remain under pressure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($36-40, +19% to +32%)

The post-Foxtel News Corp realizes value through buyback execution, AI licensing scales as additional platforms sign deals, U.S. housing recovers in 2027 as rates moderate, and REA Group continues compounding at 15-20% earnings growth. At 28x FY2027E earnings ($1.25) = $35 NWS. Add any narrowing of the conglomerate discount (perhaps through further asset sales or simplification) and upside extends toward $40.

### Bear Case ($24-26, -14% to -21%)

AI traffic cannibalization accelerates faster than licensing revenue can compensate, destroying WSJ subscription growth. U.S. housing remains frozen through FY2027, requiring Move impairment. News Media print deterioration accelerates. Murdoch governance prevents efficient capital return. Earnings stagnate at $1.00-1.05. At 24x depressed earnings = $24-25.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $35 — 28x FY2027E EPS of $1.25 (modest premium to peer median justified by REA Group asset value). 16% upside over 12-18 months.

**Bull case target:** $40 — Conglomerate discount narrows as buybacks accelerate and AI licensing scales. 32% upside.

**Bear case target:** $25 — 20% downside if housing weakness persists and AI cannibalization accelerates without offsetting licensing revenue.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q3 FY2026 earnings (May 2026), Q4/full-year FY2026 results (August 2026), additional AI licensing announcements, buyback execution pace, U.S. housing transaction data.

**Upside/downside from current ($30.23):** +16%/+32% upside vs. -17% downside. Asymmetric at approximately 2:1 on base case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Buyback execution falls below $300M annualized (signals management unwillingness to return capital)
2. Dow Jones digital subscription growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (AI cannibalization winning)
3. REA Group market share drops below 3.5x vs. nearest competitor (monopoly erosion)

## Conclusion

News Corp at $30.23 offers a quantifiable sum-of-parts discount with a meaningful SOTP gap between estimated NAV (~$37-40/share) and market price. The post-Foxtel portfolio generates $571M FCF with approximately $1.0B in remaining buyback capacity, providing a mechanism for value realization over 12-18 months. Estimate revisions are positive (+5.4% over 90 days for FY2027), the earnings beat rate is strong (3/4 quarters), and the business mix is shifting toward higher-quality recurring revenue streams.

The risks — AI cannibalization, Murdoch governance, and potential housing market weakness — are substantially reflected in the elevated short interest and the persistent conglomerate discount. If even modest discount narrowing occurs through buybacks and AI licensing execution, the upside materially exceeds downside scenarios.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% position sizing. The conglomerate discount and governance concerns prevent maximum conviction, but the valuation gap and catalyst path (buybacks + AI licensing + earnings growth) justify a position.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The original SOTP thesis relied on an erroneous $5.6B market cap; against the corrected ~$16B combined market cap, the REA stake alone does not exceed News Corp's market cap, the FCF yield is ~3.6% (not 10.2%), and buyback capacity is ~6-7% of market cap (not 23%). The SOTP discount remains meaningful (~20-30%) but is narrower than originally presented, and the margin of safety supporting the recommendation is reduced.
