**Research Date: August 10, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nicolet Bankshares is a $15.4 billion-asset commercial and agricultural bank holding company in Green Bay, Wisconsin, that on February 13, 2026 closed the acquisition of MidWestOne Financial Group — a $6.1 billion-asset Iowa institution roughly equal in size to Nicolet's prior nine acquisitions combined. Six months on, the integration is ahead of the announced financial plan in one respect and behind it in another. Tangible book value per share rose from $59.09 at December 31, 2025 to $62.19 at June 30, 2026 (+5.2%) despite $62.6 million of buybacks executed at roughly 2.4x tangible book and AOCI deterioration to −$37.0 million, against announced guidance of "mildly dilutive to tangible book value per share." Against that, the promised expense synergies are not in the run rate: adjusting Q2 noninterest expense for $7.4 million of merger charges and a $5.0 million loss on early redemption of junior subordinated debentures, and stripping the 23bp of purchase-accounting accretion out of revenue, core efficiency was approximately 54.6% versus a legacy run rate near 51%. Management's own language — "begin fully realizing our planned cost savings... restore the high level of profitability" — concedes the point.

The valuation question is therefore narrow and answerable. At the frozen basis of $171.56, Nicolet trades at 14.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $12.05 and 12.8x FY2027 consensus of $13.42, both below the 15.0x regional-bank peer median, with materially better profitability (FY2025 ROA 1.68%, Q2 2026 core ROA 1.69%, core ROTCE 21.6%, versus typical peer ROA of 1.1–1.3%). On tangible book, however, 2.76x is a full premium multiple that only holds if sustainable ROTCE stays near 20%. Backing out accretion (~$6.3 million after tax per quarter, ~1.9 percentage points of ROTCE), adding the ~$23.5 million annualized pre-tax expense gap still to be closed, and deducting Durbin interchange loss and the tax-rate reset, sustainable ROTCE plausibly lands in the 19–21% range, which supports the current multiple without leaving much cushion.

We rate the shares HOLD. The 12-month thesis is positive and catalyst-linked: the systems conversion, closing of the Denver branch divestiture in Q3, and a $150 million buyback authorization equal to 4.2% of market capitalization. But the stock sits within 3% of its all-time high after a 37.7% twelve-month advance, consensus FY2027 estimates already embed a chunk of the synergy, and the specific risks that would break the multiple (conversion disruption, agricultural credit, unverified post-merger capital and CRE concentration ratios) are not priced. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 6–7% including the 0.84% dividend does not clear the bar for new capital.

## Company Overview

Nicolet National Bank is an OCC-chartered commercial and agricultural community bank operating 114 branches across Wisconsin, Michigan, Minnesota and now Iowa, with a small Naples, Florida presence and Denver branches under contract for sale. The franchise is decidedly commercial: at December 31, 2025, 77% of loans were commercial-based, with agriculture the single largest segment at 21% of loans ($1.415 billion), ahead of C&I at 20%. Item 1A of the FY2025 10-K ties the agricultural book explicitly to dairy farm profitability. Fee businesses — wealth management ($29.6 million in FY2025), mortgage banking ($12.1 million), card interchange ($14.6 million) and deposit service charges ($8.0 million) — produced 21.8% of FY2025 revenue, a respectable but not differentiating mix.

Two structural features distinguish the earnings model. First, a July 2023 Wisconsin statute exempts from state taxable income interest and fees earned on loans to existing Wisconsin business and agricultural borrowers, which moved the assumed effective tax rate from roughly 25% to roughly 19.5%; the FY2025 effective rate was 19.4%. Second, the company is a serial acquirer, with ten bank deals completed between 2012 and 2025 and MidWestOne the eleventh. Management links the equity's premium valuation directly to that record.

Post-merger scale: $15.415 billion of assets, $10.848 billion of loans, $12.523 billion of deposits, 21.06 million shares outstanding at June 30, 2026. The loan/deposit ratio improved to 86.6% from 88.4% because MidWestOne arrived deposit-rich ($4.4 billion of loans against $5.3 billion of deposits).

## Financial Analysis

**Pre-merger trajectory.** Adjusted diluted EPS compounded from $6.72 (2023) to $7.83 (2024) to $9.82 (2025), roughly 21% annualized. The driver was margin, not balance-sheet growth: NIM expanded 58bp over two years to 3.76% while loans grew only 3% in 2025 and 4% in 2024. The 2025 NIM gain of 29bp came from a 27bp decline in the cost of interest-bearing liabilities to 2.76% plus a 14bp rise in loan yield to 6.19% — liability repricing as the Fed cut 75bp in 2H2025, not funding-mix improvement. Average noninterest-bearing demand deposits were flat at $1.754 billion in 2025 versus $1.755 billion in 2024 and down from $2.055 billion in 2023, which is the more sober read on deposit-franchise quality.

**Post-merger quarter.** Q2 2026 produced GAAP net income of $56.9 million ($2.62 diluted) and core EPS of $2.99, with NII of $141.5 million, noninterest income of $36.3 million, NIM of 4.14% and provision of $1.5 million. Three adjustments matter for anyone building a run rate:

1. *Accretion.* The release discloses 23bp of purchase-accounting accretion in Q2 NIM (18bp in Q1). On $13.86 billion of average earning assets that is roughly $8.0 million pre-tax, ~$6.3 million after tax at the 21.5% rate, or approximately $0.29 per share — about 9.6% of core EPS. This declines as acquired loans pay down.
2. *Efficiency.* Reported 58.62% includes $7.4 million of merger expense and a $5.0 million debenture-redemption loss. Ex-items, core efficiency is approximately 52.1%; ex-items and ex-accretion, approximately 54.6%. The gap to the legacy ~51% run rate is worth roughly $5.9 million per quarter pre-tax, or about $0.85 per share annually after tax.
3. *Tax.* The Q2 effective rate of 21.50% versus FY2025's 19.4% costs approximately $1.5 million per quarter, ~$0.28 per share annually. Iowa and Minnesota income sits outside the Wisconsin exemption, so directionally this is permanent, though a single quarter's rate is noisy.

**Credit.** Net charge-offs remain immaterial: $1.8 million in FY2025 (0.03% of average loans) and $0.65 million in Q2 2026 (0.02% annualized). Nonaccruals rose from $31.7 million to $71.5 million and NPAs from 0.35% to 0.49% of assets across the merger, with ACL/loans rising from 1.01% to 1.23%. H1 provision of only $7.55 million understates the reserve build: the allowance grew $64.8 million against $7.55 million of provision and $1.48 million of charge-offs, a ~$58.7 million gap created through purchase accounting on purchased credit-deteriorated loans. That is expected CECL mechanics rather than an earnings-quality problem, but it means the low provision line carries no information about the credit cycle. Pre-merger "potential problem loans" (substandard but accruing) were $71 million at year-end 2025 versus $68 million a year earlier — more than double reported nonperforming loans and about 1.04% of loans. That figure is stable and disclosed, and is not evidence of a problem, but the post-merger figure is not available.

**Capital and liquidity.** TCE/tangible assets fell from 9.94% to 9.06% as $467 million of goodwill was created; goodwill and intangibles of $961.7 million now equal 42.3% of $2,271.5 million of total equity. Holding-company CET1 was 12.0% at December 31, 2025; post-merger regulatory ratios are not in the earnings release and constitute a verification gap on a bank that just doubled in size. Pre-merger liquidity availability was $2.675 billion, of which $1.352 billion was brokered — a price-sensitive wholesale source. Uninsured deposits were 32% of the total at year-end 2025; the noninterest-bearing share of deposits fell from 23.7% to 21.7% across the merger. FY2025 operating cash flow of $154 million against $150.7 million of net income is clean 1.0x conversion.

**A data reconciliation.** The supplied profile shows trailing P/E of 19.89 alongside EPS of $9.78, which cannot both be true at $171.56 (that price is 17.5x $9.78). The 19.89x is consistent with a trailing-four-quarter GAAP EPS near $8.63, which is what results once the H1 2026 merger charges ($48.1 million pre-tax, roughly $1.74 per share after tax) and the $5 million debenture loss are included. Both figures are backward-looking artifacts of merger accounting; forward consensus is the correct valuation basis and is used throughout below.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus (five analysts) has FY2026 at $12.05 and FY2027 at $13.42, revised up 1.3% and 1.0% respectively over 90 days and 1.0% and 1.1% over 30 days. The current-quarter estimate was trimmed 1.7% to $3.04 while the next quarter was raised 2.0% to $3.25 — consistent with modelers pushing synergy realization one quarter to the right rather than reducing it. This is positive but modest revision momentum; it is not the +30% velocity that signals a systematic underestimate.

The company has beaten in each of the last four quarters (+5.9%, +2.8%, +23.2%, +15.4%). The two large beats were pre-merger quarters when models lagged NIM expansion, and the Q2 "revenue beat" of $36.8 million reported by third-party summaries reflects sell-side models not yet carrying a full quarter of merged revenue rather than operating outperformance — reported revenue of $177.75 million ties exactly to NII plus noninterest income. The recent 5.9% and 2.8% beats are the better guide to forward visibility: management delivers, but not by wide margins now that the base is understood.

Growth sources for the next 12–18 months, in order of confidence:

- **Expense synergy post-conversion.** The core system conversion, deliberately delayed roughly six months to late summer 2026 (working with Fiserv, with MidWestOne branches operating as a division in the interim), gates the residual savings. The arithmetic bridge to the legacy efficiency ratio is ~$23.5 million annualized pre-tax, ~$0.85 per share. Consensus FY2027 of $13.42 versus Q2 core annualized of $11.96 implies roughly $1.46 of improvement, so a meaningful portion of this is already in estimates.
- **Balance-sheet remix.** Management cites a shift toward higher-yielding in-market commercial loans funded by lower-cost core deposits, plus mid-single-digit organic growth, with new Iowa and Minnesota capacity.
- **Capital return.** $60 million of buyback authorization added January 20, 2026 and a further $150 million with Q2 results, against a 12% payout ratio on core EPS. At the current price a full $150 million retires roughly 4% of shares.
- **Denver divestiture.** ~$402 million of loans and ~$388 million of deposits sold to Sunwest Bank, regulatory approval received, expected to close in Q3 2026. Modestly EPS-dilutive on lost spread, capital-accretive, and the gain/loss and deposit premium are undisclosed.

Offsets are specific and quantifiable: accretion fade (~$1.15 per share annualized at the Q2 rate, declining), Durbin interchange loss (the 10-K frames ">$5 million" against pre-merger interchange of $14.6 million; post-merger interchange annualizes near $25.3 million, so the dollar impact is plausibly larger, with timing most likely from mid-2027), the ~$0.28 tax-rate reset, and ~$24.6 million per year of new intangible amortization that depresses GAAP EPS and ROA without touching tangible book or cash earnings. Interest-rate posture cuts the other way against management's assumption: the FY2025 simulation shows a gradual 100bp decline reducing NII by 2.0% and 200bp by 3.8%, so the current higher-for-longer policy setting is a modest positive relative to the two 25bp cuts embedded in management's outlook, though the post-merger sensitivity table has not been disclosed.

Peers: FIBK 11.6x, FFBC 11.9x, PRK 18.2x, CBU 15.0x, BANF 15.3x. Nicolet's forward multiple sits below the median while delivering roughly 40–50% higher ROA, which is the strongest argument for the shares. The dividend yield of 0.84% versus a 3.0% peer median reflects a deliberate choice to return capital through repurchase (FY2025: 646,002 shares for $76.6 million at ~$118.50; H1 2026: 416,809 shares for $62.6 million at ~$150.29) rather than a coverage problem.

The tangible-book multiple is where the debate resolves. Using a simple sustainable-value framework — P/TBV ≈ (ROTCE − g) / (COE − g) — at 20% ROTCE, 5% growth and a 10% cost of equity, fair value is roughly 3.0x TBV, above the current 2.76x. At 15% ROTCE the same framework gives 2.0x, or roughly $124 per share on current tangible book. The valuation therefore rests on whether ROTCE holds near 20%. Our reconstruction: Q2 core ROTCE of 21.6% less ~1.9 points of accretion contribution, plus roughly 1.4 points from closing the efficiency gap, less roughly 0.4 points for Durbin, lands at 19–21%. That supports 2.6–2.9x TBV, meaning the shares are approximately fairly valued with modest positive skew rather than mispriced.

Base case: $190, derived as 14.2x FY2027 consensus of $13.42 — holding today's forward multiple constant and letting earnings do the work — which cross-checks to roughly 2.7x an estimated mid-2027 TBVPS near $70. That is +10.7% from $171.56. Bull case: full synergy plus mid-single-digit organic growth takes the run rate to $14.50–15.00, and a 15x multiple (peer median, justified by superior returns) gives approximately $220, +28%. Bear case: conversion disruption, agricultural credit migration, accretion fading faster than savings arrive, EPS stalling near $11 with the multiple compressing toward 11.5–12x (where FIBK and FFBC trade) or 2.0x TBV, gives approximately $130, −24%.

Analyst targets span $185–$200 with a mean of $191.8 and median of $194 — all above spot, with 3 strong buys, 1 buy and 2 holds. On a stock at 12.8x forward with rising estimates, targets are more likely to be raised than defended, but a $191.8 mean implies only 11.8% upside, so consensus itself is not signaling a large gap to fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

Nicolet's competitive claim is operational rather than structural: management asserts top-decile ROA and ROTCE among US banks, and the FY2025 figures (1.68% ROA, 18.53% ROTCE, 51.2% efficiency) are consistent with that. Positioning is lead-local across smaller Wisconsin, Iowa, eastern Minnesota and northern Michigan communities, with a challenger position in the greater Twin Cities — the one genuinely competitive metro in the footprint. The Wisconsin loan-interest tax exemption is a real and durable per-share advantage, but the Iowa/Minnesota expansion dilutes it, visible already in the move from a 19.4% FY2025 rate to 21.5% in Q2.

Management's strategic argument for the deal is the most credible part of the competitive case: crossing $10 billion in assets imposes fixed compliance costs and a Durbin revenue haircut that a bank must earn back, and clearing that threshold with a single $6 billion transaction spreads those costs over a much larger base immediately rather than grinding across them. The counter is that a $6 billion acquisition brings its own integration and fixed-cost burden, and the company simultaneously blew through $15 billion, losing trust-preferred Tier 1 treatment and paying $5 million to redeem the debentures.

Two facts argue against over-crediting the franchise. Five-year cumulative TSR per the structured proxy data is 189 for Nicolet against 211 for its peer group — the stock has underperformed its peers over five years despite top-decile operating metrics, which most plausibly reflects a demanding starting multiple compressing rather than an operating shortfall, but is nonetheless the record. And the fee mix at 21.8% of revenue, with wealth management now facing explicitly disclosed AI/robo-advisor competition, does not provide a structural revenue moat. The 10-K's newly added risk factors on stablecoins and the GENIUS Act reshaping deposit intermediation are forward-looking rather than present exposures, but they touch precisely the core-deposit funding advantage on which the model depends.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and conversion (highest near-term).** The core system conversion is scheduled for late summer 2026, six months later than originally planned. Cost savings are back-loaded and unproven. Merger charges have already reached $50.0 million pre-tax, ~4.9% of the ~$1.031 billion of recorded consideration. Item 1A names cultural assimilation specifically as an "often-critical failure point." A botched conversion produces customer attrition and expense overruns simultaneously, and consensus FY2027 already assumes the savings arrive.

**Agricultural and dairy concentration.** Agriculture fell from 21% to 16.3% of loans only because MidWestOne diluted the mix; the absolute balance grew 24.8% to $1.766 billion. It accounted for 33% of year-end 2025 nonaccruals against a 14% ACL allocation — the worst-performing segment relative to its reserve. Exposure against tangible common equity improved from 162% to roughly 135%. Transmission channels are dairy commodity cycles and trade policy, both explicitly flagged, and both live risks in the current tariff environment. SBA/FSA guarantees covered 33% of year-end nonaccruals, which mitigates severity.

**Credit level reset with unverified detail.** Nonaccruals more than doubled to $71.5 million with the acquisition. The most likely explanation is acquired problem credits marked on day one; the release does not split nonaccruals between legacy and acquired books, and the pre-merger $71 million substandard-accruing bucket has no post-merger disclosure. Post-merger CRE concentration versus the 100%/300% regulatory guidance thresholds cannot be recomputed without post-merger total risk-based capital: CRE-investment plus construction grew to $2,901 million from $1,515 million, and while the pre-merger ratio of 137% was comfortable, this is a metric regulators watch and it is currently unverifiable.

**Regulatory and revenue headwinds.** Durbin interchange loss of ">$5 million" framed at pre-merger scale, plausibly larger against ~$25.3 million of annualized post-merger interchange, most likely effective from mid-2027. CFPB examination authority and enhanced supervision now apply. The permanent tax-rate drift is quantified above.

**Balance-sheet composition.** Goodwill and intangibles at 42.3% of total equity mean a substantial share of book equity is non-loss-absorbing. TCE/TA of 9.06% is adequate but no longer a fortress, and post-merger CET1 is undisclosed.

**Governance.** Michael Daniels holds the combined Chairman/President/CEO roles. Structured proxy data shows 2025 CEO total compensation of $14.94 million versus $3.21 million in 2024, while average NEO compensation fell to $1.047 million from $1.909 million. We cannot determine whether this is a Summary Compensation Table figure (implying a one-time mega-grant equal to ~9.9% of FY2025 net income for one executive) or "Compensation Actually Paid" under pay-versus-performance rules, which marks unvested equity to market and would inflate mechanically given 2025 share appreciation without any new grant. Until resolved, this is a governance flag of undetermined magnitude, and we draw no inference about motive. Anti-takeover architecture is extensive — supermajority merger mechanics, directors removable only for cause, blank-check preferred, Wisconsin business-combination statute — which removes takeout optionality from the valuation.

**Insider activity.** Across the most recent 25 Form 4 filings there were no open-market purchases. Activity comprised annual director grants (429 shares each at $139.63 on May 19, 2026), tax-withholding dispositions, the CEO's two gift-coded transfers recording matching acquisitions and dispositions (the signature of a trust transfer, not a sale, leaving 65,389 direct shares), an EVP selling 1,557 shares near $168–172 out of ~33,300 held, and the founder-director's option exercise with a 2,662-share sale. This is routine, small-size, low-signal activity — not bearish, but absent of buying while management publicly argues the stock trades below intrinsic value. The company is buying at ~$150 average; individuals are not.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.6% sits below one-year realized volatility of 30.6% (a −3.9% spread), skew is call-heavy at −5.0%, and put/call open interest is 0.02 — effectively no put protection outstanding. Term structure is in backwardation (26.6% at 10 days versus 22.4% at 157 days) with a ±3.8% implied move on the August 21 expiry, but with only 103 contracts across four expirations the chain is too thin to carry much information, and there is no scheduled catalyst before the October earnings report. Options positioning shows complacency about downside rather than confirmation of the bull case: cheap near-term vol makes protection inexpensive for holders, and the total absence of put open interest means any credit or conversion surprise would meet an unhedged shareholder base. Short interest of 5.5% of float at 2.5 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $220 (+28%)

The conversion completes in Q3/Q4 2026 without material customer attrition, and the ~$23.5 million annualized pre-tax expense gap closes, restoring core efficiency to roughly 51% and lifting the run rate toward $14.50–15.00 per share. Nicolet's demonstrated ability to close a same-size acquisition in 113 days and deliver tangible book accretion where it guided dilution is treated by the market as evidence the eleventh deal will behave like the prior ten. The Denver sale closes with a premium, capital rebuilds quickly (management expects exactly this), and the $150 million authorization retires ~4% of shares at a price still below the company's own intrinsic-value estimate. Higher-for-longer rates favor an asset-sensitive balance sheet against management's two-cut assumption, adding NII versus plan. Applying the 15.0x peer median to $14.75 gives $221; a 3.0x multiple on ~$72 forward tangible book gives $216. Credit stays where the last four years put it — 0.02–0.03% net charge-offs.

### Bear Case — $130 (−24%)

The conversion disrupts service in newly acquired Iowa and Minnesota markets, deposits leak, and expense savings arrive a year late while purchase-accounting accretion (currently ~$1.15 per share annualized) fades on schedule. Agricultural credit deteriorates as dairy margins compress and tariffs bite, moving nonaccruals from 0.66% of loans toward 1.5% and forcing provision into the $30–40 million range — a step that would be invisible in the reported provision line until it hits, given how much of the current reserve was built through purchase accounting rather than the income statement. Durbin removes $6–10 million of interchange from mid-2027, the effective tax rate settles above 21.5%, and EPS stalls near $11. The multiple compresses to the 11.5–12x where FIBK and FFBC trade, or 2.0x tangible book, giving $127–$132. The five-year TSR record (189 versus a 211 peer index) shows this stock can carry excellent operating metrics and still underperform when the entry multiple is premium.

### Which Way We Lean

The bull and bear cases are both credible, and the probability-weighted arithmetic — roughly 50% base at +10.7%, 25% bull at +28%, 25% bear at −24%, plus 0.84% yield — produces expected return near 6–7%. That is a fair return, not an asymmetric one, and it is achieved by paying an all-time-high price for an integration whose decisive event has not yet occurred. Financial-sector holdings of this profile have historically delivered index-like returns unless there is a distinct company-specific catalyst priced at a discount; here the catalyst exists but the discount does not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should maintain exposure through the conversion; new capital should wait for either evidence or a better price.

**Base case target: $190** — 14.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $13.42, holding the current forward multiple constant, cross-checked at ~2.7x an estimated mid-2027 TBVPS of ~$70. Upside from $171.56: +10.7%.

**Bull case target: $220** — 15.0x a post-synergy run rate of $14.75 (peer-median multiple on demonstrated top-quartile returns). Upside: +28.2%.

**Bear case target: $130** — 11.8x a stalled $11.00 EPS, or 2.0x current tangible book. Downside: −24.2%.

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to three dated events — the Q3 2026 report (October, first read on post-conversion expenses), the Q4 2026/FY2026 report (January 2027, first clean quarter of the merged run rate plus full-year effective tax rate), and Durbin's likely effective date around mid-2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 core efficiency ratio at or below 52% excluding both merger charges and purchase-accounting accretion, confirming the ~$23.5 million synergy is in the run rate.
2. Disclosure of post-merger regulatory capital showing CET1 at or above 11% and CRE-plus-construction concentration comfortably below the 300% guidance threshold, removing the two most material verification gaps.
3. Price toward $150–155 (roughly 2.4x current tangible book, where the company itself repurchased $62.6 million of stock in H1 2026) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 25% upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Conversion delayed beyond Q4 2026, or evidence of post-conversion deposit attrition — specifically total deposits declining more than 2% sequentially ex-Denver.
2. Net charge-offs exceeding 0.20% annualized, nonaccrual loans exceeding 0.90% of loans, or any disclosed increase in the substandard-accruing bucket beyond roughly 1.5% of loans, with agricultural detail the specific item to check.
3. Suspension or material slowdown of the $150 million repurchase program, which would signal that capital is being retained for credit or regulatory reasons rather than deployed at management's stated intrinsic-value discount.
4. Resolution of the CEO compensation question in favor of a discretionary one-time grant of ~$12 million with no performance conditions, which would materially change the governance assessment.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release (expected late October 2026), which should contain the first post-conversion expense data and, if the 10-Q is read alongside it, the post-merger capital and concentration ratios.

## Conclusion

Nicolet has executed the largest transaction in its history faster than deals of this size normally close, and produced a tangible-book-accretive outcome where it had guided to mild dilution — because the exchange ratio was fixed, goodwill is excluded from tangible equity, and 75bp of Fed cuts between announcement and closing shrank the negative fair-value marks on MidWestOne's loans and securities. That is a real credit to the operating team, and the resulting franchise earns a 1.7% ROA and roughly 20% ROTCE at a 14.2x forward multiple, below the 15.0x peer median. The company is buying its own stock aggressively, the payout ratio is 12%, and the analyst target range of $185–200 sits entirely above spot.

What the price does not offer is a margin of safety. At 2.76x tangible book, the multiple requires ROTCE to stay near 20% through the fade of ~$0.29 per quarter of purchase-accounting accretion, a permanent 200bp tax-rate reset, a Durbin interchange haircut plausibly larger than the ">$5 million" framed at pre-merger scale, and the successful execution of a systems conversion that management deliberately delayed six months and describes with the words "begin" and "restore." Two verification gaps compound this: post-merger regulatory capital and post-merger CRE concentration are both undisclosed, on a bank that just doubled its investor-CRE-plus-construction book to $2.9 billion. Nonaccruals more than doubled with the acquisition, and because roughly $58.7 million of the reserve build came through purchase accounting rather than the income statement, the small $7.55 million H1 provision tells us nothing about the credit cycle.

HOLD. Own it through the conversion for the earnings step-up that consensus already partly anticipates; add on a decline into the $150–155 range where the company itself was a buyer, or on a Q3/Q4 print that puts core efficiency back at 52% ex-accretion.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats Q2 core EPS as a clean run rate. Synergy delivery replaces fade rather than adding to it.

### Catalysts

- **High — Oct 2026 to Jan 2027:** Core systems conversion completing late summer 2026; Q3/Q4 core efficiency at or below 52% ex-merger charges and ex-accretion would confirm ~$23.5M annualized synergy in the run rate.
- **Medium — Q3-Q4 2026:** $150M repurchase authorization (~4% of shares) at a 12% payout ratio, plus the Denver branch divestiture (~$402M loans) closing in Q3 2026, both capital-accretive.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **Jan 2027:** Conversion slips beyond Q4 2026, or core efficiency ex-accretion fails to reach ~52% by the Q4 2026 print, indicating the synergy is not arriving.
- **Any quarter:** Net charge-offs above 0.20% annualized or nonaccruals above 0.90% of loans, particularly with agricultural detail deteriorating.
- **Q3 2026 onward:** Suspension or material slowdown of the $150M repurchase, signaling capital retained for credit or regulatory reasons.
