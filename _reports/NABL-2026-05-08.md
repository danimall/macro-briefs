## Executive Summary

N-able is a cybersecurity and IT management software company serving managed service providers (MSPs), trading at $5.15 with a $942M market cap. The stock has declined 43% from its 52-week high of $9.04, driven by a Q4 2025 earnings miss (-41% vs. consensus), decelerating revenue growth (13.5% → 9.7%), and negative estimate revisions (-12% over 90 days). The company generates $75M in annual free cash flow on $511M revenue, implying a 12.5x FCF multiple — optically cheap for a subscription software business with 99% recurring revenue.

However, the discount appears partially justified by structural factors: goodwill of $1.024B exceeds the company's market capitalization, indicating potential impairment risk; net revenue retention has declined from 110% to 103%; Thoma Bravo simultaneously owns both N-able and direct competitor ConnectWise; and revenue growth (9-10%) lags the estimated market growth rate (14%), suggesting share loss. The 2026 EBITDA recovery guide of $167-171M (from $153M in 2025) provides a near-term catalyst, but without clear evidence of competitive momentum improvement, confidence in sustained re-acceleration is limited.

## Company Overview

N-able provides a subscription-based platform for MSPs with three core capabilities: unified endpoint management (RMM), security operations (EDR/XDR/MDR), and data protection as a service. The company was spun off from SolarWinds in July 2021 and serves over 500,000 businesses through its channel partners. ARR stood at $539.7M as of December 2025, growing 11.9% YoY. Revenue is split approximately 50/50 between U.S. and international markets, with 1,852 employees (79% outside the U.S.).

The November 2024 Adlumin acquisition added AI-powered XDR/MDR capabilities, creating a more complete security platform but also driving significant one-time costs and margin compression in FY2025. Silver Lake and Thoma Bravo collectively control approximately 60% of outstanding shares.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** Revenue grew from $371.8M (2022) to $511.4M (2025), representing growth of approximately 38% over three years. Growth is decelerating on a year-over-year basis: 13.5% → 10.5% → 9.7% over the last three years. Management guides 2026 ARR of $581-586M, implying 8-9% growth — continued deceleration.

**Profitability:** FY2025 was explicitly an investment year. GAAP operating income collapsed from $82.4M (17.7% margin) to $36.8M (7.2%), driven by $18.2M in Adlumin transaction costs, $16.9M in acquired technology amortization, and aggressive headcount investment (+20% in S&M, +11% in R&D). Adjusted EBITDA fell from $169.4M (36.3% margin) to $153.2M (30.0%). Net income swung from +$31M to -$17M.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow improved 17% to $93.2M despite GAAP losses, reflecting the non-cash nature of the margin compression (amortization, SBC). Free cash flow of $75.1M (14.7% margin) demonstrates underlying business health. At the current market cap of $942M, FCF yield is approximately 8%.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $111.8M (primarily international) against $393.9M in variable-rate debt (6.59%). Net debt of ~$282M represents approximately 1.8x adjusted EBITDA. The revolving facility ($60M) remains undrawn. Altman Z-Score of 2.07 places the company in the "grey zone" — not distressed but not comfortable given variable-rate debt.

**Critical Balance Sheet Issue:** Goodwill of $1.024B represents 73% of total assets and exceeds the company's entire market capitalization. While a quantitative impairment test in October 2025 showed fair value exceeding carrying value, the stock has declined materially since that date. A goodwill impairment charge is increasingly probable if the stock remains depressed, though this would be non-cash.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are underwhelming relative to the addressable market. N-able's revenue growth of 9-10% against an estimated TAM growing at 14% annually indicates market share loss. Net revenue retention declining from 110% to 103% confirms limited organic expansion within the existing customer base.

Positive growth signals include: customers with >$50K ARR growing 14% YoY (reaching 2,671 and representing 61% of total ARR), the Adlumin XDR/MDR platform creating cross-sell opportunities, and strong industry tailwinds (IT spending for SMBs expected to reach $2.2T in 2026, growing to $2.6T by 2028).

Negative signals dominate: estimate revisions are running -12% over 90 days across all periods, the Q4 2025 earnings miss (-41% surprise) broke a beat streak, and the Long-Term Contract Initiative — while improving predictability — compresses NRR through volume discounts. Current quarter EPS estimate of $0.12 is down from $0.13 ninety days ago, with full-year FY2026 estimates down from $0.49 to $0.43.

## Valuation Assessment

At $5.15, NABL trades at approximately:
- 12.5x trailing FCF ($75M)
- 1.75x trailing ARR ($540M)
- ~5.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA ($167-171M mid)
- Negative trailing P/E (GAAP loss in FY2025)
- Forward P/E of ~12x on FY2026 consensus EPS of $0.43

Against peers: Progress Software (PRGS) at 14.4x, Qualys (QLYS) at 16.3x, UiPath (PATH) at 20.2x — NABL trades at a meaningful discount on most metrics. However, NABL's growth rate is lower than several peers, and the governance/control structure and competitive position issues partially justify the discount.

The valuation is genuinely cheap on a cash flow basis — 8% FCF yield is unusual for a subscription software business with 99% recurring revenue. B. Riley's $10 price target implies the market is mispricing the company by approximately 94%. The consensus mean target of $6.95 implies 35% upside. However, the current price ($5.15) sits below the analyst low target of $5.25, and one analyst maintains a Strong Sell — suggesting at least one institutional voice sees further downside.

I lean toward the discount reflecting structural impairment (governance, competitive erosion, PE exit overhang) more than mispricing, given declining NRR and estimate revisions, but acknowledge the FCF support provides a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

N-able's competitive position is deteriorating. Key evidence:

1. Revenue growth (9-10%) lagging market growth (14%) indicates share loss
2. NinjaOne, with modern UX and $250M+ in venture funding, is aggressively taking MSP share
3. Kaseya offers heavily discounted bundles that pressure N-able's pricing
4. ConnectWise (owned by Thoma Bravo, N-able's own controlling shareholder) is a direct competitor — creating an unprecedented conflict of interest

N-able's strategic response — AI-powered security via Adlumin, DRaaS, Google Backup integration — is directionally correct but hasn't yet arrested the NRR decline or translated into accelerating growth. The shift toward "cyber resilience" positioning broadens the addressable market but also positions N-able against better-resourced pure-play security vendors (CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Arctic Wolf).

The Long-Term Contract Initiative improves revenue predictability but simultaneously locks customers into committed spend levels that limit expansion — a classic retention-at-the-cost-of-expansion tradeoff.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill Impairment (High Probability):** With goodwill at $1.024B vs. market cap at $942M, an impairment charge is mathematically likely unless the stock recovers significantly. While non-cash, it would confirm value destruction from the SolarWinds separation structure and Adlumin acquisition, potentially triggering additional selling.

**PE Sponsor Exit Overhang:** Silver Lake and Thoma Bravo own ~60% of shares. These are financial sponsors with finite hold periods. Any secondary sale would represent massive supply relative to the stock's ~1.1M average daily volume.

**Thoma Bravo / ConnectWise Conflict:** The same PE firm owning both N-able and a direct competitor is a genuine governance red flag. Minority shareholders have limited recourse if strategic decisions favor one portfolio company over another.

**Variable-Rate Debt:** $394M at 6.59% = ~$26M annual interest. The November 2025 refinancing increased borrowings from $333M to $394M.

**Competitive Erosion:** NRR decline from 110% → 103% over two years, combined with growth below market rate, suggests a business losing share in a growing market. Without a clear product or execution inflection, this trajectory compresses the multiple.

**International Operations Risk:** R&D in Belarus (near conflict zone) and contractors in Ukraine create operational vulnerability. Tax exemption in Belarus extends through 2049 — any geopolitical disruption disrupts cost structure.

**No Insider Buying:** At 52-week lows with the stock down 43%, zero open-market insider purchases signal lack of internal conviction. All recent transactions are routine SBC grants and tax withholdings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8-10, +55-95%):** 2026 EBITDA recovery to $167-171M materializes, demonstrating 2025 was a transient investment year. Adlumin integration accelerates security revenue growth, lifting NRR back toward 105-107%. At 12-13x EBITDA (still below peer median), the stock re-rates to $8-10. PE sponsors initiate strategic review, attracting acquisition interest at 15-17x EBITDA (~$13-15/share).

**Bear Case ($3-4, -25-40%):** NRR continues declining toward 100% as NinjaOne and Kaseya take share. Goodwill impairment of $200-400M triggers negative headline. PE sponsors sell secondary block at discount. Variable-rate debt becomes constraining as business FCF declines. Stock de-rates to 8-9x FCF on deteriorating growth.

## Conclusion

N-able presents a genuine valuation conundrum: the stock is cheap on cash flow metrics (8% FCF yield, 1.75x ARR) for a subscription software business with 99% recurring revenue, but the cheapness reflects identifiable structural risks rather than temporary market inefficiency. Declining NRR, negative estimate revisions, PE governance conflicts, competitive share loss, and goodwill exceeding market cap collectively suggest "cheap for a reason" is the most likely explanation.

The specific near-term catalyst — 2026 EBITDA recovery to $167-171M — provides a defined point to monitor. If management delivers, the stock is materially undervalued. If NRR continues declining or estimates continue falling, the bear case gains probability. At $5.15, the risk/reward is approximately balanced, making HOLD the appropriate recommendation until the next earnings cycle provides clarity on execution trajectory.

There is a plausible path to $8-10 (Adlumin works, growth re-accelerates) and a plausible path to $3-4 (competitive erosion continues, governance issues persist). Insufficient evidence exists to make a high-conviction call in either direction.
