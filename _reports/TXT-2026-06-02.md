**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Textron is a multi-industry aerospace, defense, and industrial company (FY2025 revenue $14.8B) in the middle of a deliberate portfolio transformation. On April 30, 2026 management announced its intent to separate the Industrial segment (Kautex automotive fuel systems plus Textron Specialized Vehicles) via sale or tax-free spin, leaving a roughly $12B-revenue pure-play aerospace & defense company built around Textron Aviation, Bell, and Textron Systems. The strategic logic is straightforward: shed the 4.5%-margin, cyclical, capital-intensive auto/golf businesses and re-rate the remaining defense-and-aviation franchise toward defense-prime multiples. The stock currently trades at roughly 13.8x consensus current-year EPS ($6.51) and 12.3x next-year EPS ($7.30) versus a defense peer set that ranges from ~20x (HII) to ~34x (LHX).

The same week as the separation announcement, the U.S. Army flagged a $350M FY2026 funding shortfall on Bell's MV-75 (FLRAA/"Cheyenne") tiltrotor program, with Textron expecting to exhaust available FY2026 funds in Q3 2026 and a possible stop-work order if additional appropriations are not approved. MV-75 is the company's primary growth engine — it drove the bulk of 2025's revenue acceleration — yet it is currently dilutive to margins and carries a known $60-110M unfavorable cumulative catch-up charge at Low-Rate Initial Production award (late 2026/early 2027). Bell's Q1 2026 segment profit fell 20% year-over-year with margin down 250bps to 6.7%. The growth story and the near-term risk live in the same business unit.

We rate Textron a HOLD with a growth lean. The valuation is genuinely cheap with an identifiable re-rating catalyst, the balance sheet is solid, and a director's $989K open-market purchase the day after the separation announcement is a credible bullish signal. What prevents a BUY is the combination of a dated, near-term binary risk (the Q3 2026 stop-work possibility on the company's flagship program), near-term consensus EPS revisions that are falling rather than rising (current-quarter estimates cut 3.2% over 30 days; analyst sentiment trend formally "degrading"), and a bear case that carries roughly 17% downside toward the 52-week low. That downside profile exceeds the asymmetry we require to upgrade.

## Company Overview

Textron operates five reporting segments as of Q1 2026 (eAviation was folded into Aviation/Systems/Corporate effective January 4, 2026):

- **Textron Aviation (~40% of revenue):** Cessna Citation business jets, Beechcraft King Air turboprops, Caravan, and T-6/AT-6 military trainers, plus the largest aftermarket network in general aviation. FY25 revenue $5.96B at 11.7% margin; delivered 171 Citation jets and 146 turboprops in 2025.
- **Bell (~29%):** Military and commercial rotorcraft/tiltrotor. Programs include MV-75 (FLRAA, awarded December 2022), V-22 Osprey, and H-1, plus commercial 407/429/505/412 helicopters and the 525 Relentless in certification. FY25 revenue $4.28B; margin compressed to 8.5% from 10.3%.
- **Textron Systems (~8%):** Defense electronics, the Ship-to-Shore Connector, Aerosonde UAS, ATAC adversary air training, and Lycoming engines. FY25 revenue $1.25B at 14.0% margin, the company's highest.
- **Industrial (~22%) — to be separated:** Kautex (plastic fuel systems pivoting to EV battery enclosures and ADAS cleaning systems) and Textron Specialized Vehicles (E-Z-GO, Cushman, Jacobsen). FY25 revenue $3.21B at only 4.5% margin.
- **Finance (~0.5%):** Captive finance arm supporting aircraft sales; small, high-quality, near-zero nonaccruals.

Leadership transitioned on January 4, 2026: Lisa Atherton (former CEO of Bell and previously of Textron Systems) became President & CEO, with long-tenured Scott Donnelly (CEO since 2009) moving to Executive Chairman. The internal, defense-centric promotion aligns with the pure-play pivot.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily, with a clear acceleration in 2025:

| FY | Revenue | Net Income | Operating Cash Flow |
|----|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| 2022 | $12.87B | $861M | — |
| 2023 | $13.68B | $921M | $1.27B |
| 2024 | $13.70B | $824M | $1.02B (strike-impacted) |
| 2025 | $14.80B (+8%) | $921M | $1.31B |

2025 growth came from the Bell MV-75 ramp (+$570M military) and an Aviation recovery (+$671M) following the September-October 2024 machinists' strike; Industrial declined $302M on the Powersports divestiture and golf softness. Q1 2026 revenue rose 12% to $3.70B with diluted EPS of $1.25 (versus $1.13), Aviation up 22% with 26% profit growth, Industrial profit up 33% on restructuring, and Bell down 20% on V-22 wind-down and unfavorable military mix.

The margin picture is mixed and is the most important fundamental tension in the story. Consolidated gross margin slipped to 17.8% in 2025 from 18.0% in 2024 and 20.5% in 2023. Aviation and Systems margins expanded, but Bell — the growth driver — compressed to 8.5% and then to 6.7% in Q1 2026 as lower-margin MV-75 development volume and rising R&D outweigh program scale. The mechanism is the standard defense-development pattern: development and prototype work is largely cost-reimbursable or fixed-price-at-bid and runs below mature-production margins, so revenue growth ahead of LRIP transition mechanically dilutes segment profitability.

The balance sheet is healthy. Manufacturing net-debt/capital was 20% in Q1 2026, with $3.47B of manufacturing debt against ~$8.0B of equity and a current ratio of 1.82. The $1.0B revolver is undrawn and was refinanced to 2030 in October 2025; the company issued $1B of long-term notes across 2025 ($500M 4.95% due 2036, $500M 5.50% due 2035). FY25 free cash flow was roughly $930M, and operating cash flow has consistently exceeded net income, indicating decent earnings quality (cumulative catch-up adjustments were a modest net-favorable +$66M in 2025). The reported screening artifacts (Altman Z of 39, ROA of 105%) are revenue-normalization distortions and should be disregarded; the underlying balance sheet is solid but not extraordinary.

Capital allocation is buyback-driven: $822M in repurchases (10.7M shares) in 2025, a new 25M-share authorization in February 2026, and share count down from ~182M to ~174M. The dividend is a token ~$0.02/quarter (yield well under 0.1%), so buybacks are effectively the entire shareholder-return lever — relevant given the regulatory risk discussed below.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. Aviation is in a genuine recovery, with a refreshed product line (M2/CJ3/CJ4 Gen3 with Garmin Autoland for 2027, Beechcraft Denali turboprop entering service 2026) and the industry's largest aftermarket annuity. Bell offers the highest-ceiling growth via MV-75, a multi-decade program of record potentially worth tens of billions, with the company opening a 140,000 sq ft Wichita assembly center and exercising a $72M option to purchase the MV-75 manufacturing facility — concrete evidence of program acceleration. Backlog grew to $19.2B in Q1 2026, with Bell's MV-75 prototype phase ($1.3B award), Ship-to-Shore (+$480M), and ATAC Navy (+$475M) driving gains.

The growth quality, however, is questionable on two counts. First, the highest-growth revenue is currently the lowest-margin revenue: Bell is expanding the top line while compressing both its own and total-company margins, and the LRIP transition carries the known catch-up charge. Second, near-term consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 3.2% over 30 days and 3.3% over 90 days, and the analyst sentiment trend is formally classified as "degrading." Full-year estimates are roughly flat (+0.6% over 90 days) and next-year estimates are slightly negative (-0.7%). There is no rapidly rising estimate trajectory that would warrant treating the stock as a misunderstood acceleration story. The four-quarter beat track record (4-for-4, beats of 0.5% to 10.1%) supports confidence in consensus, but consensus itself is drifting lower at the front end.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89.59, Textron trades at a meaningful discount to the aerospace & defense peer set — TDG at ~39x (the peer median of 39.3x is skewed by premium aftermarket and high-growth names). Analyst price targets are clustered above the current price: low $90, median $102.5, mean $103.45, high $115. The stock sits just below the lowest published target.

The discount is partly justified and partly a re-rating opportunity. It is justified to the extent that Textron's mix has historically blended cyclical commercial aviation, a low-margin industrial unit, and a margin-compressing defense growth program — a structurally lower-quality blend than pure defense primes, and the company's five-year TSR (181 versus the peer group's 238) reflects that. It is an opportunity to the extent that the separation removes the Industrial business and leaves a cleaner ~$12B A&D franchise that could fairly carry a higher multiple, plus the standalone Industrial proceeds. We treat the re-rating as real but back-end-weighted: the separation timeline is 12-18 months and its structure, timing, and value are not yet certain.

We score valuation 6.5. The cheapness is concrete and the catalyst is identifiable, which warrants a constructive lean, but the discount is not a clean mispricing — it embeds genuine overhangs (Bell margin, MV-75 funding, separation execution), and absent the catalyst resolving, the stock has been a multi-year relative underperformer. This is not deeply asymmetric value.

## Competitive Landscape

Textron Aviation is the clear leader in light and midsize business jets and in the turboprop segment, competing against Gulfstream (General Dynamics), Bombardier, Embraer, and Dassault, but dominating the light/mid tier it occupies. Bell is one of only two U.S. military rotorcraft primes and won the FLRAA program over the Sikorsky/Boeing team, positioning it as a rotorcraft growth franchise. Textron Systems holds defensible niche positions in Navy connectors, adversary air, and tactical UAS.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: the FLRAA win is a strong positioning event, Aviation is refreshing its line into a recovering demand cycle, and the defense-demand backdrop is favorable. The qualifier is that Bell's near-term margin compression and the appropriations dependency mean the franchise's competitive strength is not yet translating into superior profitability. We score competitive position 7.0 — strong positions on an improving trajectory, tempered by the gap between strategic position and current margin delivery.

## Risk Assessment

**MV-75 funding cliff (most acute, dated, near-term).** On April 28, 2026, the Army Contracting Command notified Bell it is seeking the additional FY2026 funds. Absent additional appropriations, Bell could be ordered to stop work in Q3 2026, with risk of program delays, supplier claims, and unrecoverable costs. Management plans spending-reduction measures to stretch funds and believes any stop-work would not extend into Q4 (FY27 funding is indicated at $2.3B). This is a specific, time-bound, asymmetric risk on the company's primary growth program, and it is not well-reflected in current options pricing.

**LRIP catch-up charge.** At award of the largely fixed-price Low-Rate Initial Production option, Textron expects the $60-110M unfavorable cumulative catch-up reflecting higher-than-bid costs. The program stays profit-positive afterward, but this is a known earnings headwind.

**Capital-return regulatory risk.** The FY2025 10-K cites a new Executive Order addressing underperformance of defense contracts, defense production-capacity investment, and "possible limitations on dividends and share buybacks." Because buybacks are Textron's entire shareholder-return mechanism, any constraint here would remove the primary support for the equity.

**Separation execution.** There is no certainty on the timing, structure, or value of the Industrial separation; a 12-18 month target with potential for unfavorable terms or delay.

**Other.** U.S. Government revenue concentration (~27%), exposed to termination, DCAA/DCMA audit, and continuing-resolution funding gaps; a mandatory 2026 six-year USMCA joint review with no guarantee of continuation; cyclical commercial aviation demand and labor risk (the 2024 strike materially hurt that year); and Middle East conflict exposure flagged in Q1 2026 (energy/shipping inflation, delivery-schedule risk, cyberwarfare risk to defense contractors). The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized is disclosed as not material, with refund reconciliation underway.

We score risk 5.0. The cluster of specific, near-term, partly unpriced downside catalysts — led by the Q3 2026 stop-work possibility and the buyback-limitation overhang — is material and asymmetric.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent on Textron, which is worth flagging given the near-term program risk. Near-term ATM IV is 29.9% versus 25.4% trailing realized (modestly rich), but the term structure is in normal contango (1-month 29.9% < 6-month 32.8%), put/call skew is balanced at 1.3%, and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed to 0.01 (from 0.10 a month earlier) — extreme call-tilted, low put demand. Short interest is light at 3.5% of float (3.7 days to cover). The implied near-term move is ±4.9%. The derivatives market is not pricing meaningful downside around the MV-75 appropriations window or the next print, which is consistent with the broader defense-sector bid but leaves the stock exposed if the Q3 funding question resolves unfavorably. This argues for patience rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The separation unlocks a sum-of-the-parts re-rating: a ~$12B pure-play A&D company built on the Aviation recovery, Systems' 14% margins, and Bell's FLRAA franchise should command a multiple well above the current ~12-14x forward, with standalone value realized for Kautex and Specialized Vehicles. MV-75 funding is resolved (FY27 already indicated at $2.3B), Bell margins inflect as the program transitions toward production, Aviation sustains double-digit growth on a refreshed line into a strong cycle, and continued buybacks at a depressed multiple compound per-share value. Director Tom Kennedy's $989K open-market purchase at $95.98 the day after the separation announcement is a credible vote of confidence from a defense-industry veteran. Upside toward $115.

### Bear Case
The Army issues a stop-work order in Q3 2026, generating supplier claims, unrecoverable costs, and program delay on the very program the bull case depends on; Bell margins stay compressed through the LRIP catch-up charge; the Executive Order materially constrains buybacks, removing the equity's primary support; and the separation slips or completes at disappointing value. The multiple compresses back toward the low end of its range and the stock retests its 52-week low near $72-73, roughly 19% below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean / growth_hold).**

**Rebuttal to a BUY upgrade (specific and falsifiable):** Three concrete conditions keep this below a BUY despite a sub-7 but constructive score. First, the MV-75 stop-work possibility is a dated, near-term (Q3 2026) binary event on the company's primary growth program — falsifiable by watching whether FY2026 supplemental appropriations are approved before Q3 2026. Second, near-term consensus is being revised down, not up, which is the inverse of an earnings-inflection BUY signal. Third, the bear case carries ~19% downside to the 52-week low ($72-73), exceeding the limited-downside asymmetry required for a BUY. Each of these is checkable against the next earnings release and FY2026 appropriations action.

- **Base case target: ~$103 (+15%).** ~14x next-year EPS of $7.30, reflecting steady Aviation strength and incremental separation progress without full re-rating. Sits between bear and bull below.
- **Bull case target: ~$118 (+32%).** Separation completes favorably and New Textron re-rates toward 16-18x on a cleaner A&D earnings base, plus Industrial proceeds; MV-75 funding resolved and Bell margins inflecting.
- **Bear case target: ~$73 (-19%).** Q3 2026 stop-work, persistent Bell margin compression, buyback constraint, and multiple de-rate toward ~10x next-year EPS.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, keyed to the MV-75 appropriations resolution (Q3 2026), the LRIP award (late 2026/early 2027), and the Industrial separation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2026 MV-75 funding is secured before Q3 2026 with no stop-work, removing the largest near-term overhang.
2. Bell segment margin stabilizes/inflects above 9% and current-quarter EPS revisions turn positive.
3. The Industrial separation is confirmed on a tax-efficient structure with credible value (sale or spin) and a defined timeline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An Army stop-work order is issued with material cost recovery uncertainty or extended program delay.
2. The Executive Order results in a concrete restriction on Textron's buyback program, eliminating the primary capital-return lever.
3. Aviation demand rolls over (deliveries or backlog decline) alongside continued Bell deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July) and FY2026 appropriations developments through Q3 2026.

## Conclusion

Textron is a cheap, well-capitalized aerospace & defense franchise undergoing a value-unlocking transformation, supported by a strong Aviation recovery, the FLRAA win, a clean balance sheet, disciplined buybacks, and a credible insider open-market purchase. Those are real positives, and the ~12-14x forward multiple against a defense peer set in the 20-39x range, with analyst targets clustered above the current price, gives the stock a constructive lean.

What holds us back from a BUY is concrete and near-term: the company's primary growth program faces a possible Q3 2026 stop-work order, near-term consensus estimates are being revised downward with a formally degrading sentiment trend, Bell's margins are compressing into a known catch-up charge, and the buyback lever faces a new regulatory overhang. Combined, these produce a bear case with ~19% downside, more than we tolerate for a BUY. We rate Textron a HOLD with a growth lean and would upgrade on resolution of the MV-75 funding question, stabilization of Bell margins with positive revisions, or confirmation of the Industrial separation on favorable terms.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report repeatedly states current price ~$89.59 (consistent with Phase 1 price history), but the valuation multiples and downside math should be checked against this base — the bear-case downside to the 52-week low ($72.76) is ~19%, not 17%, modestly widening the unfavorable asymmetry that underpins the HOLD-not-BUY call.
