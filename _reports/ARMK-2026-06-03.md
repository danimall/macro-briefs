**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aramark is a global food-services and facilities-management operator running two segments — FSS United States (~71% of revenue) and FSS International (~29%) — following the September 2023 tax-free spin-off of its Uniform business as Vestis. The investment story changed materially in the first half of FY2026. Organic revenue growth accelerated to roughly 12% in Q2 (reported +14.7% to $4.91B, of which ~3-4 points was a calendar/53rd-week benefit), net income from continuing operations rose 64.8% in the quarter, and management raised full-year organic revenue guidance to the high end of its 7-9% range on roughly $1B of new contract wins and 98%+ client retention. Layered on top is a differentiated new vertical: the "Nexus" platform, through which Aramark was selected by a top global hyperscaler to provide food, hospitality, and facilities support across multiple AI data-center sites. This is a services contract to a hyperscaler rather than ownership of AI infrastructure, but it opens a large-campus client category that management is explicitly guiding toward expanding.

The counterweight is valuation and balance-sheet structure. The stock has appreciated 34.9% off its 52-week low ($35.07) to ~$54.16, sits within 1% of its 52-week high, and trades at roughly 24x consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.25 and ~40x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.22. Aramark carries ~$6.09B of debt (D/E ~4.2x on book equity), runs net margins of just 1.8%, and has explicit, disclosed limits on its ability to pass through food and labor inflation on the two-thirds of revenue under profit-and-loss contracts. Covenant headroom is comfortable (secured leverage 2.65x vs. 5.125x max; interest coverage 4.57x vs. 2.0x min), so this is not a distress situation, but the financial model is thin-margin and capital-intensive at the point of winning new business.

The recommendation is HOLD on a growth basis. The fundamental inflection is real and estimate revisions are positive, which supports continued appreciation toward the low-$60s high target. But after the run to within 1% of the high, the asymmetry that would justify a higher-conviction call is gone, and the path from here depends on Nexus optionality converting and on margins holding through a record new-business cycle that management itself warns is a near-term margin and cash drag.

## Company Overview

Aramark provides food services and facilities management to institutional clients across business and industry, education, healthcare, sports/leisure and corrections, and facilities. Globally, ~85% of revenue is food services and ~15% facilities. The company holds a top-2 position in North American food and facilities services and a top-3 position internationally, competing primarily against Compass Group (the global leader), Sodexo, Elior, and ISS.

Contracts come in two structures: profit-and-loss contracts (~two-thirds of revenue), where Aramark bears costs and recognizes full revenue at higher risk/reward, and client-interest/management-fee contracts (~one-third), which are lower risk. The moat rests on scale-based purchasing leverage (Sysco distributes ~43% of US/Canada food purchases under a 40-plus-year relationship), entrenched multi-year contracts (5-15 years in education and sports that require up-front capex and create switching costs), and a 98%-plus client retention rate. No single client exceeds 2% of revenue.

The September 2023 Vestis spin-off transformed Aramark into a pure-play food/facilities operator and is the reason total assets fell from $16.9B (FY2023) to $12.7B (FY2024). The relevant comparison base for current performance is the post-spin franchise.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $17.40B (FY2024) to $18.51B (FY2025, +6.4%) and reached $9.74B in H1 FY2026 (+10.3%). Operating income rose to $792M in FY2025 (+12.1%) and $437M in H1 FY2026 (+11.7%). Net income from continuing operations grew 24.8% in FY2025 to $326M and 18.5% in H1 FY2026 to $199M, with diluted EPS of $0.74 versus $0.62 in the prior-year half.

Q2 FY2026 was the standout: revenue +14.7% YoY, net income +64.8% to $102M. Roughly 3-4 points of the reported growth and approximately $24-25M of operating income reflected the calendar shift off FY2025's 53rd week, so underlying organic growth is approximately 12%. That mechanical benefit reverses in tougher forward comps, which the growth analysis below accounts for.

Margins are characteristically thin and improving at the edges. FY2025 operating margin was 4.3% and net margin 1.8%, with cost of services running ~91.3-91.6% of revenue and personnel (~42%) the largest cost bucket. International is the faster-growing engine: FSS International revenue grew 19.1% in H1 FY2026 (with a ~6-8 point FX tailwind), and adjusted operating income rose 17.3%.

Cash flow and balance sheet:
- FY2025 operating cash flow was $921M (+27% YoY), capex $489M, free cash flow $432M (FCF margin ~2.3%).
- Total debt was ~$6.09B as of April 2026, up from $5.41B at FY2025 year-end on a seasonal $625M Receivables Facility draw and revolver borrowings. D/E is ~4.2x on book equity of $3.1-3.3B.
- Covenant headroom is comfortable: Consolidated Secured Debt Ratio 2.65x vs. 5.125x maximum; interest coverage 4.57x vs. 2.0x minimum; Covenant Adjusted EBITDA ~$1.57B TTM. Fully in compliance.
- Liability management has been active: the Term B-10 due 2030 was repriced to SOFR+1.75% in December 2025, €400M 4.375% notes due 2033 were issued, 2025 maturities were redeemed, and ~$2.5B notional of interest-rate swaps fixes variable exposure through June 2028.
- Capital return is modest: a $500M buyback was authorized in November 2024, ~$53.6M was repurchased in H1 FY2026 (~$306M remaining), and the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.12/share (~0.9% yield).

The reported Altman Z-Score of 100-plus is a distorted artifact of the asset-light, high-turnover model and should be disregarded; the real leverage picture is the ~$6B debt load against covenant ratios, which are healthy with roughly 2x headroom. The financial-health score of 5.0 reflects that combination — strong and improving cash generation against high leverage and structurally thin margins that leave limited absorptive capacity for cost shocks.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is genuinely inflecting. The drivers are mid-single-to-low-double-digit organic growth driven by net new sales (~$1B in new contracts), 98%-plus retention, return-to-office tailwinds (Business & Industry revenue +23% in Q2), and faster-growing International. Management raised FY2026 organic revenue guidance to the high end of 7-9%, which is credible given H1 ran above that pace.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-quarter consensus is $0.49 (+1.8% over 90 days), current-year $2.25 (+1.1%), and next-year $2.69 (+2.6%). These are positive but moderate revisions — well short of the velocity that signals a true earnings-trough inflection. They support the growth-hold thesis without rising to a high-conviction buy signal.

The Nexus engagement is the differentiated incremental vector. Aramark launched an integrated hospitality/facilities platform and was selected by a top global hyperscaler for a multi-year, multi-site engagement at AI data-center campuses, with management framing it as "the first of many." The mechanism is straightforward: the AI capex buildout creates large new institutional campuses that need on-site dining and facilities, and Aramark is moving early to capture that category. Aramark is a services vendor to the hyperscaler, not an owner or operator of AI infrastructure; the revenue is contract catering and facilities, layered onto the existing Business & Industry sector. This is real optionality with uncertain magnitude rather than a tech re-rating.

Two qualifiers temper the growth picture. First, the 53rd-week calendar benefit flatters H1 reported growth and reverses into tougher forward comps, so headline growth will decelerate even if underlying demand holds. Second, management's own disclosures warn that high new-business rates depress near-term margins and cash flow because of start-up costs and up-front client capex — H1 FY2026 saw higher commissions, higher client payments, and seasonal working-capital outflow. Record growth carries a near-term margin and cash cost.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$54.16, Aramark trades at ~20x FY2027 EPS of $2.69. The forward-to-next-year multiple of ~20x against high-single-digit revenue growth and low-double-digit operating-income growth implies a PEG in the 1.5-2.0 range — full, not cheap, but not egregiously stretched for a franchise with 98%-plus retention and an oligopoly position.

Peer comparison is of limited use because the listed Distributors comps (GPC, LKQ, POOL) are auto-parts and pool-supply distributors with different economics; their median P/E of 16.6x is not a clean read-through. The more relevant comparables are Compass Group and Sodexo, large contract caterers. On that basis Aramark is roughly in line to slightly rich, with any premium attributable to the growth acceleration and Nexus optionality.

The analyst target distribution — low $40.00, mean $55.25, median $55.50, high $63.00 — places the current price essentially at the consensus mean. The principle that consensus targets lag inflection stocks applies only when a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates; here the stock trades above 20x forward with only modest (~1-3%) revision velocity, so the targets are not stale lowballs. The current price already discounts most of the visible good news, leaving ~16% upside to the high target against ~26% downside to the low target.

The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a full multiple after the run-up, with the burden of proof on the bull case to show what the market is missing. The specific mispricing argument — that Nexus could scale into a materially larger vertical than consensus models — is plausible but unquantified, which is why it does not yet support a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

The contract food-and-facilities-services industry is an oligopoly led by Compass Group, with Aramark and Sodexo in the next tier and Elior and ISS competing internationally. The structural growth driver is outsourcing penetration — Aramark's thesis depends on institutions continuing to outsource food and facilities rather than self-operating. Scale favors the big three through purchasing leverage and the ability to bid multi-service, multinational contracts, though Aramark concedes it can be disadvantaged versus Compass and Sodexo for clients requiring a broader geographic footprint.

Aramark's competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The retention rate, record net new sales, return-to-office recovery in Business & Industry, and the early-mover Nexus data-center positioning all point to share gains rather than erosion. The competitive-position score of 6.5 reflects a stable-to-improving trajectory in a defensible oligopoly, capped below 7 because Aramark is the challenger to Compass's global lead rather than the leader, and because the moat rests on switching costs and purchasing scale rather than any proprietary technology.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and rate risk.** The debt is partly variable and partly hedged through swaps to June 2028. The 10-K explicitly lists leverage as a constraint on strategic flexibility. Covenant headroom is adequate today, but the model has limited room to absorb a demand shock at 1.8% net margins.

**Cost pass-through.** With two-thirds of revenue on profit-and-loss contracts, Aramark has limited ability to pass through food (~28% of revenue) and labor (~42%) inflation. Minimum-wage increases and tight labor markets compress margins directly. Management repeatedly flags tariff and trade volatility affecting food costs.

**New-business margin drag.** Management's own disclosures warn that the current record new-business pace depresses near-term margins and cash flow. The growth that supports the bull case is simultaneously a near-term headwind.

**Discretionary/macro exposure.** Sports/Leisure and Business & Industry are sensitive to attendance, return-to-office, and economic downturns. In a softening consumer environment, these segments are exposed.

**Vestis litigation.** Aramark and its CEO were added (November 2024) as defendants in a securities class action tied to the Vestis spin-off. The motion to dismiss was denied on September 30, 2025, so the case proceeds — a low-probability but real tail.

**Management transition (unconfirmed).** A February 20, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) plus an unusually large ~$3.0M equity grant to COO Marc Bruno suggest succession planning. CEO Zillmer and CFO Tarangelo both signed the May 2026 10-Q, so any change is a watch item, not a near-term disruption.

**Multiemployer pensions.** ~39,000 unionized employees; a $5.6M withdrawal charge was taken in H1 FY2026, with a small number of plans deteriorating.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects a mix of priced-in structural risks (leverage, thin margins, sector cyclicality) and a couple of genuinely idiosyncratic items (Vestis litigation surviving dismissal, the unconfirmed succession signal) that the market is not obviously pricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$63, +16%).** Organic growth holds at the high end of 7-9% through FY2026 and FY2027, net new sales continue at the current record pace, and the Nexus data-center vertical scales into a multi-site, multi-year category that consensus has not yet modeled. Operating leverage on volume drives margin expansion, FCF accelerates as new-business start-up costs anniversary, and the stock re-rates modestly on EPS reaching ~$2.69 in FY2027. At ~23x that figure the stock approaches $63.

**Bear case (~$40, -26%).** Food and labor inflation re-accelerates and compresses thin profit-and-loss-contract margins, the 53rd-week comp normalization makes growth look soft on a reported basis, Business & Industry/Sports demand weakens in a consumer slowdown, and the Vestis litigation or a disorderly management transition introduces a discrete shock. With $6B of debt and 1.8% net margins, a modest demand miss compounds at the equity level, and the multiple de-rates toward the low-$40s — near the $40 analyst low and consistent with where the stock traded as recently as November 2025.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-single-digit appreciation as estimates grind higher, with the stock tracking the consensus mean in the mid-$50s and reaching the high-$50s/low-$60s if Nexus optionality begins to convert.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY2026, expected August 2026), which will show how the 53rd-week comp normalization affects reported growth and whether margins held through the new-business surge.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Nexus data-center engagement expands to additional confirmed hyperscaler contracts with disclosed revenue contribution, validating the vertical as a quantifiable new category.
- FY2027 EPS estimates revise upward by more than 10% on accelerating organic growth and confirmed margin expansion (operating margin moving sustainably above 4.5%).
- A pullback toward the mid-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside to the high-$50s/low-$60s.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating margin compresses below 4.0% on cost-inflation pass-through failure, or net new sales decelerate materially below the current ~$1B pace.
- The Vestis securities litigation produces an adverse development with a quantified liability, or a disorderly CEO transition is confirmed.
- Covenant ratios deteriorate toward limits (secured leverage above ~4x, interest coverage below ~3x) on a demand shock.

## Conclusion

Aramark has delivered a genuine fundamental acceleration — roughly 12% organic growth, raised FY2026 guidance, record net new sales, and a differentiated Nexus data-center services vertical with an AI-adjacent angle. Those are real and they justify holding the position for continued mid-single-digit appreciation as estimates grind higher; this is a growth hold, not an income or placeholder one. The yield of ~0.9% is incidental, and the case rests on the growth trajectory and Nexus optionality.

What prevents a higher-conviction call is the combination of the 34.9% run to within 1% of the 52-week high, a full ~24x forward multiple against modest revision velocity, ~$6B of debt against 1.8% net margins with limited cost pass-through, and a current price already sitting at the consensus mean target. From here the risk/reward is balanced — roughly 16% to the high target against 26% to the low — and the path higher depends on Nexus converting and margins holding through a record new-business cycle that management itself flags as a near-term drag. HOLD, with the upgrade and downgrade triggers above as the monitoring framework.
