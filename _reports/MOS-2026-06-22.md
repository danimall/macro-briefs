**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Mosaic Company is one of the world's largest concentrated phosphate and potash producers, and its stock is trading on trough earnings, depressed sentiment, and a dividend yield near a five-year high. Shares have fallen 37% over the trailing year from $36.44 to a $19.80 intraday low on June 10, and have since recovered roughly 15% to $22.9. The headline Q1 2026 result was a net loss of $(0.81)/share, but the bulk of that ($442M, or about $1.10/share pretax) was a non-recurring charge to divest the Araxá complex and idle the Patrocínio mine in Brazil. The operating story underneath is a cyclical margin squeeze: purchased sulfur costs rose 141% YoY and ammonia 50%, compressing phosphate gross margin from 19% to 8% while potash held up well.

The investment question is whether this is a structural impairment or a cyclical trough being over-discounted by the market. The evidence points to the latter. The cost squeeze is sector-wide — Nutrien flagged identical phosphate-margin pressure for its own Q2 — so this is not a Mosaic execution failure. Potash, now the largest EBITDA contributor at roughly 45%, posted +13% operating earnings on MOP prices up 19%. A StoneX analyst argued the 2026 low in U.S. phosphate prices may have already passed, and consensus mean target ($27) sits 21% above the current price. Against that, the dividend is currently uncovered by free cash flow, full-year estimates have been cut ~49% over 90 days, and a fresh antitrust class-action overhang surfaced in March 2026.

We rate MOS a HOLD with an uncertain near-term direction. The balance sheet is investment-grade and there is no terminal/structural impairment, which removes the basis for an AVOID. But we cannot identify a clean catalyst for appreciation either; the thesis hinges entirely on the timing of a phosphate margin recovery that depends on global commodity prices and Middle East supply dynamics outside the company's control. The two-sided commodity driver could reverse abruptly in either direction.

## Company Overview

Mosaic operates through three reportable segments:

- **Phosphate (~35% of 2025 adjusted EBITDA):** Florida mining and Louisiana processing, plus a 75% interest in the Miski Mayo mine in Peru. Produces DAP/MAP and the value-added MicroEssentials product. Mosaic is the leading U.S. phosphate producer.
- **Potash (~45% of EBITDA):** Mines at Esterhazy and Belle Plaine in Saskatchewan, with international sales through Canpotex, the Canadian potash export marketing entity it shares with Nutrien. This is now the largest profit driver and the company's relative haven.
- **Mosaic Fertilizantes (~20% of EBITDA):** Brazilian/Paraguayan phosphate mining, chemical plants, a potash mine, and a large South American distribution/blending business. Lowest-margin and the locus of the Q1 2026 restructuring charge.

The business is vertically integrated with captive phosphate rock in Florida and low-cost potash in Saskatchewan, providing input-cost stability on the rock side. It competes against Nutrien (potash/nitrogen), CF Industries (nitrogen), OCP of Morocco (phosphate), and Russian producers PhosAgro/Apatit. Mosaic also holds a strategic equity stake in Saudi Arabia's Ma'aden, carried at roughly $1.9B fair value and marked to market each quarter, which injects $100M+ of quarterly earnings noise unrelated to operations.

The multi-year arc reflects the commodity cycle:

Revenue collapsed ~37% from the 2022 Ukraine-war fertilizer peak; net income fell ~95% to a 2024 trough before a partial 2025 recovery. The critical financial concern is free cash flow: 2025 operating cash flow of $825M did not cover $1.36B of capex, producing negative FCF of -$535M. Against this, the company paid roughly $283M in dividends ($0.88/share annualized). The dividend is therefore being funded with balance-sheet capacity, not free cash flow; sustainability depends on a near-term margin recovery.

The balance sheet remains investment-grade: total debt of ~$5.0B (ST $760M + LT $4.25B) at YE2025 against $12.1B equity, with modest cash of $277M. The reliance on short-term funding warrants monitoring: a $2.5B commercial paper program, a $625M inventory-financing facility, a $500M receivables-purchase facility, and Brazilian structured accounts payable collectively fund a large slice of working capital. Short-term debt jumped to $1.2B by Q1 2026. The 10-Q notes derivative counterparties could demand collateral if Mosaic were downgraded below investment grade, which makes the IG rating a load-bearing element of the financing model during a trough.

**Q1 2026 was a sharp negative result.** Net loss of $(257.6)M, or $(0.81)/share, versus +$0.75 a year earlier. Revenue rose 14% to $3.0B, but gross margin collapsed from 19% to 8%. Two drivers: the $442M Brazil charge ($233M impairment + $159M contract/severance/write-offs + ~$26M accelerated D&A and idle costs), and a raw-material cost shock — consumed sulfur +141% YoY ($379/long ton) and ammonia +50% ($626/tonne in North America). Adjusted EPS of ~$0.05 missed consensus by ~79%, so the input-cost squeeze hurt even excluding one-offs. Segment-wise, Phosphate operating earnings swung from +$139M to -$48M and Mosaic Fertilizantes from +$99M to -$422M, while Potash was the lone bright spot (+13% to $177M). A +$112M Ma'aden mark-to-market gain and a +$38M FX gain partly cushioned the GAAP loss.

The earnings surprise record reinforces the visibility problem: three misses in the last four quarters (-79.1%, -53.3%, +4.5%, -30.5%). Forward estimates should be treated with caution.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term earnings momentum is negative, but the picture is bifurcating rather than uniformly deteriorating. Full-year consensus EPS has been cut from $1.71 to $0.87 over 90 days (-49.3%), and next year from $2.51 to $1.92 (-23.5%). That is the trailing reaction to Q1.

The more recent signal is more constructive. The next-quarter estimate rose +9.5% over the last 30 days (to $0.31 from $0.28), and the current-quarter cut decelerated to -1.7% over 30 days. The front-end of the cut cycle appears to be stabilizing even as the trailing 90-day numbers still look ugly, consistent with the StoneX view that the 2026 low in U.S. phosphate prices may have already passed and with the recovery framing in several independent pieces: high-cost inventory clears, higher selling prices flow through, and margins normalize.

Structurally, there is no organic growth story here. Mosaic is high-grading its portfolio rather than expanding it: it sold the Carlsbad, NM potash mine (closed April 30, 2026, for ~$2M cash plus deferred payments) and is divesting Araxá in Brazil, concentrating on core Saskatchewan potash and Florida/Peru phosphate. 2026 capex is guided to ~$1.25B, down from $1.36B. The company is also voluntarily curtailing phosphate output at Louisiana and Bartow, FL, and scaling back Brazil production because sulfur costs are uneconomic, prioritizing profitability over throughput. Growth comes from commodity price recovery, not volume or new markets.

## Valuation Assessment

The 161x trailing P/E is meaningless here; it reflects trough earnings, not normalized power, and the cycle-adjusted approach is the only sensible frame. On next-year consensus EPS of $1.92, the stock trades at roughly 12x. That is below the peer median P/E of 17.7 (SMG 17.7, CF 9.6, CTVA 40.7; FMC negative), though peer comparability is weak given the heterogeneous nutrient mix and earnings stages.

Several supports exist. The dividend yield is ~3.8%, above the peer median of 3.0% and near a five-year high. Consensus mean target is $27 (median $27, range $19–$35). Morningstar's $47 fair value (a single independent perspective, not consensus) implies the market is materially over-discounting a cyclical trough.

We are appropriately skeptical of treating cheapness as a buy signal. A low multiple on a commodity cyclical is frequently a value trap absent a catalyst, and the catalyst here — phosphate margin recovery — is real but unconfirmed and exogenous. The ~5-year-high dividend yield can be read as a market warning about uncovered cash flow rather than as a value beacon. We assign a moderate valuation score (5.5): there is genuine asymmetry support from the discount to targets and the cyclical-trough framing, but the absence of company-controllable catalysts and the uncovered dividend keep us from rating this higher.

## Competitive Landscape

Mosaic's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, not deteriorating structurally. The 2026 setup is bifurcated by nutrient. A Middle East gas/shipping supply shock (Iran tensions, Strait of Hormuz) is pushing up both fertilizer prices and input costs. Nitrogen producers (CF, Nutrien) benefit because higher gas raises nitrogen prices with their own gas as feedstock. Phosphate producers like Mosaic are squeezed because purchased sulfur and ammonia spike faster than phosphate selling prices catch up. Nutrien explicitly flagged the same pressure on its phosphate margins.

Potash is a genuine relative strength: tight global supply, strong international demand, rising MOP prices, and the Canpotex marketing structure provide a haven that now represents the company's largest EBITDA contributor. On the phosphate side, Mosaic benefits from countervailing-duty orders on Moroccan (OCP) and Russian imports — a meaningful competitive protection, but one under active legal challenge and subject to annual rate resets, so it could erode.

The trajectory is the relevant metric. Mosaic's TSR has lagged its peer group ($116.32 vs. $138.87 per the proxy), consistent with phosphate-heavy exposure underperforming nitrogen/potash-weighted peers. The portfolio high-grading (exiting high-ARO, lower-return assets) is a coherent response that should modestly improve returns on capital over time, but it does not change the company's position as a price-taker in global commodity markets.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for MOS, weighted toward what the market may not be fully pricing:

1. **Raw-material cost shock (sulfur/ammonia).** The Q1 2026 10-Q added a risk-factor update specifically citing Strait of Hormuz/Iran tensions and renewed Russia-Ukraine attacks disrupting sulfur and ammonia supply. As a commodity producer, Mosaic "cannot assure" pass-through. This is the proximate cause of the margin collapse and the curtailments now underway. It cuts both ways: a verified Hormuz de-escalation would reverse the input-cost driver.
2. **Antitrust class actions (filed March 2026).** Putative class actions allege MOS and other fertilizer producers engaged in anticompetitive N/P/K pricing, with treble damages sought; a JPML consolidation hearing was scheduled for late May 2026. Fresh, early-stage, but more than nuisance litigation. Under-appreciated overhang.
3. **Negative FCF and an uncovered dividend.** A prolonged trough pressures the dividend, which is currently funded by balance-sheet capacity.
4. **Large environmental/ARO liabilities.** ~$2.5B asset-retirement obligations, dominated by ~$2.3B undiscounted Florida/Louisiana phosphogypsum stack closure costs, including a possible New Wales West Stack liner breach ($55.5M reserve, remediation targeted for summer 2026). $630M is locked in RCRA trusts as financial assurance.
5. **Brazil contingencies.** ~$541.6M labor/civil claims and up to ~$782.8M non-income tax claims, plus a Peru (Miski Mayo) environmental penalty proceeding.
6. **Binary tax item.** $1.4B unrecognized tax benefit on the Taquari mine divestiture loss, now in an IRS Pre-Filing Agreement process — a favorable resolution would be a material positive.
7. **Short-term/off-balance-sheet financing dependence,** which is investment-grade-contingent at a cyclical low.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, balanced uncertainty rather than directional bearishness. Near-term ATM IV is 56.6% versus 44.6% historical vol — rich, in the 79th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 56.6% > long-term 54.5%), signaling expected near-term stress. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-4.2%) and the put/call OI ratio eased from 1.06 to 0.99, which does not confirm a bearish lean. The 1-week implied move is ±4.8%. The market expects volatility but is not positioned strongly to the downside, consistent with a two-sided cyclical setup rather than a one-way collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30–$35).** The phosphate input-cost squeeze is cyclical and peaking; high-cost inventory clears and rising selling prices flow through, restoring phosphate margins toward normal. Potash continues to deliver on tight supply. Next-quarter estimate revisions (+9.5% over 30 days) prove to be the leading edge of a broader estimate turn. A Hormuz de-escalation reverses the sulfur/ammonia driver. On normalized mid-cycle earnings power approaching $2.50+, a 12–14x multiple supports the upper end of the analyst range and closes part of the gap to Morningstar's $47 fair value. The discount to the $27 consensus mean target and the 5-year-high yield provide downside support while the recovery develops.

**Bear Case ($16–$19).** The cost squeeze persists longer than expected, phosphate margins stay compressed, and FCF remains negative through 2026, forcing a dividend reassessment. Estimates resume falling. The antitrust litigation advances and creates a quantifiable liability. A below-IG downgrade triggers collateral demands on the short-term financing stack. The stock retests its $19.80 trough. This is a ~13–30% downside from current levels, but it lacks a clean dated catalyst — the trough has already printed and the tape has stopped making new lows.

The reward-to-risk is roughly two-sided: ~31–53% upside to the bull case against ~13–30% downside to the bear case, with the most likely outcome a range-bound stock pending margin clarity. The absence of an identifiable downside catalyst, an investment-grade balance sheet, no terminal impairment, and valuation support from the discount to targets are what keep this out of AVOID territory; the uncovered dividend, the negative estimate momentum on the trailing window, and the exogenous nature of the recovery driver are what keep it out of BUY territory.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Phosphate segment operating earnings turn positive and gross margin recovers toward mid-teens, confirming the cost-squeeze trough has passed (next earnings, expected early August 2026).
- Current-year EPS estimates stabilize and next-quarter revisions continue rising for two consecutive months, confirming the +9.5% 30-day uptick is a durable turn rather than noise.
- Free cash flow turns positive, removing the dividend-coverage overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A below-investment-grade credit rating action, which would trigger collateral demands on the commercial paper / inventory-financing stack.
- A dividend cut, signaling management has lost confidence in a near-term margin recovery.
- The antitrust litigation crystallizes into a quantified, material liability, or estimates resume falling at the -40%+ 90-day pace.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected early August), which will reveal whether the sulfur/ammonia squeeze is easing and whether phosphate margins have turned. Any verified Hormuz de-escalation is a parallel, faster-moving input on the input-cost side.

## Conclusion

Mosaic is a high-quality, investment-grade cyclical commodity producer trading near the bottom of an earnings cycle, with a real but cyclical phosphate margin problem and a genuinely strong potash franchise. The market has already discounted the bad news, and the headline Q1 loss was dominated by a non-recurring Brazil charge rather than ongoing operational deterioration. The sector-wide nature of the cost squeeze (confirmed by Nutrien), the early stabilization in front-end estimate revisions, and the 21% discount to consensus targets argue against treating this as a structural impairment.

At the same time, there is no clean catalyst for appreciation. The recovery depends on exogenous commodity prices, the dividend is uncovered by free cash flow, and forward visibility is weak (three misses in four quarters). We rate MOS a **HOLD (uncertain)**: the risk/reward is balanced, the downside lacks a dated catalyst, and the upside lacks a controllable one. We would revisit conviction at the Q2 print, looking specifically for phosphate margin inflection and a turn in free cash flow.
