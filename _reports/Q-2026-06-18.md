**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Qnity Electronics is a scaled, diversified supplier of advanced materials and consumables to the semiconductor and electronics manufacturing supply chain, spun out of DuPont on November 1, 2025. The investment case rests on a clean secular thesis: advanced logic and memory nodes consume 3–5x more processing material per wafer than legacy nodes, and AI-driven HBM and advanced packaging stack additional content-per-wafer demand on top of unit growth. The Q1 2026 print validated this directly: revenue of $1,315M grew 18% YoY, led by the Interconnect Solutions (ICS) segment at +25% on AI packaging, PCB, and thermal demand, with ICS segment EBITDA up 48%. Management raised full-year guidance to $5.225–5.375B and consensus EPS estimates for the current quarter have risen +41.5% over 90 days.

The problem is price. Shares trade at roughly $156–174 against a 52-week low of $70.50, a re-rating of roughly 60% YTD that places the stock at ~50x trailing and forward earnings. That is below the semiconductor-equipment peer median P/E of 68.5 (KLAC, LRCX, AMAT, TER), but Qnity is a slower-growing materials company than those capital-equipment names and carries idiosyncratic baggage they do not. The bull case (AI materials intensity) is now substantially in the price; the dominant forward risk is multiple compression, not fundamental deterioration. Management repurchased stock at an average of $113.78 in Q1 2026 and has not returned to the buyback at current levels, which indicates where insiders saw value.

We rate Qnity a HOLD with a positive growth bias. The franchise quality and growth durability are real and improving (competitive position and growth both score well), and the moderate price pullback from the $171.52 high improves the entry relative to the May peak. But the combination of a premium absolute multiple, an uncapped DuPont-controlled PFAS/legacy-liability tail (44% allocated share, recorded ~$461M but explicitly "cannot be reasonably estimated"), an interim CFO during the firm's first standalone SOX cycle, and a two-year tax-driven strategic lockup keep this from clearing the bar for a BUY. We would upgrade on a multiple reset toward 35x forward or clarity on the legacy-liability ceiling.

## Company Overview

Qnity sells qualified-in consumables and materials into chip fabs and electronics manufacturers. It is a supplier to the industry, not a chip designer or fabricator. It operates two segments:

- **Semiconductor Technologies (~56% of sales):** CMP slurries and pads, photoresists and lithographic materials, advanced cleans, Kalrez sealants, and OLED display materials. These are process-node-specific consumables.
- **Interconnect Solutions (~44% of sales):** Advanced packaging metallization (copper pillar, RDL, solder bump), Laird thermal-interface and EMI materials, PCB materials (Kapton, Pyralux), and advanced flex circuits.

The moat is structural rather than reputational. Over 90% of revenue is consumable and unit-driven; materials must be re-qualified per process node, creating high switching costs and multi-year design-win lock-in. The company reports average top-10 customer relationships exceeding 30 years, counts nearly all of the world's top-20 semiconductor manufacturers as customers, and holds roughly 7,000 patents. Principal competitors are Entegris, Merck KGaA, Resonac, Element Solutions, and MKS Instruments. The company became independent via a tax-free distribution (1 Qnity share per 2 DuPont shares) and began trading on the NYSE on November 3, 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is accelerating and volume-led.** FY2023 revenue of $4,035M grew to $4,335M in FY2024 (+7%) and $4,754M in FY2025 (+10%). The FY2025 gain was +11% volume against roughly −1% price — demand-driven, not pricing-engineered. Q1 2026 inflected to +18% YoY ($1,315M), with ICS +25% and Semi +12%.

**Margins are strong and expanding, concentrated in the favorable segment.** FY2025 net income was $729M (15.3% net margin), with gross margin near 46%. Semi segment Adjusted Operating EBITDA was $945M (35.8% margin); ICS was $539M (25.5% margin, up sharply from 17.7% in 2023). The mix shift is favorable: the historically lower-margin ICS segment is both the fastest grower and the fastest margin-expander, driven by AI advanced packaging and thermal management. Q1 2026 total segment Adjusted EBITDA was $432M (+20% YoY).

**Cash generation is high quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,273M (up from $1,061M in 2024 and $882M in 2023) against capex of roughly $285M, yielding approximately $1B of free cash flow. Q1 2026 OCF of $135M was lower YoY, but the decline is mechanical: roughly $61M of new post-spin debt interest is now flowing through the cash statement. R&D runs near 7% of sales ($354M in FY2025), a steady reinvestment rate.

**The balance sheet carries spin-off leverage that requires context.** The separation loaded Qnity with $4.0B of new debt issued in late 2025: $1.0B secured notes at 5.75% due 2032, $750M unsecured notes at 6.25% due 2033, and a $2.35B term loan at 5.7% due 2032, plus a $1.25B undrawn revolver. Net debt is roughly $3.1B, D/E 0.94, and gross leverage approximately 2.0x EBITDA — manageable for a business generating >$1.2B in operating cash flow. The current ratio is 1.95 and the company is in compliance with all covenants. A screening Altman Z-Score of 1.79 ("distress zone") is a measurement artifact: it is distorted by the brand-new debt load against minimal standalone operating history and a spin-charge-depressed EBIT/assets ratio. On cash flow, margins, leverage, and growth, this is a healthy business, not a distressed one. The Z-Score should be treated as noise here.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces forward-estimate confidence: three consecutive beats since the spin (Q3 2025 +9.2%, Q4 2025 +27.7%, Q1 2026 +14.8%). Short visibility (the company has under a year of standalone reporting) is the only caveat on that track record.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has multiple independent confirmations. The processing-material multiplier — more material consumed per wafer at advanced nodes — is a structural content-growth lever that compounds on top of unit growth. AI-specific demand is flowing through ICS visibly: the +25% Q1 segment growth in AI packaging, AI PCBs, and thermal management is the cleanest evidence in the materials layer that hyperscaler capex is reaching consumables, consistent with the broad infrastructure-layer demand confirmation seen across power, networking, and packaging suppliers.

Estimate revisions are firmly positive and accelerating at the near end. Current-quarter consensus EPS rose +41.5% over 90 days and +17.1% over the last 30 days; next-quarter +17.3% over 90 days; current-year +7.5%; next-year +8.5%. The acceleration in the 30-day window for the current quarter indicates consensus is still catching up to the inflection. Revenue growth above 30% is not present (the business runs in the high-teens), so this sits between a pure inflection profile and a steady premium-grower. Management raised FY2026 guidance across sales, EBITDA, EPS, and FCF after the Q1 beat, and the "Qnity Transformation Plan" targets ~$100M of run-rate EBITDA benefit by end of 2028 from productivity and IT-independence work (Q1 transformation charges were $28M, mostly $24M of IT independence cost).

We weight growth heavily and score it 7.5. The combination of accelerating revenue, broad-based estimate revisions, favorable mix shift, and durable secular demand is among the more reliable predictors of continued operating outperformance. The reason it is not higher: revenue growth is in the high-teens rather than the 40%+ range that would mark a pure trough inflection, and the materials business is ultimately tied to a cyclical end-market that will not grow at this rate indefinitely.

## Valuation Assessment

At roughly $156–174, Qnity trades at approximately 50x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $3.30; forward consensus FY26 $4.11, FY27 $4.79). On the FY27 estimate the multiple compresses to roughly 33x, and the PEG against high-teens growth is well above 1.5.

The peer comparison cuts both ways. The semiconductor-equipment median P/E is 68.5 (TER 70.8, KLAC 68.3, LRCX 68.5, AMAT 52.0), so on a headline basis Qnity at ~50x looks cheaper than the cohort. But those are capital-equipment names with higher growth beta and no PFAS or carve-out overhang. Qnity is a slower-growing consumables business; applying the equipment cohort's multiple as a fair-value anchor overstates the case. A more defensible frame is a premium-but-not-equipment-class multiple of 30–38x forward, which on $4.79 FY27 EPS produces a $144–182 range, bracketing the current price.

The valuation history is telling. The company repurchased 219,581 shares at an average of $113.78 in Q1 2026 under a $500M authorization, leaving $475M unused, and has not stepped back in as the stock re-rated. PSUs were granted at a $191.69 fair value. The gap between where management deployed cash (~$114) and where the stock trades now (~$157–174) is the single most useful internal valuation signal available, and it indicates the easy re-rating is behind the stock.

The instruction to treat analyst targets as floors applies to cheap, inflecting stocks; this one trades at ~50x and the targets (low $140, mean $173, high $200, RBC at $200 from $150) sit around or below the current print. At a premium multiple, consensus targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent upside confirmation. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is full for the delivered growth, the recovery catalyst (the AI re-rating) has largely played out, and the asymmetry now skews toward multiple compression on any quarterly disappointment.

## Competitive Landscape

Qnity's competitive position is improving, which is what the dimension rewards. The consumable, re-qualified-per-node nature of its products produces switching costs that are difficult to replicate; design wins are sticky across multi-year customer roadmaps. The favorable mix shift — ICS growing 25% and expanding margins from 17.7% (2023) to 25.5% (2025) — indicates share and content gains in the highest-value part of the AI supply chain rather than a defensive incumbent losing ground. The 30-year average top-10 customer tenure and presence at nearly all top-20 chipmakers indicate a wide, durable moat.

The constraints are structural rather than competitive: heavy customer concentration (Samsung 11%, TSMC 8%, top-10 = 34%) and geographic concentration (Asia-Pacific 79%, China ~33%) mean the franchise is exposed to a small number of relationships and to US-China trade policy. Mitigants include ~70% local-for-local raw material sourcing and ~80% in-region manufacture-and-sale. We score competitive position 7.5, consistent with the improving trajectory in the score history (7.0 → 7.5), and the new ICS data confirms rather than reverses that trend.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is the reason this stock cannot be scored purely on its franchise quality.

**DuPont legacy and PFAS liabilities (the defining tail risk).** Qnity is contractually allocated 44% of certain DuPont legacy liabilities, including PFAS exposure and the New Jersey environmental Consent Order. Recorded DuPont indemnification liabilities were roughly $461M at YE2025 (with $323M of offsetting indemnification assets), but these are explicitly uncapped, DuPont controls all litigation and settlement, and Qnity can be required to remit cash directly. The Q1 10-Q notes reasonably-possible exposure could exceed accruals by ~$84M, with additional losses that "cannot be reasonably estimated." This does not appear in headline multiples and is not in Qnity's control.

**Two-year strategic lockup.** Under the Tax Matters Agreement, Qnity is restricted from M&A, large equity issuance, and asset sales through approximately November 2027 to preserve the tax-free spin. If the spin is later deemed taxable, Qnity may have to indemnify DuPont for significant taxes.

**Governance structure.** A single Series A Preferred share held by a noncharitable purpose Trust is required to vote against certain corporate events, entrenching DuPont's liability-control rights and functioning as a strong anti-takeover device, relevant for any acquisition-premium thesis.

**Leadership transition.** The Q1 2026 10-Q was signed by an interim CFO (Michael Goss); a January 16, 2026 8-K carried an Item 5.02 (officer departure), and no permanent CFO had been named through May 2026, coinciding with the first standalone SOX cycle and the ~$180M IT-systems build-out. This is a real execution watch-item.

**Cyclicality and concentration** (customer, China, semiconductor cycle) are real but largely priced into a cyclical-materials name. We score risk factors 4.5, reflecting the uncapped, externally-controlled PFAS tail and the governance/leadership items that are specific and not fully reflected in the multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$200–215).** AI materials intensity proves durable and broadens; ICS continues 20%+ growth with further margin expansion; the Transformation Plan delivers the ~$100M EBITDA benefit ahead of 2028; estimates continue rising and the stock holds a premium ~40x forward multiple on upwardly-revised FY27 EPS. RBC's $200 target reflects this path. A permanent CFO hire and a quantified ceiling on legacy liabilities would remove two overhangs and support further re-rating.

**Base case (~$170).** High-teens revenue growth persists, margins hold, FY27 EPS lands near $4.79, and the multiple settles around 35x forward as the AI re-rating matures into a steady premium-grower valuation. The stock roughly holds current levels with modest appreciation tied to estimate revisions, not multiple expansion.

**Bear case (~$105–115).** A semiconductor demand pause or AI-capex digestion clips ICS growth back toward single digits; multiple compresses to 24–26x forward on disappointment given the ±14.6% implied move; a material PFAS/legacy development forces an unbudgeted cash remittance; or the SOX/finance-leadership build-out surfaces a control issue. This returns the stock toward where management was buying it (~$114) and represents roughly −30% to −35% from current levels.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric from here: meaningful upside requires continued multiple support at a premium absolute level, while the downside scenarios are concrete and each individually plausible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias and a 12-month horizon, reassessed at each quarterly print (next earnings the key catalyst given the backwardated IV).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward P/E compresses toward 35x or below on stable/rising FY27 estimates (a price near $135–145 against $4.79+ EPS), restoring asymmetric upside.
- A permanent CFO is named and a clean first SOX audit is delivered, removing the finance-leadership overhang.
- A quantified cap or material settlement on the DuPont/PFAS legacy exposure, eliminating the uncapped-tail discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ICS revenue growth decelerates below ~10% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the AI materials pull is fading while the multiple is still premium.
- A material legacy-liability development requiring unbudgeted cash remittance materially above the ~$461M recorded base.
- A negative SOX/internal-control disclosure or evidence of aggressive carve-out accounting surfacing in the first full standalone audit.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the implied ±14.6% move makes this the decisive near-term event), then each subsequent print through the November 2027 expiry of the strategic lockup.

## Conclusion

Qnity is a high-quality franchise: a moaty, cash-generative, AI-leveraged materials supplier with improving competitive position, accelerating double-digit growth, and rising estimates. Those attributes (growth and competitive position scoring 7.5 each) argue for owning it through the AI cycle. The constraints are the valuation, which at ~50x trailing/forward has fully absorbed the materials-intensity thesis after a roughly 60% rise YTD, and a stack of specific idiosyncratic risks (uncapped DuPont-controlled PFAS liabilities, an interim CFO during the first SOX cycle, a two-year strategic lockup, and an anti-takeover Trust structure) that headline multiples do not capture. Management's own buyback at ~$114 versus today's ~$157–174 frames the gap between intrinsic value and price. We recommend HOLD and would become more constructive on a multiple reset toward 35x forward or resolution of the legacy-liability and leadership overhangs.
