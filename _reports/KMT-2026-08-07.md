## Executive Summary

Kennametal reported the best year in its modern history and the market sold it. On August 5, 2026 the stock opened at $40.50, traded to $41.12, and closed at $34.16, a 16% intraday reversal on a quarter that beat non-GAAP consensus by 27%. That price action is the correct reading of the release. FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.57 (versus $1.34 in FY2025) and a 41.1% fourth-quarter operating margin are products of a tungsten supply shock in which Kennametal's realized selling prices ran ahead of its raw-material cost recognition. The company quantified the benefit at approximately $316 million of FY2026 operating income against $472.5 million reported. Remove it and operating income was $156.5 million versus $157.9 million adjusted in FY2025, flat in dollars on a revenue base 20% larger, which means the ex-timing operating margin compressed from 8.0% to roughly 6.6%.

The cash flow statement confirms that this is an inventory revaluation rather than economic profit. Kennametal earned $342.4 million of attributable GAAP net income in FY2026 and generated negative $4.0 million of operating cash flow, with free operating cash flow of negative $79.1 million. Inventory rose $570.2 million to $1,108.5 million, or 47% of annual sales, from 27.4% a year earlier. FY2027 guidance projects free operating cash flow at roughly 20% of adjusted net income, about $73 million against a $61 million dividend. Two consecutive years of record reported earnings will have produced approximately break-even cumulative free cash flow.

Management's guidance shape settles the durability question without requiring any commodity forecast. FY2027 adjusted EPS is guided to $4.15-$5.15, with $2.50-$2.80 of that in Q1 alone, leaving $1.35-$2.65 for the remaining three quarters combined. Q1 is Kennametal's seasonally weakest quarter because of summer plant shutdowns and construction patterns, so the front-loading cannot be seasonal. Underneath the distortion there is a real improvement: Metal Cutting's ex-timing operating margin rose roughly 300bp to 10.1%, and in-house tungsten recycling is winning volume from material-constrained competitors. That improvement is worth owning at the right price. At 13x FY2028 consensus of $2.55, with a 2.9% forward free cash flow yield, a $330 million unaccrued litigation demand, and $1.1 billion of commodity inventory exposed to a symmetric reversal, the current price is not it. We move to REDUCE from HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kennametal converts tungsten ore concentrates and recycled scrap carbide into tungsten oxide and carbide powders, then into cutting tools and wear-resistant components. Two segments:

- **Metal Cutting** — FY2026 sales $1,397.4M (59% of total). Milling, hole making, turning and threading systems under the Kennametal, WIDIA, WIDIA Hanita and WIDIA GTD brands, sold through a direct force, distributors and integrated supply.
- **Infrastructure** — FY2026 sales $959.3M (41%). Engineered carbide and ceramic components, earth-cutting tools, metallurgical powders, frac seats, road-milling and foundation-drilling tools, and tungsten penetrators and armor for aerospace and defense.

End markets are roughly General Engineering 43%, Transportation 27%, Earthworks 15%, Energy 14%, Aerospace & Defense 13%. Geographically FY2026 revenue split Americas $1,211.9M, EMEA $687.1M, Asia-Pacific $457.7M, with 60% of sales outside the United States. Approximately 8,100 employees, about 1,900 union-represented.

Two structural features of the business model matter more than usual in the current cycle. First, the 10-K states that many smaller competitors buy cemented tungsten carbide components from original producers "including Kennametal." Kennametal sits upstream of the fragmented tail and captures revenue when that tail cannot source material. Second, the company discloses in-house tungsten recycling capability that reduces reliance on third-party primary ore. Against that, Kennametal explicitly disclaims a meaningful order backlog and discloses no material remaining performance obligations. This is a short-cycle, book-and-ship consumables business with no contracted revenue cushion; there is no backlog metric to smooth the transition when pricing turns.

## Financial Analysis

### The pre-shock baseline

| FY | Revenue | Op. income | Op. margin | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $2,012.5M | $218.1M | 10.8% | $1.71 |
| 2023 | $2,078.2M | $192.4M | 9.3% | $1.47 |
| 2024 | $2,046.9M | $170.2M | 8.3% | $1.37 |
| 2025 | $1,966.8M | $143.1M | 7.3% | $1.20 |

Four consecutive years of margin compression and three of EPS decline. FY2025 revenue fell 4% with every end market except Aerospace & Defense declining and all three regions down. FY2025 operating cash flow was $208.3M on $89.0M of capex, and the company returned $122M against $119.4M of free cash flow. Screens on FY2025 data put the Altman Z-Score near 2.59 (grey zone), current ratio ~2.5x, debt/equity ~0.95, ROE ~7.7%. That is the base against which everything below should be measured.

### FY2026 and the decomposition

| | Q4 FY26 | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | $736.6M (+42.6%) | $2,356.7M (+20%) | $1,966.8M |
| Operating income | $302.9M | $472.5M | $143.1M |
| Operating margin | 41.1% | 20.1% | 7.3% |
| Adjusted EPS | $2.96 | $4.57 | $1.34 |

Management attributes approximately $252M of Q4 and approximately $316M of full-year operating income to favorable timing of raw-material-related pricing versus raw-material cost, with roughly $198M of the Q4 figure in Infrastructure. The mechanism is straightforward: only 30% of inventory is on LIFO. On the remaining ~70% carried at FIFO or average cost, Kennametal sells material acquired at older, lower cost while pricing at current replacement cost. The LIFO portion partially offsets, and the LIFO reserve grew from $106.0M at June 30, 2025 to $160.4M at March 31, 2026.

The year-over-year bridge closes almost exactly on identified items: operating income rose $329.4M, against ~$316M of price/cost timing plus ~$27M of incremental restructuring savings, leaving a residual of roughly negative $14M to absorb volume, non-commodity pricing, FX (+2%), tariffs, inflation, higher performance-based compensation, the FY2025 divestiture, and the non-recurrence of FY2025's ~$12M net tornado insurance benefit. Every driver other than the commodity spread and cost takeout was collectively a small negative.

The segment cut is more informative than the total. Allocating the timing benefit using the disclosed quarterly figures (~$262M Infrastructure, ~$54M Metal Cutting, an allocation derived from quarterly disclosure rather than a reported annual split):

- **Metal Cutting**: ex-timing operating income ≈ $141.6M on $1,397.4M, a ~10.1% margin, up from 7.1%. Restructuring savings, non-commodity pricing and tariff surcharges are landing. This improvement is real and should persist.
- **Infrastructure**: ex-timing operating income ≈ $17.9M on $959.3M, a ~1.9% margin, down roughly $41M year over year. Infrastructure absorbed the cost inflation, the bulk of the inventory build, and the loss of FY2025's ~$13M incremental IRA advanced-manufacturing credit.

The reported consolidated 20.1% margin therefore contains one genuinely improving segment and one whose underlying economics deteriorated while its headline margin printed 29.2%.

The terms of the new instrument (coupon, size, maturity, security) are not confirmed in available disclosure, so FY2027 interest expense of roughly $40M in our bridge is an estimate.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance: sales $3.33-$3.45B (+41% to +46%), adjusted EPS $4.15-$5.15, capex ~$85M, free operating cash flow ~20% of adjusted net income.

Revenue growth of that magnitude on a business with mid-single-digit underlying volume growth is a pricing phenomenon. Revenue will have risen roughly 72% across FY2026-FY2027 from $1,966.8M, with only a modest fraction of that from volume. The guidance is internally coherent with a fading spread. At the $4.65 midpoint, adjusted net income on ~78M diluted shares is ~$363M; grossing up at a 24% tax rate, adding ~$8M of noncontrolling interest and ~$40M of interest and subtracting ~$15M of other income implies FY2027 adjusted operating income near $510M, a ~15.0% margin against FY2026's 20.5%. A normalized build (Infrastructure at 10-12% and Metal Cutting at 11-12% on the guided revenue) produces only $370-400M. The $110-140M gap is residual price/cost timing benefit expected in the first half.

Within the year, the midpoint leaves $2.00 for Q2-Q4 combined after a guided $2.65 in Q1. If the benefit persists into Q2 as management indicated on the May call, the implied second-half run rate falls to roughly $0.20-$0.50 per quarter. That is a derived sensitivity from a guidance range rather than a forecast, and management may be deliberately conservative on a period for which no tungsten forward visibility exists. The direction is management's own.

Consensus corroborates. The current-year (FY2027) estimate of $5.17 sits above the guidance midpoint and near the top of the range after a +30% thirty-day revision, while the next-year (FY2028) estimate is $2.55, a 51% decline. The Q1 FY2027 consensus of $2.01 still sits below the $2.50-$2.80 guide, so near-term estimates will likely rise further. Consensus is marking to a commodity spread, not to an earnings inflection. Four consecutive beats averaging roughly +27% confirm that the spread has consistently outrun models, which cuts symmetrically: the same models will lag on the way down.

Durable growth drivers do exist. Management raised the cumulative cost-takeout target to roughly $110M by end-FY2027 while deferring planned footprint closures to keep capacity available for share capture. Earthworks share gains were attributed partly to "availability of material," customers switching to Kennametal because competitors could not source tungsten. Energy demand was linked to power generation for AI data centers. These support volume and mix, not the $316M.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.29, with 76.21M shares and $621.7M of net debt:

| Metric | Multiple |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 adjusted EPS $4.57 | 7.3x |
| P/E on FY2027 guidance midpoint $4.65 | 7.2x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $5.17 | 6.4x |
| **P/E on FY2028 consensus $2.55** | **13.1x** |
| P/E on estimated ex-timing FY2026 EPS ($1.47-$2.12) | 16-23x |
| EV / FY2026 adjusted EBITDA (~$627M) | 5.0x |
| EV / normalized EBITDA (~$530M sticky-price case) | 6.0x |
| Price / book ($20.60) | 1.62x |
| FY2027 guided FOCF yield (~$73M) | 2.9% |
| Dividend yield ($0.80) | 2.4% |

The headline 7x is the trap. The relevant anchors are the FY2028 consensus multiple of 13.1x and the free cash flow yield of 2.9%. Peer medians in the data set (P/E 51.7) are not usable: the comparables include a negative-earnings company, a 73.5x name and a 51.7x name; the closest analogue, EPAC at 22.1x, is on undistorted earnings. Kennametal at 13x normalized is neither cheap nor expensive for a business that compressed operating margin for four straight years before the shock and delivered $92.13 on $100 invested over FY2021-FY2025 against $267.12 for its own peer group.

Two coherent forward cases bracket the value:

**Sticky-price case.** Tungsten plateaus near current levels, Kennametal's repriced book holds revenue at $3.3-3.4B, share gains from supply security stick, and ex-timing operating margin settles at the ~11.4% implied by FY2027 guidance less residual timing. Operating income ~$385M, EPS ~$3.30. At 12.7x that is roughly $42.

**Partial-reversion case.** New non-Chinese supply (Sangdong, Australian restarts, US defense-funded projects) and customer substitution toward ceramics, superhards and coated high-speed steel pull tungsten prices materially lower over the next 12-24 months. Revenue falls toward $2.6-2.8B, fixed-cost deleverage plus lower-of-cost-or-market pressure on a $1.1B inventory book takes margin to 8-9%, EPS to $1.60-$1.95. At 12x that is roughly $21.

Weighting these at 30% / 45% (base at FY2028 consensus $2.55 × 13x ≈ $33) / 25% gives an expected value near $32.70, essentially flat to the current price, with a 2.4% dividend. That is a low-single-digit expected total return against equity cost of capital, with the near-term distribution skewed to the downside because the H2 FY2027 earnings cliff is close to certain regardless of where tungsten trades.

The sell-side agrees. Ratings stand at zero buys, five holds, one sell and two strong sells, with a mean target of $34.71 (+4.3%) and a range of $31-$39. On a name where estimates were just revised up 30% in a month, the absence of a single buy rating is a meaningful signal about how analysts view earnings quality.

The research brief's suggestion that the $33.25 quote predates the FY2026 release is incorrect. The price series shows the August 5 gap to $40.50, the close at $34.16, and subsequent trades at $33.85 and $33.29. The current quote is a post-earnings, post-guidance price. The market has seen the release.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is fragmented: several hundred fabricators, toolmakers and coating specialists, with several large global competitors. In a tungsten squeeze, Kennametal's position improves for three filing-grounded reasons: in-house recycling reduces dependence on Chinese primary ore; it supplies cemented carbide to the smaller competitors who cannot source it; and vertical integration from powder to finished tool lets it capture margin at each step of a rising cost curve. Documented Earthworks share gains attributed to material availability are the observable evidence.

Metal Cutting's 300bp ex-timing margin improvement on modest volume growth is genuine operating leverage from restructuring and non-commodity pricing, and it is the part of the story worth paying for. Distinguish carefully between two things reported inside the same margin line: share gains from supply security are a volume and revenue phenomenon that should persist; the $316M is a cost-flow timing phenomenon that should not.

Two offsets. Tungsten carbide at a multiple of its prior price invites substitution; ceramics, cermets, PCD/CBN superhards and coated high-speed steel become economic, and customers redesign to consume less carbide. Kennametal produces ceramics and superhards, which cushions rather than eliminates the effect. Second, a multi-fold price increase is a powerful magnet for new non-Chinese supply, and the supply response is already visible in project announcements. That response is a multi-year process, which is why the sticky-price case deserves real weight, but it is directionally adverse and Kennametal will carry $1.1B of inventory into it. EMEA remained weak through FY2026 (+2% constant currency in Q3).

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity symmetry on a $1.1B inventory book.** The same FIFO/average-cost mechanics that produced $316M of holding gains produce holding losses on the way down, plus potential lower-of-cost-or-market writedowns. Inventory at 47% of sales is the highest commodity exposure per dollar of revenue Kennametal has ever carried. This is the dominant risk and it is not obviously priced at 13x FY2028 consensus.

**Cash conversion.** Two years of negative-to-marginal free cash flow with record earnings. FY2027 guided FOCF of ~$73M against a ~$61M dividend leaves 1.2x coverage and nothing for repurchase. Buybacks were cut to $10.1M in FY2026 from $60M and explicitly paused to preserve balance sheet flexibility, with $130M of the February 2024 authorization unused and the program expiring around February 2027.

**MachiningCloud litigation.** A February 2025 breach-of-contract suit seeks in excess of $330M, approximately 13% of market capitalization, removed to federal court, with no accrual disclosed. A damages demand is not a liability estimate, and the absence of an accrual implies management does not consider loss probable and estimable. The merits are unassessed here. The claim is an unpriced tail.

**Goodwill.** $282.7M at June 30, 2025, all of it in Metal Cutting; Infrastructure's goodwill was previously written off in full ($633.2M gross against $633.2M accumulated impairment). Kennametal escalated its FY2025 test from qualitative to quantitative, which typically indicates narrowing headroom. Metal Cutting's improving margins mitigate this today.

**Geopolitical two-sidedness.** 60% of sales outside the US, with manufacturing in China while the critical input is subject to Chinese export control, plus operations in Israel, South Africa, India and Vietnam. Offsetting optionality: a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs and Kennametal is evaluating potential refunds of tariffs previously paid, unquantified.

**Disclosure gaps.** The FY2026 10-K is not yet filed. The June 30, 2026 LIFO reserve, inventory detail, FY2026 gross profit, updated risk factors, any goodwill test result, and the terms of the new note issuance are unavailable. Seven Item 5.02 officer-change filings since January 2025 could not be retrieved; who departed is unknown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 52.6% sits 9.6 points above 43.1% one-year realized volatility, in the 76th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (52.6% at 13 days versus 50.5% at 160 days) and a ±9.2% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Put/call volume ratio is 3.93 with a 10.1% put skew flagged as elevated put demand, while standing put/call open interest is 0.36 and has fallen from 0.80 since July 22. The combination of heavy near-term put flow against call-tilted legacy open interest, with IV down 9.3 points post-print, reads as holders buying protection on positions they have not exited, rather than a directional short accumulation. Elevated 12.6% short interest with 5.5 days to cover adds squeeze risk to any bearish positioning, which is one reason we prefer trimming exposure over a short. The options market is pricing continued event risk around a name whose earnings depend on an unforecastable commodity, which is consistent with our view and argues against paying for optionality here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($42, +26%)

Chinese export controls on tungsten persist, defense and AI-power-generation demand keeps the market tight, and new non-Chinese supply arrives slowly enough that prices plateau near current levels rather than reverting. Kennametal's repriced revenue base holds at $3.3-3.4B; the share gains won on material availability prove sticky because tooling qualification is slow to reverse; Metal Cutting's ex-timing margin continues climbing past 10.1% as the $110M cost-takeout program completes; and Infrastructure's underlying 1.9% margin recovers toward 10% on volume absorption. Clean EPS power settles near $3.30, working capital releases as inventory normalizes to 30-35% of sales, free cash flow inflects sharply positive, and repurchases resume. At 12-13x, that is $40-$43. The IEEPA tariff refund provides unquantified upside.

### Bear Case ($21, −37%)

Tungsten prices retrace even part of a multi-fold move as Sangdong, Australian restarts and US defense-funded supply land, and customers substitute toward ceramics and superhards. The price/cost spread inverts, so the mechanism that added $316M subtracts. Revenue falls toward $2.6-2.8B; margin compresses to 8-9% on fixed-cost deleverage; lower-of-cost-or-market charges hit a $1.1B inventory book carried at spike-era cost. EPS lands at $1.60-$1.95 and the multiple compresses as investors re-rate the business back toward its pre-shock profile of four years of margin decline and roughly 175 percentage points of five-year TSR underperformance. Add the possibility of an adverse MachiningCloud outcome and the dividend, covered only 1.2x by guided free cash flow, comes under review. At 12x depressed earnings, $21, against a 52-week low of $17.62.

### What tips the balance

The base case is management's own guidance, extended one quarter past the fiscal year. Q1 FY2027 alone is guided above the sum of Q2-Q4 in a seasonally weak quarter. Whatever the terminal earnings power, investors will spend the next twelve months watching quarterly EPS fall from $2.65 toward well under $1.00 while free cash flow covers little more than the dividend. That sequence rarely supports multiple expansion, and it is why we prefer to own less of this at $33 and revisit at a price that discounts it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than waiting for the Q2 FY2027 print in late January 2027, which is the first quarter where the spread compression should become visible in reported results. Reduce exposure by roughly half at current levels. A residual position is defensible for holders who want participation in the sticky-price case and the Metal Cutting improvement, but it should be sized to a 2.4% dividend and a flat base case, not to the headline 7x multiple. Do not add on the August 5 pullback; the pullback is the market correctly repricing earnings quality, not a dislocation.

**Scenario targets from the $33.29 basis:**
- Bear: $21 (−37%)
- Base: $33 (−1%; 13x FY2028 consensus $2.55), plus a 2.4% dividend
- Bull: $42 (+26%)
- Probability-weighted expected value ≈ $32.70, roughly a +0.6% total expected return

**Timeline.** Twelve to eighteen months, framed by three dated checkpoints: the FY2026 10-K filing (expected August 2026), the Q1 FY2027 report (late October 2026), and the Q2 FY2027 report (late January 2027).

**Triggers that would restore HOLD:**
1. FY2026 10-K disclosure showing the June 30, 2026 LIFO reserve materially higher than the $160.4M at March 2026, evidencing a larger understated-inventory cushion against a price decline, combined with confirmed new-note terms at a coupon that keeps FY2027 interest expense near $40M.
2. Q1 or Q2 FY2027 results demonstrating that ex-timing consolidated operating margin has moved above 10% (Metal Cutting holding 10%+ while Infrastructure recovers from 1.9% toward 8%), which would validate a normalized EPS base near $3.00 rather than $2.00.
3. A price near $26-$28, which would put the shares at 10-11x FY2028 consensus and roughly 1.3x book.

**Triggers that would move us to AVOID:**
1. Any quarter showing inventory writedowns or a negative price/cost variance disclosed as a margin headwind, which would confirm the reversal has begun with $1.1B of inventory still on the books.
2. Free operating cash flow tracking below the guided ~20% of adjusted net income through the first half of FY2027, which would put the dividend at risk and eliminate the deleveraging path.
3. An adverse procedural development in MachiningCloud (denial of a dispositive motion, or the establishment of an ASC 450 accrual) given the $330M demand against a $2.54B market capitalization.
4. Confirmed CFO or segment-president departure among the unretrieved Item 5.02 filings during the working-capital build.

## Conclusion

Kennametal is a competent industrial franchise experiencing a large, exogenous and explicitly quantified accounting windfall. The $316M price/cost timing benefit accounts for slightly more than the entire year-over-year increase in FY2026 operating income; ex-timing operating profit was flat in dollars on 20% more revenue, implying margin compression from 8.0% to roughly 6.6%. The company earned $342.4M of net income and generated negative $4.0M of operating cash flow while inventory grew to 47% of sales. FY2027 guidance concentrates $2.50-$2.80 of a $4.15-$5.15 year into the seasonally weakest quarter, and consensus for FY2028 sits 51% below FY2027 at $2.55.

There is a durable improvement here (Metal Cutting's 300bp ex-timing margin gain, tungsten recycling as genuine supply security, share captured from material-constrained competitors) and it justifies a valuation above the pre-shock base. It does not justify paying 13x an out-year estimate for a business whose reported earnings currently convert to no cash, whose $1.1B inventory book carries symmetric downside on a commodity that has risen multiple-fold, and which faces a $330M unaccrued claim equal to 13% of its market capitalization. With a probability-weighted expected total return near zero, negative near-term skew from an earnings cliff embedded in management's own guidance, zero buy ratings across eight covering analysts, seven insider sales and no purchases between February and June 2026, and free cash flow that barely covers the dividend, the risk-adjusted return is below fair. **REDUCE.**
