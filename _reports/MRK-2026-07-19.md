**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merck is a high-quality pharmaceutical franchise trading in the shadow of a single defining problem. Keytruda, its anti-PD-1 immuno-oncology therapy, generated roughly $31.7B in FY2025, approximately 49% of total company revenue. U.S. biosimilar competition is expected as early as December 2028, EU data exclusivity already expired in July 2026, and Merck itself expects Keytruda to be selected for IRA government price-setting in 2027 with an effective date of January 1, 2029. The 10-K states plainly that U.S. Keytruda sales "will decline materially." Every other line of the investment case — pipeline productivity, the subcutaneous Keytruda conversion strategy, Winrevair, Animal Health, and a $26B M&A blitz — exists to answer one question: can Merck replace half its revenue before the cliff hits.

The near-term operating picture is healthier than the headline suggests. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(1.72) per share is an accounting artifact of a $9.0B IPR&D charge tied to the Cidara acquisition, not operational deterioration. Underlying franchises grew 5% reported, with Keytruda up 12% to $8.03B, Winrevair nearly doubling to $525M, and Animal Health up 13%. The company beat consensus in all four of the last reported quarters. The stock has responded, rising roughly 56% over the trailing twelve months to $127.51, near its 52-week high of $131.74.

The trajectory of our own view has been downward — 6.6 in February, 5.7 in May, 5.6 in June — reflecting a reassessment of the cliff and the 2026 loss-of-exclusivity (LOE) wave rather than price action. Current data neither reverses nor accelerates that reassessment decisively. The underlying business is compounding, the valuation is not stretched on normalized earnings, and the pipeline has real catalysts in the next twelve months. Against that, the structural overhang is the largest in large-cap pharma, the 2026 LOE wave is here now, and several litigation and regulatory threads (Halozyme, Gardasil securities class action, ACIP vaccine policy) are live and unresolved. We maintain HOLD, with the caveat that the setup is balanced rather than clearly favorable in either direction.

## Company Overview

Merck operates in two reportable segments. The Pharmaceutical segment (~89% of sales, $58.1B in FY25) covers prescription drugs and vaccines: oncology (Keytruda), vaccines (Gardasil, ProQuad/MMR/Varivax, Vaxneuvance, Capvaxive, Enflonsia), cardiometabolic/respiratory (Winrevair, Ohtuvayre), and infectious disease (Bridion, Prevymis, Lagevrio). The Animal Health segment (~11%, $6.4B) sells livestock and companion-animal products plus digital monitoring, with the Bravecto franchise running ~$379M in Q1'26 alone. Animal Health is a genuine bright spot, growing 13% in Q1'26 (+6% ex-FX).

Merck's competitive advantages are concrete: an oncology clinical infrastructure of roughly 80 Phase 3 trials spanning 19 tumor types for Keytruda, scaled global vaccine manufacturing (sole U.S. maker of MMRV and varicella vaccines), and business-development capacity funded by strong cash generation. All of these advantages are concentrated in defending or replacing one asset.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was $65.0B, up 1% reported and 2% ex-FX. The three-year trajectory — $60.1B (2023), $64.2B (2024), $65.0B (2025) — shows growth flattening as the Gardasil collapse and Januvia erosion offset Keytruda and Winrevair gains. GAAP net income was $18.25B (+7%), non-GAAP EPS $8.98 (+16%), gross margin 74.8%, net margin ~28%, and ROE ~35%. These are strong quality metrics for a mature pharma franchise.

Operating cash flow was $16.5B in FY25, down from $21.5B in 2024 on higher tax payments and upfront deal payments; FCF was approximately $12.4B. FY25 capital return was ~$13.3B ($8.2B dividends + $5.1B buyback), and the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.85 from $0.81. The current yield is 2.66%, below the peer median of 3.0% and well below Pfizer (6.7%) and Bristol Myers (4.5%).

The Q1 2026 quarter needs interpretation. Reported sales were $16.29B (+5% reported, +3% ex-FX), with U.S. +8% and International +2% (−3% ex-FX). The GAAP net loss of $(4.24)B, or $(1.72) per share, is entirely driven by the $9.0B IPR&D charge for the Cidara asset acquisition, booked to R&D with no tax benefit and distorting the tax rate to −20%. Non-GAAP EPS of $(1.28) still carries $3.62/share of that charge; underlying operations were solidly profitable. Screening on GAAP EPS would badly misread this quarter.

The balance sheet is where the strategy shows up. Cash fell from $14.6B (Dec'25) to $5.3B (Mar'26) after the $8.8B net Cidara payment. Long-term debt is ~$46.7B, and total debt/total-capital rose to 38.2%. A new $6.0B 364-day term loan (April 2026) will fund the Terns acquisition and be refinanced with long-term debt. Leverage is trending up as Merck aggressively buys pipeline. The ~1.4 Altman Z-Score is not a solvency signal here; it is an artifact of high leverage combined with XBRL segment presentation that pushes operating costs into unallocated lines. A company with ~28% net margins and $16B+ of operating cash flow is not a bankruptcy risk. The real balance-sheet story is rising leverage funding M&A, and buybacks are being throttled (Q1'26 buyback ~$874M, ~$3.0B planned for 2026 against a ~$6.4B remaining authorization) to preserve cash for deals.

We hold Financial Health at 6.0. Cash generation, margins, and ROE remain excellent, but declining operating cash flow, a rapidly falling cash balance, rising leverage into a period of peak revenue uncertainty, and throttled buybacks are legitimate offsets to the underlying quality.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided, and this is the dimension where the cliff bites hardest. Consensus for the current year has been cut 46% over 90 days (to $2.76 from $5.12), but that reflects the Cidara and Terns IPR&D charges, not operating deterioration. The more telling number is the next-year estimate of $9.66, revised only marginally (−0.9% over 90 days), and next-quarter at $2.31 (−3.7% over 90 days). Stripping the deal charges, consensus sees normalized earnings power intact near $9-10 in the near term.

Positive drivers are real. Keytruda grew 12% in Q1'26 (aided by ~$250M favorable wholesaler timing). Winrevair nearly doubled to $525M and has HYPERION with a PDUFA on September 21, 2026, plus positive CADENCE Phase 2 data in HFpEF. Welireg grew 45% and has LITESPARK-022 (PDUFA June 19). Ohtuvayre (from the Verona acquisition) contributed $131M in its first quarter. Animal Health is compounding double-digits. The 2026 pipeline calendar is dense with catalysts: ifinatamab deruxtecan (PDUFA Oct 10), enlicitide (oral PCSK9 in FDA's expedited pilot), Idvynso (HIV, approved April 2026), and the sac-TMT ADC.

The offsets are structural and near-term. Gardasil fell 39% in FY25 and −19% in Q1'26, with China shipments suspended since Feb 2025 and any late-2026 restart expected to be immaterial. Januvia/Janumet declined −28% in Q1'26; U.S. Januvia/Janumet exclusivity was lost in May 2026 with "nearly all U.S. sales" expected to go. Bridion loses U.S. exclusivity in July 2026, with Merck planning to discontinue U.S. sales. Lagevrio is down 73% as COVID fades. The pipeline is high-variance: LITESPARK-012, KEYNOTE-975, and KEYNOTE-866 all missed primary endpoints, and the MK-1308A coformulation was discontinued.

Growth prospects score 4.5. Weighting this dimension heavily is warranted: the durable multi-year growth trajectory is capped by a revenue base that is scheduled to shrink materially from 2028-2030. The near-term franchises are growing, but the pipeline must fill the Keytruda gap faster than the LOE wave erodes the rest of the portfolio. That is a demanding bar, and the estimate revisions do not show accelerating positive momentum (+30%+ over 90 days on rising revenue).

## Valuation Assessment

At $127.51 the stock trades at 35.7x trailing/forward P/E per the profile data, but this figure is distorted by the deal charges compressing current-year GAAP earnings. On the more meaningful next-year consensus of $9.66, Merck trades at ~13.2x forward earnings. That is a discount to the peer median P/E of 20.0 and below Johnson & Johnson at 27.6, roughly in line with Bristol Myers at 15.9, and below Pfizer at 20.0 on that peer's own depressed base.

The discount is the Keytruda-cliff discount, and it is rational. The relevant question is whether the discount is too large. Analyst targets span $100 (low) to $155 (high), with a mean of $134 and median of $139, placing the current price roughly 5-9% below the mean/median. The recommendation distribution (4 strong buy, 15 buy, 8 hold, 1 strong sell) skews constructive but not uniformly bullish. The five-year TSR of 158.1 versus the peer group's 198.3 confirms the market has been discounting Merck for the cliff for years.

We resist scoring cheapness as a buy signal here. A compressed multiple that reflects a genuine, scheduled, half-of-revenue structural impairment is closer to a value trap than an opportunity unless the pipeline visibly de-risks the replacement math, and it has not yet. The catalysts that would justify re-rating (Winrevair HYPERION approval, subcutaneous Keytruda IP resolution in Merck's favor, pipeline readouts converting to approvals) are real but unresolved. Valuation scores 5.0: the stock is not expensive on normalized earnings, but the discount is doing exactly what it should, and there is no identified catalyst forcing an upward re-rating today.

## Competitive Landscape

Merck's oncology and vaccine infrastructure is world-class, but the competitive trajectory is under pressure. The subcutaneous Keytruda (Qlex) conversion program is the single most important competitive lever, designed to migrate IV patients to the SC formulation before the 2028 biosimilar wave. Halozyme won a preliminary injunction in Germany in December 2025 blocking SC sales there, with broader European litigation (Netherlands hearing July 2026) and U.S. PGR/IPR battles pending. An adverse Halozyme outcome would blunt the primary cliff-mitigation strategy.

The IRA price-setting mechanism is already eroding the broader portfolio on a scheduled basis: Januvia's set price took effect January 1, 2026; Janumet/Janumet XR effective January 1, 2027; Lenvima selected for January 1, 2028; and Keytruda expected for selection in 2027 (effective 2029). Merck is suing the government, but the direction of policy is set. The December 2025 MFN pricing agreement traded pricing concessions (a direct-to-patient program, MFN on new launches, foreign-revenue sharing) for a three-year Section 232 tariff reprieve and ~$12B+ of U.S. manufacturing reshoring, a structural pivot that reshapes both cost and pricing economics in ways standard models do not yet capture.

Competitive position scores 5.0, a stabilization after the prior report's cut from 8.0 to 5.5. The moat in oncology clinical development is intact, but the trajectory is defensive: Merck is spending to hold ground against a cliff, and the key defensive lever (Qlex) faces active IP challenge.

## Risk Assessment

The risk stack here is deep and much of it is not yet fully priced beyond the cliff itself:

1. **Keytruda cliff + IRA double-hit.** The defining overhang: biosimilars from ~December 2028 and IRA pricing from January 2029 on ~49% of revenue.
2. **2026 LOE wave, live now.** Januvia/Janumet (May 2026), Janumet XR and Bridion (July 2026), with Bridion U.S. sales to be discontinued.
3. **Gardasil/China and securities litigation.** The FY25 decline triggered the Cronin v. Merck class action alleging misrepresentation of China HPV demand; motion to dismiss filed May 2026.
4. **U.S. vaccine policy / ACIP.** The CDC's January 2026 immunization-schedule overhaul touches Merck's HPV/MMRV/varicella franchises; a federal court preliminarily enjoined the changes March 16, 2026, now on appeal. Management calls impacts "not material," but this is unresolved.
5. **Halozyme SC-Keytruda IP.** Directly threatens the primary cliff-mitigation lever.
6. **Tax/DOJ exposure.** IRS NOPAs seeking +$1.3B transition tax + $260M penalties; two active DOJ False Claims Act CIDs.
7. **M&A/leverage execution.** ~$26B of deals in ~7 months, cash from $14.6B to $5.3B plus a new $6B term loan, buying early-stage (Phase 1/2/3) assets that are not de-risked revenue.

Risk factors score 4.5. Several of these are specific, dated, and asymmetric to the downside: the Halozyme rulings, the vaccine-policy appeal, the securities class action, and the pace of leverage expansion into peak uncertainty. This is more idiosyncratic binary risk than a typical mature pharma carries.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no confirming or contradicting edge to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 27.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 28.0% — fairly priced, at the 2nd percentile within the current chain, so the market is not pricing near-term stress. Put/call skew is essentially flat (−0.2%), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.27, and the term structure is flat. IV has fallen from 35.2% to 27.1% since mid-June as the price rose 5.6%, indicating resolved rather than building uncertainty. The one notable print is unusual call buying at the $135 strike (July 24 expiry, 5.1x volume/OI), consistent with mild bullish near-term positioning. Longer-dated tenors carry modest put skew (−3.8% at 3m, −3.3% at 6m), a normal hedge against the pipeline/regulatory calendar. Short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float. Nothing in the options market challenges the fundamental HOLD; it confirms a stock the market currently views as low-drama.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($155)
The pipeline delivers. Winrevair HYPERION is approved (Sep 2026) and the CADENCE HFpEF signal extends the addressable market; Welireg, ifinatamab deruxtecan, enlicitide, and the sac-TMT ADC convert to approvals and meaningful revenue. Subcutaneous Keytruda prevails against Halozyme and successfully converts a large share of IV patients before 2028, softening the biosimilar impact. Animal Health continues compounding double-digits. The ~$26B of M&A begins to show de-risked revenue by 2027-2028. On this path, the cliff is a manageable trough, the market re-rates toward the peer median, and the stock reaches the high analyst target near $155 (~22% upside).

### Bear Case ($100)
The pipeline disappoints — more KEYNOTE/LITESPARK-style misses — and the ~$26B of early-stage deals fail to generate revenue on schedule. Halozyme wins broadly, gutting the Qlex conversion strategy, and Keytruda biosimilars plus IRA pricing take the base down faster than replacements ramp. The ACIP vaccine-schedule changes are upheld on appeal, pressuring the HPV/MMRV franchises. Leverage rises further to fund additional deals. On this path the discount widens and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $100 (~22% downside).

The symmetry of these cases — roughly ±22% to the target extremes — is why this is a HOLD. The base case sits near the current price plus the analyst mean, and the two tails are genuinely balanced by unresolved binary events over the next twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The business is high-quality and growing at the franchise level, the valuation is undemanding on normalized earnings, and there are real near-term catalysts, but the defining structural overhang, the active 2026 LOE wave, and a cluster of live litigation/regulatory binaries leave the risk/reward balanced.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Welireg PDUFA (June 19, 2026), the Winrevair HYPERION PDUFA (September 21, 2026), the Halozyme Netherlands hearing (July 2026), the ACIP appeal outcome, and the Q2 2026 earnings release.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Subcutaneous Keytruda IP resolves in Merck's favor (favorable Halozyme rulings in the EU/U.S.), removing the primary threat to the cliff-mitigation strategy.
- Two or more of the 2026 pipeline catalysts (Winrevair HYPERION, ifinatamab deruxtecan, enlicitide) convert to approvals with credible peak-sales visibility, and next-year consensus revisions turn positive by +10% or more.
- Evidence of open-market C-suite insider buying, which would signal management conviction the market is underestimating the replacement math.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Halozyme outcome that materially blunts Qlex conversion, combined with additional Phase 3 pipeline misses.
- Next-year consensus EPS revised down more than 10% on operating (not deal-charge) deterioration.
- Leverage expansion accelerating (total debt/capital moving materially above ~40%) to fund further deals without visible near-term revenue return, or a dividend/buyback cut signaling cash strain.
- The ACIP vaccine-schedule changes upheld with quantified material impact to the HPV/MMRV franchises.

## Conclusion

Merck is a well-run, cash-generative pharmaceutical franchise carrying the most concentrated large-cap patent cliff in the industry. The near-term operating results are better than the GAAP headlines imply — Keytruda +12%, Winrevair +88%, Animal Health +13% — and the stock is not expensive at ~13x normalized forward earnings, a rational discount to peers rather than an obvious mispricing. Against that stand a scheduled loss of roughly half of revenue from 2028-2030, a 2026 LOE wave already in motion, a defensive strategy (Qlex) under active IP attack, and ~$26B of early-stage M&A that is depleting cash and raising leverage as uncertainty peaks. No insider conviction buying is present to counterbalance the overhang.

The score trajectory has been declining (6.6 → 5.7 → 5.6), and the current data confirms rather than reverses the reassessment: the overall score settles near 5.05. The bull and bear cases are roughly symmetric at ±22% over twelve months, gated by unresolved binary events. We maintain **HOLD**. This is a name to own for its optionality on pipeline execution and to reassess concretely at each 2026 catalyst, not one to add to at a 52-week high with the defining risks still unresolved.
