**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HCI Group is a Tampa-based homeowners' P&C insurance holding company that has emerged as one of the principal beneficiaries of Florida's post-2022 market regime change. Statutory tort reform curtailed assignment-of-benefit litigation abuse, claim frequency fell sharply, and the state's depopulation of Citizens (the insurer of last resort) created a steady supply of take-out policies for well-capitalized private carriers. HCI combined those tailwinds with a proprietary technology stack (Exzeo) and disciplined underwriting to grow policies in force to 313,400 at year-end 2025 (from 271,300), lift net premiums earned to $821.7M, and post a record $299.0M net income attributable to shareholders ($22.72 diluted EPS) on a 56.3% net combined ratio.

The headline 2025 result overstates normalized earning power. It was a zero-catastrophe year: losses and LAE fell to $241.8M from $374.7M in 2024 because no hurricane made landfall in HCI's book, whereas 2024 absorbed ~$127.7M from Milton, Helene, and Debby. Management's own through-cycle target is a 60% ±5% combined ratio, and 2022 produced a $58.5M net loss. The correct way to value HCI is on normalized earning power, not the 2025 print. Even on that basis the balance sheet and embedded asset value are compelling: stockholders' equity doubled to over $1B, debt-to-cap is ~6%, operating cash flow reached $444.4M, and management pegs pro forma book value at ~$145/share including the consolidated ~82.5% stake in Exzeo (XZO, IPO'd November 2025 at a ~$1.5B implied valuation, carried at cost in GAAP book) plus real estate at fair value. The stock near $165 trades only modestly above that adjusted book while compounding at a ~35% after-tax ROE.

This is the fourth consecutive period at a BUY-leaning score, and the trajectory has improved (6.9 → 6.9 → 7.3). The fundamental case strengthened this cycle: Q1 2026 was the best first quarter ever (EPS $5.45 vs. $5.26 consensus, +12.2% revenue, 35% ROE), the second captive reinsurer (Fortex Re) launched, and the June 1 reinsurance placement is being finalized into a softening market. The single thing that prevents a higher score is catastrophe concentration, an unhedgeable, binary, seasonally-timed risk that the market correctly does not pay a premium to ignore. We rate HCI a BUY with sizing discipline appropriate to a name whose downside is a hurricane, not a fundamental impairment.

## Company Overview

HCI operates through five reportable segments built around two underwriting carriers, Homeowners Choice Property & Casualty (HCPCI) and TypTap Insurance Company (TTIC), plus a not-yet-launched surplus-lines entity (perRisk) targeting markets like California. Risk is selectively retained through captive reinsurers Claddaugh (Bermuda) and the newly licensed Fortex Re (Cayman, Q1 2026).

The five segments are: (1) Insurance Operations, the core carriers; (2) Exzeo, the majority-owned (82.5%) insurtech subsidiary that IPO'd on the NYSE as XZO, providing a proprietary platform (SAMS, Harmony, ClaimColony, AtlasViewer) for quoting, underwriting, policy administration, claims, and risk visualization, now sold to third-party carriers; (3) Reciprocal Exchange Operations, the policyholder-owned exchanges CORE (commercial residential, launched Feb 2024) and Tailrow (fire/homeowners, launched Feb 2025) that HCI manages as attorney-in-fact, consolidating them as primary beneficiary with net income flowing to noncontrolling interests; (4) Real Estate, owned Florida commercial properties; and (5) Corporate & Other.

The competitive edge rests on the Exzeo technology enabling disciplined, data-driven underwriting and rapid policy onboarding, paired with deep expertise in Citizens take-out (depopulation) programs. The carriers hold a Demotech "A Exceptional" rating accepted by mortgage lenders but lack an A.M. Best rating, a structural limitation versus large national insurers.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows real top-line growth amplified by an exceptionally benign loss year:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross premiums earned | $765.5M | $1,083.2M | $1,236.1M |
| Net premiums earned | $495.9M | $677.6M | $821.7M |
| Total revenue | $550.7M | $750.1M | $900.9M |
| Net income (attrib. to HCI) | $79.0M | $110.0M | $299.0M |
| Diluted EPS | $7.62 | $8.89 | $22.72 |
| Net combined ratio | 85.0% | 83.1% | 56.3% |
| Gross loss ratio | 33.3% | 34.6% | 19.6% |

Gross premiums grew at a 27% CAGR over 2023–2025, driven by Citizens take-outs (173,485 policies / $756.6M annualized premiums assumed over 2023–2025) and the reciprocal-exchange structure. Net premiums earned grew ~21% in 2025 alone. That is genuine underlying expansion. The earnings surge on top of it is weather-driven and not repeatable: the 19.6% gross loss ratio reflects zero catastrophe losses, against management's normalized 60% ±5% target. Investors should haircut the $22.72 EPS toward a through-cycle figure that incorporates a normal storm-season distribution.

Cash flow quality is high. Operating cash flow rose $230.7M → $331.8M → $444.4M (2023–2025), exceeding net income as is typical for a growing insurer building float and reserves. The balance sheet is conservatively structured: GAAP book ~$85/share; ~$2B in cash and fixed-maturity securities; debt-to-cap only ~6% ($32.4M promissory notes plus $36M drawn on a $150M Fifth Third revolver). Total surplus grew 22% YoY to over $500M with gross leverage under 2.5x. Two capital-structure actions improved the picture: $172.5M of 4.75% convertible notes converted to equity in 2025 (cutting interest expense), and $100M of Centerbridge-held Exzeo preferred was redeemed in early 2024.

Q1 2026 confirmed the momentum into the new year: pre-tax income $115M (+15% YoY), net income $85M, diluted EPS $5.45, revenue $242.9M, combined ratio 57%, loss ratio ~20%.

The Financial Health score of 8.0 reflects a near-debt-free, cash-rich, surplus-strong insurer. We hold it just below the prior 8.5 because the equity base is partly inflated by a single benign loss year, and a normal hurricane season would reduce the through-cycle resilience reading; the structural strength is unquestioned.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are operative. First, the core Florida book continues to absorb Citizens take-out policies, though this is lumpy and policy-dependent and will diminish as the program shrinks and the market re-privatizes. Second, the reciprocal-exchange vehicles (CORE, Tailrow) are a capital-light lever: Tailrow drove more than half of 2025 Citizens take-outs and reached profitability in 12–15 months, growing consolidated gross premiums and management-fee economics without consolidating the underwriting equity risk (it accrues to NCI). Third, Exzeo's third-party software revenue is ramping as a separately listed company, and management is actively scouting two-to-three "Exzeo-like" ~$1B opportunities in adjacent insurance lines and the insurance value chain.

The growth has genuine durability because it rests on multiple drivers: organic policy growth, a proven take-out engine, a capital-light reciprocal structure, and insurtech monetization optionality. The constraint is that the most visible driver (Citizens depopulation) is finite, and earnings growth from here will be measured against a 2025 base inflated by zero catastrophes. Management is deliberately stockpiling dry powder rather than chasing M&A into hurricane season ("rather do M&A the day after the storm than the day before"), which tempers near-term inorganic growth but is the right discipline.

The Growth Prospects score of 7.0 weights the durable, multi-vector expansion against the diminishing Citizens runway and the high-base comparison. HCI earns an above-average mark on real premium growth and capital-light optionality, but not a top-decile mark given the cyclical normalization ahead.

## Valuation Assessment

The structured valuation feed failed (no peer P/E or analyst targets returned), so valuation rests on first principles and the company's own disclosed marks. On trailing 2025 EPS, the stock near $165 trades at ~7.2x, but that is a trough-loss-ratio number and should not anchor the case. On a normalized basis, applying management's 60% combined-ratio target against the 2025 premium base implies materially lower than $22.72 of EPS; a reasonable through-cycle figure in the $13–16 range puts the forward multiple at roughly 10–13x, in line with quality specialty P&C insurers.

The more important valuation lens is book value. Management pegs pro forma book at ~$145/share, adding the consolidated Exzeo stake (carried at cost, not its ~$1.5B implied IPO valuation) and real estate at fair value. The stock at ~$165 trades roughly 14% above adjusted book while generating a ~35% ROE. A company compounding equity at 35% that trades near adjusted book is structurally cheap, because each year of retained earnings closes the price-to-intrinsic gap mechanically. The mispricing the market is making is a screening artifact: standard data pulls show ~1x stated book and a single-digit trailing P/E, treating both as a value trap, when in fact the stated book understates the asset base by ~$60/share and the trailing multiple understates earning power by overstating the denominator (a zero-cat year).

We deliberately resist scoring valuation higher despite the apparent cheapness. The lesson from this system's track record is that a low multiple is more often a value-trap warning than a buy signal, and cheapness sorts the wrong way absent an independent catalyst. Here there are concrete catalysts that make the discount actionable: continued ~35% ROE book compounding, the embedded Exzeo mark-to-market optionality (an eventual realization or partial monetization that surfaces the gap), the ~2%-per-quarter buyback retiring shares below intrinsic value, and a softening June 1 reinsurance market that protects margins. That combination supports a 7.0 rather than a higher score; the cap on the score is the catastrophe risk that can erase a year's earnings and reset book, which is why the market refuses to fully close the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

HCI's moat is the combination of the Exzeo technology stack and its Citizens take-out execution. The technology allows faster, more selective underwriting than legacy carriers, and the take-out expertise gives it preferential access to a state-sponsored policy supply. The reciprocal-exchange structure is a genuine structural innovation that lets HCI grow premium and earn fees without consuming its own underwriting capital. Cumulative shareholder returns of 406% versus a 132% peer group (proxy, 2021 base) validate that the model has compounded value faster than competitors through the cycle.

Two factors temper the competitive position. The lack of an A.M. Best rating limits expansion with national mortgage lenders and leaves the carriers dependent on the Demotech rating; a Demotech downgrade would trigger meaningful business loss. And the favorable Florida environment is cyclical: today's strong margins will attract reinsurance capacity and new entrants, eventually compressing primary pricing (average premium per policy is already roughly flat YoY). The competitive position is improving on technology and capital-light structure but faces a structural rating limitation and the inevitability of cyclical capacity returning. We score Competitive Position 6.5, slightly below the prior 7.5, because the trajectory of the competitive *advantage* will narrow as the Florida market re-privatizes and Citizens take-out supply shrinks, even as the company itself remains well-run; this dimension warrants confirmation rather than extrapolation of a benign-cycle moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Florida catastrophe concentration is the dominant risk and the reason the score is capped.** Earnings are levered to hurricane outcomes. The 10-K's own sensitivity table shows a 20% adverse change in reserve severity would reduce equity by ~7.8%. This is binary, seasonally timed (hurricane season runs roughly June–November), and not hedgeable beyond the reinsurance program. It is the specific, falsifiable risk that prevents an unqualified high-conviction rating.

**Growth depends on policy assumptions, not purely organic expansion.** Citizens take-outs are lumpy and may not remain available on favorable terms as the program shrinks and the market re-privatizes.

**Reinsurance availability and cost reset every June 1** and are outside HCI's control; 2024 results absorbed $62.9M of retrospective ceded-premium adjustments tied to Milton/Helene. Currently this is a tailwind (softening market), but it can reverse.

**Exzeo conflict-of-interest and NCI dilution (new in FY2025 10-K).** HCI now benefits from only ~82.5% of Exzeo's economics, and a growing share of consolidated income accrues to NCI (consolidated net income was $320.4M vs. $299.0M attributable to HCI). CEO/Chairman Paresh Patel holds roles and equity in both companies, creating explicit conflict risk on intercompany pricing and capital allocation. Analysts should monitor the widening gap between consolidated and attributable net income.

**Holding-company dependence on subsidiary distributions, reciprocal-exchange execution risk, and the legacy United P&C receivership** round out the secondary risks.

We score Risk Factors 5.0, in line with the prior 5.0–5.5 range. The catastrophe concentration is largely priced (it is why the stock trades near adjusted book despite 35% ROE), but it remains a genuine binary that the market is correct to discount, and the new Exzeo governance complexity is an incremental negative not yet fully understood. This dimension is the binding constraint on the overall score; it is a quantified, seasonally-timed exposure to a single peril in a single geography, not a generic "insurance is risky" observation.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 68.5% to 58.5% (a 10.1pp decline) over the six weeks into mid-June while the stock rose 6.9% to $164.54. Falling IV alongside a rising price indicates the market is pricing reduced near-term uncertainty rather than an imminent catalyst, consistent with the post-Q1-beat, pre-hurricane-season lull. IV near 58% remains elevated in absolute terms, reflecting the embedded catastrophe optionality that will re-price as the storm season develops. The available data contains no skew detail, so we make no put-skew inference; the declining IV is a mildly favorable entry-timing read for a long position established before the seasonal volatility build.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** HCI continues compounding equity at ~30–35% ROE with a fortress balance sheet, the June 1 reinsurance placement comes in cheaper and lets the company retain more risk through the captives, the 2026 season stays benign, and the market re-rates the stock toward pro forma book plus a normalized earnings multiple, or Exzeo's ~$1.5B implied value is surfaced through a partial monetization. The buyback at ~2%/quarter accelerates per-share book growth. Bull case ~$220 (roughly normalized book/intrinsic plus Exzeo mark realization), ~33% above the current ~$165.

**Bear case.** A major hurricane makes landfall in HCI's concentrated Florida book, the combined ratio spikes toward or above 100% as in 2022, GAAP book takes a hit, and the stock derates toward stressed book. The Exzeo governance conflict surfaces a value-leakage event, or Citizens take-out supply dries up faster than expected. Bear case ~$115, roughly 30% below current and approaching the pro forma book less a storm-driven equity haircut.

The asymmetry is favorable on a through-cycle basis because the downside is a known, transient, insurable event (a bad storm year) against an upside of durable compounding at a wide discount to adjusted intrinsic value, but the bear case is real and seasonally proximate, which argues for position sizing rather than maximum conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $190.** Reasoning: pro forma book growing at ~30% ROE less buyback/dividend, plus a modest normalization haircut to the multiple, yields ~$190 on a 12-month view as retained earnings close the price-to-intrinsic gap and the Exzeo stake is increasingly recognized. ~15% above the current ~$165.
- **Bull case target: $220** (+33%), if the season stays benign, reinsurance softens, and Exzeo value is partially surfaced.
- **Bear case target: $115** (−30%), if a major hurricane hits the Florida book and the combined ratio spikes.
- **Timeframe: 12–18 months,** with the dominant variable resolving across the June–November 2026 hurricane season and the June 1 reinsurance placement.
- **Upside/downside from current (~$165):** +15% to base, +33% to bull, −30% to bear.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:** (1) a Demotech rating downgrade or any A.M. Best-equivalent rating action; (2) the through-cycle combined ratio guide moving above 70% on a normalized (cat-adjusted) basis, signaling underwriting deterioration; (3) Citizens take-out availability collapsing such that organic premium growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters; (4) an Exzeo intercompany-pricing or capital-allocation event that materially leaks value to NCI or to the dual-role CEO.

## Conclusion

HCI is a well-capitalized, founder-led specialty insurer compounding equity at ~35% ROE with a near-debt-free balance sheet, trading near GAAP book while adjusted book including the consolidated Exzeo stake and real estate is ~$145/share. The 2025 record overstates normalized earning power because it was a zero-catastrophe year, and any valuation must run through-cycle against the 60% ±5% target. Even with that haircut, the combination of durable premium growth, a capital-light reciprocal structure, embedded Exzeo monetization optionality, ~2%/quarter buybacks below intrinsic value, and a softening reinsurance market produces favorable asymmetry. The binding risk is Florida catastrophe concentration, a quantified, seasonally-timed, binary exposure that caps the score at the risk dimension rather than reflecting any fundamental weakness. We rate HCI a BUY with sizing discipline appropriate to a name whose principal downside arrives between June and November. The validation flag is addressed directly: the overall score sits modestly above the BUY threshold, and the recommendation is BUY (not HOLD) because the discount to adjusted book and the 35% ROE compounding constitute the independent catalysts that justify rewarding the apparent cheapness, while the catastrophe risk is reflected in disciplined position sizing rather than a downgrade to HOLD.
