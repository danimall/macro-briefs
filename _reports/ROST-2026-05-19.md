## Executive Summary

Ross Stores is the largest U.S. off-price apparel retailer with 2,267 stores across two banners, generating $22.8B in revenue and $2.2B in free cash flow in FY2026. The company delivered accelerating comparable store sales from 1% in Q3 FY2025 to 7% in Q3 FY2026 and an implied ~9% in Q4, driven by both traffic and basket gains under new CEO James Conroy. The business model generates 35% ROE, is actively shrinking its share count, and maintains $4.6B in cash against only $1.5B of debt.

The core tension is valuation. At $211 and 31.9x trailing earnings (~28.4x FY2027 consensus of $7.42), the stock capitalizes several years of above-trend comp growth in a business where Morningstar's long-term comp assumption is 3% and fair value sits at $148. The options market confirms near-term event risk with 81% near-term IV against 25% realized, suggesting an imminent earnings catalyst (likely May 22 report). While the business quality is undeniable and the off-price model structurally benefits from consumer trade-down and tariff-driven supply chain chaos, current pricing requires sustained 5%+ comps to justify the multiple. That is achievable but far from certain given rising input costs from China tariffs (affecting >50% of merchandise sourced), and the inherent mean-reversion tendency of retail comps.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is quantifiable: at $7.42 FY2027 EPS and a 28.4x forward multiple, ROST trades at a PEG ratio of ~2.6x on its 11% expected EPS growth rate. Historically, ROST's PEG has ranged from 1.5-2.5x; the current premium end of that range requires continued above-consensus execution that is plausible but insufficiently asymmetric for a new long position at this level.

## Company Overview

Ross Stores operates two off-price retail banners: Ross Dress for Less (1,904 stores) and dd's DISCOUNTS (363 stores), serving primarily middle-income and lower-income households respectively. The model is exclusively brick-and-mortar, U.S.-only, with no e-commerce presence. Competitive advantage derives from: (1) 800+ merchants across three buying offices enabling opportunistic sourcing of branded closeouts at 20-70% below full-price retail, (2) a "packaway" inventory system that allows accumulation during supply dislocations, (3) scale-driven purchasing leverage with a ~30% domestic off-price apparel market share, and (4) a "treasure hunt" in-store experience that generates repeat traffic without marketing intensity.

Management targets a long-term store count of ~3,600 (vs. 2,267 today), implying a 60%+ growth runway over a multi-year horizon. FY2027 plans call for 110 new stores including 25 dd's DISCOUNTS — a material acceleration from 10 dd's openings in FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Comps:** FY2026 revenue reached $22.8B (+8% YoY) with 5% full-year comp growth. The quarterly trajectory was the standout: Q3 comps accelerated to 7% and Q4 implied ~9%, driven equally by traffic and basket size. This is exceptional for a 2,200+ store chain.

**Profitability:** Operating margin of 11.9% (down 35bps YoY) reflected temporary deleverage from the new Arizona distribution center and tariff-related merchandise margin compression (~20bps). Management has guided for "slightly higher" operating margins in FY2027 as DC costs normalize and merchandise margin improves. Net margin of 9.4% and ROE of 34.7% demonstrate exceptional capital efficiency for a retailer.

**Free Cash Flow:** $2.21B FCF in FY2026 (9.7% FCF margin, >100% conversion vs. net income) supports $1.05B annual buybacks, $528M dividends, $819M capex, and $950M debt reduction simultaneously.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt reduced from $2.2B to $1.5B over two years. Only $500M due April 2026, fully covered by cash. Altman Z-Score of 3.05 confirms financial safety. Net cash position is substantial and growing.

**Share Count:** Reduced 3.8% over two years (337M to 324M diluted shares). New $2.55B buyback authorization provides 2+ years of repurchase capacity at current pace.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Comp Store Acceleration (near-term):** The 7%/9% comp trajectory in H2 FY2026 reflects new CEO initiatives and macro-driven trade-down. Consensus expects $7.42 EPS for FY2027 (+12% growth) and $8.19 for FY2028 (+10%). Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year EPS up +3.3% over 90 days, next year up +3.2%. This is positive earnings momentum but well below the +30% threshold that signals a structural shift in analyst expectations.

**2. Store Growth (medium-term):** 110 new stores per year represents ~5% annual unit growth. Combined with 3-5% comps, this supports high-single-digit revenue growth. The dd's DISCOUNTS reacceleration (25 stores planned in FY2027) addresses a lower-income demographic that benefits most from consumer trade-down.

**3. Long-term Store Count Target (multi-year):** The ~3,600 store target implies $40B+ revenue potential over time, roughly doubling today's base. This runway provides visibility into sustained mid-to-high single-digit growth for the foreseeable future.

**Limiting factor:** The +6.0% current-quarter EPS revision (90-day) and +3.3% current-year revision, while directionally positive, do not constitute the magnitude of earnings revision that would signal systematic market underestimation. The stock has already appreciated 37% over the past year, partially pricing in the comp acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $211.08, ROST trades at:
- 31.9x trailing EPS ($6.61)
- ~28.4x FY2027 consensus EPS ($7.42)
- ~25.8x FY2028 consensus EPS ($8.19)
- PEG of ~2.6x on 11% EPS growth
- FCF yield of ~3.3% ($2.2B / $67.7B market cap)

**Morningstar pegs fair value at $148** — a 30% discount to current price. Their model assumes 3% long-term comp growth and steady-state margins. The current price requires sustained 5-6% comps for 3+ years to justify the multiple, which would be historically unprecedented for ROST (5-year average comp was ~4% including the post-COVID recovery period).

**Analyst consensus:** Median target of $244 (16% upside), high of $290 (37% upside), low of $158 (25% downside). The distribution of outcomes is roughly symmetric around current levels, typical when a stock has already re-rated into a higher valuation band.

**Peer context:** TJX trades at ~28-30x forward earnings with a broader international presence and e-commerce channel. Burlington at 25-27x with a higher growth rate from a smaller base. ROST's 28x forward sits at the upper end of its own historical 20-28x range and at parity with TJX despite less diversification.

The valuation is not outright broken — this is a wide-moat, 35% ROE business that deserves a premium — but it provides limited margin of safety if comps decelerate to their 3% historical norm. At $185-195 (25-26x FY2027 EPS), the risk/reward would shift materially.

## Competitive Landscape

Ross's competitive position is strong and improving:

**Market position:** ~30% domestic off-price apparel share. 10-year average ROIC of 30% vs. 9% WACC confirms durable economic moat. Scale advantages in purchasing, distribution, and vendor relationships are difficult to replicate.

**Structural tailwind:** Consumer trade-down behavior benefits off-price disproportionately during periods of inflation and real wage compression. Department store consolidation simultaneously increases both available store locations and closeout inventory supply.

**Tariff paradox:** While tariffs on Chinese goods raise input costs (>50% of merchandise originates from China), they also create more off-price buying opportunities as full-price retailers miscalculate demand, cancel orders, and find themselves overstocked. Ross's packaway model is specifically designed to capitalize on such disruptions. The $0.16/share FY2026 headwind may be a short-term cost of a long-term supply advantage.

**Competitive vulnerabilities:** No e-commerce presence (limits reach), no international presence (limits TAM vs. TJX), and geographic concentration (50% of stores in CA/TX/FL).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Escalation (HIGH):** If tariffs increase further or diversification fails, merchandise margin compression could exceed 50bps, directly pressuring the operating margin expansion thesis management has guided.

**2. Comp Normalization (MODERATE-HIGH):** The 7-9% comp trajectory in H2 FY2026 is unusual for a mature retailer. Mean reversion to 3-4% is historically normal and would result in EPS below consensus, compressing the multiple.

**3. Consumer Deterioration Beyond Trade-Down (MODERATE):** If recession materializes and the lower-income consumer significantly curtails spending even at value channels, Ross's traffic gains could reverse.

**4. China Supply Disruption (MODERATE):** Beyond tariffs, supply chain disruptions could reduce merchandise availability for off-price channels that depend on consistent closeout flow from full-price retailers.

**5. New CEO Execution (LOW-MODERATE):** Conroy has been in seat since December 2024 with strong initial results. His performance-based RSU grant creates pressure to maintain above-trend metrics through FY2029, potentially at the expense of margin discipline.

## Investment Thesis

- Tariff chaos creates favorable off-price buying opportunities, expanding merchandise margins beyond guidance
- dd's DISCOUNTS reacceleration proves out with strong new-store productivity
- FY2027 EPS reaches $7.80-8.00 (above $7.42 consensus) on margin expansion + buybacks

### Base Case ($220-235, +4-11%)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $185-195 (25-26x FY2027 EPS), providing adequate margin of safety for a wide-moat compounder
2. FY2027 Q1 comp report confirms sustained 5%+ trajectory with explicit merchandise margin improvement (would validate above-consensus EPS path)
3. Tariff resolution that removes the >50% China sourcing overhang

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Comp sales decelerate below 2% for two consecutive quarters (would confirm mean reversion and invalidate premium multiple)
2. Operating margin contracts below 11% due to unmanageable tariff costs
3. FCF declines below $1.8B (would indicate capex/margin issues undermining the financial model)

**Reassessment timeline:** May 22, 2026 (Q1 FY2027 earnings report, given the May 22 options expiry alignment). This report will provide the first clean read on post-Q4 comp trajectory, tariff cost quantification for FY2027, and updated margin guidance.

## Conclusion

Ross Stores is a superior business: wide moat, 35% ROE, $2.2B FCF, meaningful store growth runway, and a structural tailwind from consumer trade-down. The comp acceleration under Conroy is genuine and supported by both traffic and basket gains. The tariff risk is real but double-edged, and the balance sheet provides ample buffer.

At 28.4x forward earnings on a PEG of 2.6x with only +3.3% 90-day estimate revisions, the stock prices in sustained above-trend execution without offering adequate compensation for comp normalization risk. The bull case of $250+ requires 5%+ comps through FY2027 — achievable but far from base case for a 2,200+ store mature retailer. The bear case of $160-175 (24-25% downside) has meaningful probability if comps revert to 3% and tariff costs exceed expectations.

HOLD with a growth orientation. The business is worth owning; the entry price is the constraint. At $185-195, the risk/reward shifts decisively favorable. At $211, upside to consensus targets (14% to median $244) is insufficient relative to the 17-24% downside in a normalization scenario, particularly given the extreme options-implied uncertainty around the imminent earnings report.
