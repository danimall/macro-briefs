**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Worthington Enterprises is the consumer-and-building-products entity left after the December 2023 spin-off of Worthington Steel. The historical financials (~$4.9B revenue) bear no relation to the current ~$1.3B run-rate business, so the relevant track record is short. What is visible in that record is encouraging: six consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS and EBITDA growth, 9-month FY26 net sales of $1,009.8M (+20.8% YoY), and adjusted EBITDA of $212.3M (+18.0%). The Building Products segment is the profit engine (+33.4% nine-month sales growth, ~28% EBITDA margin), supplemented by a Consumer Products segment posting solid Q3 margin expansion.

The structural feature that defines WOR is that a large share of its economic earnings flows through two unconsolidated joint ventures it does not control: WAVE (50%, ceiling suspension systems, ~46% operating margin) and ClarkDietrich (25%, light-gauge framing). In the most recent quarter, JV equity income of $30.7M roughly equaled the entire consolidated operating income of $31.5M. Any valuation that ignores the JVs understates earnings power; any valuation that fully capitalizes them must discount for the lack of control. WAVE is performing exceptionally (it returns more cash than its carrying value), while ClarkDietrich is the soft spot, with equity income down roughly 44% year-to-date on weak non-residential construction.

The scores have improved meaningfully over the past three reports (5.0 → 5.0 → 6.6), reflecting a re-rating as acquisition momentum and a data-center/liquid-cooling narrative took hold. New data this cycle is mixed rather than confirming: the stock has recovered from the high-$40s to $60, but consensus estimates are falling (current quarter -6.0% over 30 days, next quarter -6.5% over 30 days), the options market is pricing imminent stress (near-term IV 42.8% vs 29.4% historical, term structure in backwardation), and the trailing multiple has expanded back toward 27x. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: this is a quality, owner-operated compounder worth owning through the cycle, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Worthington Enterprises operates two consolidated segments plus a set of high-margin equity-method joint ventures:

- **Building Products (~61% of FY26 YTD sales)**: pressurized containment (refrigerant and LPG cylinders, well-water/expansion tanks, specialty tanks), HVAC components (Elgen), metal roofing clips (LSI), and propane assets (Hydrostat). Nine-month sales of $616.1M (+33.4%), adjusted EBITDA $171.8M (27.9% margin).
- **Consumer Products (~39%)**: tools, outdoor living (griddles, pizza ovens), celebrations (helium balloon kits), torches, and drywall tools, sold through mass merchandisers and big-box retail. Nine-month sales $393.7M (+5.2%), ~17% adjusted EBITDA margin.

The joint ventures are the swing factor. WAVE (Worthington Armstrong Venture, 50%) dominates suspended ceiling grid systems, an oligopolistic niche where its ~46% operating margins are difficult for peers to match. ClarkDietrich (25%) competes in more commoditized light-gauge steel framing and is currently pressured by soft commercial construction. Nine-month pre-tax earnings of $141.6M included $96.5M of JV equity income, which illustrates that WOR functions as a holding company over two cash-generative ventures plus a growing owned-products portfolio.

The founding McConnell family retains roughly 35% ownership, giving the company an owner-operator orientation. Management runs the "Worthington Business System," a disciplined acquisition-plus-operational-improvement playbook, and has executed three deals in roughly seven months (Elgen, Hydrostat, LSI).

## Financial Analysis

**Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended Feb 28, 2026):** Net sales of $378.7M (+24.4% YoY), with Building Products +35.9% and Consumer Products +10.8%. Acquisitions contributed $32.2M of the Building Products growth, with healthy organic growth underneath. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.92 (vs $0.79); adjusted EPS $0.98 (vs $0.91). Consolidated adjusted EBITDA was $84.6M at a 22.3% margin, up 14.6%.

**Nine-month FY2026:** GAAP EPS $2.17 (+18%); operating cash flow $154.5M (up from $147.3M).

**Margin nuance:** Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin slipped year-over-year (22.3% vs 24.2% in Q3; 21.0% vs 21.5% YTD). The decline is driven by acquisition mix (LSI and Elgen carry lower margins than the legacy base) and ClarkDietrich softness (equity income down $12.2M YTD), partly offset by WAVE strength (+$8.4M YTD). Underlying Consumer Products margins expanded, with a 24% EBITDA increase in Q3. The compression is mix and inventory step-up amortization, not competitive erosion.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Total assets $1.82B; total equity $1.0B; long-term debt $307M carrying. Cash fell sharply from $250M (May 2025) to $6.0M (Feb 2026), but this funded ~$303M of acquisitions (Elgen $90.7M, Hydrostat $9.6M, LSI $206M) with only $4.8M drawn on a $500M revolver. The $495M of remaining revolver capacity confirms this is deliberate capital deployment, not distress. The $109.6M "distributions in excess of investment" liability on WAVE reflects the JV returning more cash than its book value, a quality-of-earnings positive (WOR received $105.5M of JV distributions over nine months).

**Earnings delivery:** The four-quarter record shows three beats and one miss (most recent quarter +0.9%, prior quarter -8.6%, +4.4%, prior-year quarter +24.4%). The pattern is choppy rather than reliably ahead of consensus, which is consistent with cyclical end-markets and JV income that WOR cannot smooth.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth rate is strong but should be parsed for source. Above-market sales growth is being driven by acquisitions, share gains, and Consumer Products pricing rather than a rising end-market tide. The building-products group faces a soft cycle: HMI builder sentiment at 36 (below the neutral 50), the Architecture Billings Index in contraction, the Dodge Momentum Index down 7.3% in February, and housing starts below the prior year. Mortgage rates near 6% continue to pressure affordability and forward demand.

Three growth vectors merit attention:

1. **Acquisition roll-up.** LSI ($206M, closed Jan 2026) expands WOR into commercial standing-seam metal roofing and retrofit components, adding $108.6M of intangibles and $76.9M goodwill. The strategy is coherent and the integration playbook is proven, but three deals in seven months carry execution and integration risk, and GAAP results are currently muddied by step-up amortization.

2. **Data-center / liquid-cooling optionality.** Management repeatedly frames product lines as beneficiaries of data-center buildout and liquid cooling. This is real but should be treated as optionality, not the core driver — WOR is a component supplier of pressurized-containment and cooling-related parts, not a pure-play cooling product. The 10-Q itself describes data centers as a relative bright spot within an otherwise soft non-residential pipeline. It is not yet a dominant revenue line.

3. **ClarkDietrich recovery.** The single biggest swing variable. The YTD equity-income decline is the largest drag on otherwise strong results. If non-residential construction stabilizes, this is a meaningful tailwind; if it deteriorates, it is the most exposed profit line.

The estimate-revision picture is the most important new datapoint and it cuts against the momentum read. Current-quarter and next-quarter consensus have fallen on a 30-day basis (next-quarter also 6.5% over 90 days), with current-year and next-year both modestly negative. This is broad-based negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the inflection signal that would warrant treating the stock as a cyclical-trough buy. We weight growth prospects as a genuine strength of the business but discount the near-term trajectory given the revision trend.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.26, WOR trades at ~27x trailing EPS of $1.92 and roughly 17.5x current-year consensus of $3.44 / ~15.5x next-year $3.90. The forward multiples are more reasonable than the trailing optics suggest, but the trailing distortion here is from acquisition noise and JV swings, not a cyclical earnings trough, so the cycle-adjusted "buy the trough" framing does not apply.

Peer comparison is unusually heterogeneous. The Building Products median P/E is 23.7 (BCC), but the peer set spans Sunrun at 5.9x, Masterbrand at negative earnings, and Griffon at 99x. The dispersion means the median is a weak anchor. WOR's ~27x trailing premium to BCC requires justification, and the justification is partly there (the high-quality, cash-rich WAVE earnings stream, a disciplined owner-operator) and partly absent (falling estimates, a soft cycle, and a sub-market dividend yield of ~1.3%).

The right framework is sum-of-the-parts. The consolidated business (Building Products plus Consumer) at ~$212M nine-month adjusted EBITDA carries a defensible mid-cycle value, and the JV stakes — particularly WAVE — add material value that simple consolidated multiples miss. On that basis the stock is not egregiously expensive, but it is also not cheap: the market has re-rated WOR from the low-$50s to $60 as the data-center narrative and acquisition cadence took hold, and the current price captures much of the SOTP upside. Analyst targets (mean $65.4, median $69.0, high $76, low $47) imply ~8-15% upside to the central estimates, which on a name with falling near-term estimates does not constitute the kind of analyst-lag-on-an-inflection setup where targets function as floors. We score valuation below neutral: the price reflects fair value with limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

WOR's competitive position is the strongest part of the story and the dimension that has improved most across recent reports (5.0 → 7.0). WAVE is a structural advantage, a dominant oligopolistic position in suspended ceiling grid alongside Armstrong World Industries, with margins few building-products peers can approach. The Building Products portfolio holds defensible niche positions in pressurized containment and is being broadened through bolt-on M&A into adjacent categories (HVAC components, commercial roofing).

The trajectory is positive: above-market sales growth, expanding Consumer margins, and a widening platform. The qualifiers are that ClarkDietrich operates in a more commoditized, cyclical framing market currently losing share of profit to pricing pressure, and that a meaningful slice of the moat (WAVE, ClarkDietrich) sits in entities WOR does not control. A market-leading position WOR cannot direct is worth less than one it can. Net, the competitive trajectory is improving, and we score it accordingly while holding back from a higher mark given the control limitation.

## Risk Assessment

1. **JV dependency and limited control.** A majority of economic earnings flow through WAVE and ClarkDietrich (25-50% stakes, equity method). WOR cannot direct strategy, capital, or distributions. ClarkDietrich's equity-income decline shows the downside when a non-controlled profit source weakens.

2. **Cyclical end-market exposure.** Demand tracks residential and non-residential construction and repair/remodel, with the leading indicators currently soft. This is permanently priced for the sector, but the cycle's direction is a near-term swing factor.

3. **Customer concentration.** One big-box customer represented 12.1% of consolidated net sales in Q3. Channel destocking/restocking can move reported results.

4. **Raw material and tariff exposure.** Steel is the largest input (hot-rolled ~$984/ton in Feb, up from $855 in Nov; cold-rolled ~$1,126), with aluminum rising on Section 232 tariffs. The Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs (immediately replaced under alternate authority) creates an ambiguous, evolving cost/benefit with potential but unrecorded refunds.

5. **Related-party steel dependence.** WOR sources flat-rolled steel from former sibling Worthington Steel ($103.5M over nine months) under a supply agreement.

6. **Acquisition integration.** Three deals in seven months; the strategy is sound but execution-dependent, and GAAP results are currently obscured by step-up amortization and deal costs.

The 10-Q states risk factors have not changed materially from the FY25 10-K — no new disclosed risks. The most underappreciated near-term risk is the combination of falling estimates and an options market pricing imminent stress.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing caution that diverges from the constructive fundamental narrative. Near-term ATM IV is 42.8% versus 29.4% one-year historical volatility (rich by 13.4 points), the IV percentile sits at 75%, and the term structure is in backwardation (24-day 42.8% vs 115-day 35.9%), so the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. Put/call skew of 6.8% indicates elevated put demand, and IV rose 7.2 points (35.6% → 42.8%) even as the stock climbed 7.3%, a pattern that signals rising uncertainty alongside the rally rather than complacency. Short interest is low at 2.8% of float (5.2 days to cover), so this is hedging demand, not a crowded short. For a holder, the read is that near-term realized volatility is likely to be high and the next report carries binary risk; for a prospective buyer, it argues for patience rather than chasing into a rich-vol, falling-estimate setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$72-76):** ClarkDietrich stabilizes as non-residential construction troughs, WAVE continues to generate cash, the acquisition roll-up compounds at high incremental returns, the data-center/liquid-cooling component demand grows into a visible revenue line, and Consumer Products margin expansion continues. On a sum-of-the-parts basis with a re-rating to reflect the JV earnings quality, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range. Six consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS/EBITDA growth and an owner-operator with 35% family alignment support a durable compounding story.

**Bear Case (~$47):** The construction cycle deteriorates further, ClarkDietrich equity income keeps falling, acquisition integration disappoints or dilutes returns, falling estimates continue, and the near-term volatility the options market is pricing resolves to the downside. The premium trailing multiple compresses toward the BCC peer level (~23x) on lower forward earnings, and the stock retests the 52-week low near $45, the lower analyst target.

The base case sits between these: a quality business with genuine momentum, but priced near fair value with falling near-term estimates and an unfavorable near-term volatility setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ClarkDietrich equity income stabilizes or inflects positive for two consecutive quarters, removing the largest current earnings drag.
- Consensus estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and current-year revisions back above zero on a 30-day basis), reversing the present -5% to -6% trend.
- A pullback to the low-$50s (toward the level where management deployed buybacks and granted deferred comp) restores a margin of safety against the sum-of-the-parts value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Estimate revisions accelerate downward (current-year cut >10%) alongside a guidance reduction or a missed quarter, signaling cyclical deterioration rather than mix noise.
- Acquisition integration produces an impairment of the LSI/Elgen goodwill or a sustained organic-revenue decline.
- A material customer loss in Consumer Products (the 12.1% concentration) or a sharp WAVE distribution cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q4 FY26, expected late July, fiscal year ends May 31) is the natural reassessment point, both for the estimate-revision trend and for the elevated near-term volatility the options market is pricing. The ClarkDietrich and construction-cycle reads at that print are the key inputs.

## Conclusion

Worthington Enterprises is a well-run, family-aligned compounder whose economic value is anchored by two cash-generative joint ventures and a disciplined acquisition roll-up. The improving score trajectory across recent reports is justified by progress: above-market growth, a strengthening competitive position around WAVE, and a conservative balance sheet that has been deployed into accretive M&A rather than hoarded. We confirm the constructive direction of travel on competitive position and financial health.

What we do not confirm is that the current price offers asymmetric upside. The stock has re-rated to $60, the trailing multiple is full at ~27x, near-term consensus estimates are falling, ClarkDietrich remains a drag, the construction cycle is soft, and the options market is pricing imminent stress into the next print. This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the bar for changing it is appropriately higher, but the new data is mixed rather than decisively bullish, so HOLD remains the call. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$50s or on confirmation that ClarkDietrich and the estimate trend have inflected. For existing holders, this is a quality position to retain through the cycle; for new capital, patience is warranted.
