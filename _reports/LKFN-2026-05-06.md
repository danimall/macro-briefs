## Executive Summary

Lakeland Financial Corporation is a $7.1 billion community bank operating exclusively in Indiana through Lake City Bank. The company reported record Q1 2026 net income of $26.5M (+32% YoY), driven by NIM expansion to 3.49% and normalized credit costs after substantially resolving a $43.3M nonperforming relationship that dominated the prior two years' narrative. With CET1 at 14.77% (as of December 31, 2025), an active buyback program ($21.2M remaining), and a director purchasing $1.4M in stock at $55-58 in March 2026, the balance sheet and alignment signals are strong.

The stock trades at approximately 14.1x the estimated 2026 EPS of $4.34 (based on sell-side consensus), which represents a modest premium to the estimated peer median P/E of approximately 13.4x. This premium is justified by LKFN's materially superior profitability metrics (ROA 1.50%, efficiency ratio sub-49%, ROE 14%+), 35-year organic growth track record, and continued NIM tailwind from deposit repricing. The primary risks are Indiana geographic concentration, tariff exposure to the state's manufacturing economy (particularly the RV industry at 4.5% of loans), and a Q1 2026 brokered deposit surge that bears monitoring. At current levels, the risk/reward favors a modest position with a 12-month target of $68-70.

## Company Overview

Lakeland Financial operates Lake City Bank, a 153-year-old Indiana-chartered community bank with 55 branches across 15 counties. The bank has grown entirely organically since its 1990 expansion outside its home county, from $286M in assets to $7.1B today (approximately 10% CAGR in loans, deposits, net income, and tangible book value per share over that period). Revenue is predominantly net interest income (~82%), supplemented by wealth advisory services ($3.7B AUM), treasury management, and deposit fees.

The loan portfolio is heavily commercial: C&I at 29%, CRE/multifamily at 50%, agri-business at 8%, with consumer/residential making up the remainder. The deposit base comprises 33% public funds, 37% commercial, and 30% retail, with 99.2% classified as core deposits at year-end 2025. The bank is actively expanding into Indianapolis (9 branches currently, with 2 additional locations under development for late 2026/early 2027).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:** FY 2025 delivered record net income of $103.4M ($4.01 EPS, +10.6% YoY), and the momentum accelerated into Q1 2026 with $26.5M ($1.04 EPS, +32% YoY). The primary driver is NIM expansion — from 3.18% in FY 2024 to 3.45% in FY 2025 to 3.49% in Q1 2026. The mechanism: deposit repricing has outpaced loan repricing since rate cuts began in September 2024. As of Q4 2025, cumulative deposit beta stood at 40% vs. loan beta of 24%, a favorable gap that management indicated continued to widen into Q1 2026.

**Efficiency:** The 48.93% efficiency ratio (FY 2025) is excellent for community banks, where peers typically run 55-65% based on industry surveys. Q1 2026 showed a slight uptick to ~50.4% as the company added 27 FTEs and invested in its Innovation & Technology Center, but this reflects deliberate growth spending rather than deterioration.

**Capital Position:** CET1 of 14.77% (as of December 31, 2025) and total capital of 15.92% provide substantial buffers above well-capitalized thresholds. Tangible book value reached $29.87/share at year-end 2025 (growing 11% YoY by Q1 2026). The company returned capital through a 4% dividend increase ($2.08 annualized) and aggressive buybacks — repurchasing 337,890 shares in FY 2025 and an additional 336,853 shares in Q1 2026, at an approximate average price of $57-58/share. The board expanded its repurchase authorization to $60M in March 2026, with $21.2M remaining as of March 31, 2026.

**Asset Quality:** The dominant credit event is substantially resolved. The $43.3M nonperforming commercial relationship resulted in $28.6M net charge-offs (with $800K recovery), and NPLs have dropped to 0.39% of loans (from 1.10% a year earlier). Allowance coverage stands at 329% of NPLs. Watch list loans declined to 3.33% (from 4.13%), though the Q4 2025 release noted net downgrades concentrated with two unrelated commercial borrowers during that quarter. Provision expense normalized to $2.0M in Q1 2026 versus $6.8M a year ago.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $114.9M in FY 2025, with FCF of $103.8M after $11.1M in capex. The ~50% dividend payout ratio is sustainable with room for continued buybacks.

**Funding Concern:** Brokered deposits surged from $50.6M (0.8%) at year-end 2025 to an estimated $375.6M (6.1%) by March 2026, based on Q1 2026 disclosures. Management attributed this to seasonal public fund outflows and one-time commercial deposit reductions. If this persists beyond seasonal patterns, it could pressure funding costs and compress NIM in subsequent quarters.

## Growth Analysis

Sell-side consensus estimates project approximately $4.34 EPS for 2026 (+8.2% YoY) and $4.53 for 2027 (+4.4%). These estimates have been revised upward modestly — +1.5% over 90 days for 2026, +0.8% for 2027. The revision trajectory is positive but not aggressive enough to trigger the strong BUY signal threshold.

Growth drivers include:
1. **Continued NIM tailwind** — deposit repricing still running ahead of loan repricing through mid-2026
2. **Loan growth** — 5% annualized in Q1 2026 with line utilization at 45% (highest since 2019), suggesting organic demand
3. **Indianapolis expansion** — a much larger addressable market than Northern Indiana, with 2 new branches opening late 2026/early 2027
4. **Normalized credit costs** — provision returning to approximately $2M/quarter vs. $6-7M a year ago represents a meaningful pre-tax earnings tailwind on an annualized basis

Growth constraints include the single-state geographic footprint, mature Northern Indiana markets, and the deliberate pace of organic expansion. This is a steady mid-to-high single digit EPS grower.

## Competitive Landscape

LKFN's competitive position is improving. The 35-year organic growth track record demonstrates sticky customer relationships and deep market knowledge that larger competitors struggle to replicate. The bank's five-region structure with localized commercial/retail decision-making provides the speed and relationship depth that differentiates community banks from regional players.

Key competitive advantages:
- In-market decision-making authority (loan decisions made locally, not at distant headquarters)
- 153-year brand heritage in Northern Indiana
- Sub-49% efficiency ratio enables competitive pricing while maintaining profitability
- Wealth advisory ($3.7B AUM) creates additional relationship stickiness

The Indianapolis expansion represents the primary competitive challenge — entering a larger, more competitive market against established players. However, the bank has been in Indianapolis since 2011 and now operates 9 branches there, suggesting the model translates.

Emerging threats include credit union expansion (tax-advantaged competitors), fintech deposit alternatives, and private credit funds competing for commercial lending. None of these represent near-term existential risks but collectively compress growth opportunities at the margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic Concentration:** Operating exclusively in Indiana creates undiversifiable regional economic risk. Northern Indiana's manufacturing economy (RV industry in Elkhart County = 4.5% of total loans) faces direct tariff exposure. Agricultural lending (7.6% of loans) adds weather, commodity, and avian flu risk.

**Tariff/Trade Policy:** The 2025 10-K emphasizes tariff risk — management clearly views this as material. Indiana is a manufacturing-intensive state, and LKFN's C&I portfolio includes meaningful manufacturing exposure. A sustained trade war could weaken loan demand and credit quality simultaneously.

**Brokered Deposit Surge:** The jump from 0.8% to an estimated 6.1% brokered deposits in one quarter signals potential funding pressure. If this proves structural rather than seasonal, NIM compression follows. The June 2026 quarter-end data will clarify whether the shift is persistent.

**CRE Concentration:** At 49.5% of total loans, CRE exposure is elevated. While there is no bright-line "50% of loans" regulatory cap, regulators evaluate CRE concentration relative to capital using guidance thresholds, and LKFN's level warrants monitoring. Multi-family grew rapidly ($253M → $463M over two years). While office exposure is minimal (1.9%), rising rates and potential oversupply in multi-family represent medium-term risks.

**Elevated Short Interest:** Short interest is reported at approximately 10% of float with approximately 10.9 days to cover (per market-data vendors; not independently verifiable from SEC filings). This is elevated for a community bank and suggests some institutional investors see vulnerabilities the bull case may underweight — potentially related to funding dynamics or macro sensitivity.

## Conclusion

The stock trades at a modest premium to peers (approximately 14.1x vs. estimated 13.4x median peer P/E) that is justified by materially superior profitability metrics.
