**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gartner is the dominant independent research and advisory franchise for enterprise technology and business leaders, generating roughly $6.5B in revenue across a recurring subscription core (Insights, ~78% of revenue), a high-margin events business (Conferences, ~10%), and project-based Consulting (~9%). It is a genuinely cash-generative, asset-light business: FY25 operating cash flow was $1.29B, Q1'26 free cash flow rose 29% year-on-year to $371M, and the model runs on paid-in-advance subscriptions with ~77% contribution margins. On these metrics alone it looks like a high-quality compounder trading at a distressed multiple.

The problem is the leading indicator. Insights contract value grew only +1% FX-neutral in both FY25 and Q1'26, a sharp deceleration from years of high-single to low-double-digit compounding. Wallet retention has fallen below 100% — GTS from 102% to 96-97%, GBS from 106% to 98-99% — meaning the average retained client is now spending less on renewal. This is the clearest quantitative signal that demand is softening, and it coincides directly with the emergence of generative AI and large language models as a substitute for exactly what Gartner sells: synthesized expert research. The GAAP and adjusted EPS lines are still rising, but that growth is almost entirely a product of a ~10% annual reduction in share count and an 8% headcount cut, not organic expansion.

The stock has de-rated roughly 70% from its early-2025 peak (Q1'25 buybacks were executed near $508 per share; the shares now trade near $134), a repricing that reflects the market handicapping meaningful terminal-value impairment from AI. At ~10x forward earnings and a 7-8% FCF yield, the valuation offers absolute support and the buyback is now far more accretive than it was at the peak. The multiple compressed because the growth engine stalled, and the direction in which the AI question resolves is unresolved. We rate the shares HOLD (uncertain), with the next two to four quarters of contract value and wallet retention data as the decisive evidence.

## Company Overview

Gartner serves more than 13,000 client enterprises across approximately 90 countries through three segments:

- **Insights (~$5.07B FY25):** Subscription research and advisory, split into Global Technology Sales (GTS — CIOs, IT leaders, technology vendors, ~$4.0B contract value) and Global Business Sales (GBS — HR, finance, supply chain, marketing leaders, ~$1.3B contract value). Roughly 77% of contracts are multi-year, revenue is paid in advance, and contribution margins run near 77%. This is the crown jewel.
- **Conferences (~$645M FY25):** In-person events anchored by the Symposium/Xpo series; 53 conferences and ~83,000 attendees in 2025. Higher-margin and growing.
- **Consulting (~$552M FY25):** Project-based, CIO-focused work — the most cyclical and currently the weakest segment.

The competitive foundation is a 40-plus-year brand synonymous with independent IT research (the Magic Quadrant framework), a network of 2,400-plus analysts, and 510,000-plus annual client interactions that create strong operating leverage: content is created once and monetized across subscriptions, events, and consulting. Management itself acknowledges low barriers to entry and rising competition from free internet content and, more consequentially, generative AI.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has decelerated in a steady sequence: $5.48B (2022) → $5.91B (2023) → $6.27B (2024) → $6.50B (2025, +4% reported, +3% FX-neutral). Q1'26 revenue was $1.51B, down 1.5% reported (distorted by the Digital Markets divestiture; +1.6% adjusted).

The headline earnings picture is noisy. GAAP net income fell from $1.25B (2024) to $729M (2025), a 42% decline, but the drop is largely non-operating: a $150M Digital Markets goodwill impairment, the absence of a one-time $300M event-cancellation insurance gain booked in 2024, and a tax rate that normalized to 24.7% in 2025 from an artificially low 9.6% in 2024 (which had benefited from a $162M IP-transfer item). Operating income fell a more modest 11%, from $1.16B to $1.03B, with underlying pressure from merit-driven SG&A (+6%) and $56.6M of workforce-reduction charges.

Cash generation is the model's genuine strength. Deferred revenue sits at $3.0B, reflecting the advance-paid subscription structure, and FCF conversion runs well above GAAP net income.

The balance sheet screens as distressed but is not. Reported stockholders' equity is only $63M, which produces alarming ratios (Altman Z ~1.3, D/E ~24x), but this is an artifact of $9.6B in cumulative treasury-stock repurchases against $6.9B of retained earnings — the buybacks have hollowed out book equity. Actual leverage is modest: $3.0B of fixed-rate senior notes (weighted-average coupon 4.34%, laddered 2028-2035, no floating-rate exposure) against $1.67B of cash and a $1.0B undrawn revolver. The company was in compliance with all covenants. The automated distress flags should be disregarded here; this is an investment-grade, cash-generative franchise, not a bankruptcy candidate.

Capital allocation is buyback-centric with no dividend. Gartner repurchased $2.0B (7.0M shares) in FY25 and $535M (3.3M shares) in Q1'26 alone, and the Board added a fresh $600M authorization in April 2026. The timing has been imperfect: roughly $2B was deployed near $500 per share in 2024 and early 2025, whereas Q1'26 repurchases were executed at $158.49. The buyback is now materially more accretive, but the capital allocated at the peak destroyed value.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is where the case breaks down, and the deterioration is quantifiable rather than anecdotal.

Contract value — the paid-in-advance leading indicator for the subscription business — has slowed to just +1% FX-neutral growth (GTS +0.4% in Q1'26), a dramatic slowdown from a company that compounded CV at high-single to low-double digits for two decades.

Wallet retention has fallen through the critical 100% line:

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| GTS wallet retention | 102% | 96% | 97% |
| GBS wallet retention | 106% | 99% | 98% |
| GTS client retention | 84% | 85% | 85% |

Client counts are holding, but net dollar retention below 100% means retained clients are contracting their spend. This is the coincident signal of demand softening — whether from AI substitution, budget pressure, or both — and it directly precedes the stock's de-rating.

A discrete and severe headwind sits inside this: the U.S. federal government cliff. Insights federal contract value more than halved, from over $252M to roughly $126M, with additional termination-for-convenience notices, driven by DOGE-era discretionary spending cuts. Total government-linked revenue contracts fell from $1.2B to $1.0B, and public-sector CV continued to fall high-single digits into Q1'26. Part of this resets the base lower (one-time), but the pressure persists.

Consulting is deteriorating in parallel: Q1'26 revenue -15%, gross margin from 38% to 31%, utilization from 64% to 61%, backlog -9%, billable headcount -10%. Small in the mix but a real demand tell.

Estimate revisions are mixed and do not signal an inflection. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates rose +4.1% and next-year +4.0%, but this is buyback-driven accretion rather than organic momentum, and over the trailing 30 days revisions have flattened to roughly zero across every window. The earnings-beat record is consistent (four straight beats, most recently +10.6% in Q1'26), but those beats have been delivered through cost cuts and share retirement against a stalling top line.

The gap between narrative and data warrants attention. The CEO's Q1'26 commentary claimed "Contract Value accelerated in the quarter," yet CV grew only +1.0% FX-neutral. Investors should weight the hard CV and retention data over management framing when assessing the raised full-year guidance.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$134 against consensus FY26 adjusted EPS near $13.68, Gartner trades at roughly 9.8x forward earnings, with a ~7-8% free cash flow yield. Peer comparables in IT consulting show a median P/E of 13.3 (EPAM 13.3, KD 13.3, TDC 7.5, ADEA 29.9, DXC 88.2), so Gartner sits below or in line with a heterogeneous peer set. Against its own history — where the stock carried 30-40x multiples — the compression is extreme.

A low multiple is not, by itself, a reason to be constructive; it is more often a signal that the market is pricing structural impairment. The relevant question is whether the compression reflects a mispricing (a specific reason the market is wrong) or a value trap (a correctly de-rated franchise facing secular decline).

The evidence tilts toward the latter, or at least toward genuine uncertainty. The multiple compressed because organic CV growth flatlined and net dollar retention broke below 100%, both consistent with generative AI beginning to substitute for synthesized research. Gartner's product is precisely the category most exposed to LLM commoditization — enterprise buyers can increasingly obtain "good enough" synthesized analysis at near-zero marginal cost. This is the same dynamic re-rating application-layer software franchises across the market: the tools that package and synthesize information, rather than those that supply the underlying compute and infrastructure, face the sharpest terminal-value questions.

There is real support underneath the price. The FCF yield, the share-count reduction, and the buyback authorization can drive mid-single-digit EPS growth even at flat revenue, which limits downside if retention stabilizes. But there is no identified catalyst that would re-rate the multiple upward, and the burden of proof on the bull case is high given the magnitude of the drawdown: a decline of this size means sophisticated investors are selling on a specific view that the renewal economics are structurally impaired, not that the stock is merely volatile. We do not treat the current price as evidence of mispricing, and we do not treat analyst targets (mean $160, median $162, high $203) as floors, because the growth stall means those targets embed an assumption of CV recovery that the hard data has not yet confirmed. The valuation is fair-to-supportive, not a clear opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Gartner's moat has historically been durable: brand, independence, scale, and a proprietary network of practitioner-sourced data and analyst relationships that produced 20-plus years of double-digit CV growth. The defining new dynamic is generative AI, which attacks the value proposition directly rather than at the margin. The wallet-retention decline is the first hard evidence that the moat is compressing at the renewal, and it is corroborated externally: a Q1'26 investor letter from Madison Investments flagged "Weak Subscription Revenue Growth Pressured Gartner," and Motley Fool (July 2026) attributed the H1'26 decline to the difficulty of "competing with increasingly sophisticated AI models."

The counterargument is that Gartner's proprietary data, benchmark libraries, and human analyst relationships remain defensible, and that its AskGartner AI tool (launched August 2025) could deepen rather than cannibalize engagement. That case is plausible but unproven; AskGartner is early. The competitive trajectory, which matters more than the absolute level, is currently negative: a market leader whose retention is eroding scores lower than a challenger gaining share. Until CV growth and wallet retention stabilize, the honest read is a compressing moat.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the secular AI substitution of the core subscription model. The FY25 10-K materially expanded its AI risk language: LLMs and AI chatbots may provide substantive content that reduces the need to access Gartner's platform, and clients may load Gartner's proprietary information into large language models. This is an existential-tail question with a genuinely uncertain resolution. Much of it is priced given the 70% de-rating, but the direction of terminal resolution is not, and continued sub-100% retention would justify further downside.

Additional risks:
- **Federal government cliff:** discrete, quantifiable, and ongoing into 2026.
- **Goodwill/intangibles at 38% of assets** ($2.74B goodwill), raising the probability of a second impairment (following the $150M Digital Markets write-down) if forecasts weaken.
- **Net dollar retention below 100%,** which if sustained converges revenue growth toward zero and keeps the multiple compressed.
- **Consulting deterioration** as a demand tell.
- **Macro/FX/tariff exposure:** ~40% of revenue is international, sensitive to enterprise IT and advisory budget cuts and conference travel.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 65.2% against 58.5% one-year historical vol (rich by ~6.7 points), the term structure is in backwardation (65.2% near versus 53.4% long-dated), and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±14.6%, consistent with a market bracing for the next earnings print. The put/call volume ratio of 3.38 and the shift in put/call open-interest ratio from 0.30 to 1.41 since early June signal a meaningful buildup of downside hedging as the price fell ~20% over the same window. Short interest is very high at 24.7% of float with 7.8 days to cover, which creates two-sided risk: a genuinely negative catalyst could accelerate the decline, but any evidence of retention stabilization could force a sharp squeeze. The elevated near-term IV argues against paying up for optionality here and reinforces waiting for the retention/CV data to resolve rather than positioning ahead of it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

AI fear is overblown. Gartner's proprietary practitioner-sourced data, benchmark content, and analyst relationships prove defensible; AskGartner deepens engagement rather than cannibalizing it; and wallet retention stabilizes and re-accelerates as the federal cliff resets the base lower. Even flat revenue produces mid-single-digit EPS growth via the FCF yield and share-count reduction, and any recovery in CV toward mid-single digits combined with a partial multiple re-rating toward the mid-teens drives the stock materially higher. Consensus targets range from $120 to $203 and the upper end reflects this scenario. Bull case: ~$200 (roughly 15x on normalized ~$15 FY27 EPS with modest CV recovery), or roughly +50%.

### Bear Case

Generative AI structurally impairs renewal economics. Wallet retention keeps falling, CV growth turns negative FX-neutral, and revenue converges toward decline. The buyback-driven EPS growth masks an eroding franchise until the cost-cutting runway is exhausted, at which point earnings begin to fall. A second goodwill impairment follows. The multiple stays compressed or de-rates further toward high-single-digit earnings on a shrinking base. Bear case: ~$100 (roughly 9x on eroding ~$11-12 EPS), or roughly -25%.

Base case: ~$140-150, reflecting a business that generates strong cash and buys back stock aggressively but grows organically at ~1-2% with an unresolved terminal question, holding roughly current levels with buyback accretion offset by continued growth concerns.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (uncertain). The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction, and the classification is deliberate: the cash generation and completed 70% de-rating prevent a clean AVOID, while the growth stall and unresolved AI risk prevent a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Wallet retention recovers above 100% for at least one quarter (GTS and GBS), signaling the renewal erosion has reversed.
- Contract value re-accelerates to mid-single-digit FX-neutral growth, confirming demand is not structurally impaired.
- Evidence that AskGartner is driving measurable engagement or CV expansion rather than defensive retention.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Wallet retention continues to decline (GTS below 95%, GBS below 97%) over the next two quarters.
- FX-neutral CV growth turns negative, or a second goodwill impairment is announced.
- Management is forced to cut full-year guidance, exposing the gap between the "acceleration" narrative and the underlying data.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two earnings reports (roughly Q2'26 in early August and Q3'26 in the fall) are the decisive evidence. The wallet-retention and contract value disclosures in those prints will determine whether the AI threat is a permanent impairment or an overblown fear, and should resolve the uncertainty within six to nine months.

## Conclusion

Gartner is a genuinely strong cash-generating franchise whose balance-sheet distress signals are optical buyback artifacts, not real credit risk. That quality, plus the FCF yield and an aggressive buyback now executing at a fraction of peak prices, provides a real floor and prevents this from being a clean short or AVOID. But the growth engine has stalled — organic contract value at +1% FX-neutral and net dollar retention below 100% — precisely as generative AI attacks the core value proposition, and the GAAP EPS growth that flatters the story is entirely financial engineering rather than organic expansion. The ~70% de-rating means much of the bad news is priced, but the terminal question is genuinely unresolved, and the "cheap" multiple is better read as a value-trap warning than a mispricing until the retention data stabilizes. We rate the shares HOLD, and would need to see wallet retention recover above 100% before treating the cash-machine narrative as a buy rather than a bet on a franchise whose renewal economics are eroding.
