**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MercadoLibre is the dominant integrated commerce-and-fintech ecosystem in Latin America, and it is in the middle of a self-inflicted earnings air pocket. Q1 2026 revenue grew 49% to $8.85B and the operational metrics that matter (GMV +42% to $19.0B, total payment volume +50% to $87.2B, fintech MAUs to 83M from 64M) all accelerated. At the same time, operating income fell 19.9% to $611M and operating margin collapsed to 6.9% from 12.9% a year earlier. Two discretionary line items drove the compression: a lower free-shipping threshold in Brazil plus a $250M marketing surge, and a doubling of credit provisions (+106.5% to $1,244M) as the loan book grew 87% YoY to $14.6B. The market read the print as a guidance reset on profitability, sent the stock down roughly 17% on the day, and the shares now sit ~37% below the $2,645 high.

The central analytical question is whether this is rational investment through a competitive cycle or over-spending into a panic. The strategic rationale is documented: the FY2025 10-K added explicit language that "rapidly expanding Asian e-commerce platforms gained significant market share in Latin America through low-price strategies," and management names Amazon, Temu, and Shopee as the competitive set. The accelerating GMV and items-sold figures (+47%) argue the spend is defending and gaining share rather than papering over decay. The credit book is the swing factor, being simultaneously the biggest growth engine and the biggest risk: allowances now sit at ~26% of gross loans, net interest margin after losses compressed 490bps to 17.8%, and off-balance-sheet card commitments rose to $11.9B.

The problem for a confident call is timing. MELI has missed EPS in four straight quarters (-6.8%, -5.7%, -12.6%, -15.3%), and consensus has cut current-year EPS by 28.4% and next-year by 26.1% over 90 days. Falling estimates against a stock at ~43x trailing/forward earnings means the multiple is expanding on the denominator decline even as the price falls. The setup is neither cheap enough to be asymmetric on the long side nor structurally impaired enough to short. We rate MELI HOLD with an honest "uncertain" rationale: the business quality is high, but the timing of margin recovery and the trajectory of credit losses are not yet visible enough to underwrite a position.

## Company Overview

MercadoLibre operates across 18 Latin American countries with three geographic profit centers: Brazil (~54% of revenue), Mexico (~22%), and Argentina (~19%). The business is a five-unit flywheel:

- **Marketplace**: the region's #1 e-commerce platform by GMV; ~90%+ of GMV is third-party.
- **Mercado Pago**: fintech arm spanning on/off-platform payments, a digital wallet, an asset-management/yield product, a fast-growing credit book, debit/credit cards, insurtech, crypto, and a USD stablecoin.
- **Mercado Envíos**: proprietary logistics; fulfillment centers handle >50% of shipments, with dedicated aircraft, trucks, and a pickup network.
- **Mercado Ads**: high-margin advertising built on first-party data.
- **Classifieds.**

The moat is the integration: regional logistics scale that is genuinely hard to replicate, a captive payments rail, proprietary first-party data feeding both credit underwriting and ad targeting, and two-sided network effects. Latin American e-commerce penetration still lags the US, UK, and China, providing a multi-year secular runway. Founder Marcos Galperin moved to Executive Chairman in May 2025; Ariel Szarfsztejn (formerly Commerce President) is now President and CEO, with Martín de los Santos as CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2025 net revenue and financial income reached ~$28.9B, up ~39% YoY from ~$20.8B in 2024, nearly 3x the ~$10.5B of 2022. Q1 2026 revenue grew 49.0% (45.5% FX-neutral), with Fintech +51% and Commerce +47%. There is no top-line problem here.

**Margin compression — the crux.** Gross margin compressed to 43.7% from 46.7%. Cost of revenue rose 57.5%, S&M rose 63.9% (online/offline marketing +$250M), and the provision for doubtful accounts rose to 14.1% of revenue versus 10.2% a year earlier. Both major drivers are discretionary: the Brazil free-shipping cut and the credit-book expansion. This distinguishes MELI's situation from a company experiencing involuntary margin erosion under competitive price pressure, though the line between "voluntary defense" and "forced defense" is thin when the trigger is Temu and Shopee taking share.

**Credit book — the swing factor.** Gross loans receivable reached $14.6B at Q1'26 (vs $12.5B at YE'25), split roughly $6.6B credit cards, $5.3B consumer, $2.3B merchant. The allowance for doubtful accounts is $3.8B, up from $1.7B a year earlier. Originations grew ~81%, and the mix is deliberately shifting toward lower-yield, higher-volume credit cards, which lengthens portfolio duration. NIMAL compressed to 17.8% from 22.7% — still highly profitable, but the spread is narrowing by design. Renegotiated loans are a modest 1.4%, and the aging table shows past-due balances growing roughly in line with the book, with $658M written off in Q1 alone under a 360-day write-off policy. There is no visible blow-out yet, but the book is unseasoned and the macro backdrop in Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina is unfriendly.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Q1 operating cash flow doubled YoY to $2,075M, but adjusted free cash flow was slightly negative (-$56M) because cash is being consumed funding loan growth. The headline ~5x debt/equity and ~1.4 Altman Z-Score are misleading: they reflect a banking-style balance sheet ($14.1B funds payable to customers, $9.5B Brazilian central-bank mandatory guarantee cash, securitization VIEs), not corporate distress. On the company's own net-debt basis, leverage is a modest ~$5.7B against $46.9B total assets. Funding is diversified across non-recourse securitization SPEs ($3.3B collateralized debt), Brazilian deposit instruments, bank loans, $750M 4.90% notes due 2033, and an undrawn $800M revolver. The genuine financial-health concern is not solvency but funding concentration: the 10-K sharpened language on reliance on structured credit vehicles with a concentrated investor base, and the new CEO has publicly floated loan-book sales to support fintech funding, which signals that scaling the credit flywheel is becoming a capital constraint.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is intact and arguably accelerating. GMV +42%, items sold +47%, TPV +50%, and fintech MAUs up ~30% YoY are not the metrics of a business losing the demand fight. The secular driver — Latin American e-commerce and digital-payments penetration well below developed-market levels — remains multi-year.

The complication is earnings growth, which has gone backwards even as revenue compounds. The four consecutive EPS misses and the magnitude of the estimate cuts are the dominant forward signal here: current-quarter consensus fell 19.4% in 30 days and 29.0% in 90 days; current-year EPS was cut 15.7%/30d and 28.4%/90d; next-year was cut 14.2%/30d and 26.1%/90d. The revision velocity is moving the wrong direction across every horizon, and it is decelerating only modestly (30-day cuts smaller than 90-day cuts because the bulk of the reset happened around the Q1 print). This is the inverse of the strong-positive-revision signal that would warrant a BUY. Falling estimates of this magnitude typically precede continued multiple pressure as consensus chases the denominator lower.

The bull's growth thesis is that revenue compounding at ~45-50% plus eventual margin normalization produces a sharp EPS recovery once the investment cycle rolls off. That is plausible, and the next-year consensus of $58.13 (down from $78.61 ninety days ago) embeds some of it. But there is no firm timeline on when free-shipping intensity eases or when credit provisioning stabilizes, and the company is not guiding to a specific margin recovery quarter.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,639 the stock trades at ~43x trailing EPS of $39.39 and ~40x forward on the cut $40.60 current-year consensus. On next-year consensus of $58.13, the forward multiple is ~28x. The headline number is not absurd for a business growing revenue at 45%+, but the earnings are the issue: applying a multiple to a number that has been cut 28% in 90 days and may be cut further means the "cheap on next year" framing depends entirely on the margin-recovery thesis holding.

Peer anchoring is awkward because MELI has no clean comp: it is part marketplace, part payments network, part consumer lender. Against high-quality global commerce/payments compounders, ~28x forward earnings for ~45% revenue growth is reasonable if margins recover and unattractive if they do not. The PEG on next-year EPS growth (consensus implies ~43% EPS growth from a depressed base) is below 1.0, which would argue cheap, but that EPS growth rate is itself a product of the trough, and trough-relative growth rates flatter the math.

Analyst targets span $1,750 (low) to $2,800 (high) with a $2,217 mean and $2,225 median, all above the current price, and the recommendation distribution remains constructive (5 strong-buy, 15 buy, 4 hold). The sentiment trend is labeled improving, and the dip has drawn explicit "buy the margin compression" notes including a $2,300 target. We treat these targets cautiously: they generally predate or only partially incorporate the latest estimate cuts, and on a stock where consensus EPS has been wrong four quarters running, the target distribution carries less weight than usual.

Our view: the valuation is genuinely two-sided. It is not stretched enough to short, and not compressed enough relative to credible forward earnings to be asymmetric on the long side. A clean BUY would require either a lower entry (mid-$1,400s, the 52-week low zone) or evidence that the EPS cuts have bottomed and credit losses are stabilizing.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining dynamic is the arrival of Asian cross-border players (Temu, Shein, AliExpress) and Shopee competing on ultra-low prices and direct-from-manufacturer supply, alongside Amazon's continued push in Mexico and Brazil. MELI's response — cutting Brazil's free-shipping threshold and surging marketing — is the direct cause of the margin compression. The accelerating GMV and items-sold figures suggest share is being defended or gained, supporting the read that this is a defend-the-moat investment cycle rather than structural deterioration.

The logistics scale (>50% of shipments through proprietary fulfillment, dedicated air and ground capacity) is the hardest asset for low-price entrants to replicate quickly, and it is precisely the asset MELI is leveraging to compete on delivery speed and free-shipping economics. On fintech, the closed-loop ecosystem — marketplace data feeding credit underwriting and ad targeting — is a structural edge against both incumbent banks and standalone fintechs like Nubank. The competitive position is improving-to-stable on volume metrics, but the cost of holding it has risen, which is what the margin line is telling us. We score competitive position above neutral because trajectory on share appears favorable, tempered by the reality that the price of defense is open-ended against well-capitalized Asian platforms.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit quality is the real watch item.** An unseasoned book growing 87% into consumer and card products means a macro downturn in Brazil, Mexico, or Argentina could spike write-offs and break the bull thesis. The aging data shows no blow-out yet, but this is the line item that decides the stock.

**Argentina is both jewel and tail risk.** It is MELI's highest-margin segment (35.7% direct contribution margin vs Brazil's 8.2%), so any Argentine FX or macro setback hits profitability disproportionately. The 2025 backdrop (31.5% inflation, official peso -41%) is severe; the April 2025 easing of exchange controls allowing FY2025+ dividend repatriation is a quiet positive.

**Brazil tax overhang.** A $592M provision on the IRRF withholding dispute (probable loss), an additional $131M ICMS dispute (not provisioned), dual-VAT reform, and a December 2025 income-tax reform raising rates from April 2026 add uncertainty to the largest segment.

**Funding concentration.** The sharpened securitization-investor-concentration language and the CEO's openness to loan-book sales flag that funding the credit flywheel is a strategic constraint, not just a footnote.

**Cleared and lower-priority.** The Mexican "Buy Box"/logistics antitrust probe closed in August 2025 without penalty, removing an overhang. The distress-screen flags (Z-Score ~1.4, ~5x D/E) are false positives from consolidating a bank-style balance sheet.

We score risk slightly below neutral. The credit book and Argentina concentration are material, specific, and not fully priced; the market is debating margins, not yet stress-testing the loan book.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 51.8% against 40.0% one-year historical vol (rich by ~11.8 points, 91st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (51.8% near-term vs 41.5% at six months), the classic signature of an imminent-event premium rather than sustained directional conviction. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.3% near-term, drifting to -3.6% at six months), and the put/call OI ratio of 1.35 indicates modest defensive positioning without a one-sided bearish bet. Short interest is negligible at 2.0% of float, 1.6 days to cover, so informed investors are not pressing a short thesis. The read: the market expects volatility around the next data point (margins, credit) but is not committed to direction, consistent with our two-sided HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The margin compression is a deliberate, temporary investment cycle. Revenue compounds at 45-50%, GMV/TPV/MAU accelerate, and once the Brazil free-shipping intensity eases and credit provisioning normalizes against a seasoned book, operating margin reverts toward mid-teens. EPS recovers sharply off the trough, consensus revisions turn positive, and the multiple re-rates. Argentina's high-margin contribution plus dividend repatriation aids cash extraction. A director with global financial-services credentials bought ~$1M in the open market near current prices, and sell-side targets cluster well above spot. Bull target ~$2,500-2,800.

**Bear case.** The Asian competitive threat is structural, not cyclical, and free-shipping/marketing spend becomes a permanent tax on margins rather than a one-time defense. The unseasoned credit book deteriorates as Brazilian/Mexican/Argentine consumers weaken, write-offs accelerate beyond the $658M Q1 run-rate, and provisions keep compressing earnings. Estimate cuts continue, the multiple stays elevated on falling EPS, and funding concentration forces dilutive loan-book sales at unfavorable terms. Bear target ~$1,400-1,450 (52-week-low zone), ~12-13% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August 2026), which is the first read on whether operating margin has stabilized or troughed and whether credit metrics (NIMAL, write-offs, past-due aging) are holding.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin stabilizes or inflects upward (Q2 operating margin ≥ Q1's 6.9% with management framing the investment peak as behind), and consensus current-year EPS revisions turn flat-to-positive over a 30-day window.
- Credit metrics hold: NIMAL stabilizes near 17-18% and past-due/write-off growth tracks below loan-book growth, confirming the book is seasoning cleanly.
- A lower entry into the mid-$1,400s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside to the margin-recovery thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Credit deterioration: write-offs accelerating materially above the $658M quarterly pace, NIMAL falling below ~15%, or allowance build outpacing origination growth — signs the underwriting model is breaking.
- GMV/TPV growth decelerating below ~25% YoY while margins stay compressed, the signal that competitive spend is failing to hold share.
- Forced loan-book sales executed at terms that imply a funding squeeze, or a fifth consecutive EPS miss with continued double-digit estimate cuts.

## Conclusion

MercadoLibre is a high-quality, structurally advantaged business going through a deliberate profitability reset that the market is reasonably penalizing until it can see the other side. The operational metrics confirm the franchise is healthy and likely gaining share; the earnings, estimate revisions, and credit-book trajectory confirm that the cost and timing of that defense are uncertain. Four straight EPS misses and 28-33% estimate cuts over 90 days are not the profile of a stock to chase on the long side, and a negligible short interest with no structural impairment is not the profile of a short. At ~43x cut trailing earnings and ~28x next-year consensus, the valuation is two-sided.

We rate MELI **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. The path to a BUY is clear and monitorable: margin stabilization plus clean credit metrics, or a better entry in the mid-$1,400s. Until the Q2 print resolves whether the investment cycle is peaking and whether the credit book is seasoning cleanly, the call is to hold existing positions and wait for the data rather than underwrite the recovery in advance.
