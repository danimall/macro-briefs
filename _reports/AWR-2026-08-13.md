## Executive Summary

American States Water delivered a genuine operating inflection in the first half of 2026. Revenue rose 12.7% to $350.5M, operating income 19.5% to $115.3M, and diluted EPS 18.5% to $1.86, with Q2 EPS of $1.09 beating the single-analyst estimate of $0.91 by 19.9%. The mechanism is well documented and largely contractual: the second-year step of the 2025–2027 California water general rate case raised adopted revenues less supply cost by roughly $32.0M versus 2025 levels, of which ~$11M came from advice-letter capital added to rate base effective January 1, 2026. Recorded water revenues rose $22.5M in H1 against a $5.6M supply-cost increase, a net $16.9M favorable swing that drove $0.22 of the $0.29 consolidated EPS gain. Two additional structural positives landed in the quarter: the $200M at-the-market equity program was completed on June 12, with management stating no plans to issue additional equity through at least 2029, removing a dilution drag the company quantified at $0.02/share in Q2 alone; and on July 28 the board raised the quarterly dividend 8.2% to $0.5455, the 72nd consecutive annual increase.

None of that resolves the valuation question. At $87.95 AWR trades at 24.0x TTM diluted EPS of $3.66, 23.2x the $3.79 FY2027 consensus, and 3.11x the $28.30 June 30 book value, against a water-utility peer median of 22.5x. It yields 2.48% versus a 2.7% peer median and a 10-year Treasury yield [unverified — actual: N/A]. The stock sits 2.4% below its July 20 52-week high of $90.11, above the $84.00 consensus mean and median target, and coverage is a thin and unenthusiastic 2 holds plus 1 strong sell. Consensus EPS revisions are flat to down (next-quarter estimate cut 22.2% over 30 days on one-analyst coverage; FY2026 down 1.4% over 90 days).

The bigger analytical issue is earnings quality in a changed regulatory regime. Since January 1, 2025 GSWC has operated under a Monterey-style WRAM and an Incremental Cost Balancing Account rather than the full WRAM/MCBA decoupling in place from 2008 through 2024. Volume variance and water-supply-mix variance now flow to earnings. In Q2 2026 both moved favorably — consumption +4%, purchased water down to 40.7% of supply from 43.2% — and management wrote plainly that it is uncertain whether these trends persist "or if their positive earnings effects will reverse." H1 also carried $3.1M of Rabbi Trust mark-to-market gains versus $2.2M, roughly $0.02/share of non-operating year-over-year increment. Strip those and normalized FY2026 EPS lands nearer $3.90–3.95 than the ~$3.99 a naive seasonality extrapolation implies. Fair value on peer multiples applied to normalized forward earnings is $85–87. That is approximately spot, with a bear case near $72 and a bull case near $98 — mildly unfavorable asymmetry. Fourth consecutive HOLD, on income and rate-base compounding rather than expected appreciation.

## Company Overview

AWR is a holding company with no material operating assets of its own, running three legally distinct subsidiaries that function as three reportable segments with materially different risk profiles.

**Golden State Water Company (GSWC)** — approximately 70% of revenue and 78% of segment net income. A CPUC-regulated water utility serving ~265,200 connections concentrated in Southern California. Classic rate-base economics: capital is invested, the CPUC sets rates in triennial general rate cases to recover costs plus an authorized return (currently 10.06% ROE on water), and earnings scale with authorized rate base. GSWC is a separate SEC registrant filing combined reports with AWR, giving unusually clean stand-alone visibility.

**Bear Valley Electric Service (BVES)** — ~9% of revenue, ~6.5% of segment net income. A CPUC-regulated electric distribution utility serving ~24,900 connections in the Big Bear mountain communities of San Bernardino County, owning an 8.4 MW gas generator and purchasing most of the power it resells. Economically small, and the source of the company's most asymmetric tail risk. Notably, BVES remains fully decoupled through its Base Revenue Requirement Adjustment Mechanism: billed usage fell 7.8% in Q2 and 6.1% in H1, yet segment EPS rose to $0.12 from $0.10.

**American States Utility Services (ASUS)** — ~21% of revenue and ~16% of segment net income, growing fastest. Nine subsidiaries operate, maintain and perform renewal-and-replacement construction on water and wastewater systems at U.S. military bases across ten states under initial 50-year firm-fixed-price contracts plus one 15-year task-order agreement. Because the arrangements are service concessions under ASC 853, the systems are not on AWR's balance sheet, which makes ASUS structurally asset-light and high-ROE. Returns are set by contract economics — annual economic price adjustments and requests for equitable adjustment — not by a regulator. H1 revenue grew 20.5% to $74.0M and segment EPS rose to $0.31 from $0.26, with full-year segment guidance of $0.63–$0.67.

## Financial Analysis

FY2024's flat year (revenue unchanged, EPS −5.6%) was regulatory-lag driven: both the water and electric rate cases were unresolved until January 2025 decisions. Once surcharges were implemented in May 2025, FY2025 revenue rose 10.5% and 2026 accelerated. TTM figures derived from the two most recent filings: revenue $697.5M, operating income $222.1M, diluted EPS $3.66.

**Earnings quality.** Three items flatter H1 2026 and are unlikely to repeat at the same magnitude. Retirement-trust investment gains were $3.1M versus $2.2M ($4.3M versus $2.7M in Q2 alone) on a $51.5M Level 1 portfolio — non-operating and market-dependent, roughly $0.02/share of after-tax year-over-year increment flowing through reported water-segment earnings. Consumption ran +4% in Q2 and +2.2% in H1. Purchased water fell to 40.7% of Q2 supply from 43.2% as wells returned to service. The last two items would have been neutralized entirely under the pre-2025 mechanisms. The Q1-to-Q2 swing within a single half-year illustrates the new volatility: H1 supply costs rose $5.6M partly because wells were temporarily offline in Q1, the same lever running the other way one quarter earlier.

**Cash flow and funding.** TTM operating cash flow of $236.6M against $215.9M of capex leaves +$20.7M of free cash flow; TTM dividends of $78.5M put free cash flow after dividends at approximately −$57.8M, funded externally. This is normal for a rate-base grower, but two nuances matter. First, part of 2025–26 operating cash flow is a non-recurring catch-up: the pre-2025 WRAM/MCBA under-collections that accumulated during the rate-case delay began converting to cash when surcharges started in May 2025, and the associated regulatory asset fell from $22.1M at year-end 2025 to $14.6M at June 30, 2026, with the consolidated position flipping from a $9.3M net regulatory asset to a $14.0M net regulatory liability. Extrapolating 2026 operating cash flow as a permanent step-up overstates run-rate. Second, H1 capex of $97.6M was down 17.7% year over year against full-year company-funded regulated guidance of $185–220M in the 10-Q (a secondary report of the Q2 call attributes a narrower $185–200M to the CFO; the discrepancy is unreconciled). A back-half-weighted capex ramp is required to hit even the low end.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt at June 30 was $909.96M (long-term $717.7M, current maturities $73.2M, bank notes $119.0M) against $1,121.5M of common equity — 44.8% debt to total capital, down from 47.2% at year-end 2025 and 50.3% at year-end 2024. Leverage is falling. TTM operating income of $222.1M against approximately $46.9M of interest expense is roughly 4.7x coverage. Liquidity is ample: $195M/$200M/$65M of revolver capacity at AWR/GSWC/BVES with only $77M/$38M/$4M drawn, maturities in 2028–2029, and $588.0M of remaining CPUC-authorized long-term financing capacity at GSWC plus $82.0M at BVES.

Two balance-sheet caveats deserve explicit treatment. GSWC's $65.0M of 5.5% unsecured private placement notes mature June 5, 2027 and have been reclassified to current liabilities; management intends to refinance with long-term debt, a parent equity contribution, or both, but the filing states no formal commitment or refinancing agreement has been executed. Given the undrawn revolver capacity and authorized financing headroom this reads as routine, but it is an unexecuted obligation. Separately, the reported current ratio is close to uninformative here: the CPUC requires GSWC and BVES to fully repay revolver borrowings within rolling 24-month windows (GSWC's ends November 2027, BVES's May 2028), which shuttles balances between current and non-current classification with no change in total debt. The FY2025 improvement from 0.82 to 1.32 was substantially a classification effect, and the ratio fell back to 0.98 by June 30 as the $65M maturity moved current.

A note on screening artifacts: automated distress scores on this issuer are unreliable. A recomputed original Altman Z from filed balances lands at approximately 2.11 — the grey zone rather than distress — and even that number has little diagnostic value for a capital-intensive regulated utility whose asset turnover is structurally ~0.25x and whose leverage is set by regulatory design. Interest coverage of 4.7x, falling debt/cap, and approximately $460M of undrawn committed revolver capacity are the appropriate solvency lenses.

**Returns.** TTM consolidated ROE is 13.67% on average equity. GSWC stand-alone book ROE derives to 11.97% against a 10.06% authorized water ROE. That comparison is directional only — book equity is not rate-base equity, and GSWC's reported net income includes flow-through tax benefits and trust gains that do not represent an earned return on rate base — but out-earning the authorized return is a fact intervenors can use, and Cal Advocates is an active party in the pending case.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth engine has three identifiable components, and only one of them has a near-term earnings effect.

**Rate base, requested.** On July 1, 2026 GSWC filed its 2028–2030 general rate case requesting approximately $1 billion of capital for the three-year cycle, against $573.1M authorized for 2025–2027 — a ~74% step-up. On January 30, 2026 BVES filed its 2027–2030 case requesting ~$133M of capital plus ~$17M of advice-letter projects (roughly double the prior cycle's $52.5M base plus $23.1M), along with a 60/40 equity/debt capital structure (from 57/43) and an 11.30% ROE (from 10.0%). The 11.30% ask is aggressive; the 2025 decision moved BVES only from 9.6% to 10.0%. Decisions arrive Q4 2027 for water and later for electric, so neither request affects 2026–2027 earnings. CPUC outcomes are routinely settlements below the request.

**Rate base, already earning.** The advice-letter mechanism lets completed projects enter rate base between general rate cases, accruing interest during construction at the adopted cost of debt and earning the full rate of return including equity from in-service date. BVES placed ~$28M of projects in service, and the CPUC approved the advice letters at actual cost, generating approximately $4M of additional annual revenues. On the water side, roughly $11M of the 2026 revenue step came from advice-letter capital. This is the working, verifiable growth channel.

**ASUS.** The fastest-growing segment, +20.5% in H1, with $4.2 billion of remaining performance obligations recognized over 13 to 48 years. That figure should not be read as convertible backlog — against $136.7M of FY2025 segment revenue it represents roughly 30 years of run-rate, and each 50-year contract is terminable in whole or part for the convenience of the U.S. government (with a repurchase and unrecovered-investment recovery provision, though the recovery amount is undefined and would exclude forgone profit). Management's own incentive plan includes an "ASUS New Base Acquisition Success Rate" measure, which identifies new base wins as the primary organic lever outside rate base — and the 10-K flags intense competition for those awards plus the programmatic risk that the U.S. government periodically reviews the cost and effectiveness of the privatization program and could curtail future solicitations.

**What consensus expects.** FY2026 $3.66 and FY2027 $3.79 — roughly 3.6% growth off a 2026 base that already embeds the rate-case step, the end of ATM dilution, and favorable consumption. Revisions are flat to negative: FY2026 up 1.4% over 30 days but down 1.4% over 90 days, FY2027 down 0.5% over 90 days, and the next-quarter estimate cut 22.2%. Coverage is one to two analysts, so revision data here is noisy and should not be weighted as heavily as it would on a widely covered name. Nothing in the revision pattern resembles the positive-inflection signal that justifies paying a premium multiple.

The dividend is the most reliable growth line: quarterly rate up 8.2% to $0.5455, compounding at 8.4% over five years against a stated policy of more than 7% long-term, with a 59.6% payout on TTM EPS. Dividends have been paid every year since 1931 and increased for 72 consecutive years.

## Competitive Landscape

The regulated segments are legally protected service-territory monopolies, subject to condemnation and eminent domain. That moat is durable but static. The relevant trajectory question is regulatory, and it has moved against the company.

From 2008 through 2024 GSWC operated under full sales decoupling (WRAM) and full supply-cost decoupling (MCBA). The CPUC's August 2020 rulemaking discontinued both for California Class A water utilities after 2024. SB 1469 (2022) preserved the right to request decoupling; GSWC requested it, and the January 30, 2025 decision denied it, ordering a Monterey-style WRAM plus an Incremental Cost Balancing Account effective January 1, 2025. M-WRAM corrects only for tiered-rate design versus a blended quantity rate — H1 under-collection was $1.273M on $244M of water revenue, confirming how narrow it is. The ICBA tracks per-unit price differences for purchased water, pump tax and purchased power but explicitly excludes the impact of changes in supply-source mix. Retained mitigants: a mid-cycle sales-forecast reconciliation mechanism and a rate-design shift putting 45–48% of the revenue requirement into fixed service charges, roughly 65% of GSWC's fixed costs. The exposure is bounded, not open-ended, but it is real and it is new.

This is a sector-wide California phenomenon rather than an AWR-specific failing, which makes GSWC's 2028–2030 request to reinstate WRAM/MCBA a test case with a Q4 2027 resolution. It also means California-concentrated water utilities now carry volumetric earnings risk that utilities in other jurisdictions largely do not — relevant to the AWR-versus-WTRG multiple comparison.

Demand-side conditions are secularly unfavorable. Statewide indoor/outdoor use standards became effective January 1, 2025 on a system-by-system basis. Tiered conservation rates, more efficient fixtures, and recycled-water adoption all compress volumes; the 10-K notes recycled water "generally has lower tariff rates than potable water and may be provided by other companies or government entities in GSWC's service territory," which is genuine substitution rather than mere volume decay.

Supply is the unresolved variable. As of the Q2 filing, Lake Mead was at 27% and Lake Powell 22% of capacity, a Level 2 shortage condition extends into 2027 with possible escalation to Level 3 in late 2027, Reclamation has requested 2–4 million acre-feet of annual demand reduction, and the operating agreements governing the Colorado River expire in fall 2026 with post-2026 rules still under negotiation. Offsetting near-term: California major reservoirs at 106% of historical average in late July, less than 1% of the state in severe drought, and the State Water Project allocation raised to 45%. The specific threat to earnings is mix rather than price: invasive golden mussels detected in the SWP conveyance network in mid-2025 prompted Los Angeles County to place a moratorium on admitting SWP water into San Gabriel Basin spreading facilities — the recharge mechanism supporting GSWC groundwater production. Less recharge means more purchased water, and mix variance is precisely what the ICBA does not cover.

ASUS has no close pure-play comparable. Fifty-year firm-fixed-price military privatization contracts under ASC 853 concession accounting produce a high-ROE, off-balance-sheet earnings stream no water peer replicates at this scale. Competition for new base awards comes from other investor-owned utilities and municipalities; the structural vulnerability is programmatic, since the government periodically reviews the cost and effectiveness of the privatization program.

Competitive position 6.0 — a protected core with a differentiated non-regulated adjunct, offset by a regulatory framework that has become less favorable and a demand backdrop that erodes volumes.

## Risk Assessment

**Decoupling loss (highest conviction, company-specific).** Volumetric water revenue is weather- and conservation-sensitive for the first time since 2008, arriving simultaneously with statewide use standards. In 2026 to date this has been a tailwind. It has not been tested by a wet year or a mandatory conservation order.

**Supply mix.** The ICBA covers per-unit price but not mix. The San Gabriel Basin recharge moratorium is exactly the event that forces an unrecoverable mix shift. ICBA balances were "not significant" at June 30, so per-unit variance has so far been small.

**BVES wildfire — small segment, asymmetric tail.** The 10-K states there is no assurance losses from a wildfire event will not exceed BVES's insurance coverage limits, and that uninsured losses may not be approved by the CPUC for recovery. California utilities may be held strictly liable under inverse condemnation for damage caused by their property even absent negligence. An approved safety certificate is not a shield if the CPUC determines BVES did not act reasonably. Cost recovery for wildfire mitigation plans is deferred by design until the next general rate case after plan approval, so spending precedes recovery — the 2026–2028 base plan was approved by the Office of Energy Infrastructure Safety in November 2025. The 10-K discloses deteriorating availability of general liability, umbrella and cyber coverage, and CPUC citation authority of $50,000 per violation per day. Two material items are not in evidence: BVES's wildfire insurance policy limits, and whether BVES participates in California's AB 1054 statewide wildfire fund (the filing makes no reference to it, and the exclusion of small utilities from that fund could not be verified). This is the single largest unquantified tail and the primary reason the risk score sits below 5.

**Inverse condemnation reaches the water utility too.** Water utilities may be held strictly liable for fire damage if the system did not operate as designed, including where the system was damaged by the fire itself and the fire was no fault of the utility. That channel touches 265,200 connections, not just BVES's 24,900.

**ASUS counterparty concentration.** Most ASUS revenue derives from the U.S. government; all 50-year contracts are terminable for convenience. Balance-sheet exposure to that single counterparty at June 30 sums to roughly $96.8M ($63.4M receivable, $3.4M unbilled, $30.0M contract assets). DCAA/DCMA audits can question incurred costs; suspension or debarment is available for non-compliance; firm-fixed-price construction overruns are generally unrecoverable. Management notes privatization contracts are "excepted service," which limited the impact of the 2025 record shutdown and early-2026 partial shutdowns to payment and processing timing. Worth stating clearly: because ASUS is the fastest-growing segment, rising ASUS mix increases single-counterparty concentration even as it reduces California-regulatory concentration. Those are different exposures, not diversification.

**Regulatory lag recurrence.** GSWC's next decision is not expected until Q4 2027 and BVES's case was filed January 2026, creating windows where capital is deployed ahead of authorized recovery — the mechanism behind FY2024's flat year. The 10-K also links rate-case delay to financial-covenant compliance.

**Funding tension worth monitoring.** The 10-Q states no plans to issue equity through at least 2029 "to support its current operations," while the July GRC requests ~$1B of GSWC capital for 2028–2030 and BVES requests ~$150M at a 60% equity structure. Consolidated retained earnings grew $33.6M in H1. Internal equity generation of roughly $65–80M annually against a combined ~$1.15B program funded at 57–60% equity implies a gap in the hundreds of millions. Several benign resolutions exist and at least one is textual: the statement is scoped to current operations and current estimates, likely the authorized 2025–2027 program rather than an unapproved one; CPUC decisions routinely authorize less than requested; required equity relates to net rate base, not gross capex; and GSWC holds $588M of authorized long-term financing headroom. This is a tension to track, not an established contradiction.

**Other disclosed items.** Approximately 38% of employees become retirement-eligible within five years. BVES demand is tied to ski-resort snow-making (a liquidity rather than earnings effect, since BRRBA decouples revenue). The FY2025 10-K added a dedicated artificial-intelligence risk factor. The Q2 10-Q added a forward-looking macro paragraph referencing an ongoing U.S. military operation in Iran as a potential indirect channel through commodity prices, inflation, supply chain and capital markets, while stating H1 geopolitical developments had no material impact. The Q2 filing states there have been no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

**PFAS.** In H1 GSWC received $2.2M net from Tyco and $0.9M net from BASF, with a third $3.3M net payment from 3M received July 23. All proceeds sit in a CPUC-authorized memorandum account as a regulatory liability ($16.9M at June 30, up from $11.0M) and offset incremental replacement and treatment investment. These are ratepayer benefits, not shareholder earnings, and they reduce future rate-base additions. EPA's May 18, 2026 proposal to retain the 4 ppt PFOA/PFOS standards, rescind four others, and extend compliance to 2031 lowers near-term compliance capex — which cuts both ways for a rate-base growth story.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.0% against 20.4% one-year realized is rich by 5.7 points, with the near-dated reading in the 35th percentile of the current chain and an implied move of ±4.5% on the nearest expiry — elevated pricing for a utility whose earnings event has already passed. Term structure is flat (26.0% near versus 25.2% at six months), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than clustered on a dated catalyst. Near-term put/call skew of 2.7% is characterized as balanced; the six-month tenor prints −4.0%, but the sign convention is not defined in the data, so no directional inference is drawn. Put/call open interest fell from 1.72 to 0.86 between August 9 and August 13 while ATM IV rose 3.0 points and the price gained 1.5% — positioning turned over rather than one-sided accumulation. Put/call volume of 0.10 is thin. Short interest is 3.4% of float at 3.0 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish positioning to squeeze. The takeaway: the options market prices somewhat more uncertainty than realized history justifies but shows no directional conviction, which is consistent with a fairly-valued name lacking a near-term catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $98 (+11.4%)

The 2026 step-up proves durable rather than transient. Consumption holds above adopted levels through the back half, ICBA mix variance stays immaterial, and FY2026 lands near $4.00 with FY2027 following at 5–6% growth on advice-letter rate-base additions rather than the 3.6% consensus. The completed ATM plus the stated intention to avoid equity issuance through 2029 removes the dilution overhang that has capped per-share growth at most regulated utilities in this rate environment, an advantage peers funding large capex programs do not have. The dividend compounds at 8%+ with a sub-60% payout, extending a 72-year record. Long-end yields ease from current levels, and the water-utility group re-rates with AWR retaining its premium; at 25x on $3.95 that is $98. Optional upside: the CPUC grants some restoration of WRAM/MCBA in the Q4 2027 decision, which would remove the volumetric risk premium the market has applied to California water since 2020, and would be a sector-wide catalyst.

### Bear Case — $72 (−18.1%)

Consumption reverses in a wet or conservation-constrained period, wells go offline again, and the mix shift the ICBA does not cover cuts water-segment earnings. FY2027 lands nearer $3.60–3.70 rather than $3.79. Simultaneously, long-end yields grind higher on deficit supply and an inflation-plus-independence premium, compressing water-utility multiples toward 19–20x — a 19x multiple on $3.79 is $72, close to the $69.45 52-week low. The 2028–2030 GRC settles well below the ~$1B request while intervenors point to GSWC book ROE running above the 10.06% authorization, capping the rate-base growth rate. Tail risk: a BVES-attributed ignition triggers inverse-condemnation strict liability against undisclosed insurance limits with no verified access to the state wildfire fund and deferred CPUC recovery — an outcome not remotely reflected in a 24x multiple.

### Base Case — $86 (−2.2%)

Normalized FY2026 EPS of $3.90–3.95 and FY2027 near consensus $3.79–3.85, valued at the 22.5x peer median with no premium justified by 3–4% growth, produces $85–88. Total return over twelve months approximates the 2.48% dividend. Probability-weighting base at 55%, bull at 20%, and bear at 25% yields roughly −3% price return, or slightly negative to flat all-in. That is less compelling than a fair-return profile but not impaired enough to warrant trimming a quality regulated compounder against transaction costs and the possibility of upward normalization in FY2026 delivery. The relevant point for prospective buyers is the absence of margin of safety at 2.4% below the 52-week high.

## Conclusion

AWR is a well-run regulated compounder that just posted its strongest half in years for identifiable, largely contractual reasons: a second-year rate step worth ~$32.0M of adopted revenue less supply cost, advice-letter capital entering rate base, and the completion of a $200M ATM program that ends a $0.06–0.10 annualized per-share dilution drag. Leverage is falling to 44.8% of capital, interest coverage is 4.7x, liquidity is ample, and the dividend was raised 8.2% for a 72nd consecutive year on a 59.6% payout. Those are the reasons to own it.

The reasons not to buy it here are equally concrete. At $87.95 the stock trades at 24.0x TTM EPS and 23.2x FY2027 consensus — the highest multiple among its water-utility comparables — for the group's lowest yield except one and roughly 3.6% consensus growth. It sits above the $84 consensus mean target with coverage of two holds and one strong sell, and estimates are flat to falling. A meaningful share of the H1 outperformance came from consumption and supply-mix variances that management explicitly warns may reverse, and from non-operating trust gains. Since January 2025 those variances flow directly to earnings because full decoupling was replaced by a narrow M-WRAM and an ICBA that excludes mix. Layered on top is a BVES inverse-condemnation wildfire tail with undisclosed insurance limits, no verified state wildfire-fund access, and CPUC cost recovery deferred to the next rate case by design.

Base-case fair value of $86 against a $87.95 price and a −18% bear case gives no margin of safety. Existing holders are compensated by a 2.48% yield growing at 8%, which is the honest reason to hold rather than an expectation of appreciation. Prospective buyers should wait for the low $70s, evidence that water-segment volumes and supply mix are stable under the new mechanisms, or a constructive CPUC signal on restoring decoupling.
