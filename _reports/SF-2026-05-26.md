## Executive Summary

Stifel Financial is a diversified financial services firm delivering strong operating performance — Q1 2026 revenue grew 17.7% YoY, ROTCE reached 24.8%, and investment banking revenue surged 44%. At $72.59, the stock trades at approximately 18.6x trailing split-adjusted EPS and ~12.3x annualized Q1 earnings power. The fundamental business is performing well, with record year-end 2025 client assets ($552B), strong IB revenue growth, and a bank subsidiary generating $1.09B in NII from a captive low-cost deposit base.

Two specific factors prevent a higher-conviction call. First, 19 pending FINRA arbitration claims related to former broker Chuck Roberts represent an ongoing, unquantifiable liability. Stifel has provisioned $180M, but the remaining claims could generate additional charges. Management's latest SEC disclosure states that reasonably possible losses not accrued are not expected to have a material effect on consolidated financial statements, though the uncertainty remains an overhang on valuation. Second, the current macro environment presents crosscurrents: rising long-end rates benefit NII but threaten IB deal volumes (which grew 71% from 2023 to $1.25B in FY2025 and are now at risk of mean-reversion). The stock trades approximately 19% below its split-adjusted 52-week high, reflecting both the litigation drag and macro uncertainty. The valuation appears reasonable on normalized earnings, but the specific catalyst to close this gap depends on litigation resolution timing that management cannot control.

## Company Overview

Stifel Financial operates through three segments: Global Wealth Management (~64% of revenue, more than 2,200 financial advisors across 402 offices), Institutional Group (~35%, covering equity/fixed income trading, research, IB advisory), and Other. The company holds $41.3B in assets, operates Stifel Bancorp with $22.1B in loans, and maintains investment-grade ratings (BBB+/BBB). Key subsidiaries include KBW (financial services specialist) and Bryan Garnier (European tech/healthcare). The 3-for-2 stock split in February 2026 reflected management confidence in sustained earnings growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth**: Net revenues grew from $4.35B (2023) → $4.97B (2024) → $5.53B (2025), with Q1 2026 GAAP revenue of $1.478B annualizing at ~$5.9B (though Q1 included a $49.8M gain on sale of Stifel Independent Advisors; non-GAAP revenue of $1.442B annualizes to ~$5.8B). The revenue mix is well-diversified: 31% asset management (recurring), 23% IB, 26% transactional, 20% NII.

**Profitability (Normalized)**: Excluding the $180M Q1 2025 legal provision, Stifel's underlying pre-tax margin was approximately 19-20% in FY2025. Q1 2026 demonstrated the true earnings power: pre-tax income of $326M on $1.48B revenue (22% margin). The Wealth Management segment delivered 35.5% pre-tax margin once litigation drag abated.

**Capital Structure**: Long-term debt declined from $1.12B (2023) to $617M (2025). Tier 1 capital ratio sits at 15.8%, well above regulatory requirements. The $1.0B credit facility was extended to 2031. Liquid assets total $12.9B.

**Capital Return**: FY2025 buybacks of $244.6M plus Q1 2026 repurchase activity of $224.4M (representing approximately 2.0% of market cap in a single quarter; this figure may include broader share repurchase and tax-withholding activity beyond Board-authorized buybacks). Combined with $1.36/share annual dividend (1.9% yield), total shareholder return capacity is substantial.

**Credit Quality**: Nonaccrual loans at 0.57% of total loans (improving from 0.77%). C&I net charge-offs of 1.22% ($48.3M) on $4.0B portfolio merit monitoring but remain manageable at the consolidated level. Allowance coverage of 1.29x nonaccruals is adequate.

## Growth Analysis

The growth trajectory is supported by multiple drivers:

1. **IB Recovery Continuation**: Advisory revenue grew 25% in FY2025 and +59% in Q1 2026. The pipeline is described as strong, and deregulation of bank M&A may benefit KBW given its financial services specialization. IB revenue has grown from $731M (2023) to $995M (2024) to $1.25B (2025); further acceleration requires sustained capital markets activity.

2. **Wealth Management Organic Growth**: More than 2,200 financial advisors with continued hiring from wirehouses drive AUM growth. Year-end 2025 client assets of $552B (declining to $539B in Q1 2026 amid market volatility) with $224B in fee-based accounts provide recurring revenue growth tied to market appreciation plus net new assets.

3. **NII Dynamics**: With $22.1B in loans (86% variable/adjustable beyond 1 year) and deposit costs declining faster than asset yields (weighted-average deposit cost down to 2.58% from 3.22%), NII is rate-sensitive and balance-sheet-growth dependent. Stifel's rate sensitivity modeling shows a +100bp rate shock increases Stifel Bancorp NII by 1.9%, while -100bp decreases it by 3.1%, indicating modest asset sensitivity.

4. **European Expansion**: Bryan Garnier acquisition positions Stifel for European tech/healthcare IB. Near-term, this adds integration costs ($9M revenue reduction from restructuring in Q1). Longer-term, it expands addressable market.

The offsetting risk: if rates rise further, IB deal volumes could contract as financing costs rise, creating a regime where NII benefits are offset by IB revenue declines.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.59, SF trades at:
- ~18.6x trailing split-adjusted EPS ($3.91, based on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $5.87 adjusted for the 3-for-2 split)
- ~12.3x annualized Q1 2026 EPS ($5.92 split-adjusted; though Q1 included $49.8M SIA sale gain, ~$0.24/share)
- ~11.5x forward consensus FY2026 EPS ($6.31)
- ~10.4x FY2027 consensus ($7.00)

**Peer Comparison**: SF trades at a discount to capital-markets-focused peers such as EVR, PIPR, HLI, and MC, which trade at higher forward multiples. Stifel's diversified model (including a large wealth-management and bank component) makes direct comparison to pure advisory firms imprecise. Among more comparable diversified firms, the discount is narrower but still present.

**Why the Discount Exists**: The market is discounting (1) ongoing Roberts litigation uncertainty (19 pending claims), (2) lower perceived earnings quality given past legal charges, and (3) greater complexity (bank + broker vs. pure advisory peers). The first factor is the binding constraint; if/when litigation resolves, re-rating toward 14-16x forward earnings is probable.

**What the Market May Be Wrong About**: At 11.5x FY2026 consensus with 25% ROTCE, 14% revenue growth, and declining litigation risk, SF appears to price in continued material adverse outcomes. If remaining Roberts claims resolve within existing reserves, the per-share impact would be modest relative to the $7+ annual earnings power.

I cannot confidently state the market is mispricing this because the remaining claim exposure is genuinely unknowable — 19 claims with undefined damages creates legitimate uncertainty. The discount is a rational response to an unresolvable information asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Stifel occupies a differentiated middle-market position: larger than regional firms (Piper Sandler, Moelis) but without bulge-bracket overhead. Its competitive advantages include:

- **Captive Banking Moat**: Brokerage client deposits of $25.6B (including $14.7B in Smart Rate) funding a $22.1B loan portfolio, with a weighted-average deposit cost of 2.58%, is difficult for pure broker-dealers to replicate. This generates $1.09B in NII that peers lack.
- **FA Recruitment Engine**: More than 2,200 FAs vs. sub-1,000 at most middle-market competitors. Transition pay packages ($744.6M outstanding) are costly but create a 5-12 year revenue lock-in.
- **Specialized IB Verticals**: KBW (financials), Bryan Garnier (European tech/healthcare), and sector-focused teams create advisory capabilities in specific industries.

Competitive position is stable-to-improving. IB revenue growth of 26% (FY2025) reflects strong execution, particularly in bank M&A advisory where KBW is well-positioned amid potential deregulation tailwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**Specific Risks**:

1. **Roberts Litigation (HIGH — Primary Overhang)**: 19 pending claims with undefined damages. The largest prior award was $132.5M (Jannetti), which grew to approximately $146.2M with prejudgment interest; Stifel sought vacatur and the matter remains subject to legal proceedings per the latest SEC filings. Management provisioned $180M in Q1 2025, and the Q1 2026 10-Q states that reasonably possible losses not accrued are not expected to have a material effect on consolidated financial statements. The claims represent an ongoing overhang on valuation until fully resolved.

2. **IB Revenue Cyclicality (MODERATE)**: In a rising-rate environment where financing costs increase, deal activity could contract, and IB revenue could decline 20-30% from peak.

3. **Rate Sensitivity — Both Ways (MODERATE)**: 86% variable-rate loan book benefits from stable/rising rates but creates vulnerability if rates decline. Conversely, if rates rise further, IB volumes face pressure while NII benefits.

4. **Credit Quality in Slowdown (LOW-MODERATE)**: C&I charge-offs at 1.22% are manageable but represent early-cycle exposure. If macro conditions deteriorate toward recession, loan losses could accelerate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($87-95, +20-31%)**: Roberts claims resolve within 12 months for manageable amounts within existing reserves. IB activity remains robust through FY2026 ($1.3-1.5B). NII expands modestly on stable rates. Stock re-rates to 14-15x forward P/E on $7.00 FY2027 EPS. Catalyst timeline: next 2-3 quarterly earnings demonstrating clean, litigation-free results.

**Bear Case ($58-65, -10-20%)**: Multiple additional Roberts claims result in large awards, requiring significant additional provisions beyond existing reserves. Concurrent IB revenue decline of 25%+ from cyclical peak in a rising-rate/recession environment compresses EPS to $5.00-5.50 split-adjusted. Stock de-rates to 11-12x trough earnings.

**Base Case ($80-85, +10-17%)**: Litigation gradually resolves over 12-18 months with manageable cumulative cost within or modestly above existing reserves. IB revenue flattens or grows low-single-digits. NII supports earnings stability. Stock drifts toward 12.5-13x FY2027 estimates as uncertainty premium gradually diminishes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)**:
1. Disclosure that remaining Roberts claims have been settled in aggregate for a quantified amount within existing reserves
2. Two consecutive quarters of clean earnings (no material litigation charges) combined with maintained 20%+ ROTCE
3. Stock decline to $62-65 (~10.5-11x forward) where risk/reward becomes asymmetric even accounting for litigation

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)**:
1. New Roberts-related claims beyond the known 19, suggesting the exposure is expanding rather than contracting
2. Material credit deterioration (charge-offs >1.5% on total loan book) combined with NII decline
3. IB revenue declining >30% from FY2025 levels without offsetting NII growth

**Reassessment Timeline**: Next earnings (likely late July 2026) — specifically monitoring litigation provision updates, IB revenue trajectory, and NIM progression.

## Conclusion

Stifel Financial is a well-managed, diversified financial services firm trading at a discount to peers with strong underlying fundamentals (25% ROTCE, 11%+ revenue growth, investment-grade balance sheet). The discount reflects legitimate uncertainty from 19 pending litigation claims rather than fundamental business deterioration. At 11.5x FY2026 consensus, the stock prices in continued adverse outcomes; if litigation resolves manageably, 20-30% upside is achievable over 12-18 months.

The HOLD rating reflects the inability to time the resolution of the primary overhang. The litigation risk is specific, material, and difficult to quantify, making confident position-sizing difficult. The stock's fundamental value supports a growth-oriented hold for existing positions; the next 2-3 quarters should provide clarity on remaining claim exposure. Accumulation at $62-65 (on further weakness) would improve risk/reward to BUY-worthy levels.
