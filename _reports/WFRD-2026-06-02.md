**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Weatherford has executed one of the more complete balance-sheet turnarounds in oilfield services since its 2019 Chapter 11 reorganization. Long-term debt is down from $2.2B in 2022 to $1.46B in 2025, net debt is roughly $0.5B against ~$1.0B cash, and three rating agencies upgraded the credit in 2025 (Ba2 positive at Moody's, BB stable at S&P and Fitch). ROE is 26.9%, FCF was $450M (9.2% margin), and the company funds a raised dividend plus buybacks. The deleveraging thesis that drove a ~134% stock advance over the past year is largely complete.

The forward question is the cycle, and the cycle is rolling over. Revenue fell 11% in 2025 to $4.92B after peaking in 2024, operating income dropped 19%, and segment EBITDA margins compressed across all three businesses on negative operating leverage. Management's 2026 guidance of $4.5B–$4.95B revenue and $945M–$1.075B adjusted EBITDA frames a flat-to-down year, consistent with its own 10-K language of "muted activity, particularly in the first half of 2026." Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 26.2% over 90 days and 12.3% over the last 30 days, even as full-year estimates drifted higher — a divergence that reflects near-term disruption (Iran conflict, Pemex) against a hoped-for second-half recovery that is not yet confirmed.

At $102.71 the stock trades at roughly 16x trailing/forward EPS, a discount to the peer median (~54x, distorted by NOV and Cactus), and meaningfully cheaper than the group on absolute multiple. The discount is partly earned: WFRD is the smallest of the four global majors, carries concentrated customer/receivable risk in Mexico, and faces an Iran-conflict drag on its largest fixed-asset region. With estimate momentum turning down at the front of the curve, insider selling that zeroed the CEO's direct stake near the highs, and a redomestication vote adding near-term execution noise, the risk/reward is balanced. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Weatherford is one of the four global oilfield services majors, competing with SLB, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes (plus Expro) across the full well lifecycle: drilling, evaluation, well construction, completion, production, intervention, and abandonment. It operates in ~75 countries from ~295 locations with ~16,700 employees. Roughly 85% of revenue is generated outside the U.S., a sharper international/NOC tilt than its U.S. shale-levered peers.

The company reports three segments:
- **Well Construction & Completions (WCC)** — largest and highest-margin: $1.875B revenue (38% of 2025), ~27.5% segment EBITDA. Tubular running, cementation, completions, liner hangers.
- **Drilling & Evaluation (DRE)** — $1.371B (28%), managed pressure drilling, directional drilling, wireline, fluids. Most cyclical; bore the brunt of the 2025 decline.
- **Production & Intervention (PRI)** — $1.340B (27%), artificial lift, intervention, digital, pressure pumping. Lowest margin (~19%).

WFRD's differentiation is its integrated well-lifecycle portfolio, digitalization, and international/NOC footprint. As the smallest of the majors, it remains a relative price-taker in a commoditizing services market with three larger, better-capitalized rivals.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of the financials is a cyclical revenue contraction off a 2024 peak.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 4,331 | 5,135 | 5,513 | 4,918 (-11%) | 1,152 (-3% YoY) |
| Operating income ($M) | 412 | 820 | 938 | 756 (-19%) | 123 (-13% YoY) |
| Net income ($M) | 26 | 417 | 506 | 431 | 109 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 792 | 676 | 136 |
| Free cash flow ($M) | — | — | ~493 | 450 | 85 (adj.) |

The 2025 revenue decline was concentrated: Latin America accounted for 83% of the drop, driven by a collapse in Mexico/Pemex activity, and the DRE segment represented 52% of the decline. Cost of revenue rose to 69% of sales (from 65% in 2024) as fixed costs fell more slowly than revenue, compressing segment EBITDA margins across the board (DRE -523bps to 22.5%, PRI -279bps to 19.2%, WCC -108bps to 27.5%). This is the standard negative-operating-leverage signature of a services company moving down the activity curve.

The balance sheet is the bright spot. Long-term debt fell to $1.46B; the October 2025 refinancing issued $1.2B of 6.75% 2033 notes and repaid $1.36B of 8.625% 2030 notes, cutting Q1'26 interest expense to $27M from $37M YoY. The credit facility was upsized to $1.0B. Current ratio is 2.19. Stockholders' equity has been rebuilt to $1.7B, though it still carries a ~$1.0–1.1B retained deficit and a $1.1B deferred-tax-asset valuation allowance (88% of DTAs), both legacies of the bankruptcy. The headline D/E of ~2.06 overstates leverage risk because the denominator is depressed equity, not because debt is excessive; net debt of ~$0.5B against $450M annual FCF is comfortable. Altman Z of 2.6 sits in the "grey zone," typical for a post-restructuring capital-intensive services firm rather than a distress signal given the cash position. FCF conversion (FCF/net income) above 100% in 2025 signals high-quality earnings.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with large beats in Q4'25 (+31.2%) and Q1'26 (+39.1%) following two misses in mid-2025. The recent beats coincide with the activity downturn, suggesting cost control rather than top-line acceleration is driving them.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case. WFRD is past its cyclical peak: 2024 was the high-water mark, 2025 declined, and management guides to a flat-to-down 2026. Q1'26 revenue was down 3% YoY and operating income down 13%.

Estimate revisions tell a two-speed story that warrants careful weighting. The front of the curve is deteriorating, reflecting the Iran-conflict disruption and continued Mexico softness, while current-year EPS revisions are up 11.4% over 30 days and next-year up 6.8%, implying analysts expect a second-half 2026 recovery. This is not the clean, uniformly rising revision profile that would constitute a strong forward signal; it is a near-term cut offset by a back-half hope that depends on a quick Iran resolution and a Mexico stabilization, neither of which is confirmed.

The industry framing matters. The sector is in a soft patch within a longer up-cycle: customer capital discipline, lower oil ($65.46 average WTI in 2025 vs. $76.55 in 2024), declining worldwide rig count (1,818 vs. 1,948), and E&P consolidation are compressing activity. Management argues the industry is "entering a period of tighter physical oil and gas markets," with NOC-led and energy-security spending favoring multi-year demand and a 2H'26 recovery. That is a credible medium-term thesis, but it is also the consensus bull narrative, and the near-term data is moving the other way.

The growth score reflects a business that has rolled over off peak with declining near-term estimates and a recovery thesis that remains a forecast. Current earnings are likely near a local high, not a trough, which argues against treating a high trailing multiple as cheap.

The analyst target range is $90–$156 (mean $119.92, median $119), implying ~16% upside to the median and ~52% to the high, a wide dispersion that captures the cyclical uncertainty.

The valuation question is whether 16x is a discount worth closing or an earned discount. We see it as largely earned. WFRD's current $5.93 EPS sits near a cyclical high, with 2026 guidance pointing flat-to-down. Applying 16x to the $5.97 current-year estimate produces roughly the current price; applying it to next-year's $7.50 consensus (which embeds the 2H recovery) gives ~$120, matching the analyst median but contingent on a recovery that front-quarter estimate cuts are currently contradicting. The bear case, if Mexico softness persists and the Iran disruption extends, would compress estimates toward the $5.00–$5.50 range and the multiple toward 14x, implying $70–$80.

The valuation score reflects a stock that is neither clearly cheap (no trough-earnings to re-rate against) nor clearly expensive (a defensible 16x on a de-risked balance sheet). It sits near fair value with two-sided risk.

## Competitive Landscape

WFRD's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding. It is the smallest of the four global majors, structurally disadvantaged on scale and capital relative to SLB, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes. Its integrated well-lifecycle portfolio and ~85% international/NOC revenue mix differentiate it from U.S. shale-levered peers and provide some insulation from the U.S. land downturn, but expose it to Mexico/Pemex concentration, Russia (~7% of revenue), and FX risk.

Segment trends in 2025 were uniformly negative on margin, with the most cyclical business (DRE) hit hardest. The Argentina pressure-pumping divestiture ($97M proceeds, $70M gain in Q2'25) was sensible portfolio pruning but also reduced 2025/26 PRI revenue. The technology/digitalization positioning is real but does not confer obvious pricing power in a commoditizing services market where customer capital discipline dominates. We do not see WFRD gaining share against the larger three; it is holding its niche through international diversification while the cycle pressures the whole group.

## Risk Assessment

**Mexico/Pemex concentration** is the most company-specific risk. Pemex represented 24% of total net A/R at both December 2025 and March 2026, and Mexico was 27–28% of total A/R against only ~5% of revenue, meaning collections lag badly. Pemex "has a history of making late payments" and has been paying through third-party financial institutions; Weatherford has resorted to a credit default swap tied to a Pemex secured loan and to factoring to manage the exposure. A Pemex default or further payment delays would hit cash flow directly, and the Mexico activity collapse already drove most of the 2025 revenue decline.

**Iran conflict (new, disclosed Q1'26 10-Q, began Feb 28, 2026).** Higher oil is a near-term negative: the conflict is depressing Middle East operations via activity delays, higher logistics/insurance costs, and supply-chain bottlenecks. The Middle East is WFRD's largest fixed-asset region (Saudi Arabia alone is 13% of PP&E). Management expects a first-half operating-income hit with 2H recovery contingent on resolution by end of Q2, a binary the company does not control.

**Commodity/rig-count cyclicality** underpins the entire thesis; the soft patch in oil prices and rig counts could deepen. **Russia exposure** (~7% of revenue, $106M cash, ~$255M assets) carries sanction/nationalization risk. **Legacy items** include the $1.1B DTA valuation allowance, a ~$570–629M surety bond/LOC book concentrated in Latin America, and an ongoing multi-year ERP implementation flagged as an internal-controls risk.

**Redomestication to Texas** (announced April 2, 2026, targeted Q3 2026, subject to shareholder and Irish High Court approval) is a structural positive long-term, reducing Irish stamp duty/withholding friction and §7874 risk, but adds near-term legal cost and voting/execution noise.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 48.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 41.1% (a 7.1pp spread), with the implied 1-month move at ±8.3%. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.2% (more demand for downside protection), though the put/call volume ratio of 0.27 and OI ratio of 0.37 are call-heavy on balance, consistent with a stock that has run hard and where holders are buying near-term puts as hedges rather than positioning aggressively bearish. IV rose 4.3pp (44.0% → 48.3%) as the price fell 4.9% over the latest snapshot window, indicating rising hedging demand into the recent pullback. The term structure is in contango (near 48.3% < long 50.6%), a relatively normal structure that does not signal acute near-term event stress beyond the known Iran/Mexico overhang. The options market confirms a two-sided, elevated-uncertainty setup rather than a directional conviction, supporting a HOLD rather than a directional position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$140–156).** The balance sheet is repaired and the cycle inflects in 2H'26 as management argues. Iran resolves by end of Q2, Mexico activity stabilizes and Pemex collections normalize, and NOC-led energy-security spending drives a multi-year up-cycle. Next-year EPS of $7.50 proves conservative; the stock re-rates toward 18–20x on $7.50–$8.00, reaching $140–$156 (matching the analyst high). The redomestication closes cleanly, removing a tax/legal overhang and broadening the U.S. shareholder base. Continued buybacks ($290M remaining of the $500M authorization) shrink the share count at sensible prices.

**Bear Case (~$70–80).** The cyclical rollover deepens. Mexico softness persists, the Iran disruption extends past Q2, and oil/rig counts grind lower under continued E&P capital discipline. Current-quarter estimate cuts prove the leading edge of a broader 2026 reset, pushing full-year EPS toward $5.00–$5.50. The multiple compresses toward 14x on falling estimates, taking the stock to $70–$80, roughly -22% to -32% from current. A Pemex payment event or Russia complication adds tail risk to cash flow.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction. The balance sheet quality argues against an AVOID; the cyclical rollover, declining front-end estimates, and insider selling argue against a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-quarter and next-quarter estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive months, confirming the 2H'26 recovery.
- Iran-conflict resolution and a demonstrated Middle East activity recovery in Q2/Q3'26 results.
- Pemex receivable balance declines materially (below ~15% of A/R) with evidence of normalized collections, removing the working-capital overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 2026 guidance is cut below the current $4.5B revenue floor, or full-year EPS estimates roll below $5.00.
- A Pemex payment default or write-down materially impairs cash flow.
- Margin compression accelerates (segment EBITDA margins fall another 200bps+ across segments), signaling the cost structure cannot flex with declining activity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2'26 earnings (late July 2026), which will reveal whether the Iran disruption is resolving and whether the Mexico decline has bottomed, plus progress on the Q3'26 redomestication vote.

## Conclusion

Weatherford has done the hard work on its balance sheet: net debt of ~$0.5B, three credit upgrades, a lower-coupon refinancing, and high-quality FCF conversion. That story is largely in the price, evidenced by the ~134% one-year advance. The forward thesis now rests entirely on the oilfield-services cycle, and the near-term data is moving against the bulls: 2026 guidance is flat-to-down, current-quarter estimates have been cut sharply, and the Iran conflict is a near-term headwind to the company's largest fixed-asset region. Layered on top are a genuine Pemex receivable concentration, Russia exposure, and CEO/officer selling that zeroed out direct holdings near the February highs, which sits awkwardly against management's bullish 2H'26 narrative.

At 16x on near-peak earnings with a wide analyst target band, the stock is neither cheap enough to buy nor broken enough to avoid. The risk/reward is balanced, and we would wait for the front-end estimate trajectory to confirm or refute the second-half recovery thesis. We maintain HOLD and would revisit on a confirmed estimate inflection (upgrade) or a guidance cut/Pemex event (downgrade).
