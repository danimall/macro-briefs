**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stride is a technology-based education company that delivers full-time virtual and blended public charter schooling on a per-pupil-funded "school-as-a-service" model, supplemented by a faster-growing Career Learning line. The financial profile through FY25 was excellent: revenue grew 17.9% to $2.405B, operating income rose 44% to $360M, free cash flow reached $431M (17.9% margin), and the balance sheet carries an Altman Z of 4.26 with only $420M of convertible debt against ~$800M of cash and securities. On those figures alone the stock at roughly 13x forward earnings looks inexpensive.

The headline FY25 numbers describe a company that no longer exists. The Q3 FY26 quarter (ended 3/31/26) showed total revenue growth collapsing to 2.7%, with General Education — about 60% of revenue — declining 3.6% on a 5.0% enrollment drop. Nine-month General Education revenue was essentially flat at +0.7%. Gross margin compressed roughly 380bps to 36.8% as instructional costs rose to 63.2% of revenue, and net income fell to $88.5M from $99.3M. A securities class action (MacMahon, filed November 2025, amended March 2026) alleges the company misled investors about a legacy technology platform and a new-platform rollout for FY26, the same period in which General Education enrollment and retention weakened. Estimate revisions are negative across every horizon.

The stock has fallen ~42% over twelve months and dropped a further ~13% in a single session on June 15, 2026, on roughly 4x average volume. That move is recent and fundamentally driven, not stale drift. The balance sheet is strong enough to rule out solvency risk and limit downside, but it provides no catalyst to reverse the core-franchise deterioration. Buying a cheap multiple on a declining primary segment, with active litigation tied to the cause of the decline and no positive estimate inflection, is a value-trap configuration. We rate the stock AVOID, while acknowledging the genuine quality of the Career Learning engine and the cash flows.

## Company Overview

Stride operates as a single reportable segment but reports two revenue lines. **General Education** ($1.45B in FY25, ~60% of revenue) is the K-12 core curriculum business delivered through 89 virtual and blended public charter schools across 31 states plus DC. Schools are funded by states on a per-pupil basis; Stride provides curriculum, the learning-management platform, certified instruction, student computers, and back-office administration under contracts averaging more than five years with automatic renewals. **Career Learning** ($957M, ~40%) covers middle- and high-school career pathways in IT, healthcare, and business, plus an Adult Learning business (Galvanize, Tech Elevator, MedCerts) sold to consumers, employers, and government.

The moat historically rested on one of the largest research-based digital K-12 curriculum portfolios, long-duration embedded charter contracts, deep regulatory and operational expertise in a compliance-heavy environment, and scale that lets Stride absorb school-level operating deficits that smaller entrants cannot. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. Fiscal year ends June 30.

The central question for the equity is whether virtual General Education enrollment saturated after the post-pandemic surge. The FY26 declines suggest it has, at least for now, while Career Learning retains runway.

## Financial Analysis

**Through FY25 the trajectory was exceptional.** Revenue compounded from $1.69B (FY22) to $1.84B (FY23) to $2.04B (FY24) to $2.405B (FY25, +17.9%). FY25 operating income rose 44.3% to $360M, gross margin expanded to 39.2% from 37.4%, SG&A leverage improved to 21.8% of revenue, and net income reached $288M (12.0% margin). FY25 absorbed a $59.5M impairment of Galvanize intangibles and lease right-of-use assets, an explicit signal that the 2020 Adult Learning acquisitions have underperformed.

**FY26 broke the trend.** Nine-month FY26 revenue grew 7.4% to $1,882M, but the quarterly cadence shows the deceleration is intensifying. General Education enrollment was down 5.0% in Q3 (134.4K vs 141.5K), with nine-month enrollment off 1.1% versus +13% growth in FY25. Career Learning offset some of this (Q3 +12.3% revenue, +11.6% enrollment; 9-month +17.6%), but Adult Learning continues to shrink, down roughly 31% in Q3.

**Margins compressed.** Q3 FY26 gross margin fell from 40.6% driven by hiring in growth states and higher salaries. Net income was additionally hurt by ~$11.5M of unrealized losses on publicly-held equity securities — Stride now carries a $43.7M minority-equity portfolio that injects mark-to-market noise into other income.

**Two quality-of-earnings flags warrant attention.** A $14.3M San Francisco lease-termination gain flattered Q2 FY26 gross margin and is non-recurring. The equity-security marks cut both ways but introduce volatility unrelated to operations.

**The balance sheet is genuinely strong.** Current ratio 5.39, D/E 0.55, ROE 19.5%, ROA 12.6%. The only debt is $420M of 1.125% convertible senior notes due September 2027 at a ~$52.88 conversion price, now deeply in the money. A capped call (upper strike $86.17) limits dilution, but settlement or refinancing within ~18 months is a capital-structure event to model. The board authorized a $500M buyback in November 2025 — the company's first major repurchase — and executed ~$89M (1.27M shares near $70) in Q2 FY26, with ~$411M remaining at 3/31/26. The buyback was launched opportunistically into the selloff and partly offsets potential convert dilution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has bifurcated. Career Learning, at 12-18% growth, is a legitimate engine and the reason the equity is not simply a melting ice cube. But at ~40% of revenue it cannot carry the consolidated growth rate while the larger 60% General Education segment declines.

The estimate-revision picture confirms the deterioration rather than offering an inflection: current-quarter EPS estimate cut 6.5% over 90 days, current-year down 1.6%, next-year down 3.6%, with no positive 30-day momentum on any line. This is the inverse of the rapid upward-revision signal that typically precedes re-rating. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $8.20 and next-year at $8.76 — only ~7% growth, well below the prior multi-year cadence.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and trending the wrong way: a -41.5% miss in June 2025, then beats of 26%, 18.9%, and a slim 2.9% in March 2026. The shrinking beat magnitude is consistent with management running out of upside as the core franchise stalls.

The school-choice tailwind (vouchers, tax credits, ESAs) is real and Stride cites it as a channel for tuition-based private schools, but it has not arrested the General Education enrollment decline. The most plausible explanation, supported by the securities litigation, is that a technology-platform transition damaged enrollment and retention in FY26 — an execution wound rather than a pure demand-saturation story, but one with no clean catalyst for recovery on a 12-month view.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.36, Stride trades at ~10x current-year consensus EPS of $8.20 and ~9.5x next-year $8.76, or ~14x trailing $5.95 (the trailing figure is distorted by the FY25 impairment and timing). Against Education Services peers — LOPE 19.1x, GHC 17.1x, PRDO 13.5x, DUOL 14.2x, STRA 14.1x, peer median 14.2x — Stride is the cheapest in the group.

That discount is earned, not anomalous. The cheap multiple reflects a declining core segment, negative estimate revisions, margin compression, and an unresolved securities class action — the structural-impairment profile where a low multiple is a warning rather than an opportunity. The cycle-adjusted upgrade logic that would justify a high valuation score (estimate revisions >30%, accelerating revenue) is absent; revisions are negative and revenue is decelerating, so the trailing multiple is not masking trough earnings.

Analyst price targets ($102 low, $125 high, $113.5 mean) sit well above the current price, but these targets predate the June 15 collapse and the trend of negative revisions; they are stale and likely to be cut toward the price rather than serving as floors. On a stalling core franchise, consensus targets do not function as the lagging-indicator floor they provide for genuine inflection stocks.

A defensible fair value uses ~12x next-year EPS of $8.76 (a small premium to peer median justified by the Career Learning growth and balance sheet, discounted for litigation and GenEd decline), giving roughly $105 — close to the analyst low. But that requires confidence that General Education stabilizes, which the data does not yet support. Until enrollment trends turn, the multiple is more likely to stay compressed or fall further on continued misses. The asymmetry is not favorable enough to own.

## Competitive Landscape

Stride's structural position is deteriorating at the margin in its largest line. The "unbundling" risk — charter boards transitioning to self-managed models and buying only pieces of Stride's offering at renewal — is a live threat as virtual education normalizes and AI lowers the cost of curriculum and tutoring tools. The same AI shift that threatens application software broadly is a competitive and academic-integrity risk for an education-content provider, and it intersects with the platform-execution problems now under litigation.

Career Learning is the bright spot, where Stride still gains share in career-pathway programs and the value proposition (employer-aligned credentials) is differentiated. Adult Learning, by contrast, continues to shed revenue and was impaired, indicating Stride has not built durable competitive advantage in the bootcamp/corporate-training space.

The net trajectory is mixed-to-negative: a deteriorating larger franchise partially offset by an improving smaller one, in an industry exposed to state-by-state regulatory and funding politics that can move against for-profit virtual operators with little warning.

## Risk Assessment

Several specific, not-fully-priced risks define the downside:

1. **Securities class action (MacMahon, E.D. Va.).** Class period October 2024–October 2025, alleging misrepresentation about the legacy platform and the FY26 new-platform rollout. Stride moved to dismiss in April 2026 and calls the claims meritless, but the overhang is unresolved and ties directly to the cause of the enrollment decline. An adverse ruling or settlement is a binary negative.

2. **General Education enrollment decline.** A continued drop into the FY27 school year (the critical fall enrollment cycle) would deepen the revenue and margin damage. The next fall enrollment data is the key catalyst and could be negative.

3. **Regulatory and funding politics.** Per-pupil state funding for for-profit virtual charters faces recurring legislative attempts at restriction; charter non-renewal or revocation (e.g., Arkansas's 2025 three-year-performance law) can remove revenue outside Stride's control.

4. **Convertible maturity (Sept 2027).** The in-the-money $420M convert requires refinancing or settlement within ~18 months; manageable given the cash position but a dilution/leverage event.

5. **Margin trajectory.** Instructional costs at 63.2% of revenue, up sharply, with no evidence of reversal.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 65.8% versus 38.0% a month earlier (+27.7pp), the term structure is in backwardation (65.8% near vs 50.0% long-term), and the put/call volume ratio is 1.55. ATM IV jumped as the price fell 6.2% over the snapshot window, with rising put open interest (P/C OI 0.36 → 0.61). The 1-week put/call volume ratio of 6.12 indicates heavy near-dated put demand around the June 15 collapse. IV is "cheap" only relative to the elevated 88.7% one-year historical vol; in absolute terms the market is bracing for continued volatility, consistent with the bearish fundamental read rather than offering a complacent entry.

### Short Interest

Short interest is 21.9% of float with 8.9 days to cover — elevated, confirming a crowded bearish view and creating genuine squeeze risk on any positive surprise. This is the principal argument against actively shorting (see below) and a reason the AVOID does not extend to a short recommendation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115).** General Education enrollment stabilizes in the FY27 fall cycle as the platform transition completes; Career Learning continues compounding at 12-18% and lifts consolidated growth back toward double digits; margins recover as instructional-cost hiring normalizes; the securities suit is dismissed; the buyback shrinks the share count opportunistically at depressed prices; and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median of 14x on $8.76 next-year EPS (~$123). Value investors (Voss, Frontier) accumulating on the dip are positioned for this outcome.

**Bear Case ($60-65).** General Education decline accelerates in the fall enrollment cycle as the platform problems persist and AI-enabled alternatives accelerate unbundling; margin compression continues; the securities suit survives the motion to dismiss and creates a settlement overhang; estimate revisions keep falling and the multiple compresses toward 8x on cut estimates. This would retest the late-2025 lows near $61-66.

**Net assessment.** The balance sheet caps the bear case well above zero and the Career Learning engine is real, so this is not a distressed situation. The bull case requires a stabilization that the most recent data (Q3 GenEd -3.6%, the June 15 collapse, negative revisions) actively contradicts, and the catalyst — fall enrollment — could break either way. The reward-to-risk from $83 is roughly balanced-to-unfavorable, and a cheap multiple on a deteriorating core franchise with active litigation is the classic value-trap setup. There is no positive estimate-revision inflection to lean on. We do not see an asymmetric reason to own the stock here.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalyst is the FY27 fall enrollment cycle and the associated Q1 FY27 report (likely late October 2026), where continued General Education declines would confirm the bear thesis. The securities class action motion-to-dismiss ruling is a secondary, undated catalyst. Neither is imminent enough to drive a near-term move with confidence.

**Timing window.** The decisive enrollment catalyst is ~4-5 months out, outside the window where a short comfortably justifies carry given elevated near-term IV and borrow.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** At 21.9% of float with 8.9 days to cover, this is a crowded short. Any stabilization in enrollment, a dismissal of the suit, or a continued buyback bid creates meaningful squeeze risk, and the remaining downside (toward the $61 prior low, ~25%) is modest relative to that risk.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm bearishness (PCV 1.55, backwardation, rising put OI), but the IV spike raises the cost of expressing the view through options, and elevated SI implies elevated borrow cost.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside to ~$62 is ~26%; a squeeze on any positive surprise could move the stock 20-30% the other way given the short crowding and the $411M buyback authorization. That is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis is correct — a decelerating core franchise with litigation overhang — but at 21.9% short interest with a fortress balance sheet and active buyback, the asymmetry favors avoiding ownership rather than shorting. The risk/reward does not support a short position.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The headwinds — General Education enrollment decline, margin compression, the platform-related securities litigation, and negative estimate revisions — are likely to persist through at least the FY27 fall enrollment cycle (Q1 FY27 report, ~late October 2026). We would not commit new capital ahead of that data.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- **General Education stabilization:** two consecutive quarters of flat-to-positive GenEd enrollment, confirming the platform transition is no longer destroying the core franchise. This is the single most important condition.
- **Estimate-revision inflection:** consensus current-year and next-year EPS turning positive over a 30-90 day window, reversing the current negative trend.
- **Litigation resolution:** dismissal of the MacMahon securities class action, removing the binary overhang.
- **Valuation cushion:** a re-test toward the $61-66 prior-low zone would change the risk/reward materially if it occurs without further fundamental deterioration, given the balance-sheet floor — but only paired with at least one of the stabilization signals above.

**Timeline:** Expect the General Education headwind to persist for two to three quarters minimum. The fall 2026 enrollment cycle is the decisive checkpoint. Until enrollment trends turn, cheapness alone is insufficient.

## Conclusion

Stride is a financially healthy, cash-generative, low-leverage business with a genuinely strong Career Learning engine. It is also at a clear negative inflection in its larger General Education franchise: GenEd revenue and enrollment are declining, estimate revisions are negative across every horizon, and a securities class action ties a disrupted platform rollout to the enrollment weakness. The June 15 single-session drop on heavy volume confirms the deterioration rather than offering a mispriced dip.

The balance sheet rules out solvency risk and caps the downside, but it does not supply a catalyst to reverse the core-franchise decline. At ~10x forward earnings the stock is the cheapest in its peer group, but on a declining primary segment with active litigation and no positive estimate inflection, that cheapness is a value-trap signal. The score trajectory (6.1 → 6.9 → 6.4) and the prior BUY look stale against the Q3 data and the June collapse. We rate the stock AVOID and would revisit only on confirmed General Education stabilization, an estimate-revision turn, or litigation resolution. The setup does not support an active short given 21.9% short interest and the buyback bid.
