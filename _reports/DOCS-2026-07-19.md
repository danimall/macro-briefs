**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Doximity is the dominant professional network and workflow platform for U.S. medical professionals, with more than 85% of practicing U.S. physicians, roughly two-thirds of nurse practitioners and physician assistants, and ~90% of graduating medical students registered on the platform. It monetizes that audience rather than its members: pharmaceutical manufacturers and health systems pay to deliver targeted content, recruit clinicians, and access workflow and clinical-AI tools. On balance-sheet and cash-flow metrics the business is pristine: $748.6M in cash and marketable securities, negligible debt, a 49% free-cash-flow margin, and an Altman Z-Score of 6.53. On those static screens it looks like one of the highest-quality software franchises in healthcare.

The problem is trajectory. FY2026 revenue grew 13% to $644.9M, down from +20% in FY2025, and management guided FY2027 to only ~4% growth with adjusted EBITDA margin stepping down from 55% to ~49%. Net revenue retention fell to 109% from 119% a year earlier, the clearest leading indicator of the deceleration. GAAP net income declined to $196.1M from $223.2M despite higher revenue, because stock-based compensation rose 68% to $121.6M as the company front-loaded AI investment. The stock has fallen roughly 70% from its late-2025 peak near $76 to ~$21.52, re-rating from a premium 20%+ grower to a low-single-digit grower. A BofA downgrade in June 2026 cited limited clarity on the revenue and margin trajectory.

The debate is well framed and genuinely two-sided. The bull case is that the fortress balance sheet, a ~15x forward multiple, aggressive buybacks, and Clinical AI Suite adoption at 140+ health systems make the sell-off an overreaction. The bear case is that AI-native competitors (OpenEvidence, Abridge), pharma budget caution tied to Most-Favored-Nation drug pricing, self-reported engagement metrics, and a decelerating NRR threaten the core monetization engine precisely as the company sacrifices margin to defend it, with a new CFO in the seat, ongoing derivative litigation, and insiders selling but not buying. We rate DOCS a HOLD. The evidence for a confident call in either direction is not yet present.

## Company Overview

Doximity operates a free-to-physicians professional network combined with clinical workflow software. Revenue comes from three segments:

- **Marketing Solutions** (the dominant revenue and profit driver): pharma manufacturers and health systems pay to deliver targeted sponsored content to physicians. Approximately 94% of revenue is subscription-based and ~83% comes from customers spending more than $500K per year.
- **Hiring Solutions**: digital recruiting plus the Curative Talent staffing operation.
- **Workflow Solutions**: telehealth (Dialer), on-call scheduling (Amion), digital fax, and the Clinical AI Suite (Ask, Scribe ambient documentation, PeerCheck verification), now adopted at 140+ health systems.

The company employs 880 people, more than a third in R&D, and operates on a March 31 fiscal year. Its structural advantage is near-universal penetration of a hard-to-reach, high-value, credential-verified audience, a network effect that would be difficult and expensive to replicate. Governance is controlled: Class B shares carry 10 votes each, giving CEO Jeff Tangney and affiliates roughly 79% of voting power. Public Class A holders have essentially no ability to influence corporate matters, and the board is staggered with supermajority amendment requirements.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is decelerating on a clear glide path.** Revenue moved from $475.4M (FY2024) to $570.4M (+20%, FY2025) to $644.9M (+13%, FY2026). Within FY2026, $48.4M of the increase came from existing-customer expansion versus only $16.2M from new customers, and average revenue per existing Marketing customer rose 10%. The count of customers spending more than $500K rose modestly to 125 from 118. Net revenue retention, the single most important leading indicator, fell to 109% from 119% in FY2025 and 114% in FY2024. That 10-point drop, combined with the ~4% FY2027 guide, is what the market repriced.

**Margins compressed on deliberate reinvestment.** Operating income declined to $214.9M from $227.8M, with operating margin falling from 40% to 34%. Net margin fell from 39% to 30% despite the revenue gain. The primary cause is stock-based compensation: R&D SBC alone tripled from $19.4M to $46.2M as the company accelerated AI spending, and total R&D expense rose 40% to $130.7M. G&A rose 43%, including $9.4M in higher legal fees. Adjusted EBITDA grew to $357.8M at a 55% margin, roughly flat, but the ~25-point gap between adjusted EBITDA margin (55%) and GAAP net margin (30%) is almost entirely SBC. Investors should treat the ~19% of revenue paid in stock as a real cost, not an add-back.

**Cash flow and the balance sheet are genuinely strong.** Operating cash flow was $326.5M and free cash flow $317.5M. Cash and marketable securities stood at $748.6M against minimal debt (D/E 0.18). The current ratio is 6.09, ROE 20.6%, ROA 17.4%, and the Altman Z-Score of 6.53 places the company deep in the safe zone. There is no refinancing risk, no covenant risk, and no cash-burn concern. Capital allocation has been buyback-heavy: the company completed a $500M program (11.6M shares), repurchased $431.7M in FY2026, and authorized a new $500M program in February 2026. No dividend.

**Earnings delivery has turned mixed.** The company beat estimates for three consecutive quarters (up to +17.5%) before missing the March 2026 quarter by 10% ($0.26 versus $0.29 expected). Consensus EPS estimates have been cut roughly 11-12% over 90 days across all forward periods (current year now $1.43 versus $1.62 ninety days ago) but have stabilized in the most recent 30 days. The reset appears to have caught up to the guidance shock rather than continuing to deteriorate, which supports treating current estimates as a reasonable anchor.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest pillar of the thesis and the reason for the low score. The step from +13% actual to ~4% guided growth is severe, and the mechanism matters: Marketing Solutions, the profit engine, is pharma-budget dependent, and the 10-K explicitly attributes short-term budget caution and bookings-timing delays to Most-Favored-Nation drug pricing negotiations between pharma and the federal government. This is not a one-off comparison problem; NRR at 109% signals that existing customers are expanding less, which is the most durable component of the growth model.

The offsetting question is whether AI investment reignites growth. The company points to enterprise monetization of the Clinical AI Suite as the next leg. In July 2026 Doximity published results claiming its Ask product outperformed leading frontier models and OpenEvidence on the Stanford-Harvard NOHARM clinical-AI safety benchmark. That is a company press release, not independent confirmation of commercial share, and it does not yet appear in the numbers. The honest read is that the AI opportunity is real and potentially large but unproven as a revenue driver, while the AI spend is already visible in the margin compression. For a growth thesis to reassert, we would need NRR to stabilize or turn and bookings to reaccelerate in the Marketing segment.

We do not see the classic signal that would warrant looking through a trough: estimate revisions are negative over 90 days (down ~12%), not positive, and revenue is decelerating, not accelerating. This is the opposite of the setup that justifies a growth-driven buy.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.52 the stock trades at roughly 15x the current-year consensus EPS of $1.43 and about 13.5x the next-year estimate of $1.59, a marked de-rating from its former premium. On an EV basis, subtracting ~$749M of net cash from the ~$2.9B market cap leaves an enterprise value near $2.15B against $357.8M of adjusted EBITDA, roughly 6x EV/EBITDA. Those are undemanding multiples for a business with a 49% FCF margin and net cash equal to ~26% of market cap.

Peer comparison is muddy because the Health Care Technology group is heterogeneous: Waystar at 27x, Omnicell at 86x, Healthstream at 39x, Certara loss-making, and Solventum at 9.7x, for a peer median around 39x. DOCS at ~15x forward sits well below that median. But the discount is not obviously mispricing; it reflects the reality that DOCS has just guided to ~4% growth versus faster-growing or differently-positioned peers, and that its GAAP earnings quality is undercut by the SBC load. Adjusting the multiple for the growth rate leaves DOCS near fairly valued rather than cheap: a ~15x multiple on a 4%-grower with heavy SBC is not a bargain, even against a rich sector.

We deliberately resist rewarding cheapness here without an independent catalyst. A compressed multiple on a franchise facing a competitive and policy-driven growth threat is exactly the profile that can stay cheap. The valuation supports downside protection (the balance sheet and buyback backstop the price) but is not asymmetric to the upside absent evidence that growth stabilizes. A 5.5 reflects reasonable value with a genuine trap risk, not a conviction buy.

## Competitive Landscape

Doximity's moat with physicians remains the strongest part of the story: 85%+ penetration of U.S. physicians on a credential-verified network is a durable asset that generalist networks (LinkedIn, Meta, Google, X) cannot match for medical targeting. That structural edge underpins the entire monetization model.

The trajectory of the moat, however, has flattened. The company now discloses direct AI-native competition: OpenEvidence for marketing dollars, and Abridge, OpenAI, and Anthropic for workflow and physician mindshare. The OpenEvidence dispute is active and escalating: OpenEvidence sued Doximity, its CTO, and its Director of AI Products in June 2025, amended the complaint in October 2025 to add subsidiary Pathway Medical alleging unauthorized platform access, and Doximity counterclaimed for false advertising and defamation. In January 2026 the court allowed most claims and Doximity's core counterclaims to proceed. This is a real, contentious incumbent-versus-challenger rivalry, not a theoretical risk.

The central industry dynamic is that generative AI is simultaneously the biggest opportunity (Clinical AI Suite monetization) and the biggest threat (AI tools competing for the physician attention that Doximity resells to pharma). Falling NRR and the risk factor around self-reported, third-party-unverified engagement metrics compound the concern: if AI competitors erode physician engagement, the metric that sells to advertisers weakens. The moat is intact but no longer clearly widening, which supports a mid-range competitive score with a downward trajectory since the prior review.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration has newly elevated.** For the first time, one customer exceeded 10% of total revenue in FY2026, whereas none did in FY2025 or FY2024. Pharma contracts are generally not long-term and can be renegotiated or reallocated to another agency, so a single large customer pulling back would be material.

**Policy exposure is specific.** Most-Favored-Nation pricing negotiations are cited in the 10-K as a direct contributor to pharma budget caution. Pharma marketing budgets are discretionary and policy-sensitive, and the profit engine sits squarely in that exposure.

**Metrics integrity.** The securities class action (settled for $31M, fully insurance-covered, final approval hearing June 10, 2026) concerned disputed 2021-2023 user-count and engagement disclosures. Six derivative suits remain outstanding with no estimable reserve. The 10-K carries an unusually detailed risk factor conceding that engagement metrics are internally calculated and third-party-unverified, the same metrics that underpin the pharma value proposition.

**Governance and management transition.** Dual-class control concentrates ~79% of voting power with insiders. The CFO seat turned over mid-downturn: Anna Bryson departed April 2026 (after a large February 2026 equity grant), and Matt Sonefeldt was appointed CFO on May 13, 2026, the same day as the weak Q4 guidance. Execution uncertainty during a strategic pivot is elevated.

Most of these are at least partially priced after a 70% drawdown, which is why the risk score sits at 4.5 rather than lower. But the combination of concentration, policy exposure, self-reported metrics, active AI litigation, and a mid-transition finance function is a denser-than-average cluster of idiosyncratic risks, and several carry asymmetric downside if they resolve poorly.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 89.8% against a one-year historical volatility of 56.7%, rich by 33 points, in the 96th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (89.8% near versus 73.0% at six months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±22.5%. IV jumped 23 points since the prior snapshot even as the stock rose 6%, indicating the market is bracing for the next earnings print. Skew is modestly call-heavy (-3.9%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.31, but the put/call open-interest ratio is above 1.0 and rising. Combined with 15.3% short interest and 5.4 days to cover, positioning is bearish-leaning but not extreme. The elevated near-term IV argues against chasing here; if the thesis were higher-conviction long, selling premium or waiting for post-earnings IV crush would be the better entry mechanic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

A financially pristine category leader has re-rated to ~15x forward EPS and ~6x EV/EBITDA, a level that already prices in low-single-digit growth. Management is repurchasing stock aggressively ($431.7M in FY2026 plus a fresh $500M authorization), which shrinks the share count and backstops the price. The margin step-down to ~49% EBITDA is a deliberate AI investment, not a demand collapse, and Clinical AI Suite adoption at 140+ health systems plus benchmark-leading Ask results position Doximity to monetize the same AI wave that threatens it. If NRR stabilizes and enterprise AI revenue materializes, the multiple re-rates and the stock returns toward the low-to-mid $30s. Bull target: **~$42** (analyst high), roughly +95% from current.

### Bear Case

The core monetization engine is under structural pressure. Growth has decelerated from 20% to 13% to guided ~4%, NRR fell 10 points to 109%, pharma budgets are exposed to MFN pricing policy, and AI-native competitors are contesting both physician engagement and marketing dollars. The engagement metrics that sell to advertisers are self-reported and were the subject of investor litigation. Margin is being sacrificed with no proof yet that AI spending reignites growth, so both pillars can erode together. GAAP net income already declined year over year. Insiders are selling into the decline with zero open-market buying, and the CFO transition adds execution risk. If growth stalls near 4% and margins keep stepping down, the stock drifts toward the mid-teens. Bear target: **~$18** (analyst low), roughly -16% from current.

### Base Case

The reset appears largely complete: estimates have stabilized over the past 30 days after the 90-day cut, and the ~15x forward multiple reasonably reflects a low-single-digit grower with a fortress balance sheet. Absent a growth reacceleration or a clear competitive setback, the stock likely trades in a range while the market waits for NRR and bookings data. Base target: **~$23.50** (analyst median), roughly +9% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the evidence does not yet support a confident directional call.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the Q1 FY2027 earnings release (expected August 2026), which will provide the first read on whether NRR and Marketing bookings stabilize after the guidance reset. The June 10, 2026 securities-settlement final approval hearing is a minor clearing event.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Net revenue retention stabilizes or turns higher (back above ~112%), signaling the expansion engine is not structurally impaired.
- Marketing Solutions bookings reaccelerate, or management raises FY2027 revenue guidance above the ~4% initial guide.
- Evidence that Clinical AI Suite / enterprise monetization is converting to meaningful revenue, or clustered open-market insider buying appears after the drawdown.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- NRR falls further (below ~105%) or a >10%-of-revenue customer reduces spend, confirming demand erosion.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance steps down again below ~49% without corresponding growth, indicating margin is being spent defensively without return.
- A material adverse development in the OpenEvidence litigation or evidence of engagement/metrics deterioration.

## Conclusion

Doximity combines a genuine moat, a fortress balance sheet, and a growth-and-margin scare that has already more than halved the stock. That combination is why the score has whipsawed (5.5 to 7.0 to 5.8 to our current 5.25) and why a HOLD is the honest call. The downside is cushioned by net cash, FCF, and an active buyback, so this is not a business at risk. The upside requires believing that heavy AI investment reignites growth rather than merely defends an eroding pharma-monetization model against AI-native challengers, and that thesis is unproven while NRR is falling, guidance is ~4%, insiders are selling without buying, and near-term options are pricing a ±22.5% move.

We would want to see stabilization in net revenue retention and Marketing bookings, or a clearer signal that enterprise AI is monetizing, before paying up for the re-rating. Until then, the risk/reward is roughly balanced around the current price, and patience through the August print is the appropriate posture. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
