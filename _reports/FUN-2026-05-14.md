## Executive Summary

Six Flags Entertainment is North America's largest regional amusement park operator, formed through the July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair and legacy Six Flags. The combined company now operates 21 amusement parks, 14 water parks, and 8 resort properties after divesting 7 underperforming parks. At $20.50, the stock has declined 47% from its 52-week high of $38.47, reflecting both merger integration disappointment (including a $1.52B goodwill impairment in Q3 2025) and concerns about the company's 6.6x net debt/EBITDA leverage ratio.

The investment case is genuinely balanced. On the positive side: Q1 2026 showed material improvement (EBITDA loss narrowed by $48M, attendance +4% same-park, per capita spending +6%), current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 146.5% in 30 days (from -$0.43 to +$0.20), and a new management team under activist pressure is executing portfolio optimization and cost discipline. On the negative side: $5.2B in debt with $320-330M annual cash interest, negative free cash flow in FY2025, near-total C-suite turnover creating execution uncertainty, and the punitive 8.625% rate on recent refinancing that reflects genuine credit risk.

The stock is not shortable at these levels (risk/reward below 2:1, 22.4% short interest creating squeeze risk), but the leverage profile and uncertain execution timeline prevent a constructive recommendation. This is a turnaround situation where the early signals are positive but the margin of safety is insufficient given the debt burden.

## Company Overview

Six Flags Entertainment (FUN) operates regional amusement and water parks across North America. The company was formed through Cedar Fair's acquisition of legacy Six Flags, closing July 1, 2024 (Cedar Fair as accounting acquirer). The business model relies on repeat visitation through season passes/memberships, in-park spending on food/beverage/merchandise, and a geographic moat around local drive-to markets.

**Revenue mix:** Admissions + in-park products (~95% of revenue) with a small out-of-park segment (resorts, sponsorships, international licensing including Six Flags Qiddiya City in Saudi Arabia). The business is highly seasonal — approximately 70% of revenue concentrates in Q2/Q3 (June-August peak).

**IP rights:** Exclusive U.S. theme park rights (ex-Florida, ex-Las Vegas) for Warner Bros./DC Comics animated characters and exclusive U.S./Canada rights for Peanuts characters.

Following portfolio optimization in early 2026, the company sold 6 U.S. parks to EPR Properties (Valleyfair, Worlds of Fun, Michigan's Adventure, Schlitterbahn Galveston, Six Flags St. Louis, Six Flags Great Escape), closed the Bowie, MD park, and has a pending sale of Six Flags La Ronde (Montreal). The divested parks generated only $47M in Adjusted EBITDA on $290M revenue (16% margin) — materially below the remaining portfolio's profitability.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue was $3.10B (up 14.4% from $2.71B in FY2024), but this growth is almost entirely explained by the inclusion of legacy Six Flags parks for the full year versus only six months in 2024. On a comparable basis, underlying performance deteriorated — Adjusted EBITDA declined from $875M to $792M (-9.5%), with margins compressing 610 basis points to 27.1%.

Q1 2026 represents the first clean quarter showing operational improvement:
- Revenue: $225.6M (+12% YoY)
- Attendance: 2.9M (+4%; +7% excluding removed winter events)
- Per capita spending: $69.26 (+6%)
- Adjusted EBITDA loss: -$123M (improved $48M from -$171M prior year)
- Operating costs: down 12% on cost discipline

### Balance Sheet and Debt

The balance sheet is the central risk:
- Total debt: ~$5.2B
- Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA: ~6.6x
- Cash interest expense guidance: $320-330M (consuming ~40% of trailing EBITDA)
- Weighted average cost of debt: approximately 6.5-7%
- FY2025 free cash flow: negative (-$152M)
- Current liquidity: $462M ($117M cash + revolver availability)

The January 2026 refinancing ($1.0B in 8.625% notes to retire 2027 maturities) pushed out the maturity wall but at significantly higher coupon versus the ~5.4% rates on retired notes. This reflects market pricing of genuine credit risk.

The company's indentures restrict dividend payments above the 5.50x leverage threshold — well below current levels. No dividend is being paid.

### Goodwill Impairment

The $1.518B impairment recognized in Q3 2025 (reducing goodwill from $3.3B to $2.1B) represents a damning verdict on merger pricing. Management explicitly warns of potential further impairments if operating trends don't improve. An additional $38.6M impairment was taken in Q1 2026 related to park dispositions.

### Cash Flow Dynamics

The path to positive free cash flow requires: Adjusted EBITDA recovery to $850M+ → less $320-330M interest → less $400-425M capex → approximately $100-125M free cash flow. This math works only if the remaining portfolio delivers meaningful EBITDA growth, which Q1 2026 trends tentatively support but one quarter does not confirm.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Catalysts

The turnaround thesis rests on several operational levers:

1. **Portfolio optimization:** Removing low-margin parks (16% margin vs. company average of 27%) should lift blended margins by 200-400 basis points on the remaining portfolio.

2. **Pass base recovery:** Active pass base grew 6% on a same-park basis through April 2026. Pass/membership revenue provides revenue visibility and drives repeat visitation.

3. **Per capita spending growth:** +6% in Q1 2026 suggests pricing power and execution on in-park monetization (front-of-line passes, premium food/beverage, etc.).

4. **Cost discipline:** Operating costs down 12% YoY in Q1 reflects management focus on efficiency after the integration missteps.

### Estimate Momentum

The estimate revision trajectory is the strongest positive signal:
- Current-year EPS: revised from -$0.43 to +$0.20 in 30 days (+146.5%)
- Next-year EPS: revised from $0.13 to +$0.21 in 30 days (+57.5%)

However, context matters: the 90-day revisions are still sharply negative (current quarter -27.3%, next quarter -13.9%, next year -72.4%). The 30-day uptick reflects Q1's beat flowing through, but the longer-term trend shows analysts have repeatedly cut estimates over the past three months. The current-year estimate of $0.20 EPS implies minimal net income — this is a company barely crossing breakeven on a GAAP basis after $320-330M in interest expense.

### Structural Growth Constraints

- High fixed-cost model means weather or attendance shortfalls produce disproportionate EBITDA declines
- The regional park model has inherent attendance ceilings — these are drive-to facilities dependent on population within a 2-3 hour radius
- Capex requirements ($400-425M annually) consume the majority of operating cash flow even in good years
- Partnership park obligations (Six Flags Over Georgia purchase required January 2027) represent an undisclosed cash commitment

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.50, FUN trades at:
- Market cap: $2.1B
- Enterprise value: ~$7.3B (including ~$5.2B net debt)
- EV/Adjusted EBITDA: ~9.2x (on $792M FY2025) or ~8.6x on $850M recovery case
- Forward P/E: not meaningful (barely positive EPS expected)

**Peer comparison:**
- United Parks & Resorts (PRKS): 12.5x P/E, -37% from 52-week high
- Planet Fitness (PLNT): 17.8x P/E, -56% from 52-week high
- OneSpaWorld (OSW): 31.3x P/E, -10% from 52-week high

The EV/EBITDA comparison is more relevant given FUN's leverage: at 9.2x, FUN trades at a discount to typical theme park operators (10-12x), which partially reflects leverage risk. If EBITDA recovers to $900M, EV/EBITDA compresses to ~8.1x — genuinely cheap for a stabilized operator.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $26.08, median $26.50 (range $21-$29). 8 of 12 analysts rate BUY/Strong BUY, implying 27% upside to mean target. However, with the stock at $20.50 and the low target at $21.00, there is essentially no downside cushion in analyst models — suggesting the current price already reflects material risk.

The stock has already declined 47% from its 52-week high, which provides some margin of safety against further deterioration. But this discount reflects real fundamental issues (negative FCF, 6.6x leverage, impairment, management turnover), not just sentiment. The positive estimate momentum is encouraging but too early to confirm a durable turn.

## Competitive Landscape

Six Flags' competitive position is mixed. The regional park model provides geographic moats — each park serves a distinct local market with limited direct competition. The exclusive character IP (Warner Bros./DC, Peanuts) provides differentiation versus local competitors.

However, competitive pressure is increasing:
- Universal's Epic Universe (Orlando, opened 2025) draws incremental vacation spending away from regional parks
- Rising entertainment alternatives (streaming, gaming, experiences) compete for consumer discretionary dollars
- United Parks & Resorts operates a similar regional model with potentially superior execution (PRKS trades at a premium)

The merger should theoretically provide scale advantages in procurement, technology, and marketing — but integration missteps (eliminating then reinstating park presidents, poor early financial execution) suggest these synergies have not materialized. The reversal of centralization decisions signals that the initial integration philosophy was counterproductive.

Active pass base +6% suggests the value proposition is resonating. Per capita spending +6% suggests pricing power remains intact. The portfolio optimization (removing weak parks) should improve average park quality metrics.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Leverage (severity: high, probability: ongoing):** At 6.6x net debt/EBITDA, any EBITDA shortfall compresses free cash flow rapidly. A 10% EBITDA miss (~$80M) would eliminate most potential free cash flow and potentially pressure covenant compliance (5.0x first lien stepping to 4.5x by 2027).

2. **Management execution (severity: high, probability: moderate):** Near-complete C-suite turnover in 6 months (CEO, Executive Chair, CMO, CLO, likely CFO). New leadership under John Reilly has shown positive early signals but hasn't demonstrated sustained execution through a full operating cycle.

3. **Consumer discretionary sensitivity (severity: moderate, probability: elevated in current macro):** Regional parks are discretionary spending. With gas prices elevated and consumer confidence deteriorating, attendance volumes face headwinds. The company explicitly calls out vulnerability to economic slowdowns.

4. **Further impairment risk (severity: moderate, probability: 30-40%):** $2.1B of goodwill remains. Management explicitly warns of potential further writedowns if operating trends don't improve.

5. **Weather/seasonality concentration:** 70% of revenue in Q2/Q3 means a poor summer (like 2025) produces outsized EBITDA impact with no ability to recover.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing signals elevated uncertainty:
- Near-term ATM IV: 109.7% vs. 66.3% historical volatility — a 43.4pp premium indicating the market prices extreme near-term uncertainty
- Term structure in steep backwardation (109.7% near-term vs. 66.2% at 6-month) — consistent with imminent resolution or continued stress
- Put/call skew at 39.1% — elevated put demand reflecting hedging activity
- IV increased 27.5pp over the past month (from 82.2% to 109.7%) despite a small price increase (+4.4%)

The IV premium suggests the market does not believe the Q1 beat has resolved fundamental uncertainty.

### Short Interest

Short interest at 22.4% of float (11.2 days to cover) is very high. This confirms institutional skepticism about the turnaround, but also provides potential squeeze fuel if operating results continue to improve. If the consensus that leverage + execution risk will prevent recovery proves wrong, covering could drive significant upside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($26-29, +27-42% upside)
- New management executes: EBITDA recovers to $850-900M on remaining portfolio through cost discipline, per capita growth, and attendance recovery
- Pass base continues growing at 6%+, summer 2026 attendance exceeds expectations
- Leverage declines toward 6.0x, enabling dividend reinstatement discussion by late 2027
- Park sales generate enough proceeds for meaningful debt reduction
- Activist pressure drives further portfolio optimization or strategic alternatives
- Short covering accelerates upside on positive data points

### Bear Case ($12-15, -27-42% downside)
- Summer 2026 attendance disappoints (weather, consumer weakness, competition)
- EBITDA stagnates at $750-800M, leverage remains elevated at 6.5x+
- Further goodwill impairments ($200-500M)
- Management turnover continues, integration execution remains poor
- Partnership park obligations in January 2027 require incremental debt or dilutive equity
- Consumer recession reduces visitation, high fixed costs amplify earnings decline
- Credit markets close, refinancing becomes progressively more expensive

## Conclusion

Six Flags at $20.50 represents a legitimate turnaround situation with early positive signals but insufficient evidence to warrant conviction in either direction. The Q1 2026 beat (40.8% EPS surprise), positive estimate revisions, and operational improvements demonstrate the new management team is making progress. Eight of twelve analysts maintain BUY ratings with a mean target of $26.08.

However, 6.6x leverage with $320-330M annual interest expense leaves zero margin for execution missteps. Near-total C-suite replacement creates genuine uncertainty about sustainable execution capability. Negative free cash flow in FY2025 means the company is not yet self-funding.

The HOLD recommendation reflects this genuine uncertainty. The turnaround has directional support but has not crossed the threshold of demonstrated sustainability. Q2 2026 earnings are the decisive catalyst — a strong summer would likely drive the stock toward the $26 analyst consensus; a weak one could send it back toward the $12-15 lows. The risk/reward at $20.50 is approximately symmetric, consistent with HOLD rather than a directional call.
