## Executive Summary

TotalEnergies' second quarter of 2026 was the strongest in roughly three years: adjusted net income of $6.0bn ($2.68 per share, +67% year over year), adjusted EBITDA of $13.2bn, cash flow from operations excluding working capital of $9.8bn, net debt down $3.3bn and gearing down about 2.4 points to 13.1%. The company raised full-year operating cash flow guidance above the prior $32bn and lifted the second interim dividend 5.9% to €0.90. On current-year consensus EPS of $10.64 the shares trade at 8.1x, with a 4.9% dividend yield and a further ~3.1% of buyback at the authorized run rate.

The complication is disclosed by the company itself. Brent averaged $103.8/b in the quarter but *exited* below $70. Production restarted in the Gulf in June could not be lifted within the quarter and was booked at the end-June price. Realized liquids of $91.6/b sat $12.2/b below Brent versus a $7.4/b discount in 1Q26, and the company captured only $17.9/b of the $22.7/b sequential Brent increase because liftings were weighted to the end of a softening market. Consensus has responded: full-year 2026 EPS has been cut 6.8% in 30 days to $10.64 and 2027 cut 3.0% to $9.64, and the company has missed EPS estimates in three of the last four quarters. Applying the company's own annual sensitivities to a $70 Brent / $11 TTF / $8-per-barrel refining-margin environment takes normalized adjusted net income to roughly $13.7-16bn, or $6.12-$7.15 per share — 12.0x to 14.0x the current price. That is a fair-to-full multiple for a European integrated, not a discounted one.

What supports holding rather than trimming is the funded distribution and a genuinely strong balance sheet. Gearing of 13.1% is low for the sector, 1H26 net cash flow of $10.46bn covers the ~$9.3bn annual dividend outright, and the capital-recycling machine is producing real proceeds ($1.2bn of net disposals inside 2Q26 net investments; a 50% farm-down of a 1.2 GW European renewables portfolio to a KKR-managed insurance account at a €1.8bn portfolio enterprise value). What argues against adding is the combination of falling estimates, a two-sided Hormuz exposure in which both the price upside and the volume downside are company-specific, and a price 8.9% below the 52-week high after a 41% twelve-month advance. Base case $91, bull $107, bear $68.

## Company Overview

TotalEnergies is a French *société européenne* operating in approximately 120 countries with more than 100,000 employees, reporting through five segments. The 2Q26 mix: Exploration & Production adjusted net operating income of $3.2bn (segment cash flow $5.8bn), Refining & Chemicals $1.8bn ($2.0bn), Marketing & Services $0.5bn — described as the best quarter in at least a decade — Integrated LNG approximately $0.8bn after a poor trading result, and Integrated Power with segment cash flow above $0.7bn. Aggregate "Upstream" ANOI was $3.2bn and "Downstream" $2.3bn.

Three structural features separate it from the US supermajors. First, Integrated LNG is a portfolio business — equity liquefaction plus third-party purchases plus trading and downstream regasification — that generated $4.7bn of cash flow in FY2025 and is guided to roughly $4.5bn in 2026. It is discretionary position-taking: 1Q26 trading outperformed, 2Q26 underperformed because the desk carried bullish positions into a flat-to-bearish European market. Second, Integrated Power is a scaled fifth segment: renewables gross capacity above 37 GW at end-June 2026, 8 GW added in 2025 with a stated 8 GW/year cadence, 2Q26 net generation of 14.8 TWh (+28% YoY), segment CFFO of $2.6bn in 2025 guided above $3bn in 2026, targeting 12% ROACE by 2030. Third, unit operating cost of $5/boe in 2025, guided below $5 in 2026, with 120% reserve replacement.

A reporting caveat: as a foreign private issuer TotalEnergies files Form 20-F annually (most recent 27 March 2026, covering FY2025) and furnishes 6-Ks for interim disclosure. There are no 10-Ks, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, DEF 14As or Section 16 filings. The FY2025 20-F risk-factor text was not retrievable for this analysis, and insider-transaction data does not exist for this issuer, so the absence of insider buying carries no signal. FY2025 IFRS revenue, total equity and absolute net debt were also not obtainable; only the company-stated gearing ratio is available.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline (company-stated, 11 February 2026 call):** adjusted net income $15.6bn, CFFO $28bn, net investments $17.1bn, ROE 13.6%, ROACE 12.6%, gearing 14.7%, dividend cash-out $8.1bn, buybacks executed $7.5bn, upstream production +4%, reserve replacement 120%.

**FY2026 guidance set in February:** net investments $15bn, gearing ~15%, oil and gas production +3%, total energy production +5%, Integrated Power CFFO above $3bn, Integrated LNG CFFO ~$4.5bn, buybacks $3-6bn (down from $7.5bn executed), free cash flow ~$11bn.

**1H26 delivered:** adjusted EBITDA $25.7bn (+27% YoY), IFRS net income +72% YoY, net cash flow $10.46bn ($4,098m in 1Q plus $6,357m in 2Q). Reconstructing from the disclosed sequential changes, 1H26 CFFO is approximately $18.4bn, 1H26 net investments approximately $7.8bn (about 52% of the $15bn plan, on schedule), and 1H26 adjusted net income approximately $11.5bn — already about 74% of all of FY2025.

Two derived observations matter for the distribution question.

First, FY2025 distributions of $15.6bn ($8.1bn dividend plus $7.5bn buyback) equalled 55.7% of the $28bn CFFO, well above the stated "around 40% of annual cash flow" frame, and gearing drifted to 14.7% over the same year. The 2026 buyback step-down to $3-6bn is consistent with normalization after an above-trend distribution year. The counter-explanation: the company lists both "payout ratio" and "debt adjusted cash flow" among its non-IFRS measures, so the denominator in the 40% policy is ambiguous from the outside and the target may be measured across a cycle. Either reading supports the same conclusion — management is not treating 2Q26 pricing as a new run rate, or it would have raised the buyback rather than holding it at $1.5bn with gearing falling to 13.1%.

Second, the guidance path is achievable without heroic assumptions. With 1H26 CFFO near $18.4bn, reaching $32bn requires $13.6bn in 2H26, roughly $6.8bn per quarter — below the 1H26 average, so deliverable even at materially lower prices. Reaching the mid-$30bn range requires near-2Q26 conditions to persist. A third-party summary circulating a $34.5-38bn range is a construction, not company guidance, and should not be modelled as such.

**Distribution coverage.** The 2026 dividend at €0.90 per quarter on ~2,239m shares is about €8.06bn, roughly $9.35bn at €/$1.16. Against reconstructed FY net cash flow (CFFO less net investments) of $17-20bn at current conditions, the dividend is covered roughly twice and buybacks of $6bn are fundable from cash. The stress point is lower: at management's $50/b project screening price, applying the disclosed $2.8bn-per-$10/b CFFO sensitivity from a ~$70 environment implies roughly $24.4bn of CFFO against $9.35bn of dividend plus $15bn of net investments — essentially the entire operating cash flow, with buybacks then funded by disposals or the balance sheet. That extrapolation runs outside the company's stated $60-70 Brent validity band and is therefore indicative, but it explains the shape of the 2026 buyback guidance and defines the realistic floor scenario: the dividend survives at $50 Brent, the buyback does not.

**Balance sheet.** Gearing of 13.1% at 30 June 2026 is the only capital-structure datapoint I can source directly. I cannot verify absolute net debt, total equity, the debt maturity schedule, covenant terms, ratings, hybrid instruments or off-balance-sheet project-finance guarantees; the company also reports both "gearing" and "normalized gearing," with differing lease treatment. On the single available ratio, leverage is conservative for an integrated major and provides the flexibility that the $50-Brent case requires. Financial health scores 7.0 — strong cash generation and low leverage, discounted for the disclosure gaps and the fact that the buyback line is the first thing to break in a downcycle.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is real and contracted rather than speculative. Guidance is +3% oil and gas and +5% total energy production for 2026, with net electricity production up 25%. The near-term project set disclosed on the 2Q26 call is dense: first cargo loaded at Energía Costa Azul; North Field East (Qatar) start expected in 3Q26; Kingfisher (Uganda) nearing start-up with Tilenga ramping to plateau by mid-2027; Suriname GranMorgu FPSO about 40% built with first oil targeted in 1H2028; Papua LNG and Mozambique LNG progressing. FIDs planned in 2026 for Venus (Namibia) and the IMA gas project (Nigeria). On 28 July 2026 the company took FID on Cronos (Cyprus Block 6) with Eni — 50/50, Eni operator, gas piped to Egypt and liquefied at Damietta, start-up 2028, plateau around 500 Mcf/d (~2.8 Mtpa LNG) of which TotalEnergies markets ~1.4 Mtpa.

Against that, earnings growth is price-driven and consensus expects it to reverse. Full-year 2026 consensus of $10.64 sits above 2027 consensus of $9.64 — analysts model a 9% earnings decline next year — and both have been cut in the last 30 days. The quarterly picture is more mixed: current-quarter consensus of $2.87 is up 13.1% over 90 days and roughly flat over 30 days, plausibly reflecting the company's disclosure that July European refining margins ran near $31/b against a 2Q26 ERM average of $13.5/b. At $0.3bn of annual ANOI per $1/b of ERM, an $17.5/b uplift sustained for a quarter is worth well over $1bn — downstream can carry 3Q26 even if crude does not.

Integrated Power growth deserves a specific caution. 2Q26 net generation of 14.8 TWh annualizes to 59.2 TWh against a stated ">60 TWh/year" objective, but European solar peaks in Q2/Q3 so that annualization overstates the run rate, and the target's date and basis (gross versus net, asset perimeter) are unconfirmed. The segment's investment case has to shift from volume to returns: 12% ROACE by 2030 is an objective, below the group's current 12.6%, and segment CFFO of $2.6bn in 2025 against a sustained 8 GW/year build is the number that determines whether this is value-creating. Growth prospects score 5.5 — solid volumes and a credible project pipeline against negative earnings-estimate momentum and a segment whose returns are promised rather than demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

Take the multiples in sequence, on the $85.83 official close.

- **FY2025 adjusted net income $15.6bn:** 12.2x on $190.8bn of market capitalization.
- **Current-year consensus EPS $10.64:** 8.1x.
- **Next-year consensus EPS $9.64:** 8.9x.
- **Annualized 1H26 adjusted net income (~$23bn):** 8.3x. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast — it embeds $103.8 Brent, a decade-best Marketing & Services quarter and a $13.5/b ERM.
- **Normalized construction:** start from 2Q26 annualized adjusted net income of ~$24bn. Move liquids from $103.8 to $70 (−3.38 × $2.3bn = −$7.8bn), TTF from $15.6 to $11.0 (−2.3 × $0.4bn = −$0.9bn), ERM from $13.5 to $8.0 (−5.5 × $0.3bn = −$1.65bn). Result ≈ $13.7bn, or $6.12 per share on ~2,239m shares — **14.0x**. Because fiscal-regime progressivity damps both directions and the company limits these sensitivities to a $60-70 Brent band, linear extrapolation across $34/b probably overstates the decline; a $16bn upper bound gives $7.15 per share and **12.0x**. Treat 12-14x as the normalized range.

The reported trailing/forward P/E of 10.7 in the market-data feed is internally inconsistent with the reported $5.78 trailing EPS (which would imply ~$62) and should be disregarded; my computed multiples above are the operative figures.

The "cheap" reading (8.1x, 4.9% yield) rests on earnings the company earned at a price it no longer receives, and the normalized reading puts the shares at a full multiple for a European integrated. This is the pattern where a low headline multiple in energy marks the top of a price cycle rather than a mispricing. The two offsets are genuine, however. First, absolute yield support: €3.60 per year is 4.9% before French withholding tax (which is material for US ADS holders and is not addressed in any company document I read), and it is covered at $70 Brent and survivable at $50. Add the authorized $1.5bn quarterly buyback and total shareholder yield is roughly 7.8-8.0% at the top of the guided range, 6.5% at the bottom. Second, the persistent discount of European integrateds to US majors. Part of that gap is jurisdictional — climate litigation, tax regimes, index flows — and is unlikely to close on this cycle.

Analyst positioning is stable-to-marginally-better: 1 strong buy, 5 buy, 3 hold, 1 sell, with the buy bucket up one and the hold bucket up two over the prior month. Mean target $95 (+10.7%), median $94, high $107, low $81. Because full-year estimates are being cut rather than raised, I treat these targets as a fair-value band rather than a floor — the "targets lag reality" dynamic applies to inflecting estimates, and here the annual estimates are moving the other way.

Valuation scores 5.5: fair, with real yield support and a defensible European discount, but no identified mispricing and a normalized multiple at the upper end of the historical range for this asset base.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory is stable rather than improving. Positives: $5/boe unit operating cost (company claim, peer comparison unverified); 120% reserve replacement, above unity and not universal among European majors; an LNG portfolio whose realized price moved from $9.10 to $10.20/MMBtu year over year while realized gas barely moved ($5.63 to $5.55) despite TTF rising 31% — evidence that Brent-linked long-term indexation genuinely damps both directions and that TTF is the wrong benchmark for modelling this company's gas earnings.

The refining cycle is the more durable positive. ERM ran $4.7 → $8.4 → $11.4 → $11.4 → $13.5 across the last five quarters, and most of that recovery predates the 2026 Gulf conflict. European capacity rationalization and reduced product imports are the likeliest structural driver, which suggests a portion of downstream improvement outlasts the crude spike. The counter-reading — that the 2025 recovery reflected outages and inventory effects — cannot be excluded from this data.

The negatives are specific. Realized liquids captured only 79% of the sequential Brent increase because of a back-loaded lifting calendar; peers with different schedules captured more, and this is a recurring structural characteristic of a Gulf-weighted portfolio, not a one-quarter accident. Gas trading is discretionary and cost roughly the difference between a good and a poor LNG quarter. The renewables strategy is a live debate: TotalEnergies is acquiring Shell's entire European onshore renewables business (4 GW: 500 MW operating or under construction mainly in Italy and the Netherlands, plus a 3.5 GW development pipeline in Italy, the UK and Spain, consideration undisclosed, completion expected by end-2026) at the same moment it sells 50% of a largely developed 1.2 GW portfolio in Germany, Spain, France and Poland to a KKR-managed insurance account at a €1.8bn portfolio enterprise value while retaining 50% and operatorship. Buying pipeline from a retreating peer and farming down de-risked capacity to a financial partner is a standard develop-and-farm-down model that preserves development margin and operating fees. The alternative reading — that farm-downs are a funding necessity because segment cash generation cannot sustain 8 GW/year — cannot be distinguished from these disclosures. Cash proceeds correspond to a 50% interest, not the €1.8bn enterprise value. Competitive position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Gulf concentration (dominant near-term operating risk).** 2Q26 production impact was approximately 210 kboe/d, better than the ~360 kboe/d previously guided, with physical lifting disruptions around 350 kb/d. Management now anticipates a continuing 5-10% constraint depending on the situation. Integrated LNG production fell about 10% sequentially mainly on shut-in Qatari output. TotalEnergies is unusually concentrated in the Gulf relative to peers (UAE offshore, Qatar North Field, Iraq, Oman), which makes this company-specific rather than sector-generic. The exposure is two-sided in an awkward way: the same disruption that cost volumes lifted Brent to $103.8 and ERM to $13.5, and a verified reopening of normal transit would compress realizations faster than volumes recover. That asymmetry is the single largest determinant of 2H26 outcomes and is why I do not treat cheapness on current-year earnings as a margin of safety.

**Duty-of-vigilance climate litigation.** On 25 June 2026 the Paris Judicial Court ordered TotalEnergies to disclose climate risks linked to emissions from customers' use of its products (Scope 3) and to set out mitigation plans within six months. The company's own 26 June statement notes that the court did **not** uphold claims seeking to prohibit new fossil projects — there is no capex injunction, so the near-term P&L impact is likely immaterial. On 27 July 2026, after Board deliberation, TotalEnergies appealed, arguing that global climate change falls outside the law's scope, that the law addresses risks from its own operations and supply chain rather than customers' use, and that the CSDDD does not cover customers' activities. The risk is precedential: appellate affirmation extending duty-of-vigilance to Scope 3 would create a platform for future injunctive claims across French issuers. The stock recorded an end-of-day move of −3.04% on the appeal announcement date, but oil prices were falling in the same window and causation is not established.

**Russian exposure, unquantified.** Arctic LNG 2 was fully impaired in 2022 and is being transferred to a Russian subsidiary. On Yamal LNG, management said 2026 operations proceed as planned but is seeking sanctions clarification for 2027, which could affect its ability to market Russian LNG; the 2Q26 call flagged constraints on some cash distributions from Russian-linked assets. Residual carrying values, equity percentages and the magnitude of blocked distributions are not disclosed in anything I read. This is a live unquantified tail.

**Mozambique cost-recovery dispute.** Bloomberg reporting (27 May 2026, unconfirmed by the company) indicates Mozambique's government disputes TotalEnergies' estimate of roughly $2bn in delay-related cost overruns, with a Bayphase audit reportedly unable to verify the figure. Recoverable cost determines the state's take, so an unresolved dispute is economically material to project NPV. The company's own commentary is limited to "progress in Mozambique LNG"; no revised capex or first-cargo date was obtained.

**Operational incidents.** The SATORP refinery at Jubail suffered damage and is running at approximately 70% capacity with full capacity expected by end-3Q26; the cause was not stated and I am not attributing it to the conflict. Port Arthur was struck by lightning during a tropical storm and is progressively normalizing.

**Potential equity-funded M&A dilution.** Management has stated it aims to increase US upstream gas presence, is considering M&A, and that US-listed shares could facilitate deals by using equity rather than cash for tax efficiency. This is stated intent and capability, not a transaction. A share-funded acquisition would be economically the reverse of the current buyback and is a real, if unquantified, dilution risk for holders.

**Estimate and delivery risk.** Three EPS misses in the last four quarters (−2.0%, +7.1%, −0.4%, −3.0%) argue for modest confidence in consensus. Full-year estimates falling while quarterly estimates hold is an unusual combination that usually resolves through further annual cuts.

Risk factors score 5.0. Sector-level oil volatility is priced; the Gulf concentration, the unquantified Russian residual, the Mozambique dispute and the equity-M&A optionality are not obviously priced at 8.9% below the 52-week high.

Term structure is flat (22.5% near, 21.3% far), so uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated on a dated catalyst — consistent with an oil-path exposure rather than a company-specific binary. For a fundamental holder, cheap short-dated volatility makes covered-call or collar overlays reasonably priced if the objective is to monetize a fair-valued position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($107, +24.7%).** Gulf disruption persists or re-escalates, keeping Brent in the $90-105 band into 2027 with the 5-10% volume constraint more than offset by price. European refining margins hold near the ~$31/b July level for a meaningful part of 3Q26, delivering a downstream quarter that beats the $2.87 consensus. FY2026 CFFO lands in the mid-$30bn range, the Board tops up 4Q26 buybacks toward the $6bn upper guidance, gearing falls below 12%, and North Field East, Kingfisher and Energía Costa Azul add volumes into 2027. FY2026 EPS above $11 at 9.5-10x, plus the dividend, gets to the analyst high. This case does not require anything the company has not already demonstrated in 1H26.

**Base case ($91, +6.0% plus 4.9% dividend ≈ 11% total return over 12 months).** Brent normalizes into the $75-85 range with intermittent disruption premia; refining margins retreat from July peaks but remain structurally above 2025 levels; LNG trading reverts to a neutral contribution. FY2026 lands near consensus $10.64, FY2027 near $9.64. Applying 9.5x to FY2027 consensus gives $92; 13.5x on the mid-point of my normalized $6.12-$7.15 EPS range gives $90. Buybacks run at the mid-point of $3-6bn, gearing holds near 13-14%, and the dividend grows mid-single-digits.

**Bear case ($68, −20.8%).** Verified sustained transit through Hormuz plus completion of the OPEC+ voluntary-cut rollback takes Brent to $60-65. Realizations compress faster than the 5-10% volume constraint unwinds, refining margins revert toward $8/b, and FY2027 adjusted EPS falls toward $6.50. Buybacks are cut to the $3bn floor or suspended to protect gearing; the dividend holds but the total-yield story loses its second leg. 10.5x on $6.50 gives $68, above the $57.39 52-week low because the earnings base and balance sheet are both stronger than in October 2025.

Probability-weighting 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear gives roughly +5.5% price return plus the dividend, or about +10% total — positive, but not the asymmetry that justifies new capital in the sector with annual estimates being revised down rather than up.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should keep the position for the covered 4.9% dividend and the buyback; new capital has no urgent entry signal 8.9% below the 52-week high with normalized earnings implying 12-14x.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price:** shares at $72-75 or below with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $9.00 — that combination puts the stock at roughly 8x normalized rather than 12-14x and restores a genuine margin of safety.
2. **Distribution confirmation:** a 4Q26 buyback authorization above $1.5bn (i.e., FY delivery toward the $6bn top of guidance) alongside gearing at or below 12%, which would demonstrate that management views current cash generation as sustainable rather than transitory.
3. **Estimate inflection:** FY2027 consensus revised up for two consecutive months, reversing the current −4.2% 90-day trend, ideally driven by refining margins holding above $15/b rather than by crude alone.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **Verified normalization of Gulf transit** sustained for 72+ hours together with Brent below $70, which converts the volume-constraint offset into pure realization compression.
2. **A share-funded US upstream gas acquisition**, which would reverse the buyback economics and be dilutive at a full multiple.
3. **Buyback cut below $1.5bn per quarter, or gearing back above 16%**, either of which signals the $50-Brent stress math is becoming operative.
4. **An appellate ruling extending duty-of-vigilance obligations to Scope 3 with injunctive scope**, as distinct from the current disclosure-only order.

**Reassessment timeline:** the 3Q26 results release (late October 2026) is the natural checkpoint. It resolves three things simultaneously: whether the ~$31/b July refining margin translated into a downstream quarter that supports the $2.87 consensus, whether realizations recovered once the end-June lifting distortion cleared, and what the Board authorizes for 4Q26 buybacks. The company's mid-October indicator table (furnished on 6-K) will give the price and margin inputs roughly a week ahead of the results and is the single most useful document for this issuer.

## Conclusion

TotalEnergies is a well-run integrated major with a conservative balance sheet, a covered and growing dividend, low unit costs, above-unity reserve replacement and a dense project pipeline. At $85.83, it is priced at 12-14x the earnings it would generate at $70 Brent, $11 TTF and an $8/b European refining margin, having just reported a quarter earned at $103.8 Brent that the company itself flagged as unrepresentative, with Brent exiting the quarter below $70 and realized liquids capturing only 79% of the sequential price move. Consensus has cut FY2026 EPS 6.8% in 30 days and FY2027 by 3.0%, and the company has missed in three of the last four quarters.

The clearest read on management's own view is that with gearing at a multi-year low of 13.1% and the best adjusted quarter in roughly three years behind it, the Board authorized $1.5bn of buyback for 3Q26 rather than raising the pace, and full-year buyback guidance sits at $3-6bn against $7.5bn executed in 2025. Holders are paid roughly 7-8% in total shareholder yield to wait, and the dividend survives even at management's $50/b screening price. That combination supports keeping the position and does not support adding at 8.9% below the 52-week high. Overall score 5.8; **HOLD**, income rationale, with $72-75 the price at which the risk/reward becomes genuinely asymmetric.
