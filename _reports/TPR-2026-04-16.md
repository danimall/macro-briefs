## Executive Summary

Tapestry is a two-speed company. Coach, representing ~80% of revenue, is delivering some of the strongest results in accessible luxury: 25% constant-currency growth in FQ2 2026, 33.5% operating margins, and 3.7 million new customers acquired in a single quarter (a third of them Gen Z). This growth engine drove record quarterly revenue of $2.5B and prompted management to raise FY2026 guidance to $7.75B+ in revenue and $6.40-6.45 in diluted EPS.

The problem is the stock price already reflects this. TPR has surged 137% from its April 2025 low of ~$62 to ~$150, a move catalyzed by the blowout FQ2 2026 earnings report on February 5, 2026 (the stock gapped from $130 to $143 that day and continued to $162). At ~$150, the stock trades at roughly 23x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $6.45 — a premium to the peer median P/E of 24.4x on trailing numbers, but not obviously cheap given Coach's already-elevated margins and the material headwinds facing the business. Those headwinds include: Kate Spade's ongoing decline ($855M impairment, three consecutive years of revenue declines), approximately 230bps of operating margin pressure from tariffs on Vietnamese/Cambodian-sourced goods, a consumer environment deteriorating at record pace, and elevated leverage from the failed Capri acquisition aftermath.

The underlying business quality justifies a premium to historical valuation, but at current levels, the reward-to-risk ratio is balanced. The right entry point is lower, likely on a pullback related to tariff realization or consumer spending deceleration in coming quarters.

## Company Overview

Tapestry operates two brands following the August 2025 divestiture of Stuart Weitzman to Caleres for $105M. **Coach** (79.9% of FY2025 revenue, $5.60B) is the growth engine — an accessible luxury brand positioned at the $200-$500 handbag price point that has successfully attracted Gen Z consumers through reduced promotional activity, elevated design, and digital marketing. **Kate Spade** (17.1% of revenue, $1.20B) is in structural decline, with three consecutive years of falling revenue and an $855M impairment recognized in FY2025.

The business model is ~86% direct-to-consumer (1,371 stores globally plus e-commerce), ~13% wholesale, and ~1% licensing. Manufacturing is outsourced to independent factories predominantly in Vietnam, Cambodia, the Philippines, India, and mainland China. Approximately 40% of revenue is generated outside the United States, with meaningful Greater China exposure.

The failed $8.5B Capri Holdings acquisition (blocked by the FTC in October 2024, terminated November 2024) is the dominant balance sheet event of the past two years. Tapestry issued $6.09B in notes to finance the deal, redeemed them at 101% after termination, and redirected capital toward a $2.0B accelerated share repurchase. This sequence left the balance sheet with elevated debt ($2.39B) and compressed equity ($858M), creating a misleading 6.67x debt-to-equity ratio and a 1.79 Altman Z-Score that overstates actual financial risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Flat from FY2022-FY2024 ($6.66-$6.68B), then accelerated to $7.01B in FY2025 (+5.1%). FQ2 2026 showed dramatic acceleration: $2.5B (+14% reported, +18% pro forma ex-Stuart Weitzman). Coach drove the beat with 25% constant-currency growth. FY2026 guidance was raised to $7.75B+ (+14% YoY).

**Profitability:** Gross margin expanded from ~69% in FY2022 to 75.4% in FY2025, reflecting reduced promotional activity and brand elevation at Coach. Non-GAAP operating margin reached 20.0% (up 130bps YoY). Coach's standalone 33.5% operating margin approaches true luxury territory. GAAP results were heavily distorted by ~$985M in one-time items (Kate Spade impairment, Capri acquisition costs, Stuart Weitzman divestiture, restructuring).

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $1.22B and free cash flow of $1.09B (15.6% FCF margin). FCF covers annual interest expense of ~$131M (normalized) approximately 8x over. The $1.09B in FCF against $2.39B in total debt yields a manageable ~2.2x leverage ratio on a net debt basis (accounting for $1.10B cash).

**Balance Sheet:** The 6.67x debt-to-equity and 1.79 Altman Z-Score are mathematical artifacts of the Capri deal aftermath, not indicators of genuine distress. Debt maturities are well-laddered ($17M FY2026, $397M FY2027-28, $750M FY2029-30, $1.25B FY2031+). The company has $2.0B in undrawn revolver capacity and a 1.87 current ratio. Retained earnings are deeply negative (-$2.56B) due to cumulative buybacks.

**Capital Returns:** The company is aggressively shrinking the share base — from 264M shares (FY2022) to ~217M (FY2025), an 18% reduction. FY2026 target is $1.5B in total shareholder returns ($1.2B buybacks + dividends), with $800M remaining under the 2022 authorization. At ~$30B market cap, the annual buyback rate represents roughly 4% of shares outstanding.

**Earnings Surprise Track Record:** 4 consecutive beats over the past four quarters, with the most recent (December 2025 quarter) beating by 19.8% ($2.69 actual vs. $2.25 estimate). The March 2025 quarter beat by 15.9%. This consistent upside delivery supports confidence in the $6.40-6.45 FY2026 guide.

## Growth Analysis

Coach's growth acceleration is the central thesis. The 25% constant-currency growth in FQ2 2026, combined with 3.7M new customer acquisitions (one-third Gen Z), suggests the brand is in the early-to-middle innings of a generational customer cohort shift. Marketing spend increased from $617M (9.2% of sales) to $745M (10.6% of sales) in FY2025, and the ROI appears strong given the revenue acceleration.

Estimate revisions support the growth story: FY2026 consensus EPS has risen 15.5% over the past 90 days (from $5.62 to $6.49), and FY2027 consensus has risen 15.8% (from $6.22 to $7.21). The 30-day revision trend is flat, suggesting the post-earnings upgrade cycle has stabilized. At $6.49 current-year and $7.21 next-year, the implied EPS growth rate is roughly 11% — a deceleration from the 25%+ FY2026E growth that may prove conservative if Coach sustains momentum.

However, three structural constraints limit the growth ceiling:

1. **Kate Spade is a drag.** Revenue declined from $1.42B to $1.20B over three years. Unless Kate Spade stabilizes (not yet evident), it subtracts ~1-2% from total company growth annually.

2. **Tariff margin headwind of ~230bps in FY2026.** Products are sourced primarily from Vietnam, Cambodia, the Philippines, India, and China — all tariff targets. Management is mitigating through pricing and product innovation, but the accessible luxury price point has limits on pass-through before demand destruction occurs.

3. **Consumer environment.** Record-low Michigan consumer sentiment (47.6) pre-dates the current macro deterioration. Housing sales down 3.6%, mortgage apps down 10.4%, diesel at records, and inflation reaccelerating all pressure discretionary spending. While Coach's price point may benefit from trade-down from true luxury, it is still discretionary spending on $200-$500 handbags.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$150, TPR trades at approximately:
- **23.1x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS** ($6.49 consensus)
- **20.8x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS** ($7.21 consensus)
- **62.3x trailing GAAP EPS** ($0.82 per share, distorted by one-time items — not analytically useful)
- **~1.06% dividend yield**

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 24.4x (Ralph Lauren), with Lululemon at 13.2x and Nike at 37.9x. TPR's ~23x FY2026E multiple is essentially in line with the peer median on trailing, but the forward growth profile (11% EPS growth to FY2027) is stronger than RL's and comparable to NKE's. The premium over Lululemon (13.2x) is notable given LULU's revenue scale and margin profile — LULU at -54% from its 52-week high represents a very different risk/reward setup.

**On a normalized FCF basis:** $1.09B FCF against ~$30B market cap = ~3.6% FCF yield. This is adequate but not compelling for a consumer discretionary company facing tariff headwinds and consumer deterioration.

The stock's 137% move from April 2025 lows has priced in a substantial amount of the Coach recovery and Capri deal termination benefit. The February 5 earnings gap from $130 to $143 (and subsequent move to $162) reflects the market's recognition of accelerating growth, but the stock has pulled back ~7% from the $162 high, suggesting some initial profit-taking.

The analyst consensus target of $160 (median $165, mean $160) implies only 6-10% upside from current levels. The wide target range ($70-$190) reflects genuine uncertainty about tariff impact and consumer durability.

## Competitive Landscape

Coach occupies a strong position in accessible luxury, a segment that is currently outperforming true luxury (LVMH, Kering have struggled with macro headwinds). The 86% DTC model provides margin advantages and direct customer data relative to wholesale-dependent competitors.

Key competitive advantages:
- **Brand momentum with Gen Z**: The pace of new customer acquisition is difficult for competitors to replicate quickly.
- **Pricing power**: Gross margin expansion from 69% to 75.4% over four years demonstrates the ability to reduce promotions and elevate pricing without losing volume.
- **Scale in DTC**: 1,371 stores globally plus robust e-commerce provides distribution advantages.

Key competitive vulnerabilities:
- **Single-brand concentration**: Coach is ~80% of revenue and an even larger share of profit. Tapestry is effectively a single-brand company with a declining second brand.
- **Category headwinds at Kate Spade's tier**: Michael Kors (under Capri Holdings) and Tory Burch have also struggled, suggesting category-specific headwinds in addition to brand-specific issues.
- **Tariff exposure**: Heavy sourcing from Southeast Asia creates a cost disadvantage vs. competitors with more diversified or European-based supply chains.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure (High):** The most material near-term risk. With current tariff policy targeting Vietnam, Cambodia, India, and China — where the vast majority of TPR's manufacturing occurs — the company faces meaningful cost inflation. Inventory was pulled forward in FY2025 to buffer, but this is a temporary measure. Price increases at the $200-$500 handbag price point risk demand destruction.

**Kate Spade Structural Decline (Medium-High):** Shrinking store base (397→360), and management warns further impairments are possible. Each year of continued decline compresses consolidated margins and forces Coach to grow even faster to offset.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (High):** Record-low consumer sentiment, rising energy costs, and housing weakness create a hostile environment for discretionary spending. The accessible luxury segment has some trade-down benefit from true luxury, but is not recession-resistant.

**Greater China Exposure (Medium):** ~40% international revenue with meaningful China concentration. U.S.-China tensions, retaliatory consumer sentiment against American brands, and currency fluctuations create ongoing risk. The 10-K specifically identifies "negative perception of or sentiments towards the U.S." as a risk factor.

**Leverage (Medium):** While manageable (2.0x net debt/OCF), the compressed equity base leaves limited margin for error if cash flows compress. The credit facility contains a 4.0x maximum net leverage covenant.

**Tax Rate Risk (Low-Medium):** QDMTT expected to increase the effective tax rate above the current favorable ~17.8% non-GAAP rate in FY2026 and beyond.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 58.4% is substantially above the 39.2% 1-year historical volatility — a 19.2pp spread indicating the market expects significantly more volatility than the stock has recently exhibited. The term structure is in backwardation (58.4% near-term vs. 41.7% at 6 months), a pattern typically associated with imminent event risk or stress pricing. Put/call skew of 76.0% reflects elevated put demand, suggesting institutional hedging activity despite the stock's strong recent performance. The OI put/call ratio of 0.35 indicates more call open interest overall, but the elevated near-term skew and backwardated term structure warrant caution. Short interest at 6.8% of float with 5.9 days to cover is moderate — not extreme enough for squeeze risk but indicates meaningful bearish positioning.

The divergence between the stock's strong 12-month performance and the options market's elevated put demand warrants attention.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$180-190)
- Coach sustains 15-20% growth through FY2027 as Gen Z adoption accelerates and international expansion (Southeast Asia, Europe) gains traction
- Tariff mitigation proves more effective than expected through pricing power and supply chain diversification
- Kate Spade stabilizes (breakeven or modest growth) under new strategic direction
- FY2027 EPS reaches $7.50-8.00, and the stock re-rates to 24-25x = $180-200
- Continued aggressive buybacks (4%+ annual share reduction) provide mechanical EPS support
- September 2025 investor day catalyzes long-term growth framework re-rating

### Bear Case (~$100-110)
- Consumer spending deteriorates further, compressing Coach's growth to mid-single-digits
- Tariff headwinds exceed 230bps as retaliatory tariffs escalate and mitigation disappoints
- Kate Spade requires additional impairment and/or divestiture at distressed valuation
- Greater China revenue declines on geopolitical sentiment
- FY2027 EPS comes in at $5.50-6.00 on margin compression, and the stock de-rates to 18-20x = $99-120
- Consumer discretionary sector rotation as investors flee to defensives in stagflationary environment

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $120-130 range (~18-20x FY2026E EPS), providing adequate margin of safety against tariff/consumer risks
- Kate Spade shows sequential revenue stabilization for two consecutive quarters
- Tariff regime eases materially (e.g., Vietnam tariff reduction or exemption)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Coach constant-currency growth decelerates below 10% for two consecutive quarters
- FY2027 consensus EPS revisions turn negative (currently $7.21)
- Management cuts FY2026 guidance below $6.00 EPS
- Credit facility leverage covenant approaches 3.5x

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late April/early May 2026 for FQ3 2026), which will provide the first full quarter of tariff impact visibility and updated FY2026 guidance.

## Conclusion

Coach's brand transformation is well-documented: 25% growth, 75.4% gross margins, Gen Z customer acquisition at scale. Management has executed well under Crevoiserat, and the aggressive capital return program provides EPS support. The business quality deserves a premium multiple.

However, at ~$150 the stock has already priced in substantial recovery, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. The 137% rally leaves limited upside to the $160-165 consensus target. Tariff headwinds are quantified at 230bps of margin impact but could prove worse. The consumer macro environment is the weakest in decades. Kate Spade continues to erode. And the options market is pricing meaningfully more risk than the equity chart suggests.

The right framework is to own this stock at a lower price. At $120-130 (18-20x FY2026E), the margin of safety would be adequate for the known risks. At $150, patience is warranted. HOLD with a positive growth thesis — the underlying business is improving, but the entry point has passed for now.
