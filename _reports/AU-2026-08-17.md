## Executive Summary

AngloGold Ashanti is producing the best financial results in its history, and almost none of it is operational. H1 2026 gold income rose 43% year-on-year to $6,188m while ounces sold fell 4.3% to 1,472koz; the Group's average realized price rose 51% to $4,650/oz. Gross margin expanded from 47.0% to 58.9%, free cash flow (company-defined) nearly doubled to $1,895m, the balance sheet swung from $311m of net debt a year ago to $991m of net cash, dividends declared in the half roughly doubled to $949m (188 US cents per share), and shareholders approved a $2.0bn buyback on 23 July, roughly 3.9% of the 505.7m shares outstanding at the current price. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance and expects production weighted "significantly toward H2" at lower unit costs.

The problem is what happens beneath the year-on-year headlines. Deriving Q1 from the disclosed H1 and Q2 figures, the realized price fell about 8.3% sequentially (roughly $4,852/oz to $4,448/oz), quarterly free cash flow fell 37.8% to $727m, and EBITDA fell 13.8% to $1,974m. Group all-in sustaining cost reached $2,039/oz in Q2, up 22% year-on-year, of which management attributes $216 of the $254/oz cash-cost increase to macro factors, including $67/oz of gold-price-linked royalties, an automatic escalator that mechanically caps operating leverage on the way up. Q2 EPS of $1.98 missed the $2.25 consensus by 11.9%, and consensus has cut FY2026 EPS by 14.1% and FY2027 by 8.8% over ninety days. The stock has meanwhile risen 28.6% from its 20 July close of $74.88. Rising price against falling estimates is the less favorable of the two possible divergences.

At $96.30 the shares trade at roughly 11.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $8.62 and 9.7x FY2027 $9.95, versus about 5.6x enterprise value to annualized H1 EBITDA. Those multiples are only cheap if a $4,400-4,650/oz realized price is a base rather than a peak, and they are flattered by a structural feature the screens miss: about 37% of group profit ($1,326m of $3,609m in H1) accrues to non-controlling interests, so group EBITDA and FCF overstate the economics available to the listed equity. Add an unresolved internal-control material-weakness question in the 20-F risk language, production concentrated in Ghana, Tanzania, Guinea, Egypt, the DRC and Brazil, and a flagship Nevada project whose $3.6bn of capital generates only ~$1.7bn of after-tax NPV at the company's own $2,715/oz deck, and the discount to North American peers looks earned. HOLD: own it for gold exposure and capital returns, but the current price offers no margin of safety against a gold reversion.

## Company Overview

AngloGold Ashanti plc is a large-cap primary gold producer (silver by-product), incorporated in England and Wales with primary listing on the NYSE and secondary listings on the JSE, A2X and the Ghana Stock Exchange, and a stated global headquarters in Greenwood Village, Colorado. The current structure dates to the 2023 restructuring that moved the domicile to the UK and the primary listing to New York. It reports under IFRS, audited by PricewaterhouseCoopers, and as a foreign private issuer files Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, with no Section 16 insider filings.

The portfolio splits into consolidated "managed operations" (Obuasi and Iduapriem in Ghana, Geita in Tanzania, Siguiri in Guinea, Sukari in Egypt, Cuiabá in Brazil, plus Australian operations evidenced through AUD cost exposure) and one equity-accounted non-managed joint venture, Kibali in the DRC, reported on an attributable basis. Serra Grande (Brazil) was divested on 1 December 2025 and contributed 26koz in H1 2025, accounting for part of the year-on-year volume decline. Growth is concentrated in two southern Nevada projects, Arthur and North Bullfrog, with S-K 1300 technical report summaries filed for Arthur, Geita, Sukari and Kibali in March 2026.

Management's stated priorities are predictable operations, competitive shareholder returns, building the Nevada production centre, the continued Obuasi ramp-up, and organic projects in Tanzania, Guinea, Egypt and Brazil. A newly disclosed brownfield pipeline of mining, processing and recovery improvements at Obuasi, Geita, Sukari, Siguiri and Cuiabá is positioned to lift production from 2029 onwards using existing infrastructure. That is a capital-efficiency argument rather than a cost-curve or orebody-quality argument; at $2,039/oz AISC, AngloGold is not a low-cost operator.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** H1 2026 gold income of $6,188m (H1 2025: $4,334m) against cost of sales of $2,944m produced gross profit of $3,646m, a 58.9% gross margin versus 47.0% a year earlier (derived). EBITDA of $4,265m and profit for the period of $3,609m compare with $2,349m and $1,775m. Attributable profit was $2,283m, or 448 US cents per share, versus 219 cents.

One measurement caution matters for peer work: gold income divided by *managed* ounces sold reconciles precisely to the disclosed managed realized price (H1: $6,188m / 1,332koz = $4,646/oz vs disclosed $4,647/oz), confirming that gold income excludes Kibali revenue while EBITDA includes Kibali's attributable earnings. The apparent 68.9% EBITDA-to-revenue margin therefore overstates the consolidated business and should not be used against peers.

**Costs.** Group total cash costs rose $254/oz year-on-year in Q2 to $1,480/oz, with management attributing $216/oz to macro factors: general inflation (labour and contractors) +$71/oz, gold-price-linked royalties +$67/oz, fuel +$43/oz on a 45% rise in average Brent, and FX +$35/oz (AUD +10%, BRL +11%, GHS +9%). Only $29/oz was operational. For the half, the Full Asset Potential programme cut controllable costs by $20/oz against royalties +$93/oz, inflation +$60/oz, FX +$50/oz and oil +$20/oz. Self-help is real but roughly one-tenth the scale of external pressure. Group AISC of $2,039/oz was up $373/oz, driven by fixed-cost spreading over lower sales and sustaining capex of $332m (from $273m); non-sustaining capex doubled to $217m.

**Cash flow.** Net cash from operating activities was $3,141m in H1 (from $1,743m), with cash taxes more than doubling to $542m in Q2 and guided at $230-250m per remaining quarter. Two cautions. First, the "$1.8bn cash generated from operations" headline is pre-tax and pre-interest and exceeds the $1,432m of net operating cash inflow by about 26%. Second, reported FCF of $1,895m cannot be reproduced: operating cash flow less Group capex of $1,016m is $2,125m, a $230m gap, and Group capex includes $101m of JV capex that never passes through consolidated cash flow. Reported FCF is a company-defined measure.

Annualization is inappropriate here: production is guided H2-weighted, cash taxes are lumpy, and the realized price swung 8% between quarters. As sensitivities only: H2 equal to H1 implies FY2026 FCF of ~$3.79bn, a 7.4% yield on a ~$51.4bn market cap; H2 at twice the Q2 run rate implies ~$3.35bn, or 6.5%.

**Balance sheet.** Net cash of $991m at 30 June against total borrowings of $1,778m (from $2,297m). On 16 April the company completed capped cash tender offers retiring $665.1m principal for exactly $650.0m; the aggregate cap, not investor appetite, was binding, and $78.9m of 6.500% 2040 notes were tendered and none accepted. Note the mechanics: net cash rose only $123m sequentially from $868m despite $727m of Q2 FCF, because a near-par repurchase is approximately net-debt-neutral and dividends plus $234m of distributions to non-controlling interests consumed the balance. The release's phrasing ("net cash of $991m after debt buyback") is accurate but should not be read as the buyback having improved net cash.

**The minority leakage.** This is the single most important adjustment. Profit for the period of $3,609m against attributable profit of $2,283m implies $1,326m, 36.7%, to non-controlling interests, essentially unchanged from 37.4% a year earlier but doubled in dollars. Cash distributions to NCI were $396m in the half, so the accrued claim is building well ahead of cash paid. Any EV/EBITDA screen that does not adjust for this flatters AngloGold against peers with cleaner ownership. Applying a 37% haircut to annualized H1 EBITDA of $8.53bn implies roughly $5.4bn of attributable EBITDA, or about 8.9x EV rather than 5.6x.

**Share count and returns.** Ordinary shares rose 168,427 (0.03%) between the March and June record dates, negligible issuance. Q2 interim dividend of 72 US cents (ex-date 21 August, payable 4 September) annualizes to roughly 3.0% at $96.30, consistent with the 3.03% quoted yield; the H1 declared total of 188 cents annualizes higher but includes the peak-price Q1. The $2.0bn buyback equals about 19.8m shares, or 3.9% of shares outstanding, and is accretive if executed, but it carries no disclosed timetable.

*Data note:* the vendor profile shows trailing EPS of $5.18 with both trailing and forward P/E at 12.71, which does not reconcile with the current price on either basis. I have used the disclosed H1 EPS of $4.48 and the consensus FY2026/FY2027 figures of $8.62/$9.95 for all multiples.

## Growth Analysis

Volumes are contracting. Group production fell to 1,468koz in H1 2026 from 1,524koz, and managed production to 1,334koz from 1,386koz. Serra Grande's divestiture explains part of that, the 24 April contractor fatality at Obuasi and associated suspension another part, and planned maintenance the rest. Management expects H2 recovery at lower unit costs, which would be a timing reversal rather than growth.

Consensus is moving the wrong way. FY2026 EPS has been cut from $10.04 to $8.62 over ninety days (-14.1%) and FY2027 from $10.92 to $9.95 (-8.8%), with -8.5% and -6.3% of that coming in the last thirty days, i.e., after the Q2 miss. The estimate-revision signal that reliably precedes outperformance requires positive revisions; here the direction is negative, and it is negative while the share price rallies. That combination constrains how far I am willing to lean on either the cheap-multiple argument or the consensus target.

Medium-term growth rests on Arthur and the brownfield pipeline. Arthur's maiden Probable reserve is 4.9Moz at 1.75 g/t, supporting a ~9-year initial life at ~500koz per year and LOM AISC of $954/oz, less than half current group AISC, and ~17% of current production. But project capital is ~$3.6bn against after-tax NPV(5%) of ~$1.7bn at $2,715/oz and ~$3.4bn at $3,500/oz: NPV/capex of 0.47x and 0.94x respectively. On the company's own conservative deck the project consumes more capital than it creates value, which makes it a leveraged call on gold staying far above long-run consensus decks, competing for capital with a $2bn buyback. PFS-stage economics typically improve at feasibility, the reserve is Probable-only, and the life is explicitly "initial," but the disclosed numbers are what exist today. The brownfield pipeline contributes from 2029 at the earliest and is unquantified.

Growth prospects score 4.0: declining volumes, falling estimates, and a capital-hungry pipeline whose base-deck returns are thin.

At $96.30 with 505.7m shares, market capitalization is roughly $48.7bn (the vendor's 510.4m share count would give $49.2bn); less $991m net cash gives EV of about $47.7bn.

The optical multiple is low, and a low multiple on a gold miner at a record gold price is usually the market correctly refusing to capitalize peak earnings at a full multiple. Two adjustments make the discount look reasonable rather than anomalous: the 37% NCI leakage moves EV/EBITDA from 5.6x to roughly 8.9x on an attributable basis, and the FY2027 EPS denominator is being revised down, not up.

There is a genuine re-rating mechanism, and management is running it deliberately. The buyback was justified as "aligning AngloGold Ashanti's capital allocation with its North American peer group"; the company restated its non-GAAP presentation to unadjusted EBITDA and net debt(cash) "to enhance comparability with industry peers"; the NYSE primary listing and Nevada growth pivot point the same direction. Against that, the company is structurally invisible to conventional US screens: the ticker maps to the defunct South African CIK, and XBRL/10-K-keyed tools return nulls, which is a plausible contributor to persistent neglect but tells you nothing about intrinsic value.

Consensus targets (mean $113.13, median $114.50, high $134.00, low $76.00) imply 17-19% upside at the mean. I do not treat those as a floor: analyst targets lag reality when estimates are *rising*, and here estimates have fallen 14% in ninety days while targets have not visibly followed, which makes the mean stale on the high side. The distribution of ratings (3 strong buy, 4 buy, 1 hold, with a sell upgraded to hold over the last two months) is mildly bullish.

Valuation score 5.0: the multiple is low for identifiable and largely justified reasons, with a real but slow re-rating catalyst offsetting peak-earnings risk. The assets are real and the balance sheet is net cash, so this is not a structural value trap, but neither is it an identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Gold offers no product differentiation; the only durable competitive variables are position on the cost curve, orebody quality, and jurisdiction. AngloGold is middling-to-weak on the first and weak on the third, and both are improving only slowly.

Cost position: $2,039/oz Group AISC in Q2, up 22%. Management's own decomposition attributes most of the increase to sector-wide macro factors, which implies peers are absorbing similar inflation and that AngloGold's cost trajectory is not company-specific underperformance. It also implies the entire sector's margins are more price-dependent and less self-help-driven than headline earnings growth suggests.

Jurisdiction: production is concentrated in Ghana, Tanzania, Guinea, Egypt, the DRC (via a JV it does not manage) and Brazil, plus Australia. The direction of travel is toward Tier-1, with Serra Grande divested and both greenfield projects in southern Nevada, but a changing capex mix is not reduced jurisdictional risk today. Nevada contributes zero ounces until Arthur is built.

Operational reliability: Obuasi is simultaneously described as a strategic ramp-up priority, a brownfield opportunity, and the source of the Q2 production shortfall following a contractor fatality. A mine still ramping years after restart, which also produced a fatality and a volume miss, reads as persistent execution risk.

Competitive position score 4.5: stable-to-slightly-improving jurisdictional mix, no evidence of share gain or moat widening, upper-half cost position, declining output.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control over financial reporting — the highest-priority unresolved item.** The 31 July release's risk language contemplates "the inability to remediate one or more material weaknesses, or the discovery of additional material weaknesses," and "material weakness" appears in the FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 20-Fs. The drafting reads as presupposing existing weaknesses, but the filing text was not readable and it may be generic risk-factor language. This must not be treated as established; it is the kind of question that should be resolved before adding capital at a $51bn issuer.

**Cost ratchet with an embedded price link.** At Q2's realized $4,446/oz against $2,039/oz AISC, the margin is roughly $2,407/oz. A reversion toward $3,000/oz would compress that by about 60%. Royalties (+$67/oz in Q2) self-correct on the way down; labour, contractor and FX inflation would not.

**Minority participation.** ~37% of group profit does not belong to AU shareholders, with the accrued claim ($1,326m in H1) running well ahead of cash distributed ($396m). The identity and contractual terms of the partners are unverified inference.

**Jurisdictional and geopolitical exposure.** Resource nationalism, royalty and tax revision, and permitting risk across six emerging-market jurisdictions. The company explicitly cites the "ongoing US-Iran conflict" as the reason it prioritised fuel supply security alongside a 45% rise in average Brent, a named macro-political exposure sitting directly in the cost line.

**Arthur capital intensity.** $3.6bn against $1.7bn of NPV at the base deck, competing with a $2bn buyback.

**Reduced-transparency regime.** Semi-annual US reporting, quarterly results voluntary via 6-K, no 8-K real-time disclosure, no Section 16 insider record, and three coexisting earnings definitions (IFRS profit, JSE headline earnings, non-GAAP EBITDA). Any consensus comparison must be basis-matched; the Q2 "miss" against a $2.25 estimate should be read with that caveat.

**Governance.** One-third of votes (33.92% against, on ~80.6% turnout) opposed the off-market buyback authority on 23 July. The most likely explanation is objection to the off-market mechanic on equal-treatment grounds rather than to the return of capital itself, but the cause is unverified and the dissent level is materially above typical.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($135, +40.2%).** Gold sustains $4,500/oz or better into 2027 on the reserve-diversification bid and a widening Fed-independence premium. H2 volumes recover as guided with unit costs trending lower, restoring the Q1 earnings cadence; FY2027 attributable EPS reaches $10.50-11.00. The $2bn buyback executes, retiring ~4% of shares, and the disclosure/listing campaign narrows the multiple gap to North American peers toward 13x. Net cash builds toward $2bn+, funding Arthur without equity. At 13x $10.50, $135.

**Bear case ($62, -35.6%).** Gold reverts toward $3,200/oz as real yields hold high and the parabolic 2025-26 move unwinds. AISC proves sticky near $2,000/oz because labour, contractor and FX inflation do not reverse with the price; the royalty escalator gives back only part of its $93/oz. Attributable EPS falls toward $5.00. A jurisdictional event (royalty revision, ownership dispute, or a further Obuasi interruption) or confirmation of an unremediated control weakness compounds the derating. At ~12x $5.00, $62, versus a 52-week low of $52.05 and a low street target of $76.

**Base case ($105, +9.0%).** Gold holds in a wide band around current levels with no sustained new high; H2 delivers the guided volume recovery but the realized price stays below Q1's peak. FY2026 lands near consensus $8.62 and FY2027 settles at $9.00-9.50 after further modest trimming. Applying 11.5x to a normalized ~$9.10 gives $105. Adding the ~3.0% dividend, total return to base is about 12%.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected value of about $99.60, or +3.4% in price plus ~3% of dividend, roughly a fair, unexceptional return with a 36% left tail. That is a HOLD, not an ACCUMULATE. The specific things keeping it out of ACCUMULATE territory are the negative revision direction, the absence of any volume growth to offset price risk, the unresolved control question, and the fact that the 37% minority leakage means the headline cheapness overstates what accrues to this equity.

## Conclusion

AngloGold Ashanti is a well-financed, cash-generative gold producer in the middle of an exceptional price environment, run by a management team that has done sensible things with the windfall: cut gross debt and the 2028/2030 maturity towers, doubled the dividend, authorized a ~4% buyback, divested a non-core Brazilian mine, and rebuilt its disclosure to be screened against Newmont, Agnico and Barrick rather than as an emerging-market miner. Net cash of $991m and roughly $3.4-3.8bn of plausible FY2026 free cash flow give it capacity to fund Nevada and return capital simultaneously.

What the current price does not offer is protection. The 43% revenue increase came entirely from a 51% price increase against 4.3% lower volumes; AISC is up 22% with two-thirds to three-quarters of the increase in cost items that will not fall as fast as the gold price; roughly 37% of group profit belongs to somebody else; consensus EPS is being cut at a double-digit pace while the shares rally 29% off their July low; and the flagship growth project destroys value at the company's own conservative price deck. The 11x multiple is the market pricing peak earnings correctly, not overlooking them.

HOLD. Existing holders have a legitimate reason to keep the exposure: gold's macro bid is intact, the balance sheet is clean, and the return profile is being paid in cash. Prospective buyers should wait for either the control-environment question to close, the estimate revisions to stop falling, or a price closer to $75-80.
