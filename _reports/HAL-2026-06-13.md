**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Halliburton is one of the three dominant global oilfield services (OFS) providers and the North American leader in completions and hydraulic fracturing. The stock has appreciated roughly 80% over the trailing twelve months, from ~$22 to ~$39.60, recovering from a genuine down-cycle in 2025 in which operating income fell 41% and ROCE — management's own primary incentive metric — dropped to a five-year low of 8.9%. The recovery in the share price has been driven by a combination of estimate revisions (current-year EPS estimates up 6.5% and next-year up 8.7% over 90 days), a Q1 2026 earnings beat, and growing investor interest in the company's natural-gas-for-data-center optionality through its VoltaGrid investment.

The investment debate is binary on two unresolved questions. First, does North America services demand recover in 2026 as management forecasts (depleted DUC inventory, e-frac/automation pull-through), or does a soft, oversupplied oil market keep U.S. land activity weak? Second, is the Middle East/Asia revenue decline (-13% YoY in Q1 2026, driven by active regional conflict causing force majeure and work cancellations) transient or structural? A prolonged conflict elevates near-term oil prices but suppresses regional service work, while a de-escalation would relieve the Middle East disruption but may temper near-term oil prices. Neither outcome cleanly favors the stock.

At ~17x forward 2026 consensus EPS of $2.37 and ~13.5x 2027 EPS of $2.92, Halliburton trades at a premium to its diversified-energy peer median (~15x) but in line with OFS-specific norms, with a 1.7% dividend yield and a ≥50%-of-FCF capital-return framework. Estimates are rising and the franchise quality is real, but the stock has already retraced ~9% from its $43.59 high without a fundamental break, and the cycle remains the binding constraint. We rate HAL a HOLD. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and a higher-conviction call requires confirmation that the North America recovery is materializing and that the Middle East drag is fading.

## Company Overview

Halliburton operates in more than 70 countries with roughly 46,000 employees across two reporting segments:

- **Completion & Production (C&P)** — ~58% of revenue ($12.8B in 2025). Hydraulic fracturing/stimulation, cementing, completion tools, artificial lift, well intervention, and specialty chemicals. This is the more North America-levered and cyclical segment, where Halliburton holds the global #1 position in pressure pumping. FY2025 operating margin ~17%.
- **Drilling & Evaluation (D&E)** — ~42% of revenue ($9.4B in 2025). Directional drilling (Sperry), drill bits, drilling fluids (Baroid), wireline, testing/subsea, and Landmark digital software. More international and technology-weighted, with FY2025 operating margin ~15%.

Revenue is the second derivative of oil prices: it tracks upstream E&P capital spending and rig/completion activity rather than the spot commodity directly. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue; the U.S. is 37-39% of total revenue. Halliburton's differentiation is concentrated NAM completions leadership plus a technology lead in electric fracturing (the Zeus platform, with ~50% of its NAM frac fleet now transitioned to e-pumps), iCruise rotary steerable, and LOGIX automation.

## Financial Analysis

**2025 was a clear down-cycle year.** Revenue fell 3% to $22.18B from $22.94B, but the operating story was sharper. Operating income fell 41% to $2.26B from $3.82B, and net income roughly halved to $1.28B from $2.50B. The decline was driven by:

1. **$831M in impairments and other charges** (vs. $116M in 2024) — severance, assets held for sale, facility closures, environmental remediation;
2. Underlying margin compression in both segments (C&P operating income -21%, D&E -14%);
3. ~$89M of tariff costs; and
4. A $125M deferred-tax valuation allowance from the OBBBA's foreign-tax-credit changes.

Excluding impairments, "total operations" operating income still fell 19% ($4.32B → $3.51B), so the deterioration is operational, not purely one-time charges. North America revenue fell 6% as U.S. land rig count dropped (546 average vs. 580 in 2024 and 669 in 2023). Management chose to stack uneconomic frac fleets rather than chase low-margin work, a discipline signal that came at a near-term revenue cost.

**Q1 2026 showed early stabilization.** Revenue was $5.40B, essentially flat YoY, with reported operating income of $679M vs. $431M (the prior year carried a $356M impairment, so the underlying operating comparison is down ~11% to $790M before charges). The defining dynamic was a sharp geographic rotation: Latin America +22%, Europe/Africa/CIS +11%, while Middle East/Asia fell -13% on active regional conflict (force majeure, work cancellations, ~$0.02-0.03 EPS hit) and North America declined -4%. Reported EPS was $0.55 vs. $0.24, flattered by an effective tax rate that fell to 18.5% from 33.7% on a valuation-allowance release.

**Cash flow and balance sheet remain solid.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2.9B against $1.25B of capex (~6% of revenue), with 2026 capex guided down to ~$1.1B. The Altman Z-Score of 2.48 sits in the grey zone, typical for an asset-heavy cyclical rather than a distress signal. Debt/equity is ~1.39, long-term debt is stable at $7.1B, the current ratio is ~2.0, and credit ratings are solid investment grade (S&P BBB+, Moody's A3). There are no financial covenants or MAC clauses, a $3.5B undrawn revolver runs to 2030, and near-term maturities are minimal (~$90M due Feb 2027). The company returned $1.6B to shareholders in 2025 ($579M dividends + ~$1.0B buybacks, 42.4M shares repurchased), under a framework targeting ≥50% of FCF. The earnings track record is constructive: three beats and one minor miss over four quarters, with surprises of +9.2%, +25.2%, and +14.9% in the most recent three.

The one cautionary signal in capital returns is the downshift: Q1 2026 buybacks slowed to $100M from $252M a year earlier, and capex was cut. This is prudent cash conservation in a soft cycle, but it removes near-term buyback support for the shares.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth story rests on natural-gas demand from LNG export expansion, electrification, and data-center power. Henry Hub gas averaged $3.53 in 2025 (up from $2.19) and $4.79 in Q1 2026, and management cites gas-driven demand repeatedly.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive, which is the clearest forward signal available. Over 90 days, current-quarter estimates rose 2.0%, next-quarter 6.9%, current-year 6.5% (to $2.37), and next-year 8.7% (to $2.92). The acceleration is concentrated in the out-quarters, consistent with a market that expects the cycle to firm into 2026-2027. This is genuine positive earnings momentum, but it does not reach the +30% revision velocity that would mark a true earnings inflection, and the absolute growth rates (current-year EPS still below the 2024 base) reflect recovery off a trough rather than a structural step-change.

The 2026 setup is mixed. Management guides international revenue up mid-to-high single digits (led by Latin America) but North America down YoY, with hope for a NAM services recovery later in the year supported by depleted DUC inventory and rising demand for e-frac and automation. Worldwide rig count fell to 1,816 in 2025 from 1,948 in 2024, with U.S. land especially weak, and international also softened (1,080 vs. 1,162). The NAM recovery is a forecast, not a confirmed trend.

The **VoltaGrid investment is the most strategically significant new development**, but it requires careful attribution. On May 11, 2026, Blackstone (Tactical Opportunities) and Halliburton agreed to invest a combined $1B in equity into VoltaGrid, a behind-the-meter natural-gas microgrid/power company that supplies data centers, with VoltaGrid also acquiring Propell; both deals are expected to close mid-2026. This builds on an October 2025 collaboration under which Halliburton secured manufacturing capacity for 400 MW of modular gas power systems for 2028 delivery to Eastern Hemisphere data centers. The mechanism is credible: gas-fired distributed power is a genuine route to monetize the AI data-center buildout outside core OFS, but Halliburton is a minority equity investor and manufacturing/commercial partner, not the owner of the technology. The 400 MW capacity is for 2028 delivery, and the near-term P&L contribution is negligible. Investors should treat this as long-dated optionality, not capitalizable core earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39.60, Halliburton trades at ~17x forward 2026 consensus EPS of $2.37 and ~13.5x 2027 EPS of $2.92. Against the diversified-energy peer set, the median P/E is ~15x (EQT 9.7x, FTI 27.0x, TPL 50.7x, CTRA 15.0x, EXE 6.5x), but that group is heterogeneous and mostly E&P/royalty names rather than OFS comparables. Against the more relevant OFS peers (SLB, Baker Hughes), Halliburton's multiple is broadly in line, with a modest premium justified by its NAM completions leadership and e-frac technology edge.

The valuation case is two-sided. On the bull side, estimates are rising, the 2025 base is depressed (operating income was impairment-suppressed and ROCE hit a five-year low of 8.9%), and a mid-cycle normalization of earnings power toward the high-$2.00s to $3.00 EPS range would make the current price look reasonable to cheap. The earnings momentum supports the view that consensus is still catching up; analyst targets run from $31 low to $55 high, with a $45 median, implying ~14% upside to the median.

On the bear side, the stock has already retraced from $43.59 to $39.60 (~9% off the high) without a fundamental break, the multiple is not distressed, the dividend yield (1.7%) is below the peer median, and the buyback support has downshifted. The market is pricing a cyclical recovery that has not yet been confirmed in the activity data. A 17x forward multiple is not the kind of compressed entry point that creates asymmetric upside, and it is not stretched enough to short. This is fair value with a slight positive skew if the NAM recovery materializes.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5 — the rising estimates and depressed base argue against a low score, but the absence of a clear mispricing or asymmetric entry argues against a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

Halliburton is one of the "Big Three" OFS firms alongside SLB and Baker Hughes. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving on technology but is more exposed to the soft NAM cycle than SLB. The Zeus electric fracturing platform and LOGIX automation provide a real efficiency and service-intensity edge that partially offsets falling rig counts; Halliburton can grow revenue intensity per well even as well counts soften. Against SLB, Halliburton is more NAM-levered and less internationally and digitally diversified, which is a headwind in a year when international outpaces NAM.

The trajectory has softened from prior periods. Competitive position scored 7.5 earlier in the year and has been marked down toward 5.5-6.0 as the NAM weakness and the Middle East disruption surfaced. The franchise quality is intact, but two consecutive years of TSR lagging the peer group (163.76 vs. 181.72 in 2025) confirm that Halliburton has not been gaining relative share or value in the current cycle. The VoltaGrid optionality and e-frac lead support a stable-to-modestly-improving read, which we score at 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely matter for this company now:

1. **Commodity/cycle leverage into a soft NAM market.** This is the dominant risk. WTI averaged ~$65/bbl in 2025 (down from $77), and management expects supply to outpace demand in 2026 absent geopolitical disruption. NAM is the highest-margin, most cyclical exposure; stacking frac fleets reflects real pricing pressure.

2. **Middle East geopolitical disruption (newly acute, two-sided).** The Q1 force majeure and work cancellations occurred in Saudi Arabia and Qatar. A de-escalation would relieve this drag; a prolonged conflict raises oil but suppresses regional service work. The outcome is genuinely uncertain and does not cleanly favor the stock either way.

3. **The $640M IRS dispute.** The IRS's 2023 NOPA seeks to reclassify ~95% of the failed-2016 Baker Hughes break-up fee from an ordinary deduction to a capital loss. If Halliburton loses through appeals, ~$640M plus interest could be due. Not company-threatening, not currently reserved as payable, but a real multi-hundred-million-dollar contingent overhang.

4. **Emerging-market receivables concentration.** Mexico (Pemex) is ~7% of total receivables with ongoing payment delays, managed via credit default swaps ($374M notional at Q1 2026) tied to bank loans to the Mexican customer — an unusual structure. The $782-805M credit-loss allowance (~15% of gross receivables) is dominated by Venezuela exposure. Argentina currency controls add further EM risk.

5. **Tariffs/trade policy.** A 10% global tariff took effect Feb 24, 2026 with discussion of raising to 15%, and tariff legality is unresolved at the Supreme Court — input-cost and demand uncertainty.

6. **Management transition.** A wave of late-2025/early-2026 leadership changes (new COO Slocum, new Western/Eastern Hemisphere presidents, new CAO) appears to be orderly internal promotion rather than departures under duress, but introduces execution risk worth monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and offers no warning that contradicts a balanced view. Near-term ATM IV is 36.3% against 36.5% one-year historical vol, essentially fair, sitting at only the 10th percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew is balanced (0.2%), the term structure is in normal contango (near 36.3% < long 41.0%), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.81 is unremarkable. The headline ATM IV reading collapsed from 86.6% to 36.3% over a single session, which reflects the expiration of a near-term event-hedging spike rather than informed directional positioning. Short interest is low at 5.2% of float with 2.9 days to cover — no crowded short, but also no informed bearish consensus. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.7%. The market is pricing HAL as a fairly-valued, range-bound name, consistent with our balanced fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50-55)
North America services demand recovers in H2 2026 as DUC inventory depletes and development accelerates within budgets, pulling e-frac and automation revenue intensity higher. International strength sustains, the Middle East disruption proves transient on a de-escalation, and 2027 EPS reaches or exceeds $2.92 with margin recovery. ROCE rebuilds from the 8.9% trough toward the mid-teens. VoltaGrid begins to contribute visible optionality as the data-center gas-power theme matures into 2028 deliveries. At a 17-18x multiple on rising estimates, the stock reaches the $50-55 analyst-high range.

### Bear Case ($31-33)
A soft, oversupplied oil market keeps WTI in the low-$60s, NAM activity stays weak through 2026, and frac pricing pressure persists. Middle East conflict drags on, deepening the Middle East/Asia decline beyond the Q1 level. The IRS dispute and EM receivables (Pemex, Venezuela) crystallize incremental losses. Buybacks stay throttled, removing share-count support. Estimates roll over and the multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~15x on flat-to-lower earnings, taking the stock to the $31-33 analyst-low range, roughly -17% to -22% from current levels.

The probability-weighted outcome sits close to the current price. The bull case requires confirmation of a recovery that is currently a management forecast; the bear case requires the cycle to deteriorate further from an already-soft base. Neither is dominant.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis lacks sufficient evidence for a confident directional call. The franchise is high quality and estimates are rising, but the binding constraint is an energy-services cycle whose 2026 path is genuinely two-sided, with a Middle East disruption that could resolve either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of North America revenue growth turning positive YoY with stable or expanding C&P margins, confirming management's recovery thesis;
- Current-year and next-year EPS revisions accelerating beyond +30% over 90 days (true inflection velocity), combined with a forward P/E that remains below ~16x;
- A verified, durable de-escalation that reverses the Middle East/Asia decline while the company demonstrates the international book holding intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- WTI sustained below $60 with U.S. land rig count falling below ~500, signaling a deepening NAM down-cycle;
- A guidance cut or a quarter in which both C&P and D&E margins compress sequentially;
- Crystallization of the $640M IRS liability or a material EM receivables write-down (Pemex/Venezuela);
- Estimate revisions turning negative across the current-year and next-year horizons.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July), which will test the H2 NAM-recovery commentary and the persistence of the Middle East drag, alongside the mid-2026 VoltaGrid deal close.

## Conclusion

Halliburton is a well-managed, financially solid oilfield services leader trading at a fair multiple on rising 2026-2027 estimates after a genuinely weak 2025. The score trajectory has improved from the April low (4.5) as the panic over the down-cycle gave way to recognition of stabilization, the Q1 beat, and the VoltaGrid optionality, and we mark it modestly higher at 5.6 to reflect the positive revision momentum and intact balance sheet. The improvement does not reach the bar for a BUY. The ~9% retracement from the high is not a sign of thesis deterioration, since fundamentals are stable-to-improving, but neither does it create the asymmetric entry that would justify higher conviction, because the multiple is not distressed and the cycle remains unconfirmed.

The decisive variables are whether North America services demand recovers in H2 2026 and whether the Middle East disruption is transient. Both will become clearer over the next one to two quarters. Until they do, the risk/reward is balanced and the appropriate posture is to hold existing positions for the capital-return framework and recovery optionality without adding new capital. We rate HAL a HOLD.
