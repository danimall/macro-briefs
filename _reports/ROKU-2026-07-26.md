**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Roku has completed a transition from cash-burning growth story to profitable, cash-generative platform business. FY2025 delivered the first full-year GAAP profit ($88.4M net income vs. a $129.4M loss in FY2024) on revenue of $4.74B (+15%), and Q1 2026 accelerated the trend: revenue +22% to $1,248.9M, net income of $85.7M (vs. a $27.4M loss a year earlier), diluted EPS of $0.57, and adjusted EBITDA up 165% to $148.4M. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow reached $538.8M, up 80% year over year. The balance sheet carries $2.38B in cash and short-term investments with zero drawn debt, and management initiated a $400M buyback in August 2025, retiring $250M by Q1 2026. This is a de-risked business relative to the 2022–2023 loss years, and the improvement is cash-backed rather than accounting-driven.

The stock is up 57.7% over the trailing twelve months and re-rated sharply in mid-June 2026, spiking from ~$120 to an intraday high of $148.88 on 15M+ shares before settling near $142. That move appears tied to a June 15 material agreement (Item 1.01 8-K) whose contents I could not retrieve, plausibly a large advertising or DSP partnership, but unconfirmed. After the run, ROKU trades at roughly 58x current-year consensus EPS ($2.44) and ~38x next-year ($3.69), sits within the analyst target band (mean $158, median $160, range $126–$205), and shows a degrading recommendation mix (buy/strong-buy count fell from 25 to 10 over two months as holds rose from 3 to 15).

The core operational debate is the quality of ad growth: video ad impressions rose 59% in Q1 2026 while average price per impression fell 14%. Advertising gross margin nonetheless expanded to 61% (from 56%), so volume is currently outrunning price erosion. If CTV ad prices stabilize, operating leverage is substantial. If they keep deflating as SVOD ad-tiers flood the market with competing supply, the headline growth overstates underlying quality. Combined with weak governance (comp decoupled from any financial metric, five-year TSR of 33 versus a peer group at 247, uniformly plan-driven insider selling with no open-market buying) and a full multiple after the re-rating, the risk/reward at ~$142 is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate ROKU a HOLD with a positive growth bias.

## Company Overview

Roku is the leading TV streaming platform in the U.S., Canada, and Mexico by hours streamed, with 90+ million Streaming Households and 145.6 billion Streaming Hours in 2025 (up from 127.1B in 2024). The business runs on the Roku TV OS, a purpose-built operating system that anchors a razor/razorblade model: hardware is sold at or below cost to expand the installed base, which is then monetized through high-margin advertising and subscriptions.

Effective Q1 2026, Roku reorganized into three reportable segments (previously "Platform" and "Devices"):
- **Advertising** (~49% of revenue): digital/video ads, programmatic, and native placements across the Roku Experience (Home Screen, Roku City screensaver, The Roku Channel).
- **Subscriptions** (~42% of revenue): premium subscription revenue shares, Roku-billed subscriptions, owned-and-operated services (The Roku Channel, Frndly TV, Howdy), and remote-control app buttons.
- **Devices** (~9% of revenue): streaming players, Roku-made TVs, audio, and smart home, sold intentionally at/below cost.

The competitive asset is the Home Screen, prime real estate reached before the viewer selects an app, a monetization chokepoint individual streaming services cannot replicate. Scale, first-party viewing data, and an AI-powered content recommendation engine reinforce engagement. Device distribution is broad but concentrated: Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, and Target account for roughly 81–86% of Devices revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The profitability shift is the central fact. The following summarizes the trajectory from primary filings:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | ~$4.11B | $4.74B (+15%) | $1,020.7M | $1,248.9M (+22%) |
| Net income | –$129.4M | +$88.4M | –$27.4M | +$85.7M |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | ~$420.5M | $56.0M | $148.4M (+165%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | –$0.19 | +$0.57 |

Q1 2026 segment detail (all YoY):
- **Advertising +27%** to $612.7M, with impressions +59% but price per impression –14%. Segment gross margin expanded to 61% (from 56%); volume and improved measurement are outrunning price erosion.
- **Subscriptions +30%** to $518.5M (12% more subscribers plus 22% higher average price, boosted by Frndly TV and Howdy). Segment gross margin compressed to 41% (from 49%) as owned-and-operated content/licensing costs rose 50%.
- **Devices –16%** to $117.6M (volume –15%, ASP –3%), with a $19.1M gross loss, by design.

Cash generation and the balance sheet are the strongest part of the story. TTM FCF of $538.8M (up 80%) against a ~$18.4B market cap implies a ~2.9% FCF yield, modest for a stock at 58x earnings but real and growing. Roku holds $2.38B in cash and short-term investments with nothing drawn on its $300M revolver. The accumulated deficit of $1.50B is shrinking. Management flagged that the full U.S. deferred-tax valuation allowance may be released within 12 months given sustained profitability; that would produce a one-time non-cash tax benefit and distort a future quarter's reported net income, so investors should normalize for it.

Capital allocation has flipped. A formerly cash-burning company launched a $400M buyback in August 2025 and executed $250M by Q1 2026 (1.0M shares at an average $98.53 in Q1 2026), leaving $150M authorized through December 2026. Repurchasing at ~$98 that now trades at ~$142 was well-timed.

The earnings beat cadence supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, with surprises of 71%, 88%, 83%, and 145% over the last four quarters. That track record raises the odds that current consensus is conservative, though the magnitude of prior beats also means expectations have caught up.

## Growth Analysis

The secular tailwind is favorable and durable: the multi-year shift of viewing and ad budgets from linear to connected TV. Roku's streaming hours grew 15% (127.1B to 145.6B) and households continue to expand past 90M. Both platform engines are growing double digits, and the new three-segment disclosure gives investors clean visibility into advertising and subscriptions separately, a change that itself signals management confidence.

Estimate revisions are constructive but not strong enough to force a BUY. Current-year EPS was revised up 19.9% over 90 days ($2.03 to $2.44) and next-year up 14.6% ($3.22 to $3.69). The current-quarter estimate rose 41% over 90 days, but has gone flat over the trailing 30 days (–0.1%), and the next-quarter estimate has been cut 5.7% over 30 days and 7.8% over 90 days. Revenue growth is strong (+22% in Q1 2026), but the sub-30% pace combined with cooling near-term revisions means this does not cleanly clear the +30% estimate-revision / >30% revenue-growth threshold that flags an unambiguous inflection buy. The signal is positive; it is not extreme.

The single most important operational tension is ad monetization. A +59% impression / –14% price dynamic means the growth is currently a volume story. The bullish reading is that advertising gross margin expanded to 61%, so Roku is converting more inventory at improving unit economics even as headline price falls. The bearish reading is that SVOD ad-tiers (Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime) are flooding the CTV market with competing supply, and if price deflation continues, growth quality erodes. Subscriptions face the mirror-image problem: revenue is growing 30% but margin is compressing (49% to 41%) as Roku buys growth through owned-and-operated content licensing (+50% cost). Whether Frndly TV and Howdy become durable high-margin subscription franchises or a content-cost treadmill is unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$142, the forward-next-year multiple against ~15% revenue growth and a still-improving margin profile is defensible but not cheap; the PEG is roughly 2.5 on next-year earnings. This is a business at an early stage of monetizing operating leverage, so trailing/current-year P/E overstates richness somewhat, but even normalizing to the next-year figure, the stock is not offering an obvious discount.

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The listed Entertainment comparables (Liberty Media, SiriusXM, Liberty Live) are structurally different businesses; the peer median P/E of ~34.7 is not a clean benchmark for a CTV platform. The more relevant frame is Roku's own trajectory: the stock re-rated in the first half of June and now sits inside the analyst target band, with mean $158 and median $160 implying ~12% upside and the $126 low target below the current price. The recommendation mix has degraded, which argues that the sell-side is not treating current targets as stale floors to be revised up. The "analyst targets are floors" principle applies to cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks, and ROKU at 38–58x forward with cooling near-term revisions and a deteriorating rating mix does not fit that profile.

The historical caution that a compressed multiple is not the buy signal does not apply here; the multiple is not compressed at all. The valuation score reflects a fairly-priced stock after a strong run. Asymmetric upside would require confirmation that ad prices have stabilized and operating leverage is compounding, which is not yet evident in the data.

## Competitive Landscape

Roku's moat is the neutral aggregation layer plus the monetizable Home Screen, reinforced by 90M+ households, first-party data, and an AI recommendation engine. The trajectory is stable-to-improving at the platform level: advertising and subscriptions are both gaining scale and Roku maintains its lead in U.S. hours streamed. But the competitive vise is real and better-capitalized:

- **Walmart–Vizio** is the most strategically threatening development. Walmart is simultaneously one of Roku's largest device retail channels and now a vertically integrated OS competitor via Vizio SmartCast, creating channel-conflict risk unique to Roku.
- **Amazon (Fire TV) and Google (Android/Google TV)** subsidize hardware and bundle their own ad stacks and DSPs; they can undercut Roku on device economics indefinitely.
- **Ad-supported SVOD tiers** both compete for ad dollars and, in some cases, sell their own inventory on Roku's platform, a frenemy dynamic. Roku's explicit lack of access to YouTube video ad inventory underscores the limits of its ad supply, given YouTube is among the largest apps on the platform.
- **Content concentration** is a competitive dependency: the top three streaming services (excluding The Roku Channel) represented nearly half of all hours streamed in Q1 2026. Loss of a major app would materially hit engagement and device sales.

Roku is winning the volume battle (impressions +59%) but the price side (–14%) reflects industry-wide CTV competition. The June 15 material agreement, if it is a large ad-demand or DSP partnership, could directly address the supply/demand imbalance, but its contents are unconfirmed.

## Risk Assessment

The most material idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced:

1. **Ad-price deflation.** –14% price per impression is the key debate for the equity. Volume is offsetting it now, but continued deflation would degrade growth quality and pressure the multiple.
2. **Customer concentration.** "Customer J" represented 11% of total revenue in Q1 2026 (advertising-related); Customers J and B each represented 13% of net receivables. Loss or repricing of this relationship would be material.
3. **Content-partner concentration.** Top three apps = nearly half of streaming hours; loss of a Netflix/YouTube/Disney-scale partner would hit engagement.
4. **Subscriptions margin compression** (49% to 41%) driven by owned-and-operated content costs (+50%): the risk that O&O bets become a content-cost treadmill.
5. **Devices/tariff exposure.** All manufacturing is in China, SE Asia, Brazil, and Mexico; Q1 2026 inventory provisions jumped to $20.7M (from $6.8M) amid trade-policy uncertainty.
6. **Governance/control.** CEO Anthony Wood controls a majority of combined voting power while owning 11.5% of shares, with no "controlled company" exemption taken. Compensation uses no financial performance measures and grants time-vesting equity with no cash bonuses or performance-based equity, a stewardship weakness given the five-year TSR underperformance.
7. **Tax-benefit optics.** A likely U.S. valuation-allowance release within 12 months would inflate a future quarter's GAAP net income; investors should not extrapolate the reported figure.

Governance and control weigh here more than they might elsewhere because the compensation structure is decoupled from performance and the founder is entrenched.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 13.4% against a one-year historical vol of 46.7%; options are extremely cheap on a spread basis (–33.4pp), sitting at the 0th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (near-term 13.4% < long-term 17.4%), a complacent/normal structure, and the nearest-expiry implied move is only ±2.2%. ATM IV collapsed from 56.3% on June 3 to 13.4% by July 26 as the stock rose 16.8%: the mid-June re-rating catalyst has resolved and the market is not pricing an imminent surprise. Near-term put/call skew of 4.6% shows modestly elevated near-term put demand, but the six-month P/C open-interest ratio of 7.38 and –5.6% six-month skew suggest longer-dated positioning is more balanced-to-hedged than acutely bearish. Cheap near-term IV is a favorable entry consideration for anyone establishing or adjusting positions, but it also reflects a lack of near-term catalyst. Short interest is uncrowded at 7.2% of float and 1.1 days to cover, neither a squeeze setup nor evidence of an informed bear case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175–205).** Ad-price deflation stabilizes while impression volume keeps compounding, driving substantial operating leverage; advertising gross margin holds or expands past 61%; the June 15 agreement proves to be a meaningful demand-side partnership that closes Roku's ad-supply gap. Subscriptions margin bottoms as O&O content scales. TTM FCF crosses $700M, the buyback continues, and the multiple holds on ~$3.70+ EPS growing 15%+. The four-quarter beat streak continues and consensus re-rates upward. Target reaches the high end of the analyst band.

**Bear Case ($95–105).** CTV ad-price deflation accelerates as SVOD ad-tiers flood supply; impression growth decelerates and can no longer offset the price decline, stalling advertising revenue growth. Subscriptions margin keeps compressing on rising content costs. Walmart–Vizio and Amazon/Google intensify hardware subsidies and squeeze Roku's device channel. A large ad customer ("Customer J") reprices. The multiple compresses toward 25–30x on decelerating growth, and the stock retraces toward the low end of its 52-week range (~$78–95).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $155.** This applies roughly 42x to next-year EPS of $3.69, a modest premium to the current forward multiple, justified by the confirmed profitability shift, growing FCF, and buyback support, but capped by cooling near-term revisions and full valuation. That implies ~9% upside from ~$142.

**Bull case target: $190** (~34% upside), on ad-price stabilization plus continued operating leverage and multiple support.

**Bear case target: $100** (~30% downside), on accelerating ad-price deflation and margin compression.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings reports (Q2 2026 and Q3 2026) and disclosure of the June 15 material agreement.

**Upside triggers (to BUY):**
- Advertising price-per-impression decline moderates to single digits (better than –14%) while impressions continue to grow 40%+, confirming durable operating leverage.
- Confirmation that the June 15 agreement is a large ad-demand/DSP partnership materially expanding monetized inventory.
- Next-quarter and current-quarter estimate revisions turn positive again alongside a fifth consecutive beat, with the sell-side rating mix reversing back toward buy.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Advertising revenue growth decelerates below ~15% YoY with continued double-digit price-per-impression declines (volume no longer offsetting price).
- Subscriptions gross margin falls below ~38%, signaling the O&O content-cost treadmill.
- Loss or material repricing of "Customer J," or a major content partner (Netflix/YouTube/Disney) reducing platform presence.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026), and upon disclosure/clarification of the June 15 material agreement.

## Conclusion

Roku's transformation from cash-burning growth story to profitable, self-funding platform is real, cash-backed, and reflected in a de-risked balance sheet ($2.4B net cash, zero debt, $539M TTM FCF) and a well-timed buyback. The secular linear-to-streaming migration remains favorable, and the four-quarter beat streak lends confidence to forward estimates. These are the reasons to own the stock.

The setup at ~$142 is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock is up 57.7% over twelve months, re-rated in early June, trades at 38–58x forward earnings inside a consensus target band, and shows a degrading sell-side mix and cooling near-term estimate revisions. The ad business is a volume story currently masking a 14% price-per-impression decline, subscriptions margin is compressing, competition from Walmart–Vizio, Amazon, and Google is intensifying, and governance is a genuine weakness (comp decoupled from any financial metric, five-year TSR of 33 versus a peer group at 247, uniformly plan-driven insider selling with zero open-market buying at ~$140). We rate ROKU a **HOLD** with a positive growth bias. We would turn constructive on confirmation that ad-price deflation is moderating while volume compounds, the specific condition that would prove the operating-leverage thesis, or on a pullback that restores asymmetry to the entry.
