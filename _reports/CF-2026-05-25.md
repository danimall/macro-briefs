## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, currently experiencing an earnings surge driven by the Strait of Hormuz closure that removed 25-30% of globally traded ammonia from the market in early 2026. Q1 2026 EPS of $3.98 (vs. $1.85 prior year) and current-quarter consensus at $5.91 reflect this supply shock. Estimate revisions of +173.6% over 90 days for the current quarter are extraordinary, and the stock's 11x forward P/E on FY26 consensus of $18.07 appears cheap versus 23.8x peer median.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation is the binary geopolitical dependency. Physical LNG tankers are currently transiting the Strait of Hormuz for the first time since the conflict began, with a deal framework "largely negotiated" per Reuters. If this transit activity sustains and a peace deal materializes (estimated 25-30% probability within 60 days), CF's earnings power reverts sharply — FY26 EPS could compress from $18 toward $9-11, and FY27 consensus of $11.06 already reflects partial normalization. At $122 on normalized $9-10 EPS, CF trades at 12-13x — reasonable but not cheap enough to compensate for 30-50% downside risk in the de-escalation scenario. The investment case depends entirely on an event the investor cannot predict or control.

The EU fertilizer duty suspension announced in recent weeks adds a second headwind: political tolerance for fertilizer price inflation is eroding, creating regulatory overhang even if supply remains constrained.

## Company Overview

CF Industries operates nine nitrogen production complexes (six U.S., two Canada, one U.K.) with total ammonia nameplate capacity exceeding 10 million gross tons annually. The Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex is the world's largest ammonia production facility. Revenue splits across ammonia (31%), UAN (31%), granular urea (25%), ammonium nitrate (6%), and other products (7%).

The company is executing a strategic pivot toward clean energy, with its Donaldsonville CCS project operational since July 2025 and the $3.7B Blue Point JV with JERA/Mitsui (targeting 2029 production) positioning CF as a first-mover in low-carbon ammonia for power generation and industrial decarbonization.

## Competitive Landscape

CF Industries holds the strongest competitive position in global nitrogen fertilizers:

1. **Cost advantage:** North American natural gas (even at $4.90/MMBtu) remains below European and Asian alternatives ($8-15/MMBtu equivalent). CF is positioned on the left side of the global cost curve.
2. **Scale:** Largest single-operator ammonia network globally. The Donaldsonville complex alone produces more than most competitors' entire systems.
3. **Distribution infrastructure:** Owned terminals, pipelines, and river barge access create logistics barriers competitors cannot replicate.
4. **Clean energy first-mover:** No competitor has CF's combination of operational CCS + Blue Point JV + EU CBAM positioning. This creates structural premium pricing access.
5. **Geographic advantage:** 100% North American production means no exposure to Middle East, Russian, or Trinidadian supply disruption risks.

The competitive position is improving: every disruption to marginal producers (Middle East, Russia, Trinidad) disproportionately benefits the low-cost domestic incumbent. EU anti-dumping duties on Russian UAN through 2031 further protect CF's European export opportunity.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Binary (CRITICAL):** CF's current earnings power is 50-100% above "normal" due to Hormuz-related supply disruption. Physical tanker transits currently occurring suggest 25-30% probability of resolution within 60 days. A rapid de-escalation would compress earnings back toward $9-11 annually and send the stock toward $95-105. The risk/reward from $122 is approximately symmetric, which is the primary reason the stock cannot be recommended as a BUY.

**2. Natural Gas Cost Compression:** Henry Hub at $4.90/MMBtu vs. $2.25 in 2024. Each $1/MMBtu increase costs CF ~$300-350M annually in feedstock costs. If gas stays elevated while nitrogen prices moderate, margin compression is severe.

**3. Antitrust Litigation:** The March 2026 NPK price-fixing class action covers "2020 or 2021" to present. Treble damages on years of elevated fertilizer prices could theoretically reach multi-billion dollar exposure. CF denies wrongdoing. JPML consolidation hearing May 28, 2026. Fertilizer pricing tracks observable global commodity markets, making conspiracy claims challenging to prove, but the liability tail is real.

**4. Blue Point Execution:** $1.5B CF share of construction (2026-2029) at unprecedented scale for ATR ammonia technology. Tariff exposure on ~$1.2B imported materials. Louisiana permitting risk for Class VI CO2 injection wells. Technology risk at 1.5M ton scale.

**5. CEO Transition:** Christopher Bohn replacing Tony Will (who led the company since 2014) at a critical juncture — Blue Point construction beginning, earnings at cyclical peak, antitrust litigation filed. Limited track record as CEO.

**6. Political/Regulatory Overhang:** Trump administration food supply chain EO (December 2025), EU fertilizer duty suspension, congressional attention to fertilizer prices. Political tolerance for crisis profiteering is eroding. CF may face informal pressure not to fully benefit from current pricing.
