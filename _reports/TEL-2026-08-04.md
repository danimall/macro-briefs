## Executive Summary

TE Connectivity is midway through a structural repositioning. Two years ago Transportation Solutions was 60% of revenue; in the June 2026 quarter the two segments were exactly equal at $2,580M each. The Industrial half is the better business on every axis that matters: 21.0% organic growth in Q3 versus 4.5% in Transportation, a 20.8% operating margin versus a charge-adjusted ~21.0% (reported 17.2%), backlog up 9.8% against Transportation's 8.1% decline, and the single most informative disclosure in the 10-Q, **net positive pricing actions of +$46M in Q3 and +$131M over nine months, against Transportation's −$12M and −$46M of erosion.** For a company whose 10-K has long disclosed 1–2% annual company-wide price erosion, half the business flipping to a net price gainer is a change in economic regime, driven by spec-driven, capacity-constrained AI datacom (digital data networks +34.0% organic in Q3, +47.7% over nine months) and grid/data-center power (energy +32.7% organic).

The offsetting analysis is about earnings quality, and it is quantifiable. Reported nine-month gross margin expanded ~120bp to 36.5%, but commodity swap gains reclassified into cost of sales rose to $158M from $38M; excluding that delta, underlying expansion is roughly 40bp. Nine-month GAAP net income roughly doubled to $2,353M, but operating income contributed +$603M while tax expense fell −$608M, the prior-year comparison having absorbed a $574M Swiss valuation-allowance charge. The nine-month book tax rate of 18.1% includes a $114M discrete benefit from settling prior-period matters, while **cash taxes paid rose to $353M from $184M** and net deferred tax assets drew down from $2,507M to $2,233M. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.91 implicitly assumes the hedge tailwind and the low book rate both persist.

At $212.52 the stock is 18.6x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $11.45 and 16.5x FY2027's $12.91 (PEG ~1.29 on +12.8% forward earnings growth), with an EV/FY2026E EBITDA of ~13.4x, a ~5.1% free-cash-flow yield and a 1.47% dividend yield. Strip the $114M discrete tax benefit (~$0.39) and the incremental hedge gain (~$0.40 annualized after tax) and cleaned FY2026 earnings power is nearer $10.65, or ~20x. That is a fair multiple for a company with a genuinely improving half and a structurally deflationary half. Combined with uniformly one-directional insider selling, buybacks executed at a nine-month average of ~$225 against today's $212.52, and four consecutive fiscal years of total-return underperformance against management's own selected peer group, the risk/reward supports holding rather than adding. The recommendation moves off a prior BUY sequence because of new filing-level evidence on earnings composition, not the price decline since the June report.

## Company Overview

TE designs and manufactures engineered connectivity and sensing components: connectors, terminals, relays, sensors, antennas, heat-shrink tubing, subsea and industrial cable assemblies, and electrical distribution hardware. Roughly 93,000 employees across ~130 countries, ~55,000 in manufacturing, with ~75% of sales direct to customers. The 10-K sizes the served market at ~$200B, and describes competing against "thousands of companies."

The company is Irish-incorporated as of September 30, 2024 (previously Swiss, earlier Bermuda under the Tyco lineage). Debt is issued by Tyco Electronics Group S.A. in Luxembourg, guaranteed by TE Connectivity Switzerland Ltd. and the Irish parent. The domicile drives three things that appear later in this report: the distributable-profits constraint on dividends and buybacks, the Pillar Two tax exposure, and a risk factor flagging U.S. legislative proposals that could deny government contracts to companies that relocated to lower-tax jurisdictions (aerospace/defense/marine is ~9% of sales).

Two reportable segments since FY2025:

| Segment | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 | Q3 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transportation Solutions | 60% | 54% | 51% | 50% |
| Industrial Solutions | 40% | 46% | 49% | 50% |

Concentration facts from the FY2025 10-K: automotive end market ≈41% of net sales; customers in China ≈25% of net sales, supported by 19 principal manufacturing sites in China; digital data networks ≈13%; automation & connected living ≈12%. Geographic mix shifted to APAC 38% / EMEA 33% / Americas 29% from 34/37/29. No single customer is a significant share of sales. Approximately 60% of revenue is invoiced in non-USD currencies, and the 10-K states the company has **elected not to hedge** that exposure.

A discipline point on the mix shift: Industrial rising to half of revenue does not itself reduce concentration. Automotive end-market exposure (~41%) and China customer exposure (~25%) are separately disclosed and were not presented as improving.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Nine-month FY2026 net sales were $14,573M, +16.5%, decomposing into organic +$1,428M (+11.4%), currency +$360M (+2.9%) and acquisitions +$272M (+2.2%). Q3 was $5,160M, +13.8% (+12.2% organic). Q4 guidance of ~$5.25B against $4.75B implies +10.5%, producing a derived FY2026 of ~$19.82B, +14.8% on FY2025's ~$17.26B. Roughly a third of the nine-month growth is FX and M&A; organic is the honest operating read at +11.4%.

**Margins and the hedge caveat.**

| | 9M FY2025 | 9M FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 35.3% | 36.5% |
| Operating margin | 18.3% | 19.9% |
| Transportation op margin | 19.4% | 19.4% |
| Industrial op margin | 17.0% | 20.4% |

Industrial's Q3 operating margin of 20.8% (from 18.7%) on a 30.4% incremental margin is real operating leverage. Transportation's reported Q3 margin fell to 17.2% from 19.1%, but the segment absorbed $97M of charges including $79M of restructuring versus ~$24M prior year; adjusted for charges the margin improved roughly 90bp to ~21.0%. A reader stopping at the reported segment margin draws the wrong conclusion about the auto business.

The gross-margin qualifier is more consequential. The 10-Q discloses commodity swap gains reclassified into cost of sales of $158M (9M FY2026) versus $38M (9M FY2025). Backing those out: ~35.4% versus ~35.0%, so roughly two-thirds of the reported expansion is the larger hedge gain. The underlying input picture is severe: nine-month average gold $3,428/oz (+37%), silver $45.59/oz (+60%), copper $4.70/lb (+12%), against FY2026 expected purchases implying ~$1.38B of metals spend, about 7% of revenue. Notional commodity swaps grew to $770M from $569M and nine-month other comprehensive income included −$34M of cash-flow hedge losses (−$65M in Q3 alone), consistent with hedges marking at less favorable forward levels.

The fair alternative reading: hedges are the intended economic offset to physical cost inflation, so "ex-hedge margin" is a sensitivity rather than a normalized truth. If TE recovers metals through surcharges with a lag, and Industrial pricing was already +$131M net positive, realized price could substitute for the hedge benefit. The disclosure does not decompose Industrial pricing between surcharge recovery and value pricing, the ambiguity that makes the next two prints decisive.

**Tax is the dominant distortion in the earnings series.** FY2024 GAAP net income of $3,193M was flattered by recognition of deferred tax assets tied to a Swiss ten-year tax credit; FY2025's $1,842M was penalized by a $574M valuation-allowance increase reversing it (full-year effective rate ~42.5%). FY2026's nine-month rate of 18.1% includes a $114M discrete settlement benefit; Q3 standalone was 23.0%, which is the better near-term anchor. Decomposing the nine-month GAAP earnings doubling: +$603M from operating income, −$608M from lower tax expense. Approximately half of the GAAP surge is tax normalization.

The counterpoint most screens miss runs the other way. Cash taxes paid net of refunds nearly doubled to $353M from $184M while the book rate collapsed, net deferred tax assets fell $274M over nine months, and $261M of non-cash deferred tax expense flowed through operating cash flow. Ireland implemented OECD Pillar Two elements effective for TE from FY2025, with 50-plus countries enacting some form. The inference, labeled as such, is that the low book rate is partly funded by consuming a finite DTA stock, so cash taxes should converge upward toward the book rate over time. The alternative is that new credits, Pillar Two safe harbors, or the post-2025 CFC attribution change sustain a lower rate longer. Either way, FY2027 consensus is not stress-tested for convergence.

**Reconciling the guidance ambiguity.** The 10-Q guides Q4 diluted EPS from continuing operations of ~$2.84; consensus for the current quarter is $3.07. The $0.23 gap approximates one quarter of restructuring and acquisition-amortization add-backs, which supports reading the company figure as GAAP and consensus as non-GAAP. That reconciles cleanly: quarterly adjusted actuals of $2.72 + $2.73 + $2.94 = $8.39, plus $3.07 = $11.46, matching consensus FY2026 of $11.45. Derived FY2026 GAAP EPS is ~$10.82. The narrower-than-expected GAAP/adjusted gap in H1 is itself explained by the $114M discrete tax benefit offsetting add-backs.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow $2,997M (from $2,718M), capex $832M (from $665M), free cash flow $2,165M at a 14.9% margin versus FY2025's full-year 18.6% ($3,203M). The compression is identifiable: receivables −$355M, inventories −$365M, accrued liabilities −$240M, partly offset by payables +$433M, plus 25% higher capex at 5.7% of sales. Fiscal Q4 is seasonally strongest, so the nine-month rate should not be annualized. Working capital looks disciplined rather than stretched: inventories +12.2% against revenue +16.5%, receivables +10.2%, derived cash conversion cycle ~83 days, supply-chain-finance obligations within payables an immaterial $142M.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $5,632M against $1,239M cash, net debt $4,393M, equity $13,246M. Against FY2026E EBITDA of ~$4.94B (9M operating income $2,898M + D&A $758M + Q4 ~$1,024M + ~$260M), net leverage is ~0.89x versus a 3.75x covenant. February 2026 brought a new $3.0B five-year revolver replacing $1.5B (undrawn, with $1.0B of incremental capacity) alongside $750M of new notes and $850M of repayments; interest expense rose to $93M from $48M on Richards financing. Pro forma for the $1.4B Astrodyne cash outlay, leverage is roughly 1.17x before any acquired EBITDA. Financial resilience here is not in question.

The balance-sheet risk that is: goodwill $7,403M plus net intangibles $2,081M equals 36% of assets and 72% of equity, against **accumulated goodwill impairment losses of $4,249M** ($3,091M Transportation, $1,158M Industrial). Those write-offs are historical and largely relate to earlier automotive-era deals, so they do not establish that current deals are mispriced. They do establish that this management structure has previously overpaid at scale, which matters given ~$3.7B committed to acquisitions in eighteen months.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is concentrated and the concentration is favorable in mix but limited in scale.

Q3 Industrial detail: digital data networks $813M, +34.0% organic, attributed to AI applications; energy $516M, +32.7% organic on grid hardening and data-center power; automation & connected living $664M, +14.3% organic; aerospace/defense/marine $419M, +11.5% organic; medical $168M, −7.2% on a strategic product-line exit. On a nine-month basis, digital data networks reached $2,234M with +47.7% organic growth and energy $1,367M (+21.5% organic, with ~$271M from acquisitions). Secondary coverage of the July 22 call reported record quarterly orders above $5B and digital data networks orders +70% year to date; the exact orders figure was truncated in every retrievable snippet and the 10-Q discloses neither orders nor quarterly backlog, so book-to-bill cannot be computed. Treat those as unverified.

Q3 Transportation detail: automotive $1,913M, +2.9% organic, with APAC +5.6%, EMEA +2.6% and **Americas −3.8%**; commercial transportation $434M, +17.8% organic; sensors $233M, −2.8% organic. Management attributes automotive growth to content-per-vehicle gains offsetting declining global vehicle production, an accurate framing that also caps the upside, because content gains are won in tenders that carry annual price give-backs.

Backlog gives the cleanest directional read available: FY2025 year-end backlog of $6,188M split Transportation $2,278M (from $2,478M, −8.1%) and Industrial $3,910M (from $3,561M, +9.8%). This is a ~4.3-month order book, roughly 36% of revenue, and TE explicitly states it is not necessarily indicative of future sales and that orders may be cancelled. It should not be characterized as contracted multi-year revenue.

Sizing the AI exposure honestly: digital data networks annualizes to ~$3.25B, about 16% of FY2026E revenue. Growth is exceptional; scale is a minority of the company. The more differentiated growth vector may be grid electrification, where energy grew 32.7% organically and where TE has now deployed ~$2.3B on Richards Manufacturing (overhead/underground electrical and gas distribution hardware) and committed ~$1.4B to Astrodyne TDI (power and filter solutions). That competitive set is Hubbell and Carlisle rather than Amphenol, with better pricing structure and less direct sensitivity to hyperscaler capex timing.

Consensus supports the mid-teens revenue and low-teens forward earnings trajectory: current-year EPS $11.45 (+1.9% over 90 days), next year $12.91 (+2.2%), with current-quarter estimates +3.0% over 30 days. This is steady positive revision momentum, well short of the 30%-plus velocity that signals a mispriced inflection. Three consecutive beats of 5.6%, 0.2% and 2.4% describe a company that guides carefully and clears modestly, which raises confidence in consensus without implying large upside to it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $212.52:

- 18.6x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $11.45
- 16.5x consensus FY2027 of $12.91; PEG ≈ 1.29 on +12.8% forward earnings growth
- ~19.6x derived FY2026 GAAP EPS of ~$10.82
- EV ≈ $65.9–66.7B (market cap plus $4.39B net debt) ÷ ~$4.94B FY2026E EBITDA ≈ **13.4x**
- Free-cash-flow yield ~5.1% on FY2026E FCF of roughly $3.1–3.2B
- Dividend $3.12 annualized after a 9.9% June increase to $0.78/quarter = **1.47% yield**, ~29% of GAAP EPS
- Remaining buyback authorization $3,038.6M ≈ 4.9% of market cap

The supplied peer set (FLEX 65.2x, JBL 50.5x, FN 50.0x, TTMI 101.8x, SANM 52.3x, median 52.3x) makes TEL look dramatically cheap, and that comparison should be discarded. Those are contract manufacturers and optical/PCB suppliers earning low-to-mid-single-digit operating margins whose trailing multiples are inflated by trough earnings and AI-driven inflections. TE earns a 19.9% operating margin on proprietary engineered components. The correct reference is Amphenol, and there TE is cheaper for identifiable reasons: APH's FY2025 operating margin of 25.4% and ROE of 32.1% versus TE's 18.6% and 14.6% GAAP (~15.2% and ~20.8% on adjusted earnings that strip the valuation allowance), on ~33% more revenue converting to ~82% more operating income.

The bull-side valuation argument is a re-rating on mix: the market still prices TE partly as an auto cyclical, while half the revenue base now grows at 20%-plus with net positive pricing. A multiple move from 16.5x to 19–20x forward on that recognition is the entire upside case, and analyst targets are consistent with it (mean $246.79, median $235, high $306, with the low target of $209 sitting *below* spot).

The reason I do not score valuation higher is the composition of the earnings the multiple is applied to. Removing the $114M discrete tax benefit (~$0.39/share) and the incremental commodity hedge gain versus prior year (~$120M pretax over nine months, ~$0.40 annualized after tax at 23%) puts cleaned FY2026 adjusted earnings power nearer **$10.65**, or ~20.0x. Consensus FY2027 of $12.91 requires the hedge benefit to persist as swaps roll at current metal prices, and the book tax rate to stay near 18–23% while cash taxes are already converging upward. Neither is impossible; neither is de-risked. A 16.5x headline multiple that becomes ~20x on cleaned earnings, on a business half of which faces declining unit volumes and contractual price erosion, is fair value rather than asymmetric opportunity. A low multiple relative to a mismatched peer group is not a thesis.

Scenario arithmetic from $212.52:

- **Base $235** — 18.2x FY2027 consensus $12.91, matching the analyst median. +10.6%.
- **Bull $285** — 21x $13.50, assuming Industrial pricing proves durable, Astrodyne closes accretively, and the hedge roll is absorbed by surcharges. +34.1%. Analyst high is $306.
- **Bear $168** — 15x $11.20, if the hedge tailwind reverses without price offset, cash taxes converge toward the book rate, and Americas automotive weakness broadens. −20.9%.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is bifurcated by segment, and trajectory differs sharply.

In Transportation, TE competes with Yazaki, Aptiv, Sumitomo, Sensata and Molex in a market structured around OEM tenders and annual price concessions. The 10-K's disclosure of 1–2% annual price erosion and the 10-Q's −$46M of nine-month Transportation pricing quantify the outcome. Backlog down 8.1% and Americas automotive down 3.8% place this half of the company in defensive posture, which is why the FY2026 restructuring program is concentrated there: $84M of the $86M in program charges, targeting ~$58M of annualized savings not fully realized until end of FY2029. A nominal 1.7-year payback stretched over three years, in a segment with declining volumes, invites repeat charges.

In Industrial, the reference point is Amphenol, and TE is behind on profitability while improving on trajectory. TE's Industrial operating margin rose to 20.4% over nine months from 17.0%, with pricing net positive and backlog up. But at the enterprise level, TE has underperformed its own selected peer group in each of the last four fiscal years on indexed total shareholder return — FY2022 119.28 vs 120.36; FY2023 135.95 vs 152.54; FY2024 169.31 vs 208.32; FY2025 247.03 vs 288.00 — with the gap widening from about one point to about 41. Evercore's June 30, 2026 note (before the Q3 beat) maintained Amphenol as top pick and took a more cautious near-term stance on TE, citing choppier near-term fundamentals with long-term trends intact. That is the sell-side's articulation of the same gap, and the recommendation distribution has drifted the same way: strong-buy-plus-buy from 13 to 12 over two months, holds from 6 to 7, with the sentiment trend flagged as degrading.

Industry-level dynamics visible in the filings cut across all plated-contact manufacturers: precious-metal inflation squeezing input costs, Pillar Two compressing the tax advantage of Irish/Swiss/Luxembourg structures the industry has favored, Section 301 tariffs raising compliance burden on China-heavy footprints, and declining global vehicle production.

Net assessment: improving where it matters most for incremental earnings, structurally disadvantaged versus the best-in-class comparable, with the enterprise-level scoreboard four years negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Automotive volume and price.** ~41% of FY2025 sales in an end market where TE itself attributes growth to content gains "offsetting global vehicle production declines," with Americas automotive −3.8% and structural price erosion. This is broadly priced, but a broadening consumer weakening would pressure both units and content mix in FY2027.

**China on both sides of the ledger.** ~25% of sales to Chinese customers and 19 principal manufacturing sites there create simultaneous demand, tariff and supply-chain exposure. The live evidence: TE filed a perfected prior disclosure with U.S. Customs and Border Protection on unpaid Section 301 duties from country-of-origin determinations and **paid $14M in Q3 FY2026, with CBP's review incomplete.** The amount is immaterial; the signal about certifying origin across this footprint is not.

**Unhedged revenue FX.** ~60% of revenue is non-USD and explicitly unhedged at the revenue line. This added +$360M (+2.9%) to nine-month sales and reverses on dollar strength. TE does run net investment hedges (notional $3,888M) and a $5,755M cross-currency swap program, so the unhedged item is specifically transactional and translational revenue exposure.

**Hedge roll and cash-tax convergence — the two least-priced risks.** Both would appear as FY2027 earnings disappointment against $12.91 rather than as a headline event, which is why they are underweighted. The commodity swap notional grew 35% and Q3 OCI showed −$65M of hedge losses; cash taxes nearly doubled while the DTA stock depleted $274M in nine months.

**Goodwill.** $9.48B of goodwill plus intangibles at 72% of equity, against $4,249M of cumulative impairments, with Richards acquired at an implied ~5.0–5.75x revenue (derived from disclosed six-month contributions of $179M and $227M) and Astrodyne's revenue, EBITDA and multiple undisclosed. If grid or data-center capex decelerates, impairment is live.

**Irish domicile specifics.** Dividends and buybacks payable only from distributable profits; a 25% Irish dividend withholding tax can apply in circumstances; U.S. judgments harder to enforce; Irish takeover rules apply; and a flagged risk from U.S. legislative proposals targeting government contracts with redomiciled companies.

**Integration.** Astrodyne (~$1.4B cash, expected close by end of CY2026) plus Richards plus an unnamed FY2026 deal at $200M cash and ~$150M of earn-out fair value. Richards was excluded from the FY2025 internal-control assessment and enters scope in FY2026.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($285, +34%)

Half of TE has become a pricing-power business. Industrial's +$131M of nine-month net pricing actions inverts the company's long-standing 1–2% erosion disclosure, and pricing compounds into margin in a way volume growth does not. Digital data networks at +47.7% organic over nine months with orders reportedly +70% year to date, energy at +32.7% organic, and Industrial backlog +9.8% give the segment visible momentum from two independent demand sources: hyperscaler build-out and utility grid hardening. Transportation's underlying margin is improving ~90bp beneath the restructuring charges, so the auto half is a cash-generating stabilizer rather than a drag. The balance sheet supports the strategy: 0.89x net leverage against a 3.75x covenant, a newly doubled $3.0B undrawn revolver, and $3.04B of buyback authorization, which together represent $5–6B of deployable capacity being directed toward data-center power and grid electrification adjacency. On $13.50 of FY2027 earnings at a 21x multiple, the re-rating a company with a 20%-plus growing, price-positive half should command, the stock is $285.

### Bear Case ($168, −21%)

The FY2026 earnings the multiple is applied to are lower quality than they appear. Two-thirds of gross-margin expansion is a commodity hedge gain that grew from $38M to $158M and will compress as swaps reset against gold up 37% and silver up 60%; Q3 already recorded −$65M of hedge losses in OCI. Approximately half the GAAP earnings surge is tax normalization, and the 18.1% nine-month book rate embeds a $114M discrete benefit while cash taxes nearly doubled and the DTA stock depleted. Cleaned FY2026 earnings power near $10.65 puts the stock at ~20x, not 16.5x. Meanwhile the structurally deflationary half of the company faces declining unit volumes, Americas automotive already −3.8%, backlog −8.1%, and recurring restructuring with savings deferred to FY2029. Management has underperformed its own peer group four years running, is compensated on a metric that permanently excludes ~$225M/year of amortization created by its own acquisitions, has repurchased shares at a nine-month average of ~$225 above today's price, and has produced $4.25B of cumulative goodwill write-offs on prior deal vintages while committing $3.7B more in eighteen months at undisclosed multiples. On $11.20 at 15x, that is $168.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Base case $235 (+10.6%), bull $285 (+34.1%), bear $168 (−20.9%). Horizon 12 months.**

The base case is 18.2x consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.91, which coincides with the analyst median target of $235 and represents a modest multiple expansion from 16.5x on the continued Industrial mix shift. Reward-to-risk from $212.52 is roughly 0.5:1 to base and 1.6:1 to bull against bear, adequate to hold an existing position, insufficient to add.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Q4 FY2026 report (late October 2026) showing gross margin at or above 36.5% while commodity swap gains reclassified into cost of sales decline year over year** — the single decisive test of whether Industrial pricing has replaced the hedge tailwind. Pair with FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $13.00.
2. **Astrodyne TDI multiple disclosed below ~4x revenue or ~15x EBITDA with segment-accretive margins at closing**, combined with Industrial net pricing actions remaining at or above +$40M per quarter.
3. **Price toward $180–185** (≈14.0–14.3x FY2027 consensus, ~4.4% dividend-plus-buyback yield with unchanged fundamentals), or a cluster of open-market insider purchases, which would materially change the management-alignment read given eight months of one-directional selling.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 5%** from $12.91, which is how hedge reversal or cash-tax convergence would first surface.
2. **Industrial net pricing turning negative, or digital data networks organic growth decelerating below +15%** — the two pillars of the mix-shift thesis.
3. **A goodwill impairment charge, Astrodyne funded with equity, or net leverage exceeding 2.0x EBITDA.**
4. **Transportation backlog down more than 15% year over year with Americas automotive deteriorating further**, which would convert a stabilizer into a drag.

**Reassessment points:** Q4 FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance (late October 2026), Astrodyne closing and purchase-price disclosure (by end of CY2026), and the FY2026 10-K's disclosure of full-year commodity hedge gains, cash taxes paid and remaining DTA balance.

## Conclusion

TE Connectivity is executing a real repositioning: the Industrial half is now equal in size to Transportation, grows five times faster organically, carries a higher margin, and has flipped from price-taker to net price-gainer, with backlog and order momentum in AI datacom and grid power confirming the demand. The balance sheet is among the cleanest in the component space at 0.89x net leverage with $5–6B of deployable capacity, and capital returns absorbed 92% of nine-month free cash flow alongside a 9.9% dividend increase.

The reason this is a hold rather than a purchase at $212.52 is the composition of the earnings the market is capitalizing. Two-thirds of gross-margin expansion came from commodity hedge gains that grew fourfold and will roll at materially worse forward prices; roughly half the GAAP earnings surge was tax normalization; the 18.1% book rate contains a $114M discrete benefit while cash taxes nearly doubled and the deferred tax asset depleted. Adjust for the discrete items and the stock trades near 20x cleaned FY2026 earnings, not the 16.5x forward headline, a fair price for a company that still derives ~41% of revenue from a shrinking, contractually deflationary end market, has underperformed its own selected peer group four consecutive years, repurchased shares this year at prices above today's, and has written off $4.25B of prior acquisition goodwill while committing $3.7B more at undisclosed multiples.

Own it for the mix shift and the $235 base case. The evidence that would justify adding, margin holding as hedges roll and FY2027 guidance above $13.00, arrives with the October print.
