**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy closed its $21.8 billion acquisition of Calpine on January 7, 2026, converting a pure-play nuclear and clean-power generator into a coast-to-coast platform of roughly 55 GW spanning nuclear, natural gas, geothermal, hydro, wind, and solar, paired with the largest competitive retail electricity book in the country (~2.5 million accounts, 80% of the Fortune 100). The first full quarter including Calpine produced revenue of $11,122M (+63.8% YoY) and adjusted operating EPS of $2.74 (+28% YoY), and management reaffirmed a 20%+ base EPS growth algorithm from 2026 to 2029. Investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa1) were affirmed through the close, and the buyback authorization was raised to $5.0 billion.

The stock has fallen roughly 27% year-to-date, from about $354 at the Calpine close to $267, even as operating fundamentals strengthened. The decline does not reflect deterioration in the business. It reflects multiple compression off a richly valued AI/data-center re-rating, the December 2025 FERC draft order finding PJM's tariff unjust as it relates to co-located load, and questions about Crane Clean Energy Center deliverability. The first two items are the core of the bull case: the powered-land data-center model and the Microsoft-Crane arrangement depend on the co-located-load framework FERC has ordered PJM to rewrite, and that framework is unresolved.

I rate CEG a HOLD with a growth lean. The fundamental trajectory supports continued ownership, and the price decline on stable-to-improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward versus the start of the year. But the valuation premium to the electric-utility group (P/E ~22.8x vs. peer median 23.1x, and a higher multiple on the AI-growth framing) is underwritten by data-center optionality that cannot be priced until two specific regulatory and technical gating items resolve. A ~25M-share Calpine lock-up release on June 30, 2026 adds a near-term technical overhang. Until FERC's co-located-load order produces an actionable framework and Crane's interconnection path clears, the asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not yet present.

## Company Overview

Constellation is the largest U.S. nuclear operator and the largest U.S. producer of clean energy, spun out of Exelon in 2022 and headquartered in Baltimore. Following the Calpine acquisition, the company reports six segments: Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT, Other Power Regions, and Calpine. Approximately 93% of baseload clean MWhs sit within PJM, which makes PJM market design and regulation central to the investment case.

Management frames the moat as "assets that cannot be replicated," estimating new-build replacement cost of the ~55 GW fleet at more than 3x current enterprise value. The strategic logic is that around-the-clock, carbon-free nuclear output is uniquely matched to hyperscaler data-center demand, while Calpine's dispatchable gas and retail platform add ERCOT and West exposure plus "powered land" optionality at plant sites.

## Financial Analysis

GAAP net income to common was $1,590M ($4.49/share) versus $118M ($0.38), but the GAAP figure is heavily distorted by $721M of favorable unrealized hedge mark-to-market gains and a $174M favorable nuclear asset retirement obligation remeasurement. Calpine contributed $2,395M of revenue and $1,126M of segment revenue net of fuel.

Operating cash flow was only $425M against $1,275M of capex, reflecting working-capital and collateral movements that have not yet normalized for the combined entity. This warrants monitoring rather than alarm, given $6.7B of credit availability plus $0.8B cash and total facilities of roughly $15.1B (the revolver was upsized to $7.0B).

The balance sheet expanded materially: total assets of $96.9B (from $57.2B at year-end 2025), including $11.5B of goodwill (up from $0.4B) and $40.8B of net PP&E. Debt is $16,994M long-term plus $5,102M short-term against $33.8B of equity. CEG assumed roughly $12.6B of Calpine debt and retired about $5.3B in Q1, including $2.5B of term loans, $1.25B of secured notes, and $1.4B of unsecured notes. S&P (BBB+) and Moody's (Baa1) both affirmed investment grade in January post-close. A three-notch downgrade would be required to lose IG, and a sub-IG event would trigger an estimated ~$3.0B incremental collateral call.

On capital allocation, ~$4.7B of the buyback authorization remains; ~$335M (~1.2M shares) was deployed in April-May 2026, and cumulatively ~$2.7B / 18.5M shares since the 2022 separation. The dividend is $0.4265/quarter, a yield under 1%; this is a growth-and-buyback story rather than an income vehicle. FY2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance is $11.00-$12.00, with base EPS growth of 20%+ from a 2026 base of $6.65-6.75 to an enhanced $11.40-11.90 by 2029, and cumulative FCF before growth of $11.5-13.0B across 2028-2029.

One accounting note that distorts a naive screen: the FY2025 GAAP net income decline (~$3.75B to $2.3B) was driven by nuclear PTC revenue falling from $2.08B to $0.32B. That decline is favorable, not adverse. The PTC functions as a price floor (~$44.75/MWh in 2025) that phases out as power prices rise; rising PJM and New York prices pushed units above the phase-out, so the fleet earned more in the market and required less PTC. Adjusted operating earnings rose. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.3 similarly reflects the structurally high asset base and gross-up derivative and decommissioning trust balances of a capital-intensive merchant utility, not distress.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the AI/data-center electricity demand cycle and CEG's structural position within it. PJM capacity prices have surged: Eastern Mid-Atlantic +403.6% to $269.92/MW-day and ComEd +833% to $269.92/MW-day year-over-year, with FERC approving a $175-325/MW-day collar for the 2028/29 and 2029/30 auctions. Power prices rose sharply (PJM West +81%, ComEd +43.6%, NYISO Central +49%). Management quantifies the optionality: roughly +$0.40-1.00 EPS per 1 GW of nuclear PPA, +$0.30-1.00 per $1-3/MWh power-price move on uncontracted volumes, and $0.20-0.50 EPS per 1,000 MW of powered-land deals.

Commercial traction is visible: a new 380 MW CyrusOne agreement at Freestone (ERCOT) with exclusivity for another 380 MW, plus 400 MW at Thad Hill (780 MW signed to date), and 5,000 MW of new capacity submitted to PJM. Pin Oak Creek (460 MW gas peaker) and Pastoria Solar (105 MW) commenced operations in April 2026. The Crane Clean Energy Center (restarted Three Mile Island Unit 1, 835 MW) is a CEG-owned asset under a 20-year Microsoft offtake PPA, with a DOE guarantee for up to $1.0B issued in November 2025 (no draws yet).

Estimate revisions corroborate the operating momentum without signaling acceleration. Current-year EPS estimates rose +3.3% over 90 days to $11.75; next-year sits at $13.61. Current-quarter estimates are mixed (+1.6% over 30 days but -5.6% over 90 days), and next-quarter is slightly down. This is positive but not the +30% velocity that characterizes a high-conviction earnings re-rating. The earnings track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, Q1 2026 +5.7%).

The durability of the data-center growth thesis is high at the sector level: multiple hyperscalers are straining PJM capacity, and the capacity-price and collar dynamics confirm a structural demand shift. What is not yet underwritable is the company-specific monetization timing, which is gated by regulation discussed below.

## Valuation Assessment

At $267, CEG trades at roughly 22.8x trailing/forward earnings on the $7.40 reported EPS basis, and at about 22-24x against FY2026 adjusted guidance of $11.00-12.00 (the adjusted number is the relevant operating metric). Against the electric-utility peer set — DUK 18.6x, SO 23.1x, AEP 18.8x, VST 26.7x, ETR 27.5x, median 23.1x — CEG sits near the peer median on adjusted earnings, with the merchant/IPP comparables (VST, ETR) above it.

The valuation question is whether the premium-to-traditional-utility framing is supported. On the 20%+ base growth algorithm to 2029, a PEG near or below 1.2 is defensible if that growth is delivered. The analyst target distribution is entirely above spot: low $310, mean $367, median $377, high $441 — implying 16% to 65% upside, with even the floor target 16% above current price. On the framework that analyst targets lag reality for inflecting names with rising estimates, the gap between $267 and a $377 median target is meaningful.

I am scoring valuation at 6.5 rather than higher because the upside in the targets and in management's enhanced-earnings path is conditioned on data-center contracting that the December 2025 FERC draft order has not yet enabled. The premium portion of the multiple — the difference between a 23x utility multiple and the AI-growth re-rating — is the part most exposed to de-rating if the co-located-load framework lands unfavorably or slips. The decline from $354 to $267 is partly the market repricing that conditionality, not a free mispricing. The base business at ~23x adjusted earnings with a 20% growth algorithm is reasonably priced; the call option on powered-land monetization is what makes the stock cheap if it resolves and expensive if it does not.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's structural advantages are genuine and improving. The carbon-free baseload nuclear fleet is well suited to 24/7 data-center load and to ESG-conscious hyperscalers, and the nuclear PTC acts as an earnings floor that costs taxpayers little in a rising-price environment because it phases out as prices rise — an asymmetry few merchant generators have. Calpine adds dispatchable gas and the largest competitive retail book, complementing the inflexible nuclear fleet.

The competitive set — Vistra, NRG, Talen, regulated IPPs — has overlapping exposure to the same demand cycle, but few combine CEG's nuclear scale, geographic breadth (PJM, ERCOT, NYISO, West), and retail reach. Share trajectory is positive: CEG is winning data-center offtake (Microsoft-Crane, CyrusOne, Thad Hill) and has submitted 5,000 MW to PJM. The trajectory is the strongest of the six dimensions here, supporting a 7.5, with the caveat that the regulatory framework partly determines who captures the rents.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score is the binding constraint at 4.5, reflecting specific, dated, unresolved catalysts that the current price does not fully resolve.

**PJM co-located-load regulation (the #1 idiosyncratic risk).** The December 2025 FERC draft order found PJM's tariff unjust and unreasonable as it relates to co-located load and behind-the-meter generation — the powered-land model underpinning CEG's data-center economics and the Crane/Microsoft arrangement. FERC directed PJM to rewrite these rules; PJM's compliance filing and the final framework are unresolved. Management's own language ("regulatory clarity will unlock data center contracting opportunities") concedes this is a gating item.

**Crane deliverability.** PJM's Phase I study identified contingent transmission upgrades that could push full grid deliverability as late as December 2030. CEG filed a FERC waiver in March 2026 to transfer Eddystone's capacity interconnection rights to Crane, with resolution requested by June 1, 2026. Restart also requires NRC and state approvals.

**Calpine integration and goodwill.** $11.5B of goodwill from a deal closed weeks before the filing; purchase price allocation is preliminary with a one-year measurement period. Any souring of the data-center/gas thesis raises impairment risk.

**Required divestitures on a clock.** DOJ requires CEG to divest 5 PJM plants (~4.4 GW, sold to LS Power for $5.0B, March 2026, pending approval) plus an ERCOT plant by September 4, 2026. Execution and pricing risk if approvals slip.

**Other risks.** ~35% of 2026-2031 uranium requirements come from three suppliers including Russian sourcing; a sub-IG downgrade would trigger ~$3.0B incremental collateral; 30-40% of earnings ("enhanced") depend on elevated power prices and spark spreads that could revert; ~$19.5B of decommissioning trust assets expose earnings to market moves (a 10% equity / 25bp rate move ≈ $1.07B NDT hit); the Zion gas plant faces possible 2030 shutdown under Illinois CEJA; and inherited Winter Storm Uri tort litigation remains live.

**Technical overhang.** 25M shares (50% of the Calpine consideration) become free to trade June 30, 2026, layered on an already-weak tape.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 54.7% is rich versus 46.9% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (54.7% near-term vs. 48.5% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst rather than steady-state elevated uncertainty — consistent with the June 1 Crane waiver resolution date, the June 18 expiry cluster, and the June 30 lock-up. The put/call OI ratio rose sharply from 1.43 to 2.41 over the past two weeks as the stock fell 10.9%, and put/call skew is +5.4% (elevated put demand). Unusual activity includes 1,256 contracts at the $265 put for June 18 (5.4x OI). The market is hedging a near-term binary, which is the regulatory/technical resolution window that gates the upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$370-441).** FERC's co-located-load framework lands favorably, PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement enables bilateral data-center contracting (Phase 1 targeted ~Sept 2026), and Crane's interconnection clears on a workable timeline. CEG converts the 5,000 MW PJM submission and powered-land pipeline into signed PPAs at $0.20-0.50 EPS per 1,000 MW, capacity prices stay near the $175-325 collar, and the enhanced-earnings path to $11.40-11.90 by 2029 is delivered. At 28x a 2029 number near $11.80, the stock re-rates back above $370, with the high target of $441 achievable if multiple expansion accompanies delivery. The PTC floor caps downside while upside stays open.

**Bear Case (~$200-220).** The co-located-load order constrains the powered-land model, Crane deliverability slips toward 2030 and NRC/state approvals stall, the June 30 lock-up adds supply into a weak tape, and capacity/power prices revert from cycle peaks, compressing the 30-40% of earnings that is merchant-dependent. The AI-growth premium de-rates toward a 17-18x utility multiple on a base EPS path nearer $7, implying ~$200-220. Goodwill impairment risk on the preliminary Calpine allocation compounds the downside.

**Base Case.** Regulation resolves with workable-but-imperfect clarity over the next two to four quarters, Crane proceeds on a delayed timeline, and CEG delivers the 20%+ base growth algorithm without the full enhanced-earnings upside. The stock recovers toward the low-to-mid $300s as the binary overhangs clear, consistent with the analyst median around $377 over a 12-18 month horizon but with the realization back-half weighted.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale and a 12-18 month reassessment horizon tied to specific regulatory and technical milestones.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FERC issues a final co-located-load order, and PJM files a compliance framework, that preserves the economics of behind-the-meter/co-located data-center contracting — removing the gating item management itself flags.
- Crane interconnection clarity: the FERC Eddystone CIR-transfer waiver is granted and full deliverability is confirmed materially earlier than the December 2030 worst case, with NRC restart on track.
- Confirmation that data-center PPAs are being signed at the quantified EPS sensitivities (a second or third large offtake at powered-land terms), converting optionality into contracted earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An unfavorable final FERC framework that materially impairs the powered-land model, or Crane deliverability slipping firmly to 2030 with NRC/state delays.
- A goodwill impairment on the Calpine allocation, or a sub-IG credit action triggering the ~$3.0B collateral call.
- Capacity/power-price reversion below the collar floor combined with falling forward EPS revisions, signaling the merchant-earnings base is eroding.

**Reassessment timeline:** The June 1, 2026 Crane waiver resolution, the September 4, 2026 divestiture deadline, the ~Sept 2026 PJM Phase 1 bilateral-contracting milestone, the June 30, 2026 lock-up, and the next two earnings prints are the natural checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy is a higher-quality business after Calpine than before it, with a transformed scale advantage, a leading nuclear fleet aligned to the strongest secular electricity-demand cycle in decades, an investment-grade balance sheet preserved through the deal, and a credible 20%+ base EPS growth algorithm. The ~27% YTD price decline reflects multiple compression and unresolved regulatory uncertainty rather than operating deterioration, and the lower price improves the risk/reward.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: the premium portion of the valuation, and the analyst targets that sit 16-65% above spot, are underwritten by data-center monetization that the December 2025 FERC draft order on PJM co-located load has explicitly thrown into question, and by Crane deliverability that PJM studies place as late as December 2030. These are dated, binary items resolving over the next two to four quarters, with the June 1 Crane waiver date and June 30 lock-up creating near-term pressure that the options market is actively hedging. Until the co-located-load framework produces actionable rules and Crane's interconnection path clears, the asymmetry needed for a high-conviction long does not exist. I would upgrade on favorable resolution of either gating item with evidence of contracted data-center PPAs, and accumulate toward the low-$200s on weakness where the base utility business alone supports the price.
