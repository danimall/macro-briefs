## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is one of the largest publicly listed product tanker operators globally, running a fleet of 87 vessels predominantly composed of LR2 and MR tankers. The company has executed a balance sheet transformation over the past three years, moving from a leveraged, loss-making position in 2021 to a net cash balance of $385M with $1.7B in total liquidity as of March 2026. STNG is one of the best-capitalized cyclical shipping companies in the public markets, with downside protection that most peers lack.

FY 2025 marked a normalization year: revenue declined 24.6% to $938M and net income fell 48.5% to $344M as product tanker rates softened from the extraordinary 2022-2024 super-cycle. Even in this trough year, the company generated $417M in FCF (44.4% margin), reduced debt by $450M, prepaid all scheduled amortization through 2027, raised the dividend 13%, and continued buying back shares. The stock trades at approximately 10x trailing earnings and 9.4x trailing FCF — cheap by absolute standards and at a meaningful premium to pure-play energy services peers, though justified by fleet quality, balance sheet strength, and the structural rate tailwind from Strait of Hormuz disruptions now driving Q2 2026 LR2 spot rates above $100,000/day.

The primary risks are cyclical: product tanker rates are volatile, the global orderbook is growing, and any normalization of Russian sanctions or Hormuz tensions would reduce ton-mile demand. The newly issued $375M convertible notes add dilution risk above $100.39/share. However, the risk-reward profile at current levels is asymmetric to the upside given the geopolitical backdrop, balance sheet optionality, and management's demonstrated discipline.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers is headquartered in Monaco and incorporated in the Marshall Islands, filing as a foreign private issuer (20-F). The company operates 87 product tankers across three segments: 32 LR2 tankers (Long Range 2, ~105,000 DWT), 41 MR tankers (Medium Range, ~50,000 DWT), and 14 Handymax tankers (~37,000 DWT). The fleet averages approximately 10.1 years in age, with most vessels equipped with exhaust gas scrubbers that reduce fuel costs and position the fleet favorably under increasingly stringent IMO environmental regulations (CII ratings, EU ETS).

The company is actively reshaping its fleet through a dual strategy: selling older vessels (13+ tankers sold since September 2025) while ordering 10 newbuildings (4 MR, 4 LR2, and 2 VLCCs) for delivery between 2026 and 2029. The VLCC orders represent a strategic expansion into crude oil transportation, a departure from the traditional product tanker focus that introduces new market exposure.

As a Marshall Islands corporation, STNG pays effectively zero income taxes, adding approximately 25%+ to after-tax earnings relative to U.S.-incorporated competitors. This is a structural, durable advantage.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability

FY 2025 represented a normalization from the 2022-2024 super-cycle:

| Metric | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 | YoY Change |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|------------|
| Revenue | $1,341M | $1,244M | $938M | -24.6% |
| Gross Profit | $1,012M | $894M | $613M | -31.4% |
| Net Income | $547M | $669M | $344M | -48.5% |
| EBITDA | $908M | $950M | $536M | -43.6% |
| FCF | $842M | $732M | $417M | -43.0% |

The revenue decline is entirely rate-driven, not cost-driven. Gross margins compressed from 71.9% to 65.4%, but this remains exceptionally strong for a shipping company. Operating margin dropped from 61.5% to 37.9%, though FY 2024 was inflated by $176.5M in vessel sale gains. On an adjusted basis (excluding asset sale gains), FY 2025 adjusted net income was approximately $270M, with adjusted EBITDA of $568M.

Interest expense fell from $109.5M to $80.1M (reflecting aggressive debt reduction), while interest income surged from $12.4M to $67.1M (reflecting the large cash balance earning current market rates). Net interest cost is approaching zero, which is unusual for a capital-intensive shipping company.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the strongest feature of the investment case. As of March 20, 2026:

- **Cash: $974M** ($20/share)
- **Total debt: $589M** (down from ~$1B+ in prior years)
- **Net cash: $385M** (pro forma $513M after pending vessel sales close)
- **Revolver availability: $747M**
- **Total liquidity: ~$1.7B** (~$35/share)

Management prepaid all scheduled principal amortization through December 2027, eliminating near-term refinancing risk entirely. Total debt repayments in FY 2025 alone were $450M.

The April 2026 convertible note issuance ($375M at 1.75%, conversion at $100.39/share) adds gross debt but at an extremely low cost. Of the ~$363M net proceeds, $100M funded a concurrent share repurchase (1.35M shares at $74.36), with the remainder available for newbuilding capex. This is a disciplined capital structure decision: locking in cheap financing during a period of profitability, using a portion to reduce equity float, and maintaining flexibility for fleet investment.

### Capital Return

Share count has declined 20% in three years, from 61.3M diluted shares (2022) to 49M (2025). The quarterly dividend was increased 13% to $0.45/share ($1.80 annualized), yielding approximately 2.4%. Combined with buybacks (including the $100M concurrent repurchase), total shareholder returns are substantial relative to market cap.

### Earnings Track Record

## Growth Analysis

Growth for STNG is a function of two variables: fleet size/composition and freight rates.

**Fleet evolution** is constructive. The company is replacing older, less efficient vessels with newbuildings that carry lower operating costs and better environmental compliance. The 10-vessel order pipeline delivers through 2029, providing gradual fleet renewal without the lumpy capex burden that historically plagued shipping companies. The long-term charter-outs (STI Lombard at $33,000/day for 5 years; STI Rambla at $30,500/day for 8 years) lock in revenue visibility through the middle of the next decade.

**Rate environment** is the dominant growth variable. The Q2 2026 TCE rate snapshot suggests a potential re-acceleration:

| Segment | Q1 2026 (97% booked) | Q2 2026 (10-16% booked) |
|---------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| LR2 Spot | $51,000/day | $101,000/day |
| MR Spot | $32,000/day | $36,500/day |
| Handymax Spot | $34,000/day | $32,000/day |

The Q2 LR2 spot rate of $101,000/day, roughly double Q1, reflects the Strait of Hormuz disruption premium. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports, announced in April 2026, directly disrupts approximately 20% of global seaborne oil trade. This creates two rate tailwinds: (1) physical rerouting of product tanker cargoes around the Cape of Good Hope, increasing ton-mile demand by 30-40% on affected routes; (2) insurance cost spikes that reduce effective fleet supply as vessels avoid the region.

If Hormuz disruptions persist (probable over the next 2-4 months given current geopolitical dynamics), STNG's LR2-heavy fleet is positioned to capture outsized earnings. Even if Q2 rates moderate from the $101,000 early booking level to $70,000-80,000, Q2 2026 could rival the best quarters of the 2022-2024 super-cycle.

The medium-term growth headwind is the global product tanker orderbook, which is expanding. New deliveries will add supply capacity that, absent sustained demand growth, pressures rates. However, tightening environmental regulations (IMO CII ratings) are effectively shrinking the competitive fleet by penalizing older, less efficient vessels, a dynamic that disproportionately benefits operators like STNG with modern, scrubber-equipped fleets.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG operates in a fragmented market with several public peers (Torm, Hafnia, Ardmore, INSW) and a large private/state-owned fleet. Its competitive advantages are:

1. **Scale**: 87 vessels make STNG one of the largest publicly listed product tanker fleets, providing operational diversification and leverage in pool arrangements.

2. **Fleet quality**: Average age of ~10.1 years with scrubber technology. Environmental regulations are progressively penalizing older vessels, creating a tailwind for efficient operators and effectively shrinking the competitive supply base.

3. **Balance sheet**: Net cash and high total liquidity are rare in cyclical shipping. Most competitors carry significant leverage, which constrains their ability to invest counter-cyclically or weather prolonged rate downturns.

4. **Tax structure**: Marshall Islands incorporation means zero income taxes, adding structural margin versus U.S.-domiciled competitors.

5. **Strategic flexibility**: The VLCC newbuilding orders represent optionality into crude oil transportation. The fixed-rate charter-outs provide income stability. The vessel sale program harvests value from aging assets. These overlapping strategies demonstrate a management team operating across multiple time horizons.

The primary competitive risk is from private and state-owned fleets that are not subject to the same shareholder return expectations and may accept below-market returns on deployed capital.

## Risk Assessment

### Cyclical Rate Exposure (HIGH)
Product tanker rates are STNG's single most important variable. The 2022-2024 super-cycle was driven by non-recurring factors (post-COVID reconfiguration, Russian sanctions, Red Sea disruptions). A full normalization of these factors would return rates to historical averages that support significantly lower earnings. The company's FY 2021 loss of $234M demonstrates the downside in a weak rate environment.

### Russian Sanctions Unwinding (MEDIUM)
Any diplomatic resolution that normalizes Russian oil trade flows would reduce ton-mile demand by shortening voyage distances. This is the core bear thesis articulated by BofA. Current geopolitical dynamics make near-term sanctions relief unlikely, but the risk is real on a 12-24 month horizon.

### Rising Orderbook (MEDIUM)
The global product tanker orderbook is growing. STNG itself is adding 10 vessels. If newbuilding deliveries outpace demand growth, fleet utilization and rates decline. Environmental scrapping of older vessels provides a partial offset, but the net supply balance is a key monitoring metric.

### Hormuz Escalation — Tail Risk (LOW PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT)
While Hormuz disruptions currently boost tanker rates, escalation to actual vessel losses, insurance market withdrawal, or prolonged physical closure would create catastrophic operational risk. STNG's fleet operates globally, but concentrated exposure to Middle East trade routes during disruptions creates downside tail risk.

### Convertible Dilution (LOW-MEDIUM)
The $375M convertible notes convert at $100.39/share. At current prices of ~$76, the conversion premium is 32%, providing meaningful headroom. If the stock appreciates above $100, dilution of approximately 3.7M shares (7.5% of current float) would occur. The low 1.75% coupon partially compensates.

### VLCC Expansion Execution (LOW-MEDIUM)
The 2 VLCC orders represent a strategic pivot into crude oil transportation. This diversifies revenue but introduces exposure to a market where STNG has limited operating history. The vessels deliver in H2 2028, providing time for management to build capability.

### Options Market Signal
No options data was provided for this analysis. The negative beta of -0.31 implies STNG's stock price moves inversely to the broad market, likely reflecting the counter-cyclical rate dynamics of tanker shipping. In the current environment of heightened geopolitical risk, this negative beta characteristic provides natural portfolio hedge value.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (Price target: $90-100)
Strait of Hormuz disruptions persist through H2 2026, sustaining product tanker rates well above FY 2025 levels. LR2 spot rates average $60,000-80,000/day for the balance of the year, generating FY 2026 EPS of $10-12. The stock re-rates to 8-9x on improved earnings visibility, reaching $90-100. The fleet renewal strategy (selling older vessels, ordering newbuildings) extends the earnings power of the fleet into the next decade. The net cash position allows opportunistic vessel acquisitions or accelerated buybacks during any rate-driven pullback.

### Bear Case (Price target: $45-55)
Hormuz tensions de-escalate rapidly, Russian sanctions are eased, and the growing orderbook puts downward pressure on product tanker rates. FY 2026 rates average below FY 2025 levels (LR2 spot $30,000-40,000/day), compressing EPS to $3-5. The stock de-rates to 10-12x trough earnings, falling to $45-55. The convertible notes add financial complexity without commensurate benefit. Even in this scenario, the net cash position and liquidity provide a substantial floor; the company survived $234M losses in FY 2021 with a far weaker balance sheet.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $88** — derived from 10x FY 2026E EPS of $8.80, assuming TCE rates averaging modestly above FY 2025 levels for the full year, reflecting partial benefit from Hormuz disruptions and continued fleet optimization. This represents approximately 16% upside from the current ~$76 price.

**Bull case target: $100** — 9x FY 2026E EPS of $11.00, assuming sustained elevated rates through H2 2026. Approximately 32% upside.

**Bear case target: $50** — 12x trough FY 2026E EPS of $4.00, assuming rapid rate normalization. Approximately 34% downside.

**Upside/downside ratio: Base 16% up / Bear 34% down = 0.47:1.** Incorporating the bull case probability (which current geopolitical dynamics support), the weighted expected return is favorable. The risk/reward improves materially on any pullback below $70.

**Timeframe**: 6-12 months. The thesis is primarily driven by H1/H2 2026 rate developments, with Q1 2026 earnings providing the first hard data point on whether Hormuz-driven rate strength translates to earnings. Newbuilding deliveries (2026-2029) provide a longer-dated structural catalyst.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **LR2 spot rates fall below $30,000/day for two consecutive quarters**, indicating sustained rate normalization that would compress earnings to levels where the current valuation is no longer cheap.
2. **Net cash position turns to net debt** (excluding the convertible notes) due to excessive fleet investment or rate-driven cash flow deterioration, removing the balance sheet margin of safety.
3. **Hormuz tensions resolve comprehensively** via diplomatic agreement that restores normal shipping patterns, combined with Russian sanctions easing, eliminating both the near-term rate catalyst and the medium-term ton-mile demand support.

## Conclusion

Scorpio Tankers offers a compelling risk-adjusted entry point for energy sector exposure with counter-cyclical characteristics. The combination of a fortress balance sheet, disciplined capital allocation (20% share count reduction, 13% dividend growth), zero tax rate, and direct exposure to Strait of Hormuz rate tailwinds makes this one of the more attractive cyclical energy setups currently available.

The stock trades at 10x trailing earnings in a normalization year, with a clear near-term catalyst (Q2 2026 LR2 rate spike) and structural support from environmental regulations favoring its young, efficient fleet. The negative beta provides portfolio diversification value during a period of elevated geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainty.

The primary risks are rate cyclicality, sanctions unwinding, and orderbook growth, all real but substantially mitigated by balance sheet strength and management's demonstrated ability to navigate the cycle. BUY with a base case target of $88.
