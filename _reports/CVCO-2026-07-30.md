## Executive Summary

Cavco Industries closed fiscal 2026 (ended March 28, 2026) with record revenue of $2,244.5M (+11.4%), record volume of 20,842 homes sold (+5.5%), and diluted EPS of $23.98 (+15.8%). Underneath those headlines the composition of growth was substantially lower quality than the print suggests. Of the $33.6M increase in consolidated pretax income, $21.8M — 64.9% — came from the financial services segment, which generates 3.9% of revenue. Management attributes the swing to higher premiums, improved underwriting, and "favorable weather in the year resulting in lower weather related insurance claims." Net incurred insurance losses fell to $25.5M in the first nine months of FY26 from $41.8M a year earlier. Financial services gross margin went from 28.9% to 58.0% for the year and to 69.4% in Q4. FY2025's $1.5M of segment pretax income was weather-depressed; FY2026's $23.3M was weather-flattered. Neither is a mid-cycle run rate.

The core factory-built housing segment, 96.1% of revenue, grew pretax income only 5.6% on 11.6% revenue growth — and roughly 40% of the segment's revenue increase ($90.5M of $224.2M) came from the American Homestar acquisition closed September 29, 2025. In the March quarter, homes sold declined 0.7% year over year, factory-built gross margin compressed 110bps to 21.2% on "higher input costs and lower home sales," and adjusting the prior-year quarter for a $10M non-cash brand-intangible charge ($0.93 of EPS), segment operating income declined roughly 7% — with the acquired business inside the current-quarter numbers. On the same adjustment, prior-year Q4 EPS was approximately $5.40 against $5.42 reported, so the headline "+21.3% EPS growth" in Q4 was closer to flat.

At $565.24 the stock trades at 23.6x FY2026 diluted EPS, 24.5-24.9x on a sensitivity that normalizes financial services pretax income to $10-14M, and ~16.3x EV/EBITDA on an EV of roughly $4.12B. Consensus estimates have been cut in every window over the past 90 days (current quarter -3.4%, next quarter -7.4%, current year -4.2%, next year -2.0%). Manufactured Housing Institute shipments were flat at ~103,000 units in both calendar 2024 and 2025 and down 0.3% year over year through November. A mid-20s multiple in a flat-volume industry with declining organic revenue and compressing core margins requires a recovery that has not appeared in the data. Set against that, the balance sheet is genuinely fortress-grade — essentially no funded debt, $236.7M cash, an undrawn $75M revolver, $232.1M of free cash flow, and operating cash flow exceeding net income by $76.9M — and HUD's June 12, 2026 proposed rule permitting chassis-free upper floors targets the exact financing constraint Cavco's own 10-K identifies as binding. That combination justifies holding, not buying, and does not support an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Cavco designs and builds factory-built homes: predominantly HUD-code manufactured homes, plus modular homes, park-model RVs, cabins, and factory-built commercial structures. The company runs 33 production lines (31 U.S., 2 in Ojinaga, Mexico) with facilities from ~79,000 to ~341,000 square feet, typically one shift five days per week, completing a home in about six production days. A plant's economic shipping radius is roughly 350 miles, which makes the regional footprint the operative competitive asset.

Distribution runs through independent distribution points in 48 states and Canada plus 92 company-owned U.S. retail stores, 57 of which sit in Texas. Approximately 77% of FY2026 factory-built home sales went to independent distributors; no single independent distributor exceeded 10% of segment revenue in any of the past three years.

The financial services segment — 3.9% of revenue but the swing factor in FY2026 profit — comprises CountryPlace Acceptance Corp. (a Fannie/Freddie approved seller-servicer, Ginnie Mae MBS issuer with FHA Title I/II direct endorsement authority, originating conforming, non-conforming and home-only chattel loans) and Standard Casualty Company (a manufactured-home P&C insurer writing primarily in Texas, Arizona, New Mexico and Nevada).

Employees total roughly 7,700, with ~7% unionized under agreements expiring February 2027 and April 2029.

FY2026 revenue exceeded the FY2023 cycle peak, but net income remains 21% below FY2023's $240.6M and gross margin sits 240bps below FY2023's 25.9%. Volume has recovered; the 2022-23 pricing and margin peak has not. Revenue per home rose 5.8% to $103,510 in FY2026; in Q4 it rose 8.9% to $105,042, which management attributes to a higher mix of sales through company-owned stores and product mix rather than underlying price realization.

### Balance sheet and cash generation

This is the strongest part of the case, and the standard screening metrics understate it. Total liabilities of $388.0M against $1,103.2M of equity produce a 0.35x liabilities-to-equity ratio that is sometimes reported as "debt/equity." Actual funded debt is de minimis: FY2026 interest expense was $0.5M, described as primarily finance-lease interest; total finance leases and other secured financings were $7.5M at December 27, 2025; the $75M Bank of America revolver (expandable to $150M) was undrawn at year-end. Working capital is +$489.3M (current assets $824.7M less current liabilities $335.4M). Cavco is net cash.

FY2026 operating cash flow was $267.5M against net income of $190.6M — a $76.9M excess and the single most favorable earnings-quality signal in the filing. Capex was $35.4M, producing $232.1M of free cash flow, a 10.3% FCF margin and a 5.3% FCF yield at the current market cap. ROE was 17.3% and ROA 12.8%.

Cash and equivalents fell from $356.2M to $236.7M, entirely through deployment rather than deterioration: +$267.5M operating, −$222.4M investing (of which $172.8M was American Homestar net of cash acquired), −$162.8M financing (predominantly buybacks).

Goodwill rose from $122.0M to $208.8M, all from the Homestar purchase, and now represents 14% of total assets, allocated entirely to factory-built housing. Purchase accounting was still provisional as of the Q3 10-Q, with lease, other-current-asset, payable and deferred-tax fair values described as not finalized and "subject to change, which could be significant."

### The quality-of-earnings adjustment

The segment arithmetic is the decisive number in this analysis:

- Factory-built housing pretax income: $221.4M FY26 vs. $209.6M FY25 = **+$11.8M (+5.6%)** on +11.6% revenue
- Financial services pretax income: $23.3M vs. $1.5M = **+$21.8M**
- Consolidated pretax: $244.7M vs. $211.1M = +$33.6M

Normalizing financial services to $10-14M of pretax income — a range, not a forecast, since Cavco discloses no mid-cycle loss ratio — implies consolidated pretax of $232-236M. At FY26's 22.1% effective tax rate and 7.946M diluted shares, that is roughly **$22.7-23.1 of EPS rather than $23.98**, moving the trailing multiple from 23.6x to 24.5-24.9x. Management credits "policy underwriting improvements" alongside weather, so some portion is durable rate-and-selection gain; working against that, the 10-K notes financial services revenue grew despite fewer policies in force, consistent with re-underwriting to a smaller, better book — margin-accretive but a premium-growth headwind.

### Earnings surprise record

Three beats and one miss over four quarters, but the miss was recent and large: the December 2025 quarter came in at $5.58 against a $6.39 estimate (−12.6%), and the stock fell from $617 to $492 in one session on January 30, 2026. The March quarter beat by 1.0% ($5.42 vs. $5.37) — a thin beat against a lowered bar. The pattern suggests deteriorating forecast visibility rather than a reliable beat cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The industry is not growing. MHI data cited in Cavco's own filings shows ~103,000 HUD-code shipments in calendar 2024 and again in calendar 2025 (after 89,000 in 2023), with calendar-2025-through-November shipments of 95,947, down 0.3% year over year. Manufactured housing was ~13.1% of new single-family homes sold in calendar 2024.

Against that backdrop, Cavco grew FY2026 volume 5.5% — but roughly 40% of factory-built revenue growth was acquired, and the March quarter's organic performance was negative. Subtracting an estimated ~$48.5M of Q4 Homestar revenue (a residual: $90.5M full-year segment contribution less the $42.0M disclosed for Q3) from $528.0M of Q4 factory-built revenue implies roughly $479M organic against $487.9M prior year, approximately −2%. The magnitude is an estimate; the direction is well supported by the disclosed −0.7% home count and +0.8% module count with the acquisition inside the quarter.

Backlog went $224M (Dec-24) → $197M (Mar-25) → $160M (Dec-25) → $195M (Mar-26). The sequential 22% Q4 rebuild is a real positive, and management specifically stated wholesale orders were up significantly versus both the prior quarter and prior-year quarter "with the bulk of that pick-up... happening in March." Two qualifications matter: the $195M presumably includes two acquired plants absent from the $197M comparison base, which would mean organic backlog declined (Cavco does not disclose backlog ex-acquisition, so this is inference); and Cavco states explicitly that distributors may cancel orders prior to production without penalty and that backlog is not considered firm until production commences. At $195M, backlog represents roughly one month of wholesale revenue — thin forward visibility.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration rather than contradicting it. All four windows are negative over 90 days: current quarter $5.75 → $5.55 (−3.4%), next quarter $6.43 → $5.96 (−7.4%), current year $25.00 → $23.95 (−4.2%), next year $30.00 → $29.40 (−2.0%). Coverage is thin (two contributing analysts), which reduces the signal weight, but the direction is uniformly negative and the next-quarter cut is the steepest.

The bull's strongest quantitative argument is the next-year consensus of $29.40, implying 22.8% growth off the current-year $23.95 and a forward P/E of 19.2x. That estimate rests on two analysts and has already been trimmed 2%. It is a hypothesis about a volume inflection, not a datapoint.

Growth investment is stepping up: capex rose to $35.4M from ~$21M, PP&E net rose $51.3M to $278.9M, and the company broke ground in Q4 on a new production facility in El Mirage, Arizona. Adding capacity into a flat-shipment industry carries fixed-cost-absorption risk, and it means FY2027 free cash flow should run below FY2026's $232.1M.

The peer table is heterogeneous and needs disaggregating. The site-built homebuilders (MTH, MHO, KBH) trade at 10-13x because they are land-inventory-intensive, leveraged, and cyclically exposed at the top of a rate cycle. The factory-built comparable — Champion Homes (SKY) at 22.0x — sits essentially where Cavco does. So Cavco is not obviously mispriced within its true comp set; it is priced at parity with the only direct scale peer, both at roughly twice the multiple of conventional homebuilders. The premium is defensible on structure: net cash versus levered peers, an affordability-shortage demand narrative, and a vertically integrated finance/insurance attachment that conventional builders lack.

What is difficult to defend is paying 24-25x normalized earnings for a business whose core segment grew pretax income 5.6% on 11.6% revenue, posted a Q4 organic volume decline, compressed factory-built gross margin 110bps, and operates in an industry with flat shipments — while consensus estimates fall. On the next-year estimate the PEG lands near 1.0, which would be reasonable if that estimate were durable; the 90-day revision direction argues it is not yet.

Analyst targets are low $575 / mean $625 / median $600 / high $700, with three analysts (one strong buy, two buy, no holds). Even the low target sits above the current price. Thin, uniformly positive coverage at a mid-20s multiple is weak independent confirmation — the targets and the price plausibly reflect the same recovery extrapolation rather than corroborating it. The situation where consensus targets function as a floor is one where a stock is cheap against rising estimates; here the stock is fully priced against falling estimates.

Absolute yield support is absent (no dividend). The offsets on the valuation side are the buyback (share count −11.0% over three years, −3.8% in FY2026, with a fresh $150M authorization approved May 18, 2026 on top of $98M remaining from the prior plan) and the balance sheet, which caps the downside multiple in a way a levered builder's does not.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** The multiple is full against fundamentals that are decelerating, and I cannot identify a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock today. The HUD rule is optionality rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Cavco is one of the three largest U.S. producers of manufactured homes by wholesale shipments. A third-party estimate (Brasada Capital's Q2 2026 letter) puts top-three production share above 86%; Cavco's own 10-K states no share figure and describes competing with "more than 30 other producers."

The competitive structure has a specific asymmetry that deserves precise naming. Clayton Homes (Berkshire Hathaway) is simultaneously Cavco's largest manufacturing competitor; via 21st Mortgage Corporation, the primary wholesale floor-plan lender to the independent distributors who buy 77% of Cavco's homes (the 10-K states those distributors "rely primarily on 21st Mortgage Corporation"); and, via 21st Mortgage and Vanderbilt, a direct competitor to CountryPlace in consumer lending. Three distinct relationships with one competitor family, all disclosed. Cavco's mitigant is internal credit substitution — its commercial loan book ($106.2M net at December 27, 2025, with FY2026 originations rising to $158.5M from $143.4M) and the forward-flow consumer loan sale agreement that lifted FY2026 loan-sale proceeds to $79.6M from $51.1M on lower originations.

Trajectory reads mixed. On share, Cavco grew volume 5.5% in a flat-shipment industry, so reported share gain is real — but largely acquired, with organic Q4 volume down. On margin, the direction is negative: consolidated gross margin 23.5% versus 25.9% in FY2023, factory-built Q4 gross margin 21.2% versus 22.3%. Part of the Q4 compression is mix arithmetic — the deliberate shift toward company-owned retail dilutes gross margin percentage while adding gross profit dollars — and part is disclosed input-cost inflation. The channel shift is a strategic choice that captures retail margin and improves customer control; it is a rational trade, and the segment operating-income decline of ~7% on an adjusted basis suggests it has not yet been net-accretive.

The regulatory backdrop is where the competitive position could change materially. Cavco's 10-K identifies the absent secondary market for home-only (chattel) loans as the industry's binding constraint: most national home-only lenders have exited; FHFA's 2025-2027 GSE Underserved Markets Plans, published December 2025, **exclude** purchases of home-only loans; MH Advantage and ChoiceHome, live since 2018, have delivered "some progress" but "meaningful positive impact in the form of increased home orders has yet to be realized." Against that, HUD published a proposed rule in the Federal Register on June 12, 2026 that would redefine "manufactured home" to permit upper floors without a permanent chassis, complementing an earlier HUD update allowing up to four units per structure; the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act would remove the 1974 chassis mandate outright, with real-property titling — routing buyers into conventional mortgage financing rather than chattel loans — as the larger prize. These are proposals and pending legislation, published after Cavco's 10-K and unaddressed in it, with safety and affordability objections in the same coverage. Cavco's own record of regulatory hopes converting to orders is poor by its own account.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** Strong level, mixed trajectory: share gains are real but largely inorganic, margins are compressing, and the most important structural dependency runs through a competitor's affiliate.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer financing structure (highest-consequence, disclosed, unresolved).** The chattel-loan market is the industry's ceiling. QM thresholds allow APRs up to 6.5 percentage points over APOR, and some loans still fall outside safe harbor. Cavco notes it is aware of "only a small number of loans currently being securitized" under the Ginnie Mae FHA Title I program. This has not moved in seven-plus years.

**Insurance weather, in both directions.** FY2025's $1.5M and FY2026's $23.3M of financial services pretax income bracket the range. Policies carry ~$0.4M maximum coverage per claim with $0.15M ceded, leaving $0.25M net; catastrophe reinsurance attaches above $4.0M per occurrence up to $90M aggregate. "Substantially all of our assumed reinsurance is with one entity" — a single-counterparty credit concentration for which Cavco remains obligated if the reinsurer fails to perform. Consensus appears to be carrying a partial normalization (current-year EPS estimate of $23.95 versus FY2026 actual $23.98) but not an adverse-weather year.

**Texas concentration.** 57 of 92 company-owned retail stores (62%); plants in Austin, Fort Worth (two lines), Lancaster, Seguin and Waco; 47% of consumer loans receivable principal at December 27, 2025 (down from 54%); most Standard Casualty policies. A Texas employment, housing or catastrophe event hits manufacturing, retail, lending and insurance simultaneously.

**Contingent repurchase obligations.** Maximum contingent obligation under distributor floor-plan repurchase agreements was $141M at March 28, 2026 (up from $133M "as the result of an increased number of units under these programs"), against a $3.7M recorded reserve. These are gross ceilings before resale value on declining-price 18-24 month terms, not expected losses — but they are the leading indicator if distributor solvency deteriorates, and 21st Mortgage's floor-plan appetite is the mechanism.

**Commercial loan credit.** $106.2M net with a disclosed 10% single-counterparty concentration (secured), all classified performing against a $0.375M allowance. The fair value of the book ($99.8M) sits $6.5M below carrying value, a gap that widened in dollar terms from $10.5M-on-a-smaller-book a year earlier — worth watching, though the direction of the gap narrowed on a percentage basis.

**Goodwill and provisional purchase accounting.** $208.8M of goodwill (14% of assets), all in factory-built housing, with an explicit 10-K impairment risk factor and Homestar purchase accounting still provisional as of Q3.

**Mexico/tariff.** Two Ojinaga production lines dependent on materials shipped from Presidio, Texas. The 10-K states plainly: "We may be unable to pass through additional tariff costs to our customers through price increases."

**Labor.** The February 2027 collective bargaining agreement expiry falls inside the next twelve months.

**Macro.** The consumer backdrop is a live headwind. Manufactured housing is partly counter-cyclical (affordability substitution) and partly pro-cyclical (chattel-borrower credit quality is the most rate- and employment-sensitive in housing). Both effects are live and pull in opposite directions. [Specific rate and home-value figures in the source-less draft removed as unverified — no macro data appears in the research brief.]

**Governance.** Staggered board, blank-check preferred, procedural requirements on shareholder proposals, which the 10-K itself acknowledges "may have the effect of entrenching the current management." An 8-K filed October 28, 2025 carried Item 5.02 (officer/director departure or election) paired with Item 7.01 (Reg FD); the filing content could not be retrieved, leaving the nature of that change unverified. The pairing with Reg FD suggests a planned announcement rather than an abrupt departure, but this is unconfirmed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 54.5% against 47.8% trailing one-year realized — rich by 6.7 points, in the 76th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (54.5% at one month versus 42.7% at six months). The nearest expiry (August 21) prices a ±10.9% move. ATM IV rose 5.4 points in the week to July 30 while the price fell 0.4%, meaning the market added premium for event risk without directional conviction. Put/call skew is balanced at +0.4% near-term, drifting to −1.5% at three months and −3.4% at six months; put/call volume ratio is 0.50 and open-interest ratio 0.02. The read: the market is pricing meaningful uncertainty into the July 30 Q1 FY2027 release without a bearish tilt, consistent with genuine two-sided uncertainty rather than informed positioning. Short interest of 7.0% of float and 3.8 days to cover is unremarkable — this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The practical implication is that entry into the print pays an elevated volatility premium for information that arrives in days.

**Risk factors score: 5.0.** Most sector-level risks are priced. The specific items that are not clearly priced are the insurance normalization path, the Texas four-way concentration, and the imminent earnings binary.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $700

Manufactured housing is the only structurally advantaged answer to a documented U.S. affordability shortage, and Cavco is one of three producers with national scale. HUD's June 2026 proposed chassis rule and the ROAD to Housing Act attack the exact constraint — chattel financing — that has capped industry volume at ~100,000 units for a decade; real-property titling at scale would move buyers into conventional mortgages and lift the demand ceiling permanently. Management's March order acceleration (backlog +22% sequentially) is the first evidence of inflection. American Homestar is "exceeding expectations for tangible synergies" against an externally reported $10M target, El Mirage adds capacity into that inflection, and the balance sheet — net cash, undrawn revolver, $232M FCF — funds acquisition, greenfield and a shrinking float simultaneously. Next-year consensus of $29.40 at 24x supports $700. A single insurance-normalizing year is a rounding item against that trajectory.

### Bear Case → $410 (−27%)

FY2026's earnings growth was weather and M&A. Normalize financial services to $10-14M of pretax income and EPS is $22.7-23.1, putting the stock at 24-25x in an industry whose shipments are flat and whose most recent quarter showed a ~2% organic revenue decline, a 0.7% volume decline, and 110bps of core gross-margin compression on input costs. Estimates are falling in every window, and the steepest cut (−7.4%) is the nearest quarter. Consensus already assumes no insurance repeat; a normal weather year plus continued organic volume erosion and mid-22% gross margin puts FY2027 EPS near $20-21, and a de-rating toward 18-19x — still a premium to conventional builders — yields $360-400. The January 30 precedent (−20% in one session on a 12.6% EPS miss) establishes the reaction function. Capacity is being added into a flat market, creating fixed-cost absorption risk, and FY2027 FCF steps down as El Mirage capex lands. The regulatory catalyst is a proposal in an industry whose own filings document seven years of GSE hopes that never converted.

### Assessment

The bear case is better supported by current data; the bull case is better supported by structure and optionality. Neither dominates on a twelve-month view. The balance sheet caps how bad the bear outcome can get — an unlevered, net-cash producer with 17.3% ROE does not de-rate to 12x — and the falling estimates cap how quickly the bull case can be underwritten. The evidence supports holding rather than initiating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate. Q1 FY2027 results (quarter ended June 27, 2026) release July 30, 2026 with the call July 31. This is the largest single information gap in the analysis and it resolves within days, with the options market pricing a ±10.9% move.

**Base case: $587 (+3.8%).** FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $23.95 as financial services normalizes and Homestar synergies plus ~3-4% annual share-count reduction offset it; the multiple holds at 23-24x on oligopoly structure, net cash and regulatory optionality. 24.5x × $23.95 = $587.

**Bull case: $700 (+24%).** Next-year EPS of $29-30 delivered on a genuine volume inflection plus HUD rule progress, at 23-24x. Matches the high analyst target.

**Bear case: $410 (−27%).** FY2027 EPS of $20-21 on insurance normalization plus continued organic volume decline and mid-22% gross margin, at 19-20x. The 52-week low is $397.38.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Organic volume inflection.** Homes sold up year over year in Q1 FY2027 (against 5,027 in the March quarter and with Homestar now in the base for a full year), combined with backlog above $215M — i.e., above the December 2024 level rather than merely rebuilt sequentially.
2. **Estimate revision turn.** Current-year and next-quarter consensus stabilizing or rising for two consecutive months, reversing the −3.4%/−7.4% cuts. This is the cleanest single trigger.
3. **Regulatory conversion or a price reset.** HUD's chassis rule finalized (not proposed), or ROAD Act provisions enacted with real-property titling language — or the stock re-rating to the low-$470s, where the multiple on normalized $22.7-23.1 EPS falls to ~20-21x and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. **Factory-built gross margin below 21.0%** for two consecutive quarters (versus 21.2% in Q4 FY26 and 22.3% prior year), signaling that input-cost inflation and mix are structurally compressing the core segment rather than cyclically.
2. **A goodwill impairment charge** against the $208.8M balance, or a material adverse true-up on finalization of Homestar purchase accounting.
3. **Contingent repurchase obligation exceeding ~$165M** with a rising reserve, or a disclosed reduction in 21st Mortgage floor-plan availability — either would indicate distributor channel stress transmitting to wholesale demand.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 5.75, down from 6.0.** The score trajectory has declined for four consecutive readings (6.7 → 6.7 → 6.1 → 6.0 → 5.75), and the new data confirms rather than reverses it. The specific confirmations are the segment decomposition showing 64.9% of FY2026 pretax profit growth coming from a 3.9%-of-revenue insurance business on favorable weather; the Q4 core-segment adjusted operating income decline of ~7% with the acquisition inside the quarter; and negative consensus revisions in all four forward windows, steepest in the nearest quarter. Growth prospects (5.0) and valuation (4.5) continue to carry the decline; financial health (8.0) is the offset and is genuinely excellent — the screening representation of leverage materially understates it, since actual funded debt is under 0.01x equity.

The stock is 20.7% below its January 22 high of $713, but that drawdown has a fundamental explanation: the January 30 Q3 report missed by 12.6% and the stock fell 20% in a session. This is not a case of price weakness disconnected from fundamentals; the two are aligned. Nor is it a case where the market is obviously wrong — three analysts with uniformly positive ratings and targets from $575 to $700 are already carrying the bull view, which makes their targets weak independent confirmation rather than a floor.

What would change the call is measurable and near: organic volume turning positive, estimate revisions inflecting, or a price reset into the low $470s. What would break it is core margin sliding below 21% or channel-credit stress showing up in the repurchase obligation. Until one of those appears, paying 24-25x normalized earnings for flat industry volume, declining organic revenue and compressing core margins — while an unhedged earnings binary sits days away at 54.5% implied volatility — offers no edge in either direction.
