**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Perrigo is a pure-play consumer self-care company (store brands plus European OTC brands) that has entered a financial and governance crisis. The stock has fallen 57.5% over the trailing twelve months, from roughly $25.66 in June 2025 to $10.90, and sits 62% below its 52-week high of $28.44. Three discrete shocks drove the decline: an August 2025 gap-down (~$25 to $22), a November 5, 2025 collapse (~$19 to $14) coinciding with the infant-formula strategic-review announcement and a securities class action, and a February-March 2026 leg down to single digits following the FY2025 10-K disclosing a $1.43B net loss. The June 8, 2026 forced resignation of CEO Patrick Lockwood-Taylor for a code-of-conduct violation is the most recent blow.

The fundamental picture is one of sustained deterioration. The company has posted net losses every year from 2021 through 2025, with the 2025 loss of $1.43B driven by impairments. Q1 2026 brought a 7.2% sales decline to $969.2M, 400bps of gross-margin compression to 33.6%, a $330.8M goodwill impairment, a GAAP loss of $2.87/share, and, most critically, operating cash flow of negative $113.6M. Against this, Perrigo continues to pay a ~$160M annual dividend yielding roughly 11%, funded by asset sales and a $427.6M revolver draw. The dividend is the central tension in the equity story, and the math does not currently support it from operations.

There is a real turnaround thesis underneath the distress: an improving Specialty Care segment (Opill, ellaOne), active portfolio simplification (Rx, Rare Diseases, and Dermacosmetics all divested; Infant Formula and Oral Care under review), cost-out programs targeting $80-100M of run-rate savings, a low-beta defensive demand base, and a favorable (if appealed) federal tax ruling. But the path is narrow: $3.63B of debt, three credit-rating actions toward downgrade, ongoing impairment risk that management itself flags as one discount-rate tick away, multiple active litigations, and an interim-leadership gap arriving mid-turnaround. We rate PRGO AVOID.

## Company Overview

Perrigo (Dublin-incorporated since a 2013 inversion via Elan; North American HQ in Grand Rapids, MI) is one of the originators of the U.S. OTC market and the leading store-brand/private-label self-care provider in North America. In Europe it sells branded products (Compeed, ellaOne, Solpadeine, Jungle Formula, ACO). The model is to use store brands to generate cash that funds higher-margin, higher-growth brands, leveraging supply-chain scale across both. No single product exceeds 5% of revenue, but one customer represents ~12.9% of consolidated net sales.

As of Q1 2026, the company reorganized into three segments from a prior geographic structure: Self Care (~56% of sales: upper respiratory, digestive health, healthy lifestyle, pain & sleep), Specialty Care (~21%: skin health, women's health), and Infant Formula (~9%: the troubled Gateway/GoodStart business), with All Other (~13%: oral care plus the now-divested dermacosmetics). The portfolio is being actively shrunk: the Rx business went to Altaris (discontinued operations), HRA Rare Diseases was sold, Dermacosmetics closed April 30, 2026 (~€332.6M, mostly cash), and strategic reviews of both Infant Formula and Oral Care are underway.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is sustained deterioration culminating in a crisis-level Q1 2026.

**Profitability.** Net income has been negative for five consecutive years: -$69M (2021), -$141M (2022), -$13M (2023), -$172M (2024), and -$1.43B (2025). The 2025 figure was driven by impairments; the 10-K cites $1,542M of asset impairments over the trailing three years. This is a structural inability to convert revenue into GAAP profit, not a single bad quarter against a strong base.

**Q1 2026 (vs Q1 2025).** Net sales fell to $969.2M from $1,043.9M, driven by ~9.9% lower consumption (weak U.S. and European cough/cold/flu season plus retailer destocking). Gross margin compressed to 33.6% from 37.6%. A goodwill impairment produced an operating loss of -$372.3M and a net loss of -$398.6M. The accumulated deficit widened to -$4.08B. Operating cash flow was negative, worse than the -$64.5M of the prior-year quarter, which is the single most important data point in the filing given the dividend obligation.

**Segment Q1 trends.** Self Care operating margin fell from 18.4% to 12.6% on lower volume. Specialty Care improved from 21.1% to 26.7% on Opill/ellaOne momentum and lower ad spend, the clearest healthy area. Infant Formula swung to an operating loss (12.1% to -8.3%).

**Balance sheet and leverage (3/28/2026).** Total debt ~$3.63B against cash of $357M (down from $531M at year-end 2025). Total equity fell to $2.50B from $2.94B in a single quarter due to the loss. Goodwill plus indefinite intangibles stood at $1.71B and definite-lived intangibles at $2.26B, a large base still exposed to further write-downs. Covenants (max first-lien secured net leverage 3.00x, min interest coverage 3.00x) were reported in compliance as of 3/28/2026, but the margin of safety is thin given the earnings trajectory.

**Capital allocation tension.** The ~$0.29/quarter dividend is being paid despite negative operating cash flow and five years of net losses, funded effectively by asset sales and the revolver. The company drew $427.6M on its revolver in Q1 2026 to repay Term Loan A. No buybacks since 2023. Irish law conditions dividends on distributable reserves; the company created $4.9B of reserves in 2023 specifically to keep paying. Mechanically the dividend can continue, but doing so from a shrinking cash base and divestiture proceeds is value-destructive if the core does not stabilize.

**Tax wildcard (positive).** A September 2025 District Court opinion predominantly sided with Perrigo in the long-running IRS transfer-pricing dispute (omeprazole/Israeli affiliate). The DOJ appealed in March 2026 and Perrigo cross-appealed. A favorable final outcome could yield refunds (~$113M sought), but a new February 2026 NOPA on Perrigo Ireland transfer pricing (2016-2019) tempers the upside.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is negative. Some of the consumption decline is cyclical and sector-wide (a weak cough/cold/flu season hit competitors too) and some is company-specific (infant-formula share loss, oral-care declines). Estimate revisions confirm the soft trajectory rather than an inflection: current-year EPS consensus has been revised down 6.1% over 90 days to $2.11, and current-quarter estimates down 9.5% over 90 days to $0.36. The 30-day revision figures are flat, which signals stabilization at a lower level rather than recovery. Estimates are drifting down, not up.

The genuine growth pocket is Specialty Care, where Opill (the first OTC birth-control pill in the U.S.) and ellaOne are gaining traction and margins improved. This is real, but at ~21% of sales it cannot carry a 7%-declining consolidated top line. Management cites ongoing share gains in core U.S./European self-care driven by innovation, which is a partial offset to the volume weakness.

Portfolio simplification is intended to produce a leaner, higher-quality Self Care plus Specialty Care company. The strategic logic is defensible, but divesting toward growth is a multi-year project requiring stable leadership and balance-sheet capacity, both of which are currently impaired. Cost-out programs (Supply Chain Reinvention ~$157M, Project Energize ~$167M, plus a new Operational Enhancement Program targeting $80-100M and a ~7% workforce reduction) support margins but do not create revenue growth.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline valuation looks cheap: forward P/E of ~4.6x on the ~$2.11 current-year consensus, and an ~11% dividend yield. Both figures require heavy discounting.

The forward P/E is built on adjusted earnings that have diverged enormously from GAAP. Adjusted "PSU-OI" operating income ran in the ~$470-613M range on an adjusted basis in 2025 while GAAP net income was -$1.43B. The adjustments (impairments, restructuring) have recurred for years, which undermines treating them as non-recurring. A 4.6x multiple on adjusted EPS that the market does not believe is the same thing as a much higher multiple on sustainable earnings power. The dividend yield is similarly suspect: an 11% yield on a payout funded by asset sales and revolver draws against negative operating cash flow is a yield the market is pricing as likely to be cut, not as income to capitalize.

Peer comparison is unhelpful here; the supplied healthcare comparable (BBIO, a development-stage biopharma with negative earnings) is not a relevant valuation anchor for a consumer self-care/specialty-pharma hybrid. Against the consumer-health peer set (Kenvue, Haleon, Bayer), Perrigo's discount is justified by leverage, leadership crisis, and impairment risk rather than being a mispricing.

Analyst targets (low $14, mean $16.50, high $20) sit 28-83% above the current price, implying the sell side sees recovery value. But the guardrail here matters: this is a distressed name with falling estimates, not an inflection stock with rising ones, so consensus targets are not floors. They reflect a turnaround that has not yet shown evidence of working, and they have been trailing the stock down for a year. The valuation score reflects genuine asset value (a divestible portfolio, the tax optionality, real Specialty Care growth) capped by the fact that the cheapness is a function of distress, not a clear catalyst-driven mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Perrigo competes against branded CPG/pharma (Kenvue/J&J, Haleon, Bayer) and store-brand/generic players. Its differentiation is offering solutions across price/value tiers: store brands win during consumer trade-down, brands provide margin. The store-brand tier should be a relative beneficiary during consumer trade-down, and the low 0.52 beta reflects the defensive demand base.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Management reports core self-care share gains driven by innovation (a positive), but two segments are losing ground. Infant Formula faces a structural challenge: an FDA warning letter (Aug 2023), repeated 483 observations (most recently Feb 2026), and Operation Stork Speed plus FDA personal-importation forbearance, all of which increase competition against a sub-scale Perrigo position that has swung to operating losses. Oral Care is declining and under review. The net read is a market leader in store brands defending its core while shedding losing positions: a stable-to-slightly-improving core obscured by company-specific erosion in the segments being divested. The trajectory does not warrant a high competitive score, but the defensive franchise prevents a low one.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is among the most concentrated and binary in the healthcare space.

1. **Dividend/liquidity.** Negative operating cash flow, an 11% yield, revolver drawdowns, and credit downgrades make a dividend cut a live possibility. A cut would remove a key support for the shares but is arguably prudent. This is the central binary.
2. **Leverage and credit downgrades.** Ratings Ba3 (negative)/B+ (stable)/BB (negative), with S&P cutting to B+ from BB- in March 2026 and the 2030 notes' coupon stepping up to the 5.150% cap after a Moody's downgrade. Further downgrades raise borrowing and vendor-financing costs into a declining-revenue base.
3. **Impairment risk not over.** Management's own sensitivity disclosure is unusually candid: Women's Health fair value exactly equals carrying value, and a 25bps discount-rate increase would trigger a Skin Health impairment. The depressed market cap itself pressures the control-premium math. The Q1 charge wiped out Infant Formula and Oral Care goodwill, but Specialty Care remains exposed.
4. **Governance crisis.** The June 8, 2026 forced CEO resignation compounds chronic leadership churn (an unusually high cadence of Item 5.02 departure filings across 2024-2026) and arrives as the company executes a high-stakes turnaround. The departing CEO is a named defendant in the November 2025 securities class action.
5. **Litigation.** Securities class action (Tanner French v. Perrigo); generic-drug price-fixing MDL (Humana bellwether Sept 2026, Kroger/Cigna 2027-28); ~300 talc lawsuits; ranitidine/acetaminophen/phenylephrine (largely won/dismissed but on appeal); PLD/Conry antitrust over Gateway.
6. **Tariffs.** Material 2025 COGS impact (China sourcing); pending Section 232 pharma investigation; a ~$21M tariff-refund receivable benefited Q1 gross profit but is subject to validation.
7. **Customer concentration.** One customer at ~12.9% of sales.

### Options Market Signal

No options data was provided. Short interest is the relevant positioning read: 16.7% of float short, 6.0 days to cover, ~15.1M shares short. This is elevated, institutional bearish positioning that validates the fundamental concern, but at this level it also creates squeeze risk against any positive binary (dividend reaffirmation with a credible coverage plan, a favorable tax-appeal ruling, or an above-expectation Infant Formula/Oral Care divestiture).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
A leaner Self Care plus Specialty Care company emerges from the divestiture program. Specialty Care continues compounding (Opill/ellaOne) at 25%+ margins. Cost-out programs deliver $80-100M of run-rate savings. Divestiture proceeds (Dermacosmetics ~€332.6M already in, Infant Formula and Oral Care potentially next) reduce the $3.6B debt load, arresting the downgrade cycle. The federal tax appeal resolves favorably, unlocking ~$113M of refunds. New permanent leadership stabilizes the core, the dividend is sustained or rebased to a covered level, and the stock re-rates toward analyst targets ($14-20) on normalized adjusted earnings power of ~$2.10-2.40. Bull case ~$18 (~65% upside), roughly the upper-middle of the analyst range, contingent on debt reduction and leadership stabilization both succeeding.

### Bear Case
The dividend is cut, removing the yield-buyer support and triggering forced selling by income holders. Consumption remains weak, the core Self Care segment fails to recover margin, and further impairments (Specialty Care is one discount-rate tick from a write-down) deepen GAAP losses. Credit ratings fall further, raising financing costs against declining revenue. Divestiture proceeds are insufficient or delayed, leverage stays elevated, and the interim-leadership vacuum extends. Litigation (securities class action, price-fixing MDL) produces a material settlement. Bear case ~$6-7 (~35-45% downside), consistent with a dividend cut plus continued operational deterioration.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced rather than favorable, with the path to the bull case requiring multiple independent things to break right (debt reduction, leadership, dividend coverage, consumption recovery, tax win) while the bear case requires only the dividend and the core to disappoint.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID, not an active short.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit constructively if: (a) the dividend is cut or rebased to a level covered by operating cash flow, removing the single largest overhang and signaling capital discipline; (b) Infant Formula and/or Oral Care divestiture proceeds are applied to debt reduction, bringing total debt meaningfully below $3.0B; (c) a permanent CEO with a credible turnaround mandate is named and core Self Care volume stabilizes (consolidated organic sales growth returning to flat-to-positive for two consecutive quarters); and (d) estimate revisions turn positive after four quarters of downward drift.

**Timeline.** The headwinds (leverage, leadership vacuum, weak consumption, impairment exposure) are likely to persist 12-18 months. The natural reassessment points are the next earnings release (which should clarify the dividend stance and any new leadership decisions), the Humana price-fixing bellwether trial in September 2026, and progress on the Infant Formula/Oral Care strategic reviews.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** A dividend cut is the most plausible negative catalyst and could come at any quarterly decision point given negative operating cash flow. A further credit downgrade or a Specialty Care impairment (which management flags as a 25bps move away) would also pressure the stock. None of these has a fixed, dated trigger.

**Timing window.** The catalysts are condition-driven rather than calendar-driven. A dividend cut could materialize over the next 1-2 quarters but is not scheduled; the securities-class-action and price-fixing timelines extend into late 2026 and 2027-28. This is a diffuse-catalyst setup, which raises carry cost relative to the probability of a near-term sharp move.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is elevated and crowded. Any positive binary (dividend reaffirmation with a coverage plan, favorable tax-appeal ruling, or a higher-than-expected divestiture price) could force a sharp squeeze. The stock has already fallen 57.5% over twelve months, so much of the bear thesis is priced.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data was provided to confirm the bearish thesis through put skew or IV. The short interest indicates meaningful borrow demand and likely elevated cost-to-borrow, which adds to carry.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case downside to ~$6-7 (~35-45%) against squeeze risk toward $14-18 on a positive binary (+30% to +65%). With the stock already down 57.5% and short interest crowded, the remaining downside is limited relative to the upside risk of a squeeze. The risk/reward is below 2:1 and the catalyst is undated.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bear thesis is largely correct, but short interest is elevated, the stock is already near multi-year lows, and the negative catalysts lack firm dates while positive binaries (dividend hold, tax win, divestiture) could squeeze. Avoid owning it; do not short it here.

## Conclusion

Perrigo offers a superficially attractive 4.6x forward multiple and 11% yield, but both metrics rest on adjusted earnings the market does not believe and a dividend funded by asset sales and revolver draws against negative operating cash flow. Five consecutive years of net losses, a $1.43B 2025 loss, a Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $2.87/share, $3.63B of debt with ratings actions all pointing toward downgrade, candidly disclosed impairment risk in Specialty Care, and a CEO ousted for cause mid-turnaround constitute a distressed profile that the defensive portfolio and Specialty Care growth do not offset. The turnaround thesis (portfolio simplification, cost-out, tax optionality) is plausible but requires multiple independent things to break right against a narrow path.

We rate PRGO **AVOID**. The valuation discount reflects distress rather than mispricing, estimate revisions are drifting down rather than inflecting up, and the binary risks (dividend, impairment, leadership, litigation) skew the near-term distribution to the downside. It is not a clean short given crowded 16.7% short interest and the squeeze risk embedded in the dividend and divestiture binaries. We would reassess only on a covered/rebased dividend, debt reduction below $3.0B from divestiture proceeds, a permanent CEO, and core-volume stabilization.
