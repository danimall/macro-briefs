## Executive Summary

AAON is in the early stages of a structural transformation from a premium commercial HVAC manufacturer into a dual-platform company with dominant data center cooling exposure. Q1 2026 results confirmed this shift: revenue surged 54.3% YoY to $497M, guidance was raised from 18-20% to 40-45% growth for FY2026, and total backlog hit $2.13B (1.5x trailing annual revenue). The stock responded with a 42% single-day gain on May 7, 2026, moving from ~$98 to $129.

The investment tension is straightforward: the demand story is among the best in mid-cap industrials, but the valuation at ~59x estimated FY2026 earnings already prices substantial execution. Gross margins remain compressed (25.1% in Q1 vs. 33% in FY2024), cash flow is only beginning to recover from a year of near-zero OCF, the balance sheet carries $425M in revolver debt, and three hyperscaler customers represent >30% of revenue. The stock is 10% below its May 2026 high of $149 and 116% above its August 2025 low of $62. At current levels, the risk/reward favors patience over immediate entry.

## Company Overview

AAON designs and manufactures semi-custom and custom HVAC equipment for commercial buildings, along with high-performance cooling systems for hyperscale data centers. The company operates from facilities in Tulsa, OK; Longview, TX; Memphis, TN; Parkville, MO; and Redmond, OR through three segments:

- **AAON Oklahoma** (55.6% of FY2025 revenue): Commercial rooftop units, air handling units, controls, and aftermarket parts
- **AAON Coil Products** (22.6%): Coils and BASX liquid cooling products manufactured from Longview, TX
- **BASX** (21.9%): Custom data center cooling solutions for hyperscale customers (Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, QTS, Applied Digital)

The BASX segment, acquired in 2021, has become the primary growth engine. By Q1 2026, BASX-branded revenue represents approximately 46% of total sales, up from 16.5% in FY2024.

### Backlog Provides Exceptional Visibility

Total backlog reached $2.13B at March 31, 2026, up 107% YoY. BASX-branded backlog of $1.62B alone equals 3.5x FY2025 BASX segment revenue. This provides 12-15 months of revenue visibility at current run rates — unusual for any industrial company.

### Margin Dynamics — The Central Question

Gross margins compressed from 33.1% (FY2024) to 26.7% (FY2025) to 25.1% (Q1 2026). Drivers include: ERP implementation disruptions, sub-optimal overhead absorption at new/expanded facilities, the mix shift toward lower-margin data center projects, and refrigerant transition costs. Management guides 27-28% for FY2026.

Operating margins: 17.4% (FY2024) → 10.1% (FY2025) → 11.5% (Q1 2026). Early recovery is visible but the trajectory back toward mid-teens depends on volume leverage at Memphis and Longview.

### Cash Flow Recovery Underway

Operating cash flow collapsed to $534K in FY2025 (from $192.5M in FY2024) due to working capital build: $167M receivables increase, $112M contract assets, $74M inventory. Q1 2026 showed $34M OCF — the best quarter since Q3 2024. If FY2026 revenue reaches $2B+ at 27-28% gross margins and ~13% operating margin, normalized OCF power is $250-300M annually.

### Balance Sheet Strain

The revolver balance grew from $76.5M (Dec 2024) to $425.2M (Mar 2026). The credit facility was expanded to $600M. Leverage at 1.77x (vs. 3.0x covenant limit) provides headroom, but the $190M capex guide for 2026 combined with ongoing working capital demands means debt reduction is unlikely near-term. D/E ratio of 1.88 is elevated for a building products company.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Data Center Cooling Secular Tailwind.** Total data center construction spending expanded ~240% between 2022-2025. AI/HPC workloads are driving the transition from air cooling to liquid cooling, which BASX's CDU platform (supporting >100kW rack densities) directly addresses. The $1.62B BASX backlog provides 2+ years of growth visibility even without new orders.

**2. Capacity Coming Online.** Memphis (BASX production), expanded Longview, and expanded Redmond are all ramping. The 53% headcount increase (3,856 to 5,897 employees in one year) demonstrates the scale of investment. As these facilities mature, fixed-cost absorption improves margins.

**3. AAON-Branded Recovery.** Commercial HVAC showed +41.6% growth in Q1 2026 after declining in FY2025. The replacement cycle (average HVAC unit lifespan of 20 years) and new refrigerant regulations create durable demand.

Estimate revisions confirm the acceleration: next quarter EPS estimates rose 28.8% over 90 days, current year estimates rose 13.9%, and next year estimates rose 5.5%. The 30-day acceleration in current year (+13.3%) and next quarter (+9.3%) suggests consensus is still catching up.

## Valuation Assessment

At $133.73 with an $11B market cap, AAON trades at:
- 94x trailing EPS ($1.29 — trough-year earnings)
- 59x FY2026E EPS ($2.26 consensus)
- 41x FY2027E EPS ($3.29 consensus)
- ~5.2x FY2026E revenue ($2.0-2.1B)

**Peer comparison is unfavorable on traditional metrics.** Building products peers trade at a median 22.5x P/E. AAON trades at 4.2x the peer median on trailing earnings, and 2.6x on FY2026 estimates. The premium is justified by: (a) 40-45% revenue growth vs. peers growing 0-10%, (b) data center exposure that peers lack, and (c) $2.13B backlog visibility.

**Growth-adjusted valuation:** FY2027 PEG ratio of approximately 1.0x (41x P/E on ~40% earnings growth into 2027) is reasonable. But this assumes margin recovery progresses as guided and backlog converts on schedule.

**The problem:** the stock already priced in much of the Q1 beat. At the pre-earnings price of ~$98, the stock traded at 43x FY2026E EPS — already a premium. The 36% appreciation since then moved the stock from "attractively priced for the inflection" to "fairly priced for execution." There is limited margin of safety if margins disappoint or hyperscaler capex cycles slow.

## Competitive Landscape

AAON occupies a differentiated position in both its markets:

**Commercial HVAC:** Competes against Lennox, Trane (Ingersoll Rand), York (Johnson Controls), Carrier, and Daikin. AAON differentiates through mass semi-customization (building to order vs. catalog products), an independent rep network, and premium engineering supported by the Norman Asbjornson Innovation Center. Market share is small (<5% of addressable) but growing, and the model commands premium pricing.

**Data Center Cooling:** Competes against Vertiv, STULZ, Munters, Silent Aire (Johnson Controls), Nortek, and Modine. BASX's advantage is purpose-built liquid cooling capability for the highest-density AI racks, relationships with all major hyperscalers (Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud), and integrated design-to-installation capability. The backlog suggests customers view BASX as a primary rather than secondary supplier.

The competitive position is improving: data center cooling share is expanding rapidly, new refrigerant regulations favor R&D-intensive manufacturers, and the NAIC testing facility creates a certification moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (High).** Three customers exceed 10% of revenue. Given BASX's hyperscaler focus, loss or delay by any single customer could materially impact quarterly results. Microsoft, AWS, and Google all have demonstrated willingness to shift project timelines.

**Execution Risk (Elevated).** AAON is simultaneously scaling Memphis, implementing ERP, growing headcount 53%, and managing $2.13B in backlog. The company has never operated at this scale. ERP disruptions already cost meaningful margin in FY2025.

**Margin Recovery Not Guaranteed.** The mix shift toward BASX (lower-margin project-based work with long payment cycles) may structurally prevent return to FY2024's 33% gross margins. Management's 27-28% guide requires capacity absorption improvement that depends on sustained volume.

**Balance Sheet/Cash Flow.** $425M revolver balance with $190M capex ahead and working capital demands means free cash flow likely remains negative or break-even in 2026. Any slowdown in collections or project delays could strain the credit facility.

**Macro Sensitivity.** A 55-60x forward P/E stock is sensitive to discount rate expectations. Any perception of data center capex moderation would hit the multiple.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($160-175, +20-30%)

- FY2026 revenue reaches guided $2.0-2.1B with gross margins recovering to 28%+
- FY2027 earnings power reaches $3.50+ as operating leverage materializes at scale
- Data center cooling demand remains secular (hyperscaler capex budgets confirmed through 2028)
- 41x on $3.50 EPS = $143; 45x = $158; re-rate toward pure-play data center multiples (50x+) = $175+
- Short covering (10% SI, 8.3 days to cover) amplifies any positive catalyst

### Bear Case ($85-100, -25-37%)

- Margin recovery stalls at 25-26% as BASX mix remains below expectations
- Hyperscaler capex cycle moderates (even a pause in order timing would compress backlog)
- Working capital drains exceed revolver capacity, requiring equity dilution or growth slowdown
- Multiple compresses to 30-35x forward on any whiff of cyclicality
- 30x on $2.26 FY2026E EPS = $68; 35x on $3.00 revised FY2027E = $105

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $105-115 range (35-40x FY2027E EPS) providing margin of safety
- Q2 2026 gross margins reach 28%+ confirming recovery trajectory
- Free cash flow turns meaningfully positive ($50M+ quarterly) confirming working capital normalization

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margins fail to improve from Q1's 25.1% for two consecutive quarters
- Backlog declines sequentially by >10% in any quarter
- Major hyperscaler customer loss or project cancellation disclosed
- Revolver exceeds $500M without corresponding cash flow improvement

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026). If margins recover to 27%+ on continued revenue growth, the investment case strengthens materially. If margins remain at 25% or below despite volume growth, the "temporary investment year" thesis requires revision.

## Conclusion

AAON is a high-quality industrial company executing a structural transformation into data center cooling at the right moment. The demand is real ($2.13B backlog), the competitive position is improving (relationships with all major hyperscalers), and Q1 2026 confirmed the growth thesis that the market had been skeptical of during FY2025's trough.

The challenge is price. At $133.73 and 59x FY2026E earnings, the market has already recognized the transformation. The stock is pricing for execution, leaving limited room for stumbles during a period of maximum operational complexity (ERP rollout, Memphis ramp, 53% headcount growth, $425M debt). Consensus analyst targets ($120-$154, median $150) provide only modest upside from current levels.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth orientation. The thesis warrants monitoring for entry on pullback or margin confirmation, rather than chasing a stock that has appreciated 116% from its trough in 9 months. Patient investors should target the $105-115 range for a position, or wait for Q2 2026 results to confirm margin trajectory before paying current levels.
