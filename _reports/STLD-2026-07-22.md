**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Steel Dynamics is one of North America's largest and lowest-cost steel producers, built on an electric-arc-furnace (EAF) model that consumes internally sourced recycled scrap. The company is riding a sharp cyclical recovery in 2026 driven by the 50% Section 232 steel tariff, which has restored domestic pricing power. Q2 2026 net income reached $534M ($3.69 diluted EPS) on $6.1B revenue and $921M adjusted EBITDA, up from $2.01 EPS a year earlier. Because roughly 80% of the flat-rolled book prices on a two-month lag, recent price increases flow into second-half earnings, giving unusual near-term visibility. Consensus EPS estimates have moved up materially: current-year +17% and next-year +17% over the past 90 days.

The market has largely absorbed this. The stock has climbed ~74% over the trailing twelve months to roughly $234, sits below its 52-week high of $288.74, and trades at ~25x trailing earnings. On rising forward estimates ($16.41 current-year, $18.87 next-year), the forward multiple compresses to ~12.4x-14.2x, which is not demanding for a low-cost operator in a strong cycle. The debate is whether the current cycle proves durable (tariff persistence, secular reshoring demand) or mean-reverts as the 2022-2025 spread compression showed it can. Bank of America raised its price target to $285 while keeping a Neutral rating.

We rate STLD a HOLD (growth_hold). The business quality and near-term earnings momentum are genuine, and the H2 2026 lagged-price flow-through plus a normalizing capex profile support continued cash returns. The valuation offers limited asymmetry: the two central drivers — tariff durability and the unproven $5B+ aluminum ramp — are partly outside management's control, and steel-sector selection has been a persistent alpha drag. We would become more constructive on a pullback into the $190s or on confirmed aluminum profitability, and more cautious on evidence of tariff erosion or renewed spread compression.

## Company Overview

Steel Dynamics operates four segments around a circular, EAF-based manufacturing model, co-founded in 1993 by current CEO Mark Millett:

- **Steel Operations (~72% of 2025 sales, ~$13.4B):** Flat-roll mills (Butler, Columbus, Sinton) and long-products mills (structural/rail, engineered bar, Roanoke, Steel of West Virginia), plus coating and processing. Capacity is roughly 9.4M tons flat-roll and 4.6M tons long-products, with about 70% of steel/fabrication sales classified as value-added. The company shipped a record 13.7M tons in 2025.
- **Metals Recycling (Omni) (~11%, ~$4.3B):** The largest North American recycler, supplying about 65% of STLD's ferrous scrap internally, the core of its cost advantage.
- **Steel Fabrication (New Millennium) (~8%, ~$1.4B):** Joists and deck for non-residential construction, roughly a third of the domestic market, and a countercyclical internal steel buyer.
- **Aluminum Operations (~2%, ~$0.5B):** A new 650,000-metric-ton recycled aluminum flat-rolled mill in Columbus, MS, plus slab centers in Mexico and the (relocating) U.S. Southwest.

The structural advantages are a low-cost EAF cost base (roughly 55-65% of steel COGS is scrap, much of it sourced internally), product and end-market diversification away from commodity grades, downstream "pull-through" (fabrication and coating consuming ~1.8M tons, or 13% of shipments, in 2025), and a performance-based pay culture where >85% of senior leadership compensation is at risk. These sustain higher through-cycle utilization — 86% in 2025 and 90% in Q2 2026, against an industry rate near 81%.

## Financial Analysis

STLD's earnings are cyclical, and the standardized trailing financials in the most recent 10-K (filed February 27, 2026) capture the FY2025 trough rather than the current run-rate. This distinction is central to the analysis.

**Peak-to-trough trajectory:**

| Metric | 2022 (peak) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 (trough) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $21.5B | $18.8B | $17.5B | $18.2B |
| Operating income | $5.09B | $3.15B | $1.94B | $1.48B |
| Net income | ~$3.9B | $2.45B | $1.54B | $1.19B |
| Operating margin | 23.7% | 16.8% | 11.1% | 8.1% |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $9.84 | $7.99 |

The 2025 trough was a spread story, not a volume story: record shipments coincided with the segment metal spread falling 2% in steel and 17% in fabrication (fab operating income −39%). Aluminum was a −$173M operating drag on startup and commissioning costs, and only Metals Recycling improved (+27% operating income).

**The 2026 recovery** is now visible in the P&L. Q2 2026 delivered $700M operating income on record 3.7M tons steel shipments. The sequence — Q1 $2.78 → Q2 $3.69 — reflects flat-roll ASPs up ~$105/ton sequentially and value-added spreads up ~$70/ton off Q4'25 lows.

**Balance sheet and liquidity are healthy.** Altman Z of 3.2 (safe zone), current ratio 3.06, interest coverage 13.3x, and debt-to-capital of 32% against a 60% covenant maximum. Total debt rose to $4.2B in 2025 from $2.8B via new senior notes (5.25% '35, 5.75% '55, 4.0% '28) used to refinance maturities and fund growth. Liquidity is ~$2.2B ($1.0B cash/investments plus $1.2B undrawn revolver). The investment-grade profile is intact.

**Cash flow inflection is underway.** OCF was $1.45B in 2025 (down from $1.8B), and FCF was only $502M due to $948M of capex (aluminum) plus a $1.1B working-capital build. The forward signal is that the $5B+ aluminum/Sinton/coating build-out is completing: H2 2026 capex is guided to just $300-350M. Combined with working capital turning from a use to a source in H2, FCF should expand meaningfully. Management notes the five-year average FCF rose from ~$540M (2011-15) to ~$2.4B most recently.

**Capital returns** are consistent: the dividend was raised 9% to $0.50/quarter in 2025, buybacks totaled $901M (2025) and $1.2B (2024), and share count has fallen from 183M (2022) to 148M (2025), a ~19% reduction. About $489M of authorization remained mid-2026. We score Financial Health a 7.0 — strong cash generation, appropriate leverage for the model, and a normalizing capex profile that improves FCF conversion.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here combines cyclical recovery with structural aluminum optionality. On the cyclical side, current-quarter estimate revisions are noisy (−10.9% over 30 days, −1.9% over 90 days), but the forward quarters are strongly positive — next quarter +30.3% over 90 days, current year +17.0%, and next year +17.2%. The estimate revision signal is meaningful and constructive, but I weight it with a specific caveat: this is a commodity cyclical, and the +30% revision magnitude reflects a spread cycle that history shows can reverse quickly, not a secular volume ramp. The revision velocity supports the near-term thesis but is not the same durable recovery one sees in a structurally re-rating growth business.

Demand indicators reinforce the near-term picture. Steel fabrication backlog is up 45% YoY (volume-driven) and extends into 2027; the Dodge Momentum Index is up 30% YoY. Management cited strength across long-products, structural, rail, SBQ, energy/OCTG, and solar, with stable auto and subdued residential. Secular tailwinds — manufacturing reshoring, infrastructure funding, data-center and warehouse construction, solar structures, and supply-chain regionalization — provide a demand floor broader than the tariff catalyst alone.

The aluminum segment is the multi-year growth lever and remains a "show-me" story. Management targets ≥90% capacity exiting 2026 and $650-700M through-cycle EBITDA. Progress is real: the hot side is at rated capacity, the third cold mill started in July 2026 (enabling full 650k mt capability), the first auto CASH line is operational, and 5182/5754 auto alloys have qualified at multiple OEMs. But the segment still lost $33M in Q2 2026 (narrowing 48% sequentially), Q2 shipments of 53k mt came in below the 60-70k mt guidepost, and a slab-center relocation triggered a $16M impairment and delayed internal slab supply. CFO Wagler conceded current market spreads are "considerably higher" than the $650-700M assumption, implying upside to the target if the ramp holds. We score Growth Prospects a 6.5: genuine near-term momentum plus a credible but unproven structural option, tempered by the cyclical nature of the earnings driving the revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$234 the stock carries a ~25x trailing P/E (on $7.99 trough-year EPS) and a materially lower forward multiple on rising estimates. On current-year consensus of $16.41 that is ~14.2x; on next-year $18.87 it is ~12.4x. Applying the cycle-adjusted framework, the trailing multiple is not the right anchor: the trailing period reflects the 2025 trough, and the forward multiple on recovering earnings is the correct lens. On that basis the stock is not expensive for a low-cost operator.

Peer comparison supports a reasonable-but-not-cheap read. The steel peer median P/E is ~26.8 (RS 26.8, NUE 25.9), so STLD's trailing multiple is roughly in line with quality peers, and its forward multiple sits below them. There is no obvious discount to exploit and no obvious premium to fade: the group is priced consistently on trough/mid-cycle trailing earnings that all face the same forward normalization question.

The reason I do not assign a high valuation score, despite the favorable forward multiple, is that the earnings base is cycle-sensitive and the multiple is being applied to peak-adjacent spread economics. The bull's forward P/E of ~12-14x assumes 2026-2027 spreads hold; if the 50% tariff is relaxed or circumvented and spreads mean-revert toward the 2025 trough, forward EPS compresses and the "cheap" multiple evaporates, the classic cyclical value trap where the stock looks cheapest at the top. A low headline multiple is not a reason to buy here. Bank of America's decision to raise its target while staying Neutral encapsulates the setup: the tariff upside is largely in the price after the run.

I also weight the sector context: steel/materials selection has been a durable alpha headwind, so a positive raw thesis here needs company-specific outperformance evidence beyond "the cycle is good." STLD's low-cost structure and downstream integration provide some of that, but not enough to make the current price asymmetric. Valuation scores a 5.0 — fairly priced with balanced risk/reward, neither a compelling entry nor a clear overvaluation.

## Competitive Landscape

STLD's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving within a favorable industry structure. Against integrated producers, its EAF model and internal scrap supply confer a structural cost edge; against other EAFs (notably Nucor), its diversification and downstream pull-through sustain higher utilization (90% vs. ~81% industry in Q2 2026). The value-added mix (~70% of steel/fab sales) reduces exposure to bare commodity grades.

The dominant industry driver in 2026 is the Section 232 tariff, which underpins domestic pricing. This is simultaneously the tailwind and the central dependency — management flagged that imports rose month-over-month into July 2026, and it is actively lobbying on Section 301 cases, Buy American provisions, and USMCA melt-and-poured rules. The competitive read is qualified by peer dynamics: when STLD issued softer initial Q2 guidance in June (with the $16M writedown), Nucor topped its outlook, a reminder that STLD is not uniquely positioned within the domestic cohort.

The aluminum thesis adds a competitive expansion vector. Management points to a structural U.S. aluminum sheet deficit of >1.4M mt (widening), tariff protection on imported aluminum, and genuine commercial synergy — two-thirds of STLD's carbon flat-roll customers also buy aluminum sheet — plus countercyclical beverage-can demand. If the ramp succeeds, STLD adds a differentiated adjacency few EAF peers can match. Competitive Position scores 6.5, reflecting a strong, stable position with an improving-but-unproven aluminum extension, modestly below prior reads given the peer competition on display and the policy dependence of the current advantage.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are meaningful and partly outside management's control:

1. **Tariff/trade-policy dependence.** The 2026 boom is heavily levered to the Section 232 tariff. Any relaxation, circumvention, or import surge (already rising into July 2026) would pressure prices — the single largest swing factor for the bull case, and a policy variable STLD cannot control.
2. **Metal-spread cyclicality.** The 2022→2025 decline (operating margin 23.7% → 8.1%) demonstrates that spreads, not just volume, drive earnings. Forward estimates embed continued strong spreads.
3. **Aluminum execution.** A $5B+ organic bet into an industry where the company acknowledges "little experience." Still loss-making, shipments below guidepost, and a relocation-driven impairment/delay. The $650-700M EBITDA target is unproven.
4. **Aluminum customer concentration.** A "relatively concentrated group" of aluminum customers on sizable agreements — a new, elevated risk given the segment launch.
5. **Scrap and electricity cost inflation.** STLD is more scrap-dependent than integrated peers, and rising industry EAF capacity plus emerging electricity-cost inflation could compress the cost advantage.
6. **Operational.** Q2 2026 saw simultaneous maintenance outages at all three flat-roll mills (unusual), and a fatal accident at a New Process Steel facility in April 2026 was disclosed on the call — culturally significant for a safety-first operator.

These risks are partially, but not fully, priced. The Risk Factors score has declined from 6.0 to 5.0 across recent reports as the aluminum ramp stalled at the guidepost and tariff durability became the load-bearing assumption. We hold Risk Factors at 5.0: the tariff dependence and aluminum execution are specific, material, and not resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.5% is rich versus 1-year realized volatility of 35.0% (a ~10.5-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (45.5% near-term vs. 41.8% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress. Put/call skew is roughly balanced near-term (+0.7%) but turns negative at 3- and 6-month tenors (−3.3% and −4.5%), consistent with hedging demand against a downside cycle turn rather than acute panic. The put/call volume ratio (0.40) and OI ratio (0.45) are call-tilted, not bearish. ATM IV collapsed from 166.6% to 45.5% since the prior snapshot, so the June event stress has fully resolved. The net read: the options market is neither confirming a strong bullish thesis nor flagging a collapse, but the modest longer-dated put skew aligns with our view that the cyclical/tariff downside is a real, if not imminent, concern.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$300-320):** Tariffs persist, domestic spreads hold at elevated levels through 2027, and the lagged contract structure carries recent price gains into H2 2026 and 2027 earnings. Aluminum ramps to ≥90% utilization and turns profitable, with EBITDA potentially exceeding the $650-700M target given current spreads. Capex normalizes in H2, FCF inflects sharply, and buybacks resume at scale, retiring more shares. On next-year EPS of ~$19-20 at a ~16x multiple, the stock reaches the low-$300s, consistent with the analyst high target of $310.

**Bear Case (~$150-170, ~27-36% downside):** Tariffs are relaxed or circumvented, imports surge, and spreads mean-revert toward the 2025 trough. Aluminum losses persist as the ramp underdelivers against the guidepost, and the segment's customer concentration bites. Forward EPS compresses back toward $9-11, and the multiple derates as the market recognizes the cyclical peak. The stock retraces toward the $150s.

**Base Case (~$255-270):** Tariffs hold near-term, spreads stay firm through 2026 but begin to normalize into 2027, and aluminum turns modestly profitable without hitting the full target. On ~$16.5-18.5 of normalized forward EPS at ~14-15x, the stock trades in the mid-$250s to high-$260s — near the analyst mean ($268) and median ($266). This offers modest upside from ~$234 but limited asymmetry given the bear-case magnitude.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The near-term earnings momentum and H2 flow-through justify holding through the cycle, but the price does not offer enough asymmetry to warrant a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings report (October 2026), which will test H2 spread flow-through, aluminum ramp progress, and any change in import/tariff dynamics.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Aluminum segment reaches operating breakeven or profitability with shipments at/above the 60-70k mt guidepost, de-risking the EBITDA option.
- A pullback into the $190s (below ~12x current-year EPS) restoring meaningful asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- Confirmed tariff durability (e.g., extended Section 232 framework or successful Section 301 outcomes) that lengthens the spread cycle's visible runway.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Section 232 tariff relaxation, adverse USMCA melt-and-poured ruling, or a sustained import surge that compresses flat-roll spreads.
- Aluminum losses widening or the ramp stalling below the guidepost through Q4 2026, raising impairment risk on the investment.
- Forward estimate revisions turning negative alongside a metal-spread rollover confirming a cyclical peak.

## Conclusion

Steel Dynamics is a high-quality, founder-led, low-cost EAF operator executing well through a favorable cycle. The 2026 earnings inflection is real and largely visible for the next two quarters given the lagging-price contract structure, the balance sheet is investment-grade with a normalizing capex profile that should lift FCF, and the aluminum expansion offers a credible multi-year option. Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum.

The reason to hold rather than buy is valuation and the composition of the risks driving it. At ~$234 below a 52-week high, the tariff-and-spread upside is substantially recognized. The two load-bearing assumptions — tariff persistence and a successful aluminum ramp — are partly beyond management's control, and the bear-case downside (~30%) is larger than the base-case upside from here. Steel/materials selection has been a persistent alpha headwind, and STLD's genuine quality edge is not sufficient to make the current entry asymmetric. We would turn constructive on a pullback into the $190s or on confirmed aluminum profitability, and cautious on any sign of tariff erosion. Overall score 6.05, HOLD.
