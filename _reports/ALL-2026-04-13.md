## Executive Summary

Allstate completed a dramatic profitability turnaround, swinging from a $(1.3)B net loss in 2022 to $10.3B net income in 2025. The combined ratio improved 19.3 points over two years to 85.2%, driven by aggressive rate actions, favorable reserve development, and lower catastrophe losses. Free cash flow reached $9.9B, the balance sheet de-levered materially (D/E from 44.7% to 24.5%), and management authorized a $4.0B buyback in February 2026 (~7.3% of market capitalization).

At ~$211, Allstate trades at approximately 5.5x trailing EPS and 8.1x FY2026 consensus of $26.03, both well below the 10.6x P&C peer median. Four consecutive quarters of 40%+ EPS beats and broadly rising estimate revisions (+8.0% over 90 days for FY2026) support the thesis that the market has under-appreciated the magnitude and durability of the earnings improvement. However, the stock faces real near-term headwinds: tariff-driven auto and homeowners claims cost inflation, the mathematical certainty that $1.8B in favorable reserve releases will diminish, and the cyclical risk inherent in an 85.2% combined ratio that is historically exceptional and unlikely to persist. These risks are reflected in options markets pricing near-term IV at 33% versus 23.4% historical vol. The valuation discount to peers provides sufficient margin of safety to absorb moderate deterioration while still generating attractive returns.

## Company Overview

Allstate is the second-largest personal lines P&C insurer in the United States with 38.3 million policies in force across three operating segments. The core Allstate Protection segment (~96% of premiums written) encompasses personal auto ($38.6B), homeowners ($16.6B), other personal lines ($3.3B), and a shrinking commercial book ($0.4B). Protection Services (~4% of premiums) includes consumer product protection plans, roadside assistance, dealer services, identity protection, and Arity (telematics/mobility intelligence), with 172.2 million policies in force by count. A legacy run-off segment holds $1.43B in net reserves for asbestos and environmental claims from policies written decades ago.

Distribution operates through three channels: exclusive agents (largest), independent agents, and a rapidly growing direct-to-consumer platform. The company has been executing a "Transformative Growth" strategy focused on expanding distribution, improving customer value through telematics, and strategically exiting high-risk geographies. Allstate stopped writing new homeowners business in Florida (2023) and California (2022), reducing California homeowner exposure by over 50% since 2007, and is exiting commercial lines for the Allstate brand.

The $83.2B investment portfolio (71% fixed income, 92.1% investment grade) generated $3.45B in net investment income in 2025 (+11.5% YoY), providing a stable earnings floor regardless of underwriting results.

The combined ratio improvement of 19.3 points over two years reflects multiple tailwinds converging: cumulative rate increases exceeding loss cost trends, declining auto claim frequency, $1.81B in favorable prior-year reserve releases (3.1 points on the combined ratio), and lower catastrophe losses (8.6 points vs. 11.6 in 2023).

A combined ratio of 85.2% in personal lines is historically exceptional. Sustained sub-95% results in this segment are uncommon, and the 2025 figure benefits from transient factors — particularly favorable reserve development that represented 22.6% of underwriting income. Stripping out the $1.81B in reserve releases, the adjusted combined ratio would be approximately 88.3%, still excellent.

### Cash Flow & Capital Return

Operating cash flow of $10.1B against capex of $228M produced $9.9B in free cash flow. Capital allocation priorities:

- **Buybacks:** $1.24B repurchased in 2025 (6M shares, 2.3% of float). The new $4.0B authorization represents 7.3% of market cap. Since 1995, Allstate has retired 71.1% of shares outstanding through $44.5B in cumulative repurchases.
- **Dividends:** Increased to $1.08/share quarterly in February 2026 (8% increase), yielding approximately 2.0%.
- **Combined annual return potential:** $4B buyback + ~$1.1B dividends = ~$5.1B, or roughly 9.3% of current market cap.

### Investment Portfolio

The $83.2B portfolio is conservatively positioned (92.1% investment grade within fixed income, duration 5.1 years). Net investment income of $3.45B grew 11.5% YoY, driven by both higher yields and larger balances. Equity securities increased $3.9B in 2025, signaling a willingness to take more risk as underwriting profits generate excess capital. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this investment income provides a durable earnings floor.

## Growth Analysis

### Premium Growth Trajectory

Revenue grew 5.6% in 2025 (following 12.3% in 2024), with net premiums written reaching $59.5B. Growth is being driven by both rate actions and increasing policy counts:

- Auto premiums written: +3.6% YoY ($38.6B)
- Homeowners premiums written: +14.9% YoY ($16.6B)
- New auto applications: +25.3% YoY
- Direct channel new auto apps: +32.9% YoY
- Auto PIF: +2.3% YoY
- Homeowners PIF: +2.5% YoY

The company is transitioning from a defensive posture (rate increases to restore profitability) to growth mode (increased advertising to $2.1B from $1.9B, expanded direct channel). This transition typically characterizes the favorable phase of the P&C pricing cycle.

## Competitive Landscape

Allstate competes primarily with GEICO (Berkshire Hathaway), State Farm (mutual), Progressive (PGR), and USAA in personal auto, and with the same names plus regional carriers in homeowners. Key competitive dynamics:

**Advantages:** Brand recognition (one of the most recognized insurance brands in the US), multi-channel distribution (exclusive agents, independent agents, direct), scale in personal lines (~38.3M PIF), proprietary telematics data through Arity, and an $83.2B investment portfolio providing earnings stability.

**Vulnerabilities:** The shift toward direct-to-consumer distribution favors Progressive and GEICO, though Allstate's direct channel is growing fastest (+32.9% in auto). The 25.3% growth in new auto applications suggests competitive positioning is currently strong, but this reflects favorable pricing conditions that could shift as the industry cycle matures.

**Cycle Position:** The P&C industry is in a hard market phase following the 2022-2023 loss years. As profitability improves across the industry, the cycle will eventually turn — competitors will begin competing on price, compressing margins. Allstate's own pivot from rate increases to growth mode (higher advertising, expanded distribution) is an early indicator of this transition. The soft market turn historically follows 2-3 years of exceptional profitability; exact timing is uncertain.

**Strategic Exits:** Allstate's withdrawal from Florida and California homeowners reduces catastrophe volatility but concedes growth in two of the three largest property markets. This creates a structural growth constraint that competitors willing to underwrite in those states may exploit.

## Risk Assessment

### Tariff-Driven Claims Cost Inflation (HIGH)

The most significant near-term risk. The 10-K explicitly identifies tariffs effective April 2, 2025, as creating multi-dimensional exposure: higher vehicle and parts prices (new car prices already up ~10%), increased building material costs, and supply chain disruptions extending repair times. Allstate's 2025 auto rate increases of 3.5% may be insufficient if auto repair costs increase 8-12% as some industry estimates suggest. Each point of combined ratio deterioration represents approximately $575M in pre-tax income, so a 3-4 point increase from tariff-driven severity would reduce annual pre-tax earnings by $1.7-2.3B.

### Reserve Development Normalization (HIGH)

The $1.81B in favorable prior-year reserve releases reflect rate increases in 2023-2024 that substantially over-priced relative to actual losses across accident years 2021-2024. This excess margin has now been largely recognized. FY2026 consensus already assumes lower earnings, implying analysts expect this headwind. If reserve development turns adverse — possible if tariffs drive unanticipated severity in more recent accident years — the combined ratio could deteriorate beyond expectations.

### Catastrophe Volatility (MEDIUM-HIGH)

Despite mitigation through geographic exits and reinsurance, catastrophe losses remain inherently volatile. Wind/hail events drove $3.95B in losses in 2025. The $1.05B California wildfire loss (net of reinsurance) demonstrated ongoing exposure even after 50%+ PIF reduction. Climate change and urban sprawl continue to increase the frequency and severity of convective storm events. A major hurricane season in 2026 could meaningfully impact earnings.

### P&C Cycle Risk (MEDIUM)

An 85.2% combined ratio is historically unusual in personal lines and is likely to mean-revert as competitors respond to industry-wide profitability by moderating rate increases or investing aggressively in growth. Allstate's own 25% growth in new applications and increased advertising spend suggest this competitive dynamic is already beginning. The consensus FY2027 estimate of $25.44 (slightly below FY2026) reflects expectations of gradual normalization, but a sharper downturn in pricing is possible.

### Technology/Structural (LOW-MEDIUM)

Autonomous vehicles and ADAS systems could reduce auto insurance demand over the medium-to-long term. Allstate derives approximately 65% of premiums from auto insurance. The timeline for material impact is likely measured in decades rather than years.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.0% sits well above 23.4% historical volatility — a 9.6% spread indicating elevated uncertainty pricing. The term structure is in backwardation (33.0% near-term vs. 22.3% at 6 months), consistent with imminent event risk pricing. Put/call skew of 18.5% and put/call volume ratio of 1.20 suggest moderately elevated demand for downside protection. At the 3-month tenor, the P/C volume ratio jumps to 7.93, indicating significant institutional hedging activity at the June expiration — likely related to hurricane season catalysts and upcoming earnings. The rich IV makes outright call purchases expensive but suggests that any positive resolution (benign cat season, favorable earnings) could produce a vol-compression rally.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($260-275)

Tariff-driven claims inflation proves manageable as Allstate implements mid-year rate increases to match severity trends. The combined ratio settles in the 88-92% range — above the 2025 result but still well below breakeven. Policies in force continue to grow 2-3% annually as the direct channel scales. The buyback reduces share count by ~7%, providing meaningful per-share earnings support. Investment income continues to grow as higher-for-longer rates persist. Applying 10x to $26 FY2026E = $260; applying 10.6x peer median = $276. Favorable reserve development, while diminishing, continues at perhaps half the 2025 rate for 1-2 more years, providing an earnings cushion.

### Bear Case ($165-180)

Tariff-driven auto and homeowners claims severity exceeds rate increases by 3-5 points, pushing the combined ratio back above 95%. Reserve development turns adverse as more recent accident years prove under-reserved in the tariff inflation environment. A major hurricane or wildfire event in 2026 produces $2-3B in net catastrophe losses. The P&C pricing cycle turns faster than expected as competitors pursue growth. Applying 7x to a reduced EPS of $22-24 = $154-168. At the low end, the dividend provides support (2.0% yield at $211), and the buyback creates a price floor as management repurchases aggressively below book.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $245 (10.6x peer median P/E × $23.10 blended FY2026/27E, adjusted for share count reduction). This reflects closing approximately 70% of the valuation gap to peers over 12 months while incorporating modest combined ratio normalization.

**Bull case target:** $275 — peer-median multiple applied to full FY2026E consensus, plus ~3% contribution from buyback accretion.

**Bear case target:** $175 — 7.5x on $23 normalized EPS, reflecting meaningful combined ratio deterioration and cycle turn. This represents ~17% downside from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalyst windows: Q1 2026 earnings (likely late April/early May 2026), hurricane season (June-November 2026), and any mid-year rate filing approvals.

**Upside/downside:** +16% to base case, +30% to bull case, -17% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.9:1 to base case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Combined ratio exceeding 95% in any reported quarter, signaling rate adequacy has been lost.
2. Adverse prior-year reserve development exceeding $500M in any quarter, signaling the reserve cushion has flipped to a deficit.
3. Auto policies in force declining for two consecutive quarters, signaling competitive positioning has deteriorated.

## Conclusion

Allstate presents a compelling risk/reward at 8.1x FY2026E against a 10.6x peer median. The profitability turnaround is supported by structural improvements (geographic exits, reinsurance optimization, distribution expansion), though the 85.2% combined ratio contains significant transient components that will normalize. The ~9.3% annual capital return yield (buyback + dividend) creates a meaningful floor for shareholder value. Tariff-driven claims inflation is the primary risk to monitor; if mid-year rate filings offset the severity increase, the valuation gap should narrow. The 4-quarter streak of 40%+ earnings beats and rising estimate revisions support the thesis that consensus remains too conservative. At current levels, the stock offers roughly 2:1 upside-to-downside to a reasonable base case. BUY.
