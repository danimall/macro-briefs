## Executive Summary

TSMC delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $35.9 billion (+40.6% YoY in USD), with High Performance Computing (HPC) reaching 61% of total sales. Net income grew 58% YoY to another record, gross margins expanded to 66.2%, and management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to "above 30%" while refining capex guidance to the upper bound of $52-56 billion. Management has indicated that 2nm capacity is fully allocated through 2026. The company's dominant position at leading-edge nodes (90%+ share sub-7nm) remains intact, with Intel's packaging capabilities the only credible near-term competitive vector.

The stock has appreciated 135% over the past year to $411.68, reflecting the AI demand surge. At approximately 26.6x FY2026 consensus EPS and 21.3x FY2027 estimates, the multiple is elevated but not unreasonable for a company growing earnings 30-40% annually with 66%+ gross margins and structural scarcity value. Estimate revisions are running +7-9% over the past 90 days across all forward periods. The primary risk is geopolitical: the Trump-Xi summit on Wednesday introduces binary export control risk, and Taiwan Strait concentration remains the permanent structural overhang for any multi-year holder.

The consensus analyst target of $463 (median $472.50) implies 12-15% upside from current levels. Morningstar's fair value estimate of $428 suggests more limited near-term upside. The stock warrants continued ownership given the demand visibility, but position sizing should account for the elevated near-term geopolitical event risk.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, commanding 60%+ global foundry market share and approximately 90%+ at leading-edge nodes (sub-7nm). The company operates a pure-play foundry model, designing no chips of its own, which eliminates competitive conflicts with customers. Its client roster includes Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Qualcomm, Broadcom, and MediaTek.

Revenue segmentation as of Q4 2025: High Performance Computing 55%, Smartphones 32%, IoT/Automotive/Other 13%. By technology node: 3nm accounts for 28% of wafer revenue, 5nm 35%, 7nm 14% — meaning 77% of revenue comes from advanced nodes (≤7nm).

The company is headquartered in Hsinchu, Taiwan, with expanding operations in Arizona (first facility in high-volume production since late 2024, second facility under construction with volume production planned for 2H 2027, third facility construction commenced in 2025), Japan (output surging 130% in 2026), and Germany.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dominant Position:** TSMC holds 90%+ share at leading-edge nodes. The barriers to entry are extraordinary — a new leading-edge fab costs $20-30 billion and takes 3-5 years from groundbreaking to volume production. The learning curve advantages from decades of manufacturing optimization are not replicable quickly.

**Intel Foundry Threat:** Intel's EMIB advanced packaging technology represents the first credible competitive challenge in several years. Reports of Google potentially using Intel for custom chip packaging, and Tesla's $25B "TeraFab" project involving Intel, deserve monitoring. However: (1) Intel remains 1-2 generations behind on process technology, (2) TSMC's dominance at 2nm through 2026-2027 provides insulation, (3) Morningstar views these concerns as "overblown."

**Samsung Foundry:** Remains a distant third with persistent yield issues at advanced nodes. Not a material competitive threat in the near term.

**Advanced Packaging Bottleneck:** CoWoS and other advanced packaging technologies are the primary bottleneck in AI chipmaking, with "almost all" capacity in Asia. TSMC is building its first US packaging facility, while Intel's EMIB is the primary alternative. Nvidia has booked the majority of TSMC's packaging capacity. This bottleneck is becoming TSMC's next competitive advantage and a significant revenue contributor.

**Competitive Position Trajectory:** Improving. The capacity constraints at 2nm and advanced packaging strengthen pricing power. Customer diversification across AI (multiple hyperscalers), mobile, and automotive reduces concentration risk relative to any single end-market.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical / Taiwan Concentration (HIGH — partially priced):** TSMC's manufacturing remains overwhelmingly in Taiwan. The diversification to Arizona/Japan/Germany will take years to reach meaningful scale. The Trump-Xi summit on Wednesday introduces acute, near-term binary risk via potential export control escalation. A severe outcome (technology supply chain disruption) would impact TSM immediately.

**AI Capex Sustainability (MODERATE):** Hyperscaler capex growing 80% while revenues grow ~15.5% creates a structural question about sustainability. Morgan Stanley warns capex-to-sales ratios will exceed dot-com levels. A pullback in AI spending would compress TSMC's growth rate, though not its fundamental demand position (chips are still needed for inference, mobile, automotive).

**Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** TSMC's 2025 20-F discloses that the largest customer accounted for 19% of net revenue, the second-largest for 17%, and the top 10 customers for 78% of 2025 net revenue. Heavy reliance on a small number of customers creates vulnerability if any single customer shifts volume.

**Overseas Execution (LOW-MODERATE):** Arizona yields are reportedly close to Taiwan levels — a positive surprise. But cost structures remain higher overseas, creating persistent margin dilution of 2-4% as non-Taiwan revenue scales.

**Energy Constraints (LOW):** TSMC consumed ~10% of Taiwan's total electricity in 2023. Scaling AI workloads requires massive additional power. The company is investing in wind power, but Taiwan's energy infrastructure is structurally constrained.

## Conclusion

TSMC remains a BUY. The company is the critical infrastructure layer for the AI buildout, operating at 66%+ gross margins with 30%+ revenue growth, capacity fully allocated through 2026, and accelerating estimate revisions. At 26x current-year earnings and 21x next-year, the stock is not cheap but is appropriately priced for a structurally dominant foundry growing earnings 30-40% annually.

The Trump-Xi summit introduces meaningful near-term event risk, and position sizing should reflect this binary. For investors without existing positions, waiting for post-summit clarity is reasonable. For existing holders, the fundamental case is stronger than at any prior point in the trajectory — record demand, rising estimates, capacity fully allocated, and no credible competitor at leading-edge logic through 2027. The 15% upside to base case with 2:1 risk/reward supports continued ownership with a 12-month horizon.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The HPC revenue category (61% of Q1 sales) includes CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, and networking chips beyond AI accelerators; the direct AI-only revenue percentage is lower than the report's thesis implies, which may moderately reduce the purity of the AI demand narrative supporting the target price.
