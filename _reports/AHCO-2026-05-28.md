**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

AdaptHealth Corp is a $1.35B market cap national home medical equipment provider transitioning toward capitated population health management. The company recently onboarded 10M+ members through an unprecedented contract (likely Kaiser Permanente), representing the largest patient transition in HME history. This pivot toward predictable recurring per-member-per-month revenue is supported by $24.3M in open-market insider purchases from an OEP-affiliated director in March 2026.

The investment case confronts serious headwinds: four consecutive quarterly earnings misses (including a -786% surprise in Q1 2026), negative estimate revisions across all forecast periods (-15.8% for current year over the past 30 days), two active False Claims Act investigations covering core billing practices, and $2.54B in goodwill (59% of total assets) with demonstrated impairment history. The stock has declined 23% from its 52-week high of $13.43, driven by the Q1 miss that revealed elevated onboarding costs from the capitated contract. The bull case requires successful cost normalization by end of Q2 2026 and EBITDA margin recovery to ~19% — both achievable but unproven.

At an enterprise value of approximately $3.0B against guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $680M-$730M, AHCO trades at ~4.1-4.4x EV/EBITDA, which is cheap for a healthcare services company if the guided numbers materialize. The tension between deep value and execution/legal risk leaves the balance of evidence unclear in either direction.

## Company Overview

AdaptHealth operates as a national healthcare-at-home platform, serving approximately 4.3 million patients annually through ~640 locations across 48 states with ~10,900 employees plus ~4,400 outsourced staff. The company operates in four segments:

- **Sleep Health** (42.5% of revenue, $1.38B): CPAP/BiPAP equipment and resupply
- **Respiratory Health** (21.2%, $691M): Oxygen therapy, ventilators, non-invasive ventilation
- **Diabetes Health** (18.3%, $592M): Continuous glucose monitors, insulin pumps, supplies
- **Wellness at Home** (18.0%, $583M): Enteral nutrition, wound care, urology (being pruned via dispositions)

Revenue derives 62.6% from point-in-time sales (supplies), 33.4% from fixed monthly equipment rentals, and 4.0% from capitated arrangements (growing). Payor mix is ~61% commercial insurance, ~26% Medicare/Medicaid.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:**
FY2025 revenue of $3.24B was essentially flat (-0.5% vs. FY2024). Organic growth of +1.7% was offset by dispositions (-2.8%). The trajectory shifted materially in Q1 2026: reported revenue of $819.8M grew 5.4% YoY, with organic growth accelerating to 9.1%, driven by the large capitated contract.

FY2026 guidance of $3.45B-$3.52B implies 6.3%-8.5% growth, a meaningful step-up from the flat FY2025 performance.

**Profitability:**
Operating income collapsed from $264M (FY2024) to $91M (FY2025), a 65.5% decline driven by a $128M Diabetes goodwill impairment, cost of revenue increasing 210bps, and G&A rising 80bps. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $689M to $617M (-10.4%). EBITDA margins compressed from 21.1% to 19.0%.

The Q1 2026 miss ($-0.12 actual vs. $0.02 estimate) was attributed to $12M in elevated variable labor costs for onboarding the capitated contract's 35 de novo locations. Management guided toward normalization by end of Q2 with EBITDA margins returning to ~19%.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $602M (FY2025) was strong and growing (+11% YoY). Free cash flow of $219M (6.8% margin) provides real financial flexibility. Management expects ~$100M quarterly FCF in H2 2026 once start-up costs normalize.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $1.73B (6.125% notes due 2028, 4.625% due 2029, 5.125% due 2030, term loan due 2029)
- Cash: $106M
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~2.6x (per guided $680M-$730M midpoint)
- Goodwill: $2.54B (59% of total assets)
- Working capital: $17M (collapsed from $189M)
- Altman Z-Score: 1.64 (distress zone)
- April 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031, lowered weighted average cost

The Z-Score is somewhat misleading given $600M+ operating cash flow and $274M revolving credit availability, but the leverage is real and constrains financial flexibility.

**Debt Paydown:** The company made $218.8M in voluntary term loan repayments in 2025, demonstrating prioritization of balance sheet repair.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct elements:

**Near-term catalyst (Q2-Q4 2026):** With 35 new locations operational and onboarding costs expected to normalize, the ramp from $819.8M in Q1 revenue toward the $862M-$880M quarterly run-rate implied by FY2026 guidance represents a clear acceleration path. Respiratory Health (30.3% EBITDA margin, 6.1% growth) provides a stable high-margin growth engine.

**Secular tailwinds:** Aging demographics increase demand for sleep and respiratory equipment. The shift toward value-based care and capitated models structurally favors scaled operators. CMS increasing survey frequency creates compliance barriers that benefit large, technology-invested players.

**Growth headwinds:** GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy) represent a long-term secular risk to sleep apnea and diabetes volumes. The Diabetes segment is deteriorating (revenue -3.6%, margin collapsed to 4.4%, competitive bidding starting ~2028). E-commerce and pharmacy entrants (Amazon, CVS) could compress margins in commodity supply categories.

Estimate revision data shows a negative trajectory: current year EPS estimates cut from $1.00 to $0.84 (-15.8% in 30 days), and next year from $1.24 to $1.17 (-5.2% in 30 days). This is a material negative signal that counters the growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $10.36/share with 133M shares outstanding, AHCO has a market cap of ~$1.38B. Adding ~$1.63B in net debt gives an enterprise value of approximately $3.0B.

Against guided FY2026 EBITDA of $680M-$730M (midpoint $705M), the stock trades at **4.3x EV/EBITDA**. Against consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.17, the stock trades at **8.9x forward P/E**.

These multiples appear cheap for a healthcare services company generating $600M+ in operating cash flow. The median consensus price target of $14.00 implies 35% upside. The analyst target range of $11-$18 is wide, reflecting uncertainty.

The valuation discount reflects: (1) four consecutive earnings misses destroying credibility, (2) two FCA investigations creating tail risk, (3) $2.54B goodwill with demonstrated impairment history, (4) high leverage, and (5) negative estimate revisions.

The bull case on valuation requires accepting that the capitated contract ramp is real, EBITDA normalizes to guidance, and FCA risk is manageable. If those conditions hold, the stock is genuinely cheap at sub-5x EV/EBITDA. If they don't — particularly if EBITDA misses guidance or FCA liability proves material — the goodwill writedown risk and leverage create substantial downside.

## Competitive Landscape

AdaptHealth operates in a highly fragmented HME market alongside Accendra Health, Lincare, Rotech, Cardinal Health, Quipt, and numerous regional providers. The company differentiates through:

- **National scale:** ~640 locations enabling large capitated contracts that require geographic breadth (the 10M+ member contract proves this moat is real)
- **Technology platform:** AI-driven scheduling, e-prescribing, digital ordering, centralized revenue cycle management
- **Broad payor relationships:** National contracts with major commercial insurers

The capitated contract win demonstrates a genuine competitive advantage — smaller competitors cannot replicate the infrastructure required to onboard 10M+ lives across 8 states simultaneously. This positions AHCO for additional capitated wins as payors seek to consolidate HME vendors.

However, competitive threats are intensifying: Amazon's entry into medical supplies, pharmacy benefit managers expanding into DME, and CMS competitive bidding program resuming 2026-2028. The Diabetes segment in particular faces structural competitive deterioration.

Peer comparison is difficult — comparable companies (ENOV, IART, STAA) operate in different HME subsegments. Most peers are unprofitable on a GAAP basis, making P/E comparisons uninformative. On an EV/EBITDA basis, AHCO's 4.3x compares favorably to typical healthcare services multiples of 8-12x, but the discount reflects legitimate quality and risk differentials.

## Risk Assessment

**FCA Investigations (HIGH):** Two active civil investigative demands — one in South Carolina (PAP device humidifier billing since January 2017) and one in Eastern Pennsylvania (respiratory devices since January 2018). FCA penalties can be treble damages plus per-claim penalties. The investigation period covers 7-8+ years of billing practices, potentially implicating hundreds of millions in claims volume. The company cannot currently estimate potential liability.

**Goodwill Impairment (MEDIUM-HIGH):** $2.54B in goodwill with $972M in cumulative impairments (2023-2025). Wellness at Home fair value excess is <10% and Respiratory Health <20% — both could face impairment with modest deterioration. Additional impairment would destroy book value but not affect cash flow.

**Execution Risk on Capitated Contract (MEDIUM):** The $12M Q1 labor cost overrun and associated earnings miss demonstrates that operational execution is not guaranteed. If cost normalization takes longer than guided (end of Q2), FY2026 EBITDA targets become at risk.

**Leverage (MEDIUM):** $1.73B in debt against an equity market cap of $1.38B. While the April 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031, the interest burden (~$100M annually) limits financial flexibility.

**Regulatory (MEDIUM):** OBBBA Medicaid eligibility tightening (work requirements 2027), CMS competitive bidding for CGMs (2028), potential Section 232 tariffs on imported medical equipment.

**GLP-1 Secular Risk (LOW-MEDIUM):** Weight-loss drugs reducing sleep apnea and diabetes prevalence represents a multi-year headwind to the company's two largest revenue streams.

## Investment Thesis

- Capitated contract costs normalize by Q2 end, demonstrating EBITDA ramp toward $170-180M/quarter
- Additional capitated contract wins validate the business model transformation
- Diabetes segment divestiture removes drag, raises capital for debt reduction
- FCA investigations settled for manageable amounts ($20-50M)
- Multiple re-rates from 4.3x to 6-7x EV/EBITDA as execution de-risks

- FCA liability proves material ($100M+), requiring settlement that strains liquidity
- Capitated contract margins remain below expectations through FY2026
- Additional goodwill impairments in Wellness at Home and/or Respiratory Health
- Diabetes competitive bidding further compresses already-thin margins
- Estimates continue declining, stock de-rates to 3x EV/EBITDA distressed levels
- 23-42% downside from current levels

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**

2. FCA investigation resulting in >$100M liability or criminal referral
3. Additional goodwill impairment in Respiratory Health segment (highest-margin business)
4. Full-year guidance cut below $650M EBITDA

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) is the critical decision point. By then, the capitated contract normalization claim will be testable against actual results.

## Conclusion

AdaptHealth presents an asymmetric setup: a business generating $600M+ in operating cash flow, trading at 4.3x guided EBITDA, with $24M in recent insider buying from an informed major shareholder. The capitated contract transformation is strategically compelling and demonstrates competitive advantages that smaller operators cannot replicate.

Four consecutive earnings misses have destroyed estimate credibility, making forward projections unreliable. Negative estimate revisions of -15.8% in 30 days indicate the analyst community is losing confidence. Two active FCA investigations covering multi-year billing practices create unquantifiable but potentially material tail risk. The elevated put/call ratio (2.99) and 50%+ IV confirm the market is pricing meaningful uncertainty.

The next 2-3 quarters will determine whether the capitated model works at profitable scale or whether execution problems and legal overhang continue to compound. With estimates falling and no track record of beating consensus, the burden of proof remains on management. HOLD until Q2 results provide evidence of cost normalization.
