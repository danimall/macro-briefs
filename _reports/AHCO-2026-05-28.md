**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

AdaptHealth is a national home medical equipment provider generating $3.24B in annual revenue and $602M in operating cash flow, trading at approximately 4.3x EV/EBITDA on FY2026 guided midpoint. The stock sits at $10.36, roughly 23% below the $13.43 52-week high and within 5% of a $24.3M insider buying cluster at $9.73-$9.94. Sell-side consensus is 6 Buy/Strong Buy versus 2 Hold, with a median price target of $14.00 representing 35% upside.

The investment case is genuinely bifurcated. On the constructive side: organic revenue accelerated to 9.1% in Q1 2026 driven by a 10M-member capitated contract, the April 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031 with credit upgrades, debt has been reduced by $240M in one year, and insider buying at scale signals informed conviction. On the adverse side: the company has missed EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters (including a -786% miss in Q1 2026), current-year estimates have been cut 15.8% in 30 days, two active False Claims Act investigations target the company's most profitable segments (Sleep and Respiratory Health), the diabetes segment is in margin collapse, and $2.5B in goodwill faces ongoing impairment risk.

The resolution of this thesis depends primarily on whether the capitated contract onboarding costs normalize in 2-3 quarters (bull) or whether the FCA investigations produce material fines/compliance actions that impair the core business model (bear). At 4.3x EBITDA with credit upgrades and strong cash flow, much of the bad news appears priced, but the FCA outcomes are genuinely unquantifiable and could cause severe further downside.

## Company Overview

AdaptHealth operates as one of the largest national home medical equipment providers in the United States, serving approximately 4.3 million patients annually across all 50 states through ~640 locations. Revenue splits across four segments: Sleep Health (42.5%), Respiratory Health (21.2%), Diabetes Health (18.3%), and Wellness at Home (18.0%).

The revenue model combines point-of-sale supplies and consumables (~63%), fixed monthly equipment reimbursements (~33%), and capitated per-member-per-month arrangements (~4%, but growing rapidly). Payor mix includes Medicare (26%), Medicaid, and commercial insurance (61% private payors).

The company was assembled through rapid acquisition — evidenced by $2.54B in goodwill representing 59% of total assets — and is now transitioning toward organic growth, portfolio pruning (divesting non-core Wellness at Home assets), and capitated contracting as its strategic direction.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**
FY2025 revenue of $3.24B was essentially flat (-0.5% reported, +1.7% organic). Adjusted EBITDA declined from $689M to $617M (210bp margin compression from 21.1% to 19.0%). Cost of revenue increased from 79.1% to 81.2% of revenue, reflecting labor inflation, supply cost pressure, and payor mix deterioration in diabetes.

Net income swung to a $70.8M loss in FY2025 from $90.4M profit in FY2024, driven by a $128M goodwill impairment in the Diabetes Health segment. This followed the $831M impairment in 2023.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue of $819.8M (+5.4% YoY) with organic growth accelerating to 9.1%, driven by the new capitated contract. EBITDA missed estimates and the company reported a $16M net loss, reflecting onboarding costs for 35 de novo locations.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $602M (+11% YoY) is the clearest strength. Free cash flow of $219M after $382M in capex is solid for a company with this capital intensity ($341M annual depreciation on patient equipment). FCF conversion relative to adjusted EBITDA is approximately 36%, typical for asset-heavy healthcare services.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt declined from $1.96B to $1.72B through $219M in voluntary prepayments. The April 2026 refinancing established a new $1.1B credit facility (maturity 2031) that was oversubscribed and earned credit rating upgrades. Net leverage is approximately 2.4x on FY2026 guided EBITDA midpoint of $705M. Working capital is thin at $16.5M but somewhat mitigated by strong cash generation and the expanded $450M revolver.

Altman Z-Score of 1.64 falls in the distress zone, but this is distorted by the goodwill-heavy balance sheet and non-cash impairments. The actual cash flow profile and successful refinancing do not suggest near-term solvency risk.

## Growth Analysis

**The Capitated Contract:**
The Q1 2026 onboarding of 10+ million members under a capitated arrangement is the most significant operational development. This single contract drove organic revenue acceleration from 1.7% in FY2025 to 9.1% in Q1 2026 and prompted a revenue guidance raise to $3.45B-$3.52B for FY2026 (midpoint +7.5% YoY).

The EBITDA guidance range of $680M-$730M implies margin pressure persists. The 35 de novo locations require upfront investment in staffing, equipment, and logistics before reaching steady-state utilization. Management has characterized these as transient onboarding costs, but the 200bp margin compression seen in FY2025 was also attributed to transient factors that proved more persistent than expected.

**Segment Dynamics:**
- Sleep Health: Low-single-digit growth with margin pressure from product mix
- Respiratory Health: Stable but regulatory overhang from competitive bidding expansion
- Diabetes Health: Declining revenue (-3.6% in FY2025) with margin collapse (9.9% → 4.4%) and structural headwinds from CGM competitive bidding (2028), GLP-1 competition, and pharmacy benefit shift
- Wellness at Home: Being divested

**Estimate Trajectory:**
Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 15.8% in the last 30 days (from $1.00 to $0.84), and the current quarter estimate dropped 31.3% (from $0.21 to $0.15). This negative revision momentum suggests the market has not yet found a stable earnings expectation for the company post-contract onboarding.

## Valuation Assessment

At $10.36 per share with a market cap of ~$1.35B, implied enterprise value is approximately $3.0B (adding $1.72B debt, subtracting minimal cash).

**EV/EBITDA:** 4.3x on FY2026 guided midpoint of $705M. This is cheap for a company generating $600M+ operating cash flow with improving organic growth.

**Forward P/E:** The trailing EPS of -$0.52 is meaningless given non-cash charges. On the $0.84 current-year consensus, the stock trades at 12.3x — reasonable but not compelling given the downward revision trajectory.

**Analyst Consensus:** Median target of $14.00 implies 35% upside. Low target of $11.00 (6% upside) and high of $18.00 (74% upside). Canaccord recently raised its target to $16 after Q1 2026 results.

**Why the discount exists:** Four consecutive EPS misses, two FCA investigations, $831M+ in cumulative goodwill impairments, a catastrophic 5-year TSR versus peers, and general skepticism toward acquisition-driven roll-ups with margin compression. This discount reflects real risks rather than pure market inefficiency.

**Is the discount overdone?** At 4.3x EBITDA with credit upgrades, strong cash flow, and organic acceleration, the valuation embeds significant pessimism. The insider buying at $9.73-$9.94 by an investor with direct access to company operations suggests informed capital views the risk/reward as attractive at these levels. However, the FCA investigations represent a genuine unquantifiable downside (potential treble damages, Medicare exclusion) that justifies some discount.

## Competitive Landscape

AdaptHealth operates in the fragmented U.S. HME market alongside Lincare, Rotech, Cardinal Health, and Quipt. The company's national scale (~640 locations, 4.3M patients) provides meaningful advantages in payor contracting, particularly for the capitated arrangements now driving growth. Smaller providers cannot serve a 10M-member health plan across eight states.

Structural tailwinds include aging demographics, shift of care to home settings, and consolidation pressures that squeeze under-scale competitors. Headwinds include Amazon and CVS entering the space (particularly for supplies and CGMs), reimbursement pressure from both government and commercial payors, and GLP-1 drugs potentially reducing diabetes device demand.

The capitated model represents a competitive moat if executed well — it requires geographic scale, logistics infrastructure, and technology integration that few competitors can match. But it also carries at-risk economics: if per-member costs exceed the fixed payment, AHCO absorbs losses.

The diabetes segment is actively losing competitive ground to pharmacy benefit shift and direct-to-consumer CGM models.

## Risk Assessment

**FCA Investigations (Binary Risk — Most Critical):**
Two active investigations target Sleep Health (PAP humidifiers, South Carolina since July 2024) and Respiratory Health (respiratory devices, Eastern PA since March 2025). These are the company's two most profitable segments comprising approximately 64% of revenue. Outcomes range from modest settlements to exclusion from Medicare. The investigation timeframes (covering billing back to 2017-2018) suggest substantial potential exposure. The probability of material fines (>$100M) is difficult to estimate but is non-trivial given the precedent of similar FCA cases in durable medical equipment.

**Earnings Visibility:**
Four consecutive misses destroy forecasting confidence. The 31.3% downward current-quarter revision suggests consensus still hasn't calibrated to the new cost structure. Until the company demonstrates a clean beat, forward estimates remain unreliable.

**Diabetes Structural Impairment:**
The segment's margin collapse combined with CGM competitive bidding effective January 2028, OIG rate reduction recommendations, and GLP-1 competition suggests permanent impairment rather than cyclical weakness. The $128M goodwill impairment confirms management's view. Further impairments are possible given <10% excess fair value in Wellness at Home and <20% in Respiratory Health.

**Capitated Contract Execution Risk:**
35 de novo locations require reaching steady-state utilization to deliver expected margins. If the onboarding timeline extends beyond 2-3 quarters or per-member costs exceed assumptions, the contract could be margin-dilutive rather than accretive.

**Governance:**
CEO compensation rose from $2.3M (2023) to $10.3M (2025) while cumulative TSR underperformed peers by approximately 73 percentage points (indexed TSR of 27 vs. peer group of 110, on a $100 base). This pay-for-performance disconnect is concerning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($14-16, 35-54% upside):**
The capitated contract onboarding costs normalize by Q3-Q4 2026, driving EBITDA back toward 21%+ margins. Organic growth sustains at 7-9% as the capitated model scales. FCA investigations resolve with modest settlements ($50M or less). Debt reduction continues, leverage falls below 2x by end of 2027. Stock re-rates to 5.5-6x EBITDA on improved execution, approaching peer multiples.

**Bear Case ($6-8, 23-42% downside):**
FCA investigations produce material fines ($150M+) or corporate integrity agreements that constrain growth. Diabetes segment continues deteriorating. Capitated contract margins remain compressed. Goodwill impairments continue. The company's acquisition-fueled complexity proves too difficult to manage under compliance pressure. Stock trades to 3x a lower EBITDA base.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Consecutive quarterly EPS beats (2 or more) demonstrating earnings visibility recovery
2. FCA investigation resolution with settlement below $75M combined (removes tail risk)
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding toward 20%+ for two consecutive quarters (confirming onboarding cost normalization)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FCA investigation disclosure indicating potential settlement above $200M or Medicare exclusion risk
2. Third consecutive quarter of EBITDA margin below 18% despite full onboarding of capitated contract
3. Current-year EPS estimates revised below $0.50 (indicating the turnaround thesis has failed)

**Reassessment Timeline:**
- Q2 2026 earnings (August 2026): First clean quarter post-onboarding. If organic growth sustains and EBITDA margins inflect upward, upgrade probability increases substantially.
- Any FCA-related disclosures in 8-K filings warrant immediate reassessment regardless of timeline.

## Conclusion

AdaptHealth presents a genuinely uncertain investment case. The stock's 4.3x EV/EBITDA valuation, $602M operating cash flow, 9.1% organic growth acceleration, credit upgrades, and $24.3M insider buying cluster all argue against further downside from current levels. The median analyst target of $14.00 implies substantial upside if execution normalizes.

However, four consecutive EPS misses, 15.8% current-year estimate cuts, two FCA investigations targeting 64% of revenue, and governance concerns about CEO pay versus performance create a risk profile that prevents a BUY recommendation with adequate confidence. The FCA investigations represent unquantifiable binary risk ranging from immaterial to potentially existential (Medicare exclusion).

The appropriate posture is HOLD with heightened monitoring. The insider buying signal is strong and specific (C-suite plus major shareholder buying $24M+ at current levels), suggesting informed parties view the risk/reward favorably. But the inability to quantify FCA exposure and the absence of any demonstrated earnings stability (0-for-4 on recent quarters) warrant waiting for confirmation before committing capital. At $8.50-$9.00 (near the insider buying cluster with additional discount), the risk/reward would shift more constructively; at sustained earnings beats, the qualitative thesis upgrades regardless of price.
