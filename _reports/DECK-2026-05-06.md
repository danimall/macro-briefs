## Executive Summary

Deckers Brands trades at $98.54 (as of May 5, 2026), representing a 26% decline from its 52-week high of $133.43. The stock's valuation at approximately 14.3x forward FY2027 EPS ($6.89 consensus) creates a clear disconnect with the underlying business quality: 57.9% gross margins, 23.6% operating margins, 38% ROE, $1.9B cash with no outstanding borrowings, and nearly $1B in annual free cash flow. This business generates returns on capital that most consumer companies cannot replicate, yet trades at a multiple typically reserved for low-growth commodity producers.

The market is pricing two specific fears: (1) tariff-driven margin compression from Vietnam manufacturing concentration, and (2) HOKA growth deceleration from 24% to mid-teens. Both are real. But the valuation already reflects a significant discount to the company's FY2026 EPS guidance of $6.80-$6.85 (for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026), while the four-quarter earnings beat record (average surprise +32%) suggests guidance remains conservative. The approximately $1.8B in remaining buyback authorization (as of late December 2025) provides a floor mechanism that management has demonstrated willingness to execute.

The prior BUY recommendation remains intact. The stock has declined approximately 8% since the April 13 report, but fundamental metrics have improved slightly (estimates revised upward +0.4% over 90 days), meaning risk/reward has improved.

## Company Overview

Deckers operates a two-brand portfolio generating $5.0B in annual revenue: UGG ($2.53B, 50.8% of sales) and HOKA ($2.23B, 44.8%). The company follows an asset-light manufacturing model with all production outsourced to independent contractors, primarily in Vietnam. Distribution is split between DTC (43% of revenue, higher margin) and wholesale (57%). International sales represent 36% of revenue and grew 26.3% YoY in FY2025, representing the clearest long-term growth vector.

The fiscal year ends March 31. CEO Stefano Caroti leads the company. Deckers completed a 6-for-1 stock split in September 2024 and sold the Sanuk brand in August 2024, concentrating the portfolio on its two strongest assets.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is decelerating. Total revenue growth moderating from 18.2% (FY2024) to 16.3% (FY2025) to ~8-9% guided for FY2026. HOKA specifically is decelerating from 28% → 24% → mid-teens. The mathematical difficulty of maintaining 20%+ growth on a $2.2B revenue base is the primary driver.

However, several growth vectors remain underappreciated:

1. **International penetration:** At 36% of sales growing 26.3% (FY2025) and 15% in Q3 FY2026, international markets — particularly Asia — offer years of runway. HOKA's brand awareness outside the US remains early stage.

2. **HOKA retail store buildout:** Management is investing significantly in owned retail, with 61 HOKA company-owned stores as of the latest filing. This simultaneously drives revenue growth and margin expansion through DTC mix improvement.

3. **UGG continues to grow.** UGG grew 13.1% in FY2025 with a 39.6% brand-level operating margin — higher than HOKA's 38.0%. The market narratively treats UGG as mature, but it continues generating above-market growth with extraordinary profitability through successful category extension into year-round footwear.

4. **Estimate revisions are modestly positive:** Current-year EPS estimates revised +0.4% over 90 days, next-year +0.7%. This confirms that the fundamental trajectory is stable to improving despite the stock decline.

Consensus projects $6.89 EPS for FY2027 and $7.33 for FY2028 — representing approximately 9% growth over FY2025's $6.33 EPS and roughly flat-to-modest growth versus FY2026 guidance of $6.80-$6.85. The modest step-up from FY2026 to FY2027 consensus likely reflects tariff-related margin headwinds being partially offset by revenue growth and buybacks. These estimates are likely conservative given the four-quarter beat streak averaging 32% surprise magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

At $98.54, DECK trades at:
- 14.3x FY2027E EPS ($6.89)
- 13.4x FY2028E EPS ($7.33)
- ~14.6x trailing FCF ($958M / $14.0B market cap = 6.9% FCF yield)

Among direct comparables, Wolverine trades at approximately 14.8x. DECK's quality profile (margins, ROIC, balance sheet) far exceeds peers yet trades at or below their multiples.

Broadening the comparison to consumer branded goods companies with similar margin profiles: LULU trades at ~23x, ONON at ~45x, NKE at ~28x. DECK's 14x on better margins and balance sheet metrics represents clear relative undervaluation.

The stock has declined 26% from its 52-week high. The market's concerns:

1. **Tariff risk on Vietnam sourcing** — legitimate, quantified below in Risk Assessment
2. **HOKA growth deceleration** — acknowledged, but the stock has derated from ~25x to ~14x, pricing in far more deceleration than guided
3. **Consumer macro weakness** — valid in the current environment, but DECK sells premium footwear to a more affluent consumer cohort

At 14x forward earnings with 6.9% FCF yield, under reasonable assumptions for cost of equity and reinvestment rates, the stock appears to price in terminal growth materially below the 8-9% revenue growth the company is delivering today and well below the EPS growth consensus projects. The margin of safety is significant.

**Fair value range:** Applying 18-20x to $6.89 FY2027E EPS yields $124-138. This represents 26-40% upside from current levels. A more conservative 16x multiple (acknowledging tariff risk) yields $110, or 12% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

HOKA and UGG occupy distinct positions that are difficult to replicate:

**HOKA:** The brand pioneered maximal cushioning in performance running and has successfully crossed into lifestyle/fashion running. Its competitive moat rests on: (1) first-mover advantage in max-cushion design language, (2) strong community ties in the ultra-running segment that provide authenticity, (3) design distinctiveness (identifiable at a distance), and (4) rapid product innovation cadence with "higher frequency product drops."

The competitive threat from On Running (ONON) is real but differentiated — On occupies a different aesthetic niche (minimalist Swiss design vs. HOKA's maximalist cushioning). Nike's renewed focus under new leadership is the more material threat, as Nike's distribution and marketing scale can pressure any performance running brand.

**UGG:** Competitive position is arguably stronger than HOKA's because it is more defensible. The sheepskin construction, brand recognition, and cultural cachet are extremely difficult to replicate. UGG has survived multiple "death" narratives over two decades and continues generating 10%+ growth with 40% operating margins. The brand's extension into platform styles, men's categories, and spring/summer collections has widened its TAM significantly.

Both brands benefit from "affordable luxury" positioning — premium enough to signal taste, accessible enough for the broader consumer.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff/Vietnam Concentration (HIGH SEVERITY, HIGH PROBABILITY):**
The majority of manufacturing occurs in Vietnam. Current US tariff policy creates direct COGS pressure. Management's response — "selective, staggered, and strategic price increases" plus manufacturer cost-sharing — may not fully offset impacts. At 57.9% gross margin, a 300-500bps compression to 53-55% would reduce EPS by approximately $0.80-1.30, or 12-19% impact. This is the primary near-term risk and likely explains the depressed multiple.

On Running sources approximately 90% of footwear from Vietnam, while Nike sources approximately 51% with the remainder spread across Indonesia and other countries. Deckers' relative exposure is among the highest in the peer group alongside On Running, though the broader industry faces directionally similar headwinds.

**2. Sheepskin Supply Chain (MODERATE SEVERITY, LOW PROBABILITY):**
Only two tanneries in China process all of UGG's sheepskin — extreme concentration. A geopolitical disruption to US-China supply chains could impair UGG's ability to source its core material. At $1B in brand operating income, this is existential-level concentration risk, though the probability of a complete disruption remains low given sheepskin is not a strategic commodity subject to export controls.

**3. HOKA Growth Deceleration (MODERATE SEVERITY, MODERATE-HIGH PROBABILITY):**
Further deceleration to single digits within 2-3 years is possible as the base grows and competition intensifies. Each 5pp reduction in HOKA growth reduces total company revenue growth by approximately 2.2pp.

**4. Consumer Macro Environment:**
Premium discretionary footwear is vulnerable to consumer confidence declines. The 10-K acknowledges "a significant portion of the products we sell... are premium retail products. The purchase of these products is discretionary." However, DECK's target demographic skews higher-income (HOKA at $150+ price points, UGG at $150-250), providing partial insulation vs. mass-market brands.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-140, +32-42%):**
- Tariff impacts prove manageable (competitors face similar headwinds, price increases stick given brand premium)
- HOKA international growth reaccelerates as retail store openings drive awareness in underpenetrated markets
- Q4 FY2026 earnings beat continues the pattern; FY2027 EPS approaches $7.50+
- Multiple re-rates from 14x toward 18-20x as market recognizes quality persistence
- Buyback reduces share count 3-4% annually

**Bear Case ($78-85, -14-21%):**
- Tariffs compress gross margins 500+ bps without offsetting price increases (consumer resistance)
- HOKA growth decelerates to single digits as Nike/On capture incremental share
- Consumer recession reduces premium footwear demand; UGG seasonal demand weakens
- Stock trades to 11-12x depressed earnings of $7 → $77-84

**Base Case ($120-125, +22-27%):**
- Company delivers at or slightly above FY2026 guidance, demonstrating tariff management
- Market assigns 17-18x forward FY2027 earnings as growth stabilization becomes apparent
- 17.5x × $6.89 = $121; 18x × $6.89 = $124

## Conclusion

DECK presents a compelling risk/reward at 14x forward earnings for a business generating 24% operating margins, 38% ROE, and $1B in annual free cash flow with no outstanding borrowings. The stock is pricing in a permanent derating that would require sustained margin compression AND growth collapse — both are possible but unlikely given the brand strength demonstrated over 4 consecutive quarters of 12-63% earnings surprises, management's multi-pronged tariff mitigation strategy, and the international growth runway.

The specific concern preventing a higher-conviction rating is the Vietnam tariff exposure, which could compress margins 300-500bps without full pass-through. This affects the broader competitive set, though Deckers' concentration in Vietnam is among the highest in the peer group. The stock already discounts approximately 1,000bps of multiple compression from its peak (25x → 14x). At these levels, the asymmetry favors buyers.

Maintaining BUY recommendation with a 9-12 month horizon, targeting $121 base case. Risk management: reduce position below $88 (loss of 10% from current) and exit below $82 if accompanied by guidance cuts.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.89 represents only ~0.6-1.3% growth over FY2026 guidance of $6.80-$6.85, not 9% as originally implied; additionally, Deckers' tariff exposure relative to peers is higher than initially characterized, and remaining buyback capacity is ~$1.8B rather than $2.25B. These corrections reduce the near-term earnings growth argument but do not invalidate the quality-at-a-discount thesis.
