## Executive Summary

Arm is one of the highest-quality revenue models in semiconductors — FY2026 cost of revenue was $121M on $4,920M of revenue, a 97.5% gross margin, against a balance sheet with $3,058M of cash, total liabilities at 23% of assets and no tagged debt balance. Revenue grew 22.8% in FY2026 and 22.4% in the June 2026 quarter, data-center royalty revenue more than doubled year over year in two consecutive quarters, and the company has beaten non-GAAP consensus in four straight quarters. The architectural share shift in cloud compute is real and is evidenced by licensees' own products rather than by Arm's assertions: Nvidia Vera in production, Google Axion in AI infrastructure, AWS Graviton5 at "tens of millions of cores," Microsoft Cobalt 200 on Arm Compute Subsystems, and Qualcomm's Dragonfly C1000.

The problem is price against a forward set that decelerated in the most recent quarter. At $271.89 the stock trades at 121.9x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $2.23 and 88.6x FY2028 consensus of $3.07 — a PEG of roughly 2.35 on 37.7% expected FY2028 earnings growth — with enterprise value at 58.4x trailing revenue and a trailing-twelve-month free-cash-flow yield near 0.5%. In the same quarter that produced a 10.8% EPS beat, management cut FY2027 royalty growth expectations to "closer to the high teens" from about 20% and guided Q2 royalty growth to "low teens," while the company's own forward licensing indicator (annualized contract value) grew +13% versus +22% a quarter earlier. Independently, tagged remaining performance obligations have fallen from $2,484M (3/31/2024) to $2,123M (6/30/2026). Licensing revenue growth has been accelerating against a shrinking contracted base, and roughly 34% of last quarter's licensing revenue ($193M of $574M) came from a technology-licensing and design-services agreement with controlling shareholder SoftBank.

Three structural changes compound the valuation risk. First, the March 2026 AGI CPU launch makes Arm a merchant silicon vendor competing with the licensees that pay its royalties; management guides first-generation gross margin at high-30s to low-40s%, so scale in silicon mechanically degrades blended margin (management's $25B FY2031 framework implies roughly 69% blended gross margin, not 97.5%). Second, GAAP operating margin fell to 7.1% in Q1 FY2027 as share-based compensation reached $343M, or 26.6% of revenue and +42.3% year over year — faster than revenue. Third, insiders sold approximately $58.6M of stock on the open market between April and June 2026 with no offsetting purchases, including two executives who took ordinary-share holdings to zero. Base case fair value of $230 sits 15.4% below spot; probability-weighted expected return is roughly -9%. Trim.

## Company Overview

Arm designs and licenses processor architectures and CPU/GPU/NPU core designs. Revenue arrives in two lines: licensing and other revenue (upfront and term fees for architecture licenses, core designs, Compute Subsystems and design services) and royalty revenue (per-unit or per-value fees when licensees ship silicon, frequently years after the license is signed). FY2026 split: licensing $2.31B (+25%) and royalty $2.61B (+21%), reconciling to the $4,920M total.

The defining change is that Arm is no longer purely a licensor. On March 24, 2026 it announced the Arm AGI CPU — its first production silicon in roughly 35 years, a data-center CPU on TSMC 3nm using Neoverse V3 cores, co-developed with Meta. Reported launch partners include Meta, OpenAI, Cerebras, Cloudflare and SK Telecom, with ByteDance and Oracle added subsequently by management commentary. Management now cites more than $2B of demand across FY2027–FY2028, upgraded from roughly $1B at the March event, and has said it will provide a fuller update with Q3 FY2027 results.

Structural context worth stating plainly: Arm is a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer filing 20-F annually and 6-K quarterly. There is no 10-K, no 10-Q and no proxy statement. Quantitative figures in this report are traceable to XBRL data tagged in the 20-F filed 2026-05-26 and the 6-K filed 2026-07-29; several items normally read from risk factors, the related-party note and the cash-flow statement are sourced to management's earnings-call commentary and are labeled as such. SoftBank's exact ownership is unverified, but short-interest data implies a public float of roughly 145M shares against 1,068M outstanding — a float near 13.6% [unverified — short interest of 12.0% of float and 17.46M shares short imply a float near 145M, but this is a derivation from short-interest inputs, not a disclosed float figure], which mechanically amplifies price moves in both directions.

Three observations change how this P&L should be read.

**GAAP operating margin collapsed to 7.1% while non-GAAP was reported at ~41%, and the gap is widening for economic reasons.** Q1 FY2027 GAAP operating expense of $1,191M (COGS $36M + R&D $838M + SG&A $317M) compares with non-GAAP opex of $733M — a $458M gap, of which $343M is identifiable share-based compensation. Adding SBC back to GAAP operating income yields $434M, or 33.7% of revenue, materially below the reported ~41% non-GAAP margin, which means the non-GAAP figure excludes more than equity comp (plausibly acquired-intangible amortization and payroll taxes on vesting; the reconciliation table was not retrievable). SBC at 26.6% of revenue growing 42% against 22% revenue growth is the single largest drag on GAAP earnings quality. Management has said opex should grow slower than revenue by the end of FY2027; that promise is now the crux of the margin thesis.

**Net income is flattered by non-operating items.** Q1 FY2027 net income of $270M was three times GAAP operating income of $91M. Candidate drivers — interest income on cash that grew from $1,956M to $3,058M year over year, a low or negative effective tax rate from UK R&D incentives and excess equity-compensation deductions, and investment marks — could not be decomposed without the income statement. Core earnings power should therefore be read as the operating line plus interest income, not as reported net income.

**Free cash flow is real but extremely lumpy, and capex has stopped being a rounding error.** Management disclosed Q1 FY2027 FCF of $665M against trailing-twelve-month FCF of $1.4B, meaning roughly half of the year's free cash flow landed in one quarter. Licensing revenue is front-loaded on large upfront agreements and Q4 is seasonally largest ($1,490M in Q4 FY2026). A 4x-quarter run rate of $2.66B is a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast; the disclosed TTM figure of $1.4B implies a 0.48% FCF yield on $290B of market value. Capex went from $92M (FY2024) to $219M (FY2025) to $545M (FY2026), including $471M in the December 2025 quarter alone. Most consistent with the AGI CPU program — mask sets, emulation and test infrastructure, capacity prepayments — though attribution could not be verified from the property or cash-flow notes.

Dilution is well controlled despite the SBC. Shares went 1,040.3M → 1,057M → 1,064M → 1,068M, net dilution of only 0.4–1.6% annually, because vesting is largely net-settled (visible in code-F tax withholdings, including 143,316 shares withheld from the CEO at $209.16 on 2026-05-15). The economic cost of equity comp appears in the P&L and in cash used for withholding, not primarily in share count. Both statements are true and should not be collapsed: ownership dilution is modest; expense dilution is large.

Balance-sheet resilience is high. At 6/30/2026: assets $11,196M, liabilities $2,566M, equity $8,630M, cash $3,058M. No debt balance was tagged; the reasonable reading is de minimis leverage with liabilities consisting mainly of deferred revenue, payables, leases and taxes — an inference from absence, not a verified filing fact. Solvency is not the binding question here.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth remains strong. Q2 FY2027 guidance is $1.38B ±$50M (+22% at midpoint) with licensing up ~30% and non-GAAP EPS of $0.47 ±$0.04. My own FY2027 build — Q1 actual $1,289M plus Q2 guidance midpoint $1,380M for a $2,669M first half, with the second half growing ~20% over H2 FY2026's $2,732M — implies roughly $5.9B (+21%) and EV/sales near 48x.

But the composition of that growth deteriorated in the June quarter in three specific ways.

**Royalty guidance was cut.** FY2027 royalty growth moved to "closer to the high teens" from about 20%, and Q2 royalty growth was guided to "low teens." Management attributed handset weakness to elevated memory prices raising device costs, partly offset by higher royalty rates from Armv9 and Compute Subsystems penetration. This mechanism is corroborated elsewhere in the device chain — memory cost inflation has landed on device assemblers and their bills of materials, and Qualcomm and Arm both traded lower on smartphone-demand warnings tied to memory. Royalty is the more durable, higher-quality half of Arm's revenue; it is the line decelerating.

**The forward licensing indicator halved.** Annualized contract value grew +22% in Q4 FY2026 and +13% in Q1 FY2027, while reported licensing revenue grew +29% and +23% in those quarters. Tagged remaining performance obligations: $2,484.4M (3/31/2024) → $2,225.5M (3/31/2025) → $2,071.4M (3/31/2026) → $2,122.6M (6/30/2026). Licensing revenue has accelerated against a flat-to-shrinking contracted base. The benign reading — Arm is recognizing contracts faster on architecture and CSS deals while total customer commitment is unchanged — is live, and RPO explicitly excludes usage-based royalties and may exclude short-duration or variable-consideration arrangements whose disclosed scope I could not read. The hard fact is that neither of Arm's two forward indicators is accelerating.

**A material share of licensing growth is intercompany-adjacent.** Of $574M in Q1 licensing revenue, $193M came from the SoftBank technology-licensing and design-services agreement, with a guided run rate of roughly $200M per quarter for the rest of FY2027 — approximately 15% of total revenue and 34% of licensing revenue, annualizing near $800M, paid by the party that controls the board. This may be ordinary commercial work at arm's-length pricing; the terms, duration and pricing basis are unverified because the related-party note was not retrievable. Either way, it should be valued differently from a competitive third-party win, and its persistence depends on SoftBank's own silicon ambitions.

Against that, the data-center story is the strongest growth vector in the file: royalty revenue from data center more than doubled year over year in each of the last two quarters, Neoverse shipments have surpassed 1.5 billion cores with the most recent 500 million shipping in nine months versus six years for the first billion, and every major hyperscaler custom CPU is Arm-based. Management claims roughly 50% share of top hyperscaler cloud compute deployments (unverified).

The AGI CPU is the swing factor and the quality problem simultaneously. Management's $25B FY2031 ambition (roughly $15B silicon plus $10B IP, second-hand from one call summary) requires a 38.4% five-year revenue CAGR from FY2026's $4.92B and, at guided silicon gross margin of high-30s to ~50%, implies blended gross margin near 69%. Capitalizing near-100%-gross-margin IP economics onto a $25B revenue figure double-counts quality. Management's counter — that incremental operating cost is modest because silicon design overlaps existing IP development — would make operating dilution less severe than gross dilution, and is the item to test at the Q3 FY2027 update.

Estimate revisions are positive but far from the magnitude that signals an inflection: current quarter +8.2% over 30 days and +10.1% over 90 days, current year +2.5%/+2.7%, next quarter +0.3%/-2.0%, next year -0.4%/+2.2%. This is consensus tracking a small beat, not consensus chasing a re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is genuinely improving in the data center and genuinely deteriorating in licensee relations. Both matter.

On the positive side, hyperscalers designing their own CPUs expands Arm's royalty base while compressing merchant silicon vendors, and Armv9 plus Compute Subsystems raise royalty rate per unit — a durable content story that has insulated revenue from weak handset units. Software gravity around the architecture makes displacement slow.

Three offsets deserve weight. First, royalty rate is a partial substitute for volume growth and has a ceiling; once Armv9/CSS penetration matures, revenue reverts to unit growth. Second, Arm has become a competitor to its own licensees in data-center CPUs, and the rational licensee hedge is architectural diversification — Qualcomm's reported December 2025 acquisition of RISC-V designer Ventana Micro is the visible instance, and Arm has itself characterized RISC-V as its most significant long-term competitive threat. RISC-V operates on a multi-year lag, which is exactly why it is easy to under-weight at 58x sales. Third, Arm's principal legal lever over licensee behavior appears to have been tested and failed: reporting indicates a Delaware jury sided with Qualcomm in December 2024 on the transferability of Nuvia's licenses, the court dismissed Arm's remaining claims in October 2025, and Qualcomm's counter-suit alleging interference and self-preferencing was headed to trial. These litigation facts come from a secondary source with demonstrated numerical errors and should be treated as reported rather than established.

The operational question on the silicon side is well captured by Jim Keller's framing, quoted in the same reporting: shipping silicon on time, at scale, with data-center support is a fundamentally different business than licensing IP. Management has flagged supply constraints across wafers, substrates, test capacity and memory.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory (unverified, highest potential impact).** One tier-3 outlet, attributing the item to Bloomberg on 2026-05-15, reports that the FTC opened a formal antitrust investigation into whether Arm would refuse or degrade architecture licenses to rival designers while ramping its own chip business, alongside a document-preservation demand; the same reporting describes Qualcomm complaints to the FTC, European Commission and Korea FTC in March 2025, a Korea FTC office inspection in November 2025, and an EC inquiry. The fact pattern is structurally coherent — a licensor turning competitor is textbook — but the source misstates AGI CPU demand by roughly 10x versus management's on-record figure, and no tier-1 corroboration was found. I score this as a plausible, unpriced tail rather than an established fact. Whether the FY2026 20-F disclosed any of it is unknown; the risk-factor and legal-proceedings sections were not retrievable.

**Related-party and control risk.** Roughly 15% of Q1 revenue and 34% of licensing revenue came from the controlling shareholder, guided to continue near $200M per quarter. Terms are unverified. With a float near 13.6% of shares [unverified — derived from short-interest inputs, not a disclosed float], SoftBank's own capital needs are a live overhang for a name whose parent has been under funding scrutiny [unverified — no SoftBank funding-scrutiny detail appears in the research brief]; forced or opportunistic supply into a thin float would be disorderly in either direction.

**Margin and execution risk from the silicon pivot.** Every incremental silicon dollar at high-30s/low-40s gross margin is roughly 55–60 cents of blended gross-margin drag. Capex has risen 6x in two years, working capital and inventory will follow, and Arm has no modern track record shipping production silicon at data-center scale.

**Earnings-quality risk.** GAAP operating margin of 7.1%, SBC at 26.6% of revenue growing 42%, and net income at 3x operating income on undecomposed non-operating items together mean the reported non-GAAP profitability is a management-defined construct that excludes more than equity comp. Standard multi-factor solvency screens are also distorted by these items — solvency is not the concern; earnings translation is.

**Cyclical demand risk.** Handset royalty softness is driven by memory-price inflation raising device bills of materials — a mechanism now visible across the device chain and unlikely to resolve while HBM-driven DRAM scarcity persists [unverified — the memory/handset link is in the brief, but the "HBM-driven DRAM scarcity" characterization is not stated in the sources]. Broader AI-capex sentiment is the dominant driver of the multiple; the brief documents a sector-wide AI/semiconductor selloff in July 2026 that erased more than $1T of semiconductor market value on AI-spending concerns. ARM, at 89x FY2028, is among the most exposed names in that scenario.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Arm sits at the architectural center of the largest capital-spending cycle in computing history, and it is monetizing it three ways at once: rising royalty rates from Armv9/CSS, data-center royalty revenue doubling year over year, and now full silicon economics via the AGI CPU, where demand has already been revised from ~$1B to >$2B across FY2027–FY2028. Gross margin is 97.5%, the balance sheet carries $3.06B of cash against effectively no debt, net dilution runs under 2% annually, and the company has beaten four consecutive quarters. If management delivers on opex growing slower than revenue by late FY2027, if the silicon business carries incremental operating margin near the IP business as management argues, and if FY2029 EPS approaches $4.00, a persistent premium multiple on a ~13.6% float takes the stock to $400. Management's $25B FY2031 ambition, if remotely achievable, makes today's price look irrelevant.

**Bear case.** The forward indicators already turned: royalty growth guided down to high teens and then low teens, ACV growth halved to +13%, and remaining performance obligations have declined 14.5% over two fiscal years (from $2,484.4M at 3/31/2024 to $2,122.6M at 6/30/2026 — a 14.6% decline; roughly 16.6% from the FY2024 to FY2026 fiscal year-ends) while reported licensing revenue accelerated. Fifteen percent of revenue is paid by the controlling shareholder on unverified terms. GAAP operating margin is 7.1% because equity compensation is growing at twice the rate of revenue. The growth engine management is selling — merchant silicon — carries roughly 40% gross margin against a 97.5% corporate base and puts Arm in direct competition with the licensees that fund its royalty stream, which invites both regulatory scrutiny and RISC-V diversification. At 121.9x FY2027 and 88.6x FY2028 consensus, with a 0.5% free-cash-flow yield, there is no margin of safety for any of it. Multiple compression to 49x FY2028 — still a premium to every comparable — is $150.

**What resolves it.** The Q3 FY2027 report, where management has promised a detailed AGI CPU update, is the highest-information event: silicon revenue recognition timing, first-generation margin realization, and whether opex growth has in fact fallen below revenue growth. Royalty growth and ACV in the same print determine whether the June quarter's deceleration was a memory-price air pocket or a trend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $230 (-15.4% from $271.89), bull $400 (+47.1%), bear $150 (-44.8%). Probability-weighted expected return of approximately -9% at 45/25/30 base/bull/bear weights.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing positions into strength over the next four to eight weeks rather than at market on weakness; the stock has already retraced 40% from its June high and near-term implied volatility at 87.1% in backwardation favors selling upside calls against retained shares over outright liquidation. Reduce to a residual position sized for the bull-case optionality — for most portfolios, roughly one-third of a full weight — and hold that residual through the Q3 FY2027 print. Do not short: 12% of a thin float is already short, days to cover is 1.9, skew is call-heavy, and a squeeze in a ~13.6% float is a real hazard.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. Royalty growth reaccelerates to 20%+ year over year for two consecutive quarters, with ACV growth back above +20% and RPO rising sequentially in both.
2. Q3 FY2027 disclosure showing AGI CPU gross margin at or above the low-40s with incremental operating margin near corporate levels, plus GAAP operating margin recovering above 15% as SBC growth falls below revenue growth.
3. Price at or below $200 (65x FY2028 consensus) with FY2028 estimates stable or rising — that combination restores a defensible margin of safety and would move this to ACCUMULATE.

**Avoid triggers (downgrade to AVOID).**
1. Tier-1 confirmation of a formal FTC or European Commission proceeding seeking remedies on licensing terms, or an adverse ruling in Qualcomm's counter-suit.
2. FY2028 consensus EPS cut more than 10% from $3.07, or FY2027 revenue growth guided below 15%.
3. A named top-five licensee publicly committing a data-center or flagship mobile program to RISC-V, or the SoftBank licensing/design-services revenue stream ending without third-party replacement.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next scheduled checkpoint is the Q2 FY2027 report (late October/early November 2026) for royalty growth, ACV and GAAP margin trajectory. The decisive checkpoint is Q3 FY2027 (late January/early February 2027), when management has committed to the detailed AGI CPU update. Interim monitoring: memory pricing and handset unit data as the near-term royalty driver, and any tier-1 reporting on regulatory proceedings.

## Conclusion

Arm is a high-quality franchise at an indefensible price. The architecture is winning in the data center, the balance sheet is pristine, and the company has beaten estimates four quarters running — none of which changes the arithmetic that 121.9x FY2027 and 88.6x FY2028 consensus earnings, on a 0.48% free-cash-flow yield, requires a decade of flawless execution to be justified. In the same quarter the company delivered a 10.8% EPS beat, it cut royalty growth guidance twice over, watched its forward licensing indicator halve, and reported that 15% of revenue came from its controlling shareholder. The strategic pivot into merchant silicon is defensible on strategy and dilutive on quality: it trades 97.5% gross margins for roughly 40%, and it turns the customers who pay the royalties into competitors.

The 40% decline from June's high has removed the most extreme excess but has not created value; median analyst target sits below spot, and consensus recommendation counts have not moved in three months. REDUCE, with a base case of $230 and a residual position retained for the AGI CPU optionality that resolves at the Q3 FY2027 report.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the "no peer trades anywhere near this" valuation argument relies on Nvidia/TSMC/EDA comparable multiples that are not present in any provided source (peer_benchmarks unavailable) and have been tagged unverified; the public-float figure (~13.6%) is a derivation from short-interest inputs rather than a disclosed number; and the "SoftBank funding scrutiny" and "HBM-driven DRAM scarcity" claims are not supported by the sources. None of these change the reported valuation multiples, which check out, but the relative-valuation and control-overhang arguments are less grounded than stated.
