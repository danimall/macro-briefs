## Executive Summary

Ethan Allen closed FY2026 (June 30, 2026) with net sales of $579.5M, down 5.7%, the fourth consecutive annual decline and 29.1% below the FY2022 peak of $817.8M. GAAP operating income fell to $45.0M, a 7.8% margin, versus 17.3% in FY2023, a 67% decline in operating profit on a 29% decline in revenue, the arithmetic of negative operating leverage across 141 company-operated design centers, 11 manufacturing facilities and one national distribution center. Gross margin has held (61.2% GAAP FY2026 vs 60.5% FY2025); the entire deterioration sits below the gross line, with SG&A at 53.3% of sales versus 50.4% a year earlier.

The balance sheet is not the problem. Cash and investments totaled $187.5M against zero borrowings, equal to $7.37 per share and 31% of market capitalization; the $125M revolver (maturing January 26, 2027) is undrawn and its springing covenant is inactive at current liquidity. The distribution policy is now larger than the earnings and cash flow supporting it. The $0.39 quarterly dividend annualizes to $1.56, exactly equal to FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.56. FY2026 operating cash flow of $52.5M less $11.0M capex produced $41.5M of free cash flow (roughly $36.5M excluding a one-time $5.0M IEEPA tariff refund), against $46.3M of dividends and $4.8M of buybacks, or $51.1M returned. Cash and investments declined $8.7M over the year, corroborating the shortfall. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.25, cut 13.8% in 90 days, sits below the dividend.

Forward indicators deteriorated sharply in the final quarter. Retail written orders went from roughly flat in Q3 to **-10.8%** in Q4; wholesale orders -11.9%; customer deposits fell 16.4% to $62.7M and wholesale backlog 9.3% to $44.3M. Against that, the shares have risen 29.7% from the May 13 low of $18.28 and 12.0% since mid-June, and the only published target we can identify is Telsey's $22, below the current price. The Bergeron proxy contest ahead of the early-November annual meeting is the dominant near-term variable, and it is genuinely two-sided: the activist's own framing is reinvestment in marketing and digital over a 24–36 month horizon, which is difficult to reconcile with a payout already exceeding free cash flow. We move from HOLD to **REDUCE**, because the price has re-rated upward into falling estimates and a deteriorating order book, and both plausible governance outcomes put the yield that supports the current price at risk.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1932 and headquartered in Danbury, Connecticut, Ethan Allen is a vertically integrated manufacturer and retailer of premium residential furnishings. Two reportable segments: Wholesale (FY2026 net sales $330.7M pre-elimination) designs, manufactures and distributes; Retail (FY2026 $511.2M, ~85% of consolidated sales) operates company-owned design centers.

The footprint at FY2026 year-end was 171 North American design centers — 141 company-operated, 30 independently licensed — versus 172 a year earlier. Of the 142 company-operated locations disclosed in the FY2025 10-K, 48 were owned and 94 leased. Manufacturing spans 11 facilities: three upholstery plants in North Carolina, three in Mexico, case goods in Vermont and Honduras, plus a sawmill, rough mill and lumberyard. Roughly 75% of furniture is produced in North America; ~25% of consolidated sales are imported finished goods.

The commercial model is central to the debate over the stock. Ethan Allen sells complimentary interior design service delivered by roughly 500 in-house designers, with custom furniture built to order at one uniform delivered price across North America. Management treats the designer relationship, not the website, as the conversion mechanism, and states that designer-assisted transactions run three to five times larger than unassisted ones, an assertion made verbally and not substantiated in filings.

Structural cost reduction has been substantial: average design center size roughly halved toward 7,000–8,000 sq ft, manufacturing consolidated from about 20 plants to four geographies, logistics from roughly 15 distribution centers and 100 warehouses to one national DC, headcount to 3,062 at FY2026 year-end (-4.6% year over year and -39.4% versus March 2019). Customer concentration is modest: the ten largest clients were 11% of FY2025 sales, with GSA individually 6%.

**The retail segment carries the revenue and almost none of the profit.** Retail operating margin ran 4.6% in FY2024, 3.8% in FY2025, 1.2% over the first nine months of FY2026, and turned to an outright operating loss of $(0.785)M in Q3 FY2026 (-0.7% of retail sales). Wholesale held a 13.1% margin in FY2025 and 9.1% in Q3 FY2026. Consolidated profitability is therefore carried by manufacturing margin, while the segment representing 85% of revenue operates near breakeven. Intersegment transfer pricing allocates margin between segments as a management convention, and Q3 is seasonally weakest, so this should not be read as literal economics, but the direction across four periods is unambiguous.

**Fourth-quarter results, adjusted for the refund, were weaker than the GAAP optics.** Q4 net sales $146.8M (-8.5%); GAAP gross margin 63.1% versus 59.9%, but that includes the $4.955M IEEPA refund worth roughly 340bp, and adjusted gross margin was 59.7%, essentially flat. GAAP operating margin 9.8% versus 9.5%; **adjusted operating margin 7.4% versus 9.7%**, a 230bp decline. Adjusted EPS $0.36 versus $0.49. The reported "$0.02 beat" compares non-GAAP $0.36 to a non-GAAP consensus of $0.34 drawn from two analysts; revenue missed by roughly $1.9M.

**A reconciliation detail materially changes the FY2027 starting point.** Q4 adjustments to operating income total -$3,524K and include the -$4,955K refund; full-year adjustments total +$1,635K and contain no IEEPA line. Both reconcile exactly, so FY2026 adjusted operating income of $46.7M and adjusted EPS of $1.61 **embed** the refund. Stripping it: adjusted operating income roughly $41.7M (7.2% margin) and adjusted EPS roughly $1.46. The company's explanation is defensible, since the refunded cost was incurred earlier in the same fiscal year and nets out annually, but for forecasting purposes $1.61 is not a clean base, because FY2027 carries a tariff run-rate with no offsetting recovery.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** At 6/30/2026: cash $73.6M, short-term investments $59.6M, long-term investments $54.2M = $187.5M; total assets $719.7M; liabilities $248.2M; equity $471.5M ($18.54/share book). Inventories $148.5M (+5.4%) against declining sales is a mild negative for working-capital discipline. Reported debt/equity of 0.53 is composed of capitalized operating leases ($120.4M total) rather than borrowings; FY2026 interest and other financing costs were $0.227M. Altman Z of 3.42 places the company in the safe zone. Solvency is not a live question over any reasonable horizon.

**Earnings quality.** Interest and other income was $8.4M in FY2026, roughly 16% of pre-tax income of $53.2M. Part of the year-over-year increase came from a non-recurring contract modification fee from the former private-label credit card provider. The remainder is portfolio interest, rate-sensitive: $187.5M at ~4% is ~$7.5M pre-tax, or roughly $0.22/share after tax at the 25% effective rate. If the Fed eventually cuts, that is a direct EPS headwind independent of operations; if it hikes, a modest tailwind. Separately, the Beecher Falls sawmill fire produced $1.4M of losses against $2.3M of insurance proceeds through 9M FY2026, a net accounting gain of $0.9M that should not be read as operating improvement, with $2.5–3.5M of rebuild capex ahead.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here on current evidence, and the forward indicators are worse than the reported revenue line.

Written orders lead delivered revenue. Retail orders decelerated from -0.2% in Q3 FY2026 to -10.8% in Q4; wholesale from -7.6% to -11.9%; full-year -6.1% and -11.2%. Customer deposits, the cash collected on undelivered retail orders, fell 16.4% year over year to $62.7M, which the company attributes to delivered sales outpacing incoming orders. Wholesale backlog fell 9.3% to $44.3M, attributed to both lower order volume and shorter lead times. Neither customer deposits nor wholesale backlog is defined by the company as total backlog, and customer deposits represent partial prepayments rather than full contract value, so neither should be treated as a comprehensive order-book measure, but both point the same direction.

Consensus has followed. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 27.1% over 90 days to $0.26, next quarter 21.6% to $0.34, and current year 13.8% to $1.25. Coverage is two analysts, so these estimates carry low statistical weight, but the direction and magnitude are consistent with the order data and the Telsey target cut to $22.

Management provided **no FY2027 revenue, margin or EPS guidance** on the July 29 call. The only forward quantification was roughly $11M of capex and roughly $15M of annual tariff exposure at current rates. On a simple sensitivity — SG&A held near $305–306M, gross margin at 59.5% (tariff drag partly offset by roughly 5% average retail price increases taken during FY2026), other income $8M, 25% tax, 25.4M shares — flat sales produce EPS near $1.37 and sales down 6% produce EPS near $0.80. That is our scenario, not guidance. Consensus $1.25 sits between them. All three outcomes are below the $1.56 regular dividend.

Two offsets deserve weight. First, the State Department/GSA order stream recovered in Q4, with Kathwari noting increases continuing into early FY2027, though he also flagged a possible new bid process for government contracts. Second, five new company-operated openings are planned for FY2027 (Victoria Gardens, Aventura, Burlington, Brooklyn, Naples), so the network is being repositioned rather than only shrunk. Against a retail segment operating near breakeven, however, incremental openings are as likely to add fixed cost as earnings in the near term.

Tariffs are a genuine but partly-absorbed headwind. The sequence: IEEPA tariffs invalidated by the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026 with $5.0M refunded in Q4 FY2026; Section 232's 25% on upholstered wood products from Mexico effective October 2025 (Ethan Allen runs three Mexican upholstery plants, management's largest remaining exposure); Section 122's 10% global tariff expired July 24, 2026; Section 301 at 10–12.5% effective July 24, 2026, hitting Honduras production and Asian imports. The $15M run-rate equals 2.6% of FY2026 sales, or roughly $0.44/share after tax, but FY2026 already absorbed part of it, so the incremental FY2027 burden is smaller and cannot be sized precisely from available disclosure.

Peer medians in Home Furnishings are P/E 19.5x and 2.0% yield (LEG 6.6x, LZB 19.5x, MHK 16.4x, SGI 29.4x). On forward earnings Ethan Allen trades essentially **at** the peer median (19.0x versus 19.5x) while its revenue declines mid-single digits and its estimates fall, a poor relative proposition. The apparent cheapness lives entirely in the ex-cash and asset-backed framings: buying the operating business for $16.34/share against roughly $1.01 of operating EPS (FY2027 consensus $1.25 less roughly $0.24 of after-tax portfolio income) is 16.2x operating earnings on a declining top line. That is not a discount.

Asset backing provides a soft floor. Book value of $18.54 comprises $7.37 of net cash and investments and roughly $8.09 of net PP&E, including 48 owned design centers and 11 manufacturing/logistics properties. In a liquidation or take-private framing, the real estate and cash are worth something close to book; in an operating framing, that PP&E is impairment-exposed if the footprint shrinks further, and FY2026 already carried $1.471M of lease exit costs against $107.7M of right-of-use assets.

The distribution is where the valuation debate resolves. At $1.56 the regular dividend is 100% of FY2026 GAAP EPS, 107% of clean adjusted EPS, and 125% of FY2027 consensus; including the $0.25 special, declared distributions of $1.81 were 116% of GAAP EPS. FY2026 free cash flow of $41.5M ($36.5M ex-refund) funded $51.1M of returns, with the difference visible in the $8.7M decline in cash and investments. At that pace the $187.5M balance sheet supports the payout for many years, but the yield is being part-financed by the balance sheet while sales decline. A market that fully believed in dividend maintenance would not price a 6.6% yield on a debt-free company; the yield itself is the market's probability estimate of a reset.

Our scenario values, all 12-month:

- **Base $21** (-11.4% price, roughly -5% total return with the dividend). FY2027 EPS near consensus $1.25, 16.8x, or ex-cash 13.5x on $1.01 of operating EPS. Consistent with the $22 published target and with continued mid-single-digit revenue decline.
- **Bear $17** (-28.3%). Sales down 6–8% with retail segment losses, FY2027 EPS near $0.80–0.90, dividend reset to $0.90–1.00 to align with cash generation, and the shares reverting toward the May low of $18.28 and then toward tangible book support. A dividend cut would force turnover in a shareholder base that owns this for yield.
- **Bull $29** (+22.3%). The activist wins seats or settles, orders stabilize as the FY2026 comparison eases and the State Department stream recovers, the 1,757,364-share authorization (6.9% of shares) is used aggressively near current prices, and the market capitalizes a governance change at roughly 1.55x book / 20x a stabilized $1.45.

Probability-weighting roughly 45% base / 30% bear / 25% bull produces a low-single-digit expected total return including the dividend, which is inadequate compensation for the dispersion and negative on price alone.

## Competitive Landscape

Both sides of the activist dispute agree on the operating asset. Bergeron, on Bloomberg: "the business is fundamentally very strong… Farooq is correct, the domestic manufacturing is a huge advantage for the business in a tariff world, but there's been no attention paid to sales and marketing and especially digital." The 75% North American manufacturing base is a relative tariff advantage, though the Mexico and Honduras plants sit on the wrong side of the specific tariffs actually enacted.

The competitive record is the problem. Bergeron asserts revenue fell from $1.066B in 2006 to $579M in 2026 while Williams-Sonoma grew from $3.73B to $7.81B, RH from $713M to $3.44B and Arhaus from $495M (2016) to $1.38B (2026). Those figures come from his letter via trade press and we have not verified them against primary filings; they are also not like-for-like, since RH and Arhaus are asset-light importers and Williams-Sonoma is a multi-brand platform with a large e-commerce base. But the internal evidence — four consecutive years of revenue decline, operating margin from 17.3% to 7.8%, retail segment margin to breakeven — is disclosed and sufficient on its own.

Management's position is a deliberate strategic commitment rather than an oversight. Kathwari, unprompted: "I know the arguments were we could have sold a lot through digital mediums, we could have sold through Wayfair… we could have done that, our sales would have gone up, but we felt that was not the right thing to do." The FY2025 10-K itself frames online as a threat: "A significant shift in consumer preference toward purchasing products online could have a materially adverse impact on our sales and operating margin." Because the channel restriction is a long-held conviction, the probability of voluntary strategic change is low, which raises the importance of the board vote as the transmission mechanism and correspondingly raises the event risk in the shares over the next three months.

Trajectory matters more than level: share is being lost in both good and bad housing markets on the activist's reading, and we could not source comparable FY2026 peer same-store data to adjudicate. The macro backdrop is unhelpful in either case: premium discretionary purchases such as custom furniture are among the most deferrable lines in a household budget, and management has attributed weakness to housing starts, interest rates, consumer confidence, credit availability and "global unrest." ## Risk Assessment

**1. Proxy contest outcome (binary, ~November 2026).** Doug Bergeron, via DGB Investment, claims a 5.0% stake and has nominated six directors. We found no Schedule 13D from Bergeron or DGB on EDGAR; the most recent ETD 13D activity dates to 2015–2016, so the stake is a self-reported claim relayed by trade press and not yet confirmed by a beneficial ownership filing. The nominee slate (ex-Wayfair CCO, ex-eBay chief strategy officer, Chairish co-founder, ex-Neiman Marcus integrated retail officer) is credentialed for the specific critique. The board appears to comprise five directors, an inference from the five participants named in the August 7 DEFA14A rather than an explicit disclosure, meaning six nominees exceed available seats and raise a mechanical question about board expansion. Kathwari holds 1,491,613 shares (~5.9%), larger than the claimed activist position; the four non-employee directors hold roughly 12,524 shares combined, about $297K.

**2. Dividend sustainability.** Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.25 is 20% below the $1.56 regular dividend, and FY2026 returns exceeded free cash flow by roughly $9.6M ($14.6M ex-refund). A reset is not required by liquidity but would be by any normal payout discipline, and it is the single most likely trigger for a de-rating given the yield-oriented shareholder base. The asymmetry is that an activist victory premised on funding brand, marketing and digital investment makes a reset *more* likely, not less.

**3. Key-person and succession risk, named in the 10-K.** Kathwari, 82, has held Chairman, President and CEO simultaneously for 38 years, is the only executive officer with a written employment agreement, and is the designated Chief Operating Decision Maker for segment reporting. That agreement **expires June 30, 2027**. No succession plan is disclosed in the materials reviewed. This is a genuine binary inside the investment horizon irrespective of the vote.

**4. Fixed-cost deleveraging.** With SG&A at 53.3% of sales across 141 company-operated locations and 11 plants, each incremental point of sales decline flows through at a high rate; the CFO named this as the primary driver of Q4 adjusted margin compression. The 10-K quantifies $107.7M of right-of-use assets exposed to impairment on closures.

**5. Government concentration.** GSA was 6% of FY2025 sales; the FY2025 10-K added an explicit DOGE risk factor; FY2025 contract sales fell 23.7% and orders 25.4%. Q4 FY2026 showed recovery, but Kathwari flagged a possible rebid: "The government is thinking of perhaps sending a new bid for contracts."

**6. Tariffs.** ~$15M annual run-rate at current rates (~$0.44/share after tax on the total), concentrated in Section 232's 25% on Mexican upholstery. Partly absorbed in FY2026; the incremental FY2027 amount is not derivable from disclosure.

**7. Portfolio income sensitivity.** Roughly $0.22/share of after-tax EPS depends on short-rate levels. A material decline in short rates would compress this line while operations are still declining; a rise would be a modest tailwind. [unverified — specific Fed policy-rate path and dissent detail not in sources]

**8. Governance/compensation alignment.** CEO FY2025 total compensation of $3.833M rose 19.4% while net income fell 19.1% and sales fell 4.9%; the CEO-to-other-NEO ratio was 7.3x; the company-selected performance measure is net sales, which has declined four consecutive years. Reported cumulative TSR of $235 versus a $133 peer group was flat across FY2023–FY2025 and will fall materially in FY2026.

**9. Unverified disclosure.** The FY2026 10-K was not available; FY2026 segment detail, D&A and updated risk factors are unconfirmed. The $125M revolver matures January 26, 2027 with renewal unconfirmed — low risk given zero borrowings and $187.5M of liquidity, but unresolved.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is the informative feature. Near-term (12-day) ATM IV is 16.2% against 29.9% one-year realized volatility, at the 7th percentile of the current chain, so the market prices almost nothing for the next two weeks. The 3-month tenor jumps to 25.7% and 6-month to 30.3%, a contango that brackets the early-November annual meeting and two earnings reports. That structure is consistent with event risk concentrated on the vote rather than on near-term operations. The data source labels the near-term skew (-5.3%) and the 3-month skew (-29.7%) as call-heavy, which would suggest speculative upside positioning around a governance outcome; the same near-term tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 2.05–2.52 against an open-interest ratio of 0.80, so the flow and the standing book disagree and we would not build a directional conclusion from either. The prior snapshot's 894% ATM IV reading is a data artifact and should be discarded; the meaningful change since June 18 is the open-interest put/call ratio rising from 0.45 to 0.80 alongside a 12.0% price gain, indicating some accumulation of downside protection into the rally. Short interest of 10.8% of float at 2.8 days to cover is elevated but not squeeze-prone; with an activist involved, shorts carry their own event risk. Cheap near-dated volatility makes protective structures inexpensive for holders who want to stay through November, and rich longer-dated volatility argues against buying the event via options.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($29, +22%)

Ethan Allen owns a differentiated, hard-to-replicate asset: 75% North American manufacture in a tariffed world, made-to-order product, uniform national delivered pricing, roughly 500 designers, one consolidated national DC, and a brand ranked Newsweek's #1 premium furniture retailer three years running. It carries $7.37/share of net cash, zero borrowings and 48 owned properties, and gross margin has not eroded. The commercial layer — marketing at 3.2% of Q4 sales and a deliberate refusal of digital channels — is a fixable input, and a credentialed activist slate now exists to fix it. A settlement or partial board win would put digital and marketing capability against an intact production asset; on the activist's own 24–36 month frame, even partial recovery of the roughly $238M of lost revenue since FY2022 restores substantial operating leverage given a 61% gross margin. Meanwhile, 1,757,364 shares (6.9% of outstanding) remain authorized after Q4 repurchases at roughly $19.20, giving management a demonstrated willingness to buy near lows, and the GSA/State Department stream is recovering. At 1.28x book with a third of the market cap in cash, downside is asset-supported.

### Bear Case ($17, -28%)

Revenue has declined four consecutive years and the leading indicators just got worse: Q4 retail orders -10.8%, wholesale -11.9%, customer deposits -16.4%. The segment carrying 85% of revenue earns nothing (a $(0.785)M operating loss in Q3 FY2026), so consolidated earnings depend on manufacturing margin that tariffs are now taxing at roughly $15M a year. The $1.56 dividend equals 100% of FY2026 GAAP EPS and 125% of FY2027 consensus, is being part-funded from the balance sheet, and is the primary reason the stock holds a 6.6% yield rather than a lower multiple. A reset removes that support. The governance situation offers no clean exit: management has stated it will "continue" the payout while explicitly rejecting the digital channel, and the activist's plan requires spending that the current payout precludes. Add an 82-year-old CEO whose contract expires June 30, 2027 with no disclosed succession plan, four independent directors owning $297K of stock combined, and CEO pay up 19.4% in a year net income fell 19.1%. The stock has already recovered 30% from its May low into all of this, and the only visible published target ($22) is below spot.

**Which side has the better evidence?** The bear case rests on disclosed, quantified, sequential deterioration. The bull case rests on an unconfirmed 5.0% stake, an unresolved vote, and an operating recovery for which no leading indicator has yet turned. That is not a symmetric evidentiary base at 19x forward earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $21 (-11.4% price; roughly -5% total return including the dividend), bull $29 (+22.3%), bear $17 (-28.3%). Horizon 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints in the next four.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the November meeting. The shares are 29.7% above the May 13 low of $18.28 and 12.0% above mid-June while FY2027 consensus fell 13.8% and Q4 orders inflected to roughly -11%; the published target is $22. Holders who want optionality on the proxy outcome can retain a residual position and hedge with near-dated puts, which are unusually cheap (12-day ATM IV 16.2% versus 29.9% realized, 7th percentile of chain). We would not add new capital above $20.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Retail written orders improve to better than -3% year over year in Q1 FY2027 (report expected late October), with customer deposits stabilizing above roughly $60M.
2. The dividend is explicitly reset to a level covered by free cash flow (roughly $1.00–1.15/share) with the freed cash directed to buybacks near or below book value, removing the payout overhang rather than perpetuating it.
3. A negotiated settlement adds two or more of the activist nominees plus a disclosed CEO succession process, ideally with the FY2026 10-K confirming FY2026 retail segment operating income above breakeven.
4. Price below $20, which would restore roughly 15% to our base case and put the shares near 1.08x book.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE** requires a stabilized order book (two consecutive quarters of orders better than -3%) *and* a covered distribution *and* evidence of retail segment margin recovery above 2%, i.e., a real inflection rather than a governance headline.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. Q1 FY2027 retail written orders worse than -10% again, confirming Q4 as a run rate rather than a comparison effect.
2. The dividend is maintained at $1.56 while cash and investments fall below roughly $165M, indicating the balance sheet is being consumed to defend a payout that earnings cannot support.
3. A retail segment full-year operating loss disclosed in the FY2026 10-K, or right-of-use/PP&E impairment charges above roughly $10M.
4. Loss of the GSA/State Department business in a rebid, or a Kathwari departure without a named successor and a disclosed strategy.

**Reassessment timeline.** Three dated checkpoints: publication of the FY2026 10-K (expected late August, resolving segment detail and updated risk factors and any 13D confirmation), the definitive proxy and annual meeting in early November, and the Q1 FY2027 report in late October. The CEO employment agreement expiry on June 30, 2027 is the fourth, outside this horizon but relevant to position sizing.

## Conclusion

Ethan Allen is a solvent, asset-backed, deeply unlevered company whose operating business has shrunk 29% in four years and whose operating margin has more than halved, with the segment carrying 85% of revenue now earning approximately nothing. The distribution that supports the 6.6% yield equals 100% of FY2026 GAAP EPS, exceeds free cash flow, and is 25% above FY2027 consensus, and it is being part-financed from a shrinking cash pile. The Bergeron campaign is a legitimate catalyst and the activist critique is well-supported by disclosure, but the stake remains unconfirmed by any beneficial ownership filing, the slate exceeds apparent board seats, and the reinvestment program the activist advocates is difficult to reconcile with maintaining the payout that currently anchors the price.

Against that, the shares have risen 12% since mid-June and 30% from the May low, into a 13.8% cut to FY2027 consensus, a Q4 order book down roughly 11% on both sides of the business, customer deposits down 16.4%, and a published price target of $22 below the current $23.71. Our probability-weighted 12-month total return is low single digits with materially wider downside than upside dispersion.
