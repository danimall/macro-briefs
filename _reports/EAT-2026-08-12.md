## Executive Summary

Brinker closed fiscal 2026 (year ended June 24, 2026) with revenue of $5,807.4M (+7.9%), operating income of $619.9M (10.7% margin, +120bp), net income of $487.0M, GAAP diluted EPS of $10.87 (+30.6%) and free cash flow of $557.5M. Net debt of roughly $334M against $847.2M of adjusted EBITDA leaves leverage near 0.4x. Management guided FY2027 to revenue of $6.15–$6.27B and non-GAAP EPS of $12.60–$13.40, a midpoint about 4% above the $12.50 consensus, and the board raised the repurchase authorization to $750.0M effective August 10, 2026, equal to 7.7% of the current market capitalization. On the operating and balance-sheet evidence, this is a well-run business at a cyclical high.

The difficulty is what the market now pays for it. At $221.38 the shares trade at 20.4x trailing GAAP EPS, 17.0x the FY2027 guided midpoint, 18.0x that midpoint stripped of the guided $0.70 benefit from a 53rd operating week, 11.6x EV/adjusted EBITDA, and a 5.6% FCF yield. Three weeks ago, at $188.11, the same guidance would have implied 14.5x. Consensus current-year EPS moved +0.6% over that period. Essentially all of the recent move is multiple expansion, and the shares now sit above the consensus mean ($202) and median ($207) targets and 4.8% below a 52-week high set the prior session. Applying the peer median trailing P/E of 33.3x is not the right cross-check, because Brinker's 17.8% restaurant operating margin was built on sales leverage — 4.0 points of labor leverage in FY2025 alone — and that leverage is a function of comp growth that has decelerated sharply.

The composition of that comp growth is the decisive variable. Chili's traffic contributed +16.0% in FY2025, +4.4% through three quarters of FY2026, **−1.2% in Q3 FY2026**, and +1.5% in Q4. In Q4, 4.3 points of the 5.6-point comp came from menu price and mix turned negative (−0.2%), which is consistent with guests trading down into the $10.99 value platform. Maggiano's demonstrates in miniature what happens when comps go negative in this cost structure: comps −3.9%, traffic −9.3%, segment operating income down 73% to $16.5M and Q4 operating income of $1.0M against $13.4M. I cannot yet distinguish whether Q4's traffic recovery reflects durable brand pull or a single promotional product cycle (the Big Crispy launch), and that determination separates the bull path from the bear path. HOLD: keep existing exposure for the free cash flow, the 0.4x leverage and the buyback, but do not commit new capital at 17–18x forward with a base case of roughly $238.

## Company Overview

Brinker owns, operates and franchises Chili's Grill & Bar and Maggiano's Little Italy. As of June 24, 2026 the system comprised 1,635 restaurants — 1,163 company-owned and 472 franchised — across the U.S., 28 other countries and two U.S. territories. Company-owned units are 1,110 domestic Chili's, 4 international Chili's and 49 Maggiano's.

The economics are almost entirely company-operated Chili's. Chili's generated roughly $5,305M of FY2026 company sales and $792.6M of segment operating income (14.8% of segment revenue); Maggiano's roughly $454M and $16.5M (3.6%); corporate unallocated cost was approximately $189M, bridging to $619.9M consolidated. Franchise revenue was $56.5M, about 1.0% of total. Chili's is 29.4% franchised system-wide but only 8.2% franchised domestically versus 99.0% internationally; the U.S. business is capital-intensive and company-operated, while international expansion is asset-light royalty income.

Unit economics are the strongest part of the story. Implied FY2026 Chili's average unit volume of roughly $4.76M (from $5,305M over ~1,114 company-owned units) is up about 6% year over year, on an 18.5% restaurant operating margin. Maggiano's implied AUV of roughly $9.26M is down about 6%. Unit count is essentially static: system-wide 1,628 → 1,635 in FY2026, with six company-owned openings. Growth is therefore a same-store proposition, augmented by buyback arithmetic.

Concentration is meaningful: Texas (18.9%), Florida (11.8%) and California (9.2%) accounted for about 40% of company-owned restaurants as of June 25, 2025. The company employs roughly 83,840 team members, about 85% of hourly staff part-time, none unionized.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and comparable sales.** Revenue progressed $3,896.1M (FY2022) → $4,133.2M → $4,415.1M → $5,384.2M (+22.0%) → $5,807.4M (+7.9%). FY2026 Chili's comps were +9.2% against −3.9% at Maggiano's. The quarterly path matters more than the annual figure:

| Chili's comp decomposition | Q4 FY25 | 39wks FY26 | Q3 FY26 | Q4 FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable sales | +23.7% | +10.6% | +4.0% | +5.6% |
| Price | +2.7% | +4.4% | +4.6% | +4.3% |
| Mix | +4.7% | +1.8% | +0.6% | −0.2% |
| Traffic | +16.3% | +4.4% | −1.2% | +1.5% |

Price has been steady at 4.3–4.8% for two years. Traffic and mix are where the deceleration lives.

**Margins.** Operating margin expanded 120bp to 10.7%; restaurant operating margin (non-GAAP) improved 30bp to 17.8%. Two qualifications are material. First, "Other (gains) and charges" fell from $41.8M to $8.6M as FY2025's $22.4M of litigation and claims dropped to $3.4M and $14.1M of ERP implementation cost went to zero, a $33.2M favorable swing equal to 30.8% of the $107.9M GAAP operating income increase. Those items are excluded from non-GAAP EPS on both sides, so non-GAAP growth of +20.7% is the cleaner read on operations, and it is well below the GAAP +30.6% headline. Second, the cost mix is turning: food and beverage moved from 25.3% of company sales in FY2025 to 25.6–25.7% in FY2026 interim periods, with 130bp of commodity inflation in meat, seafood and poultry partly offset by 120bp of pricing, and the Q4 release cites higher beef costs plus a Florida freeze in produce. The offset has been labor, which fell from 33.6% (FY2024) to 32.2% (FY2025) to 31.4–31.5% (FY2026 interim) almost entirely on sales leverage rather than wage relief. The mechanism is symmetric: comps below cost inflation reverse that leverage into deleverage.

**An unusual reconciliation.** GAAP diluted EPS of $10.87 exceeded non-GAAP EPS of $10.74 because the company *removes* a $12.4M ($0.28/share) excess tax benefit from stock-based compensation, an amount larger than the $0.15 special-items add-back. This is conservative presentation, not aggressive. It also means the FY2027 guided range excludes stock-comp tax windfalls that are correlated with the share price: a rising stock produces GAAP EPS above the guided non-GAAP band, and a falling stock removes that cushion.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $789.4M against capex of $231.9M, producing $557.5M of free cash flow (9.6% of revenue, $12.44 per diluted share). FY2026 capex was *below* FY2025's $265.3M; FY2027 guidance takes it to $265–285M for the remodel program. Treasury purchases were $443.9M ($400.0M under the program) versus $90.2M in FY2025, at a Q3 average price of $156.78. Diluted weighted-average shares fell 46.1M → 44.8M; FY2027 guidance assumes 42.0–43.0M.

**Balance sheet.** Screening metrics look alarming and mostly are not. Current ratio of 0.31 and debt/equity of 7.22 reflect a business that collects at the point of sale before paying suppliers (a structural float, not a liquidity gap) and $811.2M of cumulative treasury stock against $443.7M of equity. The Altman Z-score of 2.71 is driven by working capital/assets of −0.175, which for this model is a feature. The genuine facts: $110.0M cash, net debt/EBITDA ~0.39x, covenant compliance affirmed, and a $1.0B revolver maturing May 2030.

One real change: on July 16, 2026 the company redeemed the $350.0M 8.25% senior notes using revolver borrowings. At the 4.93% revolver rate disclosed at March 25, 2026 (SOFR 3.68% + 1.25%), that is roughly $11.6M of annual pretax savings, about $0.21 per diluted share after tax at the ~17% effective rate. It also converts fixed-rate certainty into floating exposure (a 100bp SOFR rise reclaims ~$3.5M pretax) and draws down the facility that had been the liquidity backstop. At the March 25, 2026 quarter-end the revolver had $969.9M available net of a $30.1M letter of credit; a subsequent $350M draw for the redemption would leave roughly $620M available. The redemption premium was not disclosed; at the schedule's 4.125% tier it would be ~$14.4M of one-time FY2027 cost, which I treat as an estimate rather than a fact. Including ~$1.31B of operating lease liabilities, lease-inclusive obligations run near 1.9x EBITDA, comfortable, and a directional bound rather than a covenant metric, since EBITDA already deducts rent.

There is no dividend. All shareholder return is repurchase.

**Data notes.** The supplied profile carries EPS of $8.32 (FY2025) alongside a 21.67x P/E; that trailing multiple corresponds to the vendor's stale EPS tag rather than FY2026 reported EPS, and the valuation snapshot flags a market-date mismatch. All arithmetic here uses the authoritative $221.38 close and FY2026 reported figures. Separately, the stitched XBRL feed mis-tags FY2025 net income at $460.0M; the correct figure is $383.1M ($460.0M is pretax income).

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance implies revenue growth of 5.9% at the midpoint, or about 4.9% excluding the 53rd week, and non-GAAP EPS growth of 21.0% ($13.00 vs. $10.74). Decomposing the EPS guide:

- ~$0.70 from the 53rd operating week (management's own estimate) → underlying $12.30, +14.5%.
- ~5.4 percentage points from share-count reduction (42.5M midpoint vs. 44.8M).
- Residual underlying operating growth: roughly 9%.

That is a respectable but unremarkable growth rate for a 17–18x multiple, and it is the honest denominator for any PEG calculation. On guided EPS growth of 21%, PEG is 0.81; ex-53rd week, 1.17; on underlying operating growth, 1.89.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +1.7% (30d) / +2.6% (90d), current year +0.6% / +0.2%, next year +0.3% / −0.7%. This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is a stable, well-modeled business where consensus is roughly right. The company has beaten twice, matched once and missed once in the last four quarters, with the most recent Q4 non-GAAP EPS of $3.07 landing about $0.02 below consensus while revenue of $1,535.8M came in ahead.

The forward-looking growth question is traffic. Guidance of ~4.9% revenue growth ex-53rd week, on roughly 4% of carried pricing, implies approximately flat traffic. Management attributes July momentum to the Big Crispy chicken sandwich. Two growth levers are genuinely unquantified:

1. **Remodels.** Roughly 10% of ~1,200 U.S. Chili's annually, driving FY2027 capex up 15–23%. The CEO declined to disclose whether the four completed Dallas-area renovations produced a traffic lift. A strong early read would normally be volunteered.
2. **Franchise buy-ins.** The Q4 agreement to acquire 12 Chili's in Alabama and Mississippi (including real estate for six), closing August 27, 2026, is rational when company-operated units earn 18.5% restaurant margins on $4.76M AUVs against low-single-digit royalties. Twelve units on 1,163 is immaterial to FY2027 results; it is a signal about capital-allocation preference, not a program.

Maggiano's is the clearest negative. Segment operating income fell 73% to $16.5M, restaurant operating margin from 16.3% to 10.1%, traffic −9.3%. Management removed banquet service charges at the end of Q1 FY2026, stripping $8.9M of revenue over 39 weeks with essentially no cost offset, a deliberate value-perception trade. Whether that is a reset investment or a response to demand loss cannot be resolved from the disclosures. At 7.8% of company sales and roughly 2.7% of segment operating income, the consolidated drag is bounded; its informational value is that it shows the deleverage mechanism (170–250bp through labor and restaurant expenses) operating in real time within the same cost structure.

**Peer context.** The restaurant peer set shows wide dispersion: DPZ 19.9x, CAKE 29.0x, TXRH 33.3x, BROS 74.0x, CAVA 120.1x, median 33.3x, all trailing. EAT at 20.4x trailing is a large nominal discount to the median. Two adjustments matter before treating that as mispricing. First, TXRH and CAKE carry dividends and unit-growth pipelines; Brinker grew units 0.4% last year and pays nothing. Second, and more important, Brinker's trailing earnings sit on a 17.8% restaurant operating margin achieved through a traffic surge (+16% in FY2025) that has now normalized to roughly flat. Applying a peer multiple to peak-cycle margins is the standard way to overpay for a cyclical. The reverse framing is that a 20x multiple on a 1,600-unit casual-dining operator with static units is itself a re-rating from the 8–12x range this stock inhabited for a decade, and that is the more relevant history.

**What the current price requires.** For the shares to work from here, the FY2027 guide must hold *and* the market must continue paying 17–18x forward. On my base case I roll forward to FY2028 consensus of $13.60 and apply 17.5x, producing **$238** (+7.5%). That assumes traffic stays roughly flat, beef inflation is absorbed by pricing without eroding the value wedge, Maggiano's stops getting worse, and the buyback runs at the pace embedded in the 42.0–43.0M share guidance.

Consensus targets sit at a mean of $202 and median of $207 — both *below* spot — with a range of $139 to $260 and a distribution of 3 strong buy / 14 buy / 4 hold / 1 sell. Targets typically lag after a guidance raise (TD Cowen already raised in June), and I expect upward revisions. But when the multiple is already at the top of its post-transformation range and revision velocity is under 1%, targets below spot are a caution rather than a floor.

**Support under the price.** The 5.6% FCF yield is real, 0.4x net leverage gives management full optionality, and the $750M authorization equals 7.7% of the market cap; if fully executed at $221.38 it would retire about 3.4M shares, taking the count near 39.5M and lifting each remaining holder's proportional ownership roughly 8.6%. FY2027 share guidance of 42.0–43.0M implies materially less than full execution within the fiscal year, so this is capacity rather than a forecast. Add ~$0.21/share of fresh interest savings.

Netting these: valuation offers neither a margin of safety nor an obvious short. **Score 4.5** — the multiple is defensible on the guide but leaves nothing for execution error, and the cheapness relative to peers is largely an artifact of comparing a peak-margin operator to growth-unit franchises.

## Competitive Landscape

Chili's is winning share in a category that is not growing. The FY2025 10-K states directly that casual dining "has not seen significant growth in customer traffic in recent years," so FY2026 comps of +9.2% are share capture by construction. Supply rationalization helps: Ruby Tuesday has closed more than 770 restaurants over roughly two decades, handing surviving operators traffic without unit expansion. Third-party data cited by CNN (July 17, 2026) puts Chili's as the second-largest U.S. casual-dining chain by revenue per Technomic, with Placer.ai finding it taking a "meaningfully larger percentage of visitors" from both full-service and fast-food rivals. I could not access the underlying Technomic or Placer.ai reports.

Chili's competes down-market against QSR rather than sideways against casual dining, and the strategy has been effective so far. The "3 for Me" platform anchored at $10.99, plus explicit fast-food analogues (the "QP," the "Big Smasher," now the Big Crispy positioned as 80% larger than McDonald's filet), targets guests frustrated by QSR price increases. Management says the Chili's check runs $3–4 below the casual-dining average.

This is relative-price arbitrage on a QSR pricing cycle, and its durability rests on two variables Brinker does not control. QSR operators can reprice downward. Direct casual-dining competitors can match; the 10-K explicitly warns of "competitors employing our same strategies or discounting their offerings." Meanwhile Brinker has taken 4.3–4.8% of menu price for two consecutive years while holding the entry price point, which arithmetically narrows the check gap unless competitors price faster, and pushes the pricing burden onto non-value items. Q4's mix turning negative (−0.2%, from +4.7% a year earlier) is consistent with guests migrating into the value tier, so the same mechanism that supports traffic compresses check and margin. An alternative reading is that FY2025's mix gain was a one-time reset from menu simplification and Triple Dipper adoption that has simply annualized out; the disclosed data cannot separate these.

Two structural dependencies deserve weight. Off-premise runs through DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub and Google Food Ordering; delivery fees and to-go supplies were an unfavorable cost item in every FY2026 interim period, and the 10-K acknowledges exposure if aggregators change fees or visibility. And marketing is now contractual — long-term media purchase obligations of $21.2M (FY27), $21.1M (FY28), $4.4M (FY29) — which supports share-gain durability but converts advertising into fixed cost precisely when flexibility would matter if comps roll over.

Trajectory is improving, with a moat that is commercial rather than structural. **Score 6.0.**

## Risk Assessment

**1. Traffic-to-price transition reversing operating leverage (primary).** FY2025–FY2026 margin expansion came from sales leverage: 4.0 points of labor leverage and 3.8–4.2 points of restaurant-expense leverage in FY2025. With traffic at +1.5% in Q4 and price already at 4.3%, a return to flat or negative comps reverses that. Maggiano's has already demonstrated the magnitude.

**2. Commodity inflation with a pricing lag.** Beef, seafood and poultry are running 80–140bp unfavorable. The Q3 10-Q states the exposure plainly: a time lag between commodity increases and menu pricing "could be negatively affected." Tariff language was added to FY2026 operating-environment disclosures.

**3. Consumer regime.** CNN (July 2026) reported "consumer confidence is hovering at record lows, and many are pulling back on discretionary spending," which argues for weaker 2027 discretionary spending. Chili's value positioning is a partial hedge — trade-down from QSR and from higher-check casual dining is the plausible flight path — but a value-led chain with a $21.90 average check is not immune to a broad pullback in restaurant visits. Maggiano's ($39 check) is already demonstrating the downside end of that distribution.

**4. Gap risk around prints.** On October 29–30, 2025 the stock fell from $124.26 to $108.09 over two sessions, roughly −13%, and the 52-week range spans $100.30 to $232.47. The multiple, not the earnings, produced that. Realized one-year volatility of 47.3% on a $9.5B consumer name is high.

**5. Short interest and positioning.** 17.6% of float short, 5.1 days to cover, 5.41M shares. This cuts both ways: it fuels upside squeezes (and shorts have been badly wrong for nine months) while confirming that a substantial institutional cohort holds the deceleration view.

**6. Reflexive goodwill exposure.** $194.7M of goodwill, with the 10-K naming "the decline in the market price of our common stock" as a potential impairment trigger and annual testing in Q2. Dormant today; a sharp derating could itself contribute to a non-cash charge.

**7. Concentration.** ~40% of company-owned units in TX/FL/CA; a single dominant brand; international growth entirely dependent on franchisees, whose Q4 comps decelerated to +4.0% from +9.0% a year earlier; no active domestic development arrangements as of FY2025.

**8. Labor regulation.** California's $20/hour fast-food minimum wage and the empowered sector council are first-order cost drivers against restaurant labor at 31.4% of company sales and 83,840 employees.

**9. Litigation tail.** FY2025's $22.4M of litigation and claims (including alcohol-service cases and one-time employment and IP settlements) fell to $3.4M in FY2026. The low year does not establish that the tail is gone.

**10. Disclosure gaps.** All FY2026 full-year figures come from the unaudited August 12 earnings release; the 10-K was due by August 24, 2026 and is not yet available, so FY2026 risk-factor changes, footnotes and the contractual-obligations table are unverified. The June 17, 2026 8-K (Item 8.01) and March 2, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02, alongside $1.7M of FY2026 severance charges tied to "changes in our management team and organizational structure") could not be retrieved. The note redemption premium and the franchise-acquisition purchase price were not disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 80.9% against 47.3% realized (a 33.6-point spread, 83rd percentile within the chain) with a pronounced backwardation — 80.9% at eight days, 55.3% at 36 days, 49.7% at 127 days — prices an ±10.7% move on the nearest expiry. ATM IV rose from 60.6% to 80.9% since July 24 while the price rose 17.7%, and the put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.44 to 0.64. Near-dated skew is balanced (+0.9%) but the six-month tenor carries −4.4%.

The elevated front-end vol substantially reflects the August 12 earnings event and should decay quickly; the more durable signal is the rising put/call open interest and the six-month skew, which say that hedging demand is building as the price makes new highs. Neither confirms nor refutes the fundamental case, but the combination of rich near-term vol, rising put positioning and 17.6% short interest is consistent with a market that views the current multiple as the fragile part of the story rather than the earnings. For a holder, selling covered calls into 80% near-dated vol is a more attractive expression than adding stock. **Score 4.5** on risk factors: the balance sheet is genuinely strong, but the leverage-reversal mechanism, gap history and multiple fragility are not priced at 17–18x forward.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$290, +31%)

Chili's continues taking share from both QSR and casual dining while category supply shrinks. Big Crispy and the pasta/protein menu roadmap sustain positive traffic through FY2027, so the guide's implied flat-traffic assumption proves conservative and price-driven comps become volume-driven again. Remodels — 10% of the U.S. base annually — deliver a measurable AUV lift on top of the current $4.76M, justifying the step-up in capex. Maggiano's stabilizes as the banquet-charge reset annualizes out of the comparison in Q2 FY2027. The $750M authorization is executed aggressively at 0.4x leverage, taking the share count toward 40M, and the $0.21/share of interest savings plus continued FICA-tip-credit tax leverage (17.3% Q4 effective rate) compound. FY2028 EPS reaches roughly $15.25 and the market pays 19x for a demonstrated share-gainer: $290. The 17.6% short base amplifies the move.

### Bear Case (to ~$150, −32%)

QSR operators reprice downward or direct competitors match "3 for Me," narrowing the value wedge that Brinker has been compressing itself with 4.3–4.8% of annual menu pricing. Traffic turns negative as it did in Q3 FY2026, and the sales leverage that produced 140–150bp of labor improvement reverses into deleverage while beef and produce inflation persists. FY2027 EPS lands near $12.00 instead of $13.00, and the multiple compresses toward 12.5x as the market re-cyclicalizes a no-dividend, 0.4%-unit-growth casual-dining operator: $150. This path has precedent within the last twelve months — the stock traded at $100.30 in November 2025 and gapped 13% lower on the October print. Maggiano's provides the template for how quickly this cost structure deleverages, and the contractual $21M/year media commitment removes cost flexibility exactly when it would be needed.

### Base Case (~$238, +7.5%)

Traffic runs roughly flat, price carries comps to 4–5%, food cost inflation is absorbed with a lag that costs 20–40bp of restaurant margin, Maggiano's stops deteriorating without recovering, and the buyback runs at the pace embedded in the 42.0–43.0M share guidance. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS lands in the middle of the guided band and FY2028 consensus of $13.60 holds. Applying 17.5x to $13.60 as the market rolls forward through mid-2027 gives $238.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 25% bull / 20% bear yields an expected return near +5%, below what I would require for a cyclical consumer name with 47% realized volatility. That is the arithmetic behind HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Conclusion

Brinker has executed one of the better casual-dining turnarounds of the past decade. FY2026 delivered $5.81B of revenue, $619.9M of operating income, $487.0M of net income and $557.5M of free cash flow, and the company enters FY2027 with 0.4x net leverage, $11.6M of annualized interest savings from the July note redemption, a $750M repurchase authorization equal to 7.7% of the market capitalization, and guidance whose midpoint sits about 4% above consensus. None of that is in dispute.

What is in dispute is what to pay. The composition of Chili's growth has shifted from traffic to price — 4.3 of Q4's 5.6-point comp came from menu pricing with mix negative — and the FY2027 guide implies roughly flat traffic on ~4% of carried pricing. Strip the 53rd week and the buyback from the guided 21% EPS growth and underlying operating growth is close to 9%. The margin gains of the last two years were sales-leverage gains, and Maggiano's has already shown, at −9.3% traffic and −73% operating income, how quickly that leverage runs in reverse. Against that, the shares have re-rated from 14.5x to 17.0x forward in nineteen sessions on a 0.6% estimate revision, sit above the consensus mean and median targets, and trade 4.8% below a high set the prior session in a stock that traded at $100.30 nine months ago.

The result is a fair-value hold: a good business, a strengthened balance sheet, a real capital-return lever, and an entry price that leaves nothing for the traffic question to go the wrong way. Own it if you own it; buy it at 15x forward, not 17–18x.
