**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valaris stopped being a standalone equity on February 9, 2026, when Transocean (RIG) agreed to acquire 100% of the company in an all-stock transaction at a fixed exchange ratio of 15.235 RIG shares per VAL share. There is no collar and no fiduciary out to accept a superior proposal. VAL's price now moves with RIG's share price, and the ~23% decline from roughly $99.70 in early April to $76.54 today is almost entirely a function of RIG's stock falling, not any Valaris-specific news. At $76.54 against a 15.235x ratio, the market is implying RIG trades near $5.02. Any thesis on VAL is a thesis on Transocean's fundamentals, the pro-forma combined balance sheet, and the merger-arb spread and timing.

The standalone fundamentals underneath the deal are mixed and worse than the headline numbers suggest. FY2025 reported net income of $982.8M looks spectacular (44% net margin, 31% ROE, ~5.4x trailing P/E), but roughly $542M of that is non-operating: a $426.8M income tax benefit driven by a $523.2M valuation-allowance reversal, plus $115.4M of pre-tax asset-sale gains. Pretax income was only $552.3M and operating income was $477M. A scoring model keying off trailing P/E or reported ROE will badly misprice this stock. Q1 2026 then swung to a net loss of $(0.24)/share as floater active-fleet utilization halved YoY to 45% (from 81%), with multiple drillships warm-stacked between contracts. The cause is contract timing, not pricing collapse (floater day rates actually rose to $436k/day from $384k), but reported results will look weak precisely as the deal moves toward close.

We rate VAL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The situation is a merger-arb instrument, and the key unknowns (RIG's trajectory, the shareholder vote outcome, regulatory and Bermuda court sanction timing) are not resolvable from the data at hand. The prior HOLD sequence has now run five reports; we maintain it because the setup remains genuinely two-sided, but we have lowered several dimension scores as the standalone earnings picture deteriorated and the deal removed the levers (buybacks, opportunistic capital return) that supported the stock in 2023–2025.

## Company Overview

Valaris is one of the world's largest offshore contract drillers, operating on six continents. As of Q1 2026 it owns 45 rigs: 13 drillships (12 high-spec 7th-generation ultra-deepwater units), 1 benign-environment semisubmersible (down from 3 after 2025 retirements and the April 2026 recycling sale of DPS-1), and 31 premium jackups. It also holds a 50% stake in ARO, a 50/50 joint venture with Saudi Aramco that owns 9 jackups and is building a 20-rig newbuild fleet.

The business is day-rate contract drilling: customers bear well-construction and geological risk, and Valaris supplies rig plus crew for a daily rate. The company is Bermuda-domiciled, with ~86% of FY25 revenue non-U.S. It emerged from Chapter 11 in April 2021, and fresh-start accounting still runs through ARO basis differences. Segments are Floaters, Jackups, ARO, and Other. The competitive core is one of the highest-spec floater fleets in a structurally shrinking global floater supply; benign-environment floaters have declined roughly 45% from a 2014 peak near 280 rigs to about 150.

The Transocean combination is a consolidation of the two leading ultra-deepwater pure-plays. VAL holders would own approximately 47% of the pro-forma company. The strategic logic is scale, fleet high-grading, and synergy in a shrinking-supply floater market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue recovery has stalled.** Revenue grew $1,602M (2022) → $1,784M (2023) → $2,363M (2024) → $2,369M (2025, essentially flat).

**FY2025 earnings quality is poor.** Operating income of $477M (+35% YoY) reflects genuine cost discipline (contract drilling expense −7%), but net income overstates earnings power dramatically because of the tax benefit and asset-sale gains detailed above. The reported 44.3% net margin and 30.9% ROE are non-recurring artifacts. The $1.33B net deferred tax asset now on the balance sheet is a future non-cash tailwind, not cash earnings.

**Free cash flow is modest and set to compress.** FY25 operating cash flow of $546.2M less capex of $343.5M produced $202.7M of FCF, a 8.6% margin. 2026 capex is guided higher at $425–475M, which will pressure FCF further.

**Q1 2026 is a genuine operational trough.** Revenue of $465.4M fell 25% YoY (from $620.7M) and 13% QoQ. Operating income collapsed to $20.0M (from $143.0M in Q1'25 and $39.4M in Q4'25). The company posted a net loss of $(16.4)M. Total-fleet floater utilization dropped to 33% (from 57%). Floater revenue fell 46% YoY. This is timing, not pricing: four Valaris drillships are scheduled to return to work later in 2026, and the weakness is idle time between contracts.

**The balance sheet is clean.** Cash is roughly $578–599M, the only debt is $1.1B of 8.375% Second Lien Notes due 2030 (no maturities until 2030), and there is a $375M undrawn revolver against ~$3.16B of equity. The Altman Z-Score of 2.19 (grey zone) and D/E of 1.67 reflect the asset-heavy base, but near-term liquidity and solvency risk is low.

**Backlog is growing and supportive.** Backlog rose from $3.6B a year earlier to $4.67B (Feb 2026) and $4.93B (May 2026), led by floater awards and extensions (Floaters backlog $3.32B, including a $426M DS-4 extension). ARO backlog is ~$1.9B. This underwrites a 2026 H2 recovery as idle floaters recommence.

We score Financial Health at 5.5. The clean debt maturity profile and adequate liquidity support the score, but low FCF conversion, rising capex, a swing to a net loss in Q1, and reported earnings heavily flattered by non-cash items pull it down. This is a decline from the 7.0 recorded in April, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that direction.

## Growth Analysis

The medium-term industry setup is constructive. Floater fleet attrition (−45% since 2014), no economic newbuild construction, and an aging jackup fleet (~28% of global jackups over 40 years old) support day rates. Rystad estimates ~65% of expected 2026–2030 deepwater sanctioning has breakevens below $50/bbl and ~80% below $60/bbl, supportive of floater demand. Jackup utilization is solid near 88%, and Aramco is recalling seven suspended jackups in 2026.

Against that, near-term earnings momentum is negative. Estimate revisions are broadly falling: the current-quarter EPS estimate was cut 43.9% in 30 days (to $0.19), and the current-year estimate slipped 1.0% over 30 days despite a +5.7% 90-day move. The next-year estimate of $7.24 is up modestly (+4.3% over 90 days) but was trimmed 0.4% in the last 30 days. This does not meet the profile of a positive-inflection stock with rising estimates; the near-term direction is down, driven by the floater utilization gap.

The standalone growth question is subordinate to the deal. Valaris's forward equity value is now defined by the RIG exchange ratio, not by its own revenue trajectory. The 2026 H2 floater recovery matters mainly to the extent it supports the combined entity's valuation and RIG's share price. We score Growth Prospects at 5.0; the structural offshore tailwind is real but the near-term trough, falling near-term estimates, and standalone growth now being largely academic under the merger cap the score.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~5.4x is meaningless here for two reasons. First, the "E" is inflated by one-time tax and asset-sale items, and Q1 already showed a loss. Second, VAL is a merger-arb security. Its intrinsic value is 15.235 times the RIG share price, adjusted for the probability and timing of deal completion and the standalone break value if the deal fails.

Whether the implied RIG price near $5.02 is cheap or rich is a question about Transocean's fundamentals and the pro-forma balance sheet, not Valaris's. RIG carries substantially more leverage than VAL, a real consideration for what VAL holders will own post-close.

Analyst price targets — high $99, median $60, mean $67.27, low $45 — straddle the current price, with the median and mean below spot. This is not the pattern of a cheap inflection stock where targets act as floors; it reflects a stock trading above where analysts see standalone value, held up by the merger. The current price already embeds deal optimism, and the downside case (deal break, re-rating to loss-making standalone trough value near the $45 low target) is material.

A compressed multiple on flattered, non-recurring earnings, with the near-term operating picture at a trough and the equity value pinned to a more-leveraged acquirer, is a value-trap configuration rather than an asymmetric-upside one. We score Valuation at 4.5. Cheapness on trailing metrics here sorts the wrong way: it reflects the market pricing in the merger, non-recurring earnings, and RIG's downside, not a mispricing we can exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Valaris holds one of the highest-spec floater fleets among the offshore pure-plays, positioned in ultra-deepwater where structural supply is contracting. Top-five customer concentration is 49%, with Petrobras at 13–17% plus BP, Azule, and Eni. The Transocean combination consolidates the two leading ultra-deepwater specialists, which should strengthen pricing power and fleet high-grading in a shrinking-supply market. On the jackup side, NOC-driven demand (energy security, Aramco recalls) supports ~88% utilization.

The offsetting negative is the ARO joint venture's structural fragility (100% Aramco revenue concentration, no cash distributions since inception, PIK interest on the notes receivable, up to $1.1B of contingent newbuild funding calls) and Aramco's 2024 termination of three leased rigs, a precedent for downside. On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the fleet level, and the consolidation is a positive share-and-scale event. We score Competitive Position at 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion and structure risk dominates.** The 15.235x fixed ratio has no collar, so VAL holders bear full RIG price risk with no floor, and there is no fiduciary out. Interim operating covenants freeze M&A, dividends, buybacks, and asset sales. The break fee is $173M (up to $58M expense reimbursement on a failed shareholder vote). Remaining conditions are regulatory approvals and Bermuda court sanction. The June 10 8-K (Item 5.07) indicates the shareholder vote occurred, but we could not independently confirm the outcome or the regulatory/court-sanction timeline from available news. If the deal breaks, VAL likely re-rates to standalone value, which is loss-making at trough.

**Near-term earnings trough.** Recovery depends on idle drillships recommencing on schedule in 2026 H2.

**ARO joint-venture risk is material and under-appreciated.** Revenue is 100% Aramco; ARO has never paid a cash distribution; 2025 interest on the $400.7M notes receivable was paid in kind; the notes are subordinated, governed by Saudi law, and require Aramco consent to enforce; and up to $1.1B of newbuild funding calls could arise.

**Middle East / Strait of Hormuz exposure** cost $7.5M in Q1'26 war-risk insurance and downtime; escalation could hit both Valaris and ARO.

**Tax volatility** from the Bermuda structure produces wide effective-rate swings, with Section 7874 inversion and OECD Pillar Two overhangs.

The FY25 10-K added an entire "Risks Related to the Business Combination" section, and the 10-Q added explicit Hormuz/Middle East disclosure. Given the no-collar, no-fiduciary-out structure, the binary deal outcome, the ARO liability tail, and the Q1 trough, we score Risk Factors at 4.0. Short interest of 11.7% of float (6.4 days to cover) reflects meaningful institutional caution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.3% sits slightly below one-year historical volatility of 57.2%, so options are marginally cheap, and IV compressed sharply from 66.4% a month ago even as the price fell 7.8%. Falling IV into a falling price suggests the market views the situation as resolving (deal path, vote) rather than building toward a surprise. The put/call skew is call-heavy (−6.7%), and the term structure is flat (near 53.3% ≈ far 53.6%), consistent with uncertainty spread evenly across the deal timeline rather than concentrated at a single event. The implied ±12.9% one-month move is large for a merger-arb name and reflects the RIG-tracking risk in an uncollared all-stock deal. Nothing in the options data argues for overriding the fundamental HOLD; it corroborates a market that sees the outcome as deal-dependent, not fundamentally directional.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Transocean deal closes on schedule. The combined entity captures ultra-deepwater consolidation synergies in a structurally short floater market, RIG's share price recovers as the offshore cycle firms in 2026 H2 (four Valaris drillships back to work, day rates rising, backlog growing), and VAL holders realize appreciation through the 15.235x ratio on a re-rating RIG. Structural tailwinds (45% floater fleet attrition, no economic newbuilds, sub-$50 breakevens on most sanctioned deepwater) support durable day rates. In this case VAL tracks toward the high analyst target near $99.

**Bear Case.** RIG's stock stays weak or falls further, and because there is no collar, VAL falls with it; the ~23% decline since April is the template. Alternatively, the deal breaks on regulatory or court-sanction grounds, and VAL re-rates to standalone value at a loss-making trough, toward the $45 low analyst target. The ARO joint venture triggers a capital call or Aramco terminates rigs, adding a liability tail. Frozen buybacks remove the capital-return support that carried the stock in 2023–2025.

The base case sits between these: the deal closes, but VAL's return is capped by RIG's own leverage and share-price trajectory, with the median analyst target near $60 implying downside from current levels if standalone-value gravity reasserts before close.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The recommendation has now been HOLD for five consecutive reports; we maintain it because the situation is genuinely two-sided and the key variables (RIG price, vote outcome, regulatory/court timing) are unresolved from available data, not because of inertia.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) confirmed shareholder-vote approval plus a clear regulatory/Bermuda court-sanction timeline that de-risks completion, combined with a RIG share price that makes the 15.235x exchange offer clearly accretive versus VAL's standalone value; (2) evidence in Q2 (Aug 5, 2026) that the four idle drillships recommenced on schedule, converting the utilization trough into a visible recovery; (3) a widening merger-arb spread that offers a compelling annualized return with completion risk falling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) deal termination or a materially adverse regulatory/court development; (2) a RIG-side balance-sheet deterioration that impairs the pro-forma entity VAL holders would own; (3) an ARO capital call or Aramco rig terminations that crystallize the JV liability tail; (4) evidence the floater recovery is slipping into 2027 rather than recommencing in 2026 H2.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the shareholder-vote confirmation and regulatory milestones (near-term) and the Q2 earnings release on August 5, 2026. Given the all-stock structure, day-to-day RIG price monitoring is the effective proxy for VAL's mark.

## Conclusion

Valaris is a merger-arbitrage instrument. Its price tracks Transocean's stock through a fixed, uncollared 15.235x exchange ratio, and its standalone earnings, flattered in FY25 by ~$542M of non-recurring items and swinging to a Q1 loss, are secondary to deal completion and RIG's trajectory. The offshore structural tailwind is real, the backlog is growing, and the balance sheet is clean, but near-term estimates are falling, capital return is frozen, and the deal introduces a binary completion risk with no floor for VAL holders. Trailing cheapness reflects the merger and non-recurring earnings rather than an exploitable mispricing.

We rate VAL a HOLD (uncertain rationale) with an overall score of 5.0, down modestly from the prior read as the standalone earnings picture deteriorated and the deal removed the standalone valuation levers. The stock should be evaluated as a Transocean proxy and a merger-arb spread; investors comfortable with that framing and with RIG's fundamentals may find the arb attractive, but the setup does not offer the asymmetric standalone upside that would justify a BUY, nor is the operating trajectory collapsing in a way that justifies an active short given deal support and 11.7% short interest.
