## Executive Summary

Revvity reported Q2 FY2026 on the morning of this report and raised full-year guidance: pro forma organic revenue growth to 4-5% from 3-4%, pro forma adjusted operating margin to approximately 28.7%, and pro forma adjusted EPS to $5.30-5.40 (up $0.10). Pro forma adjusted operating margin in the quarter reached 29.3% against a roughly 27% guide, free cash flow was $184M at 117% of adjusted net income, net debt/adjusted EBITDA stood at 2.5x, and the company signed a definitive agreement to divest its China immunodiagnostics business, roughly 6% of FY2025 revenue. On the same print the shares traded down about 4% from the prior close.

That reaction is informative rather than irrational. Of the $1.41 pro forma adjusted EPS, management attributed roughly $0.11 to a one-time $16M IEEPA tariff refund and roughly a third of the total upside to a 16% adjusted tax rate versus the ~18% expected, which reverses in Q4. Ex-refund adjusted EPS of about $1.30 was up 13% year over year, of which roughly 5 percentage points came from a 5.0% reduction in diluted shares. Underneath, pro forma organic revenue grew 3%, and the segment split was wide: Diagnostics +11% organic with adjusted margin of 30.4% versus 25.2%, Life Sciences **-3% organic** with margin down 50bp. Signals software organic revenue declined approximately 20% after growing high-teens in FY2025 and 14.7% reported in Q1. Raising the full-year organic range to 4-5% after 3% in the first half requires roughly 5-6% in the second half, dependent on instrument shipments that slipped from Q2 into Q3, a reagent step-up exiting Q4, and easing software comparisons.

Our prior five assessments scored RVTY 5.8, 5.1, 4.8, 5.5 and 4.8, with four consecutive HOLDs and a declining velocity driven by cuts to Growth Prospects (6.5 to 4.5) and Management Quality (6.0 to 4.5). The new data partially reverses that trend: the guidance raise, the Diagnostics margin inflection, the signed China exit, retirement of the €500M July 2026 note and 2.5x leverage all argue the operating floor is firmer than it looked in July. It does not reverse the core problem, which is that the larger and higher-margin Life Sciences segment is contracting organically while carrying a $4.5B reporting-unit goodwill balance with only 10-20% fair-value headroom at the November 2025 annual test. At 20.7x guided adjusted EPS, 19.0x next-year consensus and roughly 54x trailing GAAP, the multiple already assumes the second-half acceleration lands. We move the overall score to 5.2 and maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Revvity is the successor to PerkinElmer's life-sciences and diagnostics operations following the March 2023 sale of the Applied, Food and Enterprise Services businesses. It employs approximately 11,000 people, roughly 80% outside the United States and about 4,000 covered by unions or works councils, sells into more than 160 countries through roughly 2,000 direct sales and service representatives across about 40 countries, and generates approximately 60% of revenue outside the US.

FY2025 revenue of $2,856.1M split almost evenly:

- **Life Sciences: $1,431.1M revenue, $458.3M segment operating income (32.0% margin).** Reagents and consumables (BioLegend antibodies, Dharmacon CRISPR/RNAi, AlphaLISA/DELFIA chemistries); instruments (Opera Phenix high-content screening, EnVision Nexus and VICTOR Kira readers, automation); software under the Signals brand plus ChemDraw, extended by the $72.0M cash acquisition of a Toronto scientific-software business (the ACD/Labs portfolio: Katalyst D2D, Luminata, Spectrus, AutoChrom, Percepta); and technology licensing around the Pin-point base-editing platform and CHOSOURCE cell-line engineering.
- **Diagnostics: $1,424.9M revenue, $344.2M segment operating income (24.2% margin).** Reproductive health (NeoBase/NeoLSD newborn screening, GSP instruments, EONIS, Vanadis prenatal, ViaCord cord blood, Revvity Omics labs) and immunodiagnostics (EUROIMMUN autoimmune/allergy, Oxford Immunotec T-SPOT.TB, IDS, chemagic nucleic-acid isolation, China blood screening).

The 10-K discloses no material customer concentration, which is unusual and favorable for a company of this size in tools and diagnostics. The most defensible franchises are newborn screening, where public-health programs create long-lived quasi-regulated demand, and ChemDraw/Signals, where switching costs in pharma discovery workflows are software-like.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trend.** FY2023 $2,750.6M, FY2024 $2,755.0M (+0.2%), FY2025 $2,856.1M (+3.67%), of which roughly one point was currency. The composition of FY2025 growth matters more than the rate: Diagnostics contributed +$68.5M (+5%, split Immunodiagnostics +$41.3M and Reproductive Health +$27.2M), while Life Sciences added only $32.5M (+2%) and all of that came from software (+$35.6M) against a $3.1M decline in Life Sciences Solutions. The single largest revenue pool in the company did not grow.

**Margins.** FY2025 gross margin was 54.8% versus 55.8%, a 104bp decline attributed to tariffs (approximately $25M in cost of revenue, roughly $20M net gross-margin impact), FX and mix. Operating margin fell only 10bp to 12.49% because SG&A dropped to 34.7% of revenue from 36.1%, helped by lower intangible amortization in SG&A ($194.5M versus $215.0M) and the absence of FY2024's $22.8M impairment, even while absorbing restructuring of $55.9M versus $17.5M (approximately 5% of the workforce), litigation of $12.2M versus $7.8M and $9.3M of transformation costs. Both segments deteriorated: Life Sciences -139bp, Diagnostics -194bp, with China diagnostic reimbursement policy cited explicitly.

R&D rose to $215.8M, 7.6% of revenue from 7.1%. Spending more on innovation into a flat top line is defensible; it is also a margin headwind until it produces growth.

**The amortization structure.** FY2025 intangible amortization was $335.6M ($141.1M in cost of revenue plus $194.5M in SG&A), 11.75% of revenue. Total D&A of $405.3M implies only about $69.7M of true depreciation against $73.5M of capex, so the operating footprint is genuinely asset-light beneath purchase accounting. In Q2 FY2026, GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $0.48 against $1.41 adjusted: amortization alone is $0.76 of the bridge, 54% of adjusted EPS, with add-backs totaling $0.93.

Amortization declines mechanically — the schedule runs $312.7M (FY2027), $286.8M (FY2028), $256.7M (FY2029), $227.9M (FY2030), a $25-30M annual pre-tax tailwind, or roughly $0.18-0.22 per share after tax at 18% on 112M shares. Real, and slow.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $589.0M was down $76M, driven by a $96.8M working-capital drag on timing of collections in China. FCF of $515.4M against $241.2M of net income is a conversion well above 1.0x, the expected signature of a heavily amortizing acquirer. H1 FY2026 FCF was about $300M at 108% conversion, though Q1 was only ~$106M versus Q2's $184M, Q2 included $16M of tariff refunds, and the 10-K guides capex higher in FY2026. A normalized FY2026 FCF band of $550-600M implies a 4.5-4.9% yield on the current market capitalization; we would not annualize the Q2 figure.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 repurchases were $820.8M on a cash-flow basis plus $32.8M of dividends, or 166% of FCF, funded by drawing cash from $1,163.4M to $919.9M. 8,509,531 shares were retired at an average of $95.88. Monthly disclosure shows the company kept buying into the March-April 2026 drawdown at an average $84.75. Weighted diluted shares fell from 117,538K to 111,629K year over year (-5.0%), with FY2026 guided to ~112M. Buyback pace slowed sharply in Q1 FY2026 ($86.5M), consistent with reserving cash for the €500M July maturity. The $0.07 quarterly dividend, unchanged since 2023, yields 0.25% and is not part of the case.

**Balance sheet.** At April 5, 2026: cash $860.3M ($518.3M offshore), total assets $11,996.6M, goodwill $6,610.8M, net intangibles $2,306.5M, equity $7,182.7M. Goodwill plus intangibles equals 74.3% of assets, so tangible book value is approximately negative $1,734.6M and book equity provides no downside floor. Net debt was $2,347.6M. Principal totaled $3,226.2M including the €500M 1.875% notes retired in mid-July 2026; the remaining stack is $500M at 1.90% (2028), $850M at 3.30% (2029), $400M at 2.55% (2031), $500M at 2.25% (2031) and $400M at 3.625% (2051), with a $1.5B revolver undrawn to January 2030.

The capital structure is the strongest single asset here. Management states 100% fixed-rate debt at a 2.3% weighted average with ~6-year weighted average maturity; on the post-repayment $2,650M principal the derived annual coupon is approximately $73.5M. Refinancing that stack at 5% would cost roughly $59M more pre-tax, about $0.43 per share after tax, or 8% of guided adjusted EPS. No maturity falls before 2028, so the advantage is locked for roughly two years. Corroborating this, the notes' fair value was $2,950.6M against $3,210.6M carrying, an 8.1% discount that accrues economically to equity holders and appears nowhere in book equity.

The Altman Z-Score near 2.14 sits in the grey zone, but the components (EBIT/assets 0.029, revenue/assets 0.235) are driven by $8.9B of purchase-accounting intangibles inflating the denominator. Direct solvency evidence — current ratio 1.68, $860M cash, $1.5B undrawn revolver, no maturity to 2028, 2.5x net leverage at a 2.3% average cost — points the other way. This is neither a distressed balance sheet nor a fortress one.

**Earnings surprise record.** Four consecutive adjusted beats: +1.7%, +7.4%, +2.3%, +13.1%. That supports confidence in the guidance floor, with the caveat that the largest beat was half-driven by a non-recurring refund and a tax-timing item.

## Growth Analysis

The raised FY2026 guide implies revenue of $2.83-2.86B pro forma with 4-5% organic growth. Q3 is guided to 4-6% organic, revenue $685-700M, adjusted margin ~29%, adjusted EPS ~24% of the full year (about $1.28 at midpoint), implying Q4 revenue near $754.5M in a 53-week year.

Three qualifications deserve weight.

First, **Q1's reported growth was flattered by a fourteenth week.** Q1 FY2026 ran December 29, 2025 to April 5, 2026 versus thirteen weeks in the prior year, with reported growth of +7% including ~3pp FX and ~1pp acquisition. Q2 was a clean thirteen-week comparison and also delivered 3% organic, which makes Q2 the better read on trend and Q1's underlying rate likely below 3%. The 4-5% full-year range therefore depends more heavily on H2 than year-to-date optics suggest.

Second, **the pro forma presentation adds roughly 275bp to headline growth.** H1 reported revenue growth was +4.03% ($1,440.8M versus $1,385.0M) against pro forma +6.78% ($1,398.0M versus $1,309.2M). Subtracting pro forma from reported isolates the China immunodiagnostics unit at $18.6M in Q2 FY2026 versus $39.7M a year earlier, a 53.2% decline, and $42.8M versus $75.8M in H1, a 43.6% decline. Diagnostics is back-half weighted, so seasonality explains part of the pattern, but a 40%+ first-half decline is not seasonal. The business being sold is shrinking rapidly, which strengthens the strategic logic of exiting and simultaneously implies modest proceeds and a flattering pro forma base. Price, buyer and structure are undisclosed; closing is expected by end-2027 subject to Chinese regulatory approval.

Third, **the growth mix is uncomfortable.** Diagnostics organic +11% with 520bp of adjusted margin expansion is the clear bright spot, though management has guided reproductive health to decelerate from mid-teens organic in H1 to low-to-mid single digits in H2. Life Sciences organic -3% is the larger issue: it is the segment carrying the thin-headroom goodwill, and its software line, which supplied all of FY2025's segment growth, reversed to roughly -20% organic in a single quarter. Management redirected attention to annual purchase value (double-digit growth) and ARR (mid-20s growth). Term licenses recognized at a point in time under ASC 606 genuinely do produce violent quarterly swings without economic change, and unbilled receivables of $106.7M primarily related to software are consistent with that. But Signals ARR is not disclosed in dollars, so the better metric cannot be audited from filings.

Genuine positives on the forward path: management describes the strongest instrument backlog in three to four years after Q2 shipment slippage, raised the platforms outlook from low- to mid-single-digit growth, and launched Opera Phenix OptiQ at above $1M per unit with order velocity outpacing production capacity. Management also cited a progressively larger increase in orders directly tied to AI-driven drug discovery, including non-traditional customers. Revvity built a Model Context Protocol connector allowing Signals customers to work with their data inside Anthropic's Claude; Revvity is the integration provider and Anthropic owns and operates the model. No revenue, exclusivity or economics are disclosed, so this should not carry weight in a forecast.

Consensus reflects the ambivalence: current-year EPS $5.24 and next-year $5.83, but both cut roughly 3% over 90 days and flat over 30 days. The company's own selected performance measure, average non-COVID organic revenue growth, has fallen 9.1 → 4.1 → 1.9 across FY2023-FY2025. There is no positive estimate-revision inflection signal here; the revision trend is stabilization after a downgrade cycle.

## Competitive Landscape

Revvity competes in three distinct arenas, which diversifies revenue and costs focus.

In **reagents and instruments** it faces Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Agilent, Bio-Rad, Bio-Techne and Merck KGaA. High-content imaging and plate readers are strong niches, and OptiQ's order backlog above production capacity is evidence of genuine product strength. But Life Sciences Solutions declined $3.1M in FY2025 and -3% organically in Q2 FY2026 in a market that is not shrinking that fast, which reads as share loss in the largest revenue pool. The 10-K's own framing — fragmented markets that will consolidate, with the proportion of large competitors increasing — is a candid acknowledgment that Revvity is not the scale player.

In **scientific software** ChemDraw's status as a de facto standard since 1985 and Signals Notebook's ELN footprint are the most durable moats in the portfolio, and ACD/Labs extends into analytical characterization. Competition from Dotmatics (Siemens), Benchling, Certara and Dassault/BIOVIA is intensifying, and the quarterly revenue volatility makes it hard for investors to verify whether the franchise is winning.

In **diagnostics** newborn screening is the strongest position in the company, EUROIMMUN's autoimmune and allergy menu is a solid European franchise, and 11% organic growth with 520bp of margin expansion in Q2 indicates share and mix gains. T-SPOT.TB is under pressure, with latent TB cited as an Americas headwind.

The trajectory is genuinely mixed: Diagnostics improving, Life Sciences deteriorating, and the China exit reducing footprint for defensible reasons. Cumulative TSR indexed at 68.2 for FY2025 versus a peer group at 105.15 says peers navigated the same funding and China conditions better, which limits how much of the underperformance can be attributed to cycle. We score competitive position 5.2, essentially flat to the prior 5.5, with the Diagnostics inflection offsetting Life Sciences contraction.

## Risk Assessment

**Life Sciences Solutions goodwill.** At the November 3, 2025 annual test, all reporting units' fair values substantially exceeded carrying value except Life Sciences Solutions, whose $4.5B goodwill balance exceeded carrying value by more than 10% but less than 20%. That unit is the one now shrinking organically. A modest cut to long-term growth assumptions or a higher discount rate could trigger a large non-cash charge at the next annual test, roughly November 2026. It would be excluded from adjusted EPS, so its direct effect on the metrics the market uses is nil; its significance is that it would constitute management's own acknowledgment that the acquired growth thesis has not materialized. There is no disclosed interim triggering-event conclusion after November 3, 2025.

**China execution risk.** The immunodiagnostics divestiture has an eighteen-month-plus signing-to-closing window requiring Chinese regulatory approval, with the business remaining in GAAP results throughout and price undisclosed. The Q1 10-Q added a risk factor contemplating non-closure. Asia Diagnostics revenue outside the divested unit also fell 10.9% in Q1 FY2026, so China pressure is not fully quarantined by the sale.

**Non-recurring items in the run-rate.** The $16M tariff refund is a one-time cash benefit following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; no receivable was booked as of April 5, 2026 and residual recoverable amounts are undisclosed. The 16% Q2 tax rate reverses to ~19% in Q3 and normalizes at ~18% for the year. Both make FY2027 comparisons harder.

**Genomics England.** Approximately $25M of FY2026 revenue (0.88% of the total), likely at above-average service margin, sits on a contract whose term ran only through the first half of the year, with extension discussions described as active. Q1 service revenue grew 27% to $129.7M, so this class of contract matters to reported service growth.

**Reporting complexity.** FY2026 guidance is both pro forma and adjusted and, per the company, cannot be reconciled to GAAP without unreasonable effort. Separately, the pro forma restatement of H1 FY2025 lowered base-period operating income to $117.0M from $163.0M as reported, because pro forma SG&A was $8.9M higher; isolating quarters implies a China immunodiagnostics SG&A credit of roughly $21.6M in Q1 FY2025. The source of that item is not identified in the materials available, and no inference about intent is warranted. Its mechanical effect is to depress the comparison base and flatter pro forma margin expansion, including the headline +520bp in Diagnostics. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q footnotes should resolve it.

**Funding environment.** Academic and government revenue declined low single digits in Q2, and the 10-K notes US funding changes have created a more cautious spending environment. Approximately 60% of revenue is non-US, adding FX exposure; the geopolitical risk factor was expanded in Q1 to reference energy security and the Ukraine and Iran conflicts.

**Unresolved item.** An October 24, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K (director or officer departure/election) appears in the filing index and its content was not determinable. It may be routine.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The company is executing a genuine portfolio and margin repair. Diagnostics is compounding at 11% organic with adjusted margin above 30%, up 520bp. The China immunodiagnostics business, declining more than 40% year over year, is contracted for sale, removing a policy-destroyed asset and raising go-forward growth mechanically. Pro forma adjusted operating margin of 29.3% exceeded a 27% guide with incremental margins in the low 40s, so operating leverage is real. Leverage is 2.5x and falling toward 2x, the €500M note is retired, remaining debt is 100% fixed at roughly 2.3% with no maturity until 2028, worth about $0.43 per share annually versus market refinancing rates. Instrument backlog is the strongest in three to four years, OptiQ orders exceed capacity, and management reports rising AI-driven order flow from non-traditional customers. Buybacks executed at $95.88 in FY2025 and $84.75 in the March-April drawdown are accretive on a mark-to-market basis and shrank the share count 5.0% in a year, with $879.5M remaining under the authorization. Amortization roll-off adds $0.18-0.22 per share to GAAP earnings annually from FY2027, and the November 13 Investor Day is a natural venue to set a credible mid-term framework. On $6.00-6.05 of FY2027 adjusted EPS at 23x, the stock is worth roughly $140.

**Bear case.** Core organic growth is 3%, not 4-5%. Q1's apparent 3% included a fourteenth week, so the underlying rate was lower, and the raised full-year guide requires 5-6% in H2 from shipment timing, a reagent step-up and easier software comps — all back-half loaded. Life Sciences, the larger and higher-margin segment, is contracting, and its software line swung from high-teens growth to roughly -20% organic in two quarters, with the better metrics (ARR, APV) unverifiable from filings. Half the Q2 EPS beat was a non-recurring tariff refund plus tax timing; ex-refund growth of 13% was roughly 5 points buyback. Pro forma margin expansion is flattered by a base-period SG&A credit of roughly $21.6M in Q1 FY2025. Restructuring has run three consecutive years and is excluded every time, with ~4,000 employees under works councils making European cost action slow and expensive. The $4.5B Life Sciences Solutions goodwill has under 20% headroom and could be written down at the November test. Consensus estimates are 3% lower over 90 days, the ratings mix has degraded, and 20.7x forward adjusted with roughly 54x trailing GAAP leaves no cushion. Compression to 16x on $5.40 puts the stock near $88, close to the $81.22 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence is genuinely two-sided — Diagnostics accelerating against Life Sciences contracting, a raised guide against a beat composition that is half non-recurring — and we do not have sufficient conviction for a directional call at this price.

- **Base case: $118** (+6.8% from $110.50). 20.2x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of approximately $5.85, consistent with consensus $5.83 and with continued ~5% annual share-count reduction; multiple held flat at the current forward level, which assumes H2 organic lands in the 4.5-5% area and Diagnostics margin gains hold. Total return with the 0.25% dividend is approximately 7%.
- **Bull case: $140** (+26.7%). 23x on $6.05, requiring Life Sciences organic growth to return to positive territory, Signals reported revenue to re-converge with mid-20s ARR growth, the Genomics England contract to be extended and expanded, and a credible multi-year framework at the November 13 Investor Day.
- **Bear case: $88** (-20.4%). 16.3x on $5.40, requiring H2 organic to miss the implied 5-6%, Life Sciences to stay negative, and a Life Sciences Solutions goodwill impairment at the November test that confirms the acquired growth thesis has not worked.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with three checkpoints: the Q2 FY2026 10-Q footnotes (China IDX carve-out detail and the base-period SG&A item), the Q3 print in late October (the first clean test of the H2 acceleration), and the November 13 Investor Day plus the annual goodwill test.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Life Sciences pro forma organic revenue returns to positive growth for two consecutive quarters with segment adjusted margin at or above 31.5%, confirming the reagent and instrument core has inflected rather than the mix having been rescued by Diagnostics.
2. Q3 pro forma organic growth of 5% or better ex any acquisition and ex tariff refunds, validating the raised full-year range on operating rather than one-time drivers.
3. Price toward $92-96, which would put the stock near 16-16.5x guided adjusted EPS and roughly a 5.7-6.0% normalized FCF yield, restoring asymmetry against the $88 bear case. Alternatively, disclosed Signals ARR in dollars showing mid-20s growth on a base large enough to matter.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q3 or Q4 pro forma organic growth below 3.5%, which would mean the raised guide fails and the H2 backlog conversion story did not hold.
2. A Life Sciences Solutions goodwill impairment at the November test, or disclosure of an interim triggering event, combined with a further reduction in the segment's long-term growth framework.
3. Termination of the China immunodiagnostics agreement, or FY2027 guidance implying adjusted EPS below $5.75 after the divestiture and buyback, which would signal that the pro forma cleanup has not improved earnings power.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2026 10-Q filing (expected mid-August), Q3 print (late October), Investor Day November 13, 2026.

## Conclusion

Revvity is a better-run company today than it was in July, and the score moves from 4.8 to 5.2 to reflect that: guidance raised on both organic growth and adjusted EPS, Diagnostics delivering 11% organic growth with 520bp of margin expansion, the China immunodiagnostics exit under definitive agreement, net leverage at 2.5x, the €500M maturity cleared, and a debt stack fixed at roughly 2.3% into 2028 that is worth about $0.43 per share a year versus market refinancing rates. Free cash flow conversion above 100% and a 5.0% annual reduction in diluted shares at repurchase prices that look good in hindsight round out a real improvement in financial quality.

What has not improved is the growth engine. Life Sciences, the larger and historically higher-margin segment, contracted 3% organically, its software line reversed from high-teens growth to roughly -20%, and the $4.5B goodwill sitting on that reporting unit has under 20% headroom heading into the November test. Half the Q2 EPS beat was non-recurring, consensus is 3% lower over 90 days, and management's own selected performance measure has fallen from 9.1 to 1.9 across three years while CEO grant-date compensation rose 15.8% to $13.81M against four consecutive years of peer TSR underperformance and no open-market insider purchases even at the March lows.
