## Executive Summary

First Hawaiian is the largest bank headquartered in Hawaii, with $24 billion in assets, a dominant deposit franchise (>30% market share), and a 167-year operating history in a geographically isolated market. FY2025 was a strong recovery year: net income grew 20% to $276M, EPS rose 23% to $2.20 (aided by buybacks), NIM expanded 20bps to 3.15%, and the efficiency ratio improved to 56.4%. Capital return is accelerating materially — a $250M buyback (7.6% of market cap) was authorized in January 2026, layered atop a 3.8% dividend yield.

The investment case hinges on whether this is a sustainable earnings step-up or a cyclical peak aided by deposit cost tailwinds that may now be largely captured. The sell-side is unanimously cautious (0 buy ratings, 6 holds, 4 sells/strong sells), and short interest at 12.8% of float suggests institutional skepticism. Geographic concentration in a tourism-dependent economy uniquely exposed to tariff-driven inflation, near-doubling of NPAs in 2025, and limited organic growth levers (regulatory prohibition on Hawaii acquisitions) justify a cautious stance. At ~12x forward earnings with 11%+ total shareholder yield, the stock offers reasonable income characteristics but limited re-rating catalyst.

## Company Overview

First Hawaiian, Inc. operates through First Hawaiian Bank, providing retail and commercial banking services across 49 branches in Hawaii (45), Guam (3), and Saipan (1). The bank was founded in 1858 and became independent from BNP Paribas in 2019. Its loan portfolio ($14.3B) is heavily real estate-oriented: residential mortgages (37%), commercial real estate (32%), construction (6%), and C&I (16%). The deposit base totals $20.5B, with 33% in non-interest-bearing demand deposits and 93% in core deposits.

The franchise's core advantage is geographic dominance in an isolated market. Hawaii's physical distance from the mainland, island culture favoring local relationships, and regulatory constraints (FHB cannot acquire any bank in Hawaii due to controlling more than 30% of total deposits) create meaningful barriers to competitive entry and exit.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement — 2025 Recovery:**
- Net interest income: $599.5M (est.), up from 2024 as NIM expanded to 3.15% from 2.95%
- The NIM expansion was driven primarily by deposit repricing: interest-bearing deposit costs fell 45bps (2.51% → 2.06%) while loan yields only declined 20bps
- Noninterest income benefited from absence of 2024's $26.2M securities loss
- Noninterest expense held flat at ~$500M for three consecutive years — exceptional cost discipline
- Net income: $276.3M, up 20% YoY; diluted EPS: $2.20, up 23%

**Balance Sheet Strength:**
- CET1: 13.17% — well above the 7.0% regulatory minimum, providing ~610bps of excess capital
- Zero short-term borrowings at year-end (vs. $250M in 2024)
- $6.6B in available borrowing capacity (FHLB + FRB discount window)
- Tangible book value per share: $14.46 (up from $12.83 YoY)
- Stock trades at 1.89x TBV — reasonable for a bank generating 16.3% ROTCE

**Credit Quality — Trending from Pristine Toward Normalized:**
- Net charge-offs: 0.11% (vs. 0.10% in 2024) — still very low
- NPAs nearly doubled: $20.7M → $41.0M (0.29% of loans)
- ACL/loans rose to 1.18% from 1.11% — modest reserve build
- Coverage ratio declined from 7.76x to 4.11x (ACL/NPA)
- The primary source of stress: C&I non-accruals increased $8.5M

**Capital Return:**
- Dividend: $1.04/share (3.8% yield), payout ratio 47%
- 2025 buyback: $100M (4M shares retired)
- 2026 buyback authorization: $250M — at current prices, this represents ~9.3M shares or 7.6% of market cap
- Combined total shareholder yield potential: ~11.4% (3.8% dividend + 7.6% buyback)

**Free Cash Flow:** $303M in 2025, comfortably covering the $130M dividend commitment and leaving substantial capacity for buybacks.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects for FHB are constrained by structural factors:

1. **Organic loan growth is modest.** Hawaii's population has been declining, tourism recovery from Japan remains incomplete, and the bank cannot acquire competitors due to the 30% deposit market share cap.

2. **NIM tailwinds may be largely captured.** The deposit cost reduction from 2.51% to 2.06% was the primary earnings driver in 2025. In a "higher for longer" rate environment, further deposit cost relief is unlikely. If rates remain elevated, asset yields may stabilize but incremental NIM expansion is limited.

3. **Estimate revisions are mildly positive but not transformational.** Current-year EPS estimates rose from $2.23 to $2.32 over 90 days (+4.2%), and next year from $2.37 to $2.46 (+3.8%). This is steady but far below the +30% threshold that signals a genuine earnings breakout.

4. **The efficiency ratio at 56.4% is already best-in-class.** Further expense leverage is difficult without revenue growth to absorb.

5. **Solar lease business risk.** The OBBBA legislation may impair a nascent growth channel.

Consensus projects $2.32 EPS for FY2026 (+5.5% YoY) and $2.46 for FY2027 (+6.0%). This is steady, mid-single-digit growth consistent with a mature community bank franchise.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.29 with $2.32 in forward EPS, FHB trades at 11.8x forward earnings. Against the peer median of 13.0x, this represents a 9% discount. The 3.8% dividend yield exceeds the peer median of 3.3%.

The discount is partially justified: FHB's geographic concentration, slower growth profile, and $995M goodwill burden (which inflates book value but has no tangible economic value) warrant some derating. However, the bank's superior efficiency ratio (56.4% vs. typical community bank 60-65%), low credit losses, and aggressive buyback program argue the discount is slightly excessive.

At 1.89x TBV with 16.3% ROTCE, the valuation is roughly fair using a Gordon Growth framework: 16.3% ROTCE × (1 - 47% payout) / (10% cost of equity - 3% growth) ≈ 1.2x TBV for retained earnings, plus dividend value. The market is pricing this approximately correctly.

The $250M buyback creates a mechanical floor: management is buying back stock aggressively at these levels, which provides downside support but doesn't create a catalyst for upside re-rating unless growth accelerates.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $28.00, median $28.00, range $26.00-$31.00. With the stock at $27.29, consensus implies only 2.6% upside to the mean target — effectively "fairly valued" per the Street.

## Competitive Landscape

FHB's competitive position is stable but not improving:

**Strengths:** Market-leading deposit share in Hawaii, 167-year brand, 11.7-year average employee tenure creating institutional knowledge, and regulatory barriers preventing in-state acquisition by competitors.

**Challenges:** The regulatory acquisition prohibition works both ways — FHB cannot consolidate its Hawaii advantage. Fintech penetration and digital banking increasingly allow mainland competitors (including neobanks) to serve Hawaii customers without physical presence. Japanese tourist flows — a key economic driver — have recovered slowly.

**Relative to peers:** FHB's efficiency ratio is superior, and its credit quality has historically been cleaner. But its growth rate trails faster-growing regional banks with diversified geographies.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Geographic concentration (Hawaii/tourism):** 69% of loans are in Hawaii. A prolonged tourism downturn, natural disaster (hurricane, volcanic event, wildfire repeat), or military base drawdown would disproportionately affect FHB. The tariff pass-through risk is company-specific: Hawaii imports virtually all goods, making it uniquely exposed to trade policy.

2. **Real estate concentration:** 75% of the loan portfolio is tied to real estate. Hawaii CRE and residential markets are vulnerable to interest rate stress and affordability constraints (median Oahu home: $1.139M).

3. **NPA trajectory:** The near-doubling from $20.7M to $41.0M, while still at low absolute levels, indicates emerging credit stress. The coverage ratio declining from 7.76x to 4.11x means less buffer against further deterioration.

4. **Uninsured deposits (49%):** In a stress scenario (regional bank contagion), the high percentage of uninsured deposits creates potential flight risk, though the $6.6B borrowing capacity provides a substantial backstop.

5. **Goodwill impairment risk:** The $995M goodwill represents 42% of book equity and is a latent risk if Hawaii's economic outlook deteriorates meaningfully.

6. **Short interest (12.8% / 5.5 days to cover):** Elevated bearish positioning suggests informed investors see specific downside catalysts not obvious from headline financial metrics. The 5.5 days-to-cover means it would take nearly a week of average volume to close short positions.

**Mitigating Factors:** Excess capital (CET1 13.17%), stable funding (93% core deposits), extremely low NCOs (0.11%), and $6.6B liquidity backstop provide meaningful cushion against stress scenarios.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30-31):**
- Buyback execution compresses share count by 7%+, mechanically lifting EPS to $2.50+ even without revenue growth
- Higher-for-longer rates sustain NIM above 3.15% as asset repricing catches up
- Tourism recovery accelerates (particularly Japanese visitors), driving loan demand
- Credit quality stabilizes — NPAs were one-time additions, not a trend
- Stock re-rates toward peer median (13x × $2.46 = $32)

**Bear Case ($23-24):**
- Tariff pass-through compresses Hawaii consumer spending, slowing loan growth and increasing delinquencies
- NPA trend continues: another doubling would bring NPAs to $80M+ and trigger reserve builds that compress earnings
- Deposit costs stop declining or reverse, squeezing NIM below 3.00%
- Tourism shock (geopolitical disruption, recession) reduces Hawaii economic activity
- Short-sellers are right: $23 represents ~12% downside and ~1.6x TBV

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
- Stock declines to $24-25 range (~10.5x forward EPS), creating genuine value with covered dividend yield >4.2%
- NPA trajectory reverses (returns below $30M) confirming the 2025 increase was one-time
- Loan growth accelerates to >5% annualized for two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
- NPAs breach $60M (1.5x the 2025 level), indicating systemic Hawaii credit deterioration
- NIM compresses below 2.90%, eliminating the 2025 tailwind
- Deposit outflows exceed $1B in a single quarter (concentration risk materializing)
- Short interest exceeds 18% with declining price momentum

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July) will provide data on NPA trajectory, NIM sustainability, and buyback pace.

## Conclusion

First Hawaiian is a well-run, dominant franchise bank in a concentrated market, generating attractive total shareholder yield (~11.4% combined dividend + buyback) on a conservatively managed balance sheet. The 2025 earnings recovery was real and driven by sound NIM dynamics and cost discipline.

However, upside is capped by structural low growth, geographic concentration risk, rising credit stress (NPAs doubling), and unanimous sell-side caution. The 12.8% short interest and zero buy ratings represent informed skepticism about Hawaii's economic trajectory and the sustainability of 2025's margin expansion. At ~12x forward earnings with 3.8% yield, the stock is priced approximately fairly for what it is: a stable income vehicle with limited growth, meaningful geographic tail risk, and adequate but not exceptional capital returns. Income investors with conviction in Hawaii's economic stability may find the total yield profile attractive, but the evidence for a more assertive recommendation is lacking.
