## Executive Summary

Park Hotels & Resorts is a $2.3B lodging REIT owning 34 premium-branded hotels (~23,000 rooms) concentrated in Hawaii, Florida, and major U.S. convention markets. The company faces a convergence of structural and cyclical headwinds: $1.6B in debt maturing in 2026, two consecutive years of revenue decline, four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, negative GAAP earnings (EPS -$1.43), and a dividend payout ($280M in 2025) that exceeds free cash flow ($102M) by nearly 3x. The stock's 9% yield compensates for genuine distress, not mispriced value.

The 2026 refinancing of the $1.275B Hilton Hawaiian Village mortgage — secured by the company's single most valuable asset — will almost certainly occur at a higher rate, compressing margins on an already declining EBITDA base. Management has guided 2026 Adjusted EBITDA at $580M–$610M, below 2025's $609M. With consumer confidence weakening and discretionary travel demand softening, the revenue trajectory is more likely to worsen than improve. Short interest at 24.6% of float (9.5 days to cover) reflects institutional conviction that the fundamental deterioration has further to run.

## Company Overview

Park Hotels & Resorts was spun off from Hilton Worldwide in January 2017 as a pure-play lodging REIT. The company operates as an asset owner with only 90 corporate employees — all hotel-level staff are employed by third-party managers, predominantly Hilton. The portfolio is segmented into Core (21 hotels generating ~90% of Hotel Adjusted EBITDA) and Non-Core (12 remaining hotels earmarked for disposition).

Geographic concentration is extreme: Florida and Hawaii represent 39%+ of total revenue. The Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort alone (2,860+ rooms, ~$2.3B carrying value) is the company's anchor asset and the collateral for its largest single debt obligation. Brand concentration is similarly pronounced, with the overwhelming majority of properties operating under Hilton flags, creating dependency on Hilton's reservation system, loyalty program, and management quality.

The company completed its strategic exit from San Francisco in November 2025, when a court-appointed receiver sold the Hilton San Francisco Union Square and Parc 55 (2,945 rooms) to a buyer who assumed the $725M non-recourse CMBS loan. This resolved a two-year overhang stemming from Park's June 2023 strategic default on the SF Mortgage Loan.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Two Consecutive Years of Decline

| 2023 | $2,698M | +7.9% |
| 2024 | $2,599M | -3.7% |

The revenue decline reflects Non-Core hotel dispositions (removing ~$43M), weakness in Hawaii (Hilton Hawaiian Village occupancy down 3.2pp, ADR down 3.2%), the Royal Palm Miami closure for renovation (May 2025), and broad demand softness across transient (-$35M), group (-$18M), and contract (-$12M) segments. Bright spots (Bonnet Creek Orlando, NYC Hilton Midtown) are insufficient to offset the portfolio-wide headwinds.

### Profitability: Impairment-Driven GAAP Loss, Modest Adjusted Decline

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | Change |

| Operating Income | $(33)M | $391M | — |

The GAAP net loss of $283M is distorted by a $318M Non-Core impairment charge and $56M of accelerated depreciation for the Royal Palm renovation. On an adjusted basis, EBITDA declined 6.6% while Core Hotel EBITDA declined a more moderate 2.8%. Adjusted FFO of $1.97/share covers the $1.00 dividend at a 51% payout ratio on this metric, though GAAP earnings and free cash flow do not.

### Cash Flow: Dividend Exceeds FCF

| Year | Operating CF | CapEx | FCF | Dividends |
|------|-------------|-------|-----|-----------|
| 2025 | $398M | $296M | $102M | $280M |
| 2024 | $429M | $227M | $202M | $512M |
| 2023 | $503M | $285M | $218M | $152M |

FCF of $102M in 2025 covered only 36% of dividend payments. The gap was bridged by cash reserves and, implicitly, borrowing capacity. Operating cash flow declined 7.2% YoY while capital expenditures increased 30% due to renovation programs (Hawaii, Royal Palm, New Orleans). Declining OCF paired with rising capex during the renovation cycle will persist through mid-2026.

### Balance Sheet: $1.6B Maturity Wall

Total debt stands at $3.856B. The critical near-term obligation is the **$1.275B HHV Mortgage Loan at 4.20%, maturing November 2026**. An additional $355M in other mortgages mature in 2026–2027. Management has arranged an $800M Delayed Draw Term Loan (SOFR+2.20%, approximately 6.5%+ at current rates) and maintains a $1B undrawn revolver. Liquidity is technically adequate ($232M cash + $1.8B in undrawn facilities), but the refinancing will increase the weighted average cost of debt above the current 5.2%, directly compressing net income on an already declining EBITDA base.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.29 places PK firmly in the distress zone (below 1.81 threshold). This reflects near-zero working capital, negative EBIT/total assets, high leverage, and low asset turnover — all characteristics of a lodging REIT under margin pressure.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects for PK are limited by portfolio structure, macro headwinds, and renovation-driven disruption.

**Near-term (2026):** Management guided 2026 Adjusted EBITDA at $580M–$610M, below 2025's $609M. Revenue will benefit from the Royal Palm reopening (June 2026) and completion of Hawaii renovations, but will be offset by further Non-Core dispositions reducing the portfolio base. Same-property RevPAR growth is uncertain given consumer spending weakness.

**Medium-term (2027+):** The bull case rests on renovation-driven RevPAR uplift at the Hilton Hawaiian Village, Royal Palm, and New Orleans properties, combined with supply constraints (limited new hotel development) supporting pricing power. If the $600M+ in renovation capital deployed in 2024–2026 generates the targeted 10–15% RevPAR improvement at affected properties, Core Hotel EBITDA could recover to $620M–$650M by 2027. This remains speculative — the current consumer environment does not support premium leisure pricing growth.

**Structural constraints:** Park's portfolio composition limits upside. Full-service hotels have higher operating leverage than select-service or extended-stay formats, amplifying both upcycles and downcycles. The leisure-heavy mix (now the majority of transient demand) makes the portfolio acutely sensitive to consumer discretionary spending weakness.

Next-year estimates have improved +19% over 90 days ($0.46 → $0.55), suggesting analysts see modest recovery — but the absolute level ($0.55 EPS) is thin relative to the capital structure's demands.

## Valuation Assessment

At $11.27, PK trades at a negative trailing P/E (-7.8x) due to GAAP losses. On forward consensus of $0.54 current-year EPS, the forward P/E is approximately 21x — roughly in line with the peer median of 24.7x.

| Metric | PK | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| Forward P/E | ~21x | 24.7x |
| Dividend Yield | 8.9% | 3.5% |
| Price/52-wk High | -9% | ~-2% |

The apparent discount to peers on P/E and the elevated yield are misleading indicators. The yield spread (8.9% vs. 3.5% peer median) primarily reflects the market's assessment that the dividend is at risk. A cut to $0.50 would bring the yield to ~4.4%, closer to peers but still above FCF generation. The analyst consensus mean target of $12.31 and median target of $11.50 imply limited upside from the current price, confirming the Street sees the stock as fairly valued at best.

On a price-to-Adjusted FFO basis, PK trades at approximately 5.7x ($11.27 / $1.97 AFFO), which appears cheap versus the lodging REIT sector average of ~8–10x. However, this compression reflects legitimate structural concerns: declining AFFO trajectory, heavy capex requirements, refinancing risk, and geographic concentration.

The analyst target range of $9.00–$19.50 is unusually wide, reflecting deep uncertainty about the outcome of the 2026 maturity wall and renovation cycle. The consensus distribution — 0 strong buys, 4 buys, 12 holds, 2 strong sells — leans heavily bearish by lodging REIT standards.

## Competitive Landscape

Park competes with Host Hotels (HST, the largest lodging REIT), Pebblebrook (PEB), Xenia (XHR), Sunstone (SHO), Diamondrock (DRH), and Apple Hospitality (APLE) for institutional capital allocation and hotel acquisition opportunities.

**Relative positioning:** Park's cumulative TSR of 84.35 vs. the peer group's 137.83 represents a 53+ percentage point underperformance over the proxy measurement period. This is the most significant single data point about competitive positioning — the market has systematically derated PK relative to peers.

**Portfolio quality:** Park's Core portfolio — 96% luxury/upper upscale, anchored by irreplaceable properties like the Hilton Hawaiian Village and New York Hilton Midtown — is genuinely high quality. Limited new supply in premium urban and resort markets provides a medium-term tailwind. However, the Non-Core portfolio (12 remaining hotels) is being sold at substantial impairments, indicating these assets have limited competitive value.

**Operational efficiency:** Park's 90-employee corporate structure is lean, but the third-party management model limits ability to implement cost reductions at the property level during downturns. The 2024 labor strikes at four properties (including the flagship HHV) demonstrated the vulnerability of this model to unionized workforce disruptions.

The competitive position is stable at the Core portfolio level but weakening at the total-company level due to portfolio shrinkage, geographic concentration, and TSR underperformance.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Refinancing Execution and Cost:** The rate increase from the current 4.20% to approximately 6.0–6.5% would add $23M–$29M in annual interest expense, representing a 4–5% margin compression on the current Adjusted EBITDA base.

**2. Consumer Demand Deterioration:** Weakening consumer confidence, elevated transportation costs for leisure travelers, and the ongoing cost environment all create headwinds for premium leisure travel — the growth segment Park depends on.

**3. Dividend Sustainability:** If renovation capex does not decline meaningfully in H2 2026, another dividend cut is probable. A cut to $0.50 would still strain free cash flow during the renovation cycle.

**4. Hawaii Concentration:** Hawaii tourism has weakened (occupancy down 3.2pp, ADR down 3.2%) and recovery is uncertain. A severe hurricane, further demand erosion, or insurance cost escalation at HHV would disproportionately impact the company.

**5. Short Interest at 24.6%:** Extremely elevated short positioning signals institutional consensus that further deterioration is probable. Days to cover at 9.5 creates theoretical squeeze risk but also confirms that informed investors see structural problems.

### Options Market Signal

No options data was available for this analysis. The elevated dividend yield acts as a partial short squeeze buffer (shorts must pay the dividend to borrowers), which may explain why the short interest has remained elevated rather than triggering a squeeze.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The HHV Mortgage Loan maturity in November 2026 is the most specific dated catalyst. Any refinancing difficulties, unfavorable terms, or requirement for an equity raise would drive a sharp move lower. Q2 2026 earnings (expected July–August 2026) will reveal whether the RevPAR trajectory is stabilizing or deteriorating further. A fifth consecutive miss would likely trigger another consensus estimate reduction.

**Timing window:** The HHV maturity creates a 6–7 month window. Carrying costs are elevated given the 8.9% dividend yield (shorts must pay to borrow), adding approximately $1.00/share annually to the short position's cost basis.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 24.6% of float with 9.5 days to cover, this is already a crowded short. Any positive catalyst — a favorable refinancing announcement, a consumer confidence rebound, or a strategic transaction — could trigger a violent short squeeze. The 9.5 days to cover means unwinding would take nearly two weeks at average volume, amplifying any upward move.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $7–8 represents ~30–37% downside from $11.27. Upside risk to $14 represents ~24% on a short squeeze. The risk/reward ratio of roughly 1.3:1 to 1.5:1 does not meet the 2:1 threshold for an attractive short entry at current levels.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis has merit — declining fundamentals, refinancing risk, consumer demand weakness — but the short is already extremely crowded. At 9.5 days to cover and with an 8.9% dividend carry cost, the mechanics are unfavorable for new short positions. The asymmetry favors avoiding rather than actively shorting at this price. Better short entry would be on a bounce toward $12.50+ where the risk/reward improves.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Upgrade to HOLD or BUY would require: (1) Successful HHV mortgage refinancing at ≤5.5% with no equity raise, confirmed by 8-K filing; (2) two consecutive quarters of positive RevPAR growth across the Core portfolio (not just individual properties); (3) FCF exceeding dividend payments for a full fiscal year, demonstrating sustainable coverage.

**Timeline:** The primary headwinds (maturity wall, renovation disruption, consumer weakness) are expected to persist through at least Q4 2026. The earliest realistic reassessment point is after the November 2026 HHV refinancing is resolved.

## Conclusion

Park Hotels & Resorts presents a concentrated set of risks — $1.6B maturity wall, declining revenue, FCF-uncovered dividend, geographic concentration, and four consecutive earnings misses — that the 9% dividend yield does not adequately compensate for. The stock trades at ~5.7x Adjusted FFO, which appears optically cheap but reflects legitimate structural concerns that justify the discount. No identifiable catalyst for operational improvement exists within 12 months given the consumer spending environment. The 24.6% short interest confirms institutional bearishness but also makes active shorting unattractive at current levels due to carry cost and squeeze risk. Recommendation: **AVOID**.
