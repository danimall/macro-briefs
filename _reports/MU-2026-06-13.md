**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Micron is in the strongest earnings position in its 40-year history, and the question facing investors is how long the results last. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended Feb 26, 2026) delivered $23.86B in revenue (+196% YoY, +75% QoQ), a 74% gross margin versus 37% a year earlier, $13.79B net income, and $12.07 diluted EPS. Six-month FY26 revenue of $37.5B already matches all of FY25. The engine is pricing: DRAM average selling prices rose roughly 110% with only mid-40% bit growth, and NAND ASPs rose over 100%. AI data-center demand has turned memory into a genuine supply bottleneck, handing the three-player DRAM oligopoly real pricing power for the first time in years.

The stock has discounted this in full and then some. MU is up roughly 740% over twelve months, from a 52-week low of $103.38 to a high of $1,089.29, crossing a $1 trillion market capitalization on May 26, 2026. Estimate revisions are extraordinary: current-year consensus has risen 68.3% over 90 days to $59.82, and next-year consensus has risen 114.8% to $112.17. On those forward numbers the stock trades at roughly 16x current-year and 9x next-year earnings, multiples that look inexpensive on screens. They are inexpensive on peak earnings, which is the recurring trap in memory: the lowest-looking P/Es appear at cycle tops, precisely because the market does not believe the earnings are durable. Consensus mean ($828.73) and median ($637.50) price targets both sit below the current $982 price.

We rate MU a HOLD with a growth lean. The near-term momentum is real and the June 24 Q3 print is likely to confirm the supercycle, so existing holders have reason to retain. But Micron's own 10-Q discloses that DRAM ASPs have swung from +low-40% to –high-40% within a five-year window, and it added new language warning that an HBM demand slowdown could redirect wafer capacity into conventional DRAM and crush pricing. After a 740% run, the risk/reward on fresh capital is not asymmetric in the buyer's favor.

## Company Overview

Micron is one of only three scaled producers of DRAM (with Samsung and SK hynix) and one of a handful of NAND flash makers (adding Kioxia and Sandisk), plus emerging Chinese entrants CXMT in DRAM and YMTC in NAND. Memory remains a commodity with severe cyclicality, but DRAM has consolidated to the point where three players control the vast majority of supply, which is what gives the current pricing upcycle its force.

The company reorganized into four market-facing units, and the segment data shows where margin is concentrated:
- **Cloud Memory (CMBU)** — hyperscale cloud plus all HBM. Q2 FY26 revenue $7.75B at 66% operating margin. This is the AI engine.
- **Core Data Center (CDBU)** — mid-tier cloud, enterprise, OEM, data-center storage. $5.69B at 67% margin.
- **Mobile & Client (MCBU)** — $7.71B at 76% margin.
- **Automotive & Embedded (AEBU)** — $2.71B at 62% margin.

Micron claims technology leadership in advanced DRAM nodes and is certified for NVIDIA's HBM4 (Vera Rubin platform). Manufacturing is geographically concentrated: a majority of DRAM output comes from Taiwan fabs, with additional capacity in Japan, Singapore, and the U.S. (Idaho, Virginia), plus assembly and test in Malaysia, China, and India. The company is mid-build on a multi-decade U.S. fab expansion (New York, second Idaho fab) supported by CHIPS Act incentives.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory:
- **FY2023:** ~$15.5B revenue, –$5.8B net loss (oversupply trough)
- **FY2024:** ~$25B revenue, ~$0.8B net income (recovery)
- **FY2025:** ~$37.4B revenue, $8.5B net income (AI/HBM acceleration)
- **Q2 FY2026:** $23.86B revenue, 74% gross margin, $13.79B net income, $12.07 EPS

The earnings beat record is consistent and accelerating: four straight beats, with the most recent quarter beating by 27.3% ($12.20 actual vs. $9.58 estimate) and the prior quarter by 17.4%. That track record raises confidence in current consensus, with the caveat that the surprises are themselves a function of ASPs running ahead of analyst models.

Cash generation is enormous. First-half FY26 operating cash flow reached $20.3B versus $7.2B a year earlier. The balance sheet has transformed: total debt fell from $14.6B (Aug 2025) to $10.1B (Feb 2026) after prepaying five tranches of notes and term loans; cash and investments rose to $16.6B; and shareholders' equity grew from $54B to $72B in two quarters. Net leverage is now trivial against an annualized EBITDA run-rate north of $45B. Funding more than $25B of FY26 capex, prepaying debt, and paying a (small) dividend simultaneously is a luxury afforded only by the current cash-flow surge.

Two quality-of-earnings items warrant monitoring. Trade receivables ballooned from $7.2B to $15.4B on the revenue surge, a normal but real working-capital drag. The $25B+ capex program and simultaneous fab builds in New York, Idaho, Singapore, Japan, and Taiwan convert into fixed costs that bite hard if pricing reverses mid-construction, the exact mechanism that has repeatedly damaged memory makers at cycle turns. CHIPS Act direct-funding agreements also restrict buybacks, capping near-term capital returns ($650M repurchased in 1H FY26, with the dividend at $0.15/quarter).

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth numbers are exceptional and broad-based across estimate horizons:
- Current quarter: $19.66 (+88.5% over 90 days, +3.0% over 30 days)
- Next quarter: $23.46 (+100.9% over 90 days, +3.9% over 30 days)
- Current year: $59.82 (+68.3% over 90 days, +2.7% over 30 days)
- Next year: $112.17 (+114.8% over 90 days, +10.2% over 30 days)

The combination of +196% revenue growth and 90-day estimate revisions of +68% to +115% is, in isolation, among the strongest forward indicators in equity analysis, and it normally signals continued outperformance as consensus catches up to reality. We weight this signal explicitly: it is the primary reason MU retains a growth-hold classification rather than a neutral one.

The critical qualifier is the character of the growth. This is a commodity producer at a pricing peak, not a company emerging from a trough with structurally compounding demand. DRAM revenue rose 207% YoY on ~110% ASP gains against only mid-40% bit growth. ASP-driven growth mean-reverts; volume-driven growth at stable prices does not. The +114% next-year revision implies the supercycle extends through FY27, which is a defensible base case given HBM is sold out for FY26 and the company is certified on HBM4, but it is a bet on cycle duration, not a structural growth story. Demand catalysts are genuine — global DRAM revenue hit a record ~$97B in Q1 2026 (Counterpoint), and TrendForce raised its 2026 memory forecast to ~$889B. The durability question, not the demand question, is what caps the score.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the crux of the call. On trailing GAAP EPS of $7.59 (a stale figure reflecting the earlier ramp), MU shows a 46.8x P/E. On forward consensus the picture inverts: ~16x current-year and ~9x next-year earnings. Against semiconductor peers — TSM at 36.5x, AVGO at 64.2x, TXN at 50.8x, with a peer median around 64x — MU looks dramatically cheap.

That apparent cheapness is the cyclical trap. The proper way to read a 9x forward multiple on a memory maker at peak ASPs is that the market does not believe $112 of next-year EPS is the new normal. If investors expected those earnings to persist, MU would trade at 20–30x, implying a $2,243–$3,365 price. The 9x multiple is the market pricing a mean-reversion in ASPs that the company itself acknowledges has historically run to –40% or worse. The cycle-adjusted valuation framework that rescues trough-earnings cyclicals from misleadingly high trailing multiples works in reverse here: forward earnings are at a peak, not a trough, so a low forward multiple is not evidence of a bargain.

The strongest external confirmation of this read is that consensus targets sit 16%–35% below the current price. When analyst targets trail the stock on a high-momentum name with reasonable multiples, targets usually lag reality and revise upward. But on a cyclical at a recognized peak, targets sitting below price more often reflect the sell-side's discount of the peak earnings rather than stale optimism. The valuation is genuinely two-sided: a 2+ year supercycle makes today's price cheap, while any 2026–2027 supply normalization makes it expensive. We score this dimension at the midpoint to reflect that balance rather than assigning either a discount or a premium.

## Competitive Landscape

Micron sits inside a structurally favorable industry. DRAM is a three-player oligopoly, and the AI buildout has, for now, turned memory into the binding bottleneck, restoring pricing discipline the industry lacked in prior cycles. Micron's HBM4 certification on NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform and its reported commitment of all FY26 HBM capacity under long-term contracts lock in near-term revenue.

The differentiated competitive concern is relative share. Counterpoint reports Samsung is widening its lead in AI memory over both SK hynix and Micron, and Mizuho rates MU a Sell specifically on market-share loss rather than cycle risk. Micron is the #3 player attempting to close the HBM gap, and the fact that its own executive compensation plan ties payouts to HBM3E+ market share and bit shipments confirms management views share as the live battleground. Within an oligopoly enjoying record pricing, gaining absolute HBM volume while potentially losing relative position is a mixed trajectory. HBM4 qualification timing and share data are the metrics to watch. Geographic concentration is a structural vulnerability: a majority of DRAM output flows from Taiwan, a single geopolitical flashpoint.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the weakest dimension and the reason the overall call lands at HOLD rather than higher. The specific, currently-relevant risks are not adequately reflected in a stock up 740%:

1. **Peak-cycle ASP reversal (dominant risk).** At 74% gross margins on +110% ASPs, Micron is at an extreme. The company's own disclosure notes DRAM ASPs have swung from +low-40% to –high-40% over five years. Any supply normalization reverses margins violently; FY23 was a –$5.8B net loss.
2. **HBM-to-conventional-DRAM oversupply (new disclosure).** The 10-Q explicitly warns that HBM consumes far more wafers per bit than conventional DRAM; if HBM demand weakens and the industry redirects that capacity, it could produce "a significant increase in conventional DRAM supply" and crush pricing. This is a candid, company-specific articulation of the bust scenario.
3. **Capex-as-fixed-cost.** Simultaneous multi-fab builds become a fixed burden against falling prices if the cycle turns mid-construction.
4. **Customer and end-market concentration.** One customer was ~13% of 1H FY26 revenue; the top 10 exceed half of FY25 revenue; roughly half of revenue is data-center.
5. **China/geopolitical.** The CAC ban (since May 2023) bars Chinese critical-infrastructure operators from Micron products; new Section 232 and Section 301 investigations could add tariffs.
6. **Litigation.** A $445M Netlist jury verdict (under appeal; relevant patents found unpatentable by the PTAB) plus a multi-front YMTC patent campaign.
7. **Tax.** Pillar Two is lifting the effective tax rate toward 14–15% from ~10–12%.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated event risk around the June 24 Q3 print. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 100.8% against one-year realized of 68.7% — rich, with a 32-point spread. The term structure is in backwardation (100.8% near-term versus 93.9% at six months), the signature of a market pricing a discrete near-term catalyst rather than durable stress. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±10.3%. Skew is balanced (0.3%) and positioning is mildly call-heavy (put/call volume 0.58, OI 0.62), with the largest unusual flow in upside calls ($1,050 June-26 at 7.8x volume/OI), suggesting traders are leaning long into the print rather than hedging a collapse. IV has fallen since June 8 (113.2% to 100.8%) as price rose, indicating some pre-print uncertainty has resolved. The signal is consistent with a stock priced for a strong report and a binary outcome — favorable for owners who want to overwrite premium, and a reason for new buyers to wait until the event passes.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The AI memory supercycle extends through FY27. HBM is sold out for FY26, HBM4 is certified on NVIDIA's flagship platform, and the three-player DRAM oligopoly sustains pricing discipline as memory remains the binding constraint in the AI buildout. Next-year EPS of $112 proves durable or rises further; estimates continue their +10%/month climb. On even a conservative 12–15x multiple on $112, the stock re-rates toward $1,350–$1,683. The deleveraged balance sheet and $20B+ operating cash flow let Micron fund the fab expansion without strain. Bull case target: ~$1,683.

### Bear Case
ASPs follow their historical pattern and reverse 40%+ as the industry adds capacity and HBM wafers get redirected into conventional DRAM. Gross margins compress from 74% toward the 30s, and a stock priced on $112 of peak EPS de-rates on collapsing forward estimates. Samsung's widening AI-memory lead erodes Micron's share even before the cycle turns. The capex program becomes a fixed cost against falling prices. In a genuine cycle reversal, memory names have historically lost half their value or more; the 52-week low of $103 indicates how far the floor sits. Bear case target: ~$500 (roughly –49% from current).

The base case sits between: the supercycle holds into the next two quarters, Q3 confirms it, and the stock grinds in a wide $850–$1,100 range as the market debates duration. Base case: ~$1,050, modestly above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY2026 earnings report (~June 24, 2026) is the next natural checkpoint and a binary event the options market is actively pricing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A meaningful pullback (toward the $700–$800 range) that restores asymmetric entry while estimate revisions remain positive — a lower price on intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward.
- Concrete evidence the supercycle is volume-led and durable rather than ASP-spike-led — e.g., bit-growth acceleration sustaining margins even as ASP growth decelerates, plus HBM4 share data showing Micron closing the gap on Samsung/SK hynix.
- Front-end implied volatility normalizing post-print with continued upward estimate revisions and management guiding to a multi-year pricing floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- First sequential ASP deceleration in DRAM or NAND, or any management commentary on softening HBM order patterns or capacity redirection into conventional DRAM.
- Gross margin guidance below ~60%, signaling the peak has passed.
- A material HBM4 qualification slip or share-loss confirmation against Samsung.
- Capex obligations rising while pricing softens, the classic mid-cycle squeeze.

## Conclusion

Micron is delivering the best fundamentals in its history, and the estimate-revision momentum is a genuine forward signal we do not dismiss. That is why this is a HOLD with a growth lean rather than a neutral or negative call, and why existing holders have reason to retain into the June 24 print. The deleveraged balance sheet and operating cash flow give the company resilience few prior memory upcycles afforded.

The reason we stop short of BUY is the configuration: a hyper-cyclical commodity producer at a recognized pricing peak, up 740% in twelve months, with a forward multiple that looks cheap only because the market is discounting the same ASP mean-reversion the company itself discloses can run to –40%+. The bear-case downside is far wider than the threshold we require for fresh capital, the dominant risks (ASP reversal, HBM-to-DRAM oversupply, capex-as-fixed-cost, share loss to Samsung) are specific and underpriced after the run, and consensus targets already sit below the current price. New money lacks asymmetric entry here; we would become buyers on a meaningful pullback that preserves the estimate trend, or on evidence the cycle is volume-led and durable. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
