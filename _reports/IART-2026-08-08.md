## Executive Summary

Integra LifeSciences is a $1.64B-revenue surgical device manufacturer whose equity has become a thin residual claim on a heavily levered asset. Net debt of $1,600.7M represents 55% of the $2.93B enterprise value; a 10% move in EV translates into roughly a 22% move in the equity. That structure, not the operating business, dominates the investment case.

The operating news since our last review is modestly positive. Q2 2026 (reported 7/29/2026) delivered revenue of $418.8M (+0.8% reported, +0.7% organic), adjusted diluted EPS of $0.56 against $0.48 consensus, adjusted EBITDA of $78.4M at an 18.7% margin versus 17.1% a year earlier, and quarterly free cash flow of $10.5M. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow inflected from −$37.4M to +$25.8M, driven roughly equally by higher operating cash flow and a 45% reduction in capex as the Braintree facility build-out completed. Reported leverage fell from 4.50x at year-end to 4.10x. That is the fourth consecutive EPS beat, and management reaffirmed both FY2026 organic growth (+0.8% to +3.3%) and adjusted EPS ($2.40–2.50), trimming reported revenue guidance only for FX.

The market sold the print 10%. The reasons hold up under inspection. First, the deleveraging came from EBITDA growth, not debt repayment — company-reported net debt actually rose $5.1M in the half, and the implied covenant EBITDA behind the 4.10x ratio ($1,600.7M ÷ 4.10 = $390.4M) must reach $400.2M to clear the 4.00x maximum that takes effect in Q1 2027. That is a 2.5% cushion to be earned in two reported quarters. Second, adjusted earnings and cash earnings differ by roughly 7x: the company itself discloses TTM adjusted free cash flow conversion of 13.5% ($25.8M FCF on $190.6M adjusted net income). Special charges of $147.4M (FY2024), $149.7M (FY2025) and $22.3M in Q2 2026 alone describe a normal cost structure rather than discrete events. At $17.10, the 7.0x multiple on guided adjusted EPS coexists with a 1.9% free cash flow yield. Third, gross margin fell ~290bp sequentially (Q1 ~55.4% to Q2 52.5%), plausibly because a $19.0M IEEPA tariff refund receivable recognized in Q1 flattered that quarter, plus $10.3M of Braintree transition cost in Q2 COGS.

We keep HOLD. Existing holders are paid to sit through the two quarters in which the covenant cushion is earned and the SurgiMend relaunch either lands or does not. New capital has no urgency: our base case of $19.50 offers ~14% upside against a ~$9.50 stress case (−44%), for a probability-weighted return near +7% on an equity whose options market prices 86.5% implied volatility. The overall score declines from 4.5 to 4.1 — not because the stock fell 16.5% from its July high, but because the covenant test is now two quarters away rather than three with the cushion unchanged, the seasonally critical Q4 consensus estimate was cut 7.3% in 30 days, a July 17 flood at the Cincinnati facility is disclosed but unquantified, and closer work on cash-versus-adjusted earnings argues the reported multiple overstates the margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1989 and based in Princeton, NJ, Integra employed 4,427 people at 12/31/2025. The core competency is engineered collagen and regenerative biomaterials, extended by three decades of acquisition into neurosurgery, surgical instruments and ENT. Two segments, renamed effective Q1 2026 with cost allocations recast from Corporate into the segments (a labeling and allocation change, not a portfolio reorganization):

**Specialty Surgery** — Q2 2026 revenue $309.3M, 73.9% of total, +1.7% reported / +1.6% organic. Neurosurgery ($213.3M, +1.9% organic) is the franchise: dural repair (DuraGen, DuraSeal, Durepair), ultrasonic aspiration (CUSA), ICP monitoring (CereLink, Licox), hydrocephalus management (Codman Certas Plus, Bactiseal) and cranial stabilization (Mayfield). Surgical Instruments ($54.8M, +3.2%) sells thousands of reusable and disposable instruments into hospital central sterile processing. ENT ($41.2M, −1.9%) is largely the Acclarent portfolio bought from Ethicon on 4/1/2024 for ~$282.0M cash.

**Tissue Reconstruction** — Q2 2026 revenue $109.5M, 26.1%, −1.9% reported / −2.0% organic. Five biomaterial platforms behind Integra Skin/IDRT, PriMatrix, MicroMatrix, AmnioExcel, MediHoney, SurgiMend, Gentrix and DuraSorb, plus Private Label ($28.2M) manufacturing for other medtech OEMs.

Geographic mix (FY2025): U.S. 73.7%, Asia-Pacific 11.6%, Europe 9.9%, rest of world 4.8%. China is ~5% of revenue with roughly half sourced from U.S. plants. No customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in 2025. Q4 is seasonally strongest as U.S. hospitals spend down budgets; Q1 is weakest — material for any modeling exercise here, since the covenant cushion depends on Q4.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat for five years.** FY2022 $1,557.7M, FY2023 $1,541.6M (−1.0%), FY2024 $1,610.5M (+4.5%), FY2025 $1,635.2M (+1.5%). Cumulative three-year growth is +5.0%, a 1.63% CAGR, and the only year above 1% was substantially inorganic (Acclarent from 4/1/2024). H1 2026 revenue was $810.7M, +1.6%.

**FY2025 GAAP results were dominated by a $511.365M goodwill impairment** recorded in Q2 2025: Neurosurgery $249.0M, Instruments & ENT $139.1M (fully impaired), Tissue Reconstruction $123.3M, tested at 12.0–13.5% discount rates with 2.5% terminal growth. FY2025 operating income was −$493.4M and net income −$516.5M. Goodwill fell from $1,096.9M to $615.2M, a $481.8M decline; the ~$29.6M difference from the charge is principally FX translation. The $178.1M indefinite-lived Codman tradename was tested separately at 14.5% and not impaired — a discrete, remaining exposure. Net intangibles were still $1,075.5M at 6/30/26, including $804.8M of completed technology.

**Underlying profitability is improving from a low base.** Reported gross margin compressed 390bp in FY2025 (54.8% to 50.9%) on quality, manufacturing cost and tariffs, then recovered to 52.5% in Q2 2026 from 50.4%. Adjusted gross margin was 61.3% versus 60.7%. The 8.86pp GAAP-to-adjusted gap in Q2 decomposes as intangible amortization in COGS 5.54pp, Braintree transition 2.46pp, structural optimization 0.80pp, EU MDR 0.05pp, acquisition 0.01pp. More than half of the "adjustment" is amortization of acquired intangibles and a further quarter is a factory transition cost that has recurred for three years.

Adding Q2's $22.253M of special charges back to GAAP operating income of $19.285M gives ~$41.5M, a 9.9% operating margin before special charges and after amortization — closely comparable to FY2025's equivalent 10.3%. Adjusted EBITDA of $78.4M at 18.7% versus $71.2M at 17.1% is the clearest evidence of genuine operating improvement in the file: ~160bp of y/y margin expansion on 0.8% revenue growth.

**Cash conversion is the central issue.** FY2025: OCF $50.4M, capex $81.4M, FCF −$31.1M — and OCF did not cover FY2025 interest expense of $86.3M. TTM 6/30/26: OCF $85.3M, capex $59.5M, FCF +$25.8M. The turn is real and driven as much by the capex step-down as by earnings. But adjusted net income of $190.6M against $25.8M of FCF is a 13.5% conversion rate, disclosed by the company. Per share, that is ~$2.44 of TTM adjusted EPS against ~$0.33 of TTM free cash flow. H1 2026 FCF of $5.5M was consumed by a $17.9M SIA earnout payment, and $20M more is due to Medtronic in October 2026 for Durepair rights.

**Capital structure.** At 6/30/26: revolver drawn $1,075.0M, term loan $707.2M, securitization $92.6M — $1,874.8M of principal, weighted-average senior rate 5.4% (down from 5.7%). Liquidity ~$496M including $274.1M of cash and short-term investments. Substantially all U.S. subsidiary assets excluding intangibles are collateral. The $575M 0.5% convertible notes matured 8/15/2025 and were settled entirely in cash from the revolver — no shares issued, and the associated call spread ($73.67) and warrants ($113.34) expired worthless. Contingent dilution from that instrument is permanently gone; the obligation migrated into the secured facility carrying the covenant and the March 2028 maturity. Note that the reported working capital improvement from $159.6M to $703.6M, and again to $860.6M at 6/30/26, is the product of two debt reclassifications (converts to long-term; securitization extended to 4/10/2029), not operational liquidity.

**The covenant schedule is the binding near-term constraint.** Maximum consolidated total leverage steps from 5.00x (through Q2 2026) to 4.75x (Q3 2026), 4.50x (Q4 2026) and 4.00x from Q1 2027. Actual: 4.10x at 6/30/26. At unchanged net debt, clearing 4.00x requires covenant EBITDA of $400.2M versus ~$390.4M implied — a $9.8M (2.5%) gap, or alternatively ~$39.1M of net debt reduction. With Q2 adjusted EBITDA up 10.1% and Q4 seasonally strongest, this is more likely met than not. Two caveats matter. Implied covenant EBITDA of $390–405M exceeds a bottom-up FY2025 adjusted EBITDA estimate of ~$318M, so the covenant figure is bank-defined with add-backs (stock compensation, pro-forma savings) and should not be read as an operating metric. And the terms of the Eighth Amended and Restated Credit Agreement dated 4/10/2026 were not verifiable — the Q2 10-Q footnote still describes the Seventh Amended agreement and a 3/24/2028 maturity. The entire covenant analysis is conditional on pre-April-2026 terms remaining in force. That is a material verification gap, and it cuts both ways: the constraint could have been loosened.

**Two items in the capital stack are easy to miss.** $900M notional of interest-rate swaps fixed at 1.404%, 1.415%, 2.867% and 2.885% all mature in December 2027, with $20.2M of gains expected to be reclassified into earnings over the next twelve months. Current interest expense is being subsidized by hedges struck in a near-zero-rate era. Holding rates and debt constant with no re-hedging, expiry removes roughly $20M pre-tax, or ~$0.16–0.19 after tax at the guided 18% rate against $2.45 of adjusted EPS. That lands in the same window as a ~$1.78B maturity. Treat it as a sensitivity, not a forecast — management has flagged refinancing intent since the FY2025 10-K and has already pushed the securitization facility to 2029.

**Balance sheet quality is poor on a tangible basis.** Stockholders' equity $1,043.4M less goodwill $611.1M less net intangibles $1,075.5M gives negative tangible equity of −$643.3M (−$8.27 per share). Inventories of $492.0M are 30% of annual revenue, with $265.5M of finished goods. Gross debt to covenant EBITDA is ~4.8x. The Altman Z-Score of 0.89 sits deep in the distress zone; adding back the non-cash impairment lifts it only to roughly 1.3–1.4, still distressed. The impairment is not what makes the score distressed — leverage plus a 1–2% GAAP operating margin does.

Tax rates have been erratic because pretax income is near zero: 61.9% (FY2024), 8.3% (FY2025), 105.7% in H1 2026 ($2.467M of expense on $2.333M of pretax income). Management guides ~18% for 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth was +0.7% in Q2 and ~+1.5% in H1 against reaffirmed FY guidance of +0.8% to +3.3%. Anything above the low end requires H2 acceleration, and the acceleration bridge is narrow.

**The bridge is Braintree.** Braintree began production in June 2026, ramping toward a planned Q4 2026 SurgiMend relaunch. SurgiMend and PriMatrix were pulled in the May 2023 Boston recall (covering all Boston-manufactured product distributed 3/1/2018–5/22/2023), and in Q2 2024 the company decided not to restart either at Boston, building Braintree instead. So the growth reacceleration depends materially on one facility with one quarter of production history, re-entering shelf positions competitors have occupied for three years.

**The pipeline slipped a year in five months.** The FY2025 10-K (2/26/2026) expected SurgiMend PMA approval in 2026 and hoped for DuraSorb in 2026. The Q2 10-Q (7/29/2026) moves SurgiMend to 2027 and DuraSorb to "later in 2027." Both are breast-reconstruction indications, the highest-value expansion in Tissue Reconstruction. SurgiMend's PMA has been pending since Q3 2021 and received "approvable pending GMP status" in July 2024 — the clinical file is closed and only manufacturing compliance stands in the way. The mechanism connecting quality to growth is explicit: the December 19, 2024 warning letter covering Mansfield MA, Plainsboro NJ and Princeton NJ does not restrict manufacturing, shipping or 510(k) clearances, but it does block PMA approvals "reasonably related" to the violations. A corroborating data point: the $40M DuraSorb PMA milestone under the SIA acquisition agreement lapsed unearned because approval was not obtained by the applicable date, and total contingent consideration fell from $26.0M to $7.3M in six months. The cash saving and the regulatory setback have the same cause.

**Tissue Reconstruction faces a reimbursement change on top of the share loss.** In the 2026 Medicare physician, hospital outpatient and ASC rules, payment for most sheet skin substitutes moved to a single per-square-centimeter rate regardless of product and setting. That removes the reimbursement premium differentiated products historically commanded, and it lands directly on the segment already declining 2% organically. MediHoney remains off the market with no announced relaunch date.

**Earnings growth, not revenue growth, is the realistic case.** Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $2.46 is roughly flat against TTM $2.44; FY2027 consensus of $2.61 implies ~6% growth. Revision data does not show an inflection: current-year estimates are +0.3% over 30 days and +0.4% over 90 days, next year −0.5%/−0.4%, and the seasonally critical next quarter was cut 7.3% in 30 days. This is not the estimate-revision profile that precedes re-rating; it is consensus taking management's guidance at face value. Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion (~160bp y/y) plus the capex step-down is the genuine growth vector, and it is a self-help vector with a finite runway.

The 7.0x adjusted-EPS multiple is the number bulls quote and it is the least informative one here. Two adjustments destroy most of the apparent discount. First, cash: 13.5% adjusted FCF conversion means the same business yields 1.9% on free cash flow. Second, capital structure: with debt at 55% of EV, the equity multiple compresses mechanically. On EV/EBITDA the company trades ~9.2x on FY2025 estimated adjusted EBITDA. For a business growing revenue ~1% with two open FDA warning letters, a shrinking regenerative-tissue segment facing reimbursement commoditization, and 4.1x leverage, 9x is defensible rather than cheap. Mid-single-digit-growth surgical device peers clear 12–15x [unverified], and the gap is largely earned.

The peer table is of limited use — median P/E of 85.5x is set by loss-makers and one outlier, and only CONMED at 19.4x is a genuine slow-growth levered comparable, on an unstated earnings basis. The cleaner framing is scenario-based, since the equity is a levered call on deleveraging:

- **Base $19.50** — FY2027 adjusted EBITDA ~$345M (mid-single-digit growth off ~$325M) at 9.0x = $3,105M EV, less net debt ~$1,560M after modest paydown = $1,545M equity over ~78.5M shares = $19.68. +14% from $17.10. This is consistent with the $19.00 consensus median and the $19.56 mean, and with BMO's $19 target initiated 7/9/2026.
- **Bull $27.00** — SurgiMend relaunches on schedule, Tissue Reconstruction returns to growth, PMAs land in 2027, refinancing completes at manageable spreads, covenant cleared comfortably. $365M EBITDA at 10.0x = $3,650M less $1,520M = $2,130M / 79M = $27.0. +57.9%, matching the high sell-side target.
- **Bear $9.50** — EBITDA stalls near $315M, the Q1 2027 covenant requires an amendment on worse pricing, refinancing of ~$1.78B occurs into an elevated long end, swaps roll off unhedged. 7.5x on $315M = $2,363M less $1,650M = $713M / 78.5M = $9.08. −44%, near the 52-week low of $8.70. A milder bear anchor is the $14.00 low sell-side target (−18%).

Probability-weighting 20% bull / 53% base / 27% bear gives roughly +7% expected return. That is approximately fair, not compelling, for an equity with 86.5% implied volatility and negative tangible book. Cheapness here is a function of leverage and compliance risk rather than mispricing, which is why the valuation score sits at 5.0 rather than higher.

## Competitive Landscape

Integra's competitive position is genuinely two-sided, and conflating the halves is the common error on this name.

**Specialty Surgery is defensible and slow.** Against divisions of Medtronic, Stryker, Steris and B. Braun, Integra holds entrenched positions in dural repair, ICP monitoring, hydrocephalus shunts and ultrasonic aspiration, with surgeon-level switching costs and consumable pull-through behind capital platforms. The 10-K's claim of global leadership in neurosurgery is credible. The problem is growth: neuro +1.9% organic and instruments +3.2% are respectable for mature categories but insufficient to carry 4.1x leverage. Surgical instruments in particular is tender-driven and commoditizing — the 10-K flags hospital centralized purchasing, GPO vendor limitation and physician gainsharing as sources of price sensitivity. ENT declined 1.9% organically two years after the $282M Acclarent purchase.

**Tissue Reconstruction was the growth engine and is now the drag.** Down 6.9% in FY2025 and 2.0% organically in Q2 2026, against Smith & Nephew, Organogenesis, MiMedx and Axogen, who absorbed the volume the recalls vacated. The 10-K's own line — "major shifts in industry market share have occurred in connection with product corrective actions, physician advisories, safety alerts" — describes what happened to Integra. Three years of shelf absence in a category where surgeons standardize on reliable availability is hard to reverse, and the 2025 PriMatrix relaunch plus the planned Q4 2026 SurgiMend relaunch are re-entries into occupied positions.

The regulatory cost structure compounds this. EU MDR consumed $41.9M in FY2025 with legacy-device deadlines at 12/31/2027 and 12/31/2028; FDA's QMSR replaced QSR effective 2/2/2026; the EU SoHO Regulation applies from 8/7/2027. Rising fixed compliance cost structurally favors scale players over a $1.6B-revenue company already spending ~$150M annually on remediation and compliance. The five-year relative record is unambiguous: proxy-disclosed peer group TSR of +22.6% against Integra's −80.9%. The sector was investable; Integra was the idiosyncratic problem.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Two open FDA warning letters (2019 Boston/TEI, plus 7/17/2023 and 12/19/2024 letters).** The 2024 letter names cranial perforators, disposable cottonoids and collagen-based products. It blocks PMA approvals "reasonably related" to the violations, converting a quality problem into a growth problem. This is the defining risk and it has no fixed resolution date.
2. **Covenant step-down to 4.00x in Q1 2027 with ~2.5% cushion.** Likely met given Q4 seasonality and 10.1% EBITDA growth, but the cushion is roughly one disappointing quarter wide. It also explains why the buyback lapsed 12/31/2025 with $50M unused.
3. **~$1.78B senior facility maturing 3/24/2028, to be refinanced into a higher long-end rate environment.** The FY2025 10-K says the company expects to seek to refinance all outstanding debt in 2026, and FY2026 EPS guidance already embeds "a higher interest rate environment, and potential debt refinancing actions." The December 2027 swap expiry removes ~$20M of annual interest subsidy in the same window.
4. **Medicare skin-substitute payment overhaul in the 2026 rules** — a single per-square-centimeter rate regardless of product, hitting the segment already shrinking.
5. **Bovine sourcing concentration:** 26% of FY2025 revenue came from products containing bovine-derived material, sourced from U.S. and New Zealand tissue. Low BSE risk categories, but EEA rules could restrict or ban products in certain countries.
6. **Tariffs and an uncollected receivable.** Integra paid ~$19.9M of cash tariffs in 2025 but expensed only $6.5M in FY2025 COGS. After the February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs, a $19.0M refund receivable was recognized in Q1 2026, $17.6M of claims submitted, and only $0.5M collected in cash through 6/30/2026. The remaining ~$18.5M equals ~72% of TTM free cash flow. The legal basis is strong; the risk is timing and CBP validation. Section 301 tariffs of 10.0–12.5% replaced the expired Section 122 authority in July 2026.
7. **Cincinnati flood (July 17, 2026, subsequent event).** Facility, equipment and inventory damage. Management does not currently expect a material adverse effect, citing distribution-center inventory and insurance, but the evaluation of remediation, impairments and recovery timing is ongoing and nothing is reflected in Q2 financials.
8. **Litigation and cyber.** The *Pembroke* securities class action (class period 3/11/2019–5/22/2023) was dismissed without prejudice 7/1/2025 and has since drawn a Second Amended Complaint, a Supplemental Amended Complaint (3/16/2026) and a Supplemental Motion to Dismiss (4/3/2026), with two derivative suits stayed behind it. Separately, exploitation of a third-party enterprise software vulnerability has produced several putative class actions naming Integra.
9. **Residual impairment exposure.** $611.1M of goodwill remains (Tissue Reconstruction $269.2M while shrinking organically) plus the untouched $178.1M Codman tradename tested at a 14.5% discount rate. Non-cash and covenant-neutral, but a recurring headline risk. The stock roughly doubling from March lows reduces the likelihood of a share-price-triggered interim test.
10. **Governance.** Four CEOs in roughly five years, with Stuart Essig resuming President & CEO on 5/1/2026 while retaining the Chairman role — the two roles now combined in the company's longest-tenured insider. Economic terms of Essig's employment agreement and Mojdeh Poul's separation agreement were not obtained.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 78.7% to 86.5% between 7/22 and 8/7 while the stock fell 8.3%. Rising volatility on a falling price is the market pricing wider outcome dispersion, consistent with the covenant sequence, refinancing, and an unquantified flood exposure rather than with a directional view already resolved. An 86.5% IV implies roughly ±25% over a quarter on a $1.33B medtech — extreme for the sector and appropriate for an equity that is 55% financed by debt at the EV level. Short interest of 11.0% of float with 6.2 days to cover means the bearish view is well populated but not crowded to squeeze extremes; it does create upside gap risk on any refinancing announcement or PMA clearance. Sell-side positioning is bifurcated in an unusual way: 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 7 hold, 0 sell and 2 strong sell, with targets spanning $14 to $27. That dispersion is the honest summary of the setup, and it corroborates the HOLD rather than challenging it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($27, +57.9%)

Operating improvement is real and compounding. Adjusted EBITDA grew 10.1% y/y to an 18.7% margin on 0.8% revenue growth — that is cost and mix execution, and the incentive plan is built on Revenue, Adjusted EBITDA, Operating Cash Flow and Organic Revenue Growth, which points management at the constraint that matters. Free cash flow inflected from −$37.4M to +$25.8M TTM as capex fell 45% with Braintree complete; if Braintree transition costs ($10.3M in Q2 alone) and EU MDR spend ($41.9M in FY2025, with legacy deadlines in 2027/2028) genuinely end, adjusted FCF conversion should improve structurally from 13.5% toward normal medtech levels, at which point 7.0x adjusted EPS stops being illusory. Four consecutive beats (13.6%, 30.9%, 1.7%, 21.7%) support confidence in the $2.40–2.50 guide. The 4.00x covenant is cleared in Q4 on seasonal strength, refinancing completes in 2026–2027 with re-hedging, and the equity re-rates as the balance sheet stops being the story. SurgiMend relaunches in Q4 2026 and its PMA — clinically closed since July 2024, gated only on GMP — clears in 2027, followed by DuraSorb, restoring Tissue Reconstruction to growth. Essig's inducement package (705,468 options and 365,297 RSUs, together 1.38% of shares outstanding) ties the returning CEO directly to the share price from a low base.

### Bear Case ($9.50, −44%)

Adjusted EPS of $2.45 is a bookkeeping construct: the company has excluded ~$150M of charges annually for three consecutive years, and the largest single component of the gross margin adjustment is amortization of intangibles that will still be there in 2028. On cash, the equity yields 1.9%. Revenue has grown 1.63% annually for three years with the only strong year bought for $282M. Tissue Reconstruction is shrinking while Medicare commoditizes its pricing and competitors hold the shelf space three years of recalls vacated. Meanwhile the balance sheet has no slack: net debt rose $5.1M in H1 while management described continued deleveraging, tangible equity is −$643.3M, the Altman Z-Score is 0.89 (1.3–1.4 excluding the impairment), the Q1 2027 covenant demands 2.5% more EBITDA than is currently implied, ~$1.78B matures in March 2028, and $900M of 1.4–2.9% swaps expire in December 2027. A single stumble — the flood proving material, the SurgiMend relaunch slipping, the $18.5M tariff receivable not collecting, a Q4 miss after a 7.3% consensus cut — forces an amendment negotiated from weakness, at which point 55% debt in the EV does the rest. The five-year record (TSR $100 → $19.13 against peers at $122.62; net income $169.1M → −$516.8M) is the base rate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $19.50 (+14% from $17.10), bull $27.00 (+57.9%), bear $9.50 (−44%). Probability-weighted expected return ~+7% over 12 months. Horizon: reassess at the Q3 print (late October 2026), with the decisive checkpoint at the Q4/FY print in February 2027, which reports the first quarter measured against the 4.00x covenant.

This is a HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE because the base case offers 14% while the stress case costs 44%, the cash yield is 1.9%, and the covenant analysis is conditional on credit-agreement terms that could not be verified. It is a HOLD rather than REDUCE because EBITDA is compounding at 10%, free cash flow has inflected, leverage is falling, four consecutive quarters have beaten, and the price is already 16.5% below the July high with guidance intact.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 reported leverage at or below 4.00x, or covenant EBITDA of $400M+ demonstrated ahead of the Q1 2027 step-down — removing the binding constraint two quarters early.
2. Completed refinancing of the senior facility that extends the 3/24/2028 maturity beyond 2030 at a disclosed all-in cost within ~100bp of the current 5.4%, with the December 2027 swap roll-off addressed.
3. Adjusted free cash flow conversion above 40% for two consecutive quarters, evidencing that Braintree and EU MDR charges are genuinely ending rather than rotating into new "special" buckets.
4. FDA closeout of the December 2024 warning letter, or SurgiMend PMA approval — either would unlock the highest-value pipeline assets.

## Conclusion

Integra is doing the right things too slowly, with too little balance-sheet room. The Q2 print was operationally better than its 10% share-price reaction implied — adjusted EBITDA margin up 160bp, free cash flow positive on a trailing basis for the first time in years, leverage down 40bp, a fourth straight EPS beat, guidance reaffirmed on everything except FX. But the 40bp of deleveraging was earned entirely through EBITDA growth while net debt rose $5.1M, adjusted earnings still convert to cash at 13.5%, revenue growth is 0.7% organic, and the equity must clear a 4.00x covenant with a 2.5% cushion, refinance ~$1.78B by March 2028, and absorb the December 2027 expiry of $900M of near-zero-cost swaps.

At $17.10 the market is paying 7.0x adjusted EPS and ~9.2x EV/EBITDA for that combination — approximately the right price. Our base case of $19.50 is within 3% of the sell-side median, our stress case of $9.50 is near the 52-week low, and the sell-side split (7 holds against 2 strong sells and 3 buys, targets from $14 to $27) mirrors our own dispersion. HOLD, with the honest label that this is an uncertain hold rather than a conviction one. The next two reported quarters resolve most of what matters.
