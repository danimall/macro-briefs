## Executive Summary

Lilly is the fastest-growing company of its size in any sector. FY2025 revenue reached $65,179M, up 44.7%, with net income up 94.9% to $20,640M and diluted EPS of $22.95. Q1 2026 accelerated: revenue $19,799M (+56%), net income $7,396M (+168%), diluted EPS $8.26 (+170%). Consensus has FY2026 EPS at $34.53 and FY2027 at $45.08, with next-year estimates up 3.2% over 90 days. At $1,148.84 the stock trades at 33.3x current-year and 25.5x next-year consensus, implying a PEG on next-year earnings growth of roughly 0.83. Against a pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 20.0x — where the median constituent is growing revenue in the low single digits or shrinking — the premium is defensible on arithmetic alone.

The problem is not the growth rate. It is the composition and the durability of the price realization behind it. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro plus Zepbound) was 56% of FY2025 revenue and 65% of Q1 2026 revenue — concentration deepened even as geography diversified. All growth is volume; consolidated realized price fell 6% in FY2025 and 13% in Q1 2026, the latter driven substantially by adding Mounjaro to China's National Reimbursed Drug List. Roughly three-fifths of U.S. gross list value is rebated away, and the Q1 U.S. price line (-7%, improved from -10% in FY2025) was flattered by a disclosed favorable one-time true-up of prior-period rebate estimates, with revisions to prior estimates widening from "less than 1%" to "less than 3%" of U.S. revenue year over year. Free cash flow was $8,972M in FY2025 against $20,640M of net income — a 43% conversion — because $12.9B of receivable and inventory build absorbed the difference, and shareholder returns plus business development were funded partly with $8.9B of incremental debt.

I am maintaining HOLD, with the overall score at 6.5 versus 6.7 in the prior review. The decline sits in the risk dimension and in valuation asymmetry, not in the growth engine. Base case is $1,300 (29x FY2027 consensus), roughly 13% above the current price, against a bear case of $880, roughly 23% below. Q2 results land August 5, two days after this cutoff, with a three-day implied move of ±7.1% and near-term at-the-money implied volatility at 85.6% against 38.3% one-year realized — the 94th percentile of the current chain. Owning through the print is reasonable. Adding into it, at these levels and with an unresolved question about Foundayo's U.S. launch trajectory, is not.

## Company Overview

Lilly, incorporated in Indiana in 1901, reports as a single segment — human pharmaceutical products — selling in approximately 90 countries with roughly 50,000 employees, including ~12,000 in R&D. Six products each above $3 billion (Mounjaro, Zepbound, Verzenio, Trulicity, Taltz, Jardiance and its combinations) accounted for 82% of FY2025 revenue.

The moat rests on four verifiable pillars. First, patent runway: the tirzepatide compound patent runs to 2036 in the U.S., 2037 in major European markets, and 2040 in Japan. U.S. regulatory data protection for Mounjaro/Zepbound expires in 2027, but that permits an abbreviated filing pathway, not generic entry — the compound patent is the binding constraint. Second, manufacturing scale: capex rose from $5.1B (FY2024) to $7.8B (FY2025), with new sites across ten U.S. states and four other countries, and management says spending goes higher. Peptide capacity has been the industry's binding constraint, and Lilly is buying its way out of it. Third, LillyDirect, a company-owned direct-to-patient platform (third-party pharmacies dispense) that partially bypasses pharmacy benefit managers, relevant given CVS Caremark's July 2025 removal of Zepbound as a preferred obesity medicine on some plans. Fourth, an internally funded pipeline: R&D was $13,337M in FY2025 (20.5% of revenue) and $3,510M in Q1 2026 (+28%), split $1,266M early-stage / $2,244M late-stage.

A structural caveat: management states it cannot reliably allocate R&D by project or phase, and that no individual project is material to consolidated R&D. Pipeline value cannot be traced to spending in the filings; it must be assessed from clinical-status disclosure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2022 $28,541M → FY2023 $34,124M → FY2024 $45,043M → FY2025 $65,179M. Three-year revenue multiple of 2.28x. Net income $10,590M (FY2024) → $20,640M (FY2025). Q1 2026: U.S. $12,119M (+43%), outside U.S. $7,680M (+81%).

**The price/volume bridge is the single most important table in the filings.**

| Period | Volume | Price | FX | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 consolidated | +50% | −6% | +1% | +45% |
| FY2025 U.S. | +53% | −10% | 0% | +43% |
| Q1 2026 consolidated | +65% | −13% | +4% | +56% |
| Q1 2026 U.S. | +49% | −7% | 0% | +43% |
| Q1 2026 ex-U.S. | +95% | −25% | +11% | +81% |

The consolidated price line worsened from −6% to −13%, but the mechanism is largely geographic mix: ex-U.S. revenue grew 81% at structurally lower realized prices, and the −25% ex-U.S. figure is specifically attributed to the China NRDL listing, an explicit volume-for-price trade. The U.S.-only line improved. "Price erosion is accelerating" is established at the reported consolidated level and not at the individual-market level — a distinction that most commentary collapses.

**Margins.** Gross margin 81.3% (FY2024) → 83.0% (FY2025) → 81.9% (Q1 2026), with the Q1 decline attributed solely to lower realized prices. Operating leverage was positive in Q1 2026: MS&A +19% and R&D +28% against revenue +56%. Effective tax rate 19.8% (FY2025) and 16.4% in Q1 2026 versus 20.2% a year earlier, with movement driven principally by non-deductible acquired IPR&D charges, which were $584M in Q1 2026 against $2,910M for full-year 2025.

**Cash conversion is the weakest link and it is quantifiable.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $16,813M came in *below* net income. After $7,841M of capex, free cash flow was $8,972M — a 13.8% FCF margin against a 31.7% net margin and 43% FCF/net-income conversion. The mechanism is visible on the balance sheet: trade receivables rose $6,754M and inventories $6,155M during the year, roughly $12.9B of working-capital absorption against a $20.1B revenue increase, plus a $12.9B prepaid-tax balance at year-end (falling to $11.8B at 3/31/26). Receivables grew 61% against revenue growth of 45% — a modest DSO extension, not a collections break.

In Q1 2026, OCF of $5,333M less $2,326M capex gave $3,007M of FCF against $1,548M of dividends and $2,356M of buybacks; the $897M gap was bridged with $6,461M of long-term debt issuance and $1,775M of net commercial paper. I will not annualize Q1 FCF — Q1 carries a ~$1.0B employee-compensation payout that reverses through the year. A normalized frame: applying the FY2025 OCF/revenue ratio of 25.8% to a ~$83.5B 2026 revenue midpoint gives ~$21.5B of OCF, less a $10–11B capex run rate, for FCF of roughly **$10.5–11.5B**. On a $1.02T market capitalization that is a 1.0–1.1% FCF yield. Investors are paying for 2028+ cash generation, not current cash generation.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $43,400M at 3/31/26 against $31,198M of equity and $5,282M of cash. Derived FY2025 EBITDA of roughly $28,981M (pretax income $25,731M plus ~$1,150M estimated interest plus ~$2,100M D&A) implies gross debt/EBITDA of 1.47x and net leverage near 1.1x; on 2026 revenue at Q1 pretax margins, forward gross leverage computes near 1.0x. There is $10.1B of undrawn committed credit, $10.0B of which backstops commercial paper. Long-term debt carries at $41,595M against $38,233M fair value — a benefit to the issuer from below-market fixed coupons, not a credit signal.

Two screening artifacts should be discarded. An Altman Z-Score of 1.25 with an EBIT/assets input of −0.005 is mechanically broken: Lilly's income statement presents no "operating income" line, so automated pulls return nothing. Recomputing from the statements gives Z ≈ 8.9 using market equity, and ≈ 2.07 even substituting book equity — above the 1.81 threshold either way. The related "operating margin −0.9%" is the same artifact. Net margin (31.7%), ROA (18.4%) and ROE (77.8% on year-end equity) all reproduce correctly. Genuine leverage figures are total debt/equity 1.60x and total liabilities/equity 3.24x; equity/assets is thin at 23.6% after decades of buybacks.

**Contingent commitments deserve weight.** Contract-manufacturing and materials-supply agreements could require up to approximately $10 billion if minimum purchase volumes are not met, over terms up to eight years. Unfunded venture commitments are $850M. And pending-acquisition consideration payable at closing moved from "less than $3 billion" (10-K, February 2026) to "up to approximately $12 billion" (10-Q, April 2026) — a roughly $9B increase in ten weeks whose constituent transactions could not be identified in public sources and which the July 2026 AtaiBeckley deal post-dates. That figure is a conditional ceiling subject to closing conditions and may include contingent milestone maxima; it should not be treated as certain cash outflow. It does, however, sit on top of a rising capex program and $3.9B of quarterly shareholder returns.

**Financial health score: 7.0.** Solvency is not in question — 1.0–1.5x leverage, $29B of EBITDA, $10B undrawn. But a company generating a 32% net margin that converts 43% of it to free cash flow, funds distributions and business development partly with debt, and carries up to ~$22B of conditional cash calls is not a fortress balance sheet in the sense the headline profitability implies. This is a timing constraint that should ease as growth decelerates toward the guided ~28%, not an earnings-quality problem.

## Growth Analysis

**Delivery record.** Four consecutive beats: +14.5%, +22.0%, +11.9%, +27.0%. That track record raises confidence in forward consensus, but the base against which the next print is measured has been cut hard.

**The estimate-revision pattern is the most useful new datapoint.** Current-quarter consensus fell from $8.80 to $6.58 over 30 days (−25.2%) while next-quarter estimates rose 0.7%, next-year rose 1.4% (and 3.2% over 90 days), and current-year fell only 4.7%. A demand problem does not produce that shape — it compresses the out-years. A discrete, non-recurring charge landing in the June quarter does. The most plausible mechanism is acquired IPR&D expense from closing one or more of the pending acquisitions disclosed at "up to approximately $12 billion," a line item Lilly expenses immediately and which is largely non-deductible (hence the tax-rate volatility). At $2.22 per share on ~896M diluted shares, the implied charge is roughly $2.0B. This is inference, not disclosure. The alternative reading — a step-up in Foundayo launch spending against a slower-than-planned uptake — would be more concerning, because it would indicate Lilly is buying share in orals rather than taking it. The August 5 print resolves which.

**Orforglipron / Foundayo is the swing variable.** FDA-approved for obesity in April 2026 after receiving a Commissioner's National Priority Voucher, with a Phase 3 type 2 diabetes trial also hitting its primary endpoint. Two structural features matter. It is licensed from Chugai with tiered royalties from mid-single digits to low teens of worldwide net sales, recognized in cost of sales — a permanent gross-margin drag relative to tirzepatide. And as a small molecule approved under an NDA, it faces IRA price-setting at nine years post-approval rather than thirteen, exposing it around 2035.

Against that, third-party prescription data through the second quarter reportedly shows Foundayo's U.S. launch trailing Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide, which has been strong since January. This is an external read of a third-party dataset, not company disclosure, and it is not inconsistent with separate reports of favorable head-to-head clinical data — trial superiority and slower commercial uptake can coexist when the competitor launched first with an established brand. The structural counter-argument is that Lilly's oral advantage is international and supply-driven: orforglipron is easier to manufacture, and Lilly holds $13.7B of inventory ($8.5B work-in-process at 3/31/26) plus $26.5B of net PP&E.

**Other pipeline movement since the 10-K:** retatrutide Phase 3 in type 2 diabetes met its primary endpoint (cardiovascular outcomes not expected until 2029); eloralintide entered Phase 3 in three settings; baricitinib in type 1 diabetes; brenipatide in major depressive disorder; sofetabart mipitecan in ovarian cancer with Breakthrough designation in the platinum-resistant setting. Business development cadence is accelerating: Verve (July 2025, ~$549M net plus CVR), Ventyx (March 2026, $1,178M with $977M to acquired IPR&D), and AtaiBeckley (announced July 16, 2026, ~$2.8B upfront plus up to $2.50/share in CVRs, total potentially ~$3.8B, for an intranasal psychedelic in treatment-resistant depression — corroborated across multiple outlets but appearing in no filing reviewed).

**The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program is the largest unquantified swing.** Finalized in Q1 2026, it gives Medicare beneficiaries discounted access to Lilly obesity medicines from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027, with a state option to expand Medicaid. Management's own words: "The uptake from this expanded access is unknown." Lilly traded price for access to a previously uncovered population; neither side of the trade is quantified, and the 18-month window creates a defined 2027 cliff with post-2027 economics still under discussion with CMS.

**Growth prospects score: 8.0**, unchanged. Revenue growth decelerates from 45% to a guided ~28% (the $82–85B range is secondary-sourced and unverified against a company release), and next-year EPS growth of roughly 30% is well supported. I withhold a 9 because the growth is single-mechanism, priced-down, and the oral leg has an unresolved competitive read.

## Competitive Landscape

Injectable share looks firmly Lilly's: ex-U.S. Mounjaro went from $1,186M (Q1 2025) to $4,430M (Q1 2026) following launch across all major markets, and growth is attributed to Mounjaro/Zepbound taking share against injectable Wegovy. The oral race is the contested front, and the first commercial read runs against Lilly.

The 10-K names its threats with unusual specificity. Competition "from China and other markets where research and development capabilities have expanded and accelerated significantly," with those companies increasingly licensing to multinationals and competing directly. An FDA proposal that could streamline biosimilar development, "including potentially minimizing the requirement for comparative clinical efficacy studies" — a material erosion vector for a portfolio and pipeline weighted toward biologics. A standalone AI risk factor conceding that "AI may enable new competitors in drug discovery." And a detailed treatment of PBM formulary exclusion, prior authorization and co-pay accumulators, with LillyDirect and direct-to-employer channels named as countermeasures while conceding those "may not adequately mitigate unfavorable private sector dynamics."

Lilly also faces demand leakage from counterfeit, misbranded and mass-compounded incretins, which the 10-K says have benefited from "low barriers to entry and a relative lack of regulatory oversight," producing "diversion of potential sales and potential net price erosion" that never appears in reported volumes.

Self-cannibalization is acknowledged in the filings: "the introduction of our own innovative products results in these adverse impacts for our preexisting products." Foundayo and retatrutide compete for the same patients as Zepbound.

**Competitive position score: 7.2**, versus 7.4 previously. Wide moat, decade-long patent runway, unmatched peptide capacity, with a slightly negative trajectory on the oral launch read and accelerating list-to-net compression.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-molecule concentration.** Tirzepatide is 65% of Q1 2026 revenue. The 10-K's own language contemplates that loss of patent protection, prescription-rate changes, product liability, safety concerns, demand swings or channel dynamics "could materially impact our results of operations or result in significant and sudden declines or volatility in the trading price of our common stock and market capitalization." A risk factor that explicitly names market-cap collapse should be read literally.

**U.S. pricing is now partly contractual.** November 2025 voluntary agreements with the U.S. government were finalized in Q1 2026: lower Medicaid and platform prices, participation in a government direct-to-patient purchasing channel at significant discounts to list, "a more balanced pricing approach across developed nations" for new launches, and a three-year Section 232 tariff grace period *conditional on meeting U.S. manufacturing investment commitments.* That condition converts a large portion of Lilly's capex program from discretionary investment into a compliance obligation. On the IRA: Jardiance was in the first ten selected at a 66% discount to 2023 list effective 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio were selected in January 2026 for 2028, and together were ~11% of Q1 2026 revenue ($919M + $1,302M).

**Capacity overbuild.** The 10-K states the risk in both directions: overestimation of demand "may render built or in process manufacturing capacity unnecessary, may result in supply imbalances, or may subject us to contractual payment obligations, which may be significant." With ~$8B of capex rising, ~$10B of minimum-purchase exposure, and $13.7B of inventory, the capital plan is partly locked in.

**Litigation, self-insured.** Lilly states it is "predominantly self-insured for litigation liability losses for all our currently and previously marketed products." There is no insurance backstop. Recent developments have moved against the company: the Federal Circuit reversed the Emgality/Teva invalidity finding in April 2026 and Lilly recognized a charge (Q1 asset impairment/restructuring/special charges were $279M versus $35M, consistent but not itemized); the Seventh Circuit affirmed an adverse Average Manufacturer Price qui tam verdict with certiorari filed March 2026; the Second Circuit revived the Mosaic Health 340B antitrust class action. Two federal MDLs in E.D. Pennsylvania cover alleged gastrointestinal injuries and NAION, plus state proceedings in Delaware, Indiana and New Jersey and class petitions in Israel and Canada. The NAION and GI dockets are, in my view, the least-priced idiosyncratic exposure in the story. One favorable data point: the Ninth Circuit reversed in Lilly's favor in Research Corporation Technologies in February 2026, with no further payments owed.

**Customer and supply concentration.** McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal Health each exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in 2023, 2024 and 2025 — unchanged. China-based suppliers provide portions of the chemical synthesis, reagents and starting-materials chain, with the BIOSECURE Act named as a potential disruptor. Geographic diversification improved (ex-U.S. from 33% to 39% of revenue); customer and product concentration did not.

**Gross-to-net.** Implied FY2025 U.S. gross sales of roughly $105.6B against $43.5B of net U.S. revenue put deductions near 59% of gross, versus ~58% in 2024. A 5% change in the consolidated rebate/discount/return liability moves revenue by ~$897M. Up to ~$360M of the Q1 U.S. price line could be prior-period true-up — roughly three percentage points of the reported −7%. I draw no inference about intent; the disclosure is voluntary, specific and quantified, which is the opposite of concealment. It does mean underlying U.S. net pricing is likely worse than the Q1 headline.

At-the-money implied volatility is 85.6% on the three-day August 7 expiry against 38.3% one-year realized — a 47.2-point spread at the 94th percentile of the current chain — with a term structure in steep backwardation (85.6% at one week, 48.8% at one month, 40.8% at three months, 38.2% at six months). Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.1%, roughly $82 per share, consistent with externally reported estimates of a $76B+ post-earnings value swing. Implied volatility rose 24.2 points in three sessions while the shares fell 2.4%.

Direction is not being signaled bearishly. Put/call skew is −4.8% (call-heavy) at the front, put/call open interest is 0.95, put/call volume 1.03, and the only flagged unusual print is 1,125 September $1,280 calls against 267 open interest at 14% out of the money. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float, 3.3 days to cover. Six-month skew of −4.2% is the one place where longer-dated protection carries a premium.

The takeaway for a fundamental holder: near-term optionality is expensive, so this is a poor window to establish or add to a position with options, and a reasonable window to monetize volatility against existing stock. The absence of put-heavy positioning does not corroborate the bear case, and I do not treat call-tilted flow as confirmation of the bull case either.

**Risk factors score: 4.8** (from 5.5). Single-molecule concentration at 65%, a self-insured product-liability docket including NAION, unresolved post-2027 Medicare economics, up to ~$22B of conditional cash calls including $9B of unidentified pending acquisitions, and a two-day-out binary print priced at ±7.1% together constitute meaningful downside catalysts that a 25.5x forward multiple does not fully reflect.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,600 (+39.3%)

Consensus FY2027 EPS of $45.08 proves conservative, as it has for four consecutive quarters (beats of 11.9% to 27.0%). Medicare Bridge uptake from July 2026 delivers volume that more than offsets the price concession, and CMS extends access past December 2027. Foundayo's launch curve steepens as international supply advantage compounds — orforglipron is easier to manufacture and Lilly already holds $13.7B of inventory — de-concentrating the product mix while adding an oral leg with a 2036-equivalent runway on the injectable. Retatrutide and eloralintide extend the franchise into the next decade. Working-capital absorption moderates as growth decelerates from 56% toward 28%, lifting FCF conversion from 43% toward 70%+ and funding accelerated buybacks against the $8.6B remaining authorization. At 34x FY2027 consensus, or 29x on an upside $55 figure, the stock reaches roughly $1,600 — the high end of the published target range.

### Bear Case — $880 (−23.4%)

Novo's oral semaglutide consolidates the U.S. oral market while Foundayo's launch remains subscale, forcing Lilly into heavier promotional spend that compresses margins. Consolidated realized price continues to fall as China NRDL, the government direct-purchase platform, the Bridge Program discount and PBM leverage compound; Medicare volume proves insufficient to offset. The 2027 Bridge cliff arrives unresolved. Working-capital and capacity commitments prove partly stranded, triggering inventory impairment and minimum-purchase payments against the $10B exposure. A NAION or GI MDL development produces a quantified, uninsured liability. The pending $12B of acquisitions closes into acquired IPR&D charges that suppress GAAP earnings while raising leverage. FY2027 consensus trims to ~$44 and the multiple compresses to 20x — still a premium to the peer median — giving $880, near the published low target of $850 and above the independent $900 fair-value estimate.

### Base Case — $1,300 (+13.2%)

FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $45.08 at a 29x multiple. Volume growth remains dominant with continued mid-to-high-single-digit U.S. price erosion; Foundayo becomes a solid rather than spectacular second franchise; FCF conversion improves toward 60–70% as growth normalizes. This is the most probable path, and it delivers roughly the equity cost of capital over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive with specific catalysts; the issue is entry price and the 0.6:1 base-to-bear asymmetry.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $1,020** — approximately 22.6x FY2027 consensus, which would restore roughly 27% base-case upside against ~14% bear-case downside and flip the asymmetry. (The prior "add below $1,000" threshold remains directionally right; I am raising it modestly given FY2027 estimates have risen 3.2% in 90 days.)
2. **Q2/Q3 evidence that the U.S. price line is stabilizing better than −7% excluding prior-period true-ups, combined with sequential Foundayo U.S. prescription growth outpacing oral semaglutide.** That would resolve the two live bear planks simultaneously.
3. **Operating cash flow exceeding net income for two consecutive quarters,** confirming that FY2025's 43% FCF conversion was hypergrowth working-capital timing rather than a structural feature.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2027 consensus cut by more than 10%** from $45.08, which would mark demand or pricing deterioration rather than charge timing.
2. **Foundayo U.S. scripts flat or declining through Q3 2026 alongside decelerating Zepbound volume** — the combination that would indicate share loss in orals without injectable offset.
3. **A quantified adverse development in the NAION or GI MDLs,** given full self-insurance, or class certification with damages exposure disclosed.
4. **Disclosure that the Medicare Bridge Program's volume is failing to offset its price concession** — visible as a consolidated U.S. price line worse than −12% in H2 2026.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately following the August 5, 2026 Q2 report, which should resolve (a) whether the 25.2% current-quarter estimate cut reflects a discrete acquisition charge or launch spending, (b) the underlying U.S. price line excluding true-ups, (c) Foundayo's reported launch trajectory, and (d) the composition of the "up to approximately $12 billion" pending-acquisition disclosure. Secondary checkpoint: Q3 2026 results, the first full quarter of Medicare Bridge uptake.

## Conclusion

Lilly is the best operating business in large-cap pharmaceuticals and it is priced accordingly. The growth is real, volume-driven, patent-protected into the mid-2030s on the core molecule, and supported by a manufacturing base competitors cannot replicate quickly. Four consecutive double-digit earnings beats and rising out-year estimates give the forward numbers credibility.

What restrains the recommendation is the shape of the payoff rather than the quality of the company. Two-thirds of revenue rests on one molecule whose realized price is falling and whose U.S. economics are now partly set by contract with the federal government through an 18-month window that expires with post-2027 terms unresolved. Free cash flow converts less than half of net income, leaving a ~1% FCF yield against up to ~$22B of conditional cash calls. And the base case of $1,300 offers 13% upside against 23% downside to a bear case that requires only a de-rating to 20x on a modestly trimmed estimate — still a premium to the peer median.

**HOLD.** Own the position through the August 5 print; do not add at $1,148.84 with near-term implied volatility at 85.6%. The signal that would change this is a U.S. price line stabilizing better than −7% on an underlying basis, Foundayo scripts inflecting against oral semaglutide, or a price near $1,020 that restores the asymmetry.
