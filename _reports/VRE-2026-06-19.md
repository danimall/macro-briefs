**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Veris Residential common stock no longer trades as a going concern. On May 27, 2026, an investor consortium led by Affinius Capital (a San Antonio institutional real estate manager with roughly $61B AUM), in partnership with Vista Hill Partners (co-led by Jonathan Kushner of Kushner Real Estate Group), completed an all-cash take-private of Veris at $19.00 per share, implying an enterprise value near $3.5 billion including assumed debt. The stock has been delisted from the NYSE. The $18.99 reference price in the market data is the deal consideration with a trivial residual spread, not a live market quote.

There is no forward equity to own, no future earnings to discount, and no catalyst that would re-rate the shares above $19.00, because the shares have ceased to exist as public securities. The only residual considerations are litigation-driven: activist holder Erez Asset Management publicly argued for $22–25 per share before the deal, and at least one plaintiff firm (Kahn Swick & Foti) opened an April 2026 investigation into the adequacy of the sale price and process. Those claims are speculative, unquantified, and would accrue to holders of record at the merger, not to a new buyer at $18.99 today.

We rate VRE AVOID. This is not a judgment on the underlying business, which was a high-quality, supply-advantaged Northeast Class A multifamily portfolio executing a credible deleveraging plan. Buying the stock now means paying $18.99 to receive $19.00 in a completed transaction, with the only divergence from par being asymmetric downside if appraisal litigation impairs the consideration or stretches the timeline. There is no investable thesis remaining.

## Company Overview

Veris Residential is the former Mack-Cali Realty Corporation, which spent roughly five years (2020–2025) divesting billions of dollars of suburban office assets to transform into a pure-play Class A multifamily REIT, rebranding as Veris Residential. The "Office REITs" industry tag in the data feed is stale and incorrect; the portfolio at the time of sale was concentrated in residential assets across the New York City metro (primarily the Jersey City Waterfront and Port Imperial) plus Boston and New Jersey.

The operating thesis rested on supply-constrained, Manhattan-adjacent submarkets. NYC multifamily supply grew only about 6% from 2020 to 2024, roughly half the national pace, pushing demand into transit-linked Jersey City and Port Imperial, where Veris operated newer, larger, amenitized units. Management reported average revenue per home near $4,255 (a claimed >40% premium to peers), an affluent tenant base (average new-tenant household income above $480,000, roughly 55% of move-ins from out of state, only ~2% student exposure), and top-tier ESG credentials (GRESB score of 90).

That portfolio is now owned by the Affinius/Vista Hill consortium. The company's public market history ended at the May 27, 2026 close.

## Financial Analysis

The financials documented through 2025 describe a "shrink-to-grow" deleveraging strategy that was working but never reached completion before the sale crystallized embedded value.

**Revenue** declined as a function of asset sales, not organic weakness: $355.0M (2022) → $279.9M (2023) → $271.1M (2024) → $288.4M (2025). The top-line contraction reflected the deliberate sale of office and non-strategic multifamily assets to delever.

**GAAP net income** swung from losses (–$52.1M in 2022, –$107.3M in 2023, –$23.1M in 2024) to a positive $75.2M in 2025, driven largely by gains on asset dispositions rather than operating improvement. The accumulated deficit of –$1.42B reflects years of REIT depreciation and the legacy Mack-Cali office write-downs.

The operating metric that mattered, Core FFO, was guided up twice during 2025 to $0.67–$0.68 per share, roughly 12–13% growth over 2024's $0.60. Same-store revenue rose 2.2%, blended net rental growth was +3.9% in Q3 2025, occupancy held at 94.7% (95.8% ex-Liberty Towers under renovation), and retention improved roughly 570 bps to 61%.

The balance sheet carried the central tension. Net debt/EBITDA had been reduced about 15% YTD to 10x by Q3 2025, with management targeting roughly 9x after the Harborside 8/9 sale and below 8x by end-2026. Weighted-average coupon was 4.8% but weighted-average maturity was only 2.6 years, a meaningful refinancing overhang in an elevated-rate environment. Management sold or contracted $542M of non-strategic assets YTD at an average ~5.1% cap rate and explicitly prioritized deleveraging over a $100M buyback despite asserting the shares traded below NAV. That choice signaled the board viewed the public-market discount as persistent and structural.

The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over the trailing four quarters) and the broadly positive estimate revisions (+52.8% to current-year EPS over 90 days) are now historical artifacts. They describe a company that was improving into a sale; they do not describe a tradeable forward opportunity, because the equity has been retired.

## Growth Analysis

Any growth analysis is moot for an investor today. The portfolio's forward NOI, FFO trajectory, and lease-up economics now accrue to Affinius and Vista Hill, not to public shareholders. The deleveraging path to sub-8x net debt/EBITDA, the Core FFO compounding, and the supply-advantaged rent growth in Jersey City and Port Imperial are all private-company considerations.

For completeness, the pre-deal growth picture was constructive within a bifurcated multifamily market: nationally oversupplied and decelerating (national rent growth fell to roughly +0.3% YoY by September 2025) but with NYC-adjacent submarkets tight (NYC led the nation near +4.8% rent growth in September 2025). Veris sat on the favorable side of that divide, which is precisely what made it an attractive acquisition target at a public-market discount to NAV. That growth optionality has been transferred to the buyer.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is fully resolved at the deal price. The analyst price target band of $19.00 (low) to $22.00 (high) is a relic of pre-deal estimates; the high figure tracks the activist's argument rather than any independent valuation of a tradeable security.

The only way to characterize the remaining "upside" is through appraisal litigation: if a court or settlement were to award more than $19.00 to holders of record at the merger, those holders could realize incremental value. A buyer of the stock at $18.99 today does not capture that; the entitlement attaches to record holders, and the litigation is unresolved, unquantified, and could equally result in nothing. Against a fixed $19.00 cash payout, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside: roughly 0.05% of nominal spread to par, against the possibility of deal-related delays, escrow holdbacks, or impairment.

There is no cycle-adjusted earnings power, no forward multiple, and no NAV-to-price gap to exploit, because there is no forward equity. A low "score" here reflects the absence of any asymmetric opportunity, not a view that the business was overvalued at $19.00.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position of the underlying assets was genuinely strong: a supply-constrained, Manhattan-adjacent Class A portfolio with premium rents, high-income tenants, and a tight new-supply pipeline in the Jersey City Waterfront, which saw no meaningful deliveries since mid-2022. Counterweighting that, roughly 5,000 units were delivered in nearby submarkets in Q3 2025 (the highest in five years), and national asking rents posted their largest one-month drop since November 2022. The portfolio's quality is the reason an institutional buyer paid up.

For a public-equity investor, none of this is actionable. The competitive moat now benefits Affinius and Vista Hill. The score reflects asset quality as a historical fact, not a forward edge available to the market.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is no longer operational; it is transactional and legal. The deal has closed, so the principal residual risk is litigation over price and process. The Erez Asset Management push and the Kahn Swick & Foti investigation create some probability of incremental recovery for record holders but no upside for a buyer at $18.99 today, and they introduce timeline and escrow uncertainty.

A governance feature the plaintiff firms are probing: Veris fought off a 2023 takeover attempt from Kushner Companies amid public-disclosure accusations, yet the winning consortium included Vista Hill Partners, co-led by Jonathan Kushner. The board's choice to prioritize deleveraging over a buyback while acknowledging a sub-NAV stock price, and then to sell below the activist's stated range, is the substance under examination.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
There is no bull case for a buyer of the common stock today. The only constructive scenario is for holders of record at the merger: appraisal litigation or a settlement could award more than $19.00 per share, validating the activist's argument. That is a contingent, unquantified legal outcome that does not transfer to a market purchaser at $18.99.

### Bear Case
A buyer at $18.99 receives $19.00 in a completed transaction (a nominal 0.05% return) but bears the tail risk of deal-related delays, escrow holdbacks, or any complication that impairs the consideration before final distribution. The asymmetry is unfavorable: essentially no upside against a small but real possibility of downside or dead money while the litigation overhang clears.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is a completed take-private, not an active equity. There is no horizon over which a new public-market position appreciates, because the shares have been delisted and converted to a fixed $19.00 cash claim.

**Re-entry criteria**: None applicable in the conventional sense. The only event that would change the calculus is the emergence of a publicly traded surviving security (none exists) or a re-IPO of the platform by Affinius/Vista Hill at some future date, which would be a distinct new investment evaluated on its own terms. For record holders specifically (not new buyers), monitor the Kahn Swick & Foti investigation and any appraisal action for a potential incremental award above $19.00.

**Timeline**: The take-private closed May 27, 2026. Litigation timelines for appraisal/process claims are not investable via the open market.

## Conclusion

Veris Residential was a well-run, supply-advantaged Northeast Class A multifamily REIT that executed a disciplined deleveraging strategy, raised Core FFO guidance twice in 2025, and ultimately crystallized embedded value through an all-cash take-private at $19.00 per share. The fundamental story was sound. The investable conclusion is straightforward: the equity no longer exists as a going concern, the $18.99 quote is a frozen deal price, and a purchase today returns at most the trivial spread to par while carrying asymmetric downside from unresolved deal litigation and timeline risk.

We rate VRE **AVOID**. The recommendation reflects the absence of any forward opportunity for a public-market buyer, not a negative view of the underlying portfolio.
