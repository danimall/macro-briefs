## Executive Summary

Abercrombie & Fitch has completed a four-year turnaround (FY2022 operating income $92.6M → FY2024 $740.8M) and then spent FY2025 giving part of it back. Revenue grew 6.4% to $5,266.3M, but gross margin fell 267bps to 61.5%, operating margin fell 169bps to 13.3%, and on a like-for-like basis adjusted EPS declined 7.8% to $9.86 from $10.69. The GAAP optic (–2.2% to $10.46) is flattered by a $42.9M payment-card interchange litigation settlement that FY2024 did not have. Tariffs cost $90M (170bps of sales); brand mix shifted toward store-heavy Hollister; APAC ran a $27.6M operating loss on 3.0% of revenue.

Management's FY2026 guide — sales +3% to +5%, operating margin 12.0–12.5%, diluted EPS $10.20–$11.00 — is a share-count story. The midpoint of $10.60 on the guided ~44.0M average diluted shares implies ~$466M of net income against FY2025 adjusted net income of $478.0M, so earnings are guided down ~2.4% on ~4% revenue growth, with the tax rate up ~150bps to ~30%. EPS rises because 9.2% of the equity is being retired at roughly 10x earnings, funded from $619M of net cash and ~$378M of FY2025 free cash flow. That is legitimate per-share value creation, and it is the correct use of capital at this multiple, but it is not operating improvement and should not be scored as such.

The near-term setup governs the rating. The stock has risen 55.7% from its May 15 close of $70.57 and roughly 20% in the past month, into a Q2 print on August 26 for which the options market prices a ±13.9% move. Q1 company comps were –1%. Working from Q1 actual ($1.47) and the Q2 guide ($1.80–$2.00), H2 must deliver 66–70% of full-year EPS. The mechanism for that swing exists and is credible: FY2025's tariff cost was concentrated in H2, so lapping it should produce an H2 year-over-year margin tailwind on our reconciliation of the guided quarterly tariff assumptions. The risk is equally specific: a temporary 10% global tariff was implemented under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 through July 24, 2026, and the administration is reconstituting equivalent duties under other authorities. If H2 tariffs land at Q2-like levels instead of turning favorable, roughly $60M of pre-tax income and $0.70–$0.95 of EPS come out of the guide. We rate the shares HOLD at $109.91, with a $118 base case, and would want either the print or a return toward the low $90s before committing new capital.

## Company Overview

ANF operates two brand families across three reportable segments. Abercrombie brands (Abercrombie & Fitch, abercrombie kids, YPB) generated $2,523.7M of FY2025 net sales; Hollister brands (Hollister, Gilly Hicks) generated $2,742.6M, and Hollister overtook Abercrombie in absolute revenue for the first time in this cycle. The structurally important disclosure in Item 1 is that a majority of Abercrombie-brand sales are digital while a majority of Hollister-brand sales run through stores. The two brands therefore have different fixed-cost bases, different exposure to mall traffic, and different competitive substitution dynamics.

Footprint at May 2, 2026: 834 company-owned stores plus 62 franchised, all company stores leased with terms expiring FY2026–FY2038. At fiscal year-end (1/31/26) the company operated 829 company-owned stores (Hollister 523, Abercrombie 306) plus 60 franchised. Sourcing runs through ~124 vendors in 15 countries, with Vietnam ~37% and Cambodia ~26% of FY2025 cost of merchandise receipts, and substantially all inventory purchased in U.S. dollars. Geographic mix in Q1 FY2026: Americas $899.9M (+3% reported, +1% comp), EMEA $167.4M (–10%, –11% comp), APAC $46.5M (+24%, +15% comp). APAC is under a strategic review announced in March 2026 that remains unresolved.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year shape.** Revenue compounded 12.5% annually from FY2022 to FY2025 ($3,697.8M → $5,266.3M). Operating income peaked in FY2024 at $740.8M (14.97% margin) and fell to $699.1M (13.28%) in FY2025. Funded debt went to zero in FY2024 with the redemption of the 8.75% secured notes.

**The FY2025 decline, decomposed.** Tariffs took $90M / 170bps of operating margin, net of mitigation, the majority of the 169bps decline. Brand mix reversed (Abercrombie comps –7%, Hollister +13%), shifting sales toward the lower-AUR, store-heavy brand. Segment margins fell across the board: Americas 27.7% (–240bps), EMEA 11.2% (–310bps), APAC –17.5% (–950bps). G&A fell 140bps to 13.8% of sales, but 150bps of that came from lower employee compensation, a mechanical consequence of missing the incentive plan rather than durable efficiency.

**The GAAP/adjusted wedge matters.** FY2025 selling expense carried a $42.9M interchange-settlement benefit (80bps) against $4.3M of related legal fees, a net pre-tax excluded item of $38.6M. FY2024 had no non-GAAP adjustments. Adjusted operating income was $660.6M (12.54%), adjusted EPS $9.86, adjusted EBITDA $815.6M (15.5% margin versus 18.1%, –260bps). Any comparison of FY2026 guidance to FY2025 must specify the base; the two produce opposite conclusions about direction.

**Balance sheet.** At May 2, 2026: cash $594.1M plus marketable securities $25.1M, zero funded debt, $500M ABL undrawn ($450M net availability) to August 2029. Net cash +$619.2M, or 12.7% of market cap. Altman Z-Score 3.12.

The offset is leases, which the "zero debt" headline obscures. Operating lease liabilities grew from $1,025.9M (May 2025) to $1,292.5M (May 2026), +26% year over year; ROU assets grew 28.5% to $1,115.8M. Lease liabilities equal 1.58x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA. Enterprise value is $4.26B excluding leases and $5.55B including them. Variable lease cost of $192.2M in FY2025 sits outside the contractual-obligation table entirely.

That expansion is showing up in the P&L. Q1 FY2026 store occupancy within selling expense rose 12.7% to $140.2M on +2% sales; single operating lease cost rose 18.0% to $82.4M. Occupancy, payroll and controllables contributed +190bps of the +230bps selling-expense increase, the largest single driver of Q1 margin compression. The FY2026 plan calls for ~50 openings, 20 closures and 80 remodels/right-sizes, so this fixed-cost build continues while company comps are negative. The mitigating fact is that gross square footage grew only ~4% in FY2025 against 6.4% sales growth, so historically the program has been productivity-accretive; part of the ROU growth is renewals remeasured at current market rents rather than net new doors.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 OCF $619.1M, down $91.2M, driven by a $174.4M adverse swing in payables and accrued expenses. Capex $240.8M → FCF $378.4M (7.2% margin, 7.8% yield on current market cap). OCF/net income 1.20x, the healthiest single line in the package. Q1 FY2026 OCF was $44.3M versus –$4.0M; that figure should not be annualized, since Q1 absorbed roughly $159M of payables and accrued-expense outflow and the business earns most of its cash in the Fall season. On guided capex of ~$225M, FY2026 FCF is roughly $394M (8.1% yield) if OCF holds flat, or roughly $301M (6.2%) if OCF falls 15% on the guided margin compression.

**Q1 FY2026 P&L.** Net sales $1,113.8M, +2% reported and flat in constant currency (FX added $11.0M). COGS 37.2% (–80bps: freight –180bps, tariffs +180bps). Selling 38.7% (+230bps). G&A 16.4% (+50bps). Operating income $88.8M (8.0%) versus $101.5M (9.3%); constant-currency margin –180bps. EPS $1.47 versus $1.59, and $1.47 versus $1.70 on constant currency. Tax rate 27.6% versus 24.5%. EPS beat consensus of $1.29 by 14.1% and exceeded the company's own outlook, so the reported result was better than expected even as the underlying margin deteriorated. Inventory was well controlled at $532.7M, down 1.7% on +2% sales, which limits near-term markdown risk.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is intact and modest; earnings growth is not present.

The brand divergence inverted rather than resolved. Abercrombie comps went from –7% (FY2025) to 0% (Q1 FY2026); Hollister went from +13% to –2%, having decelerated through +19% reported (Q2 FY2025) and +6% reported (Q4 FY2025). Total company comps moved from +3% to –1%. The company has not demonstrated that it can run both brands positive simultaneously, and the FY2026 sales guide requires at least one to reaccelerate.

Part of Hollister's Q1 weakness is geographic rather than brand-health: the 10-Q attributes EMEA's –11% comp to lower third-party franchise and wholesale volume in the Middle East and other European markets as regional conflict conditions escalated, and management named Hollister as the brand most affected. ANF consolidates Emirati and Kuwaiti ventures with Majid al Futtaim as variable interest entities, so the exposure is direct. Because comps are disclosed by brand and by segment but not by brand-within-segment, the split between U.S. brand health and Middle East drag cannot be quantified from the filings.

**The H2 arithmetic is the growth question.** FY2026 implies H2 EPS of $6.73–$7.73 against a full-year $10.20–$11.00, or 66–70%. Combining Q1 actual with the Q2 guide midpoint, H1 FY2026 EPS is $3.37 versus $3.49 GAAP a year earlier. [unverified — FY2025 quarterly EPS by quarter was not retrieved in the sources, so a precise H1/H2 net-income growth decomposition and the FY2025 H2-share-of-year cannot be computed.]

The mechanism is tariff lapping. FY2025's cost was concentrated in H2 (Q2 FY2025 was only 40bps). Reconciling the guided FY2026 assumption of ~20bps net unfavorable against Q1's actual +180bps and Q2's guided +120bps implies H2 tariffs turn into a year-over-year tailwind that supplies most of the required improvement. Freight, which contributed –180bps to Q1 COGS, has no natural offset once it laps.

The growth case is coherent but narrow: it rests on a policy-driven cost lap, not on volume. Estimate revisions are consistent with that reading — current-year consensus of $10.73 is roughly unchanged over 90 days (+0.5% over 30 days), next year $11.88 is flat, and the current-quarter estimate was cut 19.8% over 90 days as consensus reset from $2.48 toward the guided $1.80–$2.00. There is no positive revision momentum that would signal an underestimated inflection; there is stability around a flat earnings base.

Peer medians in apparel retail sit at 14.8x P/E, but the dispersion is the point: BOOT 20.7x and VSCO 28.5x against AEO 11.2x, URBN 14.8x and BKE 10.5x. ANF prices with the value cohort (AEO, BKE) despite better operating margins, a stronger balance sheet and no dividend obligation. That discount is not obviously an error. Teen and young-adult apparel carries genuine fashion-cycle mortality risk, and ANF's own risk factors warn that competitive intensity "can cause the Company to engage in greater than expected promotional activity, which would result in pressure on average unit retail and profitability." A 10–11x multiple on a business with negative comps and flat earnings is a defensible clearing price, not a mispricing.

The identifiable off-guidance asset is the IEEPA refund. ANF has applied for approximately $100M of refunds, carried as a gain contingency with $0 recognized and explicitly excluded from FY2026 guidance. Gross, $100M is $2.25 per share and 2.1% of market cap; because a portion would reduce inventory rather than flow immediately through income, the P&L benefit would be spread across periods. Collectability is uncertain: the Court of International Trade clarified in July 2026 that litigation is required to recover duties on finally liquidated entries, the administration is reconstituting equivalent tariffs under other authorities, and there is political pressure on retailers to pass refunds to consumers. Risk-adjusted, we credit roughly $1.00–$1.60 per share.

Assembling a base case: 11x the consensus current-year $10.73 gives $118, and 10x the FY2027 consensus $11.88 gives $119. We use **$118**, or +7.4% from $109.91. Consensus mean and median targets are $117.55 and $115.00; the stock sits 4.6% below the median. The analyst-targets-as-floors logic does not apply cleanly here, because the condition it requires (rising estimates) is absent: current-year and next-year consensus are flat over 90 days.

The compression in risk/reward since our last review is arithmetic. The fundamental picture has not changed (no new print, guidance unchanged, revisions flat), but the price is up materially from the reference three weeks ago, which has consumed most of the distance to fair value. Valuation scores 5.0: absolutely cheap on multiple, yield and net cash, with the discount structurally justified for the cohort and no catalyst identified that would re-rate the multiple above ~11x while comps are negative.

## Competitive Landscape

Abercrombie's –7% FY2025 comp was a product and brand-cycle problem playing out primarily online, where substitution is a click away and where digitally-native brands and social commerce compete most directly. Its return to 0% in Q1 is stabilization, not recovery. Hollister's problem is different: 523 of 834 company doors, exposure to the mall traffic decline the 10-K acknowledges, and a Q1 setback concentrated in EMEA third-party volume.

ANF's stated basis of competition is product newness, brand voice and experience, which is differentiation without structural lock-in. There is no subscription base, no switching cost, and no scale advantage over American Eagle, which generates slightly higher revenue in most reporting periods. The company's own risk factors now name "AI-enabled or agentic shopping experiences" as a channel it must compete in; that is disclosure of a channel risk, with no partnership or counterparty identified.

Against those, ANF's five-year cumulative TSR of $423.19 versus a peer-group $241.22 documents real outperformance through the turnaround. But the FY2025 single-year reversal was sharp: the ANF index fell ~18% while peers rose ~23%. The trajectory is flat at best: total comps negative, occupancy growing several times faster than sales, one loss-making region under review, and no evidence yet of both brands working at once. Competitive position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**H2 tariff reversal (most quantifiable).** Guidance assumes 10% on U.S. imports in Q2 and 15% thereafter and depends on an H2 year-over-year tailwind from lapping FY2025's costs. If H2 tariffs run at Q2-like levels (+120bps) rather than turning favorable, that is a large swing on ~$3.1B of H2 sales: roughly $60M pre-tax, ~$42M after tax at 30%, or ~$0.95 of EPS. That alone takes the midpoint from $10.60 to about $9.65, below the guide low. With Vietnam at 37% and Cambodia at 26% of merchandise receipts, ANF's cost base sits in exactly the jurisdictions most exposed to reciprocal-rate changes.

**Back-half concentration.** 66–70% of FY EPS is unearned, and it requires a material H2 acceleration after an H1 in which EPS declined year over year.

**EMEA / Middle East.** The –11% Q1 comp was attributed to escalating regional conflict affecting third-party volume, with consolidated Emirati and Kuwaiti joint ventures directly exposed. The 10-Q describes conditions that escalated during the quarter.

**Consumer backdrop.** [unverified — no macro consumer or mortgage-rate data appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data.] Sector-level discretionary-spending softness is partly priced into the 10x multiple and caps the upside case for a multiple re-rating.

**Occupancy deleverage.** A 26% larger lease book against negative comps converts a timing issue into structural margin pressure if flat-to-negative comps persist.

**Tax leakage.** Switzerland accounts for $178.2M of the $184.8M valuation allowance, and non-benefited pre-tax losses widened from $53.8M (FY2024) to $74.9M (FY2025), with $24.9M in Q1 FY2026 alone (vs $10.0M in Q1 FY2025). The guided ~150bps tax-rate increase is a real EPS drag independent of operations.

**Other live items.** ERP: the merchandising system went live in March 2026 with additional phases and an HCM system ahead; Q1 showed no quantified impact and no ICFR change, so the initial disruption appears contained, but the risk is not retired. APAC strategic review unresolved since March. Michael Jeffries criminal and civil litigation with court-ordered mandatory advancement of defense costs, no estimable range above accruals. Short interest 11.1% with 3.6 days to cover.

### Options Market Signal

Risk factors score 4.2: multiple dated binaries, a quantified policy exposure that can remove $0.70–$0.95 of guided EPS, and an escalating regional drag that the guide does not appear to assume.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $148 (+34.7%)

The Q1 print (EPS $1.47 versus $1.29 consensus, above the company's own outlook) plus three beats in the last four quarters establishes that management guides conservatively, and the May 27 guide was set four weeks into the year. H2 tariff lapping supplies most of the required margin step-up mechanically. Abercrombie has stabilized at 0% comps and can inflect positive against easy comparisons, while Hollister's Q1 weakness is geographic rather than brand-level. The $100M refund claim is entirely outside guidance. The buyback retires 9.2% of the equity annually at ~10x earnings; Q1 repurchases averaged roughly $87.52 per share, well below the current price. On FY2027 consensus of $11.88 at 12.5x, the shares are worth $148, close to the $153 analyst high. Requires: Q2 in the upper half of guidance, FY reaffirmed, comps returning positive, and no re-imposition of duties.

### Bear Case — $80 (–27.2%)

H2 does not deliver the required acceleration. New tariff authorities land in the second half, removing $0.70–$0.95 of EPS and forcing a guidance cut. EMEA weakness broadens as regional conflict persists, and Hollister's –2% comp proves brand-level rather than geographic. Occupancy costs, growing 12.7% against 2% sales, deleverage further. The tax rate stays at 30%+ as Swiss losses widen. On $9.50–$10.00 of realized EPS at 8x (the low end of where the value cohort trades in a downcycle), the shares are worth $76–$80, and the May low of $70.57 becomes the reference. The 11.1% short base and a $450M buyback provide partial cushion, and $619M of net cash removes solvency risk from the equation.

### Weighing them

At 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear, the probability-weighted return is approximately +2%; at a more constructive 55/20/25 it is roughly +4%. Either is a fair, not compelling, expected return. The downside case is 3.7x the magnitude of the base-case upside. Business quality and cheapness do not compensate for that when the deciding event is 13 days away and positioning is one-sided.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Base case $118 (+7.4%); bull $148 (+34.7%); bear $80 (–27.2%). Horizon 12 months; the governing event is the August 26, 2026 Q2 print and the accompanying FY reaffirmation or cut.**

Base case math: 11x consensus current-year EPS of $10.73 = $118, cross-checked at 10x FY2027 consensus $11.88 = $119. The multiple is held flat because nothing in the current data supports re-rating above the value cohort while company comps are negative.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Q2 EPS at or above $2.00 with FY guidance reaffirmed **and** company comps returning to flat or positive.
2. Explicit disclosure that H2 tariff costs are contained at or below the guided assumption under whatever authority replaces Section 122.
3. A price near $92–95 (approximately 8.7x the guide midpoint) with fundamentals unchanged, which would restore roughly 25% upside to the $118 base case.
4. Recognition of any portion of the ~$100M IEEPA refund, or resolution of the APAC review in a way that removes the segment's operating loss.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following occurs:**
1. FY2026 EPS guidance is cut below $10.20, or Q2 EPS comes in below $1.80.
2. Company comps deteriorate below –3%, or Abercrombie-brand comps turn negative again.
3. New tariff authority is imposed at a rate that eliminates the assumed H2 year-over-year benefit.
4. Store occupancy cost growth exceeds 10% year over year for a second consecutive quarter on flat or negative comps.

**Position guidance:** existing holders can carry the position through the print. Net cash of 12.7% of market cap, an ~8% FCF yield and a buyback consuming 9.2% of the float annually are a genuine floor mechanism, and the tariff lap is a real mechanism for the H2 step-up. New capital should not be committed at $109.91 with a ±13.9% implied move 13 days out and call-heavy positioning. Wait for the print or for the low $90s.

**Next reassessment: August 26, 2026 (Q2 results and FY guidance).**

## Conclusion

This is our fifth consecutive HOLD on ANF, and the composite score is essentially unchanged at 5.18 versus 5.2 in July. That flat reading reflects a change in the setup rather than stasis. Three weeks ago the shares were around $96 with more upside to a similar base case; at $109.91 that upside is 7.4%, with the same unresolved binary now 13 days away. The fundamentals have not deteriorated (Q1 beat both consensus and the company's own outlook, guidance is intact, inventory is clean, revisions are flat), but the price has absorbed the improvement.

The analytical crux is the composition of guided EPS growth. On $10.60 midpoint against ~44.0M shares, implied net income of ~$466M is below FY2025 adjusted net income of $478M. Operating margin is guided flat-to-down 50bps versus the FY2025 adjusted 12.54%, and the tax rate rises ~150bps. Every dollar of guided per-share growth comes from retiring equity. That is the right decision at 10x with $619M of net cash, and it will compound value if the multiple holds. It also means the equity story degrades quickly if free cash flow compresses, because the buyback is the growth engine.

The decisive uncertainty is the second half, which must produce 66–70% of full-year EPS. The mechanism is credible, lapping FY2025 tariff cost that was concentrated in H2, and it is also the single item most exposed to a policy reversal that is actively in progress. Until August 26 resolves whether that lap materializes, the position is fair value with two-sided risk. HOLD.
