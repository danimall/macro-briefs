**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nordson is a diversified precision-technology industrial that engineers mission-critical dispensing, coating, fluid-management, and test/inspection systems embedded in customers' manufacturing lines. The business carries software-like economics for an industrial: FY2025 gross margin of 55.2%, operating margin of 25.5%, and a 23.7% free-cash-flow margin on roughly 2% capex intensity. The central development this cycle is that Nordson's multi-year organic-growth drought has reversed. After two consecutive years of -2.5% organic decline (FY2024 and FY2025), where all reported growth came from the ARAG and Atrion acquisitions, Q2 FY2026 delivered +6.6% organic growth with all three segments contributing and an 18% YoY increase in organic backlog. Backlog growth of that magnitude is an order-driven leading indicator, not a price/FX artifact, which raises confidence the inflection is durable.

The constraint on the rating is price, not business quality. At $295.92 the stock sits within ~3% of its 52-week high ($305.28) and trades at roughly 26x the midpoint of management's FY2026 adjusted EPS guide of $11.30–$11.80, and ~24x next-year consensus of $12.43. That is below the peer median (IEX 32.1x, FTV 35.2x) and above SNA (19.9x) and PNR (18.2x). The valuation reflects the recovery rather than discounting it. With estimate revisions positive but modest (+0.8% on next-year EPS over 90 days, well short of the +30% inflection threshold that would force a forward-earnings re-rate) and the stock already pricing the backlog turn, the asymmetry required for a BUY is absent.

We maintain a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The organic reacceleration is real and the order book supports it, but the entry point offers limited margin of safety against a 56%-of-assets goodwill base (thin MFS impairment cushion), elevated leverage (D/E 1.94x), and a peer-lagging total-shareholder-return record that the inflection must still prove out. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$250s or on a second quarter of confirmed all-segment organic growth with backlog conversion to revenue.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1954 and headquartered in Westlake, Ohio, Nordson manufactures differentiated systems for the precision dispensing, applying, and controlling of adhesives, coatings, polymers, sealants, biomaterials, and fluids, plus test/inspection and surface treatment. Roughly 67% of revenue is generated outside the U.S., with manufacturing across the U.S., China, Germany, Ireland, India, Israel, Italy, Mexico, the Netherlands, and the UK, and approximately 8,200 employees.

The business runs three reportable segments (FY2025 sales mix):

- **Industrial Precision Solutions (IPS), ~48%** — adhesive dispensing, industrial coatings, nonwovens (hygiene), packaging, polymer processing, and precision-agriculture spraying. Highest-margin segment (~37% EBITDA margin).
- **Medical and Fluid Solutions (MFS), ~30%** — engineered fluid components, medical interventional solutions (catheters, balloons, cannula), single-use components, and surgical solutions (~37% EBITDA margin). The priority area for acquisitions.
- **Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS), ~22%** — electronics dispense, semiconductor/PCB test & inspection, optical sensors (~22–27% EBITDA margin). The most cyclical segment, driven by electronics/semiconductor capex.

The moat rests on leadership in niche, mission-critical precision applications where Nordson equipment improves customer yield, line speed, and material efficiency, supported by a global direct sales/service network, a large installed base that drives recurring parts/consumables revenue, and 2,100+ patents. No single customer is ≥10% of sales. The portfolio mixes recession-resistant consumer non-durables (packaging, hygiene), secular-growth medical, and high-growth/high-volatility electronics, a deliberate balance reinforced by the ARAG (precision ag) and Atrion (medical) acquisitions.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is best-in-class for the industrial-machinery group, reflecting the high consumables/parts mix and pricing power in mission-critical applications.

Revenue tells a two-part story. From FY2022 to FY2025, sales rose from $2.59B to $2.79B, but the trajectory masked weak underlying performance: FY2024 and FY2025 both posted -2.5% organic declines, with growth entirely acquisition-driven (FY2025: +3.8% total, +6.0% from acquisitions, -2.5% organic). Within FY2025, IPS organic ran -5.1% (weak polymer processing/coatings), MFS -3.1% (distorted by the divested contract-manufacturing line; +1.0% excluding it), and ATS +4.1% (early electronics recovery).

Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) marks the turn. Sales reached $740.8M (+8.5%), with all three segments positive. ATS organic was exceptionally strong (+8.5% in Q2, +13.8% in H1) on electronics dispense demand tied to the advanced-packaging/AI capex cycle. H1 FY2026 net income was $250.7M (+21%) and diluted EPS $4.47 (+23.5%).

Cash generation is high quality. FY2025 operating cash flow was $719.2M against just $58M of capex, yielding $661M of FCF; OCF consistently exceeds net income. H1 FY2026 OCF was $321M (+15%).

The balance sheet carries elevated but actively de-risking leverage. Long-term debt fell to $1.68B at FY2025 from $2.10B in FY2024 as the company prioritized paydown, with total debt $1.90B at Q2 FY2026. The leverage stems from the ~$800M Atrion acquisition (closed FY2024) and ARAG, which lifted goodwill from $1.8B (FY2022) to $3.3B (FY2025). The Altman Z-Score of 2.0 ("grey zone") is mechanically driven by that goodwill weight rather than distress; with 55% gross margins, strong coverage, current ratio 1.64, and full covenant compliance, the business is financially robust. Liquidity is ample, with ~$1.05B available under the new (Jan 2026) $1.2B revolver maturing 2031.

Capital allocation pivoted toward shareholder returns in FY2025: $306M of buybacks (vs. $33M in FY2024) plus $179M of dividends, while paying down $224M of debt. An August 2025 $500M buyback authorization left ~$599M available as of April 2026. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.82 from $0.78. H1 FY2026 saw ~$107M of debt repayment and $129M of repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case turns entirely on whether the Q2 FY2026 organic inflection is sustainable. Backlog of that size is the most reliable forward signal in this portfolio because Nordson's systems are long-lead, configured-to-order equipment; orders booked now convert to revenue over the following two to four quarters.

The cyclical driver is ATS electronics dispense and test/inspection, which swung from -11.2% organic (FY2024) to +13.8% (H1 FY2026), tracking the broader semiconductor/electronics capex recovery and advanced-packaging investment benefiting test/inspection and dispense suppliers. The recurring consumables/parts base across the installed equipment fleet provides a more stable annuity layer beneath the equipment cycle.

The medical (MFS) and precision-ag (ARAG) acquisitions were structured to dampen electronics cyclicality with higher-secular-growth, lower-volatility end markets. MFS noted near-term product start-up headwinds in H1 FY2026 that bear watching, but the segment's exit from low-margin contract manufacturing improves the mix.

Two cautions temper the growth score. First, the secular base rate is modest: management's guide implies high-single-digit EPS growth on the recovery, and consensus next-year EPS of $12.43 is only ~8% above the current-year midpoint. Second, estimate revisions, while positive, are small — +0.8% on next-year EPS over 90 days and +0.1% on current-year. This is steady positive momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a trough-to-recovery re-rating. The company is recovering toward trend, not inflecting onto a new growth curve.

## Valuation Assessment

At $295.92 the stock trades at ~26x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $11.55 and ~24x next-year consensus of $12.43. Against the peer set, that places Nordson below IEX (32.1x) and FTV (35.2x), above SNA (19.9x) and PNR (18.2x), and below the peer median of 32.1x. On an EV basis the leverage adds modestly to the multiple relative to the lower-debt peers.

The analyst consensus range is $260 low to $345 high, mean $319.13, median $331.50 — the current price sits below the mean, which offers some support but not a clear mispricing. The 1.1% dividend yield provides no meaningful absolute-yield floor.

The recovery in earnings is genuine, but it does not qualify for cycle-adjusted/forward re-rating treatment: revisions are well short of +30%, revenue growth is high-single-digit rather than >40%, and the stock is not at a cyclical trough — it sits within 3% of its 52-week high. On history, a moderate forward multiple on a steady industrial is not, by itself, a reason to expect outperformance; the market has fairly priced the recovery. We do not treat the consensus mean of $319 as a floor here because the growth profile is steady rather than accelerating off a depressed base, so the targets are not lagging reality in the way they would on a true inflection name.

The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a fairly-priced, quality industrial with limited asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Nordson holds defensible leadership across fragmented, niche precision-application markets. The combination of application engineering depth, a global direct sales-and-service footprint that competitors struggle to replicate, a large installed base generating recurring parts/consumables, and 2,100+ patents sustains pricing power evidenced by 55% gross margins. Diversification across consumer non-durables, medical, and electronics provides genuine portfolio balance.

The FY2024–25 organic decline coincided with electronics destocking and soft polymer-processing/coatings demand, and it produced a clear competitive tell: cumulative TSR of 127.4 versus a peer group at 207.3, a meaningful lag. That underperformance reflects the organic drought rather than share loss. The Q2 FY2026 reacceleration, led by ATS, suggests the position is stable-to-improving as the cycle recovers, but the peer-lagging TSR caps how strongly we can score competitive trajectory. We score this 6.8: a wide-moat franchise with a stable position and a credible recovery, but not yet demonstrating share gains that would justify a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/intangible impairment concentration.** Goodwill is 56% of total assets. FY2025 impairment tests passed with cushions of 311% (IPS), 132% (MFS), and 205% (ATS) on a ~9.5% WACC. The MFS cushion of 132% — home to the recently acquired Atrion — is the thinnest, and management flagged near-term MFS product start-up headwinds. A sustained downturn or Atrion underperformance is where a material non-cash charge would land.

**M&A dependence.** Growth was negative organically for two years and depended on acquisitions; the model requires continuous successful capital deployment. Atrion integration is an explicit forward risk.

**Tariffs/trade.** With ~67% of revenue ex-U.S. and meaningful China-sourced inputs, the FY2025 10-K expanded tariff/trade language, warning of potential cash-flow and liquidity impact from escalation and retaliation.

**Cyclicality.** ATS is tied to the volatile electronics/semiconductor capex cycle (currently a tailwind but reversed sharply in FY2024); polymer processing and coatings within IPS are also cyclical.

**FX and pension.** Material euro/yen/sterling/yuan exposure managed with ~$920M notional hedges. The Q2 FY2026 $104M pension annuitization (RGA) carried a one-time $24M charge but de-risks future earnings volatility, a quality improvement.

**Governance/leadership.** The CFO seat transitioned from Joseph Kelley to Dan Hopgood, consistent with Kelley's Feb 2026 10b5-1 plan termination and ~$10M+ option monetization. Multiple Item 5.02 officer/director changes appeared through 2025. The CEO seat (Nagarajan) is stable, mitigating succession concern, but the turnover warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 25.9% versus 21.0% one-year historical vol — modestly rich (4.8pp spread) but in the 10th percentile of the current chain, indicating low absolute option demand. The term structure is in contango (25.9% 1m vs 29.6% 3m), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew of 3.1% on the front month reflects mild put demand, but the put/call volume ratio of 0.06 and OI ratio near 0.00 indicate negligible defensive positioning. IV fell 7.1pp (33.0% → 25.9%) as the stock rose 8.3% since the May earnings print, consistent with resolved event risk rather than building concern. The implied move into the next expiry is ±5.6%. The options market is not flagging a near-term catalyst and is broadly aligned with a benign, fully-priced view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($345).** The organic inflection proves durable: backlog converts to sustained mid-single-digit-plus organic growth, ATS electronics demand compounds on advanced-packaging/AI capex, MFS start-up headwinds clear and Atrion delivers, and continued deleveraging plus buyback capacity supports EPS toward and through the $11.80 high end, with next-year EPS pushing past $12.43. At ~28x a rising forward number the stock reaches the Street high of $345 (+17%).

**Bear case ($240–250).** The electronics recovery stalls or destocks again (the ATS swing from -11% to +14% shows how fast this reverses), polymer/coatings demand stays soft, tariffs compress margins or cash flow, and MFS underperformance erodes the thin goodwill cushion toward an impairment. EPS slips below the $11.30 guide floor and the multiple compresses toward SNA/PNR levels (~19–20x), taking the stock to $240–250 (-16% to -19%).

**Base case ($310).** Organic growth holds in the mid-single digits, FY2026 EPS lands near the $11.55 midpoint and next-year reaches ~$12.40, the multiple holds around 25x, and the stock works toward ~$310 (+5%) over twelve months, supported by buybacks and dividend growth. The risk/reward from $295.92 is roughly balanced — limited upside to base, comparable downside to bear — which is the core reason this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month growth-oriented HOLD. The thesis hinges on backlog conversion and continued all-segment organic growth.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$250s (≈21x forward EPS), restoring a margin of safety against the goodwill/leverage profile; or
- A second consecutive quarter (Q3 FY2026, reported ~Aug/Sep 2026) confirming all-segment organic growth ≥5% with backlog sustained or rising and converting to revenue; or
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +5% over 90 days, signaling the recovery is broadening beyond the current steady trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ATS organic growth turning negative again or backlog rolling over (>5% sequential decline in organic backlog), signaling the electronics recovery has stalled;
- Any MFS goodwill impairment charge or a downward revision to the FY2026 EPS guide below $11.30;
- Leverage rising on a renewed large debt-funded acquisition before Atrion is demonstrably integrated.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q3 FY2026 earnings (~late summer 2026), which tests backlog conversion and the durability of the organic turn.

## Conclusion

Nordson is a high-quality, wide-moat precision-technology industrial whose multi-year organic-growth drought has inflected. The franchise economics are elite, and management is sensibly deleveraging while returning cash.

The rating is constrained by price. The stock prices in the recovery rather than discounting it, leaving balanced risk/reward (~+5% to base, comparable downside to bear). The goodwill base with a thin MFS cushion, elevated leverage, and a peer-lagging TSR record are real overhangs that the inflection must still prove out across more than one quarter. The score trajectory has drifted lower over three prior HOLD reports (6.5 → 6.2 → 6.0), and while the new organic-growth data argues against further deterioration in the growth and competitive dimensions, it does not by itself justify an upgrade at this price. We maintain HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$250s or on a confirmed second quarter of backlog-driven organic growth.
