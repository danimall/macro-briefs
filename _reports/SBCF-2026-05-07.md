## Executive Summary

Seacoast Banking Corporation is a $20.8B-asset Florida-focused community bank executing a franchise transformation. Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $0.62 (5.4% beat), NIM expanded to 3.83% (+17bps QoQ), and adjusted ROTE reached 16.3%, all ahead of schedule. The Villages Bancorporation acquisition, closed October 2025, adds a 10-year exclusive agreement with one of America's fastest-growing retirement communities, providing a captive affluent deposit/wealth management pipeline that is genuinely difficult to replicate.

The challenge is valuation. At $31.36, SBCF trades at 21x trailing EPS and approximately 12.5x guided 2026 EPS of $2.48-$2.52, a meaningful premium to regional bank peers trading at 16.1x. The premium is partially justified by superior growth (35% EPS growth guided for 2026 vs. flat-to-declining peers), but three specific concerns prevent an outright BUY: (1) estimates are currently declining (-2.7% current quarter, -3.0% next quarter over 30 days), (2) the VBI system conversion in early Q3 2026 remains an execution hurdle, and (3) 11.25M shares of convertible preferred represent ~10% latent dilution not visible in the basic share count. The stock is correctly held for its growth trajectory but needs either a valuation pullback or conversion execution proof to warrant upgrade.

## Company Overview

SBCF operates 104 full-service branches primarily across Florida through its subsidiary Seacoast National Bank (chartered 1926). The company completed two transformative acquisitions in 2025: Villages Bancorporation ($710.8M, adding $3.5B deposits and a 10-year exclusive agreement with The Villages® community) and Heartland ($153M in loans, $705M deposits). Total assets grew from $15.2B at end-2024 to $20.8B at end-2025, a 37% expansion.

The business model combines commercial/consumer banking, wealth management ($2.8B AUM, +37% YoY), mortgage services, and insurance. The strategy centers on hiring experienced relationship bankers from larger institutions to serve Florida's small/mid-market commercial segment, supplemented by acquisition-driven scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory is genuinely improving:**
- Q1 2026 adjusted EPS: $0.62 (+111% YoY, +42% sequentially)
- Full-year 2026 guidance: $2.48-$2.52 adjusted EPS (~35% growth over $1.84 in 2025)
- Adjusted ROA: 1.31% (Q1 2026) vs. 0.89% (FY 2024) — a 42bp improvement in 15 months
- Adjusted efficiency ratio: 53-55% target (vs. 58.1% in 2025, 63.8% in 2024)

**NIM expansion is the primary driver:** 3.83% in Q1 2026, up from 3.24% at the 2024 trough. Deposit costs fell to 1.54% (-13bps QoQ). Management's January securities repositioning ($277M sold at 1.9%, reinvested at 4.8%) adds ~$8M annualized NII. However, management explicitly cautioned that without Fed rate cuts, "deposit costs could stabilize or increase potentially later this year."

**Balance sheet metrics are solid for a bank of this size:**
- CET1: 11.54% (well above 8% well-capitalized threshold)
- Tangible equity/tangible assets: 9.31%
- Loan-to-deposit ratio: 75% (provides significant lending capacity without deposit pressure)
- Net charge-offs: 0.11-0.12% (excellent credit quality)
- ACL/Loans: 1.42% plus $138M remaining purchase accounting marks

**Capital deployment:** 317K shares repurchased in Q1 2026. Dividend yield ~2.5%. Combined with growth investment, capital allocation is reasonable given integration demands.

**Key concern — goodwill:** $1.035B in goodwill represents approximately 50% of tangible equity. This is typical for serial acquirers but creates vulnerability to impairment in an economic downturn.

## Growth Analysis

SBCF's growth story has three legs:

**1. Acquisition integration and cost saves (near-term).** VBI cost saves (26-27% target) are back-half loaded with full conversion expected early Q3 2026. A successful conversion should drive Q3-Q4 expense reductions that push toward the $2.50+ EPS target.

**2. Organic commercial banking (medium-term).** Commercial pipeline exceeded $1B in Q1 2026, production up 35% YoY. The high single-digit organic loan growth target is supported by a loan-to-deposit ratio of 75% and continued Florida in-migration demographics.

**3. Wealth management and The Villages® franchise (long-term).** Wealth AUM grew 37% YoY to $2.8B, with 36% revenue growth in Q1 2026. The exclusive 10-year agreement with The Villages® provides a captive pipeline of affluent retirees — a natural wealth management, mortgage, and deposit-gathering engine. This is genuinely differentiated competitive positioning that will compound over the agreement's remaining term.

**Guidance for 2026:** Revenue growth 29-31% (adjusted, FTE), adjusted ROA target 1.30%+ by Q4, ROTE target 16%+. These are achievable given Q1's 1.31% ROA starting point, though dependent on stable credit and successful VBI conversion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.36, SBCF trades at:
- 21.1x trailing EPS ($1.57 reported)
- ~12.5x 2026E adjusted EPS ($2.50 midpoint)
- ~11.5x 2027E EPS ($2.72 consensus)
- 1.5x tangible book value (estimated ~$21/share)

**Peer comparison:** Regional bank peers of similar market cap trade at a median P/E of 16.1x with a median dividend yield of 3.5%. SBCF's 21x trailing P/E represents a 31% premium. However, on forward estimates — 12.5x the $2.50 EPS target — the stock looks more reasonable given peers are growing earnings at low single-digit rates while SBCF is delivering 35% growth.

The correct framing: SBCF's forward multiple is reasonable for its growth rate (PEG ~0.35 on 2025-2026 growth), but the trailing multiple overstates the cheapness because trailing EPS reflects a partial year without full VBI contribution. The stock is fairly valued at current levels given: (a) 35% EPS growth is largely acquisition-driven and one-time, (b) underlying organic growth is high single digits (more typical of a 12-14x multiple), and (c) 11.25M convertible preferred shares (~10% dilution) reduce intrinsic value per share.

**Analyst targets:** Low $32.50, high $38.00, median $34.50. The stock at $31.36 sits 10% below consensus median and 4% below the lowest target, suggesting modest upside is expected but not urgent.

**Why the 31% peer premium is partially but not fully justified:** SBCF delivers superior growth (35% vs. low single digits) and superior profitability trajectory (ROTE 16%+ vs. peer 10-12%). A 50-75% premium to peer P/E on forward earnings would be reasonable for a bank executing this transformation. At 12.5x forward (vs. peer ~14-15x forward adjusted for those with positive growth), the premium on forward earnings is actually quite modest. The issue is whether $2.50 is sustainable — if NIM normalizes or credit turns, forward estimates compress.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- The Villages® exclusive agreement provides a moat that no competitor can replicate for the agreement's remaining 9+ years
- Granular loan book (average $435K) diversifies credit risk relative to larger banks with concentrated exposures
- 85% employee engagement (16 points above peer average) supports relationship banking model
- CRE concentration at 227% of risk-based capital (well below 300% regulatory threshold) provides growth runway

**Challenges:**
- Florida is intensely competitive (JPM, BAC, WFC all have significant presence)
- The company competes against both large banks (on rates and technology) and smaller community banks (on relationships)
- The GENIUS Act and stablecoin legislation represent a longer-term digital disruption risk, though immaterial near-term for this customer demographic

**Market position:** SBCF has assembled the scale (104 branches, $20.8B assets) to compete effectively in Florida's mid-market. The strategy of hiring experienced bankers away from large regionals to service clients who want relationship banking with institutional capability is working — evidenced by 35% YoY production growth in Q1 2026.

## Risk Assessment

**1. VBI Integration Execution (Moderate-High).** System conversion expected early Q3 2026. Management is "heads down focused on a flawless conversion." Bank technology conversions regularly produce customer attrition and operational disruptions. The 26-27% cost savings target is still being realized. This is the single largest near-term risk.

**2. Florida Geographic and CRE Concentration (Moderate).** 50% of loans are CRE-secured, 100% Florida-concentrated (minimal Georgia). Florida experiences more pronounced cyclical fluctuations than national averages. While current asset quality is excellent (NCOs 0.11%), a Florida real estate correction would disproportionately impact SBCF relative to nationally diversified peers.

**3. Latent Preferred Dilution (Moderate).** 11.25M convertible preferred shares (~10% of current basic count) convert to common upon transfer to a non-affiliate. This is a structural overhang that depresses per-share value relative to reported metrics.

**4. Rate Sensitivity and NIM Risk (Moderate).** Management explicitly warned that without Fed rate cuts, deposit costs could increase H2 2026. Current guidance assumes only 2 rate cuts (June and September 2026). NIM could plateau or compress from 3.83% if rates remain elevated longer than the 2-cut assumption.

**5. Negative Estimate Revisions (Low-Moderate).** Current quarter estimates declining 2.7% over 30 days; next quarter -3.0%. While modest, this represents negative momentum that typically constrains multiple expansion. If estimates continue declining, the peer premium compresses.

**6. Hurricane/Climate Risk (Low but Tail).** Florida-specific natural disaster exposure affecting collateral values and borrower capacity. Rising property insurance costs are a slow-burn headwind for Florida real estate values.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36-38, +15-21%):** VBI system conversion in Q3 executes flawlessly. Cost saves come in at/above 26-27% target, pushing Q4 run-rate EPS toward $0.65-0.68. Wealth management from The Villages accelerates (>$5M quarterly AUM adds). Full-year 2026 EPS comes in at $2.55-2.60, above high-end guidance. Multiple expands to 14-15x forward on demonstrated execution, putting the stock at $36-38. Fed delivers 2+ cuts, supporting continued NIM expansion.

**Bear Case ($26-28, -10-17%):** VBI conversion produces customer attrition and operational disruption. Rate cuts delayed, deposit costs rise, NIM plateaus at 3.7-3.8%. Florida CRE weakens on rising insurance costs and economic slowdown. Estimates cut to $2.30-2.40. Multiple compresses to 11-12x as growth premium evaporates. Convertible preferred creates selling pressure if holders seek liquidity.

**Base Case ($33-35, +5-12%):** Integration proceeds adequately but without meaningful upside surprise. Full-year EPS lands at $2.48-2.52 as guided. Stock re-rates modestly to consensus target range as forward estimates stabilize. NIM holds at 3.75-3.83%. Multiple settles at 13-13.5x forward 2027 EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback to $28-29 (creating >15% upside to base case at reasonable multiple)
- Successful VBI system conversion (early Q3 2026) with deposit retention >95%
- Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate revisions
- Full-year 2026 EPS tracking above $2.55 at Q2 report

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- VBI conversion results in >5% deposit outflows or material operational disruption
- NIM contracts below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters
- Net charge-offs exceed 0.30% (implying credit deterioration)
- Estimates cut below $2.30 for 2026

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will show the final pre-conversion quarter, and Q3 2026 earnings (late October), which will be the first post-conversion report. The Q3 report is the definitive catalyst.

## Conclusion

SBCF is executing well on a clear franchise transformation. Q1 2026 results confirm the earnings trajectory, and The Villages® franchise represents genuine competitive differentiation in wealth management and deposit gathering. At 12.5x guided 2026 EPS, the stock is not expensive for a bank growing earnings 35%. However, the premium to peers on trailing metrics, negative estimate revision trends, VBI conversion risk, and 10% latent dilution from convertible preferred collectively argue against paying up at current levels. Estimate revisions are declining (-2.7% to -3.0% over 30 days across current and next quarter), and the single largest identifiable catalyst (VBI system conversion) is still 2-3 months away. A confirmed successful conversion with deposit retention data is the falsifiable event that would justify upgrading. Until then, hold for the growth trajectory and reassess at Q3 earnings.
