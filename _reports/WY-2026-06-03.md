**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Weyerhaeuser is one of the largest private timberland owners in the world (~10.4M acres of U.S. timberland, plus ~14.1M acres of managed Canadian licenses) operating as a REIT since 2010, with a vertically integrated wood products business and a high-margin land/royalty/carbon segment. The investment question concerns whether the housing/lumber down-cycle is bottoming and whether the tariff regime reshaping North American softwood supply works in WY's favor, rather than trailing earnings, which are at a cyclical trough and distorted by one-time gains. The answer to both is a qualified yes, which supports a HOLD with a growth-recovery rationale rather than an AVOID.

The cyclical evidence is constructive but not yet decisive. Q1 2026 produced a +124% EPS beat ($0.11 adjusted vs. $0.05 expected), the fourth consecutive beat, and current-quarter consensus has been revised up +84% over 30 days and +49% over 90 days. Current-year EPS estimates have risen +12.5% over 90 days. The Section 232 and AD/CVD duty regime is pushing the effective tariff on Canadian softwood toward ~35%, reducing Canada's U.S. market share to levels "not seen in half a century" per the U.S. Lumber Coalition. Because WY's production is U.S.-heavy, the reduction in import competition is a structural tailwind for its core lumber franchise that outweighs the headwind on its smaller Canadian mills and Asian export logs.

The offsetting concerns keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Reported 2025 EPS of $0.45 falls to roughly $0.20 ex-special items, with ~$0.36/share of GAAP earnings coming from finite, lumpy timberland-sale gains. The 2025 dividend of $606M was not covered by Adjusted FAD of $397M, funded instead from land-sale proceeds and balance-sheet capacity, so the supplemental dividend should be expected to stay suppressed until the cycle turns. Operating cash flow fell 44% in 2025 to $562M. The options market is pricing imminent stress (near-term IV of 54.7% vs. 25.8% realized, in backwardation, with elevated put skew). The stock is fairly valued on mid-cycle earnings power, not asymmetrically mispriced.

## Company Overview

Weyerhaeuser operates three segments:

- **Timberlands** (~$2.1B segment sales, $581M Adjusted EBITDA in 2025): Grows and harvests timber across the West (2.5M acres of premium Douglas-fir with Asian export exposure), the South (6.7M acres of southern yellow pine), and the North (1.2M acres of hardwoods). Standing inventory is ~594M tons.
- **Wood Products** (~$5.0B sales, $250M Adjusted EBITDA in 2025): 33 facilities producing structural lumber, OSB, engineered wood products, plywood, and MDF. This is the most cyclical, commodity-exposed segment and a price-taker.
- **Strategic Land Solutions** (formerly Real Estate, Energy & Natural Resources; $454M sales, $411M Adjusted EBITDA in 2025): Higher-and-better-use land sales, mineral/energy royalties, and a growing Climate Solutions business (forest carbon credits, carbon capture/sequestration leasing, mitigation banking, renewable-energy ground leases). The renaming, effective Q1 2026, signals management's intent to lean into the highest-margin, lowest-capital part of the portfolio.

Competitive advantages include the scale and geographic diversity of the timberland base, vertical integration (internal logs feed mills), West Coast deep-water port access for Asian exports, 100%-certified lands, and the "AVO 2.0" data analytics process used to extract premium land value.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant theme is trough earnings in a multi-year housing/lumber down-cycle.

| Metric | 2022 (peak) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10.18B | $7.67B | $7.12B | $6.91B |
| Net income | $1.88B | $839M | $396M | $324M |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | — | $1.29B | $1.02B |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $1.01B | $562M |
| Diluted EPS | $2.53 | $1.15 | $0.54 | $0.45 |

Earnings quality is weak at the trough. 2025 net income of $324M includes $266M of pre-tax timberland-sale gains plus a $29M lumber-mill sale gain, offset by a $145M non-cash pension settlement charge. Net earnings before special items were only $143M ($0.20/share), down from $384M ($0.53) in 2024. The Timberlands segment's reported jump in earnings contribution was almost entirely land-sale gains, not operating improvement.

Wood Products operating income fell 88% YoY to $55M as OSB realizations dropped 25% and lumber/OSB prices reached multi-decade lows on an inflation-adjusted basis (Q4 2025: lumber ~$378/MBF, OSB ~$234/MSF). This is the segment most exposed to a cyclical recovery.

Cash flow and dividend coverage are strained at the bottom of the cycle. FCF was $139M. Adjusted FAD was $397M against dividends paid of $606M, funded with land-sale proceeds and balance-sheet capacity. WY's framework targets returning 75–80% of Adjusted FAD, and the variable/supplemental component is explicitly designed to flex down in weak years. The base dividend looks defensible; the supplemental should be expected to stay suppressed until lumber and OSB pricing recover.

The balance sheet is investment-grade but levered for a timber REIT: long-term debt $5.57B, net debt ~$5.1B, D/E ~0.76, weighted-average rate 5.11% with a 6.5-year maturity, ratings BBB/Baa2. Debt-to-total-capital is 36.4% versus a 65% covenant limit, leaving ample headroom. Liquidity is strong at $3.5B (a $1.75B undrawn revolver to 2030 plus a new $1.75B commercial-paper program). The Altman Z-Score of 2.02 ("grey zone") is driven by near-zero EBIT/assets at the trough, not solvency stress.

Capital allocation: a $1B buyback authorization was renewed in May 2025 ($938M remaining), but only $160M was repurchased in 2025, and a $200M voluntary pension contribution was made. The company is building a ~$500M TimberStrand engineered-wood facility in Monticello, AR (start-up 2027, +10M cubic feet of capacity), with ~$300M of related 2026 capex. Capital is rotating toward higher-margin, U.S.-based, tariff-advantaged assets.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a cyclical earnings recovery plus a structural land/carbon optionality layer.

The estimate-revision data is the clearest forward signal. Current-quarter consensus has been revised to $0.11 from $0.06 a month ago and from $0.07 ninety days ago. Current-year EPS is up to $0.32 from $0.29 ninety days ago. The track record supports confidence in these estimates: four consecutive beats, with the last two quarters beating by 124% and 187%. One caveat keeps this from being a clean recovery signal: the next-quarter estimate has been revised down (-31% over 30 days, -46% over 90 days), which is consistent with the lumpy timing of land-sale gains rather than a clean operating ramp. The full-year revision trend is positive; the quarter-to-quarter path is noisy.

The Section 232/AD-CVD tariff regime is the most important structural development. Combined Canadian softwood duties plus a separate 10% tariff keep the effective rate near 35%, and the U.S. Lumber Coalition reports Canada's U.S. market share falling to levels not seen in half a century, with BC producers being "decimated." Because the bulk of WY's production is in the U.S. South and West, reduced import competition is a net tailwind for its largest earnings driver. The flip side is real but smaller: WY's own Canadian mills pay export duties, and retaliatory tariffs plus China/Japan housing weakness (Japan starts -7.4% YTD) pressure the high-margin Western export franchise. On WY's U.S.-heavy mix, the producer tailwind likely outweighs the Canadian-mill headwind.

Strategic Land Solutions is the genuine non-commodity growth optionality. The segment runs a >90% EBITDA-margin business on land already owned, and management targets ~$425M Adjusted EBITDA for 2026. Climate Solutions (carbon credits, sequestration leasing, mitigation banking, renewable ground leases) is the highest-margin, lowest-capital part of the portfolio and provides a value stream less correlated to housing starts.

The constraint on near-term growth is demand. With mortgage rates near 6.2% and soft consumer confidence, U.S. housing starts and R&R activity remain weak. The recovery thesis is contingent on either a rate-driven housing recovery or continued tariff-driven margin capture in U.S. lumber. Management has cited "improving lumber momentum," which is encouraging but not yet a confirmed turn in volumes.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~44x is meaningless at a cyclical trough and on earnings flattered by one-time gains. The relevant frameworks are mid-cycle earnings power and net asset value.

On peer multiples, WY trades roughly in line with the Real Estate cohort (peer-median P/E 30.7, dividend yields 3.5–5.0%), but the comparison is loose because none of these peers are timber REITs with the same commodity cyclicality. WY's 3.4% yield sits below the peer median of 4.1%, reflecting the trough payout and the uncovered supplemental.

The asset-based case is the more relevant anchor. A $17.7B market capitalization against ~$143M of normalized 2025 net earnings implies the market is valuing the timberland base and mid-cycle earnings power, not trough EPS, which is the correct way to value a timber REIT. Next-year consensus EPS of $0.70 (essentially flat over 90 days) implies a forward multiple of ~35x on a partial-recovery year, which is not cheap. A fuller mid-cycle recovery toward the 2023–2024 earnings range ($1.15 / $0.54) would compress the implied multiple materially, but that recovery is not yet visible in volumes.

This is a "buy the trough" timber-REIT setup where price-to-NAV and mid-cycle earnings dominate, and on those metrics the stock is fairly valued rather than asymmetrically cheap. The stock sits ~12% below its 52-week high of $27.86 and well above its low of $21.16, a moderate drawdown that does not by itself signal mispricing. I do not treat the consensus analyst targets (mean $31.18, median $30, range $27–38) as confirmed upside; on a stock at ~35x forward EPS, those targets reflect the same cycle-recovery extrapolation embedded in the price and are not independent confirmation. The valuation score reflects a fair price with genuine asset-value downside support but limited asymmetry from here.

## Competitive Landscape

WY's competitive position is structurally strong and improving at the margin. The scale and quality of its timberland base, vertical integration, and West Coast port access are durable advantages in a fragmented, price-driven commodity market. The most important industry variable, Canadian competition, is moving decisively in WY's favor. The tariff regime is shifting U.S. market share away from Canadian producers toward domestic producers, and WY's portfolio reshaping (selling the higher-cost Princeton, BC sawmill in Q3 2025 for a $29M gain, investing in U.S. engineered-wood capacity) aligns capital with the tariff-advantaged side of the market.

The competitive risks are substitution (plastics/composites versus wood products) and the absence of pricing power in commodity lumber and OSB, where WY is a price-taker regardless of cost position. The export franchise faces structural softness in Japan and policy volatility in China, where a March 2025 log-import suspension was lifted only in Q4 2025. On balance the trajectory is improving, which supports a competitive-position score above the company's other dimensions.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Commodity cyclicality and housing dependence.** All three segments hinge on U.S. housing starts and R&R activity, both soft on elevated mortgage rates. Lumber/OSB pricing offers no pricing power. This is the central risk and it is largely priced.
2. **Earnings dependence on land sales.** Reported profits are increasingly propped by timberland divestiture gains, which are lumpy and finite. Core operating earnings ex-special items are thin at the trough.
3. **Dividend coverage.** A continued suppression of the supplemental dividend is likely; a deeper cut is possible if the cycle stays depressed. The base dividend appears defensible given balance-sheet headroom.
4. **Trade policy double-edge.** The tariff regime is net-positive for U.S. operations but pressures WY's Canadian mills and Asian export logs; policy can reverse.
5. **Pension volatility.** A $145M non-cash settlement charge in 2025; sensitivity to discount rates and asset returns.
6. **Physical/climate risk.** WY explicitly does not insure standing timber against fire, pests, drought, or windstorms.
7. **REIT/TRS structure.** The TRS asset cap rose to 25% effective Jan 1, 2026; Southern timberlands moved to a separate REIT subsidiary in tax-year 2025. Failure of either subsidiary REIT could jeopardize parent REIT status, a low-probability but high-consequence tail.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 54.7% versus 25.8% one-year realized volatility (a 29-point premium, 83rd percentile within the current chain), the term structure is in backwardation (54.7% near-term versus 33.4% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 13.7% with an implied move of ±9.2% into the June 18 expiry. ATM IV roughly doubled (27.9% → 54.7%) over the past month while price barely moved. This signals an imminent catalyst with downside hedging demand, consistent with the next earnings print and the next-quarter estimate cut. For a holder, this argues against adding aggressively ahead of the event; for an income-oriented holder, the rich near-term IV favors covered-call overwriting. The longer-dated structure (six-month skew near flat) is more benign, consistent with a cyclical-recovery thesis playing out over a longer horizon than the front-end fear implies.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$30–34):** Housing starts recover as mortgage rates ease, lumber/OSB pricing normalizes off multi-decade lows, and the Section 232 tariff regime sustains a structural share shift toward U.S. producers. Wood Products operating income re-rates from the depressed $55M base, Strategic Land Solutions delivers ~$425M EBITDA, and the Monticello facility adds higher-margin engineered-wood capacity from 2027. Mid-cycle EPS recovers, the supplemental dividend returns, and the stock re-rates to ~$30–34 on a recovering earnings base. This requires both a housing recovery and continued tariff support.

**Bear case (~$20–21):** Mortgage rates stay elevated, housing demand stays soft, lumber/OSB pricing remains at trough, and land-sale gains can no longer mask thin operating earnings. The supplemental dividend stays at zero or is cut further, and the stock retests the 52-week low near $21. Downside from the current ~$24.39 is roughly 13–14%, with the asset base and investment-grade balance sheet limiting how far below NAV the stock can trade.

**Base case (~$26):** A gradual cyclical improvement with continued tariff-driven U.S. lumber margin capture and steady Strategic Land Solutions contribution. Current-year EPS lands near or above the rising $0.32 consensus, the asset base supports the floor, and the stock drifts toward the mid-$20s as the cycle slowly turns.

The asymmetry is modestly favorable but not compelling enough for a BUY at ~35x forward EPS with the front-end of the curve pricing imminent stress and the supplemental dividend uncovered. A real recovery thesis combined with asset-value downside support and an uncovered payout makes this a growth-hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the natural reassessment point, given the June-expiry options stress and the next-quarter estimate cut. The Fed rate path and housing-starts data are the key macro inputs over the next 6–12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Lumber/OSB volumes and realizations show a confirmed turn (Wood Products operating income recovering meaningfully above the $55M 2025 base) for two consecutive quarters.
- Adjusted FAD coverage of the dividend returns above 1.0x on operating cash flow (not land-sale proceeds), signaling the cycle has turned and the supplemental can resume.
- Forward EPS estimates re-rate the stock to a PEG materially below 1.5 on a credible recovery path, compressing the implied forward multiple below ~25x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A base dividend cut, or evidence that even the base payout is being funded structurally from asset sales rather than operating cash flow.
- A reversal of the tariff regime that restores Canadian import share, removing the U.S. producer tailwind.
- Operating cash flow deteriorating further in 2026 with no offsetting land-sale pipeline, and current-year estimates rolling over.

## Conclusion

Weyerhaeuser is a high-quality timberland REIT trading at a cyclical earnings trough with a credible 12-month recovery thesis. The forward case rests on three legs: rising current-year estimates backed by four consecutive beats, a structural tariff-driven share shift toward WY's U.S.-heavy production, and a high-margin Strategic Land Solutions segment targeting ~$425M EBITDA that is less correlated to housing. The asset base and investment-grade balance sheet provide downside support that caps the bear case near the 52-week low.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of asymmetry. The stock trades at ~35x next-year EPS on a partial-recovery year, the supplemental dividend is uncovered and likely to stay suppressed, reported earnings are flattered by finite land-sale gains, and the options market is pricing an imminent downside catalyst. The recovery thesis is contingent on a housing turn that is not yet visible in volumes. I rate WY a HOLD with a growth-recovery rationale: own it for the cycle turn and the tariff tailwind, reassess at Q2 earnings, and upgrade on confirmed Wood Products margin recovery with dividend coverage restored from operating cash flow.
