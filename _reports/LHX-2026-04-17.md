## Executive Summary

L3Harris Technologies is the 6th-largest U.S. defense contractor with $21.9B in FY2025 revenue and a $38.7B backlog growing at 13% year-over-year. The company sits at the intersection of three multi-year defense spending vectors: the U.S. reconciliation package ($155B DoD, $165B DHS), NATO allies' commitment to 5% GDP defense spending, and a global munitions production surge driven by active conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East. Free cash flow reached $2.68B in FY2025 (12.3% margin, +17% YoY), and shareholder returns accelerated to $2.1B through doubled buybacks and steady dividends.

The stock has appreciated 61% over the past year, from ~$219 to ~$353, yet trades at approximately 30x FY2026E EPS of $11.69, materially below the A&D peer median P/E of 42x. This discount partially reflects execution uncertainty around three simultaneous strategic transactions: a Missile Solutions IPO, a Space Technology divestiture, and a four-to-three segment reorganization effective FY2026. Analyst estimates have declined 5.9% on current-year EPS over the past 90 days, and the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (54.6% IV vs. 23.5% HV). However, the company has beaten EPS estimates in all four of the last quarters, FCF conversion is improving, and the structural demand environment for tactical communications, rocket motors, and counter-drone systems has rarely been stronger.

The valuation discount to peers, combined with an above-average FCF yield of ~4.1% and visible multi-year growth drivers, supports a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $395. The primary risk is that the negative estimate revision trend continues or that strategic transactions create near-term earnings disruption.

## Company Overview

L3Harris Technologies operates across four segments (consolidating to three in FY2026):

- **Communication Systems (CS):** $5.67B revenue, 25.2% operating margin. Tactical radios, broadband communications, night vision, public safety. Industry-leading margins and a durable competitive moat in military communications standards.
- **Space & Airborne Systems (SAS):** $6.95B revenue, 12.3% margin. Satellites, space payloads, Intel & Cyber, FAA air traffic management, airborne combat systems.
- **Integrated Mission Systems (IMS):** $6.63B revenue, 12.2% margin. ISR platforms, maritime systems, EO/IR sensors, defense electronics.
- **Aerojet Rocketdyne (AR):** $2.85B revenue, 9.5% margin (depressed by $85M goodwill impairment; underlying ~12.5%). Solid/liquid rocket motors, missile propulsion, hypersonic systems.

The customer base is concentrated: 75% U.S. Government, 22% international end-users. Approximately 75% of contracts are fixed-price, which amplifies both upside (margin expansion on well-executed programs) and downside (cost overruns absorbed by the contractor).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $17.1B → FY2023 $19.4B (+14%, including Aerojet) → FY2024 $21.3B (+10%) → FY2025 $21.9B (+3%). Organic growth decelerated to ~3% in FY2025 after accounting for the CAS divestiture headwind ($459M). FY2026 guidance of $23.0–$23.5B implies 5–7% growth, driven by AR production ramps, international tactical radio demand, and munitions programs.

**Profitability:** Operating income grew 10% to $2.11B (9.7% margin, up from 9.0%). Diluted EPS of $8.53 grew 8% YoY. The LHX NeXt transformation program costs declined from $267M to $167M, and the initiative is now being absorbed into ongoing operations ("e3"), suggesting the heaviest restructuring spending is behind the company. Acquisition-related amortization of $769M (declining from $853M) remains a significant GAAP drag that will continue to unwind.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $3.11B (+21% YoY) and FCF of $2.68B (+17%) represent the strongest cash generation in the company's history as a combined entity. FCF margin of 12.3% compares favorably to the defense prime peer group. FCF conversion (FCF/Net Income) of approximately 168% indicates high-quality earnings.

**Capital allocation:** Total shareholder returns of $2.1B in FY2025 (78% of FCF), including $1.2B in buybacks (doubled from $554M) and $903M in dividends. CapEx is guided to ~$600M in FY2026, plus the $1.27B Virginia rocket motor facility expansion. The balance sheet carries $10.4B in total debt (Net Debt/EBITDA ~2.8x, down from ~3.1x), with $650M maturing in the next 12 months against $4.1B of total liquidity.

**Balance sheet concern:** Goodwill of $20.0B represents 49% of total assets. This is a structural feature of the L3/Harris merger and Aerojet acquisition, not a sign of operational distress, but it means any material program failure or interest rate spike could trigger impairment testing pressure. The Altman Z-Score of 1.95 (grey zone) reflects this capital structure rather than operational weakness.

## Growth Analysis

Several growth vectors converge over the next 2–3 years:

1. **Munitions and rocket motor production ramp.** The $1.27B Virginia facility expansion, combined with the AR segment's 10% organic growth in FY2025, positions L3Harris as a primary beneficiary of the munitions restocking imperative across DoD and allied nations. The global conflict environment (Ukraine, Middle East) has created a demand backlog that extends well beyond any single-year budget cycle.

2. **NATO 5% GDP commitment.** Nearly all NATO allies have committed to spending 5% of GDP on defense (3.5% core + 1.5% infrastructure/cyber). L3Harris's tactical communications and night vision products (CS segment) are among the most exportable U.S. defense technologies. International revenue grew by $179M in CS alone in FY2025.

3. **FAA modernization.** Congress appropriated >$22B for the FAA, including >$4B for facilities and equipment. SAS Mission Networks already saw higher FAA-related volume in FY2025.

4. **Counter-drone systems.** VAMPIRE high-volume production began in March 2026. Counter-UAS is among the highest-priority procurement categories globally.

5. **Missile Solutions IPO.** If executed successfully, this transaction could unlock embedded value in the rocket motor business while allowing L3Harris to retain partial ownership and operational synergies.

**Against these drivers:** FY2026 consensus EPS estimates have fallen 5.9% over 90 days (from $12.43 to $11.69). The 30-day revision trend is essentially flat (-0.6%), suggesting the downward revision cycle may be stabilizing. The downward revisions appear tied to (a) the Space Technology divestiture reducing near-term revenue, (b) increased CapEx guidance for the Virginia expansion, and (c) tariff-related margin uncertainty. The underlying demand trajectory remains intact.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris occupies a differentiated position as the largest "platform-agnostic" defense technology provider. Unlike Lockheed Martin or Northrop Grumman, which are primarily prime contractors on large platforms, L3Harris can serve as prime, subcontractor, or merchant supplier. This flexibility gives the company access to a broader set of programs and makes it a natural partner for both traditional primes and non-traditional entrants.

**Moat characteristics:**
- CS segment's 25.2% operating margin reflects sole-source positions in key military communication standards and night vision systems. These products are deeply embedded in military ecosystems with high switching costs.
- AR/Missile Solutions has sole-source positions on many rocket motor programs. The $1.27B facility expansion creates capacity that competitors cannot easily replicate.
- ~18,000 engineers/scientists and extensive classified program access create barriers to entry.

**Competitive threats:** The rise of Anduril, Palantir, and other "new space/new defense" companies is eroding traditional procurement advantages. The DoD's increased use of Other Transaction Authorities (OTAs) reduces barriers for commercial technology firms. L3Harris has responded by partnering with these firms (Palantir, Anduril, Amazon's Project Kuiper) rather than competing directly, which is a pragmatic approach but does concede some value chain economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed-price contract risk (HIGH).** With 75% fixed-price contracts, L3Harris bears significant cost-overrun risk. FY2025 saw $38M in negative EAC adjustments on a classified Maritime program and $25M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program. Net EAC adjustments were positive ($47M), but individual program blowups can be material.

**Customer concentration (HIGH).** 75% U.S. Government revenue creates binary exposure to budget politics. The 43-day government shutdown in FY2025 disrupted operations. Continuing resolutions, sequestration risk, and DOGE-type efficiency initiatives all represent budget headwinds.

**Tariff exposure (ELEVATED).** The 10-K includes a new dedicated risk factor on tariffs. While FY2025 impact was immaterial, Canada is a significant operating geography, and retaliatory tariffs from allies could affect 22% of revenue derived from international customers. The current geopolitical environment makes this risk dynamic rather than static.

**Execution risk on strategic transactions (MODERATE-HIGH).** Three simultaneous transactions (Missile Solutions IPO, Space Technology divestiture, segment reorganization) create management distraction risk and earnings comparability challenges. If market conditions deteriorate, the Missile Solutions IPO could be delayed or repriced.

**Goodwill impairment (MODERATE).** $20B of goodwill (49% of assets). All reporting units cleared the impairment threshold by >30% in FY2025, but a recession or program cancellation could narrow these buffers.

**Estimate revision momentum (MODERATE).** Current-year EPS estimates have declined 5.9% over 90 days. While the 30-day trend has stabilized, continued downward revisions would compress the forward multiple and signal weakening execution.

## Conclusion

L3Harris trades at a meaningful discount to its defense peer group despite a $38.7B backlog growing at 13%, record FCF generation, and direct exposure to the most durable defense spending cycle in decades. The negative estimate revision trend and strategic transaction complexity justify some discount, but 30x FY2026E EPS (vs. peer median 42x) overweights near-term uncertainty against structural demand that extends through the end of this decade. The 4.1% FCF yield provides downside support, and the Missile Solutions IPO represents a concrete catalyst to close the valuation gap. BUY with a $395 base case target.

The specific, falsifiable rebuttal to remaining at HOLD despite a 6.4 overall score: the negative 90-day estimate revision trend (-5.9% current year, -3.7% next year) would normally counsel patience. However, the 30-day revision has stabilized at -0.6%, the company has beaten EPS in all four recent quarters, and the peer discount of 28% is wider than the magnitude of estimate cuts. The revision trend needs to be evaluated against the probability that it reflects one-time portfolio pruning (Space Technology write-down, CAS divestiture) rather than deteriorating core demand. If Q1 FY2026 earnings show organic growth acceleration toward the 5–7% guidance range and estimates stabilize, the multiple gap will narrow. This is a specific, testable catalyst within 2–3 months.
