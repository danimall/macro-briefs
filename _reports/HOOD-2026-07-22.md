**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Robinhood has completed a genuine turnaround. A company that lost $3.69B in 2021 and $1.03B in 2022 generated ~$1.88B of net income and ~$1.62B of free cash flow in FY2025 on ~$4.47B of net revenue (+52% YoY). The operating momentum is stronger than the headline net-income growth suggests: FY2024 was flattered by a ~$347M deferred-tax-asset release while FY2025 carried a ~$225M tax provision, so pre-tax income roughly doubled to ~$2.11B. This is a real, high-margin, asset-light franchise (~42% net margin on ~$15M of capex).

The current debate is about what the business is worth and how durable its newest growth vector is. The 2026 bull case has rotated from crypto to prediction markets and tokenization: Bernstein raised its target to $160 (7/20/26) modeling event-contract revenue rising from ~$150M to ~$1.7B by 2028 via the Rothera exchange/clearinghouse and Robinhood Chain. That is the highest-conviction and highest-risk element of the story simultaneously. The same segment carries active state-level litigation over whether sports event contracts are illegal gambling: Robinhood already ceased new Nevada sports contracts on 12/1/25, with similar demands from NJ, MA, OH, and CT, plus unresolved CFTC rulemaking. Roughly 59% of net revenue remains transaction-based, and payment-for-order-flow economics face two 2026 Reg NMS milestones (odd-lot dissemination in May, public Rule 605 execution-quality comparison in August) that could compress spreads and PFOF.

At ~$104-106, HOOD trades at ~51x trailing EPS of $2.05 and ~56x FY2026 consensus of $1.87 (revised down ~9% over 90 days). The stock is a high-beta, crypto-correlated instrument that has moved from a $153.86 high to a $63.52 low and back within twelve months. We rate HOOD HOLD. The franchise quality justifies ownership through the cycle, but the combination of a stretched multiple, a growth thesis levered to an unadjudicated regulatory question, and no insider-conviction buying leaves the risk/reward balanced.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2013, Robinhood is a vertically integrated, mobile-first financial services platform that pioneered commission-free U.S. equity trading. With roughly 2,900 employees it operates its own introducing broker-dealer, self-clearing broker-dealer, registered futures commission merchant, money transmitter, and SEC-registered investment advisers, an unusually deep licensed stack built cloud-native on AWS. The business is organized around three strategic pillars:

1. **Active traders** — Robinhood Legend (desktop), options (index options rolled to all customers in 2025), futures, event contracts/prediction markets, short selling (launched Q4 2025), and the Cortex AI assistant.
2. **Next-generation wallet share** — Robinhood Gold (4.18M subscribers, +58% YoY), the Gold Card credit product (600K+ customers via Coastal Bank), Robinhood Banking (BaaS), Robinhood Strategies managed portfolios, and RIA custody via the TradePMR acquisition.
3. **Global ecosystem** — UK brokerage, EU crypto (RHEU plus the acquired Bitstamp exchange), tokenization (Robinhood Chain L2, EU Stock Tokens), a Singapore APAC HQ, and pending expansion into Canada (WonderFi) and Indonesia.

Key operating metrics for FY2025: 27.0M funded customers (+7%), Total Platform Assets of $322B (+67%), net deposits of ~$68B, and ARPU of $171 (+40%). Churned customers roughly doubled to ~1.7M, a metric worth tracking against the funded-customer growth.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 income statement is the strongest evidence for the bull case:

- **Total net revenue: ~$4.47B, +52% YoY** (from ~$2.95B).
- **Net income: ~$1.88B, +33%**; diluted EPS $2.05 (vs $1.56).
- **Adjusted EBITDA: ~$2.52B, +76%**; net margin ~42.1%; ROE ~20.6%.
- **Pre-tax income: ~$2.11B, roughly +98%** — the cleaner growth read after adjusting for the 2024 tax benefit.

Revenue mix is diversifying but remains volume- and rate-sensitive. Transaction-based revenue was ~$2.63B (59% of the total): options ~$1.12B, crypto ~$901M, equities ~$302M, plus prediction/other. Net interest revenue was ~$1.51B (34%), and other revenue including ~$179M of Gold subscriptions was ~$331M. The 34% net-interest share means management's explicit warning about Fed rate cuts is material: corporate cash interest already fell ~35% year over year, and further cuts compress the net-interest line directly.

The balance sheet and cash generation are the standout. Operating cash flow was ~$1.64B, FCF ~$1.62B on ~$15M of capex, and corporate cash ~$4.26B. Regulatory net capital at the clearing broker-dealer (~$3.53B against a ~$373M requirement) is robust. The accumulated deficit remains negative (~–$2.15B) but is shrinking quickly.

The screening-derived Altman Z-Score of ~0.84 ("distress") and D/E of ~3.17 are artifacts of a broker-dealer balance sheet inflated by customer cash, margin receivables, and payables. They do not indicate financial fragility. The operating reality — ~42% net margins, ~$1.6B FCF, and $3.5B of regulatory net capital — is the accurate picture. We score Financial Health 7.0, reflecting genuine cash-generative strength while acknowledging the earnings base is levered to reversible transaction volumes and interest rates.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed. After three consecutive beats (Q2'25 +31.8%, Q3'25 +12.0%, Q4'25 +3.2%), the company missed Q1'26 by 12.5% ($0.38 vs $0.43 estimate). The declining beat magnitude and subsequent miss suggest visibility has narrowed as the mix shifts and comparisons harden.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two engines, one maturing and one emerging.

The maturing engine is the trading-and-wallet ecosystem: options, futures, Gold subscriptions (+58%), the Gold Card, banking, and RIA custody. This produced the 52% revenue growth and 67% platform-asset growth in FY2025, and it is durable in the sense that it does not depend on a single binary catalyst, though it is cyclical and crypto-correlated.

The emerging engine is prediction markets and tokenization. Bernstein and Piper Sandler now expect prediction-market revenue to overtake crypto revenue as soon as Q2 2026, with Bernstein modeling growth from ~$150M to ~$1.7B by 2028 via the Rothera exchange/clearinghouse and Robinhood Chain. If that trajectory holds, it materially expands the addressable market and supports the $160 target. The mechanism is credible: Robinhood is positioning as a first mover in an integrated exchange-plus-distribution model where few competitors have both the retail funnel and the regulatory licenses.

The same segment carries the story's largest legal overhang. State-versus-federal litigation over whether sports event contracts are illegal gambling is active; Robinhood ceased new Nevada sports contracts on 12/1/25, and NJ, MA, OH, and CT have issued similar demands. A worst case forces exit from some or all states, which would remove the very growth line driving the target upgrades. This is why we score Growth Prospects 6.8 — high, reflecting real acceleration and a broad product surface, but below the 7.5 of prior reports because the incremental growth is now concentrated in a legally contested segment rather than the broader, better-established ecosystem.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Over 90 days: current-year EPS was cut ~9.2% to $1.87 and current-quarter cut ~12.5%, while next-year edged up +0.4% to $2.61 and 30-day revisions turned modestly positive across all windows (+4.7% CQ, +7.2% NQ, +1.0% CY, +4.7% NY). The pattern is stabilization after a downward reset, not the +30% velocity that would flag a decisive earnings acceleration. We do not apply the strong-buy revision signal here.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$104-106, HOOD trades at ~51x trailing EPS of $2.05 and ~56x FY2026 consensus of $1.87. Against the Investment Banking & Brokerage peer set — IBKR at 38.1x, RJF 14.2x, EVR 20.0x, SF 13.9x, SNEX 23.0x, median 20.0x — HOOD carries roughly a 2.5x premium to the peer median forward multiple and a premium even to IBKR, the closest high-growth comparable.

A premium is defensible given HOOD's ~52% revenue growth and ~42% net margin dwarf the traditional-brokerage peers, none of which is growing near that rate. The question is whether ~56x forward is asymmetric to the upside. We conclude it is not, for three reasons. First, the FY2026 estimate has been revised down ~9% over 90 days, so the multiple is expanding against a shrinking near-term earnings base. Second, roughly a third of revenue is net interest income facing Fed-cut compression, and roughly 59% is transaction-based facing 2026 Reg NMS/PFOF headwinds; the earnings base itself has identifiable downside pressure. Third, the incremental growth valuation rests on prediction-market revenue that could be curtailed by state litigation.

The bull case (Bernstein $160) is a legitimate scenario if prediction markets scale to ~$1.7B by 2028 without regulatory disruption, but it is a growth extrapolation vulnerable to the exact risk embedded in the segment. The analyst target range is unusually wide — $52 low to $160 high, mean $121.40, median $127.50 — which signals that the outcome distribution is bimodal rather than a tight consensus around a fair value.

We score Valuation 4.5. The multiple is not egregiously stretched given the growth, but there is no clean mispricing to exploit at this price; the setup is symmetric with a wide distribution, and a high beta that amplifies both tails. The discipline here is recognizing that a rich multiple on a contested growth line offers no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Robinhood competes across three arenas: retail brokerage (Schwab, Fidelity, who have adopted its commission-free model), crypto (Coinbase, Kraken), and emerging derivatives/prediction markets. Its durable edges are brand resonance with younger, mobile-first investors; a vertically integrated proprietary tech stack that enables rapid product launches with strong unit economics; and a wide and expanding set of regulatory licenses that raise the barrier for pure-play fintechs.

The competitive trajectory is improving in product breadth (Legend, futures, short selling, Cortex, Gold Card, banking, RIA custody, EU crypto) and in international footprint (UK crypto launch planned, EU perpetual futures broadened, Singapore APAC HQ, pending Canada/Indonesia). The structural vulnerability is a heavier reliance on PFOF and transaction volumes than diversified incumbents carry, meaning a Reg NMS-driven spread compression or execution-quality disclosure regime hurts HOOD disproportionately. The frequent pairing with Coinbase as a "rally-and-crash-hard" crypto-linked fintech is accurate: the platform's fortunes are tied to retail risk appetite and crypto cycles more than to a defensible fee annuity.

We score Competitive Position 6.0 — a genuine share-gaining innovator with a widening product moat, offset by revenue-mix concentration in the areas most exposed to regulatory and cyclical reversal. Per our sector calibration, competitive-position scores in Financials carry limited recent predictive weight, so we treat this as context rather than a lever.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced:

- **PFOF / Reg NMS market-structure reform (largest structural risk).** With PFOF a larger revenue share than for most peers, the May 2026 odd-lot/round-lot dissemination is expected to compress spreads and reduce PFOF, and the August 2026 Rule 605 execution-quality disclosures will make broker execution publicly comparable, a reputational and competitive vector. PFOF arrangements are largely not under binding contracts.
- **Event-contract / prediction-market litigation.** The growth narrative's core is legally contested; a state-by-state shutdown scenario would directly impair the segment driving target upgrades.
- **Liquidity-provider concentration.** The overnight 24-Hour Market routes primarily through a single provider (Virtu); crypto settles bilaterally with intraday single-LP net receivables that can exceed $100M, a real counterparty credit risk.
- **Interest-rate sensitivity.** Fed cuts reduce the net-interest line mechanically, and corporate-cash interest already fell ~35%.
- **Retail/crypto beta and reputation.** Revenue concentrates in the most active traders and a large first-time-investor base sensitive to bear markets; the 2021 GameStop restriction remains a cautionary precedent, though the SEC (Jan 2025), FINRA (Mar 2025), and the RHC crypto investigation (closed Feb 2025) legacy matters have been resolved.
- **Governance.** Dual-class control (founders hold ~12% economics, >50% voting) plus a Founders' Voting Agreement limits public-shareholder influence. The Rothera JV is consolidated via board control but not wholly owned (SIG co-owner), adding governance/reputational risk.
- **Possible mid-2026 restructuring and financing.** June 2026 8-Ks indicate exit/disposal costs (Item 2.05) and a new direct financial obligation with unregistered equity sales (likely a debt/convertible financing); scope and use of proceeds should be confirmed in the next 10-Q.

We score Risk Factors 4.0 — consistent with the prior report's downgrade — because specific, near-term, unpriced catalysts for material downside exist: two dated Reg NMS milestones in 2026, active event-contract litigation, and rate-cut exposure. These are not sector truisms; they are dated, company-specific, and asymmetric.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress without directional conviction. Near-term ATM IV is 79.2% versus 69.6% one-year historical vol — rich by ~9.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (79.2% near-term versus 72.3% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (2.1% near-term, turning to modest put skew at longer tenors: –5.2% at six months), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.49 is not bearish. The P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.35 to 0.73 over the past month as the stock fell 3.3%, indicating some hedging accumulation but no capitulation. The read: elevated event risk, symmetric near-term positioning, and rich IV, which argues for patience on entry rather than confirming a bull or bear signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($160, Bernstein)
Prediction markets scale to ~$1.7B by 2028 without material regulatory disruption; the Rothera exchange/clearinghouse and Robinhood Chain tokenization establish a first-mover integrated venue; international expansion (UK crypto, EU perpetual futures, Canada/Indonesia) adds a new customer funnel; Gold subscriptions and the Gold Card compound recurring revenue; and the ~42% margin structure converts the growth to FCF. At that trajectory the forward multiple is defensible, and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case ($52-70)
State litigation forces event-contract exits, removing the incremental growth line; May/August 2026 Reg NMS reforms compress PFOF; Fed cuts erode the net-interest base; a crypto/retail-risk-off cycle collapses transaction volumes (the stock already round-tripped to $63.52 within the last year). At a normalized ~$1.50-1.80 EPS with a de-rated 25-30x multiple, the stock trades $45-55, near the analyst low of $52. This is roughly –35% to –50% from current levels.

### Base Case (~$115-125)
The ecosystem grows at a decelerating but healthy rate, prediction markets contribute meaningfully but face state-by-state friction rather than a wholesale ban, and the multiple compresses toward 50-55x on FY2026 EPS drifting toward the $2.00-2.20 range as 2027 estimates ($2.61 next-year consensus) come into view. This lands near the analyst mean/median of $121-128.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise merits ownership through the cycle, but the price offers no asymmetric edge and the growth thesis is levered to an unadjudicated regulatory question. This is an uncertain hold, not a conviction growth or income call: there is no dividend, and the growth vector that would upgrade the stock is the same one that could downgrade it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Favorable resolution or clear federal-preemption clarity on event-contract legality across multiple contested states, de-risking the prediction-market revenue line.
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $80s that restores a margin of safety while fundamentals hold.
- Evidence that the August 2026 Rule 605 disclosures do not materially impair execution economics, plus prediction-market revenue confirming the overtaking-crypto trajectory in reported results.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A broad state-level shutdown of sports event contracts or an adverse CFTC ruling that removes the segment from the model.
- A quarter showing transaction revenue declining double digits YoY combined with net-interest compression, signaling both engines rolling over.
- Confirmation that the June 2026 financing/restructuring items reflect operating stress rather than opportunistic balance-sheet management.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, the May 2026 odd-lot dissemination effect and August 2026 Rule 605 disclosures, and any state/CFTC event-contract ruling. The next 10-Q is the priority document for detail on the June 2026 restructuring and financing.

## Conclusion

Robinhood is a materially better business than it was three years ago: profitable, cash-generative, asset-light, and innovating faster than any traditional-brokerage peer. That case is settled. What is unsettled is valuation and the durability of the newest growth vector. At ~56x forward earnings against a peer median near 20x, with a growth story concentrated in a legally contested segment, two dated 2026 Reg NMS headwinds, rate-cut exposure across a third of revenue, uniform insider selling (including the CEO's full ~$50M Class A liquidation in July 2026, albeit programmatic and control-neutral), and a beta profile that has swung the stock from $154 to $64 and back within a year, the risk/reward is balanced. We maintain HOLD. The bar to move to BUY is regulatory clarity on event contracts or a multiple reset toward the mid-$80s; the bar to AVOID is a confirmed shutdown of the prediction-market growth line or evidence both revenue engines are rolling over simultaneously.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: FY2026 forward P/E is ~56x (not ~40x) at $1.87 EPS, making the valuation notably richer than the report's original framing implied; net margin is ~42% (not ~63%) — ~$1.88B net income on ~$4.47B revenue.
