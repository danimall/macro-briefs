**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Trustmark Corporation is a ~$18.9 billion-asset bank holding company headquartered in Jackson, Mississippi, operating a Deep South / Gulf Coast regional banking franchise with 130+ years of history. The 2025 results were strong once adjusted for a 2024 distortion: headline net income of $224.1M (diluted EPS $3.70) is barely above 2024's $223.0M, but 2024 was depressed by a deliberate ~$137M post-tax securities restructuring loss. On a like-for-like adjusted continuing-operations basis, earnings grew 20.3%, driven by 29 basis points of net interest margin expansion to 3.80% and a mortgage-banking recovery. The bank returned ~62% of net income to shareholders while still growing tangible book value per share 13.5% to $30.28, a rational capital-allocation posture at a bank trading around 1.5x tangible book.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because of the risk/reward at the current price, not fundamental deterioration. The stock has risen roughly 21% over the trailing year to $46.61, sitting near its 52-week high of $48.00 and near the analyst mean target of $47.65. At ~12.4x trailing and ~11.9x forward EPS the stock is fairly valued against its ~13.5x regional-bank peer median, a modest discount rather than a mispricing. The 2025 earnings tailwind is not fully repeatable: management has guided 2026 provisioning to normalize toward 14-18 bps of average loans versus the ultra-low 2025 level that benefited from a qualitative reserve release. The bank is modestly asset-sensitive into a Fed-cutting cycle, and a concentrated build in Mississippi residential mortgage nonaccruals (from $42.2M to $73.0M) is the one credit blemish worth monitoring.

The prior trajectory (6.8 BUY in May, 6.0 HOLD in June) reflected a stepdown in valuation and growth scores as the stock re-rated toward fair value. New data confirms rather than reverses that read: estimate revisions are positive but modest (+2.5% current-year over 90 days, well below the +30% threshold that would justify an aggressive re-rating call), and the price has continued to grind higher with no fundamental deterioration. This is a quality franchise fairly priced; the compounding case and the income are intact, but the asymmetry that supported a BUY has been arbitraged away.

## Company Overview

Trustmark operates through its subsidiary Trustmark National Bank across six market regions: Mississippi (its core, ~51% of loans), Alabama (~21%), Texas (~13%), Georgia (~9%), Tennessee (~4%), and Florida (~3%). The company reports two segments:

- **General Banking** (~95% of net income; $212.2M in 2025): commercial and consumer lending, deposit-taking, and mortgage banking.
- **Wealth Management** (~5%; $11.9M, +52.9% YoY): trust, brokerage, and private banking, with record revenue of $40.1M and AUM/AUA of $10.9B.

Loans held for investment total $13.67B and are well diversified: non-farm non-residential commercial real estate 24%, 1-4 family residential ~22%, C&I 15%, multifamily and other real estate secured 15.5%, state and political subdivision 8%, and equipment finance leasing 6%. Mortgage banking is a meaningful franchise, with $1.53B of 2025 production, $8.96B loans serviced for others, and $131.3M of mortgage servicing rights fair value.

The company has deliberately simplified toward core banking and wealth, exiting its insurance subsidiary (FBBI) in Q2 2024 (now reported in discontinued operations). Its competitive footprint spans larger regionals (Regions, Renasant, Cadence, Synovus, Pinnacle) and community banks/credit unions. Trustmark holds an "Outstanding" CRA rating and a low-cost core deposit franchise (total deposit cost 1.72% in Q4 2025).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power is stronger than the headline suggests.** The cleaner year-over-year comparison strips out the 2024 securities restructuring. In 2024, Trustmark sold $1.56B of 1.36%-yield AFS securities at a $182.8M pre-tax loss and reinvested at 4.85%, a value-accretive move that depressed reported 2024 earnings but boosted 2025 securities yields (taxable AFS yield rose from 3.13% to 4.44%). Adjusted continuing-operations earnings grew from $186.3M to $224.1M.

**Net interest income and margin are the core story.** NII on an FTE basis grew 8.4% to $647M (Q4 exit NIM ~3.83% adjusted). The mechanism: falling deposit costs outpaced asset-yield declines, with interest expense down 16.9% while interest income fell only 1.2%. This is a favorable dynamic in a rate-cut environment because Trustmark's deposit costs reprice down faster than its loan book repositions. The offsetting consideration is that the bank is modestly asset-sensitive (+200 bps → +3.1% NII; -100 bps → -2.1% NII), so continued Fed cuts are a mild NII headwind that management is currently offsetting through deposit repricing.

**Returns and capital are solid.** ROA was 1.21% and ROATE 12.97%, both healthy for a regional bank. CET1 stands at 11.72% and total risk-based capital at 14.41%, comfortably "well-capitalized." Tangible equity to tangible assets improved to 9.61%.

**Balance sheet health is good.** Loans grew 4.5% YoY and deposits 2.6% to $15.5B, with quality commercial and personal balance growth (+$568M) offsetting volatile public-fund runoff. Uninsured deposits at 35.3% of total are moderate and stable, and contingent liquidity is deep: ~$1.96B additional FHLB capacity, $7.8B discount-window capacity, and $1.37B unencumbered securities. The AOCI drag is modest and improving: the AFS book flipped to a $34.4M net unrealized gain from a $27M loss a year earlier as rates fell.

One clarification on the financial-health read: a headline Altman Z-Score in distress territory should be disregarded. That model is built for industrial firms and is meaningless for banks, whose business model inherently involves ~7.9x leverage. Every regulatory capital measure shows Trustmark well-capitalized.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is that of a steady mid-single-digit compounder rather than an inflection story. Management's 2026 guidance calls for mid-single-digit growth in loans, deposits (ex-brokered), NII, noninterest income, and noninterest expense; NIM of 3.80-3.85%; net charge-offs of 13-15 bps; and provision normalizing to 14-18 bps, consistent with consensus.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest, which is the crux of the valuation question. Current-year consensus rose +2.5% over 90 days to $3.91, and next-year to $4.10 (+0.5% over 90 days). Current-quarter estimates ticked +1.2% over 90 days. This falls far short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a fundamental inflection warranting an aggressive re-rating. The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 beating by 6.0% ($0.95 vs. $0.90 estimate).

The organic-growth engine is credible. Trustmark added 21 production hires in Q3 2025 and 13 in Q4 2025 across C&I and equipment finance, deliberately exploiting banker disruption from competitor M&A. Wealth Management is the fastest-growing segment, aided by a brokerage-platform conversion in H2 2025. These are incremental levers on a mature deposit franchise, not a step-change. The 2025 earnings growth also flatters forward comparisons because it was aided by non-repeatable factors, the reserve release and the full-year benefit of the higher securities yield, which is why forward revenue and earnings growth should be modeled in the mid-single digits rather than extrapolated from the 20% adjusted 2025 print.

At $46.61, Trustmark trades at ~12.4x trailing EPS of $3.70 and ~11.9x forward EPS of $3.91, against a regional-bank peer median of ~13.5x. On tangible book value per share of $30.28, the stock trades at ~1.54x. The dividend yield is ~2.2%, below the peer-median 3.0%.

Trustmark's modest discount to the median is reasonable given its below-median dividend yield and its Gulf Coast catastrophe concentration, but it is not the kind of gap that signals a mispricing. The stock has re-rated toward fair value: it sits near its 52-week high and near the analyst mean target of $47.65 (median $46.50), with a target range of $45.50 to $52.25.

Cheapness here is not a strong buy argument. A ~12x multiple on a mid-single-digit grower with normalizing credit costs is appropriate rather than anomalously low. The valuation score reflects fair pricing with limited asymmetry: the stock offers the ~1.5x-TBV compounding of a well-run regional plus its yield, but the market has already recognized the franchise quality. The analyst-target-as-floor dynamic that applies to cheap inflection stocks does not apply here, because the modest estimate revisions and near-target price mean consensus is not systematically lagging reality.

## Competitive Landscape

Trustmark's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving, and management's read of the environment is its most differentiated insight. The bank's Deep South markets are undergoing heavy consolidation, and CEO Duane Dewey named Synovus, Pinnacle, Cadence, and Stellar as competitors involved in in-market mergers, including a large in-backyard deal and a Houston/Texas transaction. Each merger creates customer and banker disruption that Trustmark is exploiting through organic talent poaching rather than its own M&A. This is a lower-risk, ROIC-friendly way to gain share than paying up for a deal at cycle valuations.

The bank's moat rests on a low-cost core deposit franchise (1.72% total deposit cost, among the better in its peer set), deep community relationships built over 130+ years, and an "Outstanding" CRA rating. Management's disciplined M&A stance, preferring $1B-$10B targets in Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, and Tennessee but emphatic that "we are not going to be focused on doing a deal" while the stock trades cheaply, is shareholder-friendly and preserves optionality.

The competitive-position trajectory is not sharply improving, which keeps the score at 6.0. Trustmark is winning bankers at the margin during a consolidation window, but its core Mississippi market is low-growth, its CRE book is intentionally shrinking (NFNR CRE -6.5% in 2025), and it faces the same deposit-competition and NIM pressures as the broader regional group.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest rate risk.** The deposit-cost tailwind has more than offset asset sensitivity so far, but the margin is the single biggest earnings lever, and an accelerated cutting path would pressure NII.

**Geographic and catastrophe concentration.** Heavy Gulf Coast exposure (MS, AL, FL, TX) creates hurricane and natural-disaster risk to collateral values and borrower repayment, a specific, recurring risk versus inland peers that is only partially priced.

**Commercial real estate.** NFNR CRE is 24% of loans, including ~$375M office and ~$235M hotel/motel. The book declined intentionally in 2025 and net charge-offs remain low, but CRE bears monitoring given sector-wide stress.

**Concentrated Mississippi mortgage nonaccruals.** This is the one credit blemish. The build was driven by 1-4 family residential loans centralized in Jackson, and was masked at the consolidated level because three large commercial nonaccruals in Alabama/Texas resolved simultaneously, but total nonaccruals still rose 5.3% and the past-due-plus-nonaccrual ratio ticked up to 1.85% from 1.62%. It suggests some softening in the core-market residential book even as commercial credit heals.

**Reserve-release dependency.** 2025's low provision ($12.9M vs. $37.3M adjusted in 2024) was heavily aided by releasing a qualitative "External Factor – Credit Quality Review" reserve in Q3 2025. Management's 2026 guidance confirms this tailwind reverses, and ACL coverage already declined from 1.22% to 1.15% of loans (coverage of nonaccruals down from 200% to 186%). Investors should not extrapolate 2025's ultra-low credit costs.

**Governance / management transition.** Form 4s show Tom Owens (former CFO/Treasurer) retitled "Executive Officer" and Joseph Bond appointed Treasurer as of May 2026, alongside Item 5.02 8-Ks in March and May 2026. This appears to be an internal finance-org succession rather than a disruptive departure, but the specific 8-K detail was not fully accessible and warrants a follow-up read.

**Litigation history (context).** Trustmark settled Stanford Financial Group litigation ($100M charge in Q4 2022) and Adams/Madison Timber ($6.5M in Q3 2023), both resolved, but a reminder the bank has faced material legal exposure tied to fiduciary relationships.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-53).** Deposit costs continue to reprice down faster than asset yields, holding NIM at the top of the guide; the organic-hire strategy delivers above-guidance loan and fee growth as competitor mergers create dislocation; the Mississippi mortgage nonaccrual build proves idiosyncratic and resolves; and the bank sustains its ~62% capital-return payout, growing TBV/share at low-double digits. In that scenario the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $52.25 on ~13x forward EPS approaching $4.10, roughly +12% from current levels plus the dividend.

**Bear Case ($37-39).** An accelerated Fed-cutting path pressures the asset-sensitive book, NIM compresses below 3.80%, the Mississippi residential nonaccrual thread spreads and credit costs overshoot the guide as reserve releases end, and a Gulf Coast catastrophe event pressures collateral. In that scenario earnings stall and the stock derates toward ~10x on flat EPS near the 52-week low around $36-39, roughly -18% from current.

**Base Case ($47-49).** Mid-single-digit loan/deposit/NII growth, NIM near 3.80%, provisioning normalizing as guided, and continued buybacks and a well-covered dividend. EPS reaches ~$3.91 in 2026 and ~$4.10 in 2027; at ~12x the stock trades to roughly $47-49, modest appreciation plus the yield. This aligns with the analyst mean target of $47.65.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $48.** ~12.3x forward EPS, consistent with the peer-median discount the bank has historically carried and with the analyst mean. This sits between the bear ($37-39) and bull ($52-53) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $52.25** (~+12% from current, matching the high analyst target).

**Bear case target: $38** (~-18% from current).

**Timeframe: 12 months**, with the Q2 2026 earnings release and the 2027 guidance update at the next Q4 call as the key checkpoints.

**Upside/downside from current ($46.61):** +3% to base, +12% to bull, -18% to bear. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable, which supports a HOLD.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revision velocity accelerates (current-year EPS revised up >8% over 90 days) alongside confirmed NIM stability above 3.80%.
- Mississippi nonaccruals stabilize or decline while consolidated credit metrics improve, removing the credit overhang.
- A pullback toward the mid-$40s / ~1.3x TBV that restores asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Provision exceeds the guided 14-18 bp range for two consecutive quarters, signaling the credit tailwind has fully reversed into a headwind.
- NIM compresses below 3.70% as asset sensitivity bites in an accelerated cutting cycle.
- The Mississippi residential nonaccrual build broadens into commercial or spreads to other core markets, or a dividend/buyback pullback signals capital stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings, with the executive-transition 8-K detail worth confirming before then.

## Conclusion

Trustmark is a well-run, well-capitalized, shareholder-friendly Gulf Coast regional bank with genuine (if optically masked) ~20% adjusted 2025 earnings growth, 29 bps of NIM expansion, strong five-year TSR outperformance (165.9 vs. peer 126.8), and a rational strategy of poaching bankers and buying back cheap stock while competitors merge. The financial health is solid and capital allocation is disciplined.

The recommendation is **HOLD (growth_hold)**. This is a quality franchise fairly priced. At ~12x forward on modest positive revisions, ~1.5x tangible book, near its 52-week high and near the analyst mean target, the asymmetry that supported the prior BUY has compressed. The 2025 earnings tailwind is not fully repeatable, 2026 credit costs are guided to normalize higher, the bank is asset-sensitive into a cutting cycle, and the Mississippi residential nonaccrual build is a specific item to watch. None of this is thesis-breaking, and the compounding plus the ~2.2% dividend and ongoing buyback make the stock worth holding, but the case for aggressive accumulation requires either a cheaper entry (mid-$40s) or an acceleration in estimate revisions and confirmation that the credit softening is contained. The declining score trajectory (6.8 → 6.0) reflects this re-rating toward fair value, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it.
