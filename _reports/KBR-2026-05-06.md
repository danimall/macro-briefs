## Executive Summary

KBR trades at $35.20, approximately 38% below its 52-week high of $56.78, creating a valuation setup that is among the most compressed in the government/defense services sector at ~8.8x forward P/E. The stock is cheap for identifiable reasons: revenue decelerated to +1% in FY2025 and declined approximately 5% in Q1 FY2026, EPS estimates are drifting lower (-2% to -2.5% over 90 days), and the planned January 2027 spin-off creates structural uncertainty about capital allocation and corporate identity. However, operating fundamentals remain intact: $557M operating cash flow from continuing operations, $17.3B backlog (as of Q1 FY2026), four consecutive earnings beats, and an activist investor (Engine Capital) pushing for a full sale that could crystallize the gap between current price and intrinsic value.

The central thesis rests on two catalysts: (1) the spin-off unlocking the high-margin STS segment, which would command technology-licensing multiples far above KBR's blended ~8.8x, and (2) the activist's sale thesis gaining traction with other shareholders or strategic buyers. At current prices, the market assigns negligible value to the optionality of either outcome. Analyst low targets of ~$40 and a strong free cash flow yield provide valuation support, while upside scenarios range from $45-50 on spin-off execution to $55+ on a take-private at peer multiples.

## Company Overview

KBR operates two distinct business segments with meaningfully different economic profiles. Mission Technology Solutions (MTS, ~72% of revenue) provides defense, intelligence, space, and logistics support primarily to the U.S. DoD, U.K. MoD, and Australian Defence, generating 8.3% operating margins. Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS, ~28% of revenue) licenses proprietary process technologies for ammonia/syngas, petrochemicals, and clean energy applications, reporting 21.6% operating margins — though this figure includes substantial equity-method earnings from LNG joint ventures (approximately $177M in FY2025) that are lumpy and project-dependent rather than recurring technology-licensing income.

The company has ~36,000 employees across 28+ countries, with 57% of revenue from U.S. government sources and 9% from U.K. government. The $17.3B backlog ($23.2B including options, as of Q1 FY2026) spans multi-year contracts providing substantial revenue visibility despite near-term softness. Key joint ventures, particularly KZJV for LNG projects, contributed $210M in equity earnings in FY2025 (up 96% YoY), flowing through segment operating income.

In September 2025, KBR announced plans to spin off MTS into a separate public company, targeting January 4, 2027. This would leave KBR as a pure-play energy/sustainability technology company. In April 2026, Engine Capital (~2% stake) publicly challenged this strategy, arguing the company should explore a full sale instead.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Revenue grew from $6.96B (FY2023) to $7.79B (FY2025), but the growth rate collapsed from 11% to 1%. Q1 FY2026 showed revenue of $1.923B, down approximately 5% YoY, primarily attributable to expected EUCOM contingency activity runoff in MTS.

Operating income improved 18% to $778M (10.0% margin), driven by mix shift toward higher-margin STS work and the surge in JV equity earnings. Adjusted EPS of $3.93 grew 18% from $3.33 in FY2024. Net income attributable to KBR was $415M.

**Important analytical note on STS margins:** The reported 21.6% STS operating margin ($477M on $2.205B revenue) includes $177M of equity-method earnings from unconsolidated affiliates, primarily LNG project-related. Excluding these equity earnings, the STS operating margin from core engineering and licensing activities would be materially lower (~14%). KBR itself attributes the FY2025 STS operating income increase primarily to increased equity earnings from services on an LNG project. These JV earnings are inherently lumpy and project-dependent.

**Cash Flow Quality:**
Operating cash flow from continuing operations of $557M represents a 7.2% OCF margin and strong conversion. Simple free cash flow of approximately $515M ($557M OCF minus $42M capex) on a $4.5B market cap equates to approximately 11.5% on a simplified FCF yield basis — though this does not fully account for JV investment cash flows and other investing activities that can be material in any given period. Capital allocation priorities included $323M in buybacks, $84M in dividends, and $26M in debt reduction.

The XBRL-reported negative cash flow (-$33M) reflects HomeSafe discontinued operations cash outflows. While valuation should focus on continuing operations ($557M OCF), the discontinued operations impact reflects real contract-termination costs that affected consolidated cash.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of approximately $2.6B ($49M current maturities plus $2.547B long-term debt) against $500M cash yields net debt of ~$2.1B. Debt-to-equity is approximately 1.7x based on total debt of $2.6B divided by shareholders' equity of $1.512B. Total liabilities-to-equity is higher at approximately 3.4x. Leverage is manageable given the cash generation profile and government contract predictability. Goodwill of $2.68B (41% of assets) reflects acquisitions including LinQuest ($738M, August 2024). The U.K. pension plan carries an £84M surplus, removing a common risk factor.

Debt maturities are manageable near-term ($49M FY2026) but concentrate in FY2027 ($551M) and FY2029 ($788M). The spin-off plan envisions MTS issuing its own debt and distributing cash to KBR to delever — execution of this transfer mechanism is a key risk.

**Altman Z-Score:** 1.74 technically sits in the distress zone, but this is mechanically driven by the liabilities-to-equity ratio and goodwill-heavy asset base. With $557M annual OCF, government-backed revenue, and no near-term liquidity concerns, actual credit risk is substantially lower than the score implies.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term headwinds are real.** Estimate revisions are negative: current year EPS consensus dropped from $4.06 to $3.98 over 90 days (-2.0%), and next year from $4.31 to $4.20 (-2.5%). Revenue decelerated to 1% and turned negative in Q1 FY2026. These aren't fabricated concerns.

**However, the backlog tells a different story.** Total backlog grew to $17.3B as of Q1 FY2026, with options at $23.2B. Recent reported contract wins include a $510M Joint Data IDIQ, $449M LOGCAP extension, $200M DOT AI transportation contract, and a $200M recompete. Q1 FY2026 book-to-bill was 1.1x (excluding long-term U.K. PFIs and the Plaquemines LNG project), suggesting revenue growth should reaccelerate as new awards convert to revenue, though sustained book-to-bill above 1.0x is not yet confirmed on a trailing twelve-month basis.

**STS growth potential post-spin:** The energy transition tailwind is structural. Demand for clean ammonia (K-GreeN technology), plastic recycling (Hydro-PRT), and sustainable aviation fuel (PureSAF) positions STS in high-growth markets. However, investors should distinguish between STS's recurring technology licensing economics and the lumpy equity-method LNG earnings that currently constitute a significant portion of reported STS profitability.

**Defense spending macro:** The FY2026 national defense budget request totals approximately $1.01T, including $848B for DOD discretionary spending plus mandatory DOD funding via reconciliation. The Administration has proposed approximately $1.5T for FY2027, though this is a budget request facing Congressional debate rather than enacted appropriations. NATO targeting 5% GDP by 2035, and U.K. moving to 2.5% GDP by 2027 and 3% thereafter all support the MTS growth trajectory once government shutdown and procurement disruptions normalize. The DoD acquisition reform emphasis on "trusted capability integrators" aligns with KBR's positioning.

Growth is not in an upswing today, but the deceleration appears cyclical and contract-timing-related rather than structural, given backlog expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.20 with forward EPS consensus of $3.98, KBR trades at ~8.8x current year earnings and ~8.4x next year (based on $4.20 consensus). KBR's own FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance range is $3.87–$4.22. This compares to:
- SAIC: 12.4x P/E
- Peer median: 34.6x (skewed by OPLN losses and UNF/AMTM premiums)
- Historical KBR range: 15-20x over the past three years

The stock is at a multi-year low multiple. The simplified FCF yield of ~11.5% on continuing operations (before accounting for JV investment cash flows) provides substantial support.

**Sum-of-parts analysis:**

A proper SOTP must avoid double-counting JV equity earnings, which are already included in STS segment operating income:
- MTS ($5.6B revenue, $463M operating income): At 12x operating income = $5.6B value
- STS ($2.2B revenue, $477M operating income including $177M equity-method earnings): At 13x operating income = $6.2B value
- Less corporate/unallocated costs (~$162M): At 10x = -$1.6B
- Less net debt ($2.1B)
- **Sum: ~$8.1B / 127M shares = ~$64/share**

Alternatively, applying a blended 12x to total company operating income of $778M yields approximately $9.3B enterprise value, or ~$57/share after deducting net debt. Even conservatively, these approaches suggest meaningful upside from $35.20.

The activist's thesis gains credibility when KBR's TSR of 138 vs. peer group TSR of 213 represents 35% underperformance. Either the spin-off or a sale would surface the mispricing.

**Analyst consensus target of $51.50 median** implies 46% upside from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

KBR occupies a differentiated position as one of few companies spanning both defense/intelligence services and proprietary energy technology. In MTS, it competes with Leidos, SAIC, Booz Allen Hamilton, and Parsons for large government IT/engineering contracts. Its advantages include depth of security clearances, long-standing customer relationships (incumbent on many programs), and systems integration capability.

In STS, it competes with Honeywell UOP, Axens, and Technip Energies for technology licensing. The proprietary portfolio of 85+ technologies — particularly in ammonia/syngas production where KBR has dominant market share — creates meaningful barriers. These technologies generate recurring royalties with minimal capital requirements.

The planned spin-off would sharpen competitive positioning for both entities. MTS would compete directly with SAIC and Leidos without the distraction of an energy technology business, while STS would be positioned as a pure-play cleantech/industrial technology licensor.

Recent contract wins and the Tagup AI partnership for missile defense demonstrate that competitive win rates remain healthy.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Spin-off Execution (High):** IRS ruling, Form 10 effectiveness, MTS financing, board approval, potential for delay or cancellation. If delayed beyond January 2027, market patience may thin further.

**2. Revenue Deceleration (Moderate-High):** If subsequent quarters don't improve from Q1's decline, estimates will continue falling regardless of backlog size.

**3. Debt and Refinancing (Moderate):** $551M due in FY2027 coincides with spin-off timing. If spin-off delays, refinancing occurs at higher rates. Spin-off success depends on MTS securing its own financing to fund the distribution to KBR.

**4. Government Budget Volatility (Moderate):** Continuing resolutions, shutdowns, and procurement reform create uncertainty. The October 2025 shutdown directly impacted revenue timing.

**5. JV Earnings Normalization (Moderate):** The $210M equity earnings (up 96%) from KZJV is lumpy and project-dependent. If this normalizes to $120-150M, EPS faces a ~$0.30-0.50 headwind. Because these earnings are embedded in STS segment operating income, STS reported margins would also decline materially.

**6. Executive Order Risk (Low-Moderate):** The January 2026 EO allowing limitation of defense contractor dividends/buybacks is unprecedented. If enforced, it constrains capital return.

**7. Downside Risk:** While analyst targets currently range from approximately $40-$51+, these are opinions that can be revised downward. The stock can trade below analyst targets if estimates are cut, the spin is delayed, JV earnings normalize, or macro/contracting risks worsen. Downside is not "bounded" by analyst price targets.

## Conclusion

KBR at $35.20 presents a valuation anomaly. The stock trades at 8.8x forward earnings with strong cash flow generation, a $17.3B backlog, an activist demanding value crystallization, and a planned spin-off that would surface the value of its technology licensing business alongside its lumpy but significant JV earnings stream. The risks are real — revenue deceleration, spin-off execution uncertainty, JV earnings normalization, and debt maturities — but they are substantially priced at a 38% discount to 52-week highs.

This has been rated BUY three consecutive times with stable scores. Fundamentals have not deteriorated proportionally to the stock decline. Operating cash flow is $557M and rising. Backlog is stable and growing. Contract wins continue. The activist catalyst is new and additive.

This is the fourth consecutive BUY. The bar for maintaining it is higher. The risk/reward has improved at $35 versus $37-38 previously, the activist catalyst didn't exist in prior reports, and the spin-off timeline is now 8 months away versus 12+ before. At sub-9x earnings with multiple value-creation paths, this remains an attractive accumulation opportunity for investors with 12-18 month horizons.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The SOTP valuation has been materially revised downward (from ~$101/share to ~$64/share) after correcting for double-counting of JV equity earnings; STS margin quality is lower than originally presented due to reliance on lumpy LNG equity-method earnings; these corrections reduce but do not eliminate the upside case supporting the recommendation.
