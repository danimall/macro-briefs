## Executive Summary

Lazard's Q2 2026 results separated into three components, and only one of them is benign. The tax rate (69.7% adjusted, described by management as an anomalous equity-vesting catch-up against a full-year GAAP expectation in the high-20s) is a timing artifact. The 69.9% adjusted compensation ratio is largely an accrual mechanic — the firm holds Q2 accrual flat to Q1 by long practice, and Q1 was revenue-light against a fixed-cost base that grew with hiring. The third component is not an artifact: Financial Advisory net revenue fell 9.5% year over year in Q2 and 7% in H1, and the segment's adjusted operating margin collapsed from 23.1% to 13.5%, while Lazard's own 10-Q table shows the firm completing 29 M&A transactions above $500M in H1 2026 versus 36 a year earlier — a 19% decline against a 14% increase in the industry count of such deals. The FY2025 10-K shows the identical pattern (73 completions versus 85, against a 14% industry increase). Advisory revenue is contracting in a market that is expanding.

The market has priced part of this. The shares are 27.7% below the February 10, 2026 high of $58.75, short interest is 12.7% of float, and consensus has cut FY2026 EPS 22.2% in 30 days (to $2.23) and FY2027 15.4% in 90 days (to $3.85). The drawdown reflects genuine fundamental deterioration rather than a sentiment dislocation: operating income fell 60% year over year in Q2 and 35% in H1, and two consecutive quarters have missed (−18.1% in Q1, −66.3% in Q2 on a like-for-like non-GAAP basis). The relevant question is whether the price now compensates for the deterioration.

On our arithmetic it roughly does, without margin of safety. At $42.45 the stock is 19.0x FY2026 consensus and 11.0x FY2027 consensus, but the FY2027 number embeds approximately 15% revenue growth and a 450 basis-point compensation-ratio reduction occurring together. Haircutting to a more defensible $3.00-3.20 of FY2027 earnings power at 14x produces a base case of roughly $45, only 6% above spot, against a $32 bear case (−24.6%) if the 2027 recovery slips and dividend coverage is questioned. The 4.71% yield is the strongest single support, but it is being sustained through a trough partly on a $75.5M non-cash Edgewater gain and a cash balance that fell from $1,469.4M to $1,099.8M over H1, with up to $660M of Campbell Lutyens consideration still to fund. We rate the shares REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Lazard operates two reported segments and is building a third.

**Financial Advisory** (~59% of FY2025 net revenue) provides M&A, restructuring and liability-management, capital-markets, sovereign, and private-capital advice. The revenue model is almost entirely success-based; the 10-K states there are "typically no long-term contracted sources of revenue" and each engagement is separately awarded. Management describes the M&A/non-M&A mix as roughly 60/40 and stable. FY2025 adjusted net revenue was $1,825M at a 24.2% adjusted margin.

**Asset Management** manages $284.65B of AUM (June 30, 2026), almost entirely active and fundamental, with a stated house style oriented to relative value and quality investing, plus a fast-growing systematic platform. Fees are AUM-based; contracts are terminable on very short notice; roughly 67% of AUM carried foreign-currency exposure at December 31, 2025, generally unhedged.

**Lazard CL** is the announced (April 30, 2026) acquisition of Campbell Lutyens, a London private-capital advisor and placement agent, for approximately $460M initial plus $115M deferred plus up to $85M earn-out — up to $660M, payable in a combination of stock, cash, and loan notes with proportions undisclosed. The 8-K carried Item 3.02 (unregistered equity sales), confirming stock forms part of consideration. Closing is expected in H2 2026; termination fee $50M.

Management's framework is "Lazard 2030," with two verifiable targets reiterated on the Q2 call: $10M of revenue per advisory Managing Director by 2028, and 10-15 net MD additions annually.

## Financial Analysis

### The multi-year earnings series is the essential context

GAAP net income attributable to Lazard: **2021 $528M → 2022 $358M → 2023 −$75M → 2024 $280M → 2025 $237M.** There is no growth trend in this series. FY2025 net income of $237M on 107.34M current diluted shares is $2.21 per share; the stock at $42.45 is 19.2x that figure. The profile data supplied shows trailing and forward P/E identical at 21.77x, which is internally inconsistent with the $2.17 EPS field (42.45 ÷ 2.17 = 19.6x); we use our own computed multiples throughout.

FY2025 adjusted net revenue was $3,030M (+5%), adjusted operating income $432M (+5%), adjusted operating margin 14.3%, adjusted compensation ratio 65.5%.

The shape of the Q2 miss: total revenue of $830.2M beat a $772.19M consensus, while non-GAAP EPS of $0.12 missed a $0.42 consensus by roughly 71%. The entire gap is expenses and taxes. The top line is not collapsing; the operating leverage is inverted.

### Testing the compensation-ratio arithmetic

The 69.9% ratio is the loudest number in the release and the one most likely to be misread. H1 adjusted compensation implied by the ratio is 0.699 × $1,459M ≈ **$1,020M** (cross-check: Q2 adjusted comp $550M on $786M = 70.0%; Q1 therefore ≈ $470M on $673M = 69.8% — internally consistent). FY2025 H1 revenue was $1,413M against $3,030M full-year, so H2/H1 seasonality was 1.14x.

- **Normal seasonality, flat sequential comp dollars:** H2 revenue $1,655M (+2.3% YoY, H2/H1 = 1.13x), H2 comp held at $1,020M → FY revenue $3,114M, FY comp $2,040M, **FY ratio 65.5%** — identical to FY2025.
- Under that scenario, FY2026 adjusted operating income ≈ $3,114M − $2,040M − ~$660M non-comp = **~$414M**, versus $432M in FY2025.

Carrying that through: $414M operating income less ~$88M interest expense = ~$326M pretax; at a high-20s tax rate and after non-controlling interests, roughly $230M attributable, or **$2.14 on 107.3M diluted shares.** Consensus FY2026 of $2.23 sits just above this. The compensation-ratio panic is therefore substantially misplaced, but the destination is a flat-to-slightly-down earnings year, not a recovery. Management declined to guide the full-year ratio, deferring to the Q3 report.

### What FY2027 consensus actually requires

The bull case rests on $3.85 of FY2027 EPS, at which the stock is 11.0x. Reverse-engineering that figure: ~$413M of net income on ~107M shares requires roughly $660-700M of adjusted operating income, up more than 60% from our FY2026 estimate. One internally consistent path is revenue of ~$3,580M (+15%) with the comp ratio falling to 61% and non-comp at ~$700M, producing $696M operating income and roughly $4.00 of EPS. That requires 15% revenue growth and a 450bp compensation-ratio reduction in the same year. Orszag has said the combination "suggests a significant decline in '27 and '28 in the comp ratio," and the announced pipeline supports the revenue leg. But consensus has cut FY2027 15.4% in 90 days precisely because the market is marking down the probability of both legs landing together.

### Balance sheet and cash

$1.7B of Lazard Group senior notes maturing $500M (2028), $500M (2029), $400M (2031), $300M (2035) — **no maturity before 2028**, a genuine strength. Cash $1,099.8M at June 30, 2026, down from $1,469.4M at December 31, 2025. Net debt therefore ~$600M plus ~$463M of lease liabilities. FY2025 interest expense of $88M against $432M adjusted operating income implies ~4.9x coverage. Book equity attributable is $914.3M, or $9.38 per share — a 4.5x price-to-book that offers no asset support, which is normal for an asset-light advisory model but removes a downside floor. We regard the 1.21 Altman Z-score as uninformative for this issuer type; it penalizes thin equity and low retained earnings/assets, both structurally normal after decades of distributions.

H1 operating cash flow was −$15.9M, structurally negative because prior-year bonuses are paid in Q1 (accrued compensation fell from $794.8M to $365.8M). This is not a run-rate and should not be annualized; the $152M year-over-year improvement is a favorable working-capital signal.

### Capital returns and dilution — the under-appreciated economics

FY2025 dividends of $187M plus $91M of buybacks totaled $278M against $237M of net income — **117% of earnings.** H1 2026 dividends of $96.2M against $105.7M of attributable net income is 91%, but $75.5M of that income was the pre-tax non-cash Edgewater disposition gain; excluding it, dividends materially exceeded earnings.

Share-based compensation amortization is running 4-4.5x buybacks. FY2025 $360M versus $91M (3.96x); H1 2026 $233.3M versus $51.7M (4.5x, at a $43.79 average repurchase price). Basic weighted shares rose 88.99M (2023) → 93.1M (2024) → 97.5M (2025), and to 101.36M in Q2 2026 from 97.53M (+3.9%). Diluted rose to 107.34M from 104.91M. The new authorization ($57.3M remaining plus $200M added July 22, 2026, through December 2028) equates to roughly 6.0M shares at current prices — about 6.2% of outstanding over 2.5 years — against H1 2026 RSU grants of 8,484,681 units at a $53.88 weighted-average grant-date fair value (~$457M in H1 grant activity). Per-share earnings growth must clear a 2-4% annual dilution headwind before operating improvement counts.

On contingent dilution: reported diluted shares of 107.34M are the current fully diluted capitalization. Unvested awards of 26.10M units (19.88M RSUs plus 6.22M PIPRs, equal to 26.8% of shares outstanding) are a **maximum contingent figure**, a meaningful portion of which is already in the diluted count and roughly 42% of which is withheld for taxes at settlement rather than issued. Shareholders approved 25,000,000 additional plan shares on May 21, 2026, taking authorization to 95,000,000.

## Growth Analysis

**Asset Management has genuinely inflected, with a fragile Q2.** AUM of $284.65B is up 15% year over year and 10% sequentially. H1 net inflows of $7.4B were characterized by management as the best first half in nearly 20 years, and average AUM rose 17% in Q2, driving management fees of $310M (+23%). Two qualifications from the filings: Q2 was net *outflows* of $1.6B, implying Q1 was roughly +$9.0B; and FY2024/FY2025 net outflows of $35.7B and $18.1B mean cumulative two-year outflows of $53.8B, against which H1 recovers 14%. Margin quality also lagged growth — the AM adjusted operating margin fell to 22.5% in Q2 from 24.3%, as segment compensation outgrew revenue. The product engines are specific: the systematic Advantage platform more than doubled to $50B; the US ETF platform crossed $1B in February 2026 and $2B by July, with three more active ETF registrations filed in Q2 including first fixed-income products. The ETF platform remains under 1% of AUM.

**Advisory has a forward indicator and a current-period problem, and they point in opposite directions.** Orszag stated Financial Advisory achieved its strongest *announced* league-table position since 2014, with conflict clearances up ~40% year over year (over 100% for deals above $5B) and a weighted 2027 pipeline more than double. Revenue is recognized on *completion*, so announced strength is a 2027 revenue indicator while the completion decline is a 2026 revenue fact. The reconciliation is legitimate, but announced league tables credit full transaction value to each named advisor, so a single mandate such as lead advisor to NextEra on a ~$420B enterprise-value combination can transform a ranking without a proportionate fee. Conflict clearances and the "weighted pipeline" are company-defined, unaudited metrics with no cross-firm comparability.

Productivity is moving the wrong way. FA Managing Directors were 226 at June 30, 2026 versus 204 a year earlier (+11%), while H1 FA revenue fell 7%. Half-year revenue per FA MD was $3.55M versus $4.22M — down 15.9% (period-end MD counts, and H2 is seasonally stronger, so this overstates the full-year decline). On FY2025 figures, revenue per FA MD was $8.45M. Reaching $10M on 248 MDs implies $2.48B of FA revenue, +35.9% versus FY2025, a ~10.8% CAGR. There is also an unreconciled count: management cited "238" advisory MDs at the end of Q1 against the 10-Q's segment table, most plausibly a definitional difference involving signed-but-not-yet-joined bankers on garden leave.

Campbell Lutyens is the most coherent piece of the growth story. With sponsor M&A subdued because portfolio marks and bid prices have not converged, the release valve is continuation vehicles and secondaries — precisely Campbell Lutyens' franchise. Buying counter-cyclical exposure to the stress suppressing your core sponsor-M&A revenue is defensible capital allocation. Management expects 2027 accretion excluding synergies. A widely circulated "$500M Lazard CL 2027 revenue plan" figure appears in no filing we could verify and its scope (combined unit versus standalone) is unresolved; we do not carry it.

Estimate revisions are the dominant near-term growth signal, and they are negative. Current quarter −18.1% (30d), current year −22.2% (30d) and −18.9% (90d), next year −13.4% (30d) and −15.4% (90d). Next quarter is the sole positive (+3.6% on 90d). This is the inverse of the pattern that would support a constructive rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory in the core franchise is negative. Completed industry M&A value rose 24% in FY2025 and 29% in H1 2026; Lazard's completions above $500M fell 14% and 19% respectively. The 10-K itself names "our share of the M&A market" as a forward-looking risk, and acknowledges "intense fee competition" with expectations of continued pricing pressure as competitors reduce fees to gain share. It is candid that "there are relatively few barriers to entry impeding the launch of new asset management and financial advisory firms."

Offsetting genuine strengths: a 178-year brand and unusually deep non-US footprint (London was the standout advisory performer in Q2); no balance-sheet lending business, removing the conflicts that constrain bulge-bracket competitors; and an elite restructuring/liability-management franchise that delivered its best first half in almost a decade while the industry's completed restructuring activity fell 46-47% by both value and count. That is share gain in a contracting pool, and it provides a natural hedge, since the credit conditions that would suppress M&A would expand this line. Management noted the mix is skewed to liability management rather than traditional Chapter 11, and the fee economics of that shift are not quantified.

In Asset Management, Lazard faces the active-to-passive migration directly: the 10-K states all its strategies are actively managed and concedes that its relative-value/quality style may underperform in "a prolonged growth environment, as we have seen over the last several years." The systematic Advantage platform and active-ETF wrapper are credible responses, though still small relative to $284.65B of AUM.

## Risk Assessment

**Completed-M&A share loss (highest conviction, filing-sourced).** Measured in Lazard's own 10-Q table, not inferred. This is the risk most directly threatening the 2028 revenue-per-MD target and the FY2027 consensus.

**Compensation ratio and Q3 guidance binary.** Management explicitly deferred full-year commentary to the Q3 report. Even our favorable scenario produces flat FY2026 operating income. A Q3 disclosure that the FY ratio will exceed 66-67% would compress FY2027 estimates further.

**Dividend coverage.** $2.00 annualized on 97.45M shares is ~$195M against FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.23 — a 90% payout on adjusted earnings, and above GAAP earnings on our estimate. Coverage in H1 depended on the $75.5M Edgewater gain, and cash fell $370M over the half. The board reaffirmed the dividend and added $200M of buyback capacity on July 22, which signals confidence, though boards are not disinterested observers.

**Campbell Lutyens consideration mix and integration.** Up to $660M is 14-16% of market capitalization. The stock/cash/loan-note split is undisclosed. Illustratively, if half the $460M initial tranche were stock at roughly the announcement-period price of ~$50, that is 4.6M shares — 4.7% of outstanding and 4.5% post-issuance ownership dilution; if the entire initial tranche were stock, 9.2M shares, 9.4% and 8.6% respectively. These are sensitivities on assumed mixes, not estimates. $11.2M of transaction expense was booked in H1 before closing, and the earn-out is conditioned on continuing employment, making economics dependent on banker retention.

**Monthly AUM disclosure as recurring event risk.** Shares fell 9.1% intraday on June 10, 2026 when preliminary May AUM showed $1.4B of net outflows — a single month that accounted for most of Q2's $1.6B outflow. This mechanic converts a routine data point into a high-frequency price catalyst. Roughly 67% of AUM carries generally unhedged FX exposure.

**AI as a demand-side threat, disclosed by the company.** The FY2025 10-K states that "our clients or other third parties may use AI to perform some functions that historically drove demand for our services," and separately that competitors adopting AI more effectively could reduce Lazard's competitiveness and increase pricing pressure. This sits awkwardly beside management's aspiration to be the leading AI-enabled independent financial firm. Lazard is a commercial customer of third-party model providers (multi-model, inside its firewall, per Orszag) and does not develop these models. Risk-factor expansion often reflects defensive drafting rather than a changed internal assessment, and we cannot distinguish the two from the filing.

**Governance and alignment.** Combined CEO/Chairman role. ~$70M of cumulative senior-management transition expense (FY2025 $50.1M plus H1 2026 $19.4M). Five-year cumulative TSR of +46.6% versus the proxy peer group's +102.9% — a 56-percentage-point gap inclusive of Lazard's substantial dividend, and the cleanest external verdict on the 2030 plan to date. Two of the four disclosed performance measures (adjusted operating margin, share price) are currently moving adversely. Related-party Tax Receivable Agreement with LTBP Trust, whose owners include an executive officer, carries the disclosed risk of payments exceeding realized cash tax savings if the IRS successfully challenges basis increases. Lazard, Inc. has no independent means of generating revenue and depends on distributions from regulated subsidiaries.

**Insider activity.** Form 4s show net selling — Orszag 67,170 shares at ~$40.73 plus 2,000 at ~$41.66 in March 2026, and 41,502 in a disposition to issuer at $40.82; Hogbin 11,829 shares. Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted May/June 2026 by Orszag (84,103 shares), Farr, Hogbin, and Weideman state explicitly that sales are intended to cover estimated taxes and personal expenditures. Given a heavily equity-weighted pay package, we read this as routine tax-driven liquidity rather than a directional signal. The absence of any open-market purchase at a multi-quarter-low price is a mild negative, but absence of buying is weak evidence and we do not weight it heavily.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress and confirming the fundamental caution. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.3% sits 10.2 points above 41.1% trailing one-year realized volatility, at the 66th percentile of the current chain, in **backwardation** (51.3% near versus 48.0% at six months). Put/call skew of 13.3% indicates elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 8.25 against an open-interest ratio of 1.15 indicates fresh directional put buying rather than legacy hedges. The nearest expiry prices a ±7.1% move over ten days; the September tenor prices ±10.3%. Combined with 12.7% short interest at 3.7 days to cover, positioning is bearish but not so crowded that a squeeze dominates the setup. For a prospective buyer, rich near-term volatility argues against paying up for optionality; for a holder, it argues that the Q3 compensation-ratio disclosure is the identified catalyst the market is hedging.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($56, +31.9%)

Announced league-table position is the best since 2014, conflict clearances are up ~40% year over year and more than 100% for deals above $5B, and the weighted 2027 pipeline is more than double — all of which converts to revenue on *completion* in 2027, not 2026. The H1 compensation ratio is a demonstrable accrual artifact; holding H2 dollars flat produces a 65.5% full-year ratio identical to FY2025, and Orszag has pointed to a significant decline in 2027-2028 as fixed-comp absorption from three years of hiring (over 90 hires since 2023 against 40% MD turnover) rolls into revenue. Asset Management has turned: AUM +15%, the systematic Advantage platform doubled to $50B, ETFs at $2B under a year from launch, and the restructuring franchise is taking share in a pool down 46%. Campbell Lutyens buys counter-cyclical exposure to secondaries and continuation-fund demand created by the same sponsor-exit gridlock suppressing Lazard's core, with 2027 accretion expected before synergies. Add a 4.71% yield, no debt maturity before 2028, $1.1B of cash, and a replenished $257M buyback authorization, and a return toward 14x $4.00 of 2027 earnings produces $56.

### Bear Case ($32, −24.6%)

Lazard is structurally losing completed-M&A share — 19% fewer large completions in H1 against a 14% larger market, following 14% fewer in FY2025 against a 14% larger market — while MD headcount rose 11% and half-year revenue per FA MD fell 15.9%. That combination is fixed-cost expansion into a shrinking revenue base, which is what produced an 8.3% Q2 operating margin. Consensus is cutting hard (FY2026 −22.2% in 30 days, FY2027 −15.4% in 90 days) after two consecutive misses, and the FY2027 number still requires two simultaneous large improvements. The equity base is expanding: share-based compensation runs 4-4.5x buybacks, basic weighted shares rose 3.9% year over year, 25M additional plan shares were authorized in May, and Campbell Lutyens will issue stock in undisclosed size. Capital returns exceeded FY2025 earnings by 17%, H1 dividend coverage depended on a one-time non-cash gain, and cash fell $370M. Five-year TSR trails the company's own peer group by 56 points. If 2027 conversion slips or the Q3 report signals a comp ratio above 66%, FY2027 estimates move toward $2.60 and the stock revisits the low $30s, with the dividend itself in question.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Expected total return of roughly 6% over twelve months (probability-weighted +2% price, ~4% dividend) is inadequate compensation against a −24.6% bear case on a name with 51% implied volatility, negative estimate revisions of 13-22%, and two consecutive earnings misses.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than into the Q3 print. The stock has traded between $40.11 and $45.62 since the July 23 report; use rallies toward the mid-$40s to reduce. Holders who own the position primarily for the 4.71% yield may retain a partial position through the Q3 report (expected late October 2026), which is the dated catalyst that resolves the full-year compensation-ratio question management explicitly deferred. Residual exposure should be sized on the assumption that the dividend, while currently reaffirmed, is covered on adjusted earnings only at a 90% payout.

**Scenario targets (basis $42.45):**
- **Base $45 (+6.0%)** — 14x haircut FY2027 EPS of $3.10-3.20, cross-checked against the $44.50 analyst median.
- **Bull $56 (+31.9%)** — 14x FY2027 EPS of $4.00, requiring ~15% revenue growth and a comp ratio near 61%.
- **Bear $32 (−24.6%)** — 12.5x FY2027 EPS of $2.60 with dividend coverage under review.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD or better).** (1) The Q3 report confirms a full-year adjusted compensation ratio at or below 66% with FY2026 adjusted operating income at or above $410M. (2) Completed M&A transactions above $500M stabilize or grow year over year in the Q3 and Q4 disclosures, reversing the 19% H1 decline. (3) Campbell Lutyens closes with disclosed consideration in which stock represents less than roughly one-third of the initial tranche, capping dilution below ~3%. (4) A price near $36-37 would restore adequate margin of safety to the $45 base case even without operating improvement.

**Avoid triggers (downgrade to AVOID).** (1) A full-year compensation ratio guided above 68%, implying FY2026 adjusted operating income below $360M. (2) Two consecutive quarters of Asset Management net outflows exceeding $3B, reversing the H1 flow inflection. (3) Any reduction of the $0.50 quarterly dividend, which would confirm the coverage concern and remove the primary support for the current price. (4) Consensus FY2027 EPS falling below $3.00, at which point the 11x optical cheapness disappears.

**Reassessment timeline.** Late October 2026 (Q3 2026 results and the deferred full-year compensation-ratio commentary), with interim monitoring of monthly AUM disclosures — a mechanic that has already produced a 9.1% single-day move in 2026 — and the Campbell Lutyens closing 8-K for the consideration mix.

## Conclusion

The compensation-ratio headline that dominated Lazard's Q2 report is largely an accrual artifact, and the tax rate genuinely is anomalous. Neither fixes the underlying problem, which is measured in Lazard's own filings: the firm completed 19% fewer large M&A transactions in H1 2026 than a year earlier while the industry completed 14% more, having completed 14% fewer in FY2025 against the same 14% industry increase. Advisory MD headcount rose 11% over that period. Fixed compensation expanding into a contracting revenue base is what produced an 8.3% quarterly operating margin and a 60% decline in operating income, and it is why consensus cut FY2026 EPS 22.2% in thirty days.

The offsets are real and should not be dismissed: a best-since-2014 announced league-table position that converts to revenue in 2027, an Asset Management franchise with its best first-half flows in nearly two decades, a restructuring business taking share in a pool down 46%, no debt maturity before 2028, and a strategically coherent counter-cyclical acquisition in secondaries advisory. Those support the bull case toward $56.
