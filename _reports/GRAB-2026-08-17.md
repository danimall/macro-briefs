## Executive Summary

Grab has reached sustained profitability. FY2025 was the first full year of positive GAAP operating income ($80M on $3,370M of revenue) and positive net income ($268M), and the trailing-twelve-month figures through June 2026 show revenue of $3,731M (+21.5%), operating income of $138M and net income of $598M. Adjusted EBITDA rose 54% year over year in Q2 2026 to $168M, a 16.9% margin against 13.3% a year earlier, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $4.10–4.15B of revenue and $720M of adjusted EBITDA. This is an improving business trading 45% below its September 2025 high with unanimous Buy ratings from 26 sell-side firms and a consensus mean target of $5.86.

Two things prevent us from treating that as a buy setup. First, earnings quality. Q2 2026 net income of roughly $252M includes a **$307M one-time gain on consolidating Indonesia's Superbank**, which is non-cash by construction under IFRS 3. Strip it out and the quarter was approximately break-even to modestly negative on a GAAP basis, and trailing net income falls from $598M to roughly $291M, moving the trailing multiple from ~33x to somewhere in the 40–51x range. Operating cash flow deteriorated from +$852M in FY2024 to +$79M in FY2025 to **negative $60M** on a trailing basis, with trailing free cash flow of **negative $160M**. The most plausible driver is the digital bank's loan book scaling from a low base to $2.3B (guided above $3B by year-end), with originations flowing through operating activities. That framing makes negative cash flow a growth-funding phenomenon rather than an operating collapse, but it is still cash unavailable to shareholders and the consumption rate is accelerating.

Second, the regulatory question the market is pricing. On May 1, 2026 Indonesia's president announced a cut in the maximum commission ride-hailing platforms may take from drivers from 20% to 8%; on June 23, 2026 Grab and GoTo both committed to the new level for two-wheeler drivers. Management frames exposure as roughly 6% of *mobility* GMV, which is silent on Deliveries — 53% of total revenue — where Indonesian two-wheeler couriers also operate. The commitment landed one week before quarter-end, so Q2 results contain almost none of the effect. Q3 2026 is the first clean quarter, and the current-quarter consensus EPS estimate has already been cut 23% over the past 30 days. Base case fair value is $4.25 (+17%), bull case $5.75 (+58%), bear case $2.90 (−20%). Probability-weighted return is positive at roughly +18%, but with base-case upside barely exceeding bear-case downside and the decisive fact unverifiable until November, this is a hold.

## Company Overview

Grab operates a Southeast Asian on-demand and financial services platform across three reported segments. Trailing-twelve-month revenue mix: **Deliveries $1,987M (53%), Mobility $1,310M (35%), Financial Services $430M (11.5%)**, with a de minimis "Other" line. Financial Services has grown from 4.5% of revenue in FY2022 to 11.5% today and is the fastest-growing line at +59% year over year in Q2 2026.

Scale metrics from the Q2 2026 earnings call (secondary sourcing — see Risk Assessment): on-demand GMV of $6.5B (+21% y/y), a record 54 million monthly transacting users, monthly active drivers +19%, and over 1 million subscription members. The company spent **$706M on incentives in the quarter**, roughly 71% of revenue, which is the clearest available evidence that this is a subsidy-intensive marketplace rather than the asset-light software business its industry classification implies.

The structural feature distinguishing Grab from a pure ride-hail comparable is that it now consolidates a licensed digital bank. Superbank serves over 7.4 million customers at roughly 5.7% pre-tax ROE and a ~55% cost-to-income ratio, with management guiding cost-to-income below 50%. Grab also completed an acquisition of a wealth platform reported as "Stash," bringing total AUM to approximately $5.5B. The legal identity, price and closing date of that target could not be confirmed from any Grab filing.

A methodological caveat: Grab is a foreign private issuer and files Form 20-F annually (most recent March 6, 2026 for FY2025) plus Form 6-K interim reports. Direct retrieval of the 20-F text failed, so risk factors, the audited cash-flow statement, the liquidity note, segment footnotes and debt covenants were not read. Financial statement figures below come from a structured aggregator of SEC filings, which is reliable for magnitudes and trends but visibly imperfect in line-item standardization. Operating metrics come from secondary reporting of the Q2 call. Where a figure is unverified we say so.

Three observations drive our assessment.

**Revenue growth has stabilized, gross margin has plateaued.** Growth has run 18.6%, 20.5% and 21.5% across the last three periods — durable but no longer accelerating. Gross margin expanded from 3.2% (FY2022) to 40.0% (FY2024) and has since flattened at 39.7–40.5%. The margin story from here is operating leverage on fixed costs, not further gross-margin expansion.

**The income statement and the cash flow statement have diverged sharply.** Net income improved $703M from FY2024 to trailing June 2026 while operating cash flow deteriorated $912M over the same span. FCF margin moved from +27.7% to −4.3%. Two mechanisms are visible and both are plausible. The first is the $307M non-cash Superbank remeasurement gain, corroborated by a **−$432M "Other Operating Activities" line in Q2 2026** versus −$37M in Q1, which is the character of add-back one expects when reversing a large non-cash gain. The second is the bank balance sheet: "Change in Other Net Operating Assets" swung from **+$549M in FY2024 to −$451M in FY2025 to −$356M in H1 2026**, while standardized receivables rose from $663M to $1,034M to $1,788M and the gross loan portfolio reached $2.3B. A digital bank originating loans that sit in operating activities will produce this pattern.

We cannot adjudicate between the two without the audited statement, and the honest conclusion is narrower than "cash generation collapsed": reported free cash flow has been negative on a trailing basis while GAAP net income turned sharply positive, and the reconciliation runs through a non-cash consolidation gain plus banking working capital we could not verify line by line. Grab's own adjusted free cash flow measure — which most platform-plus-bank companies define to exclude loan and deposit flows — may show a materially better picture. We could not obtain it. We do not infer anything about management intent from the accounting; consolidating a subsidiary and recognizing a remeasurement gain on the previously held stake is required treatment, not a discretionary choice.

**The balance sheet is strong but less liquid than it looks.**

Net cash of $4,499M against 4,079M shares equals **$1.10 per share, roughly 30% of the $3.63 price**. Using the provided $14.4B market cap, enterprise value is approximately **$9.9B, or 2.65x TTM revenue and 2.40x the FY2026 guidance midpoint**. That is the strongest single argument for the stock.

The caveat matters. A meaningful and unquantified portion of the $6,532M sits inside consolidated banks as regulatory capital and as the asset side backing roughly $2.5B of what is most likely customer deposits. Cash held against depositor claims is not distributable. The jump in minority interest from $29M to $437M confirms the consolidated bank is only partly owned. Net cash should be treated as an upper bound on distributable liquidity, and EV/sales of 2.65x as a flattering floor. Total debt rose from $364M to $1,593M during FY2025 via a $1,558M issuance in Q2 2025, consistent with the widely reported ~$1.5B convertible notes, but coupon, maturity and conversion price are unverified, so we cannot state whether that instrument is economically debt at $3.63 or latent equity.

**Capital returns.** Repurchases of $274M (Q2 2025) and $400M (Q1 2026) preceded a new $750M authorization announced with Q2, lifting the total to $1.75B. Against stock-based compensation of $278M over the last four quarters, share count went 4,070M (FY2024) → 4,098M (FY2025) → 4,079M (Jun'26). Roughly $674M of buybacks reduced the base about 0.5% from its peak and left it above FY2024. This should be modelled as dilution neutralization with modest shrinkage, not as per-share value acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is real and its composition is deteriorating in one specific way that matters.

**Volume is compounding faster than revenue.** In Q2 2026, mobility transactions grew 28% and GMV grew 18% while mobility revenue grew only 12%. That 16-point wedge between transaction growth and revenue growth is take-rate compression, and management attributed it to lower average ticket sizes, country/product mix, popularity of saver products, and elevated driver incentives. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin held at 8.6%, inside the historical 8.5–9.0% band, so profitability per revenue dollar was preserved even as revenue per unit of activity fell. Two readings fit: Grab is buying volume with price, or Grab is deliberately expanding down-market to build frequency. The +42% GrabMart user growth and record 54M MTUs support the second reading. The distinguishing evidence — whether take rate stabilizes once the saver mix matures — is not yet observable.

**Grocery and financial services are the genuine expansion vectors.** GrabMart users grew 42% with grocery GMV growing roughly 1.7x the pace of food delivery, from a base where grocery reaches only ~14% of food users. That is real runway, though grocery is structurally lower take rate and higher basket, which is another reason revenue will keep growing slower than GMV. Financial Services revenue grew 59% to $134M with the loan book nearly tripling to $2.3B and guided above $3B by year-end; management targets segment adjusted EBITDA break-even in H2 2026.

**Guidance is partly inorganic.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $4.10–4.15B against FY2025's $3,370M implies 21.7–23.1% growth, and FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance rose from $700M to $720M. Management stated explicitly that the raise incorporates newly consolidated Superbank and Stash plus FX assumptions. It should not be read as pure organic momentum.

**On the estimate revision signal.** Current-year consensus EPS moved from $0.08 to $0.13 over 30 days (+59.2%) and from $0.09 over 90 days (+44.6%) — nominally the kind of revision velocity that precedes re-rating. We are discounting it, for three reasons. The current-year revision is almost certainly mechanical incorporation of the $307M one-time gain into FY2026 GAAP EPS, not a fundamental inflection. **Next-year estimates are flat: +0.4% over 30 days and +1.2% over 90 days at $0.14.** A genuine earnings inflection lifts out-year estimates too; this one did not. And the current-quarter estimate was cut 23% in the last 30 days, from $0.03 to $0.02, which reads as analysts marking down Q3 for the first clean quarter under the Indonesian commission cap. The revision table also carries only 1–4 contributing analysts for a company with 26 ratings, so precision is low.

## Valuation Assessment

Four framings, deliberately reconciled rather than cherry-picked.

**Enterprise value to revenue: supportive.** $9.9B EV on $3,731M TTM revenue = **2.65x**; on the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $4,125M = **2.40x**. For a business compounding revenue above 20% with expanding EBITDA margin, that is not a stretched multiple. Flagged caveat: EV is understated to the extent bank-held cash is non-distributable.

**Enterprise value to adjusted EBITDA: reasonable, but adjusted EBITDA flatters.** $9.9B / $720M FY2026 guidance = **13.8x**. Deduct stock-based compensation of roughly $280M annualized and capex of roughly $100M and the cash-earnings denominator falls to about $340M, implying **~29x**. That gap is the honest measure of how much of the EBITDA improvement is economically available.

**Earnings multiples: expensive on cleaned numbers.** TTM EPS of $0.11 gives ~33x. Removing the full $307M gain leaves trailing net income near $291M, EPS near $0.071, and a multiple near **51x** — an upper bound, since any tax or NCI allocation against the gain would reduce it (a 40–51x range is the fair characterization). Against FY2027 consensus of $0.14 the stock trades at **25.9x**, which is defensible if that $0.14 is genuinely organic — plausible, since FY2026's $0.13 is gain-inflated, implying underlying earnings roughly triple into FY2027. But those out-year estimates have not moved in 90 days, so consensus is not underwriting acceleration either.

A structural note on earnings composition: FY2025 operating income was $80M against net income of $268M. Most of that gap is interest income on $6.5B of cash and investments. In the current higher-for-longer rate regime that float income is supported, which is a modest tailwind. It also means a meaningful slice of GAAP profitability is rate-sensitive rather than operating.

**Analyst targets.** Consensus mean $5.86, median $5.90, low $4.60, high $8.00 — the low target sits 27% above spot, with 26 unanimous Buys. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple we would treat consensus targets as a floor. Here we do not, because the coexistence of unanimous bullishness with a share price 14% off its 52-week low is itself the diagnostic: the sell side is modelling the quantified 2026 impact of the Indonesian cap, which is small, while the market is pricing the unquantifiable precedent, which is large. When those two things disagree this sharply, the target distribution is stale information, not independent confirmation.

**Net-cash support.** Roughly $1.10 per share of gross net cash against a $3.63 price does provide a genuine floor mechanism, and the remaining ~$1.35B of buyback authorization could retire about 9% of the share base at current prices if fully executed from distributable funds. This is the most durable downside support in the story.

Net: not cheap on cleaned earnings, not expensive on EV/sales, no identified mispricing sufficient to force a re-rating. Valuation score 5.3.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining 2026 dynamic in Southeast Asian on-demand is regulatory transfer of platform economics to gig workers. The Indonesian cap applies to **GoTo and Grab simultaneously**, which cuts both ways. It compresses industry take rates symmetrically, so no competitor gains relative advantage, and it may reduce incentive competition since neither platform can undercut on commission. But it establishes a political precedent: a presidential announcement demonstrated that commission structures are subject to executive intervention. Malaysia, Vietnam, the Philippines and Thailand are plausible followers. This is a durable multiple-compression argument independent of any single quarter's numbers, and we judge it the most likely explanation for the divergence between unanimous sell-side ratings and a share price near its low.

On the demand side, competition against GoTo in Indonesia and Sea Limited's ShopeeFood region-wide is being fought on affordability rather than take rate. Grab's own disclosure that saver-product popularity is compressing take rate is that competitive equilibrium described from the inside: grow transactions ~28%, GMV ~18%, revenue ~12%, hold segment EBITDA margin at ~8.6% by squeezing cost per transaction. That works only while cost per transaction keeps falling, which is the claim Grab's AI narrative is deployed to support. Management cited trillions of tokens processed monthly, cost per interaction roughly halved while interactions rose ~10x, autonomous coding agents cutting time-to-market up to 30%, and an internal platform saving sales teams ~40,000 hours per quarter. These are activity and internal-productivity metrics, not audited cost savings. The observable outcome is 360bps of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, which cannot be attributed to AI specifically on this evidence; halved cost per interaction against 10x interaction volume means total AI spend rose.

Financial services is the more favourable competitive set. Licensed digital banking in Indonesia and Singapore carries high regulatory barriers, and Grab's edge is distribution (54M MTUs, 7.4M Superbank customers) plus proprietary transaction data for underwriting. The economics are not yet attractive: 5.7% pre-tax ROE is well below any plausible cost of equity, and a ~55% cost-to-income ratio is high for a digital bank. This is the segment that justifies a super-app multiple and also the segment consuming the cash.

Competitive trajectory: leading regional position, share gains in grocery, but take rate compressing and the regulator actively transferring economics to drivers. Score 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Indonesian commission cap — scope unresolved.** Management's "~6% of mobility GMV" is a mobility-only denominator. Indonesian two-wheeler drivers perform food and parcel delivery as well as passenger trips, and Reuters' wording ("per-trip commissions taken from their two-wheeled drivers") does not clarify coverage. If the cap extends to delivery couriers, the exposed base is a large share of Deliveries GMV in Grab's most populous market, and Deliveries is 53% of revenue. If confined to passenger transport, the direct impact is low tens of millions annually against $1.31B of mobility revenue — immaterial. We could not resolve this from any primary source. Because the commitment came on June 23, Q2 captured roughly one week of the new structure; the Q2 beat and guidance raise are close to uninformative about this risk.

**Fuel and driver economics.** Management flagged elevated Southeast Asian fuel prices and roughly $7M of targeted driver support since March 2026, already in guidance. The direct cost is trivial against $706M of quarterly incentives. The indirect risk is that driver earnings rose only 4% while active drivers rose 19% — earnings per driver appear to be falling, which is the precondition for supply attrition or further political intervention. [unverified — the specific macro cause of elevated regional fuel prices is not established in the sources; management described elevated fuel prices as a near-term headwind without stated cause.]

**Banking risk now on balance sheet.** Consolidating Superbank imports credit, capital-adequacy and deposit-run risk. A loan book tripling to $2.3B and guided above $3B within six months is growing far faster than any seasoning cycle, so reported credit quality at this stage is close to uninformative.

**FX.** Management quantified ASEAN currency weakness as a 2–3% drag on reported results. Grab reports in USD and earns in IDR, SGD, MYR, THB, VND and PHP. This is a permanent structural feature, not an event.

**Pending M&A.** The proposed ~US$600M foodpanda Taiwan acquisition remains subject to Taiwanese regulatory approval, with management declining to quantify upfront integration costs. Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi's resignation from Grab's board effective July 6, 2026 was reported explicitly in that context — removing an interlocking directorate with a Taiwan food-delivery competitor is the textbook step an acquirer takes to clear a competition review, which signals the review is live and non-trivial. Neither source quotes Grab attributing the departure to antitrust, so the causal link is inference.

**Governance and disclosure opacity.** Dual-class structure with the CEO holding roughly 75.0M super-voting Class B shares entrenches founder control well beyond economic ownership; Class A holders should not expect influence through the ballot. As a foreign private issuer Grab files no DEF 14A, so compensation, pay-for-performance alignment and related-party transactions are unexamined. Convertible note terms, the composition of the $6.5B cash balance, and the identity and price of the "Stash" acquisition are all unverified. That accumulation of unknowns is itself a risk input.

Risk factors score 4.2 — multiple binaries, at least one of which (cap scope) is both material and unquantifiable from available evidence.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.1% against 37.3% one-year realized volatility is rich by 10.7 points, so the market is paying up for uncertainty. Positioning, however, is bullish: put/call volume ratio 0.15, put/call open interest 0.70 (down sharply from 1.63 in early June), and call-heavy skew across all tenors. Term structure is flat at 48.1% near and far, with a 26% implied move over six months — consistent with a market that sees a genuine binary but does not know its timing. The put/call OI drop from 1.63 to 0.70 alongside a 6.7% price gain since June says bearish hedges have been unwound and speculative call interest has replaced them. That diverges from our neutral fundamental view: options positioning is more constructive than we are. It also means the stock has less absorbed protection heading into the Q3 print than it did two months ago. Short interest of 7.9% of float with 6 days to cover is moderate — neither a crowded short nor evidence of informed bearish conviction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($5.75, +58%)

The Indonesian cap proves confined to two-wheeler passenger transport, validating management's 6%-of-mobility-GMV framing, and Q3 shows negligible impact. Take rate stabilizes as the saver-product mix matures, so revenue growth reconverges toward GMV growth. Financial Services reaches adjusted EBITDA break-even in H2 2026 as guided, Superbank's cost-to-income ratio falls below 50%, and ROE begins climbing from 5.7% toward a cost-of-equity-covering level, at which point the loan book's cash consumption starts to look like investment rather than leakage. Grocery compounds at 1.7x food-delivery pace from 14% penetration. FY2027 revenue reaches roughly $4.8B with adjusted EBITDA near $1.05B; the market applies 18x EV/EBITDA, giving $18.9B EV plus $4.3B net cash across ~4.05B shares. The $1.35B remaining buyback authorization is executed, retiring ~9% of shares.

### Bear Case ($2.90, −20%)

The cap extends to two-wheeler delivery couriers, hitting a large fraction of Deliveries GMV in Indonesia, and Malaysia or Vietnam follow with comparable interventions. Take-rate compression proves structural rather than transitional as the mix shifts permanently toward grocery and saver products. Fuel costs stay elevated, driver earnings per head keep falling, and Grab must raise incentives above the current $706M quarterly run rate to hold supply, compressing the 8.6% mobility EBITDA margin. FCF stays negative as the loan book scales past $3B, and investors discount the whole company on cash earnings rather than adjusted EBITDA, at which point a 40–51x cleaned earnings multiple looks indefensible. The market applies 1.8x EV/sales on $4.5B of FY2027 revenue: $8.1B EV plus $4.0B net cash = $12.1B market cap.

### Base Case ($4.25, +17%)

The cap is partially scoped or partially absorbed; Q3 revenue growth decelerates from 21.7% toward the mid-to-high teens but adjusted EBITDA guidance of $720M is met, helped by the inorganic Superbank and Stash contributions. Financial Services approaches break-even a quarter or two later than guided. FY2027 revenue reaches roughly $4.8B; the market applies 2.65x EV/sales — the current multiple, unchanged — giving $12.7B EV plus $4.3B net cash over ~4.05B shares. Cross-checked against 14x EV/adjusted EBITDA on $950M FY2027 EBITDA, which yields $4.35. Base case $4.25.

Probability weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected return of approximately **+18%** over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). Expected return is positive, but base-case upside of 17% against bear-case downside of 20% is not the asymmetry that justifies adding capital ahead of an unresolvable binary, and the underlying evidence base has material gaps.

- **Base case target: $4.25** (+17%) — 2.65x EV/sales on FY2027 revenue of ~$4.8B plus $4.3B net cash over ~4.05B shares; cross-checked at 14x EV/adjusted EBITDA on $950M.
- **Bull case target: $5.75** (+58%) — 18x EV/EBITDA on $1.05B FY2027 adjusted EBITDA.
- **Bear case target: $2.90** (−20%) — 1.8x EV/sales on $4.5B FY2027 revenue.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report (expected early November 2026), the first quarter containing a full period under the 8% Indonesian commission cap.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 shows Deliveries revenue growth holding above 15% year over year *and* mobility revenue growth reconverging to within 8 points of transaction growth — evidence the cap is scoped to passenger transport and take-rate compression is mix-driven rather than permanent.
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive reported operating cash flow, or disclosure of an adjusted free cash flow definition and figure that reconciles the banking flows and shows positive underlying generation.
3. Financial Services reaching segment adjusted EBITDA break-even on schedule in H2 2026 with Superbank cost-to-income below 50%.
4. A price near $3.00–3.10, which would take EV/FY2026 revenue below 2.0x and restore base-case upside above 35%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Confirmation that the 8% commission cap covers two-wheeler food or parcel delivery, or a comparable cap announced in Malaysia, Vietnam or Thailand.
2. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $700M, or FY2026 revenue guidance reduced below the $4.10B floor.
3. Trailing operating cash flow deteriorating past negative $200M with no accompanying disclosure isolating banking loan flows.
4. Quarterly incentive spend rising above roughly 75% of revenue (from ~71% in Q2) while mobility segment EBITDA margin falls below 8.0%.

**Next reassessment: the Q3 2026 6-K**, with an interim check on any 6-K disclosing foodpanda Taiwan approval or termination, or the convertible note terms.

## Conclusion

Grab has done the hard part. Three consecutive years of 18–22% revenue growth, gross margin holding at 40%, a first full year of GAAP operating and net profitability, adjusted EBITDA up 54% year over year, and $4.5B of gross net cash against $2.0B of debt describe a company that has cleared the existential questions that dominated its 2022–2023 story. At 2.65x TTM enterprise value to revenue with roughly 30% of market cap in cash, a good deal of pessimism is already in the price after a 45% drawdown.

What holds us at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE is the intersection of three things. Trailing net income of $598M contains a $307M non-cash gain, and on cleaned numbers the stock trades at 40–51x rather than 33x, with trailing free cash flow negative $160M. The current-year estimate revision that looks like an inflection signal is mechanical incorporation of that gain, and out-year estimates have not budged in 90 days while the current-quarter estimate was cut 23%. And the single largest swing factor — whether Indonesia's commission cap reaches into Deliveries, and whether other governments follow — cannot be resolved from available evidence and will not appear in reported results until November. Twenty-six unanimous Buy ratings on a stock 14% above its 52-week low tell us the sell side is modelling the small quantified impact while the market prices the large unquantifiable precedent. On the evidence we can verify, the market's posture is the more defensible one.

Existing holders have a positive-expected-return position with genuine net-cash support and identifiable catalysts. New capital should wait for the Q3 print to resolve the commission scope, or for a price near $3.00–3.10 where the margin of safety compensates for what we cannot see.
