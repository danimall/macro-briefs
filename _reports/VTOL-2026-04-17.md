## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is the world's largest commercial helicopter operator, running 214 aircraft across 15 countries with a customer base split between offshore energy producers (~66% of revenue) and government search-and-rescue agencies (~26%). The stock has appreciated 72% over the past twelve months to ~$48.50, driven by improving offshore energy fundamentals and growing visibility into a 2026 earnings inflection. At 11.2x trailing earnings and roughly 8.9x forward earnings on FY2026E consensus of $5.43, VTOL remains meaningfully cheaper than OFS peers (median P/E 30.2x) and trades at a ~21% discount to management's third-party-appraised NAV of $60.60/share.

The 2026 catalyst is specific and well-supported: approximately half of Offshore Energy Services contracts reset this year at higher leading-edge rates, while $28M+ in Government Services transition costs (from UKSAR2G and the new Irish Coast Guard contract) roll off. Management guides to $295-325M Adjusted EBITDA, a 20-32% increase. The helicopter supply-demand balance — near 100% utilization for offshore-configured medium/heavy aircraft, OEM backlogs extending 24+ months, and fewer manufacturers building relevant models — provides structural pricing support that limits downside even in a weaker energy spending environment. Key risks include oil price cyclicality (the 10-day ceasefire and resulting oil crash to $83 is directly relevant), concentrated UK government contract exposure, and ongoing S92 parts supply disruptions. Insider selling following equity vesting is notable but appears routine.

## Company Overview

Bristow Group provides vertical flight solutions through three segments: Offshore Energy Services (OES), Government Services, and Other Services (Australian regional airline, dry leasing). The company transports personnel to and from offshore energy installations, operates search-and-rescue missions under long-term government contracts, and is positioning for future advanced air mobility opportunities. The fleet includes Sikorsky S92s (29% of aircraft), Leonardo AW139s, AW189s, and various medium/light helicopters.

The business model blends two distinct cash flow profiles. OES contracts typically last 1-5 years and are structured as Monthly Standing Charges plus Fixed Hourly Rates, providing reasonable visibility but with sensitivity to E&P activity levels. Government Services contracts last 10+ years — the UK SAR contract (UKSAR2G, ~19% of total revenue) and the new Irish Coast Guard contract (~€670M over 10 years) — providing highly visible, long-duration cash flows with lower cyclicality. The business participates in energy upside while maintaining a stable floor from government revenue.

Bristow operates as an effective oligopoly in its served markets. The offshore helicopter sector consolidated substantially during the 2014-2021 energy downturn, with competitors restructuring or exiting. Barriers to entry include Air Operator Certificate requirements in each jurisdiction, decades-long safety track records, massive capital requirements ($15-25M per medium/heavy helicopter), and limited OEM production capacity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

FY2025 revenue was $1,491M (+5.3% YoY), with operating margin expanding to 10.7% from 9.4% and net income rising 36% to $129M. The OES segment drove profitability improvement, with segment operating margins expanding from 14% to 17%. Government Services operating income collapsed from $21M to $5M due to transition costs — $28M in incremental subcontractor expenses and $15M in additional personnel costs for the IRCG and UKSAR2G ramps. This temporary drag masked the underlying earnings trajectory.

Adjusted EBITDA of $246M was in line with the $245M guidance midpoint. The EBITDA margin of 16.5% has room to expand as Government Services normalizes — management guides to segment Adjusted Operating Income of $70-80M in 2026, nearly doubling the $38M achieved in FY2025.

**Cash Flow**

Operating cash flow of $198M in FY2025 was healthy, though capital expenditures of $142M (down from $255M in the peak-investment FY2024) reduced GAAP free cash flow to $56M. The gap between GAAP FCF and Adjusted FCF of $187M is driven by growth capex (new AW189 deliveries, government contract aircraft). Maintenance capex is only $15M, implying that as fleet investment normalizes, GAAP FCF conversion should improve substantially.

**Balance Sheet**

Cash of $286M against total debt of $683M yields net debt of $397M, or 1.6x Adjusted EBITDA — down from 2.7x in 2022. The January 2026 refinancing was well-timed: $500M of 6.750% Senior Notes due 2033 replaced $400M of 6.875% Notes due 2028, extending maturity by five years, slightly reducing the coupon, and raising $100M of incremental liquidity. Total liquidity stands at $347M. No major maturities come due until the IRCG Debt facility in June 2031. The Altman Z-Score of 2.19 (grey zone) reflects the capital intensity of helicopter operations rather than genuine distress — the declining leverage, improving cash flow, and extended maturity profile all point to manageable credit risk.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern**

The trailing four quarters show three beats (Q1: +60%, Q2: +108%, Q3: +98%) and one miss (Q4: -32%). The Q4 miss — EPS of $0.61 vs. $0.89 consensus — suggests the street was running ahead of company guidance in Q4, despite FY2025 hitting the overall guidance midpoint. This pattern of large beats followed by a miss warrants caution on near-term estimate reliability.

## Growth Analysis

**FY2026 Earnings Inflection — Three Converging Drivers**

1. **OES Contract Resets:** Approximately half of OES contracts are set to renew in 2026 at leading-edge rates that are materially higher than the contracts they replace. Near-100% effective utilization for offshore-configured medium/heavy helicopters gives Bristow genuine pricing power. OEM production backlogs of 24+ months and the permanent exit of aircraft during the 2014-2021 downturn mean supply cannot respond quickly to demand growth.

2. **Government Services Transition Cost Roll-Off:** The transition costs that dragged FY2025 should largely dissipate in FY2026 as the IRCG contract reached full operational capability in February 2026 and UKSAR2G transitions through late 2026. The guided $70-80M in segment AOI vs. $38M in FY2025 is the single largest incremental earnings driver.

3. **IRCG Revenue Ramp:** The €670M Irish Coast Guard contract went fully live in February 2026, providing a new ~$65-70M annual revenue stream at government contract margins (typically 15-20% operating margins when mature).

Management guides FY2026 revenue of $1,580-1,690M (+6-13%) and Adjusted EBITDA of $295-325M (+20-32%). Consensus FY2026 EPS is $5.43, though this has been revised down 14.7% over the past 30 days — likely a hangover from the Q4 miss and general OFS sentiment pressure from lower oil prices.

**Estimate Revisions — A Yellow Flag**

Current quarter EPS estimates have been cut 16.7% in the past 30 days ($1.32 to $1.10), with FY2026 estimates down 14.7% ($6.37 to $5.43). While some of this reflects the Q4 miss recalibration, the magnitude of downward revisions during what should be an inflection year requires monitoring. Only two analysts cover the stock for EPS estimates, so individual estimate changes create large percentage swings.

**Advanced Air Mobility — Optionality, Not Revenue**

Bristow's investments in electric aviation (BETA Technologies ALIA CX300 test, Electra EL9 delivery slots for 2029-2030, Vertical Aerospace/Skyports partnership) represent low-cost optionality on a potential future market. These are correctly sized as negligible near-term financial commitments with asymmetric upside if the eVTOL market develops.

## Valuation Assessment

VTOL trades at 11.2x trailing earnings and approximately 8.9x FY2026E consensus EPS of $5.43 (on a forward P/E basis, 11.25x per the data feed, which likely reflects a different EPS base). Against the OFS peer median of 30.2x, this represents a 63-70% discount. Even accounting for Bristow's smaller market cap ($1.4B) and niche operational focus, the discount appears excessive given:

- Superior revenue visibility (long-term government contracts)
- Structural supply tightness (near-100% utilization, constrained OEM capacity)
- Improving leverage profile (1.6x net debt/EBITDA, declining)
- Credible 20-32% EBITDA growth guidance for FY2026

**NAV Analysis:** Management calculates NAV of $60.60/share based on third-party helicopter appraisals (Ascend by Cirium). At $48.50, VTOL trades at a ~21% discount to helicopter fleet replacement value. In a tight supply market, helicopter asset values are likely well-supported, making the NAV floor relatively firm.

**Analyst Targets:** The three covering analysts are unanimously Buy-rated with price targets ranging from $56 to $66, implying 15-36% upside. The median target of $60 closely matches the NAV estimate.

**DCF Cross-Check:** Using $310M Adjusted EBITDA midpoint for FY2026, 6x EV/EBITDA (conservative for an asset-heavy services business with improving margins), and $397M net debt, the implied equity value is ~$1,463M or ~$50/share — roughly in line with the current price. At 7x (closer to what the improving margin profile and government contract duration warrant), the implied value is ~$61/share.

## Competitive Landscape

The offshore helicopter market is structurally favorable for incumbents. Following the 2014-2021 downturn, the competitive field thinned substantially — PHI Group and CHC Helicopter remain the primary competitors, but neither has Bristow's combination of global scale, government contract diversification, and fleet breadth.

Supply constraints are the critical competitive dynamic. New customers face multi-year wait times for aircraft. Sikorsky and Leonardo production lines are partially allocated to military orders, further constraining civilian supply. Bristow's fleet and relationships across 15 countries represent assets that would take a competitor years and hundreds of millions of dollars to replicate.

The government SAR business provides a structural competitive advantage that is underappreciated. These contracts require demonstrated operational capability, safety records, and purpose-built infrastructure that effectively preclude new entrants. Bristow's track record operating UK SAR since 2015 and its successful IRCG award create reference cases that should support future government contract wins.

## Risk Assessment

**Oil Price Sensitivity:** The recent oil crash to $83 on the 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire is directly relevant. While Bristow's exposure is production-oriented (more resilient than exploration/drilling) and contracts have 1-5 year terms, a sustained decline to $75-85 would reduce customer urgency on new offshore projects and potentially slow the contract renewal rate improvement that underpins the 2026 thesis. Prior ceasefires have a 0-for-7 track record on durability, but any comprehensive deal extending the low-oil-price environment beyond 2-3 quarters would pressure OES fundamentals.

**Customer and Contract Concentration:** The UK SAR contract (~19% of revenue) can be terminated by the UK Department for Transport at will, subject to a cancellation fee. Nigeria represents ~13% of revenue and carries political and security risks. Losing either would be materially adverse.

**Supply Chain Disruption:** The S92 parts shortage is a live operational issue. S92s represent 29% of the fleet, and parts delays have forced aircraft groundings and forfeited business opportunities. This constrains near-term revenue capacity and increases maintenance costs.

**FX Exposure:** With 83% of revenue generated outside the US and 65% of cash held abroad, Bristow experienced a $31M earnings swing from FX movements between FY2024 and FY2025. GBP/USD is the primary exposure. A strong-dollar environment would create earnings headwinds.

**Estimate Revisions:** The 14.7% downward revision in FY2026 EPS estimates over the past 30 days is concerning during an ostensible inflection year. While driven partly by Q4 miss recalibration and thin analyst coverage (2 estimates), falling estimates should not be dismissed.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 41.0% is rich relative to 32.0% historical volatility — a 9pp spread indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. However, IV percentile at 19% within the current chain suggests this is not an extreme reading relative to VTOL's own options history. The put/call OI ratio of 0.03 is extremely call-heavy, consistent with bullish positioning. The call-heavy skew at near-term (-8.5%) flipping to put-heavy at 3-month (+11.0%) suggests near-term optimism but hedging at the 3-month horizon — roughly coinciding with the next earnings report timeline. The contango term structure (near-term 41% < long-term 43.5%) is a normal/complacent formation. Short interest at 4.2% of float with 4 days to cover is uncrowded. Overall, options positioning is mildly bullish near-term with some institutional hedging at the 3-month tenor, consistent with a stock that has run significantly and faces earnings catalyst risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60-66, +24-36%)
OES contract resets drive 200-300bp of margin improvement, Government Services AOI reaches $70-80M as transition costs dissipate, and the IRCG contract ramps to full run-rate. Adjusted EBITDA reaches $310-325M, and the stock re-rates toward NAV of $60.60/share. Tight helicopter supply-demand enables further price increases beyond 2026. Additional government contract wins (SAR mandates, defense adjacencies) extend the long-duration revenue base. Oil prices stabilize in the $85-100 range following any ceasefire resolution, supporting offshore E&P activity. Valuation: 10-12x forward earnings on $5.50-6.00 EPS = $55-72.

### Bear Case ($35-38, -22-28%)
Oil prices sustained below $80 due to a comprehensive Iran deal, reducing offshore E&P spending and delaying contract renewal rate improvements. Government Services transition costs persist beyond expectations, and the guided AOI improvement disappoints. S92 supply chain issues worsen, grounding additional aircraft and reducing revenue capacity. FX headwinds from a strong dollar erode reported earnings. Estimate revisions continue downward. The stock reverts toward the $36-38 range where it traded in September-December 2025. Valuation: 7-8x forward earnings on $4.50-5.00 EPS = $31-40.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key dates: Q1 FY2026 earnings (likely May 2026), Q2 FY2026 earnings (likely August 2026), and mid-year government contract transition updates.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **Adjusted EBITDA below $280M in FY2026** (more than 5% below guidance low-end), indicating the inflection is failing to materialize.
2. **Government Services AOI below $50M** in FY2026, suggesting transition costs are persistent rather than temporary.
3. **Oil sustained below $75 for 2+ months**, which would begin pressuring OES contract renewal rates and customer capital discipline.

## Conclusion

Bristow Group offers a compelling value proposition: a structurally advantaged operator in a tight helicopter supply market, trading at a 63-70% discount to OFS peers and a 21% discount to fleet NAV, with a well-documented 2026 earnings inflection driven by contract resets and cost normalization. The combination of 20-32% guided EBITDA growth, declining leverage, extended debt maturities, and long-duration government contracts provides a differentiated risk profile within the energy services sector.

The stock carries real risks: oil price sensitivity, concentrated contract exposure, ongoing supply chain disruption, and falling near-term estimate revisions all warrant attention. The recent 72% price appreciation means much of the re-rating has occurred, and the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. However, the valuation remains undemanding on both an earnings and asset basis, and the specific catalysts (contract resets, Government Services cost normalization, IRCG ramp) are identifiable and largely within management's control. At 8.9x FY2026E EPS with multiple paths to upside, VTOL merits a BUY recommendation with a 12-month base case target of $58.
