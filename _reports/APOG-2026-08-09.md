## Executive Summary

Apogee has rallied 41% from its January 7, 2026 low of $30.75 and now trades 14.5% below the June 26 high of $50.88. The re-rating happened while forward earnings estimates fell: management's FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.70–$3.25 sits entirely below FY2026's $3.47 actual, implying a 14.3% decline at the midpoint and a third consecutive year of contraction after $4.51 GAAP diluted EPS in FY2024, $3.89 in FY2025 and $2.52 in FY2026. At $43.52 the stock is 14.6x the guidance midpoint and roughly 8.0x pro forma enterprise value to our derived FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of about $155M. Two years ago, at peak adjusted EPS of $4.97 (FY2025), the same equity traded near 8–9x. The multiple has expanded as earnings fell, which is normal for a cyclical, but it means the recovery is already partly paid for.

The quality of the most recent quarter is the central new fact since our last review. Consolidated Q1 FY2027 adjusted EBITDA fell 6.6% year over year, but the sum of the four operating segments fell 12.2% ($32.31M vs $36.81M). The difference came from Corporate and Other improving $2.23M on what the 10-Q describes as an insurance-related benefit. The same pattern held for the full year: sum-of-segments adjusted EBITDA fell 16.6% against a 13.2% consolidated decline, with Corporate improving $9.0M largely on lower incentive compensation and risk-related insurance. Incentive-compensation accruals are an automatic stabilizer that reverses when performance recovers, which compresses the operating leverage available in an upcycle. Within the segments, Architectural Glass adjusted EBITDA margin went 22.2% (FY2025) to 16.1% (FY2026) to 8.7% (Q1 FY2027) on revenue down 25% in two years, with *price* deterioration cited in both the 10-K and the 10-Q. Architectural Metals margin did improve (7.3% to 11.2% in Q1), and Services backlog rose 8% year over year to $734.5M. The recovery pathway is real but not yet visible in consolidated numbers.

Balance-sheet risk is low. Net debt of roughly $211M against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $165.0M gives 1.28x leverage against a 3.50x maintenance covenant, interest coverage near 11.8x, $419.4M of undrawn revolver, and no maturities before July 19, 2029. Pro forma for the $105M Kalwall purchase (closed July 1, 2026), net debt is about $316M and leverage roughly 1.9x. Apogee bought Kalwall at approximately 8.2x first-year EBITDA against 12x paid for UW Solutions, and repurchased 269,500 shares at an average $35.80 in a quarter that later traded to $45.74. Those are good decisions. They do not change the arithmetic that our base case fair value sits close to the current price, that the bear case takes the stock back toward the levels it traded at seven months ago, and that management has guided the next nine months to deliver roughly $2.41 of adjusted EPS with Q2 explicitly down year over year.

## Company Overview

Apogee (Minnesota, incorporated 1949) supplies architectural building products and services for enclosing buildings, plus high-performance coated materials. Four reportable segments, with FY2026 net sales of $1,404.7M:

- **Architectural Metals — $504.0M (36%).** Aluminum window, curtainwall, storefront and entrance systems; aluminum extrusion and finishing. Tubelite, EFCO, Wausau, Linetec, Alumicor (Canada).
- **Architectural Services — $439.2M (31%).** Project management and field installation of curtainwall and façade systems under the Harmon brand; one of few national-footprint glass installers in the U.S.
- **Architectural Glass — $283.7M (19%).** Fabricated high-performance insulating, laminated and monolithic glass. Viracon and GlassecViracon (Brazil).
- **Performance Surfaces — $198.0M (14%).** Coated materials for picture framing/museum glazing, graphic arts, displays, architectural interiors and industrial flooring. Tru Vue, ResinDEK, ChromaLuxe, Unisub, RDC Coatings.

Customers are glazing subcontractors and general contractors, with specification influenced by architects and owners. Roughly 35% of revenue is longer-term fixed-price work recognized over time. Headcount was approximately 4,100 at February 28, 2026, down from 4,500 a year earlier. Warranties run 2–12 years on curtainwall, window systems and glass. The FY2026 10-K discloses no customer above 10% of revenue and carries no customer-concentration risk factor; that establishes management does not treat it as material, not that the book is demonstrably diversified.

The strategic direction under new leadership is toward "specification-driven" products — Kalwall (translucent daylighting for education, healthcare, museums) and UW Solutions (proprietary coated substrates) — and away from price-competitive volume glass.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative and management guides it to stay negative on an earnings basis through FY2027. Standalone FY2027 sales guidance of $1.38–1.43B was maintained; the range was raised to $1.43–1.48B solely to add Kalwall. Adjusted EPS guidance of $2.70–$3.25 was unchanged, because the roughly $4M of incremental interest expense from funding Kalwall offsets the accretion. Media characterizations of a "raised outlook" are imprecise; the sales increase is acquisition arithmetic.

The internal math is demanding. Q1's $0.57 is about 19% of the $2.975 midpoint, leaving roughly $2.41 across Q2–Q4 with Q2 guided down year over year and results "weighted more heavily towards the second half." Even at the low end ($2.70), the remaining nine months must deliver about $2.13. Second-half weighting is a genuine seasonal and pricing-lag feature — price increases and surcharges phase in through the year — but it concentrates risk into two prints.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than the clean positive signal a headline read suggests. Current-year consensus is $2.87, up 2.1% over 30 days but down 9.7% over 90 days. Next quarter is down 5.3% over 30 days and 15.3% over 90 days. Next year is $3.90, up 5.4% over 30 days but down 1.1% over 90 days, and rests on a single analyst. The 30-day improvement is real and follows the Q1 beat; the 90-day trend is still negative. Revision velocity does not independently support buying.

The credible growth vectors are: (1) Kalwall — approximately $85M of first-year revenue at roughly 15% adjusted EBITDA margin per management commentary, targeting 20% long term with $4M of input-cost synergies by FY2029 (these figures appear only in the earnings call, not in filings); (2) Fortify Phase 2's approximately $26M of annualized pre-tax savings against $27.4M of charges; (3) Services, with nine consecutive quarters of revenue growth and backlog up 8% year over year to $734.5M; (4) Metals margin recovery from 7.3% to 11.2%; and (5) aluminum surcharge recovery phasing through FY2027.

Against those: Glass is 19% of sales in a downcycle with price erosion and international competition the 10-K concedes can match product attributes; three of four segments depend on North American non-residential construction, where financing costs remain elevated and office demand is structurally impaired by remote work; and a meaningful slice of Performance Surfaces depends on U.S. retail custom picture framing, an unusual consumer-confidence exposure inside an industrials name. Data-center construction, the one booming category in the current capex cycle, is not an Apogee end market in any material way.

Peer medians in Building Products show a P/E of 18.8x, but the sample is unusable at the median: Quanex prints −3.0x, MasterBrand −583.5x, American Woodmark 41.1x. The comparable names are Insteel at 13.3x and Gibraltar at 18.8x. The peer table shows that every comparable trades 23–46% below its 52-week high while Apogee trades 14.5% below; Apogee has outperformed a group that is uniformly de-rating on the same non-residential and rate backdrop.

The more useful anchor is Apogee's own history. In FY2025, on adjusted EPS of $4.97, this equity traded in the low-to-mid $40s — roughly 8–9x. Market capitalization has been broadly flat between $900M and $1.0B while adjusted EPS fell 30% and guided FY2027 EPS falls another 14%. The multiple has done all the work. That is normal cyclical behavior, and it is why a 14.6x forward multiple on trough earnings is not evidence of cheapness.

Two constructive valuation supports deserve weight. First, the derived pro forma EV/EBITDA of about 8.0x on trough EBITDA is not demanding for a business with 12% ROIC at the trough. Second, the levered FCF yield of roughly 8% with a 36% dividend payout and 1.53M shares (7.3% of the count) remaining under authorization gives management the tools to compound per-share value while earnings are flat.

**Normalized-value work.** Mid-cycle adjusted EPS, blending FY2026's $3.47 with FY2025's $4.97, adding full-year Kalwall and Fortify savings and subtracting higher interest, lands near $3.75–$4.25. At 11–12x — appropriate for a small-cap cyclical with compressing ROIC, thin sell-side coverage and governance questions — that is $41–51. On EV/EBITDA, normalized pro forma EBITDA of $175–185M at 7.0–7.5x less $316M of net debt gives $43–51 per share. Both approaches bracket the current price rather than sitting above it.

We therefore set **base $45 (+3.4%), bull $54 (+24.1%), bear $32 (−26.5%)**. The consensus mean target of $44.00 (low $40, high $48) — from one to two analysts — sits essentially at spot, and at 14.6x forward on declining earnings this is not a case where consensus targets should be treated as stale floors; that treatment applies to cheap stocks with accelerating estimates, and Apogee's estimates are still net lower over 90 days.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives roughly −2.9% price return, or −0.4% including the dividend. That is the arithmetic behind the REDUCE.

## Competitive Landscape

The demand picture in the filings is bifurcated rather than uniformly weak.

Institutional and infrastructure work is holding. Architectural Services has grown nine consecutive quarters with backlog up 8% year over year, and Kalwall's end markets (education, healthcare, museums) carry municipal, state, federal and educational funding exposure. The Harmon national installation footprint is genuinely scarce — the 10-K notes Apogee is one of only a few U.S. installers with national presence — and is the segment's real moat.

Commercial office-linked premium glass is in a downcycle with a competitive component. Glass revenue is down 25% in two years and the filings cite lower *price*, not only lower volume. The CEO's characterization on the June 26 call — "there is fewer jobs. And many of those jobs are at a lower volume" — plus the 10-K's own admission that international competitors "can provide certain products with attributes similar to ours," is consistent with structural pricing pressure in fabricated premium glass rather than a purely cyclical volume trough. This matters for normalization: if Glass margin normalizes at 12–14% rather than the 20–22% of FY2024–25, roughly $20–25M of segment EBITDA does not come back.

Input costs are the acute margin issue. Aluminum is the first-named driver of FY2026's gross margin compression and is cited again in Q1 FY2027 for Metals and Performance Surfaces. Apogee hedges only $27.7M notional of aluminum against a Metals cost base of roughly $360M. Cross-border exposure compounds this: Canadian operations buy from U.S. suppliers and sell to U.S. customers, and the 10-K explicitly flags "future phases of trade negotiations with Canada." Whether the aluminum shock is a pass-through timing problem or a permanent cost-base reset is the single largest unresolved input to normalized Metals and Services margins, and we could not corroborate tariff or Midwest-premium levels independently.

Performance Surfaces' proprietary coating formulations (Tru Vue, ChromaLuxe) remain the highest-margin franchise even after UW Solutions dilution. The specification-driven pivot is strategically coherent. Whether Kalwall plus UW can offset Viracon's erosion is unresolved, and the FY2027 guide says not yet.

## Risk Assessment

**1. H2-weighted guidance concentrates miss risk into two prints.** Roughly $2.41 of adjusted EPS must come from Q2–Q4 with Q2 guided down year over year. A shortfall lands in a stock that has already priced recovery.

**2. Goodwill and intangibles are concentrated in the weakest reporting unit.** Goodwill of $236.6M plus net intangibles of $108.6M equal roughly 31% of assets and 67% of book equity. Kalwall's approximately $105M lands in Architectural Glass, the reporting unit with revenue down 25% and quarterly margin halved. The FY2026 test bypassed the qualitative screen and went straight to quantitative valuation; fair value exceeded carrying value at all four units, and disclosed sensitivities (100bp WACC increase reduces unit fair values $9–39M; 100bp lower long-term growth reduces them $11–18M) would not have changed that conclusion. The percentage cushion by unit is not disclosed. Prior impairments have recurred: $7.6M in Metals intangibles (FY2025), $11.5M inside Fortify Phase 2 (FY2026) including $5.0M in Services. The counter-argument is management's own: Kalwall's institutional, specification-driven demand may be counter-cyclical to Viracon's commercial exposure, raising projected cash flows and the cushion.

**3. Unquantified sealant product-liability tail.** Item 3 of the FY2026 10-K refers to "claims" — plural — related to a commercial sealant product formerly incorporated into Apogee products. One such claim produced a $20M arbitration award (December 2022), affirmed on appeal (March 25, 2025), and paid at $24.7M including post-judgment interest on April 7, 2025, with $9.4M hitting FY2025 cost of goods sold net of reserves and estimated insurance. The filings do not quantify remaining exposure, and the warranty reserve stands at $12.9M. Management's blanket materiality statement was also in place before the $24.7M payment.

**4. Fixed-price contract estimate revisions have turned negative.** Services net cumulative catch-up adjustments were +$16.3M (FY2025), +$8.8M (FY2026), and −$0.67M in Q1 FY2027, with gross unfavorable adjustments roughly doubling from $12.1M to $24.0M. FY2026's net +$8.8M represented about 29% of Services' $30.9M adjusted EBITDA; FY2025's +$16.3M represented about 49% of $33.5M. Reported Services margin resilience was flattered in the earlier periods. The alternative reading — that rising gross adjustments in both directions are mechanically normal for a growing long-duration book, and net near zero indicates better estimating discipline — is legitimate and we do not treat this as an accounting red flag.

**5. Corporate-cost offsets are low quality.** The insurance benefit and incentive-compensation reversals that closed the gap between segment and consolidated declines are non-repeating or self-reversing. Some portion of the Corporate improvement is durable Fortify savings, but the 10-K's ~$26M figure is enterprise-wide and unallocated, so the split cannot be determined.

**6. Governance.** Donald Nolan holds both CEO and Executive Chair titles, so the CEO chairs his own board, with no disclosure of an external search. The prior CEO separated October 31, 2025 with accelerated equity vesting and $3.0M of transition costs charged to Corporate; the filings state no reason. CFO changed January 2026, General Counsel February 2026, three of four segment presidents between August 2025 and July 2026. Proxy pay-versus-performance shows $100 invested at FY2021 year-end worth $118.17 at February 28, 2026 versus $191.65 for the peer group — 73 points of five-year underperformance, with the gap widening sharply in FY2026. Every incentive metric (adjusted EBITDA, adjusted diluted EPS, adjusted ROIC, net sales) excludes restructuring that has now recurred across FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 with a mid-year extension on January 7, 2026. Two new directors (Joseph Hayek, Suresh Krishna) joined August 5, 2026, which is a constructive signal on board refresh.

**7. Kalwall ownership completeness is unresolved.** The 10-Q describes the target as Keller Companies, Inc., "the controlling shareholder of Kalwall Corporation and Structures Unlimited Inc." Read literally, a noncontrolling interest may persist, which would mean the $85M revenue and 15% margin figures are not entirely attributable to Apogee shareholders. The more likely reading is descriptive drafting; the Q2 FY2027 10-Q will resolve it.

**8. Insider activity is neutral.** From March through August 2026 there were no open-market purchases and no discretionary open-market sales — only grants and tax-withholding dispositions at $35.47–$36.40. No one sold into the rally to $45.74, which is mildly reassuring; no one bought either. For a management team eight and four months into their roles respectively, the absence of personal capital at risk should be weighed alongside a REDUCE.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.1% sits below one-year realized volatility of 42.0% and in the 7th percentile of the current chain, following a collapse from 56.8% on June 24, a standard post-earnings vol crush. The term structure is in contango: 37.1% at 11 days, 42.5% at 39 days, 48.9% at 102 days, implying a ±20.3% move by the November tenor. The market is pricing calm now and rising uncertainty into the Q2 and Q3 prints, which aligns with the H2-weighted guidance risk. The 22.2% near-term put/call skew (elevated put demand) sits against a 0.22 put/call volume ratio and effectively zero put/call open interest across only 29 contracts in three expirations — too thin to carry directional information, and we do not read it as confirmation of anything. For holders trimming, cheap near-term volatility makes protective puts relatively inexpensive, though liquidity is poor enough that stock sales are the cleaner de-risking tool. Short interest at 3.1% of float and 1.5 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded, with no squeeze fuel in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $54 (+24%)

FY2027 guidance proves conservative from a CEO eight months in and a CFO four months in, both of whom have every incentive to set a beatable bar; Q1 adjusted EPS already came in ahead of management's own expectations. Aluminum surcharges and price increases phase through H2, restoring Metals toward the 13–15% margins of FY2024–25 (Q1 already improved 390bp year over year to 11.2%). Fortify's $26M of annualized savings holds. Kalwall delivers $85M of revenue at 15% margin moving toward 20%, bought at approximately 8.2x versus 12x for UW Solutions, and UW met its stated first-year targets of $100M revenue at 20%-plus margin, giving the acquisition framework a track record. Services converts a $734.5M backlog with better current-period execution now that favorable estimate revisions are no longer carrying the segment. Glass stabilizes as the new segment president executes a performance-improvement plan. FY2028 adjusted EPS reaches $4.00-plus; at 13x that is $52, plus buybacks against 7.3% of the share count. The equity re-rates as a specification-driven products company rather than a cyclical glass fabricator.

### Bear Case — $32 (−27%)

Glass pricing pressure proves structural rather than cyclical, capping the segment at 10–13% margins and permanently removing $20–25M of EBITDA. Aluminum is a cost-base reset, not a timing problem, and surcharge recovery lags into FY2028; Metals gives back its Q1 gain. Non-residential construction weakens further as long-end yields stay elevated and office demand remains impaired, while the corporate-cost offsets that flattered FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 reverse as incentive-compensation accruals rebuild. FY2027 adjusted EPS comes in at or below $2.70 on an H2 shortfall. Kalwall's arrival in a deteriorating Architectural Glass reporting unit produces a goodwill impairment headline within 12–18 months. FY2028 interest expense rises as $125M of swaps roll off against $316M of pro forma net debt. At 11x $2.90 of adjusted EPS the stock is $32 — where it traded in March 2026 — and the January 7 low of $30.75 becomes the reference.

### Base Case — $45 (+3%)

Apogee lands near the low-to-middle of guidance, roughly $2.85–$3.00 of adjusted EPS. Metals and Services offset continued Glass weakness; Kalwall is modestly accretive; Fortify savings are partly consumed by input costs and by incentive-compensation normalization. FY2028 adjusted EPS reaches $3.40–$3.60 rather than the single-analyst $3.90. At 12.5–13x that is $43–47, with the 2.5% dividend and ongoing buyback providing the return. Total 12-month return of roughly 6% including the dividend, against a −27% bear case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base $45 (+3.4%), bull $54 (+24.1%), bear $32 (−26.5%). Horizon 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the Q2 FY2027 print (quarter ending approximately August 29, 2026; report expected late September/early October). The stock has recovered 41% from the January low into guidance that embeds a 14% adjusted EPS decline, and the largest single risk event — a nine-month period that must deliver roughly $2.41 of the $2.975 midpoint with Q2 guided down — sits directly ahead. Reduce position size by roughly half at current levels. A residual holding is defensible for the 2.5% covered dividend, 1.3x leverage, no maturities until July 2029 and an active buyback with 7.3% of the count authorized; there is no solvency case here and no reason to exit entirely.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Architectural Glass adjusted EBITDA margin recovering above 13% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Q1 8.7% print was a trough rather than a new run-rate.
2. Sum-of-segments adjusted EBITDA growing year over year without reliance on Corporate and Other improvement — the specific test that consolidated non-GAAP is no longer flattered by insurance benefits or incentive-compensation reversals.
3. Management raising FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance above $3.25 on operating performance (not on acquisition arithmetic), or setting an FY2028 framework above FY2026's $3.47.
4. Open-market insider purchases by the CEO or CFO, which would place personal capital behind the turnaround for the first time.
5. Price toward $36–38 with FY2027 guidance intact, which would restore roughly 20% to a $45 base case and shift the arithmetic to ACCUMULATE.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $2.70, or a Q2 print that makes the low end unreachable.
2. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge in the Architectural Glass reporting unit, which would simultaneously confirm the segment thesis is deteriorating and invalidate the Kalwall diversification rationale.
3. Disclosure of an additional quantified sealant-related claim above the $12.9M warranty reserve.
4. Net leverage above 2.5x on further acquisitions before Glass margin stabilizes, or Services net cumulative catch-up adjustments turning materially negative (worse than −$3M in a quarter).

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 FY2027 report is the decision point; it will contain the first full quarter of Kalwall consolidation (resolving the noncontrolling-interest question), the first read on whether aluminum surcharges are recovering cost, and the credibility test on H2 weighting.

## Conclusion

Apogee is a financially sound small-cap industrial trading at a price that already reflects a recovery management has not yet guided to. Net leverage of 1.28x, 11.8x interest coverage, $419M of revolver availability, no maturities before July 2029, roughly 8% levered free cash flow yield and a 36%-payout dividend keep the downside bounded and make an AVOID rating unwarranted. Capital allocation over the past two quarters has been good: Kalwall at approximately 8.2x first-year EBITDA against 12x paid for UW Solutions, $9.7M of buybacks at $35.80 in a quarter that later traded to $45.74, and $52.8M of debt reduction in FY2026.

The offsetting evidence is what drives the rating. Management's own FY2027 guidance implies a 14.3% adjusted EPS decline at the midpoint, the third consecutive year of contraction. Segment-level profitability is deteriorating several hundred basis points faster than the consolidated non-GAAP headline, with the gap closed by corporate items that are either non-repeating or self-reversing. Architectural Glass margin has halved on price as well as volume, in a segment where the company itself concedes competitors can match product attributes, and $105M of fresh purchase accounting is landing in that reporting unit. A meaningful share of historical Services profit came from favorable contract estimate revisions that turned net negative in Q1. Five-year total shareholder return trails the proxy peer group by 73 points, the CEO chairs his own board, and no insider has bought stock in the open market during the rally.

At $43.52 the base case is roughly the current price, the bull case is +24% and the bear case is −27%, with the guidance test concentrated in the next two prints. Trim into strength; revisit at $36–38 or on two consecutive quarters of segment-level EBITDA growth that does not depend on corporate cost offsets.
