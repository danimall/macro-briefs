**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Xencor is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company built on an engineered antibody-Fc platform (XmAb) that occupies an unusual position in the biotech risk spectrum: it earns real, growing cash royalties from three marketed medicines while simultaneously advancing a wholly-owned clinical pipeline and collecting milestones from a broad partnered portfolio. The royalty base — led by Ultomiris (Alexion/AstraZeneca), which generated a $70.1M royalty in 2025 — grew from $55.8M (2023) to $80.3M (2025) and partially offsets an operating cash burn of roughly $135M. With $610.8M in cash and marketable securities at year-end 2025 and a management-guided runway "through 2028," Xencor carries a liquidity cushion most Phase 1/2 biotechs lack.

The stock has appreciated 124% over the trailing twelve months, from a $6.92 low to $20.45, driven predominantly by progress in partner-controlled programs: Amgen advancing xaluritamig (STEAP1×CD3, prostate cancer) into two Phase 3 trials, Zenas hitting Phase 3 endpoints for obexelimab with a BLA planned for Q2 2026, and Incyte reporting positive first-line DLBCL data for Monjuvi. The most recent leg (roughly +40% since mid-July) reflects catalyst anticipation rather than fundamental change in the operating business.

The counterweights are specific and material. The Merus B.V. bispecific patent litigation directly names lead wholly-owned asset XmAb819 and plamotamab, with unresolved catalysts (a February 2026 motion-to-dismiss hearing, a June 24, 2026 IPR oral argument). A material weakness disclosed at year-end 2023 forced a restatement of FY2023 and the first three quarters of FY2024, remediated during 2025 but recent. The two most valuable near-term inflections are entirely outside Xencor's control. Short interest sits at 22% of float with 12.1 days to cover. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised down 30.8% for the current year over 90 days. We rate the stock a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the platform-plus-royalty floor and partnered optionality are real, but the binary catalyst calendar and litigation overhang do not yet support a confident BUY at a price that has already doubled.

## Company Overview

Xencor engineers the Fc domain (the constant region of an antibody that governs its interaction with the immune system) to confer modular properties (bispecific/multi-specific structure, extended half-life via Xtend, enhanced cytotoxicity, immune inhibition) that can be inserted into antibodies as building blocks. The business runs three interlocking value streams.

First, platform licensing on marketed products generates cash today. Three medicines use XmAb technology: Ultomiris (Xtend half-life extension, low-single-digit royalty rate on blockbuster scale), Monjuvi/Minjuvi (tafasitamab, a CD19 antibody Xencor created and licensed to Incyte, $10.2M royalty plus milestones with an expanding label after June 2025 follicular-lymphoma approval and positive first-line DLBCL data in January 2026), and legacy sotrovimab.

Second, the wholly-owned clinical pipeline is the primary value-creation engine, recently re-prioritized around T-cell-engaging bispecifics in oncology and B-cell depletion / cytokine inhibition in autoimmune. Oncology assets include XmAb819 (ENPP3×CD3, renal cell carcinoma, 25% partial-response rate in target-dose cohorts in Phase 1) and XmAb541 (CLDN6×CD3, ovarian/germ cell, early confirmed PRs). Autoimmune assets include XmAb942 (Xtend TL1A antibody for ulcerative colitis, Phase 2b XENITH-UC initiated Q3 2025, >71-day half-life supporting 12-week dosing) and XmAb412 (TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific, first-in-human planned 2026).

Third, a broad partner-developed pipeline earns milestones and royalties without Xencor deploying R&D capital. The most valuable is xaluritamig at Amgen, now in two Phase 3 prostate-cancer trials, with up to $225M in milestones plus mid-to-high-single-digit royalties available. Additional partners include Astellas, J&J (up to $1.3B combined milestones across CD28 bispecific programs), Zenas (obexelimab), Gilead, Vir, Novo Nordisk/Omeros, and Novartis. The company employs 260 people (212 in R&D), outsourcing manufacturing and clinical operations.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue is entirely milestone, royalty, and license income (Xencor sells no products of its own) and is lumpy by construction. The four-year series shows the pattern: $164.6M (2022), $168.3M (2023), $110.5M (2024), $125.6M (2025). The trend beneath the lumpiness is the royalty base, which grew steadily from $55.8M (2023) to $67.5M (2024) to $80.3M (2025). Royalties are a higher-quality, more durable stream than milestones, and their upward trajectory partially de-risks the story relative to a pure pre-revenue biotech.

Operating results reflect a company investing heavily ahead of pipeline value. The 2025 operating loss was $177.5M (R&D $239.4M, G&A $63.6M), with R&D rising as wholly-owned programs advance. The reported net loss of $91.9M (versus $232.6M in 2024) is misleading: the swing is dominated by non-operating marks on marketable equity securities (+$87.9M other income in 2025 versus -$56.5M in 2024, the latter including a $20.4M impairment). The Q1 2026 print of -$128.9M (-$1.71/share) — far above the ~$44M quarterly operating run rate and the driver of the -117.5% earnings surprise — is almost certainly mark-to-market losses on equity holdings (Zenas, Vir) reversing 2025's gains. GAAP net income and EPS are close to meaningless quarter-to-quarter for this company; operating loss and cash burn are the metrics that matter, and the trailing/forward P/E of -8.85 is an artifact investors should ignore.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong for a clinical biotech. Cash and marketable securities of $610.8M at year-end 2025 (down from $706.7M) fund a current ratio of 6.25, with debt/equity of 0.38 and equity/assets of 0.73. The Altman Z-Score of 2.77 (grey zone) and deeply negative operating margins are structural features of the pre-profit R&D model rather than distress signals; the liquidity and leverage components are healthy. One nuance: a "liability related to sale of future royalties" of roughly $120M appears as debt and generates ~$32M of non-cash interest expense, which is why a cash-rich company carries meaningful leverage on the books.

Cash flow tells the runway story. Operating cash burn improved to -$135.1M in 2025 from -$202.2M in 2024 (helped by a $30M Amgen milestone collected in January 2025); free cash flow was -$138.3M with minimal capex ($3.2M). Management guides runway "through 2028," which incorporates expected milestones and royalties, roughly a three-year cushion. Dilution is a real cost: shares outstanding rose from ~65M (2024) to ~74M (2025), a $200M ATM facility is authorized (unused at year-end), a fresh S-3 shelf is being filed, and there is a large equity-comp overhang (~13.0M options, ~2.0M RSUs, ~19.9M shares available for future grants). We score financial health at 6.5 — the liquidity, leverage, and growing royalty base are strong, but the persistent nine-figure burn and dilution pipeline prevent a higher mark. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator in our framework, and here it points to a well-capitalized business, not a distressed one.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on two independent engines. The royalty base compounds mechanically as Ultomiris and Monjuvi grow their end markets and expand labels; Monjuvi's follicular-lymphoma approval (June 2025) and positive first-line DLBCL data (January 2026, sBLA expected 1H26) directly widen the royalty stream. This is the durable, low-risk portion of the growth story.

The higher-magnitude growth comes from partnered and wholly-owned pipeline inflections, which are inherently binary. Amgen's xaluritamig in two Phase 3 prostate-cancer trials is the single most important external catalyst — success would convert Xencor's up-to-$225M milestone eligibility and royalty into a material recurring stream. Zenas's obexelimab, having hit Phase 3 INDIGO (IgG4-RD) and Phase 2 MoonStone (MS) endpoints, has a BLA planned for Q2 2026, with up to $460M in milestones plus mid-single-to-mid-teen royalties, and Xencor holds ~3.1M Zenas shares that appreciate on success. On the wholly-owned side, XmAb819's 25% partial-response rate (n=20, heavily pre-treated) and XmAb541's early confirmed PRs are encouraging but early, with expansion cohorts open in CRC, NSCLC, and papillary RCC.

The estimate-revision data cuts against near-term earnings momentum: current-year EPS estimates fell 30.8% over 90 days and next-year estimates fell 16.6%. For a company where GAAP EPS is dominated by equity marks, these revisions are lower-signal than they would be for an operating business — they largely reflect analysts marking down the volatile non-operating line rather than deteriorating operations. Still, they do not support the accelerating-inflection narrative that would justify a top-tier growth score. Growth prospects score 5.5: the royalty compounding is real and durable, the partnered optionality is genuine, but the highest-value catalysts are outside management's control and the wholly-owned pipeline remains early-stage. This is a slight upgrade in conviction from the trajectory's recent 5.0, reflecting confirmed obexelimab BLA timing and Monjuvi label expansion, offset by the litigation overhang.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.45 with ~74.1M shares, market cap is roughly $1.5B. Netting out ~$611M cash (offset by ~$120M royalty-monetization debt) leaves an enterprise value near $1.0B for the pipeline and royalty stream. Trailing and forward P/E of -8.85 are meaningless given negative and equity-mark-distorted earnings; the peer P/E median of 14.2 is not a usable anchor because the comparable set (VIR at -2.7, FTRE at -3.5, SRPT at 23.5, ADMA at 12.2, HRMY at 14.2) is a heterogeneous mix of profitable and pre-profit names with wide dispersion.

The relevant valuation lens is a sum-of-parts: a growing royalty base capitalized at a biotech-royalty multiple supports a meaningful floor (a mid-single-digit multiple on royalty NPV plus net cash covers a large fraction of the current EV), and the pipeline plus partnered optionality supply the upside. Analyst targets frame the range: low $14.00, median $27.00, mean $28.33, high $43.00. At $20.45, the stock sits roughly 28% below the median target and 46% above the analyst floor. Because this is a binary-catalyst biotech rather than a cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates inflection stock, we do not treat consensus targets as floors; the wide $14-to-$43 dispersion reflects genuine disagreement about litigation and pipeline outcomes, not stale extrapolation.

The stock has doubled off its low, and prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark valuation down; the question is price versus intrinsic value today. Our read is that the run has largely closed the gap between price and the risk-adjusted value of the royalty floor plus partnered optionality, leaving the additional upside dependent on binary events that could resolve either way. We score valuation 5.0 — balanced, neither a clear mispricing to exploit nor a stretched multiple to fade. We deliberately avoid rewarding any perceived "cheapness" here; in our framework a cheap-looking screen is more often a value trap than a signal, and this stock's value case rests on catalysts, not on a low multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

Xencor's moat is a validated, modular Fc-engineering platform with three marketed products and deep IP (bispecific patents to ~2034, CD3 to ~2035-36, CD28 to ~2041). The "shots-on-goal" strategy is partly funded by others' capital, and the platform monetizes even when rivals succeed — several competitors in T-cell engagers are also Xencor licensees, a structural hedge unusual in biotech.

The competitive fields are crowded and high-stakes. In T-cell-engaging bispecifics for solid tumors, Xencor's 2+1 avidity-driven selectivity format is a credible technical differentiator for the on-target/off-tumor toxicity problem, but the field includes Amgen, Genmab, Roche, Regeneron, J&J, Janux, and Astellas. In TL1A for IBD — a ~$40B market opportunity by 2032 — XmAb942 is a later entrant behind Merck (tulisokibart, acquired via Prometheus for ~$11B), Roche/Roivant (RVT-3101), Sanofi, and Teva; the differentiation pitch is best-in-class extended half-life (12-week dosing) and the TL1A×IL-23 follow-on, meaning convenience and combination biology rather than being first. In B-cell depletion for autoimmune, the CD19/CD20 wave migrating from oncology into autoimmune is validated but contested (Amgen, Cullinan, Candid, Novartis, Roche).

The trajectory is stable-to-improving: the royalty base is growing, the platform continues to attract partnerships, and disciplined 2025 portfolio pruning (pausing vudalimab and the XmAb808+pembrolizumab expansion) conserved capital. But the company is a fast-follower in its most-hyped autoimmune target and remains dependent on partners for its highest-value assets. Competitive position scores 5.5. We weight this dimension modestly, consistent with its limited recent predictive value in our framework.

## Risk Assessment

The Merus B.V. patent litigation is the sharpest idiosyncratic risk. Merus sued in August 2024 (D. Delaware) alleging its bispecific patents cover plamotamab, vudalimab, and XmAb819, potentially implicating partnered bispecifics. Xencor won a motion to dismiss (September 2025) on §271(e)(1) safe-harbor grounds, but Merus filed an amended complaint (November 2025) asserting four patents; a second motion-to-dismiss hearing was held February 17, 2026, and Xencor's IPR challenge was instituted (September 2025) with oral argument set June 24, 2026. Merus patents putatively expire ~2033. An adverse outcome could force Xencor to cease commercializing affected candidates or expose it to treble damages for willful infringement. This directly threatens a lead wholly-owned asset and is not fully priced.

Dependence on partners for the highest-value assets is a structural risk: xaluritamig (Amgen) and obexelimab (Zenas) are outside Xencor's control, partners can deprioritize, and the Ultomiris royalty rate is only low-single-digit. The 2023 material weakness and subsequent restatement of FY2023 and three quarters of FY2024 (10-K/A filed February 2025) is a recent governance blemish; management states remediation is complete, but it warrants continued scrutiny. Clinical risk is concentrated in Phase 1/2 with no wholly-owned pivotal completed, and CRS toxicity is a known class issue for CD3 engagers. Additional vectors include China CMO exposure (WuXi Biologics, four programs, BIOSECURE-type legislative risk explicitly flagged), IRA drug-pricing pressure, ongoing dilution, and equity-mark volatility that repeatedly distorts headline results.

We score risk factors 4.5. Specific, dated binary events (Merus rulings, obexelimab BLA, Amgen Phase 3 readouts) with asymmetric outcomes, plus the litigation overhang and recent controls history, justify a below-neutral mark. This matches the trajectory's recent improvement to 4.5 from 3.5, reflecting the September 2025 MTD win and remediation, without justifying a further upgrade given unresolved catalysts.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.01 to 0.28 as the stock climbed 60% (from $12.14 to $19.45 over the delta window), indicating some hedging or profit-protection accumulating into the run but still a call-tilted book overall. Combined with the crowded short book, positioning is genuinely two-sided: a favorable catalyst (obexelimab BLA acceptance, a Merus IPR/MTD win) could force a squeeze given the thin float coverage, while an adverse Merus ruling would validate the bears. The elevated short interest is the dominant positioning signal and cuts both ways.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30-43).** The royalty base continues compounding as Ultomiris and Monjuvi expand labels, establishing a growing cash floor. Amgen's xaluritamig Phase 3 prostate-cancer trials read out positively, converting milestone eligibility and royalties into a material stream. Zenas's obexelimab BLA is accepted in 2026 and progresses toward approval, triggering milestones and appreciating Xencor's ~3.1M Zenas shares. The Merus litigation resolves favorably (IPR invalidation or dismissal), removing the overhang on XmAb819 and plamotamab. The short interest amplifies upside into any of these catalysts. Wholly-owned oncology PRs mature into a differentiated 2+1 franchise. Analyst high target of $43 reflects this scenario.

**Bear Case (~$14).** An adverse Merus ruling forces Xencor to cease or license affected bispecific programs, or exposes it to willful-infringement damages. Partnered catalysts disappoint — xaluritamig Phase 3 fails or Amgen deprioritizes, obexelimab stumbles at the FDA. Cash burn near $135M/year erodes the runway faster than milestones replenish it, forcing dilutive raises (the $200M ATM and fresh shelf are ready). The wholly-owned pipeline, entirely Phase 1/2, produces no pivotal validation. Equity-mark volatility continues to spook net-income-focused investors. The short thesis plays out and the stock retraces toward the analyst floor of $14.

The base case sits between these: the royalty floor and partnered optionality support a value modestly above the current price, but binary litigation and clinical outcomes cap conviction. We see fair value roughly at the $27 median target on a risk-adjusted sum-of-parts, contingent on the Merus overhang clearing and at least one major partnered catalyst landing favorably.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale — the 12-month thesis has genuine appreciation potential tied to specific catalysts, but the risk/reward is too balanced and catalyst-dependent for a BUY at a price that has doubled.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoints cluster in 2026: the Merus IPR oral argument (June 24, 2026) and the February 2026 MTD hearing outcome, the obexelimab BLA filing (Q2 2026) and acceptance, and any Amgen xaluritamig Phase 3 interim signals. Each earnings release should be read for cash burn and runway rather than GAAP EPS.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a favorable Merus resolution — IPR invalidation of the asserted patents or dismissal of the amended complaint — removing the bispecific-franchise overhang; (2) FDA acceptance of the obexelimab BLA and/or a positive xaluritamig Phase 3 data point that materially raises royalty visibility; (3) a pullback to the low-to-mid teens (near the $14 analyst floor) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside against the royalty floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) an adverse Merus ruling that names XmAb819 or plamotamab as infringing, or an IPR loss; (2) failure or deprioritization of xaluritamig or obexelimab; (3) a dilutive equity raise executed at a depressed price alongside a runway warning shortened below 2028; (4) evidence the 2023 controls remediation was incomplete (a new material weakness or restatement).

## Conclusion

Xencor is a better-capitalized, more diversified clinical biotech than its negative EPS and grey-zone Altman score suggest. The platform-plus-royalty hybrid gives it a growing royalty floor, a cash cushion carrying it through 2028, and asymmetric partnered optionality in xaluritamig and obexelimab that most Phase 1/2 companies cannot match. The 124% trailing-year rally reflects legitimate progress in partner-controlled programs and label expansions, not froth.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete. The Merus litigation is an unresolved, potentially franchise-threatening overhang with catalysts due in 2026. The most valuable inflections are outside management's control. A recent material-weakness/restatement history is only just remediated. Estimate revisions are negative (though partly an artifact of equity-mark distortion), and the 22% short interest signals informed skepticism even as it creates squeeze optionality. At $20.45 the price has largely closed the gap to the risk-adjusted value of the royalty floor plus optionality, leaving further upside dependent on binary events. We rate XNCR a HOLD (overall score 5.35), consistent with the improving-but-modest trajectory, and would look to upgrade on a favorable Merus outcome, a positive obexelimab/xaluritamig catalyst, or a pullback toward the low teens.
