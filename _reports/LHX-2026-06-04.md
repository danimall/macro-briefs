**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

L3Harris is a scaled defense prime and merchant supplier operating across space, communications, electronic warfare, and missile propulsion. The investment case rests on three pillars: a $40.7B backlog with strong forward visibility, a structurally favorable defense-spending environment (NATO commitments, a proposed ~$1.5T U.S. national-defense topline, FAA air-traffic modernization), and a near-irreplaceable position in solid rocket motors that the U.S. government considers strategically essential. The defining near-term catalyst is the planned IPO of the Missile Solutions (Aerojet Rocketdyne) segment, anchored by a $1.0B direct Department of War equity investment that effectively pre-validates a valuation and subsidizes factory capex.

The fundamentals are sound but unspectacular. FY25 revenue grew 3% to $21.87B; consolidated operating margin was 9.7% (segment-level margins are healthier, CSD ~25%, SMS ~10.5%, MSL ~12.5%, masked by ~$700M/year of acquisition-intangible amortization). Q1 FY2026 accelerated to $5.74B revenue (+12% YoY, partly a 13-week vs 12-week calendar effect) with operating income +24% and EPS +33%. The company has beaten consensus for four consecutive quarters. Free cash flow of $2.68B (12.3% margin) comfortably funds a 25-year dividend-growth streak.

The problem is price. At $303-308, LHX trades at roughly 26x FY26 consensus EPS of $11.70 against low-double-digit reported growth that is partly calendar-inflated, while near-term estimate revisions are drifting lower (current quarter -1.5% over 30 days; next quarter -1.6%) and buybacks have decelerated sharply (from $1.2B in FY25 to $296M in Q1'26). The MSL IPO offers a credible re-rating path, but the stock already discounts much of it. We rate LHX a HOLD with a constructive growth lean, looking for a more attractive entry near $270-285 or confirmation that the IPO surfaces a higher standalone multiple.

## Company Overview

L3Harris brands itself the "Trusted Disruptor," a platform-agnostic defense technology supplier that operates as prime, subcontractor, or merchant supplier depending on the program. It employs ~45,000 people (including ~18,000 engineers and scientists) across 100+ countries. Approximately 75% of revenue comes from the U.S. Government directly, through primes, or via foreign military sales, a concentration that has held steady at 75-76% over three years.

Effective FY2026, the company reorganized from four segments to three, which changes how it should be analyzed:

- **Space & Mission Systems (SMS):** ISR, space systems, maritime, mission networks (including FAA air traffic control), airborne solutions, intel and cyber. Largest segment at $2,990M Q1'26 revenue, 10.5% operating margin. Backlog surged to $21.1B (from $14.6B) on classified program ramps.
- **Communication & Spectrum Dominance (CSD):** Tactical and resilient communications, electronic warfare, night vision, targeting and sensors. The margin leader at 25.1% Q1'26 operating margin.
- **Missile Solutions (MSL):** Solid rocket motor propulsion (from the Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition), fuzing, advanced effects, space propulsion and power. $990M Q1'26 revenue, 12.5% margin. Being partially carved out for an IPO.

The core competitive asset is the solid rocket motor (SRM) franchise. L3Harris/Aerojet is one of only two Western suppliers (the other being Northrop Grumman's former Orbital ATK assets), a genuine strategic moat at a moment when munitions demand from Ukraine and the Middle East has strained the supply base.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $17.06B (FY22) → $19.42B (FY23, lifted by Aerojet/TDL) → $21.33B (FY24) → $21.87B (FY25, +3%). Q1'26 accelerated to $5.74B (+12% YoY across all segments), though the 13-week vs 12-week quarter inflates the comparison.

**Profitability:** FY25 net margin 7.3%, diluted EPS $8.53 (+8%). Q1'26 showed real operating leverage: operating income $652M (+24%), diluted EPS $2.72 (+33%). The headline margin understates segment economics because of the ~$700M/year of non-cash intangible amortization from the L3/Harris merger and Aerojet/TDL deals.

**Cash flow:** FY25 operating cash flow $3.11B (+21%). Q1 is seasonally negative (-$95M OCF), consistent with the company's normal pattern. Adjusted FCF is the company's primary incentive metric.

**Balance sheet — the key watch item:** $10.9B fixed-rate debt, D/E ~1.1x. Goodwill ($20.0B) plus intangibles ($6.3-6.5B) equal ~64% of the $41.4B asset base. This drives an Altman Z-Score of 1.95 (grey zone). For a defense prime with this backlog visibility, this is not a distress signal; it reflects a goodwill-heavy, leveraged structure rather than deteriorating operations (Piotroski 5/5, equity-to-assets 47.7%). The company repaid $600M of notes in FY25 and is actively deleveraging. The asymmetry: a growth stumble could trigger goodwill impairment given the heavy carrying values. An $85M impairment was already taken in FY25 on the Space Technology disposal group.

**Earnings quality nuance:** Reported operating income is increasingly flattered by one-time gains from "monetization of legacy end-of-life assets" ($184M in FY25 G&A offset; $39M in MSL in Q1'26), and a $31M unfavorable EAC adjustment on a legacy naval sensor program in Q1'26 illustrates the recurring fixed-price overrun risk (75% of revenue is fixed-price). Segment margins are the cleaner read.

**Capital allocation:** 25th consecutive annual dividend increase ($1.25/quarter, raised January 2026); FY25 dividends $903M; FY25 buybacks $1.2B, decelerating sharply to $296M in Q1'26 with $1.9B remaining. The slowdown signals capital being conserved for deleveraging and MSL factory build-out, prudent given the Z-Score, but a near-term headwind to EPS growth via share count.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth profile is the strongest part of the case, but it requires unpacking. The FY26 consensus implies low-double-digit EPS growth ($11.70 vs $8.53 trailing), part of which is calendar-driven and part of which reflects LHX NeXt cost savings flowing into segment margins.

Demand drivers are broad-based and well-documented:
- Record global defense spending; NATO allies committing to 5% of GDP.
- A U.S. reconciliation package with $155B for DoW priorities explicitly aligned with L3Harris programs — Golden Dome missile defense, munitions, shipbuilding.
- FAA air-traffic-control modernization (~$12.5B plus $22B in appropriations) directly benefits SMS Mission Networks, a top FY25/Q1'26 growth driver.
- NASA SLS/Orion preservation benefits MSL's RS-25 engine business.
- A proposed FY27 national-defense topline near $1.5T.

The MSL business is guided by management to mid-to-high-teens annual growth, the fastest-growing piece of the portfolio and the strategic focus of the IPO. SMS backlog growth (+45% to $21.1B) improves visibility, though the classified-program mix shift reduces transparency.

The countervailing signal is estimate momentum. Despite four consecutive beats, near-term consensus is drifting lower: current quarter -1.5% (30 days), next quarter -1.6% (30 and 90 days), current year roughly flat to slightly negative. Only next-year estimates are inching up (+0.5% over 90 days). This is a solid franchise with modest negative near-term revision pressure offset by a longer-dated catalyst, not the rising-estimate, accelerating-revenue profile that would justify a more aggressive stance.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$303-308, LHX trades at approximately 26x FY26 consensus EPS of $11.70 and ~22x next-year EPS of $13.63. The trailing P/E of ~33x overstates richness because of intangible amortization drag, but even the cleaner forward multiple is full for a business growing reported revenue in mid-single digits with falling near-term estimates.

Peer context is mixed. Against the aerospace and defense cohort:
- **NOC:** 16.8x — the cleanest pure-prime comp, trading materially cheaper.
- **GD:** 21.2x — comparable prime, ~15% cheaper than LHX on a forward basis.
- **TDG:** 38.9x — premium aftermarket-driven model, not a fair comp.
- **AXON/RKLB:** distorted (197x and negative) — not relevant.

LHX trades at a premium to the two most comparable primes (NOC, GD) despite a worse near-term margin trajectory than GD and a less-clean balance sheet than NOC. The peer median P/E of 38.9x is inflated by TransDigm and is not the right anchor; against NOC and GD, LHX looks expensive, not cheap.

The bull valuation argument is sum-of-the-parts. CSD's ~25% margins and MSL's scarce SRM franchise could command higher standalone multiples than the conglomerate earns. The MSL IPO is the mechanism to surface this: the DoW's $1B convertible (auto-converting at 80% of IPO price) effectively pre-validates a valuation, and L3Harris retains ~80%. If the IPO prints at a premium multiple for the scarce propulsion asset, the implied stub value of the retained stake could re-rate the parent.

We do not score valuation highly because the re-rating is contingent and largely anticipated. Analyst consensus targets (mean $382, median $390, high $443) sit 24-27% above spot, but the cohort guardrail applies: at a premium multiple, those targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price, not independent confirmation. We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — not a value trap, but no asymmetric upside at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris occupies a strong but not unassailable position. The SRM duopoly is the clearest moat — only two Western suppliers, with the DoW's direct equity stake signaling the government views this capacity as essential. CSD's tactical communications and electronic warfare franchise carries 25% margins, evidence of differentiated, hard-to-displace technology.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than clearly improving. Procurement reform favoring Other Transaction Authority awards opens the door to non-traditional entrants (SpaceX, Anduril) with lower-cost, AI-driven approaches that L3Harris explicitly acknowledges could pressure pricing and share. The company sits between the largest primes and the disruptive newcomers, and its merchant-supplier flexibility is both a strength (program-agnostic positioning) and a vulnerability (less platform lock-in than a sole-source prime). The competitive position score of 6.5 reflects genuine moats in propulsion and communications against intensifying competitive entry at the margin.

## Risk Assessment

1. **U.S. Government / budget dependency (75% of revenue).** The FY2025 record 43-day shutdown, continuing-resolution reliance, and procurement reform are explicit concerns. The proposed topline is a tailwind only if enacted.
2. **Fixed-price exposure (75% of revenue).** Cost-overrun and inflation risk sits with L3Harris. The $31M unfavorable EAC adjustment is a recurring earnings-quality swing factor.
3. **Goodwill impairment.** All units cleared annual testing by >40% post-reorg, but the scale of goodwill plus intangibles makes this a structural vulnerability if growth disappoints.
4. **Leverage.** The debt load constrains flexibility and is flagged as a standalone risk factor.
5. **MSL IPO execution.** New 10-K risk-factor language covers the planned IPO; Q1'26 10-Q added "unexpected issues related to the Department of War investment in our subsidiary." The carve-out is complex (auto-converting preferred, warrants, ~80% retained stake) and timing-dependent.
6. **Energetic-materials hazard** (rocket motors): explosion/ignition risk, heightened now that MSL is a strategic centerpiece.
7. **Environmental liabilities** of $657M (largely Aerojet legacy), $482M recoverable via government pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress against a benign longer-dated view. Near-term ATM IV is 40.0% versus 24.1% trailing realized vol (rich, +15.9pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (40.0% near-term vs 27.4% at six months); the market is pricing an imminent event, most likely the MSL S-1 becoming public and the IPO timeline. Put/call skew of 6.1% reflects elevated near-term put demand, and ATM IV rose 6.3pp (33.6% → 40.0%) over the most recent sessions on a roughly flat price. This signals event-driven hedging, not a structural bearish bet (six-month skew is negative at -4.1%, and put/call OI is a low 0.49). The implied near-term move (±1.8% one-week, ±4.2% one-month) is modest. Net read: the market expects volatility around the IPO catalyst but is not positioned directionally bearish on the franchise.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The MSL IPO surfaces a premium standalone multiple for the scarce solid-rocket-motor franchise, with the DoW's stake pre-validating valuation and subsidizing the Camden/Huntsville/Orange capacity expansion. Mid-to-high-teens MSL growth, defense budget tailwinds (Golden Dome, munitions, FAA modernization), and LHX NeXt cost savings drive EPS toward $14+ in FY27. Sum-of-the-parts re-rating pulls the stock toward $390-443 (consensus median to high), roughly 25x FY27 EPS plus an MSL stub premium.

### Bear Case
The MSL IPO disappoints or slips, near-term estimate cuts continue, and a fixed-price program stumble or growth deceleration triggers goodwill impairment given the heavy carrying value. Decelerating buybacks remove an EPS prop. Multiple compresses toward the GD/NOC range (17-21x), pulling the stock to $250-270, a 12-18% decline from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean).** The franchise quality and IPO catalyst justify holding, but the ~26x forward multiple against mid-single-digit reported growth and falling near-term estimates removes the asymmetry required for a higher-conviction call. This is the fourth consecutive report stepping down from a prior BUY stance; the declining score trajectory (6.7 → 6.0) reflects a fully-priced multiple and softening near-term revisions rather than fundamental deterioration. The price decline from the $379 high is modest relative to the franchise and is not itself a thesis-breaker, but it has not created compelling value either.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- MSL S-1 becomes public and implies an IPO valuation that surfaces a meaningful sum-of-the-parts premium to the parent.
- Near-term EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter revisions inflect from negative to positive over a 30-60 day window).
- A pullback toward $270-285 (~23-24x FY26 EPS), restoring 15%+ upside to the base case with contained downside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment of material size, or a fixed-price program writedown exceeding ~$200M, signaling the asymmetric balance-sheet risk is materializing.
- MSL IPO indefinitely shelved or repriced sharply lower, removing the re-rating catalyst.
- Reported revenue growth decelerating below 3% YoY on a clean (calendar-adjusted) basis with continued estimate cuts.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY26, late July 2026, which captures the DoW investment close) and the public filing of the MSL Form S-1, whichever comes first. The options term structure points to the catalyst arriving within weeks.

## Conclusion

L3Harris is a high-quality defense prime with a genuine moat in solid rocket motors and communications, broad-based budget tailwinds, and a credible value-unlock catalyst in the Missile Solutions IPO. The fundamentals support ownership: the backlog, four straight earnings beats, 12.3% FCF margin, and a 25-year dividend-growth streak. What it does not support at $303-308 is a buy call. The stock trades at a premium to its closest comparable primes (NOC at 16.8x, GD at 21.2x) on a forward multiple of ~26x while near-term estimates drift lower and buybacks decelerate. The IPO re-rating is real but largely anticipated, and the consensus targets that imply 24-27% upside reflect the same growth optimism embedded in the current price rather than independent validation. We rate LHX a HOLD with a growth lean and would become more constructive on a pullback toward $270-285 or on confirmation that the MSL IPO surfaces a higher standalone multiple.
