**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation is among the highest-quality businesses in public markets, and also one of the most expensive relative to its near-term cash flows. It owns roughly 881,000 surface acres concentrated in the Permian Basin plus ~224,000 net royalty acres, the bulk of which carry zero book value from the original 1888 trust conveyance. The royalty interests are perpetual and non-participating, so TPL captures top-line oil, gas, and NGL revenue while bearing none of the development capex or operating burden. This produces operating margins near 74%, net margins near 60%, ROE of 33%, and free cash flow conversion above 100% of net income, all on a balance sheet with effectively no drawn debt and a current ratio of 4.4. There is no comparable asset in the sector.

The investment problem is price. At $369.55 (last close in the price series; the profile snapshot uses $388.98), TPL trades at roughly 40x forward consensus EPS of $9.17 and ~53x trailing earnings (on $6.97 trailing EPS), against a peer median P/E of 10.2 for Permian E&Ps. That premium is defensible only if the emerging non-royalty growth vectors (produced-water royalties, the desalination project, and data-center/power land monetization) scale meaningfully and durably. The near-term tape is moving the other way: consensus EPS estimates have fallen 4-9% across all horizons over the past 30 days, realized gas prices collapsed as Waha basis went negative (-$1.40/mmbtu in Q1 2026), and Permian horizontal rig counts dropped from 296 to 222 over the trailing window. TPL has masked falling realized prices with rising production volume for two straight years, but that lever has a finite runway as rigs decline.

We rate TPL a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The structural compounding case is intact and we would not sell quality of this caliber on valuation alone, but the multiple already capitalizes the optimistic outcome. The bear-case downside to ~$280 (a still-rich ~31x forward EPS) is roughly -24% from current price, while the base case to ~$400 is only ~+8%. That asymmetry, combined with falling near-term estimates and an unhedged commodity profile, is the specific reason we will not extend a BUY recommendation at this price. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on confirmation that the water/data-center segments are converting optionality into recurring cash flow.

## Company Overview

TPL is not an oil and gas producer despite its GICS classification as an E&P. It was organized in 1888 as a business trust to hold land from the bankrupt Texas & Pacific Railway and converted to a Delaware corporation in January 2021 following a multi-year activist campaign. The company operates two segments:

- **Land and Resource Management (LRM)**, ~62-65% of revenue, consists of oil and gas royalties (the largest single line), easements, commercial leases, material sales (caliche and sand), and land sales. Royalties require zero capex or operating expense from TPL.
- **Water Services and Operations (WSO)**, ~37-38% of revenue, operated through Texas Pacific Water Resources, covers water sourcing and sales plus produced-water royalties.

TPL's contiguous surface scale is essentially unreplicable; competitors must separately negotiate water-sourcing rights and rights-of-way to deliver it, while TPL owns both. The royalty interests are perpetual non-participating royalty interests, meaning TPL captures revenue without bearing development cost. Roughly 191,000 of its 224,000 net royalty acres and the bulk of its surface acreage were conveyed in 1888 at zero book value, which means reported ROE and ROA understate the economic return on fair asset value, while also explaining why the market capitalizes far more than current royalty cash flows can justify on conventional multiples.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is exceptional by any standard:

- **Revenue**: $667M (2022) → $632M (2023) → $706M (2024) → $798M (2025), +13% YoY. Q1 2026 revenue was a record $236.8M, +20.8% YoY.
- **Net income**: $481M FY2025 (+6% YoY); Q1 2026 net income $142.9M (+18% YoY), EPS $2.07.
- **Free cash flow**: $498M FY2025; ~$136M in Q1 2026. Adjusted EBITDA $687M FY2025.
- **Margins / returns**: operating margin ~74%, net margin ~60%, ROE 33%, ROA 30%, Altman Z-Score 8.46.
- **Balance sheet**: $247.6M cash at Q1 2026, no debt drawn, current ratio 4.4, D/E 0.11.

The key operating dynamic is volume-over-price. In 2025, royalty revenue grew even as realized price per Boe fell 14% ($39.87 → $34.18), because production volume on TPL's acreage surged ~29% (26.8 → 34.6 MBoe/d) from acquisitions and operator completions. That continued in Q1 2026 (37.1 MBoe/d, +19% YoY). The risk embedded in this dynamic is that the volume growth depends on third-party operators continuing to complete wells at a pace that offsets falling rig counts and declining base production. The lever is real but finite.

Natural gas is a meaningful drag. Waha Hub differentials have been deeply negative (FY2025 average $0.69/mmbtu and Q1 2026 turning negative at -$1.40/mmbtu), crushing realized gas prices from $3.63 to $1.83/Mcf year-over-year in Q1. This is a structural Permian takeaway-capacity problem, not a one-quarter aberration, and TPL is unhedged.

The water segment is the more reliable growth engine. WSO revenue grew 16% in 2025, produced-water royalty volumes grew 24.6%, and Q1 2026 WSO operating income rose 21% YoY. Produced-water volume grows mechanically with regional production, giving WSO a cleaner secular tailwind than the price-sensitive royalty line.

After operating debt-free for 137 years, TPL put a $500M revolving credit facility in place in October 2025 (expandable to $750M, currently undrawn). Combined with ~$490M of 2025 acquisitions (mostly the $450.7M, 17,306-NRA Midland Basin royalty purchase in November) and the data-center push, this signals a more capital-deployment-oriented posture than TPL's historically passive model. Capital allocation is governed by a $700M cash target; above that, the majority of FCF flows to special dividends and buybacks. FY2025 paid $147.8M in dividends (no special dividend versus a $3.33 special in 2024), raised the quarterly to $0.60, and bought back a modest $8.4M of stock with $170.2M of authorization remaining. The dividend yield is only ~0.6%, so income is not the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors layer on top of the royalty base, in descending order of visibility:

1. **Produced-water royalties (WSO)** — the most durable. Volumes grow with regional production regardless of commodity price, and the volume and operating-income growth confirm momentum. The constraint is regulatory: Texas Railroad Commission Seismic Response Areas and the January 2024 indefinite suspension of deep produced-water injection in Culberson and Reeves counties could cap volume growth.

2. **Data-center / power land monetization** — the highest-optionality, lowest-visibility vector. In Q1 2026, TPL sold land for $42.5M to a power-generation developer supporting data center operations, recognizing $20.9M of revenue plus a financing receivable, with a separate water-supply agreement and put/call options tied to development milestones. Separately, TPL made a $50M minority investment in December 2025 in Bolt, a data/energy infrastructure company chaired and co-founded by Eric Schmidt, taking equity plus warrants and a right of first refusal to supply water. In both cases TPL is a landowner, prospective water supplier, and minority investor, not a data-center owner or operator. Management noted hyperscaler and AI-lab urgency in West Texas has "noticeably increased." Cheap power, land, and water rights are what AI compute buildouts in the region need, but the cash-flow contribution today is small and the timeline is uncertain.

3. **Royalty volume growth** — the largest line but the most price-exposed and operator-dependent. Two consecutive years of volume offsetting price is impressive, but with rigs falling, the offset depends on operator completion efficiency continuing to improve. If that plateaus, royalty growth stalls.

The estimate-revision picture cuts against the near-term growth case. Consensus current-year EPS fell from $9.56 to $9.17 over 30 days (-4.1%), the current quarter from $2.50 to $2.28 (-8.6%), and next year from $10.60 to $10.08 (-4.9%). The 90-day trend is still positive (+11% current year), reflecting the earlier oil rally, but the recent direction is down as the Strait of Hormuz situation de-escalated and gas realizations weakened. This is the opposite of the +30%/90-day inflection signal that would support a higher growth score; momentum is currently negative.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer median P/E for Permian E&Ps is 10.2 (CTRA 15.0, EQT 10.2, DVN 12.3, APA 8.5, EXE 6.9). TPL is not directly comparable to those names — it is a zero-cost-base royalty and land business, not a capital-intensive producer, and it deserves a substantial premium for its margin structure, capital-light model, and optionality. But a ~4x premium to the peer median is a large amount of forward growth and durability to underwrite.

We do not apply the "high trailing P/E at a cyclical trough" exemption here. TPL is not at a trough — Q1 2026 was a record quarter, helped by oil over $90 during the February-April Hormuz episode, which is now reversing. The forward multiple is being applied to elevated, not depressed, earnings power, which makes the trailing-versus-forward distinction less protective than it would be for a true inflection name. Nor do we treat the analyst mean target of $445 as a floor: that target sits below where the stock recently traded ($547 high in February), the consensus is unusually polarized (one buy, one sell; targets ranging from $251 to $639), and at >40x forward the target reflects the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation.

The fair-value debate hinges entirely on the non-royalty optionality. If the data-center/power land and desalination businesses scale into recurring multi-hundred-million-dollar cash flows, today's multiple is justified or even cheap on a sum-of-the-parts basis against the zero-book-value asset base. If they remain small, the stock is a richly priced royalty company facing negative gas realizations and a finite volume runway. We score valuation 3.5: the multiple is stretched against current and forward fundamentals, the recent estimate trend is negative, and the catalysts required to justify the price are real but unconfirmed in the cash flows. This is a slight downgrade reflecting the recent estimate cuts; we are not penalizing the stock for prior appreciation, only for the price-versus-forward-earnings relationship today.

## Competitive Landscape

TPL's competitive position is strong and, if anything, widening. The 10-K notes it has "few direct peers." The closest analogs are mineral and royalty companies (Viper Energy, Kimbell, Sitio), but none combines surface scale, water rights, and royalty interests at TPL's level. The company monetizes the full life cycle of land use — pre-development fees, caliche and sand sales, water sourcing, oil/gas/NGL royalties, produced-water royalties, easements, and now data-center/power land — so it captures revenue across the commodity cycle and from non-hydrocarbon uses. That breadth is the source of its resilience.

The trajectory is improving on the water and land-monetization fronts: growing produced-water royalty volumes, an emerging data-center land vector, and a desalination test facility in Orla nearing first barrels in Q2 2026. The one area where competitive position is structurally constrained rather than improving is the core royalty line, where TPL is a price-taker on both commodity prices and operator drilling decisions. On balance, the moat is among the widest in the sector and the non-royalty extensions are adding new monetization surfaces, supporting a high competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are concentrated and specific, not generic sector risk:

1. **Total dependence on third-party operators.** TPL controls nothing about drilling or completion pace. The volume-over-price offset depends on completion efficiency continuing to improve as rig counts fall.
2. **Customer concentration.** ~40% of 2025 revenue came from just three customers (all investment-grade Permian majors). A pullback from any one would be material.
3. **Unhedged commodity / Waha gas exposure.** Negative Waha basis directly compresses gas royalty realizations, and the company does not hedge.
4. **Saltwater disposal seismicity.** TRC Seismic Response Areas and the January 2024 indefinite suspension of deep produced-water injection in Culberson and Reeves counties threaten the WSO produced-water royalty growth engine.
5. **Desalination execution risk.** ~$48M cumulative spend through Q1; cost-overrun, permitting, environmental discharge, and reputational exposure.
6. **Bolt minority-investment risk.** $50M illiquid stake with no control rights.
7. **Ad valorem tax dispute.** A third party has refused to continue paying historical ad valorem taxes; TPL has paid these since 2022 with no recovery receivable recorded.
8. **Governance transition.** Murray Stahl, Horizon Kinetics co-founder and the intellectual anchor of TPL's modern shareholder base, died April 7, 2026. Peter Doyle's board appointment preserves continuity at an activism-prone company.

These risks are partially but not fully priced. The premium multiple assumes smooth execution on water and data-center optionality; any disappointment on those fronts, combined with the unhedged gas exposure and operator dependence, creates asymmetric downside.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive-to-neutral and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 48.4% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 47.1% (a 1.4% spread, fairly priced), and IV percentile is low at 22%, so the market is not pricing acute near-term event risk. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-7.7%), the put/call volume ratio is light at 0.44, and the term structure is flat (48.4% near versus 46.5% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded. The put/call open-interest ratio roughly doubled from 0.40 to 0.84 since late May as the stock fell ~3%, indicating some accumulation of downside hedges, though at 0.84 the book remains net call-heavy. Short interest is moderate at 9.5% of float with 10.5 days to cover, which provides modest squeeze potential but is not extreme. The overall signal is benign positioning with no informed-flow warning that would override the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
TPL is a perpetual, capital-light royalty and land machine with 74% operating margins, no debt, and a zero-book-value asset base whose fair value far exceeds carrying value. Produced-water royalties compound with regional production, the desalination facility opens a new water-monetization channel, and West Texas data-center/power demand offers a potentially transformative land-monetization vector that the market is only beginning to price. If the Bolt investment and the power-plant/data-center land deals scale, TPL re-rates as a hybrid infrastructure-and-royalty company rather than a commodity name. Bull case ~$520, roughly the February 2026 high and ~+41% from current, implying the optionality converts to durable cash flow.

### Bear Case
The premium multiple already capitalizes optimistic outcomes. Negative Waha basis structurally suppresses gas realizations, declining Permian rig counts eventually exhaust the volume-over-price offset, SRA disposal restrictions cap produced-water royalty growth, and the data-center optionality remains small relative to the ~$26.8B market cap. Estimates are already being cut (-4% to -9% over 30 days). In that scenario the stock de-rates toward ~31x forward EPS, or roughly $280, ~-24% from current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** This is a confident hold on a high-quality compounding asset, not an uncertain one, but the current price offers insufficient margin of safety for new capital.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026 for Q2), which should provide the first desalination revenue data point and an update on data-center land monetization, plus visibility on whether volume growth is still offsetting rig declines and gas weakness.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$300s (~33x forward EPS), which would restore an asymmetric reward profile against the bull case.
- Confirmation that the data-center/power land and water vectors are converting to recurring cash flow — e.g., a second material land/power deal or recurring water-supply revenue from Bolt-related projects, plus positive resumption of estimate revisions.
- Waha basis normalization and a stabilization or recovery in Permian rig counts that de-risks the royalty volume runway.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Royalty production volume turning flat-to-negative YoY while realized prices remain weak (the volume-over-price offset breaking).
- A material SRA-driven curtailment of produced-water disposal that caps WSO growth.
- Estimate revisions accelerating downward (next-year EPS cut below ~$9.00) without a corresponding price adjustment.

## Conclusion

Texas Pacific Land is a genuinely rare business — an unreplicable land and royalty franchise with near-zero capital intensity, a pristine balance sheet, and a credible new growth surface in West Texas water and data-center/power infrastructure. The quality is not in question, and the long-term compounding case supports holding the stock for shareholders who already own it. The constraint is valuation: at ~40x forward earnings against a peer median of ~10x, with near-term estimates falling, gas realizations negative, and Permian rig counts declining, the price already embeds the favorable resolution of optionality that has not yet shown up in cash flows. The reward/risk from current levels is roughly +8% to the base case and +41% to the bull case against -24% to the bear case, with the recent estimate trajectory pointing the wrong way. That asymmetry is not attractive enough for new money. We rate TPL HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on confirmation that the water and data-center segments are scaling into durable, recurring revenue.
