**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Darling Ingredients converts animal-agriculture and food-industry by-products into feed, food, and fuel ingredients across a vertically integrated global collection network. The investment story over the past year has been a violent cyclical trough in FY2025 (net income $62.8M, down 77% from 2024) followed by a sharp V-shaped recovery in Q1 2026 (net income $134.3M, $0.83 diluted EPS versus a loss of $0.16 a year earlier). Both the trough and the recovery were driven overwhelmingly by the 50/50 Diamond Green Diesel (DGD) joint venture with Valero and the shifting biofuel tax-credit regime — the expiration of the $1.00/gal Blenders Tax Credit at end-2024, its replacement with the 45Z Clean Fuels Production Credit (raised to $1.06/gal and made retroactive), and higher 2026–27 Renewable Volume Obligations that lifted RIN values.

The stock has run roughly 79% off its 52-week low of $29.15 to $64.78. That re-rating is fundamentally justified, but it has now taken the shares to ~13.4x current-year consensus EPS ($4.85) and ~47x trailing EPS, above the sell-side mean base case. Barclays initiated Equal Weight in late June with a $58 target, below the current price, arguing the favorable policy tailwind is already discounted. The analyst target band is $62 to $85 with a mean of ~$75, leaving current price near the low end of that range.

We rate DAR a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The core rendering franchise (Feed segment margin expanded to 25.3% from 20.3%, with base fat prices +16% YoY) is improving independently of DGD, a genuine positive. Three factors keep us from a BUY: the earnings recovery is heavily dependent on a policy regime that can be revised or reinterpreted; DGD's Q1 GAAP earnings surge was not matched by cash — the JV paid zero dividends and Darling injected $190M — so the deleveraging engine ran on accrual, not cash; and the stock now trades above where sophisticated sell-side coverage places fair value. This is a fairly-to-slightly-richly-priced recovery.

## Company Overview

Darling operates 260+ facilities across five continents and 15+ countries, processing an estimated 15% of the world's animal-agriculture by-products, producing ~30% of the world's collagen, and supplying low-carbon-intensity feedstock to renewable-fuel markets. The business is organized into three segments:

- **Feed Ingredients** (~64% of Q1'26 sales, $985M): rendering of beef, poultry, and pork by-products and used cooking oil into fats, proteins, and meals; bakery residuals; blood plasma; hides; organic fertilizer; grease-trap services. Brands include Sonac, FASA, and Valley Proteins.
- **Food Ingredients** (~26%, $405M): collagen and gelatin (Rousselot, Gelnex), natural casings (CTH), heparin, and food-grade fats. This is the higher-margin, longer-cycle segment (~29% gross margin).
- **Fuel Ingredients** (~10% of consolidated sales, $160M, but the dominant earnings swing factor): primarily the DGD JV, plus European bioenergy. Because DGD is equity-accounted, its results appear as equity in net income rather than in segment revenue, which is why Fuel looks small on the top line while dominating earnings volatility.

The core competitive advantage is the collection network. Darling is one of very few players with the scale to aggregate fats and used cooking oil to feed low-CI fuel markets. Raw-material costs in the base rendering business are largely indexed to finished-goods prices, giving structurally stable spread margins. DGD's ~1.2 billion gal/yr renewable diesel capacity (Norco, LA and Port Arthur, TX) is among the largest in North America, with Port Arthur able to convert roughly half of its 470M gal capacity to sustainable aviation fuel.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was the trough.** Net income fell to $62.8M from $278.9M in 2024 and $647.7M in 2023. The proximate cause was DGD swinging to a full-year net loss of ($68.5M) as the industry navigated the Blenders Tax Credit expiration, the transition to 45Z, catalyst turnarounds, tariff disruption, and weak RIN/LCFS values. Darling's full-year share of DGD Adjusted EBITDA fell to $104M ($0.21/gallon) on 1.003 billion gallons, down from $290M in 2024. This was a sector-wide dislocation — Valero's own renewable diesel segment posted a ($156M) FY2025 operating loss.

**Q1 2026 was the recovery.** Net income attributable to Darling reached $134.3M versus a ($26.2M) loss a year earlier. The swing was DGD: DGD net income of $222.1M versus ($59.3M), a ~$281M year-over-year turn. Darling's DGD equity income was +$107.4M versus ($30.5M). Combined Adjusted EBITDA of $406.8M more than doubled the $195.8M in Q1'25. Standalone Adjusted EBITDA rose 35% to $255.7M; Darling's share of DGD Adjusted EBITDA jumped to $151.2M from just $6.0M. Consolidated gross margin expanded to 26.1% from 22.6%. The recovery drivers per management: higher DGD production, $177.7M of net 45Z production tax credits recognized in the quarter (versus $50.9M in Q1'25), higher RIN values after EPA finalized the 2026–27 RVOs, and higher diesel prices.

**The cash-flow nuance is the most important detail in the filings.** Q1'26 operating cash flow fell to $153M from $249M despite far higher net income, because DGD paid zero dividend distributions in Q1'26 (versus $129.5M in Q1'25) and Darling contributed $190M of capital to the JV. DGD's GAAP earnings recovery has not yet been matched by cash upstreamed to Darling. This matters because management's capital plan prioritizes debt reduction, and the deleveraging engine depends on DGD distributions. The $68.7M distribution received subsequent to quarter-end signals the spigot is reopening, but the cadence needs to be tracked before crediting the recovery with a fully funded balance sheet.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$4.13B against $116M cash; the company self-describes as "highly leveraged." The June 2025 refinancing (Third Amended Credit Agreement: $900M Term A + $2.0B revolver; new €750M 4.5% Notes due 2032) pushed out maturities. Other notes: $1.0B 6% due 2030, $500M 5.25% due 2027. Revolver availability is ~$1.12B, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. The equity-method investment in DGD stands at $2.35B. This is appropriate leverage for a capital-intensive processor, but with rates higher-for-longer and cash conversion lagging earnings, deleveraging is not automatic.

**Capital allocation.** Capex was ~$95M in Q1 with ~$305M guided for the rest of FY26. $460.3M remains on the $500M buyback authorization through August 2026, but zero shares were repurchased in Q1'26, consistent with the Investor Day message prioritizing debt reduction first. No dividend.

**Earnings surprise track record is a caution flag.** Over the last four reported quarters: one beat (Q1'26, +45.9%) and three misses (Q4'25 -13.3%, Q3'25 -37.7%, Q2'25 -65.9%). This reflects DGD's extraordinary volatility rather than sandbagging or management error, but it argues for humility about forward estimate precision — a business where a single quarter's DGD result can swing net income from a loss to $134M does not have high earnings visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision picture is genuinely strong and the strongest single argument for the bull case. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised from $3.84 ninety days ago to $4.85 (+26.5%), with next year at $5.29 (+23.3% over 90 days). Current-quarter estimates rose +30.1% over 90 days to $1.41. These revisions clear the threshold that historically precedes continued outperformance.

The important qualification is what is driving the revisions. This is a policy-driven cyclical recovery from a depressed base, not a durable secular growth acceleration. The 45Z retroactive credit, the higher RVOs, and the North-America-only feedstock rule that advantages domestic collectors are all real, but they are policy inputs that reset the earnings level rather than compound it. The revenue-growth-plus-margin-expansion inflection is present, but it sits on top of a JV whose earnings swung from ($59M) to +$222M in a year. The estimate-revision signal is strongest when it reflects structural underestimation of a business inflecting off a trough — that describes DAR reasonably well — but it is weakened when the driver is a tax-credit level that can be revised and a JV earnings stream that is inherently mean-reverting.

Several structural levers support a more durable growth case:
- **Feed segment improving independently of DGD** — base fat prices +16% YoY and +15% sequentially, pet-food poultry meal +20% sequentially. The core rendering spread business is inflecting on its own.
- **Portfolio reshaping toward higher-quality mix** — the Tessenderlo collagen JV (Darling 85%-owned NewCo Collagen) consolidates collagen leadership; the UPI Bovinos Brazil acquisition (~$119.5M) bolts on rendering feedstock; the ~$90M grease-trap divestiture prunes a low-margin business. Together these shift the mix toward Food Ingredients and feedstock control.
- **SAF optionality** — Port Arthur can convert ~50% of its capacity to sustainable aviation fuel, though the 45Z SAF credit falls to $1.00/gal after 2025 from $1.86, tempering the near-term economics.

Management guided Q2 core ingredients EBITDA of $260–275M, citing the RVO boost. Growth Prospects score a 6.0 — directionally positive with real inflection, but the durability question keeps it from a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $0.39, DAR trades at ~47x — a meaningless figure at a cyclical trough, and one we do not weight. On current-year consensus of $4.85, forward P/E is ~13.4x; on next year's $5.29, ~12.2x. Those are the relevant numbers.

Against peers in Agricultural Products & Services, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 33.0 (BG 33.0, ADM 35.3), with INGR at 9.7 and VITL at 10.5. On forward earnings DAR at ~13.4x sits well below BG/ADM but above the cheapest comps. The peer set is heterogeneous and the comparison is imperfect — DAR's earnings mix (a policy-driven fuel JV plus rendering plus collagen) does not map cleanly onto pure ag processors — so the low forward multiple partly reflects the market's discount for DGD earnings quality and volatility, not a straightforward mispricing.

The analyst target band is $62 low to $85 high, mean $75.4, median $75. Current price of $64.78 sits near the bottom of that range, which nominally implies upside to the mean of ~16%. Two things temper that read. First, the sentiment trend is degrading, and the most recently published, deeply-researched view (Barclays Equal Weight, $58 target, dated 6/22/2026) sits below the current price. Second, analyst targets function as floors rather than ceilings only on cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks; DAR is inflecting, but the earnings stream is policy-contingent and mean-reverting, so consensus targets here are not obviously stale.

The valuation dimension deserves particular skepticism in this environment — a low headline multiple has repeatedly been a value-trap signal rather than a buy signal, and here the compressed forward multiple reflects a market that has correctly identified DGD earnings-quality risk. The forward multiple is not stretched, but neither is there a specific, identifiable reason the market is materially wrong at $64.78. The stock is fairly-to-slightly-richly-priced against a recovery that is largely understood. We score Valuation 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Darling's moat is its collection network. Aggregating fats, proteins, and used cooking oil at scale is a genuine barrier — few competitors can feed low-CI fuel markets with the same feedstock breadth. The 2026 North-America-only feedstock rule under 45Z is structurally favorable: it makes domestic and regional collectors like Darling more valuable relative to importers, and this is the crux of the bull case on competitive position. The collagen franchise (~30% of world production, being consolidated via the Tessenderlo JV) is a second durable position with pricing power against synthetic hydrocolloids and artificial casings.

Trajectory is improving on both the feedstock-advantage and mix-shift fronts. Against that, the fuel side competes in a market where economics are set by RFS/RINs, California LCFS, federal 45Z, and EU RED III, meaning a meaningful share of DAR's earnings power is determined by regulatory design rather than competitive execution. That caps how much credit the competitive position deserves, since a strong network is worth less if the policy that monetizes it shifts. We score Competitive Position 6.5 — a genuinely strengthening moat in feedstock and collagen, offset by policy-set fuel economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Biofuel policy and tax-credit dependence is the dominant risk.** DGD economics hinge on 45Z (base credit $1.06/gal, extended through 2029 by OBBBA, but requiring US production and, from 2026, feedstocks sourced exclusively from US/Mexico/Canada), RIN values under the RFS, and LCFS credits. Any adverse revision, non-enforcement, or eligibility failure hits the largest earnings lever directly. The SAF credit falling to $1.00/gal after 2025 is already a headwind to sustainable-aviation-fuel economics.

**DGD earnings volatility and LCM swings.** DGD's FY2025 results included a favorable $140M LCM inventory adjustment (versus a $176M unfavorable one in 2024), distorting comparability. Because DGD is equity-accounted, its volatility whipsaws Darling's reported earnings and makes any single quarter a poor guide to run-rate power.

**Cash-conversion dependence.** Debt service and deleveraging depend on upstreaming cash from foreign subsidiaries and DGD distributions, and the latter proved unreliable this quarter.

**Tariffs and feedstock sourcing.** The 2026 feedstock-origin restriction plus tariffs on Brazil, Canada, China, EU, and Mexico imports create sourcing complexity; the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling declaring some tariffs unlawful adds uncertainty (management flags potential tariff recoveries as an offset).

**Legacy environmental liability.** Darling is a named PRP at the Diamond Alkali/Lower Passaic River Superfund site (remedies estimated at $1.38B for the lower 8.3 miles plus $441M for the upper 9 miles). Settlements to date have been nominal (~$0.9M combined) and Darling asserts no COC discharge, but contribution litigation and a Consent Decree appeal remain open. Total contingency reserves ~$87.6M. Two multiemployer pension plans could produce material withdrawal liabilities; five plans are in critical/red-zone status.

Most of the sector-level cyclicality is already priced. What is less well priced is the specific combination of policy revision risk on a stream that now dominates earnings plus the cash-conversion lag. These are concrete, near-term, asymmetric downside risks, which is why we score Risk Factors 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 45.7% versus 36.1% historical volatility (rich by ~9.6 points), sitting at the 67th percentile of the current chain, with term structure in backwardation (near-term 45.7% > long-term 37.6%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.4% near-term, widening to -5.9% at six months), and put/call OI is very low at 0.09 (down from 0.38), indicating light hedging demand and no directional put-heavy positioning. Short interest is modest at 5.3% of float, 3.5 days to cover. The backwardation and rich near-term IV point to an anticipated catalyst — most likely the next quarterly print and DGD distribution update. For a fundamental holder, elevated IV argues against paying up for near-term optionality and favors patience over chasing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75–85)

The DGD recovery is durable: 45Z is locked in through 2029 at $1.06/gal, the North-America-only feedstock rule structurally advantages Darling's collection network, and higher RVOs support RIN values. Estimate revisions reflect the market systematically underestimating the inflection. The core Feed business is improving on its own, the portfolio is being upgraded toward higher-margin collagen and controlled feedstock (Tessenderlo, UPI Bovinos, grease-trap exit), and DGD cash distributions are resuming ($68.7M post-quarter). On $5.29 next-year EPS at a ~15x multiple as cash conversion normalizes and deleveraging proceeds, the stock reaches the mid-$70s to $85. This maps to the sell-side high end.

### Bear Case ($48–55)

DGD earnings are policy-contingent and mean-reverting. A 45Z reinterpretation, RVO reset, or LCFS credit weakness compresses the fuel earnings stream that now dominates results. The Q1 cash-conversion gap persists, forcing continued capital injections into DGD and stalling deleveraging on a $4.13B debt load in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The earnings surprise track record (three misses in four quarters before Q1'26) reasserts itself as DGD volatility returns. Barclays' $58 view proves right and the rally unwinds toward the high-$40s to mid-$50s as the policy tailwind gets fully discounted and a soft quarter arrives. This is roughly 15–26% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The recovery is real but largely understood by the market, the stock trades above the mean sell-side base case, and the two most important variables (45Z durability and DGD cash cadence) are genuinely uncertain rather than clearly favorable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of DGD cash distributions at or above run-rate need (roughly $100M+ per quarter), confirming the earnings recovery is converting to cash and funding deleveraging, with net debt declining as a result.
- Forward P/E compressing below ~11x on stable-or-rising estimates (i.e., a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s with fundamentals intact), which would restore genuine asymmetry.
- Confirmation that the 45Z regime and RVO levels are locked with no adverse reinterpretation through 2027, removing the dominant policy overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any adverse 45Z reinterpretation, RVO reduction, or LCFS credit deterioration that materially cuts DGD earnings power.
- A return to the miss pattern — a quarter where DGD swings back to a loss or a large unfavorable LCM adjustment resurfaces — combined with continued capital injections into the JV and no distributions.
- Net debt rising rather than falling over the next two quarters, indicating the deleveraging plan is not funding itself.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (approximately late July/early August) and the accompanying DGD distribution disclosure are the natural reassessment point. The score trajectory (6.2 → 6.0 → 5.7, three consecutive HOLDs) reflects a steady derating of valuation, management alignment, and risk scores; the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend — the fundamentals have improved sharply, but so has the price, leaving the risk/reward roughly balanced. The prior HOLDs are being maintained with a raised bar: we would need either a cheaper entry or confirmed cash conversion to move constructive.

## Conclusion

Darling has executed a genuine V-shaped recovery, and the improvement in both the DGD JV and the core Feed business is real, not an accounting artifact. The problem for a new buyer at $64.78 is that most of this is now understood and priced: the stock has run ~79% off its low, trades above the mean analyst base case, faces a fresh Equal Weight initiation below the current price, and carries a degrading sentiment trend. The forward multiple (~13.4x current-year, ~12.2x next-year) is reasonable but not compelling given that a large share of the earnings stream is set by tax-credit policy that can be revised, and that Q1's earnings surge outran cash by a wide margin.

We rate DAR a HOLD. It is a fairly-to-slightly-richly-priced recovery in a business with a strengthening feedstock moat and improving mix, but with policy dependence and cash-conversion risk that argue against paying up here. The setup that would turn us constructive: a cheaper entry (low-to-mid $50s) or two clean quarters of DGD cash distributions funding visible deleveraging. Absent one of those, the risk/reward is balanced.
