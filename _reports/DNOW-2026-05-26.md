## Executive Summary

DNOW Inc completed its transformative all-stock acquisition of MRC Global in November 2025, creating the largest PVF (pipe, valves, fittings) distributor in North America with ~$4.7B annualized revenue capacity and approximately 300 locations. The combined entity holds a dominant competitive position with meaningful diversification into less-cyclical end markets (gas utilities, downstream MRO, data centers). However, the near-term picture is severely clouded by persistent ERP system failures inherited from MRC Global, which compressed Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margins to 3.3% (versus a normalized ~7-7.5% run-rate), drove a -88% EPS miss, and triggered a securities fraud investigation.

The stock trades at $13.07, down 24% from its 52-week high of $17.26, with consensus EPS estimates for FY2026 cut by 68% over the past 90 days (from $1.04 to $0.33). While purchase-accounting artifacts (inventory step-up charges, LIFO adoption) exaggerate the reported losses, the ERP issue appears structural rather than transient — management described "design architecture resulting in inefficiencies for certain core processes." The $50M Q1 2026 share repurchase signals management confidence, but the combination of falling estimates, negative earnings momentum, securities litigation overhang, and 2.3x net leverage on a cyclical business in a softening oil market makes this a show-me situation.

## Company Overview

DNOW Inc, headquartered in Houston, distributes energy and industrial products including pipe, valves, fittings, pumps, and fabricated equipment. The company operates through three segments: U.S. (81% of revenue), Canada (8%), and International (11%). End-market exposure: Upstream (62%), Midstream (21%), Downstream & Industrial (10%), and Gas Utilities (7% pre-merger, growing to ~15-20% combined).

The November 2025 MRC Global merger approximately doubled the company's size, adding $2B+ in annual revenue, deepening its midstream and gas utility presence, and expanding the product portfolio into valve automation and measurement. The combined entity operates ~300 locations with ~5,300 employees.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue was $2.82B (+18.8% YoY), with Q1 2026 at $1.18B (approximately doubled from $599M in Q1 2025, reflecting the first full post-merger quarter). Organic growth has been minimal — the 2022-2024 trajectory showed only 2.2% growth in 2024.

**Profitability:** Reported figures are distorted by merger accounting:
- FY2025 operating loss: ($93M), driven by $135M inventory step-up charges, $62M transaction costs, and $27M LIFO reserve
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA: $39M (3.3% margin), far below normalized ~7.4%
- Q1 2026 adjusted EPS: $0.01 vs. consensus ~$0.08 (88% miss)
- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA: $209M (7.4% margin)

The adjusted gross profit margin held at 23.1% in both 2024 and 2025, indicating underlying pricing power is intact. The margin compression is attributable to ERP-driven operational inefficiencies and purchase accounting, not fundamental competitive deterioration.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Long-term debt: $571M (Q1 2026), up from effectively zero pre-merger
- Net debt leverage: 2.3x trailing adjusted EBITDA
- Current ratio: 2.34x
- Goodwill: $652M (~28% of total assets)
- Revolving credit: $850M capacity, maturing November 2030

**Cash Flow:** Q1 2026 saw $95M cash used in operations (working capital build + integration costs). FY2025 operating cash flow was $155M; FCF was $134M. The company burned cash while simultaneously repurchasing $50M of stock, funded by the credit facility.

**Altman Z-Score:** 2.63 (grey zone) — reflects acquisition-driven metrics distortion rather than operational distress.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term outlook is negative.** Consensus FY2026 EPS has been slashed from $1.04 to $0.33 over 90 days (-68%), with current-quarter estimates falling from $0.15 to $0.09 in just 30 days (-44%). The ERP system issues are actively destroying revenue in legacy MRC operations — management acknowledged revenue declines due to order processing failures, inventory visibility problems, and customer service degradation.

**Medium-term growth vectors:**
1. **Synergy realization:** Management raised the annualized synergy target to $30M for 2026, supported by procurement optimization and duplicated cost removal. Full run-rate synergies likely $50-75M+.
2. **Gas utility modernization:** 34% of U.S. gas distribution lines are >40 years old, providing a multi-decade replacement cycle. Post-merger, gas utilities represent ~15-20% of combined revenue — these are regulated, multi-year programs with low cyclicality.
3. **Data center infrastructure:** DNOW is negotiating master service agreements for PVF in data center cooling systems — a structural growth market.
4. **LNG/natural gas buildout:** Gas-fired power generation and LNG export capacity expansion support midstream demand.

**Headwinds:** WTI at $65/bbl (down 14.5% YoY), U.S. rig count declining (561 average in 2025, -6.3%), industry capex discipline limiting upstream growth, and tariff-driven project cancellations in H2 2025.

The growth trajectory is binary: if ERP fixes succeed and energy markets stabilize, FY2027 earnings power is likely $0.94-$1.27 per share (current consensus). If ERP remediation takes longer or oil prices weaken further, 2027 estimates will follow 2026 downward.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.07, DNOW trades at a market cap of $2.38B. Trailing P/E is negative (reported EPS -$0.76). On FY2026E consensus of $0.33, the stock trades at 39.6x — expensive, but this represents trough earnings during the ERP disruption year.

The relevant valuation framework is normalized earnings power:
- FY2027E consensus: $0.94/share → 13.9x forward P/E
- If synergies fully realized + ERP fixed: $1.20-1.40/share earnings power → 9.3-10.9x P/E
- Book value per share: ~$14.50 (estimated) → 0.9x P/B

Against peers trading at a median 28.5x P/E, DNOW appears cheap on a normalized basis. The discount reflects execution risk from the ERP crisis and cyclical headwinds. The two covering analysts both rate the stock a Buy with a $16.00 price target (22% upside from current).

The valuation is not egregiously cheap given the magnitude of near-term uncertainty. A pure asset-value floor exists around $10-11 (tangible book minus goodwill impairment risk), providing downside support. But realizing the $15-16 fair value requires demonstrable earnings recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

The MRC Global merger creates a clear competitive advantage. DNOW is now the dominant national PVF distributor with:
- Broadest product inventory enabling same-day fulfillment of complex, multi-line orders
- Purchasing scale creating vendor negotiating power
- Dual digital platforms (DigitalNOW® + MRCGO™) deepening customer integration
- Geographic coverage across all major energy basins
- Approved Manufacturer Listing processes creating switching costs

The PVF distribution industry is highly fragmented — competition comes primarily from small regional distributors and direct manufacturer sales. Scale matters because customers value single-source procurement for complex projects. The combined DNOW/MRC platform is difficult to replicate.

Post-merger revenue mix is more defensive: gas utilities (regulated multi-year programs), downstream MRO (non-discretionary), and emerging data center demand reduce the company's historical dependence on cyclical upstream spending.

## Risk Assessment

**ERP System Failure (Critical, Ongoing):** Management described "design architecture" issues, not merely migration glitches. The system is "slow, impedes customer service, requires more resources, increases safety stock." Remediation may require migrating MRC operations to DNOW's system ($55M capex budget includes "new ERP implementation"). Timeline for resolution is unclear; management declined to provide full-year 2026 guidance due to this uncertainty.

**Securities Fraud Investigation:** Hagens Berman is investigating whether management knew about persistent ERP problems before closing the merger on November 6, 2025, while publicly characterizing them as "isolated" and "one-time." The disclosure gap between November 5 assurances and February 20 revelations creates meaningful litigation exposure. Even if meritless, the investigation creates an overhang on the stock.

**Oil Price Cyclicality:** With 62% upstream revenue exposure and WTI at ~$65/bbl, a further decline would pressure volumes. U.S. drilling activity is already declining. However, supply disruption scenarios create potential upside optionality here.

**Leverage Risk:** Net debt at 2.3x adjusted EBITDA is manageable in normal conditions but creates vulnerability if cash flows are pressured during a cyclical downturn while simultaneously absorbing ERP-driven inefficiencies and integration costs.

**Goodwill Impairment:** $652M of goodwill (28% of assets) could face impairment testing if sustained underperformance continues. This would be a non-cash charge but signals value destruction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($15.50-16.50, +19-26%):**
- ERP issues are fixed by Q3/Q4 2026, restoring adjusted EBITDA margins to 7%+
- $30M+ synergies begin flowing through P&L
- Inventory step-up charges fully wash through by mid-2026
- Oil prices stabilize or rise, supporting energy capex
- Diversified end-market mix (gas utilities, data centers) provides earnings stability
- Normalized FY2027 EPS of $0.94-1.27 at 14-16x = $13-20 range, with momentum toward the upper half

**Bear Case ($9.50-10.50, -20-27%):**
- ERP remediation proves more expensive and time-consuming than expected (12-18 months)
- Oil prices decline further, compressing upstream volumes
- Securities fraud investigation results in settlement or reputational damage
- Synergy realization disappoints
- Goodwill impairment charge materializes
- Leverage becomes problematic as cash flows remain depressed
- Stock trades toward tangible book value

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin recovers to >5.5% (halfway to normalized), demonstrating ERP progress
2. Full-year 2026 guidance provided with specific synergy and margin targets
3. Securities investigation resolved or dismissed without material settlement

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Adjusted EBITDA margin remains below 4% for two consecutive quarters
2. Management announces ERP system replacement (vs. remediation) with 18+ month timeline
3. Net leverage exceeds 3.0x trailing adjusted EBITDA
4. Material adverse finding in securities investigation

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (likely August 2026 for Q2 results). This is the first opportunity to assess whether ERP remediation is working — a clean beat on margins would be the most powerful signal.

## Conclusion

DNOW's strategic position is strong: the dominant PVF distributor with scale advantages, sticky customer relationships, and growing exposure to less-cyclical infrastructure markets (gas utilities, data centers, LNG). The MRC Global merger, while temporarily disruptive, creates a business with long-term earnings power of $1.00-1.40/share.

However, the near-term is dominated by execution uncertainty. The ERP failure is architectural, the securities investigation creates legal overhang, estimates are falling rapidly, and the most recent quarter missed badly. The stock needs to demonstrate tangible operational improvement before a higher-conviction call is warranted. At $13.07, the risk/reward is roughly balanced between the normalized earnings recovery thesis and the possibility that remediation costs, timeline, and cyclical headwinds prove worse than expected.

HOLD with a reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings. The stock is not expensive on a 12-18 month view if execution normalizes, but paying for that normalization before evidence arrives has historically been unprofitable in the Industrials sector.
