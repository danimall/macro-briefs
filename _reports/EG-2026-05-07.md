## Executive Summary

Everest Group is a Bermuda-domiciled global reinsurer trading at $351.90, representing 0.93x book value ($379.83/share) and approximately 7.1x forward P/E on consensus FY2026 EPS of $52.48. The company is exiting $2B of unprofitable retail insurance renewal rights (sold to AIG), executing a $1.2B adverse development cover, and refocusing on core global reinsurance and wholesale/specialty insurance. Q1 2026 results showed a strong earnings recovery ($16.08 EPS vs $14.39 estimate), validating early signs that the restructuring is generating improved underwriting results.

The fundamental tension is straightforward: on one side, a deeply discounted franchise (top-10 global reinsurer, A+ ratings, $2.1B investment income floor, 7x earnings) with a clear strategic direction. On the other, $2.0B in cumulative reserve charges over two years, negative outlooks from all three rating agencies, an ADC that is already substantially consumed in its first layer, and consensus estimates that are declining (-6.1% over 90 days for FY2026). The Q1 2026 beat provides a data point of progress, but two quarters of misses preceded it, and the underlying U.S. casualty reserve question remains unresolved.

This is a restructuring story where the magnitude of potential remaining liabilities is difficult to bound externally. The valuation provides margin of safety if reserves prove adequate, but the market's discount exists for defensible reasons. A HOLD rating reflects this genuine uncertainty, with clearly defined triggers for upgrade or downgrade.

## Company Overview

Everest Group operates two segments: Reinsurance (72.4% of 2025 GWP) and Insurance (27.1%). The Reinsurance segment writes global property and casualty reinsurance through brokers (90.2%) and directly, spanning property pro rata (36.3%), property catastrophe excess-of-loss (18.3%), casualty pro rata (21.3%), and casualty XOL (11.5%). The Insurance segment — historically a drag on returns — is undergoing radical reshaping after the October 2025 sale of retail commercial renewal rights to AIG.

The company had $62.5B in total assets, $15.5B in shareholders' equity, and $45.4B in investments and cash at year-end 2025. Operating subsidiaries hold A+ ratings from AM Best, A+ from S&P, and A1 from Moody's — though all three carry negative outlooks.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained in the near term by the strategic reshaping. Gross written premiums declined 2.9% in 2025 as the company exited unprofitable lines. Q1 2026 results show premiums falling further as the AIG transaction processes, though this is intentional portfolio pruning rather than competitive loss.

The medium-term growth path depends on: (1) successfully redeploying capital from exited lines into higher-return reinsurance and specialty insurance, (2) continued hardening in property reinsurance pricing, and (3) the investment income base growing with higher-for-longer rates.

Estimate revisions are negative across all time horizons: current quarter down -8.5% over 30 days, FY2026 down -2.6% over 30 days and -6.1% over 90 days, FY2027 down -1.0% over 30 days and -2.1% over 90 days. This negative revision trajectory suggests analysts remain uncertain about reserve adequacy and the pace of restructuring benefits flowing through to earnings.

The Reinsurance segment's combined ratio has been creeping higher (86.4% → 89.7% → 91.7%) even in a hardening market, suggesting either the company's book quality was worse than previously understood, or social inflation is proving more persistent than modeled.

## Valuation Assessment

At $351.90, EG trades at:
- 0.93x book value ($379.83/share)
- 7.1x forward P/E (trailing P/E per Phase 1 data also ~7.1x on $37.80 EPS)
- 6.7x FY2026E consensus of $52.48
- 5.8x FY2027E consensus of $60.75
- 2.3% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:**
- RNR: 5.0x P/E, 0.5% yield (but smaller, purer play, superior underwriting track record)
- RGA: 11.8x P/E, 1.8% yield (life reinsurance, different risk profile)
- Peer median P/E: 9.3x
- MTG: 9.3x, SPNT: 6.4x

EG trades below peer median P/E (7.1x vs 9.3x) and below book value. The discount relative to RGA reflects the P&C reserve uncertainty, the negative rating outlooks, and the multi-year underperformance (5-year TSR of 62% vs peer group 134%). The premium to RNR is harder to justify given RNR's superior underwriting execution, but EG's greater scale and diversification partially explain it.

The valuation is objectively cheap if reserves prove adequate. At FY2027E earnings of $60.75 and a conservative 8x multiple (still below peer median), fair value would be ~$486 — 38% upside. At 6x (reflecting continued skepticism), fair value would be ~$365 — limited 4% upside from current levels. The market is pricing approximately a 6.5-7x steady-state multiple, which assumes either permanently elevated reserve charges or structurally lower earnings quality vs. peers.

**Book value provides a floor:** At $379.83 book with investment-grade fixed income backing, the 0.93x P/BV implies the market believes ongoing reserve charges will erode book value. If instead book value grows at even 10% annually (vs. 17.6% in 2025), the stock's discount to book widens further, creating mean-reversion upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Everest is a top-10 global P&C reinsurer competing with Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, SCOR, and publicly listed peers like RNR and RGA. The franchise has genuine competitive advantages:

- Scale ($17.7B GWP enables broad diversification)
- A+ financial strength ratings (still intact, enabling access to highest-quality cedents)
- Low operating expenses (reinsurance other UW expense ratio of 2.5%)
- Mt. Logan Re third-party capital vehicle (fee income + risk transfer)
- Growing investment income ($2.1B) provides earnings stability regardless of underwriting cycles

However, competitive position has been weakened by the reserve issues. The 5-year TSR gap vs. peers (62% vs 134%) is material evidence that the company has not executed as well as competitors in risk selection and pricing. The negative rating outlooks, if converted to actual downgrades, would trigger treaty termination provisions and potentially impair the franchise's ability to write business at target margins. This is the single most important competitive risk to monitor.

The reinsurance market appears to be softening modestly in 2026 after hardening in 2023-2024. Everest is selectively pulling back from casualty XOL where pricing is deemed inadequate — a disciplined response but one that will constrain near-term growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks (not fully priced):**

1. **Further U.S. casualty reserve deterioration.** The ±7.5% variability on $34.3B of gross reserves implies potential deficiency of up to $2.6B. The ADC's first layer was exhausted within 3 months; remaining protection is ~$997M. If social inflation continues accelerating (litigation funding volumes, nuclear verdicts), another material reserve charge in H2 2026 or 2027 is plausible. Probability of material additional charge (>$500M): 30-40%.

2. **Rating downgrade.** All three agencies on negative outlook. A downgrade below A+ would trigger termination provisions in many reinsurance treaties. Probability of at least one downgrade within 12 months: 25-35%.

3. **Restructuring execution.** Simultaneous exits, organizational changes (8+ executive departures/appointments in 12 months), and $150M in restructuring charges create operational risk. The AIG transaction transitions must be managed without disrupting retained business.

4. **Bermuda tax uncertainty.** OECD restriction of ETA benefits to ~20% for 2 years creates genuine uncertainty about the company's long-term effective tax rate and the value of its deferred tax assets.

5. **Catastrophe exposure.** 1-in-100-year PML of $2.4B (11% of equity) for Southeast U.S. wind. Active Atlantic hurricane season would compound reserve issues.

## Investment Thesis

- Q1 2026 beat confirms restructuring is working; combined ratio improves to sub-95% by FY2026
- Reserve charges prove adequate — no additional material development in 2026
- Rating agencies stabilize outlooks by mid-2027 after two clean years
- Investment income continues growing toward $2.3-2.5B annually

**Bear Case ($280-310, 12-20% downside):**
- Further material reserve charge ($500M-$1B) in H2 2026 or 2027
- Rating agency downgrade triggers treaty terminations, competitive impairment
- Reinsurance market softening compresses margins further
- Insurance segment tail liabilities exceed ADC remaining capacity
- Book value eroded; stock trades to 0.75-0.80x book
- Restructuring costs exceed $150M estimate; executive turnover continues
- 5.5x FY2026E $48 (reduced for charge) = $264; 0.80x adjusted BV ~$285

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters without material prior-year reserve charges (>$100M unfavorable development), demonstrating reserve adequacy
2. At least one rating agency revises outlook from negative to stable
3. Stock trading below 0.85x book value ($323) with fundamentals unchanged or improving

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Additional reserve charge >$500M in any single quarter
2. Any rating agency executes an actual downgrade below A+/A1
3. FY2026 consensus EPS declines below $45 (implying another major charge)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) is the next critical checkpoint. Two clean quarters would provide meaningful confidence; another miss or reserve charge would confirm the bear case is unfolding.

## Conclusion

Everest Group presents a genuinely uncertain investment case. The valuation (7.1x forward earnings, 0.93x book) provides meaningful margin of safety *if* the restructuring succeeds and reserves prove adequate. Q1 2026 results offer encouraging early evidence. But the magnitude of potential remaining liabilities ($2.6B range high on reserve variability), the negative rating outlooks from all three agencies, declining analyst estimates, and the history of cumulative charges over two years prevent higher conviction.

The consensus analyst distribution (3 Strong Buy, 3 Buy, 10 Hold, 0 Sell) reflects this ambivalence — a stock that's cheap enough to avoid selling but uncertain enough that few are comfortable recommending aggressive buying. The median target of $366 implies only 4% upside, suggesting consensus views the stock as fairly valued given its risk profile.

A HOLD recommendation reflects the honest assessment: the next 2-3 quarters of reserve development and rating agency actions will determine whether this is a deep value recovery or a value trap with more downside. The data available today does not resolve this question with sufficient confidence to take a directional position.
