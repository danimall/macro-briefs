## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the largest acreage holder and, per its own 10-K, the top producer in the Williston Basin: 1,302,921 net leasehold acres, 5,025 gross operated producing wells, 78% average operated working interest, and 917.5 MMBoe of proved reserves at year-end 2025. It runs an explicitly harvest-oriented model: roughly $1.4B of annual capital to hold oil volumes near 161 MBopd, with the balance of cash flow returned through a $5.20 annualized base dividend and buybacks that have cut the share count 8.1% in 18 months.

Q2 2026 was an outlier quarter, not a run rate. Realized crude was $93.99/Bbl at a $1.27 premium to WTI, versus $61.62 a year earlier, following the late-February Middle East escalation that lifted WTI spot more than 50% by the end of Q1. That produced $923.5M of adjusted EBITDA, $1,116.2M of operating cash flow and $413.4M of adjusted free cash flow. Management's own second-half planning deck is $75 WTI, roughly $19/Bbl below Q2's realization, and the forward curve moved lower during the quarter even as spot spiked, which is why Chord's net derivative position swung from a $164.6M liability to a $40.8M asset while realized oil hedge losses ran $106.2M in the quarter alone. Anyone annualizing Q2's $6.44 adjusted EPS is extrapolating a price environment neither the curve nor management expects to persist.

The valuation is cheap on every asset-based and cash-flow measure: about 2.85x 2026E adjusted EBITDA, 0.94x total PV-10, 1.03x standardized measure, a 17% guided FCF yield and 7.0x current-year consensus EPS. Cheapness in a single-basin, high-decline oil producer with 6.2 years of proved-developed reserve life is partly a correct discount rather than a pure mispricing. Three things have deteriorated since our last review: 30-day EPS revisions are down 6.0% for 2026 and 7.7% for 2027, FY26 LOE guidance was raised to $10.05-10.55/Boe, and Q4 oil volumes are guided down to 156-159 MBopd from ~163 in Q3, leaving the 2026 exit rate flat-to-below the Q1 starting point. We move the overall score to 5.65 from 6.0 and maintain HOLD, with the holding case resting on the capital return rather than on appreciation.

## Company Overview

Chord is a single-basin, oil-weighted independent E&P. Essentially all oil production sits in the North Dakota and Montana portions of the Williston Basin, targeting the Middle Bakken and Three Forks; the only other asset is a non-operated Marcellus gas interest (116.9 MMcfpd at $2.10/Mcf in Q2 2026). FY2025 production averaged 276,620 Boepd across 676 employees. Only about 6% of net Williston acreage is federal mineral acreage, which materially reduces NEPA/BLM permitting exposure relative to federal-land-heavy peers.

The scale claim is testable rather than promotional. Contiguous acreage is the enabling condition for the 4-mile lateral program: 26 wells executed as of early Q3 2026 with capital costs described as in line with expectations, alongside the basin's deepest measured-depth well (33,810 ft) and its first "trimulfrac" completion. Chord also markets its own barrels in-house, which produces a large low-margin revenue gross-up (discussed below) but also a deliberate margin-capture choice on differentials.

The return-of-capital framework is formal and leverage-gated at a $65/Bbl WTI and $3/MMBtu deck: below 0.5x leverage, 75%+ of adjusted FCF is returned; below 1.0x, 50%+; above 1.0x, base dividend only. Leverage fell below half a turn at the end of Q2, and management guided the Q3 payout to 75% from 54% in Q2.

## Financial Analysis

### The trailing statements are distorted; use adjusted and cash measures

FY2025 GAAP net income was $44.5M on $4,877.1M of revenue. Two non-cash items drive that: a $530.6M goodwill write-off taken entirely in Q2 2025 (goodwill went from $530.6M to zero; Q2 2025 GAAP loss was $(389.9)M, or $(6.71) basic per share), and DD&A of $1,470.2M. The provider-supplied trailing and forward P/E of -114.8 alongside a positive $0.739 EPS is internally inconsistent and should be disregarded; GAAP EPS was $1.90 in Q1 2026 and $9.28 in Q2 2026.

A further wrinkle: per Q1 2026 Note 12, an error in the Q2 2025 tax provision relating to goodwill impairment's effect on deferred taxes on unremitted earnings produced a $41.8M benefit recognized out of period in Q1 2026, taking Q1's effective tax rate to (15.2)%. Corrected to the proper periods, FY2025 net income would be roughly $86.3M rather than $44.5M, and Q1 2026 roughly $66.8M rather than $108.6M (EPS closer to $1.18 than $1.90). There is no Item 4.02 non-reliance filing, management concluded disclosure controls were effective, and no ICFR changes were reported, so this reads as an isolated control lapse in a technical area, not a systemic issue. It does mean both reported figures overstate the cleanliness of period earnings.

Two decompositions matter. First, revenue growth: the "+84%" headline on total revenue is flattered by purchased oil and gas sales of $678.4M in Q2 2026 (31% of total revenue), which carried a 1.0% gross margin across 1H 2026 ($1,193.4M of sales against $1,181.5M of cost). Wellhead revenue grew 57.2%. Any revenue multiple or revenue-growth screen on Chord is measuring a pass-through marketing book.

Second, oil dominance within the BOE mix: oil was 94.7% of wellhead revenue on 57.8% of BOE volumes. Realized gas was $0.94/Mcf and NGLs $9.25/Bbl (about 10% of WTI); backing out the Marcellus contribution implies a Williston gas realization near $0.47/Mcf. Chord has minimal exposure to gas-price weakness and essentially no participation in gas-demand upside. Valuing the 42% non-oil BOE volumes at oil-equivalent value would materially overstate the asset.

### Balance sheet

At 6/30/2026: cash $611.6M; total debt $1,500.0M face ($750M 6.000% due 2030, $750M 6.750% due 2033); revolver undrawn against a $2.75B borrowing base and $2.0B elected commitments, both reaffirmed in the May 2026 redetermination; total liquidity $2,581.2M; equity $8,357.3M. Net debt is $888.4M, about 0.30x guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA of ~$3.0B. The current-ratio covenant includes unused revolver commitments in current assets and is effectively non-binding with the revolver undrawn. There are no maturities before October 2030.

The cost of that resilience: total debt rose from $842.6M (2024) to $1.5B, and cash interest from roughly $56M to a guided $102-106M for 2026, on a 6.375% blended coupon. This is a manageable, deliberately termed-out structure, and it is the primary reason we hold financial health at 7.2 despite the earnings distortions.

### Free cash flow definition

Management's adjusted FCF is EBITDA less capex, cash interest and cash taxes; it excludes working capital. Q2 2026: $923.5 − $416.7 − $26.0 − $67.4 = $413.4M, matching exactly. That distinction bites in Q1, when a $264.8M receivables build (price-driven, since WTI rose >50% into quarter-end) reduced GAAP operating cash flow to $507.5M and OCF-minus-capex to $156.2M against a reported $321.2M. Receivables of $1,338.0M at 6/30/2026 are roughly 56 days of total revenue, inflated by the marketing gross-up and a disclosed 20-90 day settlement lag, and Chord states it does not generally require collateral. The allowance for credit losses rose from $11.5M to $14.1M in one quarter with $2.5M of Q1 joint-interest credit losses — small, but the direction suggests some non-operating working-interest partners are strained.

### Capital returns

Shares outstanding: 60,071k (12/31/2024) → 55,197k (6/30/2026), an 8.1% reduction. Treasury cost rose $588.1M over that span. Derived average repurchase prices: roughly $105.4 in FY2025 and $132.4 in 1H 2026 ($133.47 in Q2). The pattern of buying more heavily at lower prices is correct, and the 1H average now sits modestly below the $136.89 close. All warrants expired 9/1/2025, so 55.2M outstanding against 56.0M fully diluted is only ~1.4% of residual dilution, and performance awards vesting above 100% of target settle in cash rather than shares.

The $5.20 annualized dividend costs about $287M per year: 4.5x covered by guided 2026 adjusted FCF of ~$1.3B, and roughly 2.4x covered on the FY2025 low-price analogue (realized $62.78 produced $692.7M of OCF-minus-capex). The dividend is the resilient part of this story.

## Growth Analysis

There is no volume growth thesis here, and management is not pretending otherwise. FY26 guidance holds oil at 160.2-161.8 MBopd and capex at $1,360-1,440M, with Q4 capex falling to $242-292M from $416.7M in Q2 as the second frac crew is dropped in 3Q26, and Q4 oil volumes guided to 156-159 MBopd because completions were pulled forward. The exit rate is flat-to-slightly-below the Q1 starting point, which identifies ~$1.4B as approximately maintenance capital for ~161 MBopd.

Reserve life frames the reinvestment burden: 917.5 MMBoe total proved against 101.0 MMBoe of 2025 production is 9.1 years; proved developed alone is 6.2 years. The company must spend $1.4B annually to stand still and must extend inventory over any decade-length horizon, in a basin where the 10-K concedes competitors "may be able to pay more for productive oil and gas properties."

Normalized EBITDA is derived by stripping the spike: 1H 2026 delivered $1,636.5M, so the FY guide of ~$3.0B implies ~$1,363.5M for 2H at $75 WTI, annualizing to about $2.7B. Normalized FCF then runs $2.7B − $1.4B capex − $0.10B interest − ~$0.18B cash tax ≈ $1.05B.

Asset-value support is real. Proved-developed PV-10 of $6.41B alone covers 75% of EV, with 62% of PUD PV-10 covering the remainder and all unbooked inventory unpriced, and that PV-10 is struck on a 2025 SEC deck consistent with realized oil of $62.78, far below current strip. The precise deck price is not restated in the sections reviewed, so treat the PV-10 price basis as approximate rather than confirmed.

Against peers, Chord's 3.80% dividend yield exceeds the 2.7% peer median, and its 7.0x forward multiple is below MTDR (13.8x trailing) and RRC (10.1x), above CNX (4.4x). The peer set is heterogeneous — CNX and RRC are gas names whose multiples reflect a different commodity — so peer comparison here is directional at best. CHRD sits only 9.9% below its 52-week high while every listed peer is 13-24% below theirs, meaning Chord has already captured more of the oil-spike re-rating than the group.

The honest read: cheapness is partly structural compensation for single-basin concentration, 6.2-year PD life, rising unit costs and near-zero byproduct value. Since the July 19 review, the price is up roughly 7% while 30-day 2026 and 2027 estimates were cut 6.0% and 7.7% — multiple expansion on falling estimates, which is a modest deterioration in the entry proposition rather than an improvement. We hold valuation at 5.9: attractive on absolute yield and asset value, not a mispricing with an identified catalyst strong enough to override the oil-price dependence.

## Competitive Landscape

Scale in a mature, consolidating basin buys three specific advantages the 10-K names: long connected blocks enabling 4-mile laterals, multi-well multi-formation pads from single surface locations, and centralized production, gas and water handling that lowers per-well fixed cost. Chord is the largest acreage holder with 89% of proved reserves on operated properties.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed. Improving: differentials moved from $(2.15) to a $1.27 premium to WTI year over year, and the 10-Q attributes this to pipeline and rail expansions that "reduced prior constraints on crude oil takeaway capacity"; FY26 differential guidance is $(0.80) to $0.00. In-house marketing captures margin that peers outsource. The 4-mile program is early but on plan.

Deteriorating: LOE per Boe has risen from $9.73 (2025) to $10.28 (Q2 2026) with FY26 guidance raised to a $10.30 midpoint on higher workovers and non-operated LOE, against a 10,528-gross-well legacy stock and $479M of ARO. DD&A per Boe has climbed every year from $9.46 (2023) to $15.70, reflecting fair-value step-ups from Enerplus and XTO plus higher-cost extended-lateral development. Cash cost plus DD&A now approaches $26/Boe before G&A, production taxes and gathering. Byproduct value is negligible.

Net: a defensible leadership position with improving realizations and worsening unit costs, in a basin where inventory extension requires competing for assets against better-capitalized buyers. Competitive position 5.9, unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-basin concentration** is named explicitly in the forward-looking risk lists of both the FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q. There is no diversifying second area of scale.

**Dakota Access Pipeline.** "The possible shutdown of the Dakota Access Pipeline" is carried as a named risk in both filings. We were unable to verify the current procedural status of the Army Corps' Lake Oahe easement review or any pending court deadline, so no probability or timing claim should be attached to this. Current conditions argue the risk is contingent rather than active: a $1.27/Bbl premium to WTI in Q2 indicates ample takeaway.

**Oil price with a 2027 hedge gap.** At 3/31/2026 Chord held 16.04 MMBbl of 2026 oil hedges (roughly 36% of remaining 2026 oil), which cost $106.2M of realized losses in Q2 alone. For 2027 only ~8.05 MMBbl is hedged — about 14% of a year's oil. The next twelve months are close to a pure oil-price expression in both directions. Post-quarter additions were struck materially higher ($80.00 floor/$100.71 ceiling for 2026; $70.82/$82.68 for 2027), so management is hedging into strength, which caps the upside it monetizes.

This is where the price basis matters. At $136.89 the equity embeds a geopolitical supply premium that the futures curve and management's own $75 deck treat as temporary. A verified normalization of Hormuz transit — the specific, identifiable downside catalyst — would reset 2027 earnings power well below the $14.73 consensus. That asymmetry, unhedged, is the principal reason risk factors score 4.8.

**Minimum-volume commitments.** Aggregate crude MVCs nearly doubled in one quarter, from ~40.6 MMBbl (12/31/2025) to ~76.7 MMBbl (3/31/2026), with $587.3M of estimable future commitments and payments beginning in 2027. The benign explanation — purchase obligations inside the expanded marketing/blending book, which would be largely self-liquidating — is plausible given purchased sales scaled roughly 5x, but the disclosure aggregates "deliver, transport or purchase" and does not resolve it. The adverse alternative is new long-haul firm transportation creating deficiency exposure precisely as Q4 volumes are guided down. This is the single unresolved disclosure item we would put to management.

**Accounting and controls.** The out-of-period $41.8M tax correction approached the entire magnitude of reported FY2025 GAAP net income. Management's immateriality conclusion is defensible; the absence of an Item 4.02 filing and the effective-controls conclusion support restraint. It remains a control lapse in an area a reader would not otherwise scrutinize.

**Other:** receivables concentration disclosed but not quantified in the sections reviewed; North Dakota's 91% enforceable gas-capture goal with no assurance of continued compliance; ESA constraints from the Dakota Skipper and Golden Eagle; $32.6M letters of credit and $114.8M net surety exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Sustained Hormuz disruption keeps WTI in the $90s and Chord captures nearly all of it in 2027 with only 14% hedged. Normalized EBITDA moves toward $3.5-3.8B; at 3.5-3.75x EV/EBITDA the equity reaches roughly $205-238. Supporting mechanics: the payout steps to 75% of adjusted FCF at sub-0.5x leverage, so a $1.5B+ FCF year funds well over $1B of buybacks and dividends — a 14%+ total shareholder yield that compounds per-share value at an accelerating rate given the already-shrinking 55.2M share count. Differentials have flipped to a premium, the 4-mile program lowers maintenance capital per unit of production, and the asset is not richly valued against reserves: total PV-10 struck at a mid-$60s deck exceeds current EV. A second value-accretive bolt-on at the XTO deal's terms (no goodwill, no bargain gain, $542.2M for $557.2M of properties) would extend inventory without straining the balance sheet.

### Bear Case

A verified reopening of Hormuz transit, combined with OPEC+ completing the rollback of voluntary cuts in September, pulls WTI to $58-62. The FY2025 analogue is instructive: realized $62.78 produced $2,040.7M of operating cash flow and $692.7M of OCF-minus-capex, which against $1.4B of capex implies roughly $500M of simple FCF — a 6.5% yield on today's equity, still covering the dividend 2.4x but leaving little for buybacks. Applying 3.0-3.5x to a $2.1-2.2B EBITDA gives $99-119 per share. Compounding factors: unit costs are rising structurally (DD&A $15.70/Boe and climbing, LOE guidance raised), 6.2 years of PD life forces reinvestment or acquisitions into a competitive market, byproduct realizations near zero remove any offset, and the MVC step-up could become deficiency-payment exposure exactly as volumes decline. Insiders have sold into the strength with no offsetting purchases, and the CEO adopted a 10b5-1 plan in March to sell up to 60,000 vested shares through March 2027.

### Management Quality Assessment

Capital allocation is shareholder-aligned in substance: an 8.1% share count reduction in 18 months at a disciplined average price, a leverage-gated payout framework that forces balance-sheet priority, a cleared 2026 maturity wall, and a compensation design keyed to FCF, EBITDA, absolute and relative TSR with real downside (CEO compensation-actually-paid was negative $1,130,070 for FY2024). CEO total compensation of $3.6M is modest for a $7B+ company.

The offset is the insider record: across 25+ recent Form 4 transactions there are zero open-market purchases, with sales by the CFO, CAO, GC, COO and Chief Strategy Officer at $110-146, plus newly disclosed 10b5-1 plans for the CEO (60,000 shares) and Mr. Lou (30,000). Much of this clusters after the February/March vesting cycle and is programmatic, which is routine. We would normally discount it heavily, but insider selling carries more weight when it coincides with falling consensus estimates rather than rising ones, which is the case here. Director Dundas's 72,171-share gift removes his direct economic alignment without signaling a valuation view. Score 5.6.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis that survives all price scenarios is the capital return: a $5.20 dividend covered 4.5x at guided FCF and 2.4x at the FY2025 low-price analogue, plus buybacks stepping to 75% of adjusted FCF in Q3 — a 12.7% total shareholder yield at guided levels.

**Base case: $152 (+11% from $136.89).** Normalized EBITDA of $2.7B at management's $75 WTI deck at 3.5x EV/EBITDA gives $9.45B EV less $888.4M net debt, or $153 per share on 56.0M fully diluted shares. Cross-check: normalized FCF of $1.05B at a 13% required yield for a no-growth, high-decline single-basin producer gives $144; at 11%, $170. We anchor at $152. This sits below the $165.67 consensus mean and $170 median — appropriate, since consensus targets were largely set during the spike and the low target of $129 is already at market.

**Bull case: $205 (+50%).** Sustained $90s WTI, ~$3.5B EBITDA at 3.6x, or roughly 10x a $20+ 2027 EPS with 2027 unhedged. Aligns with the analyst high of $209.

**Bear case: $102 (-25%).** Verified Hormuz normalization plus completed OPEC+ rollback taking WTI to $58-62; $2.1-2.2B EBITDA at 3.25x. The 52-week low of $84.25 marks the deeper tail if costs continue rising into a low-price year.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 10-Q (expected within weeks — it discloses the updated hedge table, the Q2 cash flow statement and, we hope, the MVC composition), the October 2026 borrowing-base redetermination, and the Q3 print in early November, which is the first full quarter at the reduced one-frac-crew pace.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of:**
1. Price at or below $118-122 with FY2027 consensus stable at or above $14.50 — a forward multiple near 8x on unchanged estimates, restoring the asymmetry that the July-August rally consumed.
2. Evidence that unit costs have stopped rising: FY26 LOE delivered at or below the $10.30 guided midpoint with DD&A per Boe flat or lower sequentially for two quarters.
3. Disclosure resolving the crude MVC step-up as marketing purchase obligations rather than new firm transportation, plus the Q3 payout delivered at 75% of adjusted FCF with buyback pricing below the prevailing market.

**Downgrade to AVOID if any of:**
1. FY2027 consensus falls below $11.00 (a >25% cut from $14.73) on a sustained sub-$65 WTI environment, which would compress simple FCF toward dividend-only coverage.
2. The crude MVC increase is confirmed as firm long-haul transportation with deficiency exposure, coinciding with oil volumes below the 156 MBopd low end of Q4 guidance.
3. A DAPL adverse development that widens Williston differentials back beyond $(3.00)/Bbl, or an additional out-of-period accounting correction or ICFR change.
4. A large equity- or debt-funded acquisition that takes net debt/EBITDA above 1.0x and suspends the buyback.

## Conclusion

Chord is a well-financed, competently run harvest vehicle whose current earnings reflect a geopolitical price spike that its own planning assumptions treat as temporary. The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story: 0.30x net leverage, an undrawn and reaffirmed $2.75B borrowing base, $2.58B of liquidity and no maturity before October 2030. The capital return is credible and mechanical, with an 8.1% share count reduction already delivered and a payout stepping to 75% of adjusted FCF.

What argues against adding at $136.89: 30-day estimate cuts of 6-8% while the price rose 7%, LOE guidance raised, Q4 volumes guided below Q1, unit costs rising structurally through DD&A at $15.70/Boe, only 14% of 2027 oil hedged into an unresolved Hormuz situation, insiders selling with no offsetting purchases, and near-term implied volatility in backwardation at the 83rd percentile with a ±8.1% implied move. The stock has already captured more of the oil re-rating than its peer group, sitting 9.9% below its 52-week high against peer discounts of 13-24%.

Own it for the yield and the buyback, size it as an oil-price expression rather than a compounder, and require either a materially lower price or confirmed cost stabilization before adding. HOLD.
