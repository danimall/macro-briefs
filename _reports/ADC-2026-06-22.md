**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Agree Realty is a retail net-lease REIT that has built a defensible quality franchise on a tenant base where roughly 65% of annualized base rent comes from investment-grade-rated retailers, a higher mix than nearly all of its public net-lease peers. The portfolio of 2,756 single-tenant properties is 99.7% leased with a ~7.8-year weighted-average lease term, only one tenant on cash-basis accounting, and conservative leverage at ~27-28% net-debt-to-enterprise-value. The model works through external growth: in 2025 the company acquired 305 properties for $1.44B at a ~7.2% cap rate, funded with match-funded forward equity and laddered fixed-rate debt at a 4.04% blended cost. Per-share AFFO grew to $4.33 in 2025 (+4.6%), and Q1 2026 Core FFO/share rose 8.7% YoY to $1.13.

The central tension is the spread. ADC's growth engine depends on buying assets at cap rates above its marginal cost of capital, and a higher-for-longer rate path narrows that spread directly. The company's own 10-K notes that higher rates "reduced our ability to acquire properties at our historical rate." Consensus FFO/AFFO estimates confirm the pressure: current-year EPS estimates have been revised down 4.4% over 90 days and next-year estimates down 5.1%, with the most recent quarterly print mixed against a streak of small misses through 2025. The dividend is well covered (AFFO payout ~71%, monthly payer, ~4.4% yield), but per-share growth is decelerating into a rate environment that the broader stance treats as more durable than the market priced earlier in the year.

Against that, the standout positive signal is insider behavior. CEO Joey Agree, founder/Chairman Richard Agree, and two independent directors made sizable open-market purchases in May–June 2026 at $71–75, essentially at today's price of ~$73.25. That is a genuine alignment signal from people with the most information, and it tempers the downside case. We rate ADC a HOLD on income grounds: a high-quality, well-covered yield compounder with limited near-term appreciation asymmetry at ~17x AFFO until either the rate path eases (restoring spread-driven growth) or the valuation compresses toward a clearer entry.

## Company Overview

Agree Realty, founded in 1971 by Executive Chairman Richard Agree and NYSE-listed since 1994, is a fully integrated, internally managed retail net-lease REIT. As of March 31, 2026 it owned 2,756 properties totaling ~57.5M square feet across all 50 states, up from 2,674 properties / 55.5M sq ft at year-end 2025.

The business runs on three sourcing channels: acquisitions (the bulk of capital deployment), ground-up development (build-to-suit), and the Developer Funding Platform, where ADC funds third-party developers and takes fee-simple ownership on completion. Underwriting screens explicitly for e-commerce and recession resistance, fungible real estate, and avoidance of private-equity-sponsored, over-levered tenants. The portfolio carries no single tenant above 10% of ABR; sector exposure is led by grocery (10.3%), home improvement (9.0%), and convenience stores (7.7%), with geographic concentration in Texas (6.9%), Illinois (6.1%), Ohio (5.3%), Michigan (5.2%), and New York (5.0%).

The defining differentiator is credit quality: ~65.4% of ABR comes from investment-grade-rated tenants (66.8% at YE2025), versus a typical net-lease peer mix well below that. The company runs lean, with 90 full-time employees managing a ~$9.8B asset base. Market capitalization is ~$8.8B with ~120M shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

Top-line growth has been steady and acquisition-driven. Revenue progressed from $429.6M (2022) to $537.4M (2023), $616.8M (2024), and $718.2M (2025), the last a 16% YoY gain. Q1 2026 rental income was $200.7M, up 19% YoY. Net income reached $204.3M in 2025 (+8%), and Q1 2026 net income rose 32% YoY.

The metrics that matter for a REIT are per-share funds-from-operations measures. AFFO/share grew $3.95 (2023) → $4.14 (2024) → $4.33 (2025); Core FFO/share was $4.28 in 2025. Q1 2026 AFFO/share was $1.14. The per-share growth rate sits in the low-to-mid single digits, which is the consequence of funding external growth through equity issuance: share count rose from ~95M (2023) to ~120M (Q1 2026), roughly +26%, while AFFO/share still advanced. The investment case rests on management continuing to buy at cap rates above its blended cost of capital, so that per-share AFFO outpaces dilution.

Cash flow quality is high. Operating cash flow of $504M in 2025 comfortably exceeded $189M of net income, with all Piotroski signals positive. Operating margin runs ~47% and net margin ~28.5%.

The balance sheet is conservative for the sector. Total debt was ~$3.32B at YE2025, rising to ~$3.72B in Q1 2026 after drawing $250M on the 2031 term loan. Net-debt/enterprise value is ~27-28.5% and debt/equity ~0.56, low leverage relative to net-lease norms. Debt is largely fixed-rate, well-laddered, with floating exposure (commercial paper ~$470M) substantially hedged; a 100bp rate move shifts annual interest by only ~$4.7M. Liquidity at Q1 2026 was ~$2.28B (cash, $1.37B of unsettled forward equity, undrawn term loan, revolver capacity), and the company is in compliance with all covenants. The Altman Z of 2.15 ("grey zone") reflects the asset-heavy, leveraged REIT model rather than any distress, given the 99.7% occupancy, IG tenant base, and low LTV.

On capital allocation, ADC uses forward sale agreements and ATM programs to pre-fund acquisitions and match-fund growth, locking sale prices while delaying issuance, a shareholder-friendly mechanism. The dividend was raised twice in 2025 ($0.253 → $0.256 → $0.262), annualized at $3.144/share. Against 2025 AFFO/share of $4.33, the AFFO payout ratio is ~73%, well-covered, supported by a long history of increases.

The earnings track record is mixed. Over the trailing four quarters ADC has one beat and three misses: -5.9% (Q2 2025), -1.8% (Q3 2025), -1.4% (Q4 2025), then +3.1% (Q1 2026). The misses are small in absolute terms but the cadence, combined with falling consensus, signals modestly deteriorating near-term visibility rather than acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

ADC's growth is a spread business. The company deploys external capital into net-lease assets at cap rates around 7.1-7.2% (Q1 2026 acquisitions of 85 properties at $404M priced at ~7.1%) and funds them at a blended cost meaningfully below that. The accretion equals the spread times the volume deployed, divided across a growing share count. When rates rise, the cost of capital climbs and the spread narrows, slowing the pace of accretive acquisitions.

The estimate revision data confirms the deceleration is now flowing through to consensus. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 3.1% over 30 days and 4.8% over 90 days; next-quarter -3.6%/-5.1%; current-year $1.99 → $1.90; next-year $2.06 → $1.95. The direction is uniformly negative across every horizon. This is the opposite of the profile (estimate revisions >+30%, revenue growth >40% with expanding margins) that would warrant a cycle-adjusted, forward-looking re-rating. ADC's revenue growth is solid (~16-19%) but its per-share earnings momentum is negative, and the dilution-funded model means the relevant metric is the per-share figure, which is being trimmed.

The durability of the longer-term growth story is intact: ADC has three origination channels, ample liquidity to transact where over-levered competitors cannot, and a clean credit book to redeploy into. But the near-term growth rate is rate-gated. In the prevailing higher-for-longer environment, the realistic per-share AFFO growth profile is low-to-mid single digits, and consensus is pricing the lower end of that. A verified easing of the rate path, which would re-widen spreads and restore acquisition velocity, is the most direct upside catalyst, and it sits outside management's control.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$73.25, ADC trades at ~17x 2025 AFFO/share and offers a ~4.4% dividend yield. The headline trailing P/E of ~39.6x is not the right lens for a REIT (net income is depressed by depreciation); AFFO and FFO multiples are the operative metrics. The stock is roughly 11% below its 52-week high of $82.08 and near the midpoint of its 12-month range.

Relative to the most direct peer, Realty Income (O), ADC trades at a premium AFFO multiple and a lower yield (~4.4% vs. O's ~5.4%), justified by faster AFFO growth and lower leverage (~3.2x net-debt/EBITDA vs. O's higher level). Within its quality cohort, the multiple is defensible but not cheap; it reflects the market's recognition of the IG-tenant mix and balance-sheet strength rather than a mispricing.

Analyst price targets cluster from a low of $80 to a high of $92, mean $84.6, median $84, all above the current price, implying ~9-25% upside to the range. On an inflection stock these would be treated as floors. ADC's estimates are falling, not rising, so the guardrail applies in reverse: the targets reflect a quality-franchise premium that the market has not been willing to pay in recent months, evidenced by the stock drifting from $82 to $73 even as targets held. Treating those targets as a hard floor would overstate the asymmetry.

The valuation case is balanced. There is genuine yield support at ~4.4% with a ~73% AFFO payout, and the quality of the cash flow is high. But there is no specific catalyst for upward re-rating with estimates falling, and the multiple is not depressed enough to constitute deep value. A "cheap" headline multiple on a net-lease REIT is frequently a value trap when the rate backdrop is working against the spread model; the appropriate posture is to require a catalyst (rate relief or a clearer multiple compression) before rewarding the price. We score valuation at 5.0, fairly priced for the quality, with limited asymmetry today.

## Competitive Landscape

ADC competes against public net-lease REITs (most directly Realty Income and NNN REIT), private equity, and institutional funds, many with greater scale and lower cost of capital. Its differentiation is durable and trending favorably:

- **Credit quality.** The ~65% IG-tenant mix is materially higher than most peers and reflects a deliberate choice to prioritize tenant solvency over headline yield. With only one tenant on cash-basis accounting across the portfolio and impairments declining to $1.4M in Q1 2026 from $11.9M for full-year 2025, the credit book is exceptionally clean.
- **Sourcing breadth.** Three origination channels (acquisitions, development, DFP) give ADC more avenues than pure acquirers, supporting deployment even when one channel's spreads compress.
- **Balance-sheet positioning.** Low leverage and ample liquidity allow ADC to keep transacting in a higher-for-longer environment where over-levered private competitors are sidelined, a relative share-gain opportunity at attractive cap rates.

The principal industry headwind is sector-wide: cap rates have risen with rates, but so has cost of capital, narrowing spreads for everyone. ADC's discipline (avoiding PE-sponsored tenants, screening for fungible real estate) is a defensive moat against the credit losses that will likely surface among lower-quality net-lease portfolios if discretionary-retail tenants weaken. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving, which supports a 7.0 score, though the financials lessons caution that competitive-position signals deserve confirmation rather than standalone weight.

## Risk Assessment

**Spread compression / rate sensitivity.** The dominant risk is structural to the model: a positive cap-rate-minus-cost-of-capital spread is the engine, and a higher-for-longer path compresses it. This is the mechanism behind the falling estimates and is the single most important variable for the stock.

**External-capital dependence.** ADC must continuously issue equity and debt. A capital-markets dislocation or a credit-rating downgrade would directly impair growth and, in an extreme case, pressure the dividend. The ~$1.37B of unsettled forward equity is a known dilution event scheduled to settle between June 2026 and March 2028; per-share AFFO must keep pace as those shares land.

**Single-tenant and sector concentration.** Build-to-suit assets are tailored to one tenant; a vacancy means 100% income loss on that asset plus re-tenanting cost, and bankruptcy law limits recovery on rejected leases. Sector exposure to discretionary-adjacent retail (dollar stores, auto parts, tire/auto) carries the company's own caveat that its e-commerce/recession-resistance thesis "may prove incorrect." Tariffs are newly flagged in the Q1 2026 risk language as pressure on tenant retail.

**Capital decisions ahead.** The Series A preferred ($175M, 4.25%) becomes redeemable in September 2026, a refinancing/capital decision point.

**Governance nuances.** Founder-family control, a staggered board, the business-combination anti-takeover exemption for the Chairman, and the open-ended Richard Agree Reimbursement Agreement (whose loss-sharing amount is "to be determined by facts and circumstances") are items to weigh. None are red flags, but they reflect a founder-centric structure.

Most of these risks are well-priced for a REIT of this quality, and the recovery upside (rate relief) is symmetric. We score risk factors at 6.5: few binary surprises, a clean credit book, but a genuine and persistent rate-sensitivity overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and complacent. Near-term ATM IV is 20.8% versus 16.1% trailing realized (modestly rich but not stressed), with an essentially balanced put/call skew (0.3%), a low put/call volume ratio (0.11), and a contango term structure (20.8% near vs. 22.8% at six months) consistent with a normal, low-anxiety profile. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.6%. The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 4.94 to 0.86 over the past month, a meaningful de-risking of prior downside positioning. Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float (11,871 shares). The options market is not pricing a near-term catalyst in either direction, consistent with the income-hold thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** ADC is a quality compounder whose model accelerates when rates ease. A verified easing of the rate path re-widens acquisition spreads, restores deployment velocity, and lifts per-share AFFO growth back toward high-single digits. The IG-tenant mix and clean credit book outperform peers through any consumer weakening, and ADC's liquidity lets it take share from sidelined over-levered competitors at attractive cap rates. The insider buying cluster proves prescient. The stock re-rates toward the analyst range. Bull target ~$88-92 (consensus high), ~20-25% above current with the dividend on top.

**Bear Case.** Higher-for-longer persists, the spread stays compressed, and per-share AFFO growth stalls in the low single digits. Falling estimates continue, discretionary-retail tenant stress surfaces in the dollar-store/auto-parts exposure as tariffs bite, and forward-equity settlements dilute faster than accretion can absorb. The multiple compresses toward the lower end of the historical range. Bear target ~$64-66, roughly 10-12% below current, with the ~4.4% yield cushioning total return.

**Base Case.** ADC continues to grow per-share AFFO at low-to-mid single digits in a rate-gated environment, the dividend remains well-covered and rising, and the stock delivers a yield-plus-modest-growth total return without meaningful multiple expansion. Base target ~$78, ~6-7% above current plus the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A turn in the estimate-revision trend — current-year and next-year FFO/AFFO estimates moving from falling to flat-or-rising over two consecutive monthly reads, signaling the spread is re-widening.
- A pullback toward the low-$70s/high-$60s that pushes the AFFO multiple and yield into a clearly asymmetric entry (yield approaching ~4.8-5.0% with coverage intact).
- A verified, durable easing of the rate path that restores acquisition velocity above the recent ~$1.4B/year pace at maintained spreads.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AFFO payout ratio rising toward the mid-80s on declining per-share AFFO, threatening dividend coverage.
- A meaningful deterioration in the credit book (multiple tenants moving to cash-basis accounting, impairments re-accelerating well above the 2025 $11.9M level).
- A credit-rating downgrade or capital-markets access impairment that breaks the match-funding model.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, the September 2026 Series A preferred redemption decision, and the cadence of forward-equity settlements through 2026-2028.

## Conclusion

ADC is a well-run, conservatively levered, quality net-lease REIT with a best-in-class investment-grade tenant base, a clean credit book, and a well-covered ~4.4% monthly dividend. The score trajectory has been improving (6.3 → 6.7), driven by recognition of management alignment and competitive position; the current read holds the line rather than extending it. The reason is that the dominant variable — the cap-rate-minus-cost-of-capital spread — is working against the company in a higher-for-longer environment, and that pressure is now visible in uniformly falling consensus estimates (-4.4% current-year, -5.1% next-year over 90 days) and a recent string of small earnings misses. Growth is the most reliable dimension for sorting winners, and ADC's per-share growth momentum is currently negative, which caps the case.

The strongest offsetting signal is the spring 2026 insider buying cluster: the CEO, founder/Chairman, and two directors purchasing with their own cash at $71-75, near today's price. That is a credible vote of confidence and a meaningful floor under the downside. Combined with the conservative balance sheet and well-covered yield, it makes the bear case shallow and the income stream dependable. But at ~17x AFFO with no upward-revision catalyst and limited multiple-expansion room, the appreciation asymmetry is modest. We rate ADC a HOLD (income), appropriate for investors seeking a durable, growing dividend, with an upgrade contingent on either a turn in the estimate trend or a more attractive entry.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the AFFO payout ratio was overstated (annualized $3.144 dividend / $4.33 AFFO = ~73%, not ~71%) — this is a minor correction and does not change the well-covered conclusion, but the analyst's own "~71%" figure does not reconcile to the stated inputs.
