**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Healthpeak is an S&P 500 healthcare REIT operating across three segments: outpatient medical (~52% of segment NOI), lab/life science (~37%), and senior housing (~11%, now largely externalized). The investment debate reduces to one variable: the timing and trajectory of a lab-segment recovery against a backdrop of NIH funding cuts, life-science oversupply, and a 26.4% SF Peninsula vacancy rate. The outpatient-medical core is durable and growing (Q1 2026 same-store NOI +2.4%, +5.4% cash releasing spreads), senior housing carries double-digit same-store growth (+13.8% in Q1 2026), but lab same-store NOI fell 7.2% in Q1 with total lab occupancy collapsing from 97.5% to 88.2% over 2025. Management's 2026 total same-store NOI guidance of −1.0% to +1.0% is the honest tell: strength in two segments is being fully neutralized by lab drag, and 2026 earnings growth is coming from capital allocation rather than organic operations.

The most material recent development is the March 2026 Janus Living IPO, which monetized DOC's demographically favored senior-housing portfolio at a strong, upsized valuation (~$880M net proceeds) while DOC retained 81.6% ownership, management fees, and consolidation. This de-risks the senior-housing externalization thesis and provides a low-cost growth vehicle (Janus holds $949M cash, zero debt, ~$400M of acquisitions under contract). Combined with the Blackstone outpatient-medical JV (6.1% cap rate, $508/psf), private-market transactions are validating DOC's asset values above where the public equity trades.

We rate DOC a HOLD with an income orientation. At ~11x forward FFO (FY2026 guidance $1.71–$1.75) and a ~6.4% dividend yield covered by AFFO, the stock offers meaningful downside protection and trades below implied private-market NAV. However, consensus FFO/EPS estimate revisions are broadly negative (next-year EPS down 32.5% over 90 days), the dominant near-term catalyst (lab recovery) is cyclical and not in management's control, and the stock has already rallied sharply from its April lows following the Q1 print. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, appropriate for a yield-seeking holder rather than a fresh capital deployment.

## Company Overview

Healthpeak (founded 1985, headquartered in Denver, organized as an UPREIT through Healthpeak OP) owns and operates ~703 healthcare properties across three segments. The current company structure was defined by the March 1, 2024 merger with Physicians Realty Trust, which added 299 outpatient medical buildings and the DOC ticker, and which still dominates the year-over-year financial comparisons through "Merger-Combined Same-Store" reporting.

- **Outpatient Medical — $796M FY2025 segment NOI:** 507 properties, ~79% on or adjacent to hospital campuses, 96% affiliated with hospital systems, ~72% triple-net leased. Includes 9 triple-net hospitals. The stable growth engine, supported by aging demographics and supply constraint.
- **Lab (Life Science) — $567M:** 145 properties concentrated in South San Francisco (59%), Boston (22%), and San Diego (17%); 89% triple-net; ~69% of GAV in California. The segment under pressure.
- **Senior Housing — $177M:** Operated through RIDEA structures, now largely externalized via Janus Living, in which DOC retains 81.6% economics plus external management fees.

Competitive advantages include scale and institutional relationships in a fragmented sector, concentration in supply-constrained hospital-adjacent outpatient assets, internalized property management as a margin lever, and an investment-grade balance sheet (Baa1/BBB+).

## Financial Analysis

A data caveat is essential. The standardized revenue field reads $604M for FY2025, which captures only senior-housing resident fees. Total FY2025 revenue is approximately $2.7B+ (outpatient medical rental ~$1.27B + lab ~$860M + senior housing $604M + interest income). Any screen using the understated figure, including the Altman Z-Score of 0.92 that flags "distress," produces a false signal. DOC is investment-grade with Net Debt/EBITDAre of 5.4x as of Q1 2026, ~$1.17B cash post-quarter, and manageable near-term maturities ($650M notes + $345M mortgage within 12 months). For a REIT, FFO and AFFO are the relevant metrics, not GAAP net income.

**FFO trajectory:**

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nareit FFO/common | $985M | $1,093M | $1,269M |
| FFO as Adjusted | $978M | $1,232M | $1,292M (+4.9%) |
| AFFO | $884M | $1,141M | $1,184M (+3.8%) |

FFO grew steadily as the Physicians merger synergies ramped. The earnings quality picture is mixed. GAAP net income fell to $71M in 2025 from $243M, driven by $176M of non-cash other-than-temporary impairments on lab unconsolidated JVs (equity loss from unconsolidated JVs swung to −$174M from −$1.5M), lower disposition gains, and higher D&A and interest. The impairments do not hit FFO, but they are a real signal of lab JV value erosion.

**Segment same-store trends:**
- Outpatient Medical: durable. FY2025 Merger-Combined SS Adjusted NOI +3.8%; occupancy ~92%.
- Lab: the soft spot. FY2025 SS NOI +1.5%; renewal spreads of only +3.5%.
- Senior Housing: strong. FY2025 SS NOI +12.6%, on rising occupancy and rate growth.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Total debt ~$9.8B, ~88% fixed-rate at a weighted-average 4.20%. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.25B (+$181M YoY). A new $400M unsecured delayed-draw term loan (March 2026, SOFR+80bps, matures 2031) sits undrawn for liquidity. The ~88% fixed-rate debt mix insulates the company from the elevated long-end yields stressing other rate-sensitive REITs, and the 4.20% average rate means refinancing pressure is modest relative to current market rates.

**Earnings surprise record:** Three beats and one miss over the past four quarters, including a Q1 2026 FFO as Adjusted of $0.45 (beat by ~4.7%). The reported GAAP EPS surprises (515% in Q1 2026) are distorted by one-time gains and are not meaningful for a REIT; the FFO beats are the relevant signal and they have been consistent.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is flat in 2026. Management reaffirmed total same-store NOI guidance of −1.0% to +1.0%, which captures the standoff between outpatient medical (+2.4%) and senior housing (+13.8%) on one side and lab (−7.2%) on the other. The growth that is occurring in FFO comes from capital allocation: Janus Living accretion, the Blackstone OM JV fees, opportunistic acquisitions, and buybacks. FY2026 FFO as Adjusted guidance was raised modestly to $1.71–$1.75 from $1.70–$1.74.

The estimate revision picture is a headwind that deserves weight. Consensus is broadly falling: current-quarter EPS revised −5.6% over 30 days and −8.6% over 90 days; next-quarter −15.4%/−23.6%; current-year −21.1%; next-year −32.5%. While GAAP EPS estimates for a REIT are noisy and partly reflect the absence of repeatable one-time gains, the direction is unambiguously negative, and it co-occurs with flat organic guidance. This is negative earnings momentum, not the rising-estimate inflection that would justify a more constructive growth view.

The swing factor is lab. Management states lab total occupancy increased sequentially in Q1 2026 and is expected to rise through year-end 2026, and third-party data is consistent with a 2026 bottom as construction halts and biotech funding/stock prices recover. The weakness is sector-wide rather than company-specific: Alexandria (the largest lab REIT) suffered a worse Q1 leasing slowdown and 2026 downgrades. DOC's premier cluster locations (South San Francisco, Boston, San Diego) should recover faster than secondary markets. But the recovery is cyclical, dependent on NIH funding policy that remains in flux, and outside management's control. We treat the lab bottom as plausible but unconfirmed; the burden of proof rests on subsequent quarters of stabilizing occupancy.

Senior housing remains the brightest organic spot, with aging demographics and halted new construction supporting double-digit same-store growth. The Janus vehicle, with substantial dry powder, is positioned to compound that tailwind, and DOC captures 81.6% of the upside plus fees.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.07 (intraday $19.15), DOC's market cap is ~$13.1B. On FY2026 FFO guidance, the stock trades at ~11.0x forward FFO. The headline trailing P/E of 60x is meaningless for a REIT carrying heavy non-cash D&A and impairments; FFO/AFFO multiples are the correct lens.

The ~6.4% dividend yield ($1.22/share annualized) is covered by AFFO against the dividend obligation, and management raised the dividend modestly in early 2025. The yield sits above the peer median of 4.8% and provides absolute return support and downside protection.

Peer context: median Health Care REIT P/E is 31.6x and yield 4.8%. DOC's GAAP P/E is not comparable to peers given the impairment distortion, but on yield it trades at a premium (higher yield = cheaper). More telling are private-market validations. The Blackstone OM JV closed at a 6.1% cap rate / $508 psf for a 100%-occupied 6-property portfolio, and the Janus IPO priced at the high end of range and upsized, both implying underlying asset values above the public equity. Management reinforced this view by repurchasing $100M of stock at $16.81 in April 2026, below FY2025 average and below implied NAV, with $306M of authorization remaining.

The case for the stock being cheap is the gap between public valuation and private-market asset marks. The case against a higher valuation score is the negative estimate-revision trend, flat organic growth, and the absence of a near-term catalyst that closes the public-private gap on a defined timeline. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $17, mean $20.83, median $20.50, high $29), with Baird raising to $21 (Outperform) in May 2026. We assign a 6.0: modestly cheap on assets and yield, but not the asymmetric setup that earns a high score, because the discount could persist until lab stabilizes.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Outpatient medical:** Favorable and improving. Hospital-affiliated, supply-constrained, demographically supported, with private capital paying ~6% cap rates. DOC's scale and 96% hospital-system affiliation are durable moats.
- **Lab/life science:** Deteriorating cyclically, but the weakness is industry-wide, not a share-loss problem. NIH grant cancellations triggered a demand collapse and oversupply; SF Peninsula vacancy hit 26.4%; Alexandria's results were worse. DOC's premier clusters position it to recover faster than secondary markets, though re-leasing lab space is expensive and slow given tenant-specific improvements.
- **Senior housing:** Strong demand/supply fundamentals (aging demographics plus halted construction). The Janus vehicle gives DOC a dedicated, well-capitalized acquirer to consolidate a fragmented market.

Net, the competitive trajectory is mixed: improving in OM, structurally favorable in senior housing, cyclically impaired but not share-losing in lab. We score competitive position at 6.0 to reflect the balance.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Lab/life-science downturn (most acute).** The 10-K added extensive new language on NIH grant cuts, indirect-cost reimbursement caps, scientific-workforce visa policy, drug pricing, and China biotech competition. This is a newly elevated risk that is actively materializing (occupancy −9 points, Q1 SS NOI −7.2%). The recovery timing is uncertain and policy-dependent.

2. **Tenant concentration.** HCA Healthcare is 15% of outpatient-medical segment revenue / 7% of total; CommonSpirit Health is 6% / 3%. Single-tenant dependency on HCA's financial health is a meaningful exposure.

3. **Healthcare policy/reimbursement.** New disclosures flag the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," Medicaid provider-rate cuts, ACA subsidy expiration, and site-neutral payment reform as threats to outpatient/hospital tenants, particularly in non-expansion states.

4. **RIDEA/Janus operational risk.** Through TRS entities, DOC bears operational and liability risk and depends on third-party operators. The Janus structure adds conflict-of-interest risk (DOC's subsidiary externally manages Janus, with overlapping officers/directors) and economic exposure to JAN's stock price.

5. **Geographic concentration/catastrophe.** ~69% of lab GAV in earthquake/wildfire-prone California; ~69% of life-plan-community GAV in hurricane-prone Florida (Hurricane Milton drove 2024 casualty losses).

These risks are largely known and partly priced, but the lab and policy risks are genuinely elevated versus prior filings and not fully resolved. We score risk factors at 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 27.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 28.2%: fairly priced, with IV in the 19th percentile of the current chain, suggesting no acute event premium. ATM IV collapsed from 46.9% to 27.3% between early May and June 1 as the Q1 earnings catalyst resolved. Put/call skew is elevated at 8.4% (put demand) on the near tenor, and the put/call volume ratio is 0.71, a modestly cautious posture, but the P/C open-interest ratio of 0.20 is call-heavy, indicating no systematic bearish positioning. The term structure is flat (near 27.3% ≈ far 28.3%), with uncertainty evenly distributed and an implied move of ±6.3% into the June expiry. The signal is consistent with a fundamentally balanced situation: some near-term hedging demand, no informed directional bet either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Lab occupancy bottoms in 2026 as management indicates and third-party data corroborates, removing the drag that is suppressing organic growth. Outpatient medical continues +2.4–3.8% same-store growth, senior housing compounds at double digits through the well-capitalized Janus vehicle, and capital allocation (Janus accretion, Blackstone fees, accretive acquisitions, buybacks below NAV) drives FFO above the $1.75 high end of guidance. The public-private valuation gap closes as the market re-rates toward private-market cap rates. In this scenario, the stock approaches the high analyst target of $29, with the ~6.4% yield paid throughout.

### Bear Case
NIH funding cuts and life-science oversupply persist longer than expected, lab occupancy stalls or declines further, and additional non-cash JV impairments erode reported book value and confidence. Estimate revisions continue falling, site-neutral payment reform pressures outpatient tenants, and HCA concentration becomes a liability. The dividend remains covered but growth stalls, and the stock de-rates toward the low analyst target of $17 (−11% from current). A broader recession or rate spike would compress REIT multiples further.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income-oriented).** The thesis rests on a covered ~6.4% dividend, downside protection from private-market asset values, and a plausible-but-unconfirmed lab bottom, not on a defined near-term appreciation catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of sequential lab occupancy gains confirming the bottom, with lab same-store NOI inflecting toward flat or positive.
- A turn in consensus FFO/EPS estimate revisions from negative to positive over a 90-day window.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Lab occupancy declining below ~86% with further JV impairments, signaling a deeper-than-cyclical impairment.
- A dividend coverage breach (AFFO payout exceeding ~100%) or a dividend cut.
- Material adverse outcome on site-neutral payment reform or a deterioration in HCA's credit profile.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), which will provide the first post-Q1 read on whether lab occupancy is in fact rising as management projects.

## Conclusion

Healthpeak is a de-risked, investment-grade, diversified healthcare REIT with a durable outpatient-medical core, a successfully monetized senior-housing growth vehicle, and a cyclically depressed lab segment that appears to be bottoming. The score has improved over the past several reports (5.4 → 6.1), with management quality re-rated up materially on the Janus IPO execution and the disciplined sub-NAV buybacks, and financial health and valuation each improving by a point. Current data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it: the Q1 beat, raised guidance, and private-market validations are genuine positives.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because the organic growth engine is flat, consensus estimate revisions are broadly negative, and the dominant catalyst (lab recovery) is cyclical, policy-dependent, and outside management's control. The stock has already rallied sharply off its April lows following the Q1 print, compressing the asymmetry that existed at $16.81 when management itself was buying. The ~6.4% covered yield and below-NAV valuation justify holding for income and downside protection, but the evidence does not yet support fresh capital deployment ahead of confirmation that lab has bottomed. We classify this as an income hold and will revisit on the Q2 occupancy data.
