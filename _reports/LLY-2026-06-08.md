**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is the dominant player in the fastest-growing therapeutic category in pharmaceuticals. The tirzepatide franchise (Mounjaro for type-2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity) drove revenue up 45% in 2025 to $65.2B and a further 56% YoY in Q1 2026 to $19.8B. Net income grew 168% in the quarter to $7.4B. Management raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $82–85B, implying continued growth of roughly 25–30%. The company has now beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters, most recently by 27%, and consensus estimates for the current and next fiscal years have risen 4.5% and 6.0% over the past 90 days. This is the cleanest combination of revenue acceleration, margin quality (83% gross margin, ~32% net margin, 78% ROE), and forward estimate momentum in large-cap pharma.

The recommendation is BUY. At ~$1,148, LLY trades at roughly 32x the current-year consensus EPS of $36.21 and ~26x the next-year estimate of $44.49. Against an EPS growth rate of 23%+ from current year to next, the PEG is approximately 1.4, below the level at which a premium-quality grower becomes overpriced. The valuation score is moderate rather than high because the multiple does require continued execution; it is not a deep-value setup. The validation framework asks what specific risk or missing catalyst prevents a BUY, and there is no satisfying answer here. The catalysts are present and dated (retatrutide filing, the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program launch July 1, ongoing oral GLP-1 share data), estimate revisions are positive, and the bear-case downside is contained within the analyst target range.

What keeps the overall score at 7.1 rather than higher is the risk dimension. Incretin products are 65% of revenue and rising; the company has made a deliberate volume-for-price trade with the U.S. government whose payoff is uncertain by management's own admission; and Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy has out-ramped Lilly's newly approved Foundayo in the early oral GLP-1 race. These are risks to the rate of growth rather than to the existence of growth, and they are partly priced into a multiple that has compressed even as earnings have compounded.

## Company Overview

Lilly operates as a single reportable segment covering discovery, development, manufacturing, and commercialization of medicines globally. The revenue base has been transformed by the incretin franchise. As of Q1 2026 the therapeutic mix is approximately:

- **Cardiometabolic health (~80% of revenue):** Mounjaro and Zepbound (both tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist) plus Jardiance and a declining Trulicity. Mounjaro alone reached $8.7B in Q1 2026, up 125% YoY.
- **Oncology:** Verzenio (>$5.7B in 2025), Jaypirca, Inluriyo.
- **Immunology:** Taltz, Omvoh, Ebglyss.
- **Neuroscience:** Kisunla (donanemab) for Alzheimer's.

The competitive structure of the obesity/GLP-1 market is effectively a Lilly–Novo Nordisk duopoly, with the category expected to grow toward roughly $100B. Lilly holds about 60% of the GLP-1 market on the strength of tirzepatide's best-in-class weight-loss efficacy. The differentiating asset is the next-generation pipeline: orforglipron (oral GLP-1, branded Foundayo, FDA-approved April 2026), retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3 success May 2026), and eloralintide. Lilly is positioned to defend its franchise through its own innovation cycle rather than ceding share at patent expiry.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is unambiguous:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 34.1 | 45.0 | 65.2 (+45%) | 19.8 (+56% YoY) |
| Net income ($B) | 5.2 | 10.6 | 20.6 (+95%) | 7.4 (+168% YoY) |
| Diluted EPS | — | 11.71 | 22.95 | 8.26 |
| Gross margin | — | 81.3% | 83.0% | 81.9% |
| R&D ($B) | 9.3 | 11.0 | 13.3 | 3.5 |

The defining dynamic is volume swamping deliberate price erosion. 2025 revenue grew 45% on +50% volume and –6% price; Q1 2026 grew 56% on +65% volume and –13% price. The thesis hinges on demand continuing to overwhelm the price concessions Lilly is making across the cash-pay channel (LillyDirect), the China NRDL listing, and the U.S. government pricing agreements. So far the volume response has been more than sufficient.

Margin quality is high. True gross margin is ~82–83% and net margin ~31.7%; ROE is 77.8% and ROA 18.4%. A reported operating margin near zero and an Altman Z-Score of 1.25 are model artifacts, not signs of distress. The Z-Score is depressed by thin book equity (a deliberate result of buybacks and $43B+ of debt funding the capacity buildout) and by IPR&D-charge classification noise. With $16.8B in operating cash flow, $10B+ in undrawn credit, and a $1T market cap, this is the opposite of credit distress; the Piotroski F-Score of 4/5 is the more relevant read. The genuine point embedded in the leverage is that Lilly is funding expansion with debt while returning cash simultaneously, which is sustainable only as long as incretin growth holds.

Capital allocation: capex ramped to $7.8B in 2025 from $5.1B in 2024, with management guiding to "meaningfully higher" spend to build manufacturing capacity. FCF was $9.0B in 2025, modest relative to net income because of this investment cycle. The dividend was $6.00/share in 2025, raised to an indicated $6.92 for 2026 (a ~0.6% yield, immaterial to the thesis). Buybacks were $4.1B in 2025 and $2.3B in Q1 2026, with $8.6B remaining on a $15B authorization. Up to ~$12B of pending 2026 M&A (including Ventyx at $1.1B and earlier Verve at $549M) reflects continued pipeline reinvestment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, the existing injectable franchise continues to compound: Mounjaro +125% YoY in Q1 2026, with even India growing 10% against a cheaper generic per Morgan Stanley, a durability signal. Second, the FY2026 guidance raise implies the feared deceleration is not materializing on the feared timeline. Third, the pipeline extends the runway: Foundayo (oral) is approved, retatrutide's Phase 3 success (May 21, 2026) moves a triple agonist toward filing, and both could expand the total addressable market while keeping incremental revenue inside Lilly.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats of 14.5%, 22.0%, 11.9%, and 27.0%. Consensus EPS revisions are broadly positive: current quarter +8.2% over 90 days, current year +4.5%, next year +6.0%. The 90-day revision velocity is positive but below the +30% magnitude that would mark a trough-earnings inflection; this is a high-quality compounder, not a cyclical recovery. EPS is forecast to grow from $36.21 (CY) to $44.49 (NY), a ~23% rate, with consensus modeling continued mid-20s revenue growth.

The yellow flag inside the growth story is the oral GLP-1 race. Reuters reported Foundayo at ~7,335 prescriptions in its fourth U.S. week ("modest"), while Novo's oral Wegovy topped 3 million prescriptions in five months. Novo holds an early lead in the oral segment despite Lilly's overall dominance. The offsetting structural point is that orforglipron is a small molecule, not a peptide, and far more scalable to manufacture, which is a long-term advantage if Lilly can convert capacity into share. This is the single most important near-term metric to watch.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer medians sit far lower (P/E 19.9 across JNJ, MRK, PFE, BMY, ZTS), but the peer set is not comparable: JNJ at 27x and MRK at 33.8x have low-single-digit to mid-single-digit growth, while LLY is compounding earnings at 23%+ with the most durable demand driver in the sector. The relevant metric is the growth-adjusted multiple. A PEG of ~1.4 is below the 1.5 threshold that would signal overvaluation for a premium grower, and well below where comparable hyper-growth franchises have traded.

The valuation score is 6.5 rather than higher for a specific reason: at 32x forward, the multiple already embeds continued execution, so beats are largely expected and the asymmetry is less favorable than it would be at a cheaper entry. This is not a deep-value setup where the market is broadly wrong, but it is also not stretched; the stock has de-rated even as earnings have compounded. LLY traded near $1,182 at its 52-week high and as low as $623.78 (August 2025), and the current price sits below the highs reached in late 2025/early 2026 despite materially higher earnings and a guidance raise since. On unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, the lower-relative-to-peak multiple is an improvement in risk/reward, not a deterioration.

Analyst targets bracket the current price asymmetrically to the upside: mean $1,215, median $1,251, high $1,500 (Barclays at $1,400), low $850. With the stock trading below mean and median and estimates rising, consensus targets are likely to drift higher as analysts catch up to the guidance raise.

## Competitive Landscape

Lilly is the leader in the GLP-1 duopoly with ~60% share, anchored by tirzepatide's efficacy advantage in injectables. The competitive position is strong but the trajectory has flattened from "improving" to "stable-to-mildly-pressured," which is why the score sits at 7.5 rather than higher (and down from 9.0 earlier in the year). The pressure points:

- **Oral segment:** Novo's oral Wegovy is out-ramping Foundayo early. Lilly leads injectables decisively but is the challenger, not the incumbent, in the contested oral category.
- **China:** The 10-K explicitly calls out intensifying competition from Chinese research capabilities and biosimilars.
- **PBM access:** CVS Caremark stopped covering Zepbound as a preferred obesity drug on some plans in July 2025, demonstrating concrete formulary risk even for a flagship product.

The moat-widening factor that supports the score is the self-disruption strategy. By launching orforglipron and advancing retatrutide before tirzepatide faces a patent cliff, Lilly is positioned to keep franchise revenue internal through the next innovation cycle, a posture few competitors can match.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score of 5.5 reflects real, partly-unpriced idiosyncratic risks:

1. **Incretin concentration.** Mounjaro + Zepbound were 56% of 2025 revenue and 65% in Q1 2026; six products account for 82% of revenue. Any safety signal, demand air-pocket, manufacturing failure, or competitive shock to tirzepatide would be financially material and could trigger a sharp decline.

2. **Government pricing trade (newest, largest swing factor).** Lilly finalized voluntary U.S. government agreements in Q1 2026: lower Medicaid and certain prices, "more balanced pricing across developed nations," and a Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program offering discounted obesity drugs from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027. Management is candid that revenues from expanded access may be "insufficient to offset pricing concessions." This is a volume-for-price bet with an unknown payoff.

3. **IRA price-setting.** Jardiance is subject to Medicare-set prices in 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio were selected in January 2026 for 2028 pricing. Management expects other significant products to be selected in future years, eventually reaching the incretin franchise as the statutory clocks run.

4. **Litigation accreting.** Incretin product-liability MDLs (GI and NAION claims) are early-stage but material given self-insurance and 65% revenue concentration. The April 2026 Emgality/Teva Federal Circuit reversal already produced a Q1 charge.

5. **China supply chain and tariffs.** Pharma's current tariff exemption "may be terminated"; Lilly depends on China-based chemical-synthesis suppliers, with BIOSECURE Act exposure.

6. **Capacity bet.** Up to ~$10B in take-or-pay supply commitments amid a multi-year buildout; overestimating demand strands capital, underestimating caps revenue.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty but not bearish positioning. Near-term ATM IV of 42.7% is rich versus 38.2% historical vol (a 4.5pp premium), at the 73rd IV percentile, with the term structure in backwardation (42.7% near-term vs 38.0% long-term), consistent with event-hedging into known catalysts rather than a directional bearish bet. The put/call volume ratio of 0.32 and OI ratio of 0.49 are call-tilted. The most striking flow is heavy short-dated upside call buying: the $1,300 June-12 call traded 6,654 contracts against 261 open interest (25.5x), and the $1,200 June-12 call traded 3,790 against 1,252 OI. Short interest is negligible at 0.9% of float (2.4 days to cover). The options signal confirms the bullish fundamental view, though the rich near-term IV means the market is pricing meaningful event risk into the dated catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,400–1,500):** Demand continues to swamp price erosion, FY2026 lands at the top of the range, retatrutide files and reads out cleanly, Foundayo's scalable small-molecule profile converts manufacturing capacity into oral share gains, and the Medicare Bridge Program drives volume that more than offsets pricing concessions. At ~32x the next-year $44.49 EPS, that supports ~$1,425, with the Barclays $1,400 and Street high of $1,500 as the upper bound.

**Bear Case (~$850):** Novo's oral lead widens, the government pricing trade proves dilutive to margins, an incretin safety signal or demand air-pocket emerges, and growth decelerates faster than the guidance implies. A de-rating to ~23–24x a haircut next-year EPS lands near the analyst low of $850, roughly 26% below current. The August 2025 low of $623.78 demonstrates the stock's capacity for sharp drawdowns on negative surprise.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,330.** Approximately 30x the next-year consensus EPS of $44.49 = $1,335, rounded to $1,330, a multiple modestly below the current forward multiple, reflecting that some deceleration is appropriate to assume, applied to the higher forward earnings base. This represents ~16% upside from $1,148.

**Bull case target: $1,425** (~24% upside): 32x next-year EPS on top-of-guidance execution.

**Bear case target: $850** (~26% downside): analyst low, ~23x a reduced next-year estimate.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the Q2/Q3 2026 earnings cadence, the July 1 Medicare Bridge Program launch, retatrutide filing progress, and monthly oral GLP-1 prescription data.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $82B, or any quarter where volume growth fails to offset price erosion (i.e., total incretin revenue declines sequentially).
2. A material tirzepatide safety signal or FDA action.
3. Foundayo prescription trends remaining flat or declining over two consecutive months while Novo's oral Wegovy continues to gain, signaling a structural loss in the oral segment rather than a slow start.

## Conclusion

LLY presents the strongest fundamental profile in large-cap pharma, with a pipeline that lets the company disrupt its own franchise rather than defend it at a patent cliff. The valuation at ~32x forward / PEG ~1.4 is reasonable for the growth and quality, not stretched, and the stock trades below mean and median analyst targets that are likely to rise.

The reason this is a 7.1 rather than an 8 is the risk dimension: incretin concentration, the uncertain government pricing trade, and Novo's early oral lead. None of these is a missing catalyst or a valuation disqualifier; they are risks to the pace of growth that are partly already in a compressed multiple. With positive estimate momentum, dated catalysts, contained bear-case downside relative to upside, and confirming (call-tilted) options positioning, the case for a BUY is concrete. The recommendation is **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $1,330.
