## Executive Summary

Donnelley Financial Solutions is a regulatory-compliance software and services company that has spent five years converting a print-and-filing-agent franchise into a software business, and it is now roughly halfway there: software solutions reached 47.9% of trailing-twelve-month revenue ($372.7M of $777.5M, +9.4% year over year) while print and distribution fell to 13.9% ($107.9M, -7.8%). Revenue turned positive after three consecutive years of decline — LTM revenue of $777.5M versus $755.0M, +3.0%, with Q2 2026 marking the third straight quarter of consolidated growth. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 29.1% to 31.9% on that LTM basis. The balance sheet is unambiguously sound for a $1.2B company: net leverage 0.7x, LTM operating income to interest expense of 12.0x, and $227.4M of net available liquidity.

The market values this at 5.4x LTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, 1.73x EV/revenue, and 9.8x the $4.82 current-year consensus — a roughly 35% discount to the 15.2x median of its Financial Exchanges & Data peer set. That gap is not a mystery, and the discount is not obviously wrong. DFIN's demand curve is written by SEC disclosure rulemaking, and the current policy direction is contractionary. Two proposals surfaced in 2026: permitting semi-annual rather than quarterly reporting, and defaulting shareholder reports to electronic delivery, which management has tied to a print impact around 2028. Neither proposal's stage, scope, or adoption probability could be verified. What the market is discounting is the terminal earnings base, not current earnings.

Two things changed since our last review at roughly $39 in June. Constructively, Q2 delivered a fourth consecutive adjusted beat ($1.76 versus $1.67 consensus), revenue beat by $2.8M, and full-year consensus EPS rose 2.7% over 90 days with next-year up 6.6%. Against that, the Q3 2026 guide places the entire adjusted EBITDA margin band of 26-28% at or below the 28.2% achieved in Q3 2025 — the first guided year-over-year EBITDA decline in several quarters — and the stock has risen 21% while estimates rose 3-7%. The margin of safety that justified interest at $39 has largely been consumed. We stay at HOLD with a base case of $57 (+20%), a bull case of $75 (+58%) and a bear case of $35 (-26%).

## Company Overview

Spun out of R.R. Donnelley in October 2016, DFIN sells compliance and regulatory software and services across two end markets (Capital Markets, Investment Companies) and two delivery models (Software Solutions, Compliance & Communications Management), producing four reported segments. The flagship products are ActiveDisclosure (SaaS SEC/financial reporting), Venue (virtual data room for M&A diligence), Arc Suite (fund regulatory reporting and communications) and eBrevia (contract analytics), sitting alongside a large EDGAR filing-agent and transactional-document franchise and a residual print operation.

Q2 2026 segment detail:

| Segment | Net Sales | Y/Y | Adj. EBITDA margin | Prior year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Markets – Software Solutions | $65.7M | +11.2% | 36.1% | 37.9% |
| Capital Markets – Compliance & Comms | $95.9M | +2.6% | 41.9% | 39.4% |
| Investment Companies – Software Solutions | $33.7M | +1.8% | 43.3% | 42.9% |
| Investment Companies – Compliance & Comms | $28.9M | -10.8% | 41.2% | 38.9% |
| **Consolidated** | **$224.2M** | **+2.8%** | **36.7%** | **35.0%** |

Seasonality is pronounced and matters for any quarterly extrapolation: Q4 2025 revenue was $172.5M at a 26.6% adjusted EBITDA margin against Q2 2026's $224.2M at 36.7%, because investment-company annual and semi-annual report production concentrates in the first half.

The competitive advantage is best described as timing-critical embeddedness. A company mid-IPO or a fund inside its annual-report window cannot switch filing agents without risking a statutory deadline. That produces high renewal rates and real pricing power on compliance work. It also explains why SEC rulemaking is the dominant variable: the moat surrounds a filing calendar, and the calendar is what is being deregulated.

## Financial Analysis

**The reported GAAP figures understate the business, and standardized screens misread it.** FY2025 showed operating income of $141.1M (18.4% margin) but pre-tax income of only $43.1M and diluted EPS of $1.15, which is why the trailing P/E prints at 35.8x. The gap reconciles cleanly: $141.1M operating income, less $12.9M net interest, less an $82.8M non-cash pension plan settlement charge recognized entirely in Q3 2025, less $2.3M of investment and other loss, equals $43.1M pre-tax. Cash pension and OPEB contributions were $0.8M in H1 2026. The charge represents recognition of previously accumulated other-comprehensive-loss amounts on plan settlement, with no cash cost.

That single item distorts every trailing profitability and distress metric. An automated Altman Z-Score of 2.19 (grey zone) was built on an EBIT input of roughly $2.3M — apparently the "investment and other loss" line rather than the $141.1M operating income — and substituted book for market equity. Rebuilt on the June 30, 2026 balance sheet with LTM operating income of $147.0M and market equity of $1,166M against $446.9M of total liabilities, Z is approximately 4.2. We would not lean on that either: the Altman model was calibrated on manufacturers, and DFIN carries negative tangible equity of roughly -$18.1M (equity $387.4M less goodwill $405.5M). The load-bearing solvency evidence is direct — company-disclosed net leverage of 0.7x, LTM operating income to interest of 12.0x ($147.0M / $12.3M), $202.1M of undrawn revolver plus $25.3M of cash, and management's statement that the full revolver could be drawn without covenant breach.

**Margin expansion is real but roughly 30-40% of the headline is a growing add-back.** Adjusted EBITDA went from $219.4M (29.1% margin) to $248.2M (31.9%) on an LTM basis, up 13.1% on 3.0% revenue growth. DFIN's adjusted EBITDA definition excludes share-based compensation, and SBC is compounding at 28% LTM ($33.6M versus $26.2M) against 3% revenue growth. Deducting it: LTM margin expansion narrows from ~280bp to 201bp (27.60% versus 25.59%), and Q2 expansion from 170bp to 101bp. The underlying cost-structure improvement of roughly 200bp is genuine and consistent with mix shift plus what management calls permanent cost-structure changes; the reported figure overstates it, and the overstatement widens if SBC keeps compounding. Part of the SBC step-up may be non-recurring (a new Chief Revenue Officer, two board additions, retention grants after a 26% stock decline in 2025); two more quarters would settle that. Reported gross margin also improved on a like-for-like basis — Q2 GAAP 59.6% versus 57.0%, non-GAAP ex-D&A 66.0% versus 63.7%.

**Cash generation is strong but H1 was flattered by a timing item.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $69.1M versus $30.7M, with capex of $23.9M versus $30.0M, produced free cash flow of $45.2M versus $0.7M. Roughly $24.8M of the swing sits in the "accrued liabilities and other" line, which the 10-Q attributes to lower employee incentive-compensation payments reflecting 2025 results. If 2026 results are strong, a larger accrual pays out in early 2027 and part reverses. Derived LTM FCF of $152.3M is a 13.1% yield on market cap; normalizing for the incentive-comp timing gives roughly $127M, or 10.9% on market cap and 9.4% on EV. Cash taxes of $16.7M in H1 ran below the $27.6M book provision, though the effective rate rose to 30.1% in Q2 from 25.9%.

**One item deserves scrutiny.** Capex fell 20% in H1, and net capitalized software assets declined from $92.9M at year-end to $86.4M at June 30 — the two Software Solutions segments spent $19.0M of capex against $25.0M of segment D&A. For a company whose multiple depends on software durability, a shrinking capitalized-software balance alongside falling development spend is worth watching. The more likely explanation is a harvest phase after completed platform builds, and ActiveDisclosure growing ~29% is not the revenue signature of a starved product. A shift in the proportion of development cost capitalized rather than expensed would produce the identical pattern. We could not resolve this from the disclosures.

**Capital structure is a levered share-shrink.** Shares outstanding fell from 28.7M at year-end 2024 to 24.7M at June 30, 2026 — 13.9% in eighteen months — with diluted weighted-average shares down 10.3% year over year in Q2. Treasury additions of roughly $262M over that window exceeded free cash flow of roughly $153M by $109M, reconciling to a $79.3M increase in total debt (to $204.0M) and a $32.0M cash drawdown. Q2 repurchases were 763,451 shares at an average $45.48, with $125.4M remaining on a $150M authorization. There is no dividend. At a ~$150M annual pace against a $1.17B market cap, buybacks alone retire ~12% of shares per year, which can deliver low-double-digit EPS growth on flat EBITDA — but that pace exceeds normalized FCF of ~$127M, so the debt-funded increment is finite.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue inflection is real and now three quarters deep, but it is narrow. Q2 total revenue rose $6.1M; software solutions rose $7.2M, of which the 10-Q attributes roughly $6.4M to ActiveDisclosure alone (~+29% per the release). One product therefore accounts for more than 100% of consolidated revenue growth. Management described Venue as delivering only modest growth, IC–Software Solutions grew 1.8%, IC–CCM fell 10.8%, and tech-enabled services were down 0.9% in H1.

The Investment Companies stall has a plausible benign explanation — the SEC's Tailored Shareholder Report requirements, which the company had discussed as a demand driver, have annualized, removing a regulatory tailwind and leaving the segment exposed to secular print decline. Competitive share loss to Broadridge or Toppan Merrill would produce the same revenue pattern, and the disclosures do not distinguish them.

Estimate revisions are two-sided in an informative way. Current-quarter consensus fell 6.2% over 30 days to $0.86 and next-quarter fell 2.6% to $0.74, consistent with the Q3 guide. But current-year consensus rose 1.2% over 30 days and 2.7% over 90 days to $4.82, and next-year rose 3.7% and 6.6% to $5.46. Analysts are trimming H2 while raising the full-year and out-year — the arithmetic of a company that beats, guides conservatively, and retires shares. Next-year consensus implies 13.3% EPS growth, most of which is share count rather than EBITDA.

Note that this does not meet the threshold for a high-conviction inflection signal. Revisions of +2.7% and +6.6% are ordinary, not the +30% velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection, and revenue growth of 3% is nowhere near an acceleration regime. This is a modest operating improvement amplified by financial engineering.

**Our own full-year sensitivity, not company guidance** (DFIN guides one quarter forward only): H1 actual $429.7M plus the Q3 midpoint of $180M plus a Q4 assumption of ~$175M (versus $172.5M last year) implies roughly $785M FY2026 revenue (+2.3%) and adjusted EBITDA near $247M — essentially flat with LTM. Flat EBITDA plus a 10-12% share shrink is the base-case earnings engine.

The Q3 guide is the material negative datapoint. Revenue of $175-185M against $175.3M last year, at a 26-28% adjusted EBITDA margin against 28.2%, implies $45.5-51.8M of adjusted EBITDA versus $49.5M — a midpoint decline of 1.8% with the entire margin band at or below prior year. Management flagged heightened geopolitical uncertainty and market volatility and described Q2 transactional revenue as better than expected, so the range may prove conservative. Guided transactional capital markets revenue of $45-50M is the least predictable and highest-margin line, and it is entirely exogenous.

At $47.38 on 24.7M shares:

| Enterprise value | $1,345M |

| EV / LTM adj. EBITDA ($248.2M) | 5.42x |
| P/E on current-year consensus ($4.82) | 9.8x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($5.46) | 8.7x |
| PEG (9.8x ÷ 13.3% forward EPS growth) | 0.74 |
| LTM FCF yield on market cap | 13.1% (normalized ~10.9%) |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | 35.8x — distorted by the $82.8M pension charge; ~12-13x excluding it |

Peer medians in Financial Exchanges & Data are 15.2x P/E and 1.7% dividend yield (VIRT 9.2x, MKTX 13.8x, MORN 18.1x, FDS 15.2x, CBOE 25.3x). DFIN at 9.8x current-year adjusted earnings is the cheapest in the set and pays nothing.

The discount has an identifiable cause rather than being a simple mispricing, which is the crux of the call. DFIN carries software-like gross margins (66% non-GAAP) and 31.9% EBITDA margins but trades at multiples associated with declining print and BPO businesses. That is the market capitalizing terminal risk from SEC deregulation. The 2025 de-rating supports this reading: the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows DFIN's TSR index falling from 369.65 to 275.13 (roughly -26%) while the peer index rose from 178.07 to 196.54, in a year when adjusted EBITDA grew 10.4% and margins expanded. That was a multiple event, not an operating failure.

Where the market could be wrong: it is discounting proposal-stage rulemaking as if adopted at full scope, while the company retires 12% of shares annually at 5.4x EBITDA. Where the market could be right: filing-frequency reduction lands disproportionately on the highest-margin transactional and services layers, growth is one product deep, adjusted margins are flattered by 28%-growing SBC, and the buyback is partly debt-financed at a pace above normalized FCF. Repurchasing stock at 5.4x is powerfully accretive on flat EBITDA and value-destructive on structurally declining EBITDA.

Sizing the regulatory exposure: print at $107.9M of LTM revenue with a derived ~50% contribution margin (Q2 print revenue fell $6.1M against a $3.0M decline in print cost of sales — one quarter, low confidence) implies $20-25M of EBITDA at risk from an eventual halving, roughly 8-10% of LTM adjusted EBITDA. A filing-frequency reduction hitting 10-20% of the $296.9M tech-enabled services line could add $15-30M. Combined, on the order of 15-22% of EBITDA over several years, partly offset by an IPO channel whose sign is genuinely uncertain — DFIN's own position is that reduced reporting burden could boost IPO appeal, and transactional capital markets work is its highest-margin revenue.

Three analysts cover the stock, with targets of $60 to $70 (mean $64, median $62) — 27% to 48% above spot. The standard caution about treating targets as floors applies to inflection stocks trading cheaply against rising estimates, which describes DFIN reasonably well. But three estimates on a small cap is thin corroboration, and one of the three has not published a retrievable post-Q2 target value.

## Competitive Landscape

DFIN competes on four distinct fronts. ActiveDisclosure faces Workiva and filing-agent incumbents in disclosure management; Venue faces Datasite, Intralinks/SS&C and Ansarada in virtual data rooms; Arc Suite and IC–CCM face Broadridge and Toppan Merrill in fund reporting and communications; and the 10-K explicitly flags customers building internal capability, which is the least modeled vector because it never appears as a named competitor.

The single best competitive datapoint is ActiveDisclosure compounding at roughly 29% inside a company generating 32% consolidated EBITDA margins. That is a share-gainer's growth rate achieved profitably. We did not verify competitor financials, so no relative-growth or relative-multiple claim should be attributed here.

The trajectory reading is mixed rather than clean. Software mix rose to 47.9% of revenue from 45.1%, and revenue is becoming more recurring — but that is a revenue-type shift, not evidence of reduced customer concentration, and we found no explicit largest-customer disclosure. Against the ActiveDisclosure gain, IC–CCM is down 10.8%, Venue is flat, print is down 15%, and CM–Software Solutions margin fell 180bp despite 11.2% revenue growth on higher selling expense. Generative AI cuts both ways: eBrevia is a product enabler, while document assembly and tagging is a plausible near-term substitution target for client-side AI tools.

The appointment of Ken Napolitano as Chief Revenue Officer, announced with Q2 results to "advance the Company's sales transformation," is management's own acknowledgment that go-to-market needed rebuilding.

## Risk Assessment

**SEC rulemaking is the dominant risk and it is unresolved.** Two 2026 proposals matter: permitting semi-annual rather than quarterly reporting, and defaulting shareholder reports to electronic delivery, which management has tied to a print impact around 2028. Both are proposals, not final rules. We could not verify release numbers, comment periods, current stage, or adoption probability, and the 2028 date is management-attributed rather than filing-confirmed. The semi-annual channel is genuinely two-sided — fewer filing events but potentially more filers — and ActiveDisclosure's subscription structure means filing-count reduction would not flow through proportionally.

**Transactional cyclicality.** Q4 2025 capital markets activity was disrupted by a U.S. government shutdown delaying SEC review of registration statements. Guided Q3 transactional revenue of $45-50M is exogenous and high-margin.

**Cybersecurity is the most underappreciated tail.** DFIN handles pre-release material non-public information — unfiled financials, live M&A data rooms, draft registration statements. A breach would strike at the sole reason clients accept a small-cap vendor for deal-critical work.

**Goodwill and tangible equity.** Goodwill of $405.5M is 48.6% of total assets and exceeds total equity of $387.4M, giving roughly -$18.1M of tangible book. A sustained decline in either Software Solutions segment could trigger impairment — an optics risk more than a cash risk, but it removes book value as a valuation anchor.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director between June 2025 and July 2026. Roughly 158,800 shares (~$8.6M) of discretionary sales, clustered at $50-57 — at and above the current price. This requires care rather than alarm: CEO Leib's holdings rose to 621,315 shares (~2.5% of outstanding, ~$30M) because grants exceeded sales, several 2025 sales were same-day option-exercise monetizations, and executives with concentrated exposure have continuous diversification needs plausibly under pre-set plans. What it establishes narrowly is that the marginal buyer during the Q2 drawdown was the corporate treasury at $45.48, not any individual. CEO reported FY2025 compensation of ~$8.52M (compensation actually paid ~$4.90M) is high relative to a $1.17B market cap, and company-wide SBC of $33.6M equals 4.3% of revenue. Board composition includes former SEC Commissioner Luis Aguilar — regulatory literacy at the board level, not influence over outcomes.

Short interest is modest at 5.8% of float with 3.5 days to cover — an uncrowded position on either side.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is unusual and should be treated cautiously: 47 contracts across four expirations is an extremely thin chain for a $1.17B company, and wide quotes on illiquid strikes distort implied volatility. Taken at face value, near-term ATM IV of 115.5% against 48.0% one-year realized (85th percentile within the chain), pronounced backwardation to 42.3% at six months, and an implied move of ±11.8% on the August 21 expiry price something imminent. The data source characterizes the 43.8% put/call skew as elevated put demand; we do not infer direction beyond that label. IV rose from 61.2% to 115.5% between July 30 and August 9 while the price rose 8.9% — the opposite of the normal post-earnings volatility collapse, since Q2 was reported July 30.

We can identify no scheduled catalyst before August 21 that would justify a 115% near-term IV. Two readings are available: a data artifact from a thin chain, or positioning around an unannounced development. Given the source's own quality constraints, we weight the artifact explanation more heavily but treat it as a reason not to add here rather than a reason to sell. The six-month tenor at 42.3% with a slightly negative skew reads as a normal small-cap volatility profile, which argues the near-term print is mechanical.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $75 (+58%)

Flat-to-modestly-growing adjusted EBITDA of $265M by 2027 as the transactional capital markets window reopens (helped, not hurt, by reduced public-company reporting burden expanding the IPO universe), ActiveDisclosure sustains 20%+ growth and pulls Arc Suite and Venue adoption behind it, and the Q3 margin guide proves conservative as it has in each of the last four quarters. Software mix crosses 55% of revenue and the market re-rates DFIN toward a software multiple. At 7.5x $265M, less ~$180M net debt, on ~23M shares: $75. The mechanism requires only that the SEC proposals land narrowly and that the company keeps retiring 10-12% of shares annually at single-digit earnings multiples.

### Bear Case — $35 (-26%)

Both SEC proposals are adopted with broad scope. Print halves, filing-frequency reduction removes 10-20% of tech-enabled services, and adjusted EBITDA declines toward $215M by 2028. Investment Companies continues shrinking as the tailored-shareholder-report tailwind stays spent. The buyback, now partly debt-funded at a pace above normalized FCF, is revealed as retiring shares against a structurally declining earnings base — leverage rises toward 1.5-2.0x while EBITDA falls, and the multiple compresses to 4.5x. At 4.5x $215M less $220M net debt on 24M shares: $35. The stock traded to $36.11 in June 2026 on materially less resolved information, so this level has recent precedent.

### Base Case — $57 (+20%)

Adjusted EBITDA holds near $250M through 2027 (our FY2026 sensitivity is ~$247M), the SEC proposals resolve narrowly or slowly with the print impact concentrated after 2027, and the buyback retires another 8-10% of shares. Re-rating to 6.0x EV/EBITDA on confirmed revenue inflection: $1,500M EV less ~$200M net debt on 22.8M shares = $57. Cross-check on earnings: 10.5x a modestly haircut $5.40 FY2027 adjusted EPS = $57. Both routes converge.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives roughly +14% expected return — positive, but with a dispersion range of 84 percentage points on a variable (SEC rulemaking scope) we cannot verify.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $47.38, 18.9% below the 52-week high of $58.45 and 31.2% above the June low of $36.11.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October) is the decisive near-term checkpoint, followed by the FY2026 10-K in February 2027 for the risk-factor language on both SEC proposals.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 adjusted EBITDA lands at or above $51.8M — the top of the guided band, versus $49.5M last year — confirming the margin guide was conservative rather than a plateau. Combined with a price at or below $44, this would restore adequate asymmetry.
2. Growth broadens beyond ActiveDisclosure: IC–Software Solutions returns to mid-single-digit growth and Venue delivers a quarter of clearly positive year-over-year growth, removing the single-product fragility.
3. The SEC semi-annual reporting proposal is withdrawn, narrowed materially, or adopted on a timeline extending beyond 2029 — removing the terminal-value discount that explains the 5.4x multiple.
4. A price in the low $40s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 35% base-case upside against the same bear case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 adjusted EBITDA below $45.5M, or Q4 guidance implying a second consecutive year-over-year EBITDA decline — that would convert one guided quarter into a trend and invalidate the flat-EBITDA base case on which the buyback arithmetic depends.
2. Adoption of the semi-annual reporting rule with quantified guidance from management indicating a revenue impact above 5% of consolidated sales.
3. Net leverage rising above 1.5x while adjusted EBITDA is flat or declining — the signature of buying back stock into a shrinking earnings base.
4. Capitalized software assets declining for a third consecutive semi-annual period alongside capex below $45M annualized, which would indicate the FCF improvement is being funded from product competitiveness.
5. Any material cybersecurity incident involving client MNPI.

**What would not change our view:** further price weakness on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward and moves us toward the upgrade path, not away from it. Equally, further strength toward $57-60 on the same information set would move us to REDUCE, since the base case would be fully captured.

## Conclusion

DFIN is a genuinely cheap, genuinely well-capitalized company with a genuinely unresolved terminal-value question. The bull arithmetic is straightforward and does not require heroics: flat adjusted EBITDA near $250M, a 5.4x EV/EBITDA multiple, 10.9% normalized free cash flow yield, and a buyback retiring 10-12% of shares annually produce low-double-digit per-share earnings growth without any operating improvement at all. Four consecutive beats, a revenue inflection now three quarters deep, 0.7x net leverage and 12x interest coverage support the case that this is not a melting ice cube.

The reason we do not upgrade is the combination of what the market is pricing and what changed this quarter. At 5.4x EBITDA the market is capitalizing a contractionary SEC rulemaking cycle whose scope we cannot verify, and the Q3 guide — with the entire margin band at or below the prior year after four quarters of expansion — is the first company-generated datapoint pointing in the bears' direction. Layered on that: consolidated growth is carried by one product, roughly a third of the celebrated margin expansion is a faster-growing share-based-comp add-back, capex is falling while the capitalized-software balance shrinks, the buyback pace exceeds normalized free cash flow, and no insider bought a share on the open market while the stock traded from $58 to $36 and back to $47.

At $39 in June the margin of safety was wide enough to compensate for that uncertainty. At $47.38 — up 21% against annual estimate revisions of 3-7% — it is not. Base case $57 (+20%) against a bear case of $35 (-26%) is a reward-to-risk ratio below 1:1 on scenarios we can defend. HOLD, with a clear path to a more constructive rating if Q3 comes in at the top of the guided band or the price returns to the low $40s.
