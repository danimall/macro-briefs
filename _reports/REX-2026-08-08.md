## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is not a refiner despite its sector tag. It is a holding company with one reportable segment — ethanol and by-products — consisting of two consolidated plants (One Earth Energy in Gibson City, Illinois; NuGen Energy in Marion, South Dakota) and a 10.3% equity-method stake in Big River Resources, which operates four more plants. Aggregate capacity across the six facilities is roughly 730 million gallons per year, of which REX's effective share of volumes is approximately 300 million. The company carries $364.3M of cash and short-term investments against $83.9M of total liabilities and $3.1M of interest-bearing finance leases. That balance sheet is genuine and unusual for the industry.

The central analytical issue is composition of earnings. REX recognized $31.7M of Section 45Z clean-fuel production credit in FY2025 (fiscal year ended January 31, 2026), of which $28.1M was attributable to REX shareholders — 33.9% of REX net income of $83.0M. All of it was booked in the fourth quarter; none was in the prior-year comparison. Effective February 1, 2026 the company early-adopted ASU 2025-10 and moved the credit out of the tax line into gross profit, which lifted reported FY2025 gross margin from 14.4% to 19.3% with no change in economics and no change in REX-attributable EPS of $2.50. Ex-credit FY2025 EPS was approximately $1.65. On trailing twelve-month earnings through Q1 FY2026 of roughly $2.80, the stock trades at 15.3x; ex-credit that figure is roughly $1.74 and 24.6x. Enterprise value of $1,143.0M is 9.9x restated EBITDA of $116.0M and 13.6x the $84.3M ex-credit figure. For a fungible-commodity price-taker, the ex-credit multiples are the relevant ones, and they are not cheap.

The offsetting argument is optionality. REX earned only about $0.107 per gallon against a disclosed 45Z sliding scale of $0.10 to $1.00 per gallon, meaning its carbon-intensity score sits just below the 47.5 threshold and headroom is very large. Each incremental dime per gallon is worth roughly $29.5M of consolidated pretax income, or about $0.71 per REX share. Capturing it requires the One Earth carbon capture project, on which $176.3M of a $220–230M budget has already been spent. That project now needs both an EPA Class VI injection permit and an Illinois pipeline certificate; the Illinois Commerce Commission dismissed the pipeline application under the state's CO2 moratorium, and the expected federal determination slipped by roughly a quarter between the March 10-K and the June 10-Q. My base case of approximately $40.50 sits 5% below the current price, with a bull case of $58 and a bear case of $30. That distribution supports holding existing exposure and waiting for either a permit or a better price before adding.

## Company Overview

REX's economics are the corn/ethanol/natural-gas crush spread plus federal fuel policy, run from a small Dayton, Ohio corporate office by a two-member "Executive Committee" (Executive Chairman Stuart Rose and CEO Zafar Rizvi) that the 10-K identifies as the chief operating decision maker.

FY2025 revenue of $650.5M split as ethanol $504.4M (77.5%), dried distillers grains $88.2M (13.6%), distillers corn oil $52.4M (8.1%), modified distillers grains $5.4M, with a $0.3M derivative loss. Implied realized ethanol pricing of roughly $1.71 per gallon on approximately 295M consolidated gallons reconciles with the market-risk sensitivity table in the filings. Big River shipped 432.3M gallons in the twelve months ended January 31, 2026; REX's 10.3% interest carried at $41.3M as of April 30, 2026 against an original ~$20.0M investment.

Two differentiators are verifiable in the filings. First, capital structure: total liabilities are 10.4% of total assets, and the only interest-bearing item is $3.1M of finance leases on an electrical substation. FY2025 interest and other income of $15.0M — roughly 12% of restated pretax income — was earned purely from being unlevered, on a Treasury book yielding about 3.8%. Second, low-carbon optionality: REX qualified for 45Z and is building the infrastructure to deepen that qualification.

What REX does not have is pricing power, proprietary technology, brand, or scale leadership. Its ~300M effective gallons are a low-single-digit share of U.S. fuel ethanol capacity, well behind POET, ADM, Valero and Green Plains.

Revenue fell 22% over two years almost entirely on price. Ethanol revenue of $635.4M in FY2023 at roughly $2.15/gallon and $504.4M in FY2025 at roughly $1.71/gallon both imply approximately 295M gallons — volumes were flat and realized pricing fell about 20%. Gross profit nevertheless held in a $91–98M band because corn fell alongside ethanol. That is a real operating credit: REX navigated a 20% price decline without margin collapse.

Note the earnings pattern excluding the credit. REX-attributable net income was $60.9M (FY2023), $58.2M (FY2024), and $54.9M ex-45Z (FY2025). Core earnings power has been drifting slightly lower for three years, at roughly $1.65–1.82 per share. The step-up to $2.50 in FY2025 is the credit.

**SG&A is inflecting.** FY2025 SG&A rose 20% to $32.6M on flat revenue. Q1 FY2026 SG&A was $9.7M against $5.9M, up 64%, with stock-based compensation of $2.363M against $0.394M. The FY2025 tax reconciliation also shows $2.4M of nondeductible (162(m)) compensation. This is consistent with earnings-linked incentive compensation scaling against a 45Z-boosted result.

**Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026):** revenue $156.5M versus $158.3M; production tax credit income $7.5M versus nil; gross profit $29.1M versus $14.3M; pretax income $26.1M versus $13.6M; REX net income $18.5M versus $8.7M; diluted EPS $0.56 versus $0.26. Gross profit excluding the credit was $21.5M versus $14.3M, up 50% — the core crush genuinely improved on lower corn cost. Removing the consolidated credit and the roughly $1.8M of 45Z embedded in equity income, pretax was approximately $16.8M versus $13.6M, up 24%. Applying the reported 17.0% effective rate and pro-rata 14.9% noncontrolling interest share, ex-credit REX EPS was in the $0.33–$0.38 range. The company does not disclose the entity-level allocation of the credit, so that range is an estimate. Roughly 40–50% of the reported year-over-year EPS gain came from a credit absent in the comparison quarter.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $117.8M (FY2025), $64.2M (FY2024), $128.0M (FY2023); capex $68.4M, $71.3M, $37.7M. FY2025 free cash flow of $49.4M is a 3.5% yield on the current market cap; adjusting for accrued capex rising from $1.2M to $8.8M, economic FCF was closer to $41.8M. Essentially all FY2025 capex was growth spending — cumulative outlay on the CCS project plus the Gibson City expansion reached $176.3M by April 30, 2026 — against D&A of $23.2M. A steady-state proxy assuming maintenance capex approximates D&A produces roughly $96M, a 6.8% yield; that is a sensitivity, not a forecast, and it presumes 45Z persists and crush margins hold.

Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $2.1M against negative $3.5M in Q1 FY2025. The drivers are receivables up $6.9M, payables down $8.6M, accrued expenses down $8.6M (annual incentive payouts), plus a $4.9M deferred tax benefit and a $4.9M increase in "Other assets" representing the non-cash credit receivable. The pattern repeats in both years; this is seasonality, not deterioration, and should not be annualized.

**Cash conversion lags earnings recognition.** Cash income taxes paid were $6.1M in FY2025 against as-reported pretax income of $88.6M, a 6.9% cash rate — helped by bonus depreciation, immediate R&D expensing, and 45Z. But the general business credit carryforward rose from $4.2M to $24.7M, expiring in fiscal 2047, and the company states it does not intend to monetize the 2025 vintage. A meaningful portion of the credit is a deferred tax asset rather than cash.

**Balance sheet.** At April 30, 2026: total assets $807.7M; total liabilities $83.9M; REX shareholders' equity $629.2M ($19.10 per share); noncontrolling interests $94.5M; current ratio 6.76x. Standard screens misread this badly — a widely reported "debt/equity of 1.31" is the equity multiplier (total assets ÷ REX equity), not a leverage ratio; actual total-liabilities-to-equity is 0.136 and interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.005. Rebuilt Altman inputs (working capital/TA 0.467; retained earnings/TA 0.765; EBIT/TA 0.111; market equity/total liabilities 14.6; sales/TA 0.815) place the Z-score in the low double digits. Bankruptcy risk is de minimis. A Piotroski rebuild scores approximately 7 of 9, with the two negatives (falling current ratio, falling asset turnover) both consequences of converting cash into growth PP&E, which rose from $210.7M to $272.0M.

One qualification on the cash: the liquidity discussion indicates the substantial majority sits at the operating LLCs rather than the parent, and minority holders own roughly a quarter of One Earth. Look-through net cash is therefore below the consolidated $10.97 per share — plausibly $9.50–10.50 — and $44–54M of the project budget remains to be spent. Big River's debt agreements restrict distributions; REX's proportionate share of restricted net assets is $14.9M.

**Financial health: 7.5.** Fortress solvency and disciplined leverage, marked down from a pure quality read because a third of reported earnings is a non-cash-converting credit, the parent's claim on subsidiary cash is diluted, and no dividend converts the balance sheet strength into shareholder cash.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers, in descending order of controllability.

**One Earth capacity expansion.** Described as "nearing completion," with commissioning underway and the facility expected fully operational during fiscal 2026. This adds gallons regardless of permitting outcomes, and gallons multiply any per-gallon credit rate. The company has not quantified incremental volume, which limits how much I can underwrite.

**Mix shift toward fuel-linked by-products.** Distillers corn oil revenue grew 34% in FY2025 to $52.4M as renewable-diesel feedstock demand firmed, while dried distillers grains fell 37% over two years to $88.2M. DCO carries better margin characteristics and less trade-policy exposure than DDG exports.

**45Z rate escalation via CCS.** This is the large number and the uncontrollable one. At $0.107 per gallon against a $0.10–$1.00 scale, each dime is worth roughly $29.5M consolidated pretax and about $0.71 per REX share. A CCS project that cut carbon intensity by 15–25 points could plausibly add several dimes. Against that: the Illinois Commerce Commission dismissed the pipeline application without prejudice under the state CO2 moratorium tied to PHMSA rulemaking; an EPA Class VI permit alone does not enable injection without a connector pipeline; and the expected federal determination moved roughly a quarter later within two months of filings. The NuGen leg of the CCS story appears substantially impaired by South Dakota's eminent-domain prohibition and adverse North Dakota litigation against the region's principal developer. This is now a single-plant Illinois story.

**The hard constraint on all of it:** 45Z expires for fuel produced after December 31, 2029. That is a four-year runway, not a perpetuity. Any valuation that capitalizes the credit at a normal earnings multiple is implicitly assuming legislative extension.

Near-term direction of the crush is turning less favorable. The Q1 10-Q cites USDA's May 2026 projection of a 2026 corn crop near 16.0 billion bushels versus roughly 17.0 billion prior — lower acreage and yield. Lower corn cost was the explicit driver of the Q1 margin improvement. If corn firms in the second half of fiscal 2026 while ethanol pricing stays soft, core crush compresses at the same time comparisons lap the 45Z-inflated fourth quarter.

**Growth prospects: 5.5.** Real volume growth arriving, large but permit-gated credit upside, a hard 2029 policy sunset, and a reversing corn tailwind.

## Competitive Landscape

REX is a price-taker in a fragmented industry whose economics are set by the crush spread and federal policy. Three dynamics matter.

Value in the industry is migrating from gallons to carbon intensity. 45Z pays on a sliding scale tied to CI reduction, which converts carbon capture, low-carbon corn sourcing and process efficiency into the only sources of differentiated per-gallon economics. REX is currently capturing near the floor of that scale while spending $220–230M to move down the curve. Whoever sequesters CO2 first earns a durable advantage over neighbors who cannot.

Permitting, not engineering, is the binding constraint, and it is political. REX faces a federal Class VI process and an Illinois pipeline moratorium simultaneously; the South Dakota footprint's sequestration path has been set back by state eminent-domain restrictions and adverse North Dakota litigation. This is sector-wide rather than a REX execution failure, but it means the timing of the largest value driver is not management's to control, and $176.3M is already sunk.

The balance sheet is a genuine competitive weapon in a trough: REX earns interest income while levered peers service debt and is positioned to buy plants at distressed prices. The Executive Chairman's Form 4 title now reads "Executive Chairman & Head of Corporate Development," which may signal acquisition intent, though no filing confirms a role change.

Against these positives, customer concentration is severe and flat: ten customers were 93% of net sales in FY2025, 92% in FY2024, and nine were 92% in FY2023; seven customers were about 92% of receivables. The shift toward distillers corn oil improves margin mix but does nothing for counterparty concentration. Related-party corn purchases from minority equity investors were $86.4M in FY2025, 13% of consolidated cost of sales.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Improving on carbon-intensity positioning and balance-sheet optionality; structurally weak on pricing power, share, and customer concentration; the two-plant CCS story has narrowed to one.

## Risk Assessment

**Crush-spread leverage dominates.** A 10% adverse ethanol move reduces pretax income by roughly $51.6M — 43% of restated FY2025 pretax income. A 10% adverse corn move costs roughly $38.3M. REX does not use hedge accounting; derivative marks run straight through cost of sales ($5.4M gain FY2025, $2.7M loss FY2024, $15.0M gain FY2023).

**Policy concentration with a dated sunset.** 45Z supplied 33.9% of FY2025 REX net income and expires for fuel produced after December 31, 2029. The rate depends on an annually determined CI score, third-party emissions verification, prevailing-wage and apprenticeship compliance, and purchased environmental attribute certificates whose cost is embedded in cost of sales and not separately disclosed. RSM designated 45Z recognition as the sole Critical Audit Matter, citing significant judgments and the unusual nature of the credits — an appropriate flag on the highest-estimation-risk item in the statements.

**Permitting is now two-gated and has slipped.** Neither the Class VI permit nor a pipeline certificate was in hand as of May 28, 2026, and the expected federal determination moved later by roughly a quarter within two months. A slip of that size on a project carrying $176.3M of spend is a meaningful base-rate signal.

**Presentation distortion.** Reported gross margin improved approximately 490 basis points in FY2025 for purely presentational reasons under early-adopted ASU 2025-10, which is not mandatory until periods beginning after December 15, 2028. Any margin-trend or EBITDA comparison spanning the change, or against peers who have not adopted, is not like-for-like. Management's stated rationale — better alignment with potential future monetization of transferable credits — is coherent and forward-looking; I flag the metric distortion without inferring motive.

**Governance items to monitor.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed within five weeks. New employment agreements for the Executive Chairman, CEO and CFO were approved May 28, 2026, effective February 1, 2026 and executed June 29, 2026, following a Pearl Meyer review. Secondary reporting describes materially increased maximum bonus and severance caps; the substantive terms were not verified against the exhibit and should be treated as unconfirmed. The effective date coincides with the date the credit moved into gross profit, which matters because incentive compensation is earnings-based. An alternative and plausible reading is that pay had lagged and an independent consultant recommended market alignment — the $2.4M of nondeductible compensation suggests pay was already near 162(m) thresholds. Separately, the June 12, 2026 Item 5.02 filing could not be identified.

**Structural dependence on an uncontrolled stake.** Big River generated $140.5M of CY2025 net income and contributed $12.5M of equity income, but REX cannot compel distributions (it received $10.5M in FY2025) or direct Big River's CI strategy.

**Key-man risk.** The chief operating decision maker is a two-person Executive Committee; both members are long-tenured.

**Risk factors: 4.2.** The combination of commodity leverage, single-credit policy dependence with a hard expiry, binary permitting on sunk capital, and 93% customer concentration is not, in my view, fully compensated at 13.6x ex-credit EBITDA.

The options market corroborates a near-fair, event-dependent setup rather than either a compelling entry or an imminent break.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($58, +35.5%)

The One Earth expansion completes during fiscal 2026, adding gallons to a fixed cost base. The Class VI permit is granted and the Illinois pipeline path clears once PHMSA rulemaking or the statutory date lifts the moratorium; injection begins and carbon intensity falls 15–20 points. At $0.35–$0.45 per gallon, consolidated credit income rises to $90–115M annually against $31.7M today — worth $1.80–$2.50 per share of incremental REX earnings. Corn stays adequate, ethanol demand holds on E15 expansion, and DCO demand from renewable diesel keeps by-product mix improving. The unlevered balance sheet funds an opportunistic plant acquisition at trough valuations, and Congress extends clean-fuel credits past 2029, converting the annuity into a perpetuity. At that point 9x on a $95M+ core EBITDA plus a capitalized credit stream supports high-$50s.

### Bear Case ($30, −29.9%)

The 2026 corn crop comes in near USDA's 16.0 billion bushel projection, lifting input cost while ethanol pricing stays soft — the exact reversal of the Q1 margin driver. A 10% adverse crush swing removes 40%+ of pretax income. The Illinois pipeline remains unpermitted through 2027, stranding $176.3M of capital that earns no credit uplift and depreciates against a shrinking 45Z window. Investors reprice the equity on ex-credit earnings of roughly $1.65–1.75 at a sector-normal 8–10x plus cash, taking the stock back toward the high-$20s where it traded before credit recognition began in the fourth quarter of FY2025. Compounding risks: continued SG&A escalation under enhanced earnings-linked pay, customer concentration at 93%, and no dividend to support the price.

### What Would Change My Mind

The disconfirming evidence I take most seriously against my near-fair view is the ex-credit Q1 gross profit improvement of 50% year over year on lower corn cost. That is real operating improvement, not accounting. If the fiscal Q2 report shows a second consecutive quarter of ex-credit gross profit growth of that magnitude while corn cost holds, the core earnings power I have anchored at $55–60M is too low, and base case moves toward the mid-$40s.

## Conclusion

REX combines a genuinely fortress balance sheet — $361.2M of net cash, 26% of market capitalization, against $3.1M of interest-bearing debt — with an earnings statement that has become materially policy-dependent. Trailing reported earnings of $2.80 per share support a 15.3x multiple that looks reasonable; ex-45Z earnings of roughly $1.74 support 24.6x, and ex-credit EV/EBITDA of 13.6x sits at a premium to sector norms for a commodity price-taker with 93% of sales concentrated in ten customers and a disclosed sensitivity in which a 10% adverse ethanol move erases 43% of pretax income.

The upside case is quantifiable and large: earning near the $0.10 floor of a $0.10–$1.00 per gallon scale leaves roughly $0.71 per share of value for every incremental dime, and $176.3M of the $220–230M project is already spent. But delivery requires two permits the company does not hold, one of which was dismissed by a state commission under a moratorium tied to federal rulemaking, and the credit itself expires for fuel produced after December 31, 2029. Meanwhile the corn cost tailwind that drove the strong first quarter is projected to reverse.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it should be higher. What justifies it is that the price has fallen 6.1% since the last review with no fundamental deterioration, which improves risk/reward at the margin, while base case of $40.50 still sits slightly below spot and the two dominant swing factors remain unresolved. The rating stops declining here rather than improving. New capital should wait for the mid-$30s or for a permit; existing holders can carry the position through the August print with the corn-cost line and permitting language as the specific items to check.
