**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nebius Group is an Amsterdam-domiciled full-stack AI cloud ("neocloud") infrastructure provider that emerged from the 2024 carve-out and renaming of Yandex N.V. following its divestiture of Russian operations. The company designs and operates its own GPU data centers (it owns roughly 75% of its footprint, a structural contrast with lease-heavy peers like CoreWeave) and provides an integrated platform spanning data ingestion, model training, fine-tuning, and inference. Revenue has scaled sharply: $117.5M in 2024, $529.8M in 2025, and $399M in Q1 2026 (+684% YoY), with management guiding full-year 2026 annual run-rate (ARR) to $7-9B. The bull case rests on roughly $46B of contracted/committed revenue from a ~$17B Microsoft agreement (Sept 2025, with ~$7B in upfront payments) and an up-to-$27B five-year Meta supply agreement (March 2026), plus a $2B Nvidia strategic equity investment that secures scarce GPU allocation.

The financial reality underneath the headline growth is a hyper-capital-intensive build-out funded by debt, equity, and customer prepayments. The 2025 operating loss was –$611.7M (widening from –$440.7M in 2024); the reported +$82.5M net income is an artifact of divestiture/investment items, not operations, and should not be read as profitability. Capex jumped from $807.7M (2024) to $4.07B (2025), with management guiding $20-25B for 2026 against ~$0.5B of trailing revenue. Total liabilities rose from $295M to $7.84B in one year as the company raised convertible debt. Management and analysts both signal that multi-billion-dollar equity raises are coming; dilution is a near-certainty embedded in even the bull case.

At ~$287 the stock carries a ~$63B market cap, has appreciated ~493% over twelve months, and trades far above most fundamental anchors (Morningstar fair value $120; analyst price targets span $120 to $380, mean $244, median $258). The demand thesis is credible and the contracted backlog is substantial and verifiable in deal headlines, but the price embeds flawless execution on a financing program that dwarfs current revenue. We rate NBIS HOLD (growth_hold). The structural AI infrastructure tailwind, the dated catalysts (Nasdaq-100 inclusion June 22, ARR ramp, backlog conversion), and rapidly rising consensus estimates support continued ownership for risk-tolerant holders, but the absence of any margin of safety, the unresolved dilution path, and 20.3% short interest argue against initiating at this level.

## Company Overview

Nebius is the corporate successor to Yandex N.V. After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine and subsequent sanctions, the company's shares were halted; in 2024 it divested its Russian operating businesses, renamed itself Nebius, and re-listed on Nasdaq with a new AI-infrastructure mission. The 2021-2023 financials in any data feed (~$4.8B-$8.9B revenue) belong to the old Russian Yandex business and are not relevant to today's company; only the 2024 and 2025 continuing-operations figures matter.

The core business is AI cloud infrastructure: Nebius builds and runs GPU data centers and offers a unified platform for the full AI model lifecycle. It operates sites in the UK, Finland, Iceland, and New Jersey, with expansion underway in Spain, Pennsylvania, and Alabama. The ~75% owned-footprint model gives it more control over its infrastructure and economics than peers that lease, at the cost of substantially higher capital intensity. Adjacent group assets left over from the Yandex spinout include Toloka (data labeling), Avride (autonomous driving), TripleTen (edtech), and a stake in ClickHouse, but the investment thesis is now overwhelmingly the AI cloud business.

The company is led by co-founder/CEO Arkady Volozh (the original Yandex founder). It is a Netherlands-domiciled foreign private issuer that files Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, and operates a Class A dual-class share structure that concentrates voting control with founders/insiders.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** The growth is genuine and accelerating, roughly 4.5x year-over-year in 2025. Full-year 2026 ARR guidance of $7-9B implies the ramp is still in its early innings if the backlog converts.

**Profitability.** The business is deeply unprofitable at the operating level. Operating loss widened from –$440.7M (2024) to –$611.7M (2025) as scaling spend outran revenue. The reported +$82.5M net income for 2025 sits atop a –$611.7M operating loss and is almost certainly driven by divestiture-related gains or investment marks. Any financial-health model relying on headline net income or computing Altman/Piotroski scores against the old Yandex data would badly misread this company. The quarterly EPS track record is a string of narrowing losses that have beaten consensus four straight quarters: Q2'25 –$0.38 (vs –$0.48), Q3'25 –$0.40 (vs –$0.54), Q4'25 –$0.70 (vs –$1.16), Q1'26 –$0.32 (vs –$0.78). The beats reflect both operating execution and the volatility of below-the-line items.

**Capital intensity and the balance sheet.** This is the defining feature. At year-end 2025: cash $3.68B, total assets $12.4B, long-term debt $4.1B, equity $4.59B. The liability explosion reflects convertible debt taken on to fund the build. R&D rose from $129.7M to $177.3M.

**The funding engine.** The mechanism that lets a $0.5B-revenue company credibly plan its capex program is a combination of customer prepayments (the Microsoft deal reportedly carries ~$7B upfront), convertible bonds ($4.33B raised), and the $2B Nvidia equity investment. Customer prepayments convert backlog into near-term, non-dilutive capital that de-risks the capex bill. The chain that has to hold is straightforward: contracted backlog → prepayments + new financing fund GPU purchases → GPUs come online → contracted revenue converts at acceptable margins → cash flow eventually services the obligations. Each link is plausible given the named counterparties, but none is yet proven at the scale required, and the financing market staying open is an exogenous assumption.

**Financial Health assessment.** This is a 5.5, not lower and not higher. The downside risks are real: operating cash burn, a 26x liability increase, and dependence on external capital. But the company is not in distress, given $3.68B cash, equity exceeding debt, and a backlog that supports continued financing access. The score reflects a capital structure that is appropriate for a hyper-growth infrastructure builder if and only if the demand and financing assumptions hold, with meaningful covenant/refinancing/dilution exposure if they do not.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest dimension of this story and the most reliable predictor in our framework. The signals are unusually strong and broad-based:

- **Revenue velocity:** +684% YoY in Q1 2026, on top of 4.5x full-year 2025 growth. ARR guidance of $7-9B for 2026 implies an order-of-magnitude scale-up.
- **Estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have improved +58.9% over 30 days and +44.0% over 90 days (–$3.95 → –$1.62), and next-year +42.9% over 30 days and +39.0% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate is choppier (-41% over 90 days but +5.4% over 30 days), reflecting the difficulty of modeling below-the-line items, but the full-year and next-year direction is clearly up. Estimate revisions of this magnitude are among the strongest forward indicators available.
- **Contracted backlog:** ~$46B across the Microsoft (~$17B) and Meta (up-to-$27B) agreements anchors a substantial portion of the ARR ramp in signed contracts rather than projection.
- **Thematic durability:** This is a direct beneficiary of the AI infrastructure capex supercycle. The demand is confirmed across multiple independent data points: hyperscaler capex guidance (Meta guiding up to $145B of 2026 AI infrastructure spend), GPU supply remaining behind demand, and a broad neocloud cohort (Nebius, CoreWeave, IREN) validating triple-digit growth with real contracts. Multiple companies in the subsector are simultaneously posting 50%+ growth, which raises the durability of the demand thesis above a single-company story.

The standard "estimate revisions >30% + revenue growth >40%" signal is unambiguously present here. The important caveat is that the signal is most powerful when the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates; NBIS is not, which is why the strong growth score does not flow through to a strong valuation score or an automatic BUY. Growth scores a 9.0; the magnitude and breadth justify a near-maximum reading, capped just short of 10 by the fact that the unit economics of the higher-margin enterprise/inference business remain unproven at scale.

## Valuation Assessment

NBIS is not valuable on any trailing metric; a 99x trailing/forward P/E on $0.04 EPS is mechanically meaningless given negative operations, and the stock should be evaluated on a forward revenue and normalized-margin basis, not trailing earnings. The relevant question is whether ~$63B of enterprise value is justified by the ARR ramp and backlog conversion.

On the bull math: if 2026 ARR lands at the $8B midpoint and the business eventually earns a CoreWeave-style mid-teens-to-20% steady-state operating margin, with a market willing to pay a high revenue multiple for a contracted-backlog infrastructure pure-play, the current price is defensible but not cheap. On the bear math: at ~8x the $8B ARR midpoint (and far higher on trailing revenue), the stock prices in flawless execution on a capex program funded partly by capital that does not yet exist.

The analyst spread captures the uncertainty: targets range from $120 (Morningstar fair value, 1-star, no-moat) to $380, with a mean of $244 and median of $258, both below the current ~$287. The fact that the stock trades above the consensus median is itself a caution. Under our framework, analyst targets behave as floors for cheap inflection stocks but as same-extrapolation consensus on premium-multiple names; NBIS sits in the latter camp, where the targets reflect the same growth optimism embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of upside.

The valuation score is a 4.5. Cheapness is not the issue; the issue is that the multiple already discounts a near-perfect outcome, leaving no margin of safety, while the path to that outcome requires multi-billion-dollar dilution that consensus acknowledges. We deliberately resist treating prior appreciation as a valuation negative; the +493% move reflects genuine fundamental improvement. But the current price versus the current/forward fundamentals offers asymmetric downside if any link in the financing-to-revenue chain slips, which is what a sub-5 score is meant to convey.

## Competitive Landscape

Nebius is a leading European/transatlantic AI infrastructure pure-play and a direct comparable to CoreWeave. Its differentiation is threefold:

1. **Owned footprint (~75%):** more control over infrastructure, cost, and energy procurement than lease-heavy peers, at the cost of higher capital intensity.
2. **Strategic Nvidia relationship:** the $2B equity investment plus distribution partnership is a genuine advantage in securing scarce GPU allocation, which is the binding constraint in this market.
3. **Higher-margin orientation:** the strategic push toward enterprise and inference workloads (reinforced by the Eigen AI acquisition for inference optimization) targets better unit economics than the lower-margin large training jobs that dominate CoreWeave's mix.

The trajectory is improving via share gains from signed hyperscaler contracts, deepening vertical integration, and an expanding enterprise partner program (TD Synnex/Nvidia distribution). The constraint on the score is that Morningstar assigns no economic moat, and GPU cloud is structurally competitive and increasingly commoditized. Differentiation rests on execution and Nvidia access rather than a durable, defensible moat. The higher-margin enterprise/inference economics are the strategic goal but not yet proven. Competitive position scores a 6.5: clearly improving trajectory, real structural differentiators, but no durable moat and unproven margin economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Capital intensity and external funding dependence.** Even with prepayments and converts, billions of equity issuance are signaled by both management and analysts. Dilution is embedded in the base case, not a tail risk.

**Overbuild / demand risk.** The thesis assumes AI compute demand stays ahead of supply. If the AI capex cycle cools, Nebius could be left with stranded, depreciating GPU assets against fixed obligations. The broader infrastructure-demand picture remains broadly confirmed across the hyperscaler complex, but a single-source demand-plateau scenario would hit a levered, pre-profit builder hardest.

**Customer concentration.** Revenue visibility is heavily tied to a handful of counterparties (Microsoft, Meta). This concentrates counterparty risk and reduces pricing power; large training-heavy hyperscaler deals are also lower-margin.

**Unproven unit economics.** The higher-margin enterprise/inference business is the strategic goal but not yet proven at scale; the current revenue mix skews toward lower-margin training capacity.

**Governance and disclosure.** As a Netherlands FPI, Nebius files 20-F/6-K with reduced disclosure relative to domestic issuers; primary-source risk-factor and proxy detail could not be read directly. The Class A dual-class structure concentrates voting control with insiders, a consideration for minority holders facing dilution. Residual complexity from the Russian-entity carve-out (sanctions history, governance) is an overhang.

**Valuation risk.** The stock is priced for flawless delivery, leaving limited downside protection if execution slips.

Risk factors score a 4.0: multiple specific, material, and partly unpriced downside catalysts (dilution magnitude, backlog-conversion timing, margin proof) combine with a valuation that offers no cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 109.9% against 1-year historical vol of 99.3%, rich by ~10.6 points, with the market pricing a ±12.8% move on the nearest weekly expiry. IV has compressed sharply (131.1% → 109.9%, –21.2 points) even as the stock rose 38.3% since mid-May, indicating that some event uncertainty (the Q1 print, recent deal flow) has resolved while directional momentum continued. The term structure is in contango (near-term 109.9% < long-term 115.2%), a normal/complacent shape. Near-term put/call skew is mildly elevated at 4.8% with put/call OI rising from 0.34 to 0.65, suggesting incremental hedging into strength, but the longer-dated 6-month skew turns negative (–5.5%) with heavy call-side positioning. Unusual activity is dominated by short-dated call buying (multiple strikes at 5-6x volume/OI, including a $370 strike +29% out-of-the-money), consistent with momentum and the June 22 Nasdaq-100 inclusion. The 20.3% short interest with only 2.4 days to cover creates genuine two-sided squeeze potential. The net read is a volatile, reflexive setup where the options market is leaning bullish on a 6-12 month horizon while carrying elevated near-term hedging; it neither confirms nor refutes the fundamental thesis but argues for sizing discipline.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$380, +33% from current)

AI compute demand stays ahead of supply through 2027. The ~$46B Microsoft/Meta backlog converts on schedule, 2026 ARR lands at the high end ($9B), and the owned-footprint model plus inference push (Eigen AI) drives margins toward a sustainable mid-teens-to-20% operating level. Nvidia's strategic backing keeps GPU allocation flowing. Prepayments and converts minimize equity dilution, and the market continues to award a premium revenue multiple to a contracted-backlog infrastructure pure-play. Nasdaq-100 inclusion and continued momentum provide passive and reflexive flow support. The high analyst target ($380) approximates this scenario.

### Bear Case (~$120, –58% from current)

The AI capex cycle cools or hyperscalers in-source capacity, leaving Nebius with stranded, depreciating GPU assets against fixed debt and lease obligations. Margin economics on the lower-margin training mix disappoint, the higher-margin inference business fails to scale, and the company is forced into large, dilutive equity raises at a depressed price. Customer concentration bites if a key contract is renegotiated or delayed. The stock reverts toward fundamental anchors; Morningstar's $120 fair value implies ~58% downside. Given the meaningless trailing multiple and the financing dependence, the bear case is fast and sharp, not a slow grind.

### Base Case (~$258, –10% from current)

The backlog converts but on a slightly longer timeline; ARR lands mid-range ($7-8B); dilution proceeds as signaled, modestly capping per-share value; and the market re-rates toward the consensus median as the stock digests its run. This maps to the analyst median of $258, roughly 10% below the current price, reflecting a stock that has run ahead of even an optimistic central estimate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth and demand thesis is credible and the catalysts are dated, but the valuation offers no margin of safety and the dilution path is unresolved. This is a hold for risk-tolerant existing holders, not an initiation at ~$287.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the June 22, 2026 Nasdaq-100 inclusion (passive-flow event), the next quarterly print (ARR-trajectory and capex-funding update), and any announced equity raise (which will define the dilution magnitude).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward or below the $190-220 range (the May trading zone) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety against the $258 median target.
- Evidence the higher-margin inference/enterprise mix is scaling with demonstrated unit economics, e.g., a quarter showing operating-margin improvement on the contracted base.
- A dilution program that comes in smaller than feared (financed disproportionately by prepayments/converts rather than equity), de-risking per-share value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any material slippage in backlog conversion: a renegotiated, delayed, or reduced Microsoft or Meta commitment.
- A large equity raise at a discount that signals constrained financing access, or a covenant/refinancing event.
- A confirmed cooling in the broader AI capex cycle (hyperscaler capex guidance cuts, GPU supply moving ahead of demand) that threatens stranded-asset risk.
- Estimate-revision direction turning negative on the full-year/next-year lines.

## Conclusion

Nebius is one of the cleaner public expressions of the AI infrastructure build-out: explosive, contracted revenue growth; a strategic Nvidia relationship that secures scarce GPU allocation; an owned-footprint model that differentiates it from lease-heavy peers; and a forward-looking shift toward higher-margin inference. The growth dimension is exceptional and the estimate-revision momentum (+58.9% current-year over 30 days, +42.9% next-year) is among the strongest forward signals available. Those factors anchor a constructive medium-term view for holders.

The offsetting reality is that the company burns cash at the operating level (–$611.7M in 2025), is funding a $20-25B 2026 capex program against ~$0.5B of trailing revenue, will almost certainly dilute shareholders by billions, and trades above its consensus median target after a ~493% twelve-month run. There is no margin of safety in the price, and the financing-to-revenue chain, while plausible given named counterparties, is unproven at the required scale. The 20.3% short interest and ~110% implied vol mean the path will be volatile in both directions.

We rate NBIS **HOLD** with a growth_hold rationale. The thesis is sound enough to own through the backlog-conversion and ARR-ramp catalysts, but the valuation, dilution overhang, and execution dependence keep it short of a BUY. New capital should wait for a pullback toward the $190-220 zone or for proof that the higher-margin economics are scaling. The base case implies modest downside to the $258 median, with a wide bull/bear band ($380 / $120) that captures how binary the outcome remains.
