**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Four Corners Property Trust is a single-tenant net-lease REIT spun out of Darden Restaurants in 2015, owning 1,313 restaurant and retail properties across 48 states leased on long-term triple-net terms. The business is defensive and high-quality: 99.6% occupancy, 99.7% rent collection, an investment-grade balance sheet with 100% fixed/swapped debt, and a well-covered dividend yielding ~5.6% at the current $26.07. Q1 2026 delivered $78.2M revenue (+9.4% YoY) and AFFO of $0.45/share, with per-share growth held to modest levels because the acquisition machine is funded through continuous equity issuance.

The central issue for the stock is price and growth arithmetic, not quality. FCPT trades at roughly 14.5x annualized Q1 AFFO ($0.45 × 4 = $1.80) near the upper end of its $22.78–$26.86 52-week range, while consensus EPS/FFO estimates have been revised down across every window over the past 90 days (current-year EPS from $1.19 to $1.14, next-year from $1.20 to $1.14 over 90 days). The company has also missed EPS estimates in three of the last four quarters. The diversification story is real and accelerating — the $268M Mission Pet Health veterinary acquisition closed in July, and dozens of non-restaurant brands have been added — but it is a multi-year process, and the offsetting headwind is a refinancing cycle that replaces sub-4% legacy debt with 5–6.5% new debt through 2028.

We rate FCPT a HOLD (income_hold). The dividend, ~82% AFFO payout, near-perfect occupancy, and clean IG balance sheet make this a durable income vehicle, and the June 2026 switch to monthly dividends signals management confidence. But with the stock near its 52-week high, estimates falling, and per-share growth diluted by equity-funded acquisitions and rising interest costs, there is no asymmetric upside to underwrite. Own it for the yield, not for appreciation.

## Company Overview

FCPT owns and leases single-tenant properties under long-term triple-net leases, where tenants bear property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. As of March 31, 2026 the portfolio comprised 1,313 properties, 8.8M square feet, at 99.6% occupancy with a weighted-average lease term of 6.7 years (down from 6.9 at year-end 2025) and average annual escalators of ~1.5%. Roughly 52% of base rent comes from investment-grade tenants.

The company runs two segments. Real Estate Operations — the core net-lease business — represents essentially all of the value. The Kerrow Restaurant Operating Business, seven company-operated LongHorn Steakhouse locations in San Antonio held in a taxable REIT subsidiary, is immaterial (~$8.4M quarterly revenue, ~$0.25M EBITDA). A standardized XBRL pull shows revenue of only ~$31.5M — that figure captures Kerrow alone and badly understates the ~$294M consolidated total. The correct FY2025 revenue base is rental (~$262.6M) plus restaurant (~$31.5M).

FCPT competes in the fragmented net-lease space against Realty Income, Agree Realty, NNN REIT, and Essential Properties. Its differentiator, and its defining risk, is deep exposure to Darden/casual-dining real estate, which it is actively working to reduce.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 total revenue was ~$294M, up ~9.7% from ~$268M in FY2024 and ~$251M in FY2023, a steady mid-to-high single-digit growth trajectory driven by property acquisitions. Q1 2026 revenue was $78.2M (+9.4% YoY), with rental revenue up 10% to $69.8M on 92 properties acquired over the trailing year. Q1 net income was $30.3M ($0.28 diluted EPS vs $0.26 prior year).

**FFO/AFFO.** The metrics that matter for a REIT held up but grew slowly per share: Q1 2026 FFO of $0.42/share and AFFO of $0.45/share, versus $0.41 and $0.44 a year earlier. The muted per-share growth despite ~9% higher share count reflects the tension at the heart of the model: external growth is funded through dilutive equity issuance, so headline acquisition volume translates into only modest per-share accretion.

**Balance sheet.** The capital structure is conservative and clean. Total debt of $1.205B ($590M term loans + $625M senior unsecured notes), with the $350M revolver undrawn. Total equity of $1.66B puts D/E at ~0.73. All debt is unsecured, and 100% is fixed or swapped to fixed at a weighted-average term-loan rate of ~3.97% after hedges. Cash was $29.6M. This is an investment-grade, covenant-compliant issuer; the Altman Z-Score near 1.55 is a structural artifact of the asset-heavy REIT model (low revenue/assets and EBIT/assets ratios), not a genuine distress signal.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1 operating cash flow was $47.2M against $26.8M of acquisitions. Growth was funded via ATM equity ($39.1M issued in Q1 at ~$27.19 net) plus term-loan borrowings. Share count has climbed steadily from ~93.6M (2024) to 102.7M (2025) to 109.7M (3/31/26), a ~17% increase over roughly two years that quantifies the dilution drag on per-share metrics.

**Refinancing arithmetic.** The quiet FFO headwind is the maturity ladder: ~$150M in the remainder of 2026, $250M in 2027, $140M in 2028. Maturing notes carry legacy coupons of 2.7–4.9%, while new debt prices meaningfully higher; the July 2023 note came at 6.44% and the April 2026 term loan at SOFR+1.25%. As ~$540M of debt reprices through 2028, per-share FFO growth increasingly depends on acquisition cap-rate spreads (~6.5% on the vet portfolio) offsetting rising interest expense.

## Growth Analysis

FCPT's growth engine is acquisition spread arbitrage: buy net-lease properties at cap rates above the blended cost of capital and capture the difference. The 2026 cadence has been active and increasingly diversified, with the $268M Mission Pet Health deal (102 veterinary properties, ~$17.4M cash rent, ~6.5% implied cap rate) closing July 16, alongside bolt-ons in auto service (Tires Plus, Gerber Collision), QSR (Burger King via Dhanani Group), and industrial (Drilling Tools International). Management pre-funded the pipeline with a $200M delayed-draw term loan (7-year, matures 2033, SOFR+1.25%; $50M drawn, $150M reserved) and retains a fully-available $500M ATM program from October 2025.

The problem is that the growth does not compound cleanly on a per-share basis. Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current-quarter EPS revised from $0.30 to $0.28 (-4.8% over 30 days), current-year from $1.19 to $1.14 (-4.0% over 90 days), and next-year from $1.25 to $1.14 (-8.6% over 30 days). The company has missed EPS estimates in three of the last four quarters (-1.3%, -4.0%, -5.3%, -10.1%), with the miss magnitude widening. The estimate-revision signal is a caution, not a catalyst. Growth is real but decelerating on a per-share basis as dilution and refinancing costs eat into the spread.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.07, FCPT trades at ~14.5x annualized Q1 AFFO ($1.80 run-rate) and ~23.5x trailing GAAP EPS. On a REIT the AFFO multiple is the relevant lens, and 14–15x for a defensive net-lease name with the ~5.6% yield is reasonable but not cheap. The stock sits near the top of its 52-week range, having recovered fully from its December 2025–March 2026 lows.

The peer table is heterogeneous and not a clean comp set (it mixes billboard operators OUT/LAMR, gaming REIT GLPI, ground-lease specialist SAFE, and entertainment REIT EPR). Peer median P/E of 18.3x and median yield of 4.5% are of limited use given the business-model dispersion. Against genuine net-lease peers (Realty Income, Agree, NNN), FCPT typically trades at a modest AFFO-multiple discount and a yield premium, both attributable to its Darden/casual-dining concentration, a discount that is justified rather than a mispricing.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $26.00, mean $28.11, median $28.00, high $30.00. On a name with falling estimates and no earnings reacceleration, these targets do not function as floors; they reflect a stock trading close to fair value with limited dispersion. Applying the guardrail against treating a "cheap" screen as a buy signal: FCPT is not cheap on forward AFFO, its multiple already reflects its quality, and there is no identifiable catalyst to argue the market is underpricing it. The valuation score is 4.5, reflecting a stock that is fairly-to-fully valued with the return case resting on the dividend, not multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

FCPT's competitive strengths are genuine: an investment-grade, fully-hedged balance sheet that is a real advantage in a higher-rate environment; near-perfect occupancy; and portfolio-level EBITDAR rent coverage of ~5x (Olive Garden ~6x) that provides substantial cushion against a restaurant downturn. Tenant rent coverage of this magnitude supports the durability of cash flow even if casual-dining traffic softens.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, diversification is accelerating: 35+ brands added in 2025, the large vet-portfolio deal in 2026, and expansion into auto service, QSR, industrial, and pet care. Darden concentration has fallen from ~100% at spin to ~44.5% of base rent, with Olive Garden at ~31.5% and LongHorn ~9%. On the negative side, the company is partly substituting one casual-dining exposure for another: Brinker International was added as a named ~6.6% concentration in the FY2025 10-K, so restaurant-sector exposure remains ~74% of rent. FCPT is also smaller and more concentrated than diversified peers, and its higher cost of new debt (~5–6.5%) compresses the investment spreads that drive its external growth relative to the sub-4% era. The competitive position is stable-to-slowly-improving, warranting a 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Darden/brand concentration.** The single most important risk. ~44.5% of base rent depends on one former parent, and Olive Garden alone is ~31.5% (316 properties). Any deterioration in Darden's credit or casual-dining traffic flows directly to FCPT. Darden leases also carry shorter WALT (~4.8 years vs 6.7 portfolio-wide) and a right of first offer plus restrictions on selling to competing casual/fine-dining brands, which limits FCPT's flexibility to exit or maximize asset values.

**Refinancing headwind.** ~$540M of debt matures through 2028 at legacy 2.7–4.9% coupons, refinancing at 5–6.5%. This is a gradual, mechanical FFO drag that management must outrun with acquisition spreads.

**Equity-funded dilution.** The model requires continuous ATM issuance. If the share price falls or cap-rate spreads compress, external growth stalls and per-share metrics stagnate.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** As a bond-proxy income vehicle, FCPT's price is directly exposed to the long end of the curve. The higher-for-longer rate path is a headwind both to the multiple and to the refinancing math.

Most of these risks are known and reasonably priced: the concentration is thoroughly disclosed and the stock already carries a peer discount for it. There is no imminent binary catalyst. The risk score is 5.5, reflecting a business with well-understood, gradually-managed risks rather than acute, mispriced ones.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.6% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 18.4% (a 12.2pp spread), though at only the 19th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew of 3.7% shows mildly elevated put demand, and the 3-month tenor shows a more pronounced 18% skew, consistent with modest hedging appetite around an interest-rate-sensitive income name rather than a directional bet. The term structure is in contango (near-term 30.6% below longer-dated 43.6%), a normal/complacent shape. ATM IV has collapsed from 50.7% to 30.6% since late May as the stock recovered, reflecting resolved uncertainty rather than fresh stress. Nothing in the options data challenges the fundamental read; the implied ±10.6% near-term move is unremarkable for a REIT and does not signal informed positioning ahead of a catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30, ~15% upside plus ~5.6% yield).** Diversification continues to accelerate, Darden concentration falls below 40%, and the market rewards the de-risking with modest multiple expansion. Cap-rate spreads hold above the rising cost of debt, AFFO per share grows toward $1.90+, and rate cuts compress the discount rate applied to net-lease income. The monthly dividend and near-perfect occupancy attract yield-seeking flows. This gets the stock to the top of the analyst range.

**Bear Case ($22–23, ~11–16% downside).** A casual-dining slowdown pressures Darden traffic and rent coverage, refinancing at 6%+ overwhelms acquisition spreads, and continued ATM dilution combines with falling estimates to stall per-share FFO. A rate backup pushes the yield toward 6.5%, mechanically repricing the equity toward the low $20s. The three-in-four estimate-miss streak continues and confidence in forward AFFO erodes.

**Base Case ($27–28, low single-digit price gain plus dividend).** FCPT grinds out mid-single-digit revenue growth, holds AFFO per share roughly flat-to-slightly-up as dilution and refinancing offset acquisition spreads, and pays its dividend. Total return is dominated by the yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the dividend and stable cash flows, not on price appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), which will show the first full-quarter impact of the Mission Pet Health acquisition and the monthly-dividend transition.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive and FFO/AFFO per share reaccelerates to mid-single-digit growth, confirming acquisition spreads are outrunning refinancing costs.
- Darden/casual-dining concentration falls below ~40% of rent with no offsetting new casual-dining exposure, and the market begins to close the concentration discount.
- A pullback toward the low-$23s (yield >6.3%) restoring an asymmetric total-return case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A cut or freeze to the dividend, or the AFFO payout ratio climbing above ~90% as coverage deteriorates.
- Evidence of Darden/Olive Garden credit or traffic stress (rent-coverage below ~4x, occupancy slipping below ~99%, or a material lease non-renewal).
- ATM issuance persisting while the stock trades below net asset value, signaling value-destructive dilution.

## Conclusion

FCPT is a well-run, conservatively-financed net-lease REIT executing a credible multi-year diversification away from its Darden roots, with the balance-sheet quality and rent coverage to sustain its dividend through a rate cycle. The June 2026 monthly-dividend adoption and the pre-funded acquisition pipeline reflect management confidence and disciplined liability management. The stock has continued its modest score-trajectory improvement (5.7 → 6.0 → 5.5 here, essentially flat), and the recent price recovery to near the 52-week high, coupled with falling estimates and the three-in-four miss streak, leaves no asymmetric upside to underwrite at $26.07.

This is an income hold. The durable return is the yield, backed by near-perfect occupancy and an IG balance sheet; the price-appreciation case is capped by dilutive growth funding and a refinancing headwind that mechanically pressures per-share FFO. Own FCPT for the dividend and the defensive cash-flow profile, and revisit on either a fundamental reacceleration or a pullback that restores a total-return margin of safety.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the AFFO multiple was overstated ($26.07 / $1.80 = ~14.5x, not 15.5x), making the stock marginally less expensive than the report characterized — though not enough to alter the fully-valued conclusion.
