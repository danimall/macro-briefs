## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest provider of large-horsepower contract compression infrastructure in the United States, operating 4.5 million fleet horsepower at 97.7% utilization across the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. The business benefits from a structural supply constraint: new engine lead times have doubled to 110-120 weeks as data center demand competes for the same Caterpillar engines, creating an effective moat through at least 2028. Revenue per horsepower is at all-time highs, adj. EBITDA margins expanded 210bps to 54.7% in 2025, and operating cash flow nearly doubled to $600M.

The problem is price. At $68.27, KGS trades at 76x trailing EPS and roughly 25x FY2026E consensus EPS of $2.71, well above the peer median P/E of 20.8x and above every published analyst price target (consensus mean $61.75, high $70). The stock has appreciated roughly 100% in the past 12 months, driven by EQT AB's overhang removal, industry consolidation, the DPS acquisition expanding the TAM into distributed power, and outstanding operating performance. But $2.6B in debt with $198M in annual interest expense, a disclosed DOJ/OFAC investigation related to cartel-associated payments in Mexico, and a $102M Texas sales tax contingency create idiosyncratic risks that the elevated multiple leaves little room to absorb. This is an excellent business at a price that reflects excellence.

## Company Overview

Kodiak Gas Services IPO'd on July 3, 2023, and has rapidly consolidated its position as the dominant large-horsepower contract compression operator in the U.S. The company operates two segments: **Contract Services** (90.3% of 2025 revenue, $1.18B) and **Other Services** (9.7%, $127M covering station construction, maintenance, parts). Approximately 80% of its fleet consists of units >1,000 HP, which command longer contract durations, higher margins, and better recontracting economics than smaller equipment.

Geographic concentration is significant: 82.8% of assets are deployed in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. While this creates regional risk, these are the lowest-cost, longest-duration production basins in the country.

On April 1, 2026, Kodiak completed the $675M acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions (DPS), adding ~395 MW of distributed power generation capacity serving data centers, microgrids, and manufacturing facilities. This was rebranded as "Kodiak Power Solutions" and represents the company's most consequential strategic pivot, moving it from a pure-play compression provider into a broader energy infrastructure platform.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at approximately $2.6B, comprising $750M in 7.25% Senior Notes (2029), $465M drawn on the ABL Facility (due 2030), $770M in 6.50% Notes (2033), and $630M in 6.75% Notes (2035). The maturity profile is well-staggered with no near-term refinancing cliff. Leverage is approximately 3.5x adj. EBITDA, within industry norms for asset-heavy compression businesses.

The current ratio of 0.84 and near-zero cash balance ($3.2M) reflect the company's use of its $1.5B ABL facility as primary liquidity. This is standard for the business model, but liquidity is entirely dependent on continued ABL access.

Interest expense of $198M consumes 15% of revenue and 2.4x net income. In a downturn scenario where EBITDA contracted 25-30%, interest coverage would tighten materially.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 (distress zone) is structurally misleading for this business model (low working capital by design, high PP&E/goodwill from acquisitions), but the underlying reality is that this is a leveraged equity story. Upside and downside are both amplified by the capital structure.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

Three structural forces support above-average growth through the end of the decade:

1. **Supply constraint:** Engine lead times have doubled from ~55 weeks to 110-120 weeks as Caterpillar and other manufacturers prioritize data center power generation orders over oilfield equipment. No meaningful new competitive supply can enter the market before 2028. This supports continued pricing power on recontracting.

2. **Volume growth:** Raymond James estimates 26 Bcf/d of incremental U.S. natural gas production through 2030, implying ~15 million additional compression HP needed (a 25% increase in the installed base). Growing LNG export capacity along the Gulf Coast and anti-flaring regulations create incremental demand.

3. **Pricing power at peak levels:** With utilization at 97.7% and competitors also above 95%, vendors are pushing for 7-10 year contract renewals at all-time high rates. Revenue per HP is at record levels and rising.

### DPS Acquisition — Diversification into Power Generation

The $675M DPS acquisition adds distributed power generation serving data centers, microgrids, and manufacturing. Management expects immediate accretion to earnings and DCF/share. The strategic logic is sound: Kodiak's core competency is deploying and operating large-horsepower equipment under contract, and the DPS assets use the same Caterpillar/Waukesha engine platforms. The risk is that serving data center and industrial power customers involves different commercial relationships, service-level expectations, and competitive dynamics than oil & gas compression.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. large-horsepower contract compression market is effectively a three-player oligopoly: Kodiak (KGS), Archrock (AROC), and USA Compression Partners (USAC). All three reported Q4 2025 utilization above 95% with revenue per HP at record levels. Roughly $2B in acquisition capital was deployed across the industry in the second half of 2025, further consolidating the competitive landscape.

Kodiak's competitive advantages are significant and improving:

- **Scale:** Largest fleet by horsepower, concentrated in large HP units (>1,000 HP) which command longer terms and higher margins
- **Utilization:** 97.7%, near theoretical maximum, leaving essentially no spare capacity
- **Supply moat:** 110-120 week engine lead times make competitive new entry effectively impossible before 2028
- **Customer quality:** Top 4 customers are investment-grade S&P 500 companies
- **Contract structure:** Fixed-revenue contracts provide cash flow visibility; vendors pushing 7-10 year renewals

The DPS acquisition extends the competitive position into distributed power generation, where the same engine supply constraints create similar barriers to entry. Kodiak can serve both oil & gas compression and data center power from the same platform, creating cross-selling opportunities.

The key competitive risk is cyclical: if natural gas activity declines meaningfully (from a commodity price collapse or regulatory change), utilization could fall below 95%, eliminating pricing power rapidly. The current structural tightness is a function of both strong demand and constrained supply; if either changes, the oligopoly pricing dynamic softens.

## Risk Assessment

### DOJ/OFAC Investigation — Mexico Cartel Payments (HIGH)

In Q1 2025, Kodiak discovered its Mexican operations (inherited from the CSI Acquisition) had made payments to local government officials associated with designated Foreign Terrorist Organizations (cartels). The company self-reported to DOJ, OFAC, and SEC, retained outside counsel, and divested the Mexican operations at a $33.3M loss. The investigation is described as "substantially complete" and aggregate payments were "not material."

Mitigating factors: voluntary self-reporting, pre-dating Kodiak's ownership, complete divestiture. OFAC violations involving voluntary self-disclosure typically result in significantly reduced penalties. But DOJ and OFAC investigations are inherently unpredictable, and this risk has received essentially zero news coverage, suggesting the market may not be fully pricing it. At 76x trailing earnings, any headline risk from a DOJ action could trigger a disproportionate selloff.

### Leverage

Debt-to-EBITDA of ~3.5x is manageable at current activity levels but leaves limited margin for error. The DPS acquisition adds ~$587M in cash funding on top of existing debt. In a scenario where EBITDA contracts 25-30% (from a natural gas activity downturn), interest coverage drops below 2.5x and the company would likely need to cut or suspend the dividend.

### Texas Sales Tax Contingency ($102M)

A $102.3M accrued liability for Texas sales and use tax matters is outstanding. While a $28M partial settlement was reached, the remaining exposure is meaningful relative to net income.

### Customer and Geographic Concentration

One customer exceeds 10% of revenue. 83% of assets are in the Permian and Eagle Ford. These are the highest-quality U.S. basins with multi-decade production horizons, but concentration is concentration.

### DPS Integration Risk

The pivot into distributed power generation is strategically sound but operationally distinct from core compression. Data center customers have different reliability requirements, commercial structures, and competitive dynamics. Integration execution risk is meaningful for a $675M acquisition.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 51.6% is significantly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 33.2%, a spread of 18.4 percentage points. The term structure is in backwardation (51.6% near-term vs. 38.1% 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a binary near-term event. Put/call OI ratio of 0.04 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting retail positioning is overwhelmingly bullish while the volatility surface prices elevated uncertainty. This divergence between directional positioning (all calls) and the implied volatility structure (rich, backwardated) warrants caution: informed participants are paying up for optionality around a near-term catalyst, likely the next earnings report or DOJ-related developments.

Short interest at 8.9% of float with 5.3 days to cover is elevated above the typical 3-5% range for mid-cap energy names. This creates some mechanical squeeze potential but also reflects informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **Price correction to $50-55** (25-30% pullback), bringing the stock to ~19-20x FY2026E EPS and below the peer median, creating a genuine margin of safety for the quality of the franchise
2. **FY2026E EPS estimates revised above $3.20** with visible DPS contribution, confirming the acquisition thesis and justifying the premium multiple
3. **DOJ/OFAC investigation formally resolved** with quantified penalties below $25M, removing the tail risk

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **Fleet utilization drops below 94%** for any reported quarter, signaling the supply-demand balance has shifted
2. **DOJ/OFAC investigation escalates** with criminal charges, penalties exceeding $100M, or required operational changes
3. **Revenue per HP declines sequentially** for two consecutive quarters, indicating pricing power is fading

### Reassessment Timeline

Next earnings report (expected late May / early June 2026 for Q1 FY2026). The DPS acquisition closed on April 1, 2026, so Q1 FY2026 results will not include DPS contribution; the first quarter with DPS included will be Q2 FY2026. Q1 results and updated guidance will be the next critical data point for assessing whether the core compression business and forward outlook justify the current price.

## Conclusion

Kodiak Gas Services operates a near-monopoly asset base at maximum utilization in a supply-constrained market, generating excellent cash flow with strong pricing power likely to persist through 2028. The DPS acquisition creates a plausible path to diversification and secular growth exposure beyond pure oil & gas. Management's capital allocation has been generally sound: the EQT AB overhang is gone, the dividend is well-covered, and the debt maturity profile is well-staggered.

The business is outstanding. The price reflects that. At $68.27, the stock trades above every published analyst target, at a meaningful premium to the peer median P/E, and at all-time highs following a roughly 100% 12-month rally. The DOJ/OFAC investigation, $2.6B in debt, recent back-to-back earnings misses, and DPS integration risk create meaningful idiosyncratic downside that the elevated multiple leaves little room to absorb. I would be a buyer on a pullback to the $50-55 range, where the risk/reward properly compensates for these risks.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The growth thesis is intact and the structural competitive position is exceptional, but the current price fully discounts the positives and offers insufficient margin of safety against known risks.
