## Executive Summary

Bath & Body Works is a high-margin, high-cash-generation specialty retailer with a shrinking top line. Net sales fell from $7,560M in FY2023 to $7,291M in FY2025, operating margin compressed 280 basis points over the same span, and the erosion accelerated into the current year: Q1 FY2026 sales fell 3.2%, adjusted operating income fell 27.8% to $151M, and adjusted EPS fell 34.7% to $0.32. Secondary reporting puts Q2 revenue guidance near $1.49B against ~$1.55B a year earlier, implying another 3-4% decline. The most direct operating metric available (BBWI does not disclose comparable-store sales) is sales per average store, down 5.7% in Q1 on 1.4% higher selling square footage.

Against that, the balance sheet is not the binding constraint and the valuation embeds a great deal. Net debt of $2,793M against roughly $1,349M of TTM adjusted EBITDA is 2.07x; the first maturity is February 2028; the $750M ABL is undrawn with $544M available and its fixed-charge covenant is springing and currently inapplicable; ratings are Ba2/BB+ stable. At $18.72 the stock is 7.1x the $2.65 FY2026 consensus and 6.5x the $2.86 FY2027 consensus, 4.9x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA, and carries a 4.27% dividend costing ~$161M a year against FY2025 free cash flow of $865M. Negative book equity of –$1,133M drives distress screens (Altman Z 1.77) that the operating and liquidity data contradict.

This is a hold rather than a buy because the two identified paths to earnings recovery are both weaker than they look. The $250M/two-year cost program ($175M in FY2026) is stated in the 10-K to fund reinvestment in product and brand, not EPS. The distribution reversal (Amazon in February 2026, more than 600 Ulta doors and Ulta.com from July 12, 2026) adds units at wholesale economics BBWI has not quantified, into a channel the company's own risk factors identify as competing with its direct business. Consensus reflects this: FY2026 and FY2027 estimates have moved less than 1% in 90 days, and the analyst distribution is 4 buy / 13 hold / 1 sell, with the sell rating new this month. Existing owners are paid a covered 4.3% yield to wait for productivity evidence; new capital has no urgency two weeks ahead of a print the options market prices at ±13.4% (one-month tenor).

## Company Overview

BBWI is a single-segment retailer of body care, home fragrance, and soaps/sanitizers operating 1,923 company stores in the U.S. and Canada (1,927 at fiscal year end, net –4 in Q1 FY2026), e-commerce in both countries, and an asset-light international model reaching more than 45 countries through 573 partner-operated stores and 34 partner e-commerce sites. FY2025 revenue mix: Stores U.S./Canada $5,582M (76.6%), Direct U.S./Canada $1,395M (19.1%), International and Other $314M (4.3%). Headcount is 60,735, roughly 94% in stores, none unionized.

Two structural features matter to the model. First, the supply chain is predominantly domestic and vertically integrated: approximately 90 vendors, largest at 12% of merchandise purchases and top five at 40%, with third-party manufacturing and distribution concentrated in central Ohio. That gives faster newness cycles than import-dependent competitors and lower tariff exposure than peers, at the cost of single-region operational concentration the 10-K names explicitly. Second, seasonality is extreme: the fourth quarter is roughly 37-40% of net sales and the bulk of profit, so any full-year view is largely a holiday bet.

The fleet is 60% off-mall against a stated 75% target. Management launched the "Consumer First Formula" in Q3 FY2025 under CEO Daniel Heaf (arrived Q2 FY2025), organized around hero-category product, brand reignition, third-party marketplace expansion, and a $250M two-year cost program.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin trajectory

|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $7,560M | $7,429M | $7,291M |
| Gross margin | 43.1% | 43.6% | 43.7% |
| Operating income | $1,376M | $1,285M | $1,126M |
| Operating margin | 18.2% | 17.3% | 15.4% |
| Net income | $800M | $878M | $649M |
| Total debt | $4,862M | $4,388M | $3,892M |
| Stockholders' equity | –$2,206M | –$1,627M | –$1,281M |

Three consecutive down years, –3.6% cumulative on sales, –18.2% on operating income. Management's own MD&A language is unambiguous: "Our 2025 performance did not meet our expectations... we also underperformed in our sector." FY2025 gross margin fell to 43.7% from 44.3% on the comparable basis, attributed primarily to tariffs; G&A and store operating expense rose 140bp to 28.3% of sales on wage and healthcare inflation plus $15M of leadership severance and $14M of transformation cost. Adjusted operating income was $1,156M versus $1,266M; adjusted EPS $3.21 versus $3.29.

### Q1 FY2026: the reported/adjusted gap

Reported operating income rose 10.4% to $231M and reported diluted EPS was $0.90 against $0.49. Both figures are artifacts. An $88M pre-tax interchange litigation settlement gain was booked as a reduction of selling expense (selling expense appears to fall from $256M to $168M; the underlying figure is flat at $256M), and a $62M tax benefit from resolution of tax matters produced a negative 10.1% effective rate. Strip those plus $8M of transformation cost, an $8M debt-extinguishment loss and a $3M asset-sale gain, and adjusted operating income was $151M (–27.8%), adjusted operating margin 11.0% versus 14.7%, and adjusted EPS $0.32 versus $0.49.

The $0.32 beat a $0.29 non-GAAP consensus and the stock rose ~17% on May 27. Both sides of that comparison are non-GAAP and the bar had been reset; the GAAP $0.90 is not comparable to the estimate. Gross margin fell 280bp to 42.6% on tariffs, inflation, crude oil, category mix, and buying-and-occupancy deleverage.

### Cash flow quality

Q1 operating cash flow was $244M versus $188M, but $88M of that was interchange settlement cash and the $62M tax benefit reduced cash taxes. Excluding the settlement, operating cash flow was approximately $156M, below the prior year's $188M. Capex was $49M versus $37M; FY2026 capex guidance rises to ~$270M from $237M. Neither quarter should be annualized: working capital builds through summer and fall and unwinds in Q4.

The supplier finance program warrants attention. Amounts due under the SCF program went $52M (May 2025) → $115M (January 2026) → $154M (May 2026). Accounts payable rose $105M year over year, roughly $102M of which the SCF ramp explains, and the FY2025 MD&A cited a $111M cash-flow benefit in payables and accrued expenses "due to our efforts to improve working capital." The cash is real and the classification is standard (BBWI provides no guarantees and states supplier terms are unchanged), but this is a one-time level shift, not a recurring source. It may keep growing as more suppliers enroll; it cannot compound indefinitely. FY2026 operating cash flow should not be modeled as though the FY2025 payables tailwind repeats.

### Capital structure

Total debt fell to $3,613M from $3,892M after the make-whole call of the remaining $284M of 6.694% 2027 Notes for $289M (an $8M pre-tax loss). Cash was $820M; net debt approximately $2,793M. The maturity ladder is $444M in 2028, $482M 2029, $844M 2030, $284M 2033, $802M 2035, $575M 2036, $201M 2037, all fixed-rate, weighted-average coupon roughly 6.7%, reported average borrowing rate 7.0%. Net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA is 2.07x; on the company's EBITDAR basis approximately 2.6x. TTM adjusted EBITDA covers roughly $274M of interest 4.9 times.

Negative equity of –$1,133M is the legacy of the Victoria's Secret separation plus cumulative buybacks (accumulated deficit $1,292M, treasury stock $822M). It is what drives the Altman Z of 1.77, where the equity/assets term is –0.253 while the operating terms are healthy (EBIT/assets 0.222, sales/assets 1.438). Solvency is not the near-term constraint. What negative equity does mean is that there is no cushion, every dollar of debt carries a 5.25%-7.6% coupon, and roughly $274M of interest plus $161M of dividends must be serviced out of a revenue base that is shrinking. Also note $210M of contingent lease-guarantee obligations retained from the spin, running to 2037, with reserves described as not significant.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth to underwrite today. Sequencing the trend: Q1 FY2025 +2.9%, Q4 FY2025 –2.5%, Q1 FY2026 –3.2%, and a Q2 guide (secondary reporting) of ~$1.49B implying –3% to –4%. Transaction counts are declining with average order size partially offsetting, a mix that generally indicates customer loss rather than trade-down. Store productivity is deteriorating faster than headline sales: –5.7% per average store and –5.8% per average square foot in Q1 on +1.4% square footage, versus –1% and –2% on +2% for FY2025. These are not true comparable-store sales and are mechanically depressed by immature new doors, but the direction has been consistent for six quarters.

Three genuine sources of incremental volume exist. International and Other grew 4.9% in FY2025 and 9.0% in Q1, the only growing line, though it now blends franchise royalties with domestic wholesale, so it should not be read as diversification away from channel concentration when Stores U.S./Canada remained 77% of sales in both periods. The Amazon U.S. launch and the Ulta Beauty partnership (with Ulta holding exclusivity on at least one revived legacy scent) put product where younger consumers already shop. BBWI supplies the product; Ulta is the reselling channel. Neither the revenue contribution nor the wholesale gross margin has been disclosed, and the company's own direct-channel risk factor names "our other alternative distribution channels competing with our direct channel business," so cannibalization is a live question rather than a hypothetical.

The cost program is the second lever and it is explicitly capped as an earnings lever. The 10-K states the $250M of savings over two years, $175M in FY2026, "will be used to invest in revenue-generating initiatives across product and brand." Mechanically, $175M fully retained at a 25% tax rate would be ~$0.65 per share; the realized EPS contribution should be assumed materially lower, and the savings are back-half weighted.

Consensus is consistent with stabilization rather than recovery: FY2026 $2.65 (+0.6% over 90 days), FY2027 $2.86 (–0.2%). The current-quarter estimate rose 20.8% over 90 days but the next quarter fell 9.1%, which is timing, not trend. There is no positive revision velocity here to lean on.

The macro backdrop is unhelpful. The 10-K Outlook language anticipates "a macroeconomic environment similar to 2025, with continued value-oriented consumer behavior" in discretionary body care and home fragrance through the FY2026 holiday season, which is where 37-40% of sales land.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.72 with 201.56M shares, market cap is $3,773M and enterprise value $6,566M ($7,666M including approximately $1.1B of operating lease liabilities).

| Metric | BBWI | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E on FY2025 adjusted EPS $3.21 | 5.8x | — |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $2.65 | 7.1x | 21.2x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $2.86 | 6.5x | — |
| EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA ($1,349M) | 4.9x | — |
| FY2025 FCF yield ($865M) | 22.9% | — |
| Dividend yield ($0.80) | 4.27% | 1.2% |

The peer set is heterogeneous and only partially comparable: ULTA at 21.2x and TSCO at 18.0x are growing retailers, CHWY at 39.3x is a different model, and VGNT at 6.0x sits where BBWI sits. The discount to the 21.2x median is not evidence of mispricing on its own; a retailer with three years of declining sales and 370bp of adjusted margin compression in its most recent quarter should trade at a discount.

What is defensible about the valuation score is absolute cash-return support rather than relative multiple arbitrage. Trailing free cash flow of $865M against a $3,773M market cap is a 22.9% yield, though roughly $111M of the FY2025 figure came from the payables/SCF level shift and should be normalized out, taking the underlying closer to $754M and 20%. Secondary reporting cites FY2026 free cash flow guidance of ~$600M, which I could not confirm against a primary release; at that level the stock is 6.3x free cash flow, a 15.9% yield, with dividend coverage of 3.7x. Even on a conservative $500M, the yield is 13% and coverage 3.1x. The dividend costs $161M and is not at risk from cash flow; the constraint on it would be a board decision to prioritize deleveraging, and management has already pivoted that way ($289M of debt redemption in Q1 versus zero program buybacks).

A sensitivity worth stating plainly: if FY2026 adjusted EPS declined at Q1's 34.7% rate, it would be about $2.10, putting the stock at 8.9x. That extrapolation is too harsh (Q1 is roughly 19% of sales and the savings are back-half weighted) but it frames how little multiple compression is needed for the "cheap" case to fail on earnings rather than on the multiple.

Unpriced optionality exists in tariffs. The Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026; the Court of International Trade ordered liquidation without IEEPA duties in March; CBP opened a refund process in April; and as of May 2, 2026 BBWI had recognized nothing, citing uncertainty on timing and amount. Because tariffs were the primary cited driver of gross-margin compression in FY2025 (–60bp) and a named driver of Q1's –280bp, refunds plus prospective relief are the cleanest path to margin recovery that does not require the turnaround to work. But the 10-K also states the government "immediately... initiated new tariffs under alternative authorities, resulting in continued tariff exposure," and BBWI discloses no dollar amount of IEEPA duties paid. This cannot be sized and should not be capitalized into a base case.

Analyst targets span $19.00 low to $54.00 high with a $25.20 mean and $23.00 median. The $54 high is stale relative to the current earnings base and should be discounted; the low of $19.00 sits marginally above the current price. In this profile (a compressed multiple on eroding earnings) analyst targets are not independent confirmation and the recommendation distribution (4/13/1, with the first sell appearing this month) is the more informative datapoint.

## Competitive Landscape

BBWI's competitive position is deteriorating at the margin, and the 10-K risk factors are more candid than the competition section. Three admissions are material.

The company states it relies "to a greater degree than some of our competitors on physical locations in retail centers. Therefore, declines in traffic to such locations may affect us more significantly than our competitors." The 60% to 75% off-mall migration is a response to a structural disadvantage, not an offensive strategy.

The Consumer First Formula is framed around attracting "new, younger consumers" and "lapsed consumers," which is a management acknowledgment that the 40-million-member loyalty file is aging or attriting. Body care and home fragrance have been among the most disrupted consumer categories of the past five years, with DTC and social-native brands plus dupe culture attacking both price points and newness cadence. Marketing rose to 3.5% of sales in FY2025 from 3.3% and to 3.9% in Q1 FY2026 from 3.5%, while the risk factor concedes "some of our competitors may expend more for their programs than we do."

What separates BBWI from a distressed specialty retailer is real: 43-44% gross margins and mid-teens operating margins in a down year, an 11.9% FY2025 free-cash-flow margin, a domestic vertically integrated supply chain that gives faster newness cycles and less import/tariff exposure than peers, an asset-light international model where partners fund capital, and the loyalty file. This is a franchise with a demand problem, not a cost or balance-sheet problem. The wholesale pivot addresses reach; it does nothing for owned-store productivity and may work against it.

## Risk Assessment

**Top-line erosion with falling productivity.** The primary risk. Productivity is falling faster than sales while capex rises and "high return real estate" remains a stated priority. Capital into new doors while existing doors deleverage is the classic specialty-retail value-trap pattern. The Q1 net closure of four stores may be the beginning of fleet rationalization; one quarter does not establish it.

**Input cost and tariff exposure with an unresolved legal overlay.** Paraffin wax for the three-wick candle franchise and plastic packaging are petroleum derivatives, so the Middle East energy channel transmits directly into cost of goods. The Q1 10-Q added new language on U.S.–Iran escalation producing "volatility in global energy markets, commodities pricing, transportation costs and foreign currency exchange rates." The IEEPA refund is upside of unknown size against replacement duties of unknown size.

**Wholesale margin dilution and cannibalization, unquantified.** No disclosure of Amazon or Ulta channel economics. Wholesale gross margin is structurally below the 43-44% BBWI earns capturing full retail margin.

**Execution and leadership continuity.** CEO change in Q2 FY2025. Separately, the May 27, 2026 8-K carries an Item 5.02 code, Eva Boratto signed the same-day 10-Q as CFO, and a June 12, 2026 Form 4 identifies "Javitch Tom" with the title "Interim CFO." The inference is a CFO departure and interim appointment, but the 8-K text was not retrievable and no corroborating coverage was located; treat this as unverified. If confirmed, a CFO vacancy nine months into a multi-year transformation is a guidance-credibility issue. A February 25, 2026 Item 5.02 filing is entirely unidentified.

**No equity cushion against high fixed coupons.** Discussed above. Q1 adjusted operating income covered interest only 2.2x, though that ratio is not meaningful standalone given the Q4 profit concentration.

**Concentration.** Largest vendor 12% of merchandise purchases, top five 40%, manufacturing and distribution concentrated in central Ohio, plus disclosed China sourcing with UFLPA detention risk and limited sub-tier visibility.

**Governance-adjacent disclosure.** The 10-K names shareholder activism as a standalone risk and acknowledges that the stock "currently is... traded by short sellers which may put pressure on the supply and demand for our common stock." Short interest is 7.8% of float at 3.1 days to cover, uncrowded, so neither a squeeze setup nor confirmation of a widely held bear thesis.

**What is already priced.** A 42% decline from the $32.32 52-week high, 7.1x forward earnings, and 4.9x EV/EBITDA embed a substantial portion of the demand problem. The November 20, 2025 gap from ~$21 to $15.82 on the Q3 FY2025 miss is where the derating happened; since then estimates have been flat and the price has round-tripped from $15 to $23.50 and back to $18.72. The fundamentals did deteriorate, and the current price reflects it.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 66.0% is rich against 59.4% one-year realized, and the term structure is in backwardation (66.0% near versus 56.7% at six months), which points to event risk rather than a change in the long-run vol regime. The relevant event is the August 26 Q2 report: the one-month tenor prices 83.1% IV and a ±13.4% move. Put/call open-interest ratio rose from 1.12 to 1.60 between July 23 and August 13 while the price fell 7.1%, and near-term put skew is reported as elevated. One unusual print stands out: 561 contracts of December $15 puts against 174 open interest (3.2x), roughly 20% below spot, which is downside cover through the holiday quarter, where 37-40% of sales and most of the profit land. The IV percentile within the current chain is only 32%, so this is deliberate positioning around a two-sided print in a name that missed in two of the last four quarters, not extreme fear. The signal is consistent with the fundamental read: do not add ahead of August 26.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $27 (+44%)

Ulta's 600-plus doors and Ulta.com, plus Amazon, add 2-3% of incremental revenue at acceptable wholesale margins and reach lapsed and younger consumers without cannibalizing owned stores; the back-half-weighted $175M cost program partially drops through as tariff relief and IEEPA refunds reverse part of the 280bp Q1 gross-margin decline; off-mall migration and net store closures stabilize productivity; FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $3.00 and the multiple re-rates to 9x. Free cash flow near $600M funds the $161M dividend 3.7x with room to resume buybacks at genuinely low prices. The six-director cluster purchase of ~66,013 shares at $14.40-$15.58 in November 2025 marks where informed board members saw value, roughly 20-30% below today.

### Bear Case — $14 (–25%)

Store productivity keeps falling at a mid-single-digit rate, wholesale channels cannibalize owned-store and direct traffic while carrying lower margins, and tariff refunds are offset by replacement duties. Cost savings are consumed by reinvestment as stated, so the 370bp of adjusted operating margin compression persists at reduced magnitude into the holiday quarter. Consumer discretionary weakens further under continued value-oriented consumer behavior. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near $2.30, and at 6.1x the stock returns to the $14.28 52-week low. The FY2025 payables/SCF working-capital benefit does not repeat, free cash flow falls toward $450-500M, and the board prioritizes deleveraging over the dividend: not a cut, but no growth and no buyback either. Negative book equity means there is nothing between debt service and equity holders if the decline deepens.

### Base Case — $20 (+7%)

Sales decline 2-4% in FY2026, gross margin compression moderates as tariff comparisons ease and savings arrive in the back half, and adjusted EPS lands near the $2.65 consensus with FY2027 around $2.86. The market continues to apply a 7x multiple to an eroding but cash-generative franchise: 7.0x $2.86 = $20.02. Total return including the 4.3% dividend is roughly 11% over twelve months, with wide dispersion.

Probability-weighting bear 30% / base 50% / bull 20% gives an expected price return of about +5% and a total return near +9%, with a left tail of –25%. That is approximately fair compensation for the uncertainty, not the asymmetry a purchase requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: reassess at the August 26, 2026 Q2 print, then at the FY2026 holiday results in early March 2027.**

- **Base case target $20.00** (+6.8% from $18.72): 7.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.86, cross-checked at 7.5x FY2026 consensus $2.65 = $19.88. Plus $0.80 of dividends, roughly 11% total return.
- **Bull case target $27.00** (+44.2%): 9.0x approximately $3.00 of FY2027 adjusted EPS, requiring wholesale accretion, tariff relief, and productivity stabilization.
- **Bear case target $14.00** (–25.2%): 6.1x approximately $2.30 of FY2026 adjusted EPS, against a $14.28 52-week low and the $14.40-$15.58 November director purchase zone.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Sales per average store decline narrows to better than –2% year over year for two consecutive quarters, or the company begins disclosing comparable-store sales at flat or better.
2. Quantified wholesale disclosure (Ulta/Amazon revenue contribution with gross margin at or above the mid-30s) plus adjusted operating margin compression narrowing to under 150bp year over year.
3. Recognition of a material IEEPA refund, or confirmed FY2026 free cash flow at or above $600M with dividend coverage above 3.5x, at a price at or below $16.
4. Open-market purchases by the CEO or a permanent CFO, which have not appeared in any Form 4 to date.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q2 or Q3 FY2026 revenue declining more than 5% year over year, or sales per average store worse than –6%.
2. Adjusted operating margin compression of 300bp or more in a second consecutive quarter, or FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance/consensus falling below $2.30.
3. Confirmation that the FY2025 payables/supplier-finance benefit reverses, taking operating cash flow below roughly $750M, alongside capex at $270M, which would compress free cash flow toward $450M and put the buyback authorization and eventually dividend growth at risk.
4. A dividend reduction, or a permanent CFO appointment accompanied by a guidance reset.

**Reassessment date:** August 26, 2026 (Q2 FY2026 results). The one-month options tenor prices a ±13.4% move; existing holders should expect that dispersion rather than trade around it.

## Conclusion

BBWI is priced as a business in terminal decline and is currently performing as a business in moderate decline with an intact cash engine. That distinction is worth roughly the current multiple: 7.1x FY2026 consensus, 4.9x EV/EBITDA, a 4.27% dividend covered at least three times over by free cash flow, 2.07x net leverage, and no maturity until February 2028. Distress screens flagging this name are reading a post-spin negative equity balance, not operating stress.

What prevents a more constructive rating is that the recovery levers are structurally capped. The $175M of FY2026 savings is earmarked for reinvestment by management's own statement. The Amazon and Ulta channels add units at undisclosed and structurally lower margins into a channel the company itself flags as competing with its direct business. Store productivity is falling at 5-6% while capex rises to $270M and the fleet grows. Q1's headline strength was an $88M interchange settlement and a $62M tax benefit; the underlying operating result was 27.8% worse than a year earlier. Consensus has flatlined rather than turned up, and the first sell rating in the coverage set appeared this month.

Own it for the covered yield and the cash flow if you already do, and let the August 26 print and the FY2026 holiday season supply the productivity evidence that would justify paying up. New capital should require either the operating evidence listed above or a price closer to the mid-$15s, where the November director purchases were executed and where the free cash flow yield compensates for the demand trajectory rather than merely reflecting it.
