## Executive Summary

Waters closed the Reverse Morris Trust acquisition of Becton Dickinson's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions ("BDS") businesses on February 9, 2026 for $16.8B including $4.0B of assumed debt, issuing 38,541,852 shares — a 39.2% ownership transfer to BD's shareholders. Revenue roughly doubled, goodwill plus intangibles went from about $1.9B to $18.1B, and tangible book equity turned negative by approximately $2.8B. Every trailing metric on this ticker now describes a predecessor company. The provided profile carries trailing P/E of 180.6 alongside EPS of $10.76, figures that cannot both be right at $395 and are in any case uninformative. All work below uses guided adjusted EPS and forward consensus.

The August 4 second-quarter report was good. Revenue of $1.65B beat a ~$1.62B consensus, legacy organic growth was 7% reported and 9% at constant currency, adjusted EPS of $3.05 edged the $3.01 estimate, and FY2026 guidance moved up to $6.42–6.48B of revenue and $14.45–14.65 of adjusted EPS. Against Bio-Rad reporting flat revenue and Qiagen flat net sales the same week, Waters is growing while its peer set is not, consistent with the five-year proxy data showing Waters' TSR index at 153.52 versus 69.03 for its own disclosed peer group. That differentiation explains the 42% twelve-month price gain.

The problem is what the price now assumes. At $395.045 the stock trades at 27.2x the $14.55 guidance midpoint and 24.0x the $16.49 FY2027 consensus, against a peer median P/E of 26.8. Roughly $0.44 of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS is a mechanical artifact of BDS shares entering the weighted count only on February 9; on a full 98.4M-share basis the figure is closer to $14.11, or 28.0x. Standalone legacy Waters — adjusted organic net income of $783.9M in FY2025 on ~59.7M diluted shares, or about $13.13, growing high single digits — would plausibly have produced $14.20–14.40 of 2026 adjusted EPS on its own. The bases are not perfectly comparable, but the implication is that a $16.8B transaction is approximately EPS-neutral in year one on a normalized share count. Accretion depends entirely on the $200M cost and $290M revenue synergy programs, delivered while the buyback lever is contractually unavailable until roughly February 2028 and GAAP amortization rises from $956M in 2026 to $1,099M in 2027. HOLD, with a base case of $425 (+7.6%).

## Company Overview

The legacy franchise is a razor/razor-blade analytical instrument business with unusual regulatory entrenchment. Waters sells HPLC/UPLC and mass spectrometry systems, packs and distributes its own silica and polymeric chromatography columns, and layers Empower — its chromatography data system — on top. In regulated pharmaceutical QC, analytical methods are validated against a specific instrument/column/software combination; switching vendors triggers revalidation. That, rather than hardware performance, is the source of switching cost. Service was over 35% of Waters segment sales in 2025 and over 25% of TA Instruments sales, delivered by roughly 4,300 field representatives across 82 offices.

Customer diversification is real and disclosed rather than inferred: no single customer exceeded 2% of net sales in 2025, 2024 or 2023. FY2025 mix was 59% pharmaceutical, 30% industrial, 11% academic/government, with about 69% of sales outside the United States. R&D was $196M on ~7,900 employees.

The acquired BDS businesses supply diagnostic specimen collection and transport products, infectious-disease and cancer detection instruments and reagents, flow cytometry, and multiomics tools. Mechanically, BD transferred the assets to a SpinCo, distributed SpinCo shares to BD's own shareholders, and a Waters merger sub absorbed SpinCo, so BD received about $4.0B of cash pre-closing while BD's shareholders received Waters stock. Waters assumed the $4.0B of SpinCo debt by operation of the merger. The company now reports four divisions (Analytical Sciences, Biosciences, Advanced Diagnostics, Materials Sciences), with Analytical and Materials combined into one reportable segment and legacy Waters Clinical reclassified into Advanced Diagnostics, meaning Advanced Diagnostics is not a clean BDS proxy.

## Financial Analysis

**Legacy earnings were stagnant before the deal.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance table discloses GAAP net income of $692.8M (2021), $707.8M (2022), $642.2M (2023), $637.8M (2024) and $642.6M (2025) — down 9% from the 2022 peak while revenue grew. The TSR index moved from 150.59 at year-end 2021 to 153.52 at year-end 2025: four years of essentially flat absolute return. The apparent five-year outperformance was earned in 2021. Relative performance was nonetheless strong because the disclosed peer index fell from 126.55 to 69.03 over the same window. Two readings are available and both may be partly true: Waters' consumables-and-service weighting insulated it from the post-COVID biotech funding bust, and its pharma-QC concentration is inherently late-cycle and defensive, so the relative result partly reflects end-market mix rather than execution.

FY2025 reported operating margin fell 250bps to 25.4% on 7% revenue growth, with SG&A rising about $140M. The BDS deal was signed July 13, 2025, and Q1 2026 alone carried $83M of transaction, integration and financing costs, so a meaningful portion of that SG&A increase is plausibly deal-related; the underlying margin was probably roughly stable. The magnitude of that decomposition is an estimate, not a disclosed figure.

**Post-deal balance sheet.** At April 4, 2026: total assets $24,531M, equity $15,292M (almost entirely additional paid-in capital of $15,273M), debt $5,215M, goodwill $9,317M and net intangibles $8,776M. Against cash of $539M at quarter-end June, net debt is approximately $4.68B — a calculation that mixes an April debt balance with a June cash balance and should be read as approximate. Against an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2.10–2.15B (built up from guidance: adjusted net income ~$1,388M, grossed to ~$1,714M pretax at ~19%, plus ~$200M net interest for ~$1,914M adjusted EBIT, plus $210–240M of depreciation and capitalized-software amortization), leverage is about **2.2x** against a ≤3.50x credit-agreement covenant (electable to 4.25x after a material acquisition) and a ≥3.50x interest-coverage test. Compliance was certified at April 4.

Any Altman Z-score on this entity should be discarded. Recalculating on the post-deal balance sheet produces roughly 3.97 versus 2.68 pre-deal — an apparent improvement driven almost entirely by the market-equity/total-liabilities term (contributing ~2.51 of the 3.97) while every operating input deteriorated: EBIT/assets roughly halved to ~0.078 and revenue/assets fell 58% to ~0.263. Substituting a near-zero retained-earnings term gives roughly 3.10. Leverage and covenant headroom are the correct solvency lens here.

**Refinancing execution was the year's best management decision.** The assumed $4.0B included a $3.5B 364-day tranche due February 2027. Waters refinanced it on March 23, 2026 into five tranches maturing 2027 through 2036 at 4.321%–5.245%, leaving $500M due February 5, 2028 and $1.6B available on a $1.8B revolver maturing 2030. Weighted-average rate on notes rose to 4.43% from 3.35% — roughly 100bps of permanent cost in exchange for eliminating the acute refinancing risk within six weeks of closing. Terming out the bridge in March rather than waiting removed the single largest near-term financial risk at a modest blended-rate cost.

**Cash flow requires patience.** Q1 operating cash flow of $(3)M has no forecasting value: it absorbed $88M of transaction and integration payments, $140M of timing on the net BD receivable, and a $(533)M accounts-receivable change including acquired balances. Legacy FY2025 free cash flow was $539.8M at a 17.1% margin. A normalized combined estimate — adjusted EBITDA ~$2.13B less ~$200M cash interest, ~$325M cash taxes, and ~$275M capex — points to roughly $1.3B of pre-restructuring free cash flow, or about 3.4% on market cap, with 2026 actual materially lower after restructuring and integration outflows. This is a derived sensitivity, not a company figure.

**Working capital deserves monitoring.** Legacy DSO was 103 days at April 4, 2026 versus 95 days a year earlier, excluding the $415M BD receivable. Plausible benign explanations exist — a six-day-longer fiscal quarter pushing revenue into the final weeks, mix shift toward Europe (+26%), ERP transition friction — but an eight-day deterioration alongside a large integration is the kind of item that precedes revenue-recognition questions if it persists.

**Revenue quality improved.** Q1 consumables of $543M plus service of $348M was 70.3% of revenue. BDS contributed $424M of that on $520M of total BDS revenue (81.5% recurring), implying legacy recurring of roughly 62.5%. On one stub quarter, the acquired business is more recurring-weighted than legacy Waters — reagents and cleared-assay consumables are arguably stickier than chromatography columns. One 55-day period cannot establish a mix, but the direction is favorable.

## Growth Analysis

Three separate growth rates matter and they point in different directions.

Legacy Waters is growing 7–9% organic constant currency, and Q1's reported +13% legacy growth was flattered by six extra calendar days — service revenue recognized ratably captures the full ~6.7% day benefit, so underlying legacy recurring growth was plausibly nearer +10–11% than the reported +17%. That adjustment is derived from the 10-Q's own day-count disclosure, not from company commentary.

BDS grew 4% in Q2 ($817M versus $783M). The Q1 10-Q's pro forma disclosure, treating BDS as owned since January 1, 2025, showed combined revenue of $1,540M versus $1,454M — about 5.9%. The combined company is structurally a mid-single-digit revenue grower.

The bridge from 5.9% revenue growth to the 13.6% FY2026-to-FY2027 consensus EPS growth ($14.51 to $16.49) runs through three levers: cost synergies, deleveraging, and full-year ownership. The revenue synergy target of $290M over five years equals roughly 4.5% of 2026 revenue, and none of it is yet visible. Q2 restructuring of $49M versus $3M a year earlier indicates the cost program is running; annualized, that charge rate approximates the entire three-year cost-synergy target being spent in year one, which is normal for a deal this size but means cash outflows lead savings.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than supportive: current year +0.9% and next year +0.9% over 90 days, but the current quarter at -1.1% and next quarter at -3.1%. This is nowhere near the velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection, and correctly so — Waters is a mid-cycle integration story, not an inflection story. Earnings delivery has been reliable: three beats and one narrow miss in four quarters, with surprises of +0.4%, +14.4%, -0.5% and +5.1%.

**The Q4 concentration is the dominant near-term estimate risk.** H1 adjusted EPS of $5.75 is 39.5% of the $14.55 midpoint. With Q3 guided to a $4.00 midpoint, Q4 is implied at approximately **$4.80** — about 1.6x Q2 and 1.8x Q1. Supporting factors are documented: the 10-K states Q4 benefits from customers exhausting capital budgets, BDS diagnostics carries respiratory-season seasonality, and synergies build through the year. But concentrating roughly a third of the year's earnings into the fourth quarter of the first year of a $16.8B integration is the year's clearest asymmetry against the shareholder.

The peer set trades at 22.4x (IQVIA) to 30.1x (Illumina) with a 26.8x median, and all five peers sit 5–17% below their 52-week highs while Waters sits 4.6% below its own. Waters is therefore priced at a modest premium to the median on FY2026 and a modest discount on FY2027, for a business growing revenue mid-single-digits with a large integration in progress and roughly 74% of total assets in goodwill and intangibles.

Two adjustments make the multiple worse than it appears. First, the share-count artifact: BDS shares entered the weighted diluted count on February 9, so Q1 used 82,139K shares against 98,166K outstanding. Roughly $0.44 of guided adjusted EPS (about 3%) will not recur, and any 2027 model built by growing $14.55 forward starts from an inflated base. Second, the GAAP gap widens before it narrows. Disclosed BDS intangible amortization is $956M in 2026, **$1,099M in 2027**, then $1,008M, $874M and $869M — 2027 exceeds 2026 because 2026 is a partial year. At a 21% rate, 2027 amortization alone is roughly $8.82 per share after tax. H1 2026 GAAP net loss is already $(208)M. Convergence toward adjusted results does not begin meaningfully until 2029–2030. This is non-cash and does not impair covenant compliance or cash generation, so EV/EBITDA and cash-flow analysis remain valid; it does invalidate GAAP P/E, ROE and book-value screens, and it removes any prospect of the stock being re-rated on reported earnings for several years.

One argument genuinely favors the deal's arithmetic. Waters paid roughly 4.9–5.1x revenue for BDS (from $16.8B total consideration against a $3.3–3.4B revenue run rate) using equity that was trading at a rich multiple pre-close. The combined entity trades at 27x versus the legacy standalone's richer pre-deal multiple. Waters converted expensive paper into cheaper assets, which is value-accretive on a multiple basis even while dilutive to growth rate and segment margin quality.

Against that: the year-one EPS math is approximately neutral. Legacy FY2025 adjusted organic net income of $783.9M on ~59.7M diluted shares is about $13.13; growing high single digits gives $14.20–14.40 standalone for 2026, versus $14.11 combined on a normalized share count. The bases differ (adjusted organic net income is not identical to the adjusted-EPS guidance basis), so this is directional rather than exact. But it frames the question correctly: shareholders gave up 39.2% of the company and took on $4.68B of net debt for a business growing 4%, and the return on that trade arrives only through synergies and deleveraging.

The absence of capital returns compounds the problem. The February 9, 2026 Tax Matters Agreement restricts stock issuance, repurchase and business combinations for two years post-distribution to preserve tax-free RMT treatment. Consistent with that, $1.0B of authorization remains through January 21, 2028 with **no open-market repurchases in 2025 or 2026** — only $12M of RSU-vest settlement. No dividend has ever been paid. A zero-yield equity with no buyback and 24x forward earnings has no valuation floor from capital return.

Score: **4.8**. Fair-to-full, with roughly 5–8% of identifiable upside against a 20%+ de-rating risk if the Q4 ramp disappoints. Analyst targets do not function as a floor here — the stock is at a premium multiple for its growth rate, so consensus targets and the price reflect the same set of synergy assumptions rather than providing independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competition, per the 10-K: Agilent, Shimadzu, Bruker, Danaher and Thermo Fisher in LC/LC-MS; Danaher, Merck KGaA, Agilent, General Electric and Thermo Fisher in consumables; Revvity, Agilent and Thermo Fisher in service; and for BDS, **Danaher, Revvity, Cytek Biosciences and bioMérieux**. The filing is candid that competitors "have instrument businesses that are generally more diversified" and that some "have greater financial resources and broader distribution."

The legacy moat is intact and arguably widening on a relative basis: Waters is one of few suppliers that manufactures its own silica and polymeric resins, packs its own columns, and controls the Empower compliance layer, while the peer set posted flat quarters. Q1 segment margins were 34.4% for Analytical & Materials Sciences and 35.8% for Biosciences, contradicting the widespread assumption that BD's businesses were structurally lower-margin.

Two things pull the trajectory score down. Advanced Diagnostics posted a 20.1% Q1 operating margin on $349M of revenue, dragging total segment operating margin to 30.7% from 35.5% a year earlier — roughly five points of structural consolidated dilution, with $3,279M of goodwill allocated to that segment. And the naming of Cytek Biosciences acknowledges a full-spectrum flow-cytometry entrant attacking precisely the high-margin instrument-plus-reagent economics that make Biosciences attractive. The 10-K also notes new microbiology products take 3–5 years to generate significant sales and require FDA clearance, while implied BDS R&D intensity of 8%+ exceeds legacy Waters' ~6.2%: higher spend for slower growth.

Score: **6.5**. The core franchise is strengthening relative to a weak peer group; the acquired portfolio dilutes growth rate, margin and competitive clarity.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and synergy execution.** $200M of cost synergies over three years and $290M of revenue synergies over five, against a $16.8B purchase and Q2 restructuring of $49M. The FY2025 incentive framework used organic metrics only and does not appear to directly incentivize synergy capture; whether the 2026 plan added integration measures is unverified.

**Deferred Close Businesses (China and Italy).** Legal transfer could not be completed at closing. Waters recorded $129M as a prepaid deposit and operates these businesses under an Interim Operating Agreement with BD, acting as principal and recognizing revenue gross. It therefore bears the economics of businesses it does not legally own in two jurisdictions, one of which is the same market where the 10-K separately flags government procurement restrictions and volume-based procurement. No expected transfer date is disclosed.

**Counterparty entanglement with the seller.** A $140M net receivable from BD ($415M gross in AR, $275M in AP) is disclosed as a credit concentration exceeding 10% of accounts receivable, and Waters depends on BD for transition services for up to three years at roughly $90M annually — while simultaneously running a ~$130M ERP replacement ($72M spent through Q1). Back-office continuity is contractually dependent on the counterparty being integrated. Carve-out deferred closes and interim operating agreements are routine; the compounding of ERP replacement, TSA dependency and a four-segment reorganization in the same twelve months is not.

**Impairment sensitivity.** $18.1B of goodwill plus intangibles against $15.3B of equity and approximately $(2.8)B of tangible equity. This is a structural consequence of a stock-funded deal at a premium to net tangible assets rather than distress, but it means the equity is highly sensitive to any write-down, and Advanced Diagnostics at 20.1% margin with $3,279M of allocated goodwill is the plausible candidate if diagnostics growth disappoints.

**Singapore tax incentive.** A 5% concessionary rate ran from April 1, 2021 to **March 31, 2026**, with clawback to the 17% statutory rate retroactive to April 2021 if milestones were not met. Singapore manages all Asian outsourced manufacturing and distribution. Neither renewal nor milestone compliance is disclosed in the filings reviewed. Renewal is the more likely outcome for a committed manufacturer, but this is an unquantified, undiscussed exposure to the consolidated effective tax rate with a five-year retroactive tail.

**Offsetting positive tail:** the 10-K discloses that on February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated tariffs imposed under IEEPA and that Waters may be eligible for refunds of tariffs previously paid. Nothing was recognized as of April 4, 2026 and no amount is disclosed.

**Verification gap.** The Q2 10-Q was not on file at analysis date and the August 4 8-K exhibit text was not retrievable. All Q2 figures and the revised guidance come from secondary reporting (360Dx, corroborated on revenue and Q3 guidance by StockStory). The guidance build reconciles internally ($3.37B + $3.05B = $6.42B; $3.43B + $3.05B = $6.48B), and the market's ~3% positive reaction is consistent with the reported content, but the numbers are unconfirmed. One inconsistency remains unresolved: Q2's 7% reported versus 9% constant-currency organic growth implies an FX headwind, opposite in sign to Q1's disclosed +2-point FX tailwind.

Score: **4.8**. Multiple specific, quantified, dated risks with asymmetric downside, several of them not obviously priced into a 27x multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 35.2% against 36.6% one-year realized is fair — the market is not pricing a dislocation. Put/call open interest at 0.38 is call-tilted, and the term structure is flat (35.2% near versus 33.2% at six months), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a near-term event. The one dissonant reading is a 4.6% put skew on the 14-day tenor alongside -2.5% at six months, consistent with modest post-earnings downside hedging rather than a directional bet. Short interest of 3.7% of float and 4.0 days to cover indicate no crowded bearish positioning. The options market corroborates the fundamental read: a fairly priced, moderately volatile stock with no imminent catalyst priced in, which is itself the concern, since the Q4 ramp is a real risk the market has not marked.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $460 (+16.4%)

The Q2 print established that legacy Waters is growing 9% at constant currency while Bio-Rad and Qiagen report flat quarters, and management explicitly cited "previously lagging customer segments now returning to growth." BDS proves more recurring-weighted (81.5%) than legacy Waters (62.5%), improving the durability of the combined base. Biosciences at a 35.8% segment margin shows the acquired portfolio is better than feared. The $200M cost program lands ahead of schedule in Advanced Diagnostics, pulling total segment margin back toward 33–34%, and the first revenue synergies appear in 2027. Deleveraging from 2.2x transfers enterprise value to equity holders while reducing a ~$200M interest burden. Waters bought slower assets at ~5x revenue with richer paper; the arithmetic compounds as synergies land. FY2027 delivers $17.00+ against consensus of $16.49, and a 27x multiple gets to $460 — matching the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case — $310 (-21.5%)

Q4 misses the implied $4.80. Capital-goods budget flush is weaker than seasonal precedent; the six-day-longer Q1 and an eight-day DSO deterioration were early tells that revenue timing was being pulled forward. BDS growth stays at 4% or decelerates as Cytek and Danaher compete in flow cytometry, revenue synergies slip past 2028, and the Advanced Diagnostics goodwill of $3,279M comes under review. The Singapore incentive is not renewed, adding 100–200bps to the effective tax rate with a retroactive tail. FY2027 consensus falls to $15.00 and the multiple compresses to 20x on a business that is a 5% grower with no buyback, no dividend, negative tangible equity and rising GAAP amortization. $300–315.

1. Q4 2026 adjusted EPS below $4.30, or FY2026 adjusted EPS coming in below $14.00 — either would confirm the back-half slope was aspirational.

3. Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge against Advanced Diagnostics, or leverage exceeding 3.0x net debt/adjusted EBITDA.
4. Legacy organic constant-currency growth falling below 5% for two consecutive quarters, or DSO exceeding 110 days.

**Next reassessment point:** filing of the Q2 2026 10-Q, which should confirm the reported figures, resolve the FX-direction inconsistency, disclose the Q2 fiscal period end and day count, and update the Deferred Close and Singapore disclosures. ## Conclusion

The trajectory on this name has been downward — 6.8 in February to 6.0 in July, and 5.7 now — despite good operating news. The Q2 beat-and-raise confirmed the thesis rather than undermining it; legacy growth of 9% constant currency against flat peer quarters is the strongest evidence yet that the franchise is differentiated. The decline in the score reflects valuation and risk inventory, not fundamental deterioration. In February the market was pricing a 39% dilution and $4.68B of net debt at $328; today it is paying $395 for the same integration with the same synergies still unproven, the buyback lever still contractually locked, GAAP amortization set to rise in 2027, and a Q4 that must run roughly 60% above Q2 to hit guidance.

At 27.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS (28.0x normalized) and 24.0x FY2027 consensus, against a 26.8x peer median and 4.6% upside to the consensus mean, Waters offers roughly 8% to a defensible base case against 20%+ downside if the back-half ramp disappoints. The refinancing execution in March was genuinely skilled, two directors bought in the open market at $289–306 with no offsetting open-market sales, and the incentive plan's organic-only metrics avoid rewarding scale for its own sake — this is a competently run company, but not a mispriced one. Own the position through the integration; the case for adding requires either evidence that synergies are landing in Advanced Diagnostics or a price nearer $340.
