**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

WEC Energy Group is the largest Wisconsin-based regulated utility holding company, serving roughly 1.7 million electric and 3.1 million gas customers across four states. The investment case rests on a rate-base-growth model that has been sharpened by a specific, externally corroborated data-center load thesis: Microsoft's announced $20B+ of southeastern Wisconsin data-center investment and Vantage's Port Washington campus underpin management's expectation of up to 2.6 GW of incremental load in the Milwaukee-to-Chicago corridor through 2030. The most important development of the past quarter is regulatory rather than commercial: the PSCW's verbal approval (April 24, 2026) of the Very Large Customer (VLC) and Bespoke Resources tariffs, which lock in premium 10.48-10.98% ROEs on a fixed 57% equity ratio, require the data-center customers to pay even if they terminate, and exclude that revenue from future rate cases and earnings-sharing. This is an unusually shareholder-favorable cost-allocation structure that shifts stranded-asset risk onto the customer rather than other ratepayers or WEC shareholders.

The financial reality underneath this growth story is more demanding. The $37.5B 2026-2030 capital plan ($33.4B regulated + $4.1B ATC share) is funded by debt and a $3.0B at-the-market equity program, with diluted share count already up ~3% YoY (319.3M → 328.3M) and forward shares contracted at ~$114.85. Free cash flow is structurally negative: annual capex ramps from ~$5.0B in 2026 to ~$7.0B in 2027-2028, well above operating cash flow. Debt-to-total-cap stands at 60.8%, normal for the model but a direct headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment where the long end is under pressure from AI-debt supply, equity-IPO issuance, and reserve diversification. The 6.5-7% EPS growth target is net of dilution; the underlying rate-base growth is higher, but per-share economics are what shareholders own.

The stock at $112.54 trades at 22.4x trailing earnings against a peer median of 17.4x and yields 3.32% ($3.81 annualized). Q1 2026 was strong (EPS $2.45 vs $2.27, +8%; revenue +9%), but near-term estimate revisions have turned negative (current quarter -8.8% over 30 days, next quarter -10.3%), and full-year guidance of $5.51-$5.61 is unchanged. The recurring score decline (6.9 → 6.5) reflects a genuine tension: the growth thesis is improving and de-risking while the financing model and rate sensitivity are deteriorating relative to the macro backdrop. We rate WEC HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a durable mid-single-digit compounder with a defensible dividend, but no asymmetric entry at current levels.

## Company Overview

WEC operates through six segments. The Wisconsin segment (We Energies, WPS, Wisconsin Gas, UMERC) contributes ~$408M of Q1'26 net income (51% of total) and houses virtually all of the growth thesis. Illinois gas (Peoples Gas, North Shore Gas) added ~$189M; Other States gas (Minnesota MERC, Michigan MGU) ~$38M; the ~60% equity stake in American Transmission Co. ~$42M; and Non-Utility Energy Infrastructure (We Power, Bluewater storage, WECI renewables) ~$121M.

The business model is a regulated monopoly that earns an authorized return on capital deployed into rate base: ~9.9% ROE requested in Wisconsin, 10.10% in Illinois. Earnings grow as approved capital converts to rate base. WEC's specific edge over peers chasing the same AI-electricity tailwind is twofold: concrete, named data-center commitments in its service territory, and a regulator (the PSCW) that has now approved a tariff framework protecting the financial integrity of the buildout. WEC is the power provider to Microsoft and Vantage, not a co-investor; it builds and owns the generation and distribution, which keeps the relationship within the regulated-return model rather than exposing it to merchant data-center economics.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results were solid across the board.** Revenue of $3,434M rose 9% from $3,150M, driven primarily by Wisconsin rate increases effective January 1, 2026. Net income to common of $804.4M (+11%) and diluted EPS of $2.45 (+8%) beat the prior year and the $2.30 consensus by 6.7%. Operating cash flow of $1,218M grew 5%. Q1 is seasonally heavy due to winter gas heating, so $2.45 against full-year guidance is on track rather than indicative of upside.

**The effective tax rate of ~6.2% is a structural earnings tailwind and a structural dependency.** Renewable production tax credits (-15.1%), AFUDC-equity, and excess deferred-tax amortization drive the rate far below the 21% statutory level. WEC monetizes most PTCs/ITCs by selling them to third parties under IRA transferability. This is real cash benefit, but it ties a meaningful slice of earnings power to the continuation of clean-energy credits that the July 2025 OBBBA has begun phasing out (construction must begin by July 4, 2026 or be in service by 12/31/2027, plus new prohibited-foreign-entity material-assistance tests). Management states the 2026-2030 plan already reflects OBBBA; this warrants monitoring rather than alarm.

**Leverage is high but model-appropriate.** Total debt of $21.9B puts debt-to-total-cap at 60.8% (58.9% on a rating-agency-adjusted basis crediting junior notes as partial equity). Current liabilities exceed current assets by $1.4B, funded by $2.0B of commercial paper at ~4.07% backstopped by $3.9B of revolving credit (~$1.86B available). The standardized Altman Z-Score flag (~0.8) is a screening artifact: the formula penalizes the high leverage and low asset turnover inherent to rate-regulated infrastructure, and WEC's capital structure is regulator-approved and covenant-compliant. The relevant credit watch items are ratings, the dilution pace, and authorized ROE, not bankruptcy-risk models built for industrials.

**Free cash flow is structurally negative and will remain so through the plan.** Capex of ~$5.0B (2026) ramping to ~$7.0B (2027-2028) far exceeds operating cash flow, so the plan is funded by ongoing debt issuance (~$1B+ in Q1'26 alone) plus the $3.0B ATM equity program entered October 2025, with ~3.7M forward shares already contracted at ~$114.85. Diluted share count rose from 319.3M to 328.3M YoY. The dividend was raised 6.7% to $3.81 annualized in January 2026, consistent with the ~65-70% utility payout norm and a multi-decade growth record.

The earnings-surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters) is reassuring on near-term delivery, though the Q4'25 miss (-31.3%, $0.97 vs $1.41 estimate) reflected the $205M pre-tax Illinois settlement charge, not operational deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

WEC's growth thesis is the cleanest data-center-load story among regulated utilities, and the strongest single argument for the name. The mechanism is direct: announced large-load customers → new generation and distribution built into rate base → authorized returns on that rate base → EPS growth. The $37.5B 2026-2030 capital plan supports a 6.5-7% long-term EPS growth target, comprising ~$12.6B of regulated Wisconsin renewables (3,850 MW solar, 2,130 MW battery, 555 MW wind), ~$5.4B of natural gas generation (3,300 MW CTs + 180 MW RICE), and ~$11.8B of gas/electric distribution reliability.

Three points strengthen the durability of this thesis beyond a single-company story. First, the MISO long-term load forecast was revised sharply higher on data-center and manufacturing demand, providing independent grid-operator corroboration of the load growth. Second, the named commitments are large and specific: Vantage's 1.3 GW (potential 3.5 GW) campus and Microsoft's investment translate to the corridor load growth cited above. Third, the PSCW VLC tariff approval converts demand into recoverable, premium-return rate base with take-or-pay protection, which separates a credible plan from a press release. Management has indicated another data-center announcement could come by fall 2026.

The growth rate is meaningfully constrained on a per-share basis. EPS growth of 6.5-7% is net of the ~3% annual dilution from ATM funding; underlying rate-base growth is materially higher, but shareholders own the per-share figure. Near-term estimate momentum has turned negative, though full-year (-0.0%) and next-year (+0.1%) estimates are stable. The near-term cuts appear to be quarterly-phasing adjustments rather than a thesis change, but they do not support an acceleration narrative, and there is no estimate-revision-driven BUY signal here. Growth scores 7.0: high-quality, durable, externally corroborated, but capped by dilution and the absence of acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

WEC trades at 22.4x trailing earnings versus a peer median of 17.4x across PEG (17.4), EIX (7.9), FE (25.4), EXC (16.6), and PPL (21.7). On forward 2026 EPS guidance, the multiple is ~20.2x; on the $5.59 current-year consensus, ~20.1x. The dividend yield of ~3.32% sits below the 3.7% peer median.

The premium is largely earned. WEC carries the lowest regulatory risk in the group (FE and EIX trade cheaper for specific reasons: FirstEnergy's regulatory overhangs, Edison's wildfire liability), the most credible large-load growth catalyst, and an above-typical authorized return on its data-center rate base. A ~5-turn premium to the peer median for confirmed superior load growth plus a constructive regulator is defensible. The growth-adjusted picture is roughly fair: a ~20x forward multiple on 6.5-7% EPS growth plus a ~3.3% yield implies a total return in the high-single digits, which is what the price delivers.

What the price does not offer is asymmetry. The negative-FCF/dilution model caps per-share compounding, the balance sheet is exposed to a higher-for-longer rate path that compresses utility multiples (the long end is under pressure from AI-debt supply, gold-over-Treasuries reserve shifts, and carry-unwind risk), and near-term estimates are drifting lower rather than higher. Analyst targets (low $108, mean $124.19, median $124.50, high $140) bracket the current price with the mean implying ~10% upside, but for a slow-compounding utility with falling near-term estimates, the consensus target is a reasonable central estimate rather than a stale floor to be revised upward. A low multiple absent a specific re-rating catalyst is not a reason to expect outperformance. Valuation scores 5.5: fairly valued for the quality, no clear mispricing in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, WEC faces no product competition; its competitive dynamics are regulatory constructiveness and the ability to attract and serve large load. On both, the trajectory is improving. The PSCW is among the more constructive regulators in the country, and the VLC/Bespoke tariff approval is concrete evidence of that constructiveness translating into shareholder-favorable structure. Wisconsin's emergence as a data-center hub gives WEC first-mover regulated positioning in the Milwaukee-to-Chicago corridor, and the named Microsoft and Vantage commitments are ahead of most peers in specificity.

The structural soft spot is Illinois. Peoples Gas has faced repeated ICC disallowances ($236M in 2023, plus QIP/UEA disputes), a court-affirmed disallowance now on appeal to the Illinois Supreme Court, a mandated pipe-replacement program ramping to ~$500M/year by 2028 under a court-imposed 2035 deadline with civil-penalty exposure, and the recent $205M settlement charge ($130M PP&E impairment + $75M revenue reduction) that resolves the UEA/QIP disputes but at real cost. Illinois is a lower-ROE (9.38% legacy), higher-friction jurisdiction that drags on an otherwise high-quality portfolio. The February 2026 settlement caps a chronic problem but confirms it as a recurring rather than one-time drag. Net, competitive position scores 6.8: improving in the segment that matters most (Wisconsin/data centers), with a persistent Illinois offset.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center / Very Large Customer execution risk** is the single most important swing factor. The capital plan and growth rate increasingly depend on these customers materializing, building out, and remaining solvent. The VLC tariff materially de-risks this by requiring payment even on termination or downsizing (unless assets can be repurposed). But the risk is not eliminated: project cancellation, demand below expectations, or counterparty credit stress at a hyperscaler-adjacent developer would slow rate-base growth. This risk also intersects with the macro question of whether AI-capex demand decelerates; the infrastructure layer (power, transmission) has so far shown more durable demand than the chip layer.

**Rate / refinancing risk** is elevated in a higher-for-longer environment. With 60.8% debt-to-cap, $2.0B of commercial paper at ~4.07%, and ~$1B+ of debt issued quarterly, rising rates raise the cost of the entire funding model and compress the multiple investors pay for the dividend. A downgrade below investment grade would trigger ~$109M of collateral postings on a PPA: a low-probability but quantified tail.

**Dilution** is the quiet, ongoing cost of growth. The $3.0B ATM program funds the negative-FCF gap; ~3% annual share growth is a permanent drag on per-share economics that is already embedded in the EPS target.

**Tax-credit dependence** ties the ~6% effective rate and a portion of renewable economics to PTCs that OBBBA is phasing out. Management says it is in the plan; the multi-year phase-out and foreign-entity restrictions deserve monitoring.

**Solar supply-chain / tariff risk** on the ~$12.6B renewable program: AD/CVD duties on Southeast Asian (and now Laos/Indonesia/India) panels plus UFLPA enforcement raise costs and threaten timelines, with final AD/CVD rates due fall 2026.

**Illinois regulatory friction** remains the structural overhang described above, with the Illinois Supreme Court appeal and the 2026 PGL rate case (+20.95% requested) as live items.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($140-145).** The data-center thesis compounds. WEC executes the $37.5B plan on schedule, lands another large-load customer by fall 2026, and the premium-ROE VLC rate base drives EPS toward the high end of the 6.5-7% range or above. The Wisconsin 2027/2028 rate cases (decision Q4 2026) come in constructively at the requested 9.9% ROE, Illinois friction stabilizes after the settlement, and a falling-rate environment re-rates the utility multiple. At 23-24x ~$6.00+ FY27 EPS with the dividend, ~$140-145 is achievable over 18-24 months. The bull case requires both fundamental delivery and a more favorable rate backdrop.

**Bear Case ($100-108).** A higher-for-longer rate path compresses the utility multiple toward the peer median (~17-18x), and the negative-FCF/dilution model is penalized as financing costs rise. Data-center load disappoints or a hyperscaler-adjacent counterparty stresses, slowing rate-base conversion. Illinois delivers another adverse ICC outcome or the Supreme Court appeal goes against PGL. At ~17.5x on ~$5.80 EPS, the stock falls to ~$100-108, roughly the analyst low and -4% to -11% from current. The ~3.3% yield provides a partial floor.

**Base Case ($120-124).** WEC delivers EPS at the midpoint of guidance and grows ~6.5-7%, the VLC rate base contributes as designed, the Wisconsin rate cases clear constructively, and the multiple holds near 20-21x forward. ~$120-124 over 12 months, in line with the analyst mean/median, plus the ~3.3% dividend for a high-single-digit total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is a defensible mid-single-digit total-return compounder with a credible, de-risked growth catalyst, but the entry offers no asymmetry and the financing model is exposed to the prevailing rate backdrop.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back toward the low-to-mid $100s (sub-$105), widening the dividend yield toward 3.7%+ and the forward multiple toward the peer median while fundamentals stay intact, improving risk/reward on unchanged growth.
- A second named large-load (data-center) commitment is announced and contracted under the VLC tariff, materially raising the rate-base trajectory above the current $37.5B plan.
- A confirmed turn lower in the rate path (Fed cuts, long-end relief) that re-rates the utility cohort, combined with constructive Q4 2026 Wisconsin rate-case outcomes.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A hawkish rate surprise pushing the 2Y/long end materially higher, combined with a credit-rating action or ATM issuance at depressed prices that accelerates dilution.
- Cancellation, downsizing, or counterparty credit stress on the Microsoft/Vantage load that the VLC tariff cannot fully offset, slowing rate-base growth below 5%.
- An adverse Illinois Supreme Court ruling or ICC 2026 rate-case denial that materially exceeds the $205M settlement already absorbed.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (against the $0.76-$0.82 guide), the fall 2026 final AD/CVD solar rates, the potential fall 2026 data-center announcement, and the Q4 2026 Wisconsin rate-case decision.

## Conclusion

WEC offers the highest-quality, most-de-risked data-center load thesis in the regulated-utility group, anchored by named Microsoft/Vantage commitments and a PSCW-approved VLC tariff that locks in premium ROEs with take-or-pay protection and rate-case exclusion. The capital plan supports a credible 6.5-7% EPS growth target and a well-covered ~3.3% dividend with a multi-decade growth record. These are real strengths and the reason the stock holds a ~5-turn premium to peers.

The constraints are equally real and explain the modest score decline. Free cash flow is structurally negative through the plan, requiring ongoing ATM dilution of ~3% per year that caps per-share economics; the 60.8% debt-to-cap balance sheet is directly exposed to a higher-for-longer rate path that compresses utility multiples; near-term estimates are drifting lower; and Illinois remains a recurring regulatory drag. At 20x forward earnings the stock is fairly valued for its quality and growth, with no identifiable mispricing and limited asymmetry: the analyst mean target of ~$124 implies a reasonable but unremarkable ~10% upside that is consistent with the underlying total-return math rather than evidence of a re-rating catalyst. We rate WEC HOLD, with an upgrade path on a pullback toward the low-$100s, a second contracted large-load customer, or a confirmed turn lower in rates. Overall score 6.15.
