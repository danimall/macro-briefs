**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Helix Energy Solutions is no longer best analyzed as a standalone offshore-services equity. On April 22, 2026, the company signed a definitive all-stock merger agreement with Hornbeck Offshore Services in which each Hornbeck share converts into 10.27167 Helix shares, leaving Helix holders with roughly 45% of a combined company that will be controlled by former Hornbeck shareholders, renamed Hornbeck Offshore Services, reincorporated in Delaware, and kept on the NYSE. A vote to hold HLX today is a vote to roll into that combined entity. The investment question shifts from whether Helix's standalone business is attractive to whether one wants to own a Gulf-of-Mexico-heavy combined offshore platform on these relative terms, which cannot be answered without Hornbeck's financials and a view on whether the 10.27167 exchange ratio fairly reflects relative value. That diligence is not available here, which is the central reason this remains a HOLD.

The standalone business is at a cyclical trough. FY2025 revenue fell 5% to $1,291M, operating income halved to $65M, net income dropped to $30.8M, and total gross margin compressed to 12% from 16%, concentrated in Well Intervention where margin collapsed to 6% from 13% on idle/warm-stacked vessels and 131 regulatory docking days. Q1 2026 deteriorated further to a $(13.4)M net loss with a 3% gross margin, though Q1 is seasonally weakest. The offsetting fact is balance-sheet quality: the company holds a net cash position that grew to $198M at Q1 2026 ($501M cash against $304M debt), with no maturities until 2029 and an undrawn $113M ABL. That financial staying power limits downside through the down-cycle.

The trajectory of prior scores (5.7 → 4.7 → 4.9 → 5.0) reflects a declining standalone thesis interrupted by the merger announcement. New data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: estimates for next year have been cut 19% over 90 days, the current-quarter estimate is down 25%, the buyback is suspended to preserve merger capital, and there is no insider conviction buying. We maintain HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale: the merger introduces a control transaction whose economics are unverifiable from the available data, and the standalone fundamentals are troughing rather than recovering.

## Company Overview

Helix is an international offshore energy services company (not an E&P, owning a handful of mature wells only incidentally), founded in 1979 and headquartered in Houston with approximately 2,212 employees. It operates four segments:

- **Well Intervention** (~57% of 2025 revenue, $729M): purpose-built vessels (Q4000, Q5000, Q7000, Seawell, Well Enhancer, plus chartered Sea Helix 1 and Siem Helix 2) that access subsea wells to enhance production or decommission them, a lower-cost alternative to drilling rigs. Operations span the Gulf of America, Brazil (Petrobras), the North Sea, and West Africa. SLB is an alliance partner.
- **Robotics** ($323M, +9% in 2025): ROVs, trenchers, and boulder grabs plus chartered vessels, with roughly 49% of segment revenue tied to offshore wind (cable trenching/burial, seabed clearance, UXO removal). This is the principal energy-transition growth vector.
- **Shallow Water Abandonment** ($200M): the Helix Alliance unit (acquired July 2022), focused on Gulf shelf decommissioning with liftboats, OSVs, and dive vessels.
- **Production Facilities** ($73M): the HP I floating production vessel, the Helix Fast Response System, and mature owned wells (Thunder Hawk, Droshky).

The company competes against drilling rigs and peers such as Oceaneering, TechnipFMC, Expro, DOF, Fugro, and Well-Safe, with a genuine niche moat in integrated Gulf shelf decommissioning and global jet-trenching for wind.

## Financial Analysis

A clear down-cycle is underway against a strong balance sheet.

**Income statement.** The business roughly doubled off the 2022 trough ($873M) but has now rolled over. The damage to gross margin was concentrated in Well Intervention on the Seawell being warm-stacked for all of 2025, 131 docking/regulatory days across the Q-series vessels, and the non-recurrence of $14M of 2024 cancellation fees. Well Intervention day rates were actually higher in 2025; the margin compression was a utilization and dry-dock phenomenon, which is reversible, not a pricing collapse.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $288M (+4% YoY) but a net loss of $(0.09) per share, with Adjusted EBITDA of $32M (down from $52M). Production Facilities swung to a $(7.5)M gross loss on Thunder Hawk workover costs. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter, and the Seawell was reactivated in February 2026 after a year warm-stacked.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — the bright spot.** FY2025 FCF was $120M (down from $163M). Net cash was $137M at year-end 2025. Debt is well-termed: $300M of 9.75% senior notes due 2029 plus roughly $10M of MARAD debt maturing February 2027. Current ratio is 2.75. The Altman Z-score of 2.58 sits in the grey zone, typical for an asset-heavy cyclical services company and mitigated by the net-cash position.

**Earnings surprises and estimates.** The beat/miss record is poor: one beat and three misses across the last four quarters, with the lone large beat (Q4 2025, +761%) reflecting a low base off a $0.02 estimate. More telling is the estimate revision trend: next-year EPS has been cut 19.2% over 90 days to $0.56, the current-quarter estimate is down 25% over 90 days to $0.08, and current-year is down 4.6% over 90 days. Consensus is marking the standalone business down even as the stock has appreciated 23.6% over the trailing year on merger optimism.

**Capital allocation.** The $200M buyback ($128.4M remaining) was suspended on April 22, 2026 to preserve capital for the merger. Management chose to conserve dry powder rather than repurchase a stock near multi-year lows, a clear signal that capital is being marshaled for the combination.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth prospects are muted in the near term. 2026 guidance is for revenue of $1,200–1,400M and Adjusted EBITDA of $230–290M, with a midpoint roughly flat-to-down versus 2025's $272M, FCF of $100–160M, and capital additions of $70–80M. This is a stabilization story, not a growth story, at the corporate level.

Beneath the surface, two pockets are firming. Robotics grew 22% YoY in Q1 2026 on stronger international wind/trenching, and the international offshore-services cycle is bifurcating in Helix's favor: Brazil/Petrobras decommissioning, robust European and Asian offshore wind, and stronger U.K. abandonment enforcement are constructive. The U.S. offshore wind leasing ban introduced in January 2025 hits the East Coast opportunity, but Helix's actual wind growth is international and insulated from that ban. Well Intervention has a credible 2026 recovery path as the Seawell returns to service and the heavy 2025 dry-dock schedule lapses.

The dominant growth variable is the merger. The combination increases Gulf of Mexico OSV/MPSV exposure and broadens the combined fleet, with stated synergy and pro-forma economics that are not yet disclosed in available filings. Whether this enhances or dilutes per-share growth for current Helix holders depends entirely on the relative valuation embedded in the 10.27167 exchange ratio and Hornbeck's standalone earnings power, neither of which is verifiable here.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 89x is meaningless at a cyclical trough and on a near-breakeven earnings base, so it is not the right anchor. On forward consensus of $0.56 for next year, the stock trades at roughly 15.5x forward earnings, but that estimate has been falling, which undermines treating it as a floor. Against the peer median P/E of 38.2x in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, HLX looks cheap, but the peer set is heterogeneous (VTOL at 11.2x, AESI negative, CLB at 21.5x) and headline multiples are noisy across the group.

The more defensible valuation frame is the net-cash-adjusted enterprise. With market cap of $1.28B and net cash of $198M, enterprise value is roughly $1.09B against guided 2026 Adjusted EBITDA, implying an EV/EBITDA of about 3.8–4.7x. That is a low absolute multiple, but offshore services is a deeply cyclical, asset-intensive business where low single-digit-to-mid-single-digit EBITDA multiples are normal at this point in the cycle, and the recurring dry-dock/recertification burden (~$52M in 2025) plus the $18M Thunder Hawk impairment in 2025 show that reported EBITDA overstates owner free cash.

The lesson from past scoring is directly relevant: a low multiple in the absence of an independent catalyst tends to be a value trap, and cheapness has consistently sorted the wrong way. Here the would-be catalyst is the merger, but its value to HLX holders is unverifiable because Hornbeck's financials are not available. The analyst price-target range ($9.00 low, $14.00 high, $12.50 mean) likely reflects merger-completion optimism rather than independent standalone fundamentals, and on a stock where consensus EPS is being cut, those targets are not the reliable floors they would be on a clean recovery name. We score valuation at 4.5: neither a clear bargain nor clearly stretched, with the asymmetry blocked by the inability to value the combined entity.

## Competitive Landscape

Helix is a differentiated, asset-heavy niche player. It holds a genuine moat in integrated Gulf shelf decommissioning (it claims to be the only provider offering all facets of Gulf shelf decommissioning) and in global jet-trenching for offshore wind. Its SLB alliance (jointly developed 15K IRS and ROAM, with an amendment signed February 2026) reinforces its well-intervention positioning.

Trajectory is the relevant test, and it is mixed-to-flat. The international firming (Brazil, European/Asian wind, U.K. enforcement) supports the moat in growth markets, and the Robotics segment is gaining international wind share. Against that, spot-exposed Well Intervention vessels (Q4000, Q7000, North Sea units) face a soft market with explicitly uncertain 2H 2026 utilization, and the corporate margin profile has deteriorated versus 2024. The Hornbeck combination is a scale play in a fragmented market that should improve competitive standing in the Gulf, but it also subordinates Helix holders to a minority position. On balance, the competitive position is stable rather than improving, supporting a 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Merger execution and deal risk (dominant, new).** The deal requires Helix shareholder approval, HSR/antitrust and foreign-investment clearances, and S-4 effectiveness. New Q1 risk factors flag merger litigation (securities/derivative suits are routine in such deals), a termination fee, and interim operating restrictions. Helix holders end up a minority (~45%) in a Hornbeck-controlled, Hornbeck-named company, a material loss of control plus integration risk.
2. **Well Intervention spot exposure.** 2H 2026 utilization on the Q4000/Q7000 is explicitly flagged as uncertain, with several IRS rental units idle.
3. **U.K. Energy Profits Levy** raised to 38% and extended to 2030, suppressing North Sea customer spending, a core Helix region that contributed to deferred 2025 work.
4. **U.S. offshore wind ban** (January 2025 memorandum) hitting the East Coast Robotics opportunity, though international wind is unaffected.
5. **Customer and backlog concentration.** Six customers (Shell, Subsea 7, Petrobras, Talos, NKT, CNR) account for ~82–83% of backlog; Shell was 18% of 2025 revenue. Backlog is partly cancelable, sometimes with little or no fee.
6. **Oil price volatility.** Q1 2026 commentary noted crude whipsawing from the mid-$50s to above $110 on Middle East/Hormuz disruption, creating customer capex uncertainty.
7. **Asset intensity.** $1.36B net PP&E with recurring dry-dock costs and demonstrated impairment risk.

Many of these (cyclicality, oil-price exposure) are permanently priced into an offshore services name. The genuinely unpriced and binary risk is the merger outcome: both the completion path and the relative-value question. With the failure scenario, integration risk, and exchange-ratio fairness all unresolved, the idiosyncratic risk justifies a 4.3.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 56.4% versus 48.4% one-year historical volatility (rich by 8 points, 77th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 56.4% above long-term 48.1%). IV has jumped sharply since early June (39.3% → 58.5%) even as the price fell 10.4%, and the put/call volume ratio of 4.44 reflects heavy near-term put activity, though the put/call OI ratio remains low at 0.38. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.8%. This pattern is consistent with the market hedging a near-term, merger-related catalyst (a proxy/S-4 update, a shareholder-vote date, or a regulatory milestone). It reinforces the two-sided, event-driven character of the stock and argues against treating the recent price decline as a clean fundamental signal in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The merger closes on terms favorable to Helix holders, creating a larger, better-capitalized Gulf-of-Mexico offshore platform with synergy upside and a net-cash balance sheet. The standalone business troughs in 1H 2026 and recovers as the Seawell returns, dry-dock days normalize, and international wind/decommissioning demand firms. Combined-company economics re-rate the equity toward the analyst mean/high of $12.50–$14.00, roughly 43–61% above the current $8.72.

**Bear case.** The deal subordinates Helix holders to a minority in a more cyclically exposed Gulf OSV fleet at an exchange ratio that proves rich to Hornbeck. Spot Well Intervention utilization stays soft into 2H 2026, estimate cuts continue, and the standalone business generates losses while the cycle is weak. If the merger breaks, the stock reverts to standalone fundamentals near the 52-week low of $5.52, roughly 37% below current. The net-cash balance sheet limits but does not eliminate this downside.

**Base case.** The merger completes in 2H 2026 and HLX holders roll into the combined company at a value broadly consistent with where the all-stock structure already trades (no blown-out arb premium/discount, consistent with a merger-of-equals). The standalone business stabilizes near guidance midpoints. Absent visibility into Hornbeck's financials, the central estimate of fair value is close to the current price, around $8.50–$10.00.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting genuine inability to value the combined entity from available data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Disclosure of the S-4/proxy with Hornbeck pro-forma financials and synergy targets showing the 10.27167 exchange ratio is accretive to Helix holders on EBITDA and FCF per share.
- Evidence the standalone business has troughed — two consecutive quarters of Well Intervention margin recovery (back toward double digits) and stabilizing or rising forward EPS estimates.
- A genuine merger-arb dislocation (HLX trading at a meaningful discount to the implied combined-company value) that creates asymmetric upside on deal close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The S-4/Hornbeck disclosure reveals the exchange ratio is dilutive to Helix holders or that Hornbeck carries materially weaker balance-sheet quality (high leverage, near-term maturities).
- Continued estimate cuts combined with 2H 2026 Well Intervention utilization deteriorating below management's already-soft commentary.
- A deal break, which would revert the stock to standalone fundamentals at a cyclical trough and toward the $5.52 low.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoints are the June 12, 2026 8-K (likely merger/regulatory or proxy update), the S-4/proxy effectiveness, the shareholder-vote date, and the next quarterly earnings release. Given the options market is pricing a near-term catalyst, the most actionable reassessment window is the next 1–3 months.

## Conclusion

Helix has ceased to be a clean standalone equity. The all-stock merger with Hornbeck converts a HLX position into a ~45% rollover stake in a Hornbeck-controlled company, and the value of that rollover hinges on the exchange ratio and Hornbeck's economics, neither of which can be assessed from available filings. The standalone business is at a cyclical trough — halved FY25 operating income, a Q1'26 loss, and falling forward estimates — but the net-cash balance sheet materially limits downside. Cheapness on EV/EBITDA is not, by itself, a buy signal in a cyclical services name without a verifiable catalyst, and the lone catalyst (the merger) is currently a black box on relative value. The options market is hedging near-term stress, the buyback is suspended, and insiders show no open-market conviction. The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided. We maintain HOLD pending disclosure of combined-company economics, with a clear path to either upgrade (accretive exchange ratio, troughed standalone fundamentals) or downgrade (dilutive ratio, deal break, continued deterioration).
