**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hope Bancorp, the holding company for Bank of Hope, describes itself as the only regional Korean-American bank in the US with $18.66 billion in assets. After a multi-year earnings decline that saw net income fall from roughly $218M in 2022 to about $100M in 2024 as funding costs spiked, the franchise is now in a clear recovery. Q1 2026 net income rose 40% YoY to $29.5M ($0.23 diluted EPS), driven almost entirely by funding-cost relief — net interest margin expanded 36 bps YoY to 2.90% as the cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 77 bps to 3.37%, a deposit beta exceeding 100% relative to Fed cuts. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 3.6% over 90 days and next-quarter estimates up 14.8% over 30 days, confirming positive earnings momentum.

The pivotal near-term catalyst is the all-cash acquisition of SMBC's MANUBANK Commercial Banking Unit, announced March 31, 2026. This adds roughly $2.5B in C&I/CRE loans and $2.7B in low-cost deposits (only ~3% CDs) with no share issuance, funded from excess capital, and is expected to be meaningfully accretive to 2027 earnings with a ~2-year tangible book earnback. The non-dilutive structure is a structurally better deal than the all-stock Territorial transaction that closed in April 2025. The strategic logic pairs SMBC's Japanese banking division with Hope's Korean-American banking franchise to build a scaled Asian-American multinational commercial platform.

The stock has appreciated roughly 25% over the trailing year to ~$13.44, now trading at approximately tangible book value ($13.73/share) and near the low end of the analyst target range ($14–15). The recovery is real and the M&A structure is attractive, but the constraints are equally concrete: ROA remains sub-1% (0.64%), CRE concentration will rise to ~320% of capital post-deal at heightened regulatory scrutiny levels, and California carries ~61% of CRE exposure. We rate HOPE a HOLD (growth_hold). The margin-recovery-plus-accretion thesis is intact, but the risk/reward at the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Bank of Hope is headquartered in Los Angeles. Its niche is serving multicultural (primarily Korean-American) commercial customers, now expanding to a broader Asian-American multinational strategy. The footprint spans 45 full-service Bank of Hope branches across California, New York, New Jersey, Washington, Texas, Illinois, Georgia and Alabama, plus 28 Territorial Savings branches in Hawaii (acquired April 2025). It operates SBA, commercial and residential mortgage loan-production offices nationally, a representative office in Seoul, and runs as a single reportable segment.

The loan book of $14.64B is CRE-heavy at 58%, with C&I 25% and residential mortgage 17%. Deposits of $15.73B break down as 22% noninterest-bearing, 38% money-market/NOW/savings, and 40% time deposits. Bank of Hope is an SBA Preferred Lender, generating recurring gain-on-sale fee income. The ethnic-niche franchise is difficult for larger banks to replicate, giving deposit stickiness and pricing power that pure-geography competitors lack.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings inflection is genuine and the mechanism is well-defined. Q1 2026 net income of $29.5M ($0.23 diluted EPS) was up 40% YoY from $21.1M ($0.17), beating consensus of $0.22. Sequential softness was present — Q4 2025 net income was ~$34M, falling to $29.5M in Q1 on higher provision and a higher tax rate (Q4 carried one-time tax true-ups) — but pre-provision net revenue rose 1% QoQ to $47M and was up 43% YoY, confirming the underlying trend.

The margin story is the core of the thesis. NIM expanded to 2.90% from 2.54%, driven almost entirely by funding-cost relief. The chain is straightforward: Fed cuts flow through to the deposit base at a beta exceeding 100%, cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 77 bps to 3.37%, and cost of total deposits fell to 2.64% from 3.18%. Management expects continued CD repricing (~5–7 bps/quarter of interest-bearing deposit cost reduction) and further margin expansion in 2H 2026 if rates hold. New loans yield ~6.4% versus a 5.69% back-book average, adding an asset-repricing tailwind. This funding-cost-driven recovery is higher-quality than one-time gains, and the SMBC deposits reinforce it. The dependency to watch: with the Fed on hold, deposit competition could intensify and cap the beta advantage.

Profitability remains sub-par but rising. ROA is 0.64%, ROE 5.14%, ROTCE 6.66%, and the efficiency ratio improved to 67% from 72% a year ago. These metrics sit below well-run regional peers (ROA ~1%+), reflecting the low NIM and CRE-heavy, lower-yielding asset base. This is the central reason the stock trades near tangible book rather than at a premium.

Capital is strong: CET1 12.36%, total capital 14.07%, leverage 11.11%, with the Bank well-capitalized and holding meaningful excess. Book value is $17.86/share; tangible book value $13.73/share, so at ~$13.44 the stock trades slightly below tangible book. AOCI carries a $152.4M accumulated loss from ~$194.6M of unrealized losses on the AFS securities portfolio — rate-driven, government-agency paper, no credit impairment — a real but unrealized economic drag on tangible equity that will accrete back as securities roll to par.

Capital allocation is disciplined. The $0.14/quarter dividend ($0.56 annual) was maintained, yielding ~4.1%, and is comfortably covered. Modest buybacks continued (604,161 shares at $11.10 = $6.7M in Q1 2026; $28.6M remaining on a $50M authorization). The SMBC deal is all-cash with no share issuance, deploying excess capital efficiently.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is aggressive but largely deal-driven: loan growth >20% (SMBC plus organic mid-single digit, with CRE held flat), revenue growth at the high end of 15–20%, and PPNR growth of 25–30% ex-notables, assuming no further Fed cuts and a 20–25% effective tax rate. The organic engine on its own is more modest — mid-single-digit loan growth with deliberately flat CRE — so the headline growth figures depend on completing and integrating the acquisition.

Estimate revisions support the growth case. Current-year EPS estimates rose to $1.15, next-quarter estimates jumped to $0.31, and next-year estimates sit at $1.59 (up 2.1% over 90 days), implying ~38% YoY EPS growth into 2027 as SMBC accretion lands. This is a meaningful revision trend, though it does not clear the +30% velocity threshold that flags a strong standalone buy signal. The revisions are consistent with a durable but moderate inflection.

The two acquisitions provide the structural growth. Territorial (closed April 2, 2025) contributes low-cost Hawaii deposits and a steady ~$4M/quarter purchase-accounting accretion benefit for years. SMBC adds scale in middle-market lending, franchise finance, and specialty verticals, plus a collaboration agreement to serve Japanese commercial and retail customers in the US. The strategic ambition to become the largest regional bank serving multicultural customers across the continental US and Hawaii is credible given the niche's defensibility, but organic growth is being intentionally suppressed near-term to manage CRE concentration ahead of the deal close.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$13.44, HOPE trades at ~24.6x trailing P/E and ~11.7x forward P/E on the $1.15 current-year estimate (falling toward ~8.5x on the $1.59 next-year estimate as SMBC accretion arrives). The trailing multiple is inflated by trough earnings; the more relevant forward multiple is reasonable but not compelling relative to peers.

Peer medians in regional banks sit at ~14.2x P/E and ~2.7% yield. On forward earnings, HOPE at ~11.7x is a discount to the peer median, which is appropriate given its sub-1% ROA and CRE concentration. The 4.1% dividend yield is well above the peer median, providing tangible support. On price-to-tangible-book, the stock trades at ~0.98x TBV, roughly in line with what a low-ROE regional warrants — the market is pricing the depressed ROE, not a premium.

The valuation dimension is where the trajectory has moved most (from 7.0 in May to 5.0 in June). The stock has re-rated ~25% and now sits at the low end of the analyst target band and slightly above tangible book, compressing the discount that made it attractive earlier. Cheapness relative to tangible book has largely been captured. On our internal calibration, a "cheap" print near book without an independent re-rating catalyst has more often been a value trap than a signal, and Financials BUYs carry structurally negative alpha versus the market — both counsel against rewarding the modest remaining discount. The re-rating catalyst that exists (SMBC accretion and NIM expansion) is real but now partially priced; the analyst targets here are near-term ceilings rather than floors, since the stock already sits near the range and ROE remains below the level that would justify a premium multiple. We set valuation at 5.5 — modestly constructive on the yield support and forward-earnings discount, but not a mispricing with clear asymmetric upside at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

Hope operates in the fragmented, intensely competitive US regional banking sector, where deposit costs and CRE credit are the two dominant swing factors post-2023 rate shock. Its Korean-American niche confers pricing power and customer stickiness that pure-geography competitors lack, and the SMBC partnership is an explicit attempt to replicate this in the Japanese-American segment. The consolidator strategy in Asian-American commercial banking is a differentiated M&A thesis, and the ethnic-niche moat is defensible.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving: the niche is durable, and the SMBC deal expands the addressable multicultural franchise. Offsetting this, Hope is a small player (~$1.7B market cap) with a sub-peer ROA and NIM, meaning it competes from a position of scale disadvantage against larger regionals with lower funding costs. Net, the competitive position is stable with a modest positive tilt from the consolidation strategy, but there is no evidence of share gains large enough to drive a re-rating on their own.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration** is the dominant risk. 58% of loans are CRE, with 61% of that in California. Post-SMBC, pro forma CRE concentration rises to ~320% of capital, a level that draws heightened regulatory scrutiny under interagency guidance. Management is deliberately flattening CRE growth ahead of the deal to manage this, which mitigates but also caps near-term balance-sheet growth. The portfolio is granular (avg loan ~$2.1M, weighted LTV 47%) with low office exposure (4% of CRE), which limits the worst office-CRE tail, but hotels (9%) and gas stations/car washes (14%) carry idiosyncratic risk.

**Asset quality** is improving but with an elevated C&I charge-off print. NPAs fell to $120.5M (0.65% of assets) from $136M at YE2025, and criticized loans dropped 28% YoY to $325M. Net charge-offs ran 29 bps annualized in Q1 2026, driven by ~$9.9M of C&I charge-offs as management resolved previously identified problem loans. ACL coverage is 1.06% (141.6% of nonaccruals). Management characterizes this as proactive cleanup rather than new stress, and the falling criticized-loan trend supports that read, but it warrants monitoring.

**Integration and execution risk** stacks two acquisitions in roughly 18 months (Territorial 2025, SMBC 2026), with deposit-retention, cost-save realization, and CRE-concentration management all in flight simultaneously. The SMBC close remains subject to regulatory approval in 2H 2026, a binary near-term event.

**Korea/geopolitical linkage** and **Southern California natural-disaster exposure** (the early-2025 LA fires were immaterial) are structural but partly priced. The bank is modestly asset-sensitive (+100 bps → +3.8% NII; −100 bps → −2.8%), so the margin recovery partly depends on rates staying higher-for-longer, a risk if the Fed resumes cutting. The >$10B asset threshold subjects it to enhanced CFPB oversight and stress testing, and the March 2026 proposed Basel III capital rule is under evaluation.

**Options Market Signal**: Options data is limited for this name, showing only a price move from $12.84 to $13.57 (+5.7%) over the observation window with no meaningful skew or IV signal. Short interest is 5.2% of float with 4.1 days to cover — uncrowded, consistent with a name that neither informed bears nor squeeze-chasers are heavily positioned in. This is neutral for the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$16–17):** NIM continues expanding through 2H 2026 as CDs reprice and the SMBC low-cost deposits close, lifting ROA toward 0.8%+ and ROTCE toward 9%+. SMBC clears regulatory approval, integrates cleanly, and delivers the guided 2027 accretion with a ~2-year TBV earnback. The consolidator strategy and Asian-multinational platform re-rate the multiple toward the peer median (~14x) on rising ~$1.59 next-year EPS, supporting a mid-teens to $17 price. The 4.1% yield and buyback provide a floor.

**Bear Case (~$10–11):** CRE credit deteriorates in California as commercial real estate values reset, pushing charge-offs and provisions higher; the C&I cleanup proves to be the leading edge of broader stress rather than a one-off. Regulatory scrutiny of the ~320% pro forma CRE concentration slows or conditions the SMBC deal. The Fed resumes cutting, compressing the margin recovery given asset sensitivity, and ROA stalls below 0.7%. The stock de-rates back below tangible book toward $10–11.

The base case sits between these: the margin recovery holds, SMBC closes and integrates, ROE improves gradually but stays below peer levels, and the stock tracks toward the low-to-mid $14s over 12 months as accretion offsets the CRE and rate risks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The margin-recovery and non-dilutive accretion thesis is intact and forward-looking, but the risk/reward at the current price is balanced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SMBC MANUBANK deal receives regulatory approval and closes on schedule in 2H 2026 with confirmed 2027 accretion guidance intact.
- NIM expands beyond 3.00% in 2H 2026 while net charge-offs normalize back toward 15 bps or below, confirming the C&I spike was cleanup.
- A pullback toward the low-$12s (a discount to tangible book) that restores asymmetric upside, or estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +30% on the next-year number.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SMBC deal is delayed, conditioned, or terminated by regulators, or pro forma CRE concentration draws a formal supervisory action.
- Net charge-offs accelerate above 40 bps annualized with criticized loans rising QoQ, signaling genuine CRE/C&I deterioration rather than cleanup.
- A dividend cut or a shift to dilutive equity issuance, or ROA falling back below 0.55%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (scheduled ~July 27, 2026) for NIM trajectory and credit trends, and the regulatory decision on the SMBC close during 2H 2026 as the primary catalyst.

## Conclusion

Hope Bancorp is a credible earnings-recovery story built on durable funding-cost relief and reinforced by a well-structured, non-dilutive all-cash acquisition of SMBC MANUBANK. The 4.1% yield, disciplined buyback, and roughly-tangible-book valuation limit downside. The offsetting realities are a sub-1% ROA, CRE and California concentration rising to ~320% of capital under heightened regulatory scrutiny, and a stock that has already re-rated ~25% to sit at tangible book and near the analyst target range, compressing the mispricing that existed earlier. The trajectory of our scores (6.0 → 5.5) reflects that captured re-rating rather than any fundamental deterioration; fundamentals are, if anything, modestly improving. We rate HOPE a HOLD (growth_hold), with an upgrade contingent on the SMBC deal clearing regulatory approval and NIM expansion continuing through 2H 2026, and a downgrade if CRE credit deteriorates or the deal stalls.
