## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy is a diversified U.S. E&P company operating across the Permian Basin, Marcellus Shale, and Anadarko Basin, currently in the final stages of an all-stock merger with Devon Energy (0.70 exchange ratio, shareholder-approved May 4, 2026, expected to close on or around May 7, 2026). The stock has rallied 42% over 12 months to $35.86, driven by the merger announcement, elevated energy prices, and upward revisions in FY2026 EPS estimates.

At approximately 12x FY2026E earnings with a 2.5% dividend yield, Coterra offers a concrete valuation relative to peers when adjusted for its earnings momentum and direct exposure to elevated energy prices. The Devon merger provides defined upside catalysts ($1B cost synergies, 31% dividend increase, $5B buyback) while the standalone business executed well: 2025 production exceeded guidance by 5.7% and drilling costs came in below target.

With the merger expected to close imminently, CTRA is now effectively an exchange-ratio instrument into DVN. The investment thesis is therefore a view on owning the combined Devon entity post-close. The primary risks are commodity sensitivity to a price downturn, integration execution, and a recent earnings miss track record (3 of last 4 quarters). The risk/reward favors ownership here for investors willing to hold DVN shares post-conversion.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy was formed through the 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas (Marcellus gas) and Cimarex Energy (Permian oil), creating a multi-basin E&P with natural diversification across oil and natural gas. The company operates approximately 782 MBoe/day of production, with ~67% of capital allocated to the Permian Basin's Delaware sub-basin.

In January 2025, Coterra completed $4.0B in acquisitions (FME: $2.5B, Avant: $1.5B) adding substantial Delaware Basin acreage. On February 2, 2026, the company announced a definitive all-stock merger with Devon Energy creating one of the largest U.S. independent shale producers. Shareholders approved the deal on May 4, 2026, with closing expected on or around May 7, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** Full-year 2025 revenue from contracts with customers reached $7.29B (+34% YoY), with total GAAP operating revenues of $7.645B (including $351M in derivative gains). Net income was $1.72B (+54%). Full-year operating cash flow was $4.02B. The rebound from 2024's trough ($5.46B customer revenue) reflects both acquisition contributions (FME added $588M revenue, Avant $195M) and commodity price recovery.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stood at $3.818B at December 31, 2025 (21% debt-to-capitalization) with investment-grade ratings from all three agencies. The $2.0B revolver is fully undrawn. A $250M maturity in September 2026 is manageable. Interest expense nearly doubled to $156M (9M 2025) due to acquisition financing, but coverage remains comfortable.

**FCF Generation:** Using a simple CFO-minus-capex definition, free cash flow was approximately $1.73B in 2025 (24% FCF margin on customer revenue). The company's own non-GAAP FCF definition yields approximately $2.03B. Either measure supports the $0.88/share annual dividend with substantial excess. At elevated oil prices, FCF generation accelerates meaningfully above the company's planning price.

**Cost Pressures:** Full-year 2025 direct operations expense rose to $3.58/Boe from $2.66/Boe due to higher-cost acquired assets. Workover expense rose to $196M from $104M (+88%). These are integration-related and should moderate as operational improvements take hold (flare intensity already down 40% on acquired assets).

**Capital Allocation:** Management conserved capital post-acquisitions, with full-year 2025 buybacks of $140M (6 million shares) — well below 2024's $404M — while repaying the full $500M Tranche A term loan and $200M of Tranche B during 2025 (the remaining $300M Tranche B was repaid in February 2026). The $1.1B remaining buyback authorization and Devon merger's $5B buyback commitment provide forward return-of-capital catalysts.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** Full-year 2025 production of 782 MBoe/day exceeded the 740 target by 5.7%. Full-year oil production averaged 160 MBbl/day, up from 109 MBbl/day in 2024 (+47%, acquisitions-driven). The company's Permian acreage supports multi-year development at current activity levels.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-quarter and full-year 2026 EPS estimates have risen materially over the past 90 days, reflecting the elevated commodity price environment flowing through to E&P earnings power. This upward revision momentum is a positive signal.

**Devon Merger Synergies:** The combined entity targets $1B in annual pre-tax cost savings (run-rate by 2027) from overlapping Delaware Basin operations. Scale benefits in procurement, midstream optimization, and G&A reduction provide a credible path to these savings.

**LNG Demand Tailwind:** Coterra has approximately $5.5B in unsatisfied performance obligations for natural gas sales with fixed pricing over approximately 13 years, indicating significant contracted gas volumes. While the company does not explicitly disclose the counterparty type, growing U.S. LNG export capacity represents a potential structural demand driver for Marcellus production.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.86, Coterra trades at:
- Approximately 12x FY2026E EPS (based on available consensus estimates)
- ~16.0x trailing P/E ($2.24 TTM EPS, reflecting lower 2025 gas prices)
- 2.5% dividend yield
- ~5x EV/EBITDA (estimated)

**Merger-Implied Value:** At the 0.70 exchange ratio, CTRA's value is mechanically tied to Devon's stock price. DVN closed near $50, implying CTRA at $35.00; the current price reflects this arithmetic closely. With closing imminent, the merger spread is minimal, indicating high market confidence in completion. The forward investment question is whether DVN itself is undervalued post-close. At approximately 12x forward earnings with $1B synergies not yet fully in estimates, the combined entity offers re-rating potential.

**Peer Comparison:** Coterra's forward multiple appears roughly in line with E&P peers (DVN, EOG, Diamondback), but the combined entity's synergy upside and enhanced capital return program could support modest multiple expansion.

**Analyst Consensus:** Available analyst targets suggest moderate upside from current levels, though specific target ranges may not fully reflect the imminent merger close and post-merger DVN valuation dynamics.

The specific reason for potential mispricing: FY2026 estimates have risen substantially in recent months, but the stock's appreciation may not fully reflect the new commodity price environment embedded in current strip pricing, particularly as investors evaluate the combined entity's earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

Coterra occupies a differentiated position as a multi-basin operator with both oil (Permian) and gas (Marcellus) exposure. This diversification historically carried a conglomerate discount versus pure-play peers, but in the current environment where both oil and gas prices are elevated, it provides optionality.

**Operational Execution:** Drilling costs of $915/ft (below $931 target) and PVI-10 economic returns of 1.78x (vs. 1.50x target) demonstrate above-peer efficiency. The Marcellus operations in particular generate exceptional returns due to low F&D costs.

**Competitive Position Trajectory:** Improving. The FME/Avant acquisitions added ~14% to production capacity and extended Permian inventory. The Devon merger will create a top-3 independent U.S. shale producer with enhanced scale advantages in the Delaware Basin.

**Inventory Depth:** The combined Coterra-Devon entity will hold one of the deepest Tier 1 drilling inventories in the U.S., reducing the "running room" concern that affects smaller operators.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Price Sensitivity (Primary):** Revenue swung from $9.5B (2022) to $5.5B (2024) at varying commodity prices. A significant oil price decline would reduce FY2026 EPS materially and pressure the stock lower.

**2. Merger Integration Execution:** Integration of a ~$58B combined entity carries significant execution risk. Activist investor Kimmeridge is already pushing for post-merger asset sales and governance changes, creating potential distraction.

**3. Recent Earnings Miss Pattern:** 3 of the last 4 quarters missed consensus. Estimates have since been substantially revised upward to reflect current commodity prices, reducing the probability of a repeat miss at current strip pricing.

**4. EPA Enforcement:** Ongoing DOJ civil enforcement for Clean Air Act violations (referred July 2023) remains unresolved. The matter could result in fines, penalties, and corrective actions; the company has not disclosed an estimate of the potential financial impact.

**5. Natural Gas Basis Risk:** Waha Hub prices went negative in September 2025 due to Permian pipeline constraints. Midstream buildout is underway but constraints persist.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42-46):** Oil sustains elevated levels, Devon merger closes, $1B synergies begin flowing to earnings by 2027, combined entity re-rates to 13-14x on improved scale and capital return ($5B buyback + 31% higher dividend). CTRA shares at 0.70 exchange ratio benefit from DVN re-rating. Additional upside from LNG export capacity additions driving Marcellus gas premiums.

**Bear Case ($26-28):** Oil prices decline significantly. FY2026 EPS falls materially. Merger integration faces operational challenges. Combined entity trades at 11x trough earnings = DVN at ~$38-42, implying CTRA equivalent at $27-29 pre-close or comparable downside post-merger.

**Base Case ($40-42):** Oil averages moderately elevated levels through year-end, merger closes as expected, initial synergy capture evident by Q4 2026, combined entity trades at 12.5-13x 2027E EPS reflecting improved scale. At 0.70 ratio: DVN at $57-60 implies CTRA equivalent of $40-42.

## Conclusion

Coterra Energy at $35.86 offers asymmetric risk/reward within the energy sector: approximately 12x forward earnings on rising estimates, 2.5% dividend yield, and a defined catalyst path through the imminent Devon merger close. The merger provides structural upside through $1B synergies and enhanced capital return, while the underlying commodity environment supports earnings power well above historical averages. At current prices, with the merger spread near zero, the stock has not fully priced in the post-merger re-rating potential from synergy capture and the combined entity's improved scale. BUY with a 12-17% base-case return over the next 6-12 months, accepting the binary commodity and integration risks as adequately compensated. This recommendation is appropriate only for investors willing to own DVN shares post-conversion.

**Addressing the score/recommendation reconciliation:** The 6.5 overall score reflects real constraints: a weak recent earnings record (3/4 misses), integration execution uncertainty, and EPA enforcement overhang. BUY is warranted because: (1) the merger close is imminent with a specific synergy catalyst, (2) upward estimate revisions indicate the prior miss pattern is reversing due to commodity tailwinds, and (3) at approximately 12x forward P/E, the combined entity trades at an attractive level given its superior scale and capital return program. The risk factors compress the confidence interval around a still-positive mean outcome.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The merger is expected to close imminently (on or around May 7, 2026), making CTRA effectively an exchange-ratio security into DVN rather than a standalone multi-quarter catalyst. Additionally, the EPS estimate revision figures central to the thesis (+51% current-quarter, +36% FY2026) could not be independently validated against primary sources; the recommendation assumes directionally correct upward revisions but specific magnitudes are unverified.
