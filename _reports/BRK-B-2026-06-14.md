**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway is in the middle of the most consequential transition in its modern history: Warren Buffett stepped down as CEO effective January 1, 2026, with Greg Abel now running the company while Buffett retains the chairmanship and a controlling vote. The first five months of the Abel era show a more active operator than the "nothing changes" consensus expected. Abel broke Buffett's 13-quarter net-selling streak with ~$16B of net equity purchases in Q1 2026, closed the ~$9.5B OxyChem acquisition in January, and announced his first major deal, the $6.8B all-cash purchase of homebuilder Taylor Morrison, with Buffett's public endorsement. The investment debate has shifted from "will the cash ever be deployed" to "is Abel deploying it well."

The underlying business remains exceptionally resilient. Q1 2026 operating earnings grew across all four pillars (insurance underwriting after-tax $1.72B, BNSF $1.38B with operating ratio improving to 65.6%, BHE $1.11B, MSR $3.20B), and the balance sheet carries $727.2B of shareholder equity, ~$373.5B of cash and T-bills, and just $19.9B of parent debt against $176.9B of negative-cost insurance float. The April 2026 Oregon appellate reversal of the PacifiCorp Phase I wildfire verdict materially de-risked a multi-billion-dollar litigation tail that the market had been pricing.

We rate BRK-B a HOLD. This is a high-quality franchise worth owning, but at ~1.45x book value the stock is fairly valued, and management agrees: Berkshire repurchased only ~$0.24B of stock in Q1 2026, far below the pace it sets when the shares trade meaningfully below conservatively-estimated intrinsic value. Combined with a 13.6% cut to current-year EPS estimates over the past 90 days, GEICO's deteriorating underwriting margin, and no catalyst inside the next 60 days, the risk/reward does not clear the bar for an active buy. The score trajectory (7.3 in May to 6.7 now) reflects a rational repricing of transition uncertainty, not a deterioration in the business.

## Company Overview

Berkshire is a decentralized conglomerate built on four pillars sitting atop a ~$330B marketable equity portfolio funded substantially by insurance float:

- **Insurance** — GEICO (auto), Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group (commercial specialty), and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group (NICO, General Re, TransRe). Insurance generates low/negative-cost capital that funds the rest of the enterprise, a structural advantage no competitor replicates at scale.
- **BNSF Railway** — one of North America's largest railroads, >32,500 route miles across 28 states.
- **Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE)** — regulated utilities (PacifiCorp, MidAmerican, NV Energy), interstate gas pipelines, UK/Canada grids, renewables, and HomeServices real estate brokerage.
- **Manufacturing, Service & Retailing (MSR)** — PCC (aerospace), Lubrizol, Marmon, IMC, Clayton Homes, Shaw, Benjamin Moore, Duracell, NetJets, McLane, Pilot, and, new since January 2026, OxyChem.

The moat combines permanent low-cost float, a fortress balance sheet that lets Berkshire act as buyer-of-last-resort in dislocations, decentralized autonomy that attracts sellers of family and founder businesses, and a concentrated equity portfolio (Apple, American Express, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, Chevron together represent ~61% of equity holdings).

## Financial Analysis

**Scale and Q1 2026 results (10-Q filed May 4, 2026):**
- Total assets $1.252T; Berkshire shareholders' equity up $9.8B in the quarter.
- Q1 2026 total revenues $93.7B (+4.4% YoY); net earnings attributable to Berkshire $10.1B vs. $4.6B in Q1 2025.
- The YoY net-income increase is largely optical: ~$3.8B of the improvement comes from a smaller after-tax mark-to-market investment loss ($1.2B vs. $5.0B), not operating strength. GAAP net income at Berkshire is dominated by equity-portfolio marks and should not be read as a clean earnings signal.

**Operating earnings (the figure management considers meaningful) grew across segments:**
- Insurance underwriting after-tax $1.72B vs. $1.34B, aided by zero significant catastrophes in Q1 2026 versus $860M of after-tax California wildfire losses in Q1 2025.
- BNSF $1.38B (+13.4%).
- BHE $1.11B (+1.3%).
- MSR $3.20B (+4.5%).

**The cash position:** Cash plus T-bills stood at ~$373.5B at March 31, 2026 (insurance and other), with ~$6.6B more at the rail and energy units; T-bills alone were ~$339B. Operating cash flow was $10.4B in the quarter against $5.0B of capex (BNSF and BHE roughly $3.2B), with ~$12.4B of additional capex guided for the balance of the year. The effective tax rate normalized to 17.4% from an unusually low 9.2% a year earlier.

**Quality and resilience:** Float remains negative-cost. Parent-level debt is only $19.9B, and management maintains a self-imposed $30B liquidity floor. Standard screening tools mislead badly here: the Altman Z-Score flags Berkshire in the "distress" zone, which is meaningless for an insurer/holding company whose "liabilities" are insurance float and whose earnings swing with equity marks. By any reasonable measure Berkshire is among the most over-capitalized, conservatively-financed large companies in existence.

The genuine financial-efficiency question is the cash hoard. Roughly $339B in T-bills earning short-rate yields is a structural drag on return on equity in a rising equity market. Abel's early deployment suggests this drag is being addressed, but it remains the dominant determinant of forward returns.

## Growth Analysis

Berkshire is a low-growth compounder, and the recent trend confirms it. Full-year 2025 net income fell to $67.3B from $89.6B in 2024, and operating earnings grew at a mid-single-digit pace in Q1 2026. The standout operating soft spot is GEICO: the Q1 2026 loss ratio rose 4.9 points to 73.9% as bodily-injury severity ran +12–14% and frequency rose, cutting underwriting profit to $1.42B from $2.17B, a roughly 35% decline. This is an industry-wide severity phenomenon, but it directly pressures Berkshire's most visible insurance franchise.

The forward growth case rests on three deployable levers:
1. **Cash deployment.** OxyChem and Taylor Morrison ($6.8B equity / ~$8.5B enterprise value, announced May 31) signal a willingness to put the balance sheet to work in a soft housing market, a contrarian, Berkshire-style entry that vertically connects to Clayton, Shaw, Benjamin Moore, and MiTek.
2. **New premium streams.** The 10-year Tokio Marine quota-share reinsurance treaty (effective April 1, 2026) adds "meaningful premium volumes" through NICO.
3. **Operating execution.** BNSF's operating ratio improvement narrows the historical efficiency gap to Union Pacific.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-year consensus EPS was cut 13.6% over 90 days ($24.02 to $20.75), reflecting normalization away from elevated 2024 marks; the more recent 30-day trend is modestly positive (+1.6% current year, +1.1% next year), and next-year estimates are essentially stable at $21.47. The large 90-day cut means this name does not carry the positive-revision momentum that would support a stronger growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

The provided sector "peers" (Blue Owl Capital at 81.5x P/E, Klarna with negative earnings) are not comparable to a diversified insurer/holding company and are not usable as benchmarks. The relevant anchors are Berkshire's own price-to-book and its management's revealed view through the buyback.

At a $1.055T market cap against equity, the stock trades at ~1.45x book. Buffett's longstanding repurchase framework activated around ~1.2x book; at 1.45x, Berkshire bought back only ~$0.24B (33 Class A and 431,462 Class B shares) in Q1 2026. The dormant buyback is the cleanest valuation signal available: the board, with the best information on conservatively-estimated intrinsic value, is not buying at current prices. On forward earnings the stock trades at ~22.8x forward consensus, full for a mid-single-digit operating grower with a large cash drag on ROE.

Analyst targets reinforce the modest-upside read: mean $520.33 (+6.4% from $489.25), median $510, high $570, and a low of $481 that sits *below* the current price. We do not treat these targets as floors here because Berkshire is neither an inflection stock with rising estimates nor cheap relative to its growth, the conditions under which lagging consensus targets understate value. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric, which is why this dimension scores in the middle of the range despite the company's exceptional quality.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is wide and stable, with two marginal pressures.

- **Insurance.** GEICO competes with Progressive, State Farm, and Allstate in an auto market where rising severity is hitting everyone; the company-specific question is GEICO's telematics and tech catch-up to Progressive, and the Q1 margin deterioration shows the gap is not yet closed. On reinsurance, Berkshire's capital lets it write large, lumpy covers (the new Tokio Marine treaty) that smaller competitors cannot.
- **Rail.** BNSF's operating ratio narrows but has not closed the historical efficiency gap to Union Pacific.
- **Energy.** BHE is a regulated-utility growth platform with data-center/electrification capex tailwinds, but PacifiCorp's wildfire exposure illustrates the rising physical-climate liability for Western utilities.
- **Housing.** The OxyChem + Taylor Morrison + Clayton/Shaw/Benjamin Moore/MiTek combination is building a vertically-aware housing platform, an Abel-era strategic tilt deployed into a currently soft market.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-softening rather than improving, which is why this dimension sits at 7.0 rather than higher. The genuinely novel competitive variable is the erosion of the "Buffett premium," Berkshire's historical ability to source businesses cheaply because sellers wanted Buffett as their permanent home. Whether that premium transfers to Abel is unproven, but on the early evidence (a Buffett-endorsed deal at a 24% premium), it appears intact.

## Risk Assessment

- **Key-person transition (dominant risk).** Buffett's departure as CEO and the loss of the Buffett premium is the central uncertainty, but it is well-telegraphed and largely priced; the stock is down ~5% from its 52-week high of $516.85, and Buffett remains Chairman with controlling votes.
- **PacifiCorp wildfire litigation.** ~$2.9B accrued, ~$2.3B paid ($584M in Q1 2026 alone), with management stating additional material losses are "reasonably possible" and unbounded. The April 8, 2026 Oregon Court of Appeals reversal of the Phase I verdict (faulty jury instruction), the litigation stay, and mandatory mediation materially de-risked this tail and could discharge ~$719M in posted bonds. This is an underappreciated asymmetric positive.
- **GEICO margin pressure.** The 35% YoY drop in underwriting profit is a real operating thread to monitor.
- **Investment concentration and impairment.** ~61% of the equity book is in five names; Kraft Heinz carrying value exceeded fair value by $1.4B at quarter-end (no impairment taken), following 2025 impairments of both Kraft Heinz and Occidental.
- **HomeServices antitrust.** $250M settlement on appeal at the 8th Circuit; Texas deceptive-trade claims allege ~$9B.
- **Untested capital allocation.** The Q1 equity buying and the Taylor Morrison premium into a soft housing market are early Abel decisions whose returns will not be visible for several quarters.

Berkshire carries few binary catastrophic risks, beta is low at 0.617, and the known risks are mostly priced. The PacifiCorp reversal tilts the near-term risk profile positively, which supports a 7.0 risk score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (toward $570, +16%):** Abel proves a disciplined and more active capital allocator. The Taylor Morrison and OxyChem deployments earn attractive returns as housing recovers, the Tokio Marine treaty adds durable underwriting income, GEICO's severity pressure normalizes, the PacifiCorp matter resolves at or below accruals, and the cash hoard continues to fund accretive acquisitions and equity purchases. Book value compounds at a high-single-digit rate and the multiple holds at ~1.5x. Abel's ~$18M open-market purchase looks prescient.

**Bear Case (toward $440, -10%):** The Buffett premium erodes, Berkshire pays full prices for businesses (Taylor Morrison's 24% premium becomes the template), GEICO's underwriting deterioration persists as severity stays elevated, a Kraft Heinz impairment lands, and the cash hoard continues to drag ROE while the market re-rates the stock toward book value as the discipline that justified its premium becomes less certain. PacifiCorp delivers an adverse mediation outcome.

**Base Case (toward $520, +6%):** Book value compounds in the mid-single digits, the multiple holds near 1.45x, and the stock tracks intrinsic-value growth with limited multiple support. The deployment story is constructive but slow to show returns, and the valuation offers thin upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — a constructive 12-month thesis driven by cash deployment and de-risking, but with modest conviction and limited valuation upside.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (early August 2026), the Taylor Morrison close (expected H2 2026), and PacifiCorp mediation developments.

**Why this is a HOLD and not a BUY (specific and falsifiable):**
1. **Valuation.** At ~1.45x book, Berkshire's own buyback is dormant ($0.24B in Q1 vs. the multi-billion pace it sets when shares trade below conservative intrinsic value, historically near ~1.2x book). The board's revealed view is that the stock is fairly valued. *Falsifiable:* a return to material repurchases (>$5B in a quarter) would signal the stock has reached buy-level attractiveness.
2. **Estimate momentum is not supportive.** The current-year EPS cut over 90 days swamps the +1.6% 30-day uptick. A buy-grade growth signal requires positive 90-day revisions, which is absent.
3. **No catalyst inside 60 days.** The thesis-moving events (deployment returns, Tokio Marine ramp, PacifiCorp resolution, Taylor Morrison close) are 2–4+ quarters out.
4. **GEICO underwriting profit fell ~35% YoY**, a concrete operating soft spot whose trajectory must stabilize before conviction improves.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Berkshire resumes material buybacks (>$5B/quarter), confirming management sees the stock below intrinsic value.
- GEICO loss ratio improves back below ~70% with stabilizing severity.
- Price pulls back toward ~$440–450 on no fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Kraft Heinz impairment combined with a second consecutive quarter of GEICO underwriting deterioration.
- An adverse PacifiCorp mediation outcome materially above the ~$2.9B accrual.
- Evidence Abel is overpaying for acquisitions (a second deal at a >20% premium with weak return economics).

## Conclusion

Berkshire Hathaway remains one of the highest-quality, most resilient balance sheets in the market, and the Abel transition is going more smoothly than skeptics feared: the new CEO is deploying capital decisively, has put his own money into the stock, and earned Buffett's public endorsement on his first major deal. The PacifiCorp appellate reversal removed a genuine tail risk. None of that changes the fact that the stock trades at ~1.45x book with management itself not buying, current-year estimates cut sharply over 90 days, GEICO margins under pressure, and no near-term catalyst. The franchise is worth owning; the entry price offers only modest upside to the $520 consensus mean. We rate BRK-B a HOLD and would become more constructive on a pullback toward ~1.3x book or evidence that Abel's deployment is generating excess returns.
