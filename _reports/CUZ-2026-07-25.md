**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cousins Properties is a self-managed REIT concentrated in Class A "lifestyle office" assets across seven Sun Belt markets, with Austin (~36% of NOI) and Atlanta (~31%) dominating the portfolio. The company runs the strongest balance sheet in the public office-REIT complex: investment-grade rated, 87% fixed-rate debt, ~0.89x debt/equity, and an FFO payout ratio near 45%. The core thesis is a bifurcation play: tenants are consolidating into newer, amenity-rich, well-located buildings while commodity and Gateway-market office deteriorates, and Cousins owns the winning side of that split. Q1 2026 delivered the clearest operational confirmation to date: cash-basis second-generation re-leasing spreads jumped to +15.2% (from +3.5% for full-year 2025), and management is pointing to a >1M SF lease pipeline and reaccelerating Sun Belt corporate in-migration.

The problem is price. The stock has recovered 48% from its February 2026 low of $21.03 to $32.15, sitting essentially at its 52-week high ($32.35) and above the analyst mean target of $32.42. The February collapse, when CUZ traded in the low-$20s while management repurchased 3.9M shares at $23.36, was the buying opportunity, and it has been realized. On forward fundamentals the setup is now balanced rather than asymmetric: same-property cash NOI growth of ~1–2% is being partly consumed by interest expense that rose 30% in FY25 and 23% in Q1'26 as ~3.8% legacy private notes roll into ~5% coupons. FFO per share grew only 5.6% in 2025 ($2.84 vs $2.69), and Q1'26 FFO was flat year-over-year at $0.73.

We rate CUZ a HOLD on an income basis. This is the highest-quality name in a structurally challenged sector, and the 4.1% dividend is comfortably covered with retained cash for reinvestment. But at a premium to trophy-office peers with modest per-share growth, the re-rating is largely complete. We would re-engage on a pullback toward the high-$20s or on evidence that the re-leasing spread and pipeline convert into accelerating same-store cash NOI. Score trajectory has improved steadily (4.3 → 5.7 → 5.8 across three prior reports); our 5.5 reflects a modest step back on a less favorable entry point after the run, not on fundamental deterioration.

## Company Overview

Cousins Properties (NYSE: CUZ) is a fully integrated, self-managed Georgia REIT focused exclusively on Class A office in Austin, Atlanta, Charlotte, Tampa, Phoenix, Dallas, and Nashville. As of Q1 2026 the portfolio comprised ~22.0M SF of office plus ~974K SF of other space, with substantially all operations flowing through operating partnership CPLP (Cousins owns >99%). It is a lean operation: 351 employees, an executive team averaging 15 years of tenure, and CEO Colin Connolly serving as both President/CEO and chief operating decision-maker.

The strategy rests on three pillars: opportunistic acquisitions, selective development, and disposition of non-core assets, all backed by a deliberately low-leverage balance sheet that lets management buy counter-cyclically. Market concentration is meaningful: Austin (53% technology tenants) and Atlanta together represent roughly two-thirds of NOI. Submarket-industry concentrations run through Charlotte (19% banking/financial) and Tampa (25% biotech/health science). A charter provision caps any single holder at 3.9% of stock to preserve REIT status, which can inhibit a control premium.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO.** FY2025 revenue was $993.8M, up 16% from $856.8M, but the growth is almost entirely inorganic, driven by the December 2024 Sail Tower/Vantage South End acquisitions (~$838M combined) and July 2025's The Link in Dallas ($218M). The metric that matters for a REIT, FFO per share, was $2.84 in FY2025, up from $2.69 in FY2024 and $2.62 in 2023. GAAP net income to common fell to $40.5M from $46.0M, pressured by higher interest expense and $14.3M of impairments; this GAAP figure is depressed by $415M of real-estate D&A and is not the right lens for the business.

**Organic growth is modest.** Same-property NOI grew +2.3% on a straight-line basis but only +0.9% on a cash basis for FY2025, with Q1 2026 at +1.4% cash. The headline revenue jump masks low-single-digit organic momentum.

**Leasing is the operational bright spot.** The company leased 2.1M SF in 2025 at +3.5% cash-basis second-generation spreads, accelerating to +15.2% cash (+28.7% straight-line) in Q1 2026. The portfolio was 90.7% leased at YE2025 (down slightly from 91.6%), with Q4'25 economic occupancy of 88.3%; the gap between leased and occupied reflects a signed-not-occupied backlog that provides a forward revenue tailwind. Leasing capex remains heavy at ~$11.90/SF total, with TIs of $7–10/SF on new leases, real cash that erodes headline net-effective rents.

**Balance sheet.** This is genuinely the sector's strongest. Debt was ~$3.3B at YE2025 (growing to $3.77B by Q1'26 after the 300 South Tryon acquisition) against $4.68B of stockholders' equity for a D/E of ~0.89x. 87% of debt is fixed-rate; weighted-average maturity is ~4.1 years. The company achieved investment-grade ratings in April 2024, which tightened borrowing spreads. Liquidity is ample after the April 2026 recast to a $1.2B revolver maturing April 2031 (from $1.0B/2027), with ~$793M available. The Altman Z-Score of 1.53 that mechanically flags "distress" is a false signal for a REIT: the model penalizes high asset bases and low asset turnover by design, and CUZ's leverage metrics are, per management, among the strongest in public office REITs. Piotroski signals (positive NI, ROA, OCF; OCF > NI) are all healthy.

**Cash flow and coverage.** Operating cash flow was $402M in FY25, roughly flat YoY. Dividends of $215.8M were paid (~$1.28/share annualized, $0.32/quarter), an FFO payout ratio of ~45% that leaves substantial retained cash for reinvestment and buybacks.

**The interest-expense swing factor.** Every refinancing rolls ~3.8–3.9% legacy private notes into ~5% new coupons (the Feb 2026 2033 notes priced at 4.875% with a ~5.0% YTM). Two term loans ($500M total) and two mortgages mature in 2026. This rising financing cost is the primary reason FFO/share growth stays modest despite strong leasing headlines: organic NOI gains are being partly absorbed by the debt line.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is two-part: bought and organic. To date, revenue growth has been acquired through accretive purchases funded with IG-rated debt (Sail Tower, Vantage, The Link, 300 South Tryon) rather than organically generated. That is a legitimate strategy for a well-capitalized REIT buying below replacement cost, but it caps the durability of the top-line trend since it depends on continued access to cheap capital and available quality assets.

The organic engine is showing early signs of life. The re-leasing spread move quarter-over-quarter is the single most encouraging operational data point, and the signed-not-occupied backlog provides visibility into a near-term revenue tailwind. Management's characterization of the lease pipeline and reaccelerating Sun Belt in-migration, while unaudited, is consistent with the pricing-power move in the leasing data.

Estimate revisions are mixed and thinly covered. The next-year EPS consensus rose to $0.40 from $0.30 (+33% over 90 days, single analyst), while current-year estimates were cut sharply (-69% over 90 days), a distortion driven by GAAP EPS being buffeted by the Q1'26 $36.6M One Eleven Congress impairment, not by FFO deterioration. The FFO story remains intact; the GAAP EPS line is not the operative metric. On balance, growth prospects are positive but gradual: low-single-digit same-store cash NOI, offset by a rising interest line, with the leasing move providing upside optionality that has not yet flowed through to reported FFO/share.

## Valuation Assessment

CUZ does not screen on P/E; the trailing figure is negative and meaningless given real-estate D&A. On the operative metric, FFO of $2.84, the stock trades at ~11.3x price/FFO. Against the office-REIT peer set the multiple looks reasonable to slightly rich given CUZ's superior quality: peer P/E medians are noisy (KRC 21.1x, CDP 25.0x, HIW 36.2x, VNO 10.5x, SLG negative), but on a quality-adjusted basis CUZ commands a deserved premium for its balance sheet and portfolio.

The core valuation issue is the entry point, not the business. The window when CUZ traded at a clear discount to NAV, the period when management itself repurchased shares at $23.36, has closed. The subsequent move to $32 validated the buyback call and, in doing so, removed the margin of safety.

We are deliberately skeptical of the temptation to call a REIT "cheap" simply because trailing multiples look compressed; in this sector, compressed multiples frequently reflect structural impairment rather than mispricing, and cheapness alone has historically been a poor buy signal. The independent catalyst that would justify a high valuation score, a specific reason the market is underpricing the stock today, is now largely spent. Analyst targets (median $33.50, high $35.00) offer only ~1–9% upside, and with the stock already at consensus and trading at a full multiple relative to its own history, we do not treat those targets as floors; at this price they reflect the same recovery optimism embedded in the shares. Downside support comes from the 4.1% dividend yield and the well-covered payout, which anchor the low-$27s (the analyst low target of $27.00). The risk/reward is balanced, so we assign a below-neutral valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Cousins is the largest office landlord in metro Atlanta and a leading Sun Belt trophy-office owner, and third-party coverage consistently frames it as a beneficiary of the flight-to-quality bifurcation. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory: the cash re-leasing spread and CEO commentary on the pipeline are concrete evidence the thesis is working, and CUZ's investment-grade balance sheet gives it a structural cost-of-capital advantage over more leveraged peers (SL Green at -20.4x P/E, Vornado at 10.5x with higher Gateway exposure) when competing for acquisitions.

Two caveats temper the score. First, growth to date has been purchased rather than organically won; the competitive win is showing up in the acquisition market, not yet decisively in same-store cash NOI. Second, the One Eleven Congress impairment in Austin, the company's largest and most tech-heavy market, signals intra-market bifurcation even within Cousins' own portfolio, a reminder that "trophy Sun Belt office" is not monolithic and that reported acquisition-fueled growth is partly offset by selling non-core assets below book. The competitive moat is real and improving at the margin, but the trajectory is gradual and the sector backdrop remains secularly challenged by hybrid work.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** Top-20 tenants represent 38.6% of annualized rent and the single largest tenant 8.9%. A tech downturn in Austin or financial-sector stress in Charlotte would hit disproportionately. 35% of occupied SF carries early-termination options.

**Sector secular headwinds.** Even trophy office faces WFH/hybrid demand pressure, rising insurance costs (Tampa/Houston windstorm deductibles of 2–5% of building value), and the risk that the flight-to-quality thesis stalls. These are permanently priced sector features, not novel risks, but they cap the terminal multiple.

**Interest-rate/refinancing.** The 2026 maturity concentration refinances at ~5%+ against ~3.8% legacy coupons, which keeps this a persistent drag on FFO/share. This is the clearest identifiable headwind to per-share growth over the next 12–18 months.

**Impairment cadence.** $14.3M in FY25 followed by $36.6M on One Eleven Congress in Q1'26 (driving a GAAP net loss of $(0.15)/share, non-cash and FFO-excluded). This bears monitoring as a signal that even in a strong market some assets sell below carrying value.

**Joint-venture/development.** The 50%-owned Neuhoff JV (Nashville) is in lease-up with a construction loan maturing September 2026 (one 12-month extension). Cousins loaned its JV partner $19.6M at SOFR+625bp to fund the partner's own equity contribution, a sign of partner capital stress and a near-term item to track. JV losses widened to $8.2M in FY25 from $2.8M.

**Governance quirk.** The 3.9% ownership cap can inhibit a control premium.

On balance, the idiosyncratic risks are real but largely known and priced. The buyback and IG balance sheet provide downside cushion. We score risk factors just above the midpoint: no imminent binary catalyst, but the concentration and refinancing drag are genuine and not fully offset by recovery upside at this price.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from a distorted 1,111% reading on June 18 to a normalized 92.8% by July 25 as the price rose 13.7%. The prior figure was almost certainly a thin-options data artifact and should be treated as non-informative. The normalized read carries no strong directional signal; there is no elevated put skew or unusual positioning flagging informed downside bets ahead of a catalyst. This is consistent with a stock that has re-rated on improving fundamentals without a looming binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$37–39).** The re-leasing spread proves durable and flows into accelerating same-store cash NOI toward 3–4%; the pipeline converts; signed-not-occupied backlog lifts economic occupancy; and Sun Belt corporate in-migration reaccelerates as management describes. FFO/share reaches ~$3.00+ and the market pays ~12.5x on improved organic growth, with the well-covered dividend supporting the floor. Continued accretive acquisitions funded at the IG cost of capital, plus buyback support ($410M remaining authorization), compound per-share value.

**Base case (~$33–34).** Organic cash NOI grows ~1.5–2%, partly offset by the rising interest line; FFO/share grows low-single-digit to ~$2.90–2.95; the multiple holds around ~11.5x. Total return is roughly the 4.1% dividend plus modest FFO growth, mid-single-digit total return over 12 months, matching analyst median.

**Bear case (~$27).** A tech-sector slowdown pressures Austin occupancy, refinancing at 5%+ coupons drags FFO/share flat or lower, further asset impairments surface, and the flight-to-quality thesis stalls. The multiple compresses back toward the low-$27s where the dividend yield (~4.7%) provides support. This is a ~16% drawdown from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: $33.50.** ~11.5x price/FFO on ~$2.92 FFO/share = ~$33.50, roughly +4% plus the 4.1% dividend for a ~8% total return. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and aligns with the analyst median.
- **Bull case target: $38.** ~12.7x on ~$3.00 FFO if the re-leasing move converts to accelerating same-store NOI. +18% from current.
- **Bear case target: $27.** ~-16% from current, where the ~4.7% dividend yield provides support.
- **Upside/downside from current ($32.15):** +4% to base, +18% to bull, -16% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Same-property cash NOI growth accelerates above 3% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the re-leasing spread is flowing through.
2. A pullback toward the high-$20s that restores a discount to NAV.
3. Evidence the pipeline is converting to signed leases lifting economic occupancy above 90%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Austin occupancy or tech-tenant retention deteriorates materially, or a second sizable Austin impairment surfaces.
2. FFO/share guidance turns flat-to-negative as refinancing costs outrun NOI growth.
3. Dividend coverage weakens (FFO payout ratio rising above ~65%) or a Neuhoff JV complication forces a capital call/write-down.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the Q3 2026 close of the One Eleven Congress sale; the September 2026 Neuhoff construction-loan maturity is a discrete monitoring point.

## Conclusion

Cousins Properties is the best-run, best-capitalized owner of Sun Belt trophy office, and its management has demonstrated genuine capital-allocation discipline, repurchasing shares at $23.36 while simultaneously funding a $317.5M acquisition, then watching the stock rise to $32. The re-leasing spread move and the well-covered dividend make this an ownable income vehicle with real upside optionality on a Sun Belt recovery.

But the asymmetry that existed in February, when the stock traded at a clear discount to NAV and management was voting with the balance sheet, has largely closed. Near the 52-week high and above consensus, the shares price in a good deal of the recovery while FFO/share growth stays modest as rising interest expense consumes low-single-digit organic gains. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$20s or on hard evidence that the leasing momentum is converting into accelerating same-store cash NOI. Overall score: 5.5.
