**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest U.S. natural gas compression services provider by fleet horsepower, operating as a pure-play, domestic midstream infrastructure business. Roughly 85% of revenue comes from contract operations, where Archrock owns and operates its own compressor fleet for a fixed monthly fee, and the remaining 15% from aftermarket services on customer-owned equipment. The franchise has real structural attractions: 95%+ fleet utilization sustained across cycles, demand tied to production and throughput volumes rather than drilling activity, and exposure to the highest-growth basins (Permian, Eagle Ford). Record U.S. gas production, growing LNG exports, pipeline flows to Mexico, and rising gas-fired power demand from data centers all point to structurally higher compression intensity per unit of production over time.

The constraint is the price and the trajectory. AROC has appreciated roughly 60% over the trailing twelve months and now trades near $37 against a 52-week range of $21–$42, at about 20x forward EPS ($2.00 consensus). The two large acquisitions that powered 2022–2025 revenue from $846M to $1,490M (TOPS 2024, NGCS 2025) have run their course; management's reaffirmed 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guide of $865M–$915M straddles the $901M delivered in 2025, implying growth has flattened. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have fallen 3.4% over 90 days, the Q1 2026 print missed by 13%, and 2025 GAAP net income was flattered by roughly $82M of nonrecurring items (asset-sale gains plus a tax-audit benefit). The estimate-revision signal, the most reliable forward indicator in our framework, is pointing the wrong way.

We rate AROC a HOLD (growth_hold), continuing a four-report streak but with a declining score. This is a good business at a full price with fading near-term momentum, a CFO transition of uncertain quality, and options markets pricing near-term stress (near-term IV in backwardation at 43.8%). The compression franchise merits ownership at the right entry; the current entry does not offer the asymmetry required to upgrade.

## Company Overview

Archrock runs two segments. **Contract Operations** (~85% of revenue) involves owning, installing, operating, and maintaining a fleet of natural gas compressors under fixed monthly fee arrangements, with the customer supplying fuel gas. At year-end 2025 the fleet stood at 5,319 units / 4,788K horsepower, average age 10 years, with 74% classified as large-horsepower (>1,000 hp), the category aligned with Permian associated-gas and pad-drilling applications. **Aftermarket Services** (~15%) supplies parts, maintenance, overhaul, and reconfiguration for customer-owned compression.

Compression is a must-run service essential to gas gathering and processing (~60% of fleet) and gas-lift oil production (~40%). Because demand tracks production and throughput volumes rather than rig counts, the business is structurally less commodity-cyclical than most oilfield services. Archrock serves roughly 300 contract customers, with about three-quarters of operating horsepower in the Permian and Eagle Ford. Competitive strengths include the largest large-HP fleet in the industry, geographic scale and density, a strong safety record (0.22 TRIR), and long-standing customer relationships. Management itself characterizes the business as having "low barriers to entry" and being "highly competitive," candor worth weighing against the "infrastructure" framing.

The multi-year growth record is strong but acquisition-heavy:

Margin expansion is real and durable: contract-operations adjusted gross margin rose from 67% to 73% on higher pricing and scale, reflecting a genuinely tight compression market. But 2025 GAAP net income of $322M was flattered by roughly $47M of asset-sale gains and a ~$35M net sales/use-tax audit settlement, both nonrecurring. Normalized earnings are healthy but meaningfully below the headline, and the 2026 EBITDA guide confirms that underlying growth is moderating after the acquisition surge.

The balance sheet is capital-intensive and levered, appropriately for the model. Total assets are $4.35B, of which PP&E is $3.66B; long-term debt is $2.41B against $1.49B of equity, giving D/E of 1.92. The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 places AROC nominally in the "distress zone," but this is a misleading signal for a fee-based infrastructure operator: the Z-model penalizes heavy fixed assets, low asset turnover, and the legacy accumulated deficit (-$2.26B). The operating reality is $600M+ of annual operating cash flow, 95%+ utilization, no near-term maturities, and covenant compliance (Total Debt/EBITDA ≤5.25x). The Piotroski score of 5/9 confirms positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF exceeding net income.

Debt management has been sound: in January 2026 Archrock issued $800M of 6.0% senior notes due 2034 and used the proceeds to redeem the $800M 6.25% 2028 Notes on April 1, terming out maturities and lowering cost of debt (revolver rate fell to 5.1% after a December 2025 amendment cut the margin 25bps). This proactive liability management supports the infrastructure thesis. Q1 2026 delivered revenue of $373.8M (+7.7% YoY), net income of $73.8M, EPS of $0.41, and OCF of $185.9M, though aftermarket revenue fell 8% on softer major-maintenance demand and growth capex fell sharply to $64.9M from $139.4M a year earlier.

FCF conversion is the honest concern: 2025 FCF of $120M against $322M of net income is a ~37% conversion rate, reflecting the capital intensity of a fleet that must be continually maintained and grown. This is normal for the model but caps the "cash machine" narrative; the dividend and buyback are funded, but there is limited excess FCF to accelerate returns without either more leverage or slower fleet growth.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand backdrop is favorable. U.S. gas production hit a record 107.4 Bcf/d in 2025, LNG exports are growing 9–10% annually, pipeline exports to Mexico continue to rise, and gas-fired power demand from data centers adds a new secular vector. Aging fields with rising gas-to-oil ratios structurally increase compression intensity per unit of production, and Archrock's large-HP fleet is positioned for the fastest-growing applications. Multi-year Caterpillar engine lead times (~160 weeks) constrain the entire industry's ability to add supply, supporting near-term pricing power and utilization.

The near-term picture is less exciting than the theme. Management's 2026 EBITDA guide is essentially flat versus 2025, the acquisition-driven step-change in revenue has ended, and the growth constraint has shifted from demand to equipment availability: Q1 2026 growth capex of $64.9M was less than half the prior-year figure precisely because new compressors cannot be sourced quickly. Estimate revisions confirm the slowdown: current-year EPS consensus has fallen from $2.07 to $2.00 over 90 days (-3.4%), and the current-quarter estimate is down 2.2% over 30 days. The lone bright spot is next-year EPS, revised up 2.5% over 30 days to $2.40, suggesting the market expects re-acceleration as supply chains ease. The estimate-revision reversal that would argue for a BUY is absent; near-term consensus is broadly falling, not accelerating.

We score growth prospects at 5.5. The long-run compression-intensity thesis is credible and durable, but the 12-month growth path is supply-gated and flat, and the earnings-revision trend is negative. This is a downgrade continuation from the 7.5 seen earlier in the year, and current data confirms rather than reverses the deceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.14, AROC trades at roughly 20x trailing and forward EPS (~$1.83 trailing / $2.00 forward consensus). The dividend yield is 2.28% ($0.22/quarter, raised from $0.19). Against the Oil & Gas Equipment & Services peer set, the headline P/E looks reasonable: peer median P/E is 55.0, and closest comp KGS (Kodiak Gas Services, the other pure-play compression name) trades at 88.7x. But those peer multiples are distorted by depressed trailing earnings at NOV, WHD, and KGS; they are not clean growth-adjusted comps. WFRD at 15.8x is the only peer near AROC's multiple, and it carries a -29% discount to its 52-week high on its own set of issues.

The relevant question is asymmetry today, not multiple relative to distorted peers. AROC's forward P/E of ~20x on roughly flat 2026 earnings implies a PEG well above 1.0 on near-term growth; the multiple is not cheap for the growth being delivered. Normalized 2025 earnings (stripping the ~$82M of nonrecurring items) would put the effective trailing multiple higher still. The stock sits within 12% of its 52-week high after a 60% twelve-month run, and while prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, the current price fully reflects the compression tailwind that is now well understood by the market.

Analyst targets bracket the stock at $40–$46 (mean $42.5, median $43.0), implying 8–24% upside. On a stock with rising estimates I would treat those targets as floors; here, with near-term estimates falling and the stock trading above 20x on flat earnings, the guardrail applies: the targets reflect the same compression-tailwind extrapolation embedded in the price and are not independent confirmation of undervaluation.

We score valuation at 4.5. This is not a value trap in the structural sense (the cash flows are real), but the multiple is full for the growth on offer. Our framework treats a "cheap" screen as anti-predictive absent a catalyst; AROC is not even screening cheap on a normalized basis, and there is no identified near-term catalyst to re-rate it higher.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. It operates the largest large-HP fleet in the industry, benefits from scale and geographic density in the Permian and Eagle Ford, and enjoys a supply-side moat from multi-year OEM engine lead times that make it difficult for competitors to add capacity quickly. High utilization and the ability to raise contract rates in a tight market are evidence of pricing power that has fed the margin expansion.

Against this, management's own disclosures flag "low barriers to entry" and a "highly competitive" market, with customers retaining the option to self-supply compression. Customer consolidation increases buyer power over time, and contract structure is shorter than the infrastructure label implies: initial terms run 12–36 months (up to 60 for the largest units) and then convert to month-to-month, cancelable on 30 days' notice. Remaining performance obligations are only $864.6M; cash-flow stability rests on high renewal rates in a tight-supply environment rather than long-dated take-or-pay contracts. Customer concentration is moderate (top 5 = 35% of revenue; two customers = ~30% of trade receivables). Aftermarket weakness in Q1 2026 bears watching as a possible early sign of customer capex restraint.

We score competitive position at 6.0: a leader with a genuine but not impregnable moat, gaining incremental advantage from the supply constraint but exposed to a structurally competitive market.

## Risk Assessment

The principal idiosyncratic risks are: (1) the flat 2026 EBITDA guide leaving little room for upside surprise, with negative near-term estimate revisions; (2) leverage of $2.4B (D/E 1.92) in a higher-for-longer rate environment, though refinancing risk is well-managed with no near-term maturities and a lowered cost of debt; (3) short, cancelable contract terms that make the "infrastructure" cash-flow stability contingent on renewal rates; (4) the CFO transition (see Management Quality); and (5) customer concentration and the ever-present self-supply option. The regulatory backdrop is currently a tailwind: EPA methane deadlines pushed to 2027, waste-emissions charge delayed to 2034, SEC climate rules suspended, reducing a historical headwind, though state-level rules (Colorado, New Mexico) persist.

The Altman Z-Score will trip naive distress screens but is not a genuine solvency signal for this model; investors should not confuse it with liquidity risk. The 2025 earnings-quality caveat is the more relevant flag: reported earnings trajectory overstates underlying momentum.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is a near-term caution flag. Near-term ATM IV sits at 43.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.5%, a rich 12.3-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (43.8% near-term vs 40.7% long-term), pricing near-term stress, likely around the next earnings print. IV has risen 8.3 points since late June even as the price fell 5.5%, indicating rising uncertainty rather than complacency. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.14 and 0.06), so there is no aggressive bearish positioning; but the elevated near-term IV and implied ±11.4% move argue against paying up right before a catalyst. Short interest is modest at 6.1% of float with 3.3 days to cover, so squeeze dynamics are not a factor either way.

We score risk factors at 5.5: leverage, contract-term structure, and the CFO change are real idiosyncratic risks, partially offset by the favorable regulatory rollback and well-managed debt maturity profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The compression-intensity thesis compounds: record gas production, LNG export growth, Mexican pipeline demand, and data-center power load drive rising compression demand for years, while engine lead times keep supply tight and pricing firm. Next-year EPS estimates ($2.40) prove correct as supply constraints ease and Archrock deploys pre-ordered capacity, restoring double-digit earnings growth. Margins hold at 73%+, the balance sheet delivers as the fee-based cash generator it is, and the dividend continues rising. On $2.40 next-year EPS at a 22x multiple, the stock reaches ~$53. A more modest re-rating to the analyst high of $46 (~19x on $2.40) is the near-term bull target, roughly +24% from current.

**Bear Case.** The acquisition surge is over, 2026 EBITDA is flat, and aftermarket softness spreads into contract operations as customers pull back capex in a slowing macro. Negative estimate revisions continue, the market de-rates a flat-growth business from 20x toward 15x, and the nonrecurring items in 2025 make the growth deceleration look worse than the guide implies. The CFO transition introduces execution uncertainty. On $1.90 normalized EPS at 15x, the stock falls to ~$28–$29, roughly -22% to -24% from current. The higher-for-longer rate path pressures the levered, capital-intensive model further.

The base case sits in between: the franchise is sound, the tailwind is real, but the near-term earnings path is flat and the price already reflects most of the good news. We see ~$40 as the most likely 12-month outcome: 20x on a modest recovery toward $2.00–$2.05, roughly +8% plus the 2.3% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive multi-year structural view but limited near-term asymmetry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the -3.4% 90-day trend) alongside a Q2/Q3 2026 print that confirms re-acceleration and beats the EBITDA range toward the high end.
- A pullback to the low-$30s (≤16x forward EPS) that restores 2:1 upside/downside; the compression franchise is attractive at that entry.
- Evidence that supply-chain easing is translating into accelerated fleet growth (growth capex re-approaching prior-year levels) with contracts already secured.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Contract-operations utilization or pricing rolls over, or aftermarket weakness spreads into the core segment, signaling customer capex retrenchment.
- A guidance cut to 2026 EBITDA, or leverage rising toward the 5.25x covenant on debt-funded M&A at unattractive prices.
- The CFO transition produces a controls or reporting stumble.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings report, where the near-term options-implied stress will resolve and the full-year trajectory becomes clearer. The near-term IV backwardation supports waiting through that print before adding.

## Conclusion

Archrock is a well-run, structurally advantaged compression franchise with genuine tailwinds from record gas production, LNG exports, and emerging data-center power demand. The cash flows are real, the balance sheet is managed prudently, and the "distress-zone" Z-Score is a false alarm. But the investment case is constrained by the same facts that have driven our score lower across four reports: the acquisition-fueled growth surge has flattened, 2026 EBITDA is guided roughly flat, near-term estimate revisions are negative, 2025 earnings were flattered by nonrecurring items, and the stock trades at a full ~20x forward multiple within 12% of its 52-week high after a 60% run. Layer on a CFO transition of uncertain quality and options markets pricing near-term stress, and the risk/reward is close to balanced: modest upside to the analyst range ($40–$46) against a credible bear case toward $28–$29.

We maintain HOLD (growth_hold, overall score 5.4), reflecting a good business we would own at a better price. The path to a BUY runs through either a positive estimate-revision reversal with a confirming earnings beat, or a pullback into the low-$30s that restores the asymmetry the current price does not offer. This is not a short, given no dated catalyst for material downside and stable fundamentals, but it is not a chase either.
