## Executive Summary

ExxonMobil's second quarter was the strongest reported result in four years: net income attributable to shareholders of $14,525M ($3.48 basic and diluted EPS) on revenue and other income of $116,017M, up 42.3% year over year. First-half operating cash flow of $32,260M against $12,997M of PP&E additions produced roughly $19.3B of free cash flow, which covered $8,633M of dividends and $10,007M of repurchases with about $600M to spare. That is a material change from FY2025, when distributions of approximately $36.8B exceeded free cash flow of $23.6B by roughly $13B, funded largely by cutting cash from $23.0B to $10.7B. The distribution program became self-funding again because prices and refining margins recovered, not because policy changed, which is the correct causal reading and the crux of the risk.

The composition of the improvement matters more than its size. In the Energy Products earnings waterfall, estimated timing effects added $2,560M year over year on favorable derivative mark-to-market, and ExxonMobil defines timing effects as impacts "expected to unwind in subsequent periods." Management's own environmental description is that crude prices sat "within the 10-year historical range," natural gas was "elevated above the 10-year average," and industry refining margins were "sharply above the 10-year historical range due to unprecedented global refining capacity reductions." Meanwhile $2,475M of identified items (impairments and financial reserves) offset the timing benefit in the quarter, so clean Q2 earnings may be close to reported for two offsetting one-off reasons, which is not the same as clean.

The idiosyncratic risk is unusual and, on my reading, underpriced. Q2 disclosed segment drags from Middle East disruption totalled roughly $1.48B (Upstream $1,060M, Energy Products $310M, Specialty Products $110M), and Asian natural gas volumes fell 60% (3,206 → 1,274 mmcfd). Against those losses, Upstream price effects added $4,650M and Energy Products margin effects $3,180M. ExxonMobil is currently a net beneficiary of the disruption that impairs its own largest non-Americas asset base. A Hormuz reopening restores the volume earnings while removing a price-and-margin uplift several times larger. At $155.44 (11.9% below the March 30 high of $176.41 but 12.8% above the June 18 low of $137.81 and 39% above a year ago), that asymmetry is not compensated. Analyst consensus mean of $167.09 offers 7.5% upside; Morningstar's fair value of $156, reaffirmed August 3, sits at spot.

## Company Overview

ExxonMobil operates four reportable segments plus a Corporate and Financing cost center. First-half 2026 segment earnings: Upstream $13,664M, Energy Products $4,203M, Chemical Products $1,241M, Specialty Products $1,607M, Corporate and Financing $(2,007)M. Upstream contributed roughly 55% of segment earnings in the half, but Energy Products delivered the largest incremental dollar swing in the quarter (+$4,099M year over year, $1,366M → $5,465M).

The company defines its "advantaged assets" explicitly as Permian, Guyana and LNG. Advantaged volume growth added $1,140M to Upstream earnings in Q2 and $1,940M year to date, "mainly driven by Guyana and Permian growth." Cumulative structural cost savings reached $16.3B versus 2019, including $1.2B in the first half; cash operating expense excluding energy and production taxes was $44.1B in 2025 against $44.0B in 2019, with $15.1B of savings absorbing $4.9B of market inflation and $10.3B of activity growth. That is the most credible operating-execution datapoint in the filings.

On July 1, 2026, ExxonMobil Holdings Corporation, a Texas corporation, became the publicly traded parent through a 1-for-1 redomiciliation merger. Former Exxon Mobil Corporation shareholders hold identical numbers and percentage interests; the ticker, financial reporting basis and consolidated operations are unchanged. There is no economic dilution. Shareholder rights are now governed by the Texas Business Organizations Code rather than the New Jersey Business Corporation Act. I have not read the board's stated rationale; aligning state of incorporation with the Spring, Texas headquarters is a fully sufficient benign explanation, and the factual takeaway is that the governing legal regime for fiduciary-duty and books-and-records disputes changed.

Headcount fell from 62,000 (2023) to 61,000 (2024) to 58,000 regular employees at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**The cyclical arc.** Revenue moved $413.7B (2022) → $344.6B (2023) → $349.6B (2024) → $332.2B (2025). Net income attributable fell three consecutive years: $55.7B → $36.0B → $33.7B → $28.8B, a 48% cumulative decline. FY2025 net margin was 8.7% (28,844/332,238). Total assets and equity stepped up $77B and $59B respectively in 2024 on the equity-funded Pioneer acquisition.

**Leverage and solvency.** At June 30, 2026, total interest-bearing debt was $42.4B (notes and loans payable $10,139M plus long-term debt $32,229M) against cash of $10,588M and total equity of $266,111M. The company discloses debt/total capital of 13.7% and net debt/capital of 10.7%. Interest-bearing debt to shareholders' equity is 0.168 ($42.4B/$259.4B); a widely circulated 0.68 figure is total liabilities to equity ($182.4B/$266.6B), which is a different statement. Undrawn committed lines total $7.7B; commercial paper is the short-term balancing tool, with $5,500M of short-term debt reductions and $3,912M of commercial-paper additions in the half. Current ratio is 1.13, thin for a screen but normal for an integrated major with negative-cash-conversion trade dynamics.

Recomputing Altman Z from FY2025 inputs (working capital/assets 0.0246, retained earnings/assets 1.0746, EBIT/assets 0.0933, market equity/liabilities 3.496, sales/assets 0.7400) yields 4.68 with market equity and 3.44 substituting book equity. Either construction sits in the safe zone. Distress is not a live issue, and Z is in any case a weak discriminator for capital-intensive commodity producers. The debate here is earnings durability and capital allocation, not solvency.

**Cash flow lumpiness.** First-half operating cash flow was $32,260M, of which $23,555M came in Q2, implying roughly $8,705M in Q1. A Q2 run-rate annualizes to about $94B; the first-half run-rate to $64.5B; FY2025 actual was $52.0B. Working capital was a $3,857M drag in the half, with receivables rising from $44,562M to $60,558M and payables from $60,911M to $74,491M. Any forward cash-flow estimate must be built off normalized margins. The least distorted anchor is the first-half annualization of roughly $64.5B, and even that inherits favorable pricing.

**Depreciation escalation.** D&D rose from $19.0B (2019) to $26.0B (2025); first-half 2026 D&D of $15,460M is 31% above the prior-year $11,803M against 23% revenue growth. Management cites higher depreciation as reducing Upstream earnings $690M in the quarter and $1,510M year to date. This is the expected consequence of a larger, shorter-cycle unconventional base post-Pioneer, plus the $1,180M Energy Products impairment inside identified items. Mechanically it suppresses EPS, ROA and ROCE while leaving cash generation intact, which is why first-half operating cash flow grew $7.8B against a $3.9B increase in net income.

**Derivative footprint.** Gross derivative assets expanded to $19,923M from $7,456M at year-end 2025 (liabilities $19,836M from $7,037M), with $17,681M of counterparty netting. Note 6 discloses before-tax realized and unrealized losses of $2.3B on contracts held for trading purposes in the period ended June 30, 2026, against a $534M gain a year earlier. Net notional positions: long 20M bbl crude, short 55M bbl petroleum products, short 528M MMBTU natural gas. The short product and gas positions are economically consistent with a refiner-producer hedging output, but they mean a further spike in cracks or gas prices produces mark-to-market pain. The disclosed $2.3B trading loss and the +$2,560M favorable Energy Products timing swing have different scopes (trading book versus all unsettled derivatives and LIFO recognition differences) and I could not fully reconcile them from the filing text.

**Returns.** FY2025 ROE 11.1%, ROA 6.4%, rough ROCE ~9.8% — trough-year figures. The company's selected measure, cash flow from operations and asset sales, was $55.1B in 2025 versus $82.0B in 2022, $59.4B in 2023 and $60.0B in 2024, and reconciles to operating cash flow plus roughly $3.2B of asset sales.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth in the current period is price, not volume. Worldwide production fell 116 koebd year over year to 4,514 koebd in Q2: Asia liquids −154 kbd and Asia gas −1,932 mmcfd (roughly −322 koebd at 6:1), partly offset by U.S. liquids +159 kbd, U.S. gas +527 mmcfd and Canada/Other Americas +125 koebd. Refinery throughput fell 9.5% (3,562 vs 3,936 kbd), with Europe −16% and Asia Pacific −28% on scheduled maintenance and 2025 divestments (Singapore retail fuels, Mobil Argentina, France Product Solutions affiliates). ExxonMobil is capturing price on lower volume.

Structural growth is concentrated and real: record Permian production above 1.8 Mboe/d, Guyana ramping, Golden Pass LNG trains coming, and a $16.3B structural cost-savings run-rate with a stated $20B target by 2030. First-half advantaged volume growth of $1,940M nearly fully offset a 476 koebd Asian collapse, which is the growth engine working.

The estimate revision picture is the clearest forward signal and it is two-sided. Current-year consensus rose to $12.34 from $11.12 ninety days ago (+10.9%) and from $11.29 thirty days ago (+9.2%). Current-quarter estimates rose 10.7% over ninety days to $3.69. But next-year consensus is $10.58, 14% *below* the current year, and it fell 2.1% over thirty days even as the near quarters were marked up. Consensus is explicitly modelling mean reversion, and the thirty-day direction says the marginal revision is now negative where it matters for a twelve-month horizon. The company has beaten in each of the last four reported quarters (14.3%, 0.5%, 2.4%, 5.1% surprises), but Q2 2026 broke that streak modestly: GAAP EPS of $3.48 came in roughly 2.7% below consensus by one independent account, and adjusted earnings "fell slightly short" by another, with refinery maintenance cited as the cause, consistent with the disclosed −$280M base-volume and −$170M expense effects in Energy Products.

R&D of $1,007M in 2025 is 0.3% of revenue, which sizes the low-carbon portfolio honestly. Item 1A is unusually candid that CCS, hydrogen, lower-emission fuels, Proxxima, carbon materials, low-carbon data centers and lithium "will depend in part on the development of stable and supportive government policies and markets," and that "without supportive policies and the innovations they drive, net zero will remain out of reach." Treat this as a call option, not a segment.

**Growth Prospects: 5.5.** Genuine advantaged-asset volume growth and cost execution, against negative aggregate production, negative next-year consensus revisions, and revenue growth that is predominantly price.

## Valuation Assessment

**Reproducible arithmetic at $155.44.** Market capitalization $637.4B (4,112M shares). Enterprise value ≈ $637.4B + $42.4B debt − $10.6B cash = $669.2B ($675.9B including $6.7B of noncontrolling interests).

- Trailing twelve-month GAAP EPS ≈ $8.06 (first-half 2026 $4.47 plus second-half 2025 $3.59) → **19.3x TTM**. The supplied data carries an internal inconsistency: a trailing P/E of 20.04 implies EPS of $7.76, while the supplied EPS field of $6.70 approximates FY2025. I use the derived figure.
- FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS $12.34 → **12.6x**.
- FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS $10.58 → **14.7x**.
- FY2025 EBITDA proxy $67.9B (EBIT $41.9B + D&D $26.0B) → **EV/EBITDA 9.9x**; first-half-2026 annualized proxy $84.7B → **8.0x** (inherits the same timing distortion).
- Dividend $1.03/quarter, up 4.0% year over year, $4.12 annualized → **2.65% yield**. The stated $20B 2026 repurchase plan → **3.14%**. Combined shareholder yield **5.78%**.

**Peer context.** Median sector P/E is 19.6x (CVX 32.3, COP 19.6, VLO 18.6, MPC 17.0, WMB 31.4), median yield 2.8%. On trailing GAAP, XOM at 19.3x sits at the peer median with a slightly below-median yield. On 2026 forward it is materially cheaper. But CVX's 32x reflects trough earnings and WMB's 31x is a midstream multiple; the refiners at 17-19x are on cyclically inflated margins just as XOM is. The peer set does not deliver a clean discount argument.

**The normalization question decides this.** FY2026 consensus of $12.34 embeds refining margins management describes as sharply above the ten-year range and a chemical environment still below the bottom of its range. Suppose refining reverts into range and crude settles toward the $85-95 that a Hormuz reopening would imply: FY2025's $6.71 GAAP EPS is the relevant floor reference, adjusted upward for Permian and Guyana volume growth, $16.3B of structural savings and a 3.5% smaller share count, call it $8.00-9.00 of normalized power. At $155.44 that is 17.3-19.4x normalized, which is full. Consensus 2027 of $10.58 sits between the peak and that normalized range, and at 14.7x the stock is priced for consensus being roughly right.

Analyst dispersion confirms genuine disagreement rather than a mispricing: low $130, high $185, mean $167.09, median $168.00. Morningstar's $156 fair value equals spot. RBC holds at $180, Argus buys at $169, TD Cowen cut its target by $17 in early July. The mean implies 7.5% upside, respectable with a 2.65% dividend on top, but not the asymmetry a BUY requires in a sector where selection has historically added little above beta.

Second-quarter repurchases averaged $149.30 (April $152.70, May $151.92, June $143.75), below today's price, so buybacks executed at a discount to current levels. That is capital-allocation credit, not evidence the current price is cheap.

**Valuation: 5.5.** The forward multiple looks inexpensive only against a peak-cycle numerator. A low multiple on abnormal earnings is the classic setup where cheapness sorts the wrong way.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is in a supply-shock regime rather than a demand boom, which determines who captures the profit pool. Management's framing puts refining margins sharply above range on capacity reductions, gas above average, crude within range and chemicals below range. That has shifted profits toward refining, and secondarily toward advantaged-feedstock chemicals.

ExxonMobil's downstream scale is currently its edge, which inverts the usual integrated-major critique. Energy Products delivered 35% of segment earnings in the quarter on a +$3,180M margin effect. Chemical Products added +$980M of Q2 margin from increased North America ethane feed advantage, against naphtha-based Asian competitors whose weakness the filing notes directly. The same integration that caps upside in an Upstream-led cycle is amplifying results in a refining-led one, and Morningstar's assessment that Exxon is "perhaps best positioned among peers to benefit from the currently strong refining margins" is corroborated by the segment mechanics.

Two trajectory concerns temper this. First, asset and counterparty concentration is *increasing*, not decreasing: Permian and Guyana grew while the Asian portfolio's gas output fell 60%, and Golden Pass is a joint venture in which QatarEnergy is the partner, compounding rather than diversifying the Qatar relationship. The revenue mix rotated toward trading flows (revenue outside ASC 606 scope rose to $68.3B of $197.7B, 35%, from 29%), which is not evidence of reduced concentration risk. Second, Item 1A identifies technology diffusion as an erosion risk to Exxon's own technical moat: AI and service-company technology "may enhance the competitiveness of firms that may not have the internal resources and capabilities of ExxonMobil, or reduce the need for resource-owning countries to partner with private-sector oil and gas companies." Management also flags state-owned enterprises pursuing strategic objectives "with less focus on financial returns." A narrow rather than wide moat characterization is consistent with the disclosures.

**Competitive Position: 6.5**, down from 8.5 across the trajectory. Cost execution and downstream positioning are strong; the geographic base is narrowing and management itself names a technology-diffusion threat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hormuz reversal asymmetry (dominant).** Quantified in the filings: roughly $1.48B of disclosed segment drags and ~476 koebd of Asian production lost in Q2, against $4,650M of Upstream price effect and $3,180M of Energy Products margin effect. Non-U.S. Upstream equity-affiliate income fell 48% to $682M. A Hormuz reopening restores the volumes and removes a far larger price-and-margin benefit. Morningstar's counter — that refining margins will prove stickier than crude and gas given global outages and depleted inventories needing rebuild — is a reasonable hypothesis, not established. External reporting through early August (Reuters, Bloomberg, WSJ) documents that Qatari gas exports through the Strait of Hormuz were halted amid the Iran conflict, QatarEnergy bought 33 U.S. spot LNG cargoes to backfill customers, Shell warned global LNG supply could contract, and Japan shifted power generation to coal; the filings do not confirm cause attribution. The sources confirm no normalization, and the decisive test is sustained uninterrupted commercial transit.

**2. Refining-margin mean reversion.** Management's own language ("unprecedented global refining capacity reductions," "sharply above the 10-year historical range") makes this the most probable single cause of a 2027 earnings step-down. Consensus already models it at −14%; the risk is that it is deeper.

**3. Political intervention.** [unverified] Rising integrated-major profits could invite policy intervention such as windfall taxes or price measures; this is not in the filings and should be sized as a possibility, not a forecast.

**4. Earnings-quality composition.** Roughly a third of the quarter's $7.4B year-over-year improvement traces to a $2,560M favorable timing swing management says unwinds, and gross derivative assets tripled to $19.9B. First-half timing effects were *negative* in both Upstream (−$870M) and Energy Products (−$770M), and first-half identified items were a −$3,181M drag, so the half is depressed by both while the quarter is flattered by one and depressed by the other.

**5. Distribution sustainability at trough prices.** At FY2025 conditions the $20B buyback plus roughly $17.5B of dividends runs about $13B ahead of free cash flow, and it was funded by halving cash from $23.0B to $10.7B. Cash is now $10.6B versus $31.5B two years ago. The dividend is the hard commitment; the buyback is the flexible lever. Remaining repurchase authorization fell from $13.5B in April to $10.2B at June 30 — at plan pace it is essentially consumed by year-end, making a new authorization an observable, low-drama catalyst.

**6. Litigation.** Climate proceedings by state and local governments are characterized by management as meritless with a material adverse outcome "remote." All Louisiana coastal-marsh-erosion claims were settled effective July 31, 2026, described as not material, with no admission of liability. The Q2 Upstream identified item of −$1,199M "from financial reserves" is plausibly related, but the filing does not make that link explicit.

**Unresolved items.** Year-end 2025 proved reserves and reserve life were not retrievable and remain unquantified — a direct input to terminal value. Form 4 data returned nothing, most plausibly a continuity artifact of the July 1 registrant change; no inference about insider behavior should be drawn in either direction. The content of a July 7, 2026 Reg FD 8-K is unknown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.7% against 25.0% one-year realized is rich by 9.7 points, at the 61st percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (34.7% near versus 32.3% at the long end). Backwardation persisting *after* the July 31 earnings release is the notable feature, since post-earnings volatility normally collapses. The residual is geopolitical event risk, not company event risk, and it corroborates my view that the Hormuz binary is the live variable. Skew is described as balanced at the near tenor (−0.4%); the six-month tenor prints −3.2%, which I report without a directional interpretation. Put/call volume of 1.42 against put/call open interest of 0.54 suggests recent flow is more defensive than the standing book. Unusual activity is two-sided: 590 contracts of August 14 $175 calls at 4.6x open interest (+12.9% out of the money) and 1,807 August 7 $152 puts at 4.5x. Short interest is 1.1% of float at 2.4 days to cover — no crowded positioning either way. The market is pricing a ±7.9% move by September 11.

**Risk Factors: 5.0.** A fortress balance sheet and negligible refinancing or covenant risk are offset by a specific, identifiable, asymmetric geopolitical reversal that the price does not appear to compensate, plus an unpriced political-intervention tail.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation earns credit on execution: 66.7M shares repurchased in the half at an average of $149.30, below the current price; share count down from 4,353M (12/31/2024) to 4,112M (6/30/2026), a 5.5% reduction over eighteen months entirely through treasury accumulation with issued shares unchanged at 8,019M; reported diluted EPS equals basic in every period, so there is no material dilution overhang; dividend raised 4.0%; first-half execution of $10.0B repurchases, $8.6B dividends and $13.0B capex tracking the stated $20B and $27-29B plans. The $16.3B cumulative structural cost reduction is the strongest single management datapoint available.

Two items warrant caution. FY2025 "Compensation Actually Paid" to the CEO rose 23% to $60.0M while net income fell 14% and cash flow from operations and asset sales fell 8%, though granted compensation fell 25% to $33.0M and the divergence is a mechanical consequence of revaluing long-dated unvested equity. Five-year relative performance is genuinely strong — an XOM total-return index of 356 against a peer index of 228. The Texas redomiciliation shifted shareholder rights to a forum generally regarded as more management-favorable with no economic dilution; a benign alignment rationale is equally consistent with the disclosed facts, and I make no claim about motive. Insider transaction data is unavailable, so the strongest forward-looking management signal is absent.

**Management Quality: 6.3.** Demonstrated cost and buyback execution; a governance-regime change and a pay-versus-results divergence; no insider confirmation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($185, +19.0%).** Refining margins prove structurally rather than cyclically elevated — global capacity reductions are permanent, inventories need rebuilding, and Morningstar's stickiness argument holds. Hormuz stays disrupted or reopens only partially, crude grinds in the $100-120 range, and FY2027 EPS holds near $12 rather than falling to $10.58. Permian above 1.8 Mboe/d, Guyana ramping and Golden Pass add volume as the Asian portfolio recovers. Structural savings advance toward the $20B target. At 15x $12.00 plus a 2.65% dividend, this reaches the analyst high of $185.

**Bear case ($115, −26.0%).** Sustained transit through Hormuz returns Brent to the $85-95 range while refining margins revert into their ten-year band. FY2027 EPS falls to $7.50-8.00. Asian volumes recover roughly $1.5B/quarter of earnings, against a several-times-larger loss of price and margin uplift. The $2.56B timing benefit unwinds. Distributions again exceed free cash flow by roughly $13B against $10.6B of cash, forcing a buyback reduction that removes the 3.1% repurchase yield. At 14x $8.00 the stock is $112; add a windfall-tax or price-intervention proposal and the multiple compresses further.

**Base case ($160, +2.9% plus 2.65% dividend).** Consensus is approximately right: 2026 delivers near $12 of adjusted EPS, 2027 steps down to roughly $10.50 as refining normalizes partially while Permian, Guyana and cost savings cushion the decline. 15.1x FY2027 consensus of $10.58 = $160. Total return of about 5.6% over twelve months, in line with a fully-funded 5.8% shareholder yield and little multiple change: the profile of a well-run business trading at fair value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), and any confirmation of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit through Hormuz, whichever comes first.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price in the $130-135 area without fundamental deterioration. At $132, the stock is 12.5x FY2027 consensus of $10.58 with the combined shareholder yield rising toward 6.8% — the compensation the Hormuz asymmetry requires.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Energy Products earnings above $4.0B excluding timing effects, with net global refining capacity still contracting. That converts the abnormal-margin assumption into a structural one and validates the bull-case numerator.
3. A new repurchase authorization at or above a $20B annual pace while distributions remain covered by free cash flow, confirming the 3.1% buyback yield is durable rather than environment-dependent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Sustained uninterrupted commercial transit through Hormuz accompanied by Brent below $85. The volume recovery of roughly $1.5B/quarter will not offset the loss of a $4.65B quarterly price effect and a $3.18B margin effect.
2. FY2027 consensus falling below $9.00, or three consecutive months of negative thirty-day revisions to the next-year figure (the current −2.1% is the first data point).
3. Distributions exceeding free cash flow by more than $5B in any two consecutive quarters while cash remains near $10B, or failure to expand the repurchase authorization beyond the $10.2B remaining at June 30.
4. Legislative text for a federal windfall-profits tax or a mandated price intervention.

## Conclusion

**HOLD — income rationale. Overall 6.1/10.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the trajectory of individual dimensions has been downward (competitive position 8.5 → 6.5, growth 6.5 → 5.5, management 7.5 → 6.3). The current data neither reverses nor accelerates that trend: the fundamentals improved sharply in Q2, and the price rose 12.8% off the June 18 low to meet them, while next-year consensus edged down 2.1% over thirty days. Risk/reward is modestly worse than it was six weeks ago, not because anything broke but because the discount closed.

One correction to earlier framing: on current numbers the stock is 19.3x trailing GAAP earnings and 12.6x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS, less demanding than the mid-20s trailing multiple that characterized the position when FY2025 was the only reference. That argues against a downgrade. What argues against an upgrade is that the FY2026 numerator is a cycle peak by management's own description, that a third of the quarter's improvement is timing management says unwinds, and that ExxonMobil is structurally positioned to lose more from a Hormuz reopening than it gains, an asymmetry that is not compensated at 7.5% upside to consensus mean and zero upside to Morningstar's fair value.

The holding case is the balance sheet and the distribution. Net debt/capital of 10.7%, Altman Z in the safe zone on any construction, no refinancing cliff, $7.7B of undrawn committed lines, a 2.65% dividend covered several times over by first-half cash generation, and a 3.14% repurchase program executed below the current price. Own the shares for that 5.8% funded yield and for the integration hedge that is currently working as designed. Do not add at $155.44. The entry that would justify new capital is the low-$130s, or hard evidence that refining margins are structurally rather than cyclically elevated.
