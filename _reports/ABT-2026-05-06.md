## Executive Summary

Abbott Laboratories has declined approximately 38% from its 52-week high of $139.06 to $86.44, driven by three converging headwinds: (1) the dilutive near-term impact of the approximately $23B enterprise value Exact Sciences acquisition, (2) escalating NEC infant formula litigation with two large jury verdicts, and (3) a deteriorating U.S. Nutrition segment. The stock now trades at approximately 16x forward adjusted EPS ($5.48 midpoint guidance for 2026), a significant discount to diversified medtech peers and its own historical valuation range.

The core question is whether the litigation risk justifies this level of de-rating. Abbott has not recorded reserves for NEC lawsuits, stating it cannot reasonably estimate a range of possible loss, though it acknowledges that resolution could be material to financial position, cash flows, or results of operations. A multi-billion dollar aggregate liability is plausible given 1,760 pending cases and precedent verdicts. However, the company won summary judgment in three federal bellwether cases, suggesting highly divergent jurisdictional outcomes, and the total addressable liability remains uncertain enough that the market may be over-discounting it at current levels.

Meanwhile, the underlying business remains strong: Medical Devices delivered 8.1% growth excluding FX in Q1 2026, FreeStyle Libre is the global CGM leader with Diabetes Care revenue of approximately $8.0B (CGM sales of $7.6B in 2025), and the Exact Sciences acquisition positions Abbott in the high-growth cancer diagnostics market (13%+ growth). Insider buying by the CFO ($201K) and a director ($927K) at 52-week lows provides incremental confidence that current prices represent dislocation rather than permanent impairment.

## Company Overview

Abbott is a $150B-market-cap diversified healthcare company operating across four segments: Medical Devices ($21.4B, 2025), Diagnostics ($8.9B), Nutritional Products ($8.5B), and Established Pharmaceuticals ($5.5B). Total 2025 revenue was $44.3B. The company generates 61% of revenue internationally, employs 122,000 people, and holds #1 or #2 market positions across most categories. Abbott completed the transformational acquisition of Exact Sciences in March 2026, adding ~$3.2B of cancer diagnostics revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** Abbott's revenue trajectory shows recovery from the COVID testing cliff: $43.7B (2022) → $40.1B (2023) → $42.0B (2024) → $44.3B (2025). Q1 2026 revenue of $11.16B represents +7.8% reported growth (+3.7% comparable). Post-Exact Sciences, run-rate revenue is approximately $48B.

**Profitability:** Gross margin expanded to 52.6% in 2025 from 50.3% in 2023. Operating margin improved to 18.2% from 16.2%. Adjusted EPS of $5.15 in 2025 is guided to $5.38-$5.58 in 2026, which includes $0.20 of acquisition dilution, implying underlying earnings growth of approximately 8-12%.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow reached $9.6B in 2025 (up from $7.3B in 2023). Free cash flow of $7.4B represents a 16.7% FCF margin. This supports the dividend ($2.52/share, 54 consecutive increases) and debt service.

**Balance Sheet Concerns:** Long-term debt was $12.9B at year-end 2025 but increased substantially following the Exact Sciences acquisition. Abbott issued approximately $20.0B of long-term senior notes in Q1 2026 to fund the transaction, bringing total debt carrying value to approximately $34.0B as of March 31, 2026. Credit ratings remain investment grade (A+ from S&P Global Ratings, Aa3 from Moody's), but leverage has roughly tripled. The financing risk is the elevated ongoing interest expense burden and reduced financial flexibility. Near-term maturities are manageable given the cash generation profile.

**FCF Yield:** At $86.44 per share and 1.74B shares, market cap is ~$150B. Against $7.4B FCF (pre-acquisition, adjusting for incremental debt service), the FCF yield is approximately 4.5-5.0%, which is attractive for a diversified healthcare company with mid-single-digit organic growth.

## Growth Analysis

**Medical Devices (48% of revenue):** The core growth engine delivered 11.9% organic growth in 2025 and 8.1% growth excluding FX in Q1 2026. The deceleration warrants monitoring but is partially explained by tough comps. Key drivers:
- FreeStyle Libre CGM: Diabetes Care revenue of approximately $8.0B in 2025 (of which CGM sales were $7.6B, up 17.4% excluding FX), growing 7.4% comparable in Q1 2026. The FreeDM2 trial supports expansion into Type 2 diabetes on basal insulin, a significantly larger addressable market than current penetration.
- Electrophysiology: Volt PFA system positions Abbott in the fast-growing pulsed-field ablation market. Double-digit growth.
- Structural Heart: Navitor TAVI expanded to low/intermediate risk. Tendyne TMVR received FDA approval.

**Exact Sciences / Cancer Diagnostics:** Adds approximately $3.2B of 13%+ growth revenue (per S&P Global estimates). Abbott included $96M of Exact Sciences net sales in its Q1 2026 consolidated results from the March 23 acquisition date through quarter-end. Cologuard dominates non-invasive colorectal cancer screening with room for further penetration. Cancerguard (multi-cancer early detection) represents a long-term TAM expansion opportunity. Accretive to growth by approximately 100bps annually.

**Nutrition:** U.S. nutrition is deteriorating rapidly (-7.7% excluding FX in Q1 2026 for total Nutrition, with U.S. Pediatric -13% and U.S. Adult -9.2%). Management attributes this to pricing actions taken in Q4 2025, but the magnitude suggests potential share loss and possible brand damage from NEC litigation. International nutrition remains positive, partially offsetting.

**Consolidated Growth Outlook:** Guidance of 6.5-7.5% comparable sales growth for 2026 is credible given Medical Devices and Cancer Diagnostics momentum, though Nutrition drag creates downside risk to the low end.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86.44, Abbott trades at:
- 16.7x trailing adjusted EPS ($5.15)
- 15.8x 2026E adjusted EPS ($5.48 midpoint)
- 14.3x 2027E adjusted EPS (~$6.06 consensus)
- ~20x free cash flow (2025 basis, pre-acquisition)

Against diversified medtech peers (Stryker, Intuitive Surgical, Edwards Lifesciences, Medtronic), Abbott's discount is significant. The peer set spans a wide valuation range (Medtronic in the mid-teens to Intuitive Surgical above 50x forward earnings), with a median estimated in the high-20s on a forward P/E basis. Abbott has historically traded in the 22-28x forward earnings range over the past five years based on market data providers, though the exact historical range varies by source and timeframe.

The de-rating is attributable to quantifiable factors: (1) ~$0.20 EPS dilution from Exact Sciences in 2026, (2) litigation overhang, (3) Nutrition weakness, (4) increased leverage. The question is whether these factors warrant a discount of this magnitude to peers like Medtronic.

If Abbott re-rates to even 18-20x 2027E EPS of $6.06, that implies a $109-121 price range (26-40% upside). At the current sub-16x forward multiple, the market is pricing in either a permanent growth impairment or a very large litigation liability (or both).

**Estimate Revisions:** Current year estimates are down 3.4% over 90 days and current quarter down 7.9% over 90 days, per consensus tracking data. This reflects the mechanical dilution from Exact Sciences and the Nutrition weakness, both of which are known and reflected in guidance. The revisions appear to be stabilizing based on more recent data.

## Competitive Landscape

**CGM (FreeStyle Libre):** Abbott's competitive advantage is its lower price point and over-the-counter accessibility vs. Dexcom. The CGM market is expanding from Type 1 diabetics to the vastly larger Type 2 population. Abbott's FreeDM2 data supports this expansion thesis. Risk: Dexcom G8 launch could intensify competition.

**Diagnostics:** The Alinity platform provides competitive instrumentation in core laboratory diagnostics. Cancer Diagnostics via Cologuard has significant brand recognition and provider workflow integration that creates switching costs. Guardant Health and GRAIL represent competitive threats in multi-cancer detection.

**Medical Devices broadly:** Abbott competes with Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Stryker, and Edwards across multiple device categories. The breadth of portfolio provides diversification but also means Abbott is rarely the leader in any single high-growth niche (CGM being the exception).

**Nutrition:** Market position has been structurally damaged since the 2022 Sturgis recall. Recovery has been incomplete and may be further impaired by NEC litigation's impact on brand perception.

## Risk Assessment

**NEC Litigation (Critical):** 1,760 pending lawsuits as of January 31, 2026. $495M Missouri jury verdict (appealed by Abbott; the case was on appeal as of the most recent filings). $70M additional jury verdict (April 2026, Chicago; Abbott has indicated it will contest). DOJ criminal investigation since November 2022. DOJ civil False Claims Act intervention (November 2025). State AG complaints. Shareholder derivative litigation.

Abbott's year-end 2025 legal/environmental accrual of $175M (which included a $165M negotiated settlement unrelated to NEC) was a broader litigation reserve. Abbott has explicitly disclosed that **no reserves have been recorded for NEC lawsuits** because it does not believe a material loss is probable and cannot reasonably estimate a range of possible loss. By Q1 2026, the total legal accrual had declined to approximately $120M. Abbott does acknowledge that the ultimate resolution of NEC matters could be material to its financial position, cash flows, or results of operations.

This is the single most important variable for the stock. The federal bellwether summary judgment victories (three cases in 2025) suggest Abbott can defend in certain jurisdictions, but state court juries have proven hostile. A scenario analysis (author estimates, not evidence-based projections):
- Best case: federal victories create pressure for settlements at modest per-case values ($500K-2M per case average = $0.9B-$3.5B total)
- Base case: mixed outcomes force settlements averaging $2-5M per case = $3.5B-$8.8B total
- Worst case: continued large verdicts without reversal force settlements at higher per-case values or Congress/regulators intervene more broadly

These scenarios are illustrative and subject to significant uncertainty. Actual outcomes will depend on appeals, mixed verdicts, punitive-damages risk, consolidation dynamics, and the fact that cases vary substantially in facts and jurisdiction. Even the base case implies a potential liability of $3.5-9B, representing 2-6% of market cap. At ~16x earnings, the stock may already be discounting $15-20B of net present value impairment (the gap between current and historical valuation multiples), which would more than cover even the worst-case litigation outcome. This suggests the market may be double-counting the litigation risk.

**Leverage Post-Acquisition:** Debt of approximately $34B as of March 31, 2026 creates meaningful interest expense headwinds and reduces flexibility. However, $9.6B in operating cash flow provides adequate coverage, and the investment-grade rating ensures refinancing access, though Abbott should expect to maintain rather than improve leverage for several years.

**Nutrition Deterioration:** U.S. Nutrition revenue declining at double-digit rates could accelerate further if brand damage from litigation intensifies.

**IRS Tax Disputes:** $1.05B in disputed taxes plus $413M Malaysian assessment. The IRS dispute appears to have strong legal footing (contradicts prior agreed methods), but resolution timing is uncertain.

Put/call positioning suggests modest put-heavy activity but nothing extreme. The market appears to view uncertainty as persistent rather than event-driven, consistent with an overhang (litigation) rather than a single catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-125, 27-45% upside):**
- Litigation resolves favorably through settlements at manageable levels ($3-5B total, paid over multiple years from $7.4B annual FCF)
- Medical Devices growth re-accelerates as FreeStyle Libre expands into Type 2 diabetes
- Exact Sciences accretion exceeds expectations in 2027-2028 (targeted earnings accretion by year 2)
- Stock re-rates from 16x to 20-22x forward earnings as litigation visibility improves
- U.S. Nutrition stabilizes in H2 2026 as pricing actions lap

**Bear Case ($70-80, 9-19% downside):**
- NEC litigation outcomes continue worsening, forcing multi-billion dollar accrual increases
- DOJ criminal investigation results in enforcement action
- Nutrition segment enters structural decline, requiring write-downs
- Exact Sciences growth decelerates or integration falters
- Rating agencies downgrade due to leverage + litigation combination
- Stock de-rates further to 12-14x forward earnings

**Key Differentiator from Market View:** The market appears to be pricing litigation risk additively on top of the acquisition dilution and nutrition weakness. However, Abbott's FCF generation of $7.4B/year (growing) provides substantial capacity to absorb even a large litigation settlement over time without impairing the franchise. On a sum-of-parts basis, Medical Devices alone (2025 reported sales of $21.4B at 5-6x revenue) would imply a value of $107B-$128B, a substantial portion (though not the entirety) of the current enterprise value of approximately $184B (market cap plus ~$34B debt).

## Conclusion

Abbott at $86.44 is a high-quality healthcare franchise priced for permanent impairment. The NEC litigation is a legitimate and material risk, but at 16x forward earnings, well below peers and historical norms, the stock is discounting a severe outcome that may not materialize. The CFO and a director buying combined $1.1M of stock at these levels (though investors should note that a blanket "no insider selling" conclusion cannot be drawn without reconciling all recent Form 4 filings), the strong FCF generation, and the structural growth in Medical Devices and Cancer Diagnostics provide a credible path to recovery.

Position sizing should be moderate (2-3%) given the binary litigation risk. The asymmetry favors longs: base case implies 21-27% upside over 12-18 months, while the bear case implies 13-19% downside, a roughly 1.5:1 reward-to-risk ratio before accounting for the 2.9% dividend yield.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The peer-multiple discount cited in the thesis (37.5x peer median) appears overstated based on validated data; the actual diversified medtech forward P/E median is likely in the high-20s. Additionally, the Medical Devices sum-of-parts argument does not support the claim that this segment alone covers the full enterprise value. These corrections reduce the magnitude of the valuation gap but do not eliminate the discount to peers and historical norms.
