## Executive Summary

Avista is a subscale, vertically integrated regulated multi-utility serving approximately 429,000 electric and 386,000 gas customers in eastern Washington, northern Idaho and Oregon, plus 17,600 customers in Juneau through AEL&P. It owns a genuinely advantaged generation base: 53% hydro after the January 1, 2026 Colstrip exit, with FERC licenses running to 2044–2059, operated inside five separate regulatory jurisdictions. The central financial fact is that this collection of assets earned roughly 7.25% on average consolidated common equity in FY2025 ($192M net income on $2,650M average equity) against authorized returns of 9.8% in Washington and 9.6% in Idaho. The market's response is a 1.16x price-to-book multiple and a 14.0x multiple of 2027 consensus earnings, against a multi-utility peer median of 21.1x. That discount is the arithmetic expression of persistent under-earning, not an obvious mispricing.

The mechanism behind the shortfall is specific and fixable through the regulatory process. Washington's Energy Recovery Mechanism shares power-supply cost deviations between customers and shareholders; actual net power supply costs exceeded base-rate amounts by $78M in 2025, the shareholder share cost $14M pre-tax (up from $8M in 2024), the WUTC denied Avista's requested ERM modification, and 2026 guidance embeds another $(0.10) per share of drag. Electric resource costs account for $46M of the $111M first-year electric revenue requirement in the pending four-year multi-year rate plan, so the rate case is substantially an attempt to reset base power supply costs to observed reality. That case is being litigated rather than settled: the Washington Attorney General's Public Counsel Unit, per a June 23, 2026 *Spokesman-Review* interview with AG Nick Brown, concluded that roughly $626M of the cumulative four-year ask "didn't really reflect the true expenses and costs," objecting specifically to out-of-state infrastructure, CEO pay, marketing and lobbying in rates. An evidentiary hearing was scheduled for September 2026 and the WUTC's eleven-month clock from the January 16, 2026 filing implies an order around mid-December.

Two things changed since our last review at $41.77. The price is 6.7% lower on essentially unchanged full-year fundamentals — FY2026 non-GAAP utility EPS guidance of $2.52–$2.72 was affirmed on August 3, current-year consensus moved +0.4% over 90 days and next-year -0.1% — which improves the entry. Offsetting that, the Q2 10-Q disclosed that Avista is "evaluating the need for up to $100 million of additional short-term liquidity by the end of the fourth quarter of 2026," attributing it to expanding regulatory deferrals, while the decoupling surcharge regulatory asset nearly doubled from $48M to $89M in six months. Those roughly cancel. We remain at HOLD for a fourth consecutive review, held for the 5.06% yield rather than for capital appreciation, and we specify below the price and evidence that would make us buyers.

## Company Overview

Avista Corp. was incorporated in Washington in 1889 and is headquartered in Spokane. It reports two segments. **Avista Utilities** is an operating division (not a subsidiary) providing electric generation, transmission and distribution plus gas distribution across a 34,000-square-mile territory with a population of 1.5 million, employing 1,929 people, 36% of whom are in IBEW bargaining units. **AEL&P**, a wholly-owned subsidiary, is the sole electric provider in Juneau: five hydro plants totaling 102.7 MW (24.5 MW owned, 78.2 MW under the Snettisham take-or-pay PPA to 2038, accounted for as a finance lease with a $36M obligation), backed by 107.5 MW of diesel, with no transmission interconnection and no gas pipeline access. Its largest customer, a Juneau-area mine, is served on an interruptible basis.

A third, non-reportable bucket (largely Avista Capital) held $177M of assets at 12/31/25, down from $194M, including $148M of equity investments in venture funds and emerging technology. This is a small position that generates outsized GAAP earnings noise, discussed below.

Generation is 53% hydro, 32% thermal and 15% other renewables post-Colstrip. Under normal streamflow, owned hydro plus long-term hydro purchase contracts meet roughly half of average electric requirements; normal 2026 hydro generation is estimated at 648.3 aMW. The 270 MW Lancaster combined-cycle plant is third-party owned under a tolling PPA to 2041 in which Avista dispatches, supplies the gas and takes the output; Palouse Wind, Rattlesnake Flat and Lind Solar are likewise third-party owned under PPAs. Avista's asset base is therefore somewhat less owned-rate-base-heavy than the resource mix implies.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue composition is price, not volume.** Electric retail revenue grew from $887M (2023) to $982M (2024) to $1,103M (2025). Residential revenue per kWh rose from 10.58¢ to 11.78¢ to 13.37¢ — a 26.4% two-year increase — while residential kWh sales were roughly flat (4,020 → 4,018 → 4,114 thousand MWh) and average residential customer count grew about 1.3% annually. Gas retail revenue *fell* 11% from $507M to $440M as usage per residential customer declined from 662 to 605 therms against heating degree days at 89% of average in both 2024 and 2025. Wholesale electric revenue fell from $250M to $187M even as wholesale volumes rose from 3,468 to 4,457 thousand MWh, implying materially lower realized prices and a smaller resource-optimization margin to offset fixed costs.

**Returns.** FY2025 net income was $192.0M, corroborated by the proxy pay-versus-performance disclosure and by a retained-earnings reconciliation ($871M at 12/31/24 plus $192M less roughly $158M of dividends ≈ $905M at 12/31/25). On ~81.0M average diluted shares that is roughly $2.37 of GAAP diluted EPS and, on $2,650M of average common equity, a 7.25% ROE against 9.8%/9.6% authorized. The consolidated figure is depressed by AEL&P and the non-utility businesses, and Avista does not disclose segment equity, so the utility-only gap regulators actually assess is narrower than 2.5 points. The direction is nonetheless corroborated by the company's own behavior: a utility earning its allowed return does not file for a 13.9% first-year electric increase and a 10.2% ROE.

**The deferral build and the liquidity disclosure.** Comparing balances at 6/30/26 versus 12/31/25: total regulatory assets $1,037M vs $1,006M; deferred power costs $93M vs $88M; decoupling surcharge $89M vs $48M. The decoupling balance nearly doubled in six months. Avista is funding unrecovered costs with debt, and H1 2026 interest expense rose 5.3% to $80M from $76M. The company's own statement that it is evaluating up to $100M of incremental short-term liquidity by Q4 2026 because of these deferrals is the most concrete new negative in the filing set. Recovery is proceeding — $32M of ERM surcharge approved in 2025 over two years from July 1, 2025, plus $65M approved in 2026 over two years from July 1, 2026 — but slowly relative to the build.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $301M against $326M of capex and $81M of dividends: free cash flow after capex of -$25M and an external funding requirement near -$106M. We deliberately do not annualize the H1 figure. Avista's cash flow is strongly seasonal, H1 was flattered by winter receivable collection and a federal tax refund, and simple doubling would produce ~$602M of full-year OCF, which is inconsistent with management's disclosed need for more short-term liquidity. As a labeled sensitivity: 2026 capex guidance of $615M plus roughly $165M of dividends ($1.97 annualized on ~83.9M average shares) is about $780M of uses, against $230M of long-term debt priced in May ($90M three-year and $70M thirty-year issued, plus $70M thirty-year expected in August) and up to $90M of equity — $320M of announced external sources. The arithmetic implies roughly $460M of operating cash flow is needed to avoid incremental short-term borrowing. The company's own liquidity statement is more reliable than either extrapolation.

**Capital structure.** Common equity moved $2,591M (12/31/24) → $2,709M (12/31/25) → $2,821M (6/30/26). Long-term debt was $2,754M and short-term borrowings $388M at 12/31/25. At 6/30/26 the structure was 54.4% debt / 45.6% equity against a 65% covenant maximum — 10.6 points of headroom, not a near-term concern. Investment-grade market access was demonstrated by the May 2026 thirty-year issuance. The screening Altman Z-Score near 1.09 should be discarded: the model is calibrated on manufacturers and penalizes exactly the low asset turnover (~$1.96B revenue on ~$8.4B assets), high leverage and low working capital that define a healthy rate-base utility.

**Payout.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.4925 on August 4, 2026 ($1.97 annualized), the 24th consecutive annual increase. That is 75% of the $2.62 non-GAAP utility guidance midpoint and 83% of FY2025 GAAP EPS of $2.37. On the GAAP basis, retained-earnings capacity to fund the equity layer of the capital plan is thin, which is why equity issuance recurs. Up to $90M in 2026 at roughly $40 is about 2.25M shares, 2.7% of shares outstanding and roughly 2.6 percentage points of ownership dilution; share count has already risen about 3.4% from ~81.0M average diluted in 2025 to 83.752M outstanding.

**Earnings quality caution.** Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $0.43 versus $0.17 was almost entirely non-utility: the 10-Q discloses $22M of equity earnings from Energy Impact Partners I in the quarter, roughly $0.26 per share pre-tax on 83.752M shares. Avista holds a 4.71% fund interest accounted for on a one-quarter lag; portfolio company ERock completed an IPO in June 2026. Management has pre-disclosed an estimated additional $17M gain in Q3 2026 and, based on July 31 fair value, an estimated $13M loss in Q4 2026 (actual Q4 will reflect September 30 fair value). On the utility basis management guides to, H1 2026 improved from $1.30 to $1.38. Value this company on utility earnings only; the GAAP line will swing by roughly $30M across the next two quarters for reasons unrelated to the utility.

## Growth Analysis

The rate-base engine is real but modest per share. The five-year capex plan is $615M (2026), $635M, $800M, $680M and $710M — $3.44B — supporting a stated 7% rate base CAGR. Included is a $130M ERP project targeted for 2028 and wildfire resiliency of roughly $45M capital plus $20M O&M in 2026 (up from $36M/$15M). Excluded from the plan are large-load integration, regional grid expansion such as the North Plains Connector, and additional generation resources.

Translating 7% rate-base growth into per-share earnings growth requires two things Avista has not reliably delivered: earning close to the authorized return, and funding the equity layer without heavy dilution. The record is a proxy-disclosed EPS series of $2.10 (2021), $2.12, $2.24, $2.39 and $2.76 (2025) — about 7% annualized on the incentive metric, but that metric sits roughly $0.39 above GAAP diluted EPS of ~$2.37 and its precise definition is not determinable from the structured proxy data. On consensus, 2026 is $2.61 and 2027 is $2.79, +6.9%, and that already embeds some MYRP relief.

Demand-side growth is weak. Customer count grows about 1.3% annually; kWh per residential customer is flat; gas therms per customer fell from 662 to 605; industrial electric volumes fell 21% in Q2 2026 and 12% in H1 following the April 2026 departure of a large industrial customer that the FY2025 MD&A quantified at roughly $9M of net income impact. Industrial customer count declined from 1,188 (2023) to 1,159 (2025). Q2 industrial revenue fell only 11% ($31M vs $35M) against the 21% volume decline, consistent with the lost load having been low-margin, high-load-factor, specially negotiated service, but the lost contribution still has to be reallocated to remaining customers through the rate case.

The genuine growth option is large load. In May 2026 Avista signed a non-binding MOU with a data center developer contemplating 125 MW beginning in 2029 with a pathway to 500 MW by 2032, and in June 2026 paused negotiations following community concern (Item 8.01 8-K filed June 12; shares fell about 3.6% on June 15). Scale the opportunity against the system: 2025 peak native load was 1,837 MW and average hourly load 1,149 aMW, so 500 MW is 27% of peak and, at a high load factor, roughly 39% of average system load — more than the entire 490 MW of new capacity the 2025 IRP calls for by 2030. For a $3.3B utility that would be transformational rate base. It is also why the project cannot proceed quietly: two Spokane County commissioners proposed a nine-month data center moratorium in July 2026 (a proposal, not an enacted ordinance), in a territory already absorbing a 26% two-year residential rate increase. Avista would be the electricity supplier and the developer the customer; Avista would not own the facility. Treat this as unpriced optionality with a plausible path to nothing.

Estimate revisions are mixed and worth stating precisely: current-quarter consensus fell 15.0% over 30 days and 17.7% over 90 days (three analysts), next quarter -3.4%/-4.5%, but current year is +0.4% and next year -0.1% (seven analysts). The near-term cuts look like re-timing around ERM drag and the industrial loss rather than a change in the annual earnings power management just affirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 13.89 alongside EPS of $2.38, which is internally inconsistent ($38.975 ÷ $2.38 = 16.4x). We use our own arithmetic on the canonical $38.975 basis:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 utility guidance midpoint $2.62 | **14.9x** (range 14.3x–15.5x) |
| P/E on 2027 consensus $2.79 | **14.0x** |
| P/E on FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS ~$2.37 | 16.4x |
| Price / book ($33.68 at 6/30/26) | **1.157x** |
| Dividend yield ($1.97 annualized) | **5.06%** |
| Payout on guided utility EPS / GAAP EPS | 75% / 83% |

Against peer medians of 21.1x P/E and 3.6% yield (MDU 22.7x, NWE 25.5x, BKH 18.5x, OGE 21.1x, PNW 19.3x), Avista trades at roughly a 30-35% earnings-multiple discount and a 140bp yield premium. The nearest geographic comparable, NorthWestern — the counterparty that took Avista's Colstrip interest — trades at 25.5x with a 3.8% yield.

The discount is explainable rather than anomalous. A business earning ~7.25% on book against ~9.7% authorized should trade near book, and 1.16x is close to that. The five-year cumulative TSR in the proxy (121 versus a 145 peer group, indexed to 100) is the market's running verdict. Cheapness alone is not a thesis here; a compressed multiple should be treated as a warning until an identified catalyst is in view.

The catalyst is in view, and it is dated. If the MYRP resets base power supply costs, the ROE gap narrows mechanically, and both earnings and the multiple can re-rate. The offsetting reality is that the case is being litigated by an intervenor contesting the return itself, not just expense lines. Opening positions in rate cases are habitually extreme and Public Counsel recommendations routinely land far below final orders; the "$626M disallowance" and the journalist's "10% → 7%" rendering (which conflates dividends with authorized ROE — a 7% ROE would be far outside recent US precedent) should be read as an adversarial anchor, not a forecast.

Sell-side positioning offers no confirmation: mean target $41.33 (+6.0%), median $40.50 (+3.9%), range $37.00–$50.00, with a stable distribution of 1 buy, 5 holds and 1 strong sell across the last four months. Because estimates are not inflecting upward and this is not a cheap-versus-rising-estimates setup, we treat these targets as a genuine consensus band rather than as a stale floor.

**Scenario framework (12–18 months, from $38.975):**

- **Base $41.00 (+5.2%).** MYRP order around mid-December 2026 grants a partial increase sufficient to reset base power costs; 2027 EPS near consensus $2.79 at roughly 14.7x. With the 5.06% yield, total return near 10%.
- **Bull $48.00 (+23%).** A settlement or order granting a substantial share of the $111M electric ask with a workable ROE and equity layer; ERM drag largely eliminated; 2028 EPS approaching $3.05–$3.15 at 15.5–16x. Consistent with the $50 street high.
- **Bear $32.00 (-18%).** Order well below the ask, ERM sharing bands unchanged, incremental short-term borrowing plus another equity tranche, and a steeper long end compressing the multiple to ~12.5x on ~$2.55. A wildfire event or negative rating action would take this toward $30.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +3% expected price return plus the 5.06% yield, or about 8% expected total return — approximately fair for a utility carrying a live binary. That is HOLD arithmetic, not ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

There is no retail competition; the AG's "true monopoly" characterization is accurate. The dynamics that matter are regulatory, resource-market and large-load.

**The hydro fleet is a real asset in a CETA world.** At 53% hydro with licenses to 2044 (Little Falls, Spokane Tribe agreement), 2046 (Clark Fork) and 2059 (five Spokane River projects), Avista holds what the 10-K calls "typically our lowest cost source per MWh" and what is also fully compliant with Washington's Clean Energy Transformation Act. The IRP projects Avista exceeding CETA's 2030 greenhouse-gas-neutrality requirement for Washington supply. Thermal-heavy peers face costlier transitions. The counterpoint is CETA's 2045 target, which the company concedes may require hydrogen, nuclear or long-duration storage — technologies "either not currently available or not cost-effective under the lowest reasonable cost regulatory standard."

**Regional capacity tightness is the defining industry condition and the source of the ERM problem.** The 10-K describes load growth, more frequent extreme weather, a transition to variable resources, and electric and gas delivery systems "reaching full utilization during peak load conditions," while regional coal retires. Replacement energy is therefore expensive relative to embedded base rates, which drove actual net power supply costs above base by $78M in 2025. Avista participates in the CAISO-run Western EIM and plans through NorthernGrid, with a pending FERC transmission-planning complaint.

**Scale is a structural disadvantage.** Roughly 447,000 utility customers support fixed compliance costs across five commissions plus FERC, NERC/WECC reliability, Washington's CCA and CETA, Oregon's CPP, and two separate new wildfire statutes. The $130M ERP project against a $615M annual capex budget illustrates how large a routine modernization is here.

**Climate compliance is now a balance-sheet item.** Emission allowance inventory was $160M at 6/30/26 (from $136M) against CCA obligations of $161M (from $131M). Residual Colstrip exposure persists despite the transfer: roughly $14M of retained surety bonds, remediation obligations, and $9M being refunded to customers from a disallowance.

**AEL&P is idiosyncratically strong on returns.** Its May 2026 RCA filing seeks 13.2% ROE on a 58.33% equity ratio (9.75% return on rate base), with an interim 12.5% increase (+$5M) approved effective June 2026 and a further 12.6% sought from August 2027. Those parameters are far above Avista Utilities' and reflect genuine isolation risk — no interconnection, no gas, diesel backup, a single interruptible mining customer as largest load.

**One nuance on mix.** Electric retail revenue rising from $887M to $1,103M while gas fell from $507M to $440M shifts the mix toward electric, but this measures relative price escalation between two commodities. It is not evidence of diversification: industrial customer count declined, one large industrial customer left, and geographic concentration in the Spokane/Coeur d'Alene corridor is unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The MYRP order (dominant, dated).** Filed January 16, 2026: RY1 (2027) electric +$111M (13.9%) and gas +$12M (4.7%); RY2 +$43M/+$7M; RY3 +$34M/+$6M; RY4 +$28M/+$3M; requested 7.5% return on rate base with 10.2% ROE and a 48.5% equity layer in 2027, rising to 7.67%/10.5% by 2029. Public Counsel is opposing broadly, including the return itself. A virtual public hearing was set for August 27, evidentiary hearing September 2026, order implied around mid-December. Both tails are live and neither is fully priced at 1.16x book.

**2. ERM under-recovery.** The WUTC denied ERM modification; 2026 embeds $(0.10) per share within the 90/10 band; costs are expected to "significantly exceed" base rates again in 2026. If the MYRP does not adequately reset base power supply costs, this recurs indefinitely.

**3. Liquidity and financing.** The self-disclosed evaluation of up to $100M of incremental short-term liquidity, rising interest expense, negative post-dividend FCF, and recurring equity issuance at 1.16x book while earning 7.25% on that book combine into a slow-compounding headwind. Credit-agency commentary was not retrieved and would be the single most valuable missing input.

**4. Wildfire.** Several fires occurred in the territory in 2026; two ignited near company facilities but were contained, and the company does not believe improper operation or maintenance was involved. PSPS was implemented. Realized litigation is small (Rathdrum settled July 2026 for $1.3M; an Orofino claim at $0.2M). Idaho's Wildfire Standard of Care Act, effective July 2025, makes an IPUC-approved plan establish the duty of care — a meaningful liability shield. Washington's HB 1522 requires WUTC plan filing. The tail remains unpriced.

**5. Hydrology and weather.** With roughly half of average electric requirements from owned hydro plus long-term hydro contracts, streamflow variance drives power supply cost and therefore the ERM. The 2025 pattern — cooling degree days at 139% of average, heating degree days at 89% — shifts peak to summer (2025 peak of 1,837 MW occurred September 2) while eroding gas throughput.

**6. Affordability politics.** Residential electric revenue per customer is up 26% in two years. On top of the 13.9% MYRP ask, Idaho annual price adjustments filed August 1, 2026 would raise a typical residential electric bill from $119.52 to $127.28 monthly (+6.5%) effective October 1. The AG's monopoly framing and the county data-center moratorium proposal are symptoms of the same pressure.

**7. Rate sensitivity.** A 5.06%-yielding, negative-FCF, equity-issuing utility is a duration instrument. The discount-rate channel is a live headwind to the multiple independent of anything Avista does. [unverified — no interest-rate or Fed data appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**8. Earnings noise.** The pre-disclosed EIP swing (+$17M estimated Q3, -$13M estimated Q4 on July 31 marks) will dominate GAAP EPS for two quarters and is unrelated to the utility.

**9. Unresolved items.** A May 4, 2026 8-K apparently carrying Item 2.01 could not be retrieved (HTTP 403); the Q2 10-Q's silence three months later is meaningful negative evidence of immateriality, but the transaction remains unidentified. The proxy's $2.76 "company-selected measure" EPS does not reconcile to ~$2.37 GAAP, and the definition is not determinable from the available data; we draw no inference about intent, but the AG's objection to CEO pay in rates makes it a live governance topic.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 20.4% against 18.9% one-year realized is fair, and sits in the 10th percentile of the current chain: the August 21 expiry carries no premium and prices only a ±4.5% move. The information is in the term structure: contango out to 28.7% at the December 18 tenor, implying a ±12.8% move, which is the window containing the expected MYRP order. Positioning has turned more defensive: put/call open interest rose from 0.69 on July 20 to 1.14 on August 7 while ATM IV fell 6.2 points and the price fell 6.6%, and the source characterizes near-term skew (3.9%) as elevated put demand. The cheap near-dated volatility relative to the December event means an owner who wants to hold the yield through the order can hedge the catalyst inexpensively at the front, though the December tenor itself is not cheap.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $48 (+23%)

The MYRP is a mechanical fix for a mechanical problem. Power supply costs exceeded base rates by $78M in 2025 and $46M of the $111M electric ask is resource cost; that is demonstrable, not discretionary, and commissions generally allow recovery of prudently incurred power costs once the evidence is on the record. A settlement before or during the September hearing would remove the December binary and re-rate the stock immediately. Behind it: a 7% rate-base CAGR on a $3.44B plan that *excludes* large-load integration and new generation; the 2025 IRP requiring 490 MW by 2030 and 950 MW through 2035, with a 425 MW RFP already issued; AEL&P's interim 12.5% increase already approved and a 13.2% ROE request pending; a Washington gas business that is currently *over*-earning ($16M refund provision, up from $13M), which localizes the problem to electric power supply rather than the franchise; and 53% hydro that is both cheapest and CETA-compliant. Closing even half the ROE gap on $2,821M of equity is roughly $35M of net income, about $0.42 per share, which at 15x is $6 of value before any multiple re-rating from 1.16x book. The data center MOU — 125 MW from 2029 with a pathway to 500 MW, or 27% of system peak — is free optionality on top. The holder is paid 5.06% in the interim.

### Bear Case — $32 (-18%)

The 1.16x book multiple is correct and the discount persists because the under-earning does. Public Counsel is contesting the return itself; a diluted order leaves the ERM sharing bands intact and base power costs still below reality, so 2027 looks like 2026 with another $(0.10) drag and another deferral build. The flywheel then continues: unrecovered costs are debt-funded, interest expense rises (already +5.3% year over year), the ROE gap widens, and the company issues equity at 1.16x book to fund capex that earns 7.25%, which is value-destructive at the margin. Demand does not help — flat kWh per customer, gas therms down 9% in two years, industrial volumes -21%, wholesale prices down. Affordability politics are tightening around the same time: a 26% two-year residential increase, a 6.5% Idaho ask, an AG in opposition, and a county considering a data-center moratorium that would remove the only large growth option. Layer on a $100M incremental liquidity need already flagged by the company, a 5-year TSR of 121 against a 145 peer group, and a GAAP payout of 83%, and the stock has little valuation support above book. A wildfire loss or negative rating action takes it to $30.

**Which is more likely.** The base case sits closer to the bull mechanism than the bear on the specific question of power-cost recovery, because the under-recovery is documented and $32M and $65M of ERM surcharges have already been approved. But the *quantum* of relief, the ROE, and the equity layer are all contested, and the company's per-share growth is structurally taxed by ~2.5-3% annual dilution regardless of the outcome. That combination produces a fair, not asymmetric, return from $38.975.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold). Fourth consecutive HOLD; overall score 4.95 versus 5.0 in July.** The score is essentially flat because two changes offset: valuation improved (5.0 → 5.5) on a 6.7% lower price against affirmed FY2026 guidance and flat full-year consensus, while financial health declined (5.0 → 4.8) on the disclosed evaluation of up to $100M of incremental short-term liquidity and the decoupling surcharge balance nearly doubling to $89M. We are not marking the thesis down for the price decline; we are marking it down for the specific cash-flow disclosure that accompanied it.

**Reason to own:** a 5.06% yield with 24 consecutive annual increases, 75% covered by guided utility EPS, at 1.16x book. Capital appreciation is contingent on a December regulatory order we cannot handicap tightly.

**Base case $41.00 (+5.2% price, ~10.3% total return with dividend)** — 14.7x 2027 consensus EPS of $2.79, assuming a partial MYRP order that resets base power supply costs.
**Bull case $48.00 (+23%)** — substantial MYRP relief or an early settlement; 2028 EPS $3.05–$3.15 at 15.5–16x.
**Bear case $32.00 (-18%)** — diluted order, ERM drag persists, further short-term borrowing plus equity, multiple to ~12.5x on ~$2.55.
**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored on the August 27 public hearing, the September 2026 evidentiary hearing, the October 1 Idaho price adjustment effective date, and an order expected around mid-December 2026.

## Conclusion

Avista offers a 5.06% dividend backed by a 24-year growth record, at 14.0x 2027 consensus earnings and 1.157x book — a 30-35% earnings-multiple discount to the multi-utility peer median. The discount is rational rather than anomalous: consolidated FY2025 ROE of roughly 7.25% against 9.6-9.8% authorized returns, driven substantially by Washington power-supply cost under-recovery that cost shareholders $14M pre-tax in 2025 and is guided to cost another $0.10 per share in 2026. The Washington multi-year rate plan is the mechanism designed to fix that, and it is a genuine, dated, two-sided binary: $46M of the $111M first-year electric ask is resource cost that is documented in the filings, but the Attorney General's Public Counsel is contesting the ask broadly and the return itself, with an evidentiary hearing in September and an order expected around mid-December. The options market prices this consistently — near-dated volatility at the 10th percentile of the chain, December implied volatility at 28.7%.

The lower price improves the entry, and we do not treat a 6.7% decline on affirmed guidance as thesis deterioration. But the same Q2 filing that affirmed guidance also disclosed a self-identified need to evaluate up to $100M of incremental short-term liquidity, alongside a decoupling surcharge regulatory asset that nearly doubled to $89M in six months, negative free cash flow after dividends, and roughly 2.6 points of annual ownership dilution funding capex that currently earns 7.25%. Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 8% over 12–18 months, with -18% in the bear case, is fair compensation and not an asymmetry worth pressing ahead of a December decision. Own it for the yield if you already do. New capital should wait for $36 or for a constructive MYRP settlement.
