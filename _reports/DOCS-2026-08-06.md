## Executive Summary

Doximity is a high-margin, debt-free business whose growth rate has collapsed faster than its reported full-year numbers suggest. FY2026 revenue of $644.9M was up 13.1%, but the intra-year path derived from the 10-K and Q3 10-Q runs +19.3% in H1, +9.8% in Q3 and **+5.1% in Q4**. Net revenue retention — management's own stated proxy for the growth rate — fell from 119% (FY2025) to 112% (December 2025) to 109% (FY2026). Externally reported FY2027 guidance of roughly 4% revenue growth and a ~49% adjusted EBITDA margin (single-sourced; unverified against the earnings 8-K) implies adjusted EBITDA of about $329M, an ~8% decline in absolute dollars from $357.8M. Consensus non-GAAP EPS for the current fiscal year has been cut 11.6% over 90 days to $1.43.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case: $748.6M of cash and marketable securities at 3/31/2026, no borrowings, liabilities/equity of roughly 0.18, operating cash flow of $326.5M (50.6% of revenue), and a new $500M repurchase authorization approved 2/3/2026 that would retire ~12-13% of shares at current prices. Using total Class A plus Class B shares of approximately 180M (the widely quoted $2.75B market cap reflects Class A only and understates equity value by roughly 28%), equity value is ~$3.94B and enterprise value ~$3.19B. That is 8.9x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and ~9.7x the reported FY2027 estimate — a reasonable multiple for a decelerating advertising-adjacent platform, not an obvious discount.

The reason we are not buyers at a 71% drawdown is that the discount is doing work. Doximity names OpenEvidence — a private AI clinical-answer platform that monetizes physician attention through the same pharmaceutical marketing budget — as a competitor in its own FY2026 risk factors, is in active federal litigation with it (with the CTO and Director of AI Products personally named as defendants), and added new language tying Most Favored Nation drug-pricing uncertainty to bookings timing in Marketing Solutions. Stock-based compensation rose to 18.9% of revenue, and roughly $434M of FY2026 buybacks at an average near $48 left basic share count essentially flat. Base case fair value works out to approximately $22 — the current price. With a Q1 FY2027 print due within roughly two weeks carrying a ±22.9% implied move and no informational edge, there is no reason to add risk here.

## Company Overview

Doximity operates the largest verified professional network for U.S. medical professionals, with membership exceeding 85% of U.S. physicians across all 50 states and every specialty. Members pay nothing. Revenue comes from three customer-facing lines sold to pharmaceutical manufacturers (directly or through media agencies) and health systems:

- **Marketing Solutions** — the dominant line. Pharma brands purchase subscription "modules" (Newsfeed, Workflow, Peer) to place sponsored content in front of targeted physician cohorts. Pricing scales with the number and composition of physicians targeted.
- **Hiring Solutions** — recruiter access to the physician database, plus a placement staffing business (Curative) recorded in "Other."
- **Workflow Solutions** — telehealth dialer, AMiON on-call scheduling, and AI documentation tools (Scribe) plus clinical reference (Ask), sold to health systems.

Q3 FY2026 revenue was ~95% subscription ($175.4M of $185.1M). The company reports one operating segment. Substantially all revenue is domestic.

Two structural features drive the entire debate. First, monetization is indirect: Doximity converts free physician engagement into pharmaceutical promotional budget, so the revenue pool is capped by pharma marketing allocation rather than physician willingness to pay. Second, U.S. member penetration is near-saturated, so incremental growth must come from monetizing the same members harder — more modules, higher price per targeted physician, more brands per customer.

Contract structure offers no insulation. Marketing Solutions contracts are generally 12 months or less and certain ones are cancelable on customary notice; the company states it does not typically enter long-term contracts with pharmaceutical customers. Doximity elects the ASC 606 practical expedient and does not disclose remaining performance obligations, so **there is no disclosed backlog figure for this company**. Deferred revenue (which fell from $114.3M at 3/31/2025 to $66.3M at 12/31/2025 on billing and program-launch timing) is a balance, not a bookings indicator, and should not be read as one.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the exit rate.** Reported annual revenue: FY2022 $343.5M, FY2023 $419.1M (+22.0%), FY2024 $475.4M (+13.4%), FY2025 $570.4M (+20.0%), FY2026 $644.9M (+13.1%). The derived quarterly path within FY2026 is the material fact:

| Period | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| H1 FY2026 (derived: $499.5M nine-month less $185.1M Q3) | $314.4M | +19.3% |
| Q3 FY2026 (disclosed) | $185.1M | +9.8% |
| Q4 FY2026 (derived: $644.9M FY less $499.5M nine-month) | $145.4M | **+5.1%** |

The corresponding FY2025 quarters ($263.5M H1, $168.6M Q3, $138.3M Q4) provide the comparison base. Deceleration of this shape — halving twice in three quarters — is corroborated by the NRR series (119% → 112% → 109%) and by the composition of nine-month subscription growth: $45.5M of the $60.9M increase came from expansion of existing customers versus $15.4M from new customers.

**Margins.** GAAP operating margin fell from 39.9% to 33.3% on R&D up 40% to $130.7M and G&A up 43% to $65.3M. Roughly 85% of the gap between adjusted EBITDA ($357.8M) and GAAP operating income ($214.9M) is stock compensation, which rose $49.2M to $121.6M — 18.9% of revenue versus 12.7% in FY2025. The $49.2M SBC increase by itself exceeds the $12.9M decline in operating income, so adjusted EBITDA margin looks nearly flat (~56% → ~55%) precisely because it excludes the cost that is growing fastest. G&A also carried $9.4M of legal fees tied to the settled securities class action, which is non-recurring.

The cash consequence is visible in the equity statement. Roughly $434M was spent on repurchases in FY2026 (derived from the retained-earnings roll: $186.9M opening + $196.1M net income less the -$51.1M closing balance), yet basic weighted shares moved only from 186.8M to 187.0M. A large share of FY2026 free cash flow neutralized employee equity issuance rather than shrinking the share count. Part of that offset relates to legacy pre-IPO options that are burning off (options outstanding fell 13.0M → 11.1M with only $5.8M of unamortized option expense remaining), which argues the drag diminishes; the newer PSU grants carry up-to-200%/300% leverage and could reverse if targets are missed.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $326.5M in FY2026. Free cash flow is lower after capitalized software and equipment — the nine-month gap was $6.7M ($216.9M OCF vs $210.2M FCF), implying full-year FCF near **$317M**. Cash and marketable securities were $748.6M at 3/31/2026 with zero borrowings, no revolver draw and no financial covenants. Total liabilities at 12/31/2025 were $177.7M against $979.3M of equity, and the composition is benign (deferred revenue $66.5M, accrued expenses $76.7M of which $31.0M is the fully insured securities settlement with an offsetting receivable, leases $10.7M, earn-out $5.6M). Contractual commitments are trivial: $14M of remaining web hosting through December 2027 and ~$11.8M of undiscounted leases. Solvency is not a question for this company.

The deferred tax asset decline from $60.0M to $14.8M is a reclassification, not an impairment: management elected under the July 2025 tax legislation to accelerate deduction of remaining capitalized Section 174 R&D, increasing prepaid taxes by ~$39.6M.

**Earnings delivery.** Three beats and one miss over four quarters, but the sequence matters: +17.5%, +16.4%, +1.2%, then **-10.0%** in the March 2026 quarter ($0.26 versus $0.29). Visibility deteriorated in step with the revenue rate.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on monetizing an already-captured audience more intensively. Engagement is genuinely improving: quarterly unique active providers rose from 0.62M to 0.81M (+~31%), and customers with trailing-12-month subscription revenue above $500,000 rose from 115 to 126 before finishing FY2026 at 125, representing ~83% of revenue. Average revenue per existing Marketing Solutions customer rose ~13%.

Engagement growth alongside decelerating revenue is the diagnostic problem. If member supply were the constraint, more active providers would translate into more revenue. It did not, which points the constraint at customer budget. Two disclosed items support that reading. First, the FY2026 10-K adds language absent from prior filings: uncertainty around federal policy initiatives including Most Favored Nation pricing negotiations "contributed to short-term budget caution among certain pharmaceutical customers and affected the timing of bookings in our Marketing Solutions business," with management unable to quantify the impact on future periods. Second, the 10-K notes pharmaceutical customers continue to seek amendments to their arrangements, against contracts that are ≤12 months and sometimes cancelable.

Estimate revisions have reset and then stabilized. Current-year consensus fell 11.6% over 90 days to $1.43 and next year fell 10.9% to $1.59, but both are flat over 30 days. That pattern — a single large cut around the May 13 print followed by stability — means the guidance shock is in the numbers. It does not tell us whether $1.43 is conservative. On the reported FY2027 framework (~4% revenue growth, ~49% adjusted EBITDA margin), revenue of ~$671M and adjusted EBITDA of ~$329M imply a year of absolute EBITDA decline and ~640bp of margin compression, driven by AI investment. Analysts cut targets on that spending, and KeyBanc (May 14) and BofA (June 29, citing "limited clarity on the trajectory of revenue/margins") downgraded.

There is a legitimate bull argument that the Q4 +5.1% exit rate is a trough rather than a trend. Q4 is Doximity's smallest quarter, management explicitly frames the MFN effect as bookings *timing*, and if pharma budget caution resolves, some deferred programs return. The arithmetic of the deceleration is established from filings; its permanence is not. But an unquantified timing hypothesis is not a growth thesis, and the company itself has no backlog disclosure with which to demonstrate one.

Growth prospects score 3.5. This is a saturated domestic audience monetized through a discretionary, policy-exposed budget line, guided to ~4% growth with declining absolute profit dollars.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count first, because the commonly quoted market cap is wrong for valuation purposes.** As of 12/31/2025 there were 134.10M Class A and 50.90M Class B shares (184.99M total); adjusting for estimated Q4 FY2026 repurchases, shares at 3/31/2026 were likely **~180-182M total**. Class B is not publicly traded but carries full economic rights and ten votes each, so it must be included. At $21.89:

- Equity value ≈ **$3.94B** (the quoted $2.75B is Class A only, understating by ~28%)
- Less net cash and securities of $748.6M, no debt → **EV ≈ $3.19B**
- **EV / FY2026 adjusted EBITDA ($357.8M) = 8.9x**
- **EV / FY2027E adjusted EBITDA (~$329M) = 9.7x**
- **EV / FY2026 est. FCF ($317M) = 10.1x**; equity FCF yield ≈ 8.0%
- **P/E on FY2026 GAAP net income ($196.1M / $0.98 diluted) = 22.3x**
- **P/E on FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS $1.43 = 15.3x**

Keep the bases separate. The 15.3x "forward P/E" is a non-GAAP figure that excludes stock compensation running at 18.9% of revenue; the GAAP multiple is 22.3x on earnings the company expects to be roughly flat-to-down. Peer medians are close to useless here — the Health Care Technology set shows OMCL at 85.9x, CERT negative, WAY 27.4x, HSTM 39.2x and SOLV 9.7x, a dispersion that reflects heterogeneous business models rather than a sector multiple. The relevant comparison is to decelerating digital advertising platforms, which is where the market has repriced Doximity, and 9-11x EV/EBITDA is a normal range for that cohort.

The cheapness is therefore explanatory rather than exploitable. A 9.7x EBITDA multiple on a business with 55% margins, no debt, and 19% of equity value in net cash is the market discounting terminal risk to the pharma-marketing revenue pool, not a mispricing awaiting a catalyst. To score valuation highly I would need a specific reason the market is wrong about that risk, and the evidence points the other way: NRR at 109%, a first-ever >10% customer, and a competitor with accelerating institutional traction.

The one genuine source of asymmetry is capital allocation. The $500M authorization approved 2/3/2026 equals ~12-13% of equity value and is fully fundable from existing liquidity plus ongoing FCF without debt. Executed at $18-24 it is materially accretive, in contrast to the prior program's $417.0M spent through 12/31/2025 at an average of ~$48.22 per share (monthly averages of $68.88 in October, $50.40 in November, $45.40 in December 2025). That optionality is real but conditional on durable earnings power — buying 12% of a business whose EBITDA declines 8% and whose core revenue line is contested compounds an error rather than mitigating it.

Base-case math: 9.5x × $329M FY2027E adjusted EBITDA = $3.13B EV, plus $748.6M net cash = $3.88B, divided by ~180M shares = **$21.50**, or approximately $22 with modest buyback accretion. That is the current price. Valuation scores 5.0 — fair, with cheapness that reads as a warning rather than an invitation.

## Competitive Landscape

The physician-side asset is genuine and hard to rebuild: a verified, near-census graph of U.S. physicians assembled over a decade, sustained by a "physicians-first" editorial posture (the 10-K discloses it may forgo revenue opportunities deemed contrary to member interests). The revenue-side exposure is entirely different in character.

Generative AI has lowered the cost of acquiring physician attention. An AI clinical-answer engine can win a physician on first use by resolving an immediate clinical question, requiring no social graph, colleague network or curated newsfeed. That is why the FY2026 10-K names **OpenEvidence** by name as a Marketing Solutions competitor alongside WebMD's Medscape, and names **Abridge, OpenAI and Anthropic** as Workflow competitors. Naming a private startup in a risk factor is a deliberate disclosure choice. OpenEvidence monetizes physician attention through pharmaceutical advertising — the same budget line, at the same point of clinical decision-making, with a product form better matched to how physicians now retrieve information.

External evidence of that entrant's traction accumulated through July 2026: announced collaborations with NewYork-Presbyterian, Columbia's Vagelos College and Weill Cornell Medicine (July 10) and a multi-year arrangement with Boston Children's Hospital (July 16); and a report-of-a-report (Becker's, July 20, citing The Information) that OpenEvidence considered raising $200M at a $20B valuation but was unlikely to proceed. The valuation figure is the weakest evidentiary category available and supports no quantitative displacement estimate. Its only usable content is directional: private-market participants ascribe to a private entrant a valuation multiple of Doximity's entire equity value, which embeds expectations of large future share in exactly the pool Doximity monetizes.

Doximity's own AI position is dependent rather than proprietary. Its AI products rely on third-party foundation models and on AWS for a substantial portion of compute, and the 10-K's expanded AI risk factor concedes competitors "may incorporate AI into their products or operations more quickly or more successfully than us" and that model providers may change models, terms, pricing, availability or data-use practices adversely. Two of those providers (OpenAI, Anthropic) are simultaneously listed as Workflow competitors. Doximity's Workflow franchise — the segment most naturally positioned to become AI-native — is described in its own filings as "immature and volatile... relatively new and unproven."

Customer-side leverage is also worsening. FY2026 was the first year in which one customer exceeded 10% of total revenue (Customer C was 12% of Q3 and 11% of nine-month revenue; Customers A and B each 11% of receivables), against no such customer in FY2025 or FY2024. Rising active-user counts and a rising count of >$500K customers do not offset this — those measures answer a different question, and the direct concentration measure moved the wrong way. The definition aggregates purchases made through marketing agencies, so an agency consolidating brands could mechanically create a >10% "customer" without deepening single-brand dependence; the filings do not resolve which case applies.

Competitive position scores 4.0 on trajectory: a strong incumbent asset with retention, concentration and substitution all trending against it.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation with the named competitor.** *OpenEvidence Inc. v. Doximity, Inc. et al.* (D. Mass., 1:25-cv-11802-RGS, filed 6/20/2025) names Doximity, its **Chief Technology Officer** and its **Director of AI Products**; the 10/29/2025 amended complaint added subsidiary Pathway Medical and additional individuals. Claims allege unauthorized access to OpenEvidence's platform under the CFAA, plus breach of contract, unjust enrichment and trespass to chattels, seeking permanent injunction, destruction of information, lost profits and disgorgement. Doximity counterclaimed for Lanham Act false advertising, Chapter 93A and defamation. On 1/22/2026 the court dismissed the trespass claim and Pathway's Lanham Act/93A counterclaims but **denied dismissal on all other claims, including all of Doximity's counterclaims**; OpenEvidence voluntarily dropped its trade-secret claim. Surviving a motion to dismiss is a pleading standard, not a finding of fact. The consequential exposure is not damages — liquidity is $748.6M — but injunctive relief, adverse discovery findings about how competitive information was obtained, and reputational damage in a business whose asset is physician trust.

**Policy exposure.** MFN pricing pressure on pharmaceutical gross margins would land on promotional budgets, and Doximity has no multi-year contractual buffer and no disclosed RPO. This risk sits entirely outside management's control.

**Residual governance litigation.** Six derivative suits remain pending (N.D. Cal. consolidated; *Guttman* and *Wong* in D. Del.; *Stern* and *Peter* in Chancery) plus two board demands, arising from the same facts as the settled class action: the accuracy of disclosed user counts and engagement rates. The $31M class settlement (12/24/2025) is stated to be fully covered by insurance, with the accrual offset by a receivable; final approval was scheduled for 6/10/2026 and the outcome is not in the record.

**Control structure.** Class B carries ten votes; insiders held ~79% of voting power at 3/31/2026. Staggered board, 66⅔% amendment threshold, no written consent, exclusive forum. Public holders cannot influence board composition or force a change of control, which caps any strategic-buyer optionality despite an otherwise attractive asset.

**Execution and management continuity.** The former CFO departed in April 2026 with the Chief Accounting Officer serving as interim principal financial officer; Matt Sonefeldt was appointed CFO on 5/13/2026, the same day as the Q4 release. Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed 6/13/2025, 8/15/2025, 2/5/2026, 4/17/2026 and 5/13/2026 — an unusually dense cluster — and the 10-K itself warns recent senior management changes "may result in temporary disruption." The reason for the CFO departure is not established in the available record.

**Management alignment.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director appear in Form 4s from September 2025 through July 2026. Every disposition was an option exercise with immediate sale or tax withholding, and the metronomic pattern (director Wampler's monthly 2,000-share exercises continuing from $75 down to $22; Cabral 20,000 at $75.00 and 10,000 at $50.00; Benjamin 5,000 at $70.50 and 5,000 at $70.84) reads as pre-scheduled diversification, not informed selling. The informative datapoint is the absence of buying after a ~71% decline, which is a mild negative given blackout constraints around the CFO transition and Q4 earnings.

On 7/22/2026 Form 4s show CEO Jeff Tangney granted an option for **2,480,000 shares** and President Steven Zatz 150,000, ending a hiatus the Q3 10-Q describes as "no stock options since the first quarter of fiscal 2022." Stated correctly: 2.63M option shares equal ~1.42% of ~185M shares outstanding, or ~1.40% post-issuance ownership dilution — modest at the corporate level. Strike price, vesting, term and any performance conditions are undisclosed, so the economics cannot be assessed and no motive should be inferred from timing. Post-drawdown retention grants are standard practice, and FY2026 "compensation actually paid" of **-$34.1M** shows the CEO genuinely bore the shareholder loss. Against that, incentive metrics are Revenue and Adjusted EBITDA with no per-share or return measures, so an SBC-funded revenue build that neutralizes buyback capacity can still score well. Cumulative indexed TSR of 43.96 versus a peer group at 209.64 is the summary of the last five years.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent, large, two-sided event. Near-term ATM IV is **134.9%** against 56.9% one-year realized volatility — a 78-point spread at the 100th percentile of the current chain — with pronounced **backwardation** (134.9% at 15 days versus 78.9% at six months) and an implied move of **±22.9%** on the August 21 expiry. ATM IV rose from 89.8% to 134.9% since July 19 while the price moved only +0.5%, which is consistent with an approaching earnings date rather than directional repositioning. Skew is call-heavy (-10.9%), put/call volume is 0.58, and put/call open interest collapsed from 1.10 to **0.41**, indicating speculative upside positioning into the print. Against 16.1% short interest and 6.0 days to cover, that combination creates real squeeze potential on any upside surprise.

The takeaway cuts against both a long and a short here. Option premium is expensive on both sides, the distribution is wide, and the positioning is crowded on the call side while the shares are crowded on the short side. Neither leg offers favorable odds without a fundamental edge on the quarter.

Risk factors score 4.0: multiple live binaries (earnings within days, litigation in discovery with named officers, derivative suits, MFN policy, single >10% customer) whose magnitudes are not resolvable from the disclosed record.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($30, +37%).** The Q4 +5.1% exit rate proves to be a timing trough. MFN uncertainty resolves, deferred pharma programs return, and NRR stabilizes near 110%. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA holds near $340-350M rather than falling to $329M as AI spending front-loads and then flattens. Management executes the $500M authorization at $18-24, retiring 12-13% of shares and lifting per-share cash flow ~14%. With 16.1% short interest and 6 days to cover, stabilization forces a covering bid. At 12x EV/adjusted EBITDA on ~$340M with ~173M shares post-buyback, the shares reach $28, and a squeeze plus multiple recovery toward the analyst median of $23.50-high of $42.00 supports $30. Requires: a Q1 FY2027 print showing revenue growth reaccelerating above ~7%, NRR stabilizing, and demonstrated share-count reduction.

**Bear case ($15, -31%).** The deceleration continues. OpenEvidence takes measurable share of pharma promotional budget as its academic-center deployments compound; Doximity's newsfeed placement loses relevance to AI answer engines at the point of care. NRR falls below 105%, revenue turns flat-to-negative in FY2028, and adjusted EBITDA settles near $290-300M as AI spending proves permanent rather than transitional. The >10% customer renegotiates. Litigation discovery produces adverse findings about how competitive information was obtained. At 6.5x EV/EBITDA on $300M plus ~$700M net cash across 180M shares, that is $14.7. Nothing in the balance sheet prevents this outcome — solvency is not the risk, terminal value is.

**Base case ($22, ~0%).** FY2027 delivers roughly the reported guide: revenue ~$671M, adjusted EBITDA ~$329M. The market holds a 9-10x EV/EBITDA multiple appropriate for a decelerating, high-margin attention platform with contested terminal value. 9.5x × $329M + $748.6M net cash ÷ ~180M shares = $21.50, plus modest buyback accretion = ~$22. Base case equals the current price, which is the core of the recommendation: the shares are approximately fairly valued with a fat left tail from competitive displacement and a fat right tail from buyback plus squeeze, and no edge on which tail resolves.

The score trajectory (5.5 → 7.0 → 5.8 → 5.2) has declined for three consecutive reads, and the current data confirms rather than reverses the direction. Since the July 19 report the price is essentially unchanged (+1.7%), estimates are flat over 30 days, and the only new information is competitive (OpenEvidence's July institutional announcements), governance (the 2.48M-share option grant with undisclosed terms), and structural (options market repricing a ±23% event). This is not a price decline being confused for deterioration — the price is flat and the fundamental record has not improved.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The nearest dated catalyst is the Q1 FY2027 earnings release, implied by the options term structure to fall on or before the August 21, 2026 expiry (the prior print was May 13, 2026; the quarter ended June 30, 2026). A reaffirmation of ~4% growth with further NRR deterioration would likely re-test the $17.15 52-week low. Beyond that, the next scheduled catalysts are the Q2 FY2027 print (roughly early November) and OpenEvidence litigation discovery milestones, which have no public schedule.

**Timing window.** Days, not months, for the near catalyst — which is the problem. A short entered now pays 134.9% implied volatility of event risk with no informational advantage on the quarter. Beyond the print, the structural thesis needs two to four more quarters to express, during which the borrow carries against a business generating ~$300M of annual free cash flow with $748.6M of net cash.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 16.1% of float short with 6.0 days to cover. That is crowded — high enough that a modest beat or a visible buyback acceleration produces a violent covering move, and low enough that the incremental short adds to an already-consensus position.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning contradicts the bearish case. Skew is call-heavy at -10.9%, put/call volume is 0.58, and put/call open interest fell from 1.10 to 0.41 since July 19. The market is not paying up for downside protection; it is buying upside. Near-term IV at 134.9% versus 56.9% realized makes expressing the view through puts prohibitively expensive, and elevated near-term premium implies elevated carrying cost for a synthetic short.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $15 (-31%). Upside risk on a squeeze or a stabilization print is $28-30 (+28-37%), with the $500M authorization providing a mechanical bid at these levels. That is roughly 1:1, materially below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The structural thesis is credible but the mechanics are hostile: crowded short interest, call-tilted options positioning, prohibitive near-term premium, a fortress balance sheet with 19% of equity value in net cash, an authorized buyback equal to ~12-13% of shares, and an imminent binary with a ±23% implied move. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria** (any two would move this to HOLD; all three plus a stabilized exit rate would support BUY):

1. **Revenue trajectory stabilizes.** Reported quarterly revenue growth above ~7% year over year for two consecutive quarters, with net revenue retention at or above 110%. The single most important number in the next print is NRR; management has stated it is directly tied to the growth rate.
2. **Buyback converts to share-count reduction.** Diluted share count declining at least 5% year over year, demonstrating that the $500M authorization is shrinking the equity rather than offsetting stock compensation running at 18.9% of revenue.
3. **Competitive and legal overhang resolves.** Either a settlement or dismissal in the OpenEvidence matter that removes injunctive risk and the personal exposure of the CTO and Director of AI Products, or disclosure that Marketing Solutions revenue per targeted physician is holding against AI-native entrants.

**Valuation re-entry.** A price near **$16-17** would put EV at roughly $2.15-2.30B, or ~6.5-7.0x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of $329M with ~19% of the (then) equity value in net cash. At that multiple the terminal-decline scenario is substantially discounted and the buyback optionality dominates, which would justify capital despite the structural questions.

**Expected persistence of headwinds.** Two to four quarters minimum. Pharma budget caution tied to MFN pricing negotiations is a policy process with no disclosed resolution date; the AI investment cycle behind the ~640bp of guided margin compression is unlikely to abate within one fiscal year; and the litigation is in discovery with no public trial schedule.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q1 FY2027 print, due within roughly two weeks of this report, is the immediate reassessment point — specifically the NRR disclosure, quarterly revenue growth, Q1 repurchase volume and average price, and any update on the OpenEvidence docket. A second reassessment at the Q2 FY2027 print (approximately early November 2026) will establish whether the +5.1% Q4 exit rate was timing or trend.

**What would make us wrong.** If Q1 FY2027 shows revenue growth of 8-10% with NRR back above 112% and the company discloses aggressive Q1 buyback execution below $22, the base case moves from $22 to the high $20s and this call is wrong by roughly 25%. That is the scenario the call-heavy options positioning is expressing, and it is not a remote possibility — it is why we avoid rather than short.

## Conclusion
