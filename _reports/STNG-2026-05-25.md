## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is delivering extraordinary financial results driven by the U.S.-Iran conflict's impact on product tanker trade routes. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.02 beat consensus estimates, and forward Q2 pool/spot bookings at $61,000-$96,000/day for key vessel classes imply sequential earnings could be substantially higher than Q1. At $80/share, the stock trades at approximately 6.3x current-year consensus EPS ($12.74) — a multiple that appears discounted given the near-term earnings trajectory and strong pro-forma net cash position.

The critical risk is geopolitical: recent reports of LNG tankers transiting Hormuz represent potential early de-escalation signals. If Hormuz fully reopens, product tanker rates would compress significantly from current levels, though Bank of America argues inventory replenishment needs would keep rates "historically elevated" even in a de-escalation scenario. The company's $18,000-20,000/day breakeven and fortress balance sheet provide substantial downside protection. Management is executing one of the most shareholder-aligned capital allocation strategies in shipping: $605M in 1.75% convertibles paired with aggressive buybacks at prices well below the $100.39 conversion strike, plus a $500M securities repurchase authorization.

The position merits BUY with explicit sizing discipline — the likelihood of continued elevated rates justifies holding, but the binary nature of the Hormuz catalyst requires a defined reduction framework if de-escalation confirms.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates 87 owned product tankers (32 LR2, 41 MR, 14 Handymax) with an average fleet age of 10.2 years. The fleet operates primarily through Scorpio-managed pools, providing both spot market exposure and operational efficiencies. The company has 10 newbuildings under construction including its first two VLCCs (delivery H2 2028), representing a strategic expansion into crude carriers.

STNG is one of the largest publicly-listed pure-play product tanker operators globally, with 7.39 million DWT of total capacity including newbuildings. The fleet is predominantly scrubber-equipped, providing a fuel cost advantage in environments where the high-sulfur/low-sulfur fuel oil spread is wide.

The business model exhibits extreme operating leverage: vessel operating costs are largely fixed, so TCE rate increases flow nearly directly to the bottom line. Q1 2026 demonstrated this clearly — a 57% YoY increase in average daily TCE produced a 208% increase in adjusted net income.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

Q1 2026 financial performance:
- Vessel Revenue: $312.9M (+46.2% YoY)
- TCE Revenue: $303.0M (+48.4% YoY)
- Adjusted Net Income: $150.9M (+208% YoY)
- Adjusted EPS: $3.02 (vs. $1.03 Q1 2025)
- Average daily TCE: $37,697 (+57% YoY)

Q2 2026 forward pool/spot bookings are substantially higher: LR2 at $96,000/day (41% of days booked), MR at $66,000/day (53% of days booked), Handymax at $61,000/day. These represent pool and spot-market employment only; time-charter rates are materially lower (LR2 $30,300/day, MR $26,500/day, Handymax $23,000/day), and one MR operates on bareboat at $12,986/day. If blended rates remain elevated for the full quarter, Q2 EPS could be substantially higher than Q1, though the precise outcome depends on the mix of pool/spot versus time-charter days, off-hire, and voyage expenses. The company does not provide a forward-looking TCE reconciliation.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is among the strongest in shipping:
- Pro-forma net cash (as of May 1, 2026): $876M (after April convertible and vessel sales, but prior to the May 12 note reopening)
- Equity: $3.41B (83.6% of total assets)
- Gross debt: ~$932M pro-forma as of May 1 (including $375M convertibles, $200M senior notes due 2030, and vessel financings), increasing to approximately $1.16B after the May 12 reopening
- No material maturities until 2028 ($357M in vessel financings); $10.7M remaining in Q2 2026
- Debt cost: 1.75% on $605M total convertibles (after May 12 close); remainder at low floating rates on vessel financings

After incorporating the May 12 completed note reopening (~$249M net proceeds) and concurrent $55M share repurchase, the pro-forma net cash position is estimated at approximately $1.07B, positioning STNG to withstand a severe rate downcycle without covenant concerns, while providing optionality to acquire distressed assets or accelerate buybacks during weakness.

### Cash Flow Generation

Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $163.2M. Combined with $218.7M in vessel sale proceeds, total cash inflows substantially exceeded outflows (newbuildings $68.7M, debt repayment $39.3M, dividends $23.3M). The quarter produced a $232.4M net cash increase.

At Q2 forward pool/spot rates, operating cash flow could exceed $250M quarterly.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

Four consecutive earnings beats: +26.7% (Q2 2025), +8.8% (Q3 2025), +0.7% (Q4 2025), and a beat in Q1 2026. The trajectory shows management consistently underguiding relative to actual performance.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions

Current-quarter EPS estimates have risen from $1.40 (90 days ago) to $5.58 today — a **+298%** revision. Current-year estimates moved from $5.80 to $12.74 (+120%). This revision velocity reflects the market systematically underestimating earnings power at current rates.

Next-year estimates ($6.47) imply a ~49% earnings decline from current-year levels, reflecting consensus expectations of rate normalization. The spread between CY ($12.74) and NY ($6.47) is the market's embedded view on geopolitical duration.

### Fleet Growth

Global product tanker fleet growth is expected to be approximately 3% over the next 3 years per management commentary. Yard capacity constraints limit new ordering, and aging global fleets create natural attrition through scrapping. This supply-side discipline supports structurally higher average rates through the cycle relative to prior decades.

### Strategic Expansion

The VLCC orders (2x 300,000 DWT, H2 2028 delivery) diversify revenue but introduce crude oil market exposure. The newbuilding program (10 vessels total) ensures fleet modernization without near-term delivery pressure.

The investment case therefore rests on the view that "normalized" is higher than historical averages due to supply constraints.

Analyst consensus targets (per available data, which may reflect recent upgrades): median approximately $99-100 with upside from current levels. ## Competitive Landscape

STNG competes with Hafnia, TORM, Ardmore Shipping, and other product tanker operators. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Scale**: Among the largest pure-play fleets, enabling pool efficiency and commercial optimization
2. **Fleet quality**: Average age 10.2 years with scrubber equipment providing a fuel cost advantage (magnitude depends on prevailing HSFO/VLSFO spreads and consumption)
3. **Financial position**: Estimated pro-forma net cash exceeding $1B versus most peers carrying net debt; ability to pursue opportunistic acquisitions
4. **Capital allocation sophistication**: The convertible-funded buyback strategy is unique in the sector

The competitive position is stable-to-improving: fleet renewal is underway, older vessels are being sold at premium prices, and the financial flexibility gap versus peers is widening.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Hormuz Resolution (CRITICAL)

S&P Global reported in mid-May 2026 that several LNG tankers had recently transited Hormuz, representing early signs of potential de-escalation after prolonged disruption. If a diplomatic resolution materializes, product tanker rates would compress — the magnitude depending on the speed and completeness of normalization. Bank of America argues that even in a de-escalation scenario, inventory replenishment needs would sustain rates above historical averages for multiple quarters.

At $35,000/day average rates, STNG's annualized EPS would approximate $6-8 — still supportive of the current stock price but eliminating the extreme upside case. At $25,000/day (full normalization), EPS drops to $3-5, implying current prices embed modest downside risk.

### Secondary Risks

- **Cyclicality**: Tanker rates have historically exhibited boom-bust patterns. Current rates are near all-time highs.
- **Convertible dilution**: $605M at $100.39 conversion implies ~6.0M potential shares (approximately 12% of current float) if stock sustains above $100. Settlement can be in cash, stock, or combination at company's election.
- **Asset value risk**: Vessel secondhand prices are at historical peaks; a cycle turn could compress book value.
- **VLCC execution**: Entry into crude carriers introduces different market dynamics.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call OI ratio collapsed from 2.01 to 0.46 in the most recent period — a significant shift toward call positioning. The P/C volume ratio of 0.74 confirms call-dominant activity. Short interest at 3.8% (1.6 days to cover) is low, indicating the short thesis is uncrowded and squeeze risk is minimal.

The 6-month implied move of 21.2% reflects elevated uncertainty appropriate for a geopolitically-dependent earnings stream. The 2.50 P/C volume ratio at the 6-month tenor suggests some institutional protection for the Hormuz resolution scenario — rational hedging given the binary nature of the catalyst.

## Conclusion

Scorpio Tankers represents a compelling risk/reward at $80 given the combination of extraordinary near-term earnings power, a fortress balance sheet, and management executing an accretive capital structure strategy. The investment case requires active monitoring of the Hormuz de-escalation timeline. Additional tanker transits through the strait would represent the immediate decision point — if physical de-escalation confirms through sustained traffic resumption, reducing the position by 25-30% is appropriate. If early transits prove isolated, the position continues to benefit from historically exceptional rate environments.

BUY at current levels with defined exit criteria and position sizing that acknowledges the binary geopolitical dependency. This is a high-volatility, catalyst-driven tanker-cycle trade — the extraordinary earnings power is real but geopolitically contingent.
