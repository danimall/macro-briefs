## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is two businesses on one balance sheet, reported as one segment. The first is a North American pressure-pumping franchise with roughly 40 active frac fleets, $4.0bn of FY2025 revenue, and an operating margin that has collapsed from 16.0% in FY2023 to 1.8% in FY2025. The second is Liberty Power Innovations, a distributed generation business for AI data centers that has committed approximately $1.9bn to engine and balance-of-plant purchases from Bergen, Wärtsilä and Caterpillar, with deliveries from 2027 through 2030, and which has not yet disclosed a single binding power purchase agreement with an end user. Total liquidity at June 30, 2026 was approximately $1.0bn ($555.4M cash plus $448.3M of revolver availability).

The July 22 second-quarter report crystallized the problem. Revenue rose 14% year over year to $1,188.6M, but cost of services rose 21% to $980.3M — incremental revenue of $146.1M against incremental cost of services of $168.1M, meaning growth carried negative contribution margin before depreciation. Adjusted EBITDA fell 16% year over year to $151.1M and the margin compressed from 17.3% to 12.7%. Management then raised the full-year capital budget by roughly half, to a reported ~$1.5bn (a figure we could not locate in a filing and treat as unconfirmed). The stock fell 22% the following session, and consensus adjusted EPS for next year has been cut 54.3% in thirty days, to $0.34 from $0.74.

We are maintaining a HOLD, and we are explicit about why we are not writing this down to AVOID despite the deteriorating operating arithmetic. There is no structural impairment and no forcing mechanism. Net debt to TTM adjusted EBITDA is 1.31x; the $1.295bn of convertible notes carry a 0.00% coupon with no maturity until 2031 and 2032; the revolver is undrawn with a covenant-compliant borrowing base; cash paid for interest in the first half was $14.4M; and 100% expensing under the July 2025 tax act has already converted cash taxes into a $2.9M net refund. Our base case fair value of $20-23 sits at or modestly above the current price. Issuing a negative rating on a stock that has already fallen 42% from its May peak on precisely the capex-sequencing concern we would cite, where our own base case equals spot and where 12.8% of the float is short into a plausible offtake announcement, would be a view largely already in the price with poor asymmetry. Equally, we cannot underwrite a BUY: the power business is unauditable from outside, estimate revisions are falling in every window, and the company is deploying capital fastest at a ~1% adjusted pre-tax return on capital employed.

## Company Overview

Liberty provides hydraulic fracturing, wireline, proppant delivery, field gas processing and treating, CNG delivery and data analytics to onshore E&P and enhanced-geothermal operators, and owns two Permian sand mines. It operates in the Permian, Williston, Haynesville, Eagle Ford, DJ, Powder River, Appalachia, Anadarko, Uinta and San Juan basins, the Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin and Australia's Beetaloo. Headcount is approximately 5,800, non-unionized, with roughly 500 patents or patent licenses. Differentiation is technological: Quiet Fleet, Tier IV dual-fuel, and the digiFleet electric/hybrid platform (digiFrac, digiPrime), plus wet sand handling, the PropConnect logistics platform and the FracTrends well database. Two commercial additions in the second quarter: SLXRRY last-mile sand slurry delivery and the first digiPrime deployment in Canada.

Liberty Power Innovations expanded into third-party distributed power in January 2025 and acquired IMG Energy Solutions in March 2025 for ~$19.6M cash. Its commercial architecture is Forte (modular site construction), Tempo (power quality management for cyclical AI training loads) and Chorus (blending co-located generation with grid power). In the second quarter Liberty established Liberty Wholesale Commodities to participate directly in ERCOT wholesale markets.

Named frac competitors in the 10-K are Halliburton, Patterson-UTI and ProFrac, with ProPetro regional. In power, the 10-K identifies utility grid power itself as the principal alternative and concedes that power competitors "may have greater access to capital, more established customer bases, deeper vendor relationships, and fewer regulatory hurdles."

Customer concentration remains material: top-five customers were 39% of FY2025 revenue (43% FY2024, 34% FY2023), with both Occidental and XTO Energy individually above 10% in FY2025. First-half 2026 figures for the two largest customers were 10% and 10% (versus 10% and 12% a year earlier) — essentially flat, so the single-quarter easing to 8% and 9% does not establish diversification.

Revenue fell 9.1% in 2024 and 7.2% in 2025; operating income fell 90.4% from the 2023 peak. Adjusted EBITDA margin went from 21.4% to 15.8%. WTI averaged $65.45 in 2025 against $77.58 in 2023, and the US/Canada rig count averaged 709 in Q4 2025 versus 765 a year earlier.

### GAAP earnings measure three small-cap equities, not operations

FY2025 net income of $147.9M included a $162.6M net gain on investments. Reconstructing the pre-tax bridge — operating income $72.7M less interest $40.3M plus the $162.6M gain — leaves roughly $32.5M of pre-tax income from operations. The pattern intensified in 2026: first-half GAAP net income of $65.7M against adjusted net income of $24.6M, with $60.2M of investment gains. Second-quarter GAAP diluted EPS of $0.26 was 2.9x adjusted EPS of $0.09.

The portfolio at June 30, 2026 was $184.9M at fair value: Fervo Energy $104.8M (up from $40.7M following its second-quarter IPO, generating the $64.1M quarterly gain), Tamboran $43.4M, Oklo $28.1M, other $8.6M. TTM gain on investments was $135.3M against TTM adjusted pre-tax net income of $28.5M.

This matters for two reasons. First, the earnings-surprise record presented for this name — four consecutive "beats" including a 659.5% surprise in the June quarter — compares GAAP actuals against estimates on a different basis and is not informative. On a matched non-GAAP basis, adjusted EPS of $0.09 beat a $0.07 consensus, a small and ordinary beat. Second, the marks are symmetric: the same mechanics that produced $135.3M of gains can produce comparable losses, and Fervo alone is now 57% of the portfolio.

### Return on capital

The company's own disclosed metric is the cleanest summary. Adjusted pre-tax ROCE: 42.09% (2023) → 19.06% (2024) → 8.07% (2025) → approximately 1% for the twelve months to June 30, 2026 ($28.5M of TTM adjusted pre-tax net income on $2,725.3M of average capital employed). Capital employed grew 48.3% year over year while adjusted pre-tax profit shrank. Part of this is mechanical — $219.2M of construction in progress and $248.0M of capital deposits earn nothing today — and part is the frac trough, which is cyclical. But the direction of both the numerator and the denominator is unambiguous, and this is the metric management selected for its own compensation plan.

### Second-quarter cost arithmetic

Cost of services was 82.5% of revenue against 77.9% a year earlier, roughly 460 basis points of deterioration matching the adjusted EBITDA margin decline. Management attributes it to parts, materials and personnel costs "commensurate with the increase in activity levels" plus materials pricing. The $7.7M of tariffs sat in a separate line and does not explain the gap. Three benign readings cannot be distinguished from the disclosures: mix shift toward low-margin pass-through materials and logistics, power start-up costs buried in cost of services under single-segment reporting, or a front-loaded late-quarter activity ramp. DD&A fell 12% to $114.2M as equipment aged off its depreciable life, flattering reported operating income relative to cash margins.

The legacy business was barely self-funding before the power build started.

### Capital structure

Cash rose from $27.6M to $555.4M and long-term debt from $241.5M to $1,279.9M in six months. On February 6, 2026 Liberty issued $770.0M of 0.00% convertible notes due 2031 (conversion price ~$34.50, a 32.5% premium); on March 30 it issued $525.0M of 0.00% notes due 2032 (conversion price ~$37.44, a 30.0% premium). Principal must be cash-settled on conversion; only excess value may be shares. Capped calls costing $186.5M extend effective dilution protection to $65.10 and $72.00, charged to APIC (which fell $145.4M). Liberty integrated the notes and calls for tax purposes, making the $186.5M deductible as OID interest and recording $42.5M of deferred tax assets. Cash interest paid in the first half was $14.4M.

Maximum theoretical share-equivalents are 36.34M — 22.3% of existing shares, 18.2% post-issuance ownership dilution — but with both strikes 43-48% out of the money at $19.40, the notes now behave as straight zero-coupon debt with a 2031-2032 bullet. Derived leverage on TTM adjusted EBITDA of $562.2M: gross 2.30x, net 1.31x, 1.78x including finance leases.

The revolver is $750.0M committed with a $467.7M borrowing base (which explicitly includes certain power generating assets), zero drawn, $19.4M of letters of credit and $448.3M available; covenants are 3.5x leverage, 2.5x senior leverage, and a 1.0x fixed-charge test triggered only below the greater of 10% of the borrowing base or $52.5M. Compliant at June 30. One flexibility item is gone: the February 3, 2026 amendment's $600M "Permitted Bridge Indebtedness" basket required incurrence by June 30, 2026 and expired unused, and the amended convertible basket has been consumed.

A recomputed Altman Z-score on the June 30 balance sheet is 2.30 (from 2.91 on the FY2025 balance sheet), squarely in the grey zone — the score fell despite the liquidity injection because assets and liabilities grew faster than EBIT. The market-equity term supplies 0.76 of the 2.30, so the reading is highly price-dependent, and the EBIT term contributes only 0.04. This is a derived screen, not a default forecast, and it should not be read as insolvency risk against a 2031 first maturity.

**Financial health: 4.8.** Liquidity, leverage, coupon structure and covenant headroom are genuinely strong for a cyclical services company. Against that: negative first-half free cash flow of $213.5M, a second-half capex implication of roughly $1.15bn if the ~$1.5bn budget is correct, $1.9bn of committed equipment purchases, a dividend running above adjusted earnings, and the loss of the bridge basket.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue is growing: $2,209.8M in the first half against $2,020.0M, up 9.4%, with the second quarter up 14% year over year and 16% sequentially. Management points to years of industry fleet attrition and equipment cannibalization becoming visible as activity improves, and to secular intensity — proppant per well from ~6M lbs (2014) to ~25M lbs (2025), days to drill from 28 to 16, horizontal rigs at 87% of the North American fleet.

But the growth is not yet earning anything. Pricing recovery is characterized only as "modest" from cyclical lows; the rig count was essentially flat year over year (688 versus 686); and even with WTI averaging $95.65 in the second quarter on the Hormuz disruption — before moderating to roughly $76 through July 20 — management reports large US and Canadian producers "remain cautious toward increasing activity levels." That is the key macro point: the crude spike did not convert into completions demand.

Consensus has moved decisively against the name. Current-quarter adjusted EPS is $0.10 (down 22.4% in 30 days), next quarter $0.01 (down 77.6%), current year $0.22 (down 32.5% in 90 days), next year $0.34 (down 54.3% in 30 days). Six analysts contribute. Revisions of this magnitude in the wrong direction are one of the more reliable forward indicators available and they are the principal reason we are not upgrading on the lower price.

The power opportunity is real in size and unverified in contract. What exists: a Vantage Data Centers agreement to develop at least 1 GW with a firm reservation of 400 MW for 2027 delivery — qualified in the 10-K's own risk factors as "not with the end-user of the eventual data center for actual provision of power"; a preliminary energy services agreement with an unnamed developer for 330 MW in Texas, "subject to executing a final binding contract"; a ~3 GW 2029 target that the 10-K states is "not currently under a contract"; a PowerBridge JV (announced July 22) targeting over 300 MW of initial deployment in late 2027, where PowerBridge develops the campus and Liberty supplies and operates generation; and an SLB alliance in which both parties are suppliers to data center projects rather than customers of each other. No hyperscale offtaker is named in any filing or release. The PowerBridge JV does not appear in the 10-Q's subsequent-events note, most plausibly because no capital was committed at inception — consistent with pipeline rather than backlog.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Revenue is expanding and the demand backdrop for distributed generation is corroborated by confirmed hyperscaler capex across the AI complex. What is missing is any evidence that either business converts growth into returns on the capital being deployed, and estimate revisions say sell-side confidence in near-term earnings has fallen sharply.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.40: enterprise value is $3,166M equity plus $1,291.8M debt less $555.4M cash = $3,902M, or **6.9x** TTM adjusted EBITDA of $562.2M (7.4x including $264.2M of finance leases; ~6.6x if the $184.9M investment portfolio is netted). Price to book is 1.61x on $12.03 of book value per share. Forward adjusted P/E is roughly 88x current-year consensus ($0.22) and 57x next-year ($0.34), consistent with the reported 57.7 forward multiple. Trailing GAAP P/E of 26.2 is not a usable metric here given investment marks.

Peer multiples in oilfield equipment and services are too dispersed to anchor much — TDW 12.4x, OII 11.8x, INVX 38.2x, WHD 55.0x, HLX 100.5x, median 38.2x — because each reflects a different point in a different cycle. The more useful comparison is Liberty against its own mid-cycle earnings power. FY2024 adjusted EBITDA was $921.6M and FY2025 $634.1M; TTM is $562.2M. On $900M of mid-cycle EBITDA, today's enterprise value is 4.3x. That is the genuine bull argument on the legacy business: the multiple is not stretched against normalized frac earnings, and the market is paying roughly nothing for the power option after a 42% decline from May.

The counterweight is that mid-cycle EBITDA requires pricing recovery that management describes as modest, and the second quarter demonstrated negative incremental contribution margin — meaning added volume currently subtracts gross profit. If incremental margins stay negative, higher activity does not produce mid-cycle EBITDA. And the capital base against which any recovery is measured has grown 48% year over year, so a return to FY2024 EBITDA on a $4.5bn asset base is a materially worse business than FY2024 was.

Consensus targets are low $23, mean $29.81, median $29.00, high $36.00 — a mean 54% above spot, with 8 of 13 ratings at buy or better and none at sell. We treat this as evidence that the sell-side embeds power success, not as independent confirmation of value. Analyst targets function as a floor mainly where estimates are rising and the multiple is modest; here estimates are falling hard, which is the opposite configuration.

Our own fair value:

- **Base $20-23.** 6.5-7.0x on $600-620M of normalized adjusted EBITDA (modest frac pricing recovery, no power contribution), less $736.4M of net debt, plus the $184.9M investment portfolio. Midpoint ~$21.50, about 11% above spot.
- **Bull $32.** A named, binding offtake with disclosed economics for 1 GW or more, which would let the market capitalize power EBITDA rather than discount an option; plus 8x on $750M of recovering legacy EBITDA. Roughly +65%.
- **Bear $12.** Frac EBITDA reverting toward $450-500M with a 5.5x multiple, plus equity issuance at a discount to fund the equipment commitments. Roughly −38%.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Cheap on cycle-adjusted EBITDA, expensive on any forward earnings measure, with the discount to mid-cycle justified in part by falling estimates and negative incremental margins. Base case sits close to spot, which is exactly why the correct posture is neither to add nor to sell short.

## Competitive Landscape

In completions, Liberty is a technology-led operator in a business where the marginal competitor sets price. The industry-supply argument — years of under-investment and cannibalization thinning the effective fleet — is credible and is the mechanism by which pricing eventually recovers. It is not yet visible in Liberty's numbers: flat rig counts, "modest" pricing recovery, 460bp of cost-of-services deterioration.

In power, Liberty's claimed differentiation is proprietary controls (Tempo) that integrate multiple OEM engine types, plus the Chorus/LWC combination of on-site generation with ERCOT and PJM market participation. Two disclosures cut against durability. The 10-K states management "has not directly engaged in the distributed power solutions business before." And management itself observes that hyperscalers "continue to expand their internal technical and commercial capabilities, enabling a more comprehensive evaluation of long-term power and infrastructure strategies" — buyers becoming more sophisticated may favor integrated providers, or may disaggregate the stack and procure engines directly, compressing the intermediary's margin. The SLB alliance is itself evidence that a far larger oilfield services company sees the same opportunity and will compete for the same engine allocations, EPC labor and interconnection positions.

There is one genuine competitive asset in the commitment structure: with two-to-four-year engine lead times, a provider without secured allocation cannot bid credibly. Liberty has secured allocation from Bergen, Wärtsilä and Caterpillar. That is a real, if expensive, position.

The grid-displacement risk is structural: the 10-K warns customers may use Liberty's generation only until they are interconnected, which would truncate asset life relative to a 20-year engine.

**Competitive position: 5.0.** Stable-to-eroding in the core on margins, speculative but not baseless in power.

## Risk Assessment

**Funding and sequencing.** Approximately $1.9bn of committed equipment purchases ($1.1bn remaining on Bergen/Wärtsilä/others plus ~$801M Caterpillar) against ~$1.0bn of liquidity and revenue that begins in late 2027 at the earliest, contingent on contracts not yet signed. Management's stated plan — "project specific financing including non-recourse debt, and co-investments or equity" — is unexecuted. The bridge basket expired unused and the convertible basket is consumed. Equity issuance at $19.40 against $34.50/$37.44 convertible strikes would be highly dilutive.

**Zero segment transparency.** One operating and reportable segment, with the CODM using consolidated net income. No power revenue, EBITDA, capex or asset base is disclosed. Investors cannot verify whether power start-up costs are depressing completions margins, or size returns on the $1.9bn. This is the single largest disclosure gap and the main reason a BUY is not underwritable.

**A new risk factor.** The only material risk-factor addition in the second-quarter 10-Q concerns laws and regulations on data center development — moratoria, permitting, environmental review, and restrictions on energy, water, traffic and noise. Management added it in the same quarter it committed to $1.9bn of engines.

**Tariffs, uncapped.** $7.7M of second-quarter tariffs on imported pump components. Bergen is Norwegian, Wärtsilä Finnish, and the ~$801M Caterpillar contract explicitly excludes import taxes, duties and tariffs, which Liberty pays. On ~$1.9bn of largely imported equipment this is contractually unhedged.

**New regulatory perimeter.** FERC, NERC, PUCT and ERCOT exposure Liberty has never had, plus ERCOT's RTC+B program. LWC adds merchant commodity and collateral risk with no disclosed position limits, VaR framework or collateral policy.

**Cyclical dependence.** Essentially all current revenue is North American completions. WTI averaged $95.65 in the second quarter on the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption before moderating to $76.07 through July 20 after the June ceasefire, yet even at those prices management reports large US and Canadian producers "remain cautious toward increasing activity levels." The crude spike did not convert into completions demand; a normalization of transit into a period of restored global supply would pressure activity further.

**TRA acceleration.** $66.9M payable, with change of control triggering lump-sum acceleration — a structural impediment to any acquisition of Liberty, which removes a takeout floor.

**Investment-mark volatility.** $184.9M portfolio, 57% Fervo, driving GAAP results that bear no relation to operations.

**Capital allocation signals.** Zero repurchases in the first half despite $270.2M authorized, and the authorization expired July 31, 2026 with no renewal disclosed — versus $24.0M repurchased at an average $15.50 in the prior-year period. First-half dividends of $29.3M exceeded adjusted net income of $24.6M, a ~119% adjusted payout.

**Insider activity — the full split signal.** On the buy side: director Arjun Murti made two open-market purchases of almost exactly $250,000 each, 9,695.559 shares at $25.785 on February 17 and 14,053.317 shares at $17.79 on July 28, five days after the crash; CEO Ron Gusek made no open-market sales and holds 1,070,353 shares. On the sell side, which deserves equal weight: CFO Michael Stock sold 78,328 shares for approximately $2.19M across 2026, including 3,333 at $17.95 on August 3 and 3,333 at $20.00 on August 4 — continuing after the crash, leaving 767,045 shares (roughly 9% of his position); Chief Legal Officer Sean Elliott sold 25,000 at $25.40 on February 6, the day the 2031 notes closed; and Chairman William Kimble sold 7,350 at $33.92 on May 5, within about 1% of the observable 2026 peak. The CFO's transactions are all multiples of 3,333 and price-insensitive, consistent with a pre-arranged plan, and we draw no valuation inference from them. The Chairman's sale within 1% of the peak and the CLO's sale on the financing date are less easily dismissed, though neither meets the threshold of clustered C-suite selling of majority positions. Net read: the insider record is genuinely mixed. Murti's repeated purchase into weakness is the most informative single datapoint, but it totals $500,000 and is outweighed in dollars by executive sales. This supports HOLD, not BUY.

**Compensation design.** Performance measures are Adjusted Pre-tax EPS, Adjusted ROCE and Comparative ROCE, all current-period capital-efficiency metrics. A multi-year build adding $1.9bn of capital employed with revenue starting in late 2027 mechanically depresses them, creating tension between the incentive plan and the announced strategy. Comparative ROCE and committee discretion may cushion this; we did not verify 2026 grant design.

### Options Market Signal

Implied volatility has collapsed from 132.6% to 69.3% between the June and July snapshots, while the stock fell 12.3% over that window and a further ~19% after. Two readings matter. First, 69.3% ATM IV is still elevated in absolute terms for a $3bn services company, so the market continues to price a wide distribution — appropriate for a name whose outcome depends on a binary contract announcement. Second, put/call open interest collapsed from 1.68 to 0.04, an extreme call tilt. Combined with 12.8% short interest (14.97M shares, 4.0 days to cover), this is a positioning configuration that gaps upward on good news: a named hyperscale offtake would force short covering into thin put protection. That asymmetry is a direct argument against a short position and a meaningful argument against a negative rating at this price, even though it says nothing about intrinsic value. It also cuts the other way on bad news — call-heavy positioning offers no downside cushion if the company announces an equity raise.

**Risk factors: 4.3.** The risks are numerous, concentrated in one unverifiable business line, and partly unpriceable because of the single-segment presentation. Some are now discounted after the 42% decline; the funding gap and the tariff exposure are not, because neither has a dated resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($32, +65%)

Liberty secures a binding, named end-user power purchase agreement for the Vantage 400 MW reservation or the 330 MW Texas project, with disclosed economics and a cost-recovery structure, converting a $1.9bn commitment from a liability into a contracted asset base. Project-level non-recourse financing closes, removing the equity-dilution overhang. Segment reporting arrives in 2027 and reveals power returns above the legacy business's cost of capital. Concurrently the frac cycle turns: years of fleet attrition, 25M lbs of proppant per well and 87% horizontal drilling produce pricing that restores incremental contribution margin, and adjusted EBITDA recovers toward $750-900M. At 8x $750M plus power option value, the equity is worth roughly $32. The mechanics of the move would be violent given 12.8% short interest and call-tilted open interest. The financing already executed supports this path: $1.26bn net raised at a 0.00% cash coupon with capped calls to $65.10/$72.00 and an interest deduction on zero-coupon paper is competent, cheap capital, and 100% expensing has converted cash taxes into a refund.

### Bear Case ($12, −38%)

No named offtake materializes through 2027. Liberty pays Caterpillar, Bergen and Wärtsilä on contractual schedules — capital deposits already rose from $116.0M to $248.0M in six months — while completions cash flow stays negative on capex, forcing either equity issuance at a depressed price or an expensive project-financing structure. Incremental contribution margin stays negative as pass-through materials and logistics mix grows. WTI retraces on Hormuz normalization, and completions activity falls rather than recovers. Adjusted EBITDA settles at $450-500M against a $4.5bn asset base, ROCE stays near zero, and the dividend is cut. At 5.5x and with dilution, the equity is worth roughly $12. The investment portfolio, 57% concentrated in a newly public geothermal developer, marks down alongside.

### Why HOLD rather than either extreme

The negative case has no forcing mechanism and no date. The first debt maturity is 2031, the coupon is zero, cash interest was $14.4M in the first half, the revolver is undrawn with $448.3M available, covenants are met with substantial headroom, and cash taxes are a refund. Nothing compels a financing in the next twelve months. Our base case of $20-23 brackets the current price, so a negative rating would be asserting that a stock already down 42% from May and 22% in a single session on exactly this capex-sequencing concern is still overvalued — which the arithmetic does not support. Add 12.8% short interest, 4.0 days to cover, a call-tilted option book and a consensus mean 54% above spot, and the balance of near-term event risk is skewed upward, not downward.

The positive case fails on verifiability rather than plausibility. We cannot underwrite returns on $1.9bn of capital when no power revenue, EBITDA, capex or asset base is disclosed; when the anchor customer relationship is explicitly not with the end user of power; when the 3 GW pipeline is stated in the 10-K to be "not currently under a contract"; and when next-year consensus EPS has been cut 54.3% in thirty days. Energy services BUYs require company-specific evidence of outperformance, and the evidence here runs the other way: 1% ROCE, negative incremental contribution margin, negative first-half free cash flow, a lapsed buyback authorization and a dividend above adjusted earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026) is the primary checkpoint. It should establish whether second-half capex tracked toward the reported ~$1.5bn full-year figure, whether incremental contribution margin turned positive, whether a buyback authorization was renewed, and whether the PowerBridge JV agreement is filed as an exhibit with terms.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. A disclosed binding power purchase agreement with a named end user of power (not a developer or reservation), covering at least 400 MW, with sufficient economic detail to estimate contracted EBITDA — plus a closed project-financing structure that does not issue common equity below $25.
2. Separate segment reporting for the power business, or equivalent disclosure of power revenue, EBITDA and invested capital, showing returns above cost of capital. Alternatively, two consecutive quarters of positive incremental contribution margin in completions (cost of services below 80% of revenue, versus 82.5% in Q2 2026) with adjusted EBITDA margin recovering above 15%.
3. Price toward $14-15 with the balance sheet and commitment schedule unchanged, which would put enterprise value near 5x TTM adjusted EBITDA and roughly 3.4x mid-cycle — a discount wide enough to absorb a dilutive financing.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. Announcement of a common equity offering, or a project financing whose terms include equity-linked consideration below the current price.
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin below 11% for two consecutive quarters, or cost of services above 84% of revenue, indicating that activity growth is structurally value-destroying.
3. A dividend cut or suspension, which would confirm that the power build has consumed the capital-return capacity that currently supports the shareholder base.
4. Cancellation, deferral or renegotiation of the Vantage 400 MW reservation or the 330 MW Texas project, or disclosure of material Caterpillar cancellation charges — any of which would convert the power option into a sunk cost with a quantified bill.
5. Revolver drawings above $200M combined with a borrowing base decline, which would signal that working capital and capex are outrunning internally generated cash before power revenue starts.

**Position guidance:** existing holders should carry the position through the October report rather than sell into the post-crash trough; no new capital at $19.40. The next twelve months are dominated by a single unpredictable disclosure event, and neither side of that event is investable on current information.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 4.9.** This is a modest step down from 5.2 in the prior review, and the reason is fundamental rather than price-related: second-quarter incremental contribution margin was negative, adjusted EBITDA fell 16% year over year, first-half free cash flow was negative $213.5M, next-year consensus EPS was cut 54.3% in thirty days, and Liberty added a further $801M of equipment commitments in July while raising its capital budget. The lower share price, taken alone, improves rather than impairs the setup — enterprise value is now approximately 6.9x TTM and 4.3x mid-cycle adjusted EBITDA — and we have not marked the score down for the decline.

Liberty has converted an option into an obligation: $1.9bn of contractual equipment payments against no disclosed binding offtake with an end user of power, financed so far by $1.295bn of zero-coupon convertibles and a tax-deferral tailwind, with the bridge facility expired and the convertible basket consumed. The balance sheet can carry this for years — net leverage of 1.31x, no maturity until 2031, $1.0bn of liquidity, covenant compliance with headroom — which is why we reject the framing that this is a distressed or structurally impaired equity. What we cannot do is underwrite the returns, because the company reports one segment and the chief operating decision maker uses consolidated net income, which is itself dominated by marks on three small-cap energy-technology holdings.

At $19.40, with a base case of $20-23, a bull case of $32 contingent on a contract announcement that would gap the stock against 12.8% short interest, and a bear case of $12 contingent on a financing that has not occurred and has no date, the distribution is wide and roughly balanced. That is the definition of a hold. The next binding datapoint is a named offtake agreement or the Q3 disclosure of second-half capital deployment; until one arrives, this is an option on a business nobody outside the company can value.
