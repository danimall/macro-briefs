**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ADP is one of the highest-quality recurring-revenue franchises in the market: 1.1 million clients, 42 million workers paid across 140+ countries, ~92% revenue retention, ~13-year average client tenure in Employer Services, and 23% free-cash-flow margins. The Q3 FY2026 print (April 29, 2026) was a clean beat-and-raise: revenue +7% to $5.94B, diluted EPS $3.38 (+10%), EBIT margin 30.0% (+80 bps), and management lifted FY26 guidance to ~6-7% revenue growth and ~10-11% adjusted EPS growth. Estimates are drifting up modestly (next-year consensus +1.9% over 90 days), and the company has been a Dividend King for 50+ years with a fresh $6B buyback authorized in January 2026.

Despite that, the stock has fallen roughly 30% from its 52-week high (~$330 to ~$228), with most of the damage occurring as the beat-and-raise was met by multiple compression rather than re-rating. The market is not debating execution; it is repricing two things ADP cannot fully control. First, a meaningful slice of recent margin expansion came from interest earned on the $46.8B of client funds the company holds in trust ($1.19B in FY25, ~$999M in nine months of FY26 at a ~3.3% yield), near-pure-margin income that compresses if the Fed cuts. Second, ADP's own proprietary labor data is flashing deceleration: pays-per-control growth stuck at +1% and PEO worksite-employee growth slowing to +2%, both leading indicators of a cooling U.S. employment base that directly feeds ADP's per-employee fee model.

The result is a high-quality compounder trading at ~21x forward EPS, a clear discount to its industrials-peer median of ~31x and below its own recent history, but with a near-term earnings algorithm that is partly rate- and cycle-dependent at exactly the moment both variables are turning. That combination supports a HOLD with a growth bias. The franchise is intact and the entry price is more reasonable than three months ago, but the specific catalysts that would convert this to a high-conviction long (rate-path clarity and stabilizing pays-per-control) have not yet resolved.

## Company Overview

ADP is a 75-year-old global leader in Human Capital Management. It pays roughly 1 in 6 U.S. workers and moves approximately $3.3 trillion of client funds annually. The business runs in two reportable segments:

- **Employer Services (ES)** — ~67% of revenue (~$13.9B FY25). Cloud HCM platforms tiered by client size: RUN (940K+ small businesses), Workforce Now (90K+ mid/large), and Lyric HCM (launched September 2024 for large global enterprises). Segment margin runs ~36% and is expanding.
- **PEO Services (ADP TotalSource)** — ~33% of revenue (~$6.7B FY25). Co-employment for SMBs, 18,000+ clients, 750K+ worksite employees. Roughly 64% of PEO revenue is zero-margin benefits pass-throughs (healthcare, payroll taxes), so the economically meaningful PEO revenue base is closer to ~$2.4B.

The moat rests on four pillars: the industry's deepest HCM dataset (1.1M organizations, 42M workers), compliance and regulatory expertise across 140 countries that smaller competitors cannot replicate, retention (~13-year tenure, 92.1% revenue retention), and scale-driven distribution. Competitors include Paychex, Paycom, Paylocity, Workday, and Dayforce, plus in-house systems.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is among the most durable in any sector.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9-mo FY26 trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $16.5B | $18.0B | $19.2B | $20.6B (+7%) | +7% to $16.5B |
| Net income | $2.95B | $3.41B | $3.75B | $4.08B (+9%) | $3.43B (+8%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $9.10 | $9.98 (+10%) | $8.49 (+10%) |
| EBIT margin | — | — | 25.4% | 25.8% | Q3 hit 30.0% (+80 bps) |

Revenue growth of ~7% is a high-quality blend of new bookings, retention, pricing, and float interest. Margin expansion is the headline: adjusted EBIT margin reached 26.0% in FY25 and 30.2% in Q3 FY26, driven by client-fund interest, operating leverage, lower contract amortization, and AI-driven productivity. Cash conversion is excellent: FY25 operating cash flow $4.94B and FCF $4.77B on just ~$177M of capex (a 23% FCF margin, very low capital intensity). Nine-month FY26 OCF was $4.0B (+15%). ROE is ~66% and ROA ~7.6%.

Capital allocation is disciplined. FY25 returned $3.7B ($2.4B dividends + $1.3B buybacks); nine months of FY26 returned $3.4B. The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.70, and the Board authorized a new $6B buyback in January 2026, replacing the prior $5B program. ADP repurchased 5.7M shares at an average $257 in nine months of FY26 versus 3.4M at $283 the prior year, buying more aggressively as the price fell.

On the alarming-looking ratios: the Altman Z-Score of 0.7 and debt/equity of 7.6x are accounting artifacts, not distress signals. ADP consolidates $46.8B of client funds held against an equal $46.8B client-funds obligation (money held in trust to remit to employees and tax authorities). These inflate both sides of the balance sheet and crush equity-to-assets and working-capital ratios. The real leverage is just $4.0B of senior notes against ~$5B of OCF. ADP carries the highest possible short-term credit ratings (A-1+/P-1/F1+). Any quant screen flagging bankruptcy risk here is misreading the float.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is durable but has two qualifications that matter today. Management's FY26 framework of 6-7% revenue and 10-11% adjusted EPS growth is real and was just raised, and the beat track record is consistent: four straight beats, though by narrowing margins (1.7%, 0.8%, 1.1%, 0.4% over the last four quarters). The estimate revision picture is positive but modest: current-year EPS revised +1.0% over 90 days, next-year +1.9%. This is incremental, steady-compounder revision velocity.

The two qualifications:

1. **Float interest is doing meaningful work in the margin story.** Interest on client funds grew from $1.02B (FY24) to $1.19B (FY25) and ~$999M in nine months of FY26. This is near-100%-margin revenue. The laddered 5-10 year portfolio smooths rate moves, but a sustained Fed easing cycle directly compresses this line. The 10-11% EPS algorithm is partly rate-dependent, and the medium-term risk to ADP is the eventual cut cycle.

2. **ADP's own labor data is decelerating.** Pays-per-control grew only +1% in both FY25 and nine months of FY26, and PEO worksite-employee growth slowed to +2% from +3%. ADP's per-employee fee model means earnings track employment levels at existing clients. If the U.S. labor market weakens further, organic ES growth faces a headwind that compounds with any float compression.

The structural growth offsets are real: Lyric HCM moving ADP upmarket into large global enterprises, the WorkForce Software acquisition ($1.17B, October 2024) extending workforce-management capabilities, and AI products (ADP Assist agents launched January 2026) that are both a product expansion and an internal cost lever. The bet is that the proprietary dataset makes ADP an AI winner. That thesis is credible but unproven on a multi-year horizon.

## Valuation Assessment

ADP trades at ~21x forward EPS (consensus FY26 ~$11.07, FY27 ~$12.19) and a 2.9% dividend yield at ~$228. Against the industrials peer set, this is a clear discount:

ADP is classified in industrials but functions as a recurring-revenue software/data compounder, so the peer median overstates the gap somewhat. On its own history ADP routinely traded at 25-30x forward, and 21x is the low end of its multi-year range. Analyst targets span $190 low to $305 high, mean $246, median $240; the stock sits below mean and median, with the high target implying ~34% upside.

The case for a higher valuation score: a high-quality 92%-retention franchise at 21x with 10-11% EPS growth is not expensive on any growth-adjusted basis (PEG ~2.0, reasonable for this quality and durability), and the open-market director purchase plus upsized buyback at these prices signal the company views the shares as undervalued. The 30% drawdown from the high reflects multiple compression on stable-to-rising fundamentals, which mechanically improves risk/reward.

The case against pushing valuation higher: the discount is partly justified. The market is correctly pricing the risk that float income compresses and pays-per-control stalls, both of which would lower the EPS growth rate that justifies the multiple. At 21x on an algorithm that is partly rate- and cycle-dependent, the stock is reasonably priced rather than asymmetrically cheap. A 6.5 reflects a meaningful discount to quality without the specific, near-term mispricing catalyst that would warrant a 7+.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position remains strong but the trajectory has flattened, which is why this dimension has eased from 8.5 to 7.5 over recent reports. The 92% revenue retention and ~13-year client tenure confirm the moat is intact. Compliance complexity across 140 countries (Binding Corporate Rules for GDPR, ADP Trust Bank, Certified PEO status) is a durable barrier most competitors cannot match. The 1.1M-organization dataset is valuable as AI training fuel if the company executes.

The pressures are real and the market is weighing them. At the high end, Workday and Dayforce compete directly with Lyric. In the mid-market, Paycom and Paylocity grow faster off smaller bases. The central industry question, explicitly flagged in ADP's own 10-K, is whether AI compresses the value of ADP's service and expertise layer (deflationary) faster than it expands ADP's product capabilities (accretive). Across enterprise software, data-infrastructure layers tend to win while application/service layers face displacement risk. ADP sits closer to the data-infrastructure side given its proprietary dataset, which supports the 7.5, but the outcome is not yet settled.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Float rate sensitivity (most underappreciated).** Client-fund interest is near-pure margin. A 25 bps move is ~$20M pre-tax over 12 months; a sustained cut cycle would compress a line that has materially driven recent margin expansion. This is the single biggest cyclical threat to the earnings algorithm.

2. **Labor-market sensitivity.** A recession that turns pays-per-control and PEO worksite growth negative would directly pressure per-employee fee revenue.

3. **PEO margin compression.** PEO EBIT declined 2% in Q3 FY26, margin down 120 bps to 13.0%, driven by rising state unemployment insurance costs and a shrinking ADP Indemnity benefit (the captive workers'-comp insurer's pre-tax benefit fell to $4.4M in nine-month FY26 from $9.7M, reflecting less favorable actuarial loss development). An earnings-quality watch item.

4. **AI as disruption.** The 10-K notes AI could render existing technology obsolete or lower barriers for nimble competitors. The counter-thesis (data moat) is plausible but unproven.

5. **Cybersecurity/fiduciary risk.** Vast PII and $3.3T of annual fund movement make ADP a constant state-sponsored attack target; a breach or remittance failure would be materially damaging.

6. **Regulatory breadth.** GDPR, CPRA, EU AI Act, BSA/AML, co-employment liability, ERISA. An ERISA class action (TotalSource Retirement Savings Plan, filed May 2020) remains unresolved, deemed immaterial by management.

No material changes to risk factors were disclosed in the Q3 FY26 10-Q versus the FY25 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 44.4% versus 24.2% one-year historical volatility (a 20.2pp rich spread, 73rd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.4% near-term versus 29.2% at six months). Put/call skew is elevated at 13.6%, and ATM IV jumped 6.5pp intraday alongside a 1.4% price rise. This reflects hedging into a specific near-term window rather than a structural bearish view (the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.74). The signal reinforces the HOLD: the market expects a binary near-term catalyst. Front-end IV spikes of this type typically resolve without directional content, so this is a reason to wait rather than a reason to abandon the franchise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$280-305).** Float income proves more resilient than feared as the laddered portfolio smooths any rate cuts; pays-per-control stabilizes as labor data bottoms; Lyric and WorkForce Software accelerate upmarket bookings; AI products prove margin-accretive on both product and cost sides. EPS compounds at the high end of the 10-11% guide, and the multiple re-rates back toward its 25-28x history. At 25x FY27 EPS of ~$12.19, that is ~$305. This requires both macro variables to cooperate and AI to deliver as promised.

**Bear case (~$190-200, roughly -12% to -17%).** The Fed cuts into a softening labor market; float income compresses while pays-per-control turns negative; PEO margin erosion continues; AI commoditizes the service layer faster than ADP's products expand. EPS growth decelerates toward mid-single digits and the multiple stays at 18-20x. At 18x ~$11 EPS, that is ~$198, close to the director's May purchase level and the analyst low.

**Base case (~$245-250).** Steady ~7% revenue / ~10% EPS growth, modest float drag offset by buybacks and AI productivity, multiple holding near 21-22x. At 21x ~$11.50 blended forward EPS, ~$240-245.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FY26 Q4 / full-year report (late July/early August 2026), which will set FY27 guidance and update pays-per-control trends, alongside the eventual path of Fed policy bearing on float income.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pays-per-control growth re-accelerates above +2%, signaling labor stabilization and removing the organic-growth headwind.
- Rate-path clarity that limits float compression — either a steady Fed or evidence that the laddered portfolio yield holds despite cuts (next two quarterly client-fund interest disclosures flat-to-up).
- Stock pulls back toward the low-$200s / high-$190s (near the director purchase and analyst low) while estimates hold, which would push forward P/E below ~18x and create clearer asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Pays-per-control turns negative or PEO worksite growth goes flat-to-negative, confirming an employment contraction feeding directly into fee revenue.
- A guidance cut on the FY26 Q4 print, or adjusted EPS growth guided below high-single-digits for FY27.
- A material breach, remittance failure, or adverse ADP Indemnity reserve development that impairs earnings quality.

## Conclusion

ADP is a wide-moat compounder that has been de-rated to a reasonable valuation on stable-to-improving fundamentals, with a director buying in the open market at ~$206 and the company itself repurchasing more aggressively at lower prices. Those are constructive signals, and the drawdown has improved risk/reward versus three months ago.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY for a specific, falsifiable reason: a measurable share of ADP's recent margin and EPS expansion came from float interest income that compresses in a Fed easing cycle, and ADP's own proprietary labor data is decelerating into a possible H2 employment softening. The 10-11% adjusted EPS algorithm is therefore partly rate- and cycle-dependent. The options market is pricing a near-term binary event consistent with this concern. Until pays-per-control stabilizes and the rate path clarifies, both observable within one or two quarters, the stock is reasonably priced rather than asymmetrically cheap, and the honest call is to own the quality without adding conviction. The growth thesis is positive over 12 months; it lacks the resolved, near-term catalyst that would justify a high-conviction long today.
