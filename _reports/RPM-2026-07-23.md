**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RPM International closed FY2026 (fiscal year ends May 31) with record adjusted EBIT and near-record operating cash flow, but the headline financials require decoding. Reported net income fell to $661M from $689M, an outcome driven entirely by the effective tax rate normalizing from 12.9% to 23.9% (FY2025 carried a one-time $43.9M deferred-tax benefit). On a pre-tax and operating basis, the year was strong: consolidated EBIT rose to $935M from $865M, gross margin held at 41.4% despite inflation and tariff headwinds, and operating cash flow rose to $898.7M from $768M. The Q4 adjusted EPS of $1.89 beat the $1.84 consensus, and management flagged it as the 16th record adjusted-EBIT quarter of the last 18.

The complication is growth quality. FY2026 net sales of $7.86B grew 6.7%, but only 2.0% was organic; acquisitions contributed 3.6 points and FX 1.1. The Consumer segment, roughly a third of the company, shrank organically by 2.6% with volumes down about 4%, its second straight down year, tied to U.S. housing turnover at 30-to-40-year lows. Construction Products (CPG) and Performance Coatings (PCG) are the healthy engines, growing organically on data-center, energy, and infrastructure demand plus a differentiated "system selling" strategy. The bull case rests on Consumer bottoming and margin recovery as MAP 3.0 restructuring and $75M of FY27 SG&A savings land.

At $107.43, RPM trades at roughly 20.7x trailing earnings and ~18.4x forward FY27 consensus of $5.85. That is not demanding for a dividend aristocrat with 20% ROE and structurally higher cash flow, but it is not cheap for a business whose organic growth is low-single-digit and whose near-term estimate revisions are broadly negative (next-quarter consensus cut 8.4% over 30 days). Management guides to a first-half FY27 gross-margin dip from front-loaded raw-material inflation (+5-8% per quarter), tied to the Iran conflict and tariffs, before recovery in the back half. We rate RPM a HOLD, with the November 9 Investor Day (MAP 3.0 reveal) and evidence of Consumer stabilization as the specific catalysts that would move us to a BUY.

## Company Overview

RPM (Medina, Ohio; founded 1947) is a global specialty-chemicals manufacturer of coatings, sealants, adhesives, roofing systems, and building/infrastructure repair products. It runs a decentralized portfolio of well-known brands across ~120 manufacturing sites in 22+ countries, selling into ~167 countries, with about 30% of sales international. The company employs 17,546 people, only ~313 unionized.

Effective June 1, 2025, RPM realigned from four to three reportable segments:

- **Construction Products Group (CPG), ~39% of sales ($3.07B):** Roofing systems, sealants and air barriers, concrete admixtures and repair, waterproofing, building-envelope systems, restoration services. Brands include Tremco, Euclid, Dryvit, Nudura, Dri-Eaz. The strongest organic performer (+4.5%).
- **Performance Coatings Group (PCG), ~27% ($2.13B):** High-performance flooring (Stonhard, Flowcrete), corrosion-control and fireproofing coatings (Carboline), FRP structures (Fibergrate), food/pharma coatings. Serves industrial, infrastructure, and oil & gas markets. +4.1% organic.
- **Consumer, ~34% ($2.66B):** DIY and pro paints, coatings, and sealants: Rust-Oleum, DAP, Zinsser, Varathane, The Pink Stuff, Gator. Sold through home centers, mass merchants, and hardware stores. The weak spot: −2.6% organic, ~4% volume decline.

RPM does not hold large share of the total coatings market; it competes in fragmented niches where brand equity, technical service, and reputation matter. Its distinctive growth lever is "system selling": bundling walls, roofs, floors, and sealants into warranted, factory-engineered building systems that raise RPM content per project. This positions the company to grow ahead of its end markets in high-performance buildings like data centers and infrastructure. Named competitors include Sherwin-Williams, PPG, Akzo Nobel, Axalta, Sika, H.B. Fuller, Carlisle, and Masco, several materially larger.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (fiscal years ending May 31):** FY2023 $7.26B → FY2024 $7.34B → FY2025 $7.37B → FY2026 $7.86B. The multi-year picture shows a business that was essentially flat for two years before FY2026's step-up, and that step-up was primarily inorganic. Segment organic growth: CPG +4.5%, PCG +4.1%, Consumer −2.6%.

**Profitability:** Gross margin was flat at 41.4%, with pricing and MAP 2025 savings offsetting inflation and tariffs. Consolidated EBIT of $935M and IBT margin of 11.1% (up from 10.8%) both improved. The reported net-income decline is a tax artifact, not operating deterioration: the effective rate jumped 11 points on the loss of the prior-year deferred-tax benefit. GAAP Q4 EPS was $1.73.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** This is the clearest strength. Operating cash flow of $898.7M (second-highest in company history) against modest capex of ~$224M (2.8% of sales) produced roughly $675M of free cash flow, an FCF yield near 4.9% on the current market cap. Management credits MAP initiatives with lifting average annual OCF by ~90%. The company returned $349M to shareholders (up 7% YoY) and authorized a $700M buyback increase (~5% of market cap) on top of $115M remaining, with explicit intent to be opportunistic on weakness. RPM is a dividend aristocrat with 50+ years of increases. M&A spend fell to $202M from ~$595M, signaling a pause on the acquisition pace even as management flagged openness to larger deals.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt is ~$2.5B against equity of $3.3B; total-debt/equity is ~0.76, and debt declined YoY (a $197.8M revolver repayment). Liquidity is $1.09B, the current ratio 1.68, ROE ~20%, ROA ~7.9%. The Altman Z of 1.94 ("grey zone") is a broken input: its EBIT/assets term reads 0.001 when actual EBIT margin on assets is ~11%. RPM is investment-grade, de-leveraging, and cash-generative; the Z-score should be disregarded here. The genuine balance-sheet watch item is goodwill and intangibles of ~$2.5B (~30% of assets), with small FY25/FY26 impairments in the Consumer/Color Group signaling where the risk sits.

The financial-health picture supports a mid-to-high score: durable cash generation, appropriate leverage for a specialty-chemicals compounder, and working-capital discipline. The prior report's jump to 7.0 on this dimension looks slightly generous given the customer concentration and impairment exposure; we settle at 6.8.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is bifurcated. CPG and PCG (together ~66% of sales) are compounding organically in the mid-single digits on structural demand: data centers, energy, and infrastructure, plus system-selling content gains and share pickups in concrete admixtures (Euclid) where competitor consolidation and regulatory scrutiny have constrained rivals. Emerging markets (India, Middle East) are growing double-digits toward a $1B+ developing-world target. Re-roofing, which is more than 75% of U.S. roofing demand, gives CPG a repair/maintenance bias that is more resilient than new construction.

Consumer is the drag. Two consecutive years of flat-to-down DIY volumes, driven by housing turnover at multi-decade lows and elevated mortgage rates, have offset the healthier segments. FY2027 guidance assumes Consumer stabilizes on easier comps and a tentative DIY bottom, and calls for total sales +3-7% and adjusted EBITDA +5-10% (assuming stable raw costs). All three segments are expected to grow mid-single-digit.

Estimate revisions temper the optimism. Current-quarter consensus was cut 1.6% over 30 days, and next-quarter was cut 8.4% over 30 days (−7.9% over 90 days), reflecting negative near-term earnings momentum tied to the front-loaded H1 FY27 inflation guide. Current-year FY27 consensus of $5.85 slipped 1.0% over 30 days; only FY28 ($6.60) held roughly flat. This is the opposite of the accelerating positive-revision signal that would justify a growth premium.

Net assessment: growth is real in two-thirds of the portfolio but low-single-digit organic in aggregate, dependent on M&A and pricing for the headline, and facing near-term consensus cuts. A 5.3 reflects a directional positive on CPG/PCG durability offset by Consumer drag and falling estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $107.43, RPM trades at ~20.7x trailing EPS of $5.35 and ~18.4x forward FY27 consensus of $5.85, with a 2.1% dividend yield. Against the specialty-chemicals peer set the screen returns (SOLS 69x, ESI 70x, WLK negative, EMN 21x, DD 148x; peer median 69.9x), RPM looks cheap, but that peer median is distorted by earnings-trough and high-multiple names and is not a clean comparison. The more relevant benchmark is a low-growth, high-quality compounder: EMN at 21.2x with a 4.7% yield is the closest genuine comp, and RPM trades at a modest discount on P/E with a lower yield and better ROE.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the multiple here. RPM is not at a cyclical earnings trough with rapidly rising estimates: it delivered a record year and its near-term estimates are falling. The forward P/E of ~18x sits above the FY27 organic growth algorithm (mid-single-digit), implying a PEG well above 2 on organic economics. The bull case for the multiple rests on margin recovery (H2 FY27 price/cost turning positive), MAP 3.0 savings, and Consumer inflecting, none yet in the numbers.

We do not treat prior appreciation or the ~$107 price near the low end of its range as a valuation input in either direction. The relevant question is price versus forward fundamentals today: at ~18x forward on low-single-digit organic growth with negative near-term revisions, the multiple is fair to slightly full, not asymmetrically cheap. Analyst targets (low $115, mean $128.79, high $143) sit 7-33% above spot, but on a stock with falling estimates these targets reflect a margin-recovery and Consumer-stabilization scenario that is a hope, not yet a trend, rather than the "floor" they represent on a rising-estimate name. A cheap-looking screen multiple in Materials has historically been a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal; here the "discount" to the distorted peer median is not a reason to reward the name. Valuation score: 5.2.

## Competitive Landscape

RPM's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in its industrial segments and stable-to-weakening in Consumer. The moat is narrow: brand equity in fragmented niches, technical service, and increasingly the system-selling model that raises content per project and lets RPM outgrow its end markets in high-performance buildings. Concrete-admixture share gains (Euclid) as competitors face consolidation and regulatory constraints are a genuine, cited tailwind, and the re-roofing/maintenance bias adds resilience.

Against giants like Sherwin-Williams, PPG, and Sika, RPM does not compete head-to-head across the board; it wins where scale is not the deciding factor. In Consumer, however, the position is defensive: RPM is a share-holder in DIY brands facing a demand-driven volume decline, and its largest Consumer customer is ~20% of that segment's sales (down from 23%, likely a big-box retailer). That concentration is a structural exposure more than a share gain. The prior report's downgrade of this dimension from 6.5 to 5.8 reflected the growth-quality question; new FY26 data neither reverses nor deepens it materially. We hold at 6.0, weighting the industrial-segment trajectory positively against Consumer softness and the customer concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer/DIY exposure to housing.** A third of the business has been under volume pressure for two years. A prolonged higher-for-longer rate path keeps this segment depressed and makes the FY27 stabilization assumption the single largest swing factor in the guide.

**Front-loaded raw-material inflation and tariffs.** Management guides Q1 FY27 raw inflation of +5-6% and Q2 +6-8%, tied to the Iran/Middle East conflict and renewed tariff wars, with price/cost negative in H1 before neutralizing in H2. Specific tightness in propylene-oxide derivatives (a supplier plant fire) and MDI. Gross margin will dip near-term; the recovery depends on RPM retaining price increases (which it claims it does long-term, ex-silicone) and on raws moderating.

**Customer concentration in Consumer.** One customer accounts for ~20% of Consumer sales; a reduction in orders would materially hit the weakest segment.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** The ~$2.5B carrying value and small recent impairments in the Consumer/Color Group flag where a downturn could force larger write-downs.

**M&A dependence and integration.** 3.6 points of FY26's 6.7% growth was inorganic; management signaled openness to larger deals in FY27, raising integration and leverage risk.

**CEO succession.** The promotion of David Denstadt to President & COO, with Sullivan's "next couple of years" language, points to a telegraphed CEO handoff at a founder-family-led company. Orderly, but a structural change worth monitoring, and MD&A cited higher executive-departure costs in FY26.

Most of these are known and at least partially priced; the near-term inflation guide is the freshest negative and is already reflected in the estimate cuts. We assess risk factors at 5.6: not distress, but with a clustered set of specific downside vectors (Consumer/housing, H1 margin dip, concentration) that keep this below a clean score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$130-143):** Consumer volumes bottom and inflect positive on easier comps as the DIY cycle turns; H2 FY27 price/cost swings positive as pricing catches inflation; MAP 3.0 and $75M of SG&A savings drive margin expansion; CPG/PCG sustain mid-single-digit organic growth on data-center and infrastructure demand. The $700M buyback (~5% of cap) supports EPS. FY28 EPS reaches ~$6.60+, and the multiple holds at ~20x → $130-143. The November 9 Investor Day reframes a multi-year margin/cash-flow algorithm that re-rates the stock.

**Bear case (~$90-95, roughly −12% to −16%):** Housing stays frozen, Consumer volumes decline a third year, and the FY27 stabilization assumption fails; front-loaded inflation compresses margin more than pricing recovers; a goodwill impairment hits the Consumer/Color Group; M&A-driven growth masks weak organics and the market de-rates the multiple toward 16x on ~$5.60 EPS → low-$90s, near the 52-week low of $92.92. Estimate revisions continue their negative drift.

The balance of evidence sits between these outcomes. FY26 was genuinely strong on cash flow and adjusted EBIT, and the industrial segments are healthy. But the organic growth rate is low, near-term estimates are falling, and the two clearest positive catalysts (Consumer inflection, MAP 3.0) are prospective rather than proven. This is a growth-oriented HOLD: a plausible 12-month appreciation path gated by catalysts that have not yet materialized.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The November 9, 2026 Investor Day (MAP 3.0 reveal and multi-year targets) is the primary near-term catalyst, followed by the Q1 FY27 earnings report, which will show whether the front-loaded margin dip is tracking to guidance and whether Consumer volumes are stabilizing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. MAP 3.0 targets at the Investor Day materially raise the multi-year adjusted-EBITDA/margin algorithm and drive positive consensus revisions, or the stock pulls back toward the low-$90s while fundamentals hold, improving the risk/reward.
2. Consumer segment returns to positive organic volume growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the DIY bottom.
3. Next-quarter and current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive, reversing the current negative drift.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A third consecutive year of Consumer organic volume decline with no stabilization visible.
2. A material goodwill impairment in the Consumer/Color Group, or gross margin compressing more than 150bps in H1 FY27 with no H2 recovery path.
3. A leveraged, dilutive large acquisition that raises integration risk without a clear accretion path.

## Conclusion

RPM is a well-run, cash-generative specialty-chemicals compounder that delivered a record adjusted-EBIT year and near-record operating cash flow, backed by a de-leveraging balance sheet, a 50-year dividend-increase record, and a fresh $700M buyback authorization. The industrial segments (CPG, PCG) are compounding organically on durable data-center and infrastructure demand, and the system-selling strategy is a genuine differentiator.

Against that, the growth is heavily M&A- and pricing-dependent (only 2.0% organic in FY26), a third of the business has shrunk two years running on housing weakness, near-term estimates are falling, and the stock at ~18x forward on low-single-digit organic growth is fairly valued rather than cheap. The catalysts that would justify a higher rating (Consumer inflection and the MAP 3.0 margin algorithm) are prospective. We rate RPM a HOLD (growth_hold), with a specific path to a BUY at the November 9 Investor Day or on evidence that the DIY cycle has bottomed, and a downgrade path if Consumer weakness extends into a third year or margin compression runs ahead of guidance. This continues the four-report HOLD sequence; the bar for a further unchanged call rests on the two forthcoming catalysts, which will resolve the thesis within two quarters.
