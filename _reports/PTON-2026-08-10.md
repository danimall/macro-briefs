## Executive Summary

Peloton reported its first full year of GAAP operating and net income in FY2026 (fiscal year ended June 30, 2026): $160.7M of operating income and $63.2M of net income on $2,446.0M of revenue, with $468.2M of adjusted EBITDA and $377.6M of free cash flow. The mechanism was cost structure, not demand. Revenue fell 1.8%. Operating expenses fell $178.5M, subscription cost of revenue fell $37.3M, and the company exceeded its target of $100M-plus in run-rate savings. The 2025 Restructuring Plan is substantially implemented with no material further charges expected. The cost lever that produced the profitability improvement has largely been pulled.

The asset that generates the profit is shrinking. Ending paid Connected Fitness subscriptions fell 247,000, or 8.8%, to 2.553M. Paid app subscriptions fell 8.9%. Members fell from 6.0M to 5.5M. Average net monthly churn was 1.7% for the year, but 2.2% in Q4 FY2026 against 1.8% in Q4 FY2025 and 1.2% in Q3 FY2026. Ending subscriptions fell 109,000 sequentially in a single quarter, and Q1 FY2027 guidance of 2.455–2.475M embeds roughly 88,000 more losses. Because subscription gross profit was $1,196.3M of the company's $1,286.7M total, and 99% of Connected Fitness subscribers pay month-to-month with no contractual lock-in, the terminal value of this equity is a direct function of where that base settles. FY2027 revenue guidance of $2.30–2.40B (-3.9% at midpoint) against roughly $2.43B consensus is why the stock fell about 14% on August 6 and about 20% from August 5 through the following week.

At $5.45, enterprise value is approximately $2,509M against $468.2M of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA (5.4x) and $377.6M of free cash flow (6.7x). Those multiples look distressed-cheap. On per-share owner economics they do not: $198.6M of annual stock-based compensation equals 8.1% of revenue and 42.4% of adjusted EBITDA, taking the multiple to 14.0x EV/(FCF less SBC), and trailing GAAP EPS of $0.14 puts the stock at 39x. Management's own guidance concedes the transition: adjusted EBITDA guided up 6.8% at the midpoint while free cash flow is guided *down* to "at least $350M." We rate the shares HOLD. Existing owners are compensated by a genuinely repaired balance sheet ($1,206.6M of cash, $92.6M of net debt, no maturity before May 2029, and no active financial maintenance covenant) and by an option on churn stabilization. New capital has no margin of safety: our base case sits roughly 10% above spot against a bear case 28% below, with the decisive datapoint (Q1 FY2027 churn, early November) not yet observable.

## Company Overview

Peloton sells connected fitness hardware and monetizes the installed base through monthly subscriptions across the US, UK, Canada, Germany, Australia and Austria, plus a Commercial Business Unit selling Precor and Peloton for Business equipment into gyms, hospitality, multifamily and campus channels in more than 60 countries.

FY2026 revenue split: Subscription $1,675.6M (68.5% of revenue, 71.4% gross margin) and Connected Fitness Products $770.4M (31.5%, 11.7% gross margin). Subscription produced $1,196.3M of gross profit against $90.4M from hardware, 93% of consolidated gross profit. The hardware channel functions as a customer-acquisition funnel that roughly covers its own direct cost.

In October 2025 the company launched the Cross Training Series (Bike, Bike+, Tread, Tread+, Row+) and the Pro Series commercial line, discontinuing Original Series Tread, Tread+ and Row while continuing refurbished Original Series Bike and Bike+ sales. Software assets include Peloton IQ, Strength+ (iOS-only in the US, described in the 10-K as having a limited operating history and unproven scaling), Breathwrk, App+ and App One. Roughly 12% of Connected Fitness subscriptions cover households owning multiple products, which caps per-household monetization but raises switching cost for that cohort.

The $196.9M year-over-year improvement in operating income decomposes almost entirely into cost actions: G&A -$97.0M (including -$81.4M of personnel expense), S&M -$21.3M, subscription cost of revenue -$37.3M on lower music royalties and platform costs, impairment -$29.4M, restructuring -$16.0M, and supplier settlements -$23.5M. One comparability caveat: beginning Q1 FY2026 Peloton reallocated executive compensation and corporate overhead out of G&A into cost of revenue, S&M, G&A and R&D, so expense-caption comparisons are not clean year over year even though consolidated totals are unaffected.

**Margins.** Subscription gross margin expanded to 71.4% from 69.1%, reaching 73.6% in Q4, driven by lower music royalty costs and reserve releases, the December 2025 price increases, and lower depreciation. Hardware gross margin compressed to 11.7% from 13.6% on higher import tariffs, $13.5M of seat-post recall expense, heavier promotional discounting and mix. Q4 hardware margin was 13.4%, down from 17.3%. The hardware line is the fragile one: $90M of gross profit on $770M of revenue must absorb delivery, installation, warehousing and reverse logistics, so a tariff or promotional shock can drive it to negative contribution. New Section 301 tariffs of 10.0%–12.5% on goods from roughly 60 economies including Taiwan and China took effect July 24, 2026, and Peloton's contract manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan, China and Thailand.

**Cash flow quality.** Operating cash flow of $387.6M against $63.2M of net income is bridged by $359.8M of non-cash items, of which SBC is $198.6M (3.1x net income), plus $57.2M of D&A, $49.8M of non-cash lease expense and $34.6M of impairment. Working capital supplied an $81.3M release from inventory reduction and $34.2M from prepaids, against $71.1M of payables reduction and $77.3M of lease payments. Inventory has now funded roughly $381M of operating cash over three years and stands at $135.4M, roughly 73 days of hardware cost of revenue; this tailwind is close to exhausted, which is the most credible explanation for FY2027 free cash flow being guided below FY2026's despite higher EBITDA.

We show two cash figures because they answer different questions. FY2026 free cash flow of $377.6M is what the enterprise generated. Free cash flow less SBC of $179.0M approximates what accrued to existing shareholders after equity issued to employees. Per diluted share (436.2M FY2026 weighted average), those are $0.87 and $0.41 respectively, a 15.9% and 7.5% yield at $5.45.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $1,206.6M plus $40.9M restricted, against $980.0M of term loan (May 30, 2029 maturity, 0.25% quarterly amortization, SOFR-based, secured by substantially all assets) and $350.0M of 5.50% convertible notes due December 1, 2029. The $100M revolver is undrawn. The last near-term wall, $199.0M of remaining 2026 notes, was repaid in cash in February 2026. Company-reported net debt is $92.6M (down 80%), net leverage 0.3x, gross leverage 2.8x. The minimum-liquidity ($250M weekly) and minimum trailing-four-quarter subscription revenue ($1.2B, versus $1,675.6M actual) covenants apply only when revolving loans are outstanding, so there is currently no financial maintenance covenant.

Total stockholders' deficit is $139.7M with a $5,539.4M accumulated deficit. Any book-equity-anchored distress screen will misclassify this company; on the evidence, near-term solvency risk is low. Two qualifications matter. First, roughly three-quarters of the $274.1M improvement in the equity deficit came from a $200.9M rise in additional paid-in capital driven by stock compensation rather than earnings. Second, cash interest paid of $122.2M against average debt near $1.4B implies a blended cost around 8.7%, meaning the floating term loan carries a materially higher rate than the 5.50% converts, and the 2029 refinancing will be priced off subscription EBITDA as it stands then.

**Earnings track record and revisions.** Four-quarter record: one beat (Q1 FY2026, $0.03 vs $0.00), one in-line (Q4, $0.13 vs $0.13), two misses (Q2 -46.3%, Q3 -21.3%). Consensus for the current year has risen 17.4% over 90 days to $0.31, but next-year consensus has fallen 4.1% to $0.35. The +30%-plus revision pattern that would signal a genuine improvement in demand is absent at the out-year, and the current-year rise reflects cost savings already banked rather than accelerating demand.

## Growth Analysis

Peloton has no revenue growth and is not guided to have any. Revenue: -7.8% (FY2025), -1.8% (FY2026), -3.9% guided (FY2027). Hardware revenue has fallen 22% cumulatively over two years, with Q4 down 14% year over year and 16% sequentially. Every incremental subscriber now originates from a hardware sale, a rental, a secondary-market activation fee, or an app signup, and hardware unit volume is the binding constraint. The 10-K attributes weakness to reduced consumer demand for discretionary big-ticket items and constrained consumer financing.

The one arithmetic bright spot is pricing. Q4 subscription revenue of $436.6M grew 7% year over year while subscriptions fell 8.8%, implying roughly a 17% increase in revenue per subscription. Annualized, the Q4 subscription run rate of $1,746M sits above the full-year $1,675.6M, which supports the modest FY2027 total revenue decline. But the December 2025 price increase anniversaries in FY2027, so the offset fades precisely as the volume decline compounds, and it is difficult to separate the price increase from the churn acceleration that followed two quarters later.

Disclosed growth vectors: Commercial Business Unit revenue grew double digits in FY2026 (dollar contribution undisclosed); microstores "outperformed internal sales goals" with the fleet to double in FY2027 (unit count undisclosed); a Spotify "strategic partnership" whose economics, terms and duration are nowhere quantified; and the Skōp connected-Pilates acquisition, bounded as small by FY2026 total cash for business combinations of $11.4M, a $2.8M goodwill increase and a $6.1M intangibles increase. None of these is scaled to offset a 9% annual decline in a 2.5M-subscriber base. The mix shift toward commercial hardware is not diversification that reduces risk: the consumer subscription cohort that produces 93% of gross profit is deteriorating on both the year-over-year and sequential churn comparisons.

## Valuation Assessment

Using 438,639,374 shares outstanding at June 30, 2026 (the profile's 417.2M count appears stale; we use the filing figure and note the discrepancy):

- Market capitalization: $2,391M
- Enterprise value: 2,391 + 1,330.0 gross principal debt − 1,206.6 cash = **$2,514M**
- **EV / FY2026 adjusted EBITDA $468.2M = 5.4x**
- **EV / FY2027E adjusted EBITDA (midpoint $500M) = 5.0x**
- **EV / FY2026 FCF $377.6M = 6.7x**
- **EV / (FCF − SBC) $179.0M = 14.0x**
- **Trailing GAAP P/E on $0.14 diluted = 38.9x**
- Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus $0.31 = 17.6x; on FY2028 $0.35 = 15.6x

Peer medians in Leisure Products sit at 26.2x P/E (CALY 40.3x, YETI 26.2x, THO 16.1x, MAT 10.6x, PII negative). Peloton at 17.6x forward looks like a discount, but the comparison is not informative: none of these peers is shrinking its recurring-revenue base 9% annually with an accelerating exit rate. The relevant comparison is against a declining-annuity framework.

Our owner-earnings view: FY2027 free cash flow of ~$350M against roughly 455M shares (3-4% annual share creep from SBC) is $0.77 per share, or $0.35 per share after deducting SBC at a normalizing $160–190M. Capitalizing $0.35 of owner earnings on a base declining 3–5% annually at an 11–12% required return produces $2.20–$2.50. Capitalizing the unadjusted $0.77 at the same discount rates produces $4.50–$5.50. The truth sits between: SBC is a real transfer of value to employees, but it is declining (-36% from FY2024), and cash builds on the balance sheet even as the annuity shrinks, which is what supports the current price.

Two off-model items belong in the valuation. First, the IEEPA tariff refunds: the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026, Peloton filed CAPE refund claims in June, and the 10-K confirms refund payments began arriving after June 30, 2026, unrecognized in FY2026 per gain-contingency accounting and excluded from FY2027 guidance. The amount is nowhere disclosed and should not be modeled, but it partially explains why the FCF guide is a floor. Second, $3,497.9M of federal NOLs, mostly indefinite-lived, though already partly Section 382-limited and capped at 80% of taxable income for post-2017 losses. Cash taxes of $5.5M in FY2026 show the shield is already largely operative at current pre-tax income; it should not be valued as a clean $3.5B asset.

Cheapness on EV/FCF is not a catalyst. The specific reason the multiple is low is that the market has correctly identified subscriber attrition as the terminal-value driver and has no evidence it is stabilizing. We score valuation 5.0: reasonable, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own language is the most useful competitive assessment available: Connected Fitness Products "are sold in highly competitive markets with limited barriers to entry," and competitors include "much larger companies with greater resources." Peloton competes simultaneously against at-home equipment and content, fitness clubs and studios, wellness apps, and, named explicitly, GLP-1s and other weight-management treatments. In the commercial channel it competes for gym, hotel and multifamily placements with Precor's incumbency as its principal asset.

The durable advantage is a switching-cost moat on the existing installed base: hardware already in the home plus a content and instructor library producing 74.8% subscription contribution margin. That is a real asset. It is also a depreciating one, because the base is contracting 8.8% annually and content production is concentrated in two studios (New York and London) with instructors on fixed-term contracts the 10-K acknowledges "may leave Peloton prior to the end of their contracts."

On the trajectory test that matters more than the level: subscriptions down 8.8%, app subscriptions down 8.9%, members down 8%, hardware revenue down 22% over two years, churn worse both year over year and sequentially. Commercial growth off a smaller base is a genuine offset but does not reverse the direction. We score competitive position 3.5.

On GLP-1s, the mechanism could run either way: substitution away from exercise, or complementarity as weight loss increases exercise capacity. Peloton lists it as a threat; we found no evidence quantifying the effect on churn and treat any directional claim as speculation.

## Risk Assessment

**Subscriber attrition outrunning cost savings** is the dominant risk and the one that determines terminal value. Q4 churn of 2.2% is not visible in the 10-K's annual table (1.7%); an investor reading only the annual metric would materially understate recent deterioration. Some of the sequential spike from 1.2% is mechanical (Q4 is seasonally weakest, Peloton counts pauses as churn, and Q4 FY2025 was also elevated at 1.8%), but the year-over-year comparison strips most of that and still shows 40bp of deterioration. Cost savings are largely banked; the restructuring is substantially complete.

**Tariffs are two-sided and unquantified.** Favorable: IEEPA invalidation and refunds now being received; removal of fitness equipment from Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs effective April 6, 2026; expiry of Section 122 tariffs on July 23, 2026. Unfavorable: new Section 301 tariffs of 10.0–12.5% effective July 24, 2026 covering Taiwan and China, with additional USTR investigations open. The guided ~54.0% FY2027 gross margin presumably embeds an assumption we cannot isolate.

**Recall exposure.** The November 6, 2025 Original Series Bike+ seat-post recall covers ~833,000 US and 44,800 Canadian units. The accrual declined from $16.5M to $7.7M with no incremental accruals since September 2025, but the 10-K warns additional costs "may be material" if reported incidents increase. This is Peloton's third recall.

**Music licensing.** Minimum royalty commitments of $105.2M ($48.0M within a year), with disclosed most-favored-nations provisions that "could cause our payment or other obligations to escalate substantially" and DOJ consent-decree exposure. Lower music royalties and reserve releases were a named driver of the FY2026 subscription margin gain, so part of the margin expansion sits on a line with disclosed escalation risk.

**Supply concentration.** Sole reliance on contract manufacturers, single-source for most products and some critical components, with primary facilities in Taiwan, China and Thailand. The 10-K names China-Taiwan tensions explicitly. Non-cancellable manufacturer commitments of $77.2M due within a year.

**Governance and controls.** Three people occupied the CFO seat within roughly four months of 2026 (Coddington departing March, Baig interim April–June, Thacker effective June 22), and FY2027 guidance was issued by a CFO six weeks into the role at a company with material weaknesses identified in FY2021 through FY2024. The FY2026 filing represents these as remediated. A new standalone stockholder-activism risk factor appears in the past tense; we could not identify the investor or campaign. Dual-class structure gives 15.8M Class B shares 316.7M votes, roughly 42.8% of voting power in 3.6% of shares, sunsetting no earlier than approximately September 2029.

**Contingent dilution.** 36.7M unvested RSUs, 5.8M unvested PSUs and 18.7M option shares total ~61.25M, equal to 14.0% of shares outstanding, or 12.3% of proportionate ownership if all were issued. This is maximum contingent dilution, not current fully diluted capitalization: vesting and performance conditions are unverified and a legacy option tranche carries a $38.77 strike against $5.45, so a portion is economically worthless.

**2029 refinancing.** Not a FY2027 problem. The question is whether subscription EBITDA in 2029 supports refinancing $1.33B at a blended cost that is already near 8.7%. Three years of $300–350M free cash flow would substantially pre-fund it if the cash is not consumed by M&A.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 63.3% against 64.7% one-year realized is fairly priced (spread -1.3%), with balanced put/call skew (0.0%) and a put/call volume ratio of 0.41 that is call-tilted rather than defensive. The term structure is in backwardation (63.3% near versus 58.3% at six months), consistent with residual post-earnings uncertainty rather than a priced directional break; the nearest expiry implies only a ±5.4% move. The one notable feature is the six-month tenor's -5.5% put/call skew alongside 31.6% implied move: longer-dated positioning carries more downside pricing than the front end. Short interest of 15.0% of float with 6.8 days to cover is meaningful but not crowded enough to create acute squeeze mechanics; it does mean any churn improvement in the November print would be amplified upward. The options market is not corroborating either an imminent collapse or a re-rating, which is consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($8.60, +58%).** Churn reverts toward 1.5% as the December 2025 price-increase cohort washes through and the Cross Training Series drives replacement demand; subscriptions stabilize near 2.35M; IEEPA refunds prove material and land in FY2027 cash; commercial and microstore expansion adds a genuine growth line; adjusted EBITDA reaches the $525M top of guidance; the multiple re-rates to 7x EV/EBITDA as the market accepts the subscription annuity as durable. Equity ≈ $3.95B on ~460M shares. Supporting evidence: FY2026 revenue came in $6M above the guidance top; subscription revenue grew 7% in Q4 despite a 9% subscriber decline; subscription gross margin reached 73.6%; the balance sheet has no maturity for three years; and current-year consensus has risen 17.4% in 90 days.

**Base case ($6.00, +10%).** FY2027 delivers roughly as guided (revenue $2.35B, adjusted EBITDA $500M, free cash flow $350M) while subscriptions end the year near 2.30M (-10%). Cash builds roughly $250M net of amortization. The multiple compresses modestly to 5.0x EV/EBITDA as the market prices a 10% attrition rate, offset by the cash build. Equity ≈ $2.75B on ~460M shares.

**Bear case ($3.90, -28%).** Churn holds above 2%, the price-increase offset exhausts as the December anniversary passes, and subscription revenue turns negative in FY2028. Section 301 tariffs plus promotional intensity push hardware toward zero or negative contribution; music royalty reserve releases do not repeat; the patent litigation that drove the $23.8M Q4 accrual expands. Adjusted EBITDA falls to $430M, the multiple compresses to 4x, and the 2029 refinancing becomes a live question. Equity ≈ $1.9B.

Probability-weighted (20% bull / 45% base / 35% bear): expected return approximately +6%. That is positive but below a fair required return for an equity with this dispersion, which is why we neither buy nor actively reduce.

**The single most important disconfirming fact for the bull case** is that management's compensation plan, per the DEF 14A filed October 2025, uses free cash flow as its sole disclosed performance measure. There is no disclosed subscriber-growth or revenue-growth metric. An incentive structure keyed to free cash flow will rationally produce price increases and cost discipline in preference to volume reinvestment, which is exactly what FY2026 delivered. If the thesis requires subscriber re-acceleration, the pay plan is not paying for it. A board could reasonably have chosen this as the correct single objective for a balance-sheet repair phase and may re-weight later; the proxy we read covers FY2025 and would not reflect a redesign. But an investor should expect more harvesting until evidence of a change appears.

Insider activity is unhelpful in both directions. Across Form 4 filings from March to July 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director at $5–6, in the run-up to a quarter that beat on revenue and EPS. That is an absence of a positive signal rather than a bearish one: executives were likely in blackout windows for much of the period, the interim CFO's sales were small and regular, and the CCO's sale to zero directly held shares coincided with a 916,231-share RSU grant that likely increased his aggregate exposure. The April 2026 grants of 916,231 shares each to two officers at roughly $5.15 carry a distinct implication: retention grants at a depressed price require many more shares per dollar of value, which is how SBC stays at 8% of revenue while headcount shrinks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The overall score of 4.2 sits at the low end of the HOLD band. We are holding on balance-sheet strength and cash-flow multiple support, not on a positive growth thesis.

- **Base case target: $6.00** (5.0x EV/FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of $500M = $2.50B EV, plus ~$250M of net cash accumulation, on ~460M shares). +10% from $5.45.
- **Bull case target: $8.60** (7.0x EV/$525M EBITDA plus net cash). +58%.
- **Bear case target: $3.90** (4.0x EV/$430M EBITDA plus net cash). -28%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q1 FY2027 report in early November 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q1 FY2027 average net monthly churn at or below 1.6% with ending subscriptions at or above the 2.475M guidance high, the first evidence that the exit rate is not the trend.
2. Disclosure of the IEEPA tariff refund amount at a magnitude above $50M, or FY2027 free cash flow guidance revised upward from the "at least $350M" floor.
3. Initiation of a share repurchase at or below 7x EV/FCF, which would signal capital allocation shifting from employee equity issuance toward per-share value; or a price below $4.25, where the bear case is largely discounted and the bull skew becomes compelling.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q1 FY2027 churn at or above 2.2%, or ending subscriptions below 2.455M, confirmation that the price-increase strategy is accelerating base erosion.
2. FY2027 revenue or adjusted EBITDA guidance cut, or hardware gross margin falling below 10% on tariff pass-through, which would put hardware near zero contribution.
3. Any of: a new ICFR material weakness in the FY2027 Item 9A, a material incremental recall accrual, or debt-funded M&A that consumes the cash cushion that currently underpins the floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** early November 2026 (Q1 FY2027 results and churn), with an interim check on any 8-K quantifying tariff refunds or disclosing an activist position.

## Conclusion

Peloton has done the hard, unglamorous work: retired its last near-term maturity in cash, cut net debt 80% to $92.6M, exceeded a $100M savings target, expanded total gross margin 170bp, and printed the first GAAP profit in its history. On enterprise cash-flow multiples of 5.4x EBITDA and 6.7x free cash flow, with $1.21B of cash and no maturity until May 2029, the equity is not obviously mispriced to the downside, and selling after a drawdown of roughly 41% from the 52-week high with the balance sheet in this condition is unattractive.

What has not happened is stabilization of the asset that generates 93% of gross profit. Subscriptions fell 8.8% in FY2026 and are guided down 9.8% year over year in Q1 FY2027. Q4 churn of 2.2% is worse than a year ago on the seasonally adjusted comparison, and the pricing that held subscription revenue flat is the most plausible partial cause. The cost lever is largely pulled, the working-capital tailwind is exhausted, free cash flow is guided down, and the compensation plan pays for cash generation rather than volume recovery. Against $198.6M of annual stock compensation and 3–4% share creep, per-share owner earnings on a shrinking base support something closer to today's price than to a re-rating.

This is a levered option on churn stabilization at roughly fair value, with the option's strike resolving in the November print. HOLD. Existing owners should maintain exposure and monitor the churn line; prospective investors should wait for either evidence of subscriber stabilization or a price near $4.25.
