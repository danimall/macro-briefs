**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bio-Techne changed character on June 25, 2026, when Merck KGaA (the German life-science company operating as MilliporeSigma, distinct from the US-listed Merck & Co.) signed a definitive, board-approved, all-cash agreement to acquire the company for $73.00 per share, an enterprise value of roughly $11.3 billion. The stock gapped from $58.88 to $70.70 on the announcement and has since drifted to $71.60, leaving a gross spread of about 1.9% to the deal price. This is a merger-arbitrage position now, and every dimension of the analysis flows from that fact. The prior three-year debate over Bio-Techne's growth recovery, margin trajectory, and takeover multiple is largely moot except as the downside floor should the deal break.

A 1.9% gross spread realized over a 6-9 month close translates to an annualized return in the low-single digits, roughly 2.5-3.8% depending on timing. That is a spread appropriate for a low-risk, strategically-clean, investment-grade-buyer deal, which this is. The transaction pairs two life-science-tools players (US target, EU acquirer) and will require HSR, EU, and likely other antitrust clearances plus a Bio-Techne shareholder vote, but Merck KGaA has a strong track record integrating US targets (Millipore, Sigma-Aldrich, Versum, SpringWorks) and product overlap is more complementary than horizontal. The residual risk in the spread reflects the antitrust review window and vote, not a serious break probability.

The residual equity question is whether the ~1.9% arb return is worth the capital and timeline, and whether there is a credible path above $73. Leerink's Puneet Souda publicly argued the 26x FY27 EV/EBITDA price undervalues an 80%-consumables, 70%-gross-margin franchise that historically traded richer, which keeps a low-probability competing-bid scenario alive but is speculative. We rate the stock HOLD. There is no compelling reason to add here for the modest arb, and no reason to sell into it if already held, given the deal floor.

## Company Overview

Bio-Techne is a Minneapolis-based supplier of life-science reagents, instruments, and diagnostics, founded in 1976 and marking its 50th anniversary in 2026. It operates two segments:

- **Protein Sciences (~71% of revenue, ~$870M FY25, ~39-41% segment operating margin):** recombinant proteins (cytokines, growth factors), antibodies, immunoassays, ProteinSimple protein-analysis instruments (Simple Western, Ella), and GMP reagents for cell and gene therapy. This is the high-margin core, roughly 80%+ consumables, anchored by the entrenched R&D Systems brand.
- **Diagnostics & Spatial Biology (~29%, ~$346M FY25, historically ~10% margins):** diagnostic quality controls and calibrators, the Lunaphore spatial-biology platform, the ACD/RNAscope in-situ hybridization franchise, and molecular diagnostics. The former Exosome Diagnostics (ExoDx prostate) business was moved to held-for-sale and divested in August 2025 for roughly $15M.

Approximately 80% of revenue is recurring consumables at 70%+ adjusted gross margins, and roughly 44% of revenue is international. Merck KGaA's stated rationale is vertical integration across the discovery-to-manufacturing value chain, adding Bio-Techne's proteins, RNAscope, and ProteinSimple franchises to MilliporeSigma's bioprocessing scale. The acquirer specifically cited these three franchises as strategic prizes.

## Financial Analysis

Standalone fundamentals were stabilizing before the deal, and the headline GAAP deterioration in FY25 was almost entirely non-operational.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,136.7M | $1,159.1M | $1,219.6M (+5%) |
| GAAP operating income | $298.9M | $206.7M | $102.3M |
| GAAP net income | $285.3M | $168.1M | $73.4M |
| Adjusted EPS | $1.99 | $1.77 | $1.92 |
| Operating cash flow | $254M | $299M | $288M |

The FY25 GAAP net income collapse from $285M to $73M was driven by three non-recurring items lodged in SG&A: an $80.5M+ impairment of the Exosome Diagnostics business, a $41.8M arbitration award to former CEO Chuck Kummeth over disputed stock-option expiration dates (roughly $37.2M cash plus interest and fees for 779,084 options), and approximately $28M of restructuring across two programs. Stripping these out, adjusted EPS grew 8% to $1.92, and adjusted operating margin was expanding, not compressing. Q2 FY26 (quarter ended December 31, 2025) delivered adjusted operating margin of 31.1%, up roughly 100 bps year-over-year, with adjusted EPS of $0.46 against revenue flat at $295.9M.

The balance sheet is clean. Cash stood at $173M in Q2 FY26 with debt reduced to $260M (from $346M at FY25 close) on a $1B revolver, equity of roughly $2.0B, and leverage near 1x EBITDA. Free cash flow was roughly $256M in FY25. The company repurchased $275.7M of stock in FY25 (about 4.55M shares at ~$60.60) and authorized a fresh $500M buyback in April 2025; the dividend runs about $0.32/year (~$50M). Financial health poses no obstacle to the acquirer's financing, which Merck KGaA plans to fund with cash and new debt while preserving its investment-grade rating.

The one standalone liability worth flagging is the Wilson Wolf forward-purchase obligation: Bio-Techne owns 19.9% and is contractually required to buy the remaining 80.1% by roughly end-CY2027 for approximately $1 billion (a ~4.4x revenue multiple), triggerable earlier on a revenue/EBITDA milestone not yet met as of June 30, 2025. Against $173M of cash this would require major new financing for a standalone Bio-Techne. That obligation now transfers to Merck KGaA, whose scale and investment-grade balance sheet are far better suited to absorb it, and it was explicitly acknowledged in the deal announcement. It was plausibly a factor in the board's willingness to sell.

## Growth Analysis

For the purpose of this position, forward growth is secondary because the equity's terminal value is fixed at $73 if the deal closes. The standalone trajectory matters as the downside anchor.

The underlying business had been recovering from a 2023-2025 sector downturn (post-COVID biopharma destocking, weak China, the biotech funding drought, and NIH funding uncertainty). Management pointed to four consecutive quarters of double-digit large-pharma growth, improving biotech demand, stabilizing US academic spending, and three straight quarters of China/APAC growth into Q2 FY26. Organic revenue was flat in the most recent quarter, however, and estimate revisions have drifted modestly lower: current-year consensus EPS was cut 2.9% over 90 days to $1.92, next-year down 5.5% to $2.05, and the current-quarter estimate down 8.3% to $0.52. These declines are shallow and reflect a low-single-digit growth environment rather than deterioration, but they underscore that the standalone recovery was gradual and not a re-rating catalyst on its own. The compression of Bio-Techne's historical mid-teens growth to low single digits is what made it a takeover target at a below-historical multiple.

There is no estimate-revision signal here, and the growth thesis is now subsumed by the deal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.60 against a $73.00 all-cash offer, valuation is a spread calculation, not a fundamental exercise. The gross spread annualizes to roughly 2.5-3.8% over a 6-9 month expected close. That return is thin, appropriate for a low-break-risk deal, and roughly in line with short-term rates, which removes most of the arbitrage appeal for a general investor.

On a fundamental basis, the deal price implies about 26x FY27 EV/EBITDA and represents a 24% premium to the pre-announcement close. Leerink argued this undervalues the franchise relative to its historical trading multiples, which is the source of the residual competing-bid speculation. Against peers, Bio-Techne's franchise quality (consumables mix, gross margin, RNAscope leadership) supports a premium multiple, but the peer set is heterogeneous and several comps carry negative trailing P/Es (CRL -50x, AVTR -11.9x, AZTA -9.3x) reflecting the sector's earnings trough; the meaningful comps (MEDP 29.9x, BIO 46.9x) do not cleanly bracket the deal. The 103x trailing P/E on the headline data is meaningless here given the non-recurring FY25 charges and the fixed deal terminus.

The analyst price-target set is instructive: mean $70.00, median $73.00, high $73.00, low $50.00. The clustering of the median and high at exactly the deal price confirms that the sell-side now models this as a deal-priced stock. The $50 low is essentially a break-price estimate. We do not treat these targets as floors that will be revised upward, because this is an acquired asset trading just below its acquisition price. A valuation score of 5.0 reflects a symmetric, low-magnitude outcome: capped near $73 on the upside, floored somewhere in the low-to-mid $50s on a break.

## Competitive Landscape

Bio-Techne holds a differentiated position in life-science tools, with the entrenched R&D Systems brand in recombinant proteins, RNAscope leadership in spatial biology, the ScaleReady/Wilson Wolf position in cell and gene therapy manufacturing, and an 80%-consumables revenue base at 70%+ adjusted gross margins. It competes against Danaher, Thermo Fisher, Revvity, Agilent, Bruker, and MilliporeSigma itself. Merck KGaA is paying up for the proteins/RNAscope/ProteinSimple portfolios, validating the franchise value. Leerink flagged the deal as a positive read-through for peer Revvity as a potential re-rating or next-target candidate, reinforcing the view that these assets carry strategic scarcity value.

The competitive trajectory as a standalone entity was mixed, strong franchises but compressed growth in a cyclical downturn, which is why the score sits at 6.0 rather than higher. Competitive position now matters only as the qualitative backing for a possible topping bid.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile bifurcates into deal risks (primary) and standalone risks (relevant only if the deal breaks).

**Deal-completion risk** is the dominant variable and is modest. The agreement is signed and board-approved, the buyer is an investment-grade strategic with strong US-integration experience, and the transaction still requires antitrust clearance (US HSR, EU, likely others) plus a Bio-Techne shareholder vote, with an expected close in late 2026/early 2027. Product overlap is complementary rather than heavily horizontal, which lowers antitrust hurdle risk. The 1.9% spread is the market's aggregate pricing of that remaining risk and timeline.

**Standalone/underlying risks** (if the deal fails): the ~$1B Wilson Wolf forced-purchase obligation against cash on hand; US academic/NIH funding cuts hitting the Protein Sciences customer base; US-China tariff exposure (roughly $100M China revenue plus imported inputs); EU IVDR and US LDT regulatory burden on diagnostics; FX exposure (~44% international); and further goodwill/intangible impairment risk (goodwill plus intangibles are ~52% of assets). In a break scenario the stock would likely revert toward the low-to-mid $50s, consistent with the $50 analyst low and roughly where it traded before the bid.

**Governance** is a genuine soft spot. The $41.8M former-CEO arbitration award was a value-destructive legacy issue, executive and director turnover has been frequent (numerous Item 5.02 filings across 2024-2026, including one alongside the merger for change-of-control provisions), and news reporting states the sale "follows investor pressure," implying activist involvement pushed the board toward a sale from a position of relative weakness after years of underperformance. The below-historical takeout multiple is consistent with that read. Insider activity has been routine comp-driven only, with no open-market purchases.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing confirms the deal-priced, low-volatility character of the stock. Near-term ATM implied volatility is just 3.3% against a one-year historical volatility of 48.7%, an unusually wide cheap spread (-45.4%), with IV at the 3rd percentile of the current chain and an implied move of only ±1.9% into the nearest expiry. Skew is call-heavy (-7.4%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are near zero (0.04 and 0.01). This is the classic signature of a stock pinned to a cash deal price: volatility has collapsed because the payoff is binary and near-certain, and the small residual optionality skews to the upside (a topping bid). The term structure is in contango (near-term 3.3% versus 6-month 7.4%), reflecting that most of the residual event risk sits later, at the antitrust/close window. Options positioning is fully consistent with the merger-arb thesis. Short interest is 8.9% of float with 1.5 days to cover, unremarkable and not a squeeze factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($73, +2% from current, plus low-probability upside above $73).** The deal closes at $73 on schedule, delivering the gross spread. The tail-upside scenario is a competing bid: if a strategic rival or the sell-side valuation argument prompts a topping offer, the stock could move above $73. This is speculative and low-probability, but not zero given the below-historical takeout multiple and the strategic scarcity of the RNAscope/proteins assets.

**Bear Case (~$50-55, roughly -23% to -30% from current).** The deal breaks, most plausibly on an antitrust obstacle or a failed shareholder vote (the latter unlikely given the premium). The stock reverts toward its pre-announcement trading range in the low-to-mid $50s, where the standalone story reasserts itself: low-single-digit growth, the looming Wilson Wolf obligation, and governance friction. The $50 analyst low anchors this scenario.

The probability-weighted expected value strongly favors the close scenario given a signed definitive agreement, an investment-grade strategic buyer, complementary product overlap, and Merck KGaA's integration track record. But the reward for that near-certainty is only ~1.9% gross, which is why this is a hold rather than a fresh buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The natural checkpoints are the HSR clearance/expiration window, EU antitrust review milestones, the Bio-Techne shareholder vote, and the expected close in late 2026/early 2027 (a 6-9 month horizon).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a credible competing bid or public indication of interest from a rival strategic that pushes the implied value materially above $73; (2) the gross spread widening to a level that offers an attractive annualized return without a corresponding increase in break risk (e.g., a temporary price dislocation to the mid-$60s on clean, clearing antitrust news).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID/sell):** (1) a substantive antitrust second request or EU Phase II opening that materially lowers close probability; (2) any signal of a shareholder-vote failure or a financing wobble at Merck KGaA (unlikely given cash-plus-debt funding and investment-grade rating); (3) explicit deal-termination news, which would reset the analysis to the standalone framework and a low-to-mid $50s fair value.

For an existing holder, the position is worth carrying to close for the modest arb and the deal floor. For a new buyer, the ~1.9% gross spread does not clear the bar against short-term risk-free alternatives, and there is no directional equity thesis remaining.

## Conclusion

Bio-Techne is a merger-arbitrage situation, and the score trajectory (HOLD across five prior reports, with valuation and risk scores rising as the deal crystallized while growth prospects softened) reflects the transition from an uncertain standalone-recovery story to a deal-priced stock. The new data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: the offer removes fundamental upside, caps downside near the deal floor, and collapses implied volatility. The underlying franchise is high-quality, the FY25 GAAP collapse was a non-recurring artifact rather than operational decay, and the Wilson Wolf overhang has effectively been transferred to a better-capitalized owner. None of that matters much for the equity decision now.

The recommendation is **HOLD**. The ~1.9% gross spread over a 6-9 month close is unattractive for new capital, the deal is highly likely to close, and the only path to meaningful upside is a speculative competing bid. Hold to catalyst if already positioned; do not initiate for the arb alone. Overall score: 5.6.
