**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NorthWestern Energy has ceased to be a standalone utility investment and is now an event-driven merger situation. On August 18, 2025, NWE agreed to an all-stock combination with Black Hills Corp. (NYSE: BKH), under which each NWE share converts into a fixed 0.98 shares of BKH. The combined entity, to be renamed Bright Horizon Energy, would serve roughly 2.1 million customers across eight states, headquartered in Rapid City, South Dakota. Black Hills is the accounting acquirer and would hold majority board control; NWE holders would own approximately 44% of the combined company. At $72.86, the equity is a claim on 0.98 shares of BKH plus the probability-weighted risk of the last remaining approval (Montana), and the standalone fundamentals matter primarily as downside protection if the deal breaks.

The transaction is roughly 90% de-risked. Shareholder votes on both sides passed (April 2026), HSR cleared, FERC approved the Colstrip-370 rates, Nebraska settled, and the South Dakota PUC approved in late June 2026. The single outstanding gate is the Montana Public Service Commission, and the risk there is procedural and governance turmoil rather than substantive economics. Commissioner Brad Molnar was suspended for one year in July 2026 and barred from his office by a federal judge, and an intervenor group argues that improper participation by a replacement commissioner could invalidate a merger decision. The company targets a 2H 2026 close.

The stock has appreciated roughly 40% over the trailing year, most of it a re-rating toward the implied deal value as approvals accumulated. Against a consensus median target of $74.50 and an implied BKH-derived value near current levels, the remaining spread is thin. The standalone business is under-earning its authorized ROE (~6.3% realized versus ~9.6% authorized in Montana), carries a heavy capex program requiring equity issuance from 2027, and faces genuine tail risks in wildfire liability and Colstrip environmental compliance. We rate NWE a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the setup is a balanced merger-arb position where the reward is capped and the downside is contained but real.

## Company Overview

NorthWestern Energy is a vertically integrated, rate-regulated electric and natural gas utility serving approximately 850,300 customers across Montana, South Dakota, Nebraska, and Yellowstone National Park. Montana operations run through the NW Corp subsidiary, covering roughly 73% of the state's land area; South Dakota and Nebraska operations run through NWE Public Service.

The company operates two reportable segments. Electric (generation, transmission, and distribution) is the larger margin driver, contributing approximately $963M of the roughly $1,201M total utility margin in 2025. Natural gas covers production, storage, transmission, and distribution. The competitive structure is a regulated monopoly franchise, the largest in Montana, with no single-customer concentration, first- or second-quartile reliability, and residential bills below the national average.

Its generation mix is approximately 52% carbon-free on an owned-and-contracted basis, above the ~41% US average, anchored by 470 MW of Montana hydro plus wind. Colstrip coal remains central to reliability and is depreciable through 2042, alongside gas peakers. The company carries a net-zero-by-2050 goal that coexists uneasily with its continued coal dependence.

Effective January 1, 2026, NWE closed the acquisition of Avista's 222 MW and Puget's 370 MW interests in Colstrip for a nominal purchase price of $0, lifting its ownership to 55% (814 MW). The Puget interest is isolated in a FERC-regulated subsidiary (NW Colstrip 370) and sold under contract through late 2027; the Avista interest folds into the Montana regulated portfolio. The $0 price reflects the long-term environmental and decommissioning liabilities other utilities were eager to shed, alongside a MATS/decommissioning tail attached to the capacity.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 results show revenue growth alongside deteriorating earnings quality. FY2025 revenue reached $1,610.6M, up 6.4% from $1,513.9M in 2024 and about 13% over two years. Net income fell to $181.1M from $224.1M, a $43M decline despite higher revenue. Q1 2026 net income was $63.5M versus $76.9M a year prior (GAAP EPS $1.03 versus $1.25; non-GAAP $1.31).

The earnings decline was substantially non-recurring and regulatory in nature rather than demand-driven. The primary drivers: a $30.9M non-cash regulatory disallowance of Yellowstone County Generating Station capital costs (MPSC December 2025 order), $9.3M of merger-related costs (plus $3.4M more in Q1'26), higher depreciation (+$21.9M), higher interest expense (+$18.7M), the Montana property tax tracker, non-recoverable electric supply costs, and less favorable tax items. Underlying non-GAAP utility margin rose 11.2% to $1,200.8M on new rates. The regulated business grew; GAAP net income was compressed by disallowances, deal costs, and cost inflation outrunning rate recovery.

The chain here matters: authorized Montana ROEs sit at 9.60–9.65% (10.0% on Colstrip Unit 4), but consolidated realized ROE is only about 6.3%. Regulatory lag, the YCGS disallowance, and the February 2026 suspension of the PCCAM 90/10 supply-cost sharing mechanism have eroded returns. Recovery of the newly acquired Colstrip O&M (roughly $48M/year combined) is not yet in base rates, creating a near-term drag until a future rate case. Management affirmed 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $3.68–$3.83 with a 4–6% long-term rate-base EPS growth target.

Cash flow and the balance sheet are typical of a capital-intensive regulated utility. Operating cash flow was $394.5M in 2025 (down slightly from $406.8M). Capex is in a heavy cycle: $524.5M in 2025, rising to $683M (2026), $643M (2027), and $667M (2028), with roughly 70% of the five-year plan directed at transmission and distribution modernization. Equity issuances are planned beginning 2027 to fund South Dakota generation, a dilution overhang that becomes moot if the merger closes. Total assets stand at $8.46B, equity at $2.89B, D/E at approximately 1.93x, and the current ratio at 0.72.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.04 falls in the nominal "distress zone" but is diagnostically meaningless for a regulated utility with a huge asset base, regulated leverage, and low asset turnover (0.15). Investment-grade credit (Fitch BBB IDR, S&P BBB- Positive, operating subsidiaries at Baa2/BBB) confirms this is not a solvency issue. Near-term maturities ($150M short-term plus $105M long-term in 2026) are being refinanced; the term loan was extended to December 31, 2026, and NWE Public Service priced $150M of South Dakota First Mortgage Bonds at 5.51% (June 2036). The dividend is approximately $0.67/quarter (~$2.68 annualized), against a 60–70% payout target and a ~3.7% yield.

The earnings surprise record is mixed but reasonable: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent Q1'26 print beating by 6.9% ($1.31 versus $1.23). Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year consensus at $3.75 is unchanged over 90 days, next-year at $3.97 flat, which is expected for a utility in a locked merger process where the standalone earnings trajectory is secondary to deal mechanics. The current-quarter estimate cut of 18.4% over 30 days reflects the warm-weather volume headwind (Q1'26 was 22–26% warmer than normal, and NWE lacks a decoupling mechanism), not a structural deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth thesis rests on a 4–6% rate-base EPS growth target driven by the heavy T&D capex program and a genuine data-center load catalyst. NWE has development agreements with Sabey, Atlas, and Quantica, targeting roughly 150 MW by late 2027 scaling toward 1,500 MW by 2030. This aligns with the broad, multi-confirmed data-center demand signal visible across the utility sector (hyperscaler capex, transmission rate base, and generation additions), where regulated utilities are converting large-load agreements into recoverable rate base.

NWE filed a Large New Load tariff rule in March 2026 to capture this load growth while insulating existing ratepayers from cost-shifting, the mechanism that determines whether data-center demand is accretive or an affordability liability. Regional capacity tightening (SPP planning-reserve and accreditation requirements driving the ~$300M Aberdeen, SD gas plant; Western resource adequacy pressures underpinning the Colstrip acquisitions) supports a durable capex runway.

The growth story is real but must be weighed against two facts. First, the company is not currently earning its authorized return, so rate-base growth does not translate cleanly into realized EPS growth until regulatory lag and disallowance issues resolve. Second, the merger changes the calculus: NWE holders would own 0.98 shares of BKH and inherit a diversified eight-state growth profile rather than a Montana-centric pure-play. The standalone 4–6% target is a floor case relevant only if the deal breaks. As a directional signal, growth prospects here are moderate and heavily contingent on regulatory recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question is straightforward once framed correctly: NWE is a claim on 0.98 BKH shares. The research brief notes a live BKH price could not be re-fetched; the ~$74.35 implied BKH value is an inference from the merger spread rather than a confirmed quote, and the merger spread should be computed against a current BKH price × 0.98 before finalizing a view. The consensus price target set (mean $73.08, median $74.50, low $62.00, high $78.50) brackets the current price tightly, consistent with a stock trading close to its deal-implied value. The remaining spread to the median target is roughly 2%.

On a standalone basis, the trailing P/E of 26.7x is well above the multi-utilities peer median of 19.3x (MDU 22.7x, AVA 16.7x, BKH 18.5x, OGE 21.1x, PNW 19.3x). But the trailing multiple is distorted by the 2025 earnings decline from non-recurring disallowances and deal costs. On forward guidance of $3.68–$3.83, the multiple is closer to 19–20x, in line with peers. The standalone business is roughly fairly valued, not cheap. The 3.7% dividend yield is marginally above the peer median of 3.6% but does not signal a mispricing.

We deliberately avoid rewarding the compressed-versus-peer forward multiple as a value signal. Cheap-looking utility multiples have repeatedly sorted the wrong way when the discount reflects genuine impairment: here, under-earning the authorized ROE, PCCAM suspension, and a dilution overhang from planned 2027 equity issuance. There is no independent catalyst to close a valuation gap on the standalone entity; the only catalyst is deal completion, which the price already largely reflects.

The asymmetry is unattractive. If the merger closes, NWE holders realize approximately the value of 0.98 BKH shares, capped near current levels absent BKH appreciation. If the deal breaks on Montana, the stock likely reverts toward its pre-announcement standalone value, which we estimate in the low-$60s (the low target of $62.00 approximates this floor), a 12–15% downside. The reward-to-risk on the arb is roughly balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable, which supports a low-to-middling valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

NWE operates as a protected regulated monopoly, so competition exists only at the margins: large customers self-generating, distributed solar, and fuel substitution. The company's franchise position is stable to modestly improving: it is the largest monopoly utility in Montana, with below-average residential bills and top-quartile reliability, and it is positioned to capture data-center load growth through its LNL tariff.

Three industry dynamics are strategically relevant. First, the sector consolidation wave: the Black Hills combination is a direct expression of utilities seeking scale, balance-sheet strength, and capacity to fund large capex and data-center demand. Second, the data-center load boom, which is simultaneously the central growth catalyst and the central affordability controversy in Montana; watchdogs point to data-center-driven price hikes elsewhere (Texas). Third, regional capacity tightening in both SPP and the Western resource-adequacy framework, which underpins NWE's generation additions and Colstrip acquisitions.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than dynamically improving. The moat is intact and the load-growth optionality is real, but a Montana-centric monopoly is not gaining share in a way that reprices the equity; the merger is the mechanism management chose to solve the sub-scale, under-earning standalone position.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk cluster is merger-specific. Montana MPSC approval is the last major gate and the most contentious, and the risk is procedural rather than economic. Commissioner Brad Molnar's July 2026 one-year suspension and the federal court order barring him from office have created a governance vacuum. An environmental intervenor argues that if a replacement commissioner (or Molnar himself) improperly participates, a merger decision could be legally invalidated, a genuine tail risk to deal timing and validity. Governor Gianforte is soliciting a temporary replacement. Organized opposition (MEIC, 350 Montana) frames Montana as a "cash cow/afterthought" and criticizes $36M in merger labor "savings" and redacted data-center filings, but 11 of 12 intervenors did not seek delay and NWE reached settlements with key intervenors in April 2026, suggesting the opposition is a vocal minority.

The fixed 0.98 exchange ratio means NWE holders bear full BKH price risk with no collar. A $100M termination fee and a "force-the-vote" provision limit flexibility. Post-close, NWE holders own only ~44% with 5 of 11 board seats, effectively an acquisition by Black Hills. Additional deal-related exposures include Section 382 limitation risk on NWE's ~$452M federal NOLs, goodwill impairment risk, and integration risk.

Standalone risks: the December 2025 MPSC order disallowed $30.9M of YCGS capital as imprudent and temporarily suspended the PCCAM 90/10 supply-cost sharing mechanism effective February 1, 2026 (NWE filed for reconsideration). Wildfire liability is a significant Western exposure, partially mitigated by Montana HB490 (2025) and South Dakota SB36 (2026), which cap strict-liability claims and create a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness for approved mitigation plans; insurance premiums are rising. Colstrip MATS-rule compliance could cost NWE's proportionate share of $350–665M if enforced as written (currently under EPA reconsideration), and coal supply is contracted only through 2033 versus a 2042 depreciable life. Weather sensitivity is elevated given no decoupling mechanism.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows near-term ATM IV of 29.4% against 1-year historical vol of 23.4%, rich by 6 percentage points, with a term structure in backwardation (near-term 29.4% versus long-term 25.7%), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk, consistent with the pending Montana decision. Skew is call-heavy (-5.8% put/call skew), which does not signal defensive positioning; it suggests upside speculation on deal resolution rather than crash hedging. The implied one-month move is ±6.5%, which brackets the range between the deal-implied value and a break scenario. Short interest is low at 3.5% of float (3.1 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish positioning and no meaningful squeeze dynamic. The net read: the options market is pricing a binary event without a clear directional lean, reinforcing the balanced merger-arb characterization.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Montana MPSC clears the merger in 2H 2026 despite the commissioner turmoil, given that 11 of 12 intervenors did not seek delay and settlements are in place. NWE holders receive 0.98 BKH shares and inherit a diversified, better-scaled eight-state utility with stronger data-center growth optionality and a combined balance sheet better positioned to fund capex without the standalone 2027 dilution overhang. BKH shares appreciate on synergy realization and the sector's data-center re-rating, pulling the effective NWE value toward the $78.50 high target and beyond. In this scenario the stock reaches the high-$70s.

### Bear Case
The Molnar suspension and the legal challenge to any replacement commissioner's participation delay or invalidate the Montana vote, pushing the close into 2027 or breaking the deal. On a break, the standalone story reasserts: under-earning its authorized ROE (~6.3% versus ~9.6%), PCCAM suspension unresolved, a heavy capex cycle requiring 2027 equity dilution, and Colstrip environmental/decommissioning tail risk. The stock reverts toward its standalone value in the low-$60s, roughly the $62.00 analyst low, a 12–15% decline. A sharp drop in BKH shares, with no collar protecting NWE holders, would compound the downside even if the deal closes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting a balanced event-driven setup rather than a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Montana MPSC approves the merger cleanly, or the commissioner-participation legal question resolves in a way that removes the invalidation risk, tightening the arb toward a near-certain close while a residual spread remains.
- The BKH/NWE spread widens materially (NWE trading meaningfully below 0.98 × BKH) on transient headline noise while approval probability stays high, a cleaner arb entry.
- On a deal break, standalone shares fall to the low-$60s AND the PCCAM/YCGS regulatory overhang resolves favorably (reconsideration granted), restoring realized ROE toward authorized levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The Montana decision is invalidated or indefinitely delayed by the commissioner-participation litigation, converting the position to a broken-deal standalone with an unresolved regulatory drag.
- Material adverse movement in BKH shares that impairs the deal-implied value below the standalone floor with no offsetting catalyst.
- Evidence that data-center agreements are stalling or the LNL tariff is rejected, weakening the standalone growth floor if the deal breaks.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Montana MPSC decision, expected in 2H 2026, and the associated commissioner-replacement legal resolution. The Q2/Q3 2026 earnings prints and any updated close-timeline guidance are secondary monitoring points.

## Conclusion

NWE trades as a merger-arbitrage claim on 0.98 shares of Black Hills, with roughly 90% of the transaction de-risked and the entire outcome now hinging on a procedurally chaotic Montana MPSC vote. The standalone utility is a stable, investment-grade, regulated monopoly that is currently under-earning its authorized return, carries a heavy capex-and-dilution cycle, and faces real wildfire and Colstrip environmental tails, which the merger is partly designed to fix through scale. At $72.86, against a consensus median of $74.50 and an implied BKH-derived value near current levels, the remaining upside to the median target is thin (~2%) and the break-scenario downside is meaningful (12–15% toward the low-$60s standalone floor). The options market prices imminent event risk without a directional lean, short interest is low, and estimate revisions are flat, all consistent with a stock waiting on a binary regulatory outcome.

The score trajectory has improved modestly (5.1 → 5.2 → 5.3) as approvals accumulated and merger-completion probability rose, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend. But the remaining reward is capped and the tail risk is genuine, so we do not see grounds to escalate to BUY. This is a HOLD best understood as an event-driven position: own it if you want exposure to Black Hills at 0.98x and are comfortable with the Montana procedural risk, but recognize that the appreciation case is largely spent and the standalone fundamentals offer support, not upside.
