## Company Overview

Perrigo is headquartered in Dublin with an operations base in Grand Rapids, Michigan. It is the U.S. leader in store-brand OTC health products and also markets European consumer-health brands (Compeed, ellaOne, Solpadeine, Jungle Formula, ACO). The old Rx generics business was divested in 2021 (now discontinued ops, though retained legal liabilities persist).

Through 2025 the company reported two segments: Consumer Self-Care Americas (CSCA, ~61% of sales, U.S./Canada store brands plus Opill and Mederma) and Consumer Self-Care International (CSCI, ~39%, higher-margin European brands). In Q1 2026 it reorganized into three product-based segments: Self Care, Specialty Care (women's health, skin health), and Infant Formula, with Oral Care and Dermacosmetics in "All Other."

The revenue base is diversified at the product level (no single product exceeds 5% of revenue), but customer concentration is meaningful: Walmart is 12.9% of sales and the top 10 customers are 47%. The store-brand model competes on price/value against national brands (Haleon, Kenvue, P&G, Reckitt, Bayer) and against other private-label makers (PL Developments, LNK, Dr. Reddy's, Aurobindo). Differentiation comes from scale and breadth rather than proprietary IP; management explicitly notes no single trademark or patent is material. This is a structurally low-margin, scale-driven business with limited pricing power against both large retail buyers and branded competitors.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is one of sustained deterioration:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 4.45 | 4.66 | 4.37 | 4.25 |
| Operating income ($M) | 79 | 152 | 113 | (1,122) |
| Net income ($M) | (141) | (13) | (172) | (1,425) |
| Gross margin | 32.7% | 36.1% | 35.3% | 35.1% |

The 2025 operating loss was driven by a $1.3B goodwill impairment ($917M CSCA + $407M CSCI), triggered by a sustained share-price decline, weak infant-formula cash flows, and a softer self-care outlook. Stripping the impairment, operating income was only marginally positive at ~$65M on $4.25B of revenue, an operating margin of roughly 1.5% on a GAAP basis, which shows how thin the underlying franchise is once one-time items are excluded.

Q1 2026 deteriorated further. Revenue fell 7.2% YoY to $969M, gross margin dropped 400bps to 33.6%, and the company recorded another $330.8M goodwill impairment (Infant Formula and Oral Care goodwill now fully written to zero; Women's Health partially impaired). The net loss was −$398.6M (−$2.87/share). The reported trailing EPS of −$10.12 reflects the cumulative impairment charges.

Cash flow quality is the most important number in this analysis. FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $238.5M (from $362.9M in 2024). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was worse than the prior-year Q1's −$64.5M. Q1 is seasonally weak, but the trend is negative, and the company must generate materially positive operating cash flow to service its obligations.

The balance sheet carries D/E of ~1.9x and cash of $357M at Q1 2026 (down from $532M at YE25). The retained-earnings deficit deepened to −$4.08B. The current ratio (~2.7x) is healthy and the company remains in covenant compliance (max first-lien secured net leverage of 3.0x applies only to the Revolver/Term Loan B). In March 2026 Perrigo amended and restated its credit agreement, drew $427.6M on the revolver to fully repay Term Loan A, and extended the revolver to 2031. Term Loan B ($970M) matures April 2029; the earliest bond maturity is now 2030.

The debt-service math is the crux. Interest plus dividend total ~$322M in annual cash commitments against FY2025 free cash flow and negative Q1 operating cash flow. That gap can only be bridged by asset sales (Dermacosmetics ~€306M provided one tranche) or by cutting the dividend. A company covering less than half its cash commitments from internal free cash flow, while relying on serial divestitures to plug the difference, does not have a stable capital structure. The full U.S. valuation allowance on deferred tax assets recorded in 2025 (+$129.9M) is management's own admission that near-term U.S. profitability is unlikely.

Financial Health scores 2.5. The distress-zone Z-Score, four consecutive years of net losses, negative Q1 operating cash flow, single-B credit rating, and a debt load that swamps free cash flow are the reliable discriminators here, and per our own history, a weak financial-health score is the single most durable flag for underperformers.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis. Revenue has declined from $4.66B in 2023 to $4.25B in 2025, and Q1 2026 continued the trend at −7.2% YoY. The drivers are soft OTC consumption, a mild cough/cold season, store-brand deflation, and structural share loss in infant formula following the FDA warning letter (Aug 2023), production pause, lost Good Start distribution, and rising import competition under Operation Stork Speed.

The "growth" available to Perrigo is manufactured: cost-out programs (Operational Enhancement Program targeting ~7% headcount reduction and $80-100M run-rate savings, layered on the substantively complete Supply Chain Reinvention ~$157M and Project Energize ~$167M) plus portfolio pruning. These can stabilize margins but do not grow the top line. Estimate revisions confirm the absence of positive momentum: current-year consensus EPS has been cut 6.1% over 90 days to $2.11, current-quarter down 9.5%, and every window is flat-to-negative over 30 days. The estimate trend is decay, not acceleration.

The Q1 2026 EPS beat (adjusted actual $0.43 vs. the $0.32 adjusted estimate; a 34.5% surprise on the adjusted line) reflects cost-cutting and tariff-refund timing rather than demand recovery. Over the trailing four quarters the company has 2 beats and 2 misses, with no consistent positive surprise pattern that would raise confidence in forward estimates.

Growth Prospects scores 3.0. The best case is stabilization of a shrinking base, not expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP earnings the stock is meaningless (negative EPS from impairments). On adjusted earnings ($2.36 next-year), the stock trades near 5x, a steep discount to the pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 20.9x (INVA 3.9x, COLL 20.9x, ANIP 20.9x, AMPH 12.3x). The sell-side target range implies 34-91% upside.

The temptation is to treat this as deep value. The discipline our own record enforces is the opposite: a low multiple is anti-predictive and most reliably marks a value trap absent a specific catalyst. Here the compression reflects structural impairment, not mispricing. The 5x "adjusted" earnings multiple ignores that adjusted EPS excludes the very impairments that validate management's own DCF models seeing materially lower future cash flows across the entire portfolio. It also ignores that the equity sits behind $3.6B of debt: the enterprise is far more expensive on EV/EBITDA than the equity multiple suggests, and the residual equity claim is highly sensitive to any further erosion in operating income already running near 1-2% of sales on a GAAP basis.

Addressing the asymmetry directly: the current price sits above a plausible distressed bear case ($6-7) but well below the sell-side range. The reason we do not treat that gap as favorable asymmetry is that the sell-side targets are anchored to the adjusted-EPS line and a de-levering scenario that requires the infant-formula exit, the IRS refund, and cost savings all to land while operating cash flow turns positive. Each is contingent, and none addresses the core problem that the underlying self-care franchise is low-margin and shrinking. A cheap multiple on earnings that (a) exclude recurring impairments, (b) are being revised downward, and (c) sit behind a debt load consuming all free cash flow is optionality priced against a real probability of terminal equity impairment.

Valuation scores 4.5. This is above the lowest bucket because the adjusted earnings are genuinely positive, the Dermacosmetics cash is real, and there is a monetization path that could support a higher price if executed cleanly. But it is not a high score, because cheapness here is a distress signal, not evidence the market is wrong.

## Competitive Landscape

Perrigo's competitive position is stable-to-eroding. In its core store-brand OTC franchise, it retains scale leadership and benefits from a secular tailwind of store-brand share gains as consumers trade down. That volume tailwind is being offset by price investment, deflation, and mix. CSCI (Europe) carries structurally higher gross margins (~44%) than CSCA (~29%), and the retained European brand portfolio is the more defensible piece of the business.

Infant formula is the clear competitive loser: FDA regulatory forbearance on imports created a structurally more competitive market, and Perrigo's Gateway plant issues (warning letter, production pause, a fresh Form 483 in Feb 2026) mean it cannot recover share. Goodwill is now fully impaired, and the business is under strategic review for potential exit, a rational move but confirmation that this franchise's competitive position deteriorated to the point of value destruction.

The store-brand model's structural weakness is buyer power: Walmart at 12.9% and the top 10 at 47% of sales, combined with no material proprietary IP, means Perrigo competes on price against both branded and private-label rivals with limited leverage. Competitive Position scores 4.0: the core Self Care/Specialty Care franchises are defensible on scale, but the trajectory is flat-to-down and the infant-formula collapse drags the overall picture.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are largely not yet priced and are heavily skewed to the downside:

1. **Further impairments.** Management flagged that post-reorg, Women's Health has fair value equal to carrying value and Skin Health passes by less than 10% margin. Any deterioration triggers additional write-downs. This is a live, near-term risk, not a tail.

2. **Credit downgrades and refinancing.** Moody's cut to Ba3 in December 2025, and in March 2026 S&P cut to B+ from BB- and Fitch moved to a negative outlook. The 2030 notes' coupon has already stepped up to the 5.15% cap. Further downgrades raise the cost of refinancing the 2029 (Term Loan B, $970M) and 2030 maturity wall.

3. **Dividend cut.** The ~$0.29/quarter (~$160M/year, ~11% yield) dividend is uncovered by free cash flow and paid despite negative Q1 operating cash flow. A cut is a plausible near-term catalyst: negative for the income base that currently supports the stock, though positive for balance-sheet repair.

4. **Governance and litigation.** The CEO resigned effective June 2026 after the board determined his personal conduct violated company values, a for-cause exit during an active turnaround, with Albert Manzone as interim CEO and a cluster of June-July 2026 officer/board changes. A securities class action (filed Nov 2025) names the company, current CEO, CFO, and former CEO Kessler over infant-formula disclosures. Layered on top: generic drug price-fixing MDL (bellwether trials 2026-2028), ~300 talc suits (40+ trials 2026-2027), and IRS transfer-pricing disputes (favorable omeprazole ruling on DOJ appeal; new Feb 2026 NOPA on Perrigo Ireland; ANDA carryforward exposure $25M-$128M plus interest/penalties).

5. **Tariffs and demand.** Tariffs add an estimated $30-40M to 2026 COGS, partly offset by a one-time ~$21M Q1 tariff-refund receivable.

Risk Factors scores 2.5. The combination of a for-cause CEO vacancy, active securities litigation naming both current and former CEO, signaled additional impairments, and downgrade-driven refinancing risk represents stacked, partially-unpriced downside catalysts.

The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±15%. The term structure is in mild contango (65.0% near vs. 68.4% at six months). The most notable shift since May is the put/call open-interest ratio jumping from 0.23 to 3.68, and the six-month tenor shows a 4.8% put skew with a P/C volume ratio of 6.90: hedging demand building into the fall around the dividend decision, strategic-review outcome, and permanent-CEO appointment. The options market is not confirming a clean bearish setup; it is pricing a wide two-sided outcome, which is consistent with a distressed special situation rather than an orderly decline.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($16-20)
The turnaround executes. Infant formula is exited, removing a capital-intensive, value-destroying, regulatorily fraught franchise and generating divestiture proceeds. Cost programs deliver run-rate savings, stabilizing margins in the core Self Care/Specialty Care businesses. The IRS omeprazole refund is collected. Combined with the Dermacosmetics proceeds, net leverage falls, the dividend is reset to a sustainable level or maintained on recovering cash flow, and the equity re-rates toward the peer-adjusted 8-10x on ~$2.10-2.40 of adjusted EPS. Store-brand secular tailwinds provide volume support. At the sell-side mean of $16.50, this is ~58% upside.

### Bear Case ($5-7)
Operating cash flow stays negative or barely positive, the dividend is cut (removing the income support that anchors current holders), additional impairments hit Women's Health and Skin Health, and further rating downgrades raise financing costs into the 2029-2030 maturity wall. The infant-formula review drags without resolution, the securities and tax litigation produce charges, and revenue continues to decline at a mid-single-digit pace. The equity, as a thin residual behind $3.6B of debt, compresses another 30-50% toward distressed levels.

The bull case requires multiple contingent events to land in sequence; the bear case requires only that the current trajectory (declining revenue, negative operating cash flow, uncovered dividend, further impairments) persist. The base-case outcome sits between $9-11, meaning the current price already embeds much of the distress but little of the recovery. We do not see favorable asymmetry for a new long: the reward requires execution across leadership, litigation, tax, and operating dimensions simultaneously, while the risk requires nothing new.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The clearest dated catalysts are the dividend decision (likely to be revisited given negative operating cash flow), the infant-formula strategic-review outcome, and the permanent-CEO appointment, plus the next earnings report. A dividend cut would remove income-buyer support. None of these is a hard-dated collapse trigger, and several (asset monetization, IRS refund) could cut the other way.

**Timing window**: Multi-quarter. The maturity wall (2029-2030) is years out, so there is no near-term refinancing forcing event. Catalysts are diffuse rather than a single dated resolution.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 27.3% of float with 5.6 days to cover, very high. This is a crowded short.

**Borrow and options signal**: Six-month put skew (+4.8%) and P/C OI of 3.68 show bears are already positioned; near-term IV is rich (65% vs. 51.6% HV), raising the cost of expressing the view through options. Elevated borrow cost is implied.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear case $5-7 versus current $10.47 implies ~33-52% downside. Upside squeeze/monetization risk is meaningful given 27.3% SI, the sell-side $14-20 range, and a plausible asset-sale relief catalyst. Reward:risk is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold for an active short.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT**. The bearish thesis is largely correct but heavily priced: 27.3% short interest, a stock already down 62% from its 52-week high, remaining downside of only ~33-52%, and real squeeze/relief-catalyst risk. The remaining reward does not justify the carry and squeeze exposure. This is a name to avoid owning, not to short at $10.47.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID (new long) at $10.47.**

To reconcile the AVOID directly with the price level: our objection is not that the stock is expensive on adjusted earnings, but that the equity is a thin residual claim on a shrinking, sub-10%-margin franchise whose debt-service commitments (~$322M of interest plus dividend) consume more than twice its FY2025 free cash flow and turn negative on a quarterly operating basis. That is a structural-impairment risk to the equity, not a timing problem. We would not have recommended a new long at $14-20 either, because at those levels the same debt-service gap existed against an even larger downside to distressed value. The view flips to a speculative long only on de-levering evidence, not on price alone.

**Re-entry criteria** (any one insufficient; require the cluster):
- Net leverage demonstrably reduced below ~2.5x, funded by completed divestitures (infant-formula exit closed) rather than revolver draws.
- The dividend reset to a covered level, with two consecutive quarters of clearly positive operating cash flow (Q1 seasonality acknowledged; the full-year run-rate must service debt without asset sales).
- No further goodwill impairments on Women's Health/Skin Health for two quarters, and a permanent CEO installed with a credible operating plan.
- Alternatively, a distressed entry near $6 where the risk/reward on the recovery optionality genuinely turns asymmetric.

**Timeline**: The headwinds persist until the balance sheet is repaired, a multi-quarter to multi-year process gated by the infant-formula review, cost-program delivery, and litigation resolution. We would reassess at each quarterly print, watching operating cash flow as the single most important variable.

## Conclusion

Perrigo is a special-situation turnaround, not a stable compounder. The declining score trajectory (3.6 → 3.2, now 3.25) reflects genuine fundamental deterioration confirmed by the latest data: a second impairment, negative operating cash flow, a for-cause CEO exit, and three rating agencies moving negative. The counterweight of Dermacosmetics cash, a potential infant-formula exit, a contingent IRS refund, and cost savings is real optionality but subordinate to a capital structure that consumes all free cash flow servicing $3.6B of debt and an uncovered dividend.

The 62% decline has priced in much of the distress, which is why we do not recommend shorting at $10.47. But it has not created a compelling long, because the base case sits at the current price, the bull case requires flawless multi-front execution, and the bear case requires only that the present trajectory continue. For a new position, AVOID. We would revisit on demonstrated de-levering with the dividend reset and operating cash flow durably positive, or at a distressed price near $6 where the recovery option is more cheaply owned.
