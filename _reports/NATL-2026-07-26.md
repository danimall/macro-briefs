**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NCR Atleos trades as a merger-arbitrage instrument. On February 26, 2026, the company agreed to be acquired by The Brink's Company (NYSE: BCO) in a transaction valued at approximately $6.6 billion. Shareholders of both companies approved the merger on June 30, 2026, and the deal has already cleared U.S. Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust review and India's Competition Commission (July 22, 2026). Management guides to a close by the end of Q1 2027, subject to remaining regulatory approvals and customary conditions. At $47.75, roughly 9% above the pre-offer price, the relevant questions are deal spread, close probability, timeline, and downside-if-broken, not standalone DCF or multiple.

The residual return here is thin. The stock sits near its 52-week high of $48.50 and only slightly below the reported analyst-target cluster ($50.00-$50.40, which effectively reflect deal-consideration marks rather than fundamental fair value). With shareholder and two major antitrust approvals secured, completion probability appears high, but the remaining arb spread is a few percent against a ~9-month hold, and the acquirer's own shares fell 20-28% on the announcement as the market questioned Brink's incremental leverage. That skepticism is directed at Brink's balance sheet, not at deal completion, but it warrants monitoring because financing stress on the acquirer side is one of the few paths to a break.

Standalone, NATL is a leveraged, cash-generative self-service banking business (2.99x net EBITDA leverage, $326M adjusted free cash flow, 70.6% recurring revenue) with a genuine internal-controls blemish: a 2024 restatement and material weakness disclosed in a November 2025 10-K/A, remediated as of year-end 2025. Estimates are falling (current-year consensus revised down 14.9% over 90 days, Q1 2026 missed by 27%), which matters for the break case but is second-order given the merger. We rate NATL a HOLD. The upside is capped near the deal price; the downside is a re-rating toward the pre-offer standalone level (roughly $41-42, its trading range in the days immediately before the February 26 announcement) if remaining conditions fail. That is not an attractive risk/reward for new capital, but existing holders positioned for the arb have a defensible reason to hold to close.

## Company Overview

NCR Atleos was spun off from NCR Corporation on October 16, 2023 (the former parent is now NCR Voyix). It is a financial-technology company focused on self-directed and self-service banking, organized in three segments:

- **Self-Service Banking (66% of revenue, $2,881M in 2025):** ATM and ITM hardware, the AMP software platform, maintenance and services, and the fast-growing ATM-as-a-Service (ATMaaS) outsourcing model. ATMaaS revenue grew 33% to $258M in 2025.
- **Network (29%, $1,265M):** The Allpoint network, the largest retail surcharge-free independent ATM network in the U.S. at roughly 78,000 locations, monetized via interchange, surcharge, and branding fees from banks, credit unions, fintechs, and neobanks. Includes LibertyX (Bitcoin) and ReadyCode.
- **T&T (4%, $168M):** Managed telecom and network infrastructure services, a non-core, declining segment (-13% in 2025).

Approximately 55% of revenue is international, with ATMs serviced in 65 countries and roughly 20,000 employees. The company holds the leading independent ATM-network position and is a leading ATMaaS provider; competitors include Fiserv, Euronet, Hyosung, Diebold Nixdorf, and, until the transaction, Brink's itself.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue reached $4,354M in 2025, up 1% year-over-year (core segment revenue +4%, masked by the wind-down of legacy Voyix commercial-agreement revenue). Profitability continued to recover: net income of $162M in 2025 versus $91M in 2024 and a $(134)M loss in 2023. Adjusted EBITDA was $830M at a 19.1% margin, up from 18.2% the prior year; GAAP gross margin was 24.4%.

The strategic engine is mix shift toward recurring revenue, now 70.6% of the total ($3,075M), driven by ATMaaS conversion (which roughly doubles recurring revenue per customer). Operating cash flow was $356M and adjusted unrestricted free cash flow $326M, up from $242M, against $117M of capex that is rising to support ATMaaS deployment.

The balance sheet is the key vulnerability and the reason the acquirer's stock sold off. Total debt is approximately $2,798M against just $403M of book equity, a debt/equity ratio near 7x on total debt (the research brief cites a D/E of ~13x, reflecting a broader liabilities definition). The Altman Z-Score of 1.18 formally lands in the distress zone, but this reflects a leveraged spin-off capital structure with thin, goodwill-heavy book equity rather than cash-flow distress. Net leverage of 2.99x EBITDA (Q3 2025) sits comfortably below the 4.25x covenant ceiling. The capital structure includes 9.5% senior secured notes due 2029 ($1,350M, callable October 2026 at 104.75) and term loans that were repriced down in 2025 (Term Loan B spread from S+375 to S+300), cutting interest expense from $309M in 2024 to $270M in 2025. Cash was $456M; ratings are Moody's B1 / S&P B+.

The earnings pattern has deteriorated at the margin. Q1 2026 EPS of $0.65 missed the $0.89 estimate by 27% after a Q4 2025 beat ($1.49 vs. $1.38 estimate). Consensus has been cut across every window: current quarter -15.0% over 30 days, current year -14.9% over 90 days, next year -10.5% over 90 days. For a standalone equity this would be a clear warning; in a near-certain acquisition it primarily sharpens the downside case if the deal breaks.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is modest and mix-driven. Reported revenue growth was flat-to-1%, with the recurring-revenue conversion story (ATMaaS +33%, recurring mix 70.6%) the genuine positive and legacy Voyix and T&T run-off the drag. Secular demand for outsourced ATM fleets and branch rationalization supports the ATMaaS and Allpoint franchises, while declining cash usage (Venmo, Zelle, Cash App, Apple Pay) is the long-term headwind. U.S. immigration policy is a specific, non-generic drag on the Network segment through fewer prepaid-payroll-card and dynamic-currency-conversion transactions.

The falling estimate trend and the 27% Q1 2026 miss argue against extrapolating the recurring-revenue narrative into accelerating earnings. But for a company being absorbed by Q1 2027, forward standalone growth is largely academic; the operating value now accrues to Brink's, whose combined platform (cash-in-transit logistics plus ATM managed services and software) is the vehicle for whatever synergy realization occurs. Growth Prospects scores below neutral to reflect the flat top line, negative revision momentum, and the extinguishing of standalone growth optionality by the takeover.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation here is deal-mechanics, not multiple. At $47.75 the stock trades on a ~$3.5B market cap, a trailing/forward P/E of ~21x on $2.14 EPS, at a premium to the peer median P/E of 15.3x (FOUR 44.0, EEFT 9.7, SEZL 30.8, WEX 15.3, WU 5.4). That multiple dispersion indicates the sector is heterogeneous and that NATL's price reflects the acquisition, not organic fundamentals. The reported analyst targets ($50.00 low, $50.40 high, all four ratings HOLD) are best read as deal-consideration marks; they are not independent evidence of undervaluation.

The actionable metric is the arb spread. With shareholder approval secured on both sides and U.S. plus India antitrust clearances in hand, completion probability is high and the residual spread from $47.75 to the deal consideration (a cash/stock mix per the joint proxy) is a few percent over roughly nine months. The precise per-share consideration and stock-election mechanics should be confirmed from the definitive merger agreement and S-4 before sizing the arb.

The downside anchor is the standalone break level. Pre-offer, the stock traded near $40-42 in the days immediately before the announcement (having reached the low-$42s in mid-February before pulling back to ~$41 by February 25); a deal break on remaining regulatory conditions would likely re-rate NATL toward that range, compounded by the falling estimate trend and the leverage-heavy balance sheet, roughly a 12-16% drawdown from current levels. On standalone fundamentals alone, the leverage, the 2024 restatement history, and negative revision momentum would justify no better than a mid-range multiple, meaning the deal price already captures a fair amount of the recurring-revenue upside. We score Valuation modestly above neutral: the market is not clearly mispricing this, but the asymmetry (small upside to deal, moderate downside to break) is not egregious, and the arb offers a defined, event-driven return.

Cheapness is not the case here: the peer-relative "premium" P/E is a deal artifact, and applying trailing-multiple logic would mislead in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

NATL holds the #1 independent surcharge-free ATM network (Allpoint, ~78,000 locations) and a leading ATMaaS/managed-services position, with ~55% international revenue and a defensible recurring-revenue base. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the ATMaaS conversion and branch-outsourcing tailwind, offset by the secular decline in cash usage and the Network-segment immigration headwind. The strategic logic of the Brink's combination is complementary: Brink's physical cash logistics plus Atleos's ATM network and software creates a broader cash-management-plus-managed-services platform, described by analysts as accretive to Brink's.

Because the trajectory matters more than the level, and because the moat is stable rather than widening, Competitive Position scores at neutral. The prior report's step-down from 7.0 to 5.5 on this dimension reflected the merger reframing (standalone competitive optionality is being subsumed), and current data does not warrant reversing that.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is deal-completion risk. Failure to obtain remaining regulatory approvals, financing stress on Brink's (which is taking on substantial new debt), or a material adverse change would break the deal and re-rate NATL toward its pre-offer standalone level. With two shareholder votes passed and two major antitrust clearances secured, this probability is low but non-zero, and the acquirer's leverage skepticism is the primary channel to monitor.

The most substantive standalone red flag is the internal-controls history. A material weakness in ICFR, tied to contract cancellations, customer credits, unbilled receivables, and contract-liability reconciliations, was disclosed in the 2024 Form 10-K/A filed November 5, 2025, which restated 2024 financials. A separate immaterial misstatement was identified in Q3 2025. Management states the material weakness was remediated as of December 31, 2025, with auditor attestation, but this is a young public company whose finance function stumbled, and "potential undisclosed liabilities" language appears in the merger's own risk disclosures.

Other risks: the Voyix indemnification chain, including Kalamazoo River environmental remediation (expense rose to $28M in 2025 from $14M, with a $48M indemnification obligation scheduled for 2026 and an unquantified multi-year tail); vault-cash interest-rate exposure; an underfunded U.S. pension ($248M); high leverage magnifying operating risk; and the secular cash-usage decline. Short interest is modest at 6.2% of float with 4.7 days to cover, likely reflecting arb hedging rather than directional bearishness.

Risk Factors scores at neutral-to-slightly-favorable: the balance-sheet distress flag is misleading (leverage-structure driven, not cash-flow driven), the material weakness is remediated, and the merger provides a defined outcome that caps most idiosyncratic tail risk. The residual concern is a low-probability break with moderate downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options data must be read through a merger-arb lens. Near-term ATM IV of 31.0% sits below one-year historical volatility of 34.2% (cheap by 3.2 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (31.0% near-term vs. 22.5% at six months), pricing some imminent event stress into the front month with an implied move of ±9.8%. The prior-snapshot ATM IV reading of 334.6% collapsing to ~21.3% is a data artifact and should be disregarded. Skew is mildly call-heavy (-3.9%), inconsistent with a bearish setup and consistent with a stock pinned near a deal price. The options market is not flagging elevated break risk; the backwardation reflects normal event pricing around the extended regulatory timeline rather than directional fear.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$50-50.40, +5-6%):** The deal closes on schedule by Q1 2027 at the full consideration. Remaining regulatory approvals are procedural, Brink's financing holds, and holders capture the arb spread plus any stock-election upside if the mix component appreciates. This is the base expectation given approvals already secured.

**Bear Case (~$40-42, -12-16%):** A remaining regulatory condition or acquirer-financing stress breaks the deal. NATL re-rates toward its pre-offer standalone level, pressured further by the falling estimate trend, the Q1 2026 miss, and renewed focus on the leverage-heavy balance sheet and residual controls-history discount.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

- **Upgrade triggers:** (1) A widening of the arb spread to >6-8% on transient regulatory noise without a genuine break signal, which would restore attractive event-driven risk/reward for new capital; (2) confirmation that the stock-election component of the consideration carries meaningful upside optionality relative to the cash mark; (3) a definitive final regulatory clearance that pushes close probability to near-certain, tightening the spread and effectively guaranteeing the return.
- **Downgrade triggers:** (1) Any signal that Brink's financing is impaired or that a remaining regulator is inclined to block or heavily condition the deal; (2) a material adverse change disclosure or renewed accounting/controls issue at NATL surfacing undisclosed liabilities; (3) a formal termination or renegotiation of the merger agreement at a lower price.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the remaining regulatory-clearance milestones through H2 2026 and the guided Q1 2027 close. Confirm the exact per-share consideration and cash/stock election mechanics from the definitive joint proxy/S-4 to finalize spread math.

## Conclusion

NCR Atleos is a merger-arbitrage situation trading a few percent below its acquisition consideration, with shareholder votes and two major antitrust clearances already secured and a Q1 2027 target close. The standalone business, leveraged but cash-generative, with a leading independent ATM network and a recurring-revenue conversion story, matters now chiefly for gauging break-case downside and understanding the assets Brink's is buying. That downside (~12-16% to the pre-offer level) is real but low-probability; the upside (~5-6% to deal) is defined and modest.

We rate NATL a **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The classification reflects a defined event-driven return path to a Q1 2027 close, not a standalone appreciation thesis. Existing holders positioned for the arb have a reasonable reason to hold to close and collect the spread. New capital should demand a wider spread, a pullback that lifts the implied return above 6-8%, before committing, given the thin residual upside and the acquirer-financing skepticism that is the principal channel to a break. The declining standalone score trajectory (5.7 → 4.8 → 5.3 here) reflects the reframing away from fundamentals toward deal mechanics; today's data confirms high completion probability while acknowledging that the falling estimate trend and controls history would leave standalone value below the current price if the deal fails.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the pre-offer standalone level was ~$40-42 (not ~$44), widening the break-case downside to roughly 12-16% (not 8-12%), which makes the arb risk/reward modestly less favorable than the report's asymmetry framing implies.
