## Executive Summary

First Solar is the only scale solar module manufacturer whose product contains no polysilicon, and on August 6, 2026 the U.S. government announced a Section 232 action imposing a 15% tariff plus minimum import prices on polysilicon and its derivatives — $0.38/W on modules, $0.22/W on cells — effective December 4, 2026. First Solar's contracted backlog carries an implied ASP of approximately $0.3016/W. If the announced floor holds for competing crystalline-silicon product, the competitive set reprices roughly 26% above First Solar's contracted price for imported finished modules and 33-59% above it for domestically assembled c-Si at Roth Capital's estimated $0.40-0.48/W. None of that is embedded in FY2026 guidance, which explicitly "assumes the current U.S. policy environment persists." Shares rose from $199.24 on July 29 to $244.14, a 22.5% move that has already discounted part of it.

The other side of the file comes from the company's own disclosure. FY2026 guided gross profit of $2.4-2.6B "assumes $2.10 billion to $2.19 billion of Section 45X tax credits." Taking midpoints, gross profit before the credit is roughly $355M, or 7.0% of guided net sales of $5.05B; against guided operating expenses of $610-635M, operating income excluding the credit would be approximately negative $250-270M. The credit is real cash and the U.S. cost structure that suppresses the residual exists because of it, so stripping one while holding the other constant overstates fragility. The earnings stream carries a legislated 2030 phase-down and 2032 expiry, and Congress has already amended eligibility once (H.R.1, July 4, 2025, restricting foreign-entity-of-concern content). The underlying residual margin has compressed from roughly 10.0% in FY2025 to 6.7% in H1 2026.

Three things temper the policy tailwind. H1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $359.8M against $769.2M of net income, driven by a $649.3M working-capital use in government grants receivable, with no new 45X transfer agreements disclosed in H1 and the receivable balance shifting from $125.6M noncurrent at year-end to $978.8M noncurrent at June 30 (carried at $978.8M against a disclosed $953.4M fair value). H1 bookings were 2.8 GW against 7.6 GW sold, a 0.37x book-to-bill, with backlog down from 50.1 GW to 45.1 GW — a third consecutive decline. The reaffirmed guide requires H2 volume of 9.4-10.6 GW versus 7.6 GW in H1, implying a Q4 of roughly 4.9-6.1 GW, which would be the largest quarter in company history. At 14.0x FY2026 and 10.7x FY2027 consensus with a base case near $270, expected return is positive but thin relative to a credible $165 bear case. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

First Solar designs and manufactures cadmium telluride thin-film photovoltaic modules and operates as a single reporting segment following its earlier exit from systems development and EPC. Modules are priced per watt and sold to utility-scale developers, IPPs, utilities and corporate buyers. Manufacturing spans five operating U.S. facilities plus a sixth under construction in South Carolina, and plants in India, Malaysia and Vietnam. Cumulative module sales passed 100 GW in Q2 2026.

The differentiators are structural. The continuous CdTe process uses approximately 2-3% of the semiconductor material of a conventional c-Si module and produces a finished module in hours rather than through a batch sequence, and it does not touch Chinese polysilicon supply chains. That makes the company the largest thin-film manufacturer globally, the largest module manufacturer in the Western Hemisphere, and the primary beneficiary of U.S. domestic-content rules and Section 45X. The technology roadmap has three tracks: CuRe (one Ohio facility permanently converted in H1 2026, with management claiming up to 8% more lifetime energy yield versus TOPCon — an issuer claim), bifacial development, and a perovskite tandem pilot line in the Series 6 form factor expected operational in 2027.

Customer concentration remains a structural weakness: the FY2025 10-K discloses Silicon Ranch and NextEra each above 10% of net sales, and the Q2 10-Q offers only qualitative language ("primarily concentrated among a limited number of customers"). Reporting that 41 of the 45.1 GW backlog requires some domestic content establishes mix, not diversification.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat to down; margin optics are up.** FY2025 net sales were $5.219B (+24% YoY) with net income of $1,528.2M, cross-checked against the movement in accumulated earnings. Reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 17.0-18.2 GW and $4.9-5.2B implies a ~3% revenue decline at midpoint on roughly flat volume, and an implied realized ASP of ~$0.287/W versus ~$0.298/W in FY2025.

Q2 2026: net sales $1,056.2M (-3.7% YoY), gross profit $605.0M (57.3% versus 45.6%), operating income $450.4M, net income $422.6M, diluted EPS $3.92. H1: net sales $2,100.4M (+8.2%), gross margin 51.9%, net income $769.2M, diluted EPS $7.14. Revenue fell while gross margin expanded 11.7 points. Management attributes the gain to an $88.6M net benefit from expected IEEPA tariff refunds, higher 45X-qualifying volume, and lower logistics costs, partially offset by lower revenue from customer contract terminations and higher duties. Only one of those three is operational, and the tariff-refund item is a newly adopted ASC 450 loss-recovery estimate — roughly 8 points of the 11.7-point margin expansion by that reading, recognized when recovery became probable, with the filing warning that ultimate recoveries "may differ from the full amount we previously paid, and that difference may be material."

**Earnings quality, quantified.** Using the "income-related government grants recognized in cost of sales" disclosure, the residual gross profit excluding credits was $165.4M (8.5% of sales) in H1 2025, roughly $520M (~10.0%) in FY2025, and $140.0M (6.7%) in H1 2026 after also excluding the IEEPA benefit. The residual is not a fair normalized margin because the U.S. capacity that generates the ~$0.17/W credit is expensive by design, but the direction is unfavorable, and the guidance footnote establishes the magnitude of policy exposure any terminal-value assumption has to carry.

**Cash conversion is the weakest element.** H1 operating cash flow was negative $359.8M against net income. Grants receivable was a $649.3M use, inventories $306.0M, payables and accruals $166.1M, deferred revenue $155.8M, receivables $141.0M. The grants line matters most: the company monetized credits three times between December 2024 and October 2025 at roughly 95% of face (~$2.26B of face value in total), and disclosed no new transfer agreements in H1 2026. The Q2 adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation corroborates this — "Section 45X tax credit discounts" were $0 in Q2 and H1 2026 versus $28,987K in Q2 2025. Cash actually received in H1 was $95.2M as the tail of the October 2025 transaction plus $117.6M directly from Treasury for 2024-vintage credits, against FY2026 credit generation guided at $2.10-2.19B.

Two readings are available and the filings do not settle it. Electing direct IRS refund avoids the ~5%-of-face discount and is value-accretive though slower; alternatively the transfer market may have tightened following H.R.1's FEOC restrictions. The noncurrent balance also contains the Tamil Nadu 24% capex subsidy, receivable in six annual installments with the first collected in July 2026, so not all of the reclassification is 45X. Negative H1 operating cash flow also occurred in H1 2025 (-$458.4M), so seasonality is genuine. The observable consequence — GAAP earnings converting to cash more slowly and at a discount — stands regardless of cause. Guided year-end net cash of $1.7-2.3B against $1.7B at June 30, after $0.4-0.6B of remaining capex, requires a large H2 inflow that depends materially on 45X collection resuming.

**The balance sheet is genuinely strong.** Cash and marketable securities $1,727.0M; total debt $37.6M after the $500M DFC India facility was fully prepaid in May 2026; net cash ~$1,689.4M (~$15.72/share, 6.4% of the current price). Equity $10,320.3M against total liabilities $3,068.3M (0.30x). Current ratio 2.52. In February 2026 the company replaced a $1.0B secured revolver with a $1.5B unsecured five-year facility priced at Term SOFR + 1.00-1.75% on a net-leverage grid with an investment-grade-ratings conversion pathway; $1.4M of letters of credit drawn. Off-balance-sheet items are modest: $222.0M of bilateral LCs, $140.8M of surety bonds, and up to ~$300M of glass-supply termination payments if cancelled. Dilution is minimal — weighted diluted shares 107,732K in Q2 2026 versus 107,518K a year earlier (+0.2%), with no convertibles, warrants or earn-outs and $40.5M of unrecognized share-based compensation.

**Data reconciliation note.** The market-data feed reports EPS of $14.21 and identical trailing and forward P/E of 15.05x, which cannot both be true at $244.14. The trailing multiple reconciles against TTM diluted EPS of $16.21 derived from filings (H1 2026 $769.2M plus H2 2025 $976.8M, over ~107.7M shares), giving 15.06x. I use the filing-derived figure. Similarly, the provided quarterly surprise table shows a -24.7% Q2 2026 miss against a $5.21 estimate, while third-party reporting cites GAAP $3.92 against a $2.74 consensus. The two are not on a reconcilable basis and one appears mislabeled by quarter; the filing figures ($3.22 in Q4 2025, $3.92 in Q2, $7.14 for H1) are what I rely on. The current-year consensus of $17.44 does reconcile cleanly as $7.14 actual plus $4.67 for Q3 and $5.69 for Q4.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is weak on the company's own numbers and depends on policy for its 2027 shape.

**Volume and revenue.** FY2026 guidance implies roughly flat volume and a ~3% revenue decline. Growth resumes in consensus only in FY2027, where EPS of $22.74 implies ~30% growth over the $17.44 FY2026 figure. That estimate has been cut 2.5% over 90 days.

**Bookings are the problem.** Backlog moved 50.1 GW → 45.1 GW in H1 (7.6 GW sold, +2.8 GW booked, -0.2 GW cancelled), a 0.37x book-to-bill and a third consecutive decline. Coverage is roughly 2.6 years against ~17.6 GW of annual volume. Pricing in backlog, however, has not broken: $0.3054/W (9/30/25), $0.2994/W (12/31/25), $0.3016/W (6/30/26). The most coherent reading is a timing pause, with customers deferring commitments while the Section 232 outcome, announced five weeks after the balance-sheet date, redefined the price of every alternative. The competing reading is demand destruction from H.R.1's accelerated termination of the clean-electricity ITC and PTC for solar, in which case flat backlog ASP just means legacy contracts drawing down at old prices. Two or three more quarters at 0.37x converts this from a timing question into a thesis break.

**Estimate revisions are mixed.** Current-quarter consensus fell 9.9% in 30 days and 9.3% in 90; next quarter -5.4% and -4.0%. Full-year 2026 is flat (+0.1%/+0.2%) and FY2027 is down 2.2%/2.5%. The pattern is a shift of expected earnings into Q4 rather than a change in the annual total. This is a negative-to-neutral revision profile with back-end concentration.

**Guidance is heavily back-loaded.** H1 delivered 7.6 GW, 42-45% of the guided full-year volume. Q3 guidance is 3.9-4.5 GW with adjusted EBITDA of $625-775M, implying Q4 of roughly 4.9-6.1 GW and $665-915M. Finished-goods inventory rose 66% in H1, from $439.2M to $728.8M, and Q2 production of 4.3 GW exceeded sales of 3.7 GW. Read charitably, that is a deliberate pre-build consistent with the ramp. Read unfavorably, and combined with 0.37x book-to-bill, it is inventory accumulating into a market where the company itself warns that reselling modules from terminated contracts "may be constrained."

**Structural growth vectors** remain: 21.6 GW of backlog carries technology-improvement price adjusters worth up to +$0.5B, mostly recognizable in 2027-2028 (contingent, not contracted); CuRe fleet replication; the perovskite tandem pilot; the South Carolina facility designed to receive ~3.5 GW/year of semi-finished modules from Asia for U.S. completion, giving management a real option on trade policy. Whether South Carolina output earns the full ~$0.17/W credit is not disclosed and is economically decisive.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is being widened by trade policy the company helped construct, while its own commercial momentum weakens. Both are true simultaneously.

**The protective wall around the U.S. market is now unusually dense.** AD/CVD orders issued June 9, 2025 on Cambodia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam at rates from de minimis to over 3,400%. A second case on India, Indonesia and Laos with preliminary CVD rates of 125.87%, up to 143.30% and 80.67%, and preliminary AD rates of 123.04%, 35.17% and 33.57%. Standing Section 301 tariffs of 50% on Chinese cells and 25% on modules, plus new forced-labor Section 301 tariffs from July 24, 2026 (10% Malaysia/India/EU, 12.5% China/Vietnam). Section 232 on polysilicon takes effect December 4, 2026. First Solar is a member of the trade committee that filed the April 2024 petitions.

**The company is also litigating rivals' technology.** It has asserted TOPCon patents against JinkoSolar, Canadian Solar and Trina in Delaware (all stayed) and is prosecuting USITC investigation 337-TA-1494 against affiliates of Axitec, Canadian Solar, JA Solar, JinkoSolar, Mundra, Philadelphia Solar, Hanwha Qcells, Runergy, Trina and VSUN, seeking a general exclusion order. Tesla, BYD America and IC Star intervened as respondents. Hearing February 16, 2027; preliminary ruling ~May 2027; final ~September 2027. The PTAB declined all three IPR petitions, but the USPTO granted two ex parte reexaminations of the '074 patent in February 2026. General exclusion orders are hard to obtain and a single adverse validity ruling collapses the campaign; this deserves option value, not a base-case line.

**Against that, the commercial trajectory is softer.** Europe is described in the filing as "captured by Chinese solar modules, where pricing is at levels near or below manufacturing costs" — effectively unaddressable. India is "effectively closed to Southeast Asian products," and MNRE's November 2025 draft proposal to raise ALMM minimum module-efficiency thresholds from 2027 "would negatively impact our ability to sell modules within the Indian market," a structurally adverse form of regulation for thin film, whose nameplate efficiency trails modern TOPCon even where energy yield is competitive. Roughly 1.8 GW of finished international capacity is furloughed at ~$30M per quarter, reconciling with guided full-year underutilization of $115-135M. Series 6 production has been reduced at international sites.

Trajectory assessment: policy-driven moat expansion, commercially flat-to-weaker, with the resolution depending on whether H2 bookings recover after the tariff overhang clears. 6.6.

## Risk Assessment

**Policy is the business model, and it moves in both directions.** Section 45X phases down from 2030 and ends in 2032, and H.R.1 has already restricted eligibility for FEOC-linked material assistance. The same law accelerated termination of the demand-side ITC and PTC for solar, which reduces First Solar's customers' project economics. Tariff policy flipped three times in six months: IEEPA reciprocal tariffs (April 2025), struck down by the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026, replaced same day by a Section 122 10% global tariff, terminated July 24, 2026, replaced by Section 301 forced-labor tariffs. The 10-Q states plainly that "our operating results could be adversely impacted by revocation of the IEEPA tariffs on China" — the company benefits from its competitors being taxed, which is an unusual dependency to underwrite.

**Litigation, both directions.** First Solar sued BP Solar Holding and Lightsource Renewable Energy Trading on September 30, 2025, terminating master supply agreements and triggering $384.6M of contractual termination payments, of which $61.0M was recognized in revenue from advance payments; it seeks the remaining $323.6M. Defendants counterclaimed for $175M plus return of $15M of credit support based on module performance, with a motion to dismiss argued July 29, 2026. Neither side is accrued. Separately, a securities class action was filed June 23, 2026 (E.D.N.Y.) covering purchasers from February 26, 2025 to February 24, 2026, alleging the company understated the extent to which its responses to U.S. tariff policy would harm 2026 performance, naming CEO Widmar and CFO Bradley; a derivative suit followed July 28, 2026. The class period ending on the FY2025 10-K/initial-guidance date is diagnostic of the theory. No accrual. The Trabant tracker-patent indemnification (First Solar as indemnitor) has no loss estimate; the Monterey County matter carries a $21.8M accrued payable fully offset by an insurance receivable.

**International fleet impairment is explicitly flagged.** The PP&E note warns that reduced or ceased international operations "could result in a decrease in the value, and possible impairment" that "could be material" and "have a significant adverse effect on our results of operations." Total net PP&E is $5,643.5M with the international share undisclosed.

**Single-source input.** Tellurium has no substitute in the CdTe process. China tightened export controls in February 2025 and expanded rare-earth controls in October 2025 (imposition delayed one year in November). No quantified exposure is disclosed. Low probability, high severity.

**Execution and estimates.** The Q4 requirement of ~4.9-6.1 GW would be a record; finished goods rose 66% in H1; the $88.6M tariff-refund benefit is an estimate that could reverse; the Series 7 defect range is $40-65M with $47M accrued (narrowed from $35-75M/$50M, so settlement experience is landing within expectations); the Malaysian tax holiday expires in 2027 against a 5.5% H1 effective tax rate.

**Management and alignment.** Officer selling was broad and one-directional between May and August 2026 with no open-market purchases: CEO Widmar reduced holdings ~22% (105,074 → 81,861) at $230-276 across five dates in May; Chief Product Officer Buehler sold 3,000 at $302 on June 2, leaving 4,406; CTO Gloeckler and Chief Supply Chain Officer Koralewski sold on August 3-4 at $218-248. Director and co-founder Ahearn gifted 44,584 shares on August 4 (a gift, not a sale). Mitigating: four officers adopted new 10b5-1 plans in Q2, sales cluster around May RSU vestings, and absolute holdings are small relative to compensation. Widmar does not appear in the Q2 adoption table, so his May sales ran under an earlier plan or discretionarily; the filings do not permit a determination and I infer no motive. On capital allocation the record is better: debt fully repaid, revolver upgraded from secured to unsecured with an IG pathway, dilution of 0.2%, no buyback authorization disclosed. The compensation structure is the weaker point — the company-selected metric is adjusted operating margin (37.8% in FY2025), which is overwhelmingly a function of 45X, though the 2026-2028 grants add incremental ASP and adjusted gross margin, which sharpens the pricing linkage. The 2023 PSU grants certified at approximately target in February 2026, down from approximately maximum for the 2022 grants.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($330, +35.2%)

Section 232 resets the U.S. module price floor at $0.38/W for imported product and effectively $0.40-0.48/W for domestically assembled c-Si, roughly 26-59% above First Solar's $0.3016/W contracted backlog, giving it wide headroom to reprice new bookings while remaining the low-cost option. The 0.37x H1 book-to-bill was customers waiting out the ruling, and H2 bookings recover sharply, supported by stable backlog ASP through three consecutive disclosures, which is inconsistent with price capitulation. The 45.1 GW backlog is 91% domestic-content-linked, exactly where the company is advantaged. The H2 ramp delivers on the pre-built $728.8M of finished goods; 45X monetization resumes and year-end net cash lands at the top of the $1.7-2.3B guide. CuRe replication and the 21.6 GW of technology-improvement adjusters (up to +$0.5B, mostly 2027-2028) lift realized ASP further. The TOPCon case produces a general exclusion order in September 2027, adding either exclusion or royalty economics. On $23-24 of FY2027 EPS at 14x, the stock reaches the high analyst target with a strong balance sheet and 12% short interest as accelerants.

### Bear Case ($165, -32.4%)

The 45X credit is 85-88% of guided gross profit and the enterprise generates roughly negative $250-270M of operating income without it; the credit phases down from 2030 and Congress has already amended eligibility once. Underlying residual margin compressed from ~10.0% to 6.7% year over year, so the non-subsidy business is getting worse while headline margin improves on credit volume and an $88.6M tariff-refund estimate. Bookings at 0.37x for two more quarters converts a 2.6-year backlog into a genuine runway problem, and H.R.1's accelerated ITC/PTC termination plus $4-5/MWh higher PPA requirements from Section 232 shrink the developer pipeline that buys the product. The Q4 requirement of 4.9-6.1 GW is not met, and finished goods become stranded inventory the company has warned it may be constrained from reselling. An international-fleet impairment lands against $5.64B of net PP&E. The securities class action and derivative suit proceed past the August 24 lead-plaintiff deadline with no accrual. Operating cash flow stays negative as grants receivable extends further, and $1.7B of net cash gets deployed into perovskite M&A at unknown returns. On $16-17 of FY2027 EPS at 9-10x, the stock returns to the analyst low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait for either a lower entry or confirmed bookings recovery. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD. The Section 232 action is a materially new and favorable development, but the stock has already advanced 22.5% from $199.24 on July 29 and 18.6% from the level at the prior review, which consumed the near-term asymmetry. The score is essentially flat at 5.67 versus 5.8 because the competitive-position improvement is offset by the newly filed securities and derivative litigation, a third consecutive backlog decline, and cash conversion that has deteriorated further.

- **Base case $270 (+10.6%)** — 11.9x FY2027 consensus $22.74, equivalently 15.5x FY2026 $17.44; between the $256 analyst mean and $265 median.
- **Bull case $330 (+35.2%)** — 14x a FY2027 estimate revised toward $23-24.
- **Bear case $165 (-32.4%)** — 9-10x FY2027 EPS of $16-17.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October 2026), the December 4, 2026 Section 232 effective date, and FY2027 guidance in late February 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 book-to-bill above 1.0x, or H2 bookings above 8 GW, with backlog ASP at or above $0.31/W — this converts the bookings pause from demand destruction to timing and validates the repricing thesis.
2. Q3 operating cash flow positive with disclosed resumption of 45X monetization or receipt of direct Treasury refunds sufficient to bring the noncurrent grants receivable below $600M.
3. Federal Register text confirming the $0.38/W module minimum import price does **not** reach non-polysilicon thin film, preserving First Solar's Asian fleet cost advantage while flooring competitors.
4. A decline toward $200-210 with the above fundamentals intact, which would restore roughly 30% to base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 book-to-bill below 0.6x with backlog falling below 42 GW, or any FY2026 guidance reduction — the H2 ramp requires a record Q4 and there is little slack.
2. Finished-goods inventory above $850M with Q3 volume below the guided 3.9 GW low end, which would signal the pre-build is unsold rather than pre-sold.

## Conclusion

First Solar presents a genuine two-sided policy structure rather than a simple valuation call. On one side, 14.0x FY2026 and 10.7x FY2027 consensus earnings, $1.69B of net cash, $37.6M of total debt, 0.2% annual dilution, and a newly announced tariff regime that prices competing modules 26-59% above the company's contracted ASP and sits entirely outside reaffirmed guidance. On the other, a disclosure in the company's own guidance footnote that $2.10-2.19B of Section 45X credits constitute 85-88% of guided FY2026 gross profit, an ex-credit operating loss of roughly $250-270M, residual margin compression from ~10.0% to 6.7%, negative $359.8M of H1 operating cash flow with credit monetization apparently paused, a 0.37x book-to-bill, and a Q4 requirement that would be the largest quarter in company history.

The correct posture is to own what you own and wait for the Q3 print to resolve which reading of the bookings pause is right. Base-case upside of roughly 11% against 32% bear-case downside, at 60.8% implied volatility with options positioning already flipped bullish, does not justify new capital at $244.14. It would at $200-210, or on evidence that bookings recovered and 45X started converting to cash again.
