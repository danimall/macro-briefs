## Executive Summary

SLM Corporation is a monoline private education lender with a clean structural setup and a loaded near-term event. The structural piece: H.R.1 eliminated Grad PLUS federal loans and capped Parent PLUS at $20,000 annually / $65,000 lifetime effective July 1, 2026, with new per-year and lifetime caps across all federal programs. SLM already originates roughly half to two-thirds of the entire US private student loan market ($7.4B of CY2025 originations against a $11.5B College Board/Enterval or $14B company-estimated academic-year market), fields six graduate-specific products plus bar-study and residency loans, and maintains certification relationships with more than 2,100 institutions through what the 10-K describes as the largest campus relationship team in the industry. If federal graduate and parent borrowing capacity is legislatively withdrawn, SLM is the most obvious recipient of the displaced volume.

The near-term event is that none of this is yet visible in the numbers, and 2026 is a guided down year. FY2026 guidance of $3.10-$3.20 GAAP diluted EPS compares to $3.46 actual in FY2025. Decomposed, that is a ~17% decline in net income to common (from $728.9M to approximately $605M at the midpoint on an estimated ~192M average diluted shares) partially offset by a ~9% reduction in share count, so the reported EPS decline of 8.9% understates the deterioration in earnings power by roughly half. Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $0.29 missed consensus of $0.44 by 34%, revenue fell 11.7% year over year to $332.8M, net interest margin compressed 56bp to 4.75%, the efficiency ratio deteriorated to 48.6% from 41.4%, and held-for-investment loans shrank 7.7% to $19.53B. First-half originations of $3.622B were only 4.8% above 1H 2025, which means the reaffirmed 12-14% full-year guide requires 2H originations of $4.67-$4.82B against $3.94B, an 18-22% year-over-year acceleration concentrated in the Q3 peak disbursement season.

At $27.055 the shares trade at 8.6x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, 8.05x next-year consensus of $3.36, 2.29x GAAP book value of $11.83, and approximately 1.16x an after-tax fair-value-adjusted book of ~$23.41 per share. That is cheap against a peer median P/E of 12.0 and a ~30% return on common equity, and it is not cheap against a business whose quarterly results are now dominated by discretionary loan-sale timing with an undisclosed counterparty. The stock has risen 52% from the $17.77 low and sits roughly 23% above the company's own 1H 2026 repurchase average of ~$21.95. Probability-weighted, we see mid-single-digit total return to a $29 base case, against a $20.65 bear case if the Q3 acceleration fails. That is a HOLD, and the honest classification is uncertainty rather than conviction: the late-October Q3 report resolves essentially the entire thesis.

## Company Overview

Sallie Mae Bank is a Utah industrial bank chartered in 2005, regulated by the FDIC, the Utah Department of Financial Institutions, and the CFPB. SLM is deliberately **not** a bank holding company under the BHC Act, is not Federal Reserve-supervised, and is not subject to Dodd-Frank stress testing. That structure matters directly for the equity story: capital return is constrained by Bank capital ratios and FDIC/UDFI supervision rather than by a CCAR-style regime, which is why SLM has been able to shrink diluted weighted-average shares 19.4% since FY2022 (261.5M → 210.9M) and 9.9% in the trailing twelve months alone (208.5M → 187.95M).

The core product is the Smart Option Student Loan: school-certified, cosigned, and oriented toward in-school payments. FY2025 origination credit metrics were an average FICO of 755 (higher of borrower or cosigner) and 92.8% cosigned, with more than half of originations electing an interest-only or fixed in-school payment. Distribution is B2B2C; the campus relationship team drives school channel presence, and loans are disbursed directly to institutions after need certification. Headcount is only ~1,788, all US-based, against $29.7B of Bank assets.

The second, newer business line is the private credit strategic partnership announced in 2025: SLM originates, sells the loans to a partner, retains servicing and the customer relationship, and earns ongoing servicing and program management fees. The 10-K describes this as "capital-light, fee-based revenue" and states that the Bank or its affiliates retain servicing of all originated loans regardless of whether they are held, sold, or securitized. The partner is not named in the filing. April 2026 ABS transactions (2026-B, 2026-C) show the machinery running end-to-end, with a "Third-Party Seller" contributing loans previously purchased from the Bank into the trusts alongside loans the Bank transferred directly.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year earnings and the share-count engine.** Pretax income grew from $630.7M (FY2022) to $992.6M (FY2025); net income from $469.0M to $744.8M. FY2025 effective tax rate was 24.95%. GAAP diluted EPS of $3.46 reconciles: ($744.8M − ~$15.9M preferred dividends) / 210.9M shares. For context, the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows FY2021 net income of $1,160.5M, so FY2025's record EPS was achieved on net income still well below the 2021 (reserve-release-inflated) peak.

**The 2026 guide, decomposed.** This is the single most decision-relevant calculation in the file:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| FY2025 net income to common | $728.9M |
| FY2026E net income to common (at $3.15 × ~192M shares) | ~$605M |
| Implied decline in net income to common | −17.0% |
| Implied EPS change ($3.46 → $3.15) | −8.9% |
| Implied average share-count change (210.9M → ~192M) | −9.0% |
| Reconciliation: (1 − 0.170) / (1 − 0.090) | 0.912 → −8.8% ✓ |

The ~192M average share figure is our estimate interpolated from 1H actuals and the $242M of remaining authorization, not a disclosed number. The reconciliation is tight enough that the conclusion holds: reading the EPS line alone understates the earnings-power deterioration by about half.

**Margin and revenue trajectory.** Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025: NIM 4.75% vs 5.31%; yield on interest-earning assets 8.59% vs 9.25%; private education loan yield 10.25% vs 10.62%; average cash ~$5.05B vs ~$4.18B; HFI loans net $19.53B vs $21.16B. Asset yields fell 66bp while NIM fell 56bp, implying net funding costs fell only ~10bp. Two mechanisms are both plausible from the disclosure and cannot be cleanly separated: a larger low-yielding cash balance and smaller loan book diluting blended yield (mix), and variable-rate assets repricing down faster than competitively priced online deposits and CDs (repricing asymmetry). SLM's margin compresses as short rates fall. The 10-Q also discloses economic value of equity sensitivity of −17.6% for a +300bp shock (improved from −24.5%), so a sharp rate increase also hurts. The book is exposed at both ends.

**Expenses.** Q2 non-interest expense was $195M versus $167M (+17%), with professional fees $56.4M vs $41.8M (+35%) and compensation $100.3M vs $84.9M (+18%). FY2026 guidance of $750-$780M against roughly $659M in FY2025 implies 14-18% growth. Management frames this as building origination capacity for the H.R.1 opportunity. That framing is plausible and unverifiable until the volume arrives; what is verifiable today is that the efficiency ratio moved from 41.4% to 48.6% while revenue declined.

**Credit.** Delinquency (30+ DPD, % of loans in repayment) was 3.72% at 6/30/2026 versus 3.51% a year prior, with management noting part of the increase reflects mix shift from selling loans. Q2 net charge-offs were ~$113M at a 2.95% rate versus 2.36%. Recoveries fell to $9.7M from $12.6M. Allowance stood at 5.89% of exposure, roughly $1.2B against $365-385M of guided annual charge-offs, or approximately 3.1-3.4x coverage (approximate because the disclosed ratio is against exposure including unfunded commitments). Modified-loan payment defaults improved to $61.8M in 1H26 from $83.3M in 1H25.

Management attributes the charge-off increase to "misaligned third-party debt resolution practices" affecting a small, high-ability-to-pay borrower segment that moved straight from delinquency to default, and paused certain third-party recovery activity. NCO guidance was narrowed rather than raised, which is a mild credibility point. But a high-ability-to-pay cohort defaulting without passing through normal resolution steps is also consistent with a willingness-to-pay deterioration that a vendor-process explanation would obscure. Either way, 2026 reported charge-off and recovery figures are distorted by the pause, making the underlying credit trend genuinely harder to read this year. Q3 and Q4 recovery dollars are the test.

**Balance sheet and capital return.** Total equity was $2,474.7M at 6/30/2026, including $251.1M of Series B floating-rate preferred, so common equity is $2,223.6M and book value per share $11.83 (TBV $11.52 after ~$58.5M of goodwill and intangibles). Bank CET1 was 11.1% at 12/31/2025; equity/assets 8.24%. Deposits fell from $21.5B (12/31/2025) to $19.9B (6/30/2026), a 7.4% decline; brokered deposits were $8.8B, or 42% of deposits, at year-end. ABS outstanding of $4.9B equals 24% of the HFI portfolio, but only $0.5B was raised in term ABS during 2025 — securitization has been de-emphasized in favor of whole-loan sales.

The Bank upstreamed $700M to the parent in 2025 and $400M in 1H 2026 (vs $194M in 1H 2025). A $500M program authorized 1/22/2026 through 2/4/2028 funded ~$293M of 1H repurchases at roughly $21.95 average, including a $200M accelerated share repurchase completed in June (~9.3M shares); ~$242M remains. The quarterly dividend is $0.13 ($0.52 annualized), a 1.92% yield and roughly a 17% payout on guided EPS.

The chain is disclosed rather than inferred: loan sales release Bank capital, the Bank dividends to the parent, and the parent repurchases stock. The 10-K states plainly that SLM "may not be able to sell loans at prices, in volumes, or on a schedule, that will provide us with sufficient funds to effect share repurchases." Since the buyback is currently absorbing half the earnings decline, that dependency is load-bearing.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow of −$398.6M (and roughly −$211M in 1H 2026) will trip generic screens. Two structural explanations apply: capitalized in-school interest accrues as income but not cash, appearing as a large negative working-capital line; and loans originated with intent to sell are held-for-sale, so origination is an operating outflow with sale proceeds an operating inflow, producing large swings when originations and sales straddle period ends. Altman Z-Score, debt/equity, and cash-flow Piotroski components carry no meaningful interpretation for a depository lender. The bank-appropriate measures — CET1 11.1%, equity/assets 8.24%, ~3x allowance coverage, 42% brokered deposits — read adequate-to-solid rather than distressed, but not fortress.

## Growth Analysis

**The organic backdrop has been weak.** The 10-K reports private education loan originations of ~$11.5B in AY 2024-2025, flat versus the prior year, against total higher-education spending of ~$536B. Full-time-equivalent enrollment has been relatively flat since 2020/2021 and is expected to remain so. Published tuition grew at 2.1% (public four-year) and 3.4% (private nonprofit four-year) CAGRs from AY 2021-22 through AY 2025-26. The filing states that increases in total spending over AY 2020-2025 were "absorbed primarily through increased family contributions" — households self-funded rather than borrowed privately. Flat enrollment, low-single-digit tuition inflation, and share already at or above 50% is not a growth engine.

**H.R.1 is the discontinuity.** Effective July 1, 2026 with limited grandfathering: elimination of Grad PLUS; Parent PLUS capped at $20,000/year and $65,000 lifetime per student; new graduate limits of $20,500/year ($100,000 lifetime) for non-professional and $50,000/year ($200,000 lifetime) for professional degrees; a universal lifetime borrowing cap; the Repayment Assistance Plan replacing SAVE/PAYE/ICR by July 1, 2028. The 10-K's own expectation is that these changes create "substantial funding gaps" that shift demand toward private education loans, "particularly for students in high-cost educational programs."

The critical unresolved item is size. Neither the 10-K nor available sources quantify the annual Grad PLUS dollar volume being eliminated or the Parent PLUS volume being capped. Without that, the bull case is directionally sound but unsized. We are not going to manufacture a TAM estimate; the appropriate treatment is to say the tailwind is real, legislatively mandated, and of unknown magnitude, and to weight the first observable data point heavily.

**That first data point is Q3 2026, and the guide requires a lot from it.** 1H originations of $3.622B were +4.8%. The reaffirmed 12-14% full-year guide implies $8.29-$8.44B, requiring 2H originations of $4.67-$4.82B against 2H 2025's $3.94B, an 18.4% to 22.2% acceleration. Simultaneously, implied 2H EPS of $1.27-$1.37 (full-year $3.10-$3.20 less 1H's $1.83) is 21-27% below 2H 2025's ~$1.74. So volume is guided to accelerate sharply while back-half earnings decline sharply; the wedge is expenses, NIM, and loan-sale timing. Two mitigating points: Q3 is always the peak disbursement quarter with a Q1 mini-peak, so back-half loading is partly seasonal; and guidance was reaffirmed *after* the Q2 miss, which carries some credibility weight. Management characterized early peak-season indicators as "tracking at the high end of expectations or better" (a third-party summary of call commentary, not an independently verified figure).

**Estimate revisions do not confirm an inflection.** Current-quarter consensus rose 13.0% over 90 days and next-quarter 16.8%, which reflects the back-half loading. But current-year consensus is essentially flat at $3.13 (−0.6% over 90 days) and next-year $3.36 (−0.6%). This is a revision pattern of intra-year reallocation, not the >30% upward velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection. Analyst sentiment is degrading at the margin: strong-buy ratings fell from 2 to 1 and holds rose from 3 to 4 over the past month.

Implied growth from FY2026 guidance midpoint to FY2027 consensus is 6.7% ($3.15 → $3.36), and a meaningful portion of that will again come from share count rather than earnings.

The 33% discount to peer median P/E is the headline bull argument, and it is genuinely supported by the ROE differential — Enova at 15.4x, Prog Holdings at 12.0x, and Ezcorp at 16.6x are not earning 30% on equity. But peer dispersion here is wide (6.3x to 16.6x) and the group is heterogeneous — subprime consumer lending, debt purchasing, pawn, and lease-to-own are not comparable business models. The relative-value case is suggestive rather than decisive.

Two other anchors are more useful. First, the fair-value cross-check: the Q2 10-Q shows an excess of net asset fair value over carrying value of approximately $2,901M pre-tax. Adding that to common equity gives $5,124.6M / 187.95M = $27.27 per share — within a fraction of the current price. Tax-effecting at ~25% gives $23.41, so the stock trades at ~1.16x after-tax economic book. For a business earning ~30% on equity, 1.16x economic book is defensible and modestly attractive; on GAAP book at 2.29x it is not obviously cheap. The gap between the two framings is largely the CECL allowance plus the below-market carrying value of a high-coupon loan book.

Second, and more constraining, the multiple applies to earnings whose composition has shifted toward transaction-dependent gain-on-sale income. Q1 2026 loan sales of ~$3.33B (including a ~$2.04B seasoned sale in March) produced ~$146M of the $161.3M in 1H gains and a $1.54 EPS quarter; Q2 sales of $420M produced $14.9M of gains and $0.29. That single line explains most of the sequential collapse. Under CECL, a sale generates both a gain and an allowance release, so pretax income is substantially influenced by a volume-and-timing lever management controls. Over a full loan life the day-one reserve build and sale-date release net out, and the gain reflects real value realized above carrying value. But gain-on-sale earnings historically command a lower multiple than recurring spread income, and the counterparty generating those gains is unnamed with no disclosed committed capacity or contract term.

We are also mindful of the entry arithmetic. Insider tax-withholding prices of $19.19 and $19.84 in late February and early March 2026, and a 1H buyback average of ~$21.95, establish where the stock traded before the H.R.1 narrative was priced. At $27.055 the shares are up 52% from the $17.77 low and 23% above the company's own repurchase average. Prior appreciation is not a valuation input in itself, and the appreciation was accompanied by a legitimate change in the demand outlook. But the appreciation did consume the discount to economic book that existed in March, and it happened while current-year and next-year estimates went nowhere.

Analyst targets: low $23 (−15.0%), median $28 (+3.5%), mean $28.55 (+5.5%), high $35 (+29.4%). With current-year and next-year estimates flat-to-slightly-down and the stock trading essentially at consensus, these targets are neither a floor nor evidence of mispricing; the "targets lag reality" dynamic requires rising estimates, which are absent at the annual level.

**Valuation score of 5.5** reflects a modest discount on forward earnings offset by the transaction-dependence of those earnings, the guided decline, and the fact that the discount to economic book has largely closed.

## Competitive Landscape

Position level is strong; trajectory is the question. SLM's $7.4B of CY2025 originations against a $11.5B (College Board/Enterval) or $14B (company estimate) academic-year market implies roughly half to two-thirds share — the methodology and period mismatch makes precision impossible, but dominance is clear. The moat components are real and hard to replicate quickly: school certification relationships at 2,100+ institutions, a cosigner-based underwriting model producing 755 average FICO and 92.8% cosigned, and a pre-existing graduate product suite (Law, MBA, Medical, Dental, Health Professions, general Graduate, plus Bar Study and Residency/Relocation) that maps directly onto the cohort H.R.1 displaces.

Three trajectory headwinds are stated in the filings rather than inferred. First, the 10-K's competitive risk factor is unusually direct: "we expect that our competition will increase as various lending institutions, FinTechs, and other competitors enter or re-enter the Private Education Loan market with the intent of disrupting and/or capturing newly created or existing market share." When you already hold half or more of a market, a market-expansion event with free entry is a mixed outcome — SLM captures incremental volume, but likely at lower incremental spread. Named competitors include Citizens Financial Group, PNC, SoFi, and College Ave.

Second, refinance attrition is accelerating: third-party consolidations away from SLM rose ~60% year over year to $332M in Q2 2026, roughly 3.8% of the P&I repayment portfolio annualized. This strips out seasoned, high-coupon, best-performing loans — the most valuable cohort — and accelerates as rates ease.

Third, the deregulatory shift cuts both ways. H.R.1 dramatically reduced CFPB funding, and as of November 20, 2025 the administration began transferring CFPB enforcement authority to the DOJ, with the CFPB signaling intent to deemphasize student loans. Near term this lowers SLM's enforcement risk. But the 10-K itself warns that the shift may "deliver significant competitive advantages to financial services that are structured differently."

One mix note worth monitoring: approximately 16%, or $1.25B, of FY2025 originations went to students at for-profit institutions. SLM states it expects comparable repayment performance and that remaining relationships concentrate in career-training and healthcare. If originations accelerate 20% in a single half-year, channel discipline becomes a live question.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** At 12/31/2025, approximately 71% of total assets — and 83% excluding cash — were private education loans. There is no meaningful diversification. The shift in *revenue* mix toward fees does not diversify the underlying *risk*: the fee stream derives from the same single asset class, SLM retains servicing and a 5% vertical risk-retention interest in off-balance-sheet securitizations, and the risk factors describe dependence on a limited number of partners.

**Strategic partnership, untested and unnamed.** The FY2025 10-K added a distinct risk factor: "Our origination expansion initiative and strategic partnership funding model are new and untested." It warns of dependence on "a limited number of strategic partners, private credit sources, and external demand" with attendant "liquidity and concentration risks." This is the current earnings architecture. The counterparty identity, committed purchase capacity, and contract term are not disclosed. An undisclosed private credit buyer of consumer paper is a dependency that deserves explicit weight.

**Funding.** All-online deposit gathering with 42% brokered at year-end. Brokered deposits are price-sensitive and require the institution to remain well capitalized to accept, renew, or roll them without FDIC clearance — a step-function dependency on Bank capital ratios. Deposits already fell 7.4% in six months.

**Zappia v. SLM Corporation.** A putative securities class action filed December 19, 2025 alleging Exchange Act §§10(b) and 20(a) violations, with an amended complaint filed June 8, 2026. The class period, named defendants, specific alleged misstatements, and any damages exposure are unknown. Whether the allegations relate to credit-administration and charge-off disclosures, the strategic partnership, or something else is unresolved. This is an unquantified overhang.

**Guidance binary.** The Q3 print must deliver an 18-22% origination acceleration and support $1.27-$1.37 of 2H EPS. A Q3 shortfall leaves no time to recover within the year and would force a guidance cut into a stock that has already rallied 52% off its low.

**Consumer backdrop.** SLM is a monoline consumer lender, and the filing notes that recent tuition increases were absorbed through increased family contributions rather than borrowing. Family balance-sheet stress feeds directly into cosigner capacity and repayment performance.

**Unverified disclosures.** Item 1.01 material definitive agreements filed in March and May 2026 (the March filing coincident with the ~$2.04B seasoned sale) and an August 5, 2026 Regulation FD 8-K could not be retrieved. Counterparties and terms are unverified.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is the most informative element. Near-term ATM IV is 33.7% against 40.5% trailing realized volatility — cheap, in the 5th percentile of the current chain — with a call-heavy skew (−5.4% at the 1-month tenor) and a put/call open-interest ratio of just 0.06. That is complacent, directionally bullish near-term positioning, consistent with the H.R.1 narrative and with the price rising 14.8% since the late-June snapshot while ATM IV fell 3.9pp.

The 6-month tenor (November 20 expiry) tells a different story: 43.6% IV with a −15.7% skew reading, versus 33.2% at the September tenor. The November expiry is the first that captures the late-October Q3 report. The market is pricing the Q3 print as the binary and paying up for longer-dated protection while leaving near-term vol cheap: cheap near-term optionality, expensive event risk beyond it.

Short interest of 11.7% of float with 6.0 days to cover, combined with the very call-tilted near-term open interest, means a Q3 beat has squeeze fuel. It also means a miss has an air pocket beneath it, since the current positioning offers no cushion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $36 (+33%)

H.R.1 delivers a step-function increase in private education loan demand beginning with the Q3 2026 peak season, and SLM — with the certification infrastructure, the graduate product suite, and half the market — captures the majority of it. Full-year originations land at or above the $8.44B high end, 2027 originations grow another 15%+, and the expense build guided at $750-$780M proves to be capacity investment rather than margin erosion. NIM stabilizes if the rate-cutting cycle pauses, which is a better environment for SLM's asset repricing than a series of cuts. Charge-offs normalize back toward 2.4% as the third-party recovery process is fixed and recovery dollars return. FY2027 EPS reaches $3.90-$4.00 on continued ~9% annual share shrinkage, and the market pays 9-10x for a 30% ROE originate-service-and-distribute platform: $36-$40. The 11.7% short base amplifies the move.

### Bear Case — $20.65 (−24%)

The Q3 peak season delivers high-single-digit rather than 20% origination growth — competitors who entered for the same displaced volume compete on price, and families continue self-funding as they did through AY 2020-2025. Guidance is cut to $2.90-$3.00. NIM continues compressing as loan yields reprice down; consolidation attrition accelerates past $350M per quarter, stripping the highest-coupon seasoned loans. The partner's appetite for private student loan paper softens, forcing volume back onto the balance sheet at exactly the moment volumes spike — a capital and funding squeeze that also cuts off the loan-sale cash that funds the buyback, removing the mechanism absorbing half the earnings decline. Charge-offs prove to reflect willingness-to-pay deterioration rather than a vendor process defect, and the 5.89% allowance requires building. The Zappia complaint survives a motion to dismiss with a defined class period. Multiple compresses to 7x $2.95 = $20.65, roughly the February 2026 level.

### Base Case — $29 (+7.2%)

Q3 delivers meaningful acceleration but below the top of the guide; FY2026 EPS lands at $3.10-$3.15. FY2027 reaches consensus $3.36 with roughly half the growth coming from share count. NIM stabilizes in the 4.5-4.7% range; charge-offs land inside the $365-385M guide. The market continues paying 8.5-9x for earnings whose composition is transaction-dependent: $28.50-$30.25. Including the 1.9% dividend, total return of roughly 9%.

Probability-weighted at 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull: (0.30 × −24%) + (0.45 × +7.2%) + (0.25 × +33%) = **+4.3% price return, ~+6.2% including the dividend.** That is approximately a fair risk-adjusted return, with the distribution wide and the resolution date known.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure through the Q3 report; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $27.055 with the binary two and a half months out and the stock trading essentially at consensus median.

**Score trajectory context.** Prior reports scored 5.3 (Mar) → 7.2 (May) → 6.2 (Jun), with recommendations HOLD → BUY → HOLD. The May BUY was made near $21-22, close to the company's own repurchase average, when the discount to after-tax economic book was material. Since the June 24 snapshot at $23.76 the stock has risen 13.9% while Q2 missed by 34%, revenue fell 11.7%, NIM compressed 56bp, the efficiency ratio deteriorated 7.2pp, and the second-half acceleration requirement grew more concentrated. The price is higher and the operating data is worse. The modest score reduction to 5.68 reflects both.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Q3 2026 originations of $2.9B+** (implying the full-year guide is achievable) with FY2026 EPS guidance reaffirmed at or above $3.15 and FY2027 guidance introduced above $3.60.
2. **Disclosure of the strategic partner's identity, committed purchase capacity, and contract term** — this converts the largest unquantified dependency in the model into a sizeable, durable fee annuity and would support a higher multiple on the fee stream.
3. **Price in the low $22s or below** on unchanged fundamentals, restoring the ~1.0x after-tax economic book entry that existed in 1H 2026.
4. Q3/Q4 recovery dollars returning toward the $12-13M quarterly level, confirming the vendor-process explanation for the charge-off increase.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q3 originations below $2.5B** or any FY2026 EPS guidance reduction — this would invalidate the entire back-half thesis with no time to recover in-year.
2. **NCO rate above 3.25%** in Q3 or a full-year NCO guidance increase above $385M, indicating the credit issue is borrower-driven rather than process-driven.
3. Deposits declining below $18.5B or Bank CET1 falling below 10.5%, which would jeopardize brokered-deposit rollover capacity and, by extension, the Bank-to-parent dividend that funds the buyback.
4. A loan-sale gap quarter (sales below ~$500M in two consecutive quarters) combined with buyback suspension, confirming the funding-chain risk the 10-K flags.
5. Zappia surviving a motion to dismiss with a class period covering the FY2025 gain-on-sale disclosures.

The probability-weighted return of roughly 6% including the dividend, against a −24% bear case and a resolution date in late October, is approximately fair compensation for the risk.
