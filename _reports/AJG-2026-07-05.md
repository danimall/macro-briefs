**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arthur J. Gallagher is the world's third-largest insurance broker, a capital-light intermediary that earns commissions and fees without assuming underwriting risk. The business model is durable: highly recurring revenue, ~1% single-client concentration, and a century-old sales culture that has compounded through both organic growth and a serial M&A program. The reported financials look robust: FY2025 revenue of $13.94B (+21% YoY), 24 consecutive quarters of double-digit adjusted EBITDAC growth, and adjusted EPS of $10.69. The headline numbers understate two problems the market is now pricing.

First, the reported growth is increasingly M&A-driven while organic growth is decelerating on a clear trajectory: brokerage organic fell from 7% (2024) to 6% (2025) to 4% base commissions/fees in Q1 2026. This tracks the softening commercial P&C rate cycle, where CIAB rate increases fell from +4.2% in Q1 2025 to +1.6% in Q3. Because Gallagher's commissions are a percentage of premiums it does not control, decelerating rates feed directly into slower organic revenue. Second, the company is absorbing the largest acquisition in its history: the $13.8B AssuredPartners deal closed August 2025, which added ~10,900 employees, pushed goodwill to $22.6B, and carries ~$575M of integration expense over three years. Execution on this deal is the single most important variable for the next two to three years.

The stock has de-rated from the ~$280 December 2024 offering price to a low of $190.75 before recovering to ~$252. At ~19x forward EPS (consensus $13.20 for the current year, $14.87 for next year), AJG trades at the broker peer median of ~19x but offers a thin 1.2% yield and no identifiable catalyst that would drive re-rating. Estimate revisions are broadly negative, the recent earnings track record is mixed (two misses in four quarters), and a 13-year IRS micro-captive investigation with a related criminal probe remains unresolved. This is a quality franchise at a full price during a period of slowing organic momentum and integration uncertainty. We maintain a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1927 and headquartered in Rolling Meadows, Illinois, Gallagher operates in roughly 130 countries with about 72,000 employees. The company runs three segments:

- **Brokerage (~87% of revenue)**: retail P&C brokerage (~75% of segment), wholesale (~13%), and reinsurance via Gallagher Re (~12%). Revenue is commissions (a percentage of premium), fees, and supplemental/contingent revenues from carriers. Roughly 85% of commission/fee revenue is recognized at the policy effective date.
- **Risk Management (~13% of revenue)**: third-party claims administration through Gallagher Bassett, weighted toward workers' comp (~59%) and GL/auto liability (~34%). About 95% of this revenue comes from clients unaffiliated with the brokerage arm.
- **Corporate**: houses debt, acquisition costs, FX remeasurement, and legacy clean-energy (IRC §45) investments.

The competitive advantages are real: scale, deep niche/practice-group specialization (~74% of retail revenue), proprietary analytics (Gallagher Drive, SmartMarket, RISX-FACS), and a disciplined acquisition machine. Client diversification is a genuine strength: the largest single client is ~1% of revenue and the top 10 are ~3%. The company assumes no net underwriting risk, which insulates it from catastrophe losses that hit carriers directly.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Revenue moved from $8.55B (2022) to $10.07B (2023) to $11.55B (2024) to $13.94B (2025). Q1 2026 combined revenue before reimbursements was $4.72B, up 28% YoY. The growth mix is the key issue: reported growth is dominated by M&A, while organic growth is decelerating. Management attributes the deceleration to carrier competition in property and caution in casualty, and expects the trend to persist into 2026.

**Profitability and margins.** FY2025 net income was $1.49B; adjusted EPS was $10.69 (+6% YoY), while GAAP diluted EPS was $5.74 (–12%), weighed by amortization and a higher share count. FY2025 adjusted brokerage EBITDAC margin was 36.5% (+145 bps). The Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDAC margin fell ~3.6% YoY, but this is largely an artifact of the "interest income cliff": Q1 2025 included ~$143M of interest income earned on the $14B of pre-funded AssuredPartners cash. That tailwind (~$363M across 2025) vanished once the cash was deployed in August 2025, so 2026 comparisons face a mechanical headwind that is not an operational deterioration. Health care costs rose ~20% in 2025, a real cost pressure worth monitoring.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.93B (down from $2.58B, partly a $750M Willis Re earnout payment); FCF was $1.785B, a ~2.8% FCF yield on the current market cap. Total debt is ~$13.1B with D/E ~2.0x. Interest expense jumped to $639M in 2025 from $381M, reflecting the acquisition-related debt at ~5.25% on the $5B December 2024 notes. The Altman Z-Score of 0.79 flags "distress," but this is a false positive for a broker holding ~$33.9B of fiduciary client funds on-balance-sheet that inflate current liabilities. AJG is investment-grade and in compliance with all covenants; the Piotroski F-score and consistent profitability are the relevant reads.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.70/quarter for 2026 (~$719M annualized, comfortably covered by FCF). Buybacks resumed in Q1 2026: ~1.4M shares for ~$310M, the first repurchases in years, with $1.5B of authorization remaining. The company continues to fund M&A partly with equity (the December 2024 30.4M-share follow-on at $280 plus an active ATM program), so the share count has risen, diluting per-share metrics.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story bifurcates. Reported top-line growth remains strong on the strength of 31 acquisitions in 2025 adding ~$3.5B of annualized revenue, headlined by AssuredPartners and Woodruff Sawyer ($1.2B, April 2025). The organic engine is slowing in step with the P&C rate cycle. This matters because commissions follow premiums, which the broker does not set. The mechanism is direct: softening commercial P&C rates → slower premium growth → slower commission growth → decelerating organic revenue, now running at 4% on base commissions/fees versus 7% two years ago.

The bull thesis (articulated by Jefferies and Citi upgrades) rests on organic stabilizing near 5% while AssuredPartners synergies drive margin expansion. Both legs are conditional. If rates soften further, organic weakens; if integration slips, synergies do not materialize on schedule. The estimate revision data reinforces caution: current-year consensus EPS has been trimmed to $13.20 (from $13.22 90 days ago), the current quarter cut 2.5% over 90 days, and the overall trend is characterized as "estimates broadly falling." This is negative earnings momentum, not the profile that would warrant treating rising estimates as a buy signal. Next-year consensus ($14.87) is roughly flat over 90 days.

Growth durability is intact in the sense that the M&A machine continues to work and the recurring revenue base is sticky, but the marginal growth increment is now more expensive to buy (PE consolidators are inflating target multiples) and dilutive to fund (equity issuance). We score growth prospects moderately: the top-line optics are strong but the quality of that growth is deteriorating at the margin.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$252, AJG trades at ~19x forward EPS ($13.20 current year) and ~17x next-year EPS ($14.87). The trailing P/E of ~41x reflects the GAAP EPS of $5.74, which is depressed by amortization and share count; the forward figures on adjusted EPS are the correct lens.

AJG's ~19x sits right at the peer median while its organic growth is decelerating faster than the group narrative would suggest, and its yield (1.2%) is below the peer median. The company does not screen as cheap, and there is no independent catalyst — no cyclical earnings trough, no imminent margin unlock beyond the already-known synergy plan, no identified mispricing — that would argue the market has this wrong.

A low multiple relative to prior years is not a buy signal in isolation. Cheapness that reflects genuine fundamental caution (slowing organic, integration risk, negative revisions) is best treated as a warning rather than an opportunity. Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $266, median $261, low $225, high $388. The current price sits ~5% below the median target, which is not a compelling asymmetry given the negative revision trend and the fact that Citi upgraded to Buy while cutting its target to $250. We score valuation below neutral: the stock is priced for continued execution that carries real conditionality.

## Competitive Landscape

Gallagher sits behind Marsh McLennan and Aon globally and competes with Willis Towers Watson, Brown & Brown, and a growing field of PE-backed consolidators (Acrisure, HUB, USI). Its structural advantages — scale, niche specialization, data analytics, and disciplined M&A — support a stable-to-improving competitive position on the retail side. Client diversification is a durable moat feature.

Two headwinds temper the trajectory. First, the 10-K flags that PE money has invested heavily in the space, creating new competitors, strengthening existing ones, and making acquisition targets more expensive. Because Gallagher's growth is increasingly M&A-dependent, target inflation directly compresses the returns on its core growth engine. Second, the softening rate cycle makes organic share gains harder to distinguish from cyclical revenue pressure. Broker retention is also weakening at the margin as several states (CA, MN, ND, OK, WY) restrict non-competes, eroding a key retention lever. Insurtech remains largely complementary today but poses a longer-term disintermediation watch. On balance, competitive position is stable: the franchise is not eroding, but the environment is getting harder to grow into.

## Risk Assessment

**AssuredPartners integration (the defining risk).** The 10-K and Q1 press release flag heightened IT-integration and cybersecurity risk. Failure to hit revenue synergy and cost targets could trigger goodwill impairment against the $22.6B goodwill balance.

**Softening P&C rate cycle.** Decelerating commercial rates (property competition, casualty caution) pressure organic growth directly. This is cyclical and largely outside management's control.

**Leverage and rate exposure.** Post-acquisition debt of ~$13.1B with interest expense at $639M. A downgrade would raise financing costs and constrain the M&A engine.

**IRS micro-captive (§831(b)) investigation.** The IRS has investigated Gallagher's micro-captive advisory business as a potential tax-shelter promoter since 2013, with a related criminal investigation into §831(b) underwriting enterprises. Management states AJG is "not a target" and is cooperating. This is a long-running, unresolved overhang with tail risk that is difficult to size.

**Contingent/supplemental revenue volatility** and **broker-team poaching** round out the operational risks. Equity-funded M&A continues to dilute per-share metrics.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly benign. Near-term ATM IV is 27.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 29.4% — implied vol is fair-to-slightly-cheap (spread –2.0%), and IV sits at the 1st percentile within the current chain, indicating the market prices no imminent catalyst. The put/call skew of 3.4% indicates mildly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.25 is call-heavy. Since late May the ATM IV collapsed from 36.6% to 27.3% while the stock rallied 22%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.19 to 0.47 — a shift from defensive to more constructive positioning that coincided with the recent recovery and the analyst upgrades. The term structure is flat (near 27.3% ≈ far 28.2%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term event. Nothing here contradicts a HOLD; the market is not pricing an imminent binary in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300–330).** Organic growth stabilizes near 5% as casualty rate firmness offsets property softness; AssuredPartners integration proceeds on plan and synergies drive brokerage EBITDAC margin toward 38%+; the interest-income cliff annualizes out of comparisons and 2027 EPS reaccelerates. On ~$16 of 2027 adjusted EPS at ~20x, the stock reaches the low-$300s. The resumed buyback and family-continuity discipline support the case. Analyst high target sits at $388 on the most optimistic synergy assumptions.

**Bear Case ($195–210).** P&C rates soften further, dragging organic to the low single digits; AssuredPartners integration slips, synergies come late, and margin expansion disappoints; the micro-captive investigation escalates or produces an adverse resolution; and the multiple compresses toward the WTW/AON range (15–18x). On roughly flat adjusted EPS at ~15x, the stock revisits the $195–210 area, the low end of its recent range and near the $190.75 52-week low.

The current setup is genuinely two-sided. The franchise quality and recurring cash flows anchor a floor, but the growth-quality deterioration and integration overhang cap the near-term upside, and the valuation offers no cushion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale: the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction over the next 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth stabilizes or reaccelerates — two consecutive quarters of base commissions/fees organic at ≥5% would signal the rate-cycle drag has bottomed.
- AssuredPartners synergy realization ahead of plan, evidenced by brokerage EBITDAC margin re-expanding toward 38%+ once the interest-income comparison normalizes, alongside a turn in EPS estimate revisions from negative to positive.
- A pullback that compresses the forward multiple below ~16x (roughly $210 on next-year EPS) with fundamentals intact would restore an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth falls below 3% on base commissions/fees, indicating the soft market is deeper than management expects.
- Any goodwill impairment charge against the AssuredPartners/Woodruff Sawyer balance, or explicit management commentary that synergy targets are at risk.
- An adverse escalation in the IRS micro-captive criminal investigation (e.g., AJG becoming a named target).
- A credit downgrade that raises financing costs and constrains the M&A engine.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report, where the organic growth trend and integration commentary will be the decisive inputs.

## Conclusion

Gallagher is a high-quality, wide-moat broker with a capital-light model, sticky recurring revenue, and a proven acquisition machine. Those attributes justify a premium franchise multiple in a normal environment. Today the environment is not normal for this stock: organic growth is decelerating in step with a softening P&C rate cycle, the company is digesting its largest-ever acquisition with meaningful execution and goodwill-impairment risk, EPS estimate revisions are broadly negative, and the stock trades at ~19x forward earnings — at the peer median with a below-median yield and no identifiable catalyst to force a re-rating. The score trajectory has been declining (6.8 → 6.0 over four reports, all HOLD), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift; the recent price recovery from the $190s to $252 reflects sentiment stabilization and analyst upgrades more than any fundamental change. We maintain **HOLD** with an overall score of 5.5, and would want to see organic growth stabilize near 5% with visible integration progress before turning constructive, or a pullback below ~$210 to restore the risk/reward.
