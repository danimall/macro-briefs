**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Old National Bancorp has emerged from a multi-year acquisition campaign as a top-25 U.S. bank with ~$74.2 billion in assets, anchored across the Midwest and now Minnesota following the May 2025 Bremer Financial acquisition. The operating results through Q2 2026 are the best in the company's history: net income to common of $249.4M ($0.65 diluted EPS), ROATCE of 19.8%, ROAA of 1.38%, and a record efficiency ratio of 47.0% (45.2% adjusted), down from 58.8% just three quarters earlier. The efficiency-ratio compression is the cleanest evidence that Bremer cost synergies are being realized and validates the integration thesis. Net interest margin held at 3.54% despite the Fed easing to a 3.50-3.75% funds rate, and organic loans grew 8.3% annualized on a record $5.6B commercial pipeline. Credit improved rather than deteriorated, with nonaccrual loans falling to 0.91% of loans from 1.24% a year earlier.

The problem for a new-money buyer is price and momentum. ONB has run ~18% over the trailing year and trades near its 52-week high of $27.32, at roughly 10x consensus 2026 EPS of $2.58 and 9x the 2027 estimate of $2.88. That is a modest discount to the regional-bank peer median of 11.8x, not a compelling mispricing, and it comes against consensus estimates that are drifting lower across every window (current year -1.2% over 30 days, next year -0.8%). The analyst mean target of $29 implies ~11% upside, with the low target sitting at the current price. Seeking Alpha, MarketBeat, and StockStory commentary flagging the valuation as "rich" after the run is a fair read.

The operating trajectory of continued integration synergy capture, stable NIM, and healthy organic growth supports a modest 12-month appreciation path, which keeps this a growth-oriented hold rather than an income or value story (the 2.2% yield is below the peer median). The negative revision momentum, the 44% commercial-real-estate concentration, and a latent $558M accumulated-OCI hole from underwater securities argue against paying up at a 52-week high. We rate ONB HOLD.

## Company Overview

Old National Bank, founded in 1834 and headquartered in Evansville, Indiana, operates as a single-segment commercial bank with commercial and consumer operations run out of Chicago. The business model is spread banking, gathering low-cost deposits and lending across commercial, commercial real estate, residential, and consumer categories, supplemented by wealth management (~$41B AUM), capital markets (client interest-rate swaps), mortgage banking, and treasury services. Net interest income is roughly 80% of revenue.

The loan book (~$50.8B at 6/30/26) is weighted toward commercial real estate (~$22.5B, 44%), commercial/C&I (~$16.1B, 32%), residential (~$8.8B, 17%), and consumer (~$3.4B, 7%). Geographically, the footprint is now anchored in Minnesota (21%) as a direct result of Bremer, followed by Illinois (17%), Indiana (11%), Wisconsin (9%), Michigan (6%), and Tennessee (4%, from the 2024 CapStar deal). The strategic thread across First Midwest (2022), CapStar (2024), and Bremer (2025) is a consolidation roll-up in a fragmented Midwest market that has more than doubled the balance sheet from $53.9B at 3/31/25 to $74.2B at 6/30/26.

ONB's competitive differentiators are a granular, low-cost deposit franchise (total deposit cost of 1.71%, loan/deposit ratio of 91%) and a demonstrated M&A integration capability. It competes with regionals like Fifth Third, Huntington, and KeyCorp within its footprint rather than the money-center banks.

## Financial Analysis

The financials are dominated by the Bremer acquisition, which added ~$16.3B of assets on May 1, 2025 and makes year-over-year comparisons non-organic. The cleaner read is the linked-quarter trend, which is strong.

**Earnings trajectory.** The five-quarter diluted EPS progression is Q2'25 $0.34 → Q3'25 $0.46 → Q4'25 $0.55 → Q1'26 $0.59 → Q2'26 $0.65. The Q2'25 trough reflected a $75.6M CECL "Day 1" non-PCD provision plus merger charges tied to closing Bremer. The recovery therefore has two drivers: the Bremer scale addition and the roll-off of that one-time provision, with quarterly provisioning normalizing to roughly $36M. FY2025 net income was $653M, up from $523M in 2024.

**Profitability and efficiency.** Q2'26 ROATCE was 19.8% (19.9% adjusted) and ROAA 1.38%. The record efficiency ratio shows Bremer cost synergies landing as integration progresses. The six-month NIM improved to 3.54% from 3.41% a year ago, aided by Bremer's deposit base and purchase-accounting accretion. Holding NIM through Fed cuts (effective funds rate 3.64% versus 4.33% a year earlier) is a positive relative signal versus peers with higher deposit betas.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Deposits reached $56.1B, up 3.4% annualized. Capital is comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds: CET1 11.09%, Total capital 13.65%, TCE/TA 7.68% (bank-level Old National Bank CET1 11.25%). In January 2026 ONB issued $450M of 5.768% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes due 2036, adding Tier 2 capital. Book value is $21.80; tangible book value is $14.32, with the ~$2.86B goodwill/intangibles gap reflecting acquisition premiums.

**The latent AOCI drag.** The least-visible item on the balance sheet is a $558M accumulated OCI loss from underwater securities (AFS unrealized loss ~$656M; HTM ~$392M, ~$1.05B combined). This is unrealized and rate-driven, not credit-related, and ONB has no need or intent to sell, so it is currently benign. It is why tangible book sits far below book value and why the position would matter in a liquidity-stress scenario. Operating cash flow is solid ($206M in Q1'26).

**Capital allocation.** Balanced: ONB repurchased $107M (4.4M shares) in Q2'26 and pays a $0.145 quarterly dividend, for a combined payout ratio around 65%. A $400M buyback authorization runs through February 2027 (~$383M remaining as of 3/31/26). The one caveat is that buybacks are being executed at prices well above tangible book value.

## Growth Analysis

The organic engine is healthy. Loan growth on a record $5.6B commercial pipeline is genuine production on top of the acquired base, and the efficiency-ratio compression converts revenue growth into faster EPS growth. Consensus points to next-year EPS of $2.88, up ~11.6% from the $2.58 current-year estimate, a respectable trajectory for a super-regional.

The complication is estimate direction. Consensus is drifting lower, not higher: current quarter -2.3% over 30 days, next quarter -1.8%, current year -1.2%, next year -0.8%. This is modest in magnitude but consistently negative, and it runs against the "record results" framing. Rising estimates are among the strongest forward indicators; falling estimates are the reverse. There is no earnings-inflection dynamic here of the kind that would warrant valuing the stock off accelerating forward numbers: the estimates are declining, and the EPS recovery is largely the mechanical roll-off of a one-time provision plus M&A scale rather than an organic acceleration.

Earnings visibility is otherwise reasonable. Over the last four quarters ONB posted three beats and one in-line result, with beats in the 2-4% range, a steady, low-variance pattern that supports moderate confidence in near-term estimates even as they edge down.

The below-median dividend yield further limits the case for a valuation-driven long.

The stock has run near its 52-week high, and while prior appreciation is not itself a reason to be cautious, the combination of a full-ish multiple, falling estimates, and compressed upside to targets is. The analyst mean and median target of $29 implies ~11% upside; the low target of $26 sits at the current price and the high is $32. With a ~2.2% dividend, the 12-month total-return potential to consensus is in the low-teens, adequate but not asymmetric. In a sector where a low multiple has more often signaled a value trap than a bargain, and where regional-bank selection carries a structural alpha headwind, the modest peer discount does not clear the bar for a high valuation score. We score valuation 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

ONB's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The Bremer deal made it a top-25 U.S. bank and the fifth/sixth-largest headquartered in the Midwest, extending a granular, low-cost deposit franchise, a scarce and valuable asset in the post-2023 regional-bank environment. The 1.71% total deposit cost and 91% loan/deposit ratio are genuine strengths, and the ability to hold NIM through Fed cuts, when many peers face deposit-cost pressure, is a favorable relative signal. The wealth/AUM franchise (~$41B) adds fee diversification.

The offsetting reality is that ONB is a spread bank in a competitive, fragmented market, with a CRE-heavy balance sheet and growth that has depended materially on acquisitions. The moat is a deposit-and-integration advantage rather than a structural one, and the trajectory rests on the assumption that management continues to integrate cleanly. We view the competitive position as sound and modestly improving, scoring it 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration.** The bank's investor-CRE-to-Tier-1-capital ratio is 255%, below the 300% regulatory guideline but elevated. Office CRE exposure of $2.8B carries $53M of nonaccruals. Management flagged $779.5M of sub-4%-rate non-owner-occupied CRE maturing within 18 months, a repricing/renewal cluster that will test underwriting as those loans roll to higher rates. This is the most identifiable idiosyncratic risk cluster.

**Latent tangible-capital exposure.** The AOCI loss and ~$1.05B combined AFS/HTM unrealized losses are benign while ONB stays well-capitalized and holds the securities, but they explain the wide gap between book and tangible book and would become a real constraint in a liquidity-stress scenario.

**Integration and purchase-accounting risk.** Bremer ($1.35B consideration, $254.5M new goodwill, $440M intangibles) is still being integrated; ~$1.9B of acquired loans were purchased credit deteriorated, with $103.5M of contractual cash flows already estimated uncollectible at close. Fair-value/credit-mark assumptions could prove wrong.

**Credit direction — currently favorable.** Working against the bear case, net charge-offs are steady at 26 bps (22 bps ex-PCD), underperforming assets dropped to $472M, and ACL/nonaccrual coverage rose to 133%. The yellow flags are a 30+ day delinquency uptick to 0.29% (from 0.24%) and ~$3.1B of criticized/classified assets. Interest-rate sensitivity is near-neutral (NII moves <1% in +/-100bp scenarios), though a -300bp shock would cut projected NII ~9.7%.

**Options Market Signal.** Options positioning offers little contrary signal. The put/call open-interest ratio is negligible (0.04, up from 0.01 since early June) as the price rose ~12%, indicating no meaningful put-hedging demand or bearish positioning ahead of the print. This is consistent with a low-drama, well-owned regional bank rather than a stock with an impending binary catalyst. The absence of elevated put skew removes one downside signal but also provides no confirmation of a mispricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Integration synergies continue to compress the efficiency ratio toward the mid-40s, NIM holds or expands with purchase-accounting accretion and lower deposit costs, and organic loan growth in the high single digits sustains EPS growth toward and beyond the $2.88 2027 estimate. Credit continues to improve, the CRE maturity wall reprices without incident, and the market re-rates ONB toward the peer-median multiple. Bull target ~$32 (~22% upside), consistent with the high analyst target and a ~11x multiple on 2027 EPS.

**Bear case.** CRE stress surfaces as the $779.5M sub-4% book reprices, office deterioration accelerates, and provisioning re-elevates. Falling estimates continue, deposit-cost relief stalls, and the market prices ONB below peers on concentration and AOCI risk. In a rate-stress scenario the AOCI hole becomes a capital constraint. Bear target ~$21-22 (~-17% to -20%), roughly a 9x multiple on trimmed estimates and back toward the level the stock traded at through much of 2025.

**Base case.** Continued but decelerating operating improvement, modest EPS growth with estimates stabilizing, and a stock that grinds toward the consensus $29 target on synergy realization and dividend support. Base case ~$29 (~11% upside), plus ~2.2% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Timeframe: 12 months, with the primary checkpoints being quarterly earnings (Q3'26 in late October, Q4'26 in January) that will confirm or refute continued efficiency-ratio compression, NIM stability, and credit direction.

- **Upgrade triggers:** (1) estimate revisions turn positive across the current-year and next-year windows for two consecutive months, reversing the current downward drift; (2) efficiency ratio sustains below ~46% adjusted with NIM stable at or above 3.54%, confirming durable synergy capture; or (3) a pullback toward the low-$22s that restores a clear discount to peer and target levels.
- **Downgrade triggers:** (1) provisioning re-elevates materially (quarterly provision back toward $70M+) or nonaccruals reverse higher toward 1.2%+; (2) office/CRE nonaccruals climb as the sub-4% maturity wall reprices; or (3) NIM compresses below ~3.40% as deposit-cost relief stalls, cutting into the EPS-growth path.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural review is the Q3 2026 earnings release (~late October 2026); the CRE maturity-wall repricing over the next 18 months is the multi-quarter thread to monitor.

## Conclusion

Old National is executing well. The Q2'26 print validates the Bremer integration and demonstrates that the M&A roll-up is converting into per-share earnings power. Management's pay-for-performance alignment is sound, capital is adequate, and the July 2026 leadership reorganization reads as institutional maturation rather than distress.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the entry point has compressed. The stock trades near its 52-week high at roughly 10x forward earnings that represent only a modest discount to peers, while consensus estimates drift lower across every window and the mean target implies ~11% upside with the low target already at the current price. The improving fundamentals support a growth-oriented hold with a plausible path toward the high-$20s on continued synergy capture, but the negative revision momentum, the CRE concentration with a near-term maturity wall, and the latent AOCI drag argue against paying up here. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$20s or on a clean turn in estimate revisions; we would turn cautious on any re-acceleration in provisioning or CRE credit migration.
