**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Highwoods Properties is a Sunbelt-focused Class-A office REIT executing a coherent capital-recycling strategy: selling older, non-core assets in Richmond, Atlanta, Tampa, and Pittsburgh to fund acquisitions and development in higher-growth submarkets (Raleigh, Charlotte, and a new Dallas entry). The operating story is a trade of occupancy for rate: physical occupancy has slid roughly five points since 2021 (90.0% to 85.0% at March 31, 2026), while the company re-leases space at +15% to +19% GAAP spreads and pushes rent per square foot higher every year ($30.75 in 2021 to $34.80 in 2025). FFO per share has declined for three consecutive years ($3.83 in 2023 to $3.48 in 2025), but Q1 2026 FFO of $0.84 (vs. $0.83 a year prior) is the first sign the rate gains are beginning to offset volume losses.

The investment problem is timing, not quality. As of the March 31, 2026 balance-sheet date the stock closed at $21.41; at year-end 2025 it was $25.82. It now trades at $31.86, a ~49% recovery off the spring low. The prior thesis on this name rested on a deep discount to net asset value, with management authorizing a $250M buyback in April at ~$21 to arbitrage public-vs-private value. That gap has largely closed. The current quote sits above the $26.78 analyst mean and above the $31.00 high target, meaning the shares have re-rated past where the sell-side sees fair value. What remains is a well-covered ~6.6% dividend (2025 payout ratio ~57% of FFO) backed by investment-grade credit and double-digit leasing spreads that confirm the BBD assets retain pricing power.

We move to HOLD with an income rationale. The prior AVOID calls (March and May) were correct on direction when the stock was cheap and drifting lower; the fundamental improvement has been real (FFO stabilizing, spreads accelerating), but the ~49% price move has consumed the margin of safety. We would neither chase the shares at $31.86 nor sell them short: the dividend coverage, investment-grade balance sheet, and buyback authorization remove the acute downside catalysts a short would require.

## Company Overview

Highwoods owns, develops, leases, and manages Class-A office space in what it terms "Best Business Districts" across the Sunbelt. It operates as an UPREIT through Highwoods Realty Limited Partnership, holding ~98.2% of the operating-partnership units. As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio comprised 27.4M rentable square feet of in-service properties, 0.8M SF under development, and ~3.5M SF of development land.

The geographic footprint (as a share of annualized GAAP rent at 12/31/2025) is led by Raleigh at 21.6% (occupancy 89.3%), followed by Nashville at 18.7% (occupancy 81.8%, the weakest core market), Atlanta at 16.9%, Charlotte at 12.8%, Tampa at 10.3%, Orlando at 6.2%, and Richmond at 4.8%. Dallas is a new market added via 2026 joint-venture acquisitions. The strategy, "work-placemaking," is to own amenitized office in supply-constrained districts and continuously recycle capital into higher-growth submarkets while maintaining a conservative balance sheet.

The competitive positioning differs meaningfully from coastal gateway office REITs (SL Green, Boston Properties, Vornado). Highwoods is levered to net in-migration Sunbelt markets that have outperformed gateway CBD office since 2020. The offsetting structural tension is that these markets are relatively low-barrier-to-entry: management explicitly notes that undeveloped land is "readily available and less expensive" in its markets, so demand growth invites new competitive supply in a way a Manhattan landlord does not face.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI.** Full-year revenue declined from $834.0M (2023) to ~$826M (2024) to $806.1M (2025), a ~2.4% drop in 2025 driven by dispositions, lower occupancy, and reduced cost recoveries. Same-property NOI fell 1.8% in 2025 and another 1.3% in Q1 2026, as property taxes, utilities, and contract services outpaced modest revenue gains. The occupancy trend is the central negative: 90.0% (2021) to 88.0% (2024) to 85.3% year-end 2025 to 85.0% at March 31, 2026. Management's 2026 guidance is 85.5%–86.5%, implying stabilization rather than recovery.

**The offsetting positive is pricing power.** Q4 2025 new/renewal GAAP rents were +15.4% over expiring leases; Q1 2026 spreads jumped to +19.4% at $39.45 PSF. This is the crux of the business: Highwoods is losing tenants but re-leasing at materially higher rates. The next several quarters will show whether rate gains can continue to offset volume losses, and Q1 2026 FFO stabilization is the first data point suggesting they can.

**FFO, the correct REIT lens.** FFO per share declined $3.83 (2023) to $3.61 (2024) to $3.48 (2025). The dividend held flat at $2.00 annualized ($0.50/quarter), producing a ~57% payout ratio against 2025 FFO, comfortably covered and yielding ~6.6% at the current price. Reported "FCF" of $351.7M and a 43.6% "FCF margin" are misleading: they net only maintenance-level capex (–$7.6M) and exclude the large tenant-improvement, leasing-commission, and development spend that REITs fund through investing activities. True recurring capital needs run roughly $50–60M per quarter. Similarly, 2025 net margin is inflated by $100.6M of one-time disposition gains. FFO and AFFO are the appropriate metrics.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt rose from $3.55B (12/31/25) to $3.70B (3/31/26) as the revolver was drawn for acquisitions. Debt/undepreciated-assets leverage is a moderate 43.6%; the 60.8% debt-to-total-capitalization figure is elevated primarily because the stock-price decline shrank the equity component, not because of new borrowing. The company is investment-grade rated (Moody's/S&P) and in compliance with all covenants. The frequently cited Altman Z-Score of 1.04 ("distress zone") is a model artifact: the Z-Score penalizes asset-heavy, leveraged REITs mechanically (low revenue/assets turnover, low working capital) and does not reflect genuine distress here.

The debt is well-laddered and largely fixed (weighted-average 4.44% secured / 4.46% unsecured). The single most important near-term item is the $300M 3.875% notes due March 2027, the key refinancing test. Floating-rate debt rose from $375M to $525M in Q1 2026 as the revolver funded acquisitions, worth monitoring in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The $350M 5.35% notes issued in Q4 2025 illustrate the refinancing headwind: interest expense grew 3.6% in 2025 and 13.8% YoY in Q1 2026 as older 2.6%–4.2% notes roll into higher coupons.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a stabilization case, not an expansion case. FFO per share has fallen three years running; the bullish read is that the Q1 2026 print halts that decline via acquisition accretion (Advance Auto Parts Tower/Raleigh $137.9M, Legacy Union garage/Charlotte $110.2M, 6HUNDRED/Charlotte $193.4M) and the +19.4% re-leasing spreads. The Dallas entry via two joint ventures (Bloc 83, a Raleigh CBD mixed-use JV with the North Carolina Investment Authority, 10% initial stake with an option to 50%; The Terraces in Dallas Preston Center, 80% owned with Granite) adds a new growth market at the right point in the Sunbelt migration cycle.

Weighing this analytically: growth prospects is the most reliable dimension for sorting winners, and here it is only middling-to-weak. Revenue is contracting, occupancy is at a multi-year low, and same-property NOI is declining. The offsets, pricing power and accretive acquisitions, are real but incremental, and near-term lease expirations are heavy (8.9% of leased SF in 2026, 11.2% in 2027, 10.6% in 2028), which means the rate gains must keep clearing above the volume drag through a period of elevated rollover. The 4.5 score reflects a business that is arguably bottoming rather than growing, with genuine but modest earnings support from Q1.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and does not support high confidence in forward estimates: Q1 2026 beat by 161% and Q4 2025 by 48.5%, but Q3 2025 missed by 29.1% and Q2 2025 by 5.2% (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters). The recent beats are encouraging, but the volatility argues against extrapolating growth with conviction.

## Valuation Assessment

The 38.1x trailing/forward P/E is not the right lens for a REIT: earnings are depressed by depreciation and lumpy disposition timing. On the correct metric, HIW trades at roughly 9.2x 2025 FFO of $3.48. That is optically cheap versus office-REIT history and versus the peer P/E median of 25.0x, but the comparison requires care because several peers show negative or distorted P/Es (SL Green –20.4, Cousins –907, Douglas Emmett –75.0), reflecting the sector's GAAP earnings compression. On yield, HIW's ~6.6% sits above CDP (3.7%), KRC (5.6%), DEI (6.1%), and SLG (4.8%), and its dividend is well covered at ~57% of FFO.

The decisive valuation input is the price recovery, not the multiple. HIW is not an inflection stock at a low multiple with rising estimates (revenue is contracting, FFO is only stabilizing), so the "targets are floors" logic does not apply; on this name the analyst targets function more as a ceiling than a floor (mean $26.78, high $31.00, low $23.00), and the consensus has the stock through fair value.

We treat the low FFO multiple as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent a fresh catalyst. Cheapness in office REITs has been persistently anti-predictive: the discount reflects secular occupancy erosion, refinancing at higher coupons, and federal-tenant risk, not a temporary mispricing. The specific catalyst that made HIW attractive (deep discount to NAV, opportunistic buyback) has been arbitraged away by the price recovery. Management itself signaled this: the buyback was compelling at $21; at $31.86 the two-way capital toolkit (buyback plus $300M ATM/forward-sale programs) tilts back toward issuance, reducing repurchase support. The 4.0 valuation score reflects a name that has re-rated to full value with no independent catalyst to justify a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

Highwoods occupies the favorable side of the office secular trend. Its Sunbelt footprint benefits from net in-migration, and Sunbelt office has outperformed gateway CBD office since 2020. The re-leasing spreads are hard evidence that its Best Business District assets retain genuine pricing power even as physical occupancy slips, a company-specific signal that separates it from the generic office-REIT malaise.

The offsetting structural weakness is the low-barrier-to-entry nature of its markets. Cheap, available land allows new competitive supply to be built even in strong demand periods, so the reward of demand growth is partly offset by oversupply risk. Nashville, the #2 market at 18.7% of rent, is the clearest soft spot at 81.8% occupancy, tied to the cancelled Tivity backfill lease. The federal government is the #3 tenant (748,955 SF, 2.7% of rent, only a 3.6-year weighted-average term across 31 leases and 24 agencies), a real vulnerability given the current wave of federal office-footprint reductions.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving: share within its BBDs is held via pricing power and the portfolio is being upgraded through recycling, but the moat is not widening in a way the market has failed to price. This dimension has decayed to a weak recent signal across our framework, so we do not lean on it heavily. Score 5.5, stable and credible but not a source of asymmetric upside.

## Risk Assessment

Several risks are material and largely priced, which is why the risk score sits at a neutral 5.0 rather than lower:

- **Refinancing at higher rates.** The March 2027 notes are the key test; every refinancing raises interest expense. Floating-rate debt has risen to $525M.
- **Occupancy erosion and federal-tenant exposure.** The five-point occupancy decline and the federal government's short-dated, 2.7%-of-rent concentration are the operating risks that could reverse the fragile FFO stabilization.
- **Impairment risk.** The company recorded $8.8M (2025, Century Center Atlanta) and $24.6M (2024, 625 Liberty/Pittsburgh) impairments; ongoing non-core dispositions and change-in-use assumptions keep this elevated.
- **JV put rights.** NCIA (Bloc 83), Granite (Terraces, Granite Park Six), and others hold rights to put their interests back to Highwoods at fair value, creating contingent off-balance-sheet capital calls.

These are known, disclosed, and reflected in the sub-10x FFO multiple. There is no single acute binary catalyst that the market is mispricing in either direction, which is why the risk profile is neutral rather than a low-score red flag.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent, not fearful. Near-term ATM IV of 25.0% sits slightly below one-year historical volatility of 27.9% (a –2.9% spread, "fair"), with IV in only the 10th percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in contango (25.0% near-term vs. 29.8% long-term), a normal, non-stressed shape. The headline put/call volume ratio of 3.00 looks bearish, but the put/call open-interest ratio of just 0.14 tells the opposite story, and the entire chain is thin (23 contracts across three expirations), so the volume ratio is noise. ATM IV rose from 10.1% to 25.0% since the prior snapshot alongside a +21.8% price move, consistent with the general re-rating rather than a specific catalyst bet. The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis, which reinforces the neutral risk read and argues against a short.

Short interest is elevated at 10.7% of float (4.8 days to cover), reflecting institutional skepticism about office and about the rate-refinancing path. That positioning is a modest squeeze consideration but not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36–38).** Occupancy troughs at ~85% and inflects toward the 85.5%–86.5% guide; re-leasing spreads hold in the mid-teens, so FFO per share stabilizes and then grows off the $3.48 base via acquisition accretion. The March 2027 note refinances without a sharp coupon step-up as rates ease. The Dallas and Charlotte investments prove accretive, and the market awards a Sunbelt-quality FFO multiple of ~10.5–11x on a rising FFO figure. The ~6.6% dividend plus modest multiple expansion produces a total return in the low-to-mid teens.

**Bear Case ($24–25, roughly –22% to –25%).** Occupancy resumes its decline as heavy 2026–2028 lease expirations (8.9%/11.2%/10.6% of leased SF) roll at lower net effective rents; federal-tenant reductions bite; refinancing the March 2027 notes at 5%+ coupons compounds the 13.8% interest-expense growth already seen; same-property NOI keeps falling. FFO drifts toward $3.20–3.30, the multiple compresses back below 8x, and the stock retraces toward the low-$20s where it traded two quarters ago.

**Base Case ($30–32).** The most likely path is muddle-through: FFO stabilizes in the $3.40–3.50 range, the dividend remains covered and flat, occupancy hovers near the low-86% guide, and the stock trades roughly in line with its current price. The re-rating that has already occurred prices in this outcome, leaving the ~6.6% yield as the primary return driver.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income rationale). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings, July 28, 2026.**

The prior AVOID calls were appropriate when the stock was cheap and drifting; the fundamental improvement (FFO stabilizing, spreads accelerating, buyback authorized) has been real and the trajectory has improved from a 4.4 to a 5.2 to the current 5.0 composite. This is not an upgrade to BUY because the price recovery has consumed the discount to private-market value that constituted the entire opportunity, and the stock now trades above the analyst mean and high target. It is not a continued AVOID because at ~6.6% covered yield, investment-grade credit, and demonstrated pricing power, the downside is bounded and there is no acute catalyst to short against.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s (below ~9x FFO / above ~7% yield), restoring the discount-to-NAV entry.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising occupancy above 86% with FFO per share growth resuming YoY.
- Refinancing of the March 2027 notes at a coupon at or below the maturing 3.875%, removing the key interest-expense overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Occupancy falling below 84% or 2026 guidance cut below the 85.5% floor.
- A meaningful federal-tenant non-renewal or a Nashville deterioration below ~80% occupancy.
- Same-property NOI declines accelerating beyond –2% with re-leasing spreads compressing to single digits.
- A dividend coverage deterioration (FFO payout above ~70%).

## Conclusion

Highwoods is a competently run Sunbelt office REIT with a credible portfolio-upgrade strategy, demonstrable pricing power, an investment-grade balance sheet, and a well-covered ~6.6% dividend. The operating story of trading occupancy for rate appears to be reaching an FFO trough, and Q1 2026 stabilization is a genuine positive. The problem is entirely one of price: the stock has recovered ~49% from its March low and now trades above where the sell-side sees fair value, erasing the discount-to-NAV that made prior interest compelling and that management itself acted on with a $21 buyback authorization.

We rate HIW a HOLD on income grounds. Own it for the covered yield and the option on Sunbelt occupancy recovery, but the risk/reward at $31.86 is symmetric-to-slightly-negative rather than the asymmetric long it was two quarters ago. The name warrants fresh attention only on a meaningful pullback that restores the discount, or on hard evidence that FFO growth has resumed. The next checkpoint is the July 28, 2026 Q2 report.
