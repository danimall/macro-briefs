## Executive Summary

Avery Dennison reported Q2 2026 results on July 30 that broke a two-year pattern of flat volumes: adjusted EPS of $2.89 (+19.4% year over year) against $2.47 consensus, net sales of $2.46B (+10.9% reported, approximately +8% excluding currency). The stock rose 11.4% from $167.14 to $186.20 in a single session. First-half adjusted EPS of $5.36 is 13.8% above the $4.71 delivered in H1 2025, and it sits against a stale full-year consensus of $10.02 that will require upward revision.

The mechanism behind the acceleration is identifiable and it matters for durability. AVY's FY2025 10-K quantified an aggregate low-single-digit decline in its customers' overall apparel category sales across Q2, Q3 and Q4 2025, attributed to indirect tariff effects — IEEPA tariffs raised landed apparel costs, brand owners cut order volumes, fewer garments meant fewer RFID inlays and care labels, and AVY's Solutions Group organic growth stalled. The same 10-K discloses that on February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court held IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariff imposition. Order normalization with a one-to-two-quarter lag lands precisely in Q2 2026, and it laps the easiest comparison in the series. If that is what happened, the recovery is a level shift back toward trend rather than a new secular growth rate.

Three constraints keep this a HOLD rather than an upgrade. First, the price has already moved: at $186.20 the stock trades 7% below consensus mean ($199.60) and 6.7% below its 52-week high, having gained roughly 20% from mid-June levels near $153. Second, the decisive datapoint is unverified — the organic-versus-acquisition split of the ~8% ex-currency growth is not established, and Taylor Adhesives (acquired October 2025) remains inorganic in Q2, plausibly contributing 2-3 points. Third, the last verified quarter (Q1 2026, filed May 5) showed Intelligent Labels organic sales *declining* at a low-single-digit rate and Solutions Group segment margin contracting 120bp to 9.01%. My score trajectory on this name has fallen from 6.6 in February to 5.4 in July on exactly that deterioration; the Q2 print arrests the decline without yet reversing it.

## Company Overview

Avery Dennison operates two reportable segments generating $8,855.5M of FY2025 net sales, with roughly 35,000 employees across more than 200 facilities in 50-plus countries. Sixty-nine percent of sales are international and about 40% come from emerging markets; 58% of employees are in Asia-Pacific.

**Materials Group** ($6,093.3M, 69% of sales, 15.1% segment adjusted operating margin) supplies pressure-sensitive label and packaging *materials* — facestock/adhesive/liner laminates — to converters, who print and finish labels for brand owners. Performance tapes, graphics and reflective films sit here, as does the ~$390M Taylor Adhesives flooring-adhesives acquisition. The value-chain position matters: AVY's largest single-name credit exposures are converters, not brands, which is why a converter bankruptcy appeared as a new risk factor in the FY2025 10-K.

**Solutions Group** ($2,762.2M, 31%, 10.4% margin) sells finished RFID inlays and Intelligent Labels, apparel branding and care labels, and Vestcom shelf-edge media directly to brands and retailers. This is where the growth narrative — and the valuation premium — resides.

Effective Q1 2026, AVY moved incremental revenues for certain RFID products from Solutions into Materials without restating prior periods, adding roughly 1 point to Materials reported growth and subtracting about 3 points from Solutions. Segment realignment to match management structure is routine under ASC 280 and the company continued disclosing company-wide Intelligent Labels organic growth. The practical consequence is that outside triangulation of the RFID trend became harder in the same quarter that trend turned negative.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: three years of no volume growth, then a possible turn

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales ($M) | 9,039.3 | 8,364.3 | 8,755.7 | 8,855.5 |
| Organic change | — | — | +5% | **0%** |
| GAAP diluted EPS | — | $6.20 | $8.73 | $8.79 |
| Adjusted EPS | $9.15 | $7.90 | $9.43 | $9.53 |

FY2025 revenue remains about 2% below FY2022. Organic growth was zero (Materials −1%, Solutions +2%); the 1.1% reported gain came from FX (+$29M), Taylor Adhesives, and four extra calendar days following the conversion from a 52/53-week fiscal calendar. Adjusted EPS rose 21% from 2023 to 2025 on 6% revenue growth — the delta is restructuring ($48.8M of charges against ~1,200 positions in 2025, with more than $60M of incremental net savings realized in each of 2024 and 2025) plus a 3.0% annual reduction in diluted share count. Adjusted EPS grew only 1.1% in FY2025 despite that 3% share-count reduction, meaning per-share operating earnings were flat to slightly down.

Q1 2026 showed the same pattern more starkly: reported sales +7.0%, of which FX contributed 5 points ($99M) and acquisitions 1 point, leaving +1% organic. Pretax income grew 5.9% but net income grew 1.1% because the effective tax rate rose 340bp to 30.1%. Diluted EPS of $2.18 (+4.3%) outgrew net income entirely on buyback.

Q2 2026 is the break in the series. First-half adjusted EPS of $5.36 versus $4.71 is a 13.8% gain against a full-year consensus that still implies roughly $4.66 of second-half earnings — mechanically inconsistent with a Q2 that alone delivered $2.89, and against H2 2025 comparisons of $2.37 (a 2.2% miss) and $2.45.

### Margins: Materials stable, Solutions deteriorating

Materials segment adjusted operating margin: 13.58% (FY23) → 15.38% (FY24) → 15.13% (FY25) → 15.41% in Q1 2026 versus 15.56% in Q1 2025. Effectively flat at a cyclical plateau, consistent with disciplined raw-material pass-through in a scale business.

Solutions: 9.87% (FY23) → 10.55% (FY24) → 10.36% (FY25) → **9.01% in Q1 2026 versus 10.21% in Q1 2025**. The 120bp year-over-year contraction, on negative organic sales and with base business down mid-single digits, is the single most important margin datapoint in the verified filings, because it sits against $1,382.1M of Solutions goodwill.

### Balance sheet: investment-grade, with the automated screen materially wrong

Screening data on this name has circulated an Altman Z-Score of 1.57 with a 0.2% operating margin. Both are extraction errors. FY2025 GAAP operating income reconstructs to $1,046.3M (gross profit $2,546.3M − marketing/G&A $1,422.5M − other expense, net $77.5M), an 11.8% operating margin; cross-checked, pretax income of $925.1M plus $135.4M interest expense gives $1,060.5M of EBIT. Recomputing Altman with correct EBIT and *market* equity ($14.2B) rather than book equity against $6,559.6M of liabilities yields **Z ≈ 3.64 — safe zone**, and ≈3.62 on Q1 2026 data.

Actual leverage: FY2025 EBITDA of $1,374.5M (operating income plus $328.2M D&A) against net debt of $3,530.1M gives **2.57x net debt/EBITDA**, or ~2.43x on adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,452M, versus a 3.50x revolver covenant (temporarily stepped up to 4.00x for four quarters post-Taylor). Interest coverage is 7.8x. The $1.2B revolver maturing June 2029 was undrawn at both 12/31/25 and 3/31/26. Reported "D/E of 3.93" is total liabilities over equity; gross debt/equity is 1.66x, with book equity depressed by $3,957.6M of accumulated treasury stock.

Four genuine balance-sheet observations do warrant attention:

- **Current ratio 1.13**, with $605.0M of short-term borrowings and current LTD against $255.1M of cash at 3/31/26. AVY runs a commercial-paper-funded working-capital model backstopped by the revolver — normal for an IG industrial, but with little standalone liquidity.
- **$375.5M of supplier finance obligations inside $1,329.5M of accounts payable** (~28%), with cash flows classified in operating activities. Disclosed, declining modestly, and standard at this scale, but it means part of the ~$700M FCF base depends on third-party willingness to finance payables. DPO of 73-74 days should be read against that.
- **Negative tangible book:** $2,263.0M goodwill and $801.2M intangibles against ~$2,300M book equity. Q4 2025 goodwill testing was qualitative-only for all reporting units in both segments.
- **Capital returns exceeded free cash flow by 22% in FY2025**: $572.3M buybacks plus $288.4M dividends equals $860.7M against $707.1M adjusted FCF, alongside the $390M Taylor acquisition, funded by a ~$581M increase in total debt including €500M of 4.000% notes due 2035 issued in September 2025.

Marked down for the debt-funded 122% payout, thin standalone liquidity, negative tangible book, supplier-finance dependence, and sub-depreciation capex.

## Growth Analysis

The verified growth record is poor and the unverified record is good. Both need stating.

**Verified:** Intelligent Labels company-wide organic growth went from high-single-digit in FY2024 to low-single-digit in FY2025 to a low-single-digit *decline* in Q1 2026. That is a five-quarter deceleration in the franchise that justifies AVY's premium to commodity packagers. Company-wide organic growth was 0% in FY2025 and +1% in Q1 2026. R&D of $137M is 1.5% of sales. FY2026 effective tax rate guidance was raised from the "mid-twenty percent range" (10-K, February 25) to the "high-twenty percent range" (10-Q, May 5) with no explanation, roughly a 3-4% headwind to pretax EPS capacity versus what was embedded ten weeks earlier.

**Unverified but material:** Q2 2026 sales +10.9% with roughly +8% ex-currency, adjusted EPS +19.4%. Strip 2-3 points for Taylor Adhesives and organic lands at mid-single digits — a genuine change in trajectory from +1%.

Consensus estimate revisions are the one piece of evidence that cuts against the inflection, and they need honest handling: current-quarter, next-quarter, current-year and next-year estimates are all down 0.1% to 1.1% over 90 days, described as stable with no directional shift. That data predates the July 30 print (the "current quarter" estimate of $2.49 is the Q2 number already reported at $2.89). The strong estimate-revision buy signal — 30%+ revisions plus 30%+ revenue growth — is not present here and I am not manufacturing it. What is present is arithmetic: H1 actuals of $5.36 against a $10.02 full-year consensus, which will be revised upward within weeks.

The durability question resolves on mechanism. A tariff unwind restores suppressed apparel volumes to a prior level; it does not restore high-single-digit RFID unit expansion into food, logistics and general retail, which is the actual bull thesis. Competitive capacity additions in inlays (Tageos/Fedrigoni, SML, Arizon, R-pac, Checkpoint/CCL) argue for continued price/mix pressure regardless of volume recovery. Geographic mix helps at the margin — Q1 2026 Materials organic was high-single-digit in Asia-Pacific, low-single-digit in North America, comparable in EMENA, down mid-single-digit in Latin America — but 69% of sales are international.

**Growth Prospects: 5.5/10**, up from 4.5. The H1 adjusted EPS gain of 13.8% and easy H2 comparisons are real; the structural RFID thesis remains unproven and the tax headwind is confirmed.

**Valuation: 5.3/10.** Fair, not asymmetric. Cheapness on forward estimates is real but modest, and the catalyst that would have created the mispricing has already been discounted.

## Competitive Landscape

Materials Group holds share in a scale-driven, pass-through business — organic sales of −1% in FY2025 with margins essentially flat is consistent with volume tracking end-market consumption while pricing discipline holds. Named competitors are UPM Adhesive Materials, Fedrigoni Self-Adhesives, Lintec and Flexcon in label materials; 3M, Orafol, Tesa and Nitto Denko in graphics and tapes. The durable advantages are a globally distributed coating and converting footprint that is expensive to replicate near customers, and qualification/spec lock-in with brand owners. No customer exceeded 10% of net sales in 2025, and the customer base is fragmented.

Solutions is where trajectory is deteriorating. Intelligent Labels organic growth turned negative while segment margin fell 120bp, in a market where Fedrigoni appears twice in AVY's own competitor list — as Fedrigoni Self-Adhesives in materials and as Tageos in RFID — indicating a vertically integrated European rival attacking both segments. Checkpoint (CCL), SML, R-pac and Arizon complete a crowding field. The filings do not permit separating apparel-volume weakness from inlay price/mix compression, and the segment reclassification removed the cleanest way to track it.

Two forward-looking moves are constructive without being decisive: the ~$75M minority investment in Wiliot completed April 27, 2026 (AVY is an investor; it does not own or consolidate Wiliot), and the India inlay site. Against that, the $390M Taylor Adhesives acquisition moves capital into flooring adhesives — an adjacency leveraging existing chemistry and coating assets, but structurally lower-growth than the RFID story the multiple prices.

**Competitive Position: 5.5/10**, held flat. Stable in the volume half, deteriorating in the differentiated half, with visibility reduced by the segment change.

## Risk Assessment

**Intelligent Labels: structural maturation versus cyclical pause.** The dominant valuation risk. If IL is maturing rather than pausing, the multiple compresses toward a materials-company range and $150 becomes the reference point. The tariff mechanism supports the cyclical reading; competitive capacity additions support the structural one.

**Solutions goodwill.** $1,382.1M of goodwill in a segment whose Q1 2026 margin fell 120bp on negative organic sales, with Q4 2025 testing performed qualitatively only. A qualitative test is permissible under ASC 350 when headroom is large, and prior quantitative tests may show substantial cushion, but no quantitative cushion figure is available. This is the clearest un-priced impairment tail.

**Tax rate.** FY2026 ETR guidance moved from mid-twenties to high-twenties between filings without explanation. AVY's indefinite-lived NOLs are concentrated in Luxembourg and "may require decades to be fully utilized." Unrecognized tax benefits are ~$81M. Q2's operating strength more than offset the drag; a second step-up would not be absorbed as easily.

**Dutch pension conversion.** AVY's Dutch defined-benefit plan must convert to defined contribution before January 1, 2028, the minimum guaranteed funding ratio terminates, and AVY must compensate the Dutch Pension Fund. The amount is not quantified in the filings. Aggregate plan assets (~$733M) modestly exceed liabilities (~$721M), so this is a discrete cash obligation with a hard deadline rather than a solvency issue.

**Converter credit.** A large Materials customer filed prepackaged Chapter 11 in January 2026, with AVY expecting to collect prepetition receivables. The credit data corroborate that: the allowance for credit losses *declined* from $28.1M to $27.1M during Q1 2026, write-offs were $2.3M, and the Q1 2026 provision line was blank versus $1.6M a year earlier. Forward revenue effects from a reorganized converter rationalizing plants or renegotiating terms remain possible; a fragmented customer base bounds the magnitude.

**FX and the euro-debt structure.** FX added 5 points to Q1 2026 reported sales after being near-neutral in FY2025. The Q2 2026 ex-currency figures indicate FX added roughly 3 points to reported growth [unverified — from secondary reporting]. Reversal of dollar weakness removes a material share of reported growth. AVY carries $1,147.3M notional of FX forwards designated as a fair value hedge on €1B of notes maturing September and December 2026, €1.0B of net investment hedges, and $250M of cross-currency swaps; Q1 2026 forwards produced a $26.5M loss in marketing/G&A versus a $19.9M gain a year earlier, offset by the hedged item in the same line. Argentine peso remeasurement losses recur ($30M/$16M/$6M in 2023/24/25).

**End-market volume.** AVY's demand is a direct function of consumer-goods units sold. The FY2025 filings attribute soft apparel volumes to indirect tariff effects on its customers, and label volumes respond to unit volumes with little lag. This is a cyclical exposure that is largely priced, but it caps the upside case if a second-half consumer slowdown materializes.

**Governance.** At least seven Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks in roughly two years, with the June 4, 2026 filing content unread. Some of that count reflects routine board additions and compensation actions. A new VP/Controller and Chief Accounting Officer was promoted effective March 2026 while CFO Gregory Lovins remains in place; there is no evidence of accounting-related turnover. Bylaws were amended February 26, 2026.

Near-term ATM IV of 33.4% against 24.6% one-year historical volatility is rich (8.7-point spread), with term structure in backwardation (33.4% at one month versus 27.2% at six months) and an implied ±6.7% move on the nearest expiry — a residual of the just-occurred event rather than a forecast of new stress, and likely to compress. The takeaway is that near-term optionality is expensive, which argues against buying premium here and marginally favors patience on entry.

**Risk Factors: 5.5/10.** No acutely dated binary event, IG credit, genuinely low customer concentration; marked down for the Solutions goodwill tail, the unquantified Dutch pension obligation, the unexplained tax step-up, and dependence on unverified Q2 detail.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $220 (+18%)

The July 30 print confirms in the Q2 10-Q that organic growth accelerated to mid-single digits with Intelligent Labels organic back to positive. The mechanism is the IEEPA tariff unwind restoring apparel order volumes, and it runs through H2 against comparisons of $2.37 and $2.45 in adjusted EPS. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at $10.60-10.80 versus stale $10.02 consensus; FY2027 approaches $11.50. Solutions margin recovers above 10% as volume returns to a cost base already reduced by roughly 2,850 positions across three restructuring programs. Capex below depreciation sustains a 5% FCF yield while net leverage falls back toward 2.2x, restoring buyback capacity at prices management has demonstrated it will discipline. Twenty times $11.00 gives $220, consistent with the $218 street high.

### Bear Case — $150 (−19%)

The 10-Q reveals that Q2's ~8% ex-currency growth was 3 points Taylor Adhesives and 3 points price/comp normalization, leaving organic in the low single digits. Intelligent Labels organic stays flat to negative as Tageos, SML and Arizon capacity compresses inlay pricing, and Solutions margin holds near 9%, triggering a quantitative goodwill test against $1,382.1M of carrying value. Dollar strength reverses the FX tailwind, and consumer-goods unit volumes soften in H2. The tax rate settles at 29-30%. FY2027 adjusted EPS stalls near $10.00 and the multiple compresses to 15x — the level at which Crown Holdings trades — giving $150. In this path the balance sheet is not the risk; the multiple is.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on Avery Dennison, and the score stops falling here at 5.55 after four declining reads. That deserves explanation on both sides. The declines from 6.6 to 5.4 tracked a real deterioration — Intelligent Labels organic growth collapsing from high-single-digit to negative across five quarters, Solutions margin losing 120bp, company-wide organic growth of zero in FY2025, and capital returns at 122% of free cash flow funded with debt. None of that has been retracted by the filings.

What has changed is that the July 30 print delivered adjusted EPS 15.9% above consensus with sales up roughly 8% excluding currency, and there is a coherent, filing-documented mechanism for why: the tariff regime that suppressed AVY's customers' apparel volumes across three quarters of 2025 lost its statutory basis in February 2026, and order normalization lands in Q2 with easy comparisons through year-end. First-half adjusted EPS of $5.36 against a $10.02 full-year consensus guarantees upward revision.

The reason this is not an upgrade is arithmetic rather than narrative. The stock captured 11.4% of that news in a single session and roughly 20% since mid-June, leaving 7% to the consensus mean, ~17.7x on my FY2026 estimate, and a 2.15% dividend yield. Against a bear case at $150 predicated on a live goodwill exposure and an unresolved competitive question in inlays, the skew is balanced at best. Owners should hold and collect the dividend into the Q2 10-Q. Buyers should wait for either the organic figure in that filing or a retracement toward $170.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q2 adjusted-EPS beat is 15.9% (Phase 1: $2.89 actual vs $2.49 estimate), not 17%; the tax-guidance step-up is a ~3-4% pretax-line headwind, not 4-5% to EPS; and the base-case target rests on an 18.5x multiple described as a "premium" to a ~21x peer median when it is in fact a discount — none of these individually overturn the HOLD, but they modestly weaken the valuation-support leg of the thesis.
