## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is one of the world's largest product tanker operators (87 vessels) currently benefiting from an extraordinary rate environment driven by geopolitical disruptions including sanctions on Russian petroleum products, lengthened trade routes, and regional shipping dislocations. The company's balance sheet has undergone a transformation from $293M net debt in September 2025 to $385M net cash by March 2026, while management simultaneously sells mid-life vessels at premium prices and orders newbuilds for 2027-2029 delivery.

The stock has appreciated 116% over the past 12 months to $82.85, approaching its 52-week high of $84.23. EPS estimates have surged, with sell-side consensus moving sharply higher over recent months, driven by LR2 spot rates approaching $101,000/day in Q2 2026. At an estimated ~8.4x current-year EPS, the stock remains inexpensive relative to the earnings power being demonstrated, though the cyclical nature of tanker rates and approaching supply inflection from 2027 cap the sustainable multiple.

The investment case rests on two pillars: (1) the current geopolitical environment sustains elevated product tanker rates well above long-term averages, and (2) management's disciplined capital allocation — aggressive deleveraging, well-timed asset sales, and potentially accretive convertible financing — provides meaningful downside protection even if rates normalize.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates 87 product tankers (32 LR2, 41 MR, 14 Handymax) with an average fleet age of 10.2 years, transporting refined petroleum products globally. The fleet is primarily scrubber-fitted, providing a fuel cost advantage. The company participates in commercial pools for rate optimization and has recently diversified with two VLCC newbuilding orders ($256M total, delivering H2 2028) marking an entry into crude oil transport.

STNG is incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in Monaco, filing as a foreign private issuer on the NYSE. This structure provides tax efficiency but reduced disclosure requirements relative to domestic filers.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

FY2025 adjusted net income was $269.5M ($5.79 adjusted basic EPS), down 47% from peak FY2024 ($512.9M/$10.57 EPS). However, Q4 2025 marked a rate inflection: revenue of $252.7M beat consensus by $12.3M, and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.62 slightly beat the $1.61 estimate.

The trajectory from Q4 2025 into 2026:
- Q4 2025 LR2 pool/spot TCE: $34,364/day
- Q1 2026 (97% booked): $51,000/day (+48%)
- Q2 2026 (16% booked): $101,000/day (+194% vs Q4)

Sell-side consensus estimates have moved sharply higher over recent months, driven by visible rate data rather than speculation. The upward revision trajectory is among the most aggressive in the market.

### Balance Sheet Transformation

The move from $293M net debt (Sep 2025) to $385M net cash (Mar 2026) — a $678M swing in six months — is exceptional for a shipping company. As of March 20, 2026, liquidity stood at $974M cash plus $748M in undrawn revolvers, totaling $1.72B of available liquidity against $589M gross debt. After pending vessel sales close, pro-forma net cash was estimated at ~$513M.

**Important update:** Since March 20, STNG has issued $375M in convertible senior notes (April 10, receiving ~$363.3M net proceeds), used $100M for share repurchases, and announced the sale of six additional vessels for $300M (expected to close Q2 2026). The current pro-forma capital structure reflects these April transactions — adding approximately $375M in gross debt from the convertible while also adding the $363.3M in net proceeds and the pending $300M in vessel sale proceeds. Net liquidity remains robust but the gross debt figure has increased from the March 20 snapshot.

STNG prepaid $154.6M of scheduled amortization through December 2027 in Q4 2025 alone, eliminating near-term refinancing risk entirely. The $375M convertible at 1.75% (conversion at $100.39) is potentially accretive financial engineering: $100M was used to buy back shares at $74.36, creating favorable economics if shares remain below the conversion price (cheap 1.75% financing) and still providing a buffer if shares rise (given the 35% premium over the repurchase price). However, if tanker rates fall materially, the company will have added senior unsecured debt while deploying $100M of cash for buybacks at potentially elevated levels.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is driven entirely by freight rate recovery. The path from $24,000-34,000/day fleet-average TCE in Q4 2025 to $32,000-101,000/day in Q1-Q2 2026 translates mechanically to EPS growth. With operating leverage on a largely fixed-cost vessel fleet, each incremental $1,000/day in TCE adds approximately $0.60-0.70 in **annual** EPS across the 87-vessel fleet (approximately $0.15-0.16 per quarter).

The growth question is sustainability. Three vectors support continued elevated rates:
1. **Geopolitical disruptions** — including sanctions on Russian petroleum products, enforcement actions, and regional shipping dislocations (including Iran/Hormuz-related disruptions) — maintain lengthened trade routes and tonne-mile demand
2. **Chinese refinery expansion** (1.3-1.5 Mb/d through decade-end) and Indian import growth provide structural demand growth
3. **Fleet aging** — 23% of all tankers over 20 years old supports replacement demand and potential scrapping

Against this: product tanker deliveries are reaching 15-year highs, with BIMCO explicitly forecasting supply/demand weakening through 2025-2026. Vessel deliveries from 2027 onward represent the medium-term headwind.

The VLCC entry ($256M for two vessels, H2 2028 delivery) adds optionality but also execution risk in a segment where STNG has no operating history.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.85 with approximately 50.4M shares outstanding (post-April repurchase), STNG has an estimated market capitalization of approximately $4.2B. Against estimated FY2026E EPS of approximately $9.88, the stock trades at roughly 8.4x current-year earnings.

Compared to relevant product tanker and tanker-sector peers:
- INSW (International Seaways — crude + product tankers): ~13.0x
- TORM (product tankers): comparable peer
- Ardmore Shipping (smaller product tankers): comparable peer

The discount to peers reflects the market's assessment that current rates are cyclically elevated rather than sustainable. This is rational — FY2024 peak earnings produced $13.78 EPS, and the market correctly refused to capitalize that at peer multiples.

However, the current discount may be excessive given: (1) the net cash position provides meaningful balance-sheet support, (2) the aggressive vessel sales lock in gains at current asset values, and (3) the geopolitical disruption supporting rates shows no near-term resolution path.

On normalized earnings (say $5-6 EPS representing mid-cycle rates), the stock trades at 14-17x — expensive for a shipping company at mid-cycle. The bull case requires rates to sustain above mid-cycle for 12-18 months, which the current geopolitical environment supports but cannot guarantee.

The trailing P/E of 11.8x on $7.03 diluted EPS (FY2025) is reasonable given demonstrated earnings power and balance sheet strength.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG's competitive position is strong and improving:

**Scale advantages:** 87 vessels provide commercial diversification, pool negotiating power, and operational efficiency that smaller operators (Ardmore, smaller MR owners) cannot match.

**Fleet quality:** Average age 10.2 years with scrubber-fitted tonnage provides 2-3 year fuel cost advantages over non-scrubber competitors. The active fleet renewal strategy (selling 10-12 year vessels, ordering newbuilds) keeps the fleet age competitive.

**Financial strength:** The net cash position is rare among shipping companies and provides unique strategic flexibility — STNG can acquire vessels or competitors in a downturn without dilution.

**Pool participation:** Commercial pool membership optimizes rate capture across vessel classes without the overhead of maintaining a full commercial organization.

STNG is using peak-cycle cash flows to delever and renew while competitors with weaker balance sheets cannot. When rates normalize, STNG's lower debt service and newer fleet will outperform.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Freight Rate Cyclicality (HIGH)**
Product tanker rates can fall 50%+ in 12-18 months during downturns. BIMCO and Lloyd's List explicitly warn of supply/demand weakening from 2025-2027 as deliveries reach 15-year highs. If rates return to $15,000-20,000/day levels, EPS falls to $2-3, making the current price expensive at 28-40x trough earnings.

**2. Geopolitical Normalization (MODERATE-HIGH)**
Potential Russian sanctions relief, Iran diplomatic resolution, or trade route normalization could reduce effective tonne-mile demand. The probability is low near-term but nonzero, and a resolution would compress rates and asset values simultaneously.

**3. Supply Inflection (MODERATE)**
The product tanker orderbook is delivering at the fastest pace in 15 years. If demand growth from Chinese refinery expansion and route lengthening fails to absorb new supply, utilization drops below the 90%+ levels currently supporting rates.

**4. VLCC Execution Risk (LOW-MODERATE)**
$256M committed to a segment where STNG has no operational track record. Crude tanker supply growth of 3.5% forecast for 2027 creates uncertainty about returns on these vessels at delivery.

**5. Convertible Dilution (LOW)**
$375M convertible at $100.39 conversion = ~3.7M potential shares (~7% dilution). Only triggered if shares exceed $100, which would reflect excellent business performance.

**6. Newbuilding Capital Commitments (MODERATE)**
As of Feb. 10, 2026, estimated installment payments for newbuildings (four LR2s, four MRs, and two VLCCs) totaled approximately $709.8M. These forward commitments will consume cash and could pressure the balance sheet if rates deteriorate significantly.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV has jumped from approximately 48% to 67% in recent days, entering steep backwardation (67% near-term vs. ~44% at 6-month). This reflects the market pricing Q1 2026 earnings (May 5) as a major catalyst with wide outcome distribution. Put/call volume and OI ratios suggest positioning is heavily skewed toward calls, consistent with bullish sentiment. The elevated put skew indicates some demand for downside protection, likely reflecting awareness that any geopolitical normalization would immediately compress rates.

The backwardation structure indicates the options market views earnings/geopolitical catalysts as near-term events with resolution over time, consistent with the cyclical nature of the business.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95-105)
Geopolitical disruption persists through 2026 with no normalization of trade routes. LR2 rates sustain $70,000-100,000/day through Q2-Q3. Full-year EPS reaches $10-12. Management deploys $500M+ cash toward accretive buybacks. Product tanker supply growth absorbed by tonne-mile increase. At 10x $10 EPS = $100.

### Bear Case ($55-65)
Geopolitical normalization (sanctions relief or trade route shifts) collapses product tanker rates to $20,000-25,000/day within 3-6 months. FY2026 EPS reverts to $4-5. Asset values decline 15-20%, erasing paper gains from fleet sales. At 13x $5 EPS = $65. Net cash position and balance-sheet strength provide meaningful support, though forward newbuilding commitments of ~$710M partially offset this cushion.

### Base Case ($90-95)
Elevated rates persist through mid-2026 but moderate in H2 as seasonal patterns and new deliveries absorb excess demand. FY2026 EPS of $8-9. Buybacks reduce share count by 3-5%. Dividend maintained/grown. At 10.5x $9 EPS = $94.50.

## Conclusion

STNG remains a BUY at $82.85 with a base case target of $92. The company has a strong balance sheet, management is demonstrably selling assets near peak values, and the geopolitical environment sustaining elevated rates — including Russian-product sanctions, enforcement actions, and broader regional disruptions — shows no near-term resolution. The estimate revision trajectory is exceptional and still catching up to visible rate data.

The valuation compression noted in the score trajectory (from 7.2 to 5.8) correctly reflects that the stock has moved from deep value territory to fair-to-modestly-cheap for its current earnings power. What prevents a higher valuation score is the cyclical nature: paying ~8.4x peak-ish earnings for a shipping company requires confidence that rates sustain, and supply data from 2027 onward creates genuine uncertainty. The balance-sheet strength (~$10/share in net cash as of March, though partially offset by ~$710M in forward newbuilding commitments and the April convertible issuance) and management's demonstrated ability to monetize assets near peaks provide meaningful downside support that distinguishes STNG from typical cyclical shipping exposure.

Position sizing should reflect the binary geopolitical component: any normalization of sanctions or trade routes would produce a rapid 20-25% drawdown as rate expectations reset. A 2-3% portfolio weight is appropriate given this tail risk.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The operating-leverage sensitivity was corrected from ~$0.60-0.70/quarter to ~$0.60-0.70/year per $1,000/day TCE; the peer comparison set contained non-tanker companies (TDW, OII, HP) and has been revised; the pro-forma balance sheet is stale after April convertible/buyback/vessel-sale transactions; and the "net cash hard floor" framing has been softened given ~$710M in forward newbuilding commitments. These corrections reduce the certainty of downside protection but do not invalidate the directional thesis.
