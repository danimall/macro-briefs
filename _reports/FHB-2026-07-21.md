**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Hawaiian is the oldest (founded 1858) and largest bank headquartered in Hawaii, controlling over 30% of total Hawaii deposits with a low-cost funding base: roughly $6.5B of $20.5B in deposits (32%) are noninterest-bearing, producing a cost of deposits of just 1.20%. Standalone, the franchise is over-earning and improving: FY2025 net income rose 20% to $276.3M ($2.20 diluted EPS), Q2 2026 preliminary results showed EPS of $0.60, ROA of 1.23%, ROTCE of 16.3%, and net interest margin expanding to 3.25%. Capital is strong at 13.17% CET1 and 14.42% total capital. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 6-7% over 90 days, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

On July 13, 2026, FHB announced an all-stock acquisition of California's TriCo Bancshares at a fixed 2.095 exchange ratio, creating a ~$34B multi-state Western U.S. bank in which FHB shareholders will own ~65%. This deal is the defining item in the analysis because it resolves FHB's central strategic constraint: because it controls more than 30% of Hawaii deposits, federal and state law prohibit it from acquiring another in-state bank, capping growth to organic-only in its home market. The market has long applied a "boxed-in" discount to FHB, with its 5-year TSR of 132.97 trailing the peer group's 152.71. TriCo provides a path out.

The complication is that the deal trades one concentration for another (Hawaii single-market risk for California CRE exposure), and its per-share economics are not yet public. Tangible-book-value dilution, EPS accretion, cost-synergy assumptions, and the tangible-book earnback period, the metrics that determine whether this transaction creates or destroys value, were not disclosed in the announcement press release. FHB shares fell on the news, the standard all-stock-acquirer reaction. Until those figures are confirmed, the risk/reward is genuinely two-sided, and we maintain a HOLD with a note that the score has now been anchored around 5.6-5.7 across four consecutive reports.

## Company Overview

First Hawaiian operates through its principal subsidiary, First Hawaiian Bank, with 49 branches (45 Hawaii, 3 Guam, 1 Saipan) and roughly 2,000 employees whose average tenure of 11.7 years signals a deep, sticky workforce. The bank runs two reportable segments, Retail Banking and Commercial Banking, plus a Corporate/Other Treasury function. Its product suite is full-service: deposits, C&I lending, commercial real estate and construction, residential mortgage, indirect auto finance, auto dealer flooring, credit cards and merchant processing, and wealth/trust management via Bishop Street Capital Management.

The competitive core is scale. FHB's Hawaii deposit share makes it a local price-setter and drives its structurally low funding cost. The franchise carries an "Outstanding" CRA rating and a 167-year brand. It was owned by BNP Paribas from 2001 until BNPP fully exited in February 2019 following the August 2016 IPO. A balance-sheet artifact of that history: the $995.5M of goodwill on the books dates entirely to BNPP's 2001 pushdown accounting, not to operating acquisitions; FHB, until now, had never been a buyer.

The TriCo transaction changes that. The combined entity would be a ~$34B, sixth-largest bank headquartered in the Western U.S., with Tri Counties Bank (est. 1975, Chico CA) retained as a mainland brand and no expected branch closures. Four TriCo directors, including its CEO Rick Smith, join FHB's board.

## Financial Analysis

FHB's profitability is strong and re-accelerating. The multi-year trend:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income ($M) | 265.7 | 235.0 | 230.1 | **276.3** |
| Diluted EPS | ~$2.07 | ~$1.84 | ~$1.79 | **$2.20** |
| Total assets ($B) | 24.6 | 24.9 | 23.8 | 24.0 |
| Stockholders' equity ($B) | 2.27 | 2.49 | 2.62 | **2.77** |
| Diluted shares (M) | 128.0 | 127.9 | 128.3 | **125.5** |

Diluted share count fell ~2%, reflecting buybacks. Pretax income climbed to $354M from $293M. The earnings driver is NIM expansion as funding costs stabilize: Q1 2026 NIM was 3.19%, expanding 6bps to 3.25% in Q2 2026, while cost of deposits improved from 1.22% to 1.20%. Q2 2026 preliminary results (reported 7/13, formal release 7/24) showed net income of $73.4M, ROA of 1.23% (+9bps QoQ), and ROTCE of 16.3% (up from 15.3%). Gross loans grew to $14.6B and tangible book value per share rose 3% QoQ to $15.04.

The balance sheet is a source of strength rather than concern. Total capital stands at 14.42% and Tier 1 leverage at 9.27%, comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. This excess capital is the currency and the cushion for the TriCo deal. FY2025 operating cash flow of $335M and FCF of ~$303M comfortably cover the dividend (current yield ~3.5%). The $5.6B securities book (mostly UST/agency) carries industry-standard AFS unrealized losses, but the capital base absorbs them.

Two points of caution on standardized screens: an Altman Z-Score is meaningless for a bank (structurally high leverage guarantees a "distress" reading), and the loan book is 75% real estate: $10.7B, of which CRE alone is $4.6B (32%). The relevant health markers (capital, ROA/ROTCE, NIM, deposit costs) are all healthy-to-strong. We score financial health at 6.8, reflecting genuine capital resilience and improving profitability, tempered by the coming step-up in share count from the all-stock deal, which converts FHB from a capital-returner to a capital-deployer.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth momentum is real and confirmed in the numbers. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year consensus EPS has risen to $2.38 (+6.4% over 90 days), next-year to $2.53 (+6.2%), with 30-day revisions still accelerating (+2.6% and +3.0% respectively). This is a positive earnings-momentum signal, though it falls well short of the >30% threshold that would flag a structural re-rating candidate; this is steady mid-single-digit improvement, not a trough recovery. The beat track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with the two mid-2025 quarters beating by 11.8% and 17.5%.

The structural growth question is the entire point of the TriCo deal. FHB's 10-K states plainly that further Hawaii growth "will most likely have to occur organically" because it cannot legally acquire in-state. Organic growth in a mature, tourism-and-military-levered island economy is inherently capped. TriCo removes that ceiling by giving FHB a California deposit franchise and a mainland platform for future M&A. The combined ~$34B entity has materially more room to compound.

The durability of that growth thesis depends on integration success and mainland credit performance, neither of which is proven, and both of which introduce execution risk that pure organic growth did not carry. Management's "preserve what works" framing (retained branding, no closures, both leadership teams represented) reduces integration disruption risk but also signals a lighter cost-take-out, which lowers the synergy-driven accretion. We score growth prospects at 5.8, an upgrade from the prior 5.0 that reflects both the positive revision trend and the strategic unlock, held below 6.0 because the deal's accretion economics are unquantified and Financials remains a negative-alpha sector by default requiring a distinct company catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

FHB trades at ~12.6x trailing and ~12x forward earnings (on the $2.38 current-year estimate), essentially in line with the regional-bank peer median of 13.7x:

| CVBF | 13.7 | 3.8% |
| PRK | 16.3 | 2.5% |
| FFBC | 11.2 | 3.2% |
| BANF | 15.6 | 1.7% |
| **Peer median** | **13.7** | **2.7%** |
| **FHB** | **~12.6** | **3.5%** |

At ~12x forward against a 13.7x peer median, with a superior deposit franchise (32% noninterest-bearing, 16.3% ROTCE), FHB is not expensive on quality-adjusted terms, and its 3.5% yield sits above the peer median. The modest discount is not a mispricing so much as the market's pricing of two offsetting facts: high franchise quality on one side, and integration/dilution uncertainty plus a historical no-growth discount on the other. The stock has re-rated toward the peer median because the TriCo deal is expected to relieve the growth cap.

A near-peer multiple with above-peer yield reads as "cheap" on a screen, but cheapness in the regional-bank complex has historically sorted the wrong way: the discount typically reflects a reason (here, the fixed exchange ratio exposes FHB holders to TriCo performance risk until close, and the deal's per-share economics are undisclosed). The analyst community is cautious rather than enthusiastic: current ratings are 7 Hold, 2 Sell, 2 Strong Sell, with zero Buys, and price targets cluster tightly (low $28, high $32, mean $30.11). Piper Sandler set its target at $29. With the stock at $28.86, consensus mean upside is roughly 4%, and the target band is narrow enough to suggest limited near-term asymmetry. We score valuation at 5.0: fairly valued, not a value opportunity, with the deal economics the swing factor that could move this either direction once disclosed.

## Competitive Landscape

Locally, FHB is dominant. Its deposit share makes it a price-setter and underpins its low funding cost, and the brand plus "Outstanding" CRA rating create durable customer stickiness. That same dominance is the source of its growth ceiling. The trajectory is shifting from entrenched but capped to diversifying but exposed.

Industry-wide, regional banks operate under heightened post-2023 scrutiny of CRE concentration, uninsured deposits, and unrealized securities losses. FHB's high noninterest-bearing mix and strong capital position it relatively well versus stressed peers. The 10-K also flags newer structural threats: the GENIUS Act stablecoin framework (potential deposit disintermediation), AI-enabled fraud and competition, captive auto-finance companies pressuring the dealer-flooring franchise (top-5 dealers are ~40% of flooring commitments), and fintech/private-credit lenders.

The TriCo deal positions FHB as an active consolidator in the regional-bank M&A wave rather than a passive dividend-and-buyback story. Whether that improves its competitive standing depends on execution: it gains geographic diversification and a mainland platform, but it enters California, a large, cyclical CRE market under regulatory watch, as a newcomer. We score competitive position at 5.8, a modest decline from 6.0, reflecting that the local moat remains intact while the pivot introduces execution risk and dilutes the pure-play quality that the market rewarded.

## Risk Assessment

The defining pre-deal risk was extreme geographic concentration: a substantial majority of business is levered to Hawaii tourism, U.S. military spending, and the Japanese economy/yen, with acute natural-disaster exposure (the 10-K cites the August 2023 Maui wildfires and May 2023 Guam typhoon). The TriCo deal is a deliberate response to this risk, but it trades the risk rather than eliminating it, substituting California CRE and economic exposure for some island concentration. Net risk reduction is plausible but not automatic.

Real-estate concentration is high on both sides, and TriCo brings additional California CRE; management explicitly notes regulator concern over office/CRE weakness. Other structural risks include holdco liquidity dependence on Bank dividends, dealer-flooring concentration, and the residual BWHI tax-sharing exposure (~$92M).

The largest deal-specific risk is unquantified per-share economics. The fixed 2.095 exchange ratio means FHB shareholders bear TriCo's performance risk until close (expected end of 2026), and the tangible-book dilution, earnback period, and EPS accretion assumptions were not disclosed. A long earnback (say, 4+ years) with meaningful TBV dilution would be a materially worse outcome than the market currently assumes; a short earnback with credible synergies would justify a re-rating. Regulatory approval for crossing into a new state at materially larger scale adds a binary approval risk. We score risk factors at 5.0: the balance sheet quality caps downside, but the undisclosed deal economics and the concentration-swap represent genuine idiosyncratic risk not yet fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 43.0% to 37.5% over the two weeks following the deal announcement even as the stock dipped 3.8%, indicating the initial event-driven uncertainty is being absorbed rather than escalating. This is consistent with an all-stock-acquirer settling into a wait-and-see period pending the formal Q2 release (July 24) and eventual deal-deck disclosure. There is no elevated put skew or unusual positioning signaling informed bearish bets; options sentiment is neutral, neither confirming nor challenging the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$34-36).** The TriCo deal closes on schedule with modest TBV dilution and a sub-3-year earnback, and FHB re-rates toward a 15-16x multiple as the market retires the no-growth discount now that the in-state cap is circumvented. Standalone momentum continues: NIM expands past 3.30%, deposit costs keep falling, ROTCE holds at 16%+, and estimate revisions stay positive. The combined ~$34B franchise becomes a compounding Western U.S. platform with mainland M&A optionality. This roughly aligns with the high analyst target of $32 plus post-close re-rating.

**Bear case (~$22-24).** The deal deck reveals meaningful TBV dilution (say, 8-12%) with a 4+ year earnback, regulatory approval slips or attaches conditions, and California CRE deteriorates into a soft mainland economy. Integration stumbles, cost synergies disappoint given the "preserve what works" light-touch approach, and FHB's pure-play quality premium erodes without the offsetting growth actually materializing. The stock reverts toward its 52-week low of $22.65.

The base case sits at fair value near current levels. Standalone earnings power supports ~$28-30 at a peer multiple, and the deal's outcome is the swing factor in either direction. With the per-share economics undisclosed, we cannot confidently underwrite the upside or rule out the downside, which is why this remains a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD whose rationale hinges on data not yet public.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Merger investor deck (8-K exhibit) confirms tangible-book dilution below ~6% with an earnback period under 3 years and mid-single-digit-or-better EPS accretion.
- Regulatory approval progresses cleanly with no material conditions, de-risking the end-2026 close.
- Standalone NIM expands beyond 3.30% with continued positive estimate revisions (>+3% over subsequent 90 days), confirming the earnings engine independent of the deal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Deal deck reveals TBV dilution above ~10% with a 4+ year earnback, or the deal is renegotiated/terminated.
- California CRE credit metrics at TriCo deteriorate materially before close, or FHB's own CRE (32% of loans) shows rising non-performers.
- ROTCE falls below 13% or NIM contracts for two consecutive quarters, signaling the standalone earnings tailwind has reversed.

**Reassessment timeline:** Near-term at the July 24 formal Q2 release and the eventual merger investor-deck disclosure; the decisive checkpoint is regulatory approval progress and the expected end-2026 close. The score has clustered at 5.6-5.7 across four consecutive reports with an unchanged HOLD; the bar for maintaining that call unchanged is now the disclosure of deal economics, which will justify moving the rating in either direction.

## Conclusion

First Hawaiian is a high-quality regional bank: fortress capital (13.2% CET1), an elite low-cost deposit franchise (32% noninterest-bearing), 16.3% ROTCE, expanding margins, and rising estimates. It trades at a reasonable ~12x forward against peers, with an above-median 3.5% yield. On standalone fundamentals alone, it is a defensible holding.

The TriCo acquisition transforms the investment case from a stable-but-capped income story into a growth-and-integration story, and it does so by solving the strategic bind that has weighed on FHB's valuation for years. But the deal's per-share economics, the tangible-book dilution and earnback that determine whether it creates or destroys value, are not yet disclosed, and the concentration risk is being traded rather than eliminated. The Street's posture (zero Buys, tight target band, cautious reaffirmations) mirrors this uncertainty. We rate FHB a HOLD with an overall score of 5.7, and we are prepared to move in either direction once the merger deck quantifies the currently undisclosed economics.
