**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bank of Hawaii is a 128-year-old regional bank that dominates the insular, high-barrier Hawaii market alongside First Hawaiian and Central Pacific. The investment case rests on a single mechanism: a net interest margin recovery driven by 175 bps of Fed cuts between September 2024 and December 2025 that has repriced the bank's high-cost deposit base downward faster than its asset yields have declined. NIM expanded to 2.74% in Q1 2026, the eighth consecutive quarter of expansion, with management targeting roughly 2.9% by year-end. Net income rose 37% in FY2025 to $205.9M and 31% year-over-year in Q1 2026 to $57.4M, with diluted EPS of $1.30 versus $0.97 a year earlier. Credit quality is pristine: nonperforming assets are just 0.09% of loans and annualized net charge-offs are 0.03%.

The problem is price. BOH has appreciated 26% off its 52-week low to close at $84.75, near a 52-week high, trading at roughly 17x trailing earnings, a meaningful premium to the regional-bank peer median of 13.6x, including local comparable First Hawaiian at 12.3x. The margin recovery that the multiple now embeds is real but asymmetrically dependent on continued rate declines: management deliberately reduced pay-fixed swap notional by $300M, increasing fixed-rate asset exposure and effectively wagering that rates keep falling. That wager is not costless. Estimate revisions have turned modestly negative across the current-quarter, next-quarter, and next-year windows, and one sell-side analyst has already downgraded the common in favor of the 8.0% Series B preferred.

We rate BOH a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted from 6.0 in April to 5.5 in June on valuation compression, not fundamental deterioration. The bank is a high-quality, defensively positioned franchise with a well-covered 3.3% dividend, but the risk/reward on the common after a 26% run is roughly balanced-to-full. Own it for yield and stability; the appreciation case requires the ~2.9% NIM target to land while rates cooperate, and the market has largely paid for that outcome already.

## Company Overview

Bank of Hawaii Corporation is the Delaware-incorporated holding company for Bank of Hawaii, founded in 1897 and headquartered in Honolulu. In January 2026 it elected financial holding company status, expanding its permissible activities. The bank serves Hawaii, Guam, and other Pacific Islands through three segments:

- **Consumer Banking** (~$8.4B assets, $31.9M Q1'26 net income): retail deposits, residential mortgage, home equity, auto, private banking, and trust/wealth, delivered through 52 branches and 319 ATMs.
- **Commercial Banking** (~$6.2B assets, $39.1M Q1'26 net income, the largest profit contributor): C&I, commercial real estate, lease financing, auto dealer financing, cash management, and an international banking unit serving Japanese, Korean, and Chinese-owned businesses.
- **Treasury & Other** (net loss segment): the securities portfolio and asset-liability management functions.

The competitive foundation is a deep, low-cost, sticky core deposit base tied to local relationships and brand, a moat that is simultaneously the source of the bank's most important structural risk. Ninety-three percent of loans are Hawaii-based, and real estate (residential mortgage 33.9% plus commercial mortgage 29.9%) accounts for roughly 64% of the loan book.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant story is the NIM recovery cycle. Having been compressed during 2022–2024 by high-cost deposits sitting against a low-yielding legacy securities book, the margin is now rising as falling rates lower deposit costs while asset yields grind higher.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $171.2M | $150.0M | $205.9M (+37%) | $57.4M (+31% YoY) |
| ROE / ROCE | 13.9% | 10.9% | 13.3% | 13.9% |
| NIM | — | ~2.16% | ~2.45% | 2.74% (+42 bps YoY) |

Q1 2026 net interest income reached $151.0M, up 20% year-over-year, driven by a 42 bps decline in the cost of interest-bearing liabilities. Savings cost fell from 2.23% to 1.81% and time-deposit cost from 3.67% to 2.89%, while earning-asset yields rose to 4.03%. Management has now guided to overhead/expense growth of 2.5%–3%, a constructive operating-leverage setup if the revenue trajectory holds.

The balance sheet and credit quality are the clearest strengths. Total assets are ~$23.9B, loans ~$14.2B, and deposits ~$21.0B. NPAs are $12.1M (0.09% of loans), annualized net charge-offs 0.03% (down from 0.13%), and allowance/loans steady at 1.04%. The deposit mix is high quality, with ~$5.7B (27%) noninterest-bearing. Loan growth is modest (+0.8% QoQ), led by commercial mortgage (+3.2%).

Capital is solid: CET1 12.06%, Total Capital 15.44%, Tier 1 Leverage 8.62%. The dividend is held at $0.70/quarter (~3.3% yield), supplemented by two preferred series (Series A 4.375%, Series B 8.000%). Buybacks resumed modestly at $15.1M in Q1'26, with $105.9M of authorization remaining.

One flag deserves prominence: BOH holds a $7.9B securities book with ~$777M of net unrealized losses (AFS + HTM) as of Q1'26. The HTM book alone carries $613.7M of gross unrealized losses ($3.55B fair value vs. $4.16B carrying). These losses are excluded from regulatory capital but suppress tangible book value materially; AOCI stands at –$247M. This is not a solvency issue given deposit stability and $7.8B Fed plus $2.2B FHLB contingent borrowing capacity, but it represents locked-in economic loss absent a favorable rate move, and it is the crux of the bull/bear debate. Management is effectively riding these securities to maturity while the funding-cost tailwind repairs reported earnings.

A note on financial-health screening: any automated pipeline flagging BOH via Altman Z-Score (~0.16, "distress") is producing a false signal. That model is structurally inappropriate for banks, where high leverage is normal. On bank-appropriate metrics BOH is well-capitalized with pristine credit.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is cyclical margin recovery, not structural expansion. Organic loan growth is low-single-digit, constrained by Hawaii's slow, uneven economy (2.6% unemployment, soft tourism, construction supported by the Maui rebuild and military projects). The earnings recovery is entirely a rate-and-curve story: funding costs reprice down faster than assets, and the legacy low-yield securities book runs off slowly into higher reinvestment yields.

The forward picture is mixed. Estimate revisions have turned modestly negative: current-quarter consensus of $1.46 is down 1.7% over 90 days, next-quarter $1.54 down 1.9%, and next-year $6.87 down 0.7% (and down 1.4% in the last 30 days). Current-year consensus of $6.01 is essentially flat. This is not the +30% estimate-revision inflection that flags a strong buy signal; it is a business whose recovery is maturing and where consensus has stopped rising. The earnings-surprise record reinforces caution: two beats, one in-line, and a Q1'26 miss (-3.3%, $1.30 vs. $1.34 estimate) in the last four quarters, breaking the prior beat cadence.

The credible upside driver is delivery of the ~2.9% year-end 2026 NIM target combined with the lowered expense outlook, which would produce positive operating leverage and support consensus EPS moving from $6.01 (2026) toward $6.87 (2027). But that path requires benign rate conditions, and the estimate momentum is not corroborating it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $84.75, BOH trades at roughly 17x trailing EPS of $4.63 and roughly 14x consensus 2026 EPS of $6.01. The forward multiple is more defensible than the trailing figure, but the peer comparison is unfavorable on both:

| FHB | First Hawaiian | 12.3 | 3.7% |

| CVBF | CVB Financial | 13.7 | 3.8% |
| — | **Peer median** | **13.6** | **3.2%** |

BOH commands a ~26% P/E premium to the peer median and a ~39% premium to its direct local comparable, First Hawaiian, which offers a higher 3.7% yield. A premium is partly justified by BOH's superior deposit franchise, pristine credit, and the visible NIM recovery, but that is a full premium for a bank whose forward estimates are drifting down rather than up.

The cycle-adjustment argument does not rescue the valuation. BOH is past the trough (this is the eighth quarter of NIM expansion, not the first), so the trailing multiple is not artificially inflated by depressed earnings. Normalized earnings power is already largely reflected in the current price. Analyst targets frame the setup: mean $87.83, median $87.00, high $95.00, low $82.00. The current price sits above the low target and only ~4% below the mean, with the stock trading near a 52-week high. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors. Here, with estimates falling and the recovery maturing, the targets are more likely to be accurate ceilings.

We assign a valuation score of 4.3. This reflects a name trading at a premium to peers with no independent catalyst to justify further re-rating and modestly negative earnings momentum. The business is sound, so this is not a value trap in the impairment sense, but the multiple offers no asymmetric upside at current levels. The valuation dimension has historically sorted the wrong way when "cheap" is treated as a buy signal; here the opposite condition holds, and the premium is the constraint.

## Competitive Landscape

BOH operates in a concentrated, oligopolistic Hawaii banking market with high barriers to entry from geography, brand, and relationships. This produces a low-cost, stable deposit franchise but caps organic growth. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving: the deposit franchise remains durable, credit performance is best-in-class, and the merchant-services divestiture in Q4 2025 simplified the business (at the cost of a 24% year-over-year decline in fees/exchange income). Commercial Banking, the largest profit contributor, is growing commercial mortgage balances at a measured pace.

The pay-versus-performance disclosure surfaces a competitive tell: cumulative TSR of 109 has materially lagged the peer group's 153 over the measurement window. The market has been slower to reward BOH than peers, consistent with the securities-loss and NIM-repair overhang. That lag can be read two ways: either the recovery is not yet fully priced (bull), or the market correctly discounts a franchise with structural single-geography concentration and a rate-dependent earnings thesis (bear). Given the stock's recent 26% run near a 52-week high, the market has now closed much of that gap.

We score competitive position 6.3, reflecting a genuine moat with stable trajectory but limited structural growth and a single-market franchise whose strength is inseparable from its concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic and real estate concentration.** Loans are 93% Hawaii-based, with real estate ~64% of the book. A Hawaii-specific shock (tourism downturn, real estate correction, or natural disaster) would hit credit quality and collateral values simultaneously. Within the $4.3B commercial mortgage book, office exposure is low (~8%, $331M), but lodging (15%, $646M) and multi-family (28%) are tied to tourism dynamics.

**Unrealized securities losses.** The ~$777M net unrealized loss ($613.7M gross on HTM) is the key hidden vulnerability. It is not a solvency risk given granular, stable deposits and ample contingent liquidity, but it suppresses tangible book and would crystallize the SVB-style loss mechanism only under a deposit run, a low-probability but high-severity tail.

**Asymmetric interest-rate exposure.** Per Q1'26 disclosures, a –300 bps shock reduces NII 5.3% while EVE falls 11% at +200 bps. Having cut pay-fixed swap notional by $300M, BOH now carries more fixed-rate asset exposure, a directional bet that rates fall. Tariff-driven inflation (explicitly flagged for import-dependent Hawaii) is the specific tail risk to the NIM thesis. If the Fed holds or hikes, the margin recovery stalls and securities losses widen.

**CEO succession.** The most important recent qualitative development is the transition from Peter Ho (long-tenured Chairman & CEO) to James Polk, who signed the Q1'26 10-Q as President and CEO. The handoff was telegraphed via 8-Ks in February 2026 and confirmed by external reporting. It appears orderly and internally sourced, a governance positive, but it post-dates the proxy, and the elevated cadence of Item 5.02 filings across 2024–2026 warrants monitoring for strategic continuity, particularly whether the new CEO alters the conservative, dividend-focused capital posture.

**Hawaii-specific foreclosure friction** (appellate rulings complicating nonjudicial foreclosure, judicial backlog) can lengthen loss recognition, and dependence on federal/military spending was strained by the Oct–Nov 2025 government shutdown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 31.8% against 1-year historical vol of 24.7%, rich by 7.1 points, indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is 7.4% (elevated put demand), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 31.8% > long-term 29.2%), signaling the options market anticipates imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst. The implied one-month move is ±6.9%. This positioning is a modest yellow flag against the current near-52-week-high price: hedging demand is skewed toward downside protection even as the stock sits near its highs. Short interest is negligible at 0.2% of float, so there is no squeeze dynamic; the bearish tilt is genuine hedging, not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The NIM recovery has further to run. Management's ~2.9% year-end 2026 target combined with the lowered 2.5%–3% expense-growth outlook produces positive operating leverage. Continued Fed cuts repriced deposit costs faster than asset yields, driving EPS toward the $6.87 consensus for 2027. Pristine credit (0.09% NPAs) means provisioning stays low. The peer-lagging TSR (109 vs 153) suggests residual re-rating room if the target lands. Bull case: ~$95 (the high analyst target), roughly +12% from current.

### Bear Case
The recovery is priced. At 17x trailing and a ~26% peer premium, with estimates falling and a Q1 miss, the easy money in the common has been made, an assessment the sell-side already reached in downgrading the common in favor of the 8% preferred. If sticky inflation forces the Fed to hold or hike, the NIM recovery stalls and the increased fixed-rate exposure becomes a liability, while the $777M securities loss widens and further suppresses tangible book. Hawaii's soft tourism and federal-spending dependence add cyclical downside. Bear case: ~$70–72, a de-rating toward the peer median forward multiple on flat-to-lower earnings, roughly -15% to -17% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income-hold).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings release (Q2 2026, expected late July), which will test the ~2.9% NIM trajectory and the falling-estimate trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM reaches or exceeds ~2.85% in Q2/Q3 2026 with estimate revisions turning positive across the current-year and next-year windows.
- A pullback toward the high-$60s to low-$70s that restores a peer-comparable forward multiple and widens the yield.
- Evidence the new CEO is deploying the $105.9M buyback authorization aggressively at attractive prices, signaling capital-allocation confidence.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Fed shifts to holding or hiking, stalling the NIM recovery and widening securities losses; next-year EPS estimates cut more than 5%.
- A Hawaii-specific credit event (tourism-driven lodging/multi-family CRE deterioration) pushing NPAs above ~0.30%.
- A dividend action or capital-posture change under the new CEO that undermines the income thesis.

The dividend at ~3.3%, well-covered by a payout well below earnings, is the durable return; own the name for yield and franchise stability, not for appreciation from near a 52-week high.

## Conclusion

Bank of Hawaii is a conservatively run, credit-clean, well-capitalized franchise executing a genuine rate-driven NIM recovery. The fundamentals are sound and the dividend is safe. The constraint is entirely valuation and timing: the common has run 26% to near a fresh 52-week high, carries a ~26% P/E premium to peers, and faces modestly negative estimate revisions while the earnings thesis remains asymmetrically dependent on continued rate declines in an environment where inflation persistence is a live risk. The recovery is largely priced. Options positioning tilts toward downside hedging, and the sell-side has begun preferring the 8% preferred to the common.

The score of 5.4 continues a gentle decline from 6.0, driven by valuation compression rather than fundamental deterioration. This is a fourth consecutive HOLD, and after three prior identical calls the bar for maintaining it unchanged is deliberately high; the current data (a Q1 miss, falling revisions, a premium multiple near a 52-week high) supports rather than challenges the HOLD. We would become buyers on a pullback that restores peer-comparable valuation, or on confirmation that the ~2.9% NIM target is landing with estimates turning up. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced, and the appropriate action is to hold for the yield and reassess at the next print.
