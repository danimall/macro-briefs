**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

IAC has spent the last fifteen months dismantling the serial-incubator model it operated for three decades. The Angi spin-off (March 2025), the Care.com sale (March 2026, $295.7M net), and the shutdown of the entire Search segment (April 2026, following Google's non-renewal of the 2015 Services Agreement) have left a company that is effectively three assets stapled to a corporate shell: the People Inc. digital-and-print publisher (formerly Dotdash Meredith), a ~26.1% stake in MGM Resorts carried at roughly $2.47B, and a ~33% stake in Turo carried near $405M. The parent will rename itself "People Incorporated" by August 2026 and hand the reins to People Inc. CEO Neil Vogel, confirming that the holding company is collapsing into its one operating business.

The dominant fact for any current investor is the June 1, 2026 non-binding proposal by IAC to take MGM Resorts private at $48.30/share, an ~$18B enterprise value, buying the ~74% of MGM it does not own at roughly a 10.6% premium. The market's split reaction is the tell: MGM rose ~15% while IAC rose ~1%. Investors are skeptical that a $3.1B-market-cap company creates value by paying up for the rest of a company several times its size, a deal that would require substantial debt and/or outside equity partners, executed as a related-party transaction with Barry Diller sitting on both sides. This is a proposal, not a signed deal, and its financing and structure are unresolved.

Underneath the corporate machinery, the operating story is mixed. People Inc.'s Digital segment is growing (revenue +8% YoY, Adjusted EBITDA +20% to $49.9M, non-session revenue +24%) and offsetting a structural print decline, but Core Sessions fell 17% because of Google AI Overviews, a pressure on session-based advertising that management explicitly expects to continue. The stock has run ~20% since our last review and now sits near its 52-week high ($46.33), currently at $46.75, closing much of the SOTP discount the restructuring was designed to unlock. We move to a HOLD (uncertain), reflecting a binary setup where the upside case (deal creates value or discount closes cleanly) and downside case (overpayment/over-leverage, continued session erosion) are both live and unresolved.

## Company Overview

IAC, chaired and controlled by Barry Diller through Class B voting shares, now describes itself as primarily comprising "leading publisher People Inc. and its strategic equity positions in MGM Resorts International and Turo Inc."

**People Inc.** is one of the largest US digital-and-print publishers, reaching 175M+ people monthly across 40+ brands including *People, Better Homes & Gardens, Verywell, Food & Wine, Allrecipes, Real Simple, Investopedia,* and *Southern Living.* It runs two segments:
- **Digital** (~$253M Q1 revenue, growing): advertising, performance marketing (affiliate commerce), and licensing, home to D/Cipher, a cookieless intent-based ad-targeting product.
- **Print** (~$138M Q1 revenue, declining ~16% YoY): magazine subscriptions, newsstand, and print advertising, in managed secular decline.

**Non-operating stakes:**
- **MGM Resorts:** 66.8M shares (~26.1%), fair value ~$2.47B, roughly 36% of IAC's total assets.
- **Turo:** ~33% of the private peer-to-peer car-sharing platform, carried near $405M.
- **Emerging & Other:** Vivian Health, The Daily Beast, IAC Films.

The company's capital structure is unusual and central to the analysis: all $1.44B of debt is ring-fenced at People Inc., while the HoldCo carries ~$797M of unrestricted cash and no debt. Covenants can restrict IAC's ability to freely access People Inc.'s cash, so parent and subsidiary liquidity must be assessed separately.

## Financial Analysis

Standard year-over-year comparisons on the consolidated entity are close to meaningless after three restructurings in fifteen months. Consolidated figures are best read alongside the segment and parent-level detail.

**Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025 (continuing operations):**

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $422.9M | $481.7M | −12%, largely Search collapse |
| People Inc. revenue | $385.7M | $393.1M | −2% (Digital +8%, Print −16%) |
| Search revenue | $17.1M | $70.3M | −76%, segment being shut down |
| Operating income (loss) | ($40.1M) | $24.1M | Swing driven by SBC + restructuring |
| Total Adjusted EBITDA | $2.7M | $36.4M | −93% |
| Net loss (continuing) | ($2.3M) | ($258.3M) | 2025 hit by $324M MGM markdown |
| Cash & equivalents | $1.11B | — | $796.7M HoldCo, $315.8M People Inc. |
| Total debt | $1.44B | — | 100% at People Inc. |

Several dynamics matter more than the headline numbers:

- **Seasonality is extreme.** Roughly 50% of annual Adjusted EBITDA is generated in Q4; Q1 is the structurally weakest quarter. Annualizing Q1 would badly understate the operating base.
- **The operating loss is largely optical.** The swing was driven by a +$37.8M year-over-year move in stock-based compensation — Q1 2025 booked a one-time $49.8M reversal of a forfeited former-CEO award — plus ~$10M of new restructuring severance. Underlying Digital economics improved.
- **Digital is the bright spot.** Digital Adjusted EBITDA rose 20% to $49.9M, with non-session revenue up 24% (Apple News+, a Q4 2025 Meta content partnership, and AI/LLM content licensing) offsetting the 17% decline in Core Sessions.
- **The parent is a cash consumer.** Consolidated operating cash flow was +$10.0M in Q1, but excluding People Inc. the HoldCo burned $45.7M. People Inc. is the sole operating cash generator, and its cash sits behind a covenant fence.
- **Debt at People Inc.** comprises Term Loan A-1 (SOFR+2.00% ≈5.67%), Term Loan B-2 (≈7.17%), and 7.625% 2032 Notes. This is a leveraged operating business inside an otherwise unlevered parent.

**Capital allocation** signals conviction in the SOTP discount: IAC repurchased $123.8M (3.3M shares at ~$37.71) in Q1 2026 on top of $179M a year earlier, with 2.5M shares remaining under the 2025 authorization. Buying below where the MGM stake plus net HoldCo cash arguably cover the entire market cap is consistent with management's "unlock value" strategy.

We hold financial health at 5.5. The parent balance sheet is genuinely strong (~$797M cash, zero HoldCo debt, a marketable ~$2.47B MGM stake), and this is the reliable downside filter: the resources exist to absorb stress. But the ring-fenced $1.44B of subsidiary debt, the HoldCo cash burn, and the possibility of a large debt-financed MGM acquisition prevent a higher score.

## Growth Analysis

The consolidated growth picture is negative and the estimate revisions confirm it: current-year EPS consensus has swung from +$0.49 (90 days ago) to −$0.54, and current-quarter estimates fell 113% over 30 days. Read literally, this is sharply negative earnings momentum. But the revisions are contaminated by the same non-operating noise that distorts GAAP: the MGM mark-to-market alone can move reported EPS by $133.6M per $2.00 MGM share change, and the Search wind-down plus consolidation charges (~$63M total: $14M severance, $48M SBC) are one-time. Next-year consensus, which looks past these items, is roughly flat-to-slightly-up at $1.17.

The genuine growth question is whether People Inc.'s Digital pivot outruns the session erosion. The non-session revenue and Digital EBITDA growth are real and encouraging, but Core Sessions are declining because of Google AI Overviews, and management expects that pressure to continue. Print is in permanent decline. The pivot to licensing, D/Cipher cookieless targeting, and direct-sold premium advertising is working partially, but it is a race against a structural headwind, not a clean growth story.

Growth prospects score 4.5. The Digital momentum is credible and deserves recognition, but the combination of AI-driven session decline in the core business, print secularity, and the absence of a broad-based revenue growth thesis keeps this dimension below neutral. Weighting growth heavily, as the durable evidence favors, argues against treating IAC as a growth vehicle: its forward return depends on asset-value crystallization, not compounding.

## Valuation Assessment

IAC must be valued sum-of-the-parts; consolidated multiples (25.9x trailing, 16.3x forward) are artifacts of the restructuring noise. The rough parts:
- MGM stake: ~$2.47B at fair value (and the $48.30 proposal implies ~$3.2B if it clears, though that reflects a premium IAC would pay, not receive).
- HoldCo net cash: ~$797M.
- Turo: ~$405M carrying value.
- People Inc.: a scaled publisher generating meaningful Digital EBITDA, encumbered by $1.44B of debt.

At a $3.1B market cap, the MGM stake plus net HoldCo cash approximate the entire equity value, leaving the market assigning little-to-negative value to People Inc. and Turo. This is the classic IAC holding-company discount, and it is the whole reason the restructuring, rename, and buybacks exist. The peer table is not useful here: Internet Content peers (Z at 134x, BIDU negative, NBIS at 77x) are not comparable to a publisher-plus-casino-stake holdco.

Two things temper the "cheap" read. First, the stock has already re-rated ~20% and sits near its 52-week high, so a meaningful portion of the discount has closed since our last review; the valuation score should not simply extrapolate the prior +2.0 jump to 7.5. Second, the primary catalyst for further closure (the MGM deal) is double-edged: if IAC pays the premium and levers up to buy MGM, it converts a discounted passive stake into an owned, indebted operating asset, and the market has already voted skeptically. Cheapness here is real but is a value-trap risk absent clean catalyst resolution, the pattern where a low headline multiple has historically sorted the wrong way.

We set valuation at 6.5. There is a genuine, identifiable SOTP discount and an under-modeled Google antitrust option (see below), which justify an above-neutral score. But the discount has partially closed on the run to current levels, and the deal path that would close the rest carries as much downside as upside.

## Competitive Landscape

People Inc. is a scaled premium publisher, but its competitive trajectory is deteriorating in the one metric that funds the business. Google AI Overviews and chatbot answers reduce click-throughs to publisher sites (the "Google zero-click" problem), and management directly attributes the Core Sessions decline to it, expecting continued pressure on session-based advertising. Diller's own framing of the MGM thesis, that he sought "real world assets AI cannot easily replicate or disintermediate," is an implicit admission that his publishing business can be disintermediated.

The counter-strategy is a shift toward AI-resilient revenue: content licensing to LLMs and Apple News+, cookieless intent targeting via D/Cipher, and direct-sold premium advertising. The non-session revenue growth shows this working at the margin. Whether it can fully offset session decline over multiple years is unproven. MGM sits in a consolidating gaming sector (following the Fertitta/Caesars precedent) with BetMGM digital optionality, but IAC's exposure there is as a financial holder, not an operator, until and unless the take-private closes.

Competitive position scores 4.0. The trajectory is negative in the core business (session share ceding to AI search), the moat is compressing, and the offsetting pivot is early-stage. This is not a company gaining share or widening a moat.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI search disintermediation (existential).** Core Sessions decline on Google AI Overviews, explicitly expected to continue. This is the single biggest threat to People Inc.'s economics.
2. **Google concentration and Search shutdown.** Google terminated the 2015 Services Agreement effective April 30, 2026; the entire Search segment moves to discontinued operations in Q2 2026. Total Google-related revenue was still $38.5M in Q1 2026 (Google revenue fell from 20% to 9% of total revenue YoY).
3. **MGM concentration / mark-to-market volatility.** ~36% of assets is a single equity stake; a $2.00 MGM move swings GAAP results by $133.6M, unrelated to operations. The $324M swing between Q1'25 and Q1'26 net loss illustrates the noise.
4. **The MGM take-private is a binary, related-party event.** An ~$18B enterprise-value deal by a $3.1B-cap company requires major debt and/or partners. Diller sits on both sides (recusing from MGM board actions). A new April 2026 Voting Agreement caps combined Diller/IAC voting power above 25.73% by requiring proportional voting of "excess" shares. Financing structure is the central unresolved question, and overpayment/over-leverage is the primary downside.
5. **Controlled-company governance.** Class B voting control limits public-holder influence, sharpened by the related-party MGM proposal.
6. **Print secular decline and input costs** (paper/postage).
7. **Consolidation execution.** ~$63M of cost to collapse the HoldCo into People Inc. by Q1 2027, alongside CFO/COO and Chief Legal Officer departures.

**Underappreciated positive optionality:** People Inc.'s August 2025 ad-tech antitrust suit against Google in the SDNY MDL benefits from an October 2025 collateral-estoppel ruling precluding Google from relitigating that it illegally monopolized publisher ad-server and ad-exchange markets. Treble damages are sought; the case is in expert discovery. Timing and quantum are unknowable, but this is a genuine call option most screens miss.

### Options Market Signal

No dated options chain was provided, but the short-interest picture is informative: 19.0% of float short, 5.7 days to cover, 8.28M shares short. This is elevated institutional bearish positioning, consistent with skepticism about the MGM deal creating value and with the AI-disintermediation thesis on People Inc. It also creates modest squeeze potential if the deal is structured favorably or abandoned in a way that leaves the discounted holdco intact. The elevated SI is a divergence worth flagging against the improving analyst sentiment (mean target $52.18, all targets above the current price).

Risk factors score 4.0. The MGM take-private is a live binary with asymmetric structure risk, the AI-session decline is a specific and quantified catalyst for downside, and controlled-company governance limits recourse. These are specific, near-dated, and material rather than generic sector risks.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The SOTP discount is real: MGM (~$2.47B), net HoldCo cash (~$797M), and Turo (~$405M) roughly cover or exceed the $3.1B market cap, so buyers acquire People Inc. and the Google antitrust option for little. If the MGM take-private is structured without value destruction (partners share the equity, leverage stays manageable, and Diller's "AI-resilient real assets + BetMGM" thesis proves out), or if the deal is abandoned and the discount closes through continued buybacks and the corporate simplification, the equity re-rates toward the mid-$50s to $60s. Digital's EBITDA and non-session revenue growth show the publisher pivot working; a favorable Google antitrust outcome adds unmodeled upside. Analyst targets ($50 median, $66 high) sit well above the current price. Bull case: ~$60.

### Bear Case
IAC over-levers to pay the premium for the rest of MGM, converting a liquid discounted stake into an owned, indebted casino operator into a soft consumer backdrop, and the market's +1% reaction proves prescient. People Inc.'s Core Sessions continue eroding faster than the non-session pivot can offset, print keeps shrinking, and the publisher's standalone value compresses. MGM's marked-to-market price falls, dragging reported results and the stated NAV. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low ($29.56). Bear case: ~$33 (−29% from current).

The base case sits between: the SOTP discount partially persists, the MGM deal remains unresolved or is restructured/withdrawn, Digital keeps offsetting print, and the stock trades in a $44–$50 band. Base case: ~$48 (+3% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The return path is event-dependent and genuinely two-sided; there is not sufficient evidence to make a confident directional call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are (1) any binding development on the MGM proposal (structure, financing, partners, or withdrawal), (2) the Q2 2026 10-Q, which will formalize the Search discontinuation, the executive transition, and the People Inc. standalone reporting, and (3) meaningful movement on the Google antitrust case in expert discovery.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
- The MGM take-private is abandoned or restructured such that IAC retains its discounted stake while the SOTP discount stays wide (stock materially below MGM stake + net cash), OR
- Digital Core Sessions stabilize (decline narrows toward flat) while non-session revenue growth holds above 20%, converting the publisher pivot into a durable growth base, OR
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$30s while MGM and cash unchanged, restoring the SOTP margin of safety.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
- IAC signs a definitive MGM acquisition financed primarily with new debt at or above the $48.30 proposal, materially raising leverage and consumer-cyclical risk without a clear per-share value case, OR
- People Inc. Digital EBITDA turns negative on a normalized basis as session declines overwhelm the non-session pivot, OR
- MGM's public price falls materially (say, 20%+), impairing the largest asset and the entire NAV support.

## Conclusion

IAC is a well-capitalized holding company in the final act of a self-liquidation-by-simplification, wrapped around a publisher fighting AI-driven traffic decline, a large concentrated casino stake, and a car-sharing position. The parent balance sheet is strong and the SOTP discount is real, but the stock has already run ~20% to near its 52-week high, closing much of that gap, and the primary catalyst for further closure (the non-binding $48.30 MGM take-private) is as likely to destroy value (overpayment, over-leverage, related-party structure) as to create it. Digital's pivot is working at the margin but races a structural headwind; estimate revisions are noisy and negative; short interest at 19% signals credible institutional skepticism; and the Google antitrust suit is real but unquantifiable optionality.

The trajectory has been improving (4.7 → 5.8), and new data partially confirms the constructive case (Digital growth, buyback conviction, strong parent liquidity) while introducing a new binary risk (the MGM deal) that the prior reviews did not carry. On balance we hold at an overall 5.05 and rate the stock HOLD (uncertain). The next leg depends almost entirely on how Diller resolves the MGM question; until that structure is known, the risk/reward is too two-sided to warrant a directional call.
