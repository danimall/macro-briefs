## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation is the largest oil and natural gas producer in California, having consolidated its dominant position through the Aera Energy merger (July 2024) and Berry Corporation merger (December 2025). Production now stands at ~138 MBoe/d, up 60% from FY2023, with ~79% oil weighting. The company trades at 12.3x FY2026E EPS of $5.53, a discount to the peer median P/E of 13.1x, while benefiting from the strongest estimate revision cycle in its history: current-year EPS estimates have risen 59% in the last 30 days and 210% over 90 days. CRC also benefits from a specific regulatory catalyst — SB 237 resolved years of Kern County permitting gridlock, enabling a four-rig drilling program in 2026 for the first time.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) CRC is uniquely positioned as the sole scaled California E&P operator, with California crude trading at a 102-104% premium to WTI; (2) estimate revisions are accelerating sharply upward with no signs of peaking; (3) the company has $600M in remaining buyback authorization at a time when shares trade below the consensus target of $84. Against this, CRC faces a $125-135M resource adequacy revenue decline in 2026, single-state regulatory concentration in the most anti-fossil-fuel jurisdiction in the country, and integration execution risk from the Berry merger. The current estimate momentum overwhelms these headwinds in the near term, but they cap the multiple CRC can sustain relative to geographically diversified peers.

## Company Overview

CRC operates two segments: Oil & Natural Gas (the core upstream business across the San Joaquin, Los Angeles, and Sacramento Basins, plus the 550 MW Elk Hills power plant) and Carbon Management (Carbon TerraVault, a CCS JV with Brookfield). The carbon segment has generated zero revenue and $86M in cumulative losses through FY2025, but provides optionality on Section 45Q credits and California's LCFS program. First CO₂ injection is expected spring 2026.

Following the Berry acquisition, CRC holds 654 MMBoe of proved reserves with total liquidity of $1.4B (cash plus revolver). The company's workforce operates across California and, for the first time, Utah (inherited from Berry). The Elk Hills power plant provides both operational cost savings and a separate revenue stream through California resource adequacy contracts, though this stream faces a significant cliff in 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 O&G sales reached $2.91B (+15% YoY), driven by the full-year Aera contribution. Total operating revenue was $3.67B. However, net income declined to $363M from $564M in FY2023 despite 60% higher production, as average Brent fell from $82 to $68 and operating costs scaled with the larger asset base. Operating costs per Boe were well-controlled at $25.42 vs. $24.51, and G&A per Boe actually declined from $1.07 to $0.85, reflecting merger synergies.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $865M comfortably funded $322M in capex, yielding $543M in free cash flow (18.7% margin). Capital allocation was shareholder-friendly: $377M in buybacks (8.3M shares at $45.29 average) plus $136M in dividends, totaling $513M returned to shareholders. The company has repurchased $1.17B in stock since May 2021 at an average price of $43.59 — a 56% return at current prices.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt stands at $1.283B, up from $540M pre-Aera, reflecting $900M of 8.25% Senior Notes due 2029 and $400M of 7.00% Senior Notes due 2034. The Altman Z-Score of 1.63 technically signals the distress zone, but this is misleading for an E&P company with large PP&E, deferred tax liabilities, and asset retirement obligations ($1.033B) inflating the liability base. With $1.4B in liquidity and all covenants in compliance, financial distress is not a near-term concern. Net debt (approximately $1.283B - $0.132B = $1.151B) to EBITDA is approximately 1.0-1.2x — conservative for the sector.

**2026 Headwind:** Management guided a $125-135M decline in resource adequacy revenue (from ~$233M to ~$100M), a ~55% cut to this high-margin power stream. At elevated oil prices, the oil price uplift more than offsets this, but at normalized oil prices ($70-80 Brent), the RA cliff would represent a meaningful drag on earnings.

## Growth Analysis

CRC's growth story has shifted from acquisition-driven consolidation to organic production growth enabled by the SB 237 permitting breakthrough. Key growth vectors:

1. **Drilling Restart:** After years of reliance on workovers and sidetracks due to Kern County permit delays, SB 237 authorizes up to 2,000 new well permits annually for 10 years. CRC plans a four-rig program in 2026 with $430-470M in capex, up from two rigs in late 2025. This is the first organic growth capital program CRC has deployed at scale.

2. **Berry Synergies:** The Berry merger adds ~30 MBoe/d and creates integration synergies that are a key executive compensation metric. Full-year Berry contribution begins in 2026.

3. **Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen from $1.78 to $5.53 over 90 days (+210%) and from $3.48 to $5.53 over 30 days (+59%). Next-year estimates rose from $2.99 to $4.87 over 90 days (+63%). This acceleration reflects improving fundamentals flowing through CRC's California-weighted, oil-heavy production.

4. **CCS Optionality:** Carbon TerraVault's first injection in spring 2026 could de-risk the segment and unlock long-term value from CRC's position as California's largest pore space owner. However, this is a multi-year development with zero current revenue contribution.

The primary growth constraint is natural decline: CRC loses 4-7 MBoe/d annually to base declines, requiring continuous capital reinvestment just to maintain production. The four-rig 2026 program should more than offset declines and deliver modest production growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68, CRC trades at:
- **12.3x FY2026E EPS** ($5.53) — below the peer median of 13.1x
- **16.4x trailing P/E** (based on the lower FY2025 EPS of $4.15, which reflected $68 Brent)
- **~4.2x EV/EBITDA** (estimated based on ~$2.0B FY2026E EBITDA and ~$1.15B net debt + $6.0B market cap)
- **2.4% dividend yield**, in line with peer median of 2.3%
- **5.5% discount to 52-week high** of $71.98

The consensus analyst target is $84 median / $81.17 mean, with a range of $72-$88. At $68, the stock sits 6% below the low-end target and 24% below the median, providing reasonable upside even in a conservative scenario.

The market is mispricing CRC relative to peers for two identifiable reasons: (1) the +59%/30-day estimate revisions have not yet fully translated into the stock price — CRC has rallied but remains below consensus targets; and (2) the California regulatory discount is structurally excessive at current oil prices, where CRC's premium-priced California crude and monopoly positioning generate outsized free cash flow that more than compensates for the regulatory overhang.

The FY2027E EPS estimate of $4.87 implies the market expects oil prices to moderate. At sustained elevated Brent levels, FY2027E would likely be revised upward as well, providing additional price support.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC has achieved a near-monopoly position in California oil production through the Aera and Berry acquisitions. This provides several structural advantages:

- **Price premium:** California crude trades at 102-104% of WTI, unique among domestic basins
- **No competitor scale:** After absorbing Aera and Berry, no other operator has the scale to challenge CRC's position in California
- **Integrated power:** The 550 MW Elk Hills plant reduces operating costs and generates standalone revenue
- **CCS first-mover:** As the largest pore space owner in California, CRC has a multi-year head start on CCS development

However, the competitive moat depends on a narrowing market. Refinery closures (Phillips 66 Wilmington closed October 2025; Valero Benicia expected to cease by end of April 2026) reduce the number of in-state crude purchasers, potentially widening differentials and forcing CRC to sell more crude into less favorable out-of-state markets. California's long-term policy trajectory is aggressively anti-fossil-fuel, and local governments now have explicit authority to restrict or phase out oil operations. These are real structural headwinds that suppress CRC's multiple below what a peer with equivalent cash flow metrics would command in, say, the Permian.

## Risk Assessment

**California Regulatory Concentration (HIGH):** CRC's single-state exposure to the most aggressive climate policy environment in the U.S. is the defining risk. SB 237 resolves the acute permitting issue, but AB 1207 extends cap-and-trade through 2045 with tightening GHG caps, and local phase-out authority creates ongoing uncertainty. This risk suppresses the stock's terminal multiple and justifies the peer discount.

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** Oil generates ~90% of revenue. CRC's breakeven is approximately $50-55/Boe WTI. The company hedges 36-43K bbl/d at ~$65-70 Brent through 2027, providing partial protection, but a sustained decline in oil prices would compress CRC's earnings significantly.

**Resource Adequacy Revenue Cliff (MODERATE):** The guided decline represents ~15% of FY2025 FCF. At elevated Brent prices this is manageable; at $70-80 Brent it becomes painful.

**Integration Execution (MODERATE):** Berry's Utah operations add geographic and regulatory complexity (federal/tribal land, NEPA reviews). The merger closed only in December 2025, and full synergy realization remains ahead.

**Asset Retirement Obligations ($1.033B) (MODERATE):** The CalGEM $25M payment for orphaned wells sets a precedent for successor liability claims that could recur. A 1% change in inflation assumptions swings AROs by ~$90M.

**Canada Pension Plan Board 3.5M Share Sale (March 2026):** A $214M block trade (~4% of shares outstanding) from a major holder, combined with no insider buying activity, represents a mild negative signal for management alignment.

## Conclusion

CRC earns a BUY recommendation with 2-3% position sizing. The combination of a monopoly position in California crude production, accelerating estimate revisions, discount to consensus targets, and structural tailwinds creates identifiable mispricing. CRC's California premium pricing and oil-heavy production generate outsized FCF that funds aggressive buybacks and a 2.4% dividend yield. The regulatory discount is real but excessive at current oil prices.

The primary risks — California regulatory concentration, commodity sensitivity, and the resource adequacy cliff — are acknowledged but insufficient to override the near-term earnings momentum. No insider buying is a mild negative that prevents a higher-conviction position, and the CPPIB block sale warrants monitoring. Position sizing should reflect the commodity-dependent nature of the thesis: this is a cyclical oil trade enhanced by structural factors, not a secular growth story.
