**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Worthington Steel's trailing financials no longer describe the company. Until June 3, 2026, WS was an underleveraged North American value-added steel processor, with $251M of total debt against $1.26B of equity, an Altman Z-Score near 3.7, and a dividend covered many times over. On June 3 that profile ended. WS closed a voluntary takeover of Kloeckner & Co SE, a German-based global steel distribution and service-center business, and now owns roughly 62% of it. The deal was funded with up to $1.9B of bridge financing and roughly doubles the company's revenue base. The equity thesis has shifted from a cyclical processor with a secular electrical-steel niche to a levered global metals roll-up whose value depends on integration execution, $150M of promised EBITDA synergies, and management's ability to halve debt within two years.

The standalone processing business is respectable but structurally low-margin (gross margin ~11.3%), heavily concentrated in automotive (~55% of sales, Detroit Three ~33-35%), and cyclically exposed to hot-rolled coil price swings that flow directly through inventory holding gains and losses. Two genuine positives underpin the pre-deal story: a Mexican equity-method JV (Serviacero) that swung equity income from $0.4M to $16.7M over nine months and became a material profit engine, and a differentiated position in electrical steel laminations tied to EV motors, transformers, and grid electrification. But the near-term reported numbers are messy: four consecutive earnings misses, GAAP operating income depressed by more than $20M of Kloeckner deal fees, and a European lamination acquisition (Sitem) that is losing money with a stressed Swiss subsidiary under a lender standstill agreement.

We rate WS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The trailing P/E of ~212x is a data artifact of deal-fee-distorted earnings and is not usable. The forward picture is opaque: consensus estimates rose sharply (+60% for the current year) on a single-analyst basis, but they now embed Kloeckner economics that the market cannot yet verify against actual pro-forma results. The permanent financing structure was not locked as of the last primary filing, the DPLTA creates an open-ended obligation to compensate Kloeckner minorities and cover its annual net losses, and Sitem is not yet accretive. The burden of proof sits with management, and the evidence to clear it will not arrive until the first few pro-forma quarters print. The stock is down from a February high near $49 to $36, but that decline reflects real uncertainty about the deal's economics rather than a clean mispricing we can underwrite.

## Company Overview

Worthington Steel processes flat-rolled steel; it does not make it. The company buys carbon flat-rolled coils from North American mills and converts them via slitting, blanking, tailor-welded blanks, electrical steel laminations, cold-rolling, and coating into products sold to precise customer specifications. Revenue comes in two modes: **Direct** (~64% of volume, ~96% of sales), where WS takes ownership and bears steel-price and inventory risk, and **Toll** (fee-for-service on customer-owned metal), which is shrinking, down ~22% year-over-year on the closure of the Cleveland WSCP toll facility and softer mill demand.

WS holds the leading North American position in carbon flat-rolled processing and tailor-welded blanks, and it is one of the largest global producers of electrical steel laminations, a structurally attractive niche. It was spun off from Worthington Industries (now Worthington Enterprises) on December 1, 2023, and still transacts with the former parent under a supply agreement (~$56M of related-party sales in nine months of FY26). The company reports as a single segment, consolidates several majority-owned JVs (Spartan 52%, TWB 55%, WSCP 63%, Sitem 52%), and holds a 50% equity-method stake in Serviacero Worthington (Mexico) that has become a meaningful earnings contributor.

End-market exposure is dominated by automotive at ~55% of sales, with the Detroit Three at ~33-35% and two individual customers each above 12%. Machinery & Equipment (~10%), Construction (~9%), and a mix of agriculture, heavy truck, energy, appliance, HVAC, and electrical round out the base. The fiscal year ends May 31.

WS signed a business combination agreement with Kloeckner on January 15, 2026, launched a €11.00/share cash offer, lowered its minimum acceptance threshold from 65% to 57.5% in March, hit the threshold on March 31, and closed on June 3, 2026 with ~62% ownership. Management now intends to launch a delisting tender for the remainder and to execute a Domination and Profit-and-Loss Transfer Agreement (DPLTA). Post-close, WS competes far more directly with global distributors like Reliance and Ryerson, a thinner-margin, volume-driven, distribution-oriented business distinct from its historical value-added processing model.

## Financial Analysis

The standalone nine-month FY2026 numbers (ended February 28, 2026) show an underlying business holding up while GAAP earnings are distorted by deal costs.

- **Net sales** were $2,514.6M, up 11% YoY, driven by higher direct volumes, +5% direct selling prices, and the Sitem acquisition, partly offset by the −23% toll decline.
- **Gross margin** was $284.5M (11.3%), up $22.9M, aided by a $31.7M favorable swing in inventory holding (from a $31.2M loss the prior year to a $0.5M gain) plus higher direct spreads.
- **Operating income** fell to $73.1M from $80.6M, despite the gross-margin gain, because SG&A jumped $43.6M, including $20.3M of Kloeckner deal fees and Sitem-related costs.
- **Net earnings** attributable to controlling interest were $66.0M (+20%); diluted EPS was $1.30 versus $1.09.
- **Equity income from Serviacero** surged to $16.7M from $0.4M, a swing factor now material to consolidated profit, with $23.5M of cash distributions pulled from the JV.

The most recent quarter (Q3 FY26) looks alarming at the operating line, with operating income of just $3.1M versus $18.3M, but $15.4M of Kloeckner professional fees drove most of that, and adjusted EBIT was $20.0M versus $25.3M. Deal fees will keep depressing GAAP earnings through close and into early integration.

The earnings-surprise record is poor. WS has missed consensus in each of the last four quarters, most severely the March 2026 quarter at −39.0% ($0.27 actual vs. $0.44 estimate). The June 2026 quarter also missed at −4.2%. This is a business with limited near-term forecasting visibility, which matters when the forward valuation now rests on estimates that already embed unproven Kloeckner economics.

The balance sheet story is the crux. As of February 28, 2026, total debt was only $251.4M (revolver $192.7M plus Sitem/Canada term loans) against $1.26B of equity, a low-leverage profile that is now obsolete. WS obtained up to a $1.9B bridge nonrevolving loan commitment to fund Kloeckner, and pro-forma leverage jumps from near-zero to substantial. Permanent financing was not locked as of the last primary filing, leaving interest-rate and market-structure risk on the final debt package. The DPLTA layers on a distinct obligation: WS must compensate any annual net loss of Kloeckner and pay recurring or exit compensation to minority shareholders, a potentially open-ended cash drain that sits outside conventional leverage math.

Pre-deal cash generation was solid: nine-month operating cash flow of $156.3M (down from $176.4M on a working-capital build from higher steel prices), with capex of $84.1M against roughly $40-45M of maintenance need, the balance funding strategic electrical-steel expansion in Canada and Mexico. The dividend runs ~$32M/year and was well covered on a standalone basis, but new debt covenants will restrict dividend increases, capex, and additional borrowing.

Because the operative reality is now a materially higher-debt, EUR-exposed business whose pro-forma financials have not yet been reported, we score financial health at 4.5. The historical Z-Score and low leverage no longer describe the company, and the near-term direction of the balance sheet is toward stress before deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth vectors exist, and they pull in different directions on quality.

The **secular vector** is electrical steel laminations, which serve EV motors, transformers, and grid electrification. This is a real, structurally supported demand driver, and it is the reason for the Canada (Burlington, Ontario, funded partly by up to CAD $57.5M BDC loan), Mexico, and European capacity investments. It differentiates WS from a commodity processor and aligns with the broader electrification and data-center power buildout. The market-share evidence is genuine: direct auto shipments grew +13% in Q3 FY26 against roughly flat-to-+3% Detroit Three build, indicating share gains from new programs.

The **inorganic vector** is the Kloeckner acquisition, which roughly doubles revenue and adds a large European distribution footprint. Management targets ~$150M of EBITDA synergies and a plan to halve debt over ~two years. If delivered, this materially re-rates the earnings base. But it shifts the character of the business toward lower-margin distribution and introduces integration risk that the company's own recent track record does not fully support. Sitem, acquired in June 2025, is not yet accretive: it contributed $133.8M of sales but an $8.2M net loss over nine months, and its Swiss subsidiary Stanzwerk AG operates under a lender standstill agreement, a red flag about diligence and integration just as WS absorbs a far larger target.

The estimate-revision data must be handled carefully. Consensus current-year EPS rose from $2.67 to $4.29 (+60%) and next-year from $3.25 to $4.87 (+50%) over 90 days. Ordinarily, revisions of this magnitude combined with revenue growth would be a strong BUY signal. Here, three factors mute it: the revisions come from a single analyst; the higher figures embed Kloeckner economics not yet verifiable against actual pro-forma results; and the company has missed the last four quarters, undermining confidence in the estimate base. The revision surge reflects the mechanical addition of a doubled revenue base as much as underlying momentum. We treat it as informative but not a clean signal. Management guided to $15-20M of inventory holding gains in Q4 FY26 on rising HRC prices ($938/ton in Q3 FY26 vs. $702 a year prior), a genuine near-term earnings tailwind independent of the deal.

We score growth prospects at 6.0: real secular tailwinds and a doubled revenue base, discounted for the low quality and unproven accretion of the acquisition path.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~212x divides the current price by deal-fee-depressed trailing EPS of $0.17 and carries no analytical content. It should be ignored.

On the forward estimates, the picture is ambiguous. Consensus current-year EPS of $4.29 puts WS at ~8.4x forward earnings; next-year $4.87 puts it at ~7.4x. Those are low absolute multiples that appear cheap against the single analyst's $46 target (~28% above the $35.98 current price). The peer set is mostly negative or distorted (Cleveland-Cliffs at −6.0x, Alpha Metallurgical at −63.0x, Warrior Met Coal at 36.0x), reflecting a steel/coal complex where trailing earnings are near cyclical lows and P/E is a poor comparison tool. The relevant distribution peers post-Kloeckner (Reliance, Ryerson) typically trade at high-single-digit to low-teens forward multiples, so WS at ~8x forward is roughly in line, not obviously mispriced.

The core problem is that the forward estimates are not yet trustworthy. They embed Kloeckner earnings and synergies before a single pro-forma quarter has been reported, on a business acquired via a bridge loan, from a single analyst's model. If the synergy target materializes and deleveraging proceeds, ~8x forward would be cheap. If integration disappoints, DPLTA obligations bite, or steel prices roll over and drag inventory holding losses, the forward number is aspirational and the "cheap" multiple is a value trap.

A low multiple in a cyclical materials name at what may be a temporarily elevated steel-price point historically sorts the wrong way, so cheapness here demands a catalyst rather than justifying a buy. The specific catalyst that would justify a high valuation score is verified synergy capture and deleveraging, and that evidence does not yet exist. We score valuation at 5.0: the forward multiple is genuinely low, but it rests on unverified pro-forma economics and offers no clean, underwritable asymmetry today. This continues the decline in the valuation dimension from prior reports, appropriately, as the deal's uncertainty has crystallized.

## Competitive Landscape

WS holds a defensible position in its core North American niches. It is the leader in carbon flat-rolled processing and tailor-welded blanks, and among the largest global producers of electrical steel laminations. Its moat rests on scale, technical value-added capability, and a strategic footprint rather than cost leadership in a fragmented, price-competitive industry. The execution evidence is positive: WS outgrew its auto market on direct shipments through new-program share gains, and the electrical-steel investments position it for grid, transformer, and EV-motor demand that the market may not fully price into a "steel processor."

The Kloeckner acquisition complicates the competitive read. It adds a large European distribution/service-center footprint and diversifies WS away from its heavy North American auto concentration, a genuine strategic benefit if executed. But it moves WS into a distribution-oriented competitive set (Reliance, Ryerson, Kloeckner's own peers) with thinner, more volume-driven economics and different competitive dynamics. Upstream steel-supplier consolidation reduces WS's sourcing options, a supply-side risk to margins.

On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the processing side, with the acquisition adding geographic diversification at the cost of business-mix quality. We hold competitive position at 6.0, consistent with the prior report's improvement, reflecting genuine share gains and the electrical-steel niche, tempered by the shift toward lower-margin distribution.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by the Kloeckner transaction and is materially higher than the historical business would suggest.

**Deal and financing risk.** Bridge financing takes leverage from near-zero to substantial, with permanent financing not yet locked, leaving interest-rate and market-structure risk on the final package. New covenants will restrict debt, capex, and dividend growth.

**DPLTA obligation.** The obligation to compensate any annual net loss of Kloeckner and pay recurring or exit compensation to minority shareholders is open-ended and potentially large, distinct from conventional debt service, and it activates precisely if the acquired business underperforms.

**Integration track record.** Sitem is not yet accretive and its Swiss subsidiary operates under a lender standstill agreement, signaling financial stress at a unit WS acquired only a year ago. This is a specific, concrete reason to discount management's ability to absorb a far larger, IFRS-based German business with its own cultural and legal complexities.

**Customer and end-market concentration.** A UAW strike, OEM production cut, or EV air-pocket hits WS directly given automotive at ~55% of sales; the FY2025 revenue decline was driven by OEM curtailment.

**Steel-price / inventory volatility.** As a direct processor, WS carries inventory risk. Rising HRC prices are a near-term tailwind (holding gains guided for Q4 FY26), but the same mechanism produced a $31M holding loss when prices fell in FY2025. This cuts both ways and is structurally recurring.

**Tariff/trade whipsaw.** Section 232 steel tariffs rose from 25% to 50% in June 2025, and a February 2026 IEEPA ruling increased trade-policy volatility; customer supply-chain decisions can shift abruptly.

**Redeemable NCI puts (Sitem).** Minority holders of Sitem's 48% can put their stake to WS in FY2030 and FY2032 windows, classified as ~$96.8M mezzanine equity, a contingent future cash obligation.

**Governance concentration.** The McConnell family holds a large stake (~34% referenced), and the charter requires a 75% supermajority for change of control, a strong takeover defense that limits minority influence if the Kloeckner bet disappoints.

These are specific, largely binary risks not yet reflected in reported results, which supports a low risk score of 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress despite a call-heavy tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 56.7% sits rich versus 1-year historical vol of 46.9% (a 9.8-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (56.7% near vs. 50.7% at six months), signaling the market expects imminent volatility, consistent with the just-reported quarter and the uncertainty around Kloeckner's first pro-forma disclosures. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±12.4%. The put/call skew is call-heavy at −14.9%, which does not confirm a bearish stance; combined with modest short interest (6.3% of float, 3.9 days to cover), positioning is not consensus-bearish. IV fell from 70.3% to 56.7% as the price fell 11% since the prior snapshot, suggesting some event-related uncertainty has resolved. The market prices elevated near-term uncertainty rather than directional conviction, which aligns with our uncertain HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** WS integrates Kloeckner successfully, captures the full $150M of EBITDA synergies, and halves its debt within two years as promised. On roughly doubled revenue with a de-risked balance sheet, the forward ~8x multiple proves cheap and the business re-rates toward the low-teens seen at healthy distribution peers. The electrical-steel niche compounds on EV, transformer, and grid demand; Serviacero continues to contribute meaningful equity income; and rising HRC prices deliver near-term inventory holding gains. The single-analyst $46 target is met or exceeded, implying a bull case in the high-$40s to low-$50s (roughly matching the February 2026 high near $49).

**Bear Case.** Integration proves harder than the Sitem experience already warns, DPLTA obligations force WS to cover Kloeckner losses, permanent financing locks in at higher-than-expected rates in a higher-for-longer environment, and steel prices roll over, turning inventory holding gains into losses and compressing already-thin ~11% gross margins. Synergies slip, deleveraging stalls, and the forward estimates prove aspirational. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $27, roughly 25% below the current price, or lower if a covenant breach or dividend pressure emerges.

**Base Case.** Integration proceeds with partial synergy capture and gradual deleveraging, but the first several pro-forma quarters are noisy, with deal amortization, IFRS-to-GAAP conversion, and one-time integration costs obscuring the underlying earnings power. Steel prices provide a modest near-term tailwind that fades. The stock trades in a wide band roughly between $32 and $44 as the market waits for verifiable pro-forma results, with a base-case anchor near $38-40.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: we lack the verified pro-forma evidence to make a confident directional call, and the acquisition has changed the company faster than reported financials can catch up.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. First full pro-forma quarter demonstrates tangible progress on the synergy target (e.g., realized run-rate synergies above $40-50M) with net leverage declining as planned toward the halving objective.
2. Permanent financing is locked at a rate that keeps interest coverage comfortable and covenant headroom clear, removing the open financing-structure risk.
3. Kloeckner and Sitem move to breakeven or accretive on a consolidated basis, with no DPLTA loss-compensation drain materializing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Integration costs or DPLTA obligations force a dividend cut, covenant amendment, or emergency equity raise.
2. Steel prices roll over and inventory holding losses combine with distribution-margin compression to push consolidated operating margins toward breakeven.
3. A material impairment or write-down on Sitem, Kloeckner, or the Kloeckner minority-buyout, signaling the deal was overpaid or is underperforming.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural reassessment is the first one or two fully consolidated pro-forma quarters (fiscal Q1 and Q2 FY2027, reporting through late 2026 and early 2027), which will show actual Kloeckner economics, the permanent debt structure, and early synergy evidence. Until those print, the fundamental basis for a stronger call does not exist.

## Conclusion

Worthington Steel has bet its equity story on a single, large, levered acquisition. The historical business, a leading North American processor with a valuable electrical-steel niche, a strong balance sheet, and a quietly profitable Mexican JV, was a reasonable HOLD. The company that exists after June 3, 2026 is roughly twice the size, funded by bridge debt, carrying an open-ended DPLTA obligation, and dependent on integration synergies from a management team whose most recent European deal (Sitem) is still losing money with a subsidiary under a lender standstill. The trailing valuation is uninformative, the forward valuation rests on unverified single-analyst estimates that embed the deal, and the earnings-surprise record shows four straight misses.

We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the recommendation persists because the evidence to underwrite the change does not yet exist. There is a credible bull case (~8x forward earnings on a doubled revenue base with a $46 target and real deleveraging optionality), but it is unverifiable until pro-forma results print, and there is an equally credible bear case rooted in integration risk, leverage, and cyclicality. The right posture is to let the first pro-forma quarters resolve the synergy-and-deleveraging question before committing capital in either direction. Overall score: 5.05.
