**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

*Fiscal year ends May 31. FY26 = year ended May 31, 2026. Current price $129.05; market cap ~$5.1B.*

## Executive Summary

AAR Corp is one of the largest independent (non-OEM, non-airline) aviation aftermarket providers, and it is executing well against a genuine structural tailwind. Constrained new-aircraft deliveries from Boeing and Airbus are keeping older aircraft in service longer, which lifts demand for used serviceable material (USM), parts distribution, and MRO, the core of AAR's four segments. FY26 delivered $3,308M in revenue (+19% reported, +14% organic), adjusted EPS of $5.05 (+29%), and adjusted EBITDA margin of 12.1% (12.7% ex the wind-down Legacy segment). The company has now beaten consensus for four straight quarters by 7–14%, and Q1 FY27 guidance (ex-Legacy sales +21–23%) came in above the Street.

The bull case is a re-rating story: leverage falling toward the 2.0–2.5x target (2.03x at year-end from 2.72x), margins expanding ~100bps/year, and a portfolio pivot toward higher-margin, less asset-intensive businesses (Parts Supply, Repair/Engineering/Software, DoD sustainment). The bear case is that the market already knows this. At roughly 23x forward adjusted EPS near a 52-week high, the multiple prices in continued execution, while three real caveats remain: cash conversion is weak (adjusted operating cash flow was only ~$94M against $188M net income, driven by a $69M inventory build), government revenue is under active pressure from a proposed ~50% State Department budget cut ($70M annual hit), and there are uncapped legal contingencies plus a governance oddity (the CEO was personally convicted in Nepal, which the company disputes).

We rate AIR a HOLD (growth hold). The operational trajectory is strong and improving, and the score has been drifting up (6.0 → 6.2 over four reports), but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock has appreciated ~67% over twelve months; that is fundamentally justified, so it is not itself a reason to sell, though it does mean the easy re-rating has largely happened. We would upgrade to BUY on demonstrated cash-conversion improvement in FY27 or a pullback toward the low-$110s.

## Company Overview

AAR sits between OEMs (who increasingly want to bundle parts and service) and airline in-house MRO, operating in over 20 countries. In Q4 FY26 the company realigned into four operating segments:

- **Parts Supply (~45% of sales; $1,487.7M, +35% YoY):** The growth and profit engine, combining new-parts distribution (OEM aftermarket) and USM. FY26 operating income $186.2M. New-parts distribution grew ~19% organically in Q4, with management citing share gains, and a new multi-year distribution deal was signed with Woodward for CFM LEAP, GEnx, and CF34 engine consumables.
- **Repair, Engineering & Software (RE&S) (~33%; $1,080.8M, +16%):** Airframe and Component MRO plus a growing software layer (Trax MRO ERP, Aerostrat planning, Airinmar, and the new Airvoyant AI procurement platform launched April 2026). Op income $84.6M, with growth driven by Component MRO (boosted by the Triumph Product Support acquisition) and Trax recurring revenue.
- **Government Solutions (~15%; $502.3M, +1.4%):** State Department INL/A flight ops (WASS contract), DoD supply-chain logistics, and Mobility Systems. Operating income jumped to $56.7M (from $35.1M); margins improved to ~11.3%. A recent $305M follow-on award covers Navy/Marine Corps C-40A logistics support.
- **Legacy Commercial Programs (~7%; $237.2M) — being wound down** over 3–4 years. These are asset-heavy power-by-the-hour programs that no longer clear AAR's return thresholds. FY26 operating income fell to $0 (from $8.6M), and the ~$16.4M of associated goodwill is expected to be impaired.

The segment realignment and Legacy wind-down constitute a deliberate mix-shift toward higher-margin, higher-cash-flow businesses. Management now reports and guides on an "ex-Legacy" basis, a reasonable framing given that segment contributed zero operating income on $237M of sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Three-year trajectory: FY24 $2,318.9M → FY25 $2,780.5M → FY26 $3,308.0M (+19%). Q4 FY26 sales were $928M (+23%). Of the FY26 growth, ~14pp was organic, with acquisitions and the divestiture comparison contributing the rest, a healthy organic rate for an aftermarket business.

**Profitability.** GAAP net income surged to $187.7M (EPS $4.86) from $12.5M (EPS $0.35) in FY25. That headline jump is misleading: FY25 carried ~$115M of after-tax charges (the ~$55.6M FCPA settlement and ~$71.1M LGO divestiture loss). The clean comparison is adjusted EPS of $5.05 (+29%) and adjusted EBITDA of $401.1M at a 12.1% margin (up from 11.8%). Also flattering GAAP: a $29.5M non-cash bargain purchase gain from the HAECO Americas deal, which management correctly excludes from adjusted figures and should be stripped from run-rate analysis. On a go-forward, ex-Legacy basis the EBITDA margin was 12.7% (13.0% in Q4), and management targets ~100bps of annual expansion.

**Cash flow — the soft spot.** Operating cash flow was only $98.7M ($94.3M adjusted) against $187.7M of net income, a ~24% conversion of EBITDA. The driver is a working-capital build: inventory rose $69M (net inventory now $979M) and rotable assets rose $22M. Capex was $36.6M plus $28.8M of hangar expansion. Free cash flow is therefore modest relative to earnings. This is the central quality question. Distribution and USM growth are inventory-intensive by nature; if the top line keeps compounding, working capital will keep absorbing cash. Management has added "net working-capital turns" to executive compensation metrics, which signals they understand the pressure point, but the improvement has to show up in FY27 results for the earnings quality to be fully credible.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is $900M ($700M of 6.75% senior notes due 2029 plus revolver), against $84M cash — net debt $816M, net leverage 2.03x (down from 2.72x, now within target). Deleveraging was aided by a $273.9M equity raise (3.45M shares at $83.00) in Q2 FY26 that cut revolver borrowings by $227M but lifted share count from ~35.6M to ~39.6M. Equity grew to $1,703.8M; goodwill plus intangibles total $861.9M.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. A $150M buyback authorization exists but nothing was repurchased in FY26 — capital is flowing to acquisitions and deleveraging. That is a defensible priority set given the leverage and M&A cadence, but it means there is no yield support and no per-share tailwind from repurchases at present.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is durable and broad-based. Supply-chain constraints at Boeing and Airbus (including a referenced work stoppage) keep older aircraft flying longer, which structurally favors USM, distribution, and MRO. Organic growth is running well ahead of headline aftermarket rates: distribution new-parts organic growth was ~36% and government organic growth ~55% in Q3 FY26 per management commentary, with Q4 Parts Supply +39%.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than uniformly positive. Near-term quarterly estimates are rising (current quarter +4.0%, next quarter +4.4% over 30 days), consistent with the four-quarter beat streak (7.4%, 13.1%, 8.6%, 14.2%). But full-year estimates have drifted slightly lower (current year -0.8% over 30 days, -1.1% over 90 days; next year -0.6% over 90 days). This is a company delivering steadily against already-elevated expectations rather than a strong-inflection revision velocity that flags a clear buy signal. The growth is real and confirmed by multiple segments and by industry-wide aftermarket signals, so the durability is high, but the consensus is not systematically underestimating the story the way it does at a genuine trough.

Growth is being reinforced by heavy M&A: FY26 saw ADI (~$137M, electronic-component distribution), HAECO Americas (~$78M, heavy MRO, securing >$850M of multi-year contracts), ART (~$36M, interiors engineering with FAA ODA), and Aerostrat (~$19M, planning software), on top of the transformational Triumph Product Support deal (~$725M, March 2024). New wins include the C-40A follow-on, the Woodward distribution deal, and the Airvoyant launch. FY27 guidance — ex-Legacy sales growth of low-double-digits to low-teens, ex-Legacy EBITDA margin of 12.25–12.75% — is above consensus and consistent with the ~100bps/year expansion target.

## Valuation Assessment

At $129.05 the stock trades at ~23x forward adjusted EPS (roughly $5.60 consensus for the current fiscal year, $6.56 next year) and ~26.6x trailing GAAP EPS of $4.86. On EV/EBITDA the multiple is roughly 14–15x on $401M of adjusted EBITDA plus $816M of net debt.

Peer comparison is nearly useless here because the provided cohort is dominated by unprofitable or distorted names (Firefly at -11.6x, Intuitive Machines at -35x, AeroVironment at -35.7x, Arxis at 1,776x). The one clean profitable comp, Parsons at 27.4x, sits modestly above AAR. Against high-quality aftermarket/aerospace-services businesses more broadly, low-to-mid-20s forward is a full but not extreme multiple for a company compounding organic sales in the low-to-mid teens with margin expansion.

The valuation case rests on the re-rating thesis: as leverage falls, margins expand, and the mix shifts to less asset-intensive, higher-cash businesses, the multiple could hold or expand while earnings grow. Analyst targets frame the range at $128 low, $141 mean, $145 median, $155 high — the stock sits at the very bottom of that band. Because the stock is neither cheap (forward P/E ~23x is above the low-20s where analyst targets treat multiples as floors) nor at a trough, we treat these targets as a fair value corridor rather than a floor. The high target ($155) implies ~20% upside; the mean implies ~9%.

What keeps this from a higher valuation score is the absence of a clear mispricing catalyst. The forward multiple already embeds continued margin expansion and beat-and-raise cadence. The cheapness that would warrant a 7+ score is not present, and the soft cash conversion means the earnings-based multiple flatters the true free-cash-flow yield. We score valuation 5.5 — a full multiple with a credible growth story behind it, but no asymmetric discount. We note the sector-level caution that Industrials BUYs have historically been negative-alpha absent a distinct company catalyst; here the catalyst (aftermarket upcycle + mix-shift) is real but largely priced.

## Competitive Landscape

AAR's competitive position is stable-to-improving. It is one of the largest independent aftermarket players, with scale advantages in USM and distribution, an FAA-certificated repair network, industry-leading hangar turnaround times, and a differentiating software layer (Trax, Aerostrat, Airvoyant). Distribution share gains, Component MRO capacity expansion (Miami, Thailand A320 slat repair, Chonburi), and the Collins "Supplier of the Year" award for the Wellington, KS facility all point to a widening operational moat within its niche.

The countervailing forces are structural rather than acute. OEMs are pushing to capture aftermarket value by bundling parts with service and asserting proprietary rights over repair manuals and IP; airlines can bring MRO in-house. On defense, DoD sustainment is growing (C-40A, ADI government sales) while State Department funding is contracting — the Government segment grew only ~1.4% in revenue, with the margin jump to 11.3% doing the work. The independent positioning is both AAR's strength (neutrality vs. OEM lock-in) and its exposure (OEMs control the IP that AAR's repair and distribution businesses rely on). Net trajectory is positive within the niche, warranting a competitive-position score of 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack is heavier than for a typical aerospace-services name, and several items are not fully priced:

1. **Government/State Department budget pressure (material, active).** The State Department budget cut has already reduced AAR's DoS contract funding by ~$70M of annual revenue. The INL/A WASS contract ($92M in FY26) is up for re-compete in June 2028 and has been in structural decline. Total U.S. government exposure is 23.8% of sales ($787.8M).

2. **Section 232 tariff investigation (newly added FY26).** A national-security probe into imports of commercial aircraft, engines, and parts could materially affect AAR's distribution and government businesses; the filing also newly flags Commerce placing export license applications on hold.

3. **Uncapped A220 performance guarantee.** Retained from the Composites sale, this carries a customer claim of ≥$32M with no financial cap; a loss is "reasonably possible" but not yet estimable. A separate Trax arbitration contests ~$8.1M of contingent consideration.

4. **Inventory recoverability.** The $979M inventory balance is the largest working-capital item and KPMG's Critical Audit Matter (slow-moving write-down). USM and engine asset values are cyclical and can trigger impairments.

5. **FCPA overhang and the Nepal CEO conviction.** AAR settled bribery matters in December 2024 via an 18-month DOJ Non-Prosecution Agreement plus SEC cease-and-desist (~$55.6M); the NPA compliance period is ongoing. Separately, in Nepal, CEO John Holmes was personally assigned a prison sentence and fine as the "principal executive." The company disputes the process and is not participating, but this is a genuine reputational and international-operations tail risk that a standard screen would miss.

6. **Leverage and rates.** $900M debt (6.75% fixed notes plus variable revolver) is manageable at 2.03x but covenants restrict flexibility and the position is sensitive to a downturn.

7. **Integration and goodwill.** The most acquisitive year in company history raises integration risk; HAECO Americas is near-term margin-dilutive, and Legacy goodwill (~$16.4M) will be impaired during wind-down.

None of these is an imminent binary event, but the cluster (active government funding cut, uncapped legal contingency, tariff probe, and a novel governance/reputational item) is why the risk score sits at 5.3 rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral with a modest cautionary tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 50.1% is rich versus 41.3% one-year historical vol (an 8.7pp spread), pricing an implied move of ±10.8% into the next expiry, consistent with a name that just reported (July 21) and is digesting a ~10% post-earnings drawdown. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (0.1%) but turns modestly negative at 3–6 months (-5.9% and -3.9%), and the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.46 to 1.26 over the past month while IV compressed 4.6pp. The rising put OI alongside a post-earnings price fade suggests some hedging/skepticism has built, but the flat term structure and balanced front-month skew argue against reading this as a strong directional bear signal. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (2.8 days to cover), so the bearish view is uncrowded and informed investors are not pressing a short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$155–175).** The aftermarket upcycle proves durable and multi-year; distribution and Component MRO keep compounding organically in the mid-teens; the Legacy wind-down and mix-shift lift ex-Legacy EBITDA margins toward 13%+; leverage falls through 2x, opening capacity for buybacks against the $150M authorization; and cash conversion improves as working-capital turns respond to the new comp incentive. On ~$6.56 next-year adjusted EPS at a sustained 24–26x, the stock reaches the mid-$150s to $170s, with upside if the multiple expands on a proven cash-flow inflection.

**Bear case (~$95–105, ~20–25% downside).** Cash conversion stays stuck near 24% as inventory keeps absorbing growth, undermining the earnings quality; the State Department budget cut deepens and the 2028 WASS re-compete looks unlikely, pressuring the Government segment; the Section 232 probe or a Legacy/A220 charge hits reported results; and the multiple compresses toward the low-20s on a growth or margin disappointment. On decelerating momentum and a de-rating, the stock retraces toward the low-$100s.

**Base case (~$141).** Steady low-double-digit organic growth, ~100bps of margin expansion, and continued modest beats keep earnings compounding while the multiple holds near current levels. This lands around the analyst mean of $141, ~9% above the current price plus the value of continued deleveraging.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $141** (~9% upside). Derived from the FY27 adjusted EPS trajectory (~$5.60 current-year consensus, $6.56 next-year) at a multiple consistent with the analyst mean, reflecting continued execution without multiple expansion.
- **Bull case target: $165** (~28% upside). ~25x sustained on ~$6.56 next-year adjusted EPS, contingent on a demonstrated cash-conversion inflection and buyback initiation.
- **Bear case target: $100** (~22% downside). Low-20s multiple on flat-to-decelerating earnings amid a cash-conversion stall or government-segment deterioration.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the FY27 Q1 print (late September 2026) and Q2 print (December 2026) as the key checkpoints for cash-conversion progress and ex-Legacy margin delivery.
- **Upside/downside from current ($129.05):** +9% to base, +28% to bull, -22% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY27 adjusted operating cash flow converting to >40% of EBITDA (a clear break from the ~24% FY26 level), demonstrating that working-capital turns are improving.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s that widens the forward-multiple-to-growth gap and restores asymmetry.
- Initiation of buybacks under the $150M authorization at sensible prices, confirming leverage has cleared the target zone.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Ex-Legacy EBITDA margin guidance cut below ~12% or two consecutive quarters of organic growth decelerating below high-single-digits.
- A material A220 guarantee charge, an adverse Section 232 outcome affecting distribution economics, or loss/further degradation of the WASS contract ahead of the 2028 re-compete.
- Estimate revisions turning decisively negative across all windows (currently only the full-year estimates are modestly soft).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural point is the FY27 Q1 earnings release (~late September 2026).

## Conclusion

AAR is a well-run independent aftermarket operator executing against a durable industry upcycle, with four straight beats, +14% organic FY26 growth, +29% adjusted EPS, deleveraging into its target range, and a portfolio pivot toward higher-margin, less asset-intensive businesses. The improving score trajectory (6.0 → 6.2 across the prior four reports) reflects genuine operational progress, and the recent ~10% post-earnings pullback modestly improves the entry versus the July highs.

We stay at HOLD rather than upgrade because the current price already reflects most of this. At ~23x forward adjusted EPS near the low end of the analyst target band, the re-rating is largely done, and the offsetting risks are more substantive than for a typical name at this multiple: weak ~24% cash conversion, an active $70M government funding cut with a 2028 re-compete overhang, an uncapped A220 contingency, a Section 232 probe, and the Nepal CEO conviction. The upside to base case (~9%) is not compelling enough against a bear case of ~22% downside to justify a BUY at this entry. This is a growth hold: own it for the compounding thesis, but wait for evidence of cash-conversion improvement or a better price (low-$110s) before adding conviction.
