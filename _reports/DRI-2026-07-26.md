**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Darden Restaurants is the largest full-service restaurant operator in the U.S., running 2,202 company-owned units across 11 brands anchored by Olive Garden (42% of sales) and LongHorn Steakhouse (26%). FY26 was a record year: revenue of $13.21B (+9.4%), diluted EPS from continuing operations of $10.44 (+17.6%), operating margin expanding 70bps to 12.0%, and free cash flow of roughly $1.12B. The business generates high-quality earnings (operating cash flow of $1.85B exceeded net income of $1.21B), returns most of that cash to shareholders (~$1.36B across dividends and buybacks in FY26), and carries a genuinely investment-grade capital structure once the misleading distress-screen artifacts are stripped away.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY lies almost entirely in the forward setup. FY27 guidance underwhelmed: same-restaurant sales guided to 2.5-3.5% (a clear deceleration from FY26's 4.5%) and EPS of $11.10-11.35 sitting below the ~$11.40 consensus at the time, on higher input costs and marketing spend. Consensus estimates have been drifting lower across every window (current-quarter EPS revised down 3.2% over 90 days, current-year down 0.7% over 90 days, next-year down 0.8% over 90 days), and the company has now missed EPS in four consecutive quarters, albeit by small margins. The internal dynamic to watch is that the growth engine has rotated from Olive Garden to LongHorn, but LongHorn is the segment absorbing the sharpest beef-inflation margin compression (-70bps in FY26, -110bps in Q3), creating a margin-versus-growth tension at the brand carrying the story.

At $196.31 the stock trades near ~17.4x the current-year EPS estimate of $11.27 and offers a ~3.3% dividend yield with a well-covered payout. That is a fair-to-attractive multiple for a durable, cash-generative franchise, and the ~11% drawdown from the June high on unchanged fundamentals improves the risk/reward at the margin. But the combination of decelerating comps, falling estimates, and a consumer-discretionary backdrop that we view cautiously in the second half argues against a BUY. The dividend and buyback yield support holding; the appreciation case requires a comp re-acceleration or beef-inflation relief that is not yet visible.

## Company Overview

Darden operates a "house of brands" managed in four reportable segments as of May 31, 2026 (fiscal year ends the last Sunday in May):

- **Olive Garden** — 949 units, $5.59B sales (42% of total), ~$25 average check. The largest full-service Italian chain in the U.S., internally developed, and the traffic anchor.
- **LongHorn Steakhouse** — 618 units, $3.42B sales (26%), ~$28.50 check. Acquired in 2007, now the fastest-growing brand on a same-restaurant basis.
- **Fine Dining** — Ruth's Chris, The Capital Grille, Eddie V's; $1.38B (10%); $100+ checks; the most economically sensitive segment.
- **Other Business** — Yard House, Cheddar's, Chuy's, Seasons 52, Bahama Breeze, The Capital Burger; $2.82B (21%); plus franchise royalties.

The competitive foundation, as laid out in the 10-K, rests on scale (1,400+ suppliers, self-distribution through Darden Direct, leading full-service advertising spend), operating discipline (hourly turnover of 67% and management turnover of 15%, described as among the lowest in the industry, with 100% of new general managers promoted internally), a "no deep discounting" marketing philosophy that protects brand equity and margin, and a fragmented industry structure that rewards a disciplined multi-unit operator. The business is almost entirely U.S. and cash-based at the point of sale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (continuing operations):** $10.49B (FY23) → $11.39B (FY24) → $12.08B (FY25) → $13.21B (FY26). The FY26 gain decomposes into roughly 2.1% from a 53rd week, 4.5% blended same-restaurant sales, and net new units plus a full year of Chuy's. Stripping the extra week, organic growth was solid but not exceptional.

**Profitability:** FY26 net income of $1.21B. The effective tax rate of 12.6% is unusually low, driven by FICA tip credits plus OBBBA bonus depreciation and R&D expensing. This is a structural feature rather than a one-off; management guides FY27 tax rate to ~13.5%, still well below a normalized corporate rate. Investors modeling Darden on a standard tax rate would understate its earnings power.

**Segment margin trends — the key watch item.** Only Olive Garden (+20bps to 22.5%) and Other Business (+20bps) expanded margins in FY26. LongHorn compressed to 18.6% and Fine Dining compressed 90bps to 17.7% on food and beverage inflation. The Q3 10-Q showed the pattern more acutely (LongHorn -110bps, Fine Dining -50bps for the quarter). The mechanism is direct: beef and protein inflation lands hardest on steakhouse formats, and Darden's no-deep-discount model forces it to offset cost through check growth without killing traffic. The growth leader is also the margin-pressure epicenter.

**Cash flow quality is excellent.** FY26 operating cash flow of $1.85B against net income of $1.21B represents cash conversion above 150% of net income, a Piotroski positive. Capex of $734M produces FCF of roughly $1.12B (an 8.5% FCF margin). Management guides capex up to ~$875M in FY27 as unit growth accelerates to 75-80 openings, which will compress near-term FCF as the company invests for volume growth.

**The misleading distress signals.** Screening metrics flag the stock: an Altman Z-Score of 1.61 ("distress") and a current ratio of 0.31. Both are false positives. This is the normal, healthy structure of a cash-based restaurant, where guests pay immediately while suppliers are paid on 5-90 day terms, so negative working capital is a feature rather than a warning. The $654.9M unearned gift-card liability is float, not debt. Management's own investment-grade lens, adjusted debt / adjusted EBITDAR of 2.0x against a 3.50x covenant, is the right measure and it is conservative. Ratings are solidly investment grade (Baa2/BBB/BBB), and there is roughly $960M-$1.06B of undrawn revolver capacity. Reported D/E of 4.83 is inflated by years of buybacks depressing book equity, not by financial stress.

**Capital allocation.** Darden returns most of its cash to shareholders. FY26 returned $693M in dividends plus $672M in buybacks (roughly 120% of FCF). The dividend rose to $6.00/share from $5.60. A fresh $1B buyback was authorized in June 2025. Diluted share count has fallen steadily from 122.9M (FY23) to 116.3M (FY26). The above-100%-of-FCF return in FY26 is sustainable given the strong operating cash flow and modest leverage, but it does mean per-share growth is partly manufactured through repurchase rather than pure operating leverage.

## Growth Analysis

FY26 was a strong trailing year, but the forward picture is one of deceleration. FY27 guidance calls for sales of $13.60-13.75B (~3-4% growth off the FY26 base, but with no 53rd week to help), same-restaurant sales of 2.5-3.5%, 75-80 unit openings, capex of ~$875M, and EPS of $11.10-11.35. The CFO characterized the outlook plainly: "not expecting any material change to industry performance."

Estimate revisions confirm the softening. Current-quarter consensus fell 3.2% over 90 days to $2.05, next-quarter fell 2.5% over 90 days to $2.29, current-year is down to $11.27, and next-year down to $12.39. The direction is uniformly negative across every window. The four consecutive EPS misses have been small (-0.3% to -2.6%). A consistent small-miss pattern after a period of beats suggests visibility is tightening, and it argues for treating consensus as a mild ceiling rather than a base.

The internal growth dynamic is the rotation from Olive Garden to LongHorn. In Q4 FY26, Olive Garden SRS came in at +2.4% (missing 3.2%) and Fine Dining at +1.9% (missing 3.1%), while LongHorn beat at +9.5% versus 7.1% expected. The bull framing is that LongHorn's momentum diversifies earnings and reduces reliance on a maturing Olive Garden. The bear framing is that 42% of sales (Olive Garden) is decelerating and holding traffic mainly through check and catering growth, while the one brand carrying the story is the most beef-inflation-exposed. Management flagged that under-35 consumer traffic weakened slightly and characterized the overall consumer mood as "cautious."

Two portfolio moves add modest support. The Chuy's integration ($649M acquisition in October 2024) is complete, with integration costs falling to $9.5M in FY26 from $44.6M in FY25, a G&A tailwind. The disciplined pruning of underperformers (Bahama Breeze wind-down, Olive Garden Canada sold to a franchisee) reflects a capital-allocation posture that favors converting or exiting weak units and franchising internationally rather than empire-building.

Growth prospects score a 5.0. The unit-growth pipeline and structural share-gain thesis are real, but the near-term earnings momentum is negative, comps are decelerating, and the growth leader faces the steepest cost pressure. Per our own experience with this dimension, its recent predictive edge has weakened, so we weight it in confirmation with financial health rather than as a standalone driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At $196.31, Darden trades at roughly 17.4x the current-year EPS estimate of $11.27 and ~15.8x the next-year estimate of $12.39, with a ~3.3% dividend yield. Trailing P/E on the FY26 $10.44 is ~18.8x.

Against the peer set, the picture is mixed. The restaurant peer median P/E is 26.8x, so Darden looks cheap on the headline. But that median is distorted by high-growth concepts — CAVA at 169.7x, Dutch Bros at 102.6x, Texas Roadhouse at 26.8x — that carry unit-growth runways Darden lacks as a mature ~2,200-unit operator. The more relevant comparables are the mature full-service and pizza-delivery names: Brinker at 15.5x and Domino's at 18.0x. Against those, Darden's ~17x sits squarely in the middle, fairly valued for a durable, moderate-growth cash generator rather than obviously mispriced.

The valuation dimension is the one where discipline matters most. A stock can screen "cheap" and still be dead money if there is no catalyst to close the gap, and cheapness has been a poor standalone signal in this environment. Here there is no identifiable near-term catalyst: comps are decelerating, estimates are falling, and management has explicitly reset expectations to a lower-growth, cost-pressured year. The analyst mean target of ~$228 and median of $230 imply meaningful upside from spot, but these targets have not been re-set to the softer FY27 guide, and on a mature name at ~17x with negative revision momentum, we do not treat consensus targets as a floor. The "analyst targets are floors" principle applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, which is the opposite of Darden's current setup.

We score valuation a 5.0. The absolute multiple and the yield provide downside support, and the ~11% pullback from the June high improves entry. But there is no asymmetric upside case at the current price given the growth deceleration, and rewarding the modest cheapness without a catalyst would be a value-trap error.

## Competitive Landscape

Darden's competitive position remains strong in absolute terms and stable-to-slightly-eroding on trajectory. In a fragmented full-service industry pressured by fast-casual, QSR, grocery convenient-meals, and delivery aggregators, Darden's scale and operating discipline let it take share from independents. FY26 supports the thesis: blended SRS with overall margin expansion suggests it gained share while protecting profitability.

Two developments temper the picture. First, Darden quietly adopted third-party delivery through Uber Direct, which now shows up as a ~0.2% drag on restaurant expenses at Olive Garden. This is a white-label model (Darden keeps the customer relationship and order data; Uber provides the courier), which is more margin- and data-protective than a marketplace listing. But it confirms that delivery convenience is forcing the hand of a company that historically resisted third-party delivery to protect margins. Second, the Olive Garden-to-LongHorn rotation, while diversifying, means the largest brand is maturing and holding traffic through check and catering rather than unit-volume growth.

We score competitive position a 6.0, in line with the prior report's downgrade from 7.5. The moat is intact (scale, self-distribution, low turnover, brand equity), but the trajectory is flat rather than widening, and the delivery-fee concession plus decelerating Olive Garden comps prevent a higher score. We note the caution that this dimension has been a weak-to-negative predictor recently, so we anchor the score to the observable margin and share evidence rather than reading too much into it.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity/food inflation versus pricing power** is the central near-term risk. FY26 food and beverage costs ran at 30.6% of sales on 1.2% menu inflation, and the FY27 guidance shortfall was explicitly attributed to higher input costs and marketing. Beef inflation squeezes LongHorn and Fine Dining specifically. The no-deep-discount model means Darden must offset inflation through check growth without eroding traffic, a delicate balance if the consumer weakens.

**Consumer discretionary sensitivity** is the macro overhang. Full-service dining is highly cyclical, and Fine Dining most of all. Management flagged softening under-35 traffic and a "cautious" consumer mood. We view the second-half consumer picture with elevated caution given the risk of a demand air-pocket in full-service dining if the discretionary backdrop weakens.

**Other risks:** labor (209,931 team members, wage inflation ~1% impact on the labor line, immigration-enforcement exposure to the labor pool); lease obligations (2,104 of 2,202 restaurants leased, ~$2.86B of future commitments, limiting flexibility to close underperformers); goodwill/trademark impairment ($1.66B goodwill, $1.35B trademarks, with Ruth's Chris and Chuy's the newest and least seasoned, the February 2026 test showing no impairment). None of these is a binary near-term catalyst.

On the positive side, the balance sheet is genuinely sound (2.0x adjusted leverage, investment-grade ratings, ample liquidity), and the dividend is well-covered by FCF. Risk factors were unchanged between the FY25 10-K and Q3 FY26 10-Q. We score risk factors a 5.8: the idiosyncratic risks are modest and largely known, but the consumer-cyclical exposure into a cautious second half is a real, incompletely-priced downside vector.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing elevated near-term stress. ATM IV of 27.1% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 25.8% (a 1.4% spread, "fair"), and IV has collapsed 12.2 points from 39.3% since the June earnings event as that uncertainty resolved. The put/call skew is balanced at 1.8% near-term, and the term structure is flat. The 6-month tenor shows a modest -3.8% put skew and a 15.7% implied move, indicating some hedging demand further out but nothing alarming. The put/call OI ratio fell from 1.03 to 0.63 alongside the price decline, which is consistent with hedges being unwound rather than fresh bearish positioning. Net read: the market sees Darden as a relatively low-drama, range-bound name post-earnings, consistent with a HOLD, and the fair-to-cheap IV means options are not signaling an imminent catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($240-260):** Beef and protein inflation moderates, allowing LongHorn and Fine Dining margins to recover while LongHorn's 9.5% comp momentum continues. Olive Garden re-accelerates through catering and off-premise, the Chuy's integration adds a G&A tailwind, unit growth of 75-80 openings compounds, and the low tax rate persists. On $12.39 next-year EPS re-rating to ~20x on renewed comp momentum, the stock reaches the mid-$240s; a further beat-and-re-rate cycle toward the $276 street high is possible if the consumer proves more resilient than feared.

**Bear Case ($150-165):** The consumer cliff arrives in the second half, full-service traffic weakens (Fine Dining first), and Olive Garden comps go negative. Beef inflation persists, forcing further LongHorn margin compression, and the no-discount model caps Darden's ability to defend traffic. EPS lands at or below the low end of the $11.10-11.35 guide, estimates continue falling, and the multiple compresses toward Brinker's ~15.5x on ~$11 EPS, taking the stock to the low-$160s. The $156 street low anchors the downside.

**Base Case ($205-215):** FY27 plays out roughly as guided — SRS of 2.5-3.5%, EPS near the midpoint of $11.10-11.35, margins broadly stable with LongHorn pressure partly offset by Olive Garden and G&A tailwinds. The stock earns its ~3.3% dividend plus ~2-3% from buyback-driven per-share growth, drifting modestly higher toward the low-$210s as the dividend and quality provide support without a re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis rests on the well-covered ~3.3% dividend, the ~120%-of-FCF total shareholder return, and the defensive quality of a best-in-class operator with a sound balance sheet. This is a name to hold for its yield and stability, not for near-term appreciation, given decelerating comps and falling estimates.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q1 FY27 print (September 2026), which will show whether early-fiscal-year comps track the 2.5-3.5% guide and whether beef inflation is easing or worsening for LongHorn.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-restaurant sales re-accelerate above the top of the FY27 guide (>3.5%) for two consecutive quarters, particularly with Olive Garden returning above 3%.
- LongHorn/Fine Dining segment margins stabilize or expand, signaling beef-inflation relief.
- A pullback toward the low-$170s (near the 52-week low), which would push the yield toward ~3.5% and forward P/E below ~16x, restoring an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Olive Garden same-restaurant sales turn negative, indicating traffic erosion at the 42%-of-sales anchor.
- FY27 EPS guidance is cut below the current $11.10 floor, or estimates fall a further 5%+ across the current-year and next-year windows.
- Evidence of a broad full-service consumer downturn (system-wide traffic declines) combined with continued margin compression, which would convert the cyclical caution into confirmed deterioration.

## Conclusion

Darden is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise with a disciplined operating model, a sound investment-grade balance sheet once the misleading distress screens are set aside, and a shareholder-return program that consistently deploys most of its free cash flow into dividends and buybacks. Those attributes justify owning the stock for its ~3.3% yield and defensive characteristics.

They do not justify a BUY at ~17.4x forward earnings. The growth engine is decelerating (SRS guided to 2.5-3.5% from 4.5%), estimates are falling across every window, the company has missed EPS four straight quarters, and the growth leader (LongHorn) is the segment most exposed to persistent beef inflation. A cautious second-half consumer raises the probability of a demand air-pocket in full-service dining. The score trajectory has been declining (6.6 → 5.7 over five months), and today's data confirms rather than reverses that trend: competitive position and valuation have both compressed on decelerating comps and negative revisions.

We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.7. Own it for the income and the quality; wait for a comp re-acceleration, beef-inflation relief, or a pullback toward the low-$170s before adding on appreciation grounds.
