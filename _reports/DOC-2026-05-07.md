## Executive Summary

Healthpeak Properties is a diversified healthcare REIT with a $13.3B market cap operating across outpatient medical (507 properties, ~52% of NOI), lab/life science (145 properties, ~37%), and senior housing (34 properties via 81.6%-owned Janus Living, ~11%). The March 2026 Janus Living IPO represents a legitimate value-unlock event: Healthpeak's retained stake is worth approximately $5.6B at current JAN market prices, implying the remaining outpatient medical + lab portfolio trades at a compressed cap rate relative to the Blackstone transaction validation (6.1%). Q1 2026 results beat FFO estimates by 4.7%, and management raised full-year guidance.

The fundamental picture is bifurcated. Outpatient medical (+2.4% SS NOI) and senior housing (+13.8%) are performing well, but the lab segment is in acute distress: same-store occupancy collapsed 770bps to 90.1%, Q1 SS NOI fell 7.2% YoY, and the biotech funding environment remains challenged by NIH cuts, visa restrictions, and muted venture capital activity. Estimate revisions are broadly negative (-21% to -33% for FY2026-2027 EPS), and refinancing $9.8B in debt at spreads 100-200bps wider than maturing obligations creates an ongoing earnings headwind.

The stock surged 18% on May 6 (earnings day), closing at $19.50 after trading as low as $15.70 in late April. At the current price, the sum-of-parts narrative is partially priced in, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. Patience is warranted until lab stabilization provides clearer visibility on normalized earnings power.

## Company Overview

Healthpeak Properties is the largest pure-play healthcare REIT following its 2024 merger with Physicians Realty Trust. The combined entity owns 689 properties across three segments, with 411 employees and investment-grade ratings (Baa1/BBB+). The company is headquartered in Denver, Colorado and trades on the NYSE.

**Segment Breakdown (2025 Adjusted NOI):**
- Outpatient Medical: $796M (~52%) — 507 properties, 92% occupied, 79% hospital-campus adjacent
- Lab/Life Science: $567M (~37%) — 145 properties concentrated in San Francisco (59%), Boston (22%), San Diego (17%)
- Senior Housing: $177M (~11%) — 34 properties operated through Janus Living (JAN), which IPO'd in March 2026

The Janus Living IPO raised $880M in net proceeds at the high end of the valuation range, and the stock has subsequently appreciated to ~$26.49/share. Healthpeak retains 81.6% ownership and serves as external manager.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Drivers:**
- Senior housing demographics: 80+ population growing at fastest pace since Baby Boom. Janus Living targeting 10%+ NOI growth with $400M in acquisitions under contract.
- Outpatient medical: stable, low-single-digit growth supported by hospital system affiliations, in-place rent escalators (3-4% annual), and aging population utilization trends.
- Capital recycling: selling at 6.1% cap rates (Blackstone), buying back stock at implied 7%+ cap rates = accretive value transfer.

**Negative Drivers:**
- Lab occupancy decline (97.8% → 90.1% same-store; 88.2% total) is structural, not cyclical. NIH funding cuts, biotech venture capital drought, new supply deliveries, and drug pricing uncertainty create a multi-quarter recovery timeline at minimum.
- Estimate revisions are decisively negative: FY2026 EPS estimate fell 21% over 90 days; FY2027 fell 33%.
- Total same-store NOI growth is flat (0.0% in Q1 2026). Without lab recovery, portfolio growth is constrained to low-single digits.

The growth story is: (1) senior housing via Janus is excellent but now partly separated; (2) outpatient medical is steady but unexciting; (3) lab is a drag of uncertain duration. Net portfolio growth probably runs 1-3% until lab stabilizes.

DOC's 11.3x P/FFO appears cheap relative to peers, but the comparison requires adjustment: DOC has a lab segment in decline that peers do not, and the trailing EPS figure ($0.10) reflects significant impairments and non-cash items. The relevant question is whether the market is appropriately discounting lab risk or over-discounting it.

**Sum-of-Parts Analysis:**
- Janus Living stake (81.6% × $6.9B market cap): ~$5.6B
- Market Cap: $13.3B
- Net Debt attributable to DOC ex-Janus: ~$8.6B
- Implied Enterprise Value for OM + Lab: ~$16.3B ($13.3B - $5.6B + $8.6B)
- OM + Lab Adjusted NOI (~$1.36B): Implied cap rate ~8.3%

The Blackstone deal validated outpatient medical at 6.1% cap rates. If the OM portfolio ($796M NOI) is worth $13B at 6.1%, and lab ($567M NOI, declining) is worth the residual $3.3B, that implies lab trades at a ~17% cap rate — clearly punitive. This suggests either: (a) meaningful discount for lab risk that could narrow on stabilization, or (b) the market believes Janus's implied value overstates the value actually accessible to DOC shareholders (81.6% is not liquid, management agreement termination risk exists, potential for DOC to distribute or sell JAN stake creates tax/structural friction).

The truth is likely between these extremes. There is probably 10-20% upside to fair value on lab stabilization, but the May 6 rally already captured a portion of that gap.

## Competitive Landscape

**Outpatient Medical:** Healthpeak is the largest player post-merger. 79% hospital-campus adjacency creates high barriers — physicians want proximity to surgical and imaging facilities. 96% health-system affiliation and 72% triple-net structure provide stability. Competition from Healthcare Realty (HR) and Physicians Realty (now merged into DOC).

**Lab/Life Science:** Competes with Alexandria Real Estate (ARE) and BioMed Realty. The San Francisco, Boston, and San Diego markets are experiencing elevated vacancy industrywide. DOC's lab portfolio is high-quality (purpose-built, Class A) but suffering from the same demand-side pressures affecting all participants. New supply delivered in 2023-2025 still leasing up across markets.

**Senior Housing:** Through Janus Living, competes with Welltower (WELL), Ventas (VTR), and NHI. Janus is differentiated by its focus on life plan communities (continuing care retirement communities) with 100+ units in pro-business states. The IPO at premium valuation validates market acceptance of this strategy.

Competitive position is stable-to-improving in outpatient medical (scale advantages from merger), stable-to-deteriorating in lab (market headwinds), and improving in senior housing (Janus growth).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Lab Segment — Severe and Ongoing:**
The 770bp occupancy decline is the most concerning data point. Management expects improvement through 2026, but recovery depends on: (a) biotech venture funding normalizing, (b) NIH policy stabilization, (c) new supply absorption. None of these is within management's control. If lab occupancy stabilizes at 85-88% rather than recovering to 95%+, NOI from this segment could decline another 10-15% from current levels.

**2. Interest Rate / Refinancing:**
Each $1B refinanced at 150bps higher costs ~$15M annually. Total debt service is already consuming significant cash flow.

**3. Medicaid / Site-Neutral Payment Policy:**
Proposed site-neutral payments could reduce reimbursement for off-campus outpatient procedures. HCA Healthcare (15% of outpatient medical segment revenue) would be affected. This is a known risk without a precise timeline.

**4. Janus Living Structure:**
The 81.6% stake creates a holding company discount dynamic. DOC shareholders own DOC, which owns JAN, which operates senior housing. Tax friction on distribution/sale and potential conflicts between DOC and JAN boards create structural complexity.

**5. Geographic Concentration:**
59% of lab portfolio in San Francisco (earthquake risk, regulatory environment), 69% of life plan communities in Florida (hurricane risk — Hurricane Milton caused damage in 2024).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-24, 13-14x FFO):**
- Lab occupancy stabilizes and begins recovering in H2 2026 as biotech funding normalizes
- Janus Living continues to appreciate, and DOC potentially distributes JAN shares or increases monetization
- Blackstone validates further outpatient medical sales at 6%+ cap rates, demonstrating NAV well above market price
- Rate environment moderates, reducing refinancing headwind
- Sum-of-parts discount narrows as analysts upgrade

**Bear Case ($15-16, 9-10x FFO):**
- Lab occupancy deteriorates further to 85% or below; biotech funding winter extends through 2027
- Higher rates compress REIT valuations across the sector
- Site-neutral payment policies implemented, pressuring outpatient medical economics
- Janus Living management agreement terminated, removing fee income and operational control
- Medicaid cuts reduce senior housing census, impacting Janus's growth trajectory

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Lab same-store occupancy shows sequential improvement for 2 consecutive quarters (currently declining)
2. Stock pulls back below $17.50 (10x P/FFO), restoring meaningful margin of safety vs. sum-of-parts
3. Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive for FY2026-2027

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Lab same-store occupancy falls below 85% (indicating structural demand destruction rather than cyclical weakness)
2. Dividend cut or AFFO payout ratio exceeds 90%
3. Janus Living management agreement terminated or JAN stock declines >30% (reducing DOC's sum-of-parts value)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) will provide visibility on lab leasing velocity and whether Q1's -7.2% SS NOI is the trough or a continuation of deterioration.

## Conclusion

Healthpeak Properties presents a classic "good assets, complex structure, segment in distress" situation. The Janus Living IPO created real value, the outpatient medical portfolio is well-positioned with validated private-market pricing, and management is buying back stock at depressed levels — all positive signals. But the lab segment's rapid deterioration (accounting for 37% of NOI), decisively negative estimate revisions, and elevated rate environment pressuring all REIT valuations create substantial offsetting risks.

At $19.50 after the May 6 surge, the stock is no longer at the depressed levels ($15.70-$16.50) where management was repurchasing and where the sum-of-parts discount was widest. The dividend yield of 6.3% provides income support, but the growth profile is flat until lab stabilizes. HOLD is appropriate — the stock offers reasonable income and potential upside on lab recovery, but lacks the asymmetric risk/reward required for a BUY at current levels, particularly given negative estimate revision momentum and the broader rate environment.
