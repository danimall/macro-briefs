## Executive Summary

SBA Communications is a leading independent tower REIT with 46,328 wireless infrastructure sites across the US, Latin America, and Africa. The company's core business model — leasing antenna space on owned towers under long-term contracts with built-in escalators — generates predictable revenue but is under pressure from three converging headwinds: a $4B+ refinancing cliff on debt carrying sub-3% coupons, ~$170M in annualized customer churn from Sprint decommissioning and the EchoStar default, and declining AFFO per share ($12.85 in 2025, down 3.9% from $13.37 in 2024). The stock has missed earnings estimates in all four reported quarters.

On April 2, 2026, Bloomberg reported that SBAC is exploring a potential sale after receiving preliminary takeover interest, with KKR and Brookfield cited as interested parties. The stock surged 18.9% on the news and now trades at $220.09, roughly in line with the analyst consensus mean of $225.55. A take-private at 25-30x AFFO would imply $321-$386 per share, but the $13B debt load and looming refinancing needs complicate any transaction. No formal process has been announced, and the stock is now pricing in a meaningful probability of a deal. The fundamental trajectory absent a sale is deteriorating, with EPS estimates revised down 15.7% over 90 days and a current ratio of 0.29x signaling acute near-term liquidity tightness. We rate SBAC AVOID.

## Company Overview

SBA Communications operates as a REIT focused on wireless communication infrastructure, principally macro tower structures. The company generates 97.9% of segment operating profit from site leasing, with the remainder from site development services. Geographically, domestic operations contribute ~74.7% of segment profit, with international operations (Brazil, Central America, Africa) contributing the balance.

The business model is conceptually attractive: towers are irreplaceable assets in strategic locations, protected by zoning and permitting barriers, with operating leverage as additional tenants are co-located on existing structures at minimal incremental cost. Contracts typically run 5-15 years with fixed or CPI-linked escalators. SBAC owns or controls the underlying land at ~71% of its tower sites with average remaining ground lease terms of ~35 years.

The customer base is severely concentrated. T-Mobile (31.1%), AT&T (20.3%), and Verizon (15.1%) account for 66.5% of total revenue and 87.8% of domestic leasing revenue. T-Mobile alone drives 77.9% of site development revenue.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Total 2025 revenue was $2.815B, up 5.1% reported (+5.5% constant currency). The organic growth story is weak. Domestic site leasing grew just 0.2% YoY to $1.866B, with organic growth fully offset by Sprint decommissioning churn. International leasing grew 6.0% (7.7% constant currency) but was boosted by the Millicom acquisition (7,110 towers). Site development revenue surged 59.9% to $245M, but this segment contributes only 2.1% of operating profit.

Adjusted EBITDA of $1.912B grew just 0.9%, indicating margin pressure from the integration of acquired towers and rising interest costs. Net income of $1.054B was flattered by $208M in asset sale gains (Philippines, Colombia, Canada exits) and a $121.5M FX swing on intercompany loans. On a constant currency, normalized basis, growth was approximately 5.7%.

AFFO per share declined 3.9% from $13.37 to $12.85. This is the wrong direction for a company trading at ~17x AFFO.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $1.291B, down 3.3% from $1.335B in 2024. Free cash flow after capex of $225M was approximately $1.067B, implying a 4.6% FCF yield on the current $23.3B market cap. Total capital returns (dividends of $479M + buybacks of $498M) consumed ~$977M, nearly all of FCF, leaving minimal capacity for debt reduction.

### Balance Sheet

SBAC carries $12.96B in total debt against negative stockholders' equity of -$4.85B. The negative equity is a structural artifact of aggressive share buybacks and dividend payments that have accumulated $7.25B in negative retained earnings. While this is intentional within the leveraged REIT model, the resulting metrics are extreme:

- **Net Debt/EBITDA:** ~6.4x, approaching the 6.5x covenant limit
- **Current Ratio:** 0.29x (down from 1.10x in 2024)
- **Cash on hand:** $265M at year-end; revolver balance swelled to $1.205B post-year-end
- **Altman Z-Score:** 0.21

The Z-Score is mechanically inappropriate for REITs, but the leverage ratios are not. At 6.4x Net Debt/EBITDA, SBAC is at the upper boundary of covenant compliance with minimal cushion for any revenue shortfall.

### The Refinancing Cliff

This is the single most important financial dynamic. Over $4 billion of securitized tower debt matures in 2026-2028, carrying coupons ranging from 1.63% to 2.33%:

| Tranche | Amount | Coupon | Maturity |
|---------|--------|--------|----------|
| 2021-1C | $1.165B | 1.631% | Nov 2026 |
| 2021-2C | $895M | 1.840% | Apr 2027 |
| 2020-2C | $600M | 2.328% | Jan 2028 |
| 2020 Senior Notes | $1.5B | 3.875% | Feb 2027 |

If refinanced at 5.0-6.0%, the incremental annual interest expense on the tower securities alone would be approximately $80-120M, reducing AFFO per share by roughly $0.75-1.10. Interest expense already rose 17% in 2025 ($468M vs. $400M). With the Fed funds rate at 3.64% and inflation reaccelerating above 3.3%, the probability of rate cuts providing relief is low. The 2020-1C tranche ($750M at 1.884%) was already repaid in January 2026 using the revolver, replacing sub-2% fixed-rate debt with variable-rate borrowing.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Is Stalling

Domestic site leasing, which represents nearly 75% of operating profit, grew 0.2% in 2025. The company's 2026 outlook calls for domestic site leasing revenue of $1.860-1.890B, essentially flat to down slightly. Management guides for domestic churn of $132-136M in 2026, partially offset by new leasing activity.

The churn problem has three components:
1. **Sprint decommissioning:** ~$75M remaining over several years
2. **EchoStar default:** ~$56M expected in 2026 — EchoStar outright defaulted on payments in December 2025
3. **International (Oi Wireline):** $36-40M expected in 2026

Total 2026 churn guidance of $168-176M represents ~6.7% of site leasing revenue. This structural headwind will persist through at least 2027-2028.

### International Growth — Acquisition-Driven, Not Organic

The 6.0% international revenue growth was substantially driven by the Millicom acquisition. Brazil (13.6% of site leasing revenue) carries FX risk — the Real weakened 4% in 2025 — and the Brazilian tax authorities have assessed potential deficiencies of up to $282M (principal plus penalties) for 2017-2020.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 15.7% over 90 days (from $8.82 to $7.43). Next-year estimates are down 5.3% over 90 days. The 30-day revisions show stabilization (+0.5% current quarter, -0.4% next quarter), but the 90-day trajectory is decisively negative. Four consecutive quarterly earnings misses — including a -22.2% miss in Q1 2025 and a -10.3% miss in Q4 2025 — erode confidence in forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $220.09, SBAC trades at:
- **~17.1x trailing AFFO** ($12.85)
- **~22.8x trailing P/E** ($9.80 EPS)
- **~29.6x forward P/E** (on current-year EPS estimate of $7.43)

Against the peer set, the P/E of 22.8x is below the peer median of 28.4x, but the relevant comparison for tower REITs is AFFO multiples against American Tower (AMT) and Crown Castle (CCI). The 2.2% dividend yield is well below the peer median of 4.4%, reflecting both lower absolute yield and the company's preference for buybacks over dividends.

The valuation is not obviously cheap given declining AFFO. At $7.43 in current-year EPS, the forward P/E of ~29.6x is above the peer median. The stock has rallied 30%+ from its March 2026 lows of $162.41, driven entirely by sale speculation. On fundamentals alone, the Q1 2026 slide from $245 to $162 was pricing in deteriorating earnings power — a repricing that was directionally correct.

### M&A Valuation Context

Infrastructure funds typically acquire tower assets at 25-30x AFFO. At $12.85 AFFO/share, this implies a potential takeout range of $321-$386/share, or 46-75% upside. However:
- The $13B debt load means the acquirer's total enterprise commitment is ~$36B
- Refinancing the debt at higher rates reduces the cash flow available to the acquirer
- No formal process, advisor, or timeline has been disclosed
- The stock already reflects significant M&A premium (~30% from pre-report levels)

The analyst consensus target range of $182-$280 brackets the current price, with the median at $221.50. The $280 high target likely reflects partial M&A probability; the $182 low reflects a no-deal fundamental scenario.

## Competitive Landscape

SBAC operates in a three-player oligopoly alongside American Tower and Crown Castle. Barriers to entry are substantial — new tower construction requires zoning approvals, environmental review, and multi-year permitting processes. Existing tower locations are strategically chosen and difficult to replicate.

SBAC is the most vulnerable of the three:
- **Most leveraged:** 6.4x Net Debt/EBITDA vs. ~5.0x for AMT and ~5.5x for CCI
- **Least diversified:** No meaningful fiber, small cell, or data center exposure. The 10-K specifically acknowledges the company is "not as diversified into non-macro sites and other technologies and architectures as some of our competitors"
- **Technology risk:** Low-earth orbit satellites (Starlink/Amazon Kuiper), small cells, and DAS present long-term substitution risk for traditional macro towers, particularly for coverage use cases
- **Customer concentration:** 88% domestic revenue from three carriers is higher than peers

The competitive moat remains intact for the medium term — carriers cannot practically replace macro towers in the current network architecture — but SBAC's positioning relative to peers is the weakest of the three.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Refinancing cliff ($4B+ at sub-3% rates, 2026-2028):** As detailed in Financial Analysis, potential $80-120M annual interest expense increase, directly compressing AFFO by 6-9%
2. **Customer churn ($168-176M guided for 2026):** Sprint, EchoStar, and Oi collectively reduce site leasing revenue ~6.7%
3. **Leverage proximity to covenant limits (6.4x vs. 6.5x max):** Any revenue shortfall or acquisition misstep could trigger covenant discussions
4. **Customer concentration:** Three carriers = 88% of domestic revenue
5. **International risks:** FX exposure (Brazil Real), $282M potential Brazilian tax liability, right-of-use tower termination risk

### Secondary Risks

6. **Technology substitution:** LEO satellites, small cells, DAS as long-term macro tower alternatives
7. **Rate environment:** Higher-for-longer rates directly impair both refinancing economics and carrier capex willingness
8. **Management alignment:** CEO compensation increased 29% while AFFO per share declined 3.9%
9. **Sale process uncertainty:** No formal announcement; stock has priced in meaningful deal probability that may not materialize

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.1% is essentially at fair value relative to 31.2% historical volatility (56th percentile). The term structure is in backwardation (32.1% near-term vs. 21.7% at 6 months), reflecting near-term M&A uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio of 0.10 indicates extremely light put positioning, meaning options investors are not hedging downside. This creates vulnerability: if the sale process fails or is delayed, there is no protective put base to cushion the decline. The 3-month tenor shows elevated put/call volume ratio of 4.86, suggesting some institutional positioning for downside around that timeframe.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($280-$380)
- KKR or Brookfield completes an acquisition at 25-30x AFFO ($321-$386/share)
- Even absent a full sale, strategic alternatives (partial asset sales, JV structures) could unlock value
- 5G densification accelerates, driving new tenant additions that offset churn
- Rate environment eases, reducing refinancing cost and supporting the multiple

### Bear Case ($145-$170)
- Sale process collapses or fails to materialize, removing ~30% of current price premium
- Refinancing occurs at 5.5-6.5%, adding $100M+ in annual interest expense, driving AFFO to $11-$12
- Churn continues at $150M+/year for 3+ years, compressing domestic revenue
- Rate hikes further impair the multiple and carrier spending
- Brazilian tax liability materializes (~$282M)
- Stock returns to fundamental value of 12-14x AFFO = $132-$180

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The primary catalyst is the failure or indefinite delay of the reported sale process. No formal process has been announced; the stock moved 18.9% on a Bloomberg report citing "preliminary interest." If no deal materializes within 60-90 days, the M&A premium should deflate. Q1 2026 earnings (likely late April/early May 2026) could show continued AFFO deterioration and provide a second catalyst.

**Timing window:** 60-90 days. If no deal announcement by mid-June 2026, the premium erodes. The next earnings report provides a discrete event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is only 2.9% of float with 2.5 days to cover. This is uncrowded — favorable for a short position (no squeeze risk), but also means informed short sellers are not currently positioned against the stock, which warrants caution.

**Borrow and options signal:** Low short interest means borrow should be readily available at standard rates. The backwardated IV term structure and 3-month put/call volume ratio of 4.86 suggest some institutional put buying at that horizon, consistent with a view that the M&A premium fades. The near-term put/call volume ratio of 0.03 suggests no immediate hedging demand.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the sale process fails, a return to $170-$180 (pre-report fundamental range) represents 18-23% downside from $220. Upside risk from an actual bid at $320+ represents 45%+ loss on a short position. The reward:risk ratio is approximately 0.4:1 to 0.5:1 — **unfavorable for an active short.**

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE.** The binary M&A outcome creates severely asymmetric risk against the short. While the fundamental case for the stock deteriorating absent a sale is clear, the potential for a $320+ bid makes the downside-to-upside ratio roughly 0.5:1. The correct position is to not own the stock, not to short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Re-entry Criteria (AVOID)
- **M&A confirmation:** If a formal bid is announced with financing committed at $290+ per share, the risk/reward shifts to BUY on any pullback to assess deal completion probability
- **Fundamental improvement:** If AFFO per share stabilizes and begins growing (would require churn subsiding + successful refinancing at <5%), reassess at <15x AFFO ($190 or below)
- **Rate relief:** If the Fed cuts rates 150bp+ and the 10-year falls below 3.5%, refinancing economics improve materially — reassess the interest expense trajectory

### Timeline
The headwinds (refinancing cliff, churn, declining AFFO) persist through at least mid-2028. The M&A catalyst should resolve within 90 days — either advancing to a formal process or fading. If no deal by Q3 2026, the stock likely revisits $170-$185 on fundamentals.

## Conclusion

SBA Communications possesses a structurally sound business model with irreplaceable tower assets under long-term contracts with inflation protection. But the financial overlay is unfavorable on multiple dimensions: leverage approaching covenant limits, $4B+ of sub-3% debt requiring refinancing at rates 200-400bp higher, declining AFFO per share, four consecutive earnings misses, persistent customer churn, and decisively negative estimate revisions.

The sale exploration is the sole positive catalyst. Infrastructure funds value tower assets highly, and a take-private at 25-30x AFFO would imply substantial upside. But the stock has already rallied 30%+ on the report, no formal process has been announced, and the debt load complicates any transaction. Buying at $220 means paying for a deal that may not happen while bearing the full downside of deteriorating fundamentals if it doesn't. The Wall Street consensus (12 Holds, 9 Buys, 0 Sells) reflects this ambivalence.

For investors not already positioned, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside on a probability-weighted basis. We recommend AVOID.
