**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Corcept Therapeutics is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company built entirely on one scientific idea: pharmacologically modulating the effects of cortisol through the glucocorticoid receptor. For over a decade the entire revenue base was a single product, Korlym (mifepristone), for hyperglycemia secondary to Cushing's syndrome. That model is now being dismantled and rebuilt simultaneously. The legacy Korlym cash cow is being permanently eroded by generics: Teva launched in January 2024 and the Federal Circuit affirmed non-infringement on February 19, 2026, with Sun and Hikma queued behind it. At the same time, the company received an unexpected Complete Response Letter for relacorilant in Cushing's on December 30, 2025 (the expected next-generation replacement for the endocrine franchise), and then, in March 2026, won FDA approval for the same molecule, branded Lifyorli, in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer with a genuinely differentiated overall-survival dataset. The Q1 2026 net loss of $(31.8)M is the visible cost of this pivot: SG&A jumped 60% year-over-year to build an oncology commercial engine ahead of Lifyorli revenue.

The forward-looking data supports a constructive-but-guarded stance. Consensus EPS estimates for next year have risen +99.4% over 90 days (to $3.21) and current-year estimates are up +37.1%, driven by the ovarian-cancer launch and the June 17, 2026 Cushing's NDA resubmission targeting a 6-month review. Revenue grew 4.9% year-over-year in Q1 on 12.4% volume growth offset by a 6.7% price decline from authorized-generic cannibalization. The balance sheet is strong: roughly $515M in cash and marketable securities, no funded debt, Altman Z of 4.69. This is not a solvency or liquidity story.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the ~$90 price, having recovered from a $28.66 low earlier this year, now embeds a good deal of the recovery. At 223x trailing earnings the stock screens absurdly, but that is meaningless at a launch-year trough; the more relevant read is ~28x next-year consensus EPS of $3.21, which is reasonable for the growth but not cheap, and which depends on the Lifyorli ramp delivering and the Cushing's resubmission succeeding. Against that you have a dense, under-provisioned litigation cluster (securities, derivative, and antitrust cases, the last with a March 2027 trial), a CRL whose surmountability is still uncertain, mifepristone political risk, and uniformly one-directional insider selling with no open-market buying. The risk/reward is balanced, so we stay on HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and specific upgrade triggers.

## Company Overview

Corcept has discovered and patented five structurally distinct series of selective cortisol modulators spanning more than 1,000 compounds, deployed across endocrinology, oncology, metabolic disease, and neurology. Two products carry the P&L today:

- **Korlym (mifepristone)** — approved 2012 for hyperglycemia secondary to endogenous hypercortisolism (Cushing's syndrome). This has been the sole revenue engine for over a decade. Corcept launched its own authorized generic in June 2024 to defend against Teva's entry.
- **Lifyorli (relacorilant)** — a next-generation selective cortisol modulator, FDA-approved March 2026 in combination with nab-paclitaxel for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (1–3 prior regimens including bevacizumab). U.S. launch began April 2026, with an NCCN preferred-regimen listing that same month.

Relacorilant's differentiation versus Korlym is real: no progesterone-receptor affinity (so it is not an abortifacient and does not cause endometrial thickening), no hypokalemia (which affected 44% of patients in Korlym's pivotal trial and drives discontinuation), and no QT prolongation. Those attributes matter both clinically and, in the case of the progesterone-receptor point, politically. The company operates as a single reportable segment under CEO and co-founder Dr. Joseph Belanoff, with CFO Atabak Mokari.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** The financial position is strong and uncomplicated. Cash and marketable securities were $515.4M at March 31, 2026 (down modestly from $532.4M at year-end 2025), with no funded debt (reported D/E of 0.29 is lease-driven), a current ratio of 2.9x, and $611.6M in retained earnings. Altman Z of 4.69 sits comfortably in the safe zone. Management states it can fund operations for the next 12 months and beyond without raising capital. This is the dimension where CORT is unambiguously above-average, and it is what allows the company to absorb a launch-year loss without financing risk.

**Revenue and margins.** Q1 2026 net product revenue was $164.9M, up 4.9% year-over-year from $157.2M. The composition is the story: volume rose 12.4% while average price fell 6.7% as the lower-priced authorized generic cannibalized branded Korlym, partly offset by an August 2025 branded price increase. Underlying demand is healthy; realized pricing is structurally impaired by the generic transition.

**Earnings.** Q1 2026 swung to a net loss of $(31.8)M, or $(0.30) per share, from net income of $20.5M ($0.17 diluted) a year earlier. The swing is almost entirely SG&A, which rose ~60% year-over-year to $145.4M to fund the Lifyorli launch and expanded commercial infrastructure; R&D rose 9% to $66.3M. Operating cash flow turned slightly negative (−$16.8M) for the same reason. FY2025 by contrast showed ~13% net margin, ~15% ROE, and roughly $142M of free cash flow (~18.6% FCF margin). The Q1 loss reflects a deliberate investment cycle ahead of revenue, not a broken business, but management guides both SG&A and R&D higher in 2026 than 2025, so the trailing P&L will look poor throughout the year regardless of launch success.

**Earnings-surprise track record.** The recent record is mixed and deteriorating on the print itself: two beats and two misses over four quarters, with a −34.6% miss in Q4 2025 and a large −204.3% miss in Q1 2026 (reported $(0.30) vs a $(0.10) estimate). The misses are launch-spending-driven rather than demand-driven, but they do temper confidence in near-quarter estimate precision.

**Capital allocation.** The January 2024 $200M buyback is largely executed (~$11.4M remaining at March 31, 2026). No dividend. The company committed an additional $10.2M for Korlym API in March 2026 to build strategic inventory, a defensive supply move consistent with protecting the legacy franchise.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest part of the forward case, and the estimate-revision data is where it shows most clearly. Next-year consensus EPS has risen +99.4% over 90 days (to $3.21) and +28.5% over the last 30 days; current-year EPS is up +37.1% over 90 days and +14.2% over 30 days. That revision velocity comfortably clears the threshold that typically flags a genuine fundamental turn, and it coincides with the two forward catalysts: the Lifyorli ovarian-cancer launch and the Cushing's resubmission.

The oncology dataset underpinning the launch is the single most important positive. ROSELLA met its progression-free-survival endpoint in March 2025 (HR 0.70, ~30% risk reduction), and in January 2026 delivered a strong overall-survival result: HR 0.65, median OS 16.0 vs 11.9 months, p=0.0004. In a competitive but underserved space, Lifyorli's advantage is that the OS benefit held across all clinically relevant subgroups and it is biomarker-agnostic, unlike the folate-receptor (Elahere), HER2 (Enhertu), and PD-1 (newly, Merck's Keytruda) options that each address a slice of the population. The NCCN preferred-regimen listing supports uptake.

The pipeline is broad and built from the same platform: BELLA (ovarian/endometrial Ph2), TRIDENT (pancreatic Ph2), STELLA (cervical Ph2), relacorilant plus enzalutamide (prostate), nenocorilant plus nivolumab (immuno-oncology Ph1b), miricorilant (MASH, Ph2b enrollment complete), and dazucorilant (ALS — DAZALS missed its primary ALSFRS-R endpoint but showed an 87% lower risk of death at 2 years, HR 0.13, with a Phase 3 planned). Separately, the CATALYST and MOMENTUM screening studies (23.8% of difficult-to-control T2D patients and 27.3% of resistant-hypertension patients showed hypercortisolism) are a deliberate market-development effort to enlarge the diagnosed hypercortisolism population regardless of the relacorilant CRL outcome.

Weighing growth prospects heavily is appropriate here — it is the most reliable forward discriminator in this system's experience, and the revision data is unambiguously positive. The offset is that these are shots on goal against a legacy revenue base that is contracting on price, so net franchise growth in 2026 depends on Lifyorli offsetting Korlym erosion faster than SG&A consumes it.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 223x is not a usable metric in a launch-investment trough year; using it would penalize the stock for the very spending that builds the oncology franchise. The relevant anchor is forward earnings on the sharply rising estimates. At $90.52 the stock trades at roughly 28x next-year consensus EPS. That is a reasonable multiple for a company projected to swing from a launch-year loss to $3.21 the following year, and it sits below the pharmaceutical peer-median P/E of 44.0 (ALKS 49.4x, AMRX 44.0x, APLS 38.4x). On a growth-adjusted basis, a PEG well under 1.0 on the next-year turn would ordinarily argue for a higher valuation score.

The reason the valuation score is only average is that the 28x forward multiple already discounts a successful transition. To get to $3.21 next year, Lifyorli has to ramp on plan and the Cushing's resubmission likely has to succeed; both are probable but neither is certain, and the CRL demonstrated the market can be surprised by this franchise. The stock has already re-rated from its low to $90, so much of the recovery from the CRL trough is in the price. The consensus target set — low $88, median $95, mean $106, high $135 — brackets the current price closely, with the median only ~5% above spot and the mean ~17% above. Sentiment among the small analyst pool is degrading, with one buy moving to hold recently. Analyst targets act as floors on cheap inflection names, but with the stock trading near the median target and estimates dependent on binary regulatory and launch outcomes, I treat this consensus as roughly fair rather than as a floor.

The broader caution from cross-sectional history is that cheap-looking multiples have been systematically anti-predictive; the discount to the peer median is not, on its own, a reason to be more constructive. Here the multiple is not even the source of the bull case — the growth is — so I score valuation at 5.0: neither a value trap nor an obvious mispricing, with the upside and downside roughly balanced around the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

In hypercortisolism, Corcept remains the dominant pharmacologic player with deep disease-state expertise and a specialized commercial and medical-liaison force. Competition is intensifying: Recordati's osilodrostat was expanded in April 2025 to any etiology of hypercortisolism, alongside pasireotide, Xeris's levoketoconazole, and cheap off-label generic ketoconazole. The Korlym franchise itself is structurally capped by the generic loss (Teva's non-infringement affirmed, Sun and Hikma settled to launch behind it). The competitive trajectory of the legacy franchise is negative.

The offsetting positive is the entry into platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, where Lifyorli's biomarker-agnostic OS benefit and NCCN preferred listing give it a distinct position against Elahere (folate-receptor-α), Enhertu (HER2), bevacizumab, and Merck's newly approved Keytruda (PD-1+, 50–60% of patients). This is a genuinely underserved space and a larger market than Cushing's ever was. Net across the two franchises, the competitive trajectory is roughly stable-to-improving: legacy erosion is being replaced by a differentiated new-market entry, which is why competitive position scores modestly above neutral rather than lower.

## Risk Assessment

This is where the story is dense and under-provisioned, and it is the primary reason for the HOLD.

1. **The Cushing's CRL.** The FDA declined to approve relacorilant for Cushing's, requiring "additional evidence of effectiveness" even while acknowledging GRACE met its primary endpoint. GRADIENT missed its head-to-head comparison versus placebo on the primary SBP endpoint, which may be central to the agency's concern. That the June 17, 2026 resubmission targets a 6-month review implies management believes the requirement is addressable with existing data rather than a new trial. This is the key binary: approval on this cycle (roughly end-2026/early-2027) restores the endocrine growth thesis; a second rejection would be a material negative.

2. **Generic erosion.** The en banc rehearing petition on the Federal Circuit Teva ruling is a long shot. Sun and Hikma are positioned to add generics, and Korlym pricing will keep bleeding.

3. **Litigation cluster.** A securities class action (N.D. Cal., filed Feb 2026, class period Oct 2024–Dec 2025) alleges misleading statements about the Cushing's NDA; a derivative suit (LeRiger v. Belanoff) includes a disgorgement-of-insider-trading-profits claim; and Teva/payor antitrust litigation (with a parallel Aetna/Humana/HCSC/Molina suit) attacks the exclusive specialty-pharmacy model, trial set for March 2027. The open DOJ/NJ subpoena on Korlym promotion (Nov 2021) remains within scope. The company records zero loss provisions for any of it, which means these are genuine tail risks the balance sheet masks. The antitrust matter is the most under-appreciated, particularly given the mid-litigation switch from Optime to Curant, which plaintiffs have moved to target.

4. **Mifepristone political risk.** Korlym's active ingredient is the abortion pill; HHS announced in September 2025 it will re-examine mifepristone safety. Distribution restrictions could bleed into the hypercortisolism use. This is a genuinely idiosyncratic, hard-to-hedge risk.

5. **Reimbursement/policy.** IRA inflation rebates and Medicare dynamics are expected to reduce revenue and profits in 2026 and beyond; OBBBA cuts Medicaid; proposed MFN pricing models loom. Orphan status currently exempts the products from Medicare price negotiation, a protection that is not guaranteed permanent.

6. **Vendor concentration.** A single specialty pharmacy handles >99% of hypercortisolism revenue collection. The 2025 Optime-to-Curant transition was disruptive (Optime "was unable to fully meet demand"), and execution risk on the new vendor is live.

Because these risks are specific, dated, and partly unpriced (a second CRL, an antitrust trial verdict, a mifepristone distribution action), the risk-factors score is below neutral at 4.5. In this system's experience the risk dimension has been a weak forward discriminator, so I do not over-weight it, but the density of binary, under-provisioned exposures here is above what a healthy specialty-pharma balance sheet would suggest.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 61.6% sits below the 90.9% one-year historical volatility (IV is cheap on a −29.3% spread), so single-catalyst hedging is relatively inexpensive, but the term structure is in backwardation (61.6% near-term vs 54.8% long-term) with an elevated put/call skew of 8.3% on the front expiry and an implied ±15.6% move on the nearest expiry. That configuration is consistent with the market pricing an imminent binary — most plausibly launch data points and the Cushing's review clock — with elevated near-term put demand. IV has fallen since mid-June (76.6% → 61.6%) as the price rose 7.6%, suggesting some resolution of the sharpest fear, but the skew and backwardation say the market still views the next few months as event-heavy. For a HOLD, this reinforces waiting for catalyst resolution rather than adding into a priced-in event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($135)
Lifyorli ramps faster than consensus on the strength of its OS benefit and NCCN preferred listing, establishing a durable oncology franchise larger than Cushing's ever was. The Cushing's resubmission clears the 6-month review and restores the endocrine growth engine. Next-year EPS proves conservative, and the pipeline optionality (endometrial, cervical, pancreatic, prostate, the nenocorilant/nivolumab-class combo, miricorilant in MASH, and the dazucorilant ALS survival signal) begins to be priced. At the high analyst target of $135, the stock trades ~42x next-year EPS on a franchise re-rated as a multi-product oncology company. This is roughly +49% from current.

### Bear Case (~$55–60)
The Cushing's resubmission draws a second CRL, confirming the market's original December 2025 read. Lifyorli uptake disappoints against Keytruda and the antibody-drug conjugates, SG&A stays elevated, and the trailing loss persists. Generic erosion of Korlym accelerates as Sun and Hikma enter. The antitrust trial or the mifepristone re-examination introduces a discrete negative. In that scenario the stock retraces much of its recovery toward the mid-$50s, roughly −35% from current, as the growth premium is removed.

### Base Case ($95)
Lifyorli launches solidly but not spectacularly, offsetting Korlym erosion and delivering net franchise growth; the Cushing's resubmission is more likely than not to succeed but on the market's timeline, not immediately. Next-year EPS lands near consensus. The litigation cluster grinds on without a near-term resolution and without a provision. At ~30x next-year EPS the stock holds near the median analyst target of $95, roughly +5% from current. The distribution of outcomes is wide and roughly symmetric around this point, which is the essence of the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is real and supported by exceptional estimate-revision velocity, but the price already discounts much of it and the downside catalysts are specific and unpriced. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately higher, but the two-sided catalyst structure (a differentiated launch and a rising estimate curve against a CRL, generic loss, and litigation) genuinely warrants a balanced call rather than a directional one.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are (1) the next quarterly print showing early Lifyorli revenue against the SG&A ramp, and (2) the Cushing's NDA review decision, expected roughly end-2026/early-2027 on the 6-month clock from the resubmission.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Lifyorli net product revenue in the first two reported quarters running ahead of the trajectory implied by the next-year EPS estimate, with SG&A stabilizing (indicating operating leverage is arriving).
- FDA acceptance and approval of the resubmitted Cushing's NDA, or clear evidence the review is proceeding without a second CRL.
- Any open-market insider purchase, which would be a meaningful directional signal given the current uniform selling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second CRL on the Cushing's resubmission.
- Lifyorli launch metrics materially below plan alongside continued SG&A escalation, pushing next-year EPS estimates lower rather than higher.
- An adverse development in the antitrust litigation (unfavorable pre-trial rulings ahead of the trial) or a mifepristone distribution action from the HHS re-examination that threatens the hypercortisolism use.

## Conclusion

Corcept is a company in the middle of a genuine transformation from a one-drug endocrine company into a commercial oncology company, and the financial statements are temporarily ugly precisely because that transformation is underway. The balance sheet is strong enough to fund it, the ovarian-cancer dataset is differentiated, and the estimate-revision turn (+99% next-year over 90 days) is one of the stronger forward signals available. Those are the reasons this is not an AVOID.

It is not a BUY because the ~$90 price already embeds a successful Lifyorli launch and a Cushing's re-approval at roughly 28x next-year EPS, while the specific downside catalysts (a possible second CRL, an under-provisioned multi-front litigation cluster culminating in a March 2027 antitrust trial, mifepristone political risk, and uniformly one-directional insider selling with no vote of confidence at current levels) leave the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CORT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, an overall score of 6.0 consistent with its recent trajectory, and a clear path to an upgrade if the launch data and the Cushing's review break the company's way.
