## Executive Summary

Core Natural Resources reported second-quarter 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $2.51 and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $323.6M, versus $(0.70) and $144.3M a year earlier. Both figures are unusable as run-rate indicators. In June 2026 Core settled its Leer South combustion insurance claim at the full policy limit of $154.5M, of which $135.1M was recognized in H1 2026 and $125.4M in the second quarter alone: $114.9M as business-interruption income within Other Operating Income and Expense and the remainder as a reduction to cost of sales. Stripping the settlement out of 2026 and adding back the $21.2M of fire and idle costs that depressed the 2025 comparison, underlying Q2 Adjusted EBITDA was approximately $198M against approximately $165M, and H1 was approximately $368M against approximately $325M. The operating improvement is real, roughly 20% in the quarter and 13% in the half, and it is one-fifth the size the headline implies. Consensus appears to have understood this: the adjusted EPS surprise table shows $0.55 actual against $0.42 estimated, a 13-cent beat, not a $2.09 beat.

The composition of that improvement matters more than its size. Metallurgical cash cost per ton fell from $95.93 to $85.65 on the restart of the Leer South longwall, lifting segment cash margin from $8.29/ton to $28.48/ton, the single most valuable operational development at Core in eighteen months. High CV Thermal cash margin per ton fell $1.50 year over year in the quarter and $2.76 for the half, and the segment is now committed and priced at 30.9Mt for 2026 at $58.00/ton against Q2 realizations of $58.11 and prior-year realizations of $60.50, so 2026 thermal economics are locked at a level below the trailing run rate. Powder River Basin cash margin has moved $1.29 → $0.75 → $(0.57)/ton across three quarters on volume that fell from 12.6Mt to 10.2Mt, and the segment posted $(5.8)M of Adjusted EBITDA. Two of four segments are deteriorating while one improves and the export terminal contributes steadily.

The balance sheet is the strongest element of the story and is genuinely unusual for a coal producer: $447.6M of funded debt and finance leases against $473.2M of cash, total net leverage of (0.05)x versus a 2.50x covenant, first-lien gross leverage 0.19x versus 1.50x, interest coverage 32.65x versus a 3.00x minimum, and $1,016M of total liquidity with no revolver or receivables-facility borrowings. Capital returns have been aggressive: $329.2M deployed on 4.3M shares at a $77.04 average, share count down from ~53.7M post-merger to 49.85M, with $670.8M of authorization remaining, equal to about 15% of market capitalization. Against a net-cash, buyback-supported floor sits a forward earnings picture that consensus has been cutting hard: FY2026 down 60.2% and FY2027 down 21.6% over 90 days. My probability-weighted 12-month return from $89.60 is roughly 5%, and three material questions remain open: Leer South gas conditions with the insurance limit now exhausted, three Item 1.04 mine-safety filings in four months whose substance is unverified, and an unnamed CEO transition. This is a HOLD, and the honest classification of that hold is uncertainty rather than a defined growth or income thesis.

## Company Overview

Core Natural Resources was created on January 14, 2025 when CONSOL Energy completed an all-stock merger with Arch Resources. CONSOL was the legal and accounting acquirer; consideration was 24.3 million shares (approximately 45% of post-issuance shares) valued at $2,481.4M, with total consideration adjusted to $2,577.0M after Core's purchase of $98.1M of Arch's WVEDA bonds immediately pre-close. Purchase accounting produced roughly $1.4B of fair-value adjustments, with acquired PP&E recorded at $2,607.8M. The practical consequence for analysis is that pre-2025 financials in the record are CONSOL standalone, and any growth rate computed across the 2024/2025 boundary is a merger artifact. FY2025 DD&A of $621.1M against CONSOL-standalone $223.5M in 2024 reflects both the added asset base and the step-up.

The company operates 11 mines across six states in four reportable segments. High CV Thermal comprises the Pennsylvania Mining Complex (Bailey, Enlow Fork, Harvey longwalls plus the Central Preparation Plant) and West Elk in Colorado. Metallurgical comprises Leer, Leer South, Beckley, Mountain Laurel and Itmann in West Virginia. PRB comprises Black Thunder and Coal Creek in Campbell County, Wyoming. Core Marine Terminal is the Port of Baltimore export facility. Core also holds a 35% equity-method partnership interest in Dominion Terminal Associates at Newport News, where it is a partner rather than the operator.

Coal revenue of $1,126.7M in Q2 2026 split Power Generation $401.0M, Industrial $366.7M and Metallurgical $358.9M; domestic $389.9M versus export $736.8M, so Core is now approximately 65% an export business by coal revenue, up from 54% a year earlier. The shift was driven almost entirely by Industrial export revenue rising from $232.9M to $340.2M. That is a change in end-market and geographic exposure, not a change in counterparty concentration; the filings state no individual customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in either period, unchanged rather than improving.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 is not a usable base.** Reported revenue was $4,164.8M with an operating loss of $182.1M and a net loss of $153.2M, distorted by $101M of Leer South fire-extinguishment and idle costs, $124.2M of H1 G&A (against $62.7M in H1 2026) including $35.1M of post-close merger costs and $2.6M of inventory step-up, and an $11.68M loss on debt extinguishment from the March 2025 tax-exempt bond refinancing. Operating cash flow was $305.8M against $284.6M of capex, leaving $21.2M of free cash flow.

**H1 2026 is the better base, with its own distortion.** Revenue $2,225.3M (+5.0%), income from operations $188.5M against $(73.2)M, net income $147.5M against $(105.8)M, diluted EPS $2.91. Adjusted EBITDA $503.5M against $267.8M. Ex-insurance, Metallurgical Adjusted EBITDA was roughly $75M in Q2 2026 against roughly $18.5M a year earlier. That $56M swing is essentially the entire underlying improvement, and it came from the Leer South longwall replacing higher-cost continuous-miner sections. The mechanism is repeatable so long as the longwall runs. It is also now fully in the base, so the year-over-year comparison gets harder from Q4 2026 onward.

High CV Thermal deterioration is a price/mix outcome, not a volume one: tons sold were flat in the quarter and up (15.5Mt → 16.1Mt) for the half, while realized price fell $2.39/ton. Management attributes the decline to increased export logistics and transportation obligations, which mechanically inflates gross revenue and gross cost simultaneously and compresses netback. The press release separately concedes soft domestic thermal conditions from moderate temperatures, low gas prices and inflated customer stockpiles. Both effects are likely operating; the filings do not decompose them, and the margin outcome is negative under either reading.

PRB requires closer scrutiny than the seasonality explanation supports. Spring is a known shipment trough and diesel costs rose, but the Q2-over-Q2 comparison holds season constant and still shows $1.86/ton of margin deterioration on 2.4Mt less volume. Full-year guidance of 47–50Mt against 22.1Mt shipped in H1 requires 25–28Mt in H2, a demanding step-up, and the $13.25–13.75/ton cost guidance requires the absorption that only that volume delivers. PRB is $249.8M of $6,122.4M in segment assets, so the capital at risk is small, but it holds a disproportionate share of reclamation obligations and it is currently subtracting from consolidated earnings.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $6,122.4M, liabilities $2,403.5M, equity $3,718.9M. Cash $473.2M plus $171.8M restricted. Funded debt and finance leases $447.6M net of issuance costs: finance leases $127.5M at 6.92%, and three unsecured tax-exempt series issued March 27, 2025 — WVEDA $106.4M at 5.45% (2055 maturity), MEDCO $102.9M at 5.00% (2048), PEDFA $97.6M at 5.45% (2051) — each carrying mandatory tender in March 2035. There is no near-term refinancing cliff. Liquidity of $1,016M comprises $474M cash and short-term investments, $210M receivables-facility availability and $600M of revolver commitment less $268M of letters of credit; neither the revolver (maturing April 2029) nor the $250M receivables facility (maturing July 2028) is drawn.

The receivables structure is on-balance-sheet secured capacity, not a true sale: ten operating subsidiaries contribute receivables to Core Receivable Company LLC, which pledges its interest to PNC and Regions; the parent guarantees originator and servicer performance but explicitly not collectability or obligor creditworthiness, and receivables ($351.8M net inside the SPV) are not derecognized.

**The obligation stack is larger than funded debt.** Asset retirement obligations total $522.2M against a $134M PRB reclamation fund and $19M of water-treatment trusts. Long-dated employee liabilities total roughly $534M: pneumoconiosis $256.8M, OPEB $185.4M, workers' compensation $71.3M, pension $20.9M. Surety bonds outstanding are $1,062.5M (environmental $859.1M) and letters of credit $267.6M; those are contingent capacity rather than recorded liabilities and should not be stacked on top of the AROs they support. On an economic basis, adding roughly $370M of unfunded ARO and $534M of legacy employee obligations to net funded debt of approximately $(25)M gives adjusted net debt near $880M and an adjusted enterprise value near $5.4B. Core remains conservatively capitalized; the durable claim on future cash flow is reclamation and legacy benefits, not interest.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $369.8M (H1 2025 $110.5M) on $175.0M of capex. Q2 alone produced $250.4M of OCF and a company-defined $148.0M of free cash flow. Neither should be annualized. Q2 OCF includes $88.1M of insurance cash and is stated after a $(69.6)M working-capital drag; the remaining $37.9M of insurance cash landed in July. Full-year capex guidance of $325–375M against H1 spend of $175M implies an H2 step-up. Removing the $135.1M of recognized insurance from H1 OCF leaves approximately $235M, and after $175M of capex, roughly $60M of underlying H1 free cash flow. Adjusting for the working-capital drag and capex phasing, a fair FY2026 underlying free cash flow range is $220–280M, or a 5–6% yield on market capitalization. That is respectable and materially below what the single-quarter $148M figure implies.

Cash taxes are guided at 0–5% for 2026; book effective rates were 11.5% in Q2 and 9.8% in H1, driven by excess percentage depletion and Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credits. The 45X benefit appears twice, in the tax line and as a reduction to cash cost of coal sold in both Metallurgical and High CV Thermal. It is material and policy-dependent, which argues for valuing it at a discount to operating margin.

## Growth Analysis

There is no volume growth engine. FY2026 guidance is 87.3–92.4Mt total: coking 8.8–9.4Mt, High CV Thermal 31.5–33.0Mt, PRB 47.0–50.0Mt. PRB volumes are declining year over year. High CV Thermal is 30.9Mt committed and priced at $58.00 against 31.5–33.0Mt guided, so 2026 thermal is effectively sold: downside protected, upside forfeited, at a price below both the Q2 realization and the prior-year level. Coking is 6.0Mt priced at $120.86 with 2.7Mt committed unpriced.

Earnings growth therefore depends on price and cost. Cost guidance does not project further improvement from Q2 levels: Metallurgical $86.00–91.00/ton against $85.65 achieved, High CV Thermal $39.00–40.50 against $38.58 achieved. That implies Q2 benefited from favorable absorption and/or 45X timing that management does not expect to repeat evenly. Price improvement requires two things outside Core's control: a rebound in global hot-metal output after two years of contraction, and compression of the historically wide basis between U.S. East Coast coking assessments and Australian indices. Core's realized coking price was $121.43/ton in Q2, up from $114.71 a year earlier but down sequentially from $122.11.

The fastest-growing item disclosed is Industrial export revenue, up from $232.9M to $340.2M year over year. That is consistent with management's thesis on seaborne industrial coal demand (India cement) and with the domestic power-demand argument (U.S. coal fleet running below a 50% average capacity factor, IEA projecting 3.6% annual global electricity demand growth). Those are management assertions and third-party projections repeated by management, not filing-verified facts, and they sit against the near-term conditions management itself concedes.

**Consensus is being cut, and what remains still embeds a recovery.** FY2026 EPS consensus stands at $1.98, down 28.3% in 30 days and 60.2% in 90 days. FY2027 stands at $6.67, down 21.6% in 90 days. The estimate table is internally inconsistent: the quarterly figures ($1.48 and $1.37, two analysts) do not reconcile with the FY2026 figure ($1.98, three analysts) given H1 adjusted EPS of roughly $0.96, so the panels are likely on mixed bases or partially stale. I therefore use direction rather than level from that table, and anchor on FY2027 $6.67 (four analysts).

That FY2027 figure deserves decomposition. At roughly 48M shares post-buyback, $6.67 implies about $320M of net income; grossing up at the ~10% book tax rate and adding back approximately $10M of net interest and $625M of guided DD&A implies EBITDA near $1.0B. My normalized estimate, built from guidance, is materially lower: Metallurgical 9.1Mt at roughly $30/ton margin ≈ $273M; High CV Thermal 32.25Mt at $18.25/ton ≈ $589M; PRB 48.5Mt at $0.77/ton ≈ $37M; terminal third-party contribution ≈ $35M; less cash G&A of $85–100M. That totals roughly $780–840M, and it triangulates with the underlying quarterly run rate of approximately $198M. The gap between consensus and guidance-implied economics is roughly $200M of EBITDA, equivalent to about $22/ton on 9Mt of coking coal. Consensus already assumes the met recovery. That is the central valuation risk.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value at $89.60: $4.517B market capitalization less $473M cash plus $448M debt = $4.492B. On normalized EBITDA of $815M that is 5.5x; on $780M, 5.8x. Adjusting for unfunded ARO and legacy employee obligations (roughly $900M) lifts the multiple to 6.6x. On FY2027 consensus $6.67 the stock trades at 13.4x; on FY2026 consensus $1.98, 45x. Peer medians in the supplied energy comparable set are 11.8x P/E and 0.6% yield, with wide dispersion (CNX 4.4x, INSW 7.5x, WHD 55.0x), a heterogeneous group that provides limited discipline. Coal producers have generally traded 3–5x EV/EBITDA on terminal-value and ESG-exclusion grounds; Core's better asset mix (metallurgical exposure, owned Baltimore terminal, 35% DTA interest, net cash) justifies a premium to that range, and at 5.5–5.8x it is receiving one.

The "cheap on 2027" argument holds only if 2027 EBITDA reaches $1.0B, which requires the East Coast/Australia coking basis to compress, hot-metal output to recover, PRB to return to positive margin, and 2027 thermal to reprice at or above $58.00. None of those is in evidence today, and the direction of estimate revisions over 90 days has been toward the guidance-implied number rather than away from it.

Support for the price comes from three places. First, the $670.8M remaining repurchase authorization equals about 15% of market capitalization, and the company has retired roughly 7.2% of shares in eighteen months. Second, a net-cash balance sheet with no maturities before the March 2035 mandatory tenders removes the financing tail that usually caps coal multiples. Third, the 0–5% cash tax rate and the enacted federal coal royalty reduction (explicitly reflected in the $14.27 PRB committed price) are current cash benefits. Against that, the buyback's average purchase price has converged upward — cumulative $77.04, H1 2026 $88.60, Q2 2026 $87.54 — so incremental repurchases are no longer accretive against a demonstrably lower book of prior buying, and 2027 buyback capacity depends entirely on operating free cash flow under the 75%-of-FCF framework, with the insurance cushion exhausted.

**Scenarios (50M shares base, ~48.5M bull, ~49.5M bear):**

- **Base $95 (+6.0%)**: 5.75x on $815M of normalized EBITDA. Assumes 2026 guidance is met, PRB returns to roughly breakeven, coking realizations hold near $120/ton, and 2027 thermal reprices flat to modestly lower.
- **Bull $124 (+38%)**: 6.3x on $950M. Requires basis compression toward Australian indices, hot-metal recovery, PRB at $1+/ton margin, and 2027 domestic thermal repricing higher on data-center-driven load, with the buyback taking share count to ~48.5M.
- **Bear $62 (−31%)**: 5.2x on $580M. Coking stays muted and thermal reprices below $58 for 2027 while PRB remains negative; a Leer South recurrence without insurance coverage would sit at or below this level.

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly $94, about +4.9%, plus a 0.45% dividend. That is approximately fair compensation and not more.

The four-analyst consensus target range of $100–116 (mean $105.25) sits 12–29% above spot with a unanimous Buy tally unchanged over four months. Those targets warrant limited weight: they have not moved while FY2027 estimates were cut 21.6%, which makes them stale relative to the earnings they are supposed to capitalize, and the stock is not at the kind of compressed forward multiple where consensus targets function as floors.

## Competitive Landscape

Core's advantages are specific and verifiable. Longwall-centric production at PAMC, West Elk, Leer and Leer South delivers the cost position visible in the Q2 numbers — $38.58/ton in High CV Thermal and $85.65/ton in Metallurgical, both improved sequentially. Ownership of the Baltimore terminal (5.2Mt of Q2 throughput, $17.8M of segment Adjusted EBITDA on $26.9M of revenue, of which $17.0M is captive intersegment volume) plus the 35% DTA partnership gives control of export capacity that most U.S. producers rent. Third-party terminal revenue more than doubled year over year, from $4.5M to $9.9M.

Segment breadth is genuine commodity diversification: ex-insurance, thermal and metallurgical contributions are roughly balanced, so neither commodity alone determines the year. That is a defensive attribute in a sector where single-commodity producers swing violently.

The trajectory, however, is mixed rather than improving. Cost leadership in metallurgical improved substantially, but management's own guidance projects no further improvement. Thermal netback is compressing as volume shifts seaborne. PRB is a structurally declining, absorption-dependent business carrying disproportionate reclamation obligations. The metallurgical value-in-use strategy — marketing Leer-brand high-vol as a substitute for Australian premium low-vol — is credible on product quality but requires Asian buyers to accept substitution and the basis to compress, neither of which Core controls. The filings themselves flag exclusion from certain investment funds on ESG grounds as a persistent constraint on cost of capital and multiple, alongside a surety and insurance market that management describes as historically difficult though recently stabilizing, which matters directly given $1,062.5M of surety bonds outstanding.

Policy positioning is unusually favorable for a coal producer: Section 45X credits reducing both cash cost of sales and the effective tax rate, an enacted federal coal lease royalty reduction, and a July 2026 OWCP proposal that would replace the 100%-of-liabilities black lung collateral mandate with a Composite Solvency Score methodology, directionally helpful for a company with $256.8M of pneumoconiosis liabilities. Policy-derived margin is reversible and should be capitalized at a discount.

## Risk Assessment

**Leer South, unresolved and now uninsured.** The chronology is documented: combustion activity reported January 13, 2025; panel sealed; re-entry June 10, 2025; forced re-evacuation and re-sealing June 26, 2025 on rising carbon monoxide; longwall equipment recovered and operations resumed December 2025 with the affected area permanently sealed; $101M of costs fully recovered through a $154.5M full-limit settlement. The Q2 2026 press release forward-looking list names both "the prior occurrence of combustion-related activity... and the risk of future occurrences" and, separately, "the increase in combustion-related gases at Core's Leer South mine." The second phrasing is present-tense and sits uneasily against the 10-Q's account of permanent sealing and the longwall restart driving cost improvement. I cannot resolve which reading is correct from the documents available, and this is the single most consequential open item: Leer South is the engine of the only improving segment, and the policy limit on this claim is exhausted.

**Three Item 1.04 mine-safety filings in four months** (2026-01-12, 2026-05-04, 2026-05-19). Item 1.04 covers mine-safety shutdowns and patterns of violations. The cadence is elevated for an 11-mine platform, but the item triggers on individual §103(k) or §107(a) orders, so the filings may be discrete and non-systemic. The filing bodies were not retrievable, so the affected mines, order types and financial impact are unknown. This is an unresolved flag rather than established evidence of a systemic problem.

**Unnamed CEO transition.** The merger-of-equals leadership structure did not hold: Arch's pre-merger CEO Paul Lang separated under an agreement filed with the Q3 2025 10-Q following an Item 5.02 8-K on 2025-10-08, roughly nine months after closing; CONSOL's James Brock signs the Q2 2026 10-Q as Chair and CEO. The Q2 press release then lists "risks related to the recently announced CEO transition." No name, date or reason is available. A pre-negotiated succession is plausible given an August 2024 amendment to Brock's arrangements dated the merger signing date, and I decline to infer cause. What is verifiable is two CEO changes within eighteen months at a company mid-integration whose incentive plan runs on segment unit-cost continuity.

**UMWA 1992 Benefit Plan litigation.** Filed May 2020 in D.D.C.; motion to dismiss denied March 2022; cross-motions for summary judgment filed October 2025, with the plan claiming $64.8M of unpaid premiums plus $25.6M of interest and damages. No ruling, no accrual — loss characterized as reasonably possible but not probable. Core estimates roughly $10.0M of annual servicing cost in 2026 if liabilities attach, declining thereafter. Murray Energy's indemnity is worthless in bankruptcy. Roughly 2% of market capitalization, discrete and binary.

**West Elk Title V VOC non-compliance**, newly disclosed. A July 23, 2026 Colorado APCD inspection report found West Elk out of compliance with VOC emission limits and recommended opening an enforcement case. No penalty indicated; Core's environmental disclosure threshold is $1M, which bounds the exposure from below but not above.

**Other.** PRB's 47–50Mt full-year requirement against 22.1Mt in H1; a "projected drawdown of coal inventories" that has not begun (coal inventory rose $8.7M in H1 to $157.6M); a thin diesel hedge book (1.2M gallons at June 30, down from 3.6M at year-end) against a large surface fleet amid rising fuel prices; and $1,062.5M of surety bonds in a market management describes as historically difficult for coal.

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.2% against 46.4% one-year realized — fairly priced, at the 48th percentile of the current chain, with the nearest expiry (12 days) pricing a ±6.9% move. The informative feature is the term structure: 45.2% near-term against 41.9% at six months, backwardation that indicates the market prices near-term event risk above the longer-run level. The reconciliation is that positioning built up over time is balanced while recent flow has skewed toward downside protection around a near-term catalyst window. None of this contradicts a HOLD; the backwardation is mildly corroborative of the view that near-term uncertainty (PRB H2 step-up, CEO transition detail, mine-safety items) is not fully resolved.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($124, +38%).** The Leer South longwall runs uninterrupted, holding metallurgical cash cost at the low end of $86–91/ton while the U.S. East Coast coking basis compresses toward Australian indices as global hot-metal output recovers, lifting realized coking prices from $121 toward $135–140. PRB volumes reach the upper end of 47–50Mt in H2, restoring $1+/ton margin and releasing the projected inventory drawdown. Domestic High CV Thermal reprices upward for 2027 as coal-fleet capacity factors rise from sub-50% on data-center and reindustrialization load, and the 16Mt of new sales commitments booked in Q2 prove to carry the "advantageous margins" management describes. EBITDA reaches roughly $950M, the company deploys the remaining $670.8M authorization to take share count toward 48M, and the market awards 6.3x on a net-cash balance sheet. The 45X credits and federal royalty reduction persist, keeping cash taxes near zero.

**Bear case ($62, −31%).** Seaborne metallurgical markets stay muted, the East Coast basis fails to compress, and coking realizations drift below $115/ton. 2027 High CV Thermal reprices below $58.00 as low gas prices and elevated stockpiles persist, on top of the $2.76/ton half-year netback compression already visible. PRB volume misses the 47Mt low end, cost stays above $14/ton, and the segment remains a drag; the inventory drawdown never materializes and working capital consumes rather than releases cash. EBITDA falls toward $580M, free cash flow drops below $150M, and the 75%-of-FCF framework mechanically shrinks the buyback that has been supporting per-share metrics. Any of the three unresolved items — a Leer South gas event, a material MSHA closure, or a disruptive CEO change — accelerates the derating, with no insurance limit to absorb it. FY2027 consensus continues its path toward $5.00, and 12x on that is $60.

**What tips the balance to neutral.** The bull case requires external price recovery that is not in evidence and that consensus already embeds ($1.0B of implied 2027 EBITDA against $780–840M guidance-implied). The bear case requires no new bad news, only continuation of what two of four segments are already doing. The balance sheet and buyback genuinely narrow the left tail — net cash, no maturities before 2035 tenders, $1,016M of liquidity, 15% of market capitalization authorized for repurchase — which is why this is not a REDUCE. A probability-weighted return near 5% with three material unverified items and 90-day estimate cuts of 21.6% at the FY2027 anchor does not clear the bar for adding capital.

## Conclusion

Core Natural Resources is a well-financed, low-cost, four-segment coal platform eighteen months into a merger integration, with a genuine operating improvement in metallurgical coal that the headline numbers overstate by roughly a factor of five. The $135.1M insurance recovery recognized in H1 is real cash and it compensates for real 2025 losses, but it is exhausted and non-repeating. Underlying Adjusted EBITDA grew approximately 13% in the half. Two of four segments are deteriorating: High CV Thermal netback compressed $2.76/ton for the half and is locked for 2026 at $58.00, below trailing realizations, while PRB has crossed into negative cash margin on falling volume. FY2027 consensus of $6.67 — already cut 21.6% in 90 days — still implies roughly $1.0B of EBITDA against the $780–840M that current guidance supports, which makes the "13.4x forward" framing contingent on a metallurgical price recovery outside Core's control.

The offset is a balance sheet that is rare in this industry: (0.05)x net leverage, 32.65x interest coverage, $1,016M of liquidity, no maturities before 2035 mandatory tenders, and $670.8M of remaining repurchase authorization against a $4.5B market capitalization, on top of a 7.2% share-count reduction already delivered. That floor is why the bear case stops near $62 rather than lower, and why a stock with sharply falling estimates and three unresolved material items is a HOLD rather than a REDUCE. At $89.60, probability-weighted return is roughly 5% with a −31%/+38% distribution around it, and the questions that would most change the picture — Leer South gas conditions, the substance of three mine-safety filings, and the identity and mandate of the incoming CEO — are precisely the ones the disclosure record does not answer. Own it if you own it; wait for the Q3 print, or for the mid-$70s, before adding.
