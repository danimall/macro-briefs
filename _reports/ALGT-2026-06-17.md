**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegiant has executed one of the sharper turnarounds in the ULCC space, moving from a $240M operating loss in 2024 (driven by the Sunseeker Resort write-down, the costly 737 MAX fleet transition, and a pilot retention accrual) to a recovery: full-year 2025 airline operating income of $143.9M and a Q1 2026 print of $42.5M net income, EPS $2.30 (vs $1.74 a year earlier), record Q1 revenue, TRASM +16.4%, yield +20.8%, and average fare +19.8%. The stock has tracked that recovery with a 76.9% gain over the trailing twelve months to $95.89, near the upper third of its $42.56–$118 range. The Sunseeker exit (closed September 2025) returns the company to a pure-play airline, confirming that the core route economics were never the problem.

The defining strategic event is the May 13, 2026 close of the ~$1.5B Sun Country acquisition. This adds MSP-hub scheduled service plus Sun Country's Amazon cargo and charter operations, diversifying Allegiant away from pure leisure cyclicality toward more contracted, less seasonal revenue. The deal cleared antitrust (early HSR termination, March 16) and DOT approval (April 15), and management intends to run separate brands and operating certificates to reduce integration and labor friction. The combined entity operates roughly 195 aircraft across 175 cities and 650 routes. On FY27 consensus EPS of $10.68, the stock trades at roughly 9x, which is the central reason this is a hold rather than an avoid: there is a credible 12-month path to a re-rating if synergies land and the fuel environment normalizes.

We rate ALGT a HOLD. The forward multiple is cheap because the market is pricing three live binary risks that the FY27 number does not de-risk: completely unhedged fuel exposure into the active Iran-conflict shock (27.7% of Q1 opex; management warns the fuel hit will be larger in coming quarters, since only ~1 month of the spike landed in Q1); a $256M pilot retention bonus accrued and payable as a lump sum upon ratification of a CBA that has been amendable since 2021; and integration, dilution, and leverage from a deal that consolidates more debt onto a $1.8B base with a $403M senior-notes wall due 2027. Near-term EPS estimates reflect this: FY26 consensus fell 51.4% over 90 days, and the current-quarter estimate has slipped to a $0.29 loss. The options market is pricing imminent stress (near-term IV 100.2% vs 63.6% realized, backwardation, put/call skew 105.4%).

## Company Overview

Allegiant is an ultra-low-cost leisure carrier headquartered in Las Vegas, built on an "underserved markets" model: it connects small and mid-size U.S. cities directly to leisure destinations (Florida, Las Vegas, Arizona) on routes that mostly lack competing nonstop service. Management identifies 1,400 potential expansion routes, more than 75% with no competitor. As of Q1 2026 the standalone airline operated 123 aircraft (78 A320, 28 A319, 17 Boeing 737-8200 MAX) across 576 routes, 91 origination cities, and 35 destinations.

Three features distinguish the model. First, capacity flexibility: Allegiant expands and contracts flying to match seasonal and peak leisure demand. In Q1 2026 it deliberately cut capacity 5.9% while lifting load factor 3.9 points to 84.4%. Second, ancillary-heavy revenue: of $671.8M Q1 passenger revenue, roughly $313M came from air-related ancillary charges (bags, seats, fees) versus $338M of scheduled fares, so about half of passenger revenue is ancillary. Third, direct distribution and loyalty: 91.5% of sales come through the website and app, the Allways Rewards program has 21M members, and co-brand card remuneration reached $139.6M in 2025 (+3.6%).

The Sun Country acquisition (~$4.10 cash plus 0.1557 ALGT shares per SNCY share) adds a Minneapolis-St. Paul scheduled hub, Amazon cargo flying, and a charter business (including sports/"March Madness" flying that Allegiant already does on a fixed-fee basis, up 11.5% in Q1). Sun Country CEO Jude Bricker, a former Allegiant COO, has joined the Allegiant board.

2024 was the trough: a $240M operating loss and $308M pretax loss driven by Sunseeker, the accelerated Airbus retirements tied to the MAX transition, and the pilot retention accrual. 2025 staged a partial recovery — airline-only operating income of $143.9M (5.7% margin, 7.4% adjusted) with airline CASM ex-fuel and special charges down 6.1% to 8.04¢ on 12.6% capacity growth. Q1 2026 carried that momentum: net income $42.5M (vs $32.1M), record Q1 revenue of $732.4M (+9.6% ex-Sunseeker), and $268M of operating cash flow in the quarter.

The balance sheet is the central constraint. Total debt and finance leases run roughly $1.8B; net debt is about $0.9B, down $103M year-over-year on Sunseeker proceeds and discipline. Net debt/EBITDA was 2.33x at year-end, beating the comp plan's 2.40x stretch goal. Book D/E is roughly 1.6x. Interest expense of about $150M a year is a meaningful fixed drag, with a 6.6% weighted fixed rate, 5.7% variable, and roughly 40% of debt floating (a 100bp move is about $7.6M of annual interest). Liquidity is adequate: $933.6M cash and investments at March 2026 plus $250M of undrawn revolver capacity.

Two items complicate the trajectory. The 2027 maturity wall is $529M, including $403M of 7.25% senior secured notes that must be refinanced into a still-elevated rate environment; management states it intends to refinance in advance. And capital allocation pressure is building: 33 aircraft on firm order for 2026–2028, Sun Country integration costs, and roughly $291M of fresh aircraft-secured debt drawn in late April and early May. The dividend remains indefinitely suspended to preserve cash for fleet capex, and there were no open-market buybacks in Q1 despite $64.7M of remaining authority. Leverage will rise as the Sun Country balance sheet consolidates.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in near-term execution: three beats in the last four quarters (Q1'26 +10.7%, Q4'25 +40.2%, Q2'25 +59.2%) against one miss (Q3'25, -12.8%, a seasonally weak loss quarter).

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two engines. Organically, the airline is recovering unit revenue forcefully, and management has shown it can pull capacity down while pushing load factor and fares up. The route map remains under-penetrated, with most identified expansion routes lacking nonstop competition, and the co-brand card and loyalty program generate a high-margin, recurring revenue stream that is partly insulated from fare cyclicality.

The acquisition is the second engine. Sun Country adds contracted, less seasonal revenue (Amazon cargo and charter) that should dampen the pure-leisure cyclicality that has historically whipsawed Allegiant's earnings. Industry framing as "complementary rather than transformational" is fair and arguably a positive: the businesses overlap little, antitrust cleared quickly, and the separate-certificate structure reduces near-term execution risk even as it defers full synergy capture.

Estimate revisions show the tension clearly. The near-quarter estimate revised up 38.1% over 30 days (to a $0.29 loss, off a worse base), and FY27 was revised up 8.0% over 30 days to $10.68. But the 90-day picture is the warning: FY26 consensus collapsed 51.4% (to $3.87) and current-quarter estimates fell 111.5%, reflecting the fuel shock and the consolidation of Sun Country's lower-margin contribution plus integration costs. The strong-BUY signal that an analyst looks for — estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days combined with revenue growth above 30% — is not present here. The 90-day revision trend is sharply negative on the front year, and revenue growth, while solid, is not running at accelerating velocity. The positive 30-day prints reflect stabilization off a lowered base, not a clean acceleration. This argues against treating the cheap FY27 multiple as a high-conviction buy signal.

Trailing P/E is meaningless here (-50.9), distorted by the 2024 catastrophe. The relevant frame is forward and normalized. On FY26 consensus of $3.87, the stock trades at roughly 25x; on FY27 consensus of $10.68, roughly 9x. The wide gap between the two years is the entire valuation debate: FY27 assumes synergy realization, a normalized fuel path, and successful integration, while FY26 reflects the fuel shock, integration drag, and a likely loss in the current quarter.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegiant's moat rests on its niche: small-city-to-leisure routes with no nonstop competition, an all-owned fleet, and a peak-demand flying model that few legacy or low-cost competitors can profitably replicate on those thin routes. Operational reliability is a genuine differentiator: #2 among major U.S. carriers in WSJ's 2025 ranking, 99.9% controllable completion in 2025, and strong brand affinity (Newsweek "Most Loved Brands," seven straight USA Today best-airline-credit-card wins). In a sector plagued by disruption, this reliability translates into durable ancillary and loyalty economics.

The competitive backdrop favors the consolidation thesis. The shutdown of Spirit Airlines confirms that the standalone ULCC model has been under severe pressure, and acquiring Sun Country is the survival-by-scale response. The combined network plus cargo and charter diversification improves the resilience of the business mix. The trajectory of the competitive position is stable-to-improving: Allegiant is gaining scale and revenue diversity at a moment when weaker ULCC competitors are exiting, though the benefit is partly offset by the execution risk of absorbing a second carrier and the structural exposure of leisure demand to consumer softening. We score this a 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by company-specific binary events that the current valuation only partially reflects.

**Unhedged fuel into an active oil shock.** Allegiant does not hedge and has no plans to. Fuel was 27.7% of Q1 2026 opex at $3.04/gal (+16.5% YoY). Management states that Q1 captured only about one month of the Iran-conflict-driven spike (cited in the filing as beginning Feb 28, 2026) and that "we expect even greater cost impact in the future." A 10% fuel move is roughly $18M per quarter. With the conflict unresolved, this is a live, asymmetric earnings wildcard. A grinding-conflict or escalation outcome would pressure margins materially in the seasonally important upcoming quarters.

**Pilot retention bonus.** A $256M bonus is accrued, growing roughly $20M per quarter, and payable as a lump sum upon ratification of a CBA that has been amendable since 2021 (mediation ongoing). Ratification triggers both a large one-time cash outflow and a permanent step-up in pilot wages, a combined liquidity and margin event that the market may be underweighting.

**Leverage and the 2027 wall.** With $1.8B of debt, ~$150M annual interest, ~40% variable-rate exposure, $403M of 7.25% notes due 2027, fresh aircraft debt drawn in April–May, and Sun Country's balance sheet now consolidated, refinancing risk into an elevated-rate environment is real. Net debt fell to $0.9B, but the trajectory is upward.

**Integration and dilution.** The Sun Country close adds execution risk and the dilution from newly issued shares. The separate-certificate approach mitigates near-term friction but defers synergy capture.

**Boeing delivery dependence.** The fleet-renewal and growth plan hinges on 737 MAX deliveries (50 firm plus up to 80 options, only 17 delivered). Boeing quality and regulatory issues could force costlier extension of aging Airbus airframes.

**Smaller items:** a $9.9M TSA security-fee assessment under 9th Circuit appeal (oral argument April 15) and a $7.0M Q1 credit-loss reserve on a defaulted flight-simulator note.

We score risk_factors 4.5: the stack of binary, company-specific, near-term-dated risks (fuel, pilot bonus, refinancing, integration) is material and not fully priced into a forward multiple that assumes clean resolution of all four.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 100.2% against 63.6% realized volatility (a rich 36.6-point spread, 81st percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (100.2% near-term vs 65.9% six-month), and the put/call skew is elevated at 105.4%. IV jumped from 64.7% to 100.2% since early May even as the stock rose 15.4%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.70 to 0.26. This combination of rising IV, backwardation, and elevated put skew is consistent with the market hedging a near-term catalyst — most plausibly a fuel-driven earnings disappointment or post-close integration update — rather than expressing directional conviction. The signal reinforces the case for holding rather than adding fresh capital ahead of these resolutions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120–125).** Fuel normalizes as the Iran conflict de-escalates, removing the most acute near-term margin pressure. Sun Country integration proceeds cleanly, with cargo and charter diversification smoothing seasonality and synergies landing on schedule. The 2027 notes are refinanced in advance at manageable rates. FY27 EPS converges toward the $10.68 consensus, and an 11–12x multiple on those earnings supports $120–125, consistent with the Street high. The unit-revenue momentum and the under-penetrated route map carry organic growth.

**Bear Case ($65–70).** The conflict grinds or escalates, fuel costs stay elevated, and unhedged fuel compresses margins through the seasonally critical quarters, pushing FY26 to a loss as the front-quarter estimate already implies. The pilot CBA ratifies, triggering the $256M cash outflow plus a permanent wage step-up. Integration costs run ahead of plan, the 2027 refinancing prices unfavorably, and the consolidated leverage limits flexibility. A de-rating toward the lower end of the 52-week range and the $85 Street low, or below, follows — roughly $65–70 on a combination of lower earnings and a compressed multiple.

The base case sits between these: gradual recovery with FY26 depressed by fuel and integration drag, FY27 earnings recovering toward consensus, and the stock range-bound near current levels until the fuel, labor, and refinancing risks resolve.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is specific: Allegiant is a recovering pure-play airline trading at ~9x FY27 consensus EPS of $10.68, with a credible deleveraging-plus-consolidation path (Sun Country synergy capture, contracted cargo/charter diversification, continued unit-revenue strength) that supports appreciation if the near-term binary risks resolve favorably. We hold rather than buy because three dated catalysts — the fuel path, the pilot CBA, and the 2027 refinancing — must clear before the cheap forward multiple converts into a confident return, and the options market is pricing imminent stress.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A sustained de-escalation of the Iran conflict that pulls jet fuel back toward sub-$2.75/gal, removing the unhedged-fuel overhang, combined with FY26 estimate stabilization (no further downward revisions for two consecutive months).
- Pilot CBA ratification on terms the company has reserved for, removing the $256M lump-sum uncertainty and clarifying the go-forward wage base.
- Early refinancing of the $403M 2027 notes at a rate at or below the current 7.25% coupon, de-risking the maturity wall.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained fuel spike that pushes FY26 to a clear loss with FY27 estimates also revised down, signaling the recovery is stalling rather than pausing.
- Integration cost overruns or synergy delays disclosed in post-close updates, alongside a further leverage increase (net debt/EBITDA above ~3.5x).
- Refinancing the 2027 notes at materially higher rates or signs of constrained capital access.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (the first full post-close quarter, expected late July/early August 2026), which will show the larger fuel impact management has flagged and the initial integration economics. Also monitor monthly traffic updates and any 2027 refinancing announcements.

## Conclusion

Allegiant is a credible recovery story: a pure-play ULCC that has climbed out of the 2024 hole, is posting record unit revenue, and has bought scale and revenue diversity at a moment when weaker ULCC competitors are exiting. At ~9x FY27 consensus EPS, the forward valuation is supportive, and the consensus mean target of $102.75 implies modest upside from $95.89. That is enough to justify holding through the recovery.

It is not enough to buy. The cheap multiple is cheap because the market is pricing a stack of dated, company-specific binary risks: completely unhedged fuel into an unresolved oil shock that management says will hit harder in coming quarters, a $256M lump-sum pilot bonus payable on a long-overdue CBA, integration and dilution from a freshly closed acquisition, and a $403M senior-notes refinancing into elevated rates — all reflected in a 51% collapse in FY26 estimates over 90 days and an options market pricing imminent stress. The founder's well-timed ~$33M sale near the February highs and the absence of any insider buying add caution rather than conviction. We rate ALGT a HOLD, with a clear path to upgrade if fuel normalizes and the labor and refinancing overhangs clear, and to downgrade if the fuel shock and integration costs force FY27 estimates lower.
