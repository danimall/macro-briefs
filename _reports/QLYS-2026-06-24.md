**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Qualys is a high-quality, debt-free cloud cybersecurity vendor that converts roughly 45% of revenue into free cash flow and returns essentially all of it through buybacks. The business prints a 47% adjusted EBITDA margin on ~$670M of revenue, sustained by a structurally low-cost operating base (71% of headcount in India) that subscale peers cannot match. The balance sheet carries $729M of cash and securities against zero financial debt; the reported 0.95 debt-to-equity ratio and 2.84 Altman Z-score are artifacts of deferred revenue and operating leases, not distress signals. On financial resilience alone, this is one of the cleaner names in software.

The debate is strategic, not financial. Revenue growth has decelerated from the mid-teens (2023) to ~10% (2024-2026), and the net dollar expansion rate sits at 103-104%, weak for enterprise SaaS, where best-in-class peers run 110-125%. In Q1 2026, 96% of incremental revenue came from existing customers, meaning the new-logo engine has largely stalled. Qualys is a compounder that grows mid-teens EPS by adding ~10% revenue, holding margins, and shrinking the share count 2-3% per year. That works, but it leaves no cushion: if net retention drifts toward 100%, buybacks can no longer mask a flat top line.

The valuation framing requires care. Qualys trades at ~20x trailing GAAP EPS ($5.44 trailing, $115 price) and ~15x forward non-GAAP EPS ($7.57 current-year consensus). The gap between the two is stock-based compensation of roughly $77M per year (11.5% of revenue), a genuine economic cost that the non-GAAP "~15x" figure excludes. Treated honestly on a GAAP basis, ~20x for a 10%-growth business facing platform-vendor encroachment (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, now Google via Wiz) is fair, not a clear discount. We rate QLYS HOLD. The fundamentals do not justify chasing, and the price does not yet offer the asymmetric entry that would warrant a BUY.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1999 and headquartered in Foster City, California, Qualys delivers cloud-based IT, security, and compliance applications through its Enterprise TruRisk Platform. The company pioneered cloud-delivered vulnerability management (VM) in 2000 and has expanded into Cybersecurity Asset Management, Patch Management, web application scanning, cloud/container security, and Enterprise TruRisk Management (cyber-risk quantification). Revenue is recognized ratably from annual, prepaid subscriptions, producing $409M of deferred revenue at Q1 2026 and high forward visibility. Qualys reports as a single segment.

Go-to-market splits roughly evenly between direct sales and channel partners (MSSPs, cloud providers, VARs, consultancies), with the partner channel reaching 52% of revenue in Q1 2026 and growing faster than direct. Approximately 55% of revenue is U.S.-based. CEO Sumedh Thakar has led the company since 2021, succeeding founder Philippe Courtot.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (decelerating, steady):**
- FY2022: $489.7M → FY2023: $554.5M (+13.2%) → FY2024: $607.6M (+9.6%) → FY2025: $669.1M (+10.1%)
- Q1 2026: $175.6M, +10% YoY

Growth has settled into a ~10% band. Management attributes the deceleration to macro headwinds since 2023, namely extended sales cycles and tighter IT-security budgets. The composition is the concern: only 24% of FY2025 revenue growth came from new customers, and in Q1 2026, 96% of incremental revenue came from the existing base. Net dollar expansion of 104% (up from 103%) is the single most important metric and the weakest part of the story.

**Profitability (elite):**
- Operating margin: 33% (FY25) expanding to 35% (Q1 2026)
- Net margin: ~30%; adjusted EBITDA margin: 47% (stable)
- ROE 35.3%, ROA 18.1%
- FY2025 net income $198.3M; Q1 2026 net income $50.6M (+6.5%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.42 vs. $1.29

**Earnings basis reconciliation.** Two EPS series appear in the data and must be distinguished. GAAP diluted EPS runs ~$5.44 trailing (Q1 $1.42), implying a ~20x trailing multiple consistent with the reported trailing P/E of 20.17. The higher quarterly series in the surprise record ($1.95, $1.87, $1.86, $1.68 ≈ $7.36 TTM) and the consensus estimates ($7.57 current-year, $8.26 next-year) are non-GAAP/adjusted figures that exclude ~$77M/year of stock-based compensation (≈$2.18/share) and amortization. QLYS is therefore ~20x trailing GAAP and ~15x forward non-GAAP. Because SBC at 11.5% of revenue is a real, recurring economic cost, the valuation work below anchors on the GAAP basis and treats the non-GAAP "discount" as soft.

**Cash flow and balance sheet (fortress):**
- FCF $304M (FY25), 45.5% FCF margin; capex trivial (~$5M/yr, asset-light)
- $729M cash and marketable securities; zero financial debt
- The 0.95 D/E and 2.84 Z-score reflect deferred revenue ($409M) and operating leases, not borrowings

**Capital allocation.** Qualys returns essentially all FCF via buybacks — $183M in FY25, $53.5M in Q1 2026 — with authorization raised to $1.6B cumulative (Feb 2026) and $306.6M remaining. Share count has fallen from 38.5M (2022) to 35.4M (Q1 2026), a ~2-3%/yr reduction that adds meaningfully to per-share growth. No dividend. Management repurchased into the early-2026 dip (price ladder ~$132 in January to ~$93 in March), which is sensible counter-cyclical execution.

**Tax watch item.** The effective rate rose to 22.1% in Q1 2026 (from 18.5%) on OBBBA FDDEI changes, a lower federal R&D credit, and non-deductible SBC. This is a mild ongoing EPS headwind.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is mechanical: ~10% revenue + stable 47% EBITDA margin + 2-3% annual share shrink compounds into low-to-mid-teens EPS growth. Consensus reflects this — current-year non-GAAP EPS $7.57 and next-year $8.26 imply ~9% EPS growth, with 90-day revisions modestly positive (+2.6% current-year, +3.6% next-year). The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (6.2%, 2.8%, 16.8%, 11.7%), which supports confidence in near-term estimates.

The durability question is net retention. At 104%, expansion within the installed base is barely outpacing churn, and the new-logo engine has stalled. Best-in-class security SaaS runs 110-125% NDR; Qualys's figure signals limited upsell momentum in a budget-constrained environment. The +1pt move from 103% to 104% is encouraging at the margin but not yet a trend. The AI tailwinds cited by external bulls are real in concept — TruRisk's exposure-prioritization is a natural place to embed ML — but they have not yet shown up in the retention or growth numbers.

Growth Prospects score 4.5: durable single-digit revenue growth with a structurally soft retention rate, no visible re-acceleration catalyst, and growth increasingly dependent on the installed base rather than market expansion. This is the dimension that most reliably sorts winners, and Qualys does not screen well on it.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer comparables in Systems Software show wide, heterogeneous dispersion (PATH 17.6x, PRGS 16.2x, CVLT 80.3x, ATEN 53.5x, NABL negative), median ~53.5x — too scattered to anchor on. The cleaner comparison is to vulnerability-management peers (Tenable, Rapid7) and to Qualys's own quality/growth profile.

At ~$115 and trailing GAAP EPS of $5.44, QLYS trades at ~20x trailing earnings. For a business growing revenue ~10% with a soft NDR and rising competitive pressure, that is a fair multiple, not a discount. The "~15x forward / cheap" framing relies on non-GAAP EPS that strips out SBC; once that real cost is reincorporated, the apparent bargain narrows substantially. A PEG on GAAP EPS growth of ~12-13% (revenue + buyback) sits near 1.5-1.6x — full, not compelling.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $85, mean $107.39, median $96, high $161. The mean and median sit *below* the current price, which is unusual and signals that consensus views the stock as roughly fairly valued to modestly rich — the opposite of a setup where targets act as floors. The improving sentiment trend (buy ratings up from 4 to 6) is mild and dominated by 15 holds.

Valuation score 5.0: the price reflects the business reasonably well on a GAAP basis. There is no identified mispricing catalyst, and the cheapness is largely an artifact of the non-GAAP convention. A screen-cheap multiple on a decelerating business is more often a value trap than an opportunity, and the GAAP reality removes the discount entirely.

## Competitive Landscape

Qualys operates in a maturing, intensely competitive vulnerability/exposure-management market undergoing platform consolidation. The structural trend works against it: point solutions are being absorbed into mega-platforms that can bundle exposure management at low or zero incremental cost. Google's acquisition of Wiz (closed March 2026) places a hyperscaler directly adjacent to Qualys's cloud-security offerings. CrowdStrike and Palo Alto bundle exposure management into XDR/SASE platforms. Direct peers Rapid7 and Tenable compete head-on in the same ~10%-growth, margin-focused environment.

Qualys's defense is integrated single-platform breadth at attractive total cost of ownership, backed by the India-heavy cost structure that funds 47% EBITDA margins where subscale peers cannot. The risk is that "good enough" bundled offerings erode Qualys's standalone value over time. The mechanism is straightforward: a CISO already paying CrowdStrike or Palo Alto for endpoint/network gets vulnerability management thrown in, removing the reason to renew a separate Qualys contract, which shows up first as flat NDR and eventually as sub-100% retention.

Competitive Position score 5.0, consistent with the recent decline from 6.5. The trajectory is the issue: a focused mid-cap holding share but facing larger, better-capitalized platform vendors whose bundling power is increasing. This is stable-to-deteriorating, not improving.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Growth/retention is the central risk.** NDR of 104% leaves almost no margin for error; a slip below 100% means the installed base is contracting. Growth is now overwhelmingly dependent on renewals/expansion in a budget-constrained market.
2. **Platform-vendor squeeze.** CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, and now Google/Wiz can bundle vulnerability management at marginal cost, pressuring standalone pricing and average sales price.
3. **Rising channel dependence.** The 52% of revenue flowing through non-exclusive partners who also carry competitors' products is an exposure; loss of a large partner could hit sales quickly.
4. **India concentration.** 71% of headcount in India is the margin engine and a concentrated exposure to INR, labor law, and geopolitics.
5. **FX.** ~26% of revenue and ~27% of expenses are in foreign currency; hedged via forwards but producing quarterly other-income noise.
6. **Tax drift.** The effective rate rose on OBBBA/R&D-credit changes, a mild ongoing headwind.
7. **Entrenchment vs. takeover appeal.** A debt-free, cash-rich, no-controlling-shareholder asset would be a natural M&A target, but a classified board, supermajority provisions, and blank-check preferred materially raise the bar for an acquirer, capping takeover-premium optionality.

Risk Factors score 5.5: the balance sheet is pristine, so distress risk is negligible, but the strategic risks (retention, bundling) are specific, quantifiable, and not fully priced into a ~20x GAAP multiple. The 16.2% short interest (5 days to cover) confirms that sophisticated investors are positioned for the bear thesis.

### Options Market Signal

Options sentiment leans neutral-to-mildly-constructive near-term, which neither confirms nor strongly contradicts the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$155-161):** Net retention re-accelerates above 110% as AI-driven prioritization and new modules (TruRisk Management, asset management) drive upsell; revenue growth re-accelerates toward low-teens; the 47% EBITDA margin and 2-3% annual buyback compound into mid-to-high-teens EPS growth; the multiple re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Sector M&A (cf. Wiz/Google) puts a strategic floor under the asset despite entrenchment provisions.

**Bear case (~$85):** NDR drifts below 100% as platform vendors bundle vulnerability management away from standalone Qualys; new-logo additions stay weak; revenue growth slips toward mid-single-digits; buybacks can no longer offset a flat top line; the multiple compresses to the low end of the peer range on a GAAP basis. The 16.2% short interest reflects this scenario.

**Base case (~$115-120):** ~10% revenue growth, stable margins, continued buyback, low-teens EPS growth, multiple holds near 20x trailing GAAP. The stock roughly tracks earnings growth from here with limited multiple expansion.

The fundamentals support the base case. There is no identified catalyst to drive the bull case in the next 12 months, and the bear case requires a retention deterioration that has not yet begun. This is a fairly valued compounder, which is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August). The NDR print and any commentary on new-customer additions are the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net dollar retention rises above 107-110% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the installed-base/upsell engine is re-accelerating.
- A de-rating to ~15-16x trailing GAAP EPS (roughly $85-90) without fundamental deterioration, which would restore genuine asymmetric upside.
- Evidence that AI/TruRisk modules are converting to measurable revenue per customer (rising average contract value), confirming the AI tailwind thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NDR falls below 100% in any quarter (installed base contracting).
- Revenue growth decelerates below 6% YoY, signaling the GARP/compounder thesis is breaking.
- A large channel partner loss or a step-down in margin guidance below the mid-40s EBITDA range.

## Conclusion

Qualys is a financially excellent business — debt-free, 45% FCF margin, disciplined counter-cyclical buybacks — wrapped around a strategically challenged growth profile. The valuation is the crux. The "~15x forward, cheap" framing depends on non-GAAP EPS that excludes the $77M/year of stock-based compensation; on the GAAP basis the company actually earns and reports against, QLYS trades at ~20x trailing earnings for ~10% revenue growth and a soft 104% net retention rate. That is a fair price, not a discount, and the absence of a re-acceleration catalyst combined with intensifying platform-vendor competition caps the upside.

We maintain HOLD: a confident hold on a fairly valued, slowly compounding asset, not a high-conviction call in either direction. The score trajectory (6.0 → 6.5) reflected improving financial-health and management recognition, but on a reconciled GAAP basis the valuation support that underpinned prior reads is weaker than it appeared, and the competitive trajectory continues to soften. We would become buyers on a de-rating toward the mid-$80s/low-$90s, or on a demonstrated retention inflection above 107%. We would become sellers if net retention breaks below 100%.
