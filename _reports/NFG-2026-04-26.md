## Executive Summary

National Fuel Gas Company is a vertically integrated natural gas company operating across the full value chain from Appalachian Basin E&P through gathering, interstate pipeline/storage, and regulated gas distribution in western New York and northwestern Pennsylvania. The stock trades at 12.3x trailing earnings, a 40% discount to the gas utility peer median of 20.4x, despite delivering 9% production growth in FY2025, four consecutive earnings beats, and a multi-year growth pipeline that includes a $2.62B utility acquisition, data center-linked pipeline projects, and expanding Utica Shale development.

The valuation discount likely reflects the market's difficulty classifying NFG's hybrid business model (part E&P, part regulated utility) rather than any structural impairment. KeyBanc's April 2026 initiation at Overweight with a $110 target explicitly characterized NFG as "under-the-radar," consistent with an analytical orphan discount. The CenterPoint Ohio acquisition, expected to close Q4 2026, doubles the regulated utility rate base and shifts the earnings mix toward a more constructive regulatory jurisdiction. Hedging at $4.07/Mcf on over 200 Bcf of FY2026-2027 production provides meaningful downside protection even if spot gas prices weaken.

The primary risks are acquisition execution (the largest deal in company history at 31% of market cap), incremental leverage from $1.2B in seller financing at 6.5%, and secular headwinds from New York's Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act. Near-term estimate revisions have softened modestly (current year down 0.7% over 30 days, next year down 3.3%), which bears monitoring. On balance, the combination of a discounted valuation, proven operational execution, and identifiable catalysts supports a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $102.

## Company Overview

NFG operates through three segments following a Q4 FY2025 reorganization:

**Integrated Upstream and Gathering ($1.18B FY2025 revenue):** Seneca Resources produces natural gas from the Marcellus and Utica Shales in the Appalachian Basin, while the midstream subsidiary builds and operates gathering infrastructure primarily for Seneca. FY2025 proved reserves totaled 4,981 Bcfe (up 5% YoY), and production reached 427 Bcfe (up 9% YoY). The shift toward Utica development ($351M capex vs. $142M for Marcellus) signals management's view that Utica offers better returns at current prices.

**Pipeline and Storage ($428M FY2025 revenue):** FERC-regulated interstate pipeline and storage systems (Supply Corporation and Empire Pipeline) transporting gas for utilities, marketers, and E&P companies in the northeastern U.S. and Canada. Revenue is predominantly firm transportation and storage contracts with a straight fixed-variable rate design, providing high visibility and insulation from volume fluctuations.

**Utility ($818M FY2025 revenue):** NYPSC/PaPUC-regulated gas distribution serving customers in western New York and northwestern Pennsylvania. Revenue decoupling in NY and weather normalization adjustments in both jurisdictions reduce seasonal sensitivity.

The geographic concentration in western NY and PA enables operational synergies: shared management, labor, facilities, and the ability to produce, gather, transport, store, and distribute gas from the same basin. This vertical integration is rare among U.S. gas companies.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Recovery

FY2025 marked a strong recovery from FY2024's impairment-driven earnings collapse:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Revenue | $2.17B | $1.94B | $2.28B | $651.5M |
| Operating Income | $755M | $210M | $813M | — |
| Net Income | $477M | $78M | $519M | $181.6M |
| EPS (diluted) | $5.17 | $0.84 | $5.68 | $1.98 |
| OCF | $1,237M | $1,066M | $1,100M | $274.9M |

FY2024's $78M net income was distorted by $473M in non-cash ceiling test impairments triggered by low gas prices. FY2025's recovery was driven by a $0.26/Mcf improvement in average realized gas prices (to $2.70/Mcf after hedging), 9% production growth, and the absence of large impairments. Q1 FY2026 continued the trend: revenue up 18.6% YoY, net income up 304% (aided by prior-year impairments in the comparison), and production up 11.7% to 109.2 Bcf.

Consolidated EBITDA has compounded from $996M (FY2021) to $1,413M (FY2025), a 42% increase over four years.

### Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The Altman Z-Score of 11.75 places NFG deep in the safe zone. Debt-to-capitalization of 0.41 is well below the 0.65 covenant limit, with $4.54B of additional debt capacity. Long-term debt stands at $2.7B at a weighted average cost of 4.85%, all investment-grade.

The December 2025 equity raise (4.4M shares at $79.50, netting $339M) reduced revolver exposure but introduced 4.9% dilution. The share repurchase program ($82M remaining) is suspended pending the CenterPoint Ohio acquisition. Post-acquisition leverage will increase materially: the $1.2B seller note at 6.5% and additional debt financing will push total obligations higher, though the credit agreements have been amended to exclude the seller note from the D/C ratio via a defeasance provision.

Near-term debt maturities include $600M due FY2027, $300M in FY2028, and $300M in notes now current, creating refinancing needs in a period of elevated rates.

### Cash Flow Quality and Capex Trajectory

FY2025 OCF of $1.1B funded $913M in capex, generating $187M in free cash flow. The capex trajectory is declining: guidance shows $1,010M (FY2026), $920M (FY2027), and $895M (FY2028), which should expand FCF over time. At the current $2.14 annualized dividend ($0.535/quarter × 4 = $2.14; ~$203M total at 95.0M shares), the company is generating sufficient cash to cover distributions with room for debt reduction.

### Hedging Program

NFG maintains a substantial hedging book that de-risks near-term production:
- FY2026: ~220 Bcf hedged at blended ~$4.07/Mcf (swaps) and $3.67-$4.92/Mcf (collars)
- FY2027: ~138 Bcf hedged at ~$4.06/Mcf (swaps) and $3.57-$4.53/Mcf (collars)
- FY2028: ~52 Bcf hedged at ~$3.91/Mcf

These hedges lock in pricing well above the current spot Henry Hub range for a substantial portion of production. The ceiling test buffer of $1.3B (after-tax) at December 31, 2025 provides substantial insulation against accounting impairments.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Production Growth

Q1 FY2026 production of 109.2 Bcf (up 11.7% YoY) indicates the FY2025 growth trajectory is continuing. Increasing capital allocation toward Utica Shale development suggests management sees superior returns there. Estimated FY2026E EPS of $7.79 represents 37% growth from FY2025's $5.68, driven by higher production volumes, better realized prices, and the Utica ramp.

### CenterPoint Ohio Acquisition

The $2.62B acquisition of CenterPoint Energy's Ohio natural gas distribution operations (Vectren Energy Delivery of Ohio), expected to close Q4 calendar 2026, is the most consequential growth catalyst:
- **Doubles the regulated utility rate base**, adding a large Ohio distribution franchise
- **Ohio regulatory environment** is described by management as constructive and supportive of natural gas, contrasting with New York's increasingly hostile posture
- **Earnings contribution:** Should be accretive to EPS within the first full year of ownership given the size of the rate base addition
- **Financing:** $1.42B cash + $1.2B seller note at 6.5% interest

### Pipeline Projects Tied to Data Center Demand

Two pipeline projects link NFG directly to the data center/AI power demand theme:
- **Shippingport Lateral:** 205,000 Dth/day pipeline to a natural gas power generation facility supporting a co-located data center in Beaver County, PA. $57M cost, in-service as early as Fall 2026, 100% capacity contracted.
- **Tioga Pathway:** 190,000 Dth/day expansion project, $101M cost, FERC Notice to Proceed issued January 2026, targeting late calendar 2026 in-service.

These projects provide contracted, regulated returns on incremental capital and position NFG to capture additional data center-driven demand from its pipeline network in the Marcellus/Utica basin.

### Estimate Revisions — Mixed

Current-quarter estimates have risen sharply (+27.5% over 90 days to $2.84), reflecting strong operational momentum. However, next quarter (-6.2% over 30 days), current year (-0.7%), and next year (-3.3%) estimates have softened. This divergence between strong near-term and softening outer quarters may reflect pre-acquisition uncertainty and commodity price normalization assumptions. The four-quarter streak of earnings beats (averaging +6.8% surprise) provides confidence that management can deliver against current consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

NFG trades at 12.3x trailing EPS of $5.68 and 12.3x forward earnings (implied by the data). Against the gas utility peer median of 20.4x, this represents a **40% discount**. Even acknowledging that NFG's E&P exposure introduces more earnings volatility than pure-play utility peers, the magnitude of the discount appears excessive given:

1. **Earnings quality:** The hedging program provides utility-like revenue visibility for the E&P segment through FY2027
2. **Growth premium:** 9% production growth plus acquisition-driven rate base doubling vs. low-single-digit growth for typical utility peers
3. **Asset quality:** Appalachian Basin positions in the lowest-cost gas producing region in the U.S.

At the current FY2026E of $7.79, the stock trades at 11.3x current-year earnings. Applying a conservative 13x multiple (still a 36% discount to peers) yields a $101 fair value; at 14x (32% discount), $109. The analyst consensus mean target of $103 and KeyBanc's $110 bracket this range.

The 2.4% dividend yield is below the peer median of 3.4%, but the lower payout ratio reflects reinvestment for growth at high-return pipeline and E&P projects. For a company generating 37% EPS growth, the yield differential is justified.

## Competitive Landscape

NFG's vertically integrated structure is its primary competitive differentiator. Pure-play E&P peers (EQT, Range Resources) lack the regulated earnings stability that dampens portfolio volatility. Pure-play utility peers (Atmos Energy, New Jersey Resources, Southwest Gas, Spire) lack the commodity price upside and production growth that NFG delivers.

This hybrid model creates analytical complexity: generalist utility investors may shy away from E&P exposure, while energy investors may overlook the regulated utility base. The resulting valuation gap can persist for extended periods but narrows when catalysts force re-evaluation. The CenterPoint Ohio acquisition, which shifts the earnings mix further toward regulated utility income, may serve as that catalyst.

In the Appalachian Basin, Seneca Resources competes against much larger operators (EQT, Range Resources, CNX), but the gathering synergies from the integrated midstream subsidiary provide a structural cost advantage on wellhead-to-market economics.

The pipeline segment's positioning to serve gas-fired power generation and data center demand is an emerging competitive advantage that few pure-play utilities possess.

## Risk Assessment

### Acquisition Execution and Integration

The $2.62B CenterPoint Ohio deal is the largest transaction in company history. Financing involves $1.42B in cash (requiring bridge/permanent debt) plus a $1.2B seller note at 6.5%. Any credit market disruption, regulatory delay (HSR review, PUCO notification), or integration cost overrun could impair the thesis. The 18-month termination window (by April 2027) introduces deal-break risk.

### New York Regulatory and Climate Risk

The CLCPA (40% GHG reduction by 2030, 85% by 2050), the Climate Change Superfund Act (potential emission-based payments), new-building fossil fuel equipment bans (currently stayed), and mandated customer installation cost-sharing all create secular headwinds for the NY utility franchise. Post-acquisition, Ohio's constructive environment partially mitigates this risk, but NY remains a significant portion of the utility base.

### Commodity Price Volatility

Despite hedging, NFG's E&P segment carries inherent commodity exposure. The full-cost ceiling test creates accounting volatility: FY2024's $473M impairment (plus a $46M write-down of Northern Access pipeline assets) demonstrated how non-cash charges can distort reported earnings even when underlying operations are healthy. A sustained move below $2.50/MMBtu would begin eroding the $1.3B ceiling test buffer.

### Capital Market Dependence

With $600M in notes due FY2027, $300M in FY2028, $300M in notes now current, ongoing $1B+ annual capex, and acquisition financing needs, NFG requires continuous capital market access. The recent equity raise at a discount to trading levels ($79.50 vs. ~$82 at the time) demonstrates management's willingness to issue equity when needed, but further dilution cannot be ruled out.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.9% is elevated relative to 19.8% historical volatility, a 12.2pp premium suggesting the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (31.9% near-term vs. 29.0% at 6 months), typically reflecting imminent event risk. Put/call skew of 4.8% shows modestly elevated put demand. The implied move of ±6.2% at nearest expiry brackets the $83-$94 range. With only 70 contracts across 4 expirations, the options market is thin, limiting the informational content of these signals. Short interest at 5.3% of float with 5.5 days to cover is moderate.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-$112, +25-27%)

CenterPoint Ohio acquisition closes on time, integration proceeds smoothly, and the doubled utility rate base drives immediate EPS accretion. Ohio's constructive regulatory environment supports attractive authorized returns. Nat gas prices remain above $3.50/MMBtu, and hedging locks in $4+/Mcf through FY2027. Data center pipeline projects expand, creating a recurring demand growth vector. The market re-rates NFG from an E&P discount to a growth utility premium, compressing the discount to peers from 40% to 20%. At 14x FY2026E of $7.79, the stock reaches $109; including one year of EPS growth toward $8.05 at 14x = $113.

### Bear Case ($72-$75, -15-18%)

Nat gas prices decline below $2.50/MMBtu, triggering ceiling test impairments and hedging roll-off at lower prices in FY2028. The CenterPoint acquisition encounters regulatory delays or integration costs exceed estimates. NY climate legislation accelerates customer attrition in the utility franchise. Refinancing the FY2027 debt maturities proves costly in a high-rate environment. Further equity dilution to fund the acquisition compresses per-share metrics. At 10x a reduced EPS estimate of $7.00, the stock falls to $70.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $102** — derived from 13x FY2026E EPS of $7.79 = $101, consistent with the analyst consensus median of $102. This reflects a still-discounted multiple to peers (36% vs. current 40%) that accounts for E&P volatility while recognizing superior growth.

**Bull case target: $110-$112** — 14x FY2026E of $7.79 = $109, or 14x FY2027E of $8.05 = $113, bracketed at $110-$112.

**Bear case target: $72-$75** — 10x reduced EPS of $7.00-$7.50 if commodity prices weaken and acquisition integration stalls.

**Upside/downside from current ($88.18):** +15.7% to base case, +25-27% to bull case, -15-18% to bear case.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: (1) CenterPoint Ohio closing, expected Q4 2026; (2) Shippingport Lateral in-service, Fall 2026; (3) Pennsylvania rate case resolution, likely H2 2026; (4) Q2/Q3 FY2026 earnings (April/July 2026) confirming production growth trajectory.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. CenterPoint Ohio acquisition terminated or materially restructured (deal break eliminates the primary catalyst)
2. Two consecutive quarters of production decline, indicating Seneca operational deterioration
3. Credit rating downgrade to below investment grade or debt-to-capitalization exceeding 0.55 post-acquisition
4. FY2026 EPS falls below $6.50, indicating hedging and production growth are insufficient to offset commodity weakness

## Conclusion

NFG offers a rare combination in the current energy market: a 40% valuation discount to utility peers, 37% forward EPS growth, a transformative acquisition that doubles the regulated rate base, exposure to the data center power demand theme through contracted pipeline projects, and a hedging program that locks in attractive pricing through FY2027. The vertically integrated model creates analytical complexity (which suppresses the multiple) and operational synergy (which drives returns). The four-quarter earnings beat streak and declining capex trajectory support improving free cash flow generation.

The risks are real — acquisition execution, New York climate regulation, and commodity exposure chief among them — but they are identifiable, partially hedged, and in the case of the valuation discount, arguably already over-priced by the market. At $88.18, NFG offers compelling risk-adjusted upside with identifiable catalysts over the next 12-18 months. BUY.
