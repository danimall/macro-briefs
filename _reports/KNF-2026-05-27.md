**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Knife River Corporation is a vertically integrated construction materials and contracting company operating across 15 U.S. states, built on a foundation of 1.3 billion tons of aggregate reserves with an estimated 30+ year life. The company was spun off from MDU Resources in May 2023 and has since pursued an aggressive acquisition strategy, spending $611M in 2025 and $174M in Q1 2026, roughly doubling its debt load to $1.45B. This has created a temporary earnings compression — FY2025 net income fell 22% despite 8.5% revenue growth — that the market is partially pricing through a ~25% drawdown from the 52-week high.

The growth thesis rests on three pillars: (1) a record $1.2B backlog with 75% completion expected within 12 months, (2) North Dakota DOT bid lettings doubling from $345M to $745-810M in 2026, and (3) 43% of IIJA formula funding still undistributed across KNF's 15-state footprint. Q1 2026 showed early confirmation — revenue +16% YoY, volumes surging 26% in aggregates and 33% in ready-mix, and EBITDA margin improving 290 bps despite the seasonal loss quarter. Management signaled results are "trending toward the upper half of guidance ranges."

The stock trades at roughly 11.7x EV/EBITDA on 2025 figures — a discount to Vulcan (VMC) and Martin Marietta (MLM) at 30-34x P/E, but broadly in line on an EV/EBITDA basis when accounting for KNF's higher leverage and lower margins. The path to significant upside requires the acquired businesses to deliver margin expansion and the North Dakota spending surge to convert into earnings power above cost of capital. This has not yet been demonstrated over a full cycle.

## Company Overview

Knife River operates four segments: West (AK, CA, HI, OR, WA — $1.21B revenue), Mountain (ID, MT, UT, WY — $644M), Central (IA, MN, ND, SD, TX — $1.0B), and Energy Services (liquid asphalt across 8 states — $338M). Approximately 35% of aggregates produced are consumed internally for downstream ready-mix, asphalt, and contracting services. The contracting business derives ~80% of revenue from public-sector projects, primarily state DOT work.

The competitive moat derives from the fundamental economics of aggregates: heavy materials with low value-to-weight ratios create natural local monopolies around quarry locations. Permitting new quarries can take years and faces substantial community opposition, creating durable barriers to entry. KNF differentiates from larger peers (VMC, MLM) by focusing on mid-sized, higher-growth markets and offering full vertical integration from quarry to finished roadway.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth drivers are identifiable and quantifiable:

1. **North Dakota DOT Surge:** Bid lettings doubling from $345M to $745-810M in 2026. Central segment backlog surged from $175.5M to $488.1M. The Strata acquisition was strategically timed to capture this.

2. **IIJA Tailwind:** 43% of formula funding undistributed. 11 of 15 KNF states have record DOT budgets for FY2026, with combined 15% increases over 2025. This provides multi-year visibility.

3. **Acquisitions Contributing:** Q1 2026 revenue growth of 16% was partly driven by acquired operations. The three Q1 2026 acquisitions ($174M) add Utah as a 15th state with 30+ year reserve life.

4. **Data Center Demand (Emerging):** Q1 2026 10-Q mentions aggregate volumes increased $5.6M "largely as a result of data center projects" in the Central segment. This is early-stage but potentially significant as AI infrastructure buildout accelerates.

5. **Estimate Revisions:** Consensus FY2026E EPS has risen from $3.21 to $3.37 over 30 days (+4.7%). FY2027E at $4.10 (+1.7% over 30 days). The magnitude is modest but the direction is positive.

Analyst consensus implies 18.2x FY2027E P/E at current prices — more reasonable but still requires execution on margin expansion from acquired assets.

## Competitive Landscape

KNF's competitive position is strong and improving:

- **Reserves:** 1.3 billion tons, 30+ year life — irreplaceable assets that cannot be economically replicated
- **Geographic diversification:** 15 states, focused on mid-sized markets with less competition from MLM/VMC
- **Vertical integration:** From quarry to contracting provides internal demand stability (35% of aggregates consumed internally)
- **Public-sector focus:** 80% contracting revenue from government = less cyclical than private-sector-exposed peers
- **Acquisition platform:** 90+ acquisitions since 1992 demonstrate repeatable capability

The EDGE strategy (launched 2023) emphasizes "quality over quantity of work" — selective bidding for higher-margin projects. This is showing results: Q1 2026 margins improved 290 bps despite seasonal headwinds. However, the backlog itself has lower expected margins than the prior year per management's own disclosure, creating tension between volume growth and margin quality.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Acquisition Execution (HIGH):** $785M deployed in 15 months on acquisitions while net income declined. The Strata acquisition roughly doubled Central segment goodwill. If acquired operations underperform (management explicitly noted lower backlog margins), the leverage is punitive — $82M annual interest on $1.45B debt with declining returns.

**2. Leverage in a Rising Rate Environment (MODERATE-HIGH):** $1.0B of variable-rate debt. A 100bp rate increase = $10.2M additional annual interest on a $157M net income base (6.5% earnings drag). [Unverified — "Warsh FOMC on June 16-17"] creates near-term rate risk.

**3. Backlog Margin Compression (MODERATE):** Record $1.2B backlog with explicitly lower expected margins vs. prior year. Volume growth without margin improvement would validate the Seeking Alpha critique that acquisitions drive growth without creating value above cost of capital.

**4. Seasonal Concentration (MODERATE):** Business is highly seasonal with Q1 losses of ~$79M. Cash conversion cycles are elongated by weather. Two of the last four quarters missed estimates, including a significant -37.8% miss in Q2 2025.

**5. Government Funding Dependency (LOW-MODERATE):** 80% public-sector revenue creates stability but also political dependency. Post-IIJA spending normalization (2028+) could slow growth.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-110, +34-47%):**
- North Dakota DOT spending surge + IIJA tailwinds drive record revenues in 2026-2027
- Acquired businesses achieve margin normalization (Central segment EBITDA margin from 15.9% toward 18-20%)
- FY2027 EPS reaches $4.50-5.00 on volume + pricing + operating leverage
- Multiple expands toward 22-24x as leverage decreases (targeting <2.5x net debt/EBITDA by end of 2027)
- Data center aggregate demand becomes a meaningful contributor

**Bear Case ($55-60, -25-20%):**
- Acquired businesses deliver lower margins than expected; management's own disclosure of lower backlog margins proves prescient
- Rate environment forces refi at higher rates or constrains additional acquisitions
- Weather disruptions in seasonal-dependent geographies (repeated 2025 issues)
- IIJA spending delays or political interference post-2026 elections
- Multiple compresses to 14-15x forward earnings as leverage concerns dominate

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA exceeds $575M (indicating acquired business margin conversion)
2. Stock pulls back to $65-68 (creating >50% upside to base case on current estimates)
3. Management announces meaningful debt paydown schedule or pauses acquisitions to deleverage

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 revenue miss with backlog declining sequentially
2. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 3.5x on weaker-than-expected EBITDA
3. Two consecutive quarters of aggregate pricing declines (indicating competitive pressure)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026). Q2 is the first meaningful seasonal contribution period and will reveal whether acquired businesses are on track. The ND DOT spending surge should begin appearing in Q2-Q3 revenue.

## Conclusion

Knife River possesses a high-quality asset base positioned to benefit from a genuine multi-year infrastructure spending cycle. The growth thesis is real and quantifiable — doubling ND DOT lettings, record backlogs, and early AI/data center demand are concrete catalysts. However, the aggressive acquisition strategy has created $1.45B in debt, a mixed earnings track record (2-for-4 beats), and margin uncertainty that keeps the risk/reward balanced at current levels.

At $74.81 (18.2x FY2027E), the stock is reasonably priced for a construction materials company with above-average growth but above-average leverage. The consensus target of $105 implies significant upside (40%), but achieving it requires demonstrating that acquisitions create returns above cost of capital — something not yet proven over a full cycle. HOLD with a growth lean; the next natural catalyst is Q2 earnings (August 2026) where seasonal margins and acquired business contributions will be measurable.
