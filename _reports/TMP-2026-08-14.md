## Executive Summary

Tompkins Financial has executed two consequential balance-sheet actions in the past nine months and both have now flowed through the income statement. In Q4 2025 it sold Tompkins Insurance Agencies to Arthur J. Gallagher for approximately $223M in cash (a $188.2M pre-tax gain), and simultaneously sold $564.2M of available-for-sale securities yielding 1.56% at a $78.7M pre-tax loss, reinvesting at roughly 4.52%. The reinvestment is the engine of the 2026 margin story: total securities yield rose 101bp year over year in Q2 2026 (2.51% → 3.52%) versus only 15bp on loans, lifting net interest margin to 3.58% from 3.08% a year earlier and from the FY2024 trough of 2.79%. Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $2.04 beat consensus by 10.9%, ROA reached 1.36% and ROE 12.32%, both above the FY2025 adjusted levels of 1.10% and 11.56%.

The problem is that the market has already paid for this. The stock is up 49.8% over twelve months and sits 1.5% below its 52-week high of $102.585. On a consistent basis (FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $6.31 gives 16.0x, 1H 2026 annualized $7.72 gives 13.1x, consensus $7.82 for the current year gives 12.9x and $8.32 for next year gives 12.1x) Tompkins is no longer priced at a discount to its regional-bank peer group (median trailing P/E 14.2x; TMP on last-twelve-months adjusted earnings of roughly $7.22 is 14.0x). Price-to-tangible-book of 1.64x against ~13.2% annualized ROTCE is close to theoretically fair and leaves no cushion. The forward dividend of $2.68 yields 2.65% versus a 3.2% peer median. Two-analyst consensus targets bracket $104–105, 3.5% above spot.

The unresolved question is credit, and it is specific rather than generic. Special Mention plus Substandard loans rose 45% year over year to $140.0M, C&I nonaccruals quadrupled from $2.6M to $10.4M, nonaccrual loans carrying no allocated reserve rose to $10.7M, and individually evaluated collateral-dependent loans reached $30.3M against only $1.9M of allocated allowance, while the allowance for credit losses was reduced from 0.95% to 0.89% of loans because CECL unemployment and GDP forecasts improved. Commercial real estate is 56.2% of loans and, by our calculation, 418% of tangible common equity of $887.2M, above the 300% supervisory concentration threshold. Charge-offs and delinquencies are currently excellent (1H 2026 net charge-offs 0.07% annualized; 30–89 day past dues 0.07%), which is why the stock is where it is. The reserve is being released by a model input at the same time the internally graded problem pipeline is building, and that combination is the most plausible route to a negative earnings surprise. At 1.64x tangible book, none of it is priced. HOLD; add on weakness, not here.

## Company Overview

Tompkins Financial is a financial holding company headquartered in Ithaca, New York, with $8.80B of total assets at June 30, 2026 (up from $8.11B at year-end 2024). It operates one banking subsidiary, renamed **Tompkins Bank & Trust** effective July 20, 2026, from Tompkins Community Bank, through 54 offices: 38 in New York (twelve around Ithaca, fourteen in the Genesee Valley including Rochester, twelve in the Hudson Valley) and 16 in southeastern Pennsylvania. Loans and deposits are drawn almost entirely from those communities.

Following the October 31, 2025 divestiture of the insurance agency, the company reports two segments: banking and wealth management. Banking produced $53.6M of the $55.4M of 1H 2026 consolidated net income (96.7%). Wealth management (Tompkins Financial Advisors, $3.1B of assets under management or custody) contributed $1.8M, down 8.3% year over year despite 4.1% revenue growth, as technology spending and intercompany allocations outran revenue. Total loans were $6.597B, deposits $7.029B, and total equity $959.9M, with book value per share of $66.74 and tangible book value of $61.68.

The loan mix has shifted materially over five years: commercial real estate from 52% to 56.2% of the book, commercial and industrial from 18% to 18.1% (after dipping to 15% in 2023), residential real estate from 29% to 24.4%. The portfolio is higher-yielding as a result (loan yield 4.87% in FY2023 to 5.55% in Q2 2026) and higher-beta through a credit cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Separate the GAAP headline from earnings power.** FY2025 GAAP net income was $161.1M and diluted EPS $11.24, versus $70.9M/$4.97 in FY2024. Essentially all of the increase is the paired Q4 2025 transactions: +$188.2M insurance gain, −$4.3M transaction expense, −$78.7M securities loss, with a company-stated net after-tax insurance-sale impact of +$129.0M. Management's own reconciliation puts FY2025 adjusted net income at $90.4M and adjusted diluted EPS at $6.31 (FY2024: $70.8M/$4.96), with adjusted ROE of 11.56% and adjusted ROA of 1.10% against a Federal Reserve peer group of $3–10B bank holding companies at 10.64% and 1.09%. The trailing GAAP P/E of 8.2x that appears on screens is an artifact and should be discarded.

**The margin inflection is real and traceable.** Net interest income rose from $60.13M in Q2 2025 to $73.98M in Q2 2026 (+23.0%), with NIM at 3.58% versus 3.08%. Decomposing the 50bp year-over-year gain: securities yield +101bp, loan yield +15bp, cost of interest-bearing liabilities −20bp (2.44% → 2.24%), cost of interest-bearing deposits −17bp (2.24% → 2.07%), time deposit cost 3.58% → 3.19%. The securities contribution is the deliberate repositioning; the funding relief is a sector-wide tailwind, not a Tompkins advantage.

**Sizing the repositioning earn-back.** Two bounding calculations: notional-neutral, $564.2M × (4.52% − 1.56%) = **+$16.7M pre-tax annually**; proceeds-reinvested, ($564.2M − $78.7M) × 4.52% = $21.9M versus $564.2M × 1.56% = $8.8M, or **+$13.1M pre-tax**. At the 24.0% Q2 2026 effective rate that is roughly $10–13M after tax, or **$0.69–$0.88 per diluted share** on 14.33M shares. Simple payback on the $78.7M loss is 4.7–6.0 years against a disclosed ~5.5-year weighted average life on the securities bought and sold: economically rational, but close to a wash against the WAL, and dependent on holding to maturity. Tompkins has now realized large low-coupon securities losses twice in three years ($70.0M in 2023, $78.7M in 2025). Separately, the divested agency removed roughly $9.7M of annualized net income (ten-month 2025 segment net income of $8.1M). Ongoing earnings are therefore closer to a swap than an increase; what shareholders actually received was cash plus a more rate-dependent earnings stream.

**Capital is comfortable.** At June 30, 2026: total capital/RWA 14.88%, Tier 1 and CET1 both 13.98%, leverage 10.69%, all up 30–40bp from year-end 2025 and well above well-capitalized minimums plus the fully phased-in buffer. Equity rose to $959.9M as $55.4M of 1H net income covered a $13.0M OCI reversal and $19.3M of dividends.

**Two economic offsets to reported equity.** AFS unrealized losses, cleaned to $9.3M at year-end 2025, rebuilt to $26.8M by June 30, taking AOCI from −$19.1M to −$32.1M. Held-to-maturity securities carry a $31.1M unrecognized loss (amortized cost $312.6M, fair value $281.5M) on a book yielding 1.56%. Together those equal roughly 7.1% of tangible common equity. Neither is a solvency issue (HTM is Treasuries and agency paper with no credit risk), but the HTM book is a live NIM drag until it rolls, and $272.8M of it matures within one to five years.

**Cash flow requires a caveat.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $38.9M against $161.1M of net income is a divestiture artifact: the $188.2M gain is a non-cash operating add-back while the associated cash taxes are an operating outflow. Cash taxes paid in 1H 2026 were $54.4M versus $13.8M in 1H 2025, and the 1H cash flow statement carries a −$36.8M current-tax-liability adjustment; the transaction tax bill was largely settled this year. 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $58.3M, itself flattered by a $43.6M swing in loans held for sale. Neither figure should be annualized without adjustment; normalized underlying generation is plausibly $100–120M annually.

**Funding mix deteriorated on every measure in 1H 2026.** Core deposits fell to 77.9% of total from 79.5% at year-end and 80.0% a year earlier, declining $42.9M in dollars. Non-core funding reached 29.1% of total liabilities from 26.9%. Brokered time deposits rose 54.1% in six months. Pledged securities went to 63.9% of the portfolio from 52.1%, cutting unencumbered securities available to pledge to $595.8M from $799.1M. Correspondent fed funds purchased stood at $148.7M against $149.0M of available unsecured lines, effectively fully drawn at quarter-end. Board-policy on-balance-sheet liquidity fell to 9.7% from 11.1% against a 3% floor.

Management attributes much of this to seasonal municipal deposit flows, and the 10-K's description (a July–August trough, building through winter, a March state-funds inflow) plus the Q2 commentary citing "seasonal outflow of municipal deposits resulting in increased borrowings" make that the more likely primary driver: a June 30 reading versus December 31 compares a seasonal low to a seasonal high. The cleaner year-over-year comparison still shows roughly 210bp of core deposit erosion. The competing explanation, that 7% loan growth is outrunning core deposit generation and being funded at wholesale rates, is equally consistent with the data and matters more for forward NIM. Two quarters cannot distinguish them.

## Growth Analysis

Loan growth has run above sector norms: +7.1% in FY2025 and +2.3% in 1H 2026, with average loans +6.5% year over year in Q2. C&I is the fastest-growing category, +14.0% in FY2025 and +8.2% in 1H 2026 alone to $1.19B. Deposits grew 7.2% in FY2025 and 1.3% in 1H 2026.

Forward earnings growth rests on three mechanical levers rather than on volume:

1. **HTM roll-down.** $312.6M yielding 1.56%, with $272.8M maturing in one to five years. Redeploying that at ~4.5% is worth roughly $9M pre-tax annually at full replacement, about $0.48 per share after tax, arriving over several years.
2. **Loan repricing.** Loan yield of 5.55% still sits below new-origination levels; $3.5B of loans maturing beyond one year are adjustable-rate.
3. **Funding cost relief**, which is now largely realized and reverses if the Fed hikes.

Management's own market-risk disclosure guides to "a net interest margin that is increasing over the next 12 months." The May 31, 2026 simulation shows +200bp reducing net interest income 1.8% and −200bp increasing it 1.2%, slightly liability-sensitive, well inside a −10% board tolerance. The one-year static gap moved from −$106.9M (−1.23% of assets) to +$25.3M (+0.29%), so the balance sheet is now roughly rate-neutral over twelve months and the base-case margin expansion depends on reinvestment of low-coupon assets, not on rate direction.

Consensus reflects this: current year $7.82, next year $8.32, 6.4% growth. Revisions are positive but modest (+3.9% current-year and +2.1% next-year over 90 days, both entirely in the last 30 days), on two-analyst coverage. This is a normal upward drift after a 10.9% beat rather than the estimate-revision pattern that signals systematic underestimation.

Working against growth: the fee base has been cut. Insurance commissions were $39.1M in FY2024 and $35.6M in the ten months of 2025, and are now gone. Remaining recurring fee lines are wealth management (~$21M), card services (~$11.5M, which declined 4.6% in FY2025 partly on lower NYCE interchange), deposit service charges (~$7.3M) and other income. The company is materially more rate-dependent than it was in 2024, and the wealth franchise now carrying the new "Bank & Trust" brand posted declining segment net income in both FY2025 (−8.2%) and 1H 2026 (−8.3%). Deposit mix drift also caps NIM: incremental funding at brokered and municipal rates raises marginal cost of funds even as the average declines.

Multiple bases, all reconciled to the $101.0125 price:

| LTM adjusted (est.) | ~$7.22 | ~14.0x |

| Q2 2026 annualized | $8.16 | 12.4x |
| Consensus current year | $7.82 | 12.9x |

Against the regional-bank peer set (LKFN 14.2x, WABC 13.1x, PFBC 9.7x, HFWA 14.2x, DCOM 14.6x; median 14.2x on a trailing basis), Tompkins at ~14.0x LTM adjusted earnings is **at**, not below, the peer median. The forward multiples of 12.9x/12.1x look like a discount only because they are compared to peers' trailing figures. The apparent cheapness on screens is entirely the GAAP distortion.

Price-to-tangible-book is the more disciplining lens for a bank. At 1.638x TBV of $61.68, with annualized Q2 ROTCE of 13.2% ($117.2M on $887.2M of TCE), a standard justified-multiple framework — (ROTCE − g)/(COE − g) — gives 1.55x at a 10% cost of equity and 4% growth, 1.69x at 9.5%. The stock is therefore fairly valued to modestly rich on tangible book, with no discount to absorb a provisioning normalization. Book value per share of $66.74 puts P/B at 1.51x.

Income support is thin. The verifiable recurring dividend is $0.67 quarterly ($2.68 annualized), a genuine 8.1% increase from $0.62 in Q2 2025, giving a 2.65% yield against a 3.2% peer median and a comfortable 35% payout on 1H26-annualized EPS of $7.72. The "+28.3% dividend increase" implied by FY2025 declared DPS of $3.13 does not reconcile to $36.1M of cash dividends paid (~$2.53/share) and should not be read as a change in the recurring rate.

Analyst targets cluster at $104–105 (mean $104.50), 3.5% above spot, on two-analyst coverage with a stable sentiment trend and no recent revisions. On a stock trading at ~13x forward with mid-single-digit expected growth, the multiple has already re-rated as the margin story delivered, and the remaining gap between price and any reasonable fair-value estimate is single-digit.

**Scenario framework (12 months):**

- **Base $107** — 12.9x maintained on next-year consensus $8.32. NIM holds near 3.55–3.65%, provisions rise modestly from the 1H run-rate, credit classifications stabilize. **+5.9% price, +8.5% with the dividend.**
- **Bull $125** — NIM to 3.75% on HTM roll-down and loan repricing, provisions stay near 0.10%, EPS reaches $9.00, multiple to 14.0x. **+23.7%.**
- **Bear $78** — CECL macro inputs revert, classified loans migrate, provisioning steps to roughly 0.50% of loans (about $33M annually versus a $6M annualized 1H 2026 pace), costing ~$1.43 per share after tax and taking EPS to ~$6.90; multiple compresses to ~11.3x, cross-checked at ~1.25x forward tangible book. **−22.8%.**

Probability-weighting 55/20/25 gives an expected value near $103.35, roughly +2.3% in price and +5.0% total return. With the two-year Treasury at 4.20%, that is not an adequate equity risk premium, and the downside tail is four times the base-case upside. It is also not sufficiently negative to argue for trimming a quality franchise. Valuation score 5.0 — fair, with unfavorable asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Management's stated advantage is relationship-based community banking and faster credit decisioning, while explicitly acknowledging competitors with "substantially greater resources and lending capabilities." The 10-Q's competition discussion now names fintechs and, unusually for a $9B community bank, "stablecoins and other forms of cryptocurrency, tokens, and other digital assets or alternative payment systems." Credit unions matter disproportionately in upstate New York: tax-advantaged, aggressive on deposit pricing, increasingly active in commercial lending. Competition on commercial relationships turns partly on relative lending limits, precisely where an $8.8B balance sheet is structurally disadvantaged against larger regionals bidding for the same CRE and C&I credits.

On performance, Tompkins is a modestly-above-median operator, not an outlier: FY2025 adjusted ROE of 11.56% against a peer group at 10.64%, adjusted ROA of 1.10% against 1.09%, NPAs/assets of 0.60% against a 0.62% peer average. Q2 2026's 12.32% ROE and 1.36% ROA are genuine improvement over the adjusted FY2025 base, and the improvement is margin-driven rather than fee- or credit-driven, which makes it more durable but also more rate-dependent.

Trajectory is the relevant question, and it is mixed. The franchise has become more commercial and more CRE-weighted, which raises yield and cycle beta. It has removed its most diversifying revenue stream (insurance) at a good price. The remaining non-bank segment, the one the new brand name emphasizes, is shrinking in profit. Deposit market share is being defended with brokered and municipal funding rather than core growth, at least in the most recent half. Against that, the securities book is now competitively yielding rather than a legacy drag, and capital ratios give the company more room to lend than most similarly sized peers. Net assessment: stable, with mix drift toward higher-risk lending offsetting the balance-sheet improvement.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commercial real estate concentration (highest impact).** CRE of $3.709B is 56.2% of loans and, on our calculation, 418% of tangible common equity, above the 300% supervisory concentration threshold that triggers heightened examiner attention. Construction is $465.8M, 52.5% of TCE, and grew to 12.6% of CRE from 11.4% at year-end 2024; $333.2M of it is revolving draw-period structure. Every credit that drove recent charge-offs and nonaccruals was CRE. Two real mitigants a screen would miss: office is only $239.0M, 6.4% of CRE and roughly 3.6% of total loans, and it is shrinking; and there is no near-term refinancing cliff — of $3.662B of CRE at year-end 2025, only $189.9M matured within one year, with $1.624B in the 5–15 year bucket.

**2. Reserve adequacy against rising classifications.** This is the swing factor. ACL/loans of 0.89% is at the low end of the five-year 0.84%–0.95% range and fell year over year because CECL unemployment and GDP forecasts improved, while Special Mention plus Substandard rose 45% year over year to $140.0M, C&I nonaccruals quadrupled to $10.4M, nonaccruals with no allocated ACL rose to $10.7M, and individually evaluated collateral-dependent loans reached $30.3M with $1.9M of allocated allowance (1H 2026 fair-value write-downs on those loans: $3.7M). Offsetting evidence is strong: 1H net charge-offs 0.07% annualized versus 0.20% in 1H 2025, 30–89 day past dues down to 0.07% from 0.14%, NPAs in line with peers, and rising Special Mention can reflect conservative proactive grading off a low base ($140.0M is 2.1% of loans). But two credits that did resolve were under-reserved relative to realized loss — a $4.2M specific reserve against a $4.7M charge-off, and a $1.6M reserve against a $2.4M charge-off — suggesting collateral marks on individually evaluated CRE have run slightly optimistic. A CECL forecast reversion combined with the classified-loan build is the most plausible path to a negative earnings surprise. [unverified — the brief contains no macro data on private-credit default rates, mortgage rates, or Fed policy]

**3. Funding mix and encumbrance.** Discussed above. Absolute liquidity remains far above policy minimums (9.7% versus a 3% floor), so this is a margin-ceiling and marginal-cost issue rather than a stress issue. The fully drawn $148.7M correspondent line at quarter-end is worth monitoring in the Q3 filing, when the seasonal municipal explanation should reverse.

**4. Revenue concentration post-divestiture.** Roughly 97% of net income now comes from banking. The company is materially more exposed to rate and credit outcomes than it was in 2024, with less fee ballast to smooth a provisioning quarter.

**5. Unrecognized HTM mark and AOCI volatility.** $31.1M of HTM loss outside equity on a 1.56% book; AFS marks rebuilt from $9.3M to $26.8M in six months, showing the Q4 cleanup did not immunize equity. Combined, ~7.1% of TCE.

**6. Rate positioning simplified.** The $50.0M-notional designated fair value hedge outstanding at year-end 2025 was $0 at June 30, and the company reports no outstanding designated interest rate swaps. Defensible given only 1.8% of NII at risk in a 200bp up-shock, but it removes a tool, and the simulation assumes no balance sheet growth, no mix change and historical deposit betas, assumptions the observed 1H mix drift already violates.

**7. Idiosyncratic exposures.** Agricultural loans of $358.9M (5.4% of loans), dairy and crop, underwritten on borrower cash flow — small but commodity-cycle sensitive and invisible in generic regional-bank comps.

The Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors from the FY2025 10-K, and disclosure controls were concluded effective with no ICFR changes. No named litigation, enforcement action, restatement, auditor change, or CEO/CFO transition appears in the record.

### Options Market Signal

The options data are close to uninformative here and should not be over-read: 40 contracts across three expirations on a NYSE American name averaging 65,000 shares of daily volume. Near-term ATM IV of 45.8% against 27.9% one-year realized, in backwardation against 23.3% at six months, sits on a six-day expiry with no scheduled catalyst — Q2 was reported July 24 and the next print is late October. That pattern is more consistent with a single wide bid-ask on a thin strike than with the market pricing imminent stress. The more meaningful readings are the six-month tenor at 23.3% (roughly in line with realized volatility) with a put/call skew described as balanced, a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.08 that is call-tilted but on negligible open interest, and the trend since May: ATM IV fell from 42.2% to 38.4% while the price rose 16.6%. Short interest of 2.4% of float with 2.9 days to cover confirms the absence of a bear constituency. The derivatives and short-interest evidence show complacency rather than confirmation of either case, consistent with our view that the credit question is not priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $125 (+23.7%)

The margin inflection has further to run on mechanics that do not require macro cooperation. $312.6M of HTM securities yielding 1.56% roll into 4.5% assets, with $272.8M maturing inside five years; $3.5B of adjustable loans continue repricing upward; loan growth of 6–7% adds earning assets funded off a 13.98% CET1 base that has room to lever. Provisioning stays near the 1H 2026 pace because the classified-loan build reflects conservative grading rather than deterioration, supported by 0.07% net charge-offs, 0.07% delinquencies, and management's characterization of nonperformers as collateral-dependent with limited exposure. FY2027 EPS reaches $9.00 against $8.32 consensus, and a bank compounding tangible book at 13%+ ROTCE with in-line asset quality earns 14.0x, in the middle of the peer range. Roughly $160M of retained insurance proceeds plus 342,143 shares of remaining repurchase authorization provide optionality for accretive whole-bank or branch M&A in a consolidating upstate market, or for buybacks if the price retreats.

### Bear Case — $78 (−22.8%)

The reserve is the tell. Tompkins is releasing allowance because a CECL model input improved, at the same time its own internal grading pipeline expanded 45% and C&I nonaccruals quadrupled. If unemployment and GDP forecasts revert, the model rebuilds the reserve and the classified loans migrate simultaneously. Provisioning at 0.50% of a $6.6B book is $33M annually versus a $6M annualized 1H 2026 pace, costing roughly $1.43 per share after tax and taking EPS to about $6.90. CRE at 418% of TCE with construction at 52.5% means loss severity in a downturn is levered, and the two resolved credits both charged off more than their specific reserves. Multiple compression to ~11.3x, cross-checked at 1.25x forward tangible book, produces $78. The company enters this with less fee ballast than at any point in recent years, no designated rate hedge, marginal funding sourced from brokered and municipal deposits, and 63.9% of securities already pledged.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD. Reassessment point: the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected late October 2026, with the 10-Q roughly a week later.**

Base case $107 (+5.9%, +8.5% with the dividend), bull $125 (+23.7%), bear $78 (−22.8%). Probability-weighted expected value near $103 implies roughly a 5% total return over twelve months against a 4.20% two-year Treasury — approximately fair, with downside four times base-case upside. Existing holders are compensated by a 2.65% covered dividend and a franchise earning above its peer group; new capital has no entry edge at 1.64x tangible book.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price.** A decline to $85–88 (roughly 10.5x next-year consensus, ~1.35x forward tangible book) with fundamentals intact would restore a margin of safety and would be a scale-in level.
2. **Credit resolution.** Special Mention plus Substandard flat or declining from $140.0M in the Q3 filing, C&I nonaccruals below $8M, and ACL/loans held at or above 0.89% — i.e., the classified build stops without the reserve being rebuilt out of earnings.
3. **NIM plus funding.** Q3 NIM at or above 3.60% *together with* core deposits recovering above 80% of total and the correspondent fed funds line no longer near-fully drawn, confirming the mix drift was seasonal.
4. **Capital deployment.** An announced acquisition at a defensible multiple, or meaningful repurchase activity below 1.4x tangible book, which would convert the retained proceeds from idle capital into per-share value.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Provisioning step-up.** Quarterly provision above $8M, or ACL/loans rebuilt above 0.95% accompanied by rising net charge-offs — this would validate the reserve-adequacy concern and compress both earnings and the multiple.
2. **Classified migration.** Special Mention plus Substandard above $165M, or nonaccruals above $65M, or any single CRE construction relationship moving to nonaccrual.
3. **Funding.** Core deposits below 76% of total, or non-core funding above 32% of liabilities, in the Q3 filing — which would refute the seasonal explanation and cap the NIM thesis.
4. **Margin reversal.** Q3 NIM below 3.45% on rising deposit costs, indicating the funding-cost tailwind has turned.

**What would not change our view:** a 5–10% price decline on no news (that improves risk/reward and moves the stock toward the accumulation zone), or continued modest positive estimate revisions of the current 2–4% magnitude.

## Conclusion

Tompkins has done two sensible things well. It sold a non-core fee business at roughly 5.2x revenue and 23x earnings — a full price in a consolidating brokerage market — and it used the same quarter to clear a 1.56%-yielding securities portfolio into 4.52% assets, which has lifted net interest margin 179bp from the FY2024 trough and driven Q2 ROE to 12.32%. Capital ratios rose while it did so. Adjusted returns sit modestly above the Federal Reserve peer group, asset quality headlines are in line, and the recurring dividend grew 8.1%.

The stock now reflects that. At $101.01 it trades 12.9x current-year and 12.1x next-year consensus, roughly 14.0x last-twelve-months adjusted earnings against a 14.2x peer median, and 1.64x tangible book on 13.2% ROTCE — a level that a justified-multiple framework calls fair to slightly rich. The yield is 55bp below peers, two-analyst targets sit 3.5% above spot, and the multiple has already expanded as the margin story delivered. There is no identifiable mispricing to exploit.

Against a fair price sits one unresolved and specific asymmetry: the allowance was cut from 0.95% to 0.89% of loans because a macro forecast improved, while classified loans rose 45% year over year, C&I nonaccruals quadrupled, and $30.3M of collateral-dependent loans carry $1.9M of allocated reserve — all on a book where CRE is 418% of tangible common equity and both recently resolved problem credits charged off more than their specific reserves. Charge-offs and delinquencies are currently excellent, which is exactly why the market is not pricing this. The Q3 filing resolves the question in either direction.
