## Executive Summary

AIG has completed the most consequential corporate transformation in the insurance sector over the past half-decade. The deconsolidation of Corebridge Financial in mid-2024, combined with aggressive underwriting discipline, expense reduction, and $20B+ in capital returned, has reshaped the company into a focused P&C platform. Adjusted EPS rose 43% YoY to $7.09 in 2025, underwriting income exceeded $2B for the first time since 2008, and the combined ratio improved to 90.1%. S&P, Moody's, and Fitch all upgraded AIG's ratings in 2025 — an unprecedented sweep that formally closes the post-2008 credit stigma.

At ~10x forward earnings ($7.80 consensus FY2026E), AIG trades at a 26% discount to the 13.6x multi-line insurance peer median. The discount is partly explained by long-tail casualty reserve uncertainty — UK/Europe Casualty showed $216M in adverse development in 2025, the third consecutive year — and the upcoming CEO succession from Peter Zaffino to Eric Anderson. But the magnitude of the discount exceeds what these risks warrant given the credit upgrade trajectory, $3.8B remaining buyback authorization, and Corebridge monetization that will fund further capital return throughout 2026.

The stock has declined from $87.46 (52-week high, June 2025) to $77.73 currently, a pullback driven largely by the broader geopolitical and macro environment rather than company-specific deterioration. With consensus estimates implying 10% EPS growth in FY2026 and 13% in FY2027, the entry point offers a favorable risk/reward.

## Company Overview

AIG operates as a global property-casualty insurer across three segments following the Corebridge deconsolidation:

- **North America Commercial** (~$8.8B net premiums written): Property, casualty, and financial lines for businesses ranging from SMEs to multinationals across the U.S., Canada, and Bermuda.
- **International Commercial** (~$8.7B NPW): Same product lines globally through EMEA, UK, Japan, Asia Pacific, and Latin America, including Lloyd's operations (Talbot) and Global Specialty (aviation, political risk, trade credit).
- **Global Personal** (~$6.3B NPW): Accident & Health, personal auto/homeowners, warranty/device protection, and high-net-worth personal lines.

The company operates in approximately 200 countries and jurisdictions, supported by a $71B investment-grade fixed maturity portfolio (91%+ IG) and a $25B adverse development cover with National Indemnity (Berkshire Hathaway) protecting legacy U.S. commercial long-tail reserves for accident years 2015 and prior.

AIG retains a ~10% stake in Corebridge, which it intends to fully divest in 2026 following Nippon Life's waiver of the 9.9% retention requirement.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Trajectory

The earnings trajectory since 2023 demonstrates the quality of the turnaround:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Premiums Earned | $23.7B | $23.5B | $25.6B* |
| Adjusted After-Tax Income | $4.0B | $3.3B | $3.2B |
| Diluted Adjusted EPS | $7.09 | $4.95 | $4.42 |
| Combined Ratio | 90.1% | 91.8% | 90.6% |
| Core Operating ROE | 11.1% | 9.1% | 9.6% |

*2023 includes Corebridge operations

The 43% adjusted EPS growth in 2025 was driven by three sources: (1) 1.7pp combined ratio improvement, led by a 90bp expense ratio decline to 31.1%; (2) net investment income growth of 8% on the insurance book, reaching $3.8B; and (3) 73M shares repurchased (~11% reduction). Management has guided to low-to-mid-teens NPW growth for 2026 and a sub-30% expense ratio by 2027, both credible given the trajectory.

AIG has effectively replaced the full earnings contribution of divested Corebridge and Validus Re within two years — adjusted EPS of $7.09 in 2025 (pure P&C) exceeds $4.42 in 2023 (including both businesses) by 60%.

### Balance Sheet and Capital Return

- **Debt**: $9.0B with only $36M maturing in 2026. Debt-to-capital ratio of 18%.
- **Parent liquidity**: $9.3B including $3.0B undrawn revolver (expiring September 2029).
- **Capital return**: $6.8B in 2025 ($5.8B buybacks + $1.0B dividends). Share count reduced 31% since 2022 (779M → 538M).
- **Remaining authorization**: $3.8B, supplemented by Corebridge proceeds. Another 8-10% share reduction plausible in 2026.
- **Book value**: $76.44/share (GAAP); core operating BV $69.12/share.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.81 is mechanically misleading for an insurance company given the model's industrial-firm assumptions. The 2025 credit upgrades from all three agencies are the definitive signal of balance sheet health.

### Investment Portfolio

The $71B AFS fixed maturity portfolio is 91%+ investment grade with a 4.9% average yield. Duration was shortened slightly in 2025. A 100bp rate increase adds approximately $300-400M annually to investment income based on disclosed sensitivities. In the current environment where rate cuts are unlikely and hikes are possible, this is a meaningful tailwind.

## Growth Analysis

AIG's growth levers for 2026-2027:

1. **NPW growth guidance: low-to-mid-teens.** Driven by wholesale casualty pricing (mid-teens increases), Global Specialty expansion, and recent acquisitions (Everest renewal rights, Lloyd's Syndicate 2479).

2. **Expense ratio compression.** The 90bp decline to 31.1% in 2025 is on a glide path to sub-30% by 2027. Each 100bp improvement translates to approximately $240M in pre-tax underwriting income on the current premium base.

3. **Strategic platform buildout.** The Convex (~$2.1B, 35% stake), Onex ($646M, 9.9% stake), CVC partnership ($3.5B deployment), and Everest renewal rights ($301M) signal a pivot from defense to offense. These are capital-efficient minority positions that expand AIG's reach into specialty insurance and alternative investment management without full consolidation risk.

4. **Share buybacks.** At current prices and remaining authorization, buybacks alone could add 3-4% to EPS growth annually.

5. **Investment income.** Reinvestment of maturing fixed income at current yields (4.5-5.0%) and any rate increases provide organic NII growth.

Against these positives, estimate revisions have turned modestly negative: current-year EPS estimates fell 0.7% over 90 days and 0.8% over 30 days. Next-year estimates are up 1.3% over 90 days but flat to slightly down over 30 days. This is a minor headwind — the 4/4 quarterly beat rate provides confidence that consensus is achievable.

## Competitive Landscape

AIG competes with Chubb (CB), Travelers (TRV), Zurich, Allianz, and specialty carriers across its global P&C footprint. Key competitive dynamics:

- **Pricing discipline.** AIG deliberately shrank its North America retail property book by 8% in response to 10-13% price declines, demonstrating willingness to sacrifice top-line for underwriting profitability. This is the correct response in a softening property market.
- **Casualty pricing power.** Wholesale and excess casualty pricing remains favorable (mid-teens increases), driven by social inflation, nuclear verdicts, and litigation funding trends. AIG's deep expertise in these lines positions it well as long as reserving keeps pace.
- **Global reach.** Operations in ~200 countries/jurisdictions provide diversification that pure domestic carriers lack. The International segment's 89.2% combined ratio was the strongest of the three segments.
- **Specialty platform.** The Convex/Onex/CVC/Everest investments expand AIG's access to specialty and asset management capabilities at lower capital intensity than traditional M&A.

The credit upgrades (AA- FSR from S&P and Fitch, A1 from Moody's) enhance AIG's ability to write large-limit commercial policies where counterparty credit quality matters.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Long-Tail Casualty Reserve Uncertainty (HIGH)
AIG carries $15.4B in net long-tail casualty reserves across U.S. Excess Casualty ($3.2B), U.S. Other Casualty ($4.7B), U.S. Financial Lines ($5.3B), and UK/Europe Casualty ($8.3B). The sensitivity analysis is concerning: a 5pp increase in U.S. Excess Casualty loss cost trend = $900M reserve increase; a 10pp D&O loss cost increase = $750M. These are individually large relative to $4.0B adjusted net income.

UK/Europe Casualty and Financial Lines have shown persistent adverse development — $216M in 2025, $170M in 2024, $165M in 2023 — totaling $551M over three years. This pattern suggests historical pricing or reserving was inadequate. The NICO adverse development cover provides a backstop for pre-2016 U.S. long-tail lines ($16.5B in covered losses, $273M remaining deferred gain), but recent accident years are unprotected.

### 2. CEO Succession Risk (MEDIUM)
Peter Zaffino is the architect of the turnaround. His transition to Eric Anderson introduces execution risk — the new CEO must maintain underwriting discipline, expense reduction momentum, and capital allocation rigor. The transition timing during a complex strategic pivot (Convex, Onex, CVC) amplifies this risk.

### 3. Catastrophe Exposure (MEDIUM)
The 1-in-250 worldwide PML of $2.5B net of reinsurance represents 4.8% of shareholders' equity. California wildfire losses were $440M in 2025. The Gulf region faces elevated hurricane risk under current conditions. AIG's reinsurance program provides protection, but retained losses can be material in any given year.

### 4. Social Inflation (MEDIUM-HIGH)
Nuclear jury verdicts, litigation financing, and broadening liability theories directly affect AIG's largest reserve categories. The U.S. Excess Casualty line ($3.2B reserves) is the most exposed. The accident year loss ratio ex-CAT ticked up 1.0pp to 57.2% in 2025, potentially an early signal of loss cost pressure.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Sensitivity (MEDIUM)
As a global P&C insurer, AIG faces exposure through multiple channels: insured losses from conflict-related disruptions (trade credit, political risk, marine), investment portfolio mark-to-market on credit spread widening, and FX impacts on international operations. Elevated geopolitical uncertainty and potential credit cascade create elevated uncertainty for 2026 underwriting results.

### 6. Softening Property Pricing (LOW-MEDIUM)
North America retail property pricing declined 10-13% in 2025. AIG's disciplined response (shrinking the book) limits near-term impact but constrains a growth lever. If casualty pricing also softens, top-line growth becomes harder to sustain.

## Conclusion

AIG represents a turnaround entering its value-realization phase. The multi-year operating transformation is substantiated by hard data: 90.1% combined ratio, 43% EPS growth, credit upgrades from all three agencies, and a 31% share count reduction since 2022. At ~10x forward earnings and roughly book value, the stock is priced as if the turnaround hasn't happened. Long-tail casualty reserves are a genuine and quantifiable risk, and the CEO succession adds transition uncertainty. But the magnitude of the peer discount (26%), the capital return runway ($3.8B buyback + Corebridge proceeds), and the ongoing NII benefit from higher rates create sufficient upside to compensate. BUY with a $95 base case target, representing 22% upside over 12 months.
