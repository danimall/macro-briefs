**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Academy Sports & Outdoors is a full-line sporting goods and outdoor retailer operating 322 stores across 21 southern-U.S. states, trading at roughly 8.3x forward consensus EPS ($6.29 FY2026E), a steep discount to the specialty-retail peer median near 28x. The valuation gap reflects deteriorating fundamentals. Net income has declined three consecutive years ($628M → $519M → $418M → $377M), operating margin compressed from 11.0% to 8.5%, and FY2025 revenue growth of +2.0% came entirely from new stores while comparable sales fell -1.5% on -4.2% comp transactions. The core problem is traffic, not pricing, masked at the per-share line by an 18% share-count reduction over three years.

There is a genuine, if fragile, recovery signal. Gross margin expanded +90 bps to 34.8% in FY2025 and +170 bps to 35.7% in Q3 FY2025, driven by private-label mix, freight normalization, and improving shrink. Q3 comps improved to -0.9%, net income grew +8.8%, and e-commerce rose +22.2%. The balance sheet is sound (Altman Z 4.1, $330M cash, $0 drawn on a $1.0B ABL, total debt only $486M), the new-store program adds structurally-committed revenue independent of comps, and the buyback converts modest operating gains into mid-single-digit-plus EPS growth. That combination supports a 12-month case for EPS appreciation if the consumer holds.

The case is held back by the evidence pointing the other way. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every period (current year -3.5%, next year -2.9% over 90 days), the company has missed in three of its last four quarters, the most recent reported quarter missed by 5.5%, short interest sits at 15.6% of float, and the options market is pricing imminent stress into the June Q1 print (94.5% near-term IV versus 43% realized, 15.4% put skew, term-structure backwardation). ASO's core customer is the lower/middle-income household most exposed to discretionary-spending pressure. We rate ASO **HOLD** with a growth-oriented rationale, recognizing that the thesis is binary on whether the Q3 momentum reversal proves durable.

## Company Overview

Academy operates a value-oriented, locally-merchandised model across 322 stores averaging ~70,000 sq ft, supported by three distribution centers and ~23,000 team members. FY2025 net sales of $6.05B split across Outdoors (31%), Apparel (27%), Sports & Recreation (22%), and Footwear (20%). The company blends national brands with a higher-margin private-label portfolio (Magellan Outdoors, BCG, Game Winner, O'rageous, Freely). Firearms and ammunition are ~6% of sales, a differentiator versus some competitors and a source of regulatory and reputational risk. The company reports as a single segment.

Three operational levers define the growth strategy: (1) new-store expansion — 63 stores opened since the program relaunched in 2022, 24 in FY2025, 20-25 guided for FY2026, with mature units averaging ~$13M sales each; (2) omnichannel — e-commerce reached 11.7% of merchandise sales (+13.6% YoY) with stores facilitating ~95% of total sales via BOPIS and ship-from-store; and (3) loyalty (myAcademy Rewards) plus a new customer data platform. The recently-secured Jordan Brand allocation is a meaningful national-brand win and a potential traffic driver.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and comps.** FY2026 (year ended 1/31/2026) net sales rose +2.0% to $6.05B, but the entirety of that growth, roughly $142.8M, came from 24 new stores. Comparable sales fell -1.5%, with comp transactions down -4.2% partly offset by +2.9% average ticket. The customer-count erosion is the central concern: ASO is buying revenue through unit expansion while its existing-store base loses footfall.

**Margins.** The gross-margin gain was more than consumed by SG&A deleverage, which rose 150 bps to 26.3% of sales on new-store investment (+$84.8M), technology (+$13.1M), and the Jordan launch. The margin improvement was sourced from merchandise-margin gains (promotional discipline, pricing to offset tariffs), lower freight, and lower shrink. Operating income fell to $512M (8.5% margin) from $678M (11.0%) the prior year.

**Earnings quality and the buyback.** Net income fell ~10% in FY2025, but diluted EPS fell only ~3% ($5.73 → $5.54) because the basic share count dropped to 66.6M from 81.6M three years ago. The buyback is doing the heavy lifting; per-share results overstate underlying business momentum. This is rational capital allocation at ~9x earnings, but investors should not mistake EPS support for operating recovery.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** The balance sheet is a clear strength: Altman Z 4.1 (safe zone), current ratio 1.89, debt/equity 0.59, total debt of $486M (the $400M 6.00% Notes and ~$86M Term Loan both due Nov 2027), $0 drawn on the $1.0B ABL (~$992M available), and $330M cash. Operating cash flow declined to $435M from $528M, dragged ~$119M by a deliberate pull-forward of inventory at pre-tariff rates (inventory rose ~15% to $1.5B). That is a one-time working-capital event rather than structural deterioration; FCF was ~$222M. A May 2026 financing action appears to proactively address the 2027 maturity wall, a de-risking step, though the terms should be confirmed against the actual filing.

**Surprise and revision trends.** The earnings record is weak: 1 beat, 3 misses over the last four quarters, including a -5.5% miss in the most recently reported period. Consensus is broadly cutting numbers — next-quarter EPS revised -5.7% over 90 days. Negative revision momentum is one of the most reliable forward warning signs, and it argues against treating the Q3 inflection as confirmed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is structural rather than comp-driven, which is both its strength and its limitation. Three drivers can grow EPS over the next 12 months independent of same-store traffic:

1. **Unit growth.** 20-25 new stores in FY2026 at ~$13M mature sales each adds roughly $130-180M of annualized revenue capacity (at maturity), an operationally-committed top-line tailwind that does not require a comp inflection.
2. **Margin expansion.** The gross-margin gains are sourced from durable factors (private-label mix, freight normalization, shrink improvement), not one-off promotions.
3. **Buyback leverage.** The share reduction plus $436.6M remaining on the authorization (through Dec 2027) converts modest operating improvement into mid-single-digit-plus EPS growth. Consensus models +19.7% Q1 EPS growth on +6.5% revenue.

The constraint is that all three drivers sit on top of a shrinking same-store base in a soft consumer environment. Comp transactions down -4.2% in a year when management is spending heavily to drive traffic is a poor underlying signal. ASO's customer skews lower/middle-income and is dependent on consumer confidence, inflation, fuel prices, and tax-refund timing. The Q3 -0.9% comp is the first evidence of stabilization, and falling estimates suggest the sell-side does not yet fully believe the inflection is durable. We score growth at 4.5: the unit and margin machine is real, but earnings have declined three straight years and forward estimates are being cut.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.75, ASO trades at ~9.3x trailing EPS and ~8.3x consensus FY2026 EPS. Against the specialty-stores peer set the discount is extreme — peer median P/E is 28.5x (Five Below 34.1x, National Vision 28.5x), though the cohort is heterogeneous and includes distressed names (Sally Beauty 6.9x, MarineMax negative). The cleaner read is that ASO trades cheap for cyclical and structural reasons: declining net income, traffic erosion, and high consumer-discretionary sensitivity.

The question is whether the market is over-discounting. Three points support a modest mispricing: (1) the balance sheet carries net optionality with $330M cash, $992M of undrawn ABL, and only $486M debt, so this is not a solvency-driven value trap; (2) the buyback at an ~$50.62 FY2025 average, roughly in line with the current price, provides a per-share floor and converts even flat operating income into EPS growth; and (3) at ~8x forward earnings the bar for the stock to work is low — comp stabilization plus continued margin expansion re-rates the multiple meaningfully.

Against that, estimates are falling and the recent miss pattern means the forward EPS anchor itself may drift lower. Analyst targets (low $50, mean $61.42, median $60, high $78) imply ~16% upside to the median, but consensus has been cutting numbers, so the mean target is at risk of revision. We score valuation 6.0: genuinely cheap with a downside cushion, but the discount partly reflects real risks rather than pure mispricing, and the inflection-stock framework does not fully apply here because estimate revisions are negative.

## Competitive Landscape

The sporting-goods and outdoor market is fragmented and intensely competitive: mass merchants (Walmart, Target), large-format specialty (DICK'S Sporting Goods), outdoor/footwear specialists, Amazon and pure-play e-commerce, and vendor DTC channels. DICK'S, the dominant large-format competitor, has greater national scale and resources and has been gaining share. ASO's southern concentration cuts both ways — localized assortment (regional fishing, licensed team apparel, seasonal items) and value positioning create a defensible niche, but the lack of geographic diversification leaves the company exposed to regional economic swings and severe weather, both of which hurt prior quarters.

ASO's competitive trajectory is mixed. It is adding store count and won the Jordan Brand allocation (a premium-brand differentiator), but it is losing comp traffic, and competitors are described as increasingly promotional. Vendor concentration is modest (largest supplier ~12% of inventory purchases across ~1,500 suppliers). On balance the moat is stable-to-slightly-eroding: the unit-growth offense is real but does not yet offset the same-store defense problem. We score competitive position 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer cyclicality, concentrated in a sensitive cohort.** ASO derives 100% of sales from U.S. discretionary spending, with a customer base skewed toward lower/middle-income households highly dependent on consumer confidence, inflation, fuel prices, and tax-refund timing. This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk, and the comp transaction decline suggests it is already biting.

**Tariff and sourcing exposure.** A large share of merchandise, and most private label, is sourced from China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia, and Brazil. Management pulled inventory forward at pre-tariff rates (the ~$119M cash-flow drag) and is managing prices and sourcing to mitigate. The company sold a portion of its tariff-relief litigation claims for $6.5M rather than wait on a Supreme Court decision, a signal that management is not banking on a tariff reversal as a future margin tailwind.

**2027 maturity wall.** Both the $400M Notes and Term Loan mature November 2027. The May 2026 financing action appears to address this proactively, which would de-risk the cliff, but terms should be confirmed against the actual filing.

**Firearms regulatory/reputational.** ~6% of sales, subject to ATF regulation, litigation, and activist sentiment. The 2025 repeal of the DOJ "zero tolerance" dealer policy is a modest easing.

**Earnings visibility.** Three misses in four quarters and broadly falling estimates indicate deteriorating forward visibility, which raises the probability that the June Q1 print disappoints.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a near-term shock. Near-term ATM IV is 94.5% against 43.1% one-year realized volatility, rich by 51 points and in the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (94.5% near-term versus 51.5% at six months), the signature of a known, imminent binary event: the Q1 FY2026 earnings release. Put/call skew of 15.4% and a put/call OI ratio of 1.31 indicate elevated downside hedging demand, and IV jumped from 59.9% to 94.5% over the past month while the stock barely moved (+3.2%), confirming the catalyst is being priced rather than direction. Combined with short interest at 5.7 days to cover, the positioning is meaningfully bearish into the print. The asymmetry around the event is two-sided: a clean comp inflection and beat could squeeze the elevated short base, while a fourth miss in five quarters confirms the bear thesis and de-rates an already-cheap stock further.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$78).** The Q3 momentum reversal proves durable. Comps turn positive through FY2026, gross margin holds the 34.5-35% range, and SG&A leverage returns as new-store cohorts mature. EPS recovers toward $7.00+ (next-year consensus $6.90 and falling, but with upside if the inflection confirms), the buyback compounds, and the multiple re-rates from 8x toward the low-teens as the market gains confidence the earnings decline has bottomed. A short squeeze on the short base accelerates the move. ~50% upside to the high target.

**Bear Case (~$42).** The consumer backdrop pressures ASO's core customer. Comp transactions continue to erode, the new-store program adds revenue but dilutes returns as SG&A deleverages further, and net income declines a fourth consecutive year. Estimates keep falling, the June Q1 print misses, and the stock retests its 52-week low near $41. ~19% downside.

**Base Case (~$58).** Comps stabilize near flat, margin expansion partially offsets SG&A pressure, and buyback-supported EPS grows modestly. The stock drifts toward the analyst median of $58-60 as the turnaround neither confirms nor collapses. ~12-15% upside over 12 months, with the cheap multiple and net-cash optionality cushioning downside.

The balance of evidence — falling estimates, three of four misses, a soft consumer backdrop bearing on the core customer, and options pricing imminent stress — keeps us at HOLD rather than a more constructive call. The growth-hold thesis rests on the structurally-committed unit-growth and margin machine plus buyback leverage carrying EPS through a fragile comp inflection, with the cheap valuation as a floor. We need the June print to confirm the inflection before raising conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Horizon: 12 months.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY-equivalent conviction):**
- Q1 FY2026 (reported ~June 2026) delivers positive comparable sales with comp transactions inflecting to flat or better, confirming the Q3 reversal is durable.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive over a 90-day window (versus the current -3.5% current-year trend).
- Gross margin sustains ≥34.5% with SG&A deleverage moderating to <50 bps, signaling new-store cohorts are maturing into profitability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fourth earnings miss in five quarters, or a downward guidance revision on the June print.
- Comparable transactions deteriorating beyond -5% YoY, indicating the traffic erosion is accelerating rather than stabilizing.
- Net income declining a fourth consecutive year with no margin offset, or the 2027 refinancing closing on materially worse terms than the existing 6.00% Notes.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q1 FY2026 earnings release (~June 2026) is the immediate binary test, with the elevated options structure pricing an ±8-10% move. The next checkpoint is the FY2026 new-store cadence and the H2 holiday comp trend, both of which will reveal whether consumer softness is materializing in ASO's results.

## Conclusion

Academy Sports is a financially healthy, cheaply-valued retailer caught between a structurally-committed growth program and a soft same-store and macro backdrop. The new-store expansion, durable gross-margin gains, and aggressive buyback can grow EPS over the next 12 months even with soft comps, and at ~8x forward earnings with net-cash optionality the downside is cushioned. But net income has fallen three straight years, consensus estimates are being cut across every period, the company has missed three of its last four quarters, and the options market is pricing imminent stress into a June print that lands while the consumer pressures most relevant to ASO's core customer remain a concern.

We rate this HOLD. The score has moderated slightly from prior reports (6.1 in May to 5.6 now) reflecting the falling estimate revisions and the post-miss reality rather than price action, a genuine if modest fundamental softening that partially offsets the operational improvements. The June Q1 release resolves the thesis in either direction; until then, the risk/reward does not justify adding, and the balance-sheet quality and valuation floor do not justify shorting.
