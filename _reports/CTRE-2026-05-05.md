## Executive Summary

CareTrust REIT is a healthcare-focused triple-net lease REIT that has executed one of the most aggressive and accretive external growth campaigns in the REIT sector over the past two years. Total assets grew from $2.1B (FY2023) to $5.15B (FY2025), driven by $1.8B in investments at an 8.6% blended stabilized yield. Normalized FFO per share grew 17% in 2025 despite 32% dilution from equity issuances, demonstrating that capital is being deployed well above cost.

The balance sheet is extraordinarily underleveraged at 0.7x Net Debt/EBITDA against a 4.0-5.0x target range, implying approximately $1.6B of incremental debt capacity before reaching the low end of that range — with additional capacity available through equity issuance and retained cash flow. Moody's upgraded CTRE to investment grade (Baa3) in April 2026, reducing future borrowing costs. 2026 guidance of $1.90-$1.95 NFFO/share explicitly excludes new investments beyond YTD closings, while the company disclosed $214.8M in post-quarter investments at 8.9% yields with a $500M active pipeline, creating a structurally conservative baseline that should be exceeded.

The primary risk is that the stock has already re-rated significantly (+37% over the past year), and the current ~20x forward NFFO multiple with mixed near-term execution limits the margin of safety. However, the positive spread between deployment yields and cost of capital, combined with significant remaining balance sheet capacity, provides a multi-year compounding runway that peers cannot replicate at current leverage levels.

## Company Overview

CareTrust REIT owns and finances healthcare properties across 32 U.S. states and the United Kingdom. As of December 31, 2025, the portfolio consisted of 407 properties (236 skilled nursing facilities, 171 senior housing communities including 131 UK care homes, and 3 SHOP communities) totaling 37,628 operational beds/units. The company also operates a lending platform with $899M in real estate-secured loans, mezzanine loans, and preferred equity investments at weighted average rates of 8.8-12.1%.

Revenue is primarily derived from triple-net leases ($368M in FY2025 rental income) supplemented by interest income from the lending portfolio ($95.5M). The Ensign Group and related entities (including Pennant Group, which is Ensign-guaranteed) together represent approximately 25% of annualized contractual rental income, the largest single-tenant concentration. Geographic concentration is meaningful in California (25% of revenue), the UK (15%), and Texas (13%).

The company entered the UK market in May 2025 through the $595M acquisition of Care REIT plc and launched a directly-operated senior housing (SHOP) platform in Q4 2025 with three Texas properties.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Growth:**
Total revenue grew 61% YoY to $476.4M in FY2025. Net income reached $320.5M (+156% YoY), though this includes gains on sales. Normalized FFO reached $359.7M, or $1.76 per diluted share, up from $1.50 in FY2024 (+17%). Operating cash flow grew 61% to $394M.

**Balance Sheet Strength:**
Total debt of $900M ($400M in 3.875% senior notes due 2028 and $500M in a term loan at effective 4.63% due 2030) produces net leverage of just 0.7x EBITDA. The $1.2B revolving credit facility is entirely undrawn. There are no debt maturities until 2028. Cash on hand is $198M with an additional $372M in unsettled ATM forward equity contracts. The Baa3 investment-grade rating should unlock unsecured debt markets at more favorable rates.

**Dividend Coverage:**
The quarterly dividend of $0.335/share ($1.34 annualized) represents approximately 76% of NFFO — conservative for a REIT sector where 75-85% is typical. Dividend growth has been substantial: $1.16 (2024) to $1.34 (2025), a 15.5% increase.

**Rent Collection:**
100% rent collection in Q4 2025 and 99.7% for the full year, indicating no tenant distress within the portfolio.

**2026 Guidance:**
NFFO/share of $1.90-$1.95 (midpoint +9.4% vs. 2025). This guidance assumes zero new investments beyond YTD, no new equity/debt issuances, and $42M in loan repayments. Given post-quarter deployment and the active pipeline, the guidance baseline appears conservative.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine operates on a straightforward mechanism: raise capital at implied costs below deployment yields, deploy at 8.6-8.9% stabilized yields, and capture a meaningful positive spread. This arithmetic has produced consistent per-share NFFO accretion despite aggressive dilution. However, the true cost of equity incorporates equity risk premium, issuance timing, and the path to stabilization — meaning the realized spread may be narrower than the headline gap between dividend yield and investment cap rates suggests.

**Runway quantification:** At 0.7x current leverage vs. a 4.0x low-end target, CTRE's net debt of $322.7M against annualized EBITDA of $480.9M implies approximately $1.6B of incremental debt capacity before reaching the low end of its target range, and approximately $2.1B before reaching the high end (5.0x). Combined with equity issuance capacity and retained cash flow, total investment capacity over the next several years could reach $2.5-3.5B through a mix of funding sources. At 8.6% yields on incremental capital, this represents approximately $138-179M in potential annualized income from debt-funded investments alone, with additional accretion from equity-funded deployment.

**Pipeline visibility:** $500M in active pipeline as of the most recent disclosure, following $214.8M in post-quarter (Q4 2025) closings at 8.9% yields. The pace of deployment shows no sign of slowing.

**Secular tailwinds:** SNF supply has declined from 15,600+ facilities (2016) to ~14,742 (2025), while the 65+ population exceeds 59M and grows rapidly. Nursing home/CCRC expenditures are projected to grow from $229B (2024) to $386B (2033). Post-COVID occupancy recovery (76% SNF, 86% senior housing) has further room to improve toward pre-pandemic levels.

**Estimate revisions — a cautionary note:** Near-term estimates have shown some softness based on third-party consensus tracking data. The most recent earnings quarter (Q4 2025) produced a 36.7% beat on NFFO, but execution has been mixed across recent quarters. This moderates confidence in the near-term growth trajectory, though guidance conservatism may account for apparent softness.

- 20.3x 2026E NFFO ($1.925 midpoint)

- ~26.3x 2026E EPS ($1.475 midpoint of $1.45-$1.50 guidance)
- Dividend yield: 3.4% ($1.34 annualized)

**Peer context:** The Health Care REIT peer group shows wide valuation dispersion. Omega Healthcare (OHI) at approximately 22x P/E with 5.7% yield represents the closest SNF-focused comparable. Sabra (SBRA) at 31.8x with 5.9% yield. The broader peer median is distorted by development/transition-stage peers (AHR, DOC) and should be disregarded.

CTRE's premium over OHI is partially justified by its significantly lower leverage (0.7x vs. OHI's ~5x), faster growth rate (17% NFFO/share in 2025 vs. OHI low-single-digits), and larger remaining deployment capacity. The lower dividend yield (3.4% vs. peer median 5.7%) reflects the growth-oriented payout structure.

**Is the market wrong?** The consensus price target of $43.67 (median $43.50) implies ~10% upside, with a low target of $40 (essentially current price) and high of $48 (+22%). The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms — 20x forward NFFO is a growth premium that needs ongoing validation. If CTRE deploys $1.5-2.0B of additional investments at 8.6% yields over 2026-2027 using a mix of debt and equity, 2027 NFFO/share could approach $2.15-2.25, which at 20x forward implies a $43-45 target. The market appears to partially, but not fully, discount this deployment trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

CTRE competes with Sabra (SBRA), Omega (OHI), Welltower (WELL), Ventas (VTR), and private equity for healthcare property acquisitions. Its competitive advantages include:

1. **Operator relationships:** Management's background (CEO Sedgwick and team from Ensign) provides proprietary deal flow with mid-size operators underserved by larger REITs.
2. **Balance sheet capacity:** At 0.7x leverage, CTRE can move quickly without capital constraints that limit competitors operating at 5-6x.
3. **Lending platform as acquisition funnel:** $880M+ in loans at 8.8-12.1% rates serve as relationship-building tools that convert to owned properties via sale-leasebacks.
4. **Investment yields:** Consistently deploying at 8.6-8.9% stabilized yields versus the sub-7% cap rates that larger peers are often forced to accept for core assets.

**Trajectory:** The competitive position is improving. The IG rating, UK diversification, and SHOP platform expansion all represent capability buildouts that didn't exist 18 months ago. The company is moving from a mid-cap SNF specialist to a diversified healthcare REIT — a trajectory that, if executed well, warrants multiple expansion.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration (~25% Ensign-related):** Ensign is publicly traded, financially healthy, and has a strong operating track record, but combined exposure (including Pennant Group, which is Ensign-guaranteed) at approximately 25% of rental income creates meaningful idiosyncratic risk. Any financial deterioration would directly impact CTRE's largest revenue stream.

**California regulatory pressure:** SB 525 minimum wage increases ($21/hr rising to $25/hr by 2028) will compress operator margins in California, which represents 25% of revenue. If operator profitability deteriorates below lease coverage minimums, CTRE would face restructuring risk.

**UK integration and FX:** The Care REIT acquisition introduced 15% revenue exposure to GBP/USD movements and an unfamiliar regulatory regime. The company has conducted asset swaps (10 homes for 6 homes + cash), which may reflect active portfolio optimization. Income tax expense appeared for the first time in 2025 ($5M) due to UK withholding taxes.

**Dilution risk:** Shares outstanding grew from 97M (2022) to 204M (2025) to projected 225M (2026). The growth model requires ongoing equity issuance. If acquisition yields compress or execution stumbles, dilution becomes destructive rather than accretive.

**Interest rate sensitivity:** Healthcare REITs broadly correlate inversely with long-duration rates. However, CTRE's growth profile partially insulates it — investors are buying the FFO growth trajectory, not yield.

**Macro/reimbursement:** Medicaid rate uncertainty persists and represents a material risk to SNF operators. Idaho enacted a 4% Medicaid rate cut. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) imposed a moratorium on enforcing CMS minimum staffing requirements through September 30, 2034, removing a significant near-term cost overhang for operators. However, the same legislation included substantial Medicaid provisions that CBO estimates will reduce federal Medicaid outlays by approximately $990B over FY2025-FY2034 and increase the uninsured population by an estimated 10 million by FY2034. The net effect on SNF operators is uncertain: the staffing moratorium reduces compliance costs, but potential Medicaid funding reductions could pressure reimbursement rates that many SNF operators depend on heavily.

- Acquisition pace slows due to competition for assets or seller price expectations resetting higher
- Broader credit stress raises cost of capital for external growth model

## Conclusion

CareTrust REIT presents a compelling growth-at-a-reasonable-price opportunity in healthcare real estate. The positive spread between investment yields (8.6-8.9%) and blended cost of capital creates consistent per-share accretion, supported by ~$1.6B in incremental debt capacity and proven execution in 2025.

The stock is not cheap at ~20x forward NFFO, and mixed near-term execution warrants monitoring. However, guidance is structurally conservative (excludes future deployments beyond YTD), the dividend at 76% payout is well-covered with room for increases, and the secular tailwinds (aging demographics, declining SNF supply, healthcare spending growth) provide durable demand for the company's assets. The rising rate environment creates headwinds for all REITs, but CTRE's growth profile and minimal existing leverage partially insulate it from pure-yield comparisons that punish slower-growing peers.

At current levels, the risk/reward favors ownership with a 12-month target of $43.50 (+10%) and meaningful upside optionality if deployment activity continues at pace.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to leverage capacity math (from $3-4B to ~$1.6B incremental debt capacity), inability to verify the $628M April 2026 deployment figure from primary sources (SEC filings show $214.8M post-quarter), and the material finding that OBBBA includes ~$990B in Medicaid reductions (previously stated as excluded) collectively weaken the bull case assumptions underpinning the target price.
