## Executive Summary

Columbia Sportswear raised FY2026 EPS guidance from $3.55-4.00 to $4.45-4.90 on July 30, and the stock has fallen 8.7% since. The market read the print correctly. The guidance raise is arithmetically identical to a non-recurring customs refund: operating margin guidance moved up exactly 180bps at both ends of the range, EPS moved up exactly $0.90 at both ends, and the Q2 10-Q discloses a $62M IEEPA tariff refund recognized in earnings ($60M in cost of sales, $2M interest income) worth $0.92 per share in H1. Cross-checking: 180bps on a $3.465B revenue midpoint is $62.4M; $62M taxed at the H1 rate of 27.2% over ~51.9M weighted shares is $0.87. Underlying guidance was not raised. Strip the refund from Q2 itself and operating income was **negative $29.1M** versus a $23.6M loss a year earlier, and EPS was **-$0.41** versus -$0.19, an underlying deterioration corroborated by the adjusted-to-adjusted comparison in third-party reporting.

Beneath the tariff noise the operating problem is specific and has been building for four years. Revenue is essentially flat since FY2022 ($3,464M → $3,397M, -1.9%) while SG&A rose 15.2% ($1,304M → $1,503M). Gross margin *improved* 110bps over that span; operating margin fell 520bps, from 11.3% to 6.1%, and operating income fell 47.3%. The problem is not sourcing or pricing. The U.S. segment, 58.3% of FY2025 revenue and 53.3% of H1 2026, declined 4% in FY2025 and 8% in H1 2026, with U.S. segment SG&A rising to 30.9% of sales from 27.9% while U.S. gross margin improved. Management's own filing language now cites "brand perception" alongside casualization and competition from athletic, athleisure and emerging outdoor brands. International execution (EMEA H1 +21% reported/+14% constant currency, LAAP +8%) is genuinely good, but EMEA carries a 47.3% segment gross margin against 49.7% in the U.S. ex-refund, so the mix shift is dilutive to consolidated margin even as it flatters the growth line.

What holds the stock up is a real balance sheet: $532.3M cash plus $92.3M short-term investments, zero funded debt, an undrawn $500M revolver to 2031, a 2.10% dividend, and $276.5M of remaining buyback authorization equal to 9.4% of market cap. Net cash of $12.20/share is 21.2% of the price. At $57.42 the stock is 15.2x underlying FY2026 EPS of $3.775 (midpoint), 13.7x next-year consensus of $4.19, and 9.4x EV/EBIT on ~$246M of normalized operating income. That is not expensive. But the FY guide now requires implied Q4 revenue of $1,094-1,178M (+2.1% to +10.0%) and implied Q4 operating income of $143.5-163.0M, a 13.1-13.8% margin against a derived 10.9% in Q4 2025, in the same filing that warns Fall 2026 inventory will arrive late and that "a node within our supply chain has experienced congestion." Expected return on probability-weighted scenarios is roughly 5% including the dividend, against 20%+ bear-case downside. Trim.

## Company Overview

Columbia designs and distributes outdoor, active and lifestyle apparel, footwear, accessories and equipment through four brands: Columbia (1938), SOREL (2000), Mountain Hardwear (2003) and prAna (2014). FY2025 revenue of $3,397.4M breaks down as Columbia $2,972.6M (87.5% of total, +2%), SOREL $221.7M (-7%), prAna $102.8M (-1%) and Mountain Hardwear $100.2M (-8%). The three acquired brands together are 12.5% of revenue and declined 5.8% in aggregate.

Reporting is geographic: U.S. $1,979.0M (-4%), LAAP $611.1M (+9%), EMEA $576.9M (+13%), Canada $230.2M (+1%). Channel split is wholesale $1,780.6M (+3%) and DTC $1,616.8M (-1%), with roughly 565 stores globally. Manufacturing is fully outsourced, with no owned factories. Apparel comes from 16 countries (Vietnam ~35%, Bangladesh ~30%, Indonesia ~10%, India ~10%) with the top five vendors at ~30% of production. Footwear is far more concentrated: 4 countries, Vietnam ~80%, China ~10%, top five vendors ~80% and the largest at ~25%.

No customer exceeded 10% of consolidated net sales in FY2025 or H1 2026. Regional wholesale concentration is moderate: top five U.S. customers ~20% of U.S. sales; Canada is the exception at two customers ≈30% of that segment. Three related Boyle family shareholders control greater than 50% of a single class of common stock; Timothy Boyle (76) is Chairman and CEO with 9,875,315 direct shares (~19.3% of outstanding, ~$564M at the current price).

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory ($000):**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 3,464,152 | 3,487,203 | 3,368,582 | 3,397,351 |
| Gross margin | 49.4% | 49.6% | 50.2% | 50.5% |
| SG&A % of sales | 37.7% | 40.6% | 42.9% | 44.2% |
| Operating income | 393,104 | 310,284 | 270,741 | 207,039 |
| Operating margin | 11.3% | 8.9% | 8.0% | **6.1%** |
| Net income | ~311,400 | ~251,400 | 223,273 | 177,224 |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $3.82 | $3.24 |
| Diluted shares | 62,970 | 61,424 | 58,502 | 54,762 |

The mechanism is unambiguous. Cost of goods has been managed well, with four consecutive years of gross-margin expansion on flat volume, including through the 2025 tariff absorption. Operating expense has not. FY2025 SG&A rose $58.6M (+4%): omni-channel +$36.9M (new stores, store impairments), demand creation +$24.1M (the "Engineered for Whatever" campaign), plus Profit Improvement Program costs, partly offset by PIP savings. Management describes cumulative annualized PIP savings above $150M across 2024-2025 actions, exceeding the original $125-150M target, yet SG&A dollars rose and the ratio hit a four-year high. The savings were redeployed into marketing and DTC rather than dropped to the operating line. That is a defensible strategic choice, but it means the PIP is not currently a margin story, and after two years of redeployment the U.S. business it was aimed at is declining faster, not slower.

**Segment profitability.** FY2025 U.S. segment operating income fell $59.6M (-16.7%) to $297.1M, margin down to 15.0% from 17.2%. EMEA (+17.3% to $121.4M) and LAAP (+14.2% to $87.9M) grew; Canada fell 7.0%. Unallocated corporate expenses rose $29.6M, primarily $29.0M of impairments. Corporate unallocated of $343.8M against total segment operating income of $550.9M is a heavy overhead layer that has not scaled down with revenue.

**Impairments.** Goodwill has gone from $51.7M (12/31/2022) to $5.7M (12/31/2025, unchanged at 6/30/2026). FY2025 charges of $29.0M wrote off the entire Mountain Hardwear goodwill balance ($12.2M), $8.8M of prAna goodwill and $8.0M of the prAna trade name, with triggering events identified in Q3 2025. A further $10.1M of PP&E impairment hit underperforming retail stores, with additional store impairments in Q1 and Q2 2026. Remaining indefinite-lived intangibles are $71.2M, including a prAna trade name of roughly $43.8M and a $15.0M Mountain Hardwear trade name whose disclosed fair-value cushion compresses to 31% under a -50bps royalty-rate sensitivity. Further write-downs would be immaterial to intrinsic value at a $2.30B enterprise value, but the pattern matters: the 2000/2003/2014 acquisitions have not created durable value, which undercuts any thesis that management can diversify away from Columbia-brand U.S. weakness via M&A, still a listed element of the capital-allocation policy.

**Cash flow quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $282.9M was down $208.1M from FY2024's $491.0M, and the 10-K attributes $155.6M of the decline to accounts payable, "driven primarily by a reduction in the use of early payments and extension of payment terms with certain vendors enacted at the end of 2024." FY2024 was flattered by a working-capital pull-forward; FY2025 gave much of it back. Neither year is a clean run-rate. A two-year average of ~$387M OCF less ~$66M capex implies roughly $321M of normalized FCF, which I treat as a sensitivity rather than a forecast; it adjusts for neither tariff timing nor the 2026 refund. FY2025 reported FCF was $216.7M (6.4% margin), a 7.4% yield on the current $2.94B market cap.

One monitoring item: supply-chain-financing payables rose to $121.0M at 6/30/2026 from $71.9M at year-end, or 26% of the $473.2M total AP. Columbia is not a party to the supplier-bank agreements, has pledged no assets and provides no guarantees, and pays on original invoice terms regardless of participation, so this does not extend Columbia's days payable. The most likely explanation is higher supplier participation as inventory purchases ramped ($185.3M inventory build over the same six months). I draw no inference about intent, but rising SCF usage is a lever that can support reported AP, and the balance is worth tracking.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: $532.3M cash plus $92.3M short-term investments, zero drawn debt on either the domestic revolver or international lines. Total liabilities $1,196.3M against $1,601.6M of equity (0.75x), of which $461.2M is operating lease liabilities. Current ratio 2.49x. The March 2026 replacement facility is $500M unsecured to March 2031 at SOFR +1.00-1.50%, with a 3.75:1.00 funded-debt covenant and no drawings. Management frames it explicitly as bridge liquidity until Q4 receivable collections. International standby capacity was *reduced* over the same period (European overdraft €5.0M → €3.0M; uncommitted lines ~$101.2M → ~$78.1M), consistent with a company de-emphasizing borrowing rather than preparing to lever.

The screening feed reports both trailing and forward P/E at 14.97 against EPS of $3.24, which cannot both be true at $57.42 (that price on $3.24 is 17.7x). I use filed statements and guidance arithmetic throughout.

**Capital allocation.** Policy is to return at least 40% of FCF. Since 2004 the board has authorized $2.6B; 43.5M shares have been repurchased for $2,323.5M with $276.5M remaining. Diluted shares fell 18.6% from 62,970K (FY2022) to 51,264K (Q2 2026), the single largest reason per-share results have held up against a 47% decline in operating income. Execution improved: H1 2026 repurchases of $150.0M/2,499K shares average $60.02, versus $78.48 in H1 2025. But repurchases were **zero in Q2 2026**, in the same quarter the company received ~$78M of tariff refunds and while the stock traded below Q1's average purchase price. Seasonal liquidity management is the most likely explanation (the 10-Q is explicit that cash troughs before holiday, inventory consumed $191.6M in H1, and short-term investments fell $256.5M), but caution about the H2 outlook is equally consistent with the disclosed facts. The reason is unverified.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated growth is approximately nil and the composition is deteriorating. FY2025 revenue +1%; FY2026 guided +1-3% with the FX contribution *cut* from 50-100bps to 30-50bps; Q3 2026 guided to $929-943M, flat to -1.5% year over year and reported as roughly 3.6% below consensus.

The U.S. is the problem and it is self-diagnosed. The Q2 10-Q states twice that "the underlying business trends within our U.S. business remain under pressure," and attributes it to brand perception, consumer casualization and style shift, and competition from "athletic, athleisure, emerging outdoor, and other brands." Weak Spring 2025 sell-through drove lower Spring 2026 wholesale orders; the order book transmits brand weakness forward two to three quarters, which is why Q3 is guided down.

International growth is real and forward-corroborated. EMEA H1 +21% reported/+14% cc with growth in both Europe-direct wholesale and DTC brick-and-mortar including same-store productivity, plus growing Spring and Fall 2026 distributor orders. LAAP H1 +8% with China wholesale and e-commerce, Korea wholesale and distributors advancing. Q2 brand detail showed prAna +14% and Mountain Hardwear +6%, the first positive prints from the emerging portfolio in some time, against SOREL -14%.

Two qualifications temper this. First, international rose from ~39% of FY2024 revenue to 41.7% in FY2025 to 46.7% in H1 2026 substantially because U.S. revenue is shrinking, not solely because international is growing; a changing geographic mix is not the same as improved diversification, and the underlying customer-concentration disclosures are unchanged. Second, EMEA runs a 47.3% H1 gross margin against a U.S. H1 2025 comparable of 49.7%, with EMEA SG&A up 26% on 21% sales growth. Growing EMEA faster than the U.S. structurally dilutes consolidated gross margin, which is a reason revenue recovery alone would not restore FY2022's 11.3% operating margin.

**Estimate revisions require care.** Current-year consensus rose 21.5% over 90 days to $4.67, the mechanical recognition of the tariff refund rather than an operating inflection, and it does not qualify as the kind of forward-earnings acceleration that supports re-rating. Next-year consensus is $4.19, essentially unchanged (-0.8% over 30 days, +0.1% over 90 days). Current-quarter estimates were cut 17.5% to $1.28, inside the $1.15-1.35 guide. The honest read is flat forward earnings power around $4.00-4.20 against an underlying FY2026 base of $3.55-4.00, with the delta coming mostly from share-count reduction rather than operating improvement.

**Earnings surprise record** is mixed and not a source of confidence in the guide: 2 beats and 2 misses in four quarters, with the two "beats" being the same $0.29 figure duplicated across periods, a -20.4% miss in Q3 2025 and a -3.1% miss in Q2 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

**The multiple depends entirely on which earnings base you use.**

| Basis | EPS | P/E at $57.42 |
|---|---|---|
| Reported FY2026 guidance midpoint | $4.675 | 12.3x |
| **Underlying FY2026 (ex-refund) midpoint** | **$3.775** | **15.2x** |
| Next-year consensus | $4.19 | 13.7x |
| FY2025 actual | $3.24 | 17.7x |

The 12.3x figure is not a repeatable base. The refund is cash-real and permanently improves the balance sheet, and the February 2026 cessation of IEEPA collection is genuine ongoing cost relief distinct from the refund, so the true normalized base is plausibly modestly above FY2025's 6.1% margin. The 15.2x on underlying guidance is the defensible anchor.

On enterprise value: $2.94B market cap less $624.6M net cash gives $2.315B EV. Underlying FY2026 operating income at the 6.7-7.5% guided range on $3.465B is $232-260M, so **9.4x EV/EBIT** at the midpoint. FY2025 FCF of $216.7M is a 7.4% yield on market cap and 9.4% on EV; the two-year normalized $321M sensitivity would be 10.9% and 13.9% respectively.

**Peer context.** Median P/E across the apparel comparable set is 12.0x (CPRI 12.0, GIII 11.3, CRI 7.3, PVH 23.8, WRBY 431 — the dispersion makes the median weakly informative). Columbia at 15.2x underlying carries a premium to the distressed median, which the balance sheet justifies: on an ex-cash basis, underlying net income of roughly $193M against a $2.315B EV is 12.0x, in line with the peer median while carrying zero funded debt against peers with real leverage. The proxy's peer-group indexed TSR ended FY2025 at 66.56 against Columbia's 67.90, both down roughly a third over five years. Columbia's U.S. underperformance is company-specific; the multiple compression is sector-wide. That distinction matters because it means there is no obvious re-rating catalyst sitting in relative valuation alone.

**Support and constraint.** Net cash is $12.20/share, 21.2% of the price. The dividend is $1.20 annualized (2.10%), covered many times over by FCF. Remaining buyback authorization is 9.4% of market cap and has been executed at improving average prices. Those elements limit downside but do not create upside on their own; the balance sheet has been strong throughout a four-year 47% decline in operating income and did not prevent a 32.1% five-year total return.

Analyst targets span $47 to $92 with a $69.83 mean and $67.50 median. The recommendation distribution is 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 4 hold, 1 sell. On a stock with flat-to-declining underlying earnings and no positive next-year revision momentum, I do not treat the $69.83 mean as a floor; it reflects the same "cheap balance sheet, eventual turnaround" framing that has been wrong for four years. The $47 low target sits close to the $47.47 fifty-two-week low and is the more instructive end of the range.

Base case fair value: applying 15x to a normalized $4.00 gives $60; a 10x EV/EBIT on $250M plus $625M net cash gives $61. **Base $60, roughly 4.5% above the current price.** Valuation scores a 5.0: cheap on absolute metrics, but cheapness here has been persistent and has not converted, and the identified catalyst (the tariff refund) has already been recognized and is now reversing out of the comparison base.

## Competitive Landscape

The filings describe a squeeze from two directions. From the premium end, technical-outdoor brands have taken share; from everyday wear, athletic and athleisure brands have. The 10-Q's language is direct: consumers "have increasingly shifted their preferences to also incorporate versatility and style for everyday wear. Athletic, athleisure, emerging outdoor, and other brands have capitalized on this casualization and style trend in the historical outdoor space." Columbia's historical positioning — technical performance at accessible price points — is the least defensible slot in that structure. The company does not name competitors; the description maps to the documented dynamic of Arc'teryx, On and Salomon taking premium share while athletic brands take everyday share.

A second, structural threat is disclosed in the risk factors: wholesale customers "often pose a significant competitive threat by designing, marketing and distributing apparel, footwear, accessories, and equipment under their own private labels," and compete directly with Columbia's DTC business digitally. With wholesale at 52% of revenue, that is a channel where the customer is also the competitor.

What is verifiably working: EMEA and LAAP execution, with forward order-book growth in both regions for Fall 2026, which is genuine forward-looking corroboration rather than a reported-results artifact, and the ACCELERATE program, which management describes as "starting to show tangible signs of traction… That said, we know it will take more time and work to bring the newness, innovation and elevated style to our product portfolio, marketing and distribution at the level we need." That is measured language two quarters into a turnaround where the domestic business is still declining 8%.

The trajectory question, which is what matters for scoring, resolves negative: the largest segment is losing share by management's own account, the acquired-brand portfolio has shrunk and been impaired, and the winning geographies carry structurally lower gross margins. Competitive position scores 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff regime is unresolved, not resolved.** The sequence: ~$50M of incremental IEEPA tariffs paid in 2025; suit filed at the CIT in December 2025; the Supreme Court held on February 20, 2026 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorize peacetime-emergency tariffs; collection ended the same day and Section 122 tariffs took effect February 24, 2026 for 150 days; the CIT directed refunds on March 4; ~$78M received by 6/30/2026. Section 122 is statutorily time-limited, and the Q2 10-Q filed August 6 does **not** address whether it expired, was extended or was replaced. The filing warns of "additional alternative measures pursued by the U.S. government to preserve revenues from foreign imports. The timing, scope and form of such measures are unknown." Second-order exposure: for combined Spring and Fall 2026 inventory, contract manufacturers sourced ~27% of U.S.-market footwear raw materials and ~21% of apparel raw materials from China, and the 10-K notes that origin-of-raw-material tariffs "might increase meaningfully" Columbia's rate. The company has already taken tariff-driven U.S. price increases for two seasons and seen order cancellations.

**2. The Q4 bar against acknowledged supply congestion.** Working from FY guidance less H1 actuals less Q3 guidance: implied Q4 revenue $1,094-1,178M (+2.1% to +10.0% versus a derived $1,070.7M) and implied Q4 operating income $143.5-163.0M, a 13.1-13.8% margin against a derived 10.9% in Q4 2025. The same 10-Q warns that "supply chain disruptions are expected to result in later receipt of Fall 2026 inventory and potentially limit our ability to fulfill wholesale orders and DTC demand" and that "a node within our supply chain has experienced congestion." Mitigants: Q4 is structurally the largest and highest-margin quarter, the Q4 2025 base was depressed by tariff absorption and the wind-down of temporary clearance stores (28 to 8), and late receipts could shift revenue from Q3 into Q4 rather than losing it. The $15M of refund routed to inventory is expected to be "largely offset by anticipated accommodations to certain third-party factory partners," so it is not a clean H2 tailwind.

**3. Newly added risk factors in the Q2 10-Q.** Middle East conflict "which broke out in late February 2026," specifically driving "order cancellations and reductions of forecasted orders for our Middle East distributor markets"; the Fall 2026 congestion item above; a France distribution center transition to a third-party operator currently underway; and a standalone AI risk factor including the observation that AI-powered bots "may distort traffic, consumer engagement metrics, and other data on our e-commerce sites which is used to evaluate consumer demand, measure performance, and make operational decisions."

**4. Estate-tax share overhang.** The 10-Q discloses that the three Boyle holdings "could be significantly diminished… to satisfy any applicable estate or unrealized gains tax obligations of the holders," with a companion risk factor warning that sale or the prospect of sale "may have an adverse effect on the market price." Timothy Boyle is 76 and holds 9,875,315 shares directly. Succession moved in November 2025 (Joseph Boyle, 45, to President, Columbia Brand; Peter Bragdon to President of the Company). There is no evidence of any planned sale, no new 10b5-1 plans were entered in Q2 2026, and the August 7 transfer of 1,756 shares was a gift. This is a genuine, company-disclosed technical overhang that standard governance screens do not surface.

**5. Fixed-cost DTC.** Roughly 565 stores, $461.2M of operating lease liabilities and $563.0M of total commitments. Costs are largely fixed and exits "may not be possible without significant cost or loss, including impairment losses"; store impairments have now been recorded in FY2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026.

**6. Footwear vendor concentration.** Vietnam ~80% of footwear production, top five vendors ~80%, largest single vendor ~25%. A Vietnam-specific disruption or tariff action hits footwear ($685M of revenue) disproportionately.

**7. Short interest.** 13.6% of float short with 6.1 days to cover. This is meaningful two-sided risk: it corroborates the bearish fundamental read but creates squeeze risk on any Q4 beat, and it is a reason to trim rather than short.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any insider through August 7, 2026, while the CFO exercised and sold 4,000 shares at $65.50-68.00 in late May and three directors sold at $61.24-67.95. Dollar amounts are small, several were exercise-and-liquidate transactions, and the largest holder has not sold. Under normal circumstances I would treat this as low-signal noise. Here it is a mild negative for a different reason: it co-occurs with *flat* next-year estimate revisions and a deteriorating underlying earnings base, not with rising estimates and record results. The absence of any buying after a 17% drawdown by a management team that has publicly framed the stock as undervalued through aggressive buybacks is a coherent, if modest, data point.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reading, near-term ATM IV of 62.7% against 36.8% one-year realized with backwardation, should be discounted. That 62.7% sits on a three-day expiry across a chain of only 40 contracts total, and the reported put/call open-interest ratio of 0.00 indicates missing data rather than positioning. The informative tenors are the 1-month at 34.3% and 3-month at 29.6%, both **at or below** realized volatility, which does not corroborate an imminent-stress read. The 6-month tenor at 35.3% with a 19.1% implied move spans the late-October Q3 print and the January Q4/FY report, appropriately pricing the two events that matter. Put/call skew is 4.4% at one month (elevated put demand) but *negative* 5.2% at three months and -1.1% at six months, showing no consistent directional signal. The reported jump in ATM IV from 51.7% to 74.0% alongside a 9.3% price decline is drawn from the same thin chain and carries low confidence. The options market is not pricing a dislocation, and there is no volatility-based case for either accelerating or deferring a trim.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $78 (+36%)

ACCELERATE works. U.S. revenue stabilizes in FY2027 as the Spring 2027 order book reflects improved Fall 2026 sell-through, and the demand-creation spending that destroyed 520bps of margin starts producing revenue rather than just expense. International compounds at low double digits, taking the international share past 50%, with EMEA and LAAP order books already growing for Fall 2026. Section 122 lapses without an equivalently costly replacement, permanently removing roughly $50M of annualized tariff cost and validating the 52%+ gross margin. Operating margin recovers to 9-10% on $3.6B of revenue ($325-360M of EBIT), EPS reaches ~$5.00, and the buyback, with $276.5M authorized and no debt, takes the share count below 48M. At 16x, $80; a 10-11x EV/EBIT plus net cash gives $76-82. Analyst high target $92 embeds a faster U.S. recovery than I can underwrite.

### Bear Case — $45 (-22%)

The U.S. decline continues at 5-8% and international growth cannot offset it on a consolidated margin basis, because EMEA gross margin is 250bps below U.S. and EMEA SG&A is growing faster than EMEA sales. Fall 2026 inventory arrives late, Q4 revenue lands at the low end of the $1,094-1,178M implied range, and the required 220-290bps of Q4 margin expansion does not materialize, a miss of $0.40-0.60 against the reported guide, which would land FY2026 underlying EPS near $3.30-3.50. A Section 122 replacement regime reinstates tariff cost, potentially widened by origin-of-raw-material rules against 21-27% Chinese raw-material content. Mountain Hardwear's trade name takes a further write-down. Underlying EPS settles near $2.80-3.00 and the multiple compresses to the peer median of 12x, giving $34-36 before the balance sheet is credited; adding $12.20 of net cash and a covered 2.1% dividend puts a realistic floor around $44-46, near the $47.47 fifty-two-week low and the $47 analyst low target.

### Base Case — $60 (+4.5%)

Revenue lands within the guided 1-3%, Q4 delivers roughly the midpoint of the implied revenue range but only 150-200bps of the required 220-290bps margin expansion, and reported FY2026 EPS comes in at $4.30-4.60 against the $4.45-4.90 guide. Underlying earnings power is $3.90-4.10, and the share count declines 3-4% as buybacks resume in Q4. Fifteen times $4.00 is $60; 10x EV/EBIT on $250M plus $625M net cash is $61. Total return including the dividend is roughly 6-7%.

**Probability-weighted:** 20% bull (+35.8%), 50% base (+4.5%), 30% bear (-21.6%) gives +2.9% price return, or roughly 5% total with the dividend. Against an equity cost of capital in the 8-9% range and with downside four to five times the base-case upside, that is a less-than-fair expected risk-adjusted return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now rather than around a catalyst. The Q3 print in late October carries a flat-to-down revenue guide and will not resolve the Q4 question, which does not clear until the January FY report. Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized for the balance sheet and dividend rather than for the turnaround, and should not add on weakness toward $52-55 until at least one of the upgrade conditions below is met. There is no case for shorting: 13.6% short interest with 6.1 days to cover, $12.20/share of net cash, a 9.4%-of-cap buyback authorization and a 2.10% covered dividend create meaningful squeeze risk against a fundamentally correct bear view.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE):**
1. **U.S. segment revenue decline narrows to better than -3% year over year** for two consecutive quarters, with management confirming Spring 2027 wholesale orders up year over year. This is the single variable that matters; 58% of the business cannot shrink 8% and be offset.
2. **Q4 2026 operating margin of 13.0% or better on revenue of at least $1,120M**, demonstrating the guide was achievable and that the supply-chain congestion was a timing item rather than a demand item.
3. **Resumption of buybacks at $200M+ annualized pace below $60/share**, or open-market purchases by the CFO or a Boyle family member. Either would resolve the Q2 pause ambiguity in the favorable direction.
4. Price toward **$48-50** with underlying FY2027 EPS still tracking $4.00+, which would put the stock at 12x underlying with 21%+ of the price in net cash and restore genuine asymmetry.

**Avoid triggers (full exit):**
1. **A replacement tariff regime that includes origin-of-raw-material rules**, given 21-27% Chinese raw-material content; this would reinstate cost that Columbia has already twice attempted to pass through, with order cancellations resulting.
2. **FY2027 guidance implying underlying operating margin below 6.5%**, which would confirm that the SG&A base is structurally too high for a $3.4B revenue business and that the PIP redeployment has failed.
3. **Any open-market sale of Boyle family shares in size**, which would convert the disclosed estate-tax overhang from a hypothetical into a supply event against a $2.9B market cap and 646K average daily volume.
4. A further Mountain Hardwear or prAna trade-name impairment accompanied by continued emerging-brand revenue decline, confirming the acquired portfolio is a permanent drag rather than a cyclical one.

**Reassessment timeline.** Interim check at the Q3 print in late October 2026 for the U.S. trajectory and any tariff-regime disclosure. Full reassessment at the FY2026 report in late January or February 2027, when the Q4 margin question resolves and FY2027 guidance establishes whether the underlying earnings base is $3.50 or $4.50.

## Conclusion

Columbia is a well-financed company with a deteriorating core franchise, and the July 30 print made both halves of that clearer. The headline guidance raise contained no operating improvement; to the basis point and to the penny, it was a customs refund. Underneath it, Q2 operating income ex-refund was negative $29.1M versus a $23.6M loss a year earlier, the U.S. segment declined 8% in H1, and the FY guide now leans on a Q4 requiring both revenue acceleration and 220-290bps of margin expansion into a fulfillment risk the company itself disclosed.

The offsetting facts are real and are why this is a REDUCE rather than an AVOID: $624.6M of cash and investments against zero funded debt, $12.20/share of net cash equal to 21.2% of the price, an undrawn $500M facility to 2031, a 2.10% dividend covered several times over, 18.6% of shares retired in under four years at improving average prices, and $276.5M of remaining authorization. The stock is not expensive at 15.2x underlying FY2026 EPS and 9.4x EV/EBIT.

But cheapness has been the case for four years while operating income fell 47% and total return was -32%, and the specific catalyst that produced this year's optical improvement has now been recognized and will reverse out of the FY2027 comparison base. Base case $60 offers roughly 4.5% upside; the bear case at $45 is 22% below. With no positive next-year estimate revisions, no insider buying after a 17% drawdown, an unresolved tariff regime, and a Q4 that must inflect against management's own supply-chain warning, the asymmetry does not support holding a full position. Trim to a residual, monitor the U.S. revenue line, and require evidence of domestic stabilization before re-engaging.
