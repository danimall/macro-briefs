**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The August 1, 2025 merger with Windstream transformed Uniti from a passive triple-net-lease fiber REIT — whose chief asset was rent from a single financially fragile tenant — into a vertically integrated operating telecom that no longer pays a common dividend. Standard screening metrics are distorted by reverse-merger accounting (Old Uniti is the accounting acquirer), so trailing P/E of 2.86x, GAAP YoY comparisons, and the 0.29 Altman Z-Score are all misleading. The company must be analyzed through pro forma combined results and a leverage-versus-EBITDA, fiber-build-execution lens.

The investment case is two-sided, and the market has largely sorted it out. The stock has rallied ~74% over the trailing twelve months, from roughly $7 in January 2026 to $11.69, and it continued higher through a 90-day window in which GAAP-EPS estimates collapsed (current-year consensus moved from $(0.50) to $(1.83)). For a leveraged infra equity stub, falling GAAP EPS — driven by interest expense and merger amortization, not operating deterioration — is a weak bear signal. The market re-rated the equity up after digesting both the falling accounting EPS and the Q1 2026 beat (the first clean combined quarter, +29.6% surprise). EV/EBITDA, the FCF trajectory on the fiber build, and the cost of capital are the metrics that matter, and on those the picture is balanced rather than bearish.

We rate UNIT a HOLD. The bull elements are real: a hard-to-replicate ~240,000-route-mile fiber footprint, fiber subscriber revenue up 26%, hyperscaler IRU demand, the 2.0M-fiber-premises milestone, and a securitization strategy that is demonstrably lowering the cost of the build (~$2B of Kinetic ABS priced in H1 2026 at 5.2–7.7% versus 8.6%+ unsecured). The bear elements are equally real: ~$10.7B of debt against ~$1.77B EBITDA, pro forma service revenue down 8% as the legacy base melts faster than fiber grows, an RDOF buildout miss that creates clawback risk, and Elliott-controlled governance that limits minority upside. At ~7.6x EV/EBITDA, in line with leveraged peers, and with consensus targets bracketing the current price, the equity is fairly valued for its risk.

## Company Overview

Uniti operates ~240,000 fiber route miles across 47 states, serves more than 1.0M customers including 564,000 residential fiber subscribers, and passes ~1.9–2.0M fiber-equipped households concentrated in the Midwest and Southeast. The post-merger entity reports three segments:

- **Kinetic** (~$537–548M Q1 revenue, the largest): an incumbent local exchange carrier providing residential, business, and wholesale broadband, including the fiber-to-the-home build-out and a declining legacy DSL base (409K DSL subscribers versus 564K fiber subscribers).
- **Uniti Solutions** (~$190M Q1): CLEC managed services (SASE, UCaaS, SD-WAN) for mid-market and enterprise customers, provided largely over leased facilities, with legacy TDM revenue in deliberate wind-down.
- **Fiber Infrastructure** (~$260–295M Q1): legacy Uniti fiber/leasing plus Windstream wholesale, selling wholesale transport, dark fiber, and waves to carriers, content providers, and hyperscalers. This is the growth and AI-demand-leverage segment.

The competitive advantage is an extensive, capital-intensive-to-replicate fiber footprint and an incumbent rural ILEC position with regulatory subsidy support (RDOF, state USF, BEAD). Vertical integration eliminated the prior structure in which Uniti collected rent from a single distressed tenant.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (GAAP).** Total revenue was $987.5M (versus $293.9M legacy-only in the prior-year period; Windstream contributes ~82% of revenue). Operating income was $110.9M; the net loss was $(70.3)M, or $(0.34) per share after $15.5M of preferred dividends. Adjusted EBITDA was $441.6M, a ~$1.77B annualized run-rate. Operating cash flow was a positive $260.9M. Capex was heavy at $349.2M against the aggressive fiber build, leaving free cash flow roughly breakeven-to-negative, appropriate for a company in a deliberate land-grab build phase.

**Pro forma trends (Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025 combined)** reveal the real operating story and are the right lens. Service revenue was down 8% pro forma as legacy declines outpaced fiber growth. Kinetic consumer revenue fell 4% (DSL down 23%, partially offset by fiber subscriber revenue up 26%). Uniti Solutions revenue fell 21% on deliberate TDM run-off. Fiber Infrastructure service revenue rose ~8–11%, augmented by a one-time $71.4M sales-type lease (IRU) item. Operating income was down 34% pro forma, distorted by ~$30M of transaction costs and the absence of a prior-year $25.8M IPv4 gain.

**The balance sheet is the central issue.** Long-term debt was ~$10.64B net at 3/31/26 ($10.68B principal), up from $9.53B at year-end 2025. Reported equity is only $319.7M, the thin merger-accounting equity that produces the misleading 0.29 Altman Z-Score and D/E above 30x. Net leverage is ~6x EBITDA, high but typical of a fiber/telecom infra company rather than the distress the Z-Score implies. Cash stood at $982.6M with revolver availability of ~$862M, and there is no near-term maturity wall: first major maturities are 2027 ($316.5M) and 2028 ($580M), with the bulk ($7.36B) due 2031–2033. Interest expense runs ~$188.3M per quarter (~$753M annualized) at high weighted rates (8.25%, 8.625%, 9.018% on the ABS Class C).

The correct framing for valuation is EV/EBITDA, not GAAP EPS. Market cap of ~$2.87B plus net debt of ~$10.64B gives an enterprise value of ~$13.5B against ~$1.77B EBITDA, roughly 7.6x. The negative GAAP EPS and the collapsing GAAP-EPS estimate revisions reflect interest expense and merger amortization on a deleveraging-via-EBITDA story; they are not evidence of operating deterioration and carry little weight for the equity value.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is real but narrow. Fiber subscriber revenue is up 26% with 564K subscribers at 29% penetration on fiber-passed homes, and management targets 450,000–500,000 new fiber homes passed in 2026 against the 2.0M-premises milestone reached in June 2026. Penetration economics on already-passed homes — the incremental subscriber on built plant — drive the return profile, and 29% with room to climb is constructive.

The Fiber Infrastructure segment carries the strongest secular tailwind: hyperscaler and data-center connectivity demand for AI workloads. Uniti is a vendor/infrastructure provider selling and leasing fiber capacity to hyperscalers, not an AI company, but its national footprint is a scarce asset benefiting from the same demand dynamic lifting other crown-jewel fiber assets. The Q1 $68.7M one-time IRU dark-fiber sale to "one large hyperscaler" is evidence of this demand, though its non-recurring nature flattered the quarter. Management frames a "twin engines" strategy of Kinetic FTTH plus hyperscaler-driven Fiber Infrastructure builds and cited a ~$1B nonrecurring cash revenue opportunity by 2028.

The constraint is that consolidated revenue is still in net decline (-8% pro forma service revenue). The bull case requires fiber and hyperscaler growth to outrun the legacy copper/TDM melt, and on a total-revenue basis that crossover has not yet arrived. Full-year 2026 guidance was maintained at the Q1 call.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of 2.86x is meaningless — it reflects merger-accounting EPS that includes one-time items and does not represent the combined entity's earnings power. Negative forward GAAP EPS makes a P/E framework inapplicable in either direction.

On the relevant metric, EV/EBITDA of ~7.6x sits in line with leveraged fiber/telecom infrastructure peers. The peer set in the data is heterogeneous (IRDM at 50.4x P/E is a satellite operator; CCOI and LUMN both show negative P/E as fellow leveraged fiber names), which limits direct P/E comparison and reinforces that EV/EBITDA is the right anchor. At ~7.6x, UNIT is neither obviously cheap nor obviously expensive for the asset quality and leverage.

The analyst data: consensus mean target is $10.25 and median is $11.00, essentially at the current $11.69. Sentiment is described as stable, and the single sell rating was removed over the past two months (a directional upgrade), even as one rating moved to strong-sell — a split book consistent with a two-sided situation. When consensus brackets the price and sentiment is stable, neither a buy nor an AVOID valuation case exists. We do not treat the prior run-up as evidence of overvaluation, but we also see no specific mispricing to exploit at $11.69. The valuation is fair, which supports HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

Uniti competes in two distinct arenas. In consumer broadband (Kinetic), it is an incumbent rural fiber overbuilder racing cable (faster DOCSIS), fixed wireless (T-Mobile, Verizon), and satellite (Starlink), while harvesting RDOF and BEAD subsidies. This is a capital-intensive land grab in which penetration economics determine returns, and Uniti carries carrier-of-last-resort obligations competitors do not. In wholesale/infrastructure, its national fiber footprint benefits from AI/hyperscaler connectivity demand — the same dynamic supporting Lumen, Zayo, and other fiber assets.

The footprint itself is a durable moat: 240,000 route miles are not quickly replicated, and the incumbent ILEC position carries subsidy support. The trajectory is mixed — gaining share in fiber-passed homes while losing the legacy DSL/TDM base — so we score competitive position modestly above neutral, reflecting a scarce asset with an improving fiber franchise offset by structural decline in the legacy book.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing dependency (paramount, but partially mitigated).** ~$750M annual interest leaves little margin for execution error. The original concern that "the model only works if capital markets stay open" is materially rebutted by recent events: the inaugural $960.1M Kinetic FTTH securitization and a second ~$1.1B Kinetic ABS priced June 2026. Capital markets are open to UNIT's best collateral at improving rates. The offset is that securitization subordinates the corporate creditor and equity claim on the best assets, ring-fencing the highest-quality residential fiber cash flows into bankruptcy-remote subsidiaries.

**Secular decline of the legacy base.** The bull case requires fiber growth to outrun the legacy melt, which is not yet true on a consolidated basis.

**RDOF buildout miss.** Uniti missed the 40% buildout milestone (Dec 31, 2025) in several states on permitting delays and self-reported noncompliance to the FCC in January 2026 — potential clawback or penalty risk on $522.8M of awards and a signal of permitting friction on the build.

**Governance and supply overhang.** Elliott controls ~25.2% (the two largest affiliated holders own ~36.1%), with board-designation rights, a corporate-opportunity waiver (Elliott also holds Crown Castle and other telecom stakes), and anti-takeover provisions. This is effectively a controlled company; minority shareholders have limited influence and limited takeover-premium optionality. Searchlight Capital fully exited its ~2.27M-share position in Q1 2026, and registration rights mean legacy investor shares became freely tradable ~6 months post-close — a potential supply overhang.

**Other.** A $575M preferred liquidation preference at an 11% rate stepping toward a 16% cap, with Elliott able to require repurchase after year 10 (cash or dilutive stock); an assumed Windstream pension (~$103.6M obligation, $20.6M 2026 contributions); and lead-clad copper cable litigation/regulatory exposure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.7% sits below the 1-year historical volatility of 53.5% (a -7.8% spread), so options are modestly cheap and the 14% IV percentile suggests no extreme positioning. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%) and the put/call volume and OI ratios are near zero, indicating little directional hedging demand. The term structure is in backwardation (45.7% near-term versus 42.6% long-term), pricing some imminent event risk, and the 3-month tenor shows elevated IV (54.4%) consistent with the next earnings catalyst. The reported jump in one ATM IV reading to 426% is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Short interest at 11.9% of float (5.8 days to cover) reflects measurable institutional skepticism but is well below squeeze-risk thresholds. The options market is not corroborating a strong directional thesis in either direction, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Fiber subscriber growth (+26%) and hyperscaler IRU demand inflect the business toward growth as the legacy melt decelerates; the securitization strategy structurally lowers the cost of the build, accelerating deleveraging via EBITDA growth; penetration on 2.0M-plus passed premises climbs from 29%; and the ~$1B nonrecurring cash opportunity by 2028 materializes. In that scenario the equity stub re-rates meaningfully on the deleveraging path, toward the $16 analyst high.

**Bear case.** The legacy base melts faster than fiber and hyperscaler revenue can replace it, keeping consolidated revenue in decline; the RDOF miss escalates into clawbacks; rising rates raise refinancing costs as 2027–2028 maturities approach; and the thin equity stub absorbs the leverage. In that scenario the equity drifts toward the $6 analyst low.

The probabilities sit close enough to balanced that neither case dominates. The defining feature is the binary leverage profile: a thin equity layer on a heavily leveraged infra base, with high beta to both fiber-build success and credit-market conditions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Next checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (August), with the cadence of further Kinetic ABS issuance and any FCC determination on the RDOF milestone miss as interim monitorables.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pro forma consolidated service revenue inflects to flat or positive YoY, confirming fiber/hyperscaler growth has outrun the legacy melt.
- Continued securitized issuance at rates that demonstrably lower the blended cost of capital and bend net-leverage below ~5.5x.
- Fiber penetration on passed premises rising toward the mid-30s%, validating the build's return economics.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material RDOF clawback or penalty on the $522.8M of awards, or evidence of broader buildout-milestone failure.
- Net leverage rising above ~6.5x on a build that is not converting to EBITDA, or a meaningful widening in new-issue spreads signaling closing capital-markets access.
- A covenant proximity event against the 5.00x consolidated secured net leverage maintenance test.

## Conclusion

Uniti is a fairly valued leveraged fiber infrastructure equity stub. The bull and bear cases are balanced: a scarce, hard-to-replicate national fiber footprint, accelerating fiber subscriber growth, real hyperscaler demand, improving securitized cost of capital, and no near-term maturity wall on one side; ~$10.7B of debt, a still-declining consolidated revenue base, RDOF execution friction, and shareholder-unfriendly Elliott control on the other. At ~7.6x EV/EBITDA in line with leveraged peers and with consensus targets ($10.25 mean, $11.00 median) bracketing the $11.69 price, the equity is priced for its risk. The market has already absorbed the Q1 beat and the falling GAAP-EPS estimates and re-rated the stock up ~74%; there is no specific mispricing to exploit and no convincing structural-impairment case to short. We rate UNIT a HOLD: the outcome turns on whether fiber-build execution outruns the legacy melt and the debt load, which we cannot yet resolve in either direction.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: EV/EBITDA was understated — net debt is ~$10.64B (not ~$9.66B), lifting EV to ~$13.5B and EV/EBITDA to ~7.6x (not ~7.1x), which makes the equity modestly less cheap than the report's "in line with peers / fairly valued" framing implies.
