## Executive Summary

XPEL's second-quarter 2026 report (filed 2026-08-07) delivered the cleanest evidence yet that the 2024–2025 margin compression was an operating-expense investment cycle rather than competitive decay. Revenue rose 14.7% to a record $143.1M, gross margin expanded 120bp to 44.1%, product gross margin expanded 280bp to 41.4%, and operating margin improved to 16.2% from 15.5%. Company-defined EBITDA rose 17.6% to $27.6M. Against FY2024's 14.1% operating margin trough, two consecutive quarters of expansion on 44%-handle gross margins is visible in GAAP operating income rather than only in adjusted metrics.

Two adjustments materially change the growth optics. Cutbank-credit revenue fell 57.6% to $1.918M on a lower estimated fair value per credit; because cutbank credits are a bundled allocation off product sales, that revaluation shifts revenue into the product line. Product plus cutbank credits grew 14.4% in the quarter ($113.592M vs. $99.318M) versus headline product growth of 17.8% and headline PPF growth of 19.4%. Management disclosed on the August 5 call that roughly $2M of Q2 sales were pulled ahead of Q3 price increases, which puts underlying consolidated growth near 13.1% rather than 14.7%. China's reported +106.7% coexists with an approximately 20% decline in Chinese domestic car sales, because the September 2025 distributor acquisition converted wholesale revenue into direct revenue at a higher price point. Underlying demand growth is low-teens, and the China channel-conversion benefit laps around Q4 2026.

The dominating new variable is the strategic pivot. The FY2025 10-K states that the historical asset-light manufacturing model is being replaced by direct investment in manufacturing and supply chain assets. Net PP&E went from $15.8M to $104.5M in six months, first real long-term debt appeared ($44.8M PNC term loan on four San Antonio buildings, guaranteed by XPEL), a subsidiary was formed specifically to hold a Texas air permit, and 75% of a Chinese manufacturing facility was purchased for $21.4M. Six-month capex was $74.8M against $38.2M of operating cash flow, so free cash flow was roughly -$36.7M. The $12.9M of manufacturing equipment in construction-in-progress does not enter service until 2027, meaning depreciation, interest, permitting, and start-up labor hit the P&L a year or more before unit-cost benefits arrive. At 22x the FY2026 estimate with ~10% upside to a defensible base case and a bear case in the high-$30s that the stock itself visited in March 2026, the risk/reward is roughly balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

XPEL supplies protective films (paint protection film, or "PPF"; window film; and windshield protection film) plus coatings, installation labor, and pattern software into the automotive aftermarket, franchised new-car dealerships, and OEM channels. FY2025 revenue of $476.2M (+13.3%) split as PPF $249.4M (52.4%), window film $94.5M (19.9%), installation labor $87.0M (18.3%), cutbank credits $16.5M, other product $15.9M, software $8.7M, other service $4.0M.

The differentiator is the Design Access Platform, a proprietary pattern database covering over 90,000 vehicle applications that installers pay monthly to access. Patterns reduce film waste and installer labor, which management identifies as the installer's highest cost. Around that sits a service wrapper: 24/7 support, training, marketing and lead generation, Authorized Dealer gating, and now 640+ installation technicians across 34 company-owned centers in eleven countries. Roughly 51% of revenue moves through independent installers and 16% through third-party distributors. Non-US revenue was 44.2% of the total in FY2025, though the filing notes geographic splits are management estimates of ultimate destination because many international customers require shipment to US freight forwarders.

All geographies are aggregated into a single reportable segment, so there is no audited regional profitability. This is a genuine analytical limitation when a growing share of capital and income sits in China with minority partners.

## Financial Analysis

**The margin story, decomposed.** Between FY2023 and FY2025, operating margin fell from 16.9% to 13.2% while gross margin *rose* from 41.0% to 42.2%. The entire decline came from operating expenses moving from 24.1% to 29.1% of revenue — sales and marketing to 10.7% on sponsorships and dealer/end-customer spend, G&A to 18.3% on personnel, occupancy, and acquisition-related amortization. FY2024 was the worst year because revenue decelerated to +6.1% while the opex base was still scaling. FY2025 re-accelerated the top line to +13.3% without unwinding that base, and 2026 is now producing leverage: 6M'26 operating margin 13.9% vs. 13.3%, Q2 16.2% vs. 15.5%.

Within gross margin, product and service are diverging. Product margin improved to 41.4% from 38.6% on lower product costs, mix, and the sell-through of higher-cost inventory acquired in the China distributor deal. That last driver is not internally explained in the filing and interacts with a Level 3 contingent-consideration item defined as "the estimated value of acquired inventory that may be sold above its net realizable value," of which $4.541M was recognized in 6M'26. I would not assume this component of the product-margin gain recurs. Service margin fell to 53.9% from 56.5% because the fastest-growing service line, installation labor, is the lowest-margin one; excluding cutbank credits, service margin actually improved to 50.9% from 48.8%.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $66.9M against $4.0M of capex, giving $62.9M of free cash flow (13.2% margin) and FCF/net income of 1.23x, a high-quality conversion. Roughly $5.5M of that came from a non-recurring China transition-services agreement; normalizing it out gives ~$57.4M FCF, still ~12.1% of revenue. In 6M'26 operating cash flow rose to $38.2M, but capex of $74.8M (vs. $1.9M a year earlier) drove free cash flow to approximately -$36.7M. This is a deliberate asset purchase, not operating deterioration, but it removes trailing FCF as a valuation anchor for 2026.

**Balance sheet.** Cash fell $50.9M → $40.7M; net PP&E rose $15.8M → $104.5M (buildings $36.9M, land $10.2M, equipment $23.8M, CIP $27.1M); long-term debt went from zero to $43.5M plus $1.3M current. Inventory rose to $128.0M, with finished goods at $104.1M and raw materials up to $19.8M from $4.2M, consistent with in-house production starting. Against roughly $84M of recent-run EBITDA (FY2025's $77.4M plus the 6M'26 vs. 6M'25 increment of $6.7M), gross debt of $44.8M is ~0.5x and net debt of ~$4.1M is ~0.05x, against covenants of ≤3.50x leverage and ≥3.00x interest coverage. The $125M Wells Fargo revolver (maturity extended to September 2028) is undrawn; available liquidity is $128.2M of facilities plus $40.7M cash.

The PNC term loan carries Term SOFR + 125bp (4.9% at June 30), matures 2036 but amortizes over 25 years, leaving a $37.2M balloon. All debt is floating and unhedged; the 10-Q states a 200bp move may be material. Run-rate incremental interest on $44.8M at 4.9% is about $2.2M, roughly $0.06/share after tax. Contingent liabilities of $15.9M and acquisition holdbacks of $14.4M are additional future cash calls, and the revolver amendment permits up to $18.0M of equity investment into the Harvest entities.

**Earnings quality and basis discipline.** FY2025 net income attributable to XPEL was $51.226M (EPS ~$1.85); total net income of $51.589M includes $0.363M of non-controlling interest. Non-controlling interest is growing — the balance rose $4.9M → $12.4M and $0.423M of 6M'26 income accrued to minorities — so attributable income will increasingly diverge from consolidated. Q2'26 diluted EPS attributable was $0.65 (6M: $1.03). The surprise table shows a $0.68 "actual" against a $0.62 estimate; that reconciles to neither GAAP attributable ($0.65) nor total consolidated ($0.66), so the reported 10.5% beat is likely on a different basis and should not be paired with GAAP figures. The provider's 24.08x P/E implies TTM EPS near $2.08; my computed TTM attributable EPS is $1.98 (FY2025 $1.85 − H1'25 $0.90 + H1'26 $1.03), giving 25.3x. I use the latter.

Dilution is genuinely clean: diluted exceeded basic by 81K shares (0.29%) in Q2, entirely RSUs, with no warrants, converts, or share-based earn-outs disclosed. Acquisitions have been funded with cash, holdbacks, and contingent cash.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated growth is low-teens and narrowing in breadth. Adjusting for the cutbank revaluation and the ~$2M pull-forward, Q2 underlying growth was near 13% with underlying product growth of 14.4% (6M: 12.5%). Regional detail from the August 5 call: US +11.7%, Canada +10.8% but only about 4% excluding a distributor's ordering cadence, China +106.7% against a ~20% decline in local car sales, India/Middle East -5.0% on Iran-conflict-driven vehicle availability, EU/UK/Africa -2.3% on distributor timing, Japan and other APAC growing on prior direct-presence investment. In FY2025, Canada declined 5.0% and LatAm 5.7%. Double-digit consolidated growth is being produced by attach-rate gains and channel conversion inside a soft global auto backdrop, not by end-market expansion.

Three forward vectors matter. Window film grew 21.7% in FY2025 and 19.6% in 6M'26, is now ~20% of revenue, and includes the architectural VISION line, the first genuinely non-automotive product set and a path to decouple from auto cyclicality, though architectural revenue is not broken out. Windshield protection film, launched Q4 2024, contributed $7.0M in FY2025 on the same installer rails. Third, direct-market conversion continues: management's stated ambition is to sell directly into most of the top 25 car markets, pursued through distributor acquisitions (India, Thailand, Japan, China). That strategy structurally converts wholesale revenue into direct revenue, so reported growth in acquired markets should not be read as organic demand, and 2027 reported growth faces a headwind once China laps in Q4 2026.

The single-analyst consensus is $2.25 for FY2026 and $2.70 for FY2027, implying 20% earnings growth. Estimates are stable with no directional revision, so there is no revision signal here, and with one contributing analyst the consensus carries low confidence. To hit $2.25, H2 EPS must be ~$1.22 against ~$0.95 in H2'25, a 28% increase, into a quarter that gives back roughly $2M of pulled-forward revenue and begins absorbing new depreciation and interest. Achievable, but not a low bar.

The manufacturing program is the real growth-in-earnings option, and it is unquantified by the company. Management says only that it expects margin improvement to be "significant," with no target and no FY2026 guidance in any filing. Arithmetically the prize is large: on roughly $275M of 2026e PPF revenue, internalizing 500bp of third-party manufacturing margin on half the volume is ~$6.9M pretax, about $0.25/share; capturing 700bp across most volume approaches $0.70/share. Against that, incremental D&A of roughly $4–6M once equipment is in service plus ~$2.2M of interest costs about $0.17–$0.23/share, a headwind arriving in 2026–27 ahead of a benefit landing in 2027–28.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.07: 25.3x TTM attributable EPS of $1.98, 22.3x the FY2026 estimate of $2.25, 18.5x the FY2027 estimate of $2.70, and ~16.5x EV to ~$84M of recent-run EBITDA (17.9x on FY2025's $77.4M). Forward PEG on the FY26→FY27 consensus step is roughly 1.1. Trailing free cash flow is not usable as a yield because 6M'26 FCF was approximately -$36.7M.

The peer table shows a median P/E of 30.9x across Gentherm (49.2x), Adient (30.9x), Standard Motor Products (10.5x), Fox Factory (negative), and Visteon (20.1x). That dispersion — 10.5x to 49.2x with one loss-maker — tells you the "Automotive Parts & Equipment" grouping is heterogeneous and the median carries little information. XPEL's 42%+ gross margin, 13%+ FCF margin (pre-program), 18.1% ROE, and 1.24x asset turnover are structurally superior to the Tier-1 suppliers in that list, so a premium to Visteon or Standard Motor is warranted; a discount to Gentherm's 49x is not a mispricing claim. The more relevant anchor is XPEL against its own economics: the business was worth a high-20s multiple when it converted 12–13% of revenue into free cash flow with $4M of capex. It is now a business with $104.5M of PP&E, $44.8M of guaranteed debt, negative near-term FCF, and an unquantified return on a ~$110M program. That combination argues for the multiple compressing modestly, not expanding, until post-capex FCF re-establishes itself.

Scenario framework, all computed off the $50.07 basis:

- **Base $55 (+9.8%)** — FY2027 EPS of $2.70 at 20.4x. Assumes underlying growth of 12–13%, product gross margin holding near 41%, manufacturing start-up costs offsetting early savings, and the multiple settling in the low-20s on FY26 / high-teens on FY27. This is consistent with the analyst mean target of $56.67.
- **Bull $68 (+35.8%)** — Manufacturing delivers 200–300bp of product gross margin ahead of schedule, window film sustains high-teens growth, China proves to contain genuine share gain rather than pure channel conversion, and FY2027 EPS reaches ~$2.85 at 24x. A 14.6% short position with 9 days to cover amplifies upside on confirming prints.
- **Bear $37 (-26.1%)** — China laps in Q4 2026 and consolidated growth decelerates to high-single digits, FTC-driven changes to dealer add-on selling compress the dealership channel, start-up costs and new D&A/interest hold EPS near $2.15–2.25, and the multiple compresses to ~17x. The stock closed at $37.95 on March 13, 2026, so this is inside its own recent range rather than a stress construct.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear gives roughly +6% expected return over twelve months. That is positive but thin relative to the equity cost of capital in the current rate regime, and it does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar. Valuation score 5.2: the stock is neither obviously mispriced upward nor egregious, and the compressed forward multiple relative to growth is offset by a capital-intensity change the market has reasonable grounds to discount.

## Competitive Landscape

XPEL competes below Eastman Chemical (LLumar, SunTek) and above a long tail of Korean and Chinese film suppliers; the 10-K concedes principal competitors have significantly greater resources and may adopt more aggressive pricing. Historically it did not compete on film chemistry at all — it competed on the bundle of DAP patterns, support, training, lead generation, Authorized Dealer gating, and installed labor capacity. That is a distribution-and-workflow moat around a physical product with a low-cost supplier base beneath it.

The vertical-integration decision admits two readings. Management's framing is offensive: become a high-quality, low-cost producer and capture the manufacturer's margin on ~$250M of PPF. The alternative is defensive: unit costs must fall because the product beneath the moat is commoditizing, and the 10-K's own warning that "sustained price increases may lead to declines in volume as competitors may not adjust their prices" supports that reading. The filings do not resolve which dominates. Product gross margin expanding 280bp while volumes grow low-teens is mildly supportive of the offensive framing.

The idiosyncratic structural risk is channel, not cyclicality. XPEL's fastest-scaling US growth runs through the franchised dealership F&I office, competing for finite shelf space against other gross-profit-per-vehicle add-ons. The 10-K flags FTC and state-AG scrutiny of dealer accessory selling twice in Item 1A, and management said on the Q2 call that the independent channel was solid "despite challenges from dealerships due to FTC concerns," adding that XPEL is helping dealers comply. Compliance complexity can favor a large professionalized vendor and become a share-gain vector; it can equally shrink the channel. Neither effect is quantified. The 10-K names manufacturer-owned distribution and franchise-law change as risks that would push XPEL back onto the more expensive independent-installer channel.

Trajectory is mixed-to-improving: mix diversifying (window film 17.1% → 19.9% of revenue 2023→2025), product margin expanding, direct presence deepening in Asia. But no long-term contracts exist with the majority of installers and dealerships, who may reduce purchases "with little or no notice," and no customer-concentration percentages are disclosed anywhere. Mix diversification does not establish reduced customer concentration; those are different measures.

## Risk Assessment

**Manufacturing execution and the fixed-cost ratchet.** The Q2 10-Q added an entirely new risk block on in-house PPF manufacturing, naming production shortfalls, quality inconsistencies, supply disruption while running parallel operations, specialized adhesive/chemical sourcing, EHS hazards, and the possibility that anticipated volumes and efficiencies are not achieved — in which case "we would likely not recoup these investments." XPEL's brand rests on film that does not yellow or delaminate, backed by warranties; warranties assumed in 6M'26 were $1.187M versus $0.472M for all of FY2025, a 2.5x jump in the flow, though the ending liability is only $0.417M and payments of $1.077M suggest faster claim throughput rather than a quality cliff. Worth monitoring, not yet alarming.

**China: rising capital at risk into a contracting local market.** XPEL now carries $27.4M of China intangibles (above the US's $18.6M), $14.8M of China goodwill, and $17.2M of China PP&E (from $0.1M six months earlier), across a majority-owned distributor and a 75%-owned manufacturer, while Chinese domestic car sales fell ~20%. Minority holders can block or delay dividends and major transactions; PRC subsidiaries must reserve ≥10% of after-tax profit until reserves reach 50% of registered capital; SAFE registration governs intercompany funding. The NQL 25% partner made a $7.088M non-cash contribution whose composition and identity are not disclosed, and purchase accounting is preliminary for twelve months.

**Discretionary demand into a stressed consumer.** The 10-K states products are discretionary for most consumers and warns tariffs and reduced disposable income could cause buyers to delay or forego vehicle accessories. Several XPEL regions already show contraction, consistent with a soft global auto backdrop.

**Technology obsolescence tail.** Improved factory paint, film-at-manufacture, or machine application of PPF would compress both film and the installer labor XPEL monetizes; wider availability of pattern-creation technology would erode DAP. Low near-term probability, high severity.

**Financial structure.** Floating-rate, unhedged debt with a $37.2M 2036 balloon; XPEL guarantees the Harvest loan, so the SPV provides no economic ring-fencing. Finished-goods inventory of $104.1M creates obsolescence exposure if in-house film displaces purchased stock faster than it sells through.

**Governance unknowns.** Three 8-K filings could not be read: Items 3.03/5.03 (material modification of securityholder rights plus charter/bylaw amendment) on 2025-11-04 and 2026-02-25, and Item 5.02 officer/director changes on 2026-04-29 and 2026-07-31. The bylaw language now in the 10-K — eliminating stockholders' ability to call special meetings or act by written consent — is one plausible explanation, but a rights plan, officer exculpation, or authorized-capital change are equally live. The board may issue preferred stock with superior rights without common-stockholder approval. Neither CEO Pape nor CFO Wood has an employment agreement, and both were in place as of August 2026.

**Measurement risk.** EBITDA is the company-selected compensation measure. This transition converts rent (an EBITDA expense) and third-party manufacturer margin (COGS) into depreciation and interest, both EBITDA add-backs. I draw no inference about intent — the metric predates the strategy and the plan also uses revenue, EPS, and TSR — but the analytical implication is firm: EBITDA will overstate the improvement. Track operating margin, EPS, and post-capex FCF.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($68, +35.8%).** FY2024's 14.1% operating margin was the trough of an opex investment cycle, and the reversal is already in GAAP numbers: Q2'26 operating margin 16.2%, product gross margin 41.4% (+280bp), gross margin 44.1%. The manufacturing program adds a second, structurally different leg of margin: capturing third-party film margin on a $250M+ PPF base with equipment live in 2027. Window film compounding near 20% and now a fifth of revenue, plus architectural VISION, decouples the story from auto cyclicality over time. The balance sheet funds the entire program without equity: $4.1M net debt, $128.2M of undrawn facilities, 0.29% diluted-vs-basic share gap. The CEO owns 3.9% of the company (~$54M) against ~$2.7M of annual comp. At 18.5x the FY2027 estimate with 20% expected earnings growth and 14.6% short interest, confirming prints re-rate the stock.

**Bear case ($37, -26.1%).** Underlying product growth is ~14% and decelerating on a six-month basis (12.5%), the headline is flattered by a cutbank revaluation and ~$2M of pull-forward, and China's +107% is largely channel conversion into a market where car sales fell 20%, a benefit that laps in Q4 2026 and leaves 2027 reported growth exposed. The company is adding fixed cost on multiple fronts: 640+ technicians in a labor business the 10-K concedes cannot flex down in a demand trough, ~$4–6M of new D&A, ~$2.2M of interest, permitting and EHS spend, and start-up inefficiency, all landing before the 2027 payoff. The FTC-pressured dealership channel is the fastest-scaling, highest-margin part of US growth. And the entire historical return advantage came from *not* owning factories: capex was 0.8% of revenue with 1.24x asset turnover and 18.1% ROE. If in-housing underdelivers, the 10-K's own language applies — "we would likely not recoup these investments" — and the multiple compresses toward the mid-teens on flat EPS.

The prior review scores (6.4 in May, 5.8 in June) reflected this tension, and the new data cuts both ways rather than resolving it. The Q2 print confirms the margin-inflection leg and modestly strengthens financial-health confidence (covenant compliance, undrawn revolver, clean dilution). It also confirms the bear's core measurement objections in the company's own disclosure: the cutbank revaluation, the pull-forward, the China attribution, and a capex program that turned six-month free cash flow negative by $36.7M. Score holds at 5.8. The stock is up ~10% since the June review on unchanged-to-modestly-better fundamentals, which compresses rather than expands the margin of safety; this is a price-driven observation about asymmetry, not a claim of thesis deterioration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners have a business with low-teens underlying growth, expanding operating margin, and effectively no leverage; that combination justifies continued exposure. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $50.07 with ~10% to base and ~26% to bear.

**Targets from the $50.07 basis:** base $55 (+9.8%) on 20.4x the FY2027 estimate of $2.70; bull $68 (+35.8%) on ~24x ~$2.85; bear $37 (-26.1%) on ~17x ~$2.18. Probability-weighted expected return roughly +6% over twelve months. Analyst range: low $45, high $65, mean $56.67 (three analysts; two buy, one hold).

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026) are the first clean test — they must absorb the ~$2M pull-forward reversal, the Q3 price increases, and the first full quarter of interest expense. The China lap begins with Q4 2026 (reported February 2027). Manufacturing equipment enters service during 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of product gross margin at or above 41.5% *with* adjusted product revenue (product plus cutbank credits) growing 13%+ — establishing that the margin gain is not the one-off China inventory sell-through and that growth is not decelerating beneath the optics.
2. Management quantifies the manufacturing program's expected margin benefit and remaining capex-to-completion, with post-capex free cash flow turning positive on a trailing-two-quarter basis.
3. Price toward $42–44, restoring roughly 25%+ upside to the $55 base — the level at which the company itself repurchased stock (average $43.23 in Q1 2026).

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Adjusted product revenue growth falling below 8% in any quarter, or China revenue declining sequentially once the acquisition laps — either would confirm that reported growth was channel conversion rather than demand.
2. Operating margin declining year over year for two consecutive quarters as start-up costs, D&A, and interest outrun savings; or a guided delay of equipment in-service beyond 2027.
3. Disclosure that materially widens the risk set: a quantified FTC-driven dealership-channel revenue impact, an inventory write-down against the $104.1M finished-goods balance, incremental debt beyond the permitted $18.0M Harvest equity plus equipment financing, or resolution of the unread 3.03/5.03 8-K filings as an entrenchment measure.

**Items to verify before any rating change:** the composition of the ~$110M program (externally reported, not disclosed in primary filings); FY2026 guidance, of which none exists in any filing; the organic-versus-channel-conversion split in China; the identity and contributed assets of the 25% NQL partner; the terms of the perpetual license to US Patent 8,765,263, including whether it covers manufacture; and the content of the four unread 8-Ks.

## Conclusion

XPEL remains a differentiated niche business — a software-and-service moat around a physical product, 42%+ gross margins, 18.1% ROE, effectively no net debt, and clean share count — that is delivering a margin inflection after two years of opex-driven compression. Q2'26 product gross margin of 41.4% and operating margin of 16.2% are the strongest evidence yet that FY2024's trough was an investment cycle rather than competitive decay.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE. First, underlying growth is low-teens, not the high-teens the headlines suggest, and one of the largest contributors to reported growth (China) laps in Q4 2026. Second, the company is executing the largest strategic change in its history, converting the asset-light model that produced its returns into $104.5M of PP&E and $44.8M of guaranteed floating-rate debt, with the payoff equipment not in service until 2027 and six-month free cash flow at roughly -$36.7M. Third, at 22x the FY2026 estimate and 18.5x FY2027, the price already embeds a successful transition: base-case upside is about 10% against a bear case the stock visited five months ago. Add a discretionary end market under consumer pressure and an FTC-scrutinized dealership channel, and the expected return of roughly +6% does not compensate adequately for the execution binary.

Own it if you own it. New capital should wait for either evidence that the margin gain and adjusted product growth are both durable, or a price in the low-to-mid $40s where the company itself was a buyer.
