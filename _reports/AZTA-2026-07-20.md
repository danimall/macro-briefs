**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Azenta is the former Brooks Automation, reborn as a pure-play life sciences company after selling its semiconductor automation business to Thomas H. Lee Partners for $2.9 billion in early 2022. The remaining business is two segments of very different quality: Sample Management Solutions (SMS, ~55% of revenue), a sticky, recurring-revenue biobanking and cold-chain-custody franchise that is modestly profitable, and Multiomics (~45%), a genomic-services business that loses money and operates in a commoditizing, price-deflationary market against low-cost Asian competitors. The core analytical fact is that roughly half the ~$1.2B market cap is net cash and marketable securities (~$565M) against zero debt, with an activist (Politan) on the board driving portfolio simplification.

The bear case here is real and specific: three unremediated material weaknesses in internal controls, multiple prior-year financial revisions, a fresh securities-fraud investigation, a $149.1M goodwill impairment in Q2 FY26, and a structurally challenged Multiomics segment. Those are the reasons the stock fell ~37% from its January 2026 high of $41.73 to a mid-May trough near $16. But the same evidence that supports "do not own this at a premium" does not support a directional short from here. The stock has since re-rated off its low to $26.29, estimate revisions have stabilized (0.0% across all periods over the trailing 30 days after the earlier ~50% cuts), and consensus targets bracket the current price (mean $26.20, median $28.00). A director bought 10,000 shares on the open market at $16.38 in mid-May, essentially at the bottom.

We move to HOLD (uncertain) from AVOID. The prior AVOID was defensible when the impairment, the -132% March-quarter miss, and the cascade of estimate cuts were fresh and the outcome of the B Medical divestiture was in doubt. Those events are now behind the stock, the divestiture closed on July 8, 2026, and the market has priced in the impairment. What remains is a wide, genuinely uncertain distribution: a large cash floor and activist-driven restructuring optionality on the upside, versus governance dysfunction and a loss-making segment on the downside. We cannot make a confident directional call in either direction, which is what "uncertain hold" is for.

## Company Overview

Azenta serves ~14,000 customers across ~85–95 countries with ~2,900 employees. The two continuing reportable segments (FY25 revenue $593.8M) are:

- **Sample Management Solutions (SMS)** — ~$325M (55%). Automated ultra-cold and cryogenic storage systems, sample tubes, consumables and instruments, controlled-rate thawing, and Sample Repository Services (SRS), an outsourced "cold chain of custody" storage and logistics offering. This is the profitable segment (FY25 segment operating margin 6.2%, ~8.5% adjusted) and where the durable value sits. The March 2026 UK Biocentre tuck-in (~$27.5M) expands the European biorepository footprint.
- **Multiomics** — ~$269M (45%). Genomic services spanning next-generation sequencing (growing), Sanger sequencing (in secular decline), and gene synthesis. This is the problem segment: FY25 operating margin -5.7%, deteriorating to -16.9% in Q2 FY26, and the location of the bulk of the Q2 FY26 goodwill write-down.

The B Medical Systems cold-chain business was divested on July 8, 2026 (see Recent Developments), removing a large recurring loss-maker (FY25 discontinued-ops loss -$80.2M; FY24 -$140.5M) and leaving a cleaner two-segment company.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is low-single-digit and decelerating.** Continuing-operations revenue moved from $551.5M (FY23) to $573.4M (FY24) to $593.8M (FY25), +3.6% in FY25. H1 FY26 was essentially flat at $293.4M (+0.9%). Growth comes from SRS/sample storage and NGS; the drags are Automated Stores, Cryogenic Systems, Sanger sequencing, and gene synthesis.

**GAAP profitability is weak and the headline net income is misleading.** FY25 produced a GAAP operating loss of -$26.8M; adjusted operating income was only +$15.6M (2.6% margin). The reported +$24.5M net income from continuing operations was manufactured almost entirely by a one-time $45.6M tax benefit from a "worthless stock deduction" on a foreign subsidiary; the pre-tax continuing loss was -$7.1M. Anyone anchoring to the trailing EPS of $0.53 or the -10.9x P/E is looking at a number with no operating meaning.

**Q2 FY26 broke the stock.** A $149.1M non-cash goodwill impairment ($112.4M Multiomics + $36.6M SMS) drove a -$157.0M loss from continuing operations. Goodwill fell from $702M to $553M. The impairment was triggered by the sustained stock decline and downward-revised forecasts; management effectively conceded that prior Multiomics growth assumptions had failed. Consolidated gross margin slipped to 42.8% (from 43.8% YoY), with Multiomics gross margin at 39.2% on lost fixed-cost leverage from lower North America Sanger volumes plus Automated Stores rework and excess/obsolete inventory reserves. The March-quarter EPS miss was -131.7% (-$0.04 actual vs $0.13 estimate).

**The balance sheet is the anchor of any bull case.** Zero debt. Cash plus marketable securities of ~$565M against a ~$1.2B market cap, roughly half the market cap in net cash. Altman Z-Score of 4.81 (safe zone), current ratio ~3.0, equity/assets 0.84. FY25 operating cash flow was $72M and FCF ~$38M (6.5% margin). H1 FY26 operating cash flow was $34.4M, down from $44.2M, but that comparison is distorted by a prior-year tax refund.

**Capital allocation has been aggressive but is currently paused.** The company retired ~30M shares (from ~66M to ~46M) under a completed $1.5B repurchase through FY24. A new $250M buyback (through Dec 2028) was authorized on December 8, 2025, but nothing had been repurchased as of the Q2 FY26 report. There is no dividend. Given ~$565M of net cash, the undeployed buyback is meaningful dry powder, and its non-deployment at prices below $20 is a mild negative on capital-allocation timing.

The financial-health picture is genuinely two-sided: a fortress balance sheet that limits solvency risk, paired with operations that lose money on a GAAP basis and barely break even adjusted. We score financial health 6.0; the zero-debt, high-cash structure is a real downside floor, but the poor operating cash generation and the accounting revisions keep it from being a high score.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest dimension. Consolidated organic growth is low-single-digit and flat in H1 FY26. The segment mix compounds the problem: the profitable SMS franchise grows slowly on SRS and biorepository expansion, while the larger drag comes from segments in structural decline. Multiomics is the clearest issue — Sanger sequencing and gene synthesis are shrinking, NGS is growing but into a price-deflationary market against BGI, Novogene, Eurofins, GenScript, IDT, and Twist Bioscience, and the just-recorded $112.4M impairment is management's own admission that its growth model there failed.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration but also show it has run its course. Over the trailing 90 days, current-year EPS estimates were cut -50.6% (to $0.38), next-year -43.0% (to $0.61), current quarter -56.9%, and next quarter -33.7%. Over the trailing 30 days every one of those lines reads 0.0%; the cutting has stopped. The negative revision cycle appears to have found a floor, which is why the stock recovered even as the estimates sat at their lowered levels.

The forward growth thesis is restructuring rather than organic. The plausible path to value creation is Politan-driven: a Multiomics divestiture, restructuring, or JV; deployment of the buyback; and control remediation. Those are corporate-action catalysts, not revenue acceleration. We score growth prospects 3.5, up marginally from the prior 3.0 because the estimate cuts have stabilized and the B Medical loss-maker is gone, but the underlying organic growth remains poor.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are useless here. The -10.9x trailing P/E reflects the one-time tax benefit and the impairment; it carries no information. On the forward consensus of $0.38 current-year and $0.61 next-year EPS, the stock trades at ~69x and ~43x, expensive on the surface, but those estimates are on a business barely earning an operating profit, so the earnings multiple is not the right lens either.

The relevant frame is sum-of-parts against the cash floor. With ~$565M net cash (~$12.26/share on 46.1M shares) and zero debt, the market is assigning roughly $650M of enterprise value to a business generating ~$594M of revenue with slim adjusted operating margins. That is not obviously cheap, but it is not obviously expensive given a sticky SMS franchise worth a real multiple and a Multiomics segment the market is valuing at close to zero. The peer set is scattered — Bio-Rad at 46.9x, Bio-Techne at 77.8x, Medpace at 29.9x, with Avantor and Charles River carrying negative P/Es — so a peer-median comparison (46.9x) is not a reliable anchor. The sector trades at high multiples on quality; Azenta lacks the quality, which explains the discount.

The audit correctly notes that the bearish thesis is largely priced. The stock fell 37% from its high, bottomed near $16 close to where an insider bought, and consensus targets now bracket spot (low $20, mean $26.20, median $28.00, high $33.00). The valuation is not a screaming buy — there is no organic-growth catalyst to re-rate it — but at $26.29 the downside is cushioned by cash and the upside is optionality on restructuring. That is a fair, two-sided setup. We resist scoring valuation high purely because it looks "cheap on cash," consistent with the discipline that cheapness without a catalyst is a warning, not a signal. The catalyst here (restructuring optionality) is real but unscheduled, so we score valuation 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Azenta straddles two markets of opposite quality. SMS and Sample Repository Services is an attractive, sticky franchise: biobanking and cold-chain custody carry genuine switching costs, and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, so concentration risk is low. UK Biocentre extends this footprint in Europe. This is where the moat and the durable value sit.

Multiomics is the opposite: a fragmented, commoditizing, price-deflationary genomic-services market with large low-cost Asian competitors (BGI, Novogene) and specialized players (Twist, IDT, GenScript). Sanger sequencing is in secular decline. Management argues that portfolio breadth is the differentiator, but the Q2 FY26 impairment is the market's verdict that the Multiomics competitive position is deteriorating, not stabilizing. Overall competitive trajectory is mixed-to-declining: a stable, moaty SMS business paired with an eroding Multiomics business that is roughly 45% of revenue. We score competitive position 4.5, up modestly from 4.0 given that removing B Medical and the potential simplification of Multiomics would concentrate the company on its better franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is the reason this stock is not ownable at a premium, and it is why the risk score stays low despite the balance-sheet strength.

1. **Three unremediated material weaknesses in internal controls.** ICFR was concluded not effective at both September 30, 2025 and March 31, 2026. The weaknesses cover cash-flow-statement review, account reconciliations, and classification of costs between COGS and SG&A/R&D. FY23, FY24, and FY25 interim statements were revised, plus an additional COGS error in Q2 FY26. For a company of this size and vintage, this is a serious, unresolved failure that undermines confidence in reported numbers.

2. **Securities-fraud investigation.** Levi & Korsinsky announced an investigation (May 20, 2026) citing the Q2 loss/impairment "erasing prior guidance." Plaintiff-firm investigations frequently precede class actions; stacked on the control weaknesses, the litigation overhang is credible.

3. **Multiomics structural weakness and continued impairment risk.** The segment loses money and is worsening; $553M of goodwill remains after the write-down, and management explicitly warns further impairments are possible.

4. **China/geopolitical exposure.** Significant genomic operations in China with ~$25M cash still being repatriated. Exposure to the BIOSECURE Act, the DOJ Bulk Transfer Rule on cross-border genomic-data transfers, and US-China tariffs.

5. **Governance instability.** Heavy leadership turnover (new CEO Marotta, new CFO Lin, reshuffled Multiomics leadership) and an expired-but-continuing activist board presence create execution and continuity risk.

These are genuine, specific risks, but they are the reason the stock already sits ~37% below its high, not an unpriced catalyst for a fresh leg down. That distinction is central to the recommendation change.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, symmetric stress rather than a directional collapse. Near-term ATM IV is 104.5% against 65.7% historical vol (rich, 92nd percentile within the chain), with backwardation (1m 104.5% > 6m 74.7%), the signature of an event being priced near-term. The skew is call-heavy (-4.0% at 1m, -8.8% at 3m), and the put/call OI ratio is a very low 0.09. That is more consistent with positioning for an upside catalyst (a strategic action or buyback deployment) than with a market betting on further downside. ATM IV also collapsed from a corrupted-looking 490% reading to 104.5% as price rose 15%. Combined with 21.2% short interest and 7.1 days to cover, the mechanics create meaningful squeeze/upside risk on any positive strategic news, a direct argument against a short and a supporting data point for HOLD over AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$33+, matching the analyst high).** Politan and new management execute the restructuring playbook: a Multiomics divestiture/JV removes the loss-maker and concentrates the company on the profitable SMS franchise, the $250M buyback is deployed near current prices (accretive given ~$565M of cash), and the control weaknesses are remediated, lifting the governance discount. A cleaner, buyback-shrunk SMS-plus-cash entity could support a mid-to-high-$30s valuation. The 21.2% short interest and call-heavy positioning amplify the upside on any of these catalysts.

**Bear case (~$16–20, revisiting the May trough).** Multiomics keeps bleeding and triggers a further goodwill impairment against the remaining $553M; the securities-fraud investigation escalates into a class action with real settlement cost; control weaknesses persist into another fiscal year; and the buyback stays undeployed while cash slowly erodes. In that scenario the stock retests the $16–20 range where the director bought.

**Base case (~$27–28).** Estimates hold at their lowered levels, B Medical is gone, and the company muddles through on a modestly profitable adjusted basis while the market waits for a Multiomics decision. This is roughly where consensus (median $28.00) sits and where the cash floor plus restructuring optionality balance the governance overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q3 FY26 earnings report (with updated Multiomics performance and any impairment commentary), any 8-K signaling a Multiomics strategic action, and evidence of buyback deployment. Twelve months should resolve whether the restructuring thesis is real.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A definitive Multiomics divestiture, restructuring, or JV announced on reasonable terms, concentrating the company on SMS.
- Meaningful buyback deployment (say, >$75M of the $250M authorization) at prices below the mid-$20s, plus remediation of at least one of the three material weaknesses.
- Multiomics segment operating margin stabilizing (loss narrowing back toward the FY25 -5.7% from the Q2 FY26 -16.9%) alongside SMS growth holding.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further goodwill impairment against the remaining $553M, or a fresh downward estimate-revision cycle (>15% cuts to current-year EPS).
- Escalation of the Levi & Korsinsky investigation into a filed class action with quantified exposure.
- Material weaknesses carried into a third consecutive fiscal year with no remediation progress, or additional financial restatements.

## Conclusion

We rate Azenta a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The prior AVOID was appropriate when the impairment, the -132% miss, the ~50% estimate cuts, and the unresolved B Medical divestiture were all live. Those are now behind the stock: the divestiture closed July 8, 2026, estimates have been flat for 30 days, the stock has recovered off its $16 low, and consensus targets bracket spot. The governance dysfunction and Multiomics weakness are genuine and keep us from owning it at a premium, but they are the reason the stock already fell, not an unpriced catalyst for further downside. With ~$565M net cash (~half the market cap), zero debt, a Z-score of 4.81, an activist driving simplification, and 21.2% short interest with call-heavy options positioning, a clean directional short is not supported; the report's own short assessment concludes as much. The correct posture is to neither own nor short: wait for a Multiomics strategic action, buyback deployment, or control remediation to resolve the wide, genuinely two-sided distribution before taking a directional view.
