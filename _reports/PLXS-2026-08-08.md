**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Plexus has executed the operating turn it promised. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $1,304.8M rose 28.1% year over year and 12% sequentially, exceeding the company's own $1.200–1.250B guidance range; GAAP diluted EPS of $1.58 beat the $1.25–$1.41 guide; Q4 revenue guidance of $1.330–1.380B implies roughly another 28% year-over-year gain. Adding nine-month actuals of $3,538.4M to the Q4 midpoint produces approximately $4,893M of FY2026 revenue, or +21.3% against FY2025's $4,033.0M. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 6.9% (FY2026) and 8.8% (FY2027) over 90 days, and analyst sentiment is improving. Sidoti upgraded to Buy in late July, and the lowest published price target ($275) sits above the current price.

The problem is what the growth costs and what the market now pays for it. Gross margin was exactly 10.1% in Q3 FY2026 and Q3 FY2025, and in both nine-month periods, so a 28% volume increase produced zero gross-margin leverage. Segment operating margins fell in both growing regions (AMER 8.5% from 9.2%, APAC 13.9% from 14.1%), while corporate costs rose 37.7%, faster than revenue. About $17.4M of FX hedge gains flowed into cost of sales in the nine months versus $0.9M a year earlier — roughly 30% of the $55.0M gross-profit increase — and management expects only $2.6M of remaining net unrealized hedge gains to reclassify over the next twelve months. Free cash flow is negative ($35.3M) for the nine months against +$56.8M a year earlier, inventory is up $258.6M to $1,488.4M, revolver borrowings rose from $40.0M to $172.0M, and $64.1M of share repurchases were made at an average of $186.25 per share over the nine months, with the final Q3 monthly tranche at $286.24. Net cash fell from $169.0M to $39.0M.

At $270.35 the stock carries 31.1x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $8.69, 27.0x FY2027 of $10.01, and roughly 37–38x derived FY2026 GAAP EPS of $7.08–$7.24. For a contract manufacturer with 10.1% gross margins, no proprietary products, no patent strategy, top-10 customer concentration at 55% and rising, and a Malaysian tax holiday being eroded by Pillar Two in real time, that multiple already embeds the FY2027 margin expansion and cash-flow inflection management has indicated but not guided. Base case is approximately spot. HOLD, classified as uncertain: the decisive unresolved question — how much of the semi-cap ramp is durable share gain versus cyclical order flow — cannot be answered from the filings, because management itself does not quantify the split.

## Company Overview

Plexus is a design, manufacturing and aftermarket-services provider operating at the low-volume, high-complexity, heavily-regulated end of electronic manufacturing services. It serves three go-to-market sectors — Aerospace/Defense, Healthcare/Life Sciences, and Industrial (which includes semiconductor capital equipment) — but reports financially by geography (AMER, APAC, EMEA), because the CEO allocates resources that way. The company states explicitly that it does not internally track costs or resources by market sector, so sector "profitability" is not a disclosed or even internally measured quantity.

Scale: over 20,000 employees, 27 active facilities, six design centers, roughly 5.0 million square feet. Workforce distribution is 58.3% APAC, 30.1% AMER, 11.6% EMEA. Two structural features dominate the financial profile. First, turnkey procurement: Plexus buys and warehouses most customer materials, with approximately 89% of cost of sales variable and 87–88% of that variable cost being materials and components. The balance sheet is a working-capital engine. Second, there is no product IP: the 10-K states the company does not design or manufacture proprietary products and does not generally seek patents. The moat, such as it is, rests on a certification stack (ISO 13485, 21 CFR Part 820, AS9100, NADCAP, FAR 145/EASA, ITAR, ATEX/IECEx, IRIS) and on the qualification cost customers face to move a regulated program.

The stated long-term model is 9–12% compound revenue growth with 15% after-tax ROIC against a 9.0% FY2026 WACC. FY2026 is running roughly double the top of the revenue range.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** Nine-month FY2026 revenue of $3,538.4M was +19.0%. Q3 sector detail: Industrial $589.5M (+42.2%, now 45% of revenue from 41%), Aerospace/Defense $232.8M (+27.1%), Healthcare/Life Sciences $482.4M (+14.7%, down to 37% of mix from 41%). Geographically, AMER +37.2% and APAC +30.4%; EMEA fell 6.6% and remains structurally weak (revenue -18.2% in FY2025, $21.3M of segment operating income on $440.0M of sales).

One mechanical adjustment matters: FY2026 contains 14 weeks in Q1 under the 4-4-5 calendar. [unverified — actual: brief confirms 14-week Q1 but does not characterize the full year as 53 weeks or quantify the extra-week contribution] The Q1 FY2027 estimate of $2.39 is below the Q4 FY2026 estimate of $2.55, a sequential decline.

**Margins.** Gross margin held at 10.1%, which the 10-Q attributes to "an increase in fixed costs and a shift in customer mix." That second phrase is a reversal: FY2025's MD&A cited customer mix as a positive contributor. GAAP operating margin fell 60bp year over year in Q3 to 4.7% because selling and administrative expense rose $20.4M, of which $14.9M was stock compensation including $12.9M of accelerated vesting tied to executive retirement agreements. Excluding that acceleration, corporate costs still grew roughly 17.8%, below revenue growth but above what a clean fixed-cost-leverage story would produce.

The FX hedge contribution deserves weight. Nine-month gains reclassified from AOCI into cost of sales were $17.423M versus $0.871M, a $16.5M swing against a $55.0M total gross-profit increase. AOCI fell from $17.8M to $7.3M and designated-hedge fair value from a $10.1M asset to $2.6M. The economically neutral interpretation is available — these hedge foreign-currency costs, so the COGS gain may offset higher underlying local-currency expense, and the filings do not disclose the offsetting movement. What is not in dispute is that the reclassification pipeline is nearly empty: management expects $2.6M over the next twelve months. Whatever cushion this line provided to the flat 10.1% gross margin is not repeatable at that magnitude in FY2027.

**Cash flow.** This is the central negative. Nine-month operating cash flow of $39.0M (from $117.2M) less capex of $74.3M (from $60.4M) produces free cash flow of -$35.3M versus +$56.8M. Inventories consumed $259.9M against a $37.5M release a year earlier, a $297.5M swing; receivables consumed $140.2M; contract assets $43.3M. Accounts payable (+$271.4M) and advanced customer payments (+$27.4M) partially offset. Q3 alone generated $25.9M of operating cash flow against $26.6M of capex, essentially breakeven.

Do not annualize that. Plexus's working capital swings with ramp timing, supplier terms, advanced-payment returns and the fiscal calendar. As a labeled sensitivity, extending the nine-month run rate implies roughly -$47M for FY2026; management's own framing is "a usage of free cash flow for fiscal 2026 with a return to meaningful free cash flow generation in early fiscal 2027." FY2024's headline $341.3M of FCF was substantially a one-time inventory harvest (inventory fell $1,562.0M → $1,311.4M → $1,229.8M across FY2023–FY2025), so the current build is partly the mirror image of that.

Efficiency is genuinely improving on the metrics management emphasizes: cash cycle 62 days versus 69, days in inventory 116 versus 128, described as the best quarterly cash cycle in over five years. Days improved while dollars grew, because revenue grew faster than the balance. Efficiency gains and cash generation are separate facts, and only the first is positive.

**Balance sheet.** Screened "debt/equity 1.16" is total liabilities to equity and should be discarded. Actual interest-bearing debt including finance leases was $275.5M at July 4, 2026 (0.18x equity), up from $137.8M (0.09x) at FY2025 year-end: $50.0M of 4.22% notes due June 2028, $172.0M drawn on the revolver, $54.8M of finance leases and other. The revolver was refinanced June 5, 2026 into a five-year, $500M facility maturing June 2031 (expandable to $750M), removing a 2027 maturity ahead of the working-capital build. Utilization is 34%; nine-month peak daily borrowing was $233.0M versus $125.0M a year earlier. Covenant compliance was reported.

Two items reduce reported working-capital intensity without eliminating the underlying exposure. Plexus sold $664.8M of receivables in the nine months under uncommitted MUFG ($340M max) and HSBC China ($70M max) facilities, with $234.9M sold-but-uncollected and still serviced by Plexus at quarter-end; proceeds run through operating cash flow. Reported DSO of 56 days therefore understates the gross receivable cycle, and the 10-K names the loss of factoring capacity as a distinct risk. Separately, 95% of cash sat outside the U.S. at July 4, 2026, up from 85%.

**Tax.** The largest single quantified change in earnings quality. The Malaysian subsidiary holiday (expiring December 31, 2034) reduced FY2025 taxes net of GILTI by $43.1M, or $1.56 per diluted share — 24.9% of FY2025 net income of $172.9M. FY2025 effective rate was 8.05%. Q3 FY2026 was 23.8%, nine-month 19.0%, FY2026 guided 16.0–18.0% (revised down from 17–19%), attributed to global minimum tax implementation. At approximately $239M of FY2026 pretax income, a 17% rate costs roughly $21.4M, or about $0.78 per diluted share, versus the FY2025 rate. This is a permanent reduction in the conversion of operating income to EPS, and it makes any P/E comparison spanning FY2025 and earlier a comparison between an 8%-taxed and a 17%-taxed company.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is real, recent, and identifiable. AMER's nine-month increase included +$293.3M from production ramps of new products for existing customers and +$59.8M from new customers. APAC and Industrial strength is described, verbatim in three places in the Q3 filing, as "continued ramps of semi-cap wins reflecting share gains from prior periods." Management's own language separates a Plexus-specific share-gain component from an end-market demand component; the split is not quantified anywhere.

Forward indicators are supportive. Thirty-one manufacturing programs were won in Q3 representing $255M of annualized revenue when fully ramped, and the qualified funnel expanded to a record $4.5B from roughly $4B a quarter earlier. Both figures require careful handling. The funnel is a company-defined pipeline of qualified opportunities, not backlog, not contracted revenue, not remaining performance obligations. Plexus's actual contract balances are far smaller: contract assets of $193.9M and deferred revenue within advanced payments of $164.5M. Master service arrangements obligate no volume and no pricing. The "$255M annualized" is contingent on ramp completion and end-customer demand.

FY2027 framing is hedged: "potential to generate fiscal 2027 revenue growth in excess of our 9% to 12% goal," led by Aerospace/Defense and Industrial including semi-cap, with "operating margin expansion, while continuing to make important investments in talent and technology." That is an indication, not a range.

Estimate revisions corroborate momentum without reaching the magnitude that would flip this into a high-conviction inflection call: FY2026 +5.2% (30d) / +6.9% (90d), FY2027 +5.6% / +8.8%, current quarter +11.4% / +13.8%. Five analysts. The earnings track record is 3 beats and 1 miss over four quarters, with beats of 7.1%, 6.8% and 12.3%. The FY2026 consensus of $8.69 sits essentially at the midpoint of the derived company non-GAAP guide of $8.60–$8.76, and the current-quarter estimate of $2.55 is at the Q4 guidance midpoint of $2.55; consensus embeds guidance, not upside.

Growth from $8.69 to $10.01 implies 15.2% FY2027 earnings growth. Against 27.0x forward, the PEG is approximately 1.78.

Offsetting the ramp: disengagements are chronic, not exceptional. FY2025 lost roughly $87.7M of revenue to disengagements plus $13.9M from a discontinued program; nine-month FY2026 disengagements subtracted $41.9M in AMER, $50.6M in Industrial and $14.5M in EMEA. Healthcare/Life Sciences is growing at half the company rate. EMEA is shrinking.

Before this cycle, PLXS traded in the mid-teens to low-20s on forward earnings. At 27x FY2027 the market is paying roughly a doubling of that multiple for an acceleration whose durability management describes as partly cyclical. The base case sits in the $265–280 band, centered around the current price.

Three specific reasons to resist treating the analyst mean of $305.75 as a floor: the stock is not cheap relative to growth on any reasonable basis (PEG ~1.78, 31x current-year non-GAAP); consensus EPS already equals the guidance midpoint, so target upside depends on multiple expansion rather than estimate accretion; and the free-cash-flow line, which is the input a DCF-based target is most sensitive to, is negative. The improving analyst distribution (two strong buys, two buys, one hold, versus two-one-two a month ago) is sentiment corroboration, not independent valuation evidence.

Two adjustments cut against the reported multiple as well. The non-GAAP EPS Plexus headlines excludes 100% of stock compensation ($1.23 per share of the nine-month gap between $6.13 non-GAAP and $4.90 GAAP), while the company's own ROIC bridge — the metric feeding executive pay — adds back only the $12.940M of accelerated vesting. An investor comparing PLXS "adjusted EPS" to peers should know the adjustment is unusually broad. The tax step-up means FY2027 EPS growth carries no further help from rate normalization; continued Pillar Two phase-in is a residual risk to the 16–18% guide.

Valuation score of 4.3 reflects a multiple that fully capitalizes the current ramp with no margin of safety, no yield support, and negative cash conversion. This is not a value trap in the impairment sense, but there is no identified mispricing in the buyer's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

Plexus does not name competitors. It describes the field as global providers, regional specialists, and the in-house capabilities of current and potential customers. The defensible element is the regulatory certification stack plus the ability to carry a program from design through NPI, volume manufacturing and depot repair, which creates genuine switching costs in FDA-, FAA-, ITAR- and NADCAP-governed production.

The trajectory reading is mixed rather than clearly improving. In favor: management states it is winning semi-cap share, and 42.2% Industrial growth against a semi-cap capital cycle that is not growing 42% supports that claim. Aerospace/Defense +27.1% is consistent with the multi-front defense demand environment. The newest disclosed adjacency, contract manufacturing a battery energy storage system for data centers under a new Industrial partnership, attaches Plexus to AI datacenter capital spending without competing in commodity power electronics. Precise reading: Plexus is the manufacturer; the unnamed counterparty owns the product, design IP and end-customer relationship. It is one program among 31 won that quarter and is not sized.

Against: gross margin has not moved despite 28% volume growth, and segment margins fell in the two regions doing the growing. The 10-K itself enumerates competitor advantages — lower internal cost structures, greater component buying power, better excess-capacity utilization, willingness to accept lower margins — and concedes that Plexus's positioning "typically result[s] in higher investments in inventory and selling and administrative costs." A 10.1% gross margin and 5.0% GAAP operating margin are not premium-service economics, and the turnkey pass-through of materials mechanically compresses percentage margins regardless of value-add.

The mix-versus-concentration point is important and frequently misread. Industrial rising from 41% to 45% of revenue while Healthcare falls from 41% to 37% is a change in end-market composition. Over the same period top-10 customers went from 47.8% of FY2024 revenue to 49.1% in FY2025 to 55% of Q3 FY2026 revenue, up seven points year over year. The incremental revenue is landing with a narrower group of paying counterparties. Sector breadth does not offset counterparty concentration when master service arrangements obligate no volume. The mitigant, per the 10-K, is that many large customers contract through independent divisions; the unresolved gap is that Plexus has not disclosed whether the "no customer above 10%" statement from FY2025 and FY2024 still holds in FY2026.

## Risk Assessment

**Penang / Malaysia single-point concentration.** APAC generated $2,392.9M of FY2025 revenue and $337.7M of segment operating income, more than consolidated operating income before corporate costs. The 10-K names Penang specifically as a location where "an event in that geography could materially hinder our production capabilities" and notes facilities "at or near sea level." This single geography holds the profit engine, the tax structure, the physical-catastrophe exposure and the geopolitical exposure simultaneously.

**Tax holiday erosion.** Roughly $0.78 per share of recurring headwind at FY2026 pretax levels, with the 10-K using the word "materially" about further impact. The FY2026 guide was revised down to 16–18%, so the phase-in may prove milder than the language implied, but the direction is fixed.

**Semi-cap cyclicality.** Industrial is 45% of revenue and semi-cap is the named driver. Plexus supplies semi-cap OEMs under contract; it inherits their order cycle with a lag and has no pricing power over the end market. The 10-K states the semiconductor industry "has historically been subject to significant cyclicality and volatility." Concentrating 45% of revenue in the most cyclical end market at the peak multiple in company history is the primary risk that is not obviously priced.

**Turnkey inventory in a ramp.** $1,488.4M of inventory, raw materials $1,283.9M, purchased against customer forecasts. Mitigants are contractual (customers generally obligated to purchase forecast-driven inventory; $424.6M of customer deposits sit in advanced payments), but the 10-K warns Plexus "may not actually be reimbursed timely or in full." A demand air pocket converts directly into excess and obsolete exposure and would arrive before the working capital unwinds.

**Trade and export controls.** The 10-K flags changing regulations "particularly relating to advanced semiconductors and chip-manufacturing equipment," affecting shipments to China, support for China programs, and component sourcing; Mexican operations depend on the IMMEX program. No filing quantifies a tariff cost or pass-through. Tariffs lead the forward-looking risk list in both the 10-Q and the earnings release.

**Factoring dependency.** ~$235M outstanding on uncommitted facilities, named as a distinct risk in the 10-K.

**Governance and continuity.** CFO Patrick Jermain retired under an April 28, 2026 Retirement and Transition Agreement, with David Abuhl signing as CFO by August 6, 2026. The financial statements reference "executive retirement agreements" (plural) causing $12.9M of accelerated stock compensation, while only one agreement appears in the exhibit index; the second retiree is not identified in the filings. Disclosure controls were certified effective as of July 4, 2026 with no changes to internal control over financial reporting. A CFO handoff coinciding with the largest working-capital build in years is a monitoring item rather than a red flag, and internal promotion suggests prepared succession.

**What is absent.** No acquisitions, divestitures, restatements, auditor changes, impairments or covenant amendments in the 8-K record. Legal proceedings are ordinary-course with no material exposure asserted. That absence of tail-event risk is why the risk score is 4.8 rather than lower.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($330, +22%).** Q4 delivers the guided 5.5–5.9% GAAP operating margin, confirming that Q3's 4.7% was ramp inefficiency plus the one-time $12.9M acceleration rather than structural mix dilution. FY2027 guidance in November frames revenue growth above 12% with explicit margin expansion, and the working capital unwinds into $200M+ of free cash flow as ramps mature, validating management's "meaningful free cash flow generation in early fiscal 2027." Semi-cap share gains prove structural, the $4.5B funnel converts at historical rates, and the data-center BESS program scales. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $11.00–11.25; the market pays 29–30x for demonstrated cash conversion. This matches the high analyst target of $330.

**Bear case ($185, -32%).** Semi-cap orders digest in calendar 2027 after two years of equipment build, Industrial reverses from 45% of revenue, and a demand pause strands $1.3B of raw materials purchased against forecasts. Gross margin cannot expand because the incremental business carries lower value-add than the healthcare book it displaced (the Q3 10-Q's own "shift in customer mix" as a negative), the FX hedge cushion is gone, and the tax rate steps up further with Pillar Two phase-in. One or two of the top-10 customers — now 55% of revenue with no volume commitments — pauses a ramp. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS lands near $8.25 and the multiple compresses to 22x as the EMS cohort de-rates.

**Base case ($270, flat).** FY2026 finishes near $4.89B of revenue with non-GAAP EPS of $8.60–$8.76. FY2027 grows revenue 12–15% with modest margin expansion and free cash flow turning positive but not dramatically — call it $10.25 of non-GAAP EPS. The market pays 26x, which is generous relative to Plexus's history and consistent with the current EMS re-rating. That produces approximately $266, essentially the current price. Probability-weighted at 50/25/25 (base/bull/bear), expected return is modestly negative at roughly -2.5%.

The single most important disconfirming evidence for the bear case: cash-cycle days improved to 62 from 69, the best in over five years, and days in inventory fell to 116 from 128. The inventory build is proportionally smaller than the revenue growth, which indicates pre-funded growth rather than deteriorating discipline and argues against treating the negative FCF as an accounting or demand warning. The single most important disconfirming evidence for the bull case: gross margin did not move a single basis point through a 28% revenue increase, and roughly 30% of the nine-month gross-profit gain came from hedge settlements that will not recur at that scale.

Management quality scores 4.6 on forward-looking evidence. Across every Form 4 from February through July 2026 there were zero open-market purchases by any officer or director, and every discretionary transaction was a sale. CEO Kelsey reduced reported direct holdings roughly 31% (97,324 to 67,388 shares) across at least fifteen tranches at $194–$296, separately from code-F tax withholding; COO Mihm reduced holdings roughly 48%. Much of this is consistent with routine post-vesting diversification after the February 17, 2026 PSU settlement, and executives sell for taxes and liquidity, so I draw no inference about intrinsic value. What the record establishes is that nobody chose to add at these prices, and the reductions exceed what withholding required. Repurchases of $64.1M at an average of $186.25 (rising to $286.24 in the final Q3 month) occurred alongside -$35.3M of free cash flow and a $132M increase in revolver draw, leaving only $21.4M of the $100M authorization. The five-year pay-versus-performance table shows a TSR index of 202 against a self-selected peer index of 314, with CEO total compensation of $10.84M in FY2025, up 29% from FY2024. Offsetting positives: the incentive metric set (economic return, relative TSR, ROIC, revenue) is well-chosen for an EMS business, the June 2026 revolver refinancing was timely, and guidance has been beaten on every line for three of four quarters.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** — approximately fair risk-adjusted return from $270.35. Existing owners maintain exposure; new capital has no entry signal here.

**Base case target: $266–275.** Arithmetic: 26x applied to FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $10.25 (consensus $10.01 plus continued modest positive revision) = $266. Cross-check at 28x on current consensus $10.01 = $280. Midpoint of the band is roughly spot, or 0% to +2%.

**Bull case target: $330 (+22%).** 29–30x on FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $11.00–11.25, requires demonstrated positive free cash flow and non-GAAP operating margin above 6.5%. Matches the high published analyst target.

**Bear case target: $185 (-32%).** 22x on FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $8.25 following a semi-cap order pause and excess-inventory absorption.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to two dated events — the Q4 FY2026 report and initial FY2027 guidance in late October / early November 2026, and the Q1 FY2027 report in late January 2027 that tests the "return to meaningful free cash flow generation in early fiscal 2027" statement.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow above $150M with positive free cash flow after capex, confirming the working-capital unwind — the single highest-value confirmation available.
2. Gross margin above 10.5% for two consecutive quarters without an FX hedge reclassification contribution above $3M, demonstrating that ramp inefficiency was temporary rather than mix-driven.
3. A price in the $215–230 range on unchanged FY2027 estimates, which would put the stock at 21–23x forward and restore a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Industrial segment revenue declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters, or the funnel contracting from $4.5B — the leading indicator of semi-cap digestion.
2. Inventory rising sequentially while revenue is flat or down, or days in inventory reversing above 128, which would signal a forecast-driven build without demand behind it.
3. Top-10 customer concentration exceeding 60%, or disclosure of any single customer above 10% of revenue combined with a program pause.
4. Revolver utilization above $300M (60%) while free cash flow remains negative, or a further authorization funded on the revolver at current prices.
5. FY2027 effective tax rate guided above 20%.

**Reassessment date: the Q4 FY2026 report and FY2027 guidance, expected late October / early November 2026.** That release resolves whether the guided 5.5–5.9% GAAP operating margin materialized, whether the FY2027 revenue framing hardens into a range above 12%, and whether management repeats the early-FY2027 free-cash-flow commitment. For existing holders, trimming into strength above $300 is defensible on the arithmetic above; a move to $215–230 on intact estimates would warrant an upgrade rather than an exit.

## Conclusion

Plexus is executing well and being paid fully for it. The revenue acceleration is verified in filings, the beat pattern is consistent, estimate revisions are positive across all four horizons, and analyst sentiment is improving from a low base. This is a third consecutive HOLD rather than a downgrade to REDUCE because the risk/reward improved marginally even as the absolute level remains unattractive for new capital. It is classified as uncertain because the decisive question — how much of the semi-cap ramp is durable share gain versus order-cycle timing — is one management explicitly does not quantify, and the November guidance plus the January cash flow statement will answer it better than any inference available today.
