**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Horizon has converted the collapse of its 2023 TD Bank acquisition into a disciplined standalone-execution story. Net income available to common rose 38% to $956M ($1.87 diluted) in 2025, driven by 12bps of NIM expansion to 3.47%, a lower credit provision ($65M vs. $150M), and the non-repeat of a 2024 securities-restructuring loss. The improvement carried into 2026: Q1 net income to common of $257M (+21% YoY) with ROTCE of 15.1%, and Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.54 (+20% YoY) beating consensus, with NIM improving to 3.52% and loans up roughly $2B. This is genuine operating leverage from deposit-cost repricing after Fed cuts, fewer criticized loans, and a countercyclical fixed-income franchise, rather than reserve releases or accounting engineering. ROTCE holding above 15% for three consecutive quarters confirms durability.

The problem is that the market has already recognized most of this. At $25.51, FHN trades at ~12.3x trailing EPS and roughly 1.8x tangible book value of $14.20/share, near its 52-week high of $26.56 and only modestly below the ~$25/share TD deal price from 2023. The trailing P/E sits fractionally below the regional-bank peer median of 12.7x, so there is no meaningful valuation discount to exploit. Consensus estimates are drifting lower across every forward window — current-quarter EPS revised down 4.3% over 90 days and next-year down 2.0% — a negative earnings-momentum signal that argues against paying a premium multiple.

We rate FHN a HOLD (growth_hold). The 12-month case rests on continued EPS accretion from a ~7%-per-year share-count reduction ($1.2B authorization running to Jan 2027) and a well-covered 2.6% dividend, not on multiple expansion. The main tensions are a declining CET1 cushion (11.20% → 10.63% → ~10.53%) funding those buybacks, concentrated Florida CRE and property-insurance exposure, and a financial-services loan concentration unique to FHN's book. We would upgrade on a valuation reset toward tangible book or a positive turn in estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

First Horizon is a $83.9B-asset regional bank holding company headquartered in Memphis, whose sole banking subsidiary is First Horizon Bank. It operates 450+ locations across 23 states, concentrated in the Sun Belt (Tennessee, Florida, Texas, North Carolina, Louisiana), with ~7,400 employees. Its asset base sits meaningfully below the $100B regulatory tier that would trigger enhanced prudential standards.

The business runs across three segments. Commercial, Consumer & Wealth is the core engine, generating $1.5B of pre-tax income in 2025 across commercial banking, private banking, consumer, small business, and wealth/trust. Wholesale ($157M pre-tax) houses two distinctive specialty franchises: FHN Financial, a fixed-income sales and trading operation that performs counter-cyclically when rates fall, and mortgage-company warehouse lending. The Corporate segment ($417M pre-tax loss) holds treasury, funding, and unallocated items.

The $64.2B loan book is 56% C&I, 21% CRE, 22% consumer real estate, and 1% credit card/other, funded by $67.5B in deposits (95% loans-to-deposits). Book value per share is $17.53; tangible book value is $14.20. The two specialty franchises give FHN more fee and countercyclical diversification than a plain-vanilla regional bank, which matters in a falling-rate environment where NIM is under pressure.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 recovery was high-quality. Net interest income rose to $2.62B from $2.51B. The mechanism was straightforward: the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 57bps as management repriced deposits after Fed cuts, while earning-asset yields fell only 26bps, a favorable spread that is the single biggest earnings lever. Noninterest income rebounded $118M to $797M, largely because 2024 had been depressed by a $91M securities loss, with underlying fixed income (+$19M) and mortgage banking (+$8M) also improving. The efficiency ratio improved to 60.66% from 62.06%, and expenses grew a controlled 2% YoY.

Credit quality is solid and improving. The provision decline was driven by declines in criticized and classified loans rather than reserve raids; the allowance-to-loans ratio remains a healthy 1.31%. Net charge-offs were 0.19%, essentially flat and low. ROA rose to 1.22% and ROTCE to 14.01%, both approaching or exceeding historical peaks.

The 2026 momentum is confirmed by earnings delivery. FHN has beaten consensus four straight quarters (2.6% to 13.8% surprises), with Q1 2026 EPS of $0.53 (vs. $0.41 prior year) and Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.54 on revenue of $887M (+6.4%). Tangible book value per share grew 9% YoY in Q1.

The balance sheet is well-capitalized: total equity of $9.1B, tangible common equity to tangible assets of 8.37%, and $32.6B of available liquidity against $28.1B of uninsured deposits (42% of total, 8% collateralized). The self-run DFAST severely-adverse stress test showed capital remaining well above adequately-capitalized standards.

Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-friendly. In 2025, FHN repurchased $918M of common (~7% of shares, basic count falling from 540M to 505M), paid $330M in dividends, and redeemed $80M of Series B preferred. The $1.2B authorization runs through January 2027, and Q1 2026 buybacks of $233M were partly funded by a $400M Series H preferred issuance, active management of the capital stack. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.17 in 2026.

The one point of tension is the CET1 trajectory, a deliberate consequence of the buyback pace and preferred redemption, and the Series H issuance signals management is topping up capital to sustain both growth and returns. The cushion remains comfortable, but it is being drawn down.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is moderate and rate-dependent rather than structural. NIM is inflecting higher, and the Sun Belt footprint provides above-average population and economic tailwinds. FHN Financial's fixed-income revenue grew to $206M in 2025, and mortgage-warehouse balances reached $4.7B (13% of C&I), both franchises that cushion NIM compression when rates fall or the curve steepens.

The forward estimate picture is softening, which is the material caution. Consensus current-quarter EPS was revised down 4.3% over both 30 and 90 days (to $0.52), next-quarter down 2.5%, current-year down 0.8% (to $2.13), and next-year down 2.0% (to $2.31). There is no acceleration in the estimate revision cadence, and the trend is broadly negative. The four-quarter beat streak partially offsets this by suggesting the numbers may be conservative, but the direction of revisions still points to decelerating rather than accelerating earnings momentum.

The realistic 12-month EPS path is roughly $2.13 (2026) toward $2.31 (2027), with per-share growth meaningfully amplified by the buyback. On a ~505M-and-falling share count, retiring 7% of shares annually adds several points of EPS growth on top of low-single-digit revenue growth. The growth thesis is therefore more a capital-return story than an organic-expansion story, which is appropriate for a bank of this profile but does not justify a growth premium.

## Valuation Assessment

FHN trades at ~12.3x forward EPS, versus the regional-bank peer median of 12.7x (WBS 12.0x, WTFC 13.1x, UMBF 11.8x, CFR 14.2x, ONB 12.7x). FHN is priced slightly below its peer set on P/E, but not at a meaningful discount. At roughly 1.8x TBV, the stock carries a full multiple for a regional generating ~14-15% ROTCE; it prices the recent profitability improvement rather than offering upside to it.

The stock is up ~15% over the trailing year, having recovered essentially to the old TD deal price. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but the combination of a roughly peer-median multiple, a full price-to-tangible-book, and falling estimate revisions means the market is not mispricing this name in either direction. Cheapness has been anti-predictive in this environment, and there is no cheapness to lean on regardless. FHN is a fairly-valued, quality regional rather than a value opportunity.

There is no earnings inflection that would justify overriding the trailing multiple with normalized power. ROTCE is already at cycle-normal-to-peak levels of 14-15%, so this is not a trough-earnings situation. The dividend yield is slightly above the peer median (2.2%) and well-covered, providing yield support that caps downside. Applying ~12.5x to a blended forward EPS of ~$2.15-2.25 yields a fair value in the $27-28 range, consistent with the analyst consensus (mean $27.95, median $28.00, low $25, high $30). That implies modest upside to base case with the dividend, insufficient to warrant a BUY at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

FHN's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its differentiators versus peer regionals are real: FHN Financial provides a genuinely countercyclical fixed-income revenue stream that rises when rates fall, and the mortgage-warehouse book is a high-yield specialty with a historically zero charge-off record. Both give FHN earnings that are less purely rate-directional than a typical regional. The Sun Belt footprint carries structural demographic tailwinds, and five-year cumulative TSR of 222.7 versus the peer group's 152.7 demonstrates franchise value creation.

The offsetting pressures are the same ones facing the entire sector: intensified deposit competition since the 2023 regional-bank failures, and a newly emphasized competitive threat from fintech, stablecoin issuers, blockchain payment networks, and digital-asset lending platforms, which FHN's FY2025 10-K explicitly names. These are longer-dated erosion risks rather than near-term share losses. FHN is neither visibly gaining nor losing share; it is holding a differentiated position in attractive markets. Moody's upgrade to a Positive outlook (June 2025) and Fitch's stable BBB+ corroborate a franchise that is not deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**Funding and deposit risk.** With 42% of deposits uninsured, FHN carries the standard post-SVB funding-mix scrutiny. Management countered "significant but modest" 2023 run-off with a deposit campaign, and available liquidity covers uninsured deposits. NIM is asset-sensitive but only modestly so — a −200bps rate shock reduces NII by 4.5%.

**Financial-services concentration (idiosyncratic).** Roughly 25% of the C&I book is tied to the financial-services industry: mortgage-company warehouse lending (13%) and finance & insurance (12%, including $1.7B of asset-based lending to consumer finance companies). This makes FHN more sensitive to rate/yield-curve moves and financial-sector stress than a typical regional. The warehouse book has a historical 0% charge-off rate but volatile balances.

**CRE and Florida concentration.** CRE is $13.6B (21% of loans), down 6% in 2025, with office at ~$2.7B (20% of CRE) and multi-family at $4.5B (33%). CRE nonperforming ratio is 1.76% and CRE allowance coverage of NPLs is only 74%, the weakest coverage in the book. Florida represents 16% of deposits, 26% of CRE, and 29% of consumer real estate. The 10-K devotes unusual attention to Florida property-insurance instability — carrier exits, rising hurricane costs, premium spikes — which could blunt the migration-driven growth that makes the footprint attractive and raise loan-loss risk. This is a concentrated, footprint-specific vulnerability.

**Capital cushion.** The declining CET1 is a self-inflicted consequence of buybacks; it remains well-capitalized, but leaves less room for absorbing a credit shock without pausing repurchases.

**$100B threshold.** At $83.9B and growing, FHN is approaching the Category IV enhanced-prudential tier and is already bearing some compliance costs (legal/professional fees +34%, software +14% in 2025). The severity depends on the direction of regulation, which may loosen.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-mildly-cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 26.5% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 25.6% (0.9% spread, 21st percentile within the chain); the market is pricing no unusual near-term event, and IV has compressed from 33.8% a month ago following the July 15 earnings print. The term structure is in contango (26.5% near vs. 30.2% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The 1-month put/call skew of 5.1% shows modest elevated put demand, but the put/call volume ratio of 0.04 indicates negligible put activity overall. Six-month skew inverts to −2.2%. There is no material bearish positioning to challenge the fundamental view, and no cheap-IV entry signal either; the options market corroborates a fairly-priced, low-drama name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$30, +18%).** Deposit-cost repricing continues to expand NIM, loan growth holds near mid-single digits, and ROTCE sustains above 15%. The buyback retires another ~7% of shares, driving EPS toward $2.35+. Estimate revisions turn positive as the four-quarter beat streak forces consensus higher, and the stock re-rates toward 13x on rising EPS. A steepening curve boosts FHN Financial fixed-income revenue. Optionally, FHN's clean Sun Belt franchise attracts renewed M&A interest at a premium.

**Bear case (~$20, −22%).** Florida property-insurance stress and CRE/office softening push charge-offs and provisions higher, compressing ROTCE back toward the 11-12% range. Deposit competition re-intensifies, NIM stalls or reverses, and the declining CET1 cushion forces management to slow buybacks — removing the primary EPS driver. Falling estimate revisions continue, and a peer-median multiple applied to lower EPS takes the stock toward tangible-book-plus, near the $19.80 52-week low.

**Base case (~$27-28, +6-10% with dividend).** ROTCE holds in the 14-15% range, EPS lands near $2.15-2.25, buybacks continue at a moderated pace, and the multiple stays around 12.5x. The dividend adds 2.6%. This is the analyst-consensus outcome and the most likely path — modest total return dominated by capital return rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, with the next natural checkpoints being the Q3 2026 earnings release (October) and each subsequent quarterly print for NIM, CET1, and CRE credit trends.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A valuation reset toward tangible book — a pullback to the low-$20s (~$21-22, roughly 1.5x TBV) would restore asymmetric upside.
- A positive turn in estimate revisions, specifically current-year consensus rising above $2.15 and next-year above $2.35, confirming the beat streak is pulling consensus higher rather than the reverse.
- CET1 stabilizing at or above ~10.5% while ROTCE holds above 15%, demonstrating the capital-return model is sustainable without cushion erosion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE or office net charge-offs accelerating materially (CRE NPL ratio rising back above 2.0% with allowance coverage below 70%), signaling credit deterioration in the weakest-covered part of the book.
- A Florida property-insurance or hurricane-driven loss event that impairs the consumer real estate or CRE book.
- CET1 falling below ~10.0% forcing a buyback halt, which would remove the primary EPS-growth lever.

## Conclusion

First Horizon has executed a credible standalone recovery from the 2023 TD Bank collapse, with real NIM expansion, disciplined credit management, ROTCE sustained above 15%, and an aggressive, EPS-accretive buyback. The franchise is differentiated by countercyclical fixed income plus zero-loss warehouse lending in growth markets, and management has shown genuine pay-for-performance alignment (negative CEO comp-actually-paid in the 2023 downturn) and shareholder-friendly capital allocation.

The issue is price. At ~12.3x earnings, ~1.8x tangible book, and near its 52-week high, the stock already reflects the operating improvement, trades at no meaningful discount to peers, and carries softening forward estimates. The concentrated Florida and financial-services exposures and a drawing-down CET1 cushion are real, if manageable, offsets. The durable return here is the buyback plus a well-covered 2.6% dividend, not a re-rating. We rate FHN a HOLD, would look to add on a pullback toward tangible book or a positive estimate-revision inflection, and would step aside on CRE credit deterioration or a forced buyback pause.
