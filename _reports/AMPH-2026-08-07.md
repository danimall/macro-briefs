## Executive Summary

Amphastar is a vertically integrated manufacturer of technically difficult injectables, inhalation products and API, with three overlapping franchises: the BAQSIMI nasal-glucagon asset bought from Eli Lilly in 2023, the Primatene MIST OTC epinephrine inhaler, and a book of hard-to-copy generics plus insulin API. Trailing twelve-month revenue is $730.0M and TTM GAAP net income $78.5M ($1.85 per current share). The equity has fallen 36.7% from its September 2025 high of $31.26, and prior coverage has carried an AVOID with the composite score declining from 5.5 to 4.1.

Two developments change the balance of evidence. First, Q2 2026 (reported August 6) showed revenue of $183.9M (+5.4% YoY), gross margin recovering to 50.8% from 41.1% in Q1 2026, operating income of $39.7M, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $0.91 against a $0.66 non-GAAP consensus — a 43.9% beat. The Q1 margin collapse, which drove much of the February–May derating, was substantially a mix-and-absorption timing event rather than a new run-rate; new launches (ipratropium bromide HFA at $8.4M in its first partial quarter, plus teriparatide and iron sucrose) contributed higher-margin volume. Second, capital allocation has been accretive: shares outstanding fell from 47,617,691 at 12/31/24 to 42,532,225 at 6/30/26, a 10.7% reduction, with Q2 repurchases averaging roughly $19.58 — below the current basis.

What has not changed is the shape of the underlying business. Glucagon revenue fell 49% in H1 ($21.1M vs. $41.4M; management attributes $13.7M to price and states declines will continue), BAQSIMI fell 8% on a $15.9M price decline against $8.8M of volume growth, and the combined glucagon-rescue franchise contracted 21.7% year over year. The chargeback-and-rebate provision rose 52% to $284.7M while net revenue rose 3%. Consensus EPS has been cut 11.4% for the current year and 14.2% for next year over 90 days. The July 2 FDA Warning Letter at the IMS South El Monte facility carries a disclosed cost consequence ("manufacturing expenses are expected to increase") and an unquantified pipeline consequence, since an Official Action Indicated classification conventionally blocks approvals tied to the site and the company has one ANDA and one biosimilar insulin candidate on file. The buyback authorization is down to $0.4M with $100M owed to Lilly in Q3.

At 7.8x current-year consensus, 6.5-7.4x EV/EBITDA and a mid-teens normalized FCF yield, the discount is real but so are its reasons. My probability-weighted value is approximately $21 against a $19.81 basis — enough to reverse the AVOID, not enough to buy.

## Company Overview

Amphastar reports as a single segment and sells more than 30 prescription and OTC products. The portfolio splits into:

- **BAQSIMI (nasal glucagon)** — $45.5M in Q2, $77.9M in H1 (21.9% of H1 net revenue). Acquired from Lilly (closed June 30, 2023). Gross intangible cost basis $685.8M, net book value $611.9M at 6/30/26, roughly 35% of total assets and 80% of stockholders' equity. Manufactured by a contract manufacturer, so Amphastar does not control this supply chain.
- **Primatene MIST** — $50.8M in H1, supported by $5.8M of advertising. The only OTC epinephrine inhaler in the U.S.
- **Difficult generics and API** — epinephrine (vials and prefilled syringes), lidocaine, legacy glucagon kit, naloxone, albuterol HFA, ipratropium HFA (approved February 2026, launched April 2026), iron sucrose, teriparatide, phytonadione, sodium bicarbonate, dextrose, plus insulin API from France and China.

Two structural features govern how this business must be modeled. Backlog at 6/30/26 was $4.2M and management states it "has not been a meaningful indicator in any given period"; there is no material deferred revenue or remaining-performance-obligation disclosure. Amphastar has essentially no contracted forward revenue, so every forecast is a price-times-volume construction. Three distributors accounted for 64% of H1 net revenue (Cencora 25%, McKesson 22%, Cardinal 17%) versus 66% a year earlier — flat concentration. Six new products launched between August 2024 and April 2026 broadened the *product* mix; customer concentration did not improve, and receivable concentration rose (Cencora 30% from 28%, McKesson 29% from 28%).

## Financial Analysis

**The multi-year arc.** Net revenue: $644.4M (FY2023) → $732.0M (FY2024) → $719.9M (FY2025, -1.7%). Net income: $137.5M → $159.5M → $98.1M (-38.5%). FY2025 operating margin fell to 19.5% from 28.1%; gross margin to 49.5% from 51.1%. Roughly $23.1M of the FY2025 earnings decline was a one-time net litigation charge (October 22, 2025 jury verdict of $34.1M less ~$11M expected insurance recovery), which explains most of the $29.2M increase in G&A to $85.9M. The remainder was pricing and mix erosion in glucagon and BAQSIMI.

**H1 2026 and the Q1/Q2 swing.**

| | Q1 2026 (derived) | Q2 2026 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | $171.2M | $183.9M | $355.1M | $344.9M |
| Gross margin | 41.1% | 50.8% | 46.1% | 49.8% |
| Operating income | $13.6M | $39.7M | $53.4M | $79.5M |
| GAAP diluted EPS | ~$0.14 | $0.67 | $0.81 | $1.15 |
| Non-GAAP diluted EPS | ~$0.42 | $0.91 | $1.33 | $1.59 |

The ~970bp Q1 gross-margin compression on flat revenue cut operating income roughly 63% and was the proximate cause of the February 27 (-24% session) and May 8 (-21% session) declines. Q2's recovery to 50.8% is the single most important new data point in this cycle. The mechanism is consistent: higher-margin launch volume (ipratropium, teriparatide, iron sucrose) plus higher phytonadione volume, against the same ASP pressure present in Q1. The residual driver of the Q1 anomaly appears to be manufacturing cost absorption at the newly expanded Rancho Cucamonga footprint — segment "other items" including absorption and inventory provisions rose from $78.2M to $94.4M in H1, and NRV charges were $4.9M in H1 with only $2.7M in Q2. I cannot fully attribute Q1 from the filings; a gross-to-net accrual true-up concentrated in Q1 remains a plausible alternative, and recurring idle-capacity cost cannot be ruled out.

**Gross-to-net is the quantifiable pressure point.** The provision for chargebacks and rebates was $284.7M in H1 2026 versus $187.3M in H1 2025 — up 52% while net revenue rose 3%. Adding back only this deduction implies gross revenue of ~$639.8M versus ~$532.2M, so this single line moved from ~35.2% to ~44.5% of that figure. This excludes returns, prompt-pay, distributor fees and co-pay assistance, so it is directional rather than a complete GTN rate. Management's own BAQSIMI language cites "changes in chargebacks and rebates *and changes to the customer mix*," so part of the move is channel mix rather than pure list erosion. Either way, incremental gross dollars are converting to fewer net dollars.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $99.2M, capex $18.1M, FCF $81.1M. FY2025: OCF $156.1M, capex $34.9M, FCF $121.2M (14.4% FCF yield on the current $842.7M cap). H1 2026 should not be annualized: income taxes were *refunded* $3.1M versus $22.3M *paid* a year earlier, a $14.8M swing, and the prior-year base carried a $35.9M BAQSIMI inventory build. Normalizing the tax item alone puts H1 OCF nearer $84M and H1 FCF nearer $66M. Accounts payable and accrued liabilities rose $103.0M on the balance sheet but contributed only $11.6M to operating cash flow — the difference is chiefly the $100.0M BAQSIMI milestone accrual, capitalized to intangibles ($94.5M) and PP&E ($5.5M). When that $100M is paid in Q3 2026 it will most likely appear as an investing outflow, and Q3 free cash flow will be sharply negative.

**Balance sheet (6/30/26).** Cash and equivalents $223.6M, short-term investments plus restricted $68.9M, total liquid $292.6M. Debt: $345.0M 2.0% convertible notes due 3/15/2029; $250.0M Wells Fargo term loan due June 2028 (swapped to fixed); $25.0M ICBC facility due November 2033; $620.2M total principal. Equity $768.2M; working capital $367.8M (down $110.1M from year-end on the milestone accrual). Net debt $327.6M; including the accrued milestone, adjusted net obligation $427.6M, or **2.49x TTM EBITDA of $171.4M**. Convertible conversion price is $62.96 with principal cash-settled, so at $19.81 it contributes effectively zero dilution and functions as a $345M cash maturity. The notes traded at $313.3M fair value (90.8% of par) at 6/30/26 — consistent with prevailing rates plus modest refinancing concern, not distress.

The **maturity wall is $595M across June 2028 and March 2029** against $292.6M of liquid assets, $100M of which is committed to Lilly. Replacing $345M of 2.0% paper at 7% adds roughly $17M of annual pre-tax interest; refinancing the full $595M at 7-8% versus the current blended cost could add roughly $20M pre-tax, near $0.38 per share after tax, or about 15% of current-year consensus EPS. Recomputing Altman Z on 6/30/26 book data gives ~1.98 versus ~2.32 at year-end — mechanically worse because the $100M milestone reduced working capital while the offset was capitalized as a long-lived intangible. I read ~2.0 as a refinancing flag, not a solvency signal, given no maturity inside 24 months and current ratio 4.02.

**Earnings surprise quality.** The four-quarter record is 2 beats, 2 misses: +10.6%, -22.4%, -41.6%, +43.9%. Visibility is poor. Within the Q2 beat, $2.1M of pre-tax result came from mark-to-market gains on the interest-rate swap in other income, and total non-operating expense improved $1.7M YoY while operating income *declined* $2.5M YoY. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.67 is coincidentally close to the $0.66 non-GAAP consensus; the beat is non-GAAP versus non-GAAP.

Financial health scores **5.5**: real cash generation at 46-51% gross margin, 2.5x adjusted leverage, ample near-term liquidity and no maturity inside two years, against a $595M refinancing wall at materially higher coupons, a deteriorating Z-score, a $100M near-term outflow, and an exhausted buyback authorization.

## Growth Analysis

H1 revenue grew 2.9% and Q2 5.4%. Management guided in February to mid- to high-single-digit 2026 revenue growth and reaffirmed the full-year outlook in May while acknowledging BAQSIMI rebate pressure. To reach 5% full-year growth off $719.9M, H2 revenue must be roughly $400M, about +13% versus H2 2025's ~$375M. Ipratropium's first full quarters plus teriparatide and iron sucrose ramps make that arithmetically reachable, but it leaves no room for the Warning Letter remediation to reduce IMS output.

Consensus is moving the other way: current quarter $0.75 (-5.7% in 30 days, -14.2% in 90), next quarter $0.74 (-18.0% in 90), current year $2.55 (-11.4%), next year $2.89 (-14.2%). Seven analysts, uniformly negative direction. The revision signal argues that the Q2 beat has not yet changed the trajectory of forward estimates, and the beat itself post-dates the last revision marks.

Composition matters more than the aggregate. Declining: legacy glucagon (management says declines continue), BAQSIMI net price, epinephrine multi-dose vials (-$5.8M). Non-durable: epinephrine prefilled syringe (+$6.1M), phytonadione, sodium bicarbonate and dextrose gains, all attributed by management to "other supplier shortages" that "will continue to fluctuate depending on the ability of our competitors to supply market demands." Growing durably: inhalation (Primatene, albuterol HFA, ipratropium HFA) plus the 2025-26 launch cohort. The pipeline includes one ANDA and one biosimilar insulin on file and several programs in development, but the cited "$6.3B of aggregate market size" is branded addressable market sourced to IQVIA, not an Amphastar revenue opportunity; generic entrants typically capture a modest share at a large discount.

Cost of the pivot is visible: H1 R&D rose 22% to $48.9M with clinical-trial expense up 346% to $5.1M, and management says costs "will increase significantly over the next several quarters and years."

Growth scores **4.0** — a slight improvement on the prior 3.5 for the Q2 revenue and margin evidence, capped by the revision trend and the shortage-dependent share of the increment.

## Competitive Landscape

The clearest competitive dynamic is self-inflicted and unavoidable: Amphastar's legacy lyophilized glucagon kit is being displaced by ready-to-use formats, and management names BAQSIMI as the displacing category. Glucagon fell $20.4M in H1 while BAQSIMI fell $7.1M, so the combined rescue franchise contracted $27.5M (-21.7%). The acquisition slowed but did not reverse the erosion.

BAQSIMI's issue is payer leverage rather than clinical demand: H1 volume added $8.8M while price removed $15.9M on chargebacks, rebates and customer mix. A ~$900M-revenue specialty company appears to be re-contracting a brand at worse net prices than Lilly achieved. Management said in May it is addressing rebate and discount pressure. A partial one-time reset of gross-to-net accruals on assuming full commercial responsibility is a plausible alternative reading that would not compound; the 52% jump in the chargeback provision at the aggregate level argues the pressure is broader than one product.

Where the competitive trajectory is improving: metered-dose inhalation. Albuterol HFA (August 2024) and ipratropium HFA are genuinely hard ANDAs, and Primatene MIST is the only OTC epinephrine inhaler in the U.S. The ability to capture shortage revenue across epinephrine PFS, phytonadione and sodium bicarbonate is evidence of manufacturing reliability, which is the company's stated moat.

The Warning Letter attacks that moat directly. An FDA compliance action at a subsidiary manufacturing site undercuts the "we can make what others cannot" claim at the moment the company is asking investors to pay for pipeline conversion. Competitive position scores **4.0** — unchanged from prior coverage, with inhalation gains and glucagon/injectable losses roughly offsetting and the compliance event tilting the balance negative.

## Risk Assessment

**FDA Warning Letter (IMS, South El Monte).** The only substantive risk-factor change in the Q2 10-Q. Timeline: inspection December 2025; 483 response January 2026; OAI classification April 2026; Warning Letter July 2, 2026; 8-K July 8. Mitigants disclosed: no requirement to halt manufacturing or distribution; one immaterial product voluntarily suspended in consultation with FDA's Drug Shortage Staff; no anticipated material adverse effect. Concerning language, verbatim: manufacturing expenses "are expected to increase as we remediate," IMS sales may be lower with "a slowdown in production," and until deficiencies are resolved "additional regulatory or legal action may be taken without further notice." The four-month gap between OAI and the letter implies the January response did not satisfy FDA. I could not verify which products or pending applications are tied to the site, which leaves the revenue and pipeline exposure unsized. This is the largest single unquantified risk in the file.

**Intangible impairment.** $611.9M net BAQSIMI product rights (post the $94.5M milestone capitalization) against $768.2M of equity, amortized over a remaining 21-year life, on a product with declining GAAP revenue. A sustained net-price decline would pressure the recoverability test; an impairment would not affect cash but would compress book equity and could interact with undisclosed debt covenants. Covenant levels and headroom are not disclosed — a verification gap.

**Contingent consideration, $1.1B disclosed maximum.** Lilly remaining up to $475M (including two possible $100M payments at $200M annual Milestone Product net sales, $150M at $950M cumulative five-year sales, and up to $125M of assumed contingent consideration at annual net sales thresholds of $350M/$400M/$600M); Anji up to $267M ($42M development + $225M sales milestones) plus capped royalties; Hanxin up to $89M ($14M development + $75M sales milestones) plus capped royalties. The FY2026 contract year closed at exactly $175.0M, triggering the $100M payment due in Q3. Reaching $200M requires ~14% growth in a product whose realized price is falling, so the next milestone looks unlikely near term, but success in resetting gross-to-net would itself trigger a $100M cash outflow.

**Buyback exhaustion.** Cumulative authorization $485.0M with $0.4M remaining at 6/30/26. H1 repurchases were 3.66M shares for $74.2M (April $21.09, May $18.78, June $19.43). With $100M due to Lilly and a $595M maturity wall, a board could reasonably prioritize deleveraging over re-authorization. Removing that bid is a real near-term change in the demand picture for a stock with 500K average daily volume, disclosed only in a footnote.

**Pledged shares.** Drs. Zhang and Luo have pledged 5,700,000 shares (13.4% of shares outstanding) securing up to $57M of personal/affiliate credit lines, with UBS holding "an unlimited and unilateral right to call each of the credit lines for any reason whatsoever." Amphastar is not a party and does not guarantee the loans; the risk is mechanical forced selling. The company's policy caps pledges at the lower of 60% of an individual's holdings or 15% of outstanding shares, and buybacks shrinking the denominator have pushed the ratio toward that cap.

**Related-party concentration.** Amphastar owns 11.5% of Nanjing Hanxin, whose general manager and chairman is the CEO's son and in which the CEO and Chairman hold interests through an affiliate. Amphastar is simultaneously a minority owner of, contract manufacturer for, research customer of, licensee from, and supplier customer of Hanxin-affiliated entities, including a January 2026 corticotropin license carrying up to $89M of milestones plus capped royalties. Current dollar amounts are small ($2.0M upfront, ~$1.1M of revenue). Cost-plus pricing and in-licensing from a partner developer are commercially ordinary; I have no evidence of unfair pricing. The concern is the expanding number of arrangements concentrated on one family and the burden this places on board oversight.

**Other.** Three California wage-and-hour/PAGA actions with $1.0M accrued; $107.3M of long-lived assets in China generating $7.0M of H1 revenue and tariff exposure on imported APIs and components; MannKind's disclosed two-year pause on purchasing RHI API (French revenue nonetheless +11% in H1, so the P&L impact is unresolved).

Risk factors score **3.5**: the Warning Letter, the intangible concentration, the exhausted buyback, the pledge mechanic and the governance structure are all specific, and at least two (site exposure, covenant headroom) are unquantified in the filings.

## Management Quality

The strongest positive is buyback execution: 10.7% of shares retired in 18 months, at an average in Q2 of roughly $19.58, below the current basis. That is the main reason per-share results have held up better than aggregate results, and it exceeds the company's stated anti-dilution objective.

Against that: no open-market insider purchases in the Form 4 record from September 2025 through July 2026, while the stock fell roughly 32% and the company itself was buying at $18.78-$21.09. Insiders already hold large positions (~26.2% aggregate) and several have pledged shares, so this is better characterized as the absence of a positive signal than a bearish one. Two directors exercised and sold in late 2025 ($25.27-$30.98), which appears to be option housekeeping. CEO grant-date compensation rose to $8,309,350 from $7,734,759 in a year when the company-selected performance measure (sales growth) printed -1.7% and net income fell 38.5%; "compensation actually paid" was $2,413,870, so realized pay did reflect the decline, and grants sized in shares mechanically inflate grant-date value when the price falls. Equity overhang: 8.06M options at a $24.71 weighted-average strike (mostly underwater; aggregate intrinsic value only $11.33M), 1.49M RSUs, 4.56M plan reserve, 0.44M ESPP — a maximum contingent 14.54M shares, equal to 34.2% of existing shares or 25.5% post-issuance ownership dilution. That maximum assumes full exercise and full grant of the reserve, neither likely, and buybacks have more than offset actual issuance to date.

The BAQSIMI acquisition is the defining capital-allocation decision and its verdict is mixed: it bought the format cannibalizing the company's own glucagon kit, but realized price is eroding, the asset now consumes 80% of book equity, and it has just triggered a $100M cash payment. Management quality scores **4.5**.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($29).** Q2's 50.8% gross margin is the sustainable base; ipratropium annualizes above $30M with teriparatide and iron sucrose adding, taking H2 revenue to ~$400M and validating mid-single-digit full-year growth. Management's BAQSIMI gross-to-net actions stabilize realized price with volume still growing. Warning Letter remediation costs prove contained and no approvals are blocked. Normalized non-GAAP EPS reaches $3.10-3.20; the board re-authorizes repurchases after the Lilly payment. At 9x, $28-29, roughly 45% above the basis.

**Bear case ($14).** Remediation slows IMS production and blocks a pending approval; manufacturing expense rises while shortage-driven revenue in epinephrine PFS and phytonadione normalizes as competitors return. BAQSIMI net price continues to fall, forcing an intangible impairment against $768.2M of equity and further estimate cuts toward $2.00. With no buyback bid, refinancing conversations begin in 2027 at 7-8%, costing ~$0.38 per share. At 7x $2.00, $14, roughly 29% below the basis.

**Base case ($22).** Revenue grows 3-5% for the full year, short of the mid-to-high single-digit guide. Blended gross margin settles at 47-49%. Non-GAAP EPS lands near consensus $2.55 for 2026 and $2.75-2.90 for 2027 after remediation and interest drag. On 8x $2.70 → $21.6; cross-checked against normalized FCF of ~$100M after higher interest and remediation ($2.35 per share) capitalized at an 11% required yield → $21.4. Base $22, about 11% above the basis.

Probability weights of 50% base, 20% bull, 30% bear give an expected value of approximately $21.0, or **+6% from $19.81**. That is a modestly positive but low-confidence expected return with a 29% drawdown tail — consistent with HOLD, and consistent with REDUCE at the $22.32 pre-open indication, where the same weights imply roughly -6%.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have declined from 5.5 (March) to 4.1 (July) with three consecutive AVOIDs, and the underlying reasons were correct: revenue turned negative in FY2025, Q1 2026 margins collapsed, estimates fell, and a Warning Letter arrived. The upgrade to 4.6 and HOLD rests on three specific new facts, not on the price decline: gross margin recovered 970bp sequentially to 50.8%; the launch cohort is contributing measurable higher-margin revenue ($8.4M from ipratropium in a partial quarter); and the balance sheet has no maturity inside 24 months with 2.5x adjusted net leverage and $292.6M of liquidity. Valuation improves as price falls on stable-to-improving Q2 fundamentals; growth and competitive position remain impaired by falling estimates and franchise erosion, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an accumulation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders can maintain exposure; new capital has no edge at $19.81 and negative edge above ~$22.50.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (expected early November 2026). That print carries three decisive items — the first full quarter under Warning Letter remediation, the cash impact of the $100M Lilly payment, and whether the board re-authorizes repurchases.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q3 gross margin at or above 49% with H2 revenue tracking toward ~$400M, confirming Q1 was non-recurring and that remediation costs are contained.
2. Current-year and next-year consensus EPS stop falling and revise up over a 60-day window (currently -11.4% and -14.2% over 90 days).
3. A new repurchase authorization of $100M or more, or disclosure of a committed refinancing of the June 2028 term loan at a rate below 7%.
4. Price toward $16-17 without further fundamental deterioration, which would put the stock near 6x current-year consensus and a ~20% normalized FCF yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
