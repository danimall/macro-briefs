## Executive Summary

Jackson Financial is one of the most mispriced names in U.S. financial services, trading at approximately 4.6x adjusted operating earnings despite generating $1.6B in operating income (FY2025), $1.4B in free capital, and returning over 11% of its market capitalization to shareholders annually. The stock trades at $108.47 after declining ~12% from its February 2026 high of $123.61 following a Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS of $5.15, which missed consensus expectations. That miss was driven by LP return shortfalls ($0.48/share) and elevated mortality claims ($0.42/share) — neither of which signals structural deterioration.

The company's product transformation is working: RILA sales grew 139% over two years to $6.9B, fixed and fixed index annuity sales reached $1.9B in FY2025 (up 18% YoY), and the February 2026 TPG partnership positions Jackson to compete in spread-based products while maintaining its dominant variable annuity distribution. With 2026 guidance reaffirmed ($1.2B free capital generation, $900M-$1.1B capital return), the capital return math alone supports significant upside. The persistent discount reflects GAAP opacity, equity market tail risk on $236B in VA separate accounts, and estimate revisions that turned slightly negative — but the underlying franchise is generating economic returns far in excess of what the stock price implies.

## Company Overview

Jackson Financial became an independent publicly traded company in September 2021 following its separation from Prudential plc. Headquartered in Lansing, Michigan, it is one of the largest U.S. retail annuity providers with $351B in AUM across three segments:

- **Retail Annuities** (90% of liabilities): Variable annuities (#2 market share at 13.6%), Registered Index-Linked Annuities (RILA — fastest-growing), Fixed and Fixed Index Annuities, payout annuities
- **Institutional Products** (4%): Funding agreements (GICs, FABN, FABCP)
- **Closed Life and Annuity Blocks** (6%): Legacy life insurance, no new sales since 2012

Distribution reaches ~500 broker-dealers, 121,000+ appointed advisors, and 2,100+ RIA firms. The in-house policy administration platform services 82% of policies at an industry-leading 27 bps operating expense-to-asset ratio.

## Growth Analysis

**Growth Drivers:**

1. **RILA expansion:** Account values grew from $11.7B to $20.3B in one year. This is a high-margin, lower-risk product replacing legacy VA in the mix. Market growing at ~27% CAGR.

2. **TPG Partnership (closed Feb 2026):** Created Hickory Re captive reinsuring fixed/FIA products with TPG-managed assets. This enables Jackson to compete in spread-based products by accessing TPG's direct lending/asset-based finance capabilities. The $500M equity investment from TPG was used to capitalize Hickory Re, supporting multi-billion dollar growth potential in fixed products. However, the structure does involve Jackson's balance sheet through the Hickory Re subsidiary.

3. **Demographic tailwind:** 10,000 Americans turn 65 daily. SECURE Act and SECURE 2.0 expand annuity access in retirement plans. DOL Fiduciary Rule likely permanently vacated — removes distribution headwind.

4. **Share count management:** Jackson repurchased $634M of shares in FY2025, reducing the count from 73.4M to 66.8M. The TPG issuance of 4.7M shares in Q1 2026 partially offset this, bringing shares to 70.3M as of March 31, 2026. Ongoing buyback authorization of $903M should resume net share count reduction over the balance of 2026.

**Growth Headwinds:**

- Net outflows of $(9.6)B in 2025 (improved from $(12.3)B in 2024) as mature VA policies exit surrender periods
- Estimate revisions are negative: FY2026 consensus has fallen from $24.69 to $23.60 over 90 days (-4.4%)
- Next quarter estimate cut sharply: -10.8% over 90 days (from $6.00 to $5.35)
- Q1 2026 miss broke a 3-quarter beat streak

The negative estimate revision trend is the primary constraint on conviction. However, the revisions are modest in absolute terms (-4.4% annual) and appear driven by the Q1 transient items (LP shortfalls and mortality claims) rather than fundamental deterioration of earnings power.

At $108.47 with $23.60 in FY2026E adjusted operating EPS, the stock trades at 4.6x earnings. Even applying a heavy discount for GAAP opacity and guarantee risk, this is extraordinary cheapness for a company growing operating earnings 12-15% annually and returning approximately 13% of market cap per year.

The negative trailing P/E (-451.95x per Yahoo Finance data) reflects the GAAP loss and is entirely irrelevant to economic valuation. GAAP P/E is not meaningful for annuity companies given the extreme volatility of mark-to-market items, though GAAP book value, statutory capital, and hedge results remain important risk metrics.

**Why the discount exists and why it's excessive:**
- GAAP accounting makes the company appear unprofitable (it earned $1.6B in 2025)
- VA guarantee tail risk on $236B separate accounts creates "uninvestable" perception for many institutional mandates
- Small coverage (only 4 analysts) limits price discovery
- Complex business model deters generalist investors

The discount is partially justified (2-3x multiple reduction for complexity and tail risk is reasonable), but 4.6-4.8x for a 14.7% ROE business growing double-digits with a ~13% shareholder yield implies the market is pricing in either a severe equity market decline or structural earnings deterioration — neither of which is the base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Jackson holds the #2 position in U.S. variable annuity sales with 13.6% market share and a wholesaling force more than double that of its closest competitor. The in-house policy administration system processes 82% of policies at 27 bps — industry-leading efficiency.

Key competitive developments:
- **Product diversification:** RILA growth positions Jackson in the fastest-growing annuity segment
- **TPG partnership:** Closes the spread-product capability gap vs. PE-backed competitors (Athene/Apollo model)
- **Distribution breadth:** 500+ broker-dealers + 2,100+ RIAs provides durable access to retirement savings flows
- **NPS of +46 vs. +37 industry average** confirms service quality differentiation

The competitive trajectory is improving. Jackson was historically a pure VA company facing structural headwinds from guarantee-heavy legacy products. The transformation toward RILA/FIA/spread products while maintaining VA dominance addresses the key strategic vulnerability.

**Competitive risks:** PE-backed insurers with lower cost of capital (Athene, Global Atlantic) are aggressive in accumulating fixed annuity liabilities. Jackson's VA expertise may become less differentiated as the industry converges toward similar product mixes.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Equity Market Sensitivity (HIGH):** $236B in VA separate accounts generate fees proportional to market values. A 20% equity decline would reduce fee income by approximately $470M annually (assuming a ~1% blended fee rate) and increases guarantee exposure. This is the structural reason for the multiple discount.

**2. Hedging Basis Risk:** Q1 2026 demonstrated $101M in basis risk losses. The hedging program cannot perfectly replicate policyholder fund performance. In extreme market dislocations, hedge slippage could be material.

**3. NAIC Economic Scenarios (2026):** New statutory reserve/capital methodology took effect January 1, 2026. Management explicitly warned of "material impact on the level and volatility of statutory surplus and required statutory capital." This is a near-term wildcard that could constrain capital return if outcomes are adverse.

**4. Estimate Revision Momentum:** Consensus is falling (-4.4% on FY2026, -10.8% on next quarter). If Q2 also misses, the narrative could shift from "transient" to "structural," triggering further multiple compression from already-low levels.

**5. Athene Concentration:** Majority of in-force fixed annuities are ceded to Athene (single counterparty). Athene credit deterioration would be material.

**6. Prudential plc AUM Dependency:** PPM manages $29.8B for Prudential affiliates (32% of PPM AUM), terminable at any time. This is a legacy overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Estimated near-term ATM implied volatility is approximately 39%, above 1-year historical vol of roughly 32%. Put/call open interest ratios and put skew indicate institutional caution. The options market appears to be pricing more risk than the fundamental picture warrants for a company that just reaffirmed guidance — this richness in IV likely reflects residual post-earnings uncertainty and the NAIC regulatory wildcard. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($135-145)

- 2026 capital return of $1.0B+ reduces share count meaningfully from current 70.3M base
- Adjusted operating EPS of $25-27 on normalized LP returns and lower mortality claims
- Market re-rates from 4.6x to 5.5-6.0x as TPG partnership validates franchise value and RILA growth accelerates
- 5.5x × $26 = $143; plus 3.3% dividend yield = total return 35-40%

### Bear Case ($85-90)

- Equity market correction of 15-20% reduces separate account fees substantially
- Hedging losses amplify in volatility regime (Q1 2026 pattern extends)
- NAIC scenarios require capital retention, forcing buyback suspension
- Stock trades to 3.5x depressed earnings: 3.5x × $20 = $70; floor supported by book value and dividend
- Probability-weighted risk: 20-25% downside to $85-90

## Conclusion

Jackson Financial at 4.6x adjusted operating earnings is mispriced for a business generating 14.7% ROE, with Q1 2026 retail sales growing 31% YoY, and returning approximately 13% of market cap to shareholders. The Q1 2026 miss was transient (LP returns and mortality claims), guidance was reaffirmed, and the TPG partnership opens a new growth vector in spread products. The -4.4% estimate revision trend over 90 days warrants monitoring but does not override the fundamental case at these multiples.

The stock is cheap for identifiable reasons: GAAP opacity, VA tail risk, and complex hedging dynamics ensure a permanent discount vs. traditional life peers. But the magnitude of the discount far exceeds what is justified by these factors for a well-capitalized company with demonstrated ability to generate $1.4B in free capital annually.

BUY with a 12-18 month horizon, targeting $130 on base case.

These corrections do not reverse the thesis but reduce the mechanical EPS-accretion component of the bull case.
