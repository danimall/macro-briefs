## Executive Summary

Jackson Financial is a U.S. annuity pure-play trading at approximately 4.8x adjusted operating earnings ($23.60 FY2026E), generating 14.7% adjusted operating ROE, and returning approximately 12% of its market cap annually to shareholders through buybacks and dividends combined. The stock sits at $108.47, roughly 12% below its 52-week high of $123.61, while consensus analyst targets range from $114 to $136. The structural GAAP-to-economic earnings disconnect causes persistent undervaluation by investors who misread insurance accounting.

The January 2026 TPG partnership gives Jackson access to private credit asset capabilities to grow higher-margin spread-based products (RILA, FIA) while retaining independence — a differentiated model versus peers who sold outright to PE firms. Sales momentum is exceptional, with total sales up 67% over two years and RILA (the industry's fastest-growing category) up 140% since 2023.

The primary risks are equity market sensitivity (beta 1.40 with separate account values driving fee income), falling estimate revisions (FY2026 EPS down 4.4% over 90 days), and the current macro environment where elevated volatility creates headwinds for equity-linked annuity economics. However, the extreme valuation discount provides meaningful downside protection — at 4.8x earnings with 14.7% ROE, the stock prices in substantial deterioration that has not materialized.

## Company Overview

Jackson Financial is the largest independent U.S. annuity pure-play, spun off from Prudential plc (UK) in September 2021. The company manages $351B in total AUM across three segments:

- **Retail Annuities (~90% of liabilities):** Variable annuities (#2 market share, 13.6%), RILA (fastest-growing category), fixed/fixed-index annuities
- **Institutional Products (~4%):** Guaranteed investment contracts, funding agreements
- **Closed Life and Annuity Blocks (~6%):** Run-off life insurance

Distribution reaches 500+ broker-dealer partners, 121,000+ appointed advisors, and 2,100+ RIA firms. The company operates 82% of in-force policies on a proprietary platform at a 27 bps operating expense ratio, among the lowest in the industry. With 3,090 employees and a market cap of approximately $7.6B, Jackson is highly efficient relative to headcount.

## Financial Analysis

**Adjusted Operating Earnings — a key non-GAAP metric:** $1,614M in FY2025, up 12% from $1,443M in FY2024. Pretax adjusted operating earnings were $1,882M. The company reported adjusted operating EPS of **$22.67** on 71.2M weighted-average diluted shares. On a forward basis using FY2026 consensus of $23.60, the stock trades at approximately 4.6x.

*Note: Jackson considers adjusted operating earnings a useful measure because it excludes items that can be highly variable under GAAP, though the company cautions it should not be used as a substitute for GAAP net income. For an insurer that deliberately hedges to economic/statutory outcomes rather than GAAP, this non-GAAP measure provides better insight into underlying business performance.*

**GAAP Earnings Are Highly Volatile:** FY2025 GAAP net income was $(17)M versus $902M in FY2024. The $1.6B divergence between adjusted and GAAP reflects mark-to-market hedging/MRB movements. Jackson deliberately hedges to economic/statutory outcomes, not GAAP. Net hedging results swung from +$285M (2024) to -$661M (2025). The non-operating adjustments are well-disclosed, though they are highly variable and tied to changes in fair value of derivative instruments and market risk benefits, making them difficult to predict with precision.

## Growth Analysis

Jackson's growth story has two components: the denominator (share reduction) and the numerator (earnings expansion).

**Denominator:** In FY2025, the buyback program reduced shares by approximately 9%. However, the February 2026 TPG share issuance (4.7M shares) partially offset this reduction. Going forward, with $903M in remaining buyback authorization and the $900M–$1.1B capital return target (which includes dividends of approximately $240M+ annually at the new $0.90/quarter rate), net buyback-driven share reduction is likely in the range of 6–8% annually, still providing meaningful EPS accretion.

**Numerator:** The TPG partnership targets spread-based product growth. The arrangement includes a target of $12B of AUM under TPG investment management over five years, with a longer-term incentive target of $20B. Hickory Re was established with $1.2B initial premium plus $650M capitalization ($150M from Jackson + $500M from TPG's equity purchase) to support fixed/FIA liabilities. TPG provides direct lending and asset-based finance capabilities that should improve net investment income spreads over time on new spread-based production.

**Estimate trajectory is concerning:** FY2026 consensus has fallen from $24.69 to $23.60 over 90 days (-4.4%), and FY2027 is down from $26.97 to $26.04 over 30 days (-3.5%). Next quarter estimates dropped 10.8% over 90 days. This negative revision momentum likely reflects elevated market volatility reducing fee income expectations and potentially increasing hedge costs. The reported Q1 2026 slight miss ($5.94 vs. $6.02 estimate) after three consecutive beats may mark the beginning of an earnings deceleration phase.

**Industry tailwinds remain intact:** Aging demographics, shift from defined benefit to defined contribution, SECURE Act provisions expanding annuity adoption in 401(k)s, and the growing retirement income gap all structurally support annuity demand for the next decade.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108.47 with FY2026E EPS of $23.60, JXN trades at 4.6x forward adjusted operating earnings. On a FY2027 basis ($26.04), the multiple is 4.2x. Using Jackson's reported FY2025 adjusted operating EPS of $22.67, the trailing multiple is 4.8x.

**Absolute valuation:** 4.6–4.8x earnings on a 14.7% ROE business with ~12% annual total shareholder yield is deeply discounted. Even applying a 6x multiple (still well below financial services norms) to current-year earnings yields $142. At 5x FY2027E, the target is $130. The market is assigning a significant discount for GAAP opacity, equity market sensitivity, and legacy VA runoff risk.

**Relative valuation:** Jackson appears to trade at a discount to peers such as Lincoln National and Equitable, though a detailed peer multiple comparison is beyond the scope of this report. The discount likely reflects Jackson's greater VA concentration and GAAP volatility rather than fundamental inferiority.

**Total shareholder yield as valuation support:** At $108/share, the $900M–$1.1B capital return program provides approximately 3.3% dividend yield plus an estimated 8–9% buyback yield = approximately 11–12% total shareholder yield. While repurchases are discretionary and subject to statutory capital constraints, management/board decisions, and market conditions, the sustained capital return program tends to provide bid support over time as fewer shares absorb the same earnings base.

**Adjusted book value:** Jackson reported adjusted book value of $10.6B, or $155.78 per diluted share at December 31, 2025. The stock trades at approximately 0.70x adjusted book value — indicating the market believes Jackson will destroy value or that book value is overstated. The subsequent TPG share issuance modestly dilutes per-share book value from this level.

The specific reason the market misprices JXN: investors cannot easily compute a P/E from GAAP financials (which show losses in volatile quarters), screeners show "N/A" for trailing P/E, and the insurance accounting complexity creates an analytical barrier. This information asymmetry persists because most quantitative/passive strategies cannot handle the GAAP disconnect.

## Competitive Landscape

**Position: #2 in VA sales (13.6% share), growing rapidly in RILA**

Jackson's competitive trajectory is improving:
- RILA sales grew from zero in 2021 to $6.9B in 2025 — gaining share in the industry's fastest category
- Fixed Index Annuity sales up 351% YoY in 2025, reflecting improved product positioning ahead of the TPG partnership (which was announced January 2026 and closed February 2026)
- Industry-leading wholesaling force (2x sales of closest VA competitor)
- Net Promoter Score of +46 vs. +37 industry average
- Operating expense ratio of 27 bps among the lowest in the industry
- 14 consecutive years winning highest customer service award

**Strategic positioning vs. PE-backed competitors:** Jackson's TPG partnership gives it access to private credit asset capabilities without ceding control. Athene (Apollo) and Global Atlantic (KKR) are fully owned by their respective PE firms. Jackson maintains independent investment discretion while accessing TPG's origination platform — a differentiated hybrid model.

**Vulnerabilities:** The VA book is in structural runoff. While RILA growth is impressive, it requires sustained execution to prevent overall AUM erosion from overwhelming the top line. The loss of Prudential plc's $29.8B PPM relationship (32% of PPM AUM, terminable on short notice) would impair investment management capabilities.

## Risk Assessment

**Equity Market Sensitivity (HIGH):** Beta of 1.40 reflects the direct relationship between separate account values and fee income. A 20% equity market decline would reduce fee revenue significantly while simultaneously increasing hedge costs and potentially triggering guaranteed benefit payments.

**Negative Estimate Revisions (MODERATE-HIGH):** The 4.4% decline in FY2026 consensus over 90 days and the 10.8% decline in next-quarter estimates signal deteriorating visibility. If current market volatility persists through Q2-Q3, further downward revisions are likely.

**GAAP Volatility & Investor Perception (MODERATE):** Q1 2026 reported a GAAP loss while producing adjusted earnings of $5.94/share. This perennial communication challenge means the stock will likely trade with a structural discount versus peers with cleaner financial statements.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (DUAL-EDGED):** Higher rates improve new business pricing and net investment income but increase policyholder surrender incentives and reduce mark-to-market asset values. An elevated rate environment may accelerate VA surrenders (as policyholders can earn competitive yields in risk-free alternatives), partially offset by improved spread economics on new RILA/FIA sales.

**Annual Assumption Reviews:** These have been unfavorable three consecutive years ($342M in 2025, $445M in 2024, $466M in 2023). Investors should expect similar charges in 2026 given the volatile market environment.

**NAIC Economic Scenario Changes (NEW RISK):** Effective January 1, 2026, new statutory reserve calculation scenarios could materially impact capital requirements and volatility. The company disclosed this risk without quantifying the impact.

**Counterparty Concentration (Athene):** Significant reinsurance exposure to a single counterparty (Apollo-affiliated). This warrants monitoring.

**Capital Return Constraints:** Repurchases and dividends are funded by distributions from insurance subsidiaries, which are limited by applicable laws and regulations. While the 567% RBC ratio provides substantial headroom above the 425% minimum operating target, a severe market environment could constrain the pace of capital return.

No options data was provided. The reported short interest of approximately 5.2% with 5.2 days to cover represents moderate bearish positioning — not crowded but meaningful.

## Macro Sensitivity Acknowledgment

JXN's 1.40 beta warrants explicit acknowledgment of the current macro environment. With equity vol sustained above normal levels, the near-term operating environment creates headwinds for equity-linked annuity economics.

The valuation thesis rests on the stock being cheap enough (4.6–4.8x earnings, ~0.70x adjusted book) to absorb meaningful earnings deterioration while still generating acceptable returns. If FY2026 EPS falls to $20 (a 15% miss versus current consensus), the stock at $108 would trade at 5.4x — still inexpensive for a company with 567% RBC generating $800M+ in free capital. The capital return program reduces the denominator over time, providing a return component even in adverse scenarios, though buyback pace may slow in a stress environment.

## Conclusion

Jackson Financial presents a compelling value opportunity driven by structural mispricing from GAAP accounting complexity, combined with a strategic shift (TPG partnership) that diversifies the business toward higher-quality spread-based earnings. The approximately 12% annual total shareholder yield provides ongoing valuation support, while RILA growth positions the company for the next decade of retirement industry expansion.

The primary risks — equity market sensitivity, negative estimate revisions, and macro uncertainty — are real but substantially priced at 4.6–4.8x earnings and ~0.70x book. At these levels, the stock offers asymmetric upside: modest fundamental improvement drives meaningful appreciation, while significant deterioration is already reflected in the multiple.

BUY with a 12-month base case target of $120-125, representing 11-15% price appreciation plus 3.3% dividend yield.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected FY2025 EPS ($22.67 vs. $24.18), the 4.7M TPG share issuance diluting per-share metrics, and the distinction between total capital return and pure buyback-driven share retirement collectively reduce the magnitude of per-share accretion supporting the target price; the directional thesis remains intact but the margin of safety is narrower than originally presented.
