## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum has been one of the best-performing materials stocks over the past year, rising from ~$15.73 to ~$64.09 (up 307%) on the back of a near-perfect policy setup: Section 232 tariffs escalated from 10% to 50%, the IRA Section 45X production tax credit provides ~$89M annually, and a prospective 750,000 tpy new smelter in Oklahoma with EGA would be the first new U.S. primary aluminum smelter since 1980. The Midwest Premium (MWP) surged from $427/t in 2024 to $1,295/t average in 2025, directly adding hundreds of millions in revenue.

The underlying economics are considerably less impressive than the stock price suggests. FY2025 net income was $42M on $2.53B of revenue; without Section 45X credits (~$89M), the company would have been unprofitable. Trailing P/E is approximately 155x. The company disclosed a financial restatement and unremediated material weakness in internal controls in March 2026. Every named executive officer sold shares in Q1 2026, and Glencore (36.4% shareholder) disposed of $327M of stock. The Grundartangi smelter, Century's largest and lowest-cost facility, has operated at roughly one-third capacity since October 2025 due to transformer failures.

The bull case requires sustained 50% tariffs, successful Grundartangi restart, successful Inola smelter development ($4B+), and continued Section 45X credits — each carrying meaningful policy, execution, or commodity risk. At 155x trailing earnings and ~6.7x forward consensus (on estimates that have risen sharply but face a 4-for-4 quarterly miss track record), the risk/reward is unfavorable at current levels.

## Company Overview

Century Aluminum operates three primary aluminum smelters and one alumina joint venture:

- **Grundartangi, Iceland** — 320,000 tpy capacity, powered by hydro/geothermal energy. Currently running at ~one-third capacity due to October 2025 transformer failures. Restart expected by end of April 2026.
- **Sebree, Kentucky** — 220,000 tpy, MISO market-based power. Fully operational.
- **Mt. Holly, South Carolina** — 230,000 tpy, cost-of-service power through 2031. Operating at ~75%; restart to 100% expected by June 2026.
- **Jamalco, Jamaica** — 55% JV interest, ~1.4M tpy alumina. Acquired May 2023. Provides vertical integration.

Total capacity is ~770,000 tpy; FY2025 production was ~638,000 tonnes (83% utilization). Glencore plc owns ~36.4% of common stock (via Series A Convertible Preferred), accounts for ~54% of consolidated revenue, and supplies alumina to the company's smelters. This creates a uniquely concentrated customer-supplier-shareholder relationship.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

FY2025 revenue was $2.53B, up 14% YoY, driven almost entirely by the MWP surge. The tariff-driven premium increase contributed an estimated $416M in incremental revenue. Operating income was $158M (6.3% margin), up from $121M in restated FY2024. Net income attributable to Century was $42M, or $0.40 diluted EPS.

Critical earnings quality issues: FY2025 included approximately $89M in IRA Section 45X production tax credits flowing through cost of goods sold and $94.7M in net derivative losses (forward hedging positions that locked in prices below spot as MWP surged). Without the Section 45X credits, net income would have been approximately negative $47M. The company's profitability is policy-dependent in both directions — tariffs on the revenue side and tax credits on the cost side.

**Balance Sheet**

Cash was $134M at year-end, with total liquidity of $418M (plus $200M Hawesville sale proceeds received in February 2026). Long-term debt is $480M following a July 2025 refinancing that pushed maturities to 2032 (6.875% Senior Secured Notes). The $86.3M convertible notes due May 2028 are deeply in-the-money at a $18.74 conversion price, representing ~4.6M shares of potential dilution. Current ratio is healthy at 1.97x. The accumulated deficit of $1.6B against $806M of equity reflects the company's long history of value destruction.

**Cash Flow**

OCF was $185M in FY2025, a significant improvement from negative $25M in restated FY2024. Capex was $100M, yielding $85M of FCF. However, FY2026 guided capex of $170-180M (Grundartangi repairs $60-70M, Mt. Holly restart $45M, Jamalco maintenance $25M) will likely consume most or all OCF, producing near-zero FCF. Grundartangi repair costs are expected to be insurance-reimbursed but recovery timing is uncertain.

**Restatement and Material Weakness**

On March 3, 2026, Century filed an 8-K (Item 4.02) declaring non-reliance on previously issued financial statements due to errors in the consolidation of Jamalco (proportionate consolidation used instead of full consolidation). FY2024 and FY2023 were restated. A material weakness in internal controls over financial reporting remains unremediated as of December 31, 2025, specifically around business process controls at Jamalco. Management stated disclosure controls were "not effective" as of September 30, 2025.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Growth Drivers**

1. **Grundartangi restart** (expected April 2026): Returning ~200,000 tpy of low-cost capacity would increase shipments by roughly 30% and improve cost structure given Grundartangi's hydro/geothermal power advantage.
2. **Mt. Holly ramp to 100%** (expected June 2026): Adding ~57,000 tpy incremental.
3. **Sustained elevated MWP**: If 50% Section 232 tariffs hold, the MWP should remain at $1,200-1,600/t range, supporting strong pricing.

**Long-term Growth Drivers**

4. **Inola, Oklahoma new smelter**: 750,000 tpy capacity in a 60/40 JV with EGA. Estimated cost ~$4B. Depends on: (a) $500M DOE cooperative agreement (currently under review after Trump administration IRA pause), (b) competitive long-term power supply agreement with PSO (not finalized), (c) definitive JV terms, (d) environmental permitting. Groundbreaking targeted by end of 2026. Century's 60% share would still require significant capital.
5. **Hawesville data center equity**: 6.8% non-dilutive interest in a TeraWulf AI/HPC data center on the former smelter site. Optionality play valued at ~$14M currently.

**Estimate Revisions**

Consensus estimates have risen sharply: current-year EPS revised from $5.87 (90 days ago) to $9.80 (+67%), and next-year from $4.29 to $9.29 (+117%). This positive revision momentum reflects the tariff/restart tailwinds. However, the company has missed estimates in all four most recent quarters — by -36.4%, -10.9%, -34.6%, and -5.2% respectively. This 4-for-4 miss record warrants significant skepticism about whether current consensus is achievable.

The extreme divergence between trailing and forward P/E reflects the market pricing in a step-change in earnings — from $0.42 trailing EPS to $9.80 consensus for the current fiscal year. This 23x earnings step-up requires: (1) Grundartangi returning to full production, (2) MWP remaining at or above current levels, (3) no further derivative losses, and (4) continued Section 45X credits. Each of these assumptions has identifiable risk.

At $9.80 in current-year EPS, 6.7x forward earnings superficially appears cheap versus the 16.6x peer median. But the peer median is on demonstrated, repeatable earnings. CENX's consensus EPS has never been realized given the consecutive miss streak, and the earnings depend on a specific policy regime (50% tariffs + IRA credits) that could change.

Analyst price targets range from $69 (low) to $86 (high) with a median of $75. With the stock at $64.09, the low target implies only 7.7% upside, while the high implies 34.2%. Only 3 analysts cover the stock (all Buy-rated), so coverage is thin and consensus is one-directional.

The 307% appreciation in 12 months has priced in most of the positive catalysts. Continued upside requires sustained policy support AND successful Grundartangi restart AND initial Inola smelter progress, or a further aluminum/MWP price surge. Downside risk from policy reversal or execution failures is substantial at these levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Century operates in a structurally attractive position within the U.S. aluminum market. The country produces only ~770,000 tpy against consumption of several million tonnes, creating a massive supply deficit. Section 232 tariffs at 50% provide a wide competitive moat against imports. Century's low-carbon Natur-Al™ branding (leveraging Grundartangi's hydro/geothermal power) differentiates in markets with growing ESG requirements.

Relative to Alcoa (much larger, vertically integrated, diversified), Century is a purer play on U.S. aluminum tariff protection but with correspondingly higher concentration risk. The 54% revenue concentration with Glencore creates both stability (guaranteed offtake) and vulnerability (pricing leverage sits with the buyer/shareholder).

The Inola smelter, if completed, would nearly double Century's domestic capacity. The JV with EGA (world's largest ex-China aluminum producer by capacity) provides technical credibility but also reduces Century's ownership to 60%.

## Risk Assessment

**Policy Risk (Severe)**
The entire earnings thesis depends on Section 232 tariffs at 50% and IRA Section 45X credits. The Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 IEEPA ruling did not directly affect Section 232 tariffs, but it creates precedent for future challenges. A tariff reduction from 50% to 25% could cut MWP by $400-600/t, reducing revenue by $250-375M and likely making the company unprofitable. The Section 45X credit phases out starting 2031.

**Operational Risk (High)**
Grundartangi has been at one-third capacity for six months. The restart timeline has already slipped from the original 11-12 month estimate. Any further delay reduces FY2026 earnings below consensus. The Inola smelter project has multiple preconditions (DOE funding, power agreements, permitting, definitive JV terms) — any one could fail.

**Financial Reporting Risk (High)**
The unremediated material weakness, two-year restatement, and ineffective disclosure controls create legal, regulatory, and investor confidence risk. The Jamalco consolidation errors may indicate broader accounting infrastructure deficiencies.

**Insider Selling (Red Flag)**
The CEO sold 300,000 shares ($15.5M) in January-March 2026. The CFO sold shares. Every named executive sold shares in Q1 2026. Glencore sold $327M of stock on March 4, 2026. While some sales were under pre-established 10b5-1 plans, the universality and timing (coinciding with the restatement disclosure) create a strong negative signal. No insider purchases were recorded.

**Concentration Risk**
54% of revenue from Glencore. A renegotiation of commercial terms by the dominant customer/shareholder would directly impact margins.

**Earnings Miss Pattern**
Four consecutive quarterly misses (-36.4%, -10.9%, -34.6%, -5.2%) against rising consensus. This suggests either systematic analyst overestimation or operational execution gaps. The consensus FY2026E of $9.80 represents a 23x step-up from trailing EPS, and the miss pattern implies the actual figure could be materially lower.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-90)**
Grundartangi restarts on schedule (April 2026) and reaches full production by Q3 2026. Mt. Holly ramps to 100% by June 2026. Section 232 tariffs remain at 50%, sustaining MWP above $1,200/t. FY2026 EPS reaches or exceeds $9.80 consensus. Inola smelter progresses through engineering and power supply agreements, with groundbreaking in late 2026. The stock re-rates to 8-9x forward earnings on demonstrated profitability, reaching $78-88. Global aluminum tightness from geopolitical disruption provides additional support.

**Bear Case ($30-40)**
Section 232 tariffs are reduced from 50% to 25% (or exemptions granted to key trading partners), collapsing MWP by $400-600/t. Grundartangi restart is delayed beyond Q2 2026. The DOE cooperative agreement for Inola is withdrawn or restructured. The material weakness leads to SEC investigation or further restatements. FY2026 EPS comes in at $4-6 (consistent with the quarterly miss pattern), and the stock re-rates to 7-8x those lower earnings, implying $28-48. Insider selling accelerates.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Upcoming Q1 FY2026 earnings (estimated late May 2026). Given the miss track record and the 23x step-up from trailing to consensus EPS, a fifth consecutive miss would directly challenge the forward earnings narrative supporting the valuation. Secondary catalyst: any announcement of tariff exemptions, Section 232 negotiations, or Grundartangi restart delay.

**Timing window:** 4-8 weeks to next earnings. Grundartangi restart should be verifiable within 2-4 weeks (end of April 2026 target).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 13.4% of float with 3.0 days to cover is elevated but below the 20%+ danger zone for squeeze dynamics. Glencore's 36.4% ownership and recent selling reduces institutional willingness to bid aggressively. Squeeze risk is moderate — the stock has had several 8-10% single-day moves in both directions, creating painful whipsaws for short positions.

**Borrow and options signal:** Rich IV and backwardated term structure align with the bearish thesis. The 13.4% SI suggests shorts exist but aren't overcrowded. Carrying costs are elevated given the high IV environment.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target of $35-40 (40-45% downside) against upside risk to $75-80 (17-25% upside on a squeeze or earnings beat). Reward:risk of approximately 2:1 if the catalyst is a tariff change, but only 1.5:1 on an earnings miss alone given the likely rebound buying.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** at this time. The 2:1 reward/risk is borderline, and extreme daily volatility ($5+ intraday ranges are common) makes position management difficult. The tariff catalyst has no specific timeline. The earnings catalyst is plausible but the miss pattern may already be partially discounted. The correct trade is to avoid owning the stock rather than actively shorting it. Revisit if: (a) SI drops below 10% (less crowded entry), (b) a specific tariff negotiation timeline emerges, or (c) Grundartangi restart is confirmed delayed.

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum is a policy-dependent commodity producer trading at a market capitalization that assumes near-perfect execution across multiple dimensions: sustained 50% tariffs, successful restart of idled capacity, continued tax credits, and eventual construction of a $4B+ new smelter. The company earned $0.42/share in FY2025 (and would have been unprofitable without tax credits), has missed earnings every quarter for the past year, disclosed a financial restatement with unremediated material weakness, and saw every senior officer sell shares while the stock was near its highs.

The 307% appreciation reflects genuine fundamental improvement — tariff protection has transformed the U.S. aluminum market's economics, and the Inola smelter would be strategically significant. But at $64.09 the stock has already priced most of the good news, and the downside from policy changes, execution failures, or earnings disappointments is severe. AVOID.
