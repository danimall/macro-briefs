## Executive Summary

Scotiabank's turnaround has worked operationally. Adjusted ROE reached 13.2% in fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026), management reaffirmed a 14%+ ROE target for fiscal 2027 a year ahead of its investor-day timeline, CET1 stands at 13.3%, all four operating segments grew earnings, pre-tax pre-provision earnings rose 16% year over year, and the bank has beaten consensus adjusted EPS in each of the last four quarters by 3.3% to 7.8%. Trailing twelve-month EPS of C$7.22 is up 51% from FY2025's C$5.67. The dividend was raised 4% and 6.4 million shares (0.52% of the float) were retired in a single quarter.

The problem is price. The NYSE line has advanced 59% over the trailing twelve months, from US$55.76 in early August 2025 to US$88.85, and the multiple has done most of the work. BNS's trailing P/E has moved 8.2x (FY2022 year-end) → 9.8x → 12.2x → 16.3x → 16.6x, and the dividend yield has compressed from the 6-8% band into the mid-3s, while trailing EPS remains roughly 10% *below* the FY2022 peak of C$8.02. On forward consensus the multiple is more defensible — about 14.5x FY2026 (C$8.30) and 12.9x FY2027 (C$9.29) — but the implied price-to-book of roughly 2.2x against a 14% ROE target requires meaningful sustainable growth under a standard Gordon framework at a 10% cost of equity. That is achievable, not conservative, and it leaves little cushion.

The near-term risk is specifically identifiable. In the same quarter management reaffirmed the FY2027 ROE target, the Chief Risk Officer revised impaired-PCL guidance to the mid-50 basis-point range, described it as "slightly elevated compared with the bank's earlier outlook," and said moderation would arrive "more gradually than previously anticipated." Gross impaired loans rose 4bps QoQ to 99bps and the allowance ratio rose 2bps to 96bps. Fiscal Q3 results (quarter ended July 31) are due within weeks and will test that guide. At US$88.85 the stock sits above the consensus mean target of US$87.08 and roughly 3% below the median of US$91.57, with the sell-side distributed 1 strong buy / 2 buy / 9 hold / 1 strong sell. We rate BNS **HOLD** on the ~3.6% dividend plus a ~2% annualized buyback, with a bias toward trimming into strength rather than adding.

## Company Overview

Scotiabank, founded in 1832 and headquartered in Toronto, is one of Canada's Big Six chartered banks and a domestic systemically important bank. It reports four operating segments — Canadian Banking, International Banking, Global Wealth Management, and Global Banking & Markets — plus an "Other" segment. Its differentiating feature relative to Royal Bank, TD, and BMO is that its international franchise sits in Latin America and the Caribbean (Mexico, Chile, Peru, Central America, Caribbean) rather than in US retail banking. That footprint has been the source of both the bank's higher structural credit costs and its decade-long valuation discount to peers.

Under CEO Scott Thomson, the strategy has been narrowed to a "Canada-US-Mexico trading bloc" thesis with three legs: retrenchment from lower-return Latin American geographies (International Banking is the only segment with shrinking revenue — C$12,043M in FY2025 to C$11,874M TTM); a minority rather than control position in US banking, via an equity stake in KeyCorp; and a domestic mix shift toward fee and wealth income, where Global Wealth Management revenue has grown from C$5,380M (FY2021) to C$6,869M TTM with AUM of C$450B, up 18% year over year.

Two structural notes shape all figures below. First, BNS reports in Canadian dollars on a November-October fiscal year and files Form 40-F/6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q, so US-GAAP XBRL data, Section 16 insider filings, and DEF 14A compensation disclosure are structurally unavailable. Second, a USD investor holds an unhedged CAD translation layer on top of BNS's own MXN/CLP/PEN/USD earnings exposure. Reconciling the supplied data — trailing P/E of 16.63x against trailing EPS of C$7.22 — implies a CAD price of roughly C$120 and USD/CAD of about 1.35. Note a conflict in the supplied fields: the US$4.045 "EPS" line does not reconcile to the 16.63x P/E at a US$88.85 price (that combination implies 22x). The CAD-consistent basis (C$120 / C$7.22) reproduces the stated multiple and is what we use throughout; consensus estimates supplied (current year C$8.30, next year C$9.29) are likewise on the CAD adjusted basis, consistent with the C$2.02 Q2 adjusted print.

Two observations dominate. FY2025 revenue grew 11.5%, the strongest since FY2021, yet net income was flat (C$7,283M vs C$7,286M) and EPS fell 3.4%, with margin compressing 316bps. The entire revenue inflection was absorbed by expenses, provisions, and charges. The composition of those FY2025 charges was not obtainable from the primary release and remains a verification gap.

The earnings recovery shows up in the TTM period: net income C$9,040M, EPS C$7.22. But the ceiling matters — TTM net income is still 6.4% below FY2022's C$9,656M and TTM EPS is 10.0% below FY2022's C$8.02. On a per-share basis BNS has recovered roughly two-thirds of the distance from the FY2025 trough to its prior peak without exceeding it.

**Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026).** Reported net income C$2.63B and diluted EPS C$2.00; adjusted C$2.7B and C$2.02; adjusted ROE 13.2%; CET1 13.3%. NII +10% YoY, non-interest income +17%, expenses +7%, PTPP +16% — genuine positive operating leverage. Technology spend was C$1.4B, up 9%. Segment earnings (reported): Canadian Banking C$935M (+53%), International Banking C$736M at 16% ROE, Global Wealth C$476M (+19%), GBM C$457M (+11%).

Two quality caveats on that quarter. The CFO attributed Canadian Banking's +53% to PTPP growth *and* lower performing (Stage 1/2) provisions, with segment PTPP up only 13% — implying a substantial share of the headline increase came from reserve releases rather than operations. The repeatable domestic improvement is closer to the low teens. Separately, on the call the CFO cited International Banking earnings of C$701M, "up 3% year-over-year on a constant-dollar basis and excluding divested operations," against the C$736M reported figure. IB's underlying growth on management's own preferred basis is +3%, not the mid-teens a casual reading of the reported number suggests. The definitional bridge between the two figures was not obtainable.

**Credit is the swing variable and it moved the wrong way.** All-bank PCL was C$1.2B (66bps, +5bps QoQ), impaired PCL C$1.1B (61bps), allowance C$7.3B (96bps, +2bps), gross impaired loans 99bps (+4bps). Approximately 7bps of all-bank impaired PCL came from a single International Banking corporate account, which the CRO attributed to company-specific factors. A second driver was "higher formations in Canadian commercial" — which is not explained by that single name and sits in the segment carrying the ROE recovery. Cross-checking the ratios implies an average loan book of roughly C$720-760B. The guided improvement from 61bps to mid-50s is worth about C$435M pre-tax, C$322M after tax at a 26% rate, or roughly C$0.26 per share — about 3% of TTM EPS, helpful but not sufficient on its own to bridge 13.2% ROE to 14%+.

**Capital and returns.** CET1 of 13.3% against a D-SIB requirement in the region of 11.5% inclusive of OSFI's Domestic Stability Buffer (regulatory context we did not verify in a filing) leaves roughly 180bps of surplus. C$7.5B was returned over the trailing twelve months — approximately C$5.4B of dividends against roughly C$2.1B of buybacks — with the Q2 repurchase of 6.4M shares annualizing to about 2.1% of the float, suggesting the pace accelerated through the year. The payout ratio was 76% on depressed FY2025 EPS, 60% on TTM EPS, and about 56% on the Q2 adjusted run rate. Coverage is comfortable.

Two data hygiene points: the "total debt C$5,766M" and "net cash C$83,735M" lines in third-party datasets are artifacts capturing a narrow instrument class and must be discarded — BNS is not a net-cash company. Bank operating cash flow and FCF are dominated by trading-book and loan/deposit balance movements and are not decision-relevant here; dividend and buyback capacity is governed by CET1 generation against risk-weighted assets and regulatory approval.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus places FY2026 adjusted EPS at C$8.30 (12 analysts) and FY2027 at C$9.29, implying roughly 15% growth this year off the TTM base and 12% next. Revision momentum is essentially flat: +0.1% on the current year over 30 days, +0.4% on next year over 30 days, +1.0% on next quarter over 90 days. There is no acceleration signal here — the estimates are stable, which is a statement that consensus already embeds the recovery rather than that it is being revised upward into it.

The composition of forward growth splits into durable and cyclical pieces. Durable: Canadian Banking's fourth consecutive quarter of NIM expansion in a concentrated market; Global Wealth AUM +18% with seven consecutive quarters of positive net flows, C$9B of closed internal referrals year to date and commercial-to-wealth referrals doubling to C$2.8B; Mexico with revenue +8% and earnings +25%, the one geography validating the corridor thesis. Cyclical and reversible: GBM capital markets revenue +25% with a "strong" pipeline — the most cyclical line in the bank and one that should be discounted in any normalized estimate; International Banking NIM at 476bps described by the CFO as "a high point," guided down to 465-470bps in Q3 and Q4; performing-provision releases in Canadian Banking; and roughly C$328M annualized (≈3.6% of TTM net income, ≈C$0.26/share) from the KeyCorp equity stake, based on the pre-announced C$82M fiscal Q3 contribution, which should not be straight-lined.

The wealth flow story also warrants a discount. Canadian Banking total deposits fell 3% YoY on term deposits while day-to-day and savings grew 3%, and management noted maturing GIC balances moving into retail mutual funds. A client dollar migrating from a Scotiabank GIC into a Scotiabank fund registers as a wealth net inflow while reducing the deposit funding base — the reported GWM flow figure and the deposit decline are partly the same transaction seen from two segments. Real external net new money is clearly also present (the referral data supports that), but the split was not disclosed.

Net assessment: mid-teens EPS growth in FY2026 decelerating to low-double-digit in FY2027 is credible, but a meaningful minority of it is cycle, reserve release, and non-controlled equity income rather than franchise compounding. That supports a growth score modestly below neutral-plus.

*(Absolute yield levels in this series are approximate — the FY2023 8.07% implies a price below BNS's actual autumn-2023 range — but the direction and magnitude of compression are corroborated by the independent P/E and P/S series.)*

The trailing multiple has doubled while trailing EPS remains below the FY2022 peak. That is the central valuation fact. The configuration that made BNS compelling in 2023-24 — 8-10x earnings, a 6-8% yield, an unloved franchise with a credible self-help plan — has been harvested.

Forward metrics are the fair rebuttal. At an implied C$120 CAD price: 14.5x FY2026 consensus C$8.30 and 12.9x FY2027 C$9.29. Against 12-15% EPS growth that is a PEG near 1.0-1.2, which is not stretched in the abstract. But P/E is the wrong primary lens for a bank, and the P/B identity is unfavorable. Using matched-period inputs — trailing P/E 16.6x × Q2 adjusted ROE 13.2% — implied price-to-book is approximately 2.2x; because trailing EPS and a single-quarter ROE cover different periods, this is an order-of-magnitude estimate only. Inverting a Gordon framework, P/B = (ROE − g)/(COE − g): at 14% ROE and a 10% cost of equity, 2.2x book requires roughly 6.5% sustainable growth; at a 10.5% cost of equity it requires roughly 7%. Canadian bank long-run growth has more typically run in the 4-6% range. The multiple therefore prices full achievement *and* durability of the 14%+ ROE target, with no allowance for the credit guidance having just deteriorated. We flag explicitly that book value per share and tangible book were not obtainable, so the 2.2x figure is derived from the P/E × ROE identity rather than reported — it should be verified against the Q3 6-K.

The external anchors point the same direction. The consensus mean target of US$87.08 sits *below* the US$88.85 close; the median of US$91.57 implies +3.1%; the high of US$94.72 implies +6.6%; the low of US$74.95 implies −15.6%. The sell-side is 9 holds against 3 buys and 1 strong sell, with sentiment characterized as improving but the distribution unmistakably neutral. This is the situation in which analyst targets are *not* floors: BNS is neither at a compressed multiple relative to rising estimates nor showing revision acceleration. Estimates are flat and the stock trades at the top of the target band.

We score valuation 4.5 — no identified mispricing, limited margin of safety, but no structural impairment either. The absolute yield (3.63%) plus roughly 2% of annualized share shrinkage gives a ~5.5% cash return that provides some support.

## Competitive Landscape

The Canadian oligopoly remains the foundation and it is doing its job: four consecutive quarters of Canadian Banking NIM expansion, retention above 90% of maturing retail GICs, and structurally low domestic loss rates. The trajectory within that structure is mixed.

Improving: Global Wealth is winning the contested growth arena, with AUM +18%, quarterly net sales of C$4.7B (four times the prior-year quarter), and a functioning internal referral engine. Mexico is delivering. GBM gained on a strong capital markets cycle.

Deteriorating or defensive: total Canadian Banking deposits fell 3% YoY, and BNS launched a tiered relationship-priced high-interest savings product while management referenced "industry-wide pressure and deposit competition" — a defensive product launch is itself evidence of funding-side pressure. International Banking revenue is contracting and its NIM has, on management's guidance, peaked. Most importantly, the revenue-mix shift toward fees and wealth is not the same measurement as a reduction in geographic concentration. Latin American operations were divested; the remaining Mexico/Chile/Peru/Caribbean exposure is a *larger* share of the smaller international book, not a smaller one. Diversification of revenue mix has improved; concentration of residual country risk has not been shown to fall.

The strategic question the market has not resolved is the KeyCorp minority stake. BNS gets US banking economics and optionality without the capital consumption, integration risk, or regulatory burden of a control acquisition; in exchange it gets no operational control, no synergy capture, equity-accounted rather than consolidated earnings, and pass-through exposure to the US regional-bank credit and rate cycle. The commonly cited ~14.9% ownership and ~US$2.8B cost were not verified from any filing in this work; only the pre-announced C$82M fiscal Q3 contribution is sourced.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit normalization behind plan.** The single most important input to the FY2027 ROE bridge got worse in the same quarter the target was reaffirmed. Impaired PCL guidance to "mid-50 bps," explicitly called elevated versus the prior outlook; ACL +2bps to 96bps; GIL +4bps to 99bps. A rising allowance ratio alongside a rising impaired-loan ratio is not the signature of a cycle that has turned.

**Name concentration in the corporate book.** One International Banking borrower moved the all-bank impaired PCL ratio by ~7bps in a quarter. The company-specific attribution is credible; the sensitivity it reveals is the disclosure.

**Canadian commercial formations rising.** Distinct from the Latin American story and arguably more consequential, because Canadian Banking carries the ROE recovery.

**Domestic funding and household credit backdrop.** Canadian Banking's deposit decline and defensive HISA launch are visible symptoms of intensifying deposit competition and funding-cost pressure on a Canadian retail lender. [The broader rate backdrop — Fed policy stance and long-end yields — was not established in the sources provided and is not asserted here.]

**Two-tier FX.** A USD holder absorbs CAD/USD translation on top of BNS's MXN/CLP/PEN exposure. Part of the 59% trailing-twelve-month NYSE-line return reflects CAD appreciation rather than business performance; we cannot decompose it without the prior-period rate.

**Non-controlled earnings.** Roughly 3.6% of net income from an equity-accounted stake BNS cannot reprice, restructure, or cost-manage.

**Energy exposure nets across geographies.** The CRO cited elevated energy costs as a credit-risk driver while the CEO cited higher oil prices as a positive for Canada. Both hold — but the credit exposure sits in the higher-loss-rate segment while the benefit accrues to the domestic economy.

**Trade policy is first-order, not second-order.** The strategy is an explicit bet on the Canada-US-Mexico bloc holding together.

**Disclosure gaps.** The FY2025 40-F risk factors could not be read, so we cannot report which risk factors were added or modified. The Davivienda transaction status (Colombia/Costa Rica/Panama), book value per share, and the reported-versus-adjusted IB bridge are unverified. Insider transaction data is structurally unavailable for a foreign private issuer — an absence of evidence from which no inference in either direction should be drawn.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 21.0% against 18.2% one-year realized is roughly fair (2.9pt spread, 29th percentile within the chain). The informative feature is the term structure: backwardation, with 21.0% at the 10-day tenor against 18.5% at six months, and elevated put demand at the front (12.2% skew on the August 21 expiry, versus 0.2% at September 18 and −2.3% at November 20). The September 18 tenor — which captures the late-August fiscal Q3 release — carries a put/call volume ratio of 1.81 and a 5.9% implied move. Set against that, open interest is heavily call-tilted (P/C OI 0.18) and short interest is negligible at 8.05M shares (0.66% of shares outstanding, 3.5 days to cover). The read: positioning is broadly long with tactical near-term downside protection being bought into an earnings print that will test the loosened PCL guide. This corroborates rather than contradicts our view that the risk into Q3 is skewed to the downside while the structural holder base is unworried.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (US$101, +13.7%).** Q3 impaired PCL prints at or below 55bps, confirming the mid-50s guide as conservative and validating that the Q2 spike was one corporate name. Canadian Banking NIM extends to a fifth consecutive quarter of expansion, IB NIM lands in the guided 465-470bps band, GWM flows stay above C$4B, and capital markets momentum carries the "marquee transactions" the CEO referenced. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds C$9.29 with ROE through 14%, buybacks continue at a ~2% annual pace, and the market sustains a 14.5x forward multiple as estimates roll to FY2028 — roughly C$136, or US$101 at 1.35. Total return with the dividend approaches 17%.

**Bear case (US$70, −21.2%).** Canadian commercial formations continue to build, impaired PCL holds near or above 61bps, and the FY2027 ROE target slips back to the original investor-day timeline. FY2027 estimates fall toward C$8.20 and the multiple compresses toward 11.5x as the market re-applies BNS's historical discount for international and corporate credit volatility — roughly C$94, or US$70. This is not a solvency scenario; it is a round trip of nine months of multiple expansion, and the stock traded at an implied C$92 as recently as October 31, 2025. A CAD depreciation of 5% would add to the USD-line loss.

**Base case (US$93, +4.7%).** Credit improves gradually but incompletely, roughly in line with the mid-50s guide; FY2027 EPS lands near consensus C$9.29; the multiple settles at 13.5x as investors discount the cyclical and reserve-release components of the recovery. 13.5x × C$9.29 = C$125, or US$93 at 1.35. Adding the 3.6% dividend gives roughly 8% twelve-month total return.

Probability-weighting at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives approximately +0.5% price appreciation plus a 3.6% dividend, or roughly 4% total — below a reasonable 9-10% cost of equity for a bank with this credit profile, with downside (−21%) exceeding upside (+14%). That combination defines a HOLD rather than a REDUCE: the cash return is real and covered, the capital position is strong, and earnings are growing, but there is no margin of safety and the asymmetry is mildly unfavorable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The reason to own BNS from US$88.85 is the ~3.6% dividend at a ~56% forward payout plus roughly 2% annualized share shrinkage — a ~5.5% cash-and-shrinkage return — not expected multiple expansion.

**Base case target:** US$93 (13.5x FY2027 consensus C$9.29 = C$125 at USD/CAD 1.35), +4.7% price, ~8.3% total return with dividends, over 12 months.
**Bull case target:** US$101 (14.5x FY2027 C$9.29 ≈ C$136), +13.7%.
**Bear case target:** US$70 (11.5x a reduced C$8.20 ≈ C$94), −21.2%.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Fiscal Q3 (due late August 2026) prints impaired PCL at or below 55bps with gross impaired loans flat or down QoQ, and Canadian commercial formations declining. This is the single input that most changes the FY2027 ROE bridge.
2. A price decline into the US$76-79 range (≈C$103-107, ~11.5-12x FY2027) without fundamental deterioration, which would restore a ~4.3% yield and a materially lower book multiple.
3. Verification of book value per share implying a lower P/TBV with ROE tracking toward 14%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 impaired PCL at or above 65bps, or a further loosening of the PCL guide, or explicit deferral of the FY2027 14%+ ROE target.
2. Canadian Banking NIM expansion ending (first sequential decline) combined with total deposits down more than 3% YoY, indicating the funding-cost defense is failing.
3. FY2027 consensus revised below C$8.75 (a >5% cut), or suspension/material slowing of the buyback (below ~1% annualized), which would remove the per-share accretion leg of the cash-return thesis.
4. Any restatement or material adverse disclosure regarding the Davivienda transaction or the KeyCorp stake carrying value.

**Reassessment date:** Fiscal Q3 FY2026 results, expected late August 2026. Secondary checkpoints: the Q3 6-K for book value per share and updated PCL/GIL detail, and the FY2025 40-F risk-factor set once accessible.

## Conclusion

Scotiabank is a well-capitalized D-SIB executing a credible strategy with improving domestic margins, a functioning wealth franchise, disciplined capital allocation (dividend +4%, ~2% annualized buyback, M&A ambition downsized to C$200-400M tuck-ins), and four consecutive earnings beats. Management has earned the benefit of the doubt operationally, and CET1 at 13.3% against a roughly 11.5% requirement supports continued per-share accretion.

What the price no longer offers is compensation for the residual risks. At US$88.85 the stock trades above the consensus mean target, at roughly 14.5x FY2026 and 12.9x FY2027 CAD-basis consensus, and at an implied ~2.2x book that requires roughly 6-7% sustainable growth to justify at a 14% ROE. Trailing earnings remain 10% below the FY2022 peak while the trailing multiple has doubled from that year's 8.2x. Estimate revisions are flat, so the re-rating is not being pulled along by rising forecasts. And the one variable that most determines whether the 14%+ ROE target is met — credit costs — moved against the thesis in the most recent quarter, with the confirming or disconfirming print arriving within weeks. Hold for the yield and the buyback; wait for either the credit data or a materially better entry price before adding.
