## Executive Summary

Essential Properties Realty Trust is a high-growth, internally managed net lease REIT focused on single-tenant properties leased to middle-market, service-oriented businesses. The company has delivered approximately 25% annual revenue growth for three consecutive years through a differentiated sale-leaseback origination platform, deploying $1.2-1.3 billion annually while maintaining 99.7% occupancy and 3.6x rent coverage. At the current price of $31.18, EPRT trades at approximately 16.5x trailing AFFO ($1.89 for FY2025) and 15.2-15.6x 2026E AFFO (guided $2.00-$2.05 for FY2026), offering a 4.0% dividend yield with 7-8% AFFO per share growth guidance.

The Q1 2026 results show operational strength obscured by GAAP optics: AFFO beat consensus, management raised full-year AFFO guidance, and investment guidance was increased by $100M. The GAAP miss was driven by non-cash impairments on a portfolio that is otherwise improving in credit quality (properties with rent coverage below 1.5x declined 140 bps). With pro forma leverage at just 3.5x (well below the 5.5x target ceiling), approximately $1.5 billion in liquidity, and analyst consensus targets in the $33-$40 range, the current price offers asymmetric upside against a business executing at a high level.

The primary risk is the $430M 2027 term loan refinancing from a low hedged rate to likely 5%+ market rates, creating $11-14M in annual interest expense headwind. Management is proactively planning mid-2026 bond issuance to address this. At current leverage levels, the balance sheet can absorb this repricing while maintaining AFFO growth.

## Company Overview

As of December 31, 2025, EPRT owns and manages 2,300 single-tenant properties across 48 states, generating $555 million in annualized base rent. As of Q1 2026, the portfolio had grown to 2,417 properties leased to 662 different concepts with no single tenant exceeding 3.4% of ABR, providing extreme granularity. The average property investment of $3.1 million creates fungibility — these "small-box" assets are readily re-tenantable for alternative uses, unlike larger single-purpose properties.

The company's competitive differentiation rests on its middle-market focus (targeting companies with $20M-$1B in revenue and 10-250 locations), relationship-driven origination (82% of investments from existing relationships), and sale-leaseback execution (95% of 2025 investments). This sources a pipeline of transactions underserved by larger institutional capital, allowing EPRT to acquire at 7.7-8.0% cash cap rates versus sub-7% for investment-grade properties.

Tenant industries include car washes, medical/dental services, early childhood education, quick-service restaurants, automotive services, and convenience stores — businesses requiring physical presence that are structurally resistant to e-commerce displacement.

## Growth Analysis

EPRT's growth engine operates through a defined flywheel: originate sale-leasebacks at 200+ bps spreads above weighted average cost of capital → fund with a mix of ATM equity (accretive when stock trades above NAV) and investment-grade debt → compound portfolio at 7-8% AFFO per share growth annually.

The sustainability of this model depends on three factors:

1. **Deal pipeline adequacy:** Management raised 2026 investment guidance by $100M to $1.1-1.5B, signaling no slowdown in middle-market sale-leaseback demand. Tightening bank lending to commercial real estate is expanding the addressable opportunity set as middle-market operators seek alternative capital sources.

2. **Investment spread maintenance:** Cash cap rates on acquisitions remain at 7.7% against a mid-5% WACC, providing approximately 200-220 bps of positive spread. Cap rate compression from 8.1% (early 2024) to 7.7% (Q1 2026) is a trend to monitor, but spreads remain healthy.

3. **Equity cost of capital:** The ATM program requires the stock to trade at prices where issuing equity is accretive. At $31.18, with AFFO at $2.00-$2.05, the implied AFFO yield on equity issuance is approximately 6.4-6.6%, which compares favorably to the 7.7% deployment cap rate, though the two measures are not directly equivalent (AFFO yield is after corporate overhead and interest costs, while cap rates are property-level yields).

Contractual rent escalations of 1.8% per year provide organic same-store growth, supplementing external acquisition-driven growth. The 14.4-year weighted average lease term provides visibility.

## Competitive Landscape

EPRT occupies a differentiated niche within net lease REITs. The middle-market focus (unrated tenants, $3.1M average investment) is underserved by larger competitors like Realty Income ($50B+ market cap, A- rating, investment-grade tenant focus). This creates a structural sourcing advantage: 82% of deals from existing relationships, 95% sale-leasebacks rather than competitively brokered transactions.

The company's competitive position is improving along several dimensions:
- **Scale benefits accruing:** Growing from 1,000 to 2,300+ properties improves deal flow visibility and repeat tenant relationships
- **Credit facility demonstrates market confidence:** $1.0B revolving facility extended to 2030
- **Acquisition pace accelerating:** Guidance raised $100M for 2026
- **Relative TSR outperformance:** Per the 2025 10-K stock performance graph, a $100 investment in EPRT grew to approximately $167 by December 31, 2025, versus approximately $106 for the FTSE NAREIT All Equity REITs index

Vulnerabilities include the BBB- credit rating (one notch above speculative grade), which constrains cost of capital relative to higher-rated peers, and the dependence on continued capital markets access for growth.

## Risk Assessment

**1. 2027 Refinancing Headwind (HIGH):** The $430M term loan at a hedged rate of approximately 2.3% (2.36% as of December 31, 2025; 2.26% as of March 31, 2026) matures in 2027. Refinancing at 5%+ creates $11-14M annual interest headwind (roughly $0.05-$0.07/share AFFO impact). Management plans mid-2026 bond issuance to address proactively. At 3.5x current leverage, the balance sheet has capacity to absorb this, but execution timing matters.

**2. Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):** With long-term rates elevated and inflation persistent, net lease REITs face structural headwinds from required yield compression. EPRT's 4% yield needs growth premium to compensate investors versus risk-free rates. A sustained rise in rates would further compress valuation multiples. The $100M revolver balance drawn in Q1 2026 at SOFR-based rates introduces modest floating-rate exposure.

**3. Tenant Credit in Consumer Stress Environment (MODERATE):** Elevated consumer pressure on service-oriented middle-market tenants is a consideration. Current coverage at 3.5x provides substantial buffer, and coverage is trending positively, but a recession would test the unrated tenant base. Management has noted that casual dining and entertainment sectors are most vulnerable to consumer strain.

**4. Equity Dilution (MODERATE):** 45% share dilution over 3 years is the cost of growth. If the stock price declines materially, ATM issuance becomes less accretive or non-accretive, slowing the growth engine.

**5. Short Interest (ELEVATED):** Based on market data sources, short interest appears elevated relative to peers. This likely reflects the rate sensitivity thesis and refinancing overhang rather than fundamental deterioration.

### Options Market Signal

## Conclusion

EPRT represents a high-quality compounder where post-Q1 weakness appears driven by GAAP optics (EPS miss from non-cash items) rather than operational deterioration. The 4% dividend yield combined with 7-8% AFFO per share growth delivers an 11-12% total return with a defined path to re-rating once the 2027 refinancing is executed.

The hypothesis supporting current mispricing: the market may be treating a GAAP miss as fundamental deterioration when operational metrics uniformly improved. At 15.2-15.6x forward AFFO with analyst consensus targets in the $33-$40 range, the asymmetry favors a BUY with appropriate sizing given rate sensitivity risks. This is a 2-3% position, not a maximum conviction overweight, given the real refinancing headwind and macro uncertainty around rates.
