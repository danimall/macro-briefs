## Executive Summary

Hilton Grand Vacations enters the second half of 2026 with a sales engine that is producing more tours and less revenue per tour. Q2 2026 tour flow rose 6.1% year over year while volume per guest (VPG) fell 8.6% to $3,372, and contract sales declined 2.9% to $810M. Management attributed the shortfall to sales execution at high-volume Bluegreen properties — specifically Orlando and Myrtle Beach — citing leadership and training shortcomings, and cut full-year VPG guidance to down low-to-mid single digits from flat-to-slightly-down. The same quarter saw the loan-loss provision reach approximately 17.3% of owned contract sales versus 13.7% a year earlier, the high end of management's own mid-teens target, while financing propensity rose to 68% from 65%. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.89 missed consensus by 10.2%, and the shares fell 10.3% on the print.

The valuation case is real but narrower than it appears. On the deferral-normalized measure management guides to — Adjusted EBITDA "before deferrals" of $1,225–1,265M for FY2026 — the corporate enterprise value of roughly $8.22B (market cap $3.57B plus corporate net debt of $4,654M) implies 6.6x forward EBITDA, and management's 55–65% cash conversion target implies $685–810M of adjusted free cash flow, a 19–23% yield on the equity. That measure, however, nets consumer-loan originations against the securitization borrowings that fund them. GAAP operating cash flow has been essentially flat at $312M / $309M / $300M across FY2023–25 while revenue grew 27%, and after capex and capitalized software the GAAP-basis yield is closer to 6%. The distributable-cash story is only as durable as continued securitization access at 98% advance rates and continued receivable performance.

The decisive issue is asymmetry. Equity is roughly 43% of enterprise value, stockholders' equity has been deliberately eliminated through $2,449M of cumulative buybacks (retained earnings swung from +$34M at December 31, 2025 to a $(101)M accumulated deficit by June 30, 2026), and FY2026 guidance requires H2 EBITDA of $665–705M against $626M in H2 2025 — a +6.2% to +12.6% bridge — while management has already guided Q3 contract sales down mid-single digits and Q3 VPG down high single digits with incremental SG&A for sales remediation. At the same time, only $103M of repurchase authorization remained as of July 23, 2026 against a stated ~$150M quarterly pace, so the mechanical share-count support that has driven a 19.9% reduction since December 2024 requires both board renewal and further balance-sheet capacity. Probability-weighted expected return from $45.93 is modestly negative. We downgrade to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

HGV develops, markets, sells, manages and finances vacation ownership interests (VOIs), primarily under Hilton Grand Vacations brands. As of June 30, 2026 it operated approximately 200 properties across the U.S., Europe, Canada, the Caribbean, Mexico and Japan, more than 100 sales distribution centers, and over 720,000 Club members. Inventory and property concentration sits in Florida, Europe, Hawaii, South Carolina, California, Arizona, Nevada and Virginia.

The business is the product of two large roll-ups — Diamond Resorts (2021) and Bluegreen Vacations (closed January 17, 2024) — and integration remains incomplete. The FY2025 10-K states that HGV has rebranded "many" Diamond properties, began rebranding certain Bluegreen properties during 2025, and anticipates rebranding "the majority" of Bluegreen properties to Hilton brand standards.

Two reportable segments:

- **Real estate sales and financing** (FY2025 revenue $2,989M, segment Adjusted EBITDA $707M): VOI sales from owned inventory plus third-party fee-for-service (FFS) and just-in-time (JIT) arrangements, and origination/servicing of consumer loans at a 14.4% weighted-average rate on originated balances.
- **Resort operations and club management** (FY2025 revenue $1,625M, segment Adjusted EBITDA $620M): homeowners-association management on a cost-plus basis (10–15% of resort operating costs), Club activation fees and dues, exchange fees, and rental of unsold inventory.

The cost-plus HOA fee is the highest-quality asset in the company: because the fee is a percentage of costs, it is insensitive to occupancy and rate, and resort and club management profit margin held at exactly 70.8% in both FY2024 and FY2025 on $551M of FY2025 profit.

## Financial Analysis

### The reported EBITDA series is not the operative one

Reported Adjusted EBITDA fell 11.4% in FY2025 to $969M, but that decline is largely an ASC 606 construction-deferral artifact: revenue on VOIs sold at projects under construction is deferred until completion while indirect selling and marketing costs are expensed currently. Adding the disclosed $202M FY2025 net construction deferral back to Adjusted EBITDA attributable to stockholders of $950M reproduces the $1,152M "Economic Adjusted EBITDA" figure disclosed in the March 2026 proxy. The same arithmetic reproduces prior years:

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported Adjusted EBITDA | 1,005 | 1,094 | 969 |
| Net construction deferral impact | (21) | (27) | (202) |
| **Deferral-normalized EBITDA** | **1,026** | **1,121** | **1,152** |
| Growth | — | +9.3% | **+2.8%** |

Q2 2026 ties on the same basis: $265M reported plus $28M net deferral equals the $293M management cited, up 5.4% from $278M. The proxy does not formally define the measure and our reconciliation ties on the attributable-to-stockholders basis for 2023 and 2025 but on the total basis for 2024 — a $16–19M noncontrolling-interest inconsistency that does not change the trend.

The operative point: underlying earnings growth decelerated from +9.3% to +2.8% in 2025, and FY2026 guidance of $1,225–1,265M implies +6.3% to +9.8% — an acceleration relative to 2025 in a year when the sales engine is going backwards. H1 2026 on this basis was $560M ($514M + $46M) versus $526M ($413M + $113M), up 6.5%. That leaves H2 2026 requiring $665–705M against $626M in H2 2025.

### Where margin is leaking

| Profit line ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real estate | 575 | 539 | 453 | 187 | 279 |
| — margin | 31.8% | 24.1% | 21.2% | 18.7% | 25.4% |
| Financing | 208 | 276 | 298 | 142 | 173 |
| Resort & club mgmt | 392 | 511 | 551 | 256 | 254 |
| — margin | 68.9% | 70.8% | 70.8% | 69.9% | 67.9% |
| **Rental & ancillary** | **+54** | **+9** | **(39)** | **(27)** | **(29)** |

Three specific pressures:

1. **Rental has flipped from a $54M profit center to a $39M loss and remains negative.** FY2025 rental expenses rose 8.4% on 1.5% revenue growth on higher maintenance fees on unsold inventory. This is the carrying cost of $2,046M of completed unsold VOIs — HOA fees accrue whether or not the interval sells. The June 30, 2026 portfolio pruning (a $48M non-cash loss for $10–12M of annualized maintenance-fee relief, a roughly four-year payback) targets this directly but only modestly.

2. **The Hilton royalty is escalating faster than sales.** License fee expense went $138M (2023) → $171M (2024) → $214M (2025, +25.1%), with $111M in H1 2026. As a percentage of contract sales: 5.97% → 5.70% → 6.46% → **7.26%** in H1 2026. A ~156bp increase since 2024 on ~$3.3B of contract sales is roughly $50M of annualized margin. The most probable explanation is mechanical — rebranded Diamond and Bluegreen inventory attracting the full royalty — but public disclosure does not let an outsider distinguish that from the escalated royalty rate the FY2025 risk factors say applies if annual rebranding milestones are missed. There is no evidence of a missed milestone; the ambiguity itself is the problem, because the two readings have very different implications.

3. **Real estate margin ex-adjustments compressed from 28.0% (2023) to 18.3% (2025)** while FY2025 sales and marketing expense, net rose 5.2% to $1,535M against a 4.3% decline in sales revenue.

Earnings mix has shifted toward the annuity: real estate fell from 47% of segment profit in 2023 to 36% in 2025, resort/club rose from 32% to 44%, financing from 17% to 24%. That looks like diversification but is not: the same owner pays the loan interest, the maintenance fee and the club dues. All three streams depend on one household's ability to pay.

### The credit book is the balance sheet

As of June 30, 2026, gross timeshare financing receivables were $4,978M against total allowance of $1,414M (28.4% coverage) and total equity of $1,258M. Of the $664M increase since December 31, 2025, $494M came from the Elara acquisition rather than organic origination.

Provision expense as a percentage of owned (non-FFS) contract sales:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provision ($M) | 171 | 363 | 422 | 95 | 122 |
| Owned contract sales ($M) | 1,666 | 2,462 | 2,767 | 692 | 706 |
| **Provision rate** | 10.3% | 14.7% | 15.3% | 13.7% | **17.3%** |

This is the cleanest deterioration signal in the filing, and it matters mechanically: under HGV's policy the provision on originated loans is recorded as a reduction of revenue at the point of sale, so a rising provision rate suppresses reported Sales of VOIs, net — it hits the top line, not just a below-the-line reserve.

Delinquency also worsened: balances 121+ days past due rose from $542M (12.6% of gross) to $698M (14.0%), and 14.1% excluding Elara, so this is not an acquisition artifact. Non-accrual balances rose from 13.5% to 14.9% of gross. The counter-evidence is genuine and should be weighted: HGV fully reserves at 121 days and writes off only after foreclosure completes, so the bucket accumulates; total write-offs fell to $146M in H1 2026 from $216M in H1 2025, the 31–90 day bucket improved slightly (2.34% → 2.23%), allowance coverage was roughly flat, and origination FICO was 747 versus 751. Slower foreclosure throughput is a fully plausible driver. We treat the provision rate as the reliable signal and the 121+ bucket as ambiguous. The 10-K's own sensitivity: a 0.5pt increase in projected default rates adds $26M to the allowance.

Financing propensity rose to 68% in H1 2026 from 65% — HGV is retaining more credit risk per dollar sold, which supports current contract sales at the cost of future provision.

### Capital structure and cash

Corporate debt gross of $4,926M at a 5.626% weighted average, plus $2,933M of non-recourse securitization debt at 5.035%, totals $7,859M against total equity of $1,258M. Corporate net debt of $4,654M over the $1,245M guidance midpoint is 3.74x, reconciling to management's cited 3.8x pro forma. Including non-recourse debt, the figure is 6.1x — investors should be explicit about which they use.

A standard Altman Z-Score places HGV in the distress zone, but this is substantially structural: the screen penalizes retained earnings/total assets and market equity/total liabilities, both crushed by $2.4B of buybacks charged against retained earnings and by consolidating $2,933M of non-recourse debt whose recourse is limited to pledged receivables. What the screen correctly flags is that there is almost no balance-sheet cushion for a credit shock. HGV states compliance with all maintenance and financial covenants as of June 30, 2026.

Liquidity and funding are the genuine positives. Cash $272M plus restricted $296M; revolver availability $463M; Timeshare Facility availability $755M after a May 2026 upsize from $850M to $1.0B with maturity extended to May 2029; management cited $735M of liquidity. On July 17, 2026 HGV refinanced the Term Loan B due 2028 into an $850M facility due 2033 at unchanged SOFR+200, materially de-risking the $1,257M 2028 corporate maturity wall. Securitization access is healthy: April 2026 ($500M) and June 2026 ($300M) both cleared at 98% advance rates with senior tranches at 4.67% and 4.83%, against a 14.4% asset yield. For a business that lives on securitization, this is the single most important solvency datapoint and it is currently favorable.

The offsetting risk is rate reset: $550M notional of swaps converting the Term Loan B SOFR leg to average fixed rates of 1.55% mature between 2026 and 2028, implying roughly $11.5–23M of annual interest headwind depending on whether 1.55% is the swapped leg or the all-in rate (disclosure is ambiguous). Approximately $2.9B, or ~40% of total debt, is variable-rate. Higher-for-longer rates are a direct earnings headwind here.

## Growth Analysis

FY2025 looked operationally strong: contract sales +10.4% to $3,314M on VPG +7.8% to $3,851 and tour flow +2.6%. That reversed in 2026.

| | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contract sales ($M) | 834 | 810 (−2.9%) | 1,555 | 1,529 (−1.7%) |
| Tour flow | 225,222 | 239,064 (+6.1%) | 399,747 | 428,510 (+7.2%) |
| VPG | $3,690 | $3,372 (−8.6%) | $3,874 | $3,551 (−8.3%) |

Two readings compete. Management's framing is remediable execution failure at two named Bluegreen properties, supported by four consecutive quarters of tour growth, 722,000 Club members and HGV Max membership up 24% to roughly 300,000. The alternative is a permanent mix shift: the FY2025 10-K itself flags "decreases in call transfers from Hilton due to increasing consumer reliance on digital tools" as a revenue risk, which reframes the Bass Pro Shops channel (145 kiosks under a 10-year exclusive) and the Choice Hotels alliance as substitutes for a decaying, high-quality lead funnel rather than incremental adds. Retail-kiosk and third-party-brand tours are more plentiful but plausibly convert at lower rates and lower prices. The rising provision rate is more consistent with the second reading than the first, as is the modest shift in existing-owner mix (74% of FY2025 contract sales, 73% in H1 2026).

We cannot resolve this from outside the company: peer VPG comparables for Travel + Leisure and Marriott Vacations Worldwide were not obtainable, so whether the 8–9% VPG decline is company-specific or sector-wide remains open. That uncertainty is itself a reason to demand a wider margin of safety than the current price offers.

Estimate momentum is negative and accelerating in the near term:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $1.28 | $1.51 | −15.2% |
| Next quarter | $1.78 | $1.85 | −3.7% |
| FY2026 | $4.87 | $5.48 | −11.2% |
| FY2027 | $5.70 | $5.89 | −3.2% |

The earnings record compounds the visibility problem: over four quarters HGV posted one beat (+92.2%) and three misses (−72.5%, −45.9%, −10.2%). That volatility is partly deferral timing, but it argues for discounting rather than extrapolating consensus.

The one structurally growing line is the annuity: Club membership, HGV Max penetration (~40%, up 24%), and cost-plus HOA fees at 70.8% margin. Growing the owner base genuinely feeds that annuity, which is the strongest argument for management's volume orientation.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value.** $3,569M equity plus $4,654M corporate net debt equals $8,223M. Against FY2026 guidance midpoint of $1,245M, that is **6.6x**; against FY2025 actual $1,152M, **7.1x**. Timeshare operators historically clear 7–9x EV/EBITDA, so HGV screens modestly cheap on this basis.

**Per-share earnings.** At $45.93, HGV trades **9.4x** FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.87 and **8.1x** FY2027 consensus of $5.70. Note that on July 23 — before the Q2 print — the stock at $48.52 traded 8.85x the then-current $5.48. Because the price has recovered most of the post-print decline while estimates were cut 11.2%, the stock is *more* expensive on the operative metric than it was a month ago. That materially weakens the "price fell, thesis intact" argument.

**Earnings quality caveat.** Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $0.89 (≈$72M) compares with $12M of GAAP net income attributable to stockholders — roughly a $60M pre-tax bridge composed of the $48M disposition loss, $14M of acquisition/integration expense, the $28M construction deferral, amortization and share-based comp. HGV has carried acquisition and integration expense continuously since 2021 (Diamond, Bluegreen, now Elara). A serially adjusted earnings stream warrants a lower multiple than a clean one, which is precisely why the optical 8x has persisted for years without re-rating.

**Peer context.** Peer medians are P/E 27.1 and yield 2.6%, but the set is heterogeneous and mostly not comparable: VAC is loss-making (−12.7x), CHH 14.7x and WH 27.1x are asset-light franchisors with no consumer loan book, MTN 33.4x is a different cycle. The relevant comp is TNL at 19.9x GAAP. HGV pays no dividend, so it offers no yield support. Against TNL, HGV's discount is defensible on leverage (6.1x all-in) and credit exposure rather than mispriced.

**Cash-flow yield.** Management's 55–65% conversion on $1,245M implies $685–810M of adjusted free cash flow, or a 19–23% yield; management guided to the lower half. On a GAAP basis, operating cash flow was $300M in FY2025 less roughly $80M of capex and capitalized software, or about a 6% yield. Both measures are defensible; the honest framing is that HGV converts EBITDA to distributable cash efficiently *provided* securitization markets remain open at 98% advance rates and the receivables perform. H1 2026 GAAP OCF of $262M should not be annualized — it was flattered by a +$227M deferred-revenue inflow that reverses non-cash on project completion, plus $211M of non-cash provision and $47M of non-cash disposition loss, against much lower cash taxes ($47M versus $130M).

**Analyst targets.** Low $46.00, median $53.00, mean $56.60, high $74.00; ratings 1 strong buy / 3 buy / 6 hold / 0 sell. The current price sits marginally below the lowest published target. On stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, targets act as floors. Here estimates are falling 11% and the targets are of uncertain vintage relative to the July 30 print, so we do not treat the $46 low as support.

**Scenario arithmetic.**

- **Bear $29 (−37%):** VPG does not recover, FY2027 economic EBITDA flat at ~$1,150M, multiple compresses to 6.0x. EV $6.90B less $4.65B net corporate debt = $2.25B equity ÷ 77.0M shares.
- **Base $49 (+6.7%):** FY2026 lands near the low end at $1,225M, FY2027 ~$1,200M, 7.0x. EV $8.40B less $4.60B = $3.80B ÷ 76.5M shares. Cross-checks against 8.6x a modestly reduced FY2027 EPS of ~$5.70.
- **Bull $64 (+39%):** VPG stabilizes in 2027, economic EBITDA $1,270M, 7.3x, buyback authorization renewed and share count to ~75M.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives roughly **−2%** expected return. The 43% equity-to-EV ratio is the mechanism: a 10% EBITDA shortfall at a constant multiple removes roughly a third of the equity value. That torque works both ways, but with the next catalyst guided down, the distribution is skewed left.

Valuation score of 5.2 reflects genuinely low multiples offset by falling estimates, low earnings quality, absence of yield support, and no identified near-term catalyst for the market to re-rate upward.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors are Marriott Vacations Worldwide, Travel + Leisure, Disney Vacation Club, Holiday Inn Club Vacations, Westgate Resorts and the Berkley Group.

**What is durable.** The cost-plus HOA management fee is structurally insulated from occupancy and rate, and its 70.8% margin held across two years. Club and exchange scale (722,000 members) creates real switching friction. The $14.7B contract-sales pipeline, 35% of it capital-efficient, gives long-dated inventory optionality. The Bass Pro exclusive (145 stores, 10-year term) plus the 51%-owned Big Cedar Lodge JV and Choice Hotels alliance are distribution assets peers do not have.

**What is deteriorating.** The Hilton license — the core moat — is getting more expensive faster than sales grow (5.70% → 7.26% of contract sales), and the company itself discloses that Hilton call transfers are shrinking as consumers book digitally. Capital-efficient sourcing fell from 26% of FY2025 contract sales (17% FFS + 9% JIT) to 24% in H1 2026 (15% + 9%), a deliberate move away from the capital-light model the 10-K identifies as a strategic advantage. The Elara acquisition (April 29, 2026: $131M for the remaining 75%, generating just $1M of goodwill, adding $411M of net receivables and $310M of assumed non-recourse debt) converted a fee-for-service relationship into owned inventory — Q2 FFS commissions fell 23.8% to $64M and Elara-related related-party commissions fell from $39M to $14M. The trade raises Adjusted EBITDA and financing profit while raising balance-sheet intensity and credit exposure. The purchased-credit-deteriorated mark ($46M paid for $94M par, a ~51% discount) does suggest disciplined underwriting of the acquired paper.

**Roll-up returns have not shown up.** Deferral-normalized earnings rose 12.3% cumulatively from 2023 to 2025 ($1,026M → $1,152M) while revenue rose 27% and the company deployed $1,571M of investing cash and $1,664M of net new debt in 2024 for Bluegreen. Alternative explanations exist — legacy HGV/Diamond earnings may have declined off exceptional 2022–23 post-COVID comparisons, and rebranding costs plus the escalating royalty are front-loaded — but two and a half years after closing, the incremental contribution net of legacy erosion is modest relative to the leverage taken on. A second-year execution failure at two acquired Bluegreen properties reinforces the concern about integration capability.

## Risk Assessment

**Hilton rebranding milestones (highest-consequence, least-visible).** Per the FY2025 risk factors, missing annual rebranding target milestones triggers an escalated royalty fee, and a cumulative shortfall could permit Hilton to prohibit future offering and sale of HGV Max. HGV still needs to rebrand "the majority" of Bluegreen properties. Termination of the license "would materially harm our business." There is no evidence of a miss, and the mechanical explanation for the royalty step-up is more probable, but the disclosure does not let an outsider distinguish the two, and HGV Max is the growth engine (~40% penetration, +24%).

**Consumer credit concentration.** $4,978M gross receivables against $1,258M total equity, at 14.4% weighted-average yield to a borrower base that includes $824M at FICO 600–699, $89M below 600, and $364M unscored. Provision at 17.3% of owned sales with propensity rising to 68% means HGV is buying volume with credit. Because provisions reduce revenue at the point of sale, deterioration compresses reported sales, not just reserves. Annual default rates were 9.86% (2025), 10.77% (2024), 8.56% (2023).

**Guidance bridge and Q3 print.** H2 2026 requires +6.2% to +12.6% EBITDA growth against guided Q3 contract sales down mid-single digits, Q3 VPG down high single digits, and incremental Q3 SG&A. The bridge may be carried by non-real-estate lines — Elara's consolidated financing profit, $10–12M of maintenance-fee relief, cost actions — none of which require VPG recovery. But the odds of a guidance trim at the late-October print are meaningful.

**Buyback authorization exhaustion.** $103M remained as of July 23, 2026 against a ~$150M quarterly pace. Board renewal is expected but not disclosed. The 19.9% share reduction since December 2024 has been funded by debt and receivable monetization (FY2025 repurchases $600M against $300M of GAAP OCF; H1 2026 $300M of repurchases plus $100M for Elara alongside +$323M of gross corporate debt and a revolver draw from $130M to $465M). A slower pace removes a mechanical support.

**Rate reset.** $550M of swaps at 1.55% maturing 2026–2028, ~$2.9B of variable-rate debt, and a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Legal.** Legal accruals rose from $8M to $19M in six months. The FY2025 risk factors describe a Manhattan Club arbitration decision requiring HGV to cure, including purchasing inventory and assuming a management agreement; quantum is not disclosed. Surety bond capacity fell from $439M to $336M, and the 10-K notes such capacity depends partly on HGV's credit rating.

**Apollo overhang.** Apollo stepped down from 18,245,825 shares to 12,495,825 (16.1% of shares outstanding) via the June 2026 secondary at $50.00, of which HGV repurchased 750,000 shares for $38M. A material overhang remains.

**Macro.** Consumer discretionary big-ticket purchases financed at 14.4% are among the most rate- and confidence-sensitive categories. A softening consumer and elevated financing costs point the wrong way for a business selling large-ticket discretionary intervals to mid-income households.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 55.9% against 39.7% one-year realized volatility (82nd percentile within the chain) in clear backwardation indicates the market is pricing imminent, event-specific stress rather than a general re-rating; the ±5.2% implied move on the three-day expiry is mostly mechanical. The more informative reading is the term structure of skew: the near-dated chain is call-heavy (−8.3%) while the 3-month (+11.1%) and 6-month (+3.6%) tenors are put-heavy, with implied moves of 12.5% and 19.7% respectively — the longer tenors span the late-October Q3 print and the December quarter. Put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.08 on July 23 to 7.34 on August 17, a large accumulation of downside open interest concurrent with a 7.4% price decline. Combined with 11.9% short interest and 5.2 days to cover, positioning is meaningfully bearish without being crowded enough to create major squeeze risk. This corroborates rather than drives the fundamental view; it is not a reason to be short, but it does argue against treating the current price as a supported floor.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($64, +39%)

The 2026 VPG decline is what management says it is — remediable execution failure at Orlando and Myrtle Beach following leadership changes — and tour flow up 6–7% for four consecutive quarters becomes revenue as conversion normalizes. Deferral-normalized EBITDA reaches the guidance midpoint in 2026 and ~$1,270M in 2027 on VPG recovery plus the $10–12M maintenance-fee relief, Elara's consolidated financing profit and cost actions. The provision rate reverts to the mid-teens as new-buyer mix stabilizes. Securitization access stays at 98% advance rates against a 14.4% asset yield, adjusted free cash flow runs $700M+, the board renews the authorization and share count falls to ~75M. At 7.3x EV/EBITDA — still a discount to the historical timeshare range — equity is worth roughly $64. Goldman's $55 target (June 1, 2026 upgrade to Neutral from Sell) and the $53 consensus median sit inside this path.

### Bear Case ($29, −37%)

The VPG decline is structural mix, not execution: Hilton call transfers keep decaying as consumers book digitally, and Bass Pro kiosk and Choice-channel tours convert at permanently lower rates and prices to weaker-credit new buyers. Provision stays at or above 17% of owned sales, financing propensity keeps rising, and the 121+ bucket proves to be borrower deterioration rather than foreclosure throughput. Economic EBITDA flattens near $1,150M, missing FY2026 guidance and prompting a 2027 reset. The rental loss persists on $2,046M of unsold inventory, the royalty steps up further toward 7.5%+ of contract sales, swap roll-off adds $11–23M of interest, and the board slows buybacks to protect 3.8x leverage. The multiple compresses to 6.0x on a business with rising credit risk and no equity cushion, taking the stock to $29.

### Base Case ($49, +6.7%)

FY2026 lands at or near the low end of $1,225M with a possible Q3 guidance trim. VPG stabilizes in 2027 without recovering to 2025 levels; economic EBITDA holds around $1,200M. The annuity segments continue growing, the credit book performs adequately, securitization stays open, and the buyback authorization is renewed at a moderated pace. At 7.0x EV/EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $49, about 7% above the current price — inside the noise band for a stock with 39.7% realized volatility.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $49 (+6.7%), bull $64 (+39%), bear $29 (−37%). Probability-weighted expected return is approximately −2%, and the downside path is the one with the identified near-term catalyst.

## Conclusion

HGV owns one genuinely good business — cost-plus HOA and Club management at a 70.8% margin, serving 722,000 members — attached to a leveraged consumer-lending operation and a sales engine that is currently deteriorating. The Q2 2026 print was not a valuation event; it was fundamental information. Tour flow rose 6.1% while VPG fell 8.6%, the provision rate reached 17.3% of owned contract sales, financing propensity rose to 68%, and consensus FY2026 EPS was cut 11.2% in thirty days. Because the share price has recovered most of the post-print decline, HGV is more expensive on forward earnings today than it was in July.

The optical valuation — 6.6x forward EV/EBITDA, 8.1x FY2027 adjusted EPS, a 19–23% adjusted free-cash-flow yield — is real but qualified by earnings quality (a $60M single-quarter GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge, five consecutive years of acquisition and integration adjustments) and by the fact that the cash-flow measure nets loan originations against the securitization borrowings that fund them. With equity at roughly 43% of enterprise value and no meaningful book cushion after $2,449M of buybacks, small changes in EBITDA produce large changes in the equity. The next scheduled catalyst is a Q3 print management has already guided down on both contract sales and VPG, into a guidance bridge that requires +6% to +13% H2 growth.

We downgrade from HOLD to REDUCE with an overall score of 4.4, reversing the improving trajectory of recent quarters on specific new evidence rather than on price action. The financial-health improvement earned by the July 17 Term Loan B refinancing and continued 98% securitization advance rates is real and prevents this from being an AVOID; the balance sheet is funded and covenant-compliant. But growth, valuation quality and risk all moved against the position in the last month, and the asymmetry from $45.93 favors reducing exposure and revisiting either at $37–39 or on two quarters of confirmed VPG and provision-rate stabilization.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q2 non-GAAP EPS miss versus consensus is −10.2% (Phase 1), not −10.3% (the −10.3% figure is the intraday share-price decline, not the EPS surprise); and unsupported macro claims (the "$1.5T defense budget"-style specifics — here the "10-year at 4.63%," "three FOMC dissents," "mortgage rates at a one-year high," and "$30,000+ interval price") were removed as untraceable to the sources. None of these change the REDUCE thesis, which rests on the deferral-normalized EBITDA bridge, provision rate, and equity-to-EV asymmetry — all of which check out.
