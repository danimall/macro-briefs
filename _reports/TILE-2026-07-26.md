**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Interface, Inc. has moved from a low-growth, moderately levered modular-carpet maker into an earnings inflection. Net income nearly tripled from $44.5M in FY2023 to $116.1M in FY2025, gross margin expanded roughly 370 basis points to 38.7%, and the company redeemed its entire $300M of 5.50% Senior Notes in December 2025, mechanically cutting 2026 interest expense from ~$19.5M to ~$10M. That interest saving alone adds several cents to EPS in a way that is not reversible by demand cycles. Q1 FY2026 delivered net sales +11.3%, EPS $0.40 (+82% YoY), and operating income +39%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to ~$1.45B-$1.48B in net sales with a targeted ~39.9% Q2 gross margin. Backlog rose ~15% sequentially, from $222.8M in early February to $256.6M in late April.

At $32.98, the stock trades at ~15.4x forward earnings and roughly a 6.4% FCF yield ($122M FCF against a ~$1.9B market cap). That multiple does not price in a company compounding earnings at a double-digit rate with a deleveraged balance sheet. The most important calibration point is the earnings-surprise record: Interface has beaten consensus by 21.8%, 19.1%, 24.6%, and 25.2% over the past four quarters. Against that cadence, the modest -5.5%/-3.5% trims to near-term quarterly estimates are conservative anchors that the company is very likely to beat, not a deterioration signal. Full-year and next-year estimates are both rising (+1.6% and +3.8% over 90 days). All three covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, and the lowest sell-side target ($36) sits ~9% above the current price, with a mean of $40.5 (~23% upside).

The bull case has qualifications. A meaningful slice of reported growth is Euro-driven FX translation that can reverse; the 2025 effective tax rate of 15.2% was flattered by non-recurring items; and options markets are pricing near-term stress into the August 7 print. These are second-order to the durable interest-expense and automation-driven margin levers. There is also an unrecognized call option: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs may make previously paid tariffs (>$7M in 2025 alone) refundable, and management has booked nothing for it. I rate TILE a BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $39.

## Company Overview

Interface is a global commercial flooring company incorporated in Georgia in 1973 and headquartered in Atlanta. It has evolved from a pure modular carpet-tile maker into an integrated flooring provider across four product lines: modular carpet tile (Interface®, FLOR®), Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT, launched 2016, sourced from a third-party manufacturer in South Korea), nora® rubber flooring (acquired 2018, the recognized rubber-flooring leader, strong in healthcare/education/transportation), and FLOR® premium area rugs.

The business runs two geographic segments: AMS (Americas, ~61% of sales) and EAAA (Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, ~39%). Roughly 43% of revenue is denominated in non-USD currencies. Manufacturing includes carpet-tile plants in LaGrange and West Point (Georgia), the Netherlands, UK, China, and Australia; rubber flooring in Weinheim, Germany; and outsourced LVT in South Korea.

Interface's differentiation rests on design and modularity leadership plus a differentiated sustainability position: 200+ cradle-to-gate carbon-negative carpet-tile styles, CQuest™ carbon-negative backings, ReEntry® take-back/recycling, and a science-based-targets commitment. It has ranked a Top 10 global sustainability leader by GlobeScan for 28 consecutive years. Management has deliberately diversified away from cyclical corporate office into education, healthcare, government, and residential, so that over half of sales now come from non-office segments, a structural mitigant against office-vacancy overhang.

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe an accelerating, high-quality inflection rather than a mature flat-liner.

**Revenue is accelerating:** $1,261.5M (2023) → $1,315.7M (+4.3%, 2024) → $1,386.9M (+5.4%, 2025). Q1 FY2026 was $331.0M, up 11.3% YoY, though that figure is aided by an extra 14th week and ~5% of Euro FX tailwind; underlying currency-neutral volume growth was ~6-7%. Investors should anchor on the currency-neutral figure since FX can reverse.

**Margin expansion is the core story.** Gross margin climbed 35.0% → 36.7% → 38.7%, with Q1'26 at 38.3% and management guiding Q2 to ~39.9%. Operating margin rose 8.3% → 10.2% → 11.8%. Net margin more than doubled, 3.6% → 6.6% → 8.4%. The drivers are durable: higher average selling prices, favorable product mix, and manufacturing efficiency from automation and robotics investment that improves fixed-cost absorption as volumes rise.

**Earnings power roughly tripled.** Net income $44.5M → $86.9M → $116.1M; diluted EPS $0.76 → $1.48 → $1.96. Q1'26 net income was $23.6M ($0.40 diluted) versus $13.0M ($0.22), up 82%.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow rose $142M → $148M → $167.9M, comfortably above net income and covering ~$46M of 2025 capex (rising to ~$55M in 2026 for automation). FCF was approximately $122M in 2025, a ~6.4% yield on market cap.

**The balance sheet was transformed.** Long-term debt fell from $408.6M (2023) to $302.3M (2024) to $172.8M (2025). In December 2025 Interface refinanced the redeemed notes with a $170M term loan and extended maturity to December 2030 at a ~$3.1M extinguishment cost. Total debt at Q1'26 was $196.7M at a 4.94% weighted rate. D/E is 0.88, current ratio 2.34, equity $640.7M. Altman Z is 3.34 (safe zone); ROE 18.1%; ROA 9.6%.

**Two quality caveats matter.** First, the 2025 effective tax rate fell to 15.2% from 23.4% on deferred tax remeasurement and FDII benefits, partly non-recurring; Q1'26 ran at 18.3%, closer to a sustainable rate. Second, reported growth is FX-flattered. Neither undermines the operating and deleveraging story, but both mean the reported EPS growth rate overstates the underlying trend somewhat.

**Capital allocation is turning shareholder-friendly.** After no buybacks in 2023-2024, Interface repurchased $18.1M in 2025 and another $12.0M (461K shares at $26.04) in Q1'26, with ~$52.7M left on the $100M authorization. The quarterly dividend was tripled ($0.01→$0.03), though the yield remains a token ~0.4%.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs. First, operating leverage: management is investing in automation and robotics that lower unit costs as volumes climb, driving the margin expansion documented above. This is self-reinforcing when volumes hold: Q1'26 EAAA adjusted operating income rose 57.9% on fixed-cost leverage, and AMS AOI rose 20.3%. Second, the interest-expense reset adds roughly $9-10M of pre-tax income in 2026 independent of demand. Third, demand momentum is corroborated by the ~15% sequential backlog growth and by a deliberate finished-goods inventory build ($294M at Q1'26) that management ties to expected 2H'26 demand rather than a demand warning.

The earnings-surprise cadence is central to how I weight the estimate revisions. Four consecutive beats of ~20-25% is a structural pattern, not luck. It means the near-term quarterly consensus ($0.64 current quarter) functions as a conservative anchor. The prior HOLD leaned on the near-term estimate trims as a caution, but a 5% trim to an estimate that has been beaten by 20% every quarter is not a deterioration signal. The estimates that reflect the actual trajectory, full year (+1.6%) and next year (+3.8%) over 90 days, are rising. Consensus EPS of $2.14 (current year) and $2.35 (next year) very likely understates the true figure if the beat cadence continues.

The FX-neutral growth of ~3% in 2025 and ~6% in Q1'26 is solid but not spectacular; the earnings-growth story is more about margin and interest than about volume. That is a legitimate qualifier: investors extrapolating the 11.3% headline top-line will be disappointed if the Euro reverses. But margin expansion plus the interest reset supports double-digit EPS growth even at mid-single-digit currency-neutral revenue growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.98, TILE trades at ~15.4x trailing/forward earnings and ~6.4% FCF yield. For a company that nearly tripled net income in two years, expanded gross margin 370bps, deleveraged its balance sheet, and is guiding higher, that is a low multiple. On consensus current-year EPS of $2.14, the forward P/E is ~15.4x; on the more likely beat-adjusted figure (say $2.25-2.35 given the cadence), it drops toward 14x.

Peer benchmarking is noisy because the Office Services & Supplies comps are a heterogeneous group: PBI at 16.7x, MLKN at 105.5x, HNI at 1854x (distorted by trough earnings), WSC negative, MSA at 21.5x. The peer median of 105.5x is meaningless. The cleanest read is that TILE trades in line with the most-comparable name (PBI at 16.7x) despite materially stronger earnings momentum and a cleaner balance sheet trajectory.

The framework I apply is skeptical of "cheap" as a standalone signal; a low multiple is often a value trap. Here the cheapness is paired with specific, identifiable catalysts: the mechanical interest-expense halving, rising full-year estimates, a raised guidance, and the beat cadence. Because forward P/E is well under the 30x threshold where analyst targets stop being reliable floors, the consensus targets carry weight. On an inflection name where estimates are being revised up, those targets tend to lag and get raised as consensus catches up.

There is also an unpriced call option: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs means Interface may recover tariffs already paid (>$7M in 2025 plus Q1'26 on Korean LVT and German rubber), and management has recognized nothing. Any refund is pure upside to reported earnings and book value.

I assign a valuation score of 7.0, not higher, because the FX and tax-rate quality issues mean the trailing EPS overstates the sustainable run-rate somewhat, and the earnings are cyclically exposed to commercial construction. But the multiple does not reflect the transformation.

## Competitive Landscape

Interface competes in a global flooring market against carpet-tile, broadloom, LVT, other hard-surface, and polished-concrete makers, several of which are larger, vertically integrated in fiber extrusion, and therefore less dependent on outside suppliers. Interface is not the low-cost producer; it introduced the lower-priced Open Air™ line specifically to defend the value segment.

Its competitive position is stable-to-improving rather than dominant. The moat rests on design and modularity leadership (the long David Oakey Designs relationship), made-to-order flexibility, and a differentiated sustainability position that is hard to replicate: 28 consecutive years as a GlobeScan top-10 sustainability leader, carbon-negative product lines, and a science-based-targets commitment. As corporate procurement increasingly weights carbon and circularity, that position is a durable advantage.

The trajectory is favorable on two fronts. The diversification into healthcare, education, and government (now >50% of sales) reduces exposure to office cyclicality, and corporate office is itself recovering: it was the leading growth driver in Q1'26 (47% of sales, up from 45%). Gross-margin expansion outpacing peers is direct evidence of pricing power and mix improvement. The main competitive vulnerability is supplier dependence on a single primary LVT supplier in South Korea and a small number of nylon-yarn suppliers, which vertically integrated rivals do not share.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs are the single biggest company-specific swing factor, and they cut both ways.** LVT is 100% imported from South Korea and all rubber flooring from Germany, both into the U.S.; tariffs added ~$7.3M to 2025 COGS and continued to pressure Q1'26 margin. The offsetting development is the February 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of IEEPA tariffs, which makes prior payments potentially refundable, an unrecognized upside. Net, the tariff exposure is a known, partly-hedged risk with an embedded recovery option.

**Commercial office cyclicality** remains the structural overhang; office is 47% of Q1'26 sales and the filings repeatedly flag hybrid-work vacancy pressure. The diversification strategy is the direct mitigant, and the current cycle is recovering, but a downturn in commercial renovation/construction would deleverage the fixed-cost base and reverse the margin gains.

**FX translation risk** is material given 43% non-USD revenue. Euro strength added $14.9M to 2025 sales and $13.4M to Q1'26 and drove a large accumulated OCI translation loss (-$101M at Q1'26). This is currently a tailwind that can reverse.

**Supplier concentration** (single LVT supplier, few nylon suppliers) is a genuine operational tail risk since Interface cannot manufacture its own fiber or LVT.

**Governance items** are minor: insider selling by three executives (Hausmann, Foshee, Poppens) after a strong run with no offsetting open-market buying, and an unidentified June 4, 2026 Item 5.02 filing that could not be retrieved (all four NEOs were transacting normally through May, so this most likely reflects a board/director change). Worth monitoring, not a red flag.

I score risk factors 5.5: the tariff, office-cyclicality, and FX exposures are real and partly binary, but they are broadly known and priced, and the tariff-refund optionality provides asymmetric recovery upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 58.5% versus 1-year historical vol of 37.1%; the options market is pricing rich near-term uncertainty, and the term structure is in backwardation (58.5% near-term vs 41.0% long-term), signaling that traders expect a discrete event, almost certainly the August 7 Q2 print. The put/call skew of 9.6% indicates elevated put demand, and short interest is 10.8% of float (7.5 days to cover). The implied one-month move is ±13.7%. For a fundamental buyer, the rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing or for selling volatility rather than paying up ahead of the print; the elevated put skew and short interest are a divergence from the bullish sell-side consensus worth flagging, though the 10.8% SI is not so crowded as to create meaningful squeeze dynamics. The backwardation resolves at earnings: a beat consistent with the cadence would collapse near-term IV.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($42-50):** The beat cadence continues, so consensus EPS proves conservative and actual FY2026 EPS lands near $2.30-2.45. Margin expansion holds as automation investment pays off and Q2 gross margin hits the guided ~39.9%. The interest-expense halving flows straight to the bottom line. Backlog and inventory build convert to 2H'26 revenue. A tariff refund is recognized as a one-time gain. On $2.35-2.45 EPS at a 17-20x multiple (still reasonable for a double-digit grower), the stock reaches $42-50, consistent with the high sell-side target.

**Bear case ($24-26):** The Euro reverses, stripping ~5% from reported growth and turning the FX tailwind into a headwind. Commercial office demand rolls over, deleveraging the fixed-cost base and compressing margins back toward the mid-30s. The 2025 tax benefit does not repeat, normalizing EPS lower. Tariff refunds never materialize and tariff costs persist. On normalized EPS closer to $1.70-1.80 at a de-rated 14x, the stock falls to $24-26, roughly a 21-27% decline from spot.

The asymmetry favors the upside. The base case ($36-39) and bull case ($42-50) both sit above the $32.98 price; only the bear case is below. The entire covering-analyst target range ($36-50) sits above spot. That distribution, combined with a 15.4x forward multiple on a company with a mechanical, non-reversible interest tailwind and a proven ~20% beat cadence, points to BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $39** (12 months). Math: ~$2.25 beat-adjusted FY2026 EPS × ~17.3x forward multiple ≈ $39. This uses an EPS slightly above the $2.14 consensus to reflect the four-quarter ~20% beat pattern, at a multiple modestly above the current 15.4x as consensus catches up to the raised guidance. This is ~18% upside from $32.98 and sits between the bear and bull scenarios.
- **Bull case target: $50** (~52% upside) — sustained beats to ~$2.40 EPS at ~20x, plus a recognized tariff refund. Matches the high sell-side target.
- **Bear case target: $25** (~24% downside) — FX reversal, office rollover, and margin compression to normalized ~$1.75 EPS at 14x.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the August 7, 2026 Q2 print as the near-term catalyst (guided ~39.9% gross margin is the key checkpoint) and full-year execution as the primary driver.
- **Upside/downside from current:** +18% to base, +52% to bull, -24% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Q2 FY2026 gross margin comes in materially below the guided ~39.9% (e.g., below 38%), signaling the margin-expansion story is stalling rather than continuing.
2. Backlog declines sequentially below ~$220M (from $256.6M), reversing the demand-momentum signal.
3. Currency-neutral revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating the volume base is deteriorating beneath the FX and interest tailwinds.

## Conclusion

Interface is a margin-and-deleveraging inflection trading at a multiple that does not reflect the transformation. Net income roughly tripled 2023-2025, gross margin expanded ~370bps to 38.7%, the balance sheet was cut in half and refinanced to cut 2026 interest expense mechanically from ~$19.5M to ~$10M, backlog rose 15% sequentially, and management raised guidance. At ~15.4x forward earnings with rising full-year estimates, four straight ~20-25% earnings beats, an all-Buy sell-side with a target floor 9% above spot, and an unrecognized tariff-refund call option, the evidence points to a favorable asymmetry.

The prior HOLD hinged on modest near-term estimate trims, but those trims do not survive the beat cadence: the near-term consensus is a conservative anchor the company routinely beats by ~20%. The legitimate quality caveats, FX-flattered growth, a partly non-recurring 2025 tax rate, and rich near-term options-implied volatility into the August print, are real but second-order to the durable interest and automation levers. I upgrade to **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $39. Given the rich near-term IV and elevated put skew, patient buyers may prefer to accumulate on any post-earnings volatility crush rather than pay up immediately ahead of the August 7 print.
