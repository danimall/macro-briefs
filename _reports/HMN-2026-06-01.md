**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Horace Mann is an 80-year-old multiline insurer focused on the U.S. K-12 educator niche, and it has produced a clean earnings recovery off its 2022 trough. FY2025 net income rose 57.7% to $162.1M, diluted EPS reached a record $3.90, ROE recovered to 11.7%, and book value per share grew to $36.47. The driver was the Property & Casualty turnaround: P&C net income more than doubled to $112.4M as the auto combined ratio improved to 96.5% and the property combined ratio fell to 78.3%. At ~$45.37, the stock trades at roughly 10-11x core EPS guidance ($4.20-$4.50) and ~1.24x reported book (~1.13x ex-unrealized losses), a discount to the multi-line median P/E of 12.9x.

The recovery is real, but the quality of the 2025 print is partly cyclical. Catastrophe losses fell to $61.7M, the lowest in five years and well below the ~$90M management assumes for 2026, and the company booked $18.8M of favorable prior-year reserve releases. Strip those out and the underlying improvement, while genuine in loss-ratio terms, is less dramatic than the +57.7% headline. The acquired Supplemental & Group Benefits segment, which was supposed to be the growth engine, moved the wrong way: segment net income fell 25.5% to $45.0M and the Group Benefits benefit ratio jumped 8.0 points to 45.8%. The consolidated number masks a divergence in which one segment carries the franchise while the acquired one compresses earnings.

The central tension is the valuation/TSR disconnect. Despite record earnings and an 11.7% ROE, HMN's cumulative TSR index sits at 131 versus a peer group at 210, a gap that has widened every year since 2021. The market is paying ~10-11x for the recovery, which is either an opportunity (if P&C margins hold and Supplemental stabilizes) or a justified discount (if earnings are cyclical and a slowly shrinking policy count signals franchise erosion). The current-quarter estimate trend, an in-motion leadership transition, and CEO selling near highs tilt the read toward uncertainty rather than asymmetric value. We rate HMN HOLD.

## Company Overview

Horace Mann is the largest multiline financial-services company dedicated to America's ~14 million K-12 educator households. Its moat rests on direct in-school access: B2B relationships with more than half of U.S. school districts, local agent representation in over 60% of operating territories, and union/district endorsements. The 403(b) retirement product functions as the "front door," after which educators add auto, home, life, and supplemental coverage.

The company reports four segments:

1. **Property & Casualty** — personal auto and home with an "Educator Advantage" package; 326K auto and 164K property risks; ~$830M net premiums written.
2. **Life & Retirement** — 403(b) annuities (a leading K-12 player) plus life products; $5.9B annuity AUM and $21.5B life in force.
3. **Supplemental & Group Benefits** — individual supplemental and employer group products, built via the 2019 NTA and 2022 Madison National Life acquisitions.
4. **Corporate & Other** — financing, investment gains/losses, and legacy commercial claims.

The competitive vulnerability is scale. HMN competes against State Farm, Allstate, Progressive, and GEICO in P&C; TIAA, Voya, and Corebridge in retirement; and Aflac and Unum in supplemental. Many carry higher financial-strength ratings, which matters increasingly as districts emphasize ratings in 403(b) provider selection.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows a clear recovery:

| ($M) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | 1,382.9 | 1,491.9 | 1,595.2 | 1,701.4 |
| Net income | (2.6) | 45.0 | 102.8 | 162.1 |
| Diluted EPS | — | ~1.08 | 2.48 | 3.90 |
| ROE (LTM) | neg. | ~4% | 8.3% | 11.7% |
| Book value/share | — | — | 31.51 | 36.47 |

P&C carried the year. Underwriting gain swung to $82.6M from $12.8M on a 5.6-point better underlying property loss ratio and lower catastrophe losses ($61.7M vs $94.9M). Management ran rate over volume: auto average premium rose 7.6% and property 12.7% while risks in force declined ~3.7% (auto -4.7%, property -1.8%). This is deliberate margin-first underwriting, but the shrinking policy count is a watch item for long-run franchise health.

Cash flow quality is solid. Operating cash flow rose 22.4% to $553.2M, comfortably above net income. The investment portfolio is $7.3B, 97.7% investment grade, average rating A+, 6.0-year duration, generating $464.3M of net investment income (+4.2%). The balance sheet still carries $312.1M of pretax net unrealized losses on fixed maturities (down from $454.5M as rates fell); book value ex-unrealized losses is $40.21 versus $36.47 reported.

Leverage is appropriate for an insurer at 28.6% debt/total capital (26.6% ex-unrealized losses), in line with management's target. Total debt is $593.4M following the September 2025 refinancing, which replaced $250M of 4.50% notes with $300M of 4.70% notes due 2030. The Altman Z-Score of 0.31 and D/E of 9.3 are artifacts of applying a manufacturing-oriented model to an insurer whose balance sheet is dominated by policyholder reserves and a bond portfolio. They are not distress signals. The relevant reads are the 11.7% ROE, the strong operating cash conversion, and stable A-level financial-strength ratings from A.M. Best, Fitch, S&P, and Moody's.

Two minor controls flags: a Q2 2025 $10.2M reduction in net investment income for an "immaterial out-of-period correction of an error," and 2025 one-time items including a $7.1M pension termination charge and a $5.0M foundation donation in Corporate & Other.

## Growth Analysis

The earnings surprise record is strong on the surface, with four consecutive beats: Q1 2026 +16.3%, Q4 2025 +1.7%, Q3 2025 +21.0%, and Q2 2025 +76.1%. Q1 2026 delivered record core EPS of $1.28, up 20% YoY, with P&C margins up ~5 points, and management reiterated full-year 2026 core EPS guidance of $4.20-$4.50 (core ROE 11%+).

The estimate revision picture is mixed. Full-year and next-year estimates are edging up (current year +2.4%/30d, +1.9%/90d; next year +0.9%/30d, +0.3%/90d), which is mildly positive. But the current quarter has been cut, down 4.5% over 30 days and 10.8% over 90 days, with the next quarter down 5.7% over 90 days. This is a small base of two-to-three analysts revising annual numbers up modestly while marking near-term quarters down. The signal is durability, not acceleration.

The growth bull case lives in Supplemental & Group Benefits, which is where profitability is deteriorating. Segment sales grew 37.5% in 2025, but segment net income fell 25.5% to $45.0M, the benefits ratio rose to 36.8% from 32.7%, and the Group Benefits benefit ratio jumped 8.0 points to 45.8%. Management attributes part of this to a tough prior-year comparison (favorable 2024 group long-term disability morbidity review) and "investment to grow," but a segment that grows top line 37% while net income falls 25% is not yet a growth engine generating earnings. Pretax margin in the segment fell from 27.1% to 19.2%.

Management is also scaling internal Generative AI across Claims, Customer Care, and Operations in 2026, framed as an efficiency lever, alongside a December 2025 voluntary Early Retirement Offering. These are reasonable cost initiatives but not growth drivers; the associated costs hit 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

HMN trades at ~10-11x core EPS guidance and ~1.24x reported book (~1.13x ex-unrealized losses). Against the multi-line insurance peer set:

|--------|------|-----|-----------|

| GNW | Genworth | 17.0 | — |
| LNC | Lincoln National | 4.1 | 5.0% |
| AFG | American Financial | 12.7 | 2.6% |
| AIZ | Assurant | 12.9 | 1.4% |
| **HMN** | **Horace Mann** | **11.5** | **3.1%** |

Peer medians are 12.9x P/E and 2.6% yield. HMN sits modestly below the median P/E and above the median yield, a small discount rather than a deep one. The discount has a coherent justification: persistent TSR underperformance, earnings quality flattered by a benign cat year and reserve releases, a shrinking policy count, and a deteriorating acquired segment.

For a HOLD, the valuation work has to be honest about why the market is cautious. A low P/E alone is not a buy signal here. On normalized earnings, the case improves but does not become asymmetric: if 2026 catastrophes normalize to the ~$90M assumption, P&C earnings compress relative to the 2025 print even with continued rate gains. The reiterated $4.20-$4.50 core EPS guidance, despite a record Q1, implies management itself expects normalization for the balance of the year. At the midpoint of $4.35, ~12x (a peer-median multiple) would imply ~$52, consistent with the analyst targets of $51-$53. Reaching that requires the market to grant a peer multiple on earnings it currently views as partly cyclical, which is plausible but not yet supported by the TSR record. The dividend (raised to $1.40, 3.1% yield, well-covered) provides yield support but is not a deep-value cushion.

## Competitive Landscape

HMN's competitive position is stable-to-slowly-eroding. The educator niche, worksite/payroll-deduction access, and 403(b) front door are genuine structural advantages that larger generalists cannot easily replicate at scale in this specific channel. But three trends point to slow compression rather than expansion.

First, risks in force fell ~3.7% in 2025; rate-driven margin gains are coming at the cost of policy count, and if rate increases outpace the market the educator base could continue to erode. Second, the 403(b) market has converged toward the more competitive 401(k) market since post-2009 rule changes, drawing in larger, higher-rated rivals, and financial-strength rating gaps versus giants are a structural headwind in district provider selection. Third, the Supplemental & Group Benefits segment, the area with the most attractive end-market (public-sector "total rewards" expansion), is growing sales but compressing margins, and group distribution is "highly concentrated in one partner" per the 10-K, a single-point-of-failure that a standard screen would miss.

The P&C improvement is competitively meaningful in that HMN underwrote profitably through the cycle recovery, but personal lines is cyclical and the company is riding the favorable side of a cycle that the broader industry shares.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe/climate concentration** is the largest earnings swing factor. The top-10 states account for 60.9% of P&C premiums, including catastrophe-prone California (15.4% of P&C direct premium, the #1 state), Texas, North Carolina, and Louisiana. The 2026 cat assumption is ~$90M with a $35M reinsurance retention and $240M of cover above it. A heavy cat year directly compresses P&C earnings, and 2025's benign result is not a base case to extrapolate.

**Supplemental & Group Benefits margin deterioration** is the one clearly negative operating trend. Whether the Group benefit ratio increase is a one-off comparison or a structural reset is unresolved and material to the growth thesis.

**Distribution-partner concentration** in group benefits ("highly concentrated in one partner") is a specific, under-appreciated risk.

**Legacy commercial claims** for asbestos, environmental, and sexual-molestation exposures from 1960s-era policies surfaced in 2024 with $17.7M of losses after zero prior reserving. These long-tail liabilities are hard to estimate and explicitly subject to "social inflation" risk.

**403(b) regulatory/access risk** includes state-mandated single-provider plans, pension reform, and reduced payroll slots.

**Governance transition.** A heavy cadence of Item 5.02 management-change 8-Ks across 2024-2026, a new CFO (Ryan Greenier) and CMO, and director-level equity grants to Victor Fetter suggest a leadership/board reshaping or possible CEO succession is underway. This is not independently confirmed from primary filings and should be verified against the March 2026 8-K and the proxy, but it adds uncertainty to the management read.

These risks are largely known and partly priced, which is why the valuation discount exists. None is an imminent binary catalyst, but in aggregate they keep idiosyncratic risk above average for a stable insurer.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 93.5% to 44.4% over the late-May window as the May 6 earnings event passed and uncertainty resolved. The 44.4% level remains elevated for a low-beta insurer, but the sharp compression indicates the binary earnings catalyst is behind the stock and the market is not pricing a near-term shock. With short interest at only 3.0% of float (4.8 days to cover), positioning is uncrowded and there is no informed bearish concentration to fade or fear. The options picture is broadly consistent with the fundamental read: a stable franchise post-earnings, with no signal of an imminent directional move in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** P&C underwriting discipline proves durable, rate actions continue to outpace loss-cost inflation, and the educator franchise stabilizes its policy count. Supplemental & Group Benefits margin compression proves to be the one-off comparison management describes, and the segment's 37% sales growth converts to earnings. 2026 catastrophes land below the $90M assumption again. Core EPS reaches the upper end of $4.50, the market grants a peer-median ~12x multiple as the TSR gap finally closes, and the stock re-rates toward $54-$57 (12-12.5x on $4.50). The 3.1% dividend and ~$55M buyback authorization support total return in the interim.

**Bear Case.** 2025 earnings prove cyclically flattered. A normalized or heavy 2026 catastrophe year compresses P&C margins, prior-year reserve releases do not repeat, and the Supplemental segment's margin compression is structural rather than a comparison effect. The shrinking policy count accelerates as rate increases outpace the market, confirming slow franchise erosion. Legacy commercial claims require further adverse development. Core EPS lands near the low end of $4.20 or below, the multiple stays depressed on persistent TSR skepticism, and the stock drifts back toward the low-$40s ($40-$42, ~9.5-10x on $4.20), roughly the 52-week low.

The realistic path sits between these. The recovery is genuine but the durability is unproven, the discount is justified rather than egregious, and the catalysts that would close the TSR gap are not yet in evidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The earnings recovery is real and the valuation is reasonable, but the quality of the 2025 print is partly cyclical, the acquired growth segment is deteriorating, near-term estimates are being cut, and the franchise is slowly shrinking in policy count. This is a hold pending evidence on durability, not a confident growth or income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two-plus consecutive quarters of Supplemental & Group Benefits net income recovery with the Group benefit ratio retreating back toward the low 40s, confirming the compression was a comparison effect.
- Stabilization or growth in P&C risks in force alongside maintained combined ratios, demonstrating the margin gains are not coming purely at the cost of the customer base.
- Full-year 2026 results landing at or above the $4.50 upper guidance bound through a normalized catastrophe year, proving the P&C improvement is structural.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A heavy catastrophe year combined with non-repeating reserve releases pushing the P&C combined ratio back above 100%.
- Continued Supplemental margin deterioration (Group benefit ratio rising further toward 50%) confirming a structural reset.
- Material adverse development on legacy asbestos/environmental/molestation reserves, or loss of the concentrated single group-distribution partner.
- Accelerating policy-count attrition signaling franchise erosion outrunning rate gains.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), with particular attention to the Supplemental segment benefit ratio and any catastrophe-season impact. The leadership-transition situation should be re-checked against the March 2026 8-K and proxy.

## Conclusion

Horace Mann has executed a credible P&C-led recovery to record earnings, a 11.7% ROE, and a reasonable ~10-11x core EPS valuation that sits modestly below peers. The problem is that the headline overstates the underlying improvement: a five-year-low catastrophe year and favorable reserve releases flattered 2025, the acquired Supplemental & Group Benefits segment is compressing margins despite 37% sales growth, policy count is shrinking, and the stock's chronic TSR underperformance reflects a market that does not yet trust the durability of the recovery. The near-term estimate cuts, the unconfirmed leadership transition, and CEO selling near highs reinforce the uncertainty.

This is a fairly valued, stable insurer with a genuine but unproven recovery. The 3.1% dividend provides a floor and the analyst targets of $51-$53 offer ~12-17% upside if the market grants a peer multiple, but reaching that requires evidence on segment stabilization and catastrophe normalization that is not yet in hand. We rate HMN HOLD and would wait for confirmation that P&C margins hold and Supplemental stabilizes before paying for the recovery.
