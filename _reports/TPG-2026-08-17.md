## Executive Summary

TPG is running two different companies at once. The franchise is compounding at a rate that would justify a growth multiple: assets under management reached $327 billion at June 30, 2026, up from $245.9 billion eighteen months earlier; fee-related earnings grew 24.6% in FY2025 to $952.6 million with 350 basis points of margin expansion to 45.2%; management has guided to a 47% FRE margin and more than $50 billion of 2026 fundraising, and $51.7 billion of AUM is already contracted either to begin earning fees or to step up existing fee rates without any new capital raised. H1 2026 capital raised was $26.5 billion against $17.2 billion a year earlier.

The shareholder-level economics have not kept pace. Non-GAAP after-tax distributable earnings per share in Q2 2026 was $0.69 — identical to Q2 2025 — even though fee-related revenue grew 26.9%. Three mechanisms explain the gap, and all three are structural rather than transient: of $319.7 million of Q2 consolidated net income, $226.2 million was attributable to non-controlling interests; weighted-average diluted units rose from 370.1 million to 385.5 million (roughly 4% annually, driven by Common Unit exchanges and RSU settlements); and realized performance allocations grew only 5.2% in FY2025 because 63% of net accrued carry at year-end sat in 2020-and-prior vintages while the 2023-2025 vintages held $55 million combined. Q2 realizations fell 22% to $5.05 billion while capital invested rose to $13.8 billion. TPG is deploying much faster than it is harvesting, which is an industry condition, not a TPG failing, but it is the binding constraint on DE per share.

The valuation question turns on a share-count subtlety that materially misprices this security in screens. The quoted $8.4 billion market capitalization corresponds to the 160.1 million Class A voting shares alone. Total economic units — 160.1 million Class A voting, 6.6 million nonvoting Class A, and 217.8 million Class B/Common Units — are 384.5 million, so economic equity value at $52.46 is roughly $20.2 billion and enterprise value approximately $21.6 billion on a consolidated-cash basis. That works out to about 20.3x FY2025 after-tax DE per unit of $2.58, roughly 18x a defensible normalized FY2026 figure of $2.90-3.00, and 18-19x estimated FY2026 FRE. Combined with a 4.50% forward dividend yield ($2.36 annualized), that is a fair price for a manager whose fee earnings grow in the mid-teens and whose per-share earnings currently do not grow at all. We rate TPG **HOLD**, with the distribution rather than appreciation carrying the return case.

## Company Overview

TPG is a global alternative asset manager founded in 1992, public since January 2022 at $29.50 per share, organized across six investment platforms. As of December 31, 2025, AUM and FY2025 management fees by platform were: Credit $93.1B / $341.8M; Capital (flagship buyout) $90.9B / $500.7M; Real Estate $38.2B / $350.1M; Growth $32.2B / $232.9M; Impact $31.3B / $276.5M; Market Solutions $17.4B / $98.1M. Fee-earning AUM was $170.1 billion, 56% of AUM, at a weighted-average management fee rate of 1.16%.

Two acquisitions defined the current shape of the firm: Angelo Gordon (November 2023), which created the Credit platform and most of Real Estate, and TPG Peppertree (July 2025, $389.6 million), which added $7.9 billion of AUM in wireless-tower infrastructure.

The legal structure matters for every per-share calculation. TPG Inc. is an Up-C holding company owning approximately 43% of the Common Units of TPG Operating Group at June 30, 2026, with the balance held by the Founders and current and former partners. Class B shares carry ten votes each and no economic rights. A separate legacy entity group, RemainCo, retains rights to performance allocations in certain older funds and pays TPG a 1%-of-NAV administrative fee ($6.1 million in H1 2026) — a permanent disclosed conflict. TPG reports as a single operating segment.

## Financial Analysis

### Fee engine

GAAP "fees and other" grew from $2,087.1 million in FY2024 to $2,424.1 million in FY2025 (+16.1%), and H1 2026 reached $1,325.0 million (+19.1%). On the non-GAAP series management runs the business on:

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management fees | 1,625.7 | 1,800.1 | +10.7% |
| Fee-related performance revenues | 33.0 | 49.3 | +49% |
| Transaction/monitoring/other, net | 147.6 | 249.3 | +69% |
| Other income | 25.1 | 10.6 | −58% |
| **Fee-Related Revenues** | **1,831.5** | **2,109.3** | **+15.2%** |
| Fee-Related Expenses | 1,067.2 | 1,156.7 | +8.4% |
| **Fee-Related Earnings** | **764.2** | **952.6** | **+24.6%** |
| FRE margin | 41.7% | 45.2% | +350bp |
| **After-tax DE** | **837.3** | **973.5** | **+16.3%** |

Q2 2026 FRR was $628.2 million, up 26.9%, beating a roughly $565 million non-GAAP consensus. The composition of that beat is the issue. Transaction fees were $97.2 million versus $34.6 million a year earlier (+181%), and $162.1 million versus $82.1 million for the half. In FY2025, $222.0 million of $249.3 million (89%) of net transaction/monitoring fees was earned in Market Solutions — the capital-markets and broker-dealer business, the least contracted and most cyclical line in the company. Management stated on the Q2 call that transaction and monitoring fees were pulled forward into Q2 and will step down in Q3. A material share of both the FRR beat and the margin progress is timing.

The mitigating point is that capital-markets fee capacity scales with deployment, and H1 2026 capital invested of $13.8 billion versus $10.4 billion supports a structurally higher level of these fees than 2025. The step-down may be one quarter of phasing rather than a reversal. But it means annualizing Q2's $0.69 DE per share to $2.76 overstates run-rate earnings.

### Per-share economics

Flat DE per share against 27% FRR growth is the single most decision-relevant fact in this file. The drivers:

**NCI leakage.** FY2025 consolidated net income was $599.6 million; only $184.6 million was attributable to TPG Inc., with $50.8 million to Operating Group NCI and $364.2 million to other NCI. In Q2 2026, $226.2 million of $319.7 million went to NCI.

**Unit growth.** Diluted units: 364.7 million (FY24) → 374.1 million (FY25) → 385.5 million (Q2'26). Class A voting shares rose from 109.2 million at 12/31/24 to 160.1 million at 7/31/26, driven by 35.9 million Common Unit exchanges in 2025 and 6.0 million in H1 2026 plus 5.0 million net RSU settlements in H1 alone. Exchange itself is one-for-one and value-neutral on the equity; the economic transfer occurs through the Tax Receivable Agreement, which pays 85% of realized tax savings to exchanging holders and stood at an $848.2 million liability at June 30 ($508.0 million to related parties), up from $811.6 million, with $29.9 million paid in H1.

**Carry vintage gap.** Realized performance allocations, net, went from $194.6 million (FY24) to $204.7 million (FY25), +5.2%. Of $1,280 million net accrued performance allocations at 12/31/25, $809 million (63%) sat in 2020-and-prior vintages; the 2023, 2024 and 2025 vintages contributed $23 million, $12 million and $20 million. The 2022+ funds are marked above cost (TPG IX 1.3x, THP II 1.3x, Asia VIII 1.2x) but are years from full monetization.

An unrecognized compensation overhang of roughly $1.95 billion sits behind the unit-growth line: RSUs $856.8 million (25.2 million units), TPH/RPH units $324.9 million, acquisition-related Common Units $648.8 million (30.2 million unvested from the Angelo Gordon and Peppertree deals), liability-classified awards $119.5 million, with weighted-average service periods of roughly two to three years. The Omnibus Plan reserve was increased by 6.4 million shares in March 2026, leaving 30.5 million available.

### Cash flow and tax

FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,032.4 million nearly doubled FY2024's $532.1 million on $2,291.8 million of performance-allocation and co-investment proceeds. H1 2026 OCF fell 10% to $524.2 million. This line contains fund-level purchases and proceeds, so it is not a clean read on fee-business cash generation and should not be annualized. Cash interest paid in H1 was $52.7 million and cash taxes $28.4 million. The GAAP effective tax rate was 10.2% in Q2 and 8.8% for the half, because income allocated to NCI is taxed at the holder level — a structural feature that means GAAP net income overstates what accrues to Class A holders relative to a conventional C-corp.

### Leverage

Debt principal has gone from $199.3 million at 12/31/22 to $2,375 million at 6/30/26. The maturity ladder is genuinely comfortable — $125 million of drawn revolver due May 2030 and $2,250 million in 2031 and beyond, nothing sooner — and $2,375 million against $952.6 million of FY2025 FRE is roughly 2.5x, unremarkable for the sector. Two features deserve attention. The $400 million junior subordinated notes (6.95%, 2064) permit up to five consecutive years of interest deferral, real flexibility, and traded at $374.6 million versus par. The $250 million of Secured Notes carry a +4.00% step-up if not redeemed by June 20, 2028 — a dated refinancing decision with a punitive alternative.

Reported liquidity of $2,899.7 million overstates flexibility. Of $957.8 million consolidated cash, only $191.9 million is attributable to the TPG Operating Group and on-balance-sheet securitization vehicles; the rest sits in consolidated funds and VIEs restricted to VIE obligations. Genuine holdco resources are roughly $191.9 million of cash plus $1,955 million of undrawn committed facilities. On the Operating Group cash basis, net debt is approximately $2,183 million; on consolidated cash, $1,430 million. The Obligor Group, on a standalone combined basis at 6/30/26, showed $1,619.2 million of assets against $2,576.1 million of liabilities, negative revenue of $14.7 million and an H1 net loss of $101.6 million, servicing debt from distributions out of non-guarantor subsidiaries holding $4.7 billion of investments. Structural subordination is standard for Up-C managers, but the equity cushion in the obligor perimeter is thin.

Two other trend items: the net deferred tax asset valuation allowance grew from $141.3 million to $194.7 million in six months, and consolidated guarantees of employee fund-commitment loans stood at $115.2 million outstanding against a $376.0 million maximum, with maximum exposure roughly doubling from $192.9 million a year earlier.

Note a data conflict: the supplied trailing and forward P/E of 46.68 cannot be reconciled with the quoted $0.4488 EPS at $52.46 (which implies roughly 117x). GAAP multiples are uninformative here in any case, given $813.7 million of FY2025 equity compensation and NCI allocation. All multiples below are on distributable earnings and FRE.

### Capital allocation

Dividends went from $1.74 per share for 2024 to $2.06 for 2025, with $0.59 declared in each of Q1 and Q2 2026. Against FY2025 after-tax DE per economic unit of $2.58, the 2025 payout was roughly 80%; at $2.36 annualized against a $2.76 Q2-annualized run rate, roughly 86%. TPG is distributing nearly all distributable earnings while adding leverage, which means the $500 million Jackson equity stake, $235.7 million of Peppertree cash and $701.0 million of unfunded fund commitments are debt-financed rather than funded from retention. That is a defensible choice for a fee-light-capital business, but it removes the balance sheet as a shock absorber and leaves no buyback lever to offset the ~4% annual unit growth.

## Growth Analysis

The forward drivers are identifiable and largely contractual:

**Contracted fee growth without new fundraising.** "AUM Subject to Fee-Earning Growth" reached $51.7 billion at 6/30/26 from $40.5 billion at 12/31/25 — $39.3 billion not yet earning fees and $12.3 billion of FAUM subject to step-ups (rates rising from 0.35%-1.65% to 0.47%-1.75%). At the 1.16% blended rate, activating even half of the $39.3 billion adds roughly $230 million of annual management fee at high incremental margin.

**Fundraising velocity.** H1 2026 capital raised $26.5 billion (+54%), Q2 alone $16.1 billion across TPG X, THP III, TPG Sports, Rise Climate II, TPG Direct Lending and Peppertree XI. Available capital reached $76.2 billion versus $62.5 billion. The >$50 billion 2026 target was maintained.

**Permanent and retail capital.** At 6/30/26: MVP Fund $6,984M (11% return), TCAP $5,105M (10%), MMDL Evergreen $4,652M (11%), ABC Evergreen $4,410M (22%), T-POP $2,251M (34%), TPG Advantage Direct Lending $1,184M, Dynamic Credit Income $1,121M. Fee-related performance revenues, the FRE line these vehicles feed, were only $49.3 million in FY2025 — 2.3% of FRR. The retail build is real and not yet financially material.

**Insurance.** The February 2026 Jackson Financial partnership makes TPG investment manager for selected general-account assets of Jackson subsidiaries, alongside a $500 million purchase of 4.7 million JXN shares (carried at $489.2 million at 6/30/26, with $(12.9) million of H1 equity losses). This is TPG's capital-light answer to Apollo's Athene, KKR's Global Atlantic and Ares's Aspida: it avoids insurance regulatory and ALM burden but earns thinner, more contested mandates than captive permanent capital. The new CFO's background — previously CFO of Jackson National, American Equity Life, and AIG's retirement division — is consistent with intent to scale this channel, though the mandate predates the hire by four months, so the sequencing does not support any causal reading.

Against that, three growth qualifiers. Consensus is drifting lower, not higher: current-quarter estimates are down 0.7% over 30 days and 2.5% over 90 days (14 analysts), next quarter down 3.6% and 3.7% (13 analysts). The single-analyst annual figures ($1.97 current year, down 31.8%; $3.75 next year, up 2.0%) are too thin and likely too basis-inconsistent to weight. There is no earnings-inflection revision signal here; the direction is mildly negative. Second, the Angelo Gordon earnout was determined not probable of achievement during 2025 — the acquired Credit and Real Estate revenue perimeter is tracking below what was assumed in November 2023, which is notable given Credit is now the largest platform by AUM. The accounting consequence (no expense recognized since, versus $19.7 million in H1 2025) flatters FY2025 GAAP compensation, which fell from $1,006.3 million to $813.7 million. Third, Real Estate management fees were flat at $350.1 million, the only platform without fee growth, with Europe Realty III (2019) at 0.9x net MoM and (1%) net IRR and TAC+ (2021) at 1.0x / (1%).

The honest summary: franchise growth is high-single to mid-teens and well underwritten; DE-per-share growth is closer to zero until the 2022+ vintages harvest, and the timing of that is not something management can guide.

The FY2026E FRE figure is derived, not guided: H1 GAAP fees of $1,325.0 million at the FY2025 non-GAAP-to-GAAP ratio of 0.870 implies full-year FRR of roughly $2,450-2,550 million, which at the guided 47% margin yields $1.15-1.20 billion. The normalized DE per share of $2.90-3.00 reflects FY2025's $2.58 growing at mid-teens FRE-driven rates and deliberately does not annualize the transaction-fee-inflated Q2.

Roughly 18x forward DE with a 4.5% yield is a market-clearing multiple for a diversified alternative manager. Peer valuation multiples were not available in the provided data, so we do not benchmark TPG's multiple against specific peers. The unverified third-party claim of a 24.4% peer discount on distributable earnings is not something we can reproduce and we do not build on it. What we can say is that the multiple is neither stretched nor compelling. The specific reason a discount is warranted is measurable: TPG converts 56% of AUM to fee-earning capital versus peers who run higher, its carry is concentrated in exhausted vintages, and roughly 4% of the per-unit base is issued away each year. A manager whose FRE grows 15-20% but whose DE per share is flat does not deserve a growth multiple until the second series compounds.

Consensus targets frame the range: mean $60.5, median $63.0, low $46.0, high $68.0, with the recommendation distribution improving (6 strong buy / 7 buy / 3 hold, one hold moved to buy in the last month). TD Cowen's $41 Hold from April 9, 2026 is stale relative to the Q2 print but is the most bearish anchor on record. We treat the consensus mean as informative but not as independent confirmation — at ~18x forward DE with flat per-share earnings and negative near-term revisions, the "analyst targets are floors" logic that applies to cheap inflection stocks does not apply here.

One important context point on price: TPG has rallied roughly 42% off the April 2 low of $36.95 and about 18% since July 15, most of it in the two weeks around the August 4 print, and it sits ~25% below the January high of $70.38. The improvement in risk/reward that existed in April at lower EV/FRE multiples has largely been taken back. Buying today is buying after the re-rating, not into it.

## Competitive Landscape

TPG competes with Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, Ares, Carlyle, Brookfield and EQT for both LP capital and assets. Trajectory reads mixed rather than clearly improving.

**Improving:** AUM grew from $245.9B at 12/31/24 to $327B at 6/30/26; fee mix genuinely broadened, with Credit ($341.8M), Real Estate ($350.1M), Impact ($276.5M) and Growth ($232.9M) collectively exceeding the Capital platform's $500.7M in FY2025, while Capital platform fees actually declined from $514.5M. Flagship performance remains a real asset: aggregate Capital Funds show 23% gross / 15% net IRR and 1.6x net MoM across a 1993-2025 series, with TPG VII (2015) at 19% net / 1.9x. The 45.2% FRE margin heading to a guided 47% is respectable.

**Not improving:** TPG has no captive insurance balance sheet while Apollo owns Athene, KKR owns Global Atlantic, Brookfield built Wealth Solutions and Ares has Aspida. The Jackson mandate substitutes a third-party management relationship for owned permanent capital — capital-light and lower risk, also lower margin and more competitively contested. Credit, the largest platform, is structurally lower-return (TPG Direct Lending 14% gross / 10% net; Asset Based Finance 15% / 12%), so carry per dollar of AUM is thinner than in Capital or Growth, and the post-Angelo Gordon information barrier between the legacy TPG business and Credit plus certain Real Estate products is a genuine constraint on cross-platform synergy. Real Estate marks in Europe and U.S. core-plus have not held up.

On fee-mix diversification we make an explicit distinction: platform diversification is established in the filings, but TPG discloses no LP-concentration metric, and large institutions typically commit across multiple platforms of one manager. Strategy diversification should not be read as client diversification.

Industry conditions are the visible headwind. Realizations of $5.05 billion in Q2 versus $6.48 billion, capital invested up in Q2, and realized carry up 5% in FY2025 describe an exit market where deployment outpaces monetization sector-wide, forcing managers to lean on FRE and capital-markets fees. TPG's numbers show exactly that pattern — transaction fees +97% in H1 while realized carry stagnated.

## Risk Assessment

**Per-share decoupling (primary).** Measurable, not theoretical, and driven by mechanisms that persist for years. If the 2022 vintages do not begin harvesting by 2027-2028, DE per share could remain flat through the guided FRE expansion, in which case ~18x forward DE is too high.

**Transaction-fee reversal.** Management has already guided the Q3 step-down. If Market Solutions capital-markets activity normalizes toward FY2024 levels ($147.6M net) rather than FY2025's $249.3M, the 47% FRE margin becomes hard to hold.

**Balance-sheet flexibility.** $2,375 million of debt against $191.9 million of true holdco cash, an ~86% DE payout, a $250 million Secured Notes step-up decision by June 2028, and a rising DTA valuation allowance. Higher-for-longer policy rates compress alternative-manager multiples directly through the discount rate and indirectly through slower realizations.

**Clawback and reversal.** If all remaining fund investments were deemed worthless — management calls this remote — performance allocations subject to clawback would be $2,635.8 million at 6/30/26, up from $2,456.5 million. Cumulative allocations of $7.8 billion are subject to reversal on future losses, against a recorded reserve of $6.8 million.

**New policy risk.** The FY2025 10-K added a risk factor on the January 2026 Executive Order directing DOJ/FTC review of institutional single-family home acquisitions. TPG runs Essential Housing I/II/III ($4.8 billion committed, 13% aggregate net IRR) plus a new evergreen vehicle ($502 million).

**Governance and structure.** Founder succession and "Sunset" triggers, with fund-level key-person provisions that can suspend commitment periods; RemainCo's retained performance rights; and an $250 million uncommitted line of credit under which TPG Operating Group II is the potential borrower and an affiliate of T-POP — TPG's own retail evergreen vehicle — the potential lender (nothing outstanding at 6/30/26). Amounts due to affiliates, partners and employees stood at $579.9 million.

**Retail-channel scrutiny.** The 10-K cites ILPA analysis on retail vehicles and conflicts. TPG's growing perpetual complex (roughly $25 billion across MVP, TCAP, MMDL, ABC, T-POP and others) is a channel to monitor, though its contribution to FRE (2.3% of FRR) means near-term earnings sensitivity is small.

**Litigation tail.** TIM Hellas matters have largely resolved in TPG's favor, but claims filed in 2024 by another similarly-situated plaintiff group remain pending with indeterminate amounts sought.

**Employee-loan guarantees.** Maximum obligation of $376.0 million, roughly double the $192.9 million a year earlier.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term positioning is calm and cheap. ATM IV of 36.2% sits below 39.8% one-year realized volatility (a −3.6% spread) at the 5th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure rising to 47.3% at three months and 41.7% at six months. The one-week tenor shows elevated put demand (10.7-12.1% skew) on effectively no put volume — three-day expiry mechanics, not information. The more meaningful shift is in open interest: the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 10.06 on June 3 to 0.54, while price rose 29% over the same window. Protection that was in place through the drawdown has been unwound into the rally. The three- and six-month skews (−6.1% and −7.8%) are the opposite sign from the front, and the one-month tenor prices a ±9.2% move with a 3.00 put/call volume ratio. The composite read: the market is pricing no near-term event and cheap volatility for anyone who wants to own optionality into the Q3 print, when the guided transaction-fee step-down lands. Short interest is 7.3% of float at 5.9 days to cover — uncrowded, no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($68, +29.6%)

FRE compounds to $1.15-1.20 billion in 2026 at the guided 47% margin and continues into 2027 as the $51.7 billion of contracted fee-growth AUM activates. Fundraising exceeds $50 billion, available capital of $76.2 billion converts into deployment and eventually into realizations, and the 2022-2024 vintages (TPG IX 1.3x, THP II 1.3x, Asia VIII 1.2x) begin harvesting in 2027, re-accelerating DE per share toward $3.40-3.60. The Jackson mandate proves the template for a scaled third-party insurance channel and private wealth compounds off T-POP's 34% first-year return. At 20-21x a $3.30 forward DE figure the stock reaches $66-69. Cheap three- and six-month volatility offers a low-cost way to express this.

### Bear Case ($40, −23.8%)

Transaction and monitoring fees revert toward FY2024 levels, the 47% margin guide is missed, and FRR growth decelerates to high single digits. Realizations stay depressed into 2027, realized carry remains flat, and 4% annual unit growth keeps DE per share pinned near $2.60-2.80. The Angelo Gordon earnout shortfall proves indicative of Credit-platform revenue underdelivery, Real Estate marks in Europe and core-plus deteriorate further and impair re-ups, and a higher-for-longer rate path compresses the sector multiple to 14x. $2.80 × 14x = $39. Add any evergreen-redemption or credit-market event and the January low-$37 area is retested.

### Base Case ($57, +8.7% plus 4.5% yield ≈ 13% total)

FRE lands near $1.15 billion, the Q3 transaction-fee step-down materializes and is partially offset by deployment-linked activity, normalized FY2026 DE per share reaches $2.90-3.00, and the market holds roughly 19x. Probability-weighting the three cases (50/25/25) gives roughly 6% price appreciation plus the distribution, or about 10% total return — a fair, not compelling, expected value with asymmetry tilted slightly to the downside given the negative revision drift.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income-led).** The 4.50% distribution, covered at roughly 86% of distributable earnings, plus mid-teens fee-earnings growth supports maintaining existing exposure. New capital has no urgent entry signal: the stock has recovered 42% from the April low, forward DE multiples have expanded, consensus quarterly estimates are drifting lower, and the guided Q3 fee step-down is a known near-term negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of year-over-year DE-per-share growth of 10% or better, with realized performance allocations exceeding $60 million per quarter — evidence the 2022+ vintages are harvesting.
2. Q3/Q4 2026 confirming the 47% FRE margin *excluding* any further transaction-fee pull-forward, with management fees alone growing 12%+ year over year.
3. Price in the low $40s (roughly 15x normalized FY2026 DE, a 5.6% yield) without fundamental deterioration — the April setup, which we would take.
4. A second scaled insurance mandate comparable to Jackson, or fee-related performance revenues exceeding 5% of FRR from the perpetual complex.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 FRE margin guidance cut below 45%, or FRR growth decelerating below 10% year over year.
2. Dividend held flat while payout exceeds 95% of after-tax DE, or a dividend reduction.
3. Any redemption limitation or gate at T-POP or another perpetual vehicle, or a material step-down in evergreen inflows.
4. Real Estate net accrued performance allocations declining further, or additional funds falling below 1.0x net MoM.
5. Consensus quarterly estimate cuts accelerating beyond 5% in a 30-day window.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November) is the decisive near-term checkpoint — it isolates whether the transaction-fee step-down is one quarter of timing or a reversal, and whether management-fee growth alone carries the 47% margin. The June 2028 Secured Notes redemption decision and the 2027 harvest window for the 2022 vintages are the medium-term markers.

## Conclusion

TPG's operating franchise is performing: $327 billion of AUM, 24.6% FRE growth with 350 basis points of margin expansion, $51.7 billion of contracted fee-growth AUM, $76.2 billion of available capital, and credible new channels in insurance mandates and private wealth. The problem is that none of it reached the per-share line in the most recent quarter — DE per share was flat at $0.69 against 27% fee-related revenue growth — because non-controlling interests absorb most consolidated income, roughly 4% of the unit base is issued away each year, and harvestable carry is concentrated in exhausted vintages while the well-marked 2022+ funds are years from monetization. Q2's revenue beat came disproportionately from the broker-dealer line that management has already guided down for Q3.
