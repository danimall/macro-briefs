## Executive Summary

ONEOK has undergone a radical transformation over the past 27 months, tripling its asset base from ~$24B to $67B through the acquisitions of Magellan Midstream, EnLink Midstream, and Medallion Midstream. The company is now one of the largest integrated midstream platforms in North America with ~60,000 miles of pipelines spanning natural gas gathering & processing, NGL fractionation/transport, refined products, and crude oil. Q1 2026 results were solid (+13% adj. EBITDA YoY) and guidance was raised to an $8.25B EBITDA midpoint.

The stock has rallied ~45% from its November 2025 low of $64.02 to $92.71 currently, pricing in much of the integration upside. At 16.5x forward earnings ($5.73 FY2026E) and roughly 11.3x EV/EBITDA, OKE trades at a discount to WMB (32.8x P/E) and TRGP (26.0x P/E) but commands a premium to KMI (21.9x) when adjusted for its C-corp structure and growth profile. The 4.6% dividend yield provides a meaningful income floor, but the leverage (4.2x debt-to-EBITDA, $34B total debt) and integration execution risk cap the upside multiple expansion potential near term.

This is a well-managed company with a sound strategic direction, but the stock's significant appreciation since November means the margin of safety has compressed. The current price roughly reflects fair value assuming execution proceeds as planned, making this a hold-for-income position rather than a new capital deployment opportunity.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates across four midstream segments:

- **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing**: 22,600 miles of pipelines, ~7.2 Bcf/d processing capacity across the Williston, Powder River, Mid-Continent, and Permian basins. Over 4.7 million dedicated acres.
- **Natural Gas Liquids**: 14,900 miles of pipelines, 1.2 MMBbl/d fractionation, 40 MMBbl storage. Connects major supply basins to Gulf Coast demand centers.
- **Natural Gas Pipelines**: 8,300 miles including ownership of Henry Hub through Sabine Pipeline. 74 Bcf storage capacity.
- **Refined Products & Crude**: 13,000 miles (one of the largest common carrier refined products systems in the U.S.), 53 terminals, 100 MMBbl storage.

The company's C-corp structure (versus MLP/partnership peers like EPD, ET, PAA) gives it a broader investor base and simplified taxation. Approximately 90% of earnings are fee-based, providing substantial insulation from direct commodity price swings, though volumetric sensitivity to producer drilling activity remains.

The EPS trajectory is misleading in isolation. The 2024 EPS decline ($5.48 → $5.17) was entirely attributable to 21% share count dilution from the Magellan deal; operating income grew 22% that year. Similarly, FY2025 EPS of $5.42 includes further 7% dilution from EnLink/Medallion.

- Dividend coverage ratio (OCF/dividends): ~2.1x

The dividend is well-covered by operating cash flow but tightly covered by free cash flow after growth capex. Management has prioritized growth spending over buybacks ($62M of $2.0B authorized in 2025).

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $34.0B
- Debt-to-EBITDA: 4.2x (target: <4.0x for comfort)
- Interest expense: $1.8B (2025), up from $866M (2023)
- Weighted average interest rate: ~5.3% on recent issuance
- Current ratio: 0.71 (working capital deficit of $1.9B)
- Credit ratings: Baa2/BBB/BBB, all Stable

The leverage is the primary financial concern. At 4.2x, ONEOK is at the upper boundary of what investment-grade midstream companies typically carry. Management's plan is to "grow into" the leverage through EBITDA expansion rather than pay down debt, meaning deleveraging depends on volume growth materializing across the Permian, Williston, and Mid-Continent. If FY2026 EBITDA hits $8.25B guidance, leverage drops to ~4.1x; if growth projects ramp per schedule through 2027-2028 (Eiger Express, Texas City LPG, Bighorn, Medford), leverage should decline toward 3.5x by 2028.

At current levels, OKE is reasonably valued for what it is: a high-yielding, leveraged growth midstream company. The stock is not cheap enough to offer compelling asymmetric upside absent a catalyst that is not already priced.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK's post-transformation competitive position is genuinely differentiated. It is the only large-cap C-corp midstream company offering end-to-end integration across gas, NGL, refined products, and crude oil. This integration creates volume capture advantages: a producer dedicated to ONEOK's gathering system is a captive NGL supply source for ONEOK's fractionation and pipeline segments.

The 4.7 million dedicated acres provide long-duration volume visibility that is difficult for competitors to replicate without similar acquisition activity. The ownership of Henry Hub through Sabine Pipeline provides unique pricing infrastructure value.

Key competitive risks include Permian Basin overbuilding (TRGP, ET, EPD all expanding aggressively) and the potential for large producers to self-build gathering infrastructure as production concentrates.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Leverage overhang (HIGH)**: Deleveraging depends entirely on EBITDA growth materializing. Any volume shortfall extends the timeline and could trigger rating agency concern.

2. **Integration execution (HIGH)**: Four major acquisitions in 27 months is aggressive by any standard. The elevation of Medallion's former CEO to COO suggests retention is proceeding well, but operational integration across 60,000 miles of pipeline, 6,326 employees, and multiple legacy IT/control systems takes years.

3. **Volumetric sensitivity**: Despite 90% fee-based earnings, fees are earned on throughput. A sustained Permian or Williston drilling slowdown would directly impact revenue. The tariff uncertainty flagged in the 10-K is relevant in the current trade policy environment.

4. **Interest rate exposure**: $1.8B annual interest expense rising. Commercial paper and credit facility carry floating rate risk. Higher-for-longer rates compress the equity spread over cost of capital.

5. **Working capital structure**: The $1.9B working capital deficit requires continuous access to capital markets. A credit market disruption could create a liquidity squeeze, though investment-grade ratings and the $3.5B facility provide substantial buffer.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% versus 25.6% historical volatility shows the market pricing modestly elevated uncertainty (3.9pp premium). The flat term structure (29.5% near vs. 31.0% far) suggests no specific near-term event is being hedged; uncertainty is distributed evenly across time. The put/call volume ratio of 1.45 indicates slightly above-average put demand, consistent with the stock trading near its 52-week high and attracting some protective positioning. Options markets signal moderate uncertainty without extreme directional conviction in either direction.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Investment-grade ratings with stable outlooks from all three agencies
- $3.5B revolving credit facility provides liquidity cushion
- 90% fee-based structure limits downside scenarios
- Insider buying (Director Derksen, $165K at $66 in Nov 2025) aligns interests
- Pro-energy regulatory environment reduces environmental cost risk through 2034+

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-113, 13-22% upside):**
- Integration proceeds smoothly; FY2026 EBITDA hits/beats $8.25B guidance
- Growth projects complete on time (Greater Denver mid-2026, Medford late 2026)
- Permian Basin volumes accelerate, driving positive earnings revisions
- Leverage compresses toward 3.8x, triggering potential credit upgrade
- Market re-rates OKE closer to 18x forward as execution de-risks the platform

**Bear Case ($72-78, 16-22% downside):**

- Interest rates remain elevated, preventing meaningful deleveraging
- Credit spreads widen in broader market stress, pressuring midstream borrowing costs
- Stock re-rates to 13-14x forward P/E, reflecting sustained leverage premium

## Conclusion

ONEOK is a well-positioned midstream infrastructure company executing an ambitious but logical consolidation strategy. The Q1 2026 results and guidance raise confirm the integration is progressing, and the organic growth pipeline provides multi-year EBITDA expansion visibility. However, at $92.71 the stock has appreciated substantially from its November 2025 lows and now trades near analyst consensus targets, with Scotiabank explicitly calling the valuation "full."

The 4.6% dividend yield provides adequate compensation for holding through the integration period, but the leveraged balance sheet, execution risk across four simultaneous integrations, and proximity to fair value prevent a more aggressive stance. The declining score trajectory over prior reports reflected the stock's prior weakness; the subsequent rally has not been accompanied by sufficient fundamental improvement to warrant upgrading conviction. HOLD for income, with focus on leverage progression and volume growth through the back half of 2026.
