## Executive Summary

GE HealthCare's July 29 second-quarter report resolved the demand question that had been hanging over the stock since the April 29 guidance cut, and left the cost question wide open. Organic orders grew 11.1%, the fastest since the January 2023 spin from GE; book-to-bill reached 1.15x, also a post-spin high; company-reported backlog rose $2.6B year-over-year to $23.9B; adjusted EPS of $1.13 beat the $1.04 non-GAAP consensus by 8.7%. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 3-4% organic growth, 15.4-15.7% adjusted EBIT margin, $4.80-$5.00 adjusted EPS and ~$1.6B free cash flow — twice within six days, on July 23 and July 29. The stock has moved from $63.07 on July 19 to $70.31.

The cost side has deteriorated for two consecutive quarters. Adjusted EBIT margin fell 40bp in Q2 to 14.2% and 90bp in the first half to 13.8%, even though the core Advanced Imaging Solutions segment expanded 90bp and Pharmaceutical Diagnostics expanded 30bp. Two things absorbed that: Patient Care Solutions swung from +$108M of first-half EBIT to −$16M on a $153M revenue decline (an 81% decremental margin), and management quantified roughly 120bp of input-cost headwind from memory chips, oil, freight and components, adding that memory chip prices were still rising. That last point matters more than management framed it. There is no refund mechanism comparable to the tariff windfall that helped Q2.

The arithmetic reduces to one question. First-half adjusted EBIT margin was 13.8%; the guidance midpoint requires roughly 17.2% in the second half, a ~340bp step-up, and 89% of the year's guided free cash flow. The shape is not unprecedented — second-half 2025 adjusted EPS was $2.52 against $2.07 in the first half — and the required $2.68-$2.88 implies 6-14% year-over-year growth. But consensus trimmed the current quarter 3.2% and next quarter 1.2% over the past 30 days while raising the full year 1.2%, meaning analysts banked the Q2 beat and marked down precisely the quarters that must deliver. At 14.3x guided adjusted EPS against a 26.1x peer median, the discount compensates for real structural issues: 3-4% organic growth, negative tangible equity of roughly −$6.0B, a ~$4.4B underfunded pension, China organically negative and guided down again in 2026, and two unquantified unreserved contingencies. Base case $81 (+15%), bull $96 (+37%), bear $59 (−16%), probability-weighted expected return roughly +10%. That is approximately fair. HOLD, fifth consecutive, with a defined path to upgrade.

## Company Overview

GE HealthCare is the imaging, pharmaceutical diagnostics and patient-monitoring business separated from General Electric in January 2023. The company describes itself as a $20.6B business with approximately 54,000 employees. Segment reporting changed materially in Q2 2026: Imaging and Advanced Visualization Solutions were combined into Advanced Imaging Solutions (AIS), with prior periods recast.

First-half 2026 segment economics:

| Segment | 1H26 revenue | % of revenue | 1H26 EBIT | Margin | % of segment EBIT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AIS (Radiology, Procedural Guidance, Specialized Ultrasound) | $7,410M | 71.1% | $1,004M | 13.5% | 69.7% |
| PDx (Pharmaceutical Diagnostics) | $1,612M | 15.5% | $446M | 27.7% | 31.0% |
| PCS (Monitoring, Life Support) | $1,379M | 13.2% | ($16M) | (1.2%) | (1.1%) |
| Other (HealthCare Financial Services) | $24M | 0.2% | $6M | — | 0.4% |

PDx generates 31% of segment profit on 15.5% of revenue. That concentration of quality in the smallest reportable segment is the core of the investment case: contrast media and radiopharmaceuticals carry manufacturing authorizations, radiopharmacy distribution networks and asset-retirement obligations that constitute genuine barriers to entry, and the segment holds the two identified growth engines (Flyrcado PET myocardial perfusion, targeted at more than $500M of annual revenue by 2028, and Vizamyl amyloid PET, growing double digits).

Services were $3,664M of first-half revenue, 35.1% of the total, growing 7.6% versus 6.0% for products. Remaining performance obligations were $16,118M at June 30, split $5,180M products and $10,939M services.

GAAP EPS rose 17% in Q2 while adjusted EPS rose 6.6%; for the half GAAP EPS *fell* 9.2% while adjusted EPS rose 2.4%. The largest driver of the divergence is non-operating pension income, which fell to $96M from $148M on lower amortization of accumulated other comprehensive income and is excluded entirely from adjusted EPS. Other reconciling items: non-repeat of a $97M Nihon Medi-Physics remeasurement gain, restructuring up to $76M from $40M, acquisition charges $46M from $15M, acquisition intangible amortization $108M from $75M.

### Tariffs: a real reversal, correctly partitioned

FY2025 tariffs reduced operating income $245M and cash flow $285M. In February 2026 the Supreme Court held IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariffs; CBP announced a refund process in April. In Q2 GEHC received $107M of cash refunds, booked a $38M receivable, and recognized $129M of pre-tax benefit — $106M relating to 2025 (excluded from adjusted results, $0.18 per share after tax) and $23M relating to 2026 (included in adjusted results).

The in-year $23M is worth roughly $0.04 per share after tax, about 60% of the $0.07 year-over-year adjusted EPS increase. But gross Q2 tariff cost rose $25M year-over-year to $68M, so net tariff drag was ~$45M versus $43M. Management's framing — that incremental tariff expense was offset by in-year refunds — is fair. Adjusted EPS growth of 6.6% is not tariff-inflated on a net basis. Residual recoverable 2025 tariff cost is arithmetically bounded at ~$139M but only IEEPA-imposed duties qualify, and the 10-Q calls timing and amount uncertain. Do not model it.

### PCS: the decremental is the diagnosis

Patient Care Solutions revenue fell 13.3% reported and 13.5% organic in Q2; segment EBIT went from +$60M to −$26M. For the half, a $124M EBIT swing on a $153M revenue decline is an 81% decremental margin (83% in Q2). Pure volume deleverage on a hardware business does not produce that. Costs that stay in the P&L while revenue leaves — expedited freight, idle capacity, unabsorbed overhead — do. The 10-Q attributes revenue to "operational and fulfillment challenges" and EBIT to volume decline plus cost inflation.

That reading is more optimistic than the alternative: share loss with price erosion against Chinese entrants and volume-based procurement produces a similar decremental, and China PCS revenue declined. The filings do not resolve the split. One data point cuts against a pure execution diagnosis — the PCS segment leader was still filing Form 4s in that role on May 15, after the April reorganization replaced other segment leadership.

Management launched a comprehensive strategic review including a possible sale. PCS carries $2,040M of allocated goodwill against a business running roughly $2.76B annualized revenue (a doubling of the first half, seasonality-caveated) at approximately zero EBIT. The 10-Q states no interim impairment test was triggered at June 30 but warns future indicators could produce a charge.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

First-half operating cash flow $458M (from $344M), free cash flow $180M (from $106M), against guided full-year FCF of ~$1.6B — leaving ~$1,420M, or 89%, for the second half. The prior year's shape was similar ($106M in 1H25), so the concentration reflects genuine seasonality. Two qualifiers: $107M of the first-half FCF was tariff-refund cash, and excluding it Q2 FCF was roughly −$39M; pension contributions run ~$350M for the year with $171M paid, leaving ~$179M in the second half. Working capital consumed $840M in the half, inventories alone $310M.

Guided FCF of $1.6B against roughly $2,220M of guided adjusted net income implies ~72% conversion — acceptable for a business funding $350M of pension cash, rising restructuring, and capex ($278M) above depreciation ($154M), but not high-quality.

At June 30: total borrowings $10,093M with only $2M short-term; nine senior unsecured note series totaling $9,500M maturing 2027-2052 plus a $650M term loan due March 2029; nearest wall $1,750M of 5.650% notes due November 15, 2027. Cash fell to $2,105M from $4,512M, primarily on Intelerad. Net debt ~$7,988M. Undrawn: a $3,000M revolver to March 2030 and a $500M 364-day facility. Net debt to estimated FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of ~$3,743M is ~2.1x; interest coverage 6.3x on FY2025 operating income and 6.0x in the first half.

Against that: total attributable equity $10,975M versus goodwill of $15,095M plus intangibles of $1,894M, giving tangible book equity of roughly −$6,014M, with goodwill and intangibles at 45.6% of total assets. Postretirement plans were underfunded by roughly $4.365B at year-end 2025. The Altman Z-Score screens near 1.55, but the mechanical drivers are identifiable: retained earnings are only 14.3% of assets because accumulation began at the 2023 spin, working capital is 9% of assets, and the goodwill-heavy base suppresses asset turnover. A company with 6x coverage, $3.5B of undrawn committed lines and no maturity before November 2027 is not near default. The screen is still correctly flagging a goodwill-heavy, moderately levered balance sheet with negative tangible equity — which is why financial health scores mid-range rather than high.

### Capital allocation

First-half uses of cash — $2,293M for Intelerad, $278M capex, $300M buyback, $32M dividends — totaled $2,903M against $458M of operating cash flow, funded by halving the cash balance and $150M net new borrowing. The buyback was well-timed: Q2 alone repurchased 3.276M shares at an average $61.05, all in May, with $500M of the April 2025 $1,000M authorization remaining. The dividend is $0.035 quarterly, a 0.20% yield and ~3% of guided adjusted EPS. There is no income support in this equity.

Intelerad closed March 18 for $2,293M cash net of cash acquired against roughly $230M of annualized revenue — about 10x revenue, with 71% booked as goodwill and $845M as twelve-year definite-lived intangibles. The strategic logic (outpatient imaging software complementing hospital-based imaging) is coherent and software mix should be margin-accretive, but the deal is structurally more accretive to adjusted EPS — which excludes both the amortization and the transaction costs while including Intelerad's earnings — than to GAAP EPS or cash returns on the $2.3B deployed. Standalone Intelerad profitability is not disclosed, so the EBITDA multiple paid is unknown.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth of 6.6% in the first half decomposes into 3.2% organic plus Intelerad plus currency. The organic rate is the honest number, and it sits at the low end of the 3-4% guidance.

The forward signal is better than the trailing one. Record organic orders growth of 11.1% and a 1.15x book-to-bill are the best demand datapoints since the spin, and management cited an approximately 85% secured equipment rate for the remainder of the year. Both support the revenue side of second-half guidance. AIS grew 5% organically in Q2 with 90bp of margin expansion, indicating the core imaging franchise is competing effectively on price and mix outside China. PDx grew with 30bp of expansion despite a supplier issue that depressed first-half margin to 27.7% from 30.7% and is described as resolved.

Two qualifications on the order data. First, the $23.9B backlog is a management-defined measure; the audited remaining performance obligation figure is $16,118M, up only $389M from December 31, and explicitly excludes contracts the customer can cancel without substantive penalty. The ~$7.8B gap is presumably cancellable orders and lease arrangements. Backlog reportedly rose $2.6B year-over-year while RPO rose $389M over six months, so the record-backlog claim rests on the more cancellable portion of the order book. That is a scope difference, not a misstatement, but it should be priced as lower-quality visibility than an audited RPO increase of the same size.

Second, management stated the current innovation wave represented under 20% of order value in the quarter. Flyrcado's ramp (545 doses in the week ending July 24, ~40% above April) and the >$500M-by-2028 target are prospective, not in the run rate. That is upside to 2027-2028 rather than support for 2026 margins.

China was $1,149M of first-half revenue, 11.0% of the total, down 0.6% reported with the decline worse organically since growth came from currency. FY2025 China revenue fell 4.6% and management expects another year-over-year decline in 2026 on volume-based procurement pricing and provincial budget pressure. Russia/Ukraine revenue was $106M against $190M of assets. Neither is large enough to break the thesis; together they cap the organic growth ceiling at the low-single digits for the foreseeable period.

Consensus has FY2026 at $4.93 (versus a $4.90 guidance midpoint) and FY2027 at $5.41, implying 9.7% earnings growth on 3-4% revenue growth — the delta is margin recovery plus buyback. That is a coherent model, but it is an operating-leverage story, not a growth story.

The peer set (RMD 18.6x, ZBH 22.6x, STE 26.1x, DXCM 32.2x, IDXX 41.0x) is heterogeneous, and only ResMed and Zimmer Biomet are reasonable growth analogues. Even against the cheapest peer, GEHC trades 23% below. Against the median it is 45% below.

The discount is partly earned and partly excessive, and separating the two is the whole exercise. Earned: 3-4% organic growth against peers growing faster; 2.1x net leverage where several peers carry less; negative tangible equity; a 13% revenue segment losing money; China organically negative; two unreserved contingencies; and a token dividend that provides no valuation floor. Excessive: the market is applying a mid-teens multiple to a business whose largest segment is expanding margin, whose highest-margin segment holds two named growth assets, whose order book just grew 11% organically, and which throws 5% free cash flow yield.

The specific mispricing claim I am prepared to defend is narrower than the headline discount: at 13.0x FY2027 consensus, the market is pricing a business that does not recover margin toward the mid-15s. If it does, a 15-16x multiple on $5.41 gives $81-87, and that is within the band GEHC has actually traded at (the stock changed hands at 17-18x forward in January). If it does not, the multiple is right and the earnings are lower, which is the bear case. This is a binary on execution rather than a durable structural discount, which is why the valuation score is 6.2 rather than 7+.

Consensus targets — mean $82.50, median $83.00, range $70-$96 — sit almost exactly on my base case. On a stock at 14.3x forward with stable estimates, targets are not obviously stale; there is no rapid estimate-revision cycle here for them to lag. Current-year consensus was revised up 1.2% over 30 days and next year 0.9%, but the current quarter was cut 3.2% and next quarter 1.2%. The pattern is analysts banking the Q2 beat and marking down the two quarters that must deliver the 340bp margin ramp. That is the most disconfirming single datapoint in the file for the bull case.

## Competitive Landscape

**Advanced imaging (71% of revenue).** A global oligopoly with Siemens Healthineers, Philips, Canon Medical and Fujifilm internationally, and United Imaging and Mindray gaining domestically in China where volume-based procurement compresses price. Economics are moderate-margin equipment plus a multi-year service annuity, which is why the Librarian of Congress DMCA exemption permitting third-party repair firms to circumvent OEM software protections on medical imaging devices is a strategic issue rather than regulatory noise: it attacks the 35%-of-revenue service annuity directly. AIS's 90bp of Q2 margin expansion on 5% organic growth says the franchise is holding price and mix outside China. Trajectory: modestly improving.

**Pharmaceutical diagnostics (15.5% of revenue, 27.7% margin).** Bayer, Bracco and Guerbet in contrast media; Lantheus and others in PET radiopharmaceuticals. The moat is physical and regulatory — manufacturing authorizations, radiopharmacy distribution, and in the case of Nihon Medi-Physics, $124M of assumed asset-retirement and decommissioning liabilities that function as a barrier as much as a liability. Trajectory: improving, with Flyrcado and Vizamyl the named drivers.

**Patient monitoring and life support (13.2% of revenue).** Baxter, ICU Medical, Drägerwerk and Chinese entrants in more commoditized hardware with heavier exposure to hospital capital-budget discipline and volume-based procurement. Trajectory: deteriorating. Exploring a sale is consistent with a judgment that the segment is neither defensible nor synergistic enough to justify capital, and is the right call.

Two company-specific vulnerabilities deserve naming. GEHC does not own the GE monogram or word mark; it licenses them from GE Aerospace under an agreement that can terminate in certain circumstances, which would force rebranding a 130-year-old franchise. And raw-material sourcing includes helium, iodine and rare earths, with China having tightened rare-earth export controls in 2025.

Net: two of three segments improving, the weakest being divested, offset by a licensed brand, a right-to-repair threat to the annuity, and China. Competitive position 6.3, unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**The 340bp second-half margin step-up.** First-half adjusted EBIT margin 13.8%; guidance midpoint requires ~17.2% in the second half on ~$11.3B of revenue, roughly $1,942M of adjusted EBIT. Management's supports are pricing, cost actions, high-margin new products and the ~85% secured equipment rate. The obstacle it named itself is input inflation, quantified at ~120bp with memory chip costs still rising. GEHC has no refund mechanism analogous to the tariff recovery for these input costs. A 100bp shortfall on second-half revenue is roughly $113M of EBIT, about $0.19 of adjusted EPS, enough to break the low end of guidance.

**PCS goodwill.** $2,040M allocated against a segment at roughly zero EBIT. A divestiture below carrying value or a further leg down crystallizes a non-cash charge. It would not affect cash, leverage-based covenants, or adjusted EPS, but it would deepen an already negative tangible book. Impairment testing is at the reporting-unit level, which may not map to the segment, and a strategic or sponsor buyer may value the monitoring installed base above GEHC's public multiple. No bid, price or timeline is disclosed.

**Two unreserved, unquantified contingencies.** The Iraqi Ministry of Health Anti-Terrorism Act litigation was reinstated by the D.C. Circuit in January 2026, en banc review denied in April, and the parties are in discovery, with no accrual and no estimable range. Separately, the IRS may assert a material amount of additional taxes on GE's 2016-2020 consolidated returns, with allocation to GEHC under the Tax Matters Agreement and no liability accrued; resolution is described as unlikely within twelve months. Both moved against GEHC in the first half. Discovery is not liability and the ATA case has been dismissed once and vacated once, so I decline to size either — but neither adjusted EPS nor a leverage screen captures them.

**Interim CFO.** Jay Saccaro departs August 14 for a role outside medtech; Controller and Chief Accounting Officer George Newcomb is interim while an external search proceeds. Running a PCS turnaround, a potential divestiture, an Intelerad integration and a demanding second-half ramp with an interim finance chief is an execution risk. The release describes an external opportunity and elevating a ten-year controller preserves continuity in accounting specifically; there is no evidence connecting the departure to the operating problems.

**Leverage and pension.** 2.1x net leverage, $1,750M due November 2027, ~$4.4B of underfunded postretirement plans, and a 10-K disclosure that a substantial portion of consolidated cash is held overseas while debt is primarily in the U.S. The nearer risk is refinancing $1,750M of 5.650% paper; at current spreads that is a modest coupon change, not a solvency event.

**Trade policy in both directions.** The IEEPA reversal is a windfall, but the 10-Q warns that negative sentiment toward U.S. companies could affect global purchasing decisions and that non-IEEPA tariffs and foreign retaliation remain live.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 30.3% against 35.4% one-year realized, an 11th-percentile reading within the current chain, with a balanced 2.6% put/call skew and a ±4.0% implied move on the nearest expiry. Put/call volume is 0.14 and open interest 0.29, both call-tilted; open interest shifted from 0.77 to 0.29 alongside the +13.8% price move since the prior snapshot. Term structure is in contango, near 30.3% against 34.6% at six months. Read together: the options market is pricing no near-term event risk and post-print positioning has rotated toward calls, which is complacent given that the Q3 print in late October is the decisive test of the margin ramp. Cheap near-term volatility makes protection or call-replacement inexpensive for holders. The one countervailing detail is the September 18 tenor, where put/call volume is 2.22 with a 7.9% put skew — that expiry does not span Q3 earnings, so it more likely reflects macro or FOMC hedging than a company-specific view. The reported 453.6% ATM IV in the change snapshot is inconsistent with every tenor in the chain and is a data artifact; I have disregarded it.

Short interest of 4.6% of float with 3.5 days to cover is uncrowded — no squeeze dynamic in either direction, and no informed short base corroborating the bear case.

## Management Quality

The insider record is the strongest positive in the file. Between April 30 and May 22, eight individuals bought approximately $6.42M of stock in open-market transactions: CEO Peter Arduini ($250K at $59.93), then-CFO Saccaro ($201K at $60.60), General Counsel Frank Jimenez ($106K at $60.45), and five directors including H. Lawrence Culp Jr. — CEO of GE Aerospace and GE's CEO at the spin — who bought $5.0M at $61.88 and roughly doubled his stake to 151,207 shares, and Kevin Lobo at $642K. In the same month the company repurchased 3.276M shares at an average $61.05, its largest quarterly repurchase since the spin.

I weight the breadth more than the aggregate dollars. Several purchases at $60-105K are small enough to be consistent with stock-ownership-guideline compliance, and the trading window almost certainly opened right after the April 29 release, so timing partly reflects availability. Only Culp's and Lobo's are large enough that guideline compliance is an implausible sole explanation. Still, eight insiders including the CEO, CFO, GC and five directors buying within days of a guidance cut, alongside the largest company repurchase to date, is a coherent statement that the board viewed ~$60-62 as materially below intrinsic value. As a matter of record, Saccaro bought $201K on May 1 and his departure was announced July 23; such processes routinely conclude after a purchase decision, and I draw no inference.

Offsetting: the April 29 guidance cut concurrent with a leadership and segment restructuring; a $2.3B acquisition at roughly 10x revenue whose accretion accrues disproportionately to adjusted EPS; CEO FY2025 compensation of $18.56M against cumulative post-spin TSR of $136.20 per $100 versus a peer index at $121.98 — modest outperformance for that pay level; and all three incentive metrics (organic revenue growth, adjusted EBIT, free cash flow) being company-defined non-GAAP measures. To management's credit, the $106M prior-year tariff refund was excluded from adjusted EBIT, removing a benefit from the incentive metric, which is the conservative direction.

Net 6.2 — above average on demonstrated alignment, held back by the guidance reset, the interim CFO, and the price paid for Intelerad.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $96 (+37%)

Record organic orders of 11.1% and a 1.15x book-to-bill convert into second-half revenue at the ~85% secured equipment rate; pricing on the new order book plus $76M-annualized restructuring plus PCS fulfillment remediation deliver the ~17.2% second-half margin; FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at the $5.00 top of guidance and FY2027 at $5.60-$5.80 as the innovation wave (currently under 20% of order value) enters the run rate. PCS is sold at 0.9-1.2x revenue, removing a zero-EBIT drag and roughly 200bp of dilution to consolidated margin, with proceeds to debt reduction and the remaining $500M authorization. Continued IEEPA refund claims add cash. On $5.65 of FY2027 adjusted EPS at 17x — the multiple GEHC held in January and still below the peer median — the equity is worth $96. This aligns with the $96 high analyst target.

### Bear Case — $59 (−16%)

Memory, freight and component inflation persists or worsens through the second half; PCS's 81% decremental proves competitive rather than operational and does not reverse; the margin ramp delivers 16% rather than 17.2%, FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or below $4.70, and management cuts guidance a second time in nine months. A PCS sale process either fails or clears below the $2,040M of allocated goodwill, forcing an impairment that deepens negative tangible book. The multiple compresses to 11.5-12x on $5.00-$5.10 of FY2027 earnings, giving $58-61 — near the $58.75 fifty-two-week low. Tail risks that are not in any current estimate: an adverse ATA outcome now in discovery, or an IRS assertion allocated under the Tax Matters Agreement.

### Base Case — $81 (+15%)

Second-half margin steps up but lands nearer the low end, FY2026 adjusted EPS at $4.85-$4.90 within guidance, FY2027 at consensus $5.41 as AIS and PDx expansion continues and PCS moves toward breakeven or exits. The stock re-rates to 15x FY2027, $81. Consensus mean $82.50 and median $83.00 sit at the same place.

Probability-weighted: 50% base (+15%), 20% bull (+37%), 30% bear (−16%) gives roughly +10% expected return over twelve months, plus a 0.20% dividend. Positive, but not compelling against a 30% probability of a 16% loss, and dependent on a cost outcome management does not control.

## Conclusion

GE HealthCare is a mid-single-digit-growth medtech trading at 14.3x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 13.0x next-year consensus, a 45% discount to the health care equipment peer median. At $70.31 the probability-weighted return is roughly +10% over twelve months, with a 30% path to −16%. That is approximately fair. The investment case does not need more analysis; it needs one quarter of margin data. HOLD, with a specific and checkable path to ACCUMULATE on the October print or on a return to the low $60s.
