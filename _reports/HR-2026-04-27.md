## Executive Summary

Healthcare Realty Trust is the largest pure-play owner of medical outpatient buildings (MOBs) in the U.S., with 563 properties totaling 32.8 million square feet. The company is in the third year of cleaning up its 2022 merger with Healthcare Trust, which has produced $1.16 billion in cumulative impairments, a near-complete leadership overhaul, an auditor change, and the disposition of 70+ properties. Operational metrics are improving — same-store cash NOI grew 4.8% in 2025 and occupancy reached 92.2% — but the stock at $18.71 trades within 1.4% of its 52-week high, pricing in much of the recovery.

The fundamental challenge: normalized FFO guidance for 2026 ($1.58-$1.64/share) is flat year-over-year, $1.3 billion of debt matures in 2026-2027 and will refinance at materially higher rates, and estimate revisions are moving decisively negative. The 5.1% dividend yield is below the peer median of 5.8%, offering inadequate compensation for the execution risk of an ongoing restructuring. With the stock near its 52-week high and no identifiable catalyst for the next step of value creation beyond continued blocking-and-tackling, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Healthcare Realty Trust operates as a self-managed, self-administered REIT focused exclusively on medical outpatient buildings, primarily those located on or adjacent to acute care hospital campuses. This campus-proximity strategy provides tenant stickiness: physicians and outpatient facilities benefit from referral networks and shared patient populations with the adjacent hospital. The portfolio spans 563 properties across major U.S. metropolitan markets, with the largest concentrations in Dallas (9.5%), Seattle (6.1%), Houston (6.0%), and Charlotte (5.4%). No single tenant accounts for more than 10% of revenue.

The company's lease structure is predominantly fixed-escalator (96% of leases, averaging 2.93% annual increases) with a 4.4-year weighted average remaining term and approximately 15% annual expiration. Approximately 58% of leases are net, 30% modified gross, 8% gross, and 4% absolute net.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Operating Performance:** Total rental and other revenues were approximately $1.16 billion in 2025. Same-store cash NOI grew 4.8% for the full year and accelerated to 5.5% in Q4, same-store occupancy reached 92.2% (up 103 bps year-over-year), and tenant retention was 82.7% in Q4. Cash leasing spreads averaged 2.7% for the year, improving to 3.7% in Q4.

**FFO and Earnings:** Normalized FFO per share was $1.61 in 2025, up 3.2% from $1.56 in 2024. NAREIT FFO per share was $1.38, up from $0.52 in the impairment-heavy 2024. GAAP net loss was ($0.71) per share, an improvement from ($1.81) in 2024 but still negative. Funds Available for Distribution (FAD) totaled $448.3 million, up 7.0% from $419.1 million.

**Impairments:** The company recognized $361.1 million in real estate impairments in 2025, following $500.4 million in 2024 (including a $250.5 million goodwill writeoff) and $149.7 million in 2023. The three-year cumulative figure of approximately $1.01 billion in real estate impairments (or approximately $1.16 billion including the goodwill writeoff) represents significant evidence that the 2022 merger resulted in overpayment for assets.

**Balance Sheet and Leverage:** Total debt stands at $4.1 billion. Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDA improved from 6.1x to 5.4x, a meaningful 70 basis point reduction driven by $650 million in term loan repayments and $250 million in senior note retirements. Moody's upgraded the outlook from Negative to Stable (Baa2) in December 2025. Liquidity is adequate at $1.4 billion (credit facility plus cash), and the company has established a $600 million commercial paper program. Covenant compliance is comfortable, with debt/assets at 35.8% against a 60% limit.

**The Refinancing Wall:** $600 million of senior notes due 2026 (effective rate 4.94%) and $500 million due 2027 (effective rate 4.76%). With market rates for investment-grade REIT debt currently in the 5.0-5.5%+ range, refinancing will likely add incremental annual interest expense, directly compressing FFO.

**Rising Leasing Costs:** Leasing commissions increased from $35.9 million (2023) to $47.1 million (2024) to $54.8 million (2025) — a 53% increase in two years. As a percentage of cash NOI, this went from 4.3% to 7.5%. Rent abatements also rose from $14.3 million to $20.9 million. These escalating tenant acquisition costs partially offset headline same-store NOI gains.

**Operating Cash Flow:** $457.1 million in 2025, down 8.9% from $501.6 million in 2024, reflecting the portfolio contraction from dispositions. Capital expenditures totaled $308.8 million.

## Growth Analysis

**2026 Guidance:** Management guided normalized FFO/share of $1.58-$1.64 (midpoint $1.61), flat year-over-year. Management describes approximately 5% "core earnings growth" embedded at the midpoint, offset by dilution from the disposition program. GAAP EPS is guided at ($0.05) to $0.05 — essentially breakeven.

**Estimate Revisions — Decisively Negative:** Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised from –$0.05 to –$0.03 over 90 days (a +30.2% revision, i.e., a smaller loss), but have deteriorated over the last 30 days (–39.5% revision). Next-quarter estimates show a similar pattern (+30.9% over 90 days, –38.1% over 30 days). Current-year estimates have been revised down 22.2% over both 30- and 90-day windows, and next-year estimates have been revised down 66.7% over both windows — though the latter figures are based on only one covering analyst. The recent 30-day deterioration across periods is a negative signal.

**Earnings Surprise History:** One beat and three misses in the last four quarters. The sole beat (Q4 2025, +$0.046 vs. estimate) followed severe misses in Q2 2025 (-$0.368, or -446%) and Q3 2025 (-$0.120, or -241%). The pattern suggests limited management credibility on earnings visibility.

**Organic Growth Drivers:** Same-store cash NOI growth of 3.5-4.5% (2026 guide) is respectable but insufficient to generate per-share FFO growth when offset by disposition dilution and rising refinancing costs. The 96% fixed-escalator lease structure (averaging 2.93%) limits inflation passthrough in a persistently inflationary environment where operating expenses are growing faster.

**Disposition-Driven Simplification:** The company sold 70 properties for approximately $1.09 billion at a 6.7% blended cap rate in 2025. This is positive for portfolio quality and leverage reduction but mechanically dilutive to earnings and cash flow — the proceeds were used primarily for debt repayment rather than reinvestment, compressing the earning asset base.

## Valuation Assessment

HR trades at $18.71, approximately 11.6x 2025 normalized FFO ($1.61) and 11.6x 2026 midpoint FFO guidance ($1.61). The dividend yield is 5.1% ($0.96 annualized). The dividend payout ratio was 87% of FAD for 2025, improving to 75% in Q4.

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E is 32.3x and the median yield is 5.8%. HR's negative GAAP P/E (-26.3x) makes direct P/E comparison misleading. On an FFO multiple basis, the 11.6x multiple appears reasonable for a healthcare REIT in restructuring mode, but the flat FFO trajectory and negative estimate revisions limit rerating potential.

**Price Positioning:** The stock has rallied approximately 19% from its April 2025 lows near $15.63. The analyst consensus median target is $19.00 with a range of $18.00-$21.00. With 11 analysts covering (1 Strong Buy, 2 Buy, 8 Hold, 0 Sell), the consensus is overwhelmingly neutral. The mean target of $19.18 offers just 2.5% upside from the current price.

**Dividend Sustainability:** The $0.96 annual dividend appears covered by FAD ($448.3 million on approximately 347 million shares ≈ $1.29/share FAD), but the margin of safety narrows in 2026 as disposition-driven FAD dilution and higher refinancing costs compress available cash.

## Competitive Landscape

HR's competitive position as the largest pure-play MOB REIT is stable but not improving relative to peers. The company's 10-K explicitly acknowledges that some competitors have lower costs of capital, which limits acquisition competitiveness. The TSR comparison in the proxy is stark: HR's indexed TSR of 77.63 versus the peer group's 181.38 over the measurement period from 2021, indicating substantial relative value destruction.

The hospital-campus proximity strategy provides structural defensibility through referral network effects and tenant switching costs. The shift from inpatient to outpatient care is a genuine secular tailwind for MOBs. However, HR's ability to capitalize on these trends is constrained by its capital position and ongoing restructuring.

An emerging regulatory risk warrants attention: the 10-K flags the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA) passed in 2025 as subject to ongoing analysis for potential negative impacts on healthcare demand and tenant profitability. Separately, proposals to prohibit government healthcare payments to REIT-leased facilities represent a low-probability but high-impact tail risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-Term Refinancing Risk:** The 2026-2027 maturities will refinance at higher rates (see Financial Analysis for specifics), adding incremental interest expense and directly compressing FFO per share.

**Impairment Continuation Risk:** Three consecutive years of large impairments suggest the remaining portfolio may still carry legacy merger-related overvaluation. Further writedowns would erode book value and could trigger covenant pressure.

**Management Reconstitution Risk:** New CEO (Peter Scott), new CFO (Daniel Gabbay, appointed January 2026), and a change in auditing firm (February 2026) constitute an effectively new leadership team without an established track record. The auditor change, in the context of the cumulative impairments and CFO transition, warrants monitoring for any subsequent restatements or accounting methodology changes.

**Escalating Pay-Performance Disconnect:** CEO compensation rose to $12.85 million (2025) from $6.28 million (2023) while the stock dramatically underperformed the peer group. The performance metrics are weighted toward internal operating measures, though relative three-year TSR is also listed as a performance measure, which partially addresses this concern.

**Regulatory Risk (OBBBA / Site-Neutral Policies):** Changes to Medicaid/Medicare reimbursement could impair tenant profitability and, by extension, their ability to pay rent.

**Fixed Escalator Inflation Lag:** With 96% of leases on fixed escalators averaging 2.93%, HR has limited ability to pass through higher operating costs in an inflationary environment.

**Options Market Signal:** ATM implied volatility of 36.2% is elevated, reflecting meaningful uncertainty around the restructuring trajectory. The put/call open interest ratio of 0.15 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting retail-driven optimism rather than institutional hedging. This divergence between elevated IV and call-heavy positioning is consistent with momentum buying near highs rather than informed accumulation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($20-21):** The portfolio simplification succeeds in creating a higher-quality, higher-margin MOB portfolio. Same-store NOI growth sustains at 4%+, occupancy continues to improve toward 93-94%, and management successfully refinances the 2026-2027 maturities without material FFO compression. The stock rerates from 11.6x to 13x FFO, yielding $20.90-$21.00.

**Bear Case ($15-16):** Refinancing costs exceed expectations, further impairments continue, and the OBBBA or Medicaid cuts impair tenant demand. Recent negative estimate revisions prove prescient, and normalized FFO declines to $1.45-$1.50 as disposition dilution and interest expense outpace organic growth. At 10.5-11x FFO, the stock revisits $15.50-$16.50. The dividend yield would need to reach 6%+ to attract value buyers, implying $16.00.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The primary negative catalysts are: (1) the 2026 debt maturity refinancing, which will be visible in quarterly interest expense starting mid-2026; (2) recent negative estimate revisions over the last 30 days across all periods; (3) the next earnings report (likely May 2026), which could reveal whether Q1 same-store NOI growth is decelerating or leasing costs continue to escalate. No single binary event creates an acute catalyst.

**Timing window:** The debt maturity wall creates a 6-18 month window where refinancing announcements will be the dominant narrative. The next quarterly earnings is the nearest event risk.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI% of float at 8.9% with 5.3 days to cover — moderately elevated but not crowded. This level suggests some informed bearish positioning without creating acute squeeze risk. Institutional ownership is likely high given the market cap ($6.4B).

**Borrow and options signal:** ATM IV of 36.2% indicates moderate option cost. The 0.15 put/call OI ratio is extremely call-heavy, meaning the options market is not confirming the bearish thesis — a warning sign for prospective shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $15.50-$16.50 represents 12-17% downside from $18.71. Bull case of $20-$21 represents 7-12% upside. The risk/reward is approximately 1.3:1 to 1.5:1 — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a mediocre REIT recovering from a bad merger with headwinds, not an actively collapsing business with a timed catalyst. The stock is near its 52-week high, options positioning is call-heavy, and the downside/upside ratio is insufficiently asymmetric. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** HR would become interesting as a potential BUY if: (1) the stock pulls back to $15.50-$16.00, restoring a 6%+ dividend yield and creating meaningful upside to normalized FFO recovery; (2) the 2026 debt maturity is refinanced at rates confirming limited incremental annual interest expense; (3) estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive for two consecutive months. All three conditions would indicate the restructuring discount is excessive.

**Timeline:** The headwinds from the refinancing wall, management reconstitution, and negative estimate momentum are likely to persist through at least Q3 2026. Revisit after the 2026 debt maturity is addressed.

## Conclusion

Healthcare Realty Trust is executing a necessary but incomplete cleanup from a value-destructive 2022 merger. The operational improvements are real — same-store NOI growth, occupancy gains, and leverage reduction are all moving in the right direction. However, at $18.71 near the 52-week high, with flat FFO guidance, recently negative estimate revisions, near-term debt maturities requiring refinancing at higher rates, escalating leasing costs, an untested management team, an auditor change, and a dividend yield below peer medians, the risk/reward is skewed negatively. The stock has priced in the recovery that has occurred without adequately discounting the headwinds ahead. AVOID.
