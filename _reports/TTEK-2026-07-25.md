**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tetra Tech is a high-end water and environmental consulting firm that experienced a sharp revenue reset in FY2026 after the early-2025 cancellation of essentially all its USAID contracts. H1 FY2026 revenue fell 11.4% year-over-year to $2.43B and the stock has de-rated roughly 26% from its $43.14 52-week high to $31.71, but the underlying business held up better than the headline suggests. Adjusted EPS was flat (H1 $0.69 vs. $0.68), segment margins expanded because the lost USAID work was low-margin and cost-reimbursable, and backlog grew to $4.28B from $4.14B at year-end. The central debate for this name is the gap between GAAP optics (revenue down double digits) and underlying quality (flat adjusted earnings, expanding margins, growing backlog).

The company is a lightly-levered, asset-light professional-services franchise: 1.32x consolidated leverage, 18.1x interest coverage, ~$439M FY2025 FCF on negligible capex, and a covenant-comfortable balance sheet. The primary structural caveat is that ~51% of assets are goodwill from a serial-acquisition model, and the GDS impairment showed how a policy shock can convert that goodwill into a write-down. Management has responded by pivoting the federal book from foreign aid toward higher-margin defense/IT/cyber via the ~$210M Halvik acquisition, while continuing bolt-on M&A internationally.

At ~18x forward EPS (consensus FY26 ~$1.55, FY27 ~$1.71) against a peer median near 28x, TTEK trades at a discount that reflects the revenue-decline narrative more than the earnings reality. Estimates have stabilized after cutting earlier in 2026, four consecutive earnings beats support consensus, and a new director bought stock in the open market at $26.41. What is missing is a dated catalyst and clean confirmation that the ex-USAID franchise is re-accelerating in reported revenue rather than just backlog. We rate the stock HOLD (growth-oriented) with an upgrade path if organic growth turns positive and margins hold.

## Company Overview

Tetra Tech provides consulting and engineering services centered on water, environment, and sustainable infrastructure. It is a knowledge- and labor-intensive professional-services firm rather than a capital-intensive contractor, with roughly 25,000 employees working on 100,000+ projects across 100+ countries. It has been ranked #1 by ENR in Water Treatment & Desalination for 12 consecutive years and leads several environmental and consulting categories.

The company realigned into two reportable segments at the start of FY2026:
- **Government Services Group (GSG):** U.S. federal/state/local plus international development agencies — water, environment, infrastructure, IT, disaster management. Roughly 45–49% of revenue.
- **Commercial/International Group (CIG):** U.S. commercial plus international (UK, Canada, Australia, Europe, Brazil) — energy, industrial, buildings, international water/infrastructure. Roughly 52–57% of revenue.

Its competitive advantages are deep technical domain expertise, decades-long client relationships that create institutional knowledge as a bidding moat, its proprietary "Tetra Tech Delta" AI/data-analytics platform with a growing software-subscription element, and a disciplined project-management culture. It competes with AECOM, Jacobs, Stantec, WSP, Arcadis, AtkinsRéalis, Black & Veatch, ICF, Leidos, and others.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the growth-to-decline transition.** FY2022→FY2025 revenue grew from $3.50B to $5.44B, largely on the 2023 RPS acquisition ($784M) and disaster-response surges. FY2025 revenue was $5.44B (+4.7%), with net revenue (ex-subcontractor) up 6.8%. Growth then reversed in FY2026: H1 revenue fell 11.4% to $2.43B on the USAID cancellation and lower disaster work, with Q2 revenue of $1.22B (-7.7%).

**Two FY2025 charges dominate the reported numbers.** A $92.4M non-cash goodwill impairment of the Global Development Services unit (a direct consequence of USAID terminations) and a $115.0M legal contingency charge tied to the Hunters Point Naval Shipyard settlement. These masked strong operations: GAAP operating income fell 18.4% to $408M, but adjusted operating income rose 18.3% to $603.6M; adjusted EPS was $1.56 (+23.8%) vs. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.93.

**Margins are improving as low-margin work rolls off.** GSG operating margin (on net revenue) rose to 16.4% in H1 FY2026 from 14.2%, explicitly because low-margin cost-reimbursable USAID work was eliminated. CIG margin held around 14.3% on higher-end consulting mix. H1 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $0.69 (vs. $0.68) confirms the mix shift is offsetting volume loss, a quality-of-earnings positive that the revenue line obscures.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are strong.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $457.7M (+27.6%); FCF was roughly $439M on capex of only ~$19M, reflecting the asset-light model. H1 FY2026 OCF was $237.6M. Consolidated leverage is 1.32x with 18.1x interest coverage, well inside the 3.5x/3.0x covenants. The screening D/E of 2.41 overstates risk because it captures ~$230M of operating-lease liabilities against goodwill-heavy equity. Debt comprises $575M of 2.25% convertible notes (conversion ~$39.25, out of the money, maturing Aug 2028) plus ~$315M of credit-facility borrowings, roughly $880M total against ~$1.86B equity. Goodwill of $2.21B is ~51% of assets, the principal balance-sheet risk. Altman Z of 2.48 sits in the grey zone, which reflects the goodwill-heavy, moderate-margin nature of the E&C sector rather than distress.

**Earnings track record supports consensus.** The company has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters (by 4.2% to 10.9%), which raises confidence in the current forward estimates.

**Capital allocation is balanced.** The company reactivated buybacks ($250M in FY2025, $100M in H1 FY2026 with ~$498M remaining), raised the dividend to $0.072/quarter (from $0.058 in late 2024), and continued bolt-on M&A. One note of caution: H1 FY2026 repurchases were executed near $34.55, above the current $31.71, so recent buyback timing was not opportunistic.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative on the surface and stabilizing underneath. The H1 revenue decline was almost entirely the USAID roll-off (a ~$330M / -36% federal revenue decline in H1) and reduced disaster-response work. Because that revenue was low-margin, the earnings impact was far smaller than the revenue impact, leaving adjusted EPS flat.

The forward drivers management points to are U.S. federal ex-USAID/disaster, state/local water, international, and U.S. commercial ex-renewables. Several data points corroborate the recovery narrative: backlog rose to $4.28B / $4.22B RUPO at Q2 FY2026 from $4.14B / $4.10B at year-end, with ~70% converting within 12 months; UK revenue grew 12.3% in FY2025; state/local water grew ~10% ex-disaster in H1 FY2026; and recent wins include an EPA Office of Water $25M contract (Jul 2026) and Scotland water framework awards. The AI-data-center water-stress thesis is a genuine secular tailwind for a firm that leads in water and "smart water."

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current year $1.55 (+1.2% over 90 days), next year $1.71 (+0.7%), next quarter +3.1%. This does not clear the high bar for a strong BUY signal (which requires >30% revision velocity plus accelerating revenue). Growth prospects are directionally improving off a trough but the reported revenue line is still contracting, and organic re-acceleration has not yet shown up outside the backlog. This supports a mid-range growth score, consistent with the trajectory's decline from 6.8 to 5.5 as the USAID reset played through.

## Valuation Assessment

TTEK trades at ~18.2x forward EPS on consensus FY26 of ~$1.55 and ~18.5x on FY27 of ~$1.71. Against the Construction & Engineering peer median P/E of 28.4, that is roughly a 36% discount. The discount is only partly justified: peers such as AECOM (14.5x) trade cheaper, while FLR (22.8x), ACA (28.4x), ECG (32.9x), and MYRG (46.8x) trade richer. The dispersion reflects a heterogeneous sector, but Tetra Tech's higher-margin, asset-light, water-led mix warrants a premium to construction-heavy peers rather than a discount.

The bull-case valuation argument rests on adjusted earnings power, which held flat in FY2026 despite the revenue decline as margins expanded. On adjusted numbers the stock trades around 20x, reasonable for a market-leading professional-services franchise with an 8% FCF margin and negligible capex. A new director's open-market purchase and management's own buyback authorization (albeit at a higher price) support the idea that intrinsic value sits above the current price.

The counterweight is that a peer-basis discount is not by itself a reason to own the stock: a compressed multiple that reflects a genuine revenue-decline story can persist until the top line stabilizes in reported results. There is no dated catalyst forcing a re-rating, estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating, and analyst targets (low $35, mean $39.50, median $40, high $44) already sit above the current price without a near-term trigger to close the gap. We score valuation moderately positive: the stock is fairly-to-attractively priced with modest asymmetry to the upside, but not a compelling mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Tetra Tech occupies a defensible niche as the recognized leader in water and environmental consulting, differentiated from larger construction-heavy peers (AECOM, Jacobs, WSP) by higher-end, science-led advisory positioning and lower capital intensity. The moat is built on technical domain expertise, long client relationships, and the proprietary Delta AI/analytics platform. Secular tailwinds — municipal water reinvestment, climate adaptation and flood resilience, energy transition, digital/smart water, and rising water demand from AI data centers — support long-run demand for exactly the services it leads in.

The trajectory has softened modestly. The competitive-position score declined from 7.2 to 6.5 in prior reviews as the federal-policy shock cut a meaningful slice of the government book and forced a portfolio pivot. The Halvik acquisition (600-person federal defense/civilian data-analytics, modernization, and cybersecurity) is a deliberate rebuild of federal exposure toward higher-value work, and backlog growth suggests the core franchise is intact and re-accelerating. Rising fixed-price mix (48% in Q2 FY2026 vs. 36% in FY2023) shifts more estimation and cost-overrun risk onto the firm, a mild negative for the quality of the competitive position. On balance the moat is stable-to-slightly-improving, warranting a mid-6 score.

## Risk Assessment

**U.S. federal / policy concentration (the defining risk).** USAID was 10.6% of FY2025 revenue and DoD 11.6%; U.S. federal total was ~26–32%. Virtually all USAID contracts were terminated for convenience in early 2025. This risk has largely materialized and is the main driver of the FY2026 revenue decline. The forward risk is a further federal-budget or appropriations shock; the company's pivot toward defense/IT is well-directed but exposes it to fixed-price and rebid pressure.

**Goodwill impairment.** $2.2B of goodwill (~51% of assets). Management disclosed that at the FY2025 annual review all units cleared fair value by >38% except GDS, so remaining goodwill has cushion, but another federal shock or a soured acquisition could trigger further charges.

**Legal/environmental tail — Hunters Point.** Tetra Tech EC settled the government False Claims Act/CERCLA matter for $97M ($57M FCA + $40M CERCLA), plus $18M for ancillary claims (the $115M charge), with no admission of liability. Third-party private ancillary claims remain unresolved, and the company is separately litigating its insurance carrier to recover part of the settlement, an unresolved swing factor.

**Rising fixed-price exposure.** 43.5% of FY2025 revenue and 48% in Q2 FY2026, up from 36% in FY2023, shifting cost-overrun risk onto the firm.

**FX translation.** ~44% of H1 FY2026 revenue is international; FY2025 saw a $74M half-year translation hit to equity.

**Founder-CEO succession.** Dan Batrack (CEO since 2005) handed the role to Roger Argus (a 30-year insider); CFO Steven Burdick remains, providing continuity. The dense 8-K cadence reflects a substantial leadership and board refresh worth monitoring for strategic continuity.

**Acquisition/earn-out risk.** Contingent earn-out liabilities rose to $108M (max ~$205M) after Halvik.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 46.0% is rich against 36.2% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (46.0% near vs. 42.0% at six months), which typically prices an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings report. The 3-month tenor shows an 8.8% put skew, indicating some hedging demand for the fall. Near-term put/call skew is balanced (-0.8%), and the implied one-month move is ±10.8%. The very high near-term put/call volume ratio and the OI-ratio jump appear to be thin-options artifacts and are treated as low-signal. Net read: the market is pricing event risk into the next print without a strong directional put bias. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry until post-earnings vol crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The USAID cancellation is a one-time clearing event, not an ongoing bleed. It removed low-margin revenue, so adjusted EPS held flat and segment margins expanded. Backlog is growing, the pivot to defense/IT/cyber (Halvik) and international water is redeploying capital into higher-value work, and secular water/climate/AI-data-center demand supports durable long-term growth. With 8% FCF margins, negligible capex, and a lightly-levered balance sheet, the stock re-rates toward the mid-to-high $30s as reported revenue stabilizes and consensus catches up to the earnings resilience. Bull target ~$44 (top of analyst range, ~26x FY27 EPS).

**Bear case.** Federal-policy risk is not over: further appropriations shocks could hit the government book again, and the rising fixed-price mix raises the odds of cost-overrun charges. Goodwill at 51% of assets remains an impairment risk if a segment sours. Hunters Point ancillary/insurance litigation is unresolved. Organic revenue is still contracting in reported results, estimate revisions are flat, and the CEO succession introduces execution uncertainty. If organic growth fails to turn positive, the multiple stays compressed and the stock drifts toward the low $30s / high $20s. Bear target ~$26 (roughly -18% from current, near the director's buy level and the 52-week low of $25.81).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon, classified as a growth-oriented hold: the earnings-resilience-plus-backlog-growth thesis is credible but not yet confirmed in reported revenue, and there is no dated catalyst forcing a near-term re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth turns positive year-over-year (ex-USAID/disaster comps normalize, likely late FY2026/early FY2027) while GSG margins hold ≥16%.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions accelerate to >+10% over 90 days, confirming the market is re-rating the earnings base rather than just the revenue optics.
- A pullback toward the high-$20s (near the director's $26.41 buy) that widens the discount to intrinsic value without fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further federal-contract or appropriations shock that cuts >5% of the government book, or a second goodwill impairment.
- Fixed-price cost-overrun charges that compress GSG/CIG margins by >150bps.
- An adverse Hunters Point ancillary ruling or insurance-recovery loss materially above the $18M reserve.
- Backlog declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters.

**Next reassessment point:** the next quarterly earnings release (the options market is pricing a ±10.8% move around it), with the FY2026 organic-growth exit rate as the key data point.

## Conclusion

Tetra Tech is a high-quality, cash-generative water and environmental consulting leader whose FY2026 revenue decline reads worse than the underlying business performed. The USAID cancellation removed low-margin revenue, lifted segment margins, left adjusted EPS flat, and coincided with growing backlog, a clearing event rather than a structural deterioration. The balance sheet is lightly levered, FCF conversion is high, and the valuation at ~18x forward EPS sits at a ~36% discount to the peer median that is only partly justified.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are that reported revenue is still contracting, estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating, there is no dated catalyst to close the valuation gap, and the founder-CEO succession, rising fixed-price exposure, Hunters Point tail claims, and 51%-of-assets goodwill keep the risk profile two-sided. We maintain HOLD (growth-oriented) with an overall score of 6.15, consistent with the recent trajectory. The clearest path to a BUY is confirmation that the ex-USAID franchise is re-accelerating in reported results while margins hold, ideally on a pullback toward the high-$20s where the risk/reward turns more asymmetric.
