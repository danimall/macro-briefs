## Executive Summary

HCA Healthcare is the largest for-profit hospital system in the United States, operating 190 hospitals and approximately 2,500 ambulatory sites across 19 states and the UK. The company generated $75.6B in revenue and $12.6B in operating cash flow in 2025, with diluted EPS of $28.33 representing 28.8% YoY growth driven by both operational improvement and aggressive share repurchases ($10.1B in 2025 alone, reducing share count by 18.7% since 2022).

Two quantifiable headwinds define the 2026 setup: the expiration of ACA enhanced premium tax credits ($600M-$900M adjusted EBITDA impact) and Medicaid supplemental payment program reform under the Federal Budget Act ($250M-$450M net decline). Together, these represent $850M-$1.35B in EBITDA pressure against a $15.6B 2025 base. Management has guided 2026 EBITDA of $15.55B-$16.45B, implying the midpoint assumes partial offset through volume growth, labor cost improvement, and the $400M+ resiliency savings program.

At ~$507, HCA trades at approximately 17x trailing and 16.7x forward EPS (using the $30.33 consensus), a modest discount to its own recent trading range and roughly in line with the peer median of 19.1x. The stock has pulled back 9% from its March 12 high of $556.52. Given the 4-quarter consecutive earnings beat record (average 12% surprise), visible EPS growth from ongoing buybacks, and structural competitive advantages in scale and Sun Belt demographics, the risk-reward at current levels favors accumulation.

## Company Overview

HCA Healthcare operates the largest network of acute care hospitals, outpatient surgery centers, and ambulatory care facilities in the U.S. The company is headquartered in Nashville, Tennessee, and its footprint is heavily weighted toward Sun Belt states: Florida (47 hospitals) and Texas (55 hospitals) together generate 51% of total revenue and 59% of admissions.

Revenue is diversified across payers: Medicare (46% of admissions), managed care and commercial (32%), Medicaid (15%), and uninsured (7%). Government programs account for 45.4% of total revenue. Medicaid state-directed and supplemental payment programs contributed approximately $6.2B in 2025, a material line item now subject to legislative reform.

HCA's operational model centers on centralized data analytics, purchasing, revenue cycle management, and shared services across nearly 200 hospitals — scale advantages that are functionally irreplicable by smaller competitors. The company is investing in AI-driven clinical documentation, a new EHR platform rollout, and outpatient network expansion to capture the ongoing shift from inpatient to ambulatory care settings.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue grew from $60.2B (2022) to $75.6B (2025), a 7.9% CAGR driven by 2-5% equivalent admission growth annually plus 3-4% revenue per admission improvement. Same-facility revenue growth was 6.6% in 2025. For 2026, management has guided $76.5B-$80.0B, implying 1.2-5.8% growth — a wide range reflecting uncertainty around ACA coverage losses and Medicaid reform.

Profitability has improved materially. Salaries and benefits as a percentage of revenue declined from 45.4% (2023) to 43.5% (2025), reflecting the normalization of pandemic-era contract labor costs. Net income margins expanded from 8.1% to 9.0% over the same period. EBITDA grew from $12.7B to $15.6B (22.8% over two years).

### EPS Growth and Capital Return

Diluted EPS grew from $18.96 (2023) to $28.33 (2025), a 49.3% increase in two years. Approximately half of this was driven by operational improvement and half by share count reduction (276.4M to 239.5M diluted shares). The $10.1B buyback in 2025 alone retired 26.7 million shares. A fresh $10B authorization was approved in January 2026. At the current share price, this represents roughly 9% of the market cap, implying another 8-9% reduction in share count over the next 12-18 months.

Consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS are $30.33, rising to $33.27 for FY2027 — 7.1% and 9.7% growth respectively. These estimates have been stable over the past 30 days but up 2.0% and 1.0% over 90 days for the current and next fiscal years, respectively. Estimate stability despite the well-publicized ACA and Medicaid headwinds suggests the market has already absorbed these risks.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow reached $12.6B in 2025 (+20% YoY). After $4.9B in capital expenditures, free cash flow was $7.7B (10.2% FCF margin). This provides the foundation for HCA's capital return strategy.

The balance sheet requires nuanced interpretation. Total debt stands at $46.5B, and stockholders' equity is negative $6.0B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.14 falls in the "distress zone." However, negative equity is a structural artifact of cumulative buybacks exceeding retained earnings — a pattern shared by other high-FCF companies like McDonald's and Starbucks. The meaningful solvency metrics are:

- **OCF / Total Debt:** 27.1% — strong
- **Interest Coverage:** 5.5x ($9.8B pretax income + $2.2B interest / $2.2B interest)
- **Fixed-rate debt:** $44B of $46.5B total, at an average effective rate of 5.0%
- **Liquidity:** $8B unsecured revolving facility, plus a $4B commercial paper program
- **Debt maturity profile:** Well-laddered with no near-term cliff

HCA maintains an investment-grade credit rating and refinanced $8.5B in notes in 2025, transitioning from secured to unsecured credit facilities — a positive signal of improving credit quality in the eyes of lenders.

The real risk from leverage is flexibility, not solvency. At $46.5B in debt with $10B+ annual buybacks, HCA operates with thin cushion against a severe downturn. A recession that compressed volumes by 5-7% while simultaneously increasing uncompensated care would strain the buyback capacity, though it would not threaten debt service.

### Earnings Consistency

HCA has beaten consensus EPS in all four most recent quarters, with surprises of 10.5%, 8.2%, 20.6%, and 8.0%. The average surprise of 11.8% suggests either conservative guidance or genuinely superior operational execution. This track record warrants elevated confidence in the $30.33 consensus for FY2026, and the actual result may approach the $31.50 high end of management guidance.

## Growth Analysis

### Volume Drivers

HCA is positioned to benefit from three structural demand tailwinds:

1. **Aging demographics:** Baby Boomers are entering the peak healthcare utilization years. Medicare admissions (46% of HCA's mix) will grow as this cohort ages.
2. **Sun Belt migration:** Florida and Texas continue to attract net domestic migration, providing above-average population growth in HCA's core markets.
3. **Occupancy headroom:** HCA's 73% occupancy rate is near system highs but still below capacity, providing room to absorb volume growth without proportional fixed-cost increases.

Management has guided 2-3% equivalent admission growth for 2026, which appears achievable given these tailwinds even with ACA enrollment losses.

### Headwind Quantification

The two identified headwinds are specific and quantified:

- **ACA enhanced premium tax credit expiration:** Exchange plans comprised 8% of admissions and 10% of revenue in 2025. Management expects a 15-20% decline in exchange volumes and 30% utilization drop among patients who lose coverage. In Florida alone, 159,000 people have already dropped ACA coverage.
- **Medicaid SDP reform:** The Federal Budget Act caps SDP payments using Medicare rates (lower than commercial) and limits provider tax arrangements. Grandfathered arrangements will be reduced 10 percentage points annually starting January 2028, creating a multi-year overhang.

Management's 2026 EBITDA guidance implies confidence that volume growth, labor cost improvement, and the resiliency program will partially offset these pressures.

### Capital Investment

CapEx is guided at $5.0B-$5.5B for 2026, slightly above the $4.9B spent in 2025. This supports continued outpatient network expansion, technology investment (EHR rollout, AI clinical tools), and capacity additions. The investment in outpatient facilities is strategically important as CMS phases out the inpatient-only list through 2028, shifting volume toward ambulatory settings.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$507, HCA trades at:
- **17.9x trailing EPS** ($28.33)
- **16.7x FY2026E EPS** ($30.33)
- **15.2x FY2027E EPS** ($33.27)
- **9% below 52-week high** of $556.52

Against the peer group, HCA's 17.9x P/E compares to a median of 19.1x. Tenet Healthcare (THC) trades at 12.8x, Universal Health Services (UHS) at 9.4x, and Ensign Group (ENSG) at 29.8x. HCA's premium over THC and UHS is justified by its dramatically larger scale, superior margin profile, and proven buyback-driven EPS accretion. The discount to ENSG reflects different sub-industry dynamics (skilled nursing vs. acute care).

The consensus mean analyst price target is $543 (median $551), implying 7-9% upside from current levels. The range spans $425 to $635. The bull case target of $635 implies ~18x FY2027 EPS, which is achievable if ACA headwinds prove smaller than feared and the resiliency program delivers.

On a FCF yield basis, $7.7B of 2025 FCF against a $112B market cap yields 6.9% — attractive for a company growing EPS 7-10% annually. Even assuming FCF compresses modestly in 2026 due to the ACA/Medicaid headwinds and higher capex, a 6%+ FCF yield on a dominant franchise is compelling.

The stock's pullback from its $556 high reflects the market pricing in the ACA and Medicaid headwinds. With estimates stable (not declining) and the earnings beat record intact, the market appears to have reached a reasonable equilibrium that leaves room for upside if the headwinds prove manageable.

## Competitive Landscape

HCA's competitive position is among the strongest in healthcare services. Its 190-hospital footprint generates purchasing power, data analytics capabilities, and operational efficiencies that smaller systems cannot match. Revenue is 2-3x larger than the next for-profit competitor (Tenet Healthcare). Key competitive factors:

**Scale advantages:** Centralized supply chain, revenue cycle management, and staffing analytics across 190 hospitals create structural cost advantages. The $400M+ resiliency program is an example of system-wide optimization that only HCA's scale permits.

**Geographic positioning:** Sun Belt concentration provides above-average demographic growth. Florida and Texas are among the fastest-growing states by population, supporting sustained volume increases.

**Capital deployment:** HCA's annual OCF and investment-grade credit rating enable aggressive investment in technology, capacity expansion, and M&A that smaller competitors cannot match.

**Vulnerabilities:** The shift from inpatient to outpatient settings benefits HCA (it operates surgery centers and ambulatory sites) but also exposes it to new competitors. Not-for-profit systems with tax-exempt status can access cheaper capital. Payer consolidation gives large insurers increasing bargaining power on rates.

## Risk Assessment

### Regulatory / Legislative Risk (High)
The ACA and Medicaid SDP headwinds (quantified in Growth Analysis) represent the primary near-term threats. The SDP reform tail — the 10-percentage-point annual reduction in grandfathered arrangements starting January 2028 — creates ongoing erosion that could reach $1-2B in cumulative revenue impact over 3-5 years. Any further Medicaid cuts in budget reconciliation would disproportionately impact HCA given its 15% Medicaid admissions mix.

### Geographic Concentration (Moderate)
Florida and Texas represent 51% of revenue. Hurricane exposure ($250M cost from Hurricanes Helene/Milton in 2024), state-level regulatory changes, or regional economic weakness would have outsized impact.

### Leverage Risk (Moderate)
$46.5B of debt is manageable at current cash flow levels but leaves limited margin for error. In a recession scenario where volumes decline 5-7% and uncompensated care rises, the buyback program would need to be curtailed. Debt service ($2.2B interest) would remain covered, but equity returns would compress.

### Labor Market (Low-Moderate)
Salaries at 43.5% of revenue have improved from 45.4% in 2023, but any resurgence in healthcare labor costs (nurse shortages, physician recruitment pressures) would directly compress margins.

### Professional Liability (Low but Deteriorating)
Unfavorable development on prior years' claims has increased from $46M (2023) to $82M (2024) to $126M (2025). While still small relative to revenue, this trend warrants monitoring.

### Inflation Environment
Rising energy costs and broader input cost inflation could pressure supply expenses (15% of revenue) and construction costs for capacity expansion. In a stagflationary environment with embedded 3.3%+ CPI and oil-driven cost pressures, HCA's cost base faces headwinds partially offset by its ability to negotiate rate increases with commercial payers.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV is 30.5% vs. 26.2% historical volatility, a 4.4pp richness suggesting the market is pricing modestly elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew at 7.6% indicates elevated put demand, consistent with the known ACA/Medicaid headwinds. The 1-month tenor shows the highest IV (36.4%) with a put/call volume ratio of 2.58, suggesting institutional hedging around the next earnings catalyst window. The 3-month tenor has a put/call volume ratio of 11.18 (extremely put-heavy), likely reflecting positioning around mid-year Medicaid policy implementation. Short interest at 2.3% with 3.1 days to cover is low and unremarkable. The options market is pricing known headwinds, not undiscovered risk, which is consistent with the fundamental case for accumulation.

## Conclusion

HCA Healthcare at ~$507 represents a high-quality franchise trading at a reasonable valuation during a period of quantified, well-understood headwinds. The company's competitive position as the dominant U.S. hospital operator is secure. FCF generation of $7.7B (10.2% margin), a 4/4 earnings beat record averaging 12% surprise, and $10B in authorized buybacks provide visible EPS growth of 7-10% annually.

The ACA and Medicaid headwinds are real but bounded. The 9% pullback from the 52-week high has partially priced these in, and consensus estimates have remained stable rather than declining.

The specific risk that warrants monitoring is the Medicaid SDP reform tail, which could erode $1-2B in revenue over 3-5 years. This is the primary reason the valuation score is not higher. However, HCA's scale, data capabilities, and management track record of operational adaptation provide confidence that the company will navigate this transition.

At 16.7x FY2026 earnings with 7%+ EPS growth, a 6.9% FCF yield, and structural demand tailwinds from aging demographics and Sun Belt migration, HCA is a BUY for investors with a 12-month horizon.
