**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Par Pacific is a Houston-based downstream energy company operating four refineries (~219 Mbpd) in geographically isolated western U.S. and Hawaii markets, plus retail fuel/convenience and logistics segments. The stock has roughly doubled off its 2025 lows, closing at $77 against a 52-week low of $26.83, on a crack-spread surge tied to Middle East tension and the reported effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Trailing EPS of $7.16 puts the stock at ~8.5x, visually cheap for a profitable refiner, but that multiple sits on peak-cycle earnings inflated by two non-recurring drivers that will not repeat at the same magnitude.

The central analytical issue is earnings quality. FY2025 GAAP net income of $367M included a one-time ~$199.5M Small Refinery Exemption (SRE) benefit under the Renewable Fuel Standard. In Q1 2026, management booked the full RFS obligation with zero SRE assumption because the EPA has not ruled on 2025-compliance-year exemptions. Separately, the Q1'26 turnaround (net income of $54.5M vs. a $30.4M loss a year prior) was driven by the Singapore 3.1.2 crack rising from $13.12 to $36.01/bbl, roughly $60M of favorable feedstock costs, and a ~$151M FIFO tailwind that reverses if crude prices fall. Much of the reported strength is macro and timing, not durable operational improvement. Operating cash flow was negative $40.7M in that "record" quarter, driven by working-capital build and $76.9M of unrealized derivative losses.

We rate PARR a HOLD (uncertain). The business is well-managed, logistically moated, and carries a strong balance sheet, and Hawaii Renewables is a genuine multi-year diversification catalyst. But the current price capitalizes peak crack spreads plus a one-time regulatory benefit, and the Hormuz situation is a two-sided binary: a verified reopening compresses the very margins driving the stock, while a prolonged closure supports them. After two consecutive quarterly EPS misses (-9.9% in Q4'25, -13.9% in Q1'26) against elevated expectations, we require a better entry or evidence of durable normalized earnings before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific operates through four segments. Refining (~97% of revenue) comprises four facilities: Kapolei, Hawaii (~94 Mbpd), Billings, Montana (~63 Mbpd, acquired from ExxonMobil in 2023), Tacoma, Washington, and Newcastle, Wyoming. The geographic isolation of Hawaii (essentially a captive market) and the inland Rockies gives Par a structural logistics advantage and insulates it from direct Gulf Coast competition. Retail operates fuel/convenience stores in Hawaii, Washington, and Idaho under the Hele, nomnom, and 76 brands, providing a steadier, counter-cyclical stream. Logistics runs a multi-modal network (single point mooring, rail, pipelines, terminals, marine vessels), largely intercompany but a stable contributor (~$24.5M Q1'26 operating income).

The company also holds equity interests: 46% in Laramie Energy (Colorado natural gas), 65% in Yellowstone Energy LP (Billings cogeneration), 40% in Yellowstone Pipeline, and 63.5% in Hawaii Renewables, the consolidated renewable-fuels JV with Mitsubishi and ENEOS (via Alohi, 36.5%) that started operations in April 2026.

Par is a niche, logistically-moated regional refiner rather than a scale player like Marathon or Valero. That positioning is the durable part of the story; the crack-spread cyclicality is the volatile part.

## Financial Analysis

Par's earnings history illustrates the cyclicality:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income ($M) | 364 | 729 | (33) | 367 |
| Modified FCF/share | $8.70 | $9.21 | $0.63 | $7.98 |

2024 was a trough (a $33M net loss on weak margins); 2025 rebounded to $367M. Valuing PARR on any single year overstates or understates normalized power. Stripping the ~$199.5M SRE benefit leaves normalized 2025 net income closer to ~$168M, materially below the headline that underpins the 8.5x trailing multiple.

Q1 2026 net income attributable to Par was $54.5M ($1.10 diluted EPS), with Adjusted EBITDA of $91.5M vs. $10.1M. Refining Adjusted Gross Margin jumped to $11.16/bbl from $6.59, and the Combined Index rose 160% YoY. The Wyoming refinery, idled 47 days in Q1'25 after a February 2025 incident, ran at full rate. The turnaround was real but macro-driven: roughly $79M from higher cracks, ~$60M from favorable feedstock, and the FIFO tailwind that reverses when crude prices fall.

The balance sheet is a strength. Total debt is ~$959M (ABL $321M + Term Loan $632M) against $1.52B equity, with liquidity of $937.7M ($172M cash + $765M ABL availability). Leverage is manageable, though the ABL was drawn up $146M in Q1'26 to fund a working-capital build (inventory +$133M, receivables +$169M on rising prices). This is timing, not deterioration, but it demonstrates that reported EPS overstates cash generation in rising-price environments.

Capital allocation has been sensible. Share count fell from ~55.3M (Dec 2024) to ~49.3M (Mar 2026), an ~11% reduction, with $28M repurchased in Q1'26 at an average $38.76, well below the current $77. $109.2M remains on the $250M authorization. No dividend is paid, restricted by credit agreements.

The environmental credit position is the swing factor to watch: $272.7M of RINs/environmental credit inventory against a $266.7M gross environmental credit obligation. With the EPA's April 2026 "Set 2" rule setting record-high RVOs for 2026–2027, the cost of compliance and the stakes on any SRE outcome are both rising.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data shows strong upward momentum: current-year EPS consensus has risen +72.9% over 90 days (to $15.81), and current-quarter estimates +119.5%. On the surface this triggers a classic inflection-buy pattern. We weight it with caution here for two reasons. First, the revisions are chasing a crack-spread spike, not a structural earnings step-change; refiner estimates track the futures curve and reverse as fast as they rise. Second, the next-year EPS estimate ($10.51, +38.5%/90d) sits materially below current-year, which is consensus explicitly modeling cyclical normalization: the crack tailwind is expected to fade. That built-in fade caps the durability of the growth signal.

The genuine structural growth story is Hawaii Renewables, commissioned in April 2026 at the Kapolei site. Designed to add renewable diesel/SAF earnings and smooth the refining cycle, it is the one lever that could diversify Par away from pure crack-spread dependence. Near term it is a drag: Q1'26 recorded a $8.3M noncontrolling-interest start-up loss, and Alohi (Mitsubishi/ENEOS) holds put/exit rights under the July 2025 Equity Contribution Agreement. The ramp is real but its earnings contribution is unproven, and it will take multiple quarters before the market can underwrite it as a stable margin source.

The two consecutive EPS misses against a prior streak of beats indicate visibility has deteriorated as expectations rose. That pattern argues against extrapolating the estimate-revision velocity into a high growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $77, PARR trades at ~8.5x trailing EPS and ~8.5x forward. Against peer medians in Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing (P/E 15.3; PBF at 10.8x), the stock looks cheap. This is where discipline matters. The trailing multiple sits on peak-cycle earnings that were flattered by the SRE benefit and a geopolitical crack-spread spike, neither of which is guaranteed to recur. Normalizing out the SRE benefit and mean-reverting cracks toward mid-cycle, the effective multiple on sustainable earnings is meaningfully higher than 8.5x.

A low headline P/E on peak earnings is a value-trap signature in cyclicals, not a buy signal; cheapness reliably sorts the wrong way when it reflects unsustainable earnings. The forward estimate structure confirms the market's view: next-year consensus of $10.51 versus current-year $15.81 implies the earnings base contracts ~33% as the cycle normalizes. On that next-year number, the stock is at ~7.3x, but that "cheapness" simply reflects the expected earnings decline rather than a mispricing.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price tightly (low $65, median $78, high $85, mean $77.71), with the stock trading essentially at consensus. On a premium-momentum name this proximity would be a floor; here, on a cyclical near peak earnings, the targets and the price both reflect the same crack-spread optimism and are not independent confirmation. Sentiment is degrading: one buy rating was cut and a hold added over the trailing period.

The valuation is not egregiously stretched, but there is no identifiable independent catalyst for the market being wrong to the upside at $77. A more attractive entry would come on a pullback toward the mid-$50s (where the stock traded as recently as June, and near recent buyback levels) or on evidence that Hawaii Renewables plus retail/logistics support a durably higher normalized earnings base.

## Competitive Landscape

Par's competitive advantage is structural and durable: geographic isolation in Hawaii, the Pacific Northwest, and the Rockies insulates it from mainland/Gulf Coast competition and confers pricing power. The Hawaii Kapolei refinery is effectively a captive-market asset, and Par captures the Singapore 3.1.2 crack (its benchmark) with limited competitive erosion. The Billings acquisition from ExxonMobil (2023) added inland Rockies exposure with similar logistics moats.

Within the small/mid-cap refiner cohort, Par is positioned as the healthier balance-sheet name; a frequently-cited comparison frames the sector as "one refiner drowning in debt (Delek US) vs. one generating cash (Par Pacific)." Company TSR of 251 (indexed) versus a peer group at 179 confirms multi-year outperformance. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on the strength of the renewables diversification, but the trajectory is not accelerating share gains; it is a maintained niche moat. That warrants a moderate, not high, score.

## Risk Assessment

**Crack-spread cyclicality (dominant risk).** 2024's net loss shows margins can collapse. Current earnings ride a geopolitically-driven spike that could reverse sharply. A verified Strait of Hormuz reopening, the primary macro variable, would compress the Singapore 3.1.2 crack and reverse the FIFO tailwind, directly undercutting the earnings driving the stock.

**RIN/SRE swing factor (critical and unique).** With RIN inventory roughly matching the obligation and record-high 2026–2027 RVOs, this is a swing factor of large magnitude, and the political fight over SREs (farm-state pressure) adds uncertainty. Investors should treat much of the 2025 beat as non-recurring.

**Single-asset concentration.** With only four refineries, unplanned outages are material; the February 2025 Wyoming incident idled a plant for 47 days.

**Environmental/legal liabilities.** Multiple consent decrees: the Hawaii Clean Air Act Consent Decree (resolution "could be material"), Wyoming remediation (~$15.6M accrued plus ~$11.6M wastewater work), a December 2025 Montana/Yale Oil Superfund complaint, Washington cap-and-invest costs, and Hawaii foreign-trade-zone false-claims litigation.

**Labor.** ~49% of the Hawaii and Tacoma workforce is United Steelworkers; the CBA expired January 31, 2026 and is operating on 24-hour extensions, a live risk.

**Renewables JV execution.** Alohi's put/exit rights and near-term start-up losses are drags on the diversification catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 64.2% against 1-year historical vol of 56.3%, rich by ~7.9 points, and in the 71st percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (64.2% near vs. 57.2% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress around the August 4 Q2 print and the Hormuz binary. The implied one-month move is ±16.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.7% near term) but steepens to -9.7% at the six-month tenor, indicating growing demand for downside protection further out, consistent with the crack-normalization risk. Short interest is elevated at 13.1% of float (5.4 days to cover), reflecting institutional bearish positioning on the peak-cycle thesis. The options configuration supports our balanced view: the market is not complacent, and the elevated IV makes this a poor moment to pay up for exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$100–115):** Hormuz disruption persists or grinds, keeping the Singapore 3.1.2 and Combined Index cracks elevated through 2026. Hawaii Renewables ramps ahead of schedule and contributes stable margin, smoothing the cycle. The EPA grants meaningful 2025 SRE relief, monetizing the RIN inventory. Continued buybacks at attractive prices compound per-share value. On sustained elevated cracks, EPS runs near the current-year $15.81 consensus and the stock re-rates on a normalized 7–8x plus renewables optionality.

**Bear Case (~$45–52):** A verified Hormuz reopening compresses cracks toward mid-cycle, the FIFO tailwind reverses, and the full RFS obligation (accrued with no SRE relief) weighs on 2026 earnings. Next-year EPS falls toward the $10.51 consensus or lower on further normalization. Renewables start-up losses persist, a labor disruption or unplanned outage hits, and the stock de-rates toward the mid-$40s where it traded in early 2026, roughly 33% downside from current.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced. The macro variable driving the upside (elevated cracks) is the same variable whose reversal drives the downside, and the market's own forward estimates already model a ~33% earnings contraction into next year.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The business quality and balance sheet do not warrant an AVOID, but the peak-cycle earnings base, the one-time SRE flatter, and the two-sided Hormuz binary do not support a BUY at $77.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$50s (near recent buyback levels) that restores asymmetry against normalized earnings, OR
- Evidence that Hawaii Renewables plus retail/logistics generate a durably higher normalized earnings base (two consecutive quarters of positive renewables segment contribution), OR
- A favorable EPA 2025 SRE determination that de-risks the RIN inventory and confirms recurring earnings power above ~$10/share.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, sustained Hormuz reopening that compresses the Combined Index back toward mid-cycle with next-year EPS estimates cut below ~$8, OR
- A material adverse resolution of the Hawaii Clean Air Act Consent Decree or a labor stoppage, OR
- A third consecutive EPS miss on the August 4 print combined with negative estimate-revision velocity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release on August 4, 2026, alongside any EPA SRE ruling and confirmation of the Hormuz transit status.

## Conclusion

Par Pacific is a well-run, logistically-moated regional refiner with a strong balance sheet, genuinely aligned management (CEO actually-paid comp swung from $3.1M in the 2024 loss year to $13.8M in the 2025 recovery, tracking peer-beating TSR), value-accretive buybacks executed at an average $38.76 in Q1'26, and a real structural diversification catalyst in Hawaii Renewables. Those attributes keep it well clear of AVOID territory.

The reason we do not rate it a BUY at $77 is earnings quality and timing. The visually cheap ~8.5x multiple sits on earnings flattered by the one-time SRE benefit and a geopolitically-driven crack-spread spike, both of which the market's own forward estimates expect to fade (~33% earnings contraction into next year). The stock has doubled off its lows on macro tailwinds rather than durable operational improvement, negative operating cash flow in a "record" quarter underscores the noise, and two consecutive EPS misses show expectations have outrun delivery. The trajectory of our own view (declining scores across valuation, growth, and competitive position over recent reports) reflects this progressive re-rating of peak-cycle optimism, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it.

We would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$50s, on a favorable SRE ruling, or on demonstrated durable earnings from the renewables and non-refining segments. Until then, HOLD.
