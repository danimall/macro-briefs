**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SS&C Technologies is the world's largest independent fund administrator and the largest third-party mutual fund transfer agent, running a "technology-enabled services" model in which it owns the full software stack and uses it to deliver outsourced, mission-critical operations: NAV calculations, tax returns, regulatory filings, transfer agency, and claims adjudication. FY2025 revenue was $6,272M (+6.6% YoY), adjusted EBITDA margins run near 40%, client retention has exceeded 95% for five consecutive years, and the company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters. On the operating numbers, this is a high-quality, recurring-revenue compounder.

The investment question concerns the gap between those trailing numbers and the price. Shares have declined from ~$91 (August 2025 52-week high) to ~$68, roughly a 25% drawdown, during a period when the company beat Q1 2026 estimates ($1.69 adjusted vs. $1.68 consensus) and raised FY2026 guidance to $6.664–6.824B revenue and $6.74–7.06 adjusted EPS. At ~$68 against the midpoint of that guidance, the stock trades around 10x forward adjusted EPS, a steep discount for a business of this quality and growth profile. The five-year cumulative TSR of 128 versus a peer group at 222 confirms the de-rating is sustained, not a one-week dislocation.

A >25% drawdown on improving fundamentals demands a specific explanation of what sophisticated sellers are pricing, and there are two concrete candidates rather than generic "multiple compression." First, the $2.0B of 5.5% senior notes maturing September 2027 will almost certainly be refinanced at a higher coupon, mechanically pressuring the EPS bridge in a business already carrying ~$426M of TTM interest expense. Second, and more important to the terminal multiple, the bear case rests on AI disintermediation of the outsourced back-office labor that is SS&C's economic core, a thesis management is implicitly validating by claiming ~$200M/year of internal AI-driven cost savings. Neither overhang is resolved, both are visible in elevated put skew, and together they justify a HOLD with a constructive growth lean rather than a BUY, notwithstanding the optically cheap multiple.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1986 by William C. Stone (still Chairman and CEO), SS&C operates ~28,800 employees across 100+ offices in 35 countries serving 23,000+ clients. The differentiated model is that SS&C owns its proprietary software and uses it to run embedded operational workflows for clients, rather than licensing software or providing pure labor-based BPO. On the Q1 2026 call, management disclosed that only ~11% of the technology-enabled services line is actual software subscription; the balance is deeply embedded operational service tied to multi-year contracts. That embeddedness, combined with high switching costs in regulated workflows, is the moat.

Key franchises:
- **SS&C GlobeOp** — hedge fund and private-markets administration, >$2.9T alternative assets under administration, +$581B AUA since 2024.
- **GIDS (Global Investor & Distribution Solutions)** — global transfer agency and investor servicing; fastest-growing organic line (+10.4% in Q1 2026), expanding aggressively in Australia's $4T superannuation market (won Insignia, $321B assets).
- **SS&C Health** — pharmacy and medical claims (DomaniRx), 600M+ claims processed in 2025, positioned as a non-conflicted alternative to vertically integrated PBMs.
- **Blue Prism** — intelligent automation / agentic AI (2022 acquisition, $1.6B).
- **Intralinks** — virtual data rooms for M&A and capital markets.
- **Wealth/Advent** — Black Diamond, Geneva, Eze, Genesis.

Revenue diversification is genuine: the largest client is <5% of revenue, the top 10 are ~15%, and the geographic split is 67% U.S. / 33% international. The company reports as a single operating segment, which limits margin visibility by business line, a modest governance/transparency negative.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results:** Revenue $1,647M, +8.8% YoY (organic +5% constant-currency; +$34.9M acquisitions; +$22.4M FX). Technology-enabled services grew +10.8%; license/maintenance fell −1.7% as the legacy license book continues its secular decline. GAAP operating income was $398M (24.2% margin), net income $226M, GAAP diluted EPS $0.91, adjusted EPS $1.69 (+14%). Adjusted consolidated EBITDA was $651M at a 39.5% margin (+40bps YoY). Operating cash flow was $300M (+10%). Gross margin slipped slightly to 48.7% from 49.3%.

**Quality and trajectory:** Revenue is highly recurring with >95% retention for five straight years. FY2026 guidance implies ~5.3% organic at the midpoint and ~12% adjusted EPS growth, with a target of a 40% Q4 EBITDA margin. Management cited ~$7/share of cash generation in 2025. The four-quarter beat record (0.3%, 3.1%, 4.5%, 2.1%) supports confidence in consensus, though the magnitude of beats has narrowed over the last two quarters.

**Balance sheet — the headline tension.** Total debt is ~$7.43B: Term B-8 $3.86B (2031), Term A-9 $0.77B, 5.5% notes $2.0B (2027), 6.5% notes $0.75B (2032), plus an $80M revolver draw, at a weighted-average rate of ~5.6%. Goodwill of $9.93B plus intangibles of $3.96B equals ~$13.9B, or 68% of $20.3B total assets, the residue of 70 acquisitions since 1995 (DST $5.4B in 2018, Advent $2.6B, Eze, Intralinks, Blue Prism). Equity is $6.9B.

The Altman Z-Score reads in "distress" territory, but this is a false signal for a serial-acquirer, recurring-revenue model: it is driven by goodwill weighting and leverage, not operational stress. Actual net secured leverage is 1.69x against a 6.25x covenant; total net leverage is 2.76x per management; all covenants are in compliance. Interest coverage is comfortable (quarterly operating income $398M vs. ~$105M interest). Rate sensitivity is partially self-hedged: client fund balances (~$2.6B average) earn interest that offsets variable-rate debt cost; a 100bps move adds ~$12.8M of net income from client balances against ~$47M of added interest on variable debt, so SS&C is modestly net-short rates.

**Capital allocation.** Q1 returned $233M to shareholders (98% of allocated capital): $168M of buybacks (2.3M shares at $72.60 average) plus $65M of dividends ($0.27/quarter, raised from $0.25). A $1.5B authorization (May 2025) had ~$750M remaining at quarter-end. Management is explicitly prioritizing buybacks over debt paydown and absent accretive M&A, citing strengthened conviction at the depressed share price. Repurchasing at $72.60 with the stock now at $68 is roughly break-even to slightly underwater, but at ~10x forward earnings the buyback math is defensible.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is mid-single-digit (~5% constant-currency), with the headline closer to 9% on M&A and FX contributions. GIDS at +10.4% is the standout, driven by Australian superannuation wins. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year EPS revised +4.1% over 90 days to $6.89, and next-year +4.4% over 90 days to $7.50, with the next-year figure accelerating (+3.9% in the last 30 days alone). Rising estimates alongside a falling stock is the classic setup the bull case relies on.

Two qualifiers temper the growth score. First, the headline number leans on acquisitions (Calastone $1.03B in October 2025, Curo in November 2025, Battea $671M in 2024); the multiple depends on continued disciplined, accretive dealmaking, and the capital-allocation pivot toward buybacks suggests the near-term deal pipeline may be thinner. Second, the +30% estimate-revision threshold that flags genuine earnings inflection is not present here — revisions are a healthy +4% range, consistent with a steady compounder rather than a trough-to-recovery inflection. This is a durable mid-single-digit organic grower levered up to ~12% adjusted EPS growth via buybacks and bolt-ons.

Structural tailwinds are real: outsourcing and cloud adoption, regulatory complexity, alternatives/private-markets proliferation, AI-driven digital transformation, and asset tokenization (where Calastone positions SS&C on the rails). The GLP-1 healthcare opportunity (Humana as an SS&C client potentially administering a government program) is optionality, not a base-case driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$68 against FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, SS&C trades at roughly 10x forward adjusted EPS and ~22x trailing GAAP EPS of $3.15. For a business with >95% retention, ~40% EBITDA margins, and ~12% adjusted EPS growth, 10x forward is a meaningful discount to financial-technology and data-services peers. The 1.6% dividend yield provides modest support; total shareholder yield including buybacks is closer to 6–7%.

The valuation is genuinely attractive on current and forward fundamentals, which is why this dimension scores high (7.5). The discount is not explained by deteriorating numbers — the company beat and raised. It is explained by two forward concerns the market is discounting: the 2027 refinancing wall and AI-disruption fear on the terminal multiple. Both are legitimate, neither is quantified, and the market is choosing to apply a structural discount until they resolve. Analyst targets (low $78, median $92, high $115, mean ~$95) sit well above the current price and would imply roughly 15–70% upside if the company simply continues delivering, which makes those targets look like floors on a cheap, estimate-rising name rather than ceilings. The risk is that the targets are stale relative to a market that has decided the terminal growth rate is lower than analysts model.

On a sum-of-evidence basis, the multiple is the strongest part of the bull case. A re-rating from 10x to 13–14x forward EPS on $7.50 next-year EPS would put the stock near $98–105, broadly aligning with consensus, and requires only that the AI fear prove overblown and the refinancing pass without incident.

## Competitive Landscape

SS&C competes across fragmented verticals: alternative fund administration (vs. State Street, BNY, Northern Trust, CITCO), asset management/insurance software (SimCorp, Clearwater, FIS), wealth (Envestnet, Orion, Addepar, SEI), healthcare claims (PBM-owned vendors), intelligent automation (Microsoft, UiPath, Pega, Appian, and emerging agentic players), and VDRs (Datasite, DFIN). Its independence (no custody or prime-brokerage conflict) is a genuine selling point in fund administration, and its non-conflicted positioning in healthcare claims processing is differentiated against vertically integrated PBMs.

The competitive trajectory is stable to modestly improving in the core (GIDS share gains in Australia, GlobeOp AUA growth), but the moat faces a real long-term test from agentic AI. The embedded-workflow argument (that regulated NAV, tax, and filing processes are defensible) is sound for now, but the same automation SS&C deploys internally is precisely the technology that could compress the value of the outsourced labor that generates its revenue. Whether the firm captures that productivity as margin or competes it away as price is the unresolved structural question. The trajectory is more "stable with a tail risk" than "clearly widening moat."

## Risk Assessment

- **Refinancing/leverage (concrete, dated).** Refinancing the 2027 notes at higher rates would add to an already heavy interest burden, an earnings-bridge headwind. Leverage ratios are healthy, so this is an earnings-bridge risk, not a solvency risk.
- **AI disintermediation (the central debate, unquantified).** Bears worry agentic AI compresses the value of outsourced back-office labor. Management counters that only ~$100M of revenue is in "pointed software businesses" most exposed and that embedded regulated workflows are defensible. This is the dominant driver of the terminal-multiple discount and is hard to falsify in the near term.
- **Acquisition dependence and integration risk.** Headline growth leans on M&A; organic is ~5%. Goodwill in a single reporting unit creates impairment exposure in a sustained downturn.
- **Macro/market sensitivity.** Fees scale with AUM/AUA, transaction volumes, and claim counts. Equity drawdowns and M&A slowdowns (hitting Intralinks) flow through. Management flagged geopolitical and tariff-driven client hesitancy.
- **FX.** ~24% of revenue is non-USD (mainly GBP); Q1 saw a $72M negative FX translation hit to OCI.
- **Transparency.** Single-segment reporting limits margin visibility by business line.

The 10-Q states no material changes to 10-K risk factors. The risk score is 5.0 — below neutral — because the combination of a dated refinancing event and an unresolved, unquantifiable AI overhang represents specific downside that is being actively priced, not generic sector risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 30.9% versus 24.4% one-year historical volatility — rich by 6.6 points, though only the 5th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is +6.2% (elevated put demand) with a 2.00 put/call volume ratio, signaling defensive positioning into the near-term window. The term structure is in contango (30.9% near vs. 39.0% at six months), a normal/complacent shape rather than acute stress, but the rising longer-dated IV and the negative six-month skew (−3.2%) indicate the market sees the larger uncertainty further out, consistent with the 2027 refinancing and AI-terminal-value debates rather than an imminent binary. The implied ±5.3% move into the June expiry is modest. Net read: the options market is mildly defensive and confirms the fundamental overhangs are forward-dated, not immediate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$98–105).** A high-retention, ~40% EBITDA-margin compounder at 10x forward EPS is mispriced. Estimates are rising, the company beats consistently, and management is buying back stock aggressively at depressed levels with ~$750M of authorization remaining. If AI fears prove overblown and the 2027 refinancing passes without material EPS damage, the multiple re-rates toward 13–14x on $7.50 next-year EPS, delivering $98–105. Founder Stone's ~13% stake (~$2B+) aligns incentives unusually well, and the absence of panic insider selling supports the view that the de-rating is sentiment, not substance.

**Bear case (~$52–55).** The market is right that agentic AI structurally lowers the terminal growth rate of an outsourced-labor business, and the multiple compresses further toward 8x or stays there indefinitely as organic growth fades to low-single-digits. The 2027 refinancing resets coupons higher, the M&A pipeline thins (as the buyback pivot implies), and goodwill impairment becomes a live risk in a market drawdown. At 8x a flat ~$6.90 EPS, the stock is ~$55.

The asymmetry favors the bull case on the numbers, but the bear case is precisely the unfalsifiable terminal-value concern that has kept sophisticated capital on the sidelines and produced the drawdown. That is why this is a HOLD with a growth lean rather than a BUY: the upside is real but gated on resolution of two overhangs that cannot be checked off in the next quarter or two.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a confident neutral-with-upside, not an uncertain hold. The franchise quality and valuation are compelling; what prevents a BUY is concrete: (1) the September 2027 $2.0B 5.5%-notes maturity, which will likely reset to a higher coupon and pressure the EPS bridge, and (2) the unquantified AI-disintermediation risk to the terminal multiple, which the company's own ~$200M internal-savings claim implicitly validates and which the market is pricing via the five-year peer TSR gap and elevated put skew. Both are specific, dated or structurally identifiable, and falsifiable.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July) for organic-growth trajectory and any refinancing commentary; the 2027 refinancing itself is the decisive event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic constant-currency revenue growth re-accelerates above 6% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the embedded-workflow moat is holding against AI rather than eroding.
- The 2027 notes are refinanced (or a clear plan announced) at a coupon increase of <100bps versus the current 5.5%, removing the EPS-bridge overhang.
- Management provides segment-level disclosure or hard data showing AI is net-accretive to revenue and margin, not just internal cost savings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth decelerates below 3% for two consecutive quarters, signaling AI-driven displacement of the outsourced-services core.
- 90-day EPS revisions turn negative, reversing the current positive trend.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a refinancing forced at materially higher rates (>150bps coupon increase), confirming the bear case.

## Conclusion

SS&C is a genuinely high-quality, cash-generative compounder trading at a conspicuous discount: ~10x forward adjusted EPS on ~12% EPS growth, >95% retention, ~40% EBITDA margins, rising estimates, four straight beats, and a founder with a 13% stake leaning into buybacks. On the trailing and forward numbers alone, this would be a buy.

It is a HOLD because the ~25% drawdown reflects two specific forward concerns that current results do not address. The 2027 refinancing will mechanically pressure earnings at higher rates, and the AI-disintermediation question hangs over the terminal multiple of a business whose core is outsourced back-office labor that management itself is automating. Both are live and unresolved. The valuation provides a margin of safety and the franchise provides durability, so the risk/reward is favorable but gated. Existing holders should retain and let the buyback and rising estimates work; new capital can accumulate on weakness toward the low-$60s. The path to a BUY is checkable: organic growth holding above 6%, a benign refinancing, and evidence that AI is accretive rather than corrosive to the revenue base.
