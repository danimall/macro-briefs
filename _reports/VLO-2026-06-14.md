**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valero is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, and its earnings have inflected hard off the 2024–2025 cyclical trough. Q1 2026 net income was $1.26B ($4.22 EPS) versus a $595M loss ($1.90) a year earlier, a $2.6B swing in operating income, driven by wide distillate cracks, favorable crude differentials, and global product-supply tightness tied to Middle East and Russian disruptions. Consensus has chased this: current-year EPS estimates have been revised from $14.30 (90 days ago) to $29.66, a +107% move, with next-quarter estimates up a similar +102%. The company has beaten estimates in all four reported quarters of the recovery, twice by 30%+.

The complication is that this earnings power is a crack-spread phenomenon, not a structural growth story. Refining revenue is flat-to-declining (FY2025 revenue of $122.7B was below FY2024's $129.9B), and the entire estimate inflection reflects margin expansion that is, by construction, mean-reverting. The forward P/E of roughly 8.7x on the $29.66 current-year estimate looks cheap, but refiners always look cheapest at the top because the denominator is peak earnings. On normalized mid-cycle earnings (roughly $4–5B net income, between the $2.35B trough of 2025 and the $8.8–11.5B peaks of 2022–23), the stock trades closer to 17x at $258.67, broadly in line with the peer median P/E of 17.6x rather than at a discount.

The stock sits at $258.67, within ~3% of its 52-week high of $265.61 and up roughly 94% over the past year. The dominant near-term swing factor is the Iran/Hormuz situation: the disruption premium currently inflating cracks would compress on a verified, sustained chokepoint reopening, and the probability of de-escalation has risen following a strike cancellation and a draft U.S.–Iran memorandum of understanding. Options markets are pricing imminent stress: near-term IV is rich at 43.5% versus 34.9% realized, the term structure is in backwardation, and put skew is elevated. With a strong balance sheet, shareholder-friendly capital returns, and a durable Gulf Coast cost advantage offset by peak-cycle earnings, a 52-week-high price, and an asymmetric downside catalyst, the position warrants a HOLD. Existing holders have reason to retain; new capital lacks an attractive entry.

## Company Overview

Valero operates three segments. Refining, ~95% of revenue, comprises 15 refineries in the U.S., Canada (Quebec), and the U.K. (Pembroke) with ~3.2M BPD throughput capacity, heavily concentrated on the U.S. Gulf Coast (Port Arthur, Corpus Christi, St. Charles, Texas City, Houston). The Gulf Coast footprint is a structural advantage: high complexity, low feedstock cost, and export logistics into Latin America and Europe. Valero has no upstream production and no company-owned retail, which makes it a purer, higher-beta play on crack spreads than integrated peers Marathon Petroleum (MPC) and Phillips 66 (PSX).

The Renewable Diesel segment runs through Diamond Green Diesel (DGD), a 50/50 JV with Darling Ingredients that Valero consolidates, with ~1.2B gal/yr capacity and SAF optionality. Ethanol comprises 12 dry-mill plants (~1.7B gal/yr). The $6.0B invested in low-carbon fuels ($4.1B RD + $1.9B ethanol) is currently earning sub-par returns given policy whipsaw.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is a textbook cyclical trough-to-recovery.

| ($B) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 176.4 | 144.8 | 129.9 | 122.7 | 32.4 |
| Net income (to VLO) | 11.5 | 8.8 | 2.77 | 2.35 | 1.26 |
| Operating income | 15.7 | 11.9 | 3.76 | 3.18 | 1.73 |

FY2025 was the cyclical bottom: net income $2.35B, ROE 9.5%, net margin 1.8%, burdened by a $1.1B ($877M after-tax) California asset impairment. Stripping the impairment, adjusted operating income rose to $4.4B from $3.8B in 2024 as refining margins improved. Q1 2026 then swung sharply higher: refining segment margin of $3.9B (vs $2.5B), renewable diesel back to +$139M (from -$141M), ethanol +$90M. The mechanism was global product-supply tightness: USGC ULS diesel less Brent reached $27.60/bbl vs $16.69 a year earlier.

Cash generation and the balance sheet are solid. FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.8B and FCF $5.1B on modest capex (~$1.9B; 2026 guided to $1.7B). Liquidity stood at $10.8B at Q1 2026, the credit profile is investment grade (Baa2/BBB/BBB, all stable), Altman Z is 3.3, and the current ratio is 1.65. D/E of 2.44 is elevated but normal for a refiner at this rating. The LIFO inventory cushion (replacement cost exceeding carrying value by $11.2B at Q1 2026, up from $2.6B at YE2025) reflects the crude price spike and provides a notional reserve.

Capital allocation is shareholder-oriented and, in trough years, generous beyond earnings: $4.0B returned in 2025 ($2.6B buybacks + $1.4B dividends) and $4.3B in 2024, both exceeding net income, funded by balance-sheet capacity. Share count fell from 395M (2022) to ~298M (Q1 2026), a ~25% reduction. The dividend was raised to $1.20/quarter in 2026, and a new $2.5B buyback (~$3.7B total available) was authorized in February 2026. Returning more than net income in down years is a confidence signal but is sustainable only if the cycle cooperates.

## Growth Analysis

The headline estimate revisions are extraordinary: current-year EPS up +107.4% over 90 days to $29.66, next-quarter up +101.8%, all from a large analyst panel (16–18 estimates). Under a pure quantitative screen, +30% revisions plus rising estimates are among the strongest forward signals. The qualification here is material: this is a margin-driven commodity mark from a declining year-over-year refining revenue base, not a structural inflection. The earnings expansion is crack-spread expansion, and crack spreads are tied to OPEC+ output, sanctions enforcement, unplanned outages, and global refining capacity, none of which Valero controls and none of which persist indefinitely.

Two distinctions matter. First, the inflection is occurring at a cyclical peak, not a trough. Estimate-revision momentum is most durable when a stock is cheap relative to recovering estimates from a depressed base; it is least reliable when estimates are racing toward peak margins that the cycle will subsequently compress. Second, structural growth is limited. Refining demand faces secular pressure over the long term, the renewable diesel segment is hostage to volatile federal policy (the §45Z clean-fuel credit transition, RFS Set II RVO obligations, feedstock-origin sourcing rules), and the California exit reduces capacity by ~10%. The near-term earnings momentum is genuine, but the growth is cyclical rather than compounding.

## Valuation Assessment

| Forward P/E (CY est. $29.66) | ~8.7x | — |

On trailing earnings ($7.57, depressed by the 2025 trough and the CA impairment), the 18.8x multiple is roughly in line with the peer median of 17.6x (MPC 17.0x, PSX 17.6x, KMI 21.1x, SLB 24.7x). On the $29.66 current-year estimate, the forward P/E of ~8.7x appears cheap, and this is the crux of the bull case. The problem is that the $29.66 estimate captures peak-cycle margins; refiners routinely print single-digit forward multiples at cyclical tops precisely because the market discounts the unsustainability of peak earnings.

The cleaner lens is normalized mid-cycle earnings. With trough net income of $2.35B (2025) and peak of $8.8–11.5B (2022–23), a mid-cycle figure of roughly $4–5B implies ~$15/share normalized EPS on ~298M shares, or ~17x at $258.67 — fair, not cheap, and consistent with where peers trade. Analyst targets (mean $261, median $270, against the current $258.67) cluster near the current price; because the stock is at a cyclical peak rather than relative to recovering estimates, these targets reflect the same peak-margin optimism embedded in the price and should not be treated as a floor. The yield of 1.9% is below the peer median of 2.8% and provides limited downside support. The valuation is balanced at best and offers little asymmetric upside at a 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's competitive position is durable but not improving. The Gulf Coast concentration (high complexity, low feedstock cost, deepwater export access) is a genuine structural advantage that positions Valero well against West Coast and European refiners exiting the market, and the current environment of structural capacity reductions plus unplanned Russian/Middle East outages favors survivors with high utilization. DGD's feedstock flexibility (up to 100% waste feedstocks) is a differentiated margin lever when policy cooperates.

Offsetting factors keep the trajectory flat-to-slightly-negative. The California exit (Benicia idled April 2026, Wilmington under review) is strategically clarifying, removing a chronically hostile regulatory drag, but reduces ~10% of capacity and carries decommissioning, ARO ($337M), and litigation tails. The absence of upstream and retail diversification makes Valero a higher-beta play that amplifies both upside in tight markets and downside in gluts. The renewable diesel segment is earning sub-par returns on $6.0B invested. There is no evidence of share gains or moat widening; the position is stable, anchored by the Gulf Coast advantage, with long-term refining demand the open question.

## Risk Assessment

**Crack-spread / cycle risk (dominant).** Earnings swing roughly 5x peak-to-trough. The current estimate run-up is a margin mark, and any normalization of global product-supply tightness reverses it. This is the sector's permanently-priced risk, but its magnitude at the current peak is the key consideration.

**Hormuz reopening — the asymmetric near-term catalyst.** A material share of present crack strength is a disruption premium. De-escalation probability has risen amid oil at a three-month low. A verified, sustained chokepoint reopening would compress Gulf Coast cracks and pull the $29.66 current-year estimate downward. The signal remains below the threshold of sustained physical verification, and prior de-escalation signals have repeatedly failed, so the failure tail (and elevated cracks) also persists. The asymmetry, with the stock near its high on peak margins, leans against new entry.

**Port Arthur fire (March 2026).** A distillate hydrotreater fire forced a full shutdown, with Q2 2026 Gulf Coast throughput guided down to 1.69–1.74M BPD. Repair capex is largely insured subject to retention; class-action and personal-injury/property suits have been filed.

**Renewable diesel policy whipsaw.** DGD's 2025 operating loss stemmed from the §45Z credit transition and feedstock tariffs. Partial relief came from the SCOTUS invalidation of IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2026; $51M refund claim) and the OBBB §45Z extension through 2029, but RFS Set II rules and feedstock-origin requirements remain two-sided. RVO compliance cost rose from $5.85/bbl in 2025 to $9.41 in Q1 2026 ($653M cash cost in the quarter).

**California and climate litigation tails.** Decommissioning costs, the federal LCFS antitrust class action, the Oregon climate tort, and NY/VT "climate superfund" strict-liability laws.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 43.5% versus 34.9% one-year realized (rich by 8.5 points), the term structure is in backwardation (1-week 43.5% > 3-month 39.5%), and put skew is elevated at 3.3% on the front expiry. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.5%. Put/call OI is light at 0.27, so this is more an event-hedging configuration than persistent bearish positioning, consistent with binary risk around the June 16–17 FOMC and the Hormuz resolution. The backwardation argues against new exposure ahead of these events; it does not signal durable structural stress.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Global refining tightness is structural, beyond a Hormuz artifact: U.S. and European capacity reductions, slow emerging-market ramp-ups, and resilient diesel/jet demand support high utilization and margins for low-cost survivors well beyond the current geopolitical premium. The Gulf Coast footprint and export logistics let Valero capture this. If mid-cycle margins reset higher than the prior decade, the ~8.7x forward multiple is too cheap, the $3.7B buyback shrinks the share count further, and the stock works toward the $300 high analyst target as estimates continue to rise. The four-quarter beat streak (twice >30%) supports confidence in near-term numbers.

### Bear Case
The estimate inflection is a peak-cycle crack-spread mark that mean-reverts. A verified Hormuz reopening compresses Gulf cracks, RFS compliance costs keep rising, the Port Arthur outage dents Q2 volumes, and renewable diesel stays policy-impaired. The forward multiple expands as the "E" falls, exposing the stock as fairly valued at ~17x mid-cycle. At a 52-week high with a sub-peer 1.9% yield providing little support, the downside toward the $181 low analyst target and the prior trading range (low-$200s) is real, roughly 15–30% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The thesis is genuinely two-sided: a strong balance sheet and durable Gulf Coast franchise on one side, peak-cycle earnings, a 52-week-high price, and an asymmetric de-escalation catalyst on the other. This is a balanced risk/reward where existing holders are reasonably compensated to wait and new capital lacks an edge.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$200s that restores a margin of safety on mid-cycle earnings while crack spreads and estimates hold.
- Evidence that elevated refining margins are structural rather than disruption-driven — sustained crack strength after a confirmed, held Hormuz reopening — alongside continued positive estimate revisions.
- Resolution of the front-end vol/backwardation (event risk clearing) at a more favorable price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, sustained Hormuz reopening that compresses Gulf cracks and begins pulling consensus EPS estimates down.
- A guidance cut or a meaningfully extended Port Arthur outage with uninsured cost.
- Capital returns continuing to exceed FCF while leverage climbs above current levels in a deteriorating margin environment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the June 16–17 FOMC (rate/long-end implications for the broader complex), the evolution of the Hormuz situation, and the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will quantify the Port Arthur volume hit and whether peak margins held). At the current price of $258.67, base-case fair value sits near current levels (~$255–270, mid-cycle ~17x), the bull case is ~$300 (+16%), and the bear case is ~$200–215 (-17% to -23%).

## Conclusion

Valero is a high-quality refiner with a genuine earnings inflection, a strong balance sheet by refiner standards, and disciplined capital returns. The +107% current-year estimate revision is real but is a crack-spread mark layered onto cyclical-peak margins, not a structural growth event, and the stock is being valued at a forward multiple that flatters peak earnings. At $258.67, within 3% of its 52-week high and roughly fairly valued on normalized earnings, the risk/reward is balanced: the most probable near-term catalyst, a verified Hormuz reopening whose probability has risen, works against the position by compressing the very margins driving the estimate run-up.

This is a HOLD. Existing holders are adequately compensated to retain through the cycle and capture the buyback and dividend; new money should wait for either a pullback that restores a margin of safety on mid-cycle earnings or confirmation that elevated margins are structural.
