## Executive Summary

Petrobras printed one of the three best quarters in its history — R$52.4bn of reported net income, up 96% year over year, roughly $20bn of adjusted EBITDA excluding one-offs, a record $19.5bn gross profit, and $7.7bn of free cash flow. On per-ADS trailing earnings of about $3.00, the stock trades at 5.99x. Enterprise value of roughly $175.3bn is 5.35x trailing operating income. Trailing free cash flow of $20.1bn is a 17.5% yield on the $114.9bn market capitalization; on a capex-normalized basis the yield is closer to 13.9%. Against integrated-major peer multiples, this is one of the widest discounts in large-cap energy.

The discount has specific, current causes. Reuters attributed the Q2 profit surge directly to the Brent spike from the U.S.–Iran conflict, and Q2 alone accounted for roughly 54% of trailing-twelve-month net income, so the cheap trailing multiple is measured against a peak quarter that management itself expects to fade toward strategic-plan assumptions. Brazil imposed a 12% tax on crude oil exports in March 2026 as prices rose, extended it for a further 60 days on July 9 by decision of the Gecex trade committee, and set a 30-day review. The Q2 charge was reportedly $965M. This mechanism switches on when oil prices rise and taxes the growth vector — exports were up 12% in the quarter, increasingly to China. Beyond that is an October 2026 general election in which Bloomberg reported Lula's lead over Flávio Bolsonaro narrowing, in a country where the historical mechanism of value destruction at Petrobras has been domestic fuel priced below import parity.

Operationally the company is executing well. Oil production reached 2.7 mmbbl/d (over 3.0 mmboe/d with gas), roughly 200 kbbl/d above the internal Q2 target; the Almirante Tamandaré FPSO at Búzios peaked at 270 kbbl/d against a 225 kbbl/d design, with six other units adapted above nameplate; management quantified about 270 kbbl/d of remaining 2H ramp headroom and said the annual field decline rate has fallen to roughly 4% from about 12%. Refinery utilization hit 101.2% with a 68% high-value yield, imports down 40% and exports up 12%. That combination of volume growth without incremental platform capex and structural diesel import substitution is the bull case, and it is real. It does not resolve the two binaries that land within ten weeks, and net debt rose from $47.1bn to $60.6bn across FY2023–FY2025 while cash fell from $15.5bn to $9.2bn. HOLD, owned primarily for the distribution.

## Company Overview

Petrobras is Brazil's state-controlled integrated oil and gas company, founded 1953, operating exploration and production, refining and transportation, gas and low-carbon energy, and domestic fuel marketing. The federal government controls the company through a majority of voting common shares (the exact percentage was not verified for this report). PBR (ADS on common) and PBR.A (ADS on preferred) both trade in New York; **one ADS represents two common shares**, a detail that distorts most published metrics for this issuer.

FY2025 segment gross revenue was $59.5bn in E&P, $84.2bn in refining/transport/marketing and $8.7bn in gas and low carbon, against $63.5bn of eliminations, for $89.2bn consolidated. The elimination line at 71% of segment gross revenue reflects the same barrel being counted upstream and again downstream; consolidated revenue is approximately the external sales figure. The economics sit in pre-salt upstream. The refining system is the domestic monetization channel and, historically, a policy instrument, because Petrobras is effectively the price-setter for Brazilian diesel and gasoline.

The units issue matters for the data feed. The quoted trailing P/E of 11.14x on EPS of $1.56 mixes a per-ADS price with what appears to be a per-common-share earnings figure; the forward P/E field is identical to the trailing field, so it carries no independent forward information. Reconciling from net income — $114.9bn market capitalization divided by $19,335M trailing net income — gives 5.94x, and $17.96 divided by $3.00 per-ADS trailing EPS gives 5.99x. Reported Q2 GAAP EPS of $0.81 against a $1.40 "consensus," widely relayed as a 42% miss, resolves as a units artifact: $10.44bn of Q2 net income divided by $0.81 implies 12.89bn shares, the common-share count, while per-ADS metrics use roughly 6.40bn units. Converted to per-ADS, Q2 was approximately $1.62, a beat rather than a miss. The accompanying $160.39bn "revenue estimate" exceeds the company's entire trailing-twelve-month revenue of $104.2bn, which corroborates that the comparison set is mis-scaled. Neither the beat nor the miss should be used as evidence; what is reliable is that reported net income roughly doubled year over year.

The FY2024 collapse in net income was not an operating event. Operating margin was 29.4% in FY2024 and 29.3% in FY2025 — flat. But applying the disclosed pretax margins to revenue gives FY2024 pretax of roughly $11.1bn against $26.9bn of operating income, a drag of about $15.7bn below the operating line, versus a small positive contribution in FY2025. The year-over-year swing in below-operating-line items was therefore on the order of $16bn, larger than FY2025 net income itself. The most plausible dominant driver is FX translation on USD-denominated debt against a BRL functional currency, plus interest; some portion may be impairments or provisions, and the composition could not be verified from the annual report. The magnitude is arithmetic; the attribution is inference. Any net-income-based screen on PBR — P/E, ROE trend, accrual quality — will misfire in years when the real depreciates. Operating margin, cash flow and leverage are the reliable lenses.

**Cash flow.** Trailing operating cash flow was $40,667M and capex $20,547M, giving trailing free cash flow of $20,120M, a figure that reconciles arithmetically. The vendor's annual FCF series is unusable: FY2021, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 "FCF" equals operating cash flow because capex is absent, and the resulting "FCF margins" of 40%+ should be discarded. Normalizing instead: 1H 2026 capex was about $10.4bn ($5.1bn plus $5.3bn), implying roughly $10.15bn in 2H 2025 and an FY2025 run-rate near $20bn. Against FY2025 operating cash flow of $36,047M, normalized FY2025 free cash flow was approximately $16bn. That is 13.9% of the current market capitalization. The trailing 17.5% figure embeds the Brent-spike quarter.

**Q2 2026 detail.** Revenue $33.61bn (+43% q/q, roughly +60% y/y); net income excluding one-offs about $11.1bn; adjusted EBITDA ex-one-offs about $20bn (+70% q/q, near double y/y); free cash flow about $7.7bn; operating cash flow about $12.3bn; capex $5.3bn with 82% of 1H investment in E&P; ROCE 9.3%. Drilling activity was up 40% q/q, completions 45%, interconnections 43%. Part of the revenue jump reflects recognition of cargoes previously in transit, a timing effect the company cited. Do not annualize: trailing net income of $19,335M less Q2's $10,440M leaves about $8,895M across the other three quarters.

Net debt rose about $13.5bn, or 28%, from year-end 2023 to year-end 2025, through a period of $36-43bn annual operating cash flow. Cash fell by a third from FY2023 to June 2026. Q2 2026 stabilized rather than reversed the trend: net debt was essentially flat quarter over quarter, with $2.9bn of loans and financings repaid (including $1.4bn of bank transactions and $700M of bond repurchases/redemptions) against roughly $600M raised. Net debt to trailing operating income is 1.84x, which is manageable for an integrated producer. On a peak-quarter annualized EBITDA basis it would be under 1x, but that is a sensitivity rather than a normalized ratio. No covenant language, maturity ladder, company-stated debt ceiling, or DD&A disclosure was obtainable, so refinancing risk on $70.8bn of gross debt is unassessed. That gap is material and caps the financial-health score.

**Distributions.** Q2 shareholder remuneration of R$17.4bn, at the implied R$5.02/USD from the company's own figures, is approximately $3.47bn, or about $0.54 per ADS. Annualized that is roughly $2.17 per ADS, a 12.1% yield at $17.96 — a Brent-spike sensitivity, not a base case. The trailing declared yield is 6.24%. The coverage test is the point: a $13.9bn annual distribution run-rate would absorb about 87% of normalized $16bn free cash flow, leaving nothing for $60.4bn of net debt. That is why the CFO said extraordinary dividends appear unlikely, with capital allocation prioritizing high-return projects and debt reduction. Management renegotiated recharter and well-service contracts for more than $1bn of cash savings over 2026–2030 (currency label ambiguous in the source). The vendor's historical dividend-per-share series appears BRL-denominated under a USD header and was not used.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is the durable part of the story and it is running ahead of plan.

- **Production:** 2.7 mmbbl/d of oil in Q2, over 3.0 mmboe/d including gas, up 15% y/y and roughly 200 kbbl/d above the internal 2.5 mmbbl/d quarterly target. The CEO told analysts on August 7 that the 2026 production forecast will "very likely" be exceeded.
- **Ramp headroom without capex:** P-79 (Búzios) started in May 2026 against a 180 kbbl/d nameplate; P-78 was running at 120 kbbl/d and Alexandre de Gusmão (Mero) at 100 kbbl/d, both against 180 kbbl/d nameplates. Management quantified about 270 kbbl/d of remaining 2H ramp potential. Almirante Tamandaré peaked at 270 kbbl/d against a 225 kbbl/d design, with six other units adapted above original capacity. Incremental barrels arriving from existing hulls is the highest-return growth available to any producer.
- **Decline rate:** management states the annual decline rate fell to about 4% from about 12%, attributed to 4D seismic, intelligent completions, water injection and infill wells. This is a management claim not traceable to a filing, but it is consistent with the observed volume trajectory.
- **Downstream:** record 101.2% utilization (about 102% in April and May), oil-products output +5.6% q/q, 68% high-value yield sustained at higher throughput, imports down 40% and exports up 12%. Diesel import substitution captures the import-parity spread internally and is independent of Brent direction.
- **New FPSOs:** P-80 and P-82 complete in Q3 2026 with production planned for Q2 2027 (P-80 possibly pulled to Q1); P-83 completes early Q1 2027 with production in 2H 2027.
- **Exploration optionality:** the equatorial-margin well had roughly 500 metres to the reservoir with results expected by end-August 2026, with authorization sought for three contingent wells; a gas discovery was announced in Colombia; exploration partnerships exist with TotalEnergies in South Africa and involving Shell in Namibia. These are partnerships and joint arrangements, not acquisitions, with no working-interest detail available.

Against that, earnings growth from here is largely a price question, and consensus is drifting lower. Current-quarter EPS estimates are down 9.0% over 90 days (flat over 30 days), next-year estimates down 3.1% over 30 days, on thin coverage of 1-5 analysts per period and with unit bases that are not internally consistent across the feed. There is no positive estimate-revision inflection signal here; the revision trend is mildly negative, consistent with management's own expectation that Brent reverts toward strategic-plan assumptions. Reserves, reserve life and reserve-replacement ratio were not obtainable, which is a genuine omission for an E&P-weighted valuation.

## Competitive Landscape

On operational execution Petrobras is currently a positive outlier within the majors. Pre-salt Búzios and Mero deliver low-cost barrels at scale; running seven FPSOs above nameplate design and cutting field decline from about 12% to about 4% is better capital efficiency than the industry norm, because the incremental barrels arrive without incremental platform capex. Domestically, Petrobras is the price-setter and the dominant refiner; record utilization at a 68% high-value yield with imports down 40% converts what used to be an import bill into internal margin. Growing crude flows to China give Brazilian medium/heavy pre-salt grades an expanding outlet.

The offsets are structural. The controlling shareholder both sets the policy that determines realized prices and taxes and receives the largest share of distributions, a permanent divergence of interest from minorities. Petrochemical spreads remain weak, which is why the Braskem stake is a problem asset rather than a valuable one; Petrobras became co-controller with IG4's Shine I when that transaction closed June 3-4, 2026, and the CEO's description of having "gained greater political influence through a new shareholders agreement" cuts both ways for a leveraged business. Brazil's simultaneous role as environmental-policy leader, owner of an expanding deepwater producer and beneficiary of hydrocarbon tax revenue produces the licensing friction visible at Foz do Amazonas: a roughly 15 m³ synthetic drilling-fluid leak halted the Morpho well on January 6, 2026, with ANP re-authorization only on February 4. Reported 25% U.S. tariffs on Brazilian products add trade friction of unverified scope for crude and products.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Export tax, active and renewable by decree.** The 12% crude export tax cost a reported $965M in Q2 (single secondary source, unconfirmed against the 6-K). The renewal window runs into roughly early September. Because renewal requires no legislation, it cannot be modeled as a one-off. It is also asymmetric: the tax appeared because prices rose, meaning the state captures a share of oil upside while downside accrues to shareholders.

**2. October 2026 general election.** Bloomberg reported on August 5 that Lula's lead over Flávio Bolsonaro narrowed as campaigning began, with reported 25% U.S. tariffs complicating the challenger's position. The historical value-destruction mechanism at Petrobras is domestic fuel priced below import parity. I could not verify current pricing versus parity in either direction — record utilization tells you about throughput, not realized price — so this must be carried as an open, thesis-relevant gap rather than a resolved comfort.

**3. Fiscal dependence.** Petrobras disclosed roughly R$88.6bn of taxes paid/remitted in Q2 versus R$17.4bn of shareholder remuneration. That figure includes consumption taxes collected from customers and is not a corporate income-tax charge, so it should not be compared to net income; what it establishes is the scale of state revenue dependence on one company, which is why the export tax is unlikely to be abandoned casually.

**4. Equatorial-margin binary within weeks.** Results were expected by end-August 2026, plus three contingent wells awaiting authorization. A commercial discovery extends reserve life and validates the exploration narrative; a dry hole removes an unpriced option. The regulator has already demonstrated willingness to suspend operations in this basin over a small incident, during an election year.

**5. Leverage and cash buffer.** Gross debt is $70.8bn with no covenant, maturity-ladder or debt-ceiling visibility. USD debt against BRL cash flows is the same exposure that produced the FY2024 below-the-line drag.

**6. Braskem contingency.** The CFO flagged discussions as sensitive ahead of Braskem's August 13 earnings and an injunction expiring October 24. The subject matter, parties and financial exposure of that injunction are unknown, so the contingency cannot be bounded. Co-control brings governance responsibility for a leveraged petrochemical business in a weak-spread market.

**7. Deferred turnarounds.** REGAP and REPLAN downtime was postponed to early 2027 in connection with expansion projects and reliability assessments, with a Cubatão shutdown in August and catalytic-cracking and REPAR work in 2H 2026. Whatever the engineering rationale, the mechanical effect is that 2026 throughput is not a clean run-rate for 2027.

**8. Asset concentration.** Growth is overwhelmingly Búzios and Mero; slippage in a handful of FPSOs moves the whole production profile.

**9. Oil price direction.** The Q2 result was explicitly attributed to the conflict-driven Brent spike. Management expects Brent to revert toward the levels assumed in its 2025–2030 strategic plan, and a normalization of Gulf transit would compress realizations. [unverified — no OPEC+ supply action is documented in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Disclosure limitation to weigh explicitly:** as a foreign private issuer, Petrobras files 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K/DEF 14A. The FY2025 20-F (filed April 8, 2026) text could not be retrieved for this report, so there is no verified risk-factor, legal-proceedings or footnote content, no covenant language, no reserves data, no dividend-policy formula and no multi-year capital plan. Officers and directors of foreign private issuers are exempt from Section 16 reporting, so no insider-transaction signal exists in either direction. This constrains confidence and is part of why the recommendation is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($26.00, +44.8%).** Brent stays elevated on unresolved Gulf disruption while the export tax lapses at a review rather than being renewed, removing roughly $3.5-4bn of annual leakage. The 270 kbbl/d of 2H ramp headroom converts, production exceeds guidance as the CEO indicated, and P-80/P-82 completion in Q3 keeps the 2027 profile intact. Refining continues to substitute imports at 100%-plus utilization. The equatorial-margin well is commercial, restoring a reserve-replacement narrative that the market currently assigns no value. The election resolves without a change to fuel-pricing practice, and net debt begins falling as free cash flow exceeds distributions plus capex. On $3.80-4.00 of per-ADS earnings power and a re-rating to 6.5-7x with the political discount narrowing, $26 is reachable. Distributions in that state comfortably exceed 10%.

**Bear case ($13.00, −27.6%).** Gulf transit normalizes, Brent reverts toward the low $70s or below as supply returns, and the earnings base halves from the Q2 run-rate. The export tax is renewed indefinitely or widened. The election produces renewed pressure to hold diesel and gasoline below import parity, compressing downstream margins in the segment that generated the record gross profit. Distributions are cut to protect a balance sheet where net debt has already grown 28% in two years with no covenant visibility, and the multiple compresses toward the 4-5x the market has assigned in prior interventionist periods. A dry hole at the equatorial margin and an adverse Braskem outcome remove the option value and add a contingency. $13 is roughly 4x normalized earnings and near the 52-week low of $11.43.

**Base case ($21.00, +16.9%).** Brent settles between the current spike and strategic-plan assumptions. Production growth delivers roughly as guided; refining normalizes off 101% utilization as 2027 turnarounds arrive. The export tax persists in some form and is absorbed. Distributions run at 60-75% of a normalized $16bn free-cash-flow base, giving a 7-9% yield, with modest net-debt reduction. The multiple stays in the 6-6.5x range because the governance discount does not disappear. Total return in the mid-teens including the distribution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** The reason to own PBR is the distribution stream and a genuinely cheap claim on normalized cash flow; the reason not to add at $17.96 is that two of the largest value drivers — the export-tax regime and the fuel-pricing posture after the October election — resolve within ten weeks and are outside the company's control.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Export tax lapses or is formally terminated** at a Gecex review, removing roughly $3.5-4bn of annualized leakage and re-opening the export growth vector. This alone is worth 1.0-1.5 turns of multiple.
2. **Post-election clarity on domestic pricing** — explicit confirmation that diesel and gasoline are being set at or above import parity, verifiable in a quarterly release, plus refining margin sustained above the FY2025 level after the 2027 turnarounds are absorbed.
3. **Net debt declines two consecutive quarters** below $58bn while distributions are maintained, demonstrating that deleveraging and the payout are not mutually exclusive at mid-cycle Brent. Alternatively, a price near **$14.50-15.50** would restore enough margin of safety to accumulate on the current fundamentals.
4. **A commercial equatorial-margin result** with licensing granted for the three contingent wells.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Any reintroduction of below-parity domestic fuel pricing**, or a formal change to the shareholder-remuneration policy that reduces the distribution outside a Brent-driven formula adjustment.
2. **Net debt above $65bn** or cash below $8bn, which would signal that the balance sheet is absorbing the cost of maintaining the payout at declining prices.
3. **Export tax raised above 12% or extended to refined products**, which would convert a cyclical charge into a structural repricing of the export franchise.
4. **A Braskem cash call, consolidation, or adverse October 24 injunction outcome** that puts a quantified liability on Petrobras.

**Reassessment timeline:** three near-dated checkpoints — the equatorial-margin well result expected by end-August; the export-tax review in early September; and the Braskem injunction expiry on October 24, immediately ahead of the October general election. Full reassessment at the Q3 2026 release, which will show whether the export-tax charge recurs and whether the Q2 earnings level was a spike or a new base. Given that near-dated implied volatility sits in the 18th percentile of the chain, holders who cannot tolerate the election outcome can hedge the September–December window more cheaply than they can exit and re-enter.

## Conclusion

Petrobras is operationally the strongest it has been in a decade and financially cheaper than any integrated peer of comparable scale: 5.99x trailing per-ADS earnings, 5.35x EV to trailing operating income, and a 13.9% free-cash-flow yield on capex-normalized numbers. Volume growth is arriving through existing hulls at negligible incremental capex, decline rates are improving, and diesel import substitution is a structural margin gain independent of oil prices.

The reasons the market applies the discount are active rather than historical. The 12% crude export tax, renewable by executive decree every 60 days, taxes the fastest-growing part of the business. A general election in October, with narrowing polls, sits directly on top of the mechanism that has destroyed value at this company before. Q2 earnings were driven by a conflict-related oil spike that management itself expects to reverse. Net debt is 28% higher than two years ago and the distribution would absorb 87% of normalized free cash flow at the Q2 run-rate, which is why the CFO has already signalled that extraordinary dividends are unlikely. Add the absence of verified covenant, maturity, reserves and dividend-policy disclosure, and expected return of roughly +8% on price plus a 6-9% distribution is not enough compensation to add capital ahead of the calendar.

Existing holders should stay for the yield and the production ramp. New capital should wait for the export-tax review, the election, or a price near $14.50-15.50.
