**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Mirion Technologies is a global supplier of radiation detection, measurement, and monitoring products across nuclear power, medical (radiation therapy QA and dosimetry), defense, and scientific-research end markets. The core investment attraction is a structural moat: the company states its products are installed at the vast majority of addressable active nuclear power reactors worldwide, creating a razor/razor-blade service and replacement franchise over 40-to-100-year reactor operating lives, reinforced by heavy regulatory certification barriers. This installed base positions Mirion as a picks-and-shovels beneficiary of the nuclear revival driven by data-center power demand and the small modular reactor (SMR) buildout, with the December 2025 Paragon acquisition ($588M) deepening its US and SMR exposure.

The headline growth understates how acquisition-dependent the business is. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 27.5% year-over-year to $257.6M, but roughly $42.2M of the ~$52M Nuclear & Safety increase came from the Paragon and Certrec acquisitions; organic volume growth contributed only about $1.8M — low single digits. GAAP operating income fell to $3.7M from $8.7M despite the higher revenue, weighed down by $8.4M of acquisition-related intangible amortization, and the company posted a net loss of $(3.4)M. Adjusted EBITDA grew 16% to $54.3M, so the underlying cash-generative business is healthier than the reported loss, but the gap between the two is a direct consequence of the roll-up model, which has also left goodwill and intangibles at $2.44B, or 69% of total assets.

The stock has fallen roughly 15% since our May reassessment and sits near its 52-week low of $15.58, versus the prior report price near $18. Trailing P/E of ~178x and forward P/E at a similar level are not meaningful given the amortization-driven GAAP compression; the more useful anchors are ~21% Adjusted EBITDA margins, positive free cash flow, and a materially de-risked balance sheet after 2025's refinancing. We maintain a HOLD. The two central tensions — acquired-not-organic growth and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet with impairment risk — cap conviction until organic acceleration or synergy realization becomes visible. Our score has improved from 5.2 (March) to 6.3 (May) and now settles at 5.4 as the modest negative estimate revisions and continued organic softness temper the balance-sheet improvement.

## Company Overview

Mirion was formed through a 2021 SPAC merger with GS Acquisition Holdings Corp II and is headquartered in Atlanta. It operates across the US, Canada, UK, France, Germany, Finland, China, Belgium, the Netherlands, Estonia, Japan, and South Korea, with roughly 32% of Q1 revenue generated outside North America (37% for FY2025).

The business splits into two reportable segments:

- **Nuclear & Safety (~72% of revenue, $185.5M in Q1)** — radiation detection and analysis across the full nuclear power plant lifecycle (new build, operation, decommissioning), personal radiation detection for defense and civil safety, and labs/research instrumentation. Within the segment: nuclear power $125.8M, labs & research $28.3M, other/defense $31.4M.
- **Medical (~28% of revenue, $72.1M in Q1)** — radiation therapy quality assurance (the core product), nuclear medicine and radionuclide therapy handling, and personal dosimetry for medical staff. Cancer care is the primary driver at $55.3M.

Despite the "Information Technology / Electronic Equipment & Instruments" sector label, Mirion is more accurately a specialized industrial-instruments company; management itself benchmarks compensation against the Russell 2000 Industrials index. The distinction matters for valuation and for interpreting sector-level selection signals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Annual revenue has moved from roughly $800M to $860M to $925M over the last three fiscal years — steady high-single-digit growth, but accelerating primarily through acquisition. The mechanical decomposition of the Q1 revenue growth is the key insight: of the ~$52M Nuclear & Safety dollar increase, ~$42.2M was acquired (Paragon plus Certrec), FX added $6.4M, price added $1.7M, and organic volume added only ~$1.8M.

**Profitability.** Gross margin compressed to 46.2% from 47.6% on unfavorable mix and lower-margin acquired revenue. GAAP operating income was driven down by $8.4M of purchase-accounting amortization, higher compensation, and M&A expense. Adjusted EBITDA (~21% margin) is management's preferred metric and reflects the cash-generative core once the ~$31M/quarter of amortization is stripped. Whether to underwrite the adjusted picture or the GAAP loss is the central analytical fork; the amortization is genuinely non-cash, but a business whose entire GAAP profitability is consumed by the amortization of prior deals carries real reinvestment and impairment obligations, not accounting artifacts to be waved away.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Cash rose to $397.9M from $175.6M a year earlier, funded by 2025 equity and convertible raises. Total debt is ~$1.23B: a $450M term loan (SOFR+2.00%, ~5.67%, due 2032) plus $775M of convertible notes ($400M at 0.25% due 2030, conversion $23.11; $375M at 0.00% due 2031, conversion $28.82). Both converts are struck 35–68% above the current $17.38 price, and capped calls raise effective cap prices to $34.88 and $42.70, so near-term dilution risk is contained. The defining balance-sheet feature is $1.86B goodwill plus $576M intangibles, equal to ~69% of $3.54B total assets; tangible book is deeply negative and $211.8M of goodwill has already been impaired historically. The company is compliant with covenants — the 7.0x First Lien Net Leverage covenant only springs if the revolver is more than 40% drawn, and the revolver is undrawn.

**Cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow fell to $18.9M from $35.6M on working-capital timing (AR billing), with modest capex of ~$9.2M. Q1 is seasonally weak, and the business is FCF-positive over the year, but reported earnings are heavily masked by $40.8M/quarter of D&A.

**Capital allocation.** A $100M buyback authorized in December 2024 has seen ~1.9M shares / $34.6M repurchased through Q1, with ~$65.4M remaining. The company is simultaneously repurchasing stock, issuing converts, and making large acquisitions — a mixed signal that reflects the roll-up model rather than a coherent return-of-capital philosophy.

2025's refinancing cut the term loan by $244.6M and dropped Q1 net interest expense to $5.0M from $10.6M. Leverage cost has fallen materially, which supports a higher financial-health score than the prior GAAP-loss and negative-tangible-book optics would suggest. That improvement is the reason financial health has risen from 4.5 (March) to 5.5, though the goodwill concentration and impairment history keep it from crossing into strong territory.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers that must be kept separate. The reported top line reads like a growth stock; the organic engine (low single digits) does not. This is the credibility gap the market is pricing, and it is confirmed by the company's own 2026 PSU design, which specifically rewards cumulative organic revenue growth (35% weight) alongside adjusted EBITDA margin and adjusted FCF — the Board is signaling it needs to prove organic capability.

Estimate revisions have turned modestly negative. Current-quarter EPS consensus has drifted from $0.12 (90 days ago) to $0.10 (current), a -15.5% revision, while current-year ($0.54) and next-year ($0.65) estimates are essentially flat to slightly down (-1.6% and -2.2% over 90 days). This does not meet the +30% inflection threshold that would flag a strong buy signal; it is mild deterioration in near-term visibility against a stable full-year picture. The earnings track record is reasonable (3 beats, 1 miss over the last four quarters, including a +21.2% beat in September 2025), which lends some confidence to consensus but not enough to override the flat-to-down revision trend.

The secular tailwind is real. Nuclear demand is being pulled forward by data-center and AI power needs, government acceptance of nuclear as clean domestic baseload is rising, and the SMR commercialization wave gives Mirion a genuine (if early and indirect) growth vector via Paragon. Medical growth rests on aging demographics, rising cancer incidence, and low RT QA penetration in emerging markets. These are durable multi-decade drivers. The caution is timing: SMRs remain largely pre-commercial, and the near-term organic number has not yet inflected. Mirion monetizes the theme through instrumentation sold into reactors — it supplies the buildout rather than being an AI or reactor company, and its new internal "AI function" (~$0.8M of Q1 cost) is an operational tool, not a product line.

We score growth prospects at 5.5 — the theme is credible and durable, but the organic engine has not yet demonstrated the acceleration that would justify a higher mark, and revisions are pointing gently down.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E both read ~178x, which is uninformative because GAAP earnings are compressed by acquisition amortization near zero. On the cleaner metric, the ~$4.2B market cap against $54.3M quarterly Adjusted EBITDA (annualizing toward ~$220M+) implies an EV/EBITDA in the mid-to-high teens once the ~$1.23B debt and ~$398M cash are netted — a full multiple for a business growing organically in the low single digits.

Against the peer set in Electronic Equipment & Instruments, the median P/E is 26.5x (VNT 10.1x, BMI 29.5x, ITRI 12.9x, OSIS 26.5x, PI negative). Mirion cannot be compared on P/E given its amortization drag, but on an EBITDA basis it is not obviously cheap relative to these peers, several of which trade at lower multiples with comparable or better organic profiles. The stock sits ~43% below its 52-week high, similar to the drawdowns across the peer group (VNT -38%, BMI -46%, ITRI -43%), which suggests a sector-wide de-rating of small/mid-cap instruments rather than a Mirion-specific mispricing to exploit.

The consensus analyst target range of $26–$29 (mean $27.9) sits 50–67% above the current price. Under our framework, analyst targets function as floors only on cheap, inflecting stocks with rising estimates and forward P/E below ~30x or PEG below 1.5. Mirion does not qualify — estimates are flat-to-down, and the valuation is full on cash metrics — so this target range is more plausibly stale optimism that will be revised toward the price than a signal of imminent re-rating. The stock trading well below convert strikes is a fact, not a valuation argument.

Applying appropriate skepticism to cheapness, the balance-sheet quality (negative tangible book, 69% goodwill, impairment history) argues against rewarding the depressed GAAP multiple. We score valuation at 4.5: the stock is not egregiously overpriced on EBITDA, but there is no identified catalyst or clear mispricing that offers asymmetric upside from here, and the "cheap trailing P/E" is a mirage.

## Competitive Landscape

Mirion's competitive position is its strongest attribute. The installed base at the vast majority of global reactors creates a structurally advantaged, regulation-gated franchise with recurring replacement and service revenue tied to multi-decade reactor lifecycles. Radiation safety is heavily regulated and certification-gated, which entrenches incumbents and limits credible new entrants at scale. The breadth across the nuclear lifecycle — new build, operation, decommissioning — plus the dosimetry and RT QA franchises are difficult to replicate.

The Paragon and Certrec acquisitions strengthen the position: Paragon adds highly engineered solutions for large NPPs and SMRs in the US, deepening the domestic footprint as the SMR buildout gathers momentum; Certrec adds regulatory-compliance and digital-integration software, building recurring software revenue and a "digital ecosystem" in nuclear. These moves improve the moat's trajectory, not just its level.

The offsetting pressures are buyer concentration with government and utility customers, competition at the individual product-line level against local and regional players, and exposure to government budget cycles, sequestration, and shutdowns. On balance, the competitive trajectory is improving-to-stable, and we score it 6.5. We weight this dimension with the recognition that competitive position alone has recently been a less reliable predictor of returns, so we treat it as supporting context rather than the load-bearing element of the thesis.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition-dependent growth and integration risk.** Management explicitly acknowledges that a large driver of historical growth has been acquisition and integration. Each future deal deepens amortization drag and impairment exposure. The $211.8M of prior impairments is a reminder this is not theoretical.

**Fixed-price nuclear contracts.** New-build NPP contracts are long-dated and often fixed-price; management flags that price-escalation clauses may not fully cover cost increases, creating cost-overrun and inflation exposure.

**Russia overhang.** ~$8.7M of net contract assets/AR and $98.4M of backlog are tied to Russian-related projects. A prior ~$21M Russian customer claim (capped at $14M) was rescinded and finally executed in March 2026 after sanction-related delays — a resolved but illustrative example of the recurring collection and sanctions friction that most peers do not carry.

**Tariff and geopolitical risk (newly expanded).** The Q1 10-Q added a standalone risk factor on tariffs, export controls, and trade wars, explicitly citing the US-Israel-Iran conflict and US-China tensions. FX and trade exposure is meaningful; Q1 carried a $3.7M FX loss and a $14.8M OCI translation hit, partially hedged via $238.8M notional cross-currency swaps.

**Leadership transition in the core segment.** Two 2026 Item 5.02 filings (April 13 and July 2) include Loïc Eloy, President of the Nuclear & Safety Group, entering a "letter agreement and secondment addendum." The transition language suggests reassignment rather than a clean exit, but continuity at the top of the most important segment warrants tracking.

Most of these risks are partially priced given the ~43% drawdown. The unpriced tail is a goodwill impairment event, which would hit reported book value but not cash flow. We score risk factors at 5.0 — a balanced set of identifiable but largely-priced risks, with the impairment and organic-growth-disappointment scenarios the ones most capable of moving the stock further.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has risen to 61.3% from 54.4% since late May, above 1-year historical volatility of 54.3% (a 7.0-point rich spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 61.3% > long-term 53.6%), which typically signals an anticipated near-term catalyst. The put/call open-interest ratio has jumped from 0.04 to 1.75, indicating a marked buildup of downside positioning. Combined with 17.1% short interest and 7.6 days to cover, institutional positioning is meaningfully bearish. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±9.8%. For a HOLD, this reinforces caution on timing rather than contradicting a bullish fundamental view; the market is bracing for near-term downside, and the elevated short interest introduces two-sided squeeze potential should organic results surprise to the upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Mirion is a structurally advantaged, regulation-gated niche leader with an installed base at the vast majority of global reactors, riding a durable multi-decade nuclear tailwind. Paragon adds genuine SMR and US exposure; Certrec adds recurring software. The 2025 refinancing materially de-risked the balance sheet, cutting interest expense nearly in half. Adjusted EBITDA is compounding at mid-teens rates. If organic growth accelerates as new builds and the fleet service cycle ramp, and Paragon synergies land, the stock re-rates toward the $26–$29 analyst range. Elevated short interest and low convert dilution risk create asymmetric upside if the organic number inflects. Bull case: $26–$29.

**Bear case.** Growth remains predominantly acquired, organic stays in the low single digits, and the SMR narrative stays pre-commercial for longer than expected. GAAP losses persist as amortization consumes operating income, and a goodwill impairment on the roll-up hits book value and sentiment. The stock continues to de-rate with the small-cap instruments group and drifts toward the low-to-mid teens. Bear case: ~$13, roughly 25% below current.

The base case is a range-bound holding pattern around the current price while the market waits for organic-growth evidence. Base case: ~$18–$20, modestly above current, reflecting a stable business without a near-term re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the evidence does not yet support a stronger call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth accelerates above mid-single digits for two consecutive quarters, with the Nuclear & Safety organic contribution visibly inflecting.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive by +15% or more over 90 days, reversing the current near-quarter drift.
- Clear evidence of Paragon synergy realization and SMR order flow converting to backlog, alongside stabilization of Nuclear & Safety segment leadership.
- Clustered open-market insider buying — currently absent, and its appearance would be the strongest confirming signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, signaling the roll-up model is destroying value.
- Organic revenue turning flat-to-negative, indicating the installed-base service franchise is not delivering.
- A covenant issue or forced deleveraging, or a convert-related dilution event if the stock re-rates disorderly.
- Deterioration in Adjusted EBITDA margin below the high-teens.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 print (expected late July / early August), which will show a second quarter of Paragon integration and the first read on whether organic growth is inflecting. The options backwardation suggests the market expects a near-term catalyst around this window.

## Conclusion

Mirion is a high-quality niche franchise with a defensible moat and a credible, durable nuclear tailwind, sitting on a balance sheet that is meaningfully healthier than it was a year ago after the 2025 refinancing. Those attributes support the improvement in scores from March to May. But the investment case is held back by the gap between headline and organic growth — 27.5% reported versus low single digits organic — and by a GAAP profitability profile consumed by acquisition amortization on a balance sheet where 69% of assets are goodwill and intangibles. Estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative, the options market is pricing near-term stress, short interest is elevated, and there is no open-market insider buying to confirm a bottom.

The ~15% decline since our last review reflects legitimate market skepticism about organic execution and dilution rather than a clear mispricing to exploit. At the same time, the business is not deteriorating, the balance sheet is de-risked, and the moat is intact, so this is not an AVOID. We maintain **HOLD** at an overall score of 5.4, reverting slightly from the May 6.3 as the negative near-term revisions and continued organic softness offset the balance-sheet improvement. The path to a BUY runs through demonstrated organic acceleration and Paragon synergy realization; the path to an AVOID runs through impairment or organic stagnation. The next earnings print is the decisive input.
