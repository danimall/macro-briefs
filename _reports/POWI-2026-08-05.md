## Executive Summary

Power Integrations is a fab-lite analog semiconductor company whose products convert AC mains and high-voltage DC into low-voltage DC. The investment question is narrow and quantifiable: the company earned $170.9M of net income on $651.1M of revenue in FY2022 and $22.1M on $443.5M in FY2025, a 87.1% earnings decline against a 31.9% revenue decline. Gross margin explains almost none of that gap — it fell only 1.8 points, from 56.3% to 54.5%. Core operating expenses rose 13.8% in absolute dollars (roughly $186M to $212M) while revenue fell by a third. R&D now runs 22.8% of revenue. The stock is therefore a revenue-recovery call, and the arithmetic of that recovery is demanding: holding gross margin at 54.5% and core opex flat, a 20% operating margin requires roughly $614M of revenue, 38% above FY2025 and above the FY2022 peak.

At $62.852 the equity capitalizes that recovery aggressively. Enterprise value is approximately $3,245M after netting $257.2M of cash and short-term investments against zero funded debt — 7.3x FY2025 revenue for a business whose four-year revenue CAGR is −10.9%, and roughly 43x FY2025 free cash flow adjusted for an accrued-but-unpaid litigation charge. Against consensus non-GAAP estimates of $1.34 (current year) and $1.85 (next year), the multiples are 46.9x and 34.0x. Trailing GAAP EPS of $0.39 puts the reported P/E at roughly 161x. Reverse-engineering the $1.85 next-year figure implies a materially higher operating margin on a materially higher revenue base than the current run rate; Q1 2026 delivered a 1.3% GAAP operating margin on $108.3M. That is the gap the buyer is underwriting.

The offsets are real but slower-acting than the multiple assumes. PowiGaN is genuine differentiated technology — a 1,700V monolithic GaN switch integrated with the controller, and an August 2026 demonstration of 2,200V GaN. The 800V DC transition in AI racks structurally favors that capability. But the disclosed opportunity is auxiliary power (15W and 35W reference designs against NVIDIA's published Kyber specification, an architecture the trade press describes as due in 2027), auxiliary rails are a small slice of a 600kW rack, and no SEC filing discloses a data-center design win, customer, or dollar of revenue. The Communications franchise has fallen roughly 59% in two years ($129M to $53M) and has not recovered through a semiconductor upcycle, China revenue fell 11.5% year over year in Q1, top-10 customer concentration is flat at 81% of revenue and 87% of receivables, the share-repurchase authorization is exhausted at zero, and insiders sold roughly $24.2M of stock into the May high with no observed open-market purchases — including none at $44–48 in February near the lows. Q2 results are released after the close today, with the options market pricing a ±16.5% move.

## Company Overview

Power Integrations sells high-voltage power-conversion ICs into applications management describes as spanning "milliwatts to megawatts." It reports a single segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker, so there is no segment margin disclosure, which constrains outside analysis of where profit is earned.

Wafers come from three named third-party foundries: Epson and Lapis in Japan (agreements to December 2035 and December 2028 respectively) and X-FAB (December 2028). Assembly and test are subcontracted with some in-house test; net property and equipment was $143.6M at March 31, 2026, so this is fab-lite rather than fabless. Management states qualifying an alternative wafer source at acceptable yields would take 12 to 24 months.

One cost mechanic is recurring rather than one-time: wafer purchases from the Japanese foundries are priced in dollars but subject to contractual yen/USD true-up provisions, with exchange-rate movement shared between Power Integrations and each supplier. The company absorbs roughly half of adverse yen moves directly in cost of revenue. Management named this as the primary driver of the Q1 2026 gross-margin decline.

End-market mix is shifting. Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025: Industrial 41% from 34%, Consumer 38% from 44%, Computer 11% from 12%, Communications 10% from 10%. Approximately 98% of revenue is international, APAC was 80% of Q1 revenue (from 84%), Hong Kong/China alone was $50.0M or 46% of the total, and 71% of revenue moved through distributors, which management concedes limits visibility into end demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026, the most recent filed period):** revenue $108.3M, +2.6% year over year; gross margin 52.6% versus 55.2%; R&D $26.3M (up $2.2M); SG&A $24.4M (down $3.0M); restructuring $6.6M; a $1.4M credit in other operating expenses (partial reversal of the former-CEO equity modification); operating income $1.454M, a 1.34% margin, versus $6.718M and 6.4%; other income $2.466M; effective tax rate 15.8% versus 11.1%; net income $3.300M; diluted EPS $0.06 versus $0.15.

Three derived observations matter more than the headline.

**First, the revenue growth is one end market.** Applying disclosed mix percentages: Industrial contributed roughly +$8.5M (+23.7%), Consumer roughly −$5.2M (−11.3%), Computer roughly −$0.8M, Communications roughly +$0.2M. The sum reconciles to the reported +$2.78M. Industrial growth more than accounts for the entire consolidated increase. This is a narrow recovery, and the FY2025 10-K disclosed that the ship-and-debit allowance rose from $26.4M to $33.6M "primarily" on higher distributor inventory in the industrial end market — the same market driving 2026 growth. The allowance was flat sequentially at $33.66M in Q1, which is mildly reassuring, but the stock-rotation and rebate allowance rose 51% to $3.359M. Some portion of industrial growth may be channel fill; sell-through commentary on the Q2 call is the test.

**Second, the gross-margin decline is mostly structural.** Restructuring charges in cost of goods were $0.365M, roughly 0.34 points of the 2.6-point decline. The residual ~2.3 points is the yen true-up plus mix and pricing. On ~$433M of annualized revenue, 2.3 points is roughly $10M of annual gross profit — larger than FY2025's entire GAAP operating income of $10.2M.

**Third, there is no verifiable net opex reduction yet.** The 10-Q discloses that $3.0M of engineering department cost moved from sales-and-marketing into R&D as personnel shifted to product development. Reported SG&A fell approximately that same $3.0M. Stripping stock compensation and combining the two lines so the reclassification nets out: Q1 2025 cash R&D+SG&A of $43.49M versus Q1 2026 of $45.27M, an increase of $1.78M or 4.1% — despite a 7% workforce reduction. The benign explanation is timing: the reduction in force completed during the quarter, and Q1 absorbed a new CFO, a new SVP of Worldwide Sales, and merit increases. The less benign reading is that savings are being reinvested into GaN and data-center headcount rather than dropping to the operating line. Q2 is the first clean test, and it is the single most important line item in the print.

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the story, with one adjustment.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $111.5M against $24.4M of capex, so reported free cash flow was $87.1M — a 19.6% FCF margin on a 2.3% operating margin, the gap being ~$26M of depreciation and $39.7M of stock compensation (9% of revenue, and roughly 3.9x GAAP operating income). "Other accrued liabilities" rose ~$11.0M over FY2025, closely matching the $11.3M employment-litigation reserve that was expensed but not paid because the company appealed. That correspondence is inferential — the line aggregates multiple accruals — but the direction is right: an accrued, unpaid legal charge flatters operating cash flow and reverses when paid. Cash-adjusted FY2025 FCF is therefore closer to $76M.

Q1 2026 FCF of $18.0M should not be annualized: capex of $2.0M was well below the FY2025 run rate of ~$6.1M per quarter and below Q1 2025's $5.7M. Normalizing capex, Q1 FCF was ~$14M. A reasonable normalized FY2026 range is $60–80M, presented as a sensitivity rather than a forecast given seasonality, working-capital swings, and remaining cash restructuring payments.

**Balance sheet:** cash plus short-term investments $257.2M at March 31, 2026 (down from $300.0M at the end of 2024), zero funded debt, current ratio 6.88x, total liabilities to equity 0.147. Inventory of $163.0M against annualized Q1 cost of goods implies roughly 290 days, down modestly from ~302 days at year-end. Management frames this as an intentional, fungible "wafer bank" of largely undifferentiated die, which is credible for this business model, but 290 days carries genuine write-down exposure if demand air-pockets again.

**Capital allocation shifted decisively.** FY2025 returned $145.3M ($98.1M of buybacks plus $47.2M of dividends) against $87.1M of FCF — a $58.2M excess funded by liquidating marketable securities, which explains the cash decline. As of December 31, 2025 the company "did not have any amount authorized to repurchase shares," repeated in the Q1 10-Q, and Q1 2026 buybacks were zero versus $23.1M a year earlier. The quarterly dividend was simultaneously raised to $0.215, implying ~$47.9M or $0.86 per share annually — a 1.36% yield at $62.852, 60–80% of normalized FCF, and roughly 3.6x annualized Q1 GAAP EPS. The dividend is covered by cash flow (depreciation plus non-cash stock compensation), not by GAAP earnings. The practical market consequence is that roughly $98M per year of price-insensitive buyback demand has been removed.

The credit facility was replaced and upsized: the $75M Wells Fargo revolver was terminated April 10, 2026 and replaced with a $100M unsecured PNC revolver at SOFR+1.60% through February 2031, with a minimum-liquidity covenant of $50M and a 2.00x funded-debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA test. No advances were outstanding. Because there is no funded debt, the leverage covenant is currently non-binding.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue troughed at $419.0M in FY2024, recovered 5.9% to $443.5M in FY2025, and grew 2.6% in Q1 2026. That is a shallow, industrial-led recovery well behind the broader semiconductor tape — the proxy's own peer group returned +168% over five years while Power Integrations returned −54%.

Consensus embeds a much steeper path than the run rate. Current-year non-GAAP consensus of $1.34 against Q1 actual (non-GAAP) of $0.25 requires the remaining three quarters to average roughly $0.36, with Q2 consensus at $0.32. Next-year consensus of $1.85 implies a substantial margin recovery on higher revenue. Estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting: current quarter +5.4% over 90 days, current year +4.8% over 90 days, next year +0.6% over 90 days, and next quarter −1.6% over 90 days. This is a modest positive drift on a demanding baseline, not the revision velocity that would signal a fundamental change the market is systematically underestimating.

Three growth vectors are worth sizing separately.

**Industrial.** Real and currently the whole story: +23.7% in Q1, with EMEA +34% and Americas +24%. The question is what portion is end consumption versus distributor inventory build, given the ship-and-debit disclosure.

**800V DC data-center auxiliary power.** Technically credible and strategically well-positioned. The DER-1114 (15W flyback) and DER-1110 (35W isolated multi-output) reference designs convert 800V to 14V using InnoMux-2 with integrated 1,700V PowiGaN, claiming ~30% reductions in PCB area and BOM count with ≥88% efficiency. The scoping caveat comes from the same source that described the products: auxiliary supplies "account for only a fraction of a modern AI server's power budget," powering microcontrollers, gate drivers and housekeeping functions rather than the main conversion path where silicon carbide content sits. Kyber is described as a 2027 architecture. Publishing reference designs against a public specification does not establish that NVIDIA or its ODM partners have selected these parts, and no SEC filing discloses a data-center design win, customer, or revenue figure. "Computer" was 11% of Q1 revenue. Anyone underwriting an AI-power thesis here should size the auxiliary rail, not the rack, and should date the revenue to 2027 at the earliest.

**Vertical GaN.** The Odyssey Semiconductor acquisition (~$9.52M, July 2024) placed $4.9M in in-process R&D, and the FY2025 10-K states amortization is not expected to begin in 2026 — a filing-level signal that commercialization remains years out. The August 2026 2,200V GaN demonstration supports genuine process leadership at high voltage but is not a revenue event and should not be modeled.

Against these, the Communications decline is the most consequential open question. Communications revenue was roughly $128.9M in FY2023 (29% of $444.5M), $50.3M in FY2024, and $53.2M in FY2025 — down roughly 59% in two years and stuck at 10% of revenue through Q1 2026. Persistence through a broad upcycle is more consistent with permanent share or content loss in cellphone chargers, plausibly to lower-cost Chinese GaN suppliers, than with destocking. The alternative reading, a deliberate exit from low-margin sockets, is supported by gross margin recovering from 51.5% to 54.5% over the same period. The filings do not settle it, and the answer determines terminal revenue.

Tax is a mechanical headwind independent of operations. FY2025 net income exceeded pretax income on a negative effective rate; the 10-K states all federal R&D credit carryforwards were utilized as of December 31, 2025; the NCTI rate rises from 10.5% to 12.6% in 2026; and the Q1 rate already moved to 15.8% from 11.1%. Structurally, U.S. operations generated a $25.3M pretax loss against $46.3M of foreign pretax income, with a $34.9M valuation allowance against state and Canadian deferred tax assets. Immediate domestic R&D expensing and the OECD side-by-side safe harbor cut the other way, so the magnitude is uncertain; the direction is not.

The peer table is close to uninformative. Of the five same-sector comparables, two carry negative P/Es (NVTS −35.6, SYNA −109.1), one trades at 92.1x (PENG) and one at 57.7x (DIOD); all sit 24% to 65% below their 52-week highs. A "peer median P/E of 57.7" derived from that set is not a valuation anchor; it is a set of companies in varying states of earnings depression. The more useful reference is what profitable analog franchises with high-single-digit revenue growth and 25%+ operating margins fetch on forward earnings: typically 25–35x non-GAAP.

On that basis, 34x next-year consensus $1.85 already sits at the upper end of the normal range, and consensus itself requires operating margin to expand substantially from Q1's 1.3% GAAP. Cross-checking with a cash-based approach is sobering: capitalizing a normalized $100M of free cash flow at a 9% discount rate and 3% terminal growth yields roughly $1,667M plus $257M of net cash, about $34.50 per share. Reaching a mid-$60s value on discounted cash flows requires normalized FCF closer to $180–200M, which in turn requires the $614M revenue scenario or structurally higher GaN gross margins that the company does not disclose by product line.

Triangulating: a forward-multiple approach supports roughly $55–68; a cash-flow approach supports substantially less unless the recovery is larger than consensus; a bull scenario with genuine 800V DC content and 18–20% operating margins supports the $90s. Fair value is therefore approximately spot with unusually wide dispersion. Analyst targets span $46 to $95 with a $76.20 mean and $85.00 median. That range reflects two different companies rather than a rounding difference.

The valuation score of 4.3 is a modest improvement from the prior review purely because the price is 13.5% lower on stable estimates. It is not a high score, because there is no identified mispricing: the multiple is expensive on trailing, forward, and cash-flow bases, and the low-P/E-equals-cheap argument does not apply here in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is genuinely mixed, and trajectory rather than level is what matters.

**Improving:** monolithic integration of a 1,700V GaN switch with the controller is a real technical advantage against discrete silicon-carbide alternatives in low-power auxiliary rails, where component count and board area dominate the design decision. The 2,200V demonstration extends that lead at very high voltage, relevant to industrial, grid and traction applications. Industrial share appears to be growing, with EMEA and Americas both up more than 20%. Board composition is well-matched to the strategy: Gregg Lowe (former Wolfspeed CEO) and Ravi Vig (former Allegro MicroSystems CEO) are substantive governance assets for a company betting on wide-bandgap power.

**Deteriorating:** the 59% Communications decline and the 11.5% year-over-year China revenue decline, occurring while every other region grew, are consistent with share loss to domestic Chinese suppliers in price-sensitive charger sockets. The company's own risk factors state that governments "such as China" may provide significant financial assistance to competitors and that this may prevent it from maintaining average selling prices. Management also discloses that U.S. patents expire "ranging from 2026 to 2046" and that "as our patents expire, we will lose intellectual property protection previously afforded by those patents." For a franchise resting substantially on an integrated high-voltage patent estate, expirations beginning this year are a slow-acting but real erosion.

**Flat:** customer concentration. Top-10 customers were 79%, 80% and 81% of revenue across FY2023–FY2025 and 80% in both Q1 2025 and Q1 2026, with top-10 representing 87% of receivables at March 31, 2026. Avnet was 32% of FY2025 revenue; Customer A was 30% in Q1 2026, and three additional customers each crossed 10% (each below 10% a year earlier). Separately, 15% of Q1 revenue derived from a single OEM purchasing through contract-manufacturer customers, up from 13%. Because 71% of revenue flows through distributors, revenue concentration overstates true end-customer concentration, but the receivables concentration is a real credit exposure regardless. The end-market mix shift from Consumer toward Industrial is a genuine change; it is not diversification of the customer base, and the two measures should not be conflated.

## Risk Assessment

**Imminent binary.** Q2 2026 results are released after today's close. Guidance ranges issued May 7 and Street estimates for the quarter were not obtainable in this review, so the report is anchored to March 31, 2026 filings while the price reflects four additional months of information. The track record is supportive but not decisive: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with surprises of +7.7%, +18.7%, +2.0% and −0.8%.

**Customer and supplier single points of failure.** No customer has a long-term contract and orders are cancellable without significant penalty. On the supply side, requalifying an alternate wafer source takes 12 to 24 months.

**Recurring yen margin drag.** The cost-sharing true-up repeats every quarter the yen is unfavorable.

**Inventory.** 290 days of inventory is defensible as a fungible wafer bank but exposes the P&L to write-downs if industrial demand pauses.

**Litigation.** The employment verdict remains reserved at $11.3M (roughly $3.2M compensatory, $6.0M punitive, $2.1M fees) and is on appeal, unpaid. Punitive damages at ~1.9x compensatory sit within ranges appellate courts sometimes reduce, but the outcome is uncertain and the cash has not left. Offsetting this, the CogniPower patent matter resolved entirely in the company's favor: verdicts of noninfringement and invalidity in 2H2025, followed by an April 2026 settlement granting a full release and covenant not to assert against the company or its customers, with no payment made. That removes a multi-year overhang, the associated customer-indemnification tail, and a recurring legal cost.

**Newly added risk factors in the Q1 10-Q** deserve attention: a new "debt obligations we incur in the future could adversely affect our financial condition" factor for a company with zero debt (alongside the revolver upsize from $75M to $100M), a new factor on the reduction in force possibly failing to deliver anticipated savings with unusually specific language about attrition beyond plan and unplanned contract-support expense, expanded employment-litigation language, expanded Strait of Hormuz and sanctions language, and new AI supply-chain strain language. The filings warn about AI in both directions, including "a perceived overbuild of AI capacity" and that front-loaded AI investment "may increase the magnitude and volatility of industry cycles, making downturns more abrupt." Management is not marketing an unqualified AI story in its legal disclosures.

**Governance and alignment.** Total shareholder return indexed from December 2020 stood at 45.83 at the end of FY2025 against a peer group at 268.23 — shareholders lost 54.2% while the peer group gained 168.2%. FY2025 PEO granted compensation was $8,243,433 with "compensation actually paid" of negative $839,142, so the equity component is genuinely at risk; two individuals served as principal executive officer during 2025, and the single reported figure cannot be attributed to either without reading the full proxy tables. Performance metrics are Net Revenue, Non-GAAP Operating Income, and Relative Revenue — two of three revenue-based, with no EPS, margin-percentage or ROIC metric disclosed, which provides limited incentive for the opex discipline the margin arithmetic requires. The former-CEO transition produced an $8.4M ASC 718 modification charge in FY2025 (partially reversed by $1.4M in Q1) equal to 82% of FY2025 operating income; the accounting is mechanical and the disclosure clear, but the compensation is real. An additional executive officer departed in Q1 with $0.4M cash severance and $1.8M of accelerated stock compensation. Item 5.02 filings cluster heavily across 2025–2026.

**Insider activity.** Form 4 data through July 22, 2026 shows one-directional selling. The former CEO reduced holdings from 587,619 shares to 279,516 between February 6 and May 28, a 52.4% reduction, with roughly 296,740 shares sold for approximately $24.2M in May alone at $70.71–$87.05. An SVP and a director also sold in May; the CEO sold 12,690 shares at $73.73 on July 22 (~$936K) and holds 143,705. A retiring long-tenured founder-CEO diversifying a concentrated position is an ordinary pattern, and whether these sales were executed under pre-existing 10b5-1 plans is unverified (the 10-Q confirms only that no plan was adopted or terminated during Q1). The more robust observation is the absence of any open-market purchase across roughly six months of filings, including at $44–48 in February near the lows. Combined with an exhausted buyback authorization and no new one established, no one inside the company has been a buyer of this equity at any price in 2026.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing this print as a genuine event. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 97.1% against 61.3% one-year realized — a 35.9-point spread, in the 85th percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (97.1% at 15 days versus 90.6% at six months). The nearest expiry implies a ±16.5% move. Since the July 5 snapshot, ATM IV rose 14 points from 83.1% while the price fell 13.5%, and the put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.08 to 1.03, so open interest that was overwhelmingly call-weighted a month ago is now balanced. The supplied skew reading is call-heavy (−3.9% near-term, −5.5% at six months), so the structure is a large two-sided position around an event rather than a one-sided bearish bet. Short interest of 13.9% of float with 5.6 days to cover means a strong print carries squeeze potential in both the equity and the front-month calls.

For a holder, near-term optionality is expensive to buy and the distribution around today's release is wide. Nothing in the options data resolves the fundamental question; it confirms that the market shares the view that Q2 guidance is the discriminator.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (path to $90+)

Q2 shows the February restructuring landing in combined cash operating expense, with R&D plus SG&A ex-stock-compensation down sequentially, while industrial revenue growth continues at a 20%+ pace and sell-through commentary confirms end consumption rather than channel build. Gross margin recovers toward 55%+ as the yen headwind abates. Full-year non-GAAP EPS lands at or above $1.34, and the FY2027 path toward $1.85 becomes credible. Layered on this, PowiGaN converts published 800V DC reference designs into disclosed design wins for 2027 Kyber-class and ±400V DC architectures, and the Computer end market — 11% of revenue today — begins to grow at rates that change the mix. Revenue reaches $550–600M by FY2028 with operating margin back toward 18–20%, producing normalized non-GAAP EPS near $3.00. At 30x that is roughly $90, consistent with the $95 high analyst target. The balance sheet supports the wait: $257.2M of net cash, zero debt, and a covered dividend.

### Bear Case (path to $42)

The Communications decline proves to be permanent share loss rather than deliberate exit, and the same dynamic extends into consumer appliances as Chinese GaN suppliers move up in voltage and integration. China revenue continues to decline, and the industrial gain proves partly to be distributor inventory that unwinds in the second half. Restructuring savings are absorbed by GaN and data-center headcount, so operating margin stays in the low-to-mid single digits. The yen true-up keeps ~2 points of gross margin suppressed. Tax normalization takes several cents off EPS mechanically. Inventory at 290 days generates a write-down. Non-GAAP EPS stalls near $1.10–1.20, and the market re-rates a low-growth, low-margin analog franchise to 30–35x that number — roughly $37–42 — with support near the February 2026 area of $44 and the 52-week low of $30.86. That is roughly 33% below spot. The removal of ~$98M per year of buyback demand and the absence of insider buying leave no natural bid on the way down.

### Where I come out

Fair value is approximately spot with wide dispersion. Base-case fair value of roughly $64 (about 34x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP $1.85, sanity-checked against a normalized cash-flow approach that argues lower and a bull scenario that argues higher) offers no margin of safety in either direction, while the bear case is a 33% decline and the bull case a 43% gain. That is a wide, roughly symmetric distribution around an unresolved question, which argues for owning what you already own and committing no new capital.

The score trajectory (4.5 in March, 4.8 in May, 5.2 in July) reflected an improving balance-sheet read and a modest estimate uptrend. This review holds it essentially flat at 5.1. The 13.5% price decline since July on stable estimates does improve the valuation input slightly, and I have not marked anything down for price action. What has not improved is the evidence base: there has been no new filing since the May 7 10-Q, no 8-K between June 4 and August 4, and no disclosed company event to explain either the run to $91.18 or the retreat from it. The financial-health score is trimmed marginally from 7.0 to 6.8 not for balance-sheet reasons — solvency is simply not the question with $257M of net cash and no debt — but because ROE of 3.3%, a dividend at 3.6x GAAP EPS, FY2025 capital returns exceeding free cash flow by $58.2M, and 290 days of inventory describe capital deployment running ahead of earnings power.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately following today's Q2 2026 release and Q3 guidance (after market close, August 5, 2026), then the Q3 print in late October 2026 and the FY2026 10-K in early February 2027.

**Fair-value reference:** $55–68 on forward-multiple approaches, with a base-case anchor near $64 (approximately 34x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP $1.85). Bull $90 (30x roughly $3.00 of normalized non-GAAP EPS on $550–600M of revenue at 18–20% operating margin, +43% from $62.852). Bear $42 (roughly 35x $1.15–1.20 of stalled non-GAAP EPS, −33%).

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Verified operating leverage.** Combined cash R&D plus SG&A (excluding stock compensation) declines sequentially and year over year in Q2 and again in Q3, with non-GAAP operating margin above 12% in at least one of those quarters. This is the direct test of whether the 7% reduction in force produced savings or was reinvested.
2. **Revenue breadth.** Consumer revenue returns to year-over-year growth, or Computer rises above 15% of revenue, while total revenue grows above 8% year over year — establishing that recovery extends beyond a single industrial channel.
3. **Price.** The stock trades at or below $48–50, roughly 26–27x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS, which would provide the margin of safety currently absent. A disclosed 800V DC design win with a named customer or quantified revenue, or a new share-repurchase authorization funded from free cash flow, would each substitute for this condition.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. Gross margin falls below 52% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the yen cost-share and pricing pressure are structural rather than transitory.
