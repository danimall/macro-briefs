## Executive Summary

Home BancShares is a bank holding company that, as of March 31, 2026, held approximately $23.2 billion in assets, operating 226 branches across Arkansas (75), Florida (78), Texas (59), Tennessee (8), Alabama (5), and New York City (1), including Tennessee via the recently completed Mountain Commerce acquisition. The company delivered a 2.10% ROA and 40.9% efficiency ratio in FY2025 — profitability metrics that place it in the top decile of US regional banks. Net interest margin expanded to 4.51%, and net charge-offs collapsed to 0.02% of average loans following a deliberate credit cleanup in 2024.

At $27.03 (current price $26.70 as of May 5), the stock trades at approximately 11x forward earnings with a 3.2% dividend yield, representing a discount to the peer median P/E of 12.0x despite superior profitability. The 5-year total shareholder return of 164% lags the peer group's 211%, suggesting persistent market discounting of CRE concentration risk and CEO succession uncertainty. Two developments alter the risk/reward calculus: (1) the founder/CEO's $2.7M open-market purchase at $27 in April 2026, and (2) the stock's 12% decline from its 52-week high appears to have absorbed recent estimate adjustments.

The primary risk preventing a higher-conviction call is the 53.2% CRE loan concentration (194% of equity), including $1.16B in hotel exposure and $1.01B in office — sectors facing structural headwinds from elevated rates and remote work. Q1 2026 saw significant deterioration in non-performing loans, with NPLs rising to 1.16% of total loans from 0.54% at year-end, driven primarily by one $92.1 million loan relationship placed on non-accrual. In a recession scenario with broader CRE stress, book value impairment of 10-15% is plausible.

## Company Overview

Home BancShares was founded in 1998 by John W. Allison and has grown through 23 bank acquisitions since 1999. The company operates through Centennial Bank with five principal business segments:

- **Community Banking** (Arkansas, Florida, Texas, Alabama, Tennessee): Traditional deposit-gathering and lending through 226 branches
- **Centennial Commercial Finance Group (CFG)**: A national CRE/C&I lending platform based in NYC with offices in LA and Dallas ($2.01B in loans)
- **Shore Premier Finance (SPF)**: Marine lending focused on high-end boats ($1.38B in consumer loans)
- **Trust & Wealth Management**: $7.46B in AUM/custody through Centennial Bank and GoldStar Trust
- **Residential Mortgage**: Portfolio and secondary-market lending

The company completed its acquisition of Mountain Commerce Bancorp (Knoxville, TN) on April 1, 2026, marking its entry into Tennessee. MCBI had approximately $1.8B in assets, $1.5B in gross loans, and $1.6B in deposits. System conversion is planned for November 2026, with partial cost saves assumed in 2026 (approximately 25% of targeted savings) and full run-rate savings thereafter.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability

HOMB's profitability metrics are exceptional relative to the banking industry:

|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------------|
| ROA | 2.10% | 1.77% | 1.77% | ~1.0% |
| ROE | 11.61% | 10.43% | 10.82% | ~10% |
| NIM (FTE) | 4.51% | 4.27% | 4.25% | ~3.5% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 40.88% | 42.74% | 46.21% | ~55-60% |
| Net Charge-offs/Avg Loans | 0.02% | 0.41% | 0.16% | ~0.3% |

Net income grew 18.2% YoY to $475.4M, driven by NIM expansion (interest-bearing liability costs declined 40bp from 3.08% to 2.68%) and $922M in loan growth. The efficiency ratio improvement from 46.2% to 40.9% over two years reflects deliberate cost optimization — a $15.9M headcount reduction in 2024 followed by $11.8M reinvested in performance-based incentive comp in 2025.

### Balance Sheet Strength

- **CET1 Ratio**: 16.30% (vs. 7.0% minimum)
- **Total Equity/Assets**: 18.78%
- **Tangible Book Value/Share**: $14.60 (record), growing at ~15% annualized
- **Liquidity**: $5.96B available, exceeding uninsured/uncollateralized deposits by $1.4B
- **Uninsured Deposits**: 26.1% of total (after netting intercompany/collateralized)

The company paid off $140M of 5.50% subordinated notes in July 2025 and repurchased $20M of 3.125% 2032 notes at a gain ($1.9M) in September 2025. Remaining subordinated debt of $279.3M bears 3.125% fixed until January 30, 2027, at which point it switches to floating rate (3-month Term SOFR + 182bp) and becomes callable. Whether calling this debt produces net savings depends on replacement funding costs and regulatory capital considerations, but it represents an optionality event.

### Credit Quality

Following a deliberate asset quality cleanup in 2024 (primarily Texas CRE, $50.5M of $60.8M in total NCOs from Texas), FY2025 year-end credit metrics showed significant improvement:
- NPLs: 0.54% of total loans (down from 0.67%)
- Net charge-offs: 0.02% of average loans
- ACL/Loans: 1.90%
- ACL/NPLs: 350%
- Texas portfolio showed net recoveries of $4.4M in 2025, suggesting meaningful progress on legacy problem assets

**However, Q1 2026 revealed a material credit development:** Non-performing loans increased to $182.1M, or 1.16% of total loans, from $85.0M / 0.54% at year-end 2025. Non-performing assets rose to $224.1M, or 0.97% of total assets (from 0.55% in Q4). ACL/NPL coverage fell to 163% from 350%. Management attributed the increase primarily to one $92.1 million loan relationship placed on non-accrual. Texas non-performing loans jumped to $120.4M from $26.6M at year-end, indicating the Texas credit story requires continued monitoring despite the 2025 improvement in charge-offs.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are modest, consistent with a mature community bank, though Q1 2026 introduced some near-term headwinds:

**Organic Loan Growth**: $922M (6.2%) in FY2025 after $340M in FY2024. The CFG platform returned to growth (+$194M) after contracting in 2024. Multi-family was the largest growth category, more than doubling from $496M to $1.14B. However, in Q1 2026, total loans declined $52.6M, with community banking footprint loans down $100.5M partly offset by CFG growth of $47.9M. Whether this represents seasonal variability or a deceleration warrants monitoring.

**Acquisition-Driven Growth**: Mountain Commerce adds approximately $1.8B in assets and entry into Tennessee's growing Knoxville market. The all-stock structure (~5.4M shares at 0.85x exchange ratio, valued at ~$150M) preserves capital for further M&A.

**Headwinds to Growth**:
- NIM expansion may plateau as funding cost reductions approach a floor
- Non-interest-bearing deposits continue declining industry-wide ($4.6B → $3.96B over two years)
- Q1 2026 loan contraction raises questions about near-term growth trajectory
- Earnings beat/miss pattern shows no positive surprise momentum in the last 4 quarters

Forward consensus calls for approximately $2.47-$2.51 in FY2026E and $2.55-$2.63 in FY2027E. These estimates may be conservative if the MCBI acquisition delivers cost synergies and loan growth resumes, but the Q1 credit development and loan contraction temper near-term optimism.

## Competitive Landscape

HOMB occupies an advantaged position among regional banks:

1. **Efficiency leadership**: The sub-41% efficiency ratio reflects structural advantages — technology investment, lean staffing models, and scale across 226 branches. Most peers operate at 55-65%.

2. **NIM superiority**: 4.51% vs. a typical peer range of 3.3-3.8%. This reflects a higher-yielding asset mix (CRE, marine lending) and disciplined deposit pricing in community markets where competition is less intense than major MSAs.

3. **National lending platforms**: CFG and Shore Premier Finance provide diversified origination channels beyond the branch footprint, enabling growth without branch expansion costs.

4. **Acquisition infrastructure**: 23 deals completed since 1999 demonstrates repeatable integration capability. The MCBI deal expands into Tennessee with minimal execution risk given the company's experience.

**Competitive risks**: Larger banks (JPM, WFC) increasingly compete in HOMB's Sunbelt markets. Fintech competition for deposits continues. The company's CRE focus exposes it to structural headwinds in office and potentially hotel segments that other banks have reduced.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. CRE Concentration (HIGH)**: At 53.2% of loans and 194% of equity, CRE represents the dominant risk factor. Office ($1.01B) faces secular pressure from remote work. Hotels ($1.16B) are cyclically sensitive. Multi-family ($2.2B total including construction) has surged and could face oversupply in Sunbelt markets. A 5% loss rate across the portfolio would consume approximately $800M against $300M in reserves, requiring capital erosion.

**2. Q1 2026 Credit Deterioration (HIGH)**: The spike in NPLs from 0.54% to 1.16% introduces uncertainty about whether this is idiosyncratic or the beginning of broader credit migration. Texas NPLs jumping from $26.6M to $120.4M in one quarter warrants close monitoring. The concentration risk inherent in large CRE exposures means additional single-name events remain possible.

**3. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)**: If rates remain elevated, asset yields benefit but deposit competition intensifies. If rates fall materially, the NIM advantage compresses as the 61% floating-rate loan book reprices downward faster than already-low funding costs can decline further.

**4. CEO Succession (MODERATE)**: John Allison is 79. The company's strategy, culture, and credit discipline are closely tied to his leadership. The company's 10-K specifically names five executives whose loss "may materially and adversely affect" the company. COO J. Stephen Tipton (44) appears positioned as successor, but execution through leadership transition creates uncertainty.

**5. Geographic/Hurricane Risk**: 34% of CRE in Florida. The 2024 hurricane reserve ($33.4M) demonstrates vulnerability. Active hurricane seasons could produce recurring charges.

**6. Macro Environment**: In a recession scenario, CRE credit losses would likely emerge first in construction/development ($2.73B) and potentially office. The 16.3% CET1 provides meaningful buffers before capital impairment.

## Conclusion

Home BancShares trades at a discount to peers despite industry-leading profitability. The 11x forward P/E is too cheap for a bank generating 2.1% ROA and operating at 41% efficiency, particularly with the CEO demonstrating personal conviction through a $2.7M purchase at current levels.

The investment case is tempered by the Q1 2026 credit development: the spike in NPLs to 1.16% from 0.54% introduces near-term uncertainty about credit trajectory. While management attributes this to a single relationship, the concentration risk inherent in large CRE exposures means this development cannot be dismissed without further evidence.

The combination of (a) valuation discount to peers, (b) superior profitability metrics that remain intact (2.09% Q1 ROA), (c) strong insider purchase, (d) 3.2% dividend yield, and (e) 16.3% CET1 capital providing ample buffer collectively argues for ownership with the understanding that the credit situation requires close monitoring over the next 1-2 quarters. The risk/reward at 11x earnings remains asymmetric to the upside given the quality of the franchise, contingent on the Q1 credit deterioration proving manageable.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 2026 NPL deterioration (0.54% → 1.16%, driven by $92.1M non-accrual) materially weakens the "pristine credit quality" pillar of the thesis; resolution of this relationship is a key condition for the base-case target.
