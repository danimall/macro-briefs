**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is a debt-free, cash-rich corn ethanol producer whose fiscal 2025 (year ended January 31, 2026) results showed a 43% jump in net income to $83.0M, with nearly all of the gain attributable to the federal 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit (~$28.1M tax benefit). Strip out 45Z and pretax income actually declined slightly year-over-year ($88.6M vs. $92.9M). The operating story is flat-to-soft: revenue rose 1% to $650.5M, ethanol selling prices fell roughly 6% in Q1 FY2026, and the company's earnings power now hinges on a sliding-scale tax credit whose Treasury implementing rules were still only proposed as of February 2026. This is a policy-arbitrage business sitting on a volatile crush spread, with a balance sheet that both protects the downside and drags on returns.

The stock has nearly doubled over the trailing twelve months (from ~$22.55 to $43.94), and the share count doubled to ~33M on a 2-for-1 split in September 2025. At ~15.3x trailing/forward earnings, REX trades above the refining-and-marketing peer median P/E of ~10.6, a premium that is partly justified by the ~$364M cash and Treasury hoard (about 25% of the $1.45B market cap, leaving an enterprise value near $1.08B) and partly an artifact of credit-inflated earnings. The recent early adoption of ASU 2025-10, which reclassifies 45Z credits from the tax line into gross profit, flatters the optics of operating profitability and requires normalization before drawing conclusions about underlying margins.

We rate REX a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The thesis is two-sided: the balance sheet and the demand floor (EPA set 15.0B-gallon conventional RVOs for 2026-27) cap downside, while two carbon-sequestration option paths — One Earth's Illinois project and NuGen's Summit Carbon partnership — are both stalled by state-level legal and regulatory action, eroding embedded option value. Near-term options markets are pricing imminent stress (one-week ATM IV at 78.8% vs. 31.7% historical vol, in backwardation), and insiders have been consistent one-directional sellers since the post-split grants. None of this argues for a confident BUY, and the cash and demand floor argue against an AVOID.

## Company Overview

REX American Resources is a pure-play corn ethanol producer operating through a holding-company structure with equity interests in three ethanol entities running six plants:

- **One Earth Energy, LLC** (Gibson City, IL) — 76.1% owned and consolidated. The flagship asset: 150M gal/yr capacity, permitted to expand toward 175M and ultimately 200M gal/yr, and the site of REX's carbon sequestration initiative.
- **NuGen Energy, LLC** (Marion, SD) — 99.7% owned and consolidated.
- **Big River Resources, LLC** — ~10.3% effective interest, accounted for under the equity method (four plants in IA/IL/WI).

Across the six plants, the system shipped roughly 722M gallons over the trailing twelve months; REX's effective ownership corresponds to about 294M gallons. The product slate is approximately 78% ethanol, with the balance from dried/modified distillers grains (animal feed) and distillers corn oil (a renewable-diesel feedstock). The company exited the refined-coal tax-credit business in November 2021, leaving it a single reportable segment.

Leadership is concentrated: Stuart A. Rose serves as Executive Chairman and Head of Corporate Development, and Zafar A. Rizvi as CEO and President. The two form an "Executive Committee" that functions as the chief operating decision-maker. Both signed the June 2, 2026 10-Q and presented on the May 28 earnings call, indicating no disruption at the top despite a set of routine June 2026 governance 8-Ks (director elections, the 2026 Incentive Plan, and a charter amendment raising authorized shares from 45M to 90M consistent with the post-split count).

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was up about 1% from $642.5M, and below FY2023's $855M; the top line is volume-driven and largely flat. The earnings swing comes from margin and tax credits, not sales growth. Net income attributable to REX rose from $58.2M, with the effective tax rate swinging to a -7.3% benefit from a +23% provision the prior year. The reported "growth" is a tax-policy outcome, not operational improvement.

**Accounting reclassification.** In Q1 FY2026, REX early-adopted ASU 2025-10 and moved 45Z credits out of the tax line into gross profit as "Production tax credit income." On a restated basis, FY2025 gross profit jumps from $93.7M to $125.4M and net income to $98.7M. This makes government credits look like operating income. Any margin or operating-leverage assessment should be done on a pre-credit basis to avoid mistaking a subsidy for pricing power.

**Balance sheet.** The balance sheet is the core of the investment case. At April 30, 2026, REX held $117.7M in cash plus $246.6M in Treasuries, roughly $364.3M of cash-equivalents, against essentially no debt ($2.7M of finance leases). The current ratio runs 5.9-6.8x and equity/assets is 77%. The Altman Z-score of 2.91 (grey zone) is misleading; it is depressed by the EBIT/assets ratio reflecting heavy idle cash rather than any distress signal. ROE of 15.6% understates underlying operating returns because of the cash drag; ex-cash returns on the operating business are materially higher.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow rose to $117.8M in FY2025 from $64.2M, with free cash flow near $49M after $68M of capex. Q1 is seasonally cash-negative on working capital and bonus timing. The primary return lever is buybacks: 1.65M shares repurchased for $32.9M in FY2025, with 2.36M shares still authorized. There is no dividend. Management has earmarked $220-230M for the One Earth expansion plus sequestration, funded entirely from cash, with $70-80M to be spent in FY2026.

**Most recent quarter.** Q1 FY2026 EPS was $0.56 versus $0.26, net income to REX $18.5M versus $8.7M, and gross profit $29.1M versus $14.3M. The quarter benefited from $7.5M of 45Z credit income, higher distillers corn oil prices (+17%) and DDG (+7%), partly offset by softer ethanol pricing ($1.66/gal vs. $1.76, down 6%). The earnings-surprise record is strong on a small visible sample — three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with the most recent quarterly print beating by 63%.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth in this business is structurally limited. The U.S. ethanol industry carries roughly 18.5B gallons of capacity against a ~15B-gallon RFS mandate; this persistent overcapacity caps pricing power and means volume growth at the industry level is largely policy-gated. REX's growth levers are therefore specific rather than secular:

1. **One Earth expansion** from 150M toward 175M and eventually 200M gal/yr, targeted for completion by end of 2026. This is a meaningful capacity addition at the consolidated crown-jewel asset, fully funded from cash.

2. **Carbon-intensity reduction and the 45Z sliding scale.** By lowering One Earth's CI score (toward sub-47.5), REX can capture higher tiers of the 45Z credit ($0.10-$1.00/gal with prevailing-wage compliance). Carbon sequestration would further reduce CI and potentially unlock 45Q. This regulatory-arbitrage path is the primary value-creation thesis beyond the crush spread.

3. **Demand-floor support.** The House passed HR 1346 (year-round E-15) in May 2026, with the Senate pending; passage would expand the addressable demand pool.

The constraint on all three is that they are demand- and policy-dependent, not driven by share gains or a widening competitive moat. The 45Z credit runs through 2029 (extended by OBBBA), which provides a defined runway but also a hard cliff that the market will increasingly discount as 2029 approaches. The earnings jump is a one-time policy step-up, not a compounding ramp, so we do not treat this as a structural growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

REX trades at ~15.3x trailing and forward earnings (the two are identical given the data), against a peer median P/E of ~10.6 in oil & gas refining and marketing (PARR 6.2, PBF 10.8, DINO 10.6; WKC and CVI carry negative/distorted multiples). On headline P/E, REX sits at a meaningful premium to refiners.

Two adjustments matter. First, backing out the net cash implies an enterprise value near $1.08B against ~$83M of reported net income (or ~$99M restated), so the operating business carries an EV/earnings multiple around 11-13x. That is roughly in line with, not cheap to, the refiner peer set. Second, a large share of that earnings base is the 45Z credit. On a pre-credit basis, the operating multiple looks richer and the policy dependency is the binding question. A low-CI, fully-funded expansion combined with continued 45Z capture could re-rate the stock; a Treasury rule change or a CI-score shortfall would compress earnings and the multiple simultaneously.

The cash hoard provides a margin of safety — roughly $11.06/share of net cash provides hard downside support — but it is also a capital-efficiency drag, earning ~3.7% on Treasuries with management repeatedly describing it as "investigating various uses." The market discounts idle cash; it does not pay full multiples for it.

We do not see a specific, falsifiable mispricing that would justify a high valuation score. The premium to refiners is defensible given the balance sheet, but it is not asymmetric upside; the operating business is fairly valued once credits and cash are normalized. The valuation score of 5.3 reflects a balanced setup rather than either a clear bargain or a clear overvaluation. The score trajectory shows valuation declining from 6.0 to 5.5 over recent reports, appropriate given the stock rose ~95% over the period while the operating earnings base was flat-to-soft ex-credit. The decline reflects price catching up to (and somewhat exceeding) normalized earnings power, not fundamental deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

REX's competitive edge is not scale. It competes against far larger integrated players — ADM, POET, Valero, Green Plains — and against Brazilian sugarcane ethanol in the advanced-biofuel pool. Its differentiation is capital discipline (a debt-free balance sheet that lets it self-fund expansion and weather negative-margin periods) and CI-score positioning at One Earth, which lets it capture higher 45Z tiers than higher-carbon competitors.

The competitive trajectory has improved modestly; the trajectory data shows this dimension rising from 4.5 to 6.0 over recent reports, reflecting the funded expansion and the CI-reduction strategy. The improvement is bounded by industry overcapacity that structurally caps pricing power for everyone, and by the fact that the CI/sequestration advantage is contingent on permits and pipeline access that are currently stalled. The moat is real but narrow and policy-contingent rather than durable and self-reinforcing. We hold competitive position at 5.5 — stable-to-slightly-improving, with the upside gated by regulatory outcomes outside the company's control.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is concentrated and largely policy/commodity driven:

1. **Crush-spread volatility with no diversification.** REX is 100% dependent on the corn→ethanol spread; corn is ~74-75% of COGS. Management can hedge only ~4 months out and explicitly warns it "may operate at negative or minimally positive margins." The 10-Q sensitivity table shows a 10% adverse move in either ethanol or corn prices hits pretax income by roughly $40-52M — material against an $83M net income base.

2. **45Z credit dependency.** The entire FY2025 beat rests on 45Z. Treasury rules were still proposed as of February 3, 2026, with CI-score modeling, prevailing-wage/apprenticeship requirements, and monetization markets all uncertain. A rule change could erase the benefit, and the credit cliffs in 2029.

3. **Carbon sequestration execution risk.** ~$58.7M is sunk; the EPA Class VI well permit decision is expected around November 2026 and is not yet granted. Illinois SB 1289 imposed a CO₂-pipeline moratorium (to ~July 1, 2026), forcing a refiling, and SB 1723 bans sequestration over aquifers. Failure could trigger a write-off and a CI-score competitive disadvantage.

4. **NuGen/Summit Carbon pathway impaired.** NuGen's separate carbon path depends on Summit Carbon Solutions, whose North Dakota storage permits were voided as unconstitutional in March 2026, with South Dakota banning eminent domain for CO₂ pipelines. This pathway is materially impaired.

5. **RFS, SRE, and trade risk.** The demand floor depends on EPA RVOs, 40 pending small-refinery exemptions, year-round E-15 (Senate pending), and tariffs (36% of ethanol exports go to Canada; 20% of DDG to Mexico — both tariff-exposed).

6. **Secular EV/liquid-fuels erosion** is a long-term demand headwind.

The combination of concentrated commodity exposure, a binary EPA permit decision in late 2026, two stalled carbon paths, and a 2029 credit cliff yields a risk score of 4.8; specific catalysts for material downside exist and are not fully priced. The cash buffer mitigates rather than eliminates these.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. One-week ATM IV stands at 78.8% against 31.7% one-year historical vol — a 47-point premium and the 89th percentile within the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (78.8% near-term vs. 46.4% six-month). ATM IV jumped 18 points in a single session (60.7% to 78.8%) on a flat price. The implied one-week move is ±8.4%. The near-term skew is -32.3% (call-heavy), which together with the backwardation suggests the market is positioning for a sharp move and leaning toward upside on the front-week, consistent with event-driven hedging or speculation rather than a directional bearish bet. This is a signal to avoid initiating fresh positions into a vol spike, not a fundamental sell signal; the longer-dated structure normalizes toward 46% IV with roughly neutral skew.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The expansion at One Earth completes on schedule by end-2026, adding capacity at the lowest-CI asset; the EPA grants the Class VI permit, validating the sequestration option and unlocking higher 45Z tiers plus 45Q; year-round E-15 passes the Senate, expanding demand; and the cash hoard gets deployed into accretive M&A or accelerated buybacks rather than sitting at 3.7%. In that scenario, normalized earnings power steps up, the operating multiple re-rates toward the better-positioned end of the peer set, and the stock works back toward and above its 52-week high near $53. Buybacks at sensible prices compound per-share value. A bull target in the high-$50s to low-$60s is achievable if both the operational and policy/permit pieces fall into place.

### Bear Case
The crush spread compresses (a plausible outcome given industry overcapacity and softening ethanol prices), Treasury finalizes 45Z rules in a less favorable form, the EPA delays or denies the Class VI permit, and the Summit Carbon impairment forces a write-down at NuGen. Strip the credit and the operating business is fairly-to-fully valued at ~11-13x EV/earnings with no growth, leaving the stock exposed to multiple compression. Continued one-directional insider selling and tariff exposure on Canada/Mexico-bound exports add pressure. The cash floor (~$11.06/share) caps downside, but the operating component could re-rate the stock toward the low-$30s, where it traded for much of the second half of 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The thesis is balanced. The cash and demand floor cap downside; the policy and permit dependencies cap upside conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- EPA grants the One Earth Class VI well permit (decision expected ~November 2026), validating the sequestration option and the higher-tier 45Z capture.
- Treasury finalizes 45Z rules in a form that confirms or improves the credit economics, removing the single largest earnings uncertainty.
- Clear, accretive deployment of the cash hoard (disciplined M&A at sensible multiples or a materially accelerated buyback), turning the capital-efficiency drag into per-share value creation.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A material adverse change to 45Z (rule change, CI-score shortfall, or monetization-market failure) that removes the credit benefit.
- Sustained crush-spread compression pushing the operating business toward negative margins, evidenced by two consecutive quarters of pre-credit operating losses.
- A sequestration write-off at One Earth or NuGen coupled with clustered, accelerating C-suite selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the next quarterly earnings release and the EPA Class VI permit decision expected around November 2026. The fiscal year ends January 31, so the FY2026 full-year print (spring 2027) will reveal the first clean read on operating margins under the new ASU 2025-10 presentation and the realized 45Z capture.

## Conclusion

REX American Resources is a well-run, conservatively financed corn ethanol producer whose recent earnings strength is overwhelmingly a function of the 45Z tax credit rather than operating improvement. The balance sheet is a genuine asset — net cash against the market cap, no debt, and a self-funded growth pipeline — and it underpins real downside protection. That same cash sits idle at low returns, the operating business is fairly valued once credits and cash are normalized, the carbon-sequestration option value that the market may be paying for is being eroded by state-level legal action on both pathways, and insiders have been consistent one-directional sellers since the post-split grants. Near-term options are pricing a sharp move, which argues against initiating into the current vol spike.

We rate REX a HOLD. The stock has done its work over the past year, and from here the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would become more constructive on a favorable EPA permit decision, final 45Z rules that confirm the credit economics, or accretive deployment of the cash; we would turn negative on a 45Z rule reversal, sustained crush-spread compression, or a sequestration write-off. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on REX, and the higher bar for that continuation is met: the fundamentals confirm a fairly-valued, policy-dependent business rather than a mispriced opportunity in either direction.
