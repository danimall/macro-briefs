## Executive Summary

Cactus Inc. is a dominant U.S. wellhead manufacturer (~43% market share) with exceptional financial characteristics: a near-debt-free balance sheet, mid-30% EBITDA margins, and a capital-light model generating ~$220M annual FCF on ~$1.1B revenue. The January 2026 acquisition of 65% of Baker Hughes' surface pressure control business for $344.5M is strategically sound, adding Middle East international exposure that hedges against the secular decline in U.S. land rig counts.

The problem is price. WHD trades at $62.23, near its 52-week high ($62.75), after appreciating 51% over the past year and 87% from its October 2025 low of $33.20. The trailing P/E of ~58x reflects the transition period; on forward consensus of $2.99 (FY2026), the stock trades at ~21x forward earnings. That forward multiple is reasonable for the quality of the franchise but offers limited margin of safety given declining domestic activity (U.S. rig count trending below 545), tariff-driven margin pressure on Vietnam/China manufacturing, and the structural complexity of the Baker Hughes put/call arrangement (potential $200M+ cash outlay from 2028). Insider selling by the founding Bender family ($20M in March 2026 at ~$50) with zero open-market purchases adds cautionary context.

The investment case requires the Baker Hughes JV to deliver above-expectation earnings accretion while domestic headwinds remain contained. At current prices, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Cactus operates in two segments: **Pressure Control** (~66% of revenue), which designs and manufactures wellhead systems, production trees, and frac rental equipment; and **Spoolable Technologies** (~34% of revenue), which produces FlexSteel reinforced composite pipe for production and gathering applications. The company was founded by the Bender family (operators in wellheads since the late 1970s) and IPO'd in 2018.

On January 1, 2026, Cactus closed the Baker Hughes surface pressure control acquisition. For $344.5M, Cactus acquired 65% of Baker Hughes' former surface pressure control franchise, which derives approximately 85% of revenue from the Middle East. This creates "Cactus International," expanding geographic scope from predominantly U.S. onshore to a global pressure control platform.

Manufacturing is vertically integrated across Bossier City, LA (rapid turnaround for North America), Suzhou, China, Hai Duong, Vietnam (lower-cost for longer-lead-time international work), and Baytown, TX (spoolable pipe). Revenue mix: Products 76%, Field Service 16%, Rentals 8%.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**
1. Baker Hughes JV revenue consolidation (~$300M+ annualized from Middle East operations)
2. Natural gas price recovery (Henry Hub +61% YoY in 2025, $3.52 vs. $2.19) supporting gas-directed drilling
3. AI-driven power demand increasing long-term natural gas investment

**Near-term headwinds:**
1. U.S. land rig count declining below 545, with oil at ~$65 WTI insufficient to drive completion growth
2. Tariff costs: 50% Section 232 on steel + tariffs on China/Vietnam manufacturing
3. Estimate revisions trending negative near-term: current quarter estimate revised -13.2% over 90 days ($0.75 → $0.65)

**Estimate trajectory:** FY2026 consensus is $2.99 (up 0.8% in 30 days but down 2.3% over 90 days). FY2027 consensus is $3.45 (+1.2% in 30 days, -1.1% over 90 days). The revisions are essentially flat — neither strongly positive nor negative. This is inconsistent with a high-conviction growth story.

**Medium-term optionality:**
- FlexSteel composite pipe has potential for PHMSA regulatory easing that could open midstream/transmission markets (substantial TAM expansion)
- Full acquisition of Baker Hughes's 35% stake (callable from 2028) would further consolidate international earnings
- Supply chain diversification via new Vietnam JV plant

Growth is real but moderate: the domestic business is flat-to-declining, and the international JV is additive but unproven at scale under Cactus management. FY2027 consensus implies ~15% EPS growth, which is respectable but does not justify the degree of re-rating already reflected in the stock.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus's ~43% U.S. wellhead market share is a formidable competitive position built over decades of family-operated manufacturing expertise. Key advantages include:

1. **Vertical integration:** Owned manufacturing (not outsourced) with rapid-turnaround domestic capacity in Bossier City and lower-cost international capacity in China/Vietnam
2. **Installed base:** Wellheads are non-commodity items requiring ongoing service, creating recurring revenue streams
3. **FlexSteel differentiation:** Composite pipe offers faster installation, corrosion resistance, and lower lifecycle cost vs. traditional steel in suitable applications
4. **Baker Hughes JV:** Leverages existing Baker Hughes customer relationships and contracts in the Middle East, reducing cold-start risk

The concern is execution: Cactus has no track record operating in Middle East markets, and the JV structure (65/35 with Baker Hughes retaining minority interest) creates governance complexity. The trajectory from prior reports shows competitive position improving from 6.0 to 7.0, which appears warranted given the JV's strategic logic.

## Risk Assessment

**1. U.S. Drilling Cycle (High):** Rig counts have declined from 667 (2023) → 580 (2024) → 545 (2025) and are expected to decline further in 2026 with WTI at $65. This directly compresses the domestic Pressure Control segment, which remains the majority of revenue.

**2. Baker Hughes Put/Call Complexity (Moderate-High):** From January 2028, Baker Hughes can put its 35% stake to Cactus at 6x TTM EBITDA (cap $660M, floor $530M). The 35% obligation ranges from $186M to $231M. If the JV performs well, Cactus faces a large mandatory cash outlay; if poorly, Cactus owns 65% of a declining asset. This creates path-dependent risk that is difficult to quantify.

**3. Customer Concentration (Moderate):** One unnamed customer = 17% of total revenue (increasing from 10% three years ago). Loss of this relationship would materially impair results.

**4. Tariff Headwinds (Moderate):** 50% Section 232 steel tariffs + China/Vietnam surcharges are elevating input costs. Supply chain diversification efforts are ongoing but partially neutralized by Vietnam also being tariff-exposed.

**5. TRA Structural Overhang:** The Tax Receivable Agreement requires 85% of tax savings to be paid to original owners. In a change-of-control scenario, ~$251M would be accelerated. This acts as a takeover defense that eliminates M&A premium optionality for public shareholders.

## Conclusion

Cactus is an excellent business that is fairly priced after a significant re-rating. The Baker Hughes JV is strategically sound and already contributing revenue, but the stock at $62.23 versus a $63.22 mean analyst target offers no upside to consensus. The valuation score decline in prior reports (6.0 → 4.0) reflects an accurate read: fundamentals have improved, but the price has moved faster than earnings. With flat estimate revisions, insider selling, a declining domestic rig count, and the stock within 1% of its 52-week high, the asymmetry favors patience. The quality of the franchise warrants continued ownership for holders, but new capital should wait for a meaningful pullback or evidence that international earnings are exceeding expectations.
