## Executive Summary

Liquidia has delivered the commercial result its bulls underwrote. YUTREPIA (inhaled dry-powder treprostinil) was approved on May 23, 2025 for both pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and PH-ILD, shipped in June 2025, and produced net product sales of roughly $58M across two partial quarters, then $90.1M in Q4 2025, then $129.9M in Q1 2026 — sequential growth of 44.2%. Q1 2026 revenue was $132.9M against $71.4M of total costs, a 46.3% operating margin, $52.9M of net income, and $53.0M of operating cash flow. Cash rose to $222.8M and book equity went from $44.7M to $108.6M in one quarter. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from $2.58 to $3.48 over 90 days (+35.0%), and the current quarter from $0.53 to $0.84 (+57.7%).

The estimate-revision inflection signal is present and I am weighting it as genuine support for the operating trajectory. It is not, however, capable of pricing the variable that dominates this equity. The District of Delaware's decision in United Therapeutics' '327 patent case — tried in June 2025, post-trial briefing complete — remains pending and unscheduled. The Q1 10-Q's own language is that there is a "material risk of an adverse ruling … which could result in an injunction that affects the entire product, thereby preventing us from commercializing YUTREPIA at all or at least until PH-ILD is removed from the label." A separate trade-secret action in Durham County goes to trial in January 2027 and also seeks relief that "may require YUTREPIA to be removed from the market." The company states it cannot estimate a range of loss on any of the five UTHR matters. Layered on top is a HealthCare Royalty (HCR) revenue-interest obligation whose terms force immediate repayment of 100% of funded amounts less payments received if the FDA grants final approval to a therapeutically equivalent inhaled treprostinil.

At $89.71 the stock trades above the consensus mean target of $84.88 and 20% above the $74.50 median, with the recommendation mix having shifted one notch from Buy to Hold over two months. My probability-blended value is $88, essentially spot, against a modal base case of $95 (+5.9%), a bull case of $135 (+50.5%), and an adverse-ruling bear case of $28 (-68.8%). A director liquidated roughly 72% of his position — about 1.28M shares, $90M+ gross — between June 8 and July 23, 2026, precisely across the S&P SmallCap 600 inclusion window. Near-term implied volatility of 160% against 63.8% realized, in backwardation, says the options market expects imminent resolution of something. The business is performing; the risk-adjusted entry is not there. Trim.

## Company Overview

Liquidia is a single-segment commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company in pulmonary hypertension, operating through Liquidia Technologies and Liquidia PAH (formerly RareGen). Three assets carry the story:

**YUTREPIA** is essentially the entire equity value. It is an inhaled dry-powder treprostinil formulated with the company's PRINT particle-engineering platform (partly licensed from UNC Chapel Hill) and delivered via Plastiape's RS00 inhaler. The differentiation claim in the filings is deep-lung delivery at higher achievable dose levels than the labeled doses of competing inhaled treprostinil products — a dose-ceiling argument against United Therapeutics' Tyvaso DPI. Approval covers both PAH and PH-ILD.

**Generic treprostinil injection** is promoted under a 2018 agreement with Sandoz running to December 2032, under which Liquidia PAH receives 50% of net profits. Sandoz owns the ANDA. Service revenue was $2.98M in Q1 2026 versus $3.12M in Q1 2025 — a flat-to-declining annuity, structurally capped because ICU Medical no longer manufactures or supports the CADD-MS 3 pump required for subcutaneous administration, existing pump supply is believed sufficient only "through at least the end of 2026," and neither Liquidia nor Sandoz has submitted a 510(k) for a replacement.

**L606** is an investigational liposomal sustained-release inhaled treprostinil licensed from Pharmosa Biopharm, dosed twice daily by nebulizer, now in the worldwide placebo-controlled Phase 3 "Re-Spire" study in PH-ILD. An October 2025 Vectura license adds US device rights ($2.0M upfront, up to $12.0M development and $92.5M sales milestones, mid-single-digit royalties).

Liquidia manufactures bulk PRINT powder in Morrisville, NC but owns neither the YUTREPIA device, the Sandoz ANDA, nor the L606 device. It depends on LGM Pharma as sole API supplier and Lonza Tampa as sole encapsulation and packaging supplier (contract to December 31, 2028, with binding 12-month firm orders and minimum annual quantities).

Trailing-twelve-month revenue (Q2'25 through Q1'26) is $288.1M and TTM operating income roughly +$45M. Q1 2026 cost of product sales was $11.079M on $129.881M of product revenue — a 91.5% gross margin. Operating expenses grew but far slower than revenue: SG&A +56% year over year to $46.9M, R&D +80% to $12.6M. Stock-based compensation of $9.217M sat mostly in SG&A ($8.663M, about 18% of the line).

### Cash conversion is positive but materially below reported net income

Q1 operating cash flow was +$53.0M against $52.9M of net income. Non-cash add-backs of $16.2M (SBC $9.2M, debt accretion $6.5M, D&A $0.5M) were nearly offset by $10.7M of unfavorable working-capital movement — receivables +$20.1M, inventory +$4.6M, partly funded by accrued expenses +$14.5M.

Three adjustments matter for anyone annualizing that figure:

1. **HCR service is in financing, not operating.** The 10-Q states interest expense "consists of non-cash interest charges on long-term debt." All $6.494M of Q1 interest expense is accretion. The $19.969M of actual cash paid to HCR is classified in financing activities. Conventional free cash flow was $53.0M − $2.8M capex = $50.2M; after HCR service it was $30.2M.
2. **Cash capex understates economic capex.** The 10-Q discloses a $6.586M non-cash PP&E increase through accounts payable, so Q1 economic capex was closer to $9.4M. Construction in progress rose from $5.1M to $14.0M and gross PP&E from $25.8M to $38.5M.
3. **Q1 had favorable one-offs.** A $14.5M accrued-liability build, a $14.4M release of accrued compensation (2025 bonus payout), and a 6.9% effective tax rate reflecting only a partial valuation-allowance release. Normalizing those, quarterly operating cash generation at Q1 volumes is plausibly $35-45M rather than the $53M print. A naive $212M annualization is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

### Balance sheet transformed, capital structure still expensive

Cash of $222.8M, current ratio 2.22x, debt/equity on carrying value 1.64x (from 4.28x at year-end). Recomputing Altman Z on the Q1 balance sheet with current market capitalization gives roughly 16, versus a distress-range reading on FY2025 inputs — the difference is almost entirely the market-equity-to-liabilities term and the shift from a loss to a profit in the EBIT term. The FY2025-based distress flag is a stale-input artifact; the accumulated deficit of $573.5M and thin book equity that drove it are real facts but not a solvency conclusion for the current entity.

The HCR revenue-interest financing is the capital-structure fact that matters. HCR has funded $175.0M of a $200M facility, with the remaining $25M available only by mutual agreement. Payments are capped at 175% of funded amount plus an IRR true-up (18% on the first $67.5M, stepping to 12% on the last $25M). The effective rate has stepped down through six amendments to 14.4%. Remaining fixed contractual payments total $258.685M against a $177.9M carrying value: $38.4M for the rest of 2026, $47.1M in 2027, a $54.5M peak in 2028, then declining to 2033. Covenants include a $15.0M minimum cash requirement and restrictions on additional debt, liens, investments, acquisitions, asset sales and dividends. Change of control or default accelerates to Hard Cap plus true-up. The weighted-average operating-lease discount rate of 15.2% is a useful independent read on Liquidia's collateralized cost of capital.

Two additional balance-sheet items sit off the current statements: $58.1M of non-cancelable manufacturing and supply commitments, and $36.3M of estimated fixed obligations on the second Morrisville facility, excluded from the lease-maturity table because commencement is not expected until late 2026.

**Financial health score: 6.0.** Positive EBIT, positive cash flow, 2.22x current ratio and $222.8M of cash against an expensive, amortizing, covenant-bearing obligation whose cash claim escalates into 2028 and whose acceleration clause is tied to a competitive event, plus only ~9.0M unreserved authorized shares limiting the equity-raise valve.

## Growth Analysis

The launch metrics are internally coherent. Cumulative unique prescriptions exceeded 4,500 and roughly 3,750 patients had started treatment as of the Q1 release, with prescription-to-start conversion at or above 85%. Dividing Q1 product sales by cumulative starts gives about $34.6K per quarter, or ~$138.5K annualized — inside the plausible band for branded inhaled treprostinil net pricing. That cross-check argues the ramp is driven by patient additions rather than channel loading or price. The caveat is that "patients started" is cumulative rather than active, so the denominator is imperfect and channel inventory is not disclosed.

Gross-to-net accruals rose from $35.5M to $54.0M in the quarter, proportionally faster than the 44% product-sales increase but not alarmingly so, and prior-period true-ups ran below 1% of total revenue — the estimates are tracking.

Consensus revisions are among the strongest in my coverage: current quarter $0.53 → $0.84 (+57.7% over 90 days), current year $2.58 → $3.48 (+35.0%), next year $5.24 → $5.81 (+10.9%, with +4.2% in the last 30 days). Zacks consensus for the August 12 print is $165.25M of revenue and $0.70 of EPS, the latter on an unverified GAAP/non-GAAP basis and therefore not directly comparable to Q1's $0.52 GAAP diluted figure.

Three constraints temper the forward path:

**Gross margin steps down mechanically.** The 10-Q states plainly that as of March 31, 2026 the company "sold through all inventory previously expensed to research and development and accordingly, our future cost of product sales will reflect the full cost of manufacturing YUTREPIA." Q1's 91.5% was a subsidized number. Magnitude is undisclosed; at Q1 volumes each 5 points of margin is about $6.5M of quarterly operating income. Tiered Lonza pricing by batch size and the new Morrisville facility could offset part of it, and continued volume leverage on fixed SG&A could hold operating margin roughly flat even as gross margin falls. This should be modeled as a range.

**Tax normalizes upward.** The 6.9% Q1 effective rate reflects a partial valuation-allowance release; management states the evidence "was not sufficient … to support a full release." Full release produces a one-time GAAP benefit and then convergence toward statutory. Extrapolating a 6.9% rate materially overstates forward GAAP EPS.

**Out-year consensus needs scrutiny.** $5.81 on roughly 101-103M diluted shares implies about $590M of net income. Reaching that on revenue plausibly in the $900M-1.2B range requires both a high operating margin and a low tax rate simultaneously. My own FY2027 GAAP build — $900M revenue, 85% fully costed gross margin, ~$340M of combined SG&A and R&D, ~$22M of accretion, 22% tax on ~103M diluted shares — lands nearer $3.00-3.10. On $1.1B of revenue it reaches roughly $4.25. I treat the $5.81 as either adjusted or aggressively taxed and do not anchor on it.

Beyond YUTREPIA, management plans label-expansion studies in PH-COPD, IPF, PPF and Raynaud's phenomenon in systemic sclerosis, plus the L606 pivotal program, all stated to be funded from operations. The GSK collaboration restricts PRINT for inhaled applications with certain identified compounds, which limits the platform-optionality argument.

**Growth prospects score: 7.5.** Rare launch velocity with verified profitability and rising estimates, discounted for a disclosed margin step-down of unknown size, a tax headwind, and out-year estimates I cannot reproduce.

## Competitive Landscape

United Therapeutics is simultaneously Liquidia's litigant, the incumbent it is taking share from, and the source of the medium-term innovation threat.

The share-shift evidence is real but not cleanly attributable. UTHR's Q2 2026 results showed revenue of roughly $783M, described as essentially flat sequentially "as competition pressured nebulized TYVASO sales," with a non-GAAP EPS beat ($7.27 versus $6.82) on a revenue miss. That confirms inhaled-treprostinil economics are under pressure. It does not apportion the pressure between YUTREPIA and the generic treprostinil inhalation solution that took effect January 1, 2026. Do not read it as a clean YUTREPIA share-gain datapoint.

Forward competitive threats are specific and dated. UTHR reported positive Phase 3 data for ralinepag, an oral prostacyclin receptor agonist, in March 2026, and announced a treprostinil soft-mist inhaler in February 2026 with a planned 2026 filing. Either could reset the inhaled/oral prostacyclin frame before L606 arrives. Insmed's TPIP, a third inhaled dry powder, is in Phase 3 for PAH and PH-ILD. And the sharpest structural tail is an AB-rated inhaled treprostinil generic, because FDA final approval of a therapeutically equivalent product triggers immediate repayment of 100% of HCR's funded amount less payments received — converting a revenue event into a liquidity event.

Liquidia's structural position is fragile in ways the share trajectory obscures. It does not own the YUTREPIA device (Plastiape), the Sandoz ANDA, or the L606 device (Pharmosa/Vectura). It has single-source suppliers for API (LGM, $2.7M/year minimum) and encapsulation/packaging (Lonza, sole). For L606, single sources for API, drug product, packaging and device, "some of which are located in Taiwan," with the company stating it is working to establish a secondary supply chain outside Taiwan but cannot say when. The RG Cartridge for the Sandoz generic is made in Chengdu with China-based facilities, explicitly flagged for tariff and trade-restriction exposure.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** Trajectory is clearly improving — a challenger taking share from an incumbent with a genuine dose-ceiling differentiation and a dual-indication label. The underlying position depends on third-party devices, single-source suppliers, and a right to exist that is the subject of active litigation.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Five UTHR actions, two of which seek market removal.** The '327 patent case (D. Del., Judge Andrews) was tried in June 2025 with post-trial briefing complete; the decision is pending and unscheduled. UTHR seeks an injunction removing YUTREPIA from the market. Because the label carries both PAH and PH-ILD, an infringement finding limited to PH-ILD could still disrupt the whole product until the label is amended. The '782 case in M.D.N.C. continues after a denied preliminary injunction. The 2021 trade-secret and unfair-trade-practices case in Durham County survived Liquidia's summary-judgment motion in July 2025 and goes to trial in January 2027, with relief sought that "may require YUTREPIA to be removed from the market." A May 2024 breach-of-contract action seeks a declaratory judgment assigning to UTHR all inventions conceived by a former employee while at Liquidia — which, if successful, would strip Liquidia's offensive '494 patent lever and, per its own risk factor, "could create a precedent" affecting other patents. Liquidia cannot estimate a range of loss on any matter and flags that UTHR may assert further patents, naming US 11,723,887. A July 22, 2026 report that a judge criticized UTHR's counsel in a filing is procedural tone, externally sourced only, and establishes nothing about the merits.

**2. Customer concentration is severe and worsening.** Q1 2026: Customer A at 47% of gross product sales and 47% of receivables; Customer B at 39% and 46%. Two counterparties therefore represent 86% of gross product sales and 93% of receivables. Sandoz is 100% of service revenue. Receivables grew from $54.1M to $74.2M with no allowance for credit losses recorded; DSO on Q1 revenue is 50.8 days. The shift from a service business to a product business replaced one concentration with another. Limited-distribution rare-disease drugs normally run through two or three well-capitalized specialty pharmacies, so this reflects channel design more than counterparty fragility — but a dispute or renegotiation at either node would hit cash conversion immediately.

**3. Disclosed, unquantified gross-margin step-down** as fully costed manufacturing replaces R&D-expensed inventory.

**4. HCR cash claim escalates into the period of least slack.** The 2027-2028 step-up to a $54.5M peak coincides with peak L606 Phase 3 spend and the new-facility ramp, when only ~9.0M unreserved authorized shares are available without a charter amendment.

**5. Reimbursement mix.** IRA Manufacturer Discount Program rebates of 10% in initial coverage and 20% in catastrophic coverage apply to a Medicare-heavy PH-ILD population, a structural net-price headwind as mix builds.

**6. The $70.6M RareGen judgment accrues nothing to LQDA shareholders.** Under the November 2020 PBM Litigation Funding and Indemnification Agreement, "Liquidia PAH and its affiliates will not be entitled to any proceeds resulting from … the RareGen Litigation unless there is an event of default by PBM." The balance sheet carries an $8.547M indemnification asset against an $8.547M litigation-finance payable — a wash. Exclude the judgment from sum-of-the-parts work.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 160.0% against 63.8% one-year realized, an IV/HV spread of 96 points, in clear backwardation (160.0% at three days, 123.4% at one month, 105.5% at three months, 84.6% at six months). The three-day tenor prices a ±14.0% move, consistent with the August 12 earnings date; the December tenor prices ±41.5%, which is the market's read on the litigation distribution rather than on earnings. Put/call volume ratio is 2.36 but open-interest put/call is 0.36 — flow is buying near-dated puts while the standing book remains call-tilted, the signature of event hedging on top of directionally long positioning. Skew is broadly balanced (1.8% near-term, 5.0% at one month, -1.4% at six months). Short interest is 17.2% of float at 5.3 days to cover, which makes an outright short unattractive and would amplify any favorable ruling.

The takeaway supports the recommendation rather than contradicting it: the options market is pricing an imminent, high-magnitude, two-sided event and charging heavily for the protection. Long holders looking to reduce exposure into that pricing are selling into rich near-term volatility, which argues for outright trimming rather than buying puts.

### Management and Governance

Execution has been strong. CEO Roger Jeffs, formerly president and COO of United Therapeutics, took the company from approval to GAAP profitability in three quarters and states that expanded YUTREPIA studies and L606 pivotal trials will be funded from operations. The company-selected performance measure is Revenue, and the 523,004 executive PSUs granted in 2026 vest on YUTREPIA net-product-sales goals — a genuine operating metric, an improvement on the 2025 tranche, which vested on first commercial sale. FY2025 cumulative TSR indexed to 708.21 versus a 120.69 peer index, and CEO compensation actually paid of $34.9M against $7.7M of total compensation is mark-to-market on that move.

Against that, the insider record is one-directional. Director Stephen Bloch's reported holdings fell from roughly 1,785,242 shares to 505,077 between June 8 and July 23, 2026 — approximately 1.28M shares, about 72% of the position, roughly $90M+ gross at prices from $64.27 to $90.71 — with sales in nearly every session across the window in which S&P SmallCap 600 index funds were required to buy (announced June 8, effective June 22). Paul Manning sold roughly 199,000 shares on June 4 at $62.62-$64.42. No open-market purchases appear anywhere in the recent Form 4 record. I separate the July 10 PSU/RSU settlements and July 13 sales at $71.39/$71.52 across essentially the whole C-suite from this: the 10-Q confirms sell-to-cover is the standard withholding method, and those are tax mechanics, not signal. Whether Bloch's and Manning's sales were executed under pre-existing 10b5-1 plans is unverified, and a fund-level distribution or LP redemption is a plausible non-informational explanation. Selling into index liquidity is also rational execution irrespective of view. But a 72% position reduction by a long-tenured director clears the threshold at which insider selling carries information, and it is the one insider datapoint that is not explained by withholding mechanics.

**Management quality score: 5.5.** Launch execution and PSU design are positive; the absence of any open-market buying alongside a 72% director liquidation, and a litigation-funding structure under which shareholders capture none of a $70.6M judgment, offset it.

**Risk factors score: 2.8.** An unscheduled ruling that the company itself calls a material risk of an injunction affecting the entire product, a second market-removal trial in January 2027, a generic-approval debt-acceleration clause, 86%/93% customer concentration with no credit allowance, single-source Taiwan and China supply exposure, and an undisclosed-magnitude margin step-down landing in the next print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $135 (+50.5%)

The '327 decision goes Liquidia's way, or resolves into a royalty-bearing settlement that leaves the product on the market. With the injunction risk removed, the equity reprices to the franchise: YUTREPIA's dose-ceiling differentiation and dual-indication label support meaningful share gains, and FY2028 revenue of $1.3B at a 40% operating margin produces roughly $400M of net income. At 32x on ~104M diluted shares that is $123, with L606 in Phase 3 and label expansions in PH-COPD, IPF, PPF and Raynaud's as unpriced optionality. Consensus catches up — next-year estimates have already risen 10.9% in 90 days and 4.2% in 30 — and 17.2% short interest at 5.3 days to cover accelerates the move. The top published target of $130 is the anchor.

### Bear Case — $28 (-68.8%)

The court finds infringement of the '327 patent and enjoins the product, or the January 2027 trade-secret trial produces the same result. YUTREPIA revenue stops or is limited to PAH pending a label amendment, and Liquidia is left with a declining Sandoz annuity capped by an unavailable infusion pump, L606 in mid-Phase 3, $222.8M of cash against $258.7M of remaining fixed HCR payments escalating to a $54.5M peak in 2028, a $15.0M minimum-cash covenant, and roughly 9.0M unreserved authorized shares with which to recapitalize. Even before any injunction is upheld on appeal, the immediate repricing is 60-70%. A deeper tail below $15 exists if the injunction survives and HCR must be restructured. Independently, a therapeutically equivalent inhaled treprostinil approval would force immediate repayment of 100% of HCR's funded amount less payments received.

### Base Case — $95 (+5.9%)

No ruling lands within the horizon or the outcome is manageable. Revenue compounds from the $132.9M Q1 base toward $650-700M in FY2026 and $900M-1.0B in FY2027; gross margin steps down from 91.5% into the mid-80s and the effective tax rate normalizes toward statutory, so GAAP EPS lands nearer $3.00-3.10 in FY2027 than the $5.81 out-year consensus; the market keeps the multiple around 12-13x forward EV/revenue, which supports roughly $9.5B of diluted equity, or ~$93-95 per share. Upside from here is thin because the multiple already discounts a favorable litigation path.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base $95 (+5.9%), bull $135 (+50.5%), bear $28 (-68.8%). Probability-blended value $88, or -1.6% from the $89.705 basis.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst. Existing holders should reduce to a position size they would accept losing roughly 70% of, since that is the magnitude of the adverse-ruling outcome and the ruling is unscheduled. A reasonable execution frame is to sell half the position ahead of the August 12 print — near-term implied volatility of 160% versus 63.8% realized means the market is charging heavily for downside protection, which argues for reducing outright rather than hedging — and to stage the remainder if the stock holds above $95 post-print. Do not add new capital at spot.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. A '327 decision in Liquidia's favor, or a settlement that keeps YUTREPIA on the market on defined royalty terms. This alone would justify moving to ACCUMULATE with a $135 target.
2. Q2 or Q3 disclosure that fully costed product gross margin holds at 85% or better, with operating margin sustained at or above 40%, confirming the margin step-down is smaller than the disclosure implies.
3. A price near $65-70, which brings the entry below the $74.50 median analyst target and restores positive expected value against the same probability weights.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. An adverse '327 ruling or any injunction affecting the label, in whole or in part.
