**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NeoGenomics has delivered the operating turnaround it promised. Q2 2026 revenue rose 11.2% to $201.7M, adjusted gross margin expanded 260 basis points to 48.1%, G&A fell 11.3% to $63.6M, adjusted EBITDA grew 36% to $14.5M, and the company printed its first GAAP net income quarter ($2.2M) in at least five years. Management raised FY2026 guidance across all three lines: revenue to $802–806M from $797–803M, adjusted EBITDA to $56–58M from $55–57M, and GAAP net loss narrowed to $(34)–(42)M from $(50)–(63)M. Two multi-year binary overhangs cleared within seven months: the Natera patent dispute concluded in NeoGenomics' favor in December 2025 (asserted patents invalidated, appeal withdrawn), and the self-disclosed DOJ/OIG consulting-agreement matter settled on July 20, 2026 for $10.0M with **no corporate integrity agreement** against an $11.2M reserve the company had previously described as an unboundable minimum. IP litigation expense collapsed from $7.443M in H1 2025 to $0.165M in H1 2026, a permanent margin benefit. In June the company refinanced, issuing $316.25M of 0.75% notes due 2032 and retiring ~$276M of the January 2028 maturity, leaving roughly $69M due in 2028 against $145.5M of cash and annual cash interest of about $2.5M.

The problem is price. At $16.57 the equity is worth roughly $2.15B on management's guided 130.0M diluted shares, and enterprise value is approximately $2.394B ($2.15B + $385.25M debt principal − $145.5M cash). That is 2.98x FY2026 guided revenue, **42.0x guided adjusted EBITDA**, and 89.6x guided adjusted diluted EPS of $0.185. Guidance embeds $35–37M of stock-based compensation as an EBITDA add-back, approximately 63% of the $57M midpoint, so on EBITDA net of equity compensation (~$21M) the multiple is roughly 114x. FY2026 remains guided to a GAAP net loss.

More decision-relevant than the multiple is what sits underneath the growth rate. Q2 clinical test volume grew **1.9%** (363,498 tests) while average revenue per clinical test grew **11.7%** to $515. Essentially all reported growth is modality mix (NGS revenue +26%) and payer-channel migration within a near-static requisition base. The pharma/data leg that would represent genuine end-market diversification is contracting roughly 14% year over year. Consensus is moving the wrong way despite the raise: over the past 30 days, next-quarter EPS estimates were cut 18.7% to $0.07 and next-year estimates 7.2% to $0.34, while the stock rose 17.3%. Multiple expansion on falling out-year estimates, at a 52-week high, with 79% near-term implied volatility and effectively zero put open interest, is a poor entry. We move from HOLD to **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

NeoGenomics is a pure-play oncology reference laboratory operating CAP-accredited/CLIA-certified labs in Fort Myers FL, Aliso Viejo and Carlsbad CA, Durham NC, Ramsey NJ, and Houston TX, plus an ISO 15189 sample-processing facility in Cambridge, UK, and several small non-processing "dry lab" analysis sites. It employed roughly 2,500 FTEs at December 31, 2025, including approximately 135 MDs and PhDs, and reports as a single segment.

The test menu spans cytogenetics, FISH, flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, PCR/NGS molecular testing, and morphologic analysis, with named franchises including the PanTracer portfolio (Tissue, Tissue+HRD, LBx liquid biopsy), Neo Comprehensive heme and myeloid panels, NeoTYPE targeted panels, and RaDaR, the circulating-tumor-DNA minimal-residual-disease assay acquired with Inivata in June 2021.

The structural feature that distinguishes NeoGenomics from its higher-profile peers is the channel model. FY2025 payer mix was 71% client direct billing, 16% commercial insurance, and 13% Medicare/government, essentially unchanged from 72/15/13 in each of FY2024 and FY2023. Roughly seven-tenths of revenue is billed to the client institution (community pathology practice, hospital lab, reference lab, academic center) rather than to the patient's insurer. NeoGenomics sells technical-component "tech-only" services so the client's own pathologist performs and bills the professional interpretation, positioning itself as a non-competitive partner to community pathology. This differs materially from Tempus, Caris, Foundation Medicine, or Guardant, which bill payers directly and in doing so displace the local pathologist's fee.

The trade-off is explicit: channel access and lower direct reimbursement risk in exchange for price realization. A 71% client-billed book is also a price-shopped, contract-renewable business exposed to hospital cost pressure, group-purchasing fees, and in-sourcing. Customer concentration is genuinely low — no single client exceeded 10% of revenue in FY2023, FY2024, or FY2025 — so the concentration risk sits at the channel and payer-policy level rather than in any individual account.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is price and mix, and it is arithmetically self-limiting. Migrating existing requisitions from FISH, flow, and IHC toward NGS lifts ASP at high incremental margin, which is precisely why adjusted gross margin expanded 260bp. But once the addressable requisition base has migrated, growth reverts toward the volume line, which is currently 1.9–3.9%.

Three counterarguments deserve weight. First, NGS penetration in community oncology remains low, so the mix runway may extend several years. Second, MRD/RaDaR represents a genuinely incremental volume pool — surveillance testing recurs per patient rather than occurring once per diagnosis — and the 10-K identifies therapy selection as the segment carrying the highest reimbursement. Third, part of the ASP lift may reflect improved collections and payer contracting on existing work, which would be a one-time step-up rather than a trend. The disclosures do not permit separating these.

Against that, the pharma/data leg is contracting ~14%, making the business *more* concentrated in community clinical testing, not less. The 10-K's client-contract risk factor notes pharma development clients may terminate without cause upon notice.

Guidance requires an H2 operating-leverage step-change. Implied H2 revenue is $413.7–417.7M versus H1's $388.3M (+6.6% to +7.6%), while implied H2 adjusted EBITDA is $32.5–34.5M versus $23.5M (**+38% to +47%**), lifting H2 EBITDA margin to roughly 8.1% from 6.1%. Part is structurally supported by disclosed H2 revenue weighting and the demonstrated G&A lever. But roughly six times more EBITDA growth than revenue growth leaves little room for a volume disappointment in a quarter that carries summer-vacation softness and Florida hurricane exposure.

Consensus is already trimming the phasing. Over the past 30 days, current-quarter EPS went to $0.05 from $0.06 (−4.2%), next-quarter to $0.07 from $0.09 (**−18.7%**), and next-year to $0.34 from $0.36 (−7.2%), on 11 analysts. Current-year is essentially unchanged at $0.18, consistent with the raised guidance. The pattern — FY2026 intact, Q3 and FY2027 cut — reads as analysts absorbing the pharma weakness and back-half phasing risk. This revision velocity does not signal an underestimated inflection; it points the opposite way on the out-year.

The earnings-surprise record is constructive: four quarters, three beats, one in-line, with adjusted EPS surprises of 51.1%, 38.9%, 53.5%, and 40.8%. On a base of $0.01–0.06 per share these percentages overstate the dollar magnitude, but the direction supports confidence in the FY2026 guide.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K frames two competitive tiers. Against Quest, Labcorp, and ARUP, NeoGenomics concedes inferior name recognition, client base, financial resources, and headcount, and notes that consolidation lets larger players exploit "cost efficiencies afforded by large-scale automated testing," producing "greater price competition." Against Guardant, Natera, Exact, Caris, Tempus, and Myriad, it competes with more narrowly specialized molecular players.

Stated differentiation is not technological primacy: turnaround time, an oncology-only menu that avoids splitting a patient sample across labs, ~135 MDs/PhDs available for consultation, and a ten-region direct sales force. The company's self-description is asymmetric — "a leading provider of Heme oncology diagnostic testing" but only "one of the key providers of solid tumor NGS testing solutions." That asymmetry matters because solid tumor is where the capital and the competitive intensity are.

The tech-only architecture is a real moat in the community setting precisely because it is not winner-take-all: NeoGenomics monetizes the technical work and leaves the interpretive fee with the referring pathologist, aligning it with the constituency direct-billing competitors disintermediate. The corollary is a ceiling on realized price — $515 blended average revenue per clinical test sits far below a payer-billed comprehensive genomic profile. The counterargument is that $515 is a blend across FISH, flow, IHC, cytogenetics, and NGS, and that 11.7% ASP growth suggests the ceiling is not currently binding.

The trajectory question governs the score. Competitive position is scored on direction, not level, and 1.9% clinical volume growth in a market with rising cancer incidence and expanding companion-diagnostic requirements is not evidence of share gain. Revenue is growing because the company is selling more expensive tests to roughly the same requisition base. That is margin improvement, not competitive advance. **4.5.**

Three pending RaDaR ST submissions to MolDx are the live competitive variable. MRD/recurrence monitoring is the segment where incremental volume genuinely exists, and Medicare coverage is the gate. Three submissions pending is candid disclosure that coverage breadth remains unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth composition.** If NGS penetration of the existing base matures, or if the ASP uplift proves a one-time repricing rather than a repeatable mix shift, revenue growth compresses toward the ~2–4% volume line. The 10-K itself states that pricing "is often one of the most significant factors" in lab selection.

**PAMA/CLFS reductions are live.** The 15% annual Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule rate-reduction cap, repeatedly delayed, was pushed to 2026 and extended through 2028, with the 10-K stating that effectiveness "as of January 31, 2026, further reduces Medicare program payments." Direct exposure is limited by Medicare/government at 13% of revenue, but CLFS medians derive from private-payer rates and anchor commercial negotiation. The precise 2026 mechanics and cap application warrant confirmation.

**MolDx/Z-code gating.** Palmetto GBA's program governs coverage for many molecular tests; laboratories must obtain Z-codes and submit dossiers with no assurance of coverage. Three RaDaR ST submissions are pending.

**Client-billed channel exposure (71%).** Provider consolidation, group-purchasing fees, and hospital in-sourcing bear directly on the largest channel.

**Impairment risk against negative tangible book.** Goodwill of $524.0M plus $271.3M of intangibles equals 101% of book equity. The 10-K states that FY2025 included "material impairment charges related to goodwill and certain intangible assets" and warns of more. Single-segment goodwill testing at high aggregation delays recognition but makes any eventual charge larger. Goodwill declined only ~$0.3M through H1 2026, so the FY2025 charges were predominantly intangible (InVisionFirst-Lung wind-down, inventory write-off, disposal groups held for sale, $20.0M of it in Q2 2025). The counterargument: negative tangible book is normal and largely uninformative for an asset-light services business whose value resides in payer contracts, menu breadth, and channel relationships, with $175.4M of receivables and ~$80.9M of PP&E as the real collateral.

**Newly disclosed remote-cytology constraints.** CMS guidance dated September 23, 2025 ended pandemic-era enforcement discretion for remote cytology screening on digital slides, requiring appropriate CLIA certification for remote sites by March 2026; New York's DOH permits remote review of digital cytology images only at sites holding their own NY permit and separate CLIA certificate effective March 23, 2026. This bears directly on NeoGenomics' distributed non-processing analysis sites and appears new relative to prior filings.

**LIMS implementation.** An in-flight, phased Laboratory Information Management System rollout continuing "over the next year" is critical to accessioning, result delivery, and billing, with explicit warnings about delays, cost overruns, and design/implementation deficiencies.

**Residual litigation.** The Goldenberg securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on March 13, 2026, but plaintiff filed a notice of appeal to the Second Circuit on April 10, 2026 — still pending.

**Operational single points.** FedEx is the sole point-to-point transport provider; the 10-K discloses no emergency backup generator at the Tampa, Nashville, Atlanta, or Phoenix dry labs; Fort Myers headquarters has been and may again be affected by severe weather. Tariffs on imported reagents and sequencers carry a dedicated risk factor.

Net of the two resolved overhangs and the termed-out maturity, the live binary set remains substantial. **Risk factors 4.0.**

One benign reading of elevated near-term vol with call-heavy skew is takeout speculation. A $2.1B pure-play oncology lab is a plausible target. That optionality is genuine and is part of why this is a REDUCE rather than an AVOID. It is not a thesis.

Short interest of 12.7% of float (13.76M shares, 3.6 days to cover) cuts both ways: it evidences informed skepticism but also means adverse momentum can be violent, which argues for trimming into strength rather than shorting.

FY2025 carried approximately $3.5M of leadership transition costs (severance, retention, search), with $2.831M in H1 2025 and $0.563M of retention cost still running in H1 2026. Compensation alignment reads poorly on the disclosed metrics. The fair counterargument is that EBITDA and revenue are defensible metrics for a turnaround emerging from a $(48)M EBITDA trough, and the 10-K equity footnote references PSUs carrying a market condition (recalled at roughly 552,000 units, ~241,000 forfeited in 2026) that would partially offset the criticism. Insider activity is one-directional. A ~79.5% position reduction by the chief accounting officer at the high is small in absolute dollars ($321K), may have been executed under a 10b5-1 plan, and permits no inference about motive, but it is the wrong direction relative to the market's enthusiasm.

Offsetting genuinely well-executed capital allocation: retiring $276M of principal for $262.9M cash (a 4.7% discount), repurchasing $25M of stock at approximately $10.49, terming out to 2032 at 0.75%, and cutting G&A 11.3% while reinvesting into a dedicated pathology/oncology field organization (S&M +13.3%). Netting design flaws and churn against demonstrated recent execution: **4.5.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($24, +44.8%)

The mix runway is longer than skeptics assume because NGS penetration in community oncology remains low, and RaDaR ST secures MolDx coverage on one or more of the three pending submissions, opening a recurring surveillance volume pool that breaks the 2% volume ceiling. Permanent cost removal compounds: ~$11M of annualized IP litigation gone, G&A running 11% below prior year, no corporate integrity agreement to fund. FY2027 revenue reaches $920M with adjusted EBITDA above $110M as the 48% adjusted gross margin flows through a flat cost base, and the market pays 3.6x revenue for a de-risked, profitable oncology lab. Cash interest of $2.5M annually against a 2032 maturity means the balance sheet is a non-issue, and capped calls neutralize dilution to $20.98.
