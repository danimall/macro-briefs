## Executive Summary

Rogers Corporation is a specialty engineered materials manufacturer emerging from a three-year revenue decline cycle. Q1 2026 marked a clear operational turning point: revenue grew 5.2% YoY (the first positive quarter since FY2022), gross margins expanded 230bps to 32.2%, and adjusted EPS of $0.75 beat consensus by 10%. The balance sheet carries zero debt, $196M in cash, and a 4.0x current ratio, while GAAP results show net losses driven by non-cash impairments.

The investment case centers on whether this recovery is sustainable. Three consecutive earnings beats, accelerating estimate revisions (+20-24% on the current quarter over 90 days), and Q2 2026 guidance implying continued sequential improvement all support the recovery narrative. Against this: the stock has appreciated 123% from its 52-week low of $61.17, the CEO position remains interim after 9+ months, the curamik® power substrate business suffered a catastrophic competitive failure in China (full $67.3M goodwill write-off), and 41% revenue exposure to APAC (primarily China) creates persistent trade-policy overhang.

At ~$140 per share, Rogers trades at approximately 38x FY2026E EPS ($3.64) and 32x FY2027E EPS ($4.37). This multiple is stretched relative to underlying earnings power but potentially justified if the recovery trajectory continues through FY2027 and margins normalize toward historical levels. The growth recovery thesis is credible and specific, but the current price already reflects meaningful recovery expectations, limiting asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

Rogers designs and manufactures specialty engineered materials for high-reliability applications across two segments:

**Advanced Electronics Solutions (AES, ~55% revenue):** Circuit materials for 5G/radar, ceramic substrates for power electronics (EV inverters, renewables), and thermal management solutions. Serves automotive (ADAS, EV power), aerospace & defense, and wireless infrastructure markets.

**Elastomeric Material Solutions (EMS, ~43% revenue):** Polyurethane, silicone, and PTFE-based materials for cushioning, sealing, vibration management, and thermal protection across industrial, portable electronics, and automotive applications.

Geographic revenue mix is heavily international: APAC 41% (China alone ~31%), EMEA 29%, Americas 28%. Manufacturing facilities span China, Germany, Belgium, South Korea, Hungary, England, and the U.S. — a "local-for-local" structure that partially insulates from direct tariff impacts.

## Growth Analysis

**The cyclical recovery thesis rests on four pillars:**

1. **End-market normalization:** Inventory corrections in EV/HEV and wireless infrastructure that depressed 2023-2025 demand appear to have run their course. Industrial and electronics/communications drove Q1 2026 growth. Automotive remains soft but stabilizing.

2. **Restructuring savings:** $25M in targeted FY2025 cost savings are now annualizing. Belgium facility wind-down completed Q4 2025. These structural cost reductions provide operating leverage as revenue recovers.

3. **New design wins:** Management noted Chinese power module customers qualifying Rogers' China-manufactured curamik® substrates. Design-in cycles in specialty materials typically create 2-3 year revenue tails once qualification is achieved.

4. **Estimate momentum:** Current quarter estimates have risen 24.4% over 30 days and 20.3% over 90 days. FY2027 estimates are up 13.3% over 90 days. This positive revision velocity typically precedes further upward revisions as the cycle matures.

**Growth constraints:** Automotive EV adoption uncertainty remains a headwind for the AES power electronics business. The curamik® competitive failure in China demonstrates that growth in power substrates will be harder to capture than originally planned — local competitors have scaled capacity, and Rogers is now consolidating rather than expanding. FY2026 consensus of $3.64 EPS implies roughly a quadrupling from FY2025 adjusted earnings, which relies heavily on continued margin recovery and no trade-war disruption.

## Valuation Assessment

At $140, Rogers trades at:
- 38.5x FY2026E EPS ($3.64)
- 32.1x FY2027E EPS ($4.37)
- ~2.8x EV/Sales (FY2025)
- ~20-23x FY2026E EV/EBITDA (assuming 12-14% EBITDA margin recovery)

**Peer context:** The peer median P/E of 47.7x suggests the specialty electronic components sector generally trades at premium multiples. BDC at 19.3x represents the value end; Corning at 87.4x the growth end. Rogers' 38x on recovering earnings is not extreme within this peer set but offers limited margin of safety.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target of $150 (median $150), range $135-$165. The stock at $140 sits within the target range and 7% below the mean — limited analyst-implied upside relative to consensus.

**The valuation challenge:** The stock has already re-rated from $61 to $140 as the market front-ran the recovery. At 38x current-year earnings, the price already discounts meaningful further recovery. The upside case requires FY2027 estimates continuing to rise (toward $5+ EPS) to justify current levels on a forward-looking basis.

**Key question: what is normalized earnings power?** At peak (FY2022), Rogers generated ~$7.50 EPS on $971M revenue. At a normalized 12-14% EBITDA margin on $850-900M revenue (achievable by FY2027-2028), EPS could reach $5.50-6.50. At 25x normalized EPS, fair value would be $137-162 — essentially the current range.

## Competitive Landscape

Rogers occupies a niche position in specialty engineered materials where competition varies by segment:

**AES competitive dynamics:**
- Circuit materials (5G, radar): Strong position with proprietary formulations, but facing commodity pressure as Chinese base station build cycles mature
- Power substrates (curamik®): Competitive position severely damaged. Chinese competitors scaled rapidly, forcing full goodwill impairment and German facility consolidation. Rogers now must compete on technology differentiation in a market trending toward commoditization.
- Design-in moats provide stickiness but don't prevent margin compression from new entrants

**EMS competitive dynamics:**
- Broader competitive set including DuPont, 3M materials divisions, and Asian specialty companies
- Margin compression from 38.4% to 34.2% in FY2025 suggests intensifying competition or weaker mix
- Recovery to 35.4% in Q1 2026 partially reverses, but the trajectory is unclear

**China competitive risk — structural:** Rogers' risk factors explicitly note China's "reducing dependence on foreign suppliers" industrial policy. The curamik® outcome demonstrates this risk materializes faster than expected. Whether similar localization pressure emerges in other product lines (circuit materials, elastomers) represents an ongoing threat to the ~31% of revenue derived from China.

**Positive differentiation:** Rogers' products are designed into customer systems with qualification cycles creating switching costs. The company competes on technical service and reliability rather than price. This works in new technology applications (next-gen radar, 5G mmWave) but provides diminishing protection as technologies mature and more suppliers qualify.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **China/Trade exposure (HIGH):** Temporary tariff suspension through November 2026 provides reprieve, but structural decoupling risk persists. If U.S.-China tensions escalate beyond the current suspension, Rogers faces both demand destruction (customers shifting away) and competitive displacement (Chinese suppliers qualifying in Rogers' applications).

2. **Leadership vacuum (MODERATE-HIGH):** An interim CEO for 9+ months is unusual and suggests either strategic uncertainty or difficulty attracting permanent leadership. Continued 8-K filings for executive departures (most recently May 7, 2026) compound the concern. Strategic direction is unclear until a permanent CEO articulates a multi-year plan.

3. **Recovery priced in (MODERATE):** Any stumble in Q2/Q3 execution — whether from tariff impacts, slower EV demand, or competitive losses — could trigger 20-30% drawdown given elevated expectations and 38x P/E providing no margin of error.

4. **Remaining goodwill ($249M in EMS):** If EMS margins remain compressed (34-36% vs. historical 38-40%), this could face impairment testing pressure.

5. **PFAS regulatory exposure:** Use of PFAS chemicals in some product lines creates emerging liability risk as U.S. and EU regulations tighten. Quantification is difficult, but product reformulation costs and potential market access restrictions represent tail risk.

## Conclusion

Rogers Corporation presents a credible cyclical recovery story supported by improving fundamentals: first revenue growth in 3 years, expanding margins, strong FCF generation, pristine balance sheet, and accelerating estimate revisions. The 12-month thesis is specific — continued recovery toward normalized earnings power of $5-6+ EPS as end markets heal and restructuring benefits flow through.

However, at $140 (38x FY2026E, 32x FY2027E) the stock has already discounted meaningful recovery. The leadership vacuum, China concentration risk in a hostile trade environment, and demonstrated competitive failure in power substrates create execution risk that the valuation does not adequately compensate. Elevated options IV and backwardated term structure suggest informed participants share this concern about near-term risk.

HOLD at current levels. The growth thesis justifies maintaining positions for holders, but new money should wait for either a valuation pullback below $115-120 or positive catalysts (permanent CEO, sustained estimate momentum pushing FY2027E above $5.00) that would improve the risk/reward asymmetry.
