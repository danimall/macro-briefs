## Executive Summary

Ryder System has delivered a 62% price appreciation over the past year, driven by a Q1 2026 earnings beat, raised guidance, and growing investor confidence that the multi-year freight downturn is nearing its end. At $244, the stock trades at approximately 16.7x FY2026 consensus comparable EPS of $14.59 — a mid-cycle valuation that is reasonable but no longer cheap relative to the embedded growth trajectory. The analyst consensus target range of $245–$290 confirms limited near-term upside from current levels.

The investment case rests on two pillars: (1) operating leverage into a freight recovery, with rental utilization at 70% (vs. historical 75%+) and used vehicle pricing potentially bottoming, and (2) the ongoing structural shift toward contractual revenue streams in Supply Chain Solutions and Dedicated Transportation that dampen cyclicality. Estimate revisions are trending positively (+4.1% for FY2026, +5.4% for FY2027 over 90 days), confirming improving fundamentals.

The primary risk is that the stock has already priced in a meaningful recovery without one having fully materialized. Freight volumes remain depressed, used vehicle pricing is still below normalized levels, and the company carries $7.6B in debt with a rising effective interest rate (now 5.2%). At $244, essentially at the analyst consensus low target, the asymmetry favors holding existing positions while waiting for either a pullback to create an entry or a freight recovery to justify further upside.

## Company Overview

Ryder System operates three integrated segments serving the North American commercial fleet market:

**Fleet Management Solutions (FMS, 38% of revenue):** Full-service leasing (ChoiceLease), commercial vehicle rental, maintenance services, fuel, and used vehicle sales. The segment operates ~184,900 vehicles across 789 locations. This is the most cyclically sensitive segment, with earnings tied to rental utilization and used vehicle residual values.

**Supply Chain Solutions (SCS, 43% of revenue):** End-to-end logistics including distribution management, transportation brokerage, e-commerce/last-mile delivery, and contract manufacturing. Operates 319 warehouses totaling 105 million square feet. Key verticals include omnichannel retail, automotive, consumer packaged goods, and industrial/healthcare.

**Dedicated Transportation Solutions (DTS, 18% of revenue):** Turnkey transportation solutions combining dedicated vehicles, professional drivers, and fleet management for 210 customer accounts. This $31B outsourced market has significant penetration upside from a $500B total addressable market.

The company completed a CEO transition in March 2026, with John Diez (previously President/COO and before that CFO) succeeding Robert Sanchez, who moved to Executive Chair.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**

Total revenue of $12.7B in FY2025 was essentially flat year-over-year. However, comparable EPS grew 8% to $12.92, driven by lease pricing discipline, maintenance cost management, and $519M in share repurchases. The disconnect between flat revenue and growing EPS reflects Ryder's contractual base providing a floor while transactional revenue (rental, used vehicles) remains depressed.

Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.54 beat the $2.32 consensus by 9.6%, prompting management to raise full-year guidance to $14.05–$14.80 (midpoint $14.43). Consensus now sits at $14.59 for FY2026 and $17.49 for FY2027, implying 20% EPS growth next year as freight recovery takes hold.

**Free Cash Flow Inflection:**

Free cash flow improved from negative $54M (2023) to $133M (2024) to $946M (2025), driven primarily by reduced capex ($2.1B vs. $3.3B in 2023). Management guides to ~$800M FCF in 2026. At the current market cap of $9.4B, this represents an 8.5% FCF yield — attractive for a company with structural growth characteristics, though partially explained by depressed investment levels that will need to normalize.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of $7.6B against equity of $3.1B produces a 250% D/E ratio. This is structural for an asset-heavy leasing company and not indicative of distress — Ryder maintains BBB+/Baa2 ratings with Stable/Positive outlooks, has $936M in available credit facilities, and generates $2.6B in operating cash flow. The Altman Z-Score of 0.92 is a model failure when applied to leasing businesses.

The more relevant concern is the rising cost of debt: effective interest rate increased from 4.4% (2023) to 5.2% (2025), adding approximately $108M in annual interest expense over two years. In the current macro environment with potential for further rate increases, this trajectory bears monitoring.

**Used Vehicle Market:**

Used vehicle sales produced net losses of $22M in 2025 (improved from $72M in 2024 and $196M in 2023). Management stated residual estimates are at "historically low levels," and Q1 2026 guidance cited improved used vehicle sales as a positive driver. A 10% reduction in residual values would add ~$340M in depreciation over remaining fleet life — the single largest earnings sensitivity.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth (12 months):** Consensus estimates imply 13% comparable EPS growth in FY2026 ($14.59 vs. $12.92) and 20% in FY2027 ($17.49). The key drivers are: (1) used vehicle market stabilization/recovery, (2) rental utilization recovery from 70% toward historical 75%+, (3) continued SCS contract wins (record sales activity in 2025), and (4) DTS margin expansion from Cardinal Logistics acquisition synergies.

**Secular growth (3-5 years):** The outsourcing penetration story is compelling. Of ~5 million privately-owned commercial vehicles in the U.S., only ~1 million are leased from third parties. Supply chain complexity, technology requirements, and sustainability mandates all support increasing outsourcing rates over time. Management's shift toward contractual revenue (SCS grew from 6.4% to 8.7% EBT margin over three years) reduces cyclicality while maintaining growth optionality.

**Cyclical recovery leverage:** The embedded operating leverage is substantial. With rental fleet 11% smaller and utilization at 70%, a normalized demand environment could add several hundred million in incremental EBIT without proportional capital investment. Used vehicle inventory of 9,500 units (above the 7,000–9,000 target) represents additional conversion potential if pricing recovers.

**Estimate revision momentum:** FY2026 estimates up 4.1% over 30 days and 3.0% over 90 days; FY2027 up 4.6% and 5.4% respectively. This is positive but does not reach the 30%+ threshold that would signal a major re-rating. The revision trend is gradual and consistent with a slow freight recovery rather than a sharp cyclical turn.

## Valuation Assessment

At $244, Ryder trades at:
- 16.7x FY2026E comparable EPS ($14.59)
- 14.0x FY2027E comparable EPS ($17.49)
- 20.2x trailing EPS ($11.98 GAAP per Phase 1 data)
- ~3.3x EV/EBITDA on $2.87B comparable EBITDA (2025)
- 8.5% FCF yield on $800M FY2026E FCF

**Relative to peers:** The peer median P/E of 59.6x is misleading — it reflects deeply depressed earnings at trucking companies (KNX at 335x, SAIA at 48x, Landstar at 53x). Ryder's business model (asset-heavy leasing with contractual revenue) is structurally different from asset-light brokers and LTL carriers. A more appropriate comparison is Penske (private) or the broader industrials universe, where 16-17x forward earnings is approximately fair for mid-single-digit organic growth with cyclical recovery optionality.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus range $245–$290, mean $261, median $257. The current price of $244 sits at the low end of this range. Analyst upgrades would be needed to provide a meaningful re-rating catalyst.

**Valuation verdict:** At $244, the stock is fairly valued for a company delivering low-teens EPS growth with a clear path to 20% growth in FY2027. The valuation is not stretched on forward estimates, but it no longer offers the margin of safety that existed at $150–$190. The risk/reward is approximately symmetric: base case upside to $260–$270 (7-11%) vs. downside to $200–$210 (14-18%) if the freight recovery disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

Ryder's competitive position is stable to improving:

**Strengths:** One of few providers offering port-to-door integration across fleet management, supply chain, and dedicated transportation. The $1.5 trillion U.S. logistics market has only ~$234B outsourced, suggesting significant secular tailwind. 789 maintenance locations create a physical moat for full-service lease customers. SCS achieved record new business sales in 2025, indicating market share gains.

**Vulnerabilities:** Finance lessors with lower cost of capital can underprice on pure fleet leasing. OEM-direct programs (particularly electric vehicle programs from manufacturers like Daimler Truck, Volvo) could disintermediate traditional fleet managers. Large logistics companies (XPO, J.B. Hunt, Penske) compete across multiple dimensions. Technology disruption from autonomous vehicles and warehouse automation represents a longer-term structural threat.

**Trajectory:** Improving. The pivot toward integrated solutions (combining fleet + supply chain + dedicated) creates switching costs that pure finance lessors cannot replicate. Customer retention rates in ChoiceLease remain high, and the SCS margin expansion from 6.4% to 8.7% over three years demonstrates improving operational quality.

## Risk Assessment

**Freight Market Extension (HIGH probability, MODERATE-HIGH impact):** The freight downturn has persisted for nearly two years. If macroeconomic conditions deteriorate further — particularly given economic uncertainty and potential trade disruption — rental utilization could decline below 70% and used vehicle pricing could resume declines. The 10% residual sensitivity ($340M additional depreciation) is the most quantifiable risk.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** Further rate increases would add approximately $38M in annual interest expense per 50bp increase on variable/rolling maturities. The company's commercial paper program is particularly exposed.

**Tariff and Trade Policy (MODERATE probability, LOW-MODERATE impact):** Recent tariffs on imported vehicles and parts have "modestly increased" maintenance costs per the 10-K. Broader trade disruption could reduce freight volumes — a second-order effect that matters more than the direct cost impact.

**CEO Transition Execution (LOW probability, MODERATE impact):** While the Diez succession appears well-planned (internal promotion, deep institutional knowledge), any strategy pivot or execution stumble during the transition could impact results. Q2 2026 will be the first full quarter under Diez.

**Customer Concentration (LOW probability, HIGH impact):** Top 10 customers represent approximately one-third of SCS and DTS revenue each. Automotive plant shutdowns in Q4 2025 demonstrated this vulnerability. Extended shutdowns in key verticals would pressure both segments simultaneously.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows limited liquidity (only 136 contracts across 4 expirations) but a 3:1 put/call volume ratio on the nearest expiry. The 6-month implied move of ±21% (vs. 33.9% historical volatility) suggests the market is pricing reduced uncertainty relative to recent realized volatility. The thin options market limits signal reliability, but elevated put activity on a stock near 52-week highs suggests some participants are hedging against a pullback.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($270–$290, +11% to +19%):**
Freight market recovery materializes in H2 2026, driving rental utilization back to 75%+. Used vehicle pricing stabilizes and begins recovering, eliminating the residual value drag and potentially creating tailwinds if estimates prove conservative. SCS continues winning contracts at record pace. FY2027 EPS reaches $18–$19, and the stock re-rates to 15–16x forward earnings. Share repurchases (guided $400M+) provide additional per-share growth.

**Bear Case ($190–$210, -14% to -22%):**
Freight recession extends through 2026 as macroeconomic uncertainty (trade disruption, elevated rates) depresses commercial activity. Used vehicle prices resume declining, forcing additional residual value writedowns. Rising interest expense compresses margins. FY2026 comparable EPS comes in at $13–$13.50 (below low end of guidance). The stock de-rates to 14–15x earnings as cyclical investors exit.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Price pullback to $200–$215 (13.5–14.5x FY2026E EPS), restoring asymmetric upside
- Q2 2026 rental utilization improvement above 73%, confirming freight recovery
- Used vehicle pricing turning positive YoY (currently still negative), which would trigger estimate upgrades

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 EPS miss with guidance reduction below $13.50 comparable EPS
- Rental utilization declining below 68%, signaling accelerating freight weakness
- Credit rating downgrade to BBB- or lower (would impair commercial paper access)
- Sustained increase in borrowing costs that makes incremental debt uneconomic relative to returns

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected July 2026) provides the most important data point on freight recovery trajectory and new CEO's strategic direction.

## Conclusion

Ryder System is a well-run company with structural competitive advantages and significant operating leverage into a freight recovery. The constraint is timing and price: at $244 (up 62% in 12 months), the stock has already priced in considerable improvement. Forward estimates are rising (+4-5% over 90 days) but not at a pace that signals the market is systematically underestimating the recovery.

The prior two HOLD recommendations remain appropriate. The score trajectory shows stability at 6.0 overall, with management quality appropriately elevated after the smooth CEO transition and valuation compressed after the significant appreciation. The stock offers a legitimate path to $270–$290 if the freight cycle turns decisively, but also carries meaningful downside risk ($190–$210) if the recovery disappoints, particularly given elevated leverage in a rising-rate environment. Hold existing positions; establish new ones only on pullbacks to $200–$215 where the risk/reward materially improves.
