## Executive Summary

Amazon's Q2 2026 report resolved the central question hanging over the stock since February — whether the AI capital program was buying growth or destroying returns — in favor of growth, at least on the revenue line. AWS grew 36.8% to $42.232B, the fastest since late 2021, against street expectations near 31%, and did so while expanding segment operating margin from 32.91% to 39.36% (38.05% after stripping $551M of unrealized energy-derivative gains). Consolidated revenue rose 20% to $200.606B and operating income 43% to $27.461B. Advertising, at $19.809B in the quarter and roughly $80B annualized, grew 26.2%. The market re-rated the shares roughly 15% from a $235.50 close on July 30 to $270.50, within 3% of the $278.56 all-time high, and within six months moved from punishing capex (the stock traded near $196–$200 in mid-February on capex-guidance shock) to rewarding it.

Three things happened simultaneously and all three are documented in the filings. AWS accelerated and expanded margins. Reported net income roughly doubled, to $62.647B for the quarter and $5.75 in diluted EPS. And trailing-twelve-month free cash flow turned negative, at −$7.604B against +$18.184B a year earlier. The second of those is largely not an operating result: Q2 "other income, net" was +$53.415B, of which $50.486B was upward fair-value marks on privately held equity, primarily Amazon's nonvoting preferred in Anthropic. On a trailing basis, roughly $80.4B of $175.5B of pretax income — about 46% — came from non-cash investment revaluation. Core pretax income excluding that line is approximately $95.07B, which cross-checks against operating income of $93.712B plus $4.679B of interest income less $3.331B of interest expense.

The reported ~22x trailing P/E is unusable, and the honest multiples are less flattering. On core trailing earnings of roughly $6.54–$6.98 per share the stock trades at 39–41x. On stated consensus of $8.76 for the current year it trades at 30.9x, and 26.9x the $10.04 next-year figure — implying 14.6% forward EPS growth and a PEG near 2.1. That is more expensive on forward earnings than either Microsoft or Alphabet, both of which generate positive free cash flow. The operating story is better than it was three months ago; the entry price is worse. We keep the position and stop adding.

## Company Overview

Amazon reports three segments. North America generated Q2 revenue of $116.2B (+16%) and $9.1B of operating income; International $42.2B (+15%) and $1.7B; AWS $42.2B (+36.8%) and $16.621B. The profit concentration is the defining structural fact: in FY2025 AWS produced 18.0% of revenue ($128.725B of $716.924B) but 57.0% of consolidated operating income ($45.606B of $79.975B). In Q2 2026 that widened to 21% of net sales and 60.5% of segment operating income.

Product-line disaggregation shows where retail growth originates. Online stores grew 14.6% to $70.432B, third-party seller services 15.9% to $46.780B, subscriptions 12.5% to $13.730B, physical stores 3.6% to $5.794B. Advertising services grew 26.2% to $19.809B, making it the second-fastest-growing major line and, at high incremental margin, a plausible contributor to North America's margin improvement from 7.51% to 7.85% of segment revenue. Amazon does not disclose advertising segment profitability, so that attribution is inference.

Custom silicon is the differentiated asset that emerges from the 2026 filings. Both the OpenAI and Anthropic contract expansions are described as including contractual obligations tied to the performance of AWS chips, designed by Amazon's Annapurna Labs subsidiary. That vertical integration is the most credible mechanism by which AWS can grow 37% while expanding margin against a 67% year-over-year increase in segment depreciation and amortization ($4.844B to $8.076B).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and operating income.** Trailing revenue is $775.680B, derived as FY2025 $716.924B less H1 2025 $323.369B plus H1 2026 $382.125B. Trailing operating income is $93.712B, a 12.08% margin against 11.15% in FY2025. That comparison flatters the trend: FY2025 absorbed roughly $5.2B of identified charges — a $2.5B FTC settlement on Prime enrollment and $2.7B of severance — so adjusted FY2025 margin was closer to 11.9%. Underlying margin improvement is real but narrower than the headline.

The same adjustment matters for guidance. Q3 2026 operating income is guided to $22.5–26.5B against a reported $17.4B in Q3 2025, implying +29% to +52%. But that base contained the $2.5B settlement and roughly $1.8B of the severance. On an estimated ~$21.7B adjusted base, implied growth is roughly +4% to +22%. Amazon has not restated segment detail, so that adjusted base is an approximation, but the direction is unambiguous: the underlying guide is far more modest than the headline range suggests.

**Cash flow is where the tension sits.** Trailing operating cash flow of $161.403B grew 33% from $121.137B. Trailing purchases of property and equipment were $173.028B gross, $169.007B net of proceeds and incentives, giving trailing free cash flow on Amazon's own definition of −$7.604B. Cash capex understates the build: increases in property and equipment acquired but remaining unpaid or unfinanced rose $29.267B over the trailing period, operating-lease asset additions were $24.897B, and total net additions to property and equipment in H1 2026 alone were $118.648B, of which $90.120B (76%) was AWS. Capex is running at 22.3% of revenue and roughly 2.3x trailing D&A of $75.200B. Depreciation will therefore continue climbing sharply for several years as an arithmetic consequence of assets already committed, independent of demand.

Full-year 2026 capex has been reported at approximately $220B, attributed partly to higher memory costs. That figure comes from the earnings release and call rather than the filed statements; the 10-Q corroborates direction ("expected to increase in 2026") and mechanism, adding "resource and supply volatility, including for memory chips" to its forward-looking factors. Memory cost inflation is a live sector input that lands on Amazon as capex per unit of capacity.

**Financing.** Long-term debt rose from $65.648B at year-end to $128.894B at June 30. H1 issuance included $37.0B of USD notes (coupons 3.85%–6.05%), €14.5B (~$16.550B), CHF 2.8B (~$3.487B) and C$14.0B (~$9.853B), with a further $25.0B of USD notes at 4.60%–6.25% priced after quarter-end and a new $17.5B undrawn delayed-draw term loan committed in June. Roughly $92B of new notes plus $17.5B of committed bank capacity has been mobilized in seven months against a starting debt balance of $65.6B. Interest expense rose from $516M in Q2 2025 to $1.314B, up 155%. Cash interest paid of $736M in the quarter was well below accrued expense, implying material capitalization into construction in progress, a timing effect that suppresses current expense and re-emerges as depreciation.

Total contractual commitments are $650.034B, including $220.309B of debt principal and interest, $116.350B of operating lease liabilities, $130.065B of unconditional purchase obligations, and $137.214B of leases not yet commenced (of which $93.476B falls beyond 2030). Present-value lease liabilities on balance sheet are $109.771B. Against $122.988B of cash and marketable securities and $93.7B of trailing operating income, net leverage of roughly $145B is not a solvency problem. A recalculated Altman Z using the June balance sheet and market equity is approximately 4.71 — comfortably safe, and well above a vendor screen output of 2.22 that substituted book for market equity. Two screen signals are genuine: a current ratio of ~1.03 and asset turnover falling from 0.88 to 0.708 as the balance sheet inflates faster than revenue.

**The most durable positive in the filings is cash taxes.** Cash taxes paid fell to $6.635B on a trailing basis from $11.788B, and to $8.3B in FY2025 from $12.3B in FY2024 despite pretax income rising from roughly $61B to $97.3B. Drivers are 2025 legislation restoring 100% accelerated depreciation and immediate domestic R&D expensing, plus IRS Notice 2026-7 (February 18, 2026), which the 10-Q says will produce a significant decrease in 2024 and 2025 cash taxes. For a company deploying $170B+ annually into depreciable assets, immediate expensing is a structurally large and multi-year cash shield that partially offsets the negative-FCF headline.

Financial health score of 6.0 reflects the combination: very high resilience (operating earnings cover everything, no note covenants, 14.2-year weighted-average maturity, ample liquidity) against a capital structure being stretched deliberately into a higher long-end funding environment, with negative cash generation and a current ratio near 1.0.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue growth case is strong and getting stronger at the segment that matters. AWS accelerated from 20% in FY2025 to 36.8% in Q2, with 514bp of underlying margin expansion, while reportedly running capacity-short. Remaining performance obligations, primarily AWS, on contracts with original terms exceeding one year were approximately $496B at June 30 with a 6.4-year weighted-average life on long-term contracts — 3.34x trailing AWS revenue of $148.404B. That is a real contracted book, though it explicitly excludes contracts of one year or less and is distinct from unearned revenue of roughly $24.9B, which was essentially flat versus year-end.

Two very large expansions underpin it. In Q1, AWS and OpenAI expanded an existing $38.0B commitment by $100.0B over eight years. In Q2, AWS and Anthropic expanded their arrangement by more than $100.0B over ten years. Both include obligations tied to AWS chip performance, which implies conditionality whose specifics are undisclosed.

The constraint on the equity case is that revenue growth is not translating proportionally into per-share earnings. Consensus has current-year EPS at $8.76 and next year at $10.04 — 14.6% growth. Depreciation (AWS D&A +67% YoY), interest expense (+155%), and Amazon Leo satellite costs (roughly $1B of incremental year-over-year cost flagged for Q1, with launch deposits expensed to Technology and Infrastructure on launch rather than capitalized) absorb much of the operating gain. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: +2.9% over 90 days for both current and next year, +1.0% and +0.9% over 30 days. This is steady, unremarkable upward drift on an already-rich multiple, not the revision velocity that signals a mispriced repricing.

The earnings track record is mixed — two beats and two misses across four quarters, including a 3.1% miss on the street's operating EPS basis in one of the last two reports even as revenue and operating income came in strong. Consensus of $1.91 for the coming quarter appears to require delivery at or above the top of the $22.5–26.5B operating income guide at a favorable tax rate, which is a demanding bar rather than a conservative one.

Growth score 7.3: excellent top-line and segment-mix trajectory, tempered by mid-teens per-share earnings growth, a flattered guidance comparison, and unremarkable revision velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $270.50 the shares trade at:

- **30.9x** stated current-year consensus EPS of $8.76 (the quarterly consensus build sums to roughly $8.16, so the basis is not fully consistent; the honest range is ~31–33x)
- **26.9x** next-year consensus of $10.04
- **39–41x** trailing core EPS of $6.54–$6.98, derived by taxing core pretax income of ~$95.07B at 20–25% on ~10.9B diluted shares
- **~22x** reported GAAP trailing EPS of ~$12.42 — the figure to discard, since roughly 46% of trailing pretax income is non-cash revaluation
- **PEG ~2.1** (30.9x current-year against 14.6% consensus forward EPS growth)
- **No FCF yield support**: trailing free cash flow is negative
- **No capital return**: no dividend, no repurchases in either H1 2025 or H1 2026, $6.1B still unused under a March 2022 authorization

Broadline retail peers are close to useless as comparables — EBAY 25.3x, ETSY 28.0x, OLLI 20.7x, Macy's 10.3x, Kohl's 7.3x, median 20.7x. Amazon's roughly 50% premium to that median is justified by AWS and advertising, but the relevant comparison set is the other hyperscalers, and there Amazon screens as expensive with the weakest cash conversion. Amazon at 26.9x next-year with −$7.6B of trailing FCF is not the cheap way to own cloud AI infrastructure.

Consensus targets run $207 low, $313.07 mean, $315 median, $370 high, with 61 buy/strong-buy ratings against 3 holds. Analyst targets function as floors on stocks that are cheap relative to their growth; at a PEG above 2 they do not. The mean implies roughly 15.7% upside, but the same analysts who raised targets after the print are extrapolating the same AWS acceleration the price already reflects.

Our base case is **$300**, roughly 29.9x next-year EPS of $10.04, the multiple holding as AWS growth durability is confirmed across two more quarters. That is +10.9% from the current price. Bear case is **$205** (-24.2%), roughly 22.8x a reduced $9.00 as capex digestion, depreciation drag, and a private-market mark reversal compress both earnings and multiple; the stock traded near $196–$210 in February on a milder version of exactly that fear. Bull case is **$360** (+33%), 33.5x an above-consensus $10.75 on continued AWS acceleration plus a visible free-cash-flow inflection.

Base-case upside of 11% against 24% bear-case downside is roughly 0.5:1. That asymmetry, not the size of the recent move, is what caps the valuation score at 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is improving, which matters more than level. AWS reaccelerated from 20% to 37% while expanding margin, an unusual combination that points to pricing power under capacity constraint plus a silicon cost advantage. Trainium and the Nitro system, designed in-house, are the plausible explanation for margin expansion against a 67% depreciation increase, and both mega-contracts reference AWS chip performance explicitly. Advertising at +26.2% is taking share in digital advertising against a slower-growing market.

Two qualifications. First, Amazon's own 10-K notes that AI both lowers barriers to competitive entry and makes comparison shopping easier, a two-sided development for a retailer whose moat partly rests on being the default product-search destination. Second, the clearest articulated bear mechanism is efficiency rather than demand: if inference cost per unit of useful output falls faster than usage grows, revenue realized against a $496B long-term RPO could compress even if contracts are honored, and the private valuations underpinning $219.1B of Amazon's balance sheet would face pressure. Reporting in late June described hyperscalers pivoting toward efficiency-focused offerings and open-source models emerging as cheaper alternatives. Against that, Q2 is strong contrary evidence: 37% growth, 500+bp of margin expansion, and capacity described as short. Capacity constraint implies the near-term ceiling is supply, not demand.

Competitive position score 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The reflexive loop is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Anthropic and OpenAI carry $219.1B of value at June 30 — 20.0% of total assets and 7.6% of market capitalization — of which only $7.4B of the broader $229.7B investment book is publicly traded. The same two counterparties are the largest contributors to the $496B contracted RPO, and Anthropic is additionally the borrower under a facility not to exceed $20.0B (reduced to $15.0B after the $5.0B Series H investment) where availability unlocks as Amazon reaches compute-delivery milestones. Amazon has funded $50.0B into OpenAI (including $21.3B after quarter-end) and $18B+ into Anthropic. If AI compute demand disappoints, AWS bookings, the carrying value of the stakes, and utilization of a rapidly depreciating AWS asset base all deteriorate together. These exposures are correlated, not diversifying. Anthropic is also a model supplier to Amazon for certain consumer products, so the commercial relationship runs in both directions; unconfirmed reporting that Anthropic raised the prices Amazon pays should be treated as unverified.

**2. Level 3 mark asymmetry.** The $50.486B Q2 adjustment reflects observable prices from third-party funding rounds — a better basis than pure modeling, and Microsoft's independently disclosed $3.2B gain on its own Anthropic stake corroborates that a genuine repricing occurred. But private marks under the measurement alternative move on observable transactions, which cluster in up-rounds and are sparse in down-markets. Cumulative unrealized gains on the Anthropic notes in AOCI are roughly $92.0B net of $22.4B of tax; total AOCI rose from $28.230B to $66.287B in six months.

**3. Xockets ITC proceeding.** In May 2026 Xockets filed an ITC complaint against Amazon, AWS, Annapurna Labs, NVIDIA and Microsoft alleging five patents are infringed by EC2 P6e-GB200 UltraServers, DGX Cloud with GB200 on AWS, SageMaker HyperPod, and EKS with P6e-GB200, seeking injunctive relief. The ITC instituted an investigation in June. ITC remedies are exclusion orders on imported articles, aimed at the specific fleet Amazon is expanding. Institution is procedural and co-respondents have aligned defense incentives, but Amazon quantifies no exposure.

**4. Antitrust and privacy.** Motions to dismiss have been granted in part with claims surviving in each US case, one US class certified and three pre-certification; two UK classes certified with a third pending; Canadian certification denied with appeal pending. Plaintiffs seek treble damages and structural relief. Illinois BIPA claims now extend to AWS cloud services and Amazon Connect, a scope expansion from consumer devices to enterprise services.

**5. Tax disputes requiring remittance.** Luxembourg has denied the tax basis of intangibles distributed to the US in 2021, with remittance required on assessment; India asserts tax on cloud-services fees paid to Amazon in the US, also requiring remittance pending resolution, with payments that "could be significant in the aggregate." Italy is newly flagged. Income tax contingencies rose from $6.6B to $7.1B. None is individually quantified.

**6. Quality-of-earnings items in the quarter.** At least $1.19B of identifiable non-recurring or non-cash help: $551M of energy-derivative gains in Technology and Infrastructure (a portfolio covering ~270 million MWh, ~15-year weighted duration, Level 3, with management noting significant judgment) and ~$640M of IEEPA tariff refunds recorded mostly as a reduction to cost of sales — roughly 7% of North America segment operating income, and stated to be the significant majority of expected refunds.

**7. Risk-factor language changes.** The acquisitions-and-investments risk factor gained a bullet stating that "investees may have different strategic priorities than us, and we do not control their decisions regarding strategy, operations, governance, or compliance." The timing, immediately after committing $50B to OpenAI, points to the AI-lab stakes, though routine counsel updating is an alternative reading.

**8. Concentration has not improved.** China-based sellers remain a significant portion of third-party seller and advertising revenue, and China-based suppliers a significant portion of components. Revenue mix is shifting toward advertising and AWS, which is a mix change rather than reduced counterparty concentration; within the contracted AWS book, concentration is rising given two disclosed $100B+ expansions. Amazon discloses no customer-level AWS concentration.

Risk score 5.0: several specific, unquantified, non-diversifying catalysts against a business with enormous liquidity and no covenant risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 51.2% against 31.7% one-year realized looks alarming until you note the nearest expiry is one day away with an implied move of 0.5% — same-day pinning mechanics, corroborated by 117,056 contracts of $272 calls and 63,587 $270 puts trading into that expiry. The informative part of the surface is the back end: 11-day IV of 33.5%, 41-day 30.4%, and 111-day 32.8% with a −3.5% skew. The upward slope from three to six months plus the wider six-month skew indicates some demand for longer-dated downside.

Positioning has flipped decisively bullish since the print: put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.00 to 0.38 as the price moved from $229.91 to $271.58, and put/call volume is 0.33. Unusual flow includes $305 October and $300 September calls at 32–33x open interest. Short interest is 1.1% of float at 1.8 days to cover — no crowding, and no informed bearish positioning. The reading is complacency rather than confirmation: the options market is now positioned for continuation with little protection held, the configuration in which a Q3 guidance disappointment or a capex re-shock produces an outsized move. It does not challenge the fundamental view; it argues against adding at this price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($360, +33%).** AWS growth holds in the mid-30s through 2027 as the $496B contracted book converts, custom silicon sustains 38%+ segment margins against the depreciation ramp, and advertising compounds past $100B annualized at high incremental margin. Immediate expensing of capex plus the R&D provisions keep cash taxes near $7B, so operating cash flow crosses $200B and free cash flow inflects positive in 2027 as capex growth decelerates from 61%. Anthropic and OpenAI reach liquidity events, converting part of the $219.1B carrying value into realizable value. Next-year EPS beats to $10.75 and the market pays 33.5x for confirmed AI-infrastructure durability.

**Bear case ($205, −24%).** Inference efficiency improves faster than usage grows; enterprises rein in AI spend; AWS growth decelerates back toward the low 20s while depreciation, now compounding off a $118.6B six-month build, compresses segment margin. Anthropic or OpenAI raises at a flat or down valuation, and the mark mechanism reverses — a 30% markdown on $219.1B is $66B of pretax reversal, which would swamp a year of operating income growth. Interest expense annualizes past $6B as another $25B+ of notes price at 4.6–6.25%, free cash flow stays negative through 2027, and the multiple compresses to 22–23x. The market paid $196–$210 for this scenario in February; it is not hypothetical.

**Base case ($300, +10.9%).** AWS grows 28–33% through 2027 with margin in the 37–39% band, advertising sustains low-20s growth, and consolidated operating income reaches roughly $115–120B. Next-year EPS lands near consensus $10.04, capex plateaus near $220–240B, free cash flow remains modestly negative in 2026 and turns positive in late 2027, and the multiple holds near 30x. Investment marks are approximately neutral.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — base case $300, +10.9% — but base-case upside of 11% against bear-case downside of 24% is roughly 0.5:1, which does not support incremental capital at $270.50.

**Reassessment timeline.** The October 2026 Q3 report is the decisive event: it tests whether AWS holds above 30% growth, whether the 38% underlying segment margin survives a full quarter of the new depreciation base without energy-derivative help, and whether management's 2026 capex figure moves again. A secondary checkpoint is the next Anthropic or OpenAI funding round, which sets the direction of the mark mechanism.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price at or below **$235–245** with AWS growth still above 30% and next-year consensus at or above $10.00 — restoring roughly 25%+ upside to the $300 base case.
2. Trailing free cash flow turning positive, or a Q3/Q4 report in which operating cash flow growth exceeds capex growth for two consecutive quarters — the single change that would most improve the financial health score.
3. AWS segment operating margin sustained at or above 38% ex-derivative gains for two consecutive quarters alongside growth above 32%, which would justify paying above 30x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. AWS revenue growth decelerating below 25% year over year, or segment margin falling below 34% ex-derivative gains.
2. A downward fair-value adjustment to the Anthropic or OpenAI holdings of $20B or more, or a flat/down funding round at either lab — this would validate the reflexive-loop bear case and simultaneously pressure the RPO's credibility.
3. Q3 or Q4 operating income landing below the low end of guidance, or 2026 capex guidance rising again without a corresponding acceleration in AWS bookings.
4. An adverse ITC determination or exclusion order affecting the P6e-GB200 fleet.

**Monitoring items with no immediate action:** interest expense trajectory (accrued interest annualizing above $5B), the $17.5B term loan draw due on or before September 30, memory-chip cost commentary, and the Globalstar close expected in 2027 (~$10.9B including debt, ~0.22% estimated dilution — immaterial to the thesis).

## Conclusion

The score moves from 6.6 to 6.0 and the recommendation from BUY to HOLD. The trajectory of the underlying business improved this quarter: AWS reaccelerated to 37% with genuine margin expansion, advertising is compounding at 26%, and the contracted book at $496B is 3.34x trailing AWS revenue. Two dimensions moved the other way. Valuation deteriorated because the price is 18% higher than three sessions ago at 30.9x current-year and 26.9x next-year consensus, a PEG near 2.1, and a premium to both Microsoft and Alphabet on forward earnings despite the worst cash conversion of the three. Financial health and risk deteriorated because trailing free cash flow turned negative as a filing fact, long-term debt roughly doubled in six months, and 20% of total assets now sits in two private counterparties who are simultaneously the largest contracted customers of the segment that produces 60% of profit.

None of that makes Amazon a poor business or a short. It makes the risk/reward at $270.50 roughly symmetric-to-unfavorable, in a stock where reported earnings overstate economic earnings by nearly a factor of two and where the options market has stopped paying for protection. The February selloff to near $196–$200 on capex fear and the July rally to $270 on the same capex reinterpreted as growth are the same information priced twice, in opposite directions, within six months. That sentiment volatility argues for owning the position and waiting for the next dislocation rather than paying up into the print that just cleared.

Own it. Add at $235–245, or on demonstrated free-cash-flow inflection.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: peer comparison figures for Microsoft (~23.8x FY27, $67.0B FCF) and Alphabet (~24x, Cloud +63%, $462B RPO), and the Azure/Google Cloud percentage growth figures cited in the Competitive Landscape section, are not present in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed as unverified; the valuation section's relative-cheapness conclusion versus those specific peers now rests on the (supported) observation of negative trailing FCF rather than on unverified peer multiples.
