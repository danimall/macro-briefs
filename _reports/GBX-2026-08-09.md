## Executive Summary

Greenbrier is in the deepest North American railcar production trough in more than a decade, and the company's own numbers confirm it: nine-month FY2026 revenue of $1,870.1M is down 24.6% year over year, deliveries fell 32.7% to 10,700 units, and diluted EPS dropped to $2.21 from $5.18. Management has reset FY2026 revenue guidance from an original $2.7–3.2B to $2.4–2.5B and cut EPS guidance to $3.00–$3.15. Backlog has fallen to 13,800 units and $2.0B from 30,900 units at the FY2023 peak, implying a nine-month book-to-bill of roughly 0.74.

The bull argument is that the floor of the cycle has moved up. FY2026 guidance implies net income of roughly $93–98M on ~$2.45B of revenue, about twice the $46.9M Greenbrier earned in FY2022 on 18% *more* revenue, and a net margin of ~3.9% versus 1.57%. That improvement traces to identifiable actions: in-sourcing component capacity in Mexico, a 30% European headcount reduction with capacity preserved, closure of Polish and Turkish plants, and a growing leasing mix that carried a 69.4% gross margin in FY2025. The balance sheet supports the thesis: recourse debt is only 0.47x Greenbrier equity, liquidity is roughly $887M, 87% of debt is fixed or swapped, and the company placed $300M of non-recourse GBXL I ABS notes at a 5.14% weighted average in February 2026 and extended its leasing term facility to 2032 in May 2026, both executed with earnings halved.

The problem is price and the quality of what is being capitalized. At $47.22 the stock trades at 15.6x the $3.03 FY2026 consensus, 12.1x a FY2027 consensus of $3.90 that has been cut 4.1% in 90 days, and 0.93x stated book value of $50.85. Trinity Industries, the direct railcar comparable, trades at 10.8x with a 3.7% yield and sits 1% below its 52-week high. 31% of Greenbrier's nine-month consolidated operating earnings came from $36.7M of asset-disposition gains (versus $16.8M prior year), and the Leasing segment's apparent 24.5% earnings growth is entirely those gains — excluding them, segment operating earnings were $72.4M versus $71.1M, up 1.8%. Two new trade contingencies of unknown size attach to the same Mexican footprint that generated the margin story: a May 2026 CBP Enforce and Protect Act determination that the company evaded antidumping and countervailing duties on freight rail couplers dating to April 2024, and April 2026 Section 232 tariffs on tank cars entering the US. Downgrading to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Greenbrier designs, builds, and leases freight railcars through two reportable segments. **Manufacturing** (FY2025: $2,991.2M revenue, 14.5% gross margin) builds most North American car types except coal cars (covered hoppers, gondolas, boxcars, flat cars, tank cars, intermodal double-stack platforms, and automotive carriers) plus wheel services, AAR-certified maintenance shops, a component-parts business, and a retrofit line. European operations build freight and tank wagons and bogies. **Leasing & Fleet Management** (FY2025: $249.0M revenue, 69.4% gross margin) combines an owned lease fleet with a fee-based management platform covering maintenance management, railcar accounting, car-hire administration, remarketing, and tank-car regulatory services.

The structurally distinctive capability is syndication: building a railcar, attaching a lease, selling the asset-with-lease to a financial investor, and retaining a multi-year management contract. That converts a cyclical build into a fee stream. The offsetting constraint is disclosed in the 10-K: at least twenty North American institutions offer comparable leasing services and "many of them are also customers." Greenbrier competes with the same lessors it sells new and used cars to.

Joint ventures matter for interpreting the financials. Mexican manufacturing JVs are consolidated with a 50% noncontrolling interest; roughly half of the company's ~11,000 employees are in Mexico. Greenbrier-Maxion (Brazil, 60%) is equity-method, not consolidated, and represented ~12% of backlog units at August 31, 2025. Greenbrier-Astra Rail B.V. (~75%) carries a contingently redeemable NCI subject to a put option discussed below.

R&D was $5.5M on $3,240.2M of FY2025 revenue, or 0.17% of sales. This is a capital-and-execution business, not a technology-moat business.

## Financial Analysis

### Quarterly and nine-month deterioration

| | Q3 FY26 | Q3 FY25 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $576.5M | $842.7M | −31.6% |
| Deliveries | 3,200 | 5,200 | −38.5% |
| Aggregate gross margin | 14.1% | 18.0% | −390bp |
| Manufacturing margin | 9.9% | 15.2% | −530bp |
| Earnings from operations | $31.9M | $92.6M | −65.6% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.60 | $1.86 | −67.7% |

Nine-month figures: revenue $1,870.1M vs $2,480.7M; deliveries 10,700 vs 15,900; aggregate margin 13.6% vs 18.7%; Manufacturing margin 9.6% vs 16.0%; operating earnings $118.1M vs $288.0M.

One constructive detail: Q3's 14.1% aggregate gross margin was a sequential improvement from Q2's 11.8% on comparable revenue ($588M), so mix and cost actions are working at the margin even as volume falls.

### Earnings quality — the disposition-gain dependency

Consolidated nine-month net gain on disposition of equipment was $36.7M versus $16.8M, equal to 31% of the $118.1M of consolidated operating earnings. Excluding gains, operating earnings were roughly $81.4M versus $271.2M, a ~70% decline rather than the reported 59%. In the Leasing segment specifically, the reported $108.7M of nine-month operating earnings decomposes as $88.2M gross margin less $15.8M S&A plus $36.3M of gains; the prior year was $90.7M less $19.6M plus $16.2M. Strip the gains and the segment produced $72.4M versus $71.1M.

Proceeds from asset sales rose to $170.3M from $75.4M, and full-year expected proceeds were raised in the 10-Q to ~$175M from ~$115M in the October 10-K. The 10-K discloses that fleet assets are periodically sold to accommodate customer demand, manage risk, and maintain liquidity, so this is a stated element of the model and gains were present (smaller) last year. Harvesting appreciated fleet assets in a weak new-build year is defensible capital allocation. The issue is repeatability at escalating scale: the gain contribution roughly doubled year over year and cannot expand indefinitely. Q3's 19.5% effective tax rate, driven by discrete items against a 32.1% FY2025 rate, is a second non-durable support.

### Cash flow — classification, not distress

Nine-month operating cash flow was $8.1M versus $167.7M. The dominant driver is a $189.9M increase in "Leased railcars for syndication," which sits in operating activities; the balance-sheet line rose from $225.9M at August 31, 2025 to $426.7M at May 31, 2026. These are assets held for sale that convert on syndication. Adding back the build gives roughly $198M of adjusted nine-month operating cash — presented as a sensitivity, not a forecast, because conversion depends on third-party investor appetite. If the syndication market tightens, that $426.7M becomes a liquidity absorber rather than a timing item. A separate $72.7M drag came from accrued payroll falling from $169.0M to $96.3M as bonuses were paid.

Investing activities were a net $16.5M inflow (capex $147.2M against $170.3M of asset sales). Full-year gross capex guidance was *raised* to ~$285M Leasing plus ~$95M Manufacturing from ~$240M plus ~$80M. FY2025 free cash flow was already negative at −$14.7M ($265.7M OCF less $280.4M capex).

### Balance sheet

At May 31, 2026: total assets $4,348.2M; cash $273.7M plus $49.1M restricted; inventories $619.3M; equipment on operating leases net $1,298.4M; PP&E net $708.5M; goodwill $129.9M. Greenbrier shareholders' equity $1,573.2M, NCI $123.1M (down from $165.2M), contingently redeemable NCI $31.9M.

Debt of $1,805.6M splits into $733.3M recourse and $1,072.3M non-recourse. Recourse includes the $373.8M 2.875% convertible due April 2028, a $240.6M corporate term loan, and European and Mexican revolvers. Non-recourse is dominated by $743.5M of GBXL I ABS notes; the warehouse facility is drawn to zero.

Derived: recourse debt/Greenbrier equity 0.47x; net recourse debt $459.6M; total debt/total equity 1.06x; equity/assets 36.2%; book value per share $50.85, so P/B 0.93x. Total liquidity is ~$887M ($273.7M cash plus $613.5M available). This is an appropriate structure for a builder-lessor hybrid: the fleet is financed non-recourse against the assets, and the corporate balance sheet is modestly levered.

One caveat on screens: an Altman Z of 3.12 and uniformly positive Piotroski signals derive from FY2025, the peak year. Re-basing the EBIT/assets and sales/assets terms on FY2026 guidance moves the Z-score to roughly 2.8, inside the grey zone. That reflects depressed EBIT and asset turnover in a trough, not leverage or liquidity impairment, and should not be read as solvency risk. It does mean the headline screen overstates current financial position.

## Growth Analysis

The order book is the decisive datapoint, and it is deteriorating faster than deliveries.

| | Aug-23 | Aug-24 | Aug-25 | May-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backlog units | 30,900 | 26,700 | 16,600 | 13,800 |
| Backlog value | $3,800M | $3,400M | $2,200M | $2,000M |

Units are down 55% from the FY2023 peak and 16.9% in nine months of FY2026 alone. Implied net orders over the nine months are roughly 7,900 units against 10,700 delivered, a book-to-bill near 0.74, approximate because Brazil is equity-method and cancellations and mix affect the balance.

Two partial offsets. First, backlog value per unit rose 9.4%, from $132.5K to $144.9K, consistent with a mix shift toward higher-content cars (tank cars are ~20% of backlog), so dollar backlog fell only 9.1% against a 16.9% unit decline. Second, management cites rising inquiry levels, firmer trucking rates, and intact replacement demand, arguing that macroeconomic uncertainty is deferring rather than destroying orders. Industry forecasts point to fewer than 25,000 North American deliveries in CY2026.

Against that, roughly $720M of the $2.0B backlog (36%, up from ~21%) is earmarked for syndication, so a growing share of backlog conversion depends on financial-investor appetite rather than customer delivery. And Greenbrier's reported backlog is not the same as ASC 606 remaining performance obligations, which total $1,010.1M for railcar sales, $32.7M maintenance, and $143.9M fleet management, with ~$792.3M of railcar-sales obligations expected through 2027.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction: current quarter −8.5% over 30 days and −11.5% over 90; current year −2.7% and −3.7%; next year −1.3% and −4.1%. The FY2027 consensus of $3.90 implies ~29% EPS growth from the FY2026 guide. With backlog units down 16.9% and book-to-bill at 0.74, FY2027 deliveries plausibly fall again from FY2026's ~14,000, which requires simultaneous sharp margin recovery and sustained gain contribution to reach $3.90. That number looks high, and the 90-day revision trend suggests consensus is still working toward it.

The strategic growth vector is the stated goal of doubling recurring revenues by 2028. Progress is harder to verify than it appears. Management describes an owned fleet of ~20,600 railcars at 99% utilization including ~4,400 secondary-market purchases in Q3, while the company's own May 21, 2026 press release described ~16,800 railcars and the FY2025 10-K ~17,000. Over the same period, equipment on operating leases *net declined* from $1,328.5M to $1,298.4M while leased railcars for syndication rose $200.8M. The fleet-count discrepancy is unreconciled from available filings; the net operating-lease decline is fully explainable by depreciation plus $170.3M of asset sales, so it does not by itself prove classification. Investors should not treat the 20,600 figure as evidence of recurring-revenue asset growth without a reconciliation, because syndicated cars generate management fees rather than lease revenue.

**Growth score 3.5.** Revenue, deliveries, backlog, and estimates are all falling simultaneously with no confirmed inflection.

The FY2025 trailing multiple of 7.4x on $6.35 EPS is the number that makes the stock screen distressed, and it is meaningless because it capitalizes peak earnings. The relevant framing is 15.6x guided trough earnings and 0.93x book.

Whether 15.6x trough is undemanding depends on the trough being genuine. Two adjustments argue caution. Stripping the nine-month disposition gains (roughly $0.94/share after tax at the Q3 rate, annualizing toward $1.10–1.20) and normalizing the tax rate toward the low 20s puts "clean" FY2026 EPS closer to $1.90–2.05. On that base the stock is at 23–25x. That does not make Greenbrier expensive on a normalized-cycle view, but it removes the cushion.

Peer context cuts against the cheapness argument. Peer median P/E is 18.5x, but the median is distorted by ASTE at 44.9x and by GATX at 18.5x, a pure lessor with far more stable earnings and not a builder comparable. Trinity Industries, the direct North American railcar peer facing the same trough, trades at 10.8x with a 3.7% yield and 1% off its 52-week high. Greenbrier trades at a premium to its closest comparable on earnings while carrying two unquantified trade contingencies that Trinity's US footprint does not face in the same form.

Sell-side positioning is unambiguous and unusually one-sided: zero buy ratings, one hold, one sell, one strong sell, with a mean target of $45.33 and a median of $42.00 against a $47.22 price. The high target is $52.00. Analyst targets act as floors for inflection stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are falling across every period, so the targets are functioning as a ceiling and the stock trades above the mean.

**Valuation score 4.5.** The 0.93x book and covered 2.88% yield provide real downside support, and 12.1x FY2027 would be attractive if that estimate held. But there is no identified catalyst to re-rate the multiple, the direct comparable is 30% cheaper, the earnings base is flattered, and the forward estimate the bull case depends on is still being revised down.

## Competitive Landscape

Greenbrier self-describes as one of the two largest North American railcar manufacturers, top-tier in Europe, and leading in South America through Greenbrier-Maxion. It does not build coal cars, which insulates it from structural decline in US coal volumes. The integrated model spanning manufacture, maintenance, components, leasing, syndication, and fleet management is difficult to replicate, and syndication capability is the most defensible element.

Trajectory, however, has turned mixed for reasons specific to trade policy. Roughly half of Greenbrier's ~11,000 employees are in Mexico, and expanded Mexican in-sourcing was a stated driver of the 830bp gross-margin expansion between FY2023 and FY2025. That footprint is now the exposure point for both the CBP coupler determination and the Section 232 tank-car tariffs. The 10-Q states directly that "our competitors may be less exposed to tariff impacts or in a better position to mitigate the increased costs of tariffs," an unusual concession of relative disadvantage. A US-footprint-heavy competitor is structurally advantaged under this regime, and the mix shift toward tank cars (~20% of backlog) that helps revenue per unit simultaneously raises the tariff-exposed share.

The lessor channel is both customer and competitor, and with 36% of backlog now earmarked for syndication, backlog conversion increasingly depends on the risk appetite of institutions Greenbrier competes with. In a higher-for-longer rate environment with credit spreads that have repriced at issuance in other sectors, that dependency is a live variable rather than a formality.

**Competitive position score 5.0.** Solid structural position, no share erosion evident, but the trade-policy exposure is a genuine and newly disclosed relative disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**CBP Enforce and Protect Act determination — unquantified and unbounded from available evidence.** CBP initiated an investigation with interim measures in August 2025 and in May 2026 determined that Greenbrier evaded antidumping and countervailing duties on certain freight rail couplers from Mexico and/or China attached to newly built railcars, with a period of investigation dating back to April 2024. CBP specified no liability amount; Greenbrier has appealed and states loss is not estimable and not probable. A new risk factor warns of possible future assessments with potential material adverse effect. The mechanism matters: an evasion finding typically brings suspension of liquidation and cash deposits at applicable AD/CVD rates, a working-capital drain independent of final assessment, running retroactively across more than two years of imports. The applicable rates and affected import volumes are not verifiable from the evidence available, so the exposure cannot be bounded. Against $273.7M of unrestricted cash and $1,573.2M of book equity, a low-rate outcome is immaterial and a high-rate outcome across two years of coupler imports is not. Treat the size as unknown rather than small.

**Section 232 tank-car tariffs.** Imposed April 2026 on freight rail tank cars entering the US. Management stated on the Q3 call that Greenbrier is not currently paying tariffs on tank cars shipped from Mexico and is seeking CBP guidance, noting contractual pass-through provisions. That is a management position, not an adjudicated ruling, and the matter appears unresolved.

**Earnings-quality and delivery-timing risk into the October print.** FY2026 guidance requires Q4 EPS of $0.79–0.94, a sequential improvement from $0.60 driven by a production ramp and delivery timing, the same variables that already forced a −17% revenue guidance reset within eight months. Three of the last four quarters missed consensus, including a −43.5% miss in the March quarter.

**Astra put option.** Astra Holding's ~25% interest in Greenbrier-Astra Rail B.V. carries a put priced at the higher of fair value or a defined EBITDA multiple. The exercise window, originally to June 1, 2026, was extended during Q3 to 20 business days after a written price determination, and that price was undetermined as of the July 1, 2026 filing. The $31.9M carrying value is not a reliable proxy for the cash requirement.

**Convertible maturity.** $373.8M of 2.875% notes due April 2028, with cash settlement at the company's option. Conversion terms and moneyness were not verified, so contingent conversion shares should not be added to the 30.94M current share count; any dilution analysis needs to be scenario-stated.

**Concentration.** Two customers were ~26% of FY2025 consolidated revenue (28% of Manufacturing); top ten suppliers 36% of inventory purchases, top supplier 14%. The concentration disclosure has not been updated in the 10-Q, and the segment mix shift toward Leasing establishes nothing about whether those two Manufacturing customers have become more or less important. With Manufacturing deliveries down 33%, deferral by one of them would be proportionally more damaging.

**Residual value.** A risk factor states plainly that initial-term rent typically covers only a small portion of railcar acquisition or production cost, so economics depend on re-leasing and residual realization across $1,298.4M of operating-lease equipment plus $426.7M held for syndication. Contracted future minimum lease receivables total $438.1M. A cleaner read on underlying traffic is the utilization-based rental component, which fell 15% over nine months ($14.1M vs $16.6M), a mild contradiction of the "utilization is healthy" framing.

**Portland Harbor Superfund.** Greenbrier is one of 140+ noticed parties at a site with a $1.7B site-wide undiscounted EPA remediation estimate; ~100 parties are in mediated allocation, and Greenbrier received a Special Notice letter in November 2024 opening settlement negotiations. Litigation is stayed to January 2028. No allocation exists, and the site-wide figure must not be used as a proxy for Greenbrier's share. Low-probability, long-dated, unquantified.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is markedly bearish and has shifted hard since the prior snapshot. Put/call open interest moved from 0.15 on June 30 to 6.80, the most extreme reading in this coverage set, with put/call volume at 3.25 and near-term put skew of 4.3% indicating elevated put demand. ATM IV collapsed from 61.8% to 33.6% across the Q3 print, a standard post-event vol crush, and now sits roughly in line with 31.6% realized volatility (2.0pp spread, IV percentile 7% within the current chain), so protection is not expensive. Term structure is in contango, 33.6% near versus 40.1% at six months, with the six-month tenor pricing a ±16.1% move, so the market locates event risk at the October FY2026 print and the FY2027 guide rather than in the next eleven days. Short interest of 14.1% of float with 5.2 days to cover corroborates institutional bearishness and creates modest squeeze risk on any order inflection.

Options are cheap, positioning is one-sided bearish, and the market has moved its risk pricing out to the next guidance event. That configuration is consistent with the REDUCE call and argues that anyone who wants to retain exposure should do so with inexpensive downside protection rather than by holding unhedged equity.

**Risk score 3.8.** Multiple unquantified contingencies, a guidance credibility problem, and a binary October print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $63 (+33%)

Trough earnings power has genuinely doubled. On ~18% less revenue than the FY2022 trough, Greenbrier guides to roughly twice the net income and ~2.5x the net margin. In-sourcing, the 30% European headcount reduction with capacity preserved, plant closures, and the leasing mix shift are documented in segment data rather than management adjectives. Replacement demand for an aging North American fleet is intact, inquiry levels are rising, and CY2026 industry deliveries below 25,000 are unsustainably low against fleet-replacement math. If orders inflect in FY2027, deliveries recover toward 16,000–18,000 units at the higher structural margin, EPS reaches $5.00+ by FY2028, and 13x on that gives $65. The balance sheet gets Greenbrier there without dilution: recourse leverage 0.47x, ~$887M liquidity, proven non-recourse ABS access at 5.14% in February 2026, a term facility extended to 2032, 87% of debt fixed or swapped. The stock trades below stated book, and stated book likely understates economic book given that asset sales are generating gains against depreciated carrying values. The dividend was raised to $0.34 quarterly at a 44% payout. Trade contingencies resolve immaterially on appeal or through contractual pass-through.

### Bear Case — $37 (−22%)

Backlog is the leading indicator and it says FY2027 deliveries fall again: 13,800 units against ~14,000 of FY2026 deliveries, a 0.74 book-to-bill, and 36% of backlog dependent on syndication investor appetite in a higher-for-longer rate regime. The FY2027 consensus of $3.90 implies 29% EPS growth on declining volume and has already been cut 4.1% in 90 days with further to go. Reported FY2026 earnings are flattered: $36.7M of nine-month disposition gains equal 31% of operating earnings, gains proceeds doubled year over year and cannot keep escalating, and a 19.5% Q3 tax rate against a 32.1% FY2025 rate adds a second non-durable support. Clean FY2026 EPS is closer to $1.90–2.05, making the stock 23–25x rather than 15.6x. The CBP evasion determination brings cash-deposit requirements of unknown size retroactive to April 2024, and Section 232 tank-car treatment is unresolved, both landing on the Mexican footprint that produced the margin gains. Q4 guidance requires a sequential ramp after three misses in four quarters. At 0.75x book the stock is $38, and the 52-week low is $38.23.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $49 (+3.8% from $47.22), bull $63 (+33.4%), bear $37 (−21.6%). Weighting bear 35% / base 40% / bull 25% to reflect falling estimates and a Q4 guide requiring sequential improvement gives a probability-weighted price return of roughly +2%, or ~+5% including the 2.88% dividend. That is below a reasonable hurdle for an equity with 32% realized volatility, a 0.74 book-to-bill, and two unquantified trade contingencies.

**Base case math:** $49 blends 14x a haircut FY2027 EPS of $3.50 ($49) with 0.96x book value of $50.85 ($48.8). It is deliberately below the $3.90 consensus because backlog units are down 16.9% in nine months and the 90-day revision trend is negative.

**De-risking plan:** trim into strength ahead of the October FY2026 fourth-quarter report, which will carry the first FY2027 guidance. The six-month option tenor prices a ±16.1% move and near-term IV at 33.6% is roughly fair to 31.6% realized, so holders who wish to retain exposure through a possible order inflection can do so with protection at reasonable cost rather than unhedged. A residual position sized to income (2.88% covered yield, 44% payout) is defensible for holders unwilling to exit entirely; new capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Book-to-bill above 1.0 for a quarter, or backlog units stabilizing at or above 13,800 at the August 31, 2026 fiscal year end.
2. FY2027 guidance at or above $3.75 EPS with disposition-gain assumptions disclosed at or below FY2026's ~$175M of proceeds, demonstrating operating rather than harvested earnings.
3. Resolution of the CBP EAPA appeal with a quantified liability below roughly $50M, or a CBP ruling confirming no Section 232 tank-car exposure.
4. Price below $40 (≤0.79x book, ~13x FY2026 consensus) with fundamentals unchanged.

**Avoid triggers:**
1. A quantified CBP duty assessment or cash-deposit requirement material against $273.7M of unrestricted cash.
2. Q4 FY2026 EPS below $0.70, confirming the guided sequential ramp did not materialize, combined with FY2027 guidance below $3.00.
3. Leased railcars for syndication rising above ~$500M without corresponding syndication closings, indicating investor appetite has stalled and the balance sheet is absorbing unsold cars.
4. Dividend coverage deteriorating below ~1.5x on clean earnings, or the buyback authorization lapsing unused while capex guidance rises further.

**Reassessment timeline:** the FY2026 fourth-quarter report and first FY2027 guidance, expected late October 2026. Secondary checkpoints are any 8-K on the CBP appeal or the Astra put price determination.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory for Greenbrier has declined from 5.6 in March to 5.0 in June, with valuation falling from 7.0 to 5.0 and growth from 5.0 to 3.8 while financial health improved from 5.5 to 6.8. The July 1 third-quarter print and guidance cut confirm that direction rather than reversing it, and consensus estimates have been cut across every period since. The price is roughly unchanged over that period, which means risk/reward has worsened rather than improved. That justifies a downgrade to REDUCE at 4.66.

The structural improvement in trough profitability is real and is the reason this is not an AVOID. Greenbrier should earn roughly twice what it earned in the last trough on less revenue, the balance sheet is appropriately structured with non-recourse fleet debt and modest recourse leverage, capital markets access was demonstrated twice during the downturn, and the stock trades below stated book with a covered dividend. Those facts cap the downside near $37–38.

What is missing is a reason to pay 15.6x guided trough earnings, or 23–25x after stripping disposition gains and normalizing tax, for a business whose order book is contracting at 0.74 book-to-bill, whose FY2027 consensus is being revised down, whose closest comparable trades 30% cheaper, and which carries two newly disclosed trade contingencies of unbounded size on the manufacturing footprint that generated its margin expansion. Sell-side positioning is unanimous with zero buy ratings and a mean target below spot; put/call open interest at 6.80 and 14.1% short interest say institutions agree. Trim into strength, revisit below $40 or on a confirmed order inflection at the October print.
