## Executive Summary

Evergy is a regulated electric utility serving 1.7 million customers across Kansas and Missouri that has positioned itself as a significant beneficiary of data center demand growth. The company has signed electric service agreements for approximately 1,900 MW of new data center load — a 17% increase on peak demand — with service beginning 2026-2028. This growth opportunity has driven the stock from a May 2025 low of $63.29 to a current price near its 52-week high of $85.27 (currently $82.59).

The fundamental challenge is that this growth story requires $21.6 billion in capital expenditures over 2026-2030, necessitating ~$12.3 billion in new debt and ~$3.3 billion in equity issuance. With adjusted EPS growth guidance of 4-6% annually but estimates currently falling 1-8% across timeframes over 90 days, and a trailing record of 3 earnings misses in 4 quarters, the stock's ~22x forward P/E offers limited margin of safety against execution risk. The data center thesis provides genuine growth optionality, but the stock already reflects much of this optimism.

## Company Overview

Evergy is a regulated electric utility holding company operating through Evergy Kansas Central, Evergy Metro, and Evergy Missouri West. The company owns approximately 15,800 MW of generating capacity with a fuel mix of coal (37%), wind (29%), natural gas/oil (27%), and uranium (7%). Revenue splits roughly 60/40 between Kansas and Missouri jurisdictions, with customer composition of residential (37%), commercial (33%), industrial (11%), wholesale (5%), and transmission (9%). The company employs approximately 4,691 people, 53% unionized, and holds a 94% interest in the Wolf Creek nuclear plant.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Evergy generated $5.96 billion in revenue in FY2025, up 2% from $5.85 billion in FY2024. Operating income grew to $1.53 billion from $1.47 billion. However, GAAP EPS declined from $3.79 to $3.66 as rising depreciation (+$49 million), interest expense (+$53 million), clean energy investment losses ($48.7 million), and convertible note dilution (~$0.05/share) offset revenue gains. Adjusted EPS grew modestly from $3.81 to $3.83.

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure:** Cash flow from operations of ~$2.05 billion in FY2025 falls well short of capital expenditures of $2.8 billion, producing deeply negative free cash flow. The funding gap will widen as capex ramps to an average of $4.3 billion annually through 2030. Debt-to-equity stands at 3.32x, elevated even for a utility. Interest expense has grown 52% over three years (from $404 million to $616 million), and the credit facilities provide the sole source of liquidity given only $19.8 million of cash on hand.

**Dilution Overhang:** The $1.16 billion of remaining convertible notes (conversion price ~$61.80) are 34% in-the-money and will likely convert in 2027, adding approximately 18.8 million shares (~8% dilution) unless repurchased at a premium. Combined with $3.3 billion of planned equity issuance via ATM programs, cumulative dilution over the next 5 years could reach 25-30%. Evergy has already paid a 26% premium ($308.6 million) to repurchase $244.1 million face value of converts, signaling acute awareness of this issue.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue of $1.44 billion with adjusted EPS of $0.69, beating estimates of $0.66. Guidance was reaffirmed and a new large customer was announced.

## Growth Analysis

The data center opportunity is the dominant growth catalyst. The ~1,900 MW of signed ESAs represent a transformational increase in Evergy's demand profile. Two new CCGT plants (~705 MW each, 2029-2030) and one simple-cycle plant (440 MW, 2030) are under development specifically to serve this load. The company's service territory in Kansas/Missouri offers affordable power, available land, and a favorable regulatory environment.

Supporting the growth thesis: Missouri's SB 4 legislation enables CWIP recovery for gas plant construction costs (improving cost recovery timing), Kansas and Missouri both approved Large Load Power Service rate plans with robust customer protections (80% minimum monthly bills, 12-year terms, termination fees), and the company's 2026-2030 rate base growth should support 4-6% EPS CAGR according to management guidance.

Against the growth thesis: estimate revisions are negative across all timeframes. Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen 7.9% over 90 days. The company has missed EPS estimates in 3 of its last 4 quarters. The magnitude of capital required ($21.6 billion over 5 years) introduces significant execution risk, and the 4-6% EPS growth target — while reasonable for a utility — does not inherently justify a premium multiple given the financing headwinds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.59, EVRG trades at approximately 22.1x forward earnings on $3.83 FY2025 adjusted EPS and roughly 19.5x the FY2026 consensus estimate of $4.24. The peer median P/E is 22.7x (Alliant Energy 22.7x, FirstEnergy 24.7x, PPL 23.2x, Eversource 15.0x, Edison 7.5x).

The dividend yield of ~3.4% is below the peer median of 4.1%, reflecting the market's growth premium for the data center story. Analyst consensus mean target of $90.38 (median $88.50) implies roughly 9-10% upside, with a range of $80-$99.

The stock is trading within 3% of its 52-week high and near the low end of analyst targets ($80). With negative estimate revision momentum and 3 misses in 4 quarters, the valuation is pricing in successful execution of the data center capex plan without adequately discounting execution risk or dilution impact. The growth premium relative to peers appears warranted directionally but stretched at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Evergy's regulated monopoly franchise in Kansas and Missouri provides a durable competitive moat — there is no retail competition in its service territories. The emerging differentiation is its data center positioning. Few utilities in the Midwest have signed ESAs of this magnitude, placing Evergy alongside AEP, Dominion, and Duke as a primary data center utility beneficiary.

The regulatory framework is supportive: Missouri's new CWIP mechanism, Kansas's PISA provisions, and the approved LLPS rate plans all facilitate the growth strategy. However, PISA caps (1.5% in Kansas, 2.5% in Missouri) limit annual rate increase pace, and the Kansas Central rate case settled at $128 million vs. $196 million requested (a 35% haircut), suggesting regulatory outcomes may not always match management expectations.

The Prairie Wind transmission JV (with AEP and Berkshire Hathaway Energy) provides additional transmission optionality, and the Wolf Creek nuclear plant could benefit from nuclear production tax credits (pending IRS guidance), though benefits likely flow to ratepayers rather than shareholders.

## Risk Assessment

**Data Center Demand Materialization (HIGH):** The $9.3 billion in new generation capex is largely predicated on ~1,900 MW of data center demand materializing on schedule. The 10-K explicitly warns that if demand "does not occur as projected or is not sustained as projected, for any reason, it could have a material adverse effect." While the LLPS rate plan includes termination fees and collateral requirements, customer delays or cancellations would leave Evergy with significant stranded capital.

**Financing and Dilution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** The $21.6 billion capex plan requires ~$15.6 billion in external financing. Rising interest rates increase the cost of this debt. Equity dilution of potentially 25-30% over 5 years places a ceiling on per-share earnings growth even if operating metrics improve.

**Single-Vendor Construction Risk (MODERATE):** Selection of a single vendor for power island equipment on both CCGT plants creates concentrated delivery risk for a $3-4 billion construction program. Tariff uncertainties on imported equipment compound this.

**Regulatory Risk (MODERATE):** The 35% haircut on the Kansas Central rate case demonstrates that regulatory outcomes can disappoint. Multiple pending cases (Metro Missouri $140 million) introduce uncertainty.

**Weather and Wildfire (LOW-MODERATE):** Kansas wildfire legislation provides meaningful protection (2-year statute of limitations, $5M punitive cap), but extreme weather events could still disrupt operations.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 23.6% sits 7.6 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 16.1%, indicating the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The put/call volume ratio of 3.38 (heavily put-dominated by volume) but low P/C OI ratio of 0.13 suggests recent defensive positioning rather than structural bearish conviction. The rapid IV compression from 29.1% to 23.6% on a 2% price move indicates unstable options pricing around a recent event (likely the Q1 beat). The flat term structure (near 23.6% ≈ far 25.4%) suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a near-term catalyst.

Short interest at 6.6% of float with 7.5 days to cover is modestly elevated for a utility — not concerning in isolation but worth monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-99, +9 to +20%):** Data center demand materializes on schedule and potentially expands beyond the current 1,900 MW. Rate cases produce constructive outcomes. The 4-6% EPS growth rate proves conservative as load growth exceeds expectations. Regulatory mechanisms (CWIP, PISA) enable efficient cost recovery. The stock re-rates toward 23-24x on demonstrated execution. Interest rate moderation reduces financing costs.

**Bear Case ($70-75, -10 to -15%):** Data center customers delay or downsize commitments. Convertible note dilution hits EPS harder than expected. Rate cases disappoint. Rising interest costs compress earnings despite revenue growth. The stock de-rates toward 16-18x on missed estimates and dilution concerns. Negative estimate revision trend persists.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $72-75 range (providing ~17-18x forward P/E entry), OR
2. Estimate revisions reverse to positive (2+ consecutive quarters of beats + upward revisions), OR
3. Convertible note situation resolved (full repurchase eliminating dilution overhang)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Data center ESA cancellation or material delay announcement (even one major customer)
2. Equity issuance significantly above $3.3B plan (suggesting capital needs are worse than modeled)
3. Two or more rate case outcomes with >30% haircuts to requested amounts

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (expected August 2026, Q2 results — the critical summer quarter that typically represents ~50% of annual EPS). Rate case decisions in Missouri (expected late 2026/early 2027). Progress on CCGT construction timelines.

## Conclusion

Evergy presents a genuine utility growth story centered on data center demand, differentiated from peers by the magnitude of signed ESAs in its service territory. The regulatory framework is supportive, and management has taken proactive steps (LLPS rate plans, convertible buybacks, CWIP legislation) to de-risk execution. However, the stock at ~22x forward earnings with negative estimate revisions, a 3-for-4 miss record, deeply negative free cash flow, and significant dilution ahead does not offer compelling risk/reward at current levels. The thesis is positive on a 2-3 year horizon, but the entry point is premature. A pullback to the low-to-mid $70s or a reversal in estimate momentum would create a more attractive setup.
