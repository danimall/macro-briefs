**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brighthouse Financial should be valued as a signed merger-arbitrage instrument, not on earnings, book value, or hedging results. On November 6, 2025, BHF agreed to be acquired by Aquarian Holdings VI L.P. for $70.00 per share in cash. Shareholders approved the transaction on February 12, 2026, and the Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust waiting period has expired. The single remaining gate is state insurance regulatory approval of Aquarian's change of control over a large life and annuity insurer. At $63.22, the stock trades at a ~10.7% gross spread to the $70.00 deal price, with a stated close "in 2026."

That spread is the entire investment case. It is wide for a deal that already carries shareholder and antitrust approval, which tells you the market is assigning non-trivial probability to a regulatory rejection or a prolonged delay. Private-capital ownership of annuity balance sheets (the Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield template) is the structure drawing heightened NAIC and state-department scrutiny. Aquarian is an insurance-focused holding company acquiring a $237B general account, exactly the profile regulators are examining most closely. The reported GAAP numbers are noise for deal purposes: the Q1 2026 net loss of $792M (-$13.82/share) is driven almost entirely by non-cash mark-to-market on variable annuity and Shield hedges, not operational deterioration. Adjusted earnings were +$239M, essentially flat versus the prior year.

We rate BHF a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The math is straightforward: ~11% upside to $70 if the deal closes on reasonable timing, against a fall to the low-$50s or worse if regulators say no. That is roughly a 1:1 to 1.3:1 reward-to-risk on a binary regulatory outcome we cannot handicap with confidence from public disclosure. Holders positioned for the arb can carry it; new capital does not have a compelling asymmetry here. This continues the improving trajectory (4.8 → 4.6 → 5.4, now 5.15) but does not upgrade to BUY, because the spread's width itself signals that better-informed arbitrageurs see enough risk to demand an ~11% cushion.

## Company Overview

Brighthouse was spun out of MetLife in 2017 and remains one of the largest U.S. providers of annuity and life insurance, distributed through independent channels and partner networks. The business runs across four segments:

- **Annuities** (~$163B of $237B total assets): variable, fixed, index-linked (the flagship Shield RILA), and income annuities. This is the earnings engine — Q1 2026 adjusted earnings of $324M, roughly all of company adjusted profit.
- **Life:** term, universal, whole, and variable life. Small and near breakeven (Q1 2026 adjusted loss of $6M).
- **Run-off:** universal life with secondary guarantees (ULSG), structured settlements, pension risk transfer, COLI, and 100%-reinsured long-term care. A chronic drag (Q1 2026 adjusted loss of $48M).
- **Corporate & Other:** funding agreements, excess capital, debt interest, preferred dividends. Adjusted loss of $31M.

The business is heavily market-sensitive because of a large legacy variable annuity guarantee block (GMIB/GMWB/GMAB/GMDB) plus Shield embedded derivatives. Statutory capital and GAAP earnings swing with equity markets and rates. The company targets a combined RBC ratio of 400–450% in normal markets, and all insurance subsidiaries currently exceed required RBC levels.

## Financial Analysis

BHF's GAAP results are dominated by non-cash market items. Q1 2026 net loss available to common was -$792M (-$13.82/share) versus -$294M (-$5.04) a year earlier. The drivers were mark-to-market on variable annuity and Shield hedges (-$1,327M), MRB remeasurement (-$748M), and net derivative losses (-$509M), partially offset by Shield embedded-derivative gains (+$805M). Management's adjusted earnings, which strip out this volatility, were +$239M in Q1 2026 versus +$235M in Q1 2025 — the truer read on underlying profitability, and it is stable and modestly positive.

The earnings surprise record underscores how noisy the GAAP series is: over four quarters, one beat (+230.5% in Q3 2025) and three misses (-7.5%, -29.8%, -23.9%). These are evidence that quarterly EPS is largely a function of market moves through the guarantee block rather than consistent operational misses. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-quarter +5.6% over 90 days, next-quarter +11.0%, current-year +1.7%), but for a name in the middle of a take-private, revision trends carry little signal.

The balance sheet shows total assets of $236.8B against total equity of $5.6B (down from $6.8B at YE2025), including $1.75B of preferred stock. Common equity carries a retained-earnings deficit of -$1.45B and negative AOCI of -$4.16B, driven by $5.9B of net unrealized losses on the AFS fixed-maturity portfolio. These are rate-driven, not credit-driven, and would pull to par as bonds mature. The investment portfolio is high quality: $81.2B of fixed-maturity AFS is 97.2% investment grade, and $22.6B of mortgage loans are 99% performing (avg LTV 68%, DSCR 2.2x). Office exposure is ~$2.7B (22% of commercial loans), monitored but not flagged as material adverse. Long-term debt is $3.15B (fair value ~$2.5B). Short-term liquidity is $3.9B, with $48.2B of liquid assets.

Capital allocation is frozen by the merger covenants: no common dividends (the company has never paid one) and no buybacks, with $441M of authorization suspended. Preferred dividends of $26M/quarter continue. This is standard for a pending take-private and is not a governance concern in itself.

## Growth Analysis

For a company being acquired for cash, growth prospects are largely irrelevant to the return you will realize — you are being paid $70.00 regardless of 2027 RILA sales. The standalone growth profile matters as the downside scenario if the deal breaks, and it is unremarkable. The Shield index-linked annuity has been an industry bright spot amid higher rates, giving the Annuities segment a genuine growth vector. That positive is offset by chronic Run-off losses (ULSG bleeds), a shrinking legacy variable annuity block, and a business whose reported results are dominated by market volatility rather than franchise expansion.

Separate account assets fell to $80.8B at Q1 2026 from $85.5B at YE2025 on weaker equity markets, which mechanically lifted guarantee liabilities. The Fed's three 2025 cuts (to 3.50–3.75%) pressure reinvestment yields and spread-product economics. On a standalone basis, BHF is a low-multiple, capital-intensive legacy annuity insurer. We score growth 4.5 to reflect the muted standalone profile; it is not the axis on which this investment turns.

## Valuation Assessment

The only valuation anchor that matters is the $70.00 deal price. Trailing and forward P/E figures (-21.4x, distorted by GAAP losses) and price-to-book (a deep discount driven by rate-driven AOCI) are both meaningless for deal purposes and misleading standalone.

Peer multiples reinforce that the standalone equity is a low-multiple asset: the Life & Health median P/E is 20.0x, but the closest structural comp — Jackson Financial (JXN), another variable-annuity-heavy insurer — trades at a negative reported P/E on the same guarantee-block volatility, at a 12% discount to its 52-week high. On adjusted earnings of roughly $19.60 for the current year, BHF at $63.22 trades at ~3.2x adjusted EPS, which is where a volatile, capital-intensive legacy annuity book trades absent a deal. That low multiple reflects the market's structural discount to this business model, not a value opportunity; cheap-looking multiples in this space are traps without a catalyst. Here the catalyst is the merger, which is why we score valuation moderately favorable (6.0) rather than low: the $70 cash bid is a specific, identified reason the market price should converge upward, subject to the regulatory gate.

The arithmetic of the arb: buying at $63.22 to receive $70.00 is a ~10.7% gross return. If the deal closes by Q4 2026, that is a compelling annualized IRR. If it slips into late 2026 or drags, the IRR compresses. If it breaks, the stock likely re-rates toward pre-deal levels, which were in the mid-$40s to low-$50s through much of 2025 (the stock traded in the low-to-high $40s before the October 31 pop to $57 and the November 6 move to $65+). That defines the downside at roughly $50–$52, about a 17–21% loss from here.

## Competitive Landscape

BHF is a scale player in variable and index-linked annuities, competing against Equitable, Jackson, Corebridge, Lincoln, and Prudential. Competition is on price, product features (especially RILA), and financial-strength ratings — the last directly relevant to the deal, since a change of control to a private-capital owner could affect ratings, a named risk in the filings. The Shield franchise is a legitimate competitive strength in the current rate environment.

The broader industry theme is the migration of annuity balance sheets to alternative-capital owners. Aquarian fits this template, and the strategic logic (a large general account for an insurance-focused holding company) is sound. This same trend is what has drawn NAIC and state-department scrutiny of private-equity control of life insurers, the crux of the remaining approval risk. Competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-negative standalone (legacy block shrinking, Run-off drag), which supports a 4.5 competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deal-completion / regulatory risk — dominant.** Insurance regulatory approvals for Aquarian's change of control remain outstanding across the relevant states (Delaware, likely New York and Massachusetts). A denial or extended delay collapses the equity toward standalone value. The 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor noting the merger's conditions are "largely outside the parties' control." The spread is wide enough to signal that sophisticated arbitrageurs are demanding meaningful compensation for this risk.

**2. Market-sensitivity of the guarantee block.** Even absent the deal, MRBs ($8.6B liability) and Shield embedded derivatives ($9.4B) drive large earnings and statutory-capital swings, and hedge mismatches can be costly. Relevant primarily in the deal-break scenario.

**3. ULSG interest-rate exposure.** A chronic Run-off drag; the 10-K flags ULSG net-income volatility from rates.

**4. Litigation.** Two certified cost-of-insurance class actions are live: *Newton v. Brighthouse* (N.D. Ga., Georgia UL class certified, includes RICO and treble/punitive claims) and *Martin v. Brighthouse* (S.D.N.Y., nationwide class certified Sept 2025, 2nd Circuit denied appeal Feb 2026). A MOVEit data-breach class action is also pending. Aggregate reasonably-possible losses are estimated modestly (~$10M litigation, up to ~$100M tax/reinsurance), but the certified nationwide COI class carries indeterminate tail risk. This is relevant both standalone and as a potential closing consideration.

**5. Deal economics detail.** The $328M MetLife Tax Receivables Agreement accelerates on closing — an incremental cash outflow for the acquirer, a datapoint on total deal cost but not a threat to the $70 headline.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning sends a mixed but ultimately consistent signal for an arb situation. Near-term ATM IV is 17.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 47.2% — a -30.1% spread, which is unusually cheap. This is what you expect when a stock is pinned to a cash deal price: realized volatility collapses because the equity trades on binary regulatory headlines rather than fundamentals. The term structure shows 1-month IV of 17.2% rising to 31.3% at 3 months, with the 3-month put/call skew at 15.7% (elevated put demand). The market is pricing calm near-term and a fatter tail into September, consistent with participants hedging a possible negative regulatory decision landing in that window. The 5.60 put/call volume ratio and the elevated skew both reflect deal-break hedging, not a fundamental bearish thesis on the business. For a HOLD/arb position, the cheap near-term IV means downside protection (puts) is inexpensive relative to realized risk — a reasonable way to define the deal-break tail.

**Short interest:** 10.8% of float short, 10.8 days to cover. In a merger context this is often a mix of genuine deal-skeptics and hedged arb structures; it does not, by itself, confirm a directional bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($70.00, +10.7%)
The deal closes in 2026. State insurance regulators approve Aquarian's change of control on reasonable terms and timing, and buyers collect $70.00 cash. If the close lands within six to nine months, the annualized IRR is attractive for a low-beta, market-insensitive position. This is the base-case-adjacent scenario for a signed deal that has cleared its two most difficult gates.

### Bear Case ($50–$52, -17% to -21%)
State regulators reject or materially condition Aquarian's control, or the deal drags past 2026 amid intensifying NAIC scrutiny. The stock re-rates toward pre-deal standalone levels (mid-$40s to low-$50s), where a volatile, capital-intensive legacy annuity book with chronic Run-off losses, suspended capital returns, and certified COI class-action tail risk trades at a structural discount. In a disorderly break, the low-$40s (the 52-week low of $42.07) is reachable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a defined binary event with roughly balanced reward-to-risk (~11% up, ~17–21% down), not the asymmetry required for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- State insurance departments (Delaware, NY, MA) publicly signal or grant change-of-control approval, compressing the spread's risk and making the remaining ~11% a near-riskless carry.
- The spread widens materially (stock into the mid-$50s) on non-fundamental noise while regulatory progress remains on track — improving the reward-to-risk to clearly above 2:1.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any state regulator issues an adverse or materially conditioned decision on Aquarian's control, or the deal is formally terminated.
- The close slips definitively beyond 2026 with no clear regulatory path, eroding the IRR and raising break odds.
- A rating-agency action tied to the change of control that would materially impair the standalone franchise if the deal fails.

**Reassessment timeline:** Monitor state insurance-department dockets and any 8-K on regulatory milestones. The next natural checkpoints are quarterly filings and any change-of-control approval announcements, expected through H2 2026. The elevated 3-month options skew points to a September window for a decision.

## Conclusion

BHF should be held or traded strictly as a merger-arbitrage position. The $70.00 all-cash bid from Aquarian, with shareholder and HSR approval secured, defines both the ~10.7% upside and the framework for downside. The GAAP losses, book-value discount, and hedging volatility that dominate a standard screen are secondary; the only variable that matters is state insurance regulatory approval of a private-capital owner taking control of a large annuity insurer, a structure under active regulatory scrutiny industry-wide.

The trajectory has improved (AVOID → AVOID → HOLD), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that: the deal has cleared its two hardest gates, and the residual risk is now narrowly regulatory. The ~11% spread that persists after shareholder and antitrust approval is itself the market's verdict that the regulatory outcome is not a formality. With upside of ~11% against downside of ~17–21% on a binary we cannot confidently handicap from public filings, the reward-to-risk is roughly balanced. Existing holders positioned for the arb can carry it with cheap put protection defining the tail; new capital lacks a compelling asymmetry. HOLD, uncertain, with the state regulatory decision as the fulcrum.
