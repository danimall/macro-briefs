**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CSG Systems International is no longer an actionable public equity. The company was acquired by NEC Corporation and delisted from NASDAQ on May 14, 2026, with a final trade price of $80.69 (May 13, 2026) and a takeout market capitalization of approximately $2.24 billion. The corporate-action record corroborates this: a dense cluster of proxy statements filed October 2025 through January 2026, an Extraordinary General Meeting at which shareholders voted on nine proposals (including a merger, a spin-out, a name change, an incentive plan, and an option exchange), and a run of final 8-Ks in May 2026 immediately preceding delisting. Any analysis framed around a going-concern equity thesis would be fundamentally mis-specified. The relevant question is whether the $80.69 exit fairly captured value, and whether any residual security was distributed to former holders through the approved spin-out.

The underlying business was a mature, high-quality communications software franchise: recurring, contract-based revenue tied to subscriber counts at large communications service providers, deeply embedded billing and revenue-management systems with high switching costs, and asset-light economics generating roughly $141 million of free cash flow in 2025 on $1,223 million of revenue. Growth was modest — low-single-digit organic (2025 revenue +2.2%) — so the equity story rested on margin, buybacks, and M&A rather than top-line expansion. Operating momentum was strong into the close: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.37 beat consensus by roughly 19% (actual $1.37 vs. estimate $1.15 per Phase 1 data; the brief's ~$1.04 estimate is not corroborated by the structured earnings-surprise data), supporting the view that this was a full-value strategic sale rather than a distressed exit. The takeout multiple of approximately 36x trailing GAAP earnings is rich for a 3% grower and reflects recurring-revenue quality plus synergy value to NEC, which owns Netcracker, a direct competitor in telecom BSS/OSS.

We rate CSGS HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale, reflecting that the primary decision (own or not own the equity) has been resolved by the completed transaction, and that one open item — the nature and terms of the shareholder-approved spin-out — cannot be confirmed from the available filing record. Former holders should verify the definitive merger proxy and merger 8-K to determine whether any spun-out security was received beyond the cash consideration. For a prospective investor, there is nothing to buy or sell.

## Company Overview

CSG Systems International, headquartered in Englewood, Colorado, provided revenue management, digital monetization, customer-experience, and payments software primarily to the global communications industry across the Americas, EMEA, and APAC. The company employed roughly 5,500 people and had been public since its February 1996 IPO.

Its core platforms included the Advanced Convergent Platform (ACP), a private-cloud billing and revenue-management system serving large North American cable and telecom operators and functioning as the legacy cash cow; a public-cloud SaaS revenue-management and payments platform; CSG Xponent for customer engagement and journey management; and adjacent solutions spanning field-service technician management, electronic bill presentment, configure-price-quote (CPQ), digital partner management, and a newer fraud-detection product, CSG Payments Protection.ai. Revenue was sticky and recurring, with billing systems that are mission-critical and costly to replace. Historically the company depended on a small set of anchor cable and telecom clients (Comcast, Charter, DISH), and had been actively diversifying into financial services, retail, and healthcare and expanding geographically. Commercial validation into the close included a DISH Network renewal through 2030 and an ePLDT (Philippines) win.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth was modest and durable rather than expansive. Revenue advanced from $1,089.8 million (2022) to $1,223.3 million (2025), a compound rate of roughly 4%, decelerating to +2.2% in the final year. The recurring, subscriber-linked revenue base is high-visibility, but the core cable/telecom end market faces cord-cutting, subscriber declines, and operator consolidation that pressure per-subscriber billing revenue. Management's diversification into financial services, retail, and healthcare, and into customer-experience (Xponent) and payments (including Payments Protection.ai), was the offset, supported by wins such as ePLDT and the DISH renewal through 2030.

Earnings visibility into the close was strong: the company beat consensus in each of the four reported quarters, with quarterly surprises of 8.6%, 12.0%, 12.5%, and 19.4% over the trailing four quarters. That beat cadence supports the view that operating execution was intact and that the acquisition was a full-value strategic sale. As a forward growth thesis, however, there is nothing to project — the equity ceased to exist as a public security in May 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is now historical. The transaction cleared at approximately 36x trailing GAAP earnings. For a business growing revenue ~3% and having posted a ~36% net-income decline in the final year, that headline multiple is rich; it reflects the quality and stickiness of recurring, mission-critical billing revenue, the strategic value to NEC/Netcracker of a larger North American cable/telecom billing footprint, and synergy value. On free cash flow, ~$2.24 billion enterprise value against ~$141 million FCF implies a mid-teens FCF multiple, more defensible for a stable software franchise.

The prior report trajectory (valuation improving from 3.0 in early May to 5.0 in late May as the deal terms crystallized) is consistent with a full-value exit being confirmed. We hold valuation at 5.0: former holders received a fair premium for a slow-growing but high-quality asset, but there is no mispricing to exploit at this point, and the "cheapness" or "richness" of a completed takeout is not an actionable input. Absent confirmation of the spin-out terms, we cannot assess whether any residual security was fairly valued at distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

CSG competed in communications BSS/OSS and customer-engagement software against Amdocs, Oracle, various CX vendors, and Netcracker — an NEC company. The strategic logic of the acquisition follows directly from that competitive map: NEC, through Netcracker, consolidates two communications-software providers and gains CSG's entrenched North American cable/telecom relationships (Comcast, Charter, DISH via ACP), plus growing CX (Xponent) and payments assets. This is coherent consolidation in a mature, sticky niche and explains the premium.

The company's competitive position was stable-to-improving on trajectory — deep switching costs and a diversification push offsetting a structurally challenged core end market — but not a share-gaining challenger. Customer concentration remained the classic risk, mitigated by the DISH renewal through 2030. As a standalone competitive story this is moot post-close; within NEC/Netcracker, CSG's assets add scale in a segment where incumbency and integration depth are the durable advantages.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk — deal completion and delisting — has resolved. Residual risk to a former holder is limited to any remaining deal mechanics and the terms of the shareholder-approved spin-out, which the available record does not clarify. Because the primary uncertainty (whether the transaction would close) is behind us and shareholders received cash at a full-value premium, idiosyncratic downside risk not already reflected in the outcome is very low, which supports a high risk-factors score. The prior report's jump in this dimension from 4.5 to 8.0 correctly reflects deal certainty replacing deal uncertainty.

The unresolved item warranting attention is the spin-out. The EGM approved both a merger and a spin-out, raising the possibility that a business was carved out and a residual security distributed to shareholders rather than sold to NEC for cash. Former holders should confirm against the definitive merger proxy and merger 8-K what security, if any, was received beyond cash consideration. We also flag material data-integrity caveats: several tools failed for CSGS (financial-health scores, 10-K text/sections, 10-Q, insider transactions, 8-K resolver, direct EDGAR fetch), and the acquirer identity (NEC) and final deal terms come from a third-party aggregator corroborated by, but not directly read from, the primary merger 8-K.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** For a former holder, the transaction delivered a full-value exit at ~36x trailing earnings, with a premium consistent with the stock's ~22% trailing-year gain. The business executed well into the close (four consecutive beats), so the sale monetized quality rather than distress. If the approved spin-out distributed a residual security with independent value, total consideration may exceed the headline cash figure — an upside to be verified against the merger proxy.

**Bear Case.** There is effectively no bear case for a completed cash takeout, only opportunity cost: former holders now hold cash and must redeploy at prevailing valuations. The residual analytical risk is that the spin-out mechanics or any escrow/holdback provisions reduce net realized value below the $80.69 reference, or that the spun-out security (if any) trades below its distribution value. This is a mechanical, low-magnitude risk, not a going-concern impairment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Because CSGS is delisted, the standard forward-price-target framework does not apply. We provide reassessment guidance appropriate to a completed M&A situation.

- **Upgrade triggers (to a positive residual-security view):** Confirmation from the definitive merger proxy/8-K that the approved spin-out distributed a residual, tradeable security to former holders at terms above expectations, or that total consideration (cash plus any stock/CVR) exceeded the ~$80.69 cash reference.
- **Downgrade triggers:** Discovery that escrow/holdback, indemnification, or spin-out mechanics reduced net realized proceeds below the $80.69 reference, or that a distributed residual security trades materially below its notional distribution value.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Resolved. The next and final action item is a one-time verification of the definitive merger terms and spin-out treatment against primary filings. No recurring earnings-based reassessment applies.
- **Practical guidance:** For prospective investors there is no position to establish. For former holders, confirm the cash-plus-any-residual consideration received and treat proceeds as cash for redeployment.

## Conclusion

CSGS was a high-quality, sticky, cash-generative communications-software franchise — steady ~3% revenue growth, ~$141 million of free cash flow in 2025, 13 consecutive years of dividend increases, ~10% share-count reduction over three years, and well-aligned management (CEO Brian Shepherd's pay tracked realized value, with company TSR of 110 versus peer 43.3 in 2024). It was acquired by NEC Corporation and delisted on May 14, 2026 at a final price of $80.69 and a ~$2.24 billion takeout, roughly 36x trailing earnings, with strong operating momentum into the close.

Our recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting that the buy/sell decision has been resolved by a completed transaction and that one item — the terms of the shareholder-approved spin-out — remains unverifiable from the available record. Former holders received a full-value cash exit and should confirm whether any residual security was distributed beyond the cash consideration by reviewing the definitive merger proxy and merger 8-K. There is no live equity thesis to underwrite.
