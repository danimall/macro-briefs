## Executive Summary

PROG Holdings has spent 2026 remaking itself. The January 2 acquisition of Purchasing Power for $420M added a payroll-deduction employee purchase platform; Four Technologies, the in-house BNPL app, more than doubled its volume; Vive was divested in October 2025. Consolidated Q2 2026 revenue rose 22.3% to $719.7M, gross merchandise volume rose 60.1% to $902M, and management raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to $4.75–5.00 from $4.40–4.80. On the headline numbers this is a company inflecting after four years of erosion.

The segment detail says something different. Diluted GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $0.92 versus $0.93, a 1.1% decline, and consolidated operating margin compressed 96 basis points to 9.12%. The arithmetic is clean: Progressive Leasing generates roughly 10.6 cents of pre-tax profit per dollar of GMV, Four generates 2.3 cents, and Purchasing Power currently generates none. Progressive is 47% of GMV but approximately 88% of segment pre-tax profit, and its revenue fell 3.4% year over year while its provision for lease merchandise write-offs rose to 8.4% of leasing revenue from 7.5% — roughly $5.0M a quarter on the current revenue base, which substantially accounts for the $6.1M decline in the segment's pre-tax earnings. Inside a raised consolidated guide, Progressive's FY2026 EBT range was cut from $191.0–198.5M to $188.5–193.0M. The stock fell about 4.9% on the print despite the raise.

At $42.945 the shares trade at 8.8x the guided non-GAAP EPS midpoint of $4.875, 10.9x the guided GAAP midpoint of $3.94, 7.6x next-year consensus of $5.64, and roughly 6.1x FY2026 guided Adjusted EBITDA on recourse net debt of ~$515M. That is inexpensive against a Consumer Finance peer median P/E of 14.0 and below the lowest published analyst target of $45. Against it: about 60% of the $51.5M implied pre-tax non-GAAP add-back is recurring acquired-intangible amortization, so the honest anchor is closer to 11x than 8.8x; tangible equity is $42.3M (~$1.06/share) against $763.1M of goodwill and intangibles; and second-half guidance requires Purchasing Power to swing from a $(7.8)M first-half pre-tax loss to $24.8–29.3M of EBT, with implied Q4 consolidated revenue of ~$868M, 18.4% above the Q3 midpoint. We rate the shares HOLD with a base case of $50 and a defined accumulation zone in the $36–38 range.

## Company Overview

PROG Holdings was spun out of Aaron's in November 2020 and now runs four reportable units from Salt Lake City.

**Progressive Leasing** is the economic engine, a virtual lease-to-own provider that buys merchandise from a third-party retailer at the point of sale and leases it to a credit-constrained consumer. It owns no stores; it operates inside other retailers' checkout flows. E-commerce was 25.6% of Progressive's Q2 2026 GMV, up from 20.9% a year earlier and 23.3% for FY2025.

**Purchasing Power**, acquired January 2, 2026 for $420.0M ($391.8M net of cash acquired), contracts with employers whose employees buy branded merchandise and services repaid through payroll deduction or military allotment. It takes title and drop-ships, so it carries gross product revenue and cost of product sales, a different P&L shape from the rest of the group. It arrived with ~$338.6M of non-recourse securitization and warehouse debt, $293.7M of which was outstanding at June 30.

**Four Technologies** is a direct-to-consumer BNPL app funded through virtual payment cards usable at any U.S. retailer. It has no integrated retail partnerships and acquires customers through app stores.

**MoneyApp** (reported in Other) offers interest-free cash advances and remains loss-making at the segment level.

Vive Financial's receivables were sold in October 2025 for ~$143.9M and the business sits in discontinued operations, which is why FY2025 revenue of $2.409B is presented on a continuing-operations basis.

Progressive's revenue declining 3.4% while its GMV grew 3.4% is consistent with the FY2025 GMV decline of 8.6% still flowing through the lease-revenue recognition tail. This is an interpretation, not a disclosed fact, but the most economical explanation.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $277.9M versus $279.8M, apparently flat despite adding an entire segment. Two adjustments matter. First, cash taxes swung $60.7M favorably: $15.6M received in H1 2026 versus $45.0M paid in H1 2025, driven by bonus depreciation on lease merchandise under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Neutralizing cash taxes in both periods gives $262.3M versus $324.8M, down 19.2%. That benefit is genuine cash and recurs while the lease portfolio is replenished at scale, but it is timing, not earnings. Second, comparability is impaired by classification: lease merchandise additions ($814.0M in H1) run through operating activities while Four's and Purchasing Power's originations run through investing ($599.5M invested against $592.5M of proceeds). As those two segments grow relative to Progressive, reported operating cash flow will increasingly overstate cash conversion.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt was $893.7M at June 30 against $85.2M of cash and $7.2M restricted. Of that, $293.7M is Purchasing Power's non-recourse VIE debt, matched against $381.4M of contractual receivables usable only to settle those obligations; creditors have no recourse to the parent. Recourse net debt is therefore ~$514.8M, giving enterprise value of ~$2,226M and 6.1x the $365M FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint. On an all-debt basis, EV of ~$2,506M is 6.9x.

Deleveraging has been executed well. The company repaid $304.9M since the acquisition, retiring the $125M incremental term loan and all revolver borrowings within six months, taking management-stated net leverage to ~1.7x from ~2.5x post-close, inside its 1.5–2.0x target. Each 25bp rate move equals $0.9M of annual interest at full revolver draw, which is immaterial.

The weakness is asset quality of the equity itself. Goodwill and intangibles of $763.1M equal 94.75% of $805.3M of shareholders' equity; tangible equity is $42.3M, roughly $1.06 per share. For an asset-light fee business that is not a solvency issue, but it removes any asset-value floor and makes goodwill impairment a live headline risk if Purchasing Power underperforms.

One reported-data inconsistency worth flagging: the quote feed shows trailing and forward P/E both at 11.2 alongside trailing EPS of $3.04, which does not reconcile ($42.945/$3.04 = 14.1x). We use guidance and consensus figures throughout rather than the supplied ratio fields.

**The five-year frame.** Net income fell from $243.6M (2021) to $146.8M (2025), and Adjusted EBITDA from $390.7M to $273.1M, while the company retired ~44.8% of its post-spin share count for $1.12B. EPS is approximately flat across that span. Cumulative TSR through 12/31/2025 was $56.42 on a $100 base against $228.99 for the disclosed peer group. 2021 was a stimulus-inflated peak for subprime lease-to-own, so measured from 2022's $256.2M the Adjusted EBITDA trend is modestly upward, and FY2026 guidance of $355–375M would be a post-spin high. Both readings are defensible; the buyback-treadmill reading is the harsher one and the more useful discipline when judging an 8.8x multiple.

## Growth Analysis

Revised FY2026 guidance (all continuing operations): revenue $3,025–3,100M, net earnings $155.0–164.5M, Adjusted EBITDA $355–375M, diluted GAAP EPS $3.82–4.06, diluted non-GAAP EPS $4.75–5.00. Q3 guidance is revenue $715–750M, net earnings $36.0–42.5M, GAAP EPS $0.86–1.06.

The composition of the raise is the important part. Four's FY EBT midpoint rose $5.0M (to $22.0–25.0M), Purchasing Power's rose $1.0M, Other losses narrowed $1.25M, and Progressive Leasing's fell $4.0M, or 2.1%, even as its revenue range midpoint rose $10M. The most likely driver is credit: a 90bp increase in the write-off provision rate on ~$2.27B of guided annual leasing revenue is worth roughly $20M pre-tax annually, and management appears to be carrying part of that forward.

Consensus has moved with the guide: current-year EPS estimates rose 6.2% over 90 days to $4.94 and next-year 5.7% to $5.64, across eight analysts. The company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (+23.4%, +54.4%, −3.3%, +20.5%), which supports confidence in the range. This is positive earnings momentum, though not the order of magnitude (+30% or more in 90 days) that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is a mid-single-digit upgrade to a business whose profit engine is flat to declining.

Two structural questions govern the growth thesis:

**Does Four's per-dollar economics converge upward?** Four's EBT margin on revenue improved from 18.4% to 20.2%, and its provision ran 39.0% of segment revenue in both Q2 2026 and Q2 2025 — stable, with the disclosed credit mix shifting toward higher quality (Category A 37.5% versus 19.6%). Gross loans fell from $108.9M to $94.1M while total past due rose from 19.3% to 27.2% and 60+ days from 2.9% to 6.3%, but December 31 sits immediately after a holiday origination surge that mechanically dilutes past-due ratios, and the June 2025 aging table needed for a same-season comparison is not available. In dollars, past-due balances rose from roughly $21.0M to $25.6M on a book 13.6% smaller, which is not nothing. We treat Four's credit as unresolved rather than deteriorating.

**Does Purchasing Power deliver the second half?** H1 revenue was ~$237.5M with a $(7.8)M pre-tax loss. Full-year guidance of $620–640M and $17.0–21.5M EBT implies H2 revenue of ~$382–402M (roughly 50% above the Q2 run rate) and H2 EBT of ~$24.8–29.3M. At the consolidated level, guidance implies Q4 revenue of ~$868M, 18.4% above the Q3 midpoint. Purchasing Power is a holiday-gifting employee purchase catalog and the 10-K confirms demand is greatest in Q4, so genuine seasonality is the most likely explanation. It is a concentrated bet: essentially all of the acquisition's promised first-year profit sits in two unreported quarters.

Guidance explicitly assumes no change in current consumer financial pressures, no material change in decisioning posture, and no meaningful rise in unemployment among the target consumer base. That third assumption is doing real work, though the specific macro claims about labor and mortgage markets are not established in the provided sources. [unverified]

The peer set is heterogeneous and low quality — two of five have negative earnings, and the closest true comparables (Upbound's Acima, Katapult) are not in the group — so the 14.0x median is context rather than an anchor. More useful is the internal question of which earnings figure to capitalize. The gap between guided non-GAAP ($4.875) and GAAP ($3.94) midpoints is $0.935 per share, roughly $38.1M after tax or ~$51.5M pre-tax on ~40.8M diluted shares. Identifiable components reconcile closely: estimated Purchasing Power acquired-intangible amortization of ~$31M annually, ~$10.7M of H1 acquisition and transaction costs, ~$3.9M of Purchasing Power restructuring, and a $4.8M intellectual-property litigation settlement charge in Q2. About 60% of the add-back is recurring, non-cash intangible amortization that will persist for a decade. An investor who declines that add-back is buying at ~11x, not ~8.8x.

Applying that discipline, the stock is inexpensive rather than deeply mispriced. The case that the market is wrong rests on three specific points: (1) rising estimates and a raised guide place the price below the lowest published analyst target of $45 against a mean of $53.43 and median of $50, with the rating mix improving to six buy/strong-buy and one hold; (2) recourse leverage at ~1.7x with $299.4M of buyback authorization equal to 17.5% of market capitalization gives management a lever it has begun to pull, repurchasing in Q2 at an average of $36.37; (3) FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance would be a post-spin high.

The case that cheapness is warranted is equally specific: Progressive Leasing's FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint of $2.266B sits at or slightly below our estimate of its FY2025 revenue (~$2.32B), so the highest-margin business is still not growing; its EBT guidance was cut; the write-off rate is rising; and the last five years demonstrate that a low multiple on this asset has not translated into shareholder return. A stock that stayed cheap while EPS went nowhere and TSR ran 173 percentage points behind its peer group requires an explicit catalyst before cheapness is rewarded. The catalysts here are real but back-half-loaded and dependent on assumptions management has explicitly conditioned.

## Competitive Landscape

The FY2025 10-K is unusually candid about the limits of Progressive's position, and that candor should be taken at face value. Progressive depends on point-of-sale partners "over whom Progressive Leasing does not exercise full control" for advertising, application assistance and even explaining the transaction; it has "no recourse against its POS partners if they do not prominently present, integrate or support it as a payment option"; competitors seek "more prominent placements ahead of Progressive Leasing within our POS partners' payment platforms"; competition for smaller partners "has intensified significantly in recent years"; many regional agreements run three years or less; and the company holds no patents at all.

Concentration is quantified and has twice been realized. In 2025, 54.8% of consolidated revenue came from customers of Progressive's top three POS partners and 77.0% from the top ten. Big Lots, a former top-ten partner, liquidated in December 2024 and hurt 2025. American Signature filed in November 2025 and is liquidating, which the 10-K states will hurt 2026. Both were furniture and home-goods retailers, the core of lease-to-own demand, and the partner base is structurally populated by financially fragile mid-market chains. The 10-K also flags renegotiation risk on merchandise pricing, exclusivity and rebate terms, bargaining leverage that moves against Progressive as its own volume shrinks.

Purchasing Power adds ~20% of revenue on the FY2026 base, which will mechanically reduce the reported concentration percentages in the next 10-K. The absolute dollars at risk with those top three partners are approximately flat, so the percentage improvement will describe mix, not risk reduction. The genuine defensive value is that a top-three partner loss would now hit a company with two additional profit pools, one of which (employer payroll deduction) is structurally lower-loss than open-market subprime credit and reaches consumers through a channel competitors do not occupy.

The upstream dynamic is the more durable threat. As BNPL and near-prime installment lenders extend further down the credit spectrum, they intercept applicants before the lease-to-own waterfall ever runs. PROG's answer is to own a BNPL app of its own, which is coherent strategically and dilutive economically at current unit margins.

Trajectory matters more than level here: the core franchise is holding scale while losing revenue and pricing power, and the company is buying adjacent distribution rather than widening the original moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit is the swing variable.** Progressive's write-off provision at 8.4% of leasing revenue consumed essentially the entire year-over-year change in segment EBT. Guidance assumes no meaningful rise in unemployment for its consumer base. Tightening decisioning protects losses but suppresses GMV, a trade-off the 10-K acknowledges directly. Sensitivity: 100bp on the guided leasing revenue base is ~$0.41 per share after tax, roughly $3.70 of value at 9x.

**Partner concentration.** 54.8% of revenue from three undisclosed partners, with two former top-ten partners destroyed in 14 months and no contractual protection against deprioritization at checkout.

**Second-half concentration at Purchasing Power.** All of the acquisition's guided first-year profit is in unreported quarters, requiring a ~50% sequential revenue step-up and a swing from a $(0.3)M Q2 loss to ~$25–29M of H2 EBT.

**Goodwill against thin tangible equity.** $763.1M of goodwill and intangibles versus $42.3M of tangible equity. A Purchasing Power shortfall creates impairment-headline risk with no book-value cushion, even though recourse leverage at ~1.7x is not a solvency concern.

**Regulation across three regimes.** Progressive operates under the April 2020 FTC settlement ($175M paid) with continuing compliance obligations dependent on partners it does not control, and received an FTC compliance document request in Q3 2024. Four faces New York's May 2025 BNPL statute (fee caps, licensing, ability-to-repay) and a December 2025 coordinated inquiry by seven state attorneys general that targeted six providers — Four was not among them, but multistate actions rarely stop at the first recipients. Purchasing Power introduces wage-assignment, payroll-deduction and retail installment sales law exposure. A putative class action alleges Military Lending Act and Truth in Lending Act violations at MoneyApp. A $4.8M IP litigation settlement charge landed in Q2, notable given the company holds no patents and therefore cannot counterclaim.

**Financing dependence at Purchasing Power.** The ~$338.6M of non-recourse securitization and warehouse facilities carry covenant and performance triggers that could force early amortization or loss of servicing rights. PROG has no recourse obligation, but losing the facilities would halt originations.

**Internal controls.** The FY2025 10-K adds risk language on integrating Purchasing Power into ICFR while simultaneously implementing a new ERP.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 56.5% against 44.6% one-year realized (82nd percentile within the chain) with backwardation into the longer tenors indicates the market is pricing near-term event risk despite earnings having already passed on July 29. Put/call skew is balanced at −1.0%, put/call volume is 0.60 and put/call open interest is 0.03; positioning is not directionally bearish. Since the prior snapshot in late May, ATM IV compressed from 54.2% to 42.5% while the stock rose 14.3%, consistent with resolved earnings uncertainty rather than accumulating stress. Two caveats: the chain contains only 38 contracts across five expirations, so these readings are low-confidence; and short interest at 9.1% of float with 4.7 days to cover is moderate — enough to amplify a positive surprise, not enough to constitute a squeeze setup. The net read is elevated near-term uncertainty pricing without a directional bet, which does not challenge the fundamental view either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $62 (+44.4%)

Progressive's write-off rate stabilizes near 8.4% as the 2025 GMV decline finishes rolling through the revenue tail, and its GMV growth (+3.4% in Q2) turns revenue positive in 2027. Four's EBT margin continues improving from 20.2%, and its FY EBT roughly doubles from the raised $22–25M range as scale absorbs fixed app-acquisition cost. Purchasing Power delivers mid-range H2 EBT, transaction and restructuring costs of ~$14.6M do not repeat, and the platform annualizes toward $40M+ of EBT in 2027 on an employer channel with structurally lower loss rates than open-market subprime. Net leverage at 1.7x frees the full $299.4M authorization, retiring 15%+ of shares over two years at a high single-digit earnings multiple. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $5.64 or better and the multiple re-rates toward 11x as the market accepts that the earnings base has stopped eroding. Analysts' $64 high target becomes the reference rather than the outlier.

### Bear Case — $30 (−30.1%)

Progressive's loss rate rises another 100–150bp as subprime household finances deteriorate, and lease-to-own demand concentrates in the discretionary durables most sensitive to consumer stress. Tightened decisioning protects the P&L but pushes GMV back to decline, resuming the pattern that took FY2025 GMV down 8.6%. Another mid-market partner in the top ten follows Big Lots and American Signature into liquidation. Purchasing Power's Q4 falls short of the implied ~$868M consolidated revenue quarter, forcing a guidance cut and putting $763.1M of goodwill under review against $42.3M of tangible equity. Non-GAAP EPS settles near $4.30 and the multiple compresses to 7x, returning the stock toward the $30 area it occupied for most of the past twelve months. The five-year record — Adjusted EBITDA down 30% from 2021, TSR of −43.6% against a peer group at +129% — reasserts itself as the base rate.

- **Base case target: $50** — 9.5x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of ~$5.30, a modest haircut to $5.64 consensus for mix dilution and credit. **+16.4% from $42.945.** This sits at the analyst median and just above the low target of $45.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Progressive Leasing's provision for lease merchandise write-offs holds at or below 8.4% of leasing revenue for two consecutive quarters while its revenue turns year-over-year positive.

## Conclusion

PROG Holdings is doing several sensible things at once. It sold Vive, bought a differentiated payroll-deduction platform at 7.1–7.9x Adjusted EBITDA, cut $304.9M of debt in six months to reach ~1.7x net leverage, and restarted repurchases at prices below today's. Consensus estimates have risen 6% over 90 days and the shares trade below the lowest published analyst target.

What holds the rating at HOLD is the composition of the earnings. Consolidated GMV grew 60% and GAAP EPS from continuing operations declined, because Progressive Leasing earns 10.6 cents of pre-tax profit per GMV dollar while Four earns 2.3 cents and Purchasing Power currently earns none, and Progressive is the segment whose revenue is falling and whose full-year EBT guidance was reduced inside a raised consolidated guide. Roughly 60% of the non-GAAP add-back is recurring amortization, which makes 11x the honest anchor rather than 8.8x. Tangible equity is about a dollar a share. The plan requires a Q4 that is 18% larger than Q3 at the consolidated line, resting on a seasonal business acquired eight months ago, and it assumes a subprime consumer base holds up.

Existing holders should stay long: the balance sheet is materially better than a year ago, estimates are rising, and $299.4M of authorization against a $1.7B market capitalization is a real lever. New capital has a better entry available, either the $36–38 range, or the October print showing Progressive's loss rate flat and Purchasing Power's ramp underway before the Q4 concentration risk resolves. Base case $50, bull $62, bear $30.
