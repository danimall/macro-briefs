## Executive Summary

EnerSys is a well-run industrial stored-energy franchise whose reported earnings are, to an unusual degree, a function of one line of tax policy. Section 45X advanced manufacturing production credits — recorded as a reduction of cost of sales — contributed approximately $158.3M in FY2026 against $293.6M of GAAP net earnings (~54%), and $47.2M against $116.5M in the June-quarter (Q1 FY2027, ~41%). The credit is essentially untaxed (Q1 as-reported effective tax rate 13.7% versus 21.8% before the credit) and it is cash-real: the company collected a $115M federal refund in Q1. The FY2026 10-K risk factor warns the credit could "expire" but names no date or phase-down schedule, and we were unable to verify the statutory schedule independently. That single unknown determines whether the correct anchor is 16.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $12.29 or roughly 25x the ~$8.20 of ex-credit earnings power implied by management's own Q1 actual and Q2 guide.

The operating story underneath has improved. Backing 45X out of both years, FY2026 gross margin was flat at ~25.1% versus ~25.1% in FY2025; the reported 94bp compression was entirely a $26.5M smaller credit. Q1 FY2027 is the first quarter of genuine expansion: core gross margin of 25.2% was up 110bp, and adjusted EPS excluding both 45X and the tariff refund rose to $1.78 from $1.26 (+41%). Net leverage fell to 0.8x credit-agreement adjusted EBITDA of $671.6M, primary operating capital improved $135M, and the dividend was raised 10% to $0.2875 per quarter. The May 2026 segment realignment produced the year's most useful disclosure: the pure-play aerospace and defense business (Precision Power Solutions) printed 18.2% adjusted operating margin on $101M of revenue, roughly double what the old "Specialty" segment implied, and grew 24%.

Against that, the August 12 print was a lower-quality beat than the headline suggests. Reported adjusted EPS of $3.66 benefited from a newly loosened non-GAAP definition (SBC now excluded, worth $0.16) and a non-recurring $31M IEEPA tariff refund worth $0.63 that was not in guidance. On a comparable basis the actual was $3.50 against $2.82 consensus and a $2.85 guidance midpoint, a $0.65-0.68 beat of which roughly $0.63 was the refund. The stock rose ~9% on it and has added a further 4.9% since. Material handling orders fell high single digits and consolidated backlog was flat year over year, so the second-half recovery embedded in FY2027 expectations rests on customer conversations rather than bookings. At $207 our base case is $215 and the probability-weighted return is roughly +2%. HOLD.

## Company Overview

EnerSys designs, manufactures and services industrial stored-energy systems: industrial batteries (historically lead-acid, including proprietary thin plate pure lead, or TPPL), chargers, power electronics, and installation/aftermarket services. Roughly 40% of FY2026 net sales were non-U.S., with Europe about one-fifth. Manufacturing spans the U.S. plus eighteen other countries including the PRC. Fiscal year ends March 31.

Effective May 28, 2026, four legacy segments were collapsed into three:

| Segment | Content | Q1 FY27 revenue | Q1 FY27 adj. op. margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network & Infrastructure Solutions | Telecom/broadband power, data-center DC power, services | $428M (+9%) | 10.5% (+280bp) |
| Industrial Mobility Solutions | Material handling **plus** Class 8 transportation | $407M (−3%) | 9.3% (−70bp) |
| Precision Power Solutions | Aerospace & defense: thermal batteries, counter-drone, soldier power, space | $101M (+24%) | 18.2% (+280bp) |

The realignment is analytically meaningful. Moving transportation out of Specialty and into IMS revealed that the residual defense franchise earns 18.2% margins while the combined material-handling/transportation business earns 9.3%. The caveat is that segment margins also reflect cost allocations that changed with the realignment, and one quarter under a new methodology is thin evidence of steady state.

Two moats are identifiable from the filings. First, TPPL, which the 10-K states plainly "is important to our business and [is] not protected by patents," defended instead as a trade secret. Second, service and credential density: six U.S. CMMC- and ITAR-compliant A&D production facilities plus A&D production in the U.K. and France, and a global field-service network that underpins both the data-center and warehouse battery-storage strategies.

## Financial Analysis

### The 45X bridge

Everything begins here. Derived from the company's own non-GAAP reconciliations:

- FY2026 45X ≈ **$158.3M** (adjusted diluted EPS $10.56 less ex-45X $6.41 = $4.15 × 38.14M shares)
- FY2025 45X ≈ **$184.8M** ($10.15 less $5.58 = $4.57 × 40.44M shares)
- FY2026 gross margin ex-45X = ($1,097.6M − $158.3M) / $3,745.8M = **25.07%**
- FY2025 gross margin ex-45X = ($1,092.4M − $184.8M) / $3,612.4M = **25.12%**

Underlying gross margin was flat. The reported decline was a smaller credit. Similarly, the $38.2M drop in GAAP operating earnings ($464.7M → $426.5M) is 85% explained by two identifiable FY2026 charges: a $21.7M July 2025 restructuring charge and $10.8M of accelerated stock compensation on the CEO transition. Any mechanical screen reading FY2026 as margin, ROA and net-income deterioration is misreading a policy variable, and the mirror-image screen reading FY2026's cash-conversion as excellent is misreading a refund timing effect.

The credit's tax treatment is the underappreciated part. Q1 FY2027 pretax earnings of $135.0M less $47.2M of 45X leaves $87.8M; at the 21.8% ex-credit adjusted rate that implies $19.1M of tax against $18.5M actually booked. The benefit flows to net income nearly one-for-one. LTM 45X through Q1 FY2027 is approximately $167.3M and rising, with $42-47M guided for Q2. Management's own footprint decisions are explicitly credit-optimizing: the Monterrey-to-Richmond, Kentucky transfer added $9M of incremental 45X in Q1 alone.

### Cash flow quality

|  | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $260.3M | $547.6M | $230M |
| Capex | — | ~$100-120M (est.) | $12M (FY27 guide ~$70M) |
| Net earnings | $363.7M | $293.6M | $116.5M |

The FY2025-to-FY2026 swing is a 45X monetization timing effect, not an operating inflection. FY2025 operating cash flow fell well below net income because prepaid taxes built by roughly $192M as credits accrued ahead of cash recovery; FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 are the unwind. Normalizing across both years: $807.9M of combined operating cash flow against $657.3M of combined net income, or roughly $404M/yr against $329M/yr. Healthy conversion, considerably less dramatic than the FY2026 print alone.

Q1's $218M of free cash flow should not be annualized: it includes the $115M non-recurring federal refund. Ex-refund, core free cash flow was approximately $103M against $116.5M of net earnings.

One structural caveat. The receivables purchase agreement was expanded December 15, 2025 to $250M plus a $50M accordion (maturing December 2028), and receivables sold during the quarter rose to $336.2M from $189.9M a year earlier. Program fees of ~$9.2M in FY2026 sit in *other (income) expense, net* rather than interest expense. The CFO attributed the $135M primary-operating-capital improvement ($993M → $858M) to the expanded program "as well as" working-capital discipline without disaggregating. Increased securitization utilization is genuine cash but a one-time level shift that structurally depresses reported receivables; the filing discloses flows sold and collected within the quarter, not a period-end sold balance, so program utilization change cannot be computed.

### Balance sheet and capital allocation

Total debt including finance leases was $1,120M at March 31, 2026; cash was $531M at July 5, 2026, leaving net debt of $522M, down more than $160M since fiscal year-end. Net leverage of 0.8x credit-agreement adjusted EBITDA of $671.6M sits below the company's target range. There is no visible refinancing cliff at this leverage.

Buybacks deserve specific attention because the timing detail is a real finding. Derived average repurchase prices: approximately $86/share in Q1 FY2026 ($150.0M / 1,740,161 shares); approximately $108-111 for FY2026 as a whole ($370.7M against a ~3.35-3.44M treasury share increase); approximately $228 in Q1 FY2027 ($50.0M / 219,204 shares); and approximately $200 for July 6 – August 7, 2026 (249,893 shares / $50.0M). EnerSys retired roughly 8-9% of its shares in FY2026 at an average near $110 while the stock roughly doubled. The ~5.7% reduction in diluted shares supplied about half of FY2026's 15% ex-45X adjusted EPS growth ($5.58 → $6.41) against 8.2% growth in ex-45X adjusted net earnings ($225.8M → $244.4M); per-share growth was substantially financial. Management has cut the pace from $150M to $50M per quarter at $200-229. Roughly $901M of authorization remains.

The dividend was raised 10% to $0.2875 per quarter (0.56% yield at $207). The stated policy is the most informative sentence in the release: a dividend "that grows with earnings excluding 45X benefits." Management is refusing to underwrite a fixed obligation against the credit, which is prudent, and which tells you how they think about its duration.

Diluted shares of 37.63M against ~36.46M outstanding imply a ~1.2M share gap from in-the-money awards, equal to ~3.3% of existing shares (~3.2% post-issuance ownership dilution). No convertibles or warrants surfaced.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew 3.7% in FY2026 and 4.8% in Q1 FY2027 to $935.6M. Q2 FY2027 guidance of $955-995M is set against a prior-year quarter management describes as having "an unusually high proportion of sales phased in the second quarter." Consolidated Q1 orders rose 7% with a 1.06 book-to-bill, but backlog was flat year over year and up only 2% sequentially. Three end markets are running in opposite directions:

**Data center and communications (NIS)** is the strongest. Data center orders rose 80% year over year with deliveries extending 12-36 months; data-center revenue rose low teens against a high-single-digit-to-low-teens FY2027 expectation. Communications delivered record shipments on DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades. Management positions TPPL on resilience, deployment speed and lifecycle support rather than acquisition cost. The competitive threat is lithium battery backup units; the response, DataSafe Noir (launched June 2026, >500 units in quotation), is not expected to be revenue-material until FY2028 and depends on third-party cell supply.

**Material handling (IMS)** is in a cyclical trough that has not turned. Management expresses "a high degree of confidence that material handling demand will improve later this fiscal year," but Q1 material handling orders fell high single digits, IMS revenue fell 3% and IMS adjusted operating earnings fell 11%. Transportation orders rose ~91% but explicitly "off a low point." The offensive move is Fortix, a 172 kWh warehouse battery storage system with UL and NFPA 855 approval and the "first hundred systems on the water." Forklift battery demand may lag Class 8 truck orders by several quarters, in which case a transportation-first recovery sequence is consistent with management's framing; the second half may also disappoint. The order book does not yet distinguish these.

**Aerospace and defense (PPS)** is structurally accelerating: +24% revenue, 18.2% margins, demand "projected to continue to accelerate at least through 2030 driven by stockpile depletions," and European A&D revenue doubled in FY2026. Management estimates 9-11% annual market growth. This is only ~10.7% of revenue, so mix contribution builds slowly, but it is the segment least exposed to lead-acid substitution and the one where FEOC-origin requirements from the U.S. and allied nations function as an entry barrier.

The gigafactory was substantially descoped on July 23, 2026. The DOE grant was finalized at ~$150M (down from $199M), total facility cost is ~$650M with ~$500M of EnerSys net investment funded from operating cash flow, initial capacity is ~1 GWh and deliberately defense-focused, construction begins in H1 FY2028 with full production roughly three years later, and target IRR is mid-20s%. A separate ~$200M South Carolina/Greenville County package supports ongoing operations rather than offsetting capex. Management frames this as de-risking offtake, which is credible. It also means EnerSys remains dependent on third-party lithium cells for commercial data-center and material-handling lithium products for years, and pushes roughly $165M/yr of incremental capex into FY2028-FY2030, competing directly with the buyback in the same window in which the tax credit may be stepping down.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus rose to $12.29 from $11.78 ninety days ago (+4.3%) and from $12.09 thirty days ago (+1.7%); next year is $13.56, down 0.9% over 90 days. This is positive but modest, nothing resembling the >30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection. Consensus of $12.29 sits below what Q1 actual plus Q2 guidance plus a ~$180M annual 45X run rate would build to (roughly $13.5-13.7 on the new non-GAAP definition), suggesting consensus has not fully absorbed the August 12 print. That is mildly supportive of near-term estimate follow-through. The four-quarter beat record (28.2%, 5.6%, 0.8%, 7.7%) is real but the largest beat was almost entirely non-recurring.

At $207.00 with 36.456M shares outstanding, market cap is $7.55B and enterprise value approximately $8.07B against $522M of net debt.

| EV / same less ~$167.3M LTM 45X ≈ $504M | derived | **~16.0x** |

The ex-45X sensitivity is built from disclosed figures, not forecast: Q1 clean adjusted EPS of $1.78, Q2 guided ex-45X of $1.95-2.05, and second-half quarters of roughly $2.15 and $2.30 assuming the top-line acceleration management guides to. It is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The supplied set — POWL at 40.5x, VICR at 71.0x, ST at 75.9x, MRCY negative, AYI at 23.9x — has a median P/E of 71.0 that is arithmetically true and analytically meaningless. Against AYI (23.9x), the closest thing to a comparable diversified electrical franchise, ENS at 16.8x forward looks discounted; against the same 23.9x on ex-credit earnings, ENS at ~25x looks full. The peer set does establish that ENS is not the expensive name in its industry on headline multiples.

The honest framing: on headline adjusted earnings, ENS trades at a mid-teens multiple that a mid-single-digit-revenue-growth industrial with 0.8x leverage, 12% operating margins and a defense mix ramp can support comfortably, and consensus looks conservative relative to the current run rate. On ex-credit earnings, it trades in the mid-20s, which is a full multiple for that growth profile. The market appears to be capitalizing 45X at close to the full 16.8x without a duration haircut. We cannot verify the statutory phase-down schedule and therefore assign no sunset year, but the asymmetry of that unknown is unfavorable at 15-17x, because the upside from confirmation of durability is a modest re-rating while the downside from a step-down is a multiple reset on 40% of earnings.

**Scenario values, from $207.00:**

- **Bear $145 (−29.9%)** — 45X steps down materially into FY2028-29 and/or FEOC eligibility becomes a live question, material handling stays flat, Greenville capex crowds out buyback; 18x ex-credit earnings of ~$8.00 = $145.

The low target of $202.92 is already below the current price. We treat these as directional context, not independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's self-assessment is unusually candid: EnerSys operates in "an extremely competitive industry and [is] subject to pricing pressures," facing major international manufacturers plus numerous regional competitors, with three named structural pressures — excess capacity in some industry sectors, consolidation among industrial battery purchasers (buyer-side power), and foreign producers with significantly lower labor costs expanding export capacity into the Americas and Europe. It also concedes a specific cost disadvantage: "certain of our competitors own lead smelting facilities which, during periods of lead cost increases or price volatility, may provide a competitive pricing advantage." Raw materials are over half of cost of goods sold, lead is the largest, and EnerSys uses third-party toll operators and hedges only a portion of requirements.

On substitution: "We cannot assure you that our portfolio of primarily lead-acid products will remain competitive with products based on new technologies." The gigafactory descope reduces the company's optionality on that front: a broad commercial cell facility that would have provided self-supply against lead-acid obsolescence became a ~1 GWh defense-specific plant. Trajectory is therefore mixed: clearly improving in defense credentials (nine lithium chemistries across six CMMC/ITAR plants is not quickly replicated) and in data-center share of wallet (80% order growth), stable-to-eroding in commercial lithium self-supply and in material handling where the offensive move (Fortix) is nascent.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Section 45X duration — the dominant unpriced risk.** The FY2026 10-K risk factor warns that "future legislative enactments or administrative actions could limit, amend, repeal, or terminate IRA policies" and that "any reduction, elimination, or discriminatory application or expiration" of the credit may materially adversely affect results. It provides no phase-down schedule and no termination date. For a credit worth ~54% of FY2026 net earnings, that omission is conspicuous. We could not verify the statutory schedule and assign no sunset year, which is itself the risk: the market is pricing a policy variable it cannot date.

**2. FEOC / prohibited-foreign-entity eligibility gating.** Externally reported (JD Supra summaries, March 2026, unconfirmed by any EnerSys filing) IRS Notice 2026-15 provides guidance on the "material assistance cost ratio" that clean-energy component manufacturers must satisfy under new prohibited-foreign-entity rules to claim 45X, with interim safe harbors and unresolved "effective control" triggers. EnerSys manufactures in the PRC, discloses "a limited number of fully qualified suppliers" for battery components, and sources DataSafe Noir lithium cells from a third party. Management discussed FEOC on the August call solely as a customer-demand tailwind for defense products, never as a risk to its own credit eligibility, and the 10-K Item 1A does not mention the framework. This is plausible and entirely unquantified.

**3. Material handling has not turned.** Orders down high single digits, IMS revenue −3%, IMS adjusted operating earnings −11%, backlog flat year over year. The H2 recovery is a management expectation corroborated by conversations and third-party data, not by bookings.

**4. Earnings-composition volatility.** A single $31M tariff refund moved reported gross margin ~330bp in one quarter. The same mechanism can run in reverse.

**5. Greenville capex timing.** Roughly $500M of net investment across FY2028-FY2030 (~$165M/yr against current ~$70M annual capex) arrives in the same window in which the credit may be stepping down and competes with the buyback. At 0.8x leverage and ~$400M of normalized annual operating cash flow the claim that it is self-funding is plausible, but it constrains flexibility.

**6. Housekeeping items that lower confidence in disclosure precision.** The 10-K contains a broken and self-contradictory share-repurchase risk factor ("has authorized one share repurchase program… The other program authorizes"), retains COVID-19 and Brexit boilerplate, and describes the DOE award as $199M and "paused" with Verkor SAS as partner — descriptions overtaken two months later. The Q1 10-Q appears to place $21.7M of FY2026 restructuring cost "during the current quarter of fiscal 2027" while elsewhere stating the plan was substantially complete at March 31, 2026 and Q1 restructuring was ~$10.7M. Most plausibly drafting errors; management concluded internal control over financial reporting was effective. Also unresolved: whether the incentive-comp "Adjusted Operating Earnings" metric (up 9.8% to $547.3M in a year GAAP operating earnings fell 8.2%) includes 45X. Because the credit sits in cost of sales, inclusion is the default absent explicit adjustment.

**7. Legacy items.** Multi-year global ERP rollout, with the 10-K noting prior efforts produced "significant production and shipping delays, increased costs"; soil and groundwater investigation at "several" properties with potential joint-and-several liability; ~40% non-U.S. revenue with the largest FX exposure being conversion of dollar-based lead costs into European currencies.

We did not verify customer concentration in either direction; no named single-customer risk factor appeared in Item 1A, and improving segment mix toward NIS and PPS is not evidence of customer diversification.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 48.0% sits 6.9 points above 1-year realized of 41.1%, with the term structure in backwardation (48.0% at one week versus 44.0% at six months) and a 6.7% put/call skew at the front tenor characterized as elevated put demand. IV has already compressed from 55.7% on July 25 to 48.0%, so the earnings event premium has partly bled out; the residual front-end richness with a print already behind the stock is unusual and suggests either a near-dated catalyst not visible in the filings or post-event stickiness. Put/call volume of 0.04 is heavily call-skewed while open-interest put/call is 0.45 and rising from 0.40; recent flow is bullish against a moderately protected standing book. Short interest of 6.7% of float and 4.6 days to cover is uncrowded. Net read: the options market is pricing meaningful two-way movement (±5.6% on the nearest expiry, ±11.0% at one month) with a modest near-term downside tilt, which is consistent with our view that the current price embeds a favorable resolution of the credit question without pricing the alternative.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($270, +30%)

The credit persists or is extended, and EnerSys keeps expanding the eligible base through U.S. reshoring (the Monterrey-to-Richmond transfer alone added $9M of quarterly 45X). Core gross margin continues the Q1 trajectory (+110bp) toward 26%+ as the FY2026 restructuring and four plant closures annualize. Material handling inflects in H2 FY2027, consistent with a transportation-first recovery sequence (transportation orders +91%). Data-center orders (+80%, 12-36 month delivery) convert into revenue while DataSafe Noir adds lithium participation from FY2028. PPS compounds at 9-11% market growth with share gains at 18%+ margins, lifting consolidated mix. Consensus of $12.29 proves stale against a $13.50+ run rate; the market pays 19x, giving $275.

### Bear Case ($145, −30%)

The credit steps down on a schedule the market has not modeled, or FEOC/MACR eligibility becomes a live question given PRC manufacturing and third-party cell sourcing. Ex-credit earnings power of roughly $8.20 gets a normal industrial multiple of 18x, giving $145, and that assumes no operational disappointment. Material handling does not recover, backlog stays flat, and the H2 acceleration embedded in FY2027 does not arrive. Greenville capex of ~$165M/yr from FY2028 arrives simultaneously, ending the buyback that supplied half of recent per-share growth. Lead cost volatility exposes the structural disadvantage versus vertically integrated competitors.

### Why HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE

The base case of $215 is 3.9% above the current basis, and the probability-weighted outcome is roughly +2%. The margin of safety at $207 is negligible against a 30% bear case whose trigger is a statute we cannot date. The operating improvement is genuine but modest in magnitude (mid-single-digit revenue, 110bp core margin), the estimate revision profile (+4.3% over 90 days) does not reach inflection thresholds, and the largest recent beat was 93-97% non-recurring. The catalysts here — H2 material handling recovery, DataSafe Noir revenue, Greenville — sit two to six quarters out. This is a good business at a price that already reflects most of the good news, with one large unresolved variable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment point:** Q2 FY2027 results, expected early November 2026. Guidance is revenue $955-995M, adjusted diluted EPS $3.15-3.25 including $42-47M of 45X, ex-45X $1.95-2.05, adjusted ETR before 45X 21.5-23.5%, FY2027 capex ~$70M.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Material handling orders return to year-over-year growth and consolidated backlog rises 5%+ year over year — this converts the H2 recovery from assertion to bookings and validates the $13.56 FY2028 consensus.
2. The 45X phase-down schedule is disclosed or confirmed such that credits above ~$120M/yr are supportable through FY2029, with an affirmative statement on FEOC/MACR eligibility. This alone would justify anchoring on the 15-17x headline multiple.
3. Price weakness to **$160-170** (approximately 19-21x ex-credit FY2027 earnings power of ~$8.20, and 12-13x FY2028 headline consensus) without fundamental deterioration. At that level the bear case is largely priced.
4. Core gross margin (ex-45X, ex-tariff) sustains 26%+ for two consecutive quarters with PPS above 15% of revenue.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. Any disclosure quantifying a 45X step-down or an eligibility challenge under the prohibited-foreign-entity rules. Given the credit is ~40-54% of net income and essentially untaxed, this is a step-function event.
2. Two consecutive quarters of declining ex-45X, ex-one-time adjusted EPS, or core gross margin reverting below 25%.
3. Material handling orders down again in Q2 with backlog declining year over year, indicating the H2 recovery has slipped to FY2028.
4. Greenville capex guidance rising above ~$650M total or the buyback suspended to fund it, removing the lever that supplied half of recent per-share growth.
5. A restatement or material weakness finding — not our expectation given the effective ICFR conclusion, but the disclosure inconsistencies noted above lower the threshold for concern.

**What we are watching in the interim:** monthly repurchase disclosures (a return to $150M/quarter at these prices would be a negative signal on capital discipline; continued $50M is appropriate); any 8-K or 10-Q language addressing MACR/FEOC eligibility; any reconciliation of the Verkor SAS partnership status, which the 10-K names and subsequent announcements do not mention; and whether the November proxy-adjacent disclosures clarify whether incentive "Adjusted Operating Earnings" includes the credit.

**Existing holders** should maintain exposure: the balance sheet is strong, the dividend is covered many times over and is explicitly indexed to ex-credit earnings, and the defense mix shift is a genuine multiple support. **Prospective buyers** have no entry signal at $207; the $160-170 band offers materially better asymmetry for the same fundamentals.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 5.8.**

The score trajectory for this name has been declining (6.4 → 5.5 across five reports, with valuation down 1.7 points), and the August 12 print stabilizes rather than reverses it. What improved is real: core gross margin up 110bp, ex-credit ex-tariff EPS up 41%, net leverage down to 0.8x, primary operating capital down $135M, dividend up 10%, and the segment realignment surfacing an 18.2%-margin defense franchise growing 24%. That supports lifting financial health to 7.0 and holding growth at 6.0. What has not improved is the two things that would change the recommendation: the price is 8.5% higher than at our last review while the incremental earnings news was overwhelmingly one-time, and the central unknown — how long a credit worth 40-54% of net income persists, and whether the company satisfies newly articulated eligibility rules it has not addressed in any filing — remains unresolved and is now joined by an additional identified vector.

At $207 the stock is 16.8x FY2027 consensus and roughly 25x ex-credit earnings power. Those two numbers describe two different companies, and the market is currently paying for the first. Our base case of $215 offers 3.9% upside against a 30% bear case with a policy trigger. That is a fair-value holding, not a purchase.
