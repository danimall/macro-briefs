**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Leggett & Platt is no longer trading on its own fundamentals. On April 13, 2026, the company signed a definitive all-stock merger agreement to be acquired by Somnigroup International (NYSE: SGI), the former Tempur Sealy and the world's largest mattress company, at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.1455 SGI shares per LEG share. The headline equity value was roughly $2.5 billion based on SGI's April 10 price. US HSR antitrust clearance was obtained June 3, 2026; the deal is targeted to close by year-end 2026 pending LEG's shareholder vote and approvals in the EU, UK, Canada, Korea, and Austria. Any assessment of LEG today must be anchored to SGI's share price times 0.1455 plus a closing spread, not to LEG's standalone earnings power.

The standalone picture that drove two prior AVOID ratings (3.9 in March, 4.0 in May) remains weak and, if anything, deteriorated in Q1 2026: sales fell 10.2% YoY to $918M, EBIT dropped 29%, EPS came in at $0.14 against $0.22, and operating cash flow swung to negative $56M on a working-capital build. The company withdrew 2026 guidance after the merger announcement. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative (current-year consensus cut 18.8% in 30 days), and the quarterly track record is three misses in four quarters, including a 37% miss in Q1 2026. None of this would support a standalone HOLD.

The deal changes the call. At $10.77, LEG implies a modest spread to its all-stock consideration. With US antitrust cleared and the merger structured with a $64M break fee, a no-shop, and a unanimous board approval that followed the board's own rejection of a lower December bid, the path to close is reasonably visible. This is a HOLD classified as uncertain: the position is a probabilistic bet on deal completion against SGI's own share price rather than a directional bet on home furnishings demand, and the downside on a break is material. I would not add on standalone merits, and I would not short into a cleared, defined-spread arb.

## Company Overview

Leggett & Platt, founded in 1883 as the pioneer of the steel coil bedspring, is a diversified manufacturer of engineered components organized into three segments:

- **Bedding Products (~38% of sales):** Vertically integrated from a US steel rod mill (~500K tons/year) through drawn wire, innersprings (ComfortCore fabric-encased coils are >75% of US innerspring units), specialty foam, and private-label finished mattresses. This segment took the brunt of the multi-year downturn and the bulk of the impairments.
- **Specialized Products (~28%):** Automotive seat comfort, lumbar, and actuator systems (Schukra) plus hydraulic cylinders, roughly 84–86% manufactured outside the US. The Aerospace Products Group was divested in August 2025 for $280M net cash and a $91M pretax gain.
- **Furniture, Flooring & Textile Products (~34%):** Furniture motion hardware, flooring underlayment and carpet cushion, textiles, and geo components.

The company employs roughly 15,900 people across 104 facilities in 18 countries and claims #1 revenue share in several component categories. Its largest customer, at roughly 7% of sales, is Somnigroup, the acquirer; the top ten customers represent about 31%. The competitive moat is vertical integration, scale purchasing, proprietary product design, and a thicket of antidumping/countervailing duties protecting domestic innersprings and mattresses against imports.

## Financial Analysis

The standalone financials describe a business in a prolonged downcycle that briefly stabilized in 2025 on one-time gains, then weakened again in Q1 2026.

**Revenue trajectory:** $5.15B (2022) → $4.73B (2023) → $4.38B (2024) → $4.06B (2025), a 21% peak-to-trough decline driven by housing and bedding demand softness, raw-material price deflation, restructuring-related sales attrition, and divestitures.

**Earnings:** Two years of impairment-driven losses, -$137M in 2023 (including a $444M long-lived asset impairment in Bedding) and -$511M in 2024 ($676M goodwill impairment, mostly Bedding). The company returned to profit in 2025 at +$235M net income, EPS $1.69, but this was flattered by roughly $155M of one-time items: the $91M Aerospace gain, a $35M insurance gain, and $29M of real estate gains, partly offset by a $22M pension settlement. Organic volume fell 6% in 2025 and 9% in Q1 2026.

**Q1 2026:** Sales -10.2% YoY to $918M (organic -5%, divestitures -5%), EBIT $45M (-29% YoY), EPS $0.14 versus $0.22. Operating cash flow was -$56M against +$7M a year earlier, driven by inventory and scrap stockpiling. Management withdrew its 2026 guidance after the merger announcement.

**Balance sheet:** Goodwill fell from $1.49B (2022) to $751M after the write-offs. Debt/equity is 3.46x; $1.5B of senior notes sit in equal 2027/2029/2051 tranches at roughly 3.8%, with no commercial paper or revolver drawn. Cash was $587M at year-end 2025 and $511M in Q1 2026, but substantially all of it is held offshore, with roughly $111M effectively inaccessible and a $31M withholding-tax cost to repatriate. Altman Z is 2.06 (grey zone); current ratio 2.25; the credit-facility leverage covenant is 3.50x and the company is in compliance. The dividend was slashed from $1.82/year in 2023 to $0.20/year in 2025, ending roughly a half-century as a dividend aristocrat to prioritize deleveraging. FCF was $281M in 2025.

The financial-health score of 4.5 reflects a leveraged, structurally declining business with negative operating cash flow in the most recent quarter and a trapped offshore cash position, partly offset by covenant compliance, no near-term refinancing crisis (2027 is the first maturity), and the fact that the acquirer's balance sheet becomes the relevant one if the deal closes.

## Growth Analysis

There is no standalone growth thesis. Revenue has declined for three consecutive years, organic volume is negative, and management expects 2026 demand to remain muted. The estimate-revision picture confirms negative earnings momentum: current-quarter consensus is down 12.5% in 30 days, current-year consensus down 18.8% in 30 days and 20.6% over 90 days, and next-year consensus down 13.9%. The earnings surprise record is three misses in four quarters, capped by a 37.2% miss in Q1 2026.

The only forward-looking growth is the pro forma combined entity: approximately $11.2B in sales and $1.7B adjusted EBITDA across 175 facilities in 36 countries, with LEG holders receiving roughly 9% of the combined company. That is exposure to SGI's consolidated franchise post-close, not LEG growth. The growth-prospects score of 3.0 reflects the absence of any standalone expansion path and the heavily negative revision trend.

## Valuation Assessment

Standalone valuation metrics are misleading here. The 6.7x trailing P/E on $1.69 EPS rests on earnings that were roughly two-thirds one-time gains; on the cleaner forward consensus of $0.88 for the current year, the multiple is closer to 12x on a declining business. Against Home Furnishings peers (LZB at 19.5x, ETD at 14.0x, MHK at 16.4x, and SGI itself at 29.4x), LEG screens cheap, but the discount reflects genuine structural impairment, falling estimates, and a trapped balance sheet. A low multiple on a deteriorating fundamental is a value trap, not an opportunity.

The relevant valuation is the merger math. At a fixed 0.1455 SGI-share ratio, LEG's intrinsic value is SGI's share price times 0.1455, less a discount for time-to-close and residual regulatory risk. At $10.77, LEG trades at a modest spread to that consideration, consistent with a year-end close and the deal premium of roughly 10–15% over the unaffected price reported by Zacks and Seeking Alpha. The analyst price targets (low $11.00, high $12.00, mean $11.50) cluster around the implied deal value, as expected for a cleared arb.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a defined arb spread to a likely outcome: there is identifiable, time-bounded upside as the spread closes and as SGI shares move, but the upside is capped by the fixed ratio and the downside on a break is steep. This is an arb with bounded asymmetry, not a deep-value mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

LEG sits upstream of a structurally challenged US bedding market that has bifurcated, with imports dominating the online and low-mid tiers and a wave of industry bankruptcies and consolidation. Its moat is real but eroding: vertical integration from steel rod to finished mattress, scale, and a protective wall of antidumping/countervailing duties (innersprings 116–234% through 2030, China mattresses up to 1,732% through 2030). LEG is an active petitioner in these cases, so any duty expiration or circumvention erodes share.

The Somnigroup combination is vertical integration, with SGI (Tempur Sealy, Mattress Firm, Dreams) folding in its largest component supplier after roughly 50 years of collaboration. This internalizes the customer-concentration risk but creates a fresh one: during the pre-close period, other bedding customers who compete with Tempur Sealy may reduce business with LEG. On the automotive side, Chinese EV makers are taking share from LEG's multinational OEM customers. The competitive-position score of 4.5 reflects a market leader in declining categories with a deteriorating trajectory, partially supported by duty protection.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by deal mechanics rather than the usual operating risks.

1. **Merger completion risk (dominant):** The fixed 0.1455 ratio means LEG holders bear full SGI share-price risk between now and close. Remaining conditions are the LEG shareholder vote, foreign antitrust (EU, UK, Canada, Korea), Austria FDI clearance, and S-4 effectiveness. A no-shop clause and break fee constrain alternatives. Rating agencies placed LEG on negative credit watch after the announcement.

2. **Customer-as-acquirer concentration:** Somnigroup is both the largest customer and the acquirer. A deal break removes the most natural buyer and reverts LEG to a standalone with negative Q1 operating cash flow.

3. **Secular demand decline:** The US mattress market continues to bifurcate; management expects muted 2026 demand.

4. **China withholding-tax assessments (new in 2026):** Authorities in Zhaoqing, Jiaxing, and Wuxi issued assessments totaling roughly $24M over beneficial-owner/dividend withholding. LEG is contesting and has recorded no expense, a fresh overhang absent from standard datasets.

5. **Geopolitical and tariff whipsaw:** The Feb 2026 Middle East conflict raised Hormuz shipping, helium, and petrochemical cost risk (relevant to automotive and foam). Section 232 steel tariffs help metal margins but hurt innerspring demand; the Supreme Court's invalidation of IEEPA tariffs leaves LEG with roughly $23M paid and an uncertain ~$8M refund, with potential customer claw-backs.

6. **Impairment cushion is thin:** At mid-2025, Bedding and Home/Work Furniture reporting units had fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 50%; a sustained standalone decline could trigger fresh charges if the deal breaks.

The risk-factors score of 4.5 reflects a binary outcome that is not symmetric: deal completion delivers the spread, while a break reverts the stock toward a distressed standalone value below current levels. US antitrust clearance has materially de-risked the most common deal-killer, which keeps this from scoring lower.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity is thin and the recent data is not highly informative: the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.20 to effectively zero between early May and mid-June, and the underlying barely moved (-3.3%). Zacks reported in early June rising implied volatility around the antitrust catalyst, consistent with an event-driven name where positioning concentrates near binary dates rather than reflecting a clean directional view. Short interest is elevated at 17.0% of float with 5.6 days to cover; in a cleared, defined-spread arb this is more likely arb-related hedging and merger-spread positioning than a clean fundamental short, and it raises modest squeeze risk on positive deal news.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The deal closes by year-end 2026. US antitrust is already cleared; foreign approvals follow; the LEG shareholder vote passes given the board's unanimous endorsement and the absence of a competing standalone path. LEG holders receive 0.1455 SGI shares each. If SGI shares hold or appreciate, the spread to current price closes and LEG holders capture SGI's combined-company upside as a ~9% pro forma stake. Implied value lands in the $11.50–$12.00 range, consistent with analyst targets and the deal arithmetic.

### Bear Case
The deal breaks on a foreign-regulatory snag (EU or Korea), an SGI financing or share-price shock, or a failed shareholder vote. LEG reverts to a standalone with negative Q1 operating cash flow, falling estimates, a negative credit watch, a trapped offshore cash position, a China tax overhang, and a structurally declining end market. Standalone fair value with no acquirer in sight is well below current levels; the prior AVOID thesis valued the business in the high single digits, implying meaningful downside from $10.77.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain because the outcome is binary and the position is an arb, not a fundamental conviction call.

**Reassessment timeline:** The LEG shareholder vote and the foreign-antitrust milestones (EU, UK, Canada, Korea, Austria) over the next two to four quarters, with a targeted year-end 2026 close. Each clearance is a discrete re-rating event that narrows the spread.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY as the spread widens or de-risks):**
- The spread between LEG's price and 0.1455 × SGI widens meaningfully while regulatory milestones continue clearing, offering an attractive defined-return entry.
- All foreign antitrust approvals clear and the shareholder vote passes, leaving only mechanical close, at which point the remaining spread is near-riskless return.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any foreign regulator opens an in-depth (Phase II) review or signals divestiture demands that threaten the timeline or the ratio.
- SGI's share price deteriorates materially, since the fixed ratio passes that decline directly to LEG holders.
- The merger is terminated or the parties signal renegotiation, at which point LEG reverts to the standalone AVOID thesis with meaningful downside.

**Reassessment point:** Next quarterly filing and each disclosed regulatory milestone.

## Conclusion

The recurring AVOID rating was correct on the standalone business: LEG's fundamentals are weak and still deteriorating, with three years of revenue decline, organic volume down 9% in Q1 2026, negative operating cash flow, uniformly falling estimates, and a value-trap valuation on declining earnings. What has changed since the last review is the structure, not the fundamentals: LEG is now a cleared, defined-spread merger-arbitrage position into Somnigroup at a fixed 0.1455 ratio, with the most common deal-killer (US antitrust) already removed.

The honest call is HOLD with an uncertain rationale. Holders are being paid a modest spread to a reasonably likely year-end close; selling here forfeits that spread, while shorting into a cleared arb with elevated short interest and a $64M break fee carries asymmetric squeeze risk on positive deal news. I would not buy on standalone merits, and I would not add unless the spread widens meaningfully as a defined return. The position tracks SGI from here, and the binary downside on a break is the real risk to monitor. Existing holders should hold to close and watch the foreign-regulatory calendar and SGI's share price as the live variables.
