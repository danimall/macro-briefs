**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Boeing is midway through an operational recovery from the deepest crisis in its history. First-half 2026 deliveries of 314 aircraft are the highest since 2018, the FAA has approved the 737 MAX rate to 47/month, FY2025 revenue rose 35% to $89.5B, and the company returned to GAAP profitability (+$2.2B net income) after two catastrophic loss years. Management used the $10.55B Digital Aviation Solutions divestiture to cut debt from $54.1B to $47.2B in a single quarter, and all three rating agencies moved their outlooks to stable at the lowest investment-grade rung. The DOJ 737 MAX criminal overhang was resolved with a non-prosecution agreement. On operational and balance-sheet-repair metrics, the turnaround is playing out largely as bulls hoped.

The financial profile remains fragile and the reported numbers overstate underlying earnings power. FY2025 net income was materially inflated by a $9.6B one-time divestiture gain; the core commercial airplanes business (BCA) still lost $7.1B, consolidated free cash flow was −$1.9B, and core EPS was only $1.19 versus $2.48 GAAP. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was −$0.2B and net income was roughly breakeven. The 777X program remains a chronic loss generator (−$4.9B reach-forward charge in 2025, delivery slipped to 2027, a fresh engine durability issue under investigation), and ~$27B of deferred 737/787 production costs sit in inventory awaiting recovery from future deliveries. Near-term consensus EPS estimates have fallen sharply over 90 days.

We rate Boeing a HOLD (growth_hold). The entire investment case hinges on free-cash-flow inflection in 2026–2027 as the 737/787 ramp converts the $695B backlog into cash, the same metric on which the CEO's compensation is built. That inflection is plausible and supported by the delivery data, but it is not yet in the reported results, and the stock at ~$205 trades at 82x trailing GAAP EPS with negative near-year estimates. The price has fallen ~11% over the recent window and sits ~19% below its 52-week high, which improves risk/reward but does not yet justify a BUY absent evidence of the cash turn. We would upgrade on confirmed positive FCF and clearance of the October 2026 SPEEA labor negotiation.

## Company Overview

Boeing is one of two global manufacturers of 100+ seat commercial jetliners (competing against Airbus) and one of the largest U.S. defense contractors. It operates three reportable segments:

- **Commercial Airplanes (BCA):** 737, 767, 777/777X, and 787 programs. FY2025 revenue $41.5B (46% of total), with a backlog of $567B, roughly 85% of which is non-U.S. airlines. Still loss-making (−$7.1B operating loss in 2025) on reach-forward charges, but recovering as deliveries ramp.
- **Defense, Space & Security (BDS):** military aircraft (F/A-18, F-15, KC-46, P-8), rotorcraft, weapons, satellites, and space (Starliner, SLS, ULA JV). FY2025 revenue $27.2B (30%). Operating loss narrowed to −$128M from −$5.4B in 2024 as legacy fixed-price charges were largely absorbed.
- **Global Services (BGS):** aftermarket parts, MRO, training, and logistics. FY2025 revenue $20.9B (23%). The consistently profitable segment at ~18% underlying margins; the reported 64% margin in 2025 is distorted by the one-time DAS divestiture gain.

The backlog is the defining asset: $694.7B at Q1 2026, roughly 7.6x annual revenue. Cumulative firm 737 orders total ~13,700 aircraft, and 777X firm orders rose from 358 to 568 in 2025. Boeing's binding constraint is manufacturing throughput, not demand.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory from crisis to recovery is clear in the numbers:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $77.8B | $66.5B | $89.5B (+35%) | $22.2B (+14% YoY) |
| Operating income | −$0.8B | −$10.7B | +$4.3B | +$0.45B |
| Net income (to shareholders) | −$2.2B | −$11.8B | +$2.2B | −$4M |
| Operating cash flow | +$6.0B | −$12.1B | +$1.1B | −$0.2B |
| Free cash flow | — | — | −$1.9B | negative |
| Stockholders' equity | −$17.2B | −$3.9B | +$5.5B | +$6.0B |
| Total debt | — | $53.9B | $54.1B | $47.2B |

Three points matter for the score. First, the top-line recovery is real and delivery-driven — 600 commercial deliveries in 2025 versus 348 in 2024. Second, earnings quality is lower than headline suggests: the DAS gain flatters both net income and BGS margins. Third, the cash story remains negative. The recovery becomes self-funding only when the 737/787 ramp converts backlog into cash.

The balance sheet is repairing but still stressed. Debt fell $6.9B in Q1 2026, funded by DAS proceeds, and stockholders' equity is back positive. The Altman Z-Score of 0.83 sits in the distress zone, reflecting thin equity on $168B of assets and $108B of current liabilities (much of it customer advances). We read the Z-Score as a legacy-of-losses artifact rather than an imminent bankruptcy signal, given $20B+ liquidity and $10B of undrawn revolvers, though it correctly captures a company with little margin for error. The reported ~30x D/E is distorted by the small equity base rather than a signal of aggressive leverage per se.

The hidden risk is the deferred production costs ($14.3B on 787, $12.5B on 737) sitting in an $87B inventory balance that must be recovered from future deliveries. This is an accounting bet on sustained production ramps; any protracted rate stall would pressure these balances and force charges.

**Financial Health: 4.0.** Improving from the distress trough, but negative FCF, distress-level Z-Score, thin equity, and deferred costs to recover keep this below neutral. A low financial-health reading is the most reliable loser-flag; Boeing has genuinely improved but has not yet cleared the bar for a mid-range score. We nudge the prior 3.5 up modestly on the deleveraging and equity restoration.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is strong. IATA industry net profits of ~$39.5B in 2025 rise to ~$41B in 2026, Boeing's 20-year outlook projects ~43,600 new aircraft, and both single-aisle and wide-body demand exceed supply. Boeing's problem is not orders; it is converting orders to deliveries.

The delivery ramp is the clearest evidence the thesis is working: 60 aircraft in May 2026, 64 in June, and 314 for the first half. The FAA has approved 737 rates to 47/month, with a fourth line and an eventual 63/month goal. Revenue grew 14% YoY in Q1 2026 on this cadence. On the defense side, the budget backdrop is favorable (FY26 DoW ~$839B, a $1,450B FY27 request cited in the 10-K), and BDS's problem was legacy fixed-price contracts, not demand, with those losses now largely absorbed.

The offset is estimate momentum, which is negative. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from −$0.18 to −$0.50 over 90 days, and next-year EPS from $4.37 to $4.10. The −100.9% current-quarter and −234.6% next-quarter 90-day revisions reflect a still-loss-making commercial business and continued charge risk. This is the opposite of the accelerating-upward revision pattern that would justify a high growth score. Growth is real at the delivery/revenue level but has not yet reached the bottom line, and analysts keep pushing the profit inflection out.

**Growth Prospects: 6.5.** The delivery ramp and enormous backlog support a durable multi-year revenue recovery, but falling near-term earnings estimates cap this. We hold the prior 6.5.

## Valuation Assessment

Boeing trades at ~$205, with a $163B market cap, at 82x trailing GAAP EPS — a number that is nearly meaningless given trough/inflection earnings and the one-time DAS gain distortion. The relevant metric is normalized forward earnings power. Consensus next-year EPS is $4.10, putting the stock at ~50x forward, still expensive against the peer group.

| NOC | Northrop Grumman | 17.3 | 1.8% |

Against a peer median of 26.6x, Boeing at ~50x forward looks stretched, but this is the wrong lens because Boeing is not a steady-state earner. The bull case is that Boeing's mid-cycle earnings power, once 737 hits 63/month and 787 normalizes, is substantially above the $4.10 next-year consensus, potentially producing normalized EPS well above the current forward figure by 2028–2029 at full rate. On that mid-cycle basis, the stock is not obviously expensive. Consensus price targets bracket this uncertainty widely: low $230, high $300, mean $270 — all above the current price, implying upside from $205.

The difficulty for a high valuation score is that the upside is entirely dependent on a normalization that has not yet begun to show in cash flow, while the near-term estimates are being cut. We do not treat proximity to consensus targets as a ceiling here (targets should re-rate up if FCF inflects), but neither is the current multiple cheap; the price already discounts a successful ramp. This is a fairly-valued option on a turnaround. Applying appropriate skepticism to nominal cheapness (a cheap trailing multiple that reflects trough earnings is not a buy signal on its own), we score this modestly below neutral.

**Valuation: 4.5.** Asymmetry to consensus targets exists, but the multiple already prices a successful ramp, near-year estimates are falling, and there is no independent catalyst forcing a re-rating before FCF turns.

## Competitive Landscape

Boeing's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving within a structurally advantaged duopoly. The backlog and 20-year demand outlook favor Boeing regardless of near-term execution (MAX backlog alone is ~4,900 units). But Airbus is winning the rate race, targeting 75 A320neo/month against Boeing's approved 47 (goal 63). Every month Boeing under-delivers versus Airbus is a month of relative share loss into a supply-constrained market.

The Spirit AeroSystems acquisition (closed Dec 8, 2025, ~$8.4B total consideration) re-insources 737/787/767/777 fuselage and structures production, addressing the root cause of prior manufacturing defects. It adds ~15,000 employees and integration risk, and Boeing must continue supplying certain divested Spirit lines to Airbus, an awkward but manageable arrangement. BGS remains the strongest segment at ~18% underlying margins with growing aftermarket demand.

Boeing is regaining certification and rate authority it lost after the door-plug event, which is share recovery rather than share loss, but it starts from a compromised position and remains gated by the FAA. The 777X's development saga (launched 2013) and repeated slips are a genuine competitive liability in the wide-body segment against the A350.

**Competitive Position: 6.5.** Advantaged duopoly with recovering share, offset by Airbus outpacing on rate and the 777X delays. We hold the prior 6.5, noting the dimension has declined from 8.0 earlier in the year as the relative-rate gap and 777X problems became clearer.

## Risk Assessment

Boeing carries an unusually dense set of company-specific, partly binary risks:

1. **Production ramp gating.** Rate increases require FAA concurrence; any KPI slippage stalls the FCF recovery that the entire thesis depends on.
2. **777X reach-forward losses.** The −$4.9B 2025 charge came on top of −$3.5B in 2024, and the fresh engine durability issue is under root-cause investigation. This is the most likely source of negative surprises in Q2–Q4 2026.
3. **737-7/737-10 not yet certified.** ~35 aircraft in inventory awaiting the engine anti-ice fix; certification "expected in 2026."
4. **BDS fixed-price development programs.** KC-46A (−$714M in 2025), VC-25B, T-7A, MQ-25, and Starliner carry ongoing charge risk, plus ~$1.0B in T-7A supplier termination liabilities.
5. **SPEEA labor negotiation (Oct 2026).** ~16,000 engineers; both prior strikes (IAM 751, 53 days in 2024; IAM 837, 101 days in 2025) occurred despite leadership-recommended tentative agreements, so the market may underappreciate this near-term binary to the H2 ramp.
6. **China/tariffs.** ~60% of BCA revenue is non-U.S.; China customers paused deliveries in Q2 2025, and a Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA tariff ruling introduced refund/replacement-tariff uncertainty.
7. **Dilution.** The 6.00% mandatory convertible preferred converts Oct 2027 into ~115M depositary shares; share count already rose from ~647M to ~788M via the Spirit stock deal and equity issuance. The common dividend has been suspended since 2020.
8. **Deferred production and DTA.** ~$27B of deferred 787/737 costs to recover, and a $9.75B deferred-tax valuation allowance that distorts reported tax rates (126.9% effective in Q1 2026).
9. **ULA Vulcan launch anomaly.** A new, small contingent liability that emerged in the Q1 2026 10-Q; a financial-support call on the 50/50 JV is possible.

On the positive side, the DOJ criminal overhang was resolved ($244M fine + $445M victim fund; dismissal approved Nov 2025, though a further appeal is pending), and the 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors from the 10-K. Rating outlooks are stable at all three agencies.

**Risk Factors: 4.5.** The 777X charge risk, SPEEA labor binary, and structural dilution are not fully priced, but much of the tail (DOJ, credit-rating spiral) has been removed. Net, still below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 40.8% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 32.1% (8.7pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 40.8% > 6-month 33.8%), and the near-dated put/call skew is elevated at 5.2%. This is consistent with hedging into a specific near-term catalyst, likely the upcoming Q2 earnings print and continued 777X charge risk. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, ~150K shares), so the bearish tilt is expressed through options rather than an outright short base, which limits squeeze dynamics in either direction. IV eased modestly (43.7% → 40.8%) and P/C OI fell (1.09 → 0.88) alongside the recent price bounce, suggesting some de-risking, but the backwardation indicates the market expects a resolving event soon. We read this as corroborating caution on near-term earnings, not as a fundamental red flag.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$300, +46%)
The 737 ramp holds at 47/month and progresses toward 63, 787 normalizes, and H1 2026's deliveries prove sustainable. FCF turns decisively positive in 2026 and accelerates in 2027 as deferred production balances are recovered. The 777X finally certifies and delivers in 2027 without further material charges. SPEEA settles without a strike. Deleveraging continues, credit outlooks upgrade toward higher IG rungs, and the backlog converts into rising normalized EPS by 2028–2029, supporting a $300 handle as the multiple compresses onto rising earnings.

### Bear Case (~$150, −27%)
A fresh 777X charge and/or a SPEEA strike in H2 2026 stalls the ramp and pushes FCF inflection into 2027+. Deferred production balances face write-downs, near-year losses persist, and the mandatory-preferred conversion in Oct 2027 dilutes into a weaker earnings base. Any China/tariff escalation freezes non-U.S. deliveries again. The stock de-rates toward the low $150s as the market re-prices the timeline.

### Base Case (~$245, +20%)
Deliveries continue climbing but with the occasional charge and integration friction. FCF turns modestly positive in 2026, confirming the cash inflection without a clean beat. SPEEA settles with disruption risk but no prolonged strike. The stock grinds toward the mid-$240s, near the analyst low target, as consensus gradually re-rates the forward earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to (a) the Q2 2026 earnings print and subsequent quarterly delivery/charge cadence, (b) the SPEEA labor negotiation resolving around October 2026, and (c) evidence of sustained positive FCF.

**Base case target: ~$245 (+20%).** Derived from a normalizing forward EPS trajectory discounted for execution risk and dilution — roughly midpoint of the $230 analyst low and $270 mean.

**Bull case target: ~$300 (+46%).** Full-rate normalization, clean 777X, and multiple compression onto rising EPS.

**Bear case target: ~$150 (−27%).** Fresh 777X charge and/or SPEEA strike delays the FCF turn and forces deferred-cost write-downs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow, confirming the ramp is self-funding.
- SPEEA contract signed without a prolonged strike.
- Next-year consensus EPS revisions turning positive (currently falling).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new 777X reach-forward charge above ~$2B or a delivery-date slip beyond 2027.
- A SPEEA strike materially disrupting the H2 2026 ramp.
- FCF remaining negative through 2026 with deferred production balances beginning to require write-downs, or a credit-outlook reversal back to negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, then the October SPEEA outcome.

## Conclusion

Boeing has done the hard operational work: record deliveries, restored profitability and positive equity, decisive deleveraging, and removal of the DOJ criminal overhang. The demand backdrop and backlog give the recovery a long runway. But the reported profits overstate underlying earnings power, free cash flow is still negative, the balance sheet remains thin, deferred costs must be recovered from future deliveries, and the 777X keeps generating charges. The stock at ~$205 already prices a successful ramp, and near-year estimates are falling.

The difference between a HOLD and a BUY here is a single metric: free cash flow. Until FCF inflects positive and the October SPEEA labor risk clears, the risk/reward is balanced — meaningful upside to consensus targets against a distress-level financial profile and dense near-term binary risks. We rate Boeing a HOLD (growth_hold) and would accumulate on either confirmed positive FCF or a pullback toward the mid-$180s that widens the margin of safety. The recent ~11% decline improves the entry, and the score edges up from 5.1 to 5.25 on the deleveraging and delivery evidence, but the case for a confident BUY awaits the cash turn.
