**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zscaler is a cloud-native zero-trust security vendor whose Zero Trust Exchange routes user, device, workload, and increasingly AI-agent traffic through a global inline proxy network, displacing perimeter appliances. The business is growing at 25% (Q3 FY2026 revenue $850.5M, ARR $3,525M, both +25% YoY), generating substantial free cash flow ($718.4M over nine months, 29% margin), and carrying a strong balance sheet ($3.54B cash against $1.725B of zero-coupon converts due 2028 that sit well out of the money). On the metrics that matter for a prepaid-SaaS model, this is a healthy franchise.

The stock tells a different story. Shares are down roughly 54% from June 2025 levels and ~60% from the November 2025 high of $337, including a ~25-30% single-session drop after the May 26 Q3 print. That print beat on revenue, non-GAAP EPS, and ARR and delivered a record 23% non-GAAP operating margin. What broke the stock was forward-looking: a soft Q4 guide ($875-878M against ~$878.6M consensus), an FY2027 ARR/revenue growth guide of only 16-17% (down from the current 25%), and an explicit disclosure that two sales leaders recently departed, which management cited as a driver of the conservative outlook. Management also flagged pulled-forward equipment spending to lock in pricing ahead of memory-cost inflation.

The investment question is whether the deceleration and execution stumble are temporary and addressable, in which case the compressed valuation (~29x forward non-GAAP EPS, ~6x EV/sales) is an entry, or whether they mark the early stages of structural growth maturation compounded by the AI-disruption-to-software overhang. The evidence is genuinely two-sided. The business quality and valuation reset argue for ownership; the topline deceleration, 25%-of-revenue stock-based compensation, leadership churn, and the absence of any insider buying on a 54% drawdown argue against high conviction. We rate ZS a HOLD with a constructive lean, contingent on execution evidence over the next two quarters.

## Company Overview

Zscaler operates a pure-subscription SaaS model built around the Zero Trust Exchange, a multi-tenant platform delivered through 160+ public exchanges and thousands of private edge exchanges. Core products are ZIA (Internet Access) and ZPA (Private Access), extended via a land-and-expand motion into data security, AI security, and security operations. Approximately 98% of revenue is subscription/support, billed annually or multi-year in advance, on 1-3 year contracts.

Scale is meaningful: more than 9,400 customers, presence in 185+ countries, and roughly 40% of the Forbes Global 2000 as customers. The company runs a single reportable segment. Distribution is channel-led, with ~85% of revenue flowing through partners and the top five partners representing ~30% of revenue; one channel partner accounts for 12% of accounts receivable. Geographically, revenue is ~53% U.S., ~28% EMEA, ~15% APAC. Around 37% of the total workforce is based in India, concentrated in R&D, finance, and operations.

The company is founder-led and founder-controlled. CEO and Chairman Jay Chaudhry and affiliates own ~16.6%, with insiders and 5%+ holders together near 34.9%. The board is classified with supermajority amendment provisions and no action by written consent.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and bookings.** Nine-month revenue reached $2,454.3M, up 26%. Remaining performance obligations totaled $6,459.3M, with 46% expected to be recognized within twelve months, providing solid forward visibility. The customer base grew 17% YoY, and the larger growth driver is now upsell to existing customers ($102.7M of the $172.4M Q3 ARR increase), a signature of a maturing land-and-expand model.

**Profitability and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap.** On a GAAP basis the company remains unprofitable: Q3 operating loss of $(29.6)M, net loss of $(13.9)M, and a nine-month net loss of $(59.8)M, against an accumulated deficit of $1.25B. The cause is stock-based compensation. SBC plus related payroll tax was approximately $212M in Q3 (25% of revenue) and $626M over nine months. Stripping SBC, non-GAAP operating margin reached a record 23% in Q3 (22% nine months), with non-GAAP gross margin ~80-81% versus a steady 77% GAAP gross margin. SBC is non-cash but it is real dilution; investors should weigh the strong cash generation against persistent GAAP losses and rising share count (158.3M to 161.7M YTD).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $850.4M over nine months, up from $554.8M a year prior. Cash and short-term investments total $3.54B against stockholders' equity of $2.37B. The capital structure includes $1.725B of 0.0% convertible senior notes due July 2028, with a conversion price of $439.52 (well above the current ~$134) and a capped-call cap of $784.85. The company repaid its $1.15B 2025 notes in July 2025 (cash plus 3.8M shares) and entered interest-rate swaps on $750M notional. A low Altman Z-score reading is misleading here; it penalizes large current deferred revenue (a feature of prepaid SaaS) and negative GAAP earnings. Liquidity is more than adequate to service or repay the 2028 notes.

**Capital allocation pivot.** Nine-month FY2026 acquisitions totaled $770M, more than doubling goodwill ($418M to $1.09B). There are no buybacks or dividends. The M&A spend concentrates in AI security: Red Canary ($651M, MDR/agentic security operations), SPLX/SPLXAI ($40.6M, with a measurement-period intangible markdown from $14.1M to $3.3M), SquareX ($112.8M, browser security), and the announced Symmetry Systems ($175M, data/identity mapping). This is a deliberate effort to convert the AI threat into product expansion, but it adds integration and goodwill-impairment risk, and the SPLX markdown is an early diligence caution.

## Growth Analysis

Zscaler is decelerating from a high base. The 25% ARR growth in Q3 is strong in absolute terms, but the FY2027 guide of 16-17% is the dominant new data point, representing a step-down of roughly one-third in the growth rate. Management attributes the conservative outlook to two specific factors: sales-leadership turnover (one of two departed leaders already replaced, one in process) and pulled-forward equipment spend to beat memory-price inflation. The first is a go-to-market disruption that should be addressable but requires evidence over the next two quarters; the second is a margin and FCF-timing issue rather than a demand signal.

Consensus EPS revisions are modestly positive: current-quarter +5.3% over 30 days, current-year +2.6% over 90 days, next-year roughly flat (+0.2% over 30 days). This reflects the margin story more than a topline acceleration. The earnings revision trend is constructive but the topline deceleration is the more important directional read.

The structural growth bull case rests on three planks: securing non-human and agentic AI traffic (which expands the addressable surface for an inline proxy), continued upsell within a large installed base, and TAM expansion via the AI-security M&A. The bear case is that growth maturation is now visible, competition is intensifying, and the AI-disruption narrative threatens both pricing and entry barriers in security software broadly. The company sits closer to security infrastructure than to displaceable application SaaS, which provides some insulation, but the deceleration is real and the durability of the re-acceleration thesis is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$134 and a market cap near $21.8B, ZS trades at roughly 29x forward non-GAAP EPS ($4.60 next-year consensus), ~32x current-year non-GAAP EPS (~$4.13), ~6x EV/sales (EV ~$20B after netting cash against converts), and ~21x EV/FCF, for a free cash flow yield near 3.6%. This is a meaningful reset from the multiples the stock carried at $300+, and it is cheap relative to the security-software cohort, where higher-growth peers trade at substantially richer EV/sales and forward earnings multiples.

The critical valuation caveat is the SBC framing. Non-GAAP EPS excludes ~25% of revenue in stock compensation. On a GAAP basis the company loses money, and the dilution is recurring. A buyer of ZS on a 29x non-GAAP multiple is implicitly accepting that SBC is a "real but acceptable" cost of an asset-light, founder-equity-heavy model. The honest economic anchor is free cash flow, which is robust but flattered by the SBC add-back. On EV/FCF of ~21x for a business growing 16-25% with 23% non-GAAP operating margins and strong retention, the valuation is reasonable rather than deeply asymmetric.

Analyst targets bracket the current price meaningfully: low $145, high $250, mean $193.79, median $190 — all above spot. Targets were cut across the board after the print (UBS $260→$225, Wedbush $300→$220, Citizens $290→$210), yet the lowest standing target ($145) still implies modest upside, and the mean implies ~44%. With positive estimate revisions and a forward multiple below 30x, consensus targets here lean toward floors rather than ceilings, but the recent cuts signal that even bullish analysts want execution evidence before pressing. The valuation supports a constructive hold; it does not, on its own, clear the bar for a high-conviction long given the unresolved execution and growth-durability questions.

## Competitive Landscape

Zscaler retains an architectural and scale advantage in inline zero-trust: 160+ public exchanges, a purpose-built multi-tenant proxy, and penetration into ~40% of the Forbes Global 2000. The strategic counter-thesis to AI disruption is coherent: AI expands the attack surface and creates non-human, agentic traffic that must be secured inline, which plays to Zscaler's design rather than against it.

The competitive trajectory is clouded rather than clearly improving. The company competes against the Palo Alto/Cisco/Fortinet cohort moving to cloud and point-solution vendors across CASB, DLP, SWG, and ZTNA. Larger rivals can bundle and undercut on price. Growth decelerating from a larger base, combined with sales-leadership departures that disrupt go-to-market, suggests pace pressure even if the moat is intact. The AI-disruption overhang affects the entire security-software group; for Zscaler specifically, the 10-Q now discloses that AI-enabled vulnerability tools are surfacing more vulnerabilities in its own code, diverting engineering capacity from features to patching. The net read is a strong moat on a stable-to-slightly-deteriorating trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth deceleration and execution.** The FY2027 guide and the departure of two sales leaders are the proximate causes of the selloff and the central risks. Management framed both as addressable, but the next two quarters carry a high burden of proof. Continued sales attrition or a further guide-down would confirm a structural rather than transitory problem.

**Stock-based compensation and dilution.** SBC at ~25% of revenue is the bridge between Zscaler's cash generation and its GAAP losses. The accumulated deficit is $1.25B and rising, and share count continues to grow. CEO compensation actually paid was $47.4M in FY2025 on a heavily equity-weighted structure, and five-year TSR (220) lags the peer group (281) even before the 2026 drawdown.

**AI as a double-edged sword.** The risk factors now frame AI both as the largest opportunity and as a threat that could let customers build their own tooling and lower entry barriers for AI-native competitors. This is a market narrative echoed in the filing, not a confirmed fundamental trend, but it is the dominant sector overhang.

**Concentration.** Top-five channel partners represent ~30% of revenue and one partner is 12% of accounts receivable; ~37% of the workforce is in India. Loss of channel relationships or India operational disruption would be material.

**Integration and M&A risk.** Goodwill more than doubled to $1.09B on ~$980M of AI-security acquisitions in roughly ten months; the SPLX intangible markdown is an early caution flag.

**Convertible notes.** The 2028 converts are out of the money and well-covered by cash, a manageable but monitorable item.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation (near-term ATM IV 71.2% vs. 66.4% at six months), indicating the market is pricing near-term stress. IV is rich relative to one-year historical volatility of 62.6% (an 8.6-point spread), at the 75th percentile of the current chain. The skew is call-heavy (-9.2% near-term), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.94 is balanced (put/call OI ratio 1.03), so the positioning is not dominated by put demand despite the recent crash. Short interest is elevated at 10.6% of float with four days to cover. The combination of backwardation pricing imminent stress and call-tilted skew reads as a market braced for volatility with some positioning for a bounce. The rich IV makes ZS an expensive vehicle for buying premium and a reasonable one for selling covered calls against existing holdings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$190-225, +41% to +67%).** The Q3 print beat across revenue, non-GAAP EPS, and ARR, reached a record non-GAAP operating margin, and produced strong nine-month FCF. The soft guide is attributable to two addressable factors (sales-leadership turnover, with one replacement already made, and timing-driven equipment spend), not a demand collapse. As go-to-market stabilizes over the next two quarters and the AI-security M&A begins to contribute, growth re-rates toward the high teens to low 20s, and the EV/sales multiple expands back toward the security-infrastructure cohort. Analyst targets at a mean of $194 and high of $250 frame the upside. The inline zero-trust architecture is a structural beneficiary of agentic AI traffic.

**Bear Case (~$95-110, -18% to -29%).** The FY2027 16-17% guide is the first visible sign of structural growth maturation, not a one-quarter air pocket. Sales-leadership departures reflect deeper go-to-market issues. The AI-disruption narrative compresses security-software multiples broadly, and Zscaler's own disclosure of AI tools surfacing more vulnerabilities in its code diverts engineering capacity. SBC keeps the company GAAP-unprofitable while diluting shareholders, and the EV/sales multiple compresses toward 4x (~$95-100) as the market reprices a 16% grower with no GAAP earnings and no insider conviction to support the dip.

The balance between these cases is roughly even, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy. The business quality and reset valuation are real; so are the deceleration, the execution stumble, and the lack of an insider buy signal.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected late August/early September 2026), which will provide the first read on whether the sales-leadership transition has stabilized bookings and whether the FY2027 guide is conservative or accurate.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q4 ARR growth holds at or above 22-23% with no further guide-down, and management confirms the replaced sales leadership is restoring pipeline conversion.
- Net new ARR re-accelerates and the FY2027 growth framing is revised upward, alongside continued positive EPS revisions.
- Open-market insider purchases by C-suite executives near current levels, providing a conviction signal absent today.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further FY2027 guide-down below ~15% or additional unplanned sales/executive departures.
- Free cash flow margin compressing materially below the mid-20s on a sustained basis, signaling that the "timing" of the memory-cost pull-forward was masking a structural margin issue.
- A goodwill impairment on the AI-security acquisitions or evidence that the deals are not contributing to ARR.

**Monitoring:** SBC as a percentage of revenue (watch for it to fall as the company matures), customer-count growth, upsell mix, and the AI-vulnerability-tooling engineering-diversion disclosure.

## Conclusion

Zscaler is a high-quality, cash-generative zero-trust security infrastructure platform whose stock has been reset by roughly 54% on a guidance deceleration and a self-inflicted sales-leadership disruption rather than a broken demand picture. At ~29x forward non-GAAP EPS, ~6x EV/sales, and a ~3.6% free cash flow yield, the valuation is reasonable and the analyst target distribution sits above the current price. Against that, the FY2027 16-17% growth guide is a genuine fundamental deceleration, stock-based compensation at 25% of revenue keeps the company GAAP-unprofitable and dilutive, the AI-disruption overhang is a live sector risk, and insiders did not buy the dip after selling materially higher.

The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the next fundamental catalyst is more than 60 days out. We rate ZS a HOLD with a constructive lean. The path to a BUY is concrete: evidence at the next two prints that sales-leadership turnover has not impaired bookings, that growth is holding above the high teens, and ideally an insider conviction signal. The path to an AVOID is equally concrete: a further guide-down, additional executive attrition, or margin compression that reveals the recent FCF strength as timing-driven.
