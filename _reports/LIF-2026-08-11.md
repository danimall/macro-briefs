## Executive Summary

Life360 runs a freemium family location-sharing platform with 102.4 million monthly active users, 3.2 million Paying Circles and 3.7 million paid subscriptions, supplemented by Tile Bluetooth trackers, Jiobit GPS wearables and, since the January 2026 Nativo acquisition, a third-party advertising technology business. Q2 2026 revenue grew 37.8% to $158.959M with subscription revenue up 31% and core app subscription revenue up 34%. Paying Circles rose 27% while ARPPC rose 5% and ARPPS 11%, so volume and price are advancing together rather than growth being bought with discounts. International Paying Circles grew 32% with ARPPC up 14%. The subscription franchise is genuinely healthy.

The problem is what happens between revenue and profit. GAAP operating income was **$(0.057)M** in Q2 2026 versus +$1.992M a year earlier, and the first half swung to an **$(8.135)M operating loss** from +$4.181M. Reported net income of $7.840M for the half was produced on a **pretax loss of $(7.821)M** by a $15.661M income tax benefit, $11.7M of which was discrete and largely excess tax benefits on stock compensation. Diluted EPS fell 25% to $0.06 from $0.08. Trailing-twelve-month GAAP operating income works out to **$6.5M on $572.6M of revenue** (FY2025 $18.8M + 1H26 $(8.135)M − 1H25 $4.181M), a 1.1% margin. Stock-based compensation reached 14.4% of Q2 revenue and grew 50% year over year against revenue's 38%, with $194.0M of unrecognized RSU expense remaining over roughly three years.

Three specific mix effects explain the operating-line erosion. Advertising revenue grew 315% but its gross margin fell from 90.3% to 56.8%, delivering only 46.1% incremental gross margin on the increase. Hardware's reported 43% Q2 gross margin includes a disclosed $3.6M tariff-refund benefit booked in cost of hardware revenue; excluding it, the underlying margin is approximately 6.6%, and by subtraction Q1 2026 hardware ran a roughly $(4.1)M gross loss. Operating expenses rose to 80% of revenue from 77%, with R&D +47%, S&M +35% and G&A +57%. On the authoritative close the enterprise carries 7.5x FY2026E revenue and 36.9x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA, a measure that excludes an estimated $80M+ of 2026 stock compensation. That combination of premium multiple, decelerating gross-profit quality and unfavorable scenario asymmetry supports **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

The core product is the Life360 app: location sharing, safe-driver reports, and crash detection with emergency dispatch, free at the base tier with paid Gold and Platinum tiers. Around it sit Tile (Bluetooth item trackers, acquired 2021), Jiobit (GPS wearables, 2021), a recently launched Pet GPS tracker, and Nativo's ad serving, content distribution and measurement platform. Four revenue lines in Q2 2026: subscription $115.6M (72.7% of total, +31%), advertising $21.966M (13.8%, +315%), other data and partnerships $11.6M (7.3%, +25%), hardware $9.806M (6.2%, −20%).

The defining structural fact is distribution. The apps are accessed almost exclusively through the Apple App Store and Google Play. **Apple accounted for 50% of Q2 2026 revenue and Google 19%** (51%/20% for the half; 52%/19% for FY2025), with Apple at 44% of accounts receivable at June 30. Both are named in the 10-K as potential competitors that "control the app stores that are the principal means by which our members access our platform." App-store commissions are recorded in Sales & Marketing rather than cost of revenue, so the reported 87% subscription gross margin is not comparable to peers who book platform fees in COGS; management attributed part of the 35% S&M increase to "higher app store commissions tied to subscription revenue growth." The dollar amount is not separately disclosed, so the magnitude of that overstatement is unquantified.

Several features that justify the premium tiers are sourced from third parties Life360 does not control: AWS for compute, Arity 875 LLC for the driving engine (an exclusive sourcing obligation), Signature Motor Club for roadside assistance, AvantGuard for crash-detection dispatch, and Jabil as sole contract manufacturer for hardware in Malaysia and the PRC, with the 10-K stating "we do not currently have alternative or replacement suppliers."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 ~$304.5M → FY2024 $371.5M → FY2025 $489.5M (+31.8%). 1H26 $302.082M versus $219.005M (+37.9%). Excluding advertising entirely, Q2 grew 24.4% ($136.993M vs $110.094M), and the company's disclosed core app subscription line grew 34% to $111.1M. The headline 38% blends a 24–34% organic business with a step-function from Nativo, whose standalone revenue is not disclosed.

**Gross profit.** Consolidated gross margin was 80% in Q2 (78% prior year), but the improvement is entirely the $3.6M tariff refund; excluding it, gross profit grew approximately 37%, roughly in line with revenue. Within that, advertising gross margin fell from 90.3% to 56.8% as cost of advertising revenue rose from $0.515M to $9.496M, reflecting Nativo's managed, publisher-facing media with cost pass-through rather than monetization of Life360's own high-margin first-party inventory. Hardware's underlying economics are worse than reported: revenue $9.806M against cost of $5.561M as reported, or $9.161M excluding the refund, leaves roughly $0.645M of gross profit (~6.6%). Hardware units fell 18% in Q2 and 21% in the half, hardware guidance was cut, and hardware-attached subscription revenue declined from $5.7M to $4.5M, so the funnel rationale is weakening on its own terms.

**Operating leverage is negative.** Opex at 80% of revenue versus 77%, with SBC at $22.821M in Q2 (14.4% of revenue, from 13.2%) and $39.076M in the half (+50%). LTM SBC of $69.5M is more than ten times LTM GAAP operating income. Adjusted EBITDA was $31.113M in Q2 (19.6% margin, +53%) but $48.212M for the half at a **16.0% margin, below 1H25's 16.6%**; the half-year adjusted margin declined year over year despite 38% revenue growth.

**Earnings quality.** Reported net income has been produced by tax items for consecutive periods. FY2025's $150.8M of net income rested on a $118.2M tax benefit from an approximately $118.4M valuation-allowance release against pretax income of $32.7M. The 1H26 benefit landed on a pretax loss. Because the discrete portion is largely excess tax benefits on stock compensation, reported net income is partly reflexive to the share price. The captured earnings-surprise table (Q2 actual $0.27 versus a $0.02 estimate) cannot be reconciled to either GAAP diluted EPS of $0.06 or the $0.36 implied by adjusted EBITDA per diluted share, so I treat the surprise history as basis-inconsistent and give it no weight.

**Cash flow is real.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $41.019M (+62%) less $2.548M capitalized software and no PP&E gives $38.5M of free cash flow, 12.7% of revenue. FY2025 FCF was approximately $86.8M (17.7%). Capital intensity is genuinely minimal. Two qualifications: the half included a $13.170M working-capital release from receivables presented net of the Nativo acquisition (organic receivables actually declined), and Q2 carried the $3.6M refund in COGS plus $2.256M of refund-monetization proceeds routed through financing. I have not annualized a single quarter; the disclosed seasonality (Q3 subscription, Q4 hardware and advertising) implies an H2-weighted year, so full-year FCF in the $85–95M range is a reasonable frame.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $267.061M + short-term investments $197.923M + restricted $2.691M = $467.675M against $320.0M principal of convertible notes, for net cash of approximately **$147.7M**. Total liabilities $438.5M against $613.4M of equity. The convertible notes are the strongest financial decision in the file: 0.00% coupon, 0.68% effective rate, due June 2030, no maintenance covenants, not callable before June 2028, conversion price $80.97, capped calls to $122.22. Principal settles in cash, so a portion of the cash balance is functionally reserved, but at $63.195 the conversion price sits 28% above spot and the 130% conversion trigger sits at approximately $105.26, 67% above spot. Economic dilution from the notes today is effectively nil.

Asset composition is a caveat: goodwill $173.609M, intangibles $77.481M and deferred tax assets $149.490M together are 38% of $1,051.9M of assets. The DTA alone is 14.2% of assets and depends on sustained GAAP profitability that has not yet been established at the operating line.

**Dilution.** Diluted weighted shares were 85,594,461 in Q2 against 81,216,330 outstanding. Outstanding options (3,463,900, all exercisable at a $5.20 weighted average) plus RSUs/PRSUs/MRSUs (5,239,355) total 8,703,255, or 10.7% of shares outstanding and 9.7% post-issuance ownership dilution. A further 17,050,737 shares are authorized for future grant, which is board capacity rather than current dilution but relevant against $194.0M of unrecognized RSU expense. Shares outstanding rose from 79,359,589 at year-end to 81,216,330 at June 30, **+2.3% in six months**, despite $13.225M of repurchases.

Financial health scores 6.0: liquidity, maturity profile and cash conversion are strong; earnings quality, rising SBC intensity, DTA fragility and negative operating leverage offset it.

## Growth Analysis

The subscription engine is working. Paying Circles +27%, ARPPC $142.56 (+5%), ARPPS $128.38 (+11%), and international Paying Circles +32% with international ARPPC +14% on price and mix. Prior price increases in the US (2022, 2023) and UK/ANZ (1H24) stuck. The clearest organic option is international monetization: 48.4M of 102.4M MAU are international (47%) but international subscription revenue was $20.8M of $115.6M (18%).

Against that, four data points argue the growth mix is degrading:

**Guidance arithmetic contradicts the acceleration framing.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $650–685M less 1H26 actual of $302.082M implies H2 revenue of $347.9–382.9M against H2 2025 of $270.5M — growth of +28.6% to +41.6%, a **midpoint of +35.1% versus 1H's actual +37.9%**. Only the top of the range delivers the acceleration management described. Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $130–140M was left unchanged despite a Q2 beat, implying H2 adjusted EBITDA of $81.8–91.8M (23.5–24.0% margin) against 16.0% in 1H. In fairness, H2 2025 ran 21.0% against 1H25's 16.6%, so a first-half-to-second-half step-up is the disclosed seasonal pattern and the year-over-year ask is only about +3pp. The profit guide is more credible than the half-over-half optics suggest.

**Advertising must nearly double sequentially.** Quarterly history: Q1'25 $4.584M, Q2'25 $5.287M, Q3'25 $7.3M, Q4'25 $13.9M, Q1'26 $19.661M, Q2'26 $21.966M. Unchanged FY guidance of $98–115M requires H2 advertising of $56.4–73.4M, or $28.2–36.7M per quarter. Q4 seasonality (Q4'25 was 1.9x Q3'25) supports the low end; the high end requires commercial ramp, and the FY2025 10-K added a dedicated risk factor stating the advertising business is "new," with "limited experience," and that revenue "may not grow as we expect or at all."

**MAU is running below the full-year guide.** Q2 MAU growth was +16% against a 17–20% target explicitly weighted to H2. Q2 net additions of 4.6M restored the trajectory after Q1 Android registration issues resolved in April, but the target still requires acceleration.

**Consensus revisions are mixed, not inflecting.** Current-year EPS of $1.24 is +0.9% over 90 days, but next quarter is **−10.9%** and next year **−5.9%**, on only two contributing analysts. This is not the >+30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection; the aggregate "estimates broadly rising" label is not supported by the out-period detail.

Revenue growth remains high and gross-profit growth roughly tracks it, but the incremental gross margin on the fastest-growing line is 46% and hardware is contracting at near-zero margin. Growth prospects score 6.3.

## Competitive Landscape

Family location sharing is a different product from item finding, and that is where Life360's moat sits. A Circle requires multiple household members to install the app, creating switching friction a single-user utility lacks, and the premium tiers bundle crash detection with live dispatch, roadside assistance and driver scoring that Apple and Google do not offer. Word-of-mouth referral inside households keeps customer acquisition cost low. The 102M-MAU first-party precise-location dataset is genuinely scarce in a post-ATT world.

The vulnerabilities are structural. Apple ships Find My and AirTag; Google operates a device-finding network. Both are pre-installed, free and OS-integrated, and both gate the distribution and collect the billing for roughly 69% of Life360's revenue. Tile's unit decline of 18% is what competing against a bundled OS feature looks like. The strategic response, connecting Tile to Hubble Network's satellite Bluetooth constellation, recognized $0.4M of revenue in the half and is entirely prospective. Hubble's founder and CEO, Alex Haro, is a Life360 co-founder and sitting director, and part of that small revenue is amortization of a non-cash warrant Life360 received from Hubble.

On advertising, Life360 is entering as a subscale player with a differentiated data asset and an undifferentiated technology stack, competing against platforms orders of magnitude larger. The 90% → 57% gross margin shift quantifies which side of that trade is currently scaling. The 10-K describes both end markets as "in a relatively early stage of development" with "low switching costs and a consistent stream of new products, services, and entrants."

Trajectory is improving in subscription (Paying Circles +27%, international +32%, pricing power demonstrated) and deteriorating in hardware and gross-margin mix. Net: 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Channel concentration in two competitors (highest materiality).** Apple 50% and Google 19% of Q2 revenue; Apple 44% of receivables. Either can change fees, terms, data access, store visibility or OS-level functionality unilaterally. A second-order disclosure is easy to miss: subscription fees are collected by the channel partners, and "the number of new and retained members recorded by Life360's internal database has differed from the number recorded by our Channel Partners," creating metric and revenue-recognition dependence on third-party systems. Revenue mix has diversified (subscription 76.8% → 72.7%) but channel dependence inside the subscription business is unchanged; Apple's share fell from 54% to 50% arithmetically because Nativo revenue is billed outside the app stores.

**Regulation colliding with the growth engine.** COPPA's updated rule requires separate parental consent for sharing data with third parties for targeted ads. State laws impose heightened restrictions specifically on precise geolocation, which management calls "a core component of our products and services." Add GDPR, the UK Age-Appropriate Design Code, the EU Digital Services Act and the UK Online Safety Act. The 10-K states plainly that if the ability to collect or process precise geolocation is materially restricted, "our products and services may become less effective or attractive to members." The advertising strategy monetizes family location data including minors, so the fastest-growing revenue line runs directly into the largest regulatory exposure. A separate churn-driver disclosure names ad load itself: members may feel "their experience is diminished" by ad frequency and prominence.

**Deferred tax asset durability.** $149.490M, 14.2% of assets, following the FY2025 valuation-allowance release, against a 1H26 pretax loss. Sustained GAAP losses could require re-establishing an allowance, a non-cash charge that would reverse much of FY2025's reported net income. Federal NOLs of $339.9M and state NOLs of $135.4M begin expiring in 2027.

**Hardware supply and tariffs.** Jabil is sole manufacturer with no qualified alternative, in Malaysia and the PRC. Tariff outcomes drove both FY2025's margin collapse (18% → 1%) and Q2 2026's $3.6M refund benefit, with the 10-K conceding the refund process "remains uncertain." The company is simultaneously exiting brick-and-mortar retail and warns of reduced brand visibility.

**Data-partner concentration.** Approximately 7% of revenue in each of the last three years from key data partners at high margin, so the gross-profit exposure exceeds 7%. The 10-K discloses that executives hold an immaterial passive interest in the identified data partner.

**Aura note markdown.** The $25.0M zero-interest convertible note funded in May 2025 alongside a three-year advertising partnership was written down from $24.7M to $20.0M, a $4.727M loss in 1H26 and roughly a 19% markdown within about 13 months. The filings do not attribute the cause, which could be Aura-specific deterioration or ordinary discount-rate mechanics on a Level 3 zero-coupon instrument. It is a recurring GAAP drag excluded from adjusted EBITDA.

**Offsetting de-risking.** The Tile stalking-misuse class action ended favorably: Ireland-Gordy dismissed with prejudice August 6, 2025; the Ninth Circuit compelled remaining claims to arbitration March 3, 2026; the certiorari deadline passed June 1, 2026 without a petition. No accrual was recorded and the 10-Q states no pending matter is expected to be material. This removed a real overhang at no cash cost, though the underlying reputational and regulatory vector around tracker misuse persists independently.

Risk factors score 3.8: the concentration, regulatory and DTA exposures are specific, quantified, and not obviously priced at 7.5x forward revenue.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to approximately $80, +27%)

Subscription execution continues at 30%+ with international the driver: 47% of MAU generating 18% of subscription revenue is a large arithmetic option, and international Paying Circles +32% with ARPPC +14% shows the price ladder works outside the US. Advertising hits the top of the $98–115M range on Q4 seasonality plus Nativo ramp, blended ad margin recovers as first-party inventory scales faster than acquired managed media, and the H2 adjusted EBITDA step-up to 23.5–24.0% lands, taking FY2026 revenue to the upper half of $650–685M. Hardware stabilizes at low-but-positive margin post-retail exit, ending the gross-profit drain. FCF compounds toward $120M+ in FY2027 on 12–15% of revenue with near-zero capex, the $211.8M remaining under the buyback (4.1% of market capitalization) begins to reduce share count rather than offset dilution, and the $320M of 0.00% money to 2030 funds it all. At 8.0x FY2027E revenue of $860M the equity is worth roughly $80, near the $76 analyst high.

### Bear Case (to approximately $40, −37%)

The gross-margin mix continues to deteriorate: advertising scales at 46% incremental gross margin, hardware stays near breakeven while shrinking, and SBC keeps growing faster than revenue, holding GAAP operating margin at 0–3% while opex stays at 80% of revenue. Advertising lands at the low end of guidance, MAU misses the 17–20% target, and the FY2026 revenue outcome falls to the bottom of the range, confirming the deceleration the midpoint already implies. Sustained GAAP losses force re-establishment of a valuation allowance against the $149.5M DTA, a headline reversal of FY2025's reported net income. Either Apple or Google adjusts store economics, data access or OS-level functionality, or a state precise-geolocation or COPPA action restricts the ad targeting that underpins the growth narrative. The multiple compresses to 3.75x forward revenue and the equity trades to roughly $40, near the $42 analyst low.

### Where the Evidence Points

The organic subscription business justifies a growth multiple; the consolidated income statement does not yet justify this one. LTM GAAP operating income of $6.5M on $572.6M of revenue, an adjusted EBITDA measure that excludes $80M+ of annual equity compensation, and a share count rising 2.3% in six months despite buybacks mean the cash accruing to shareholders is materially below the headline. At $63.195 the market is paying 7.5x forward revenue for that. Probability-weighted return is approximately −9%.

Do not initiate new capital here. At the $52.01 extended-hours level, base case $61 represents +17% upside and the appropriate action would be HOLD, not further reduction; do not sell into a gap that has already discounted much of the bear path.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026). Interim checkpoints: any 8-K on channel-partner terms, and buyback disclosure in the Q3 10-Q showing whether repurchases are reducing share count or merely offsetting SBC-driven issuance.

## Conclusion

Life360 has a defensible household network, demonstrated pricing power, minimal capital intensity, $147.7M of net cash and the best-priced financing in its peer group at a 0.00% coupon to 2030. The 27% growth in Paying Circles and 32% growth in international Paying Circles are the numbers a long-term owner should care about, and the resolution of the Tile stalking litigation removed a real overhang at no cash cost.

The reason not to own it at $63.195 is arithmetic rather than narrative. Trailing-twelve-month GAAP operating income is $6.5M on $572.6M of revenue; the fastest-growing revenue line carries 46% incremental gross margin; hardware's reported margin improvement is a $3.6M tariff-refund artifact concealing an underlying 6.6%; equity compensation is growing 50% against revenue's 38% with $194M unrecognized; and the FY2026 guidance midpoint implies revenue deceleration rather than the acceleration management described.
