**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Riot Platforms is a large-scale U.S. Bitcoin miner repositioning itself as an AI/HPC data-center landlord, anchored by a 10-year lease with AMD signed in January 2026 and doubled to 50 MW in April. The stock has run 222% off its 52-week low of $7.99 to a recent $27.32, a market capitalization of roughly $10.3 billion, and now sits within ~3% of its 52-week high of $28.94. That move reflects two things working in tandem: a recovery in the Bitcoin complex and investor enthusiasm for the "miner-to-AI-landlord" repricing trade also playing out at Hut 8, Cipher, and Core Scientific.

The fundamentals underneath the rally are weak and getting weaker on the metrics that matter for an equity with no earnings. Q1 2026 produced a net loss of $(500.5)M (more than double the year-ago $(296.4)M), adjusted EBITDA of $(311.1)M, and operating cash burn of $(182.7)M that was funded by selling 3,778 BTC for $289.5M. Mining is unprofitable on a fully-loaded basis — cost to mine one BTC including depreciation was $96,283, or 126.7% of production value. The headline "first data-center revenue" of $33.2M was 97% non-recurring tenant fit-out reimbursement; recurring base rent was $0.9M. Management explicitly warns it may need to sell more Bitcoin than anticipated to fund operations, which is forced selling into weakness.

The valuation prices an option on a buildout that has one anchor tenant and just lost its Chief Data Center Officer in the same month AMD expanded. Insiders are net sellers into the rally with zero open-market purchases. Forward EPS estimates are negative and the current-year consensus has been revised down sharply (-2,641.7% over 90 days, from roughly breakeven to $(1.09)). At the top of its 52-week range, with structural dilution, a levered balance sheet tied to a volatile asset, and an unquantified $496M+ litigation tail, the risk/reward favors avoiding the name. We rate RIOT **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

Riot operates a vertically integrated digital-infrastructure business built on owned, large-scale, low-cost electrical power capacity in Texas and Kentucky. As of Q1 2026 it reports three segments:

- **Bitcoin Mining** — 42.5 EH/s deployed hash rate (up 10.4% from 38.5 at year-end 2025), 36.4 EH/s average operating, mined 1,473 BTC in Q1. Among the largest U.S. miners.
- **Data Center** (new reportable segment in Q1 2026) — anchored by the AMD lease at Rockdale.
- **Engineering** — custom electrical and power-distribution equipment (ESS Metron, E4A Solutions), serving Riot's own buildout plus third parties.

The genuine competitive asset is the power portfolio: ~1,262 MW developed across Rockdale (~700 MW), Corsicana (400 MW built, ~1 GW planned), and Kentucky (162 MW), with 345 MW under a fixed-price Rockdale PPA at roughly 3.0¢/kWh all-in. That same power base that historically fed mining now underwrites the data-center pitch. Corsicana buildout plans have reportedly been raised toward ~756 MW (single secondary source, treat as directional).

Management is selling a transition from cyclical, increasingly marginal mining into long-duration, contracted AI/HPC leasing. The transition is at its earliest stage while the legacy business is bleeding cash.

The loss is dominated by a $(326.7)M negative change in the fair value of Bitcoin (BTC fell from 2025 all-time highs to roughly $68K average in Q1), $97.7M of D&A, a $(51.9)M derivative (PPA) revaluation, and $39.2M of stock-based compensation. The P&L is fundamentally a leveraged proxy on the Bitcoin price; the 10-Q sensitivity table shows a 10% BTC move swings net income by roughly $107M.

Two operating realities matter more than the GAAP headline:

1. **Mining economics are underwater on a fully-loaded basis.** Cost to mine one BTC was $44,629 excluding miner depreciation (58.8% of production value) but $96,283 including depreciation — 126.7% of the $75,964 production value. All-in power cost improved to 3.0¢/kWh from 3.8¢, but Bitcoin Mining segment gross profit still collapsed from $68.0M to $25.1M YoY as production fell 3.7% on rising network difficulty. The next halving (~mid-2028) is a further structural headwind.

2. **"Data Center revenue" is mostly pass-through.** Of the $33.2M, only $0.9M was operating-lease base rent; $32.2M was tenant fit-out reimbursement (cost-plus, lumpy, non-recurring). Data Center segment gross profit was $2.4M. The recurring annuity is real but tiny relative to a $10.3B market cap. (The Seeking Alpha estimate is ~$55.6M annualized run-rate by exit-2027 — single secondary source, treat as directional.)

**Liquidity is managed but tight.** Net working capital was only $33.3M; cash was $205.7M plus $76.9M restricted. Riot funds its burn by selling Bitcoin (holdings fell from 18,005 to 15,679 BTC in the quarter) and has a $500M ATM equity program (untapped as of 3/31/26) plus a $200M Bitcoin-collateralized Coinbase facility (5,802 BTC pledged). Total debt is ~$854M, anchored by $594M of 0.75% convertible notes due 2030. Of the ~$1.07B in Bitcoin on the balance sheet, $395.8M is restricted/pledged.

The earnings quality is poor and the funding model is pro-cyclical: when Bitcoin falls, GAAP losses widen and the company sells more of the asset that is falling to cover an operating burn — precisely what management flagged in the MD&A.

## Growth Analysis

The bull thesis rests entirely on the data-center pivot scaling into a contracted, hyperscaler-grade annuity. The evidence so far is thin:

- AMD is effectively the only data-center customer. The April 2026 amendment took contracted capacity to 50 MW, with 50 MW reserved and a 100 MW conditional first-priority right (a theoretical path to 200 MW). The full ramp and any additional tenants are unproven.
- Engineering grew nicely (+59.7% YoY to $22.2M) but is sub-scale and partly captive to Riot's own buildout.
- Mining hash rate is still growing (MicroBT deliveries completing in Q2 2026, ~49.2 EH/s of capacity for ~$779.5M), but the incremental capacity is being deployed into GAAP-unprofitable economics.

On the estimate side, the signal is negative. Current-year EPS consensus was revised from roughly breakeven to $(1.09) over 90 days (-2,641.7%), and next-year was cut to $(0.62) (-235.9% over 90 days). The near-term quarterly revisions turned modestly positive over 30 days (+35.5% and +48.0% on the current and next quarter, off small negative bases), but the trajectory of the full-year numbers — the ones that capture the structural earnings power — is decisively lower. The estimate revisions here do not constitute a buy signal; they confirm deterioration.

Growth optionality exists, but it is an option, not an earnings stream, and the market is paying $10.3B for it.

## Valuation Assessment

RIOT has no positive earnings to anchor a multiple. Trailing and forward P/E are negative (EPS -$1.95), so conventional earnings-based valuation does not apply. The relevant question is whether the $10.3B market cap is supported by asset value plus a reasonably priced growth option.

On a sum-of-parts basis the math is unflattering:

- **Bitcoin holdings:** ~$1.07B (much of it restricted), against ~$854M of debt — net of debt, roughly breakeven to modestly positive.
- **Recurring data-center earnings:** ~$0.9M of quarterly base rent today. Assigning a fully-ramped multiple requires AMD to exercise options that are not yet committed; the durable annuity remains nascent.
- **Mining business:** cash-generative at the gross level but GAAP-unprofitable fully loaded and structurally pressured by difficulty growth and the 2028 halving. Hard to assign a premium multiple to a business management is implicitly de-emphasizing.

The gap between defensible asset/annuity value and a $10.3B equity value is the embedded AI/HPC option — a wide premium to pay for a single-tenant, early-stage buildout that just lost its lead executive. The stock trades within 3% of its 52-week high and above the analyst mean target of $26.75 and median of $25.50; the consensus range runs $12.90 to $45.00. We do not treat the high target as a floor here; this is a richly valued option on execution where full-year estimates are falling.

The 222% appreciation from the low is not itself a reason to be bearish; price action is not fundamentals. The bearish valuation case rests on current price versus current fundamentals: negative free cash flow, GAAP-unprofitable core operations, structural dilution, and a buildout option priced as though execution is largely de-risked when it is not. Valuation score: low.

## Competitive Landscape

Riot's defensible edge is its owned, fixed-price power portfolio and in-house Engineering capability, which lower counterparty and execution risk in buildouts relative to miners renting power. The industry trend is real and broad-based: post-halving, pure-play mining is increasingly marginal, and the largest miners are converting power capacity into AI/HPC data centers, with Hut 8, Cipher, and Core Scientific all pursuing similar hyperscaler deals. Investors are repricing the cohort as data-center landlords.

The competitive risk is that Riot is a late, sub-scale entrant in HPC against dedicated operators with deeper capital, established hyperscaler relationships, and operating track records. Converting cash-generative (if cyclical) mining capacity into long-duration capex bets is a strategic gamble whose payoff depends on winning tenants Riot has not yet signed. The AMD relationship is a landlord/tenant lease; AMD is a customer, not a co-developer, so the contracted compute and hardware belong to AMD, not Riot. Trajectory is improving at the segment level (Data Center now exists, Engineering growing), but the position is unproven and the moat in HPC leasing is far weaker than in the power-acquisition layer where Riot is genuinely advantaged.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Bitcoin price leverage / GAAP volatility.** Management warns the recent decline may force larger Bitcoin sales than anticipated, compounding dilution and balance-sheet erosion.
2. **Single-tenant data-center concentration.** AMD plus one mining pool each exceed 10% of revenue. The entire growth thesis depends on AMD exercising uncommitted options and on landing additional tenants at Corsicana, against power-timing, permitting, water, and specialized-talent constraints.
3. **Loss of the pivot leader.** Chief Data Center Officer Jonathan Gibbs departed via "mutual separation" in April 2026, forfeiting ~1.15M shares and all options — the same month AMD doubled its lease. The executive most identified with the strategy left at the moment of supposed acceleration.
4. **GMO litigation tail.** The legacy Whinstone hosting dispute with GMO seeks ~$496M+; summary-judgment motions are pending and unresolved. Two other disputes resolved favorably (GRC patent verdict won 4/17/26, with GRC able to appeal; SBI settled $20M in Q1), and a new Malikie patent suit (filed 12/2025, also naming Cipher and Foundry) is unquantified. Net de-risking, but not a clean slate.
5. **Capital intensity and dilution.** Share count rose from 344.9M (YE2024) to ~379M (4/29/26); the 2030 notes add ~40M shares of overhang. Full Corsicana buildout requires heavy capex funded by ATM equity, BTC sales, or BTC-backed debt — all dilutive or pro-cyclical to BTC.
6. **Mining structural headwinds:** rising network difficulty, the ~mid-2028 halving, and 89% of 2025 revenue from a single FPPS mining pool.

These risks are concrete, mostly not yet resolved, and several (forced BTC selling, the CDCO departure, the GMO tail) are not fully priced into a stock sitting near its 52-week high. Risk score: low.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 104.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 83.5% (rich by 21.3 points, 87th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-week IV 104.8% > 6-month 84.0%); the market expects a near-term move and demands a premium for it. The 1-week implied move is ±6.1%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.0%) and put/call volume (0.64) and OI (0.32) ratios are not heavily bearish, so the options market is pricing volatility rather than a clear directional crash. Combined with 18.5% short interest and 3.1 days to cover, the setup is a volatile, contested name where elevated near-term vol argues against fresh long exposure and the modest days-to-cover caps how violent a squeeze can be.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (target ~$45):** Bitcoin holds or appreciates, lifting mining economics back above the fully-loaded cost line and reducing forced selling. AMD exercises its 50 MW reserved option and the 100 MW conditional right, and Riot signs additional hyperscaler tenants at Corsicana, converting the power portfolio into a contracted multi-hundred-MW data-center annuity. The market continues to reprice Riot as an AI infrastructure landlord rather than a miner, expanding the multiple on a rising recurring revenue base. Engineering scales with third-party demand. The embedded option pays off and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target.

**Bear Case (target ~$13–16, roughly -40% to -50%):** Bitcoin softens, widening GAAP losses and forcing additional BTC sales and ATM issuance into weakness. AMD does not exercise its uncommitted options at the pace modeled, and no second anchor tenant materializes, leaving Corsicana as a capex sink with thin contracted cash flow. The CDCO departure proves symptomatic of execution problems. The GMO litigation moves against Riot. Dilution accelerates as the buildout is funded with equity. The "AI landlord" multiple compresses back toward miner economics. The stock falls toward the low analyst target of $12.90, near where it traded in late March 2026.

The asymmetry at the current price favors the bear case. Upside requires several uncommitted, sequential things to go right; downside requires only Bitcoin weakness or a buildout stumble, either of which is plausible.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No single dated, high-conviction catalyst is in hand. The most likely near-term triggers are (a) the next quarterly print (Q2 2026, late July/early August) showing continued operating burn and forced BTC sales, (b) a renewed leg down in Bitcoin, or (c) an adverse development in the GMO summary-judgment motions. None is precisely dated or guaranteed to resolve lower.

**Timing window:** The Q2 print is roughly two months out; a Bitcoin move and the GMO ruling are open-ended. A short would carry through a high-volatility window with no fixed resolution date.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 18.5% of float with only 3.1 days to cover. That is elevated but not extreme; the crowd is already meaningfully short, and the low days-to-cover means a positive Bitcoin move or AMD headline could squeeze the stock quickly. The name is a high-beta proxy on a volatile underlying, which makes short carry expensive and dangerous.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options are not confirming a clean directional short — skew is balanced (-1.0%), put/call volume and OI are not bearish, and near-term IV is rich (104.8%) in backwardation. The market is pricing volatility, not a one-sided collapse. Shorting through 100%+ implied vol with a balanced skew is poor risk/reward; near-term puts are expensive and the underlying can gap either way on a Bitcoin move.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target ~$13–16 (-40% to -50%) against a credible squeeze/Bitcoin-rally upside back toward $35–45 (+30% to +65%). That is roughly 1:1, not the 2:1 a short requires. The reflexive feedback loop with Bitcoin and the AI-landlord narrative can drive the stock well past fundamentals in either direction.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental view is sound, but there is no specific dated catalyst, the squeeze risk is real with low days-to-cover, options offer no directional confirmation, and the Bitcoin proxy makes the position effectively a leveraged bet against a crypto rally rather than a clean fundamental short. Avoid owning it; do not press it short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. We expect the headwinds — GAAP-unprofitable mining, operating cash burn funded by Bitcoin sales, single-tenant data-center concentration, and structural dilution — to persist over at least the next 12 months and through the next halving cycle.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- **Recurring data-center cash flow becomes material.** Several consecutive quarters of growing base operating-lease revenue (not fit-out reimbursement), with at least one additional anchor tenant beyond AMD signed at Corsicana, would convert the option into an earnings stream.
- **Mining returns to fully-loaded profitability.** Cost to mine one BTC including depreciation falling sustainably below production value (i.e., below 100%), reducing the need to sell Bitcoin to fund operations.
- **Self-funding without dilution.** Operating cash flow turning positive and share count stabilizing, removing the forced-selling / ATM-issuance overhang.
- **Valuation reset.** A pullback toward defensible asset-plus-annuity value (roughly the low-teens area, near the $12.90 low target) would materially improve the risk/reward even before the operating thesis is proven.

**Timeline:** Reassess at the Q2 2026 earnings print and again after the 2026 annual meeting (governance declassification, executive bench). The structural headwinds are unlikely to clear within 6–12 months.

## Conclusion

Riot Platforms is a levered call on the Bitcoin price wrapped around an early-stage, single-tenant AI/HPC data-center option, valued at $10.3B after a 222% run from its low and sitting within 3% of its 52-week high. The Q1 2026 results showed the strain: a $(500.5)M net loss, $(182.7)M of operating cash burn funded by selling 3,778 BTC, mining that is unprofitable on a fully-loaded basis, and a "first data-center revenue" headline that was 97% non-recurring pass-through. Full-year EPS estimates have been revised sharply lower, the executive most identified with the pivot departed in April, insiders are net sellers with zero open-market buys, and an unquantified $496M+ litigation claim survives to summary judgment.

The power portfolio is a genuine asset and the AI-landlord trend is real, but the market is paying a wide premium for an option on a buildout that has one committed tenant and unproven execution. At the top of its 52-week range, with negative free cash flow and structural dilution, the risk/reward is unfavorable. We rate RIOT **AVOID**. It is not a recommended short — the Bitcoin proxy, low days-to-cover squeeze risk, and balanced options positioning argue against pressing it — but it is not a stock to own at this price.
