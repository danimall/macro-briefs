**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vistra is the largest integrated retail electricity and merchant power generation company in the U.S., combining a ~40+ GW generation fleet (gas, nuclear, coal, solar, batteries) with a large retail book across 16 states. Its nuclear assets (Comanche Peak in ERCOT and the PJM fleet of Beaver Valley, Perry, Davis-Besse) are the scarce, prized resource at the center of the data-center power thesis: 24/7 carbon-free baseload that hyperscalers cannot replicate quickly. The stock has fallen ~26% from its September 2025 high of $219.82 to $163.75, while the underlying business has continued to improve.

The most important analytical point in this name is that the headline GAAP figures are misleading. FY2025 net income fell to ~$944M from ~$2.66B, and standardized credit screens flag VST as distressed (Altman Z ~0.76). Both are artifacts. The Z-Score model is calibrated for manufacturers and does not apply to a capital-intensive IPP carrying ~7x debt/equity as a structural norm. The net income decline is overwhelmingly non-cash mark-to-market movement on forward hedges: rising power prices create paper losses on forward generation sales that reverse as positions settle. The cash story moved the other way: Q1'26 Adjusted EBITDA rose 21% to $1.475B, operating cash flow doubled to $1.20B, and revenue grew 43% to $5.64B. Any scoring model anchored to GAAP net income will badly misread this company.

We are upgrading VST to BUY. Three developments tip the balance. First, the credit profile inflected positively: Vistra Operations was upgraded to investment grade (BBB-) by both S&P (Dec 2025) and Fitch (Mar 2026), triggering automatic release of all collateral liens in April 2026, followed by a $4.0B unsecured refinancing and swap termination. This structurally lowers cost of capital. Second, Vistra has converted the AI-power thesis into contracted cash flow: 20-year PPAs with Meta (2,609 MW) and AWS (1,200 MW) with delivery ramps beginning late 2026. Third, at ~16.8x forward consensus EPS of $9.72, a discount to the 19.0x regulated-utility peer median despite higher growth and contracted offtake, with current-year estimates revised up 10.9% over 90 days, the valuation offers asymmetric upside. The principal risks (commodity VaR doubling, Moss Landing, AI-capex sensitivity) are real but largely funded by dedicated facilities and partly insulated by long-dated contracts.

## Company Overview

Vistra operates an asset-heavy, vertically integrated model that uses its retail load as a natural sales channel for its generation, producing earnings stability through hedging. The company reports in five segments: Retail (TXU Energy, Ambit, Dynegy, Energy Harbor, others); Texas (ERCOT generation); East (PJM, MISO, ISO-NE, NYISO, including the nuclear fleet acquired via Energy Harbor); West (CAISO, including the Moss Landing batteries); and Asset Closure (retiring coal and remediation).

The generation portfolio spans natural gas combined-cycle and peaking units, nuclear, coal/lignite, and a growing solar and storage book. The nuclear fleet is the strategic core: it provides firm, carbon-free, 24/7 power that is the most-prized resource for hyperscalers, and it is essentially impossible to replicate on the demand timeline that data centers require. Vistra's closest comparable is Constellation Energy (CEG), the other dominant nuclear-heavy IPP. The two are differentiated by capital-return philosophy: VST returns capital primarily via buybacks (roughly 30% share-count reduction since late 2021), while CEG leans on a fast-growing dividend.

## Financial Analysis

**Cash earnings are the relevant metric, and they are growing.** Q1'26 versus Q1'25:

- Operating revenue: $5.64B vs $3.93B (+43%, partly the Lotus acquisition plus higher capacity and energy margins)
- Operating cash flow: $1.20B vs $0.60B (doubled)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $1.475B vs $1.216B (+21%), management's primary metric and the cleanest read
- GAAP net income: $1.029B vs a $(268)M loss, a swing driven by MTM ($723M unrealized commodity gain in Q1'26 vs a $567M loss in Q1'25)
- Diluted EPS: $2.87 vs $(0.93)

The quarterly GAAP swings are extreme and dominated by hedge accounting, which is why the trailing earnings-surprise record is so noisy: +112.2% (Q1'26), −77.2% (Q4'25), −17.6% (Q3'25), −42.5% (Q2'25). Investors should not read these as visibility problems in the underlying business; they reflect the mechanical pass-through of forward-curve moves into reported earnings. Annual consensus is the more reliable anchor: current-year EPS of $9.72 and next-year $11.11.

**Capital allocation has been disciplined and shareholder-aligned.** Vistra has repurchased ~$6.3B since November 2021 under a $7.75B authorization (~$1.5B remaining as of May 2026), cutting share count roughly 30%. The dividend is deliberately modest ($0.229/quarter, sub-1% yield), reflecting a buyback-weighted return philosophy.

**The credit inflection is the structural positive.** Long-term debt was ~$19.2B at 3/31/26. The twin investment-grade upgrades to BBB- triggered automatic release of all collateral liens on secured notes and credit facilities in April 2026, lowering cost of capital and freeing collateral. The company then issued $4.0B of unsecured notes (4.55%–5.55%) to refinance Term Loan B-3 and 2027 maturities, and terminated its interest-rate swaps. Liquidity was ~$4.2B at quarter-end, supplemented by a dedicated $1.75B Commodity-Linked Facility built specifically to fund collateral swings. One caveat: the collateral release reverts if ratings fall below IG within a 60-day grace period.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on contracted, long-dated demand rather than speculative thematics, which materially raises its durability. Two signed 20-year PPAs anchor the nuclear monetization: Meta (Jan 2026, 2,609 MW from PJM nuclear plants, ramping from late 2026 to full volume by YE2027, with uprate volumes 2031–2034) and AWS (Sep 2025, 1,200 MW from Comanche Peak, delivery from Q4 2027 ramping to full by 2032). These are power-supply agreements: Vistra owns and operates the plants and sells power to the hyperscaler. They are not asset sales or JVs, so the plants and their economics remain Vistra's.

On the dispatchable side, Vistra is expanding gas capacity to capture near-term AI load that nuclear cannot serve quickly: Lotus (~2,600 MW, closed Oct 2025), the pending Cogentrix acquisition (~5,500 MW gas; $2.3B cash + $1.5B assumed debt + 5M shares; close 2H'26), and up to 2,000 MW of new ERCOT gas (peakers plus Coleto Creek repowering) with Texas Energy Fund loans in final due diligence. Perry's nuclear license was renewed through 2046 (July 2025), extending the runway on the highest-value asset class.

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum: current-year consensus moved up 10.9% over 90 days to $9.72, next-year up 4.0% to $11.11. This is meaningful positive revision velocity, though short of the +30% threshold that defines the strongest inflection signals. The combination of contracted long-dated offtake, double-digit EBITDA growth, and rising consensus across the regulated-utility complex gives the demand thesis broad-based confirmation rather than a single-company story.

| **Peer median** | **19.0** | **3.3%** | — |

VST trades at a discount to the peer median despite a materially higher growth profile (EBITDA +21% vs low-single-digit regulated growth) and contracted 20-year hyperscaler offtake. The relevant comparison is more nuanced than a simple multiple line-up: regulated utilities earn allowed returns on rate base, while VST is a merchant IPP whose earnings carry commodity exposure. That justifies some discount. But the discount is wider than the risk differential supports given that a large share of the growth is now contracted under investment-grade-rated 20-year agreements, and given the credit inflection that removed the secured-debt overhang.

The specific reason the market is mispricing this is identifiable: standardized screens read the GAAP net-income decline and the distressed Z-Score as deterioration, when cash earnings actually grew. As that confusion resolves and the PPA volumes begin ramping from late 2026, the multiple should re-rate toward and above the peer median. The sell-side mean target of $223 (median $225; range $99–$320) implies ~36% upside, with 19 buy-side ratings (4 strong buy + 15 buy) against 0 holds and 1 sell.

I temper this view in two ways. Cheapness alone is not a thesis, and the extreme quarter-to-quarter GAAP volatility makes any single forward P/E print less reliable than for a regulated peer. But on annual normalized earnings the valuation case holds, and the catalyst (PPA ramps + credit de-risking) is specific and dated.

## Competitive Landscape

Vistra and Constellation are the two dominant pure-play IPPs positioned as picks-and-shovels for AI/data-center power. The industry tailwind from data centers, electrification, and EVs driving the fastest load growth in decades is large and confirmed across multiple infrastructure data points. Within that, firm carbon-free nuclear is the scarcest resource, and Vistra's fleet plus its retail-hedging integration give it a durable cost and capital-markets advantage at scale.

Relative to CEG, VST is more diversified (gas + retail + nuclear + storage), monetizes via buybacks rather than dividend growth, and is moving faster into dispatchable gas to serve near-term demand. CEG carries the larger pure-nuclear fleet and a margin lead. Both are competitively advantaged; VST's trajectory is improving as it converts nuclear capacity into contracted offtake and expands gas to capture the dispatchable layer. The competitive risk is valuation rather than displacement: a countervailing industry view holds that with the 10-year near 4.45%, utility valuations broadly are stretched and much AI optimism is priced in. That argues for paying attention to entry level, not against the franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The genuine idiosyncratic risks, several not fully reflected at current valuation:

1. **Commodity-price and collateral risk.** Average VaR more than doubled, from $224M (FY2025) to $469M (Q1'26), reflecting higher power-price volatility. Rising prices force margin and collateral postings ($1.5B cash/eligible assets + $3.2B letters of credit at 3/31/26). This is the defining structural risk and the volatility cost of the upside the thesis monetizes; it is funded by the dedicated Commodity-Linked Facility and quarter-end liquidity.
2. **Moss Landing battery fire (Jan 2025).** ~$400M write-off (300MW unit) + $155M impairment (100MW unit) + ~$110M EPA-supervised remediation; $500M insurance limit fully collected by Feb 2026. The 350MW unit's mid-2026 restart is uncertain, and multiple lawsuits remain outstanding. This overhangs the storage growth leg.
3. **Counterparty concentration.** One counterparty represents 40% ($298M) of net wholesale credit exposure.
4. **M&A integration.** Rapid roll-up (Energy Harbor, Lotus, pending Cogentrix) adds leverage and integration risk.
5. **Regulatory/environmental.** Illinois coal-ash remediation costs are "not estimable" and could rise materially; ~4,578 MW of coal slated for 2027–28 retirement.
6. **Nuclear PTC interpretation.** $220M of PTC revenue recognized in 2025; an adverse Treasury interpretation of "gross receipts" could force a restatement.
7. **AI-capex sensitivity.** The bull case is tied to hyperscaler capex durability, which is being stress-tested. The contracted 20-year PPAs with Meta and AWS substantially insulate the near-term cash flows from a demand pullback, but a sustained AI-capex retrenchment would compress the merchant-power premium embedded in the multiple.

The put/call volume ratio is 0.13 and put/call OI 0.54, both call-skewed. The term structure is in contango (48.8% near vs 51.0% long-dated), a normal/complacent shape. None of this contradicts the fundamental BUY thesis; the call-heavy positioning corroborates it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($250, ~+53%):** PPA volumes ramp on schedule from late 2026, gas expansion (Cogentrix, Lotus, ERCOT) closes and integrates cleanly, the credit upgrade compounds into lower financing costs, and the market re-rates VST toward a premium to the regulated-utility median as it recognizes the contracted, IG-backed growth. ~22.5x next-year EPS of $11.11. The reported KKR-backed $10B AI venture (single-source, unconfirmed) would be additive optionality.

**Bear case ($120, ~−27%):** A sustained AI-capex retrenchment compresses the merchant-power premium; commodity volatility forces larger-than-expected collateral demands; Moss Landing litigation and Illinois coal-ash costs surprise to the downside; the Cogentrix integration disappoints; and a higher-for-longer rate path de-rates the whole utility complex. ~12x current-year EPS.

**Base case ($190, ~+16%):** PPA ramps proceed, EBITDA growth continues in the high-teens-to-20% range, the credit profile holds at IG, and the multiple re-rates to roughly the peer median on a higher-growth name (~19.5x $9.72). The asymmetry favors the upside: ~36% to the sell-side mean against ~27% downside in the bear case, with the contracted offtake providing a floor under the most-cited risk.

The audit-flagged tension is fair and I am resolving it toward the bull. My prior neutral stance leaned heavily on leverage rising into Cogentrix against a higher-for-longer backdrop. That pillar is weaker than it looked: the credit profile is inflecting positively (twin IG upgrades, automatic collateral release, swap termination, $4.0B unsecured refinancing), and the remaining commodity-VaR risk is the funded cost of the upside, not an unhedged solvency threat. With that anchor removed, the inflection-BUY profile (forward P/E ~17x, current-year estimates +10.9%/90d, EBITDA +21%, OCF doubling, and dated contracted catalysts) is a defensible BUY rather than a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $190** (~19.5x current-year EPS, re-rating to the peer median on a higher-growth, contracted-offtake name). **Upside to base: ~+16%.**
- **Bull case target: $250** (~22.5x next-year EPS; premium re-rating as PPA volumes ramp). **Upside to bull: ~+53%.**
- **Bear case target: $120** (~12x current-year EPS; AI-capex retrenchment + commodity/litigation stress + multiple compression). **Downside to bear: ~−27%.**
- **Timeframe: 12–18 months,** tied to Meta PPA delivery ramp (from late 2026), AWS delivery (from Q4 2027), Cogentrix close (2H'26), and Moss Landing 350MW restart (mid-2026).
- **Preferred add zone:** scale into the low-$150s to low-$160s; the current level is near the lower end of a reasonable accumulation band given residual volatility.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:**
  1. Adjusted EBITDA growth turns negative YoY for two consecutive quarters (signals the demand thesis or hedging economics are breaking).
  2. Ratings fall below investment grade, reinstating collateral liens and the higher cost of capital.
  3. A Meta or AWS PPA is materially renegotiated, delayed beyond schedule, or terminated, undermining the contracted-cash-flow floor.
  4. Cogentrix closes at terms materially worse than disclosed, or quantified Moss Landing/coal-ash liabilities exceed reserves by a meaningful margin.

## Conclusion

Vistra's reported GAAP results understate a cash-earnings franchise that is improving on every metric that matters: EBITDA up 21%, operating cash flow doubled, an investment-grade credit upgrade that released collateral and lowered the cost of capital, and 20-year contracted hyperscaler offtake that converts the AI-power thesis into bankable revenue. At a discount to a slower-growing peer set, with positive estimate revisions, ~36% upside to the sell-side mean, and the stock down ~26% from its high on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside. The genuine risks (doubled commodity VaR, Moss Landing, AI-capex sensitivity) are real and warrant disciplined sizing, but they are largely funded by dedicated facilities and partly insulated by contracted long-dated PPAs. We rate VST a **BUY**, with a preferred accumulation zone in the low-$150s to low-$160s.
