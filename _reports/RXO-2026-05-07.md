## Executive Summary

RXO is an asset-light freight brokerage platform that doubled its scale through the $1.04B Coyote Logistics acquisition from UPS in September 2024. The company is currently unprofitable (net loss of $100M in 2025, $36M loss in Q1 2026), operating with elevated leverage (D/E 2.13), and facing a $355M debt maturity in November 2027. The investment thesis is entirely cyclical: management and bulls argue capacity is contracting rapidly and a supply-driven freight recovery will restore margins and profitability.

The stock at $19.62 (up 87% from its November 2025 lows near $10.50) has already priced in significant recovery optimism, while the fundamental evidence remains mixed. Consensus estimates have deteriorated dramatically — current year EPS has been revised from +$0.12 to -$0.03 over 90 days (-121% revision). Brokerage gross margins remain compressed at 12%, and the structural mechanic where buy rates rise faster than contracted sell rates means early-cycle recovery actually hurts margins before it helps. With 23.6% short interest reflecting significant informed skepticism and the stock trading within 16% of its 52-week high, the asymmetry favors the bears.

## Company Overview

RXO operates three segments: (1) Truck Brokerage (core — digital marketplace connecting shippers with carriers), (2) Managed Transportation (outsourced freight management), and (3) Last Mile (heavy goods delivery, largest U.S. provider). The company was spun off from XPO in November 2022 and subsequently acquired Coyote Logistics to create one of the largest freight brokers in North America, ranking #16 on Transport Topics' Top 100.

The business model is asset-light, which in a normalized environment should convert well to free cash flow. The acquisition of Coyote brought proprietary market intelligence tools (the "Curve" index), expanded carrier relationships, and European presence. Management positions RXO as a technology-differentiated operator in a highly fragmented market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory shows scale improvement masking profitability collapse:**
- 2022: $4.80B revenue, $92M net income
- 2023: $3.93B revenue, $4M net income (freight recession begins)
- 2024: $4.55B revenue, -$290M net loss (Coyote from Sept, plus $216M one-time charge)
- 2025: $5.74B revenue, -$100M net loss
- Q1 2026: $1.425B revenue, -$36M net loss

The cost of transportation as a percentage of revenue has marched steadily higher: 75.6% (2023) → 78.4% (2024) → 80.3% (2025). This compression is the fundamental problem — RXO pays carriers more, faster than it can raise prices to customers on fixed-duration contracts.

**Balance sheet stress is moderate but time-constrained:**
- Cash: $17M (year-end 2025)
- Total debt: ~$408M ($355M unsecured notes at 7.50% due Nov 2027, plus revolver)
- Goodwill: $1.11B (34% of total assets, with Managed Solutions only 9% above fair value)
- New $450M ABL Facility (Feb 2026) provides liquidity runway
- Altman Z-Score: 2.72 (grey zone)

**Free cash flow is negative:** OCF of $51M minus $59M CapEx = -$8M FCF in 2025. For an "asset-light" model, this is concerning. The theoretical FCF conversion advantage has not materialized during the downturn.

**Q2 2026 guidance of $27-37M Adjusted EBITDA** represents a sequential improvement from implied ~$8.5M in Q1, which would be a meaningful step up if delivered. But even at the $37M high end (annualized $148M), this barely covers the $31M annual interest expense and leaves minimal room for debt reduction ahead of the 2027 maturity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Freight cycle recovery:** Net reduction of 2,648 carrier operating authorities in 2025 (fastest since deregulation). Regulatory enforcement (English proficiency rules, non-domiciled CDL enforcement) driving capacity exits. RXO's spot rate index shows 18.7% YoY gains. Management claims contract rates trending to "high single-digit gains."

2. **Coyote synergies:** Integration expected to deliver cost savings and cross-selling opportunities. Late-stage brokerage pipeline grew 50%+ in Q4 2025. $200M+ in managed transportation awards.

3. **Secular share gains:** Freight brokerage continues to penetrate the for-hire trucking market at the expense of direct carrier relationships.

However, the estimate revision data tells a different story than management's optimism. Current year EPS consensus has collapsed from +$0.12 to -$0.03 over 90 days (-121%). Next quarter estimates fell 53% over 90 days. Next year estimates declined 15%. This is uniformly negative earnings momentum — the consensus of 17-19 analysts is that the recovery is further out than management implies.

The "J-curve" structural challenge is critical: as capacity tightens and spot rates rise, RXO's buy rates increase immediately while its contract sell rates (majority of revenue) lag by 3-6 months. The early stages of recovery compress margins further before contracts reset. This means even a genuine cycle turn initially appears as deterioration in the numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.62, RXO trades at:
- Forward P/E: -33x (negative earnings)
- EV/Revenue: ~0.73x (on $5.74B 2025 revenue)
- Market cap: $3.76B
- EV: ~$4.15B

On next year's estimates ($0.39 EPS), the stock trades at 50x forward earnings — in line with peers SNDR (55.3x), ARCB (49.4x), and LSTR (50.1x). The entire cargo ground transportation sector trades at elevated multiples reflecting trough earnings.

RXO's case for trading at peer multiples is weaker given: (a) the weakest balance sheet of the group, (b) the most negative near-term earnings trajectory, (c) the most integration risk, and (d) deteriorating estimates while peers are more stable.

Analyst consensus median target is $16.00, with a high of $20.00 and low of $13.00. The stock at $19.62 is **near the high end of the analyst target range.** This is the opposite of a mispricing opportunity — the stock has run past where even the most optimistic analysts see value.

## Competitive Landscape

RXO competes against C.H. Robinson (larger, profitable, using AI for cost discipline), J.B. Hunt (asset-heavy hybrid model), TQL (private, growing rapidly), and Uber Freight (technology-native). The Coyote acquisition scaled RXO into a top-tier position, but competitors also acquired during the downturn — consolidation is industry-wide.

The proprietary technology platform (RXO Connect, Curve index) provides differentiation, but C.H. Robinson's Navisphere platform and Uber Freight's tech stack are comparable. The moat in freight brokerage comes from carrier density, customer relationships, and pricing algorithms — all of which are replicable over time. RXO's competitive position is improving due to scale from Coyote, but it has not yet demonstrated that scale translates to superior margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Freight recovery fails to materialize in 2026.** The recovery has been "imminent" since mid-2024. Each quarter brings new reasons for optimism but the P&L continues to show losses. If rates don't inflect meaningfully by Q3-Q4 2026, the 2027 debt maturity becomes a serious concern.

2. **2027 refinancing risk.** $355M in 7.50% notes mature November 2027. If the company remains unprofitable at refinancing, terms will be punitive. The shift from unsecured to secured (ABL) lending already signals tighter credit conditions for RXO.

3. **Goodwill impairment.** $1.11B in goodwill with Managed Solutions only 9% above fair value. Another weak quarter in managed transportation could trigger a write-down.

4. **Customer concentration.** Top customer represents 11.4% of revenue ($653M). Loss of this relationship would be material.

5. **Tariff/trade policy impact.** Current macro environment includes trade policy uncertainty and potential supply chain disruptions that could reduce domestic freight volumes.

6. **Structural margin compression.** The J-curve effect means even genuine recovery initially shows up as margin deterioration, which could trigger another leg down in the stock before improvement becomes visible.

## Conclusion

RXO presents an unattractive risk/reward at $19.62. MFN Partners' conviction buy at $12 was well-timed, but at $19.62 the remaining upside requires everything to go right on cycle timing, integration execution, and refinancing. AVOID.
