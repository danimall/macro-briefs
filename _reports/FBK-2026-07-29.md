**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FB Financial Corporation is a Nashville-based regional bank holding company operating FirstBank across Tennessee, Alabama, Kentucky, and Georgia, with roughly $16.5B in assets following the July 2025 Southern States merger. The investment case rests on a normalized earnings power that is meaningfully higher than a naive screen of FY25 results would indicate. Reported FY25 return metrics (0.84% ROAA, 6.9% ROAE, $2.45 EPS) were depressed by a stack of one-time items: a $60.5M securities repositioning loss, ~$28.4M of Day-2 CECL provisioning on acquired loans, $23.8M of merger costs, and a CECL methodology change. Strip those out and the franchise runs at ROAA ~1.43%, ROATCE ~14.7%, and an EPS run-rate of $1.10-$1.14 per quarter, which is what the last two prints have actually delivered.

The current setup is a well-capitalized Sun Belt bank generating double-digit annualized organic loan (11.6%) and deposit (7.7%) growth, with capital ratios comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds (CET1 11.5%, TCE/TA 9.91%) and a genuine shift toward returning excess capital: the buyback authorization was upsized from $150M to $175M and management repurchased $21.8M in Q1'26 at prices well below the current $59.98. The counterbalance is a set of watch items that prevent a stronger call: nonperforming construction loans tripled over the past year to ~$30.6M, early-stage delinquencies are migrating higher, and roughly 17bps of the reported 3.94% Q1'26 NIM is non-recurring purchase-accounting accretion that management's own FY26 guide (3.70-3.80% NIM) confirms will fade.

We rate FBK a HOLD (growth-hold) with an overall score of 5.85. The stock trades at ~12.8x forward EPS against a Regional Banks peer median of 13.5x, a modest discount rather than a compelling one, and consensus price targets of $65-67.50 imply ~8-13% upside. That is a respectable but not asymmetric return, and it depends on construction credit staying contained and NIM landing within guidance. The prior three reports have all landed on HOLD with an improving score trajectory (5.2 → 6.0 → 5.8); the current data confirms the fundamental improvement while keeping the credit and NIM-normalization overhangs intact.

## Company Overview

FB Financial operates through FirstBank, a Tennessee state-chartered bank with 90 full-service branches across four states plus a presence in Asheville, NC. The business runs two segments. Banking is the primary engine, offering commercial and consumer lending and deposits across the major Tennessee MSAs (Nashville, Chattanooga, Knoxville, Memphis, Jackson), Alabama (Birmingham, Huntsville, Auburn, Anniston), Bowling Green KY, and Columbus/Newnan GA, with roughly $15.7B of segment assets. The Mortgage segment originates and services retail mortgages, servicing $9.46B of loans for third parties (MSR asset $147.3M at fair value); mortgage banking income was $52.4M in 2025 but is a smaller, cyclical contributor (only $0.6M pretax in Q1'26).

The company pursues a serial-acquirer growth strategy in fast-growing Sun Belt markets, combining a relationship-driven community banking model with periodic M&A to expand its footprint. Its structural strengths are attractive-demographic markets (in-migration, business formation) and disciplined underwriting; its structural vulnerabilities are heavy geographic concentration (Tennessee accounts for ~55% of loans and ~62% of deposits) and intensifying competition as national banks push into Nashville, its most important and most contested market.

The Estate of former Chairman James W. Ayers holds roughly 14% of shares. Because the IPO shareholder agreement's director-designation rights terminated on Ayers' death, this is now a pure economic and voting concentration rather than a control-rights issue, but it remains a latent supply overhang if the estate distributes or sells.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale transformation via M&A.** Total assets rose from ~$13.2B (Q1'25) to $16.47B (Q1'26), driven by the July 1, 2025 Southern States merger (+$2.83B assets, $368.4M all-stock, +13 net branches). Loans held for investment reached $12.5B and deposits $14.08B.

**Q1 2026 (most recent 10-Q).** Net income was $57.5M, diluted EPS $1.10 (up from $0.84 in Q1'25). ROAA of 1.43%, ROAE of 11.9%, and ROATCE of 14.7% are strong for a regional bank. NIM (tax-equivalent) was 3.94%, up sharply from 3.55% a year earlier, though ~17bps came from purchase-accounting accretion on acquired loans, a non-recurring tailwind. The efficiency ratio of 55.2% (54.3% adjusted) reflects good operating leverage, and the cost of total deposits fell to 2.27% from 2.49% in FY25 as rates declined.

**Q2 2026 (reported July 13, 2026).** Net income was $58.6M, EPS $1.13 (adjusted $1.14), which slightly missed consensus of ~$1.16. PPNR grew to $83.3M, loan growth ran +11.6% annualized, and deposit growth +7.7% annualized. Management guided FY26 NIM to 3.70-3.80% (implying moderation from Q1's 3.94% as accretion fades) and banking expense to $325M-$335M.

**FY2025 was an intentionally noisy trough year.** Net income was $122.6M and EPS $2.45, but the depressed reported ROAA of 0.84% and ROAE of 6.90% reflected the one-timers listed above. Normalized earnings power is close to the ~$1.10-$1.14 quarterly run-rate now being posted, which is the figure that matters for forward modeling.

**Capital and balance sheet are strong.** Total risk-based capital is 13.4%, CET1 11.5%, and Tier 1 leverage 10.4%, all comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. TCE/TA is 9.91%, tangible book value per share is $31.00, and book value per share $38.39. The AOCI loss of only $35.3M (AFS unrealized losses of $51.5M against $16.5B assets) means this is not an underwater-securities story; the bank opted out of including AOCI in regulatory capital. Liquidity is strong: on-balance-sheet plus available sources equal 255% of estimated uninsured/uncollateralized deposits, which are a manageable 28.8% of total. There were no FHLB or Discount Window advances outstanding, and the company cleaned up its capital stack in 2025 by redeeming $30.9M of junior subordinated debentures and $100M of subordinated notes (while assuming $92.5M of Southern States sub notes).

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent two quarters missing consensus by 0.5% and 2.2%. This is a bank delivering solid but not consistently surprising results, which is consistent with the modest revision picture below.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a blend of organic Sun Belt loan/deposit demand and inorganic M&A, with the reported top-line inflated by the Southern States acquisition. Q2'26 revenue of $174.8M rose 26.6% YoY, but a large portion is acquisition-driven; the cleaner organic signal is the double-digit annualized loan growth and 7.7% deposit growth, which reflect broad-based demand across the Southeast footprint.

Estimate revisions are broadly stable-to-slightly-positive rather than an inflection. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $4.68 (essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days), next-year at $5.14 (+1.3% over 30 days, +0.4% over 90 days). This is not the >30% revision velocity that would flag an earnings inflection; it is a steady-compounder profile. Forward growth in the high single digits to low double digits (from $4.68 to $5.14, roughly +10%) is the reasonable expectation, driven by continued organic loan growth, deposit-cost relief in a declining-rate environment, and the annualization of Southern States.

Two headwinds temper the growth score. First, NIM will normalize lower as purchase accretion runs off, and the balance sheet is asset-sensitive (+100bps → +3.5% NII; −100bps → −3.6% NII), so falling rates are a modest NII headwind partly offset by cheaper deposits. Second, the mortgage segment is a cyclical swing factor that added little in Q1'26. The structural Sun Belt tailwind (in-migration, business formation) supports a durable mid-cycle growth path, but the reported acceleration is not a runway of accelerating estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

FBK trades at ~$59.98, a market cap of ~$3.0B, at approximately 12.8x the $4.68 current-year EPS estimate and ~11.7x the $5.14 next-year estimate. Against the Regional Banks peer median P/E of 13.5x, this is a modest discount. The dividend yield of ~1.4% is well below the peer median of 2.5%, reflecting a management preference for buyback-led capital return over dividends.

The peer set shows wide dispersion: PFS at 9.9x with a 4.2% yield, SBCF at 21.0x, TBBK at 11.0x, BANC at 16.0x, NBTB at 13.5x. FBK sits near the middle. On price-to-tangible-book, the stock trades at ~1.9x TBV ($59.98 / $31.00), a premium multiple that reflects the ~14.7% ROATCE: a defensible relationship, but not a bargain. The discount to peer P/E is partly justified by the credit watch items (construction NPLs) and the NIM normalization ahead, and partly by the ~14% Ayers estate overhang.

Consensus price targets of $65.00-$67.50 (mean $66.09, median $66.00) sit ~8-13% above spot. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, analyst targets can be floors, but FBK is not an inflection story. Estimate revisions are flat, not accelerating, so the targets are better read as fair anchors than as stale numbers destined for upward revision. The valuation is reasonable but not asymmetric: you are paying a fair price for a fair-quality regional bank with a modest peer discount, which supports a HOLD rather than a BUY. We assign a valuation score of 5.3; the multiple offers no clear mispricing catalyst, and cheapness alone is not a reason to lean in.

## Competitive Landscape

FBK competes against national banks (deeper resources and technology budgets), regional peers, community banks (deeper local ties), credit unions, and fintechs. Its competitive edge is a relationship-driven community model in attractive Sun Belt markets; its trajectory is improving on the growth side (double-digit organic loan growth, expanding footprint via M&A) but faces genuine pressure in Nashville, where the 10-K candidly flags escalating large-bank entry pressuring both share and margins.

The counter-strategy is twofold: consolidation and adjacency M&A (Southern States deepened Alabama/Georgia), and selective fintech partnerships, including an equity-method stake in a manufactured-housing fintech lender (two board seats, a master loan-purchase agreement up to $250M, $148.4M on the books). This is a modest, novel channel worth monitoring rather than a moat. The Sun Belt demographic backdrop is a durable structural tailwind evidenced by the loan-growth cadence.

Net trajectory: the competitive position is stable-to-improving, warranting a score of 6.2. The bank is gaining scale and holding profitability metrics (ROATCE ~14.7%) that sit above many peers, but it is a mid-cap community bank without a durable structural moat, and its most valuable market is under intensifying competitive pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Construction credit is the single most important item to monitor.** Nonperforming construction loans surged from ~$10.4M (Q1'25) to $34.2M (YE25), sitting at $30.6M at Q1'26 (of which $19.4M is consumer/residential construction). Total NPLs/loans rose to 0.96% from 0.79% a year earlier, and nonperforming assets grew to $162.0M from $110.4M. Early-stage 30-89 day accruing delinquencies rose to $86.4M from $66.8M at YE25, concentrated in C&I and construction. The offsetting comfort: net charge-offs remain very low (0.11% annualized) and ACL coverage is solid at 1.49% of loans (156% of NPLs). This is a watch item, not yet a loss event, but it is where a negative surprise would originate.

**Real estate and geographic concentration.** Roughly 77% of the loan book carries real estate collateral (CRE 40%, C&I 18%, construction 10%). CRE concentration is 260% of Tier 1 + ACL (below the 300% guideline but elevated) and construction 64% (below the 100% guideline). Combined with Tennessee/Nashville concentration, a Sun Belt CRE or housing downturn would hit disproportionately.

**Acquisition and purchased-credit risk.** FBK is a serial acquirer, so integration missteps and deal-related provisioning are recurring risks. 17% of the Southern States portfolio was classified PCD (credit-deteriorated) at acquisition, indicating the target ran a weaker book; underestimated losses would flow through future provisions.

**Mortgage/MSR sensitivity** to rates and GSE policy adds cyclicality. The Altman Z-Score of 0.26 flagged by standard health screens is not meaningful for a bank (banks are structurally leveraged by deposits); the relevant lens is credit quality, capital, and liquidity, all of which are currently healthy-with-a-watch-item.

We assign a risk-factors score of 5.2. The credit-migration trend in construction is a specific, partly under-appreciated downside vector; capital and liquidity are strong enough to absorb moderate stress, but the trajectory in nonperformers is the wrong direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.0% against 27.1% one-year historical volatility, a ~4.9pp premium. The put/call skew of 12.0% at the 3-month tenor indicates elevated put demand, consistent with hedging around the credit-quality and NIM-normalization uncertainty. The put/call open-interest ratio of 0.07 is low, and the term structure is flat (near 32.0% ≈ far 32.2%), suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single dated catalyst. ATM IV fell 5.1pp intraday (37.1% → 32.0%) as the last earnings event resolved. The elevated near-term put skew is a mild divergence for a name we rate HOLD; it signals some downside hedging appetite consistent with the construction-credit watch item, though it does not override the fundamentally sound capital and liquidity position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($67-72).** Normalized earnings power (ROATCE ~14.7%, EPS $1.13+/quarter compounding toward $5.14 next year) is durable; Sun Belt organic loan growth stays double-digit; construction NPLs stabilize and roll off without meaningful charge-offs; deposit costs continue to ease, cushioning the accretion runoff so NIM holds near the top of the guide; the upsized $175M buyback (repurchasing below intrinsic value) drives per-share accretion. At ~14x the $5.14 next-year estimate, that is roughly $72, with the $65-67.50 analyst targets as the near-term anchor. Continued M&A at sensible prices adds optionality.

**Bear Case ($46-50).** Construction and C&I early-delinquency migration converts to charge-offs, forcing higher provisions and pressuring EPS; a Sun Belt CRE/housing softening amplifies losses given 77% real-estate collateral; NIM normalizes toward the low end or below as accretion fades faster than deposit-cost relief; the Ayers estate distributes its ~14% block, pressuring the stock. That scenario re-rates the bank toward tangible book (~$31) plus a distressed-earnings multiple, roughly $46-50, near the 52-week low.

The base case sits between these: a fair-quality Sun Belt bank compounding EPS in the high-single-to-low-double digits, trading at a modest peer-discount multiple, with credit contained but bearing watching. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable, which is a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Construction nonperforming loans stabilize or decline over two consecutive quarters (Q3'26 and Q4'26 prints) with net charge-offs remaining below 0.20% annualized.
- NIM lands at or above the top of the guide with deposit costs continuing to fall, demonstrating the accretion runoff is being offset.
- Open-market insider buying emerges to confirm management's buyback-implied conviction, or the stock pulls back toward the low-$50s where the peer-discount widens and the risk/reward turns clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Nonperforming construction/C&I loans continue climbing with net charge-offs breaking above 0.30% annualized, signaling the delinquency migration is converting to losses.
- FY26 NIM guidance is cut below 3.70% or an EPS guidance reduction is issued.
- A significant Ayers estate distribution/sale is announced without offsetting demand, or a material integration/credit surprise on the acquired Southern States book.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3'26 earnings release (expected mid-October 2026), with particular attention to the construction NPL and early-delinquency tables and the pace of purchase-accretion runoff against the NIM guide.

## Conclusion

FB Financial is a well-run Sun Belt regional bank whose clean earnings power (ROATCE ~14.7%, EPS run-rate ~$1.13/quarter) is materially better than depressed FY25 GAAP metrics imply, backed by strong capital, strong liquidity, and a shareholder-friendly capital-return pivot. The improving score trajectory across our prior three reports (5.2 → 6.0 → 5.8) is confirmed by the current data: financial health and competitive position have genuinely strengthened. But the stock has recovered ~20% since the trough, trades at only a modest discount to peers, and offers ~8-13% upside to consensus targets, a respectable but not compelling return that hinges on construction credit staying benign and NIM normalizing within guidance. Rising construction nonperformers, fading purchase-accounting NIM tailwinds, elevated near-term put demand, and the absence of insider conviction buying collectively hold this at a growth-hold rather than a buy. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.85 and would look to upgrade on stabilizing credit metrics or a pullback toward the low-$50s.
