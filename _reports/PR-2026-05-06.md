## Executive Summary

Permian Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin operator with exceptional operational execution — drilling costs at ~$700/foot (guided to decline approximately 8% in 2026) represent industry-leading capital efficiency, enabling $1.64B in adjusted free cash flow (32.4% adjusted FCF margin) even as oil prices fell 14% in 2025. The company's "ground game" — over 700 small bolt-on transactions in 2025 — has replaced 100% of developed inventory through accretive M&A for three consecutive years, creating a self-reinforcing growth runway without the execution risk of mega-mergers.

EPS estimate revisions across the forward four quarters represent one of the strongest revision trends in the E&P sector, driven by three converging factors: continued cost deflation, the Waha gas pricing fix (company-guided to realize $0.25–$0.75/Mcf premium versus Waha in 2026), and structural production growth on reduced capex. At ~12.0x forward P/E on FY2026 estimates and ~11.0x on FY2027, PR trades at a modest premium to peers but is growing faster and operating at lower costs than most comparables.

The primary risks are binary commodity exposure (a 10% decline in oil reduces proved reserve PV10 by 23%), the stock's significant appreciation from its 52-week low, and universal insider selling — though the latter is largely attributable to the January 2026 corporate reorganization that converted Class C units to Class A shares.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources operates exclusively in the Delaware Basin across ~480,000 net leasehold acres (67% Texas, 33% New Mexico) with ~105,000 net royalty acres. The company was formed through the 2022 combination of Centennial Resource Development and Colgate Energy Partners, and has since scaled production from ~195,000 Boe/d (2023) to ~393,000 Boe/d (2025). Production is approximately 46% oil by Boe volume, with total liquids (oil plus NGLs) comprising roughly 71% of production — making the company heavily liquids-weighted with oil dominating revenue.

The co-CEO structure (William Hickey III and James Walter, co-founders of Colgate Energy) provides operational continuity, though it creates succession ambiguity. The January 2026 corporate reorganization eliminated the dual-class share structure (Class A/C), simplifying governance and removing a historical discount factor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**
Revenue grew from $2.13B (2022) to $5.07B (2025), though 2025 growth was effectively flat (+1.4%) as 14% production growth was offset by 14% lower oil realizations ($64.06/Bbl vs. $74.87). Operating income declined from $1.74B to $1.46B. Net income of $1.10B includes a $270M non-cash loss on convertible note redemption.

The cost structure is the critical differentiator. Per-unit LOE of $5.26/Boe (down 3% YoY), cash G&A of $0.83/Boe (down 11%), and D&C costs at ~$700/foot (down from ~$840 in 2024) place PR among the lowest-cost operators in the basin.

**Cash Flow Generation:**
Operating cash flow of $3.61B funded $1.97B in capex, yielding $1.64B in adjusted free cash flow (non-GAAP) — approximately an 8.7% adjusted FCF yield on the current ~$18.9B market cap (based on ~842M shares outstanding at $22.41). Adjusted FCF per share grew 18% YoY to $1.94.

**Capital Allocation:**
- Dividends: $503M ($0.60/share base in 2025; increased to $0.64 annualized in Feb 2026)
- Buybacks: $73.7M (4.4M shares at $11.57 average — well-timed)
- Bolt-on acquisitions: ~$1.1B (including $608M Apache acreage deal)
- Debt reduction: ~$640M in redemptions

**Balance Sheet:**
Long-term debt of $3.55B (down from $4.18B) with no revolver borrowings against $2.5B available capacity. Debt/equity at 0.62x is manageable for an E&P. The Altman Z-Score of 2.3 sits in the "grey zone" — not distressed but not pristine. Current ratio of 0.78 is below 1.0 technically but immaterial given the undrawn revolver.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth of 14% in 2025 will moderate in 2026 — company guidance of 400,000–430,000 Boe/d implies approximately 2%–10% total production growth (midpoint ~6%), with oil specifically guided to grow ~4%. This growth comes on $120M less capex, enabled by:

1. **D&C cost deflation:** ~$700/ft in Q4 2025, with 2026 budget supported by an approximately 8% reduction in D&C costs per foot versus 2025 (implying roughly $645/ft). This follows a 14% decline in 2025 versus 2024.
2. **Higher-productivity acquired acreage:** The bolt-on transactions in 2025 added approximately 250 high-return locations, extending inventory life.
3. **Waha gas fix:** New midstream agreements provide firm capacity and sales agreements for approximately 330 MMcf/d in 2026 (rising to ~700 MMcf/d by 2028), with company-guided realizations of $0.25–$0.75/Mcf premium versus Waha. PR has stated this represents over $100M in uplift to free cash flow in 2026.

The Q1 2026 results (reported May 6, 2026) showed adjusted EPS of $0.39, indicating continued operational execution despite lower oil prices, and follows strong beats in Q3 and Q4 2025. The pattern suggests estimates may still be lagging operational reality, though the gap is narrowing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.41, PR trades at:
- ~17.5x trailing P/E ($1.28 EPS)
- ~12.0x FY2026E P/E ($1.86 consensus)
- ~11.0x FY2027E P/E ($2.04 consensus)
- ~8.7% adjusted FCF yield (on 2025 actual adjusted FCF and ~$18.9B market cap)

The modest premium to peers appears justified by: (a) faster production growth versus peers guiding 0-3%, (b) lowest unit costs in the basin as reported by the company, (c) the unique acquisition strategy that extends inventory without mega-deal risk, and (d) the Waha gas fix providing a structural margin improvement.

However, the stock has appreciated significantly from its 52-week low. Recent analyst initiations include Truist at Buy/$24 (March 2026) and KeyBanc at Overweight/$25 (April 2026).

On forward estimates, 12x FY2026E = $22.32 (current level) and 12x FY2027E = $24.48. A modest premium to peers (say 13-14x) on FY2027 earnings gives $26.50-$28.50. The stock is no longer cheap in absolute terms, but the rising estimate base creates a moving target that partially offsets the multiple expansion already achieved.

## Competitive Landscape

PR's competitive position is improving along measurable dimensions:

- **Cost leadership:** D&C at ~$700/ft (guided ~8% lower in 2026). LOE at $5.26/Boe is among the lowest reported in the Delaware Basin peer set based on company disclosures.
- **Acquisition strategy differentiation:** The bolt-on approach avoids competitive auction premiums and integrates seamlessly. PR has replaced 100% of developed inventory through accretive M&A for three consecutive years.
- **Divestiture wave opportunity:** Post-mega-merger integration (ExxonMobil/Pioneer, Diamondback/Endeavor, ConocoPhillips/Marathon) will generate non-core Permian asset divestitures. PR is the natural acquirer given its integration capability and Delaware Basin focus.
- **TSR track record:** Indexed cumulative return of 1,015 (on a $100 base investment) vs. peer composite of 243, demonstrating sustained value creation relative to the peer group.

The primary competitive vulnerability is scale — at ~$18.9B market cap, PR lacks the diversification and financial flexibility of larger peers (OVV, FANG, COP). Geographic concentration in a single basin amplifies regulatory and infrastructure risks.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity — HIGH:** A 10% decline in oil prices reduces proved reserve PV10 by $2.2B (23%). The SEC reserve pricing used $66.01/Bbl for 2025 reserves. With current WTI in the low-to-mid $60s range, oil prices are at or below the reserve price deck, creating potential for further reserve revisions if prices remain depressed.

**Geographic Concentration:** 100% Delaware Basin exposure creates vulnerability to regional infrastructure bottlenecks, Waha gas pricing (partially mitigated by new midstream contracts), induced seismicity regulations, and water availability constraints.

**Customer Concentration:** Enterprise Crude Oil at 34% of revenue is elevated, though oil's fungibility limits the practical risk.

**PUD Conversion Risk:** 29% of reserves are PUD requiring drilling within 5 years. 27.2 MMBoe of PUD revisions in 2025 (including 36 MMBoe reclassified to unproved) indicates some inventory quality dispersion.

**Insider Selling:** All executives sold in January and March 2026. While largely attributable to the Class C/A conversion event and year-end vesting, the absence of any open-market purchases is a neutral-to-negative signal.

**OPEC Supply & Tariff Risks:** Higher-than-anticipated OPEC supply increases and tariff/trade policy uncertainty are cited as key 2025 headwinds in the company's filings.

## Conclusion

Permian Resources represents a high-quality E&P operator with demonstrable cost leadership, a differentiated acquisition strategy, and positive earnings momentum. The Waha gas fix represents a structural margin enhancement worth over $100M annually in 2026, scaling further as contracted volumes increase toward 700 MMcf/d by 2028.

The challenge is valuation entry point. At $22.41, the stock has appreciated significantly from its 52-week low and trades near the top of its recent range. The forward P/E of ~12x on FY2026 consensus is reasonable but not cheap, and the stock has already re-rated significantly. The BUY recommendation is maintained given strong adjusted FCF generation, positive earnings revision trajectory, and structural tailwinds from cost deflation and the Waha gas fix. However, position sizing should be moderated (2-3%) given the reduced margin of safety versus prior entry points and binary oil price risk.

The most favorable entry would occur on any pullback toward $19.50-20.50, which would compress the forward P/E to 10.5-11x FY2026 and restore more attractive risk/reward.
