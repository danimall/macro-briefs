**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Warrior Met Coal is a pure-play U.S. producer of premium hard-coking (metallurgical) coal sold almost entirely to global steelmakers via the Port of Mobile. The investment case rests on a specific, quantifiable event: the October 2025 startup of the Blue Creek longwall mine, eight months ahead of schedule and on budget, which raised nameplate capacity 88% from 7.3M to 13.7M metric tons per year. This lifted the company off a cyclical earnings trough (FY2025 net income of just $57M) into a volume-driven recovery. Q1 2026 net income of $72.3M already exceeded all of FY2025 in a single quarter, with cash cost of sales falling 14% to $106.02/ton as low-cost Blue Creek tons dilute the base. The combination of higher volume, falling unit cost, and a recovering seaborne price (Platts Premium Low Vol averaged $234.67/ton in Q1 2026 versus $185.08 a year earlier) is a genuine three-way operating lever.

The stock reflects much of this. HCC ran roughly 120% into a $110.39 all-time high on June 2, 2026, then pulled back ~27% to $80.07. At current levels it trades at ~30x trailing earnings and ~13x this year's consensus EPS of $6.11. The problem for a stronger call is that the forward estimate picture is deteriorating even as the operational story improves: current-year consensus EPS has been cut 25.4% over 90 days, the company missed the last two quarters (-10.4% and -21.0%), and Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $11.7M because of a $145.8M working-capital build. The bear counterpoint is timing rather than earnings quality, but the falling estimate trajectory carries real weight against a cyclical Materials name where cheap valuation has historically warned of a value trap rather than signaled a buy.

We rate HCC a HOLD (growth_hold). The fortress balance sheet, low-cost position, and 45X critical-mineral tax tailwind justify holding through the Blue Creek ramp and a working-capital unwind that should turn free cash flow sharply positive as capex collapses from ~$320M to a guided $155-190M. But we require either a stabilization in estimate revisions or a cheaper entry before upgrading. The five-year-unresolved labor contract and full seaborne-price exposure keep the risk profile two-sided.

## Company Overview

Warrior Met Coal operates three underground longwall mines in Alabama: Mine No. 4, Mine No. 7, and the new Blue Creek mine. Mine No. 7 produces Premium Low-Vol coal that prices in line with or at a slight discount to the Platts Premium Low Vol FOB Australia benchmark; Mine No. 4 and Blue Creek produce High Vol A coal priced off East Coast HVA and Pacific indices. The company sells essentially all output to blast-furnace steelmakers, with a Q1 2026 geographic mix of 61% Asia, 25% Europe, and 14% South America, shipped almost entirely through the McDuffie Terminal at the Port of Mobile.

The distinction between metallurgical and thermal coal matters here. HCC's product is a steelmaking input tied to global steel production, not electricity, and metallurgical coal is now classified as a critical mineral under U.S. law, which brings a different regulatory and demand profile. The company was formed from the 2016 bankruptcy-asset acquisition of Walter Energy and inherited legacy black-lung liabilities from that estate.

Warrior's core competitive claim is that it is a first-quartile (low-cost) producer on the global cost curve with high-quality, low-sulfur, high-fluidity coal. That cost position is what allowed it to remain cash-generative and profitable through the weak 2025 environment, and it is what gives the company maximum torque when prices recover.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant pattern is a commodity trough followed by a volume-driven recovery. Net income collapsed roughly 91% from the 2022 peak of $641M to $57M in FY2025 as realized met coal prices fell to roughly $146/ton for the year. Throughout that decline, the balance sheet remained strong (Altman Z ~3.8) because the company funded the ~$1.0B Blue Creek build largely from cash rather than debt.

Q1 2026 confirms the recovery is underway:
- Revenue of $458.6M, up 53% YoY from $299.9M.
- Net income of $72.3M ($1.37/share diluted) versus a $(8.2)M loss in Q1 2025.
- Production of 3.17M tons (+55% YoY) and sales of 2.72M tons (+38% YoY), driven by the Blue Creek ramp.
- Cash cost of sales down to $106.02/ton from $123.87/ton (-14%); cost of sales fell from 82% of revenue to 63%.
- Realized price of $164.70/ton, up from $149.71.
- Adjusted EBITDA of $143.4M, a 3.6x increase from $39.5M.

The bear counterpoint is in the cash flow statement. Despite record earnings, Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $11.7M because of the working-capital build: receivables rose $114.6M on higher volumes and timing, inventories rose $15.6M. Cash fell from $300M to $202.6M. This appears to be a ramp/timing artifact rather than a quality-of-earnings problem: receivables are backed by trade credit insurance with no historical credit losses, and the working capital should unwind as Blue Creek shipments normalize. It is the reason the "cash flow hasn't caught up" narrative has taken hold, and it explains part of the recent pullback.

The balance sheet at March 31, 2026 is genuinely defensive. Total debt is only ~$154M (7.875% Senior Secured Notes due December 2028, with $193.5M already paid down since issuance), no ABL borrowings, $140.5M available on the ABL, total liquidity of $363.7M, and equity of $2.2B. Financing leases (~$79.6M) are growing as Blue Creek equipment is leased. This low-leverage profile is atypical for the coal sector and materially de-risks the cyclicality; the company can self-fund through extended price weakness.

Capital allocation is modest and consistent. The dividend is $0.08/quarter (a ~0.4% yield), raised from $0.05 since 2017, with a $0.50 special in 2024. There is $59.4M remaining on the buyback authorization but it is essentially dormant. Management's stated policy is to return more cash in strong markets and prioritize strategic growth.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and worth weighting. HCC beat sharply in the two loss-quarters of 2025 (Q2 +140.3%, Q3 +309.0% off negative estimates) but then missed the last two quarters: Q4 2025 by 21.0% and Q1 2026 by 10.4%. Two consecutive misses as the recovery took hold reduces confidence in current consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth driver is real and confirmed. Blue Creek came in at a total project cost of $1,022.9M with construction complete and no material additional project capex expected. 2026 total capex is guided to just $155-190M, down from ~$320M in 2025. As capex collapses and the working-capital build unwinds, free cash flow should turn sharply positive.

The November 2025 federal coal lease acquisitions (Mine No. 1 at ~32.9M recoverable tons for ~$32M, Mine No. 4 at ~15.3M tons for ~$15M, with mining plan approvals received January 2026) extend reserve life and support the longer-term production runway.

The July 2025 OBBBA legislation adds a structural tax tailwind that a standard screen would miss. Metallurgical coal now qualifies for the Section 45X advanced manufacturing credit at 2.5% of eligible production costs for 2026-2029; HCC booked an $8.4M benefit in Q1 2026 alone as a reduction to cost of sales, implying a potential $30M+ annualized benefit. The FDII change also lowers the effective rate on foreign-derived income toward ~14%, meaningful for an export-heavy producer.

The offsetting concern is forward earnings momentum. Despite the operational strength, consensus estimates are broadly falling: current-quarter EPS revised down 16.8% over 30 days and 22.7% over 90 days; current-year EPS down 9.9% over 30 days; next-year EPS down 20.3% over 90 days. This reflects a softer seaborne price outlook and cautious buyer behavior (subdued Chinese steel activity, uneven seaborne demand) working against the volume ramp. The estimate-revision signal for HCC is clearly negative, which materially tempers what would otherwise be a strong volume-growth story. Actual output is management-discretionary; the company has stated it will not push tons into a weak market, so the volume upside is genuinely conditional on price recovery holding.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.07, HCC trades at ~30x trailing EPS of $1.08 (a figure distorted by the FY2025 trough). On next-year consensus of $7.21, the forward multiple is ~11x. Against the Steel peer set, the median P/E is 18.1x (WS 18.1x, CMC 17.4x), while several met-coal-exposed peers carry negative or distressed multiples (CLF -6.0, AMR -63.0), reflecting the depth of the cyclical trough across the group.

The forward multiple is not stretched. The difficulty is that cheapness on a cyclical Materials name is not, by itself, a buy signal; a compressed forward multiple that reflects a falling earnings trajectory is a value-trap risk. Here the earnings recovery is real, but this is not a clean estimate-revision buy setup: revisions are moving sharply the wrong way. The estimate-revision BUY signal (rising estimates + accelerating growth) is absent and running in reverse. That distinguishes HCC from a trough stock where estimates are climbing.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $90.00, median $100.00, mean $103.00, high $123.00, with UBS at Buy/$102. Because the stock trades below every analyst target while estimates are falling, we treat these targets with caution rather than as firm floors; targets on a commodity name lag the seaborne price, and a further downdraft in met coal pricing would pull both.

Our base case values HCC at approximately $95, roughly midway between the analyst floor and consensus mean, on the view that Blue Creek volumes and the 45X tax benefit support mid-cycle earnings power near current-year consensus while the seaborne price holds in the recovering-but-uneven range. That implies modest upside from $80. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a balanced setup: genuinely cheap on forward earnings, but with earnings momentum falling and a commodity price that can move the whole picture in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

Global seaborne met coal is an oligopolistic, cyclical market dominated by Australia, with the U.S., Canada, and Russia as major exporters and China and India as the dominant buyers. Warrior's structural position is favorable within this group. It is a first-quartile-cost producer with premium-quality coal, and post-Blue Creek it has materially larger scale. Its cost advantage protects margins in troughs and delivers torque in recoveries.

Two supply-side dynamics support seaborne pricing. Australian producers face a punishing royalty-tax regime that discourages investment, and Chinese domestic supply is periodically disrupted by safety crackdowns: a deadly Shanxi mine blast in May 2026 triggered safety checks and an ~8% jump in Chinese coking coal prices. On the demand side, China remains subdued (relying on domestic and Mongolian supply), while India is the key growth engine for seaborne demand. Europe and the Atlantic Basin remain sensitive to thin steel margins and trade policy.

The competitive trajectory is improving through scale and cost dilution, but it is contingent on the seaborne market absorbing incremental tons at acceptable prices. That is why we hold competitive position at 6.5 rather than higher: the low-cost moat is real, but Warrior is a price-taker in a market where Chinese steel demand is soft and tariff risk is unquantifiable.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price cyclicality** is the primary risk. Contracts are short-dated, pricing is set on quarterly index averages, and the company runs essentially no coal hedging. Full seaborne exposure cuts both ways, and FY2025 demonstrated how quickly earnings compress.

**Single-mine concentration of the bull thesis.** After the 88% expansion, the incremental output and the entire upside case depend on Blue Creek ramping and optimizing as planned. Any longwall or geologic issue in this one asset would disproportionately hit results.

**Unresolved labor contract.** The collective bargaining agreement expired April 1, 2021 and remains unresolved nearly five years later, following a roughly two-year UMWA strike (2021-2023), one of the longest in recent U.S. history. Management's language has not changed in years, indicating an uneasy status quo rather than resolution. This is a latent production-continuity and cost risk that no ratio captures.

**Logistics chokepoint.** Nearly all coal ships through the McDuffie Terminal at the Port of Mobile. Rail performance, weather, and port mechanical failures are explicitly cited concentration risks.

**Demand and tariff softness.** Q1 2026 MD&A describes uneven seaborne demand, subdued Chinese steel activity, cautious Indian procurement, and warns that U.S./retaliatory tariffs could hit demand and pricing, with the impact "not reasonably estimable."

**Legacy black-lung liabilities and self-insurance collateral.** The DOL's 2025 Final Regulations require self-insurers to post 100% of projected liabilities; the DCMWC previously sought to raise HCC's collateral from ~$10M toward $28-40M. In regulatory limbo, but a potential cash drain.

**Customer concentration.** Two customers each exceeded 10% of mining revenue in Q1 2026 (Customer A $62M, Customer B $51M).

The risk_factors score of 5.0 reflects that these are largely known and partly priced, but the falling estimate trajectory, unquantifiable tariff exposure, and the quiet labor tail keep the profile from being cleanly asymmetric.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 60.2% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 52.6%, and the term structure is in backwardation (60.2% near versus 53.4% at six months), with an implied move of ±13.6% into the nearest expiry. Put/call OI is elevated at 2.89 (down from 3.26), and the put/call volume ratio is 1.57, indicating defensive positioning. The near-term put/call skew is balanced (0.4%), but the six-month skew is meaningfully negative at -7.8%, showing hedging demand for a larger downside move over the medium term. Short interest at 10.1% of float (5.3 days to cover) confirms bearish institutional positioning without being crowded. The options picture reinforces the HOLD: the market is pricing a two-sided, event-heavy setup, not a clean directional entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($123).** Seaborne met coal prices continue recovering as Chinese safety crackdowns and Australian tax-driven underinvestment tighten supply while Indian demand grows. Blue Creek ramps to full run-rate at first-quartile cost, driving unit costs lower still. The working-capital build unwinds, capex falls to the guided range, and free cash flow turns sharply positive, funding accelerated buybacks against the ~$59M authorization. The 45X credit adds $30M+ annualized. Estimate revisions turn positive, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target and prior all-time high.

**Bear Case ($60).** Chinese steel demand stays soft, tariffs bite, and the seaborne price rolls back over. Current-year estimates continue their decline, and the two-quarter miss streak extends. The stock is a price-taker with no coal hedges; a return toward $146/ton realized prices would compress earnings toward FY2025-like levels. A labor disruption or a Blue Creek geologic issue would compound the downside. At $60, the stock would sit ~25% below current price and back near its post-run consolidation zone.

**Base Case ($95).** Blue Creek volumes and the tax benefit support current-year earnings near consensus while the seaborne price holds in an uneven, range-bound recovery. Free cash flow turns positive as capex and working capital normalize, but estimate revisions stabilize rather than re-accelerate. The stock drifts toward the analyst floor-to-mean band, roughly 19% above current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $95.** Derived from ~15x current-year consensus EPS of $6.11 supported by Blue Creek volume and the 45X benefit, cross-checked against the analyst low ($90) and mean ($103) targets and midpoint of that band. Roughly 19% upside from $80.07.

**Bull case target: $123.** The high analyst target, reached if seaborne prices re-rate and estimate revisions turn positive; ~54% upside.

**Bear case target: $60.** If the seaborne price rolls over and the estimate-cut trend continues; ~25% downside from current.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next two earnings reports (the next print will show whether the working-capital build reverses and free cash flow turns positive) and the seaborne price trajectory through H2 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-quarter estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive over a 30-60 day window (reversing the current -25% CY trend).
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating cash flow turns clearly positive as the receivables build unwinds and free cash flow inflects.
- A pullback toward $65-70 that widens the margin of safety on forward earnings without fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Realized met coal prices decline toward FY2025 levels (~$146/ton) with continued estimate cuts.
- A labor disruption or Blue Creek operational/geologic issue that impairs the volume ramp.
- Free cash flow remains negative beyond Q3 2026, signaling the working-capital build is structural rather than timing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and any material move in the Platts Premium Low Vol benchmark.

## Conclusion

Warrior Met Coal offers a genuine operating-leverage inflection: an 88%-capacity expansion completed at the bottom of the cycle, at first-quartile cost, funded from a fortress balance sheet, arriving as seaborne prices recover and a structural 45X tax tailwind kicks in. Q1 2026 results validate the mechanism. Against that, the forward estimate picture is deteriorating sharply, the company has missed the last two quarters, operating cash flow is negative on a working-capital build, and the stock has already run 120% before giving back 27%. Cheapness on a cyclical commodity name where estimates are falling is a caution flag, not a green light.

The trajectory of scores has improved (5.1 in March to 6.0 in May), and the new data confirms the financial-health and competitive improvements while flagging that the growth momentum has softened at the estimate level. We maintain HOLD (growth_hold) at an overall 5.75. The balance sheet and cost position justify holding through the ramp and the free-cash-flow inflection, but we want to see estimate revisions stabilize or a cheaper entry before committing to a BUY. The setup is asymmetric enough to own, not yet compelling enough to add aggressively at $80.
