**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brinker International has engineered one of the most dramatic turnarounds in casual dining. Under CEO Kevin Hochman, Chili's — roughly 90% of revenue — has posted 20 consecutive quarters of same-store sales growth, driving FY25 revenue up 22% to $5.38B, operating margin from 4.1% (FY22) to 9.5%, and net income to roughly $460M. The balance sheet recovered from negative shareholder equity as recently as FY23 (–$144M) to +$406M at Q3 FY26, funded entirely by retained earnings while the company simultaneously repaid debt and accelerated buybacks. Free cash flow of $413.7M (7.7% margin) and a clean debt structure (essentially one $350M 8.25% bond callable in July 2026) make this a genuinely high-quality business.

The engine that drove the re-rating is now changing character. In Q3 FY26 (ended March 25, 2026), Chili's comps decelerated to +4.0% against a +31.6% prior-year comparison, and traffic turned negative at –1.2%. The +4.0% comp is now carried almost entirely by menu pricing (+4.6%), the inverse of the FY25 traffic-led story (+16% traffic on a +25.3% comp). Maggiano's, the second brand, is in outright decline with –4.6% comps and –10.4% traffic. The stock fell roughly 8% after the print and has declined about 15% over the trailing twelve months, sitting at $153.55 versus a 52-week high of $187.

The central question is whether Chili's structurally captured share or simply harvested a fast-food value-gap window that is now closing as competitors copy the playbook. At ~14.5x forward EPS with consensus estimate revisions essentially flat over the last 90 days, the market is pricing neither collapse nor continued acceleration. We rate EAT a HOLD. The fundamentals do not justify the kind of asymmetric upside required for a BUY in a sector where company-specific outperformance is the exception, and they are too solid — strong FCF, repaired balance sheet, near-term debt-redemption catalyst — to warrant an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Brinker owns, operates, and franchises two casual-dining brands: Chili's Grill & Bar (the flagship) and Maggiano's Little Italy (upscale Italian-American). As of March 25, 2026, the system spanned 1,632 restaurants — 1,162 company-owned and 470 franchised — across the U.S., 28 countries, and two U.S. territories. The model is overwhelmingly company-operated; franchise revenue is only about 1% of total (~$49M), even though franchisees generated roughly $984M in system sales in FY25.

The turnaround strategy under Hochman, who arrived in 2022 from Yum/KFC, has three pillars: simplify the menu around five core equities (burgers, fajitas, Chicken Crispers, margaritas, the Triple Dipper); lean into everyday value through the "3 for Me" platform starting at $10.99; and amplify through viral social-media marketing such as the "Big Smasher." As quick-service operators raised prices faster than casual dining after 2021, Chili's positioned itself as a value destination and pulled in trade-down traffic, which converted into the traffic-led comps that drove the re-rating.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory is exceptional:

| Metric (FY ends late June) | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.90B | $4.13B | $4.42B | $5.38B (+22%) |
| Operating income | $159.5M | $144.4M | $229.6M | $512M |
| Operating margin | 4.1% | 3.5% | 5.2% | 9.5% |
| Net income | $115M | $91M | $165M | ~$460M |
| Stockholders' equity | –$268M | –$144M | +$39M | +$371M |
| Long-term debt | $989M | $912M | $786M | $426M |

FY25 was the peak of the story. Chili's comps rose 25.3% with 16.0% from traffic rather than pricing, which delivered powerful operating leverage: restaurant labor improved 140bps and restaurant expenses 280bps as a percentage of sales. The deleveraging was equally striking: equity rebuilt from –$268M to +$371M, debt fell from $989M to $426M, and the company repaid both the revolver and the $350M 5.00% notes in October 2024. Interest expense fell from $65M in FY24 toward a ~$42M run rate.

Management noted Chili's February/March recovered to +5.9% with positive traffic after a weather-disrupted January (+0.6%, Winter Storm Fern), so the trend is not collapsing, but the easy-comp tailwind is exhausted. Restaurant operating margin slipped to 18.4% of sales from 18.9% on commodity inflation in meat, seafood, and poultry. FY26 guidance was tightened rather than raised: revenue $5.78–5.82B and adjusted diluted EPS $10.60–10.85 (the bottom end lifted from $10.45, the top unchanged), with capex trimmed to $240–250M.

Cash generation remains the strongest part of the story. FY25 FCF was $413.7M; 9-month FY26 operating cash flow rose to $571.8M. The remaining debt is essentially the $350M 8.25% senior notes maturing July 2030 plus finance leases, with the $1.0B revolver undrawn ($969.9M available). Management is actively assessing early redemption of the 8.25% notes once they become callable July 15, 2026 — a concrete, self-help EPS catalyst independent of comp trends.

Headline balance-sheet ratios look alarming but are artifacts of an asset-light, lease-heavy model recovering from a thin equity base: current ratio 0.31 (normal for restaurants, where cash sales precede payables), D/E 7.2x, and ROE 103% (distorted by the recently rebuilt equity). The real signals — EBIT/assets of 0.19 and asset turnover of 2.0x — point to genuine operating health. A screen-based model would misread these as distress; they are recapitalization legacy.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis hinges on a single variable: whether Chili's traffic gains are structural or cyclical. The FY24–25 re-rating rested entirely on traffic-led comps fueled by a fast-food price gap. With Q3 FY26 traffic now negative and comps reliant on pricing, the composition of growth has flipped to the more fragile kind. Casual dining is structurally low-growth on traffic — the company's own 10-K notes the segment "has not seen significant growth in customer traffic in recent years" — so Brinker's outperformance has been a share-gain story, and share gains decelerate when the conditions that created them fade.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the plateau rather than an inflection. Current-year EPS sits at $10.76, up just 0.6% over 90 days; next-year at $12.48, up 1.3%; current-quarter $3.09, up 1.9%. These are stable, not accelerating, and they fall well short of the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection worth chasing. The earnings surprise track record is solid (3 beats and 1 in-line over four quarters, with the December and September quarters beating by 8.2% and 7.8%), but the March quarter came in essentially in line (0.1% surprise) as visibility narrows and comparisons toughen.

The competitive dynamic compounds the deceleration risk: the value playbook is being copied. The 10-K explicitly cites the risk of "competitors employing our same strategies or discounting," which would compress the value gap that drove Chili's traffic. If Brinker must raise menu prices to offset commodity and labor inflation, it risks eroding the value perception that pulled in the trade-down traffic in the first place. Maggiano's, with traffic down 10.4% and restaurant operating margin collapsing to 9.6% from 14.3%, offers no offset and undercuts the two-brand narrative, though management's elimination of banquet service charges explains part of the reported revenue weakness.

## Valuation Assessment

At $153.55 against FY26 adjusted EPS guidance, EAT trades at roughly 14.2–14.5x forward earnings, with consensus next-year EPS implying ~12.3x. On its face that is undemanding versus the restaurant peer median P/E of 38.3x:

| Ticker | Name | Mkt Cap | P/E |
|--------|------|---------|-----|
| BROS | Dutch Bros | $9B | 102.6 |
| WING | Wingstop | $4B | 38.3 |
| CAKE | Cheesecake Factory | $4B | 22.0 |
| CAVA | Cava Group | $11B | 169.7 |
| DPZ | Domino's | $11B | 18.0 |

The peer set is heterogeneous and dominated by high-growth fast-casual names (BROS, CAVA) that distort the median. The relevant comparable is CAKE at 22.0x — another mature casual-dining operator — and Brinker trades at a meaningful discount to it. That discount is partly earned: EAT is past its growth peak, traffic has turned negative, and the multiple correctly reflects a normalizing rather than accelerating franchise.

The case for a higher valuation score requires demonstrating the market is wrong, and the evidence is mixed. The bull argument: ~14.5x is cheap for a business generating 7.7% FCF margins, with a clean balance sheet, a near-term debt-redemption catalyst that adds EPS, and aggressive buybacks shrinking the share count. The bear argument: the discount to CAKE is appropriate because Brinker's earnings are now leaning on pricing into a value-positioned concept, and a 14.5x multiple on potentially peak-cycle earnings is not the bargain it appears. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising, so this is not the inflection profile where analyst targets function as floors — the stock has already re-rated substantially off its lows, and that re-rating reflected the now-fading traffic story.

Analyst consensus targets ($184.9 mean, $186.5 median, $160 low, $210 high) sit roughly 20% above the current price, and TD Cowen raised its target on June 11. With revisions stalled, those targets are more likely to be trimmed than treated as floors. We assign a valuation score of 5.5 — modestly below neutral. The absolute multiple offers downside support, but there is no identifiable catalyst for re-rating higher, and cheapness without a catalyst has historically been a weak basis for outperformance.

## Competitive Landscape

Chili's competitive position is improving relative to where it stood three years ago but is no longer demonstrably gaining ground. The brand took real share through a differentiated value platform combined with viral marketing, and 20 consecutive quarters of comp growth is genuine evidence of operational competency. The moat, however, is shallow: the value gap that drove the gains is competitor-replicable, and the 10-K flags exactly this risk.

The primary competitors — Darden (Olive Garden), Bloomin' Brands, Texas Roadhouse, Dine Brands (Applebee's) — alongside fast-casual and grocery substitution, are all positioned to narrow the price differential. Chili's restaurant operating margin is now slightly compressing (18.4% versus 18.9%), the opposite of the expanding-margin signal that would confirm durable share capture. Maggiano's deterioration removes the option of a credible second growth leg. The position is best characterized as stable-to-modestly-improving with a moat that is operationally real but structurally narrow — a 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is traffic normalization. The entire re-rating rested on traffic-led comps, and that engine has stalled. If the fast-food value gap continues to close and February/March's return-to-positive-traffic proves transient, comps roll over to pricing-only and eventually negative, with operating deleverage reversing the margin gains.

Secondary risks: Maggiano's outright decline tests management's credibility on the second brand; re-accelerating commodity and labor inflation (Q3 food costs unfavorable 60bps, with tariffs newly emphasized in the 10-Q's operating-environment language); geographic concentration (Texas 18.9%, Florida 11.8%, California 9.2% of company restaurants, exposing the chain to regional shocks, weather — already a Q1 and Q3 FY26 factor — and California wage legislation); and the value-positioning trap, where raising prices to cover inflation erodes the value perception that drives traffic. Single-brand dependence on Chili's amplifies any reputation event.

The macro backdrop is a risk for a value-positioned casual diner: casual dining is squarely discretionary, and trade-down dynamics that helped Chili's against fast food could reverse if households cut restaurant visits entirely in a downturn. Governance is a yellow flag rather than red. FY25 CEO "compensation actually paid" of $122.3M is mechanically driven by the +750% cumulative TSR (versus 182% for peers), so it reflects alignment rather than extraction, but the absolute magnitude invites say-on-pay scrutiny. The CEO adopted a 10b5-1 plan in March 2026 to sell up to 120,000 shares, and a director (Frances L. Allen) made small open-market sales in the $135–143 range. There has been no open-market C-suite buying. After a large multi-year run, pre-planned sales are not alarming, but the absence of insider conviction is a concern.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 69.7% versus 46.6% historical, an IV-rich spread of 23.1pp and an 86th-percentile reading within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (69.7% near-term versus 47.9% six-month), which the market uses to signal an expected near-term event. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.0%. Put/call positioning is actually call-heavy (skew –29.5%, P/C OI ratio 0.32, down from 0.80 a month ago), and IV has spiked 19.9pp alongside a 19.9% price recovery off the May lows. The combination of sharp IV spike, backwardation, and call-skew reads as positioning ahead of a catalyst rather than an outright bearish bet. Short interest is elevated at 18.9% of float with 4.5 days to cover, which creates squeeze potential on any positive surprise but also confirms a meaningful institutional bearish camp. The options picture reinforces the HOLD: the market expects volatility and is divided on direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185–210):** Chili's traffic gains prove structural rather than cyclical, with February/March's return to positive traffic persisting through FY27. The brand has rebuilt a durable value position and operational competency that competitors cannot easily replicate at scale. The 8.25% notes are redeemed in July 2026, removing a high-coupon liability and adding EPS. Aggressive buybacks (2.5M shares repurchased in 9M FY26) continue to shrink the share count. EAT re-rates toward the CAKE multiple (22x) on ~$12.50 next-year EPS, supporting the analyst high target of $210.

**Bear Case ($110–125):** The fast-food value gap closes as competitors copy the playbook, Chili's traffic stays negative, and comps roll to pricing-only then negative. Operating deleverage reverses the FY25 margin gains, FY27 EPS falls below FY26, and Maggiano's continues to deteriorate. A weakening discretionary consumer accelerates the rollover. The multiple compresses toward 11–12x on falling earnings, taking the stock toward the spring 2026 lows near $100–125, roughly 20–35% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between: comps grind out low-single-digit positive prints carried by pricing, margins hold roughly flat, the debt redemption and buybacks provide modest EPS support, and the stock trades in a range as the market waits for evidence on traffic durability.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 6–12 month reassessment horizon, anchored to the FY26 Q4 print (expected August 2026) and the July 2026 debt-redemption decision.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Chili's traffic returns to and sustains positive territory for two consecutive quarters, confirming the share gains are structural rather than a closing value-gap window.
- The 8.25% notes are redeemed and management reallocates the interest savings plus buyback capacity at prices below ~$140, with consensus EPS revisions turning positive (>5% over 90 days).
- Maggiano's comps stabilize toward flat, validating the two-brand turnaround.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Chili's comps turn negative or restaurant operating margin compresses more than ~150bps year-over-year, signaling the value-positioning trap is playing out.
- FY27 guidance implies EPS below FY26, confirming peak earnings.
- A discretionary-consumer downturn drives traffic declines that pricing cannot offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FY26 Q4 earnings release (~August 2026) for full-year traffic and FY27 guidance, with the July 2026 debt-call decision as an interim event.

## Conclusion

Brinker is a high-quality, well-managed business that executed a textbook turnaround — repaired balance sheet, strong free cash flow, disciplined capital return, and a near-term self-help catalyst in the callable bond. The score trajectory has improved over the past several months (5.9 → 6.6 → 6.9), driven by genuine gains in financial health and competitive standing. The Q3 FY26 print marks a real change in the growth profile: traffic turned negative, comps are now pricing-dependent, Maggiano's is deteriorating, and consensus estimate revisions have flattened. The stock has already re-rated substantially off its lows and trades at ~14.5x forward EPS — undemanding versus distorted peer medians but appropriately discounted to its mature casual-dining comparable given the deceleration.

The central question of durable share gains versus a fading value-gap tailwind is unresolved, and the data points in both directions. There is no asymmetric upside that would justify a BUY in a sector where company-specific outperformance is rare and hard to earn, and the business is too sound to AVOID. We rate EAT a HOLD: the next two quarters of traffic data will determine whether this re-rates higher or rolls over. We pull our score modestly back to 5.8 from the prior 6.9, reflecting not a fundamental deterioration in the business but the confirmation that the growth engine has shifted to a more fragile, pricing-dependent footing with no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher.
