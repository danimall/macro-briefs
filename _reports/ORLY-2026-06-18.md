**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

O'Reilly Automotive is among the highest-quality businesses in U.S. retail: a tiered distribution network (32 distribution centers, 399 hub stores, ~6,644 total locations) that enables same-day and overnight parts availability no online or sub-scale competitor can match, paired with a dual DIY/professional model that lets it operate profitably in both dense urban and sparsely populated markets. The result is 33 consecutive years of record revenue and earnings, 51.6% gross margins, and a 19.5% operating margin. Q1 2026 marked a sharp acceleration: comparable store sales rose +8.1% (versus +3.6% in the prior-year quarter and +4.7% for full-year 2025), revenue grew +10% to $4.56B, and operating margin expanded to 18.5% from 17.9%. The company is fundamentally strong and executing well.

The investment question is not business quality but price and growth durability. At $88.40, ORLY trades at roughly 28.8x earnings versus an Automotive Retail peer median of 21.5x — a ~34% premium. That premium has historically been earned by consistent compounding, but two things give me pause. First, consensus EPS estimates have not moved despite the Q1 blowout: the current-year estimate rose only +0.7% over 90 days and the next-quarter estimate is flat. A genuine acceleration of the magnitude the Q1 comp implies would normally pull estimates higher; the absence of that revision tells me analysts view the comp acceleration as price-driven and partly transient. Second, the comp gain is increasingly built on average-ticket inflation (tariff pass-through plus part-complexity) against declining DIY transaction counts, a higher-quality concern that could fade if tariff inflation normalizes or consumers trade down.

I am maintaining a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The moat, capital discipline, and aging-fleet tailwind justify owning the stock through cycles, and the recent pullback toward the low-$88s improves entry. A BUY requires either a multiple closer to the company's growth rate or an estimate-revision turn confirming the comp acceleration is durable rather than a tariff-driven ticket bump. Neither condition is met today.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1957 and public since 1993, O'Reilly is one of the largest specialty retailers of automotive aftermarket parts in North America. As of Q1 2026 it operated 6,495 stores in the U.S. and Puerto Rico, 121 in Mexico, and 28 in Canada (~6,644 total), supported by distribution centers spanning ~14M square feet and carrying 156,000+ SKUs, plus hub stores that hold deeper inventory for rapid replenishment.

The defining feature is the dual-market strategy: roughly 50% DIY (do-it-yourself retail customers) and 50% professional service providers, with the Pro mix growing faster. This shared store-and-distribution infrastructure lets O'Reilly profitably serve both ends of the market. Competitive advantages stem from technically proficient store staff, a promote-from-within culture (264 senior managers averaging 20 years' tenure), and distribution density that supports parts availability competitors struggle to match.

The company reports as a single segment. It estimates a ~$435B total U.S. aftermarket, with an addressable parts subset of ~$165–175B, leaving substantial runway for continued share gains in a still-fragmented industry. Principal competitors are AutoZone, Advance Auto Parts, NAPA/Genuine Parts, CARQUEST, and Walmart and Amazon at the periphery.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and comps.** FY2025 revenue reached $17.78B, +6.4% YoY, with comps of +4.7% (up from +2.9% in 2024). The Q1 2026 comp of +8.1% was the strongest print outside the COVID-stimulus era. Growth was driven by rising average ticket on both Pro and DIY, Pro transaction growth, and same-SKU price increases (partly tariff pass-through). The persistent soft spot is declining DIY transaction counts, pressured by constrained discretionary spending and longer repair intervals on better-engineered vehicles.

**Margins.** FY2025 gross margin was 51.6% (up from 51.2%), operating margin 19.5%, net margin 14.3% — best-in-class for retail. The Q1 2026 operating-margin expansion reflected strong comps leveraging fixed store costs. Headwinds: the shift toward lower-margin Pro mix and SG&A inflation in medical/casualty insurance and store-level wages.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2.76B and FCF $1.56B (down from ~$1.99B in 2024, primarily renewable-energy tax-credit payment timing rather than operational deterioration). Capex rose to $1.17B (from $1.0B) on distribution expansion and accelerated new-store growth (207 net new in 2025; 225–235 guided for 2026). Buybacks dominate cash use: $28.7B cumulative since 2011 at a ~$19.41 split-adjusted average price, reducing the share count from a ~1.5B equivalent. The Board re-upped the authorization by $2B in November 2025 ($29.8B cumulative; ~$1.5B remaining at Q1-end). Q1 2026 alone saw $923M repurchased at an average $92.45.

**Balance sheet — context required.** Stockholders' equity is negative (–$1.07B at Q1 2026) and the Altman Z-Score of 1.62 nominally flags "distress." Both are artifacts of the buyback strategy: O'Reilly has repurchased more equity than its cumulative retained earnings, producing a retained deficit (–$2.6B). This is not financial distress. The credit metrics confirm a fortress balance sheet: fixed-charge coverage of 6.14x (vs. a 2.50x covenant minimum) and a leverage ratio of 1.92x (vs. a 3.50x maximum). Long-term debt is ~$6.2B, all unsecured senior notes (1.75%–5.75%), plus a $525M commercial-paper balance and no revolver drawn. The current ratio of 0.77 reflects a deliberate negative-working-capital model: inventory is funded by payables (AP/inventory ~124%) plus a $5.2B supplier-finance program. This reflects supplier-financing strength typical of best-in-class retailers, not a liquidity concern. The risk worth watching is that a credit-rating downgrade could raise interest costs and impair the attractive supplier-finance terms.

## Growth Analysis

The long-term demand backdrop is favorable. U.S. miles driven are ~3 trillion/year (+0.9% in 2025, +1.3% YTD early 2026); there are 286M registered light vehicles; the average vehicle age is a record 12.6 years (up 10.5% over the decade); and SAAR is ~16.0M (2025), ~16.3M estimated for 2026. Aging fleets and elevated used-vehicle prices push consumers to maintain existing vehicles, which is structurally supportive of parts demand. The industry remains fragmented, giving scale players room to consolidate independents.

The near-term growth signal is more nuanced than the headline comp suggests. The comp acceleration is increasingly price/ticket-driven rather than unit/traffic-driven: average ticket is rising (tariff pass-through plus part-complexity inflation) while DIY transaction counts decline. Management is candid that durable, complex parts simultaneously raise ticket and reduce repair frequency. This makes the comp's durability dependent on continued tariff inflation and pricing power holding. If tariff-related increases reverse or consumers trade down, the ticket tailwind could fade.

The clearest evidence on durability comes from the estimate revisions, and they are flat. Current-year EPS consensus moved only +0.1% over 30 days; next-quarter estimates are flat (-0.1%). For a company that just printed a comp roughly double the prior-year quarter, analysts declined to raise forward numbers, treating the acceleration as a price-driven, partly transient phenomenon rather than a structural step-up in earnings power. The earnings-surprise record reinforces caution: over the last four quarters, ORLY posted one beat, one in-line, and two small misses, so the forward estimates do not carry a high beat-rate cushion. Modeled growth of mid-single-digit revenue with low-double-digit EPS (helped by buybacks) is reasonable but does not show the accelerating profile that would justify chasing a premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

ORLY trades at ~28.8x trailing and forward earnings. Against the Automotive Retail peer set — AutoZone 21.2x, Carvana 39.6x, Penske 13.1x, Murphy USA 21.5x, CarMax 30.7x, median 21.5x — that is roughly a 34% premium to the median and a clear premium to its closest direct comparable, AutoZone.

The premium is partly defensible: O'Reilly's margins, ROIC, distribution moat, and execution consistency are superior to most peers, and the aging-fleet thesis is durable. But a premium multiple needs to be earned by growth each year, and the current setup does not support paying up further. Consensus current-year EPS is $3.26 and next-year $3.62; on the next-year figure the stock trades at ~24.4x. Applying a still-generous 24x to next-year EPS implies a fair value around $87, essentially the current price. The implied forward growth in consensus is high-single to low-double digits; at PEG terms the stock is not cheap, and the flat revision trend removes the case that consensus is stale and about to be revised up.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue the valuation here. Earnings are at record levels, not depressed, and the recent acceleration is price-driven rather than a recovery from a depressed base. The premium multiple discounts continued compounding; any disappointment on comp sustainability or DIY traffic would compress the multiple toward the peer median, which from 28.8x toward ~22x is a ~24% derating risk on the multiple alone. I score valuation 4.5: the absolute level is not absurd for the quality, but there is no identifiable mispricing to exploit and the asymmetry skews to multiple compression rather than expansion.

I am not penalizing the stock for prior appreciation; the issue is the current price versus current and forward fundamentals, where the multiple already embeds the strong quarter while estimates have not moved.

## Competitive Landscape

O'Reilly's competitive position is strong and arguably still improving. It is gaining share in a fragmented industry where scale players (O'Reilly, AutoZone, NAPA) consolidate independents, while Advance Auto Parts struggles. Its distribution density and balanced dual-market model differentiate it from a more DIY-weighted AutoZone and let it serve markets that DIY-only chains cannot. The Pro-side momentum (faster-growing, supported by hub-store availability) is the right place to be gaining share as DIY traffic softens industry-wide.

Supplier concentration is moderate (top five suppliers ~23% of purchases, largest ~8%) and manageable. The structural threats are long-dated: EV adoption could eventually eliminate certain ICE-parts categories, better-engineered vehicles reduce repair frequency (a headwind already visible in DIY transaction counts), and OEM restrictions on telematics/diagnostic access could push some repairs back to dealers. None of these is acute over the investment horizon, but they cap the terminal growth rate that justifies a premium multiple. International expansion (Mexico, Canada via the Vast Auto acquisition) is optionality, still immaterial to consolidated results.

## Risk Assessment

The principal company-specific risks: (1) DIY demand erosion and consumer pressure — the clearest current soft spot, with declining DIY transaction counts; (2) tariff and input-cost inflation, which has been a ticket tailwind via pass-through but could compress demand if it outpaces wage growth; (3) the vehicle-technology shift toward longer-lived and EV parts; (4) leverage and credit-rating risk, where a downgrade would raise interest costs and could impair the supplier-finance program; and (5) competition and online price transparency. Risk factors were unchanged between the FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q.

The 10-K added language flagging the risk of coordinated buying/selling "by a group of individuals," an unusual acknowledgment likely tied to the June 2025 15-for-1 split and increased retail-investor accessibility. This warrants monitoring as a volatility, not solvency, factor.

I score risk 6.0, slightly below neutral. The business has few binary or balance-sheet risks, but the comp-durability question (price-driven gains against falling traffic) is a genuine, not-fully-priced concern, and the premium multiple amplifies the consequences of any disappointment.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is sending a near-term caution signal. Near-dated ATM IV is 133.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 22.7%, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (133.8% at the 1-week tenor collapsing to 27.0% at one month and 32.8% at six months). This pattern prices an imminent event-driven move (implied ±11% on the nearest expiry). The 1-week figure is likely distorted by a near-zero-day expiry; the more informative read is the 6-month tenor, where put/call skew has steepened to -4.7% and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.45 to 0.75 since early May as the stock fell 7.5%. Rising put demand and widening downside skew into a price decline indicate the options market shares the downside-asymmetry view embedded in my valuation score. Short interest is low at 2.5% of float (3.2 days to cover), so this is hedging and positioning, not a crowded bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$120, the high analyst target).** Comp momentum proves durable rather than transient: Pro-side share gains compound, ticket inflation holds without crushing traffic, and the aging-fleet/record-vehicle-age tailwind sustains mid-single-digit comps. Operating margin expands further on store-cost leverage, EPS compounds at low-double digits, and buybacks continue to shrink the share count ~3-4% annually. The premium multiple holds or expands modestly, supporting ~$115–120 over 18 months. This requires the +8.1% comp to mark a step-up, which estimate revisions do not yet confirm.

**Bear case (~$72, ~18% downside).** Tariff-driven ticket inflation normalizes and DIY traffic continues to decline, slowing comps back toward the low-single digits. The market re-rates the multiple toward the AutoZone/peer level of ~21x, which on roughly $3.40 of EPS implies the low-$70s. The negative-equity/Z-Score optics, while economically benign, could amplify selling pressure during a broader Consumer Discretionary drawdown, and the rich near-term options pricing suggests the market is alert to this risk.

The realistic base case sits near the current price: ~24x next-year EPS of $3.62 implies ~$87, with buyback accretion providing a modest tailwind over 12 months. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric, with neither the upside nor the downside that would justify a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on a growth-hold rationale: a high-quality compounder worth owning through cycles, but not a fresh-money BUY at the current multiple.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the monthly Reg-FD sales updates as interim data points on comp trends and DIY traffic.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision turn — current-year or next-year consensus EPS rising more than +3% over a 90-day window, confirming the comp acceleration is structural rather than a tariff-driven ticket bump.
- A pullback that compresses the forward multiple to ~22–23x (roughly $80 on next-year EPS), restoring a margin of safety relative to peers.
- Evidence that DIY transaction counts stabilize or turn positive while ticket holds, removing the traffic-erosion overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of comps decelerating below +3% with continued DIY traffic declines, signaling the ticket-driven comp has faded.
- A credit-rating downgrade that raises interest costs and threatens the $5.2B supplier-finance program terms.
- Operating-margin compression below ~17% from Pro-mix shift and wage/insurance inflation outpacing leverage.

## Conclusion

O'Reilly is a best-in-class business: a durable distribution moat, sector-leading margins, exceptional capital discipline (buybacks at an average ~$19 against a ~$92 stock), and standout management alignment (CEO comp ~$4.2M and a 2.2x CEO-to-NEO ratio at a $72B company, with TSR roughly doubling the peer group). The Q1 2026 comp acceleration is genuine evidence of pricing power and Pro-side momentum, and the aging-fleet tailwind is real.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the stock trades at a ~34% premium to the peer median while consensus estimates have stayed flat despite the strong quarter, the comp gain is increasingly price/ticket-driven against declining DIY traffic, and the options term structure prices a near-term downside event. A premium multiple with no estimate-revision turn and a comp whose durability depends on tariff pass-through is a payment for quality already made. I would become a buyer on either a revision turn (>3% upward over 90 days) or a pullback toward ~22–23x forward (~$80). At $88.40, hold the quality; do not add.
