**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AeroVironment operates as two distinct businesses inside one ticker. The first is a category-leading tactical drone and loitering-munition franchise (Switchblade, Puma, P550) that grew roughly 80% year-over-year in the June quarter, carries a $1.2B funded backlog, and rode Ukraine-validated demand and allied resupply to a record ~$642M quarterly revenue print. The second is BlueHalo, the all-stock acquisition (~17.4M shares issued) that closed May 1, 2025, tripled the company's size, and has so far delivered a $240.7M goodwill impairment on a cancelled space program, negative segment EBITDA, three inherited material weaknesses, a financial-statement restatement, and an active DoD cybersecurity investigation. The market's celebration of the Q4 beat (an 18-23% pop) priced the first business; the accumulating governance and legal problems in the second remain unpriced.

The core issue is whether the accounting, controls, and legal exposure are sound enough to underwrite; the drone franchise itself is real. On June 22, 2026, AeroVironment filed an Item 4.02 non-reliance notice: previously issued financials cannot be relied upon because of a newly identified material weakness in the goodwill-impairment analysis for the space unit, and the impairment was itself miscalculated. That filing coincided with an officer departure, sits atop an unremediated inherited material weakness (IT user-access controls), and runs alongside a February 2026 internal investigation into the accuracy of the company's data in the Supplier Performance Risk System — the fact pattern behind recent DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Act settlements. Multiple securities-fraud class actions over prior "SCAR $1 billion franchise" characterizations have a lead-plaintiff deadline of ~July 27, 2026.

We maintain AVOID. The stock has fallen ~45% over twelve months and trades at ~$149, well below the ~$232 consensus mean target, which will tempt value-oriented buyers. But the drawdown reflects genuine deterioration in earnings quality, control environment, and legal exposure that the trailing revenue growth masks. Estimate revisions are falling sharply (current-year EPS -19.8% over 90 days, current quarter -62.5% over 30 days), the space unit still carries ~$291M of goodwill vulnerable to further write-down, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal management conviction. We would revisit only after the restatement is filed, controls are remediated, and the SPRS investigation resolves without material penalty.

## Company Overview

AeroVironment is a diversified defense-technology prime, reorganized into two segments effective May 1, 2025:

- **AxS (Autonomous Systems)** — FY26 revenue $1.36B (69% of total), segment adjusted EBITDA $288.7M. Contains the legacy AV franchise (Switchblade loitering munitions, Puma/P550 small and medium UAS) plus BlueHalo's integrated air-and-missile-defense, electronic warfare, and unmanned underwater vehicles. This is the profitable engine.
- **SCDE (Space, Cyber & Directed Energy)** — FY26 revenue $618.8M (31%), entirely from BlueHalo. Posted **negative adjusted EBITDA of –$2.6M** in FY26 and is the source of most of the year's problems, including the cancelled SCAR space program.

Revenue is ~85% U.S. government (63% DoD directly) and ~28% international including foreign military sales. Fixed-price contracts are roughly 70% of revenue, which concentrates execution and cost-inflation risk on the company. The strategic thesis behind BlueHalo — moving AV from a small-drone specialist to a multi-domain prime spanning space, cyber, directed energy, and counter-UAS — is defensible given where defense demand is heading. The execution and accounting quality of that transition, in year one, have been poor.

## Financial Analysis

FY2026 (fiscal year ends April 30) was a transition year distorted by the acquisition and a large impairment:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $716.7M | $820.6M | $1,976.8M (+141%) |
| Gross margin | 40% | 39% | 25% |
| Operating income | $71.8M | $40.8M | –$311.0M |
| Net income | $59.7M | $43.6M | –$265.1M |
| Diluted shares | 27.3M | 28.2M | 49.1M |
| Operating cash flow | +$15.3M | –$1.3M | –$78.4M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | –$140.9M |

The margin story requires unpacking. The reported gross-margin collapse from 39% to 25% reflects three things: ~$92.7M of purchase-accounting intangible amortization flowing through COGS, a mix shift toward lower-margin BlueHalo cyber/mission services, and a shift toward lower-margin Switchblade production volume. Adjusted product gross margins (ex-amortization) run closer to 32-36%, so the underlying business is not as impaired as the headline suggests. The $240.7M goodwill impairment on the space reporting unit (triggered by the SCAR cancellation) drove the GAAP operating loss, and that charge was subsequently found to have been miscalculated.

Cash generation turned sharply negative. Operating cash flow of –$78.4M and free cash flow of –$141M reflect a working-capital build (inventory and unbilled receivables ramping to meet demand) layered on top of the loss. The charitable read is that this is demand-driven, cash-consumptive growth that reverses as backlog converts to billings. That read is only correct if the backlog is real and converts on schedule — a live question given how quickly SCAR's $1.49B of unfunded backlog evaporated.

The balance sheet has two faces. On the surface, leverage looks conservative: management drew a $700M term loan plus revolver to close BlueHalo, then in July 2025 raised ~$1.70B via a stock offering (4.06M shares at $248) and $747.5M of 0% convertible notes due 2030 (conversion price ~$322.40), retiring the term loan. That leaves only $747.5M of zero-coupon debt against $377M cash and a 4.3x current ratio. The refinancing was well-executed. But 60% of total assets ($3.4B of $5.7B) is goodwill and intangibles, free cash flow is deeply negative, and the Altman Z-score sits at 2.01 in the grey zone. The reported debt-to-equity of ~1.3 overstates cash leverage; the real financial risk is further intangible impairment (the space unit still carries ~$291M of goodwill) and continued cash burn through the capacity ramp, not near-term solvency.

**Earnings surprise track record** underscores the visibility problem: over the last four quarters AV posted one beat (Q4 FY26 +23.2%) and three misses (–9.3%, –44.6%, –15.1%). The Q4 beat is encouraging, but the pattern shows management's own guidance has repeatedly overshot delivery — relevant when weighing the FY27 revenue guide of $2.125B–$2.225B and the ≥15% organic-growth-to-end-of-decade target.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is genuine and broad-based. Drone warfare has been validated in Ukraine, counter-UAS demand is surging (RBC and other sell-side analysts flag it as potentially a larger long-term prize than strike drones), and allied defense budgets are elevated. Within this, AV's Switchblade franchise is a category leader with pull-through: YTD book-to-bill was 1.60x through Q3, and Switchblade task orders include a $186M award under a $990M IDIQ ("GENESIS"). AxS grew ~80% YoY in Q4. Funded backlog stands at $1.2B. The FY27 guide implies continued strong growth off the enlarged base.

The growth thesis has two problems. First, the estimate-revision trend is sharply negative, which contradicts the top-line momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 62.5% over 30 days and 60.6% over 90 days; current-year down 19.8%/20.1%; next-year down 16.3%/15.7%. When consensus is cutting earnings this fast while revenue guidance is strong, the market is signaling that the growth is coming at deteriorating margins and questionable earnings quality — the AxS-strong/SCDE-weak divergence AV reported. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision pattern that would support a growth-driven re-rating.

Second, the SCDE/BlueHalo half of the company is the growth disappointment, not the growth engine. Negative EBITDA, the SCAR cancellation, and the write-off of $1.49B of unfunded backlog show that a meaningful chunk of the acquired growth story did not materialize. Management's residual $3.0B unfunded-backlog figure (now ex-SCAR) deserves skepticism given how fast SCAR disappeared. We score growth prospects a 6.0 — the AxS franchise is real and the demand tailwind is durable and multi-confirmed, but the negative revision velocity and the SCDE drag prevent a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

With trailing EPS of –$5.40, trailing P/E is not meaningful. On forward consensus, current-year EPS of $3.24 puts the stock at ~46x and next-year EPS of $4.53 at ~33x — both against a peer median P/E of ~29.5x (Aerospace & Defense comparables: HXL 64.7, SARO 29.5, AIR 28.2, PSN 27.4). AV trades at a premium to that median on forward earnings that are themselves being cut.

The value-trap dynamic is the concern. At ~$149 against a ~$232 mean target and a ~$135 52-week low, the stock looks cheap versus where it traded and versus sell-side targets. But a compressed price sitting on falling estimates, unresolved control weaknesses, and open litigation is where "cheap" fails to work — the discount reflects structural impairment in earnings quality rather than a mispriced growth story. The consensus targets are stale; RBC's July 9 downgrade to Sector Perform (one day after management's long-term-target presentation) reflects growing skepticism that the aggressive goals are achievable, and the sharp estimate cuts suggest more target revisions downward are coming.

There is a plausible bull argument on normalized earnings: if BlueHalo integration stabilizes, SCDE turns EBITDA-positive, and Switchblade continues compounding, forward EPS power well above $4.53 could justify a materially higher price. But that requires clean resolution of the restatement, the SPRS investigation, and the litigation — none of which is dated or assured. We do not reward the cheapness because there is no independent catalyst that de-risks the earnings base; the next binary events (restatement filing, lead-plaintiff developments, potential further space-unit impairment) skew downside. Valuation score 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

AeroVironment's competitive position is strong in its core loitering-munition and counter-UAS categories, and the trajectory in AxS is positive — 80% Q4 growth and a 1.60x book-to-bill point to share gains, not erosion. The differentiator is a dominant installed base in tactical loitering munitions with real allied and U.S. resupply demand.

The offsets are real. Anduril Industries is a well-funded private disruptor that appears as a competitive threat across nearly every AV category — loitering munitions, counter-UAS/EW, and increasingly the broader multi-domain stack. Traditional primes (RTX, Lockheed, L3Harris, Boeing, BAE) and services players (Leidos, Booz Allen, CACI) compete in the BlueHalo-adjacent categories where AV is weakest. Fixed-price contracts are ~70% of revenue, so intensifying competition pressures the margins AV depends on. The SCAR cancellation is a direct competitive/programmatic loss in space. The China angle — sanctions (Jan 2024) and export-control listing (March 2025), plus rare-earth dependence for motors and batteries — is a structural supply-chain vulnerability. Net, the trajectory in the core franchise is improving while the acquired portfolio is uneven; we score competitive position 5.5, consistent with the recent decline in this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the reason for the AVOID, and it is dominated by specific, unresolved, largely binary items:

1. **Restatement and fresh material weakness.** The June 22, 2026 Item 4.02 non-reliance filing means prior financials cannot be relied upon, driven by a newly identified material weakness in the goodwill-impairment analysis for the space unit. This is a self-inflicted internal-control failure, paired with an officer departure the same day.

2. **DoD cybersecurity / SPRS investigation.** The February 2026 internal investigation (external counsel) into Legacy AV's compliance with DoD contract cybersecurity requirements and the accuracy of its SPRS data mirrors the fact pattern behind recent DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Act settlements. Outcome is unknown and could threaten new DoD awards, trigger penalties, or, in a tail scenario, debarment.

3. **Securities class actions.** Multiple firms have filed or are investigating suits over the SCAR "franchise" characterizations.

4. **Inherited material weaknesses.** One of BlueHalo's three disclosed material weaknesses (IT user-access controls) remains unremediated as of 4/30/2026, compounding item 1.

5. **Goodwill overhang.** $2.49B of goodwill remains, with the space unit carrying ~$291M — vulnerable to further impairment if SCDE continues to underperform.

6. **Governance signals.** Both Arlington Capital Partners board designees resigned effective June 17, 2026 with no replacements named, even as Arlington retains ~26.3% ownership — an engaged, informed holder disengaging from oversight as control problems surface. A cluster of Item 5.02 executive departures across February–June 2026 adds to the concern.

7. **Concentration and budget risk.** 85% U.S. government / 63% DoD, with explicit exposure to DOGE-driven spending reviews, FMS stop-work risk, and continuing-resolution/shutdown risk.

Almost none of these is priced by the market that celebrated the Q4 print. We score risk factors 3.0 — the confluence of a restatement, an FCA-adjacent investigation, active securities litigation, and further impairment risk is materially more severe than typical sector risk, and much of it could move the stock on discrete, near-term dates.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress without a strong directional short bet. Near-term ATM IV of 81.5% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 73.4% (rich by ~8 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (81.5% near-term vs 75.5% long-term), consistent with the market pricing imminent event risk (litigation deadline, potential restatement filing). The put/call skew is call-heavy (–12.7%) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.51, which does not corroborate an aggressive bearish tilt — though the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.45 to 1.12 since June 30, suggesting building hedging demand. IV has compressed from 97.3% at the June 30 earnings event to 81.5% now, so some post-earnings uncertainty has resolved. The signal is elevated but two-sided: the market anticipates volatility but is not uniformly positioned short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** AV's core AxS franchise is a category-leading loitering-munition and counter-UAS business riding a durable, multi-confirmed defense-demand tailwind. Q4 revenue of ~$642M (+133% YoY), 80% AxS growth, a 1.60x book-to-bill, and a $1.2B funded backlog show real momentum. The balance sheet is cleverly de-levered (only $747.5M of zero-coupon converts, $377M cash). Negative free cash flow is a demand-driven working-capital build that reverses as backlog converts. If BlueHalo stabilizes, SCDE turns EBITDA-positive, and the litigation/investigation resolve without material penalty, normalized EPS power well above the $4.53 next-year consensus justifies a return toward the ~$232 mean target and potentially the ~$326 high, a 55-118% gain from ~$149.

**Bear Case.** The market bought the record revenue and ignored a restatement, a fresh material weakness, an unremediated inherited weakness, an active DoD cyber-compliance/SPRS investigation with FCA exposure, and multiple securities-fraud class actions. Estimates are being cut across every window. SCDE runs negative EBITDA, ~$291M of space-unit goodwill remains at risk, and management's backlog credibility is damaged by SCAR's rapid collapse. FCF is deeply negative during a capacity ramp. If the SPRS investigation produces penalties or threatens awards, if the restatement reveals further problems, or if SCDE impairs again, the stock revisits the low-$130s and potentially lower, a 12-25%+ decline with a long tail if the FCA outcome is adverse.

The bull and bear cases coexist inside the same reporting cycle. That tension is unresolved and skewed toward unpriced downside, which is why we avoid rather than buy the pullback.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** — we would reconsider a constructive stance if:
- The restatement is filed and audited without revealing additional material problems, and the newly identified goodwill-analysis material weakness plus the unremediated BlueHalo IT-access weakness are remediated with a clean auditor attestation.
- The February 2026 SPRS/DoD cybersecurity investigation resolves without material financial penalty, award suspension, or debarment risk.
- SCDE demonstrates positive segment adjusted EBITDA for at least one quarter, evidencing BlueHalo integration is working rather than continuing to impair.
- Estimate-revision momentum turns positive (current-year EPS revisions flip from –20% toward flat/positive) and the securities-litigation exposure is bounded.
- A pullback that prices the tail risk — mid-to-low $120s or below on continued forward EPS above ~$4.50 — would improve the risk/reward independent of catalyst resolution.

**Timeline** — the near-term overhang (lead-plaintiff deadline ~July 27, 2026; restatement filing; potential further impairment) resolves over the next one to three quarters. We expect the governance and control cleanup to take at least two to three quarters, and the SPRS/FCA matter potentially longer. The next natural reassessment is the restatement filing and the first post-restatement quarterly report.

**What would deepen the AVOID / trigger a short reassessment:** an adverse SPRS/FCA finding, a second space-unit impairment, a covenant or refinancing complication, or a further wave of executive departures.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There are several potential negative catalysts — the securities-litigation lead-plaintiff deadline, the pending restatement filing, and the open-ended SPRS/DoD cybersecurity investigation — but none is a cleanly dated event with a quantifiable, near-term financial impact. The restatement is a control/carrying-value correction, not necessarily a cash-earnings hit. The SPRS investigation has no stated resolution date. The strongest fundamental catalyst against the stock is the continued negative estimate-revision cycle and the risk of a further space-unit impairment, neither of which has a fixed date.

**Timing window.** The most impactful items (SPRS resolution, litigation progression) are multi-quarter, not multi-week. This works against a short given carry costs.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 10.7% of float with only 2.3 days to cover — elevated but not extreme, and thin days-to-cover means limited squeeze fuel. The stock demonstrated its squeeze capacity on June 30, gapping ~18-23% on the Q4 beat. A single strong print can inflict large mark-to-market pain on shorts.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is not confirming an aggressive bearish thesis: skew is call-heavy, put/call volume is 0.51, and IV has compressed since the earnings event. Near-term IV is rich and the term structure is in backwardation, so carrying a short through the event window is expensive.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear outcome takes the stock to the low-$130s (–12%) with a longer tail toward $100 if an FCA finding is adverse. Upside risk is a re-rating toward the $232 mean target on continued AxS momentum (+55%) or a squeeze on any positive investigation/litigation resolution. With upside risk of 50%+ against a base bear downside of ~12-25%, the asymmetry does not clear a 2:1 short threshold — the genuine growth franchise and demonstrated squeeze capacity cut against shorting even a legitimately troubled name.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is "troubled company with unpriced tail risks," not "actively collapsing with a dated catalyst." The real drone franchise, strong bookings, proven ability to gap up on beats, and the absence of a timed, quantifiable negative catalyst make this a stock to avoid owning, not to short.

## Conclusion

AeroVironment is a legitimate defense-growth franchise wrapped around an acquisition that has, in its first year, produced a GAAP loss, a $240.7M miscalculated goodwill impairment, a financial-statement restatement, an unremediated inherited material weakness plus a newly identified one, an active DoD cybersecurity/SPRS investigation with False Claims Act exposure, multiple securities-fraud class actions, negative free cash flow, and the resignation of both large-holder board designees. The market has priced the record Q4 revenue and the strong FY27 guide; it has not priced the accumulating control, governance, and legal problems.

The ~45% twelve-month decline and the gap to consensus targets will attract value buyers, but this is a case where cheapness is a warning rather than an opportunity — the discount reflects deteriorating earnings quality and unresolved binary risk, not a mispriced compounder. Estimate revisions are falling across every window, the space unit carries further impairment risk, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal conviction. We rate AVAV **AVOID** (overall 4.5), consistent with the declining score trajectory, and would revisit only after the restatement is cleanly filed, the control weaknesses are remediated, and the SPRS investigation resolves without material penalty.
