## Executive Summary

Adtalem Global Education is the largest healthcare-focused educator in the United States, serving 90,000+ students across Chamberlain (nursing), Walden (online graduate), and three Caribbean medical/veterinary schools. The FY2025 numbers describe a business in acceleration: revenue grew 12.9% to $1,788.3M, GAAP operating margin expanded 540bps to 19.1%, diluted EPS rose 82.3% to $6.18, adjusted EPS rose 33.1% to $6.67, and free cash flow reached $283.4M (~15.8% margin) with operating cash flow exceeding net income. Management has since raised FY2026 adjusted EPS growth guidance to 17-20% on the tenth consecutive quarter of enrollment growth and Walden enrollment up 13% in the most recent quarter.

On roughly $7.80-$8.00 of adjusted EPS for FY2026, and using the ~$85-90 area implied by the FY25 buyback price and post-run trading, the stock trades around 11-12x forward earnings. That is a low-teens multiple on a business compounding earnings at high-teens-to-low-20s percent, a PEG comfortably below 1.0. The margin expansion is partly structural (operating leverage on Walden's online model) and partly mechanical (Walden's student-relationship intangible fully amortized after FY2024, removing ~$24M of annual amortization and converging GAAP toward adjusted earnings). Capital allocation is shareholder-aligned: the share count has fallen ~20% since FY2022, debt has been actively paid down, and a $150M buyback authorization runs through May 2028.

The stock trades at a value multiple despite compounder characteristics because of regulation. Adtalem derives ~77% of revenue from Title IV federal funding, all five institutions operate under provisional certification with a depressed (Walden-acquisition-driven) composite score, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, enacted July 2025) phases out Grad PLUS loans and introduces "Do No Harm" earnings tests. That risk is real and partly binary, and it is two-sided: OBBBA applies the same rules to all Title IV institutions, removing the proprietary-school singling-out (90/10, Gainful Employment) that historically disadvantaged Adtalem, and the company is actively lining up private financing partners to backfill Grad PLUS. On the balance of evidence — durable growth, raised guidance, a low-teens multiple, and a regulatory outcome that skews neutral-to-favorable — the asymmetry favors ownership. I rate ATGE a BUY with sizing discipline appropriate to the regulatory binary.

## Company Overview

Adtalem operates five "like-kind" healthcare-credentialing institutions across three reportable segments (fiscal year ends June 30):

- **Chamberlain University** — FY25 revenue $725.8M (+14.6%). The largest pre-licensure nursing program in the U.S. by enrollment, with the largest BSN, MSN, FNP, and DNP programs nationally. 23 campuses across 15 states plus online BSN in 36 states. Growth is led by undergraduate BSN and MSN enrollment.
- **Walden University** — FY25 revenue $693.4M (+16.5%). A fully online university for working adults with 100+ degree programs, acquired from Laureate in 2021. A leading conferrer of doctoral degrees in nursing, public health, education, and psychology. The fastest-growing and highest-margin segment (adjusted operating margin 26.5%, up from 21.9%).
- **Medical & Veterinary** — FY25 revenue $369.1M (+3.7%). AUC (St. Maarten), RUSM (Barbados), and RUSVM (St. Kitts), offshore schools serving primarily U.S./Canadian students who place into U.S. clinical rotations and residencies. First-time residency match rates remain high (RUSM 96%, AUC 95% in 2025), though both ticked down 2-3 points from 2024.

The competitive moat rests on scale plus accreditation depth in a market with structural supply/demand imbalance. Traditional 4-year and community-college nursing programs are capacity-constrained, generating persistent excess demand that Chamberlain captures. The Caribbean schools serve U.S. students unable to secure domestic MD/DO/vet seats.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 income statement shows an accelerating, high-quality business:

- **Revenue $1,788.3M, +12.9%** — growth across all three segments, driven by enrollment, 2-4.3% tuition increases, and higher credit hours.
- **GAAP operating income $341.5M (+57.4%); operating margin 19.1%** (from 13.7%). Adjusted operating margin 20.7%.
- **Net income $237.1M (+73.3%); diluted EPS $6.18 (+82.3%).** Adjusted EPS $6.67 (+33.1%).
- **Adjusted EBITDA $459.7M, margin 25.7%** (from 23.8%).
- **Operating cash flow $333.7M; FCF $283.4M; FCF margin ~15.8%.** OCF > net income signals clean accruals.

The margin expansion is durable for two reasons. First, operating leverage on the fixed-cost online model, most visible at Walden where adjusted operating margin jumped ~460bps. Second, a multi-year roll-off of acquisition-related drags: the Walden student-relationship intangible fully amortized at June 2024 (~$24M annual reduction), litigation reserves declined, and business-integration expense ended. That second factor means GAAP earnings should keep converging toward adjusted figures even without further operating improvement, a quality-of-earnings tailwind.

**Balance sheet and capital structure.** Total assets $2,752M, of which goodwill ($961M) plus intangibles ($765M) equal ~63%, a Walden-acquisition legacy. The FY25 quantitative goodwill test showed the RUSM reporting unit fair value exceeding carrying value by ~165%, a comfortable cushion. Debt totals ~$558M ($405M of 5.50% Senior Secured Notes due 2028 plus $153.3M Term Loan B due 2028); D/E is 0.92 with ~$200M cash, so net leverage is modest. Deleveraging is underway: a $100M Term Loan B prepayment in January 2025, net debt repayments of $100M (FY25) and $50M (FY24), and the Term Loan B no longer requires quarterly amortization. Interest expense fell to $52.3M from $63.7M. The revolver was upsized from $400M to $500M in August 2025, partly as a buffer for ED letter-of-credit requirements.

Two screening metrics look weaker than the operating quality warrants. The Altman Z of 2.4 (grey zone) and current ratio of 0.82 are distorted by the goodwill/intangible-heavy asset base and by education-sector working-capital dynamics (tuition collected up front creates large deferred-revenue balances). These are balance-sheet-composition artifacts, not leverage or liquidity distress signals. The Piotroski 4/5 partial-signal read confirms underlying strength.

**Capital allocation.** Repurchased 2.32M shares at an average $91.21 (~$213M) in FY25; diluted shares fell to 38.3M from 40.3M, and from ~48M in 2022. A 15th repurchase program ($150M through May 2028) was authorized in May 2025. No dividend. Capex is modest at ~$50M (IT and Chamberlain campus development), so FCF is largely returnable.

I score Financial Health 7.0: strong, growing free cash flow with OCF>NI, modest and declining leverage, and disciplined capital return, lightly capped by the goodwill-heavy asset base and the Title IV letter-of-credit obligations that sit against the revolver.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth signals are aligned. First, the raised FY2026 adjusted EPS growth guidance of 17-20% (reiterating revenue guidance of 6-8.5%, ~$1.92B) is the company-level analog of a sharp upward estimate revision. Second, the tenth consecutive quarter of enrollment growth, with Walden up 13% in the most recent quarter, confirms the demand backdrop is intact and broad. Third, the FY2025 margin trajectory (540bps of GAAP operating margin expansion) shows the growth is converting to earnings at an accelerating rate, not being competed away.

The structural demand drivers are durable: persistent U.S. shortages of nurses, physicians, and veterinarians; capacity constraints at traditional nursing programs that Chamberlain exploits; and growing acceptance of online graduate education that benefits Walden. The combination of low-double-digit revenue growth, expanding margins, and a shrinking share count produces high-teens-to-low-20s EPS growth without heroic assumptions.

The one segment to watch is Medical & Veterinary: +3.7% revenue, flat enrollment, and declining segment adjusted operating income (-3.2%). M&V is the most exposed to OBBBA's professional-loan caps and sits closest to the 90/10 cliff. It represents ~21% of revenue, so weakness there is a drag, not a thesis-breaker, but it is where negative surprises are most likely.

I score Growth Prospects 7.5. This is the dimension I weight most heavily, and the evidence — raised guidance, ten quarters of enrollment growth, margin expansion, and a broad-based structural tailwind — is among the cleaner growth profiles in the dataset.

## Competitive Landscape

Adtalem's position is improving within its niche. Chamberlain holds the #1 position in U.S. pre-licensure nursing and the largest BSN/MSN/FNP/DNP programs, capturing demand that capacity-constrained public and nonprofit programs cannot serve. Walden is the fastest-growing, highest-margin segment and a leading conferrer of doctoral degrees in healthcare-adjacent fields. The healthcare-credential focus differentiates Adtalem from broad for-profit peers and underpins pricing power and the high residency/licensure outcomes (RUSM 96%, AUC 95% match rates) that form the "value" defense under any Do No Harm / Gainful Employment outcomes regime.

Headwinds are real but slower-moving: a shrinking traditional college-age population, intensifying competition from public/nonprofit institutions moving online (often at subsidized, lower tuition), and affordability pressure. These cap the long-run growth ceiling more than they threaten the next several years. The Caribbean schools face the most acute competitive pressure — they compete for U.S. students against domestic MD/DO programs and other offshore schools, with declining segment profit and clinical-placement dependence.

I score Competitive Position 7.0: a widening lead in nursing and online graduate healthcare education against a structural shortage backdrop, tempered by the weaker M&V franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The risk cluster is concentrated in regulation and Title IV dependence, and it is the reason this report stops short of a higher conviction score.

1. **OBBBA (enacted July 2025).** The Grad PLUS phase-out (existing borrowers grandfathered through June 30, 2026 plus remaining program length or 3 years) is a real funding headwind for Walden, Chamberlain graduate, and the Caribbean programs. New federal loan caps ($100K graduate aggregate, $200K professional, $257.5K lifetime) could constrain higher-cost medical programs. The "Do No Harm" earnings provisions can strip Title IV eligibility if completer earnings don't exceed those of a HS grad (undergrad) or bachelor's grad (graduate) for 2 of 3 years; implementing regulations are not yet written. The offsetting positives are material: a higher professional unsubsidized loan limit ($20.5K → $50K), the level-playing-field provision that ends the proprietary-school-specific penalties, and management's disclosed effort to replace Grad PLUS with private financing partners. The net competitive effect plausibly skews neutral-to-favorable, but the implementation is uncertain and partly binary.

2. **Provisional certification / composite score.** The ED composite score fell to 0.2 in FY2022 on the Walden acquisition, below the 1.5 threshold; all five institutions operate under provisional certification with heightened cash monitoring, backed by $179M in letters of credit and $67.3M in state surety bonds. The cross-default/joint-liability structure — ED can hold one institution liable for another, and a parent or subsidiary bankruptcy triggers immediate portfolio-wide Title IV loss — is the genuine tail risk. This is a known, acquisition-driven artifact rather than operating distress, but it is real.

3. **Revenue concentration.** ~77% of revenue is Title IV-derived. 90/10 rates (FY24): Chamberlain 68%, Walden 82%, AUC 87%, RUSM 87%, RUSVM 78%. The two medical schools sit ~3 points from the 90% eligibility cliff with limited headroom.

4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $1.73B combined (63% of assets); a sustained downturn or reporting-unit forecast cut could force a write-off, though the RUSM cushion (~165% above carrying value) is comfortable.

5. **Caribbean structural risks.** Clinical-rotation affiliation dependence (some states restrict offshore placements), hurricane exposure, and potential legislation limiting U.S. enrollment in foreign medical schools. M&V is the only segment with flat enrollment and declining profit.

I score Risk Factors 5.0. The setup has specific, partly binary regulatory catalysts that are not fully priced in either direction, which precludes a high score; but the dominant risk (OBBBA) skews two-sided rather than clearly negative, and the most alarming headline metrics (composite score, current ratio) are artifacts with disclosed mitigants, which keeps it out of the low band.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Adtalem compounds revenue at low-double-digits, sustains the structural margin expansion (operating leverage plus the acquisition-amortization roll-off), and grows EPS at high-teens-to-low-20s with continued buybacks shrinking the share count. OBBBA's level-playing-field provision proves a net competitive positive, private financing partners successfully backfill Grad PLUS, and the multiple re-rates from a low-teens forward level toward the high-teens that a debt-light healthcare compounder warrants. On ~$8.00 FY26 adjusted EPS and an 18-20x multiple, that supports a price in the $145-$160 range, roughly 70-90% above the ~$85-90 area.

**Bear case.** "Do No Harm" or FVT/GE implementing regulations land harder than expected; one or both Caribbean schools breach the 90% cliff or lose clinical placements; private financing fails to fully replace Grad PLUS, depressing graduate/medical enrollment; and a regulatory action against any single institution triggers cross-default concerns. A multiple de-rating toward 9-10x on flat-to-down earnings would imply a price in the $55-$65 area, roughly 25-35% below the ~$85-90 area.

**Base case.** Growth moderates toward the guided low-double-digit revenue / high-teens EPS path, the regulatory overhang persists but does not produce a segment-level eligibility loss, and the multiple re-rates modestly as private-financing backfill is demonstrated. A 16x multiple on ~$8.00 adjusted EPS supports a ~$128 base-case target.

## Conclusion

Adtalem is a high-quality, broadly-tailwinded education compounder priced as if its growth has stalled. The operating evidence — 12.9% revenue growth, 540bps of GAAP operating margin expansion, 82.3% diluted EPS growth, ~16% FCF margin with OCF>NI, a ~20% lower share count since FY2022, and a raised FY2026 adjusted EPS growth guide of 17-20% on the tenth straight quarter of enrollment growth — describes an acceleration, and the ~11-12x forward multiple (PEG well below 1.0) sits comfortably inside the criteria for a valid inflection BUY. The regulatory overhang is real and partly binary, but on the analyst evidence it skews two-sided to net-favorable (OBBBA's level-playing-field provision, high licensure/residency outcomes as a "Do No Harm" defense, disclosed private-financing backfill), while the most alarming headline risk metrics (0.2 composite score, 0.82 current ratio) are acquisition- and sector-driven artifacts with disclosed mitigants and a $500M revolver buffer. The setup hedges a regulatory binary that the evidence suggests is less one-sided than the discount implies, against a durable compounder trading at a value multiple. I rate ATGE a **BUY** with sizing discipline calibrated to the genuine regulatory tail — overall score 6.8.
