## Executive Summary

CIBC has had two exceptionally good years. Reported net income rose 18.2% in FY2025 to C$8,454M, adjusted diluted EPS rose 16.4% to C$8.61, the reported efficiency ratio improved 200bp to 54.4% and then to 52.0% in H1 FY2026, adjusted ROE reached 16.4% in Q2 FY2026, and the bank raised its quarterly dividend 10.3% to C$1.07 while quadrupling buybacks to roughly C$1.73B. The franchise is sound: Canadian Personal & Business Banking earned a 25.3% segment ROE at a 50.6% efficiency ratio in Q4 FY2025, and Capital Markets delivered a 20.1% segment ROE at 46.6% efficiency with 14.2% share among its strategic and focus clients in Canada.

The problem is the source of the earnings and the price now attached to them. Decomposing FY2025's net interest income shows interest income *fell* 6.6% (C$52,185M to C$48,761M) while interest expense fell 14.3% (C$38,490M to C$32,992M), of which deposit interest expense alone fell C$5,366M. The two changes net to +C$2,074M, exactly the reported NII increase. Every dollar of FY2025 NII growth came from the Bank of Canada easing cycle repricing an C$808B deposit base faster than a C$590B loan book. That mechanism is finite, and the first evidence of exhaustion is on the tape: NIM excluding trading was 2.05% in Q2 FY2026 versus 2.06% in Q1, the first sequential decline in the observable series after 1.86% → 1.94% → 2.00% → 2.06%. Q1 FY2026 reported earnings contain a C$422M (C$0.45/share) tax recovery, so trailing reported EPS of C$10.09 overstates the adjusted figure of C$9.67 by 4.3%.

Against decelerating, lower-quality earnings drivers, the multiple has done the opposite. CIBC-reported market-to-book went 1.53x (FY2024 year-end) to 1.86x (FY2025 year-end) and now sits near 2.5x on a rolled-forward book value of roughly C$65-66. Trailing P/E has gone 9.46x → 11.97x → 13.56x → ~16.1x reported (~16.8x adjusted). Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of C$10.28 implies essentially flat sequential earnings for the two remaining quarters (H1 delivered C$5.30 adjusted; the C$2.50 and C$2.54 quarterly estimates sum to C$5.04), and FY2027 at C$11.13 implies +8.3%. At US$118.70 the stock trades above the consensus mean target (US$111.45) and median (US$115.06), 2.0% below the high (US$121.06), with the analyst distribution degrading: strong buys have fallen from 4 to 2 over three months while holds rose from 5 to 7. Expected total return from here is roughly flat to low single digit against a bear case near US$92. The rating is a trim.

## Company Overview

CIBC is one of Canada's Big Six banks, serving approximately 15 million clients across Canada, the United States and internationally. It reports five segments. FY2025 revenue of C$29,133M (+13.8%) and reported net income of C$8,454M (+18.2%) broke down as follows:

| Segment | FY25 Revenue | FY25 Net Income | FY24 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canadian Personal & Business Banking | 12,031 | 3,107 | 2,905 | +7% |
| Canadian Commercial Banking & Wealth | 6,902 | 2,341 | 2,063 | +13% |
| U.S. Commercial Banking & Wealth | 3,216 | 958 | 500 | +92% |
| Capital Markets | 6,148 | 2,273 | 1,629 | +40% |
| Corporate & Other | 836 | (net loss) | — | — |
| **Total** | **29,133** | **8,454** | **7,154** | **+18%** |

The balance sheet at October 31, 2025 carried total assets of C$1,116,938M, net loans and acceptances of C$589,504M, deposits of C$808,124M, common equity of C$57,760M and risk-weighted assets of C$357,803M. Loan mix shifted deliberately toward commercial: business and government loans grew 10.8% to C$237,416M (40.0% of gross loans, up from 38.1%) while residential mortgages grew 2.3% to C$287,033M (48.3% of gross loans, down from 49.9%).

One reported metric requires care. CIBC discloses assets under administration of C$3,998,199M, but footnote disclosure specifies that this includes C$3,117.4B representing the full contract amount under a 50/50 joint venture with BNY Mellon. Non-JV AUA is therefore roughly C$881B. AUM of C$430,982M (+12.4%) is the better proxy, and CIBC attributes the increase substantially to market appreciation rather than net new assets.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025 and the funding-cost mechanism

The NII decomposition above defines the durability of everything downstream. Interest income declined as asset yields reset lower; interest expense declined more because deposits repriced faster and further. A rate-driven liability tailwind of this shape produces high reported growth for two to six quarters and then stops.

An honest counter-reading exists. Canadian P&BB NIM rose 33bp to 3.02%, and management attributes this to "higher deposit and loan margins, and a favourable business mix," so some portion is deposit-pricing discipline and mix rather than pure rate mechanics. Both readings fit the data; the rate-cycle component is clearly the larger one, and the sequential NIM flattening in Q2 FY2026 is consistent with the tailwind exhausting rather than compounding.

### Q2 FY2026

| Metric | Q2/26 | Q2/25 | YoY | Q1/26 | QoQ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 8,006 | 7,022 | +14% | 8,398 | −5% |
| Adjusted net income | 2,471 | 2,016 | +23% | 2,685 | −8% |
| Adjusted PPPT earnings | 3,815 | 3,214 | +19% | 4,079 | −6% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | 2.54 | 2.05 | +24% | 2.76 | −8% |
| Adjusted ROE | 16.4% | 13.9% | — | 17.4% | — |
| CET1 | 13.6% | 13.4% | — | 13.4% | — |
| PCL | 605 | 605 | flat | 568 | +6.5% |

Adjusted pre-provision pre-tax earnings +19% year over year is the cleanest evidence of genuine operating improvement, because it is pre-credit and therefore not a reserve artifact. The sequential column matters as much: revenue −5%, adjusted PPPT −6%, adjusted EPS −8%. Consensus for the next two quarters (C$2.50, C$2.54) implies no recovery of the Q1 peak. H1 FY2026 efficiency of 52.0% (8,528/16,404) is a real improvement over 53.8% in H1 FY2025.

### The credit rotation inside a flat provision line

Consolidated PCL was C$605M in both Q2 FY2026 and Q2 FY2025. The composition did not hold still:

| Segment PCL | Q2/26 | Q2/25 | H1/26 | H1/25 | H1 change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canadian P&BB | 474 | 389 | 920 | 817 | +13% |
| Canadian Commercial & Wealth | 121 | 54 | 205 | 93 | **+120%** |
| U.S. Commercial & Wealth | 21 | 123 | 42 | 191 | **−78%** |
| Capital Markets | (15) | 34 | (8) | 55 | reversal |
| **Total** | **605** | **605** | **1,173** | **1,178** | **−0.4%** |

Canadian commercial provisions more than doubled on a half-year basis while U.S. provisions fell 78% and Capital Markets swung to a reversal. Releases of previously built reserves are finite; credit normalization in a 40%-commercial loan book is not. Two mitigants are legitimate: the Canadian increase partly reflects 10.8% growth in business and government loans, and CIBC raised allowance coverage to 0.739% of gross loans from 0.697% (allowance +12.1% against loans +5.6%), a mildly conservative posture. This is a monitorable, not yet a problem; the stability of the headline is a coincidence of offsetting moves.

### Capital and capital return

CET1 13.6% at April 30, 2026 (13.3% at October 31, 2025), with a further +24bp expected on the Caribbean close. Tier 1 15.1%, total capital 17.4%, leverage 4.3%, LCR 131-132%, NSFR 116%, TLAC 31.9%. Average VaR of C$11.4M against C$6.1B of Capital Markets revenue indicates the trading upcycle was fee-, financing- and flow-led rather than directional, though VaR is a one-day normal-market measure and does not settle the question.

Capital return accelerated: approximately C$1,731M of buybacks in FY2025 versus C$419M in FY2024; share count fell from 942.3M to 926.6M and, on the market-cap-to-price arithmetic, to roughly 913M by August 2026 (a further ~1.4%). Reported payout ratio fell to 45.0% from 49.4%; the C$4.28 annualized dividend against consensus FY2026 EPS of C$10.28 is a 41.6% payout, leaving room for further increases.

Two offsets deserve mention. Preferred and other-equity distributions rose 38% to C$364M in FY2025 and 46% to C$114M in Q2 FY2026, as the non-common capital balance grew 28.8% to C$6,369M; more pre-tax income is being routed away from common holders, so common EPS growth will lag net income growth. And buying back stock at roughly 2.5x book is a materially different capital-allocation decision than doing so at 1.5x book; the acceleration coincides with the highest valuation in a decade, which may reflect a shortage of attractive redeployment at 13.6% CET1 rather than a view that the shares are cheap.

**Financial health: 7.5.** Capital, liquidity and coverage ratios are all comfortably above requirements and improving; earnings quality is good on a pre-provision basis; the deductions are the rising non-common capital cost, the mortgage and commercial concentration, and the fact that the headline provision stability is compositional.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is the weakest part of the case, and the evidence is internally consistent across three independent sources.

**Consensus embeds no near-term momentum.** Current-year adjusted EPS of C$10.28 against H1 actual of C$5.30 implies C$4.98 for H2, below H1. FY2027 at C$11.13 is +8.3%. Thirty-day revisions are −0.2% (current quarter), −0.1% (current year), +0.1% (next year); ninety-day revisions are +0.6% and +0.4%. Estimates are stable, which removes the strongest forward indicator from the bull column. There is no estimate-revision inflection here.

**The delivered growth driver has plateaued.** NIM ex-trading 2.06% to 2.05% sequentially. Future NII growth must come from volume and mix, both slower and lower-quality than a repricing tailwind.

**Segment mix is unhelpful.** The 92% FY2025 net income growth in U.S. Commercial came with only +11% adjusted PPPT (C$1,235M to C$1,373M); in Q2 FY2026, net income +56% but adjusted PPPT +10% in USD. The recovery is provisions. Capital Markets grew segment revenue 28% to C$6,148M in a sector-wide dealer upcycle; extrapolating that is the single largest forecasting error available in Canadian bank models.

**Track record is good but modest in magnitude.** Four consecutive beats: +4.8%, +3.0%, +11.7%, +1.3%. The 11.7% Q1 beat was the quarter containing the tax recovery; the most recent beat was 1.3%. The surprise magnitude is compressing.

**Growth prospects: 5.5.** Mid-to-high single-digit forward EPS growth on stable estimates, with the largest recent driver arithmetically finite and the second-largest cyclical.

## Valuation Assessment

Because the NYSE listing is the same common share as the TSX line, the USD price and CAD fundamentals must be bridged. Using the reconcilable inputs (trailing P/E of 16.0x on TTM reported diluted EPS of C$10.09) the implied CAD price is ~C$162.6, and C$162.6 / US$118.70 gives an implied FX of ~1.37, consistent with the quoted 2.5% dividend yield against a C$4.28 annualized dividend. I note one inconsistency in the supplied data: the quoted EPS field of $6.11 does not reconcile with the quoted 16.0x P/E at US$118.70 (which implies US$7.42, or C$10.16 at 1.37, essentially the reported TTM figure). I use the reconstructible basis. The frozen snapshot also flags a market-date mismatch between the official close and daily history; both carry the same US$118.70 price, so it is immaterial.

| Multiple | Now | FY2025 y/e | FY2024 y/e | FY2023 y/e |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P/E, reported TTM EPS | ~16.1x | 13.56x | 11.97x | 9.46x |
| P/E, **adjusted** TTM (C$9.67) | **~16.8x** | — | — | — |
| P/E, FY2026E (C$10.28) | ~15.8x | — | — | — |
| P/E, FY2027E (C$11.13) | ~14.6x | — | — | — |
| Price / book | **~2.5x** | 1.86x | 1.53x | — |
| Dividend yield | ~2.5-2.6% | 3.3% | 4.1% | ~5.0% |

Book value: rolling C$62.33 BVPS at October 31, 2025 forward by roughly C$3.60/share of retained H1 earnings (C$5.74 reported less C$2.14 dividends) net of buyback effects gives ~C$65-66, or US$47.5-48.2, a P/B of approximately 2.45-2.50x. Backing average common equity out of the Q2 adjusted ROE of 16.4% produces a similar answer.

The re-rating is not baseless. A bank sustainably earning 16-17% adjusted ROE with a 52% efficiency ratio deserves a higher multiple than one earning 13.4% at 56.4%. But the multiple has expanded from 1.53x to ~2.5x book (a 63% re-rating) on ROE that has expanded from 13.7% to 16.4% adjusted, or 20%. The multiple has run roughly three times as far as the return metric it capitalizes, and the incremental return improvement was funded by a mechanism that has stopped working.

Three independent checks say the price is full rather than cheap:

1. **Consensus targets sit below spot.** Mean US$111.45 (−6.1% from US$118.70), median US$115.06 (−3.1%), high US$121.06 (+2.0%), low US$98.24 (−17.4%). On a stock with flat estimate revisions and decelerating sequential earnings, analyst targets are the mid-point of a distribution that already reflects the reported results through Q2 FY2026, not a stale floor waiting to be revised up.
2. **Rating sentiment is degrading.** Strong buys 4 → 4 → 3 → 2 over three months; holds 5 → 6 → 6 → 7; one persistent strong sell.
3. **Trailing multiples flatter.** Reported TTM EPS is 4.3% above adjusted, so screens showing ~16.1x are really looking at ~16.8x.

**Valuation: 3.8.** The multiple is at a decade high against a decelerating earnings driver, with no identified catalyst for upward re-rating and no margin of safety. A low absolute P/E for a bank would not by itself earn a high score; a high P/E relative to the bank's own history, its peer group's historical range, and its forward growth rate earns a low one.

## Competitive Landscape

Canada's Big Six operate under OSFI in a concentrated market where a C$808B deposit base, 13.6% CET1 and 132% LCR are structurally non-replicable by new entrants. Within that structure, CIBC's positioning is mixed on trajectory:

**Improving.** Capital Markets at 46.6% efficiency and 20.1% segment ROE (from 14.9%) with 14.2% share among strategic and focus clients is punching above its balance-sheet weight. Canadian P&BB at 25.3% ROE and 50.6% efficiency with record net promoter scores in Personal Banking and Imperial Service is the profit engine. Consolidated efficiency has improved 56.4% → 54.4% → 52.0% across three observable periods.

**Deteriorating.** U.S. Commercial Banking & Wealth consumes C$11.2B of allocated common equity, the second-largest allocation, for a 9.7% Q4 FY2025 segment ROE, with the efficiency ratio worsening to 61.8% from 56.7% and USD operating leverage of −9.8%. Roughly a fifth of allocated common equity is plausibly earning below cost of equity. Branch-closure costs are investment rather than decay, and a 21bp NIM expansion to 3.84% is genuine, but +10-11% PPPT growth says the improvement has not reached the operating line.

**Structural threat.** CIBC lists Canadian open banking and payments reform explicitly. Both attack the deposit-and-payments franchise that generated all of FY2025's NII growth.

**Competitive position: 5.8.** Domestic franchise trajectory is positive; the U.S. platform is a capital-efficiency drag; the sector-wide nature of both the funding tailwind and the dealer upcycle means little of the recent outperformance is CIBC-specific.

## Risk Assessment

**Multiple compression is the largest identifiable risk.** At ~2.5x book on adjusted ROE of 16.4-17.4% versus 14.4% for FY2025 as a whole, the market is capitalizing peak-cycle returns. A reversion of the multiple toward the FY2025 year-end 1.86x, holding book value, implies roughly 25% downside. Neither ROE reversion nor multiple reversion is a forecast; both are live sensitivities with no offsetting cushion in the current price.

**Canadian household and commercial credit.** C$287B of residential mortgages is 48.3% of gross loans, the largest single concentration on the balance sheet, against a softening labour market that CIBC itself flags. Canadian commercial PCL +120% and retail PCL +13% on a half-year basis are the first quantified evidence. The offsetting U.S. releases that kept headline PCL flat are finite.

**U.S. tariffs on Canada.** CIBC states that because of tariffs and countermeasures "there is inherently more uncertainty associated with our assumptions as compared to prior periods." For a bank with 40% of gross loans in business and government and a U.S. commercial franchise, this is a direct credit channel into IFRS 9 expected-credit-loss models.

**U.S. commercial real estate.** The U.S. segment's improvement is provision-release-driven and reversible if office and CRE outcomes deteriorate again.

**Caribbean disposal execution.** The Butterfield transaction (US$1.0B cash plus 52.1M Butterfield shares valued at US$645M for the 91.67% stake, CET1 +24bp, close H1 2027) leaves 39% of consideration as unmonetized equity in a small Bermuda-based bank whose value depends partly on integrating the very assets CIBC is selling. Closing requires Butterfield shareholder approval. Whether an accounting gain or loss will be recognized, and the segment's earnings contribution, are unresolved from available disclosure.

**Currency.** The USD-listed shares rose 64.9% over the trailing twelve months (US$71.96 to US$118.70). CIBC's own FY2025 total shareholder return was +39.05% on a CAD basis. Part of the USD-listed return therefore reflects CAD/USD movement that I cannot decompose from the supplied data, and which is reversible for a USD-based holder.

**Governance is unassessed rather than clean.** As a foreign private issuer CIBC files a 40-F and Canadian proxy circular, not a DEF 14A, and its insiders report on SEDI rather than filing Forms 4. There is no evidence on compensation alignment, performance metrics or insider transactions. The absence of insider-purchase data is a disclosure-regime artifact and carries no signal in either direction. Modest positives: the board reviews the release, and the CEO and CFO voluntarily provide SOX-style certifications for interim financials.

**Risk factors: 4.8.** No near-term binary event, but a specific, already-visible credit rotation and a decade-high multiple with no priced cushion.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is priced for calm and positioned mildly bullish, the opposite of what a valuation-risk thesis would want to see. Near-term ATM IV is 17.5% against 20.0% one-year historical volatility, and IV sits in the 2nd percentile of the current chain, so the market is charging almost nothing for protection. Skew is −4.1% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is 0.07 and OI ratio 0.40, and the single unusual print is 2,278 August 21 $125 calls against 461 open interest (4.9x) at +4.8% moneyness. Term structure is flat (17.5% near, 18.7% at six months), so no catalyst is being priced. Short interest of 12.9M shares is roughly 1.4% of shares outstanding at 6.4 days to cover; the position is uncrowded, meaning the bearish view is not consensus, but also that there is no squeeze support if credit data disappoints. Cheap volatility makes protective puts an efficient way to hold a residual position rather than a reason to add exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (US$139, +17%)

Adjusted ROE holds at 16.5% because the efficiency gains are structural rather than cyclical: the ratio has improved three consecutive observable periods to 52.0%, and adjusted PPPT grew 19% year over year in Q2 FY2026 on a pre-provision basis. A steeper yield curve (long ends under supply and inflation pressure while short rates are anchored) restores NII growth through asset repricing rather than funding relief. Capital Markets sustains its share gains, the U.S. platform's provision cleanup converts into operating leverage as branch-closure costs annualize away, and 13.6% CET1 plus 24bp from the Caribbean close funds accelerated buybacks that lift EPS above the C$11.13 FY2027 consensus toward C$11.60. At 16.5x that is C$191, or US$139. This case requires the multiple to expand slightly from an already-high base and exceeds the top analyst target, so it needs both operating delivery and continued sector-wide sentiment support.

### Bear Case (US$92, −22%)

The funding-cost tailwind ends (already visible), Canadian commercial and retail credit continues normalizing while the U.S. reserve releases that masked it are exhausted, and PCL rises from C$1,173M in H1 FY2026 toward C$1,400M+ per half. Tariff-driven deterioration in the business and government book (40% of gross loans) compounds it. Adjusted EPS settles near C$10.50 rather than C$11.13, and the market re-rates a normalizing bank from ~2.5x book back toward 1.9-2.0x, or 12x earnings. C$126 equals US$92. This does not require a recession, only the removal of peak-cycle capitalization.

### Base Case (US$118, roughly flat)

Consensus is delivered: FY2026 adjusted EPS C$10.28, FY2027 C$11.13, and the multiple holds near 14.5x forward as improving efficiency offsets flattening margins and rising Canadian provisions. C$161 equals US$118. Adding the 2.5% dividend gives a total return of roughly 2%.

Weighting base 45%, bull 25% and bear 30% produces an expected price return of approximately −2.6% and a total return near zero to +2%, against a cost of equity in the 8-9% range for a Canadian bank. The asymmetry runs the wrong way: 17% up, 22% down, with the downside anchored to an identified and already-visible mechanism and the upside requiring multiple expansion from a decade high.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Horizon 12 months; the primary decision points are the Q3 FY2026 release (late August 2026) and Q4/FY2026 (early December 2026).

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength: the stock is 3.1% below its US$122.50 fifty-two-week high and above the consensus mean and median targets. For holders with large embedded gains, retaining a core position and financing protection with the cheap near-term volatility (IV at the 2nd percentile of chain) is a reasonable alternative to outright sale. Target residual exposure of roughly half a normal weight. Do not add new capital at US$118.70.

**Targets.** Base US$118 (−0.6%); bull US$139 (+17%); bear US$92 (−22%). Analyst range US$98.24 to US$121.06, mean US$111.45.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. NIM excluding trading resumes sequential expansion above 2.06% for two consecutive quarters, evidencing asset-side rather than funding-side margin growth.
2. Canadian Commercial Banking & Wealth PCL stabilizes at or below C$120M per quarter for two consecutive quarters while consolidated PCL stays below C$650M, removing the credit-rotation concern.
3. Price toward US$100-105 (approximately 13x FY2027E and ~2.1x book), restoring a margin of safety without any fundamental change.
4. U.S. Commercial segment adjusted PPPT growth exceeds 20% in USD with the efficiency ratio back below 57%, converting the provision-driven recovery into operating improvement.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. Consolidated PCL exceeds C$750M in a quarter with the increase concentrated in Canadian retail or commercial impaired provisions.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 5% from C$11.13, which would remove the only remaining growth leg.
3. Adjusted ROE falls below 14% for two consecutive quarters while the multiple remains above 2.2x book.
4. Butterfield shareholders reject the Caribbean transaction, or CIBC discloses a material loss on sale or held-for-sale reclassification with an unfavorable earnings impact.

**Reassessment timeline.** Late August 2026 (Q3 FY2026) is the first hard test, specifically the ex-trading NIM series and the Canadian commercial PCL line. December 2026 (FY2026 full year) tests whether consensus C$10.28 was delivered and whether FY2027 guidance implies the +8.3% embedded in estimates.

## Conclusion

CIBC is a well-capitalized, well-run bank whose recent results are genuinely better than they were two years ago, and nothing here constitutes a thesis of business impairment. The judgment is about price against durability. Every dollar of FY2025's net-interest-income growth is traceable to falling deposit costs, an arithmetic that ties to the reported figure exactly and that has now visibly flattened. Trailing reported earnings are inflated 4.3% by a one-time tax recovery. Consensus sees no sequential earnings growth for the balance of FY2026 and 8.3% in FY2027, with estimates flat over 30 and 90 days. The U.S. segment's headline recovery is provisions rather than operations. Consolidated credit stability is a coincidence of Canadian deterioration offsetting finite U.S. releases.

Set against that, the multiple has expanded from 1.53x to roughly 2.5x book and from 9.5x to nearly 17x adjusted trailing earnings, the dividend yield has compressed from 5.0% to 2.5%, the shares trade above both the mean and median analyst target, and rating sentiment is moving the wrong way while the options market charges 2nd-percentile volatility for protection. Probability-weighted expected total return is approximately zero to 2% against a 22% downside scenario anchored to a mechanism already showing up in the disclosures. Trim the position, keep the residual hedged if the volatility stays this cheap, and revisit near US$100-105 or on two consecutive quarters of asset-side margin expansion with stable Canadian commercial credit.
