**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merit Medical is a diversified manufacturer of single-use interventional, diagnostic, and therapeutic medical devices that has compounded revenue from $1,151M (2022) to $1,516M (2025) while expanding gross margin from 46.4% to 48.7% and converting earnings into $216M of free cash flow in 2025. The business is mid-single-to-low-double-digit organic growth with improving profitability, a strong liquidity position ($488M cash, 4.3x current ratio, undrawn $697M revolver), and modest leverage concentrated in a single 3.0% convertible note due 2029. On its merits as a business, this is a healthy, durable franchise.

The stock has fallen from its 52-week high of $96.74 to ~$61, while reported fundamentals improved: four consecutive earnings beats, a Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.94 versus $0.84 consensus, and a record first-quarter operating margin. The valuation is undemanding, roughly 15x the FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.07 and 14x the FY2027 estimate of $4.40, against a peer-group median P/E of 57.7. The most compelling forward signal is a coordinated cluster of open-market insider purchases by five C-suite executives (CEO, CFO, Chief Legal Officer, CHRO, Chief Commercial Officer) in early May 2026 at prices of $60-63, near today's level.

The reason this is a HOLD with a bullish lean rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 9.0% over 30 days and 10.2% over 90 days, the strongest near-term forward indicator is pointing down, and there is no identifiable catalyst within the next 60 days to force a re-rating. That near-term revision deterioration sits against two structural headwinds: ongoing China volume-based-procurement (VBP) price compression in the largest international market, and an effective tax rate that has risen from 15.8% (2023) to 24.8% (2025), which is taxing away operating-margin gains at the net-income line. The setup favors accumulation on weakness rather than a chase at current levels.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1987 by Fred Lampropoulos, Merit Medical produces consumable devices for interventional and therapeutic procedures across cardiology, radiology, oncology, critical care, and endoscopy. The model resembles a "razor-blade" franchise: a broad portfolio of single-use devices tied to recurring procedures, sold into U.S. (~60% of revenue) and international (~40%) markets, with China the largest international geography. The company operates 11 manufacturing facilities, employs roughly 7,500 people across 43 countries, and holds ~2,200 patents.

Effective Q1 2026, new CEO Martha Aronson collapsed the prior two-segment structure (Cardiovascular ~95% of sales, Endoscopy ~5%) into a single operating segment with two product groupings: **Foundational** (access devices, procedural solutions, OEM, vascular intervention; Q1'26 $255M, +6.3%) and **Therapeutic** (cardiac therapies, oncology, renal therapies, endoscopy; Q1'26 $126M, +9.9%). The Therapeutic bucket is the higher-growth, higher-value strategic focus and the locus of the company's pivot up the value chain.

A founder-to-professional-management transition has defined the past nine months. Lampropoulos resigned as CEO on October 3, 2025 and as Board Chair on January 4, 2026, ending a ~38-year era. Aronson, an external medical-device executive, has already reset segment reporting, executed the company's first notable divestiture (DualCap, February 2026), and closed a strategic oncology acquisition (View Point Medical, April 2026).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth.** Revenue grew from $1,151M (2022) to $1,257M (2023, +9.2%) to $1,357M (2024, +8.0%) to $1,516M (2025, +11.8%). Q1 2026 revenue was $381.9M, up 7.5% reported (about +5% constant currency). FY2026 guidance is for 5.6-7% constant-currency growth, implying roughly $1.62B. Growth has been a blend of organic mid-single-digit and acquisition-led contribution.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded 230bps over three years (46.4% → 47.4% → 48.7%), driven by mix shift toward Therapeutic products and pricing discipline. GAAP operating margin rose from 9.9% to 12.2%; non-GAAP operating margin reached ~20.3% in 2025. The margin trajectory reflects operating leverage in a consumables business.

**The tax drag.** Net income grew $74.5M → $94.4M → $120.4M → $128.5M, but the pace of net-income growth has decelerated relative to operating income because the effective tax rate has climbed from 15.8% (2023) to 19.8% (2024) to 24.8% (2025), with Q1'26 at 23.4%. The drivers (OBBBA provisions, OECD Pillar Two, and fewer discrete share-comp benefits) are structural. Investors should model a high-23%-to-25% go-forward rate, which means operating-margin expansion will translate into less net-margin expansion than the headline gross-margin trend implies.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $297M and free cash flow $216M (a 14.2% FCF margin), with FCF below net income partly because capex is rising ($82M in 2025, guided $80-100M for 2026, including a new South Jordan, Utah distribution facility). Liquidity is excellent: $446M cash at year-end 2025 ($488M at Q1'26), a 4.34x current ratio, and an undrawn $697M revolver. The only material debt is $747.5M of 3.0% convertible notes due February 2029 (conversion price $86.83; capped-call cap $114.68), both well above the current $61. Debt/equity is ~0.7. The Altman Z-score of 2.84 sits in the "grey zone," but that reflects an intangible-heavy, M&A-built balance sheet (goodwill $507M + intangibles $609M ≈ 42% of $2.69B assets) rather than financial stress; the liquidity profile is not consistent with distress.

**Earnings quality.** Four consecutive beats (Q2'25 +16.4%, Q3'25 +8.4%, Q4'25 +6.9%, Q1'26 +10.7%) and OCF exceeding net income support confidence in the reported numbers. Q1'26 GAAP EPS of $0.68 included a $12.5M pretax gain from the DualCap divestiture; the cleaner comparison is the $0.94 non-GAAP figure.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is mid-single-digit, supplemented by a steady acquisition cadence. The strategic emphasis is shifting toward higher-growth Therapeutic categories: oncology (SCOUT radar localization, now augmented by the View Point OneMark imaging and tissue-marker platform, which management projects will materially expand its oncology opportunity), cardiac therapies, renal therapies (the Wrapsody cell-impermeable endoprosthesis for AV dialysis outflow is flagged as a disproportionately important growth driver), and endoscopy (C2 CryoBalloon for Barrett's esophagus).

The growth concern is near-term, not structural. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been revised down 9.0% over 30 days and 10.2% over 90 days, from $1.07 (90 days ago) to $0.96. Full-year revisions are flat-to-slightly-positive (FY2026 +1.6% over 30 days to $4.07; FY2027 +0.2% to $4.40), so the cut is concentrated in the upcoming quarter rather than reflecting a structural deterioration in the multi-year trajectory. The near-term revision direction is the strongest single forward indicator available, and it is negative. Combined with the absence of a 60-day catalyst, this keeps the growth score below the level a BUY would require.

The persistent headwind is China VBP, where government price-cutting has compressed Merit's revenue and pricing since 2022 and is expected to continue into 2026 in its largest international market. This caps the rate of organic acceleration regardless of how well the U.S. and Therapeutic businesses perform.

## Valuation Assessment

Merit trades at ~15.0x the FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.07 and ~13.9x the FY2027 estimate of $4.40, with the FY2025 trailing P/E at ~27x on GAAP EPS of $2.13 (the trailing figure is inflated by GAAP items and tax effects; forward is the relevant lens). Against the Health Care Supplies peer set (NVST 57.7x, ICUI 73.1x, HAE 33.1x, with XRAY and NEOG at negative trailing multiples), Merit's low-teens-to-mid-teens forward multiple is a clear discount. The peer median P/E is 57.7x.

The discount is not unjustified, which is why the valuation score is good rather than exceptional. Merit's organic growth rate is slower than several premium-multiple peers, its net-income growth is being throttled by the rising tax rate, and the founder transition introduces execution uncertainty that the market is reasonably discounting. The analyst price-target range ($74 low, $90.6 mean, $122 high) implies the sell-side sees 21-100% upside, with the consensus mean implying ~49% upside. That distribution is entirely above the current price; with near-term revisions falling, I treat the low target ($74, ~21% upside) as the more reliable anchor and the mean as aspirational pending revision stabilization.

The valuation offers downside support: the absolute forward multiple is low enough that further multiple compression is limited, and the insider buying clusters right at current levels. The case for asymmetric upside requires the near-term revision trend to stabilize and the Therapeutic pivot to demonstrate accelerating growth. Neither is yet confirmed in the data.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit competes against far larger players (Boston Scientific, Medtronic, Abbott, BD, Teleflex, Terumo, Cook Medical, Edwards, Stryker, Johnson & Johnson, Hologic, Olympus, and Getinge) across a fragmented set of niches. Its competitive edge is breadth of ancillary consumable lines, customer-focused custom kitting, deep manufacturing integration, and a fast new-product cadence, rather than scale. The competitive position is stable-to-slowly-improving: gross-margin expansion signals retained pricing power and favorable mix, and the strategic move toward higher-value Therapeutic products is an attempt to escape commoditization in basic access devices.

Industry headwinds (GPO/IDN purchasing power, reimbursement pressure, China VBP, EU MDR recertification, and EtO/PFAS regulation) are sector-wide and largely priced; Merit will discontinue some EU products rather than recertify ahead of the Dec 2027/2028 deadlines. Merit's disposable, procedure-tied revenue base gives it relative stability against macro cyclicality.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Founder succession (the dominant new risk).** The departure of the 38-year founder and the arrival of an external CEO introduce culture, customer, and execution risk. The 10-K explicitly notes no key-man insurance on Aronson. Early signals (portfolio pruning, segment reset, disciplined oncology acquisition) are constructive, but the transition is unproven across a full cycle.

2. **China VBP price compression.** A structural cap on organic growth and international margins.

3. **Near-term estimate cuts.** The current-quarter consensus cut raises the probability of a disappointing next print relative to where expectations stood a quarter ago.

4. **Rising tax rate.** The structural climb to ~24-25% throttles net-income conversion of operating-margin gains.

5. **Acquisition integration and impairment.** Five acquisitions in ~24 months (Cook, EndoGastric, Biolife, C2 CryoBalloon, View Point); goodwill plus intangibles are ~42% of assets, creating impairment exposure if any underperform.

6. **Wrapsody concentration.** A single product flagged as disproportionately important; clinical, reimbursement, or manufacturing-scale setbacks would matter.

7. **Convertible refinancing 2029.** Manageable given the cash position and the long runway, but a $747.5M maturity to monitor.

Offsetting positives: the 2020 DOJ-related Corporate Integrity Agreement expired in October 2025 with OIG formal completion confirmed January 2026, removing a compliance overhang; and the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized leaves Merit (an importer of record) potentially owed refunds, with no benefit yet booked, a modest, unquantified upside.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and slightly constructive. Near-term ATM IV is 33.1% against a 1-year historical volatility of 30.6% (fair, not stressed), and IV sits in only the 6th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (33.1% near-term < 35.6% six-month), the complacent/normal configuration. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-9.1% near-term), which is unusual and mildly bullish. The one cautionary note is a put/call open-interest ratio of 3.00, indicating accumulated downside hedging, though that is offset by the call-tilted near-term skew. The implied move into the June 18 expiry is ±6.0%, with the 3-month expiry pricing ±11.4%; the market is not pricing an imminent binary event but is leaving room for a meaningful move around the next earnings print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($85-95).** Near-term revisions stabilize, the Therapeutic franchise (oncology via View Point/SCOUT, renal via Wrapsody, endoscopy via C2) accelerates organic growth toward the high single digits, and the founder transition proves seamless under Aronson. On the FY2027 estimate of $4.40 at a 19-21x multiple (a partial re-rating toward the lower end of the peer band, justified by accelerating growth and removed governance overhangs), the stock reaches $85-95. Insider buying near $61 underwrites the conviction. The mean analyst target of ~$90.6 sits in this zone.

**Bear case ($50-53).** China VBP deepens, the next quarter misses against the lowered bar, the tax rate stays elevated, and one of the five recent acquisitions underperforms and triggers an impairment, prolonging the founder-transition discount. A de-rate to ~12x the FY2026 estimate puts the stock around $49-52, roughly 15-20% below the current price.

**Base case ($68-72).** Modest multiple recovery toward 16-17x on FY2026 EPS as revisions stabilize and the transition stays on track, with no major acceleration. This is ~11-18% upside, supported by the insider-buying floor and the undemanding starting valuation.

The asymmetry is reasonable but not exceptional: roughly 11-18% to base case and 15-20% downside in the bear case, with the near-term revision trend being the swing factor. That balance, with downside near the BUY tolerance limit, argues for accumulation on weakness rather than a full-weight purchase here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a bullish lean (growth-oriented), best accumulated on weakness toward the high-$50s where the C-suite insider purchases cluster.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions stabilize or turn positive (a single quarter without a downward revision, or any +5% move), removing the near-term overhang.
- The next earnings print (expected late July 2026) beats and management raises or affirms FY2026 guidance with evidence of Therapeutic-category acceleration.
- The stock pulls back to the high-$50s, where the bear-case downside compresses and the insider-buying zone provides support, improving the risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An earnings miss accompanied by a guidance cut to below 5% constant-currency growth.
- A goodwill or intangible impairment from any of the five recent acquisitions.
- Evidence the founder transition is destabilizing the business — customer or key-employee attrition, or a sharp deceleration in U.S. organic growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), which will test the lowered current-quarter bar and provide the first read on whether the near-term revision trend has stabilized.

## Conclusion

Merit Medical is a high-quality, cash-generative consumable medical-device compounder with expanding margins, a strong balance sheet, and a coordinated cluster of C-suite open-market insider buying near the current price, all genuinely supportive signals at a forward multiple that is a steep discount to peers. The reason this is not yet a BUY is specific and testable: consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut over the past 90 days, the strongest near-term forward indicator is deteriorating, and there is no 60-day catalyst to force a re-rating, while China VBP compression and a structurally rising tax rate cap net-margin upside through 2026. The bear-case downside (~15-20%) sits at the edge of what a BUY should tolerate.

The call is HOLD with a bullish lean. The insider buying and undemanding valuation make this a name to accumulate into weakness rather than avoid, and the bar for upgrading to BUY is clear: stabilization of near-term estimate revisions or a pullback toward the high-$50s. Investors who already own it should retain. New capital is better deployed on confirmation that the next quarter has cleared the lowered bar.
