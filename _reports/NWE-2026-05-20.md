## Executive Summary

NorthWestern Energy is a regulated multi-utility serving ~850,300 customers across Montana, South Dakota, Nebraska, and Yellowstone National Park. The stock has appreciated 26% over the past year, driven primarily by the August 2025 merger announcement with Black Hills Corporation (BKH) — an all-stock "merger of equals" at a 0.98 exchange ratio creating Bright Horizon Energy. At $71.54, NWE trades at 26x trailing EPS of $2.94, a substantial premium to the multi-utility peer median of 19x.

The investment case rests on two pillars: (1) successful completion of the BKH merger, which requires Montana PSC approval following contentious May 2026 hearings, and (2) a data center pipeline totaling 1,100 MW by 2030 that could accelerate the company's rate base growth trajectory. Management guides 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $3.68-$3.83 with 4-6% long-term EPS growth, implying the standalone business (or combined entity) grows into its current valuation over 2-3 years. The holding thesis depends on these specific catalysts materializing; without them, the stock is meaningfully overvalued relative to peers.

The primary risk is Montana PSC rejection of the merger (estimated 25-35% probability based on the contentious hearing record and political dynamics), which would likely send the stock back toward $58-62 — the pre-merger announcement range. Secondary risks include rising interest expense on a capital-intensive balance sheet ($683M capex planned for 2026 alone), all nine labor agreements expiring simultaneously in 2026, and Colstrip environmental liabilities.

## Company Overview

NorthWestern Energy operates electric and natural gas services across a vast service territory covering 73% of Montana's land area plus portions of South Dakota, Nebraska, and Yellowstone National Park. The company serves approximately 440,700 electric customers in Montana, ~65,600 in South Dakota, and ~344,000 natural gas customers through 1,667 employees.

The generation portfolio includes 470 MW of hydroelectric capacity (100% carbon-free), 814 MW of coal (Colstrip Units 3&4, recently expanded to 55% ownership via $0 acquisitions from Avista and Puget), 325 MW of natural gas, and 131 MW of wind. Approximately 52% of owned/contracted resources are carbon-free.

**Rate base** stood at approximately $5.57 billion at year-end 2025, with authorized returns on equity of 9.65% (electric) and 9.60% (gas) from the December 2025 Montana rate review. The company participates in the Western EIM and SPP organized markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** Revenue grew from $1.42B (2023) to $1.61B (2025), representing 6.5% CAGR. Growth is driven by rate case approvals ($93.3M new base rates in 2025) and transmission revenue (+$14M), partially offset by supply cost timing differences.

**Earnings Quality:** 2025 GAAP EPS of $2.95 was depressed by two non-recurring items: a $30.9M YCGS regulatory disallowance and $9.3M in merger-related costs. Normalizing for these (~$0.50/share after-tax), earnings power was closer to $3.45/share. The 2026 guidance midpoint of $3.76 implies 8-9% normalized growth, consistent with the 4-6% rate base growth target plus modest regulatory tailwinds.

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure:**
- Operating cash flow: $394.5M (2025)
- Capital expenditure: $524.5M (2025), rising to $683M planned for 2026
- Free cash flow: negative $130M — a structural negative funded through external capital markets
- Long-term debt: $3.30B; debt-to-equity: 1.93x
- Interest expense: $150.4M (2025), up from $131.7M (2024) — a 14% increase reflecting cumulative debt issuance
- Dividend: $2.64/share ($161.4M total), within 60-70% target payout range

The company carries $452M in federal NOL carryforwards, resulting in minimal cash income taxes through 2029. This partially offsets the negative free cash flow profile.

**Balance Sheet Stress:** The current ratio of 0.72 and Altman Z-score of 1.04 are structurally typical for capital-intensive regulated utilities and do not indicate distress given investment-grade credit ratings (BBB range, S&P positive outlook). However, $255M of debt matures in 2026, and refinancing at current rates (30Y >5%) will be incrementally expensive.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth:** The five-year capital plan (approximately 70% distribution/transmission) supports the stated 4-6% long-term rate base EPS growth target. Major projects include a 131 MW Aberdeen gas plant (~$300M) and participation in North Plains Connector transmission (10% ownership, 300 MW, targeting 2032).

**Data Center Pipeline:** Three LOIs (Sabey, Atlas, Quantica) target 175 MW by late 2027, growing to 1,100+ MW by 2030. For context, current Montana peak load is approximately 1,300 MW, so this represents potential load growth of 85%. Montana's cheap hydroelectric power, cold climate, and available land create structural advantages for data center siting.

However, this pipeline remains speculative. The critical dependency is Montana PSC approval of large-load tariff structures that prevent cost-shifting to existing ratepayers. Quantica's "self-funded power" pledge and NWE's proactive rate design filing (expected H1 2026) are positive signals, but regulatory approval is not guaranteed. If the large-load tariff is structured appropriately, data center customers would fund their own infrastructure, and NWE would earn regulated returns on incremental capital. If it isn't, political backlash could block development entirely.

**Merger Synergies:** The combined Bright Horizon Energy would cover approximately 20% of U.S. land area with ~$15.4B enterprise value. Scale benefits include shared services, procurement leverage, and stronger regulatory/political influence. However, no specific synergy targets have been quantified publicly, making this difficult to model.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter (Q2 2026) estimates have risen 26.1% in the past 30 days ($0.42 → $0.53), likely reflecting favorable weather or the Q1 beat flowing through. However, full-year 2026 and 2027 estimates have declined modestly (-1.3% and -0.5% respectively), suggesting the quarterly shift reflects timing rather than a fundamental upgrade to the annual earnings trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.54, NWE trades at:
- **24.3x trailing P/E** (on depressed $2.94 GAAP EPS)
- **19.0x forward P/E** on 2026 guidance midpoint of $3.76 (non-GAAP)
- **18.0x forward P/E** on 2027 consensus of $3.97
- **3.7% dividend yield** ($2.64 annualized)
- **~1.5x book value** ($2.89B equity / 61.5M shares = ~$47/share book)

**Peer Comparison:**

| Metric | NWE | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| Trailing P/E | 24.3x | 19.1x |
| Dividend Yield | 3.7% | 3.6% |
| Forward Growth | 4-6% | ~5% typical |

NWE trades at a 27% premium to peer median P/E on trailing earnings. On forward (normalized) earnings, the premium narrows to approximately par — 19.0x forward vs. 19.1x peer median. This suggests the market has correctly identified that 2025 earnings were depressed and is pricing toward normalized 2026 levels.

The critical valuation question is whether the stock deserves any premium for the data center optionality. If 1,100 MW materializes, rate base could grow at 8-10% annually rather than 4-6%, which would justify a premium multiple. But this optionality is currently unpriced in analyst targets (mean $71.42, essentially at market) and uncertain in both magnitude and timing.

**Merger Arbitrage Math:** At BKH's current implied price and the 0.98 exchange ratio, NWE's implied merger value is approximately $74-75. The current $71.54 price represents a ~4% merger spread, which is thin for a deal still requiring Montana PSC approval. This suggests the market assigns high probability (~80%+) to completion.

## Competitive Landscape

NWE operates as a regulated monopoly within its service territories — competition in the traditional utility sense is limited. The relevant competitive dimension is regulatory positioning and growth opportunity capture.

**Regulatory Trajectory:** Mixed. Montana PSC has demonstrated willingness to disallow costs (YCGS $30.9M), suggesting a moderately constructive but not rubber-stamp environment. Authorized ROEs of 9.60-9.65% are slightly below the national average of ~10%. The wildfire liability legislation (HB 490) was a positive legislative outcome. South Dakota and Nebraska are generally more supportive.

**Data Center Positioning:** Montana is competing with Idaho, Wyoming, and other Pacific Northwest states for data center investment. NWE's advantage is existing transmission infrastructure and hydroelectric resources. The risk is that political opposition in Montana pushes developers to competing states with more accommodating regulatory environments.

**Scale vs. Peers:** As a standalone entity, NWE is small ($4.4B market cap) relative to utility peers. The BKH merger addresses this directly, creating a ~$10B equity value entity with broader geographic diversification and stronger capital market access.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Montana PSC Merger Rejection (25-35% probability):** The May 2026 hearings were contentious. Opposition framed the deal as benefiting executives (~$30M golden parachutes for top 5 executives) and data center companies at ratepayer expense. The $10M customer credit was dismissed as "$18 per household." Five elected Republican commissioners must balance economic development vs. consumer protection. Rejection would likely produce 15-20% downside from current levels.

2. **Rising Interest Expense:** The company must refinance $255M in 2026 and fund $683M capex. At current long-term rates, incremental interest expense will compress returns earned between rate cases. The S&P positive outlook provides some buffer, but credit metrics are already stretched (D/E 1.93x).

3. **Colstrip Environmental Liability:** MATS compliance costs estimated at $350-665M for the total plant (NWE's share at 55% ownership = $193-366M). Coal combustion residual litigation and permitting uncertainty for Rosebud Mine add tail risk. The depreciable life extends to 2042.

4. **Labor Concentration Risk:** All nine CBAs expire in 2026, being renegotiated simultaneously during a period of merger uncertainty. Work stoppages at a utility cause immediate operational and reputational damage.

5. **PCCAM Under-Collection:** Net regulatory receivable grew from $5.9M to $44.8M during 2025 — a $39M cash drag with uncertain recovery timing.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term ATM IV of 27.8% sits 6.5pp above 1-year historical volatility of 21.2%, pricing elevated event risk. The term structure is in backwardation (27.8% near-term vs. 22.5% at 6 months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst resolution — consistent with the Montana PSC decision timeline. The call-heavy skew (-4.4%) and near-zero put/call volume ratio suggest positioning is biased toward upside completion, with minimal protective put activity. This is consistent with high merger completion expectations but creates vulnerability to a surprise rejection.

IV has declined from 29.2% to 23.6% over the past month (spread basis; current chain shows 27.8% for nearest term), suggesting gradual de-risking as the hearing concluded. Short interest at 4.9% of float with 4.6 days to cover is moderate and does not suggest meaningful directional positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($78-82):** Montana PSC approves merger; Bright Horizon Energy trades at 19-20x combined 2027 EPS. Data center tariff structure approved, and first projects begin interconnection (2027). Rate base growth accelerates to 7-8% on data center infrastructure spending. Combined entity achieves scale-driven financing advantages, compressing cost of capital. NWE shareholders receive 0.98 BKH shares worth $78-80 at current BKH levels, with post-merger re-rating potential as the market prices in the combined growth story.

**Bear Case ($56-60):** Montana PSC rejects the merger on consumer protection grounds. NWE returns to standalone valuation at 16-17x on guidance midpoint ($3.76 × 16x = $60). Data center LOIs stall without the combined entity's capital capacity. Rising interest costs and Colstrip liabilities compress earnings. Downside: 17-22% from current price.

**Base Case ($71-75):** Merger approved with conditions (additional customer credits or rate freezes). Combined entity valued at 18x 2027 estimates. Data center pipeline advances but at slower pace than projected. Translates to 0-5% total return plus ~3.7% dividend = 4-9% total return over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Montana PSC approval with no material adverse conditions, creating a clear path to close; AND stock trades below $68 (implying >8% spread to implied merger value)
- Alternatively, data center tariff approval creating quantifiable rate base growth above 6% annually with first customer commitments

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Montana PSC rejects merger or imposes conditions that make deal uneconomic (extended rate freeze >5 years, disallowance of merger costs)
- BKH stock declines >15% from current levels (reducing implied value of NWE exchange ratio below $64)
- Full-year 2026 EPS guidance cut below $3.50 (indicating fundamental deterioration beyond one-time items)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Montana PSC decision expected within 30-60 days of the May 13-18 hearing conclusion (target: June/July 2026). This is the single most important catalyst. Secondary: Q2 2026 earnings (late July), data center tariff filing response.

## Conclusion

NWE is a hold for investors already positioned for the merger arbitrage, with the 4% spread to implied merger value providing modest compensation for the remaining regulatory risk. The data center growth narrative provides a genuine 12-month catalyst for the combined entity, supporting a growth-oriented holding thesis rather than a pure income play.

The stock is not a compelling new entry at $71.54. The premium to standalone fundamental value is largely explained by merger expectations, and the current spread does not adequately compensate for the 25-35% probability of Montana PSC rejection. For new investors, a more attractive entry would be in the $63-66 range (where the merger spread widens to 10%+) or upon confirmation of PSC approval.

The 3.7% dividend yield is market-appropriate for a regulated utility but does not compensate for the binary event risk embedded in the stock. The overall score of 5.2 reflects a business that is fundamentally sound but trading at a valuation that assumes successful execution of multiple pending catalysts — the merger, the data center pipeline, and continued rate case support — with limited margin of safety if any of these disappoint.
