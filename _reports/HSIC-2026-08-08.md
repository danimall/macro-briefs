## Executive Summary

Henry Schein's second-quarter FY2026 print was the strongest operating result in the period under review, and it moved the shares to within 4.4% of a 52-week high. Net sales grew 6.7% to $3,458M with internal growth of 4.6%, operating margin expanded 29 basis points to 4.95%, GAAP diluted EPS rose 17.1% to $0.82, and management raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to $5.29–$5.39 from $5.23–$5.37 while lifting the sales growth range to 4.5%–5.5% from 3%–5%. That is the fourth consecutive quarterly beat, and consensus estimates have drifted up (+1.4% for next year over 30 days). The new leadership team — CEO Frederick Lowery arrived from Thermo Fisher on March 2, 2026 — attributed part of the gross margin improvement to "early benefits from our value creation initiatives."

Underneath the headline, the composition is uncomfortable. Roughly a fifth of reported Q2 growth was foreign exchange (+1.4 points of the 6.7%), which does not persist. The Global Distribution & Value-Added Services segment, 84.3% of FY2025 revenue, saw its adjusted operating margin fall from 6.49% to 6.11% in FY2025 while Specialty Products (13.55% → 17.42%) and Technology (24.13% → 26.67%) expanded; those two segments were 15.6% of revenue but 38.3% of segment operating income. U.S. dental equipment internal sales fell 1.1% in Q2 — the highest-ticket, most discretionary category going backwards. Consolidated gross margin declined 55 basis points in FY2025 to 31.14%, and consolidated operating margin only rose because SG&A fell an offsetting 55 basis points. A $14M FY2025 intangible impairment on GDVAS customer relationships, taken because of "lower than anticipated operating margins in these businesses," corroborates the segment data.

Capital structure is the second constraint. Net debt reached $3,305M at June 27, 2026, up $354M in six months, against first-half operating cash flow of $145M and roughly $60M of free cash flow — while $325M went to buybacks. Tangible book attributable to Henry Schein is negative $2.0B; goodwill of $4,213M is 130% of stockholders' equity. Leverage on our construction is 2.84x TTM adjusted EBITDA, or 3.62x if the $906M of redeemable noncontrolling interests is treated as debt-like, and the company discloses only that it must maintain "certain maximum leverage ratios" without quantifying the threshold or its headroom — after amending all three debt agreements in 2025 to "modify certain financial definitions and covenants." Interest expense has risen from $87M (FY2023) to $150M (FY2025); in a higher-for-longer rate regime that trajectory continues.

At 16.5x the guidance midpoint the equity looks inexpensive against a peer median trailing P/E of 21.6. On a like-for-like basis it does not. Adjusted EPS exceeded GAAP by $0.85 in the first half ($2.59 versus $1.74), so the same guidance implies roughly 24x forward GAAP earnings and about 27x trailing GAAP, above Cencora (21.6x) and McKesson (20.5x). EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA of 11.3x (12.6x including both noncontrolling interest lines) is a full multiple for a company earning a 4.95% operating margin on mid-single-digit organic growth. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the score rises from 4.9 to 5.1 because the quarter and guidance genuinely improved, not because the risk/reward at $88.13 has improved.

## Company Overview

Henry Schein is the largest distributor of health care products and services to office-based dental and medical practitioners, with 94 years of history, more than one million customers, over 25,000 employees (48% U.S. / 52% international), operations or affiliates in 34 countries, more than 300,000 stocked SKUs, 38 distribution centers spanning 5.4 million square feet, 17 manufacturing facilities, 127 equipment sales and service centers, and roughly 150,000 cartons shipped daily.

FY2025 net sales of $13,184M split across three reportable segments:

| Segment | FY2025 sales | % of total | Adj. operating income | Adj. margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global Distribution & Value-Added Services | $11,120M | 84.3% | $680M | 6.11% |
| Global Specialty Products | $1,389M | 10.5% | $242M | 17.42% |
| Global Technology | $675M | 5.1% | $180M | 26.67% |
| Corporate, net | — | — | $(130)M | — |
| Consolidated operating income | | | $653M | 4.95% |

Dental was 52.0% of consolidated sales (merchandise 36.6%, equipment 13.6%, value-added services 1.8%); medical 32.5%. The U.S. contributed $9,096M (69.0%). Customer and supplier concentration are both low: no customer exceeded 2% of sales in any of the last three fiscal years, the top ten distribution suppliers were ~24% of purchases, and the largest single supplier ~4%.

The economics are share-of-wallet, not exclusivity. Practices generally buy from more than one distributor with one acting as primary supplier; Henry Schein's strategy is to be that primary supplier, bundling software, financing, education, consulting and equipment service around consumables. Global Technology reported an active base of ~95,000 practices as of December 27, 2025, and many practice-management competitors connect to Henry Schein's platform through its API program — the company is simultaneously the platform of record and a competitor to its own integration partners.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Net sales grew 2.71% in FY2024 and 4.03% in FY2025 to $13,184M, then 6.5% in H1 FY2026 to $6,826M. The Q2 decomposition — internal 4.6%, acquisitions 0.7%, FX +1.4% — puts durable organic growth at approximately 4.6%, the best in the period reviewed but with roughly 21% of the reported figure attributable to currency.

**Margins.** FY2025 gross margin of 31.14% was down 55 basis points from 31.69%, offset exactly by SG&A falling to 23.39% of sales from 23.94%, leaving operating margin marginally higher at 4.95%. The segment detail is the more informative disclosure: GDVAS adjusted operating margin declined 38 basis points on 3.6% sales growth, while Specialty and Technology expanded. The most straightforward reading is that price competition and cost inflation in commodity distribution are outrunning efficiency programs, and consolidated margin stability is a mix artifact. A plausible alternative is that the January 2025 reporting-unit realignment shifted costs between GDVAS and "Corporate, net" — which worsened by $53M to $(130)M in the same year — mechanically overstating core deterioration. The filings do not disclose enough allocation detail to separate the two, and this is the single most important unresolved question in the analysis.

Q2 FY2026 operating margin of 4.95% versus 4.66% and first-half margin of 5.17% versus 5.09% are genuine improvements and the first data points that argue the erosion may be arrested. One quarter does not settle the segment question.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $500M (FY2023), $848M (FY2024) and $712M (FY2025); free cash flow after capex and capitalized software was $313M, $661M and $521M. The FY2025 decline is a receivables artifact rather than an earnings-quality problem: accounts receivable were a +$315M source in FY2024 versus a $(124)M use in FY2025, with receivables at 11.7% of sales in FY2024 against 12.5% in FY2025 and 15.1% in FY2023. FY2024 was the outlier, most plausibly normalization following the October 2023 cyber incident. FY2025's $712M is the representative run rate; $521M of FCF equals a 5.30% yield on the current $9.82B market cap. Cash conversion lengthened modestly, from 78.4 to 79.8 days. Stock compensation of $39M (0.30% of sales) is unusually low and supports reported cash flow quality.

**Balance sheet.** At December 27, 2025: cash $156M, current ratio 1.38x, goodwill $4,213M and other intangibles $1,018M together 46.6% of the $11,215M asset base, total debt $3,107M against $2,536M a year earlier. Tangible book attributable to Henry Schein is negative $1,986M. By June 27, 2026 total debt was $3,462M and net debt $3,305M. Interest expense progression: $87M → $131M → $150M; FY2025 interest coverage 4.35x gross on EBIT of $653M.

The debt itself is prudently structured: $1,149M of private placement notes at a 3.93% weighted average maturing 2027–2033; a $749M term loan to June 6, 2030 roughly 90% hedged at a 5.69% effective rate; a $390M trade receivables securitization (facility expires December 2027, housed in a consolidated VIE with recourse to Henry Schein); $764M of short-term bank lines at 4.68%. The maturity ladder is manageable — $534M in 2027, $221M in 2028, $810M in 2030.

Leverage on our construction: FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of approximately $1,100M (operating income $653M + D&A $311M + restructuring $105M + intangible impairment $16M + litigation $5M + advisory/value-creation $36M, less the $20M net cyber insurance gain and $2M contingent-consideration gain), rolling to roughly $1,162M TTM using disclosed H1 adjusted EBITDA of $577M versus $515M. Net debt of $3,305M is therefore 2.84x, or 3.62x including redeemable NCI. Our Altman Z of 2.83 (2.71 treating redeemable NCI as a liability) sits in the grey zone, though the Z-Score systematically penalizes goodwill-heavy, high-turnover distributors and is sensitive to the market-cap input. The signal is "not comfortably safe," not distressed.

**Capital allocation.** Repurchases escalated from 3.21M shares ($252M) in FY2023 to 5.42M ($388M) in FY2024 to 12.06M ($850M cash) in FY2025. Share count fell from 129.25M to 111.9M in 30 months, a 13.4% reduction. No dividend. FY2025's $1,175M of discretionary outflows (buybacks, acquisitions, NCI purchases and distributions, contingent consideration) against $521M of FCF was bridged by the $250M KKR equity issuance and $553M of net new borrowing. In H1 FY2026, $325M of buybacks and $94M of acquisition/NCI outflows were funded against roughly $60M of FCF, with total debt up $355M.

The seasonality caveat is material and cuts in Henry Schein's favor: the 10-K states sales and profitability are concentrated in Q3 and Q4 on influenza vaccine timing, year-end equipment tax incentives and tradeshow cadence. First-half cash flow must not be annualized. As a labelled sensitivity: if FY2026 FCF matches FY2025's $521M and buybacks continue near the $650M annualized H1 pace, the shortfall is roughly $130M before acquisitions and NCI purchases — continued net borrowing, but at a fraction of the H1 rate. Remaining authorization was $455M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is arithmetic rather than thematic. Guided sales growth of 4.5%–5.5% for FY2026 against 4.6% Q2 internal growth implies stable underlying volume with acquisition and currency contribution on top. Consensus next-year adjusted EPS of $6.00 versus the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $5.34 embeds 12.4% growth, which requires roughly 5% revenue growth, modest margin expansion, and 3–4% share count reduction to work. That is achievable but leaves no room for GDVAS margin deterioration to resume.

Three specific reservations:

**H2 deceleration is embedded in the guide.** H1 adjusted EBITDA grew 12.0% ($577M versus $515M) while full-year adjusted EBITDA growth is guided "mid-to-high single digits." Management is signalling that the first-half profit pace does not persist. The company did not disclose the drivers, and no earnings call transcript was retrievable, so the reason is unverified.

**U.S. dental equipment is contracting.** Internal sales fell 1.1% in Q2. Equipment is 13.6% of consolidated revenue and the highest value-added category, subject to year-end tax-incentive purchasing and biennial tradeshow timing. Deferred capital equipment purchases are usually delayed rather than cancelled, so this can reverse; sustained deferral in a higher-for-longer rate environment compresses the mix in the wrong direction. Practice-level financing costs remain a headwind to discretionary equipment purchases.

**Structural demand is real but slow.** Census projections cited in the 10-K show the 45-and-older population growing ~10% between 2025 and 2035 versus ~4% for the total population. That supports low-single-digit volume, not acceleration. Against it, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act is expected to reduce Medicaid enrollment and federal funding to state programs, a direct volume risk to community health center, government site-of-care and home-health businesses including Shield Healthcare.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but nowhere near inflection velocity: current year +0.5% over 30 days, next year +1.4%, with the current quarter revised down 0.9%. The four-quarter beat record (0.7%, 6.3%, 1.1%, 5.8%) supports confidence in consensus without suggesting systematic underestimation.

There is a mechanical GAAP tailwind worth naming because it is arithmetic rather than operating improvement: scheduled amortization of existing intangibles declines from $172M (2026) to $118M (2030), roughly $54M of pre-tax relief, about $0.37 per share after tax at a 24% rate. Absent new deals, GAAP earnings converge toward adjusted over the next four years.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is distribution density and breadth: 38 distribution centers, 127 equipment service centers, 300,000 SKUs, and — per the 10-K — a claim to being the only global distributor of supplies and equipment to dental practices outside the U.S., where it faces only local and regional competitors (Cadence, Proclinic, DD Group, Nuent, Lifco, Planmeca, Dental Union, Dental Bauer). International dental was the strongest region in Q2, consistent with that structural position.

Three pressures are working against it, and the FY2025 segment margins suggest they are currently winning in the core.

**Buyer-power concentration.** Item 1 states that customer consolidation "has accelerated the growth of ... Dental Service Organizations and Group Purchasing Organizations, which, in addition to their emphasis on obtaining products at competitive prices, tend to favor distributors capable of providing specialized management information support." Low reported customer concentration (nothing above 2% of sales) does not confer immunity: GPO and DSO buying groups aggregate negotiating power across members without appearing as a single billed customer. The counterparty setting Henry Schein's price is getting larger.

**Supplier disintermediation, disclosed as already occurring.** Item 1: manufacturers "could seek to sell directly to end-users," and "in certain parts of the dental end market, such as those related to dental specialty products, and medical end market manufacturers already sell directly to end customers." This is most acute in specialty products, exactly where Henry Schein has been acquiring — and where it competes head-on with Straumann, Envista, ZimVie and Dentsply Sirona in implants.

**Software as both asset and channel conflict.** Global Technology's 26.67% adjusted operating margin is the highest in the portfolio and the strongest evidence that the bundling strategy earns its keep. But the segment's intellectual-property foundation is partly licensed: a ten-year royalty agreement entered in connection with the 2018 formation of Henry Schein One requires approximately $31M annually to Internet Brands, recorded at $31M in each of FY2023–FY2025. A ten-year agreement dated 2018 runs to roughly 2028, renewal terms are not disclosed, and the counterparty holds a 33.6% stake in the subsidiary plus a put right against Henry Schein. The royalty is only 4.6% of Technology segment revenue, so even material repricing is small consolidated — but the negotiation is dateable and the counterparty has leverage.

The FY2025 10-K continues to name Patterson Dental and its Eaglesoft division as competitors without ownership commentary. Reports that Patterson has been taken private could not be verified here and should not be assumed; if accurate, private ownership could support either more aggressive share-taking on price or greater discipline, and the direction matters for U.S. dental pricing.

Competitive position score of 5.0 reflects a defensible level with a deteriorating trajectory in the segment that generates 84% of revenue. Trajectory, not level, drives the score.

## Risk Assessment

**Core margin erosion with negative equipment growth (highest-weighted risk).** GDVAS adjusted margin down 38bp, U.S. dental equipment internal sales -1.1%, consolidated gross margin down 55bp, and a $14M impairment on GDVAS customer relationships explicitly tied to lower-than-anticipated operating margins. Consolidated earnings currently depend on continued outperformance by two segments totalling 15.6% of revenue. If Specialty's one-year margin jump from 13.55% to 17.42% proves partly attributable to TriMed and S.I.N. contributions maturing rather than underlying leverage — the 10-K does not disaggregate — there is little cushion.

**Undisclosed covenant headroom during a leveraging cycle.** All three debt agreements were amended and restated in 2025 to "modify certain financial definitions and covenants": the revolver and term loan on June 6, 2025 (the latter extending maturity from July 2026 to June 2030), and the private placement facilities on December 19, 2025. All require maintenance of "certain maximum leverage ratios." Neither the 10-K nor the 10-Q quantifies the threshold or current headroom. Maturity extensions are prudent; simultaneous definitional changes across three documents in a year when net debt rose roughly $400M and buybacks more than doubled are consistent with creating financing flexibility, though routine conforming amendments (rate-benchmark mechanics, add-back definitions) are an equally plausible explanation. We do not infer management motive. The material finding is the absence of quantified headroom disclosure, which means our 2.84x estimate cannot be reconciled to the contractual calculation — likely favorably to Henry Schein, since credit-agreement EBITDA definitions typically permit more add-backs than ours.

**Redeemable noncontrolling interest puts of $906M.** Carried outside permanent equity at estimated redemption amounts based on recent transactions or implied earnings multiples, with minority holders in several consolidated subsidiaries entitled at certain times to require Henry Schein to purchase their interests at fair value. Internet Brands' interest in Henry Schein One has grown from 26% at formation to 33.6%, with a freestanding put right that ripened after the fifth anniversary. A January 29, 2025 Memorandum of Understanding extended Henry Schein's call trigger to July 1, 2032 and paused Internet Brands' put for four years to January 29, 2029 — signed the same day the KKR investment was announced. The effect was to remove a ripened fair-value put from the near-term balance sheet as an $850M buyback year began. That is an observation about effect, not motive; a mutually negotiated standstill benefiting both parties is equally consistent with the facts. Critically, the filings do not allocate the $906M among subsidiaries — Biotech Dental (57% owned), TriMed (60%), Shield (90%) and others all created redeemable NCI — so the balance must not be equated with the Henry Schein One obligation.

**Goodwill concentration.** $4,213M of goodwill against $3,245M of equity. The January 2025 reporting-unit realignment required goodwill reallocation and testing under both old and new structures; both passed. BDO USA designated valuation of acquired intangibles — revenue growth and discount rate assumptions for certain trade names and customer relationships — as the sole critical audit matter, tied to $392M of FY2025 acquisition consideration.

**Open-ended restructuring.** The 2024 Plan runs to the end of 2027, has cost $178M across FY2024–FY2025 with $48M accrued, and the 10-K states charges for 2026–2027 have not been determined. Investors cannot size the remaining GAAP drag.

**Policy and regulatory load.** OBBBA Medicaid reductions are a direct volume risk to government and home-health channels. Tariffs are disclosed as having increased costs, with Item 1 noting an evolving and litigated legal position that we did not independently verify. Newly effective compliance obligations include the European Health Data Space Regulation, mandatory EUDAMED modules from May 28, 2026, FDA QMSR alignment effective February 2026, DSCSA stabilization through November 2026, and state privacy laws in 20 states.

**Contained items.** Opioid litigation is fewer than ten stayed suits with opioid sales below 0.4% of revenue and an immaterial accrual. The 2023 cyber incident is financially closed: FY2025 expense of $0M and $20M of insurance proceeds completing the $60M policy limit.

**Governance and execution risk from the leadership reset.** Stanley Bergman retired as CEO on March 1, 2026 after roughly 37 years and subsequently as Chairman; Frederick Lowery took over March 2. Three executives with 30+ year tenures — the COO, Chief Strategic Officer, and SVP Global Supply Chain — transition to Senior Advisor roles on October 30, 2026. The Executive Management Committee was succeeded by a "Henry Schein Leadership Team" in July 2026, and the new Executive Change in Control Plan names only three executives against roughly seven previously. A KKR-associated director, William K. Daniel II, is now Chairman. This is a comprehensive reset within five months of the new CEO's arrival. It removes an incumbent team whose pay decoupled from shareholder outcomes; it also removes decades of relationship and supplier-negotiation knowledge in a business whose stated advantage is precisely those relationships. FY2026–FY2027 GDVAS margin is the test.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 27.6% against 25.9% one-year realized is fair, and IV sits at the 20th percentile within the current chain — the post-earnings vol crush is complete (ATM IV fell from 37.9% on July 24 to 27.6%). Term structure is in contango (27.6% at 12 days rising to 32.1% at 103 days), which is the normal, non-stressed configuration and prices more uncertainty around the November print — the quarter that must deliver the seasonally concentrated cash flow the buyback depends on. The 12-day tenor shows an 11.8% put/call skew (elevated put demand) while the 40-day and 103-day tenors show -0.2% and -2.6%; near-dated protection was bought around the earnings event and has not extended out the curve. Volume-based put/call ratios were unavailable. Short interest is 6.9% of float with 5.0 days to cover — meaningful but not crowded, and not a squeeze setup. The net read: the options market prices no near-term dislocation, with the greater implied uncertainty attached to the second-half seasonal delivery. This corroborates rather than challenges a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110, +24.8%)

Value-creation initiatives are working and Q2 was the first evidence: operating margin up 29 basis points, gross margin improving on management's stated "early benefits," internal growth of 4.6% the best in the period, and guidance raised on both sales and EPS. A new CEO with an outside operating background and roughly 50% of his initial equity grant in options struck at $77.60 is compensated only for share-price appreciation, and the Chairman bought 10,000 shares in the open market at $69.19 on May 11, 2026, doubling his position. Roughly $54M of scheduled intangible amortization roll-off converges GAAP toward adjusted earnings by 2030. The KKR structure was accretive: 3,285,151 shares issued at ~$76.10 on May 16, 2025 against 3,491,483 shares retired at an average $71.60 three days later, a net share reduction of 206,332 and roughly $15.7M of value transfer to continuing holders, with pre-existing shareholders retaining approximately 97.4% of the company. Share count is down 13.4% in 30 months with $455M of authorization remaining, and performance-RSU measurement adjusts for repurchases, preventing management from earning vesting by shrinking the denominator. If GDVAS margin returns to 6.5%, that is roughly $43M of incremental operating income on FY2025 revenue, and the multiple re-rates toward the group.

### Bear Case ($71, -19.4%)

GDVAS margin erosion is structural, driven by DSO and GPO buyer power and manufacturer direct-selling that the 10-K discloses as already occurring in specialty and medical. Q2's consolidated improvement was assisted by 1.4 points of currency and by two small segments whose margin gains may partly reflect acquisition maturation rather than operating leverage. U.S. dental equipment at -1.1% signals practice-level capital deferral that persists while financing costs stay elevated; equipment is the highest value-added line. Management's own guide embeds H2 profit deceleration from 12.0% first-half adjusted EBITDA growth to mid-to-high single digits for the year, with no disclosed explanation. Leverage at 2.84x (3.62x including redeemable NCI) with negative $2.0B tangible book, interest expense up 72% in two years, undisclosed covenant headroom after three amendments, and a buyback consuming five times first-half free cash flow leaves no capacity for a demand shock. The Internet Brands royalty renews around 2028 and the paused put ripens January 2029 with the counterparty holding 33.6% of the highest-margin segment. A wholesale departure of executives averaging 30+ years of tenure into a period requiring supplier and DSO renegotiation raises execution risk precisely where the margin problem sits. At roughly 24x forward GAAP with a 3.02% net margin, the shares are priced for the recovery to happen.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing owners should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $88.13.

- **Base case: $95** — 15.8x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $6.00, a modest discount to today's 16.5x on guidance to reflect that FY2027 consensus requires GDVAS margin expansion not yet demonstrated. **+7.8%** from $88.13.
- **Bull case: $110** — 18.3x $6.00, requiring GDVAS adjusted margin above 6.5%, U.S. dental equipment back to growth, and leverage below 2.75x. **+24.8%.**
- **Bear case: $71** — 13.1x flat adjusted EPS of $5.40 with the buyback curtailed by leverage. **-19.4%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 FY2026 report (late October/early November 2026, the first seasonally strong quarter and the test of the guided H2 deceleration) and the Q4/FY2026 report in February 2027 with initial FY2027 guidance.
- Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +4% against 19.4% bear downside gives base-to-bear asymmetry near 0.4:1 — adequate for holders, insufficient for new purchase.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. GDVAS adjusted operating margin at or above 6.49% (the FY2024 level) for two consecutive quarters, disclosed in segment reporting — the direct test of whether the core erosion has reversed.
2. U.S. dental equipment internal sales returning to positive growth alongside net debt / adjusted EBITDA declining below 2.70x on a company-defined or reconcilable basis.

## Conclusion

Henry Schein has produced four consecutive earnings beats, raised FY2026 guidance twice on the non-GAAP EPS line, expanded operating margin 29 basis points in Q2, and installed an entirely new leadership team with equity compensation weighted toward share-price appreciation. Those facts justify a modest improvement in our assessment from the prior review and argue against treating the stock as impaired.

They do not make it a purchase at $88.13. The company earns a 4.95% operating margin on 4.6% organic growth, and 84% of revenue sits in a segment that lost 38 basis points of margin last year while the two segments carrying the earnings improvement account for 15.6% of sales. Net debt rose $354M in six months to fund $325M of repurchase against roughly $60M of first-half free cash flow, tangible book is negative $2.0B, covenant headroom is undisclosed after three 2025 amendments, and $906M of redeemable minority puts sit outside permanent equity with the largest identifiable counterparty also licensing the intellectual property behind the highest-margin segment on an agreement expiring around 2028. Management's own guidance implies second-half profit growth decelerating from 12.0% to mid-to-high single digits, with no disclosed explanation.

On matched accounting bases the shares are not cheap: roughly 24x forward GAAP earnings and 11.3x EV to TTM adjusted EBITDA, versus Cencora at 21.6x and McKesson at 20.5x trailing. A base case of $95 against a $71 bear, from a price 4.4% below the 52-week high and 42% above the October low, is not the asymmetry that warrants new capital. **HOLD.** Own it through the October print; buy the $78–$80 range or two quarters of GDVAS margin stabilisation, whichever comes first.
