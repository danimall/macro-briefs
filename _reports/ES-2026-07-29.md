**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eversource Energy is a pure-play regulated transmission-and-distribution utility serving ~4.6 million electric, gas and water customers across Connecticut, Massachusetts and New Hampshire. It carries no unregulated generation, so earnings are a function of rate base multiplied by allowed returns, with decoupling in CT and MA removing volume risk. The business is defensively boring by design; the investment question is entirely about regulatory outcomes, the balance sheet, and price.

At $75.18, ES trades at roughly 16.2x the midpoint of 2026 non-GAAP guidance ($4.65) with a 4.2% dividend yield. That is a discount to the electric-utility peer median of 22.1x P/E and above the peer median 3.4% yield, and the discount is deserved rather than opportunistic. Three overhangs explain it: (1) the March 2026 FERC Opinion No. 594 cutting the New England transmission owners' base ROE to 9.57% from 11.14%, with a reasonably-possible retroactive refund range of $60.4M to $932M and a ~$70M annual prospective after-tax hit; (2) a bottom-of-investment-grade credit profile (senior unsecured ~BBB+/Baa1, new junior subordinated notes at BBB-/Baa3/BB+) against an ~$18B+ multi-year capital plan that requires continuous equity and debt issuance; and (3) chronic Connecticut regulatory hostility, with a $2.1B unrecovered storm-cost regulatory asset and a CL&P prudency review pending. The Aquarion water sale ($1.6B equity proceeds earmarked for parent debt paydown) is a genuine de-levering catalyst, but it is still contested in the courts.

We maintain a HOLD, classified as an income hold. The 4.2% covered dividend plus 5-7% guided long-term EPS growth is a reasonable total-return package for a defensive allocation, but there is no asymmetric entry here. Estimate revisions are broadly negative across every window, the company has missed EPS in four consecutive quarters, insiders are net sellers into strength, and the stock sits within 2% of its 52-week high. The prior report trajectory (5.6 → 5.3 → 5.1) reflects a steady deterioration in Financial Health and Management Quality that the current data confirms rather than reverses. We would turn constructive on a clean Aquarion close and a favorable FERC Section 205 outcome, or on a pullback toward the mid-$60s where the yield and growth math improve.

## Company Overview

Eversource is a regulated utility holding company operating twelve regulated subsidiaries organized into four reportable segments. In Q1 2026 the segment net-income contribution was: Electric Distribution ~$203M (CL&P in Connecticut, NSTAR Electric in Massachusetts, PSNH in New Hampshire); Electric Transmission ~$224M ex-charges (FERC-regulated, the highest-return growth engine); Natural Gas Distribution ~$295M (Yankee Gas, NSTAR Gas, EGMA); and Water Distribution ~$6M (Aquarion, being sold).

The competitive structure is a regulated service-territory monopoly. Returns are set by state regulators (PURA in Connecticut, DPU in Massachusetts, NHPUC in New Hampshire) on distribution, and by FERC on transmission. The parent has no revenue-generating operations and depends entirely on subsidiary dividends and capital-markets access to service its obligations and fund the dividend. The competitive advantages are a large and growing transmission rate base under FERC-formula ratemaking, revenue decoupling in CT and MA (breaking the link between sales volumes and distribution revenue), and regional scale in a high-cost, high-electrification market. The chief structural weakness is concentration in Connecticut, among the most adversarial regulatory jurisdictions in the country.

The 2024 exit from offshore wind (Sunrise sold to Ørsted; South Fork and Revolution Wind 50% stakes to Global Infrastructure Partners for ~$745M gross, at a $524M after-tax loss) completed a strategic retreat to a T&D pure-play, but left a shrinking contingent liability tail that is still resolving.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026.** Net income to common was $606.8M ($1.61 diluted), up 10% from $550.8M ($1.50) a year earlier, inclusive of a $43.9M ($0.12/share) after-tax charge for the FERC ROE decision; non-GAAP EPS was $1.73. Operating revenue rose 9.4% to $4.50B, driven by base-rate increases across all utilities plus higher pass-through supply and transmission costs that are largely earnings-neutral. Segment growth was broad-based: Natural Gas +$76.9M on major rate cases effective November 2025, Electric Distribution +$14.4M, and Transmission +$24.9M ex-charge.

**Balance sheet.** This is the central issue. Total assets are $64.7B against long-term debt of $26.9B, current long-term debt of $2.6B, notes payable of $0.5B, and common equity of $16.5B. Book debt/equity of ~1.8x is at the elevated end even for a capital-intensive T&D utility. The parent's credit ratings sit at the bottom of investment grade, and the new junior subordinated notes rated BBB-/Baa3/BB+ are one notch into high-yield territory at one agency. An Altman Z-Score in the "distress" zone is a known false positive for regulated utilities (the model penalizes structurally normal leverage and low asset turnover), but the underlying credit tightness is real: management is issuing equity via an ATM program and expensive junior subordinated debt (6.10%/6.35% in February 2026) specifically to defend Baa-level ratings while funding capex.

**Cash flow.** Free cash flow is structurally negative. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $1.32B against capex of $1.01B, but the ~$18B+ multi-year capital plan and the ~$4B+/year run-rate mean continuous external financing. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a direct headwind: rising rates lift the cost of both the debt and the equity that fund the plan (Q1 interest expense rose $64.5M YoY), and the equity dilution (7.1M ATM shares issued in 2025 for $465M, taking share count from 367M to 376M) mechanically caps per-share growth.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** The company has missed consensus EPS in all four of the last four quarters, including a sharp -15.9% miss in Q3 2025. The magnitude of the recent misses is modest (-2.4% to -2.5% in the last three quarters), but the track record undermines confidence in near-term estimates. Decoupling improves underlying earnings quality relative to the volatile GAAP headline, since regulatory decisions rather than the economy are the swing factors, but it also means the FERC and PURA outcomes are the entire story.

## Growth Analysis

Management guides to 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $4.57-$4.72 (midpoint $4.65), revised down from prior expectations to reflect the FERC ROE cut, and to long-term 5-7% EPS growth through 2030, weighted toward the upper half by 2028. The growth engine is rate base: transmission (FERC-formula), aging distribution and gas infrastructure replacement, and named projects such as the ~$1.84B Greater Cambridge Energy Program (in service 2029-2031).

Secular tailwinds are real. Massachusetts Executive Order 654 (March 2026) targets 10 GW of new energy and 5 GW of storage by 2035, directing accelerated interconnection and grid modernization, and explicitly cites projected load growth. Electrification, EV adoption, and data-center demand are supportive to rate base over a multi-year horizon.

The estimate revisions do not confirm the growth narrative. Current-year consensus has drifted from $4.71 to $4.65 (-1.4% over 90 days), next-year from $5.01 to $4.92 (-1.8%), and current-quarter estimates have fallen 6.3% in the last 30 days alone. Broadly negative revisions across every window are a warning that near-term earnings momentum is deteriorating, not accelerating. The 5-7% growth rate is achievable but leans on continued rate-base additions funded by dilutive external capital, and it does not justify re-rating the multiple. This is a mid-single-digit compounder.

## Valuation Assessment

At $75.18, ES trades at ~16.2x the 2026 guidance midpoint and ~16.5x trailing EPS of $4.56, with a 4.2% dividend yield. Against the electric-utility peer set — FE at 25.4x, PPL at 21.7x, EVRG at 22.1x, WEC at 22.5x, and EIX at 7.9x (the latter distorted by its own California wildfire liabilities) — the peer median is 22.1x P/E and 3.4% yield. ES trades at roughly a 27% discount to the peer median multiple and offers a higher yield.

The instinct to read that discount as opportunity should be resisted. A compressed multiple that reflects specific, identifiable overhangs is the market pricing risk. Here the overhangs are concrete: the FERC ROE cut permanently lowers the return on the highest-margin segment, the retroactive-refund tail is genuinely open, the credit profile is stressed and requires dilutive funding, and Connecticut remains hostile. Each of these is a reason the market assigns ES a lower multiple than WEC or PPL, and none is resolved. The discount is deserved, which places the valuation score at neutral rather than cheap.

There is a two-sided element that most screens miss. Eversource's Section 205 filing proposes a new 11.39% base ROE effective ~June 2026; if it succeeds, the prospective transmission ROE could end up above the current 10.57% billed rate, a positive surprise. Conversely, if the D.C. Circuit stay fails and the ~12-year retroactive refund period stands, the hit dwarfs the accrual. That two-sidedness, combined with a fair 16x multiple on decelerating estimates, argues for a valuation score near the midpoint. The analyst price-target dispersion (low $52, high $85, median $75, mean $74) brackets the current price almost exactly, confirming there is no consensus mispricing to exploit; the stock trades essentially at fair value with a wide risk range.

## Competitive Landscape

Eversource competes for capital, not customers, and its position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the margin. The positive structural forces are decoupling (which insulates distribution earnings from volumes and mild recession), FERC-formula transmission ratemaking, and regional load-growth optionality from electrification and data centers.

The negative competitive dynamics are sector-wide and hit Eversource disproportionately. FERC transmission ROE compression via Opinion No. 594 uses a methodology that also hit Avangrid and other New England transmission owners and mirrors the 2024 MISO remand, producing a structurally lower FERC ROE regime across the peer group but one that bites harder for an unusually transmission-heavy company. FERC's push for competitive transmission solicitations (ISO-NE LTTP) threatens the traditional incumbent cost-of-service model, with future projects potentially facing cost caps or bid-down ROEs. New England's affordability crisis — among the highest electricity and gas bills in the country, driven by pipeline constraints and clean-energy mandates — fuels the political and regulatory pressure that manifests as tougher prudency reviews and rate-case disallowances, concentrated in Connecticut.

The prior report's Competitive Position score of 6.0 (down from 7.0) captured the FERC compression. The current read holds that direction: the moat is intact (regulated monopoly) but the return on the moat is being squeezed by regulators, and the competitive-transmission threat is a longer-term erosion vector. We mark this at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**FERC ROE decision (dominant, newly modified risk factor).** Opinion No. 594 applied both to the complaint period and retroactively from October 2014, with ~$871M of the reasonably-possible range attributable to the retroactive period. Eversource is contesting on multiple fronts (rehearing request, D.C. Circuit stay motion with Avangrid, and a Section 205 filing at 11.39%). This is a two-sided binary with genuinely uncertain timing and outcome.

**Offshore wind contingent liability (shrinking legacy tail).** The Revolution Wind contingent liability fell to $298.2M at 3/31/26 from $448.2M at year-end 2025 after $150M of payments. Revolution Wind absorbed two federal BOEM stop-work orders (August-September 2025 and December 2025-January 2026); a federal judge cleared resumption in January 2026, and a Q3 2025 $284M pre-tax charge already flowed through. The purchase price assumed a 40% ITC level; downward changes in ITC eligibility or further construction delays could force additional charges. Total guarantee/indemnification exposure is disclosed up to ~$1.62B plus $300M in RW tax positions, largely expected to roll off as construction completes. The late-2026 purchase-price-adjustment settlement with GIP should cap this exposure.

**Connecticut regulatory hostility and storm costs.** The $2.1B net storm-cost regulatory asset (CL&P ~$1.03B) plus ~$978M of 2018-2023 CL&P storm costs under a PURA prudency review (decision expected ~July 29, 2026) create disallowance risk. Storm-cost securitization in New Hampshire (April 2026) and exploration of RRBs in Connecticut would ease cash flow if approved.

**Structural/financial.** Bottom-of-IG ratings against the capex plan, structurally negative FCF requiring continuous dilution, rising interest expense, pension exposure, and physical climate risk in a storm-prone territory.

**Aquarion sale (two-sided catalyst).** The ~$2.4B enterprise-value sale to the Aquarion Water Authority was denied by PURA (November 2025), remanded by the CT Superior Court (January 2026), then reversed and approved by PURA (March 25, 2026) with conditions. Legal appeals still create closing uncertainty; the company has not met held-for-sale criteria and warns a loss could be recognized at reclassification/close. The ~$1.6B of equity proceeds directed at parent debt paydown would meaningfully help the credit profile; a clean close is a positive credit and sentiment catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is muted and slightly cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 21.0% sits below one-year historical volatility of 25.3% (a -4.3% spread, 17th percentile within the current chain), indicating the market is not pricing an imminent large move and that options are relatively cheap. The put/call skew of 4.0% at the front month reflects modestly elevated put demand, but this fades to slightly negative skew (-2.1%) at the six-month tenor, and the term structure is essentially flat. The collapse in the delta snapshot (ATM IV 472% → 21%) reflects a prior stale/corrupted reading normalizing rather than a genuine sentiment shift; the P/C OI ratio of 0.24 is benign. Net read: the options market sees ES as a low-volatility, fairly-valued utility with mild near-term hedging demand tied to the July prudency-review and pending FERC/Aquarion events, consistent with a HOLD rather than a directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$88, +17%).** Aquarion closes cleanly, proceeds pay down parent debt, and the credit profile stabilizes at or near BBB+/Baa1. The FERC Section 205 filing secures the 11.39% base ROE, turning the transmission overhang into a modest positive versus the current billed rate. The CL&P storm-cost prudency review results in minimal disallowance, and the offshore-wind purchase-price adjustment settles at the low end. EPS delivers toward the upper half of the 5-7% range by 2028, the multiple re-rates toward the ~20x peer level on de-risking, and the yield plus growth compound. This maps to roughly 18-19x forward EPS approaching $5.00.

**Bear case (~$60, -20%).** The D.C. Circuit stay fails and the ~12-year retroactive refund stands, forcing a charge well above the accrual toward the reasonably-possible high end. A rating agency downgrades the parent below investment grade at one or more agencies, raising financing costs on a company that must continuously tap capital markets. The CL&P prudency review disallows a meaningful portion of unrecovered storm costs, and higher-for-longer rates compress the equity multiple on a negative-FCF, dilutive funding model. This maps to ~13x on flat-to-down EPS near the 52-week low.

**Base case (~$78, +4%).** Aquarion closes with some delay, the FERC outcome nets to a modest prospective drag with the retroactive tail settling below the high end, EPS grows ~5% off the $4.65 midpoint, and the multiple holds around 16x. Total return is dominated by the 4.2% dividend plus low-to-mid-single-digit EPS growth, with the multiple range-bound as overhangs resolve slowly. This is the most probable path given decelerating estimates and unresolved binaries.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The recommendation rests on the ~4.2% covered dividend plus 5-7% guided EPS growth as a reasonable defensive total-return package, not on appreciation to a specific target near the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A clean Aquarion close with proceeds applied to parent debt paydown and confirmed credit-profile stabilization (no negative rating action).
- A favorable FERC Section 205 outcome (base ROE at or near 11.39%) or a D.C. Circuit stay that removes the retroactive-refund tail, combined with positive estimate-revision velocity.
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s that widens the yield toward ~4.8% and improves the risk/reward on the same fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A parent credit downgrade below investment grade at one or more agencies, or a forced acceleration of ATM issuance that materially increases dilution.
- A D.C. Circuit stay denial with the ~12-year retroactive refund period upheld, forcing a charge toward the $932M high end.
- A material CL&P storm-cost disallowance in the July 2026 prudency review combined with continued negative estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Near-term inputs cluster over the next several months: the CL&P prudency-review decision (expected ~July 29, 2026), FERC rehearing/Section 205 developments through mid-2026, and the offshore-wind purchase-price-adjustment settlement in late 2026. The next quarterly earnings release and any Aquarion appellate ruling are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

Eversource is a well-run but constrained regulated utility whose equity is capped by three unresolved overhangs: the FERC transmission ROE cut with an open retroactive-refund tail, a bottom-of-investment-grade balance sheet that demands continuous dilutive funding against the capex plan, and chronic Connecticut regulatory friction. The 27% valuation discount to utility peers is a fair reflection of those risks rather than a mispricing to exploit. The 4.2% covered dividend plus 5-7% guided growth justifies holding for income, but four consecutive earnings misses, broadly negative estimate revisions, net insider selling into strength, and a price within 2% of the 52-week high argue against upgrading. The declining score trajectory (5.6 → 5.3 → 5.1) is confirmed, not reversed, by the current data. We rate ES a HOLD (income) and would revisit constructively on a clean Aquarion close, a favorable FERC resolution, or a pullback toward the mid-$60s.
