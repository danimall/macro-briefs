**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Encore Capital Group is the leading US and one of the largest European purchasers of defaulted consumer receivables. After two consecutive years of net losses ($206.5M in 2023, $139.2M in 2024) driven mostly by goodwill impairments at its European Cabot unit, the company swung to a $256.8M net profit in FY2025 on record US collections, a $197.8M collections over-performance versus forecast, and the elimination of goodwill impairment. The turnaround carried into Q1 2026, where diluted EPS of $3.86 beat consensus of roughly $3.05 by about 27% and management raised its lifetime recovery forecasts for recent vintages by $16.7M. This is the fourth consecutive large earnings beat (surprises of +72%, +68%, +57%, +27% across the last four quarters), and 90-day consensus EPS revisions for the current and next quarter are up roughly 50% and 43%.

The stock has re-rated from the high-$30s in mid-2025 to ~$83 today, roughly a double, and that prior appreciation is sometimes cited as a reason for caution. Prior appreciation is not a valuation input. What matters is the current price against forward fundamentals, and on that basis ECPG trades at ~6.4x forward earnings — well below the consumer-finance peer median P/E of 16.6x and implying a PEG far below 1.0 given the earnings inflection. The entire analyst target range of $105-120 sits 26-45% above spot. This is the inflection archetype the market systematically under-rewards: rising estimates, four consecutive beats, a revenue and net-income inflection, and a single-digit forward multiple.

The reason ECPG is not scored higher is the structural earnings volatility of the business. Under CECL, quarterly reassessment of expected lifetime recoveries ("changes in recoveries") can swing reported revenue by hundreds of millions of dollars, and the 2025 result was flattered by a large favorable swing in that line. The company also carries ~$4.0B of debt with coupons up to 9.25% and a 2028-2031 maturity wall. These risks are partly offset by management actively re-underwriting recovery forecasts upward, terming out debt at lower rates, and a favorable US supply/pricing backdrop. The weight of estimate velocity, beat magnitude, a single-digit forward multiple, and analyst-target asymmetry points to BUY, sized for the CECL volatility tail.

## Company Overview

Encore buys portfolios of charged-off consumer receivables, predominantly defaulted credit-card debt, at deep discounts to face value and recovers them via call-center/digital channels, the legal/litigation channel, and third-party agencies. The business operates as one reportable segment through two principal units:

- **MCM (United States):** the US market leader, contributing roughly 75% of collections ($1,949M in 2025, +24% YoY). Supply is described as "robust" and pricing "favorable," sourced largely through multi-year forward-flow contracts with major card issuers.
- **Cabot (Europe/UK):** one of Europe's largest credit-management providers, also running fee-based servicing. European pricing is competitive enough that management is deliberately shrinking deployment there (Europe purchases fell to $234M).
- **LAAP** (Latin America / Asia-Pacific): immaterial.

The competitive moat rests on three pillars: proprietary account-level valuation and behavioral analytics built on a large historical-collections dataset; regulatory scale, where rising compliance costs squeeze smaller competitors and issuers concentrate sales among a few certified "trusted buyers"; and deep forward-flow relationships. The company employs roughly 7,350 people, 79% internationally, including lower-cost operations centers in India and Costa Rica.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a sharp turnaround year:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $1,222.7M | $1,316.4M | **$1,768.8M** (+34%) |
| Net income (loss) | $(206.5)M | $(139.2)M | **$256.8M** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $333.6M | $332.9M | **$677.0M** |
| Goodwill impairment | $238.2M | $100.6M | **$0** |
| Total collections | $1,862.6M | $2,162.5M | **$2,592.8M** (+20%) |
| Operating cash flow | $153.0M | $156.2M | $153.2M |

Three drivers explain the recovery: record US collections on a larger, higher-quality receivable base; $197.8M of collections over-performance versus forecast (up from $78.2M in 2024), attributed to new digital/AI collection tools expanding the payer book; and the absence of goodwill impairment versus $338.8M cumulative Cabot impairments in 2023-2024.

**On operating cash flow.** OCF held essentially flat near $153M across all three years, and this deserves direct treatment because it is easy to misread as weak cash generation. It is not. Flat OCF coincided with record gross portfolio deployment — $1,408M of purchases, with US purchases rising to $1,174M. For a debt purchaser, operating cash flow is reported net of cash used to acquire new receivable portfolios; a scaling book that reinvests its collections into larger forward-flow commitments will show flat or muted OCF even as the underlying earning asset grows. The relevant visibility metric is estimated remaining collections (ERC) of $9.66B on a 180-month basis ($8.26B on 84 months), against a net receivable book of $4.37B at year-end 2025 ($4.44B at Q1 2026) — a collections-to-purchase multiple of roughly 2.5x. Flat OCF during a period of record gross deployment is the expected signature of a debt purchaser growing its book.

**Q1 2026 confirmed momentum:** revenue $475.4M (+21%), net income $86.2M (versus $46.8M), diluted EPS $3.86. Collections were $718.4M (+19%), US over-performed forecast by $46M, and management recorded a +$16.7M positive change in expected future recoveries, raising lifetime forecasts for recent vintages rather than simply booking timing favorability.

**Balance sheet and capital structure.** Total debt is ~$4.0B against equity of ~$1.0B (D/E ~4.5x). This leverage is structural to the debt-purchasing model; the relevant question is serviceability and refinancing, not the ratio itself. Interest expense was $293.9M in 2025 (up from $252.5M), a genuine headwind, but management is terming out debt at lower rates: the Global Senior Facility now carries 5.53% versus 6.56% a year ago, the company issued $500M of 6.625% 2031 notes in October 2025 to retire higher-cost and floating-rate paper, and in April 2026 noticed €200M of €415M 2028 floating-rate notes for redemption. Covenants (LTV ≤0.75, fixed-charge coverage ≥2.0) are met, and $791M of revolver capacity is available. ROE reached 26.3% and ROIC recovered to 13.7% from ~7% in the loss years.

A note on screening flags: the Altman Z-Score of 0.79 ("distress zone") is a false signal. It reflects the inherently leveraged business model and an XBRL artifact that read revenue as only the $88M servicing line rather than the $1.77B total. Operating cash flow is steadily positive, covenants are met, and revolver capacity is ample. The real risks here are earnings volatility and refinancing, not insolvency.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a confirmed earnings inflection rather than a single-quarter pop. Revenue grew 34% in 2025 and 21% in Q1 2026; net income swung from a $139.2M loss to $256.8M; and the company has now beaten consensus four straight quarters by 26.5%, 57.5%, 68.5%, and 72.2%. Consensus quarterly EPS estimates were revised up 49.9% (current quarter) and 42.6% (next quarter) over 90 days; current-year and next-year estimates rose 7.7% and 7.5%. The combination of revenue growth above 30%, an earnings recovery from a trough, and 90-day estimate revisions of this magnitude is among the more reliable forward signals in the data, and it applies here because the forward multiple is single-digit rather than premium — the multiple does not already price in continued beats.

The growth engine is US supply. Elevated lending and charge-off rates sustain robust portfolio availability at favorable pricing, and Encore is deploying into it ($1,174M of US purchases). The $9.66B ERC provides multi-year cash-flow visibility, and the durability question — whether the digital/AI-driven collections over-performance is structural or a pull-forward — was answered constructively in Q1 2026 when management raised forward-recovery forecasts rather than treating the gains as timing-related. Europe is the offset: low charge-off rates and competitive pricing have led Encore to pull back deployment, so near-term growth is a US story with a European drag.

## Valuation Assessment

ECPG trades at roughly 6.4x forward earnings (trailing P/E 6.43x on $10.91 EPS; consensus current-year EPS of $13.00 and next-year $13.82 imply a forward multiple in the low-6x range). Against the consumer-finance peer median P/E of 16.6x (EZPW 16.6x, PRG 12.0x, WRLD 25.7x; PRAA and NAVI are loss-making and unmeaningful), ECPG sits at roughly 40% of the peer median while delivering the strongest earnings inflection in the group.

Analyst targets function as floors rather than ceilings here. The stock is cheap relative to rising estimates (forward P/E well under 30x, PEG far below 1.5), so consensus targets lag reality and tend to be revised up as results compound. The entire target range of $105-120 (mean $111, median $108) sits 26-45% above the current $83.05, with a 3-buy/1-hold analyst skew. On the four-beat track record and the upward revision trend, the asymmetry favors the long side: a re-rating from 6.4x toward even 9-10x forward, holding estimates flat, implies a price in the $117-130 range, before any further estimate increases.

The valuation discount is not free. It reflects (1) the volatility of CECL "changes in recoveries," which can swing earnings violently in either direction; (2) leverage and the 2028-2031 maturity wall; and (3) residual European/Cabot impairment risk. A debt purchaser with this earnings profile should trade at a discount to a stable consumer lender. But a roughly 60% discount to the peer median, on rising estimates and a four-beat streak, overcompensates for those risks. The cheapness here is paired with a specific, identifiable catalyst set — continued US over-performance, falling interest expense from refinancing, and ongoing buybacks — rather than being a static low multiple, which is what distinguishes a mispricing from a value trap.

## Competitive Landscape

The charged-off-receivables market is competitive, with PRA Group the most direct peer. The structural dynamic favors scaled players: rising regulatory and compliance costs, plus a higher cost of capital, squeeze smaller buyers, while issuers concentrate sales among a few certified buyers via multi-year forward flows. Encore's proprietary analytics and historical-collections dataset improve pricing discipline and recovery rates, and its US market leadership gives it first call on robust domestic supply.

The trajectory is mixed by geography. In the US, Encore is gaining share of a growing, favorably priced supply pool, an improving position. In Europe, the company is ceding deployment because pricing is unattractive, which is a defensive rather than offensive posture and the source of the cumulative $338.8M Cabot impairment. The blended picture is a strengthening core franchise with a structurally weaker European tail that management is shrinking deliberately. Cost inflation in the legal collection channel (court costs +25%, legal collection costs +21.7% in 2025) is a margin headwind that affects the whole industry and is not unique to Encore.

## Risk Assessment

**CECL "changes in recoveries" volatility (primary risk).** Quarterly reassessment of expected lifetime recoveries flows directly to revenue and earnings. This line swung favorably in 2025, and a downward re-forecast in any future quarter would hit reported earnings hard. The risk is genuine and the source of the discounted multiple. It must be weighed symmetrically: management raised forward-recovery forecasts in Q1 2026, the US over-performance has now persisted across multiple quarters, and the favorable swing reflects re-underwriting recent vintages upward rather than booking one-off timing gains. The bear framing that the favorable swing will reverse violently is one scenario; the management signal and US backdrop argue the recovery has a structural component. (A note on source consistency: the magnitude of the FY2025 changes-in-recoveries swing was cited inconsistently in the underlying materials — both a smaller and a larger figure appear. Either way, the directional point holds: a meaningful portion of the 2025 GAAP recovery came from this line and from the absence of impairment, so earnings are more volatile than cash generation, and forward earnings carry this two-way risk.)

**Leverage and refinancing.** Covenants are met and the active terming-out at lower rates is an earnings tailwind, but a sharp rise in credit spreads or a tightening in the higher-for-longer rate path would raise refinancing cost.

**European/Cabot impairment.** $529M of goodwill remains after the cumulative impairments; further write-downs are possible if European performance lags.

**Regulatory intensity.** CFPB/FDCPA/Regulation F (US), FCA Consumer Duty (UK), and the EU NPL Directive. A legacy 2020 CFPB Stipulated Judgment expired in October 2025, removing one overhang, but the sector remains regulation-heavy.

**Convertible dynamics.** The $230M 2029 converts have an effective conversion price of $82.69, essentially at the current share price; above this, cash-settlement/dilution mechanics become relevant, though the capped call mitigates dilution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 35.5% against 34.0% one-year historical volatility — fairly priced, with IV at the 3rd percentile of its own chain, indicating no elevated event premium. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%) and the term structure is in normal contango (35.5% near versus 39.3% longer-dated). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.2%. The reported jump in ATM IV to 439% in the options-delta feed is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Short interest is 8.2% of float with 4.2 days to cover — moderate, not a crowded short, and not a squeeze setup. The options market is not pricing distress or an imminent catalyst, consistent with a fundamental thesis playing out over earnings cycles rather than a single binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($117-130+).** US charge-off supply stays robust and favorably priced, collections continue to over-perform forecast, and management keeps re-underwriting recent vintages upward, confirming the over-performance is structural. Interest expense declines as floating-rate and high-coupon paper is refinanced lower. Buybacks (~$282M authorization remaining) shrink the share count further on a single-digit multiple. Estimates continue to revise up, and the multiple re-rates from 6.4x toward 9-10x forward, putting the stock in the $117-130 range, in line with the $105-120 analyst target range plus modest estimate growth.

**Bear case ($55-65).** A downward CECL re-forecast reverses a meaningful portion of the 2025 earnings recovery; European pricing deteriorates further and triggers additional Cabot goodwill impairment; refinancing costs rise in a higher-for-longer environment, compressing net margins. In that scenario, the stock de-rates back toward where it traded before the inflection (~$55) and the cheap multiple proves to be a correct discount for an earnings stream that cannot be relied upon. This is roughly a 22-34% loss from current levels.

The asymmetry favors the bull case. At 6.4x forward earnings, the market is already pricing substantial skepticism about the durability of the recovery. A four-quarter beat streak, +40-50% 90-day estimate revisions, a $9.66B ERC backstop, management's affirmative upward re-forecast, and improving capital structure constitute specific, falsifiable evidence that the inflection is real. The remaining genuine uncertainty is the magnitude and direction of future CECL swings, which is why this is a sized BUY rather than a high-conviction one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $108 (≈30% upside).** This applies ~8.3x forward earnings to the $13.00 current-year consensus EPS — a multiple that remains a discount to the peer median but credits the demonstrated inflection. It aligns with the analyst median target and sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $130 (≈57% upside).** ~9.5x forward on consensus EPS approaching $13.82 next year, plus the effect of continued buybacks on share count.

**Bear case target: $60 (≈28% downside).** A CECL re-forecast and/or Cabot impairment de-rates the earnings stream and the multiple back toward pre-inflection levels.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two to three quarterly earnings releases (the next report following the May 6, 2026 print), each of which will test whether US over-performance and the upward recovery re-forecasts persist.

**Upside/downside from current ($83.05):** +30% to base, +57% to bull, −28% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. A negative quarterly "changes in recoveries" adjustment that reverses the prior favorable swing, or management lowering forward-recovery forecasts for recent vintages.
2. Additional Cabot/European goodwill impairment, signaling the European drag is worsening rather than being managed down.
3. US collections falling below forecast for two consecutive quarters, or a deceleration in revenue growth below ~10% YoY, indicating the supply/over-performance engine is fading.

## Conclusion

Encore Capital is an earnings inflection trading at a single-digit forward multiple. The swing from two years of losses to a $256.8M profit, four consecutive large beats, +40-50% 90-day estimate revisions, a $9.66B ERC backstop, and management's decision to raise forward-recovery forecasts together constitute the inflection profile the market reliably under-rewards. The 6.4x forward multiple against a 16.6x peer median, with an analyst target range 26-45% above spot, reflects a discount that overcompensates for the real risks — CECL earnings volatility, leverage, and European weakness. Those risks justify disciplined position sizing and warrant a moderate risk score, but they do not justify passing on a cheap, improving, cash-generative franchise with a favorable US supply backdrop and an improving capital structure. **Recommendation: BUY**, with sizing calibrated to the CECL volatility tail.
