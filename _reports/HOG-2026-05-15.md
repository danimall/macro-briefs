## Executive Summary

Harley-Davidson is a premium motorcycle manufacturer undergoing a severe cyclical and structural downturn. Revenue declined 26% from 2023 to 2025, motorcycle shipments fell 31%, and the core HDMC segment reported an operating loss in 2025. The new CEO (Artie Starrs, since October 2025) announced the "Back to the Bricks" strategy in May 2026 targeting affordable models and $350M+ HDMC EBITDA by 2027, but execution evidence is zero at this stage.

The balance sheet improved materially through the KKR/PIMCO receivables transaction, reducing debt from $7B to $3B. However, this permanently lowers HDFS earnings power from ~$250M normalized to a $45-60M run-rate in 2026. The stock trades at ~$25.24, roughly at tangible book value ($3.14B equity vs. ~$2.7B market cap), which appears cheap in absolute terms but reflects the market's correct assessment that near-term earnings are negligible. Full-year 2026 EPS consensus is $0.39 — a forward P/E of ~65x on current-year earnings. Only next year's estimate of $1.82 generates the 13x forward multiple visible in screening data, and that estimate requires a dramatic turnaround that hasn't begun.

The company faces compounding headwinds: shrinking total addressable market, $75-90M annual tariff costs against near-zero operating income, 17.7% short interest, S&P CreditWatch Negative status, and a LiveWire subsidiary consuming $70-80M annually with 653 units shipped. While CEO insider buying at $19 is a genuine conviction signal, the strategic plan's earliest measurable deliverables (Sportster at ~$10K, cost savings) won't appear until 2027. No specific near-term catalyst exists to reverse the stock's trajectory within a 12-month investment horizon.

## Company Overview

Harley-Davidson operates through three segments. HDMC (motorcycles, parts, apparel) generated $3.58B in 2025 revenue across 124,477 wholesale shipments through 1,174 dealership points globally. HDFS (financial services) underwent a transformative transaction in H2 2025, selling ~$4.1B of retail receivables to KKR and PIMCO, transitioning to a capital-light servicing model. LiveWire (electric motorcycles) shipped 653 units for $25.7M revenue and a $75M operating loss.

The company holds 34.5% of U.S. 601+cc motorcycle registrations (down from 37.3% in 2024) and 3.4% in Europe (down from 5.0%). Products concentrate in Grand American Touring (55.6% of shipments) and Cruiser (33.1%).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

The trajectory is unambiguous: revenue from $4.93B (2022) to $3.60B (2025), a 27% decline. Motorcycle shipments dropped from ~180K to 124,477 over the same period. Retail sales declined 12.4% in 2025 on top of 7.1% in 2024, confirming underlying demand is contracting, not merely reflecting production alignment.

HDMC operating margin compressed from 13.6% (2023) to an operating loss of $28.7M in 2025 (-0.8% margin). The negative manufacturing leverage is severe: $149M in unfavorable cost variances from lower production volumes. EPS fell from $4.87 (2023) to $2.78 (2025), with Q4 2025 posting a -$2.44 loss (driven by restructuring charges related to the HDFS transaction).

For 2026, management guided HDMC operating income of -$40M to +$10M, LiveWire losses of $70-80M, and HDFS income of $45-60M. Consolidated operating income is roughly -$65M to -$10M — the company is guiding to breakeven at best.

### Balance Sheet

The HDFS restructuring improved the balance sheet considerably: total assets shrank from $11.9B to $8.0B, cash rose to $3.1B, and total debt dropped from $7.0B to $3.0B. D/E remains at 2.56x but is more manageable. S&P placed the company on CreditWatch Negative; Moody's (Baa3) and Fitch (BBB+) remain stable. Any downgrade below investment grade would raise funding costs materially.

### Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

FCF was $415M in 2025 ($569M operating CF minus $154M capex), representing 11.5% FCF margin — healthy relative to the operational distress. The company repurchased $347M in shares in 2025 and ~$250M in H2 2024, but buybacks are now paused pending the strategic plan. Dividends of $0.72/share ($86M annual) provide a ~2.9% yield.

### Q1 2026 Results

Net income of $25M (vs. $133M year-ago). Revenue beat estimates, but $45M in tariff costs compressed earnings. EPS of $0.30 beat the $0.27 estimate by 11.7%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are severely impaired on a 12-month horizon:

**Volume:** Guidance for 130-135K shipments in 2026 is a slight increase from 2025's 124K. No growth catalyst materializes until the Sportster reintroduction in 2027.

**Market:** Total U.S. 601+cc registrations declined from 256K (2023) to 238K (2025). European registrations in the segment fell 16.4% in 2025. The TAM is shrinking.

**Demographics:** Harley's core customer (50+ year-old males) is aging out. The "Back to the Bricks" strategy explicitly acknowledges this problem but its affordable-model solution won't deliver measurable results until late 2027.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 36-41% over 90 days (from $0.29 to $0.39), but this is from an extremely depressed base — the absolute number ($0.39) implies the company is barely profitable. Next-quarter estimates have been cut 35% over 90 days ($0.71 to $0.46), suggesting deteriorating near-term visibility.

**2027 thesis:** The strategic plan targets $350M+ HDMC EBITDA by 2027 with $150M in cost savings. If achieved, this would represent a substantial improvement. But the Sportster hasn't launched, cost savings haven't been demonstrated, and management has no track record under this strategy. The next-year estimate of $1.82 EPS (consensus) requires roughly 4.7x earnings growth from the $0.39 current-year estimate — an assumption that embeds significant execution risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $25.24, HOG trades at:
- ~65x current-year EPS ($0.39)
- ~14x next-year EPS ($1.82) — assumes successful turnaround
- ~1.0x tangible book value ($3.14B)
- ~2.9% dividend yield

Relative to peers: median Consumer Discretionary P/E of 14.4x is comparable to HOG's forward multiple on FY2027 estimates. But peers like Whirlpool (14.6x on current earnings) and LCI Industries (13.8x) are profitable now. HOG's "cheap" forward multiple requires faith in a turnaround that hasn't started delivering.

The valuation case for HOG requires believing: (1) the Sportster launch generates meaningful incremental volume in 2027, (2) $150M in cost savings materialize, (3) tariff headwinds don't increase, and (4) U.S. motorcycle demand stabilizes. These are all plausible but unverified assumptions. The market isn't obviously mispricing at $25.24 — it's pricing ~14x a turnaround scenario with appropriate uncertainty.

Analyst consensus target of $25.18 (mean) essentially implies the stock is fairly valued today. The range is $12-$32, reflecting deep disagreement.

## Competitive Landscape

Harley's competitive position is deteriorating. U.S. market share eroded from 37.3% to 34.5% in one year. European share fell from 5.0% to 3.4%. These are share losses within a shrinking market — the worst possible combination.

The brand retains pricing power (average selling price ~$22K per unit) and a loyal installed base, but the dealer network is stressed by declining foot traffic and rising used-bike inventory. Foreign competitors (Honda, Yamaha, BMW, Indian) benefit from weaker currencies and face lower tariff exposure on U.S. sales because they manufacture overseas.

Harley's decision to introduce a $10K Sportster acknowledges that premium-only pricing was losing the volume battle. Expanding the customer base via lower-priced products while maintaining premium brand positioning creates a strategic tension with high execution risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Secular demand decline (CRITICAL):** U.S. 601+cc registrations declining ~6% annually with no floor established. Demographic headwinds (aging rider population) compound year-over-year.

**Tariff exposure (SIGNIFICANT):** $75-90M annual cost against near-zero operating income. Harley faces asymmetric tariff disadvantage — taxed on both imported inputs (steel/aluminum) and exported finished goods (EU/Asian retaliatory tariffs).

**Turnaround execution risk (HIGH):** New strategy with zero quarters of execution evidence. Sportster doesn't launch until 2027. Cost savings haven't been demonstrated. CEO has no motorcycle industry experience.

**S&P CreditWatch Negative:** Potential downgrade would raise funding costs and impair HDFS commercial paper access.

**LiveWire cash drain:** $70-80M annual losses on 653 units. Cap on further investment limits downside but doesn't eliminate it — $75M term loan matures Dec 2027.

**Short interest at 17.7%:** Elevated institutional bearish positioning with 4.3 days to cover, signaling informed skepticism about the turnaround timeline.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No single dated binary event, but sequential fundamental deterioration is the mechanism. Key dates: Q2 2026 earnings (est. late July/early August) where $45M quarterly tariff headwind meets weak consumer demand. If Q2 shipments miss the 130-135K full-year cadence (i.e., <33K quarterly), consensus estimates would require another cut.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The next two quarterly reports will test whether the strategic plan's first deliverables (Sprint model, initial cost actions) generate any traction. Failure would confirm the bear thesis; success would invalidate it.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 17.7% with 4.3 days to cover is meaningfully elevated — not extreme enough to preclude shorting but creates squeeze risk on any positive news flow (strategic announcements, partnership deals, or macro relief). The May 5 earnings pop ($23 → $25+) demonstrated this risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options P/C OI ratio of 0.09 suggests call-heavy positioning, contrarian to the short thesis. Near-term IV cheap vs. HV (36.5% vs. 40.2%) means put protection is relatively affordable. No cost-to-borrow data available but 17.7% SI suggests moderate borrow cost.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target $17-19 (analyst low $12 implies extreme downside). From $25.24, downside to $18 = 29%. Upside risk to $30 on squeeze/turnaround = 19%. Risk/reward approximately 1.5:1 — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The 17.7% short interest, call-heavy OI positioning, CEO insider buying, and sub-2:1 risk/reward make this unfavorable for an active short. The stock is already 19% below its 52-week high and near its 52-week low. Much of the bear thesis is priced. Avoid owning; do not actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Two consecutive quarters of retail registration growth (currently declining 12%+)
- HDMC operating income demonstrably positive (>$50M run-rate) for two quarters
- Sportster launch with >5,000 units shipped in its first full quarter
- Tariff headwinds resolved or offset by pricing
- Stock trading below $18 on unchanged/improving estimates (would create >3:1 risk/reward for turnaround bet)

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist through at least Q3 2026. The earliest the turnaround can be validated is mid-2027 when Sportster launches and cost savings begin flowing. 12-18 months minimum before revisiting.

## Conclusion

Harley-Davidson possesses a genuinely iconic brand and a balance sheet that has been materially de-risked. The new CEO's insider buying at $19 is a legitimate conviction signal, and the "Back to the Bricks" strategy addresses the right problems (demographics, affordability, volume decline). However, the stock at $25.24 prices in substantial turnaround execution that won't be verifiable for 12-18 months, during which time the company is guided to near-zero operating income while facing $75-90M in tariff costs, shrinking end-market demand, eroding market share, and a consumer environment hostile to $25K discretionary purchases.

The improving score trajectory (4.0 → 4.3 → 4.6) reflects genuine progress on balance sheet health and management quality. But the validation system's prior AVOID recommendations remain correct: no specific, measurable catalyst exists within the next 12 months to change the fundamental trajectory. The turnaround is a 2027-2028 story at the earliest, and the market's consensus target ($25.18 mean) confirms the stock is approximately fairly valued for its current risk profile. Maintain AVOID with a specific re-entry framework tied to operational evidence.
