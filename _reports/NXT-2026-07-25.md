## Executive Summary

Nextracker is the world's leading supplier of utility-scale solar tracking systems, holding the #1 position by gigawatts shipped for ten consecutive years with more than 160 GW deployed across six continents. The business is financially strong: FY2026 (fiscal year ended March 31) revenue grew 20% to $3.56B, net income reached $586M on a 25.1% ROE, the balance sheet carries effectively zero funded debt with a $1.0B undrawn revolver, and free cash flow was $514M. This is a high-return, capex-light compounder with a competitive moat built on scale, software (TrueCapture/NX Navigator), reliability track record, and a domestic-content U.S. supply chain that competitors cannot easily replicate.

The central complication is policy. Nextracker's reported gross margin of 32.6% is materially augmented by the Section 45X advanced manufacturing tax credit, which contributed $379.9M of cost-of-sales reduction in FY2026, roughly 55% of the $697M in operating income. Separately, the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) sharply accelerated the phase-out of the Section 48E/45Y investment and production credits that Nextracker's customers rely on: projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026 or be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to qualify. This mechanism is almost certainly pulling U.S. demand forward into FY2026-FY2027; the record $5B backlog and the U.S. revenue surge (+34% while Rest-of-World fell 11%) partly reflect customers racing to safe-harbor. The market is being asked to price continued growth into a policy structure that management itself says will "likely reduce overall project volume over time."

The stock has fallen roughly 39% from its May 2026 high of $163 to $100, which improves the entry point but does not resolve the binary. Estimate revisions are mixed (near-term flat-to-negative, next-year positive), and the setup is two-sided: platform diversification (eBOS, storage, power conversion, international) and pulled-forward backlog against a demand air pocket that begins in FY2028. We rate NXT a HOLD (uncertain) at $100, with the recommendation resting on a policy question that will not resolve cleanly within the next several quarters.

## Company Overview

Nextracker designs and sells single-axis solar tracking systems that rotate photovoltaic panels to follow the sun, boosting energy yield by up to ~25% versus fixed-tilt installations. The company was founded in 2013 by CEO Dan Shugar, spun out of Flex Ltd., and IPO'd in February 2023. Its flagship product is the NX Horizon tracker (self-powered, independent-row, mechanically balanced), sold alongside variants for terrain-following (XTR) and weather resilience (Hail Pro), plus separately-licensed yield-optimization and weather-control software.

The operating model is capex-light. Nextracker manufactures through more than 100 contract facilities across 19 countries, providing ~1,500 MW/week (~80 GW/year) of capacity while spending only $49M of capex in FY2026. Customers are EPCs, developers, and independent power producers: more than 275 active accounts across 40+ countries. Revenue mix is roughly 88% trackers and 12% non-tracker, with the non-tracker segment (electrical balance-of-system, steel frames, robotics, software) the fastest-growing line, up from ~8% a year earlier.

FY2026 marked an aggressive push beyond pure-play tracking. Four acquisitions closed (Bentek/eBOS, OnSight/robotics, Origami/steel frames, Fracsun/soiling measurement) for roughly $149M aggregate, and a definitive agreement (May 2026) to acquire Zigor's power-conversion business plus Apex Power for ~$80.5M signals an explicit move into battery storage and data-center markets. The November 2025 rebrand to Nextpower reflects the intended shift from tracker vendor to integrated solar-and-power platform. A minority (49%) Saudi JV, Nextpower Arabia, targets MENA growth but is not consolidated and accounted for as an equity-method investment, not consolidated capacity.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is strong and improving on nearly every axis except cash conversion.

**Revenue and earnings trajectory ($M):**

| FY | Revenue | Gross margin | Op income | Net income | GW delivered |
|----|---------|--------------|-----------|------------|--------------|
| 2023 | 1,902 | 15.1% | 168 | 1 | — |
| 2024 | 2,500 | 32.5% | 587 | ~306–496 | 26.0 |
| 2025 | 2,959 | 34.1% | 639 | 517 | 33.6 |
| 2026 | 3,559 | 32.6% | 697 | 586 | 38.0 |

FY2026 revenue growth was driven entirely by the United States, which rose 34% to $2.73B and now represents 77% of total revenue. Rest-of-World revenue fell 11% on weak Latin America. This geographic concentration concentrates the business in precisely the market most exposed to policy risk.

Gross margin compressed ~150 bps, driven by a $110.7M increase in tariffs (to $130.4M) that was not fully passed through to pricing, partly offset by the 45X credit. Operating margin held at 19.6%, net margin 16.5%, FCF margin 14.4%. ROE of 25.1% and ROA of 14.4% are top-decile for the sector.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Altman Z-Score of 3.54 places the company firmly in the safe zone. Debt-to-equity is nominally 0.74, but funded debt is effectively zero: the new $1.0B revolver (September 2025, matures 2030) is undrawn, with only $77.9M in letters of credit outstanding. Current ratio is 2.45, and total liquidity approaches $2.0B. Capital deployment in FY2026 was $117M on acquisitions, $49M capex, $12M into the Saudi JV, plus a $500M buyback authorized in January 2026 (only $0.4M used by fiscal year-end). No dividend.

Two quality-of-earnings items warrant emphasis. First, operating cash flow declined to $563M in FY2026 from $656M in FY2025 despite higher net income, so OCF fell below net income (one of the failed Piotroski signals; the score is 4/5). The culprit is a $267M increase in the Section 45X credit receivable: the company is recognizing the credit benefit in earnings faster than it collects the cash. This is not alarming given the fortress balance sheet, but it is a real cash-conversion watch item. Second, and more consequentially, the 45X credit contributed $379.9M of cost-of-sales reduction (up from $224.9M in FY2025), equal to roughly a third of gross profit. The reported gross margin is materially inflated by this credit. The 45X credit begins stepping down 25% per year starting in 2030 and ends after 2032, and any FEOC-driven disqualification of Chinese-steel-linked suppliers would hit margins directly.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is robust and the medium-term picture is genuinely uncertain, which is the crux of the investment case.

Near-term, the earnings-surprise record is clean: four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters (13.2%, 16.9%, 17.2%, 8.6%), which supports confidence in the current fiscal year. The record backlog (POs plus Volume Commitment Agreements) and 20% revenue growth reflect strong demand. Management frames a structural "electricity super-cycle" — AI/data-center load growth, electrification, and solar being the cheapest new generation (Lazard data shows solar cost down 84% from 2009-2025), reinforced by interconnection reform (FERC Order 2023).

The problem is that a meaningful portion of the near-term strength is likely pulled forward from the future. The July 2025 Executive Order, together with IRS Notice 2025-42 (which eliminated the 5% cost safe harbor effective September 2, 2025), imposes the hard begin-construction and place-in-service deadlines. Customers are rationally racing to safe-harbor projects now, which inflates FY2026-FY2027 volumes and creates a demand air pocket in FY2028 and beyond unless the credits are extended. Management states plainly that the changes "will reduce the number of projects in future years... likely reducing overall project volume over time."

Estimate revisions capture the tension. The current-quarter estimate is roughly flat over 30 days (+0.1%) but down 5.9% over 90 days. Next-quarter is down 1.6%/2.4%. Current-year is essentially flat (+0.9%/-1.0%). The one bright spot is next-year, revised up +1.1% over 30 days and +6.6% over 90 days, which suggests the sell side does not yet model a sharp FY2028 decline. This mixed pattern is why the strong estimate-revision BUY signal does not apply here: there is no clean +30% inflection; the revisions are two-sided and dominated by an unresolved policy question.

The offset is platform diversification. The non-tracker segment grew from ~8% to ~12% of revenue and is the fastest-growing line. The pending Zigor/Apex power-conversion deal pushes explicitly into storage and data centers, markets with independent demand drivers less tied to the solar-specific tax cliff. International expansion (the Saudi JV, RoW rebuild) offers another bridge. Whether these adjacencies can grow fast enough to offset a U.S. tracker air pocket that begins in FY2028 is the open question the market cannot yet answer.

## Valuation Assessment

At $100 with a $15.2B market cap, NXT trades at roughly 26x trailing net income ($586M) and, on consensus, roughly 21x current-year EPS ($4.72) and ~17x next-year EPS ($5.92). Against the peer group, this is inexpensive: peers in Electrical Components & Equipment show a median P/E of 56.7, with Regal Rexnord at 49x, Generac 80x, Vicor 100x, Powell 57x, and Acuity — the closest analog on multiple — at 21x. NXT's forward multiple sits at roughly a third of the peer median.

That discount is not a straightforward mispricing. The peer set is heterogeneous and heavily populated by names re-rating on AI/power-infrastructure themes, whereas NXT carries a policy overhang that none of them share. The market is applying a discount because a material share of NXT's earnings power depends on tax credits (45X in COGS, and customer demand on 48E/45Y) that are legislated to decline. A ~17x forward multiple on next-year earnings looks cheap only if you believe FY2028 earnings will not fall off the pulled-forward peak. If the air pocket materializes, then FY2027 earnings are near-peak and the forward multiple is being applied to an earnings base that mean-reverts lower, the classic cyclical-with-a-policy-twist value trap.

Cheapness alone is not a reason to own this. The valuation dimension has been a reliable value-trap warning across this analytical framework, and NXT fits the profile: a compressed multiple that reflects a legitimate structural question, not obvious market error. The bull case requires a specific reason the market is wrong — either credit extension, or platform diversification bridging the gap, or FEOC rules proving benign. None of those is confirmed. Analyst targets (mean $148.6, median $149, high $182, low $64) span an enormous range, which signals the binary nature of the setup rather than a consensus that the stock is undervalued.

On balance, the valuation is reasonable-not-compelling. The absolute multiple is low, but the earnings base it is applied to carries genuine downside risk that the discount is correctly pricing. We score this a 5.5, neither a clear opportunity nor clearly stretched.

## Competitive Landscape

Nextracker's competitive position is strong and, on most axes, improving. The company has led the tracker market by GW shipped for ten straight years, competing against Array Technologies, GameChange Solar, Arctech, PV Hardware, Shoals, and Trina. Its differentiation rests on four pillars: an installed-base track record (>160 GW), yield-optimization software (TrueCapture/NX Navigator), reliability, and, most durably, a U.S. domestic-content supply chain with more than 30 U.S. fabricators and 40+ GW of domestic torque-tube capacity.

That domestic-content moat is the most valuable asset in the current environment. U.S. domestic-content bonus-credit rules reward projects using American steel, and Nextracker's supply chain is difficult for competitors to replicate at scale. This is a real, structural advantage that the FEOC rules under OBBBA both reinforce (by penalizing China-linked supply) and threaten, because a "significant portion" of Nextracker's own steel derives directly or indirectly from Chinese mills, creating disqualification risk pending evolving Treasury guidance.

The market is fragmented and price-competitive. Falling solar-panel prices can, at the margin, make trackers relatively less economic versus fixed-tilt in some geographies. The platform strategy of moving into eBOS, steel frames, power conversion, and storage is a rational response, widening the addressable content per project and reducing single-product exposure. Execution across four FY2026 acquisitions plus the pending Zigor deal is unproven, and integration risk is real, but the strategic direction improves competitive positioning over time. The competitive trajectory is favorable; the primary threat is regulatory rather than competitive.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by policy-driven binary outcomes that are not fully priced, which is why this dimension scores low (4.0).

**U.S. tax-credit cliff (the dominant risk).** OBBBA's acceleration of the 48E/45Y phase-out engineers a demand pull-forward into FY2026-FY2027 followed by a likely air pocket in FY2028+. With 77% of revenue now U.S.-sourced, the exposure is concentrated. This is the single most important variable and it resolves over years, not quarters.

**45X credit dependence.** Roughly 55% of operating income flowed through COGS via the credit in FY2026. The credit steps down starting 2030 and ends after 2032. Any adverse legislative change, or FEOC disqualification of Chinese-steel-linked suppliers, would compress margins sharply. Treasury guidance (Notice 2026-15, February 2026) is still evolving.

**FEOC rules.** OBBBA disqualifies credits tied to China-linked entities. A significant portion of Nextracker's steel is China-derived directly or indirectly. Disqualification would raise COGS and erode competitiveness.

**Tariff and AD/CVD exposure.** Layered tariffs (Section 232, 301, 122) drove the FY2026 tariff increase. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs may bring refunds, but an unresolved AD/CVD certification issue on off-grid modules from Malaysia/Thailand carries a potential retroactive duty liability that management describes as "unknown."

**Securities litigation.** A December 2024 securities class action, plus January/March 2025 derivative suits, alleges false or misleading statements about the business and prospects. This is a live legal overhang not captured in headline financials.

**Structural cash leakage.** The Tax Receivable Agreement carries a $393M liability to Flex/TPG affiliates (85% of realized IPO-structure tax benefits), with ~$27M paid in FY2026. TRA acceleration on change of control both deters takeovers and reduces effective free cash to common holders.

**Customer concentration.** A "relatively small number of customers," with pay-if-paid EPC clauses and cancellable POs / non-binding VCA backlog, means the backlog is less firm than it appears.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term stress. ATM IV of 89.4% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 67.0% (a 22.4-point rich spread), in the 79th percentile of the current chain, with an implied move of ±19.9% into the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 89.4% > long-term 79.4%), consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst, likely the next earnings report and continued policy/FEOC-guidance news flow. ATM IV has fallen from 113.5% to 89.4% since mid-June as the price dropped 18.9%, so some of the acute event stress has already unwound. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%) near-term but turns negative at the 6-month tenor (-5.0%), where the put/call OI ratio spikes to 4.60, indicating hedging demand building against a longer-dated downside scenario, consistent with the policy-cliff thesis. This confirms rather than contradicts the fundamental view: the market is not complacent about NXT's downside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Nextracker is a debt-free, market-leading compounder generating 25% ROE and $514M FCF, trading at ~17x next-year earnings against a peer median near 57x. The demand backdrop is structural: AI/data-center power growth, electrification, and solar as the cheapest new generation. The domestic-content supply chain is a durable moat that FEOC rules actively reinforce. Platform diversification (eBOS now 12% of revenue and growing, plus storage and power conversion via Zigor/Apex) can bridge the U.S. tracker air pocket, while international rebuild and the Saudi JV reopen RoW growth. If credits are extended, or if the air pocket proves shallower than feared, the stock re-rates toward the $148 consensus and potentially the $182 high target. Bull case: **$160-180** (roughly +60-80% from $100), predicated on demonstrated FY2028 revenue resilience and platform revenue exceeding 20% of the mix.

### Bear Case
The reported profitability is a policy artifact: 45X credits contribute ~55% of operating income, and customer demand rides tax credits that are legislated to decline. The FY2026-FY2027 strength is pulled forward; FY2028 U.S. tracker demand falls into an air pocket. FEOC rules disqualify China-linked steel suppliers, compressing margins. Cash conversion continues to lag earnings as the 45X receivable balloons. The securities litigation and TRA leakage compound the picture. In this scenario, next-year estimates prove too high, earnings mean-revert, and the low multiple is revealed as appropriate rather than cheap. Bear case: **$64-75** (roughly -25% to -36% from $100), matching the analyst low target and reflecting a de-rated multiple on a declining earnings base.

The base case sits between these: the pull-forward supports FY2027, diversification partially offsets the FY2028 cliff, credits are not extended but not made dramatically worse, and the stock trades in a wide range around current levels as the market waits for FY2028 visibility.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is a two-sided policy binary that will not resolve within the next few quarters, and neither the bull nor bear case has enough confirmed support to justify a directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Legislative or regulatory relief on the 48E/45Y phase-out timeline, or FEOC guidance (following Notice 2026-15) that confirms Nextracker's supply chain remains credit-qualified.
- Platform/non-tracker revenue sustainably exceeding 20% of total with positive next-year estimate revisions accelerating above +10%, demonstrating the FY2028 bridge is materializing.
- A pullback toward $75-80 that prices in a substantial portion of the air-pocket risk while fundamentals stay intact, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog conversion deterioration or a management guidance cut signaling the FY2028 cliff arriving early.
- Adverse FEOC determination disqualifying key suppliers, or a 45X-receivable collectability problem.
- Material adverse development in the securities class action, or a step-change in cash conversion (OCF falling further below net income).

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report and continued FEOC/Treasury guidance are the near-term inputs; the July 4, 2026 begin-construction safe-harbor deadline and subsequent order-flow commentary through late 2026 will be the decisive read on the size of the pull-forward and the shape of the FY2028 air pocket.

**Trajectory note:** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, with the composite score having risen from ~5.3 (March) to a 6.7 peak (May) and easing to 6.2 (June), now 5.75. Financial Health has genuinely improved (the debt-free balance sheet and 25% ROE are confirmed by the FY2026 10-K), which the trajectory correctly captures. Growth Prospects have declined modestly, appropriately, as the OBBBA cliff crystallized. The ~39% drawdown from the $163 high does not itself deteriorate the thesis — fundamentals remain intact and the entry point improves — but at that magnitude the burden of proof shifts, and the specific thing sophisticated sellers are pricing is credible: an earnings base inflated by tax credits and pulled-forward demand. We do not lower scores for the price decline, but we do not treat the lower price as a clear buy signal either, because the fundamental question (FY2028 durability) is genuinely unresolved.

## Conclusion

Nextracker is a high-quality business — global market leader, debt-free, 25% ROE, $514M FCF, genuine domestic-content moat — whose reported profitability and near-term demand are both heavily leveraged to solar tax credits that U.S. policy is actively winding down. The ~17x forward multiple looks cheap against peers but is applied to an earnings base that carries real downside from an engineered FY2028 demand air pocket and a 45X credit that is ~55% of operating income. Platform diversification and international expansion are the plausible bridges, but they are unproven at the required scale.

The stock at $100, down 39% from its high, offers a better entry than three months ago without resolving the core binary. Options positioning confirms the market is pricing meaningful uncertainty, and analyst targets span $64 to $182, a range that argues against conviction in either direction. We rate NXT a **HOLD (uncertain)**, with a clear path to upgrade on policy relief, FEOC clarity, or demonstrated platform-driven FY2028 resilience, and a path to downgrade on early signs of the demand cliff arriving.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing P/E was overstated as "~21x" in the Valuation section (correct value is ~26x trailing net income; consensus forward ~21x/17x is unchanged), which made valuation appear modestly more attractive than the sources support.
