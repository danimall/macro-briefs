**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the largest US nuclear operator and the largest producer of carbon-free electricity, now reshaped by the January 2026 close of the $21.8B Calpine acquisition into a ~55 GW generation-plus-retail platform with roughly 70% of output inside PJM. The investment debate is narrow and well-defined: CEG owns scarce, 24/7 carbon-free baseload that hyperscalers will pay premium long-term prices for (the Microsoft/Crane PPA is the flagship), against a stock that re-rated enormously on the AI-power theme and is now de-rating as that theme is stress-tested. Shares are down ~10.6% over the trailing year and ~33% from the October 2025 high of $412.70, trading at $274.74.

The fundamentals underneath the price are mixed rather than deteriorating. Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS rose 28% to $2.74 and the company beat by 5.7%, but the GAAP $4.49 was inflated by $721M of unrealized hedge gains and a $174M ARO benefit, operating cash flow was soft at $425M, and consensus EPS estimates have fallen 3-5% across the front quarters over the last 90 days. The merchant earnings base is now more cyclical: Q1 benefited from PJM West day-ahead power up 81% and PJM capacity prices up 400-833% YoY, the same leverage that cuts hard if those prices normalize. A FERC capacity price collar (~$325 cap/$175 floor) for the 2028-2030 auctions caps the upside the market was extrapolating.

We rate CEG HOLD. The asset quality and structural demand tailwind are real and support a competitive-position score of 7.5, but three specific items prevent a BUY at this price: (1) the December 2025 FERC draft order finding PJM's co-located load tariff "unjust and unreasonable," which directly threatens the economics of the behind-the-meter data-center deals that justify the premium multiple; (2) the June 30, 2026 lock-up expiry releasing 25M Calpine shares into a market already showing institutional selling; and (3) falling consensus estimates that contradict the BUY-grade growth narrative. The risk/reward is balanced until the FERC co-location framework resolves and the LS Power divestiture closes.

## Company Overview

CEG was spun off from Exelon in February 2022 as the largest US merchant power producer and the largest US nuclear operator, with a fleet running at ~92% capacity factor. The Calpine acquisition, which closed January 7, 2026, added predominantly natural-gas generation, the Geysers geothermal complex, battery and solar assets, and the leading competitive retail electricity platform (~62 TWh/year, ~2.5M accounts, 80% of the Fortune 100). The combined company runs ~55 GW and supplies roughly 10% of all US clean energy.

The strategic logic is to pair clean baseload nuclear with dispatchable gas and a large retail book, smoothing the merchant nuclear earnings profile while positioning the company as a primary supplier to data-center and AI electricity demand. Reporting now runs across six segments (Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT, Other Power Regions, and Calpine), with PJM the dominant market and the single most important regulatory variable. CEO Joseph Dominguez and CFO Shane Smith lead the company from Baltimore.

The acquisition consideration was 50M new shares (~$17.6B at $354.58) plus $4.5B cash, with ~$12.6B of Calpine debt assumed and $11.1B of goodwill created. DOJ conditions require divestitures: five PJM gas plants (4.4 GW) are under agreement to LS Power for $5.0B (announced March 2026, pending close), plus an ERCOT plant and a minority interest already sold; all required divestitures must be under agreement by September 4, 2026. Former Calpine holders own ~14% of CEG, with lock-ups expiring 50% on June 30, 2026 and 50% on June 30, 2027. Credit ratings were affirmed post-deal at BBB+/Baa1.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** Revenue was $11,122M versus $6,788M a year earlier (+63.8%), with Calpine contributing $3,136M. Operating income jumped to $2,332M from $451M. GAAP net income to common was $1,590M ($4.49/share) versus $118M ($0.38). The cleaner figure is adjusted operating EPS of $2.74, up 28% from $2.14, on 354M diluted shares (up from 314M on the Calpine issuance).

Earnings quality requires care. The GAAP headline was flattered by ~$721M of after-tax unrealized hedge gains and a $174M decommissioning/ARO benefit, both non-operating. Operating cash flow was a weak $425M against $1,275M of capex, driven by a ~$1.4B decline in payables (working-capital timing). The 5.7% beat against the $2.59 estimate continues a solid track record — three beats and one miss over the last four quarters — but the magnitude of beats is modest (2-6%).

**Full-year context.** 2025 net income (~$2.3B) was down from ~$3.75B in 2024. This is not business deterioration but the reversal of the nuclear production tax credit dynamic. The PTC is a price floor (max ~$15/MWh) that phases out as power prices rise; because PJM power prices rose sharply, PTC revenue collapsed to just $10M in Q1 2026, and state ZEC/CMC net revenue turned negative -$285M (versus +$110M) on PTC pass-through/refund provisions. High prices above the subsidy floor are the favorable outcome, but a naive screen reads the lower net income as decline. The same PTC structure provides a meaningful downside hedge: if power prices fall through 2032, the floor re-engages.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets rose to $96.9B (from $57.2B at YE2025) on the Calpine consolidation. PP&E net is $40.8B, goodwill $11.5B, nuclear decommissioning trust funds $19.5B. Debt rose to $5.1B short-term borrowings plus $17.0B long-term plus $0.37B current. Total equity is $33.8B, cash only $0.8B. Liquidity is adequate: $15.1B aggregate credit facilities with $6.7B available. A three-notch downgrade to junk would trigger ~$3.0B incremental collateral, a tail risk the BBB+/Baa1 affirmation contains for now. The Altman Z-score of ~1.3 is a false distress signal typical of capital-intensive merchant utilities carrying large debt and ARO balances, not a bankruptcy indicator given investment-grade ratings and liquidity.

Financial health scores 5.5. The business generates real cash and carries appropriate leverage for a merchant utility, but the combination of soft Q1 operating cash flow, $11.5B of fresh goodwill subject to impairment, a thin $0.8B cash balance, and the collateral-call sensitivity to ratings caps the score below the 7+ threshold. The pending $5.0B LS Power proceeds will deleverage materially and are the clearest path to upgrading this dimension.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on data-center and AI electricity demand. Three concrete anchors: the Crane Clean Energy Center (Three Mile Island Unit 1 restart, 835 MW, 20-year Microsoft PPA, DOE loan guarantee up to $1.0B); a new 380 MW CyrusOne deal at Freestone (Texas) with a 380 MW Phase 2 and 400 MW at Thad Hill; and the broader thesis that scarce 24/7 carbon-free baseload commands premium long-term offtake. The PJM capacity price spike validates a tightening supply/demand backdrop that benefits CEG's owned generation directly.

The breadth of the data-center demand signal is corroborated across the power complex — regulated utility commercial load growth, transmission rate-base expansion, electrical equipment orders, and multiple hyperscaler capex commitments — which argues the demand is structural rather than a single-company story. CEG is a direct beneficiary.

The countervailing data is the estimate revision trend, which is unambiguously negative. Current-quarter consensus fell 3.9% over 90 days, next quarter -4.6%, and current year -3.3% to $11.72. This is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection. The growth thesis here is intact at the asset level but is being repriced lower at the earnings level as the market digests merchant-price normalization risk and FERC capacity collars. The Crane restart is a binary 2027-2028 catalyst with NRC-approval and interconnection-deliverability execution risk (grid upgrades could slip to December 2030 absent the requested FERC waiver).

Growth prospects score 6.5, down from the 8.5 carried earlier in the trajectory. The structural demand is real and durable, but falling consensus estimates and the absence of positive revision momentum prevent a higher score. This dimension is the most reliable predictor in our framework, and rising estimates would be required to restore it; right now they are falling.

## Valuation Assessment

CEG trades at ~23.4x trailing/forward earnings on $7.40 EPS, with a 0.64% dividend yield. Against electric-utility peers (median P/E 23.8, yield 3.0%), CEG trades roughly in line on the multiple but at a fraction of the peer dividend yield, reflecting its merchant-growth rather than regulated-income profile. The closer comparable is Vistra (P/E 24.7), the other AI-power merchant name, which has also de-rated sharply (-32% from its 52-week high versus CEG's ~-33%).

On forward consensus, current-year EPS of $11.72 and next-year $13.54 put CEG at ~23.4x current-year and ~20.3x next-year. That is not a distressed multiple, but neither is it cheap relative to a business whose earnings are now more merchant-cyclical and whose growth estimates are falling. The proximity to consensus warrants weighing carefully: the mean analyst target of $360 sits 31% above the current price, and the low target ($296) is itself 8% above spot, which would normally argue the market is too pessimistic. The caveat is that those targets were largely set before the recent estimate cuts and the FERC co-location overhang crystallized; the analyst recommendation sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, with the count of holds rising. Targets here are stale rather than a reliable floor.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the bull case the way it would for a trough-earnings inflection stock. CEG's Q1 was a peak-price quarter, so trailing earnings reflect favorable, not depressed, conditions. The risk is asymmetric in the wrong direction: estimates are being cut from an elevated base, and the FERC capacity collar caps the 2028-2030 upside the market had been extrapolating.

Valuation scores 6.0. There is a plausible mispricing case if the data-center premium proves durable and merchant prices hold, but a 23x multiple in a re-rating sector is not a buy signal absent rising estimates or a resolved regulatory overhang. The setup is balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is the strongest part of the story. The nuclear fleet is genuinely irreplaceable: licensing, siting, and capital barriers make new entry effectively impossible, and the ~92% capacity factor and clean profile command premium offtake from hyperscalers that cannot source equivalent 24/7 carbon-free baseload elsewhere. The Calpine addition layers in dispatchable gas and the leading competitive retail platform, broadening the company from a pure merchant nuclear play into a vertically integrated generation-plus-retail operator.

The trajectory is improving on the demand side: the PJM capacity price increase confirms a tightening market that favors incumbent generation owners, and CEG owns the scarce assets. Total shareholder return since the 2022 spin of $734 per $100 invested versus $162 for peers reflects how decisively the market has rewarded this positioning.

The threat to the moat is regulatory rather than competitive. The FERC draft order on PJM's co-located load tariff directly challenges the behind-the-meter "power plant plus adjacent data center" model that underpins the highest-value deals. If FERC's final framework constrains co-location economics, the premium that hyperscalers pay compresses, and the differentiation between CEG and ordinary merchant generators narrows. Competitive position scores 7.5: the asset moat is wide and the demand backdrop is improving, but the regulatory thread keeps it short of the highest tier.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and not fully priced, which is why this dimension scores low.

**FERC co-located load ruling (highest stakes).** The final framework will determine whether the lucrative co-location model scales. This is a binary regulatory event with no fixed date, and the outcome directly affects the premium embedded in the stock.

**Lock-up overhang.** The June 30, 2026 unlock is a near-term technical pressure with a known date, landing into observed institutional selling.

**Merchant commodity leverage.** Earnings are now levered to PJM power and capacity prices that just printed at extreme levels. The PTC floor cushions nuclear downside, but the gas and capacity exposure cuts both ways, and the FERC capacity collar caps the 2028-2030 upside.

**Integration and goodwill.** The $11.5B of goodwill creates impairment risk; IT/OT integration, customer/employee retention, and synergy realization are unproven one quarter in.

**Crane restart execution.** NRC approval and interconnection deliverability (potential slip to December 2030) gate a key catalyst; cost overruns or delays risk impairment and PPA penalties.

**Other.** Russian uranium import ban with ~35% of 2026-2031 supply from three sources; Illinois CEJA potentially forcing the Zion gas plant offline by 2030; California wildfire/earthquake and geothermal resource risk at the Geysers; management's own disclosed risk that AI demand "may not grow at the pace currently anticipated by the market."

Risk factors score 4.5. The FERC co-location ruling, the lock-up expiry, and the merchant-price/capacity-collar dynamics are concrete catalysts for downside that the market is in the process of pricing but has not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive on balance and does not confirm a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 44.6% against 1-year historical vol of 46.9% — fair to slightly cheap, with IV at the 2nd percentile of its own chain. The put/call skew is -3.0% (call-heavy), and IV collapsed from 78.6% to 44.6% over the past week as the post-earnings event passed and the price recovered ~10%. The 6-month tenor shows a -6.3% put skew and a ±25.4% implied move, reflecting the binary FERC/lock-up risks priced further out. Short interest is low at 3.6% of float (3.5 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish setup. The options market reads as moderate, two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional warning, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The FERC co-location framework resolves favorably, preserving the behind-the-meter data-center premium. The LS Power divestiture closes, deleveraging the balance sheet and funding buybacks at depressed prices. Crane restarts on schedule under the Microsoft PPA. Merchant power and capacity prices stay elevated on structural data-center demand, and consensus estimates inflect higher. The stock then re-rates toward the analyst mean of $360 and beyond, with the high target of $441 representing the full-AI-premium outcome. Bull case $400-441.

**Bear case.** FERC constrains co-location economics, compressing the data-center premium. PJM power and capacity prices normalize from extreme Q1 levels, and the merchant earnings base re-rates lower. The lock-up adds supply pressure, estimate cuts continue, and goodwill impairment or integration friction surfaces. The stock then revisits the 52-week low of $240.51 and potentially the low-$220s. Bear case $220-240, roughly -13% to -20% from current.

**Base case.** Estimates stabilize near current levels, the LS Power divestiture closes and modestly improves the balance sheet, and FERC delivers a workable but less generous co-location framework. The stock trades in a $270-310 range while the regulatory and lock-up overhangs clear, with the analyst low target of $296 a reasonable mid-cycle anchor. Base case ~$300.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis is genuinely balanced: a wide-moat asset base and durable demand tailwind offset by falling estimates and two specific, dated overhangs (FERC co-location, June 30 lock-up). I do not have sufficient evidence for a confident directional call until those resolve.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FERC issues a final co-located load framework that preserves data-center deal economics, removing the largest regulatory overhang.
- Consensus current-year EPS estimates stop falling and revise upward (e.g., FY26 estimate back above $12.00), restoring growth momentum.
- The June 30 lock-up clears without sustained selling pressure, AND the $5.0B LS Power divestiture closes, deleveraging the balance sheet.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FERC's final co-location ruling materially constrains behind-the-meter data-center economics, structurally impairing the premium thesis.
- Q2 or Q3 adjusted operating EPS misses while management cuts full-year guidance, confirming the negative revision trend is fundamental rather than conservative resetting.
- Evidence of goodwill impairment or material integration shortfall on the Calpine deal, or a ratings action threatening the BBB+/Baa1 profile.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early-to-mid August) combined with the June 30 lock-up expiry and any FERC co-location order. These three events bunched into Q3 should resolve much of the current uncertainty.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy owns one of the best asset bases in US power — an irreplaceable nuclear fleet, now paired with dispatchable gas and a leading retail platform — sitting at the center of a structural data-center electricity demand wave that is confirmed broadly across the power complex. That is the durable part of the story and supports a strong competitive position. The problem is that the stock re-rated on the most optimistic version of this thesis and is now de-rating as the market prices three concrete uncertainties: the FERC ruling that threatens co-located-load economics, the June 30 lock-up adding supply into observed institutional selling, and falling consensus estimates against a peak-price comparison quarter. The trajectory of declining scores reflects these specific developments, not price action alone; the estimate cuts and the FERC overhang are fundamental inputs, not noise.

At 23.4x earnings with a sub-1% yield and falling estimates, the valuation is not cheap enough to underwrite the regulatory and technical risks, and the analyst targets that sit well above spot were largely set before the recent estimate cuts and are likely stale. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not present today; it would emerge if FERC resolves favorably, estimates inflect, and the LS Power proceeds deleverage the balance sheet. Until then, HOLD is the appropriate call, with a clear path to upgrade on the catalysts above and a defined downgrade trigger if the co-location ruling impairs the premium.
