**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SunCoke Energy is the largest independent producer of metallurgical coke in the Americas, operating five U.S. cokemaking plants on long-term take-or-pay contracts, a Brazilian licensed facility, three bulk-logistics terminals, and (since August 2025) the Phoenix Global mill-services business. The 2025 financials show a sharp GAAP reversal: revenue fell to $1,837M, operating income swung to ($44M), and net income to SXC came in at ($44M). The headline loss is misleading. It was dominated by a $90.1M non-cash impairment at Haverhill I, triggered when Algoma Steel breached its take-or-pay contract. Stripping the impairment, the cash engine kept running: operating cash flow was $109M in 2025 and $72.7M in Q1 2026, and management has reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $230-250M EBITDA, $18-36M net income, and $230-250M operating cash flow.

The investment question is whether SXC is a transitory-loss recovery story or a structurally impaired coke producer facing the electric-arc-furnace (EAF) transition. The evidence cuts both ways. On the constructive side: cash conversion is strong, a coordinated four-insider open-market buying cluster (CFO, an SVP, and two directors) at $5.53-$6.86 signals informed conviction near the lows, the Phoenix pivot nearly doubled Industrial Services EBITDA year-over-year ($26.2M vs $13.7M in Q1), and the dividend yields ~5.4%. On the cautious side: customer concentration is extreme (Cliffs + U.S. Steel ≈ 73% of revenue), the Algoma breach proved take-or-pay is only as good as the counterparty, leverage rose ~40% to fund Phoenix while EBITDA fell, capex is stepping up to $90-100M against a deleveraging goal, and consensus estimates are falling sharply, with the current-year EPS estimate cut 64% over 90 days and next year now guided to a loss.

At $8.64 the stock has already recovered ~57% from its $5.51 52-week low, and all sell-side targets ($9.00-$10.00) sit just above the current price. The recovery the data describes has largely played out in the price. The balance of evidence supports a HOLD: the cash-generative core and insider conviction offset the secular and concentration risks roughly evenly, but the falling estimate trend and thin dividend coverage keep us from a constructive growth or clean income call. We classify this as an uncertain hold and would become genuinely interested again near the $5.50-$6.00 range at which insiders themselves were buying.

## Company Overview

SunCoke operates two segments after a 2025 reorganization:

- **Domestic Coke (~88% of revenue):** Five U.S. heat-recovery cokemaking plants (Jewell, Indiana Harbor, Granite City, Haverhill, Middletown) with ~3.7M tons of blast-furnace coke nameplate capacity, representing roughly 38% of U.S. blast-furnace coke market capacity. SunCoke is the only North American coke producer using heat-recovery technology, which yields higher-quality coke, lower emissions, and monetizable waste heat (steam/power). The core business runs on long-term take-or-pay contracts with coal, transport, and regulatory cost pass-throughs plus a fixed return-on-capital fee. The structural catch: if the company misses contractual coal-to-coke yields, it absorbs the excess coal cost, a risk that materialized in 2025.

- **Industrial Services (~12% of revenue, growing):** Three transload terminals (Convent Marine, Kanawha River, Lake Terminal) with 40M+ tons of capacity, plus the newly acquired Phoenix Global mill-services business (15 molten-slag/scrap-handling sites across the U.S., Brazil, Slovakia, and Spain).

The Phoenix acquisition is a deliberate hedge: mill services are agnostic to whether a customer runs a blast furnace or an EAF, so if steel production shifts away from coke-consuming blast furnaces, SunCoke can still serve those mills. This is the most important forward narrative for the equity.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows a clear downshift in 2025:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,972 | 2,063 | 1,935 | 1,837 |
| Operating income ($M) | 154 | 125 | 152 | (44) |
| Net income to SXC ($M) | 101 | 58 | 96 | (44) |
| Adjusted EBITDA ($M) | 298 | 269 | 273 | 219 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 169 | 109 |
| Long-term debt ($M) | 529 | 490 | 492 | 686 |

**What drove the 2025 loss.** The bulk was the Haverhill I impairment following the Algoma breach. The operating shortfall beyond that came from lower coke pricing/mix (more low-margin non-contracted blast coke), unfavorable coal-to-coke yields, lower Algoma-related volumes, and weaker Granite City extension economics. Domestic Coke EBITDA/ton fell from $58.27 to $46.35. A $34M income-tax benefit (Section 48 investment tax credits plus the impairment tax shield) cushioned the net result.

The central analytical point is that the earnings collapse was largely non-cash. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $72.7M (aided by an inventory drawdown) enabled a $26M revolver repayment that cut long-term debt to $659.9M. Treating a non-cash impairment as evidence of terminal business deterioration would be an error; the cash flows do not support that reading.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Debt rose ~$193M in 2025 to fund the $294M Phoenix acquisition (drawn on the revolver), taking total borrowings to $693M at year-end ($500M of 4.875% senior notes due 2029 plus ~$193M revolver due 2030). D/E is ~1.95 and the Altman Z-Score sits at 1.88 (grey zone). Offsetting this, the current ratio is a healthy 2.11, the company is comfortably within covenants (max net leverage 4.5x, min interest coverage 2.5x), and credit ratings are S&P BB- (stable) / Moody's B1 (stable). Management targets gross leverage below 3x by year-end 2026, funded by the guided cash flow.

**Free cash flow and the dividend.** FCF was a thin $42.3M in 2025 (2.3% margin) against ~$41M of dividends; the payout was nearly, but not comfortably, covered, with debt partly funding capital. The 2026 capex step-up to $90-100M (from $67M) compresses FCF available for both deleveraging and the dividend. The dividend (27 consecutive quarters at $0.12, ~5.4% yield) is defensible if the EBITDA recovery materializes, but its coverage depends on that recovery and management explicitly reviews it quarterly. A $96.3M buyback authorization remains dormant since Q1 2020.

**Earnings visibility is poor.** The company missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 -69.6%, Q4 2025 -265%, Q2 2025 -88.7%; only Q3 2025 beat, +60.9%). This is consistent with the operating volatility inherent in yield and contract-mix swings, and it argues for caution in extrapolating the guidance.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth signal is the most worrying element of the file. Consensus EPS estimates are falling broadly and rapidly:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | $0.08 | $0.14 | -42.9% |
| Current Year | $0.20 | $0.56 | -64.3% |
| Next Year | $(0.19) | $0.56 | -133.9% |

Estimate revisions of this magnitude are typically a reliable negative forward indicator, and here they sit against a single-analyst consensus (thin coverage), which adds noise but does not flip the sign. This is a depressed base with a guided recovery that the Street is revising lower.

Against the falling estimates, there are real bright spots. Management described the coke business as "sold out for the full year," with Haverhill II (Cliffs) extended through 2028 and Granite City (U.S. Steel) extended through 2026. The clearest growth point is Phoenix: 2026 segment guidance of $90-100M approaches the scale of Domestic Coke's guided $162-168M. The segment is integrating "seamlessly" with further cost synergies expected.

The net read: a services-led diversification growth story is being built, but it is offsetting a structurally shrinking core (revenue declined from $2,063M in 2023 to $1,837M in 2025) and is not yet large enough to drive consolidated growth while consensus estimates fall. Growth prospects are modest.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative (a GAAP loss), so the headline -11x multiple is uninformative. On a cash basis the picture is more reasonable: EV of roughly $1.4B (~$745M market cap plus ~$660M net debt) against guided 2026 EBITDA implies ~5.6-6.1x EV/EBITDA, a fair multiple for a cyclical with secular headwinds.

Peer comparison is of limited use given heterogeneity in the steel cohort (peer median P/E 36.0, but several peers carry negative or distorted multiples — AMR -63x, CLF -6.0x). The point estimate to anchor on is that SXC's cash-flow multiple is undemanding while its earnings are depressed by a one-time charge.

The valuation case is not a clean asymmetric setup, for three reasons. First, the stock has already captured most of the impairment-reversal narrative in its ~57% rally to $8.64. Second, the sell-side targets sit only modestly above the current price (limited implied upside), and those targets are vulnerable to the falling-estimate trend. Third, a cheap absolute price on a cyclical with deteriorating estimates is the classic value-trap profile; cheapness alone is not a catalyst. We do not see a specific, near-term mispricing the market is missing: the cash flows are understood, the recovery is guided, and the price reflects both. The dividend yield provides a soft floor, but its coverage is contingent on the EBITDA recovery, so it does not anchor a hard valuation case. Fair value is roughly in line with where the stock trades.

## Competitive Landscape

SunCoke's heat-recovery technology is a real differentiator: cleaner, higher-quality coke with monetizable energy byproducts, to the point that the EPA used SunCoke facilities to set MACT standards. Within its niche (outsourced coke supply to integrated steelmakers, ~38% of U.S. blast-furnace coke capacity), the moat is genuine and reinforced by long-term take-or-pay contracts and ~17.8M tons of coke backlog (~8-year average term).

The trajectory of that core market is the problem. The U.S. steel industry is tilting structurally toward EAF/mini-mills, which use little or no coke, and toward DRI, a multi-year secular erosion the 10-K flags explicitly as the long-term existential risk. The Algoma breach demonstrated that the contractual moat is only as durable as the financial health and strategic intent of counterparties, and SunCoke's two largest customers are exactly the integrated, blast-furnace-dependent producers most exposed to that transition. U.S. Steel is now under Nippon ownership, adding uncertainty to Granite City's blast-furnace future, and the Granite City contract recontracts annually.

Management's response (the Phoenix pivot into EAF-agnostic mill services, plus foundry coke from Jewell and export/spot tons) is the right strategic hedge and is showing early traction. The competitive position is therefore deteriorating in the legacy core but improving in services; on balance it is stable-to-slightly-eroding with a credible diversification path.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Customer concentration (~73%).** Cliffs ($265M) plus U.S. Steel ($66.9M) accounted for ~73% of Q1 2026 revenue. The entire core thesis depends on two integrated steelmakers' health and contract behavior.

2. **Counterparty/contract fragility (materialized).** The Algoma breach at end-Q3 2025 caused the impairment and the Haverhill I closure. SunCoke is pursuing recovery, but litigation outcomes are uncertain. The receivables allowance dropped from $11.1M to $0.7M, suggesting a write-off resolution.

3. **Granite City recontracting.** Extended only through December 2026 (590K tons, ~16% of capacity) at less favorable economics, with annual recontracting risk and strategic uncertainty under Nippon.

4. **Secular EAF transition.** The long-term existential threat to blast-furnace coke demand, hedged but not eliminated by Phoenix.

5. **Operational fragility.** Heat-recovery ovens cannot be idled without risking battery integrity, forcing production even absent a buyer. The Middletown turbine failure knocked out power revenue into late Q2 2026.

6. **Leverage and rising capex.** Debt up ~40% YoY post-Phoenix while EBITDA fell; the 2026 capex competes directly with the deleveraging goal and dividend.

7. **Falling estimates.** Consensus has cut current-year EPS 64% over 90 days and flipped next year to a loss, the cleanest forward warning in the file.

8. **Environmental/regulatory and import pressure.** New EPA MACT standards in litigation/reconsideration, a pending Middletown PM2.5 nonattainment designation, and coke import/dumping pressure on non-contracted tons.

Several of these risks are not yet fully priced; the falling estimate trend suggests the market is still digesting the recovery's durability. The impairment-driven loss, the leverage step-up, and the customer concentration are all in the public record and at least partly reflected.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The 2025 loss was a one-time, non-cash impairment that masked a cash-generative core. Operating cash flow stays strong, funding deleveraging to below 3x and the dividend. The four-insider open-market buying cluster signals that management and board, with full information, viewed the loss as transitory and the franchise as intact. The Phoenix pivot diversifies away from the EAF threat and is scaling fast (+91% YoY services EBITDA), reframing SunCoke as a diversified steel-services provider. As estimates stabilize and the recovery prints, the stock re-rates toward $10.

**Bear case.** The core coke business faces multi-year secular decline as steel shifts to EAF, and the Algoma breach proved the take-or-pay moat is conditional on counterparty solvency. With 73% revenue concentration in two stressed integrated steelmakers, contract or volume disappointments recur. Consensus estimates are falling hard, and the company missed in three of the last four quarters. Leverage is up 40%, capex is rising, and FCF barely covers the dividend, putting the payout at risk if EBITDA disappoints. After a 57% rally, much of the recovery is priced; downside re-tests the $5.50-$6.50 range, roughly 25-35% lower.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The recovery thesis and the insider conviction are genuine, but they are offset by falling estimates, customer concentration, and a price that already reflects the rebound. The honest classification is uncertain: the directional evidence does not point cleanly up or down.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $5.50-$6.50, the level at which insiders themselves bought, restoring asymmetric upside on the cash-flow base.
- Estimate revisions stabilizing and turning positive (next-year consensus returning to profitability) alongside two consecutive in-line or beating quarters.
- A successful Algoma recovery/settlement, or a Granite City re-contracting on durable terms, removing the two clearest contract overhangs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut materially below the $230-250M EBITDA range, or a covenant approach as leverage fails to track toward <3x.
- A dividend cut, which would confirm FCF strain and undermine the income floor.
- Loss or material renegotiation of a Cliffs or U.S. Steel contract, or evidence the EAF transition is accelerating customer attrition faster than Phoenix can offset.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (testing whether the Middletown turbine recovery and inventory dynamics support the guided cash flow) and progress against the year-end <3x leverage target. The Granite City recontracting decision before December 2026 is the key dated catalyst.

## Conclusion

SunCoke's 2025 GAAP loss looks alarming in isolation but was driven by a non-cash impairment; the cash engine kept running. That, plus the insider buying cluster near the lows and a fast-scaling Phoenix services business, makes the structural-impairment case too strong a conclusion. At the same time, the bullish case is constrained by extreme customer concentration, a real multi-year EAF threat, leverage up 40% against rising capex, thin dividend coverage, and consensus estimates falling sharply. After a ~57% rally off the 52-week low, with sell-side targets only modestly higher, the favorable and unfavorable factors roughly cancel.

We rate SXC a **HOLD**. The cash flows and insider conviction warrant owning through the recovery if already held and collecting the ~5.4% yield, but the falling estimate trend, concentration risk, and a price that has captured most of the rebound keep us from a stronger call. We would turn constructively interested again near $5.50-$6.00, where management and the board themselves were buyers.
