**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Euronet Worldwide is a 30-year-old global payments and cross-border transaction processor operating in 200+ countries through three roughly balanced segments: EFT Processing (ATMs/POS, DCC, and now CoreCard issuing), epay (prepaid/digital content distribution), and Money Transfer (Ria, Xe, Dandelion). The business is profitable, generating roughly $434M of free cash flow in 2025 and a ~24% ROE, with adjusted EPS that has grown from $3.66 in 2021 to $9.40 in 2025, a ~2.5x increase. Despite that earnings growth, the stock has roughly halved over two years and sits down ~36% over the trailing twelve months at $64.41, against a 52-week high of $107.91. Five-year cumulative total shareholder return of 52.5 versus a peer group at 153.9 quantifies the central feature of this name: it is a multiple-compression story, not an earnings-collapse story.

The compression is not arbitrary. Q1 2026 results show the reality beneath a headline +11% reported revenue print: roughly $60M of the revenue gain was FX translation (74% of revenue is non-USD), operating income declined to $72.0M from $75.2M, and operating margin compressed to 7.1%. The two largest segments both saw margin erosion: EFT margin fell from 10.0% to 7.9% as low-margin CoreCard/Bilt issuing volume scaled, and Money Transfer margin fell from 10.8% to 9.9% with ex-FX revenue declining outright on U.S.→Mexico immigration enforcement. The bear case that the market is pricing is structural erosion in two of three legs; the bull case is that a profitable, cash-generative payments franchise trading at ~6.5x earnings is mispriced and due to re-rate.

On balance we maintain a HOLD. The valuation offers real asymmetry if the digital pivot (direct-to-consumer +35%, Dandelion B2B rails) and the platform repositioning hold the line against corridor softness, and management alignment is unusually strong (CEO "comp actually paid" went negative, a director bought stock in the open market at ~$67). But the near-term fundamental trajectory is negative: operating income falling, both major segments compressing, and the most acute headwind (remittance corridors) is materializing now, not abating. We need evidence the 10-15% EPS growth target survives the immigration drag before underwriting a re-rating. This is an uncertain hold rather than a conviction call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Euronet (HQ: Leawood, Kansas) is one of the world's largest independent, non-bank payment networks. Its three segments are roughly balanced by revenue:

- **EFT Processing (~29% of revenue):** Owns/operates ~52,600 ATMs and ~608,000 POS terminals across 70 countries. Revenue is driven by ATM withdrawal fees, dynamic currency conversion (DCC) margin, surcharges, outsourced ATM/card management, the REN software platform, and, since the October 2025 CoreCard acquisition, U.S. card issuing/processing including the marquee Bilt credit-card relationship.
- **epay (~29% of revenue):** A large retail distribution network (~731,000 POS) across Europe and Asia distributing prepaid mobile airtime, digital media, gift cards, and branded payments for 1,000+ brands. About 75% of segment revenue is now digital content, a meaningful shift away from legacy mobile top-up.
- **Money Transfer (~42% of revenue):** Ria Money Transfer (cash + digital, 20M+ customers/year, emerging-market focus), Xe (consumer/SMB FX and cross-border), and Dandelion (API-based real-time cross-border rails reaching 4.1B bank accounts / 3.7B wallets, Euronet's asset-light B2B infrastructure play).

The competitive moats are the physical ATM/POS estate (hard to replicate), regulatory licenses across dozens of jurisdictions, and the Dandelion correspondent network. The company competes with Wise, Remitly, and Western Union in money transfer; its ATM economics are governed substantially by Visa/Mastercard rules; and epay faces fragmented retail-distribution competition.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue has compounded at ~6%: $3.36B (2022) → $3.69B (2023) → $3.99B (2024) → $4.24B (2025). Adjusted EPS grew from $3.66 (2021) to $9.40 (2025), aided heavily by an aggressive buyback that reduced shares from ~50.2M (2022) to ~38.1M (Q1 2026), a ~24% reduction. Net income reached ~$309.5M in 2025. The earnings power is real and the cash conversion is high.

**Q1 2026 — the texture beneath the headline.** Revenue of $1,011.8M (+11% reported) was substantially FX-flattered. Net income to Euronet of $37.5M was essentially flat. Segment detail tells the real story:

- **EFT Processing:** Revenue +27% (CoreCard + REN + acquiring) but operating income flat at $23.4M. The cause is the Bilt credit card on CoreCard's platform — high revenue at low margin — plus expansion into lower-priced markets and inflation/headcount costs.
- **epay:** The standout. Operating income +21%, margin expanding from 10.0% to 11.0% on favorable transaction mix.
- **Money Transfer:** Revenue +2% reported but down ex-FX. Operating income −7%. The driver is U.S.→Mexico transfers hit by immigration enforcement plus Middle East weakness, partly offset by +35% direct-to-consumer digital growth and Dandelion. Gross margin expanded (46.3% → 47.6%), but management is reinvesting the gains in digital marketing to defend share.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 FCF of ~$434M is strong. Q1 operating cash flow of −$122M reflects normal money-transfer working-capital seasonality, not deterioration. Total debt is $2.58B, with key components being the $1.0B 0.625% convertible due 2030 (conversion price ~$127, far above the current ~$64 stock), $693M 1.375% Senior Notes maturing May 2026 (imminent refinancing, almost certainly at a higher coupon), $570.7M on the revolver, and a $250M uncommitted ATM vault-cash line. Against this sit ~$1.2B unrestricted cash and ~$1.23B revolver availability.

A critical interpretive point: screening tools that flag an Altman Z of ~1.4 and D/E of ~4x are misleading for this business. Those ratios are distorted by ~$1.4B of settlement assets/obligations and ~$871M of ATM vault cash that are structurally offset. This is a profitable payments processor with normal industry mechanics. The genuine refinancing exposure — $693M of notes rolling from a 1.375% coupon and ~$820M+ of floating-rate debt at ~5.4% in a higher-for-longer rate environment — is a real and quantifiable headwind to net interest expense.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks have been aggressive: $667.7M repurchased in 2025, $101M in Q1 2026 at ~$71.58 average, with $168.4M remaining on the $400M authorization through June 2027. Management has been buying above the current price, leaning into a depressed multiple. They also purchased $99.8M of capped calls to limit convertible dilution above $127.

The financial-health picture is mixed. Cash generation, ROE, and the diversified revenue base support a score above the midpoint, but compressing operating margins, the imminent refinancing at higher rates, and rising floating-rate interest cost temper it. We score Financial Health 5.5, recovering slightly from the prior 4.5, on recognition that the balance-sheet "distress" signal was a screening artifact while acknowledging the genuine margin and refinancing pressures.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers that point in opposite directions. The structural-bull layer: digital is working. Direct-to-consumer money transfer grew +35% YoY, Dandelion provides an asset-light B2B rail to banks and fintechs, epay's digital content mix (75%+) is expanding margin, and the CoreCard acquisition adds U.S. issuing scale and the Bilt relationship. The 2026 Investor Day repositioned the company from "an ATM company" to a unified global payments platform with a stated 10-15% EPS growth target.

The bear layer: ex-FX, the consolidated business is barely growing, and the largest segment (Money Transfer, ~42%) is declining ex-FX as the U.S.-Mexico cash corridor shrinks under immigration enforcement. The headline +27% EFT growth comes with a margin collapse, meaning a meaningful portion of incremental revenue is low-quality issuing volume rather than high-margin DCC/surcharge transactions.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current-year EPS consensus of $10.97 has drifted +1.0% over 90 days, and current-quarter has slipped −1.4% over 90 days. This is well short of the revision velocity that signals a genuine inflection; the buy signal associated with rapidly rising estimates plus accelerating revenue is absent here. The earnings-surprise record is a concern: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, with the three misses concentrated in the higher-EPS quarters. That pattern argues for modest, not high, confidence in forward consensus.

Net, the growth profile is mid-single-digit organic at the consolidated level, with a credible but unproven path to faster EPS growth via mix shift to digital, buyback support, and the platform strategy. The growth dimension is the most reliable predictor of returns in our framework, and here the signal is genuinely mixed: digital momentum and the platform thesis on one side, ex-FX softness and a stalled revision trend on the other. We score Growth Prospects 5.5, slightly below the prior 6.0, because the Q1 detail (operating income decline, ex-FX money-transfer contraction) tempers the optimism that drove the prior upgrade.

## Valuation Assessment

At $64.41 on ~$9.40 trailing adjusted EPS and a current-year consensus of $10.97, EEFT trades at roughly 6-7x forward earnings. That is an unusually low multiple for a profitable, diversified payments franchise generating ~24% ROE and ~$434M FCF.

The peer comparison is heterogeneous and instructive. The Transaction & Payment Processing median P/E is 19.7. Within the set, the dispersion is wide: Western Union at 5.4x (a declining-remittance franchise yielding 12.8%), Shift4 at 44x, Payoneer at 33.9x, NCR Atleos at 19.7x, and Evertec at 12.2x. EEFT at ~6.5x sits near the bottom of the range, closest to Western Union, and that comparison is the crux of the bear case. The market is pricing EEFT's money-transfer and ATM exposures as structurally eroding the way Western Union's are, rather than as a diversified platform with a working digital pivot. The bull case is that EEFT's epay engine, EFT's DCC economics, and the Dandelion B2B rail differentiate it enough to warrant a re-rating toward the mid-teens.

Our calibration is explicit that a low P/E alone is not a buy signal: cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive, and the discount must be backed by an identifiable catalyst. Here the candidate catalysts are real but unproven: the platform repositioning, the 10-15% EPS target, the continued buyback at a depressed multiple, and the possibility that the U.S.-Mexico corridor headwind proves cyclical rather than permanent. Against that, the multiple compression has persisted for two years through ~2.5x EPS growth, suggesting the market has a settled (and not obviously wrong) view that the quality of earnings — FX-flattered, margin-compressing, buyback-driven — does not merit a premium multiple.

Analyst price targets (low $75, median $87.5, high $102, mean $88.3) sit well above the current price. We treat these as informative but not as a floor: this is a cheap stock with stable-to-soft estimates and three recent misses, not a cheap stock with rising estimates, so the proximity-as-floor logic does not apply cleanly. The targets imply roughly 16-58% upside from the current price if the corridor headwind abates and the platform thesis is credited.

We score Valuation 6.0 — above midpoint to reflect the asymmetry of a ~6.5x multiple on a cash-generative franchise with a covered downside (FCF, buyback support), but capped below 7 because the cheapness has been persistent and is partly explained by deteriorating earnings quality rather than pure mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Euronet's competitive position is differentiated but pressured. Strengths: it is one of the largest independent payment networks globally, with a physical estate that is genuinely hard to replicate, regulatory licenses across dozens of jurisdictions, and an asset-light B2B rail (Dandelion) that reaches 4.1B bank accounts and 3.7B wallets, a credible infrastructure-as-a-service vector sold to banks, fintechs, and potentially big tech.

The pressures are segment-specific. In money transfer, digital-native players Wise and Remitly continue to compress pricing, and Euronet's response of spending its gross-margin gains on digital marketing to defend share confirms the competitive intensity. In EFT, secular cash-usage decline in mature markets and EU DCC regulation threaten the high-margin transaction economics, which is why the company is pushing into under-penetrated Asia-Pacific (high volume, low revenue per transaction) and into CoreCard issuing. The CoreCard pivot itself is double-edged: it powers the platform narrative and the Bilt relationship but is the proximate cause of EFT margin dilution, and the $40.2M "Large Individual Customer Relationship" intangible (Bilt) amortizing over just 2.4 years implies meaningful single-customer concentration and attrition risk.

The trajectory matters more than the level for this dimension. epay is improving (margin up, mix favorable). Money Transfer's consumer franchise is eroding on the legacy cash corridor while the digital piece gains. EFT is growing revenue but at lower margin. The net trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-deteriorating, which supports the prior modest decline in this dimension. We score Competitive Position 5.5, holding the prior reading.

## Risk Assessment

The most acute near-term risk is materializing now: U.S. immigration enforcement is directly reducing U.S.→Mexico remittance volumes, with some customers avoiding physical agent locations. This affects ~42% of revenue and is explicitly cited in the Q1 10-Q as the driver of the Money Transfer ex-FX decline. It is a specific, currently-active, company-relevant headwind with no clear timeline for resolution.

Other material risks:
- **EFT margin compression** from low-margin CoreCard/Bilt issuing and lower-priced geographies — structural so long as the growth narrative leans on EFT/CoreCard.
- **FX translation** — with 74% of revenue non-USD, a 10% USD move swings net income/working capital by ~$125-135M; recent reported growth is heavily FX-aided and reverses on a dollar rebound.
- **Refinancing/rate exposure** — the $693M Senior Notes due May 2026 reprice from 1.375%, and ~$820M+ of floating-rate debt sits at ~5.4%. In the current higher-for-longer environment, this is a quantifiable drag on EPS.
- **Regulatory** — AML/sanctions, EU PSD2/DCC rules (DCC is a high-margin EFT source vulnerable to EU regulation), data privacy, OECD Pillar 2.
- **Emerging stablecoin/AI disruption** — both opportunity (the Fireblocks infrastructure relationship enables stablecoin settlement on Euronet's network) and threat to traditional remittance economics.
- **Contingent items** — Italian withholding-tax dispute (~€19.4M, "reasonably possible"), Malaysian banknote fire loss (~$11M, expected recoverable). Neither currently material.
- **Governance flag** — an April 28, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 + 8.01) indicating a director/officer change concurrent with an "Other Events" disclosure that was not fully readable. This warrants follow-up.

Many of these risks are already reflected in the depressed multiple, which argues against an extreme low score. But the combination of a materializing corridor headwind with no resolution timeline, ongoing margin compression, and a refinancing that will raise interest cost means the idiosyncratic downside is real and not fully discounted. We score Risk Factors 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating the elevated 12.5% short interest with bearish positioning. Near-term ATM IV of 34.0% is roughly in line with one-year historical volatility of 32.8% (a 1.2% spread — fair), and IV sits in only the 3rd percentile of its own chain, indicating complacency rather than fear. The put/call skew is −4.7% (call-heavy) at the front month, and IV compressed 7.7 points (41.7% → 34.0%) as the stock rose 3.6% into this snapshot. The term structure is in normal contango (34.0% near vs 41.7% at six months). The six-month tenor shows a more put-tilted, higher-vol profile, consistent with the market pricing an upcoming earnings event and the rate/refinancing overhang. Net, the options market reads as neutral-to-mildly-constructive near-term, a mild divergence from the 12.5% short interest (6.2 days to cover): informed positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the elevated short base.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$95-102).** The market is pricing a profitable, diversified, FCF-generative payments franchise at ~6.5x earnings as if it were Western Union. The platform repositioning, +35% direct-to-consumer digital growth, the Dandelion B2B rail, and epay's expanding margins justify a re-rating toward the peer mid-teens. The U.S.-Mexico corridor headwind proves cyclical, the buyback retires shares at a depressed multiple (accretive), and the 10-15% EPS target is achieved, taking EPS to ~$12+ and supporting an analyst-target-range outcome of $95-102.

**Bear Case (~$48-52).** The Western Union comparison is correct: the legacy ATM and cash-remittance economics are in secular decline, EFT margin compression continues as low-quality CoreCard volume scales, ex-FX revenue stays roughly flat, and a USD rebound reverses the FX tailwind. The refinancing raises interest cost, the 10-15% EPS target is quietly abandoned, and the multiple stays at ~5x on flat-to-declining quality-adjusted earnings, taking the stock toward $48-52 (roughly −20-25% from current).

**Base Case (~$72-76).** The corridor headwind persists but is partly offset by digital and epay strength; EPS grows modestly toward the low end of the target on buyback support; the multiple holds at ~6.5-7x as the market waits for proof. A modest recovery toward the low-$70s, roughly +12-18% from current, with analyst targets acting as a soft pull higher if any single quarter clarifies the digital-versus-corridor trajectory.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The valuation asymmetry is genuine but the near-term fundamental trajectory is negative, and the catalysts for a re-rating are unproven. This is a name to monitor closely rather than to add or exit at current levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings release is the key checkpoint. The May 2026 Senior Note refinancing outcome and the trajectory of U.S.-Mexico remittance volumes are the proximate data points.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Money Transfer ex-FX revenue returns to growth, or the U.S.-Mexico corridor decline stabilizes, in two consecutive quarters.
- EFT operating margin recovers above 9% (signaling the CoreCard/Bilt mix dilution is being absorbed rather than structural).
- Consensus EPS estimates inflect upward (>5% over 90 days) on credible delivery toward the 10-15% target.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Operating income declines for a second consecutive quarter with ex-FX revenue contraction broadening beyond Money Transfer.
- The Senior Note refinancing comes at a materially punitive coupon (>5.5%) that meaningfully impairs EPS.
- Management abandons or materially lowers the 10-15% EPS growth target.
- The April 28 board/officer change, on follow-up, reveals a governance or strategic problem.

## Conclusion

Euronet is a cash-generative, diversified global payments processor trading at a multiple that would be cheap if the earnings were higher quality. The data confirm the prior HOLD thesis rather than reversing it: this remains a multiple-compression story, with adjusted EPS having grown ~2.5x over five years while the stock halved and total shareholder return badly lagged peers. The constructive elements — a ~6.5x multiple, ~$434M FCF, ~24% ROE, an aligned management team (negative CEO "comp actually paid," a director buying at ~$67), and a working digital pivot — are offset by a materializing remittance-corridor headwind, ongoing margin compression in the two largest segments, FX-flattered headline growth, and a refinancing that will raise interest cost.

The stock's ~36% twelve-month decline does not, by itself, signal further deterioration, but at this magnitude the burden of proof is on the bull, and the Q1 detail (operating income falling, ex-FX money transfer declining) shows the market is reacting to genuine softness, not just rate-driven sentiment. We need a quarter or two of evidence that digital and epay strength can offset the corridor drag, and that the 10-15% EPS target is credible, before underwriting a re-rating. Overall score 5.6; HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reassess at the next quarterly print.
