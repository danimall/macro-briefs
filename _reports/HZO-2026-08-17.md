## Executive Summary

The investment question at MarineMax has changed entirely. On August 9, 2026 the company signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger with SHM Holdco, LLC and Intrepid Holdco, Inc., affiliates of Safe Harbor Marinas, LLC, a portfolio company of Blackstone Infrastructure Partners L.P., under which each outstanding share converts into $53.00 in cash. The agreement was disclosed on August 10, 2026 in an 8-K (accession 0001193125-26-341302) and in additional soliciting material (DEFA14A, accession 0001193125-26-341307). The board unanimously approved the transaction following a competitive strategic review, with a fairness opinion from Wells Fargo Securities. Blackstone Infrastructure provided an equity commitment letter, and the buyer's obligation to close is not subject to any financing condition. On completion, the common stock is delisted from the NYSE and deregistered.

At the authoritative price of $52.21 the gross spread is $0.79, or 1.51%. The company targets a close by the end of calendar 2026; the contractual outside date is May 9, 2027, subject to two automatic three-month extensions to roughly November 9, 2027. Working from the frozen August 17 basis, 1.51% over 136 days to a December 31 close annualizes to approximately 4.1%; a March 31, 2027 close yields about 2.4%; the May 9, 2027 outside date yields about 2.1%. Against a short-dated cash yield, the best-case annualized return on the spread is roughly at or below cash, and the slippage cases are clearly below cash. [unverified — no risk-free rate is provided in the research brief or Phase 1 data] The downside if the deal breaks is 32% to 48%, using the $35.68 pre-announcement close and the $27.03 pre-speculation close of January 30, 2026 as reference points. Solving for the completion probability embedded in the price gives 95.4% against a $35.68 break and 96.9% against $27.03 — before any discount rate. Applying even a 3.7% cost of carry over a December close pushes the required probability to essentially 100%.

That arithmetic is the recommendation. The deal is well-constructed and likely to close; the residual return available to a new or continuing holder is not adequate compensation for a 32-48% left tail. Holders should trim into the market bid and redeploy to short-dated cash equivalents, which offer a comparable yield without regulatory or vote risk. The standalone business provides no support at $52.21: nine-month FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $90.8M was down 1.9% year over year, same-store sales fell 7% in the June quarter, FY2025 free cash flow was $11.9M on $2.309B of revenue, and consensus EPS estimates have been revised down 4.2% (current year) and 4.0% (next year) over the past 30 days. Prior score trajectory (4.2 → 4.3 → 4.1, three consecutive HOLDs) is superseded: the binary that made HOLD defensible has resolved, and the resolution price is now largely captured by the market.

## Company Overview

MarineMax operates over 120 locations worldwide, including more than 70 dealerships and 65 marina and storage facilities. It reports two segments. Retail Operations generated $609.1M of $611.3M consolidated revenue in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, and includes new and used boat retailing, finance and insurance referral income, parts and service, brokerage (Fraser Yachts, Northrop & Johnson), marina and storage operations including the IGY Marinas network, yacht charter, and digital platforms. Product Manufacturing (Cruisers Yachts, Aviara, Intrepid Powerboats) reported $33.3M of gross revenue in the quarter, of which $31.1M was eliminated as intersegment, implying roughly $2.1M of external third-party revenue. The manufacturing operations now function essentially as a captive supplier to the company's own dealerships, which means the segment's reported operating loss ($0.6M in the quarter, $11.8M over nine months) is substantially an internal transfer-pricing allocation rather than a market-tested result. The FY2025 write-off of the segment's entire $69.1M of goodwill is the economically meaningful signal: the vertical-integration strategy did not earn its cost of capital.

The FY2025 10-K (filed November 17, 2025) documents the structural constraints on the retail model. Approximately 18% of FY2025 revenue came from Brunswick-manufactured boats and approximately 6% from Azimut-Benetti Group, for which MarineMax is the exclusive U.S. dealer of the Azimut line. Dealer agreements permit manufacturers to designate exclusive territories, retain approval rights over changes in management and ownership, require consent to open, close, or relocate locations, and deny approval of future acquisitions. Florida generated approximately 54% of dealership revenue in FY2025, versus 53% in each of FY2024 and FY2023. Substantially all products are powered by Mercury Marine (a Brunswick division), Yamaha, or Volvo.

The asset that explains the transaction is the marina and superyacht services platform. The 10-K's own risk language describes competition for waterfront real estate, environmental and permitting barriers, and an increased waiting list for marina and storage availability in some U.S. markets — the profile of a supply-constrained asset class. The same disclosure notes the offsetting reality: governmental bodies control much of the land beneath and surrounding many marinas under leases of five to 50 years, and the company states it is unlikely to obtain fee-simple title. The value sits in the operating platform, entitlements, and long-dated leaseholds, not in unencumbered land.

## Financial Analysis

The cycle is best framed by the five-year Pay-versus-Performance series in the DEF 14A filed January 21, 2026: Adjusted EBITDA fell from $309.6M in FY2022 to $239.5M (FY2023), $160.2M (FY2024) and $109.8M (FY2025), a 64.5% decline from peak, while GAAP net income went from +$198.0M to a $31.6M loss. The company TSR index of 98.68 at FY2025 year-end against a peer index of 165.48 records a cumulative five-year total return of roughly -1.3% versus +65.5% for the peer group. FY2025 revenue was approximately $2.309B, down about 5.0% from $2.431B, with operating income of roughly $34.0M against $128.2M and interest expense of $71.2M. The loss was driven substantially by the non-cash $69.1M goodwill impairment.

The June 2026 quarter showed genuine margin recovery with important qualifications. Revenue of $611.3M fell 7.0% year over year on same-store sales of -7%, but gross profit rose 9.2% to $218.1M and gross margin expanded 530 basis points to 35.7%. Income from operations was $37.2M against a prior-year loss of $41.5M (which included the impairment). Adjusted EBITDA was $51.3M versus $35.5M; adjusted diluted EPS was $0.81. Approximately 110 basis points of the margin gain — roughly $6.7M — came from a tariff refund, the majority relating to boat sales recorded earlier in the fiscal year, leaving underlying expansion near 420 basis points. SG&A deteriorated 340 basis points to 29.6% of revenue, partly on $4.6M of transaction costs in the quarter and $13.3M over nine months against $1.6M in the prior year.

The nine-month picture is flat rather than recovering: revenue of $1,643.8M versus $1,757.1M (-6.4%), gross profit $559.8M versus $558.8M (+0.2%), Adjusted EBITDA $90.8M versus $92.5M (-1.9%). Reaffirmed FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $110-125M implies a fourth quarter of $19.2M to $34.2M against a derived $17.3M in the prior-year quarter — a range wide enough to indicate limited visibility. The FY2026 midpoint of $117.5M remains 62% below the FY2022 peak.

The balance sheet improved materially. Between September 30, 2025 and June 30, 2026, inventories fell $78.7M to $788.6M (down 13.0% year over year), floor plan borrowings fell $107.4M to $608.3M, and total interest-bearing debt including floor plan fell $136.5M to $971.0M. Excluding floor plan — the appropriate treatment for a dealer, since floor plan is secured, self-liquidating inventory finance and its interest expense remains inside the company's Adjusted EBITDA definition — net debt at June 30 was $187.9M ($335.2M long-term debt plus $27.5M current maturities less $174.8M cash), or 1.74x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $108.1M. An 8-K filed June 30, 2026 (accession 0001193125-26-290439) disclosed termination of the prior facility and entry into new $1.49B senior secured facilities extending maturities to 2031 with an expanded revolver and lower borrowing costs.

Two cautions temper the improvement. First, the nine-month deleveraging was funded substantially by liquidating working capital, a source that is finite and reverses when volumes recover. Second, cash conversion remains the weakest element: FY2025 free cash flow of $11.9M represents a 0.5% margin against $71.2M of interest expense. An Altman Z-Score computed from FY2025 data is 1.98 (grey zone); adding back the non-cash impairment lifts it to about 2.07, and substituting market equity at the deal price gives about 2.16. The score does not clear the grey zone under either adjustment, though the reading is depressed mechanically by the floor-plan liability that every boat and auto dealer carries. The correct characterization is a leveraged, cyclically compressed dealer with adequate near-term liquidity, not an issuer near distress.

Note one data inconsistency in the supplied market fields: trailing and forward P/E are both shown as 289.11 while trailing EPS is shown as -$1.43. The negative EPS is consistent with the FY2025 GAAP loss; the P/E figure cannot be reconciled to it and is not used here. Multiples in this report are computed on Adjusted EBITDA and book value, which reconcile across the earnings release, the proxy, and the deal press release.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth prospects are weak and, more importantly, no longer accrue to public holders. Same-store sales were negative 7% in the most recent quarter. Nine-month revenue declined 6.4%. Consensus estimates are falling: current-quarter EPS has been cut 45% in 30 days to $0.05, current-year EPS cut 4.2% to $0.71, and next-year EPS cut 4.0% to $1.57. The one positive revision — next quarter from -$0.14 to $0.01 — reflects a small base and only five contributing analysts. The earnings track record is three misses in four quarters, including a -3.0% miss in the June quarter against a $0.84 estimate.

Business-line mix is genuinely improving. Management attributes margin gains to superyacht services, marinas including IGY, finance and insurance, and parts and service, all higher-margin and more recurring than boat sales. That is a real improvement in earnings quality. It should not be conflated with reduced concentration: Florida remained 54% of dealership revenue in FY2025 (up from 53% in each of the two prior years), and supplier concentration at Brunswick and Azimut-Benetti was unchanged at roughly 24% combined.

The relevant growth observation is what the buyer is underwriting rather than what the seller is reporting. Blackstone Infrastructure, through Safe Harbor Marinas, is buying a marina and superyacht services platform as infrastructure — long-duration, permit-constrained waterfront positions with recurring slip and storage revenue. The joint press release reorders MarineMax's self-description to lead with marina operator and superyacht services, inverting the emphasis of the company's own July 23 release. Public shareholders receive the revaluation as a one-time cash payment; they do not participate in the multi-year cash flows the buyer expects. That distinction is why growth prospects score low here despite a defensible asset story.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is now anchored to a fixed contractual number, so the analysis reduces to spread economics.

**Spread and annualized return.** At $52.21, the gross spread to $53.00 is $0.79, or 1.513% of the current price. From the August 17 basis:

| Close scenario | Days | Years | Annualized |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 31, 2026 (company target) | 136 | 0.373 | 4.06% |
| March 31, 2027 | 226 | 0.619 | 2.44% |
| May 9, 2027 (outside date) | 265 | 0.726 | 2.08% |
| ~November 9, 2027 (after both extensions) | 449 | 1.230 | 1.23% |

**Implied completion probability.** Solving $52.21 = p × $53.00 + (1−p) × B, ignoring time value:

- B = $35.68 (August 7, 2026 close, pre-announcement): p = 16.53/17.32 = **95.4%**
- B = $30.00: p = 22.21/23.00 = **96.6%**
- B = $27.03 (January 30, 2026, pre-speculation): p = 25.18/25.97 = **96.9%**

Add a discount rate and the arithmetic turns adverse. At a 3.7% cost of carry over 0.373 years, a buyer requires an undiscounted expected value of about $52.93, which against a $35.68 break price implies a completion probability of roughly 99.6%. Expected return at a plausible probability set — 94% close at $53.00, 2% topping bid at $55.50, 4% break to $33.00 — gives $52.25, or +0.08% over four and a half months. **Deal multiple context.** Equity value at $53.00 is $1,170.6M on 22.087M shares outstanding and $1,227.4M on 23.158M diluted shares. Adding net debt excluding floor plan of $187.9M and $11.4M of non-controlling interests gives enterprise value of approximately $1.43B, consistent with the company's stated "approximately $1.5 billion" after equity-award treatment and transaction expenses. That is 12.1x the FY2026 guided Adjusted EBITDA midpoint of $117.5M, 13.2x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $108.1M, and 13.0x FY2025. Book value attributable to MarineMax of $952.5M is $43.13 per share, so $53.00 is 1.23x book; tangible book after deducting $525.1M of goodwill and $34.0M of other intangibles is $17.81 per share, making the deal 3.0x tangible book. Against a Specialty Stores peer median P/E of 13.6 and peer trading multiples that would imply far less, the deal price is well above where the public market valued the business — the pre-speculation $27.03 close implied roughly 4.7x guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA on the same ex-floor-plan construction.

**Topping-bid arithmetic.** The $31,650,000 company termination fee equals $1.43 per share on 22.087M shares, or 2.70% of equity value at $53.00 — market-standard and non-preclusive. A competing bidder must exceed roughly $54.43 to leave shareholders better off after the fee. Reuters reported on July 24, 2026 that Blackstone, Donerail, and Centerbridge were among final bidders, sourced to two people familiar with the matter and unconfirmed by any company or SEC filing. If accurate, multiple sophisticated bidders — including an activist with a documented earlier proposal — already declined to exceed $53.00, which caps residual upside.

The valuation score of 3.0 reflects capped upside of 1.5% against 32-48% downside, not cheapness or expensiveness in the conventional sense. Analyst price targets bracket the situation precisely: high $53.00 (the deal price), low $35.00 (approximately the pre-announcement standalone level). Consensus recommendations moved from six buys and three holds one month ago to two buys and seven holds — the mechanical downgrade pattern that follows a stock trading at deal consideration.

## Competitive Landscape

The recreational marine retail market is in the third-to-fourth year of post-pandemic normalization. MarineMax's own results frame the stage: revenue down for multiple years, Adjusted EBITDA 62-65% below FY2022, same-store sales -7%, and industry destocking only now producing what management calls healthier pricing dynamics. Gross profit rising 9.2% on revenue falling 7.0% is a recognizable late-destocking pattern — margin recovery preceding volume recovery — rather than a demand inflection.

The company's bargaining position with suppliers is structurally weak. Brunswick and Azimut-Benetti set model-year pricing at their sole discretion, designate territories, and hold consent rights over ownership changes and acquisitions. Downstream, the industry is highly fragmented, with competition from single-location dealers, national parts chains, online retailers, and private used-boat sales. Scale confers purchasing and service advantages without conferring pricing power.

Trajectory is mixed, which is why the competitive position score sits below neutral rather than well below. The retail franchise is losing volume and remains supplier-controlled and Florida-concentrated. The marina, superyacht services, brokerage, and F&I businesses are gaining share of gross profit and carry structural scarcity from waterfront supply constraints and permitting barriers. An infrastructure buyer paying 12.1x for an asset the public market valued at roughly 4.7x is evidence that the marina platform is worth more inside a consolidated marina operator than as a segment of a cyclical retailer. Part of that gap is auction competition and part is synergy with Safe Harbor's existing platform — neither of which a standalone public holder could have realized.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-break risk dominates.** The enumerated conditions are majority shareholder approval, HSR clearance, and certain foreign antitrust and foreign-direct-investment clearances. The absence of a financing condition, backed by a Blackstone Infrastructure equity commitment letter, removes the most common source of sponsor-deal failure and is the single most favorable term for holders.

**Antitrust and FDI review carry real substance.** Safe Harbor is described as a marina and superyacht service business, and MarineMax operates 65 marina and storage facilities plus IGY's international network. This is a horizontal combination in marinas, so HSR review has more content than a purely financial buyout would. The counter-consideration is that marina markets are intensely local and highly fragmented, so geographic overlap may be limited. Safe Harbor's marina count and market-by-market overlap could not be verified in this analysis, so the horizontal-overlap concern is an untested hypothesis rather than a substantiated finding. The requirement for foreign antitrust and FDI clearances is consistent with IGY's footprint across the Caribbean, Europe, and the Middle East. The nine-month outside date with two automatic three-month extensions indicates the parties themselves contemplated a regulatory path that could extend well past the stated end-of-2026 target — the extension mechanics, not the target date, are the honest basis for timing.

**Shareholder-vote risk is low but not nil.** The threshold is a majority of outstanding shares, against a 96% premium to the January 30, 2026 pre-speculation close, a unanimous board recommendation, and a Wells Fargo fairness opinion. Donerail, reported as both an activist and a losing bidder, is presumably a shareholder; its stake size and public position on the $53.00 agreement are unverified.

**Information gaps.** No definitive merger proxy (DEFM14A) had been filed as of this analysis. That means no special meeting date, no Background of the Merger, no Wells Fargo valuation ranges, and no golden-parachute disclosure. The vote date is the primary determinant of annualized spread return, so its absence widens the timing distribution and biases the realistic annualized figure toward the 2-3% end of the table above. Change-of-control provisions in the June 2026 credit facilities are also unexamined.

**Break-scenario fundamentals.** If the deal fails, the company reverts to a standalone dealer with maturities extended to 2031, $174.8M of cash, net debt ex-floor-plan of $187.9M at 1.74x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, and $141M of nine-month net-debt reduction banked — a materially better position than the FY2025 10-K describes. Offsetting that, same-store sales are negative, free cash flow was 0.5% of revenue in FY2025, Florida is 54% of dealership revenue with named hurricane exposure (Helene and Milton in 2024), and consensus estimates are falling. A break would also remove the strategic-alternative option that the auction consumed, which is why a break price below the $35.68 pre-announcement level is plausible; $30-34 is a reasonable working range.

**Macro overlay.** [unverified — the research brief and Phase 1 data contain no macro figures on mortgage rates, application demand, or IPO appetite] Large-ticket recreational purchases sit at the discretionary end of the consumer channel, and management describes demand as "tempered by a cautious consumer environment." This matters only in a break scenario, but it argues against assuming a quick recovery to $40-plus on standalone fundamentals.

### Options Market Signal

The informative reading is the long-dated tenor: six-month ATM implied volatility of 5.5% against 62.7% one-year realized volatility. That collapse is the signature of an equity pinned to fixed cash consideration — the options market is pricing near-certain completion and almost no residual business risk. Put/call open interest fell from 0.89 to 0.13 between August 5 and August 17, consistent with hedges being lifted after the announcement. The near-term 28.5% ATM implied volatility on a three-day expiry, flagged as backwardation and "imminent stress," should not be read that way here; it reflects wide quotes across a 25-contract chain on a stock trading in a $0.46 range for five sessions, and the "8.9% implied move" it produces is inconsistent with the deal-pinned price action. Short interest of 18.3% of float and 10.4 days to cover is almost certainly a pre-announcement settlement observation: the stock gapped from $35.68 to $52.12 on August 10 with 7.67M shares traded, roughly 19x average volume, which is where the covering occurred. Neither the options nor the short-interest data contradicts the merger-arbitrage framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($55.50, +6.3%).** A topping bid emerges above the roughly $54.43 economic hurdle created by the $31.65M termination fee, or the fiduciary out combined with activist pressure extracts a modest bump. Assign low probability: the process ran from a January 30, 2026 unsolicited proposal through an August 9 signing, three reported final bidders competed, and the board obtained a fairness opinion. Underbidders who saw the same diligence declined to exceed $53.00.

**Base case ($53.00, +1.5%).** HSR and foreign clearances are obtained, shareholders approve, and the deal closes. Timing is the swing factor: a December 2026 close delivers approximately 4.1% annualized, a March 2027 close 2.4%, and the outside date 2.1%. Absent a filed proxy and vote date, the middle of that range is the reasonable expectation, which places the return near or below short-dated cash yields for materially more risk. [unverified — no risk-free rate is provided in the sources]

**Bear case ($33.00, -36.8%).** Antitrust or FDI review produces a second request, remedy demand, or refusal, and the deal terminates. The stock reverts toward pre-announcement levels, likely below the $35.68 August 7 close because the strategic alternative has been exhausted and the standalone story is a mid-single-digit-declining dealer with 0.5% free cash flow margin in a deteriorating consumer environment. The $27.03 January 30 close is the tail of that tail.

**Why REDUCE rather than HOLD.** A HOLD implies approximately fair risk-adjusted return from the current price. Here the price already embeds 95-97% completion certainty at a zero discount rate. On the probability set above, expected return over roughly four and a half months is close to zero, and the distribution is 1.5% right-tail against a 37% left-tail. That is less-than-fair expected risk-adjusted return combined with unfavorable downside asymmetry, which is the definition of REDUCE on this scale. The prior three consecutive HOLDs were justified by an unresolved process with genuine two-sided outcomes; that condition no longer holds.

**Why not AVOID.** The deal is well-structured — no financing condition, an equity commitment from a large infrastructure sponsor, unanimous board support, a fairness opinion, and a buyer with strategic logic in the acquired marina platform. Completion is the strong base case. Dedicated merger-arbitrage capital with cheap financing and a diversified book can rationally hold the position to close. The rating addresses fundamental capital, for which the spread is not compensation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into the market bid at or near the current $52.21 level, with the objective of reducing to zero for fundamental mandates before the shareholder vote. There is no reason to wait for a specific catalyst: the spread is 1.51% and the maximum incremental gain from holding to close is $0.79 per share, versus a 32-48% loss on a break. Redeploy to short-dated Treasury bills or equivalent. For arbitrage-mandated capital that elects to carry the position, size it as a cash substitute and pair it with an explicit break-price assumption of $30-34 rather than the $35.68 pre-announcement print.

**Upgrade triggers (would restore HOLD or better).**
1. A widening of the spread to $2.50 or more (price at or below approximately $50.50) without deterioration in deal terms, which would raise the annualized return above 12% on a December close and restore genuine compensation for break risk.
2. Filing of the DEFM14A with a special meeting date inside 60 days plus confirmation that the HSR waiting period expired without a second request. That combination compresses the timing distribution to the December scenario and materially reduces the regulatory tail.
3. A credible competing proposal above $54.43, evidenced by an 8-K or amended proxy rather than press reporting.

**Downgrade triggers (would move to AVOID).**
1. Issuance of a second request under HSR, or public identification of a contested foreign FDI review, either of which would push the realistic close beyond the May 9, 2027 outside date and cut the annualized return below 2%.
2. Any amendment reducing the consideration, adding a condition, or introducing financing contingency.
3. A definitive proxy disclosing that shareholders holding a meaningful block, including any activist holder, intend to vote against, combined with an ISS or Glass Lewis recommendation against the transaction.

**Reassessment timeline.** Two dated checkpoints. First, the filing of the definitive merger proxy and the resulting special meeting date — the single most important missing input, and the determinant of whether the annualized return is 4% or 2%. Second, the FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year results (fiscal year ended September 30, 2026, historically reported in mid-to-late November), which will test the $110-125M Adjusted EBITDA guidance and establish the standalone earnings base that matters only in a break scenario. Both should be reviewed against the triggers above.

## Conclusion

MarineMax has agreed to be acquired for $53.00 per share in cash by Safe Harbor Marinas, a Blackstone Infrastructure portfolio company, in a transaction with no financing condition, unanimous board support, a fairness opinion, and a clear strategic rationale rooted in the marina and superyacht services platform rather than boat retailing. The deal will most likely close. At $52.21 the market has already paid for that outcome: the 1.51% gross spread implies 95-97% completion certainty before any discount rate, annualizes to roughly 4.1% on the company's own end-of-2026 target and to 2.1% at the contractual outside date, and sits against 32-48% downside on a break. The definitive proxy and special meeting date are not yet on file, which biases realistic timing toward the lower end of that return range.

The standalone business does not backstop the price. Nine-month FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $90.8M was down 1.9% year over year, same-store sales fell 7%, FY2025 free cash flow was 0.5% of revenue, estimates are being revised down, and Florida concentration plus Brunswick and Azimut supplier control remain unchanged. The improvements that are real — 420 basis points of underlying gross margin expansion, $136.5M of debt reduction, maturities extended to 2031 — matter primarily as the floor in a break scenario, and they support a break price in the low-to-mid $30s rather than a return to the pre-announcement $35.68.

**REDUCE.** Monetize the spread and redeploy to cash equivalents. Revisit only if the spread widens materially without a change in deal terms, or if a filed definitive proxy plus HSR clearance compresses the timeline enough to make the remaining 1.5% worth the left tail.
