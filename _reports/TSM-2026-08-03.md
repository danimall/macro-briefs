## Executive Summary

TSMC delivered its strongest quarter on record in Q2 2026 and the stock fell. Revenue of $40.20B landed at the top of the $39.0-40.2B guided range, gross margin of 67.7% beat the 67.5% ceiling of guidance, and operating margin of 60.3% beat the 58.5% ceiling by 180bp. Management then raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to "slightly above 40%" from "more than 30%" — roughly ten percentage points of full-year growth added mid-year. The market response on July 16 was a 4% decline, against a 1.4% decline in the Nasdaq 100 the same day, attributed by contemporaneous reporting to the simultaneous capex raise to $60-64B and an additional $100B Arizona commitment.

The resulting setup is the reason we stay constructive. At $403.42 the ADR trades at 18.7x next-year consensus EPS of $21.61 and 24.0x current-year $16.82. Next-year estimates have risen 12.0% over 90 days and 7.3% over 30 days; the current-quarter estimate is up 6.7% in 30 days and the next-quarter estimate up 11.3%. Earnings have beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters by 4.4% to 10.9%. The implied PEG on next-year earnings is roughly 0.65-0.73 depending on which growth base is used. The lowest published analyst target of $430 sits 6.6% above spot, and the mean of $540 sits above the market price — a configuration in which even the most bearish covering analyst is above spot.

The bear case is arithmetic that management itself published. Q3 gross margin is guided to a 66.0% midpoint, 1.7pp below the Q2 actual, and operating margin to 57.0%, 3.3pp below. Capex intensity has climbed from 33.0% of revenue in FY2024 to 33.6% on a trailing basis, and free cash flow margin has compressed from 30.06% to 26.32% to 25.75% across the same period even as gross margin expanded roughly eight points. TSM is therefore cheaper than a year ago on earnings and more expensive on cash flow (P/FCF from 38.6x to roughly 54x). An investor buying on the forward P/E is implicitly underwriting that this capex vintage earns an adequate return, which will not be verifiable for several years. Combined with Taiwan geographic concentration and the AI-capex dependency that runs through the entire complex, we score risk factors below average at 5.0 and size accordingly. We maintain BUY with a base case of $475 (17.7% upside), bull $560, bear $320.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry. It manufactures integrated circuits designed and owned by other companies — NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, and Broadcom are among the named customers — and does not design or market its own branded chips into end systems. The commercial relationship runs one direction: those companies pay TSMC to fabricate silicon whose architecture, software ecosystem, and end-market economics they retain. TSMC captures a manufacturing margin across whichever accelerator architecture wins, which is the structural attraction of owning it as an AI-compute exposure rather than owning any single design house.

Reported segmentation is coarse. FY2025 revenue of NT$3,809,054M splits into Wafer at NT$3,272,553M (85.9%) and Other Product at NT$536,501M (14.1%). We could not obtain node-level or platform-level revenue mix from the filings, so we do not state HPC-as-percent-of-revenue or 2nm-as-percent-of-revenue as facts. The "Other Product" line plausibly contains advanced packaging (CoWoS/SoIC), mask-making, and testing, which would make it strategically weightier than its revenue share implies, but we cannot confirm what sits inside it.

A sourcing constraint worth stating up front: TSMC is a foreign private issuer filing Forms 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K, and its insiders are exempt from Section 16 reporting. The FY2025 20-F (filed April 16, 2026) and the Q2 earnings 6-K (July 16, 2026) were not directly readable in this research cycle. Consequently there is no proxy-based compensation data, no board-independence disclosure, no Form 4 insider record, and no filing-sourced customer-concentration figure. The absence of insider-buying data here is a function of filer status and is not scored as a signal in either direction. Multi-year financials below come from a third-party aggregator of TSMC's reported statements; quarterly guidance and actuals and monthly revenue come from TSMC's own investor-relations disclosures.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement trajectory (NT$M; EPS shown per ADR-equivalent share):**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,161,736 | 2,894,308 | 3,809,054 | 4,440,492 |
| Growth | -4.51% | +33.89% | +31.61% | +30.56% |
| Gross margin | 54.36% | 56.12% | 59.89% | 64.23% |
| Operating margin | 42.63% | 45.68% | 50.83% | 56.10% |
| Net income | 851,740 | 1,158,380 | 1,697,604 | 2,237,087 |
| EPS (NT$/ADR) | 164.25 | 223.35 | 327.35 | 431.35 |

The central observation is that gross margin expanded 8.11pp from FY2024 to the trailing twelve months (64.23% less 56.12%) while revenue grew above 30% in each period. In semiconductor manufacturing, rapid volume growth normally compresses margin during node ramps, because depreciation lands ahead of mature yields. TSMC has done the reverse, which is most consistent with pricing power in a supply-constrained leading-edge market.

Two qualifications on that reading. First, currency. The USD/NTD assumption embedded in guidance moved from 31.01 (Q4 2025 actual) to 31.60 (Q2 2026 actual) to 32.0 (Q3 2026 guide). NT$ depreciation of 1.90% (31.60 versus 31.01) applied against the widely cited management heuristic of roughly 0.4pp gross margin per 1% move implies approximately 0.76pp of the 5.4pp gross-margin gain from Q4 2025 (62.3%) to Q2 2026 (67.7%) — about 14% of the improvement. We did not verify that sensitivity in the 20-F, so the decomposition scales directly with it and should be read as order-of-magnitude. Second, mix. At least one commentary source attributed part of the Q2 margin beat to a shift toward mature, largely depreciated 5nm capacity rather than to leading-edge pricing. If correct, some of the current margin peak is a transitional state occurring before the full weight of 2nm and Arizona depreciation arrives, which would make the guided Q3 decline the start of normalization rather than a one-quarter dip. We could not verify node-level mix.

**Cash flow and capital intensity (NT$M):**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 1,826,177 | 2,274,976 | 2,634,679 |
| Capex | (956,007) | (1,272,411) | (1,491,123) |
| Free cash flow | 870,171 | 1,002,565 | 1,143,557 |
| FCF margin | 30.06% | 26.32% | 25.75% |
| Capex / revenue | 33.03% | 33.41% | 33.58% |

Free cash flow is 43.4% of operating cash flow on a trailing basis (1,143,557 / 2,634,679). This is the crux of the debate. Operating cash flow is enormous and growing, but a third of every revenue dollar returns to plant and equipment, and FY2026 capex guidance of $60-64B raises that further. FCF still grew in absolute terms (up 15.2% FY2024 to FY2025, and up further on a trailing basis), so this is a reinvestment choice funded by growth, not a deterioration in cash generation. The FCF margin trend and the P/FCF re-rating are the honest counterweight to the forward-P/E argument.

**Balance sheet (NT$M):**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | Jun-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash & investments | 2,485,158 | 3,128,298 | 3,518,013 |
| Total debt | 1,047,042 | 1,064,583 | 982,447 |
| Net cash | 1,438,116 | 2,063,715 | 2,535,566 |

Net cash reconciles (3,518,013 − 982,447 = 2,535,566) and has risen roughly 76% over eighteen months while total debt declined NT$82,136M in the most recent period. At the 31.60 realized rate, net cash of NT$2,535,566M is approximately $80.2bn. TSMC is self-funding the largest capex program in the industry's history while deleveraging. No conventional distress screen was computable (no 10-K, hence no Z-score or F-score), but the condition such screens test for is plainly absent: positive free cash flow in every year of the last five including the FY2023 downturn, 56% trailing operating margin, and net cash rising.

**Two data reconciliations worth flagging.** The profile feed reports trailing and forward P/E of 150.5 on EPS of $2.086; neither is internally consistent with the other or with reported earnings, and both should be disregarded. Trailing EPS per ADR is approximately NT$431.35 / 31.6 = $13.65, implying roughly 29.6x trailing. Separately, we correct a payout-ratio error in the underlying research: FY2025 dividends of NT$22.00 are per common share, while the NT$327.35 EPS figure is per ADR-equivalent (five common shares). True payout is approximately NT$22.00 / NT$65.47 = 33.6% of FY2025 earnings, not the 6.7% a unit mismatch would imply. That reconciles to the reported ADR dividend yield of 0.87% ($3.48 per ADR at 31.6). Capital allocation is still reinvestment-weighted, but the distribution is a third of earnings, not a token.

## Growth Analysis

**Company-published quarterly cadence:**

| | Q4-25 actual | Q2-26 guide | Q2-26 actual | Q3-26 guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (US$B) | 33.73 | 39.0-40.2 | 40.20 | 44.6-45.8 |
| Gross margin | 62.3% | 65.5-67.5% | 67.7% | 65.0-67.0% |
| Operating margin | 54.0% | 56.5-58.5% | 60.3% | 56.0-58.0% |
| USD/NTD | 31.01 | 31.7 | 31.60 | 32.0 |

The Q3 midpoint of $45.2B implies 12.4% sequential revenue growth from $40.20B — an unusually steep guided step for a company of this scale — alongside a 1.7pp gross-margin and 3.3pp operating-margin decline from the Q2 actual. Revenue accelerating while margins decline is the signature of 2nm ramp costs plus geographic cost mix, not of demand weakness. Management has characterized the overseas margin impact as "manageable," which is an assessment rather than an independent verification; the guidance table already embeds several points of decline.

Monthly revenue disclosures corroborate the trajectory and provide the highest-frequency check available. H1 2026 revenue of NT$2,404,484M was up 35.6% year over year and reconciles exactly to the reconstructed quarters (Q1 NT$1,134,103M, Q2 NT$1,270,381M). The Q2 NT$ figure divided by the realized 31.60 rate produces $40,202M, independently validating the reported $40.20B. Individual monthly year-over-year prints range from +17.5% (April) to +67.9% (June) and should not be extrapolated — they are NT$-denominated and therefore contaminated by year-over-year FX moves, shipment timing, and calendar effects, and the June disclosure was itself delayed two days by a typhoon that closed Taiwan's markets.

The full-year guidance implies a demanding but coherent H2. Deriving H1 2025 from H1 2026 and the +35.6% growth rate gives approximately NT$1,773,220M, leaving H2 2025 at roughly NT$2,035,834M. Growth of "slightly above 40%" for FY2026 requires H2 2026 of roughly NT$2,928,000M, or about +44% year over year — an acceleration from H1's +35.6%. The Q3 guide midpoint converts to roughly NT$1,446,400M at 32.0, which puts the implied Q4 at approximately NT$1,482,000M, or +2.4% sequentially. That is an achievable sequential step given Q3's guided +12.4%, but it leaves no slack: the full-year guide requires Q3 to land at or above the midpoint.

Consensus is broadly consistent. Summing reported and estimated quarters (Q1 $3.49, Q2 $4.31 actual, Q3 $4.45E, Q4 $4.95E) gives approximately $17.20 for FY2026 against the published current-year consensus of $16.82 — modest conservatism in the annual figure. Next year at $21.61 implies 25.6% growth off the quarter-sum or 28.5% off the annual consensus.

The highest-conviction estimate-revision signal — 90-day revisions above 30% — is **not** present here. Revisions of +12.0% (next year) and +8.8% (current year) over 90 days are strongly positive but well below that threshold. The valuation case therefore rests on the gap between an 18.7x forward multiple and mid-to-high-20s forward earnings growth, not on revision velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $403.42:

- 24.0x current-year consensus EPS of $16.82
- 18.7x next-year consensus EPS of $21.61
- PEG on next-year earnings: 18.7 / 28.5 = 0.66 (or 0.73 using the 25.6% quarter-sum growth base)
- Trailing P/E approximately 29.6x on TTM EPS of ~$13.65 per ADR
- P/FCF approximately 54x; FCF yield approximately 1.73% (TTM FCF of NT$1,143,557M / 31.6 = $36.2bn against ~$2.09tn market cap)
- Enterprise value approximately $2.01tn after ~$80bn net cash; EV to next-year net income (implied off consensus EPS of $21.61) approximately 17.9x
- Dividend yield 0.87%

The specific mispricing we are underwriting: a company guiding above 40% revenue growth, delivering 60%+ operating margins, funding its expansion without leverage, and carrying rising consensus estimates trades at 18.7x forward earnings — a discount to the broader large-cap semiconductor and AI-infrastructure cohort, despite comparable near-term earnings growth, no circular-financing structure, and no customer guarantee exposure. The ADR is 15.8% below its June 30 high of $479 while next-year estimates rose 12.0% over the same 90-day window. That combination — price down, forward estimates up, forward multiple below the growth rate — is the core of the case.

Because the forward multiple is well under 30x, we treat the analyst target distribution as a lagging rather than a confirming indicator, and the distribution is unusual: low $430, median $534.50, mean $540.20, high $700. Spot is 6.6% below the *lowest* published target. Sentiment is improving (18 buy/strong-buy against one hold, up from 17/2).

The offset is the cash-flow lens. P/FCF has expanded from 38.6x (FY2024) to roughly 54x, and P/S from 11.6x to 13.6x, while P/E compressed. The flat-to-lower P/E is a function of a 46.6% FY2025 EPS increase and a 53.3% trailing increase rather than of multiple discipline. On the measure that reflects the capex burden, the stock has re-rated substantially. Anyone anchoring exclusively on "cheaper than last year on P/E" is looking at the one multiple that flatters the capex story. We resolve this by scoring valuation 7.0 rather than higher: the earnings-based discount is real and specific, but a 1.7% FCF yield means the thesis depends on this capex vintage earning its cost of capital, and that will not be demonstrable inside our twelve-month horizon.

## Competitive Landscape

We cannot state TSMC's foundry market share or leading-edge share as a sourced fact; no filing-based share data or competitor discussion was retrievable. The competitive case therefore rests on observable pricing and margin behavior rather than on share statistics.

That evidence is strong. A capital-intensive manufacturer sustaining 67.7% gross and 60.3% operating margins while simultaneously raising capacity is not a price-taker. Commodity foundries do not earn those margins. The margin structure is the observable consequence of node leadership: 2nm is management's stated driver of next-quarter revenue, with A16/A14 referenced as successor nodes, and the Q2 profit surge was externally attributed to 2nm and 3nm demand. The absence of any reported customer defection to Samsung Foundry or Intel Foundry at the leading edge, alongside the ability to raise capex and keep utilization high enough to expand margin, is consistent with a stable-to-widening technical lead.

Two trajectory qualifications lead us to trim competitive position from 8.5 to 8.0 rather than hold it. First, the Arizona build-out shifts a rising share of leading-edge capacity to a structurally higher-cost geography — higher construction, labor, utility, and supply-chain costs, with depreciation preceding mature yields. Whether that is offset by US-wafer pricing premiums, customer co-investment, or tariff-risk mitigation is unknown; the announcement channel (reported as coming from the US administration, in the context of a Taiwan-US trade agreement) does not establish the economics either way, and we decline to infer motive from it. Second, if part of the current margin peak reflects favorable mix toward depreciated 5nm capacity, the durable moat premium is somewhat smaller than the trailing margin suggests.

On position within the AI value chain: NVIDIA's data-center segment reportedly grew 92% to $75B at roughly 75% gross margin against TSMC's 64-68%. NVIDIA captures the higher margin because it owns the architecture and ecosystem. TSMC earns a lower but architecture-agnostic margin — it is paid whether the winning accelerator is NVIDIA's, AMD's, a Broadcom custom ASIC, or a hyperscaler's in-house design. That diversification across designs is the most defensible element of the franchise, and it is what makes TSM a lower-idiosyncratic-risk way to hold AI compute exposure than any single design house.

We explicitly do not claim customer diversification. No top-customer disclosure was obtained. A mix shift toward HPC/AI is a *mix* change and is not evidence of reduced customer concentration; it may coincide with worsening concentration if AI revenue originates from a small number of very large accelerator and hyperscaler programs. We assert neither direction.

## Risk Assessment

**Taiwan geographic concentration.** The most advanced capacity remains concentrated in Taiwan. A cross-Strait disruption or a major natural event would affect a disproportionate share of global leading-edge logic supply. This is unhedgeable, low-probability, and extreme in impact. It is partly why the forward multiple sits below the peer cohort, so it is priced to a degree — but not fully, and it caps the risk score.

**Company-guided margin compression.** Q3 gross margin guided to a 66.0% midpoint against a 67.7% Q2 actual, operating margin to 57.0% against 60.3%. This is management's own number, not an analyst worry. Mitigant: TSMC beat the top end of both margin ranges last quarter, which argues the guide is conservative. One quarter of overshoot is not a pattern.

**Return on the capex vintage.** Capex intensity of 33.6% with FY2026 guidance of $60-64B and a multi-year US commitment reported at roughly $265B across twelve facilities (only the $100B increment is well corroborated; the $265B/twelve-facility framing is single- and low-tier-sourced). The risk is not solvency; it is that AI accelerator demand plateaus while this vintage depreciates. FY2023 is the in-sample precedent: revenue −4.51%, EPS −14.21%, FCF margin collapsing from 23.32% to 13.51%. TSMC is cyclical, and the same operating leverage that is expanding margins now reverses.

**AI-capex dependency.** TSM is the most direct manufacturing beneficiary of hyperscaler capex, and Reuters (July 14, 2026) framed the demand backdrop as "booming AI infrastructure spending." TSMC is insulated on the funding side specifically because it is self-funded with net cash rising, which is a genuine differentiator. It is not insulated from demand-side deceleration if frontier-lab or hyperscaler financing tightens. [Note: specific hyperscaler capex figures and AI-credit-market stress claims are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed.]

**Arizona resource and permitting constraints.** Local reporting suggests the expansion could make TSMC Phoenix's largest tap-water customer. Fabs are water- and power-intensive and Phoenix is water-constrained. Single local source, no company disclosure on secured allocations; identified vector, not a quantified constraint.

**FX two-sidedness.** The NT$ has been a tailwind (31.01 to 31.60 to a guided 32.0). Reversal to NT$ strength compresses reported margin roughly as mechanically as depreciation expanded it.

**Disclosure and governance opacity.** No proxy, no compensation data, no board-independence disclosure, no Form 4 record, no filing-verified customer concentration, no read of the FY2025 20-F risk factors. We cannot report which risk factors were added or modified versus FY2024. The absence of adverse findings reflects absence of access.

**Short interest** is negligible at 0.7% of float, 2.3 days to cover — no crowding, no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The chain prices near-term stress without a company-specific catalyst. Near-term ATM IV of 56.2% against 40.6% one-year realized is rich (15.6pp spread, 79th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation — 56.2% at three days against 45.0% at six months. Put/call skew is +5.0% at the one-week tenor (elevated put demand) but flattens to +1.1% at one month and inverts to −0.5% and −3.9% at three and six months. Put/call open interest is 1.06, and the September 11 tenor carries a put/call volume ratio of 4.85.

TSMC does not report again until mid-October, so none of this is earnings positioning. The concentration of put volume at the September tenor and the near-term backwardation are more consistent with macro and sector hedging than with a stock-specific information event. The negative six-month skew, with calls bid relative to puts further out, is directionally consistent with our constructive twelve-month view.

Practical reading: the options market is pricing a ±8.7% one-month and ±12.4% three-month range. Near-term vol is expensive to own and the implied path is wide, so scaling entry across the August monthly revenue release and the September window is preferable to a single-print entry. This argues against paying up on strength in the next several weeks.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $560

Q3 lands at or above the $45.8B high end with margins at or above the 67.0% gross-margin ceiling, replicating the Q2 overshoot and demonstrating that 2nm ramp dilution is smaller than guided. FY2026 growth of "slightly above 40%" is met or exceeded, pulling next-year consensus above $22 and drawing the current $16.82/$21.61 pair higher again. Advanced packaging localization in Arizona relieves the binding constraint on accelerator supply and is accompanied by evidence of US-wafer pricing premiums, so overseas margin dilution proves modest. At 26x a next-year figure of $21.61 the ADR is worth roughly $562, and if estimates move to $23 the same multiple implies close to $600. The analyst mean of $540 and high of $700 sit within this zone. Supporting conditions: multi-quarter hyperscaler capex confirmation, no Taiwan escalation, NT$ stable or weaker.

### Bear Case — $320

AI accelerator order rates decelerate in late 2026 as hyperscalers digest the 2026 capex vintage. Utilization on leading-edge capacity falls just as 2nm and Arizona depreciation lands at full weight, and the FY2023 pattern repeats in compressed form: growth stalls, gross margin retraces toward the high 50s, and next-year EPS is revised toward $16-17 instead of $21.61. On 19-20x a $16.50 figure the ADR is worth roughly $315-330. A more severe path — Taiwan escalation, or an export-control regime change affecting leading-edge shipments — is not modelable and would produce a substantially worse outcome; we treat it as an unquantified tail rather than embedding a probability. Bear case is 20.7% below spot.

### Base Case — $475

We apply 22x to next-year consensus EPS of $21.61, yielding $475, or 17.7% above $403.42. The 22x multiple sits between the current 18.7x and the roughly 26-29x at which the ADR ended FY2024 and FY2025 on then-forward earnings, and it does not require multiple expansion beyond the historical range. It embeds the guided Q3 margin decline as real, assumes FY2026 lands within guidance rather than above it, and assumes no re-rating on the Arizona return question inside twelve months. Cross-check: at 22x, EV/next-year net income is roughly 21x after net cash, and the implied FCF yield remains under 2%, so the cash-flow constraint is the binding one and is why we do not carry the base case to the analyst mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: BUY. Fifth consecutive BUY.** The specific incremental justification: the ADR is 15.8% below its June 30 high while next-year consensus rose 12.0% over 90 days, FY2026 revenue growth guidance was raised by roughly ten percentage points, and the company beat the top end of both margin guidance ranges. Risk/reward improved on lower price and higher estimates. We trim competitive position (8.5 to 8.0) to reflect the Arizona cost-mix shift and the possibility that part of the current margin peak is transitional 5nm mix, and hold management quality at 6.8 given structurally unavailable governance and insider data alongside an unproven return on a reported multi-year US commitment.

- **Base case target: $475** — 22x next-year consensus EPS of $21.61. Upside 17.7%.
- **Bull case target: $560** — 26x $21.61. Upside 38.8%.
- **Bear case target: $320** — approximately 19-20x a revised $16.50. Downside 20.7%.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to two Q3/Q4 prints (mid-October 2026 and mid-January 2027) plus monthly revenue disclosures.
- **Reward/risk from spot:** 17.7% to base and 38.8% to bull against 20.7% to bear — roughly 0.9:1 on base and 1.9:1 on bull. Adequate rather than exceptional, which supports a scaled position rather than a single-entry maximum position.

**Entry approach:** scale, do not chase. Near-term IV of 56.2% against 40.6% realized in backwardation, with heavy September put volume, prices a wide path over the next six weeks with no company catalyst inside it. Add on weakness toward $370-385 rather than on strength.

**Thesis invalidation triggers — exit or downgrade if any occur:**
1. **Q3 revenue below the $44.6B guided low end, or Q3 gross margin below 65.0%.** Either would break the pattern of guidance conservatism that underpins the base case and would put the FY2026 "slightly above 40%" figure out of reach given the required +2.4% Q4 sequential step.
2. **Next-year consensus EPS revised below $19.50** (roughly a 10% cut from $21.61), or two consecutive months of year-over-year revenue growth below 20% in the monthly disclosures.
3. **FY2027 capex guided above $70B without a corresponding revenue-growth guide above 25%.** That combination would signal capex outrunning demand and would push the FCF yield below 1.5%, at which point the forward-P/E argument no longer compensates for the cash-flow profile.

**Upgrade to higher conviction if:** Q3 beats the top end on both revenue and gross margin, next-year consensus moves above $23, and management quantifies Arizona-specific margin dilution or US-wafer pricing in a way that bounds the overseas cost question.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the most direct manufacturing beneficiary of AI compute buildout and the one holding that exposure with a strong balance sheet: net cash of NT$2.54tn, total debt declining, and a $60-64B capex program funded entirely from a NT$2.63tn operating cash flow. TSMC needs no external capital to fund its expansion, which is a material differentiator and is not reflected in a forward multiple below the peer cohort.

The valuation asymmetry is specific and falsifiable: 18.7x next-year consensus EPS of $21.61 against 26-28% forward earnings growth, estimates up 12.0% over 90 days, four consecutive beats ranging 4.4% to 10.9%, and a price 6.6% below the lowest published analyst target. The offsets are equally specific: management has already guided Q3 gross margin down 1.7pp and operating margin down 3.3pp; free cash flow margin has compressed from 30.1% to 25.8% on 33.6% capex intensity; the FCF yield is 1.7%; and Taiwan concentration is an unhedgeable extreme-impact tail. The FY2023 record — revenue −4.5%, EPS −14.2% — shows from this company's own recent history that the operating leverage now expanding margins runs in reverse.

We maintain **BUY** with an overall score of 7.4, a base case of $475, and explicit tolerance for a $320 bear case. Position sizing should respect the Taiwan tail and the near-term volatility structure; the thesis is the multiple-versus-growth gap, and the checkpoints are the October print and the monthly revenue series in between.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the executive summary's "$540 implies 33.9% upside" was inconsistent with the base-case $475/+17.7% target and reward/risk framing; the trailing P/E is ~29.6x (not a "cheap" trailing figure); and unsupported AI-credit-stress and specific hyperscaler-capex claims underpinning the "self-funded differentiator" narrative were removed — none change the arithmetic of the multiple-versus-growth thesis, but the reward/risk is closer to balanced (0.9:1 base) than the summary's upside framing implied.
