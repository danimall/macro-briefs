## Executive Summary

CBRE Group trades at $145.16, roughly 17% below its February 2026 all-time high of $174.27 and 22% above its 52-week low. The stock sold off sharply in mid-February (down 20% in two sessions around the Q4/FY2025 earnings release, as the market digested elevated leverage and integration costs), then drifted lower through March on broader CRE sector concerns before stabilizing around current levels. The Q1 2026 earnings beat (core EPS $1.61 vs. $0.89 in the year-ago quarter, an 81% YoY increase that exceeded market expectations) provided fundamental validation but the stock remains range-bound as the market digests macro uncertainty around rates and commercial real estate.

The investment case centers on CBRE's transformation from a traditional CRE brokerage into a diversified infrastructure services platform. Critical infrastructure services revenue grew 71% in Q1 2026 (65% in local currency), and management raised full-year core EPS guidance to $7.60-$7.80, implying 20%+ growth. At ~18.9x forward core EPS, the stock trades at an attractive valuation relative to its growth rate. The primary risks are elevated leverage (total debt of $7.0B as of Q1 2026, with an additional $750M issued in May 2026), CRE transaction sensitivity to rates, and integration execution on $2.7B of recent acquisitions.

The combination of accelerating infrastructure-driven growth, a significant earnings beat, rising consensus estimates, and a price 17% below recent highs creates asymmetric upside. The 12/13 analyst Buy/Strong Buy consensus and $180.50 median target reinforce this view.

## Company Overview

CBRE Group is the world's largest commercial real estate services and investment firm, generating $40.6 billion in revenue in 2025 across over 100 countries with approximately 155,000 employees. The company reorganized into four segments in January 2025:

- **Building Operations & Experience (BOE)** — $23.2B, 57% of revenue. Enterprise facilities management, property management, digital infrastructure services, and flexible workplace solutions. Long-term contracts, low margin (3.0% operating) but highly resilient.
- **Advisory Services** — $8.8B, 22% of revenue. Leasing, capital markets, loan servicing, and valuation. Highest margin (15.9% operating) but most cyclical.
- **Project Management** — $7.7B, 19% of revenue. Through 70%-owned Turner & Townsend. Program management, cost consultancy.
- **Real Estate Investments (REI)** — $0.9B, 2% of revenue. $155.5B AUM investment management and Trammell Crow development ($29.5B pipeline).

Approximately 82% of revenue comes from contractual/recurring sources, making the business significantly more resilient than the traditional CRE brokerage model suggests.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
Revenue has accelerated from $30.8B (2022) to $31.9B (2023, +3.6%) to $35.8B (2024, +11.9%) to $40.6B (2025, +13.4%). Q1 2026 revenue of $10.53B represents 18.6% YoY growth, indicating further acceleration driven by infrastructure services.

**Profitability:**
- Core EPS grew 25.1% in 2025 to $6.38, with guidance of $7.60-$7.80 for 2026 (midpoint +20.7%)
- Core EBITDA: $3.3B in 2025, up 22.3% YoY
- GAAP operating margin of 4.3% is misleading due to $16.7B in zero-margin pass-through costs; on an analyst-calculated basis, operating income divided by net revenue (revenue less pass-through costs of ~$23.8B) yields an effective margin of approximately 7.4%
- Q1 2026 delivered $1.61 core EPS, up 81% from $0.89 in Q1 2025, reflecting genuine upside in infrastructure services

**Balance Sheet:**
As of March 31, 2026, total debt (excluding warehouse lines and non-recourse real estate debt) was **$7.013B** against $1.664B cash, resulting in net debt of approximately **$5.35B**. Net leverage stood at **1.54x** trailing core EBITDA, substantially below the company's primary debt covenant of 4.25x. On May 4, 2026, CBRE issued an additional **$750M in 5.250% senior notes due 2036**, further increasing gross debt. While the debt-to-equity ratio (approximately 0.79x as of Q1 2026) reflects growth-oriented leverage, the absolute debt level warrants monitoring. The Altman Z-Score of approximately 2.2 places CBRE in the "grey zone," though this metric is driven by capital structure rather than operational distress and should be weighed against the company's reported net leverage of 1.54x and strong covenant headroom.

Goodwill stands at $7.1B (23% of total assets, up from $5.6B in 2024) from serial acquisitions. No impairment was identified in 2025, with management stating fair values "substantially exceed" carrying values.

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $1.56B (2025)
- Conventional free cash flow (CFO minus capex): approximately $1.19B
- Company-reported free cash flow (which adds gains on real estate dispositions to operating cash flow before subtracting capex): $1.652B for 2025
- Trailing 12-month company-reported FCF as of Q1 2026: approximately $1.7B

CBRE's reported FCF definition includes gains on disposition of real estate sales ($459M in 2025; $301M in Q1 2026 alone), which can be lumpy. Q1 2026 profits benefited from earlier-than-anticipated data-center land development profits. Not all of this FCF is purely recurring services cash flow.

- Share repurchases of $956M in 2025 at $135.52 average, with $4.8B remaining authorization through 2029

Management bought back shares at prices below current levels throughout 2025, demonstrating capital allocation confidence.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has three distinct layers:

**1. Infrastructure Services (Structural — 65-71% growth):** Critical infrastructure services revenue grew **71% in Q1 2026** (65% in local currency), including contributions from Data Center Solutions and the Pearce Services acquisition. Management has indicated infrastructure-related work across data centers, power, telecom, transportation and other assets has become a significant profit and growth source across all four segments. The Pearce acquisition ($1.2B) created end-to-end data center capabilities from project management through ongoing facilities management. This segment benefits directly from hyperscaler AI capex and represents a multi-year structural growth driver independent of traditional CRE cycles.

**2. CRE Transaction Recovery (Cyclical — 20-40% growth):** Property sales grew 20% in 2025 and 39% in Q1 2026 as transaction volumes normalize from the 2023 trough. U.S. leasing revenue rose 21% in Q1 2026, driven by industrial, office, and data centers. This recovery faces headwinds from elevated rates.

**3. Outsourcing & Platform Consolidation (Secular — mid-teens to 20%+ growth):** Large occupiers continue consolidating real estate providers, favoring CBRE's integrated platform. In Q1 2026, BOE revenue grew 20.4% in USD (16.0% in local currency) with segment operating profit growing 28.4% in USD (22.5% in local currency).

**Estimate Revisions:** Current year consensus has risen from $7.48 to $7.67 (90-day revision +2.6%), and next year from $8.72 to $8.85 (+1.5%). These are modest upward revisions that likely understate momentum given the Q1 beat. The consensus current-year estimate of $7.67 is within management's $7.60-$7.80 guidance range — if Q2-Q4 deliver similar outperformance to Q1, there is meaningful upside to the $7.67 consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

At $145.16 with core EPS guidance midpoint of $7.70, CBRE trades at 18.9x forward core earnings. Against 20%+ EPS growth, this implies a PEG ratio of approximately 0.9x — attractive for a market leader with accelerating growth.

**Peer Comparison:**
CBRE at ~18.9x forward core EPS compares to JLL, which reported 2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $18.80. On a consistent forward adjusted-earnings basis, CBRE's valuation appears reasonable relative to its closest peer given CBRE's larger scale, greater diversification, and higher infrastructure exposure. Broader peer sets that include CoStar (a software/data company) or smaller, more levered peers are less directly comparable and can distort median multiples.

**Analyst Targets:** Consensus median $180.50 (24% upside), with a range of $137-$200. 12 of 13 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy.

**Where the market may be wrong:** The market appears to be valuing CBRE as a traditional CRE services company (cyclical, rate-sensitive) rather than recognizing the structural transformation toward infrastructure services. A business with critical infrastructure services growing 71% should command a growth premium that current multiples do not reflect. The February selloff around earnings created a valuation reset that hasn't fully reversed despite the Q1 operational beat.

**Base case valuation:** 22x $7.70 core EPS = $169. With continued infrastructure growth and potential guidance raises, 22-24x $8.00+ is achievable = $176-$192.

## Competitive Landscape

CBRE's competitive position is strengthening. Key advantages:

1. **Scale:** CBRE's $40.6B revenue is approximately 1.55x that of nearest competitor JLL ($26.1B). This enables integrated service delivery that smaller competitors cannot easily replicate, and provides leverage on technology/data investments.

2. **Infrastructure positioning:** CBRE has assembled a differentiated end-to-end capability set through Turner & Townsend (project management), Pearce (advanced technical services for digital and power infrastructure), and organic data center capabilities, creating comprehensive value chain coverage for hyperscale clients.

3. **Revenue quality transformation:** Moving from 60% to 82% resilient revenue over several years fundamentally reduces cyclical volatility relative to history and relative to smaller peers who remain more transaction-dependent.

4. **Client relationships:** Nearly 90% of Fortune 100 companies are clients. The outsourcing consolidation trend favors the largest platform — once a major occupier selects CBRE for integrated services, switching costs are substantial.

5. **Development pipeline:** Trammell Crow has an in-process projects and pipeline of $29.6B, including data center sites that management expects to generate significant development profits over coming years, though monetization timing will be "lumpy and operationally challenging."

The competitive trajectory is positive. CBRE is gaining share in the highest-growth segments (infrastructure, data centers) while maintaining its dominant position in traditional services.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Sensitivity (Moderate-High):** Elevated rates and CRE maturities at elevated rates directly pressure transaction volumes in Advisory. Advisory property sales revenue of $2.1B represents approximately 5% of total revenue but a disproportionate share of segment profit given Advisory's 15.9% operating margin. Transactional revenue streams across Advisory (including leasing and capital markets) are highly sensitive to rate environments. A sustained rate environment above current levels would cap Advisory recovery and could force write-downs in the REI segment's development pipeline.

**Leverage (Moderate-High):** Total debt of $7.0B as of Q1 2026 (plus $750M issued in May 2026) represents meaningful financial risk in a recession scenario. Net leverage of 1.54x trailing core EBITDA is well below the 4.25x covenant, and company-reported FCF of approximately $1.7B (trailing) provides coverage of debt obligations, though the lumpy real-estate-gains component within that FCF figure reduces its predictability. The May 2026 senior notes issuance indicates continued willingness to lever up for growth.

**Integration Execution (Moderate):** $2.7B deployed in 2025 on acquisitions with $303M in integration costs (up from $93M). Pearce, Industrious, and Turner & Townsend must deliver on growth expectations to justify the leverage taken on. Management track record on M&A is strong (Turner & Townsend has performed well since 2021 acquisition) but scale of recent activity increases execution risk.

**Telford Remediation (Low-Moderate):** Liability grew from $204M to $321M in 2025 with "variables outside Telford's control." Against trailing FCF this is manageable but represents an ongoing cash drain with uncertain terminal value.

**Macro/Geopolitical (Moderate):** Trade disruptions (the 10-K specifically flags "unpredictable changes in U.S. trading policy" and "increased tariffs"), geopolitical tensions, and potential energy price shocks all impact client capital investment decisions and transaction activity, particularly in CBRE's international operations (43.6% of revenue in foreign currencies).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-$200, +21% to +38%):**
Infrastructure services growth exceeds expectations as data center demand accelerates. CRE transaction recovery continues despite rate headwinds as pent-up demand releases. Management raises guidance again in Q2/Q3. Multiple re-rates from ~19x to 22-24x as market recognizes CBRE as an infrastructure platform. At 24x $8.50 (upside scenario), stock reaches $204.

**Bear Case ($118-$130, -11% to -19%):**
Sustained high rates compress CRE transaction volumes further. European recession and tariff uncertainty reduce corporate outsourcing demand. Integration of Pearce/Industrious disappoints. Leverage becomes a concern if FCF deteriorates. At 16x $7.50 (downside), stock reaches $120.

**Base Case ($169-$180, +16% to +24%):**
Infrastructure growth of 50-60%+ drives earnings acceleration. Advisory recovers modestly despite rate headwinds. Consensus converges toward $8.00+ as beat/raise cadence continues. Multiple normalizes at 21-22x forward. At 22x $7.80 = $172.

3. Infrastructure services growth decelerates materially for two consecutive quarters (indicating AI infrastructure thesis exhaustion)

## Conclusion

CBRE at $145 represents a favorable entry point for a market-dominant franchise undergoing a structural growth transformation. The company trades at 18.9x forward core earnings with 20%+ growth — a PEG below 1.0x for the largest, most diversified commercial real estate services platform globally.

The primary concern is elevated and growing leverage, though this is mitigated by net leverage of only 1.54x core EBITDA, diversified revenue (82% resilient), and management's demonstrated capital allocation track record. The infrastructure services transformation aligns CBRE with the multi-year AI/data center buildout cycle, providing durable growth that the current valuation does not fully reflect. Investors should note that company-reported free cash flow includes lumpy real estate disposition gains and that several infrastructure growth figures cited by management on earnings calls have not been broken out in SEC-filed financial statements at the same level of granularity.

Rate BUY with a 12-month base case of $172 and a position size of 2-3%.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Updated debt figures (Q1 2026: $7.0B + May 2026: $750M issuance), clarified FCF quality (includes lumpy real estate gains), and corrected property-sales revenue exposure from ~18% to ~5% of total revenue collectively reduce the margin of safety in the leverage/risk analysis, though the thesis remains supported by verified operational momentum.
