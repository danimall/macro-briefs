**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is an oil-weighted independent E&P operating almost entirely in the Delaware Basin, distinguished by a 51%-owned integrated midstream JV (San Mateo) that provides guaranteed gas, oil, and water takeaway in a basin chronically short of capacity. The operational story remains intact: Q1 2026 production rose ~5% YoY to 207,594 BOE/d, adjusted EBITDA of $577.2M fell only 10% despite a sharp decline in realized gas prices, and management raised 2026 production guidance in May while holding capex flat. The reported GAAP net loss of $(35.9)M / $(0.29) EPS is optical — it was driven by a $255.5M non-cash unrealized derivative mark that occurred because oil spiked to ~$100 in late March, breaching collar ceilings. Operating cash flow was $470.5M and the company deleveraged its own credit facility to just $45M by May 6, 2026.

The forward earnings picture is deteriorating in a way the constructive prior calls did not fully price. Consensus next-quarter EPS has been cut 22.3% in 30 days ($2.12 → $1.64), current-year down 11.6% ($7.82 → $6.91), and the analyst sentiment trend is flagged as degrading. The proximate cause is the Waha basis blowout: realized gas collapsed to $0.64/Mcf in Q1 2026 from $3.56 a year earlier (–82%) on roughly 42% of production, and Waha–Henry Hub remained near –$6.57/MMBtu into May. The structural fix — 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transport on Energy Transfer's Hugh Brinson pipeline — is not in service until year-end 2026, with realization benefit landing in 2027. That leaves two to three more quarters of depressed gas realizations weighing on cash flow.

The counterweight is a strong insider-buying cluster (CEO Foran, CFO Calvert, COO Stetson, CAO Colodney, and a director all bought in the open market at $49–56 in May–June 2026), a well-covered ~2.8% dividend raised 20% in October 2025, and embedded optionality in a possible future San Mateo monetization following the Cardinal acquisition. Netting these against falling estimates, an unrelieved gas headwind, single-basin/customer concentration, and rising midstream leverage, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We move to HOLD, reversing the prior BUY, and will re-engage on either estimate stabilization or a pullback that restores margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Matador operates two segments. Exploration & Production is ~98% concentrated in the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring plays of the Delaware Basin (Southeast New Mexico / West Texas), with a small Haynesville/Cotton Valley gas position in Northwest Louisiana. Midstream runs primarily through San Mateo, a 51%-owned JV with Five Point Infrastructure that gathers and processes gas, transports oil, and handles produced-water disposal for both Matador and third parties.

The production base is oil-weighted: of Q1 2026's volumes, roughly 58% was oil and 42% gas, with the Delaware Basin supplying 100% of oil volumes. Long-lateral development (averaging ~10,400 ft in 2025) underpins capital efficiency. The integrated midstream is the differentiator: one of the few systems in the northern Delaware Basin offering all three streams, giving Matador flow assurance that peers must contract out. Founder Joseph Foran has led the company since inception and also founded San Mateo, and directors/officers own roughly 5.8% of equity.

## Financial Analysis

Production growth is real; commodity mix is the swing factor. Q1 2026 total revenue was $671.6M versus $1,014M a year earlier (–34%), but that headline includes the non-cash unrealized derivative loss. Revenue from contracts with customers was $941.6M versus $1,006M (–6%), a better read on the underlying business. Realized oil held at ~$72.83/Bbl (though collars capped the hedged realization at $68.04 when oil spiked), while realized gas cratered to $0.64/Mcf from $3.56, the single most consequential financial development in the quarter.

Adjusted EBITDA (attributable) of $577.2M, down only 10% YoY, confirms that operations remain robustly cash-generative even with gas realizations near zero. Operating cash flow was $470.5M, or $564.2M before working-capital swings. The four-quarter earnings surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats, most recently +18.8% in Q1 2026 ($1.53 vs. $1.29 estimate) — which supports confidence that the operating engine delivers even as forward estimate revisions turn down.

The balance sheet requires careful reading. Total long-term debt at 3/31/26 was ~$3.47B: $185M on Matador's own facility, $918M on the non-recourse San Mateo facility, and $2.37B of senior notes. The $918M San Mateo debt consolidates onto Matador's balance sheet but is non-recourse to it. Matador's own recourse leverage is modest — the corporate facility was paid down to just $45M by May 6, 2026, against ~$2.0B of availability, and the company sits comfortably within its 3.5x debt/EBITDA and 1.0x current-ratio covenants. Corporate ratings are crossover high-yield (S&P BB–, Moody's Ba3, Fitch BB). The March 2026 refinancing — $750M of 6.00% 2034 notes issued to tender/redeem the $500M 6.875% 2028 notes — extended maturities and modestly lowered the coupon at the cost of a $15.6M extinguishment charge. This is prudent liability management, and the Altman Z-Score "distress" flag that appears in screens is a false positive driven by full-cost PP&E accounting, low working capital, and the consolidation of non-recourse San Mateo debt.

Capital allocation is disciplined. The dividend was raised 20% in October 2025 to $1.50 annualized (~2.8% at $54), a $400M buyback authorization has been used only opportunistically (~$0.7M in Q1 near $40), and the 2026 capex budget is $1.35–1.44B D/C/E plus $100–110M midstream. External estimates put 2026 FCF near $1.1B, though that figure is sensitive to how long gas realizations stay depressed.

## Growth Analysis

The production growth engine is intact — FY2025 volumes rose ~21% and Q1 2026 was up ~5% YoY, with management raising 2026 production guidance in May while holding capex, a sign of well outperformance and capital efficiency. Two large recent bets extend the growth runway: the $1.14B BLM federal lease acquisition in May 2026 (5,154 net undeveloped acres in Lea County, NM) and the San Mateo-level Cardinal Midstream acquisition ($752M, expected to close by end-July 2026, adding a 320 MMcf/d cryo plant, ~145 miles of gathering, 9 new third-party customers, and up to $110M annualized EBITDA by 2028).

Against this, forward earnings momentum is negative, and this is the material development that reverses the prior constructive stance. The revision profile is the opposite of the positive-revision signal that would justify leaning aggressively into cyclical upside. The driver is the Waha gas headwind, not an operational stumble: on ~42% of production, gas realizing $0.64/Mcf against a comparable prior period mechanically drags reported earnings and cash flow. The structural relief (Hugh Brinson, firm Gulf Coast/LNG-access transport) does not begin until year-end 2026, with the realization benefit accruing in 2027. Until then, the growth-in-volumes story is partly offset by weak gas price capture, and the next-year estimate ($8.48, +3.1% over 90 days) is the only line still rising — consistent with a market that expects the gas drag to lift in 2027 rather than 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

Matador trades at ~13.8x trailing EPS of $6.09 and ~$54, well below the peer median P/E of 32.5x. The peer set is heterogeneous and noisy — Chord (–112x) and CRC (–10.6x) carry negative earnings, CNX sits at 4.4x — so the median overstates the "cheapness" signal. On forward estimates, the picture is more sober: current-year consensus of $6.91 implies ~7.8x, and next-year $8.48 implies ~6.4x, both of which look inexpensive in absolute terms but are declining estimates, which is the core reason a low multiple here should be treated as a warning rather than a buy signal.

The value case rests on two catalysts rather than the trailing multiple. First, the Hugh Brinson pipeline should structurally lift gas realizations in 2027, which is why the next-year estimate is still rising while nearer-term estimates fall — the market is discounting today's gas weakness but pricing an eventual normalization. Second, the Cardinal acquisition is explicitly framed as positioning San Mateo "for potential strategic alternatives," hinting at a future monetization (IPO, sale, or drop-down) that would surface midstream value not captured in MTDR's E&P multiple. That optionality is real but undated and unquantified. Morningstar's ~21%-below-fair-value read and analyst mean/median targets of $70/$68 (range $60–97) sit meaningfully above the current price, but with degrading sentiment and falling estimates, those targets are more likely to compress toward price than the reverse in the near term. We score valuation at 5.5: cheap on the surface, but the discount reflects a live commodity impairment (gas basis) and negative revision momentum, and the re-rating catalysts are 2027-weighted.

## Competitive Landscape

Matador competes in the most active and most competitive US basin against far larger, better-capitalized majors and post-consolidation large-caps. Its structural edge is San Mateo's integrated gas/oil/water midstream, which provides flow assurance in a basin where takeaway is the binding constraint — a moat peers cannot easily replicate and one the Cardinal deal deepens by lifting processing capacity above 1 Bcf/d and adding third-party customers. Long laterals and the BLM inventory expansion sustain the drilling runway.

The Waha basis weakness is an industry-wide problem, not a Matador-specific failing — the entire Permian gas complex is suffering from takeaway shortfalls, and the structural fix is new pipe (Matterhorn, Hugh Brinson). Oil differentials were favorable in May 2026 (Midland-Cushing +$2.65/Bbl). The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: Matador is gaining midstream scale and third-party volumes while its peers remain exposed to the same gas headwind without the same integrated takeaway. We hold competitive position at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is heavier than the operating quality would suggest, which drives a risk score of 4.5.

**Waha gas basis (the dominant risk).** Depressed realized gas is dragging cash flow now and will continue to do so until Hugh Brinson comes online at year-end 2026 (2027 realization benefit). Mitigants — Houston Ship Channel pricing on much of the gas, NGL processing that reduces Waha-exposed volumes, and the firm-transport contract — cushion but do not eliminate the drag. A sensitivity frame: gas at ~$0.64/Mcf versus a normalized ~$3+ level on roughly 87,000 BOE/d of gas equivalent represents hundreds of millions of dollars of annualized revenue that will not be recovered until the pipeline flows. This is the single largest reason the constructive thesis must wait.

**Hedging give-ups.** Oil collar ceilings near $66/Bbl (2026) meant Matador paid out when oil spiked (realized $68.04 hedged vs. $72.83 unhedged), and the unrealized mark drove the Q1 GAAP loss. Hedges protect downside but cap upside.

**Concentration.** ~98% single-basin exposure and three purchasers accounting for ~72% of oil/gas/NGL revenue create both operational (weather, NM seismicity/SWD rules, takeaway) and counterparty tail risk. ~33% of Delaware leasehold is on BLM federal land, favorable under current policy but reversible.

**Rising midstream leverage and MVCs.** The Cardinal financing adds a $650M 364-day term loan at San Mateo on top of the existing $918M facility. While management frames it as "cash neutral to Matador," the short-dated term loan is refinancing-sensitive, and aggregate minimum-volume/deficiency commitments (~$2.29B third-party plus $701M to San Mateo) could trigger take-or-pay penalties in a drilling slowdown. The "cash neutral" framing holds only if San Mateo's third-party cash flows and partner contributions cover the obligations; a downturn stresses that assumption at the same time it would stress Matador's own capital program (compounded by the $1.14B BLM lease bet).

**Leadership transition.** Calvert's title moved from CFO to COO in Form 4 metadata while the 10-Q still lists him as CFO, and there were multiple officer-change 8-Ks in 2025–2026 plus an advisor agreement for the EVP-Marketing/Midstream. Worth monitoring, not yet a governance red flag.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 44.6% is modestly rich versus 40.5% one-year historical vol, sitting at the 90th percentile of the current chain, with a flat term structure and a balanced 1.2% put/call skew — the market is pricing evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a directional collapse, and the implied ±10.3% one-month move is consistent with an E&P name in a volatile commodity tape. Short interest is elevated: 11.0% of float with 7 days to cover signals meaningful institutional bearish positioning, which aligns with the degrading estimate trend. The 6-month –4.7% put skew shows some downside hedging demand further out. For a HOLD, the takeaway is confirmatory: options and short-interest positioning corroborate near-term caution rather than contradicting it, though the crowded-ish short base and insider buying create squeeze potential on any positive gas-basis or San Mateo-monetization surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$68–97).** Oil holds in the $70s, the Cardinal deal closes and San Mateo scales toward >1 Bcf/d with growing third-party EBITDA, and management executes a San Mateo monetization that surfaces midstream value at a premium to MTDR's E&P multiple. Hugh Brinson comes online on schedule, gas realizations recover sharply in 2027, and the next-year $8.48 EPS proves conservative. The insider-buying cluster is vindicated as a bottom signal, short interest covers, and the stock re-rates toward the $68 analyst median and beyond toward the $97 high. Base case within this range: ~$68, roughly 12x the still-rising next-year EPS with midstream optionality partly credited.

**Bear Case (~$40–45).** Oil weakens toward the $60s, Waha stays deeply negative through 2026, and estimates keep falling as the gas drag persists longer than the pipeline timeline. The aggressive $1.14B BLM and $752M Cardinal capital deployment strains FCF, San Mateo's short-dated term loan reprices in a higher-for-longer environment, and MVC deficiency risk surfaces if drilling slows. The stock retests the low-$40s (near the November 2025 low of ~$37) as the crossover credit and single-basin concentration reprice, roughly –17% to –25% from current levels.

The two cases are close to symmetric in probability weighting, which is why this is a HOLD. The bull case is real but 2027-weighted and dependent on undated catalysts (San Mateo monetization); the bear case is live now via the gas basis and negative revisions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an uncertain HOLD, not a conviction call in either direction. The operating quality, insider buying, and covered dividend keep it out of the AVOID bucket; the falling estimates, unrelieved gas headwind, and rising midstream leverage keep it out of BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-quarter EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive over two consecutive monthly readings (reversing the current –22.3% next-quarter / –11.6% current-year revisions), signaling the gas drag is troughing.
- Hugh Brinson confirmed in service and Waha differentials narrowing (toward –$2 or better), with management guiding to materially improved gas realizations for 2027.
- Concrete progress on San Mateo monetization (announced IPO/sale/drop-down process) that surfaces midstream value, or a pullback into the mid-$40s that restores margin of safety while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Waha basis worsens or the Hugh Brinson timeline slips into 2027, extending the gas drag beyond current expectations.
- San Mateo term-loan refinancing stress or an MVC deficiency trigger emerges in a drilling slowdown, undermining the "cash neutral" framing.
- Oil breaks below the mid-$60s and stays there, pushing FCF negative against the aggressive Cardinal/BLM capital commitments.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint — it will show how much the gas drag is compressing cash flow, whether estimates continue to fall, and the closed Cardinal transaction's initial contribution. The Hugh Brinson in-service update by year-end 2026 is the second key checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Matador is a well-run, oil-weighted Delaware Basin operator with a differentiated integrated midstream, disciplined shareholder returns, a clean four-quarter beat record, and a broad open-market insider-buying cluster that reflects management conviction at current prices. Those attributes argue firmly against selling. But the case for owning it aggressively has weakened since the prior BUY calls: forward estimates are falling sharply, the Waha gas basis is crushing realized gas prices on ~42% of production with no structural relief until 2027, San Mateo is levering up into an aggressive capital-deployment cycle, and 11% short interest corroborates the near-term caution. The trailing valuation looks cheap, but on falling estimates that cheapness is a warning flag rather than a buy signal, and the re-rating catalysts (Hugh Brinson realizations, San Mateo monetization) are 2027-weighted and undated.

The prior trajectory (7.1 → 6.7 → 6.8, BUY → HOLD → BUY) and the –0.06/month score velocity already pointed to erosion; the current negative-revision evidence confirms rather than reverses that trend. We move to HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting balanced risk/reward. The path to upgrade runs through estimate stabilization and gas-basis relief; the path to downgrade runs through a prolonged Waha drag or San Mateo funding stress. We would become buyers again on a pullback toward the mid-$40s or on the first two consecutive months of positive estimate revisions.
