## Executive Summary

G-III Apparel Group is navigating one of the most significant business transitions in its 50-year history: the wind-down of Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger licensed brands, which at peak contributed over $1.5 billion in annual revenue. The company has already lost $254M in CK/TH sales in FY2026 and guides for an additional $470M loss in FY2027. While the balance sheet is genuinely strong ($407M cash, near-zero debt, $264M FY2026 FCF), the earnings trajectory is deteriorating rapidly: consensus estimates have been slashed 31-36% across all forward periods in the last 90 days.

The fundamental problem is that replacement revenue from new licenses (Converse, BCBG, French Connection) and owned brand growth cannot offset the magnitude and speed of the PVH brand wind-down. Management guides FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $2.00-2.10, down from $2.61 in FY2026 and $4.42 in FY2025. With tariffs creating margin headwinds across 72% of sourcing, PVH litigation escalating publicly, a securities fraud class action pending, and 23.3% of the float sold short, the risk profile overwhelms the valuation support. The options market confirms stress: near-term IV of 74.5% vs. 39% historical vol, extreme backwardation, and put/call OI of 5.93x.

## Company Overview

G-III Apparel Group designs, sources, and distributes apparel across 30+ owned and licensed brands, operating primarily through wholesale (96.7% of sales) to approximately 1,500 retail customers. The company's business model functions as a platform, leveraging shared infrastructure in sourcing, design, and distribution to operate multiple brand portfolios efficiently.

**Key Owned Brands:** DKNY (~$650M sales), Karl Lagerfeld (~$630M), Donna Karan (relaunched Spring 2024), Vilebrequin (luxury swimwear, 104 stores globally)

**Key Licensed Brands:** Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger (both expiring through Dec 2027), Levi's, Nautica, Halston, Champion, Converse, BCBG, French Connection, and major sports leagues (NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL)

**Customer Concentration:** Extreme — Macy's 20.6%, TJX 11.4%, Ross 11.0%. Top 10 customers = 67.6% of revenue. Macy's is closing 150 stores by 2028; Saks Global filed for bankruptcy (costing GIII $57.5M in impairments and bad debt).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is unambiguously negative for the next 12-18 months:

**Estimate Revisions — Falling Sharply:**
- Current year EPS: $2.09 (down from $3.04 ninety days ago, -31.3%)
- Next year EPS: $2.29 (down from $3.60, -36.3%)
- Current quarter: expected loss of $(0.30) vs. prior estimate of $(0.02)

**Revenue Replacement Math:**
G-III must replace ~$830M in CK/TH revenue. Management's "go-forward portfolio" strategy relies on:
1. Owned brand growth (DKNY, Karl Lagerfeld, Donna Karan) — growing mid-single digits
2. New licenses (Converse launched Fall 2025, BCBG, French Connection) — multi-year ramp
3. International expansion via AWWG partnership — early stage

At mid-single-digit owned brand growth on a ~$1.7B base, that's ~$85M annual organic revenue gain. New licenses might contribute $50-100M annually as they ramp. Total: $135-185M per year in replacement revenue vs. $830M in lost revenue. The math suggests the revenue trough extends through FY2028 at minimum.

**Owned Brand Mix Improvement:**
Owned brands grew from 47% to 57% of revenue over three years. Higher ownership correlates with better margins (licensing royalty income, no license fees). If managed brands reach 65-70% of revenue by FY2028-2029, structural margin improvement is achievable — but this is a 3+ year thesis with significant execution risk.

**Peer Comparison:**
Among apparel peers, GIII trades at a modest premium to CRI (13.4x) — the only profitable comparable. Under Armour, Oxford Industries, and Capri Holdings all report losses. GIII's premium reflects its net-cash balance sheet and continued profitability despite the transition.

**Valuation Floor:**
Net cash of $395M = $9.36/share. Book value of $1.76B = $41.71/share. These provide meaningful downside protection. The stock trades at 0.70x book, which is cheap for a profitable company with a fortress balance sheet.

**However:** The valuation is cheap because the earnings trajectory is deteriorating. Forward P/E of 14.4x on declining estimates is not compelling — if FY2028 EPS recovers to $2.50-3.00, the stock at $29 represents reasonable value. If the transition takes longer or tariffs compress margins further, $2.00 EPS could be the new normal, implying fair value of $24-28 at peer multiples.

## Competitive Landscape

G-III's platform model (shared sourcing, design, distribution across brands) is a legitimate competitive advantage for acquiring and operating licensed/owned brands. The company can onboard new brands at lower incremental cost than competitors building from scratch.

**Positive trajectory signals:**
- Hired Dana Perlman (PVH's former Chief Strategy Officer) in Jan 2024
- International expansion via AWWG partnership (18.7% equity stake)
- Off-price channel strength (TJX + Ross = 22.4% of sales) in a consumer-trading-down environment

**Negative trajectory signals:**
- PVH deciding to bring CK/TH in-house demonstrates structural vulnerability in the licensee model — the licensor can always reclaim brands
- Department store channel contracting (Macy's store closures, Saks/Hudson's Bay bankruptcies)
- Tariff headwinds hit importers disproportionately vs. domestic-sourcing competitors

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **License Revenue Cliff (STRUCTURAL):** $470M in additional CK/TH sales loss in FY2027, $372M expiring Dec 2026. No renewal options. This is realized damage occurring in real time.

2. **Tariff Exposure (SEVERE):** 72% of product from Vietnam (36.9%), China (25%), Bangladesh (9.7%). Two Vietnamese vendors represent 27% and 15.6% of total purchases respectively. Tariff regime highly uncertain. Every 10% tariff increase on these countries represents ~$200M in additional costs pre-mitigation.

3. **PVH Litigation Escalation:** The dispute has moved from commercial disagreement to allegations of threats against PVH executives (May 2026). Legal costs, management distraction, and potential for adverse discovery all weigh on the thesis.

4. **Securities Fraud Class Action:** Filed April 2026 alleging concealment of Saks losses. Likely ambulance-chasing given the Q4 surprise, but creates headline risk and legal expense.

5. **Customer Concentration:** Macy's (20.6%) closing 150 stores. If Macy's revenue declines 20%, that's ~$120M in additional headwind.

6. **Key Person Risk:** Morris Goldfarb is 75 with no publicly announced succession plan. His son Jeffrey Goldfarb serves as EVP and director, and the hire of Dana Perlman (ex-PVH Chief Strategy Officer) could signal succession planning, but no formal plan has been disclosed.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Multiple converging — (1) Q1 FY2027 guided loss of $(0.30)-(0.40), earnings report likely June-July 2026; (2) CK/TH $372M expiration on Dec 31, 2026 creates visible revenue cliff through H2; (3) PVH litigation ruling or escalation (timing uncertain); (4) consumer spending deterioration during May-June 2026 earnings season.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The Q1 loss and continued estimate revisions downward should drive price pressure through summer 2026.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 23.3% with 15.7 days to cover is extremely elevated. This creates meaningful squeeze risk — any positive catalyst (acquisition announcement, tariff relief, settlement with PVH) could drive violent short covering. The SI concentration means the stock has already absorbed significant selling pressure, and remaining shorts are likely committed to the thesis.

**Borrow and options signal:** Borrow cost at this SI level is likely elevated. Rich near-term IV makes buying puts expensive — cost-of-carry for puts or short stock is a real drag.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base bear case $22-24 (25% downside from $29.43). Bull case squeeze risk to $34-36 (18-22% upside). Risk/reward ratio: approximately 1.2:1 — insufficient for new short initiation.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE — Crowded, and the balance sheet floor ($9.36/share in net cash, 0.70x book value) limits further downside relative to squeeze risk at 23.3% SI. The thesis is correct (fundamentals deteriorating) but the trade is too crowded and the margin of safety on the short side is insufficient given the company's tangible asset backing.**

## Conclusion

G-III Apparel Group presents a classic value trap configuration: cheap on trailing metrics, fortress balance sheet, but with rapidly deteriorating forward fundamentals and no near-term catalyst for recovery. The CK/TH license loss is a structural impairment of the business model's most profitable revenue stream. Estimate revisions of -31% to -36% across all periods confirm that consensus has not yet finished repricing the earnings power downward.

The balance sheet provides meaningful downside protection (net cash = 32% of market cap, P/B = 0.70x), which is why this is AVOID rather than SHORT. But collapsing estimates, expanding tariff headwinds, PVH litigation escalation, and an options market pricing imminent stress make this an unfavorable risk/reward from the long side at current levels.

Wait for evidence of revenue stabilization or substantially lower prices ($20-22) before re-engaging.
