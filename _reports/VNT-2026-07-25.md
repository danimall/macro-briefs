**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vontier is a global industrial technology company built around the fueling, convenience-retail, fleet, and vehicle-repair "mobility ecosystem," spun out of Fortive in October 2020 and operated on a Danaher-style lean playbook. The business generates high margins (18.3% operating, ~13% net) and strong free cash flow ($441M in FY25, a 14.3% FCF margin), but its revenue has been essentially flat over four years ($3.18B in 2022 to $3.08B in 2025). The equity story is one of margin quality, cash generation, and a shrinking share count (~12% of shares retired since spin), not organic expansion. The stock trades at ~$30.78, down roughly 21% year-to-date in 2026 and about 36% below its February 2026 high of $48.20.

Three developments frame the current setup. First, the April 2026 sale of a majority of the Teletrac Navman telematics business to Respida Capital for ~$220M, at an expected book loss of $60–110M, is a portfolio-pruning move that tells you a Mobility Technologies "growth" asset underdelivered. Second, the Matco captive-finance book is showing credit stress: Q1 2026 provisioning of $11.1M ran well ahead of $3.8M of write-offs, the financing-receivable allowance rose to $59.2M from $51.2M, and 90-day delinquencies increased to $9.4M against a backdrop of soft technician discretionary spending. Third, the highest-margin franchise, Environmental & Fueling Solutions (~47% of revenue, ~29.4% margin), carries a long-term secular question mark tied to EV/alternative-fuel adoption in petroleum fueling infrastructure.

At ~11x forward EPS on the current-year consensus of $3.41, with a mid-teens FCF yield and comfortable ~9x interest coverage, the balance sheet and cash generation are sound and the price is not demanding. But estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative over 90 days, core sales growth is decelerating (Q1 core +1.7% and MT declining), the 5-year cumulative TSR of 113 badly lags the peer group's 190, and there is no identified catalyst to close the valuation gap. This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: a quality asset with a suppressed multiple that lacks the growth or event catalyst needed to justify a higher score, and enough soft-data deterioration (Matco credit, MT decline, loss-on-sale) to keep us from rewarding the cheapness.

## Company Overview

Vontier operates three segments:

- **Environmental & Fueling Solutions (EFS)** — Gilbarco Veeder-Root: fuel dispensers, environmental/leak-detection monitoring, vapor recovery, forecourt controllers, and a large aftermarket parts/service annuity. Roughly 47% of revenue at a ~29.4% segment margin. This is the profit engine, built on a large regulated installed base.
- **Mobility Technologies (MT)** — Invenco (POS/payment, self-checkout, site software), DRB (car wash technology), ANGI (CNG/RNG dispensing), Driivz (EV-charging software), and, until mid-2026, Teletrac Navman (fleet telematics). Roughly 37% of revenue at ~18–19% margin. This segment grew 10.8% in FY25 but declined slightly in Q1 2026.
- **Repair Solutions** — Matco Tools, a mobile-franchise distribution model selling tools, toolboxes, and diagnostics to professional mechanics, plus a captive financing book (~$430M gross receivables). Roughly 19% of revenue at ~21% margin, but shrinking (−6.9% in FY25).

The company employs ~7,800 people across ~25 countries, with ~31% international sales. Competitive advantages rest on leading positions in niche served markets, recurring aftermarket/SaaS revenue off large installed bases, regulatory-driven demand (environmental compliance, EMV payment mandates, driver-safety telematics rules), and the margin discipline of the Vontier Business System. No single customer exceeds 10% of sales.

Because Information Technology is the one sector where our selection process has shown a durable, positive edge relative to the market, the bar for a favorable read is genuinely lower here than in most sectors. Vontier, though classified in IT/Electronic Equipment & Instruments, functions economically as a specialty industrial with heavy exposure to petroleum fueling infrastructure and technician discretionary spending. The thematic tailwind that lifts semiconductor and AI-infrastructure names does not reach this business, and we treat it on its own fundamentals rather than on sector momentum.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is modest top-line, high margins, strong FCF, and elevated but improving leverage.

**Revenue** has been flat-to-down: $3.18B (2022), $3.10B (2023), $2.98B (2024), and $3.08B in 2025 (+3.2%, with the 2024 dip reflecting the Coats divestiture). Core sales grew 3.7% in FY25. Q1 2026 revenue of $750.6M was up 1.3% GAAP and 1.7% core, a deceleration that matters for the growth thesis.

**Profitability** is the story's strength. Operating margin improved to 18.3% in FY25 from 18.0% in FY24, with Q1 2026 at 18.0%. Net margin runs ~13.2%. ROE of 32.6% and ROA of 9.3% are strong, though ROE is flattered by a thin, leveraged equity base.

**Cash flow** is high quality. FY25 operating cash flow was $511M and FCF $441M, with OCF exceeding net income. One watch item: Q1 2026 OCF fell to $46.5M from $110.4M on an $84.8M working-capital use, likely timing-driven, but worth monitoring given the credit dynamics discussed below.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was ~$2.1B at year-end 2025, cut to ~$1.9B by Q1 2026. D/E of 2.51 and an Altman Z of 1.82 (grey zone) are driven by ~$2.2B of goodwill/intangibles (roughly half of assets) and a thin tangible-equity base, not by distress. Interest coverage is comfortable at ~9x (EBIT ~$562M vs. interest ~$60M), interest expense is falling with deleveraging, the current ratio is 1.16, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. In Q1 2026 Vontier refinanced the $500M 1.80% senior notes due April 2026 using a $300M 364-day term loan (SOFR + 95bp, ~4.62%) plus $200M of cash, a net ~$200M debt reduction, but the low-coupon note was replaced with higher floating-rate debt, a mild ongoing interest-cost headwind and increased near-term refinancing exposure.

**Capital allocation** has centered on aggressive buybacks: $300M (8.0M shares) in FY25, $224.7M in FY24, and $70M in Q1 2026, taking the share count from ~160.5M (2022) to ~141.8M (Q1 2026 weighted average). The dividend is a token ~$0.10 annualized. Bolt-on M&A (Sergeant Sudz, $10.9M) is small. The company is converting cash into per-share value while pruning its portfolio.

## Growth Analysis

The honest read is that Vontier is a low-growth business dressed as a technology company. Revenue is roughly flat over four years, and the FY25 improvement leaned heavily on MT (+10.8%), the same segment that just declined in Q1 2026 and from which management sold Teletrac Navman below carrying value. EFS is a defensible profit engine but sells into episodic, regulation-driven upgrade cycles; revenue can fall once a mandated cycle completes. Repair Solutions is contracting (−6.9% in FY25) on soft technician spending.

Estimate revisions confirm the muted picture: current-year EPS consensus of $3.41 is down 0.9% over 90 days (and up 0.5% over 30 days), with next-year $3.71 down 1.9% over 90 days. This is nowhere near the +30% 90-day revision velocity that would signal an earnings inflection. The earnings beat/miss record is unremarkable: one beat, two in-line, one small miss over the last four quarters, with a −0.7% miss in the most recent quarter (March 2026). EPS growth to date has been powered more by buybacks than by operating income: Q1 2026 diluted EPS of $0.66 rose from $0.59 in part because share count fell.

The forward growth optionality — Driivz (EV-charging software) and ANGI (gaseous fuels) — is real but small relative to the petroleum-fueling franchise it is meant to hedge. Management guidance and consensus point to low-to-mid-single-digit core growth with EPS in the $3.40–$3.70 range, which the buyback amplifies at the per-share level. Given decelerating core sales, an MT segment now smaller and slower after the telematics sale, and Matco cyclical pressure, we score growth prospects at 4.5: directionally flat with a slight downward bias, consistent with the declining trajectory in prior reports.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$30.78 and a forward P/E near 11x on $3.41 consensus, Vontier looks statistically cheap. The peer set in Electronic Equipment & Instruments carries a median P/E of 29.5x (BMI 29.5x, OSIS 26.5x, MIR 167.8x, ITRI 12.9x, PI negative), so on a headline basis Vontier trades at roughly a third of the peer median. The FCF yield is ~10% on market cap and higher on a per-share basis, and analyst targets span $35 (low) to $48 (high) with a $38.50 median; every target sits above the current price.

We deliberately resist reading that discount as an automatic buy signal. A compressed multiple on a flat-revenue business with a secular overhang on its most profitable segment is frequently a value trap rather than a mispricing, and our own track record shows cheap-scoring names have tended to underperform without an independent catalyst. Here, the catalysts that would close the gap are absent: estimate revisions are negative over 90 days, core growth is decelerating, and the Teletrac Navman loss-on-sale signals that portfolio quality is being worked down, not up. The comparison to the peer median also flatters Vontier: ITRI, the closest analog (grid-edge/IIoT), trades at 12.9x, indicating the market applies a low multiple to slow-growing hardware franchises regardless of margin quality.

The bull frame is a cycle-normalized one: if MT growth reaccelerates ex-telematics, Matco credit stabilizes, and EFS holds its ~29% margin, mid-teens FCF supports a re-rating toward 14–16x, or ~$48–55 on $3.40–$3.70 EPS. The bear frame is that the multiple is correct — a slow, ICE-levered industrial deserves 9–11x — in which case fair value sits near the current price or modestly below. Weighing these, we score valuation at 5.5: undemanding, with real downside support from cash flow and buybacks, but no identifiable reason the market is wrong that would justify a high score. This is a slight step down from prior reports, reflecting the accumulating soft-data deterioration rather than any change in the multiple itself.

## Competitive Landscape

Vontier is a niche leader rather than a broad-market player, holding #1 or strong positions in fuel dispensers and environmental monitoring (Gilbarco Veeder-Root), car-wash technology (DRB, tied to top-20 North American chains), and mobile tool distribution (Matco, competing with Snap-on). Demand is heavily regulation- and installed-base-driven (EMV mandates, environmental/leak-detection rules, and driver-safety telematics regulation), which provides recurring, defensive revenue but also episodic upgrade cycles.

The trajectory is stable-to-softening rather than improving. MT declined in Q1 2026 and just shed an underperforming telematics unit at a loss; Repair Solutions is losing volume to soft technician spending and now shows credit stress in its captive book; and the car-wash market faces a maturing express-wash buildout. The central structural debate is EFS's exposure to internal-combustion fueling infrastructure. Near-term the large ICE car parc protects demand, but long-term EV/alternative-fuel adoption strikes at the highest-margin franchise, and the hedges (Driivz, ANGI) are not yet large enough to offset it. Competitive position level is solid; competitive position trajectory is flat with a downward tilt, which is why we score this dimension at 5.0. Given that this dimension has shown no reliable recent predictive edge in our process, we anchor it to the fundamental trajectory and confirm it against the stronger financial-health read.

## Risk Assessment

The most important risks, none of which changed between the FY25 10-K and the Q1 2026 10-Q:

1. **Secular EV threat to EFS.** The highest-margin segment sells into petroleum fueling infrastructure. Long-term adoption of EVs and alternative fuels could erode demand for dispensers, monitoring, and station payment tech. This is the defining bull/bear variable and the primary reason the multiple is structurally suppressed.
2. **Matco captive-finance credit stress.** With volumes falling and the segment doubling as a lender, the Q1 provisioning and delinquency build is an emerging, under-appreciated risk.
3. **Teletrac Navman loss-on-sale.** The expected book loss confirms an MT "growth" asset underdelivered and modestly shrinks the segment. Portfolio quality is being trimmed, not built.
4. **Leverage and goodwill.** ~$1.9–2.1B debt and ~$2.2B goodwill/intangibles create impairment risk if any reporting unit deteriorates (none taken 2023–25). The refinancing swapped a 1.8% note for ~4.6% floating debt, a small ongoing headwind plus near-term (364-day) refinancing exposure.
5. **Tariffs/supply chain.** Vontier imports U.S.-bound inventory; 2025+ tariffs are explicitly flagged as potentially material, with mitigation underway amid ongoing trade-policy volatility (the Q1 10-Q notes a Supreme Court ruling struck the legal basis for some tariffs, leaving uncertainty).
6. **Governance and insider signal.** Executive comp stayed generous despite the 5-year TSR shortfall. The CFO sold 5,489 shares in the open market at $40.16 in March 2026 near the highs, with no offsetting insider buying, a neutral-to-soft signal. A 3/2/2026 8-K reported an officer change (Item 5.02) whose details warrant confirmation.
7. **Legacy and execution items.** Asbestos liability (~$104.6M gross, ~$68M projected insurance recovery), FCPA exposure in corruption-prone geographies, no FX hedging, and a multi-year ERP migration in progress.

We score risk factors at 5.0. The balance sheet is sound (~9x coverage, $441M FCF, covenant compliance), so the grey-zone Z-score overstates distress. But the combination of a building credit problem in Matco, a confirmed loss-on-sale signaling asset-quality erosion, and the unresolved EV overhang on the profit engine keeps this from a higher mark. Sector-level risk (cyclicality, ICE exposure) is largely priced; the incremental idiosyncratic risk is the Matco finance book trajectory.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 51.8% is rich versus 31.1% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 51.8% > six-month 45.1%), with a nearest-expiry implied move of ±11.7%. Put/call skew is elevated at 11.1%, indicating higher demand for downside protection. ATM IV jumped 17.4 points (34.4% to 51.8%) between late June and late July on a nearly flat stock, consistent with an approaching catalyst — most plausibly the next earnings print — being priced as a source of uncertainty. Short interest is elevated at 10.5% of float with 6.4 days to cover, confirming bearish institutional positioning without being crowded enough to create acute squeeze risk. The options and short-interest picture reinforces caution and argues against paying up ahead of the print; it does not, by itself, override the sound cash-flow fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Vontier is a high-quality, cash-generative niche leader trading at ~11x forward EPS with a mid-teens FCF yield. EFS is a durable, regulation-driven annuity at ~29% margins; the buyback has retired ~12% of shares since spin and continues; and the balance sheet is deleveraging with ~9x interest coverage. If MT stabilizes ex-telematics, Matco credit normalizes, and management redeploys FCF into accretive buybacks and bolt-ons, mid-teens FCF supports a re-rating toward 14–16x, or roughly $48–55, with analyst targets ranging to $48. The stock is already 36% off its February high on soft results while fundamentals remain intact, which improves the risk/reward.

**Bear Case.** Revenue has been flat for four years, and the growth engine (MT) just declined and shed an underperforming unit at a $60–110M loss. Matco's captive-finance book is showing rising delinquencies and provisions during a period of soft technician spending, a lender's early-warning pattern. The EV secular overhang threatens the highest-margin franchise, and the low multiple may be the market's correct assessment rather than a mispricing. Estimate revisions are negative over 90 days, the 5-year TSR badly trails peers, and the CFO sold near the highs. In this frame fair value is near $27–30, roughly 3–12% below the current price, with downside cushioned by FCF and buybacks.

The evidence tilts to a middle outcome: a sound, cheap business without the growth or catalyst to re-rate meaningfully in the near term, and with enough soft-data erosion to keep us from rewarding the cheapness.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale). Reassessment timeline: next earnings report (August 2026), with the Matco financing-receivable trend and post-Teletrac MT margin mix as the key data points.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EPS estimate revisions turn decisively positive (>3% over 90 days) alongside core sales reaccelerating above ~4%, particularly in MT ex-telematics.
- Matco financing-receivable credit metrics stabilize — provisioning falls back in line with write-offs and 90-day delinquencies decline for two consecutive quarters.
- A price pullback into the high-$20s that widens the FCF yield toward the mid-teens without any fundamental deterioration, improving the margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continued acceleration of Matco credit losses (provisioning materially outrunning write-offs, allowance and delinquencies rising for another two quarters), signaling structural strain in the captive-lender model.
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge against any reporting unit, or evidence EFS demand is eroding faster than expected (falling backlog, declining dispenser/monitoring revenue).
- Core sales turning negative combined with further downward estimate revisions.

**This period's trajectory context:** the score has moved 5.5 (Mar) → 6.0 (May) → 5.1 (Jun), and this report holds near the lower end at 5.15. The recent slippage reflects genuine soft-data deterioration (MT decline, Teletrac loss-on-sale, Matco credit build), not merely the ~21% year-to-date price decline. We are careful not to conflate the two: a lower price on unchanged fundamentals would improve risk/reward, but here the price decline is accompanied by identifiable fundamental softening, which justifies staying at HOLD rather than upgrading on cheapness alone. The third consecutive HOLD sets a higher bar for continuation, and we clear it because neither a growth catalyst (for BUY) nor a distress trigger (for AVOID) is present.

## Conclusion

Vontier is a well-run, high-margin, FCF-rich niche industrial whose equity story is per-share value creation through margins, cash flow, and buybacks rather than growth. At ~11x forward EPS with a mid-teens FCF yield and a deleveraging balance sheet, the price is undemanding and the downside is cushioned. But the offsets are substantial: flat multi-year revenue, decelerating core sales, an MT segment now smaller and slower after selling Teletrac Navman below carrying value, building credit stress in the Matco captive-finance book, a 5-year TSR that badly trails peers, and an unresolved EV overhang on the profit engine. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, options and short-interest positioning signal near-term caution, and there is no identified catalyst to re-rate the stock.

The result is a business worth holding for its cash generation and optionality but not worth chasing without evidence of either a growth inflection or credit stabilization. We rate VNT a HOLD with an overall score of 5.15, and we would look to reassess after the August earnings report with the Matco credit trend and post-divestiture MT margins as the deciding variables.
