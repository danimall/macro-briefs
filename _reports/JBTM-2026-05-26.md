## Executive Summary

JBT Marel Corporation is a newly formed global food and beverage technology solutions provider, created through the January 2025 acquisition of Iceland's Marel hf. by legacy JBT Corporation. The combined entity generated $3.8B in revenue in 2025 and is guiding to $4.0-4.1B in 2026 with adjusted EPS of $8.00-$8.50. At $132-135, the stock trades at approximately 16x forward adjusted EPS — reasonable for an industrials company with 50% recurring revenue, a dominant competitive position in protein processing equipment, and a credible path to 20% EBITDA margins by 2028 (from 15.8% currently).

The investment case hinges on successful merger integration execution. Q1 2026 results were encouraging: $100M in FCF, 210bps of YoY margin expansion, orders exceeding $1B for the second consecutive quarter, and net leverage declining to 2.6x. However, two unremediated material weaknesses in Marel's legacy internal controls remain a meaningful overhang — disclosure controls are formally "not effective" — and the company faced a $403M convertible note maturity requiring immediate refinancing. The stock dropped ~25% from its February 2026 peak of $170 to its late-April trough near $115 before recovering post-Q1 earnings, reflecting market uncertainty around macro conditions and integration pace.

The risk/reward profile is moderately favorable for patient holders. The 5-7% organic revenue growth target, $65-75M synergy savings program, and high-teens EPS growth trajectory support appreciation over 12-18 months, but the material weakness overhang, elevated leverage, and macro sensitivity (particularly to potential rate hikes and consumer spending weakness) argue against aggressive positioning today.

## Company Overview

JBT Marel Corporation operates through two segments:

**Protein Solutions (~49% of revenue):** Equipment and services for primary processing and harvesting of animal proteins — poultry, pork, fish, and beef. Core technologies include primary processing systems, cut-up, bone detection/removal, portioning, and robotic batching. This segment delivered 21.7% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1 2026.

**Prepared Food and Beverage Solutions (~51% of revenue):** Downstream preparation, preservation, and packaging of foods/beverages. Includes pet food, dairy, bakery, pharmaceutical, nutraceutical applications, and warehouse automation (AGV). Q1 2026 margin was 14.7%.

The company operates 50+ manufacturing/distribution facilities globally, derives approximately 62% of revenue internationally, and generates roughly 50% of revenue from recurring sources (aftermarket parts, services, leases, software subscriptions). The combined entity is one of only a handful of global full-line providers spanning primary through secondary/further processing, creating meaningful cross-selling opportunities and customer switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

The GAAP figures for 2025 ($51M net loss) require normalization. The loss was driven by $147M in pension plan termination charges, $115M in M&A costs, $31M in restructuring, and $95M in incremental interest expense — all non-recurring or merger-related. On an adjusted basis, FY2025 produced $333M in net income ($6.41/share) on $3.8B revenue at 15.8% EBITDA margin.

Q1 2026 demonstrated improving fundamentals: revenue of $936M (+10% YoY, +3.5% organic excluding FX), adjusted EBITDA of $142M (15.2% margin, +210bps YoY), and adjusted EPS of $1.58 (vs. $0.97 prior year, +63%). Orders of $1.07B represent a book-to-bill of 1.14x, supporting backlog of $1.49B.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Net debt stood at $1.63B as of Q1 2026 (down from $1.89B at merger close), representing 2.6x TTM adjusted EBITDA — rapid improvement from approximately 3.0x a year earlier. The company has $2.1B in total liquidity including $1.8B in undrawn revolver availability. Bank covenant compliance is comfortable (2.5x vs. 4.0x maximum). Approximately 48% of debt is variable rate, creating interest rate sensitivity.

The $403M convertible notes maturing May 2026 required immediate attention at report time and will be settled from cash/revolver capacity. The 2030 convertible notes ($575M at 0.375% coupon with ~$188 conversion price) add dilution risk only if the stock appreciates substantially.

### Cash Flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $342M (inclusive of $101M in merger transaction payments). Free cash flow was approximately $250M. Q1 2026 FCF of $100M annualizes to $400M — representing a meaningful improvement trajectory as one-time merger costs fade. On a $7B market cap, this implies a prospective FCF yield of approximately 5.7%, attractive for an industrial compounder.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth is modest (3-5%) but steady.** The food processing equipment market grows with global protein consumption, driven by population growth and rising incomes in emerging markets. Poultry demand is particularly strong. The company's 5-7% organic CAGR target through 2028 assumes healthy end-market conditions plus market share gains from cross-selling the combined portfolio.

**Margin expansion is the primary growth driver.** The delta between current margins (15.2-15.8%) and the 2028 target (20%) represents the largest source of earnings growth. Q1 2026's 210bps YoY improvement validates the trajectory. Cumulative annualized synergy savings of $38M (toward $65-75M target) are tracking well. The hiring of a new Chief Accounting Officer from CONMED specifically to address SOX remediation signals continued organizational investment.

**Revenue per quarter growth trajectory:** $936M in Q1 2026 vs. ~$950M/quarter implied at guidance midpoint suggests growth weighted to later quarters, likely from backlog conversion and seasonal patterns.

**Risk to growth:** Organic growth acceleration beyond 5-7% would require broader capital spending recovery from food processors, which is sensitive to consumer confidence and commodity protein prices.

## Competitive Landscape

JBT Marel holds a strong and improving competitive position in food processing equipment. The Marel acquisition eliminated what was JBT's primary competitor in protein processing and created one of very few full-value-chain providers globally. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Installed base lock-in:** ~50% recurring revenue from aftermarket parts and services creates high switching costs once equipment is deployed.
2. **Full-line capability:** Spanning primary processing through secondary/further processing is rare — most competitors are either specialized in specific protein types or specific processing stages.
3. **Global scale:** 50+ facilities across 14+ countries enable local service delivery and reduce logistics costs.
4. **Technology leadership:** Robotic batching, bone detection, portioning optimization, and increasingly digital/software-enabled solutions raise competitive barriers.

The competitive position is improving post-merger due to cross-selling opportunities — Protein Solutions customers can now access Prepared Food & Beverage equipment through a single vendor relationship, and vice versa. The book-to-bill ratio of 1.14x in Q1 2026 supports this thesis.

**Competitive risk:** Larger industrial conglomerates with greater financial resources could enter the space through acquisition. However, the specialized domain knowledge required in food safety, biological variability, and processing chemistry creates meaningful barriers to entry.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Material Weakness Overhang (HIGH):** Two unremediated material weaknesses in IT general controls and journal entry controls mean disclosure controls are formally "not effective." While no material misstatements have occurred, this creates regulatory risk, potential headline risk, and limits investor confidence. Remediation timeline extends through FY2026. The new CAO hire is encouraging but not yet sufficient.

2. **Leverage and Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH):** At 2.6x net leverage with 48% variable rate debt, the company faces meaningful interest rate risk. Offsetting: deleveraging trajectory is rapid ($250M+ annual debt reduction from FCF).

3. **Poultry Concentration (MODERATE):** Protein Solutions (the higher-margin, faster-growing segment) is heavily exposed to poultry processing demand. An avian flu event (H5N1) could dramatically reduce customer capex. This is an unhedgeable biological risk.

4. **Integration Execution (MODERATE):** Synergy cost estimates have already escalated from $25-30M to $55-60M. While $38M in cumulative savings have been achieved, remaining synergies require plant closures and headcount reductions that carry execution and cultural risk. The dual-listing structure (NYSE + Nasdaq Iceland) adds administrative complexity.

5. **Macro/Consumer Sensitivity (MODERATE):** Food processors' capex decisions are ultimately linked to consumer spending on protein. In a weakening macro environment, food companies may defer equipment orders. Counter-argument: food is less discretionary than other industrial end markets, and automation/labor replacement spending may be less cyclical.

6. **Tariff Exposure (LOW-MODERATE):** Management estimates 25-50bps of margin impact from current tariff policies. Manufacturing across 14+ countries creates complex cross-border supply chain exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($165-185, +22-37% upside)
- Synergy savings achieve or exceed $65-75M target by 2027
- Material weakness remediated by year-end 2026 without incident
- EBITDA margins reach 18%+ in 2027, on track for 20% in 2028
- Organic growth sustains at 5-7% as protein processing capex cycle extends
- Net leverage declines below 2.0x, enabling capital return acceleration
- Stock re-rates to 18-20x forward earnings ($10+ 2027E EPS × 18x = $180)

### Bear Case ($100-115, -15-25% downside)
- Macro deterioration → food processors defer capex → organic growth stalls at 0-2%
- Material weakness leads to restatement or delayed filing → governance discount widens
- Synergy execution falters → margins plateau at 16-17% → 2028 targets missed
- Avian flu event compresses Protein Solutions demand
- Rate hikes increase interest expense → EPS compression
- Stock de-rates to 12-14x earnings on persistent execution concerns ($8 EPS × 13x = $104)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Material weakness remediated (formal announcement of effective controls)
- Two consecutive quarters of EBITDA margin above 17.5% (demonstrating trajectory toward 20%)
- Stock declines to $115-120 on no fundamental deterioration (provides 14-15x forward P/E entry point)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material weakness leads to restatement, delayed filing, or SEC enforcement action
- Orders decline below $850M per quarter for two consecutive periods (demand deterioration)
- Net leverage reverses course and rises above 3.0x
- FY2026 guidance cut below $7.50 adjusted EPS

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late July/early August 2026 for Q2 results). Key items to monitor: margin trajectory, order trends, synergy savings progress, and material weakness remediation status.

## Conclusion

JBT Marel is a fundamentally sound business undergoing a complex post-merger integration. The combination is strategically compelling — creating a full-value-chain food processing equipment provider with 50% recurring revenue and significant margin expansion opportunity. At ~16x forward adjusted earnings with ~5.7% FCF yield, the stock is reasonably priced relative to its growth profile and discounted versus peers.

However, the unremediated material weaknesses, elevated leverage, and current macro uncertainty prevent a higher-conviction call. The stock requires continued execution — margin expansion, synergy delivery, SOX remediation — to unlock value. For investors already positioned, the risk/reward is favorable enough to hold with a 12-18 month horizon targeting $160-170. For new capital, waiting for either a pullback to $115-120 or resolution of the material weakness overhang would improve the asymmetry.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Growth Hold)** — Positive 12-month thesis supported by margin expansion trajectory and FCF generation, but execution risk warrants patience rather than conviction positioning at current levels.
