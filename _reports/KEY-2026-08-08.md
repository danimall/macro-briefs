## Executive Summary

KeyCorp's earnings recovery from the 2024 trough is real and measurable. Q2 2026 diluted EPS from continuing operations was $0.44 versus $0.35 a year earlier (+26%), net interest margin expanded 23bp year over year to 2.89%, pre-provision net revenue rose 8.9% to $747M, and ROTCE from continuing operations reached 12.9% against a FY2024 figure of negative 3.01%. Management raised FY2026 guidance on July 21 (revenue TE +7–8%, NII TE +9–11%, average commercial loans +8–10%) and Moody's upgraded the holding company to Baa1 from Baa2 on July 15, with KeyBank's long-term deposit rating moving to A1. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters by 3–6%.

Two pieces of evidence from the August 4 Form 10-Q materially change how I underwrite the next twelve months. First, the composition of the NII gain: pre-tax cash-flow-hedge losses reclassified into interest income on loans fell from $(183)M in 1H25 to $(64)M in 1H26, a $119M swing that accounts for roughly 51% of the $233M increase in 1H net interest income (TE). That is a favorable disclosure for guidance confidence; the path from 2.89% toward the 3.00–3.05% Q4 exit rate is partly contractual and does not depend on the Fed. It also means the underlying franchise contribution to NII growth is smaller than the headline suggests. Second, the credit cushion is thinning while problem-loan formation accelerates: loans placed on nonaccrual in Q2 were $365M against a trailing four-quarter average near $236M, nonperforming loans rose from $615M to $809M in six months, yet provision of $92M came in below net charge-offs of $115M (a $23M net release), and ALLL/NPL coverage fell from 232% to 179%.

At $22.73 the stock trades at 12.4x FY2026 consensus of $1.83, 10.6x FY2027 consensus of $2.14, 1.67x tangible book of $13.62, and 1.40x book of $16.19, with a 3.6% dividend covered at a ~45% payout. Against peer medians (P/E 13.0, yield 2.7%) the discount is modest on current-year earnings and wider on the out-year. The binding constraint is the tangible-book math: on a 10.5% cost of equity and 3.5% terminal growth, a 12.9% ROTCE supports roughly 1.34x TBV and a 15% ROTCE supports roughly 1.64x. The current 1.67x already pays for full achievement of the 2027 target. The segment that is producing the balance-sheet growth (Commercial Bank, average loans +10.3% year over year) delivered net income of $423M in both Q2 2026 and Q2 2025, exactly flat, because noninterest expense rose 11.5% and servicing fees fell. I rate KEY a HOLD. Own it for a ~9% total shareholder yield (dividend plus buyback) while the operating-leverage question resolves; the appreciation case is not yet paid for by the evidence.

## Company Overview

KeyCorp is the Cleveland-based holding company for KeyBank N.A., a Category IV institution with $191.3B of total assets at June 30, 2026, up from $184.4B at year-end 2025. (A note on data integrity: a structured financial pull for this name returned total assets near $8.6B, which is a parent-only or mis-mapped figure; audited consolidated assets are $191.3B, and any ratio built on the smaller number is unusable. Altman Z-scores are similarly inapplicable to depositories.)

Two segments. **Consumer Bank** operates a 15-state branch network plus a national digital channel and wealth/trust business; Q2 net income $203M on revenue (TE) of $1,010M, average loans $33.3B, average deposits $87.4B. **Commercial Bank** combines a footprint middle-market unit with a national Institutional platform organized around seven industry verticals and housing KeyBanc Capital Markets (syndicated finance, underwriting, sales and trading, M&A advisory, public finance); Q2 net income $423M on revenue (TE) of $1,108M, average loans $76.2B, average deposits $58.9B.

What distinguishes KeyCorp from a typical $190B regional is the weight of the commercial and capital-markets franchise. Commercial loans were $81.6B, or 73.9% of the $110.4B book, at June 30; C&I alone was 56.8%, up from 54.1% six months earlier. Noninterest income was $2,842M in FY2025, roughly 38% of revenue (TE) of $7,513M, an unusually broad fee mix for the asset size, spanning investment banking ($169M in Q2), trust and investment services ($159M), cards and payments ($94M), corporate services ($80M), and commercial mortgage servicing ($49M). The company also administers or services $602.5B of loans, of which $588.5B is commercial real estate (roughly 35x its own $16.8B CRE book) as a national third-party master and special servicer.

The consumer book is in deliberate runoff ($30.0B to $28.9B in six months, described in the 10-Q as intentional run-off of low-yielding loans). Office is only 4% of CRE (about $599M, or 0.5% of total loans), with multifamily at 44.8% of CRE and no exposure to New York City rent-controlled multifamily.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income was $967M (FY2023), $(161)M (FY2024, reflecting a deliberate securities repositioning loss and the FDIC special assessment), $1,829M (FY2025), and $1,031M in 1H26. ROTCE followed: 9.63%, (3.01)%, 11.85%, and 13.0% for 1H26 continuing operations.

**Q2 2026 detail.** Income from continuing operations attributable to common shareholders $472M, diluted EPS $0.44. NII (TE) $1,258M with NIM 2.89%, up $108M and 23bp year over year. Noninterest income $706M (+2.3%); noninterest expense $1,217M (+5.5%). PPNR $747M versus $686M.

**Efficiency and returns are mid-pack, not top-quartile.** Q2 noninterest expense / revenue (TE) = $1,217M / $1,964M = 62.0%; 1H26 = 61.2%; FY2025 adjusted = 62.9%. Improving, but a low-60s efficiency ratio is not a competitive advantage. 1H26 annualized net income of $2,062M on average assets of $187,478M is a ~1.10% ROA, below the 1.2–1.3% top-quartile regionals target.

**Rate positioning is genuinely neutral.** The 10-Q simulation shows 12-month NII changing +0.15% in a −200bp scenario and +0.17% in a +200bp scenario, against a −5.00% Board tolerance. The disclosed sensitivity that matters is deposit beta: a 5-percentage-point change in the interest-bearing beta assumption moves the simulation by roughly 99bp. Cumulative interest-bearing beta was reported at 56% on the Q2 call. The NIM path is a deposit-pricing question, not a Fed question.

**Capital is being consumed by design.** Reconciling 1H26: net income $1,031M − dividends $517M − common repurchases $727M − excise tax $6M − comp-plan repurchases $47M − AOCI deterioration $394M + reissuance $78M − deferred comp $1M ≈ −$583M, matching total equity's decline from $20,381M to $19,798M. Capital returned (dividends plus buybacks) of ~$1,291M equaled roughly 125% of net income. With RWA up 4.4% to $152,317M and CET1 capital down 1.0%, the CET1 ratio fell 61bp to an estimated 11.17% and TCE/tangible assets fell 70bp to 7.7%. Book value per share slipped from $16.27 to $16.19; tangible book from $13.77 to $13.62.

Headroom remains adequate. Against a 7.70% minimum including a 3.20% stress capital buffer, 11.17% leaves roughly 347bp (~$5.3B of CET1). Adjusting for the AOCI opt-out ($1,919M of AFS losses added back) and the ~$297M after-tax HTM mark yields a "marked" CET1 near 9.72%, consistent with the ~9.8% cited on the Q2 call, still ~200bp above requirement.

**Two mechanical drags on tangible book.** AFS securities carry a ~$2,545M gross pre-tax unrealized loss (fair value $38,459M versus amortized cost $41,004M) with a 7.8-year weighted-average maturity; HTM carries a further ~$391M unrecognized loss. Separately, repurchases at an average $21.95 in Q2 against $13.62 TBV (roughly 1.6x) are TBV-dilutive by construction. A steepening curve improves NIM through lower short-rate deposit costs while worsening reported tangible book through the AFS mark. Earnings-based and book-based measures of the same quarter point in opposite directions; the economically correct read is that the AFS mark accretes back over 7.8 years while the NIM benefit is recognized now.

**Credit: flow metrics improving, stock metrics deteriorating.** Accruing loans 30–89 days past due fell from $266M (6/30/25) to $220M (12/31/25) to $138M (6/30/26), and 90+ days from $99M to $85M, the most forward-looking series, and it supports management's characterization of the problem as not broad-based. Net charge-offs held at 40–42bp, at the low end of 40–45bp full-year guidance. Against that: NPLs $615M → $809M, NPAs $627M → $818M, Q2 nonaccrual inflows of $365M (44% above the trailing four-quarter average), C&I NPLs $256M → $358M, CRE mortgage NPLs $157M → $256M, ALLL/loans 1.34% → 1.31%, ACL/loans 1.63% → 1.56%, and construction ALLL cut 33% from $55M to $37M. Management attributes the increase to idiosyncratic exposures in select C&I industries and multifamily; a third-party call summary describes three specific credits with possible resolution later in 2026. That characterization is not verifiable from a filing. Partially offsetting, commercial ALLL rose $29M in 1H specifically for qualitative reserves tied to geopolitical and energy-price risk.

**A note on the supplied multiples.** The data feed reports trailing and forward P/E both at 13.2565 alongside EPS of $1.52; at $22.73 those are mutually inconsistent (13.2565 implies EPS near $1.72, close to trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.70). I use consensus estimates and the company's own guidance for all valuation arithmetic below.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance, raised July 21: revenue (TE) up 7–8% from $7,513M; NII (TE) up 9–11% from $4,671M; NIM exiting Q4 at 3.00–3.05%; adjusted noninterest income up 5–6%; adjusted noninterest expense up 3–4%; average loans up 4–5% (from up 2–4%); average commercial loans up 8–10% (from up 6–8%); NCOs 40–45bp. Medium-term run-rate targets by end-2027: ROTCE 15.0%+ and NIM 3.25%+.

Reproducing the FY2026 arithmetic: midpoint revenue $8,077M − adjusted expense $4,895M = $3,182M PPNR; less ~$468M provision (42.5bp on ~$110B average loans) = $2,714M pre-tax; less 22% tax = $2,117M; less ~$144M preferred dividends = $1,973M; on ~1.07B diluted shares = **$1.84**. That brackets consensus of $1.83 and implies the guide is neither heroic nor padded.

Three growth engines are working. C&I balances grew 8.7% in six months to $62.7B. Utilities C&I grew 12.4% and data-center CRE grew from $152M (YE2024) to $695M (YE2025) to $766M (6/30/26), a five-fold increase in eighteen months, now the fourth-largest nonowner-occupied CRE property type at ~0.7% of total loans. The mass-affluent wealth strategy launched in 2023 has added 59,000 households and over $4B of AUM at under 10% penetration. Management guided Q3 investment banking fees up more than 20% sequentially from $169M, a specific and falsifiable near-term commitment.

Two things temper the growth score. First, consensus revisions are not confirming: current-quarter EPS was cut 2.5% over 30 days, next-quarter 1.4%, next-year 0.6%, with only current-year up 0.2%. This is flat-to-slightly-negative earnings momentum, not the accelerating revision pattern that precedes re-rating. Second, and more important, the growth is not converting. Commercial Bank grew average loans 10.3% ($7.1B) and produced net income of $423M in both periods, because expense rose 11.5% ($451M → $503M) and noninterest income fell 3.3% on a $21M decline in commercial mortgage servicing fees. Consumer Bank's net income rose 23% on an 8.0% *decline* in average loans, and $29M of its $50M pre-tax improvement (58%) came from lower provision. At the consolidated level the mix is healthier: 1H pre-tax income from continuing operations rose $251M, of which PPNR contributed $191M (76%) and lower provision $58M (23%), but the segment producing the balance sheet is not yet producing the earnings. Management's commitment that revenue grows roughly 2x expenses in 2026 is the load-bearing element of the 15% ROTCE target.

On that target: 1H26 annualized continuing-ops earnings to common of $1,916M on average TCE of $14,912M is 12.85%. Reaching 15% on that base needs ~$2,237M, a ~17% earnings increase. But TCE is contracting ($15,175M → $14,784M → $14,597M), so if above-TBV buybacks and AOCI drift take TCE toward $14.0B while earnings reach ~$2.1B, 15% arrives partly through denominator shrinkage. That is a legitimate path to the metric, but it is not the same thing as 17% earnings growth, and investors paying 1.67x TBV for the 15% should understand which mechanism delivers it.

A low multiple in a bank whose reserve coverage is falling into rising nonaccruals is a condition to monitor, not a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

KeyCorp's durable advantages are specific. The loan-to-deposit ratio of 73% against a stated target near 80% implies roughly $12B (~11%) of loan capacity fundable from existing deposits; most regionals run 85–95% and must bid for funding to grow. Fee breadth at 38% of revenue is unusual at this asset size and gives the P&L a second cylinder when spread income compresses. The $602.5B servicing platform is a genuine national franchise. Office exposure at 4% of CRE with no NYC rent-controlled multifamily is a real differentiator versus several peers.

The trajectory is mixed rather than improving. The 3.20% stress capital buffer is on the high side for a $190B bank, a supervisory read on the volatility of stressed revenue from the capital-markets and CRE businesses. KBCM competes nationally against far larger institutions, and the cost shows: investment banking fees fell 5.1% year over year in Q2 despite being up 3.8% for the half, and Commercial Bank expense grew 11.5% to pay producers. Uninsured deposits rose from 42% to 45% of total ($68.8B), covered 1.37x by $94.1B of contingent liquidity; adequate, but the trend direction is unfavorable. The FY2025 10-K names stablecoins among non-bank deposit competitors, an acknowledgment that funding pressure has a structural component.

The portfolio is being rotated toward capital-markets-adjacent, largely variable-rate commercial credit and away from consumer and slower-growth verticals (healthcare commercial −7.5% in six months; lease financing $2,270M → $1,997M). This raises spread income and asset velocity. It also concentrates the book in fewer, larger exposures, which is consistent with the pattern of large idiosyncratic nonaccruals now appearing. A change in industry mix does not by itself establish diversification; KeyCorp does not disclose single-name or top-20 borrower concentration, so whether name concentration is improving cannot be determined from the filings.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The undisclosed $13.7B "Finance" exposure.** The Finance industry bucket is 16.8% of commercial loans, second only to CRE at 25.9%, and grew 10.2% in six months. KeyCorp does not disaggregate it; whether it is fund-finance subscription lines, consumer-lender warehouses, private-credit facilities, or insurance lending is not disclosed. Non-depository financial institution lending has been an industry-wide focus of supervisory and investor attention since late 2025, and it is where the least-tested credit in the system may sit. This is the largest single unresolved credit question in the name and it is not quantifiable from the filings. [unverified — no external industry context on regional-bank NDFI exposure was obtained; the specific market events described are not in the sources.]

**2. Reserve coverage thinning into accelerating nonaccrual formation.** Detailed above. If the idiosyncratic characterization holds and the credits resolve in 2026, this is a non-event. If the pattern extends, provisions must rise faster than 40–45bp implies. Sensitivity: NCOs at 60bp on $115B ($690M) plus ~$200M of reserve build to restore 1.40% coverage would cost roughly $420M pre-tax, ~$0.31 of EPS.

**3. Multifamily, not office, is the CRE exposure.** Multifamily is 44.8% of CRE ($7,546M) and 75% of the $2.9B construction book; the 10-K flags urban oversupply pressuring underwritten rents, and multifamily was named in the Q2 NPA increase.

**4. Off-balance-sheet recourse.** Total guarantees carry $22,565M of maximum undiscounted potential payments against $176M of recorded liability. The largest is a FNMA DUS-style arrangement: $25.1B of principal sold with $8.2B of maximum potential payments reserved at $62M. KeyCorp originates and underwrites, FNMA purchases, and KeyCorp retains a first-loss share. Remote, but real in a severe multifamily downturn.

**5. Rate two-sidedness.** Further long-end pressure would widen the AFS mark and erode tangible book while leaving NII roughly unchanged (the ±200bp simulation is symmetric at +0.15%/+0.17%). The 137bp gap between reported and marked CET1 measures how much regulatory capital is an accounting election. [unverified — specific claims about the Fed's meeting cadence, dissents, rate path, and the 30-year yield level are not in the provided sources.]

**6. Structural constraints.** KeyBank's dividend capacity to the parent was $797M without prior approval at June 30, against a $3.0B authorization plus ~$890M of common and ~$144M of preferred dividends annually; this constrains the pace of capital return, not the ability. Goodwill of $2,752M equals 19% of TCE. Scotiabank's 14.9% stake with two board designation rights is a practical takeover deterrent, removing M&A optionality from the bull case.

**Offsets that are already priced or benign:** FDIC proposed a 1bp assessment reduction plus up to 1bp for resolution readiness on June 25, and exempted all IDIs from 2026/2027 resolution plan filings. Moody's upgraded July 15; S&P holds a positive outlook. Early-stage delinquency is improving. No covenant or refinancing cliff exists at the holding company, which held $5.3B of cash and short-term investments.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $28.00 (+23.2%)

The NIM path delivers because roughly half of it is contractual. Legacy receive-fixed swaps with a 1.6-year weighted-average maturity on $40.25B of notional carry ~20bp of negative spread (3.4% receive versus 3.6% pay), roughly $80M/year of drag that continues to roll off independent of Fed policy; deposit costs fell from 2.49% to 2.01% on interest-bearing balances in 1H with a 56% cumulative beta and further room. NIM exits 2026 at 3.00–3.05% and reaches 3.25%+ by end-2027. The three problem credits resolve, NCOs stay at 40–45bp, and the improving 30–89 day bucket ($266M → $138M) proves to have been the leading indicator. Commercial Bank's front-loaded producer hiring converts, revenue grows 2x expenses, and ROTCE reaches 15% with FY2027 EPS at $2.20+. The $3.0B authorization retires 10%+ of the share count over two years at accretive returns on equity, and if long rates fall the AFS mark accretes back, lifting tangible book while EPS compounds. At 13x $2.20 the stock is $28.60; on 1.93x a recovered $14.50 TBV, $28.00.

### Bear Case — $18.50 (−18.6%)

The "idiosyncratic" framing fails. Nonaccrual inflows at a five-quarter high were the leading edge, and the $13.7B undisclosed Finance bucket (grown 10.2% in six months into a segment of heightened supervisory attention) produces losses that force provisions well above 45bp. ALLL/NPL coverage at 179% versus 232% a year earlier means the cushion has to be rebuilt out of earnings: ~$0.31 of 2027 EPS at 60bp NCOs plus reserve restoration, taking FY2027 to ~$1.80. Simultaneously, the Commercial Bank never converts; 10% loan growth at tighter spreads with 11.5% expense growth is a structurally uneconomic trade, ROTCE stalls near 13%, and the multiple compresses to the 1.34x TBV that a 13% return justifies. Buybacks at 1.67x TBV continue to shrink tangible book, so the denominator flatters the ratio while destroying per-share book value. Long rates stay elevated, widening the $2,545M AFS mark further. At 1.36x a $13.60 TBV, $18.50.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The core reason to own KEY is a ~9.0% total shareholder yield (a 3.6% dividend covered at a 45% payout on FY2026 guidance, plus a committed $1.3B+ of 2026 repurchases against a $3.0B authorization equal to 12.3% of market cap) while the operating-leverage question resolves. The new evidence cuts both ways: better NII visibility from the swap roll-off, worse credit-stock and capital-consumption detail. It nets to a fair-value price rather than a directional call.

- **Base case: $24.00 (+5.6% price, +9.2% total return with dividend).** 11.2x FY2027 consensus of $2.14, cross-checked at ~1.71x a mid-2027 TBV near $14.00.
- **Bull case: $28.00 (+23.2%).** 13x FY2027 EPS of $2.20 on delivered 15% ROTCE and contained credit.
- **Bear case: $18.50 (−18.6%).** 1.36x current TBV of $13.62, the multiple justified by today's 12.9% ROTCE at a 10.5% cost of equity, with FY2027 EPS reset to ~$1.80 on provision normalization.
- **Probability-weighted (25/50/25): ~$23.63, +3.9% price, +7.5% total return.** Approximately fair.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 print (mid-October 2026) as the first decision point and the Q4 print (January 2027) as the test of the 3.00–3.05% NIM exit.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Nonaccrual inflows revert to $250M or below for two consecutive quarters *and* ALLL/loans stabilizes at or above 1.31% with provision covering net charge-offs — this removes the primary bear leg.
2. Commercial Bank pre-tax income grows at least 8% year over year in a quarter, demonstrating that the 10% loan growth and 11.5% expense growth trade is economic.
3. Price toward $20.00–20.50 (roughly 1.48x TBV, 9.5x FY2027 consensus) on unchanged guidance, which would restore a genuine margin of safety against the 12.9%-ROTCE fair value.
4. Disaggregation of the $13.7B Finance exposure showing predominantly investment-grade, secured fund-finance rather than warehouse or private-credit facilities.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Net charge-offs above 50bp in any quarter, or a provision that exceeds NCOs by more than $75M — the signal that the idiosyncratic characterization has failed.
2. CET1 below 10.75% or TCE/tangible assets below 7.25% without a corresponding pause in repurchases, indicating capital return is outrunning the balance sheet.
3. FY2026 revenue or NII guidance cut, or a Q4 NIM exit below 2.95%, which would break the swap-roll-off thesis and remove the highest-confidence element of the growth case.
4. A material adverse reset of the 3.20% stress capital buffer, which would compress buyback capacity quickly.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 results in mid-October, with the specific check being whether investment banking fees exceed ~$203M as guided (>20% sequential from $169M) and whether Q3 nonaccrual inflows moderate from $365M.

## Conclusion

KeyCorp has done the hard part. Net interest margin is 73bp above the FY2024 trough, ROTCE has moved from negative to 13.0%, four consecutive quarters have beaten consensus, Moody's has upgraded the credit, and the funding position (a 73% loan-to-deposit ratio with roughly $12B of loan capacity fundable from existing deposits) is a competitive luxury few peers hold. The disclosure that roughly half of the 1H NII increase came from the contractual roll-off of legacy swap drag raises rather than lowers my confidence in the guided 3.00–3.05% Q4 exit, because that component does not require a favorable Fed or a won deposit battle.

What stops this from being an ACCUMULATE is the price relative to what has actually been demonstrated. At 1.67x tangible book, the market is paying for a 15% ROTCE that today stands at 12.9%, and the segment carrying the growth (Commercial Bank, +10.3% average loans) produced identically flat net income of $423M year over year. Reserve coverage against nonperforming loans fell from 232% to 179% while nonaccrual formation hit a five-quarter high, capital return ran at 125% of earnings and drew CET1 down 61bp, and consensus estimates are drifting slightly lower rather than rising. The largest concentration after CRE — $13.7B of "Finance" exposure, up 10.2% in six months — is not disaggregated anywhere in the filings, in a period when non-depository financial lending is a focus of supervisory and investor attention.

The probability-weighted return of roughly 7.5% including the dividend is approximately fair compensation for that risk profile. Existing holders should keep the position and collect a covered 3.6% yield alongside a buyback retiring 4–5% of the share count annually. New capital has no urgency here; the setup improves materially either at $20.00–20.50, or on two quarters of evidence that the Commercial Bank converts loan growth into earnings and that the Q2 credit formation was in fact idiosyncratic.
