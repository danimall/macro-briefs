**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Graham Holdings is a diversified holding company (the former Washington Post Company) that operates a Berkshire-style decentralized model: cash flows from operating businesses (Kaplan education, seven TV stations, home health/infusion, manufacturing, auto dealerships, restaurants) are recycled into acquisitions, a large public-equity portfolio, and aggressive buybacks. The classification as "Consumer Discretionary / Education Services" is a screening artifact. GHC is functionally a capital-allocation vehicle, and its reported GAAP earnings are nearly useless for assessing operations because mark-to-market on its securities portfolio flows through the income statement (the 2024 net income of $725M and 2025's $292M are largely a function of unrealized equity gains and losses, not operating performance).

The core of the thesis is a sum-of-the-parts discount. The marketable equity portfolio (~$967M–$1.08B, anchored by Berkshire Hathaway ~$534M and Markel ~$106M, carrying ~$710M of unrealized gains) plus a roughly $2.79B prepaid pension asset (an overfunded defined-benefit plan that throws off ~$31M/quarter of non-operating income) together approach or exceed the entire $3.8B market cap, before assigning any value to Kaplan, the TV stations, or the growing healthcare segment. Management has retired roughly 10% of shares over three years (4.82M in 2022 to 4.33M in 2025) and continues buying back stock ($34.5M in Q1 2026), which mechanically compounds per-share value while the discount persists.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the largest and highest-margin segment, Kaplan, is absorbing a realized headwind from international student-visa tightening across the US, Australia, Canada, and the UK, compounded by Title IV / Purdue Global regulatory exposure and a March 2025 executive order to dismantle the Department of Education that creates uncertainty for the federal aid systems Kaplan's clients depend on. Forward EPS revisions for the next two quarters are negative (-4.4% on the next quarter over 30 days, -8.7% over 90 days), confirming that near-term operating momentum is decelerating even as the long-run SOTP case stays intact. The dual-class control structure means there is no activist forcing mechanism to close the discount, so value realization depends entirely on continued buybacks and the passage of time. The combination of a real near-term operating headwind plus a structurally uncloseable discount is what caps the rating below BUY.

## Company Overview

Graham Holdings (controlled by the Graham family through a dual-class structure where Class A elects 70% of the board and publicly traded Class B elects only 30%) is led by CEO Timothy O'Shaughnessy, son-in-law of Chairman Emeritus Donald Graham. The board is unusually high-quality for a sub-$4B company, including Markel CEO Tom Gayner and Davis Advisors' Chris Davis, a value-investing orientation that informs the capital-allocation discipline.

Six reportable segments:
- **Education (Kaplan)** — the largest segment, served ~1.16M students in 2025. Three units: Kaplan International (UK/Asia-Pacific pathways, professional, language), Kaplan Higher Education (university services anchored by Purdue Global), and Supplemental Education (test prep).
- **Television Broadcasting (Graham Media Group)** — 7 stations, ~54% advertising / balance retransmission. Even-year political cyclicality is a 2026 tailwind.
- **Healthcare** — CSI (home infusion specialty pharmacy) plus Graham Healthcare Group (home health/hospice, >90% Medicare-funded). The fastest-growing segment.
- **Manufacturing** — Hoover, Dekko, Joyce/Dayton, Forney.
- **Automotive** — 8 Ourisman-branded DC-area dealerships.
- **Other** — Clyde's Restaurant Group, Framebridge, Saatchi Art, Society6, Foreign Policy, City Cast.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** has grown steadily: $3.92B (2022) → $4.41B (2023) → $4.79B (2024) → $4.91B (2025), with Q1 2026 at $1,236M (+6% YoY). This is a genuinely growing top line, distinct from the headline-earnings volatility.

**Operating performance** is improving but structurally thin. Operating income rose from $69M (2023) to $235M (2025), but operating margin remains ~4.8%, diluted by low-margin auto dealerships, restaurants, and manufacturing. Q1 2026 operating income was +22% YoY ($57.8M), with TV broadcasting +39% (political/Olympics/Super Bowl) and manufacturing +46% (Arconic acquisition).

**Net income is not a clean read on operations.** ASU 2016-01 forces unrealized equity gains and losses through the P&L. Q1 2026 net income was depressed by a $68.9M unrealized equity loss (-$11.66/share after tax); Q1 2025 absorbed a $66.4M non-cash interest charge marking up the healthcare manager NCI. The 2024→2025 "earnings collapse" of roughly 60% was an artifact of portfolio marks while operating income rose. Any screen ranking GHC on reported net income or trailing P/E will misjudge it. The company's own primary metric, Operating Income before Amortization and Impairments, is the correct operating lens.

**Earnings surprise pattern reflects this volatility, not a clean beat streak:** Q1 2026 +26.8%, Q4 2025 -19.3%, Q3 2025 +16.3%, Q2 2025 +40.1%. Three beats and one miss across four quarters, but the magnitude swings reflect mark-to-market and the judgment-based timing of the Purdue Global fee (Kaplan recorded only part of it in Q1 2025, the full fee in Q1 2026), which can swing quarterly results independent of underlying demand.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $8.4B but segment operating assets are only ~$3.76B. The non-operating remainder:
- **Marketable equity portfolio ~$967M–$1.08B**, with ~$710M of unrealized gains.
- **Prepaid pension cost ~$2.79B** — a massively overfunded DB plan (assets ~$3.2B, ~38% in Berkshire stock plus a private fund) generating ~$31M/quarter of non-operating income and even funding acquisitions (Arconic was funded largely by assuming $107.5M of pension obligations) and workforce restructuring. This asset is largely invisible in GAAP earnings.

Debt: LT debt ~$706M, total borrowings ~$822M, D/E 0.74. November 2025 refinancing (issued $500M 5.625% notes due 2033, redeemed $400M 5.75% 2026 notes, upsized the revolver to $400M to 2030) cleared the near-term maturity wall. The Ba1/BB rating reflects the conglomerate structure, not distress, and the Altman Z of 2.44 ("grey zone") is a false negative driven by low asset turnover and the securities/pension surplus the model ignores.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** 2025 OCF $347M, FCF $275M (~7% FCF yield on market cap). Dividend $7.52/year (sub-1% yield; this is not an income story). Remaining authorization ~430,000 shares (~10% of shares outstanding).

## Growth Analysis

The operating segments are growing in aggregate, but unevenly, and the growth durability question centers on Kaplan, the highest-margin segment.

**Healthcare is the genuine growth engine:** Q1 2026 revenue +20%, CSI +31%. This offsets secular decline elsewhere but faces a 1.3% aggregate Medicare home-health cut for 2026 plus value-based purchasing pressure.

**Kaplan faces realized headwinds.** US visa office closures in 2025 and stricter vetting "materially adversely affected" US Pathways recruitment; Australia's student-visa allocation caps will hit Kaplan Business School in 2026; Canada and the UK are also tightening. UK-specific pressures (VAT on private-school fees from January 2025, Level 7 apprenticeship funding cut from January 2026 affecting ~50% of UK Professional, higher employer national insurance) compound the international-education softness. Management is pruning Kaplan toward its best units (the KLG language-schools sale closed May 1, 2026 with a $19M held-for-sale impairment), a portfolio refocus that also confirms parts of the segment are under structural pressure.

**TV broadcasting** is in secular cord-cutting decline (retransmission revenue net of rising reverse-retrans network fees fell $2.9M in Q1 2026), partly offset by 2026 political-year cyclicality.

The forward estimate picture captures the deceleration: while current-year EPS revisions are +4.2% (30d) and next-year +1.5%, the more telling near-term reads are the next-quarter revisions at -4.4% (30d) and -8.7% (90d), and current-quarter at -5.6% (90d). GHC is a steady compounder with a near-term operating soft patch concentrated in its best segment, not a stock with the revision velocity that would force a more aggressive rating. Growth Prospects accordingly score in the mid-range.

## Valuation Assessment

The standard multiples are misleading. Trailing/forward P/E of ~16.8x is computed on EPS that includes equity marks, so it overstates and understates earnings power depending on the quarter. The meaningful valuation lens is sum-of-the-parts.

Against the peer median P/E of 14.4x (LOPE 19.2x, LRN 15.7x, DUOL 12.5x, PRDO 13.1x, STRA 14.4x), GHC screens at a modest premium on headline P/E, but the peer comparison is nearly meaningless because none of these pure-play education companies carry a securities book or an overfunded pension of GHC's scale. The relevant frame is the holding-company discount.

The math: the marketable securities portfolio plus the prepaid pension asset sum to roughly $3.76B–$3.87B, approximately the entire $3.8B market cap. The buyer at today's price is effectively getting the operating businesses (Kaplan, seven TV stations, the healthcare segment, manufacturing, auto, restaurants) generating ~$235M of operating income and ~$275M of FCF for free, net of debt. Even haircutting the pension surplus heavily for the fact that it cannot be freely extracted (it can be monetized indirectly via obligation assumption and restructuring funding, as demonstrated), the discount is real.

This supports a valuation score above neutral, but not a high-conviction mispricing score, for a specific reason: conglomerate discounts of this type are persistent and self-perpetuating absent a forcing mechanism, and the dual-class structure removes the activist catalyst that closes such gaps elsewhere. The value accrues through buybacks (management buying back stock at a discount to intrinsic value is the principal discount-narrowing engine) and through time, not through a re-rating event. A specific mispricing exists; the path to its realization is slow and management-dependent. The sole covering analyst's $990 target sits below the current $1,139.83 price, reflecting that analyst's focus on the operating businesses rather than the SOTP, and should be read as a floor on the operating-business value rather than a ceiling on intrinsic value.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaplan competes in fragmented education markets and differentiates on brand, scale, university relationships (the 30-year Purdue Global TOSA is unique and difficult to replicate), and regulatory navigation. That same regulatory dependence is a double-edged moat: the OPM/university-services revenue-sharing model faces ongoing federal scrutiny, and Kaplan North America is a Title IV "Third Party Servicer" jointly and severally liable with Purdue Global to the Department of Education.

Healthcare is gaining share organically, and GHC increased CSI ownership to 93.4%. TV broadcasting is a share-stable cash cow in a declining medium. The competitive trajectory is mixed: a strengthening healthcare position and stable broadcasting cash flows against a Kaplan International franchise losing volume to visa policy outside its control. Net, competitive position is roughly stable, scoring in the mid-range, with the visa headwinds becoming realized rather than theoretical partly offsetting the otherwise improving trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in the holding-company discount are concentrated and specific:

1. **International student-visa tightening** — actively pressuring Kaplan now across four jurisdictions; the most immediate operating risk.
2. **Purdue Global / Title IV exposure** — Contributor Compensation is 13% of Purdue Global revenue (through 2027) plus an 8% service fee; KNA is jointly and severally liable to the DoE; borrower-defense-to-repayment claims (~$35M+ on formerly owned schools) remain an overhang; $72.9M receivable from Purdue Global at Q1 2026.
3. **March 2025 EO to dismantle the Department of Education** — creates uncertainty for the federal aid systems Kaplan's clients depend on.
4. **Healthcare reimbursement** — the Medicare home-health cut on a >90% Medicare-funded book, plus plasma supply exposure at CSI.
5. **Controlled-company governance** — Class B holders cannot influence control, and there is no mechanism to force discount closure.
6. **Earnings optics** — GAAP volatility from equity marks will continue to produce headline "misses" that are mechanically meaningless but can pressure the stock short-term.

Offsetting these, the balance sheet carries minimal distress risk, the securities and pension surplus provide a deep floor, and the refinancing has cleared the maturity wall. Risk Factors score below neutral because the visa/Title IV/DoE cluster is real, specific, and concentrated in the most valuable segment.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,400, +23%):** The conglomerate discount narrows as buybacks continue retiring shares at a discount to intrinsic value, healthcare growth compounds, the 2026 political cycle lifts TV broadcasting, and the visa headwinds prove cyclical rather than structural. Markel/Berkshire portfolio appreciation and the overfunded pension provide ongoing tailwinds invisible to GAAP. Management's demonstrated discipline (share retirement, portfolio pruning, opportunistic financing) continues to compound per-share value at a high-single-digit rate. At a modestly narrowed discount, fair value approaches $1,400.

**Bear Case (~$880, -22%):** Kaplan International deteriorates further as visa caps bite through 2026, the DoE restructuring disrupts federal aid flows and impairs the Purdue Global relationship, Medicare cuts pressure the healthcare engine, and a broad equity-market drawdown marks down the securities portfolio and pension assets simultaneously. The discount widens as headline earnings turn volatile, and the absence of an activist forcing mechanism means no relief. This revisits the 52-week low near $882.

**Base Case (~$1,225, +7%):** Steady operating growth (mid-single-digit revenue, healthcare offsetting Kaplan softness), continued buybacks providing ~2-3% annual per-share tailwind, and a stable-to-slightly-narrower discount. The stock grinds toward the upper end of its 52-week range, supported by the floor that the securities-plus-pension value provides.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — the thesis is positive per-share compounding via buybacks plus eventual discount narrowing, not yield.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August), with the Kaplan International visa-impact commentary and the next-quarter revision trajectory as the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Kaplan International enrollment stabilizes or visa headwinds demonstrably abate (management commentary plus segment revenue inflecting positive YoY), removing the realized operating drag.
- Forward EPS revisions turn positive for the next two quarters, confirming operating momentum re-accelerating.
- An accelerated buyback (using the existing authorization) at a discount to SOTP, or a value-crystallizing portfolio action (a segment sale at a premium that re-rates the rest), demonstrates the discount is being actively closed.
- A pullback toward the low-$900s would itself widen the SOTP margin of safety enough to upgrade.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse change in the Purdue Global relationship or a DoE liability crystallizing beyond the disclosed borrower-defense claims.
- Operating income before amortization turning negative YoY for two consecutive quarters (indicating the operating businesses, not just the marks, are deteriorating).
- A pension funding reversal (a sustained equity drawdown that erodes the surplus and forces contributions).

## Conclusion

Graham Holdings is a genuinely undervalued holding company on a sum-of-the-parts basis: the securities portfolio and overfunded pension together approximate the entire market cap before crediting Kaplan, the TV stations, or the healthcare business, and management's track record of retiring ~10% of shares in three years at a discount to intrinsic value is a credible compounding engine. The board's value-investing pedigree (Gayner, Davis) and the cleaned-up maturity profile reinforce the quality of the capital-allocation thesis.

The rating stays at HOLD rather than BUY for two concrete, falsifiable reasons. First, the largest and highest-margin segment is absorbing a realized headwind (international visa tightening across four jurisdictions plus Title IV/DoE regulatory uncertainty) and the near-term estimate trajectory confirms it. Second, the conglomerate discount that constitutes the bull case has no forcing mechanism to close given the dual-class control structure; value realization depends on buybacks and time, not a catalyst. The combination caps the asymmetry: the SOTP floor is real and deep, but the path to upside is slow and the near-term operating momentum is decelerating. A pullback toward the low-$900s, or evidence that the Kaplan visa drag is abating, would change the call. For now, hold the optionality on a high-quality compounder while the operating soft patch and the structurally uncloseable discount keep it below a buy.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the SOTP "floor ≈ market cap" framing leans heavily on the ~$2.79B prepaid pension asset, which the report itself concedes cannot be freely extracted; if a more conservative haircut is applied, the margin of safety supporting the HOLD-with-upside floor is narrower than stated.
