**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Global Net Lease is a diversified net lease REIT that has undergone a multi-year restructuring — internalizing management, selling $3.3 billion in assets, and achieving an investment-grade rating — but AFFO per share has declined every year since 2021 ($1.77 to $0.99 in FY2025 to guided $0.80-$0.84 in FY2026), and the announced $535M all-stock acquisition of Modiv Industrial introduces further dilution and execution risk before the balance sheet has fully stabilized.

The current 8.1% dividend yield is sustained at only ~108% AFFO coverage at the midpoint of FY2026 guidance, leaving no margin for error and raising the probability of another dividend cut if any operating metric deteriorates. With a 2.7-year weighted-average debt maturity requiring continuous refinancing in a 5%+ rate environment, 27% office exposure facing structural headwinds, and cumulative total shareholder return that has lagged the peer group by approximately 53 percentage points over the measurement period, the risk/reward profile remains unfavorable.

The stock has appreciated 21% over the past 12 months, reflecting recognition of the deleveraging progress. But the turnaround thesis requires that acquisitive growth (starting with Modiv) can reverse a multi-year earnings decline, a bet against structural portfolio headwinds that has not yet been validated by results.

## Company Overview

Global Net Lease owns 820 properties totaling 40.7 million square feet, 97% leased, across the U.S./Canada (74%) and Western Europe (26%). The portfolio generates rent from 231 tenants across 71 industries, with no single tenant exceeding 6% of annualized straight-line rent. The asset mix is 46% industrial/distribution, 27% retail, and 27% office. 66% of rental income comes from investment-grade rated tenants.

The company internalized its management in September 2023 via merger with The Necessity Retail REIT, then executed a massive disposition program culminating in the $1.8 billion sale of 99 multi-tenant retail properties in H1 2025. It obtained a Fitch BBB- investment-grade rating in October 2025 and now employs only 56 people to manage the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**
Revenue declined from $805M (FY2024, including discontinued operations) to $495M (FY2025) to a $109M quarterly run-rate in Q1 2026 (~$437M annualized). This contraction is intentional (dispositions), but AFFO per share has declined in parallel: $1.77 (2021) → $1.67 (2022) → $1.40 (2023) → $1.32 (2024) → $0.99 (2025) → $0.80-$0.84 (2026E).

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt has been reduced from $4.7B to $2.6B. Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDA stands at 7.2x versus a target of 6.5-6.9x. Liquidity is $911M including a $1.5B revolving credit facility (maturing 2029). 98% of debt is fixed-rate at a weighted-average 4.2%. The critical vulnerability is the 2.7-year weighted-average maturity: every refinancing in the current rate environment replaces cheaper legacy debt with more expensive new issuance.

**Dividend Coverage:**
The $0.76/share annual dividend is covered at 130% on FY2025 AFFO ($0.99). On FY2026 guided AFFO of $0.82 (midpoint), coverage drops to 108%. In FY2025, operating cash flow ($222.8M) did not fully cover total dividends paid ($235.8M including preferred). The company explicitly acknowledges it may fund dividends from non-operational sources.

**Cash Flow:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $223M with FCF of $189M (38.2% margin). This is adequate but declining with the asset base. Preferred dividends of $43.7M annually represent a senior claim on cash flow.

**Impairments:**
$157.5M impaired across 106 properties in FY2025 and $90.4M across 56 properties in FY2024 — a total of $248M over two years. This confirms that assets acquired during the RTL merger were significantly overvalued and required write-downs during the repositioning.

## Growth Analysis

GNL's growth thesis rests entirely on the pivot from disposition/deleveraging to acquisitive growth:

1. **Modiv Industrial Acquisition ($535M):** Expected to be 4% AFFO accretive, adding industrial assets with 15-year WALT and 2.4% annual rent escalations. However, it is an all-stock deal issuing approximately 23M new shares (~11% dilution), and at an implied cap rate that requires flawless execution to deliver the projected accretion.

2. **Investment-Grade Access:** The BBB- rating theoretically lowers GNL's cost of capital and enables unsecured bond issuance. With elevated long-term rates and rising rate uncertainty, the actual borrowing cost advantage is narrower than it would be in a normal rate environment.

3. **Rent Escalations:** 86% of leases contain escalators averaging 1.4% annually — below inflation and below the 2.4% available on new Modiv leases.

4. **Organic Limitations:** WALT of 6.1 years means substantial lease rollover annually. Re-leasing office space in the current environment creates downward pressure on rental rates. The pipeline of dispositions continues ($132M YTD 2026), partially offsetting any acquisition-driven growth.

The fundamental problem: AFFO/share has declined every single year despite management's best efforts. Acquisitive growth via stock issuance has historically not reversed this trend for GNL. The Modiv deal needs to succeed as the first positive data point — until it closes (Q3 2026) and delivers accretion (probably not visible until Q4 2026/Q1 2027 reporting), growth remains negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.48, GNL trades at:
- 11.6x FY2026 guided AFFO ($0.82 midpoint)
- ~11.3x annualized Q1 2026 AFFO ($0.84)
- ~0.46x book value (implied by $4.35B assets, $2.6B debt, $0.6B preferred, ~$1.15B equity on ~211M shares)
- 8.1% dividend yield

Peer comparison reveals the discount is substantial: peer median P/E of 31.8x, peer median yield of 5.4%. GNL yields 270bp more than peers and trades at roughly one-third the multiple. However, this discount has persisted because the business has consistently underperformed peers — it reflects structural risk, not mispricing.

The analyst consensus target is $10.50 (median), with a range of $8.00-$12.00. The stock is already trading within the mid-range after its 21% appreciation. BMO downgraded to Market Perform at $10 target following the Modiv announcement.

The discount to peers is partially justified by: (1) declining AFFO trajectory, (2) higher leverage, (3) shorter WALT, (4) office exposure, (5) complex corporate history with value destruction, (6) thin dividend coverage. Meaningful gap closure would require sustained AFFO growth, which has never been demonstrated under current management.

## Competitive Landscape

GNL operates in a crowded net lease REIT space dominated by much larger players. Realty Income ($60B+ market cap), W.P. Carey ($17B), and EPRT ($7B) all have lower leverage, longer WALTs, better tenant credit profiles, and access to cheaper capital.

GNL's differentiation is primarily its European portfolio (unique among U.S.-listed net lease REITs) and its higher yield. The European exposure introduces FX risk and operational complexity that peers avoid. Industrial net lease is the most competitive segment, with cap rates compressed to levels where only larger REITs with low cost of capital can acquire accretively.

The office exposure (27%) is the most acute competitive weakness. While GNL argues its office tenants are mostly government/IG-rated, the asset class itself trades at significant cap rate discounts, impairs regularly, and faces secular demand decline. The company acknowledges this by targeting office for further disposition.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Dividend Sustainability:** At 108% FY2026 AFFO coverage, one bad quarter (tenant default, unexpected vacancy, FX headwind) could push coverage below 100%. A third dividend cut would likely cause significant stock price decline.

2. **Refinancing Risk:** With $94.8M maturing in 2026 and continuous market access required, each refinancing likely increases blended interest costs unless rates decline substantially.

3. **Modiv Integration:** If AFFO accretion is less than projected (due to integration costs, tenant issues, or rate environment), the ~11% dilution without commensurate earnings growth would further compress AFFO/share.

4. **Office Disposal Losses:** Continued office dispositions in a weak market will likely generate further impairments and potentially losses on sale, reducing NAV and AFFO.

5. **Rate Environment:** Elevated long-term rates directly compress net lease REIT valuations and create a hostile backdrop for leveraged real estate equities with short debt maturities.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV has nearly doubled from 45.1% to 88.3% over three weeks while the stock rose only 3.4%. This extreme IV expansion on modest price appreciation signals that the options market is pricing a significant binary outcome — likely related to Modiv closing risk or a potential dividend cut. The put/call OI ratio of 0.29 (call-dominant) suggests some participants are positioned for upside, but the IV level implies materially elevated uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($11-12):** Modiv closes on time and delivers 4%+ AFFO accretion. AFFO stabilizes near $0.87/share. The dividend remains covered. GNL demonstrates it can grow acquisitively with its IG rating. WALT extends to 7+ years. The stock re-rates toward 13-14x AFFO as the growth narrative gains credibility. Rate cuts in H2 2026 compress cap rates and support REIT valuations.

**Bear Case ($6.50-7.50):** Modiv integration underperforms. AFFO/share continues declining. The dividend is cut. Rising rates compress REIT multiples. Office dispositions generate losses. The stock trades down to ~8x AFFO with a reduced/eliminated dividend.

**Base Case ($8.50-9.50):** Modiv closes with modest accretion. AFFO stabilizes around $0.83-$0.85. Dividend maintained but not grown. Stock oscillates in the $8.50-9.50 range, offering current yield as total return. No meaningful multiple expansion because the track record of value destruction prevents institutional accumulation.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two potential triggers: (1) Modiv deal fails to close or is renegotiated at worse terms (low probability but would reset growth narrative), (2) Q2/Q3 2026 AFFO comes in below the annualized guidance run-rate, forcing acknowledgment that the $0.80-$0.84 range is optimistic. Timeline: next earnings report (likely August 2026).

**Timing window:** 3-6 months (Modiv closing expected Q3 2026, next 1-2 earnings reports will clarify AFFO trajectory).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Essentially zero (9,393 shares short, 0.0% of float). Informed short sellers are not positioned here, which could mean either (a) the stock is too cheap to short or (b) the borrow is difficult/unattractive given the 8% dividend yield that must be paid by short sellers.

**Borrow and options signal:** The 88% ATM IV makes puts expensive. The 8.1% dividend yield creates significant carrying cost for short sellers. These mechanics make this expensive to short regardless of fundamental view.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case ~$7.00 (26% downside from $9.48). Bull case ~$11.50 (21% upside). Risk/reward for a short is approximately 1.2:1, well below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The 8% dividend yield creates prohibitive carrying costs, options are too expensive for synthetic shorts, and the zero short interest suggests no available borrow at reasonable rates. The stock may be a poor investment, but it is not a viable short candidate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Consider upgrading from AVOID if: (1) AFFO/share demonstrates positive year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters post-Modiv integration (likely not assessable until Q1 2027), (2) Net Debt/EBITDA reaches the 6.5x target, (3) Office exposure declines below 20% of rent, or (4) the stock price declines below $7.50 (creating >10% yield with comparable coverage metrics, improving risk/reward).

**Timeline:** The structural headwinds (declining AFFO, thin dividend coverage, high leverage, short debt maturities, office exposure) require 12-18 months minimum to resolve. The Modiv acquisition's accretion impact will not be measurable until Q4 2026/Q1 2027 earnings. The rate environment is unlikely to provide relief within 6 months given current Fed dynamics.

## Conclusion

GNL has executed genuine operational improvements — internalizing management, achieving investment grade, reducing leverage from extreme levels, and repurchasing shares at a discount. But AFFO per share has declined every year since 2021 with no demonstrated reversal. The dividend is barely covered at 108% on guided AFFO. The Modiv acquisition introduces further dilution and execution risk. The rate environment creates a hostile backdrop for leveraged real estate with short debt maturities.

The 8.1% yield compensates for risk rather than representing mispricing. Until GNL demonstrates actual AFFO/share growth, the stock remains a value trap. The appropriate recommendation is AVOID, with re-evaluation triggered by two consecutive quarters of positive AFFO/share momentum post-Modiv integration.
