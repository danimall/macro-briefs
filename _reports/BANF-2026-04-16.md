## Executive Summary

BancFirst Corporation is an Oklahoma-headquartered $14.8 billion-asset community bank operating 109 locations across 62 Oklahoma communities, with small Texas operations through Pegasus Bank (Dallas) and Worthington Bank (Fort Worth). The bank employs a "super community bank" model of decentralized lending decisions with centralized back-office operations that has produced consistently strong profitability: 1.70% ROA, 13.9% ROE, and a 55% efficiency ratio in FY2025. Net income grew 11.2% to $240.6 million, with diluted EPS rising 10.4% to $7.11.

The investment case turns on three competing dynamics. First, BancFirst's dominant Oklahoma franchise (7.6% deposit share, 40+ acquisitions over its history, 31% insider ownership) provides a durable competitive moat that supports premium valuation. Second, the bank's concentrated geographic exposure to Oklahoma, where economic activity is heavily influenced by energy prices, creates meaningful risk in an environment where oil above $100 could initially boost Oklahoma's economy but where broader macro deterioration (potential recession, rising credit stress) would eventually weigh on asset quality. Third, the stock trades at 15.9x forward earnings and approximately 2.0x tangible book value, a clear premium to regional bank peers at 11.9x median P/E and 3.4% median yield, which limits upside unless the growth trajectory accelerates.

The recent two-quarter pattern of earnings misses (Q3 and Q4 2025 missed by 2.5% and 4.0% respectively) after two beats earlier in the year introduces uncertainty about near-term execution. Combined with noninterest-bearing deposit migration, Durbin Amendment interchange risk from crossing $10 billion in assets, and rising OREO balances, the risk-reward at current valuation does not support a BUY despite strong fundamental quality. The 1.7% dividend yield provides modest income support while the thesis evolves.

## Company Overview

BancFirst Corporation operates primarily through BancFirst, an Oklahoma state-chartered bank that has been the state's dominant community banking franchise for decades. The company's strategy centers on providing the personalized service and local decision-making of a community bank while leveraging the scale advantages of a larger institution: lending limits, product breadth, centralized compliance, and technology infrastructure that smaller competitors cannot match.

The bank's loan portfolio totals $8.54 billion, heavily weighted toward real estate (71% of loans secured by real estate), with CRE non-owner occupied representing the largest category at 21.1%. Oil and gas lending is 6.4% of total loans, though indirect energy exposure through Oklahoma's economic dependence on the sector is substantially higher. The bank holds $4.5 billion in cash and equivalents (30.3% of assets), reflecting an extremely liquid balance sheet, and manages an additional $4.9 billion in off-balance-sheet sweep accounts.

Insiders and affiliates control approximately 35% of outstanding shares, with directors and officers holding 31%. The Rainbolt family has been the controlling force since the company's founding, creating strong alignment between management and shareholders but also concentration risk in governance.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 net interest income grew 9.8% to $490.5 million, driven by average earning asset growth of 9.9% ($1.2 billion). The net interest spread improved to 2.54% from 2.29% as deposit costs declined 50 basis points faster than asset yields, but this was partially offset by the declining contribution of noninterest-bearing deposits (from 1.44% to 1.20% of NIM). The reported NIM was essentially flat at 3.74% versus 3.73%.

Noninterest income grew 8.4% to $200.1 million, but two components carry regulatory risk: debit card interchange ($27.2 million, 13.6% of noninterest income) faces Durbin Amendment compression, and NSF/overdraft fees ($31.6 million, 15.8%) face ongoing political and regulatory pressure. Together these represent nearly 30% of fee income.

The provision for credit losses declined 36.7% to $5.7 million, reflecting management's view of stable credit conditions. Net charge-offs were just $8.5 million (0.10% of average loans). However, OREO increased to $49.1 million from $33.7 million, concentrated in two large positions: a ~$25 million commercial property (written down from $28 million) and a new $16 million construction/development foreclosure.

**Balance Sheet:** Total assets grew 9.5% to $14.84 billion, with $511 million in loan growth (6.4%), of which $244 million came from the ABOK acquisition and the remainder organic. The equity-to-assets ratio of 12.5% is well above regulatory minimums. Tangible book value per share grew 14.6% to $49.20. The allowance for credit losses covers nonaccrual loans at 170.6%.

**Capital Allocation:** The dividend payout ratio of 26.3% ($1.90 per share) is conservative. No share repurchases occurred despite 479,784 shares authorized. The ABOK acquisition for approximately $33 million was modestly sized and consistent with the long-standing Oklahoma consolidation strategy.

**Quarterly Trend:** The Q3 and Q4 2025 earnings misses (EPS of $1.85 vs. $1.90 estimate and $1.75 vs. $1.82 estimate) followed Q1 and Q2 beats. This pattern often signals that the operating environment tightened in the back half of the year or that analyst models were too optimistic on NIM trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

BancFirst's growth is driven by three mechanisms: organic loan growth, deposit gathering in Oklahoma, and periodic acquisitions of smaller community banks. FY2025 organic loan growth was approximately 3.3% (excluding ABOK), which is moderate for a community bank franchise. The ABOK acquisition added $413 million in assets, $330 million in deposits, and $243 million in loans for approximately $33 million, an accretive transaction at roughly 0.1x assets.

Forward estimates reflect modest expectations. FY2026E EPS of $7.04 represents a 1.0% decline from FY2025's $7.11, and FY2027E of $7.11 is flat. The 30-day estimate revision trend is modestly positive (current quarter +0.2%, next quarter +3.1%), but 90-day revisions for FY2027 are negative (-1.6%). DA Davidson's Gary Tenner expects mid-single digit NII growth in 2026 driven by earning asset growth, with NIM stable to slightly lower.

The growth ceiling is structural: Oklahoma is a slow-growth state with population increasing at roughly 0.5% annually. BancFirst already holds 7.6% deposit share, and further market share gains require either organic displacement of smaller competitors or continued acquisitions. The Texas operations (Pegasus and Worthington) provide optionality for growth outside Oklahoma, but they remain small relative to the core franchise.

M&A remains the primary growth lever. Tenner noted continued M&A interest focused on Oklahoma roll-ups. The August 2025 shelf registration positions the company to use stock as acquisition currency. However, acquisition-driven growth at this scale requires increasingly larger targets to move the needle.

## Valuation Assessment

BANF trades at 15.9x forward P/E (based on $7.04 FY2026E EPS) versus a regional bank peer median of 11.9x, a 34% premium. The dividend yield of 1.7% compares unfavorably to the peer median of 3.4%. On a price-to-tangible-book basis, BANF trades at approximately 2.3x ($112.10 / $49.20), which is elevated for a community bank growing earnings in low single digits.

The premium is partially justified by superior profitability metrics (1.70% ROA versus ~1.0% peer average, 55% efficiency ratio versus mid-60s for many peers) and the dominant Oklahoma franchise with its proven consolidation strategy. The insider ownership alignment and consistent track record of growing book value also warrant some premium.

However, with FY2026 EPS expected to be flat to slightly down, the premium leaves limited margin for error. Analyst consensus targets average $123.67 (range $123-$125), implying approximately 10% upside, but all three covering analysts rate the stock Hold. The narrow target range with no Buy ratings suggests sell-side consensus views the stock as fairly valued.

At a peer-average 11.9x multiple on $7.04 FY2026E EPS, BANF would trade at $83.78, implying 25% downside. At its own historical premium of 14-15x, fair value would be $98.56-$105.60. The current 15.9x multiple prices in continued execution and growth, which the recent earnings miss pattern calls into question.

## Competitive Landscape

BancFirst's competitive position within Oklahoma is strong and defensible. The "super community bank" model creates advantages in both directions: against smaller community banks, BancFirst offers larger lending limits ($100M+), broader product suites, and superior technology; against larger national banks, it provides local decision-making, relationship continuity, and community presence.

The 7.6% Oklahoma deposit share (up from 6.84% the prior year) demonstrates continued organic market share gains. The 40+ acquisition track record over decades has systematically consolidated smaller competitors. In rural and non-metropolitan markets, the competitive dynamics are especially favorable: smaller banks face succession challenges, regulatory burden, and technology investment requirements that make BancFirst an attractive acquirer.

The key competitive risk is the Texas expansion. Pegasus (Dallas) and Worthington (Fort Worth) compete against much larger, more established institutions in highly competitive metro markets. These operations are subscale relative to the Oklahoma franchise and face a fundamentally different competitive dynamic.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic Concentration:** Oklahoma exposure is the dominant risk factor. The state's economy correlates with energy prices, agricultural conditions, and federal government spending. A decline in any of these would disproportionately impact BancFirst's loan portfolio, deposit base, and fee income.

**Energy Price Sensitivity:** The current oil environment above $100 per barrel is generally positive for Oklahoma's economy in the near term, supporting employment, commercial activity, and real estate values. However, direct oil and gas loans at 6.4% of the portfolio are subject to significant volatility, and the indirect economic effects of energy price swings amplify the impact beyond direct exposure. Historical precedent shows that periods of oil price spikes followed by sharp declines (2014-2016) create significant credit stress in Oklahoma.

**Regulatory Threshold Risk:** Crossing $10 billion in assets triggers Durbin Amendment debit interchange caps (potentially reducing interchange revenue by 20-30%, or $5-8 million annually), CFPB examination authority, and Volcker Rule compliance costs. This structural earnings headwind will fully manifest over the next 12-18 months.

**NIM Pressure from Deposit Migration:** Noninterest-bearing deposits declined from approximately 36% of total deposits in 2022 to 30.8% in 2025. Each percentage point decline reduces the "free funds" contribution to NIM by approximately 4-5 basis points. If the trend continues toward 28-29%, NIM will face persistent headwind even in a stable rate environment.

**Credit Quality Deterioration Signals:** While headline metrics remain strong (0.10% net charge-offs), the $15 million increase in OREO balances driven by two concentrated problem assets, combined with the 10-K's explicit flagging of "deterioration in the market for commercial office property," suggests emerging stress in CRE.

**Inflation / Stagflation Risk:** The current macro regime of elevated inflation, higher-for-longer rates, and potential recession creates a challenging environment for community banks. Borrowers with adjustable-rate loans (64% of the portfolio) face payment pressure as rates remain elevated, while a recession would compress both loan demand and asset quality simultaneously.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 83.6% versus 27.3% historical volatility represents a 56.3 percentage point spread, an extreme reading. The term structure is in steep backwardation (83.6% near-term versus 26.3% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a specific imminent event. With Q1 2026 earnings scheduled for April 17, this likely reflects uncertainty around the earnings release. Put/call skew of 7.7% at the 1-month tenor confirms elevated put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.88 is relatively balanced but with only 73 contracts across 4 expirations, liquidity is thin and the signal should be interpreted cautiously. The elevated IV makes new long positions expensive to hedge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-135, ~16-20% upside):** Oklahoma energy economy benefits from sustained $100+ oil, driving stronger loan growth, improving CRE values, and reducing credit losses. BancFirst executes another accretive acquisition in 2026, accelerating earnings growth above current flat estimates. NIM stabilizes as deposit migration slows and the rate environment supports spread expansion. Multiple re-rates to 18-19x on improved growth visibility.

**Bear Case ($85-95, ~16-25% downside):** Oklahoma economy weakens as broader recession materializes despite elevated oil prices, with agricultural stress and federal spending cuts compounding the downturn. CRE office deterioration accelerates, requiring material provision increases. Durbin Amendment and potential NSF fee regulatory actions compress noninterest income by 10-15%. NIM compresses further as deposit migration continues and competition for funding intensifies. Multiple contracts toward peer median of 12x on $6.50-6.75 reduced EPS. A path to $85 at peer-average multiples on impaired earnings is plausible in a deep recession.

**Why Not BUY:** The specific, falsifiable concern preventing a BUY recommendation is the convergence of three quantifiable risk factors: (1) the two consecutive quarterly earnings misses represent a deteriorating execution trend not yet resolved by the upcoming Q1 2026 report; (2) the 34% P/E premium to peers provides insufficient margin of safety given flat-to-down FY2026 EPS estimates; and (3) the Durbin Amendment interchange compression will structurally reduce noninterest income by an estimated $5-8 million annually, representing 2-3% of pre-tax income. A BUY recommendation would require the Q1 2026 report to show reversal of the miss trend (beat of $1.76 estimate) AND management guidance confirming mid-single digit NII growth can offset Durbin headwinds. Until these conditions are met, the premium valuation creates asymmetric downside risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Q1 2026 earnings beat (>$1.76 EPS) with management confirming mid-single digit NII growth and stable NIM, reversing the two-quarter miss pattern
2. Stock pullback to $100-105 range (~14x forward earnings), reducing the valuation premium and providing margin of safety
3. Announcement of an accretive acquisition adding >$1 billion in assets, demonstrating continued franchise consolidation and earnings growth acceleration

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Third consecutive quarterly earnings miss, especially if driven by NIM compression below 3.50% or provision increases above $15 million
2. Nonperforming assets (nonaccrual + OREO) exceeding 2.0% of total assets, signaling material credit deterioration
3. Noninterest-bearing deposits declining below 28% of total deposits, indicating structural franchise erosion

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (April 17, 2026) is the next evaluation point. The earnings call commentary on NIM trajectory, Durbin Amendment impact quantification, and M&A pipeline will determine whether the thesis changes.

## Conclusion

BancFirst is a high-quality community bank franchise with a proven operating model, dominant Oklahoma market position, and strong insider alignment. The financial metrics are consistently above peer averages and reflect decades of disciplined management. The 1.7% dividend yield provides modest income support.

However, the 34% premium to regional bank peers requires a growth trajectory that current estimates (flat FY2026 EPS) do not support. The two consecutive quarterly earnings misses introduce execution uncertainty. Geographic concentration in Oklahoma, emerging CRE stress signals, structural Durbin Amendment headwinds, and the secular decline in noninterest-bearing deposits all represent quantifiable risks to the premium valuation. The options market's steep IV backwardation ahead of earnings further suggests elevated near-term uncertainty.

HOLD with an income orientation. The dividend is well-covered (26% payout ratio) and likely to continue growing. Revisit the thesis after Q1 2026 earnings provide clarity on NIM trajectory and Durbin impact.
