**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Mondelēz is one of the world's largest snacking companies, generating $38.5B in FY2025 net revenue across chocolate, biscuits, gum/candy, and powdered beverages, anchored by globally dominant brands (Oreo, Ritz, Cadbury, Milka, Toblerone). The investment debate is narrow and specific: the entire chocolate industry absorbed a cocoa-cost shock in 2024-2025 that added roughly $3.6B to MDLZ's FY2025 input costs, swamping $2.9B of pricing and collapsing GAAP operating income 44% to $3.55B. The honest read is the adjusted figure: adjusted EPS fell ~13% to $2.92 in FY2025 and continued falling ~10-15% in Q1 2026, with adjusted operating margin compressing to 11.7% from 14.8%.

The critical operational insight is that cocoa relief is real but lagged. Spot cocoa prices fell in Q1 2026, yet MDLZ's realized costs did not fall, because existing hedge positions and high-cost inventory carried into the period. Margin recovery is a second-half-2026-into-2027 story, not an immediate one. This timing gap is the source of both the opportunity and the risk: investors anchoring to falling cocoa futures are early, while the eventual roll-off of elevated hedges provides a mechanical path back toward mid-teens margins on a depressed earnings base.

I rate MDLZ a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale. The thesis is that adjusted EPS inflects upward as cocoa hedges normalize, lifting earnings off a trough that the current ~20x normalized multiple does not fully discount. The stock has fallen ~8% over twelve months and sits ~14% below its 52-week high while fundamentals are still deteriorating, so this is not a cheap-and-recovering value play. It is a quality franchise where the margin trough is identifiable and dated, but the recovery has not yet appeared in reported numbers. Until Q2/Q3 prints confirm margin re-expansion, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Mondelēz operates as four regional segments: Europe ($15.0B), North America ($10.7B), AMEA ($7.9B), and Latin America ($4.9B). The business is heavily international, with 75.8% of revenue generated outside the U.S. and roughly 40% from emerging markets, which serve as the structural growth engine tied to GDP, urbanization, and middle-class expansion. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. The competitive moat rests on global scale, brand equity, distribution breadth (including direct store delivery), and local commercial agility.

The portfolio was built largely through acquisition (Cadbury, Nabisco, LU, Clif Bar, Tate's Bake Shop, Evirth), which is reflected in a balance sheet where goodwill ($24.3B) plus intangibles ($19.6B) account for roughly 61% of total assets. The company is led by CEO Dirk Van de Put (also Chairman, age 65, in role since 2017), with CFO Luca Zaramella elevated to EVP, COO & CFO in February 2026.

## Financial Analysis — The Cocoa Shock Defines the Year

Revenue grew +5.8% to $38.5B (organic +4.3%), driven entirely by pricing (+8.0pp) and partially offset by volume/mix of −3.7pp as price elasticity hit demand in the U.S. and Europe. The cost mechanism: input costs rose +$3.6B (mostly cocoa, plus dairy, packaging, oils, nuts), against +$2.9B of pricing and −$0.9B of unfavorable volume/mix. Gross profit fell from $14.3B to $10.9B despite higher revenue. Adjusted operating income margin fell from 16.2% to 13.2%.

GAAP results overstate the damage in both directions. The FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.89 (−44.7%) was amplified by $1.34B of non-cash derivative mark-to-market losses and $336M of pension settlement losses from U.S. and Canadian plan buy-outs. The cleaner read is adjusted EPS of $2.92, down 12.8% (−14.6% constant currency).

Q1 2026 confirmed that pressure persists. Revenue rose to $10.08B (+8.2%), with organic +3.0% (pricing +3.5pp, volume/mix −0.5pp). GAAP EPS of $0.44 (+41.9%) is flattered by smaller year-over-year MTM losses. The real signal is adjusted EPS of $0.67, down 9.5% (−14.9% constant currency). Management explicitly stated cocoa market prices fell in Q1 2026 but realized costs did not, because of hedge positions and high-cost inventory. Regional Q1 operating income shows the strain: Europe −36.4%, North America −20.8% on soft U.S. biscuits, with AMEA resilient (volume/mix +5.8pp). Quarterly operating cash flow dropped to $467M from $1.09B on a working-capital build.

Cash generation and balance sheet: FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.51B against $1.28B capex, yielding $3.24B FCF (8.4% margin). Total debt is $21.2B against $25.8B equity (D/E 0.82), with a current ratio of 0.59. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 ("distress zone") is a model artifact, not a credit concern. It is driven mechanically by negative working capital (normal for a CPG using a $3.3B supply-chain financing program and commercial paper) and the intangible-heavy asset base depressing the EBIT-to-assets and equity-to-assets ratios. With $3.2B+ FCF, investment-grade credit, and a $4B untapped financing authorization, genuine solvency risk is minimal. I score Financial Health at 5.5: the FCF base and credit quality are solid, but margin and cash-flow erosion are still in progress, and the leverage plus intangible-impairment risk warrant the downside filter.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest dimension. Adjusted EPS is contracting, not growing, and consensus estimates reflect this: current-quarter EPS of $0.68 has been revised down 1.8% over 90 days, and next-quarter EPS of $0.82 has been cut 4.1% over the same period. Current-year consensus sits at $3.05 (essentially flat, +0.6% over 90 days) and next-year at $3.39 (unchanged). These are stabilizing, not rising, estimates; the +30% revision velocity that would flag a fundamental inflection is absent here.

The forward growth thesis is mechanical rather than demand-driven. As elevated cocoa hedges roll off and high-cost inventory clears through H2 2026 into 2027, adjusted operating margin should re-expand from the 11.7% Q1 trough toward the mid-teens. On the FY2025 revenue base, recovering ~300bp of operating margin represents meaningful operating leverage on flat-to-modest top-line growth. The earnings beat track record supports modest confidence in the floor: 3 beats and 1 in-line over the last four quarters, including an 8.1% beat in Q1 2026. But volume/mix has been negative for multiple quarters, and the elasticity risk from sustained pricing is real. Emerging-market volume strength (AMEA +5.8pp in Q1) is the brightest spot. I score Growth Prospects at 4.5: the recovery is identifiable but lagged, and near-term earnings are still falling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.86, MDLZ trades at 30.1x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.89, a meaningless figure at a trough distorted by non-cash charges. On adjusted EPS of $2.92, the multiple is ~20.8x. On current-year consensus of $3.05, it is ~20x, and on next-year $3.39, ~18x. These are reasonable multiples for a wide-moat consumer staples franchise, but not cheap relative to the depressed earnings base.

Against peers, MDLZ sits below the Packaged Foods & Meats median P/E of 33.7x, but the peer set is heterogeneous and several comps carry their own distortions: Kraft Heinz at −5.0x (loss-making), Hershey at 33.7x (also cocoa-impacted), General Mills at 8.3x with a 7.2% yield (structural concerns), Tyson at 44.0x. The dividend yield of 3.2% is below the peer median of 4.8%, and the payout was raised 6% to $2.00/share annualized, well covered by FCF. Analyst targets bracket the stock: mean $67.2, median $67.5, range $55-$75, implying ~10% upside to the mean from $60.86.

The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside today. The market is pricing in continued near-term margin pain (appropriately) but only a partial recovery. The path to a re-rating requires confirmation that margins are inflecting, which the data does not yet show. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal here, and the recovery catalyst (cocoa cost roll-off) is real but unconfirmed in reported results. I score Valuation at 4.5: fair, not mispriced, with the recovery optionality offset by the absence of a confirmed inflection.

## Competitive Landscape

MDLZ's moat is genuine and largely intact. Category-leading positions in chocolate and biscuits, global distribution scale, and brand equity (Oreo, Cadbury, Ritz, Milka) underpin pricing power that was demonstrated through the +8.0pp of FY2025 pricing the company pushed through. The cocoa shock is sector-wide: Hershey, Nestlé, Mars, and Ferrero faced the same input event, so this is a cost event, not company-specific mismanagement or share loss to a more efficient competitor.

The competitive trajectory shows some erosion at the margin. The 5-year TSR of 104.4 trails the peer group's 115.3, indicating MDLZ has underperformed packaged-food peers through this period. Volume/mix has been negative, raising the risk of trade-down to private label during sustained inflation, with U.S. biscuits and European chocolate notably soft. Structural pressures (retailer consolidation, discounter/private-label growth, e-commerce channel shift, and the well-being/GLP-1/UPF demand overhang) are real but slow-moving and broadly priced across the sector. The competitive position score declined from 8.0 to 6.5 in prior reports; I maintain 6.5, reflecting a strong but no longer expanding moat with healthy emerging-market positioning offset by developed-market volume softness.

## Risk Assessment

**Cocoa cost and hedging lag** is the dominant risk. Management expects 2026 cocoa lower than 2025 but elevated versus history, and the Q1 evidence that costs stayed high even as spot fell means the recovery timing is genuinely uncertain. The EU Deforestation Regulation adds structural supply/compliance risk.

**Price elasticity and volume erosion** raise the risk that further pricing accelerates trade-down. **Five brand intangibles carry less than 10% fair-value cushion** ($1.5B carrying value, mostly biscuit/candy brands), impairment-prone if forecasts deteriorate. **Russia** (3.7% of FY2025 revenue, still fully consolidated) carries explicit expropriation/deconsolidation/impairment risk, with profitability "above historic levels" of uncertain durability. **Geopolitics** reduced Bahrain plant capacity and disrupted shipping after the February 2026 strikes on Iran (~1.0% of revenue impacted). **Tariffs** are an evolving overhang. **The UPF/GLP-1 secular demand risk** (including the Martinez litigation) is a longer-term threat. The $1.2B ERP transformation through 2028 is an ongoing margin/cash drag.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that most of these are sector-priced or non-cash in nature, but the binary cocoa-timing and brand-impairment risks are specific and not fully discounted.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing acute stress. ATM IV of 77.4% (1-week tenor) sits dramatically above 1-year historical volatility of 22.4%, with the term structure in steep backwardation (near-term 77.4% vs. 3-month 23.4%): the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, likely the next earnings print. The put/call skew of 7.6% indicates elevated put demand, consistent with downside hedging. ATM IV jumped from 22.6% to 43.6% since the prior snapshot even as price fell only 2.8%, signaling rising uncertainty. The 1-week implied move is modest at ±1.7%, so the spike appears event-anchored rather than reflecting an expectation of a large immediate gap. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (4.6 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish position. The signal reinforces caution on near-term timing rather than contradicting the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($72-75):** Cocoa hedges roll off faster than expected and high-cost inventory clears through H2 2026, driving adjusted operating margin back toward 15-16% in 2027. Adjusted EPS recovers toward $3.40+ and the market re-rates toward 21-22x on a clean earnings base. Emerging-market volume growth (AMEA) accelerates, pricing sticks without further volume loss, and the buyback resumes from the $6.7B remaining authorization. The 3%+ yield and brand moat provide downside support while the margin recovery plays out.

**Bear case ($50-53):** Cocoa stays structurally elevated (EUDR supply constraints, weather), and elasticity forces further volume declines as the company is unable to take more pricing. Margin recovery slips into 2027 and beyond. A brand intangible impairment (the five sub-10%-cushion brands, $1.5B) produces a non-cash charge, and Russia deconsolidation crystallizes. Adjusted EPS stalls below $3.00, and the multiple compresses toward 17x on a trough base, implying ~13-18% downside.

**Base case ($65-67):** Cocoa relief flows through gradually beginning in H2 2026, with adjusted margins re-expanding modestly into 2027. Adjusted EPS recovers toward $3.20-3.40, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the analyst mean as the inflection becomes visible. The dividend continues to grow, covered by FCF.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-to-18-month thesis tied to the cocoa cost roll-off and margin normalization.

- **Base case target: $66.** ~20x next-year consensus of $3.39, modest re-rating as margin recovery becomes visible. Represents ~8% upside from $60.86 plus a 3.2% yield (~11% total return).
- **Bull case target: $73.** ~21.5x a recovered adjusted EPS of ~$3.40, ~20% upside.
- **Bear case target: $51.** ~17x a stalled ~$3.00 EPS, ~16% downside.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the key confirmation points being the Q2 2026 print (late July) and Q3 2026 (late October), where adjusted operating margin should begin re-expanding off the 11.7% trough.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) adjusted operating margin expands sequentially for two consecutive quarters back above 13%; (2) consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive by +5% or more on confirmed cocoa relief; (3) buyback resumes meaningfully, signaling management confidence the trough has passed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) adjusted EPS declines accelerate beyond the −15% constant-currency rate into H2 2026; (2) a brand intangible impairment or Russia deconsolidation charge materializes; (3) volume/mix erosion worsens beyond −3pp, signaling structural demand loss rather than transitory elasticity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) is the next natural checkpoint and the catalyst the options market is pricing.

## Conclusion

Mondelēz is a high-quality, wide-moat snacking franchise enduring a sector-wide cocoa shock that has roughly halved GAAP earnings and continued to pressure adjusted earnings into Q1 2026. The trajectory in prior reports has been declining (6.3 in February to 5.6 in May), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend: adjusted EPS is still falling, estimates are stabilizing rather than rising, and the margin recovery has not yet appeared in reported numbers. What separates this from an AVOID is the specific, dated growth catalyst: cocoa spot prices have already fallen, and the mechanical roll-off of elevated hedges and high-cost inventory provides a clear path back toward mid-teens operating margins through H2 2026 and 2027, lifting adjusted EPS off a depressed base. Management is acting rationally through the trough, halting buybacks to conserve cash while protecting a well-covered dividend, and the compensation structure genuinely shares the downside (CEO realized pay went negative in FY2024).

The risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling at $60.86. The stock is fairly valued at ~20x normalized adjusted EPS with ~8-11% base-case total return, meaningful bull-case optionality if cocoa relief accelerates, and bounded downside given the brand moat and yield support. I rate MDLZ a HOLD (growth_hold), with the path to a BUY upgrade running through confirmation that adjusted operating margin is inflecting upward in the next two quarters.
