**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Century Communities is a top-10 U.S. homebuilder concentrated in the affordable/entry-level segment, operating across 16 states with a near-total speculative-build (~98% spec) model. That positioning, which delivered outsized operating leverage on the way up, is now working in reverse. Q1 2026 home sales revenue fell 16.9% to $734.1M, net income dropped 38% to $24.4M ($0.84 diluted EPS), and homebuilding gross margin compressed to 17.8% from 19.9%, driven directly by mortgage rate buydowns and price/closing-cost concessions needed to move inventory that is built before buyers are secured. Deliveries fell 11.9%, average selling price fell 5.7% (incentive-driven), and backlog declined 15.9% in dollar value. This is a sector-wide affordability problem amplified by Century's exposure to the most rate-sensitive cohort.

The forward picture is deteriorating in the data that matters most. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut hard: current-quarter -64.6% over 90 days, next-quarter -54.8%, and current-year -17.2%. A specific, legislated headwind compounds this: the Section 45L energy-efficient home credit is disallowed for homes delivered after June 30, 2026, which will raise Century's effective tax rate and pressure net income through 2H'26 and 2027. Net homebuilding debt/net capital jumped roughly 460 bps in a single quarter (25.9% to 30.5%) as the revolver was drawn from $51.5M to $203.7M, partly funding $40M of buybacks and a 10% dividend increase during a quarter of -$50.3M operating cash flow.

The stock trades at ~13.5x trailing earnings and ~15.6x falling forward estimates, with a 2.1% dividend yield. On trailing numbers it looks cheap, but trailing earnings reflect a fading peak, not a trough. With estimates falling, no demand catalyst visible in the entry-level cohort, and a higher 2H tax rate already legislated, the cheapness is a warning rather than an opportunity. We rate CCS AVOID, consistent with the prior two reports, with re-entry contingent on absorption stabilization and a halt in estimate downgrades.

## Company Overview

Century Communities builds homes priced predominantly below FHA mortgage limits (~96% of Q1'26 deliveries) under a no-frills, internet/retail-studio brand strategy. The company runs five homebuilding segments — West (CA, WA), Mountain (AZ, CO, NV, UT), Texas, Southeast (FL, GA, NC, SC, TN), and Century Complete (entry-level across 10 states) — plus a Financial Services arm (Inspire Home Loans, Parkway Title, IHL Insurance/Escrow) and a small Colorado multifamily rental unit, Century Living.

The defining operational trait is the spec-build model: roughly 98% of Q1'26 homes were built as move-in-ready inventory rather than build-to-order. This enables fast cycle times (3-4 months) and subcontractor pricing leverage when demand is firm, but it forces the company to sell what it has already built, which limits pricing flexibility and concentrates inventory/margin risk when demand softens. The captive Financial Services unit (80% mortgage capture rate of Century buyers) provides a counter-cyclical earnings buffer and supports the rate-buydown strategy.

The company is founder-controlled. Brothers Dale Francescon (Executive Chairman) and Robert Francescon (CEO/President) serve as Co-Principal Executive Officers, an unusual dual-leadership structure that concentrates decision-making and carries explicit key-person and succession considerations. The planned move upmarket is a strategic departure: "The Village at Landmark" in Greenwood Village, CO will offer 90 luxury homes priced $1.7M-$3.0M, with sales beginning in 2027. This is immaterial to near-term results but represents a meaningful change from the pure-play affordable model.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Full-year 2025 net income fell to $147.6M from $333.8M in 2024, a ~56% decline. The company-selected adjusted pre-tax income measure dropped to $263.2M from $492.0M. Q1 2026 continued the slide: net income of $24.4M, on total revenue of $789.7M (-12.6%).

**Margin and volume.** The central problem is margin. Adjusted homebuilding gross margin was 19.7% vs 21.6%, with the decline attributable to incentives — rate buydowns and price/closing-cost concessions. Deliveries fell to 2,013 units, ASP to $364.7K, and absorption slowed to 2.6 homes/month from 2.8. SG&A as a percent of home sales revenue rose 210 bps to 15.8% as the partly fixed cost base deleveraged on lower revenue. Net new contracts fell 11.6% to 2,379, and backlog declined to 1,155 homes ($438.5M), down 8.2% in units — a leading indicator pointing to continued revenue pressure.

**Segment divergence.** The pain is concentrated in higher-ASP Western and Mountain markets. Q1'26 pretax income fell 62.6% in Mountain, 72.9% in Texas, and 53.5% in West. Southeast grew 36.8%, but that was aided by a $32.5M one-time Georgia land sale, not underlying operating strength. Century Complete was roughly flat (-1.8%). Financial Services pretax income tripled to $7.6M on favorable MSR/derivative fair-value marks — a non-cash swing that flattered headline results and should not be extrapolated.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $4.51B with inventory of $3.53B (homes under construction rose to $1.38B from $1.21B). Total debt is $1.53B, split between $500M senior notes at 3.875% due 2029 (trading below par at ~$461M, a genuine low-cost legacy asset) and $500M at 6.625% due 2033. The company is in compliance with all covenants and carries ~$886M of total liquidity including revolver capacity. Cash fell to $78.2M from $109.4M.

**The leverage and cash flow tension.** Homebuilding debt/capital rose to 32.2% from 29.1%. Operating cash flow was -$50.3M (vs -$36.6M prior year) on a $164.7M inventory build. Q1 negative operating cash flow is seasonally normal for homebuilders, but the combination matters: the company is building inventory into softening demand, repurchasing stock, and raising the dividend, with capital returns partly debt-funded during a negative-cash-flow quarter. In a sustained downturn this combination warrants monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here on a 12-month horizon, and the forward indicators are uniformly negative. Estimate revisions are the clearest signal: current-quarter EPS has been revised from $1.78 (90 days ago) to $0.63 — a 64.6% cut, with a 35% cut in just the last 30 days, indicating accelerating downgrades. Next-quarter estimates are down 54.8% over 90 days, and current-year consensus is down to $3.84. Estimates are collapsing toward reality, not recovering from a trough.

The volume and order trends confirm the deterioration. Management is managing this prudently — owned-plus-controlled lots were cut 25.9% year-over-year (79,014 to 58,537), with a rebalancing toward optioned/controlled lots (41.3% controlled) to reduce capital at risk. This is sensible counter-cyclical discipline, but it is a defensive posture, not a growth lever; it reduces future delivery capacity to match weaker demand.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and does not support confidence in a near-term rebound. The company beat by 68.4% in the March 2026 quarter and 61.7% in September 2025, but missed by 9.0% in December 2025 — the pattern is volatile rather than reliably improving, and the large beats partly reflect low estimate bars and one-time items (land sales, fair-value marks) rather than core operating strength. Management's bull case rests on U.S. housing undersupply and demographics supporting affordable-home demand, which are real over a multi-year horizon but do nothing to offset the rate-driven affordability squeeze in the next 12 months.

## Valuation Assessment

CCS trades at $59.89 (with the analyst snapshot referencing $63.25), ~13.5x trailing EPS of $4.86 and ~15.6x forward consensus of ~$3.84. Against the homebuilding peer set, this is a premium to the group: peer median P/E is 10.7, with LGIH at 18.2, GRBK at 10.7, DFH at 8.7, KBH at 10.4, and MHO at 10.7. Century's higher multiple is hard to justify given it is among the more cyclical, spec-heavy, and rate-sensitive names in the cohort, and given its 3-year TSR has lagged peers (indexed 145 vs 184).

The temptation is to treat the trailing multiple as cheap. The valuation discipline here cuts the other way. Trailing earnings reflect a fading peak, and the run-rate is still falling. The trailing P/E understates the true forward multiple precisely because earnings are declining, not troughing. On forward estimates that are themselves being cut, the multiple expands rather than contracts, and the legislated 45L tax-credit expiration raises the effective tax rate on post-June 2026 deliveries, mechanically pressuring net income in 2H'26 and 2027. A low headline multiple paired with falling estimates and a known earnings headwind is the classic value-trap profile.

For the valuation to offer asymmetric upside, there would need to be an identifiable catalyst the market is mispricing — a mortgage-rate decline easing affordability, an absorption-rate inflection, or estimate stabilization. None is present. The 3.875% 2029 notes are a genuine cost-of-capital advantage versus refinancing peers, and the balance sheet supports the dividend, but neither is a re-rating catalyst. Analyst targets span $45 to $92 with a $67 mean and $64 median — barely above the current price and not independent confirmation of upside given the falling estimate base underneath them. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is not stretched in absolute terms, but the combination of a peer-premium multiple, falling forward estimates, and a legislated earnings headwind offers no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The entire homebuilding sector is contending with elevated mortgage rates and affordability-driven demand softness. Century's incentive-heavy margin compression is largely a sector phenomenon — D.R. Horton, Lennar, and KB Home are deploying similar rate-buydown strategies. Where Century differs is in how acutely it feels the cycle. Its concentration in entry-level/affordable buyers — the most rate-sensitive cohort — and its near-total spec-build model mean demand swings hit revenue and margin faster and harder than at move-up-focused builders.

On trajectory, Century's competitive position is stable-to-deteriorating rather than improving. It is not visibly losing market share to peers, but its segment-level results and 3-year TSR underperformance indicate it is on the wrong end of the quality spectrum within homebuilding during a downturn. The spec model's cycle-time and subcontractor-leverage advantages are real in firm markets but become liabilities when buyers retreat, as they must sell inventory at concession. The lot-pipeline reduction is prudent risk management but also reflects a smaller forward footprint. The luxury Village at Landmark pivot is a strategic question mark — it could diversify revenue or dilute the operational focus that underpins the affordable model, but it is immaterial until 2027. We score competitive position 4.5: the trajectory is downward within a sector that is itself under pressure, and Century's structural exposure amplifies rather than buffers the cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin compression from incentive dependence (central risk).** Gross margins are eroding because of rate buydowns and concessions needed to move spec inventory. With ~98% spec building, Century must sell what it has already built, limiting pricing flexibility if demand weakens further. This is the dominant near-term driver and it is actively deteriorating, not stabilizing.

**45L tax-credit cliff (specific, dated).** The July 2025 reconciliation legislation disallows Section 45L energy-efficient home credits for homes delivered after June 30, 2026. This credit contributed a ~1.7% net favorable item within the 26.3% 2026 effective tax rate. Post-June 2026 deliveries lose it, raising the effective tax rate and pressuring reported net income in 2H'26 and 2027. This is a legislated, datable headwind that most screens will miss.

**Rising leverage in a downturn.** The revolver was drawn 4x higher in a single quarter. The company is building inventory, buying back stock, and raising the dividend simultaneously with negative operating cash flow. Q1 negative cash flow is seasonally normal, but the combination bears watching if the downturn extends.

**Affordability/rate sensitivity and tariff input-cost inflation.** Century's entry-level buyer pool is the most rate-sensitive. The one risk factor modified since the 10-K was the inflation factor, expanded to address tariffs on imported homebuilding products (cabinets, lumber, wood products effective late 2025) and Middle East-related fuel/transport costs, against a 3.3% March 2026 inflation reading. No significant cost increases realized yet, but a forward threat to already-thin margins.

We score risk factors 4.5. The 45L cliff and accelerating estimate cuts are specific catalysts for downside not fully reflected in a trailing multiple that screens as cheap. These are partly offset by the solid balance sheet, covenant compliance, and the low-cost legacy debt, which prevent the risk score from falling lower.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and provides no strong directional confirmation either way. Near-term ATM IV is 43.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 42.0% — essentially fair, with a 1.7% spread, and the IV percentile is in the 9th percentile of the current chain, indicating no elevated near-term event premium. Put/call skew is -1.6% (balanced), and the term structure is in contango (43.8% near-term vs 49.3% three-month), a normal structure that suggests the market is not pricing imminent stress. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±7%. Short interest is moderate at 8.8% of float with 5.3 days to cover — not a crowded short, and not an elevated squeeze risk. The options market is not flagging a binary catalyst, consistent with a slow-grind fundamental deterioration rather than an acute event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Mortgage rates ease materially in 2H'26, restoring affordability for the entry-level cohort. Absorption rates re-accelerate, incentive intensity falls, and gross margins recover toward 20%+. Century's spec model captures the recovery faster than build-to-order peers, the structural housing undersupply provides a demand floor, and the low-cost 2029 notes preserve a financing advantage. The dividend is well covered and buybacks reduce share count at sub-$65 prices. In this scenario, earnings stabilize near $4.50-5.00 and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range, roughly $80-92.

**Bear Case.** Rates stay higher for longer, the entry-level buyer pool keeps shrinking, and incentives remain elevated, compressing gross margin toward 16-17%. The 45L credit expiration raises the 2H'26 and 2027 effective tax rate, and estimate cuts continue. Leverage keeps climbing as inventory builds against soft demand, eventually forcing a pullback in buybacks or the dividend. Earnings drift toward the ~$120M / ~$4.10 bear estimate or lower, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median of 10.7x on forward earnings. In this scenario the stock falls toward the low end of the analyst range, roughly $45-48, near its 52-week low.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric to modestly negative from the current price, with the weight of current evidence — falling estimates, eroding margins, a legislated tax headwind, and rising leverage — favoring the bear path over the next two to three quarters.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The 45L tax-credit expiration raises the effective tax rate on post-mid-year deliveries and will be visible in Q3'26 and Q4'26 results. Continued estimate cuts suggest the next two earnings reports (late July 2026 for Q2, late October for Q3) carry downside-revision risk. None of these is a sharp, single-event catalyst — they are a slow grind of margin compression and tax-rate normalization, not a debt wall or a binary regulatory ruling.

**Timing window.** The fundamental deterioration plays out over the next two to four quarters (Q2-Q4 2026). That is a long carry window for a thesis without an acute trigger.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is uncrowded enough to add a position without extreme squeeze risk, but not low enough to suggest the bearish view is contrarian. Moderate.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are neutral, not confirming a bearish thesis: near-term IV is fair, skew is balanced, and the term structure is in contango. The absence of put-side urgency is a warning sign for shorts — the market is not pricing acute downside.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case target is roughly $45-48 (~20-25% downside from $59.89). Upside risk is meaningful: a rate-driven affordability relief, a single large land-sale or fair-value-mark beat (as in Southeast and Financial Services this quarter), or an acquisition could squeeze the stock toward $80+. The reward:risk is below 2:1 once carry, the dividend yield paid to the lender, and the rate-relief upside risk are accounted for.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental view is sound, but this is a cyclically weak business in a soft environment, not an actively collapsing one. There is no acute catalyst, the balance sheet is solid with covenant compliance, the options market is not confirming downside, and rate-relief upside risk is real. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a 6-12 month horizon.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the name constructively on any of the following: (1) estimate revisions stabilize — a halt to the current-quarter/current-year downgrade trend, with at least one quarter of flat-to-rising forward consensus; (2) absorption rate inflects back above 2.8 homes/month and backlog value stops declining year-over-year, signaling genuine demand recovery in the entry-level cohort; (3) gross margin stabilizes or expands above 19% on an adjusted basis, indicating incentive intensity has peaked; or (4) the stock compresses toward the peer-median forward multiple (~10-11x on credible forward earnings) while fundamentals hold, providing a real margin of safety.

**Timeline of headwinds.** The affordability/rate squeeze persists as long as mortgage rates stay elevated; a sustained decline in the 10-year and mortgage rates is the single most important macro variable. The 45L tax-credit headwind is permanent from mid-2026 onward. We expect the cyclical pressure to persist through at least 2H'26, with the next reassessment points being the Q2'26 earnings release (late July 2026) and Q3'26 (late October 2026), which will reveal whether absorption and margins are stabilizing or deteriorating further.

## Conclusion

Century Communities is a soundly financed, well-run homebuilder caught in the wrong part of the cycle with the wrong structural exposure for current conditions. Its affordable/entry-level focus and ~98% spec-build model mean it feels the rate-driven affordability squeeze more acutely than its peers, and the data confirm it: Q1'26 net income -38%, home sales revenue -16.9%, gross margin down 210 bps on incentives, backlog value -15.9%, and forward estimates cut 17-65% across the curve. The 45L tax-credit expiration adds a legislated earnings headwind from mid-2026, and leverage is rising while the company funds buybacks and a dividend increase through negative operating cash flow.

The stock screens cheap on trailing earnings (~13.5x), but trailing earnings reflect a fading peak, and on falling forward estimates the multiple is a peer premium rather than a discount. Cheapness without a demand catalyst in the most rate-sensitive buyer cohort is a value trap, not an entry point. The balance sheet, the low-cost 2029 notes, and the covered dividend keep this from being a short — there is no acute catalyst and rate-relief upside risk is real. We rate CCS AVOID, consistent with the prior two reports, and would reassess only on estimate stabilization, an absorption-rate inflection, or a compression to the peer-median forward multiple with fundamentals intact.
