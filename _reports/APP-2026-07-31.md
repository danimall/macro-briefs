**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AppLovin has become one of the most profitable software businesses of scale in the public market and simultaneously one of the least resolvable. Following the June 30, 2025 divestiture of its mobile game studios to Tripledot, the company operates as a single-segment advertising technology platform built around Axon Ads Manager. FY2025 continuing-operations revenue was $5,480.7M (+70.0%), operating income $4,151.9M (75.8% margin), diluted EPS from continuing operations $10.04, and free cash flow $3,952.0M. Q1 2026 extended the pattern: revenue $1,842.4M (+59.0%), a 78.2% operating margin, $3.56 diluted EPS, and $1,286.7M of free cash flow on essentially no capital expenditure. Net debt at March 31, 2026 was approximately $755M against roughly $6.2B of annualized adjusted EBITDA, with no bond maturities before 2029.

Against that, the stock is down roughly 46% from its September 29, 2025 high of $745.61 and trades at $403.87 (July 30 close; the options and analyst snapshots reference $395.90). The market is discounting something specific, and it is identifiable. AppLovin's two disclosed operating metrics have diverged violently: installations grew 50% in FY2024, 3% in FY2025, and **declined 18%** in Q1 2026, while net revenue per installation rose 22%, 72%, and 93% across the same periods. Reported revenue growth decelerated 75% → 70% → 59%. Either web e-commerce advertisers (onboarded since 2024, generating revenue but no app installs) are mechanically distorting a now-obsolete KPI — the benign reading — or core mobile-gaming volume is contracting and all growth is coming from price. The filings disclose no gaming-versus-e-commerce revenue split, so the two cannot be separated from primary sources. A July 13, 2026 third-party analysis reported to indicate slowing e-commerce ad growth took 11% off the shares in a single session; that claim is externally sourced and unconfirmed by any company disclosure.

The valuation now embeds meaningful skepticism. At $403.87 on a full ~336M share count (Class A plus Class B), market capitalization is approximately $135.7B, which puts the stock at 25.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $16.03 and 19.1x FY2027 consensus of $21.16. Consensus estimates have been stable-to-slightly-higher over 90 days (+1.4% current year, +2.1% next year) through the entire drawdown, and the company has beaten EPS in four consecutive quarters. That combination — a 46% derating with rising estimates — is the bull case in one sentence. We are not upgrading yet, for two reasons that the calibration of our own process weights heavily: management's forward-looking behavior is poor (broad-based, one-directional insider selling at $470-600 with no observed open-market purchases into the decline; 2025 repurchases struck at an average ~$398.8 and Q1 2026 repurchases at ~$406-561, all at or above today's price; a $50M director-affiliated venture investment written to zero), and the single unresolved question resolves within weeks at the Q2 print, for which the options market is pricing a ±16% move at the 13-day tenor. **HOLD**, with a defined upgrade path.

## Company Overview

AppLovin sells advertising software. Axon Ads Manager, the auction and bid-optimization engine, "comprises the vast majority of revenue" per the FY2025 10-K. MAX provides publisher-side mediation and in-app bidding for a percentage of client spend. Adjust (acquired 2021) sells mobile measurement and attribution on annual subscription. Wurl (2022) distributes CTV content and advertising on usage/CPM terms; the 10-K describes Wurl's markets as "nascent and may not develop as we expect."

One accounting fact governs every comparison. AppLovin states it "does not control the advertising inventory," is "not primarily responsible for fulfillment," and is therefore "an agent in these arrangements and presents revenue **net of advertising inventory costs**." The $5.48B FY2025 revenue figure is a net take after publisher payments, not gross advertiser billings, which the company does not disclose. Reported margins are therefore structurally higher than gross-revenue adtech peers, and "net revenue per installation" measures AppLovin's retained economics per install rather than what an advertiser pays. Comparisons to FY2024 as originally reported (~$4.71B including Apps) are invalid; the recast continuing-operations FY2024 base is $3,224.1M.

Customer concentration is genuinely low — Note 2 states no individual customer represented 10% or more of total revenue in 2025, 2024, or 2023, and the same for receivables. Ecosystem concentration is high: Item 1A says the advertising solutions "primarily operate in the mobile app ecosystem and specifically mobile gaming." Geographic mix is balanced, at $2,827.2M U.S. (51.6%) and $2,653.5M rest-of-world.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (continuing operations, $M):**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,841.8 | 3,224.1 | 5,480.7 | +70.0% |
| Total costs & expenses | 1,069.4 | 1,313.1 | 1,328.8 | +1.2% |
| Operating income | 772.4 | 1,911.0 | 4,151.9 | +117.3% |
| Net income | 457.8 | 1,589.5 | 3,433.2 | +116.0% |
| Diluted EPS | $1.26 | $4.56 | $10.04 | +120% |
| Adj. EBITDA | 1,236.3 | 2,411.8 | 4,512.5 | +87.1% |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 67.1% | 74.8% | 82.3% | +750bp |

Revenue rose $2,256.7M while total costs rose $15.7M, so 99.3% of incremental revenue converted to operating income. That figure requires decomposition rather than extrapolation. Stock-based compensation fell $149.4M (from $357.4M to $208.0M) because the March 2023 performance-share mega-grants — 6,902,000 PSUs each to CEO Adam Foroughi and CTO Basil Shikin, vesting on $36-$79 stock-price hurdles — had fully vested by December 31, 2024. R&D fell $148.2M, which MD&A attributes "primarily to a decrease of $151.1 million in personnel-related expenses related to a decrease in stock-based compensation related payroll costs"; S&M fell $49.2M on the same driver plus lower headcount. Roughly $347M of the $2,241M operating-income increase came from absolute cost reduction driven largely by a non-repeatable grant cycle.

The offsetting evidence is real: datacenter costs, the true variable input, grew 38% ($392.5M → $542.7M) against 70% revenue growth, and 32.5% in Q1 2026 ($122.4M → $162.2M) against 59% revenue growth. That is genuine, repeatable compute leverage independent of the SBC effect. The forward direction of SBC, however, is up: $489.0M of unrecognized compensation at year-end over a 1.95-year weighted average, plus $410.5M of grant-date fair value from the October 2025 PSUs expensed over the derived service period regardless of whether the market-cap conditions are met. R&D at 4% of revenue, down from 18% in 2023, is unusually thin for a company whose Item 1A lists "the timing and efficacy of improvements to our algorithms, models and Axon AI" as a primary results driver.

**Cash flow and quality of earnings.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,971.1M against $3,334M of net income (1.19x conversion), aided by add-backs for a $188.9M goodwill impairment (recorded against the *Apps* reporting unit within discontinued operations, not the advertising business), a $50.0M investment impairment, $210.4M of SBC and $194.8M of D&A, partially offset by a $542.2M accounts-receivable build. Free cash flow of $3,952.0M reflects near-zero PP&E purchases ($0.5M) because servers arrive through finance leases ($212.2M of ROU assets) and compute is rented. Management notes FCF "reflects cash flows from both continuing and discontinued operations," so the headline 72% FCF margin against continuing-ops revenue is modestly overstated as a continuing-business ratio.

The tax profile deserves attention. The FY2025 provision was $519.7M (13.1% effective) but cash income taxes paid net were only $194.8M, of which **federal was $0** against $2,210.6M of U.S. pretax income, with $178.0M paid in Singapore. Singapore incentives reduced the provision by $272.1M ($0.80 per diluted share) and are "expected to expire in June 2028," renewable at the company's election. Pillar 2 already clawed back $82.7M. The effective rate moved 1.4% (2024) → 13.1% (2025). Partially offsetting, the 10-K discloses the OECD's January 5, 2026 "side-by-side" elective safe harbor exempting U.S.-parented multinationals from certain global-minimum-tax provisions for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Consensus EPS of $21.16 for FY2027 embeds an assumption about this path that is not knowable from disclosure.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $2,487.1M at year-end ($2,759M at March 31, 2026) against $3,550.0M of senior unsecured notes in four tranches (5.125% 2029s, 5.375% 2031s, 5.500% 2034s, 5.950% 2054s), plus a $1.0B undrawn revolver to December 2029. Net debt of approximately $755M at March 31, 2026 is 0.12x annualized Q1 adjusted EBITDA; on FY2025 actuals, 0.24x. Interest coverage is 20.1x. The revolver covenant caps total net debt/EBITDA at 3.50x. Current ratio 3.32x.

Book equity of $2,134.7M against $1,540.0M of goodwill and $396.7M of intangibles leaves roughly $198M of tangible equity, and debt/equity of 1.65x. This is the arithmetic residue of cumulative buybacks retired to retained earnings and $2.68B of withholding-tax payments on equity settlements across 2023-2025, not operating losses. ROE of roughly 213% is a meaningless artifact of that; ROA of 52.3% is the cleaner read. Accounts receivable of $1,819.4M rose 41.8% against 70% revenue growth — an improvement, and not a true DSO figure since receivables are gross advertiser invoices while revenue is net of publisher costs. Stated payment terms are 30 days after month-end.

There is also a share-count issue that changes every multiple. The supplied $120.4B market capitalization implies roughly 305.7M shares at ~$394, matching the Class A count alone and excluding roughly 30.2M Class B shares whose economic rights are identical. On approximately 336M total shares at $403.87, market capitalization is approximately $135.7B and enterprise value approximately $136.5B — about 10% above the screened figure. All multiples below use the full count.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth remains exceptional and is decelerating in an orderly fashion: +75% (FY2024), +70% (FY2025), +59% (Q1 2026). Consensus for FY2026 EPS is $16.03 (+60% on FY2025's $10.04) and FY2027 $21.16 (+32%). Estimate revisions over 90 days are +1.9% for the current quarter, +1.4% for the current year and +2.1% for next year — stable and mildly positive, notably through a period in which the stock fell more than 40%. The company has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (+3.6%, +9.5%, +1.0%, +16.6%), which supports reasonable confidence in near-term consensus.

The composition problem is the reason we are not upgrading on those numbers. Assembling the two disclosed KPIs:

| Period | Installations | Net revenue per installation | Reported revenue growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | +50% | +22% | +75% |
| FY2025 | +3% | +72% | +70% |
| Q1 2026 | **−18%** | **+93%** | +59% |

The KPIs do not multiply to reported growth (1.03 × 1.72 = +77% vs +70% reported for FY2025; 0.82 × 1.93 = +58% vs +59% for Q1 2026), because they describe Axon Ads Manager specifically while reported revenue also includes MAX, Adjust and Wurl, and the percentages are rounded. Directionally, though, the signal is consistent across three periods: unit volume has gone from the primary growth driver to a drag, and all growth now comes from monetization per unit.

Four explanations compete. The most likely primary driver is metric obsolescence from mix shift — web e-commerce advertisers, onboarded since 2024 per Item 1A, generate revenue but no app installations, inflating the ratio mechanically while deflating the denominator. Under that reading the divergence is evidence of successful diversification and the KPI has stopped measuring the business. The alternatives are genuine take-rate expansion (advertisers accepting higher cost per install because return on ad spend improved), deliberate curation of low-value inventory, and core mobile-gaming volume contraction. No filing discloses a gaming/e-commerce split, so these cannot be discriminated from primary sources. Item 1A's own language is not reassuring: "we expect that our revenue growth rate may decline in the future… including because of more difficult comparisons to prior periods and the saturation of the market."

Two dated third-party data points bracket the question. Morgan Stanley turned more constructive after Q1, reportedly citing e-commerce advertising accelerating faster than expected (May 8). A Bank of America analysis on July 13 was reported by Barron's and Benzinga as indicating slowing e-commerce ad growth, and the shares fell about 11% in a fifth consecutive down session. Neither is verifiable from company disclosure. Item 1A anticipates exactly this mechanism as a price risk: "third-party data published about us or other advertising companies, whether or not such data accurately reflects circumstances."

One structural tailwind is underappreciated. In October 2025 Google retired the Privacy Sandbox initiative and announced plans to discontinue most associated technologies across Chrome and Android, removing a multi-year Android signal-restriction overhang for Axon's targeting data. Apple's ATT framework has, per the company, "not had a significant impact on our overall business."

The macro overlay is a caution. E-commerce performance advertising is levered to discretionary consumption, so if AppLovin's growth engine is increasingly e-commerce take-rate, its cyclicality is rising even as its reported margins improve.

## Valuation Assessment

At $403.87 on ~336M shares:

| Metric | On FY2025 actuals | On Q1 2026 annualized (sensitivity) |
|---|---|---|
| EV/Revenue | 24.9x | 18.5x |
| EV/Adj. EBITDA | 30.3x | 21.9x |
| P/E | 40.2x | 28.4x |
| P/FCF | 34.3x | 26.3x |

On consensus rather than run-rate arithmetic: 25.2x FY2026 EPS of $16.03 and 19.1x FY2027 EPS of $21.16. Against FY2027 consensus EPS growth of 32%, forward PEG is approximately 0.6; against FY2026's implied 60% growth, approximately 0.42. The Q1 annualization is explicitly a sensitivity, not a forecast — digital advertising is seasonally weakest in Q1, Q1 FCF of $1,286.7M exceeded net income (107% conversion) on favorable working-capital timing, and cash taxes are lumpy. Applying the observed deceleration to a full-year 2026 revenue estimate of roughly +45-55% implies $7.9-8.5B, a higher base than naive annualization, so the sensitivity cuts both ways.

The peer table is of limited use. Median application-software P/E of 19.3x is set by CRM (19.3x, -37% from its high), ADBE (12.8x, -38%) and INTU (16.9x, -64%) — all names caught in the seat-based software derating, growing revenue in the single digits. CDNS at 89.4x and DDOG at 614.1x mark the other end. AppLovin at 25x forward on 45-55% revenue growth and an 82% adjusted EBITDA margin does not belong to either cohort. The more relevant comparison is to its own history and to the growth it must deliver: at 19x FY2027 consensus, the stock requires roughly 30% EPS growth in 2027 to be worth today's price on a 20x exit; it does not require the 60%+ growth that the 2025 multiple assumed.

Where we withhold a higher score is earnings quality inside the consensus. FY2025 EPS of $10.04 was flattered by (a) the one-time SBC roll-off, which will partially reverse as $489.0M of unrecognized compensation plus $410.5M of October 2025 PSU fair value are expensed, and (b) a 13.1% effective tax rate that reflected a $272.1M Singapore benefit expiring June 2028 and $0 of federal cash tax. If either normalizes faster than modeled, the FY2027 $21.16 is optimistic and 19x becomes 22-23x. The compressed multiple is not a structural-impairment discount, but neither is it a clean mispricing — it is the market pricing a real probability that the take-rate-led growth engine pauses. Analyst mean target of $654.6 (median $660, low $406, high $860) sits 62% above spot with 29 of 32 ratings at Buy or Strong Buy; on a stock at 25x forward with rising estimates, that consensus is more likely stale-to-the-downside in its distribution than independently confirming, and we treat it as context rather than evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

AppLovin's competitive set is structurally unusual, and the 10-K states it plainly: competitors are "Meta, Google, Amazon, and Unity Software as well as various private companies, several of which are also our partners and clients," while MD&A confirms advertising clients include "some of the largest global internet platforms, such as Meta and Google." Those firms simultaneously buy user-acquisition inventory through AppLovin, compete with it, and control app distribution, identifiers and consent frameworks. Item 1A: "Because Meta and Google are also significant partners of AppLovin and Adjust, a deterioration in our or Adjust's relationship with such companies would have a greater impact on our business." Note 2 adds that "substantially all of the Company's contracts with customers are cancelable at any time," and Item 1A warns that "clients who are also competitors may decide to invest in their own offerings."

The moat, on the evidence, is a two-sided data flywheel: MAX supplies publisher-side supply and bidding data, Adjust supplies attribution, and Axon converts both into bid optimization. The financial corroboration is strong — 87.9% gross margin, 82.3% adjusted EBITDA margin, datacenter costs growing at roughly half the rate of revenue, no customer above 10% of revenue, and self-serve onboarding extending reach. The corroboration against durability is the volume trend and the thin R&D line.

Trajectory is therefore genuinely ambiguous rather than clearly improving: per-unit economics are widening while unit throughput contracts, and the company itself concedes on e-commerce that "there can be no assurance that we will achieve broader adoption among e-commerce advertisers." Regulatory direction is a durable headwind spanning GDPR, state privacy regimes with private rights of action, children's-privacy codes, the EU AI Act, and the DOJ final rule implementing Executive Order 14117 (effective April 8, 2025) restricting bulk transfers of personal identifiers and geolocation data to China and other listed jurisdictions, which the company flags as creating "operational challenges and legal risks… particularly with regard to China, where we have operations."

## Risk Assessment

**Growth composition (highest impact, least resolvable).** Addressed above. If the −18% install decline reflects mobile-gaming saturation rather than e-commerce mix shift, the durability of 80% EBITDA margins at 25x forward earnings is the question, and the equity has considerably more to give back.

**Securities litigation, with a discrete unresolved event.** Item 3 discloses the consolidated Brownback Action (N.D. Cal.) under §10(b)/§20(a) and Rule 10b-5 against the company, CEO Adam Foroughi, CFO Matthew Stumpf, and director Herald Chen, with CTO Basil Shikin added via the September 12, 2025 amended complaint. The alleged class period is November 7, 2024 to March 27, 2025, concerning "materially false and misleading statements regarding the Company's advertising solutions and financial growth." Defendants moved to dismiss in November 2025 with a hearing scheduled for March 2026; the outcome is not in our sources. Consolidated derivative suits are stayed pending that ruling. The company says the allegations lack merit and cannot estimate a range of loss; Note 6 records no material loss contingency. Item 3 names **no** SEC investigation or subpoena — a third-party reference to "SEC probes" in a March 2026 article is uncorroborated by disclosure and should not be treated as fact.

**Key-person and lean-organization risk.** Item 1A: "We do not have employment agreements, other than offer letters, with Mr. Foroughi or other members of our senior management team, and we do not maintain key-man insurance," and the lean structure means "key employees often hold multiple responsibilities, making us more vulnerable to disruptions." Class B shares carry 20 votes each; Foroughi and Chen held approximately 67% of voting power at year-end, and all Class B converts if Foroughi ceases to be a director or officer — linking control structure directly to key-person exposure.

**Governance and disclosure items.** The Q1 2026 10-Q disclosed the resignation of the Principal Accounting Officer effective April 30, 2026. An April 7, 2026 8-K carrying Items 5.02/7.01/9.01 exists but its substance is unverified in our review; whether it relates to that departure, a board change, or another matter is an open item. Deloitte issued unqualified opinions on both the financial statements and internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025, with the sole critical audit matter being the valuation of the October 2025 PSUs — notably not revenue recognition, which is where external criticism has focused.

**Tax cliff.** Singapore incentives worth $0.80 per diluted share expire June 2028 (renewable at the company's election); Pillar 2 has already reduced the benefit by $82.7M; the effective rate has moved 1.4% → 13.1% in one year.

**Cloud commitment concentration.** Non-cancelable minimum purchase commitments of $702.8M ($398.5M within 12 months) relate primarily to a multi-year arrangement with a single unnamed cloud provider, under an August 2024 $1.3B three-year minimum of which approximately $780.4M had been paid by March 31, 2026.

**Credit quality of the new advertiser base.** G&A rose 41.6%, including $24.5M of bad debt expense "primarily related to new initiatives," alongside disclosed exposure to gambling, alcohol, CBD/hemp, financial services and health advertisers. At 0.45% of revenue this is small and the year-end allowance was "not material," but it is the metric to watch as self-serve e-commerce scales.

**Goodwill trigger language is live.** Item 1A cites "a significant decline in the market price of our Class A common stock and our market capitalization" as an impairment indicator. With $1,540.0M of goodwill in a single reporting unit against roughly $135.7B of market capitalization, a test failure remains remote, but post-divestiture single-unit testing also means weakness at Wurl or Adjust can now be masked by Axon's strength.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 144.0% against 74.5% one-year historical volatility overstates the stress: that tenor expires in one day and carries an implied move of only ±1.0%, so it reflects same-day expiry mechanics. The informative reading is the 13-day tenor at 102.8% IV with a ±16.2% implied move and a 1.26 put/call volume ratio — the market pricing the Q2 print (expected early-to-mid August on the reporting cadence, following the May 6 Q1 release) as a large two-sided event. Term structure then falls to 80.6% at three months and 77.6% at six months, so the elevation is event-specific rather than a regime change. Put/call open interest of 1.67 indicates protection held in size; skew readings are inconsistent across tenors (the data source characterizes the 9.2% near-term figure as elevated put demand, while three- and six-month skews print slightly negative), so we take no directional inference from skew beyond that. The one unusual trade is 504 contracts of November 2026 $300 puts against 129 open interest (3.9x), a position that pays on a further 24% decline — consistent with someone hedging or expressing the growth-composition bear case over a two-quarter window. Short interest is only 4.3% of float with 2.1 days to cover, so the bear thesis is not crowded in the equity. Net read: the options market corroborates our view that the Q2 print is the binary, and that entering ahead of it pays 100%+ implied volatility for the privilege.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($640-660).** The install decline is metric obsolescence, not demand loss: e-commerce advertisers generate revenue without app installs, and the Q2 print either discloses a gaming/e-commerce split or shows growth holding near 50%+ with take-rate expansion intact. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $21.16 proves conservative, given four straight beats and 90-day revisions still positive. On 30x FY2027 EPS the stock is $635; the Wedbush $640 target and $654.6 consensus mean sit there. The supporting facts are unusually clean: 78% operating margins, 70% FCF margins, $0.5M of annual capex, 0.24x net leverage, no maturities before 2029, $3.3B of buyback authorization, no customer above 10% of revenue, and the removal of Google's Privacy Sandbox as a targeting-data threat. An aggressive repurchase into this drawdown would be the clearest signal management shares this view.

**Bear case ($250-280).** Core mobile-gaming install volume is contracting, e-commerce growth is decelerating (as the July 13 third-party work reportedly indicates), and take-rate expansion has a ceiling because advertisers only accept higher costs while return on ad spend improves. Growth decelerates from 59% toward 20-25% during 2026-2027 while SBC rises ($489.0M unrecognized plus $410.5M of October 2025 PSU fair value) and the tax rate steps up toward the 2028 Singapore expiry, so EPS lands nearer $17-18 in FY2027 than $21.16. On 15x that base, the stock is $255-270. An adverse ruling on the motion to dismiss in the Brownback Action, or any disclosure event connected to the accounting-officer transition, accelerates the derating. Insiders sold into $470-600 and bought nothing at $393.

**Our base case ($450).** Growth decelerates but holds in the 40%+ range through 2026 as e-commerce partially offsets flat-to-down gaming volume; FY2027 EPS lands near $20 after a haircut for SBC and tax normalization; the market pays 22x for a business with these margins and this balance sheet once composition is disclosed. 22x × $20 = $440, and 27x FY2026 $16.03 = $433 — call it $450, roughly 11% above the July 30 close.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the dimension that most reliably separates winners from losers in this framework — forward-looking management behavior — is the weakest part of the file, and the decisive information arrives in days rather than quarters.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q2 2026 results, expected early-to-mid August 2026 (options price a ±16% move at the 13-day tenor around the August 14 expiry). Secondary: any 8-K disclosing the Brownback motion-to-dismiss ruling, and the Q2 10-Q issuer-purchase table showing whether management repurchased into the $390-450 range.

**Upgrade to BUY on any two of:**
1. Q2 revenue growth of 45% or better **with** either a disclosed gaming-versus-e-commerce revenue split or installations stabilizing (better than −10% year over year) — this converts the central unresolved question into a knowable one.
2. Disclosed Q2 repurchases materially above the ~$550M quarterly run-rate implied by 2025 at prices below $450, evidencing that management treats this price as value. With $3.3B authorized and 0.24x net leverage, capacity is not the constraint.
3. Any open-market insider purchase by Foroughi, Stumpf or Shikin, which would break the one-directional May-July selling pattern.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Q2 revenue growth below 35% with net revenue per installation growth decelerating — the signature of take-rate exhaustion rather than mix shift.
2. Denial of the motion to dismiss in the Brownback Action combined with adverse expansion of the class period, which would extend litigation overhang into the 2026-27 disclosure record.
3. Bad debt expense rising above roughly 1.5% of revenue, or an accounts-receivable build outpacing revenue growth, indicating that self-serve e-commerce TAM expansion is being bought with credit quality.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $18 (currently $21.16), which would remove the valuation support at 19x forward.

**Upside to base case: +11%. Upside to bull case: +59-63%. Downside to bear case: −31% to −38%.** The skew is roughly balanced at today's price, which is precisely why we wait for the Q2 print rather than pay 100%+ implied volatility to pre-position.

## Conclusion

AppLovin is a genuinely excellent business at a price that is no longer demanding: 25x FY2026 and 19x FY2027 consensus earnings, 30x EV/EBITDA on trailing and roughly 22x on annualized Q1, with 78% operating margins, $3.95B of FY2025 free cash flow, near-zero capital intensity, 0.12-0.24x net leverage and no maturities before 2029. Consensus estimates have risen slightly over 90 days while the equity fell 46% from its high, and the company has beaten EPS four quarters running. That is the shape of a mispricing.

What prevents an upgrade is the specificity of the bear case rather than its volume. Growth has flipped from volume-led to price-led within eight quarters, with installations down 18% in Q1 2026, and the filings do not disclose the gaming-versus-e-commerce split needed to separate metric obsolescence from core contraction. FY2025's margin step-up was roughly one-sixth a non-repeatable equity-compensation roll-off, and the tax profile that produced $0 of federal cash tax on $2.2B of U.S. pretax income has two identifiable step-ups ahead. Management's own recent behavior argues against paying up: broad selling by the CEO, CTO, CFO, legal officer and two directors at $470-600 with no observed open-market purchase into a 46% decline; 2025 buybacks struck at an average ~$398.8 that reduced net share count by 0.51% because roughly 85% was absorbed offsetting equity-award issuance; Q1 2026 repurchases at ~$406-561; and a $50M director-affiliated venture investment written to zero while the associated Axon commercial agreement never generated a single transaction.

The question that matters resolves in days, at a print the options market expects to move the stock 16% in either direction. Hold the position, do not add ahead of the event, and use the criteria above to size up on confirmation or exit on composition deterioration.
