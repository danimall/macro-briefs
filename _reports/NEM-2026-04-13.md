## Executive Summary

Newmont delivered transformational financial results in 2025: $7.1B net income, $7.3B free cash flow, and a $7.4B swing from $5.3B net debt to $2.1B net cash. The company completed all seven planned divestitures ($2.8B in proceeds), reduced debt by $2.5B, and repurchased $3.6B in stock while bringing the low-cost Ahafo North mine into production. Four consecutive quarterly earnings beats averaging +25% surprise demonstrate execution that consistently exceeds expectations.

The stock has pulled back ~13% from its January 28 high of $134.88 to $120.90, creating a more attractive entry point. Analyst consensus targets $140 (median), with estimates revised upward +19% (current year) and +25% (next year) over the past 90 days. The balance sheet is the strongest it has been in at least five years, with $11.6B in total liquidity providing substantial cushion against the two key emerging risks: the Barrick/NGM joint venture dispute and Ghana's deteriorating fiscal regime.

The prior rating trajectory moved from BUY (February) to HOLD (March), driven by the sharp selloff from January highs and emerging risk factors around NGM and Ghana. That selloff has partially stabilized, and the fundamental earnings trajectory has continued to strengthen through estimate revisions. At ~19x trailing earnings and ~12.6x current-year consensus ($9.35), the valuation is reasonable for a company generating 32% FCF margins with the strongest balance sheet in gold mining. The risk factors that drove the downgrade remain, but the price decline has improved the risk/reward calculus.

## Company Overview

Newmont is the world's largest gold mining company by market capitalization ($125.6B) and production, and the only gold producer in both the S&P 500 and Fortune 500. The November 2023 acquisition of Newcrest Mining (~$13.5B) created a globally diversified portfolio spanning the US, Australia, Ghana, Suriname, Argentina, Peru, Mexico, Papua New Guinea, Canada, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, and Chile.

Following portfolio rationalization, Newmont operates approximately 12 core mines focused on Tier 1 and Tier 2 assets. Revenue in 2025 was $22.7B: gold 85%, copper 6%, silver 5%, zinc 3%, lead 1%. The company does not hedge gold production, meaning earnings are directly leveraged to the gold price — advantageous in the current environment.

Key operations by scale: Nevada Gold Mines (38.5% JV with Barrick, 999K oz), Lihir (PNG), Cadia (Australia, significant copper co-product), Boddington (Australia), Peñasquito (Mexico, polymetallic), Ahafo South/North (Ghana), and Merian (Suriname).

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

Revenue grew 21% to $22.7B in 2025, driven entirely by a 45% increase in average realized gold price ($3,498/oz vs. $2,408/oz). Gold ounces sold declined from 6,539K to 5,519K, reflecting asset divestitures. Net income swung from $3.3B (2024) to $7.1B (2025), with adjusted net income of $7.6B ($6.89/share).

All-in sustaining costs rose to $1,609/oz (+6% YoY), with increases across nearly every continuing mine. The cost increase was driven by higher government royalties (mechanically linked to gold price), workers' participation in Mexico and Peru, and higher mining/milling costs at NGM and Brucejack. The critical fundamental tension: costs are rising, but at roughly one-sixth the rate of gold price appreciation. The margin gap between $3,498 realized price and $1,609 AISC is $1,889/oz — the widest in the company's history.

Cost outliers to monitor: Cerro Negro ($2,220/oz AISC, safety shutdowns), Brucejack ($2,020/oz), and the newly-divested operations that were the worst performers. The best performer is Ahafo North at $696/oz, providing meaningful portfolio cost dilution.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow reached $10.3B (up from $6.3B), and free cash flow hit $7.3B (up from $2.9B). FCF margin is 32.2%, and the FCF-to-net-income conversion ratio exceeds 100%, indicating high-quality earnings without aggressive accrual accounting. Capital expenditure was $3.0B, down from $3.4B, reflecting divestiture-related reductions. Key forward capex: Tanami Expansion 2 ($1.7-1.8B total, H2 2027 production), Cadia Panel Caves ($2.0-2.4B, through 2029).

### Balance Sheet

Cash rose from $3.6B to $7.6B. Long-term debt declined from $7.6B to $5.1B via a $2B debt tender and additional redemptions. Net debt swung from positive $5.3B to negative $2.1B. Current ratio is 2.29x. Total liquidity is $11.6B ($7.6B cash + $4.0B undrawn revolver). Investment-grade ratings of BBB+/A3/A-.

The $9.9B undiscounted remediation and reclamation liability ($922M current) is the most significant off-balance-sheet obligation and warrants ongoing monitoring, particularly at Yanacocha and Peñasquito.

### Capital Returns

Newmont has authorized $6B in cumulative share buybacks since February 2024 and executed $3.6B through the filing date. Share count declined from 1,146M to 1,106M (~3.5% reduction). The quarterly dividend is $0.26/share ($1.04 annualized), yielding approximately 0.9%. Total capital returns (buybacks + dividends) of ~$4.7B represent a ~3.7% total yield on current market cap.

### Earnings Consistency

Four consecutive quarterly beats: +38.2% (Q1), +20.4% (Q2), +18.3% (Q3), +24.7% (Q4). The average surprise of +25% across a full year suggests management is either deliberately conservative with guidance or operating with meaningful margin of safety in execution. This track record warrants higher confidence in forward consensus estimates.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimates stand at $9.35, up 19.1% over 90 days. Next-year (FY2027) at $11.03, up 25.1% over 90 days. The 30-day revision for current year is -1.0%, suggesting a brief pause in the upward trajectory but not a reversal. The next-year revision of +1.6% over 30 days confirms continued positive momentum on the outer year.

These revisions imply FY2027 earnings growth of ~18% over FY2026, driven by volume growth from Ahafo North (full year contribution), Tanami Expansion 2 (H2 2027 commissioning), and potential gold price appreciation.

### Organic Production Growth

Ahafo North achieved commercial production in October 2025 at $696/oz AISC. At full run-rate, this mine should contribute 250-350K oz annually to Newmont's production. Tanami Expansion 2, the next major growth project ($1.7-1.8B, completion H2 2027), will add another ~100K oz annually and extend mine life by approximately 10 years. Cadia Panel Caves ($2.0-2.4B through 2029) ensure continued production from one of the world's lowest-cost gold-copper operations.

After the divestiture-related production decline (5,519K oz from 6,539K), production should stabilize and grow through 2027-2028 as these organic projects ramp.

### Gold Price Tailwind

Newmont's realized gold price was $3,498/oz in 2025. Gold is currently trading above $3,300/oz with structural support from: central bank purchases (particularly by non-Western central banks diversifying away from dollar reserves), inflation running persistently above targets, geopolitical risk premiums from broader de-dollarization trends, and Treasury market dysfunction concerns. A sustained move to $3,500-4,000/oz is within the distribution of reasonable outcomes over the next 12-18 months.

The sensitivity is substantial: every $100/oz change in gold price translates to roughly $550M in annual revenue and $400-450M in pre-tax income, given that AISC is relatively fixed in the short term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $120.90, NEM trades at ~18.9x trailing EPS ($6.39), ~12.9x current-year consensus ($9.35), and ~11.0x next-year consensus ($11.03). Against Materials sector peer medians of 33.4x P/E, Newmont trades at a 43% discount on trailing earnings and a 61-67% discount on forward estimates.

The peer comparison requires nuance: FCX, SHW, ECL, and CRH are different businesses (copper, coatings, water treatment, building materials). Against direct gold mining peers (Barrick, Agnico Eagle, Kinross), NEM typically commands a premium multiple given its S&P 500 inclusion, liquidity, and asset quality, but currently trades at the lower end of its historical range relative to peers.

The analyst consensus price target of $140 (median, with $70-177 range) implies approximately 16% upside from current levels. BofA recently raised its target from $134 to $151. The 18 analysts covering the stock split 5 Strong Buy, 13 Buy, 2 Hold, 1 Sell.

On a P/FCF basis, at $7.3B FCF, NEM trades at ~17.2x trailing FCF. For a company with 32% FCF margins, net cash, investment-grade balance sheet, and accelerating production growth, this is not expensive.

The valuation score remains constrained by the stock's 137% appreciation over the trailing year and 143% gain from 52-week lows. Much of the gold cycle tailwind is priced, but the forward multiple compression (from 18.9x trailing to 11.0x next-year) suggests the market has not fully captured the earnings growth trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Newmont is the clear category leader in gold mining. Post-Newcrest, no competitor matches its combination of scale, geographic diversification, asset quality, and S&P 500 inclusion. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Scale economies**: $22.7B revenue provides operating leverage and corporate cost absorption that smaller miners cannot match. The "Project Catalyst" restructuring is designed to further reduce overhead.

2. **Liquidity premium**: As the only gold miner in the S&P 500, NEM attracts passive index flows and institutional mandates that smaller peers cannot access. This structural demand supports valuation.

3. **Balance sheet**: Net cash position enables counter-cyclical acquisitions if gold prices decline, as demonstrated with the Newcrest acquisition at cycle lows.

4. **Multi-metal diversification**: Copper (6% of revenue via Cadia, Boddington, Red Chris) provides partial hedge against gold price volatility and exposure to electrification demand.

The principal competitive dynamic to watch is the NGM joint venture dispute with Barrick, which could reshape the industry's two largest players' operational footprints in Nevada.

## Risk Assessment

### NGM/Barrick Dispute (Critical, NEW)

The formal default notice to Barrick (February 3, 2026) over alleged mismanagement and resource diversion at Nevada Gold Mines represents the most significant operational risk. NGM produced 999K oz in 2025 (~18% of total production). Barrick is the operator with 61.5% ownership. Possible outcomes range from negotiated resolution (best case) through complex litigation to potential JV restructuring (worst case). Assuming 6 months of production disruption, the revenue impact could be $1.5-2.0B.

### Ghana Fiscal Deterioration (Significant, NEW)

The expired Revised Investment Agreement exposes Ahafo operations (South + North, ~$2.1B pretax income combined) to higher corporate tax (32.5% → 35%), customs duties (5-20% on mining goods), a proposed sliding-scale royalty (5-12% based on gold price), the 3% Growth and Sustainability Levy, and 8% dividend withholding tax. The effective tax rate already rose from 35% to 41% in 2025. Under the proposed sliding-scale royalty at current gold prices, the marginal fiscal take could increase by $100-200M annually.

### Gold Price Dependency (Structural)

Newmont does not hedge gold production. The $1,889/oz margin is historically wide, but AISC is rising. If gold declined 20% to ~$2,800/oz while AISC continued rising toward $1,700/oz, the margin would compress to ~$1,100/oz — still profitable but a ~42% reduction in per-ounce economics. EPS under that scenario would approximate $5-6.

### Cost Inflation (Moderate, Ongoing)

AISC rose at virtually every continuing mine in 2025. Government royalties are mechanically linked to gold prices (rising automatically), and labor/energy costs are structurally elevated. If gold prices moderate while costs continue their trajectory, margin compression could be meaningful.

### Operational/Jurisdictional Risk

Argentina (Cerro Negro: safety shutdowns, 90/10 employment law, peso volatility), Mexico (Peñasquito: water stress, proposed open-pit mining ban, union profit-sharing), Peru (Yanacocha: political instability, 8th president in a decade), Papua New Guinea (Lihir: new tax act, proposed gold mint). Each jurisdiction carries tail risk of nationalization, tax changes, or operational disruption.

### Remediation Liability

$9.9B undiscounted remediation and reclamation obligation. Peñasquito estimates increased due to tailings embankment stability concerns. Yanacocha water treatment costs remain uncertain.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 56.8% vs. 45.0% historical volatility indicates the market is pricing 11.8pp of excess uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (56.8% near-term vs. 47.5% 6-month), consistent with imminent event risk pricing. Put/call skew of 5.4% on the weekly expiry shows modestly elevated put demand, though the overall OI put/call ratio of 0.83 is not extreme. The IV richness relative to HV means options are expensive for buyers, which limits the utility of protective puts at current prices.

Ahafo North ramps to full production at sub-$700/oz AISC. NGM dispute resolves without material production disruption. Tanami Expansion 2 on schedule for H2 2027. ## Conclusion

Newmont's 2025 financial results — net cash position, $7.3B FCF, 4/4 earnings beats, 32% FCF margins — establish it as the highest-quality vehicle for gold exposure available in public equities. The 13% pullback from January highs creates a more favorable entry point, and 90-day estimate revisions of +19-25% confirm the earnings trajectory remains intact.

The risks identified in March (NGM dispute, Ghana fiscal, cost inflation) remain real and partially drove the prior downgrade to HOLD. However, the balance sheet provides substantial cushion, the earnings beat streak demonstrates execution quality, and the forward P/E compression from 18.9x trailing to 11.0x next-year indicates the market hasn't fully captured growth. Gold's structural support from central bank buying, persistent inflation (now tracking toward 4.0%+), and geopolitical risk premiums provides a favorable macro backdrop.

The recommendation is BUY with a $137 base case target and a 12-month horizon. Position sizing should reflect the inherent volatility of a gold miner (56.8% near-term IV) and the binary nature of the NGM dispute outcome.
