**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Badger Meter is a Milwaukee-based water technology company that, from FY22 through FY25, was a near-textbook quality compounder: revenue grew at a ~17% CAGR to $916.7M, operating margin expanded from 15.4% to 20.0%, the balance sheet carried zero funded debt, and total shareholder return beat the proxy peer group. That profile earned a premium multiple, and the stock peaked at $251.54 within the past year. The compounding story broke in the March 2026 quarter, and the price has followed: shares are down 43.7% over twelve months and roughly 46% off the high, last at $134.65.

Q1'26 revenue fell 9% YoY to $202.3M, missing consensus by ~$28.6M; diluted EPS came in at $0.93 versus a $1.24 estimate, a 24.8% miss; operating margin compressed ~480bps. This was the third miss in the last four quarters. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut across every horizon — the current quarter down 18.6% over 90 days, the current year down 9.4%, next year down 7.3%. Management attributes the softness to project timing and short-cycle municipal ordering; a cluster of plaintiffs' firms attributes it to concealed demand deterioration, with a securities class action covering April 2024 through April 2026 and a lead-plaintiff deadline of August 3, 2026.

We are reversing the prior BUY rating to AVOID. The structural drivers of the long-term thesis — the conversion of U.S. water connections to AMI (only ~40% penetrated) and a growing margin-accretive SaaS layer — remain intact, and the balance sheet eliminates any solvency concern. But on a 12-month view we cannot identify a positive catalyst. Estimates are falling rather than rising; the next several quarters face tough comps; the multiple remains premium (~25.8x forward EPS) on declining earnings; and the litigation and goodwill-impairment risks are not yet priced. A premium multiple on deteriorating earnings momentum is an unfavorable setup for forward returns.

## Company Overview

Badger Meter operates as a single reportable segment with roughly 95% of sales tied to water applications. Its BlueEdge solution suite spans four layers: measurement and control hardware (water meters, water-quality sensors, pressure/acoustic leak detection, and newly added sewer/lift-station monitoring), communication (ORION radio endpoints including a cellular Network-as-a-Service offering built on AT&T/Verizon partnerships), data and software (the BEACON SaaS suite), and services.

Two product lines sit within the single segment. Utility Water (~89% of 2025 sales) sells meters, radios, and software to the more than 50,000 U.S. water utilities; the secular driver is the conversion of manually-read meters to AMR and then to advanced metering infrastructure (AMI), alongside the shift from mechanical to ultrasonic/static meters. Flow Instrumentation (~11%) sells meters, valves, and sensors into industrial, HVAC, and water/wastewater applications and is roughly flat, with management de-emphasizing general-industrial uses.

Two acquisitions in fifteen months have repositioned the company toward wastewater and software: SmartCover (Hadronex, closed January 2025 for $184M, sewer-line and lift-station monitoring) and UDlive (UK-based, signed April 2026, ~$100M plus an EBITDA earnout, hardware-enabled sewer monitoring). Together they push BMI from a clean-water meter maker toward a broader "full water cycle" data platform, raising recurring-software content while adding ~$235M of goodwill and ~$118M of intangibles to the books.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year record is genuinely strong, which is what makes the recent break consequential.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 565.6 | 703.6 | 826.6 | 916.7 | 202.3 (−9% YoY) |
| Gross margin | 38.9% | 39.3% | 39.8% | 41.7% | 41.7% (vs 42.9% PY) |
| Operating margin | 15.4% | 16.8% | 19.1% | 20.0% | 17.4% (vs 22.2% PY) |
| Net income ($M) | 66.5 | 92.6 | 124.9 | 141.6 | 27.3 (vs 38.4 PY) |
| Diluted EPS | $2.26 | $3.14 | $4.23 | $4.79 | $0.93 (vs $1.30 PY) |

FY25 was a record: revenue up 10.9%, gross margin up ~190bps on favorable mix (ultrasonic meters, ORION Cellular, water quality, SmartCover). Roughly $40M of the growth came from SmartCover; organic utility water growth was still healthy.

Q1'26 reversed the trend. Utility water sales fell 9.6%, blamed on the timing of previously completed project deployments and customer short-cycle order pacing, against a very strong $222M comp in Q1'25. Margin compressed as fixed costs deleveraged against lower volume. The mechanism matters: utility water revenue is tied to large, discrete AMI deployment projects and municipal ordering cadence, which can swing sharply quarter to quarter, particularly against tough comparisons. We cannot yet determine whether the air-pocket is timing (management's framing) or the early stage of a structural digestion of AMI deployments, share loss to lower-barrier static-meter entrants, or municipal budget softening.

The balance sheet is the offsetting strength. Net cash was ~$205M at the end of Q1'26 (down from $226M at year-end 2025), with zero funded debt and zero interest expense. The current ratio is 3.36 and equity funds 73% of assets. The screening figure of "D/E 1.36" is a misleading artifact that counts operating liabilities including ~$73M of long-term deferred revenue and leases; there is no actual leverage, and the company carries a $150M unused revolver. Cash flow quality is high: FY25 operating cash flow of $183.7M exceeded net income of $141.6M, free cash flow was $169.7M (an 18.5% FCF margin), and capex is light at ~1.5% of sales. Even with depressed Q1'26 earnings, operating cash flow held at $33.9M on working-capital timing. Altman Z-Score of 3.47 confirms the safe zone. The debate here is about growth and multiple, not solvency.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: the dividend was raised to $0.40/quarter (from $0.34), extending a ~33-year streak of increases; the buyback authorization was doubled to $150M across November 2025 and February 2026; and $38.2M was repurchased in Q1'26 at roughly $147 per share. That repurchase, executed weeks before the April 17 decline, looks ill-timed in hindsight and forms part of the plaintiffs' narrative.

## Growth Analysis

The single most reliable forward signal — the direction of consensus estimates — is negative, and that is the heart of the AVOID call.

| Current Quarter | $1.03 | $1.27 | −18.6% |
| Next Quarter | $1.29 | $1.35 | −4.8% |
| Current Year | $4.65 | $5.13 | −9.4% |
| Next Year | $5.22 | $5.63 | −7.3% |

These are broad, multi-horizon downgrades, the opposite of the upward pattern that precedes re-ratings. The earnings track reinforces the point: three misses in the last four quarters. Revenue is contracting, not accelerating, so cycle-adjusted or normalized-earnings framing does not apply; this is a downturn, not a trough recovery, and there is no basis for assuming earnings power above the falling consensus.

The long-run structural story remains valid. The ~40% AMI penetration leaves a multi-decade conversion runway, and drivers such as aging infrastructure, an aging utility workforce, non-revenue-water reduction, and regulatory water-quality monitoring are durable. The mechanical-to-ultrasonic mix shift is margin-accretive, and the BEACON/Active Site Monitoring SaaS layer adds recurring revenue. The two wastewater acquisitions extend the addressable market into collection-system monitoring. None of this is in dispute. The problem is timing: the structural runway is a multi-year tailwind, while the next several quarters face declining year-over-year comparisons and falling estimates. A growth thesis that pays off over five years does not justify holding through a 12-month window in which the most likely path is continued YoY declines and possible further estimate cuts.

## Valuation Assessment

At $134.65, BMI trades at 25.8x forward EPS and 30.5x trailing. On the current-year consensus, the multiple is ~29x; on next year's, ~25.8x. For a company whose revenue is contracting 9% and whose EPS fell 28% in the most recent quarter, this is a premium multiple, not a value entry.

The valuation guidance for inflection stocks — use forward/normalized earnings, treat analyst targets as floors — does not apply here, because BMI is not inflecting upward. Estimates are falling, revenue is contracting, and margins are compressing. There is no warranted upward cycle adjustment. If demand recovers on management's timing thesis, normalized earnings power may be higher; but with three of four quarters missed and estimates still being cut, assuming a quick snapback is speculative.

The risk is asymmetric to the downside. A premium multiple is sustainable only while growth and margin expansion continue; both have reversed. If the multiple compresses to the ~18-20x range more typical of a flat-to-modest-growth flow-instrument business with open questions about demand durability, that implies roughly $94-104 on next year's depressed EPS. Analyst targets span a wide $110-169 (median $160), but the median appears to lag the deterioration: several houses cut forecasts after the Q1 miss while the median target still embeds the prior compounding trajectory. We would weight the low end of that range more heavily than the median.

The peer comparison set provided is too thin to anchor on (a single, loss-making software name), which itself signals that the sector classification ("Scientific & Technical Instruments / Technology") obscures BMI's true comparables — water and flow-measurement infrastructure peers that trade well below 26x forward. Against that more appropriate cohort, BMI looks expensive for its current growth profile.

## Competitive Landscape

BMI's moat is real but its trajectory is the relevant question. The durable advantages are an entrenched utility distribution channel (the barrier static-meter new entrants lack), a decade-plus lead in ultrasonic metering, a strong brand across more than 50,000 fragmented utility customers, favorable patent positions, and a cellular AMI offering that removes the need for utilities to build their own networks. The growing SaaS layer raises switching costs and recurring revenue.

The watch-items cut the other way. Static metering carries lower barriers to entry than mechanical, and the 10-K concedes the North American competitive landscape "could" shift as adoption accelerates. The Q1'26 demand softness raises the question of whether some of the decline reflects share loss rather than pure timing, which management's "project timing" framing does not fully answer and which the plaintiffs' bar is pressing. The pivot into wastewater via SmartCover and UDlive diversifies away from pure meter cyclicality and adds recurring software, but it also raises integration and goodwill-impairment stakes and consumed the cash that previously generated $5-9M of annual interest income. On balance the moat is intact, but the near-term trajectory is uncertain enough that we cannot score competitive position as clearly improving.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully reflected in the price.

**Securities litigation.** At least five firms have announced investigations or filed actions. The theory is that BMI overstated demand while the CEO's pay-actually-paid peaked and the board doubled the buyback and repurchased $38.2M at ~$147 just before the decline. These suits are early-stage and opportunistic, but they create headline overhang and a dated near-term catalyst.

**Demand durability.** If the Q1'26 softness proves structural rather than timing-driven, both the earnings base and the multiple have further to fall, and the goodwill plus intangibles from the two acquisitions become impairment candidates. No impairment had been recorded as of year-end 2025.

**Concentration and supply.** Single-supplier dependence for certain brass castings, resins, and electronic subassemblies; reliance on municipal budgets and federal funding timing, including government-shutdown risk. Warranty reserves rose to $22.7M (Q1'26) from $16.7M (year-end 2024) as the electronics/radio mix increases claim rates.

**Governance signal.** An officer departure was disclosed December 11, 2025, roughly four months before the weak quarter; the specifics were not retrievable from filings, and whether it was routine is worth monitoring.

Offsetting these is the balance sheet, which removes financial-stress risk and means even a multi-quarter soft patch is survivable. The risk is to growth and valuation, not solvency.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing materially elevated uncertainty. ATM implied volatility jumped from 36.4% to 67.3% over the six weeks into mid-June even as the price rose 8.4%, indicating the market expects large moves around upcoming catalysts (next earnings, the August litigation deadline) rather than complacency. Rich IV at this level makes bearish option expression expensive and increases the carrying cost of any short. Combined with 20.4% short interest and 7.7 days to cover, the structure points to crowded bearish positioning where any positive surprise could force a sharp squeeze, which argues against treating BMI as an active short despite the weak fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Q1'26 miss is genuine project-timing lumpiness and tough comps, not structural deterioration. AMI conversion resumes, ultrasonic mix continues to lift margins, the SaaS layer compounds, and the SmartCover/UDlive wastewater pivot adds recurring revenue and a new growth vector. The zero-debt balance sheet funds dividends, buybacks, and M&A through the soft patch, and the multiple re-rates back toward the historical premium as growth reaccelerates. The litigation is dismissed or settled modestly. Upside to roughly $165-175 (high-20s multiple on a recovered ~$6 EPS), with the short interest providing squeeze fuel on any beat.

**Bear Case.** The demand softness is the early stage of AMI deployment digestion and/or share loss to lower-barrier static-meter competition, compounded by municipal budget pressure. Estimates continue to fall, the premium multiple compresses toward the 18-20x range appropriate for a flat-growth flow-instrument business, and goodwill from the two acquisitions is impaired. The litigation generates headline and modest financial cost. Downside to roughly $95-105, about 22-29% below the current price.

The asymmetry favors the bear case at today's price because the multiple still embeds the prior compounding trajectory while estimates point the other way. The most probable 12-month path is continued YoY declines against tough comps with the multiple drifting lower, landing the stock in the $110-125 range, at or below the current level, with downside risk if estimates cut further.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The plausible catalysts are the next earnings report (likely late July 2026), which faces a tough comp and could show continued YoY decline, and the August 3, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline that keeps litigation in the headlines. Neither is a clean, high-conviction trigger; management's "timing" framing means a stabilizing print is a real possibility.

**Timing window.** The earnings catalyst is roughly 5-7 weeks out; the litigation milestone is early August. Both fall within a two-month window, which is reasonable, but the outcomes are uncertain rather than mechanical.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 20.4% of float with 7.7 days to cover, high enough to create significant squeeze risk on any positive surprise. The bearish thesis is already widely held, which limits the incremental edge.

**Borrow and options signal.** Rich ATM IV makes put expression expensive and implies elevated borrow cost. The options market is confirming the bearish view, which is itself a crowding warning rather than an edge.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$95-105 (~22-29% downside) against squeeze upside toward $165-175 (~25-30% upside on a beat). That is roughly 1:1, not the 2:1 a short requires, especially with the stock already 46% off its high and much of the bad news priced.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis — a premium multiple on deteriorating earnings — is reasonable, but short interest at 20.4%, high days-to-cover, rich IV, and a stock already well off its highs make the remaining downside limited relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a 9-18 month expected headwind window, tied to the pace of utility-water demand recovery and resolution of the litigation overhang.

**Re-entry criteria (specific and monitorable):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive YoY revenue growth in utility water, confirming the Q1'26 softness was timing rather than structural.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turning positive (current-year or next-year estimates rising over a 90-day window), reversing the present downtrend.
- Multiple compression to ~18-20x forward EPS (roughly $94-104 on next-year consensus) without further estimate cuts, which would restore an asymmetric entry on a still-strong balance sheet.
- Clarification of the litigation path (dismissal, or a quantified, modest settlement) removing the overhang.

**Timeline.** We expect the demand and estimate headwinds to persist through at least the next two to three quarters given tough comparisons, with the litigation milestone in August 2026 a near-term focal point. Reassess after the next earnings report and again following the August lead-plaintiff deadline.

## Conclusion

Badger Meter remains a high-quality business by balance-sheet and cash-flow standards — zero debt, ~$205M net cash, 18%+ FCF margins, a ~33-year dividend-growth record, and a genuine multi-decade AMI conversion runway. Those qualities prevent this from being a falling-knife with solvency risk and keep the worst-case tail contained. They do not, however, support owning the stock today.

The growth thesis that justified the prior premium multiple and the recent BUY ratings broke in Q1'26. The prior reports treated the April decline as a buying opportunity on a quality compounder; the subsequent data — continued falling estimates, three misses in four quarters, a securities-litigation cluster, and a sharp spike in implied volatility — confirms that the price drop reflected genuine fundamental deterioration, not a mispricing. At ~25.8x forward EPS on declining earnings, with no identifiable 12-month positive catalyst and the litigation deadline approaching in August, the multiple is vulnerable to further compression. We rate BMI **AVOID** and would revisit only on a confirmed demand recovery, positive estimate revisions, or a meaningfully lower entry multiple.
