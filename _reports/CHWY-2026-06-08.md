**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chewy is the largest pure-play pet e-commerce retailer in the United States, generating $12.6B in FY2025 net sales with 21.3 million active customers and an 83.3% Autoship subscription mix that anchors a structurally recurring revenue base. The operating story over the past three years has been genuine and improving: operating income rose from a loss of $23.6M in FY2023 to $254.3M in FY2025, adjusted EBITDA grew to $719M, free cash flow expanded 24% to $562M, and the balance sheet carries zero funded debt against $860M in cash. By the numbers that matter for a high-turnover retailer, this is a healthy, cash-generative business.

The stock has declined roughly 57% over the trailing twelve months and trades near $20.64, essentially at its 2019 IPO price, even as cash flow grew. The compression reflects two real concerns rather than balance-sheet or accounting risk: active-customer growth has slowed to mid-single digits while net sales per active customer growth decelerated to +2.2% from +4.1%, and CEO Sumit Singh told the JPMorgan conference in May 2026 that U.S. consumers "appear more financially stretched than at the start of 2026." A warning of consumer stress from a company that markets itself as defensive is the single most important new data point, and it directly explains the multiple de-rating.

We rate CHWY a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The valuation has compressed to a point where the downside asymmetry has narrowed materially — at ~13x forward earnings and a high-single-digit FCF yield, much of the bad news appears priced. But there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple, the consumer backdrop is softening in ways the CEO has explicitly flagged, and a controlling shareholder continues to create a structural share overhang.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2011, Chewy built a "neighborhood pet store" customer-service model delivered at e-commerce scale. The business offers roughly 190,000 SKUs across approximately 4,000 brands. FY2025 revenue divides into:

- **Consumables** $8.78B (70% of sales) — the durable recurring base of food and supplies
- **Pet health & specialty** (Rx, healthcare) $1.98B (+11.8%) — the fastest-growing, higher-margin vertical
- **Hardgoods** $1.44B (+13.4%) — beds, crates, toys, the most discretionary line
- **Other** $0.41B — private brand, telehealth, insurance, vet clinics

The core competitive asset is Autoship, the subscription auto-replenishment program, which reached $10.5B of net sales, up from 76.2% two years prior. This recurring engine drives retention, supports demand forecasting, and creates a defensive revenue base relative to transactional retail. Chewy operates as a single reportable segment and is pushing toward higher-margin health and wellness through pet pharmacy, veterinary clinics (Chewy Vet Care), pet insurance, and private brands. The February 2026 $175M cash acquisition of SmartPak Equine extends this strategy into equine health and nutrition.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows steady top-line growth coupled with a deliberate profitability inflection:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $11.15B | $11.86B | $12.60B (+6.2%; +8.3% ex-53rd week) |
| Gross margin | 28.4% | 29.2% | 29.8% |
| Operating income | –$23.6M | $112.6M | $254.3M |
| Net income | $39.6M | $392.7M* | $222.8M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $368M | $570M | $719M (5.7% margin) |
| Operating cash flow | $486M | $596M | $692M |
| Free cash flow | $343M | $452M | $562M (+24% YoY) |

*FY2024 net income was inflated by a one-time $275.7M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release; on an operating basis FY2025 is materially stronger.*

The margin expansion is structural rather than cosmetic. Gross margin gained 60bps on higher-margin sponsored ads (retail media) and consumables mix, with SG&A leverage improving to 21.2% of sales from 21.5%. FCF conversion is strong on modest $129M capex, supported by an asset-light model with 3.74x asset turnover.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. The headline 5.76x debt-to-equity ratio is a screening artifact — it reflects operating lease liabilities (~$557M) and trade payables, not financial debt, divided by a thin book equity of $498M that has been eroded by a –$1.36B accumulated deficit from historical losses and buybacks. The Altman Z-score of 4.21 places it firmly in the safe zone. The 0.88 current ratio is normal for a retailer running on negative working capital, where payables exceed inventory. The company also carries an undrawn $800M ABL revolver.

The item that demands scrutiny is stock-based compensation. SBC including related taxes was $311M in FY2025, roughly 2.5% of sales and approximately 58% of GAAP net income, with $479M of unrecognized RSU comp remaining. This is a large, recurring economic cost that adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS exclude. The honest way to evaluate Chewy is on FCF net of the buybacks required to offset dilution. On that basis the business still generates meaningful cash — diluted shares fell from 432M to 426M over two years despite ~$298M/yr of SBC — but the buyback program is doing double duty as a dilution offset rather than pure shareholder return.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is decelerating, and this is the heart of the bear case. The underlying drivers are softening:

- Active customers reached 21.3M, up only 4.0% YoY
- Net sales per active customer growth slowed to +2.2% from +4.1%
- Hardgoods (+13.4%) and pet health & specialty (+11.8%) remain healthy, but these are smaller lines against the 70% consumables base

The growth offsets are the higher-margin verticals: pharmacy, vet clinics, insurance, and retail media. Management is guiding to further margin expansion from AI applications in advertising, logistics, and a finance-IT modernization going live at end of FY2026, with at least one analyst modeling +100bps of adjusted EBITDA margin to ~6.7%. The SmartPak acquisition adds an equine health channel.

The estimate revision picture is stable rather than inflecting. Current-year EPS consensus moved to $1.60 (+3.0% over 90 days), next-year to $1.96 (+3.2%). These are modest positive revisions, not the +30% velocity that would signal a fundamental inflection being missed by the market. The earnings track record is consistent: three in-line quarters and one miss over the last four, with a 19% miss in the June 2025 quarter. This is a company hitting its numbers, not one materially exceeding them.

The CEO's May 2026 warning matters because the pet category has historically been marketed as recession-resistant. Discretionary trade-down risk in hardgoods and premium food is the central swing factor for the next several quarters, and it is consistent with a broader pattern of mid-market consumer weakness. We weight this warning heavily; it is direct testimony from the operator most exposed to the data.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.64, Chewy trades at roughly 13x the $1.60 FY2026 EPS consensus and roughly 10.5x the $1.96 FY2027 estimate, with a free-cash-flow yield around 11% against the ~$5.0B market cap. This is a substantial de-rating from the 30x-plus multiples the stock carried during periods of higher growth expectations.

Peer context within Other Specialty Retail shows a median P/E of 19.3 (BBWI 5.3, WINA 33.8, TSCO 14.8, ULTA 19.3). The peer set is heterogeneous, but on a trailing basis CHWY's ~40x P/E (on depressed GAAP earnings burdened by the FY2024 tax-release base effect) is misleading. On forward earnings, CHWY at ~13x trades below the peer median and at a discount to its own structurally improving margin profile and recurring revenue base. The forward multiple is not demanding for a debt-free category leader generating double-digit FCF growth.

The case for the stock being cheap rests on the FCF yield and the forward multiple compression. The case against treating this as a clear buy is that the de-rating is justified by decelerating customer growth and a softening consumer, and that there is no specific catalyst to force a re-rating. The 57% decline is large enough that the burden of proof sits with the bull, and the market is pricing a slowing growth trajectory and macro fear, not balance-sheet or accounting risk. We do not see the multiple as stretched, but we also do not see a forcing function for upside. That balance places the valuation score modestly above neutral.

Analyst targets range from $25 low to $48 high, mean $38.85, median $40 — well above the current price. We treat these with caution given the realized decline; consensus targets have lagged the stock's deterioration, and the analyst recommendation distribution (8 strong buy, 12 buy, 5 hold) has not adjusted to the weaker customer-spend data. The targets are a floor argument only if the consumer stabilizes.

## Competitive Landscape

Chewy's moat is the Autoship lock-in combined with a customer-service reputation and a growing pharmacy/vet/insurance ecosystem that raises switching costs and lifetime value. The recurring base is genuinely defensive against pure transactional competition. Retail media (sponsored ads) is an emerging high-margin profit pool that scales with traffic.

The structural threat is Amazon, which has greater scale, logistics density, and AI resources, and the 10-K candidly acknowledges that most competitors have superior resources. Walmart, warehouse clubs, Petco/PetSmart, and Tractor Supply round out the field. PetSmart is itself BC Partners-affiliated, an unusual overlap with Chewy's controlling shareholder.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than deteriorating. Chewy is gaining share in pharmacy and health verticals and expanding retail media, and its margin expansion is evidence of pricing and mix discipline rather than competitive erosion. The risk is that the secular "humanization of pets" tailwind is offset by post-pandemic normalization of pet adoption and softer household formation, which pressures active-customer growth regardless of execution. The moat is intact; the addressable growth is the question.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Consumer weakness in a "defensive" category.** The CEO's May 2026 warning is the central fundamental risk. If trade-down accelerates, per-customer spend and hardgoods growth compress further. This is partly priced into the multiple but not fully if the slowdown deepens.
2. **Vendor concentration.** The three largest vendors are ~39% of net sales, stable but meaningful; losing a key food supplier is hard to replace given brand stickiness.
3. **Tariff/supply-chain exposure.** A meaningful share of private-brand and non-consumable sourcing is in China; the 10-K flags new U.S. tariff policy as a cost and supply risk.
4. **Pharmacy/vet regulatory friction.** Rx growth depends on veterinarian script authorization, with ongoing vet resistance and extensive FDA/state oversight of the fast-growing health vertical.
5. **Controlled-company overhang.** BC Partners and co-investors control >80% of voting power via Class B super-voting shares and continue selling. Repeated secondary offerings create a structural share overhang, and the dual-class structure makes Chewy ineligible for FTSE Russell indices, reducing passive demand.
6. **Stock-based compensation.** At ~58% of GAAP net income, SBC is a real economic cost the adjusted metrics exclude.

On governance, the buyback program has effectively absorbed BC Partners' exit at prices ($28–$42) well above the current ~$21, which has not been value-accretive for public holders. This is a related-party dynamic to monitor rather than a solvency concern.

The "restatement" language in the MD&A is benign — it reflects a common-control recast to consolidate Chewy Pharmacy KY after the 2023 BC Partners reorganization. Deloitte issued an unqualified opinion on both financials and internal controls. A screen flagging "restatement" would mislead here.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 131.0% against 46.8% trailing realized volatility, an 84-point spread placing IV at the 91st percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (131% near-term versus 64.3% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent event, consistent with the next earnings print being the binary. The 1-month skew at -13.8% indicates elevated put demand. Short interest is 11.6% of float with 3.4 days to cover, meaningful but not extreme. P/C open-interest ratio fell from 1.59 to 1.16 over the past three weeks while IV rose 22 points, suggesting hedging demand rather than directional conviction in either direction. The elevated IV makes this a poor moment to express a directional view through long options. The fundamental thesis is not overridden, but the options structure reinforces that the next catalyst is the swing factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Chewy is a debt-free category leader trading near its IPO price. The consumer slowdown proves transitory, per-customer spend reaccelerates, retail media and pharmacy drive continued margin expansion, and the AI/logistics initiatives deliver the guided margin gains. The multiple re-rates toward the peer median and the stock recovers toward the $35–$40 range. Bull case ~$35.

**Bear Case.** The consumer slowdown the CEO flagged deepens, active-customer growth stalls below 4%, per-customer spend turns flat to negative, and hardgoods/premium trade-down compresses gross margin. Amazon intensifies pet competition. The BC Partners overhang keeps a structural lid on the stock through continued secondaries. SBC remains a 2.5%-of-sales drag, and the buyback program continues absorbing sponsor shares rather than reducing float meaningfully. The forward multiple compresses to ~9x on falling estimates. Bear case ~$15, roughly 27% downside.

**Base Case.** Growth stays in the mid-single-digits, margins expand modestly, FCF holds, and the stock trades in a $20–$26 range as the market waits for evidence the consumer has stabilized. Base case ~$23.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call: the valuation is genuinely attractive on cash flow, but the consumer deceleration is real and CEO-confirmed, and there is no near-term catalyst to force the multiple higher.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net sales per active customer growth reaccelerates above +4% YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the consumer warning was transitory
- Active customer growth re-accelerates above +5% YoY, or adjusted EBITDA margin reaches the guided ~6.7% ahead of schedule
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, which is currently absent and would signal genuine conviction at these levels
- A meaningful reduction in the BC Partners overhang (Class B conversion progress or completed exit) removing the structural share supply

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Per-customer spend turns flat-to-negative or active customers decline sequentially, confirming structural rather than cyclical demand erosion
- Gross margin compresses on accelerating hardgoods/premium trade-down
- Estimate revisions turn negative by more than 10% on a 90-day basis
- A debt-funded acquisition or a shift in capital allocation that impairs the FCF profile

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings is the natural reassessment point, and the options market is pricing it as a binary event. Watch the active-customer and per-customer-spend lines specifically; those two metrics, more than the EPS headline, determine which way the thesis breaks.

## Conclusion

Chewy presents an improving-fundamentals-versus-collapsing-sentiment tension. The business is healthier than it was two years ago — operating income doubled, FCF grew, the balance sheet is debt-free, and the Autoship moat is widening. The stock is down sharply and trades near its IPO price at a forward multiple below the peer median. That combination would ordinarily build a buy case.

What holds us back from conviction is the quality of the deceleration and the absence of a forcing catalyst. The CEO's own warning of a stretched consumer in a category sold as defensive is the most credible signal in the data, and it explains the de-rating better than any mispricing argument. Per-customer spend growth halving to +2.2% is a real deterioration, not noise. Layered on top are heavy stock comp, a controlling-shareholder overhang that has used buybacks as an exit vehicle at unfavorable prices, and an options market pricing imminent stress. The downside asymmetry has narrowed enough that we would not short this, and the cash generation is real enough that we would not avoid it. But the bull case requires the consumer to stabilize, and we do not yet have that evidence. We rate CHWY a HOLD and would revisit on the next print with specific attention to the customer-growth and per-customer-spend lines.
