**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is a pressure-pumping (hydraulic fracturing) services provider whose core business has been in a multi-year cyclical decline — revenue fell from $4.75B in 2023 to $4.01B in 2025, and operating income collapsed from $761M to $73M over the same span, a 1.8% operating margin. The stock has more than doubled off its 52-week low of $9.90 not on the frac business but on Liberty Power Innovations (LPI), the company's pivot into distributed power generation targeting AI data-center load. The equity now trades as a call option on that transition, and the fundamental debate has almost entirely migrated away from completions economics.

On one side, estimate revisions are among the most violent in our coverage — next-year EPS consensus rose +660% over 90 days (from -$0.15 to +$0.87) and +58.2% in the last 30 days alone, the company beat by triple-digit percentages in three of the last four quarters, and the July 2026 SLB strategic alliance provides serious third-party validation of the power strategy. On the other side, Liberty raised $1.295B of 0% convertible notes before a single binding end-user power contract exists, management has openly stated the team "has not directly engaged in the distributed power solutions business before," and 2025 net income of $148M was flattered by $162.6M of non-cash mark-to-market gains on Oklo and Tamboran equity stakes — the underlying business's profitability was materially weaker once you strip the accounting.

We rate LBRT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted up (4.2 → 5.3 across prior reports, now 5.25), reflecting genuine improvement in the balance sheet cash position and the estimate trajectory. The central question — whether LPI converts a non-binding 3 GW-by-2029 pipeline into contracted cash flow before the leverage and dilution overhang bites — is unresolved, and the stock has already priced in a meaningful chunk of the bull case. We do not have the evidence to underwrite either tail with conviction.

## Company Overview

Liberty grew from a single frac fleet in 2011 to roughly 40 active fleets by year-end 2025, making it one of the largest North American completions providers alongside Halliburton, Patterson-UTI, ProFrac, and ProPetro. The core offering set spans hydraulic fracturing, wireline, proppant delivery and logistics (PropX), two Permian sand mines, CNG delivery, and a data-analytics business (FracTrends, ~500 patents). It reports as a single segment, employs ~5,800 people, and operates across all major North American shale basins plus Australia's Beetaloo Basin.

The strategic story is the LPI pivot. Liberty is building out modular distributed power generation (Forte), power-quality management for AI loads (Tempo), and grid integration (Chorus), positioning itself as a "Power-as-a-Service" provider to hyperscalers that want to bypass grid interconnection queues. This slots directly into the data-center power bottleneck theme: ERCOT projects Texas grid demand could quadruple by 2032, and hyperscalers are increasingly turning to distributed self-generation. The 3/2025 IMG Energy Solutions acquisition (~$19.6M) added power-system engineering, software controls, and utility-interconnection expertise. The July 2026 SLB alliance — a partnership for modular infrastructure plus integrated power generation for data centers, not an acquisition by either party — is the strongest external endorsement to date.

A governance discontinuity sits underneath all of this. Founder/CEO/Chairman Christopher Wright resigned in February 2025 to become U.S. Secretary of Energy in the Trump administration. Ron Gusek, the former President, was promoted to CEO, and William Kimble became non-executive Chairman. The political tie is unusual — the company's founder now runs the federal agency overseeing energy policy — and cuts both ways as a reputational tailwind and a first-time-CEO execution risk.

## Financial Analysis

The core business is contracting. Revenue declined 7% in 2025, driven by pricing rather than activity (volumes were roughly flat). The operating leverage worked violently in reverse: operating income fell from $389M (2024) to $73M (2025), and Adjusted EBITDA dropped 31% from $921.6M to $634.1M. FY2025 free cash flow was just $14.1M — operating cash flow of $609.6M against capex of $595.5M — the completions engine barely self-funded before any power capex ramp.

The quality of reported 2025 net income deserves emphasis. $162.6M of it came from non-cash mark-to-market gains on the Oklo (nuclear) and Tamboran (Beetaloo) equity stakes. Strip those out, and the underlying business was substantially weaker, given operating income of only $73M. Screens showing "profitable" materially overstate operating reality; the reported EPS of $0.89 and 26x trailing P/E are the wrong lens.

Q1 2026 showed modest sequential improvement in the frac backdrop: revenue of $1.02B (+4% YoY on better activity and utilization), operating income of $22.3M, and net income of $22.6M ($0.14 EPS, a 205.6% beat vs. the -$0.13 estimate). But Adjusted EBITDA fell year-over-year to $125.9M from $168.2M as materials and personnel costs outpaced revenue — margin pressure persists even as the top line stabilizes.

The balance sheet was transformed in Q1 2026 by the power buildout financing. Cash jumped from $28M to $699M after Liberty issued the 0% convertible senior notes (2031 Notes: $770M at a $34.50 conversion price, 32.5% premium; 2032 Notes: $525M at $37.44, 30% premium). It bought capped calls ($109.3M + $77.2M) that push the effective dilution strike to roughly $65-72. Total debt rose from ~$247M to $1.28B, and the $750M revolver was fully repaid with note proceeds. The 2/3/26 credit-agreement amendment permits up to $600M of additional bridge indebtedness by 6/30/26 and raised the convertible basket to $600M, indicating that more financing for the power buildout is coming. The company was in compliance with its 3.5x max-leverage / 2.5x senior-leverage covenants as of 3/31/26, and the Altman Z of 2.91 (grey zone) and Piotroski of 5 were both computed off pre-financing FY2025 data, so the current leverage profile is materially higher than those screens suggest.

The financial-health picture is mixed. The cash cushion is real and buys time; the zero-coupon structure means no near-term cash interest drag. But this is a company adding $1.3B of debt against a business that generated $14M of FCF, spending capital ahead of contracted revenue. That combination — improving liquidity, deteriorating capital efficiency (Adjusted ROCE fell from 42% in 2023 to 19% in 2024 to 8.1% in 2025), and rising financing dependence — supports a score in the middle of the range, not higher.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth stories run in parallel, and they point in opposite directions.

The frac base is in structural decline on pricing, though the industry is entering a tighter-balance phase. Persistent pricing pressure has driven equipment attrition and underinvestment, leaving fewer crews to meet incremental demand — a setup for pricing recovery if activity rises. WTI averaged $65.45 in 2025 (down from ~$77) but improved to $72.74 in Q1 2026, with a brief spike to ~$99.85 in April 2026 tied to the Iran/Hormuz disruption. The completions business is unlikely to return to 2023 earnings power without a durable commodity and activity upcycle.

The power story is where the growth thesis lives. LPI targets ~3 GW of deployed capacity by 2029, and the estimate-revision data reflects the market rebuilding its earnings model around it (current-year consensus swung from -$0.35 to +$0.33). This is the kind of revision velocity — well above the +30% threshold — that historically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to a fundamental turn.

The guardrail on that signal is that it applies most cleanly to turnaround stocks that are cheap relative to the improving estimates. Here, the +$0.87 next-year estimate implies ~27x forward on the current $23.84 price — not cheap, and the estimate itself rests on power revenue that is not yet contracted. The Vantage Data Centers agreement is a development agreement with a 400 MW firm reservation for 2027 delivery, not a binding end-user power purchase; the 330 MW Texas deal is a preliminary reservation still subject to a final binding contract. The estimate revisions are pricing in execution that has not yet happened. We weight the revision signal as supportive but not decisive — it is real earnings momentum, but a large portion of it is analyst extrapolation of a pipeline rather than reported contracted backlog.

Growth prospects are genuinely above-average if LPI converts. We score this at 6.2, reflecting a credible multi-year power opportunity confirmed by the SLB alliance and ERCOT demand projections, discounted for the fact that the near-term numbers depend on unsigned contracts.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the hardest call here, and it is where we deviate most from the bullish reading of the estimate-revision data.

On trailing metrics the stock is not cheap: 26x trailing P/E on EPS that was inflated by non-cash gains. On forward metrics it screens at ~27x the current $0.87 next-year consensus and ~72x the $0.33 current-year figure — both rich for a company whose core operating income is $73M annualized. Peer medians in oil & gas equipment and services sit at 15.8x P/E (WHD 55x is a data-center-power comparable outlier; OII 11.8x, TDW 12.4x, WFRD 15.8x are the traditional-services cohort). Liberty trades at a premium to the traditional services group and is being valued as a power/infrastructure name, not an oilfield-services name.

The bull would argue this is a cycle-adjusted / optionality story and that trailing P/E is meaningless at a completions trough. There is merit to that. If LPI hits even a fraction of 3 GW at attractive Power-as-a-Service economics, the earnings base transforms and 27x forward is cheap. But two things restrain us from awarding a high valuation score. First, the estimate revisions already embed the optimistic path — the +660% revision means the market has moved from pricing decline to pricing a successful power transition, so the mispricing that would justify a high valuation score is largely gone. Second, our own discipline treats "cheap on a low multiple" as a warning rather than a buy signal absent a specific, independent catalyst; here the multiple is not low, and the catalyst (binding power contracts) is prospective, not confirmed.

The convertible structure adds a valuation anchor of sorts: the notes convert roughly 45-57% above the current price, and the capped calls extend the effective dilution point higher. The market is implicitly pricing a path toward the mid-$30s for the converts to matter, but that is a management/financing signal, not intrinsic value.

We score valuation at 4.7 — below neutral. The market is not obviously wrong in either direction. The optionality is priced, the base business is worth less than the current cap on operating fundamentals, and the asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not present at $24.

## Competitive Landscape

In completions, Liberty is a scaled, technology-forward operator (FracTrends, ~500 patents, PropX logistics, two owned sand mines) competing against Halliburton, Patterson-UTI, ProFrac, and ProPetro. Its position is solid but not widening — the whole subsector is fighting pricing pressure, and Liberty's declining margins mirror the industry.

In power, Liberty is a new entrant against a fundamentally different and better-capitalized competitive set: utilities, GE Vernova, Caterpillar-based genset providers, and other distributed-power specialists with deeper relationships and balance sheets. Management's own disclosure that the team has no prior experience in distributed power is the honest read on competitive standing — Liberty is buying and partnering its way to capability (IMG acquisition, SLB alliance) rather than leveraging an incumbent moat.

The SLB alliance is the single most important competitive development. It pairs Liberty's modular-infrastructure and logistics capability with SLB's engineering scale and hyperscaler credibility, materially strengthening Liberty's standing as a serious data-center power provider rather than a speculative pivot. That, plus the ERCOT demand backdrop and the hyperscaler shift toward distributed self-generation, is what earns competitive position a 5.8 — improving trajectory in a large structural market, tempered by the reality that Liberty is a challenger with no proven contracts against entrenched, deeper-pocketed rivals.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution risk on the power pivot is the central risk.** The 3 GW-by-2029 plan is not under contract. Management explicitly states it has not run this business before. Sales cycles for hyperscaler power are long and uncertain, and Liberty is committing capital ahead of firm revenue.

**Financing dependence and leverage.** Total debt went from ~$247M to $1.28B in a quarter, with authority for another $600M of bridge debt. The company plans further debt, project-level, and possibly equity financing. Revenue to service and justify this is not expected until roughly 2027-2029. If the power thesis slips, Liberty is a levered, low-FCF frac company with a maturity structure it built for a growth story that did not arrive.

**Cyclical core in decline.** Frac pricing pressure, equipment cannibalization, and full exposure to E&P capex cycles, OPEC+ decisions, and commodity volatility remain. The mark-to-market equity stakes (Oklo, Tamboran) inject earnings volatility unrelated to operations.

**Customer concentration.** Top 5 customers were 39% of 2025 revenue; Occidental and XTO (ExxonMobil) each exceeded 10%.

**New regulatory surface.** Entering FERC/NERC/ERCOT/PUCT-regulated power markets is an entirely new compliance regime with its own execution and legal risk.

**TRA overhang.** A $66.9M Tax Receivable Agreement liability could trigger an accelerated lump-sum payment on a change of control.

We score risk factors at 4.3 — below neutral — because the combination of unproven execution, front-loaded leverage, and a business whose reported profits are propped by non-operating gains represents material, not-fully-priced downside if the power narrative disappoints. The score improved from prior reports (3.0 → 5.0 → now 4.3, a slight pullback) as the cash raise removed near-term liquidity risk, but the fundamental binary — power contracts convert or they don't — keeps this below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 69.3% against 59.3% one-year historical vol (rich by ~10 points), the term structure is in backwardation (69.3% near vs. 60.7% at six months), and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±16.8% — consistent with the July 22 Q2 earnings catalyst falling just outside this data window. ATM IV collapsed from 132.6% to 69.3% and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 1.68 to 0.04 since mid-June, indicating that heavy prior hedging/bearish positioning has unwound and the balance now leans neutral-to-constructive. Short interest is elevated at 12.8% of float (3.8 days to cover) — meaningful but not squeeze-territory. The balanced put/call skew and the IV crush suggest the market is not aggressively betting on a downside break; it is pricing a wide two-sided move around the power-contract and Q2 newsflow.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$40, +68%)
LPI converts its 3 GW pipeline into binding, high-margin Power-as-a-Service contracts, with the Vantage 400 MW firm reservation delivering in 2027 and the SLB alliance driving hyperscaler wins. The zero-coupon converts prove to be cheap growth capital, the frac base stabilizes into a tighter-supply pricing recovery, and Liberty re-rates as a distributed-power infrastructure company at a multiple untethered from oilfield-services comps. Next-year EPS of $0.87 proves to be a waypoint, not a peak, and the converts push into the money. This is the $40 high analyst target and the mid-$30s convert-conversion zone.

### Bear Case (target ~$16-18, -25% to -33%)
Power contracts slip, stay non-binding, or convert at margins below the buildout assumptions. Liberty is left as a levered frac company with $1.3B+ of debt, near-zero FCF, an 8.1% and falling return on capital, and a stock that gave back the entire power-optionality premium. Estimate revisions reverse as fast as they rose. The frac cycle disappoints on soft oil, and the stock retraces toward the mid-teens where it traded before the power narrative took hold in late 2025.

This is a HOLD rather than a directional call because both cases are plausible, the stock at $24 sits roughly in the middle, and the resolving evidence (binding power contracts, Q2 detail, the June 25 material agreement) has not yet been fully digested.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 print (released 7/22/2026, just outside this data window) and any 8-K disclosure of binding power contracts. The June 25, 2026 Item 1.01 material agreement warrants immediate review as a potential catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of a binding end-user power purchase agreement (not a development agreement or reservation) with a hyperscaler, ideally with disclosed MW, pricing, and margin economics.
- Evidence that LPI revenue is beginning to appear in reported results with EBITDA margins above the frac base, validating the Power-as-a-Service unit economics.
- Frac-segment pricing turn confirmed by two consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, which would de-risk the base business independent of power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Deployment of the $600M bridge facility to fund power capex without corresponding binding contracts — this would confirm capital committed ahead of demand.
- A reversal in estimate revisions (next-year consensus falling back below $0.50) signaling the power thesis is losing analyst support.
- Any dividend cut or equity raise below the convert conversion price, signaling financing stress.
- Deterioration in the frac base below breakeven operating income excluding equity-stake gains.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy is a legitimately interesting reinvention story, and the estimate-revision velocity plus the SLB validation are not signals to dismiss. But the current price already reflects a successful power transition that has not yet been contracted, the reported profitability of the core business is thin and flattered by non-operating gains, and the balance sheet has taken on $1.3B of debt against a business that generated $14M of free cash flow. The risk/reward at $24 is close to symmetric, and the resolving evidence lies ahead.

We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the improving score trajectory reflects real progress on liquidity and estimates, but the bar for upgrading to BUY is a binding power contract with disclosed economics, and the bar for downgrading to AVOID is confirmation that capital is being committed ahead of demand. Until one of those resolves, we let the position sit and reassess on Q2 results and power-contract newsflow. We would become buyers on either a binding-contract catalyst or a pullback toward the mid-to-high teens that restores asymmetry to the base business.
