**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Moelis & Company is a pure-play, advisory-only independent investment bank whose earnings are a high-beta bet on the M&A and restructuring cycle. The model is capital-light and conflict-free (no lending, no proprietary trading, no balance-sheet risk), which produces exceptional returns on equity (45.7% in 2025) and heavy free-cash conversion (FCF margin 35.6%, ~$540M) when deal flow is strong, but also produced an operating loss in the 2023 trough because management protects its talent base rather than cutting compensation proportionally. The 84%→67% swing in the compensation ratio from 2023 to 2025 is the mechanism: incremental revenue drops to the bottom line at a high rate on the way up and reverses violently on the way down.

The balance sheet is the clearest positive. At year-end 2025 the firm held roughly $849M of cash and short-term sovereign-debt investments against zero borrowings, funding a $2.60/share dividend (~3.9% yield) and a newly authorized $300M buyback (~6% of market cap), 1.9M shares of which were retired in Q1 2026. This is real, growing, well-covered capital return that provides genuine downside support. The Altman Z of 2.17 that screeners flag is a false distress signal — it reflects operating-lease liabilities and an advisory asset mix, not financial fragility at a debt-free firm earning 45% on equity.

The problem is that none of this is mispriced. MC trades at ~22.8x earnings, essentially in line with the independent-advisory peer median (~22.0x), while near-term estimate revisions are drifting negative (current-quarter -2.1%, next-year -3.0% over 90 days), Q1 2026 missed consensus on both EPS and revenue, and reported revenue growth of +4% trailed the +7% rise in completed large-deal count, an early hint the franchise is running slightly behind the industry pace. With a fresh sell-side downgrade taking the sell count from 1 to 2 and consensus targets clustered near the current price ($71.5 mean, $70.5 median), there is no valuation asymmetry to reward. We rate MC a HOLD on an income basis: hold for the yield and buyback support, not for near-term appreciation. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that stance.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2007 by Ken Moelis and public since April 2014, Moelis is a single-segment global independent investment bank offering M&A/strategic advisory, restructuring and recapitalizations, capital-markets advisory, and a rapidly scaling private capital advisory practice (fund secondaries, GP-led continuation vehicles, primary fundraising). As of early 2026 the firm employed ~1,416 people including ~1,014 advisory bankers and 178 Managing Directors across 20+ offices. It has advised on more than $5.5 trillion of transactions since inception.

Moelis competes against five other publicly traded independent advisory firms (Evercore, Houlihan Lokey, Lazard, Perella Weinberg, and PJT Partners) and against the bulge brackets. Its differentiation is the conflict-free, capital-light structure and a discretionary (non-commission) compensation model that supports a collaborative culture. The 10-K highlights low client concentration (top 10 transactions ~18% of 2025 revenue) and a built-in countercyclical hedge: restructuring and liability-management work cushions M&A downturns within the same franchise.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year picture is a V-shaped cyclical recovery. Revenue fell into the 2023 trough, then rebounded: $854.7M (2023) → $1,194.5M (2024, +40%) → $1,516.8M (2025, +27%). Operating income swung from a $(40.4)M loss in 2023 to $172.9M in 2024 to $273.9M in 2025. Net income moved from $(27.5)M to $151.5M to $259.6M over the same span. The compensation ratio (the dominant, largely variable cost) compressed from 84% to 69% to 67%, and total operating expense/revenue fell from 105% to 82%. Because comp is variable, incremental revenue drops to the bottom line at a high rate on the upswing, and the reverse happens in a downturn when management chooses to protect talent rather than gut pay.

The balance sheet is the standout. Year-end 2025 held ~$508.6M cash plus $340.2M of short-term sovereign-debt investments (~$849M liquid) against zero debt. ROE was 45.7%, ROA 14.9%, and FCF ~$540M (35.6% margin). The Piotroski F-score is maxed on the applicable signals (positive NI, positive ROA, positive OCF, OCF > NI). A debt-free firm generating 45% ROE and holding $849M of liquid assets is not a bankruptcy candidate.

Q1 2026 warrants careful reading. Revenue was $319.8M (+4% YoY), which management framed as a "record Q1," and pretax income rose 7% to $46.2M with the comp ratio improving to 66%. GAAP net income fell 21% to $42.3M, but that decline is entirely a tax-timing artifact: Q1 2025 carried a $10.7M tax benefit versus a $3.9M tax expense this year. Underlying operating performance improved. The seasonal Q1 cash drain to $153.7M is normal, reflecting bonuses, partner tax distributions, a $32.2M TRA payment, and ~$117M of buybacks all landing in the quarter. The genuine watch item is that Q1 revenue/EPS missed consensus (~5%/15%) and +4% revenue growth trailed the +7% rise in completed >$100M deals, with management flagging AI-driven softness in software-sector M&A.

## Growth Analysis

The 2024–2025 recovery is largely behind the stock. Forward growth depends on the deal cycle broadening in 2026: strategic acquirers pursuing scale deals, financial sponsors under pressure to monetize a large backlog, record GP-led secondaries feeding private capital advisory, and rising liability-management/restructuring activity. Moelis is investing into this — 8 MD hires in Q1 2026 and continued expansion of the private capital advisory practice, now described as a core pillar.

The near-term data does not yet confirm re-acceleration. Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current-quarter -2.1% (30d and 90d), current-year -0.5% (30d) and flat (90d), and next-year down -1.0% (30d) / -3.0% (90d). The one bright spot is next-quarter, revised +1.4% (30d) / +3.0% (90d). This is a franchise growing roughly in line with a maturing up-cycle, and possibly slightly behind the industry pace. The earnings track record is respectable — three beats and one miss over the last four quarters — but the most recent print (Q1 2026, -11.5% surprise) breaks a streak of large beats (+41%, +14%, +34%), which reduces visibility. On balance, growth prospects are moderate: the up-cycle is real but decelerating in its contribution, and consensus is trimming rather than raising forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

MC trades at ~22.8x trailing/forward earnings (they are effectively equal here given advisory earnings visibility) on a ~$5.0B market cap. Against the independent-advisory peer set — PIPR 19.7x, PJT 22.0x, HLI 21.8x, BGC 30.4x, SNEX 23.0x, peer median 22.0x — Moelis sits right at the median. There is no discount to exploit and no clear reason the market is mispricing the stock. A multiple in line with peers, on softening estimates, is not a value opportunity.

The dividend yield of ~3.9% is well above the peer-median 0.7% and is the most defensible part of the valuation case. It is covered by ~$540M of FCF and supported by a debt-free balance sheet, and the $300M buyback adds a further ~6% of market cap in potential share retirement. That yield-plus-buyback profile is genuine downside support and justifies owning the stock at these levels; it does not, by itself, argue for appreciation.

Analyst targets bracket the current price rather than pointing higher: low $60, high $86, mean $71.5, median $70.5, against a current price of ~$67. This is a peer-multiple stock with falling estimates, so consensus targets are fair anchors rather than stale lowballs. The cycle-adjusted case cuts both ways: 2025 earnings are near cyclical-peak comp-ratio efficiency (67%), so trailing EPS is more likely at the high end of the normalized range than the low end, which caps the "cheap on normalized earnings" argument. We score valuation slightly below neutral: no asymmetry, a fair-to-full multiple on possibly peak-cycle margins, offset by real yield support.

## Competitive Landscape

Moelis holds a solid, defensible position within a structurally advantaged model. The independent-advisory format continues to take share from bulge brackets, and the built-in M&A/restructuring counter-cyclicality is a genuine structural edge over pure M&A shops. Low client concentration reduces single-client risk, and the private capital advisory build-out addresses a growing GP-led secondaries market.

The trajectory, though, is more stable than improving. The independent-advisory space is increasingly crowded — Evercore, Houlihan Lokey, PJT, Perella Weinberg, plus newer entrants — which pressures fees and MD recruiting economics. The Q1 2026 signal that revenue growth lagged the industry completed-deal count is a mild negative on the share-trajectory question, and management's flag on AI-driven software M&A softness is a segment-specific headwind. The franchise's asset walks out the door each night; retention of senior MDs, and of the founder principals specifically, is the core competitive vulnerability, made harder by rising legal hostility to non-competes. Competitive position is stable-to-modestly-positive, not clearly widening.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risks are cyclical volatility and governance concentration. Nearly all revenue is transaction-contingent advisory fees recognized at milestones outside the firm's control, so there is no recurring base to smooth quarters, and comp-ratio inflexibility in downturns (the 2023 operating loss) means shareholders absorb earnings collapse in troughs. This is a leveraged bet on the deal cycle and should be sized accordingly.

Governance is the defining structural feature. Ken Moelis holds ~37% voting power through Class B super-voting shares, and the January 2026 Delaware Supreme Court reversal restored the entrenched approval-rights/director-vacancy provisions that the Chancery court had voided, a win for management continuity but a constraint on minority-shareholder rights and take-out optionality. The FY2025 CEO compensation spike to $35.2M (from ~$7.6M) tied to the founder-to-co-founder CEO transition is a governance flag, though it is performance-linked and appears to be a one-time retention grant. The Tax Receivable Agreement (~$269–301M owed to MDs) could accelerate on a change of control. Two recent litigation resolutions were favorable (Delaware Supreme Court reversal; dismissal of the Moelis entities from the Atlas Crest/Archer matter), which reduces the legal tail. The CEO succession itself was orderly (co-founder Navid Mahmoodzadegan to CEO, Ken Moelis to Executive Chairman), a continuity positive.

These risks are largely known and priced, which supports a middling risk score rather than a low one. The main un-priced tail is the combination of a decelerating up-cycle with the possibility that a macro shock (rate re-acceleration, a stalled deal cycle) collides with the firm's comp inflexibility, a scenario the 2023 experience makes concrete.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.4% is rich versus 36.7% historical volatility (a ~7.7pp spread), so the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.4%. Put/call skew is balanced (2.4%), the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.10 (light put demand), and the term structure is flat (near 44.4% ≈ far 43.4%), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a single catalyst. ATM IV ticked down 3.5pp intraday (47.9%→44.4%). The 6-month tenor shows a mildly negative skew (-4.3%), a modest hedging tilt further out. Overall the options market is neutral-to-mildly-cautious — it neither confirms a strong bear thesis nor contradicts the fundamental HOLD; the rich IV argues against paying up for near-term optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80–86):** The 2026 up-cycle broadens as expected — sponsors monetize backlogs, GP-led secondaries hit records feeding private capital advisory, restructuring/liability-management demand rises, and the 8 new MDs plus prior hires convert to fee growth. The comp ratio holds at ~66–67% or improves, operating leverage re-accelerates EPS, and the $300M buyback compounds per-share value against a debt-free balance sheet. Estimate revisions turn positive and the multiple re-rates modestly above peers on demonstrated share gains. This maps to the high analyst target of $86.

**Bear Case ($52–56):** The deal cycle stalls — a rate re-acceleration or macro shock freezes M&A — and Moelis's comp inflexibility drives the earnings decline harder than peers, echoing 2023. Revenue continues lagging the industry deal count, AI-driven software M&A softness spreads, and MD retention frays as competitors poach into a slowing market. The stock retraces toward the 52-week low ($51.06) and low analyst target ($60). This is a plausible ~18–22% downside.

**Base Case (~$68):** Neither scenario dominates over the next 12 months. Revenue grows in the low-to-mid single digits, roughly in line with a maturing up-cycle, the comp ratio holds near 66–67%, and the ~3.9% dividend plus buyback provide total-return support in the high single digits with limited multiple re-rating. The stock oscillates around consensus targets (mean $71.5).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income basis with a 12-month horizon. The reassessment cadence is quarterly earnings, with the next natural checkpoint the Q2 2026 print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision inflection — current-year and next-year consensus turning positive (rising >5% over 90 days) rather than the current -0.5% to -3.0% drift.
- Revenue growth re-accelerating to meet or exceed the industry completed-deal pace (i.e., closing the +4% vs +7% gap), confirming share gains rather than lag.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s (near the low analyst target and prior support), which would push the dividend yield toward ~4.3% and create genuine valuation asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed stall in the deal cycle (two consecutive quarters of declining announced-transaction activity) that would expose the comp-ratio inflexibility.
- Comp ratio expanding back above 70% on flat or falling revenue, signaling the 2023 dynamic returning.
- Departure of a senior MD cohort or the founder principals, or a dividend/buyback cut signaling deteriorating cash generation.

**Base case target:** ~$72, derived from roughly the peer-median 22x applied to ~$3.20–3.25 of normalized EPS, plus the ~3.9% dividend — total return in the high single digits with limited re-rating. Bull $86, bear $56. Upside to base ~7% plus yield; upside to bull ~28%; downside to bear ~17% from the current ~$67.

## Conclusion

Moelis is a high-quality, capital-light franchise with a fortress balance sheet, industry-leading returns on equity, and a well-covered, growing capital-return program that provides real downside support. Those attributes justify owning the stock and warrant a strong financial-health score. But the investment case stops short of BUY: the stock trades at a peer-median multiple on possibly peak-cycle comp efficiency, near-term estimates are being trimmed rather than raised, Q1 missed consensus, and revenue is running slightly behind the industry deal pace. There is no valuation asymmetry and no confirmed earnings inflection to underwrite appreciation. The recurring HOLD stance is confirmed, not stale — the trajectory of scores (5.8 → 6.2 → 6.1) reflects a business whose fundamentals have improved but whose price already reflects that improvement.

We rate MC a HOLD (income). The thesis is the ~3.9% dividend plus buyback support against a debt-free balance sheet, with a call option on the deal cycle broadening. We would upgrade to BUY on a positive estimate-revision turn or a pullback into the low-$60s, and downgrade to AVOID on evidence the up-cycle is stalling into the firm's comp inflexibility.
