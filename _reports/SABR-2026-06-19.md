**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sabre is one of three major Global Distribution System (GDS) operators worldwide, alongside Amadeus and Travelport, running a two-sided marketplace that connects airlines and other travel suppliers to travel agencies and corporate buyers. The provided sector tag ("Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines / Consumer Discretionary") is incorrect: Sabre is a B2B travel-technology and software-infrastructure company that divested its hospitality software business to TPG in July 2025. The right comparison set is Amadeus and Travelport, not lodging or cruise operators, and this misclassification distorts any naive peer screen.

The operating business is stabilizing. Q1'26 revenue grew 8% YoY to $760.3M, operating income rose to $115.9M from $91.4M, and adjusted EBITDA reached $158.7M (management cited "normalized" $169M, +21% YoY). The Q1 print beat on revenue and adjusted EPS and pushed the stock up 16–22% on the day. The balance sheet is distressed: ~$4.27B net debt, a stockholders' deficit of -$1.03B, recent debt carrying 10.75%–11.125% coupons, roughly $490M of annual cash interest, and an Altman Z-Score near 0.85, firmly in the distress zone. Q1'26 free cash flow was -$155M, and management guides full-year 2026 FCF to roughly negative $70M.

The equity is effectively a levered option on the company's ability to refinance ~$4.3B of debt down from double-digit coupons while growing EBITDA fast enough to overcome cash interest. That can work, but it is fragile, and the leading earnings indicator is moving the wrong way: current-year EPS estimates have been cut 91.7% over 30 days and 95.3% over 90 days. We maintain AVOID, consistent with the prior two reports, and rate the score slightly lower on continued estimate deterioration partly offset by the EBITDA stabilization and the Constellation Software governance development. Given the security of the franchise's cash-flow core and meaningful refinancing/M&A optionality, this is an avoid rather than an active short.

## Company Overview

Sabre runs a GDS marketplace connecting travel suppliers (primarily airlines, plus hotels, car rental, rail, cruise, tour operators) with travel buyers (travel management companies, OTAs, corporate travel departments). Following the July 2025 sale of Hospitality Solutions, Sabre reports as a single segment with two revenue lines, renamed in Q1'26 to reflect a "Sabre Mosaic" / "AI-native" rebrand:

- **Marketplace** (formerly Distribution): ~81% of revenue ($618M of $760M in Q1'26). Transaction-based booking fees on the GDS, plus agency solutions (payments via Conferma, media). Revenue keys off booking volumes, not ticket prices.
- **Airline Technology** (formerly IT Solutions): ~19% ($142M in Q1'26). Recurring SaaS/hosted fees for airline reservation and retailing systems (PSS), priced on passengers boarded, plus license and professional-services fees.

The moat rests on network effects (deep airline content plus a large agency subscriber base) and high switching costs (airline PSS migrations run 12–18 months). The moat is eroding at the edges as airlines push New Distribution Capability (NDC) and direct channels to bypass GDS economics, and as consolidation among both airlines (buyer power) and travel agencies (incentive-cost inflation) compresses GDS margins from both sides. Sabre is the most financially constrained of the big three; Amadeus is investment-grade with far lower leverage.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature is a distressed, over-leveraged balance sheet against a modestly recovering operating business.

**Leverage and solvency.** Net debt is approximately $4.27B ($4.44B face) at 3/31/26, against a stockholders' deficit of -$1.03B and an accumulated deficit of -$3.79B. Altman Z-Score is roughly 0.85, in the distress zone. Interest expense of about $123M per quarter (~$490M+ annualized) consumes virtually all operating income; Q1 operating income of $115.9M sat below the run-rate quarterly interest burden.

**Cost of debt.** Recent issues carry 10.75%–11.125% coupons (e.g., $1.325B 11.125% notes due 2030; $1.0B 11.125% notes due 2029). Through 2024–2025 the company executed a near-continuous series of refinancings and exchanges to push out maturities, recognizing roughly $85M of loss on extinguishment in 2025. The wall is now pushed mostly to 2029–2030, with two near-term items: $150M Exchangeable Notes due August 2026 (cash settlement; $4.50 conversion price versus a $1.88 stock) and a $203M securitization facility due March 2027.

**Operating trend.** Income from continuing operations was +$9.4M (vs. -$3.4M) and direct billable bookings rose 5.1%. The divested hospitality business removed a lower-margin line, commercial wins are adding bookings, and a ~$65M "inflation offset" restructuring program is holding tech and SG&A costs roughly flat versus 2025.

**Cash generation is the problem.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was -$134M (worse than -$64M a year prior), driven by +$67M higher cash interest and $19M of restructuring severance. Management guides FY2026 operating cash flow to only about +$10M. A company carrying $4.3B of debt that cannot generate positive free cash flow is the central risk. The 2025 GAAP net income was flattered by a one-time $723M after-tax gain on the Hospitality sale and is not indicative of run-rate earnings.

**Liquidity.** Cash was $643.6M at 3/31/26, down from $791.6M at year-end 2025, a ~$148M draw in a single quarter. Management asserts sufficient liquidity for at least 12 months but explicitly warns the negative impact on results and liquidity "is material" and "may continue." A May 19, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03/3.02/8.01 — material agreement, new financial obligation, unregistered equity sales) signals another financing transaction, most plausibly an early refinance of the August 2026 Exchangeable Notes, likely with an equity or equity-linked component that implies dilution.

The chain to watch: cash interest of ~$490M against adjusted EBITDA of roughly $585M (FY26 outlook) leaves thin coverage; any softening in air-booking volumes flows directly to EBITDA, which flows directly to the ability to service and refinance the stack. The fixed interest burden makes the equity acutely sensitive to volume shocks.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line recovery is modest and the forward earnings trajectory is deteriorating. Management expects only low-to-mid-single-digit air bookings growth for 2026 and flagged late-Q1 headwinds from Middle East conflict and higher fuel prices suppressing bookings, expected to persist into Q2. Q2 pro forma adjusted EBITDA is guided to roughly $130M, a sequential step down from Q1's $158.7M, consistent with that booking softness.

The leading indicator is negative. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised down 91.7% over 30 days and 95.3% over 90 days (current-year consensus now $0.01 versus $0.21 ninety days ago). Next-year estimates are down 46.3% over 30 days and 42.2% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate has gone from +$0.05 ninety days ago to -$0.04 now. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate profile that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple. The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over four quarters, with a strong Q1'26 beat ($0.06 actual vs. -$0.03 estimate) but a -122.6% miss in Q3'25, so the forward estimates carry low visibility.

The structural growth question is whether GDS volumes hold up against disintermediation. NDC and direct-channel adoption by airlines is a slow erosion of GDS transaction economics, not a cliff, but it caps the franchise's secular growth at low-single-digit volumes in a normal year. The "AI-native" Sabre Mosaic rebrand is positioning rather than a demonstrated new revenue engine. We see a stabilizing, low-growth cash-flow franchise, not a growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional equity multiples are not meaningful here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-3.0x) on negative GAAP EPS; the negative stockholders' equity makes price-to-book uninformative. The provided peer set (PRSU 43.9x, HGV 27.8x, MTN 31.2x, TNL 20.6x, median 31.2x) is the wrong cohort: these are lodging and timeshare operators, not GDS or travel-software infrastructure. Benchmarking SABR against a 31x lodging P/E would be analytically wrong.

The economically honest frame is enterprise value relative to EBITDA and, more importantly, to free cash flow. With ~$743M equity market cap plus ~$4.27B net debt, enterprise value is roughly $5.0B against FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance of ~$585M, about 8.6x EV/EBITDA. That is not obviously cheap for a low-growth franchise, and it is the wrong anchor because EBITDA overstates cash available to equity: cash interest of ~$490M means the equity sees almost none of that EBITDA. On free cash flow, the company is guided to burn ~$70M this year, so there is no positive equity cash yield to support the price.

The equity is a residual claim on a heavily leveraged capital structure. Small changes in the enterprise value, driven by EBITDA trajectory or refinancing terms, produce large percentage swings in the thin equity sliver, which is what the price action shows (the stock has traded between $0.81 and $3.52 over the past year, a 4x range). A low absolute price ($1.88) is not a valuation argument; the relevant question is whether the levered equity option has positive expected value, and falling estimates plus negative FCF guidance argue it does not at this entry. We score valuation 3.0: a compressed-multiple security where the compression reflects genuine structural impairment (negative equity, distress-zone solvency) rather than a mispricing with an identifiable catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Sabre sits in a three-way GDS oligopoly (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport) with durable network effects and high switching costs. The competitive trajectory is the issue, not the level. Airlines are pushing NDC and direct distribution to bypass GDS transaction fees; supplier consolidation increases airline buyer power; agency consolidation inflates the incentive consideration GDSs must pay subscribers. This is a margin vise specific to GDSs, and it is structural.

Within the group, Sabre is the weakest balance sheet by a wide margin. Amadeus is investment-grade and can invest through cycles, absorb pricing pressure, and weather downturns in ways Sabre cannot while carrying its debt load at double-digit coupons. The financial constraint is itself a competitive disadvantage: capital that should fund product and content acquisition is diverted to interest. The cash-flow core of the GDS toll is durable enough that Constellation Software, a disciplined serial software acquirer, built a stake and took a board seat, a credible third-party signal that the underlying franchise has value beneath the leverage. We rate competitive position 4.0: a real moat that is eroding at the edges, held by the most financially constrained of the three players.

## Risk Assessment

**Solvency and refinancing risk is paramount.** Negative equity, Z-Score 0.85, ~$490M annual cash interest, negative FCF guidance, and reliance on capital markets the 10-Q itself describes as "constrained." A credit downgrade or a market closure would be existential. Covenants restrict additional secured debt, so future borrowing is likely unsecured and more expensive.

**Volume sensitivity to exogenous shocks.** Revenue keys off air-booking volumes. Given the fixed interest burden, any travel-volume shock is acute at the equity level.

**Structural disintermediation.** NDC and direct channels, plus supplier and agency consolidation, compress GDS economics from both sides over time.

**Customer concentration.** A majority of revenue comes from airlines and a small number of large travel buyers; an airline bankruptcy or large-agency defection is material.

**Governance / control overhang.** Constellation Software built a ~9.7% economic position (reported as high as ~12.7%), prompting a March 1, 2026 poison pill and a March 5 Strategic Governance Agreement that added a Constellation executive to the board and eliminated the pill. A disciplined, long-horizon acquirer on the board raises the probability of a value-surfacing event (recapitalization, asset action, or eventual buyout) while also capping minority upside.

**Dilution.** Low-share-price comp packages (CEO received 1.8M+ shares in one award) and equity-linked refinancing both dilute. The May 19 8-K's reference to unregistered equity sales points to more.

**Tail items (collectively noise):** UK OFSI penalty notice (Jan 2026, accrued, immaterial), Indian income-tax litigation (~$21M), digital-services taxes, ~$38M unfunded pension, and a Nasdaq sub-$2 delisting discussion in the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Options data is mixed and low-conviction. The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.77 to 0.02 between the May 7 and June 19 snapshots, which on its face reads as a sharp reduction in put positioning (less bearish hedging). Reading too much into this is unwise on a sub-$2 name with thin options; the OI shift may reflect expiry roll-off as much as a sentiment change. Short interest is the more reliable positioning signal: 14.6% of float short, 5.8 days to cover, with ~41M shares short, meaningful institutional skepticism that also creates squeeze risk on any positive refinancing or M&A headline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The EBITDA-to-FCF inflection plays out: commercial wins and the $65M cost program push adjusted EBITDA above $585M, air bookings recover as Middle East headwinds fade, and the company refinances the August 2026 Exchangeable Notes and subsequent maturities at materially lower coupons, turning FCF positive in 2027. Constellation Software catalyzes a recapitalization or take-private at a premium. In a successful deleveraging, the thin equity sliver re-rates sharply, toward the $2.30 analyst high and beyond.

**Bear case.** A booking shock (oil/fuel, recession, an airline failure) cuts EBITDA, coverage thins toward 1x, and the company must refinance into a constrained market at double-digit or worse rates, with heavy equity-linked dilution. FCF stays negative, the cash balance continues drawing from $644M, a downgrade follows, and the equity falls toward the $0.81 prior low or lower. With negative equity already, the downside is the option going to near-zero in a restructuring.

The asymmetry is not favorable at $1.88. Estimate revisions are collapsing, FCF guidance is negative, and the entry requires betting that refinancing and EBITDA both break right. The Q1 optical beat does not change the cash-interest math.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest negative catalysts are dated: the $150M August 2026 Exchangeable Notes maturity (cash-settle vs. dilutive refinance), the May 19 financing details, and Q2 (early-August) results carrying the Middle East booking drag. None is a clean, single-day binary likely to gap the stock down 30%+; the August maturity could equally resolve positively if refinanced cleanly.

**Timing window.** Q2 results land in early August; the August notes resolve mid-Q3. A short here carries through both events, which cut both ways.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is elevated but below the 20%+ danger zone. The bigger squeeze risk is event-driven: a clean refinancing announcement or a Constellation-led take-private would gap the stock up violently against shorts, and the franchise's durable cash core makes such an outcome plausible.

**Borrow and options signal.** The put/call OI collapse to 0.02 suggests bearish options positioning has thinned rather than built, which is a warning sign for new shorts; the options market is not confirming a fresh bearish thesis. Cost-to-borrow on a heavily shorted sub-$2 name is likely elevated, raising carry.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** If a booking shock and adverse refinancing play out, the equity could revisit the $0.81 prior low (~-57%). But the upside risk, a Constellation take-private or a clean low-coupon refinance, could easily produce a +50% to +100% squeeze. That is roughly symmetric, not the 2:1 reward/risk a short requires, and the squeeze tail is fat.

**Verdict.** NOT SHORTABLE. The thesis is "distressed balance sheet, no positive FCF," a bad capital structure without a clean dated trigger, sitting against an activist board seat and refinancing optionality that could squeeze shorts hard. Avoid the equity; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the equity constructively if: (1) the company demonstrably refinances a meaningful tranche of the ~$4.3B stack at coupons in the 6–8% range, materially cutting the ~$490M cash interest burden; (2) free cash flow turns sustainably positive (a quarter of positive FCF plus guidance to positive full-year FCF), removing the cash-burn overhang; or (3) a Constellation-led transaction (recapitalization or take-private) is announced at a defined premium with terms that protect minority holders. Any one of these would convert the security from a distressed-residual claim into an analyzable cash-flow franchise.

**Timeline.** The headwinds (double-digit coupons, negative FCF guidance, and falling estimates) are likely to persist through at least FY2026. The August 2026 notes maturity and Q2 results (early August) are the next decision points. We would not establish a position ahead of clarity on those.

**What would make us more negative.** A downgrade by a rating agency, evidence of a constrained or failed refinancing (a coupon above 11.125% or a heavily dilutive equity-linked deal), or a sequential acceleration in the cash draw below ~$500M would deepen the distress case and argue for continued avoidance.

## Conclusion

Sabre is a stabilizing GDS oligopoly franchise trapped inside a distressed, negative-equity balance sheet. The operating recovery is real, but it is being consumed by the cash interest burden, producing negative free cash flow. The equity is a levered option on refinancing the debt stack down from double-digit rates while EBITDA outruns interest, and the leading indicator is moving against that thesis: current-year EPS estimates have been cut more than 90% in 90 days. Constellation Software's board presence is a genuine swing factor that raises the odds of a value-surfacing event while capping minority upside.

We maintain AVOID, consistent with the prior two reports, with the overall score nudged lower on the sharp estimate deterioration, partly offset by the credible EBITDA stabilization and the Constellation governance development. Given the durable cash-flow core, elevated short interest, and event optionality, this is an equity to avoid rather than to short.
