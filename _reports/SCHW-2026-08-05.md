## Executive Summary

Schwab has completed one of the cleanest earnings recoveries in large-cap financials. Adjusted diluted EPS went nowhere from 2021 through 2024 ($3.25 → $3.90 → $3.13 → $3.25) and then rose roughly 50% in 2025 to $4.87. The mechanism was mostly mechanical rather than operational: interest expense fell $2.64B (−41%) in FY2025 as bank supplemental funding was cut approximately 90% to $5.1B, retiring the expensive FHLB advances and retail CDs that had plugged the 2023 deposit flight. Net interest margin went from 2.12% (FY2024) to 2.74% (FY2025) to 2.88% in Q1 2026, with full-year 2026 guided to 3.00–3.10%. Q2 2026 was a record: $7.1B of revenue (+21%), adjusted EPS $1.62 (+42%), a 54.3% adjusted pre-tax margin, $120B of core net new assets, and 11.9M daily average trades, while management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to 17.5–18.5% and doubled its operating-leverage assumption from 400bp to 800bp.

Consensus has followed. Current-year EPS is $6.49 (up 7.7% in 90 days) and next-year is $7.80 (up 11.7%), putting the stock at 16.7x 2026 and 13.9x 2027 against a same-industry peer median trailing P/E of 19.3x. That is not a stretched multiple for a business earning 23% ROE and 40% ROTCE with mid-teens through-cycle earnings growth. Schwab is also asset-sensitive (+100bp = +3.5% net interest revenue; +200bp = +7.3%) into a Federal Reserve that has held rates unchanged through four 2026 FOMC meetings, with half of policymakers expecting at least one 2026 increase — an unusual position for an equity, and the company-specific catalyst that a Financials long needs.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a third consecutive BUY is entry arithmetic and two new frictions. At $108.21 the stock is at its 52-week high ($108.55) and roughly 15.5% below the $125 consensus mean, against a plausible bear case near $88 (−18.7%). When this name was last upgraded at ~$101.50, that gap was ~23%. Buybacks were cut from $2.4B in Q1 to $1.0B in Q2, the FY2026 scenario explicitly excludes second-half repurchases, and adjusted Tier 1 leverage sits at 6.8% — the bottom of the stated 6.75–7.00% objective — because risk-weighted assets grew 12.5% in a single quarter as the margin book expanded. Separately, the outgoing leadership generation monetized heavily into strength in late July and early August. None of this reverses the thesis; it compresses the reward relative to the risk. Own the position; the level to add is $95–98.

## Company Overview

Schwab is a savings and loan holding company, Federal Reserve–supervised, operating a broker-dealer (Charles Schwab & Co.), three bank charters (Charles Schwab Bank SSB, Charles Schwab Premier Bank SSB, Charles Schwab Trust Bank), an asset manager, and the largest RIA custody platform in the United States. At March 31, 2026 it reported $11.77 trillion of total client assets across 39.1 million active brokerage accounts, with $6.04 trillion receiving ongoing advisory services (+19% YoY).

The revenue mix is the single most important structural fact. For Q1 2026 (total net revenues $6,482M, +16%):

| Line | Q1 2026 | YoY | % of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest revenue | $3,144M | +16% | 48.5% |
| Asset management & admin fees | $1,759M | +15% | 27.1% |
| Trading revenue | $1,089M | +20% | 16.8% |
| Bank deposit account fees | $295M | +20% | 4.6% |
| Other | $195M | −7% | 3.0% |

Roughly 53% of revenue is spread and deposit-fee income. Schwab is a bank with an extremely large client-acquisition funnel attached, which is why the 2023 asset-liability episode was so damaging and why the 2025–2026 recovery has been so leveraged.

Two segments: Investor Services (Q1 2026 net revenues $5,077M, +14%; pre-tax income $2,509M, +25%) and Advisor Services (net revenues $1,405M, +22%; pre-tax income $679M, +53%; net interest revenue +31%). Advisor Services gathered $85.8B of the $139.9B Q1 net new assets while Investor Services NNA fell 22% YoY. The growth engine in early 2026 was institutional and RIA, not retail.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin.** FY2025 net income was $8,852M (+49%) on total net revenues of $23,921M (+22%). GAAP diluted EPS was $4.65; adjusted was $4.87. Q1 2026 delivered ROE of 23% and ROTCE of 40%. The Q2 2026 figures are issuer-reported from the July 21 earnings call and press coverage — the Q2 10-Q was not yet on file — and should be treated as such until the filing lands, which historically occurs in the first half of August.

**Earnings quality.** Three beats in four quarters, all modest: +3.0% (Q3'25), −2.3% (Q4'25), +0.5% (Q1'26), +2.5% (Q2'26). The Q2 comparison is like-for-like non-GAAP ($1.62 adjusted versus a $1.53 non-GAAP consensus), with $170M of pre-tax transaction-related costs excluded. This is a company that beats by small amounts and raises guidance, not one that produces surprises. That pattern supports confidence in the 2026 consensus and modestly less in 2027.

**Balance sheet composition is shifting fast.** Securities fell to 39% of total assets from 47% a year earlier. Margin and bank lending replaced them:

| | 12/31/2025 | 3/31/2026 | 6/30/2026 (issuer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Margin balances | $112.3B | $126.7B | $165.1B |
| Bank loans | ~$58B | $60.9B | $67B |

That is roughly +47% margin growth in six months. Credit quality is pristine — allowance of $36M on $60.9B of bank loans (0.06%), nonaccruals $32M, zero net charge-offs in both Q1 2026 and Q1 2025 — because the exposure is over-collateralized (Pledged Asset Lines $28.8B, first mortgages $31.3B to affluent borrowers). The risk here is market risk transmitted through collateral, not borrower default. A fast, deep equity drawdown triggers simultaneous margin and PAL calls across a book that has grown roughly 47% in six months. Concentration detail: 41% of the first mortgage/HELOC portfolio sits in California, $27.0B carries adjustable rates, and roughly 23% is interest-only.

**Capital is the binding constraint.** Reported CSC Tier 1 leverage was 8.9% at 3/31/2026; the company's non-GAAP adjusted measure, which absorbs the AOCI securities marks, was 6.8% — held at 6.8% again in Q2, the bottom of the 6.75–7.00% objective. CET1 fell from 30.4% to 26.3% in one quarter, but this reconciles as an RWA-growth story rather than capital erosion: 30.4% × $118.8B = $36.1B of CET1 versus 26.3% × $133.6B = $35.1B, a ~$1.0B decline consistent with $2.4B of Q1 buybacks net of retained earnings. RWA rose 12.5% in three months. The lending-led strategy is consuming the same capital that funds repurchases, and management has stated the ranking explicitly: deploying resources into the franchise ranks above returning capital.

**AOCI.** At 3/31/2026 AOCI was −$10,757M, improved from −$10,983M at year-end 2025 and −$14,839M at year-end 2024. AFS carried a $3,860M gross unrealized loss; HTM a $9,911M gross unrealized loss. These cannot simply be summed and called hidden losses — AOCI already contains AFS marks net of tax plus the frozen $7.7B after-tax loss from the prior AFS-to-HTM transfer, which is amortizing back, so the HTM mark overlaps substantially with what is already in equity. The March 2026 banking-agency proposal to include AOCI in regulatory capital over a five-year phase-in is plausibly immaterial to Schwab precisely because the ratio it manages to already absorbs the marks.

**Funding composition has re-leveraged.** Other short-term borrowings rose from $6.9B to $12.5B in Q1 2026 while FHLB advances went to zero. CSC had $4,726M of commercial paper against a $5,000M program authorization — effectively at the cap — and newly authorized a $10.0B CP program at the broker-dealer. Funding self-liquidating, over-collateralized margin loans with commercial paper is materially different in character from the 2023 situation, which involved funding an underwater long-duration securities book against deposit flight. Liquidity backstops are deep: LCR averaged 135% in Q1 (from 131%), HQLA $52.5B, FHLB availability $33.5B, discount window $28.6B, roughly $136B of additional unpledged securities, senior ratings A2/A−/A. This is the fastest transmission channel for a market shock and warrants quarterly monitoring, but it is not a present problem.

**Rate positioning has inverted.** Static 12-month net interest revenue sensitivity at 3/31/2026: +200bp = +7.3% (from +8.6%), +100bp = +3.5%, −50bp = −1.9%, −200bp = −7.6% (from −8.8%). The downside case improved 1.2 percentage points in one quarter because management began hedging its newly enormous floating-rate loan book — cash flow hedge notional went from $18.7B to $66.5B, including newly designated hedges on $20.0B of margin loans and $19.8B of PALs. Management traded away upside to buy downside protection. The hedges are currently a small drag: cash flow hedge AOCI swung from +$49M to −$158M in Q1, with roughly $74M of pre-tax losses expected to reclassify into interest revenue over the next twelve months.

Financial health scores 6.8. Cash generation and liquidity are excellent and credit is pristine; the offsets are capital at the bottom of target range, RWA growth outpacing capital formation, commercial paper at authorization cap with a new program layered on, and a $10.8B AOCI hole against 4.5x book value.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance for FY2026, raised on July 21: revenue growth 17.5–18.5% (implying ~$28.1–28.3B on the $23.921B base), full-year NIM 3.00–3.10%, Q4 average NIM 3.25–3.30%, expense growth 9.5–10.5% (of which ~100bp is Forge), daily average trades raised to 10.6M, 13% market appreciation, 5% organic growth, and one 25bp hike assumed for December 2026 — too late to affect 2026 results but a 2027 tailwind if delivered.

Consensus tracks this: $6.49 for 2026 (+33% on FY2025 adjusted EPS of $4.87) and $7.80 for 2027 (+20%). Revisions are broadly and consistently positive — current quarter +9.2% over 90 days, next quarter +11.4%, current year +7.7%, next year +11.7%, with 30-day revisions running faster than the 90-day pace on three of four periods. That acceleration matters. It falls short of the +30% threshold that marks a genuine trough inflection, so I treat it as confirmation rather than as a standalone buy signal.

**The structural runway is credible and quantified.** Management disclosures: only 5% of Schwab retail households use a fee-based advice solution while 31% say they would pay for advice; only 0.5% of clients use a lending product versus a ~4% industry average; Schwab Wealth Advisory clients generate 3x the return on client assets of self-directed clients. The financials corroborate the direction — managed investing net flows +53% YoY, proprietary wealth flows +86% year-to-date, bank lending +33% YoY, PAL originations up roughly 60%. These are management-sourced market-share and TAM estimates and were not independently verified, but the flow data is the company's own reported result.

**Three reasons to discount the 2026 number as a base for extrapolation.** First, the interest-expense retirement that drove roughly half the 2025 step-up is done — it cannot repeat. Second, Q2's 11.9M daily average trades against a 10.6M full-year assumption implies roughly 10.3M in the second half, a ~13% sequential decline; trading was 16.8% of Q1 revenue and revenue per trade is already falling (−12% YoY to $1.80). Third, management itself anchors to "mid-teens earnings growth through the cycle," well below the ~33% implied for 2026. The 2027 consensus of +20% therefore requires the December hike to land, NIM to hold above 3.25%, and no cash-sorting relapse.

**One number I could not reproduce.** First-half core NNA of $260B on beginning client assets of ~$11.90T annualizes to ~4.4% (or ~4.7% adding back a disclosed $17.5B mutual-fund clearing deconversion), against management's stated confidence in "5% or higher." Methodologies may differ; I am flagging the gap rather than asserting an error.

Growth prospects score 7.4 — high-quality, multi-year, partly mechanical (IDA fixed tranches rolling from a 1.16% to a 1.63% net yield, securities reinvestment at higher rates, lending penetration rising from 0.5%), discounted for a 2026 base that is above trend.

The supplied profile reports both trailing and forward P/E as 19.37x, which cannot both be right at $108.21 — that multiple implies EPS of $5.58, close to trailing-twelve-month adjusted EPS of $5.75 ($1.31 + $1.39 + $1.43 + $1.62). I use explicit bases:

| Basis | EPS | Multiple at $108.211 |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 GAAP diluted | $4.65 | 23.3x |
| TTM adjusted | $5.75 | 18.8x |
| FY2026 consensus | $6.49 | 16.7x |
| FY2027 consensus | $7.80 | 13.9x |

Peer set: peer median trailing P/E 19.3x (GS 19.0x, MS 19.3x, RJF 14.2x, IBKR 38.1x, HOOD 44.8x). On a trailing adjusted basis Schwab at 18.8x is essentially at the peer median and at a premium to Raymond James, the closest structural comparable. The cheapness appears only on forward estimates. That is the honest framing: this is not a cheap stock on delivered earnings, it is a reasonably priced stock on estimates that have been revised up every month for three months.

Book-value multiples, back-solved from reported Q1 ROE of 23% and ROTCE of 40% on $2,397M of net income to common, imply roughly $41.7B of common equity and $24.0B of tangible common equity — approximately 4.5x book and 7.8x tangible book. Those are approximations from annualized quarterly ratios on average equity. They establish that Schwab trades far above a typical depository, defensible only if the fee, trading, and organic-growth franchises persist. There is no book-value floor under this stock.

**The specific mispricing argument, and its limit.** The market is applying roughly 14x to 2027 consensus for a business generating 23% ROE and 40% ROTCE with a documented multi-year penetration runway and asset sensitivity into a Fed with a hawkish tilt. If 2027 estimates keep drifting up at anything like the recent 11.7% quarterly pace, 16x an $8.20 number is $131. Consensus mean is $125, median $126.5, high $145, low $91, and the sentiment trend is stable with 18 of 22 ratings at buy or strong buy.

**The limit is the earnings base, not the multiple.** If 2026-27 earnings are above trend — trading volumes normalize toward 9–10M DATs, the December hike does not arrive, market appreciation undershoots the 13% assumed, and cash intensity keeps falling — then normalized power is closer to $6.00–6.50 and 17–18x on that is $105–115. That is roughly today's price. Reward and risk are close to symmetric from here: +15.5% to base, +34% to bull, −18.7% to bear.

Dividend support is thin: $1.28 annualized after a 19% raise gives a 1.18% yield, below the 1.4% peer median. Approximately $11.1B of buyback authorization remains (5.9% of market cap), but the FY2026 scenario excludes second-half repurchases and Q2's pace was $1.0B versus $2.4B in Q1. Models carrying FY2025's ~$7B annual repurchase pace into 2026-27 will overstate share-count shrinkage.

Valuation scores 6.7 — attractive if you underwrite consensus, fair-to-full on normalized earnings, with no asset-value floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab is the scale leader: #1 in total client assets, #1 in RIA custodial assets, and by management's account #1 in daily average trades and options contracts, executing roughly one third of retail brokerage trades. Those are company assertions, not third-party verified. The Ameritrade integration reads as net-additive rather than merely retentive — 27% of thinkorswim users are now legacy Charles Schwab clients, meaning the acquired platform is winning share inside the legacy base.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed, which is why this dimension has drifted down from 8.5 in earlier reviews.

**Improving:** Advisor Services revenue +22% with pre-tax income +53% and 61% of Q1 net new assets. Schwab is the balance-sheet provider of choice for RIA long/short tax-loss-harvesting strategies ($21.3B of related margin balances at 3/31/2026), which management credits for the RIA acceleration. Advice and lending penetration are rising from very low bases.

**Deteriorating on the retail edge:** Interactive Brokers grew customer accounts 34% YoY against Schwab's 6% active-account growth. Absolute additions still favor Schwab enormously off a 39M base, but the rate gap matters for terminal-value arguments. More telling: asked about the June SpaceX IPO, management said interest was "tremendous" and it "drove high levels of volume into our service centers," but that it "did not meaningfully impact NNA in any way," noting that other firms reported large NNA bounces. Schwab is not the venue of first resort for the newest and most speculative retail cohorts. The Forge Global acquisition (closed 3/2/2026, $636M, ~55% goodwill, $14M revenue and a $6M net loss for the March stub) reads partly as an answer — owning private-market access rather than competing for allocation. Schwab has also declined to support one of the three components of prediction markets, defensible for a bank charter but ceding ground to less-regulated venues.

**Mix pressure inside trading.** Revenue per trade fell 12% YoY to $1.80, and management guided only "modest" improvement from Q2 levels because rate and volume are inversely correlated. Order flow revenue was $560M in Q1 (+26%), of which options order flow was $363M (+34%) — roughly 8.6% of total revenue, growing faster than the company, concentrated in a payment-for-order-flow line that has been a recurring SEC target.

Competitive position scores 7.2.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI-enabled cash optimization is the bear thesis worth tracking.** Client cash as a percentage of total client assets was 9.9% at 3/31/2026, down from 10.6% a year earlier. Absolute sweep cash can rise (+$24.2B in Q2) while cash intensity falls, because client assets grow faster; only one of those metrics drives the franchise that produces ~48% of revenue. The structural risk is that automated agents continuously sweep idle cash into money-market and T-bill alternatives, converting cash sorting from a rate-cycle phenomenon into a one-way ratchet. BMO's July 20 downgrade named this thesis while stating there is no evidence it is affecting current results. It is a hypothesis, and the falling ratio is equally explicable by equity appreciation inflating the denominator — but 9.9% is the number to check every quarter.

**2. The Q2 sweep-cash improvement is partly a balance-sheet artifact.** The RIA long/short business generates both the margin-loan asset and the sweep-cash liability, inflating both sides. Management said long/short is "roughly 1% of our revenue"; other interest revenue rose from $30M to $167M and other interest expense from $2M to $122M in Q1, a net $45M for the quarter, or roughly $180M annualized against a $21.3B ending balance — a ~0.85% net spread, thin versus the 100bp-plus incremental spread cited for PALs. Schwab is renting balance sheet at a low spread to win RIA relationships, consuming the same regulatory capital that would fund buybacks or higher-spread lending.

**3. Collateral risk in a rapidly grown margin book.** $165.1B of margin plus $28.8B of PALs, grown ~47% in six months and increasingly funded with commercial paper. Zero charge-offs tell you nothing about tail behavior in a fast drawdown. With the equity market near highs, this is the channel through which a market shock reaches Schwab's income statement and its adjusted leverage ratio simultaneously.

**4. The IDA is an eight-year constraint.** The TD depository agreement runs through July 1, 2034; Schwab may withdraw at discretion subject to a $60B floor and $90B cap with a nonperformance payment below the floor. The balance was $73.6B at 3/31/2026 ($59.6B fixed-rate), leaving roughly $13.6B of headroom. Offsetting positive: BDA fees grew 20% on a 14% smaller average balance because net yield rose from 1.16% to 1.63% as fixed tranches roll — a mechanical multi-year tailwind embedded in a structural cap.

**5. The rate-hike trade cuts both ways.** A hike adds net interest revenue (+3.5% per 100bp) but also deepens AFS/HTM unrealized losses, pressures the AOCI-inclusive adjusted leverage ratio already at the bottom of target, risks reviving cash sorting by widening the gap to money-market yields, and is unhelpful for the 13% market-appreciation and 10.6M DAT assumptions inside the same guidance. Schwab's revenue lines are not independent in a hiking scenario.

**6. Q4 NIM guidance was left unchanged despite a better environment**, and management deflected when two analysts pressed. Either genuine conservatism on the exit rate or unquantified offsets — deposit repricing, mix shift toward the thin-spread long/short book, and a securities-lending environment the CFO described as not contributing meaningfully in the back half. The full-year 3.00–3.10% guide against a 2.88% Q1 actual already requires meaningful second-half expansion, so the set is internally consistent. This is a flag on the slope, not the level.

**7. Governance and turnover.** The January 29, 2026 reorganization retired the Charles Schwab Bank CEO and the General Auditor simultaneously, effective July 1, 2026 — two control-relevant roles turning over while the bank loan book grows 33% YoY. The filings characterize these as retirements of long-tenured executives and nothing adverse was found. The only material legal matter is the Corrente antitrust settlement (non-monetary undertakings plus immaterial fees, final approval November 24, 2025, objectors appealed to the Fifth Circuit).

**8. Digital assets are a newly added risk factor.** Spot bitcoin and ethereum trading launched April 2026, with Charles Schwab Premier Bank as custodian of record and Paxos Trust Company engaged for sub-custody and execution — Paxos is Schwab's vendor, and Schwab separately holds a minority investment in it. Private-key irreversibility, third-party reliance on an early-stage provider, and regulatory uncertainty are all live.

Risk factors score 6.0. No binary regulatory ruling or refinancing cliff, pristine credit, deep liquidity — but a structural threat to half the revenue base that is not yet observable, a rapidly grown collateral book, and capital flexibility already at its floor.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $145 (+34%)

The 2026 guide is delivered or beaten (Schwab has raised, not cut, all year), the December 25bp hike lands and NIM exits above 3.30%, and 2027 consensus drifts from $7.80 toward $8.25 as the IDA fixed tranches continue rolling from 1.63% toward market yields and lending penetration climbs from 0.5% toward the 4% industry norm. Advice penetration moves off 5% against 31% stated willingness to pay, lifting the revenue on client assets mix toward the 3x that Wealth Advisory clients already generate. Trading volumes hold above 10.6M DATs on structurally elevated retail participation. At 17.5x an $8.25 number the stock is $144. Options: capital returns resume in 2027 once RWA growth normalizes, with $11.1B of authorization available.

### Bear Case — $88 (−18.7%)

An equity-market drawdown does three things at once: it cuts the 13% appreciation assumption embedded in asset management fees, triggers simultaneous collateral reduction across the $165B margin and $28.8B PAL books (shrinking the highest-spread assets), and deepens AFS/HTM marks that flow into the 6.8% adjusted leverage ratio, forcing buybacks to zero rather than merely lower. Concurrently, cash intensity continues below 9.9% as automated cash optimization spreads, so net interest revenue misses even without a rate cut. Trading normalizes toward 9.5M DATs with revenue per trade still declining. On a normalized $6.50 of earnings power, 13.5x is $88 — roughly the late-May trough of $84 and slightly below the low analyst target of $91. With 4.5x book and 7.8x tangible book there is no asset-value floor to arrest the derating.

### Base Case — $125 (+15.5%)

16.0x FY2027 consensus of $7.80 = $124.80. This also matches the consensus mean of $125 and sits just below the median of $126.5. It assumes 2026 guidance is delivered near the high end, 2027 grows roughly 20% as consensus expects, the multiple stays near the current 13.9x-to-16x band as capital return resumes only gradually, and no market drawdown severe enough to break the collateral-funded lending flywheel.
