**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Encompass Health is the largest inpatient rehabilitation facility (IRF) operator in the United States, running 174 hospitals across 39 states and Puerto Rico after its 2022 spin-off of the home-health/hospice business. The investment case is a high-quality compounder trading at a discount to its own growth rate. Revenue has grown at an ~11% CAGR since 2022 to $5.94B in 2025, adjusted EBITDA reached $1.27B, ROE is 31.1%, and operating cash flow was $1.18B. The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (by 5.8%, 11.3%, 1.9% and 14.6%), raised full-year 2026 guidance with the Q1 print, and consensus estimates are rising (+1.5% current-year and +1.0% next-year over 90 days). Despite this, the stock has fallen ~12% over the trailing year and roughly 18% from its late-2025 high near $126.83, compressing the forward P/E to 15.9x against an EPS growth rate near 11%.

The price action and the fundamental trajectory have diverged. The decline is multiple compression layered on a sector-wide regulatory overhang, not a deterioration in delivered results or estimates. The 13-analyst consensus is 13 buy/strong-buy and zero holds, with a $125 low target, $140.5 mean and $152 high — implying roughly 35% upside to the mean from $103.93. The defensive characteristics are unusual for a name growing double digits: beta 0.59, non-discretionary demand (92% of patients admitted from acute-care hospitals), a structural supply/demand imbalance (flat IRF count since 2010 against a 75+ population growing ~4%/yr), and ~79% owned real estate.

The single reason a constructive view requires care is regulatory: CMS is expanding its Review Choice Demonstration (RCD) into Texas and California, bringing 33 hospitals and ~11.9% of IRF Medicare claims under 100% pre-claim or post-payment review. We size this as a manageable collections-timing friction rather than a structural impairment — claims that pass review get paid, and only ~$39M of claims sit in the appeals pipeline against $5.94B revenue and $1.18B operating cash flow. MedPAC's recommended 7% rate cut and the proposed unified post-acute payment system are real overhangs but are unenacted and apply to the entire sector. We rate EHC a BUY with a 12-month base case of $128.

## Company Overview

Encompass Health (formerly HealthSouth) operates inpatient rehabilitation hospitals treating patients recovering from stroke, hip fracture, brain and spinal-cord injury, neurological and complex orthopedic conditions, cardiac and pulmonary events, and amputations. Care is largely non-discretionary: 92% of patients are admitted from acute-care hospitals via physician referral. The company is reimbursed predominantly under Medicare's IRF prospective payment system (IRF-PPS), a fixed payment per discharge, so the core profit lever is cost discipline against a set rate.

The business has several structural features that matter for valuation. Roughly a third of its hospitals (67 of 173 at year-end 2025) are joint ventures with acute-care systems and non-profits, which produces a large and growing noncontrolling-interest line — consolidated net income of $759M in 2025 versus $566M attributable to EHC. The company owns ~79% of its hospital real estate, an asset backing that differentiates it from lease-heavy peers and underpins its modest financial leverage. The moat rests on scale (the only sizeable public competitor runs ~38 IRFs), proprietary clinical-outcomes data, disease-specific certifications (148 of 173 hospitals hold stroke certifications), prefabricated de novo construction enabling fast time-to-market, and certificate-of-need barriers in roughly 36% of licensed beds.

The secular tailwind is concrete. The patient base averages ~77 years old and the 75+ population is growing ~4%/yr through 2030, while the number of IRFs has been flat at ~1,170 since 2010. Only about 15% of IRF-eligible acute discharges currently convert to IRF admission, leaving a long runway for both share capture and de novo expansion.

Revenue growth decomposes into ~6% discharge growth (3.4% same-store in 2025) plus ~4% net revenue per discharge, and the 2026 IRF rule adds a ~2.9% net Medicare rate increase. Margins have expanded: total operating expenses fell from 84.8% of revenue in 2023 to 82.3% in 2025, driven mainly by salaries and benefits dropping from 54.2% to 52.5% as contract labor normalized (contract FTEs down to 355 from 427) and sign-on/shift bonuses fell. ROA is 10.7% and net margin 12.8%.

Cash generation is strong. Free cash flow was $439M after $736M of capex. Capex is heavy and rising — guided to $920M–$995M for 2026 — but only ~$225–240M of that is maintenance; the balance funds de novo construction and bed additions. This is growth capex that compresses near-term FCF (Q1'26 adjusted FCF fell 12.9% YoY) but is discretionary, which matters for the risk case: if reimbursement deteriorates, management can dial back de novos and protect cash flow and the balance sheet.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.72 ("distress zone") is a false negative and should be disregarded. It is dragged down by the asset-heavy, modestly-levered structure typical of a real-estate-owning hospital operator. Net leverage is ~1.9x against $1.27B adjusted EBITDA, well inside the 4.5x covenant maximum; D/E is 1.56; the revolver has $824M of availability; the company is in full covenant compliance; and there are no significant debt maturities until 2028. EHC has already addressed that wall, completing a $500M senior-notes offering in June 2026 to term out debt ahead of the 2028 maturities. Capital allocation is sensible: growth capex first, then returns — the dividend was raised to $0.19/quarter from $0.17 in mid-2025, and $158M of stock was repurchased in 2025 (up from $31M in 2024) with ~$332M left on a $500M authorization.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis combines volume, price and footprint. Discharge growth has been running ~6% with same-store at 3.4%; the 75+ demographic provides a multi-year volume tailwind that the flat industry facility count amplifies in EHC's favor. The 2026 rule's ~2.9% net rate increase supports per-discharge revenue, and the company opened 7 new hospitals plus 177 beds in 2025 with a de novo pipeline spanning SC, PA, GA, TX, ME, AZ, FL, UT and IN for 2026–27. A 49-bed hospital in Irmo, SC opened in Q1'26.

Estimate revisions are constructive, a more important forward signal than the price chart. Current-year consensus EPS has risen to $6.01 from $5.92 ninety days ago (+1.5%), and next-year to $6.54 from $6.48 (+1.0%). Management raised FY26 guidance with the Q1 print to revenue of $6.375–6.470B, adjusted EBITDA of $1.350–1.380B, and adjusted EPS of $5.89–6.11. The four-quarter beat record (average surprise ~8.4%) gives reasonable confidence that consensus is, if anything, conservative. These revisions do not reach the +30% velocity that flags a trough-earnings inflection, so we are not treating this as a deep-cyclical recovery; rather it is durable double-digit compounding being priced at a discount.

One caveat on margins: the step-down in contract labor and bonuses (S&B from 54.2% to 52.5% of revenue) is largely a one-time post-COVID recovery. Future margin gains will lean more on volume leverage and pricing than on continued labor deflation, so we model margin expansion decelerating from here even as revenue growth holds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $103.93, EHC trades at 15.9x forward and 17.8x trailing earnings against ~11% EPS growth, putting the PEG comfortably below 1.5. The peer set provided is thin — the proximity comparable, BridgeBio, is an unprofitable clinical-stage pharma with a negative P/E and is not a meaningful read-across for a cash-generative facility operator. More relevant is the company's own history and growth-adjusted math: a mid-teens forward multiple on a business compounding earnings double-digits with 31% ROE, defensive demand and a structural supply tailwind is undemanding.

The ~18% drawdown reflects multiple compression, not estimate cuts — estimates rose over the same window. At a >30% drawdown the burden of proof shifts to the bull, but here the decline is ~18% and the falsifiable bear thesis (RCD-driven collections friction and unenacted MedPAC cuts) is identifiable and, on our sizing, modest relative to the company's cash generation. The market is discounting regulatory tail risk that is real but unenacted and already partly priced.

Analyst targets span $125 low / $140.0 median / $140.5 mean / $152 high, with 13 buy/strong-buy ratings and zero holds. The low target alone implies ~20% upside, the mean ~35%. On names with rising estimates and sub-30x multiples, consensus targets behave more like floors than ceilings, and EHC sits at the cheap-relative-to-growth end of that spectrum. Our base case applies ~17x to next-twelve-months EPS of roughly $6.30 (blending the FY26 raised midpoint and the FY27 estimate of $6.54) for a $128 target, modestly below the consensus mean to allow for the RCD overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

EHC's competitive position is improving rather than merely stable. It is the natural JV partner for non-profit and acute-care systems given its centralized administration, proprietary clinical datasets, prefab construction and national footprint, and it is taking share in a fragmented industry where the next-largest public operator runs ~38 hospitals. CON laws and disease-specific certifications raise barriers to entry, and the flat industry facility count means incremental demand disproportionately accrues to incumbents with the capital and operational capability to add capacity.

The genuine competitive threat is the payor system, not a rival operator. Medicare Advantage is now ~16% of revenue and growing (54% of beneficiaries chose MA in 2025; CBO projects ~64% by 2034). MA pays less than traditional Medicare, converts referrals to admissions at lower rates, and uses prior authorization and "conveners" to steer patients toward cheaper settings such as home health and skilled nursing. The mandatory TEAM episodic-payment model went live 1/1/2026 in 189 markets, though only ~2% of EHC discharges are currently in scope. EHC's defense is its outcomes data — higher discharge-to-community rates and lower readmissions — which is the strongest argument for IRF-level care in value-based contracting. This is a structural margin headwind to monitor over a multi-year horizon, not an immediate threat to the volume thesis.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is regulatory and reimbursement concentration: ~82% of revenue is Medicare plus Medicare Advantage, and ~65% is traditional fee-for-service Medicare.

**RCD expansion — sized.** The Texas expansion is effective 3/2/26 and California 5/1/26. RCD does not cut rates; it subjects claims to documentation review before or after payment. Claims that pass get paid; the friction is timing of cash conversion and the administrative cost of compliance. The relevant magnitudes: claims under review against $5.94B total revenue; only ~$39M of claims sit in the entire ALJ/DAB/district-court appeals pipeline; the company contests $18M of an extrapolated UPIC determination; and it has the operating cash flow and $824M of revolver availability to absorb any working-capital swing. Even if a meaningful share of Texas/California claims were temporarily delayed, the cash impact is a fraction of annual OCF and well within liquidity. The legitimate concern is that EHC's Alabama hospitals failed the opt-out affirmation threshold (85–90%) in two consecutive RCD cycles, which management attributes to the MAC applying improper standards. That signals real friction with MAC coverage interpretations and could pressure 2026–27 cash conversion even if claims ultimately affirm. We treat this as a collections-timing headwind, not a structural impairment, and it is the primary reason our base case sits below the consensus mean.

**MedPAC and PAC-PPS.** MedPAC recommended a 7% IRF-PPS base-rate cut at its January 2026 meeting and has floated a unified post-acute payment system (estimated ~15% IRF reduction) and an IRF transfer-payment policy (~6–7%). None are enacted; all apply sector-wide and have existed as overhangs for years. They are real but unpriced-to-certainty risks rather than near-term cash events.

**OBBBA.** The 2025 law limits Medicaid provider taxes and directed payments. EHC received ~$148M in state directed/supplemental payments in 2025 and paid ~$127M in provider taxes (net ~$21M favorable); most provisions hit in 2027+. Net Medicaid is only ~3% of revenue, so the direct exposure is limited, though the supplemental-payment swing flows through "Other income." OBBBA's bonus-depreciation and R&D-expensing provisions delivered ~$22M of cash tax savings in 2025, a near-term offset.

**Labor inflation** against fixed reimbursement is the perennial squeeze; currently favorable but cyclical, and the easy margin gains are largely captured.

**Governance filings.** Three Item 5.02 director/officer-change filings appear in the recent window (9/18/2025, 2/2/2026, and an 8-K/A on 2/20/2026), coinciding with the September–February price slide. The specifics of these filings could not be confirmed from the available primary sources. The executives who produce results — CEO Mark Tarr, CFO Doug Coltharp (still filing Form 4s, including an option exercise and sale on 5/15/26, retaining ~69K shares), and COO Patrick Tuer — are all confirmed in role and active through mid-2026. Item 5.02 filings routinely cover board-member retirements, committee changes, and scheduled appointments, and the absence of any departure of the operating leadership team, combined with continued guidance raises and beats through the period, argues against a destabilizing C-suite event. We flag these as worth confirming but do not believe they reflect a fundamental impairment. The price decline tracks the late-2025 healthcare-services de-rating and the RCD/MedPAC news flow far more closely than any single dated filing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($152).** RCD review affirms EHC's claims at high rates, removing the collections overhang; MedPAC cuts remain unenacted; discharge growth holds at 5–6% with the demographic tailwind; the de novo pipeline executes on schedule; and the multiple re-rates toward 19–20x as the regulatory cloud clears. On FY27 EPS approaching $6.60–6.80, that supports the high end of the analyst range. The defensive, double-digit-compounding profile commands a premium in a stagflationary, late-cycle macro backdrop where non-discretionary healthcare demand is attractive.

**Bear case (~$85, roughly -18% from current).** RCD friction proves persistent — affirmation rates lag as they did in Alabama — and cash conversion deteriorates through 2026–27; MA mix shift accelerates margin pressure; and one of the MedPAC proposals gains legislative traction, prompting a structural de-rating to ~13x on flat-to-down forward EPS. Even in this scenario the ~1.9x net leverage, owned real estate, $824M revolver and discretionary growth capex provide downside support, which is why the bear target is a multiple-driven derating rather than a solvency event.

**Base case ($128, ~+23%).** Estimates continue their modest upward drift, the company delivers within its raised guidance, RCD proves to be a timing friction that washes out on appeal, and the multiple recovers to ~17x on NTM EPS of ~$6.30. This is our most-likely outcome and sits below the consensus mean to reflect the unresolved RCD and MedPAC overhangs.

**Base case target:** $128 (~17x NTM EPS of ~$6.30). **Upside to base: ~23%.**

**Bull case target:** $152 (~19–20x FY27 EPS approaching $6.70). **Upside to bull: ~46%.**

**Bear case target:** $85 (~13x derated forward EPS). **Downside to bear: ~-18%.**

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q2'26 (late July/early August) and Q3'26 (late October) earnings releases, which will provide the first read on RCD collections impact in Texas, and to any 2027 IRF rule and MedPAC legislative developments through the period.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. RCD collections friction becomes quantified and material — e.g., management discloses a working-capital drag or DSO increase attributable to RCD exceeding ~$100M, or affirmation rates in Texas track the failed Alabama pattern, signaling a structural rather than timing problem.
2. A MedPAC-recommended IRF rate cut or PAC-PPS proposal advances to enacted legislation or a CMS proposed rule, converting an overhang into a dated cash event.
3. Same-store discharge growth decelerates below ~2% for two consecutive quarters, or consensus current-year EPS estimates turn negative on revisions, breaking the compounding thesis.

## Conclusion

Encompass Health is the dominant operator in a structurally undersupplied, demographically tailwinded niche, compounding revenue ~11% and earnings double-digits with 31% ROE, four consecutive EPS beats, raised guidance, and rising consensus estimates. The stock has de-rated ~18% to 15.9x forward earnings (PEG well under 1.5) against a 13-0 buy/hold consensus and ~35% upside to the mean target. The de-rating reflects multiple compression and a sector regulatory overhang, not deteriorating fundamentals.

The principal risk, RCD expansion into Texas and California, is a collections-timing friction on ~11.9% of Medicare claims that affirm on appeal, sized small ($39M in the appeals pipeline) against $1.18B of operating cash flow, $824M of revolver availability and ~1.9x net leverage. MedPAC's cuts and PAC-PPS are unenacted, sector-wide overhangs. The governance-filing cluster has specifics that could not be confirmed from the available sources, but it does not coincide with any departure of the operating leadership team and does not appear to be the driver of the drawdown. The risk/reward favors a long position. **BUY**, 12-month base-case target $128.
