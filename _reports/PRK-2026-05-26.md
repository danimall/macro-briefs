## Executive Summary

Park National Corporation is an Ohio-headquartered community bank holding company that crossed the $10 billion asset threshold in February 2026 via its $324 million all-stock acquisition of First Citizens Banc Corp (Ohio). Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.06 beat the $2.66 consensus estimate, driven by a 4.80% net interest margin and low-cost deposit franchise. Annualized adjusted earnings power is approximately $12.23/share for FY26, placing the stock at ~13.9x forward earnings — modestly below the peer median of 15.7x.

The investment case hinges on whether post-acquisition scale benefits and continued NIM strength can overcome the regulatory cost burden of the $10B threshold (Durbin Amendment interchange fee caps, CFPB supervision, enhanced compliance infrastructure). Estimates are rising across all periods (+9.5% over 90 days), and the Tennessee region appears to be contributing meaningfully. However, two consecutive quarterly EPS misses (Q4 2025 at -5.9%, Q1 2026 at -9.0% on a GAAP basis) indicate the market has already absorbed the beat narrative when measured against reported rather than adjusted figures, and the stock sits only 5% below its 52-week high.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation at $169.58 is the combination of: (1) Durbin Amendment implementation likely costing $8-12M annually ($0.45-0.70/share pre-tax) with no offsetting revenue stream identified; (2) two consecutive GAAP EPS misses despite positive adjusted figures, indicating merger costs create noise that makes forward visibility genuinely uncertain; and (3) current valuation at ~14x forward leaves only 8-11% upside to consensus targets ($180-188), which does not provide sufficient asymmetry given the $10B threshold regulatory unknowns.

## Company Overview

Park National Corporation operates through Park National Bank, serving communities across Ohio, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, and (since February 2026) Tennessee through approximately 87+ financial service offices. The bank's $9.67 billion loan portfolio breaks down into commercial (42.9%), residential real estate (34.5%), and consumer (22.6%, predominantly indirect auto lending). Niche businesses include aircraft financing ($339M via Scope Aircraft Finance) and lending to non-bank consumer finance companies ($268.8M nationally). The wealth management division generates approximately $12.3M quarterly in fiduciary fee income.

Total assets reached $12.98 billion post-acquisition, deposits stand at $11.0 billion, and equity totals $1.70 billion (13.1% equity-to-assets). Capital ratios are well above minimums: CET1 at 13.48%, Total Capital at 14.71%.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** Net income grew 18.9% in FY2025 to $180.1M ($11.11/diluted share). Q1 2026 adjusted net income of $53.3M ($3.06/share) reflects full-quarter contribution from First Citizens. GAAP net income was $41.7M ($2.39/share) after $15.5M in merger-related costs. The consensus current-year estimate of $12.23 implies continued mid-single-digit growth, reasonable given the acquisition accretion math.

**Net Interest Income:** NII of $125.8M in Q1 2026 (+20.5% YoY) reflects both acquisition volume and margin expansion. The 4.80% NIM is roughly 130-180 bps above the community bank average, driven by: cost of deposits at only 1.62% (declining from 1.76%), loan yields at 6.36%, and non-interest bearing deposits comprising 27.8% of total funding. Minimal reliance on wholesale funding.

**Credit Quality:** Net charge-offs of 0.12% annualized are very low. The allowance for credit losses at 1.12% of loans provides adequate coverage (130% of nonaccrual loans). NPLs at 0.86% are slightly elevated from the First Citizens portfolio acquisition ($9.4M Tennessee region nonaccruals) but manageable. The $24.5M in OREO (almost entirely acquired) warrants monitoring.

**Capital Allocation:** The $175M subordinated debt retirement in September 2025 reduces ongoing interest expense and demonstrates capital strength. The $4.40 annual dividend ($1.10 quarterly) represents a 38-40% payout ratio — sustainable with room for growth.

**Efficiency:** Adjusted efficiency ratio of 55.55% is top-quartile for community banks. Adjusted ROAA of 1.83% and adjusted ROATE of 16.21% demonstrate strong profitability execution.

## Growth Analysis

**Acquisition Accretion:** First Citizens adds approximately $1.6B in loans and $2.2B in deposits. Assuming system conversion completes in Q3 2026 with cost synergies fully realized by Q4 2026, the run-rate EPS uplift is likely $0.50-0.80 annualized (deposit cost savings, overhead elimination, and loan growth opportunities in Tennessee).

**Organic Growth Drivers:** Loan growth in the existing portfolio was $230.8M (+3.4%) in 2025 prior to acquisition, concentrated in commercial and aircraft financing. The Tennessee market provides geographic diversification but is not a high-growth market.

**Estimate Trajectory:** Consensus FY26 EPS has risen from $11.17 to $12.23 over 90 days (+9.5%), and FY27 from $11.68 to $12.78 (+9.4%). These revisions reflect acquisition arithmetic more than organic acceleration. The +9.5% revision over 90 days does not meet the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal on estimates alone.

**Headwinds to Growth:** Durbin Amendment implementation will reduce debit interchange income by an estimated $8-12M annually (roughly $0.45-0.70/share pre-tax). This partially offsets acquisition accretion. Enhanced compliance costs (CFPB, OCC supervision, Volcker Rule) could add $3-5M annually in recurring expense.

## Valuation Assessment

At $169.58, PRK trades at:
- 15.3x trailing EPS ($11.11)
- 13.9x FY26 consensus ($12.23)
- 13.3x FY27 consensus ($12.78)
- ~2.6% dividend yield

Versus the peer median P/E of 15.7x (on a trailing basis across BOH, PFS, BANC, SBCF), PRK trades at a slight discount on trailing but a more meaningful discount on forward estimates. However, the peer median dividend yield of 3.6% exceeds PRK's 2.6%, suggesting the market demands more income from this group.

The analyst consensus target range of $180-188 (mean $184) implies 8.5-10.9% upside from current levels. PRK is only 5% below its 52-week high of $179.48 and hit that high in early July 2025 before spending nearly a year in a $149-179 range; the upside skew is insufficient to warrant a BUY. The forward P/E of 13.9x on $12.23 estimates is reasonable but already reflects acquisition accretion — the market is not mispricing this name.

For PRK to warrant a BUY at 14x forward, there would need to be identifiable upside to consensus estimates or a catalyst for multiple expansion. The positive estimate revisions (+9.5% over 90 days) are constructive but moderate, and the Durbin Amendment headwind creates a ceiling on how much earnings can exceed current expectations.

## Competitive Landscape

Park's competitive position is strong within its niche: small/mid-market community banking in Ohio and adjacent states. The 4.80% NIM indicates pricing power and a sticky, low-cost deposit franchise that larger banks cannot easily replicate. The 117-year operating history, 89% associate ownership, and 9.7-year average tenure create institutional stability.

The First Citizens acquisition extends this model into Tennessee, a market with similar community banking dynamics. Risks include: (1) fintechs eroding deposit relationships over time, (2) larger regional banks with superior digital platforms, and (3) the $10B threshold forcing Park to invest in compliance infrastructure that dilutes the cost advantage of being a smaller community bank.

Park's niche specialties (aircraft financing, non-bank consumer finance lending) provide revenue diversification but also concentrate credit risk in segments that could deteriorate in a downturn.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **$10B Threshold Regulatory Impact (HIGH — quantifiable):** Durbin Amendment interchange caps represent $8-12M in annual revenue loss, or approximately 5-7% of pre-provision net revenue. CFPB supervision, enhanced OCC oversight, and Volcker compliance will add ongoing expense. Management has been preparing, but the full cost burden is not yet reflected in run-rate financials.

2. **Integration Execution (MODERATE — time-bound):** System conversion for Tennessee region is planned for Q3 2026. Dual-system operation is expensive. First Citizens' $21.3M OREO and $65.1M in purchased credit-deteriorated loans indicate legacy asset quality issues that could surface.

3. **CRE Concentration (MODERATE — macro-dependent):** $3.0B CRE portfolio (31% of loans) faces stress in an environment of elevated rates. Management's conservative ACL modeling (50/50 baseline/recession) acknowledges this risk. Full adverse scenario would require $32.5M additional reserves.

4. **Two Consecutive GAAP Earnings Misses (MODERATE — informational):** While adjusted figures beat, the GAAP misses create uncertainty about whether merger costs are accurately projected. If costs overrun, the accretion math deteriorates.

5. **DOJ Consent Order (LOW — ongoing):** Active through 2028, requiring management attention and $7.75M community investment in Columbus MSA. Constrains operational flexibility in Park's largest metro market.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185-195):**
- First Citizens integration proceeds ahead of schedule with system conversion in Q3 2026 generating immediate cost savings
- NIM sustains above 4.50% through FY26-27 as deposit costs remain low
- FY26 EPS approaches $12.50-13.00 (beating current $12.23 consensus)
- Multiple re-rates to 15x forward on demonstrated acquisition accretion
- Dividend increase to $4.60-4.80 by early 2027
- CRE portfolio performs without material NCOs

**Bear Case ($145-150):**
- Durbin Amendment implementation reduces debit interchange by $10-12M annually with no offset
- Integration costs overrun (currently projected at $15.5M Q1 + $5-10M Q2-Q3)
- CRE stress materializes — if full adverse scenario triggers, $32.5M additional provision compresses EPS to $10.50-11.00
- Consumer auto lending deteriorates as H2 2026 consumer weakness materializes
- Stock de-rates to 13x trough earnings = $137-143

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) showing clean GAAP beat with merger costs below $5M, confirming integration is largely complete — would validate the $12.23+ earnings trajectory
2. Stock pulls back to $155-160 range (12.7-13.1x forward) while estimates remain stable or rising — would provide 15%+ upside to consensus targets
3. Management provides explicit quantification of Durbin Amendment impact below $8M annually, indicating the market is overestimating the regulatory headwind

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NPLs exceed 1.5% of loans or NCOs exceed 0.40% annualized — would indicate First Citizens credit book is worse than modeled
2. NIM compresses below 4.20% — would signal deposit franchise erosion
3. FY26 EPS estimates decline below $11.50 (reversing recent positive trajectory)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026) is the next critical data point. This will be the first quarter with lower merger costs and the first indication of organic Tennessee region performance.

## Conclusion

Park National is a well-run community bank with an exceptional NIM, successful CEO transition, and a strategically sound acquisition that crosses the $10B threshold deliberately. The positive estimate revision trajectory and strong Q1 adjusted results support a constructive view. However, at $169.58 (~14x forward), the stock offers only 8-11% upside to consensus targets while facing quantifiable regulatory headwinds, GAAP visibility questions from merger costs, and integration execution risk through Q3 2026.

The specific reason to HOLD rather than BUY: the risk/reward is approximately symmetric — ~10% upside to the bull case target of $185 versus ~12% downside to bear case of $150, with no identifiable catalyst the market is mispricing. The estimate revisions, while positive, reflect acquisition arithmetic already captured in price action (stock up from $149 low to $170). A cleaner entry point at $155-160 or confirmation of clean post-merger economics in Q2 earnings would shift the risk/reward favorably.

This is a growth_hold: the underlying business is improving, the management transition is orderly, and the acquisition appears strategically sound. The recommendation reflects timing and valuation rather than fundamental concern.
