## Executive Summary

Coherent has become an AI-interconnect company with a legacy industrial tail. Datacenter and Communications is now roughly 75% of revenue, datacenter sales grew 37% year over year in fiscal Q3 2026, and the fiscal Q4 guidance midpoint of $1.98B implies about 28% year-over-year growth against a derived Q4 FY2025 base of roughly $1.55B — acceleration, not deceleration. Non-GAAP gross margin reached 39.6% in Q3 with management targeting above 42%. Net leverage fell to 0.5x from 2.1x a year earlier. NVIDIA bought 7,788,161 shares at $256.80 for $2.0B in March 2026 alongside a multiyear co-packaged-optics supply arrangement. Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 7.5% in 90 days to $8.37. The operating story is intact and improving.

The problem is what sits underneath the income statement. Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was approximately $10M against roughly $503M in the prior-year period, while capital expenditure ran approximately $547M — implying about -$537M of free cash flow in a year of record earnings. Inventories rose from $1,437.6M at June 30, 2025 to approximately $2,127M at March 31, 2026, a 48% increase against 19% nine-month revenue growth; inventory days moved from roughly 139 to roughly 173. Management's explanation — a multi-month lag between indium phosphide device production and transceiver shipments during an accelerated capacity doubling — is operationally coherent and consistent with the disclosed industry-wide InP shortage. It is not yet verified. The balance-sheet improvement was also financed rather than earned: the roughly $2.14B increase in total cash since June 30, 2025 traces to the $2.0B NVIDIA placement plus about $400M of Aerospace & Defense divestiture proceeds, not to operations. Separately, a year-to-date effective tax rate near 3% flatters GAAP EPS by roughly 15%.

At $326.55, the shares are 25.8% below the 52-week high of $440 and 287% above the August 2025 low of $84.35. On FY2027 consensus of $8.37 the multiple is 39x with 54% expected EPS growth — a PEG of about 0.72, which is not demanding for the position Coherent occupies. On the fiscal year now closing, the multiple is roughly 60x with negative free cash flow. Fiscal Q4 results land within days: near-term at-the-money implied volatility is 153.8% against 84.6% one-year realized, the term structure is in backwardation, and the two-day expiry prices an ±11.4% move. Last year's equivalent print produced a 19.6% single-day decline (August 13, 2025 close $114.01 to August 14 close $91.65). Probability-weighting a $370 base, $465 bull and $200 bear case yields roughly +8% to +12% expected return against 85% realized volatility. That is a hold, with an identifiable upgrade path if Q4 operating cash flow inflects.

## Company Overview

Coherent is a vertically integrated photonics manufacturer formed from the July 2022 combination of II-VI with Coherent, Inc. — a transaction still visible in the balance sheet as $4,471M of goodwill and $3,205M of intangibles at FY2025, together 51% of the $14,911M asset base. The company employs approximately 30,216 people (83% in manufacturing) and holds roughly 3,100 patents.

Effective July 1, 2025 the company realigned from three segments (Networking, Materials, Lasers) into two end-market segments: Datacenter and Communications, and Industrial. Recast historical segment data was furnished by 8-K in December 2025. FY2025 10-K segment disclosures are therefore not directly comparable to FY2026 reporting.

The portfolio spans 800G/1.6T pluggable transceivers and the components inside them (VCSELs, DMLs, EMLs, CW lasers, detectors, ICs, isolators, thermoelectric coolers), silicon photonics, co-packaged-optics enabling technology, an emerging optical circuit switch family, telecom transmission components and line systems, and an Industrial segment covering precision lasers, semiconductor and display capital equipment, SiC substrates and GaN-on-SiC RF.

Portfolio pruning has been active. Aerospace & Defense was divested September 2, 2025 for roughly $400M with an approximate $115M gain. A Munich materials-processing tools division closed January 30, 2026, removing about $25M of quarterly revenue and producing an approximate $96M total loss once prior impairment is included.

### FY2026 year-to-date

Fiscal Q3 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) revenue was $1,806M, +21% year over year and +7% sequentially (management cited +27% and +9% adjusting for divestitures). Non-GAAP gross margin was 39.6%, up 105bps year over year; non-GAAP operating margin 20.3%; non-GAAP EPS $1.41, +55%. GAAP net earnings attributable to Coherent were $191M, GAAP diluted EPS $0.97. Nine-month revenue was $5,073M (+19%) with nine-month GAAP diluted EPS of $2.92.

Working from these disclosures: Q3 $1,806M with Q2 implied near $1,688M leaves Q1 near $1,579M; adding the Q4 guidance midpoint of $1,980M gives FY2026 revenue of roughly $7,053M, or +21.4%. Nine-month FY2025 of $4,263M implies Q4 FY2025 near $1,547M, so the Q4 FY2026 guide is roughly +28% year over year.

Fiscal Q4 guidance (issued May 6, 2026): revenue $1.91B–$2.05B; non-GAAP gross margin 39%–41%; non-GAAP operating expense $360M–$380M; non-GAAP EPS $1.52–$1.72. Consensus for the quarter sits at $1.62 — precisely the guidance midpoint, with 19 analysts contributing. That matters, because in Q3 non-GAAP EPS of $1.41 matched consensus while revenue beat, and the stock fell about 2.7% afterward. The bar is set at the midpoint, and matching it has recently not been enough.

### Cash conversion is the central financial issue

|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | ~$503M | ~$10M |
| Capex | — | ~$547M |
| Free cash flow (derived) | — | ~(-$537M) |

FY2025 full year for reference: OCF $633.6M, capex $440.8M, FCF $192.8M.

The proximate drivers disclosed in the cash flow statement are an inventory build of roughly $690M plus about $229M of adverse movement in other operating net assets. Q3 capex alone was $290M, nearly double the prior-year quarter. Two independent framings of the inventory build: inventory days moved from approximately 139 (FY2025) to approximately 173 (annualizing Q3 COGS), and inventory relative to one quarter of revenue moved from 0.93x to 1.18x. Revenue grew 19% over nine months while inventory grew about 48% — the build is not an artifact of scale.

Management's stated cause is a multi-month lag between InP device production and final transceiver shipments, with internal InP capacity doubling by calendar year-end (a quarter ahead of plan), doubling again by end-2027, and a third site coming up in Zurich. The disclosures do not rule out two alternatives: build-ahead against customer qualifications that could slip (management flagged that 6-inch wafers, CPO, multi-rail power and Optical Connectivity Systems are all early-stage with qualifications pending), or defensive safety stock against China, rare-earth and InP supply risk. A single quarter should not be annualized here — working capital is tied to discrete capacity milestones — but nine months is long enough to conclude that FY2026 will very likely be a negative-FCF year despite record earnings. As a sensitivity, a strong Q4 rebound to $400M of OCF against roughly $320M of capex would still leave FY2026 FCF near -$457M.

### Balance sheet

Approximate Q3 FY2026 components: cash ~$1,593M, short-term investments ~$825M, restricted cash ~$633M, total borrowings ~$3,194M. Management's stated "$3.0 billion of cash" reconciles as $1,593M + $825M + $633M = $3,051M — meaning roughly 21% of the headline figure is restricted and should not be treated as fungible. Unrestricted cash plus short-term investments is about $2,418M, giving net debt near $776M. Back-solving management's stated 0.5x leverage from that net-debt figure implies TTM company-defined adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1.55B; the FY2025 proxy reported a company-selected Adjusted EBITDA of $1,318.6M, so the trajectory is consistent.

Solvency risk is low. But the deleveraging from 2.1x to 0.5x was funded: total cash rose approximately $2.14B since June 30, 2025 against approximately $2.4B of identifiable inflows from the NVIDIA placement and the A&D sale, with roughly $10M of operating cash flow. Long-term debt has nonetheless declined organically each year since FY2023, and interest expense fell from $288.5M (FY2024) to $243.3M (FY2025).

### Earnings quality

Year-to-date FY2026 effective tax rate was approximately 3%, attributed to German tax law changes, uncertain-tax-position releases and excess stock-compensation benefits. Normalizing to 18% while holding pretax income constant reduces nine-month GAAP diluted EPS of $2.92 by roughly 15%, to about $2.47. None of these items is recurring in nature. Separately, the supplied profile fields show trailing EPS of -$0.52 alongside a trailing P/E of 167.76; neither reconciles to the 10-Q's nine-month GAAP diluted EPS of $2.92 (a positive P/E cannot derive from a negative EPS), and the two profile fields are internally inconsistent. I use filing and consensus figures throughout and disregard the profile EPS/P/E fields.

GAAP returns look poor — FY2025 ROE 0.87%, ROA 0.33% — because merger intangible amortization runs through the income statement against a goodwill-heavy asset base. That depresses net-income-based ratios without affecting operating cash flow, EV/EBITDA or the non-GAAP margin path. It is a reason to weight cash- and EV-based measures more heavily, not a reason to dismiss the returns entirely: EBIT/assets of 2.3% is genuinely weak, and it is that operating term, not the market-cap term, that describes the business.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on four verifiable legs and one unverified one.

**Verified: datacenter revenue acceleration.** Datacenter sales +13% sequentially and +37% year over year in Q3, with Datacenter and Communications up more than 40% year over year and now roughly 75% of the company. The Q4 guide implies company-level acceleration to +28% year over year from +21% for the nine months.

**Verified: margin expansion.** Non-GAAP gross margin 39.6% in Q3, guided 39%–41% for Q4, against 30.9% at the FY2024 trough. Management targets above 42%. Non-GAAP operating margin 20.3%.

**Verified: estimate revisions rising.** FY2027 consensus EPS rose from $7.79 to $8.37 over 90 days (+7.5%), with the current and next quarter each up 5%–6%. Next-year revisions rose 1.4% in the last 30 days. This is positive earnings momentum, though below the +30%/90-day threshold that marks a violent inflection — the signal here is supportive rather than decisive.

**Verified: capacity investment tied to a named constraint.** 6-inch InP yields for key devices already exceed legacy 3-inch yields; first transceivers built with 6-inch components have shipped; Sherman, Texas expansion is tied to the InP shortage; Zurich is the third site.

**Unverified: the order book.** Management describes commitments into calendar 2028 and long-term agreements running through the end of the decade, and a multi-billion-dollar NVIDIA purchase commitment. No quantified backlog or remaining-performance-obligation figure was disclosed, and the commitment's size, term and conditionality (take-or-pay versus volume- or qualification-contingent) are undisclosed. This should not be treated as booked revenue.

Offsets: Industrial declined both sequentially and year over year, with newer thermal products not expected to ramp until the second half of calendar 2027. Divestitures removed roughly $100M of annualized Munich revenue plus the A&D business. External suppliers have raised prices on items such as lasers.

Consensus FY2027 EPS of $8.37 implies 54% growth over the FY2026 estimate of $5.45. Reaching it requires roughly 25% revenue growth to about $8.8B with continued margin expansion — demanding, but consistent with contracted capacity and the guided Q4 exit rate.

Because the fiscal year ends June 30, FY2027 is the operative forward year, and 39x with 54% expected EPS growth is the fairest single characterization of the multiple. A PEG near 0.73 is not a stretched valuation for the leading Western supplier into an interconnect bottleneck, and it sits below the level at which analyst targets stop being informative. The sell-side mean of $394.62 (median $400.00) implies 20.8%–22.5% upside; the range is $230 to $465.

Peer comparison is close to useless here. The Electronic Components median P/E of 77.2 is contaminated by loss-makers and trough earners (LFUS at -1380x, VSH at 171x, ROG at 77x), none of which has Coherent's AI-interconnect exposure or growth rate. The more relevant comparison set — AI-optics and interconnect suppliers — is not in the supplied peer table, so I anchor on absolute forward multiples and PEG rather than sector medians.

What the price requires: several consecutive years of 20%+ revenue growth, delivery on the path to above 42% gross margin, and no multiple compression. It also requires the inventory to convert. A company at 39x forward earnings with negative trailing free cash flow has no valuation floor from cash generation; the entire support is the forward earnings path. That is the reason this is a 5.5 and not a 7 — the multiple is justifiable but not asymmetric, and the margin of safety is thin against the identified binaries.

An illustrative FY2027 build (my assumptions, not guidance): 25% revenue growth to $8.8B, 22% non-GAAP operating margin = $1.94B operating income, less ~$180M interest, at a 15% tax rate over ~200M diluted shares = roughly $7.48 non-GAAP EPS, or 43.7x. That sits modestly below consensus and highlights how sensitive the multiple is to the tax rate and share count.

## Competitive Landscape

The strongest verifiable edge is indium phosphide. Management describes InP as constrained industry-wide; Coherent owns multiple InP fabs in the U.S. and Europe, has moved 6-inch yields above legacy 3-inch on key devices, and has shipped first transceivers using 6-inch components. In a supply-constrained market, owning expanding internal capacity converts a constraint from a revenue cap into a share-gain opportunity. That is the defensible core of the bull case, and it is near-term rather than speculative.

Two structural offsets sit against it.

First, the company's own 10-K observes that "aside from datacenter transceivers, there are very few industry-standard products." The fastest-growing part of the portfolio is also the most standardized, and therefore the most exposed to price competition from Innolight and Eoptolink, while the differentiated legacy businesses are the slower-growing ones.

Second, co-packaged optics. Moving the optical engine onto the switch package reduces the number of discrete pluggable transceivers sold per switch. Coherent's answer is to be a CPO supplier — it lists CPO-enabling technologies, sizes the opportunity above $15B, and per the Q3 call has a multiyear CPO supply agreement with NVIDIA. Whether CPO is margin-accretive or margin-dilutive relative to pluggables at scale is unresolved by anything in the disclosures. Broadcom is the recognized incumbent in switch-integrated CPO.

The NVIDIA investment should be read carefully. The 10-Q describes the agreement as non-exclusive, and NVIDIA invested $2.0B in Lumentum on comparable terms plus roughly $1B elsewhere (externally reported, not verified against filings). NVIDIA is de-risking its own interconnect bottleneck by capitalizing multiple suppliers. That is a strong qualification signal for Coherent's roadmap and meaningful revenue visibility; it is not share protection.

Direct customer concentration has not improved. The FY2025 10-K states two customers each exceeded 10% of revenue, and no updated FY2026 disclosure is available. The mix shift to 75% Datacenter and Communications establishes end-market composition, not buyer diversification — with AI purchasing concentrated among a handful of hyperscalers and accelerator vendors, the likelier direction is worse concentration. The FY2026 10-K disclosure is a specific item to check.

No independent third-party transceiver share data was obtainable, so the competitive assessment rests on filings and industry structure and is the least well-evidenced part of this analysis.

## Risk Assessment

**BIS/Huawei inquiry (unquantified).** The 10-Q discloses a Bureau of Industry and Security inquiry relating to sales to Huawei; Coherent has ceased shipping and states it is unable to estimate a possible loss. Open-ended, unquantified regulatory exposure at a company with substantial China manufacturing and sales.

**AI demand durability and model efficiency.** The risk factors explicitly contemplate that AI model-efficiency advances could reduce interconnect intensity. With 75% of revenue in one end market and capex more than doubling to serve it, the cyclical cushion that Industrial once provided is gone.

**Inventory conversion.** The $690M build and the roughly -$537M nine-month FCF are the single largest fundamental uncertainty. If the InP-to-transceiver lag is the cause, this reverses in one to two quarters. If qualifications slip, inventory sits.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** $4.47B and $3.20B at FY2025. The Munich exit already demonstrated the mechanism with a roughly $96M total loss including prior impairment. A datacenter-led valuation does not protect the carrying value of declining legacy assets.

**Bain overhang.** The Series B preferred converted into approximately 30.1M shares at an $85 conversion price — on the order of 15% of shares outstanding (my calculation; post-conversion governance rights and any selling activity were not verified).

**NVIDIA price-protection provision.** Reference price $256.80, effective for six months from execution (through approximately September 2, 2026). The stock traded near $236 within the window (mid-March 2026). At $326.55 it appears out of the money on a spot basis, and the mechanics are unverified. Illustrative sensitivity: share settlement at a $236 reference would create roughly 0.68M additional shares, about 0.35% of the count. Worth noting, not material to value.

**Tax normalization.** GAAP EPS growth in FY2026 overstates underlying improvement by roughly 15%.

**Tariffs.** A February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs creates a potential refund that has not been recorded — a possible one-time gain, not run-rate earnings.

Against these, short interest is only 5.2% of float with 1.6 days to cover, so crowded-short mechanics are not a factor in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent, large, single-name event and is unusually asymmetric in composition. Near-term at-the-money implied volatility is 153.8% against 84.6% one-year realized — a 69-point spread, at the 89th percentile within the current chain — with the term structure in clear backwardation (153.8% near-term versus 86.7% at six months). The two-day expiry prices an ±11.4% move; the 16-day tenor prices ±17.3%. Fiscal Q4 results have not yet been reported (last print May 6), and last year's equivalent August print produced a 19.6% single-day decline. Since the June 1 snapshot, at-the-money IV rose from 106.0% to 153.8% while the price fell 6.7% and put/call open interest rose from 1.19 to 1.29 — rising uncertainty on a falling price.

Composition cuts the other way from the level: put/call volume is 0.76 and near-term skew is described as call-heavy at -13.2%, while open interest sits at 1.29 puts per call. The most consistent reading is that standing positions carry protection while fresh near-dated flow is buying upside into the print. Near-term volatility is expensive relative to realized, which argues against paying up for exposure in the next two sessions and, for existing holders, makes covered-call or collar structures unusually well-compensated. None of this changes the fundamental view; it argues for entry discipline around the event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $465 (+42.4%)

Q4 FY2026 delivers above the $1.98B/$1.62 midpoint and operating cash flow inflects sharply as the InP inventory converts, validating management's lag explanation and confirming that FY2026's negative free cash flow was pre-positioning against contracted demand. FY2027 revenue grows 25%+ to roughly $8.8–9.0B with gross margin crossing 42%, delivering EPS above the $8.37 consensus. Internal InP capacity doubling lands on schedule, giving Coherent share gains in a supply-constrained market while competitors are capacity-limited. The NVIDIA CPO agreement converts to disclosed revenue in the second half of calendar 2026, and Optical Connectivity Systems begins contributing against a stated TAM above $4B. Industrial troughs and turns in H2 2027. At 55x FY2027 consensus — or roughly 42x a FY2028 EPS near $11 — the shares reach the sell-side high of $465.

### Bear Case — $200 (-38.8%)

Two mechanisms, either sufficient. First, cash: Q4 operating cash flow disappoints again, inventory days stay above 170, and the market re-rates a 39x-forward stock that cannot fund its own capex. Second, demand: an AI digestion phase or CPO transition compresses pluggable volumes faster than Coherent's CPO position ramps, FY2027 EPS is cut toward $6.00, and the multiple compresses to the low 30s. Contributing factors include the BIS inquiry resolving with a material penalty, Bain distributing its ~15% stake, worsening customer concentration disclosed in the FY2026 10-K, and goodwill impairment on the declining legacy assets. My bear case sits below the $230 sell-side floor because at 85% realized volatility, with a 28% single-month drawdown already recorded this summer and a 19.6% single-day post-earnings decline last August, the left tail is wider than the published range implies.

### Base Case — $370 (+13.3%)

44x FY2027 consensus EPS of $8.37. Revenue grows roughly 25% in FY2027, gross margin advances toward 41%, and operating cash flow recovers to positive but not to a level that produces meaningful free cash flow until FY2027 given the capex program. Coherent holds its InP advantage but shares AI-optics growth with Lumentum and the Chinese module vendors, and CPO proves roughly margin-neutral. The multiple holds in the low-to-mid 40s on FY2027 as growth decelerates toward 25–30% in FY2028.

Probability-weighted (base 50%, bull 25–30%, bear 20–25%), expected return is approximately +8% to +12% — positive, but modest against this level of volatility and with a binary event inside 48 hours.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is positive with identifiable catalysts, and existing owners are compensated for holding. New capital lacks an adequate margin of safety at 39x forward with negative trailing free cash flow into an unreported quarter priced for an ±11.4% two-day move.

**Reassessment timeline:** Fiscal Q4 2026 results, expected within days of this report, followed by the FY2026 10-K (customer concentration, restricted-cash purpose, BIS status) and the FY2026 proxy (whether the operating-cash-flow compensation metric pays out).

**Upgrade triggers — to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. **Q4 FY2026 operating cash flow above $400M with inventory days falling below 160.** This is the decisive test of the InP-lag explanation. Confirmation converts the FY2026 FCF deficit from a red flag into pre-positioned capacity and removes the largest single objection to the multiple.
2. **Q4 revenue above the $2.05B high end with FY2027 revenue growth guided above 25%,** combined with FY2027 consensus EPS revisions extending above $9.00. That would put the forward multiple below 36x on rising numbers.
3. **Price weakness to $260–$280 on unchanged fundamentals** (31x–33x FY2027 consensus, PEG below 0.62). At that level the base case offers 32%–42% upside against the bear case, and staged accumulation is warranted regardless of the near-term print.

**Downgrade triggers — to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. **A second consecutive quarter of operating cash flow below $150M,** or inventory days above 180 at June 30, 2026. That would indicate the build is not converting on the disclosed timetable.
2. **FY2027 revenue growth guided below 15%, or FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $7.00.** Either would break the earnings path that supports a 39x multiple.
3. **A quantified BIS penalty above $250M, or disclosure that customer concentration worsened to a single customer above 20% of revenue.** The first is an unaccrued cash liability; the second materially changes the risk profile of the 75% datacenter mix.
4. **A goodwill or intangible impairment above $500M,** which would signal that the legacy asset base is deteriorating faster than the datacenter business is compounding.

**Position management for existing holders:** near-term implied volatility at 153.8% against 84.6% realized makes overwriting attractive around the print. Do not add before the release.

## Conclusion

Coherent's operating trajectory is the strongest it has been since the II-VI merger. Revenue is accelerating into the fiscal fourth quarter, non-GAAP gross margin has expanded roughly 870bps from the FY2024 trough, net leverage is 0.5x, and NVIDIA has made a $2.0B equity investment alongside a multiyear co-packaged-optics supply arrangement (the direction and dollar size of the associated purchase commitment are undisclosed). Forward estimates are rising, and at 39x FY2027 consensus with 54% expected EPS growth, the multiple is defensible rather than stretched.

Three specific items prevent a stronger call at $326.55. Nine-month free cash flow of approximately -$537M in a record-earnings year, driven by a 48% inventory build, has an operationally credible explanation that remains unverified until the Q4 cash flow statement is filed. The balance-sheet transformation was financed by the NVIDIA placement and asset sales rather than earned, and roughly $633M of the headline $3.0B cash balance is restricted. And the shares carry a dense set of unquantified binaries — the BIS inquiry, FY2026 customer concentration, the Bain stake, CPO mix economics — into a print that the options market has priced at ±11.4% over two days, with last August's equivalent event having produced a 19.6% single-day decline.

The overall score of 6.1 is unchanged from the prior review, but the composition has shifted: financial health steps down from 7.0 on the quantified cash-conversion breakdown, while the growth and estimate-revision picture holds and the entry price is 6.9% better. That combination — improving numerator, deteriorating cash quality, better price — nets to a hold with a clearly specified upgrade path. If Q4 operating cash flow inflects above $400M with inventory days falling, this becomes a straightforward accumulate; if it does not, a 39x forward multiple on a business that cannot fund its own capex will be re-examined by a market that has already shown it will take 20% out of this stock in a single session.
