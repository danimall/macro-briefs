## Executive Summary

SunCoke has delivered a real operational recovery from a bad FY2025. The company raised 2026 guidance at the July 30 Q2 print — consolidated Adjusted EBITDA to $250-265M from $230-250M, operating cash flow to $240-260M, with free cash flow of $150-160M disclosed for the first time — and reported gross/net leverage of 2.73x/2.55x at June 30 versus 3.16x/2.76x at December 31. Q2 diluted EPS of $0.15 (GAAP per the 10-Q; the surprise data shows $0.16 against a $0.08 estimate, a basis discrepancy) compared with $0.02 a year earlier. On guidance, the stock trades at 5.5x EV/EBITDA with a 19.4% headline equity free-cash-flow yield and a 5.1% dividend covered 3.8x.

The composition of that improvement is what constrains the rating. All of the H1 2026 consolidated EBITDA growth came from Industrial Services (+$39.2M year over year); Domestic Coke H1 EBITDA declined $12.6M. Management stated on the call that the Q2 Industrial Services result was inflated by "extraordinary" terminal volumes (6.7 million tons, attributed partly to coal price arbitrage and supply-chain concern related to the Iran conflict) and non-recurring seasonal slag sales, guiding H2 back toward roughly $25-27M per quarter versus $34.4M in Q2. Roughly $10M of the guided free cash flow is a prior-year cash tax refund that reverses to an outflow in 2027, and H1 free cash flow of $12.6M against a $150-160M full-year guide implies H2 free cash flow of $137-147M, most of which is balance-sheet conversion (the ~$65M of receivables collected in early July) rather than earnings.

The binary the market has not priced is Granite City. The U.S. Steel contract expires December 31, 2026 (four and a half months from now) and was extended in January 2026 for only 590 Kt at what the 10-K explicitly calls "lower economics," with the plant in "turn-down mode." No renewal, non-renewal, or negotiation update has been disclosed, and no analyst asked about it on the Q2 call. U.S. Steel operates its own coke capacity at Clairton and Gary and can self-supply. Non-renewal would remove roughly $20-30M of EBITDA and, following the Haverhill I template, likely trigger an impairment test against $1,155.3M of Domestic Coke segment assets supported by $585.6M of equity and $40.3M of retained earnings. At $9.40, with the stock at the $9.50 consensus mean and up 70% from the February low of $5.515, the covered dividend and deleveraging path justify holding; they do not justify new capital ahead of an undisclosed contract decision. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

SunCoke is the largest independent producer of high-quality coke in the Americas, operating five owned U.S. cokemaking plants with 730 ovens and approximately 3,690 Kt of nameplate capacity following the Q1 2026 closure of Haverhill I. Management estimates its facilities represent roughly 38% of U.S. blast furnace coke capacity (a self-reported figure). It is the only North American producer using heat-recovery technology and holds 102 U.S. and 116 foreign patents. The company also operates, but does not own, the 1,700 Kt Vitória plant in Brazil for ArcelorMittal Brasil under licensing and operating agreements expiring January 2028 — fee income (~$9.4M in Q2 2026) rather than tonnage economics.

The contract architecture is the business. Coke sales agreements are take-or-pay with pass-through of coal, operating and maintenance, transportation, tax and regulatory costs, plus a fixed fee per ton set for the contract term. SunCoke retains one operating risk: it bears the cost of coal consumed above the contractual coal-to-coke yield standard and captures the benefit when it beats it. Customers hold an overriding vote on coal procurement through a coal committee, which limits independent input optimization.

Contract expiries define the forward story: Indiana Harbor (Cliffs, 1,220 Kt) to September 2035; Middletown (Cliffs, 550 Kt) to December 2032; Haverhill II (Cliffs 500 Kt to December 2028; Algoma 150 Kt to December 2026, already breached); Granite City (U.S. Steel, 650 Kt nameplate) to December 31, 2026; Jewell (720 Kt) uncontracted foundry, spot and export.

Industrial Services comprises three bulk terminals (Convent Marine Terminal in Louisiana, Kanawha River, Lake Terminal) with over 40 million tons of annual transload capacity, plus 15 molten slag handling and processing sites across the U.S., Brazil, Slovakia and Spain acquired with Phoenix Global on August 1, 2025 for $271.5M of cash net of cash acquired ($294.0M total net consideration), funded largely on the revolver.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue has declined for three consecutive years: $2,063.2M (2023) → $1,935.4M (2024) → $1,837.3M (2025). Adjusted EBITDA fell from $272.8M to $219.2M in 2025, and GAAP operating income swung to a $44.4M loss on a $90.3M long-lived asset impairment ($90.1M at Haverhill I; $68.1M after tax) plus $34.7M of higher D&A and a $23.6M SG&A increase. The FY2025 Domestic Coke bridge separates cyclical from structural clearly: volume −$45.0M, price −$39.8M, O&M +$12.9M, energy/other +$7.2M. Domestic Coke EBITDA per ton fell 20.5% to $46.35 and capacity utilization from 100% to 93%.

**FY2025 cash conversion was the weak point.** Operating cash flow of $109.1M less capex of $66.8M produced $42.3M of free cash flow — a 2.3% margin — against $41.4M of dividends. Coverage was approximately 1.0x. Cash fell from $189.6M to $88.7M and long-term debt rose to $685.5M from $492.3M funding Phoenix.

**H1 2026 shows real improvement with a large timing distortion.** Q2 revenue of $475.3M (+9.5%) and Adjusted EBITDA of $69.6M (+59.6%) look strong, but segment detail matters: H1 Domestic Coke EBITDA of $77.8M was down from $90.4M, while Industrial Services rose from $21.4M to $60.6M. Q2 operating cash flow was negative $27.2M as receivables rose from $111.5M to $209.3M; management stated roughly $65M was collected in early July, so Q2 OCF is not a run rate. Equally, the full-year OCF guide is not de-risked: H1 OCF of $45.5M against $240-260M implies H2 of $194.5-214.5M, 4.3-4.7x the first half.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026).** Total assets $1,743.2M; SXC equity $585.6M; noncontrolling interest $27.9M. Debt is $500M of 4.875% senior notes due 2029 plus $160.5M drawn on a revolver due July 2030 — $660.5M gross, $617.8M net of $42.7M cash. Total liquidity $207.2M. Covenants are 4.50x maximum net leverage and 2.50x minimum interest coverage; on guidance, coverage runs roughly 7.7x ($257.5M against ~$33.5M cash interest). No maturities before 2029. The notes are marked at $633.4M against $660.5M carrying, about 4% below par, consistent with BB-/B1 ratings.

**The Altman Z-Score debate.** The screen returns 1.88 on FY2025 data (grey zone). Recomputed on the June 30 balance sheet with actual retained earnings of $40.3M and $798M of market equity, Z is approximately 1.63 on trailing EBIT and 1.89 on guided 2026 EBIT of roughly $86.5M ($257.5M EBITDA less ~$171M D&A). The weakness is driven by near-zero retained earnings — a legacy of the former MLP structure — and high asset intensity, not by the impairment. Given 2.55x net leverage against a 4.50x covenant, 7.7x coverage and no near-term maturities, the score is better read as a warning about book-value fragility and impairment absorption capacity than as a default probability. Financial health scores 5.8: the forward credit metrics are solidly investment-grade-adjacent for a materials cyclical, while thin equity relative to a $1.16B segment asset base is the specific vulnerability.

**The D&A-versus-capex gap is the central earnings-quality question.** Guided 2026 D&A of roughly $171M against capex of $90-100M is what produces a 19.4% free-cash-flow yield alongside a high forward GAAP P/E. Historical capex ran $66.8-72.9M against D&A of roughly $130-170M [unverified — full D&A series not provided in sources]. Coke oven batteries at plants built in 1962, 1998, 2008, 2009 and 2011 require periodic relines and rebuilds, and 2026 capex guidance already stepped up roughly 40% from 2025. A defensible normalized owner-earnings frame: $257.5M EBITDA less $33.5M interest less roughly $120M of economic depreciation = $104M pre-tax, or approximately $78M after a 25% tax rate — $0.92 per share, about 10.2x. That is the valuation anchor, sitting between the flattering FCF yield and the punitive GAAP multiple.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative and the acquired growth is partly non-durable.

**Domestic Coke is shrinking by design and by attrition.** Haverhill I closed in Q1 2026 ($6.4M of closure costs in Q1, $0.4M in Q2), removing capacity. Algoma's 150 Kt is permanently gone: SunCoke disclosed at the end of Q3 2025 that Algoma breached and refused further tons, and Algoma has since permanently halted blast furnace operations in favor of a new EAF melt shop (externally reported; Algoma's filings not read directly). If Granite City is not renewed, owned capacity falls to roughly 3,040 Kt, of which 2,270 Kt is contracted entirely to Cliffs. Guided 2026 domestic coke sales are approximately 3.4M tons versus 3,668 Kt in 2025 and 4,028 Kt in 2024.

**Industrial Services carries the growth, with acknowledged non-recurrence.** Full-year guidance of $110-115M against H1 actual of $60.6M implies roughly $25-27M per quarter in H2 against $34.4M in Q2 — a management-confirmed step-down. Management framed the ~$60-61M Phoenix EBITDA baseline as a floor with $5-10M of synergies achieved in 2026 and "full synergies in 2027," which is the one genuine forward growth lever. Against that, the Q2 terminal surge was explicitly attributed in part to energy and supply-chain concern tied to the Iran conflict. Should Hormuz transit normalize on a verified basis, the coal arbitrage that drove 6.7 million tons of Q2 handling volume compresses.

**2027 arithmetic.** Assume Industrial Services at $115-120M with full synergies offsetting normalized terminal volumes, Domestic Coke at roughly $170M if Granite City renews at similar economics, and corporate costs near −$30M: consolidated EBITDA approximately $255-260M, flat. Cash taxes turn from a net $8-12M receipt to an outflow, which alone removes $25-35M of free cash flow. Absent a Granite City renewal at improved terms or a Brazil extension beyond January 2028, 2027 free cash flow of $110-125M is the reasonable central case versus $150-160M guided for 2026.

**On the estimate revisions.** Consensus current-year EPS moved from $0.20 to $0.32 (+60%) and next year from −$0.19 to $0.29 (+252.6%). The magnitude is large, but it rests on a single contributing analyst, and the revised path shows next-year EPS *below* current-year — no earnings growth to support a PEG-based case — with the stock already at roughly 29x current-year and 32x next-year GAAP EPS. The revision signal is real but does not meet the bar that would make it an independent buy trigger; the multiple already reflects the improvement. Growth scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

SunCoke's moat is technical and locational: sole heat-recovery producer in North America, the only greenfield U.S. coke plants built in 35+ years, long-dated take-or-pay contracts with pass-through economics, and a 2025 TRIR of 0.55 against BLS benchmarks of 2.40 for coke manufacturing. Convent Marine Terminal's CN rail access is a real logistics advantage.

The trajectory is nonetheless deteriorating, for three reasons.

**The end market is in structural transition.** Electric arc furnaces do not consume metallurgical coke. Algoma is the live proof: contract breach, permanent blast furnace halt, and 150 Kt of contracted volume eliminated rather than deferred. SunCoke says it is "actively pursuing all avenues to enforce the contract and recover any financial losses," but no litigation is disclosed in the 10-K Item 3 or the Q2 10-Q, and no receivable or gain contingency is recognized. Any recovery is an unmodeled option constrained by Algoma's own condition (a $68M Q2 2026 net loss on $13.8M of Adjusted EBITDA, externally reported).

**Customer concentration is worsening, not improving.** Cliffs was 61.0% of Q2 2026 revenue (from 57.8%); top-two concentration rose to 74.6% from 71.7%. The half-year comparison improved (77.0% → 73.8%), so the direction depends on the window. The Phoenix acquisition diversified *revenue mix* — Industrial Services revenue went from $13.9M to $97.6M in the quarter — while Cliffs revenue rose $39.3M and cokemaking revenue fell $39.7M. That arithmetic is consistent with a meaningful share of acquired mill-services revenue being generated at Cleveland-Cliffs facilities, which would deepen rather than dilute single-counterparty exposure. SunCoke does not disaggregate customer revenue by segment, so this is an inference from the totals, not a disclosed fact; coal-cost pass-through timing and the FOB New Orleans index change are alternative contributors. What is a filing fact is that the concentration ratio rose.

**Granite City cuts both ways.** The externally reported sequence is constructive: U.S. Steel announced in September 2025 it would halt raw steel processing at Granite City Works, reversed within ten days, and restarted one of two blast furnaces in late April 2026, with Nippon Steel raising its U.S. Steel profit forecast to $1.1B in August 2026. SunCoke appears to have signed the January 2026 extension at "lower economics" before the restart became visible, which improves its 2027 negotiating position relative to that baseline. But U.S. Steel operates Clairton — generally described as the largest coke facility in North America — plus Gary batteries, and can self-supply. A running furnace raises coke demand without guaranteeing SunCoke captures it. Competitive position scores 4.0 on trajectory, not on asset quality.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Granite City expiry, December 31, 2026 — the dominant near-term binary.** No renewal status disclosed as of the July 30 10-Q or call, and no analyst raised it. At guided Domestic Coke EBITDA per ton of $51-52, 590 Kt implies roughly $30M of EBITDA, though the 10-K's explicit "lower economics" language suggests actual per-ton contribution is below fleet average, so $20-25M is the more likely at-risk figure. Add lost fixed-cost absorption and a probable impairment.

**2. Impairment as the transmission channel.** Haverhill I is the template: contract loss → asset group test → $90.1M write-down → GAAP loss. Domestic Coke segment assets were $1,155.3M at June 30 against $585.6M of equity and $40.3M of retained earnings. Granite City's facility net book value is not separately disclosed, so magnitude cannot be sized. Repeated write-downs would change the tone of covenant and rating conversations even without a cash event.

**3. Cliffs' blast furnace footprint is the terminal-value variable.** At 61% of revenue with contracts to 2032 and 2035, Cliffs' long-run operating configuration matters more to SunCoke's intrinsic value than anything management controls. This was not independently examined here and should be treated as an open item.

**4. Coal-to-coke yield is the retained operating risk.** SunCoke bears above-standard coal consumption. Q1 2026 yield was 48.4% against 51-52% guided, and the Middletown turbine failure plus Indiana Harbor weather cost roughly $10M of Q1 EBITDA. The turbine returned to service in May 2026 with insurance proceeds embedded in H2 guidance.

**5. Terminal earnings are geopolitically contingent.** Management tied part of the Q2 volume surge to Iran-related supply-chain and energy concern. Verified normalization of Hormuz transit would remove that tailwind; sustained disruption sustains it. This is a genuine two-sided exposure that should not be capitalized at Q2 run rates.

**6. Brazil, January 2028.** ArcelorMittal operating and licensing fee revenue of $9.4M in Q2 (~$37M annualized, likely high-margin) with no disclosed EBITDA contribution, so the 2028 cliff cannot be sized.

**7. Second-order items.** Phoenix internal-control integration is still in process and the purchase price allocation remained preliminary at Q2; goodwill of $51.9M is not tax deductible. An unexplained $10.9M reduction in the allowance for doubtful accounts (from $11.1M to $0.2M) most plausibly reflects write-offs of acquired Phoenix receivables against the existing allowance, with no P&L effect, but a collection-and-reversal explanation would have credited SG&A; H1 SG&A of $61.8M versus $35.3M is too coarse to distinguish. Low materiality, flagged for earnings quality. Labor: 53% unionized under ~10 CBAs. Legacy: $12.6M of black lung obligations, $18.8M of asset retirement obligations.

**8. Coal procurement.** 5.6M tons purchased in 2025, all third-party on one-year contracts, with $738.2M of contractual obligations through 2026. Costs pass through, but annual re-contracting plus the yield standard means coal dislocation is not fully neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options data here carry low information value and should not be leaned on. The prior ATM IV reading of 2,280% is an artifact; the current 98.7% is high in absolute terms for an $800M materials name but almost certainly reflects thin option liquidity rather than a priced catalyst. Put/call open interest of 0.08 is heavily call-tilted and offers no corroboration of a bearish view, though at this open-interest scale it also offers little corroboration of a bullish one. Short interest of 4.8% of float and 2.3 days to cover indicate an uncrowded, non-squeeze setup — informed capital is not positioned aggressively on either side of the Granite City decision, which is consistent with my read that the expiry is under-attended rather than mispriced in a specific direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (approximately 20% probability) — $13.00, +38%

Granite City renews for 2027 at improved economics on a restarted blast furnace and a strengthening U.S. Steel earnings outlook under Nippon ownership. Phoenix delivers full synergies in 2027, lifting Industrial Services to $120M+ on a normalized terminal base. Brazil extends beyond January 2028. Net debt falls to roughly $470M by year-end 2026, and management restarts the $96.3M repurchase authorization — unused since Q1 2020 and equal to 12.1% of shares at $9.40 — once leverage clears the low end of the target band. On $270M of 2027 EBITDA at 6.0x, EV of $1,620M less $480M net debt yields $1,140M of equity, or $13.43 per share. Section 232 tariffs at 50% continue to support domestic blast furnace utilization, and any Algoma recovery is incremental and unmodeled.

### Base Case (approximately 55% probability) — $10.50, +11.7%

Granite City renews on roughly current terms or is partially replaced with foundry, spot and export tons at Jewell. 2027 EBITDA lands near $255M, flat versus 2026, with Industrial Services synergies offsetting normalized terminal volumes and Domestic Coke roughly stable. Cash taxes revert to a $25-30M outflow, taking free cash flow to $115-125M — still a 14-15% yield. Year-end 2026 net debt of approximately $500M. At an unchanged 5.5x on $255M: EV $1,402M less $500M = $902M equity, or $10.63 per share; I round the base to $10.50. Total return with the 5.1% dividend approximately 17%.

### Bear Case (approximately 25% probability) — $5.75, −39%

U.S. Steel self-supplies Granite City from Clairton and Gary. SunCoke loses $20-25M of EBITDA plus fixed-cost absorption and books a second impairment against a $585.6M equity base, pushing GAAP back into loss and reopening the rating conversation at BB-/B1. Simultaneously, verified Hormuz normalization compresses the coal export arbitrage driving terminal volumes, and 2027 cash taxes turn negative. EBITDA falls to roughly $220M, free cash flow to $85-95M, and the multiple compresses to 4.5x on a shrinking asset base: EV $990M less $520M net debt = $470M equity, or $5.54 per share. The February 2026 low of $5.515 — set on the Algoma disclosure and impairment — establishes that the market will price this outcome quickly when it materializes.

**Probability-weighted price return:** 0.55(+11.7%) + 0.20(+38%) + 0.25(−39%) = **+4.2%**, plus a 5.1% dividend for roughly **+9%** over twelve months, against a bear case that removes nearly two years of dividends in a single quarter. That expected value is consistent with fair, not compelling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: 6-12 months, with the decisive event inside 4.5 months.**

- **Base case target: $10.50** — 5.5x EV/EBITDA on $255M of 2027 EBITDA, less approximately $500M of year-end 2026 net debt, divided by 84.87M shares. **+11.7%** from $9.40, or roughly +17% including the dividend.
- **Bull case target: $13.00** — 6.0x on $270M less $480M net debt. **+38%.**
- **Bear case target: $5.75** — 4.5x on $220M less $520M net debt. **−39%.**
- **Reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 print (late October) and any Item 1.01 8-K disclosing a Granite City agreement. The contract expires December 31, 2026, so disclosure should arrive between now and January 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosed Granite City renewal for 2027+ at economics at or above the January 2026 extension, with volume at or above 590 Kt. This alone would remove the dominant overhang and support the base-to-bull path.
2. Restart of the $96.3M repurchase authorization at or below roughly $10, which at 12.1% of shares outstanding would be a materially better use of capital than incremental revolver paydown once net leverage is below 2.0x.
3. Q3/Q4 free cash flow delivery within the $137-147M implied H2 range with Domestic Coke EBITDA per ton at or above $51, confirming that the yield improvement is operational rather than weather-driven. A price near $7.50 with these conditions intact would also change the arithmetic.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Granite City non-renewal, or renewal at volumes below roughly 450 Kt or at further reduced economics — particularly if accompanied by an impairment charge against the $1,155.3M Domestic Coke asset base.
2. Full-year operating cash flow tracking below $220M or free cash flow below $135M at the Q3 print, which would indicate the H2 skew was guidance optimism rather than banked receivables.
3. Any dividend reduction, or 2027 free cash flow guidance implying coverage below 2.0x.
4. Cliffs disclosing blast furnace idling or EAF conversion at Indiana Harbor, Middletown or the Haverhill II-served facilities. At 61% of revenue, that is the terminal-value event.

## Conclusion

My view on SunCoke has improved for two consecutive assessments (4.7 in May, 5.0 in June), and the current evidence supports the improvement in financial health specifically: net leverage of 2.55x heading to a guided 1.83-1.98x, interest coverage near 7.7x against a 2.50x covenant, $207.2M of liquidity, no maturities before 2029, and a 5.1% dividend covered 3.8x on guided free cash flow versus roughly 1.0x on FY2025 actuals. That combination makes the stock ownable for income.

It does not make it a purchase at $9.40. The 19.4% headline free-cash-flow yield thins to 18.2% excluding the one-time cash tax receipt and to 14-15% on 2027 once cash taxes revert, and the gap between $171M of D&A and $90-100M of capex means normalized owner earnings of roughly $0.92 per share (about 10.2x) is a fairer anchor than either the enterprise multiple or the 29x GAAP forward P/E. Organic Domestic Coke EBITDA declined $12.6M in H1; every dollar of consolidated growth came from a segment whose best quarter management explicitly described as non-repeatable and partly attributable to war-driven coal arbitrage. Against that, a contract representing roughly $20-30M of EBITDA expires in four and a half months with nothing disclosed, at a company whose $585.6M of equity and $40.3M of retained earnings leave limited room to absorb another write-down of Haverhill I's magnitude.

The overall score of 4.95 is essentially unchanged from June: the balance sheet got better, the price got 8.8% higher, the guidance got better by more than the price, and the single largest binary got closer without getting clearer. Existing holders should collect the dividend and watch for the Granite City disclosure. New capital should wait for that disclosure, or for a price closer to $7.50 where the bear case is substantially pre-paid.
