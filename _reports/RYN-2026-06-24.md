**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Two events define the current investment case: the June 2025 sale of its New Zealand operations for $710M (a $404.5M gain), and the January 30, 2026 merger-of-equals with PotlatchDeltic. The combined entity owns roughly 4.1 million acres of US timberland (3.2M in the South, ~930K in the Northwest), now paired with PotlatchDeltic's six sawmills and a plywood mill, making RYN one of the largest US pure-play timberland owners and a top-tier domestic lumber producer. The headline GAAP figures are misleading: trailing revenue collapsed from $1.26B to $484M on the NZ deconsolidation, and Q1 2026 showed a $(12.5)M net loss driven by $70.4M of merger costs. The pro-forma combined picture is materially healthier: Q1 2026 pro-forma EPS of $0.16, Adjusted EBITDA of $94.1M, 28% debt/cap, and 7.4x interest coverage.

The central debate is dividend coverage. Management held the dividend flat at $0.26/quarter (~$316M/yr) post-merger, but Q1 Adjusted CAD of $62.7M covered only ~80% of the ~$79M quarterly payout. Full-year coverage hinges on a second-half ramp in Wood Products and Real Estate, against a lumber backdrop where current trade-policy tailwinds (Section 232 softwood duties, Canadian AD/CVD duties, weak USD) are partly offset by the April 2026 AR7 preliminary determination signaling lower Canadian duties to be finalized in H2 2026. Independent analyses peg the stock at roughly a 27% discount to estimated NAV, but the discount has persisted because there is no near-term catalyst to close it.

We rate RYN a HOLD on an income basis. The hard-asset value support, the low ~2.3% effective debt cost in a high-rate environment, and the NAV discount justify owning the position for its ~5.0% yield and inflation-hedge characteristics. What is absent is a trigger to convert the discount into appreciation: synergies are unproven, the dividend is thinly covered, and lumber prices face a two-sided policy risk. The score trajectory (4.8 → 5.9 → 5.75) reflects the merger improving the financial profile, but our current read trims slightly from the prior 5.9 because the dividend-coverage gap and the AR7 duty risk have come into sharper focus.

## Company Overview

Rayonier operates as a land-resources REIT across four post-merger segments:

- **Southern Timber** — the largest EBITDA contributor (Q1'26 Adjusted EBITDA $45.5M). Harvests pine sawtimber and pulpwood across the US South and monetizes land through hunting licenses, mineral and cell-tower leases, and land-based solutions (carbon capture/storage and solar).
- **Northwest Timber** ($8.6M Q1 Adjusted EBITDA) — absorbed PotlatchDeltic's Idaho acreage. A significant portion of Idaho sawlogs are indexed to lumber prices on a roughly four-week lag, making the segment more lumber-cyclical than legacy Rayonier.
- **Wood Products** (new from PotlatchDeltic) — six sawmills plus one industrial plywood mill in AR, ID, MI, and MN, with ~1,155 MMBF lumber and 150 MMSF plywood capacity. Direct commodity lumber exposure.
- **Real Estate** — higher-and-better-use (HBU) land sales, master-planned communities (Wildlight FL, Heartwood GA, Chenal Valley AR), and a country club. Q1'26 Adjusted EBITDA of $46.2M, tied with Southern Timber as the largest profit driver.

The REIT structure provides tax efficiency: most income is treated as capital gains, keeping distribution requirements low. The differentiated assets are the HBU real estate development pipeline and land-based solutions; the Q1 sale of 2,226 solar acres at ~$10,100/acre against a ~$6,457/acre average rural price illustrates the embedded land optionality above timber-only value. Primary public peers are Weyerhaeuser and the now-absorbed PotlatchDeltic.

## Financial Analysis

The GAAP numbers require adjustment to be interpretable. Q1 2026 sales of $276.8M (vs. $82.9M in Q1'25) reflect PotlatchDeltic consolidating only from January 31. The $(12.5)M / $(0.05) net loss was driven by merger costs and a $1.2M inventory fair-value step-up, partly offset by a $40.3M tax benefit from releasing a TRS NOL valuation allowance that the merger made usable.

On a pro-forma combined basis (as if combined since 1/1/25), Q1 2026 revenue was $350.2M, net income $47.9M, versus a pro-forma Q1'25 loss of $(0.41). Adjusted EBITDA rose from $27.1M, with CAD of $90.2M and Adjusted CAD of $62.7M after $27.5M of mandatory debt repayment.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Cash stood at $681.7M against total debt of $2,059.5M (up from $1,050M as PotlatchDeltic's ~$1.04B term loans were assumed). Timber and timberlands carry at $5.87B, total assets at $7.75B, and equity at $5.33B, for net debt/EV of 18%. The debt is structured to neutralize rate risk: $450M of 2.75% fixed notes due 2031 plus ~$1.6B of variable Farm Credit term loans that are 100% swapped to fixed, producing a weighted-average effective rate of ~2.3% after swaps and patronage refunds (~$14.8M received in Q1). Covenant headroom is large: EBITDA/interest at 7.4x (minimum 2.5x) and debt/(net worth + debt) at 30% (maximum 65%). No goodwill was recorded, as the purchase price approximated the fair value of net assets. The $200M 2016 term loan was repaid in April 2026 with cash.

The earnings-surprise record supports forward-estimate confidence: three beats and one in-line across the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 actual EPS of $0.07 against a $(0.54) estimate. A standardized Altman Z-score flags "distress" here, but that is an artifact of discontinued operations, one-time merger costs, and the structurally low asset turnover inherent to a timber REIT. The pro-forma profile describes a financially sound business.

The capital-allocation question is dividend coverage. The 2026 plan: capex of $104–108M, real estate development of $28–32M, dividends, and a $300M buyback ($198.4M remaining; 1.48M shares repurchased in Q1 at ~$20.98). With Q1 Adjusted CAD covering only ~80% of the payout, full-year coverage depends on a second-half ramp.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are broadly positive, which is the strongest forward indicator in the data set. Current-year EPS estimates rose +19.5% over 90 days (to $0.47), next-year rose +6.6% (to $0.66), and next-quarter rose +12.3%. The current-quarter estimate slipped -11.7% over 30 days, signaling near-term lumber softness, but the year-level trend is clearly upward.

We do not treat this as a +30% signal warranting a BUY upgrade. The revisions are driven by the mechanical addition of PotlatchDeltic's earnings and synergy expectations rather than a demand inflection in the underlying timber/lumber market. Growth durability rests on cyclical end-markets — US housing starts and repair/remodel activity — which face their own headwinds. The differentiated growth levers are real but slow-burning: HBU real estate development, solar leasing, and carbon capture/storage monetization can crystallize land value well above timber-only worth, but these are episodic, lumpy transactions rather than a recurring growth engine.

The merger delivered scale, geographic diversification, and a vertically integrated fiber-to-lumber model (the Timber segments supply a sizable portion of Wood Products' fiber). Synergy realization remains unproven, and the purchase price allocation is still preliminary, with potential material revision within the measurement year.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 62x is meaningless for a timber REIT — earnings are depressed by merger costs and the structural low-turnover nature of the asset base. The relevant frame is NAV and cash flow. Independent analyses peg RYN at ~57% below historical price-to-book. The stock at $21.17 trades below the $22.74 price used to value the merger consideration and below management's own enterprise-value reference price of $20.62 at quarter-end.

Against Real Estate peers, the comparison is imperfect — none are direct timber comps — but the peer median P/E of 24.1x and yield of 3.1% put RYN's yield at a premium, consistent with the market demanding compensation for the dividend-coverage uncertainty. On forward earnings, the current-year $0.47 and next-year $0.66 estimates imply a forward P/E of ~32x at next-year EPS, elevated, but timber REITs are valued on asset value and CAD, not earnings multiples, given non-cash depletion.

The asymmetry is moderate. The NAV discount provides downside support, and analyst targets (low $22, median $25, mean $26, high $32) all sit above the current price, implying ~23% upside to mean. But a cheap stock without a catalyst is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. The NAV discount has persisted because synergies are unproven, the dividend is thinly covered, and there is no identified trigger to close the gap. We score valuation moderately: the discount is real and provides a floor, but cheapness alone, in a structurally low-alpha Real Estate sector, does not earn a high score absent a catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger meaningfully improved RYN's competitive position. The combined company has greater scale, geographic diversification, and vertical integration than legacy Rayonier. The irreplaceable hard-asset base, REIT tax efficiency, and the optionality from real estate, solar, and CCS are durable advantages.

The trajectory is positive but two-sided. Trade policy is currently a net tailwind for domestic lumber: the October 2025 10% Section 232 softwood duty, higher Canadian AD/CVD duties, and a weak USD all support domestic realizations. The AR7 preliminary determination cuts the other way, which management explicitly warns could pressure domestic lumber prices, wood-products production, and log demand. Southern pine pricing is being pressured by the merger's geographic mix and weak pulpwood demand from regional pulp/paper mill closures; management guides full-year combined Southern pine pricing below legacy Rayonier. Idaho faces federal-timber supply risk from executive orders expanding BLM/USFS harvests.

Net, RYN's competitive position is stable-to-improving on scale and integration, but the lumber-price exposure it acquired makes it more cyclical and more policy-dependent than it was as a timber-only REIT.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Dividend coverage** is the central risk. Full-year coverage depends on a Wood Products and Real Estate ramp; a flat or soft lumber market would keep coverage thin and could force a coverage conversation that pressures the yield-supported valuation.
2. **Lumber-price sensitivity** is newly elevated. Wood Products plus lumber-indexed Idaho sawlogs make RYN materially more exposed to lumber than legacy RYN. The AR7 final determination in H2 2026 is the binary policy event.
3. **Integration risk** is explicit in the 10-K: synergy realization, retention, investment-grade rating maintenance, and a preliminary purchase price allocation subject to material revision. PotlatchDeltic was 59% of total assets and 57% of revenue at Q1 — a large bite to digest.
4. **Underfunded pension** from PotlatchDeltic: ~90.9% funded ($197M PBO vs. $181M assets), ~$7.3M 2026 contribution, with settlement/curtailment risk.
5. **Structural REIT/sector risks**: uninsured timber (fire/storm/pest), REIT qualification (notably the charter lacks share-ownership/transfer restrictions, an unusual gap relative to the 5/50 test), 100% prohibited-transactions tax, Port Gamble environmental remediation (~$8.9M, 15–20-year monitoring), and a sawmill collective-bargaining agreement expiring in 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 22.5% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 26.8% (cheap, -4.3% spread; 6th IV percentile), indicating no near-term event premium and a complacent term structure in contango. The put/call skew of 5.2% at the 1-month tenor shows modestly elevated put demand, and the 1-month put/call volume ratio of 3.90 leans defensive, but the open-interest P/C ratio of 0.51 is not bearish. The implied move at nearest expiry is ±5.1%. The reading is consistent with a non-catalyst stock: cheap protection, no priced binary, and mild near-term hedging. Short interest at 3.9% of float (4.2 days to cover) is uncrowded and not a meaningful signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26–32).** The market is pricing RYN below its parts at the NAV discount. As merger costs roll off and synergies materialize, pro-forma earnings power normalizes, the H2 Wood Products/Real Estate ramp validates dividend coverage, and the ~$2.06B of 2.3%-cost, fully-swapped debt proves a structural advantage in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Trade-policy tailwinds (Section 232, weak USD) hold, HBU/solar/CCS land monetization continues at premium valuations, and the buyback at sub-NAV prices is accretive. The stock re-rates toward NAV. Analyst high target $32.

**Bear Case ($18–19).** Lumber prices weaken on the AR7 final determination cutting Canadian duties in H2 2026, Southern pine pricing stays soft on pulpwood demand erosion, and the Wood Products/Real Estate ramp underdelivers. Dividend coverage stays below 100%, forcing a coverage conversation that breaks the yield support. Integration friction and synergy shortfalls compound. A weakening US housing market suppresses end-demand. The stock retests the 52-week low near $19.49.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income hold with a 12-month reassessment horizon, anchored to dividend sustainability and lumber-policy resolution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of Adjusted CAD covering the full dividend (≥100% coverage), confirming the H2 ramp.
- AR7 final determination resolving favorably (Canadian duties maintained or raised), removing the lumber downside.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, which would be a strong signal given the stock trades below NAV and is currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut, or guidance language signaling coverage stress.
- AR7 cutting Canadian duties materially with lumber prices breaking lower.
- Synergy shortfall, loss of investment-grade rating, or material adverse purchase-price-allocation revision.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the first natural checkpoint for dividend coverage and the H2 ramp. The AR7 final determination in H2 2026 is the key policy catalyst.

## Conclusion

Rayonier post-merger is a scaled, financially sound timberland-plus-lumber REIT trading at a meaningful discount to NAV with a low-cost debt structure. The score trajectory has improved as the merger and NZ exit reshaped the balance sheet, and the pro-forma earnings power is genuine. But the stock fits the value-trap pattern this regime has repeatedly punished: a cheap asset without a catalyst. The dividend is only ~80% covered by Q1 Adjusted CAD, the newly-acquired lumber exposure faces a binary policy event in H2 2026, and there is no identified trigger to close the NAV discount near-term.

We rate RYN a HOLD on an income basis. The hard-asset value support and ~5.0% yield justify continued ownership for inflation-hedge and income purposes, and the analyst target range (low $22, mean $26) implies modest upside. The path to a BUY runs through validated dividend coverage and favorable AR7 resolution; the path to an AVOID runs through a dividend cut or a lumber-price break. Until one of those resolves, this is a yield position to hold, not to add aggressively.
