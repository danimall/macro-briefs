**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Resideo screens as a distressed company but is not one. The 2025 GAAP net loss of −$527M (−$3.77 EPS) and the −$1.25B free cash flow that produce the −8.5x trailing P/E in standard data feeds are the consequence of a single, strategically positive event: in August 2025 the company paid Honeywell $1.59B to permanently terminate the indemnification agreement that had hung over the equity since the 2018 spin-off. That payoff drove a $972M non-operating P&L charge and roughly $1.7B of operating cash outflow. Stripped of it, the business grew revenue 10.5% to $7.47B, expanded operating income 16.7% to $607M, lifted operating margin to 8.1%, and returned to GAAP profitability in Q1 2026 ($0.17 diluted EPS versus −$0.02 a year earlier). The Honeywell overhang (up to $140M/year of payments running to 2043 plus restrictive covenants) is gone.

The central investment dynamic is the announced tax-free spin-off of ADI Global Distribution, expected to close between mid-Q3 and mid-Q4 2026. Post-spin, RemainCo Resideo is the high-margin Products & Solutions manufacturer (≈24% adjusted EBITDA margin), and ADI becomes a standalone lower-margin distributor (≈7.5% adjusted EBITDA margin). The thesis is that the market values the blended entity at a multiple that under-rewards the manufacturer, and separation lets each attract its natural investor base. The 24.2%-versus-7.5% segment margin gap is the quantified core of the value-unlock case, and the transaction is dated and concrete: Form 10 filed May 11, 2026, ADI spin notes priced June 16, 2026, investor days mid-July.

This is a genuine special-situation catalyst on a 12-month horizon, which supports a HOLD with a growth thesis rather than a pass. It is offset by real costs: total debt rose to ~$3.23B to fund the Honeywell payoff, interest expense is up ~67% YoY and is a structural drag, the Altman Z-score sits in the grey zone at 2.15, and consensus near-term EPS estimates have drifted down 3-4% over 90 days. The stock has also round-tripped from $45.29 to $30.41, a ~33% drawdown, with elevated put skew and a recent miss on the December quarter. The setup is attractive but not yet a high-conviction buy until the spin terms and standalone capital structures are finalized.

## Company Overview

Resideo manufactures and distributes sensing-and-controls products for residential and commercial buildings. Spun from Honeywell in October 2018, it runs two structurally different segments:

- **Products & Solutions (P&S)** — A building-products manufacturer of HVAC controls, thermostats, water and air solutions, smoke and CO detection, and residential security. Brands include Honeywell Home (licensed), First Alert, BRK, Braukmann, and Resideo. 2025 revenue ~$2.69B at ~24% adjusted EBITDA margin, serving ~100,000 pro installers and 14M+ connected customers.
- **ADI Global Distribution** — A global wholesale distributor of low-voltage security and AV products selling 500,000+ SKUs from third-party suppliers plus exclusive brands (Control4, OvrC, Araknis, WattBox, acquired via Snap One). 2025 revenue ~$4.78B at only ~7.5% adjusted EBITDA margin.

The defining corporate event is the July 30, 2025 announcement of a tax-free spin-off of ADI into a separate public company. Post-spin, RemainCo Resideo is the margin/cash-flow manufacturer; ADI is the larger-revenue, thinner-margin distributor with M&A optionality. Competitive advantages rest on entrenched installer relationships, recognized brands, broad SKU availability, and an installed base across 150M+ spaces.

## Financial Analysis

The operating fundamentals are healthier than the screen implies:

- **Revenue:** $6.24B (2023) → $6.76B (2024) → $7.47B (2025), +10.5%. About $446M of the 2025 increase came from Snap One; the remainder from price/mix and modest volume. Q1 2026 revenue $1.91B, +8.0% YoY.
- **Operating income:** $520M (2024) → $607M (2025), +16.7%, with operating margin moving 7.7%→8.1% and gross margin up 130bps to 29.4%.
- **The −$527M net loss** is entirely attributable to the $972M non-operating Indemnification Agreement expense. EPS was −$3.77 versus +$0.61 in 2024.
- **Cash flow:** 2025 operating cash flow −$1.137B and FCF −$1.253B, of which ~$1.7B was the cash paid to Honeywell. Underlying operations remain cash-generative. Q1 2026 OCF was −$145M on seasonal working-capital build, ADI separation costs, and legal settlements; Q1 is seasonally weak.
- **Profitability restored:** Q1 2026 net income +$38M, diluted EPS $0.17, now that the indemnification charge is behind it.
- **Balance sheet:** Total debt ~$3.23B (up from ~$2.0B) to fund the Honeywell payment. Altman Z 2.15 (grey zone), D/E ~1.9x, current ratio 1.91. Goodwill plus intangibles ~$4.2B (Snap One-heavy). Cash $438M. Interest expense roughly doubled (Q1: $47M vs $25M) at a weighted-average rate of ~5.67%, an ongoing drag.
- **Capital allocation:** $150M buyback authorization with $108M remaining, but no repurchases in 2024 or 2025; cash went to the Honeywell payoff and Snap One. A ~$35M/year preferred dividend is paid to CD&R.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one miss across the last four quarters, including a +22.9% beat in September 2025 and a −6.3% miss in December 2025. The mix matters because the December miss broke a streak of large beats, and it coincides with the period when estimate revisions turned negative.

Financial Health scores a 5.0. The operating engine is sound and cash-generative once the one-time charge is excluded, but the leverage taken on to clean up Honeywell is real, the Z-score is grey-zone, interest expense is structurally higher, and the standalone capital structures of two entities are still being finalized. This is the dimension that most reliably flags downside, and the leverage step-up plus the spin-driven uncertainty keep it from clearing a higher mark.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is steady (10.5% in 2025, 8% in Q1 2026), with operating leverage visible in the 130bps gross-margin expansion and 16.7% operating-income growth. Management guides to mid-single-digit revenue growth for 2026 and reaffirmed the outlook with Q1; June commentary indicated Q2 may land at the high end of ranges, with ADI revenue $1.24-1.26B and adjusted EBITDA $81-87M.

The forward earnings picture is two-sided. Full-year consensus held flat over 30 days but rose +3.7% over 90 days to $2.90, and next-year estimates rose +6.5% over 90 days to $3.13, constructive medium-term momentum. The nearer-term quarters cut the other way: current-quarter consensus fell 4.0% and next-quarter 3.1% over both 30 and 90 days, reflecting ADI separation costs, one-time legal settlements ($19M in Q1), and tariff/input-cost absorption ahead of price increases now being implemented. Forward-year estimates are rising while near-term quarters soften on transaction-related costs, consistent with a business absorbing separation friction while underlying earnings power improves.

The dominant growth lever is the spin itself. The thesis is sum-of-the-parts re-rating: a 24.2%-margin manufacturer trapped inside a blended multiple with a 7.5%-margin distributor should re-rate when separated. Snap One integration continues to drive ADI growth, R&D, and intangible amortization, and execution there underpins ADI's standalone narrative.

Growth Prospects score a 6.0. Revenue growth is solid and the medium-term estimate trend is positive, but it is mid-single-digit organic growth tied to cyclical housing/repair-remodel end markets, not a structural acceleration. The +30%/90-day estimate-revision signal is not present here, so I weight this as a steady compounder with an event-driven re-rating overlay rather than a momentum growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here; it reads −8.5x because of the Honeywell charge. On forward earnings, REZI trades at roughly 10.5x the current-year consensus of $2.90 and ~9.7x next-year $3.13 at the $30.41 price. That is a substantial discount to the Trading Companies & Distributors peer median P/E of 31.1 (MSM 31.1, DXPE 31.3, AIT 30.1, CNM 21.2), though the peer set is heterogeneous and includes pure distributors that look more like ADI than the high-margin RemainCo.

The cleaner frame is the spin arithmetic. If RemainCo P&S (≈$2.69B revenue, ~24% adjusted EBITDA, implying ~$645M EBITDA) commands a building-products manufacturer multiple of 10-12x EBITDA, and ADI (≈$4.78B revenue, ~7.5% EBITDA, implying ~$359M EBITDA) earns a distributor multiple of 7-9x, the combined enterprise value materially exceeds the current ~$4.8B equity plus ~$3.2B total debt. The analyst price-target band of $48-50 reflects this gap; the stock at $30.41 sits roughly 37-39% below the low end of published targets.

I score Valuation a 6.0 rather than higher for two reasons. First, the cheapness is real but contingent: the value unlock depends on the spin executing cleanly and on the post-split entities being capitalized to attract their natural investor bases, which is exactly the variable still being finalized. Second, a low headline multiple in isolation is not a reliable buy signal; the discount here needs the catalyst to convert, and a 35%+ discount to analyst targets has persisted because the market is pricing execution risk and the higher leverage. The catalyst is dated and specific, which is why this is a 6 and not a 4, but it is not the asymmetric, market-is-clearly-wrong setup that would justify a 7+.

## Competitive Landscape

Resideo operates in fragmented, competitive markets. P&S competes with global controls and safety manufacturers and faces encroachment from cable, telecom, and tech entrants in connected home. ADI competes with other low-voltage distributors and, increasingly, direct-to-installer and e-commerce models; its structurally thin ~7.5% EBITDA margin is classic distribution economics.

The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on trajectory. Gross margin expanded 130bps and operating income grew 17%, evidence of pricing discipline and mix improvement rather than share erosion. The installer relationships, the Honeywell Home brand license, and the 150M+ space installed base are durable, if not widening, moats. The Honeywell Home trademark license is both an asset and a dependency, a continuing risk flagged in the 10-K. Competitive Position scores a 6.0: a credible mid-tier position with margin trajectory pointing the right way, capped by fragmentation, the encroachment threat in connected home, and the brand-license dependency.

## Risk Assessment

- **ADI spin-off execution (dominant risk):** Customer loss, dis-synergies, IT/process separation, and the leverage allocated to each entity. ADI is raising its own senior notes (priced June 16, 2026) to carry debt post-spin. Investors are buying into a transaction whose final capital structures and standalone economics are still being finalized.
- **Leverage and rising interest cost:** Net debt rose materially to fund the Honeywell settlement; interest expense up ~67% YoY at ~5.67%; Z-score grey-zone at 2.15. Covenants are in compliance and liquidity (cash + undrawn $500M revolver) is adequate.
- **Tariff/Mexico exposure:** ~6,400 of 14,800 employees are in Mexico; most P&S US products are manufactured there. Largely USMCA-exempt today, but a 10% surcharge (Feb 2026) and 15-50% metals tariffs (Apr 2026) apply to non-exempt goods. Management says no material impact and is implementing offsetting price increases.
- **Honeywell dependence:** The Honeywell Home trademark license remains a continuing reliance even after the indemnification termination.
- **CD&R influence:** $500M of convertible participating preferred (from the Snap One deal) plus board seats give a PE sponsor structural influence and a preferred dividend claim ahead of common; the 10-K flags that CD&R interests "may diverge from or conflict with" common holders.
- **Cyclical end-markets:** Tied to housing turnover, repair/remodel, mortgage rates, and construction. Management guides to "slow growth" in US residential and "moderating" non-residential in 2026, a near-term headwind to organic growth.

Risk Factors score a 5.0. The binary, dated nature of the spin and the leverage step-up are the live idiosyncratic risks not fully resolved in the current price; they are balanced against adequate liquidity and the fact that the headline distress signals are one-time-driven.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case:** The ADI spin closes cleanly in 2H 2026, and RemainCo Resideo re-rates as a ~24%-EBITDA-margin building-products manufacturer toward 10-12x EBITDA while ADI trades at a distributor multiple. The Honeywell overhang is permanently gone, freeing up to ~$140M/year of avoided payments. Price increases offset tariff costs, operating leverage continues, and next-year EPS reaches the $3.13 consensus or higher. Sum-of-the-parts supports the $48-50 analyst target band, ~58-64% above the current price.

**Bear case:** Spin execution stumbles on dis-synergies, customer attrition, or unfavorable debt allocation between the two entities. The cyclical housing/repair-remodel backdrop weakens organic growth below mid-single digits, near-term estimates keep falling, and the doubled interest expense compresses RemainCo's free cash flow. CD&R's preferred claim sits ahead of common in a stressed scenario. The stock retests the low-$20s area (52-week low $20.75), ~32% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth (event-driven) thesis on a 6-12 month horizon, anchored to the ADI spin closing between mid-Q3 and mid-Q4 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Spin terms and standalone capital structures finalized with leverage allocated such that RemainCo retains a manufacturer-quality balance sheet (RemainCo net debt/EBITDA below ~2.5x), confirmed at the mid-July investor days or Form 10 amendments.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or rising near-term EPS estimates (reversing the current −4%/−3% drift), with FY2026 revenue tracking the mid-single-digit guide or better.
- Resumption of buybacks under the $108M remaining authorization, signaling management confidence that the leverage is manageable.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Spin delay or cancellation, or a debt allocation that leaves either entity with a stressed balance sheet (Z-score deteriorating below 1.8, covenant pressure).
- Organic revenue growth turning negative as the housing cycle weakens, or gross margin reversing the 130bps expansion.
- Tariff escalation that materially hits the Mexico-manufactured P&S cost base without offsetting price recovery.

**Reassessment timeline:** Mid-July 2026 investor days, then the Q2 earnings release, then the spin close in 2H 2026. Each is a discrete checkpoint to confirm or invalidate the value-unlock thesis.

## Conclusion

Resideo is a misunderstood-by-the-screen situation with a genuine, dated catalyst. The 2025 net loss and negative cash flow are the one-time cost of permanently removing the Honeywell liability, not operating deterioration: operating income grew 17%, margins expanded, and the company is profitable again. The 2H-2026 ADI spin-off is a credible sum-of-the-parts unlock built on a quantified 24.2%-vs-7.5% segment margin gap, and analyst targets imply ~58-64% upside if it converts. Against that sit materially higher leverage, doubled interest expense, a grey-zone balance sheet, cyclical end-market softness, CD&R's preferred overhang, and near-term estimate drift. The forward valuation (~10x) is cheap relative to peers, but the cheapness is contingent on clean execution that is still being finalized.

The risk/reward is favorable but not yet asymmetric enough to commit ahead of the spin terms being locked. I rate REZI a HOLD with a growth thesis and would move to BUY on confirmation of a manufacturer-quality RemainCo balance sheet at the July investor days or a stabilization in near-term estimates.
