**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commerce Bancshares is a Kansas City-based regional bank distinguished by a fee-heavy revenue mix (~37% non-interest income versus 20-25% for most regional peers), a granular sticky-deposit base (91.4% core deposits, ~73% loan/deposit ratio), and fortress capital (17.09% CET1, roughly double the well-capitalized minimum). It generates elite through-cycle returns for the sector: FY2025 ROA of 1.79% and ROE of 15.76%, on record net income of $570.7M. Credit quality is pristine, with non-performing assets at 0.06% of loans. The defining recent development is the January 1, 2026 close of the $524.5M FineMark acquisition, an uncharacteristic move for this normally organic-growth, family-controlled institution that adds $8.7B of wealth assets under administration and a Sun Belt foothold in Florida, Arizona, and South Carolina.

The fundamentals continue to improve modestly. Q1 2026 net income rose 7.6% YoY, net interest margin expanded to 3.59%, and trust fees jumped 25.5% on the FineMark AUA. Consensus EPS estimates are rising across every window (+6.0% for the current year over 90 days, +4.3% for next year), and the Q2 report on July 16 beat consensus at $1.10, though that print carries large non-recurring items (a Visa Class B exchange gain, likely offset by a securities-repositioning loss) that require normalizing. The constraint is not risk but that the market has already rewarded the quality. The stock has recovered roughly 24% from its March low near $47.49 back to the high-$50s, trades at ~14.4x earnings against a peer median of 12.2x, and sits about 4% below its 52-week high. A Seeking Alpha downgrade on July 16 explicitly framed the excess capital as priced in.

We rate CBSH a HOLD (growth_hold). This is a genuine mid-single-digit EPS compounder with a well-covered dividend, a rising estimate trajectory, and a strategically sound wealth-and-Sun Belt pivot. At the current multiple and with analyst consensus targeting a mean of ~$64.56 (about 9% upside), the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on evidence that FineMark integration is running ahead of plan, on continued estimate-revision velocity, or on a pullback toward the low-$50s that restores a wider valuation cushion.

## Company Overview

Commerce Bancshares operates through its subsidiary Commerce Bank, a super-community/regional bank with roughly 150 branches concentrated in Missouri, Kansas, central Illinois, Oklahoma, and Colorado, plus a commercial and trust presence extending into Texas, Iowa, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Florida, and Tennessee. Total assets were $35.7B as of Q1 2026. Post-FineMark, the footprint now reaches Florida, Arizona, and South Carolina.

The business runs on three segments:
- **Commercial** (~$84M Q1'26 pre-tax income): corporate lending, leasing, international services, cash management, card/merchant services, and a Commercial Tradable Products division for fixed-income and municipal underwriting.
- **Wealth** (~$63M pre-tax, fastest-growing): trust, estate planning, discretionary investment management, brokerage, and private banking. This is the strategic growth engine, and its pre-tax income rose 40.5% YoY.
- **Retail Banking** (~$50M pre-tax): consumer deposits and loans, mortgage, indirect auto, and card products.

The company is family-controlled across multiple generations. John W. Kemper is CEO; David W. Kemper is Executive Chairman. The character of the bank is quality-over-growth: an unusually fee-heavy franchise anchored in trust, corporate card, and cash management, funded by a sticky low-cost deposit base. That mix produces returns above the regional-bank median at lower balance-sheet risk than scale-driven competitors.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** Q1 2026 net income was $141.6M, up 7.6% YoY, on annualized ROA of 1.62% and ROE of 13.22% — strong for a regional bank, though down from ~1.7%/15.8% a year prior, reflecting FineMark acquisition costs and share dilution. FY2025 delivered a record $570.7M in net income (up from $534M in 2024), with a ~55.5% efficiency ratio.

**Net interest income and margin.** NII was $299.8M in Q1'26, up 11.4% YoY, with FTE net interest margin of 3.59% (up from 3.56%). The growth was driven largely by FineMark's $2.7B of added loans plus $6.9M of purchase-accounting accretion. Deposit rates paid fell 11bps YoY even as NIM expanded, a benefit of three Fed cuts in 2026 easing funding-cost pressure. The book is modestly asset-sensitive (+$46.5M NII in a +300bp scenario, -$38.6M in -300bp), and became slightly less asset-sensitive after FineMark added rate-sensitive deposits.

**Fee income.** Non-interest income was $175.9M, up 10.6%, led by trust fees +25.5% (to $71M) on the FineMark wealth assets. Bank card fees were flat. The fee franchise is the durable differentiator versus balance-sheet-dependent peers.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Loans stood at $20.5B (up from $17.8B at YE'25, almost entirely FineMark), deposits at $28.4B, a ~73% loan/deposit ratio, with $12.8B of liquid assets. Capital is fortress-level: Tier 1 common risk-based 17.09%, total risk-based 17.90%, Tier 1 leverage 12.60% — roughly double regulatory minimums.

**Credit quality.** Non-performing assets are just 0.06% of loans, down from 0.10%. Net charge-offs annualized at 0.30%, concentrated in consumer credit card (5.21%), which is normal seasoning. Allowance/loans is 0.97%. Non-accruals are small and concentrated in business real estate (85.8% of the total), including a senior-living charge-off in Q1. Office CRE is $611M (~3% of loans) with $51.7M substandard — manageable.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters, CBSH logged two beats, one in-line, and one miss (Q3 2025 at -5.3%). The beats have been modest (2.1% and 7.1%), consistent with a low-variance, well-guided franchise. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year consensus of $4.29 has risen +6.0% over 90 days, and next-year of $4.54 is up +4.3%. The momentum is positive but not the +30% velocity that would flip a mature bank into a re-rating candidate.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth engine is Wealth. Trust fees grew 25.5% and Wealth pre-tax income 40.5% YoY, and FineMark structurally accelerates this by adding $8.7B in AUA and a Sun Belt footprint that CBSH lacked. Wealth is the highest-margin, most capital-light segment, so growth there flows disproportionately to returns and consumes little of the bank's already-abundant capital.

FineMark is the swing variable. For a famously non-acquisitive bank, a $524.5M deal (9.9M shares at $52.34) is a genuine strategic pivot. Management is already actively managing the acquired book, moving $1.0B of high-cost money-market deposits off balance sheet and repaying $351M of FineMark FHLB debt immediately. That deliberate shedding of rate-sensitive funding is a favorable early sign, though integration synergies are still unproven, and a large regional wealth-and-banking integration carries meaningful execution risk.

Beyond FineMark, the growth profile is mid-single-digit: modest loan growth, NIM stability-to-slight expansion in an easing-rate environment, and continued fee compounding. The rising estimate trajectory (+3.2% current-year, +3.5% next-year over 30 days) supports the view that consensus is being nudged up, not down. This is a durable compounder rather than a high-growth story, with the growth thesis resting on Wealth mix-shift plus deployment of excess capital via buybacks (authorization raised to 7.5M shares in April 2026).

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.09, CBSH trades at ~14.4x trailing and forward EPS ($4.04 trailing; consensus $4.29 current-year implies ~13.8x forward). Against the Regional Banks peer median P/E of 12.2x, that is a ~15-20% premium. The premium is defensible on quality: CBSH earns a ~1.8% ROA and ~15%+ ROE versus peers, carries pristine credit, and generates a fee mix others cannot match. But a premium that is warranted is not the same as one that offers asymmetric upside.

| Dividend yield | 1.9% | 2.9% |

The dividend yield of 1.9% is well below the peer median of 2.9%, further evidence the market is capitalizing CBSH's quality into a lower yield. Analyst consensus targets a mean of $64.56 and median of $64.00 (about 8-9% upside), with a low of $57.00 (below current price) and a high of $73.00. The tight low-to-mean band signals a stock the sell side views as fairly-to-slightly-undervalued, not deeply mispriced. On our lessons, cheapness in this sector has been an anti-predictive value-trap signal — CBSH is not cheap, and its premium is earned, so we do not read the discount peers as an argument to rotate into them.

The July 16 Seeking Alpha downgrade crystallized the bear valuation view: the fortress capital and quality are already reflected in the multiple, and the excess capital represents opportunity cost until deployed. The offsets — buyback capacity and FineMark deployment — are real but incremental. We score valuation 4.5. The multiple is not stretched to a level that flags downside, but at a peer-relative premium with a below-median yield and single-digit consensus upside, there is no specific mispricing to exploit. This is consistent with the valuation score's decline from 7.0 (May) to 5.5 (June) as the stock recovered; the further step down reflects the additional recovery back toward the highs.

## Competitive Landscape

CBSH competes against larger national banks with lower funding costs and bigger technology budgets, other regionals, and fintechs. Its defense is franchise stickiness rather than scale: a fee-heavy revenue mix and a granular core-deposit base that together produce best-in-class returns with lower balance-sheet risk. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. Wealth is gaining share and mix, FineMark extends the franchise into faster-growing Sun Belt markets, and the trust/private-banking business builds switching costs that seat-based deposit competitors lack.

The offsetting reality is geographic concentration in the Midwest/Plains and sub-scale technology spend relative to money-center banks. Where deposit-gathering and payments increasingly reward scale and digital investment, CBSH's moat is defensive rather than expanding. FineMark is the attempt to change that trajectory, and its success will determine whether competitive position moves from "stable" to "improving." We score competitive position 6.5 — a durable, quality franchise with a modest positive tilt from the Wealth pivot.

## Risk Assessment

**FineMark integration (newly elevated).** This is the single biggest change to CBSH's risk profile and the newly added risk factor in the 2025 10-K. FineMark was ~12% of consolidated assets at close. Deposit attrition, unrealized synergies, and management distraction are live concerns. The early moves are constructive, but execution is unproven over a full cycle.

**AOCI unrealized securities losses.** The AFS portfolio carries a net unrealized loss of ~$687.5M (Q1'26), concentrated in agency MBS (~$578M). This is rate-driven, not credit, and CBSH classifies nothing as held-to-maturity, so the mark is fully transparent. It depresses tangible book (TCE/TA 11.07%) but is benign in character: high-quality, agency-heavy, and expected to accrete back to par as ~$1.2B matures or pays down over 12 months. It becomes a genuine problem only in a forced-sale scenario, which the ~$12.8B liquid-asset position and 17% CET1 make remote.

**Q2 2026 earnings quality.** The Q2 print included a Visa Class B-2 exchange gain (411,723 shares carried at $0 cost basis), likely offset by a deliberate securities-repositioning loss to soak up the gain tax-efficiently. GAAP Q2 numbers therefore contain large non-recurring items and should be normalized; the underlying trends to watch are trust fees and NIM.

**Other risks.** Midwest/Plains geographic concentration, deposit-competition/confidence risk carried over from the 2023 regional-bank episode, and CRE exposure (business real estate 19.8% of loans, office $611M/~3%). All are manageable and largely priced.

We score risk factors 6.0, consistent with the June improvement from 5.0. The known risks are transparent (no HTM hidden marks, disclosed integration risk) and the balance sheet has ample cushion to absorb them; the offset is the fresh, unproven FineMark integration and the earnings noise in the near-term print.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.0% versus 21.9% trailing realized — rich by about 7 points, though only the 17th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (29.0% near-term versus 24.8% at six months), which typically signals the market is pricing near-term event stress. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (0.3%), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.8%. There is no directional bearish tilt in the options positioning; the elevated near-term IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bet against the stock. For a holder, the rich IV argues against chasing here; for anyone adding, waiting for a vol crush toward the low-$50s would improve entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$73 / +24%).** FineMark integration proceeds cleanly, deposit attrition stays low, and Wealth mix-shift continues to compound trust fees at a mid-teens-plus rate. NIM holds above 3.55% as easing rates lower funding costs faster than asset yields reprice. Estimate revisions keep running positive, consensus catches up to a ~$4.60+ next-year EPS, and CBSH deploys its excess capital via the 7.5M-share buyback at accretive prices. The premium multiple holds or expands modestly as the market rewards a de-risked, faster-growing franchise. This is the high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$50 / -15%).** FineMark integration disappoints — deposit or advisor attrition erodes the acquired AUA, synergies underwhelm, and the goodwill ($107.3M allocated to Wealth) faces scrutiny. A renewed rate move or regional deposit-confidence episode pressures NII, and the AOCI overhang caps tangible-book growth. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~12x, taking the stock to the low-$50s. The below-median dividend yield offers limited support on the way down.

**Base Case (~$64 / +8%).** CBSH compounds EPS at mid-single-digits, FineMark integrates without drama but without dramatic upside, and the stock tracks consensus estimate revisions higher. Applying ~14x to a ~$4.55 forward EPS supports roughly $64, in line with the analyst mean. The 1.9% dividend plus modest buyback provides a small additional return floor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q3 2026, mid-October) and the first clean post-FineMark full-year print.**

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately higher. The trajectory has been range-bound (6.2 → 7.0 → 6.2), with the May peak coinciding with a lower stock price and wider valuation cushion, and the subsequent step-down reflecting the recovery back toward the highs. Our current call holds because the fundamental thesis is intact and modestly improving while the valuation asymmetry has compressed — a balanced, not deteriorating, setup.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- A pullback toward the low-$50s (restoring ~10%+ upside to the base case and a wider margin of safety), OR
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating FineMark accretion — Wealth pre-tax income sustaining >20% growth with stable acquired deposits and no goodwill impairment, OR
- Continued estimate-revision velocity that lifts next-year consensus above ~$4.65 while the multiple stays near 14x.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Evidence of FineMark deposit/advisor attrition materially eroding the acquired AUA, or a goodwill impairment charge, OR
- NIM compression below ~3.45% coupled with falling estimate revisions, OR
- The premium multiple expanding meaningfully (toward ~17x+) without commensurate growth acceleration, indicating the market has over-capitalized the quality.

## Conclusion

Commerce Bancshares is one of the highest-quality regional banks in the U.S.: fee-heavy, fortress-capitalized, pristine on credit, and now strategically pivoting toward Wealth and Sun Belt geography through the FineMark acquisition. The fundamentals are improving at the margin, estimates are rising, and the dividend is well covered. The constraint is price: after the recovery off the March low, the stock trades at a premium to its peer group with a below-median yield and single-digit upside to the analyst mean, leaving the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. The excess capital that makes CBSH so resilient is, on the current multiple, an opportunity-cost debate rather than an accretion story until it is visibly deployed. We rate CBSH a HOLD and would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$50s or on hard evidence that FineMark is compounding ahead of plan.
