## Executive Summary

ResMed is a $30B market-cap medical device company trading at $210, down 28% from its 52-week high of $294, despite delivering consistent 10%+ revenue growth, dramatic gross margin expansion (55.8% → 62.2% over three years), and generating $1.66B in free cash flow on $5.15B in revenue. The stock currently trades at approximately 19-20x forward FY2026 EPS of ~$11.12, with a 5.4% FCF yield — unusually cheap for a company with this margin profile, growth rate, and competitive position.

The selloff appears driven by three factors: (1) the CFO retirement announced alongside Q3 earnings on April 30, (2) persistent GLP-1 drug overhang narrative despite no material visible financial impact to date, and (3) broader market risk-off in healthcare equipment. None of these represent fundamental deterioration. Revenue grew 11% in Q3, gross margins expanded 290bps YoY, and EPS beat consensus. The balance sheet has flipped from ~$1.4B net debt in FY2023 to ~$1B net cash today. The company is generating cash faster than it can deploy it, buying back stock aggressively, and benefiting from Philips' continued U.S. market absence.

The primary bear case centers on GLP-1 drugs reducing the addressable OSA population and Philips' eventual re-entry normalizing competitive dynamics. Both are real long-term risks, but neither is priced correctly at 19-20x forward earnings for a business compounding EPS at 20%+ with 32% FCF margins.

## Company Overview

ResMed designs, manufactures, and distributes medical devices for diagnosing and treating sleep-disordered breathing (primarily obstructive sleep apnea) and other respiratory conditions. The company operates two segments:

**Sleep and Breathing Health (~88% of revenue, $4.5B FY2025):** CPAP/BiPAP devices, masks, headgear, accessories, ventilators, and diagnostic equipment. The AirSense 11 platform features cloud connectivity, over-the-air updates, and digital health algorithms that create meaningful switching costs.

**Residential Care Software (~12% of revenue, $641M FY2025):** SaaS platforms for home medical equipment providers and residential healthcare organizations, including the MEDIFOX DAN platform in Europe.

ResMed is the global market leader in CPAP devices and benefits significantly from Philips' absence from the U.S. market following its 2021 foam degradation recall and the court-entered FDA consent decree in April 2024 that restricts Philips from selling new CPAP/BiPAP devices in the U.S. until certain conditions are met. ResMed's own filings describe global competition as "intense," naming multiple competitors including Fisher & Paykel Healthcare, DeVilbiss, Apex, BMC, React Health, Jiangsu Yuyue, and Löwenstein, among others. The company manufactures across multiple locations including Singapore, Sydney, Malaysia, the U.S., and other sites, and sells in 140+ countries with ~145M diluted shares outstanding.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular demand drivers remain intact:**
1. ~1 billion people globally estimated to have OSA; diagnosis rates are 15-20% in developed markets, lower elsewhere
2. Philips absence from U.S. market continues to channel demand to ResMed
3. Masks/accessories as recurring revenue (replacement every 3-6 months) provide visibility
4. Home healthcare shift structurally benefits both device and software segments
5. Digital health ecosystem (AirView, myAir) creates data-driven switching costs

**Near-term growth vectors:**
- Q3 FY2026 device revenue +6% constant currency, mask revenue +12% constant currency
- VirtuOx acquisition moves into direct sleep testing/diagnosis

**Deceleration concern:** Software segment growth decelerated from 10% (FY2025) to 4% CC in Q3 FY2026. This segment has lower margins and faces "low barriers to entry" per management's own risk disclosures. Sustained deceleration would be meaningful as a diversification pillar, though at 12% of revenue, the near-term consolidated growth impact is contained.

**Long-term headwinds:** GLP-1 drugs may reduce the severity of OSA in obese patients. ResMed's own 10-K identifies injectable GLP-1 weight-loss drugs as a competitive/market risk, and Zepbound (tirzepatide) received FDA approval for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity in December 2024. Drug adherence rates are poor (~50% discontinuation within 12 months), many OSA patients have anatomical rather than obesity-driven conditions, and ResMed's revenue has continued to grow through this period. No material impact is visible in reported growth to date, but the long-term risk remains unresolved as the relevant OSA-specific drug approval is recent.

The discount reflects GLP-1 overhang and Philips re-entry risk, but the magnitude appears excessive given ResMed's superior FCF generation and growth consistency.

The CFO transition (announced April 30) drove the most recent selloff, but a CFO change at a company generating $1.66B in predictable FCF with minimal balance sheet complexity is a 1-2 quarter headwind, not a structural risk.

## Competitive Landscape

**Philips absence (competitive advantage widening):** The FDA consent decree entered in April 2024 has kept Philips from selling new CPAP/BiPAP devices in the U.S. until certain requirements are met. Each quarter that passes deepens ResMed's installed base advantage: more patients on ResMed devices, more providers trained on ResMed systems, more data flowing through ResMed's digital platform. When Philips eventually returns, it will face a market where provider/patient habits have shifted. The global market remains competitive with multiple manufacturers, and ResMed itself describes competition as intense.

**Digital health ecosystem:** AirView (provider platform) and myAir (patient app) create bidirectional data flows that improve therapy adherence, reduce provider workload, and generate competitive lock-in. Replicating this requires years of data accumulation that a returning Philips or smaller competitors lack.

**Mask replacement cycle:** The shift toward direct-to-consumer resupply programs and faster mask replacement cadences (every 3-6 months) creates a high-margin recurring revenue stream. Masks grew 12% constant currency in Q3 versus 6% CC for devices, indicating the installed base is monetizing faster than new patient additions alone.

**Manufacturing footprint:** ResMed manufactures across multiple global locations including Singapore, Sydney, Malaysia, the U.S. (Atlanta, Calabasas, Chatsworth), and other sites. The Singapore and Australia operations provide some tariff insulation relative to China-based competitors, though the company also has exposure to China (Suzhou facility) and other regions. A new Indiana distribution center (operational 2027) further strengthens U.S. supply chain resilience.

**Risks to position:** Philips re-entry (timing unknown, impact uncertain); multiple global CPAP competitors including Fisher & Paykel Healthcare gaining share; GLP-1 drugs reducing addressable market over 5-10 year horizon.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **GLP-1/Obesity Drug Threat (Long-term, Moderate):** Zepbound received FDA approval for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity in December 2024. Drug adherence is poor (~50% discontinuation within 12 months), many OSA cases are non-obesity-driven, and under-diagnosis provides multi-year growth runway. No material impact visible in financial results to date. Timeline for potential material impact: uncertain.

2. **Philips Re-entry (Medium-term, Moderate):** The court-entered consent decree restricting new U.S. device sales began in April 2024 and remains in effect until conditions are met. When Philips returns, pricing pressure and normalized demand growth are likely. ResMed's digital ecosystem advantage may limit share loss. Timeline: uncertain; consent decree has no stated expiration date.

3. **CFO Transition (Near-term, Low):** Brett Sandercock retiring after long tenure; Aaron Bloomer (from Exact Sciences) joining. The filing states Sandercock's retirement did not result from any disagreement with the company. The new CFO inherits a pristine financial situation with minimal balance sheet complexity.

4. **Tariff & Supply Chain (Near-term, Low-Moderate):** Chinese rare earth magnet export controls, general tariff environment. Diversified manufacturing footprint across Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, and the U.S. provides some insulation, though the company also has China-based operations. New Indiana distribution center addresses U.S. supply chain proactively.

5. **Software Segment Deceleration:** Growth slowed from 10% to 4% CC. Competitive pressure from vertical SaaS providers with "low barriers to entry." At 12% of revenue, near-term consolidated impact is contained but warrants monitoring.

6. **VirtuOx Compliance Exposure:** Corporate Integrity Agreement through 2027 creates compliance tail risk. VirtuOx had a prior $3.2M civil settlement in 2022. Any government investigation could result in penalties or federal program exclusion.

## Conclusion

ResMed at $210 represents a quality compounder trading at a meaningful discount to intrinsic value due to an overhang narrative (GLP-1 drugs) that has produced no material visible financial impact and a transient catalyst (CFO retirement) that is immaterial to a business generating $1.66B in predictable FCF. The 19-20x forward P/E and 5.4% FCF yield provide margin of safety, while 10%+ revenue growth, expanding margins, and aggressive buybacks drive compounding. The competitive position benefits significantly from Philips' U.S. absence, though the global market remains competitive with multiple manufacturers.

The risk/reward is attractive: 30%+ upside to base case targets with 15-20% downside in a bear scenario where multiple risks materialize simultaneously. Elevated short interest provides additional fuel for any re-rating catalyst.

**Recommendation: BUY** at $207-210, with position sizing of 2-3%.
