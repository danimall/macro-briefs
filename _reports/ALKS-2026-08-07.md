## Executive Summary

Alkermes has spent 2026 remaking itself. The February 12 acquisition of Avadel for $2,199.2M in cash plus a contingent value right added LUMRYZ, a once-nightly sodium oxybate for narcolepsy, and was funded with $731.2M of pre-funded escrow cash plus $1.525B of new secured term loans at SOFR + 2.75% (approximately 6.45% all-in). The company entered 2026 essentially debt-free and now carries roughly $1.0B of estimated net debt with maintenance leverage and coverage covenants and a lien on substantially all assets. Q2 2026 revenue of $496.0M grew 27% year over year, LYBALVI grew 21% in the first half, and LUMRYZ contributed $96.6M in its first full quarter. GAAP operating income was $18.4M, against $25.9M of quarterly interest expense.

The equity, however, is not priced on that commercial base. At $49.05 on 173.5M diluted shares, equity value is approximately $8.51B and enterprise value approximately $9.5B — roughly 5.3x annualized 1H26 revenue and, on a reconstructed adjusted EBITDA sensitivity of $320–380M, roughly 25–30x. A business whose total revenue declined 5.2% in FY2025, whose Q3 2026 revenue is guided to $450–470M (7.3% below Q2), and whose largest product loses exclusivity to a licensed Teva generic on January 15, 2027 does not command that multiple on commercial merits. The multiple prices alixorexton, the oral OX2R agonist that entered Phase 3 (Brilliance, NT1 and NT2) on April 1, 2026.

That is where the news since our last review cuts against the position. On August 5, 2026 the FDA approved Takeda's Orzeyful (oveporexton) for adult narcolepsy type 1 — the first approved orexin-signaling agonist. Alkermes has lost the first-mover position on the mechanism that carries its valuation, and it now needs to deliver registrational data in NT2 and idiopathic hypersomnia, indications where no OX2R agonist has yet succeeded in a pivotal trial, in order to execute the label-breadth strategy its new CEO has articulated. The offsetting reads are real: FDA approval validates the mechanism and clarifies the regulatory path, Alkermes already owns a narcolepsy sales force and REMS infrastructure, and reported large-cap appetite for orexin assets implies strategic value. We keep the rating at HOLD, with the overall score marked down to 4.95 from 5.2 — the fifth consecutive HOLD, and one we hold with low conviction rather than comfort.

## Company Overview

Alkermes is an Irish-domiciled, US-operating neuroscience company with four commercial products and a declining legacy royalty and manufacturing stream.

**Products.** VIVITROL (extended-release naltrexone, alcohol and opioid dependence), ARISTADA/ARISTADA INITIO (aripiprazole lauroxil, schizophrenia), LYBALVI (olanzapine/samidorphan, schizophrenia and bipolar I; Orange Book patents 2032–2041), and LUMRYZ (once-nightly sodium oxybate, narcolepsy ages 7+, orphan exclusivity to May 1, 2030 for adults and October 16, 2031 for pediatric).

**Royalty and manufacturing.** Alkermes manufactures RISPERDAL CONSTA for Janssen and earns royalties on Janssen's paliperidone long-acting injectables and on Biogen's VUMERITY. It has no control over commercialization of any of these. The FY2025 10-K discloses plainly that ARISTADA and LYBALVI compete against the INVEGA franchise from which Alkermes collects royalties — the company's own sales force competes with a revenue line it also collects.

**Pipeline.** Alixorexton (ALKS 2680), oral selective OX2R agonist, Phase 3 in NT1 and NT2 (initiated April 1, 2026), Phase 2 Vibrance-3 in idiopathic hypersomnia; Breakthrough Therapy designation in NT1, Orphan Drug Designation in IH. Valiloxybate, an oxybate candidate acquired with Avadel.

**Manufacturing structure.** The entire legacy portfolio — ARISTADA, ARISTADA INITIO, LYBALVI, RISPERDAL CONSTA, VIVITROL — is made solely at Wilmington, Ohio, with what the 10-K describes as limited ability to shift or outsource production. LUMRYZ is made by third parties, with certain manufacturing elements outside the US.

**Leadership.** Richard Pops, CEO since 1991, transitioned to Chairman; Chief Operating Officer Blair Jackson became CEO effective August 1, 2026, per external reporting corroborated by a February 2026 Letter Agreement referenced in the Q2 10-Q.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025: mix loss, not operating deterioration

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietary product net sales | $1,083.5M | $1,184.6M | +$101.1M (+9.3%) |
| Manufacturing & royalty revenue | $474.1M | $291.3M | −$182.8M (−38.6%) |
| **Total revenue** | **$1,557.6M** | **$1,475.9M** | **−$81.7M (−5.2%)** |

The dominant identified driver of the royalty decline was the August 2024 expiry of the US royalty on INVEGA SUSTENNA, worth −$126.9M in FY2025. That is permanent.

Operating income fell from approximately $420M to approximately $254M, a decline of roughly $166M. Revenue fell $81.7M and R&D rose approximately $79M (approximately $245M to $324M) — together roughly $161M, essentially the whole decline, implying cost of goods and SG&A were roughly flat in aggregate. Operating margin compressed from approximately 27.0% to approximately 17.2%. The mechanism matters: royalty revenue carries near-zero incremental cost, so its loss drops almost one-for-one to operating income. This is mix loss plus deliberate orexin investment rather than pricing or manufacturing deterioration. The reconciliation is arithmetic, not proof of causation — offsetting COGS and SG&A movements could mask each other — but it is a materially more benign read than "margins collapsed."

Net income was $241.7M in FY2025 (16.4% net margin) versus $367.1M in FY2024. Operating cash flow was $520.8M, roughly 2.15x net income, consistent with meaningful D&A and stock compensation add-backs. FY2025 capex was not obtained, so no free cash flow figure is asserted.

Underneath the mix story, product growth is decelerating: proprietary product net sales grew 23.9%, 18.3%, 17.8% and then 9.3% across 2022–2025 (four-year CAGR 17.2%).

### Post-acquisition: growth real, GAAP profitability not yet

Q2 2026 was the first full quarter with LUMRYZ.

- Revenue $496.0M, +27% year over year; 1H26 $888.9M, +27.5%
- LUMRYZ $96.6M (19.5% of quarterly revenue)
- LYBALVI 1H26 $186.3M, +21%
- VIVITROL 1H26 approximately $237M (approximately 26.7% of 1H26 revenue; single-read figure)
- Operating income $18.4M (3.7% margin); net income $0.5M
- 1H26 net loss $66.0M, implying roughly −$66.5M in Q1 as transaction and step-up charges landed
- Diluted shares 173.5M

Operating cash flow was **−$20.6M in 1H26 versus +$249.0M in 1H25**, a $269.6M swing driven by transaction costs, working capital and first debt service. This is a period flow contaminated by one-time items and should not be annualized. Whether it normalizes in 2H26 is a hypothesis, not a fact.

### Capital structure — the part a stale screen misses

$750M Term Loan A plus $775M Term Loan B, SOFR + 2.75%, approximately 6.45% effective. Q2 interest expense $25.9M (approximately $104M annualized; $1,525M × 6.45% = $98.4M, with the balance plausibly issuance-cost amortization). The facility is secured by a lien on substantially all assets, requires maintenance leverage and coverage ratios each quarter, and limits restricted payments including share repurchases. Covenant levels were not disclosed in the sections reviewed, so headroom is unquantified.

From the 12/31/2025 balance sheet ($388.6M cash, $199.6M investments, $731.2M restricted escrow, $83.0M goodwill, no term debt), less 1H26 operating outflow and Q1 buybacks, plus the residual between funding sources and disclosed consideration, cash at 6/30/2026 is estimated at roughly $480–570M and net debt at roughly $0.96–1.05B. This is a derived estimate; all leverage multiples inherit that uncertainty.

**Interest coverage on a GAAP basis does not exist right now.** Q2 operating income of $18.4M is below roughly $26M of quarterly interest. Coverage exists only after adding back purchase-accounting amortization: reconstructing Q2 EBITDA from $18.4M operating income plus the quarter's share of the $43.7M 1H26 inventory step-up amortization, plus LUMRYZ intangible amortization of roughly $25–32M (on a $1,768.6M asset over roughly 14 years), plus other D&A of $12–15M, gives roughly $80–95M for the quarter, or $320–380M annualized. That implies coverage of roughly 3.1–3.7x and net leverage of roughly 2.6–3.1x. Two mechanical tailwinds help: approximately $77.9M of the $121.6M inventory step-up remains to amortize, substantially all within a year of the February 12 close, after which the non-cash drag largely leaves cost of goods; and LUMRYZ was only partially in the period. One headwind cuts the other way: Q3 revenue is guided below Q2.

Any Altman Z, Piotroski F, debt-to-equity or current ratio computed from the FY2025 10-K describes a company that no longer exists in balance-sheet terms — no term debt, $731.2M of escrow cash. Leverage conclusions here come from the Q2 10-Q.

**Financial health: 4.5.** Real cash-generating capability (FY2025 OCF $520.8M) and a manageable 2.6–3.1x cash leverage, set against no GAAP interest coverage, negative first-half operating cash flow, undisclosed covenant headroom, floating-rate exposure, and a paused buyback. Unchanged from the prior review; nothing in the Q2 print resolves the covenant or cash-balance gaps.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, with different quality.

**LUMRYZ.** $96.6M in Q2, orphan exclusivity through 2030/2031, a settled coexistence with Jazz's XYWAV after the October 2025 settlement (Alkermes now pays Jazz a 3.85% royalty on narcolepsy-indication LUMRYZ sales, roughly $15M annualized at current run rate). The May 2026 REVITALYZ Phase 3 topline in idiopathic hypersomnia was positive enough that Alkermes raised the CVR probability from 75% to 90%. Label expansion into IH is the near-term growth driver here.

**LYBALVI.** +21% in 1H26 against AbbVie's VRAYLAR and Janssen's long-acting injectables, in a category facing Medicaid funding contraction under the OBBBA and IRA price negotiation on VRAYLAR effective 2027. Twenty-one percent growth in that environment is a competitive result, not a tailwind artifact.

**Alixorexton.** Phase 2 data in NT1 (Vibrance-1) and NT2 (Vibrance-2) were characterized by the company as clinically meaningful and statistically significant, generally well tolerated at 10, 14 and 18 mg, with a nine-month long-term-extension interim readout in July 2026 showing sustained benefit. Effect sizes were not obtained, so no cross-trial comparison against Takeda's pivotal data is possible from the available evidence. Program spend is escalating: alixorexton-specific R&D was $95.8M in FY2025 versus $46.0M in FY2024 and $54.3M in 1H26 alone, with "other orexin programs" at $21.9M in 1H26 versus $6.2M in 1H25 — Alkermes is building a franchise around the mechanism.

**The offsets.** Q3 2026 revenue is guided to $450–470M, a 7.3% sequential decline from $496.0M and the first of the post-acquisition era. Management's stated reason was not obtainable; plausible explanations include channel-inventory normalization after integration, gross-to-net and Medicaid/340B rebate timing, or seasonality. This should not be read as demand deterioration without that explanation, but it does argue against extrapolating +27%. More concretely, the Teva license permits a generic VIVITROL from January 15, 2027, against a product at roughly 27% of 1H26 revenue; Amneal is licensed from sometime in 2028 or earlier and holds authorized-generic distribution rights whose economic split is undisclosed. Alkermes won its recent VIVITROL patent challenges (Apotex IPR discretionarily denied July 2025; Argentum's challenged claims confirmed October 2025), but those wins do not reopen exclusivity because the licenses were granted by settlement.

**Estimate revisions require care.** Consensus FY2026 EPS moved to $1.27 from $0.98 thirty days ago (+29.6%) and FY2027 to $1.83 from $1.56 (+17.5%). The 90-day figures (+2,025% and +161%) are base-effect artifacts from near-zero starting estimates as analysts rebuilt models around purchase accounting. Meanwhile current-quarter consensus fell 31.6% and next-quarter 72.0%. The pattern is analysts pushing profitability out within the year while raising annual totals — a revision profile that does not carry the same signal as a clean inflection. On four contributing analysts, the annual lines also carry low statistical weight. We are not treating this as a standalone buy trigger.

**Growth prospects: 6.0**, down modestly. The pipeline TAM is genuine and LUMRYZ is scaling; the Q3 sequential guide down and the five-month-away VIVITROL cliff cap the near-term rate.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count reconciliation.** 173.5M diluted shares × $49.05 = $8.51B equity value. The provided market cap of $8.23B implies approximately 167.8M shares, consistent with a basic or outstanding-share basis. We use diluted for enterprise value and per-share arithmetic and flag the difference rather than reconcile it. The CVR (maximum $165.7M, carried at $136.3M) is a cash-settled obligation owed to former Avadel shareholders, not dilution, and does not belong in the share count.

**Enterprise value.** $8.51B equity plus approximately $1.0B estimated net debt = approximately $9.5B.

- EV / annualized 1H26 revenue ($888.9M × 2 = $1,777.8M): **5.3x**
- EV / annualized Q2 adjusted EBITDA sensitivity ($320–380M): **25–30x**
- P/E on FY2026 consensus $1.27: **38.6x**
- P/E on FY2027 consensus $1.83: **26.8x**

The trailing P/E of approximately 125x reported by data providers reflects TTM GAAP EPS of roughly $0.38 (Q3'25 $0.49 + Q4'25 $0.29 + Q1'26 −$0.40 + Q2'26 $0.00) and is contaminated by acquisition charges. Neither the trailing figure nor a single-year annual figure is the right anchor; the FY2027 figure is.

**Peer context is low-information.** The pharmaceuticals comparable set shows a P/E median of 44.0 with extreme dispersion — CORT at 207x, PTCT negative, AMRX 44x, APLS 38x, ACAD 9.7x. Mid-cap pharma multiples here are functions of individual pipeline binaries rather than a sector norm, so the median tells us little about ALKS.

**Sum of parts is the more useful frame.** The commercial base in 2027 — VIVITROL eroding, LYBALVI compounding, LUMRYZ scaling, royalties maturing — plausibly produces roughly flat revenue near $1.8–1.9B with adjusted EBITDA in the $450–550M range once the inventory step-up rolls off. At 10–11x EBITDA, appropriate for a levered specialty portfolio with a live generic cliff and public-payer concentration, that is $4.5–6.0B of enterprise value, or roughly $23–29 per share after net debt. The remaining $20–26 of the current price, approximately $3.5–4.5B, is the market's risk-adjusted valuation of the orexin franchise. Against an unrisked peak-sales frame in the low single-digit billions (Jefferies put roughly $2B of peak net sales on Takeda's NT1-only asset at the low end of its price range — a single-source sell-side estimate), a $3.5–4.5B risk-adjusted value implies a probability-weighted view of multi-indication success in the neighborhood of 50%. That is neither obviously generous nor obviously conservative.

Which is the honest answer: at $49.05 the shares are approximately fairly valued on a probability-weighted basis, with the dispersion around that fair value unusually wide because the swing factor is a clinical binary. Analyst targets bracket this — low $38, high $72, mean $56, median $58. The mean of $56 implies +14% from $49.05. On a name where the multiple embeds a Phase 3 outcome, we treat the consensus target as a mid-point of opinion rather than a floor; the guardrail against reading targets as floors applies here because both price and target rest on the same growth extrapolation.

**Valuation: 4.5**, up modestly from 4.0 on a price decline since the last review and higher FY2027 estimates, but still no identifiable margin of safety. Alkermes itself repurchased 1.0M shares at an average $27.82 in Q1 2026 and has bought none since.

## Competitive Landscape

**The orexin class now has an approved product, and it is not Alkermes'.** On August 5, 2026 the FDA approved Takeda's Orzeyful (oveporexton) for adult narcolepsy type 1. Launch still requires DEA scheduling review, which can take up to 90 days, and pricing has not been disclosed. Takeda reached approval after two of its own OX2R-selective agonists failed, which is a useful reminder that mechanism validation does not guarantee molecule success — cutting in both directions here.

Alkermes' counter is label breadth. CEO Blair Jackson has framed it directly: because diagnosis between NT1, NT2 and IH is often blurred, an NT1-only label forces payers to demand diagnostic proof, and Alkermes intends to arrive with approval across all three indications so physicians need not justify a diagnosis. The strategy is coherent and testable — positive Phase 2 data exist in NT1 and NT2, a Phase 2 is running in IH, and Alkermes holds Breakthrough designation in NT1 and Orphan designation in IH. The risks are equally clear: Takeda gains roughly two to three years of prescriber-relationship and formulary build; off-label use of an approved NT1 agent in NT2 and IH patients may erode the differentiation regardless of label; and Alkermes must succeed registrationally in indications where no OX2R agonist has yet done so. Eisai is developing in the class, and Eli Lilly reportedly entered via a $6.3B acquisition of Centessa (single-source, unverified against filings) — which, if accurate, tells us large-cap pharma is willing to pay for orexin assets, a positive for Alkermes' strategic optionality.

Alkermes does own an unusual structural asset in this fight: a commercial narcolepsy product, a narcolepsy sales force, a REMS infrastructure and prescriber relationships. Incremental commercial cost of launching alixorexton is far lower than for a company entering the sleep channel cold. The mirror image is that alixorexton would eventually cannibalize LUMRYZ, structurally the same self-competition dynamic as ARISTADA versus INVEGA.

In the legacy business, VIVITROL's competitive position is deteriorating by contract (licensed generics 2027 and 2028). LYBALVI's is improving (+21% growth against branded incumbents). Manufacturing concentration at a single Ohio site with limited ability to shift production is a structural vulnerability rather than a competitive strength.

**Competitive position: 5.8**, down from 6.5. The loss of first-mover status on the mechanism carrying the valuation is a genuine trajectory negative, partially offset by mechanism validation and Alkermes' existing channel infrastructure.

## Risk Assessment

**VIVITROL generic entry, January 15, 2027.** The most quantified risk: a royalty-free, non-exclusive Teva license on approximately 27% of 1H26 revenue, five months away, with Amneal licensed from sometime in 2028 or earlier and holding authorized-generic distribution rights on undisclosed economics.

**Binary Phase 3 dependence.** Roughly $20–26 per share of the current price is orexin value. A Phase 3 failure or a safety signal compresses the equity toward base-business economics.

**Leverage with unquantified covenant headroom.** GAAP operating income does not cover cash interest; covenant levels were not disclosed; the facility is secured by substantially all assets and restricts repurchases. Floating-rate debt adds a direct cost channel.

**Public-payer exposure, specific to Alkermes' therapeutic areas.** The FY2025 10-K flags OBBBA Medicaid funding reductions and the December 31, 2025 expiry of enhanced PPACA subsidies, both expected to reduce Medicaid enrollment and covered services. VIVITROL (opioid and alcohol dependence) and the antipsychotic franchise skew heavily to public-payer and safety-net channels. This is a demand-side risk more acute here than for typical specialty pharma. Separately, CMS selected VRAYLAR — which the 10-K states competes with LYBALVI and ARISTADA — for Part D negotiation effective 2027; a lower negotiated competitor price could compress LYBALVI's relative payer economics. A screen keyed to whether Alkermes' own products were selected would miss this entirely.

**Operational and legal.** Single-site manufacturing at Wilmington, Ohio for five products; DEA annual manufacturing and procurement quotas on LUMRYZ's Schedule I API that the 10-K notes "may not be sufficient to meet commercial demand," plus REMS dependence; wholesaler concentration across Cardinal, Cencora and McKesson; a Value Drug Company antitrust class action alleging fraudulent patent procurement on VIVITROL (filed approximately October 2025); LYBALVI Paragraph IV litigation against Teva, Apotex and MSN. Offsetting: the Acorda arbitration was affirmed in Alkermes' favor by the Second Circuit on July 15, 2026, and the Janssen NANOCRYSTAL arbitration was previously won.

**Governance and control.** A new CEO (August 1), a CFO who joined approximately September 2025, and a "VP, Finance (Interim Principal Accounting Officer)" with no permanent chief accounting officer — all concurrent with first-time purchase accounting on a $2.2B acquisition, new maintenance covenants, a Schedule III controlled substance, and a CVR fair-value estimate requiring quarterly probability judgments. No material weakness has been disclosed; this is a monitoring item for the FY2026 Item 9A disclosure.

**Unresolved items.** An 8-K filed July 8, 2026 under Item 1.02 (Termination of a Material Definitive Agreement) whose content could not be retrieved. Tax-structure tails: potential IRS challenge to Irish corporate treatment under Section 7874, an Irish Revenue question over whether the May 2024 Athlone sale discontinued trade (jeopardizing approximately $87.5M of deferred tax assets), and challenge risk to the Mural Oncology separation's tax-free treatment.

**Activist setup.** Prior engagement with Elliott and Sarissa, 2020 and 2021 board settlements, and a contested 2023 election. A management transition plus a levered acquisition plus a 2027 revenue cliff is a recognizable pattern; the company retains this as a standing risk factor.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 37.3% against 40.8% one-year realized — cheap by 3.4 points, with implied volatility having collapsed from 54.3% on July 19 as the Q2 print resolved. The term structure is in contango (37.3% at 13 days, 43.2% at 41 days, 40.9% at 104 days), which is the normal, non-stressed shape and prices no near-term dislocation. Put/call skew is reported as balanced at the front (−0.6%) with a larger reading at six months (−4.1%); we report the values without inferring direction. Put/call open interest of 0.36 is call-tilted. The implied move is ±6.0% into August 21 and ±18.6% into November 20 — the back end is where event risk sits, consistent with clinical catalysts rather than earnings.

The informative divergence: short interest is 12.4% of float at 5.7 days to cover, yet options positioning is call-tilted with cheap front-end volatility. Institutions carrying a bearish view are expressing it in stock, not in protection, and the options market is not corroborating an imminent negative catalyst. For a name whose central variable is a Phase 3 outcome, cheap longer-dated volatility is the more relevant observation than direction.

**Risk factors: 4.0**, down from approximately 4.5. The VIVITROL cliff has moved inside six months, leverage is now confirmed rather than prospective, and the commercial risk to alixorexton's addressable peak rose with a competitor approved first.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $75 (+53%)

Alixorexton reads out positively in both Brilliance Phase 3 indications and the Vibrance-3 IH Phase 2 supports a three-indication filing. Label breadth converts into the payer-friction advantage management describes, and Alkermes launches into a narcolepsy channel it already owns, with LUMRYZ prescriber relationships and REMS infrastructure defraying commercial cost. LUMRYZ wins the IH label, triggering the CVR and taking the franchise toward $500M+. LYBALVI compounds past $500M annually behind 2032–2041 patents. The $77.9M residual inventory step-up rolls off, adjusted EBITDA moves toward $600M+, leverage falls below 2x, and repurchases resume. FY2027 consensus of $1.83 proves conservative. Reported large-cap appetite for orexin assets creates strategic-value support at a premium to standalone value. On $700M of 2028–29 EBITDA at 12x less declining net debt, equity approaches $13B, or roughly $75 per share.

### Bear Case — $28 (−43%)

Brilliance misses in NT2, or delivers an efficacy margin that cannot compete with an established Orzeyful franchise, or surfaces a tolerability signal in longer dosing. Orexin value collapses toward option value. Simultaneously the VIVITROL generic takes share faster than modeled from January 2027 while OBBBA Medicaid contraction reduces covered volumes in both the addiction and antipsychotic channels, and the VRAYLAR negotiated price pressures LYBALVI's payer positioning. Total revenue declines in 2027; adjusted EBITDA sits near $420–450M against roughly $104M of cash interest and covenants whose headroom nobody outside the company can size. The multiple compresses to 8–9x, and the equity trades toward the pre-Avadel range adjusted for LUMRYZ and debt — approximately $28, against a 52-week low of $25.17 and the FY2025 closing range of $26.13–$36.00.

### Base Case — $54 (+10%)

The commercial base holds roughly flat through the VIVITROL transition as LUMRYZ and LYBALVI offset erosion; adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $500M in 2027 as the step-up amortization clears; at 10–11x that is $23–29 per share of base-business value. Alixorexton retains a risk-adjusted value in the $3.5–4.5B range as Phase 3 progresses without resolution, giving $20–26 per share. The sum is $51–56; we take $54. Probability-weighting the three cases at 30% bull, 45% base and 25% bear gives an expected return of approximately +10% — positive, but not enough per unit of dispersion to justify new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on ALKS, and the overall score has declined from 6.2 in May to 4.95 now. The trajectory is real and reflects confirmed changes: the balance sheet levered in February, the VIVITROL cliff moved closer, Q3 revenue is guided below Q2, and a competitor was approved first in the mechanism that carries the multiple. Against that, the price decline since July 19 and higher FY2027 estimates improve entry arithmetic, and mechanism validation plus strategic appetite for orexin assets partially offset the loss of first-mover position. We hold because expected return is modestly positive and the dominant variable is a clinical outcome we cannot handicap from available evidence (Phase 2 effect sizes were not obtainable, precluding any cross-trial comparison with Orzeyful). That is an uncertain hold, not a conviction one.

- **Base case target: $54** (+10.1% from $49.05). Sum of parts: $23–29 base-business value at 10–11x approximately $500M of 2027 adjusted EBITDA less approximately $1.0B net debt, plus $20–26 of risk-adjusted orexin value.
- **Bull case target: $75** (+52.9%).
- **Bear case target: $28** (−42.9%).
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to Q3 2026 results (which should clarify the sequential guide-down), the January 15, 2027 VIVITROL generic date, CVR milestone determination expected by end-2027, and Brilliance Phase 3 timing.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price below $40 with Phase 3 enrollment and timelines intact — that level restores roughly 35% upside to base case against approximately 30% bear downside.
2. Disclosure of the 6/30 cash balance and covenant levels showing net leverage below 2.5x with material headroom, plus two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow.
3. A positive Vibrance-3 IH readout or Brilliance interim, or FDA acceptance of a LUMRYZ IH filing — either converts the label-breadth strategy from plan into evidence.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 revenue guidance below $450M, or VIVITROL net sales declining more than 40% in the first two quarters after generic entry.
2. Any Phase 3 delay, dose modification, protocol amendment or tolerability disclosure in the Brilliance program.
3. Covenant amendment, waiver, or a going-concern-adjacent liquidity disclosure; or a material weakness in the FY2026 Item 9A disclosure given the interim principal accounting officer arrangement.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 results (expected late October 2026), and immediately on any 8-K covering the Brilliance program, covenant terms, or the unresolved July 8 Item 1.02 termination.

## Conclusion

Alkermes is two businesses valued as one. The commercial portfolio — $1.8B of revenue, growing LYBALVI and LUMRYZ, eroding VIVITROL and royalties, roughly $500M of normalized adjusted EBITDA once purchase-accounting charges clear — supports something in the mid-$20s per share after $1.0B of secured net debt. The other half of the price is a risk-adjusted claim on an oral orexin franchise that has produced encouraging Phase 2 data, entered Phase 3 in April, and now faces an approved competitor in its lead indication.
