**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Marriott Vacations Worldwide (MVW) is a global vacation-ownership (timeshare) developer and consumer lender that licenses the Marriott, Sheraton, Westin, Ritz-Carlton, and Hyatt brands. The investment case is a turnaround in its first two innings. FY2025 produced a $308M net loss against $217M of net income the prior year, driven by $577M of impairments, ~$122M of restructuring charges, and rising loan-default reserves. An S&P downgrade to B+ followed. A new external CEO/COO team (Matthew Avril and Michael Flaskey) took over, and the stock fell to a $44.58 52-week low in November 2025.

The stock has since rallied roughly 40% off that low to $94, and the central question for the report is whether that move is unfounded sentiment or a rational response to identifiable de-risking. The evidence favors the latter on several counts. The Spanish Supreme Court overturned the 2015 rulings that had voided certain timeshare contracts (November 2025), followed by a January 2026 decision striking down another owner legal theory, together removing a multi-year contract-cancellation tail risk. The 2026 convertible was repaid at maturity, the ABS funding channel is open (a post-quarter $470M securitization priced at 4.86%, in line with peer HGV at ~5.16%), and asset monetization is underway ($50M Cancún sale in Q1, $200-250M targeted by end-2027). Forward estimates for the out-year are rising: next-year EPS consensus moved +6.8% over 90 days, next-quarter +11.2%.

The bearish elements are real but largely in the price. VAC trades at roughly 11-12.5x forward EPS ($7.51 current-year / $8.45 next-year consensus) versus a peer median P/E of 27.8x; short interest sits at 11.1% of float; the stock trades right at the consensus median target of $94.50; and the Street is net-positive (7 buy-equivalent ratings versus 3 sell-equivalent). What remains unresolved is execution: development margins compressed 610 bps in Q1, the Q1 EPS came in 25.5% below consensus, leverage is 4.2x, the loan book is subprime-skewed (~$485M of sub-700/no-FICO paper against a 13.4% modeled default rate), and the sales recovery is back-half weighted and unproven. Fundamental concerns offset by a removed litigation tail, adequate liquidity, a deep valuation discount, and constructive out-year revisions, with no dated downside catalyst, supports a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Company Overview

MVW operates two segments. **Vacation Ownership (~95% of revenue)** develops, markets, sells, and manages timeshare interests under licensed brands, with four revenue streams: VOI sales, resort/association management, consumer financing (it finances more than 55% of VOI sales), and rental of unsold inventory. The financing operation is a major profit center and effectively makes MVW a consumer-finance company wrapped in a hospitality brand. **Exchange & Third-Party Management (~5% of revenue)** runs Interval International (3,200+ resorts, ~1.5M members) and Aqua-Aston, a high-margin fee business (~45% segment EBITDA margin).

The competitive moat rests on long-term brand license agreements with Marriott International and Hyatt running to 2090-2095, granting access to powerful loyalty programs and co-located hotels (~25% of resorts) as marketing channels. The corresponding vulnerability is that the brands are licensed, not owned; termination for breach or brand-standard failure would be severe, and the licensors retain rights to compete under certain conditions. MVW has been an independent public company since its 2011 spin-off; Marriott International is a licensor, not a parent.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was the defining negative event.** The drivers were $577M of impairment charges (a North America development strategy shift plus a share-price-triggered goodwill writedown), the ~$122M of "Modernization" restructuring charges, and higher VOI notes-receivable reserves on elevated defaults.

**Q1 2026 is the first quarter under new management and the data is mixed.** Total revenue rose 5% to $1,257M, but that was driven by pass-through cost reimbursements; "real" VOI product-sales revenue fell 3% to $343M. Net income fell to $22M from $56M, with diluted EPS of $0.64 versus $1.46. Adjusted EBITDA fell 16% to $161M, with the margin compressing 370 bps to 19.5%. The most important red flag was development-profit margin collapsing to 16.1% from 22.2%, a 610 bps decline driven by intentionally higher marketing and sales spend on lower volumes. Contract sales fell 2% to $411M, tours fell 3%, VPG rose 1% to $4,016, and first-time-buyer contract sales fell 12%. Rental profit fell 27% on higher unsold-inventory maintenance costs.

The reported Q1 EPS of $1.24 (adjusted basis per the surprise table) missed the $1.66 estimate, the only miss in the last four quarters after beats of 17.3%, 3.4%, and 9.7%. That single miss is the strongest near-term bear data point, but it should be weighed against the rising out-year estimate trend rather than treated as terminal proof the turnaround has failed.

**Leverage is the central balance-sheet risk.** Corporate debt is ~$3.3B plus $2.3B of non-recourse securitized debt. Corporate net debt / Adjusted EBITDA is 4.2x. The financial-health screen flags a distressed Altman Z (~0.9) and D/E ~3.9, though the Z-Score is of limited relevance for a finance-heavy model with large securitized receivables. Goodwill of $2,958M is ~1.5x total equity ($1,992M), leaving meaningful further impairment exposure if the turnaround stalls.

**Liquidity is adequate.** Cash is $268M with $787M available on the revolver and a functioning ABS market. The $575M 2026 convertible was repaid at maturity via the 2033 Notes issuance, removing the nearest debt cliff. Operating cash flow was –$4M in Q1 (a seasonally weak quarter). The company pays an $0.80/quarter dividend (~3.4% yield) and has $322M of buyback authorization, but repurchased zero shares in Q1, a deliberate pivot toward deleveraging and liquidity preservation. Dividend sustainability bears watching if FCF conversion disappoints, but the cash and revolver capacity provide a runway.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest pillar of the thesis on a trailing basis and the most contested looking forward. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 3-7% contract-sales growth and Adjusted EBITDA, with the recovery explicitly back-half weighted. The Modernization program is expected to incur another ~$85M in 2026, with benefits accruing in late-2026 and 2027.

The estimate-revision picture is genuinely two-sided and worth reconciling carefully. Current-quarter estimates fell 4.4% over 30 days and 10.2% over 90 days, and current-year EPS fell 5.3% over 90 days, reflecting the Q1 miss and conservative near-term sales. But next-quarter estimates rose 11.2% over 90 days and next-year EPS rose 6.8% over 90 days. This is a near-term reset paired with rising confidence in the 2027 recovery. The +6.8% next-year revision does not clear the +30% threshold that would constitute a high-conviction growth signal, so it should be read as constructive but not decisive.

The growth thesis depends on several unproven assumptions: that the new sales-talent investments lift VPG and tours in H2; that first-time-buyer weakness (down 12% in Q1) reverses; and that development margins recover from the 16.1% trough as marketing spend normalizes against higher volumes. None of this is yet visible in the reported numbers. The asset-monetization plan ($200-250M gross proceeds by end-2027) and the Asia-Pacific scale-back are deleveraging and capital-efficiency levers rather than growth drivers. I assign a below-average growth score reflecting genuine forward uncertainty, with the rising out-year revisions preventing a lower mark.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is meaningless given the FY2025 loss, so forward and normalized earnings are the only sensible anchors.

Peer comparables: HGV at 27.8x, MTN at 31.2x, TNL at 20.6x, CHH at 14.5x, PRSU at 43.9x. VAC's nearest direct comp on business model is HGV (also a timeshare developer-lender), trading at more than double VAC's forward multiple. The discount is partly earned: VAC carries higher leverage, a fresh credit downgrade, a more visible margin reset, and an unproven management team. But a roughly 55% discount to the peer median for a business with a functioning funding channel, an improved litigation outlook, and rising out-year estimates is wide enough to limit downside.

The stock currently sits at the consensus median target of $94.50, against a mean of $87.30, a high of $105.00, and a low of $51.00. The wide target dispersion ($51 to $105) accurately reflects a binary turnaround. Trading at the median target with a net-positive Street rating means the easy de-rating has occurred; further upside requires execution proof, and meaningful downside requires a fresh negative catalyst. The valuation supports a constructive-to-neutral score: cheap on forward multiples, but the cheapness reflects real execution risk rather than a clean mispricing with an identified catalyst. I score valuation moderately above neutral, recognizing both the genuine discount and the absence of a near-term re-rating trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

MVW holds a defensible position anchored by brand licenses to 2090-2095 and the Interval International exchange network (~1.5M members). Interval trails RCI (owned by Travel + Leisure) in affiliated resorts, and MVW competes with HGV, Travel + Leisure, and alternative lodging (short-term rental marketplaces, hotels).

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-deteriorating in the near term. The development margin and first-time-buyer decline suggest sales productivity is under pressure, partly from intentional reinvestment and partly from a softer lower-prime consumer. The industry-wide "timeshare exit" theme is a reputational and demand headwind affecting the whole VOI sector, pressuring both default rates and secondary-market pricing on new sales. Against that, the brand-license moat and recurring management/financing revenue provide a durable base that competitors cannot easily replicate. The replacement of capital-intensive owned development with capital-efficient inventory deals (Puerto Vallarta, Nashville, Thailand, Bali, Waikiki) is a sensible structural adjustment. On balance, the competitive position is intact but not improving, warranting a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer credit risk is the most acute and company-specific.** MVW holds a $3.1B VOI notes-receivable portfolio with a 13.4% modeled average remaining default rate, a 730 weighted-average FICO, and ~$485M of loans to sub-700/no-score borrowers. A 0.5-point increase in defaults adds ~$15M of reserves. This is a leveraged bet on lower-prime discretionary spending. Rising defaults already contributed to the 2025 loss, and further macro deterioration could re-accelerate reserve builds.

**Leverage and the B+ rating** constrain flexibility and create dependence on a healthy ABS market. All 12 securitization pools are currently compliant, but a pool breaching default/delinquency triggers could trap cash. **Goodwill (1.5x equity)** is a mechanical risk: further share-price weakness could trigger additional writedowns, compounding equity erosion. **Brand-license dependency** is a low-probability, high-severity tail. **Execution risk** on the back-half-weighted turnaround is the swing factor.

The Spanish litigation is the main risk improvement: the November 2025 Supreme Court reversal plus the January 2026 decision removed a long-standing contract-cancellation tail. This is a genuine value event and part of what justifies the rally off the low.

Most of these risks — leverage, the downgrade, the weak Q1 — are already discounted into the forward multiple, the rating, and the short interest. What is not obviously priced is a back-half sales miss or a fresh credit-reserve acceleration; those are the live downside catalysts, but neither is dated or imminent.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 86.9% versus 55.5% one-year historical vol, an 88th-percentile reading, with a steep backwardation (near-term 86.9% > six-month 49.5%) signaling the market expects a near-dated catalyst to resolve. ATM IV jumped from 55.6% to 86.9% as the stock rose 25% since mid-May, consistent with positioning into a binary event rather than directional bearishness. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.9%, and the six-month skew is slightly negative (-2.6%), meaning the market is not pricing a structural collapse. The implied one-week move is ±3.6%. Options confirm elevated near-term uncertainty without a directional bearish lean, consistent with a HOLD rather than a high-conviction call in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The new Avril/Flaskey team executes the back-half-2026 sales recovery; development margins normalize from the 16.1% trough toward the low-20s as marketing spend leverages higher volumes; the Modernization program delivers its 2027 cost benefits; deleveraging from 4.2x progresses via the asset-monetization plan; and the removed Spanish litigation tail plus a functioning ABS market support a re-rating toward the peer cohort. On the $8.45 next-year consensus, even a modest re-rating to 14-15x forward (still a deep discount to HGV at 27.8x) supports a price of roughly $118-127. The bull-case high target of $105 from the Street is conservative against that math.

**Bear Case.** The consumer-credit cycle turns against the subprime-skewed loan book, forcing renewed reserve builds; the back-half sales recovery fails to materialize and contract sales miss the 3-7% guide; development margins stay depressed; FCF conversion disappoints and the dividend comes under pressure; and further share weakness triggers incremental goodwill impairment. In that scenario, the stock re-tests the low-$50s, roughly the $51 Street low target and consistent with a return toward the November 2025 lows, a 40-45% decline from current levels.

The balance between these cases is genuinely two-sided. The litigation tail is removed, liquidity is adequate, the nearest debt maturity is repaid, and out-year estimates are rising, which is why the bearish thesis does not support an active short. But the turnaround is unproven in the numbers, leverage is high, and the loan book is exposed to consumer credit deterioration, which is why it does not yet support a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** This is an honest acknowledgment that the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction. The bearish elements are heavily discounted; the bullish recovery is unproven.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of development-margin recovery (back above ~20%) confirming the sales-productivity reinvestment is working.
- Contract sales tracking to the upper half of the 3-7% guide in Q2/Q3 2026, with first-time-buyer sales returning to growth.
- VOI notes-receivable default rate stabilizing or declining below 13.4% with no incremental reserve build, demonstrating the loan book is holding through consumer stress.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Renewed reserve acceleration or a default-rate increase pushing toward the mid-teens, signaling the credit cycle has turned against the subprime book.
- A contract-sales miss below the 3% guidance floor combined with continued margin compression, indicating the turnaround has stalled.
- A securitization-pool trigger breach trapping cash, or a dividend cut signaling FCF distress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural reassessment point, the first quarter that should begin to show whether the back-half recovery is materializing. The narrative is fully back-half-2026/2027 weighted, so visibility improves materially with each of the next two prints.

## Conclusion

VAC is a leveraged timeshare developer-lender in the early, unproven stage of a new-management turnaround. The prior AVOID stance is no longer supported by the evidence balance: the bearish thesis (high leverage, B+ rating, weak Q1) is already heavily discounted via the forward multiple, the completed S&P downgrade, the short interest, and the stock trading at its median target. Meanwhile, identifiable de-risking events — the Spanish Supreme Court reversals removing a long-standing litigation tail, the management reset, the repaid 2026 convertible, and the asset-monetization program — make the 40% rally a rational response to value events rather than pure sentiment. Out-year estimates are rising, and the Street is net-positive.

At the same time, the turnaround is genuinely unproven: development margins compressed in Q1, the Q1 EPS missed consensus, leverage is high, and the core profit engine is a subprime-skewed loan book exposed to a weakening consumer. There is no dated downside catalyst to justify an active short, and no confirmed turn to justify a buy. The score's upward trajectory (4.3 → 4.9 → 5.1) reflects incrementally improving risk/reward as the litigation tail cleared and liquidity stabilized, not a fundamental breakout. This is a balanced, watch-and-reassess situation best expressed as a HOLD, with the next two earnings prints determining whether it migrates toward BUY or AVOID.
