**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zillow Group has reached an operating milestone that materially changes how the business should be valued: after losing $158M in 2023 and $112M in 2024, the company posted its first full-year GAAP profit in 2025 ($23M net income on $2.583B revenue, +16%), and Q1 2026 accelerated to $46M net income on $708M revenue (+18% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA margin reaching 26%. Operating leverage is real: FY25 operating expenses rose 2% against 16% revenue growth, and the balance sheet is among the strongest in the digital-marketplace space (Altman Z 5.6, current ratio 3.1, debt/equity 0.16, no corporate term debt). Adjusted free cash flow of $420M in FY25 funds an aggressive buyback program that retired roughly $670M of stock in FY25 and an outsized $626M in Q1 2026 alone.

The market has nonetheless taken the stock down 52% from its $93.88 September 2025 high to $32.11, and the burden of proof at that magnitude of drawdown falls on the bull case. Two issues explain the de-rating and remain unresolved. First, the quality of growth: visits (-3% YoY to 2,276M) and unique users (-3% to 220M) are declining, so all revenue growth comes from higher monetization per visit (Residential revenue per visit +12% in Q1'26). This works while pricing power holds, but it raises a structural question about how long monetization can outrun a shrinking funnel, particularly with AI search assistants threatening portal traffic and CoStar's Homes.com spending aggressively to take share. Second, the listing-data dependency that underpins the entire business is under active assault: two MLSs (MRED Chicago, Realtracs Tennessee) cut their feeds in mid-2026, and the FTC's challenge to the Zillow-Redfin rentals partnership survived a motion to dismiss in May 2026, sending the case that bears on the fastest-growing segment into discovery.

At ~14x the $2.31 current-year EPS estimate and ~10.6x the $3.02 next-year estimate, the valuation has compressed to a level that is no longer obviously expensive for a profitable, cash-generative platform, but estimate revisions are flat (no 30-day movement; current-quarter consensus actually cut 14% over 90 days), so there is no positive earnings-momentum signal to lean on. The combination of a real fundamental improvement, a cheaper entry, and two unresolved structural and legal overhangs produces a balanced setup. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Zillow operates the most-visited U.S. residential real estate digital platform (Zillow, Trulia, StreetEasy, HotPads), built on a database of approximately 173 million U.S. homes and the proprietary Zestimate valuation model. The business is an advertising and marketplace platform with attached transaction services, not a brokerage or home-flipper; the iBuying "Zillow Offers" operation was wound down in 2021, though related litigation persists.

The revenue base splits into four segments (FY2025):
- **Residential — $1,704M (66%):** Premier Agent advertising on share-of-voice pricing, the "Zillow Preferred" pay-when-you-close program (formerly Flex), plus SaaS tools (Follow Up Boss CRM, ShowingTime, dotloop), Zillow Showcase, and New Construction.
- **Rentals — $630M (24%):** The growth engine. Multifamily advertising plus rental applications; multifamily revenue grew 58% and property count rose 44% to 72,000.
- **Mortgages — $199M (8%):** Zillow Home Loans originations (sold on the secondary market) plus lead generation.
- **Other — $50M (2%):** Display advertising.

The strategic thesis is the "Enhanced Markets" integrated-transaction model: funnel high-intent movers into Zillow Preferred agents and Zillow Home Loans, monetizing one mover across multiple touchpoints. Enhanced Market connections exited 2025 above 40% of total. Management cites a $202B TAM and notes that roughly 80% of U.S. transactions involve an agent using at least one Zillow product.

## Financial Analysis

The profitability improvement is the central fact, and it is supported by cash flow rather than accounting choices.

| Revenue | $1,945M | $2,236M | $2,583M (+16%) | $708M (+18% YoY) |
| Net income | -$158M | -$112M | +$23M | +$46M (vs $8M) |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $498M (22%) | $622M (24%) | $182M (26%) |
| Operating cash flow | — | $428M | $368M | $200M |

Operating leverage is evident in the expense structure: G&A fell 5% on lower stock compensation. Adjusted free cash flow reached $420M in FY25 (from $309M), with $127M in Q1'26.

The balance sheet is built to absorb stress and fund continued investment. There is no corporate term debt; the only borrowings are mortgage warehouse repo facilities ($650M capacity, $335M drawn at ~5.2%, uncommitted and non-recourse to the parent), supplemented by an undrawn $500M revolver established in January 2026. Cash and investments stood at roughly $788M as of Q1'26, down sharply from $1.3B, the decline driven by buybacks rather than operations.

Two items deserve specific attention. First, gross margin compressed from 76% to 74% in FY25 and from 77% to 73% in Q1'26, driven by lead-acquisition costs tied to the Redfin rentals syndication agreement. This is a deliberate trade of margin for rentals traffic and scale, defensible as long as the rentals segment continues to grow at the rate it has shown. Second, $1.8B of federal NOLs sit behind a full valuation allowance; management states there is "a reasonable possibility within the next several quarters" the allowance will be partially released, which would produce a one-time GAAP earnings boost and signal management confidence in durable profitability.

The earnings track record is mixed: over the last four quarters, one beat (Q1'26 +14.2%), one in-line, and two misses (Q4'25 -4.4%, Q2'25 -7.3%). This argues for moderate rather than high confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has a clear bright spot and a clear concern. Rentals is the bright spot — segment revenue +42% in Q1'26 — and it benefits from a structural advantage: there is no MLS equivalent in rentals, so aggregation is a durable moat that does not depend on third-party listing feeds the way the residential business does. Mortgages also grew rapidly (revenue +56%, loan volume +97% in Q1'26), supporting the integrated-transaction attach thesis.

The concern is engagement. Every dollar of revenue growth came from higher monetization per visit, not from a growing audience. The mechanism that makes this work — Enhanced Markets conversion plus share-of-voice pricing power on a fixed-to-shrinking base of high-intent movers — is real today. The question is durability: if AI search assistants reroute consumers away from real estate portals (a risk management explicitly flags), or if CoStar's Homes.com captures meaningful traffic with its aggressive spend, the funnel that monetization depends on erodes. Higher monetization on a declining audience can continue for several quarters; it cannot continue indefinitely.

Estimate revisions offer no support for an acceleration thesis. Current-year EPS consensus of $2.31 is up 4.1% over 90 days but flat over 30 days; next-year of $3.02 is up only 1.7% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate was cut 14.1% over 90 days. This is a stable-to-modestly-positive revision picture, not the +30% velocity that would constitute a strong forward signal. The growth thesis rests on management execution and rentals scaling, not on rising consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 132x is meaningless given the profitability transition — the trailing period spans the loss-to-profit transition, so trailing multiples reflect trough rather than steady-state earnings. The appropriate lens is forward: at $32.11, the stock trades at ~14x the $2.31 current-year EPS estimate and ~10.6x the $3.02 next-year estimate. On EV/EBITDA, with ~$788M net cash and ~$622M trailing adjusted EBITDA growing into the high-$600s/low-$700s, the enterprise trades at roughly 7-8x forward EBITDA, which is undemanding for a dominant platform franchise generating 24-26% margins and $400M+ in free cash flow.

The valuation has genuinely compressed: the stock is down 52% from its September high while fundamentals improved, which on its face is the profile of improving risk/reward. But two things prevent a high valuation score. First, the magnitude of the drawdown is too large to dismiss as volatility; sophisticated investors have been selling, and the things they are pricing (engagement decline, AI/CoStar threat to the funnel, surviving FTC litigation on the rentals engine) are specific and falsifiable, not vague. Second, there is no positive estimate-revision catalyst; consensus is flat over 30 days. A cheap multiple without a re-rating catalyst is the classic value-trap configuration, and the lesson from prior cycles is that cheapness alone sorts the wrong way.

Analyst targets ($40 low, $61 median, $76 high) sit 25% to 137% above the current price, implying the sell side views the de-rating as overdone. These targets function as a directional floor only if the engagement and litigation overhangs resolve favorably; given that estimates are flat and the consensus is heavily HOLD-weighted (5 holds, 2 buys), the targets reflect lagging models more than active conviction. We assign a 5.5: cheap enough that downside is partly cushioned, but without the catalyst or revision momentum to justify rewarding the cheapness as asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Zillow's moat rests on brand dominance ("Zillow" is searched more than "real estate"), the proprietary Zestimate and homes database, the largest rental listing coverage, and deep MLS/agent relationships. Trajectory is the relevant question, and it is genuinely mixed.

On the positive side, Zillow won its battle with Compass: the brokerage voluntarily dismissed its antitrust suit in March 2026 after losing a preliminary-injunction bid, and Zillow went on offense with Illinois litigation against MRED/Compass. The rentals segment is gaining share in a category with no MLS dependency, a durable structural advantage.

On the negative side, the listing-data leverage that the entire residential business depends on is shifting against Zillow at the MLS level. Zillow does not own its core inventory; it depends on MLSs and listing providers, many cancellable on short notice. In 2026, MRED (Chicago) dropped tens of thousands of listings and Realtracs (Tennessee) cut its feed June 1 over IDX compliance. These cutoffs are early evidence that Zillow's hardball "listing access standards" — the same standards that beat Compass — can backfire into lost inventory. CoStar's Homes.com is spending aggressively to challenge traffic dominance, and AI search assistants represent a longer-term disintermediation threat that management acknowledges. The 2024 NAR commission settlement, which decoupled buyer-broker commissions, may pressure agent marketing budgets — Zillow's core revenue base — over time. The competitive position is defensible at the franchise level but the trajectory shows real friction, which caps the score at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Listing-data access is the existential, company-specific risk.** Zillow does not own its inventory, and any broad erosion of MLS access directly degrades traffic and revenue. The MRED and Realtracs cutoffs are concrete, recent instances, and they were partly self-inflicted by Zillow's aggressive listing standards.

**Antitrust litigation is a two-front, partly binary exposure.** The FTC plus five state AGs challenge the Zillow-Redfin rentals partnership (the $100M Feb 2025 deal that drives rentals traffic); the motion to dismiss was denied in May 2026, sending the case into discovery. An adverse outcome could force restructuring of the partnership and dent the trajectory of the fastest-growing segment. The Compass suit was dismissed, but CoStar continues to circle.

**Legacy Zillow Offers liability remains open.** The securities class action over iBuying disclosures was certified (Aug 2024), affirmed by the 9th Circuit (Sept 2025), with en banc rehearing denied (Jan 2026), heading toward trial or settlement. No accrual is recorded; the loss is "not estimable," an unquantified tail.

**Housing-market dependence** caps the ceiling. Revenue is geared to existing-home sale volumes near multi-decade lows on affordability and rate lock-in. Zillow is outgrowing the market via share gains, but a further downturn limits upside. **Governance discount is structural.** Class C shares (the listed ticker) carry zero votes; co-founders Barton and Frink control ~58% of voting power via Class B super-voting stock. Investors own economics without influence.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing imminent distress. Near-term ATM IV of 57.4% is rich relative to 44.9% one-year historical vol (12.5pp spread), but sits in only the 12th percentile of its own chain, and put/call skew is balanced (-1.6%). The term structure is in contango (57.4% near vs 64.2% long-term), a normal/complacent configuration rather than a panic curve. IV has collapsed from 95.3% to 57.4% since early May while the stock fell 26.5%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.12 to 0.70: the price decline has come with declining, not rising, hedging demand and falling implied vol, which suggests the selling is fundamental repricing rather than a fear-driven cascade. The 6-month tenor shows an elevated P/C volume ratio of 2.50, worth monitoring as possible positioning ahead of the FTC discovery process. Short interest at 9.9% of float (3.7 days to cover) is moderate — neither a squeeze setup nor a heavily crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward $55-60)
The profitability improvement is durable and accelerating. Rentals continues to compound at 40%+ on a structural no-MLS-equivalent advantage, mortgages attach scales the integrated-transaction model, and Enhanced Markets conversion drives monetization per visit faster than engagement declines. The NOL valuation-allowance release lands in the next several quarters, providing a GAAP earnings boost and confidence signal. The FTC rentals case settles or resolves without forcing a partnership restructuring, and the MLS feed disputes prove isolated. At 24-26% EBITDA margins growing toward $700M+ with $400M+ FCF and aggressive buybacks shrinking the share count, the stock re-rates to ~18-20x the $3.02 next-year EPS, implying $55-60. This roughly aligns with the $61 analyst median and the $76 high.

### Bear Case (toward $24-26)
Declining visits and users prove to be the leading edge of structural traffic erosion as AI search assistants and CoStar's Homes.com capture intent that previously routed to Zillow. Monetization per visit cannot outrun a shrinking funnel, and revenue growth decelerates toward the low single digits. The FTC discovery process forces a restructuring of the Redfin rentals partnership, denting the one segment carrying the growth narrative, while MLS feed cutoffs spread, degrading the residential listing base. The Zillow Offers securities case produces a material settlement. In that scenario, forward EPS estimates roll back below $2.50 and the multiple compresses to ~10x, taking the stock toward $24-26, roughly 20-25% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with genuine two-sidedness rather than a confident directional view, classified as uncertain.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Visits/unique users stabilize or return to YoY growth for two consecutive quarters, removing the engagement-erosion overhang that defines the bear case.
- Favorable resolution or settlement of the FTC rentals case that leaves the Redfin partnership intact, OR clear evidence the MLS feed disputes (MRED, Realtracs) are isolated rather than spreading.
- Positive EPS estimate-revision momentum (current-year consensus rising >5% on a 30-day basis) confirming the monetization thesis is converting to durable earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth decelerates below ~8% YoY alongside accelerating traffic declines, signaling the monetization-on-shrinking-funnel model is breaking.
- Adverse FTC outcome forcing restructuring of the rentals partnership, or a material Zillow Offers settlement that is quantified at a level meaningful to cash.
- Broad MLS feed erosion that visibly degrades listing coverage and Residential revenue.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) for the engagement and rentals trajectory; FTC discovery developments through H2 2026; and any 8-K confirming the NOL valuation-allowance release.

## Conclusion

Zillow is a genuinely better business than it was two years ago — profitable, cash-generative, and balance-sheet secure, with a real growth engine in rentals and an attach story in mortgages. The 52% decline from the September high has reset the valuation to levels that no longer demand heroic growth assumptions. If the only question were price versus current fundamentals, this would be a more constructive call.

The reason for restraint is that the drawdown is large enough to take seriously, and the specific concerns driving it — declining engagement masked by monetization gains, the AI/CoStar threat to the traffic funnel, MLS feed friction that is partly self-inflicted, and an FTC challenge to the rentals partnership that survived dismissal — are unresolved and partly binary. Estimate revisions are flat, so there is no earnings-momentum catalyst to lean on, and the share class carries no votes. The risk/reward is balanced: roughly 90% upside to the analyst median in the bull case against roughly 20-25% downside in the bear case, but with the probability weighting genuinely uncertain pending the next two quarters of engagement data and FTC developments. We rate the shares HOLD and would look to upgrade on traffic stabilization or favorable litigation resolution, and to downgrade on revenue deceleration or an adverse FTC outcome.
