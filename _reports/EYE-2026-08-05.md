## Executive Summary

National Vision is a genuinely improved business trading at a price that no longer compensates for its central unresolved question. FY2025 revenue rose 9.0% to $1,987.5M (7.1% adjusting out the 53rd week's $35.6M), adjusted operating income rose $37.0M to $102.5M, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 120bps to 9.7%. Q1 FY2026 accelerated the margin story: revenue $543.9M (+6.6%), SG&A growth of 0.2% against 6.6% revenue growth producing 300bps of leverage, adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.5% versus 12.6%, and operating cash flow of $61.7M versus $32.2M. Net leverage is 0.91x adjusted EBITDA, the 2.50% convertible notes are fully retired, and the revolver is undrawn with $293.3M available.

The problem sits in the composition of that growth and in a disclosure management made in the Q1 10-Q. FY2025 comps of +6.0% and Q1 comps of +4.5% were both driven, in management's own words, by higher average ticket and managed-care strength "partially offset by" lower customer traffic — in a retailer whose stated historical strategy was targeting lower-income consumers with lowest-price messaging, and which raised the opening-offer price at both banners while shifting mix to frames above $99. Separately, the 10-Q discloses that at the beginning of Q2 the company re-platformed AmericasBest.com, that "traffic was disrupted as search and social optimization resets," and that impacts could persist if remediation takes longer than anticipated. America's Best is 87.5% of revenue. The magnitude is not quantified anywhere in the evidence, Q2 results land August 12, and consensus has cut the current-quarter estimate 12.9% over 90 days to $0.18.

The stock has already traded the round trip. Form 4 transaction prints establish the path: roughly $27.90 in early March, $22.39 on April 29, $15.52 on June 5 (where a director bought 50,000 shares from a zero base for $776,000), $19.41 on July 8, $22.04 on July 31. At $23.15 the shares sit 22.9% below the 52-week high and 49% above the director's June purchase price. On the numbers that matter — 24.1x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $0.96, 19.6x FY2027's $1.18, 40.6x TTM GAAP EPS of $0.57, 9.8x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA, a 3.9% FY2025 free-cash-flow yield — the shares are neither cheap nor egregious. Buying seven days before a binary print whose downside driver management has declined to size, with estimates falling in every forward period and 29.3% of the float short, is not a favorable use of capital. HOLD.

## Company Overview

National Vision Holdings operates 1,274 optical retail locations (April 4, 2026) through four banners, selling eye exams, eyeglasses and contact lenses in the value tier of U.S. optical retail:

| Brand | Stores (Apr 4, 2026) | FY2025 revenue | % of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| America's Best | 1,062 | $1,743.3M | 87.7% |
| Eyeglass World | 122 | $202.4M | 10.2% |
| Vista Optical – Military (AAFES) | 72 | $23.6M | 1.2% |
| Vista Optical – Fred Meyer | 18 | $9.6M | 0.5% |
| Corporate and other | — | $21.0M | 1.0% |

The economics are unusual in two respects that matter for interpretation. First, the America's Best signature offer bundles an eye exam with two pairs of eyeglasses; because the exam is not distinct within the bundle, no revenue is allocated to it, and all of it lands in net product sales ($1,604.6M, 80.7% of FY2025 revenue). "Services and plans" revenue of $382.9M therefore carries the optometrist cost without the corresponding exam revenue, which is why that line runs at 93.6% of revenue in cost. Second, optometrist availability, not consumer demand, has been the capacity constraint. The company's answer is remote/telehealth exams, now installed in over 800 locations, which management states carry higher costs applicable to revenue as a percentage of revenue than in-store exams. Capacity is being purchased at a gross-margin cost, and several states either restrict or prohibit optometrist telehealth exams.

Managed care is 42% of FY2025 revenue, with a small number of payors comprising the majority of it. Supply is highly concentrated: approximately 86% of FY2025 eyeglass-lens expenditures with a single supplier under a near-exclusive purchase commitment, 96% of contact-lens spend with three vendors, 55% of frame spend with two. Some of those suppliers, and some of those payors, are affiliates of competing retailers.

## Financial Analysis

### The FY2025 turn, decomposed

GAAP operating income moved from a $10.4M loss to $58.8M of income, a $69.2M swing. Roughly 55% of that ($37.9M) is the non-recurrence of FY2024's $39.9M impairment — chiefly Eyeglass World goodwill and the Fred Meyer contracts intangible. The cleaner measure is adjusted operating income, which excludes impairment in both years and rose from $65.5M to $102.5M (+$37.0M), with adjusted operating margin moving 3.6% → 5.2%.

That $37M is real, and the drivers are identifiable: costs applicable to revenue improved 70bps to 41.2% (eyeglass mix and margin +140bps, optometrist-cost leverage +10bps, offset by contact-lens and other mix), and SG&A improved 40bps to 51.1% (advertising leverage +170bps, offset by 100bps of variable incentive comp and 30bps of stock comp). The offsetting deterioration: costs of services and plans rose to 93.6% of services revenue from 91.9%, because optometrist costs outgrew exam and add-on revenue.

Investors should also note what the adjusted series excludes: $23.7M of stock compensation, $6.4M of ERP/CRM implementation costs, $3.6M of severance, $2.1M of shareholder-activism costs and $1.9M of litigation settlement — $37.7M of FY2025 items, several of which have recurred every year in the disclosed series. Stock comp alone is $0.29 of the $0.80 adjusted EPS, 36% of it. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.37.

SG&A grew 0.2% on 6.6% revenue growth, driven by lower payroll (−220bps) and lower advertising (−70bps). Costs of services and plans improved 210bps to 88.4%. Interest expense net fell to $2.8M from $4.6M.

Q1 cash flow should not be annualized. The 10-K states the business earns a higher share of revenue, operating income and cash flow in the first half, driven by January insurance resets, with Q4 weakest. Q1 also collected the full $9.0M CARES Act receivable, released $13.2M from receivables, added $8.2M in payables, and paid $3 thousand of cash taxes against an $11.2M book provision. FY2025 provides the better anchor: OCF $146.3M, capex $72.8M, FCF $73.5M (3.7% margin, 3.9% yield on today's market cap). FY2026 capex is guided to $73–78M. A normalized FY2026 FCF range of roughly $90–130M is a reasonable sensitivity band given Q1's $14.2M year-over-year adjusted operating income improvement, higher cash taxes and guided capex, though it is a scenario, not a forecast, and it is directly exposed to the Q2 traffic disruption.

### Balance sheet

At April 4, 2026: cash $67.9M, Term Loan A $234.3M (June 13, 2028 maturity; $13.25M amortizing in 2027, $211.1M in 2028; 5.5% weighted-average rate with $100M notionally swapped to 3.43% fixed), finance leases $8.5M, revolver undrawn with $293.3M available. Gross debt $242.9M, net debt $175.0M. Net debt/FY2025 adjusted EBITDA 0.91x; interest coverage 3.4x on GAAP operating income. All covenants (maximum total leverage, minimum interest coverage) complied with.

Two qualifications. Operating lease liabilities total $450.3M; on a lease-inclusive basis obligations are approximately $625M, which against EBITDAR puts the company in the normal range for a 1,274-store lessee but removes any claim to an unlevered balance sheet. And goodwill of $701.0M plus indefinite-lived trademarks of $240.5M equals $941.5M, or 46.6% of total assets — with an impairment already taken at Eyeglass World, the weaker-comping banner (−2.2% in FY2024). Management states neither banner was at risk of failing the FY2025 test and that a 100bp discount-rate increase would not trigger impairment, which is a point-in-time sensitivity on management's own forecasts.

A note on screening artifacts: a mechanical read of the reported figures produces an Altman Z of 1.77 and a debt/equity of 2.28, both of which are wrong. The 2.28 figure is total assets divided by equity (the equity multiplier), not debt to equity; interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.27x. Rebuilding Altman properly on the FY2025 balance sheet with market equity gives Z ≈ 2.19, the grey zone. The 0.55 current ratio is likewise dominated by $207.2M of current operating-lease obligations, deferred revenue and unearned revenue — capitalized lease accounting and customer prepayments, not trade claims — and excluding them yields roughly $20.5M of positive working capital. Solvency is not the live question here.

## Growth Analysis

Three things are growing, and each carries a qualification.

**Ticket, not traffic.** FY2025 comps +6.0% (America's Best +6.3%, Eyeglass World +4.2%) and Q1 comps +4.5% were both price- and mix-led with traffic explicitly declining. Two readings survive the evidence. The benign one: traffic is capacity-constrained by optometrist scarcity, so higher ticket on constrained exam slots is rational yield management and the telehealth build is the unlock. The adverse one: a value retailer raising both banners' opening-offer prices while pushing frames above $99 is extracting price from an unchanged cohort, and elasticity eventually binds. The filings do not disaggregate traffic into demand-driven versus capacity-constrained, so neither reading can be dismissed. What can be said is that management describes the current environment as having "resulted in reduced customer demand in 2025… primarily for the cash pay consumer," which raises the burden of proof on price-led comps in the value tier.

**Managed care, with a stated deceleration.** At 42% of revenue this is the cohort driving comps, and management states directly that managed-care revenue growth "will slow over time" as penetration approaches industry levels. The cash-pay consumer, where the 10-K describes predictability as uncertain and demand as reduced in 2025, is the weaker half. Payors set coverage and payment levels unilaterally, some own competing retail networks, and the 10-K warns they may decline to admit new or existing stores to those networks.

**Store count, throttled and re-mixed.** New store openings fell from 70 (FY2023) to 69 (FY2024) to 33 (FY2025), guided to 30–35 in FY2026, with new-store capex collapsing from $51.9M to $44.3M to $16.8M as capital shifted to existing-store experience and IT ($34.1M). The AAFES military expansion is the clearest positive of 2026: the agreement was extended through January 2036 and a February 2026 amendment added 20 stores across two new states, purchased via an asset acquisition from U.S. Vision Corp., taking military count from 53 to 72. Military was 1.2% of FY2025 revenue at roughly $444K per store, so 19–20 additional units imply approximately $8–9M annualized, about 0.4% of consolidated revenue. Strategically attractive and it materially de-risks the Host-brand intangible; not financially transformative. Changing channel mix is not diversification: America's Best rose from 85.6% of FY2024 revenue to 87.7% of FY2025 and 87.5% in Q1 FY2026, so brand concentration increased.

**The estimate-revision signal is negative, and it matters.** All four forward periods have been cut: current quarter $0.21 → $0.18 (−12.9% over 90 days), next quarter −6.7%, current year $0.99 → $0.96 (−2.8%), next year $1.25 → $1.18 (−5.7%), across 12 analysts. The current-quarter cut is the market pricing the re-platform. Falling estimates are among the more reliable forward warnings available, and they cut directly against the argument that consensus price targets (mean $29.27) represent a stale floor — that logic holds when estimates are rising, not when they are being reduced. Against this: the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (+4.1%, +146.3%, −1.5%, +30.2%), which supports confidence in the reduced bar being clearable, if not in the direction of revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K describes U.S. optical retail as highly competitive and fragmented, and two structural features deserve weight.

**The supply chain and the payor base are partly owned by competitors.** With approximately 86% of eyeglass-lens expenditures concentrated in a single supplier under a near-exclusive commitment, and 96% of contact-lens spend across three vendors — some of them affiliates of vertically integrated retail competitors — National Vision buys the products it resells from the value chain its rivals control. The 10-K notes it has previously been subject to contact-lens manufacturers' unilateral pricing policies mandating minimum retail prices, and that such manufacturers could refuse supply if they deem the company in breach. For a retailer whose proposition is price, supplier-imposed price floors are a direct constraint on strategy. The counterargument: $819M of annual costs applicable to revenue makes National Vision a very large customer, exclusivity is normally exchanged for preferential pricing, no dispute is disclosed, and FY2025 product gross margin actually improved 90bps, which is not the signature of a supplier squeeze.

**The moat is operational rather than proprietary.** Scale in the value tier, a centralized lab network (Lawrenceville GA, St. Cloud MN, Plano TX, Salt Lake City UT plus outsourced Mexican labs), 20-plus-year host relationships, and the 800-plus-location telehealth installed base are real advantages, and telehealth in particular addresses the industry's binding constraint. But leases, third-party lenses and payor contracts are replicable, the 10-K concedes vertically integrated competitors may advantageously leverage their structure, and consolidation is named as an active threat both on the retail side (purchasing advantages) and the payor side (reimbursement leverage).

The trajectory reads stable-to-deteriorating rather than improving. Traffic has declined for two consecutive reported periods. Brand concentration in America's Best increased. The Fred Meyer host channel shrank from 29 stores to 18, two closures because the host ceased operations at those locations. The demographic repositioning — higher price points, premium product including Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses, a rebrand under "Every Eye Deserves Better," a consultative selling model — moves the company toward better-capitalized, better-branded competitors while risking the value proposition that generated its traffic in the first place. Management also concedes in the risk factors that e-commerce growth may come partly by attracting existing store customers, "thereby detracting from the financial performance of our stores," which is a candid admission given that the Q2 stumble is a digital-channel failure.

## Risk Assessment

**The August 12 print is the dominant near-term risk and its magnitude is unmeasured.** Management disclosed a self-inflicted traffic disruption on the brand producing 87.5% of revenue, characterized it only as showing unquantified "sequential improvement," and warned impacts could continue. This lands on a business already reporting declining traffic. The adverse reading is specific and plausible: a re-platform that destroys accumulated SEO and social-referral equity can permanently reset the top of the funnel on a value brand where customer acquisition is marketing-dependent. Consensus has cut the quarter 12.9% but no one outside the company knows the number.

**Optometrist cost inflation caps gross margin structurally.** The 10-K states recruiting has become harder, demand exceeds supply in certain markets, wage pressure continued through 2025 and is expected to continue in 2026, and costs may increase further, potentially materially. FY2025 costs of services and plans deteriorated 170bps to 93.6% of services revenue for exactly this reason. The mitigation — remote medicine — is explicitly higher-cost per exam and gated state by state, with some states prohibiting optometrist telehealth entirely.

**Managed-care concentration with a stated growth cliff.** 42% of revenue, a small number of payors comprising the majority, payors setting terms unilaterally, some payors owning competing retail networks, and management guiding that growth will slow. The cohort driving current comps is the one flagged to decelerate.

**Regulatory and structural.** State authorities may find contractual relationships with the 27 consolidated professional corporations violate corporate-practice-of-optometry rules, requiring restructuring. State regulators or optometry boards may challenge the bundled offer or the "free exam" claim. FirstSight's California HMO license is essential to America's Best in that state and requires DMHC approval for material changes.

**Tariffs, unresolved at the cutoff.** The Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that the IEEPA tariffs exceeded presidential authority; the Administration replaced them with a 10% Section 122 tariff effective February 24, 2026 that expires July 24, 2026 unless extended, under court challenge. Company-quantified exposure is modest — under 10% of costs applicable to revenue directly subject to China tariffs, under 1% to Mexico — but the post-July-24 regime is outside the evidence set, and the company states it is uncertain whether or when duty refunds would arrive, so no receivable should be assumed.

**Impairment tail and refinancing.** $941.5M of goodwill and indefinite-lived trademarks, 46.6% of assets, at a company that has already written down Eyeglass World goodwill and the Fred Meyer intangible. $211.1M of Term Loan A due 2028 within the investment horizon, manageable at 0.9x net leverage but a real event.

**Short interest cuts both ways.** 29.3% of float short with 7.5 days to cover. A clean Q2 print with quantified remediation could force a violent covering rally; a miss confirms the short thesis and the exit is crowded. This raises realized volatility around the catalyst in both directions rather than establishing a direction.

Resolved risk: the Delaware Chancery derivative action was dismissed February 11, 2026 with no appeal filed by the March 13 deadline; the matter is closed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing the August 12 earnings as a discrete stress event and nothing beyond it. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 124.9% against 51.4% one-year realized — a 73.5-point spread at the 89th percentile of the current chain — with a pronounced backwardation (124.9% at one month, 93.0% at three, 64.2% at six). The nearest expiry implies a ±20.7% move. Put/call skew of 17.1% at one month and 14.8% at three indicates elevated put demand, flattening to 4.9% at six months; the market is buying protection through the print, not for the year.

Two signals conflict with the put demand and deserve flagging. Put/call open interest is 0.11, call-dominated, having collapsed from 6.91 at the June 18 snapshot while the price rose 38.2%. And there is one conspicuous block: 10,025 October $25 calls traded against open interest of 194, a 51.7x volume-to-open-interest ratio at 58.5% implied volatility, 7.8% out of the money. Positioning has flipped from heavily protective to call-tilted over the recovery, which reduces the residual fuel for a squeeze and means the marginal buyer is now paying for upside rather than hedging downside. The combination of rich near-term vol, elevated near-term put skew and call-heavy standing open interest describes a market with no consensus on the direction of the August 12 result, consistent with the fundamental read: the decisive datapoint does not yet exist.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$30–32, +30% to +38%)

Q2 confirms that the re-platform damage was transient and largely remediated by quarter-end, adjusted comps stay positive, and the SG&A leverage demonstrated in Q1 (payroll −220bps, advertising −70bps) proves structural rather than timing. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $0.96 consensus and FY2027 at $1.18 or better; the market applies 25x to next-year earnings, giving roughly $29.50, with the consensus median of $30.00 and the $39 high target defining the optimistic tail. Supporting mechanics: net leverage under 1x with $293M of revolver capacity, FY2026 free cash flow in the $90–130M range against a $1,855M market cap, the AAFES concession extended to January 2036, and a fully unused $50M buyback authorization running to December 2030 that could be deployed. The consumer backdrop cooperates if managed-care penetration continues to insulate the top line from cash-pay softness. Estimate revisions turn positive as consensus catches up to the reduced Q2 bar being cleared.

### Bear Case (~$15.50, −33%)

The re-platform proves to have permanently reset the digital funnel on a brand that was already losing traffic, and Q2 shows negative comps. Consensus cuts FY2026 adjusted EPS below $0.85 and FY2027 toward $1.00; at 16x that is $16, near the $15.52 June print and within reach of the $14.75 52-week low. The mechanism that makes this a genuine bear case rather than a scare: the company has been holding comps up with price in the value segment for two consecutive periods while traffic falls, so a demand shock arrives on an already-thinning funnel with no pricing lever left. Optometrist wage inflation continues to push costs of services and plans back above 93%, managed-care growth decelerates as management has guided, and a soft Q4 (seasonally the weakest quarter) confirms the deceleration. An Eyeglass World goodwill impairment — a banner already written down once, with the weakest comp record — would add a GAAP shock. Consumer-discretionary multiple compression supplies the multiple contraction.

### What resolves it

Q2 results on August 12, specifically: the traffic-versus-ticket decomposition of comps, whether management quantifies re-platform recovery, and whether any FY2026 framing is provided. Management has issued capex guidance ($73–78M) and store guidance (30–35 plus the 20 military additions) but no revenue, comp or EPS guidance in the evidence set, so the print is unusually information-dense.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** August 12, 2026 (Q2 FY2026 results, 8:30 a.m. call), seven days after this report's information cutoff. This is the natural and unavoidable reassessment point; the options market prices a ±20.7% move around it.

**Base-case reference value: ~$25 (+8%).** 21x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.18, which is also approximately 26x FY2026's $0.96 — reflecting a business compounding adjusted EPS in the high teens with a 4–6% free-cash-flow yield, offset by declining traffic and a maturing managed-care mix. Base $25, bull $30–32, bear $15.50.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 revenue at or above roughly $520M with **positive** adjusted comps and a management statement quantifying re-platform traffic recovery, alongside adjusted EPS of $0.18 or better; followed by the 30-day estimate-revision trend turning positive across current-year and next-year lines.
2. Comp decomposition showing a non-negative traffic contribution for two consecutive quarters — the single datapoint that would resolve the price-versus-demand ambiguity in the company's favor.
3. A price back toward $18–19 with fundamentals intact, which would put the shares at roughly 19x FY2026 and 16x FY2027 consensus and restore the asymmetry that existed in June.
4. Actual deployment of the $50M authorization (unused at April 4, 2026, following a prior $50M authorization that expired unused on January 3, 2026), which would convert stated capital-allocation intent into evidence.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 adjusted comps negative, or revenue below roughly $500M, with management indicating the re-platform impact extends into Q3.
2. FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS cut below approximately $0.85, or FY2027 below $1.05.
3. Costs of services and plans reverting above 93% of services revenue, signalling that Q1's 210bp improvement was timing rather than structural.
4. A goodwill or trade-name impairment at Eyeglass World, or disclosure that a material managed-care payor has excluded stores from its network.
5. Any covenant commentary or a downward revision to the FY2026 store-opening plan, which would indicate the transformation capex is not self-funding.

## Score Trajectory

Prior reports scored EYE 4.3 (April 26), 4.8 (May 12) and 5.5 (June 18), moving from AVOID to AVOID to HOLD, with valuation, management quality and financial health all improving. This report holds the recommendation at HOLD but marks the overall score modestly lower at 5.05, and the reason is specific rather than reflexive.

Nothing in the operating record has deteriorated since June 18 — Q1 remains the last reported quarter, the balance sheet is unchanged, and the AAFES expansion is intact. Two things did change. First, the price rose 38.2% from $16.78 to $23.15, taking the multiple on FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS from roughly 16x to 24.1x. A higher price on unchanged fundamentals is worse risk/reward, which is a valuation fact rather than a thesis reversal, and it is why the valuation component comes down. Second, the estimate-revision picture is now visible and it is uniformly negative — the current-quarter estimate has been cut 12.9% over 90 days and next-year 5.7% — which downgrades the forward-growth read. Financial health nudges up on the confirmed 0.91x net leverage, undrawn revolver and closed derivative litigation. Management quality comes down marginally: the June director purchase at $15.52 was already in the record at the last review, while the re-platform is a self-inflicted execution failure, the CEO sold $928,900 at $26.54 in March (not under a plan adopted that quarter), and two consecutive buyback authorizations went unused across a 47% drawdown.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** National Vision has done the operational work: adjusted operating margin up 160bps in FY2025, 300bps of SG&A leverage in Q1 FY2026, the convertible notes retired, net leverage under 1x, and a 10-year AAFES concession secured. Those are not accounting effects, even though roughly 55% of the headline GAAP swing was the non-recurrence of impairment.

What the company has not yet demonstrated is that its comparable-store growth survives without price increases in the value segment, and it has disclosed a self-inflicted disruption to the digital funnel of the brand that produces 87.5% of revenue without quantifying it. Consensus has cut every forward period. The stock has recovered 49% from where a director committed $776,000 of his own money on June 5, leaving it at 24.1x current-year and 19.6x next-year adjusted consensus, a premium to a specialty-store peer group at 12.9x, with a 3.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield and no dividend. The August 12 print carries a ±20.7% implied move, 29.3% of the float is short with 7.5 days to cover, and near-term implied volatility at 124.9% against 51.4% realized makes any position taken now an expensive way to express a view on a number nobody outside the company has.

Existing holders have a business that is improving with a balance sheet that can absorb a bad quarter. New capital should wait for the traffic decomposition on August 12, or for a price back toward $18–19 where the growth on offer is once again available at a discount to it.
