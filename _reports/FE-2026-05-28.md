**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

FirstEnergy is a fully-regulated electric utility serving 6 million customers across six states, currently executing a $36 billion capital plan ("Energize365") heavily weighted toward transmission investment in the PJM region. The company's growth thesis centers on surging data center electricity demand, with PJM-awarded transmission projects (Valley Link ~$1B, Grid Growth ~$448M FET share) providing visible earnings growth through FERC formula rates. Moody's revised FE's outlook to positive in March 2026, and TD Cowen upgraded the stock to Buy in May 2026 citing data center tailwinds.

The offsetting concerns are substantial. FE is generating negative free cash flow (-$1B annually), requires continuous debt market access, trades near the $47.78 conversion price on $2.5B of convertible notes (creating dilution overhang), and faces an unquantifiable securities class action related to the HB 6 bribery scandal. Ohio — its largest service territory — remains a difficult regulatory jurisdiction with a 9.63% authorized ROE, $275M customer restitution payments, and 25-year storm cost amortization. At 25.4x trailing earnings with a 4% dividend yield, the stock is priced in-line with peers but without a margin of safety for execution risk on a plan this large.

The stock has recovered from a $39.27 low in mid-June 2025 to $46.45 currently, an 18% move driven by the data center narrative and Moody's upgrade. The growth thesis is real but adequately reflected in the current multiple. This is a hold with a positive directional lean, awaiting either a valuation pullback or a specific catalyst (Ohio rate case resolution, additional PJM transmission awards) to upgrade.

## Company Overview

FirstEnergy operates through three segments: Distribution (Ohio/Pennsylvania, ~4.3M customers), Integrated (NJ/WV/MD, ~2M customers including 3,610 MW regulated generation), and Stand-Alone Transmission (24,000+ miles, 49.9% owned by Brookfield via the FET partnership). Revenue reached $15.1B in 2025, up 12% year-over-year.

The company repositioned itself as a pure regulated T&D utility after exiting merchant generation. Its transmission assets in the PJM interconnection — where data center load growth is most acute — represent the primary growth engine. Brookfield's 49.9% FET stake (consolidated as a VIE) partially monetized the transmission portfolio while maintaining operational control.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**
- Revenue: $12.5B (2022) → $12.9B (2023) → $13.5B (2024) → $15.1B (2025). Q1 2026 revenue of $4.2B grew 12% YoY.
- Q1 2026 EPS: $0.70, up 13% from $0.62 in Q1 2025. Full-year 2025 net income was $1.02B.
- Operating income in 2025 was $2.21B, down slightly from $2.38B in 2024, impacted by a $352M Ohio impairment charge from rate case disallowances.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total assets: $56.9B. Long-term debt: $26.3B (up from $22.5B in 2024 as capex accelerates).
- Debt/equity: ~2.1x. Consolidated interest coverage: 4.3x (covenant minimum 2.5x).
- Goodwill of $5.62B is stable. No impairment risk flagged.
- $2.5B in convertible notes (2029/2031 maturities) at a $47.78 conversion price — the stock at $46.45 is 3% below strike.

**Cash Flow:**
- 2025 operating cash flow: $3.70B. Capex: $4.71B. Free cash flow: -$1.0B.
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow collapsed to $148M (from $637M) due to $163M Ohio restitution payments, storm costs, and timing. Capex was $1.26B in Q1 alone.
- The Energize365 plan allocates $36B over 2026-2030, funded via organic cash, debt (including hybrid securities), and ~$270M/year equity issuance (~1% annual dilution).

**Dividend:**
- $1.86 annualized ($0.465/quarter), up 4.5% YoY. Yield: 4.0%. Payout ratio ~65-70% of guided EPS ($2.73 for 2026).

The financial profile is typical for a high-growth regulated utility: strong revenue growth but negative FCF, increasing leverage, and reliance on capital markets. The 4.3x interest coverage provides adequate but not exceptional headroom.

## Growth Analysis

**Transmission is the growth engine.** The Energize365 plan allocates 35% of $36B to stand-alone transmission, where FERC formula rates provide real-time recovery on invested capital. Specific awarded projects include:

- Valley Link JV (with Dominion/Transource): ~$3B total, FET share ~$1B
- Grid Growth JV (with Transource, Feb 2026): ~$1B PJM project, FET share ~$448M
- Additional supplemental projects and the forthcoming 2026 PJM Open Window

**Data center demand is the structural tailwind.** PJM's backstop reliability procurement of up to 14.8 GW of new resources (April 2026) confirms the resource adequacy challenge. FE's lower-than-peer retail rates position it favorably for load interconnections.

**Earnings growth guidance:** Consensus estimates show $2.73 for 2026 and $2.95 for 2027, implying ~8% EPS growth — consistent with management's 6-8% long-term growth rate target. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current quarter +6.1% over 90 days) but the full-year estimate is essentially flat (+0.1% over 90 days).

**Risks to growth:** The FERC Large Load Interconnection Rulemaking (action expected June 2026) could require data centers to pay directly for network upgrades or build their own transmission. If this shifts investment away from utilities, FE's transmission growth runway narrows. The proposed $2.7B West Virginia gas plant represents re-entry into generation construction — a different risk profile than pure wires investment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.45, FE trades at 25.4x trailing EPS ($1.76 — note this reflects trailing twelve months which include depressed quarters) and approximately 17.0x forward 2026 EPS ($2.73). The forward P/E is more relevant given the earnings ramp.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median $53.00, range $48-$56. Current price represents 14% upside to median. However, the low target ($48) is only 3% above current price — limited downside protection from the sell-side.

**Assessment:** At 17x forward earnings with 8% growth, FE is not cheap (PEG ~2.1x) but not egregiously expensive for a utility with visible regulated growth. The execution risk on $36B of capital deployment is not reflected in a discount. The stock has re-rated from ~$39 to ~$46 on the data center narrative, and the current price appears to reflect fair-value equilibrium rather than an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Extensive PJM transmission footprint positions FE as a direct beneficiary of data center interconnection
- Lower-than-peer retail rates attract load growth
- Brookfield partnership provides financial flexibility on transmission projects
- FERC formula rates on transmission provide real-time capex recovery — no regulatory lag

**Challenges:**
- Ohio authorized ROE of 9.63% is below sector average, reflecting HB 6 penalty
- JCP&L reliability failures resulted in a recommended $44M penalty from NJBPU
- Offshore wind project terminations in NJ remove a potential growth driver
- West Virginia coal plant lifetimes (2035/2040) create decommissioning liability and replacement cost

**Trajectory:** FE's competitive position is improving. The transmission growth pipeline is expanding, the data center demand backdrop is structural, and Moody's positive outlook revision reflects restored credibility. The company is transitioning from remediation to growth mode.

## Risk Assessment

**Quantified risks:**
1. **Securities class action (unquantifiable):** Management acknowledges a "probable" loss but cannot estimate the amount. The Sixth Circuit vacated class certification in August 2025, which is favorable, but the case is not dismissed.
2. **Convertible dilution:** $2.5B at $47.78 strike. If FE appreciates to $50+, dilution becomes meaningful. The $300M 2026 notes matured May 1, 2026, with a conversion price of $46.37, creating near-term financing activity.
3. **Negative FCF and market access:** At -$1B/year FCF, FE needs uninterrupted debt market access.
4. **FERC Large Load Rulemaking (June 2026):** Could disintermediate utilities from data center transmission investment.
5. **Ohio rate case (filing May 2026):** Initial indications suggest modest allowed increases — the $34M net increase and $14M DCR cap increase from prior proceedings were disappointing.

**Mitigating factors:**
- Moody's positive outlook suggests credit trajectory is improving
- DPA obligations nearly complete; RICO indictments target former officers, not the company
- 4.3x interest coverage provides buffer
- Transmission formula rates reduce regulatory lag risk

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($53-56, +14% to +21%):**
- Transmission investment pipeline exceeds plan as PJM awards accelerate
- Ohio rate case delivers a constructive outcome above $34M net increase
- FERC rulemaking preserves utility role in data center interconnection
- Moody's upgrades FE to full investment-grade ratings, lowering borrowing costs
- Data center load materializes faster than PJM forecasts, driving supplemental transmission projects

**Bear Case ($40-42, -10% to -14%):**
- FERC rulemaking allows data centers to self-build transmission, reducing FE's addressable investment
- Securities class action settles at a material amount (>$500M)
- Rising rates make the $36B capex plan uneconomic as cost of capital exceeds allowed returns
- Ohio regulatory environment remains punitive, dragging on earnings growth
- Convertible dilution caps share price near $47.78 strike for extended period

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $42-43 (forward P/E compresses to ~15.5x) on broader market weakness without fundamental deterioration — this would create a more attractive entry with ~4.5% yield support
2. Ohio rate case outcome exceeds $100M net revenue increase (expected resolution Q4 2026)
3. FERC Large Load Rulemaking (June 2026) explicitly preserves utility transmission role

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Securities class action settlement announced at >$750M (material to equity value)
2. Credit downgrade from any major agency (Moody's/S&P/Fitch) on execution concerns
3. FERC rulemaking materially shifts data center transmission investment away from utilities
4. Two consecutive quarters of earnings misses by >5%

**Reassessment timeline:** June-July 2026 (FERC rulemaking outcome + Ohio rate case filing details + Q2 earnings in late July).

## Conclusion

FirstEnergy's transformation story is credible and progressing. The transmission growth pipeline driven by data center demand provides a visible 6-8% EPS growth path, and Moody's positive outlook confirms improving financial health. However, at $46.45 (17x forward, 4% yield), the stock has already re-rated significantly from its June 2025 lows and trades at a modest premium to peers without adequate compensation for execution risk on $36B of capital deployment, unquantifiable litigation exposure, and convertible dilution overhang.

The score trajectory shows improvement (5.5 → 5.7 → current 5.65), with growth prospects justifying continued attention. The directional thesis is positive, but entry point matters for a utility with this risk profile. HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, awaiting either a valuation pullback or resolution of the June 2026 FERC rulemaking to upgrade.
