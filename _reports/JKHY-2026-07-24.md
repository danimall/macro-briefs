**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jack Henry is a 50-year-old core-banking and payments software company serving ~7,400 U.S. community and regional banks and credit unions. It is one of the highest-quality franchises in financial technology: ~25% free-cash-flow margins, zero year-end debt, 22 consecutive years of dividend increases, and organic revenue growth that has accelerated to 7-8%. Over the trailing twelve months the stock fell roughly 18% (from ~$180 to ~$148), touched a 52-week low of $121, and has since partially recovered. That decline reflects multiple compression rather than deteriorating fundamentals. The company is executing its best operating stretch in years while the market prices in a long-dated fear that AI-native entrants will erode the switching-cost moat that protects legacy core providers.

A quality compounder has been de-rated on a hard-to-disprove narrative. Every measurable fundamental is intact or improving: Q3 FY26 revenue grew 8.7%, nine-month EPS rose 20.4% to $5.41, margins expanded across all four segments, and consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimates have been revised up over the past 90 days. The company has beaten estimates in each of the last four quarters (average beat ~13.6%). Consensus targets run $158 to $208 (median $186.50), placing the current price below the entire analyst range. Management is buying: the CEO and CFO both made open-market purchases near the lows in May 2026, and the board expanded the buyback authorization by 5 million shares while accelerating repurchases roughly eightfold year-over-year.

We rate JKHY a BUY. The central risk is real but slow-moving: AI could, over a multi-year horizon, lower the barriers that make core-banking systems sticky, and the first place that would show up is renewal price compression, which management has already flagged. That risk merits a valuation discount but does not justify pricing the stock as if the compounding is broken. At ~20.9x forward EPS on rising estimates, with a strong balance sheet and a compounding return-of-capital program, the risk/reward is skewed to the upside. Base case $180 over 12-18 months; bull $205; bear $120.

## Company Overview

Jack Henry (founded 1976, Monett, Missouri) provides core processing, payments, and complementary software almost exclusively to U.S. community and regional financial institutions. It serves ~1,670 core clients and ~5,710 non-core clients with ~7,240-7,300 associates. Fiscal year ends June 30.

Revenue splits into two streams and four reportable segments. Services & Support (~57% of revenue) includes private/public cloud hosting on ~six-year contracts, product delivery and licenses, and on-premise maintenance. Processing (~43%) covers card, payments/remittance (ACH, RTP, FedNow, Zelle), and digital transaction fees. By segment: Core (~30%), Payments (~37%), Complementary (~30%), and a small Corporate Services catch-all.

The moat is structural. Core banking systems are mission-critical, deeply embedded, and expensive and risky to replace ("deconversion"). Contracts run ~six years, retention is very high, and the company's stated differentiator is service quality measured through continuous NPS-style surveys. Its core platforms are SilverLake (banks $1B-$55B+ in assets; 520 banks, ~12% share of sub-$55B banks), CIF 20/20, Core Director, and Symitar/Episys for credit unions (~715 CUs, the #1 CU core above $25M in assets). Primary competitors are FIS, Fiserv, Corelation, and Finastra.

The strategic centerpiece is the Jack Henry Platform, a public-cloud-native, API-first modernization effort intended to become a modular alternative to legacy core functions (deposits, GL, wires, data hub). This is the multi-year growth and defensibility bet, and it is also the company's direct answer to the AI/fintech-entrant threat that the market is currently discounting.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash generation anchor this thesis. An Altman Z-Score of 3.83 places the company firmly in the safe zone. FY2025 delivered ROE of 21.4% and ROA of 15%, on an FCF margin of ~25%.

Revenue growth is durable and overwhelmingly organic:
- FY2023 $2,078M → FY2024 $2,216M (+6.6%) → FY2025 $2,375M (+7.2%; +6.5% ex-deconversion)
- FY2026 nine-month revenue $1,900M, +8.0% (+7.6% ex one-time items)

Acquisitions add less than one point of growth. The drivers are cloud migration, card, digital, and faster payments — recurring, transaction-linked revenue rather than lumpy license sales.

Earnings are compounding faster than revenue on genuine operating leverage. FY2025 operating income was $569M (23.9% margin) versus FY2024 $489M (22.1%), helped by the absence of prior-year severance and declining cost of revenue as a percentage of sales. FY2025 net income reached $456M (+19.4%), diluted EPS $6.24 (+19.3%). In Q3 FY26, net income was $122.9M (+10.6%), EPS $1.71. Cost of revenue declined as a share of revenue in every segment, confirming the leverage is broad-based rather than a one-time mix effect.

Cash flow and balance sheet are pristine. FY2025 operating cash flow was $642M and FCF $588M, with capitalized software (~$172M) the largest investing item — appropriate for a software compounder. The company ended FY2025 with zero debt and had drawn only $90M on its revolver by Q3 FY26, largely to fund accelerated buybacks. Equity/assets is 70% and D/E only 0.43; interest coverage is effectively unlimited. Reported balance-sheet cash swings (e.g., $102M at 6/30/25 to $21M at 3/31/26) are misleading in isolation: JKHY deliberately runs on a thin cash balance and sweeps FCF into dividends, buybacks, and capex. This is capital-return discipline, not distress.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly. The dividend has risen for 22 consecutive years, with the quarterly payout raised to $0.61 in FY26 against a low ~35% payout ratio. Buybacks accelerated sharply: $284M repurchased in nine months of FY26 versus $35M in the prior-year period, and on May 8, 2026 the board added 5 million shares to the authorization. A new five-year $1.0B unsecured revolver signed in March 2026 (replacing a $600M facility) expands optionality. The OBBBA tax law enacted July 2025 restores immediate R&D expensing and 100% bonus depreciation, providing a modest cash-tax tailwind.

The one genuine fundamental risk visible in the financials is renewal price compression. With ~six-year contracts, a meaningful tranche renews annually, and the FY2025 10-K explicitly states that certain renewals "have resulted in price compression" and that JKHY may be unable to fully pass through inflation because vendor contracts are shorter than client contracts. This is the leading indicator to monitor, discussed further in Risk Assessment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is mid-single-digit-plus organic revenue with margin expansion driving high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS growth. The near-term durability of that algorithm is well supported.

The beat/miss track record is strong and consistent: four straight beats averaging ~13.6% surprise (14.9%, 17.8%, 12.9%, 8.7% over the last four quarters). This gives high confidence in current consensus.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive on the horizons that matter. Current-year EPS was revised to $6.57 from $6.44 ninety days ago (+1.9%), and next-year to $7.10 from $6.99 (+1.5%). The current-quarter estimate slipped 5.3% over 90 days, but this reflects normal quarter-to-quarter timing rather than an annual trend — the full-year figures, which smooth timing, are rising. This is the steady positive drift consistent with a quality compounder whose estimates the market gradually catches up to, not the +30% revision velocity that flags a violent re-rating.

The growth is structurally underpinned. Cloud migration continues to shift clients off-premise into stickier six-year hosting contracts. Card, digital, and faster-payments (FedNow, RTP, Zelle) volumes are secular tailwinds. Management reiterated a "very healthy sales pipeline" with particular strength among larger institutions in Q3 FY26. The Jack Henry Platform modernization effort, if it lands, both defends the base and opens modular upsell.

The offsetting headwind is client consolidation: the bank/CU customer base is shrinking ~3% per year through M&A (banks down 13% and CUs down 15% over 2019-2024). JKHY has more than offset this by growing net footprints and rising assets-under-management per client, but it caps unit-count growth and makes revenue-per-client expansion the load-bearing lever.

## Valuation Assessment

At $148.23, the stock trades at ~23.8x trailing FY25 EPS of $6.24 and ~20.9x forward on the $7.10 next-year consensus (or ~22.6x current-year $6.57). PEG on the ~10-12% forward EPS growth rate sits around 1.7-2.0 — a modest premium to market, appropriate for a debt-free, 25%-FCF-margin, high-retention recurring-revenue franchise, and well below the mid-20s multiple the stock carried before the AI-driven de-rating.

The peer table is heterogeneous and requires care. Median peer P/E is 19.7x, but the comparables (WEX 15.3x, Euronet 9.7x, NCR Atleos 19.7x, Sezzle 30.8x, Shift4 44.0x) are structurally different businesses — merchant acquirers, BNPL, ATM networks — with lower recurring-revenue quality, higher cyclicality, and (for several) meaningful leverage. JKHY's near-zero net debt, mission-critical stickiness, and dividend record justify a premium to the low-multiple names and are not comparable to the high-multiple growth-payments names. The more instructive benchmark is JKHY's own history: the stock has compressed from the mid-20s to ~21x forward on a narrative rather than a numbers change.

The clearest valuation signal is that the current price sits below the entire analyst target range: low $158, mean $185.71, median $186.50, high $208. Even the most bearish published target implies ~7% upside; the median implies ~26%. On a stock with positive estimate revisions and a forward P/E near its growth rate (not a premium 50x+ multiple where targets simply extrapolate the price), consensus targets function as a floor that lags reality, not a ceiling. RBC trimmed its target to $173 in June but kept an Outperform rating — a de-rating of the multiple, not the business.

We treat the "cheap" framing with appropriate caution. A low headline multiple is not, by itself, a buy signal, and the AI overhang is precisely the kind of structural fear that can turn a modest discount into a value trap if the moat genuinely erodes. The valuation score reflects a specific mispricing thesis — the market has applied a moat-erosion discount to a franchise whose renewal and organic-growth data show no erosion yet — rather than reflexive reward for a compressed multiple. We assign a 7.0.

## Competitive Landscape

JKHY operates in an oligopolistic core-banking market alongside FIS, Fiserv, Corelation, and Finastra. Its differentiation is service quality and a deliberate focus on community and regional institutions, a segment the mega-vendors serve with less attention. Switching costs are among the highest in software: replacing a core is a multi-year, high-risk deconversion, which sustains ~six-year contracts and very high retention.

The competitive trajectory is a genuine debate. Level is high (a market leader with a defensible niche), but the market is worried the trajectory is at risk. Two forces work in opposite directions. On the negative side, structural client consolidation shrinks the unit base ~3% per year, and AI-native entrants could, over a multi-year horizon, lower the technical barriers that make core replacement painful. On the positive side, JKHY has been a net share gainer (footprints and average client assets rose in both banks and CUs in 2024), technology spending by surviving, larger institutions remains strong, and the company is itself embedding AI (Payrailz, fraud/risk ML, the Victor embedded-payments acquisition) rather than standing still. The Jack Henry Platform is a credible cloud-native modernization answer.

We score competitive position 6.5, down modestly from prior reports. The downgrade reflects the AI threat, which, while unproven, raises the tail risk to a moat that was previously treated as near-inviolable, with renewal price compression as the concrete early tell. It is not a call that share is currently eroding; the observable data show the opposite.

## Risk Assessment

**AI disruption of the core-banking model.** This is the dominant market concern and the reason for the de-rating. The FY2025 10-K added an expanded generative-AI risk factor addressing both product risk and the implicit threat that AI-native entrants could lower the switching-cost barriers protecting incumbents. The risk is real but slow-moving and hard to disprove — core replacement remains a multi-year, high-risk undertaking regardless of tooling, and there is no evidence of accelerated deconversion in the data.

**Renewal price compression.** The more concrete near-term fundamental risk. The 10-K explicitly warns certain renewals have already produced price compression, and that shorter vendor contracts limit inflation pass-through. If AI does erode the moat, it should appear here first — as tougher renewal pricing — before any wholesale displacement. This is the single most important thread to monitor in future filings and calls.

**Client consolidation.** The ~3%/year shrinkage of the bank/CU base is a persistent unit-count headwind, offset so far by revenue-per-client growth.

**Vendor concentration and long-dated commitments.** JKHY depends on a single strategic card-processing platform (agreement extended to January 2038, ~$1.02B remaining) and signed a $450M cloud-services commitment through 2033. These lock in scale economics and signal management conviction, but they are large, long, and non-cancelable, concentrating dependence on key vendors.

**Other.** Leadership/talent concentration (no executive employment agreements, a small management group, hybrid workforce). Regulatory examination by proxy under the Bank Service Company Act. An IRS audit of the FY2023 return opened in Q1 FY26 with ~$25.6M of gross unrecognized tax benefits — ongoing but not flagged as material. There are no material legal proceedings, no restatements, and internal controls are effective.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning shows near-term caution without a directional bearish tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 36.5% versus 27.0% one-year historical vol — rich by ~9.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (36.5% near-term vs. 31.6% at six months), indicating the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the upcoming fiscal Q4/FY26 earnings report. Put/call skew is essentially balanced at the front (0.2%) but turns modestly negative at six months (-4.2%), consistent with some longer-dated downside hedging tied to the AI overhang. The put/call OI ratio has fallen from 1.52 to 0.90 as the stock rallied ~22% off the June lows — bearish positioning has unwound as price recovered. Short interest is low at 6.6% of float and 2.3 days to cover, so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing (post-earnings IV crush) rather than any change to the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($205)

The AI fear proves overblown on the timeframe that matters to a 12-24 month position. Renewal pricing holds, organic growth stays in the 7-8% range, and margins continue to expand on operating leverage, driving EPS toward $7.10+ and beyond. Consensus estimates keep rising, the multiple re-rates back toward the mid-20s as the narrative fatigues, and the accelerated buyback compounds per-share value. A $205 target implies ~29x the $7.10 next-year estimate or ~26x a normalized $7.90 FY27 figure — a return to the multiple the market awarded before the de-rating, supported by insider buying and the below-range analyst targets.

### Bear Case ($120)

The AI-disruption narrative gains empirical support: renewal price compression accelerates, a large client or two migrates to an AI-native alternative, and organic growth decelerates toward low-single-digits while margins stall. The market extends the moat-erosion discount, and the multiple compresses toward ~17-18x on flatter estimates. This retraces the stock to its 52-week low area (~$120), a ~19% loss from current levels. The balance sheet and dividend limit further downside, but the compounding thesis breaks and the stock becomes a slow-declining value trap.

The asymmetry favors the long: ~21% to the base case, ~38% to the bull, against ~19% to the bear, with a strong balance sheet and rising return-of-capital cushioning the downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $180** over 12-18 months. Reasoning: ~25.4x the $7.10 next-year consensus, in line with JKHY's pre-de-rating multiple and supported by continued mid-single-digit organic growth plus margin expansion. This sits within the analyst range and between the bear and bull scenarios. Upside to base: ~21%.

**Bull case target: $205** (~38% upside) — multiple re-rates to the mid-20s on normalized FY27 EPS as the AI narrative fades.

**Bear case target: $120** (~19% downside) — renewal compression and organic deceleration validate the moat-erosion fear.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, keyed to the fiscal Q4/FY26 print (August 2026, first natural catalyst) and successive quarters that either confirm or refute renewal-pricing durability.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic revenue growth decelerates below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters (signals the growth algorithm is breaking).
2. Management discloses accelerating renewal price compression or a net decline in core client footprints (the concrete AI-erosion tell).
3. Operating margin contracts YoY for two consecutive quarters, reversing the operating-leverage story.

## Conclusion

Jack Henry is a debt-free, ~25%-FCF-margin core-banking compounder growing organic revenue 7-8% with margin expansion across every segment, and it has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters. The stock de-rated ~18% over the past year on an AI-disruption narrative that has not yet appeared in any fundamental metric — revenue is accelerating, margins are widening, and estimates are rising. Management responded with open-market insider purchases near the lows and an 8x acceleration in buybacks, while the board expanded the authorization. The stock trades below the entire published analyst target range.

The AI threat is legitimate and warrants a valuation discount versus the stock's peak multiple, and the honest early-warning indicator is renewal price compression, which we will monitor closely. But the market is pricing a slow, unproven, multi-year fear as though the compounding is already breaking. At ~20.9x forward on rising estimates, with a strong balance sheet and a compounding return-of-capital program, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside. We rate JKHY a **BUY** with a $180 base-case target over 12-18 months, and would use the rich near-term implied volatility to scale entry around the upcoming earnings print. This is a Financials-sector name, where selection alpha is structurally scarce, so the call rests on a company-specific mispricing rather than sector beta: the below-range analyst targets, the insider-buying signal, and the intact-and-improving fundamentals supply that catalyst.
