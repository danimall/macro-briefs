**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On June 1, 2026, People Inc. (Barry Diller's rebranded IAC, which already holds roughly 26% of the shares) offered $48.30 per share to acquire the remainder, valuing the company at approximately $18 billion of enterprise value. At the current price of $47.32, the stock trades about 2% below the offer. That spread is the most important data point in this report: the market views the transaction as plausible but materially uncertain. The dominant variable for the next two-to-four quarters is whether gaming regulators across Nevada, Macau, and multiple US states will clear a media-company controller of MGM's casino licenses, a suitability review that has no clean precedent and a high bar.

The arbitrage math is unfavorable on its own. Upside to the offer is roughly 2%; if the deal breaks, the stock likely retraces toward the low-$40s or high-$30s where it traded before the bid and where insiders transacted in early 2026, an implied loss of 10-18%. The spread has widened since the announcement: the stock printed $50.69 on June 1, pricing a topping bid or fast close, and has since drifted to $47.32, which is the market dialing back deal confidence rather than a benign pullback on unchanged fundamentals.

Underneath the deal sits a two-speed operating business. MGM China (+9-11%), MGM Digital (+43% in Q1), and a BetMGM joint venture that finally turned profitable are the growth engines; the Las Vegas Strip, the largest segment at roughly 48% of revenue and the primary EBITDAR driver, is flat-to-declining against an 8% drop in Las Vegas visitation in 2025. Consensus estimates are falling, not rising. We rate MGM a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the binary deal outcome and unattractive arb asymmetry preclude a BUY, while a credible bidder, real free cash flow, and a defensible asset base preclude an AVOID.

## Company Overview

MGM operates four reportable segments. **Las Vegas Strip Resorts** comprise nine marquee properties including Bellagio, Aria, The Cosmopolitan, MGM Grand, and Mandalay Bay; over half of Strip revenue is non-gaming (rooms, food and beverage, conventions, entertainment). **Regional Operations** (~21%) span Borgata, National Harbor, MGM Grand Detroit, Beau Rivage, MGM Springfield, and Empire City; MGM Northfield Park (Ohio) was sold in April 2026 for $546 million. **MGM China** (~25%, consolidated at ~56% ownership) runs MGM Macau and MGM Cotai and is the fastest-growing brick-and-mortar segment. **MGM Digital** (~4%) is the consolidated LeoVegas international online business; separately, BetMGM (US sports betting and iGaming) is a 50/50 unconsolidated joint venture with Entain, accounted for under the equity method and not included in segment revenue.

The defining structural feature is the asset-light operating-company model. MGM has sold essentially all of its domestic real estate and now leases its properties under triple-net leases, principally to VICI, plus the Bellagio REIT Venture, the Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand landlord, and The Cosmopolitan lease. This structure drives the entire interpretation of the balance sheet and the risk profile.

Growth bets include MGM Osaka (the Japan integrated resort, with a ~$2.1 billion remaining funding commitment through 2028 and an eventual stake near 43.5%), continued BetMGM scaling, and LeoVegas international expansion.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was $17.54 billion, up 2% year over year, with growth from MGM China (+11%) and MGM Digital (+19%) offsetting a 4% decline on the Las Vegas Strip. Operating income fell 33% to $1.00 billion, weighed by a $279 million goodwill impairment ($256 million at Empire City) and a further $93 million of Empire City write-downs. Net income attributable to MGM fell to $205.9 million from $746.6 million in 2024 and $1.14 billion in 2023.

The GAAP figures understate cash generation. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was roughly flat at $2.43 billion because the impairments are non-cash. Operating cash flow was $2.53 billion and free cash flow approximately $1.46 billion (an 8.3% margin). The Piotroski score of 5/5 — positive net income, positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, OCF above net income, and a current ratio of 1.23 — reflects the genuine cash engine.

The widely cited Altman Z-Score of 0.65 ("distress") is an accounting artifact and should be set aside. It is mechanically depressed because the OpCo lease structure puts roughly $25 billion of operating-lease liabilities and ~$23 billion of right-of-use assets on the balance sheet under ASC 842, against just ~$2.4 billion of equity. Actual funded debt is modest at $6.4 billion principal (of which $2.7 billion is non-recourse at MGM China), against $2.3 billion-plus of cash, no draw on the $2.3 billion revolver, and full covenant compliance.

The real leverage is the rent. MGM owes approximately $1.8 billion per year in cash rent with annual escalators, an absolute, un-abatable fixed cost senior to almost everything. Total undiscounted future operating-lease payments are $54.2 billion. The chain is straightforward: in a downturn, EBITDAR falls but rent does not, and the leases require cash collateral or letters of credit equal to six months to two years of rent if financial covenants are breached. This is the structural vulnerability that the asset-light model embeds.

Q1 2026 confirmed both the strengths and the core problem. Revenue rose 4% to $4.45 billion (MGM China +9%, Digital +43%, Regional +2%, Strip flat). Operating income fell 22% to $301 million and Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 9% to $580 million. Las Vegas Strip EBITDAR margin compressed to 34.4% from 37.3%. Roughly $30 million of that decline is optical: a $30 million drop in cyber-insurance proceeds ($6 million versus $36 million) plus a one-time $37 million self-insurance reserve build, but underlying table-games volume and RevPAR ($238 versus $242) genuinely softened. MGM's share of the BetMGM JV swung to +$7.4 million in Q1 from -$15.2 million a year earlier, and to +$59.6 million for FY2025 from -$110 million in 2024.

Capital allocation has been aggressive on buybacks — $1.2 billion in FY2025 and $1.4 billion in 2024 — cutting basic share count from 409 million in 2022 to 256 million by Q1 2026, a 37% reduction. The dividend was suspended in February 2023. Buybacks slowed sharply to $90 million in Q1 2026 (versus $489 million in Q1 2025), with $1.5 billion of authorization remaining, likely reflecting the takeover backdrop.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is genuinely bifurcated. MGM China is the standout, with Macau visitor arrivals up 15% in 2025 in a mass-market-driven, post-junket recovery; MGM has been gaining share among the six concessionaires. MGM Digital growth is driven by LeoVegas international momentum, and the BetMGM JV reached profitability after years of losses. These are real, working engines.

The drag is the Las Vegas Strip, the dominant EBITDAR contributor. Strip revenue fell against an 8% decline in Las Vegas visitation, with persistent midweek and lower-tier softness partially offset by luxury and weekend strength. Whether this is a cyclical air-pocket (higher travel costs, macro uncertainty) or structural erosion of the midweek and lower-tier customer is the central operating debate, and it is precisely the bet Diller is making.

The forward earnings picture is not improving. Consensus revisions are broadly negative: current-year EPS estimates are down 6.0% over 30 days and 0.5% over 90 days; current-year revenue is decelerating to the low single digits. Estimate-revision direction is among the more reliable forward indicators, and here it points down. The BetMGM revenue guidance cut (to $2.9-3.1 billion from $3.1-3.2 billion, though EBITDA of $300-350 million was maintained) reinforces a "profitable but slowing" digital trajectory. The earnings surprise record is also choppy — one beat, one in-line, and two misses (including a 40% miss in Q3 2025 and a 9% miss in Q1 2026) over the last four quarters — which lowers confidence in the standalone forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 67x is meaningless here because the trailing earnings base is depressed by the impairment and write-downs. The relevant standalone anchors are forward consensus EPS of $1.96 for the current year and $2.32 for next year. At $47.32, that is roughly 24x current-year and 20x next-year earnings. The casino peer median P/E is 19.9x (Wynn 30.9x, Boyd 3.8x, Churchill Downs 15.9x, Red Rock 19.9x, Caesars negative). On forward earnings, MGM is therefore trading at or slightly above the sector median, not cheap relative to its growth, particularly with the largest segment flat and estimates falling.

The deal reframes the valuation. The $48.30 offer provides a credible value anchor near current levels — a strategic owner with 26% already in hand validated the asset base — but the present price captures essentially all of that value already, leaving roughly 2% of upside. There is no asymmetric standalone upside at $47.32: the stock is not cheap on fundamentals, and the only near-term lever for appreciation above the offer is a competing or topping bid, which is speculative.

We do not award a high valuation score for two reasons. First, the low trailing multiple reflects impaired earnings, not value, and the forward multiple sits at the peer median with worse near-term growth momentum. Second, the risk/reward is skewed to the downside: the deal anchor disappears if regulators block the transaction, and the standalone fair value (forward earnings on a sector multiple, adjusted for the lease-rigidity discount) sits below the offer, likely in the low-to-mid $40s. The deal provides the catalyst but caps the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

MGM holds the premier Las Vegas Strip portfolio (Bellagio, Aria, Cosmopolitan) and a strong Macau franchise as one of six concessionaires, with MGM China actively gaining share in a recovering mass market. These are durable, hard-to-replicate assets. BetMGM is a credible top-tier US digital player behind FanDuel and DraftKings, and reaching profitability strengthens its competitive standing even if it is unlikely to take the top spot.

The trajectory, however, is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating outside Macau. Las Vegas visitation and midweek/lower-tier demand softened in 2025, pressuring the largest profit center. In the Northeast, New York issued three downstate casino licenses in 2025, a direct competitive threat to Empire City and Borgata that was the proximate cause of MGM withdrawing the Empire City commercial license application and writing off goodwill. The Macau concession runs only through December 31, 2032, with government redemption/termination rights and mandatory investment commitments, an open-ended renewal risk. Net, the moat is real but the share trajectory is mixed, consistent with the gradual step-down in our competitive-position assessment over recent quarters.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the binary deal outcome, and it is not fully priced. The gaming-license suitability review is the crux: regulators in Nevada, Macau, and multiple US states must approve a media company as controller of MGM's casino licenses, a non-trivial and arguably unprecedented review that could delay or derail the transaction. The ~2% spread and its widening since June 1 indicate the market is pricing meaningful break risk. If the deal fails, the stock likely retraces toward its pre-bid levels (insiders transacted near $34-37 in February-March 2026), a 10-18% downside.

Standalone risks compound the binary. The un-abatable annual rent against a cyclical EBITDAR base is the defining structural exposure; MGM also guarantees roughly $3.01 billion (Bellagio) plus $3.0 billion (Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand) of its landlords' debt, currently carried at immaterial fair value but a real contingent liability. The Macau concession expiry (2032), the ~$2.1 billion remaining MGM Osaka commitment with an uncapped completion guarantee, the residual cybersecurity litigation tail from the 2023 incident, and labor CBAs expiring in 2026 are all live. None is individually fatal, but together with the lease rigidity they limit downside resilience in a recession.

Short interest of 21.3% of float (4.9 days to cover) is high and cuts both ways: it reflects deal skepticism but creates squeeze potential if the transaction firms up. The level is large enough that any concrete sign of regulatory progress could force covering.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent event risk. The term structure is in backwardation — near-term ATM IV of 36.6% sits above the six-month 28.2% — which flags stress around deal resolution. Near-term IV is roughly fair versus one-year historical volatility of 38.9% (a -2.3% spread). Since the bid, ATM IV has risen 4.5 points (32.1% to 36.6%) while the stock fell 6.6%, and the put/call open-interest ratio climbed from 0.08 to 0.24, consistent with rising hedging demand as deal confidence softened. The overall skew remains call-heavy (-37.1%) and put/call OI is low at 0.24, which tempers the bearish read, but the one-month tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 7.86 and a 6.2% implied move, and unusual September put activity at the $43 strike (6.8x volume/OI) suggests positioning for a break scenario. The composite signal: the market expects a binary resolution and is paying up for downside protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The People Inc. transaction closes near $48.30, delivering ~2% to current holders plus the optionality of a topping bid in a sector seeing consolidation interest (a Caesars-related approach surfaced the same week). Diller's pre-bid open-market purchase of 1 million shares in March 2026 telegraphs conviction, and his rationale — that Las Vegas Strip softness is cyclical and MGM's assets are undervalued — is plausible given Macau's recovery and BetMGM's turn to profit. If regulators clear the deal, the 21.3% short interest could amplify the move toward the offer. Standalone, the growth engines (China, Digital, BetMGM) plus the 37% reduction in share count support per-share value even on flat EBITDA. Bull target $52-55, reflecting deal close plus modest topping-bid optionality.

**Bear Case.** Gaming regulators block or stall the acquisition of MGM's licenses by a media-company controller, the offer evaporates, and the stock reverts to its standalone fair value. With the largest segment flat-to-declining, consensus estimates falling, un-abatable rent against a cyclical cash flow base, and forward earnings already at the peer multiple, the standalone equity is worth low-$40s at best and high-$30s if Strip softness proves structural and a macro downturn hits the lease-heavy cost structure. Bear target $38-40, a 15-19% loss from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The outcome is path-dependent on a binary regulatory event with unfavorable arb asymmetry, and the standalone case offers no independent reason to add.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a signed definitive merger agreement at $48.30 or higher with a clear regulatory path articulated, which would convert this into a tighter-spread arb with squeeze tailwind; (2) a competing or topping bid that establishes a higher floor; (3) standalone evidence that Las Vegas Strip RevPAR and table-games volume have re-accelerated alongside positive estimate revisions (>+5% over 90 days), which would support value above the offer if the deal breaks.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) a regulator (Nevada, Macau, or a key state) signals suitability concerns or formal opposition to People Inc. as controller; (2) the board rejects the proposal without an alternative; (3) Q2 2026 results show Strip EBITDAR margin compression continuing on organic volume rather than insurance/reserve optics, with current-year estimates cut a further 5%+.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are any 8-K confirming a definitive agreement or regulatory filing, the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July), and gaming-commission docket updates over the coming two-to-four quarters. The options backwardation indicates the market expects resolution within months.

Holders with existing positions can reasonably hold through resolution given the credible value anchor; new capital has no asymmetric reason to enter at a ~2% spread with 15-18% downside on a break.

## Conclusion

MGM is a merger-arbitrage situation wrapped around a two-speed operating business. The People Inc. offer dominates the thesis, and at the current price the stock prices a credible-but-uncertain deal, with the spread widening since June 1 on growing doubt about regulatory clearance. The reward to the offer is thin (~2%) against a real downside (10-18%) if the deal fails, and the standalone business — flat-to-declining Las Vegas Strip, falling consensus estimates, peer-median forward multiple, and un-abatable rent against a cyclical base — does not provide an independent buy case. The genuine positives (Macau share gains, digital growth, BetMGM profitability, the share-count reduction, and a strategic owner who accumulated stock before bidding) anchor value but are already in the price.

We rate MGM a HOLD. The recommendation is uncertain rather than conviction-based: the equity's near-term return is governed by a binary regulatory event we cannot handicap with confidence, and the underlying operating trends offer no compensating asymmetric upside. Watch for a definitive agreement, a competing bid, or any regulatory signal, and reassess at the next earnings print.
