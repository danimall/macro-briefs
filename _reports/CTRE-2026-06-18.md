**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CareTrust REIT is a self-administered healthcare REIT focused on skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) and senior housing, financed primarily through triple-net leases. The investment case rests on a working accretive acquisition flywheel: the company deploys capital into sale-leasebacks and structured loans at 8.8–9.4% yields against a sub-4.7% cost of debt and an investment-grade-rated balance sheet, generating per-share FFO growth even as the share count roughly doubled from ~106M (2023) to ~224M (Q1 2026). FY2026 normalized FFO is guided to $2.00–2.04/share, a midpoint increase of about 15% over 2025, and management raised the quarterly dividend 16.4% to $0.39.

The setup today is a positive divergence between fundamentals and price. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised up +15.3% over 30 days (to $1.80 from $1.57) and +13.7% over 90 days (from $1.59), while the stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $43.08 to roughly $36.38, a ~15% drawdown driven by the broad rate-sensitive REIT selloff rather than any company-specific deterioration. Q1 2026 was a beat-and-raise quarter: normalized FFO $0.48/share (+14% YoY), 100% rent and interest collection, a guidance raise, and a Moody's investment-grade upgrade. At ~18x forward NFFO the stock now trades at a discount to where it was when the same estimates were lower.

The thesis is qualified. The growth model depends on continued access to equity at prices above acquisition cap rates; Ensign Group represents 23% of contractual rent; and the UK (Care REIT plc) and SHOP/RIDEA expansions are unproven and shift the risk profile. Those constraints argue for disciplined sizing rather than abstention. With base-case appreciation toward the low-$40s plus a 4.2% yield, and analyst targets clustered at $42–49 against a $36.38 price, the risk/reward is favorable.

## Company Overview

CareTrust was spun out of The Ensign Group in 2014 and elected REIT status that year. As of December 31, 2025 the portfolio comprised 407 properties / 37,628 beds-units across 32 U.S. states and the U.K. The business runs on three engines:

1. **Triple-net leasing** — the core. SNFs are 74% of leased revenue (76% occupancy); senior housing triple-net is 25% (86% occupancy). The tenant pays taxes, insurance, maintenance, and capex.
2. **Other real estate-related investments** — $899.3M of mortgage loans (8.8% weighted rate), mezzanine loans (12.1%), and preferred equity (11.5%), plus a $92.2M financing receivable (12.0% effective yield). Management uses this lending arm both for yield and as a proprietary funnel into future sale-leaseback acquisitions and purchase options.
3. **SHOP/RIDEA platform** — launched Q4 2025 with three Texas senior-housing communities, giving CareTrust direct operating exposure through third-party managers. A second SHOP transaction was announced in Q1 2026.

The company is lean (43 full-time employees) and scales externally through operator relationships rooted in the Ensign/Pennant heritage of its leadership team. The structural concentration to watch is the Ensign Master Leases, which represent 23% of total annualized contractual rental income (~$92.1M), with an Ensign-guaranteed Pennant lease adding another 2%. Geographically, California is 25% of revenue, the U.K. 15%, Texas 13%, and Tennessee 12%; the latter two jumped from roughly 1%/0% in 2024, evidence of how concentrated and rapid the recent M&A has been.

## Financial Analysis

CareTrust is in a phase of capital-markets-fueled growth. Full-year revenue stepped from $228M (2024) to $476M (2025): rental income $368M (+61% YoY), interest income $107M, and modest resident fees. The jump reflects ~$1.5B of 2025 acquisitions (162 properties, $123M initial annual rent) plus the ~$595M Care REIT plc UK acquisition that closed May 8, 2025.

Q1 2026 (filed May 7, 2026):
- Net income $80.2M; diluted EPS $0.36 (+3% YoY).
- Normalized FFO $107.4M, $0.48/share (+14% YoY); normalized FAD $0.48/share (+12%).
- Total revenue $142.8M vs. $96.6M in Q1'25 (+48%).
- 100% rent and interest collection.

FY2026 guidance was raised: net income $1.49–1.53/share; normalized FFO $2.00–2.04 (midpoint +14.8% over 2025); normalized FAD $1.98–2.02. Guidance assumes no further investments, debt, or equity beyond YTD activity; additional deals would be incremental upside.

The balance sheet is the standout. Total debt is just $900M ($400M 3.875% senior notes due 2028; a $500M term loan at an effective 4.63%, with a swapped portion at 3.5%) and no revolver balance at year-end. Net Debt / annualized normalized run-rate EBITDA is 0.6x, far below the company's own 4.0–5.0x target and extraordinarily low for a REIT. Net debt-to-enterprise value is ~3.6%. There are no maturities before 2028. Total assets are $5.2B against $4.1B of stockholders' equity. The Moody's investment-grade upgrade announced alongside Q1 results lowers future cost of capital and widens the spread on every new deal.

Cash-flow quality is solid: operating cash flow rose to $394M in 2025 from $244M, and the FAD payout ratio is ~81%, indicating healthy coverage. The central tension is funding. The 2025 growth was heavily equity-financed: ~$1.1B net raised from common stock, including a $736M follow-on at $32.00 (August 2025) and ~$370M via ATM at an average $29.34, plus forward sales. Diluted share count went from ~106M (2023) to ~187M (Q1'25) to ~224M (Q1'26), with guidance assuming ~234M. The model is accretive only as long as deployment yields (8.8–9.4%) exceed cost of capital, which they currently do, producing double-digit per-share FFO growth despite the dilution.

The 0.6x leverage is the single most striking data point. CareTrust has effectively pre-funded its pipeline by raising equity faster than it can deploy it. That is dry powder and balance-sheet safety, but also a drag from un-deployed cash and a reliance on continued issuance. With ~$1.1B already closed YTD 2026 at ~8.9% and a $360M pipeline remaining, the un-deployed cash is being put to work.

## Growth Analysis

Three reinforcing drivers support the growth thesis.

First, the estimate revisions are positive and material. Next-quarter and current-quarter estimates each rose 3.8–4.2% in the last 30 days. Revisions of this magnitude on a name where the multiple is ~18x forward, well below the threshold at which beats are fully priced, typically precede further re-rating as consensus catches up. The earnings inflection here is driven by accretive deployment, not financial engineering.

Second, the deployment pace is confirmed in hard numbers, not guidance. $245M was invested in Q1 2026 at an 8.8% blended yield; another ~$864M closed post-quarter at 8.9% (including a $747M April announcement), for ~$1.1B closed YTD at ~8.9%. With a $360M pipeline still outstanding and guidance that explicitly excludes further deals, the bias to estimates is upward.

Third, the industry backdrop is favorable and supply-constrained. The nursing-home facility count fell from ~15,600 (2016) to ~14,742 (mid-2025) even as the 65+ population (59M+ in 2024) is projected to nearly double through 2060. CMS projects nursing-home/CCRC spending to grow from $228.8B (2024) to $385.9B (2033). The regulatory overhang that depressed the sector has broken in CareTrust's favor: the OBBBA (July 2025) did not include the feared SNF Medicaid cuts, the FY2026 Medicare SNF rate rose 3.2%, and the burdensome CMS minimum-staffing mandate was vacated, placed under a legislative moratorium to 2034, and formally repealed by HHS in December 2025, removing a major cost cloud over tenant rent coverage.

The dividend growth (16.4% YoY to $0.39/quarter) is among the fastest in the REIT space and is supported by FAD growth rather than a payout-ratio stretch.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$36.38 against FY2026 normalized FFO guidance of $2.00–2.04, CTRE trades at ~18x forward NFFO. On the reported $1.80 current-year EPS estimate, the P/E is ~20x. The headline trailing P/E of 23x is not the right metric for a REIT mid-flywheel; FFO multiples and dividend coverage are the relevant frame.

Peer context is mixed and the SNF-heavy peer set is heterogeneous. Among healthcare REITs, the closest read-across is Omega Healthcare (OHI), the large SNF triple-net peer, at ~21.9x P/E and a 5.8% yield, and Sabra (SBRA) at ~29.3x and 6.4%. The larger diversified names (Healthpeak/DOC at 64x, American Healthcare/AHR at 81x) carry distorted GAAP multiples and are not clean comparables. CareTrust's ~18x forward NFFO sits below the cited peer-median P/E of 64x but above the cheapest SNF comps on a headline basis, a premium that the ~15% FFO growth, 0.6x leverage, fresh IG rating, and 16% dividend growth reasonably justify. The 4.2% yield is below OHI/SBRA/DOC (5.8–6.4%), reflecting the market's pricing of CareTrust as a growth REIT rather than a high-yield one.

The valuation score is held at the midpoint rather than higher for two honest reasons. A compressed FFO multiple is not, by itself, a high-conviction mispricing signal in a sector where cheapness has frequently been a value trap, and the model's reliance on continued favorable equity-issuance terms means the multiple is contingent on the stock not derating further. The specific catalyst, +15% upward current-year estimate revisions over 30 days against a ~15% price decline from the 52-week high, is a concrete reason the current price lags fundamentals. Analyst targets of $42–49 (mean/median $45.5) against a $36.38 price imply 15–35% upside before yield. On a base case of 19–20x the $2.02 NFFO midpoint, fair value lands in the low-$40s, consistent with the analyst low end.

## Competitive Landscape

CareTrust competes against larger, lower-cost-of-capital REITs (Omega, Sabra, Welltower, Ventas) but differentiates by targeting mid-sized operators and creative structured deals (loans converting to sale-leasebacks, purchase options) that fall below the radar of mega-cap peers. The deep operator relationships from the Ensign/Pennant lineage are a genuine sourcing advantage, and the lending book functions as a proprietary acquisition funnel rather than pure yield.

The fortress balance sheet is a competitive weapon in a higher-for-longer rate environment. With 0.6x net leverage and an IG rating, CareTrust can outbid and out-execute capital-constrained peers and deploy through a window when others are sidelined. The trajectory of the competitive position is improving, with share of the fragmented SNF/senior-housing market being gained through M&A at attractive spreads, but two factors temper the score below the high band: the Ensign 23% concentration caps the moat, and the UK and SHOP expansions are new, unproven platforms where CareTrust does not yet have a demonstrated edge. The chief industry headwind remains the labor-cost/minimum-wage/reimbursement squeeze on tenants (e.g., CA SB 525 lifting healthcare-worker minimum wage to $25/hr by 2028), which ultimately governs rent coverage.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration — Ensign (23%).** As a triple-net landlord, CareTrust has no operational control. Ensign deterioration, bankruptcy, or non-renewal would be highly material, and cross-default provisions across the Original Ensign Leases concentrate the exposure. Mitigant: Ensign is a publicly traded, financially strong operator.

**Reimbursement/regulatory risk.** The defining sector risk. Idaho enacted a 4% Medicaid rate cut (Q3 2025); North Carolina cut then reversed. CA SB 525 pressures the largest-revenue state's tenants. These are offset, for now, by the OBBBA's exclusion of SNF Medicaid cuts, the 3.2% FY2026 Medicare rate increase, and the repeal of the staffing mandate.

**Equity-funding dependence.** The flywheel requires continuous equity access above acquisition cap rates. A sustained share-price decline below deal yields, or a rate-driven REIT selloff, would stall the model. The pre-funded balance sheet (0.6x leverage, large dry powder) gives runway, but the dependence is real.

**Integration/international risk.** The Care REIT plc UK acquisition introduces GBP/USD exposure, the UK regulatory regime (CQC), and integration risk; the 10-K warns it "may not be accretive… in the near term or at all." Tennessee revenue went from ~$2M to ~$43M in a year, a rapid concentration in newly entered markets that historical performance does not yet reflect.

**SHOP/RIDEA operational risk.** The platform shifts CareTrust from passive rent collector to bearer of direct operating risk (occupancy, labor, liability) through managers it cannot fully control. Small today, but a strategic direction expanding the risk profile.

**Lending-book credit/mark risk.** ~$900M of investments carried at fair value; 2025 results included $15.8M of unrealized gains that can reverse.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 76.8% against a 1-year historical vol of 23.7% (rich), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (76.8% at 1-week vs. 28.0% at 1- and 6-month tenors). This pattern reflects an imminent technical event (the 1-day expiry) and a small dividend-related dislocation rather than a fundamental warning; the longer-dated 28% IV is normal for a REIT. Put/call OI is 0.59 (call-skewed) and the put/call skew is balanced at 1.5%, so the options market is not pricing directional downside. Short interest is elevated at 10.4% of float with 8.8 days to cover, a meaningful but not extreme bearish positioning that creates modest squeeze potential into positive catalysts (continued estimate revisions, deal closes). The net read: no options-implied confirmation of the bear thesis, with a slightly favorable squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Management keeps deploying its large dry powder at ~8.9% yields against a sub-4.7% cost of capital, the IG upgrade further compresses funding costs, and FY2026 NFFO lands at or above the $2.04 high end with additional deals (excluded from guidance) providing upside. Estimate revisions continue upward, the multiple re-rates toward 20–21x forward NFFO, and the 16% dividend growth supports the equity. Target $44–49.

**Bear case.** A rate-driven REIT selloff pushes CTRE below deal cap rates, stalling the equity-funded flywheel; Ensign or a newly concentrated market (UK, TN) shows tenant-coverage stress; the Care REIT integration disappoints; and the multiple compresses toward the cheapest SNF comps (~15–16x forward NFFO). Target $30–32, roughly 12–18% below current.

The asymmetry favors the bull case at this entry: ~15% upside to the analyst low end before yield, ~12–18% downside in the bear case, plus a 4.2% dividend. The decisive variable is the spread between deployment yields and cost of capital, which remains positive and was just improved by the IG upgrade.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $42.** 20–21x the $2.02 FY2026 NFFO midpoint, consistent with the IG balance sheet and ~15% growth. ~+15% from $36.38, plus a 4.2% yield.

**Bull case target: $47.** ~23x forward NFFO if deployment continues and FY2027 NFFO approaches $2.25–2.30 with continued estimate revisions. ~+29% from current.

**Bear case target: $31.** ~15–16x forward NFFO in a rate-driven REIT derating or tenant-coverage scare. ~-15% from current.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (Q2 2026 in early August, Q3 in early November) and the pace of pipeline closings.

**Upside/downside from current ($36.38):** +15% to base, +29% to bull, -15% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Blended acquisition yield on new deals falls below ~7.5% or quarterly net deployment stalls (pipeline closings cease), signaling the flywheel has jammed.
2. Any normalized FFO guidance cut, or full-year NFFO trending below the $2.00 low end.
3. Material tenant-coverage deterioration — a rent-collection rate below 98%, an Ensign or UK/TN-market non-renewal, or a Medicaid rate action that demonstrably impairs tenant EBITDARM coverage.

## Conclusion

CareTrust pairs a fortress, freshly investment-grade-rated balance sheet (0.6x net leverage) with a demonstrably accretive acquisition flywheel: ~$1.1B closed YTD 2026 at ~8.9% yields, FY2026 NFFO guided to +15%, and a 16.4% dividend increase. The recent ~15% price decline from the 52-week high is a function of the rate-driven REIT selloff, not fundamental deterioration; over the same window current-year EPS estimates were revised up +15.3% (30-day), compressing the forward NFFO multiple to ~18x and opening a gap between rising fundamentals and a derated price. That divergence, combined with analyst targets of $42–49 against a $36.38 price and a supportive 4.2% yield, defines a favorable risk/reward.

The constraints are real and keep this from being a maximum-conviction call: the model depends on continued favorable equity-issuance terms, Ensign is 23% of rent, and the UK/SHOP platforms are unproven. Those risks argue for disciplined position sizing rather than abstention. **Recommendation: BUY**, base-case target $42 over 12 months, with the deployment yield-vs-cost-of-capital spread as the variable to monitor and the invalidation triggers above as the exit discipline.
