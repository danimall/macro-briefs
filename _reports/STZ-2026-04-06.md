## Executive Summary

Constellation Brands is the #1 imported beer company in the U.S. market, with Modelo Especial as the best-selling beer brand nationally by dollar sales. The company's perpetual sub-license for Modelo/Corona in the U.S. is an exceptionally valuable franchise asset, generating 39%+ beer operating margins and $3.15B in annual operating cash flow. At 12.2x forward earnings and a 2.7% dividend yield, the stock appears inexpensive relative to the quality of the beer business.

The investment case faces a key question: beer depletions turned negative for the first time in recent history, declining 2.7% in 9M FY2026 and 3.0% in Q3 FY2026. This volume decline coincides with $2B+ in capacity expansion at Mexican breweries. The mismatch between declining demand and rising capacity represents a material risk to capital efficiency. Additionally, 100% of beer production is located in Mexico, creating tariff tail risk that current pricing (0.43 beta, 12x forward P/E) does not adequately reflect.

The stock has declined 23% from its 52-week high of $196.91. A new CEO (Nicholas Fink, appointed February 2026), aggressive buybacks ($3.18B remaining authorization), and recent earnings beats support a constructive view, but uncertainty around volume trajectory and tariff exposure prevents a high-conviction directional call.

## Company Overview

Constellation Brands operates primarily as a premium beer company with a smaller wine & spirits segment:

- **Beer (~84% of net sales):** Modelo Especial, Corona Extra, Corona Premier, Pacifico, Victoria, and extensions. Produced exclusively at breweries in Nava and Obregón, Mexico, with a third (Veracruz) under construction. Current capacity ~48M hectoliters, expanding to ~55M by FY2028.
- **Wine & Spirits (~16% of net sales):** After divesting mainstream brands for $846M in June 2025, the portfolio consists of higher-end labels: Kim Crawford, Robert Mondavi Winery, The Prisoner, High West, SVEDKA, and others. Operating margins collapsed to 1.3% post-divestiture.
- **Employees:** 10,600. Headquartered in Rochester, NY.

The company holds an **exclusive perpetual sub-license** for Modelo/Corona brands in the U.S. market. This license is the single most important asset; it cannot be revoked by AB InBev (which sold the brands to STZ as a condition of its Grupo Modelo acquisition in 2013).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

FY2025 (ended Feb 2025) consolidated net sales were $10.21B, with beer at $8.54B (+5% YoY) and W&S at $1.67B (-7%). The 9M FY2026 period shows consolidation at $7.22B (-10% YoY), though the decline is heavily influenced by the W&S divestiture — beer revenue was $6.59B (-4%).

Beer operating margins remain the standout: 39.7% in FY2025 (up from 37.9%), holding at 39.3% for 9M FY2026 despite volume decline. This resilience reflects $116.5M in pricing benefits and $145M+ in cost savings offsetting volume softness. Gross margins have improved to the 52-53% range as lower-margin wine brands were divested.

W&S operating margins collapsed from 18.6% to 1.3% in 9M FY2026, reflecting stranded costs after the mainstream brand divestitures. The annualized W&S revenue run-rate is approximately $840M — small enough that the segment's margin trajectory, while concerning, has limited consolidated impact.

### Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow was $3.15B in FY2025 (up from $2.78B). For 9M FY2026, OCF was $2.11B (down from $2.56B, partly due to deferred tax timing). Capital expenditure is declining — $656M in 9M FY2026 vs. $1.21B in FY2025 — as the Nava and Obregón expansions near completion.

Capital returns are substantial:
- **Dividends:** $4.08/share annualized ($1.02/quarter), ~$720M annually, yielding 2.7%
- **Buybacks:** $824M (5M shares) repurchased in 9M FY2026 at an average price of ~$165. $3.18B remains under the authorization (through Feb 2028), representing ~12% of current market cap
- **Share count:** Declining from ~183M diluted in FY2024 to ~174M in Q3 FY2026

The buyback math at current prices (~$151) is accretive — STZ is buying back shares well below the $135 average it paid in Q3 FY2026, and far below the $260+ prices paid in prior years.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $10.66B (down from $11.50B at Feb 2025), with $152M in cash. Net leverage is approximately 3.0x EBITDA, within management's target range and well within the 4.0x covenant limit. The $2.25B revolving credit facility has $1.86B available.

Debt maturity profile is manageable: $603M in FY2027, $1.8B in FY2028, $900M in FY2029. In the current rate environment (no cuts expected through 2026 per IMF/OECD), refinancing costs will be elevated but not threatening given the cash flow profile.

The FY2025 10-K reported $2.74B in W&S goodwill impairment plus $478M in assets-held-for-sale and $57M in trademark impairments. These are non-cash and reflect the strategic decision to exit mainstream wine, but they underscore the capital destruction embedded in the W&S segment's history.

### Earnings Quality

STZ beat EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters: +14.3% (March 2025), +6.3% (September 2025), and +15.0% (December 2025). The lone miss was June 2025 at -3.6%. The beat pattern supports reasonable confidence in current consensus estimates of $11.63 for FY2026 and $12.36 for FY2027.

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story. Current-quarter EPS was revised down 13.9% over 90 days (from $1.99 to $1.71), though the most recent 30-day revision was +1.5%. Full-year FY2026 estimates are stable at $11.63 (+1.2% over 90 days). The current-quarter downward revision suggests analysts see near-term softness despite the longer-term trajectory remaining intact.

## Growth Analysis

### Beer Volume: The Critical Variable

For the first time in recent history, STZ's beer depletions (a proxy for consumer demand) turned negative: -2.7% for 9M FY2026 and -3.0% in Q3 FY2026. This compares to +2.9% growth in FY2025. Management attributes the decline to "non-structural socioeconomic factors" — subdued consumer spending, elevated unemployment, and value-seeking behavior.

The structural vs. cyclical question cannot be resolved with current data. Arguments for cyclical:
- Consumer sentiment at 56.6 (-12.5% YoY), consistent with temporary demand compression
- Hispanic consumer demographics (key STZ customer base) are disproportionately affected by current economic conditions
- STZ continues to gain market share even as volumes decline

Arguments for structural:
- GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide/tirzepatide) reducing alcohol consumption — LLY's oral Foundayo approval expands this threat
- Health/moderation trends and WHO cancer risk warnings
- Cannabis substitution in legalized states
- Overall U.S. beer consumption in secular decline

### Capacity Expansion Disconnect

STZ is spending $2B+ through FY2028 to expand brewery capacity from 48M to 55M hectoliters. The Veracruz Brewery is expected to begin initial production in late FY2026/early FY2027. If beer volumes don't recover, the company will face excess capacity — a risk explicitly acknowledged in the 10-K. Fixed costs on underutilized facilities would compress margins.

### Wine & Spirits

The repositioned W&S segment (higher-end brands only) has a ~$840M revenue run-rate with 1.3% operating margins. Management expects growth "beyond FY2026." The premium wine category faces its own headwinds — younger consumers increasingly prefer spirits, RTDs, and non-alcoholic alternatives. W&S is unlikely to contribute meaningfully to growth in the next 2-3 years.

### Revenue Growth Model

At current run-rates, beer revenue growth hinges on volume recovery plus pricing. With ~$8.5B in beer sales, a 1% volume improvement plus 2% pricing would yield ~$8.8B — modest 3% growth. The FY2026 current-year EPS estimate of $11.63 implies roughly flat-to-slightly-up earnings, with buyback accretion (share count declining ~5% annually) providing the primary EPS growth lever.

## Valuation Assessment

At $151.20, STZ trades at:
- **12.2x forward P/E** (FY2026 EPS estimate $11.63)
- **12.2x next-year P/E** (FY2027 estimate $12.36)
- **~23.7x trailing P/E** (distorted by W&S impairments)
- **~2.7% dividend yield**
- **30% below 52-week high** ($196.91)
- **20% above 52-week low** ($126.45)

The 12x forward multiple is historically cheap for STZ, which traded at 15-20x for much of the past five years. Analyst consensus median target is $172.50 (14% upside), with a range of $120-$220. Of 24 analysts, 14 rate it Buy/Strong Buy, 8 Hold, and 2 Strong Sell.

The valuation discount appears to price in: (1) beer volume deceleration, (2) tariff risk, and (3) W&S segment weakness. Whether 12x is sufficient compensation for these risks or overshoots is the central valuation debate.

Beer peers: AB InBev at ~16-18x, Molson Coors at ~10-11x. STZ's 12x places it between the two, which is reasonable given STZ's superior growth profile (even with the recent deceleration) but meaningful risk concentration in Mexico.

A simple DCF approach: $3.15B operating cash flow, $1.0B normalized capex = ~$2.15B FCF on ~$26.3B market cap = 8.2% FCF yield. For a consumer staples company with category-leading brands, an 8%+ FCF yield is attractive if the beer franchise is not impaired.

## Competitive Landscape

STZ holds a structurally advantaged position in U.S. beer:

1. **Modelo Especial** is the #1 beer brand in U.S. dollar sales, a position it assumed partly due to Bud Light's 2023 controversy and associated share loss. STZ holds 5 of the top 15 share-gaining beer brands.
2. The **perpetual U.S. license** for Corona/Modelo is an irreplaceable asset. AB InBev cannot compete with these brands in the U.S.
3. The **high-end/imported segment** has been the only growing subsegment of U.S. beer, though even this has softened.
4. **Vertical integration** — STZ co-owns a glass production plant (with Owens-Illinois) adjacent to its Nava brewery, supplying ~60% of glass bottle needs.

The primary competitive risk is category-level demand destruction. STZ can gain share in a declining market for some time, but if total beer consumption continues falling, share gains eventually slow against a shrinking denominator. The market is partially pricing this in.

**Customer concentration** is notable: Reyes Beer Division accounts for 25.4% of consolidated net sales. Southern Glazer's handles ~60% of W&S volume. Neither is at risk of switching in the near term, but the concentration limits negotiating leverage.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Mexico Tariff Exposure (Severe, Binary)
100% of beer production occurs in Mexico. Current tariff impact is ~$70M in FY2026 (manageable). A hypothetical 25% tariff on Mexican beer imports would add $2B+ in costs on $8.5B of beer revenue — devastating. STZ has no U.S. brewing infrastructure. In the current trade policy environment (IEEPA authority struck down by SCOTUS, but Section 232 and congressional alternatives being pursued), the probability of severe Mexican tariffs is not negligible. The 0.43 beta underprices this tail risk.

### 2. Beer Volume Trajectory (High, Ongoing)
Three consecutive quarters of negative beer depletions (-2.7% cumulative, -3.0% in Q3) while spending $2B+ on capacity expansion. If volumes don't recover, margin compression from underutilized capacity follows. Management's "non-structural" characterization is unverified.

### 3. Consumer Macro Headwinds (Elevated)
The stagflationary macro environment (core PCE 3.1%, consumer sentiment 56.6, unemployment 4.4%) directly pressures STZ's core consumer — the $10+ six-pack buyer who may trade down to domestic brands. Oil at ~$107 and diesel at +40% increases distribution costs.

### 4. Secular Alcohol Moderation
GLP-1 drugs, cannabis substitution, health consciousness, and WHO cancer risk warnings create a long-duration overhang on per-capita alcohol consumption. STZ's premium positioning provides some resilience (premium consumers are less price-sensitive and less likely to moderate entirely), but the secular direction is negative for the category.

### 5. Securities Litigation
Class action (Meza v. Constellation Brands) alleging material misstatements about beer business prospects. Derivative suits also filed. Motion to dismiss pending. Financial exposure is uncertain but could be material.

### 6. Sands Family Pledged Shares
Certain Sands family members have pledged shares as collateral for personal credit facilities. In a severe stock decline, forced liquidation of pledged shares could create a cascading selloff.

### 7. FX Exposure
Significant Mexican peso exposure on production costs. STZ hedges actively — $97.4M in unrealized FX hedge gains as of November 2025 expected to flow through within 12 months — but hedges roll off and the peso's direction against the dollar is uncertain.

### Options Market Signal
No direct options sentiment data was provided for STZ. The stock's 0.43 beta implies low market-perceived correlation with broader equity risk, which undercounts the tariff tail risk. Short interest at 4.5% of float with 4.3 days to cover is moderate, suggesting some bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- **12x forward P/E on a category-leading beer franchise** with 39%+ operating margins is historically cheap. If beer volumes stabilize (returning to flat-to-low-single-digit growth), the stock re-rates toward 14-16x, implying $163-$186+.
- **Aggressive buybacks** at depressed prices: $3.18B remaining authorization (~12% of market cap). At $151, every $1B in buybacks retires ~3.8% of shares outstanding, adding directly to EPS.
- **New CEO catalyst:** Nicholas Fink takes over at a low-expectations moment. Strategic clarity on tariff mitigation, capacity right-sizing, or W&S restructuring would improve sentiment.
- **$97.4M in unrealized FX hedge gains** flowing through in the next 12 months provides a near-term earnings tailwind.
- **The restructuring program** ($200M+ annualized savings by FY2028) partially offsets volume weakness.
- Beer category weakness may be cyclical; even a modest consumer spending rebound could restore low-single-digit growth.

### Bear Case
- **Beer volume decline is structural, not cyclical.** GLP-1 adoption accelerates, moderation trends intensify, and STZ's growth engine stalls permanently, turning the $2B+ capacity expansion into a stranded-cost drag.
- **Mexican tariffs escalate.** A 10-25% tariff on beer imports would compress margins by 400-1000 basis points. STZ has no ability to relocate production in the medium term (5+ years to build U.S. brewing capacity at scale). In the current trade environment, this risk is elevated.
- **Consumer contraction deepens.** With core PCE at 3.1%, diesel +40%, and no rate cuts through 2026, the consumer is increasingly squeezed. Premium beer is a discretionary purchase vulnerable to sustained trade-down.
- **W&S remains a margin drag.** At 1.3% operating margins, the segment contributes minimally to earnings while consuming management bandwidth.
- **Leverage at 3.0x** in a no-rate-cut environment means refinancing the $1.8B FY2028 maturity at higher rates, increasing interest expense.
- Fair value at 10-11x forward earnings (Molson Coors range) implies $116-$128 — 18-23% downside from current levels.

## Conclusion

STZ is a high-quality franchise at a fair price with identifiable risks. The beer business is excellent: 39%+ operating margins, the #1 beer brand in America, and a perpetual license that no competitor can replicate. At 12x forward earnings with an 8%+ FCF yield and aggressive buybacks, the valuation compensates for meaningful near-term headwinds.

Three factors prevent a BUY recommendation:

1. **Beer volume deceleration is confirmed and unexplained.** Three quarters of negative depletions require explanation. Until there is evidence of stabilization (Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 data), the growth trajectory is uncertain.
2. **Tariff tail risk is asymmetric.** 100% Mexico production concentration in the current trade policy environment creates a low-probability, high-impact scenario that the stock's low beta fails to capture.
3. **Capacity expansion timing is unfavorable.** Spending $2B to add 15% more capacity while volumes decline is a capital allocation decision that will look either prescient or destructive depending on demand recovery. The new CEO has not yet articulated a position.

The constructive elements (cheap valuation, buyback accretion, restructuring savings, new management, earnings beat history) are sufficient to prevent an AVOID call. But the demand uncertainty, tariff exposure, and macro backdrop (stagflation, consumer contraction) warrant a HOLD until beer volume data clarifies. The next quarterly earnings report (expected early April 2026 for Q4 FY2026) is the key data point for reassessment.
