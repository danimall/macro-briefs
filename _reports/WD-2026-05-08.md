## Executive Summary

Walker & Dunlop is the #1 Fannie Mae DUS lender in the United States, with an 11.2% combined GSE market share and a $144B servicing portfolio generating $337M in annual recurring fees. The business is positioned to benefit from a CRE transaction volume recovery — 2025 debt financing volumes grew 38% YoY to $41.5B, and Q1 2026 revenue surged 27% with adjusted EPS beating consensus by 260%.

However, the stock trades at $54.15, down 40% from its 52-week high of $90, primarily due to a borrower fraud issue that surfaced in late 2025. The company repurchased or indemnified $221.6M of loans over the past two years after internal investigations revealed employees failed to detect fraudulent borrower behavior. The scope of remaining exposure is uncertain, with 14 defaulted loans ($159M UPB) and ongoing GSE negotiations. This binary risk creates genuine uncertainty about near-term earnings power and the potential for additional charges.

The Q1 2026 beat and four separate insider purchases near the lows in March 2026 suggest the worst may be priced in, but until the GSE fraud negotiations conclude with quantified liability, the risk/reward remains ambiguous. At ~12x FY2027 consensus EPS ($5.79), the stock is not expensive for its normalized earnings power, but the 33x trailing P/E reflects depressed 2025 results distorted by one-time charges.

## Company Overview

Walker & Dunlop operates as a vertically integrated CRE finance platform through three segments:

**Capital Markets:** Agency lending (Fannie Mae DUS, Freddie Mac Optigo, HUD/FHA), debt brokerage, property sales, appraisal services, and investment banking (Zelman & Associates). This segment drives the cyclical upside via origination and placement fees.

**Servicing & Asset Management:** $144B servicing portfolio with weighted-average remaining term of 7.2 years, generating 23.6 bps in annual fees. Also includes $18.6B AUM in LIHTC/affordable housing investment management and principal lending activities.

**Business Model Nuance:** WD originates Agency loans under delegated authority — it underwrites, funds through warehouse lines, and sells within ~60 days to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. The DUS program requires WD to share in losses (first-loss position up to prescribed thresholds), creating a quasi-insurance obligation that aligns its interests with the GSEs. Only 25 lenders have DUS authorization.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Recovery:**
The CRE transaction market is recovering after a brutal 2022-2024 downturn. WD's debt financing volume grew 38% in 2025 ($41.5B) with property sales up 37% ($13.3B). Q1 2026 revenue of $301M (+27% YoY) confirms accelerating trajectory.

**Structural Tailwinds:**
- 2026 GSE lending caps expanded 21% to $176B (from $146B in 2025)
- Multifamily supply pipeline declining while absorption continues — favorable rent growth dynamics
- 72% of refinancing volume from "new to us" loans — competitive position strengthening
- 19% of transaction volumes from new customers

**Headwinds to Margin Recovery:**
- Origination fee rates compressed 9 bps (83 vs 92 bps)
- MSR rates fell 18 bps on Agency volume (96 vs 114 bps)
- Weighted-average servicing term shortening (7.2 vs 7.7 years) — reduces future fee duration
- Escrow placement income declining as rates fall (contra-cyclical to volume)

**Consensus Estimates:**
- FY2026E: $4.57 EPS (estimates falling — down 9.3% in 30 days)
- FY2027E: $5.79 EPS (estimates rising — up 5.8% in 30 days)

The bifurcation in estimate revisions is informative: near-term estimates are being cut (likely reflecting lingering fraud charges and margin pressure), while out-year estimates are being raised (reflecting confidence in the volume recovery trajectory). The critical estimate revision signal (>30% in 90 days) is not present in either direction.

- Forward P/E on FY2027E: 9.4x ($5.79)

**Peer Comparison:**
Mortgage insurance peers (NMIH 7.8x, ESNT 8.9x, ACT 9.5x) trade at 8-10x earnings. These are less cyclical, pure-play underwriting businesses with different margin profiles. WD's normalized earnings power of $5-6/share at current CRE market recovery rates places it at 9-11x, roughly in line with peers. However, WD's business includes higher-growth brokerage, property sales, and investment management that warrant a modest premium.

**Analyst Targets:**
Consensus mean target of $68 (range $65-$70) implies 26-29% upside from current levels. The Stephens initiation at $69 (Overweight) provides the most recent independent assessment.

**Valuation Verdict:**
At 9.4x FY2027 earnings, WD is not expensive for a business with double-digit volume growth and a 5% dividend yield. The discount reflects fraud uncertainty and 2025's compressed earnings. If the fraud issue resolves at manageable cost and volumes continue recovering, the stock could re-rate toward 12-14x normalized earnings ($70-$80). But that conditional carries genuine binary risk.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Moat:**
WD's DUS authority represents a meaningful barrier — only 25 lenders are approved, and the license requires demonstrated underwriting capability, capital adequacy, and historical performance. The company's 7-year run as #1 Fannie Mae DUS lender with 11.2% market share reflects deep institutional relationships that take decades to build.

**Position Trajectory:**
Market share expanded from 10.7% to 11.2% in 2025, and 72% of refinancing volume came from loans not previously in WD's servicing portfolio — active share gains from competitors. The $144B servicing book provides recurring revenue and client retention for refinancing activity.

**Competitive Threats:**
Wells Fargo, CBRE, JLL, and Berkadia operate at larger scale with more diversified capital sources. WD's failure to meet its "Vision 2025" targets ($2B revenue goal vs. $1.23B actual; $60B debt volume goal vs. $41.5B) suggests management overestimated its ability to expand beyond core GSE lending into small-balance, institutional, and international markets. The GeoPhy acquisition ($33M impairment) exemplifies this overreach.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Borrower Fraud / GSE Repurchase Liability (HIGH — BINARY)**
The most critical near-term risk. $221.6M repurchased over two years, 14 loans in default ($159M UPB), and negotiations with GSEs still ongoing. The $194.6M total originated by terminated employees represents the outer bound of additional exposure. Historical 10-year net write-offs of just $9.2M make this unprecedented for WD. Resolution timing is unknown, and additional charges are possible.

**2. GSE Political/Structural Risk (MODERATE)**
FHFA conservatorship has no end date. Any restructuring of GSE programs could fundamentally alter WD's business model. The 2026 cap expansion to $176B is currently positive, but political winds shift.

**3. Margin Compression on Growth (MODERATE)**
Volume recovery is occurring at lower per-unit economics: fee rates declining, servicing terms shortening, institutional deals commanding lower margins. Revenue growth may not translate proportionally into earnings growth.

**4. Goodwill Concentration (MODERATE)**
$869M of goodwill (50% of equity) from acquisitions including Alliant Capital (LIHTC), Zelman, and others. Further impairments are possible if integration continues to disappoint.

**5. Rate Sensitivity (TWO-SIDED)**
Lower rates drive transaction volumes but reduce escrow income. Higher rates suppress volumes but maintain servicing margins. The current environment (rates elevated but expected to moderate) creates uncertainty in both directions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($70-$80, +29-48%):**
- Fraud liability resolves at manageable cost ($30-50M additional charges, vs. the $65M already absorbed)
- CRE transaction volumes continue accelerating through 2026, supported by expanded GSE caps
- Operating margins recover toward 10-12% as one-time charges fade and volume scale absorbs fixed costs
- Stock re-rates to 12-14x FY2027 EPS of $5.79 = $69-$81
- 5% dividend yield provides downside cushion during recovery

**Bear Case ($35-$42, -23-35%):**
- GSE fraud negotiations result in substantially larger liability ($100M+) or partial loss of DUS authority
- Additional employee misconduct surfaces, requiring expanded investigation
- CRE recovery stalls due to persistently elevated rates (30Y > 5% scenario)
- Goodwill impairments on underperforming acquisitions compress book value further
- Stock de-rates to 6-7x depressed earnings = $32-$40

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. GSE fraud negotiations conclude with quantified, bounded liability under $50M additional charges — removes the binary overhang
2. Two consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS above $1.25 with operating margins above 10% — confirms earnings recovery trajectory
3. Stock pulls back to $45-48 on non-fundamental volatility while earnings trajectory remains intact

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. GSE imposes restrictions on DUS lending authority or requires material capital reserves beyond current provisions
2. Additional fraud surfaces involving different employees/different time periods — suggests systemic control failure
3. FY2026 EPS estimate revisions turn negative beyond -15% (currently -9.3% over 30 days)

**Reassessment Timeline:**
- Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July/early August): critical for trajectory confirmation
- Any announcement of GSE fraud resolution terms: immediate reassessment
- If neither occurs by August 2026, re-evaluate based on volume/margin trajectory in H1 data

## Conclusion

Walker & Dunlop possesses a strong franchise — #1 Fannie Mae DUS lender, $144B servicing portfolio, expanding market share, and a recovering CRE market driving 27-38% volume growth. At 9.4x FY2027 consensus EPS with a 5% dividend yield, the stock is reasonably valued for its normalized earnings power. Four insiders buying in a tight window near the lows adds conviction that the sell-off is overdone relative to long-term value.

The blocking issue is the unresolved fraud liability. Until GSE negotiations conclude with bounded exposure, the downside scenario includes material additional charges and potential damage to WD's delegated authority. This creates a genuinely binary risk that prevents full conviction in either direction.

At current levels, the risk/reward skews modestly positive (29-48% upside vs. 23-35% downside), but the uncertainty around timing and magnitude of fraud resolution prevents a BUY recommendation. The stock becomes a compelling BUY candidate once the fraud overhang clears or if it pulls back to $45-48 where downside is more bounded by book value and dividend yield.
