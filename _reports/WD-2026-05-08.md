## Executive Summary

Walker & Dunlop is the #2 combined GSE multifamily lender in the United States with a $146.4B servicing portfolio recovering off a cyclical trough. Q1 2026 delivered transaction volumes of $13.7B (+94% YoY), revenue of $301M (+27%), and adjusted core EPS of $1.02 versus $0.85 in Q1 2025 — a 20% year-over-year improvement. Full-year 2025 EPS of $1.64 represented the nadir of a multi-year decline from $8.15 peak EPS in 2021, driven by rate-shock-induced volume collapse, $41M in borrower-fraud-related loan repurchase costs, and $37M in asset impairments.

At $54.21, WD trades at approximately 12x the consensus FY2026 estimate of $4.57 and 9.4x the FY2027 estimate of $5.79. Peer mortgage insurers (NMIH, ESNT, ACT) trade at 8-10x earnings but lack WD's growth profile — those are steady-state businesses, while WD is in early-cycle recovery with volumes accelerating. The CEO and three directors made open-market purchases in March 2026 at $45–49, and the company repurchased $13.3M of stock in Q1 2026 at a weighted average price of $47.13. GSE lending caps were raised 21% for 2026 to $176B combined, providing a structural volume tailwind independent of market conditions.

The primary risk is loan repurchase exposure from systemic borrower fraud. As of the Q1 2026 10-Q, the company has repurchased, agreed to repurchase, or expects to indemnify approximately $191.9M of loans. The previously disclosed $34.3M probable repurchase request is no longer considered probable, as the company entered into an indemnification agreement under which the GSE forebears repurchase unless specified trigger events occur. The exposure has been partly restructured, but Q1 2026 still included $10M of indemnified/repurchased loan expenses and forbearance agreements with repurchase dates extending into 2027–2028. The higher interest rate environment creates dual pressure: it suppresses transaction volumes while supporting placement fee income on the servicing portfolio. On balance, the market recovery is the dominant force.

## Company Overview

Walker & Dunlop operates as a commercial real estate finance and services company focused predominantly on the U.S. multifamily sector. The business model involves originating loans through GSE programs (Fannie Mae DUS, Freddie Mac Optigo, HUD), selling the loans to the agencies while retaining servicing rights, and earning origination fees, mortgage servicing rights (MSR) income, and ongoing servicing fees. The company also operates property sales brokerage, investment management ($18.6B AUM), and advisory services.

Three operating segments:
- **Capital Markets:** Transaction-driven revenue from loan origination, debt brokerage, property sales, and advisory. This segment is highly cyclical and drives earnings volatility.
- **Servicing & Asset Management:** Recurring fees on the $146.4B portfolio plus placement fees on escrow deposits. Servicing fees are contractual for the life of each loan, providing earnings stability.
- **Corporate:** Overhead and technology.

The company holds the #1 Fannie Mae DUS lender position for 7 consecutive years and #3 Freddie Mac Optigo position, combining for 11.2% GSE multifamily market share (up from 10.3% in 2024).

The FY2025 earnings collapse (-49% EPS) occurred despite 37% volume growth and 9% revenue growth. The culprits are identifiable:
1. **$40.9M indemnified/repurchased loan expenses** (+286% YoY) from borrower fraud
2. **$36.7M asset impairments** (+3,011% YoY) from affordable housing contract disposals
3. **$42M swing in contingent consideration** (prior year benefit vs. current year neutral)
4. **$88.6M increase in personnel costs** (+16%) from hiring ahead of volume recovery

Items 2 and 3 are non-recurring. Item 1 is partially recurring but has been partly restructured through forbearance and indemnification agreements. Item 4 reflects operating leverage on a fixed cost base as volumes recover.

**Cash Flow:**

The reported -$664M operating cash flow is a structural artifact of the originate-to-sell model. WD funds loans through warehouse facilities and sells them within 60 days. At year-end 2025, loan originations exceeded sales by $834M due to timing (heavy Q4 closing activity). Normalized operating cash flow excluding loan-origination activity was approximately +$169.5M. The warehouse lines ($825M net borrowings) are secured by the underlying loans and function as working capital facilities, not permanent leverage.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Net worth: $1.0B vs. $350M Fannie Mae requirement
- Operational liquidity: $291M vs. $70M requirement
- Goodwill: $869M (~50% of equity; $33M GeoPhy impairment in 2024)
- Fannie Mae DUS risk-sharing: $69.4B at-risk portfolio, $14.2B maximum exposure, default rate 24 bps (14 loans, $167.5M) as of March 31, 2026
- Altman Z-Score of 1.04 is misleading for this business model (warehouse lines distort ratios)

**Dividend:**

$2.72 annualized, 5.0% yield at current price. Payout ratio on FY2025 trough earnings was elevated (~166%), but on normalized FY2026E of $4.57, coverage is 1.68x. The dividend has grown 3% annually and appears sustainable through the cycle.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is volume recovery from a cyclical trough combined with structural tailwinds:

**Cyclical Recovery (In Progress):**
- Q1 2026 transaction volumes of $13.7B represent +94% YoY growth
- WD management described an increasingly active CRE transaction market with broad-based growth
- GSE lending caps raised 21% to $176B combined for 2026
- Multifamily new supply is declining (construction activity slowed in 2023–2024), which management believes creates favorable fundamentals for existing properties and future transaction activity

**Structural Growth Levers:**
- Investment management AUM ($18.6B) growing through fund launches
- Property sales brokerage gaining share
- International expansion (London office opened)
- Technology platform (Apprise) improving productivity

**Management's 2030 Targets:**
- Revenue: $2B (vs. $1.234B in 2025; implies 10% CAGR)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $400-500M (vs. $263M; implies 9-14% CAGR)

These targets appear achievable if multifamily markets normalize toward 2021-2022 volume levels. Peak revenue was ~$1.4B in 2022; reaching $2B by 2030 requires the combination of market recovery and share gains.

**Estimate Trajectory:**
Current-year FY2026 consensus is $4.57 (down 9.3% over 30 days after a reset from the Q4 2025 loss). FY2027 consensus is $5.79 (up 5.8% over 30 days). The divergence reflects analysts' uncertainty about loan repurchase tail risk in 2026 coupled with growing confidence in the recovery trajectory for 2027+.

## Valuation Assessment

At $54.21, WD trades at:
- 12x FY2026E of $4.57
- 9.4x FY2027E of $5.79
- 33x trailing (trough) EPS — irrelevant for a cyclically depressed business
- ~7x mid-cycle EPS of ~$7-8 (based on normalized volumes at historical margins)

**Peer Comparison:**

Mortgage insurer peers (NMIH 7.8x, ESNT 8.9x, ACT 9.5x) trade at lower multiples but are fundamentally different businesses — they are actuarial risk-takers with steady-state earnings profiles. WD is a fee-income business with high operating leverage to transaction volumes. Among CRE services peers, CBRE trades at approximately 22x forward earnings, while JLL trades at approximately 14x. WD deserves a discount to CBRE (smaller, more concentrated, higher risk) but not a 50%+ discount, and trades at a discount to JLL as well.

At 12x FY2026E, the market is pricing in continued fraud-related charges and uncertainty about the rate environment. If WD delivers $5+ in EPS in 2027 (consensus: $5.79) and re-rates to 14-15x, the stock reaches $80-87 — 48-60% upside.

**Analyst targets:** Low $65, high $70, median $69. Stephens initiated at Overweight with $69 target in April 2026.

The valuation discount to historical ranges (WD averaged 15-18x forward EPS during 2019-2022) and to CRE services peers creates meaningful asymmetry at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

WD holds the #2 combined GSE multifamily lending position with 11.2% market share (growing from 10.3% in 2024). Competitors include Wells Fargo (now de-emphasizing CRE), CBRE, JLL, Marcus & Millichap, Berkadia, and Northmarq.

**Competitive advantages:**
- Scale with GSE platforms (operational efficiency improves with volume)
- Servicing portfolio provides recurring revenue and escrow float income
- Relationship depth in multifamily specifically — WD is a specialist competing against generalist divisions of larger firms
- Risk-sharing track record (0.2 bps loss rate over 10 years) provides credibility with agencies

**Competitive risks:**
- Larger bank competitors have lower cost of capital
- CBRE and JLL have broader geographic/product reach
- Technology disruption (though WD's Apprise platform is an offsetting investment)

Market share gains in both 2024 and 2025 despite the trough environment indicate the competitive position is improving. The specialist focus on multifamily creates switching costs with borrowers who value expertise and execution speed.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Borrower Fraud / Loan Repurchase Exposure (HIGH):**
As of Q1 2026, WD has repurchased, agreed to repurchase, or expects to indemnify approximately $191.9M of loans since 2024. Q1 2026 included $10M of indemnified/repurchased loan expenses (up 263% YoY per the adjusted EBITDA bridge), and the company reported indemnified loans of $91.2M and repurchased loans of $41.6M on its balance sheet. The previously disclosed $34.3M probable repurchase request is no longer considered probable following execution of an indemnification agreement under which the GSE forebears repurchase unless specified trigger events occur. However, two portfolios with approximately $100M aggregate UPB each carry delayed repurchase dates in Q4 2027 and Q1 2028 under forbearance/indemnification agreements, representing unresolved tail risk. The 10-K acknowledges the industry-wide nature ("systemic, no longer anecdotal"). Enhanced underwriting controls should reduce future exposure, but the issue has been restructured rather than fully resolved, and active costs persist.

**2. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):**
Higher rates suppress transaction volumes (negative for Capital Markets) while supporting placement fees (positive for SAM). A 100bp rate cut would boost volumes but reduce ~$24M in annual placement fee income. The net effect of modestly lower rates is positive; a significant spike in rates would be negative.

**3. Fannie Mae DUS Risk-Sharing (MODERATE-TO-HIGH):**
$14.2B maximum exposure on the $69.4B at-risk portfolio as of March 31, 2026. Default count includes 14 loans (12 Fannie Mae, 2 Freddie Mac SBL), with defaulted loan balances of $167.5M (0.24% of at-risk portfolio). Historical loss rates are extremely low, but the current CRE stress environment (multifamily prices still well below 2022 peaks) creates elevated tail risk. A major multifamily default wave could produce material losses.

**4. GSE Political Risk (LOW-MODERATE):**
The business is fundamentally dependent on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac programs. Any change to GSE structure could disrupt operations. However, the current political environment shows no appetite for GSE reform, and lending caps were recently increased.

**5. Concentration (MODERATE):**
Near-total dependence on U.S. multifamily CRE. Geographic and asset-class diversification is limited. The London expansion is nascent.

## Conclusion

Walker & Dunlop at $54.21 represents a cyclical recovery opportunity in early stages. Q1 2026 results demonstrated that transaction volumes are recovering rapidly, the core business model is intact, and management is executing on margin recovery. Clustered insider buying at $45–49 in March 2026 combined with company share repurchases at $47.13 provides conviction that those with the deepest information view the stock as undervalued. At 12x FY2026 consensus and 9.4x FY2027, with a 5% dividend yield providing downside support, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.

The borrower fraud issue is the legitimate constraint preventing a higher-conviction call. The $191.9M cumulative exposure and forbearance agreements extending into 2027–2028 create real earnings uncertainty. However, this is an industry-wide phenomenon, enhanced controls are in place, and the $34.3M probable repurchase request is no longer considered probable under the new indemnification structure. The stock is pricing in permanent impairment of a business that is demonstrably recovering.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% portfolio allocation. The combination of cyclical earnings recovery, structural volume tailwinds, clustered insider and company buying, 5% dividend yield, and 12x forward earnings on depressed estimates creates favorable asymmetry for a 12-18 month holding period.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The Q1 2026 earnings beat versus consensus was less dramatic than originally presented (adjusted core EPS of $1.02 vs. Q1 2025's $0.85, not a 260% beat vs. consensus), and loan-repurchase/fraud costs remain active ($10M in Q1 2026, up 263% YoY) with unresolved forbearance tail risk through 2028 — both factors reduce confidence in the near-term earnings acceleration component of the thesis.
