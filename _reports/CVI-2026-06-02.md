**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CVR Energy is a diversified energy holding company roughly 71% controlled by Carl Icahn / Icahn Enterprises, structured as a barbell of two mid-continent refineries (Coffeyville, KS and Wynnewood, OK; ~206,500 bpd combined) and a consolidated nitrogen fertilizer business (CVR Partners, ticker UAN). The equity is coming off a cyclical earnings trough. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $1,164M in 2023 to $317M in 2024 and recovered modestly to $393M in 2025 as crack spreads normalized off post-Ukraine highs. The stock has rallied roughly 50% from its February 2026 low of $19.62 to $34.92, tracking a turn in refining cracks, rising forward estimates, and visible accumulation by the controlling shareholder.

This report moves the rating to HOLD from a prior sequence of AVOID calls. The change reflects evidence that the earlier decline was valuation compression off a post-Ukraine peak rather than structural impairment, and that the forward setup is a cyclical recovery: consensus current-quarter EPS estimates have risen +154.5% over 90 days and next-year estimates +60.4%, Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was already positive at +$37M (the GAAP loss being dominated by a $158M non-cash derivative mark and a $28M debt-extinguishment charge), and 2026 carries no refinery turnaround with capex guided down to $200-240M, which should drive a free-cash-flow inflection if cracks hold. Carl Icahn bought 263k, 245k, and 275k-share open-market blocks at $20.75-$21.41 in February 2026, the strongest insider conviction signal in the file.

The case against owning the stock is equally concrete and largely two-sided rather than clearly mispriced: the RFS/RIN regulatory regime is a multi-hundred-million-dollar binary that swung from a +$488M one-time gain in 2025 to a $204M liability by Q1 2026; the current crack strength is partly a geopolitical premium that compresses if Middle East transit normalizes; the stock trades at roughly 21x next-year EPS of $1.63 versus refining peers at 10-11x and sits at the top of the analyst range ($32 median, $35 high, $35.63 current); and the 71% control stake leaves minority holders without influence over timing or strategic outcomes. The balance of constructive cyclical signals against genuine binary downside, with no clean directional edge, supports a HOLD.

## Company Overview

CVR Energy operates two reportable segments after winding down its renewables unit in late 2025.

**Petroleum (Refining).** Coffeyville, KS (132,000 bpd, full coking, complex) and Wynnewood, OK (74,500 bpd) sell gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel into the Group 3 / PADD II market (Kansas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Missouri). The principal structural advantage is an owned crude gathering and logistics system supplying roughly 67% of Coffeyville and 94% of Wynnewood crude, which lowers transportation cost relative to peers buying at posted hubs. Competitors include CHS McPherson, HF Sinclair (El Dorado/Tulsa), Phillips 66 (Ponca), and Valero (Ardmore).

**Nitrogen Fertilizer (CVR Partners LP).** CVI owns ~37% of LP units plus 100% of the general partner and consolidates the entity as a variable interest entity. Two plants, Coffeyville, KS (the only pet-coke gasification-fed nitrogen plant in North America) and East Dubuque, IL (natural-gas-fed), produce UAN (~67% of fertilizer sales) and ammonia (~24%). Competitors include CF Industries, Nutrien, Koch, and LSB.

**Renewables (exited).** The Wynnewood renewable diesel unit reverted to hydrocarbon service in December 2025 after the Biodiesel Blenders' Tax Credit expired 12/31/2024 and economics turned deeply negative. The exit produced $93M of accelerated depreciation in 2025 and removed a loss-making segment.

The most important structural fact about the equity is the ~71% Icahn/IEP control stake. Capital allocation, dividend timing, and any strategic transaction (refining roll-up, options around CVR Partners, or a take-private) are at the controlling shareholder's discretion.

## Financial Analysis

CVI is a textbook cyclical at or near trough earnings, with fertilizer now providing roughly half the earnings power.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10.9B | $9.25B | $7.61B | $7.16B |
| Net income to CVI | $463M | $769M | $7M | $27M |
| EPS | — | $7.65 | $0.06 | $0.27 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1,164M | $1,164M | $317M | $393M |

Earnings collapsed roughly 96% from the 2023 peak as crack spreads normalized. The 2025 net income of $27M was flattered by a one-time $488M favorable RFS/SRE adjustment; underlying adjusted EBITDA was $393M. The segment mix has inverted: in 2025, Fertilizer adjusted EBITDA ($211M) exceeded Petroleum adjusted EBITDA ($199M), with ammonia/UAN pricing up 22-27% year over year. Fertilizer is currently the stronger leg of the barbell.

The most recent quarter shows why headline EPS is nearly meaningless for this company. Q1 2026 reported a net loss of -$160M (CVI share -$192M, EPS -$1.91) versus -$105M a year prior. The drivers were a $158M unrealized derivative loss on crack swaps, a surge in RIN compliance cost (RIN $/bbl up 102% year over year to $9.46; net RINs expense $143M, or $7.37/bbl, cutting capture roughly 34%), and a $28M debt-extinguishment loss. Crude utilization rebounded to 96.8% from 52.7% in the year-ago turnaround quarter, and the NYMEX 2-1-1 crack averaged $36.84 versus $22.64. Adjusted EBITDA was positive at +$37M; the GAAP loss is heavily non-cash mark-to-market.

The balance sheet is leveraged but liquid. Cash was $512M and total liquidity ~$1.1B (post-ABL upsize) against long-term debt of ~$1.72B and equity of $538M at 3/31/2026. The accumulated deficit of -$969M reflects years of distributions, heavily to IEP, in excess of earnings. Altman Z-Score of 2.5 sits in the grey zone, D/E is ~5x, and the current ratio is 1.79. The depressed Z-score is a function of thin EBIT at the cycle trough, not liquidity distress. The February 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031/2034 but raised coupons to 7.50% and 7.875% from retired 5.75%/8.50% paper, lifting run-rate interest expense.

Cash flow is the cleanest forward signal. 2025 FCF was -$41M in a turnaround-heavy year (~$190M of capitalized turnaround). 2026 capex is guided to $200-240M with no refinery turnaround (Wynnewood's was pushed to 2027), so FCF should improve materially if cracks hold. The Board reinstated a token $0.10/share dividend ($10M; IEP receives $7M) after suspending it in October 2024, signaling that management and IEP see improving cash-flow visibility, though it is a fraction of the $1.50/quarter plus specials paid in 2023-24.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is cyclical recovery, not secular expansion, and the forward indicators are genuinely strong on that basis.

Estimate revisions are the most actionable data point. Consensus current-quarter EPS has risen to $0.99 from $0.39 ninety days ago (+154.5%) and from $0.90 thirty days ago (+9.4%). Next-year EPS has risen to $1.63 from $1.02 ninety days ago (+60.4%) and from $0.98 thirty days ago (+66.3%). These are large upward revisions consistent with cycle normalization rather than a permanent step-up. The current-year figure is noisier (-35.2% over 30 days) because it absorbs the Q1 GAAP loss, but the forward-quarter and next-year trajectory is clearly positive.

The earnings surprise history reinforces that this is volatility, not a clean trend: two beats and two misses over four quarters, with a -210.8% Q1 2026 miss driven by the derivative mark and RIN spike. Visibility is low because RIN mark-to-market and inventory FIFO swings dominate the reported numbers.

Underlying operational drivers support a 2026 recovery if conditions hold. Refining utilization has returned to ~97%. Fertilizer is benefiting from tight nitrogen inventories and elevated ammonia/UAN prices. The Coffeyville plant's potential dual-feedstock (pet coke plus natural gas) project, a 2026 initiative, would be unique among U.S. nitrogen plants and add cost optionality. The caveat that determines whether this growth is durable: a meaningful share of current crack strength reflects a geopolitical disruption premium tied to Middle East and Russian refinery outages, which compresses if transit normalizes.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings, CVI is not cheap. At $34.92 against next-year EPS of $1.63, the stock trades at roughly 21x forward earnings. Refining peers trade materially lower: peer P/E median is 10.7, with PARR at 6.2, PBF at 10.7, DINO at 10.6, and REX at 16.7. On an enterprise basis, ~$3.5B equity plus ~$1.72B debt less ~$0.5B cash gives EV of roughly $4.7B, or about 12x the 2025 adjusted EBITDA, rich for a subscale refiner with no geographic diversification.

The stock sits at the top of the sell-side range: mean target $31.67, median $32.00, high $35.00, against a $35.63 reference price. Goldman Sachs initiated coverage in April 2026 with a Sell and a $30 price target. Analyst recommendation distribution is bearish (3 hold, 2 sell, 2 strong sell, zero buys).

I do not treat the depressed peer-relative starting point as a reason to short the recovery. The decline from the 2023 peak was multiple compression as cracks normalized, not structural impairment, and valuation-compression bear cases on cyclicals that are inflecting upward are a common error. At the same time, this is not a deeply mispriced asymmetric setup. The forward multiple is full versus peers; the normalized earnings power embeds crack spreads that are partly geopolitically contingent; and the upside optionality (SRE/RFS windfall, crack persistence, an Icahn-driven corporate action) is offset by symmetric downside on the same levers. Fairly to fully valued, with a wide outcome distribution, supports a HOLD rather than a directional call in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

CVI occupies a defensible niche without scale. Its owned crude gathering and logistics network is a genuine cost moat in the Group 3 market. Its fertilizer plants sit at the low end of the global nitrogen cost curve, and the Coffeyville pet-coke gasification plant is unique in North America.

The structural backdrop for refining is supportive: U.S. operable refining capacity has declined on a net basis since 2020, few new global refineries are scheduled, Russian refinery outages and Middle East infrastructure damage have tightened product supply, and cheap U.S. natural gas gives domestic refiners a durable cost edge over Europe. Diesel cracks have been elevated into 2026.

The offsetting weakness is scale and concentration. Two adjacent mid-continent refineries with no geographic diversification leave CVI fully exposed to Group 3 cracks and the RIN/RFS regime, with no upstream hedge against margin compression, versus Valero, Marathon, and HF Sinclair. Competitive trajectory is mixed: the gathering moat and the strengthening fertilizer leg are positives, but the company is a price-taker on the variable that matters most.

## Risk Assessment

**RFS / RIN compliance is the dominant and most binary risk.** The RFS liability ballooned from $72M (12/31/2025) to $204M (3/31/2026) as RIN prices spiked. CVI accrues at 100% of obligation while Wynnewood (a "small refinery" under 75k bpd) petitions for exemptions. The August 2025 SRE decisions produced the one-time gain; the 2025 SRE petition remains unruled-on by EPA past its legal deadline; and the March 2026 RFS Final Rule mandates record biomass-diesel volumes plus 70% reallocation of exempted volumes (~2.89B RINs). Outcomes could swing earnings by hundreds of millions in either direction. The E15 Council legislative process could narrow small-refinery SRE eligibility for affiliated refiners like CVI, a company-specific downside. This is a coin-flip on regulation, and it is the single largest source of variance.

**Crack-spread cyclicality and geopolitical contingency.** Current crack strength is partly a disruption premium. A normalization of Middle East transit or de-escalation would compress Group 3 cracks and the fertilizer pricing tailwind, removing much of the forward earnings recovery the rising estimates embed.

**Controlling-shareholder governance.** IEP's ~71% stake means minority holders are passengers on any strategic transaction, dividend decision, or take-private. The -$969M accumulated deficit is evidence that historical capital allocation prioritized the controlling shareholder.

**Leverage and refinancing cost.** The February 2026 refinancing locked maturities to 2031/2034 but at higher coupons, raising interest expense in a period of cyclical earnings volatility.

**Litigation overhang.** October 2025 Coffeyville ammonia-release lawsuits, a Kansas environmental class action (medical monitoring), the ExxonMobil 1993 guaranty dispute, and Call Option insurance-coverage litigation. A USDA/DOJ antitrust MOU (September 2025) scrutinizes fertilizer pricing. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 61.8% versus 51.5% one-year historical volatility, modestly rich, with the implied move on the nearest expiry at ±10.6%. The term structure is flat (1-month 61.8% ≈ 6-month 61.1%), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly across time rather than concentrated at a single dated catalyst, consistent with an unscheduled binary (the RFS/SRE outcome) rather than an earnings event. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.9%), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.67 is call-tilted, not the structure of a market braced for a directional decline. The options market is not pricing a clean directional short, which argues against a high-conviction negative call. The 20.4% short interest with 6.1 days to cover creates squeeze risk into any positive RFS or strategic-action surprise, a further reason the negative thesis is already heavily expressed in positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The cyclical trough is confirmed and the recovery is underway: rising forward estimates, positive Q1 adjusted EBITDA despite the GAAP loss, and a 2026 free-cash-flow inflection on no turnaround and lower capex. The controlling shareholder is buying aggressively in the open market roughly 40% below the current price, and the recurring "strategic transactions" language hints at potential corporate action (refining roll-up or CVR Partners restructuring) that could surface value. Refining capacity has structurally declined, cracks are elevated, and the fertilizer leg is at a cyclical high. A favorable resolution of the overdue Wynnewood SRE petition would add a large one-time benefit. Re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range and continued crack strength point to roughly $41 (the 52-week high), about +17% from current levels.

**Bear Case.** The crack premium is geopolitical and transient; de-escalation of Middle East transit compresses Group 3 cracks and fertilizer pricing, collapsing the estimate recovery. An adverse RFS/RIN ruling or denial of the Wynnewood exemption converts the $204M liability into realized expense and removes the windfall optionality. Higher refinancing coupons drag run-rate earnings, the 71% control overhang caps the multiple, and the stock derates toward the $30 sell-side target (-14%) or, in a sharper crack reversal, toward the $19-20 February lows (-43% to -45%).

The two cases are roughly balanced. The same RFS lever and the same crack spreads drive both, and the controlling-shareholder accumulation neutralizes much of the directional negative.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next reassessment points are the EPA's overdue ruling on Wynnewood's 2025 SRE petition (no fixed date, but past its legal deadline), the next quarterly earnings release, and any disclosure of a strategic transaction. The flat options term structure suggests the market expects the binary to resolve at no single scheduled date.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY).**
- Confirmation that mid-cycle adjusted EBITDA is settling near or above $393M with the 2026 FCF inflection materializing (positive FCF on the $200-240M capex base), at a forward multiple that compresses toward the 10-13x peer range as estimates rise.
- A favorable resolution of the Wynnewood SRE petition or a 2026 RFS outcome that converts the $204M liability into a benefit rather than realized expense.
- A concrete, value-accretive strategic transaction (refining roll-up or CVR Partners restructuring) on terms favorable to minority holders.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
- A sustained reversal in Group 3 cracks (2-1-1 falling back toward the $20-22 area) driven by Middle East/Russia de-escalation, with forward estimates turning down.
- An adverse RFS/RIN ruling, denial of the small-refinery exemption or an E15 Council outcome narrowing affiliated-refiner eligibility, that crystallizes the compliance liability.
- Evidence of fundamental deterioration beyond the cycle: utilization or capture structurally falling, liquidity tightening below the ~$1.1B level, or a covenant/refinancing problem.

## Conclusion

CVR Energy is a leveraged, 71%-controlled refining-plus-fertilizer barbell coming off trough earnings. The constructive evidence is real and has accumulated since the prior reviews: forward estimates have risen sharply, Q1 adjusted EBITDA is already positive, the 2026 turnaround-free year should inflect free cash flow, and Carl Icahn, the controlling shareholder with the most information, has been buying aggressively in the open market at prices roughly 40% below current levels. The prior decline was multiple compression off a post-Ukraine peak, not structural impairment.

Those positives are offset by a genuinely two-sided book. The RFS/SRE regime is a multi-hundred-million-dollar binary that has whipsawed earnings in both directions within twelve months; current crack strength is partly a geopolitical premium that compresses on de-escalation; the stock is full at ~21x forward versus peers at 10-11x and sits at the top of the analyst range; and the 71% control stake leaves minority holders without influence. The options market confirms the absence of a clean directional edge, and the heavy short interest means the negative thesis is already widely expressed. With balanced bull (+17%) and bear (-14% to -45%) cases anchored to the same uncertain levers, and no differentiated insight that the market is mispricing one side, the call is HOLD. I would become a buyer on confirmation of the FCF inflection and SRE resolution at a peer-relative multiple, and a seller on a crack reversal or adverse RFS ruling that the recovery thesis cannot survive.
