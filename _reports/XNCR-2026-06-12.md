**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Xencor is a clinical-stage antibody-engineering company built around its XmAb protein platform, with two value engines: a royalty and milestone annuity from three already-marketed partnered drugs (Ultomiris, Monjuvi/Minjuvi, and the Zenas-licensed obexelimab), and a wholly-owned five-program clinical pipeline now reorienting toward autoimmune disease. At ~$12.14 the equity is backed by roughly $6/share of net cash (after the OMERS royalty-monetization liability) plus a $60.6M Zenas equity stake, meaning the market currently prices the entire platform and clinical pipeline at approximately $6/share. The setup is a clinical biotech trading near a cash-plus-optionality floor.

The investment debate of the last quarter has centered on three negatives that have all now been disclosed: the March 4, 2026 notice from Alexion (AstraZeneca) that it will not pay additional U.S. Ultomiris royalties, the April guidance cut that followed, and the headline Q1 net loss of $(1.71)/share reported May 6. The stock fell from a December 2025 high of $18.69 to a $11-13 range and has held there, posting a +27% net move across the most recent trading window even as the bad news printed. That price behavior argues the known negatives are substantially absorbed rather than freshly breaking. The Q1 "miss" of -117.5% versus consensus is overwhelmingly a $(51.9)M non-cash mark-to-market on the Zenas stake, an item analysts cannot model and which says nothing about operations.

The case is two-sided rather than directional. The Alexion dispute threatens Xencor's single largest royalty stream with an unknowable outcome, a 2025 financial restatement and auditor change remain a governance flag, the autoimmune pivot points into the most crowded fields in biopharma, and a fresh shelf plus ATM signal dilution risk at depressed prices. Offsetting these are >$540M of liquidity, runway into 2028, no traditional debt, a validated platform (three marketed drugs), and a near-term catalyst calendar, the most concrete of which is the May 28, 2026 Zenas obexelimab BLA submission resting on a Phase 3 trial (INDIGO) that already met its primary endpoint. Downside is bounded by cash; upside is binary and event-driven. We rate the shares HOLD with an uncertain rationale, and we name an explicit constructive threshold below.

## Company Overview

Xencor (Pasadena, CA; ~74.1M shares; ~$900M market cap) engineers antibodies rather than discovering novel targets. Its edge is the XmAb toolbox of Fc-domain and bispecific modifications that confer extended half-life (Xtend), enhanced cytotoxicity, T-cell engagement (the 2+1 format), and conditional CD28 co-stimulation. The company runs an explicit advance/partner/discontinue portfolio model.

The two value engines:

**Partnered, marketed-drug annuity.** Ultomiris (ravulizumab, Alexion/AstraZeneca) uses Xtend technology and generates Xencor's largest royalty stream at a low-single-digit rate. Monjuvi/Minjuvi (tafasitamab, Incyte) was created and initially developed by Xencor; it carries up to $195M in milestones plus royalties and recently expanded into follicular lymphoma (approved June 2025) with a first-line DLBCL sBLA expected in 1H 2026. Obexelimab (Zenas BioPharma), another Xencor-originated molecule, is partnered out, and Xencor additionally holds 3,098,380 Zenas shares.

**Wholly-owned pipeline (5 programs enrolling).** Oncology bispecifics: XmAb819 (ENPP3×CD3, renal cell), XmAb541 (CLDN6×CD3, ovarian/germ cell), XmAb808 (B7-H3×CD28). Autoimmune (the strategic priority): XmAb942 (Xtend anti-TL1A for IBD/ulcerative colitis, the lead value driver), plamotamab (CD20×CD3 for RA), and XmAb657 (CD19×CD3 for myositis). A new XmAb412 (TL1A×IL-23p19) program and a "XenLock" bispecific format were unveiled in 2026.

A structural feature affects how the annuity should be valued: in November 2023 Xencor sold future Ultomiris and Monjuvi royalties to OMERS for $215M upfront, accounted for as debt ($108.5M liability at Q1 2026, at effective interest rates of 15.6%-16.8%). Royalty revenue appears on the P&L but the cash largely flows directly to OMERS until contractual caps are met. The reported royalty line is therefore non-cash, and the annuity is partly mortgaged.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue is lumpy and milestone-driven. Q1 2026 total revenue was $4.5M versus $32.7M in Q1 2025, with the decline reflecting the absence of a $14.5M Incyte milestone booked a year earlier and a $6.6M negative royalty adjustment tied to the Alexion dispute. Q1 2026 components were $4.4M royalties (non-cash), $0.1M license, and zero milestone revenue.

The headline loss is dominated by a non-cash item. Q1 2026 net loss was $(128.9)M, or $(1.71)/share, against $(48.4)M a year prior. The driver was a $(51.9)M unrealized loss on the Zenas equity stake (marked from $112.5M at YE2025 to $60.6M), a ~46% single-quarter markdown that flows through other income. Stripping that mark, the operating loss was $(77.9)M versus $(43.2)M in Q1 2025. The reported -117.5% earnings "miss" relative to a $(0.79) consensus is almost entirely this mark; analysts do not forecast quarter-to-quarter movements in a private-stage equity position, so the miss should not be read as operational deterioration. The prior three quarters all beat (44%, 89%, 85%), consistent with milestone timing rather than a broken franchise.

R&D is rising and reallocating. Q1 2026 R&D was $64.7M (up from $58.6M), with spend shifting toward XmAb942 ($7.4M), XmAb819 ($8.9M), and the new XmAb412 ($7.1M from zero), while XmAb657, plamotamab, and XmAb808 declined, consistent with active pruning. A $4.9M impairment was recorded in Q1 2025 for paused programs.

The balance sheet is the load-bearing fact. Xencor held $541.8M in cash and marketable securities at Q1 2026 (down from $610.8M at YE2025), plus the $60.6M Zenas stake, with runway guided into 2028 and no traditional debt beyond the OMERS royalty liability. Quarterly operating cash burn accelerated to $(67.6)M from $(16.2)M (the prior-year figure flattered by large milestone collections). A $200M+ universal shelf became effective February 25, 2026, and a $200M ATM with Leerink remains undrawn, both keeping dilution options open at a time when the stock sits near 52-week lows.

Net cash math: $541.8M liquidity plus the $60.6M Zenas stake, less the $108.5M OMERS liability, is roughly $494M, or about $6.66/share against zero traditional debt; a more conservative treatment excluding the Zenas stake yields roughly $5.85/share. Either figure means roughly half the share price is backed by cash and securities, and the market ascribes approximately $6/share to the platform plus the entire wholly-owned pipeline.

## Growth Analysis

There is no clean growth story here, and the falling estimate trend reflects that. Consensus current-year EPS has moved from $(2.62) ninety days ago to $(3.99), and next-year from $(2.31) to $(3.56), declines of -52.4% and -54.3% respectively. These are net-loss estimates for a pre-commercial developer, and a large share of the revision is the mechanical pull-through of the Zenas markdown into modeled other income rather than a deterioration in operating prospects. They do not constitute the positive revision momentum that would support a constructive growth thesis, but they also should not be read with the same weight one would apply to a revenue-generating company's earnings cuts.

Forward value is concentrated in a small number of binary events. The lead wholly-owned driver, XmAb942 (anti-TL1A), produced Phase 1 data in May 2026 showing a 74-day half-life consistent with potential 12-week dosing, a plausible differentiator in a validated field, but the Phase 2b XENITH-UC induction readout is not expected until 2027. Near-term incremental milestones could come from the Incyte first-line DLBCL sBLA (1H 2026) and the Zenas obexelimab BLA filed May 28, 2026, which would trigger regulatory milestones and re-rate the value of the 3.1M Zenas shares if it advances to approval. The growth profile is optionality stacked on a 2026-2027 catalyst calendar, not a forecastable revenue ramp.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative and not meaningful for a clinical-stage biotech, so the valuation has to be assessed against net asset value plus pipeline optionality. The peer table underlines the futility of multiple-based comparison: Biotechnology comparables show P/Es of -1.1 (MYGN), -2.8 (VIR), -3.3 (FTRE), 24.7 (SRPT), and 86.1 (VCEL), a heterogeneous set with no usable median.

The relevant anchor is the cash floor. At $12.14 against roughly $5.85-6.66/share of net cash, the market values the platform, the five-program pipeline, and the partnered royalty/milestone economics at approximately $6/share, or ~$445M. For a company whose platform has produced three marketed medicines and whose partnered obexelimab is at the BLA stage, that is a modest ascription rather than a stretched one. Sell-side coverage corroborates a floor: 12 of 14 analysts rate the shares Buy/Strong Buy, the lowest target ($14) sits above the current price, and the mean is $28.5. We treat the low target as a floor and the mean as aspirational optionality rather than a base case, given the binary clinical and litigation risks.

The asymmetry is two-sided, which is why the valuation supports a hold rather than either a buy or an avoid. On the downside, an adverse Alexion outcome, a further Zenas markdown, and dilution via the shelf/ATM could compress the stock toward the cash floor near $8 (roughly -34%). On the upside, obexelimab approval, a favorable Merus IPR outcome, and positive XmAb942 progression could support re-rating into the $14-20 range. We do not assign the valuation a higher score because the negative estimate revisions, the disputed largest royalty stream, and the dilution overhang cap how much of the optionality can be confidently capitalized today.

## Competitive Landscape

Xencor occupies a hybrid position: a platform-licensing royalty company and a clinical developer. The platform side is validated by three marketed drugs using XmAb technology, and historically supplied non-dilutive capital. The Alexion dispute exposes the fragility of relying on partner goodwill and patent-term interpretation to sustain that annuity; a counterparty repudiating a royalty obligation despite a patent-term extension is a competitive-position risk distinct from clinical failure.

On the wholly-owned side, the autoimmune pivot points into the most contested arenas in biopharma. The TL1A field (XmAb942) pits Xencor against Merck/Prometheus (tulisokibart), Roche/Roivant, and Sanofi/Teva, all far better capitalized; the differentiation bet is dosing frequency via extended half-life, plausible but unproven through Phase 2b. B-cell depletion (CD19/CD20) is validated by the obexelimab INDIGO win and the broader autoimmune CAR-T wave but is similarly crowded. The oncology bispecifics are earlier-stage and face the well-documented efficacy/toxicity-window challenges of solid-tumor T-cell engagers. The trajectory is mixed: platform validation is a real moat input, while the pipeline reorientation moves Xencor into fields where capital depth matters and incumbents are entrenched.

## Risk Assessment

**Alexion/Ultomiris royalty dispute (dominant, new).** On March 4, 2026 Alexion notified Xencor it does not believe it owes additional U.S. Ultomiris royalties and will not pay, contradicting a December 2025 patent-term extension that should have run royalties into December 2028. Xencor constrained disputed U.S. royalties to zero under ASC 606, booked a $6.6M reversal, and cut guidance in April. Because these royalties were sold to OMERS, the dispute also complicates amortization of a 16%+ effective-rate liability. Outcome and timing are unknowable. This risk is partly priced (it drove the stock from $18.69 toward $12 and the equity has since stabilized), but the ultimate resolution is not.

**Merus patent litigation.** Merus alleges Xencor's manufacturing infringes its patents. Xencor won a motion to dismiss on safe-harbor grounds (September 30, 2025), Merus filed an amended complaint adding a fourth patent (November 2025), and a renewed motion was heard February 17, 2026. Xencor's IPR challenging two Merus patents has an oral argument set for June 24, 2026.

**Financial-reporting quality.** A February 2025 Item 4.02 non-reliance filing forced restatement of 2023 and 2024 interim periods, followed by an auditor change (March 2025). Management states the weakness is remediated and controls were effective as of March 31, 2026, but the filing flags risk of additional weaknesses. This governance red flag weighs on the management and risk scores.

**Binary clinical risk in XmAb942.** The autoimmune thesis rests heavily on the TL1A asset, whose Phase 2b UC induction readout is not due until 2027. Failure would gut the wholly-owned narrative.

**Dilution.** Runway into 2028 with accelerating burn, a fresh shelf, and an undrawn ATM at a stock near lows make a depressed-price raise a realistic possibility.

**Zenas stake volatility.** The 3.1M-share position swung the headline loss by $51.9M in one quarter; it can re-rate sharply in either direction on obexelimab news.

### Options Market Signal

The options dataset shows the put/call open-interest ratio collapsing from 0.58 to 0.01 between May 8 and June 12, alongside a -6.2% price move. The near-zero ratio reflects thin, illiquid single-name options open interest rather than a clean directional signal; it should not be over-read. The more informative positioning is in the cash market: short interest of 20.9% of float with 14.6 days to cover. That level of crowded short positioning into a dense binary-catalyst calendar (obexelimab BLA, Merus IPR June 24, Incyte sBLA) creates two-sided squeeze risk that argues against initiating a short and tempers conviction on the downside case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The shares are backed by ~$6/share of net cash with runway into 2028, so the entire platform and pipeline are valued at roughly $6/share. The known negatives (Alexion repudiation, April guidance cut, Q1 headline loss) have all printed and the stock has held $11-13, suggesting absorption. The Q1 "miss" is a non-cash Zenas mark, not operational decay. A partly de-risked catalyst slate runs near term: the May 28 obexelimab BLA rests on a Phase 3 trial that already met its primary endpoint, the Incyte first-line DLBCL sBLA is due in 1H 2026, and a favorable Merus IPR outcome (oral argument June 24) removes a litigation overhang. With 12 of 14 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and the lowest target ($14) above spot, modest positive resolution of any single thread re-rates the equity toward $14-20. Bull case: $20 (+65%).

### Bear Case

The Alexion dispute resolves against Xencor, permanently impairing the largest royalty stream and complicating the OMERS arrangement. XmAb942 disappoints or the field's deeper-pocketed incumbents win share, while burn at ~$68M/quarter forces a dilutive raise at depressed prices off the new shelf/ATM. The Zenas stake takes a further markdown. The restatement legacy resurfaces. The equity drifts toward the cash floor near $8 (-34%).

The base case sits between these: the stock oscillates with binary catalyst outcomes around a cash-anchored level, with no confident directional edge. The downside is bounded by net cash; the upside requires specific events to clear. This supports an uncertain hold rather than a buy or an avoid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Merus IPR oral argument (June 24, 2026), the Incyte first-line DLBCL sBLA decision/filing progress (1H 2026), obexelimab BLA acceptance and any associated Zenas milestone, and the next quarterly report. The XmAb942 Phase 2b UC induction readout (2027) is the longer-dated swing factor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) A favorable or settled Alexion outcome that restores or monetizes the U.S. Ultomiris royalty, removing the dispute overhang; (2) FDA acceptance of the obexelimab BLA accompanied by a milestone payment and a recovery in the marked value of the Zenas stake; (3) positive estimate-revision inflection on operating (ex-mark) lines combined with a price near or below the ~$9-10 level, which would push the implied value of the platform plus pipeline back toward the cash floor and tilt risk/reward to ≥2:1. A constructive entry threshold is roughly $9.50 or below absent fundamental deterioration; at that level the market would be paying close to zero for the platform and pipeline, an asymmetry that would justify a long.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) An adverse Alexion ruling combined with an announced equity raise off the shelf/ATM at a discount, which would simultaneously impair the annuity and dilute the cash floor; (2) XmAb942 Phase 2b failure or a clinical hold on a lead program; (3) re-emergence of a material weakness or restatement, which would compound the existing governance flag and break the case that the cash floor is reliable.

## Conclusion

Xencor is a clinical-stage biotech trading near a cash-plus-optionality floor. Roughly half the $12.14 share price is net cash, the company has runway into 2028 with no traditional debt, and the platform has produced three marketed drugs. The bearish drivers (the Alexion royalty repudiation, the April guidance cut, and the $(1.71) Q1 loss) have all been disclosed, and the equity has stabilized in the $11-13 range, with the headline loss overwhelmingly attributable to a non-cash Zenas mark rather than operational decay. A short position is not warranted: 20.9% short interest, a cash floor, and a dense calendar of positive binary catalysts create real squeeze risk, and our own analysis finds the downside small relative to the catalyst-driven upside.

At the same time, the case for owning the stock is not yet made. Estimate revisions are falling, the largest royalty stream is in genuine dispute with an unknowable outcome, the autoimmune pivot lands in crowded fields against better-capitalized rivals, dilution at depressed prices is a live risk, and a 2025 restatement plus auditor change keep a governance flag open. With downside bounded by cash and upside dependent on binary events, neither a BUY nor an AVOID is supportable on the current evidence. We rate XNCR **HOLD (uncertain)**, and would turn constructive on a pullback toward ~$9.50 or below absent fundamental deterioration, or on favorable resolution of the Alexion dispute or obexelimab regulatory milestones.
