**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lennox International is a North American pure-play HVACR manufacturer that has executed one of the more impressive margin transformations in industrials: operating margin expanded from 13.9% in 2022 to 20.0% in 2025, and gross margin from 27.2% to 33.4%, on a revenue base that declined in 2025. The mechanism behind that expansion is stated openly by management: industry-wide, disciplined price increases through the R-410A-to-R-454B refrigerant transition, layered on top of a 2023 exit from lower-margin European operations. The residential direct-to-dealer model, the replacement-heavy revenue mix, and the consolidated oligopoly structure of the North American HVAC market are all genuine, durable competitive advantages.

The same pricing story that drove the margins is now the subject of litigation. In March 2026, Lennox and six other HVAC manufacturers were named in a federal antitrust class action alleging coordinated price-fixing from 2020 to present. Management cannot yet estimate a loss range and concedes an adverse outcome "could have a material adverse impact." This sits on top of a residential business that is contracting sharply, and a 2026 outlook that depends on an unproven second-half replacement recovery that management admits is contingent on consumer confidence that "doesn't seem to be improving."

We maintain a HOLD. At $537.89, the stock trades near 22x consensus 2026 EPS of $24.46, modestly below the peer median of 24.8x, offering limited valuation cushion for the twin overhangs of an unquantified antitrust liability and a residential business whose recovery is unconfirmed. The score trajectory has declined from 6.7 in May to 5.6 in June, driven by deteriorating competitive-position, management-quality, and risk marks; the current data confirms rather than reverses that direction. This is an uncertain hold: a high-quality franchise at a full price with a specific, freshly-surfaced legal risk directly linked to the source of its earnings power.

## Company Overview

Lennox operates two segments in North American heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration:

- **Home Comfort Solutions (~64% of 2025 revenue, $3.34B):** Residential furnaces, air conditioners, heat pumps, packaged systems, indoor-air-quality equipment, and replacement parts. The distinguishing feature is a direct-to-dealer model: roughly 75% of Q1 2026 residential revenue goes directly to independent installing dealers, partly through company-owned Lennox stores. This creates a large installed base and durable pull-through parts and service revenue.
- **Building Climate Solutions (~36%, $1.85B):** Light commercial rooftop units, VRF, commercial heat pumps, and commercial refrigeration (Heatcraft), plus a growing installation and service business serving national accounts and emergency replacement.

After divesting its European commercial HVAC and refrigeration businesses in Q4 2023, Lennox is now roughly 93% U.S.-domiciled by revenue, with manufacturing concentrated in the U.S. and Mexico. The strategic thesis is deliberate simplification into a higher-margin, replacement-driven North American model. Replacement demand is structurally less cyclical than new construction, which provides relative insulation from housing starts.

## Financial Analysis

**Margins and profitability are strong; the top line is soft.** Revenue moved $4.72B (2022) → $4.98B (2023) → $5.34B (2024) → $5.20B (2025, -3%). The 2025 decline conceals a divergence central to understanding the company: reported volumes fell 13%, but price/mix added 9% and the Duro Dyne/Supco acquisition added 1%. Net income was roughly flat at ~$806M against 2024's $811M, and diluted EPS edged up to $22.79 from $22.66. Operating margin held at 20.0%.

**ROE of 69% overstates fundamental returns.** That number reflects a deliberately thin equity base created by aggressive buybacks; the company carried negative book equity as recently as 2022 and now has only ~$1.16B of equity against ~$1.39B of total debt (D/E 2.5x). ROA of ~20% is the cleaner measure of underlying asset productivity and is genuinely strong for a building-products manufacturer.

**Cash flow is high-quality but dropped in 2025 on an intentional working-capital build.** Operating cash flow fell to $758M from $946M, driven by a $217M working-capital build; management characterizes roughly $200M of that as excess inventory positioned ahead of the 2026 all-R-454B selling season. FCF was ~$639M (12.3% FCF margin), still healthy. Capital allocation remained aggressive: $482M of buybacks plus $173M of dividends in 2025, with the dividend raised 13% to $1.30/quarter. Roughly $990M remains on the $5.0B repurchase authorization. CapEx is guided up sharply to ~$250M in 2026 from $119M in 2025.

**The Altman Z-score of 2.11 is an artifact of financial engineering, not distress.** Lennox is investment-grade (Baa1/BBB, stable), net leverage sits well inside the 3.5x covenant, and the current ratio is 1.6. The "grey zone" Z-score reflects the minimal equity base from cumulative buybacks. The thin liquidity cushion is the real observation: $34-48M of cash and rising reliance on commercial paper ($361M by Q1 2026 at ~4.2%) to fund both the acquisition and continued repurchases. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, that CP reliance is a modest, ongoing cost headwind rather than a solvency issue.

**Earnings track record and estimate revisions are constructive but muted.** Over the trailing four quarters, Lennox posted three beats and one miss, but the misses and beats have been small and the Q4 2025 print was a 7.2% shortfall. Estimate revisions are positive but marginal: current-year consensus rose +1.2% over 90 days to $24.46, next year +0.6% to $26.76. These are not the +30%-in-90-days revisions that flag a systematically underestimated earnings recovery. This is a stable, slow-grind estimate picture.

## Growth Analysis

The 2026 growth story is an unresolved bet on second-half residential recovery. Management guides to 6-7% total revenue growth in 2026, second-half weighted, split as Home Comfort ~+2% and Building Climate ~+15%. Q1 2026 was explicitly flagged as the weakest quarter, and it delivered: revenue +6% to $1.135B but operating income down to $163.5M and diluted EPS $3.35 versus $3.63 a year prior, with gross margin compressing 90bps on product-cost inflation and factory under-absorption.

The segment divergence is stark and worsening. In Q1 2026, Building Climate revenue rose 38% (profit +63%), aided by the acquisitions and by commercial emergency-replacement and national-account strength, while Home Comfort revenue fell 10% (profit -30%). The larger, historically higher-quality residential replacement business is contracting while commercial carries the company.

The core debate is the "deferred demand" thesis. Management argues that consumers are choosing repair over replacement, that repaired units return for replacement in 12-24 months, and that rising R-410A refrigerant costs plus efficiency savings will eventually push consumers back to replacement. This is plausible and consistent with the physics of an aging installed base, but it is unproven, and management itself concedes consumer confidence is not improving. If deferral persists rather than converting to pent-up demand, the ~$200M excess inventory becomes an absorption and margin risk rather than a well-timed bet on a strong summer season.

Structural tailwinds favor incumbents: rising minimum SEER standards, the refrigerant transition, and electrification/heat-pump adoption all reward scale and R&D. The Duro Dyne/Supco acquisition (~$554M, closed October 2025) deepens the higher-margin, recurring parts-and-supplies business and is strategically sound and margin-accretive. But the near-term growth print is contingent on a residential recovery the company cannot yet demonstrate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $537.89, Lennox trades at ~22x consensus 2026 EPS of $24.46 and ~20x the 2027 estimate of $26.76. The trailing P/E of 23.85x on $22.79 EPS is not distorted by trough earnings: 2025 was not a trough for EPS, only for volumes, and price/mix held margins up. The earnings are near peak-margin levels, not depressed, so this is not a cycle-adjusted valuation case.

Against the Building Products peer set, Lennox sits roughly at or modestly below the median. Peer P/Es: Masco 18.3x, Allegion 18.3x, WMS 24.8x, AAON 90.6x, Owens Corning negative. The peer median of 24.8x is skewed by AAON's outlier multiple; screening out AAON and OC, Lennox is priced at a premium to Masco and Allegion and in line with WMS. Lennox's superior margins and replacement mix justify some premium to the more construction-levered names, but there is no meaningful discount to exploit; the quality is fairly reflected in the price. The dividend yield of ~1.0% is below the peer median of 1.7% and provides limited support.

The valuation dimension warrants caution in the current environment, where cheap multiples have consistently marked value traps and full multiples on quality names have not offered protection when a specific overhang materializes. Lennox is neither cheap nor obviously expensive; it is a full price for a good business carrying an unquantified legal risk. At ~22x forward with the antitrust suit unreserved and residential volumes falling, there is little margin of safety on the multiple.

Analyst targets span $450 (low) to $651 (high), with a mean of $572.9 and median of $570. The stock at $538 sits ~6% below the mean target. On a stock with rising estimates and a cheap multiple, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors; here, with modest revision velocity and a full multiple, the targets are better read as a fair-value band, and the stock is trading inside it.

## Competitive Landscape

The North American HVAC OEM market is a consolidated oligopoly: Lennox, Carrier, Trane, Daikin/Goodman, Johnson Controls, Rheem, and Nortek. Barriers to entry are high in equipment, driven by regulatory complexity, brand, and distribution. Lennox's direct-to-dealer model and replacement-heavy mix are genuine competitive advantages, and its 20% operating margin is among the best in the group.

The trajectory is where the concern lies, and it is why the competitive-position score has declined. Two factors weigh. First, management's own candor: it has stated that competitors are "aiming at similar price increases" and that pricing discipline "remains consistent across the industry." This rational, coordinated pricing behavior is precisely what drove the margin expansion and precisely what is now the subject of the antitrust class action. If that pricing discipline is disrupted by litigation, discovery, or a settlement that changes conduct, the margin thesis erodes at its foundation. Second, the company itself flags that barriers to entry are low in parts, supplies, distribution, and commercial service, the very areas it is now buying into. Growth in those adjacencies is strategically correct but competitively less defensible than the equipment moat.

The residential share picture is muddied by the volume declines. Management attributes the drop to industry-wide destocking and repair-vs-replace deferral rather than share loss, and the price/mix gains support that read. But until residential volumes stabilize, it is difficult to confirm the moat is holding rather than the whole category shrinking.

## Risk Assessment

The risk-factors score of 4.5 reflects specific, material risks that are not fully priced into a ~22x multiple.

**Antitrust class action (the dominant risk).** On March 20, 2026, Lennox and six other manufacturers were named in *In re HVAC Equipment Antitrust Litigation* (E.D. Mich.), alleging a conspiracy to fix and raise HVAC equipment prices from January 2020 to present. Lennox states it cannot estimate the loss range and that an adverse outcome could be material. Two features make this acute: it is unreserved (no quantified provision), and it is directly connected to the pricing behavior that produced the margin expansion the entire investment case rests on. Antitrust class actions carry treble-damage exposure, multi-year timelines, and the possibility of conduct remedies that constrain future pricing. This is an asymmetric, unquantified overhang.

**Residential demand persistence.** Home Comfort volumes -17% (2025) and -21% (Q1 2026). The deferred-replacement thesis is plausible but unproven; if deferral continues, the intentional ~$200M inventory build converts from a well-positioned bet into an absorption and margin drag.

**Refrigerant transition execution.** 2026 is the first full year with R-410A residential equipment (~40% of 2025 mix) fully phased out. Pricing carryover supports mid-single-digit price/mix, but the transition has already created destocking and dealer-confidence disruption.

**Tariff and commodity exposure.** Steel, aluminum, and copper are core inputs. The Q1 2026 10-Q flags an evolving tariff regime: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling against IEEPA tariffs, a new 10% Section 122 global surcharge, and continuing Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs. USMCA review in 2026 is relevant given Mexican manufacturing.

**Governance items to monitor.** A March 2025 auditor change (Item 4.01), coinciding with the LIFO-to-FIFO accounting change (which added $106.6M to retained earnings as of January 2023), and a December 2025 Item 5.02 filing warrant continued attention for succession/governance context. Neither is a red flag on the current evidence, but the combination of an auditor change, a discretionary accounting change, and a relatively new management team merits watching. Insider activity is neutral-to-slightly-negative: no open-market purchases, routine RSU/PSU grants with associated tax-withholding sales, and small director/CAO sales.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 42.7% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 35.5% (a 7.2-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.7% at 1 month versus 35.6% at 6 months), which typically flags an imminent catalyst: here, the Q2 earnings print. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±9.7%. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (-0.7%) but steepens to -4.6% at the 6-month tenor, indicating some demand for longer-dated downside protection consistent with the antitrust and residential-demand overhangs. Short interest is moderate at 9.6% of float (6.7 days to cover): not a crowded short, but elevated enough to reflect genuine bearish conviction. The IV backwardation is a caution against adding size into the next earnings release rather than a directional signal on its own.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($620-651)
Residential replacement demand recovers in H2 2026 as management predicts: repaired units cycle back for replacement, rising R-410A refrigerant costs push deferring consumers to replace, and the ~$200M inventory build is monetized into a strong all-R-454B summer season. Building Climate continues its ~15% growth trajectory, aided by commercial demand and the Duro Dyne/Supco integration. Pricing discipline holds, margins stay near 20%, and the antitrust suit resolves without a material penalty or conduct remedy over a multi-year timeline. EPS reaches the upper end of guidance-driven estimates (~$25+), and the March 2026 Investor Day resets long-term targets favorably. At ~25x on $26 forward EPS, the stock re-rates toward $650.

### Bear Case ($430-450)
Residential deferral persists as consumer confidence fails to improve, volumes decline again, and the excess inventory becomes a factory-absorption and margin problem. The antitrust litigation advances through discovery, producing damaging disclosures about coordinated pricing and forcing a reserve or settlement; the market re-rates the durability of the 20% margin as pricing discipline comes under legal and competitive pressure. Tariff and commodity inflation compress margins further. EPS growth stalls, and the multiple compresses toward the low-20s or high-teens on peer-relative de-rating. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $434 and the low analyst target of $450, roughly 16-19% downside.

### Base Case
Building Climate carries modest total revenue growth to the guided 6-7%, residential stabilizes but does not sharply recover, margins hold near current levels, and the antitrust suit remains an unresolved, unquantified overhang that caps the multiple. EPS lands near consensus (~$24.50 in 2026, ~$26.75 in 2027). The stock trades in a $520-580 band, roughly flat-to-modestly-higher from current levels, awaiting resolution of the residential-demand question and clarity on the litigation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: a high-quality franchise at a full price with a specific unquantified legal risk directly tied to its earnings power. We lack the evidence for a stronger call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (the options market is pricing a ±9.7% move around it), which will provide the first read on whether the H2 residential recovery is materializing. The March 4, 2026 Investor Day has passed; any reset long-term targets from it should be factored in. Continued monitoring of the *In re HVAC Equipment Antitrust Litigation* docket is essential.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Home Comfort volumes stabilize or turn positive year-over-year for two consecutive quarters, confirming the deferred-replacement thesis is converting to demand.
- The antitrust litigation is dismissed, narrowed materially, or settled at a quantified, immaterial amount that removes the margin-durability overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$450s (near the low analyst target and 52-week low) that opens a genuine valuation cushion of 15%+ to base-case fair value while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Residential volumes decline a further double-digit percentage in Q2 2026 with no evidence of the pent-up demand recovering, forcing an inventory writedown or guidance cut.
- The antitrust suit produces a material reserve, an adverse class-certification ruling, or discovery disclosures that credibly threaten the industry's pricing discipline.
- Operating margin compresses below ~17% on sustained cost inflation, factory under-absorption, or competitive pricing erosion.

## Conclusion

Lennox is a well-run, high-margin North American HVACR pure-play with a defensible dealer model, a replacement-heavy revenue mix, and genuine regulatory tailwinds. But the investment case has a structural tension that has sharpened over the past two quarters: the margin expansion that made the stock attractive was built on industry-wide pricing discipline that is now the explicit subject of an unquantified antitrust class action, while the residential business that anchors the franchise is contracting hard and the 2026 recovery is a bet on consumer behavior management itself cannot confirm.

At ~22x forward earnings, modestly below the peer median and offering limited valuation cushion, the stock does not adequately compensate for these overhangs. The declining score trajectory reflects a franchise whose quality is intact but whose risk profile has deteriorated and whose price leaves little room for disappointment. We maintain **HOLD**. We would need either a stabilization in residential volumes plus clarity on the antitrust exposure, or a meaningful price decline that restores a margin of safety, to move constructive.
