**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegion is a pure-play security and access-control manufacturer with a genuinely strong North American franchise. The Americas segment (~79% of revenue) earns a 27.9% operating margin on spec-driven brands — Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN — that are written into building codes and architect specifications, creating real switching costs and durable pricing power. Company-wide returns are excellent: 31.1% ROE, 16.9% FCF margin, and steady net-income compounding from $483M (2021) to $644M (2025). On those metrics alone this looks like a high-quality compounder trading at a discount (~14.8x current-year consensus EPS of $8.77 versus a building-products peer median P/E near 28x).

The problem is the quality of the growth. The 7.8% headline revenue increase in 2025 decomposed into +3.1% price, +3.1% acquisitions, +1.0% volume, and +0.6% FX. Roughly 80% of the growth came from price and deals, not real demand. That trend deteriorated into 2026: Q1 total volume was –2.0% and International volume –6.6%. Q1 2026 produced the first year-over-year net-income decline in years ($138.1M vs $148.2M), with operating margin falling to 18.9% from 20.9% and the International segment margin collapsing to 3.7% from 6.3% on ELATEC integration costs and volume declines. The stock has fallen ~29% from its February high of $183 to ~$130, and consensus EPS revisions are drifting flat-to-lower.

The central debate is whether the Q1 margin compression is a transitory integration cost or the early signal of structural International deterioration in a business that has already booked $573.6M of accumulated goodwill impairment in that segment. The valuation discount is real but earned, and there is no clean near-term catalyst to close it. The balance of evidence supports HOLD. The franchise is too good and the price too undemanding to short; the volume trajectory and integration drag are too poor to call a turn. The next two quarters of earnings will resolve the central question, and the entry point improves if the stock approaches book value or if International margins inflect.

## Company Overview

Allegion is an Irish-domiciled (Dublin-incorporated for tax purposes), operationally US-centric provider of mechanical and electronic locks, locksets, door closers, exit devices, access-control systems, and related software/services. It was spun off from Ingersoll Rand in 2013. The business runs in two reportable segments:

- **Allegion Americas** ($3.22B in 2025): the economic engine. Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN, and Stanley Access Technologies (licensed from Stanley Black & Decker) anchor a leading non-residential institutional position in education, healthcare, and government. Segment operating margin of 27.9% is exceptional for an industrial.
- **Allegion International** ($848M in 2025): CISA, SimonsVoss, Interflex, AXA, Gainsborough, ELATEC across Europe/Asia/Oceania. Structurally lower margin (9.0%) and currently a drag.

The defining industry dynamic is the convergence of mechanical and electronic/IoT access control. Electronic products grew low-double-digits in Americas in 2025 and are management's stated long-term growth driver. This is also where competition is fiercest (Assa Abloy, dormakaba, plus non-traditional tech entrants) and where Allegion's recent M&A — notably the July 2025 ELATEC acquisition (German RFID/credential reader modules) — has been aimed.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins (FY2025).** Revenue $4.07B, +7.8% YoY. Operating income $859.5M, operating margin 21.1% (up from 20.7%). Net income $643.8M, net margin 15.8%. Diluted EPS $7.44 vs $6.82. ROA 12.3%.

**Cash generation.** 2025 operating cash flow $783.8M; FCF $685.7M on only ~$98M of capex. This is a low-capital-intensity model that throws off cash, which funds both M&A and shareholder returns.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt ~$2.0B; net debt ~$1.65B, ~88–90% fixed-rate, current ratio 1.84. The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 ("grey zone") is a false distress signal here: it reflects a goodwill/intangible-heavy, M&A-funded balance sheet ($1.93B goodwill plus $0.83B intangibles, ~53% of assets), not operational stress. With 31% ROE, 103%-funded pensions, fixed-rate debt, and an upsized $1.0B revolver (maturity 2030), solvency is not a question.

**The live problem — Q1 2026.** The drivers of the net-income decline were acquisition/integration/restructuring expense, unfavorable volume/mix, and FX. The effective tax rate also moved against the company, rising to 16.2% in 2025 (from 14.5%) and hitting 19.5% in Q1 2026.

**Earnings delivery.** The recent surprise record has turned: Q1 2026 missed by 6.9% ($1.80 vs $1.93), Q4 2025 missed by 4.2%, after two narrow beats in mid-2025. Two misses in four quarters, with both misses most recent, reduces confidence in the forward path and is consistent with the volume softness in the operating data.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth masks a stagnant-to-shrinking unit base. The 2025 revenue bridge was ~half price/FX and ~40% acquisitions, with only +1.0% from volume. Into 2026 the volume number turned negative outright, and residential Americas volume declined in 2025.

This matters because price and M&A are finite levers. Pricing has fully offset tariff inflation so far, but it is being achieved at the cost of volume — exactly the dynamic that suppresses real demand if customers push back. The acquisition lever (ELATEC, DCI, Next Door, Lemaar, ~$631.6M deployed in 2025 plus more in 2026) adds revenue but is currently diluting margin during integration and concentrating goodwill in a segment with a poor M&A track record.

Consensus reflects the ambivalence. Current-year EPS sits at $8.77, revised down 0.4% (30d) and 1.0% (90d). Next-year sits at $9.54, essentially flat (+0.3% / –0.4%). Current-quarter estimates were cut 3.1%, partially offset by a +1.1%/+4.6% next-quarter revision. There is no positive revision velocity here — the +30% inflection signal that would justify aggressive forward weighting is entirely absent. This is a low-single-digit EPS grower with a quality core and a volume problem, not a re-accelerating franchise.

The one structural growth vector worth crediting is electronic access control, which grew low-double-digits in Americas in 2025. If electronics scales and ELATEC integration costs fade, there is a path to mid-single-digit organic growth. That path is unproven, and the burden of proof now sits with management given the Q1 print.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$130, ALLE trades at ~17.8x trailing EPS of $7.44 and ~14.8x current-year consensus of $8.77. Against a building-products peer median P/E of 28.3x (AAON 98.7, WMS 25.5, MAS 17.4, WTS 28.3; OC negative on a charge), the stock is optically cheap — roughly half the group multiple.

The discount is earned, not a mispricing. The peers carry either faster volume growth or cleaner organic trajectories. Allegion's own five-year total shareholder return (147 indexed) has trailed its capital-goods peer group (220), with the gap widening every year. The market has consistently discounted the same thing visible in the operating data: weak organic volume, reliance on price/M&A to manufacture growth, and an International segment that is a structural laggard.

On forward earnings, the analyst target range is $150 (BofA, Neutral, cut from $157 on April 29) to $189, with a $165 mean. The low end implies ~15% upside; the mean ~27%. But these targets predate full visibility on whether International margin compression is transitory, and the sentiment trend is degrading (one strong-buy downgrade in the last month). At ~14.8x forward on a low-single-digit grower, the stock is reasonably valued — neither a trap nor an obvious bargain. To justify a high valuation score, one would need either a specific catalyst to re-rate the multiple or evidence that current earnings are trough. Neither is clearly present: the trough case rests on ELATEC integration being transitory, which is exactly what the next two quarters must prove. The 1.7% dividend yield (raised to $0.55/qtr in 2026), well covered by a 17% FCF margin, provides modest support but is not the thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegion's North American moat is genuine. Schlage, Von Duprin, and LCN are embedded in building codes and architect specifications, creating high switching costs and durable pricing power — the ~28% Americas margin is the direct evidence. In non-residential institutional markets (education, healthcare, government), specification-driven demand is sticky and recurring through replacement and remodel cycles.

The trajectory is mixed. Americas competitive position is stable-to-improving on electronics share gains, but International is structurally weaker — lower share, lower margins, prior goodwill impairments ($573.6M accumulated), and current volume declines. The company competes against Assa Abloy and dormakaba internationally and Spectrum Brands/Kwikset in US residential, with increasing pressure from non-traditional tech entrants and private-label retail in electronic access control. The mechanical-to-electronic transition is both the growth opportunity and the source of intensifying competition; it raises defect/cyber/recall risk and invites well-resourced new entrants.

Net assessment: a strong, defensible Americas franchise paired with a competitively disadvantaged International segment. The blended trajectory is stable, not improving, which is why the competitive score sits modestly above neutral rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

**Mexico sourcing concentration.** Allegion sources 20–25% of COGS from Mexico (<5% China, 5–10% other non-US). This is an atypical and underappreciated profile — far more exposed to US-Mexico/USMCA trade policy than to China. Management has fully offset tariff inflation via pricing, but at the cost of volume, and pricing power has limits. A standardized screen would miss this.

**International deterioration — transitory or structural?** The Q1 International margin is the single most important watch item. If ELATEC integration costs are truly transitory, the segment normalizes and the stock has a setup. If the deterioration is structural, the entire M&A strategy comes into question given the segment's prior $573.6M of impairments.

**Goodwill/integration risk.** $1.93B goodwill, aggressive M&A pace, and a segment with a value-destruction history together create real impairment risk if International does not recover.

**Cyclicality.** Demand depends on non-residential construction/remodel and residential housing. Volume has been persistently negative; a construction downturn would expose the price/M&A-driven growth model. This is a sector truism that is largely priced, but combined with negative volume it carries more weight than usual here.

**Tax/domicile.** OECD global minimum tax (15%), Irish withholding tax, and treaty risk are pushing the effective rate up (14.5% → 16.2% → 19.5% in Q1 2026). Irish corporate law also affords shareholders less protection than US law.

**Upside risk — tariff refunds.** The February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and CIT decision suggest Allegion may be due refunds of previously paid tariffs; nothing is booked. This is unquantified optionality, partially offset by new tariffs imposed under other statutes.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign-to-mildly cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 30.4% is rich versus 25.0% trailing realized vol (5.4pp premium), with the term structure flat (30.4% near ≈ 30.0% far) — uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-dated binary event. Put/call skew is –5.1% (call-heavy) and P/C OI is 0.44, both indicating an absence of aggressive downside hedging. Since early May, IV ticked down (31.0% → 30.4%) and P/C OI fell from 0.81 to 0.44 even as the price dropped 4.2%, consistent with the selloff being a steady fundamental de-rate rather than panic positioning. Short interest is modest at 4.2% of float (3.1 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The implied ±5.2% move into the June expiry is unremarkable. The market is pricing a slow grind rather than a catalyst, which aligns with the HOLD posture.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Americas franchise is a high-return, spec-driven compounder (28% segment margin, pricing power embedded in building codes). ELATEC integration costs are transitory; International margins normalize back toward high single digits over 2026–2027, and electronics scale into mid-single-digit organic growth. At ~14.8x forward EPS, a low-single-digit grower with 31% ROE and 17% FCF margin re-rates toward the high-teens as the volume picture stabilizes. Unbooked tariff refunds surface as a one-time benefit. Buyback ($500M replenished authorization) plus a covered dividend support the floor. Bull target ~$185–189, roughly the upper analyst range and ~21x normalized $9+ EPS.

### Bear Case
The negative organic volume is the leading edge of structural demand weakness, not a temporary integration cost. International margin compression proves durable, raising goodwill-impairment risk on $1.93B of goodwill in a segment with a $573.6M impairment history. Tariff-driven price hikes continue to suppress volume; pricing power exhausts. The effective tax rate continues climbing toward ~20%, pressuring EPS. Estimates drift lower and the stock de-rates further toward 12–13x trough earnings. Bear target ~$110–115, roughly –12% to –15% from current.

The two cases hinge on the same variable: whether the Q1 2026 margin compression is integration noise or structural deterioration. That is unresolved, which is the honest reason this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality and undemanding multiple offset the deteriorating volume trajectory and integration drag, leaving genuinely balanced risk/reward into a two-quarter resolution window.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- International segment operating margin recovers toward 6%+ for two consecutive quarters, confirming ELATEC integration costs were transitory.
- Total organic volume turns positive (>0%) on a reported quarter, ending the price/M&A dependency.
- A clean re-rating entry: stock toward $110–115 (near ~12–13x forward) with stable estimates, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Goodwill impairment taken in the International segment, or guidance language signaling structural rather than cyclical International weakness.
- Two further quarters of negative EPS revisions combined with continued sub-(-3%) volume, indicating pricing power has exhausted and demand is contracting.
- Effective tax rate sustaining near or above 20%, materially impairing the EPS bridge.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint — it is the first read on whether International margins inflected after the Q1 collapse. A second checkpoint is the Q3 print, which would confirm or refute any Q2 improvement.

## Conclusion

Allegion pairs a best-in-class North American security franchise with a growth model that is increasingly carried by price and acquisitions rather than real demand. The ~29% decline from the February high reflects a fundamental de-rate, not just volatility: Q1 2026 delivered the first YoY net-income decline in years, International margins collapsed, organic volume turned negative, two of the last four quarters missed, and consensus is drifting flat-to-lower. At ~14.8x forward EPS the stock looks cheap against peers, but the discount is earned by exactly these issues, and there is no positive revision velocity or near-term catalyst to close it.

The decision comes down to whether the Q1 margin compression is transitory ELATEC integration cost or the start of structural International deterioration — a question the next two earnings reports will answer. Until then, the franchise is too high-quality and the price too undemanding to short, while the volume and integration trajectory is too weak to call a turn. The prior BUY thesis rested on a valuation re-rating that has not materialized and on a forward EPS path that is now drifting down. The current evidence supports stepping back to HOLD, with a clear path to upgrade on International margin recovery or a more attractive entry near book value, and a path to downgrade on impairment or sustained estimate cuts.
