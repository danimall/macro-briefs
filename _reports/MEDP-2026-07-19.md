**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medpace is one of the highest-quality operating businesses in the contract research organization (CRO) space: debt-free, generating a 27% free-cash-flow margin, and compounding revenue at roughly 20% annually for four straight years. Reported growth is superb (FY2025 revenue +20.0% to $2.53B; Q1'26 +26.5% led by Metabolic/GLP-1 work up 60% YoY), but the leading indicator that governs CRO revenue two-to-four quarters out has rolled over. Book-to-bill fell to approximately 0.87x in Q1'26 (net awards $618M against $707M of revenue burn), and backlog stagnated to $2,929M at 3/31/2026, down sequentially from year-end and up only 2.9% YoY. Revenue is currently outrunning the order book, a configuration that historically precedes deceleration in this industry.

The equity has recovered to ~$538 from a February 2026 low near $416 (the stock fell roughly 16% in a single session on the Q4'25 print), and now trades at ~33.8x trailing and ~31x forward earnings, a re-rating that fully re-embraces the growth narrative while the bookings data argue for patience. Layered on top is a securities class action (Durbin v. Medpace, filed 4/6/2026, S.D. Ohio) alleging management misrepresented book-to-bill and understated cancellations during a 4/22/2025–2/9/2026 class period, made materially more concerning by heavy C-suite open-market selling inside that window (CEO Troendle ~$40M, President Geiger ~$32M+, plus the CFO, GC, and EVP Operations), a complete buyback halt in Q1'26, and two executive-departure 8-Ks in April and June 2026.

We rate MEDP a HOLD. The franchise quality and cash generation are genuine and support the downside, but the combination of a stretched multiple against a decelerating order book, an unadjudicated fraud allegation targeting the exact metric that is weakening, and a governance/insider-selling cluster leaves risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the current data neither confirms a growth re-acceleration nor a fundamental break, and the score is essentially unchanged.

## Company Overview

Medpace is a full-service, therapeutically specialized CRO providing outsourced Phase I–IV clinical development to biopharma and medical-device sponsors. It employs roughly 6,300 people across 46 countries and runs proprietary trial-management software (ClinTrak) alongside owned lab infrastructure (central, bioanalytical, and core imaging/cardiovascular labs, plus a Phase I clinic in Cincinnati). Therapeutic depth is concentrated in Oncology, Metabolic disease, CNS, Cardiology, and Antiviral/Anti-infective.

The defining strategic choice is client mix. In FY2025, 82% of net revenue came from small biopharma and 13% from mid-sized, meaning roughly 95% of revenue is tied to funding-sensitive, frequently pre-profit sponsors. Management deliberately declines the large, low-margin strategic-partnership arrangements that IQVIA, ICON, Thermo Fisher/PPD, and Fortrea pursue with big pharma, instead competing on therapeutic expertise and integrated full-service delivery for the small/mid segment. Medpace operates as a single reporting segment.

That mix cuts both ways. It gives Medpace above-peer sensitivity to the biotech funding cycle: sharper deceleration on the downside, faster recovery on the upside. The current Metabolic/GLP-1 boom is the visible upside expression; the softening order book is the countervailing signal that the small-biotech funding environment remains uneven.

## Financial Analysis

The income statement and cash flow are the strongest part of the story.

- **Revenue:** FY2025 $2.53B, +20.0% YoY (2024 $2.11B; 2023 $1.89B; 2022 $1.46B), a multi-year ~20%+ CAGR. Q1'26 revenue $706.6M, +26.5% YoY.
- **Margins:** FY2025 operating income $535M (21.1% operating margin); net income $451M (17.8% net margin). Q1'26 operating income $141.5M.
- **The net-income optical trap:** Q1'26 net income rose only ~8% to $123.9M despite +26.5% revenue and stronger operating income, because the effective tax rate normalized from an unusually low 3.0% in Q1'25 (share-comp excess benefits) to 16.1% in Q1'26. FY2025 ETR was 16.8%. Modeling EPS off the top line will overstate net-income growth going forward.

Cash conversion is exceptional. FY2025 operating cash flow was $713M against only ~$31M of capex (1.2% of revenue), producing free cash flow of $682M, a 27% FCF margin, with operating cash flow exceeding net income (a high-quality-earnings marker). The balance sheet carries zero debt beyond an undrawn $10M revolver; cash was $497M at year-end 2025 and rose to $653M by Q1'26. Roughly $854M of advanced billings (customer prepayments) fund working capital, giving Medpace a structural negative-working-capital advantage.

Screening metrics look weak and must be reframed. The Altman Z of 2.47 ("grey zone"), a 0.74 current ratio, negative retained earnings (–$460M at YE2025), and 3.3x debt/equity are artifacts of a debt-free company that repurchased $913M of stock in FY2025 (2.96M shares), retiring equity against retained earnings. This is aggressive shareholder return, not distress. ROE of ~98% and ROA of ~23% are inflated by the shrunken equity base but still reflect genuinely high returns on capital. A model that penalized leverage or liquidity here would misread the business.

The soft spot is the order book, and it is material. FY2025 net new business awards were $2,647M against $2,530M of revenue (~1.05x book-to-bill), already thin. Q1'26 net awards fell to $618M against $707M of revenue (~0.87x), backlog dropped to $2,929M (down sequentially, +2.9% YoY), and remaining performance obligations for active projects declined from $3.6B to $3.5B. That is sustainable for a quarter or two but not indefinitely without a bookings re-acceleration, and it is the mathematical core of both the bear thesis and the litigation.

**Track record on estimates:** four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +8.1%, Q4'25 +9.6%, Q3'25 +7.5%, Q2'25 +2.6%), so near-term consensus is credible on the reported line. This is a backward-looking comfort that says nothing about the forward bookings problem.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth engine is real and, for now, accelerating: Q1'26 revenue +26.5% with Metabolic up 60% YoY to $238M, a direct beneficiary of the GLP-1/obesity clinical pipeline. That is a structural tailwind, and it is broad enough across the industry that it is not a single-company artifact.

The problem is durability. Estimate revisions are mixed-to-flat rather than the +30% inflection that would signal a systematic underestimation: current-year EPS consensus is $17.12 (30-day revision +0.3%, but 90-day –1.3%); next-year $18.75 (30-day +1.0%, 90-day –2.6%); next-quarter revised down 4.9% over 90 days. The 30-day marks have firmed modestly, which is the "estimates broadly rising" read, but on a 90-day basis estimates are still net lower. This does not clear the bar for a high-conviction growth signal, and it is inconsistent with a deteriorating bookings picture.

Revenue burn is drawing down a backlog that is not being replenished at the same rate. Unless net awards re-accelerate above ~1.0x book-to-bill in the next one to two quarters, reported revenue growth will decelerate mechanically as the backlog thins. The GLP-1 tailwind is the swing factor that could re-fill the order book, but the Q1'26 net-award figure shows it had not done so as of the most recent disclosure. Weighting the durable-growth signal heavily, the single most reliable winner-selection dimension, the evidence here is a strong present tense with a weakening forward derivative, which caps the score at midpoint.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$538, MEDP trades at ~33.8x trailing EPS of $15.28 and ~31x consensus current-year EPS of $17.12. The stock has appreciated ~68% off its 52-week low of $306.70 and sits ~14% below its 52-week high of $628.92.

Peer context is muddied by the sector's earnings dispersion. Same-industry comparables show a peer-median P/E of 46.9x (TECH 77.8x, BIO 46.9x, MTD 26.8x, with CRL and AVTR at negative/loss multiples), so on a headline basis MEDP screens below the median. But those peers are diversified tools/instrument businesses, not pure-play small-biotech CROs, and the loss-making comps distort the median upward. The cleaner benchmark is against Medpace's own growth-quality and its history: ~31x forward for a business whose leading indicator (book-to-bill <1.0x) points to decelerating growth is not obviously cheap, and it is arguably rich if 2027 revenue growth compresses toward the mid-single-to-low-double digits as the backlog thins.

The analyst target set reinforces the caution: consensus mean $472.33 and median $480.50 sit ~11–12% below the current price, with a high of $586 and a low of $329. When a stock trades above its consensus mean and median with estimate revisions that are flat-to-negative on a 90-day basis, targets function as a ceiling rather than a floor. Here the price has run ahead of both the targets and the order book.

Cheapness is not the issue; the issue is that the multiple already prices continued high growth that the bookings data does not yet support. We treat the modest discount to a distorted peer median as insufficient evidence of mispricing and assign a below-neutral valuation score. In a regime where cheap multiples have repeatedly sorted the wrong way absent an independent catalyst, and where this specific multiple embeds optimism rather than pessimism, the asymmetry does not favor the buyer at $538.

## Competitive Landscape

Medpace's moat is real but narrow. Its edge is therapeutic depth plus full-service delivery tuned to small and mid-sized biotech, a segment global CROs serve less efficiently. Cumulative TSR of 403% since 2021 versus a peer group's 99% evidences durable historical value creation and share gains. The GLP-1/Metabolic surge is a live expression of that positioning: +60% YoY segment growth is a standout.

The trajectory question is more nuanced than the level. On reported revenue, Medpace is clearly gaining share and outgrowing the diversified peers. But the same 95%-small/mid-biotech concentration that drove outperformance in the up-cycle is now the source of the bookings softness, and a bearish external read (Seeking Alpha, 7/6/2026) argues Medpace's cancellations and awards are worsening versus CRO peers. Contracts are terminable on 30 days' notice, and backlog is explicitly not a reliable revenue predictor. The competitive position is improving on the demand it is winning today but exposed on the pipeline it is booking for tomorrow, which nets to a modestly above-neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are unusually concrete and, in several cases, not fully priced.

1. **Customer concentration in funding-sensitive small biotech (95% of revenue).** In a soft biotech funding environment, cancellations rise and awards slow. This is the direct mechanism behind the sub-1.0x book-to-bill and is the central near-term risk.

2. **Bookings/backlog deterioration.** The leading indicator is flashing amber. This is the falsifiable data point the bull case must reverse.

3. **Securities class action (Durbin v. Medpace, S.D. Ohio, filed 4/6/2026).** §10(b)/§20(a)/Rule 10b-5 claims allege materially false statements about book-to-bill and understated cancellations across the class period. The company has not booked a liability (loss not deemed probable/estimable). The suit is unadjudicated, but the fact pattern (April 2025 guidance that a 1.15x 2H book-to-bill was achievable, cautious tone by July, a ~15–16% single-day drop on the February 2026 print) is precisely what plaintiffs' firms target.

4. **Governance/insider cluster.** Nearly the entire C-suite sold heavily inside the class period: Troendle ~$40M, Geiger ~$32M+, plus CFO Brady, GC Ewald, and EVP Burwig. Ordinarily insider selling is low-signal against rising estimates, but this selling co-occurs with the litigation window, a decelerating order book, and a complete buyback halt in Q1'26 (zero repurchases versus $390M in Q1'25, despite $822M of remaining authorization). Two executive-departure 8-Ks (4/22/2026 and 6/4/2026) sit inside the same window. This clustering meets the threshold for treating insider activity as materially cautionary rather than routine.

5. **Founder control.** CEO Troendle controls ~19% of shares and effective board/bylaw/M&A control, with a related-party web deeper than typical: he is Medpace's landlord (HQ and multiple buildings leased from CEO/family entities to 2032/2040, ~$82.6M related-party lease liabilities), its charter-jet provider, and a board member/equity holder in Medpace customers (LIB Therapeutics, CinRx — $1.2M and $6.1M of Q1'26 revenue). Individually small, collectively they raise revenue-quality and conflict questions.

6. **Expanded AI and tariff risk factors** in the FY2025 10-K: AI as both competitive threat and operational-control risk, and China retaliatory tariffs on imported trial supplies.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that aligns with the litigation/bookings overhang. Near-term ATM IV is 65.8% versus 62.8% one-year historical volatility (roughly fair on spread but in the 94th percentile of the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-month IV 65.8% falling to 48.0% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst (the next earnings print). The implied one-month move is ±15.9%, consistent with the large single-day gaps this name has produced on prints. Skew is roughly balanced (-1.1% near-term, widening to -4.7% at six months), and the put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.18 and 0.05), so positioning is not aggressively bearish. IV has risen 27 points since the May snapshot alongside a ~28% price gain, and the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 2.04 to 0.05; the hedging that was in place ahead of the recovery has been unwound. The signal is elevated event risk into the next report rather than a strong directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$586–$629).** The GLP-1/Metabolic wave re-accelerates net awards above 1.0x book-to-bill within one to two quarters, refilling the backlog and confirming that the Q1'26 dip was a timing artifact rather than a trend. Revenue continues at 20%+, the FCF margin and zero-debt balance sheet compound intrinsic value, buybacks resume, and the litigation is dismissed or settled immaterially. On a re-accelerating order book, ~31x forward is defensible and the stock revisits its 52-week high near $629, with the bullish analyst target of $586 as an interim marker. Estimate revisions turn convincingly positive as consensus catches up.

**Bear Case (~$375–$415).** The sub-1.0x book-to-bill proves to be the leading edge of a small-biotech funding air pocket. Backlog continues to thin, 2027 revenue growth decelerates to mid/high-single digits, and the market re-rates the multiple toward the low-20s on slower growth. The securities suit survives a motion to dismiss, extending the governance overhang and keeping buybacks paused. This is roughly the level the stock traded at in late April 2026 after the Q1 print (~$393), implying ~25–30% downside from current levels, a plausible retest if bookings disappoint again.

The base case sits between these: reported growth stays healthy near-term on backlog burn while the order book determines whether the stock holds ~$538 or drifts toward the analyst mean of ~$472. The two-sided setup is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2'26 report (following the 4/22/2026 last-reported earnings date) is the key catalyst. Net awards and backlog will confirm or refute the deceleration thesis, and the ±15.9% implied move signals the market expects a material reaction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill returns above 1.0x for one to two consecutive quarters with backlog resuming YoY growth above ~8–10%.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn convincingly positive (30-day and 90-day both positive, next-year revision above +5% over 90 days).
- Securities litigation dismissed or resolved on immaterial terms, and/or buyback resumption signaling management confidence.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Book-to-bill deteriorates further (below ~0.85x) with a second consecutive sequential backlog decline.
- Revenue growth guidance cut below the low double digits, confirming backlog burn is exhausting.
- Litigation survives a motion to dismiss with an expanding class, or additional executive departures/insider selling.

**Natural reassessment point:** Next earnings release; secondarily the June 8, 2026 lead-plaintiff developments and any 8-K clarifying the June 4, 2026 executive departure.

## Conclusion

Medpace is a high-quality, cash-rich, debt-free CRO with a genuine GLP-1 tailwind and a four-year record of ~20% growth. That quality is not in dispute and anchors the downside. What holds the recommendation at HOLD is the gap between what the business is reporting and what it is booking: revenue is outrunning an order book that has fallen below replacement, the stock has re-rated on the reported story rather than the leading one, and a securities-fraud allegation targeting the weakening metric coincides with heavy inside selling, a buyback halt, and executive departures. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative on a 90-day basis, not the inflection that would justify chasing the multiple.

The risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric. With ~25–30% downside to the bear case and ~10–20% upside to the bull case from current levels, and consensus targets sitting below the current price, we would not add here. We prefer to reassess at the next print for evidence that net awards have re-accelerated (which would upgrade the name to BUY) or deteriorated further (which would move it to AVOID). **HOLD — uncertain**, pending the order-book and litigation resolution.
