## Executive Summary

Spire Inc is simultaneously closing a $2.48B acquisition (Piedmont Natural Gas Tennessee from Duke Energy), divesting its Gas Marketing segment ($215M to Boardwalk Pipelines), and selling its storage assets ($650M to I Squared Capital). The net effect transforms Spire from an ~85% regulated utility with commodity-exposed marketing and midstream operations into a ~95%+ pure-play regulated gas distributor serving approximately 1.95 million customers across Missouri, Alabama, Mississippi, and now Tennessee.

The strategic logic is sound: Nashville is one of the fastest-growing MSAs in the US, and the move to a fully regulated earnings profile should reduce earnings volatility and support a narrower equity risk premium over time. However, the execution burden is substantial. Spire must finance a $2.48B acquisition (representing ~46% of its pre-deal market cap) with only ~$865M in offsetting divestiture proceeds, meaning roughly $1.6B must come from new debt, equity, and hybrid issuance. S&P already has the company on negative credit outlook, free cash flow has been negative for years, and management's FY2027 EPS guidance was lowered from $5.65-$5.85 to $5.40-$5.60 to reflect the loss of marketing earnings. At ~16.5x FY2027 guided midpoint EPS, the stock is priced at a reasonable but not compelling multiple for a gas utility bearing above-average integration and financing risk.

## Company Overview

Spire Inc., headquartered in St. Louis, is one of the five largest publicly traded natural gas distribution utilities in the United States. The company operates regulated gas distribution utilities in four states:

- **Spire Missouri**: ~1.2 million customers, largest gas distribution utility in the state
- **Spire Alabama**: ~430,000 customers, largest gas distribution utility in Alabama
- **Spire EnergySouth (Gulf + Mississippi)**: ~100,000 customers in the Mobile, AL area and south-central Mississippi
- **Spire Tennessee** (acquired March 31, 2026): ~200,000 customers in the Nashville metro, now the largest investor-owned gas utility in Tennessee

The Gas Marketing segment (being sold) provides natural gas procurement and physical delivery across central and southern US markets. The Midstream segment (storage assets being sold) includes ~34 Bcf of working gas storage capacity in Wyoming and Oklahoma, plus the 65-mile Spire STL Pipeline and 263-mile MoGas Pipeline. Post-divestitures, Spire will retain the STL and MoGas pipelines but shed the storage operations and marketing business entirely.

The company's franchise positions are effectively monopoly or near-monopoly within its service territories, with regulated rate structures that include Missouri's Infrastructure System Replacement Surcharge (ISRS) for expedited capital recovery, Alabama's Rate Stabilization and Equalization (RSE) mechanism, and weather normalization riders across most jurisdictions.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

Spire's headline revenue has declined ($2,666M → $2,593M → $2,476M over FY2023-2025), but this reflects lower pass-through gas costs, not operational deterioration. Contribution margin — the more relevant metric for a regulated utility — grew from $1,274M to $1,362M to $1,455M over the same period, a 14.2% cumulative increase.

Adjusted EPS progression: $4.05 (FY2023) → $4.13 (FY2024) → $4.44 (FY2025), representing ~10% growth over two years. GAAP diluted EPS was $4.37 in FY2025. Operating income expanded from $419M to $488M to $524M, driven by ISRS surcharge growth ($33.5M in FY2025), Alabama RSE adjustments ($5.0M), and strong midstream storage performance.

Midstream was Spire's fastest-growing segment — net income grew from $12.0M (FY2023) to $56.3M (FY2025), nearly a fivefold increase — and it is being sold. The rationale is that regulated earnings command higher multiples and lower cost of capital, which should more than offset the absolute earnings loss.

### Capital Structure and Liquidity

Total debt (long-term + short-term) stood at $4,686M against stockholders' equity of $3,389M at FY2025 end, implying total debt-to-equity of approximately 1.38x. The company maintains a $1.5B revolving credit facility and $1.5B commercial paper program. The current ratio of 0.32 is characteristic of utilities with seasonal working capital needs funded through short-term facilities.

Interest expense was $204M in FY2025. Credit ratings are BBB (S&P, negative outlook) and Baa2 (Moody's, stable). The S&P negative outlook is a material concern because the Piedmont acquisition adds $2.48B of financing on top of an already leveraged balance sheet.

### Free Cash Flow

Spire generated $578M in operating cash flow against $922M in capital expenditures in FY2025, producing negative FCF of approximately $344M before dividends (~$210M annualized). The company has been consistently FCF-negative, a structural feature of its high capital intensity. Planned FY2026 capex is $809M excluding acquisition-related spending. With Piedmont now in the portfolio, total capital needs will increase further. Spire remains dependent on continuous external financing — a vulnerability if borrowing costs rise or credit ratings deteriorate.

### Earnings Surprise Track Record

The last four quarters show a mixed 2-for-4 beat/miss record: a +3.2% beat in Q1 FY2026 (Dec 2025), a -7.6% miss in Q4 FY2025, a large beat in Q3 FY2025 (+110% on a small base), and a -4.9% miss in Q2 FY2025. This inconsistency reduces confidence in forward estimate precision, particularly during a period of major structural change.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-quarter (Q2 FY2026) estimates have risen modestly (+1.9% over 30 days to $3.69), but next-quarter estimates have been cut sharply (-24.0% over 30 days to $0.08), and next-year estimates are falling (-2.3% over 90 days to $5.61). The pattern suggests analysts are adjusting for the earnings profile change (loss of Marketing, addition of Piedmont) with net downward revisions for the outer periods. This negative earnings momentum is a headwind.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth Drivers

1. **Piedmont Tennessee integration**: The Nashville metro adds ~200,000 customers in one of the fastest-growing US regions. Management expects Tennessee to represent ~20% of the capital investment plan through 2030. Organic customer growth in Nashville should materially exceed the ~4,000 net customer additions Spire achieved annually across legacy territories.

2. **Rate base expansion**: Combined utility capex of ~$800-900M annually supports steady rate base growth. Missouri ISRS, Alabama RSE, and Tennessee's constructive regulatory mechanisms provide relatively predictable capital recovery pathways.

3. **Pure-play premium**: Shifting to ~95%+ regulated earnings should reduce the earnings multiple discount associated with commodity-exposed businesses. This may support multiple expansion over 12-18 months as the market re-rates the simplified business.

### Long-Term Growth Guidance

Management guides 5-7% long-term adjusted EPS growth off an original FY2027 midpoint base of $5.75. The updated FY2027 range of $5.40-$5.60 reflects the loss of Marketing earnings. This creates an ambiguity: is the 5-7% growth rate applied to $5.75 (the original base) or the revised $5.50 midpoint? The distinction matters for long-term valuation.

### Secular Headwinds

Gas utilities face long-duration challenges from electrification trends, building code restrictions on gas hookups, heat pump competition, and climate-driven warming that reduces heating demand. FY2025 saw temperatures 8.7% warmer than normal in Missouri and 4.2% warmer in Alabama. Weather normalization riders provide partial protection, but sustained warming erodes the core demand thesis. These headwinds are partially offset by RNG (renewable natural gas) opportunities and the essential nature of gas infrastructure for decades-long transition periods.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.92 with FY2025 adjusted EPS of $4.44, Spire trades at ~20.5x trailing adjusted earnings. Against FY2027 guided midpoint EPS of $5.50, the forward multiple is ~16.5x. Against the FY2026 consensus estimate of $5.27, the multiple is ~17.3x.

**Peer comparison**: The gas utility peer median P/E is 20.0x. Spire at ~20x trailing sits right at the median. UGI trades cheapest at 13.8x (diversified/international complexity), SWX trades richest at 27.8x. On forward earnings, Spire's ~17x FY2026 multiple appears slightly cheap to the peer group, but this should be weighed against: (a) S&P negative credit outlook, (b) active integration/divestiture execution risk that peers do not share, and (c) negative estimate revisions for FY2027.

The dividend yield of ~3.7% compares to a peer median of 3.1%, suggesting either a modest yield premium for risk or an attractive income entry. The dividend appears covered by current earnings ($4.44 EPS vs. ~$3.54/share annualized dividend) but is not covered by FCF, which is structurally negative.

Analyst consensus targets range from $87 (low) to $106 (high) with a median of $100.50, implying ~10.5% upside from current levels. The distribution of coverage is 1 Strong Buy, 4 Buy, 3 Hold — constructive but not uniformly bullish.

At current prices, the stock is reasonably valued for a utility in a period of elevated execution risk. The market is not offering a significant discount for the acquisition-related uncertainty, nor is it paying a premium for the growth optionality of Nashville.

## Competitive Landscape

Spire's competitive position rests on regulated franchise monopolies — the strongest form of economic moat in utilities. Within its service territories, customers have no alternative gas distribution provider. This moat does not erode from competitive entry; the risk is from demand substitution (electrification, heat pumps) and regulatory changes.

Tennessee strengthens the franchise portfolio by adding a high-growth demographic market. Nashville's population growth trajectory is structurally superior to St. Louis and Birmingham, providing a multi-decade organic growth runway that legacy territories lack. Management's decision to consolidate Tennessee operations under an existing executive (Joe Hampton) rather than adding new leadership suggests confidence in operational similarity across territories.

However, the competitive position improvement comes at a price. Spire is now the most leveraged it has been in recent history, competing for capital against peers with cleaner balance sheets. A utility that must continuously access external capital markets is at a structural disadvantage to peers that can self-fund through operating cash flow.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Acquisition financing and credit risk**: The ~$1.6B net financing requirement (after divestiture proceeds) on top of an already leveraged balance sheet creates downgrade risk. A downgrade to BBB- would increase borrowing costs on significant short-term debt needs (peak working capital can exceed $1.3B) and could trigger unfavorable terms on future issuance. The rating agency disagreement (S&P negative vs. Moody's stable at Baa2) adds uncertainty.

**Divestiture execution risk**: Two major divestitures must close on schedule (Gas Marketing in fiscal Q3 2026, Storage in fiscal H2 2026). Delays would extend bridge financing costs and potentially force more dilutive permanent financing. Regulatory approvals, buyer financing, and operational separation must all proceed concurrently with Piedmont integration.

**Regulatory risk**: Missouri's ISRS statute expires August 28, 2029 without legislative renewal. This mechanism is critical to the infrastructure replacement investment thesis. Failure to renew would force full rate cases for capital recovery, slowing the investment cycle and compressing returns.

**Rising interest rates**: Each 50bp increase in borrowing costs on the company's ~$4.7B debt stack adds roughly $23M in annual interest expense (~$0.40/share pre-tax). With structural FCF deficits requiring continuous market access, rate sensitivity is above the utility peer median.

**Negative estimate revisions**: The trajectory of downward FY2027 revisions during a transition year reduces confidence in management's guided growth rate.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity on SR is extremely thin (37 contracts across 3 expirations), limiting the signal value. Near-term ATM IV at 36.4% vs. 18.2% historical volatility — nearly 2x realized vol. The term structure is in steep backwardation (36.4% near-term vs. 19.4% at 6 months), and put/call OI is 0.01 (essentially all calls). The rich near-term IV suggests the market is pricing elevated uncertainty over the next 2-4 weeks, consistent with pending corporate actions (divestiture closings, potential equity issuance). However, the extremely low OI means this signal should carry minimal weight — the market for SR options is too illiquid for reliable inference.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($100-$106, ~10-17% upside)

Nashville's demographic profile supports 2-3x the organic customer growth of legacy territories. Divestitures close on schedule, generating $865M that de-levers the balance sheet faster than expected. S&P upgrades the outlook to stable as leverage metrics improve through FY2027. The pure-play regulated profile commands a re-rating toward the 20-21x forward multiple that peers like OGS and CPK carry. On $5.50 FY2027 EPS, a 19-19.5x multiple yields $104-$107.

### Bear Case ($72-$78, ~14-21% downside)

S&P downgrades to BBB-, increasing borrowing costs and triggering utility fund rebalancing (some mandates require BBB or better). Divestiture closings are delayed, extending bridge facility costs. Equity dilution from permanent financing is larger than expected (10-15% share increase). Nashville integration proves costlier or slower than projected. Rising rates compress utility multiples broadly — at higher long-term yields, gas utilities trading at 18-20x would need to compress to 15-16x to maintain competitive income yields. On $5.30 EPS (low end of guidance minus slippage) at 14-15x, the stock reaches $74-$80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY)**:
1. Both divestitures close on schedule with proceeds matching or exceeding announced terms, AND S&P revises outlook to stable
2. FY2027 EPS estimates stabilize or rise above $5.60 (confirming management's growth guidance is credible post-restructuring)
3. Stock pulls back below $82 (providing ~15x FY2027 EPS and 4.3%+ dividend yield), creating asymmetric entry

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID)**:
1. S&P downgrades to BBB- or Moody's revises outlook to negative
2. Either divestiture fails to close or terms are materially revised downward
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falls below $5.20, indicating the growth thesis is failing

**Reassessment timeline**: Next earnings report (likely May 2026 for Q2 FY2026), which should provide the first look at Piedmont contribution, divestiture progress, and updated financing plans.

## Conclusion

Spire is betting that the Nashville growth market and pure-play regulated simplification justify the near-term cost of a leveraged acquisition, simultaneous divestitures, and potential credit pressure. The logic is defensible — Nashville is genuinely a premium growth market for gas distribution, and eliminating commodity-exposed earnings should reduce the cost of equity over time. But the execution burden is real: three major transactions must close in a compressed timeline, S&P is already signaling concern, and estimates are falling.

At ~17x FY2026 consensus EPS with a 3.7% yield, the stock is fairly priced for the risk profile. There is no compelling mispricing in either direction. The right approach is to wait for either (a) evidence that the transformation is succeeding (divestiture closings, stable credit outlook, estimate stabilization) before buying, or (b) a price dislocation to $82 or below that provides adequate margin of safety.

**Rating: HOLD** — pending resolution of multiple concurrent execution risks over the next 2-3 quarters.
