**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MYR Group is one of the largest U.S. specialty electrical construction contractors, split roughly evenly between a Transmission & Distribution (T&D) utility segment (54.7% of FY25 revenue) and a Commercial & Industrial (C&I) segment (45.3%) heavily levered to data-center and reshoring buildouts. The investment case is anchored on a sharp, broad-based margin recovery: gross margin has moved from 8.6% in 2024 (a year crushed by negative percentage-of-completion estimate changes on clean-energy T&D and one C&I project) to 11.6% in 2025 and 13.4% in Q1 2026, with both segments hitting multi-year-high operating margins simultaneously. Net income doubled year-over-year in Q1'26 to $46.8M, and consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 23.1% over the past 90 days to $11.47. The company sits directly in the grid-investment and AI-driven electrification cycle that the world model treats as a durable, multi-source demand theme.

The problem is price. At $379.17 the stock has appreciated 94% over the trailing twelve months and trades at roughly 32x annualized Q1 run-rate earnings (~$12) and ~33x consensus FY26 EPS, a premium multiple for a cyclical contractor that earns 3-5% net margins and whose earnings quality is inseparable from percentage-of-completion estimate judgment. The balance sheet is genuinely excellent (net cash, essentially debt-free, Altman Z 3.72), and estimate momentum is unambiguously positive, which is what keeps this a hold rather than an avoid. But the $75M buyback authorization lapsed on February 4, 2026 without renewal, insiders have been net sellers into strength at $442-461 (above the current price), and analyst sentiment is degrading with Oppenheimer initiating at Perform. This is a high-quality business at a full price with a two-sided near-term risk profile.

We rate MYRG a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.05, confirming the declining trajectory from 6.7 (May) to 6.0 (June). The fundamentals have, if anything, improved since the prior reads, but the ~24% price decline from the June 30 high (~$500) has not created a compelling entry because the multiple remains stretched against normalized earnings power. We would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the high-$200s/low-$300s (roughly 22-25x normalized EPS) or on a renewed buyback authorization plus continued positive estimate-change momentum.

## Company Overview

MYR Group traces its T&D roots to 1891 and operates as a holding company through approximately 15 subsidiary brands (The L.E. Myers Co., Sturgeon Electric, CSI Electrical, Huen Electric, Powerline Plus) across the U.S. and Canada. The two reportable segments are:

- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — $2.00B FY25 revenue. Prime contractor to investor-owned utilities, cooperatives, IPPs, and independent transmission companies for high-voltage transmission lines, substations, distribution systems, clean energy, EV charging, and emergency storm restoration. Transmission is ~60% of the segment.
- **Commercial & Industrial (C&I)** — $1.66B FY25 revenue. Typically a subcontractor to general contractors; end markets include data centers, airports, hospitals, stadiums, manufacturing, water/wastewater, and transportation.

Competitive advantages per the 10-K: a skilled ~7,200-person craft workforce (~85% IBEW-unionized), a large centralized self-maintained specialty-vehicle fleet, a strong safety record, and superior bonding capacity ($2.70B face of surety bonds outstanding as of Q1'26) that allows it to bid larger, more complex projects than smaller rivals. Customer concentration is low: top 10 customers are ~38% of revenue, with no single customer above 10% for three straight years. The company pays no dividend and is headquartered in Thornton, CO.

MYRG participates in the AI/data-center theme as the electrical construction contractor building facilities and grid interconnections, not as a hyperscaler or chip company. This is picks-and-shovels exposure to data-center and grid demand, occupying the labor-and-installation layer of that supply chain.

## Financial Analysis

The central financial story is estimate-change-driven margin volatility, not revenue volatility.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.01B | $3.64B | $3.36B | $3.66B | $1.00B (+20%) |
| Gross margin | 11.4% | 10.0% | 8.6% | 11.6% | 13.4% |
| Operating margin | 3.8% | 3.5% | 1.6% | 4.6% | 6.5% |
| Net income | $83M | $91M | $30M | $118M | $46.8M (2x) |
| Diluted EPS | ~$4.91 | $5.40 | $1.83 | $7.53 | $2.99 |
| Operating cash flow | — | $71M | $87M | $327M | $84.7M |

The mechanism behind the swing: FY24 was hit by negative estimate changes worth –4.4% of gross margin, concentrated on T&D clean-energy projects and a single C&I project (labor/project inefficiencies, unfavorable change orders). By FY25 the negative estimate impact shrank to –1.4%, and in Q1'26 it flipped to +0.8% as a higher share of projects progressed at contractual margins and favorable job closeouts landed. The troubled fixed-price work has largely rolled off, and productivity is now running favorable. The recovery is broad-based: T&D operating margin reached 9.7% and C&I 8.1% in Q1'26, both multi-year highs, which reduces the risk that the improvement is a single-project artifact.

The balance sheet supports a high financial-health score. As of 3/31/2026 the company had zero revolver borrowings (it paid down $47.4M in Q1), only ~$9.4M in equipment notes, $163M cash, and $460.5M available on its $490M facility, a net-cash position. Altman Z is 3.72 (safe zone), ROE 17.9%, ROA 7.2%, current ratio 1.33. The 1.49 debt/equity in the screen is misleading; it reflects operating leases and current liabilities rather than funded debt. FY25 free cash flow was $232M (6.3% margin), a large swing from working-capital-driven weakness in 2024. FCF conversion at ~197% of net income in FY25 is unusually high but partly reflects the reversal of the 2024 working-capital drag, so it should not be extrapolated as steady-state.

The real off-balance-sheet leverage is contractor-specific: $2.70B face of surety bonds ($899M estimated cost-to-complete) and multi-employer pension withdrawal exposure, some plans classified "critical." These are structural to the business model rather than financing decisions.

The four-quarter earnings surprise record is strong and accelerating: +9.9%, +4.9%, +22.1%, +44.1%. The 44.1% Q1'26 beat is the largest of the streak, and it aligns with the positive estimate-change turn. This raises confidence in forward consensus, though the beat magnitudes are themselves a function of the same percentage-of-completion judgment that swings both directions.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are unambiguously positive across every window:

| Next Year | $13.47 | $10.78 | +25.0% |

Current-year revisions of +23.1% and next-year of +25.0% are directionally strong but do not clear a +30%-over-90-days threshold, and revenue growth of ~20% in Q1'26 is below the 40% bar that would trigger the strongest inflection signal. This matters for how we treat valuation: the estimate-revision momentum is real and directionally strong, but combined with a stock already at ~32-33x forward, this is not a cheap inflection stock where analyst targets should be treated as floors. The guardrail applies: at premium multiples, rising estimates alone do not justify a BUY because the multiple already prices continued beats, and the asymmetry turns unfavorable (beats expected, misses punished).

The demand backdrop is durable. T&D drivers include an aging grid, reliability and congestion needs, connection of new generation, load growth, and grid-hardening against wildfires and storms. C&I is led by data centers (explicitly a "primary market"), reshoring, and public infrastructure. Management specifically cites AI-adoption electricity demand as requiring significant customer investment in both segments. Backlog stood at $2.84B at Q1'26, up from $2.58B at YE24. Management flagged that large multi-year transmission awards booked in 2026 will not materially benefit 2026 revenue (construction starts 2027+), so near-term growth comes from small/medium projects and distribution work, with the larger transmission ramp deferred. This tempers the 2026 revenue-growth ceiling even as it strengthens the multi-year visibility.

Data-center and grid investment is a widely cited structural driver across regulated utilities and the electrical-equipment supply chain, rather than a single-company story, which supports the growth-prospects score.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the binding constraint. At $379.17:
- Trailing P/E on FY25 EPS of $7.53 is ~50x.
- On annualized Q1'26 run-rate ($2.99 × 4 ≈ $12) the multiple is ~32x.
- On consensus FY26 EPS of $11.47 it is ~33x; on FY27 of $13.47 it is ~28x.

Even giving full credit to the cyclical recovery and using forward/normalized earnings rather than trailing (which is the correct approach given the estimate-revision velocity), the stock is not cheap. Peer medians in Construction & Engineering sit at a P/E of ~28.4, with the group ranging from PRIM at 20.9x and FLR at 22.8x to GVA at 37.5x and ECG at 32.9x. MYRG at ~33x forward trades at the upper end of this cohort. That premium is defensible on balance-sheet quality (net cash vs. leveraged peers), broad-based margin recovery, and best-in-class bonding capacity, but it is a premium, not a discount.

The equity-lesson framework applies here: a "cheap" valuation score has been reliably anti-predictive, but the inverse also holds. This is a fully-valued stock where the multiple genuinely constrains forward returns, and there is no identifiable catalyst that argues the market is mispricing MYRG to the upside. The consensus price target range is $295 (low) to $564 (high) with a $445.5 mean and $459.5 median. The mean sits ~17% above the current price, but analyst sentiment is degrading and Oppenheimer initiated at Perform, so the target set is a lagging rather than leading signal given the premium multiple, and we do not treat the mean target as a floor.

On a growth-adjusted basis, PEG on FY27 growth (13.47 vs 11.47, ~17%) against a ~33x FY26 multiple gives a PEG near 1.9, reasonable for a quality compounder but not the sub-1.5 that would justify treating the stock as undervalued. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is stretched against normalized 3-5% net-margin contractor economics with no identified mispricing catalyst, but the business quality and estimate momentum keep it above the ≤4 value-trap zone.

## Competitive Landscape

MYRG holds a defensible position in a fragmented, price-driven industry. Its edge is bonding capacity, fleet standardization, safety record, and long-standing utility relationships, barriers that thin the competitive field on large, specialized T&D projects. Competitors include the much larger Quanta Services and MasTec, plus Primoris, IES Holdings, and Argan.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. The 2024 margin stumble reflected execution problems that peers in fixed-price construction also face, and the recovery restores parity rather than establishing new share gains. MYRG has actively reduced its T&D fixed-price mix (34% in 2025 vs 53% in 2023), lowering estimate-change risk, a sensible defensive posture rather than an aggressive share grab. The 5-year TSR indexed to 363.6 versus a peer group at 517.4 confirms MYRG underperformed its construction/engineering peer set over five years, largely due to the 2024 stumble; the 2025-26 rally is closing that gap rather than establishing outperformance.

The reduced disaggregation in Q1'26 (MYRG stopped breaking out T&D revenue by transmission-vs-distribution) is a minor negative for outside analysts' ability to track segment mix. On balance, competitive position is solid and stable: a well-capitalized mid-tier player benefiting from a rising tide, not a company demonstrably taking share. We hold competitive position at 6.5, consistent with the prior read and reflecting stable trajectory with structural tailwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**Percentage-of-completion estimate risk** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. FY24 demonstrated this is not theoretical: a handful of troubled fixed-price projects halved earnings. C&I is ~85% fixed-price. If a new crop of problem jobs emerges, margins can compress as fast as they expanded. The entire recovery thesis rests on the durability of the current favorable estimate-change environment, which must be re-verified every quarter.

**Clean-energy policy exposure** was the specific source of 2024 pain. Management flags that state clean-energy mandates could be reduced or made optional and that clean-energy funding is uncertain, a live policy risk under the current administration.

**Contract cancellability** — most contracts and MSAs can be canceled on 30-90 days' notice; backlog is not contractually assured revenue.

**Labor and pension** — skilled-craft shortages, work-stoppage risk from the ~85% union workforce, and multi-employer pension withdrawal liability (some plans "critical").

**Insurance/wildfire** — rising deductibles and shrinking coverage as MYRG works extensively on utility assets in wildfire-prone areas.

**Buyback lapse** — with a net-cash balance sheet generating $230M+ FCF, the absence of a return-of-capital program is a mild negative for the near-term equity story and worth tracking in 8-Ks.

The risk-factor score of 5.0 reflects that the most material risk (estimate-change reversal) is idiosyncratic, recurring, and only partially priced at a 33x multiple: a re-emergence of problem jobs would compress both earnings and the multiple simultaneously. Balanced against this: low customer concentration, a fortress balance sheet, and the deliberately reduced fixed-price T&D mix.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 74.9% versus 47.7% trailing historical volatility (rich, at the 87th percentile within the current chain). The term structure is in backwardation (74.9% near-term vs 60.6% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±16.2%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at –2.8% near-term, widening to –5.2% at six months, and the put/call volume ratio of 2.00 tilts defensive but is not extreme. ATM IV has already collapsed from 113.0% to 74.9% since the June snapshot as the price fell 16.3%, so a portion of the prior event premium has bled out. The market expects a large move around the next earnings release and leans modestly defensive, consistent with our view that the risk/reward is two-sided at the current premium multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($500-560):** The electrification/grid-investment supercycle drives multi-year revenue growth as large 2026 transmission awards begin construction in 2027+. Estimate-change momentum stays favorable, margins hold in the 12-14% gross range, and FY27 EPS reaches or exceeds the $13.47 consensus. A renewed and enlarged buyback authorization deploys the net-cash balance sheet and $230M+ FCF. Data-center C&I demand accelerates. At a sustained 35-40x on rising earnings, the stock reaches the high-$500s, matching the $564 analyst high. This requires no estimate-change reversal and continued multiple support.

**Bear Case ($270-300):** A new crop of troubled fixed-price projects re-emerges (as in 2024), estimate changes turn negative, and gross margin compresses back toward 9-10%. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer low (~21x, PRIM) on ~$10-11 normalized EPS, taking the stock to $270-300, roughly 21-29% downside, aligned with the $295 analyst low. Clean-energy policy cuts and tariff/inflation pressure on fixed-price contracts add drag. The lapsed buyback removes a support that was present in 2024-25.

**Base Case ($400-420):** Margins hold near current levels, FY26 EPS lands around $11.50-12.00, and the multiple compresses modestly toward the high-20s as growth normalizes. Backlog conversion is steady but 2026 revenue growth is capped by deferred transmission starts. The stock trades in a $400-420 range, modest upside from the current $379, roughly matching consensus at ~28x FY26. This is the most likely 12-month outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment point is the Q3 2026 earnings release (Q2 was already reported per the 7/15/26 scheduling release). Given the options backwardation, this is the near-term catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$200s/low-$300s, compressing the forward multiple to ~22-25x normalized EPS (~$12), restoring asymmetric upside.
- Renewal or enlargement of the share-repurchase authorization, redeploying the net-cash balance sheet, a concrete signal in an 8-K.
- Continued positive estimate-change momentum with two consecutive quarters of net-favorable percentage-of-completion revisions, confirming the durability of the margin recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A return to negative estimate changes (gross-margin impact turning materially negative), signaling a repeat of the 2024 problem-project cycle.
- Gross margin compressing below 10% for two consecutive quarters.
- A material clean-energy policy cut or a large fixed-price project write-down disclosed in an 8-K or 10-Q.

**Base case target:** $410, derived from ~34x annualized run-rate/consensus FY26 EPS of ~$12, reflecting a modest multiple as growth normalizes, roughly 8% upside from $379.17. **Bull target:** $530 (~40x rising earnings). **Bear target:** $290 (~24x on compressed margins/multiple), roughly 24% downside. The risk/reward from the current price is close to symmetric, which is why we hold rather than buy.

## Conclusion

MYR Group is a high-quality specialty electrical contractor with a fortress net-cash balance sheet, a broad-based and confirmed margin recovery, and durable multi-year demand from the grid-investment and data-center electrification cycle. The estimate-revision momentum and four consecutive beats, culminating in the largest Q1'26 surprise, are genuine positive forward signals. Financial health and growth prospects are the strongest dimensions and warrant high scores.

The stock is fully valued. At ~32-33x forward earnings on a cyclical 3-5% net-margin contractor, with a lapsed buyback, net insider selling into strength, degrading analyst sentiment, and options markets pricing a ±16% near-term move, the near-term risk/reward is roughly symmetric. The ~24% decline from the June 30 high improves the entry marginally but does not create a compelling one against normalized earnings power. The durability of favorable percentage-of-completion estimate changes must be re-verified each quarter, and its reversibility is what makes the premium multiple risky.

We maintain a **HOLD** (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.05, confirming rather than reversing the modest downward trajectory in prior reads. The fundamentals have improved since June while the price has fallen, but valuation remains the binding constraint. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the high-$200s/low-$300s or on a renewed buyback plus sustained favorable estimate-change momentum.
