**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FirstEnergy is a pure-play regulated electric utility serving roughly 6 million customers across Ohio, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, West Virginia, Maryland, and New York. The investment case rests on a single mechanism: rate-base growth funded by a large multi-year capital program, now amplified by a data-center load-growth thesis within its PJM footprint. Management has guided to a ~$36 billion 2026–2030 "Energize365" capital plan, and the concrete near-term data-center demand pipeline reached roughly 6.4 GW as of Q2 2026. Geographically, FE sits in one of the prime AI/data-center corridors in the country, which is the durable, structurally supported part of the story.

The counterweights are substantial and specific. FE runs a leveraged balance sheet (debt/equity ~3.36x) with persistently negative free cash flow by design: capex (~$4.7B) far exceeds operating cash flow, forcing continuous debt and periodic equity issuance. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this financing model becomes more expensive and per-share growth gets diluted. The company also carries a residual House Bill 6 litigation tail (securities class action outstanding, former officers indicted January 2025, potential rider-disgorgement exposure in Ohio) that a generic utility screen misses. Rising PJM capacity costs are pushing customer bills up, generating affordability backlash in New Jersey and Ohio (Senate Bill 2) that directly conflicts with the pace at which FE can recover its capital plan.

At ~$49.65, FE trades at ~27x trailing consensus EPS and ~18x forward consensus EPS ($2.74 CY / $2.95 next year), near its 52-week high of $52.34 and up ~20% over the trailing year. Current-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.1–0.2% over 90 days), the company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters, and analyst sentiment is degrading with a hold-weighted consensus (9 holds vs. 8 buys). We rate FE a HOLD on an income basis. The ~3.8% dividend and mid-single-digit rate-base compounding justify owning it for yield, but there is no asymmetric upside at the current price, and we would want a pullback into the low-$40s or a clean litigation-clearing event before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

FirstEnergy operates as a regulated monopoly across its six-state service territory. It exited competitive generation years ago and now runs three segments:

- **Distribution** — ten regulated distribution utilities (Ohio Edison, Penn Power, Met-Ed, JCP&L, Mon Power, Potomac Edison, and others) delivering electricity to end customers.
- **Integrated** — West Virginia operations that still own regulated coal generation (Mon Power / Potomac Edison), where FE both generates and delivers power.
- **Stand-Alone Transmission** — the FERC-regulated, formula-rate-driven high-voltage transmission business, historically FE's highest-return and fastest-growing segment.

FE does not compete for customers. Its economic engine is regulatory: the size of its rate base and its ability to earn authorized returns on invested capital. The equity thesis is therefore almost entirely a function of (a) how much capital it can deploy into rate base at reasonable ROEs, (b) whether regulators keep approving cost recovery, and (c) whether the data-center load-growth pipeline converts into recoverable investment. This structure is common to regulated utilities; what differentiates FE is its PJM location (favorable for data-center demand) and its HB6 legacy (unfavorable for governance/litigation risk).

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe a growth-through-rate-base story on a leveraged balance sheet.

- **Revenue:** Grew from roughly $12.5B to ~$15.1B over three years — mid-single-digit top-line growth from rate increases and rising demand.
- **Net income:** ~$1.27B (2025), up from ~$1.13B (2024). Reported earnings history is volatile: $1.28B (2021), $439M (2022, depressed by one-time items), $1.18B (2023). Management steers to core/operating earnings, which is the more meaningful metric given the noise.
- **Trailing EPS:** $1.76, producing a headline trailing P/E of ~27x. The forward multiple sits at ~18x — a gap driven by the reported/core earnings divergence rather than a cyclical trough.
- **Capital intensity is the defining feature.** Capex ran ~$4.7B in the most recent year, producing persistently negative free cash flow. This is by design; FE is mid-program on Energize365.
- **Balance sheet:** Debt/equity ~3.36x. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.84 screens as "distress," but this is a false alarm if read literally — the Altman model penalizes asset-heavy, debt-funded balance sheets and is not calibrated for regulated utilities. It is not a bankruptcy signal. The real message is that leverage and financing risk are the key vulnerabilities, especially with rates elevated.
- **Financing needs:** The capital plan far exceeds operating cash flow, so FE relies on ongoing debt issuance and periodic equity issuance. This creates dilution and refinancing risk and makes credit-rating maintenance a strategic priority.
- **Dividend:** ~3.8% yield, a meaningful income component, but it competes with the capital program for cash — a structural tension in a negative-FCF model.

**Earnings quality and delivery:** The recent beat/miss record is a concern. FE missed EPS in three of the last four quarters (Q2 2026 -3.9%, Q1 2026 -2.1%, Q4 2025 -6.6%) after a single beat in Q3 2025 (+4.3%). For a regulated utility where earnings should be relatively predictable, a string of small misses suggests either conservative-then-slipping guidance or cost/regulatory pressures eating into recovery. Missing its own quarterly numbers three times running is inconsistent with the "credibility rebuild" narrative and tempers confidence in forward estimates.

The financial-health picture is below-average on our framework, reflecting the combination of a leveraged balance sheet, negative FCF, external-capital dependency, and a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the cost of the entire model. This is where we place the most analytical weight, and it is the primary reason the overall score sits near the middle of the range.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest part of the case, and it is broad-based rather than a single-company story.

The regulated-utility sector is entering a capex supercycle. Industry estimates point to roughly $1.3 trillion of U.S. utility capex through 2030, driven by data-center/AI electricity demand, electrification, and grid hardening. The demand signal is confirmed across the sector — regulated-utility hard data now corroborates the data-center load surge, and FE's PJM footprint (Ohio, Pennsylvania, New Jersey) is a prime corridor.

Earlier 10-K disclosures reference a much larger potential pipeline (up to ~17,000 MW through 2035), but that figure should be discounted heavily; the 6.4 GW is the actionable number, and even that requires interconnection, tariff structuring, and regulatory approval before it converts to rate base. These are FE's regulated interconnection loads: the upside is rate base and delivered volume, not merchant generation margin. The capital plan underpins a rate-base growth trajectory that supports a mid-single-digit EPS growth target.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive on the near-term windows (current quarter +5.4% over 90 days, next quarter +3.8% over 90 days) but flat on the annual figures that drive valuation (current year +0.2%, next year +0.1% over 90 days). The near-term upticks reflect quarter-to-quarter timing, but consensus has not revised its view of FE's structural earnings power. The +30%+ revision velocity that would flag a genuine acceleration is absent. This is a steady mid-single-digit compounder, not an accelerating story.

Growth is real and durable, but it is bounded — capped by affordability constraints on the recovery pace and by the dilution/financing drag of the negative-FCF model. We score growth above the midpoint but not high, reflecting the flat annual revisions.

On a forward basis FE screens below the peer median P/E of 21.7, with a marginally better-than-median dividend yield of 3.8%. That is not a compelling discount given FE's higher leverage, residual HB6 litigation exposure, and weaker recent earnings delivery relative to cleaner peers like WEC and EVRG. The utilities trading at deep discounts (EIX at 7.9x, ES at 14.6x) carry their own idiosyncratic overhangs, so the median is the right anchor rather than the low end.

Applying the cycle-adjusted discipline: there is no revenue growth >40%, no margin expansion story, and no +30% estimate revision velocity. Annual estimates are flat. Trailing P/E is not distorted by a cyclical trough — it reflects the persistent gap between reported GAAP and core earnings. The forward multiple is therefore the appropriate lens, and at ~18x for a mid-single-digit grower with a leveraged balance sheet, the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap.

The stock sits near its 52-week high after a ~20% trailing-year advance. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but the combination of a full-ish forward multiple, flat annual revisions, a hold-weighted analyst consensus, and degrading sentiment means the current price offers little asymmetric upside. Analyst targets (low $48, mean/median $53, high $56) imply ~7% upside to the mean plus the ~3.8% yield — a total-return profile of roughly 10-11%, which is respectable for a utility but not distinguished, and it comes with the affordability and litigation tails attached.

A below-median forward P/E on a leveraged utility with flat estimates is the kind of setup that historically sorts the wrong way; cheapness without a catalyst is a warning, not a buy signal. There is no identifiable near-term catalyst to close a valuation gap, so we score valuation below the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, FE faces no customer competition; its competitive position is defined by regulatory relationships and the trajectory of its rate base. On trajectory, the position is improving at the margin:

- **Structural tailwind:** The PJM footprint places FE in a prime data-center corridor at the start of a multi-year utility capex supercycle. This is a genuine, structurally supported demand driver that the sector broadly shares but that FE is well-positioned to capture.
- **Transmission franchise:** The FERC-regulated formula-rate transmission business is historically the highest-return, fastest-growing segment and a durable earnings contributor.
- **Capital competition:** FE competes for capital against AEP, Exelon, PPL, and PSEG. Its relative TSR outperformance (5-year TSR 179.8 vs. peer group 143.8) suggests the market currently rewards its turnaround/growth narrative.

The offsets are regulatory rather than commercial. Affordability backlash (NJ regulatory action on JCP&L, Ohio SB2) constrains the pace of cost recovery and can compress achievable ROEs. West Virginia coal exposure in the Integrated segment carries environmental and stranded-asset risk. On net, the competitive/positioning trajectory is modestly favorable — the load-growth structural story outweighs the regulatory friction — but it is not a widening-moat situation. We score it slightly above the midpoint, with the caveat that this dimension has been a weak predictor recently and we lean on it lightly.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are more specific than a generic utility screen would suggest, which is why the risk score sits below the midpoint.

1. **Legacy HB6 / anti-corruption liability (the defining idiosyncratic risk).** FE's Ohio subsidiary was central to the House Bill 6 bribery scandal. The Deferred Prosecution Agreement completed in July 2024 (~$230M penalty), but the shareholder securities class action remains outstanding, and two former FE officers were indicted in January 2025. Residual exposure includes litigation costs, potential disgorgement of amounts collected under HB6-related riders, and ongoing Ohio regulatory scrutiny. This is a live, quantifiable tail that is not fully priced.

2. **Financing and leverage risk.** With a ~$36B capital plan and negative FCF, FE must continuously access debt and equity markets. Higher-for-longer rates raise the plan's cost and pressure credit metrics; a downgrade would raise financing costs across a large debt stack. Equity issuance dilutes per-share growth.

3. **PJM capacity / affordability backlash.** Sharply higher PJM capacity auction prices have raised customer bills, generating political and regulatory pushback (NJ affordability actions, Ohio SB2). This threatens the pace at which FE can recover its capital plan, placing the growth story and the affordability backlash in direct tension.

4. **Multi-jurisdiction rate-case risk.** Earnings depend on outcomes across six jurisdictions simultaneously. Adverse ROE determinations or disallowed cost recovery hit earnings directly.

5. **Data-center execution risk.** The load-growth thesis depends on speculative pipelines materializing and being served economically. FERC's 2026 large-load order is reshaping how large loads are treated — a tailwind if it clarifies cost recovery, a risk if it shifts cost allocation unfavorably.

6. **Coal exposure (Integrated segment).** West Virginia coal carries environmental, decarbonization, and stranded-asset risk.

The earnings-miss streak adds a visibility risk not captured in the risk factors — for a business that should be highly predictable, repeated small misses erode confidence in guidance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$56)
Data-center demand converts faster than expected, adding recoverable rate base ahead of schedule. FERC's large-load order clarifies favorable cost recovery. Regulators approve the capital plan at reasonable ROEs despite affordability noise, and the HB6 securities litigation resolves without material additional liability. Management's credibility rebuild continues, the multiple holds at the upper end of the peer range, and EPS compounds at the high end of the mid-single-digit target. This gets the stock to the analyst high of ~$56 (~13% upside plus the yield).

### Base Case (~$53)
FE compounds rate base at mid-single digits, data-center load ramps gradually (6.4 GW pipeline converting in stages), and regulatory outcomes are adequate but constrained by affordability pressure. Estimates stay roughly flat-to-modestly-up. The stock tracks the peer median forward multiple and the dividend, delivering total returns around 10-11% (mean target ~$53 plus ~3.8% yield). This is the most likely outcome given flat annual revisions and a hold-weighted consensus.

### Bear Case (~$42-44)
Affordability backlash intensifies in NJ and Ohio, regulators disallow or delay cost recovery, and the earnings-miss pattern continues. Rising rates raise the cost of the $36B plan, credit metrics deteriorate, and equity issuance accelerates dilution. The HB6 securities litigation produces a material adverse ruling or rider disgorgement. The multiple compresses toward the lower end of the peer range. This takes the stock back toward the analyst low of ~$48 and potentially into the low-$40s (roughly -11% to -15% from current), near the 52-week low of $41.30.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income rationale).** The ~3.8% dividend and mid-single-digit rate-base compounding support owning FE for yield and steady total return, but the current price offers no asymmetric upside and several specific overhangs argue against adding here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the low-$40s (near the 52-week low of $41.30), which would restore an asymmetric entry with the yield above 4% and forward P/E compressed further below the peer median.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity on the annual figures (current-year/next-year EPS revised up meaningfully, not just quarterly timing), confirming the data-center load is converting into structural earnings power.
- A clean resolution of the HB6 securities class action without material additional liability or rider disgorgement.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse HB6 litigation ruling or Ohio rider-disgorgement order.
- Evidence the negative-FCF/dilution model is breaking under the higher-rate path — a credit downgrade, an outsized equity raise, or a cut to the capital plan/EPS growth target.
- Continued EPS misses combined with falling annual estimate revisions, indicating regulatory recovery is lagging the capital spend.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, plus monitoring of the PJM capacity/affordability regulatory docket in New Jersey and Ohio (SB2 developments) and any HB6 litigation milestones. The natural checkpoint is the next earnings release and any FERC large-load order clarifications.

## Conclusion

FirstEnergy is a competently-managed regulated utility with a genuine, structurally supported data-center load-growth tailwind in a prime PJM corridor. That is the durable part of the thesis, and it justifies holding the stock for its ~3.8% yield and mid-single-digit rate-base compounding. The offsets are specific and material: a leveraged, negative-FCF balance sheet whose financing cost rises with a higher-for-longer rate path; a residual HB6 litigation tail with former officers indicted and a securities class action outstanding; affordability-driven regulatory friction that caps the recovery pace; and an earnings-miss streak that undercuts the credibility narrative.

Near the 52-week high, on ~18x forward EPS with flat annual estimate revisions and a hold-weighted, degrading analyst consensus, the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap. The total-return math (~7% to the mean target plus the yield) is respectable but unremarkable for a utility carrying this specific set of tails. We rate FE a HOLD on an income basis and would look for a pullback into the low-$40s, a durable turn in annual estimates, or a litigation-clearing event before turning constructive. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the trajectory is modestly improving (growth up, offset by declining risk scoring), and nothing in the current data warrants either an upgrade or a downgrade.
