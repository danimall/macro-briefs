## Executive Summary

Alcoa is a two-speed company operating at an extraordinary divergence between its segments. The Aluminum division generated $694M in Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA—driven by realized prices of $4,209/mt (up 31% YoY) and a Section 232-inflated Midwest premium that rose 215%—while the Alumina division posted a negative $40M EBITDA as API prices collapsed 50% to $309/mt. The stock's trajectory depends entirely on which segment's dynamics dominate over the next 12 months.

The stock has appreciated 113% from its May 2025 low of $25.83 to its current $62.24, closely tracking the aluminum price surge driven by Middle East conflict disruptions (>2,500 kmt of global smelting capacity curtailed) and tariff-driven premiums. Forward EPS estimates have risen dramatically—the current quarter consensus of $2.22 is up 84% in 90 days—indicating genuine fundamental improvement. At ~16x trailing and ~8.6x current-year estimates ($7.21 consensus), the valuation appears reasonable for the earnings trajectory, though the most recent quarter missed estimates by 9% and analyst sentiment is degrading (strongSell recommendations increased from 2 to 3).

The stock warrants a HOLD with a growth orientation. The aluminum thesis is structurally supported by global supply constraints, but three risk factors prevent a higher-conviction call: (1) the alumina segment is bleeding money with no near-term catalyst for reversal, (2) tariff policy is binary—if Section 232 is modified, $500M+ of annual profitability evaporates overnight, and (3) Australian mine approval risk creates an existential multi-year overhang.

## Company Overview

Alcoa Corporation is the world's largest vertically integrated aluminum company, with operations spanning bauxite mining, alumina refining, aluminum smelting, and energy generation across 25 locations in 8 countries. The business is organized into two reportable segments:

- **Alumina:** 11.7M kmt base refining capacity (1.0M kmt curtailed). Approximately two-thirds of production is sold to third parties at Alumina Price Index (API)-linked pricing; the remainder feeds internal smelters.
- **Aluminum:** 2,645 kmt base smelting capacity (only 112 kmt idle after extensive restarts). Products sold at LME + Midwest premium + product premium. Also owns energy generation assets.

Key recent strategic actions include the August 2024 acquisition of Alumina Limited (gaining full AWAC ownership), the July 2025 sale of a 25.1% Ma'aden stake for $1.35B, the formation of the San Ciprián JV with Trento EQT, and the permanent closure of Kwinana refinery. The San Ciprián smelter restart was completed April 7, 2026, bringing the full 228 kmt capacity online.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

Revenue recovered to $12.83B in 2025 (from $10.55B in 2023 trough) and is running at approximately $12.8B annualized in Q1 2026. Net income swung from -$651M (2023) to $1,157M (2025), though the 2025 figure was inflated by $786M in Ma'aden divestiture gains and $197M in mark-to-market gains.

Q1 2026 net income was $425M ($1.60 EPS diluted), driven almost entirely by the Aluminum segment. The critical dynamic:
- Aluminum segment EBITDA: $694M (vs. $134M Q1 2025)—a 5.2x improvement driven by LME price increases and Midwest premium expansion
- Alumina segment EBITDA: -$40M (vs. +$664M Q1 2025)—a total reversal caused by API collapse from $612 to $309/mt

**Balance Sheet**

Cash of $1.35B against ~$2.44B total debt (no maturities until 2029 after May 2026 redemption of remaining 2028 Notes). The $1.25B revolver is fully undrawn. Net debt is approximately $1.1B.

A hidden asset: $1.49B in Ma'aden shares (locked until mid-2028+), representing 9% of current market cap in a single non-core holding.

Credit ratings are improving: S&P upgraded to BB+ (March 2026), Fitch revised to positive outlook. The trajectory toward investment grade is constructive.

**Cash Flow**

Operating cash flow was -$179M in Q1 2026, driven by seasonal working capital build and delayed Australian shipments (cyclone-related). This should normalize in Q2. Full-year capex guidance is $750M (up from $618M in 2025), reflecting smelter restarts and maintenance.

The $500M share repurchase authorization remains entirely unused since July 2022. Management is prioritizing debt reduction and operational investment over returns to shareholders, which is reasonable given the cyclical environment but signals conservatism.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.24, Alcoa trades at:
- 16.0x trailing EPS ($4.37)
- 8.6x current-year consensus ($7.21)
- 9.3x next-year consensus ($6.70)
- Median analyst target: $76 (22% upside)

Versus peer medians (P/E 24.8x), Alcoa's 16x trailing appears cheap. However, the appropriate comparison framework matters: Alcoa is a deeply cyclical commodity producer whose current earnings represent a near-peak configuration (aluminum at multi-year highs, tariff premium at maximum). The trailing 16x is artificially low because recent-quarter earnings are elevated; the forward 8.6x on $7.21 FY26 estimates is more relevant.

If aluminum prices normalize toward $3,500/mt (from $4,200+ currently) and Midwest premiums compress by 30-40% (under any tariff modification scenario), mid-cycle earnings power is more likely $3.50-4.50 per share, implying a 14-18x mid-cycle P/E. This makes the stock roughly fairly valued on normalized earnings, with upside optionality from sustained supply disruption and downside risk from policy change.

The $1.49B Ma'aden stake (~$5.65/share) provides a valuation floor component that is not reflected in earnings but will become realizable post-2028.

## Competitive Landscape

Alcoa's competitive position benefits from several structural factors:
- **Vertical integration:** Bauxite-to-aluminum control provides cost visibility (when mines are producing high-grade ore)
- **North American smelting capacity:** Rare among Western producers; positioned to benefit from tariff-created price umbrella
- **ELYSIS technology:** Joint venture with Rio Tinto for zero-carbon smelting; commercial deployment by 2030s
- **Geographic diversification:** Operations in 8 countries reduce single-jurisdiction risk

However, competitive challenges are mounting:
- **Lower-grade bauxite** in Australia is increasing costs and reducing yield
- **Chinese/Indonesian refinery expansion** is structurally oversupplying the alumina market
- **5-year TSR underperformance** (241 vs. peer group 331) reflects persistent operational challenges vs. peers like Rio Tinto's aluminum division

The Middle East conflict has temporarily improved Alcoa's relative positioning by removing competitors' capacity from the global supply base, but this advantage is not durable.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks (High Impact, Medium-to-High Probability)**

1. **Section 232 Tariff Policy Binary:** The 50% tariff on Canadian aluminum has inflated Midwest premiums by ~$2,400/mt, creating an estimated $500M+ annual profit uplift for the Aluminum segment. Any modification (US-Canada trade agreement, policy reversal) would collapse premiums within weeks. This is the single largest source of near-term earnings risk and is entirely outside management's control.

2. **Alumina Segment Losses Persisting:** API at $309/mt vs. $334/mt adjusted operating costs means the segment is destroying capital. Chinese refinery capacity additions show no signs of slowing. If alumina remains depressed through 2026, this represents a $150-200M annual drag vs. 2025.

3. **Australian Mine Approval Risk:** The extended timeline (new mining areas not available until 2029), $36M penalty for "unlawful clearing," and ongoing investigations create genuine operational constraints. If approvals are severely restricted, Alcoa's #1 bauxite source is impaired.

**Moderate Risks**

4. **Embedded Derivative Losses:** $1.25B in Level 3 liabilities (LME-linked power contracts) generating ~$421M in realized losses over the next 12 months. These are burning off over time but represent a persistent headwind.

5. **Geopolitical Conflict Reversal:** The supply disruption supporting aluminum prices could partially reverse if Hormuz transit normalizes. A 15-20% aluminum price decline from current levels would significantly reduce aluminum segment profitability.

6. **San Ciprián JV Losses:** Continuing cash drain ($153M convertible note from Alcoa in December 2025), though Alcoa's 75% ownership limits exposure.

## Conclusion

Alcoa's aluminum segment profitability is structurally supported by tight global supply and tariff-inflated premiums, generating estimates momentum that is genuine and rapid. The forward P/E of 8.6x on $7.21 FY26 EPS is optically cheap, but this reflects peak-cycle earnings dependent on the confluence of geopolitical supply disruption and trade policy—both of which can reverse quickly.

The alumina segment's losses, the Q1 earnings miss (-9%), degrading analyst sentiment (strongSell count rising), and binary tariff policy risk collectively create a risk profile that doesn't support a BUY recommendation at current prices. The stock has already moved 113% from its lows, discounting much of the positive revision momentum. The consensus median target of $76 offers 22% upside, but the $52 downside target (if tariffs are modified or supply normalizes) represents meaningful risk.

HOLD with a growth orientation. The positive trajectory in estimates and structural aluminum tightness create a constructive medium-term setup, but entry should await either a pullback to $50-52 (where risk/reward improves materially) or clarity on tariff policy sustainability.
