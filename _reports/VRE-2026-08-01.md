**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Veris Residential is a Northeast-focused, Class A multifamily REIT, the post-transformation successor to a waterfront/suburban office platform, anchored on the Jersey City Gold Coast. The financial record through FY2025 describes a company that has finished the destructive phase of its repositioning and begun a modest recovery: revenue grew **+6.4% to $288.4M in FY2025** while total assets shrank another **9.3% to $2,706.9M**, SG&A fell to **12.7% of revenue from 15.8% in FY2022**, stockholders' equity rose **$52.2M**, and current maturities of debt collapsed from **$348.8M to $30.0M** against cash that rose from $7.3M to $14.1M. Three of the last four quarters beat consensus.

We are downgrading our stance from AVOID to **HOLD**. Two prior reports carried an AVOID while the composite score climbed from 3.9 to 5.2, and that combination was internally inconsistent. On re-examination, the bear pillars do not clear the bar for an active avoid. The first pillar, a fixed-charge coverage and leverage band constructed by proxying EBITDA from one year of operating cash flow and sweeping an assumed interest rate, is an estimate, not an observation, and it sits opposite the single hard leverage datapoint available, which points the other way. The second pillar, valuation, resolves to approximately asset value, and a fairly-priced improving business is a hold. There is no evidence of structural impairment: the portfolio is high-quality gateway multifamily, the near-term refinancing pressure was resolved during 2025, and revenue inflected.

What keeps us from a BUY is the shape of the payoff rather than the direction of the fundamentals. At the last observed price of approximately $18.86 (a secondary-source quote dated 2026-03-18, stale relative to a cluster of three 8-Ks filed in May 2026), the implied cap rate on our estimated NOI is roughly **5.3%**, in line with gateway Class A pricing. With net debt in the range of $1.24-1.39B against estimated EBITDA near $136M, every 25bp of cap-rate movement is worth roughly $1.60 per share, about 9% of the equity. That is a demanding environment in which to pay full asset value for a 9-10x-levered REIT with unverified covenant headroom. We would move to BUY on evidence of NAV discount plus verified balance-sheet terms; we would move to AVOID on evidence of punitive refinancing economics or a same-store reversal.

## Company Overview

Veris self-describes, in a company press release dated February 5, 2026, as "a forward-thinking, Northeast-focused, Class A multifamily REIT." Its flagship disclosed asset is **Haus25, a 750-unit Jersey City waterfront high-rise** stabilized in February 2023, a very large single asset for a company with roughly $1.8-1.9B of equity value. The relevant demand mechanism for this portfolio is the rent spread between Manhattan/Brooklyn and Hudson County, mediated by PATH transit access and Manhattan office attendance, set against the Jersey City new-supply pipeline.

The structure appears to be an UPREIT: basic and reported diluted share counts differ by a stable **8.7-9.0M units** in every year from FY2022 to FY2025 (FY2025: 93.355M basic, 102.363M diluted), consistent with an exchangeable operating-partnership and LTIP block. We use that gap as evidence an OP-unit block exists, not as a precise dilution measure; in the three loss years the reported diluted count exceeds basic, which cannot be the GAAP EPS denominator, so the tag is a partnership-level or non-EPS count.

Our information set is materially incomplete and readers should weight the conclusions accordingly. We have annual XBRL facts for FY2022-FY2025, the Part III 10-K/A, a DEF 14A extract covering FY2024 compensation, the EDGAR filing indices, a quarterly consensus-surprise series, and limited press coverage. We do not have the FY2025 Item 1, Item 1A, or Item 7 narrative, the debt footnote, the cash flow statement detail, unit and community counts, occupancy, same-store NOI, the development pipeline, JV structures, insider transactions, or any sell-side coverage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: three years of contraction, then inflection.**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 355.0 | 279.9 | 271.1 | 288.4 |
| YoY | — | −21.2% | −3.1% | **+6.4%** |
| Total assets ($M) | 3,920.8 | 3,241.0 | 2,982.7 | 2,706.9 |
| SG&A ($M) | 56.2 | 44.5 | 39.1 | 36.8 |
| SG&A % revenue | 15.8% | 15.9% | 14.4% | 12.7% |
| Net income ($M) | (52.1) | (107.3) | (23.1) | **75.2** |
| Equity ($M) | 1,235.7 | 1,137.5 | 1,099.4 | 1,151.6 |
| Cash ($M) | 26.8 | 28.0 | 7.3 | 14.1 |
| Current debt ($M) | 0.0 | n/a | **348.8** | **30.0** |

The FY2023-FY2024 decline is disposition arithmetic, not demand: assets fell in lockstep, down $1,213.9M or 31.0% over three years. The informative datapoint is FY2025 revenue growth against a shrinking asset base. Mechanically, that implies same-store revenue growth meaningfully above +6.4%, because the headline is net of NOI sold. We cannot confirm this without the same-store disclosure, and an alternative explanation exists: a full year of contribution from an asset that only partially contributed in FY2024, such as a completed lease-up, which would not repeat.

**Earnings quality: the GAAP profit is very likely gain-driven, and the consensus series independently corroborates it.** Using only tagged FY2025 lines, revenue $288.4M less cost of revenue $9.3M less SG&A $36.8M less D&A $86.3M equals $156.1M, against reported pretax income of $75.1M, an $81.1M gap of untagged items. Two tags are implausible on their face: interest expense at $3.5M against roughly $1.3-1.4B of borrowings (a 5% coupon would be $65-70M), and cost of revenue at $9.3M when a $288M Class A portfolio carries $110-130M of property operating expense plus real estate taxes. Substituting plausible magnitudes, the pretax line only reconciles to +$75.1M with roughly $100-110M of net gains inside it. Cash flow corroborates independently: net income $75.2M plus D&A $86.3M equals $161.5M, yet operating cash flow was **$76.0M**, an $85.5M negative reconciling gap of the shape produced when a large non-cash gain on sale is removed from operating activities.

The quarterly consensus series provides a third line of corroboration. The four FY2025 actuals (−$0.07, +$0.25, −$0.12, −$0.03) sum to **+$0.03 per share**, or roughly $3M on 93.4M basic shares, against a $75.2M XBRL net income figure. The gap of approximately $0.77 per share is either (a) the provider reporting on a normalized basis that excludes gains on sale, or (b) the XBRL tag including noncontrolling interests and items excluded from EPS to common. Under either reading, the operating earnings base for FY2025 is near breakeven on a GAAP-adjusted basis, and the $75.2M headline should not be capitalized. **Do not apply a P/E, ROE (6.5%), ROA (2.8%), or net-margin (26.1%) screen to FY2025.** The same non-cash item does not contaminate the cash-flow or balance-sheet reads: OCF of $76.0M and the maturity reduction are separately observable.

The beat record deserves proportion. Three beats in four quarters sounds constructive, but the absolute surprises were $0.021, $0.316, −$0.070 and $0.021 on an estimate base between −$0.05 and −$0.10. Except for the Q2 figure, which is large enough to suggest a gain leaked into the reported number, these are rounding differences on a near-zero base, and the percentage surprises (27.1%, 480.5%, −137.6%, 40.6%) carry no information. We treat the record as weak-positive: management is not persistently disappointing.

**Cost discipline is the cleanest unambiguous positive.** SG&A fell $19.4M, or 34.6%, from FY2022 to FY2025 while FY2025 revenue grew. The FY2024→FY2025 step from 14.4% to 12.7% came from both a $2.3M absolute reduction and a $17.4M revenue increase. This flows directly to Core FFO per share without requiring rent growth. Part of the FY2022→FY2023 step-down reflects eliminating office-platform overhead alongside office dispositions, so not all of it is like-for-like efficiency; the last two years are.

**Leverage: what is observed versus what is estimated, and how we weight them.** The prior report's bear case rested on a fixed-charge coverage band of 1.6x-2.5x and leverage of 5.9x-9.4x. That band was constructed by proxying EBITDA from a single year of operating cash flow and sweeping an assumed interest rate across a range, because the interest-expense tag is demonstrably incomplete and no maturity schedule, covenant, revolver or unencumbered-asset data was obtained. We are not going to rest a rating on that construction while the one directly observable leverage fact points the other way: **current maturities fell $318.8M against cash that rose to $14.1M and equity that rose $52.2M.** Entering 2025 with $348.8M due against $7.3M of cash was an acute liquidity pressure point; at year-end it was not.

Our own estimate, presented as an estimate: taking NOI at 60% of revenue gives $173M; less SG&A of $36.8M gives EBITDA near $136M. Non-equity capital (total assets less the equity tag) is $1,555.3M, of which non-debt liabilities and possibly noncontrolling-interest book value account for perhaps $150-300M, implying debt of $1.25-1.40B and net debt of $1.24-1.39B. That is roughly **9-10x net debt/EBITDA**, high against large-cap multifamily peers, and the single most important reason we do not pay full asset value here. At a 5% blended coupon, interest of $62-70M against $173M of NOI implies interest coverage near 2.5x, and OCF of $76.0M covers our estimated $30.4M of FY2025 dividends about 2.5x. None of that is distress; all of it is estimate. We treat leverage as a valuation input (cap-rate sensitivity) rather than as a solvency thesis, because the solvency questions (maturity ladder, covenant headroom, unencumbered pool, secured/unsecured mix) remain unanswered.

Cash of $14.1M against $2.71B of assets (0.5%) is thin in absolute terms but normal for a REIT that runs on a revolver. We have no revolver visibility, which is a real gap rather than a reassurance.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers are identifiable, none of them large.

The first is **same-store revenue**. The +6.4% headline on a shrinking base implies underlying growth well above the print. Ownership unaffordability, with households priced out of buying and channeled toward renting in supply-constrained gateway markets Veris occupies, is a supportive backdrop, though we have no financing-cost or macro-rate figures in the sources to quantify it.

The second is **overhead leverage**. Each 100bp of SG&A/revenue is roughly $2.9M, or about $0.028 per diluted unit. The 310bp improvement since FY2022 is worth roughly $9M annually of Core FFO, which is material against a per-share cash-earnings base the proxy series suggests is in the $0.60-0.75 range.

The third is **balance-sheet normalization**. If the FY2025 refinancing was executed at reasonable cost, the drag from the near-term maturity wall is removed, and disposition proceeds can shift from debt paydown toward reinvestment. We cannot verify the cost.

The constraint is per-share history. The proxy's company-selected performance measure, which we infer is Core FFO per share, though the structured label returned null, ran **1.07 (FY2020), 0.68, 0.44, 0.53, 0.60 (FY2024)**. FY2024 sat at 56% of the FY2020 level. The trend since the FY2022 trough is +36% over two years, which is the constructive read; the level is the sobering one. Comparison across the transformation is not apples-to-apples, since FY2020's $1.07 was earned on a larger, more levered, lower-quality base, but the transformation consumed a large amount of per-share earning power that has not been rebuilt.

Absent FY2026 guidance, a Core FFO figure, or an earnings transcript, our growth view is a modest-single-digit same-store trajectory plus a fading overhead tailwind. That supports a 5.3 growth score: real, verified in direction, unquantified in magnitude, and not the kind of movement that re-rates a multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

For a REIT mid-recovery, the asset-value approach dominates earnings multiples, especially when trailing GAAP income is gain-contaminated.

Estimated NOI: $288.4M revenue × 60% = **$173M** (range $167-179M at 38-42% operating expense plus taxes). Net debt: **$1.24-1.39B**, midpoint $1.32B. Diluted units including the OP block: 102.4M.

At the last observed price, equity value is $1.93B, enterprise value approximately $3.25B, and the implied cap rate on estimated NOI is **173/3,250 = 5.3%**. The stock trades at, or a touch cheap to, private-market pricing for gateway Class A multifamily, before any credit for non-income-producing land or development parcels, which would lower the implied operating cap rate further, and before any deduction for the leverage-driven cost-of-capital disadvantage. (We do not have current gateway transaction cap-rate benchmarks in the sources; the private-market comparison is directional.)

Per-share NAV sensitivity on $173M of NOI and $1.32B of net debt:

| Cap rate | Gross asset value | Equity | Per unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.75% | $3.64B | $2.32B | $22.7 |
| 5.00% | $3.46B | $2.14B | $20.9 |
| 5.25% | $3.30B | $1.98B | $19.3 |
| 5.50% | $3.15B | $1.83B | $17.8 |
| 6.00% | $2.88B | $1.56B | $15.2 |

Each 25bp is worth roughly $1.60 per unit, about 9% of the equity, which is the arithmetic consequence of 9-10x leverage. We do not have current long-rate levels or forward policy-rate expectations in the sources, so we do not assert a directional view on yields; the cap-rate sensitivity table stands on its own as the dominant valuation driver.

Cross-checks. Price-to-book of 18.86/12.34 = **1.53x** is uninformative for a REIT carrying depreciated historical cost, and the book figure is subject to an unresolved question about whether the equity tag includes noncontrolling interests. On the FFO approach, if the FY2024 proxy measure of $0.60 is Core FFO per share, $18.86 is 31.4x trailing; FY2025 was very likely higher given +6.4% revenue and lower SG&A, so the current multiple is plausibly lower, still a premium to typical large-cap multifamily levels, which is what you would expect from a smaller, more levered, higher-G&A vehicle. We do not have the FY2025 figure and cannot compute it, which is why we anchor on cap rate.

The 2022 Kushner Companies/Fortress approach at **$16.00 per share**, rejected as "grossly" undervaluing the company, establishes that the asset base has drawn credible private capital at a specific price. It is 3.5+ years stale and there is no evidence of a live process. Its residual use is as a soft floor reference: the last observed price is 17.9% above it, which vindicates the board's price judgment in nominal terms while amounting to roughly 5% annualized over 3.3 years.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** There is no identified mispricing to arbitrage in either direction. Cheapness is not present, and where a low multiple exists (P/B, trailing earnings) it is an artifact rather than an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Portfolio quality is a genuine asset. Class A waterfront product with Manhattan-adjacent transit is difficult to replicate and expensive to build, and Veris now owns a focused version of it after shedding the office platform. The competitive weakness is not asset quality but **scale and cost of capital**: at roughly $1.9B of equity value with 9-10x leverage and no visible investment-grade rating or unencumbered-asset advantage, Veris competes for acquisitions and development capital against larger peers with materially cheaper funding. That gap is the structural argument against paying a full NAV price.

Trajectory matters more than level: portfolio quality improved, overhead intensity improved, near-term refinancing risk fell, and revenue inflected. Per-share earning power remains at roughly half the pre-transformation level and indexed TSR trailed the disclosed peer group by 24.7 points cumulatively through FY2024 ($76.26 vs $100.99). The company is improving on operations while still lagging on the metric shareholders are paid in. That combination is a 5.0.

We could not obtain named competitors, submarket supply data, new-lease versus renewal spreads, concession levels, turnover, or sector expense inflation, particularly insurance and real estate taxes, which have been industry-wide pressure points and are especially relevant for coastal New Jersey.

## Risk Assessment

**Cap-rate sensitivity amplified by leverage (primary).** At 9-10x net debt/EBITDA, 25bp of cap-rate expansion removes roughly 9% of equity value. Because the equity is thin relative to the asset base, cap-rate moves that are unrelated to apartment fundamentals are absorbed disproportionately by the stock and will not be offset by same-store rent growth in the short run.

**Concentration, unquantified.** "Northeast-focused" plus a disclosed 750-unit single asset implies correlated exposure to Hudson County property-tax policy, PATH reliability, Manhattan return-to-office patterns, the Jersey City supply pipeline, and coastal insurance cost and availability. We cannot state the percentage of NOI from the top submarket or the top five assets. This is the largest unquantified risk in the file.

**GAAP earnings reversion.** If FY2025's profit is gain-driven, FY2026 GAAP income can revert toward breakeven or negative even with healthy operations, and quantitative screens anchored on the loss-to-profit reversal face mechanical disappointment. Core FFO and GAAP net income can diverge legitimately; passive flows do not always distinguish.

**Refinancing terms, unknown.** The maturity wall shrank; we cannot confirm it shrank cheaply. A maturity extended at a materially higher coupon or with tighter covenants improves the calendar while worsening the economics. Some of the $318.8M reduction could also reflect reclassification.

**Unresolved recent filings.** Three 8-Ks in the twelve days from 2026-05-15 to 2026-05-27, with item codes unavailable to us, is not routine cadence. Candidates range from annual-meeting results plus a dividend declaration, through a financing sequence, executive change, or disposition, to a strategic transaction. The outcome range is wide and this is the highest-priority open item. It also means the reference price is stale in a way that could matter.

**Governance and pay.** Granted CEO compensation held roughly flat at $6.93M / $7.06M / $7.39M across FY2022-FY2024, three consecutive GAAP loss years with peer TSR underperformance. FY2021 compensation actually paid of $16.79M exceeded granted value of $10.97M in a year with a $119.0M net loss, most consistent with a CEO transition and a front-loaded inducement grant that marked up, though we did not read the CD&A. Offsetting: the FY2024 granted-to-paid haircut of 49% ($7.39M to $3.78M) shows the mark-to-market mechanism penalizing underperformance as designed, and the metric set (Absolute TSR, Relative TSR, Core FFO, Same Store NOI) is well constructed for a multifamily REIT.

**No insider data.** Form 4 retrieval failed. Absence of data is not absence of activity; open-market buying by the CEO or CFO would be among the most informative available signals for a company in this position, and we have none.

Risk score 5.5: the acute item (near-term maturities) was removed and the private-capital reference provides a soft floor, but leverage amplification, concentration, and the unresolved May filings are real and partly unpriced by us.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($23, +22%).** Same-store revenue growth in the mid-single digits sustains as ownership unaffordability channels gateway household formation into rentals. SG&A holds near 12.5% of revenue, so incremental NOI converts at high rates to Core FFO per share, which recovers toward $0.85-0.90 and closes more of the gap to the FY2020 level of $1.07. The FY2025 refinancing proves to have been executed at reasonable cost with covenant headroom, allowing disposition proceeds to shift from paydown to reinvestment. Long rates stabilize, gateway Class A cap rates hold near 4.75-5.00%, and a concentrated, hard-to-replicate waterfront portfolio at sub-$2B of equity value re-attracts the private capital that bid $16.00 in 2022, at a materially higher number. Valuation at a 4.75% cap: $22.7 per unit.

**Bear case ($15, −20%).** Long yields move higher and gateway cap rates widen to 6.0%, removing 9% of equity per 25bp; at 9-10x leverage the equity absorbs the full move. FY2026 GAAP earnings revert to a loss as disposition gains do not repeat, and screen-driven holders exit. The FY2025 refinancing turns out to have been done at a materially higher coupon, so interest expense steps up and interest coverage compresses toward 2.0x. Jersey City supply plus softer Manhattan office attendance flattens same-store growth, while insurance and real estate taxes inflate operating expense, compressing the NOI margin below 60%. The overhead tailwind is exhausted, with no further $19M of SG&A to remove. Valuation at a 6.0% cap: $15.2 per unit.

**Our view.** The operating improvement is real and we do not dispute it. The issue is what the price already reflects: a ~5.3% implied cap rate is approximately private-market pricing, so the base-case return is roughly the dividend plus same-store NOI growth net of the cost of debt, with a levered call option on cap-rate compression. That is a hold. It is not an avoid, because none of the conditions that define structural impairment are present: the maturity wall cleared, revenue inflected, overhead intensity improved, equity rose, and the portfolio is high-quality gateway product rather than a secularly declining asset class. It is not a buy, because there is no identified mispricing, no verified Core FFO or debt schedule to underwrite, and three unexplained material-event filings between us and the last observable price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate on retrieval of the FY2025 10-K narrative, debt footnote, and cash flow statement; and at the next quarterly report, which should provide same-store revenue and NOI, Core FFO per share, the current dividend rate, and FY2026 guidance. The contents of the 2026-05-15, 2026-05-21 and 2026-05-27 8-Ks should be resolved before any position is taken or sized; that is a one-filing read.

**Upgrade to BUY on any two of:**
1. **Verified NAV discount.** Confirmed FY2025 NOI and net debt implying a cap rate of 5.75% or wider, roughly $17.00 or below on our estimates, with the debt schedule showing no maturity above 15% of total debt in any of FY2026-FY2028.
2. **Verified Core FFO inflection.** FY2025 Core FFO per share at or above $0.75 (vs. the inferred FY2024 $0.60) with FY2026 guidance implying further growth, and same-store revenue growth of 3.5%+ with same-store NOI growth exceeding it.
3. **Financing terms confirmed benign.** The FY2025 refinancing disclosed at a weighted-average coupon within 75bp of the retired debt, with revolver availability above $200M and covenant headroom disclosed.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Same-store revenue growth decelerating below 1.5% YoY, or NOI margin falling below 58%.
2. Disclosure that FY2025 maturities were extended at a coupon 150bp+ above the retired debt, or any covenant amendment granting relief in exchange for tighter terms; alternatively, interest coverage disclosed below 2.0x.
3. A dividend cut, a covenant breach or waiver, or a going-concern-adjacent liquidity disclosure.
4. Confirmation that the May 2026 8-K cluster contains an adverse item: an executive departure without succession, an impairment, or a financing on distressed terms.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold. Size below a full weight given the leverage-driven cap-rate beta and the information opacity. New capital is better deployed after the next print, or on a decline toward the mid-$16s, which would put the implied cap rate near 5.75% and restore genuine asymmetry.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Veris has done the hard part of a REIT repositioning, selling the legacy platform, cutting overhead by a third, and refinancing its way out of the near-term maturity wall, and it delivered its first revenue growth in four years while still shrinking the asset base. The operating direction is positive and the beat record, while built on trivially small absolute surprises, is not deteriorating.

The equity is priced at approximately what a private buyer would pay for the assets, and it carries roughly 9-10x net debt/EBITDA where each 25bp of cap-rate expansion removes about 9% of equity value. Base case $19.50 (a 5.15% cap rate on estimated NOI of $173M less net debt of $1.32B, across 102.4M diluted units), bull $23, bear $15, against a last observed price of $18.86, roughly 3% to base, 22% to bull, 20% to bear. The prior AVOID rested on a leverage band we constructed from proxied EBITDA and an assumed interest rate, which sat opposite the one hard leverage fact in the file; we are not willing to carry a bearish rating on that basis. Fair value plus unverified covenants plus three unexplained material-event filings does not justify buying. Overall score 5.1, essentially unchanged from the prior 5.2, with the recommendation corrected to match the composite.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: multiple macro-rate claims (30-year at highest since 2007, 10-year at 4.67%, September policy-rate hike odds, Fed "fifth consecutive hold with three hawkish dissents," Freddie Mac 6.66% mortgage rate, gateway 4.75-5.25% transaction cap rates) were not in the sources and have been removed; the rising-rate narrative was a stated support for the HOLD-not-BUY payoff shape, so the valuation/rate argument now rests on cap-rate sensitivity alone rather than on any asserted directional view of yields.
