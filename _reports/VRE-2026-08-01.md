**Research Date: August 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Veris Residential has been acquired and its common stock has stopped trading. An investor consortium led by Affinius Capital, partnered with Vista Hill Partners, completed an all-cash purchase at **$19.00 per share** on **May 27, 2026**, at a reported enterprise value of roughly **$3.4-3.5 billion** including assumed debt. NYSE trading was suspended and delisting initiated the same day; common and preferred operating-partnership units and equity awards were converted to cash, and the board and officers departed at closing. The company stated it intends to deregister and terminate public reporting.

The analytical question shifts from "what is Veris worth?" to "was $19.00 a fair clearing price, and is there any residual claim?" The answer to the second question appears to be no. The consideration was fixed cash — no stock component, no contingent value right, no earn-out identified — and press coverage indicates preferred partnership units were cashed out rather than left outstanding. For a capital allocator, there is nothing to own, nothing to short, and no yield. That is the basis for the AVOID rating, which reflects the absence of an investable instrument rather than a judgment that the underlying real estate was poor.

Two points bear on reconciling this report against prior work. First, prior coverage flagged three unexplained 8-K filings between May 15 and May 27, 2026. Those filings resolve as the shareholder vote, a pre-closing milestone, and the closing itself; VRE common was an arbitrage-pinned quote sitting just below a fixed $19.00 cash consideration, not a market price for an operating business. The earlier read that Jersey City Class A multifamily was worth more in private hands than the public market was marking it was directionally correct — the announcement produced an approximately 12% share-price gain — but the trade was over before it was identified. Second, the deal facts here are corroborated across six independent sources but the underlying 8-K and merger agreement were not readable (SEC document fetches returned HTTP 403; ticker-to-CIK resolution failed, itself consistent with deregistration). An independent reconciliation of equity value to enterprise value materially raises confidence, described below, but the primary source remains unread and I label it as such.

## Company Overview

Veris Residential (CIK 924901) was a Northeast-focused Class A multifamily REIT headquartered in Jersey City, operating through an UPREIT structure with Veris Residential, L.P. as the operating partnership. It is the successor to Mack-Cali Realty, historically one of the largest suburban office landlords in New Jersey. The rebranding and wholesale rotation out of office into apartments concentrated on the Hudson County waterfront explains essentially every line in the financial statements: the shrinking asset base, the multi-year GAAP losses, the disposition gains, and the falling overhead.

Portfolio detail available here is thinner than I would prefer. Item 1, Item 1A, Item 7, and Item 8 of the FY2025 10-K (filed February 23, 2026) could not be read; the section-level tools resolved instead to a **Part III-only 10-K/A filed March 23, 2026**. That filing pattern is itself informative: furnishing executive-compensation disclosure by 10-K amendment rather than by proxy statement is the mechanical consequence of not expecting to hold an annual meeting within 120 days of fiscal year end. As of late March 2026 the company did not plan to remain public through a 2026 meeting cycle. That is a filing fact, and it is what first surfaced the transaction in this research.

The business description available is third-party summary of the company's own self-description: Class A apartments in the Northeast, an in-house "technology-enabled, vertically integrated" operating platform, with Liberty Towers (648 units, two buildings, Jersey City) as a named asset. I could not test the platform claim against filing disclosure and there is no evidence in what I read that it produced a measurable margin advantage against peers.

**Revenue decline was disposition-driven, and FY2025 shows genuine operating improvement.** Revenue fell 21.2% from FY2022 to FY2023 and a further 3.1% into FY2024, then rose **6.4%** in FY2025 (derived: 288,434/271,074 − 1) while total assets shrank a further **9.3%** (1 − 2,706,871/2,982,727). Derived asset turnover improved from 0.091x to 0.107x, roughly 17%. SG&A fell every year, from $56.2M to $36.8M, a **34.6%** reduction, while FY2025 revenue grew. Growing revenue on a smaller asset base while cutting overhead by a third is the signature of successful portfolio high-grading plus real operating leverage. The caveat is that year-end asset balances distort turnover when dispositions occur mid-year, and part of the SG&A decline simply reflects a structurally smaller company. Without same-store NOI from Item 7 I cannot separate organic rent growth from mix, and I make no claim that the revenue increase was organic.

**FY2025 GAAP net income is not run-rate earnings power, and the cash flow statement plus the quarterly EPS series both say so.** Net income swung $98.4M, from −$23.1M to +$75.2M. Operating cash flow was **$75.97M**, almost identical to net income of $75.24M, despite $86.3M of non-cash depreciation. For a REIT, operating cash flow should normally exceed net income by roughly the depreciation charge. Pre-gain operating capacity of $161.5M (75.2 + 86.3) against actual OCF of $76.0M implies roughly **$85M** of non-cash, non-operating items, most plausibly gains on sale of real estate, which are excluded from operating cash flow and offset the depreciation add-back.

The provided quarterly EPS series corroborates this independently. Reported quarterly actuals for FY2025 sum to approximately **+$0.03 per share** (−0.07, +0.25, −0.12, −0.03), against GAAP net income of $75.2M, which on 93.4M basic shares is roughly **$0.81 per share**. Those two series are not on the same basis: the quarterly figures appear to be an adjusted measure excluding real-estate gains and impairments, which is the conventional treatment for REITs, so this is a basis mismatch rather than a data error. But the ~$0.78 per share gap, about $73M, brackets the $85M implied by the cash flow reconciliation and points the same direction. Do not capitalize FY2025 GAAP net income. An alternative reading of part of the gap is an adverse working-capital swing or settlement of accrued liabilities rather than disposition gains; either explanation supports the same conclusion.

**The metric that matters improved sharply.** Q4 FY2025 FFO was **$0.19 per share versus a $0.16 non-GAAP consensus and $0.11 in the prior-year quarter**, a 73% year-over-year increase (externally reported, not verified against the company's own release). Both figures are non-GAAP and therefore comparable to one another; neither is comparable to GAAP EPS.

**Leverage was high and the near-term maturity wall was resolved in the year before the sale.** Derived total liabilities of **$1.555B** (2,706,871 − 1,151,621) against $2.707B of assets is a **57.5%** liabilities/assets ratio and **1.35x** liabilities/equity, within normal range for a REIT on a book basis, though book equity understates economic leverage given a −$1.42B accumulated deficit reflecting two decades of depreciation and impairment against properties carried below replacement cost. The largest movement is short-term debt: $0 at FY2022, **$348.8M at FY2024**, **$30.0M at FY2025**. At FY2024 the company carried current maturities roughly **48x** its $7.3M cash balance. That is a genuine liquidity overhang, and it was cleared during FY2025. These are ending balances, not flows: simple extension of maturities into long-term buckets would produce identical figures with no deleveraging, and I cannot distinguish repayment from extension without the debt footnote. I do not infer board motive from the sequencing.

**Two data items I flag rather than use.** The XBRL-tagged interest expense reads −$0.8M (FY2024) and −$3.5M (FY2025), implausible against ~$1.5B of liabilities where true interest expense should plausibly run $80-120M. These are almost certainly a narrow sub-tag and are not used for coverage analysis. Separately, the financial-health screen returned "insufficient data"; Altman Z-Score requires working capital and retained earnings inputs that are structurally distorted for a REIT with a −$1.42B accumulated deficit. That failure is an input-incompatibility artifact, not evidence of distress, and equally not evidence of health.

**Share count and the deal reconciliation.** Diluted shares rose only 2.1% over four years. The persistent ~9.0M spread between basic (93.4M) and diluted (102.4M) is stable across all four years and is most plausibly operating-partnership units held by legacy Mack-Cali contributors — reported diluted capitalization, not contingent dilution. Press coverage confirming that "all outstanding common and preferred partnership units and equity awards" were cashed out supports this. Derived: 102.4M × $19.00 = **$1.945B** of equity consideration; adding $1.555B of total liabilities gives **$3.50B**, matching the reported $3.4-3.5B enterprise value. Roughly $171M (9.0M units × $19.00) went to OP unit holders rather than common shareholders. The separately cited "$1.78B market cap" is simply the basic share count at $19.00 (93.7M × $19.00 = $1.78B); the two figures are different share bases, not a discrepancy. This internal consistency is the strongest available substitute for the unread 8-K.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is not available to public investors. The consideration is fixed cash, so whatever the portfolio earns from June 2026 forward accrues to Affinius and Vista Hill.

Retrospectively, the growth trajectory going into the sale was the best it had been in five years. Revenue inflected positive (+6.4%), the asset base was smaller and higher-quality, overhead was down a third, and the proxy's disclosed "company selected measure" — most plausibly Core FFO per share, inferred from units and the disclosed measure list rather than confirmed — troughed at 0.44 in FY2022 and recovered to 0.53 and 0.60 in FY2023 and FY2024. Q4 FY2025 FFO of $0.19 versus $0.11 extends that recovery. Net PP&E rose steadily ($99.1M to $118.8M FY2022-FY2025) even as total assets shrank, which suggests continued deployment into development or repositioning, though the PP&E tag for a REIT may capture corporate fixed assets rather than investment property, so I leave that unresolved.

The macro setting argues the acquirers took on real cyclical exposure. Higher interest rates suppress for-sale housing, which supports apartment demand, but they also raise the discount rate on stabilized multifamily cash flows and compress exit values. New York-metro employment concentration in financial and professional services is the single-market bet embedded in the portfolio. None of that is a public-investor concern any longer. [The specific rate figures — a 6.66% mortgage rate and a 30-year Treasury at its highest since 2007 — are unverified against the provided sources and have been removed.]

Growth Prospects scores 2.5. The business had a credible improving trajectory; the security has zero forward growth participation.

## Valuation Assessment

The clearing price is known and fixed at $19.00. The question is what multiple that represents.

Annualizing Q4 FFO of $0.19 gives $0.76 and a **25.0x** multiple (19.00/0.76). I treat that as a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast: multifamily Q4 carries leasing and expense seasonality, and FY2025 still had a shifting asset base from ongoing dispositions. Using the proxy-implied full-year figures instead (0.60 for FY2024, trending upward), a normalized FY2025 run rate nearer $0.60-0.68 implies **28-32x**. The honest range is **25-32x FFO**, wide because full-year FY2025 FFO per share is unknown. On any point in that range, this is a full-to-rich private-market print for Northeast multifamily, and it is above where the public market was marking the equity: the stock rose approximately 12% on announcement, though that move was contaminated by the same-day Q4 FFO beat, so the clean deal premium is smaller and not separable from the data available.

The valuation score is 2.0. This is the arithmetic absence of upside, not a cheap-looking stock at risk of being a value trap. A fixed-cash consideration already received offers no asymmetric return, and the final sell-side position (Hold with a $19.00 target) converged on the deal price with no expectation of a topping bid, consistent with a spread that closed within days of announcement. That the equity was already marked at the takeout is consistent with this outcome.

For the broader coverage universe, the read-through is a hypothesis worth holding with low confidence. A $3.5B take-private of a clean Northeast apartment pure-play at 25-32x FFO indicates private capital in early 2026 valued that asset class above public marks. One transaction is not a market, and VRE's specific setup — five years of TSR underperformance, a maturity wall just cleared, a finished portfolio transformation — made it an unusually clean target that may carry a "cleanup completed" premium unavailable to other listed REITs.

## Competitive Landscape

Affinius Capital led the consortium and is now the controlling owner: an institutional real estate firm with $61 billion in AUM and more than $14 billion of U.S. multifamily transactions. Vista Hill Partners is a partner in the acquiring consortium, focused on "clustered property markets." Both are paying parties; former public shareholders are the recipients. Veris was merged into a buyer affiliate, and that affiliate — not Veris — survives. Wilentz, Goldman & Spitzer acted as counsel to the buyers. I have not verified the equity split between Affinius and Vista Hill, the identity of other unnamed consortium participants, or the debt-financing sources.

The strategic logic reads as a cost-of-capital and structure arbitrage. A concentrated Hudson-waterfront portfolio is penalized in a small-cap public REIT — no index-inclusion benefit, no diversification, a higher equity cost after five years of underperformance — and rewarded in private hands where submarket density and pricing power can be underwritten directly. Vista Hill's "clustered markets" focus maps onto that. An equally plausible alternative is that no operational thesis was required and institutional capital was simply cheaper than VRE's public equity.

Against the pure-play apartment REITs (AvalonBay, Equity Residential, UDR, Camden, Essex), the durable disadvantages were scale and single-market exposure. The plausible durable advantages were geography-derived rather than franchise-derived: irreplaceable Hudson waterfront land with high supply barriers and a structural rent discount to Manhattan, plus operating density within one submarket cluster. Both transfer intact to the new owner.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk is resolved, and with it the entire forward risk profile.** The dominant risk between February 23 and May 27, 2026 was non-consummation. Six sources confirm closing.

**Residual risks for former holders are narrow.** Appraisal or dissenters'-rights litigation is possible; no search was performed and I have no evidence either way. The cash received is a taxable event, potentially with return-of-capital components for a REIT holder. Golden-parachute and change-in-control acceleration amounts are unquantified despite confirmation that equity awards were cashed out and officers departed.

**Verification risk is the live analytical exposure.** I could not read the closing 8-K, the merger agreement, the FY2025 Item 1A risk factors, MD&A, or the financial-statement footnotes. Every deal fact — price, closing date, consideration mechanics, delisting — is externally reported. The equity-and-liabilities reconciliation to the reported enterprise value would be an improbable coincidence if the terms were misreported, which is why I hold this at high confidence. But the possibility that closing was subsequently unwound, while remote, is formally unverified. I also could not confirm whether any Veris-linked instrument still trades — operating-partnership debt or a separately listed preferred. Press coverage indicates preferred units were cashed out; the absence of surviving instruments is not confirmed.

**Legacy risks now transferred to the buyer** are worth naming for read-through purposes: single-labor-market concentration in New York metro, New Jersey rent-regulation and property-tax regime risk, correlated insurance exposure, and dependence on debt capital markets with only $14.1M of cash at year-end.

Risk Factors scores 4.0. The binary event resolved, but the standard for a high score — known risks priced with asymmetric recovery upside — cannot be met when there is no upside at all, and unquantified residual items (appraisal actions, tax treatment, unverified surviving instruments) sit against a fixed payout.

No options data was provided for VRE, consistent with a delisted security, so no options signal is available.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (for the buyers, not for public investors).** Affinius acquired a finished transformation: a Class A pure-play with revenue re-accelerating, overhead cut, FFO per share up year over year in Q4, and the acute maturity wall cleared. If Hudson-waterfront rents compound at mid-single digits against constrained supply while for-sale housing stays priced out at elevated mortgage rates, 25-32x entry FFO becomes defensible over a 7-10 year private hold. Public shareholders captured roughly 12% on announcement and were made whole in cash.

**Bear case (also for the buyers).** They paid a full multiple for a single-market portfolio at a moment of elevated long-term interest rates. Each 25 basis points of cap-rate expansion on a ~$3.5B enterprise value moves equity value by roughly 4-5% at the leverage implied by $1.555B of liabilities against $1.945B of equity. If New York-metro white-collar employment weakens, a concentrated waterfront portfolio has no diversification offset.

**There is no public-investor case, because there is no security.** The prior HOLD at 5.1 rested on a fundamental thesis about Jersey City multifamily NAV; that thesis was resolved by cash at $19.00 before it could be expressed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** There is no path to re-entry in VRE common equity. The three conditions that would restore any analytical relevance:

1. **Documentary evidence that closing was not consummated** — specifically, a readable 8-K or Form 25/15 record contradicting the May 27, 2026 closing, or resumption of NYSE quotation. Confidence that this occurs is low; the equity-to-enterprise-value reconciliation argues strongly against it.
2. **Identification of a surviving, separately traded Veris-linked instrument** — operating-partnership debt or an exchange-listed preferred not cashed out at merger terms. This is an open verification gap, not an established fact, and would need to be confirmed against a trustee report or exchange listing before any capital is committed.
3. **A re-IPO or public re-listing** of the assets under new ownership. Nothing in the available record suggests this is contemplated; a typical institutional hold period would place any such event well beyond a 5-10 year horizon.

**Timeline.** Immediate and permanent for the common equity. Filing of a Form 15 terminating registration is the confirming administrative step to watch; once filed, the name should be removed from the coverage universe entirely.

**Operational action.** Suppress the stale price, earnings-estimate, and target-price feeds for this ticker. The provided quarterly surprise history (three beats, one miss) and any "current market data" describe a terminated security and will produce misleading screen outputs if left in the universe.

**Read-through to keep, at low confidence.** The transaction supports a hypothesis that private multifamily bids in the Northeast cleared above public marks in early 2026. If a second comparable public-REIT take-private prints at a similar FFO multiple, the hypothesis strengthens into a positioning input for listed apartment REITs. One transaction with an unusually clean setup is not sufficient.

## Conclusion

**AVOID — overall 3.5.** The rating reflects the absence of an investable instrument, not a deteriorating business. Veris Residential's operating trajectory was improving into the sale: revenue up 6.4% on a 9.3% smaller asset base, SG&A down 34.6% from FY2022, Q4 FFO of $0.19 against $0.11 a year earlier, and a $348.8M near-term maturity wall reduced to $30.0M. Set against that, five consecutive GAAP loss years through FY2024 and a documented 24.7-point TSR shortfall against the company's own peer group in FY2024 explain why the public market never paid for the transformation and why a $19.00 all-cash exit — reflected immediately as roughly 12% above the prevailing price, with sell-side converging on a $19.00 target — was a rational resolution.

This is a reversal from the prior HOLD at 5.1, and the reason is a change in the character of the security rather than any new fundamental deterioration. VRE common was an arbitrage-pinned quote sitting just below a fixed cash consideration, and the three previously unexplained 8-Ks between May 15 and May 27, 2026 were the vote, a pre-closing milestone, and the closing. The directional judgment in that report — that private-market Jersey City multifamily pricing sat above the public mark — was correct, and it was already fully expressed in the price.

Two caveats belong in the record. First, no primary source for the merger was readable; the deal is corroborated across six independent outlets and reconciles internally ($1.945B diluted equity consideration plus $1.555B of FY2025 liabilities equals $3.50B against a reported $3.4-3.5B enterprise value), but the 8-K and merger agreement remain unread. Second, FY2025's $75.2M of GAAP net income should not be capitalized by anyone using this data downstream: operating cash flow of $76.0M against $86.3M of depreciation, and a quarterly adjusted-EPS series summing to roughly $0.03 per share against $0.81 of GAAP EPS, both point to approximately $73-85M of non-recurring gains on sale in the reported figure.
