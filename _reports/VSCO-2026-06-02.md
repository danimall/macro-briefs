**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Victoria's Secret & Co. has staged a credible operational turnaround that the market is only beginning to re-rate. The Q1 FY2026 print (reported June 2, 2026) showed net sales up 15% to $1.560B, above guidance, with adjusted EPS of $0.60 against a $0.30 estimate, and management raised the full-year FY26 outlook above consensus. This is the fourth consecutive beat. The drivers are operationally legitimate: new-customer acquisition, broad category strength, a 27% increase in international revenue, and gross margin holding on reduced promotional activity, which signals returning pricing power rather than discount-driven volume. CEO Hillary Super's "Path to Potential" turnaround, in place since September 2024, is producing measurable results off a depressed base.

At a current price of $54.30, VSCO trades at roughly 13x forward earnings ($3.48 FY26 consensus, $4.17 FY27), well below its 28x trailing multiple and at a premium to the Apparel Retail peer median of 10.5x that is defensible given accelerating top-line growth. Analyst price targets sit entirely above spot — low $50, mean $65.78, median $67, high $81 — implying roughly 21% upside to the consensus mean with no target below the current price. The fundamental setup leans bullish.

I am rating VSCO a HOLD with a bullish (growth) lean rather than a BUY for three specific reasons. First, the estimate-revision signal, while large on a 90-day basis (current-quarter +275.7%), has stalled over the last 30 days (-0.9% current quarter, +0.3% FY26); the re-rating velocity already happened. Second, a contested June 11, 2026 proxy vote is a genuine binary event days away, pitting an entrenched, long-tenured board against a competitor-conflicted activist (BBRC's Brett Blundy, ~13% holder), with credible governance red flags on both sides. Third, the apparel-retail backdrop and the recurring Adore Me impairment drag keep underlying earnings quality below the GAAP-adjusted optics. The right posture is to own the thesis on a pullback or post-vote clarity, with explicit upgrade triggers, rather than to add into a squeeze-amplified rally ahead of a binary vote.

## Company Overview

Victoria's Secret & Co. is the world's largest intimate-apparel company, operating as a single reportable segment across three brands plus a beauty business. The flagship Victoria's Secret brand anchors the franchise around bras ("bra authority"); PINK targets younger lifestyle shoppers and is being repositioned as apparel-led; and Adore Me, acquired in December 2022, is a digitally-native, size-inclusive intimates business (with DailyLook styling). A sizable beauty and fragrance business (the Bombshell franchise) functions as both a loyalty driver and an international-expansion lever.

Distribution spans roughly 790 company-operated North American stores, a 51%-owned consolidated China joint venture (~65 stores), about 560 partner-operated stores across ~70 countries, and company-owned digital channels. FY26 channel mix: North America stores $3.544B (54%), Direct $2.042B (31%), and International $0.967B (15%). The company spun out of L Brands in 2021 and is based in Reynoldsburg, Ohio, with ~12,000 employees.

The competitive position is that of a scaled market leader with deep brand recognition and in-store bra-fitting expertise, competing against specialty chains (Aerie/American Eagle), DTC entrants (Savage X Fenty, Skims), department stores, and discounters. Its structural disadvantage is mall dependence; its clearest structural advantage is a globally diversified beauty/fragrance business and an international footprint that is growing faster than the core.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Annual revenue moved from $6.344B (FY23) to $6.182B (FY24) to $6.230B (FY25) to $6.553B in FY26 (+5%), with comparable sales also +5%. The acceleration is the story: Q3 FY25 sales rose 9% to $1.472B. International is the engine, up 27% YoY to $967M, driven by China, sourcing/wholesale, royalties, and a reporting shift of EU direct sales now fulfilled from a Netherlands distribution center. North America stores grew 3%; Direct was roughly flat.

**Profitability and earnings quality.** This is where caution is warranted. GAAP operating income has eroded from a post-pandemic peak: FY23 $478M → FY24 $246M → FY25 $310M → FY26 $271M (4.1% margin, down from 5.0%). Net income was $161M, EPS $1.93. The GAAP figure was depressed by a $120M Adore Me long-lived asset impairment (-$90M after-tax), $36M of Adore Me/DailyLook fulfillment restructuring, and ~$85M of net tariff costs, partly offset by a +$69M credit-card interchange litigation gain and a $24M China deferred-tax valuation release. On management's adjusted basis, operating income rose to $403M and adjusted EPS reached $3.00 (vs $2.69). The underlying trend is better than GAAP optics suggest, but the Adore Me drag is recurring and the gap between GAAP and adjusted is wide enough to demand scrutiny.

**Gross margin** was 36.4%, down 30 bps, pressured by tariffs and inventory reserves but partly offset by reduced promotions and more regular-price selling. The mix shift toward full-price selling is a genuine pricing-power signal and directly contradicts a "weakening consumer forced to discount" narrative for this specific company.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY26 operating cash flow was $499M, capex $187M, and FCF $312M (a 4.8% FCF margin, ~7.2% FCF yield on the $4.3B cap). Cash rose $291M to $518M. Total debt is ~$975M (Term Loan $379M due 2028 at ~6.5%; $600M 4.625% notes due 2029; $750M ABL undrawn at year-end with $589M availability). Debt-to-cap improved to 53% from 60%. The headline 4.79 D/E and 1.94 Z-Score look distressed but are dominated by ~$1.8B of operating lease liabilities against a thin equity base; the company is FCF-positive, covenant-compliant, and repriced both the ABL (May 2025, maturity pushed to 2030) and Term Loan (December 2025) to lower spreads, which is concrete de-risking rather than deterioration.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. A $250M buyback authorized in March 2024 remains entirely unused. Against the de-leveraging and cash build, this is unused optionality and the central point of activist contention.

**Seasonality.** The model is heavily Q4-weighted, with operating losses in Q1-Q3 and seasonal ABL draws (peaking at $375M in Q3 FY25). The Q1 FY2026 swing to a $76M operating income (adjusted $80M) is therefore a meaningful positive surprise against a seasonally weak quarter.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the strongest part of the thesis and is supported by hard data rather than forecast. Revenue is accelerating off a multi-year trough (+5% annual, but +9% in Q3 FY25 and +15% in Q1 FY2026). International (+34% in Q3) is a durable structural lever tied to China, travel-retail beauty, and franchise/wholesale economics. Margin holding on less promotional selling indicates the demand is regular-price demand, not clearance volume.

Estimate revisions confirm an inflection has been recognized: current-quarter EPS is up 275.7% over 90 days, FY26 +14.5%, and FY27 +9.7%. The 30-day revision figures, however, are flat-to-modestly-positive across every period (current quarter -0.9%, next quarter -0.3%, FY26 +0.3%, FY27 +1.6%). The brief's own characterization — "Estimates stable, no significant directional shift" — is accurate. The textbook inflection-BUY signal requires *accelerating* revisions (larger 30-day than 90-day moves); here the velocity has stalled. The large 90-day numbers reflect a re-rating that has substantially occurred, partly off a low base and partly on tariff-relief optionality from the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court strike-down of IEEPA tariffs (offset by a new 10% Section 122 tariff with uncertain net effect). This is positive but no longer the fresh, accelerating signal that would justify overriding sector caution.

The four-for-four beat record (most recently +96.7%/+100.1% on Q1 EPS, +7.8% on the prior quarter) raises confidence in forward consensus and management's raised FY26 guidance. The growth thesis is real and durable enough to support a constructive lean.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 28x is misleading because trailing earnings are depressed by the Adore Me impairment and tariff costs; for an inflecting name, forward and normalized metrics are the right lens, and on those VSCO is not expensive.

Against Apparel Retail peers (P/E median 10.5x: BOOT 23.1x, ANF 7.4x, AEO 9.8x, URBN 14.0x, BKE 10.5x), VSCO's 13x forward sits modestly above the median but is well-justified by its top-line acceleration (+15% Q1 vs flat-to-negative comps at several peers) and international growth profile. URBN at 14.0x is the most apt comparable given similar growth, and VSCO trades inside that.

Analyst targets imply roughly 21% upside to the mean and ~23% to the median, with zero targets below the current price. On a name at 13x forward with rising (if now-stalled) estimates, these targets are more likely floors than ceilings; the constraint is event risk and sector reliability, not the multiple. A reasonable base case applies ~16x to FY27 EPS of $4.17, yielding ~$67, consistent with the analyst median. The valuation is genuinely attractive on fundamentals; it earns a 6.8, not higher, because the cheapness is partly a function of binary governance overhang and the cyclical/structural risks endemic to mall-based apparel.

## Competitive Landscape

Victoria's Secret retains scale leadership in intimates and a differentiated in-store bra-fitting and beauty/fragrance franchise. Trajectory matters more than level: the comp acceleration, international growth, and full-price mix recovery indicate the brand is regaining relevance with younger consumers (the PINK reposition and "brand heat" initiatives, plus the revived Fashion Show in October 2025). That said, the category is structurally contested by inclusive/DTC brands (Skims, Savage X Fenty, Aerie), and the company's mall dependence is a real disadvantage as traffic migrates. The competitive position is improving from a weak base, which supports a mid-range 5.5 rather than a higher score; the turnaround is working but not yet proven across a full cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Proxy fight (dominant near-term risk).** Australian billionaire Brett Blundy's BBRC International (second-largest holder, ~4-year position) launched a May 2026 campaign to withhold votes from Chair Donna James and director Mariam Naficy at the June 11 annual meeting, centered on the failed $591M Adore Me deal and TSR underperformance (company TSR has lagged peers for three consecutive reported years). The board adopted a 15%-trigger poison pill in May 2025. The dispute is unusually personal: the board disclosed that it rejected Blundy partly over an alleged "pattern of hiring executives with a history of serious sexual harassment allegations" (acutely sensitive given L Brands' Epstein-era history), Blundy's ownership of competing intimates brand Léays, and an alleged corporate-espionage incident in which a BBRC adviser misrepresented himself to extract store sales data. Naficy is not standing for re-election. The governance red flags run both ways, and I treat the vote as a variance event rather than a directional one.

**Tariffs.** The February 20, 2026 SCOTUS strike-down of IEEPA tariffs is a potential tailwind, but a new 10% Section 122 global tariff was announced; refund eligibility and forward net levels are uncertain. Southeast Asia sourcing concentration heightens sensitivity.

**Adore Me.** $591M total cost, $120M impairment in FY26, ongoing earnout dispute; a recurring drag and the centerpiece of the activist critique.

**Other.** Leadership turnover ($20M FY25 reorganization charges), mall/traffic dependence, fashion-cycle relevance risk, and a May 2025 cybersecurity incident (-$20M sales/-$14M operating income, resolved). High beta (2.25) amplifies all of the above.

On consumer demand specifically: I find no VSCO-specific evidence of a demand cliff. The +15% Q1 comp, +27% international growth, and full-price margin gains directly contradict applying a generic "weakening consumer" overlay to this name. I am not crediting that as a risk.

### Options Market Signal

The most consequential market-structure feature is short interest at 22.5% of float (12.98M shares, 7.2 days to cover). This is a double-edged signal. It amplified the ~40% post-earnings spike on June 2 (a squeeze dynamic explicitly flagged in coverage), which means recent price action overstates the strength of fundamental buying and is unreliable in both directions, but it also means an in-line-or-better proxy outcome combined with continued operational delivery could force further covering. For position sizing, the squeeze dynamic argues against chasing strength into the June 11 vote; it does not, on its own, argue against the underlying fundamental thesis. Crowded-short mechanics raise near-term volatility around a binary event, which is a reason to be patient on entry, not a reason to abandon a 13x-forward name with rising estimates.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$67-81).** The turnaround is real and accelerating. Raised guidance, four straight beats, and full-price margin recovery indicate durable demand. At 13x forward, a re-rating to 16-19x on FY27 power yields $67-79. Insider conviction supports this: on April 2, 2026, Director Anne Sheehan bought 4,842 shares at $45.86 (~$222K) and Chair Donna James bought 2,175 shares at $46.23 (~$100K) in open-market purchases, uncommon for directors and a credible confidence signal ahead of the proxy contest. Either proxy outcome can be constructive: incumbent victory validates the turnaround narrative; an activist-driven outcome likely forces the unused $250M buyback and accelerated de-leveraging. A short squeeze on continued delivery adds upside torque toward the $81 high target.

**Bear Case (~$40-45).** The estimate-revision velocity has stalled, suggesting the easy re-rating is done. GAAP operating income fell YoY and the recurring Adore Me impairment signals a capital-allocation mistake that keeps draining earnings quality. Mall dependence and intensifying DTC competition cap the structural growth ceiling. A disruptive proxy outcome, a tariff reversal (net Section 122 burden), or a single soft holiday quarter against a high-beta, squeeze-inflated price could drive a 20-25% drawdown toward the mid-$40s or low-$40s, the level at which insiders themselves bought.

The asymmetry is favorable (roughly 21% to base case, ~49% to bull, vs ~17-26% downside), but it is gated by a binary vote nine days out and by sector-level reliability concerns that have repeatedly converted attractive-looking apparel setups into losses.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The June 11, 2026 annual meeting is the immediate reassessment point, followed by Q2 FY2026 earnings (~September 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Proxy vote resolves without disruptive board upheaval AND management commits to deploying the unused $250M buyback, removing the central governance overhang while signaling capital discipline.
2. Estimate-revision velocity re-accelerates — specifically, 30-day FY27 EPS revisions turn positive by >3% alongside a Q2 beat and another guidance raise, confirming the inflection is ongoing rather than complete.
3. A pullback toward the mid-to-low $40s (where insiders bought) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring a wider margin of safety against the structural risks.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Comparable-sales growth decelerates below +5% with a guidance cut, signaling the turnaround has stalled.
2. Gross margin compresses materially on a return to heavy promotional selling, invalidating the pricing-power thesis.
3. A disruptive proxy outcome that destabilizes the management team or forces a strategy reset, or a net tariff burden that exceeds the FY26 ~$85M hit.

## Conclusion

VSCO presents a genuine operational turnaround at an attractive 13x forward multiple with analyst targets entirely above the current price; the fundamental setup leans clearly bullish, and I am weighing the insider open-market purchases and the full-price margin recovery as legitimate confidence signals, while explicitly rejecting any generic "consumer cliff" overlay that the company's own +15% comp acceleration contradicts. The reasons I stop at HOLD-with-bullish-lean rather than BUY are specific and not merely "the stock has run": the estimate-revision velocity has stalled over the trailing 30 days, the June 11 proxy vote is a true binary event days away that I treat as a variance rather than a directional catalyst, and the underlying earnings quality (GAAP operating income down YoY, recurring Adore Me impairment) sits below the adjusted optics. In a sector where I hold myself to an especially high bar for conviction longs, the disciplined call is to own this on confirmation — a clean proxy resolution plus buyback commitment, re-accelerating revisions, or a pullback toward the mid-$40s where insiders themselves stepped in — rather than to add into a squeeze-amplified rally ahead of an unresolved governance contest. I expect to upgrade this HOLD rather than hold it neutral.
