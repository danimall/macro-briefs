**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Korn Ferry is a global talent and organizational consulting firm whose earnings are emerging from a multi-quarter cyclical trough in corporate hiring. The headline 45% FY25 net-income jump overstates the story: that increase was almost entirely the non-recurrence of FY24's $68.6M restructuring charge plus a ~7% average headcount cut, against a 1% fee-revenue decline. The more encouraging development is Q3 FY26 (January 2026), where fee revenue grew 7% year-over-year with all eight segments expanding — led by Executive Search EMEA (+16%), North America (+13%), and APAC (+11%). Roughly 3 percentage points of that growth was FX tailwind, but the breadth points to a sector-wide recovery in executive hiring rather than a company-specific anomaly.

The balance sheet and capital-return profile are the strongest part of the case. Korn Ferry sits in a net-cash position (~$1.2–1.3B cash and securities against $400M of 4.625% notes due December 2027), generates ~$302M FCF (an 11% FCF margin) off a low-capex, asset-light model, and has raised its dividend repeatedly — from $0.10 in 2014 to $0.55 declared March 2026, a ~3.1% yield. That cadence signals management confidence in through-cycle cash generation. The offsetting concern is that consensus estimates are flat-to-falling (current-year EPS $5.33, essentially unrevised over 90 days; next-year $5.76, down 0.1%), so the market is not yet pricing a durable growth re-rating.

We rate KFY a HOLD on income grounds. The recovery is real and the valuation (~14x trailing/forward EPS) is undemanding, but two things keep this from a BUY: the structural AI-disruption question over Korn Ferry's recruiting and assessment IP is unresolved and is the dominant reason external analysts hold a "show-me" stance, and the near-term options market is pricing imminent stress (43.9% one-month IV in backwardation, elevated put skew). The stock offers a defensible yield and a cyclical tailwind, but limited upside.

## Company Overview

Korn Ferry operates in 51 countries, with ~47% of fee revenue generated outside the U.S., across five Solution areas comprising eight reportable segments:

- **Executive Search** (~32% of fee revenue, four geographic segments) — board, C-suite, and senior recruiting; over 80% of searches are senior-level.
- **Consulting** (~24%) — organizational strategy, assessment and succession, leadership development, total rewards.
- **Professional Search & Interim** (~19%) — permanent professional placement plus interim talent.
- **Digital** (~13%) — IP-based SaaS/subscription products, anchored by the Korn Ferry Talent Suite and proprietary behavioral-science data.
- **RPO** (~12%) — high-volume outsourced enterprise hiring.

The competitive model rests on cross-sell and account concentration rather than individual rainmakers. Over 75% of fee revenue comes from clients using multiple solutions, 83% of FY25 assignments came from repeat clients, and the Marquee & Diamond Accounts program (350 strategic accounts) now represents ~40% of fee revenue. Korn Ferry's top 10 consultants generate only ~5% of revenue, which materially de-risks the "rainmaker walks out the door" fragility that afflicts search firms and that standardized screens miss. Competitors span Heidrick & Struggles, Russell Reynolds, Spencer Stuart, and Egon Zehnder in search; AON, Mercer, WTW, Deloitte, and McKinsey in consulting; and Indeed/LinkedIn-type platforms, iCIMS, HireVue, and Eightfold in digital/RPO.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is one of cyclical margin recovery layered onto an asset-light, cash-generative base.

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fee revenue ($M) | 2,643 | 2,835 | 2,763 | 2,730 (-1%) | 2,148 (+6% YoY) |
| Net income ($M) | 326 | 209 | 169 | 246 (+45%) | 204 (+12%) |
| Adj EBITDA margin | 17.3% | 16.1% | 14.8% | 17.0% | 17.1% |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | — | $4.60 | $3.84 (9-mo) |

A few points deserve emphasis. First, the FY25 net-income surge is a margin story off a trough, not organic top-line strength — the prior-year restructuring charge dropped out and the cost base had been cut. Second, the Q3 FY26 inflection is the more meaningful signal: broad-based growth with every solution expanding is the clearest evidence yet that the hiring cycle has turned.

Cash-flow quality is solid. FY25 operating cash flow was $364M against capex of only ~$62M. The balance sheet carries ~$2.0B of equity, and a new $850M Wells Fargo revolver (July 2025, maturing 2030) is fully undrawn. The Altman Z of 2.5 ("grey zone") is a screening artifact for an asset-light services firm; real bankruptcy risk is negligible.

One caveat on the cash figure: a large portion is held in trust for deferred compensation (ECAP) and accrued bonuses. Net of those obligations, cash and securities were ~$685M at January 2026 — still ample, but the headline cash number overstates discretionary firepower.

A non-cash item to flag: Korn Ferry is retiring its legacy Digital platform and replacing it with the Korn Ferry Talent Suite, driving accelerated depreciation (9-month FY26 D&A up 29% to $77M, with Digital-segment D&A nearly doubling). This suppresses near-term GAAP earnings but is excluded from Adjusted EBITDA. A $13.9M one-time gain on a landlord-initiated office-lease modification flattered FY26 results in the other direction.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two layers, and they point in different directions. The cyclical layer is favorable: the broad-based Q3 FY26 growth, the strongest segments in executive search, and a $1,295M performance backlog suggest the demand environment is improving. The earnings-surprise record reinforces near-term visibility — three beats and one in-line print over the last four quarters, with surprises of 2.5%, 0.4%, 4.9%, and 3.7%. Korn Ferry consistently clears conservative guidance.

The secular layer is where conviction breaks down. Consensus estimates are not rising despite the revenue inflection, with next-quarter estimates down 0.6%. This is the most important constraint on the growth score: a durable-growth thesis would show estimates climbing as the cycle turns, and they are flat-to-falling. The market is treating the recovery as a cyclical bounce that returns Korn Ferry to roughly its pre-downturn earning power, not a step-change.

Growth is the dimension that most reliably separates winners from losers, and on the evidence here Korn Ferry does not clear the bar. The revenue acceleration is real but partly FX-aided, and the absence of positive estimate revisions — no acceleration in the 30-day versus 90-day windows — means there is no inflection signal that would warrant rewarding the name on growth. The bull would point to Digital subscription/license growth (+7–8% in FY26) accelerating after the Talent Suite migration completes, but that is a watch-item, not a current driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.46, Korn Ferry trades at ~14.2x trailing/forward EPS and carries a ~3.1% dividend yield. Against peer medians in Human Resource & Employment Services (P/E 23.2, yield 4.4%), the headline multiple looks cheap, but the peer set is heterogeneous and several comparables are distorted: Manpower (-94x) and Insperity (-57x) carry negative or near-zero earnings, while Robert Half (24.4x) and Paylocity (23.2x) are the cleaner reads. Korn Ferry's discount to those is partly explained by its lower secular-growth profile and the unresolved AI overhang, so the discount is at least partially deserved rather than a pure mispricing.

The low multiple does not pair with the inflection signals that would make cheapness a buy rather than a value trap. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising; revenue growth is ~7%, not the >40% that would justify treating the trailing multiple as meaningless trough-earnings noise. This is a moderately-valued cyclical recovering toward mid-cycle earnings power, where a low P/E in isolation has historically been an unreliable buy signal absent an independent catalyst. The catalyst here — durable growth plus AI resilience — has not yet been demonstrated.

Analyst targets bracket $70 (low) to $84 (high), with a mean of $79.25 and median $81.50. At the current price the stock sits below mean and median, implying ~11% upside to the mean. On a stock with flat estimates and a structural overhang, those targets are not "floors" that will mechanically revise upward; they reflect the same wait-and-see posture as the consensus EPS line. A reasonable base-case fair value of roughly $76–78 (≈14.5x on $5.33 EPS plus the yield) leaves modest upside and limited asymmetry, which holds the valuation score to a middling level.

## Competitive Landscape

Korn Ferry's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on the cyclical recovery, with a defensible but not widening moat. The cross-sell franchise, repeat-client base, and Marquee/Diamond account concentration are durable structural advantages, and the low consultant concentration insulates the firm from the talent-flight risk that periodically destabilizes pure search peers. Five-year TSR of 230 versus a peer-group index of 147 confirms it has outperformed its direct comparables through the cycle.

The trajectory question is whether AI commoditizes the recruiting and assessment layer. The bull view is that Korn Ferry's "10 billion data points" and behavioral-science IP, plus deep advisory relationships, are defensible and even enhanced by AI tooling — and management is positioning Digital and RPO as "AI-enabled" rather than AI-threatened. The bear view, which the firm itself discloses prominently in its risk factors, is that generative AI could reduce hiring volumes (fewer searches/RPO), enable clients to insource recruiting, and devalue the proprietary IP that underpins the Digital segment. The Talent Suite re-platforming is management's bet to stay on the right side of that line. Until Digital subscription growth visibly accelerates post-migration, the competitive trajectory reads as holding share in a recovering market rather than structurally gaining it.

## Risk Assessment

**AI/automation as a structural demand threat** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and the central bear thesis. It is partly priced (the discount to clean peers reflects it), but it is unresolved and could re-rate the stock either way depending on Digital-segment evidence over the next several quarters.

**Cyclicality** is permanently embedded in the business and demonstrated by the FY24–25 downturn; it is largely priced but amplifies any macro slowdown in corporate hiring.

**Goodwill impairment** is a live tail: goodwill is ~$949M (25% of assets), no impairment to date, but the acquisition-heavy strategy plus cyclical/AI pressure keeps it on the table.

**FX** swings both ways — ~47% of revenue is international, and the ~3 points of FX-aided Q3 FY26 growth can reverse.

**Notes refinancing**: the $400M due December 2027 will likely refinance above the 4.625% coupon in a higher-for-longer rate environment, a modest but real earnings headwind.

**Governance yellow flag (mild)**: CEO Burnison sold ~77K shares in early October 2025 (~$5.4M at ~$70), and the CFO gifted 22,470 shares in December 2025, with no open-market insider purchases. These follow large July 2025 RSU vesting events and read as post-vest diversification rather than a conviction signal, but the absence of any buying is a negative. An unconfirmed 3/6/2026 8-K (Item 5.02, officer departure/election) could not be retrieved and warrants follow-up; Burnison remains CEO per the proxy, so this is most likely a routine officer matter.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is the most cautionary near-term signal in the dataset. One-month ATM IV is 43.9% against 26.6% one-year historical vol — rich by 17 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (43.9% near-term versus 28.2% at six months), which markets typically price ahead of a discrete stress event. Put/call skew is elevated at 25.1% and the put/call volume ratio is 3.33, indicating active near-term put demand. IV has risen 3.7 points since late May even as the stock climbed 3.7%. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.4%. This positioning argues for patience on entry timing: the market is pricing a binary event (most plausibly the next earnings print or a development around the unconfirmed officer-change 8-K), and the asymmetry of buying ahead of it is unfavorable. Short interest is modest at 5.0% of float (4.9 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The hiring cycle has turned, and the broad-based Q3 FY26 growth is the leading edge of a multi-quarter recovery that carries fee revenue back toward and past the FY23 peak (~$2,835M). The net-cash balance sheet, 11% FCF margin, and the dividend underpin a defensible ~3.1% yield with room to grow. The Talent Suite re-platforming positions Digital as AI-enabled, and as accelerated D&A rolls off, GAAP EPS converges toward adjusted economics. Estimates revise upward as consensus catches up to the cyclical recovery, and the stock re-rates toward the cleaner peer multiple (~23x), supporting a path to the $84 high target.

**Bear Case.** Generative AI compresses hiring volumes and lets clients insource recruiting, structurally shrinking the search/RPO addressable market and triggering goodwill impairment on the ~$949M balance. The "recovery" proves a shallow cyclical bounce that fades into a soft labor market, FX tailwinds reverse, and estimates begin to fall. The stock de-rates toward the low end of its range. Bear-case value sits around $58–60 (the 52-week low area of $58.95), roughly 16–18% below current.

The evidence sits between these poles. The cyclical recovery is confirmed in the hard segment data; the secular AI question is genuinely open; and consensus estimates — the cleanest forward tell — are flat rather than rising, which keeps this a balanced rather than asymmetric setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon, anchored to quarterly fee-revenue and Digital-segment prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimates begin rising — specifically, current-year EPS revised above $5.50 with positive 30-day momentum — confirming the recovery is durable rather than a cyclical bounce.
- Digital segment subscription/license growth accelerates above ~10% post-Talent Suite migration, demonstrating AI resilience rather than threat.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s that restores meaningful margin of safety (sub-12x forward with the recovery intact).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Fee-revenue growth decelerates back toward flat or negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- A goodwill impairment charge against the ~$949M balance, or explicit management commentary that AI is reducing search/RPO volumes.
- A dividend cut or a halt to the raise cadence, which would invalidate the income thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q4 FY26 / fiscal year-end April 30, reporting ~June 2026) is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to whether the elevated near-term implied volatility resolves into a beat or a disappointment. Resolution of the unconfirmed 3/6/2026 officer-change 8-K should be confirmed before adding to any position.

## Conclusion

Korn Ferry is a well-managed, net-cash, asset-light franchise in the early innings of a cyclical hiring recovery, paying a defensible and growing ~3.1% dividend. Those attributes support owning it for income and cyclical optionality. The case stops short of a BUY: consensus estimates are flat-to-falling despite the revenue inflection, the structural AI-disruption question over its recruiting and assessment IP is unresolved and is the reason the stock trades at a deserved discount to cleaner peers, and the near-term options market is pricing imminent stress. The valuation offers modest upside (~11% to the mean target) but not the asymmetry that warrants a more aggressive stance. We maintain HOLD (income), consistent with the prior trajectory, and would look to upgrade on rising estimates or demonstrated Digital/AI resilience, or on a pullback toward the low-$60s that restores margin of safety.
