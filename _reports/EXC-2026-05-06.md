## Executive Summary

Exelon is the largest regulated transmission and distribution (T&D) utility in the United States, serving 10.5 million customers across six subsidiaries in Illinois, Pennsylvania, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, and Washington D.C. The company separated from its generation business (Constellation Energy) in 2022 and is now a pure "wires and pipes" play, earning regulated returns on $84.3 billion in PP&E.

The investment case rests on two competing forces. On the positive side: a $41 billion capital plan through 2029 supporting 5-7% EPS CAGR, a 19 GW data center load pipeline (26% CAGR in northern Illinois), $12-17 billion in incremental transmission opportunities, a 3.6% dividend yield with 5% annual growth, and a meaningful P/E discount to peers. On the negative side: the forced withdrawal of PECO's approximately $575 million rate case in April 2026 after direct intervention by Pennsylvania's governor signals politicization of utility regulation during a period of elevated consumer costs, persistent negative free cash flow requires continuous capital market access, and $43.4 billion in debt makes the company acutely sensitive to interest rates remaining elevated.

Q1 2026 results (reported May 6) beat estimates at $0.91 vs. $0.89 consensus, and management reaffirmed 2026 EPS guidance of $2.81-$2.91 despite the PECO setback. The stock trades at 16.2x forward earnings versus a peer median of 26.6x, reflecting both the PECO overhang and Exelon's lower growth premium as a pure T&D utility. The discount is partially warranted; the path to re-rating requires regulatory stability and load growth execution.

## Company Overview

Exelon operates exclusively as a regulated transmission and distribution utility. It does not generate electricity, eliminating commodity risk but making regulatory outcomes the singular driver of financial performance. Its six subsidiaries — ComEd (41% of utility earnings), PECO (29%), BGE (21%), and PHI entities (Pepco, DPL, ACE) — cover dense urban service territories including Chicago, Philadelphia, Baltimore, and Washington D.C.

Revenue has grown from $16.9 billion (2022) to $25.0 billion (2025), though much of this reflects pass-through purchased power costs rather than economic growth. Operating income reached $5.15 billion in 2025 with a 20.6% operating margin. The company targets approximately 9.7% operating ROE, achieved through rate base growth funded by annual capex of $8.5-$10 billion.

The strategic thesis is grid modernization: aging infrastructure requires replacement, electrification increases load, and data center demand in Exelon's territories is accelerating. Northern Illinois alone has a ~26% data center demand CAGR based on the current pipeline.

## Growth Analysis

Exelon's growth framework operates through rate base expansion. Every dollar of approved capital investment earns a regulated return (approximately 9.7% operating ROE). The path to 5-7% EPS CAGR requires:

1. **Execution on $41B capital plan (2026-2029):** ~$10B/year in transmission and distribution investment, growing rate base and earnings proportionally.

2. **Data center demand materialization:** The 19 GW load pipeline with ~45% secured through Transmission Security Agreements represents the upside optionality. If data center interconnections accelerate, Exelon could identify $12-17 billion in incremental transmission investment beyond the current plan, pushing rate base CAGR closer to 15%.

3. **Rate case approvals:** Growth requires regulators to approve rate increases that recover new investment. The PECO withdrawal creates uncertainty about one jurisdiction (29% of utility earnings) but leaves Illinois, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, and D.C. unaffected.

**Estimate revisions** are essentially flat: FY2026 consensus at $2.85 (unchanged over 30 days, +0.7% over 90 days), FY2027 at $3.04 (-0.2% over 30 days, +0.7% over 90 days). The stability reflects both management's conservative guidance philosophy and the PECO uncertainty offsetting data center optimism. Four consecutive quarterly beats (1.7% to 9.6% surprise magnitude) suggest management guidance is achievable.

At $46.18, Exelon trades at 16.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.85 — a 39% discount to the electric utility peer median of 26.6x. This discount has several explanations:

1. **No AI/generation premium.** Vistra (70.5x) and Constellation carry massive AI power generation premiums. Exelon is a distributor, not a generator, and the market assigns lower growth multiples to T&D pure-plays.

2. **PECO regulatory overhang.** The triple downgrade in April compressed the multiple from approximately 17.5x (pre-event, stock at ~$49-50) to the current 16.2x.

3. **Persistent dilution.** ATM equity issuance and convertible notes dilute earnings growth per share relative to absolute earnings growth.

**Fair value range:** At 17-18x forward earnings on $2.85 (low end of peer range, reflecting pure-T&D discount), fair value is $48-51. The analyst consensus median of $50 ($43-$58 range) is consistent with this. Upside to fair value from current price: 4-11%.

The valuation discount is partially warranted by PECO risk and capital intensity but overstates the company's challenges given the data center pipeline and reaffirmed guidance. A re-rating toward 18-19x requires PECO resolution and evidence of load growth conversion.

## Competitive Landscape

**Position: Market leader in regulated T&D by customer count (10.5 million).**

Exelon's competitive advantages are structural rather than dynamic:
- **Territorial monopoly:** Electric distribution is a natural monopoly; there is no direct competitive threat to existing service.
- **Dense urban territories:** Chicago, Philadelphia, Baltimore, and D.C. offer organic load growth and data center proximity that rural utilities lack.
- **Scale efficiencies:** Shared services (BSC) across six utilities reduce overhead per customer.
- **Multi-jurisdictional diversification:** No single regulator controls more than 41% of earnings.

**Trajectory considerations:**
- **Improving:** Data center demand in northern Illinois is a structural tailwind that peers in non-data-center markets don't share. Transmission rate base growing at approximately 15% CAGR.
- **Deteriorating:** The PECO withdrawal introduces Pennsylvania as a more hostile jurisdiction. Customer affordability pressure is rising across all territories as supply costs increase (even though pass-through, they raise total bills and create political pressure on the distribution component).

**Chicago franchise risk:** The City of Chicago's franchise agreement has been terminable on one year's notice since December 2020, and municipalization remains theoretically possible. While no action has been taken, this represents a low-probability, high-consequence tail risk to ComEd (41% of utility earnings).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Regulatory Risk (HIGH):**
The governor's direct intervention in an ostensibly independent regulatory process is concerning. If the precedent spreads to Illinois or Maryland during periods of high energy costs, Exelon's growth model breaks down. The next PECO rate case attempt isn't expected until 2027, creating a dead zone for that subsidiary's earnings growth.

**2. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH):**
With $43.4 billion in debt and $10B+ annual issuance needs, each 100bp increase in long-term rates adds approximately $100 million in annual interest expense as debt rolls. At current 30-year rates above 5%, new issuance costs significantly exceed older fixed-rate debt being retired.

**3. Capital Markets Access (MODERATE):**
Persistent negative FCF requires continuous market access. A credit downgrade or market dislocation would force capex deferrals, slowing growth.

**4. Data Center Execution Risk (MODERATE):**
The 19 GW pipeline and $12-17B transmission optionality depend on permitting, interconnection agreements, generation adequacy (not in Exelon's control), and continued hyperscaler demand. Any slowdown in AI infrastructure buildout reduces this opportunity.

**5. IRS NOLC PLR Issue (LOW-MODERATE, potentially HIGH impact):**
An IRS private letter ruling methodology could reduce regulatory liabilities by $1.2-$1.7 billion. Outcome uncertain; adverse resolution would reduce rate base for all subsidiaries except PECO.

**6. Dilution (LOW-MODERATE but persistent):**
$2.5B ATM equity program plus $1B in convertible notes create ongoing per-share dilution. At current pace (~$690M/year), annual dilution of approximately 1.5%.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-$58, +13% to +26%):**
- PECO situation resolves by 2027 with a compromise rate case filing
- Data center load growth materializes faster than expected, triggering incremental transmission investment
- Interest rates moderate (Fed cuts), reducing financing costs and improving utility sector sentiment
- ComEd and BGE rate cases proceed normally, confirming PECO was idiosyncratic
- Multiple re-rates from 16.2x to 18-20x on restored confidence

**Bear Case ($38-$42, -9% to -18%):**
- PECO regulatory hostility spreads to other jurisdictions under affordability pressure
- Interest rates remain elevated, compressing ROE and forcing capex deferrals
- Data center demand disappoints (hyperscaler capex cuts, permitting delays)
- Chicago franchise challenge materializes
- Multiple compresses further to 13-14x on sustained regulatory risk
- Convertible notes trigger dilution if stock appreciates modestly then retreats

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**

2. PECO files a new, politically acceptable rate case with constructive reception from Pennsylvania PUC
3. Confirmation of data center interconnection conversions from pipeline to committed load (look for secured TSA percentage increasing from 45% toward 60%+)

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Another jurisdiction (particularly Illinois/ComEd) experiences regulatory pushback or political intervention on rate increases
2. FY2026 or FY2027 EPS guidance revised below current range floor ($2.81 for 2026)
3. Credit rating downgrade from any major agency
4. Dividend growth suspended or reduced

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August). Key developments to monitor before then: (a) any ComEd or BGE rate case developments, (b) interest rate trajectory post-June FOMC, (c) data center load growth updates at utility sector conferences.

## Conclusion

Exelon presents a classic utility income holding: reasonable yield (3.6%), modest growth (5-7% EPS CAGR target), and defensiveness through regulation. The PECO rate case withdrawal in April 2026 created genuine uncertainty about the regulatory compact in one jurisdiction representing 29% of utility earnings, driving analyst downgrades and a multiple compression from approximately 17.5x to 16.2x forward.

At current prices, the stock is fairly valued given this uncertainty. The discount to peers is partially justified by the pure-T&D model (no AI generation premium) and partially by PECO overhang that should prove temporary. The data center demand pipeline represents meaningful upside optionality over a 2-3 year horizon, but near-term catalysts for re-rating are limited. Dividend yield of 3.6% with 5% annual growth provides adequate compensation for holding through the uncertainty period.

Recommendation: **HOLD** for income investors with existing positions. Prospective buyers should wait for either price weakness to the $42-43 level or positive PECO regulatory developments before initiating.
