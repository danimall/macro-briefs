## Executive Summary

First Hawaiian Inc is the largest bank headquartered in Hawaii, with $24.0 billion in total assets, $14.3 billion in loans, and a >30% share of total Hawaii deposits. FY2025 was a strong earnings year: net income rose 20% to $276.3M ($2.20 diluted EPS), NIM expanded 20 basis points to 3.15%, the efficiency ratio improved over 500 basis points to 56.4%, and the bank repaid all FHLB borrowings to run an entirely deposit-funded balance sheet. Capital return is accelerating — a $250M buyback (roughly 8% of market cap) was authorized for 2026 on top of a $1.04/share dividend yielding approximately 4.2%.

The fundamental question with FHB is whether a dominant franchise in a structurally limited market deserves a premium or a discount. Hawaii's economy is dependent on tourism, the U.S. military, and an overheated real estate market (Oahu median single-family home: $1.14M). International visitor arrivals remain below pre-pandemic levels, population out-migration continues, and the 10-K explicitly flags gradual tariff pass-through to already elevated consumer prices. Non-performing assets nearly doubled in FY2025. The $995.5M goodwill balance — a relic of BNP Paribas's 2001 acquisition — inflates book value metrics and represents an existential impairment risk. Short interest at 12.9% of float signals institutional skepticism. FHB is a solid income vehicle, but the growth ceiling is low and the risk profile skews asymmetrically to the downside.

## Company Overview

First Hawaiian was founded in 1858 as Bishop & Company, making it the oldest bank formed west of the Mississippi. It operates 49 branches across Hawaii (45), Guam (3), and Saipan (1), with approximately 2,000 employees averaging 11.7 years of tenure. Former parent BNP Paribas completed its exit in 2019.

The bank restructured reporting segments in Q4 2025 into two lines: Retail Banking (2025 net income: $250.5M, +10% YoY) and Commercial Banking ($130.0M, -9% YoY), with Treasury activities reported in Corporate/Other (net loss of $104.3M). Loan composition is 69% Hawaii, 23% U.S. mainland, 7% Guam/Saipan, and 1% other — making FHB functionally a single-state economy bet.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 marked a meaningful recovery in earnings power:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Interest Income | $663.7M | $622.7M | $636.1M |
| Noninterest Income | $217.0M | $185.8M | $200.8M |
| Net Income | $276.3M | $230.1M | $235.0M |
| Diluted EPS | $2.20 | $1.79 | $1.84 |
| NIM | 3.15% | 2.95% | 2.92% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 56.43% | 61.57% | — |
| ROA | 1.16% | 0.96% | — |
| ROE | 10.26% | 9.00% | — |
| ROTCE | 16.27% | 14.74% | — |

NIM expansion was driven by deposit cost declines outpacing asset yield compression — the rate paid on interest-bearing deposits fell 45 bps to 2.06%, while loan yields dropped only 20 bps to 5.45%. The noninterest income increase includes a favorable YoY comparison due to $26.2M in one-time investment portfolio restructuring losses booked in FY2024.

Expense discipline was solid. Noninterest expense decreased slightly ($499.3M vs. $501.2M) while revenue grew meaningfully. Regulatory assessment fees dropped from $32.1M (FY2023) to $12.1M (FY2025) as the FDIC special assessment wound down — a meaningful tailwind that is largely non-recurring.

### Balance Sheet and Capital

FHB is very well capitalized with a clean funding structure:

- CET1 ratio: 13.17% (minimum requirement: ~7%)
- Tier 1 leverage: 9.27%
- Zero borrowings — $250M FHLB advance repaid in Q3 2025
- Available borrowing capacity: $6.6B ($3.3B FHLB + $3.3B FRB)
- Tangible book value per share: $14.46 (up from $12.83)

However, two balance sheet items demand scrutiny. First, goodwill of $995.5M represents 36% of total equity and creates an $8.11/share gap between book value ($22.57) and tangible book ($14.46). At a stock price around $25, FHB trades at ~1.1x book but ~1.7x tangible book — a distinction that matters in bank valuation. Second, unrealized losses in the securities portfolio total $(521.9M), improved from $(792.7M) but still material relative to tangible equity of $1.77B (a 29% ratio).

### Credit Quality

Credit trends deteriorated in FY2025:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Non-accrual loans / total loans | 0.29% | 0.14% |
| NPAs | $41.0M | $20.7M |
| ACL / total loans | 1.18% | 1.11% |
| Net charge-offs / avg loans | 0.11% | 0.10% |
| ACL / NPA coverage | 4.11x | 7.76x |

Non-performing assets nearly doubled, with C&I non-accruals surging from $0.3M to $8.8M. Net charge-offs remain very low at 11 bps, but the NPA trajectory is the critical forward indicator. The allowance for credit losses includes qualitative adjustments for "cyclical losses consistent with a recession" — management is implicitly reserving for economic deterioration.

Provision for credit losses increased to $27.2M from $14.8M, up 84% YoY. While the absolute level remains manageable, the rate of change warrants monitoring.

### Capital Return

FHB is returning capital aggressively:

- FY2025: $100M buyback completed (4.0M shares, ~3.2% of float), $1.04/share dividend
- FY2026: $250M buyback authorized (roughly 8% of market cap at current prices)
- Payout ratio: 47.3% (down from 58.1% as earnings grew faster than dividends)

At an estimated market cap of ~$3.1B, the combined dividend ($128M) and 2026 buyback ($250M) yield approximately 12% in total capital return. This is the core of the income thesis.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are structurally constrained:

1. **Geographic ceiling.** Hawaii's economy is small, tourism-dependent, and facing population out-migration. FHB already holds >30% of deposits, and by law cannot acquire another Hawaii bank. Organic loan growth in FY2025 was modest — total loans declined slightly from $14.4B to $14.3B.

2. **NIM expansion may be peaking.** The 20 bps NIM improvement in FY2025 was driven by falling deposit costs. In a stable-to-higher rate environment (current Fed stance), further deposit cost declines are limited. FHB is modestly asset-sensitive (+200 bps = +6.3% NII), which provides some protection if rates stay elevated, but NIM expansion from current 3.15% levels will be harder to achieve.

3. **Fee income upside is limited.** Noninterest income of $217M benefited from favorable YoY comparisons. Structural fee growth at a Hawaii-focused bank tracks the island economy's nominal GDP growth — low single digits at best.

4. **Solar lease headwind.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act may impair FHB's ability to originate solar leases, a niche business that generated energy tax credits.

The earnings surprise track record over the last four quarters (3 beats, 1 in-line, with two substantial beats of 11.8% and 17.5% in mid-2025) suggests management has been conservative on guidance, but this likely reflects one-time NIM expansion and regulatory cost tailwinds rather than a sustainable growth acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At an estimated price of ~$25/share:

| Metric | FHB | Context |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (trailing) | ~11.4x | Reasonable for regional bank |
| Price/Book | ~1.1x | Optically cheap |
| Price/Tangible Book | ~1.7x | Expensive for low-growth bank |
| Dividend Yield | ~4.2% | Attractive |
| ROTCE | 16.27% | Solid |

The valuation appears fair to slightly rich on a tangible book basis. A bank generating 16% ROTCE typically deserves 1.5-1.8x tangible book, putting FHB squarely in the middle of that range. However, ROE of only 10.3% (depressed by the goodwill-inflated equity base) and a structurally low growth ceiling argue against a premium multiple.

Compared to Hawaii-specific peers Bank of Hawaii (BOH) and Central Pacific Financial (CPF), FHB trades at a slight premium justified by its dominant market position and superior profitability metrics. Relative to the broader regional bank universe, the valuation is unremarkable.

The $250M buyback for 2026, if completed at current prices, would retire approximately 10M shares (~8% of outstanding), providing meaningful EPS accretion. This is the primary valuation support mechanism — FHB is buying back shares at 1.7x tangible book, which creates value as long as ROTCE exceeds the implied cost of equity at that multiple (roughly 10-11%). At 16.27% ROTCE, the math works.

## Competitive Landscape

FHB's competitive moat is real but narrow:

**Strengths:**
- 167-year brand history and deep community entrenchment
- >30% deposit market share — dominant position in a small market
- 11.7-year average employee tenure preserves institutional knowledge and customer relationships
- $6.5B in noninterest-bearing demand deposits (though declining) provides cheap funding
- 93% core deposit ratio — among the highest in U.S. banking

**Limitations:**
- Legally prohibited from acquiring Hawaii banks due to deposit concentration
- No meaningful product differentiation vs. competitors
- Fintech and mainland digital banking create increasing competition for deposit growth — the 10-K explicitly acknowledges this
- Private credit and direct lending platforms are competing for commercial relationships

The franchise value of Hawaii's dominant bank is significant, but largely static. FHB cannot grow market share in its primary market through acquisition, and competitive pressures from digital channels erode the branch-based moat over time.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Geographic concentration (Critical).** A Hawaii-specific recession driven by tourism decline, military base closures, or natural disasters would impair the entire loan portfolio simultaneously. There is no geographic diversification safety valve. The Maui wildfires of August 2023 demonstrated the potential for localized catastrophic events.

2. **Goodwill impairment ($995.5M / 36% of equity).** A sustained decline in stock price, earnings, or franchise value could trigger impairment testing failure. A full write-down would eliminate approximately $8.11/share in book value and produce a massive one-time loss. Low probability, high impact.

3. **Rising NPAs.** The NPA trajectory in FY2025 warrants monitoring. Hawaii's judicial foreclosure process extends resolution timelines, meaning credit deterioration, once begun, persists longer than in non-judicial states.

4. **Real estate concentration.** 75% of total loans ($10.7B) are secured by real estate in an overheated market. CRE exposure of $4.6B is subject to heightened regulatory scrutiny.

5. **Tariff and inflation risk.** Hawaii already has the highest consumer prices in the U.S. Further tariff-driven cost pass-through would pressure consumer credit quality, reduce tourism spending, and potentially accelerate population out-migration.

6. **Uninsured deposit concentration.** At 49% of total deposits ($10.1B), uninsured deposits are elevated. While FHB's dominant position and community relationships reduce flight risk, the SVB/Signature Bank episode demonstrated how quickly confidence-driven withdrawals can materialize.

### Options Market Signal

Short interest at 12.9% of float with 6.6 days to cover represents a meaningful institutional bearish position. This level is not extreme enough to signal imminent squeeze risk, but it indicates informed skepticism — likely reflecting concerns about Hawaii economic exposure, NPA trajectory, or the goodwill overhang. The bearish positioning is consistent with the structural risk profile rather than a catalyst-specific trade.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$28-30)
- NIM stabilizes at or above 3.15% as deposit repricing cycle completes
- $250M buyback in 2026 drives meaningful EPS accretion to $2.40+
- NPAs stabilize and begin declining as isolated credits resolve
- Hawaii tourism recovery accelerates (especially Japanese visitors)
- Market awards premium for dominant franchise with 16%+ ROTCE
- Valuation: 12-13x $2.40 EPS = $28.80-$31.20

### Bear Case (~$18-20)
- Hawaii enters recession driven by tourism decline, tariff pass-through, or natural disaster
- NPAs continue rising, provisions increase to $50-75M annually
- Noninterest-bearing deposit runoff accelerates, compressing NIM toward 2.80-2.90%
- Goodwill impairment becomes a discussion topic, depressing multiple
- Short thesis plays out: 12.9% SI reflects informed positioning on Hawaii-specific deterioration
- Valuation: 9-10x $1.80-$2.00 EPS = $16.20-$20.00

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
- NPAs decline for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating credit improvement
- Stock price declines to $21-22 range (~1.5x tangible book), creating a more favorable entry with >5% dividend yield
- Evidence of Hawaii tourism recovery to pre-pandemic levels, particularly Japanese visitor spend

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
- NPAs exceed $75M or non-accrual ratio exceeds 0.50%
- NIM contracts below 2.90% for two consecutive quarters
- Management signals goodwill impairment testing concerns
- Hawaii unemployment rate rises above 4.5% (currently ~3%)

### Reassessment Timeline
- Q1 2026 earnings (late April 2026): Key data point on NPA trajectory and NIM stability
- Mid-2026: Progress on $250M buyback execution and Hawaii tourism data for peak summer season
- Q3 2026: Full year of post-rate-cut NIM data

## Conclusion

First Hawaiian is a well-managed, dominant bank franchise in a structurally limited market. The FY2025 earnings recovery was genuine — NIM expansion, expense discipline, and regulatory cost relief produced a 20% earnings increase. Capital return is accelerating, and the 4.2% dividend yield provides income support.

However, the growth ceiling is low, the geographic concentration is extreme, NPAs are rising from a low base, and $1B in legacy goodwill creates an asymmetric impairment risk. At 1.7x tangible book, the stock is fairly priced for the current earnings level but offers limited upside in a no-growth scenario. The 12.9% short interest reflects legitimate institutional concern about Hawaii-specific risks in an environment of tariff pass-through and elevated consumer costs.

FHB is an appropriate income-oriented hold for investors who want Hawaii banking exposure and are comfortable with the geographic concentration risk. The combined buyback-plus-dividend total return of ~12% for 2026 provides a margin of safety, but upgrading to BUY requires either a more attractive entry price or evidence that credit quality concerns are abating. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Income)**
