**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Collegium Pharmaceutical is a commercial-stage specialty pharma operating a debt-funded "buy-and-build" roll-up. It carries no meaningful internal pipeline; growth comes from acquiring marketed products and driving organic script growth on top of a legacy pain franchise that is now eroding to generics. The strategic story is a deliberate pivot away from opioids (~80% of current revenue) toward the growing ADHD market, anchored by Jornay PM (acquired via Ironshore, +36% YoY in Q1'26) and the pending $650M cash acquisition of AZSTARYS from Corium.

The setup is genuinely two-sided. On the bull side, forward estimate revisions are strong: next-year consensus EPS rose 24.3% over 90 days to $7.50, and current-year rose 5.2% to $7.76, while the stock trades at roughly 4.6x forward adjusted EPS on a $35.57 base. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$460M against a ~$1.2B market cap and FCF margins above 40% support the argument that the market is discounting the pain-franchise decline too aggressively. A 2027 amortization roll-off (Belbuca and Nucynta intangibles fully amortize in 2026) is a real, underappreciated tailwind to reported GAAP earnings.

On the bear side, the Nucynta generic cliff is live now: Hikma launched authorized generics of the IR (Feb 2026) and ER (Mar 2026) formulations, and branded Nucynta contributes ~$47M/quarter that will decline. The AZSTARYS deal re-levers a balance sheet already carrying ~$935M of debt against ~$312M of equity, precisely as the opioid base rolls off. Layered on top are ~20% short interest with 11.9 days to cover, a near-the-money convertible overhang, and two large binary litigation claims (a $500M+ NSP arbitration and a Grünenthal patent suit against the company's own AG program). The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.0 → 5.8), and nothing in the current data reverses that direction decisively. We rate COLL a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Collegium is headquartered in Stoughton, MA and led by CEO Vikram Karnani (who succeeded Joe Ciaffoni) and CFO Colleen Tupper. The company commercializes two franchises:

**Pain (~$155M/quarter, +4% YoY, the legacy cash generator):**
- Belbuca (buprenorphine buccal film) — $52.6M Q1'26; patent protection to ~2027 on the '866 patent, with a favorable Alvogen injunction extending to Dec 2032 on the '539 patent
- Xtampza ER (abuse-deterrent oxycodone) — $50.8M, +7%; ongoing Purdue litigation
- Nucynta franchise (tapentadol IR/ER) — $47.0M, now facing generic competition
- Symproic (naldemedine) — $4.2M

**ADHD / Neuropsychiatry (the growth engine):**
- Jornay PM (evening-dosed methylphenidate) — $38.9M in Q1'26, +36% YoY, 206K+ scripts at all-time highs, ~30K prescribers. The only FDA-approved evening-dosed stimulant.
- AZSTARYS (serdexmethylphenidate/dexmethylphenidate) — being acquired from Corium for $650M cash plus up to $135M in milestones, funded by existing cash and a $300M delayed-draw term loan.

The stated strategic objective is to extend revenue durability into the late 2030s by replacing generic-threatened opioid cash flows with ADHD assets. The mechanism is straightforward: each acquisition must generate enough cash to service the debt raised to buy it before the older assets erode. The model has cycled through BDSI (2022), Ironshore (2024), and now AZSTARYS (2026).

## Financial Analysis

Operating performance is solid and cash generation is real. FY2025 revenue was ~$780M (+24% YoY, boosted by a full year of Jornay PM), up from ~$631M in FY2024. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was ~$460M against GAAP net income of ~$63M. Q1'26 revenue was $193.5M (+9% YoY), with GAAP net income of $14.5M ($0.40 diluted EPS) versus adjusted EPS of $1.76 (up from $1.49). Q1'26 adjusted EBITDA was $103.9M (+9%) and operating cash flow was $57.1M.

FY2026 guidance (which excludes AZSTARYS) is revenue of $805–825M, Jornay PM of $190–200M, and adjusted EBITDA of $455–475M, essentially flat adjusted EBITDA year over year, reflecting the pain-franchise transition offsetting ADHD growth.

The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap is the defining feature of these numbers. Intangible amortization runs $55.5M per quarter (~$222M annually), which is why GAAP EPS is a fraction of adjusted EPS. This is inherent to an acquisition roll-up: reported earnings are largely a function of non-cash amortization of purchased product rights. Belbuca and Nucynta intangibles are fully amortized during 2026, removing ~$130M+ of annual amortization and lifting reported GAAP EPS meaningfully in 2027, before AZSTARYS amortization is layered back on. This is a genuine catalyst that a trailing-P/E screen misses.

The balance sheet is leveraged but serviceable:
- Cash + marketable securities: $421.8M (Q1'26)
- Term loan: $572.8M (2025 Truist facility, ~6.85% effective, matures 2030, unhedged floating)
- 2029 convertible notes: $241.5M (2.875%, ~$36.56 conversion price — at the money)
- Deferred royalty obligation (Jornay/Ironshore): $121.6M at a 13.6% effective rate
- Total debt ~$935M+ against ~$312M equity (D/E >4x)

The Altman Z-Score reading near 1.4 is a false positive. The Z-Score penalizes the thin equity base and heavy intangibles inherent to a leveraged roll-up. With $460M+ EBITDA, $421M cash, 42%+ FCF margins, and no near-term maturity wall (term loan to 2030, convert to 2029), this is not a distress candidate. The real question is cash-flow durability against debt service as generics erode the base.

The December 2025 refinancing was a positive: the high-cost 2024 Pharmakon term loan was replaced with a cheaper Truist facility, lowering interest expense ~$5M/quarter YoY, at the cost of a $16.0M debt-extinguishment charge. The $300M delayed-draw tranche is pre-positioned to fund AZSTARYS. The $121.6M Jornay deferred royalty carries a ~13.6% rate and a rising cash royalty (7.4% → 9.7% of Jornay net sales through 2032), so as Jornay grows, an increasing slice of its economics is diverted to this obligation, a drag that headline EBITDA masks.

The earnings surprise record is decent but not clean: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with a -14.1% miss in Q4'25 followed by an +8.4% beat in Q1'26. Visibility is adequate, not high.

## Growth Analysis

Growth durability hinges entirely on the ADHD pivot outrunning the pain-franchise decline. The evidence is mixed but leans constructive on the forward numbers.

Jornay PM's growth to all-time-high scripts is the strongest data point in the story, and it is the only FDA-approved evening-dosed stimulant, a differentiated profile in a large, growing market. AZSTARYS, if it closes and integrates as management claims (immediately accretive to adjusted EBITDA), adds a second ADHD asset and extends the revenue runway.

Estimate revisions corroborate the momentum. Next-year consensus EPS rose from $6.04 to $7.50 over 90 days, and current-year from $7.38 to $7.76. Next-quarter estimates rose +8.2% over 90 days. The 90-day next-year revision sits below the +30% threshold that historically flags the strongest forward momentum. The modest -1.4% next-year revision over the trailing 30 days suggests the upward revision velocity is decelerating rather than accelerating.

Against this, the pain franchise is contracting in real time. Nucynta's pediatric exclusivity has expired (ER Dec 2025, IR July 2026), and a third-party generic of Nucynta IR was approved Jan 2026. Collegium retains a profit share on the Hikma AG, softening but not offsetting the decline of branded Nucynta. FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA against FY2025's implies the ADHD growth and the pain decline roughly net to flat this year.

The structural growth question is whether AZSTARYS's cash flows outrun the debt taken to buy it, and whether Jornay can keep compounding against competition from Supernus, Tris, and J&J and against DEA methylphenidate quota volatility. The next 12–18 months are the proof point.

## Valuation Assessment

On headline multiples, COLL looks cheap: ~4.6x forward adjusted EPS ($7.76 current-year estimate on $35.57), or ~4.7x next-year ($7.50). The peer set (PCRX 196x, AMPH 12.3x, PRGO negative, PAHC 13.4x, INVA 3.9x; median 13.4x) is heterogeneous, but even against the median, COLL screens inexpensive on adjusted earnings. Trailing P/E of 22.0x reflects the GAAP earnings depressed by amortization, not the cash-generating reality.

The cheapness is not sufficient on its own for a high valuation score. A compressed multiple on a specialty-pharma roll-up facing a live generic cliff and re-levering into an acquisition can be a value trap: the market is pricing the risk that acquired-asset cash flows fail to outrun debt service and generic erosion. Cheapness in this sector, absent an independent catalyst, warrants skepticism rather than reward.

There are, however, two identifiable catalysts that support a moderately positive valuation view. First, the 2026 amortization roll-off will structurally lift GAAP EPS in 2027, narrowing the GAAP-adjusted gap and improving the reported-earnings optics that drive some algorithmic and screen-based flows. Second, the +24.3% 90-day next-year revision signals consensus is still catching up to the ADHD trajectory. Analyst targets (low $45, high $60, mean $53.6, median $56) sit well above the current price, but given the balance-sheet leverage and litigation tails, these should be read as an optimistic bookend rather than a floor. The stock trades below every analyst target, yet has round-tripped from a $50.79 high to $35.57, which indicates the market is weighting the cliff and leverage risks more heavily than the sell side.

Net, the valuation offers some asymmetric upside if the pivot executes, tempered by the genuine possibility that the cheapness reflects structural risk rather than mispricing. We score valuation moderately, not aggressively.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is mixed and trending toward net erosion in the near term.

In pain, Collegium competes with Endo, Mallinckrodt, Purdue, and Teva, and increasingly with Vertex's suzetrigine (Journavx), the first new non-opioid oral analgesic class in ~20 years, approved Jan 2025 and a genuine long-term structural threat to opioid demand. This is a deteriorating position: ~80% of revenue is opioid/pain, and that base faces both generic entry and a new therapeutic class.

In ADHD, Jornay's evening-dosing differentiation is a real moat feature, but the field is crowded (Supernus, Tris, J&J, Novartis, UCB, generic methylphenidate), and industry-wide DEA quota constraints periodically cause shortages. The DEA raised the methylphenidate quota in Oct 2025, but supply reliability remains a swing factor Collegium does not control.

Distribution is concentrated among three large wholesalers, pressuring pricing. Manufacturing carries sole-source risk: Patheon (Thermo Fisher) is the sole manufacturer for Xtampza ER and Nucynta ER, with some API sole-sourced and shared with Purdue.

The trajectory matters more than the level: the profitable legacy franchise is losing ground while the growth franchise is gaining it, and whether the mix shift is net accretive to the competitive position depends on AZSTARYS integration and Jornay's ability to sustain share against well-capitalized competitors. On balance, the position is roughly stable-to-slightly-deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk load here is heavy and largely not fully reflected in the cheap headline multiple.

1. **Nucynta generic cliff — live.** The single most important near-term revenue headwind. Branded Nucynta will erode; the Hikma AG profit share softens but does not eliminate the hit.

2. **Re-leveraging into AZSTARYS.** The deal adds floating-rate debt precisely as the opioid base rolls off. A 1% rate move on the $572.8M term loan is a ~$5.7M annual interest swing; the AZSTARYS draw adds ~$300M more unhedged floating exposure.

3. **NSP arbitration.** David Lickrish (as assignee of North Sound Pharmaceuticals) seeks damages "in excess of $500M" over pre-acquisition Ironshore conduct, larger than annual EBITDA in notional terms. Low visibility on probability, but a material tail. ~$37M of Ironshore consideration remains in escrow.

4. **Grünenthal patent suit (filed Feb 2026).** Collegium's Nucynta licensor is suing both Collegium and Hikma over the Nucynta ER authorized generic, alleging infringement of two Orange Book patents. Hikma has demanded indemnification, complicating the company's own generic-defense strategy.

5. **Convertible dilution.** The 2029 convert with a ~$36.56 strike sits at the current price, creating a ~6.6M-share overhang.

6. **Opioid overhang.** July 2025 class-wide labeling changes, REMS burden, state AG scrutiny, and the Journavx competitive threat.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. ATM IV of 61.2% is rich versus 40.2% one-year historical vol (a 20.9pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 61.2% > long-term 56.7%), which markets typically use to price an imminent catalyst, plausibly the AZSTARYS close/guidance update or the next earnings print. The put/call skew is -19.9% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.40, suggesting near-term positioning leans bullish despite the high SI. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.02 to 1.15 since May, indicating a build in open put positions even as flow skews to calls, a mixed picture. IV ranking at the 48th percentile means the elevated vol is not extreme within its own chain. The implied ±14.7% one-month move is large and consistent with a binary event on the horizon.

Combined with 20.1% short interest and 11.9 days to cover, the setup carries genuine two-sided volatility: a clean AZSTARYS close plus reaffirmed accretion could squeeze shorts, while any Jornay stumble or litigation escalation could break the pivot thesis. This is not a low-risk holding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The ADHD pivot works. Jornay PM keeps compounding above 30%, AZSTARYS closes and proves immediately accretive, and the two ADHD assets extend revenue durability into the late 2030s. The 2026 amortization roll-off lifts GAAP EPS in 2027, narrowing the optics gap and re-rating the stock as reported earnings converge toward adjusted. At ~4.7x forward adjusted EPS with rising estimates, even a modest re-rating toward the analyst target band ($45–60) implies 27–69% upside. Short covering amplifies any positive catalyst. Bull case: $55–60.

### Bear Case
Nucynta erodes faster than the profit share offsets, AZSTARYS integration disappoints or the debt service on ~$1.2B of total leverage strains free cash flow, and a Jornay stumble (competition, DEA quota) undercuts the growth engine. One of the litigation tails (NSP, Grünenthal) crystallizes into real liability. The convert dilutes at the money. The cheap multiple turns out to reflect structural risk, not mispricing, and the stock drifts toward the low-$20s as the roll-up model runs out of accretive acquisitions to outrun erosion. Bear case: $24–28.

### Base Case
The mix shift roughly nets to flat adjusted EBITDA in 2026 (consistent with guidance), the pivot progresses without a clean win or a clean break, and the stock trades in a wide band as the market waits for AZSTARYS proof points and litigation clarity. Base case: $38–42 over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The forward estimate momentum and cheap adjusted multiple are real, but they are offset by a live generic cliff, re-leveraging, heavy litigation tails, and an insider signal that diverges from the bullish narrative. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the next 12–18 months are the proof point.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings and the post-AZSTARYS-close guidance update are the immediate catalysts. Reassess at the next quarterly print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AZSTARYS closes and management raises FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance above ~$500M with confirmed accretion
- Jornay PM sustains >25% YoY growth for two consecutive quarters while the Nucynta profit share materially offsets branded erosion
- The stock compresses toward the low-$30s while forward estimates hold or rise, restoring clearer asymmetry, or the NSP/Grünenthal claims resolve favorably, removing the largest tail

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Jornay PM growth decelerates below ~10% YoY or scripts turn sequentially negative
- Free cash flow after debt service turns negative for two consecutive quarters, or the company draws further debt for a new deal before AZSTARYS proves accretive
- An adverse development in the NSP arbitration or Grünenthal suit with quantified material liability, or a guidance cut

## Conclusion

Collegium is a cash-generative, leveraged specialty-pharma roll-up executing a credible but unproven pivot from opioids to ADHD. The bull case rests on Jornay PM's momentum, AZSTARYS accretion, rising forward estimates, and a 2027 amortization-driven GAAP earnings lift, against a cheap forward adjusted multiple. The bear case rests on the live Nucynta cliff, re-leveraging into AZSTARYS, ~20% short interest, a near-the-money convert, and two large binary litigation claims, with insiders net selling and zero open-market buys.

The score trajectory has drifted from 6.0 to 5.8, and the current data neither reverses nor accelerates that trend. Execution over the next several quarters decides the direction. We rate COLL a **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. The valuation offers real upside if the pivot executes cleanly, but the risk load is too heavy and too binary to justify a BUY at this stage.
