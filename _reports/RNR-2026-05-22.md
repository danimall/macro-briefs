## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe is delivering one of the strongest periods in its 30+ year history. The company produced $56.03 in EPS for FY2025 — a year that included the California Wildfires ($785.7M net loss impact) and Hurricane Melissa — demonstrating that its three-driver model (underwriting + fees + investment income) functions as designed even under severe stress. Book value per share compounded 27% in 2025 and 19.4% in 2024. At $296, the stock trades at 5.3x trailing earnings and approximately 1.18x book value, a substantial discount to demonstrated earning power when benchmarked against reinsurance peer multiples.

The current operating environment is favorable but evolving. Property catastrophe pricing remains adequate despite new capacity entering the market. Rate adequacy remains favorable across the portfolio, though increased competition and reinsurance capacity are putting downward pressure on rates. Q1 2026 delivered a 73.0% combined ratio and 21.8% annualized operating ROE with no major catastrophe events. Management continues to repurchase shares aggressively ($457M year-to-date through April 25, 2026), signaling confidence that intrinsic value exceeds market price.

The primary risk is casualty reserve uncertainty. The Casualty & Specialty segment ran a 104.4% combined ratio in FY2025, and accident years 2017-2019 are developing adversely. IBNR of $12.9B in this segment represents latent exposure to social inflation trends. Management is responding by reducing general liability exposure, but the lag between underwriting action and reserve outcome is measured in years.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe is a Bermuda-domiciled global reinsurer operating through two segments: Property (42% of gross premiums written) and Casualty & Specialty (58%). The Property segment — primarily catastrophe excess-of-loss reinsurance — is the high-margin core, delivering a 61.4% combined ratio in FY2025. The Casualty & Specialty segment provides diversification and premium volume but generates thinner underwriting margins.

The company differentiates through three mechanisms: (1) proprietary catastrophe risk modeling (REMS©), (2) a Capital Partners platform managing third-party capital that generates fee income while sharing catastrophe losses, and (3) a $36B investment portfolio producing $1.7B annually in net investment income. Post-Validus acquisition in November 2023, RNR has significantly expanded its scale, with FY2025 gross premiums written of $11.74B positioning it among the largest global reinsurers.

Financial strength ratings are A+ (S&P/Fitch) and A1 (Moody's), all with positive or stable outlooks. A.M. Best revised its issuer credit outlook to "positive" in October 2025, suggesting a potential upgrade.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** FY2025 net income of $2.65B ($56.03 diluted EPS) on net premiums earned of $9.9B and investment income of $1.7B. This followed $1.83B ($35.21 EPS) in FY2024 and $2.53B in FY2023. The earnings trajectory is lumpy by nature — catastrophe years depress results significantly (FY2022 produced a net loss with ROE of -22.0% and diluted EPS of -$25.50). The underlying franchise has generated 20%+ ROE in the recent hard-market period (2023–2025), though the full-cycle record includes meaningful volatility.

**Q1 2026 Performance:** Operating income of $590.5M, combined ratio of 73.0%, GAAP net income of $284.5M (depressed by $421.9M in unrealized investment losses from equity market volatility and rising yields). Property combined ratio of 34.1% (light catastrophe quarter) offset by Casualty & Specialty at 100.4%. Annualized operating ROE: 21.8%.

**Balance Sheet:** Total equity of $11.6B, debt of $2.3B (debt-to-equity of 20%), total investments of $36.1B. The investment portfolio carries AA-average credit quality with 2.8-year duration in fixed income. Reserves of $22.3B are supported by consistent favorable prior-year development of $1.09B (2025) and $851M (2024) in the Property segment.

**Capital Return:** Share count declined from 50.2M (end-2024) to 44.0M (end-2025), with FY2025 repurchases totaling $1.6B at an average price of $247.62 per share. In Q1 2026, the company repurchased an additional $352.5M at an average price of $289.36, plus $104.8M through April 25, 2026. Dividends of $1.60/share (~0.5% yield) are de minimis; capital return is equity-repurchase-driven.

**Book Value Trajectory:** BVPS grew from $104.65 (2022) → $165.20 (2023) → $195.77 (2024) → $247.00 (2025) → $250.48 (Q1 2026). This 33% CAGR since 2022 — through a period that included one of the most severe catastrophe years in history — validates the structural earning power, though the 2022 starting point reflected a trough after significant losses.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at RNR is measured differently than in most equities. The company's stated objective is BVPS + dividends growth, not premium volume expansion.

**Positive factors:** (1) Elevated investment yields ($1.7B income on $36B at ~4.7% running yield) provide a structurally higher earnings floor than the 2017-2021 period. (2) The Capital Partners platform continues scaling — the Medici UCITS launch (March 2025) expands European investor access. (3) Continued share buybacks mechanically accelerate per-share growth. (4) Rate adequacy remains favorable across the portfolio despite competitive pressures.

**Constraining factors:** (1) Reinsurance rate softening is confirmed by management and Fitch. Gross premiums in Q1 2026 Property declined ~20% YoY. The company is "prioritizing margin over growth." (2) Current-year estimates are declining: consensus FY2026 EPS of approximately $40.74 represents a ~27% decline from FY2025's $56.03 (which included significant investment gains). (3) The Bermuda 15% CIT implementation creates a material tax headwind that did not exist before 2025 — FY2025 tax expense was $396.3M, up $363.7M from 2024, though the actual annual impact will vary with profitability, jurisdictional mix, and available tax credits.

The growth trajectory is best characterized as high-quality compounding at a moderating rate. The recent hard-market ROE of 20%+ is likely to moderate as the catastrophe cycle normalizes and rates soften incrementally. A full-cycle view must account for years like 2022 when catastrophe losses produced negative returns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $296, RNR trades at:
- 5.3x trailing EPS ($56.03)
- ~7.3x estimated FY2026 EPS (~$40.74)
- 1.18x book value ($250.48 as of Q1 2026)
- ~1.27x tangible book (~$233/share estimated)

**Peer comparison:** Reinsurance sector P/E multiples generally range from 6-12x (EG at approximately 7.3x, RGA at approximately 11.7x, SPNT at approximately 5.9x, MTG at approximately 8.3x). RNR's trailing 5.3x appears cheap, but the forward ~7.3x on normalized earnings is closer to the peer range. The key question is whether RNR's superior recent ROE (25.9% vs. peer median of ~15%) and market position justify a premium.

**Why the market may be underpricing RNR:** (1) On forward earnings of ~$41/share, the ~7.3x multiple undervalues a franchise that has demonstrated 20%+ ROE in recent years with the ability to compound BVPS through catastrophe years. If the company can sustain 18-20% annual book value growth, a 1.5-2.0x book multiple would be justified, implying $375-500/share — though sustaining this growth rate through a full cycle remains unproven. (2) The trailing P/E of 5.3x reflects EPS inflated by investment gains, but even "normalized" operating earnings of ~$40-45/share produce a P/E of 6.6-7.4x for a franchise with RNR's track record. (3) Aggressive buybacks at $289/share in Q1 2026 signal management's view that intrinsic value exceeds market price.

**What justifies the discount vs. theoretical fair value:** (1) Catastrophe tail risk — a single mega-event could erase 30-50% of book value (as 2022 demonstrated with a -22% ROE). (2) Casualty reserve risk — $12.9B IBNR could develop adversely. (3) Rate cycle turning — if combined ratios normalize to 95%+, ROE compresses to 12-14%. (4) 2025 earnings quality included $1.18B in net realized/unrealized investment gains, which may not recur.

At ~7.3x forward earnings for a franchise with RNR's track record and recent ROE profile, the valuation offers asymmetric upside if the current favorable cycle extends, with downside protected by a strong balance sheet and low leverage.

## Competitive Landscape

RenaissanceRe holds a leading global position among pure reinsurers post-Validus, with $11.74B in FY2025 gross premiums written. Key competitive advantages:

**Proprietary risk modeling (REMS©):** 30+ years of catastrophe modeling development provides pricing precision that smaller competitors cannot replicate. This matters most in property catastrophe, where the ability to price tail risk better than competitors allows RNR to write business others mispriced, creating structural positive selection.

**Capital Partners platform:** Third-party capital reduces balance sheet risk while generating fee income ($329M in 2025). This creates a variable-cost structure where catastrophe losses are partially absorbed by non-controlling interests ($489M of California Wildfire losses borne by NCI). Few reinsurers have replicated this model at scale.

**Financial strength ratings (A+/A1):** Ratings are critical in reinsurance — cedants (primary insurers) require financially secure counterparties. RNR's positive outlook from A.M. Best and S&P suggests a possible upgrade, which would widen its addressable market.

**Competitive trajectory:** The market is experiencing modest softening as new capital enters attracted by 2023-2024 returns. RNR's response — reducing growth to maintain margins — is the correct strategic choice for a franchise prioritizing long-term value. While increased competition is putting pressure on rates, rate adequacy remains favorable across the portfolio.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Concentration (Primary):** A major hurricane making landfall in Miami, or a Tokyo earthquake, could generate losses of $3-5B+ for RNR. The California Wildfires cost $785.7M net, and that was one event in one geography. Climate change is increasing the frequency and severity of these events. The company mitigates through retrocession, Capital Partners loss-sharing, and portfolio limits, but tail risk cannot be eliminated in this business.

**Casualty Reserve Deterioration:** The $12.9B IBNR in Casualty & Specialty represents the most uncertain liability on the balance sheet. Accident years 2017-2019 are developing adversely. Management's sensitivity analysis shows a 10% increase in loss ratios could add $2.7B to reserves (23.4% of equity). Social inflation — rising jury awards, litigation funding, expanded legal theories — is an industry-wide secular trend with unclear terminal state.

**Rate Cycle Inflection:** If property catastrophe pricing returns to 2018-2019 levels, the Property combined ratio moves from 61% toward 80%+, compressing ROE to the low-teens. This would not justify the current 1.2x book multiple.

**Bermuda Tax Implementation:** The 15% CIT increased effective tax from near-zero to ~10% on a blended basis. This is a permanent structural change, though the annual impact will vary with profitability, jurisdictional mix, catastrophe experience, and available Bermuda substance-based tax credits.

**Broker Concentration:** 81.3% of GPW flows through three brokers (Aon/Marsh/Gallagher). Further consolidation could increase intermediary pricing power.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($375-431, +25-45%):** The favorable catastrophe cycle extends through 2027 as climate-driven loss trends support pricing discipline. Casualty reserves prove adequate. Management continues buying back shares at 5-7x earnings, mechanically growing BVPS. Rating upgrade triggers institutional re-rating to 1.5x book ($375) or 10x normalized earnings ($410+). The three-driver model delivers 20%+ ROE for 3+ more years.

**Bear Case ($225-250, -15-25%):** A major catastrophe event (Category 5 hurricane, $100B+ industry loss) depletes capital by 20-30% of book value. Simultaneously, casualty reserves prove deficient by $2-3B, requiring material strengthening that erases 1-2 years of book value growth. Rate softening accelerates as new capital enters, compressing ROE below 15%. The stock de-rates to 0.9-1.0x book ($225-250).

**Base Case ($345-365, +15-22%):** The current cycle moderates gradually. Combined ratio normalizes to 85-90%. Book value compounds at 15-18% annually. Share buybacks reduce outstanding to ~40M shares by year-end 2027. At 1.4x a projected $260-265 BVPS (YE2026), the stock reaches $345-365 within 12 months. This requires no heroic assumptions — merely continuation of demonstrated performance at slightly lower intensity.

## Conclusion

RenaissanceRe at ~7.3x forward earnings and 1.18x book represents a compelling risk-reward for a franchise that has compounded book value at 20%+ in the recent hard-market period (2023–2025). The three-driver earnings model — validated through the California Wildfires and multiple catastrophe years — provides structural resilience that justifies a premium to peers. Rate adequacy remains favorable despite increased competitive pressures, and continued aggressive buybacks at current prices provide both downside support and per-share growth acceleration.

The primary risks (catastrophe tail, casualty reserve uncertainty, rate softening) are real but manageable, and the current valuation already embeds meaningful skepticism relative to demonstrated earning power. The stock warrants a BUY rating with position sizing of 2-3% given the inherent catastrophe tail risk.

The geopolitical "structural demand floor" claim and "top-3 global reinsurer" ranking could not be validated against primary sources. These corrections temper the conviction level of the base-case target and the implied margin of safety.
