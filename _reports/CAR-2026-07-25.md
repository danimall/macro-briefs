**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avis Budget Group is a leveraged, fleet-cost-driven vehicle rental operator whose equity trades on a technical foundation rather than a fundamental one. The stock closed at $159.48 on July 24, 2026, roughly 40% above Goldman Sachs's recently raised $95 fundamental target and above the $138.86 consensus mean, the residue of a parabolic March–April 2026 meme-stock squeeze that carried intraday prints toward $847 before collapsing. Beneath the price action sits a business with negative shareholders' equity of -$3.13B at year-end 2025 (-$3.42B at Q1 2026), an Altman Z-Score of 0.05 that places it deep in the distress zone, and roughly $25.3B of total debt ($6.1B corporate, $19.2B vehicle-backed) supported by a fleet whose realizable value is the ultimate collateral.

The core problem is fleet depreciation and residual-value exposure. After the 2021–22 used-car boom inflated profits, normalization plus two rounds of accelerated fleet rotation produced $2.5B of impairments in 2024 and $518M in 2025 (largely an EV write-down following the OBBBA credit phase-out). Eighty-four percent of the fleet is "risk vehicles" with no manufacturer residual guarantee, meaning any softening of used-car prices flows directly to the P&L and potentially into ABS collateral coverage. FY2025 net loss was -$0.89B; the seasonally weak Q1 2026 net loss widened to -$283M. The frequently cited $748M FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA excludes the $518M impairment, $390M of "other fleet charges," and $131M of restructuring; the gap between adjusted and GAAP is the entire debate.

The single genuine near-term positive is the $650M Pentwater Section 16(b) short-swing profit settlement announced July 21, 2026 (pending SDNY approval), which recaptures the meme-squeeze profits speculators made in Avis's own stock. If approved, that cash roughly equals a full year of Adjusted EBITDA and meaningfully offsets negative equity and thin liquidity. Even crediting it fully, the stock is priced above where a repaired balance sheet would justify. We rate CAR AVOID. It is not a clean short given 55.8% short interest and an SRS-controlled thin float that makes the name structurally squeeze-prone.

## Company Overview

Avis operates three flagship brands — Avis (premium), Budget (value), and Zipcar (car-sharing) — plus Payless and regional marques across roughly 180 countries and 10,000 locations. FY2025 average fleet was ~684,000 vehicles, shrinking toward ~620,000 by Q1 2026 as management rightsizes.

Two reportable segments define the story. Americas contributed ~76% of FY2025 revenue ($8.9B) and is the structurally challenged core, with revenue-per-day down 3% in 2025 on pricing pressure. International (~24%, $2.75B) is the bright spot, with Adjusted EBITDA up 80% YoY to $290M in 2025 on lower per-unit fleet costs. By brand in Q1 2026: Avis $1.43B, Budget $0.95B, Other/Zipcar $0.15B. The competitive moat rests on brand recognition, airport concession footprint, and corporate/travel distribution, which are durable but operate within a capital-intensive, price-competitive commodity industry with thin structural margins.

The company is effectively controlled by SRS Investment Management (Karthik Sarma), which owns more than 45% and has installed its own management team: CEO Brian J. Choi and Executive Chairman Jagdeep Pahwa, both SRS-affiliated, with CFO Daniel Cunha. Longtime CEO Joseph Ferraro moved to a Board Advisor role effective June 30, 2025. The aggressive fleet impairments, novel financing structures, and rightsizing bear the fingerprints of an incoming SRS-directed turnaround team.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory captures a swing from record profitability to deep losses driven almost entirely by the fleet-depreciation cycle:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $11.99B | $12.01B | $11.79B | $11.65B |
| Net income | +$2.76B | +$1.63B | -$1.82B | -$0.89B |
| Vehicle D&A | $0.83B | $1.74B | $2.98B | $3.02B |
| Impairments | — | — | $2.5B | $0.52B |
| Stockholders' equity | -$0.70B | -$0.35B | -$2.33B | -$3.13B |

The mechanism is straightforward. During 2021–22, elevated used-car prices produced low depreciation and large gains on vehicle sales, temporarily inflating profits industry-wide. As used-car prices normalized, depreciation reverted higher and gains disappeared. Layered on top, the SRS team accelerated fleet rotation and recognized the impairments (the 2025 charge an EV write-down after the July 2025 OBBBA legislation eliminated/phased out EV tax credits, prompting Avis to shorten EV useful lives). With 84% of the fleet in risk vehicles, residual-value swings hit earnings directly.

Revenue is flat-to-declining (-1% in 2025), and the top-line problem is pricing, not demand — Americas revenue-per-day fell 3% while volume held roughly stable. That is the harder problem to fix, because it reflects competitive intensity rather than a cyclical demand trough.

The balance sheet is the central concern. Negative equity, a 0.05 Altman Z-Score, and a current ratio of 0.72 all signal distress. Liquidity is thin at ~$818M (2025) and ~$915M (Q1 2026). Operating cash flow of $3.3B in 2025 looks healthy on the surface but is almost entirely the non-cash D&A add-back; the business must continually consume cash to replace fleet. The company reported compliance with all covenants as of Q1 2026, but the equity value ultimately rests on the fleet's realizable value exceeding the ~$19B of vehicle-backed debt while the used-car market stays firm.

Q1 2026, the seasonally weakest quarter, showed revenue of $2.53B (+4% YoY on +3% RevPerDay), but the Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to -$113M from -$93M. Under HLBV accounting tied to the Interpace Ventures VIE, +$49M of Q1 income was allocated away from Avis shareholders to non-controlling interest holders. Q3 is historically the profit driver, so Q2 (due July 28, 2026) will be a bridge quarter rather than a verdict.

The share count is unusually low (~35M weighted shares) after years of aggressive buybacks (treasury stock -$10.75B), which amplifies EPS volatility and, combined with SRS's stake, drives the extreme float dynamics behind the squeeze.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here in the conventional sense. Revenue has been flat-to-declining for three consecutive years, and the pressure point is pricing rather than volume — a competitive rather than cyclical problem. International momentum (+80% Adjusted EBITDA in 2025) is the one genuine bright spot, but at ~24% of revenue it cannot carry the consolidated picture while Americas pricing erodes.

Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum. Current-year EPS consensus fell 5.3% over 90 days (to $3.41), next-quarter estimates fell 4.8% over 90 days, and next-year estimates fell 6.8% over the trailing 30 days (to $7.82). The current-quarter revision was marginally positive (+2.0% over 30 days), but the broader trend is downward. The earnings-surprise record reinforces low visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including a -665.9% surprise in Q4 2025 and a -94.6% surprise in Q2 2025. The one large beat (Q3 2025, +25.8%) reflected the seasonal profit concentration, not durable improvement.

The forward EPS estimates ($3.41 CY, $7.82 next year) imply the Street expects a return to GAAP profitability, which would require the fleet-depreciation reset to have created a cleaner baseline — the incoming-management "kitchen-sink" thesis. This is plausible but unproven, and it hinges on used-car prices cooperating. It does not constitute a growth story; it is a normalization bet layered on a distressed capital structure.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is meaningless (negative EPS of -$25.26), so valuation rests on forward estimates and, more instructively, on the explicit gap between price and fundamental fair value. Goldman Sachs raised its target to $95 from $85 in July 2026, a raise that still leaves the target ~40% below the market price. The analyst consensus median is $148.00, with the high target at $170 and low at $95. When the most recent analyst action raises a target and it still sits well below the current price, the market price carries a residual speculative premium over any reasonable estimate of intrinsic value.

The peer set (Industrials, market-cap proximity) trades at a median P/E of 43.5, but the comparison is not useful — those are asset-light or growth names (Paylocity 23.2x, GXO 43.5x, Construction Partners 46.4x, AAR 28.2x), not leveraged fleet operators with negative equity. The more relevant comparable is peer Hertz, which cratered on a mid-2026 stock offering and lowered guidance, confirming a sector-wide margin reset that is repricing fleet-heavy, negative-equity balance sheets downward across the industry.

A compressed or negative multiple that reflects structural impairment is a value trap, not a buy signal. Avis's "cheapness" on some metrics reflects genuine distress. Even applying the bullish case — that the Pentwater settlement clears court approval and the fleet-depreciation reset produces the ~$7.82 forward EPS the Street models — a normalized multiple on a distressed, cyclical fleet operator would not support $159. On $7.82 next-year EPS at a distressed-appropriate 12–14x, fair value lands in the $94–$109 range, precisely where Goldman and the low end of consensus sit. The valuation score is low because the stock is stretched relative to a repaired-balance-sheet scenario, not merely optically expensive.

## Competitive Landscape

The US car-rental market is a rational oligopoly (Enterprise private, Hertz, Avis), but it remains price-competitive and fleet-cost-driven, with thin structural margins. Avis's relative edge is its International franchise momentum and brand strength; its relative weakness is the most leveraged, deepest-negative-equity balance sheet among the peer set. Hertz's mid-2026 distress demonstrates the industry-wide nature of the margin reset following the used-car normalization.

Structural threats cited in the 10-K include ride-hailing, car-sharing, and OEM-direct mobility. The EV policy reversal (OBBBA) is a shared headwind that created a fleet/consumer-preference/policy mismatch, which drove the 2025 impairment. Avis's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating: Americas pricing is eroding (a competitive tell), while International outperforms. The trajectory does not warrant a high score, but the brand and airport-concession footprint prevent it from being a clear decliner.

## Risk Assessment

**Insolvency-adjacent balance sheet.** Negative equity and a 0.05 Altman Z-Score mean the equity is a residual claim on fleet value exceeding ~$19B of vehicle-backed debt. A used-car price decline could trigger ABS collateral shortfalls and forced early repayment or vehicle sales.

**Fleet cost / residual-value risk.** The company's single largest expense, with 84% risk vehicles exposed to used-car prices, rates, tariffs, and EV policy.

**Refinancing and rate risk.** Virtually all vehicle-program debt and some corporate debt matures within five years; ~$5.9B is unhedged variable-rate. Recent notes carry high coupons (8.375% 2032 notes; 7–8.25% notes), and vehicle interest ran ~9% of revenue in Q1 2026.

**Concentrated control and change-of-control triggers.** SRS owns >45%; crossing 50% could trigger a 101% change-of-control offer on senior notes if ratings are downgraded. A take-private is a live possibility given the stake.

**Dilution risk.** A new ATM equity program (up to 5M shares, established March 2026) signals management may issue equity — rational given negative book value, but dilutive.

**Financial engineering.** The Interpace Ventures VIE (tax-credit monetization, $965M ABS, redeemable NCI of $120M) and HLBV accounting reallocated income away from Avis shareholders. Escalating structural complexity around a distressed capital base is itself a risk signal.

**Offsetting positive.** The Pentwater Section 16(b) settlement, if court-approved, is a cash windfall that flows directly to the company, materially improving liquidity and reducing negative equity. This is the one asymmetric positive and the reason the risk score is not at the floor.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is unusually stressed. Near-term ATM IV is 80.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 111.6%, so IV screens "cheap" on a spread basis, but the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 80.4% > long-term 68.5%), which the market uses to price imminent stress — consistent with the July 28 earnings date and the pending settlement approval. The put/call skew is elevated (+4.5%), put/call volume ratio is 1.22, and put/call OI ratio is 1.44, reflecting net bearish/hedging demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.9%. The delta snapshot shows the P/C OI ratio rising from 1.01 to 1.44 alongside a 14.3% price decline. The corrupted ATM IV reading (1469.2%) in the delta feed is a thin-options artifact and non-informative. The options market corroborates the bearish fundamental view but also prices meaningful two-sided event risk into late July.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($200+)
The Pentwater settlement clears SDNY approval, injecting cash equal to a full year of Adjusted EBITDA and repairing liquidity/negative equity. The SRS management team's two rounds of fleet impairments prove to have been a "kitchen-sink" reset that establishes a clean forward-earnings baseline; Q3 seasonal strength plus International momentum drive a return to GAAP profitability toward the ~$7.82 forward EPS the Street models. The 55.8% short interest and thin SRS-controlled float re-ignite a squeeze, and/or SRS takes the company private at a premium. In this scenario the stock trades on recovery optionality rather than fair value.

### Bear Case ($75–$95)
Used-car prices soften, pressuring residual values on the 84%-risk-vehicle fleet, triggering further impairments and ABS collateral stress. The meme premium fully unwinds toward Goldman's $95 fundamental target and below, as the market re-anchors to negative book equity and deteriorating estimate revisions. The ATM equity program is activated, diluting the thin share base. Refinancing occurs at elevated coupons, compressing already-thin margins. A -50%+ drawdown from $159 to the low end of consensus is the fundamental gravity.

### Base Case
The stock drifts toward the analyst consensus band of $95–$148 as the squeeze premium erodes and fundamentals reassert, partially cushioned if the Pentwater cash arrives. This implies meaningful downside from the current price even in a benign scenario, which is why the fundamentals do not support ownership.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Two dated events: Q2 2026 earnings on July 28, 2026 (a seasonally weak quarter with negative estimate momentum and a 3-of-4 miss record), and SDNY approval/timing of the Pentwater settlement. The fundamental catalyst — reversion toward Goldman's $95 target — is real but lacks a single dated trigger.

**Timing window.** The July 28 print is imminent, but its downside is partially offset by the pending settlement, a positive catalyst that could arrive in the same window. This two-sided setup within a compressed timeframe is unfavorable for a short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 55.8% of float with 8.0 days to cover — extreme. Combined with SRS's >45% stake creating an ultra-thin tradable float, this is the definitional squeeze setup. The March–April 2026 episode already demonstrated the stock can move from ~$100 to over $800 intraday on this structure.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms the bearish thesis (elevated put skew, put-heavy OI, backwardation pricing near-term stress), but that same crowding raises borrow cost and squeeze fragility. The near-term ±10.9% implied move plus the pending positive settlement catalyst mean any short carries asymmetric upside risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Fundamental downside to ~$95 is ~40%. But the upside risk in a squeeze or take-private is unbounded on this float structure, and the pending $650M cash windfall is a genuine positive. Risk/reward is well below the 2:1 threshold required for a short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis is correct — the stock trades ~40% above fundamental value on Goldman's target — but 55.8% short interest, an SRS-controlled thin float with demonstrated squeeze history, and a pending positive settlement catalyst make the mechanics prohibitive. Avoid on the long side; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with no ownership case on either side.

**Re-entry criteria (for reconsideration as a long).** (1) The stock re-rates down toward the $94–$109 fundamental band, closing the meme premium and offering a margin of safety on a repaired balance sheet; (2) the Pentwater settlement receives final court approval AND management applies proceeds to corporate debt reduction rather than fleet, materially reducing negative equity; (3) two consecutive quarters of GAAP profitability confirm the fleet-depreciation reset produced a durable baseline, with used-car prices stable; (4) Americas revenue-per-day inflects positive, signaling the pricing problem is resolving rather than structural.

**Conditions that would deepen the AVOID.** Activation of the ATM equity program (dilution of the thin float); a further fleet impairment on softening used-car prices; a covenant/ABS collateral event; or SDNY rejection of the Pentwater settlement.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — negative equity, residual-value exposure, refinancing at elevated coupons, and industry-wide margin reset — are likely to persist through at least the 2026–2027 fleet-cost normalization cycle. The residual squeeze premium could unwind on any timeframe. Next natural reassessment: Q2 2026 earnings (July 28, 2026) and the SDNY settlement docket.

## Conclusion

Avis Budget trades at $159.48 on a foundation that its own fundamentals cannot support. Goldman Sachs, in the act of raising its target, still values the equity at $95, roughly 40% below spot, and consensus sits below the market price. Behind the price is a company with negative equity, a 0.05 Altman Z-Score, ~$25B of debt collateralized by a residual-value-exposed fleet, three losing years, and negative estimate-revision momentum. The Pentwater settlement is a genuine and unusual positive that improves liquidity if approved, but it does not close the gap between price and intrinsic value, nor does it repair a business model that consumes cash and depends on used-car prices holding firm.

The score trajectory has been declining (4.0 → 2.7) across four consecutive AVOID calls, and the new data — deepening negative equity in Q1, widening seasonal EBITDA loss, negative revisions, and a price still detached from a below-spot fundamental target — confirms rather than reverses that trend. The Pentwater windfall is the one factor that arrests further deterioration in the risk and management dimensions, which is why the overall score holds at 3.0 rather than declining further. The recommendation is **AVOID**. It is not an actionable short: 55.8% short interest, an SRS-controlled thin float with a demonstrated squeeze history, and a pending positive settlement catalyst make the mechanics prohibitive. The right posture is to stay off both sides until the meme premium clears and a repaired balance sheet is confirmed.
