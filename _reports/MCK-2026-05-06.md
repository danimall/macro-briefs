## Executive Summary

McKesson is the largest pharmaceutical distributor in the United States, generating $359B in FY2025 revenue and approximately $5.2B in free cash flow. The company is in the middle of a multi-year strategic transformation: separating its Medical-Surgical Solutions (MMS) segment, building a differentiated oncology/specialty platform, and compounding EPS growth at ~15% annually through aggressive share repurchases. At approximately $744 per share, MCK trades at approximately 19x FY2026 guided EPS of $39, broadly in line with direct peers Cencora and Cardinal Health on a forward basis.

Three concrete catalysts support a BUY recommendation: (1) the MMS separation — validated by Apollo's April 2026 investment valuing the unit at approximately $13B enterprise value — provides a potential value-unlocking event as management pursues a planned IPO (timeline not yet specified); (2) the oncology ecosystem buildout (Core Ventures + PRISM Vision acquisitions) is producing 37% segment revenue growth with higher margins than the core distribution business; and (3) buyback-driven share count reduction of ~4% annually mechanically supports EPS growth independent of operating performance. The primary risks are customer concentration (CVS = 24% of revenue), incremental opioid liability beyond the approximately $5.7B currently accrued, and GLP-1 revenue deceleration.

## Company Overview

McKesson operates as a diversified healthcare services company across four segments following its Q2 FY2026 reorganization:

- **North American Pharmaceutical** (~$305B revenue, FY2025): Core wholesale distribution to pharmacies and healthcare providers
- **Oncology & Multispecialty** (~$37B revenue): Practice management, technology, and clinical support for specialty practices — highest strategic priority
- **Prescription Technology Solutions (RxTS)** (~$5.2B revenue): Technology platform connecting healthcare ecosystem participants — highest margins at 17-18%
- **Medical-Surgical Solutions (MMS)** (~$11.4B revenue): Supply distribution to physician offices and post-acute facilities — planned for IPO separation

The U.S. pharmaceutical distribution industry is an oligopoly: McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health collectively handle ~90% of all U.S. pharmaceutical volume. This structural concentration creates durable competitive positioning but limits organic pricing power.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth Acceleration:**
Revenue grew from $276.7B (FY2023) to $359.1B (FY2025) — a two-year CAGR of 14%. Q3 FY2026 reported $106.2B (+11.4% YoY), demonstrating sustained momentum. Growth drivers include GLP-1 volume surge, specialty drug proliferation, and onboarding of a new strategic distribution partner.

**Profitability Structure:**
Operating margins are structurally thin (1.2% GAAP in FY2025), inherent to the distribution model. The critical metric is adjusted operating profit, which grew 15% to $5.6B in FY2025. Gross margin compression (5.0% in FY2022 to 3.7% in FY2025) reflects the mix shift toward higher-volume, lower-margin pharma distribution rather than deteriorating unit economics. Absolute gross profit dollars continue growing ($13.3B, FY2025).

**Cash Flow Generation:**
Free cash flow of approximately $5.2B (FY2025) on approximately $0.9B total capex (including capitalized software) demonstrates the capital-light nature of the model. The negative working capital float ($55.3B payables vs. $25.6B receivables) provides ~$7B in interest-free supplier financing. Operating cash flow of $6.1B covers the estimated $776M near-term annual opioid payment obligation (as of March 31, 2025) with substantial excess.

**Capital Allocation:**
Share count has declined from 154M (FY2022) to 128M (FY2025) — a 17% reduction. The $7.5B remaining buyback authorization provides approximately two additional years of repurchases at current pace ($3.1B/year). At ~$744/share, each $1B in buybacks retires approximately 1.0% of float. Total shareholder returns (buybacks + dividends) were $3.5B in FY2025 ($3.1B repurchases + $345M dividends). Combined organic profit growth (~8%) plus buyback yield (~4%) produces structural mid-teens EPS growth.

**Balance Sheet (as of Q3 FY2026, December 31, 2025):**
Total debt of $6.6B against cash of $3.0B and operating cash flow of ~$6B. Net debt of approximately $3.6B is manageable at less than 1x OCF. Negative stockholders' equity (-$1.3B) reflects cumulative buybacks exceeding retained earnings — a known feature of the capital return strategy, not financial distress. Credit quality is supported by strong interest coverage, consistent free cash flow generation, investment-grade ratings, and manageable debt maturities.

**FY2026 Guidance (raised in Q3):**
- Adjusted EPS: $38.80-$39.20 (midpoint $39.00, +18% YoY vs. $33.05 in FY2025)
- Q3 YTD adjusted EPS of $27.46 implies FQ4 adjusted EPS of approximately $11.34-$11.74, broadly consistent with consensus of ~$11.57

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Growth Drivers (FY2026-FY2028):**
1. **Oncology ecosystem expansion** — 37% revenue growth in Q3 FY2026, driven by Core Ventures integration and expanding specialty practice management footprint. Adjusted margins of 2.5-2.8% are roughly double the core pharma distribution margin.
2. **Specialty/biosimilar drug proliferation** — MCK positioned at the intersection of the highest-growth pharmaceutical categories (oncology, GLP-1, immunology)
3. **RxTS technology platform** — 17-18% margins with recurring revenue characteristics from electronic prior authorization, prescription price transparency
4. **Buyback accretion** — 3-4% annual share count reduction provides mechanical EPS lift

**Consensus estimates:** FY2027E EPS $44.11 (+13% vs. FY2026), supported by continued specialty growth and buyback math. Estimate revisions over 90 days are modestly positive (+0.9% current year, +0.7% next year), though 30-day revisions are slightly negative — not accelerating but broadly stable.

**Long-term Secular Tailwinds:**
- Aging U.S. population driving drug utilization
- Specialty drug spending expected to reach 50%+ of total pharma spend
- Oncology remains the largest therapeutic area by spending growth
- Biosimilar adoption creates distribution complexity that favors scale players

## Competitive Landscape

**Oligopoly Moat:** The Big 3 distribution model has existed for decades because scale economics are insurmountable. New entrants would need to replicate the logistics infrastructure, manufacturer relationships, and technology systems that MCK has built over 190+ years. Amazon's pharmaceutical ambitions have been contained to direct-to-consumer models that complement rather than displace wholesale distribution.

**Differentiation Through Oncology/Specialty:** While Cardinal and Cencora compete in wholesale, MCK's oncology ecosystem (US Oncology Network, Core Ventures, iKnowMed EHR, Ontada data platform) creates switching costs that don't exist in commodity distribution. Specialty practice management is a higher-margin, recurring-revenue business that deepens the competitive moat.

**Technology Platform:** RxTS's network effects (connecting pharmacies, PBMs, manufacturers, and patients) provide a durable competitive advantage that neither CAH nor COR have replicated at scale.

**Market share trajectory:** Stable to gaining. The addition of a new strategic distribution partner in FY2025 demonstrates continued ability to win large accounts.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Customer Concentration — HIGH SEVERITY, PARTIALLY PRICED**
CVS Health = 24% of revenue. Top 10 = 72%. Loss of CVS would be catastrophic (though contract renewals have historically proceeded without incident). Rite Aid's bankruptcy produced $725M in charges in FY2024, though subsequent periods have seen partial recoveries ($206M credit in FY2025, $160M credit in Q3 FY2026) — customer credit risk in retail pharmacy is real and non-trivial.

**2. Opioid Liability — MODERATE SEVERITY, PARTIALLY PRICED**
The opioid-related litigation liability stood at approximately $5.7B as of December 31, 2025 (Q3 FY2026), down from $6.4B at FY2025 year-end due to payments made. MCK explicitly states it "cannot estimate the upper bound" of potential additional liabilities. At estimated annual payments of ~$776M (as of March 31, 2025, representing payments expected within the following 12 months), the known liability appears manageable relative to ~$6B annual OCF. Incremental claims represent the tail risk.

**3. GLP-1 Revenue Concentration — MODERATE SEVERITY, NOT FULLY PRICED**
A meaningful portion of recent revenue acceleration comes from high-priced GLP-1 drugs. Distribution margins on these are thin, but absolute dollar contribution matters for fixed-cost leverage. Volume or pricing moderation in GLP-1s would slow revenue growth, though oncology/specialty diversification partially offsets.

**4. IRA Drug Pricing Impact — LOW-TO-MODERATE SEVERITY, TIMELINE UNCERTAIN**
Medicare drug price negotiations will reduce prices on selected medications, potentially reducing MCK's inflation-linked compensation from manufacturers. Implementation is gradual (2026-2029+) and the company has time to adjust contract structures.

**5. MMS Separation Execution — MODERATE SEVERITY, TIME-BOUND**
Management distraction, potential dissynergies, and $650-700M in restructuring charges through FY2028. Apollo's involvement provides a financial anchor and discipline to the process.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility appeared elevated relative to historical volatility, with steep backwardation indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term event — likely the upcoming FQ4 earnings report. Elevated put/call activity and put skew suggest heightened downside hedging demand.

This diverges from the fundamental thesis: four consecutive quarterly beats, raised guidance, and stable estimates suggest the event risk is manageable. The options market may be pricing broader healthcare policy uncertainty (tariffs on medical supplies, IRA implementation) or MCK-specific concerns about GLP-1 deceleration. Longer-dated implied volatility closer to historical volatility suggests the market views current stress as temporary and event-specific rather than structural.

## Conclusion

MCK offers structural mid-teens EPS growth driven by buybacks and oncology expansion, a potential value-unlocking catalyst in the MMS separation (validated at ~$13B enterprise value by Apollo's investment), and a valuation that does not fully reflect its superior growth profile relative to direct peers. The stock's ~25% pullback from its March 2026 high reflects macro volatility and sector rotation rather than fundamental deterioration — estimates are broadly stable, guidance was raised in Q3, and the Apollo MMS transaction validates meaningful asset value.

The primary risks (CVS concentration, opioid tail liability, GLP-1 deceleration) are real but either adequately reserved or already partially reflected in the current multiple. Near-term options pricing indicates elevated event uncertainty around the upcoming earnings report, which may create entry volatility. The approximately 34% upside to analyst median target ($1,000), supported by concrete math (22-24x $44 FY2027E EPS), justifies a BUY recommendation with 2-3% portfolio sizing.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The original valuation framework contained material errors: MMS was valued at $9.6B (correct figure per McKesson's announcement is ~$13B EV); the peer P/E "30% discount" claim is not supported on a consistent forward basis (forward P/Es are approximately comparable across the Big 3); and the sum-of-the-parts methodology was flawed. The revised analysis supports the BUY thesis primarily on growth-adjusted valuation and catalyst optionality rather than a definitive peer-discount argument.
