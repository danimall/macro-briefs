## Executive Summary

Nasdaq is now a subscription software and index-licensing business that owns exchanges. Only 22.6% of first-half 2026 net revenue came from Market Services; the remaining 77.4% is index licensing, listings, market data, and enterprise software sold to banks, regulators, and corporates. That transformation is working: first-half net revenue of $2,908M grew 14.4%, GAAP operating margin expanded from 43.8% to 47.1%, annualized recurring revenue reached $3,258M (+12% organic), and Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $1.07 beat consensus by 9.2%, the fourth consecutive beat. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 5.2% over 90 days to $4.14, and next year to $4.65.

The problem with the setup is price, not performance. The stock closed the June quarter at $78.82 and now sits at $94.56, up 20% in five weeks, 7.1% below the 52-week high and 23.5% above the June low. At $94.56 the shares trade at 22.8x current-year and 20.3x next-year consensus, with an implied PEG of roughly 1.9 on 12.3% expected EPS growth. Trailing GAAP P/E is 27.5x (TTM GAAP diluted EPS of approximately $3.44; the $3.09 in the data feed is the FY2025 figure, and the "forward P/E" field duplicates the trailing value and should be ignored). Against a peer median trailing P/E of 31.7x that looks like a discount, but the median is set by pure data franchises (MSCI 34.0x, Moody's 31.7x) while the exchange comparables trade at 25.3x (CBOE) and 20.2x (ICE). Nasdaq is a hybrid and is priced like one.

Two things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, the composition of the margin expansion: Capital Access Platforms contributed 60% of the consolidated increase in GAAP operating income on 52% of the revenue increase, driven by Index, where tracked ETP AUM rose $369B year over year — $260B of that (70.5%) from market appreciation and $109B from net inflows. A 20–25% drawdown in large-cap U.S. technology would run that operating leverage in reverse on roughly 18% of net revenue at the company's highest incremental margin. Second, two unresolved binaries: the European Commission's formal antitrust proceeding opened in November 2025 (no provision taken; statutory ceiling of 10% of worldwide turnover would be ~$826M against FY2025 total revenue), and the SEC access-fee cap under the Rule 610(c) amendments with a compliance date reported around November 2026, which would compress the gross fee/rebate spread in U.S. cash equities precisely where Nasdaq has been gaining matched share. Neither is priced; neither is quantifiable today.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating momentum is genuine and reconcilable across bases.** First-half GAAP operating income of $1,369M grew 22.7% on 14.4% net revenue growth. Non-GAAP operating income of $1,658M gave a 57% margin against 55%. First-half GAAP diluted EPS was $1.80 (from $1.46); non-GAAP $2.03 (from $1.64). The Q2 divergence — GAAP EPS +14% versus non-GAAP +25% — is explained rather than engineered: Q2 2025 GAAP net income included a $27M one-time tax benefit related to payments to former Adenza employees that Nasdaq excluded from its 2025 non-GAAP figure. Adjusting the prior-year GAAP base for that item gives roughly 19% growth, consistent with pretax growth of 19.2% ($653M versus $548M). Underlying growth sits in the high teens to low twenties.

**One correction to the trailing record matters.** FY2025 net income grew 60.1% while pretax income grew 48.0%, because the effective tax rate fell from 23.0% to 16.7%. Roughly $136M — about $0.24 per diluted share — of the FY2025 gain came from the rate, not operations. Management guides the 2026 non-GAAP rate to 22.5%–24.5%, and the Q2 2026 GAAP rate was already 22.4% versus 17.5%. Any screen ranking Nasdaq on trailing GAAP earnings growth is capturing a non-repeating item.

**Balance sheet: investment grade, with an accounting artifact that misleads screens.** Total debt of $8,761M at 6/30/26 is down $1,693M (16.2%) from the post-Adenza peak of $10,454M. Weighted average interest rate is approximately 3.7%, maturities ladder from 2028 to 2063, and the only short-term debt is $269M of commercial paper; the $431M 2026 notes were retired at their June 30 maturity. The June 2026 revolver amendment raised the facility from $1.25B to $1.50B and extended it to June 30, 2031, undrawn, with a single maximum-leverage covenant in compliance. Against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $3,590M, gross leverage is 2.44x and net leverage 2.23x; on a stricter annualized GAAP basis, gross leverage is 2.58x. Interest coverage runs near 10x on ~$340M of annual interest.

The screening Altman Z-Score of approximately 1.5 is not informative here. The balance sheet grosses up member default funds and margin deposits identically on both sides ($2,323M at 6/30/26), inflating assets and current liabilities without economic leverage; the sales/assets term uses gross revenue of which roughly three-eighths is pass-through; and $20.5B of goodwill and intangibles crush the market-equity and retained-earnings terms. Reported working capital of $28M is the same artifact. The economically relevant measures — 2.2–2.6x net leverage, ~10x coverage, no maturity wall — describe a comfortably investment-grade issuer.

The real balance-sheet exposure is different: tangible equity is approximately negative $8,475M ($11,993M equity less $14,245M goodwill and $6,223M intangibles), and goodwill plus intangibles are 74.9% of total assets. Remaining acquired-intangible amortization totals $5,051M, of which $3,162M is scheduled for 2031 and beyond. Equity value rests on FinTech growth persisting for a decade. With FinTech ARR compounding at 15.7% (organic), there is no visible impairment trigger; goodwill declined only $126M in the first half, entirely from FX translation.

**The one item deserving genuine scrutiny is first-half cash conversion.** Operating cash flow was $1,400M versus $1,409M — flat — while net income rose 21.2%. Within the half, the "Section 31 fees payable to SEC" balance went from $0 at 12/31/25 to $313M at 6/30/26 (money collected and owed to the SEC, which reverses on remittance), receivables consumed $248M off the largest revenue quarter in company history, accrued personnel costs fell $121M on bonus payment, deferred revenue added $153M, and cash taxes rose to $284M from $176M. Removing only the identified Section 31 build as a sensitivity gives normalized first-half operating cash flow near $1,087M and free cash flow near $950M, against $1,301M in the prior-year half. On that normalization, underlying half-year cash generation declined.

Timing is the most likely explanation — receivables build behind record revenue, Q1 bonuses reflecting strong 2025 results, and cash taxes normalizing off an abnormally low 2025 rate. The alternative, that revenue recognition is running ahead of collections in the software businesses, cannot be distinguished from a single half-year, and FY2025 full-year conversion was excellent (free cash flow of $1,989M, 1.11x net income, 37.9% of net revenue). This should be re-tested against the FY2026 cash flow statement rather than annualized now. It is the single most important unresolved item in the financials and is the reason financial health scores near 6.8 rather than above 7.

**Capital returns.** The first half saw 10,392,733 shares repurchased at an average $86.91 for $903M — 8.1% below the current price — plus $327M of dividends, with a further $250M accelerated share repurchase launched in July. Authorization was raised to $3.0B in February; $2.5B remained at 6/30/26. The quarterly dividend rose 14.8% to $0.31, an annualized $1.24 for a 1.33% yield and roughly a 29% payout against FY2026E non-GAAP EPS. Share count fell 1.39% net against 1.82% gross repurchased, implying ~0.43pp of share-based compensation issuance. Combined dividends and buybacks of $2.4–2.5B equal 4.6%–4.8% of market capitalization, modestly above estimated FY2026 free cash flow of $2.1–2.3B, with the excess funded from balance-sheet capacity while leverage still falls.

## Growth Analysis

Net revenue growth of 14.4% decomposes into three quite different streams.

**FinTech (+16.3%) is the highest-quality piece.** ARR of $1,870M grew 15.7%, SaaS revenue grew 15% organic, and remaining performance obligations of $3,614M — covering only contracts with original expected duration above one year — equal 1.52x combined FinTech and Workflow & Insights ARR, roughly 18 months of contracted, unrecognized multi-year revenue. Commercial evidence supports the number: Verafin signed 11 enterprise deals year to date (through early Q3), exceeding all of 2025, with 750 clients using the Agentic Workforce; Calypso is live in more than 70 countries; cross-sells remained above 15% of the FinTech pipeline with 7 cross-sells, 58 new clients, and 107 upsells in Q2. Segment margin declined 40bp to 46.4% because Nasdaq is funding agentic-AI product development, an acceptable trade if ARR keeps compounding mid-teens.

**Index (+38.4%) is the highest-margin piece and the most market-dependent.** Of the $369B AUM increase, $260B was appreciation. Q2 Index revenue also included a $6M one-time contract-modification benefit, which management strips to get 35% adjusted growth. Index ARR is only $87M against $271M of quarterly revenue, because AUM-linked contracts are excluded from Nasdaq's ARR definition, which is why ARR growth (+12% organic) trails Solutions revenue growth (+17%). Record $109B of trailing-twelve-month net inflows and new licensees (BlackRock's IQQ, State Street's QNDX) expand the durable base, though additional licensees plausibly arrive at lower blended fee rates with some cannibalization of the incumbent QQQ. No fee-rate disclosure exists to adjudicate that.

**Market Services (+11.2%) participated in a record volume environment without fully monetizing it.** Cash equity industry ADV rose 17.5% in the first half and options ADV 21.9%. Nasdaq's cash equities net revenue grew 18.7% to $160M with revenue capture per 1,000 matched shares improving 1.8%. But U.S. tape plan net revenue *fell* to $33M from $37M in Q2 and $66M from $70M in the half.

**Estimate revisions are positive but do not clear the inflection bar.** Current-year consensus rose 4.9% in 30 days and 5.2% in 90 days to $4.14; next year the same to $4.65. That is durable positive earnings momentum consistent with four straight beats, and it argues against reducing growth or valuation scores. It is nowhere near the +30% ninety-day revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection, so the associated aggressive-buy signal is not present here. Implied EPS growth from $4.14 to $4.65 is 12.3%.

Guidance is limited: Nasdaq guides operating expense and tax rate only. FY2026 non-GAAP operating expense was *raised* to $2.530–$2.570B (first-half actual $1,250M, implying a 2.4%–5.6% sequential-half step-up), and the tax rate was maintained at 22.5%–24.5%. Building from 12%–14% second-half net revenue growth gives FY2026E net revenue of $5,939M–$5,993M, non-GAAP operating income of $3,369M–$3,463M (56.7%–57.8% margin, consistent with the first half), and after ~$340M interest expense and a 23.5% tax rate, FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $4.10–$4.25 on ~566M shares. Consensus at $4.14 sits inside that range.

## Competitive Landscape

The share picture requires precision, because "Nasdaq is losing share" and "Nasdaq is gaining share" are both defensible depending on which book you look at.

| Cash equities | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | 1H26 | 1H25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Industry ADV (bn shares) | 20.2 | 18.4 | 20.1 | 17.1 |
| Nasdaq matched share | 14.7% | 13.9% | 14.9% | 14.2% |
| FINRA/Nasdaq TRF share | 46.4% | 47.7% | 46.0% | 47.9% |
| Total share | 61.1% | 61.6% | 60.9% | 62.1% |

On-exchange matched share improved 70–80bp with capture per 1,000 matched shares up 1.8%, a genuine reversal. Off-exchange, the FINRA/Nasdaq trade reporting facility lost 130bp in the quarter and 190bp in the half, and the consequence shows up directly: U.S. tape plan net revenue fell in absolute dollars during the strongest volume environment in the company's history. SIP revenue is allocated across venues partly on trades and volume attributed to them, including trades reported through a TRF, and the direction and magnitude of the two series line up. Alternative explanations — changes to SIP allocation formulas, or falling professional and non-professional subscriber counts — cannot be ruled out without verifying the allocation mechanics. The fact stands regardless of mechanism.

Options presents an unresolved capture question. U.S. equity options industry ADV rose 26.7% to 66.5M contracts in Q2 while Nasdaq's matched share slipped to 29.1% from 29.4%, implying matched volume growth of roughly 25%. U.S. Equity Derivatives net revenue grew only 8.2% to $123M, implying net capture per matched contract fell roughly 14%. Transaction rebates grew only 11.3% against gross Market Services revenue growth of 24.6%, which argues against rebate inflation as the cause. Management states index options revenue "more than doubled year over year for the fourth consecutive quarter," which if reported inside this line implies the multiply-listed book contracted. Classification of index options revenue, differing trading-day counts, and the presence of Nordic derivatives and clearing revenue in the same line all remain possible explanations. Internally, flow reshuffled across Nasdaq's six venues (PHLX matched share to 11.8% from 9.4%; Nasdaq Options Market to 2.6% from 4.7%), consistent with pricing-model changes rather than share won from Cboe or MIAX.

Listings mix deteriorated in composition even as proceeds set records. Operating-company IPOs on Nasdaq fell 51% to 41 in the half while SPAC IPOs rose 53% to 90 — SPACs are now 69% of Nasdaq's first-half IPOs. Nasdaq won 7 of the top 10 largest operating-company IPOs in Q2, including SpaceX, Cerebras, Quantinuum, and Parabilis Medicines, plus SK hynix in Q3. Because annual listing fees are market-cap tiered rather than proceeds-based, a barbell of a few megadeals plus a large SPAC cohort is lower-quality than a broad operating-company cohort; SPACs that fail to complete a combination liquidate. Of 421 net new listings year over year, 329 were ETPs; operating-company listings rose only 2.8% and Nordic/Baltic listings fell 3.4%.

On the SpaceX listing, Nasdaq's filings describe an "$86 billion raise" while contemporaneous coverage of the June 12 debut described $75 billion. The most plausible reconciliation is that press reported the base deal size at pricing while Nasdaq's figure reflects total proceeds after full exercise of the overallotment and/or a concurrent secondary, but this is a hypothesis, not verified, and the two figures should not be treated as interchangeable. It matters little to Nasdaq's economics; what matters more is that SpaceX fell roughly 35–40% from its post-IPO peak and more than 900 million shares unlocked on August 6, more than doubling the public float. A weak anchor megadeal is a headwind to the IPO window; a doubled float in one of the largest names on Nasdaq's own venue is plausibly supportive of cash equities and options volumes. These effects are not commensurable and should not be netted.

Index is where the competitive position is strongest and most singular: crossing $1 trillion of tracked ETP AUM with record inflows places Nasdaq alongside S&P DJI and MSCI in index economics, on an unusually concentrated franchise. Operating leverage is measurable — headcount rose 1.5% (9,630 versus 9,492) against 14.4% net revenue growth, lifting annualized net revenue per employee 12.7% to roughly $604K.

On the perpetual-derivatives question, management characterized present overlap as minimal and expressed willingness to partner or license.

## Risk Assessment

**European Commission formal antitrust proceeding (largest quantifiable tail).** Following a September 2024 unannounced inspection at Nasdaq Stockholm, the EC opened a formal investigation in November 2025 into whether Nasdaq and Deutsche Börse coordinated conduct in the listing, trading, and clearing of EEA financial derivatives. Nasdaq states the matter concerns a 1999 cooperative arrangement between Eurex and the Helsinki Stock Exchange (acquired via OMX in 2008) that fully ended before Nasdaq learned of the investigation. Accruals to date are $6M in Q2 and $12M in the half — legal fees, not fine provisions. EU law permits fines to 10% of worldwide turnover, ~$826M against FY2025 total revenue or ~$525M against net revenue; those are statutory ceilings, not expectations. Nasdaq's framing argues for a settlement well below any ceiling, but the EC opens formal proceedings selectively and the counterparty is a direct competitor in the same products. The absence of a provision is not evidence of low exposure.

**SEC access-fee cap (Rule 610(c) amendments), compliance date reported around November 2026.** This surfaced in the FY2025 10-K and could not be re-verified against a primary source; treat status as unconfirmed. If accurate, it compresses the gross fee/rebate spread in U.S. cash equities from late 2026 into 2027 — directly in the line where Nasdaq has been gaining matched share and improving capture. This is the largest identifiable 2027 revenue headwind and it is not in consensus in any visible form.

**Index beta concentration.** Roughly 18% of net revenue, at the company's highest incremental margin, is levered to Nasdaq-100 levels. In a large-cap technology drawdown, the reverse operating leverage is the single most probable path to a material earnings miss.

**Listing-standard regulatory footing.** The SEC approved Nasdaq's rule requiring delisting of companies below $5M market value of listed securities for 30 consecutive days with no cure period on July 28, 2026, then placed the rule on hold on July 31. Immediate revenue effect is small; the sequence signals contested footing for small-cap listing standards at a time when SPACs are 69% of Nasdaq's IPO cohort.

**Unverified items a reader should not treat as settled:** the ~$99M Consolidated Audit Trail receivable and the Eleventh Circuit vacatur of the CAT funding model (regulatory expense fell 34%–35% year over year, consistent but not confirmatory); the FY2025 non-GAAP operating income figure in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table, which did not tie to a GAAP reconciliation; and the geographic revenue shift (U.S. share of gross revenue falling from 81.1% to 73.6%), which is at least partly a Section 31 pass-through artifact — Q1 2026 brokerage/clearance/exchange fees were roughly $6M versus $274M a year earlier — and should not be read as counterparty diversification. Customer concentration disclosure is unchanged: no single customer at 10% or more of revenue.

**Clearing.** Nasdaq's own capital in the default waterfall is $20M junior and $24M senior against $428M of member default fund contributions and $7,457M of margin deposits, with 230% power of assessment. First-loss exposure is small; the exposure in a member default is reputational and regulatory. Nasdaq reduced this surface by exiting Nordic power futures — positions transferred in Q1 2026, trading and clearing ceased in June, producing an $88M incremental net gain in Q1 and a $20M intangible impairment in Q2.

**FX.** SEK net assets of $3,146M (including $2,362M of goodwill) mean a 10% adverse SEK move reduces other comprehensive income by roughly $315M; accumulated OCI loss already stands at $(1,874)M. This flows through equity, not earnings.

### Options Market Signal

Headline near-term IV of 35.1% against 27.5% one-year realized looks like priced stress, but the 35.1% sits on a one-day expiry (August 7) with a 1.4% implied move — same-day pinning mechanics rather than event risk. Stripping that tenor, the term structure is unremarkable: 26.1% at one month (*below* realized vol), 26.2% at three months, 27.9% at six months. Volume and open interest both tilt toward calls (put/call volume 0.42, open interest 0.72). The aggregate skew reading of 4.5% is labeled as elevated put demand, driven almost entirely by the one-day tenor at 10.4%; the one-month tenor reads 0.9% and the six-month reading is negative, which under the same convention is inconsistent with put accumulation. Short interest is 2.4% of float with 1.7 days to cover — no crowded bearish position. The options market prices no medium-term dislocation and no informed bearish positioning, and one-month vol at or below realized makes optionality inexpensive for anyone wanting to own upside into the Q3 print. This mildly supports the fundamental view but does not change it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($125, +32%).** FinTech ARR keeps compounding at 15%–16% organic and RPO of $3,614M converts on schedule; Verafin's agentic-AI modules and Calypso's international expansion (70+ countries, shared-infrastructure deals like the Georgian bank consortium) convert free users to paid at the pace management described; index AUM continues to grow with both inflows and market levels, and event options on the Nasdaq-100 launch in Q4 2026 add a proprietary product line; the access-fee cap slips or lands with less pricing impact than feared and the EC matter settles for a nine-figure sum absorbed in a quarter. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $4.85–$4.90 and the shares re-rate to 26x on a mix that is 80% recurring. Analyst high target is $135.

**Bear case ($76, −20%).** A 20%–25% drawdown in large-cap U.S. technology compresses tracked ETP AUM, and the same high incremental margin that drove 60% of the consolidated operating income increase runs in reverse on ~18% of net revenue. The access-fee cap takes effect in late 2026, compressing cash equities capture into 2027 just as TRF share erosion continues to drain tape revenue. The EC imposes a fine toward the upper end of expectations. Options capture continues to decline while volumes grow. First-half cash-conversion softness proves structural rather than timing in the FY2026 statement. FY2027 EPS lands near $4.10–$4.30 and the multiple compresses to 18x, taking the shares to the 52-week low area of $76.55.

**Base case ($104, +10%).** Net revenue grows 12%–14% in the second half, FY2026 non-GAAP EPS lands at $4.10–$4.25 in line with the $4.14 consensus, and FY2027 reaches consensus $4.65 on continued mid-teens FinTech ARR growth and high-single-digit index growth as appreciation contribution normalizes. Multiple holds near current levels: 22.4x × $4.65 = $104. The dividend and buyback add roughly 4.6% of annual shareholder yield with buybacks executed below prevailing prices, and leverage continues drifting toward 2.0x.

The scores reflect a business whose operations are performing better than they were a quarter ago against a price that has moved faster than the estimates. Financial health at 6.8 credits 2.2–2.6x net leverage, ~10x coverage, a 2031 undrawn revolver, and 1.11x FY2025 FCF conversion, discounted for negative tangible equity and the unresolved first-half cash-conversion question. Growth at 6.3 credits 14.4% net revenue growth and 12% organic ARR while marking down the ~70% appreciation share of index AUM growth, the reversing tax tailwind, and the 51% decline in operating-company IPOs. Competitive position at 6.4 nets improving matched share and capture, a $1.1T index franchise, and a Verafin consortium with real network effects against declining TRF share, tape revenue falling in absolute dollars, ~14% options capture erosion, and a SPAC-heavy listings cohort. Management at 6.5 credits $903M of buybacks at $86.91 (below today's price), a 14.8% dividend raise at a 29% payout, $1.7B of debt reduction, and a clean filing history against a combined chair/CEO role, an incentive plan with no return-on-capital metric despite ~$10.5B deployed on Adenza, and a 5% voting cap that insulates the board — including from the 10.4% economic holder, Investor AB, which added 56,782 shares at $85.98 in June (a 0.1% increment to a 58.4M-share position, constructive but small). Risk at 5.4 reflects two unquantified regulatory binaries and index-beta concentration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026), which delivers three specific tests — nine-month cash flow against the first-half conversion question, index revenue growth as the appreciation base laps, and any update on EU proceedings or the access-fee cap compliance date. The November 2026 access-fee compliance window is the second checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A price near $82–$85 — roughly 18.5x–19x FY2027E consensus $4.65 and near Investor AB's June purchase level and the company's own first-half repurchase average — with estimates unchanged or higher. That would restore roughly 25% upside to a $104 base case against a $76 bear.

3. Confirmation that the SEC access-fee cap is deferred beyond 2027 or lands with quantified impact below ~1% of net revenue, combined with FinTech ARR growth sustained at 15%+ organic.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**

2. FinTech organic ARR growth decelerating below 10% for two consecutive quarters, or SaaS revenue growth falling below 10% organic — the FinTech compounding story is what justifies both the peer-relative multiple and the $20.5B of goodwill and intangibles against negative tangible equity.

**Position guidance:** hold existing positions. New capital should wait for either the mid-$80s or the Q3 cash-flow confirmation. ## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Nasdaq is executing well. Net revenue grew 14.4% in the first half with GAAP operating margin up 330bp, ARR reached $3,258M at 12% organic growth, the Adenza restructuring closed with more than $160M of net expense synergies actioned, debt is down 16% from the post-acquisition peak, and the company has beaten consensus four quarters running while consensus estimates rose 5.2% over 90 days. Capital allocation has been sensible: $903M repurchased at $86.91, a 14.8% dividend increase at a 29% payout, and continued deleveraging.

The reason this is not a BUY is arithmetic rather than doubt about the business. At $94.56 the shares trade at 22.8x current-year and 20.3x next-year consensus, an implied PEG near 1.9, and a 4.0%–4.3% free-cash-flow yield, having advanced 20% from the June quarter-end while estimates moved 5%. A defensible base case of $104 offers 10% upside against a bear case near $76 that requires only a large-cap technology drawdown — which would compress the index revenue that produced 60% of the consolidated operating income increase — or an adverse resolution of the access-fee cap and EU antitrust matters, neither of which is provisioned or priced. The prior score trajectory (7.1 in February to 6.4 in July) reflected the same dynamic: a business improving in the recurring lines while the traded price absorbed the improvement and while two regulatory binaries stayed open. The current 6.2 reflects one further quarter of compressed asymmetry, not deterioration in the franchise.

Own it for the FinTech and index compounding. Add on the mid-$80s or on a Q3 cash-flow statement that resolves the conversion question.
