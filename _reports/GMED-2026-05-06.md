## Executive Summary

Globus Medical has transformed from a mid-cap spine pure-play into a leading global musculoskeletal company — and the second largest pure-play spine technology company — through two acquisitions (NuVasive in 2023, Nevro in 2025) executed with strong financial discipline. The company now generates $2.94B in revenue with a clean margin profile (cost of revenue dropped from 41.1% to 32.6% of sales as one-time inventory step-ups burned off), producing $589M in free cash flow (20% FCF margin) and enabling simultaneous debt paydown ($450M), share repurchases (~$300M), and further M&A.

At approximately 20-21x forward earnings on $4.47 FY2026E EPS (with 90-day upward revisions of 6.2% for the current year and 7.4% for next year), GMED trades at a meaningful discount to broader health care equipment peers. This discount partially reflects legitimate concerns: an open FDA warning letter on the ExcelsiusGPS robotic system, declining domestic robot placements, CEO transition, and controlled-company governance. However, Q4 base business acceleration to 10.6% growth, the margin normalization story, and management's 8-9% revenue / 11-13% EPS growth guidance for FY2026 suggest the market is overweighting near-term integration noise relative to the structural earnings power emerging from these deals.

The Nevro acquisition is value-accretive: a $117.7M bargain purchase gain (driven primarily by recognition of deferred tax assets including pre-acquisition NOL carryforwards) means GMED paid below the fair value of Nevro's identifiable net assets. However, Nevro contributed a net loss excluding the bargain gain during the post-acquisition period, so the operating economics of this acquisition remain a work-in-progress.

## Company Overview

Globus Medical develops and commercializes musculoskeletal solutions across two product categories:

**Musculoskeletal Solutions (~95% of revenue):** Spinal implants, biologics, orthopedic trauma fixation, hip/knee arthroplasty, spinal cord stimulation (SCS) therapy, and intraoperative neuromonitoring (IONM) services. This segment grew from a spine-focused business to a comprehensive musculoskeletal platform through NuVasive (spine) and Nevro (SCS/neuromodulation).

**Enabling Technologies (~5% of revenue):** The ExcelsiusGPS® robotic navigation platform, Excelsius3D™ intraoperative imaging, and the newly-launched ExcelsiusXR® extended reality headset. Though a small portion of revenue, this segment represents an important technology differentiator — robotics adoption in spine surgery remains in its early phases, and the installed base creates switching costs and recurring instrument revenue.

GMED operates across 65 countries with 6,000+ employees. The company is controlled by founder David C. Paul, who holds 66.1% of voting power through Class B shares.

2. NuVasive synergy capture continuing — Q4 acceleration suggests realization is underway
3. International expansion (Enabling Technologies grew internationally even as domestic declined)

**Medium-term growth drivers (2-4 years):**

2. SCS market share gains with an integrated spine+neuromodulation offering — GMED now combines its broad spine platform with Nevro's 10 kHz HFX SCS technology, creating a continuum-of-care positioning for chronic back pain patients

**Growth concerns:**

## Competitive Landscape

GMED is now among the top three spine companies globally (behind Medtronic and J&J/DePuy), having consolidated the space through NuVasive. This creates meaningful competitive advantages:

1. **Combined product portfolio:** One of the broadest spine implant lineups in the industry, enabling surgeons to standardize on one vendor
2. **Robotic differentiation:** ExcelsiusGPS is one of a few viable robotic spine platforms (alongside Medtronic Mazor and Stryker navigation). The installed base creates switching costs.
3. **SCS entry via Nevro:** GMED now combines its spine hardware platform with Nevro's 10 kHz HFX SCS technology, creating a continuum-of-care positioning. Medtronic also offers both spinal devices and neuromodulation products, so GMED is not unique in spanning these categories — though its integrated go-to-market approach with the combined NuVasive/Nevro sales force may offer differentiated commercial execution.

**Competitive threats:**
- Medtronic's vastly larger sales force, broader product portfolio, and existing neuromodulation franchise
- Stryker's navigation/Mako ecosystem expanding into spine
- The domestic robot placement decline suggests either market saturation of early adopters or competitive pressure
- Potential for POD-related pricing pressure and hospital GPO consolidation

GMED is gaining share through product breadth and an integrated technology ecosystem, but the Enabling Technologies domestic decline requires monitoring as a potential signal that robot adoption is stalling.

## Risk Assessment

**1. FDA Warning Letter (Material — Timeline Unknown):**
The July 2024 warning letter for ExcelsiusGPS complaint handling deficiencies remains open as of February 2026. The FDA did not identify safety concerns with the device's use or manufacturing-process issues, but an open warning letter may restrict new 510(k) submissions and creates reputational risk with hospital purchasing committees evaluating the robotic system. Resolution timeline is uncertain — typically 12-24 months for medtech warning letters.

**2. Controlled Company / Governance:**
David Paul's 66.1% voting control eliminates shareholder recourse on strategic decisions. The CEO transition (Scavilla → Pfeil) and a March 2026 board member resignation (the 8-K noted the resignation was not due to any disagreement with the company) suggest some organizational flux, though the board departure does not appear adversarial. Under the NYSE controlled-company exemption, committees including compensation and nominating/governance are not required to consist entirely of independent directors.

**3. Integration Execution:**
Two large acquisitions in 18 months ($2B+ in goodwill/intangibles total). Restructuring costs of $15M (2025) and $24M (2024) are modest but ongoing. The Q4 acceleration suggests integration is proceeding, but cultural integration and IT consolidation are multi-year processes. Nevro contributed a net loss excluding the bargain purchase gain during the post-acquisition period, indicating that SCS economics need further improvement.

**4. Enabling Technologies Domestic Decline:**
If robot placements are structurally peaking in the U.S. (vs. cyclically pausing), the company's primary technology differentiation erodes. Though Enabling Technologies represents only ~5% of total revenue, its strategic importance as a pull-through mechanism for implant sales far exceeds its direct revenue contribution. This is the most important metric to track in FY2026 quarterly results.

**5. Pimenta Litigation ($43.1M Accrual):**
Significant legal contingency that could expand. Details are limited in public filings.

## Conclusion

GMED warrants a BUY recommendation at ~$90 based on: (1) a forward P/E of approximately 20-21x that appears undervalued relative to the company's growth profile and cash generation; (2) Q4 base business acceleration suggesting synergy realization is underway; (3) ~5.9% FCF yield providing downside support and ongoing capital return capacity; (4) the Nevro bargain purchase creating a platform extension acquired below net asset value; and (5) positive 90-day estimate revisions (6-7%) confirming the earnings trajectory.

The primary risk is continued decline in domestic robot placements, which would undermine the technology differentiation thesis and signal that GMED's competitive moat in Enabling Technologies is narrowing. The FDA warning letter is a meaningful but likely resolvable overhang. At approximately 20-21x forward earnings for a company generating 8-9% revenue growth and 20% FCF margins, the risk/reward is attractive for a 12-18 month holding period.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to capital deployment funding (not fully funded by current-year operations), Nevro post-acquisition net loss (excluding bargain gain), Enabling Technologies' small revenue share (~5% vs. prior 15%), competitive positioning (Medtronic also offers spine + neuromodulation), and peer valuation discount methodology collectively reduce the strength of the quantitative support for the target price range, though the directional thesis based on margin normalization, cash generation, and estimate revisions remains intact.
