**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FedEx enters a new structural chapter. On June 1, 2026 the company completed the spin-off of FedEx Freight (NYSE: FDXF), retained a 19.9% stake, and simultaneously shifted its fiscal year-end to December 31. What remains is essentially a global express/parcel pure-play (Federal Express was ~87% of pre-spin revenue) plus FedEx Logistics, Office, and Dataworks, the FDXF stake, and a pending 37% minority co-investment in InPost. The trailing 17.3x P/E and the $88B consolidated revenue base both describe a conglomerate that no longer exists in that form, so the standard data pull understates the degree of transformation underway.

The investment case rests on one demonstrable engine: the DRIVE and Network 2.0 self-help programs are converting a flat-revenue freight recession into real earnings growth. Federal Express segment operating margin expanded to 7.4% in Q3 FY26 (from 6.7%) and to 7.0% across the first nine months (from 5.9%). Nine-month EPS rose 19% on revenue up only 6%, the company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (most recently by 25.7%), and management reaffirmed $1.0B in incremental structural cost reduction for FY26. Composite U.S. yields are up mid-single digits and international priority yields +11%, evidence that pricing discipline is holding even as mix shifts toward cheaper deferred services.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concrete. FedEx is simultaneously a plaintiff seeking IEEPA tariff refunds from CBP and a defendant in five class actions seeking tariff refunds from FedEx; management states the impact is "reasonably possibly material" but cannot estimate it. The Amazon disintermediation threat is structural and intensifying. Forward estimates are split: current-year revisions are positive (+6.3% over 90 days) but next-quarter (-12.6%) and next-year (-15.2%) are sharply negative, with part of the next-year cut reflecting the loss of Freight revenue rather than pure deterioration. The options term structure is in steep backwardation (near-term ATM IV 112.5% vs 36.4% six-month), pricing imminent stress around the first standalone earnings print on a disrupted calendar. On balance this is a growth-oriented hold: the self-help thesis is real and worth owning through the next print, but the binary legal exposure and decelerating forward estimates cap conviction.

## Company Overview

FedEx provides global transportation, e-commerce, and business services across 220+ countries. Following the June 1, 2026 separation, the reporting structure is:

- **Federal Express** — the dominant segment and the world's largest integrated air-ground express network plus a leading North American small-package ground carrier. Q3 FY26 segment revenue was $21.2B (+10% YoY), with U.S. ground revenue alone at $9.9B/quarter. This is now the company's core.
- **FedEx Freight (FDXF)** — North America's largest LTL carrier, spun off into a standalone S&P 500 constituent. FedEx retained 19.9%.
- **Corporate, Other & Eliminations** — FedEx Logistics, FedEx Office, and FedEx Dataworks (the digital/data-monetization arm).

The competitive moat is the integrated global air-ground network, the brand, and the capital barriers to entry (aircraft fleet, the Memphis World Hub, sortation infrastructure). The U.S. parcel duopoly with UPS is the structural advantage, now under pressure from Amazon's in-house network.

Post-spin, FedEx is more cyclically exposed: it has shed its highest-margin segment and become more directly correlated to e-commerce and global trade flows. The 10-K explicitly warns that both RemainCo and NewCo will be "smaller, less diversified… more vulnerable to changing macroeconomic conditions."

## Financial Analysis

**Consolidated trajectory.** FY2025 revenue was ~$88B with net income ~$4.09B, roughly flat-to-modestly-growing as the multi-year goods/freight recession weighed on volumes. The recent quarters show the self-help inflection:

- **Q3 FY26 (qtr ended 2/28/26):** revenue $24.0B (+8% YoY), operating income $1.35B (+4%), net income $1.06B (+16%), diluted EPS $4.41 (+17%).
- **9M FY26:** revenue $69.7B (+6%), operating income $3.9B (+14%), net income $2.84B (+16%), diluted EPS $11.91 (+19%).

The EPS growth running far above revenue growth reflects operating leverage from cost takeout, not top-line expansion.

**Segment margins tell the real story.** FedEx Freight margin appears to have collapsed to 0.4% in Q3 (from 12.5%), but this is largely an accounting artifact: ~$126M of spin-separation costs were loaded into Freight in Q3, plus 1,500+ Federal Express employees were transferred into Freight ahead of the separation and standalone IT was built out. The underlying LTL economics are mid-teens margins historically — relevant both to valuing the retained 19.9% stake and to understanding why management wanted the separation.

**Cost program.** Network 2.0 is live in ~390 U.S./Canada locations (Canada complete; U.S. targeted for end of calendar 2027). A ~1,400-role Europe workforce reduction is substantially complete (~$150M annualized). A new January 2026 international transformation program targets ~5,000 operational role reductions over 18 months at a cost of $225–325M through 2028.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 9M FY26 operating cash flow was $5.66B (up from $4.52B); capex $2.34B; FY26 capex guided to ~$4.1B (flat YoY, with aircraft spend cut $0.3B to ~$1.0B — a genuine capital-discipline pivot). Long-term debt is $24.1B carrying with ~$16.8B operating-lease liabilities. Debt/adjusted EBITDA is 1.9x, well inside the 3.5x covenant; ratings are investment-grade (S&P BBB / Moody's Baa2, stable). Cash was $8.0B plus $3.7B restricted (FedEx Freight Holding notes proceeds, since distributed as spin consideration). The Altman Z-Score (~1.2) is a false distress signal — it penalizes the asset/lease-heavy model but is contradicted by 1.9x leverage, ample liquidity, and a pension credit balance requiring no contributions for years.

**Capital returns.** Dividend $1.45/quarter ($5.80 annualized, raised ~5%). The $5.0B March 2024 authorization is being throttled — only $776M repurchased in 9M FY26 (none in Q3), with $1.3B remaining — to fund the spin and the $2.6B InPost investment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth here is earnings-led, not revenue-led, and that distinction matters for durability. Revenue growth of 6% in a freight recession is respectable but not exciting; the 19% EPS growth is the product of margin self-help that has a multi-year runway (Network 2.0 not fully implemented in the U.S. until end of calendar 2027, plus the new international program through 2028). This gives the margin-expansion thesis a definable, multi-year horizon rather than a one-time step-up.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term consensus: four straight beats, accelerating from +3.4% (Jun-25) and +5.5% (Sep-25) to +16.3% (Dec-25) and +25.7% (Mar-26). A company that consistently beats by widening margins is one where consensus is systematically lagging the cost program.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and requires care. Two distinct forces are at work. First, the next-year cut is partly mechanical — those estimates now describe a RemainCo that no longer includes Freight's revenue, so the headline cut overstates fundamental deterioration. Second, the next-quarter cut on a thin 5-analyst panel reflects genuine near-term softening in international volumes (de minimis removal, tariff disruption) plus the MD-11 grounding drag. The net read is positive momentum on the current fiscal period fading into real near-term caution on the next, which is why I do not treat this as a clean +30%-revision inflection BUY signal — the revision profile cuts both ways, and the disrupted fiscal calendar reduces forecast precision.

Strategic optionality comes from the InPost minority stake (European out-of-home/locker logistics) and Dataworks data monetization, but neither is large enough or controlled enough to be a near-term driver. The 2029 financial framework gives a long-term anchor, with management adding a candid risk factor that it "may not be able to achieve" the targets.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 17.3x trailing P/E is not a clean comparable because it spans the old conglomerate. On forward FY-equivalent earnings the picture is more nuanced. Current-year consensus of $19.83 against $326.99 is ~16.5x; the more relevant next-year RemainCo figure of $18.89 puts the multiple near 17.3x on the de-conglomerated business.

**Peer context.** The Air Freight & Logistics peer set shows wide dispersion: UPS at 17.6x (and a stressed 6.4% yield), CHRW at 38.7x, EXPD at 26.9x, with a median of 26.9x. FedEx trades at a meaningful discount to the asset-light forwarders (CHRW/EXPD) and roughly in line with UPS — appropriate, since UPS is the closest structural comp. The cleaner valuation logic post-spin is sum-of-the-parts: a parcel/express core valued against UPS, plus the 19.9% FDXF stake (a pure LTL carrier where comps like Old Dominion trade at premium multiples), plus the InPost holding. That SOTP is the genuine mispricing argument the bulls make.

The reasons I do not score valuation as cheap: the stock has already re-rated roughly 51% off its 52-week low ($216) in anticipation of the spin and the cost cuts, much of the SOTP unlock is now in the price, and the freight cycle that would drive a further upside surprise remains in a multi-year trough. A low multiple alone does not earn a high valuation score absent a fresh catalyst, and here the principal catalyst (the spin) has already occurred. The asymmetry today is roughly balanced rather than clearly favorable, which lands the valuation score at 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

FedEx holds a durable position in the U.S. parcel duopoly with UPS, backed by capital barriers that no new entrant can replicate quickly. The trajectory, though, is what matters, and it is mixed-to-eroding at the margin. Amazon has built its own air/ground network, launched inbound LTL, and signaled intent to sell delivery capacity to third parties — the 10-K names Amazon explicitly as a competitive risk. This is a structural threat to the duopoly economics that supported FedEx's pricing power, and it is the primary reason the competitive-position assessment has drifted lower over the past several reviews (from 7.5 in February to 5.5).

On the positive side, FedEx is defending yield effectively, which shows the brand and network still command premium pricing despite mix shift toward cheaper deferred and ground services. The integrated network remains a genuine moat. But the combination of a slower-service mix shift compressing structural yields and Amazon's vertical encroachment caps the competitive trajectory at stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**IEEPA / tariff legal overhang (material, unquantified, two-sided).** Following the 2/20/2026 Supreme Court invalidation of IEEPA tariffs, FedEx filed suit (2/23/26) seeking refunds from CBP, while five separate class actions in SC, FL, NY, TN, and DE seek tariff refunds from FedEx; no adjustment is recorded. This is a genuine binary, off-model exposure that pulls the risk score down.

**Smaller, less-diversified RemainCo.** Post-spin FedEx loses its highest-margin segment and is more cyclically exposed to e-commerce and global trade.

**Trade-policy sensitivity.** The August 2025 removal of the de minimis exemption (ex-China) and broader trade volatility are pressuring international export volumes and adding customs-brokerage costs and credit losses.

**MD-11 grounding.** The November 2025 FAA emergency Airworthiness Directive grounding all Boeing MD-11s hit Q3 results and is a reminder of fleet concentration.

**Pilot labor (RLA).** The ALPA contract has been amendable since November 2021; a tentative deal was rejected (July 2023) and arbitration declined (April 2024). A ratified deal triggers a pension remeasurement.

**Independent-contractor / joint-employer litigation** around ground service providers, potentially amplified by Network 2.0.

**2029 targets execution risk**, explicitly flagged by management as a new risk factor.

**Macro backdrop.** The freight/industrial cycle remains in a multi-year trough; a consumer slowdown in late 2026 would pressure parcel volumes directly given the more cyclical RemainCo.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 112.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 29.0% — an 83.5-point spread, in the 93rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (112.5% near-term vs 52.4% one-month, 36.4% six-month), signaling an imminent binary event: the first post-spin standalone earnings print on a disrupted fiscal calendar. The put/call volume ratio is elevated at 2.63 and the OI ratio has risen from 0.98 to 1.26 since early May as the price fell ~10%. The skew itself is balanced (2.8%), so this is uncertainty rather than a directional crash bet, but the combination warns against treating this as a low-risk entry. For a BUY thesis this divergence (elevated put demand and a vol structure pricing stress) would be a flag; for a HOLD it reinforces waiting for the print to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $400–425)

The DRIVE/Network 2.0 self-help program continues to expand Express margins past 8% as the $1.0B FY26 savings land and the U.S. Network 2.0 rollout completes through 2027. The SOTP unlock is recognized: the parcel/express core re-rates toward a premium multiple while the 19.9% FDXF stake (LTL comps trade rich) and InPost add value. Forward-year estimates prove to be mechanically depressed by the de-conglomeration rather than fundamentally weak, and they revise upward as the standalone RemainCo reports cleanly. The IEEPA situation resolves favorably (FedEx collects from CBP, class-action liability proves immaterial). The bull target maps to ~21x on normalized $19–20 RemainCo EPS plus stake value.

### Bear Case (→ $230–250, roughly -25% to -29%)

The freight recession deepens into a consumer slowdown, and a less-diversified, more cyclical RemainCo sees parcel volumes fall faster than cost cuts can offset. The next-quarter and next-year estimate cuts prove to be real deterioration, not calendar noise. Amazon accelerates third-party delivery capacity, compressing the duopoly's pricing power. The IEEPA class actions crystallize a material liability. The first standalone print disappoints against the high-IV setup and the stock gives back its post-spin re-rating. The bear case maps to ~12–13x on a depressed RemainCo earnings base.

The asymmetry today is roughly balanced. The self-help engine is genuine and the SOTP logic is sound, but the principal catalyst (the spin) has already been delivered into the price, the forward-estimate momentum is split, and the legal overhang is unquantified.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis for holding over the next 12 months is the multi-year margin self-help runway — Express margin expansion (+70bps to 7.4% in Q3) with $1.0B incremental FY26 savings and Network 2.0 not fully implemented until end of calendar 2027 — combined with the realized SOTP unlock and the 19.9% FDXF stake. This is a specific, quantified growth thesis, not a default. Conviction is capped by the unquantified IEEPA exposure, the Amazon trajectory, and the split forward-estimate picture, which keep this short of a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** The first post-spin standalone earnings report (now on a December 31 fiscal year, expected in the coming quarter) is the decisive reassessment point. The high-IV backwardation in the options market confirms the print is the pivotal near-term event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Express segment operating margin sustained above 8% with reaffirmed/raised structural-savings guidance, AND next-year EPS estimates stabilizing or revising upward once the clean RemainCo reports (removing the mechanical de-conglomeration distortion).
- Favorable resolution or material de-risking of the IEEPA/tariff litigation (CBP refund recognized, class-action exposure quantified as immaterial).
- A pullback toward the high-$200s that restores clear SOTP asymmetry while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse IEEPA class-action ruling, or management quantifying a liability that is sizeable relative to net income.
- Express segment margin reversing below ~6.5% or the $1.0B savings guidance cut, signaling the self-help engine is stalling.
- Parcel volumes declining and yields rolling over together (the structural Amazon-pressure scenario), confirming competitive erosion in hard data.

## Conclusion

FedEx is a credible turnaround executing a real, quantified cost program inside a freight recession, now reconstituted as a parcel/express pure-play after the FedEx Freight spin. The earnings evidence is strong — 9M EPS +19% on +6% revenue, four straight beats, Express margins expanding — and the SOTP logic plus the retained FDXF stake give the equity definable optionality. Against that sit an unquantified two-sided tariff/legal exposure management flags as "reasonably possibly material," an intensifying Amazon disintermediation threat, sharply negative forward-year estimate revisions (partly mechanical, partly real), and an options market pricing imminent stress into the first standalone print. The spin catalyst that drove the roughly 51% move off the lows has now been delivered, leaving roughly balanced near-term asymmetry.

The recommendation is **HOLD with a growth rationale**: own it through the next print for the margin self-help and SOTP thesis, but the binary legal overhang and decelerating forward estimates do not support a BUY at the current price. Overall score: 5.7.
