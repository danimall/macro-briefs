**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Home Depot has declined roughly 26% from its 52-week high of $426.75 (September 2025) to ~$317, a drawdown that maps to fundamental deterioration rather than market noise. Net income has fallen for three consecutive years ($17.1B FY2023 → $15.1B FY2024 → $14.2B FY2025), diluted EPS has fallen alongside it ($15.11 → $14.91 → $14.23), and the latest quarter (Q1 FY2026) showed GAAP EPS of $3.30, down 4.3% year over year, despite a 4.8% revenue beat. The headline "beat-and-reaffirm" narrative masks the underlying problem: revenue growth is almost entirely acquisition-led and margin-dilutive, while the organic engine is barely positive and unit demand is shrinking.

Comparable sales were +0.3% in FY2025 and +0.6% in Q1 FY2026, but that positive comp is composed entirely of higher average ticket (+2.2% in Q1) offset against falling transactions (−1.3% in Q1, −1.0% FY2025). Pricing and inflation are carrying the comp; foot traffic and units are declining. The two distribution acquisitions — SRS (~$18B, June 2024) and GMS (~$5.1B, September 2025) — are structurally lower-gross-margin businesses that have compressed consolidated gross margin to 33.0% (from 33.8%) and operating margin to 11.9% (from 12.9%) in Q1. The CFO stated that "most of the margin decline was due to the GMS acquisition."

The investment question is whether ~21x current-year EPS (consensus FY2026 $14.97) adequately prices a high-return franchise sitting at the trough of a housing-turnover cycle. We think it roughly does. The strategic pivot toward Pro distribution is sound and the Pro channel is taking share, but the master variable — housing turnover suppressed by mortgage-rate lock-in — has no near-term catalyst, estimate revisions are broadly negative, the buyback engine that drove HD's historical EPS growth is paused through at least FY2026, and there is no insider buying to signal conviction at these prices. We rate HD HOLD with an uncertain rationale: a fairly-priced, fundamentally sound business waiting on a rate-driven demand recovery that the company cannot manufacture itself.

## Company Overview

Home Depot is the world's largest home improvement retailer, operating 2,361 big-box stores (as of May 3, 2026) across the U.S., Canada, and Mexico, with ~13.8% of stores in Canada/Mexico. The business is now reported in two pieces:

- **Primary segment** — the retail home-improvement operation (stores + online) serving DIY consumers, "Do-It-For-Me" customers via installation, and professional contractors. Product is evenly balanced across Building Materials, Décor, and Hardlines (~$12–13B/quarter each). Online is ~16.5% of sales, growing ~10%+.
- **"Other" (SRS + GMS)** — a fast-growing specialty-trade distribution business serving Pros across roofing/building products, interior/construction products (GMS), landscape, and pool, operating 1,280+ branches. This segment generated ~$4.0B in Q1 FY2026, up from $2.6B a year earlier, with GMS adding ~$1.3B.

The strategy is a deliberate pivot from a pure big-box retailer toward an interconnected retail-plus-Pro-distribution model. SRS, GMS, and the bolt-on HVAC distributor Mingledorff's are aimed at capturing complex-project Pro spend (builders, remodelers, roofers) that the big-box format alone could not fully serve. Competitive advantages remain scale-based: purchasing power, vendor relationships, a vast store footprint serving as fulfillment hubs, higher-margin proprietary brands, and a maturing delivery network.

*Fiscal note: HD's "fiscal 2025" ended February 1, 2026. "Fiscal 2026" ends January 31, 2027; Q1 FY2026 ended May 3, 2026.*

## Financial Analysis

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 (53 wks) | FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $157.4B | ~$159.5B | $164.7B | $41.8B (+4.8% YoY) |
| Comparable sales | (3.2)% | (1.8)% | +0.3% | +0.6% |
| Operating margin | 14.2% | 13.5% | 12.7% | 11.9% |
| Net income | $17.1B | $15.1B | $14.2B | $3.3B |
| Diluted EPS | $15.11 | $14.91 | $14.23 | $3.30 (−4.3% YoY) |

The defining financial story is margin compression and a multi-year earnings decline despite top-line growth. Two forces are at work.

First, mix dilution from acquisitions. SRS and especially GMS are lower-gross-margin distribution businesses, and management attributed most of the consolidated gross-margin slip to GMS.

Second, weak organic demand plus cost inflation and SG&A deleverage. Comps were negative for two straight years before turning marginally positive, and that positive comp is built entirely on higher ticket while transactions decline. SG&A has deleveraged to 18.6% of sales (FY2025) from 17.4% (FY2023).

Cash generation remains strong but is being absorbed by dividends and deleveraging. FY2025 operating cash flow was $16.3B and FCF was $12.6B (7.7% FCF margin); Q1 FY2026 OCF was $6.0B. FY2025 uses were $9.2B dividends, $5.0B debt repayment, $3.7B capex, and $5.5B for GMS. The dividend was raised only 1.3% to $2.33/quarter in February 2026, a small increase by HD's historical standards that signals balance-sheet caution.

The balance sheet is the key risk dimension. HD runs a deliberately thin-equity, high-return model: D/E of 7.2x and stockholders' equity of just $12.8B against $105B of assets, the product of decades of buybacks ($96B treasury stock). ROE of 110% and ROA of 13.5% validate the model in normal conditions but leave little cushion. The Altman Z-Score of ~2.5 (grey zone) reflects this structure rather than a distress signal. Long-term debt stands at $46.3B (down from $48.5B and trending lower), short-term debt $4.5B, with ~$25.4B of future interest. Goodwill jumped from $8.5B to $22.3B with the acquisitions; combined with $10.3B of intangibles, that is ~31% of total assets and now larger than equity, creating real impairment exposure if SRS/GMS synergies disappoint.

ROIC has declined from 36.7% (FY2023) to 31.3%, to 25.7% (FY2025), to 25.4% TTM — still excellent in absolute terms, but the trend reflects the larger capital base from acquisitions and is the direction the market is watching.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has to be read carefully because the reported numbers overstate it. Revenue grew from $157.4B to $164.7B over two fiscal years, and Q1 grew 4.8% YoY, but strip out SRS and GMS and the organic engine is roughly flat: comps of +0.3% and +0.6% driven by price, not units. Transactions fell 1.0% in FY2025 and 1.3% in Q1. HD is buying revenue and ticket-inflation growth while underlying unit demand shrinks.

The master variable is housing affordability. Both the 10-K and 10-Q flag that the high-rate environment and elevated home prices have produced "historically low levels of housing turnover," which suppresses demand for large remodels. Home turnover drives the big cross-category projects that are HD's highest-value transactions, and CEO Decker repeatedly conceded that "the large cross-category project is muted." This is a demand ceiling outside management's control, and it will not lift until mortgage rates fall enough to unlock turnover.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of a near-term inflection. Consensus EPS has been revised down across every period: current quarter −0.3% (90d), current year −0.6% (to $14.97), and next year −1.4% (to $16.11). These are modest moves, but they are uniformly negative, the opposite of the signal that would support a more constructive view. There is no estimate momentum here.

The durable part of the growth thesis is the Pro/distribution pivot. The Pro and complex-project channel is holding up and taking share — SRS "took share despite a weak roofing market," and the GMS roll-up deepens drywall, ceilings, and steel-framing distribution. This is the more resilient demand pool, and the strategic logic is sound. But it is margin-dilutive by construction, and Lowe's is pursuing the same playbook, so this is a competitive arms race for Pro wallet share rather than a unilateral HD advantage.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$317, HD trades at ~21.2x consensus FY2026 EPS of $14.97 and ~19.7x next-year $16.11, with a 2.9% dividend yield. Against the Consumer Discretionary peer median P/E of 23.2, HD trades at a modest discount, but that comparison flatters it — the relevant comp is Lowe's at 18.3x, a direct competitor facing the same housing-turnover headwind with a cleaner balance sheet and no acquisition-integration overhang.

The case for the stock being cheap rests on cycle-adjusted earnings power: if housing turnover normalizes, HD's organic comps and operating margin recover toward the 14%+ levels of FY2023, and earnings re-rate higher off a depressed base. That is a legitimate mean-reversion thesis. The problem is timing and the absence of independent confirmation. Estimate revisions are negative, so the market is not yet pricing a near-term inflection, and there is no catalyst to force one absent a Fed-driven decline in mortgage rates. The cycle-adjusted-valuation framework that would justify looking through trough earnings applies when estimate revisions are rising and revenue is accelerating; neither is true here. Revisions are falling and organic transactions are declining.

A specific point against the cheap thesis: HD's historical EPS growth leaned heavily on shrinking the share count, and that lever is off. Buybacks have been paused since March 2024 to delever, with $11.7B authorized but explicitly not to be deployed in FY2026. The 19.7x next-year multiple therefore prices EPS growth that must now come from operations and acquisition synergies rather than financial engineering, a harder path.

Analyst targets span $310 (low) to $430 (high), with a mean of $370 and median $374. The stock sits essentially at the analyst low. On a stock with negative estimate revisions and no organic inflection, we do not treat the gap to the median as evidence of upside — analyst targets lag both up and down, and with consensus EPS being cut, target compression is more likely than the stock catching up. We assess the stock as roughly fairly valued: not a value trap (the franchise quality and cash generation are real), but not asymmetrically cheap either.

## Competitive Landscape

The home-improvement market is "highly competitive, highly fragmented, and evolving." HD's big-box rival is Lowe's, but the real strategic battleground is the fragmented Pro/contractor supply market historically served by regional distributors. HD's SRS/GMS/Mingledorff's roll-up is an explicit land-grab there, and Lowe's is pursuing the same strategy with its own Pro-distribution acquisitions. This is an arms race for Pro wallet share, which tempers the structural advantage either name can claim.

Within that contest, the trajectory favors HD on share but not on margin. SRS took share in a weak roofing market, and HD's Pro Trade Credit traction was confirmed in the Q1 analyst Q&A. The competitive position is improving in the Pro channel and stable-to-soft in DIY, where large discretionary remodels are muted by affordability. The 10-K also flags AI-driven price transparency as lowering barriers to entry and enabling comparison shopping, a structural margin and traffic headwind for all retailers, HD included.

Scale advantages remain intact: purchasing power, vendor relationships, the store footprint as a fulfillment network, and higher-margin proprietary brands. The competitive position is solid and the Pro pivot is the right strategic response to where durable demand lives, but the gross-margin trade-off and Lowe's parallel push cap how much of an edge this builds.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing-affordability lock-in** is the master risk and the master opportunity. Low housing turnover suppresses big-remodel demand and will not reverse without lower mortgage rates. This is largely priced, but it is also the gating factor on any recovery.

**Acquisition integration and impairment risk.** Two large, rapid acquisitions into a distribution model HD did not previously operate. With $22.3B goodwill and $10.3B intangibles sitting on $12.8B of equity, any SRS/GMS underperformance triggers impairment that would dwarf the equity base. Acquired customer-relationship intangible amortization (~$518M remaining in FY2026, then ~$600–680M/yr) is a recurring GAAP drag.

**Leverage and the paused buyback.** Debt-funded M&A raised interest expense to $2.4B (FY2025) and pushed the Z-score into grey territory. The deleveraging priority idles the historical EPS-accretion engine, capping near-term EPS growth. Resumption of buybacks would be a meaningful re-rating catalyst; continued pause caps upside. Watch the debt-reduction trajectory ($48.5B → $46.3B → $44.8B).

**Tariffs and trade policy.** HD sources heavily from China, Mexico, and Canada. The Supreme Court's decision striking down IEEPA tariffs creates refund optionality — HD had booked zero refunds as of May 3, 2026, with only "immaterial" receipts since. This is a small, unquantified upside asymmetry, but the broader trade regime is genuinely unpredictable.

**Weather dependency.** SRS/roofing demand is storm-driven; management said "storm activity could decide the rest of 2026." A quiet hurricane season is a real downside to the distribution segment.

**Macro consumer pressure.** Discretionary big-ticket home projects are the category most exposed to consumer retrenchment if a recession materializes. HD's customer base skews more Pro-weighted, but large discretionary remodels remain muted by affordability and would deteriorate further in a broad consumer pullback. [unverified — the broader stagflationary framing, negative-real-wage and household-spending figures are not supported by the research brief.]

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 38.6% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 23.5% (a 15.2pp spread, 73rd percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.6% near-term vs. 32.9% at six months), so the market is pricing an imminent event rather than persistent elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (−0.0% near-term, mild −2.3% at one month), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 1.58 to 0.99 since the May 19 earnings print, indicating de-risking after the quarter cleared. IV compressed sharply (59.7% → 38.6% post-earnings), consistent with resolved event risk. Net read: the options market is neutral-to-mildly-cautious, not signaling directional conviction either way, consistent with the fundamental HOLD view.

Short interest is negligible at 1.4% of float (2.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short and there is no squeeze dynamic to consider.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Home Depot is a best-in-class franchise trading at the trough of a housing-turnover cycle. When mortgage rates fall and housing turnover normalizes, the suppressed large-remodel demand returns, organic comps re-accelerate, and operating margin recovers toward the 14%+ of FY2023. As the balance sheet deleverages (LT debt already declining), HD resumes its $11.7B-authorized buyback, restoring the EPS-accretion engine. The Pro/distribution pivot through SRS/GMS positions HD in the most durable demand pool, taking share even in weak end-markets. Tariff refund optionality from the IEEPA ruling provides a small unquantified upside. At a recovered ~$17–18 EPS power and a 23–24x multiple, the stock returns to the $400+ range. Bull case: ~$430.

### Bear Case

Mortgage rates stay elevated, housing turnover remains locked, and organic transactions keep declining. Acquisition synergies underdeliver, SRS/GMS margins stay dilutive, and a goodwill impairment against a $12.8B equity base hits GAAP earnings. A quiet storm season undercuts the distribution segment. A broad consumer retrenchment pulls discretionary big-ticket projects lower. Estimate revisions continue down, the buyback stays paused, and the multiple compresses toward Lowe's 18x on $14.50 EPS. Bear case: ~$260, roughly −18% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** HD is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise that is fairly priced for a demand trough with no near-term catalyst. The mean-reversion thesis is real but rate-dependent and uncrystallized, while the organic data (declining transactions, falling estimates, paused buyback) argues against pre-positioning for a recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Comparable-sales acceleration driven by **transactions turning positive** (not just ticket), confirming organic demand recovery rather than inflation.
- Resumption of share buybacks, or a clear, dated commitment to resume in FY2026, signaling deleveraging is complete and restoring the EPS engine.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (FY2026/FY2027 consensus rising), particularly if accompanied by a sustained decline in mortgage rates that would unlock housing turnover.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge against SRS/GMS, or evidence that distribution-segment margins are deteriorating rather than stabilizing.
- Comparable sales turning negative again with continued transaction declines, signaling the affordability headwind is worsening rather than bottoming.
- A guidance cut (HD has reaffirmed twice; a cut would break the managed-expectations pattern and signal genuine demand deterioration).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, ~mid-August 2026) and the June FOMC, which will inform the rate path that gates the housing-turnover recovery thesis.

## Conclusion

Home Depot's three-year EPS decline, acquisition-led-but-margin-dilutive revenue growth, declining organic transactions, and paused buyback together explain the ~26% drawdown from the 52-week high; this is fundamental, not noise. The franchise quality, cash generation (FCF $12.6B), and Pro-distribution strategy are genuine and durable, and at ~21x current-year EPS the stock is not expensive for a business of this caliber. But it is not asymmetrically cheap either: estimate revisions are negative, the historical EPS-accretion lever is off, and the recovery thesis depends entirely on a rate-driven housing-turnover normalization with no near-term catalyst and no insider conviction to confirm it. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric: modest upside to mean-reversion against modest downside to continued demand softness and multiple compression toward Lowe's. We rate HD HOLD and would become buyers on either a transaction-led organic inflection or a dated buyback resumption, neither of which is yet in evidence.
