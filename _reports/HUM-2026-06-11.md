**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Humana is the #2 Medicare Advantage (MA) insurer, with ~93% of premiums and services revenue coming from government programs (~86% Medicare) and a vertically integrated care-delivery arm, CenterWell. The company is in the middle of a deliberate "shrink-then-grow" turnaround: FY2025 was the trough year, when management exited unprofitable MA counties (membership –7.3%) to protect margin, then in 2026 grew individual MA membership 22.6% and Part D 58.7% to take share while competitors retrenched. Reported FY2025 net income of $1.20B (EPS $9.84) was depressed by roughly $1.3B of pre-tax special items: WCAS put/call valuation marks ($513M), restructuring ($449M), impairments ($253M), a business-sale loss ($67M), and litigation, which reduced diluted EPS by about $7.38. Underlying Insurance segment operating income rose 29% to $1.66B, so normalized earnings power is materially higher than the headline.

The stock has reflected the improving narrative aggressively, rising 57% over the past twelve months and roughly 50% since early May, to $370.26 against a 52-week high of $370.87. The recovery is real, but it is now substantially priced. At $370 the stock trades about 32% above the median analyst target of $279.5 and about 25% above the mean of $296.6; only one published bull (Deutsche Bank, $441) sees upside from here. On next-year consensus EPS of $15.71 the forward multiple is 23.6x, which is reasonable for the franchise but not cheap relative to Elevance (17.7x) given Humana's heavier Medicare concentration and an unresolved Star Ratings overhang.

We rate HUM a **HOLD** with a growth rationale. The 12-month thesis is concrete: a recovery off a deliberately depressed earnings base, supported by rising next-year estimates and four consecutive beats, with the 2026 Star Ratings (which drive 2027 quality-bonus revenue) and the RADV 5th Circuit appeal as identifiable catalysts. We stop short of a BUY because the price has run ahead of consensus, two binary legal outcomes cut both ways, management has flagged that 2027 MA funding is insufficient (forcing benefit cuts), and cash-flow quality has weakened sharply. The options market is pricing near-term stress, consistent with our view that the easy recovery upside has been captured.

## Company Overview

Humana operates two segments:

- **Insurance** (~96% of external revenue): individual and group Medicare Advantage, stand-alone Part D plans, state Medicaid contracts, Medicare Supplement, specialty products, the TRICARE T-5 East military contract, and an in-house PBM. FY2025 Insurance premiums were $122.8B with segment operating income of $1.66B.
- **CenterWell** (care delivery): pharmacy solutions, senior-focused primary care, and home health. FY2025 total segment revenue was $22.5B (including $17.7B intersegment), with operating income of $1.34B. Structurally lower margin (operating cost ratio ~93%) but central to the value-based-care model: roughly 64–68% of individual MA members are in value-based arrangements.

The differentiation is vertical integration: aligned physicians, an owned pharmacy, and home-health services that serve Humana's own members and improve cost capture. Florida is a critical concentration, with about 1.0M individual MA members and ~14% of total revenue tied to Florida CMS contracts. Total revenue grew ~10% to $129.7B in FY2025.

One data note: third-party screening tools mislabeled CenterWell "services revenue" (~$5.8B) as total revenue, producing a spurious Altman Z-Score of 1.55 ("distress") and a ~46% operating margin. Humana's actual total revenue is ~$129.7B; the company is investment-grade (BBB/Baa2) with ample regulatory capital. Those screening figures should be disregarded.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income attributable to Humana fell from $2.81B (2022) to $2.49B (2023), $1.21B (2024), and $1.20B (2025). The 2025 decline was almost entirely driven by special items, not operating deterioration. Normalized FY2025 EPS, adding back the ~$7.38 of one-time charges, is closer to $17.

**Margin pressure remains the live issue.** The consolidated benefit ratio rose to 90.2% in FY2025 and jumped 240bps to 89.4% in Q1 2026, driven by the lost Star Ratings quality bonus ("Bonus Year 2026 headwind") and a flood of new, higher-loss-ratio MA members from the 22.6% growth. CenterWell operating income fell 26% in Q1 on the final-year v28 risk-model phase-in and MaxHealth integration costs. Q1 2026 revenue rose 23.5% to $39.6B, but net income was $1.18B (EPS $9.83, –4.6% YoY). The recovery front-loads cost before pricing catches up.

**Balance sheet is sound; cash flow and parent liquidity are the concrete concerns.**
- Total assets $55.3B; stockholders' equity $18.6B; debt ~$14B after a $1.0B 6.625% junior subordinated note issued in March 2026. Debt/cap is 43.0% against a 60% covenant; the $4.99B revolver is undrawn.
- Investment portfolio ~$17.6B, 97% investment-grade (avg A+), with ~$1.0B of rate-driven (not credit) unrealized losses and ~3.5-year duration.
- Statutory surplus is strong: regulated subsidiaries hold ~$14.1B against a $7.5B minimum, with ~$1.1B of ordinary dividends available to the parent in 2026.
- The weak spot: FY2025 operating cash flow collapsed to $0.9B (from $3.0B in 2024 and $4.0B in 2023), distorted by IRA receivable timing and CMS Part D settlement swings, with FCF of only ~$375M. Parent-company cash fell from $1.5B at year-end 2025 to $111M at Q1 2026 after the MaxHealth purchase and subsidiary capital contributions. Q1 OCF rebounded to $1.25B on IBNR build, but the trailing cash-generation profile is lumpy and thin relative to the franchise.

**Capital allocation has pivoted to defense.** Buybacks fell from $1.6B (2023) to $817M (2024), $151M (2025), and only ~$103M in Q1 2026. The dividend is held flat (~$0.885/quarter). Cash is being redirected to debt service, statutory capital, and bolt-on primary-care M&A (MaxHealth, $908M in February 2026). A $1.38B WCAS put liability remains, with the first two clinic cohorts callable in 2026 for roughly $1.0–1.6B, a near-term cash call worth monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three points. First, the 2026 membership surge as peers retrench. Second, the earnings recovery off a deliberately depressed base, with management framing a multi-year path to "sustainable" MA margins by 2028. Third, the estimate-revision pattern, which is positive but mixed: the current-quarter estimate has been revised up 55.3% over 90 days, and next-year EPS has risen 3.0% in 30 days and 1.0% over 90 days. The company has beaten consensus four quarters running (Q3 2025 by 13.8%, Q2 2025 by 4.9%, Q1 2026 in line), which lends credibility to forward estimates.

The mixed elements temper the bull read. The current-year EPS estimate ($8.84) has been cut 7.6% over 90 days, and the next-quarter estimate has swung to a loss of –$0.21 (a –228.6% 30-day revision and a –125% 90-day revision), reflecting the cost-trend front-loading from new members and the Stars headwind. This is not the clean inflection signal where full-year estimates are rising >30% alongside expanding margins. The strongest revision is concentrated in one quarter while the full-year number is being trimmed and margins are compressing in the near term.

The durable component is the multi-year repricing of the MA book toward a 2028 margin target combined with CenterWell's value-based integration. The risk to growth is that the 22.6% membership intake runs at higher loss ratios for longer than planned if medical-cost trend stays ahead of the annually fixed premium.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is 38.9x on the $9.84 depressed EPS, which is not the right anchor at a trough. On normalized FY2025 EPS (~$17) the trailing multiple is closer to 22x, and on next-year consensus the forward multiple is **23.6x**. That is reasonable for the #2 MA player, but it sits awkwardly within the peer set: UnitedHealth at 30.2x, Elevance at 17.7x, Molina at 53x, HealthEquity at 33.3x, Centene negative. Against the cleanest large-cap MA comp, Elevance, HUM is not cheap, and HUM carries more Medicare concentration and a still-unresolved Stars overhang than the diversified peers.

The more telling signal is the relationship to analyst targets. Only the high target ($441, Deutsche Bank) implies upside, and that is +19% versus roughly –20% to the mean. The price has blown past the consensus target after a strong run, so the consensus reflects fair value the price has already overshot rather than a floor that will be revised up. On a longer 2–3 year view, if the 2028 margin recovery delivers EPS of $20–25, $370 looks cheap (15–18x), but that path is contingent on the 2027 funding year and the litigation outcomes resolving favorably. The asymmetry at the current price is mediocre: limited upside even to the most bullish target, with meaningful downside if the recovery stalls or a binary breaks the wrong way. This supports a below-average valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Humana is the second-largest MA carrier and a vertical-integration leader through CenterWell. The integrated model — aligned physicians, owned pharmacy, home health, and value-based arrangements covering most individual MA members — is a genuine cost-capture advantage that diversified peers cannot fully replicate. The trajectory off the 2025 trough is stabilizing, and the 2026 share gains demonstrate underwriting confidence.

The competitive negatives are real. Humana's Star Ratings deterioration cut the share of members in 4-star-plus plans for PY2025, directly reducing quality-bonus revenue, which diversified peers absorbed better. Its Medicare concentration and lack of a large commercial book mean it has less cushion than UNH, ELV, or Cigna against the sector-wide MA funding squeeze. The 2027 rate notice (~2.48%) lags medical-cost trend across the industry, forcing benefit cuts (dental, vision, gym), which pressures member retention precisely where Humana is most exposed. The offset is deeper integration, but the net trajectory is recovery from a self-inflicted trough rather than the extension of a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Two binary 5th Circuit lawsuits dominate the risk profile, and they cut in opposite directions.** Humana lost its Star Ratings challenge at the district court (Oct 14, 2025) and has appealed; the loss is the source of the 2026 quality-bonus headwind already flowing through results. Separately, Humana won summary judgment vacating the CMS Final RADV Rule (Sept 25, 2025), which the government appealed (Nov 21, 2025; Humana v. Kennedy). An adverse RADV reversal could produce material extrapolated clawbacks back to PY2018; a win removes a significant tail. These are the dominant catalysts and they are two-sided.

**2027 MA funding.** Management has publicly stated the ~2.48% 2027 rate is insufficient and signaled supplemental benefit cuts for 2027 to protect margin. Premiums are fixed annually and cost overruns are not recoverable mid-year, so benefit cuts trade margin protection against retention risk.

**Growth-driven margin dilution.** The MA and PDP intake brings higher-MLR lives; the Q1 240bps benefit-ratio rise is the early evidence. If trend outpaces repricing, the multi-year recovery slips.

**Other exposures.** DOJ civil inquiries into Part C risk-adjustment coding; a DOJ-intervened qui tam (Shea v. eHealth) over broker/call-center marketing; In re Humana Securities Litigation in Delaware plus derivative suits; Florida and broader Medicare concentration; weak FY2025 cash flow and parent cash of $111M.

These risks are not fully reflected at a 52-week high above analyst targets, which is why the risk score is low.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress, not complacency. Near-term ATM IV is 53.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 50.3% (rich by 3.4pp), at the 67th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 53.8% > long-term 51.2%), which typically signals an imminent catalyst or event risk. The put/call skew is elevated at 9.9%, the one-week skew is –8.4%, the put/call volume ratio is 1.23, and the six-month skew is –4.1%. Implied move at the nearest expiry is ±2.5%. After a strong price run, this configuration is consistent with the market hedging downside into the next data points (Stars/RADV developments, cost-trend updates) rather than extrapolating further upside. It corroborates the view that risk/reward at $370 is two-sided.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** 2025 was a deliberate trough; normalized earnings power (~$17 in 2025, $15.71+ consensus next year) is well above the depressed GAAP print. Four consecutive beats and rising next-year estimates support the recovery. A 2026 Star Ratings rebound (released Oct 2025, driving 2027 quality bonuses) reverses the current headwind; a RADV appellate win removes a major clawback tail. The 2027 benefit-design discipline protects margin on the path to a 2028 target, and CenterWell integration compounds cost capture. On a 2028 normalized EPS of $20–25, $370 is 15–18x. Bull target ~$441 (Deutsche Bank), roughly +19%.

**Bear case.** The recovery is already priced: the stock is above the median analyst target and at 23.6x next-year EPS, richer than Elevance with more Medicare concentration. The 2027 funding shortfall forces benefit cuts that pressure retention; the membership surge runs at elevated MLR for longer than planned; and one or both 5th Circuit cases break the wrong way (Star Ratings appeal lost, RADV reversed), reintroducing a large clawback liability. Cash flow stays weak, the WCAS put cash call lands, and buybacks remain on hold. A re-rating toward the mean target ($296.6) is –20%, and a worse litigation/funding outcome could push toward the low target ($195).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **HOLD** with a 12-month growth rationale. The specific thesis: an earnings recovery off a deliberately depressed 2025 base toward $15.71+ next-year EPS, validated by four consecutive beats and rising 30-day revisions, with two concrete catalysts inside the window — the 2026 Star Ratings outcome (driving 2027 quality bonuses) and the RADV 5th Circuit appeal. The reason it is a HOLD rather than a BUY is price: at $370 the stock is above the median analyst target and most of the easy recovery upside is captured, while the 2027 funding cliff and binary litigation keep the risk/reward two-sided.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 2026 Star Ratings show a clear rebound in the share of members in 4-star-plus plans, restoring 2027 quality-bonus revenue, AND next-year consensus EPS revisions continue rising above $16.
- A favorable RADV 5th Circuit ruling (affirming the district-court vacatur), removing the extrapolated-clawback tail.
- A pullback toward $290–300 (near the analyst mean) that restores asymmetric upside while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The consolidated benefit ratio rises further (above ~90.5%) in subsequent quarters, indicating the 2026 growth intake is structurally underpriced rather than transitional.
- An adverse RADV appellate reversal that reinstates material extrapolated recoveries, OR a Star Ratings appeal loss that locks in the bonus headwind into 2028.
- Full-year and next-year EPS estimates begin falling together, breaking the recovery narrative, OR parent liquidity deteriorates further alongside the WCAS put cash call without offsetting subsidiary dividends.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, late July) for cost-trend and benefit-ratio confirmation; the 5th Circuit dockets on RADV and Star Ratings; and the 2027 bid/benefit-design disclosures. The 2026 Star Ratings already released (Oct 2025) feed 2027 economics and are the key medium-term swing factor.

## Conclusion

Humana is a credible turnaround that the market has rewarded quickly. The operating story underneath the depressed GAAP earnings is better than the headline, normalized earnings power is meaningfully higher than $9.84, estimates are rising for next year, and the company has beaten four quarters running. Those facts, combined with strong positive momentum and two-sided binary catalysts, argue against a bearish call despite the valuation having run.

At the same time, the stock is above the median analyst target, the forward multiple of 23.6x is not cheap against the cleanest peer, management itself has flagged that 2027 funding is insufficient, cash flow has weakened sharply, and the options market is pricing imminent stress. The recovery is largely priced, and the asymmetry from here is mediocre: limited upside even to the most bullish target against material downside if the cost trend, funding, or litigation disappoint. We rate HUM a **HOLD (growth rationale)** and would become more constructive on a Star Ratings rebound plus a favorable RADV ruling, or on a pullback toward the high-$290s that restores upside.
