**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Walmart is the highest-quality operator in global retail, and its investment case has shifted from a top-line story to a margin-mix story. FY26 net sales reached $706.4B on ~5% growth, but operating income grew faster than sales, gross margin expanded 50 bps over two years to 24.2%, and the incremental profit is increasingly coming from structurally higher-margin businesses: Walmart Connect (retail media), membership income (Sam's fee hike from $50→$60 effective May 2026), marketplace/fulfillment services, and OnePay fintech. Q1 FY27 confirmed the trajectory: net sales +7.1%, eCommerce +26%, Walmart U.S. comps +4.3%, Sam's +5.9%. The bull case rests on a defensive grocery anchor generating traffic that feeds a fast-growing, higher-margin digital ecosystem.

The problem is price. At $111.84 the stock trades at roughly 39x trailing and ~39x forward EPS, against consensus FY27 EPS of $2.90 and FY28 of $3.29 — high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS growth. That is a Costco-adjacent multiple on a business growing earnings materially slower than Costco's revenue base implies, and more than double the 18.5x peer median (Target, Dollar General, Dollar Tree, BJ's). The multiple already capitalizes the margin-transformation thesis; there is no independent catalyst to argue the market is underpricing the stock today. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative (-0.6% on the current year over 90 days), which removes the earnings-momentum tailwind that would justify paying up.

We maintain HOLD, the fifth consecutive hold on this name, classified as a growth hold. The 12-month thesis is real appreciation via continued margin-mix compounding, but the entry price offers little cushion. The ~17% drawdown from the $135.16 52-week high improves the setup at the margin, yet the stock still trades near the upper end of its historical multiple. We would become buyers in the low-$100s (a ~34x forward multiple), where the compounding math starts to work with a margin of safety. The upcoming CEO transition (Doug McMillon out, John Furner in, alongside a near-complete C-suite refresh in February 2026) adds execution risk to the ecosystem strategy as the new team inherits it.

## Company Overview

Walmart operates 10,900+ stores across 19 countries plus a fast-growing eCommerce and services ecosystem, serving roughly 280 million customers weekly. FY26 total revenue was $713.2B (net sales $706.4B) across three reportable segments:

- **Walmart U.S.** (~68% of net sales, $483.0B): the crown jewel and highest-margin segment (5.2% operating margin), anchored by grocery (~60% of the segment), plus general merchandise and health & wellness.
- **Walmart International** (~19%, $130.4B): Mexico/Central America, China, Canada, India (Flipkart/PhonePe majority stakes), Chile, Africa — the fastest grower (+18% net sales in Q1 FY27, aided by a $2.3B FX tailwind).
- **Sam's Club U.S.** (~13%, $93.0B): 601 membership warehouse clubs where membership income is the profit engine.

The competitive moat is scale-driven: 192 U.S. and 179 international distribution facilities, EDLP/EDLC pricing, ~2.1M associates, AA/Aa2 credit ratings, and a store footprint doubling as an eCommerce fulfillment network with 8,400+ pickup/delivery points. The strategic thesis is a self-reinforcing flywheel in which retail traffic feeds higher-margin advertising, marketplace services, membership, fintech (OnePay), and health & wellness.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Net sales compounded from $567.8B (FY22) to $706.4B (FY26), a ~5% CAGR that is remarkable for a company of this size. Q1 FY27 accelerated to +7.1% net sales / +7.3% total revenue. The standout is eCommerce at +26% (~$27.1B of U.S. sales in the quarter, roughly 23% of Walmart U.S.), which is the mix-quality driver behind the margin story.

**Profitability.** FY26 operating income was $29.8B (4.2% margin); net income $21.9B (+12.6% YoY); diluted EPS $2.73, up from $2.41. Gross margin expanded to 24.2% (FY26) from 23.7% (FY24) on disciplined inventory management and the higher-margin advertising and membership businesses. The mechanism matters: as low-margin retail sales run through the store network while high-margin services grow double-digits, blended operating income can outgrow sales, which is the explicit management goal and is showing up in the numbers. Offsetting pressures: FY26 opex rose 20 bps on ~$0.9B higher self-insured general-liability claims, a $0.7B PhonePe stock-comp modification charge, and rising depreciation; Q1 FY27 opex deleveraged 33 bps on depreciation, ~$0.2B reorganization charges, and medical inflation. At a ~4% operating margin, small cost swings have outsized EPS effects.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY26 operating cash flow was $41.6B; FCF $14.9B after record $26.6B capex (FY27 guided $25–27B for technology and supply-chain automation). The balance sheet is strong: $38.2B LT debt against $99.6B equity, high investment-grade ratings, and an intentional working-capital deficit ($22.6B at FY26 year-end) reflecting supplier-financing efficiency, not distress. Q1 FY27 FCF was -$1.9B on seasonal inventory build and capex timing, normal for the quarter rather than deterioration.

**Capital return.** The dividend rose to $0.99/share for FY27 (53rd consecutive annual increase, ~$7.5B/yr, but a thin ~0.9% yield). A new $30B buyback was authorized in February 2026 ($28.2B remaining at Q1). FY26 repurchases jumped to $8.1B at an average of $95.13; Q1 FY27 slowed to $2.1B at an average of $125.51 as the stock ran up, sensible discipline buying less as the price rose.

**Earnings quality.** The four-quarter surprise record (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss, including a -9.2% miss in the September 2025 quarter) shows respectable but not perfect visibility. FCF conversion (FCF/net income ~68% in FY26) is depressed by the elevated capex cycle rather than accrual issues; underlying operating cash generation is high-quality.

Financial health earns a 7.5 — strong FCF, appropriate leverage for a retailer, working-capital discipline, and durable investment-grade access, with the thin operating margin the only meaningful constraint on a higher mark.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects score 6.5, reflecting durable but decelerating top-line growth with genuine mix upgrade underneath. The 5% net-sales CAGR is defensive and reliable, but the earnings-growth engine is the margin mix. Three specific levers support forward EPS:

1. **Sam's Club membership fee increase** ($50→$60 Club, $110→$120 Plus, effective May 2026) — recognized ratably, a direct high-margin tailwind to membership income beginning mid-FY27.
2. **Walmart Connect / retail media** — advertising is the highest-margin revenue line and is growing double-digits; external coverage cites +26% advertising growth in Q1, consistent with the eCommerce/marketplace expansion (treat the advertising-specific figure as external/unconfirmed at the precise level).
3. **OnePay fintech and health & wellness** — structurally higher-margin, though each carries incremental regulatory load.

There is also embedded optionality: a potential PhonePe IPO (the $0.7B FY26 stock-comp charge was tied to a possible listing) could crystallize value in the India fintech asset.

The counterweight is estimate momentum. Consensus FY27 EPS sits at $2.90 (vs. $2.91 30 days ago, $2.92 90 days ago) and FY28 at $3.29 — essentially flat revisions. There is no earnings-inflection signal here; growth is steady, not accelerating. The revision velocity that would flip growth into a strong BUY signal is absent. This is a mid-single-digit revenue / high-single-digit EPS compounder, and the growth score reflects reliability rather than acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the case is weakest, and we score it 4.5. Peer medians in Consumer Staples merchandise retail are 18.5x (Target 17.5x, Dollar General 16.2x, Dollar Tree 18.5x, BJ's 20.9x); only Costco at 49.1x trades richer, and Costco's revenue base and membership-margin profile justify more. Walmart at ~2x the peer median needs a clear growth premium, and it has one in the margin transformation and defensive quality, but high-single-digit EPS growth does not support a ~39x multiple on a value-metrics basis. The implied PEG is well above 3.

The cycle-adjustment guidance does not rescue the multiple here. Walmart is not at a cyclical trough with earnings tripling; it is a mature compounder at peak margin visibility with flat estimate revisions. The trailing P/E is a fair representation of forward earnings power, not a distortion. Nor is prior appreciation the reason for the low score — the ~14.6% year-to-date price gain is not itself disqualifying. The issue is current price versus current and forward fundamentals: the market is paying a premium multiple for a business whose earnings grow at roughly half that multiple's implied rate.

Analyst targets (mean $138.59, median $140, high $155, low $81) sit ~24% above spot, which would normally argue upside. But on a stock already at ~39x forward with flat revisions, consensus targets reflect the same defensive-compounder optimism embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of mispricing, and the guardrail against treating targets as floors applies here. The $81 low target shows the multiple can compress meaningfully if the margin story stalls.

A "cheap" valuation label has historically been a poor predictor of returns; here the opposite condition holds — a rich multiple with no independent catalyst — which is its own caution. The path to a high valuation score is a pullback into the low-$100s, where a ~34x forward multiple begins to align price with the compounding math.

## Competitive Landscape

Competitive position scores 7.0 — strong and stable, though we mark down the trajectory slightly from prior reports. Walmart's differentiators are physical scale plus grocery dominance (a defensive, staple category that drives repeat trips) fused with a fast-growing digital ecosystem. Against Amazon (the primary omnichannel/advertising/fulfillment rival), Costco, Target, grocers, and dollar stores, Walmart is gaining share in eCommerce and building out advertising and marketplace at rates that widen the services moat.

The new and underappreciated risk is agentic AI. The FY26 10-K repeatedly flags "emerging agentic platforms" for product search, discovery, and purchasing as a threat that could reduce store traffic and cross-sell, and require heavy AI investment to keep pace. This is a new risk-factor theme relative to prior filings, and it is why we no longer treat competitive trajectory as unambiguously improving: if AI shopping agents disintermediate the discovery layer, Walmart's traffic-driven flywheel weakens. The company is responding organizationally (a new EVP of "AI Acceleration, Product and Design," ex-Instacart), which is credible but unproven. Social commerce (TikTok Shop, Temu/Shein) adds a second competitive vector on general merchandise. Grocery dominance and same-day fulfillment scale remain the durable defenses.

## Risk Assessment

Risk factors score 6.0 — a solid business with several specific, largely-priced overhangs and one new watch item.

- **Tariffs/trade policy.** Less than one-third of U.S. merchandise is imported, but management explicitly warns the "dynamic tariff environment" continues into FY27, cannot be predicted, and "could be material." There is offsetting optionality: Walmart is pursuing IEEPA tariff refunds via U.S. Customs and has recognized $0 to date, a one-time upside if the process breaks its way.
- **Litigation overhang.** DOJ opioid civil case (trial Nov 2027), False Claims Act suit, 130+ MDL cases; FTC/State Spark Driver settlement ($100M judgment, ~$37M accrued); Asda Equal Value Claims (UK, ~73,000 claimants, WMT indemnifies post-divestiture, phase 3 hearing Nov 2026); Mexico COFECE, India CCI/FDI, DOJ AML probe, EPA refrigerant matter. Individually bounded, collectively a persistent tail.
- **Thin-margin cost sensitivity.** At ~4% operating margin, swings in labor, healthcare (medical inflation cited in Q1), self-insurance, and fuel move EPS more than at higher-margin peers.
- **Macro/consumer.** New CEO Furner publicly flagged high gas prices as the consumer "stress point" (June 2026). A genuine consumer contraction cuts two ways for Walmart: trade-down benefits the value channel, but a real downturn pressures general-merchandise mix and discretionary basket size. [unverified — the specific H2 inflation, Hormuz oil-price tail, and second-half consumer-cliff macro claims are not supported by the research brief.]
- **Management transition** (see below) is itself an execution risk.
- **False alarm to correct:** the 11/20/2025 Item 3.01 "Notice of Delisting" 8-K reflects Walmart's voluntary move from NYSE to Nasdaq, not a compliance issue. Automated screens will misread it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150–155).** The margin-mix transformation compounds: Walmart Connect advertising, marketplace, the Sam's fee increase, OnePay, and health & wellness push operating income to grow meaningfully faster than sales, gross margin continues expanding, and the multiple holds near 40x on rising EPS. A PhonePe IPO crystallizes India fintech value, and tariff refunds provide a one-time boost. EPS reaches ~$3.50–3.75 within 18–24 months and the market pays a premium for the best defensive compounder in retail. This aligns with the $155 analyst high.

**Bear case ($81–95).** Multiple compression toward the low-30s or high-20s as high-single-digit EPS growth fails to justify ~39x, exacerbated by any of: agentic AI eroding the traffic flywheel, a genuine consumer contraction pressuring general-merchandise mix, tariff costs sticking without refund recovery, or an execution stumble under the new leadership team. At 28–32x on $2.90–3.00 EPS, the stock trades $81–95, with the $81 analyst low as the tail. From $111.84, that is roughly 15–28% downside.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-slightly-negative at the current price. The quality is not in question; the price leaves little room for the transformation to disappoint.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** This is the fifth consecutive hold; the bar for continuing it is met by the genuine margin-mix thesis, but the elevated multiple and flat estimate revisions keep it from becoming a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price pullback into the low-$100s (~$100–105), a ~34x forward multiple that restores a margin of safety on the compounding math.
2. Positive estimate-revision velocity — FY27 EPS consensus rising +3% or more over 90 days on advertising/membership acceleration, confirming the margin story is beating plan.
3. A confirmed PhonePe IPO or a material IEEPA tariff-refund recognition that adds visible per-share value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Evidence agentic-AI or social-commerce disintermediation is measurably eroding Walmart U.S. traffic/comps (comps decelerating below +2% on falling transactions).
2. Gross-margin reversal — the 24.2% figure contracting for two consecutive quarters, signaling the mix thesis is stalling under tariff/cost pressure.
3. A leadership-transition execution stumble (guidance cut, strategy reset) under the new C-suite.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural point is the Q2 FY27 print (~August 2026), which will show the first full quarter of the Sam's fee increase in membership income and updated advertising/eCommerce trajectory. The China CAC data re-approval (due August 2026) and the Asda phase 3 hearing (November 2026) are secondary monitoring items.

## Conclusion

Walmart is a best-in-class operator executing a credible, visible margin-mix transformation: the shift from thin-margin retail toward higher-margin advertising, membership, marketplace, and fintech is real and showing up in a 50 bps two-year gross-margin expansion with operating income outgrowing sales. Financial health is strong, and the defensive grocery anchor plus digital ecosystem is a durable competitive position. The single constraint is price: ~39x forward EPS on high-single-digit EPS growth, more than double the peer median, with flat estimate revisions and no independent catalyst to argue the market is currently underpricing the stock. The ~17% pullback from the $135 high improves the setup but does not create asymmetry. We rate WMT a HOLD (growth hold) and would add in the low-$100s, where the compounding thesis works with a margin of safety and the agentic-AI and consumer risks are better compensated.
