## Executive Summary

Supermicro's FY2026 was the most operationally consequential and financially awkward year in the company's history. Revenue grew from $21.97B to roughly $39.0B (+78%), the June quarter printed adjusted gross margin of 17.6% against management's own 8.2-8.4% guide issued six weeks earlier, adjusted EPS of $1.70 beat consensus near $0.92-0.98, and management guided FY2027 revenue to $65-72B versus a consensus around $53B. On the growth axis, this is one of the strongest data sets in enterprise hardware.

The problem is what sits underneath. Stitching the filed nine-month figures to the reported June quarter puts full-year FY2026 gross margin at approximately 10.1-10.9%: below FY2025's 11.06%, below FY2024's 13.75%, and roughly 790 basis points below FY2023's 18.01%. The multi-year margin compression is intact; the June quarter is a single period management attributed to "favorable customer and product mix," a composition effect rather than demonstrated pricing power. Nine-month operating cash flow was **negative $7,556.8M** against nine-month net income of $1,052.2M, cash fell from $5,169.9M to $1,290.3M, and the cash conversion cycle stretched from roughly 94 days to 148 days. The June 2026 raise of ~$4.9B net ($1.22B common at $27.50 plus $3.68B of 7.00% mandatory convertible preferred, alongside a $2.0B ATM) funds under six months at that burn rate.

Layered on top: four material weaknesses in internal control, unremediated since the FY2024 10-K and unchanged as of March 31, 2026; an SDNY indictment (March 19, 2026) of three individuals formerly with the company for alleged conspiracy to commit export-control violations; SEC subpoenas dated November 19, 2024 and April 28, 2026; and a board investigation that the company's own July 21 8-K says "could affect our forecasts, these preliminary results and prior period results." Customer A accounted for 38.5% of nine-month sales, and four customers held 75.1% of $8,413.4M of gross receivables — roughly $6.3B — against an allowance for credit losses of $488 thousand. The ~46% drawdown from the $58.78 high is the market discounting margin durability, cash conversion, further dilution, and restatement risk. Three of those four are confirmed in filings. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Supermicro designs and builds application-optimized servers, storage, motherboards, chassis, power supplies, switches and, increasingly, rack- and data-center-scale integrated systems around its Data Center Building Block Solutions architecture. It reports as a single operating segment, manufactures in San Jose, Taiwan and the Netherlands, and is ramping a Malaysian campus that carries a 10-year tax exemption beginning in FY2026. Q3 FY2026 revenue was 68.7% United States.

Economically, the company is an integrator: it buys accelerators, CPUs and memory and sells assembled systems. The FY2025 10-K discloses that **one supplier represented 64.4% of total purchases**, up from 30.7% in FY2023 (the filing does not name it; Nvidia is a reasonable inference, not a fact). Two mechanical subsystems come almost entirely from related parties — Ablecom made 94.0% of chassis and Compuware 92.4% of power supplies in Q3 FY2026 — both Taiwan entities controlled by relatives of CEO Charles Liang.

The competitive proposition is speed and configurability: being first to market on each accelerator generation, with component-level control over form factor, power and liquid cooling. That is a genuine operational advantage but not a durable economic moat, which the margin trajectory demonstrates.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 | FY2026E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $7,123M | $14,989M | $21,965M | $27,943M | ~$39,043M |
| Gross margin | 18.01% | 13.75% | 11.06% | 8.17% | 10.1%-10.9% |
| Net income | $640M | $1,153M | $1,049M | $1,052M | n/a |

FY2025 net income was *lower* than FY2024 on 47% revenue growth. The nine-month FY2026 margin decline of 3.4 points was quantified in the 10-Q: tariff expense +$273.6M (+891%), inventory write-downs +$79.9M to $239.3M, and **vendor rebates down $276.3M** — roughly a full point of margin on nine-month revenue, and the cleanest evidence that commercial terms with the dominant component supplier moved against Supermicro. Q3 reversed two of these at the margin (write-downs fell $54.9M on higher component spot prices; rebates rose $12.3M), which shows the exposure cuts both ways.

The June quarter's 17.6% adjusted gross margin is the single most important open data point. Two readings are available and I cannot yet separate them: either the favorable mix reflects the customers and products that populate the $60B order book (in which case FY2027 sits in the mid-teens and the stock is materially undervalued), or it is a one-quarter composition effect on top of a structurally 10-11% business. The full-year arithmetic favors the second reading; the guidance favors the first. Q1 FY2027 margin, not yet disclosed, is the test.

**Cash flow is the binding constraint.** Nine-month operating cash flow of –$7,556.8M against +$795.9M a year earlier decomposes cleanly: receivables absorbed $6,209.8M, inventories $6,669.6M, prepaid $381.7M (–$13,261.1M), partly offset by payables +$2,406.9M, deferred revenue +$1,404.3M and accruals +$232.9M (+$4,044.1M). Net working-capital drag of ~$9.2B. Derived metrics: DSO ~74.8 days versus ~34.9 in the June 2025 quarter; DIO ~109.5 versus ~81.8; DPO ~36.4 versus ~22.4; cash conversion cycle ~148 days versus ~94.

The 10-Q's own explanation is that customers are demanding longer payment terms because system costs have risen. That is the mechanism to hold onto: a GB-class rack costs a multiple of a legacy server, buyers push the financing onto the vendor, and the vendor with the weakest balance sheet in the peer set absorbs it. I deliberately do not annualize the nine-month burn ($839.6M/month); it is a step-function build ahead of an order ramp, deferred revenue rose $1.4B (customers are prepaying), and an uncommitted $1,790.0M receivables purchase facility (first used in Q3: $831.7M sold, $5.7M discount) has substantial unused capacity. The forward math is unforgiving: growing revenue from ~$39B to the $68.5B guidance midpoint at anything close to a 148-day cycle implies incremental working capital in the billions. The rate of burn should fall as growth decelerates; it does not obviously turn positive without a structural change in customer terms.

**Capital structure at March 31, 2026:** cash $1,290.3M; total assets $23,452.0M; equity $7,575.6M; lines of credit and term loans $4,113.7M; convertible principal $4,725.0M; gross debt ~$8,838.7M; net debt ~$7,548.4M. Non-cancelable purchase commitments $10.1B, mostly within twelve months, against $11,103.4M of inventory of which $8,154.1M is finished goods.

The borrowing stack was assembled in six weeks: a **$2.0B JP Morgan revolver (Dec 29, 2025, fully drawn, secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets while below investment grade)** with a maximum net leverage covenant stepping 4.00x → 3.50x → 3.00x; and **$1,765M of CTBC facilities in Taiwan, $1,763.0M drawn, contractually restricted to component procurement** — functionally inventory floor-plan financing, with $1,805.9M of principal scheduled in FY2027 (extendable twice by one year). The convertibles ($700M 2028s at $61.06; $1,725M 2029s at $83.44; $2,300M 2030s at 0.00% coupon, $55.20) are all deeply out of the money at $31.60 and are now structurally subordinated to the secured revolver. Their disclosed fair values at March 31 (86.0, 80.1 and 73.2 cents on the dollar, each below June 30, 2025) are predominantly credit spread and coupon, not lost equity optionality, though broad 2025-26 convertible and rate repricing explains part of it.

**Pro forma for the raise**, ~$4.9B of net proceeds against $7,548M of net debt takes net debt to roughly $2.6B before any June-quarter cash flow, which is comfortably inside a 4.00x covenant on FY2026 EBITDA I estimate at $3.0-3.3B. I did not verify the credit agreement's EBITDA definition, add-backs, or whether the preferred counts as equity for covenant purposes, so that headroom is an estimate. The preferred carries $262.5M of annual dividends ranking ahead of common.

**Earnings surprise record:** three beats and one miss over four quarters (+74.1%, +32.3%, +38.7%, –14.9%). The magnitude of the beats reflects forecasting variance at this company as much as outperformance: a nine-point gross margin error on its most-watched metric six weeks before quarter-end, at a company with a documented material weakness in "completeness and accuracy of information produced by the entity," is evidence about information quality in both directions.

## Growth Analysis

The demand signal is real and corroborated by hyperscaler capex trends and by the utility/electrical-equipment constraint on power, cooling and networking that management cited for the June revenue shortfall. Orders slipping on site readiness is a different problem from orders disappearing. [unverified — specific hyperscaler capex figures are not in the provided sources.]

Against that: **Q4 revenue of $11.1B missed consensus near $11.6B** and landed near the low end of the $11.0-12.5B guide, while FY2027 revenue guidance of $65-72B sits 23-36% above the ~$53B consensus. Both facts are informative. Conversion is slower than order intake implies, and management is nonetheless underwriting 67-84% growth.

Quantitatively, the order figures require discipline. Remaining performance obligations were **$2,135.6M** at March 31, 2026 (up from $731.4M), and RPO explicitly excludes contracts with original expected duration of one year or less — which is where server orders overwhelmingly sit. The **">$60B of Q4 orders"** is an issuer-sourced, unaudited, undefined measure that the company itself cautions "may not constitute firm commitments and may be subject to cancellation or delays." It should never be added to or compared with RPO. It is meaningful directionally; it is not a contractual backlog.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus $3.31 (+4.4% over 30 days), next year $3.86 (+4.0%). These almost certainly do not yet reflect the August 11 guidance, and the current-quarter estimate of $0.76 against Q1 FY2027 revenue guidance of $14.5-15.5B looks stale. Revisions of this magnitude are well short of the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal, and the underlying setup — a cash-consuming, governance-impaired integrator — does not fit that signal.

Growth Prospects at 7.0 reflects genuine volume expansion with a guidance raise far above consensus, discounted for the fact that each incremental revenue dollar has so far consumed cash and diluted margin.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.60 with 646.87M shares, market capitalization is $20.44B. Enterprise value: $20.44B + $8.84B gross debt + $3.75B preferred – pro forma cash of ~$6.19B (March cash plus net proceeds, before June-quarter burn, which is unverified) ≈ **$26.8B**. Against FY2026 revenue of ~$39.0B that is **0.69x**; against the FY2027 guidance midpoint of $68.5B, **0.39x**.

Peer context is close to useless as posted: the hardware/storage comparables show P/Es of 11.1x (HPQ), 29.8x (NTAP), 46.5x (CRSR) and 126-139x for the Pure/Everpure entries, a median of 46.5x that reflects idiosyncratic earnings bases rather than a sector multiple. The relevant comparison is Dell and HPE, both investment grade, both competing for the same accelerator-based demand, both benefiting from balance-sheet capacity precisely when customers demand extended terms.

Earnings power is entirely a function of gross margin, so I model it that way on FY2027, with opex ~$2.0-2.2B, interest ~$450-500M, an 18% tax rate, $262.5M of preferred dividends, and ~780M shares (646.9M plus ~120M from mandatory preferred conversion plus modest ATM issuance):

| FY2027 scenario | Revenue | GM | EPS to common | Implied multiple at $31.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Margin reverts | $55B | 8.5% | ~$2.00 | 15.8x |
| Base | $62B | 12.0% | ~$4.40 | 7.2x |
| Mix durable | $70B | 15.0% | ~$7.90 | 4.0x |

The stock currently trades at roughly 9.5x stale consensus of $3.31, so the market is priced close to a 9% gross margin outcome on guided revenue. The bull case is a straightforward extrapolation of one quarter.

So why not upgrade? Because the cheapness is a symptom, not an opportunity, until three things resolve. First, **dilution is structurally open-ended**: 45.45M shares already issued at $27.50, $3.75B of mandatory convertible preferred that will become 109-136M shares regardless of price (conversion rates unverified), a $2.0B ATM at management's discretion, only 7.65M shares left under the equity plan against 35.4M options and 19.0M RSUs outstanding (a share-authorization request is likely), and 73.8M shares of convertible dilution above $55.20. A maximum-loaded scenario reaches ~915M shares, +52% against the 601.4M March base. Second, **the cash-flow model requires repeat financing** at whatever the market will bear, and AI-linked credit is repricing wider at issuance elsewhere in the complex. Third, **restatement risk is company-acknowledged**, and a restatement resets the entire earnings base the multiple is applied to.

Cheap multiples on a business whose margin structure has more than halved over three years, whose control environment is documented as ineffective, and whose growth is lender- and shareholder-funded, reflect impairment rather than mispricing. Valuation 5.5: the asymmetry is real but unreliable.

## Competitive Landscape

The margin data quantify where the value went. Gross margin fell from 18.01% (FY2023) to ~10.5% (FY2026E) while revenue grew roughly 5.5x; over the same period the top supplier's share of purchases went from 30.7% to 64.4% and vendor rebates fell $276.3M. As mix shifted to GPU-heavy rack-scale systems, a much larger share of each revenue dollar became pass-through silicon and the accelerator vendor captured the economics. At ~10.5% gross margin on ~$39B, Supermicro generates gross profit per revenue dollar comparable to a distributor.

The read-through is symmetric with the primes: on July 21, Supermicro's disclosure lifted Dell +5% and HPE +4% after hours. That is the market saying these three are competing for the same demand pool with differentiated speed but no differentiated technology. In the current environment, the decisive competitive variable is who can finance customer terms. Dell and HPE are investment grade; Supermicro is funding the same growth with a first-lien secured revolver, one-year Taiwanese component facilities, and 7% mandatory convertible preferred.

Competitive Position 4.2: the time-to-market advantage is genuine and share gains are visible in revenue, but the trajectory of realized economics and supplier bargaining power is deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**Control environment and restatement risk.** Disclosure controls were not effective at March 31, 2026, and four material weaknesses first identified in the FY2024 10-K remain unremediated with no ICFR changes in the quarter: IT general controls, segregation of duties, completeness/accuracy of entity-produced information, and documentation of control procedures "including timely identification and disclosure of new related party transactions." ICFR was also ineffective at June 30, 2025. This is the sixth quarter.

**Legal and regulatory.** SDNY unsealed an indictment on March 19, 2026 of three individuals formerly employed by or associated with the company for alleged conspiracy to commit export-control violations involving brokers with China-based customers; the company is not a defendant. A board investigation led by the Lead Independent Director began April 7, 2026 and is ongoing. Two SEC subpoenas (Nov 19, 2024; Apr 28, 2026). Five securities class actions and nine-plus derivative actions. The company's own language — the investigation "could affect our forecasts, these preliminary results and prior period results" — makes restatement risk explicit rather than hypothetical, at a company that filed the FY2024 10-K and two 10-Qs late under Nasdaq extensions.

**Counterparty credit is the sharpest quantified tail.** Customer A was 38.5% of nine-month sales. Four customers held 75.1% of $8,413.4M gross receivables (~$6.3B) against a **$488 thousand** allowance — 0.006%, up from zero at June 30, 2025. A 1% loss rate on that exposure is ~$63M; a single default by a capital-hungry AI infrastructure buyer would be an order of magnitude larger and would hit an equity base of $7.6B. The 10-Q cautions that letters A-D may denote different customers across periods, so period-over-period letter comparisons are unreliable. Product mix diversified within AI (GPU-related products +150.5% in Q3); counterparty exposure concentrated. Those are different measures and should not be blended.

**Inventory and commitments.** $11.1B of inventory ($8.15B finished goods) against $10.1B of non-cancelable purchase commitments concentrated within twelve months, in a technology where each accelerator generation obsoletes the prior within roughly a year. Nine-month write-downs of $239.3M; a $4.1M loss liability was recorded on commitments (none a year earlier).

**Related-party expansion in the exact domain of a documented control weakness.** A $200.0M trade credit line to Compuware (Nov 19, 2025, net 90); a new Q3 arrangement for Ablecom to resell Supermicro products to a Japanese end customer, moving a related party onto the distribution side; Ablecom and Compuware jointly holding ~29% of Leadtek, an authorized reseller; both developing campuses adjacent to the Malaysia site. Related-party purchases actually fell as a share of cost of sales (2.2% in Q3 versus 2.8%), the relationships predate the control findings, and the CEO's personal related-party loan was repaid in full in October 2025, so this is a governance concern about process, not an inference about intent.

**Vendor-financier structure.** Supermicro leases 21MW of Vernon, California colocation through 2035, coterminously sublicenses it without relief of obligation ($411.5M of future receipts against $490.6M of total lease payments), holds **$92.5M of equity in the sub-licensee, which is also a customer**, and MD&A references "financing arrangements with a customer." Amounts are small relative to $27.9B of revenue and disclosure is voluntary and specific, but the pattern of a vendor supplying capacity, capital and credit to its own demand is worth tracking if it scales.

**Governance.** Classified board, supermajority provisions, blank-check preferred authority (used in June 2026), no disclosed CEO succession plan. The 2023 CEO performance award vested all five **revenue** milestones ($13B-$21B) and four of five stock-price milestones — revenue-based vesting in a business where incremental revenue consumed $7.6B of cash is a live alignment problem. Four Item 5.02 officer/director departures between December 2025 and May 2026 remain unidentified; Form 4s confirm the CFO and Chief Accounting Officer stayed in seat through July 1, 2026.

**Insider activity.** Across roughly 30 Form 4 filings from February through July 1, 2026, there were **no open-market purchases and no open-market sales** — only vesting, exercises, tax withholding, grants and gifts (Charles Liang and Sara Liu each reported gifting 340,000 shares on May 26, 2026 with identical post-transaction holdings, indicating joint reporting of one position; gifts generate no proceeds). The absence of buying after a ~46% drawdown and immediately following management's bullish preliminary disclosure is relevant; blackout constraints from the active investigation and subpoenas plausibly explain it. Net: neutral-to-mildly-negative, not a signal to lean on.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 108.5% against 91.6% one-year realized (rich by 16.9 points), with a backwardated term structure (108.5% front, 93.6% at three months, 87.8% at six) and a ±15.0% implied move on the nearest expiry. The market is pricing event risk in the front rather than a directional regime change. Skew is call-heavy (–10.9% headline; +3.0% at the 1m tenor, negative at 3m and 6m) and put/call open interest is 0.63, so positioning is not defensively tilted, which is a caution for bears as much as comfort for bulls. The snapshot showing ATM IV jumping from 89.5% to 210.6% is an earnings-day artifact and should not be read as a regime shift. Short interest of 18.4% of float with only 2.1 days to cover means squeeze fuel exists but is easily exhausted; it neither supports a short nor validates the bull case. Practical implication: options are expensive here, so express any view in stock, and expect the front-month vol to collapse once Q1 FY2027 margin guidance is digested.

Risk Factors 3.0: the concentration, control and legal exposures are specific, quantified, and capable of step-function repricing in either direction, not the generic "hardware is cyclical" risk that is permanently priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The June-quarter 17.6% adjusted gross margin reflects the customer and product mix that populates the $60B order book, not a one-off. FY2027 lands at or above the $70B end of guidance, gross margin holds near 15%, and EPS to common reaches roughly $7.90 on ~780M shares. Working capital stops growing as revenue plateaus, operating cash flow inflects positive in FY2027, the $2.0B ATM goes unused, and net debt falls further. The board investigation closes with no adverse finding, material weaknesses remediate, and the multiple re-rates from 9x stale consensus toward 7-8x a much higher number. **Bull target $58** (+84% from $31.60), which coincides with the high analyst target and sits just below the $61.06 conversion price of the 2028 notes.

**Bear case.** The margin quarter was mix. FY2027 revenue lands near $55B as customers continue to slip on power, cooling and networking, gross margin reverts to 8.5%, and EPS to common is ~$2.00. Working capital consumes several billion more, forcing full use of the ATM plus another equity-linked raise at a lower price; share count moves toward 900M. Or the discrete tail hits: the board investigation produces a restatement of prior periods, or one of the four counterparties holding ~$6.3B of receivables against a $488 thousand reserve defaults. At 8x depressed EPS, **bear target $16** (–49%), consistent with the $15 low analyst target and near the $19.48 52-week low.

**Base case.** FY2027 revenue of ~$62B (below guidance, reflecting the demonstrated conversion slippage), gross margin of 12% (between the ~10.5% full-year FY2026 level and the 17.6% June quarter), yielding roughly $4.40 of EPS to common. At 8.5x, reflecting a persistent governance and cash-conversion discount, **base target $37** (+17% from $31.60; +6.6% from the $34.72 last trade).

Probability-weighting bear 30% / base 45% / bull 25% gives an expected value near $36, about +14% from the $31.60 basis and about +3.5% from the actionable ~$34.7. That is fair-to-marginally-positive expected return with a –49% left tail and low confidence in the central estimate. It is a HOLD.

On the drawdown specifically: at ~46% below the 52-week high, the burden of proof sits with the bull, and I do not think it has been met. The sellers are discounting margin durability, cash conversion, dilution, and restatement risk. The first is genuinely unresolved and the full-year margin math leans against the optimistic reading; the second, third and fourth are documented in filings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The rating reflects insufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction, not a positive 12-month thesis and not income (no dividend on the common).

**Targets:** base $37 (+17% from $31.60), bull $58 (+84%), bear $16 (–49%). Timeframe: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q1 FY2027 report (late October / early November 2026), which will disclose gross margin against the $14.5-15.5B revenue guide and the FY2026 full-year cash flow statement in the 10-K.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Gross margin at or above 14% in Q1 FY2027** with management attributing it to sustained mix rather than one-time items — the single test of whether the June quarter was structural.
2. **FY2026 full-year operating cash flow better than –$3.0B, with Q4 FY2026 operating cash flow positive**, plus DSO back below 55 days — evidence the working-capital build is a step function that has crested rather than a recurring cost of growth.
3. **Remediation of at least two of the four material weaknesses** disclosed in the FY2026 10-K, combined with closure of the board investigation with no restatement of prior periods.
4. A price near $24-26 on unchanged fundamentals would restore enough margin of safety to warrant staged accumulation even with items 1-3 unresolved.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q1 FY2027 gross margin below 11%**, which would confirm the June quarter as mix and put the base-case EPS estimate roughly in half.
2. **Any use of the $2.0B ATM below $30, or a new equity-linked raise** — evidence the burn has not decelerated and that dilution is the funding mechanism of record.
3. **A restatement announcement, an adverse finding from the board investigation, or the company becoming a named defendant** in the export-control matter.
4. **A material increase in the allowance for credit losses, or disclosure of a receivable write-off from any of the four concentrated counterparties.** Also watch for the FY2027 CTBC maturity ($1,805.9M) being refinanced on worse terms or not extended.

**Reassessment timeline:** the FY2026 10-K (expected late August / September 2026) for the full-year cash flow statement, June 30 balance sheet, updated risk factors reflecting the June raise and secured debt, and any ICFR change; then the Q1 FY2027 print for the margin test. I would not add capital ahead of either.

## Conclusion

Supermicro is winning volume in the largest hardware buildout in a generation and losing the economics of it. Revenue up 78% to ~$39B, orders above $60B in a single quarter, FY2027 guidance 23-36% above consensus, set against full-year gross margin of ~10.1-10.9% (below the prior year), nine-month operating cash flow of –$7.56B, a first-lien secured revolver, 7% mandatory convertible preferred, and a control environment that has been formally ineffective for six consecutive quarters while the company itself warns that prior-period results may be affected.

At $31.60 the stock is cheap against every margin scenario above 10%, and the June quarter's 17.6% adjusted gross margin makes the bull case arithmetically straightforward. But cheapness here is compensation for identifiable impairment risk rather than evidence of mispricing, and the actionable price after the print is closer to $34.7, which leaves roughly 3-4% of probability-weighted expected return against a –49% left tail. Existing holders have a defensible reason to wait for the Q1 FY2027 margin print, which is the cleanest test available and arrives within roughly eleven weeks. New capital should not be committed until that test resolves, or until the price offers the margin of safety that the governance and cash-conversion profile demands.

**HOLD.** Overall score 4.7, unchanged from the prior review: the guidance raise and margin beat offset the confirmation that full-year margin did not recover and that revenue conversion is slipping.
