**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a Bermuda-domiciled global specialty insurer and reinsurer running three diversified underwriting engines (Insurance, Reinsurance, Mortgage) on ~$26.9B of total capital with minimal financial leverage (debt/total capital 10.1%). The franchise is genuinely top-tier: full-year 2025 book value per share grew 22.6% to $65.11, net income ROAE reached 20.1%, and the mortgage unit posted a fourth consecutive year of >$1B underwriting income. Q1 2026 extended the headline strength: net income to common of $1,037M ($2.88 diluted EPS) versus $564M a year earlier, with a consolidated combined ratio of 81.7%.

The 84% EPS jump in Q1'26 was driven almost entirely by lower catastrophe losses (the comparison quarter absorbed the California wildfires), not by growth. Net premiums written fell in both Insurance (-1.4%) and Reinsurance (-6%, with property cat NPW down 36%) as management deliberately walked away from underpriced business. The cycle has turned from hard to soft, and profitability is increasingly leaning on favorable prior-year reserve development (8.3 points in Reinsurance, 19.2 points in Mortgage). Management's response — quadrupling the buyback pace to $783M in the quarter and adding a $3.0B authorization in April — is the textbook late-cycle move: when underwriting returns compress, return capital. A director's open-market purchase at $94 corroborates that the stock is viewed as reasonable near current levels.

At $91.18, ACGL trades at ~1.38x Q1'26 book value of $66.19 and ~6.99x trailing EPS, with consensus 2026 EPS of $9.28 putting the forward multiple near 9.8x. That is defensible for a 17%+ ROE compounder but not cheap if returns normalize toward the mid-teens as the soft market deepens. Estimate revisions are modestly negative (current-year -1.1% / next-year -2.2% over 90 days), the score has declined across the last four reports, and the prior call already shifted from BUY to HOLD. We maintain HOLD. The business quality is not in question; the timing of the cycle and the durability of peak-cycle earnings are.

## Company Overview

Arch operates three segments designed to let management allocate capital dynamically to whichever offers the best risk-adjusted return — the "cycle management" discipline that defines the franchise:

- **Insurance** — commercial specialty P&C (E&S casualty, professional/financial lines, property and short-tail, workers' comp, commercial auto/multi-peril, construction, programs) across North America, Bermuda, the UK, Europe, and Australia. Materially enlarged by the MCE acquisition (Allianz U.S. MidCorp + Entertainment, closed August 2024 for ~$450M).
- **Reinsurance** — worldwide treaty reinsurance: property cat, property ex-cat, casualty, specialty, marine and aviation. The highest-margin segment (Q1'26 combined ratio 75.9%).
- **Mortgage** — U.S. primary mortgage insurance, GSE credit-risk transfer, and international MI. Capital-light and high-margin, providing diversifying earnings.

Arch also holds minority stakes in affiliated (re)insurers (30% of Greysbridge/Somers Re, ~25% Premia, ~30% Coface), which add earnings and strategic optionality but also create related-party complexity, including a $2.0B reinsurance recoverable from Somers worth tracking for concentration.

## Financial Analysis

**Full-year 2025:** Revenue of ~$19.9B (up from $17.4B in 2024 and $13.6B in 2023), reflecting both hard-market organic premium growth and the MCE acquisition. Net income to common was ~$4.36B; operating ROAE was 17.1%. Debt/total capital sat at just 10.1%, conservative even by industry standards.

**Q1 2026:** The consolidated combined ratio improved to 81.7% from 90.1%, but the swing is dominated by catastrophe normalization rather than underlying improvement. BVPS rose to $66.19 (+1.7% in the quarter, restrained by an AOCI swing to -$333M from +$5M at year-end as rates rose). Net investment income was $408M (+8% YoY) on a conservative book — ~3.99% book yield, 3.43-year duration, AA-/Aa3 average quality. Operating cash flow was $1.19B for the quarter, underpinning the buyback capacity.

**Segment detail tells the real story:**

- **Insurance:** Underwriting income of $66M (versus a $2M loss a year ago), combined ratio 96.5% (from 100.1%). But net premiums written fell 1.4% (would have been +1.1% ex-MCE non-renewals). Management explicitly chose "profitability over volume."
- **Reinsurance:** Underwriting income $441M (+164%), combined ratio a standout 75.9%. NPW fell 6%, with property cat NPW down 36% ($307M vs $477M) as the company retrenched into softening pricing. Favorable prior-year development of 8.3 points flattered the loss ratio.
- **Mortgage:** Underwriting income $221M (-12%), combined ratio rising to 22.3% (from 16.1%). Loss ratio rose to 5.3% from 1.1% as delinquencies tick up; favorable development (19.2 points) remains a large profit component.

FCF/earnings concepts apply imperfectly to insurers, but operating cash conversion is high and the balance sheet is liquid. The earnings surprise record is mixed — three beats and one narrow miss over four quarters (Q1'26 missed by 1.1%, the prior three beat by roughly 9-21%) — though the trajectory of beats is fading as the cat tailwind from prior-year comparisons exhausts.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the screen-level optimism breaks down. Revenue growth from 2022 to 2025 was rapid (~$9.6B to ~$19.9B), but a large share was hard-market pricing plus the MCE acquisition — both of which are now reversing or maturing. The leading indicator of future earned premium, net premiums written, is contracting in two of three segments. That is a deliberate choice, and a good one for protecting return on equity, but it caps top-line growth.

Consensus reflects the deceleration: current-year EPS of $9.28 is below the trailing $11.60, and next-year consensus of $9.89 implies only modest recovery. Estimate revisions are negative across all horizons (current quarter -3.2%, current year -1.1%, next year -2.2% over 90 days), the opposite of the +30% inflection signal that would warrant aggressive cycle-adjusted re-rating. The trajectory points to mean reversion from peak-cycle returns.

The durable growth levers are real but bounded: book value compounding through retained earnings and disciplined buybacks (diluted shares down to 359.7M from 381.9M a year ago), the mortgage segment's structural profitability, and the capacity to redeploy into the next hard market. None of these is a near-term accelerant. The growth score of 5.0 reflects a high-quality compounder in the down-phase of its cycle, with consensus estimates drifting lower rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $91.18, ACGL trades at ~6.99x trailing EPS of $11.60 and ~9.8x consensus 2026 EPS. Against P&C peers (median P/E 9.6 — HIG 9.0, CINF 9.6, WRB 14.3, ORI 9.4), ACGL is roughly in line on a forward basis and at a discount to the higher-quality specialty name WRB.

A sub-7x trailing multiple is not cheap here. The trailing EPS reflects peak-cycle underwriting margins and elevated favorable reserve development, neither of which is sustainable through a softening market. On normalized mid-cycle earnings power — closer to consensus 2026 and with combined ratios reverting toward the high-80s/low-90s — the franchise is fairly valued. The 1.38x book multiple is reasonable for a mid-to-high-teens ROE business but offers no margin of safety if ROE normalizes to ~15%.

This is the inverse of a cycle-adjusted-trough setup: estimate revisions are negative, and the trailing multiple is low because earnings are at a cyclical high, not because the market is missing an inflection. Analyst targets (mean $108.71, median $110.00, range $88-125) imply ~19-21% upside to the mean from current levels, but on a name with decelerating premiums and falling estimates, those targets are not a reliable floor — they reflect the same peak-cycle ROE assumptions now under pressure. We assign a 5.5: balanced, with the burden of proof on the bulls to show where the soft market troughs.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch's diversified, returns-focused model positions it better than monoline cat reinsurers to weather a softening market. The decision to shrink property cat NPW while abundant alternative capital (ILS, sidecars) compresses returns, and to non-renew underpriced MCE program business, is textbook cycle discipline that should protect ROE relative to peers who chase volume. An AM Best report (May 2026) confirmed sector-wide softening reinsurance pricing, and peers (Hamilton's $300M casualty sidecar) illustrate the abundant competing capital.

In mortgage, Arch competes in a consolidated, well-capitalized U.S. MI oligopoly (MGIC, Radian, Essent, NMI) with high in-force credit quality (weighted-average FICO ~750) supporting continued favorable development. PMIERs sufficiency is 175% (173% pro-forma under fully phased-in September 2026 GSE rules) — comfortable but narrowing.

The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-defensive rather than improving. Arch is choosing to shrink rather than gain share aggressively in this environment. That is the right move, but the trajectory is one of disciplined retrenchment, not expansion. The score of 6.5 reflects a durable moat with a flat-to-slightly-defensive trajectory in the current cycle.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cycle softening (dominant near-term risk).** Property cat and short-tail reinsurance rates are declining; the top-line contraction confirms the turn. A multi-year soft market would pressure the elevated ROEs that justify the current multiple.
2. **Casualty/social-inflation reserve risk.** Long-tail casualty reserves ($4.7B third-party occurrence, $2.9B claims-made) are where adverse development surfaces. Q1'26 already showed $20M of adverse casualty development in Reinsurance (2022-24 years). Profitability's heavy reliance on favorable development flatters current combined ratios and bears monitoring.
3. **Catastrophe concentration.** 1-in-250-year net PML is capped at ~25% of tangible equity; peak exposures include Florida Tri-County windstorm $1.9B (8.2% of tangible equity). A major event could meaningfully dent book value.
4. **Mortgage/housing credit.** Delinquency rate 2.06% and rising; affordability constrains originations. The PMIERs cushion is narrowing toward the September 2026 rule change.
5. **Tax volatility.** The new Bermuda 15% regime plus OECD Pillar II structurally raise the tax burden. The Q1'26 effective rate of 8.6% (vs 17.4% a year prior) was boosted by refundable credits and law changes — a moving target that makes any single quarter unreliable for extrapolation.
6. **Interest-rate sensitivity.** A +100bp move reduces fixed-income fair value ~$1.2B; the AOCI swing already weighed on Q1'26 book value growth.

These risks are largely understood and partly priced. The franchise has few binary catalysts; the main vulnerability is the gradual normalization of peak-cycle returns rather than a sudden event. Score: 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 24.2% sits modestly rich to 20.7% one-year historical vol (a 3.5-point spread), and the put/call OI ratio rose to 5.07 with a 5.1% near-term put skew — elevated put demand. This is consistent with hedging of a fairly valued, slow-grinding name rather than a directional bearish bet (put/call *volume* ratio is just 0.07, so the OI skew is structural, not freshly aggressive). The flat term structure (24.2% near vs 22.6% far) signals uncertainty evenly distributed with no specific catalyst priced. IV ticked down from 26.5% over the prior three weeks as price rose 3.2%, indicating resolved near-term uncertainty rather than building stress. Net read: confirms a balanced, range-bound setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-120):** The diversified model and cycle discipline protect ROE through the soft market better than peers expect; mortgage and reinsurance margins hold on continued favorable development; the accelerated buyback (now $3.0B+ authorized) compounds BVPS faster as share count shrinks toward 350M; tax credits keep the effective rate below the headline 15%; and the next hard market arrives sooner than feared. Book value compounds at low-double-digits, and the multiple holds at ~1.5x, supporting analyst targets toward $110-120.

**Bear Case ($75-82):** The soft market deepens into a multi-year cycle; NPW declines accelerate; favorable reserve development reverses as casualty social inflation surfaces (the $20M Q1 adverse development is the first sign); a major catastrophe dents book value; and normalized ROE settles in the low-teens. The trailing ~7x multiple proves to be on peak earnings, and the stock re-rates toward 1.1-1.2x book on ~$9 normalized EPS, implying the high-$70s to low-$80s — near the 52-week low of $82.45.

The base case sits between: a high-quality compounder growing book value mid-single-digits through the down-cycle, fairly valued today, with upside dependent on cycle timing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business quality merits continued ownership, but the cycle has turned and estimate revisions are negative — there is insufficient evidence for a confident directional call in either direction at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net premiums written stabilize or return to growth in Insurance and Reinsurance for two consecutive quarters, signaling the soft market is troughing.
- Consensus 2026/2027 EPS revisions turn positive.
- The stock pulls back toward 1.2x book (~$80) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety against normalized ~$9 EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material adverse casualty reserve development beyond the initial $20M, indicating the favorable-development tailwind is reversing.
- Combined ratios in Reinsurance/Mortgage deteriorate toward the high-80s/90s as development normalizes, confirming peak-cycle earnings are unwinding faster than book-value compounding can offset.
- A capital-eroding catastrophe event that pushes book value down materially while the soft market limits the rebuild.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the key checkpoint — watch NPW trajectory, the favorable-development contribution to combined ratios, and any commentary on July 1 reinsurance renewal pricing. The September 2026 PMIERs phase-in is a secondary input for the mortgage segment.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital is one of the better-run specialty (re)insurers, with a genuinely diversified model, low leverage, 17%+ ROE, and a management team executing a textbook late-cycle pivot to buybacks. The underwriting cycle has turned: premiums are shrinking by design, headline earnings are flattered by catastrophe normalization and heavy favorable reserve development, and consensus estimates are drifting lower. At ~1.38x book and ~9.8x forward earnings, the stock is fairly valued against normalized returns, not the bargain the sub-7x trailing multiple implies, and not the inflection setup that would justify a cycle-adjusted re-rating.

The score trajectory (declining across the last four reports, with the prior call already moved from BUY to HOLD) reflects this turn, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The reasonable course is to own the franchise for its book-value compounding and capital discipline while waiting for either a better entry near 1.2x book or evidence that the soft market is troughing. The director's open-market purchase at $94 and the aggressive buyback offer some downside support, but neither resolves the central question of where peak-cycle earnings normalize.
