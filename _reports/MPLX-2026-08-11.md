## Executive Summary

MPLX is a $59.6B-market-cap midstream partnership whose economics rest on two unlike halves. Roughly two-thirds of segment EBITDA comes from crude and refined-product logistics contracted almost entirely to Marathon Petroleum — third-party revenue in that segment was $157M of $1,688M in Q2 2026 (9.3%) — on take-or-pay throughput, storage and marine capacity terms that make the cash flow highly predictable and growth structurally capped near contractual escalators. Q2 illustrated the trade cleanly: total pipeline throughput fell 3.7% year over year, average tariffs were roughly flat at $1.07/bbl, and segment EBITDA still rose 2.0% on $77M of higher rates and fees. The other third is a genuinely competitive, 91%-third-party natural gas and NGL franchise growing gathering volumes 12.9% in the Marcellus and 14.8% in the Southwest, absorbing 84% of segment capital expenditure.

The decision does not hinge on business quality. It hinges on the gap between distribution growth and cash-flow growth. The Q2 2026 distribution of $1.0765 per unit is 12.55% above the year-ago rate, while 1H 2026 Adjusted EBITDA attributable to MPLX grew 1.65% ($3,504M vs $3,447M) and distributable cash flow *declined* 1.65% ($2,858M vs $2,906M). Coverage fell from 1.49x in 1H 2025 to 1.31x in 1H 2026, and 1.46x to 1.33x in the June quarter. The partnership funded the first half by drawing cash from $2,137M to $1,031M while gross debt was essentially unchanged at $26,005M — MPLX's own disclosure puts Adjusted free cash flow after distributions at −$968M for the half and −$424M in Q2. On an LTM basis, after-distribution free cash flow is roughly −$3.8B, and even excluding net M&A the organic figure is about −$1.65B. This is the MLP model working as designed, not a liquidity event: liquidity totals $5,031M and ratings are BBB/Baa2/BBB stable. But it means the 2026–2027 plan requires incremental debt, and it is being executed while the cost of that debt rises. One $1.5B refinancing in February 2026 — $1.0B of 5.300% 2036s and $500M of 6.100% 2056s replacing 1.750% notes — added $57M of annual interest by itself; consolidated interest expense rose 30% year over year in 1H.

At $58.85 the units yield 7.32% on the annualized $4.306 distribution, trade at roughly 11.9x LTM EV/Adjusted EBITDA (12.2x including MPLX's $2.2B share of JV-level debt), 10.4x LTM DCF, and 12.3x the $4.80 next-year consensus. Consensus is moving the wrong way: current-year EPS estimates are down 5.0% in 30 days and 3.2% in 90 days, next-year down 2.6% and 1.8%, and the mean analyst target of $61.46 sits 4.4% above spot with a median of $60.00. The units are 3.4% below the 52-week high after a 16.9% twelve-month advance. Probability-weighted total return over twelve months is roughly 7–8% — essentially the coupon — with a bear case near $47.50 that is fully plausible if the long end stays where it is. Own it for the distribution; do not put new capital to work at this price.

## Company Overview

MPLX LP, formed by Marathon Petroleum in 2012 and headquartered in Findlay, Ohio, operates two reportable segments:

- **Crude Oil and Products Logistics** — pipelines, terminals, refining logistics, fuels distribution, inland marine (331 barges, 30 towboats at 6/30/26), rail, storage caverns. Q2 2026 segment Adjusted EBITDA $1,161M (65.4% of reportable-segment total).
- **Natural Gas and NGL Services** — gathering, treating, processing, fractionation, NGL pipelines and marketing. Q2 2026 segment Adjusted EBITDA $614M, on 84.0% of Q2 segment capex ($688M of $819M).

The MPC relationship is the defining structural feature and it is multi-directional. MPC owns the general partner and approximately 64% of limited partner units (647M of 1,014M), accounted for 50% of total revenues and other income in Q2 2026 (up from 49% a year earlier), supplies executive management and back-office services under omnibus and employee-services agreements, acts as MPLX's treasury agent, is a derivative counterparty, provides a $1.5B demand loan facility, and receives approximately 64% of distributions — $1,394M of the $2,184M declared in 1H 2026 (63.8%), or roughly $2.79B annualized at the current rate. Of $4,168M in minimum future rentals receivable, $3,643M (87.4%) is from MPC.

The 2025 portfolio reshaping added the competitive half: Northwind Midstream (closed 8/29/25, $2,399M cash, sour gas treating in Lea County NM expanding from 150 MMcf/d toward over 400 MMcf/d by 2H 2026), the remaining 55% of BANGL (7/1/25, $703M plus a cash-settled earnout carried at $235M), Whiptail ($235M), and an additional 5% of Matterhorn ($151M), funded largely by the August 2025 $4.5B notes issue and partly offset by the $980M Rockies divestiture to Harvest Midstream in November 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q2 2026 total revenues and other income of $3,312M rose 10.3% year over year; income from operations rose 6.6% to $1,378M; net income attributable to MPLX rose 2.8% to $1,077M ($1.06 diluted per unit). The half tells a different story: revenue +3.6%, income from operations −2.5% ($2,592M vs $2,659M), net income attributable −8.5% ($1,989M vs $2,174M), diluted EPS $1.96 vs $2.13. The implied Q1 2026 result was $912M, or $0.90 per unit, against $1.10 a year earlier — an 18% decline that shows up in the earnings record as a 15.6% miss.

The 1H bridge is identifiable and mostly structural: net interest and other financial costs +$117M, D&A +$73M, the Rockies divestiture removing roughly $79M of segment EBITDA, the absence of a $37M non-recurring customer-agreement benefit and a $25M JV-formation gain booked in 1H 2025, and $33M of adverse NGL pricing and derivative effects — offset by $77M from acquisitions and $56M from equity-method investments.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income attributable to MPLX of $4,912M was up $595M year over year, but the year contained roughly $668M of non-recurring gains (BANGL remeasurement ~$484M, Rockies sale ~$159M, Texas City JV formation $25M) against roughly $171M in FY2024. That swing accounts for the large majority of the reported increase, while Adjusted EBITDA grew about 3.7%. The Q3 2025 headline "beat" ($1.52 versus a ~$1.09 estimate) is largely explained by the BANGL remeasurement — roughly $0.48 per unit — which leaves the underlying quarter at or below estimate. The individual gain amounts derive from the FY2025 10-K and are not independently re-verifiable in the Q2 10-Q beyond the $25M Texas City item and the $766M BANGL step-up methodology; treat the exact split as approximate but the direction as well supported. GAAP earnings comparisons for this partnership are noisy; DCF and Adjusted EBITDA are the operative metrics.

**Cash flow and the funding gap.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $3,049M (vs $2,982M), including a $190M working-capital draw, so it is not a clean run rate. Additions to PP&E jumped to $1,400M from $568M; investments in unconsolidated affiliates were $441M; distributions to LP unitholders $2,185M; buybacks $100M. Cash fell $1,106M to $1,031M with gross debt flat at $26,005M. MPLX's disclosed Adjusted FCF after distributions was −$968M for the half.

At the half-year burn rate the remaining cash cushion covers roughly two quarters — but that is a sensitivity, not a forecast. Management guides 2026 EBITDA growth as second-half weighted; Harmon Creek III starts in August 2026 and Northwind's treating expansion progresses toward >400 MMcf/d in 2H. Both are capacity-additive and dated. The reasonable conclusion is that MPLX will fund the balance of the plan with incremental debt in 2H 2026 or 2027, against $5,031M of stated liquidity ($2,500M undrawn revolver maturing April 2031, $1,500M undrawn MPC demand facility, $1,031M cash). Note the composition: the MPC facility, extended to July 2029 at SOFR+1.25%, is repayable on demand by the controlling owner, which makes it a qualitatively weaker liquidity source than the committed bank line.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $42,969M, liabilities $28,717M, equity $14,252M at 6/30/26. Debt of $26,005M is essentially all fixed-rate senior notes maturing 2027–2058 at 2.650%–6.200% coupons, with no variable-rate exposure and no open interest-rate derivatives. Gross debt to LTM Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $7,074M is 3.68x; net 3.53x; adding MPLX's $2.2B share of non-recourse JV debt lifts effective leverage to about 3.8x. That compares to roughly 3.1x gross at year-end 2024 — approximately 0.6 turns added across 2025 to fund acquisitions, with no deleveraging in 1H 2026. For large-cap midstream, 3.5–3.7x sits at peer norms; the issue is direction, not level.

Two balance-sheet items deserve flagging. Goodwill of $8,736M is 20.3% of assets and 61.3% of book equity; with $1,320M of intangibles, tangible partners' capital is roughly $4.2B, and "further impairments" appears in the 10-Q forward-looking risk enumeration. And the bonds' fair value of $24,673M against $25,816M carrying value — roughly 4.4% below par — signals that the low-coupon tranches maturing over the next several years reprice upward on refinancing. That is a multi-year, recurring DCF headwind and the main reason 1H DCF fell while Adjusted EBITDA grew.

**Distribution mechanics.** Q2 2026 distribution of $1.0765 per unit ($1,092M) is 12.55% above the year-ago $0.9565. Annualized $4.306 on $58.85 is a 7.32% yield. DCF coverage: Q2 1.33x (vs 1.46x), 1H 1.31x (vs 1.49x), LTM 1.31x, FY2025 1.40x. The partnership agreement requires distribution of "available cash" as defined but the filing states there is no legal obligation to distribute any particular amount per unit.

If the reported 12.5% pace holds into 2027, the run-rate distribution reaches roughly $4.84 per unit, about $4,912M in aggregate. Against LTM DCF of $5,743M growing 5%, coverage lands near 1.23x; at 3% growth, near 1.20x. Retained cash of roughly $1.1B against a $2.9B growth plan implies about $1.8B of incremental annual debt, which holds leverage roughly flat at ~3.74x while eliminating any cushion. There is no equity-dilution offset available on the current structure — the Series A preferred fully converted in February 2025, the BANGL earnout is cash-settled, and anti-dilutive potential units are under one million. That is a genuine improvement over the historic equity-funded MLP model, and it also means debt is the only funding lever.

Buybacks are immaterial to the story: $100M in 1H 2026 (2M units at $56.32 average) versus $200M in 1H 2025, with $1.0B remaining authorized. Units outstanding fell 0.16% in six months.

## Growth Analysis

The growth that exists is concentrated and dated. Marcellus gathering volumes rose 12.9% to 1,680 MMcf/d, Southwest gathering 14.8% to 1,990 MMcf/d, C2+ fractionated volumes 5.9% to 608 mbpd, and consolidated NGL pipeline throughput 52% to 719 mbpd on BANGL consolidation. Harmon Creek III begins operations in August 2026; Permian sour gas treating expands toward >400 MMcf/d in 2H. Management raised 2026 growth capital by $500M to $2.9B ($3.2B total net of reimbursements), specifically to accelerate Gulf Coast fractionation and export capacity.

Against that, the legacy segment is running on escalators with declining volumes: crude pipeline throughput 3,830 mbpd from 4,012 (−4.5%), products 2,046 from 2,091 (−2.2%). The filings do not attribute the decline, and MPC refinery turnaround timing is a plausible explanation, but the growth mechanism in the larger segment is contractual rate increases rather than volume — durable, and capped.

The consensus view is deteriorating rather than improving. Current-quarter EPS estimates are down 9.4% in 30 days and 4.5% in 90 days; current year down 5.0% and 3.2% to $4.32; next year down 2.6% and 1.8% to $4.80. This is the mirror image of the estimate-revision pattern that signals an earnings inflection, and it should be weighted accordingly. The three-of-four-quarter shortfall record (Q1 2026 −15.6%, Q2 2026 −3.7%) reduces confidence that the second-half-weighted guidance lands at the top of its range.

Structurally, the demand thesis management articulates is credible — Gulf Coast LNG feedgas, rising Permian gas-oil ratios, international ethane and LPG demand, and incremental power generation for data centers. Independent confirmation exists in Energy Transfer's Nederland expansion (240,000 bpd ethane, 55,000 bpd LPG) being described as fully subscribed before construction. The complication is that the same evidence identifies the competition: Phillips 66 took FID on the Zeus gas plant and a third Coastal Bend fractionator using nearly identical "wellhead-to-market" framing in the same corridor and month. MPLX does not disclose contracted coverage on its specific Gulf Coast expansion projects, which is exactly the evidence that would distinguish a tight market from a capacity race.

## Competitive Landscape

The affiliate-contracted logistics franchise is defensible in the sense that it is embedded in MPC's refining system under take-or-pay and capacity terms, including a fixed fee for 100% of available marine capacity and fuels-distribution services used solely by MPC. Whether that constitutes a moat or simply a contract set by the counterparty that controls the board is the central interpretive question, and Q2 2026 provided a data point: a refining-logistics agreement between MPLX and MPC was renewed, triggering ASC 842 reassessment and reclassification of the lease from sales-type to operating — recognizing $216M of PP&E, reducing the lease receivable by $102M and the unguaranteed residual asset by $100M. Mechanically, that reclassification is why rental income rose $62M in Q2 while sales-type lease revenue fell $27M. The disclosure does not quantify any change in cash fees, and the accounting treatment says nothing about whether the renewed economics are better or worse for MPLX. What it does establish is that these terms are periodically renegotiated with a counterparty that appoints the board.

In the competitive half, MPLX's Marcellus/Utica processing position (6,232 MMcf/d operated Marcellus plus 964 MMcf/d Utica in Q2) is genuinely scaled, and the Permian-to-Gulf-Coast chain assembled through Northwind, BANGL and Matterhorn is coherent. The concentration trajectory, however, runs the wrong way for the "diversification" reading: capital is flowing to the third-party segment, but directly measured MPC revenue share rose to 50% from 49% in the quarter and 50% from 48% for the half. Capex mix establishes intent; revenue share measures present concentration, and it worsened.

Competitive position scores 5.0 — stable-to-slightly-improving in the growth segment, capped and volume-declining in the legacy segment, with concentration increasing and simultaneous peer capacity additions in the corridor MPLX is accelerating into.

## Risk Assessment

**MPC dependence, increasing.** 50% of revenue, 87.4% of minimum future lease rentals, 63.8% of distributions received. The 10-Q explicitly enumerates "the suspension, reduction or termination of MPC's obligations under MPLX's commercial agreements" and the effects of MPC's portfolio optimization and divestitures. MPC's current earnings strength (Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $8.5B, adjusted EPS $17.73 on refining cracks) reduces near-term counterparty risk, though MPC consolidates MPLX so those results are not independent.

**Coverage compression against accelerating distributions.** The cleanest quantified risk: 1.49x to 1.31x in twelve months, with distributions growing 12.5% against 1.65% EBITDA growth and −1.65% DCF. Either the 2025–2026 capital program converts and DCF inflects, or coverage grinds toward 1.20x, or the distribution pace slows. Any of the three is survivable; the third would reprice the units.

**Refinancing repricing.** $57M of incremental annual interest from one $1.5B refinancing; interest expense +30% year over year; bonds trading 4.4% below par with 2.650% coupons still to mature. This is recurring and unhedgeable.

**Governance.** No DEF 14A exists. Unitholders do not elect directors, do not vote on compensation, and have no proxy-based related-party disclosure. MPC's CEO chairs and leads the GP. Related-party G&A charged by MPC rose 9% in 1H to $170M, MPC-charged engineering capitalized into PP&E rose to $117M from $95M, and MPLX attributes cost inflation partly to "higher employee costs from MPC." The amounts are modest relative to $1,775M of quarterly EBITDA and are plausibly genuine labor pass-throughs, but they are set by the controlling owner.

**Commodity and contract specifics.** Q2 NYMEX Henry Hub averaged $2.94/MMBtu versus $3.51 (−16%); C2+ NGL $0.90/gal versus $0.80 (+12.5%). A keep-whole processing agreement with a Southern Appalachia producer expiring December 2027 carries an embedded derivative liability of $47M valued assuming 100% probability that the customer exercises its unilateral five-year extension — a carrying value contingent on a counterparty's future choice.

**Joint ventures without control.** $5,149M of equity-method investments (12% of assets) contributed $180M of Q2 income while distributions and adjustments of $234M contributed more to Adjusted EBITDA than reported equity income. MPLX is not the primary beneficiary of several VIEs because significant decisions require other investors' consent.

**Named contingencies.** Tesoro High Plains Pipeline BIA trespass (originally ~$187M assessed, ~$4M paid, litigation over the 2021 vacatur continuing); Dakota Access (9.19% indirect, final EIS and May 2026 Record of Decision, with the filing warning new litigation may follow, ~$78M maximum contingent equity contribution); WPC Parent $109M indemnity; IRS examination of 2019–2022 returns; $17M environmental accrual. Newly specific items in the 2026 forward-looking enumeration include U.S.-Iran conflict commodity volatility, One Big Beautiful Bill Act tax guidance, California margin-penalty and cap-and-invest regimes, and competitor infrastructure completion alongside industry overcapacity.

**Unresolved gaps.** Five 8-K Item 5.02 director/officer events in about fourteen months whose specifics could not be retrieved (routine for a parent-appointed GP board, but unverified); the multi-year 12.5% distribution commitment and mid-single-digit EBITDA guidance are secondary reporting not confirmed in a filing; the terminal-services agreement amendment (Exhibit 10.2) content was not reviewed.

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 23.3% against 17.0% one-year realized is rich by 6.3 points, in backwardation (23.3% at nine days versus 20.0% at six months), with a ±3.5% implied move on the nearest expiry. For a holder, selling upside against units near the 52-week high is priced attractively; for a buyer, rich near-term vol offers no timing advantage. This neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($70, +19% price, ~+26% total return with distributions).** Second-half 2026 EBITDA inflects as guided: Harmon Creek III enters service in August, Northwind treating scales past 400 MMcf/d, BANGL and Gulf Coast fractionation volumes ramp, and LTM DCF grows 6–8% rather than declining. Coverage stabilizes near 1.30–1.35x even with a 12.5% distribution increase, validating the capital program and removing the compression narrative. Rates ease, and a $4.84 run-rate 2027 distribution capitalizes at a 6.9% yield. The absence of unit dilution means all of that accrues per unit. Gulf Coast export demand proves as tight as Energy Transfer's fully-subscribed Nederland expansion implies, and MPLX's incremental $500M earns its return.

**Bear case ($47.50, −19% price, ~−12% total return).** Second-half conversion slips — a recurring pattern for large midstream projects, and consensus has already cut current-year EPS 5.0% in 30 days. Coverage grinds to 1.20–1.23x by 2027 while incremental debt of roughly $1.8B a year holds leverage at 3.7–3.8x with no cushion. The long end stays at current levels or rises further, so each maturing low-coupon tranche reprices materially higher and a 7.3% yield is no longer competitive against Treasuries plus a widening credit spread. Management slows the distribution pace to 5% to protect coverage, and the market re-rates the units to a 9.5% yield on $4.52. Any goodwill impairment against $8.7B carried on $14.3B of equity, or an adverse renegotiation of an MPC logistics agreement, deepens it. The 52-week low of $47.80 is a reasonable anchor.

**Base case ($60.50, +2.8% price, ~+10% total return).** Second-half EBITDA improves but delivers mid-single-digit growth at best, DCF grows 3–4%, and coverage settles near 1.25–1.30x. The distribution grows toward $4.84 by 2027 while the market demands roughly an 8.0% yield to compensate for thinner coverage and higher leverage — arithmetic that yields approximately $60.50. Total return is roughly the distribution.

Probability weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives an expected total return near +7.7%, delivered almost entirely by the coupon rather than by re-rating. That is approximately fair compensation for a 3.5x-levered, single-counterparty-concentrated income vehicle with negative skew — the definition of a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q3 2026 report (late October/early November 2026) and the Q4/full-year report (February 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if any two of the following occur:**
1. **Coverage stabilizes or improves.** Reported DCF coverage of 1.35x or better for a quarter, or LTM DCF growth of 5%+ year over year, confirming that Harmon Creek III and the Northwind treating expansion are converting on schedule.
2. **Price provides the margin of safety.** Units at $52–54 (yield 8.0–8.3% on the current $4.306 rate, and 9.0%+ on a $4.84 2027 run rate), which would restore roughly 15% upside to the base case.
3. **Estimate revisions turn positive.** Next-year consensus rising rather than falling for two consecutive months, reversing the current −2.6% 30-day trend.
4. **Rate relief.** A sustained decline in long-end rates, materially reducing the refinancing headwind on 2027–2030 maturities and compressing the required yield.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following occur:**
1. **Coverage falls below 1.20x** on a quarterly or LTM basis while the distribution continues to rise, or management raises the distribution again with DCF flat to declining.
2. **Incremental debt exceeds roughly $2.0B in a twelve-month window** without corresponding EBITDA growth, taking net leverage above 3.9x (4.1x including JV debt), or any negative ratings outlook change from Fitch, Moody's or S&P.
3. **Adverse affiliate contract outcome** — a renewed MPC logistics or terminal-services agreement disclosed with quantifiably lower fees, or a reduction in MPC minimum volume commitments.
4. **Legacy volume decline broadens** — crude and products throughput down more than 5% year over year for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural rather than turnaround-driven erosion, since escalator-driven growth cannot offset volume loss indefinitely.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release. That print is the first clean test of the second-half-weighted guidance and the first quarter in which Harmon Creek III contributes (the plant begins operations in August 2026, within Q3).

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold for the distribution; consider writing covered calls into rich near-term implied volatility given the units sit 3.4% below the 52-week high with a mean target 4.4% above spot. Note the K-1 and taxable-income allocation characteristics — a suitability constraint for tax-exempt accounts that the filings do not quantify.

## Conclusion

MPLX is a well-run, investment-grade midstream partnership whose distribution is currently covered 1.31x and whose funding structure no longer relies on equity issuance. Those are real strengths, and they are why this is a hold rather than a reduce. The reason it is not a buy is that the distribution is growing at roughly seven to ten times the rate of the cash flow that funds it, the gap is being bridged with debt whose cost is rising on each refinancing, consensus estimates are falling in every forecast period, and the units trade at a full 11.9x EV/EBITDA and 12.3x next-year earnings within 4% of the mean analyst target. The expected twelve-month total return is approximately the 7.3% coupon, against a plausible bear case of −12% total return if second-half conversion slips or the long end stays where it is.

The scoring reflects that shape: financial health 5.4 (peer-normal leverage and strong liquidity against structural after-distribution cash deficits and compressing coverage), growth prospects 5.0 (dated, capex-funded volume growth offset by negative revisions and escalator-capped legacy earnings), valuation 4.5 (full multiple, no identified mispricing, absolute yield support), competitive position 5.0 (improving growth segment, rising concentration, simultaneous peer capacity additions), management quality 4.5 (no unitholder vote, no proxy, distribution pace ahead of cash generation with ~64% flowing to the controlling owner, insider activity limited to grants and tax withholding with no open-market conviction in either direction), and risk factors 4.5 (concentration, affiliate renegotiation, refinancing and coverage risks that are only partly priced at a 7.3% yield). Overall 4.86.

**HOLD.** Collect the distribution, monitor coverage at the Q3 print, and wait for $52–54 or evidence of DCF inflection before committing new capital.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $58.85
- Fair value: $60.50
- Margin of safety: +2.8%
- Initiate/add below: $54.00
- Reduce/exit above: $70.00
- Rationale: Fair value anchors to the base case: a ~$4.84 2027 run-rate distribution capitalized near an 8.0% required yield on thinner coverage and higher leverage. Roughly 3% above spot is no margin of safety.

**Thesis:** Two-thirds of EBITDA is MPC-contracted logistics growing only on contractual escalators against declining throughput, while the third-party gas/NGL half grows but absorbs 84% of segment capex. Distributions are rising ~12.5% while 1H Adjusted EBITDA grew 1.65% and DCF fell 1.65%, so coverage slid from 1.49x to 1.31x and the gap is bridged with debt that reprices upward on each refinancing. The twelve-month return is the coupon, not a re-rating.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats MPLX as a safe compounding yield vehicle. We read it as a levered, single-counterparty income annuity whose distribution growth runs 7-10x its cash-flow growth; the funding gap is debt, and each maturing low-coupon tranche is a recurring DCF headwind that caps multiple expansion.

### Catalysts

- **Medium — Late Oct / early Nov 2026:** Q3 2026 print is the first quarter including Harmon Creek III and the Northwind treating ramp toward >400 MMcf/d, the clean test of second-half-weighted EBITDA guidance.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **Q3 or Q4 2026 print:** Reported DCF coverage falls below 1.20x on a quarterly or LTM basis while the distribution is raised again.
- **Next 12 months:** Incremental debt above ~$2.0B in a twelve-month window without matching EBITDA growth, net leverage above 3.9x, or a negative ratings outlook.
- **Next two prints:** Crude and products throughput down more than 5% year over year for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural rather than turnaround-driven erosion.
