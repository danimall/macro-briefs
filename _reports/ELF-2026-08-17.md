## Executive Summary

e.l.f. Beauty has become two businesses wearing one income statement. The consolidated numbers look excellent: Q1 FY2027 net sales of $479.4M (+35.5%), FY2027 guidance raised to $1.938-1.968B (+18-20%), adjusted EPS guidance raised to $3.50-3.55 from $3.27-3.32, and a 30th consecutive quarter of net sales growth. Underneath, the FY2026 10-K MD&A attributes $293.5M of the $323.0M full-year revenue increase to rhode, leaving the pre-existing business up $29.5M, or 2.24%. In Q1 FY2027, using the ~$160M rhode contribution reported from the earnings call, the non-rhode business declined roughly 9.7% — corroborated independently as a "high-single-digit" organic decline ex-rhode — while Circana reports US mass beauty dollars grew 7% and mass makeup grew 5% in H1 2026. The flagship is losing share in a growing category.

The Q1 headline also requires deflating. The 10-Q discloses a $50.1M IEEPA tariff refund recorded within cost of goods sold plus $2.1M of interest income. Stripping the refund puts underlying gross margin at 72.75% versus 69.13% a year earlier — a genuine 362bp improvement — but roughly 1,050bp of the reported 1,410bp expansion was the refund. Adjusting operating income both ways (removing the refund, adding back the $16.1M contingent-consideration charge) gives 14.27% of sales versus a comparable 13.77% prior year: about 50bp of like-for-like improvement, not 760bp. Management has said the refund is being fully reinvested into pricing and marketing, with marketing and digital spend running above 25% of sales for the balance of FY2027. Consistent with that, consensus for the next two quarters was cut 14.5% and 28.5% over 30 days even as the full-year number rose 9.4% — the "raise" is arithmetically the Q1 windfall.

At $91.44 the stock trades at 25.9x the $3.525 FY2027 guidance midpoint and 23.8x next-year consensus of $3.84, against roughly 20.7x for the cosmetics sub-industry and a 22.3x median for the personal-care peers screened here. EV of ~$5.88B ($5.96B including the $80.8M contingent consideration) is ~17.5-17.8x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $335.2M — a multiple that requires the rhode-led growth to hold and the legacy business to stabilize. The stock is up 87% from its $48.82 June low. The improvement in fundamentals over that period is real but narrower than the price move; base-case fair value now sits approximately at spot with a materially larger bear case than bull case. We move to REDUCE from four consecutive HOLDs — a price-versus-value call, not a claim that the franchise is impaired.

## Company Overview

e.l.f. Beauty is a value-priced, multi-brand beauty company reporting as a **single segment**. The 10-K states it is "impracticable" to provide revenue by product line and that the CEO, as chief operating decision maker, evaluates the business on consolidated net income. Brand-level revenue therefore cannot be verified in filings; everything known about rhode, Naturium, or e.l.f. SKIN individually comes from earnings-call commentary.

The portfolio is e.l.f. Cosmetics, e.l.f. SKIN, rhode, Naturium, and Well People. Keys Soulcare was transferred to Alicia Keys in May 2026 (amount unquantified), and e.l.f. Hair launched in June 2026. The flagship's average US price point is approximately $7 versus ~$10 for other leading mass brands and ~$30 for prestige, per Nielsen data cited in Item 1. The same section discloses that e.l.f. Cosmetics held four of the top ten new products in US mass cosmetics in 2025, down from six of ten in 2024 — a small, self-disclosed data point on innovation productivity.

The model is asset-light: 849 full-time employees, all manufacturing outsourced (primarily to third parties in China, with US, Italy and South Korea sourcing), all distribution run by third-party logistics providers, and FY2026 capex of $22.4M on $1,636.5M of sales (1.4%). FY2026 channel mix was 76% retailers / 24% e-commerce; geography 79% US / 21% international. Marketing and digital spend was $399.8M, about 24% of net sales.

Customer concentration is the structural feature that never improves: Target 18%, Walmart 13%, Amazon 11%, Sephora 10% — 52% of FY2026 net sales across four accounts, all transacted on discrete purchase orders with no long-term commitment. Management frames the shift toward non-e.l.f. brands (now >30% of sales, from <1% three years ago) and skincare (from ~10% to nearly 25%) as diversification. That is accurate at the brand and category level and inaccurate at the channel level: rhode's expansion runs through Sephora, e.l.f. SKIN's through Target and Dollar General. Brand risk is being reduced; gatekeeper risk is not.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** $578.8M (FY23) → $1,023.9M (+76.9%) → $1,313.5M (+28.3%) → $1,636.5M (+24.6%). The composition is the issue. FY2026 price/mix added $333.5M while volume subtracted $10.5M; a global price increase took effect 8/1/2025. Q1 FY2027 repeated the pattern: price/mix +$137.4M, volume −$11.7M. Two consecutive periods of dollar growth with declining units, in a category where Circana shows unit demand positive across mass and prestige, is the single most important operating fact in this file.

**Margins.** The company's own preferred non-GAAP measure, disclosed in the proxy pay-versus-performance table, shows deleverage: adjusted EBITDA of $234.7M (FY24), $296.8M (FY25), $335.2M (FY26) against revenue of $1,023.9M, $1,313.5M, $1,636.5M — margins of 22.9%, 22.6%, 20.5%. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA grew 12.9% on 24.6% revenue growth. GAAP operating margin fell from 14.6% (FY24) to 12.0% (FY25) to 4.5% (FY26).

The FY2026 GAAP compression has identifiable drivers that are partly non-cash or non-recurring: a $57.6M non-cash remeasurement of the rhode earnout (a charge that occurs *because* rhode outperformed), $35.0M higher D&A from rhode intangibles, ~$58.5M of IEEPA tariffs since substantially refunded, interest expense up to $35.3M from $13.8M, and an effective tax rate of 35.0% versus 23.0%. But the SG&A ratio moved 56.1% → 59.2% → 62.7%, and adjusted SG&A in Q1 FY2027 was reported at 54% of sales versus 50%. Operating leverage is running backwards even after normalizing.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $71.2M (FY24) → $133.8M (FY25) → $212.5M (FY26); FY2026 FCF of $190.1M is 11.6% of sales and $3.22 per share. Q1 FY2027 OCF was $111.7M on $1.4M capex, but roughly $52.2M of that is refund cash; ex-refund Q1 OCF was approximately $59.5M. Annualizing either figure would be wrong — the 10-K states Q3 and Q4 are the seasonally largest quarters and that working capital builds in Q1/Q2. Q1 also drew down accrued marketing ($43.3M → $16.0M) and accrued compensation ($41.9M → $21.6M) while taxes payable rose from $9.0M to $38.4M, a source that reverses. The defensible normalized base is the FY2026 $190M FCF level plus modest underlying improvement, which implies a ~3.5% FCF yield on today's market cap.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash $344.2M, total debt $834.2M (revolver $256.7M + term loan $577.5M), net debt $489.9M. Gross debt / FY2026 adjusted EBITDA is 2.49x; net 1.46x. Quarterly interest expense net of $2.1M income implies roughly $40M annualized gross interest, giving ~8.4x coverage against a 3.50:1.00 minimum interest-coverage covenant — ample headroom, with the caveat that credit-agreement EBITDA differs from company adjusted EBITDA and the maximum net leverage threshold (raised by the Fifth Amendment on 8/5/2025) is not quantified in the disclosure reviewed. The maturity ladder is manageable: $22.5M remaining FY2027, $30.0M FY2028, $37.5M FY2029, then a $744.2M FY2030 cliff. Effective rate ~5.4%. Revolver undrawn capacity $243.3M. In compliance with all covenants.

Two balance-sheet items deserve weight. Goodwill of $853.5M plus net intangibles of $542.0M equals $1,395.5M, or 56.7% of total assets, tested as a **single reporting unit** with an October 1 annual test date. Tangible book value is negative $226.6M. Because impairment testing aggregates everything into one unit, rhode's strength currently shields any weakness in the legacy business — and the reverse would apply if rhode stumbled, putting the full goodwill balance at risk without segment ring-fencing.

Second, inventory of $246.8M at 6/30/2026 is up 44.9% year over year and 12.1% sequentially against 35.5% sales growth. There are benign explanations — e.l.f. Hair's June launch, the autumn rhode rollout into 19 European Sephora markets, Boots UK, Sephora Brazil, Dollar General for e.l.f. SKIN — and right-of-use assets obtained in the quarter of $20.7M versus $2.1M supports the expansion reading. This is the metric that will confirm or refute a sell-through problem at the November print.

**Screening correction.** A commonly cited Altman Z-Score of 2.21 for ELF substitutes book equity for both retained earnings and market equity. Using Altman's original public-company specification on the 3/31/2026 balance sheet and current market capitalization — WC/TA 0.1893, RE/TA −0.0651, EBIT/TA 0.0308, MVE/TL 4.348, Sales/TA 0.6835 — Z ≈ 3.53, above the 2.99 "safe" threshold. Solvency is not the risk here; organic demand and multiple compression are.

**Beat record.** Four consecutive beats: +18.3%, +69.7%, +10.8%, +143.1%. The two largest were tariff- and mix-assisted, which reduces the read-through of the streak to a clean forecasting-quality signal. Notably, current-year consensus of $3.61 now sits *above* management's $3.50-3.55 guidance midpoint.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on three legs, only one of which is currently working.

**rhode distribution runway (working).** rhode is in fewer than 20% of Sephora's global store base and launches across 19 European countries in autumn 2026, plus Mecca in Australia. Its June summer range generated a reported $27M of DTC sales in a single day with 90,000 new consumers and 70% repeat buyers. rhode contributed $293.5M over the ~8 months from 8/5/2025 to 3/31/2026, an annualized run-rate of roughly $440M; if rhode moves toward management's stated $1B ambition, that alone is a substantial contribution to consolidated growth. The acquisition was struck at approximately 2.0x deal-year revenue run-rate ($897.5M total consideration against ~$440M annualized) — an attractive price for an asset growing this fast, which is precisely why the earnout is marking up.

**Category-mix repositioning (working, directionally).** Circana's H1 2026 data shows mass skincare outperforming overall mass beauty with dollar and unit gains, mass fragrance +15% dollars / +7% units, and mass makeup the weakest at +5% dollars with units declining. e.l.f. Cosmetics is a makeup brand; rhode, e.l.f. SKIN (#11 US mass skincare) and Naturium (fastest-growing in the top 50) are skincare. Moving skincare from ~10% to ~25% of sales is aligned with where the category is actually expanding.

**Legacy stabilization (not yet evidenced).** The non-rhode business declined roughly 9.7% in Q1 FY2027 on the derived basis, or "high single digits" as independently characterized. Two mechanical items partly explain it: the Keys Soulcare divestiture in May 2026 (unquantified) and the deliberate partial rollback of tariff-driven price increases announced in May 2026, which reduces dollars at flat units. Neither is quantified in filings, so the true demand component cannot be isolated. But the direction is corroborated, and it sits against a category growing 5-7%.

**The organic guidance question matters more than its level.** Management raised FY2027 organic growth guidance to 6-7% from 4-5%. rhode closed 8/5/2025, so under standard convention it enters the organic base from roughly Q2 FY2027. If that is the definition being used, rhode's own growth flows into "organic" for most of the year and +6-7% is achievable with the legacy business still shrinking. If "organic" excludes rhode for the full year, the guide implies a sharp H2 inflection in the legacy business — plausible, because the named drivers (e.l.f. Hair, Boots UK, Sephora Brazil, Dollar General, Sephora Canada/Mexico, an H&M fragrance tie-up) are mostly new-door distribution wins that generate sell-in shipments rather than proven same-door velocity. The company's definition of "organic net sales" does not appear in the 10-K or 10-Q. This is an unresolved gap, and it is central to underwriting the guide.

**Estimate revisions are internally contradictory.** Current-year consensus rose 9.4% over 30 days (to $3.61) and next-year rose 5.1% (to $3.84), but current-quarter estimates fell 14.5% (to $0.58) and next-quarter fell 28.5% (to $0.84). Backing out the $1.75 Q1 actual leaves roughly $1.86 for the remaining nine months against $1.75 in Q1 alone. Analysts are marking down the earnings path precisely where management said it would reinvest the tariff windfall. This does not qualify as the +30% ninety-day revision pattern that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is a one-quarter windfall reallocated forward.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | ELF at $91.44 | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| P/E on FY2027 guidance mid ($3.525) | 25.9x | Cosmetics sub-industry forward ~20.7x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($3.84) | 23.8x | Peer median P/E 22.3x (KVUE, PG) |
| EV / FY2026A adj. EBITDA ($335.2M) | 17.5x (17.8x incl. earnout) | — |
| FCF yield (FY2026 normalized $190M) | 3.5% | — |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | ~92x (not meaningful) | Distorted by earnout marks and tariffs |
| Dividend yield | 0.0% | Peer median 3.0% |

Trailing GAAP multiples are uninformative here: FY2026 net income of $26.3M absorbed a $57.6M non-cash earnout mark and ~$58.5M of tariffs since largely refunded. Forward adjusted earnings are the correct lens, and on that lens the stock is at a 25% premium to the cosmetics sub-industry and a ~16% premium to the personal-care peer median while its organic core shrinks.

Two adjustments make the multiple less comfortable rather than more. First, stock compensation is running roughly $19.7M per quarter, or ~$79M annually — about $1.32 per diluted share pre-tax, 37% of the $3.525 adjusted EPS midpoint. Whether the company's adjusted EPS definition excludes it is not verifiable from the sections reviewed; if it does, the fully-loaded multiple is materially higher. Second, EV should carry the contingent consideration: $80.8M is on the balance sheet against a $200M maximum, with the first measurement period ending 9/30/2026 and cash payable within 45 days. Up to $119.2M of further charges remain, and they will be recorded precisely when rhode outperforms.

Against those, the honest bull-side valuation arguments are: EV/EBITDA on a forward basis compresses meaningfully if adjusted EBITDA grows in line with the 18-20% sales guide (roughly 15x on a ~$395M sensitivity, though FY2027 EBITDA guidance was not obtained); FY2026 FCF conversion was strong (OCF $212.5M against $26.3M of GAAP net income); and net leverage of 1.46x with 8.4x coverage leaves capacity for continued buybacks against the $350M remaining authorization.

**Scenario framework (all from the $91.44 basis):**

- **Bear $58 (−36.6%):** legacy decline persists through FY2027, H2 marketing reinvestment holds adjusted EBITDA margin near 20%, FY2028 adjusted EPS of ~$3.30, multiple compresses to 17x on a portfolio the market again treats as single-brand-dependent. The 52-week low of $48.82, set ten weeks ago, demonstrates the market's willingness to reprice this name to that zone on organic-growth data points.
- **Base $92 (+0.6%):** 24x next-year consensus $3.84. rhode's European rollout delivers, legacy stabilizes to roughly flat, adjusted EBITDA margin holds near 20-21%, and the multiple settles at a modest premium to the sub-industry for above-peer consolidated growth of lower quality.
- **Bull $121 (+32.3%):** legacy returns to positive units, rhode approaches a $1B run rate, FY2028 adjusted EPS of ~$4.20 at 29x. This matches the high end of the sell-side distribution.

Probability-weighting 25% / 50% / 25% gives an expected return of approximately −0.8%. The sell-side mean target of $97.25 (+6.4%) and median of $100 (+9.4%) sit modestly above spot, with a low of $65 and one bank at $64 with a Hold as of 6/16/2026. On a stock at a premium multiple, proximity to consensus targets is not independent confirmation of upside — target and price reflect the same growth extrapolation.

Valuation score: 4.5. The multiple is not absurd, but there is no identified mispricing to exploit, no yield support, and no cycle-adjusted earnings power meaningfully above the guided figures. The asymmetry runs the wrong way.

## Competitive Landscape

The original e.l.f. edge — dupe-speed innovation at a $7 price point, marketed natively on TikTok — is replicable, and the same platforms that built the brand now build competitors continuously. The 10-K acknowledges that "small independent companies continue to enter the market with new brands," and names L'Oréal, Estée Lauder, Coty, Unilever, LVMH, Shiseido, Beiersdorf and P&G as competitors. e.l.f.'s answer has been to buy the disruptors: Naturium for $333.0M in October 2023, rhode for $897.5M in August 2025. That is coherent, and both deals look well-priced. It also converts an asset-light, high-return organic compounder into a serial acquirer requiring debt, share issuance and integration execution, with goodwill and intangibles now 56.7% of assets.

Relative positioning is genuinely mixed. Consolidated guided growth of 18-20% is far ahead of Estée Lauder (FQ3 organic +2%, full-year outlook ~3%) and ahead of Ulta (FQ1 net sales +11.1%, comps +5.3%). The legacy portfolio's high-single-digit decline is behind both. Nielsen data in the 10-K shows top-ten new-product slots falling from six to four. rhode is unambiguously gaining share; e.l.f. Cosmetics is unambiguously losing dollar share in US mass makeup.

Trajectory is what matters for scoring, and the trajectory splits: improving at the portfolio level, deteriorating at the flagship that still represents the majority of revenue. Combined with unchanged channel concentration (52% in four accounts, no contractual protection), a 5.0 is the honest read — consistent with the prior two reports rather than a further downgrade.

## Risk Assessment

**Key-person concentration in rhode (first-order).** The risk factors explicitly name Hailey Rhode Bieber as "essential to the rhode brand." With rhode contributing essentially all of FY2026's incremental revenue and roughly a third of Q1 FY2027 sales, this has become an enterprise-level exposure. No employment or endorsement agreement term is disclosed in filings.

**Tariff and China-sourcing whipsaw.** Sourcing is primarily Chinese. The disclosed sequence: 25% Section 301 tariffs since 2019; 2025 rates ranging to as high as 170%; IEEPA tariffs invalidated by the Supreme Court on 2/20/2026 triggering refunds; a 10% Section 122 global tariff imposed 2/24/2026 (statutorily capped at 150 days); Court of International Trade ruling Section 122 unlawful on 5/7/2026 but enjoined only as to named plaintiffs; and new Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% on goods from 60 economies imposed 7/23-24/2026. Cumulative IEEPA payments of $60.3M, $51.1M refunded, $9.2M unrefunded with no receivable recorded. A first-order gross-margin variable has changed direction three times in eighteen months and remains outside management's control.

**Retailer concentration.** 52% of FY2026 sales in four accounts on discrete orders. The 10-K's own seasonality language: "a change in the order pattern of one or more of our large retail customers could cause a significant fluctuation of our quarterly results or impact our liquidity."

**Securities litigation with a near-term procedural catalyst.** Consolidated §10(b)/§20(a) action in N.D. Cal. against the company, CEO and CFO. On 2/4/2026 the court dismissed nearly all challenged statements but sustained claims as to statements made 11/21/2024. Discovery is underway; plaintiffs moved for class certification 7/24/2026 with opposition due 9/25/2026. Five derivative actions are pending. No loss range is estimable. The case is materially narrower than filed, which lowers tail risk, but a certification grant is a discrete negative event and a discovery-disclosure channel.

**Earnout cash and accounting inversion.** Contingent consideration rose from $7.1M at acquisition to $80.8M ($28.2M current), against a $200M maximum with measurement periods ending 9/30/2026, 9/30/2027 and 9/30/2028. Good operating news generates GAAP charges; the first cash payment is due within 45 days of 9/30/2026. Specific performance thresholds are not disclosed, so the probability-weighted payout is unknowable — treat $119.2M of residual charges as a ceiling, not an expectation.

**Goodwill tested as a single reporting unit.** $853.5M of goodwill with an October 1 test date, no impairment in FY2024-26, and negative tangible book of $226.6M. A rhode disappointment would put the entire balance at risk.

**Other.** Worldwide SAP/ERP rollout during a multi-market international expansion; a classified board with a 75% removal supermajority and no written-consent action, which materially reduces the probability of an unsolicited control premium; and disclosed geopolitical exposure specific enough to name a retail partner temporarily shuttering stores in Bahrain following the February 2026 Israel/US operation in Iran. On the macro side, a value beauty franchise is a plausible trade-down beneficiary if the US consumer weakens, which partially offsets discretionary-spending risk — but management's own May 2026 commentary that units "drop[ped] off a bit more in the last few months as consumers have particularly been suffering" argues the offset is not automatic.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 60.8% sits below one-year realized volatility of 71.6% (−10.8 spread), and IV has collapsed from 126.1% on 7/23 to 60.8% now, with price up 22.9% over the same span. Post-event volatility crush plus a term structure in contango (60.8% near versus 63.1% at six months) describes a market that has resolved the earnings binary and is not pricing near-term dislocation. Put/call volume ratio of 0.15 and OI ratio of 0.28 (down from 0.45) show hedging demand being withdrawn into the rally — complacent positioning rather than confirmation of the bull case. The one-week tenor shows a 6.6% put/call skew characterized as elevated put demand, but on a three-day expiry that is low-information. The six-month tenor prices a ±28% move, consistent with a name that has repeatedly re-rated violently on organic-growth data points. Separately, short interest at 16.0% of float with only 3.0 days to cover is the main mechanical risk to a REDUCE: a positive H2 data point could force covering quickly. Cheap near-term volatility argues that expressing caution through long-dated puts or collars is more efficient than outright selling for holders with tax constraints.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($121, +32%)

rhode is one of the fastest-scaling beauty brands on record, bought at roughly 2.0x deal-year revenue, sitting in fewer than 20% of Sephora's global doors with a 19-country European launch landing this autumn and Mecca Australia behind it. A $27M single-day DTC launch with 70% repeat purchase indicates brand equity rather than a viral spike. Management's portfolio pivot is aimed correctly: skincare and fragrance are the growing mass categories per Circana, and skincare has gone from ~10% to ~25% of sales. The underlying gross margin improvement of 362bp is real, net leverage of 1.46x with 8.4x coverage leaves capacity, and $350M of buyback authorization remains after management repurchased 900,063 shares at an average $55.53 during the May drawdown — a ~68% mark-to-market gain. Four consecutive beats and two guidance raises in one year suggest a management team that under-promises. If the legacy business merely returns to flat while rhode approaches $1B, FY2028 adjusted EPS near $4.20 at 29x supports $121.

### Bear Case ($58, −37%)

Consolidated growth is 100% acquired and 100% price-driven: volume fell in both FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 while category units rose. The legacy portfolio is shrinking high single digits against a category growing 5-7%, which is share loss in the business that still produces most of the revenue. The company's own preferred metric — adjusted EBITDA — grew 12.9% on 24.6% revenue growth in FY2026, compressing margin 210bp, and adjusted SG&A moved to 54% of sales from 50% in Q1. Half of the Q1 earnings beat was a non-recurring tariff refund that management is explicitly reinvesting, which is why forward-quarter consensus was cut 14.5% and 28.5% in thirty days. The entire growth engine now depends on one brand tied to one individual, with that dependence explicitly flagged as a risk factor and the associated $853.5M of goodwill tested as one aggregated unit. Add a $200M-maximum earnout with the first cash payment due in November, a class-certification ruling in the autumn, tariff policy that has reversed three times in eighteen months, and 52% customer concentration on discrete purchase orders. At 25.9x guided adjusted EPS with a 3.5% normalized FCF yield and no dividend, there is no valuation cushion for any of it.

### Reconciling with the score trajectory

Prior reports moved 6.0 → 5.8 → 6.2 → 5.3 → 5.1 with four HOLDs, driven by declines in growth prospects (7.5 → 5.0) and competitive position (7.0 → 5.0). The Q1 print and guidance raise partially arrest that decline: the underlying 362bp gross-margin gain, the raised guide, and rhode's dated distribution pipeline justify nudging growth prospects to 5.5 and financial health to 5.9 on the strengthened balance sheet and $110M quarterly FCF. What has changed against the holder is price: the stock is 20% higher than at the July report and 87% above the June low, while the fundamental improvement is narrower than that move and the next two quarters' estimates have been cut. Four consecutive HOLDs into a rally that has closed the gap to base-case value warrants a change of rating, not a fifth repetition.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $92 (+0.6%), bull $121 (+32.3%), bear $58 (−36.6%); probability-weighted expected return approximately −1%. Horizon for resolution: 6-9 months, spanning the Q2 FY2027 print (early November 2026), the first earnout payment, and the autumn European rhode rollout.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst — near-term IV at 60.8% versus 71.6% realized means the option market is not paying much for patience, and the November print carries both the H2 marketing step-up and the first read on whether the legacy business inflects. A residual position of roughly one-third to one-half of a full weight is defensible for holders who want exposure to rhode's European launch; do not add new capital here. For holders with tax constraints, cheap long-dated puts or a collar around the $58-92 range is the more efficient expression, given 16% short interest and a 3.0-day cover create genuine squeeze risk against outright exits.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q2 FY2027 discloses or management quantifies non-rhode net sales returning to flat or positive growth, with company-wide unit volumes no longer a drag (they subtracted ~3 points in Q1).
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin holds at or above 21% for two consecutive quarters *while* marketing runs above 25% of sales — evidence the reinvestment is productive rather than defensive.
3. A price in the $68-72 zone, which would put the stock near 18-19x next-year consensus of $3.84 and restore a margin of safety against the base case.
4. Explicit disclosure of the "organic net sales" definition confirming the 6-7% guide excludes rhode entirely.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any adverse development in Hailey Bieber's relationship with the rhode brand, or a rhode growth deceleration below ~30% year over year.
2. Inventory continuing to grow faster than sales at the November print (inventory was +44.9% year over year against +35.5% sales in Q1) combined with a guidance cut — the combination that would signal sell-through failure rather than launch pre-build.
3. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance reduced below $3.30, or a goodwill impairment charge against the $853.5M single-unit balance.
4. Class certification granted with an expansion of the surviving claims beyond the 11/21/2024 statements.

**Reassessment timeline.** Formal reassessment at the Q2 FY2027 report (expected early November 2026). Interim monitoring points: the 9/25/2026 class-certification opposition deadline, the 9/30/2026 close of the first earnout measurement period with cash payable within 45 days, and the October 1 annual goodwill test date.

## Conclusion

e.l.f. Beauty is a well-run, cash-generative business that made a good acquisition at a good price and is allocating capital sensibly — the May 2026 repurchase of 900,063 shares at $55.53 during a drawdown created by management's own guidance reset is among the better-timed buybacks in consumer staples this year, and a newly seated director with mass-consumer operating credentials bought stock on the open market at $92.96. Net leverage of 1.46x, coverage near 8.4x, no maturity wall before FY2030 and $190M of normalized annual free cash flow mean solvency is not the question.

The question is what an investor pays for a portfolio in which the growth engine has been fully replaced by one acquired brand tied to one individual, while the original engine shrinks high single digits in a category growing 5-7%. At $91.44 the answer is 25.9x guided adjusted EPS and ~17.5x trailing adjusted EBITDA — a premium to the cosmetics sub-industry and to the personal-care peer median, on earnings whose most recent quarter was roughly half a one-time tariff refund and whose next two quarters have just been marked down 14.5% and 28.5%. Base-case fair value is approximately spot, with a bear case 37% lower that the market demonstrated it will pay ten weeks ago and a bull case 32% higher that requires the legacy business to inflect on evidence not yet visible.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength, keep a residual position sized for rhode's European launch, and require either evidence of legacy stabilization or a price in the high $60s before adding capital back.
