## Executive Summary

Cheniere has finished building. FERC cleared feedgas into Train 7 of Corpus Christi Stage 3 in late July, completing a seven-train, >10 mtpa expansion on the schedule management guided in its Q1 10-Q. Combined with Sabine Pass, the platform now carries over 60 mtpa of expected production capacity inclusive of debottlenecking, roughly 90% of it contracted through the mid-2030s at a weighted-average remaining life near 15 years. The 10-K schedules $107.7B of contracted fixed fees ($6.6B in 2026, $29.4B across 2027-2030, $71.7B thereafter), which is the number that matters for debt service on $23.9B of principal. Net debt/Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA is 3.19x against a stated ~4x target, liquidity is $8.3B with all five credit facilities undrawn, and Moody's, S&P and Fitch have all upgraded within fifteen months.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is arithmetic, not deterioration. At $265.44 the stock trades at roughly 11.8-11.9x consolidated EV/Adjusted EBITDA (EV of ~$82.7B including $4.92B of non-controlling interest) and 13.4x the $19.74 FY2027 consensus. My base case is $290, converging from three methods: 15x FY2027 EPS ($296), ~11.5x EV on $7.5B of Adjusted EBITDA ($285), and a 7.5% yield on 2026 guided distributable cash flow per share of ~$21.90 ($292). That is +9% from spot, against a bear case of $195-205 (-25%) if the 2026-2030 global supply wave compresses spot margins while $11.8B of debt refinances above the embedded 4.65% coupon. Bull case $340 (+28%) requires sustained wide international-to-Henry-Hub spreads plus a positive Sabine Pass expansion FID.

Two specific items argue against paying up here. First, initial FY2026 guidance of $6.75-7.25B Adjusted EBITDA is roughly flat against FY2025's $6,943M in a year when Stage 3 goes from one-to-four trains to seven — the volume step-up is being absorbed by margin normalization and cost step-ups, including ad valorem property tax incentives that begin expiring at Corpus Christi this year and at Sabine Pass in 2027 off an ~$89M FY2025 base. A reported +$500M raise in May would take the range to ~$7.25-7.75B, but that figure is single-sourced and unverified. Second, five insiders sold roughly $29M in a five-day cluster at $290-300 in late March, and the company repurchased zero shares that month after buying 2.24M in January at $200.30. Both the executive team and the corporate buyback treated $290-300 as full value; the current price sits between the two anchors.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates as a single reportable segment across two Gulf Coast liquefaction complexes. Sabine Pass (Louisiana, >30 mtpa) is held through Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), where Cheniere owns 100% of the general partner interest, a 48.6% limited partner interest and 100% of the incentive distribution rights; the remaining 49.4% LP interest sits with affiliates of Blackstone and Brookfield plus the public. Corpus Christi (Texas, >30 mtpa including debottlenecking) is 100% owned. The company has exported over 325 million tonnes across more than 4,760 cargoes.

The commercial model determines everything about how to read the financials. Sale-and-purchase agreements pay a fixed fee on contracted volumes whether or not the customer lifts the cargo, plus a variable fee indexed primarily to Henry Hub that covers feedgas, transport and fuel. Integrated production marketing (IPM) agreements buy gas priced off a global LNG or international index less a fixed liquefaction fee — economically a tolling arrangement, and accounting-wise the single largest source of reported earnings noise.

Under construction: Corpus Christi Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (~5 mtpa, 36.9% complete overall but only 2.5% on construction at March 31, targeting 2H 2028, Bechtel fixed-price ~$2.9B). Pre-FID: Sabine Pass Expansion (up to ~20 mtpa, FID targeted 2026/2027) and Corpus Christi Expansion (up to 24 mtpa, FID 2027/2028). A CQP subsidiary signed a lump-sum turnkey EPC contract with Bechtel for Sabine Pass Expansion Phase 1 (>6 mtpa) in late May, reported at $4.69B, and issued a Limited Notice to Proceed — pre-FID, with FERC's environmental assessment, the NGA Section 3 order and DOE non-FTA authorization all still pending.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings are not usable here, and the supplied multiples reflect that.** The company profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 38.36 against EPS of $24.13 — internally inconsistent ($265.44/$24.13 = 11.0x), and neither figure is decision-relevant. The $24.13 corresponds to FY2025 GAAP EPS; the 38.36 appears computed on a trailing twelve months that includes Q1 2026's $(16.75) per share. The driver is mark-to-market on Liquefaction Supply Derivatives with a net notional of 12,199 TBtu and fixed terms up to ~15 years:

| Period | Pre-tax derivative FV change | Reported net income to Cheniere |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | +$1.3B | $3,252M |
| FY2025 | +$3.6B | $5,330M |
| Q1 2026 | **−$4.8B** | **$(3,502)M** |

The Level 3 Liquefaction Supply Derivative swung from a +$2,902M asset at December 31 to a −$1,663M liability at March 31. Management quantifies the sensitivity precisely: a 10% commodity price move changes fair value by $3,075M. Consequently the earnings-surprise record supplied here (−495.9%, +171.2%, +60.1%, +185.7%) carries almost no information about operations, and estimate revisions for this issuer warrant more skepticism than the +16.8% current-year and +9.5% next-year moves would normally command.

The measure to underwrite is Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, which is also what management is paid on:

| FY | Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA | GAAP consolidated net income |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $4,867M | $(1,565)M |
| 2022 | $11,564M | $2,635M |
| 2023 | $8,771M | $12,059M |
| 2024 | $6,155M | $4,492M |
| 2025 | **$6,943M** | $6,794M |

FY2025 revenue was $19,976M on 2,439 TBtu, up $4.3B from FY2024 — but roughly $2.9B of that was Henry Hub pass-through against a ~$3.1B increase in feedstock cost of sales. Headline revenue growth materially overstates margin growth.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $5,539M against $3,078M of cash capex gives $2,461M of free cash flow, 12.3% of revenue and $11.71 per share — a 4.4% FCF yield at $265.44. That capex is predominantly growth (Stage 3 $1.3B, Trains 8&9 $1.0B), so it understates steady-state generation of the completed asset base while remaining the honest measure of cash available during the build. Q1 2026 OCF fell to $1,080M from $1,228M on working-capital timing and a swing to $(509)M of cash used for derivative settlements plus a $213M margin-deposit build; I would not annualize it. The company's own 2026 distributable cash flow guidance of $4.35-4.85B (~$20.71-23.10 per share) is the better anchor, implying a 7.8-8.7% DCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt principal $23,942M at March 31 against Cheniere stockholders' equity of $3,755M (down from $7,915M, almost entirely the non-cash derivative loss flowing through retained earnings plus $542M of buybacks). Current ratio 0.57, mechanically depressed by current derivative liabilities rising $618M → $2,546M and current debt $306M → $1,606M. Net debt of $22,174M against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA is 3.19x.

Screens that key off book equity or net income misfire here. An Altman Z of 1.41 is driven by low equity/assets and negative working capital, but book equity is thin by design — $9,394M of cumulative treasury stock across 69.5M shares, and the company carried negative equity as recently as FY2022. The Piotroski failure on OCF>NI in FY2025 is a direct artifact of $3.6B of non-cash gains inflating net income. What remains true after those adjustments is that there is little tangible equity cushion beneath $23.9B of debt, and the mitigants are contracted fixed fees and structural separation, not the balance sheet.

The debt sits in four separate silos — SPL senior secured $6,527M, CQP senior unsecured $7,800M, CCH senior secured $4,865M, parent senior unsecured $4,750M — with no recourse to the parent for consolidated VIE liabilities. The maturity ladder is the live issue: $0.3B in 2026, **$11.8B across 2027-2030**, $10.9B thereafter, against a weighted-average contractual rate of 4.65%. March 2026 issuance priced at 5.200% (2036) and 6.000% (2056), a reasonable proxy for the step-up on roughly half the stack. That refinancing is a recurring drag on distributable cash flow rather than a one-time event.

Liquidity is $8,349M — $1,305M cash, $463M restricted, $6,581M undrawn across five facilities, all undrawn at quarter-end, full covenant compliance. The caveat is structural: SPL and CCH must fund reserves and clear a 1.25:1.00 historical and projected DSCR before distributing, CCH must confirm sufficient funds to complete Stage 3, and parent access to Sabine Pass cash runs only through the 48.6% LP interest, GP interest and IDRs. Consolidated liquidity overstates parent-level flexibility.

**Capital returns.** FY2025: 12.1M shares repurchased for $2,724M at an average $221.55, $451M of dividends, $652M of debt principal retired. Q1 2026: 2.65M shares for $537M at an average $202.58, of which 2.24M in January at $200.30 and **zero in March**. The February authorization increase to ~$10B running 2026-2030 leaves $9,663M available. If fully executed at $265.44 that would retire ~36.4M shares, 17.3% of the current count — a maximum-utilization sensitivity, not a forecast, since the authorization spans five years and imposes no obligation. Basic weighted shares have fallen from 251.1M (FY2022) to 210.5M (Q1 2026), a 16.2% reduction.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth is delivered rather than promised. Stage 3 Trains 1-6 were operating by late June; Train 7 received FERC clearance for hot oil, fuel gas and then feedgas across July 17-29. That completes the >10 mtpa addition and means FY2027 will be the first full year of run-rate contribution.

What tempers enthusiasm is the guidance shape. Initial FY2026 guidance of $6.75-7.25B Adjusted EBITDA has a midpoint of $7.0B against FY2025's $6,943M — approximately flat, in a year with a large volume increase. Q1 2026 volumes recognized as LNG revenue were 682 TBtu versus 616 TBtu, and management said results excluding derivatives were favorable on volume, optimization and a higher proportion of cargoes sold at global LNG pricing. Yet Q1 cost of sales also carried a **$370M non-recurring reduction** from federal alternative fuel excise tax credits covering 2018-2024, roughly 5% of guided full-year EBITDA at the $7.0B midpoint, and it is not clear from available evidence whether that sits inside or outside the Adjusted EBITDA definition. Against that one-time benefit, ad valorem property tax incentives begin expiring at Corpus Christi in 2026 and Sabine Pass in 2027, off an ~$89M FY2025 base with the increase unquantified by the company.

The medium-term pipeline is real but contingent. Trains 8&9 add ~5 mtpa by 2H 2028 with construction only 2.5% complete — the bulk of execution risk ahead. Sabine Pass Expansion Phase 1 has an EPC contract and a Limited Notice to Proceed but no FID, no FERC Section 3 order and no DOE non-FTA authorization. Management's own investment criteria require targeted unlevered returns exceeding cost of equity and return on stock at prevailing prices, which makes expansion FIDs partly a function of where the shares trade — disciplined, and also self-limiting.

Demand-side evidence cuts both ways. FY2025 saw European demand rise 27% to a record ~125 mtpa and the European Parliament vote to ban residual Russian gas including LNG by 2027, both structurally supportive of Gulf Coast supply. But Asian demand fell 4% and China alone fell 16% (−12 mtpa), removing the assumed growth engine, and roughly 20 mtpa of global supply was added in 2025. Management's own February framing was that the market was "transitioning from a multi-year tight market into a period of rapid growth" with a "more ample supply landscape." By May the language had inverted to "tightening supply conditions, transit constraints and heightened geopolitical uncertainties from the conflict and instabilities across parts of the Middle East." These are not strictly contradictory — a multi-year supply build and a near-term disruption can coexist — but the second is the source of both the guidance raise and the $4.8B derivative charge, and its duration is unverifiable from the evidence available.

Net: mid-single-digit Adjusted EBITDA growth with per-share amplification of roughly 4-5% annually from buybacks, and a step function in 2028 if Trains 8&9 land on schedule. That supports a growth score of 6.5 — durable and visible, but not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

Three approaches converge near $290:

1. **Forward earnings.** Consensus is $17.93 for the current year and $19.74 for next, revised +16.8% and +9.5% over 90 days. At $265.44 that is 14.8x and 13.4x. Applying 15x to FY2027 gives **$296**.
2. **EV/EBITDA.** EV = $55.6B market cap + $22.17B net debt + $4.92B NCI = **$82.7B**. Against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $6,943M that is 11.9x; against the $7.0B initial 2026 midpoint, 11.8x; against a $7.5B reported-raise midpoint, 11.0x. At 11.5x on $7.5B, equity value per share is **$285**.
3. **Distributable cash flow.** 2026 guidance of $4.35-4.85B on 210.1M shares is $20.71-23.10 per share, a 7.8-8.7% yield at spot. Capitalizing the $21.90 midpoint at 7.5% gives **$292**.

Peer comparison requires care. The supplied median P/E of 20.3 is distorted — FANG at 197x and OXY at 75x reflect impairments, not earnings power. The relevant comparable is OKE at 15.9x P/E with a 4.7% yield. Cheniere at 13.4x FY2027 with a 0.88% yield and a 4-5% annual share-count reduction is defensibly cheaper on total shareholder return, but the discount is not dramatic once you include the $4.9B of non-controlling interest that consolidated EBITDA multiples otherwise flatter.

The specific reason to be cautious about calling this cheap: the two most informed marginal participants both declined to transact at higher prices. Insiders sold ~$29M at $290-300 in late March; the company bought 2.24M shares at $200.30 in January, 412K at $214.91 in February, and nothing in March. The buyback authorization is large but the pace has historically flexed with price, which means the per-share compounding engine throttles down precisely as the stock rises. At $265 we are 32% above the January repurchase level and 12% below the 52-week high of $300.89.

Analyst consensus sits at a $304 mean and $304.50 median, range $255-340 — above my base case. At 13.4x forward this is not a premium-multiple stock where targets merely echo the price, so consensus deserves weight as a plausible upper-middle outcome. But the gap between $265 and $290-305 is not wide enough to compensate for a bear case near $200, which is where the stock traded eight months ago on a similar contracted asset base. Valuation score 6.0: reasonable, defensible, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere is the largest U.S. LNG producer and second-largest LNG operator globally by liquefaction capacity, with ~30 distinct long-term third-party SPA counterparties, brownfield expansion land already owned at both sites, and a repeat Bechtel relationship that secured a fixed-price EPC slot for Sabine Pass Phase 1 ahead of FID in a tight contractor market. Recent commercial progress includes a second long-term SPA with Taiwan's CPC Corporation for up to ~1.2 mtpa running 2026 through 2050. The January redemption of the remaining non-controlling interest in the Gregory Power Plant VIE takes that gas-fired asset to 100% ownership, integrating power supply adjacent to Corpus Christi.

The moat is contract structure plus scale, and the trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. The 10-K itself describes an "increasingly competitive North American LNG landscape" and acknowledges that securing long-term contracts at target returns depends on global market conditions. The 2026-2030 wave of U.S. and Qatari capacity is what makes the pre-FID projects contingent, and it will eventually set the price at which legacy SPAs recontract. Customer concentration is moving the wrong way: Customer A rose to 18% of trade receivables and contract assets from 15% at year-end, with Customer B at 10% of Q1 revenue, and neither is identified.

One counterintuitive feature is worth stating plainly. Because IPM purchase legs are indexed to international prices while the offsetting LNG sales are accrual-accounted, a *tightening* global market that widens the international-to-Henry-Hub spread produces large accounting losses while simultaneously improving the spot margin Cheniere Marketing earns on uncontracted volumes. Middle East supply and transit disruption is therefore economically favorable and cosmetically damaging for this issuer — the opposite of most energy names.

## Risk Assessment

**Derivative estimation uncertainty.** A 10% commodity move shifts Liquefaction Supply Derivative fair value by $3,075M. Level 3 unobservable inputs are extraordinarily wide: Henry Hub basis spread from $(6.340) to $0.195, and international LNG pricing spread relative to Henry Hub from 60% to 537% with a 183% weighted average. Some marks depend on assumptions about third-party gas gathering and transport infrastructure Cheniere does not control. This makes reported results unforecastable by billions in either direction.

**Refinancing into a higher-rate regime.** $11.8B matures 2027-2030 against a 4.65% embedded coupon, with new issuance pricing at 5.20%/6.00%. Disclosed interest obligations are $1.1B in 2026 and $3.2B across 2027-2030. Every 100bp of step-up on $11.8B is ~$118M of annual pre-tax cash, roughly 2.5% of guided DCF.

**Two dated compliance and cost cliffs.** Ad valorem incentives expire beginning 2026 (Corpus Christi) and 2027 (Sabine Pass) off ~$89M. Separately, USTR's Section 301 action requires 1% of U.S. LNG exports to move on U.S.-built vessels from April 2029, rising to 15% by 2047; the November 2025 White House deferral of China-linked vessel fees did not modify that timeline, and there is no domestic LNG carrier construction capability at relevant scale today. How the obligation allocates between Cheniere and its FOB customers is undisclosed.

**Off-balance-sheet commitments.** Beyond $2,658M of operating and $1,441M of finance lease liabilities, ~$6.0B of legally binding minimum payments exist for leases already executed but not yet commenced, primarily vessel charters up to 15 years. Marine finance-lease assets grew from $1,060M to $1,604M gross in one quarter. Total purchase obligations aggregate $44.6B.

**Structural cash competition.** If CQP retains cash ahead of a Sabine Pass FID — reported by a third party, unverified — the parent's inflow falls, competing directly with the buyback for funding. CQP distribution guidance of $3.10-3.40/unit may absorb it, or may not.

**Near-term event risk.** Q2 results are scheduled for August 6 and are not in evidence. Given the $3.075B sensitivity, GAAP results could differ from Q1 by billions either way.

The informative tenors are one month at 38.6% and three months at 35.8% against 26.3% one-year historical volatility — vol is genuinely rich into the August 6 print, with a ±6.1% implied move at one month. Term structure is in backwardation (30.7% at six months), consistent with event risk concentrated at the print rather than a regime change.

Positioning is not bearish. Put/call volume is 0.44 and put/call open interest 0.34 — call-tilted, with skew balanced to modestly negative (−1.4% near term, −4.7% at one month, −3.6% at six months). Short interest is 2.0% of float at 1.8 days to cover. There is no institutional downside bet of size here. The practical read: options are pricing a fair amount of uncertainty around a single date, buying protection or upside into the print is expensive, and the absence of put demand means the market is not corroborating a bear thesis. That supports patience over adding, and is one more reason a HOLD is the right posture two trading sessions before earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $340 (+28%)

Stage 3 completion delivers a full 2027 of run-rate volumes, pushing Adjusted EBITDA toward $8B. Middle East supply and transit disruption persists, keeping international-to-Henry-Hub spreads wide and lifting Cheniere Marketing's spot margins on uncontracted volumes; European demand holds near the record ~125 mtpa while the 2027 Russian LNG ban tightens the Atlantic basin. Sabine Pass Phase 1 reaches positive FID with FERC and DOE authorizations in hand, converting the $4.69B EPC contract into a committed >6 mtpa project and re-rating the pre-FID pipeline from optionality toward earnings. The company executes meaningfully against the $9.66B authorization at prices below $250, compounding per-share DCF at 6-8%. At 17x $20 of FY2027 EPS, $340.

### Bear Case — $195-205 (−25%)

The 2026-2030 supply wave arrives as management originally described in February: ~20 mtpa of annual additions loosening global balances, with Asian demand still contracting (China −16% in 2025). Spot margins on uncontracted volumes compress toward fixed-fee economics, and Adjusted EBITDA holds near $6.5-7.0B rather than growing. The $11.8B 2027-2030 refinancing lands at 5.5-6.5%, absorbing $100-200M annually. Ad valorem tax roll-off and the FY2025/Q1 2026 non-recurring items ($370M excise credit, $380M CAMT refund, near-zero cash taxes) reverse out of the comparison base. CQP retains cash for Sabine Pass, cutting the parent's inflow and slowing the buyback. Expansion FIDs slip past 2028. The stock returns to the December 2025 range of $186-200, roughly 10x a reduced FY2027 estimate.

### Base Case — $290 (+9%)

Adjusted EBITDA of $7.3-7.6B in 2026 and $7.8-8.1B in 2027 as Stage 3 runs full-year, partly offset by the tax-incentive roll-off and higher interest. DCF per share of $22-24. Buyback executes at a moderate pace, retiring 3-5% of shares annually. Sabine Pass Phase 1 reaches FID in 2027 rather than 2026. 15x FY2027 EPS of $19.74 = $296; 11.5x EV/EBITDA on $7.5B = $285; 7.5% DCF yield on $21.90 = $292. Call it $290, plus 0.88% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Position: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — completed Stage 3, contracted fixed fees, a large authorization, and improving credit — but $265 does not offer the asymmetry that justified the prior BUY. This is the third assessment in the current cycle and the bar for repeating a BUY at an unchanged price with a 9% base-case upside is not met.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price.** Entry in the **low-$230s or below**, restoring 25%+ upside to the $290 base case and approaching the zone where management itself was repurchasing (2.24M shares at $200.30 in January, 412K at $214.91 in February).
2. **Confirmed earnings power.** August 6 (or Q3) results showing Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA on track for **≥$7.5B in 2026** with the guidance raise verified in a company release, and distributable cash flow tracking to the upper half of $4.35-4.85B *excluding* the $370M excise-tax credit.
3. **Sabine Pass Phase 1 FID** with FERC Section 3 order and DOE non-FTA authorization received, converting >6 mtpa from optionality to committed capacity, provided CQP distribution guidance is reaffirmed at $3.10-3.40/unit.
4. **Buyback re-engagement at scale** — repurchases resuming above ~$400M/quarter at prices under $270, alongside insider open-market purchases.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA rising above **4.0x**, or 2027-2030 refinancing pricing above 6.5%.
2. Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below **$6.75B**, or 2027 consensus EPS revised below $17.50 — signalling the supply-wave bear case is arriving.
3. A **Trains 8&9 schedule slip beyond 2029** or a material Bechtel change order (construction was only 2.5% complete at March 31).
4. Customer A concentration rising above **25% of trade receivables** without disclosure of counterparty credit quality, or any SPA termination notice.
5. CQP cash retention that reduces parent distributions enough to force a buyback pause for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 6 Q2 print is the immediate checkpoint — specifically the non-GAAP reconciliation (does the $370M credit sit inside Adjusted EBITDA?), the confirmed 2026 guidance range, Q2 repurchase activity, and any change to CQP distribution guidance. Second checkpoint is the Q3 print for Train 7 run-rate contribution and any Sabine Pass FID timing update. Track also the three unread 8-Ks (July 2 Items 1.01/2.03 — a material agreement plus creation of a direct financial obligation; July 28 Item 7.01) for financing or charter commitments that would add to the $6.0B of not-yet-commenced lease obligations.

## Conclusion

Cheniere is a well-run, structurally advantaged contracted-infrastructure business that has just finished the largest phase of its build program. Roughly 90% of production is contracted through the mid-2030s, $107.7B of fixed fees are scheduled, leverage is inside target at 3.19x, all three agencies have upgraded, and management is compensated on Adjusted EBITDA, absolute TSR and cumulative DCF per share — the right metrics for an issuer whose GAAP earnings swing by billions on non-cash marks. Capital allocation has been genuinely disciplined: buying 2.24M shares at $200 in January and none in March at $290-300 is the behavior you want to see.

The constraint is price. At $265.44 the stock trades at 13.4x FY2027 consensus, ~11-12x consolidated EV/Adjusted EBITDA and a 7.8-8.7% guided DCF yield, with a base case near $290 and a credible path back to $195-205 if the 2026-2030 supply wave compresses spot margins while half the debt stack refinances above its embedded 4.65% coupon. Initial FY2026 guidance being flat against FY2025 despite the Stage 3 volume step-up, ad valorem incentives beginning to roll off, and $370M of non-recurring credit in the Q1 base all argue for treating the reported momentum with some discount. Insiders sold ~$29M in a five-day cluster at $290-300 and the company stopped buying in that range.

Own the position through the completion of Stage 3 and the buyback program. Add in the low-$230s or on verified 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $7.5B+ excluding one-time items. **HOLD.**
