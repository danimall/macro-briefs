**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Flavors & Fragrances is a global scale leader in flavors, fragrances, and biotech-derived ingredients that spent the last several years unwinding the debt and complexity created by two acquisitions: Frutarom (2018) and the ~$26B DuPont Nutrition & Biosciences merger (2021). The investment case today rests on a turnaround that is measurably working: total debt has been cut from roughly $10.8B in 2022 to ~$5.85B in Q1 2026, net leverage sits at 2.53x against a 3.75x covenant, and the pending ~$4.3B sale of the Food Ingredients segment to CVC Capital Partners (announced May 29, 2026) accelerates deleveraging while shedding the company's lowest-margin business, the same segment whose goodwill was already written to zero.

The headline financials mislead. FY2025 reported a GAAP net loss of -$361M (-$1.41/share), but that figure is driven almost entirely by a $1.153B non-cash goodwill impairment on the Food Ingredients unit. The underlying business generated ~$2.086B of Adjusted Operating EBITDA at a 19.2% margin, and Q1 2026 returned to GAAP profitability at $0.66/share with a 20.7% Adjusted EBITDA margin and organic growth across all four segments. On a comparable, currency-neutral basis the core is compounding low-single-digits with improving profitability.

We rate IFF a HOLD with a constructive, growth-oriented lean, continuing a HOLD sequence that has run three consecutive reports while the underlying score has improved from 4.7 (February) to 5.9. The trajectory reflects real fundamental progress: deleveraging on track, the DOJ antitrust probe closed, a capstone divestiture signed, and a ~$20M open-market director purchase near current levels. What holds us short of BUY is that the risk/reward is not yet asymmetric: forward EPS estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative on the near quarters, Health & Biosciences carries only ~9% goodwill headroom, and open EU/UK/Swiss/Mexican cartel probes remain a genuinely unquantified downside where fines are formula-based on global revenue.

## Company Overview

IFF operates in a consolidated global oligopoly alongside Givaudan, dsm-firmenich, and Symrise, supplying flavors, fragrances, enzymes, cultures, probiotics, and texturants into food & beverage, home & personal care, and health & wellness end markets. As of Q1 2026 the company reports four segments:

- **Taste** — ~$2.48B FY25 sales; flavor compounds and savory/seasoning solutions.
- **Health & Biosciences** — ~$2.28B; enzymes, cultures, probiotics. The highest-margin segment at ~26% EBITDA margin and the strategic crown jewel post-transformation.
- **Scent** — ~$2.48B; fragrance compounds and ingredients.
- **Food Ingredients** — ~$3.28B; texturants, emulsifiers, plant-based proteins. Now being divested to CVC.

The competitive moat rests on inclusion on customers' core-supplier lists, proprietary formulations and trade secrets, and biotech capabilities in the Health & Biosciences franchise. The customer base is diversified: roughly 20,000 customers, the 25 largest representing ~32% of sales, with no single customer above 10%. About 72% of sales are generated outside the United States.

The corporate story of the past three years is deleveraging and simplification. IFF has completed 13+ divestitures, including Pharma Solutions and Nitrocellulose (May 2025), Soy Crush/Concentrates/Lecithin (March 2026), and an agreement to sell botanical extracts and natural colors to SuanNutra (July 2026). The Food Ingredients sale is the capstone.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Reported revenue has declined from $12.44B (2022) to $10.89B (2025), driven by divestitures, not underlying demand erosion. On a comparable, currency-neutral basis FY2025 sales grew +2%, and Q1 2026 comparable currency-neutral growth was +3% with all four segments contributing organically. The organic figure is the one that matters for the core business.

**Profitability.** The FY2025 GAAP operating loss of -$382M and net loss of -$361M are largely a $1.153B goodwill impairment on the Food Ingredients unit, triggered by the January 1, 2025 segment reorganization (following a $2.623B Nourish impairment in 2023). Strip the non-cash charges and the picture inverts: FY2025 Adjusted Operating EBITDA was ~$2.086B (19.2% margin) with gross margin improving to 36.2%. Q1 2026 confirmed the cleaner run-rate: net income of $170M ($0.66/share), a 10.0% operating margin, and a 20.7% Adjusted EBITDA margin (+39 bps YoY). Any screen keying off GAAP net income or reported operating margin materially mischaracterizes this company.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $850M (7.8% of sales, down from 9.3% in 2024 on a working-capital build and higher incentive payout), with FCF of ~$256M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow improved to $257M (9.4% of sales). Capex is guided to ~6% of sales for 2026. FCF conversion is modest but positive and improving off the seasonally weak first quarter.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** FY2025 included a $488M gain on debt extinguishment from repurchasing $2.0B of senior notes below par. The company has a $2B undrawn revolver (amended and extended to 2030 in June 2025, with covenant-relief restrictions removed) and remains investment-grade. Net debt is ~$5.29B.

A note on distress screens: an Altman Z-Score near 1.56 flags "distress," but that reading is mechanically produced by impairment-driven negative EBIT and post-M&A leverage. It is a false positive for a covenant-compliant, IG-rated, positive-FCF business that has nearly halved its debt in three years. The Piotroski partial signals (positive net income in Q1, OCF exceeding net income, current ratio ~1.42) better capture the trajectory.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was cut ~50% in February 2024 (to $1.60/year) to fund deleveraging. A $500M buyback was authorized in August 2025 (~$73M executed through Q1 2026). The stated priority stack (IG rating, reinvestment, dividend, buyback, debt paydown) is coherent for a company mid-turnaround. The current ~2.1% dividend yield is well-covered.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signal is directionally positive but modest. Organic growth is running low-single-digits with Adjusted EBITDA margins expanding. The earnings surprise record is solid: three beats and one small miss over four quarters, including a 15.3% beat in Q1 2026 ($1.25 actual vs. $1.08 estimate). The 4-quarter track record supports reasonable confidence in consensus.

Estimate revisions are the check on enthusiasm. Current-quarter EPS estimates ($1.12) are flat over 30 days and down 3.5% over 90 days; next-quarter ($1.15) is down 1.7% over 90 days. The current-year ($4.50) and next-year ($4.81) figures are modestly higher over 90 days (+2.1% and +1.1%), so the full-year picture is nudging up while the near quarters soften slightly. This is not the kind of large upward revision velocity that would flag a genuine acceleration; it is a stabilizing business with mild forward drift, which is why we do not treat the estimate-revision signal as a BUY trigger here.

The structural growth argument is the portfolio remix. Post-Food-Ingredients, the mix shifts toward Health & Biosciences and Scent/Taste, structurally higher-margin and higher-growth than the divested texturants/proteins business. If IFF converts the CVC proceeds into debt paydown and disciplined buybacks, per-share earnings power should improve independent of top-line acceleration. The offset is a real demand risk the company itself flags: GLP-1/weight-management drugs altering food consumption patterns, plus multinational CPG customers facing private-label and pricing pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.77, IFF trades at ~22.9x forward earnings on the reported figure, though that trailing/forward P/E reflects the impairment-suppressed GAAP base and is not a meaningful anchor. On the ~$4.50 current-year and ~$4.81 next-year consensus EPS, the stock trades around 16.4x and 15.3x forward, reasonable for a scale specialty-ingredients leader with improving margins and a deleveraging tailwind.

Peer comparison is muddied by the sector's earnings dispersion. Among specialty-chemical comps, LYB and ALB show negative reported P/Es (cyclical/commodity distress), DD sits at 147.7x, RPM at 20.7x, and SOLS at 69.3x, producing a peer-median P/E of 69.3x that is not a usable benchmark. The more relevant read is against the flavor/fragrance oligopoly (Givaudan, Symrise, dsm-firmenich), which typically trades at higher multiples than IFF's ~16x forward, a discount justified partly by IFF's higher leverage and antitrust overhang, and partly by its lagging TSR record.

Analyst targets cluster meaningfully above spot: mean $91.5, median $91.0, low $71, high $105, with Morgan Stanley at $95 (Overweight), Benchmark at $100 (Buy), and Oppenheimer upgrading to Outperform. On a stock at ~16x forward with a specific catalyst (the CVC deal) and rising full-year estimates, these targets are more floor-like than ceiling-like. We weight this constructively but not decisively; the antitrust tail and thin H&B goodwill headroom cap how aggressively we lean on target-implied upside.

We assign a valuation score of 6.0. There is a genuine, identifiable case that the market is under-pricing the deleveraged, remixed business (forward multiple below the flavor-house cohort, a signed value-crystallizing divestiture, insider conviction near current levels). The reason it is not higher: the discount is partly deserved (leverage, litigation overhang, weak TSR history), and the sector-caution lesson for Materials argues for demanding a clear company-specific edge before rewarding cheapness. Cheapness alone is treated as a warning, not a buy signal; here the cheapness is paired with a real catalyst, which is what supports the above-neutral score.

## Competitive Landscape

IFF competes in a four-player global oligopoly where scale, R&D depth, regulatory-compliance capability, and core-supplier-list status are decisive. Its position is genuinely leading, comparable in scale to Givaudan and dsm-firmenich. The trajectory question is what matters: the portfolio remix toward Health & Biosciences and Scent moves up the margin and growth curve, and organic growth across all four segments in Q1 2026 suggests the core is holding share rather than ceding it.

The offsets are real. Regulatory headwinds are specific and reformulation-forcing: the EU microplastics ban, EU CLP/Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability, and TSCA delays each require ingredient reformulation across fragrance and enzyme lines. The industry-wide fragrance antitrust probe (all four majors raided simultaneously in March 2023) is a sector overhang rather than an IFF-specific competitive disadvantage, which slightly softens its bite for relative positioning even as it remains a hard downside risk in absolute terms. We score competitive position 6.2: leading scale, improving mix, holding organic share, but with regulatory reformulation pressure and a history of TSR underperformance that argues against a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust probes (most material idiosyncratic risk).** Coordinated raids and subpoenas since March 2023 from the EU Commission, UK CMA, Swiss ComCo, U.S. DOJ, and Mexican Competition Commission. The positives are meaningful: the DOJ closed its investigation on February 5, 2026, and IFF settled the U.S. direct-purchaser class action ($26M funded) plus indirect/end-user matters, recognizing a $43M provision in 2025. The unresolved tail is the EU/UK/Swiss/Mexican regulatory probes, where EU cartel fines can reach 10% of global revenue. IFF has applied for leniency in multiple jurisdictions, which caps but does not eliminate exposure. This is the single largest unquantified downside.

**Goodwill impairment fragility.** After the 2025 charge, the Health & Biosciences reporting unit had only ~9% valuation headroom; a 50 bps WACC increase could tip it into impairment. Remaining goodwill is ~$8.2B and intangibles ~$5.9B. In a higher-for-longer rate regime, this is a live, non-trivial risk to the segment that is supposed to carry post-transformation growth.

**Execution risk on the Food Ingredients divestiture.** The CVC sale carries stranded-cost, dis-synergy, and separation risk, and IFF retains a ~10% minority stake whose value depends on the buyer's execution.

**Leverage and rating.** A downgrade would raise revolver pricing (grid-based) and could trigger dividend/buyback restrictions.

**N&B tax indemnification.** A "Spinco Tainting Act" could void the tax-free Reverse Morris Trust treatment of the 2021 merger, creating a potentially large indemnification liability to DuPont.

**Demand.** GLP-1/weight-management adoption altering food consumption, plus private-label and pricing pressure at CPG customers.

We score risk factors 5.0 — neutral. The DOJ closure and cheap civil settlements de-risk the most acute leg, but the open European/Mexican probes, the thin H&B goodwill headroom, and the DuPont tax indemnity are three genuine, partially-unpriced binary tails that keep this from scoring above neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 36.8% against 34.5% one-year historical vol — roughly fair (2.3pp spread). The put/call skew is -5.1% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.13, and OI P/C is 0.59, all of which indicate the options market is not positioned defensively, with no elevated put demand contradicting a constructive fundamental view. The one flag is term-structure backwardation (near-term 36.8% > long-term 33.3%) with an ±8.4% implied move on the nearest expiry, consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst (the next earnings print and deal progress). IV rose ~4pp since early June while price rose ~4%, so the market is pricing incrementally more near-term uncertainty into a rising stock. Net read: options positioning is mildly supportive, but the backwardation argues for patience on entry timing rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95–105).** The CVC deal closes, proceeds accelerate deleveraging below 2x and fund opportunistic buybacks, the remixed portfolio (H&B + Scent + Taste) compounds organic growth at low-to-mid single digits with margin expansion, the open European antitrust probes settle without a formula-based fine, and the flavor-house multiple discount narrows toward peers. On ~$4.81 next-year EPS at a ~20x multiple, that supports ~$95, with analyst high targets at $105. Director Fribourg's ~$20M open-market purchase at ~$74 sits squarely in the bull-case entry zone.

**Bear case (~$55–62).** An adverse EU/UK/Swiss cartel ruling produces a revenue-based fine, or H&B tips into a fresh impairment on a higher WACC, or the CVC deal terms slip / dis-synergies prove larger than modeled, and estimate revisions turn decisively negative. Combined with the persistent TSR-lag reputation, the multiple compresses back toward the low-$60s where the stock traded as recently as autumn 2025.

**Base case (~$85).** The deal closes on terms, deleveraging continues, organic growth holds low-single-digits, the antitrust tail resolves without a catastrophic fine, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward the flavor-house cohort. That is ~15% above the current $73.77 — attractive but not the 2:1-or-better asymmetry with a dated near-term catalyst that would justify a BUY today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, with the CVC deal close (expected over the coming quarters) and the next 1–2 earnings prints as the key checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Food Ingredients sale closes on stated terms with proceeds applied to debt paydown driving net leverage below ~2.0x, confirmed on a subsequent 10-Q.
- Resolution (settlement or closure) of the open EU/UK/Swiss/Mexican antitrust probes without a material revenue-based fine.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turning positive, signaling organic acceleration rather than stabilization.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse European/Mexican cartel ruling imposing a fine material relative to ~$2B annual EBITDA.
- A fresh goodwill impairment in the Health & Biosciences unit (the ~9%-headroom fragility materializing).
- Net leverage rising back toward covenant, a credit-rating downgrade, or a dividend/buyback restriction.

## Conclusion

IFF is a credible, measurable turnaround: debt nearly halved since 2022, net leverage inside covenant and falling, GAAP losses that are non-cash impairment artifacts masking a ~19–21% Adjusted EBITDA-margin business, and a capstone divestiture that crystallizes the deleverage-and-simplify strategy. Director Fribourg's ~$20M open-market purchase days after the deal announcement, at a price near today's, is one of the stronger management-alignment data points in the set and underpins the improved management-quality score. The trajectory across our last three reports (4.7 → 5.5 → 5.9) reflects genuine fundamental progress, and the lower recent price on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward at the margin rather than degrading the thesis.

We stay HOLD (growth-oriented) rather than upgrade because three specific items keep the setup below the asymmetry bar: near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, the Health & Biosciences goodwill headroom is thin in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and the open European/Mexican antitrust probes are an unquantified, revenue-based-fine tail. The Materials-sector caution reinforces demanding a clear catalyst before rewarding a modestly cheap multiple; here the catalyst exists (the CVC close and antitrust resolution), but it has not yet resolved. On deal close below-2x leverage, or a clean antitrust resolution, this becomes a BUY. Until then, own it for the deleveraging-plus-remix growth thesis and the ~$85 base case, and use the near-term options backwardation as a reason to be patient on entry.
