## Executive Summary

Inspire Medical Systems is a single-product implantable neurostimulation company whose revenue has gone from +53% growth (2023) to an outright decline (-7.6% in Q2 2026). The August 3 second-quarter report produced an 18% single-day rally on an adjusted diluted EPS print of $0.14 against a non-GAAP consensus loss of $(0.22) to $(0.27), plus a raise to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance from $0.75–1.25 to $1.05–1.45. The substance of the revenue guidance change was narrower: the low end rose $10M to $835M, the high end did not move, and the $855M midpoint less first-half actual revenue of $405.2M implies second-half revenue of $449.8M against $493.6M a year earlier — an 8.9% decline, consistent with management's guided Q3 decline of 8–10% and requiring no fourth-quarter inflection. The company is not forecasting a return to growth.

What did improve is real. Gross margin reached 85.5% (+150bp year over year) on Inspire V mix. Operating expense fell 7.4% year over year. First-half operating cash flow swung to +$36.1M from -$4.0M. Cash and investments rose $10.6M in the half to $415.2M, or $14.36 per share — 23.8% of the current price — against zero funded debt and an Altman Z-Score near 6.8. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 35.5% in 30 days and next-year EPS up 15.1% in 90 days. On a company with 85% gross margins, a fixed cost base that management is now cutting, and a top line that troughs, operating leverage works in both directions.

The problem is price and unresolved binaries. At $60.28 the stock is 48.2x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $1.25 and 39.7x next-year consensus of $1.52; net of cash it is 36.7x and 30.2x. It trades at the consensus mean target of $60.40 with 11 holds, one sell and one strong sell against four buys. The central operating issue is a reduction of roughly 30% to the implanting surgeon's professional fee at two Medicare Administrative Contractors under the -52 modifier, with the stated fix being creation of a new CPT code on the AMA's multi-year cycle; this is unresolved. The Q2 10-Q newly conceded that GLP-1 adoption "is being adversely impacted" in the short term. Alongside that sit a DOJ Civil Investigative Demand under the False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute, a securities class action whose class period was expanded on July 30, 2026 to cover nearly the entire share-price decline, three derivative suits, and a live qui tam — none carrying a recorded accrual. Base case fair value of roughly $62 sits at spot against a bear case near $38. We move from AVOID to **REDUCE**: the balance sheet and cost execution no longer support the harshest rating, but there is no margin of safety at $60 and the downside asymmetry argues for trimming.

## Company Overview

Essentially all revenue derives from the Inspire system, an implantable hypoglossal nerve stimulation (HGNS) device for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in patients who cannot tolerate positive airway pressure. It is positioned as second-line therapy behind CPAP. Management describes it as the only closed-loop HGNS system approved by the FDA, EU MDR and Japan PMDA (it senses respiration and times stimulation to the breath), against open-loop devices from Nyxoah and LivaNova.

Geographic mix is overwhelmingly domestic: FY2025 U.S. revenue $872.1M (95.6%) versus international $39.9M. In Q2 2026, U.S. revenue was $187.3M (-9.6% year over year) and international $13.3M (+33.6%). International would need to roughly quadruple to offset the dollar decline in the U.S. business.

Inspire V, approved August 2024 and launched in the U.S. in May 2025, integrates the respiratory sensor into the neurostimulator, eliminating a separate lead. It carries a higher gross margin and lower manufacturing cost. It also created the coding problem that dominates the current story: a redesigned device launched into an unsettled reimbursement code.

The commercial organization scaled from 186 sales and marketing employees at end-2019 to 954 at end-2025. U.S. territories peaked at 295 (12/31/2025) and were cut to 280 by 6/30/2026, while field clinical representatives rose from 275 to 301, a reallocation from territory expansion toward clinical support. The IP estate comprises 119 issued U.S. patents (expiring 2029–2043) and 83 issued foreign patents. All manufacturing is U.S.-based with many sole-source suppliers; R&D and back-office functions sit in two Golden Valley, Minnesota facilities with no disclosed redundancy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $407.9M → FY2023 $624.8M (+53.2%) → FY2024 $802.8M (+28.5%) → FY2025 $912.0M (+13.6%) → H1 2026 $405.2M (-3.2%) → Q2 2026 $200.6M (-7.6%). FY2026 guidance of $835–875M implies -8.4% to -4.1%.

**Margins.** Gross margin has improved steadily: 84.5% (FY2023), 84.7% (FY2024), 85.4% (FY2025), 85.5% (Q2 2026). One qualifier deserves attention: first-half gross margin was 86.0% ($348.4M on $405.2M), implying Q1 near 86.5% and a roughly 100bp sequential decline into Q2. The Inspire V mix effect on margin is not monotonic.

Below the gross line the picture is weak. FY2025 GAAP operating income was $51.0M on $912.0M of revenue — 5.6% margin on an 85.4% gross margin — because SG&A of $624.6M consumed 68.5% of revenue and grew 17.9% against 13.6% revenue growth. R&D *fell* 9.6% to $103.2M. In Q2 2026 the GAAP operating loss was $(0.5)M and adjusted operating margin was 1.6%, down from 4.4% a year earlier.

**Stock-based compensation.** Q2 SBC was $31.2M, or 15.6% of revenue (H1: $61.9M, 15.2%). Reported Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $38.9M (19.4% margin) is arithmetically dominated by that add-back; EBITDA before it was $4.0M. Any EV/adjusted-EBITDA multiple on this name (~8.8x annualized) is not the right lens. Adjusted *operating income* and adjusted EPS do not add back SBC — only legal fees, restructuring and the SBC-related tax shortfall — so the $1.05–1.45 adjusted EPS guidance is a reasonably clean number that carries SBC as a cost. Unrecognized SBC of $199.3M over a weighted-average 2.1 years implies a forward run-rate near $95M/year versus roughly $124M annualized today, so the burden should ease absent large new grants.

**The tax distortion runs both ways, and the reported trailing P/E is an artifact.** Quoted trailing EPS of $4.89 and a 12.8x trailing multiple reflect FY2025 net income of $145.4M on pretax income of $65.7M — the difference being a $79.7M net tax benefit including roughly $88.8M from releasing the deferred tax valuation allowance. Normalizing to pretax income less current tax gives roughly $56.6M of FY2025 economic net income, meaning approximately 61% of reported net income was one-time. Any screen anchored on $4.89 of EPS or the resulting 12.8x is meaningless.

The mirror image is now hurting reported results. With the valuation allowance released, SBC tax shortfalls flow through income tax expense; because the share price collapsed, actual deductions on vesting equity fall far below book SBC expense. The H1 2026 effective tax rate was 299.2%, turning $5.5M of pretax income into a $(11.0)M net loss. FY2026 guidance carries a GAAP effective rate of 95–100% and GAAP diluted EPS of $(0.42) to $0.17 against adjusted $1.05–1.45. Federal NOLs were fully utilized at 12/31/2025, so cash taxes are now a recurring cost.

**Balance sheet and cash flow — the genuine strength.** Zero funded debt; total liabilities of $100.4M against $926.0M of assets, with operating leases ($29.1M non-current) the only debt-like item. Cash and investments of $415.2M at 6/30/2026 (up $10.6M in the half, after falling $111.9M during FY2025 on buybacks). H1 operating cash flow of +$36.1M versus -$4.0M. Current ratio 6.08. FY2025 free cash flow was $78.5M (8.6% margin) on $38.5M of capex; FY2026 capex is guided to $35–40M with $18.9M spent in the first half.

Two cautions on the cash-flow improvement. It is substantially working capital: receivables fell $16.4M in the half (a source), while inventories rose $25.2M (a use) — a smaller build than the roughly $65M full-year 2025 build, so the improvement is in the *rate* of accumulation. Q4 is seasonally strongest and Q1 weakest, so annualizing first-half cash flow is not reliable. Separately, purchase obligations of $90.5M at 12/31/2025 were all due in FY2026, a committed outflow against declining revenue.

**Inventory is the balance-sheet item to interrogate.** Inventories: $11.9M (2022) → $33.9M (2023) → $80.1M (2024) → $145.3M (2025) → $170.5M (6/30/2026), of which finished goods are $132.3M. First-half COGS of $56.8M annualizes to roughly $113.6M, putting total inventory at about 1.5 years (548 days) of COGS and finished goods alone at roughly 425 days. Inventory rose 17.3% in the half while revenue fell 3.2%. The excess-and-obsolete reserve was $1.3M at 12/31/2025, under 1% of gross inventory, after a $2.1M FY2025 charge for excess Inspire IV components. A deliberate finished-goods build is a defensible response to supplying two device generations across 280 territories through an uncertain launch, and Inspire V has high margin and long shelf life. But Project Horizon's $16–20M non-cash charge component, described as including production consolidation, is consistent with asset or inventory write-offs, and further write-downs are a live risk if a next-generation generator supersedes current stock.

## Growth Analysis

The top line is contracting and management is not guiding to a recovery. Q3 2026 is guided to decline 8–10%; the FY midpoint requires no fourth-quarter inflection.

Management attributes $120–130M of FY2026 revenue impact to coding disruption plus the CMS WISeR prior-authorization pilot. That figure was relayed by secondary sources summarizing the earnings call and does not appear in the 10-Q or press release, so it should be treated as an issuer estimate rather than a filing fact. If accurate, $855M plus $125M implies roughly $980M of "underlying" revenue, or +7.5% over FY2025. Two problems with leaning on that arithmetic: it is management's own attribution, and the company separately concedes GLP-1s are currently hurting revenue — so some of what sits in the "coding" bucket may be substitution.

The growth case therefore rests on three unresolved questions:

1. **Does the physician professional fee normalize?** Facility reimbursement is intact and CMS's April 2026 C-codes have been adopted into WISeR for the six pilot states, but C-codes address facility billing only. Two MACs require the -52 modifier when billing CPT 64568, with observed physician reimbursement ranging from no reduction to roughly 30% below the national average. The implanting surgeon gates procedure volume; a durable 30% fee cut for the same work is an economic problem, not a documentation problem. The stated long-term fix is a new CPT code through the AMA's multi-year cycle.
2. **Are GLP-1s a sequencing delay or a substitute?** The FY2025 10-K argued GLP-1s should *expand* the eligible pool by driving diagnosis and reducing BMI into Inspire's treatable range (ADHERE registry average BMI is 29; Inspire is contraindicated for complete concentric collapse). The Q2 10-Q added that revenue is currently being adversely impacted by GLP-1 adoption. A company survey of 200+ sleep physicians found some will trial GLP-1s first. If patients convert after a drug trial, this is a timing issue; if they never convert, the addressable market resets.
3. **Does WISeR expand geographically?** The pilot covers six states from mid-January 2026 and is expected to continue affecting the second half, though to a lesser extent.

On the positive side, the estimate revision picture is strong at the annual level: current-year consensus of $1.22 is up 35.5% in 30 days and 36.6% in 90 days; next year at $1.52 is up 18.3% and 15.1%. That magnitude of revision would normally be a powerful forward signal. Here it is degraded in quality for three reasons: the revenue guidance midpoint moved only +0.6%, so the revision is a cost-and-tax revision on a declining top line rather than a demand inflection; the current-quarter estimate was simultaneously cut 45.9% in 30 days, so the annual raise is back-half and tax-weighted; and the stock at 49.4x current-year consensus is not cheap relative to the improving estimates, which is the condition under which revision momentum carries the strongest predictive weight.

## Competitive Landscape

Nyxoah's scale largely exonerates competition as the cause of Inspire's 2026 decline. Nyxoah reported Q2 2026 worldwide preliminary net revenue of €7.7M (+21% sequentially), roughly 4.5% of Inspire's $200.6M quarter, against an Inspire year-over-year revenue *decline* of $16.5M. Even if every Nyxoah U.S. dollar came directly out of Inspire, direct share loss cannot account for most of the shortfall. Nyxoah reporting reimbursement stability while Inspire's U.S. revenue falls 9.6% supports the interpretation that the problem is specific to Inspire V's coding transition.

The forward picture is less comfortable. Nyxoah cited 262 trained surgeons, 123 ordering customers and 427 patients in prior authorization at its July 8 investor day, and raised $95M in June 2026, so it is capitalized to compound off that small base. Zacks reported on August 5 that LivaNova received U.S. approval for its aura6000 HGNS system; that claim is not confirmed in any Inspire filing or LivaNova primary source reviewed here, but if accurate it establishes a third U.S. entrant that Inspire's disclosures have not yet addressed. Share loss is a 2027+ risk rather than the 2026 cause.

Inspire's structural advantages remain: sole closed-loop approval, coverage for over 300 million U.S. lives, positive policies at all seven MACs, and a patent estate that just survived a challenge — the PTAB denied institution of all three Nyxoah IPR petitions on April 14, 2026, sending Inspire's infringement case (which seeks lost profits) to district court with the patents intact. Against that, Nyxoah has counterclaims in Delaware and a Unified Patent Court action in Munich against Inspire IV, where an injunction would affect the fastest-growing (if smallest) revenue base.

Beyond HGNS, the competitive set includes CPAP incumbents (ResMed, Philips, Fisher & Paykel), alternative surgical approaches, oral appliances, and now pharmacotherapy — Zepbound approved for OSA in December 2024 with SURMOUNT-OSA showing roughly 50.7% AHI reduction, and Apnimed's oral candidate in Phase 3.

Trajectory, not level, drives the score here: the franchise is the clear category leader, but territories were cut 5%, revenue is contracting, and the number of approved U.S. competitors is rising.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement (dominant).** The chronology matters: Inspire IV billed under CPT 64582; 2025 Inspire V procedures under 64568; November 2025 Medicare determinations created code confusion; the FY2025 10-K anticipated a transition to 64582 with a -52 modifier cutting the professional fee by approximately 10% to 50% of base and warned this "will likely influence physicians' willingness to perform the Inspire V procedure"; March 2026 AHA guidance recommended unlisted code 64999 for commercial cases; April 2026 C-codes addressed facility billing only. Two MACs now require the -52 modifier. More MACs may follow.

**Litigation and government investigations — unusual density for a $1.7B company.** A DOJ Civil Investigative Demand received 1/17/2025 from the U.S. Attorney (D. Minn.) under the False Claims Act, including Anti-Kickback allegations, concerning marketing, promotion and reimbursement practices; no accrual. A securities class action (*Indiana Public Retirement System*, D. Minn.) whose July 30, 2026 amended complaint expanded the class period to 11/4/2024–5/4/2026 and added fraud allegations regarding Medicare/CPT coding; no accrual. Three derivative suits naming the CEO and all directors. A qui tam FCA case in which DOJ declined to intervene (1/28/2026) but the relator is proceeding through a second motion to dismiss. Legal costs already run $5.1M in the first half, guided to $0.23–0.25 of FY2026 EPS. All four matters concern reimbursement and promotion practices and predate the coding disruption becoming public. No loss estimate is possible from the disclosures; amended class periods routinely follow price declines and settlements typically resolve at a fraction of nominal damages with partial D&O coverage, but this is an unquantified claim on the $415M cash balance.

**Inventory write-down risk.** $170.5M against a $1.3M reserve, with production consolidation underway and $16–20M of non-cash restructuring charges.

**Concentration.** Single product line; many sole-source suppliers; all manufacturing U.S.-based; R&D and back-office in two facilities without disclosed redundancy; finished goods at two third-party sites. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but Medicare policy is the concentration.

**Execution.** The Q2 10-Q added a risk factor that Project Horizon "may not achieve our intended outcome," citing attrition and morale. Six Item 5.02 filings (officer/director departures or elections) occurred between August 2025 and July 2026, including two within three days in late July 2026; the identities behind the January and July events could not be established. Unexplained C-suite churn during a turnaround reduces confidence in guidance delivery.

**One clarification on Project Horizon:** the ~$30M of annualized savings is explicitly described as capacity "expected to be invested in revenue growth initiatives." Treating it as incremental EBITDA misreads the disclosure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility collapsed from 82.3% (July 22) to 44.6% following the print, against 70.0% trailing realized volatility — a -25.4 point spread and a 3rd-percentile reading within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (44.6% at 13 days, 46.7% at 41 days, 63.0% at 104 days), so the market has discharged event risk near term while pricing meaningful uncertainty into the November tenor that spans the Q3 print. For a holder trimming into strength, near-dated downside protection is unusually cheap relative to how this stock has actually traded.

Positioning is mixed rather than confirming: put/call open interest fell from 1.18 to 0.45 (call-heavy), while put/call volume ratio is 1.54 (put-heavy flow). Short interest of 19.8% of float with only 3.8 days to cover is the main mechanical risk to a bearish view; any credible resolution of the coding issue would meet a crowded, quickly-coverable short base. That squeeze risk is a reason to trim rather than to short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Management's attribution is correct and the drag is transitory: coding plus WISeR cost $120–130M of FY2026 revenue, meaning underlying demand grew roughly 7.5% to ~$980M. Prior-authorization submission trends are already improving, C-codes are embedded in WISeR for the pilot states, most MACs do not require the -52 modifier, and CMS has proposed 2027 OPPS and ASC facility payment increases (12% and 15% per a competitor's characterization of a proposed rule). GLP-1s ultimately expand the funnel by driving diagnosis and pulling BMI into Inspire's treatable range, as the 10-K argues. Against that recovering revenue, an 85.5% gross margin business with opex already cut 7.4% delivers extreme operating leverage: $1.0B of revenue at 12% adjusted operating margin produces roughly $2.85 of EPS, worth $88 with net cash. The PTAB's denial of all three Nyxoah IPRs preserves the patent moat, the balance sheet carries no debt and $415M of investments, and $100M of buyback authorization remains through August 2027. At 1.55x EV/sales, very little of this is priced.

**Bear case.** The -52 modifier is technically defensible for Inspire V, since one fewer lead is implanted, which means the surgeon's fee reduction may be permanent, and the stated remedy runs through the AMA's multi-year CPT process. Procedure volume is gated by surgeons whose economics were cut roughly 30% for the same work. The company's own disclosure on GLP-1s moved from "expands our long-term pool" to "adversely impacting our revenue." Revenue keeps declining into 2027, at which point Nyxoah is compounding 21% sequentially with fresh capital and LivaNova may be a third U.S. entrant. Adjusted EPS of $1.05–1.45 is achieved partly through R&D cuts (already -9.6% in FY2025) that mortgage the next product cycle; GAAP operating income is negative; SBC runs 15% of revenue with real share-count consequences (first-half gross grants of 1,861,382 shares at PSU target equal 6.44% of shares outstanding); $170.5M of inventory sits against a $1.3M reserve; and the DOJ CID, expanded securities class action, three derivative suits and live qui tam all carry no accrual against a $415M cash balance. At 48x current-year adjusted earnings with revenue shrinking, the multiple has no support if 2027 fails to inflect.

**Where we come out.** The bear case requires nothing new to happen — only for the current run-rate to persist. The bull case requires a favorable resolution of two independent binaries (professional fee, GLP-1 sequencing) plus no share loss from three approved competitors. Base case fair value is at spot, and the sell side agrees: consensus mean $60.40 against a $60.28 price, with 13 of 17 ratings at hold or worse after an 18% rally.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is an upgrade from four consecutive AVOID ratings, and the reason is specific rather than a reaction to price: first-half operating cash flow turned positive ($36.1M versus -$4.0M), operating expense fell 7.4%, cash and investments rose $10.6M, and adjusted EPS guidance was raised $0.30 at both ends. Solvency was never the question and is now further from it. What has not changed is that revenue is declining with no guided recovery, the multiple is 48x current-year adjusted earnings, and the reimbursement and litigation binaries are open.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the post-print strength rather than waiting for the Q3 report, which is guided to a revenue decline of 8–10% and is therefore a downside-skewed event. Holders willing to retain a residual position should size it to the net-cash floor: the $14.36 per share of investments plus zero debt underwrites roughly $38–42 of downside support, so a position sized to tolerate a 37% drawdown is defensible for those with a multi-year view on the HGNS category. Given 19.8% short interest and 3.8 days to cover, we do not recommend shorting. Near-dated implied volatility at 44.6% against 70.0% realized makes collars or protective puts a cheaper expression of caution than outright exit for tax-constrained holders.

**Targets from the $60.28 basis.** Base $62 (+2.9%), bull $88 (+46.0%), bear $38 (-37.0%). Analyst range: low $40, high $85, mean $60.40.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD or ACCUMULATE).**
1. Evidence that the professional fee is normalizing: the two MACs requiring the -52 modifier withdraw the requirement, or the AMA CPT Editorial Panel accepts an application for a distinct Inspire V code with a stated effective date.
2. Sequential U.S. revenue growth in Q3 2026 *and* a Q4 guide implying year-over-year U.S. growth — the specific evidence that would convert the "coding drag" hypothesis from management attribution into observed demand.
3. Price below $46–48 (approximately 1.1x EV/sales on the guidance midpoint, or roughly 21x FY2027 consensus ex-cash), which would restore a margin of safety even without resolution of the binaries.
4. Resumption of buyback under the $100M remaining authorization at current prices, which would signal management's own confidence and support the float.

**Downgrade triggers (back to AVOID).**
1. Additional MACs adopting the -52 modifier, or commercial payers migrating to unlisted code 64999 at scale.
2. A FY2027 revenue guide below $835M, confirming the decline is structural rather than transitional.
3. A material inventory write-down (>$25M) or a disclosed reasonably-possible loss range on the DOJ CID or securities class action.
4. Confirmation of LivaNova's aura6000 U.S. launch accompanied by evidence of Inspire share or price erosion.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next hard checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report (early November 2026), which should show whether the coding and WISeR headwinds ease sequentially as management expects and whether Project Horizon actions — substantially complete by year-end — hold opex flat while the $30M is reinvested. The FY2027 guide in early 2027 is the decisive test of whether this is a trough or a reset.

## Conclusion

Inspire has a strong balance sheet attached to a franchise whose durability is genuinely in question. The facts on the left side of the ledger are unambiguous: $415.2M of cash and investments (23.8% of market cap), zero funded debt, 85.5% gross margins, positive operating cash flow, an Altman Z-Score near 6.8, and a management team that just delivered a real cost reduction and a $0.30 adjusted EPS raise. The facts on the right side are equally clear: revenue fell 7.6% in the quarter, the second half is guided down roughly 9%, the surgeon's professional fee has been cut about 30% at two MACs with a multi-year fix path, the company newly concedes GLP-1s are hurting revenue, three HGNS devices may soon compete in the U.S., $170.5M of inventory sits against a $1.3M reserve, and a DOJ False Claims Act inquiry plus an expanded securities class action carry no accrual.

At $60.28 the stock prices most of the good and little of the bad: 48.2x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint, 39.7x next-year consensus, and precisely at the $60.40 consensus mean target after an 18% rally on a cost-driven beat. Base case fair value of $62 offers 2.9% upside against 37% downside to $38 if the reimbursement reset proves permanent. That distribution favors reducing exposure into strength and revisiting in the mid-$40s or on the first hard evidence — sequential U.S. revenue growth, or a CPT code application accepted — that the franchise is recovering rather than resetting.

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Overall score 4.5/10.**
