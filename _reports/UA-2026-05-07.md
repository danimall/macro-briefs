## Executive Summary

Under Armour is mid-restructuring with declining revenue, negative earnings, and a brand repositioning strategy that remains unproven. FY2025 revenue fell 9.4% to $5.16B, the first nine months of FY2026 show a further 4.7% decline, and North America (60% of revenue) continues contracting at roughly 8% annually. The company reported net losses of $452M in the first nine months of FY2026, driven by a $270M tax valuation allowance, restructuring charges, and insurance litigation accruals.

The bull case centers on early restructuring benefits (gross margins improved 180bps in FY2025 before giving back 310bps on tariffs), strategic hires from Adidas, raised FY2026 operating income guidance to $110M, and Prem Watsa's $70M+ accumulation at $5.50-$6.30. The bear case is straightforward: revenue is still falling, the company competes against Nike and Adidas with a fraction of their marketing budgets, tariff escalation is a direct hit to the supply chain (Vietnam, Indonesia, Cambodia), and management itself signaled doubt about U.S. profitability by recording full valuation allowances on federal deferred tax assets.

At $6.25 with negative trailing EPS (-$0.47) and a forward P/E of approximately -5x, the stock is uninvestable on a fundamental earnings basis today. The turnaround thesis requires several quarters of positive execution (stabilizing North America, demonstrating brand elevation works, managing tariff costs) before the risk/reward becomes favorable for new capital.

## Company Overview

Under Armour develops, markets, and distributes branded performance apparel (67% of revenue), footwear (23%), and accessories (8%), plus a small licensing business (2%). Distribution splits approximately 58% wholesale / 40% DTC / 2% licensing. Geographically: North America 60%, EMEA 21%, APAC 15%, Latin America 4%.

The company operates ~195 retail stores in North America (180 Factory House, 15 Brand House). Manufacturing is outsourced primarily to Jordan, Vietnam, Cambodia, and Indonesia — creating direct tariff exposure under current trade policy. Kevin Plank, the founder, returned as CEO in April 2024 after stepping back in 2020. His Class B shares provide majority voting control regardless of economic ownership.

- Gross margin compressed to 44.4% in Q3 FY2026 (-310bps): tariffs (-200bps) + resumed promotions (-140bps)

**Balance Sheet:**

**Cash Flow:**

**Key Concern — Interest Expense:** The new $400M at 7.25% costs $29M annually. Against a $110M operating income target, that's a 26% burden from interest alone.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is negative across nearly every metric. North America revenue fell 11.4% in FY2025 and is guided to decline ~8% in FY2026. The only geographic bright spot is EMEA (~9% growth guided) and Latin America, but these represent 25% of revenue combined and cannot offset the North American erosion at current scale.

The strategic pivot — hire Eric Liedtke from Adidas, reduce promotional activity, elevate brand positioning, focus on fewer/better products — is theoretically sound. Liedtke built Adidas's cultural relevance during its 2014-2019 renaissance. The NFL official supplier partnership provides visibility. But brand turnarounds in athletic apparel typically take 2-4 years to show top-line results, and Under Armour is executing this amid consumer headwinds and tariff escalation.

The Curry Brand separation removes a culturally relevant asset in exchange for cost savings ($69.7M in termination charges taken). This simplifies the business but narrows the brand's appeal in footwear.

The Q3 FY2026 beat (actual $0.09 vs. estimate -$0.02) was the first significant positive surprise, but it was followed by a $270M valuation allowance that signals management's own skepticism about near-term U.S. earnings power.

## Valuation Assessment

At $6.25 with market cap of ~$1.28B, the stock trades at:
- Trailing P/E: negative (N/A)
- Forward P/E: approximately -5x (still losing money on forward estimates)
- P/Sales (TTM ~$5.0B): ~0.26x
- P/Book ($1.44B equity): ~0.89x
- EV/Revenue: ~0.35x (EV ~$1.75B including net debt)

Against peers: G-III Apparel trades at 21.2x P/E (profitable); Carter's at 14.7x. Under Armour is not directly comparable because it's currently loss-making. Capri Holdings trades at -2.0x P/E — another loss-making luxury/apparel company.

The 0.26x P/Sales and 0.89x P/Book are optically cheap, but these metrics are only relevant if the company can return to sustainable profitability. At the $110M operating income target for FY2026, after $29M interest and assuming a normalized 25% tax rate, net income would be approximately $60M — implying a forward P/E of roughly 21x on a full-year run-rate. That's in line with peers, meaning current pricing already embeds a successful turnaround to the guided $110M OI level.

The upside case requires operating income expansion well beyond $110M — which requires revenue stabilization (not currently visible) or further cost reduction (limited after $255M restructuring).

## Competitive Landscape

Under Armour's competitive position is its weakest dimension. The company competes against:
- **Nike:** ~$50B revenue, dominant marketing spend, cultural relevance across multiple sports and lifestyle
- **Adidas:** ~$24B revenue, successfully rebuilt cultural positioning 2014-2019, now recovering from Yeezy crisis
- **Lululemon:** ~$10B revenue, dominant in premium athleisure with superior unit economics
- **Puma, New Balance, On Running:** Growing challengers taking share in running/lifestyle

UA's $5.2B revenue gives it roughly 1/5th of Nike's scale. This translates directly to disadvantages in athlete endorsements, marketing spend, retail distribution leverage, and R&D investment. The brand's heritage in performance apparel (moisture-wicking fabric technology) has been commoditized — competitors offer equivalent technical fabrics at every price point.

The decline is evident in sell-through data: North America wholesale revenue continues falling despite the broader athletic apparel market growing low-single-digits. Under Armour is losing share, and whether brand elevation can reverse this without the marketing budgets of larger competitors remains unclear.

Positive signals: Eric Liedtke's hire demonstrates serious intent. The reduced SKU count and premium pricing strategy mirrors what worked at Adidas and what Lululemon executes consistently. But execution against significantly larger competitors in a weakening consumer environment is genuinely difficult.

## Risk Assessment

**Material Weakness in Internal Controls:** Disclosed failure to design effective controls over balance sheet account reconciliations — produced immaterial errors going back to FY2021. Expected remediation by end of FY2026 but not guaranteed. This raises questions about the reliability of reported financials.

**Tariff Exposure:** ~$80M estimated FY2026 COGS impact (~160bps gross margin). With primary sourcing from Vietnam, Indonesia, Cambodia, and Jordan, further tariff escalation under current trade policy creates open-ended downside.

**Insurance Litigation ($98.5M):** Fourth Circuit reversed a favorable ruling on D&O insurer payment obligations. If UA loses the rehearing petition, $98.5M+ cash outflow. This is ~7.5% of current market cap.

**Consumer Spending Environment:** Consumer discretionary spending is under pressure from uncertainty. Under Armour's mid-price positioning makes it vulnerable to trade-down behavior (consumers choosing private label or value alternatives).

**Valuation Allowance Signal:** Management recorded full valuation allowances on all U.S. federal deferred tax assets, effectively stating they cannot demonstrate probable near-term U.S. profitability. This is inconsistent with a bullish turnaround narrative.

**Short Interest:** 22.4M shares short with 9.3 days to cover — elevated but not extreme. This creates potential squeeze dynamics but also reflects informed bearish positioning.

**Kevin Plank Key-Man Risk:** Majority voting control via Class B shares. All strategic decisions flow through one individual whose prior tenure ended with significant shareholder value destruction (stock down ~80% from peak under his watch).

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No specific dated catalyst for further decline is identifiable in the near term. The next earnings report (Q4 FY2026) should occur around late May/June 2026 and could disappoint if tariff impacts accelerate, but the company just raised guidance. The insurance litigation resolution timing is uncertain (appellate process, months to years). Without a specific downside catalyst on a defined timeline, the short thesis relies on "bad company" rather than active deterioration with a trigger.

**Timing window:** Indeterminate. The restructuring is expected to be substantially complete by end of FY2026 (March 2026), meaning the period of maximum negative GAAP earnings is ending. Short interest at 9.3 days to cover suggests meaningful crowding already.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Combined with Watsa's accumulation and $385M remaining buyback authorization, there is meaningful demand-side support. A positive earnings surprise could trigger covering.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data provided. The 9.3 days-to-cover metric suggests somewhat elevated borrow demand.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case fair value ~$4.00-4.50 (0.6-0.7x book value, distressed multiple). Current price $6.25. Upside risk on short: $7.50-8.00 (1.0-1.1x book). Risk/reward approximately 1:1 — insufficient for active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** No specific timed catalyst, elevated short interest with squeeze potential from Watsa accumulation + buyback, and the restructuring negative news flow is largely behind us. The stock is a poor investment but not an active short candidate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8.00-9.00, +28-44%):**
- Brand elevation strategy succeeds; North America revenue stabilizes by H2 FY2027
- Liedtke replicates Adidas-style cultural renaissance at Under Armour
- Operating margins normalize to 7-8% on $5.0B revenue = $350-400M operating income
- Tariff impact contained or offset by pricing actions
- Watsa's conviction proves correct; additional value investors accumulate
- Stock re-rates to 1.2-1.5x book value ($8.50-10.50)

**Bear Case ($3.50-4.00, -36-44%):**
- North America decline accelerates as consumer weakens further
- Tariff escalation erodes gross margins below 42%
- Insurance litigation resolved unfavorably ($98.5M cash outflow)
- Brand elevation fails to drive demand; market share losses become permanent
- Management burns through cash on restructuring without revenue recovery
- Stock trades to 0.5x book value (~$3.50)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive revenue growth in North America (not yet achieved; earliest visibility: Q1-Q2 FY2027 reports, fall 2026)
2. Gross margins stable above 46% despite tariff environment (demonstrating pricing power)
3. Resolution of material weakness in internal controls (expected end of FY2026)
4. Insurance litigation resolution quantified and manageable (<$100M total cash impact)

**Timeline:** The headwinds likely persist 12-18 months. The brand turnaround, if successful, will show in revenue data by late calendar 2026 / early 2027 at the earliest. Until then, the stock remains a negative-earnings consumer discretionary name in a weakening spending environment with execution risk across multiple dimensions.

## Conclusion

Under Armour presents a speculative turnaround thesis with genuine positive signals (Watsa accumulation, raised guidance, improving cash flow, strategic hires) offset by fundamental deterioration (declining revenue, negative earnings, material weakness, tariff exposure, consumer headwinds). The stock's 0.26x P/S and 0.89x P/B are optically cheap but require sustained profitability to be relevant — and management's own valuation allowance recording signals doubt about near-term U.S. earnings.

The correct approach is to wait for evidence that the turnaround is working before committing capital. Evidence means positive North America revenue growth, sustained gross margins above 46%, and resolution of the material weakness. None of these conditions are met today. The risk/reward at $6.25 does not adequately compensate for the probability of continued operational deterioration in a hostile consumer and tariff environment. AVOID.
