**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SAIC is one of the largest pure-play technology services providers to the U.S. government, running a labor-arbitrage integration model where the moat is scale, relationships, and cleared workforce rather than proprietary IP. The central fact of the story is a multi-year organic revenue decline — FY23 $7.70B to FY26 $7.26B (down 3% last year, ~3.3% organic) — that management is deliberately trading for margin and free cash flow. Adjusted EBITDA margin improved to ~9.7% in FY26, operating cash flow rose to $609M from $494M and $396M in the prior two years, and the company retired ~10% of its share count over the last twelve months. Management's strategy is to walk away from commoditized Enterprise IT recompetes, protect profitability, and lean on buybacks to hold EPS up while the top line contracts.

The investment question is whether this is disciplined pruning or a mask over competitive erosion. The February 2026 guidance cut — FY27 organic growth revised from +0/+3% to -2%/-4% on "two recent unfavorable recompete award decisions" — argues for the latter, because recompete losses mean incumbent work lost to competitors on price, the worst kind of revenue loss for a services contractor. The Q1 FY27 print (reported June 1) argues for the former: revenue back to +0.5% organic, operating margin expanding to 9.4% from 6.4%, net income up 69%, and funded backlog up to $3.74B. One good quarter after a guidance cut is not confirmation, and Q1 was flattered by a $12M investment-sale gain, but it does suggest the recompete losses were contained rather than the leading edge of a cliff.

At ~$119 the stock trades near 11.7x the FY27 EPS consensus of ~$10.18 and ~10.8x next-year ~$11.00, a discount to the diversified-support-services peer median but appropriate for a business with no organic growth, 3.6x leverage, goodwill at roughly twice book equity, a leadership team still settling after broad churn, and a DOJ criminal antitrust investigation that carries a low-probability but existential debarment tail. We rate SAIC a HOLD. The cash-return story is real and the valuation is not demanding, but there is no asymmetric edge here and no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. Our score sits at 4.9, essentially in line with the recent 4.5–5.3 range, reflecting a balanced setup where improving cash quality is offset by structural top-line and governance risk.

## Company Overview

SAIC delivers IT modernization, digital engineering, AI, mission systems support, training and simulation, and ground vehicle support to federal customers, with roughly 23,000–24,000 employees, ~1,700 active contracts and task orders, and a workforce that is largely security-cleared. Revenue is ~98% U.S. government, split roughly 52% Department of War and 46% Intelligence and other federal agencies. The business runs in two segments:

- **Defense & Intelligence** — ~77% of revenue ($5.58B FY26), lower margin (~8.6% adjusted operating margin), heavily cost-reimbursement (79% of that segment's work).
- **Civilian** — ~23% of revenue ($1.68B FY26), higher margin (~13.6% adjusted OM), mostly time-and-materials (67%).

SAIC competes as a prime integrator against Leidos, Booz Allen, CACI, Parsons, Peraton, ManTech, and the services arms of the primes (GD, Lockheed, Northrop, RTX). The 10-K states patents are "not generally" material — this is an execution business, not a technology-ownership business. That framing matters for how the market should value it: the earnings quality is stable and cash-generative, but there is no defensible IP moat to underwrite a premium multiple.

The company operates on a January/February fiscal year; FY2026 ended January 30, 2026, and Q1 FY2027 ended May 1, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is the core problem.** FY23 $7.70B → FY24 $7.44B → FY25 $7.48B → FY26 $7.26B, a 3% decline last year that was ~3.3% organic after backing out the SilverEdge acquisition. Management attributes the erosion to contract completions, recompete losses, and volume ramp-downs concentrated in larger, commoditized Enterprise IT programs — the segment it is deliberately de-emphasizing.

**Margins and cash flow are the offset.** GAAP operating margin fell from 10.0% (FY24) to 7.2% (FY26), but adjusted EBITDA margin improved to ~9.7% from ~9.0% in FY24 as low-margin work was shed. FCF was $577M in FY26 (a 7.9% margin), and operating cash flow continued a multi-year uptrend. Capex is minimal (~$32M), consistent with an asset-light services model. FY26 net income and cash were flattered by a low 7.5% effective tax rate, driven by OBBBA R&D expensing and a $47M IRS audit-settlement benefit, part of which is non-recurring.

**Balance sheet is leveraged and goodwill-heavy.** Total debt is ~$2.5B against only $1.5B of stockholders' equity, so D/E is ~3.6x. The Altman Z of 1.96 sits in the grey zone, depressed by leverage and thin equity rather than operating distress. Goodwill ($2.94B) plus intangibles ($0.73B) equal ~69% of total assets, so goodwill alone is roughly twice book equity. The annual (qualitative) and a January 2026 quantitative post-reorganization impairment test both passed, and debt is termed out across 2028–2033 with no revolver draw and covenant compliance as of Q1 FY27. A material goodwill write-down driven by lower reporting-unit cash-flow assumptions would optically wipe out equity, even if non-cash.

**Capital return is aggressive relative to a shrinking top line.** FY26 buybacks were $422M (~4.0M shares) plus $175M in Q1 FY27 alone, taking share count from ~48M to ~43M year over year. The quarterly dividend is $0.37 (~1.3% yield). Buying back ~10% of shares on 3.6x leverage into a declining revenue base is shareholder-friendly and the primary support under EPS, but it is a lever that could be constrained by the executive order targeting buybacks/dividends for underperforming defense contractors.

**Q1 FY27 reversed the guidance-cut narrative.** Revenue rose 2% to $1.906B (+0.5% organic), operating margin expanded to 9.4% from 6.4%, net income rose 69% to $115M, EPS was $2.61, and funded backlog grew to $3.74B from $3.57B, though a $12M investment-sale gain flattered the quarter.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here in the near term. Management's own FY27 guide is -2% to -4% organic ($7.0–7.2B revenue), and the company is explicitly trading revenue for margin. The earnings surprise record is strong — four straight beats, including 39% in the most recent quarter — but that pattern reflects conservative guidance and margin discipline, not top-line acceleration.

Estimate revisions are mixed and modest. Current-year EPS consensus has risen 5.7% over 90 days to $10.18 and next-year is up 2.6% to $11.00, but next-quarter estimates have been cut 7.2% over the same window. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection; it is a company where cost discipline and buybacks are nudging EPS up while revenue stays flat-to-down. The strongest forward signal — rising estimates plus accelerating revenue — is absent. Revenue is not accelerating.

The pivot toward higher-margin digital engineering, AI, and mission systems (and away from commoditized Enterprise IT) is the right strategic direction and mirrors what the entire peer group is attempting. The SilverEdge acquisition ($203M net, October 2025) adds IP-differentiated mission solutions and contributed $27M in FY26 and $19M in Q1 FY27, a modest mix-shift lever. On the demand side, the July 2025 reconciliation package added ~$150B non-border defense and ~$175B border-security funding usable through GFY2029, directly relevant to SAIC's Naval, border-surveillance, and Homeland Security exposure. That tailwind competes against a procurement environment moving toward multi-award vehicles, OTAs, and small-business set-asides — all of which compress SAIC's addressable market and pricing. Total backlog of $22.6B ($3.74B funded) provides cushion, but the IDIQ/task-order structure means bookings must refresh annually.

Growth prospects score 4.5: the strategic pivot is credible and the Q1 backlog uptick is encouraging, but the company is guiding to organic decline, revision velocity is thin, and the recompete losses undercut the growth-durability case.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$119, SAIC trades at ~11.7x FY27 consensus EPS of $10.18 and ~10.8x next-year $11.00. Against the diversified-support-services peer set, that is a discount to the median P/E of 36.8, but the peer median is heavily distorted by AMTM (36.8x, itself a depressed federal-services turnaround), UNF (37.3x), and CXW (21.9x). The cleaner comparable is KBR at 10.4x, a similar government-services model, which suggests SAIC is fairly valued rather than cheap on a like-for-like basis. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $93, mean $120, median $125, high $137), consistent with a stock trading near fair value with no clear directional edge.

Per our own scoring discipline, a low headline multiple on a structurally declining business is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal — cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way. SAIC's discount reflects genuine issues: no organic growth, high leverage, goodwill fragility, governance churn, and a criminal-investigation tail. This is a fairly priced no-growth cash generator, not a mispriced compounder. The buyback provides a mechanical EPS tailwind, and the FCF yield (~$577M against a ~$4.9B market cap, ~11.7%) is supportive of the price. There is no identifiable catalyst that forces the multiple higher — the FY27 guide is for decline, and a re-rating requires proof that the recompete losses were an aberration, which will take more than one quarter to establish.

We score valuation 5.5 — the FCF yield and buyback support keep it from being a value trap, but the compressed multiple appropriately reflects structural headwinds rather than a mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

SAIC's competitive position is stable-to-eroding. The pay-versus-performance data is telling: cumulative TSR of 113.75 badly trails the peer-group TSR of 195.65 over the measurement window, indicating the market views SAIC as a laggard in the industry-wide pivot from commoditized IT toward differentiated technology transformation work. The recompete losses that drove the FY27 guidance cut are the sharpest evidence — losing incumbent positions on price is a share-loss signal, not a cyclical one.

The moat is relationship-, scale-, and clearance-based, which provides some stickiness (recompetes are contested, not guaranteed losses) but no pricing power in a procurement environment consolidating around competitive multi-award vehicles and OTAs that lower barriers to non-traditional entrants. The DoW's Acquisition Transformation Strategy intensifies this. SAIC's response — pruning low-margin work and pushing into digital engineering, AI, and mission systems via SilverEdge — is sound but undifferentiated; Leidos, Booz Allen, and CACI are running the same playbook. The Q1 FY27 backlog uptick is the one recent data point suggesting the share bleed may be contained.

Competitive position scores 4.5, consistent with the recent decline in this dimension from 6.0. The trajectory is the concern: a competitor losing recompetes and trailing peer TSR is positioned defensively, not offensively.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dense with specific, hard-to-quantify tails:

1. **Recompete losses / top-line erosion.** The FY27 guidance cut on two recompete losses is the dominant near-term risk. If more incumbent positions fall, the shrink-to-profitability story flips into managed decline.

2. **DOGE-era spending disruption.** Risk factors were materially expanded around agency contract reviews, personnel reductions, stop-work orders, and the FAR overhaul. A 43-day federal shutdown (Oct–Nov 2025) cost ~$26M of revenue, and a separate DHS shutdown ran Feb–April 2026.

3. **Buyback-restriction executive order.** An executive order could limit buybacks/dividends for contractors that underperform on critical defense supply contracts. Given that buybacks are the primary EPS support, constraining this lever would remove a key prop.

4. **Goodwill impairment.** Goodwill at ~2x equity means a downward revision to reporting-unit cash-flow assumptions on a declining revenue base could trigger an optically severe (though non-cash) write-down.

5. **DOJ criminal antitrust investigation.** Federal grand jury subpoenas received April 2022 and October 2023; SAIC is cooperating and cannot estimate potential penalties. For a 98%-government-revenue contractor, an adverse antitrust or False Claims outcome carries suspension/debarment risk — a low-probability but existential tail that a standard screen would miss.

6. **Leadership churn.** CEO Toni Townes-Whitley departed (with $16M of Q3 FY26 executive-transition costs), Jim Reagan moved from CFO to Interim CEO to a permanent appointment around June 2026, and a dense cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (Oct 2025 through June 2026) alongside a full operating-model reorganization signals a broad reshuffle. Execution risk is elevated until the new structure proves out.

7. **DCAA indirect-cost audits** remain open, and the legacy Engility pension adds GAAP earnings volatility.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.3% is essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 39.8% (spread -0.5%), so options are not pricing a directional edge — IV is fair, not rich or cheap. Put/call skew is balanced at -0.5%. Term-structure backwardation (near-term 39.3% > long-term 36.6%) and a jump in the put/call open-interest ratio from 0.33 to 1.34 since early June suggest the market is positioning for near-term stress or hedging around the next print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.4%. This is consistent with a stock whose next earnings report is a genuine test of whether the Q1 inflection holds. Short interest is moderate at 7.9% of float (4.8 days to cover) — not a crowded short, and not a squeeze setup.

Risk factors score 4.5: the DOJ investigation, buyback-restriction EO, goodwill fragility, and leadership churn are specific, material, and not fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** SAIC is a disciplined portfolio-pruning turnaround. Management is walking away from commoditized, price-competitive Enterprise IT and reinvesting toward higher-margin digital engineering, AI, and mission systems, with SilverEdge accelerating the mix shift. Adjusted EBITDA margin is heading toward ~10%, FCF exceeds $577M with a >$600M FY27 guide, and the buyback is retiring ~10% of shares annually. The Q1 FY27 print — organic revenue back to positive, operating margin up 300bps, backlog up — suggests the recompete losses were contained. If the next two quarters confirm stabilized organic revenue and sustained margin, the stock re-rates from ~11.7x toward the mid-teens as the market pays for FCF durability. The $150B/$175B reconciliation defense and border funding provides a demand tailwind. Bull case ~$137 (analyst high), roughly +15% from current.

**Bear Case.** The shrink-to-profitability framing masks competitive erosion. The FY27 guide is for -2%/-4% organic decline, driven by recompete losses that signal share loss on price in a procurement environment that structurally favors multi-award competition and new entrants. Peer-lagging TSR (113.75 vs 195.65) confirms the market's skepticism. Buybacks on 3.6x leverage prop up EPS but could be constrained by the buyback-restriction executive order, and a goodwill impairment on the declining base would optically impair equity. The DOJ antitrust investigation carries debarment tail risk. If organic decline reaccelerates and the buyback lever is curtailed, the stock re-rates lower toward ~$93 (analyst low), roughly -22% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The improving cash quality and reasonable valuation offset the structural top-line and governance risks, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stable or positive organic revenue with sustained operating margin above 9%, confirming the recompete losses were contained.
- Funded backlog growth above $4.0B alongside a demonstrable improvement in recompete/new-award win rates.
- Resolution or dismissal of the DOJ antitrust investigation, removing the debarment tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY27 organic decline reaccelerating beyond the -4% guide, or a further guidance cut citing additional recompete losses.
- A material goodwill impairment or an executive order that restricts SAIC's buyback authorization, removing the primary EPS support.
- An adverse development in the DOJ antitrust matter (charges, settlement with debarment implications).

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly print (Q2 FY27, expected early September 2026) is the natural checkpoint — it is the first quarter that can confirm or reverse the Q1 inflection without the benefit of the one-time investment gain. The term-structure backwardation in options confirms the market views this print as the key near-term event.

## Conclusion

SAIC is a fairly valued, cash-generative federal-services turnaround in the middle of a deliberate shrink-to-profitability transition. The cash story is genuine — ~$577M FCF, ~10% adjusted EBITDA margin, ~10% annual share-count reduction — and the ~11.7x forward multiple is not demanding. But the top line is guided to decline, the recompete losses are a real share-loss signal, leadership is still settling after broad churn, goodwill is twice equity, and the DOJ antitrust investigation is an existential-but-unquantifiable tail for a 98%-government contractor. The Q1 FY27 beat is encouraging but is one quarter after a guidance cut, flattered by a one-time gain, and insufficient to confirm the benign interpretation.

We rate SAIC a **HOLD**. The setup is balanced: buyback-supported EPS and FCF-yield support against structural organic decline and governance/regulatory risk. Own it for the cash return and the option on a confirmed inflection, but there is no asymmetric edge and no reason to add conviction until the Q2 print shows the Q1 stabilization was real. Our overall score of 4.9 sits within the recent 4.5–5.3 band, reflecting improving cash quality partly offset by continued competitive and governance concerns.
