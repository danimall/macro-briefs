**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Terex is two-thirds of the way through a deliberate portfolio transformation. Through the late-2024 ESG acquisition and the February 2026 REV Group merger, management has re-weighted revenue toward U.S.-based, replacement-driven, less-cyclical end markets: refuse/recycling (Heil), electric-utility equipment (Terex Utilities), and fire/emergency vehicles (REV). Pro-forma roughly 80% of revenue is now North American and roughly 85% of that is U.S.-manufactured, explicitly designed to reduce tariff and macro sensitivity. The legacy cyclical core, Aerials (Genie), is under strategic review for divestiture. The 2026 guidance of $7.5–8.1B revenue, $930M–$1.0B adjusted EBITDA, and $4.50–5.00 adjusted EPS is driven almost entirely by acquired scale rather than organic growth.

The investment question is whether the de-cyclicalization thesis holds through execution. The company carries net leverage of 2.4x, ~$2.7B of debt, and a balance sheet where goodwill ($2.54B) plus intangibles ($2.99B) total roughly 54% of assets. Interest expense nearly doubled to $177M in 2025. The Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $(0.93)/share is optical, driven by $112M inventory step-up, $164M purchase-price-allocation amortization, and $68M deal costs; adjusted EBITDA grew 35% with adjusted EPS of $0.98 beating the $0.78 consensus. But beneath the adjusted figures, gross margin has compressed from 22.8% (2023) to 19.4% (2025), operating profit fell to $475M (8.8% margin) from $637M (12.4%) two years prior, and near-term consensus EPS estimates are drifting down (current quarter -8.1% over 90 days).

We rate TEX a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The pivot is logical and the backlog ($7.1B, book-to-bill 109% pro-forma) supports the 2026 plan, but the thesis rests on three unproven legs: realizing $75M of REV synergies, deleveraging from 2.4x via strong second-half free cash flow, and executing the Aerials divestiture at a sensible price near what management calls a cyclical bottom. At ~14x forward adjusted EPS, the stock is priced fairly for a transformation that has not yet been tested through a downcycle. The risk/reward does not yet justify a BUY, but the catalysts are monitorable and the trajectory has been improving.

## Company Overview

Terex manufactures specialty industrial equipment across four reportable segments following the REV close:

- **Environmental Solutions (ES)** — refuse collection bodies, compaction/recycling equipment, and Terex Utilities (digger derricks, insulated aerial/bucket trucks for the electric grid). Highest-margin segment at ~18% Q1 2026 EBITDA, built around the ESG acquisition.
- **Materials Processing (MP)** — crushers, screens, washing systems, material handlers, concrete equipment, sold into construction, infrastructure, and quarrying/mining. ~15% Q1 2026 EBITDA margin.
- **Specialty Vehicles (SV)** — the new REV-derived segment: fire trucks, ambulances, terminal trucks, industrial sweepers, and recreational vehicles. ~14.2% Q1 2026 EBITDA margin with roughly two years of backlog.
- **Aerials** — Genie mobile elevating work platforms and telehandlers. The most cyclical and most tariff-exposed segment, breakeven EBITDA in Q1 2026, under strategic review for divestiture.

The strategic logic is to shift weight toward verticals with structural demand and replacement-driven cadence (waste, grid, fire & emergency) and away from the highly cyclical, China-import-pressured aerial work-platform market. The mechanism that ties the segments to the broader macro environment runs through U.S. grid capex: electric-utility equipment demand is supported by grid build-out tied to data-center and AI electricity load, which management cites as 8–15% annual capex growth through 2030. Terex Utilities was the company's fastest-growing business in Q1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory ($M):** 2022: 4,418 → 2023: 5,152 → 2024: 5,127 → 2025: 5,421 (+5.7%). The 2026 guidance is a discontinuity created by REV scale, not organic acceleration.

**The core tension is growth via M&A against margin and EPS dilution.** FY2025 operating profit fell to $475M (8.8% margin) from $526M (10.3%) in 2024 and $637M (12.4%) in 2023. Gross margin compressed 340bps over two years to 19.4%. Interest expense nearly doubled from $89M to $177M, reflecting the ~$2B raised for ESG. Segment dispersion was stark in 2025: ES operating margin rose to 13.8%, MP held at 13.9% (flattered by the crane-sale gain), but Aerials collapsed to 5.0% from 11.2% on volume cuts, tariffs, and a litigation charge.

**Q1 2026 — first quarter with REV:** Revenue of $1,734M (+41% reported, +10.8% pro-forma, +8.1% organic). The GAAP operating loss of $(82)M and net loss of $(89)M are purchase-accounting artifacts: $112M inventory step-up amortization plus $164M total PPA plus $68M deal costs (including $25M accelerated stock comp). Adjusted EBITDA was $173M (+35%) and adjusted EPS rose 18% year-over-year. One caveat: Q1 adjusted EPS included roughly $0.10 of one-time tax benefit (11% Q1 rate versus a 21% full-year expectation), so the quality of the beat is slightly lower than the headline suggests.

**Balance sheet.** Post-REV total assets reached $10.2B (from $6.1B). Shares outstanding jumped from ~65M to ~113.5M (47.9M issued for REV), a roughly 75% increase that the acquired earnings must cover before per-share metrics improve. There are no maturities until 2029; the term loan was repriced down to SOFR+1.75%. Liquidity is ~$1.0B. The revolver requires first-lien net leverage ≤3.0x, tested only when drawn above 30%, so the covenant cushion is real but the variable-rate term loan exposes earnings to the higher-for-longer rate path.

**Cash flow.** Q1 free cash flow was a seasonal $(57)M outflow, with management guiding 80–90% FCF conversion for the full year. Working capital improved to 16.7% of sales from ~26% a year earlier, partly structural: SV/REV carries customer advances ($413M) and a less seasonal profile. The Altman Z-score of 2.23 sits in the grey zone, driven by the goodwill-heavy, leveraged balance sheet rather than operational distress; the company is solidly profitable on an adjusted basis with positive operating cash flow and a 2.3x current ratio.

The financial health picture is mixed. The business generates cash and has no near-term maturity wall, but leverage at 2.4x, the doubled interest burden, the intangible-heavy balance sheet, and the impairment exposure on a cyclical Aerials unit cap the score in the middle of the range. The deleveraging plan depends on second-half cash conversion that has not yet been demonstrated post-REV.

## Growth Analysis

The reported revenue step-change is acquisition-driven, so the organic and pro-forma figures are the relevant gauges. Several end-market tailwinds are genuine:

- **Electric utilities (within ES):** grid capex growth of 8–15% annually through 2030, pulled partly by data-center and AI electricity load. Terex Utilities is adding 30% capacity by end-2027.
- **Waste & recycling:** replacement-driven demand plus a 2027 EPA-regulation pre-buy expected to skew orders to H2 2026.
- **Fire & emergency (SV):** ~2-year backlog tied to municipal budgets; ladder-truck capacity expanding 35% at Ocala, FL.
- **Construction/infrastructure (MP, Aerials):** government-funded mega-project pipeline cited through 2030.

The constraint on the growth score is estimate momentum, which is the more reliable forward signal here. Current-quarter consensus EPS has fallen 8.1% over 90 days and next-quarter is down 1.1%, while current-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.6%). There is no +30% revision velocity that would mark a re-rating opportunity. Full-year and out-year estimates holding flat while near-term estimates drift down is consistent with a business whose acquired growth is delivering as expected but whose organic cyclical exposure (Aerials, MP) faces near-term softness from tariffs and high rates suppressing rent-to-own conversions. The synergy track record lends credibility: ESG delivered $25M ahead of schedule, and management is running the same playbook for REV's $75M run-rate target (with $28M planned in 2026), but synergies support margin, not top-line growth.

The growth thesis is real but maturing into a margin-and-integration story rather than a revenue-acceleration story. We score growth above midpoint, weighting the structural end-market tailwinds and backlog against the falling near-term estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP figures the stock screens at ~47x P/E, distorted by REV purchase accounting. The meaningful metric is forward adjusted EPS. At the midpoint of guidance ($4.75) the stock at $67.94 trades at ~14.3x current-year adjusted earnings, and on the $5.82 next-year consensus at ~11.7x. The implied PEG is reasonable given mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth plus synergy-driven margin recovery.

Against peers, the picture is fair rather than cheap. The peer median P/E is 18.5x (OSK 14.9x, FSS 25.1x, GATX 18.5x, RUSHA 20.9x, TRN 10.8x). On forward adjusted earnings Terex sits at the lower end of this band, but the discount is justified by higher leverage (D/E 1.93 versus an industry where lower-debt structures are common), the integration overhang, and the unproven nature of the de-cyclicalization claim. The dividend yield of ~1.0% is below the peer median of 1.5% and provides little valuation support.

Analyst targets bracket the name from $65 (low) to $95 (high), mean $76.0, median $75.0, with Raymond James at $85 (Strong Buy, May 2026). At ~14x forward earnings with positive full-year revision trends, these targets carry some weight as upside reference points, implying ~10% to the median and ~25% to the Raymond James target. But the stock sits within striking distance of its 52-week high ($71.50), the recommendation distribution shows meaningful dissent (4 holds, 1 strong sell among 13 analysts), and the near-term estimate drift argues against treating the consensus as a stale floor.

We score valuation just above midpoint. The forward multiple is not stretched, but cheapness without an identified near-term catalyst beyond the binary Aerials sale is not a reason to underwrite outperformance; the deleveraging and integration must execute before the multiple re-rates. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Terex's competitive position is improving on trajectory, consistent with the +1.0 point upgrade in this dimension over the prior two reports. The brand franchises are genuine: Heil leads in refuse, Genie in aerials, and REV brings established fire/emergency positions with multi-year backlogs and high switching costs in municipal procurement. The lifecycle parts-and-service annuity and the in-house "3rd Eye" Smart Truck situational-awareness product add stickiness and modest digital differentiation.

The mix shift itself improves the competitive profile: re-weighting toward replacement-driven utility and waste markets reduces exposure to the price-sensitive, China-import-pressured aerial market. Terex sits within a U.S. coalition that won anti-dumping and countervailing duties against low-cost Chinese MEWP imports, with the EU imposing similar duties, a structural defense for the segment it may nonetheless divest.

The de-cyclicalization is genuine but unproven through a downcycle. SV and ES margins now carry the company while the legacy cyclical core is being shed at trough margins. The competitive trajectory is favorable, but Materials Processing and Aerials remain cyclical and tariff-exposed, and the bet that the new mix is durably less cyclical will only be validated when the next industrial slowdown arrives.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Integration and synergy execution.** Two large acquisitions (ESG, REV) integrated within ~15 months. The synergy target within 24 months is the top-listed risk; failure weighs on the entire deleveraging-and-margin thesis.
2. **Leverage and covenants.** Variable-rate term loan; the 10-K explicitly flags the risk of being unable to achieve long-term leverage targets. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the model.
3. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** With $5.5B of goodwill plus intangibles, underperformance at any reporting unit, especially cyclical Aerials, could trigger impairment. The 2025 test passed, but PPA amortization (~$204M remaining in 2026, $266M in 2027) will depress GAAP earnings for years.
4. **Aerials divestiture is binary.** A sale would de-risk, de-lever, and remove the biggest tariff/China headwind, but management says Aerials has "bottomed" (book-to-bill 132%, $1B backlog), meaning a sale risks selling near a cyclical trough. Timing and price are uncertain. The North Bend, WA plant closure (117 jobs, May 2026) is already underway.
5. **Tariff optionality.** A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated IEEPA tariffs; Terex has filed CBP refund claims but booked zero benefit. Any recovery is upside not in guidance, though recoverability and timing are uncertain.
6. **Off-balance-sheet contingencies.** Credit guarantees ($51M max), repurchase agreements ($430M, new with SV), and customer-owned chassis ($241M) expanded with REV.
7. **Legacy SEC injunction.** A 2009 anti-fraud/books-and-records settlement injunction remains in force, raising penalty exposure on any future violation; the company is also a defendant in early-stage industry antitrust litigation.

The risk score sits at midpoint. Several of these are specific, partly unpriced, and asymmetric in both directions (the Aerials sale and the tariff refund could cut either way), which prevents a higher score; but none is an imminent solvency threat given the 2029 maturity wall and the cash-generative adjusted earnings base.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent uncertainty without a directional bias. Near-term ATM IV of 50.3% is roughly fair to the 48.0% one-year historical vol (2.4pp spread). The term structure is in backwardation (50.3% near versus 47.9% long-dated), signaling the market expects a near-term catalyst, likely the Q2 print, which management says carries ~25% of full-year EPS. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.2% and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.13, with put/call OI at 0.44. There is no elevated put demand that would contradict a constructive fundamental view, but the implied move of ±10.3% on the nearest expiry confirms the Q2 report is a real binary. IV has been flat (50.3% → 50.3%) even as the stock rose 19.7% since late May, suggesting the rally has not generated complacency or panic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The transformation works: REV synergies hit the $75M run-rate, second-half free cash flow drives net leverage below 2x, Aerials is divested at a respectable price into improving book-to-bill, and the tariff refund delivers upside not in guidance. Utility/grid demand compounds through 2030, the waste pre-buy pulls H2 2026 orders, and the company re-rates toward the peer median multiple on a de-cyclicalized earnings base. Adjusted EPS reaches the upper end of guidance and grows to ~$5.80+ in 2027. At ~16–17x that figure the stock approaches $90–95.

**Bear case.** Integration absorbs more management bandwidth than planned, the Aerials sale either fails or executes near a cyclical bottom at a poor price, an industrial slowdown exposes the still-cyclical MP segment, and a goodwill impairment on Aerials hits GAAP book value. Near-term estimate drift continues, leverage stays elevated against a higher-for-longer rate path, and the multiple compresses toward OSK/TRN levels (~11–15x). Adjusted EPS comes in at the low end and the stock retraces toward the low-$50s, roughly its November 2025 levels.

**Base case.** Guidance is delivered near the midpoint (~$4.75 adjusted EPS in 2026, growing toward ~$5.50 in 2027), synergies track on plan, leverage improves modestly, and the Aerials review resolves without a value-destroying outcome. The stock holds a ~13–15x forward multiple, supporting a price in the low-to-mid $70s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings report is the next natural reassessment point — it carries ~25% of full-year EPS, will show the second full quarter of REV integration, and the options term structure prices a ±10% move around it. The Aerials strategic-review outcome is the larger binary, likely resolving over the next two to four quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Aerials divestiture announced at a sale price implying a reasonable multiple, with proceeds directed to deleveraging below ~2.0x net leverage.
- Current-year and out-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (>+5% over 90 days), reversing the current near-term drift, alongside confirmation of $75M synergy run-rate progress.
- Second-half FCF conversion tracking toward the 80–90% guide, validating the deleveraging plan.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge on Aerials or another reporting unit, signaling the M&A premiums were overpaid.
- Net leverage rising rather than falling through 2026, or covenant pressure emerging if the revolver is drawn.
- Pro-forma organic revenue turning negative or backlog falling meaningfully below $7B, indicating cyclical end-markets are rolling over faster than the mix shift can offset.

## Conclusion

Terex has executed a credible, deliberate pivot away from its most cyclical, tariff-exposed business toward U.S.-based, replacement-driven end markets with structural tailwinds in grid capex, waste, and fire/emergency. The Q1 adjusted beat, the backlog, and the synergy track record support the 2026 plan. The stock is fairly valued at ~14x forward adjusted EPS.

The reasons to wait outweigh the reasons to commit capital today. The thesis depends on three unproven legs: synergy realization, deleveraging via H2 cash conversion, and a binary Aerials divestiture executed near what management calls a cyclical bottom; and near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling rather than rising. The balance sheet carries 2.4x leverage and ~54% intangibles with embedded impairment risk, and the sector backdrop for cyclical machinery offers no margin of safety. We maintain a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the catalysts are monitorable and the trajectory has been improving, but the risk/reward does not yet clear the bar for a BUY. We would turn constructive on a confirmed Aerials sale at a sensible price plus a turn in estimate revisions, and cautious on any impairment or leverage backslide.
