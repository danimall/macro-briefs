## Executive Summary

Alpha Metallurgical Resources is the largest U.S. pure-play metallurgical coal producer, operating 19 mines across Central Appalachian Virginia and West Virginia. Revenue has collapsed 48% from peak ($4.09B to $2.12B over three years), FY2025 produced a $62M net loss, but the balance sheet carries $366M cash against only $13.4M debt. A director purchased $10M+ in open-market shares in March 2026, signaling informed conviction that the cycle is bottoming.

The core tension preventing a stronger recommendation despite these recovery signals is threefold: (1) current-quarter EPS estimates collapsed from +$1.62 to -$0.86 over the past 30 days, representing active deterioration in near-term fundamentals; (2) options markets price imminent stress with 76.9% near-term IV in steep backwardation versus 58.3% at the 6-month tenor; and (3) 19.1% short interest with 6 days to cover means institutional capital is actively positioned against the stock, creating squeeze risk in both directions. The stock has already run 90% from its 52-week low of $97.41, pricing in substantial recovery expectations. A BUY would require either confirmation that met coal prices are sustainably recovering above $200/ton or resolution of the current-quarter earnings deterioration.

## Company Overview

AMR produces metallurgical (coking) coal used in steelmaking, with ~93% of volume and ~96% of coal revenues derived from met coal. The company exports approximately 73% of production, primarily to India, Brazil, and Europe, through its 65%-owned DTA terminal in Newport News, Virginia. AMR holds 294.5 million tons of reserves (96% met coal) and operates at roughly 47% unused capacity, providing significant operating leverage if demand recovers.

The company completed its strategic exit from thermal coal in 2020 and has positioned itself as the U.S. market leader in met coal supply. Key growth markets are India (steel production +10.1% YoY) and other developing economies requiring blast furnace steel.

Revenue declined 28% YoY in FY2025, driven by both price (realized coal prices fell 17.9% to $117.08/ton) and volume (down 10.8% to 15.3M tons). The company swung to an operating loss of $61M.

**Balance sheet strength:**

At current burn rates ($18M FCF in the worst year), AMR can sustain losses for years without distress. The balance sheet provides optionality to acquire competitors, resume buybacks, or simply outlast the cycle.

**Capital allocation:** $1.14B in share repurchases completed (of $1.5B authorized), reducing shares from ~17.5M to ~13.0M. Buyback was suspended March 2024 through August 2025 during weak pricing, then resumed at ~$147-150/share. Dividend discontinued after late 2023 special distribution.

**Section 45X tax credit:** Met coal was designated a "critical mineral" in July 2025, providing a refundable 2.5% production cost credit for 2026-2029. AMR estimates $30-50M annually — meaningful relative to $18M FY2025 FCF. This effectively lowers breakeven pricing by $2-3/ton.

The valuation reflects the classic commodity dilemma: expensive on trailing/current metrics, cheap on forward recovery estimates. The FY2027 estimate of $28.05 implies a sharp recovery, but with only 2 analysts covering the stock for next year (and 1 for current year), estimate reliability is low.

**Book value floor:** At ~0.85x book, the stock has asset backing, though mine reserves are subject to impairment risk if prices remain depressed.

The stock has rallied 90% from its $97.41 low (June 2025) to $185. At $128 in May 2025, the risk/reward was clearly asymmetric. At $185, most of the recovery is priced.

## Competitive Landscape

AMR is the largest U.S. pure-play met coal producer with structural advantages:
- 65% DTA terminal ownership provides captive export infrastructure
- 294.5M tons of reserves across multiple coal quality grades
- Established relationships across 77% of revenue concentrated in top 10 customers
- 47% unused capacity creates operating leverage without incremental capital

**Competitive position is stable but not improving:**
- CAPP geology is inherently higher-cost than Australian or Mozambican deposits
- ESG constraints limit AMR's ability to raise new equity or debt capital
- Wage reductions (Q2 2025) may create labor retention challenges if cycle turns up

Peers include Arch Resources (HCC segment), CONSOL Energy, and Warrior Met Coal. AMR's pure-play focus and terminal ownership differentiate it, but all U.S. met coal producers face the same structural headwinds from EAF adoption domestically.

India's growth (10.1% steel production increase, blast-furnace-intensive development) represents the primary long-term demand growth vector. AMR's export infrastructure positions it well for this market.

## Risk Assessment

**Specific near-term risks:**

1. **Current quarter earnings collapse:** EPS estimate went from +$1.62 to -$0.86 in 30 days. This suggests either pricing retraced or operational issues that aren't yet public. The next earnings report could disappoint significantly.

2. **Black lung collateral rule:** $80-100M potential additional collateral requirement would consume 15-19% of liquidity. Currently delayed but not eliminated.

3. **Legacy liabilities:** $1.85B undiscounted future obligations (pension $983M, ARO $498M, black lung $284M) represent decades-long cash drains. Pension requires ~$32M/year in contributions.

4. **Customer concentration:** Top customer = 14% of revenue. Export dependency (73%) exposes AMR to tariff retaliation and foreign economic downturns.

5. **Impairment risk:** Multiple mining complexes tested for impairment in Q3 2025 with no charges yet, but management flagged that "future impairment charges may occur if projected coal pricing weakens further."

6. **DTA terminal capex:** $25M/year for 5 years ($125M total) required regardless of production levels.

## Conclusion

Alpha Metallurgical Resources presents a textbook commodity cyclical recovery setup: fortress balance sheet, trough-level profitability, informed insider buying, structural supply constraints, and growing Indian demand. The Section 45X credit provides a new cash floor that didn't exist in prior cycles.

However, the stock has already rallied 90% from its low and trades near analyst targets ($194-195). Current-quarter estimates have deteriorated sharply, options markets are pricing imminent stress (backwardated IV structure, elevated put skew), and 19.1% short interest means substantial institutional capital disagrees with the recovery thesis. With only 1 analyst providing current-year estimates and a 5% consensus target upside, there is insufficient near-term catalyst identification to justify a BUY at $185 when the next earnings report could produce significant downside volatility.

The correct entry point for a high-conviction BUY is either: (a) a pullback to $145-155 where risk/reward becomes asymmetric, or (b) a positive earnings surprise confirming the pricing recovery, even if that means paying $200+. At $185, the fundamental recovery thesis is credible and supported by informed insider behavior, but the near-term setup contains identifiable risks that options markets are explicitly pricing.
