## Executive Summary

Churchill Downs Inc. is a $6.3B market cap entertainment and gaming company whose crown asset, the Kentucky Derby, provides irreplaceable brand equity and pricing power. The company operates across three segments: Live and Historical Racing ($1.44B revenue, $637M EBITDA), Gaming ($1.05B revenue, $483M EBITDA), and Wagering Services ($526M revenue, $177M EBITDA). Record consolidated revenue of $2.93B and adjusted EBITDA of $1.2B in 2025 demonstrate strong underlying business performance.

The stock sits 22% below its 52-week high for identifiable reasons: (1) the $920M racetrack expansion was paused due to tariff/trade uncertainty, removing the primary near-term growth catalyst; (2) regulatory risk in Virginia, where proposed legislation could authorize a competing casino near CHDN's Rose Gaming Resort; (3) $5.15B in total debt with $600M of notes maturing April 2027, creating refinancing pressure in a tight credit environment; and (4) the Louisiana Supreme Court ruling that eliminated HRM operations in that state, raising questions about HRM regulatory classification in other jurisdictions.

At ~16.8x forward earnings with 12 of 12 covering analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and a median price target of $139, the stock offers meaningful upside if these risks resolve favorably. But the combination of leverage, regulatory binary outcomes, and macro sensitivity in consumer discretionary spending creates enough uncertainty that conviction for an outright BUY is premature without greater clarity on the 2027 refinancing and Virginia outcome.

## Company Overview

Churchill Downs Inc. has transformed over 150+ years from a single horse racing venue into a vertically integrated gaming and wagering platform. The company's competitive architecture rests on three pillars:

**Kentucky Derby & Live Racing:** The longest continuously held annual sporting event in the U.S. generates premium pricing, sponsorship revenue ($39M Paddock sponsorship deal in 2024), and media distribution fees. Derby Day alone generates outsized EBITDA relative to any comparable single-day sporting event.

**Historical Racing Machines (HRMs):** CHDN operates ~10,190 HRMs across 18 venues in Kentucky (8), Virginia (8), and New Hampshire (2). These machines replay historical horse races and are classified as pari-mutuel wagering rather than casino gaming in these jurisdictions, allowing CHDN to operate slot-equivalent machines in markets without traditional casino authorization. CHDN's proprietary Exacta Systems technology powers these machines.

**Regional Casino Portfolio:** 10 wholly-owned casinos across 9 states plus equity interests in Rivers Casino Des Plaines (IL, 61.3%) and Miami Valley Gaming (OH, 50%). These provide stable cash flow to service the capital structure.

## Growth Analysis

**Current Year Estimates:** FY2026E EPS $6.87 (consensus, 8 analysts), up 3.2% in last 30 days. FY2027E EPS $7.30, though this has been revised down 7.8% over 90 days, reflecting concern about growth beyond the current year.

**Growth Drivers:**
1. **HRM expansion in Virginia and Kentucky:** The Rose Gaming Resort (opened Nov 2024, 1,610 HRMs) is still ramping. Marshall Yards (225 HRMs) opened Feb 2026 in Kentucky.
2. **Rockingham Grand Casino (NH):** $180-200M investment, opening mid-2027, adding 825 HRMs and 32 table games.
3. **Kentucky Derby pricing power:** Premium pricing and sponsorship escalators provide mid-single-digit organic growth from the core asset.
4. **TwinSpires digital wagering:** Online horse racing platform with integrated sportsbook functionality.

**Growth Headwinds:**
1. **$920M expansion paused:** The transformative multi-year racetrack project (Skye Terrace, infield, first-turn building) was suspended in April 2025 citing tariff uncertainty. Only $30M of renovations proceeding for 2026 Derby.
2. **Gaming segment declining:** EBITDA fell 4.7% in 2025 due to Rivers Des Plaines competition, Mississippi road construction, and general regional casino softness.
3. **Louisiana exit:** HRM operations ceased after adverse court ruling, eliminating a revenue stream.
4. **FY2027 estimate revisions negative (-7.8% over 90 days):** Analysts are cutting outer-year numbers, likely reflecting the expansion pause and regulatory uncertainty.

The organic growth rate, excluding M&A and major capex projects, is likely mid-single digits. The paused expansion was supposed to drive a step-function improvement in Derby-related revenue; its absence compresses the medium-term growth ceiling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92.25, CHDN trades at:
- Forward P/E: 16.8x
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 9.4x (EV ~$11.4B / $1.205B EBITDA)
- P/E vs. peer median: CHDN at 16.8x vs. peer median 16.9x (RRR) — essentially in line

**Analyst Targets:** Consensus median $139, range $110-$155. All 12 analysts rate Buy or Strong Buy. This implies ~51% upside to median target — an unusually large gap suggesting either (a) the street hasn't cut targets enough to reflect recent risks, or (b) the market is pricing in a bear-case scenario that analysts don't believe will materialize.

**Valuation Context:** The 16.8x P/E looks reasonable given:
- Record EBITDA generation ($1.2B)
- Unique, irreplaceable asset (Kentucky Derby)
- FCF yield of approximately 8-9% (OCF $770M less ~$150M maintenance capex on $6.3B market cap)
- Compounding share buyback reducing diluted count

The stock is trading closer to the low end of its historical valuation range. The discount appears justified by the risk stack (leverage + regulatory + macro), but the magnitude of the discount (51% below analyst consensus) suggests the market may be overweighting near-term uncertainty.

BYD's 3.7x P/E is misleading (likely one-time items inflating trailing earnings). The more relevant peer comparison is RRR at 16.9x and MCRI at 19.9x. CHDN's comparable or lower multiple is difficult to justify given its superior asset quality (Kentucky Derby) and diversified revenue streams — unless the regulatory risks are assigned high probability of materializing.

## Competitive Landscape

CHDN's competitive position is strengthening in some dimensions and under threat in others:

**Strengthening:**
- Kentucky Derby has no competitor; pricing power continues to expand
- Exacta Systems technology gives CHDN a proprietary HRM platform advantage
- Vertically integrated model (content + technology + distribution) creates higher barriers than pure operators
- The Rose Gaming Resort (Virginia) has first-mover advantage in Northern Virginia market

**Under Threat:**
- Virginia Fairfax County casino legislation could directly cannibalize The Rose
- Louisiana ruling created precedent for HRM legal challenges in other states
- Prediction markets (Polymarket, Kalshi) represent emerging competition for wagering dollars
- Regional casino operations face secular pressure from market saturation and online gaming expansion

The Kentucky Derby moat is exceptional — no amount of capital can replicate 150+ years of cultural significance. But the HRM regulatory moat is legally fragile, as Louisiana proved. The critical question is whether Kentucky and Virginia maintain their current regulatory frameworks.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Virginia Casino Legislation (Binary, High Impact):** If a Fairfax County casino is authorized, it would directly compete with The Rose for the Washington D.C. metro market. CHDN invested heavily in Virginia infrastructure; a competing facility 10-20 miles away would materially impair returns on that investment. Probability: difficult to assess, but CHDN is actively lobbying against.

**2. Refinancing Risk ($600M April 2027 Notes):** In the current credit environment, refinancing $600M at reasonable rates is achievable but not guaranteed. A 200-300bps increase in borrowing costs would add $12-18M in annual interest expense. The springing maturity mechanism amplifies the risk if this issue is mishandled.

**3. Macro/Consumer Sensitivity:** As a consumer discretionary company, CHDN faces headwinds from consumer spending pressure. Regional casino volumes correlate with disposable income.

**4. HRM Regulatory Classification Risk (Tail Risk, Catastrophic Impact):** If courts in Kentucky or Virginia rule that HRMs constitute illegal gambling rather than pari-mutuel horse racing wagering, the Live and Historical Racing segment ($637M EBITDA) faces partial or full impairment. This is existential but appears low-probability in the near term.

**5. Construction Tariff Impact:** The expansion pause demonstrates management is unwilling to commit capital under cost uncertainty. If tariff levels persist, the project may be permanently downsized, limiting long-term earnings growth from the Derby facility.

## Conclusion

Churchill Downs is a high-quality business trading at a reasonable valuation, but the risk stack is substantive and several key uncertainties are binary (Virginia legislation, 2027 refinancing). The Q1 2026 beat (+18.2%) confirms operational execution remains strong. The combination of an irreplaceable asset, record cash generation, and depressed valuation creates a compelling setup if the risks resolve favorably.

At 4.3x leverage with $600M maturing in under 12 months, a consumer discretionary business facing regulatory threats to its growth engine (HRMs) and a paused expansion project doesn't warrant full conviction in the current macro environment, particularly with consumer spending data deteriorating and credit markets under stress. The options market (backwardation, elevated put volume) confirms near-term uncertainty.

The appropriate posture is HOLD with an explicit path to upgrading: refinancing execution + Virginia resolution would clear the two largest overhangs. At ~16.8x forward earnings with 8-9% FCF yield, the downside is reasonably well-defined unless the bear scenarios compound. The catalysts for re-rating depend on specific events: the 2027 refinancing (expected H2 2026 or early 2027) and the Virginia legislative session (expected by year-end 2026).
