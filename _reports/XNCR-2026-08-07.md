## Executive Summary

Xencor enters 2H26 in materially better financial shape and materially worse long-term revenue shape than it entered 2026. Two July events dominate. On July 29 the company settled its royalty dispute with Alexion/AstraZeneca for $105.0M in two equal $52.5M installments, with filing language stating explicitly that "Alexion has no further obligation to pay royalties on U.S. sales of Ultomiris." That converts what was, in FY2025, a roughly $70.1M annual Ultomiris royalty line into a fixed, finite sum. On July 7-9, Merus voluntarily dismissed its patent suit against Xencor **with prejudice**, both parties terminated the instituted IPRs, and Merus paid Xencor $7.0M — the plaintiff paying the defendant to end litigation it initiated, which removes a real overhang on XmAb819, plamotamab and vudalimab.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case. At 6/30/26 Xencor held $486.4M of cash and marketable debt securities plus a $78.6M Zenas equity stake, against $104.9M of royalty-monetization liability and **no conventional borrowed debt**. Adding both Alexion installments, the Merus payment and the Zenas Japan milestone gives roughly $601M of identified resources against underlying cash burn of approximately $240M per year, consistent with management's guidance of $420-440M at year-end 2026 and resources "through 2028." The $200M Leerink ATM has been entirely undrawn since February 2023 — management chose 19%-effective-cost royalty monetization in 2023 rather than issuing equity at depressed prices, which is a genuine mark of financing discipline.

What keeps this a HOLD is the intersection of price and pipeline positioning. The stock is up 167% over twelve months and sits 5% below its 52-week high, having re-rated from roughly $15 in mid-July on the litigation resolutions and Q2 print. Stripping out net financial assets of roughly $460M ($6.07 per economic share), the market is paying approximately $1.1B of enterprise value for the pipeline and residual royalty economics. The lead wholly owned autoimmune asset, XmAb942, reads out its Phase 2b week-12 induction endpoint in 2H27 — by which point Merck's tulisokibart, which met its Phase 3 UC induction endpoint on June 22, 2026, will plausibly be approved or filing. Xencor's differentiation there is dosing convenience (74.1-day measured human half-life supporting Q12W maintenance), not mechanism, and Spyre is pursuing the same axis. The nearest-term value driver, XmAb819 in clear cell RCC, gets a proffered-paper oral slot at ESMO in Madrid (Oct 23-27). That is the event worth waiting for.

## Company Overview

Xencor is a Pasadena-based, single-segment clinical-stage protein engineering company built on the **XmAb** platform — a library of engineered antibody Fc domains conferring immune inhibition, enhanced cytotoxicity, extended half-life ("Xtend"), and heterodimeric formats enabling bispecifics. A second-generation bispecific platform, **XenLock**, produced its first clinical candidate in 2026. The 10-Q states three marketed medicines have been developed using XmAb technologies, and more than 20 XmAb-engineered candidates are in clinical development across Xencor and partners.

The business model has two legs. **Out-licensing**: Xencor licenses Fc domains or whole molecules to Johnson & Johnson, Genentech, Vir, Amgen, Incyte, Alexion, Zenas and INmune Bio, collecting upfronts, milestones and royalties while doing little development work. **Wholly owned pipeline**: seven clinical studies were enrolling at 6/30/26.

Royalty-bearing economics today reduce to three assets. **Ultomiris** (Alexion): low-single-digit royalty on net sales, U.S. now extinguished, ex-U.S. running to a 2028 patent expiry. **Monjuvi/Minjuvi** (Incyte, originally XmAb5574 created by Xencor): up to $195.0M in remaining milestones plus royalties, U.S. patent to 2033. **Obexelimab** (Zenas, with Bristol Myers Squibb holding Asia-Pacific rights): up to $450M in remaining milestones, tiered royalties from mid-single-digit to mid-teens, plus 3,098,380 Zenas shares on Xencor's balance sheet.

Wholly owned programs and their next disclosed events: XmAb942 (Xtend anti-TL1A, Ph2b XENITH-UC, blinded interim ~YE2026, primary endpoint 2H27); XmAb819 (ENPP3×CD3, Ph1, ESMO oral October 2026, registration-enabling study to start 2027); XmAb541 (CLDN6×CD3, pivoting to combination); XmAb808 (B7-H3×CD28); XmAb412 (TL1A×IL-23p19, XenLock, Ph1 initiated Q3 2026, healthy-participant data 1H27); XmAb657 and plamotamab (updates 2H26).

The same caution applies to the estimate revision data. The current-quarter consensus moving from $(0.85) to $0.00 (+100.5%) reflects the **$77.4M of Alexion settlement revenue to be recognized in Q3 2026**, which is non-recurring and non-operational. The high-conviction estimate-revision buy signal — large upward revisions reflecting a genuine earnings inflection — is **not present here**; the revisions are an accounting recognition event with an unchanged out-year.

### The OMERS royalty monetization, and why reported revenue overstated cash

In November 2023 Xencor sold future royalty interests to OMERS for $215.0M ($192.5M Ultomiris, $22.5M Monjuvi). Because the arrangements are accounted for as debt rather than as sales, Xencor continues recognizing the royalties as revenue while the cash is routed to OMERS, offset by non-cash interest expense. The cash-flow reconciliation quantifies it: non-cash royalty revenue related to the sale of future royalties was **$36.831M in 1H25 against $36.840M of total 1H25 royalty revenue** — essentially all of it. In 1H26 the figure was $17.201M against $45.637M (38%).

Liability roll-forward: $163.6M at the start of FY2025 → $119.7M at 12/31/25 → $104.9M at 6/30/26, split $27.4M current / $77.5M non-current. Estimated effective interest rates at 6/30/26 are **19.0% (Ultomiris) and 18.5% (Monjuvi)**. That was expensive capital for a company with $600M+ of investments. The offsetting argument is that it monetized a short-dated, patent-cliff-exposed royalty at a fixed price rather than bearing duration risk — a defensible trade in hindsight, given that the U.S. Ultomiris royalty has since been extinguished entirely.

Cash conversion is improving. The **$29.3M cumulative Monjuvi cap was reached in Q2 2026**, so Monjuvi royalties now revert to Xencor as real cash ($0.8M retained in Q2). This should not be confused with growing royalty economics: the mix is improving in quality while the gross royalty pool shrinks.

## Growth Analysis

The honest framing is that reported revenue is about to peak on a non-recurring event and then decline. Q3 2026 will include $77.4M of Alexion settlement recognition. From FY2027, the revenue base consists of: ex-U.S. Ultomiris royalties (expiring 2028, with OMERS entitled to the first $35.0M annually through 2028 and $12.0M annually thereafter); Monjuvi royalties, now reverting to Xencor, on a franchise with FDA approval in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma (June 2025) and newly diagnosed DLBCL submissions accepted for review in Q2 2026; and prospective obexelimab royalties, most likely commencing 2027.

Obexelimab is the credible growth engine. Positive Phase 3 INDIGO in IgG4-related disease (January 2026), BLA submitted May 2026 triggering a $10.0M milestone in Q2, EMA MAA expected 2H26, Japan PMDA MAA submitted July 30, 2026 triggering $2.5M in Q3, plus a positive Phase 2 MoonStone in relapsing MS and an ongoing SLE Phase 2. Xencor's tiered royalty runs from mid-single-digit to mid-teens depending on geography, with up to $450M of remaining milestones. The problem is duration: the 10-K discloses obexelimab patent expiry of **2029 in the U.S. and 2028 ex-U.S.** For a product whose BLA was submitted in May 2026, that nominal window is alarmingly short absent patent term extension, and the 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor on failure to obtain PTE and data exclusivity. This is the single most important unresolved input to Xencor's largest prospective royalty stream.

The wholly owned pipeline is where the equity value sits, and R&D allocation is unusually legible. Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025 external program spend: XmAb942 $14.4M vs $3.0M; XmAb412 $2.9M vs $1.3M; XmAb819 $8.1M vs $6.3M — against XmAb657 $1.5M vs $5.8M, plamotamab $0.7M vs $2.1M, XmAb808 $0.2M vs $1.7M, and terminated-program wind-down $0.5M vs $3.1M. Capital is concentrating into three programs with defined paths. The alternative reading — that XmAb657 and plamotamab are being quietly deprioritized rather than sitting between cohorts — cannot be distinguished from the filings; the promised 2H26 updates on both are the tell.

**Growth prospects: 5.2.** Real optionality in XmAb819 and XmAb412, a scaling obexelimab royalty, and Monjuvi label expansion, against a structurally declining legacy royalty base, a 2H27 readout on the lead autoimmune asset, and a gap in FY2027-28 revenue.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer multiples are of limited use here. The Biotechnology comparables (VIR -2.7x, FTRE -3.5x, SRPT 23.5x, ADMA 12.2x, HRMY 14.2x; median 14.2x) mix loss-making clinical-stage names with profitable commercial-stage ones. XNCR's own forward and trailing P/E of -9.2x is a statistical artifact of a GAAP loss driven substantially by non-cash marks. Sum-of-parts is the only defensible frame.

**Net financial assets.** At 6/30/26: $486.4M liquid securities + $78.6M Zenas − $104.9M royalty liability = $460M, or **$6.07 per economic share** on approximately 75.8M shares (74.3M outstanding plus roughly 1.5M pre-funded warrants, which are economically already shares and are included in the reported basic denominator — Q2 weighted-average shares of 75,859K exceeded 74,329K shares outstanding for exactly this reason).

**Implied pipeline value.** At $20.76 on 75.8M economic shares, market capitalization is approximately $1.57B; less $460M of net financial assets gives roughly **$1.11B of enterprise value** for residual royalty economics plus the wholly owned pipeline. Reasonable risk-adjusted ranges: residual royalties and milestones (Monjuvi to 2033 with label expansion, obexelimab subject to the 2029 patent question, small ex-U.S. Ultomiris net of the OMERS waterfall) $350-650M; wholly owned pipeline (XmAb819 as a potential first-in-class ENPP3×CD3, XmAb942 most likely monetized via partnership, XmAb412, plus platform residual) $550M-1.0B. Midpoints sum to roughly $1.25B, or $16.50/share of non-financial value against $1.11B implied — before adjusting for the NPV drag of a likely $250-350M equity raise inside the next 24 months.

That arithmetic produces a **base case of approximately $23** (+10.8% from $20.76), a **bull case of $36** (+73.4%) if the ESMO data support a registration path, the XENITH-UC interim permits continuation, obexelimab wins approval on schedule and XmAb942 is partnered with a meaningful upfront, and a **bear case of $12** (-42.2%) if ESMO data disappoint, XmAb942 fails to attract a partner ahead of a 2H27 readout into a launched Merck franchise, obexelimab PTE is not granted, and the company raises equity at a compressed price. Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly $23.5, or +13% expected return with a -42% left tail.

Sell-side consensus is above that: mean $28.17, median $27.00, range $14.00-$44.00, with 12 of 14 ratings at buy or strong buy and no changes in three months. On stocks that look cheap relative to rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag reality. That condition does not hold here — the "rising estimates" are a settlement recognition, and the stock is not obviously cheap against a shrinking royalty base. I treat the $28 mean as an optimistic scenario rather than a floor.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Roughly fair. There is no identified mispricing to underwrite: the near-term positive catalysts (Alexion cash, Merus resolution, Monjuvi reversion, obexelimab BLA) are all public and were substantially absorbed in the mid-July to early-August re-rating from roughly $15 to $20.76.

## Competitive Landscape

Xencor's durable advantage is engineering craft: dramatic half-life extension (XmAb942 measured at 74.1 days in humans; XmAb412 predicted at 60-70 days) and avidity-selective 2+1 bispecific formats. It has repeatedly converted that into partner value — tafasitamab (created by Xencor, now Incyte's, expanding label), obexelimab (created by Xencor, now Zenas'/BMS's, at BLA), Xtend licensed to Alexion and Vir. What it lacks is target novelty in its lead autoimmune programs and any commercial infrastructure.

Anti-TL1A in IBD has become validated, crowded and well-capitalized. Merck paid roughly $10.8-11B for Prometheus to obtain tulisokibart and announced on June 22, 2026 that the Phase 3 ATLAS-UC induction study met its primary endpoint of week-12 clinical remission plus key secondaries — reported as the first positive anti-TL1A Phase 3 in UC. Merck's program is not uniformly clean (FierceBiotech and Clinical Trials Arena reported on August 4-5, 2026 a Phase 2 miss and discontinuation in SSc-ILD alongside a dermatology win), but the UC beachhead is established. Spyre reported a positive Phase 2 for SPY002 on June 15, 2026 and is explicitly pursuing the same low-frequency-dosing axis. Roche/Roivant's afimkibart and Sanofi/Teva's duvakitug are also in class.

The implication: class-validation risk on XmAb942 has fallen while commercial-positioning risk has risen. Entering Phase 3 in late 2027 against an approved Merck brand with global IBD commercial reach means XmAb942's realistic value-realization path is a partnership or licensing transaction, which the 10-K strategy explicitly contemplates. XmAb412 (TL1A × IL-23p19 with dual pathway blockade, 60-70-day predicted half-life, citrate-free high-concentration SC formulation) is arguably the more differentiated autoimmune asset, but healthy-participant data only arrive in 1H27.

Oncology is where positioning is strongest. XmAb819 (ENPP3 × CD3) in clear cell RCC is described as potential first-in-class, with high differential tumor expression and low normal-tissue expression, and it is Xencor's own 2+1 format. Enrollment has broadened into ENPP3+ CRC, NSCLC and papillary RCC, with a post-nivolumab/ipilimumab ccRCC sub-study opening Q3 2026 and a planned anti-PD1 combination. The ESMO **proffered-paper oral** slot (an oral, not a poster) is the most important near-term catalyst and will disclose the IV expansion-cohort dose levels under RP3D evaluation. The Merus dismissal with prejudice removes the IP overhang on exactly this program.

XmAb541 is the weak link: reversible **hearing impairment** limited exposure above 60 mg Q3W, which management describes as "potentially on target for CLDN6, which is expressed on cochlear hair cells." On-target toxicity is much harder to engineer around. Monotherapy activity at the putative RP3D was modest — approximately 14% ORR in ovarian cancer and 28% in germ cell tumors in heavily pretreated patients — and the response is a pivot to combination with XmAb808. July 2026 Fast Track designation in relapsed/platinum-refractory germ cell tumors is a modest positive.

**Competitive position: 5.3.** Platform validated and improving in oncology; deteriorating in the lead autoimmune indication where a much larger competitor is a full development cycle ahead.

## Risk Assessment

**Revenue durability.** The U.S. Ultomiris royalty is permanently extinguished. The sequence matters: a patent term extension announced December 9, 2025 appeared to extend the U.S. royalty term into December 2028; Alexion asserted no further obligation on March 4, 2026; the parties settled July 29, 2026. This resolved a genuine legal dispute over royalty-term interpretation for cash rather than forfeiting a clear right, but the outcome is the same — a ~$70.1M annual line replaced by $105M finite.

**Financing.** Roughly $240M/year underlying burn against ~$601M of identified resources, with a stated plan that increases spend. A raise before end-2028 is the base case. The undrawn $200M ATM and a fresh shelf effective to February 2029 mean the mechanism is in place; the question is price. The alternative — a partnership upfront on XmAb819 or XmAb942 — is the company's stated preference and historical pattern.

**Obexelimab exclusivity.** U.S. 2029 / ex-U.S. 2028 disclosed expiry against a May 2026 BLA. PTE prospects are undisclosed and material.

**Governance and controls.** Disclosure controls and ICFR were **not effective at 12/31/2023**, producing a restatement of FY2023 audited and Q1-Q3 2024 unaudited financials (Item 4.02 non-reliance February 2025, followed by 10-K/A and three 10-Q/As) and an auditor change in March 2025. Management states remediation completed in FY2025 and both the FY2025 10-K and Q2 10-Q conclude controls were effective. Since the non-routine transaction accounting at issue plausibly concerned the royalty monetization and equity-consideration arrangements — still the most judgment-intensive items on the balance sheet — this warrants continued attention rather than dismissal. Separately, executive officers, directors, 5% holders and affiliates beneficially owned approximately **68.1% of voting stock** at 12/31/2025, alongside blank-check preferred authority, no written-consent action and no stockholder-called special meetings.

**Unresolved disclosure.** An 8-K filed March 9, 2026 reporting Item 1.02, Termination of a Material Definitive Agreement, remains unidentified (retrieval blocked; no news coverage). Its timing three business days after Alexion's notice invites an Alexion-related hypothesis, but termination of an unrelated legacy license is equally plausible. I am not assuming either.

**Operational concentration.** Predominantly California operations with wildfire and earthquake named explicitly; all antibodies originate from single master cell banks; Xencor manufactures nothing itself, with some third-party manufacturers outside the U.S. and "biotechnology company of concern" legislation flagged as a potential supply constraint.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 134.6% (July 22) to **90.5%** (August 7) while the price rose 6.8% — the classic post-event crush following the Alexion settlement and Q2 print. Put/call open interest sits at **0.31**, meaning positioning is heavily call-tilted. That is not independent confirmation of the fundamental thesis; it is consistent with momentum positioning after a 167% twelve-month move.

The more informative datapoint is the interaction with **20.8% short interest and 11.0 days to cover** (10.84M shares short). Call-heavy options open interest against a fifth of the float short creates genuinely asymmetric mechanics into the October ESMO oral: a positive dataset could force covering into thin liquidity (average volume ~911K shares), while a disappointing one lands on a crowded long. At 90.5% IV, near-term optionality is no longer cheap in absolute terms, but it is materially cheaper than three weeks ago — for holders who want to retain upside into ESMO with defined downside, that is a more efficient structure than adding equity at $20.76.

**Risk factors: 4.3.** Multiple binary events inside 18 months, a near-certain financing need, an unresolved patent-term question on the largest prospective royalty, a documented ICFR failure within three years, and concentrated voting control — only partly offset by the fact that much of this is known and the balance sheet absorbs shocks.

## Management Quality

The read is genuinely mixed, and the mix has improved slightly.

**In favor:** the $200M ATM has been untouched since February 2023, including through 2025-1H26 when the stock traded between roughly $7 and $15; the 2023 choice of royalty monetization over dilution looks correct ex post given the subsequent Ultomiris outcome; cost discipline is visible (Monrovia five-year renewal declined for a one-year extension; Pasadena sublease with $8.284M of minimum receipts); the Merus litigation ended with the plaintiff paying $7.0M and dismissing with prejudice; and R&D reallocation into the three programs with defined paths is transparent and defensible.

**Against:** there were **no open-market purchases** by any officer or director from December 2025 through June 2026, while the stock traded at $7-15. Every disposition examined has a mechanical explanation — the December 2025 CEO and CSO transactions show derivative balances going to zero, the signature of expiring options; the March and June 2026 sales are sized and timed as sell-to-cover on vesting. The CSO adopted a 10b5-1 plan on 6/1/2026 for 41,883 shares and terminated it on 6/23/2026 before any sales. So there is no meaningful *selling* signal — but absence of conviction buying at those prices is a soft negative.

The compensation mechanics deserve weight. At 6/30/26 there were 14,968,548 options at a weighted-average $24.45 strike (9,926,176 exercisable at $29.17, entirely out of the money) plus 2,324,382 RSUs, total 17,292,930 awards with aggregate option intrinsic value of only $16.3M. During 1H26 the company granted 2,716,094 options at $12.35 and 1,394,881 RSUs at $12.32, including a single 583,748-option grant to the CEO in March. Stockholders added 3.0M shares to the 2023 Plan in June 2025 and 4.0M more in June 2026, bringing the reserve to 23,135,269. If all 17.29M outstanding awards settled, share count would rise from 74,328,804 to 91,621,734 — new shares equal to 23.3% of existing shares, and existing holders' ownership falling to **81.1%, i.e. 18.9% ownership dilution**. That is not current fully diluted capitalization (most options are out of the money and all were excluded from diluted EPS as anti-dilutive, with vesting contingent on 2-3 years of service). The relevant point is the pattern: out-of-the-money legacy options being supplemented by large low-strike grants and repeated reserve increases is a recurring value transfer that does not require the share price to recover to be earned.

Proxy extraction failed, so I have no verified NEO compensation, say-on-pay result, performance-measure design or related-party disclosure. That is a gap, not an assumption.

**Management quality: 4.8** (up from 4.5). The Merus outcome, the Alexion settlement, cost actions and continued ATM restraint justify a modest upgrade from the prior report; the grant pattern and absence of insider buying cap it below neutral.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $36 (+73%)

XmAb819's ESMO oral in October discloses response rates and durability in ccRCC that support the 2027 registration-enabling study, and the market begins valuing a potential first-in-class ENPP3×CD3 asset with the Merus overhang removed. The XENITH-UC blinded interim around year-end permits continuation, and a large pharma partner takes XmAb942 with a meaningful upfront that funds the company past 2028 without equity issuance — leaning on convenience differentiation (74.1-day half-life, Q12W maintenance) as a genuine commercial wedge against Merck. Obexelimab wins FDA approval in IgG4-RD in 2027 with PTE granted, and the MS and SLE Phase 2 successes broaden the royalty base while Monjuvi's 1L DLBCL expansion lifts the now-cash-generating Incyte stream. With 20.8% of float short and call-heavy options positioning, a positive catalyst sequence forces covering into a thin book.

### Bear Case → $12 (-42%)

ESMO data show modest single-agent activity typical of solid-tumor T-cell engagers, and the 2027 registration study is delayed or narrowed. XmAb942 attracts no partner because Merck's approved product plus Spyre's identical dosing pitch leaves insufficient commercial white space, forcing Xencor to fund Phase 3 alone or shelve the asset after the 2H27 readout. Obexelimab PTE is denied, compressing the royalty window to 2029 in the U.S. and 2028 elsewhere on the very asset that was supposed to replace Ultomiris. Legacy royalties run off on schedule — ex-U.S. Ultomiris expires 2028 with OMERS taking the first $35M/year until then — leaving FY2028 revenue near the milestone floor. Burn escalates above $260M, and the company raises $300M+ of equity at a depressed price, with the 2023 ICFR history and 68.1% insider voting concentration limiting minority influence over terms.

### What tips the balance

Both cases are live and the events that resolve them are dated. The reason this is not an ACCUMULATE at $20.76 is arithmetic: +11% to base against -42% to bear, with the first resolving binary (ESMO) roughly ten weeks out and IV already crushed to 90.5%. The reason it is not a REDUCE is that the balance sheet genuinely absorbs a bad outcome — $6.07/share of net financial assets plus a Monjuvi stream now reverting to cash means the downside is a derating, not a solvency event.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be high. The case for repeating it is that the two variables that moved since the last review moved in opposite directions. Fundamentally, Xencor is stronger: $105M of Alexion cash replaced a contested claim, Merus paid $7M and walked away with prejudice, Monjuvi royalties now reach Xencor's own cash flow for the first time, obexelimab is at BLA with milestones flowing, and management reaffirmed $420-440M year-end liquidity and resources through 2028 without touching a $200M ATM. Against that, the price is 6.8% higher than at the last review and 167% higher than a year ago, sitting 5% below the 52-week high, and the same settlement that improved liquidity permanently extinguished the U.S. royalty that generated roughly $70M of annual revenue.

The pipeline arithmetic is what prevents an upgrade. Roughly $1.11B of enterprise value rests primarily on two assets: XmAb819, where positioning is strongest and where an ESMO oral in ten weeks is the first real test, and XmAb942, where Xencor will enter Phase 3 roughly when Merck launches and where the realistic outcome is a partnership rather than independent commercialization. Add a near-certain equity raise inside 24 months, an obexelimab exclusivity window that nominally ends in 2029 absent patent term extension, and a documented internal-control failure within the last three years, and the +11% to base case does not compensate for the -42% left tail.

Existing holders should stay long through ESMO — the balance sheet supports the wait and the catalyst calendar is dense. New capital should wait for either the data or a better price. The mid-teens is where this becomes an accumulate.
