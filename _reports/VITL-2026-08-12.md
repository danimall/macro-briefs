## Executive Summary

Vital Farms' reported annual financials and its current operating condition describe two different companies. The FY2025 Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, shows net revenue of $759.4M (+25.3%), net income of $66.3M, and zero long-term debt. The Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, filed 161 days later, shows a 6.6% gross margin, a $31.1M quarterly net loss, $21.2M of cash, a waived fixed-charge-coverage covenant default, and a newly signed $125M term loan from a special-situations credit manager priced at SOFR (1.00% floor) plus 7.50%. Both the trailing P/E printed in market data (3,974x, an artifact of TTM net income near zero) and the "7.5x FY2025 earnings" framing are useless as valuation signals.

The mechanism is specific and disclosed. Vital Farms contracts with more than 625 family farms to buy all of their egg production and, in the 10-K's own words, is "contractually obligated to purchase these eggs irrespective of our ability to sell such eggs," at a price adjusted quarterly for feed cost on contracts averaging roughly six years. In the 2024-25 HPAI-driven shortage this was a windfall: locked-in premium supply into a spiking market produced 8.8% and 8.7% net margins. In 2026, with industry flocks recovered and total-category retail egg prices down more than 35% year over year (management's figure, August 6), the same structure converted a variable input into something close to a fixed charge. Vital Farms' contractually formula-set price cannot fall as fast as the spot commodity price, the retail price gap widens mechanically, shell-egg velocity declines, and surplus eggs get dumped into the breaker and wholesale channels. In Q2 those excess channel sales destroyed $19.5M of gross profit while adding $0.1M of net revenue.

The August 4 refinancing is the single most important new fact since our prior review and it cuts both ways. It removes the near-term probability of a distressed equity raise: pro forma cash is roughly $111M ($21.2M plus $125M drawn, less the $30M revolver repayment and estimated $4-6M of fees), against an undrawn $60M ABL. It also installs approximately $14.4M of annual cash interest — more than the entire $0-10M FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance range — a first lien on essentially all assets, and a fixed-charge-coverage test of 1.10x beginning around August 2027. Meanwhile FY2027 consensus EPS has been cut from $0.63 to $0.26 over 90 days. We upgrade from AVOID to REDUCE on the financing fact while lowering dimension scores on confirmed operating deterioration. Base case is approximately the current price; the distribution is wide and asymmetric to the downside.

## Company Overview

Vital Farms is the leading U.S. brand of pasture-raised shell eggs by retail dollar sales and, per its own disclosure, the second-largest U.S. egg brand by retail dollar sales. Shell eggs were 93% of net revenue in FY2025 and retail was $158.0M of $166.0M (95%) of Q2 FY2026 net revenue. The company is a Delaware public benefit corporation and a Certified B Corp; the 10-K explicitly states the board must balance stockholder pecuniary interests against other stakeholders and that this "may result in actions that do not maximize stockholder value."

The asset-light supply model is the defining feature. Farmers own the birds, feed, and working capital; Vital Farms is the obligated buyer. Processing runs primarily through Egg Central Station in Springfield, Missouri (~153,000 sq ft, capacity above 7.5 million eggs per day after a third line completed in Q4 FY2025), with contracted cold storage nearby — a concentration the 10-K carries as a dedicated risk factor.

The genuine demand-side asset is penetration runway: Vital Farms shell eggs reach roughly 10.0% of U.S. households versus category penetration of approximately 97.3%, across more than 24,000 stores (up from more than 23,500). The butter business is being wound down; accelerator-farm development is paused; roughly 526 of 1,540 acquired Indiana acres are being sold; and headcount was cut approximately 12% of remote crew.

## Financial Analysis

### The annual record and what the balance sheet said before the income statement did

| Fiscal year | Net revenue | Growth | Net income | Net margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $362.1M | — | $1.2M | 0.3% |
| FY2023 | $471.9M | +30.3% | $25.6M | 5.4% |
| FY2024 | $606.3M | +28.5% | $53.4M | 8.8% |
| FY2025 | $759.4M | +25.3% | $66.3M | 8.7% |

Revenue growth decelerated each year and margin plateaued in FY2025. FY2025 revenue also landed below the "at least $770M" guidance raised in August 2025. More important, the FY2025 balance sheet already contradicted the income statement: inventories rose 181% to $66.5M against 25.3% revenue growth; combined cash plus available-for-sale investments fell from $160.3M to $113.4M; capex of roughly $82M produced approximately negative $48M of free cash flow in a record earnings year. Management disclosed an ERP go-live in September 2025 that caused Q4 fulfillment disruption, which would also inflate year-end inventory. Both factors likely contributed; FY2026 results indicate supply overhang was the larger one.

The half was +1.8% only because Q1 was still growing (implied revenue $187.2M, net loss $1.5M). The damage is Q2: revenue -10.1% year over year and a 32-point gross margin collapse.

Adding back the three company-disclosed items — $19.5M of breaker/wholesale gross profit destruction, $7.8M of butter-exit inventory obsolescence, $0.8M of farmer contract amortization — gives an underlying gross margin near 23.5%, still roughly 15 points below prior year. That is the most generous defensible framing.

The cost of shrinking supply is now an explicit cash line. Voluntary farmer contract amendments (paying farmers to cease production from existing flocks or delay placements) are expected to cost **$35-45M over the next twelve months**, straddling H2 FY2026 and H1 FY2027. Because these contracts are accounted for as leases, the effect appears in lease lines that an unwary screen would classify as real estate: operating lease ROU assets rose $80.4M → $122.4M, and current operating lease liabilities $4.7M → $32.1M. Variable lease cost on long-term supply contracts was $164.3M in H1 FY2026 versus $116.2M a year earlier — a $48.0M increase on 1.8% revenue growth.

### Liquidity, the covenant breach, and the new capital structure

Cash fell from $48.8M to $21.2M over the half, with the entire $64.5M AFS portfolio liquidated and $30M drawn on the JPMorgan revolver. The current ratio fell from 2.16 to 1.10. As of June 28, 2026 the company was out of compliance with its fixed-charge-coverage covenant; it notified JPMorgan July 25 and obtained a waiver August 3. On August 4 the JPMorgan facility was terminated and replaced with:

- **Silver Point term loan:** $125M, three years, fully drawn, SOFR (1.00% floor) + 7.50%. First lien on all assets other than AR/inventory; second lien on those.
- **JPMorgan ABL:** up to $60M revolver, three years, borrowing-base on AR ($50.7M) and inventory ($73.9M) at quarter-end. Covenants: minimum revolving availability of $15M for twelve months; fixed charge coverage ≥ 1.10x for months 13-36.

Three observations. First, pro forma cash of roughly $111M plus ABL headroom is genuinely adequate against remaining FY2026 capex of $32.5-37.5M and roughly $6-7M of post-close interest this year. Second, annual cash interest rises from $0.6M of H1 total interest expense to approximately $14.4M, which exceeds the top of FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance. Third, the ABL commitment overstates usable capacity: the borrowing base shrinks precisely as AR and inventory shrink under the supply-reduction plan, and $15M of availability must be preserved.

The lender identity carries information. Pricing a first-lien loan to a company with a $491M equity value at SOFR+750bp is a market judgment about available alternatives. The competing reading — that management chose speed, certainty, and a twelve-month covenant holiday over price — is supported by the CFO's language and is plausible. Either way, the equity now sits behind $125M of expensive senior secured debt where nine months ago it sat behind nothing.

### The FY2026 guidance requires a severe second-half inflection

Guidance reaffirmed August 6: revenue $775-800M, Adjusted EBITDA $0-10M, capex $70-75M, with two stated assumptions — a return to positive shell-egg volume growth in H2, and that competitive activity does not intensify further.

- **Revenue:** $775-800M less H1 $353.2M = $421.8-446.8M for H2 versus $412.4M in H2 FY2025, implying +2.3% to +8.3% year-over-year growth against Q2's -10.1%. A 12- to 18-point swing in growth rate within one quarter.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $0-10M less H1 $(21.6)M = +$21.6M to +$31.6M for H2, a 5.1-7.1% margin against Q2's -16.0%. A 21-23 point margin swing.

The bridge items are identifiable rather than hand-waving: eliminating the $19.5M/quarter breaker drag, non-recurrence of the $7.8M butter charge and $3.3M restructuring charge, the ~12% overhead reduction, seasonal Q4 baking demand, and distribution gains. Together they account for a large share of the required swing. But the guide was reaffirmed on the same day the financing closed, the moment a management team is most institutionally committed to its plan, and it depends on a commodity price gap that is outside management's control.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth stories are in tension. The household-penetration gap (10.0% versus 97.3%) and store expansion (>24,000) are real and unexhausted, and H1 price/mix was a **positive** $9.2M contributor with Q2 still positive at $1.1M — the revenue decline was almost entirely volume, not realized price. That is consistent with a supply-and-channel problem rather than brand rejection.

Against that, the earnings growth trajectory is being cut hard. Consensus FY2027 EPS has moved $0.63 → $0.44 → $0.26 over 90 days (-58.7%), while current-year estimates improved marginally ($(0.45) → $(0.41)). The pattern says analysts are pushing recovery out rather than cancelling it — the near term is stabilizing while the recovery year is being repriced. This is the single most important disconfirming signal against the recovery case, and it arrives alongside a 1-beat, 3-miss record over the last four quarters, including a -132% miss in Q1 FY2026.

Growth is also being deliberately reversed on the supply side. Farm count went from "more than 500" to "more than 625" in a year; the company is now paying $35-45M to unwind part of that. Closing retail price gaps to restore volume is by construction margin-dilutive. And Vital Crossroads — the second processing facility that was to be "fully operational in early 2027" per the February 10-K — will be mothballed after the shell is completed, deferring capacity growth to 2028 or later while still consuming $55-80M of committed capex over the next twelve months.

## Valuation Assessment

Reported multiples are not usable. TTM net income is approximately break-even (H2 FY2025 of roughly $32.7M plus H1 FY2026 of $(32.6)M), producing the 3,974x trailing P/E. FY2027 consensus EPS of $0.26 puts the stock at 44x. Peer medians (INGR 11.3x, DAR 16.2x, BG 21.1x, ADM 21.0x; median 21.0x) are not comparable because those companies are profitable and none carries VITL's contractual supply rigidity.

The only defensible approach is normalized earnings power, and it does not produce an obvious mispricing.

**Enterprise value:** $491M equity + $125M term debt − $111M pro forma cash ≈ **$505M**, before treating $122M of operating lease liabilities that are economically supply commitments.

**Normalized case.** Assume the supply/demand rebalance holds and FY2027 revenue reaches roughly $820M. At a 26-28% gross margin — above Q2's 23.5% underlying but well below the 38.7% HPAI-inflated H1 FY2025 level and below the unverified "~30% exit run rate" attributed to the call — gross profit is $213-230M. Against SG&A of roughly $160-165M (H1 annualized less the 12% headcount reduction), operating income is $50-70M; adding roughly $20-25M of D&A gives EBITDA of $70-95M. Subtract $14.4M interest and cash taxes, and normalized EPS lands near $0.55-0.90. At $11.45 that is 13-21x — a market multiple for a business that has just demonstrated a 32-point single-quarter gross margin swing, carries first-lien debt at ~11.5%, faces a 1.10x coverage test in roughly twelve months, and holds a $103.1M impairment candidate on its balance sheet.

**On EV/EBITDA:** at 7x normalized $80M EBITDA, EV is $560M, less net debt of roughly $14M, giving equity near $546M or $12.70 per share. At 6x, $11.20 down to about $9.60 depending on cash. The valuation clears spot only if normalized EBITDA lands at the top of that band with a multiple at or above 7x.

**Scenario values used throughout this report:**

- **Bull $18.50 (+62%):** supply/demand balance holds through Q4, gross margin exits FY2026 near 28-30%, FY2027 EBITDA $90-100M, Silver Point refinanced at conventional pricing, Vital Crossroads restarted rather than impaired. Roughly consistent with the $18 high analyst target.
- **Base $11.50 (approximately flat):** guidance is met at the low end, FY2027 revenue ~$820M with a 26% gross margin, EBITDA ~$70M, capex still elevated, coverage test passed with limited headroom, multiple stays compressed at 6.5-7x on leverage and volatility.
- **Bear $6.00 (-48%):** H2 volume does not inflect because the commodity price gap persists, H2 Adjusted EBITDA lands between $0 and $(10)M, the $55-80M Vital Crossroads commitment plus $35-45M of farmer payments plus $14.4M interest produce negative FCF through FY2027, cash falls toward $30-40M into the month-13 coverage test, and the company either impairs Vital Crossroads, raises equity at a depressed price, or renegotiates with a secured lender that holds all the collateral.

Probability-weighting at roughly 28% / 40% / 32% gives an expected value near $11.70, about 2% above the current price, with a 48% left tail. That is not a margin of safety. Analyst mean of $13.22 and median $14.00 sit above spot, but the low target of $10.00 and the 6-hold / 4-buy split reflect the same wide distribution. On a stock with negative current-year earnings and a 58.7% cut to the recovery-year estimate, consensus targets are not a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

The 2026 shock is category-wide, which matters for attribution. Cal-Maine — the largest U.S. shell egg producer, vertically integrated, with no comparable six-year take-or-pay structure — was also driven into a loss by the same oversupply in its fiscal fourth quarter. That materially weakens any thesis that Vital Farms' collapse was primarily idiosyncratic mismanagement; the better reading is an industry cycle turn amplified by Vital Farms' contractual rigidity.

Brand evidence remains supportive on the demand side: store count rose, price/mix stayed positive in both quarters, and management claims more than 200bps of category dollar-share gain. That share figure is measured in dollars during a period when Vital Farms' realized price fell less than the category's, which mechanically flatters it, and we could not verify it independently.

Three structural items are deteriorating. First, retailer private label in pasture-raised eggs is the named competitive threat the 10-K flags, and management's response — investing in price — is margin-dilutive by construction. Second, single-facility processing risk is now increasing rather than decreasing: mothballing Vital Crossroads extends Egg Central Station's status as a single point of failure, with contracted cold storage nearby, well past the original 2027 plan. Third, customer concentration worsened even as the largest relationship shrank. UNFI was 25%/24%/22% of net revenue in FY2023-25; in H1 FY2026, Customer A was 22% and Customer B crossed above 10% to 12% (from below 10%). Disclosed 10%+ customers went from one to two, aggregating 34%; top-three AR concentration is 41%. A falling largest-customer percentage is not evidence of diversification when a second 10%+ customer emerges. Note that the identification of Customer A as UNFI is an inference from matching revenue percentages, not a disclosed fact.

Supplier fragility is underappreciated: substantially all shell egg cartons come from a single supplier, and the 10-K discloses **no written contracts** with the liquid egg co-manufacturer or the shell egg co-packers, meaning those relationships can be altered or terminated at will. The butter co-packer already terminated.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural deflation exposure.** The take-or-pay contracts have a roughly six-year average tail. The $35-45M of amendment payments reduce near-term supply; they do not remove the structure. Any renewed decline in commodity egg prices reopens the same price-gap channel.

**Fixed-charge coverage test, ~August 2027.** The ABL requires ≥1.10x coverage from month 13. With approximately $14.4M of interest plus capex and lease obligations in the denominator, this test is a live binary within the investment horizon, and it is tested against a borrowing base that shrinks with AR and inventory.

**Vital Crossroads: $103.1M of construction in progress on a paused facility.** No impairment has been recorded and none is disclosed as expected; capitalized costs are tested against eventual recoverable value, not near-term utilization. But the reversal from "fully operational in early 2027" (February 10-K) to "halt construction by end of 2026" (May) within roughly three months is the sharpest available indicator of how fast management's demand view changed, and $55-80M of further capex is still expected over the next twelve months for equipment and prior commitments — capital outflow that produces no revenue.

**Litigation.** The FY2025 10-K stated no material litigation as of February 26, 2026. Since then: *Wilkerson* (§10(b), Rule 10b-5, §20(a); W.D. Tex., filed March 27, 2026; amended complaint due September 21, 2026) and *Mody* (derivative; W.D. Tex., filed July 8, 2026). The company cannot estimate possible loss. The 10-K's unusually specific oversupply risk-factor language helps on adequacy of warning; the plaintiffs' case will more likely focus on February 2026 guidance and on when management knew supply and demand had diverged — and the 181% year-end inventory build is suggestive on that point.

**Agricultural disease.** Egg Drop Syndrome affected nine farms in FY2024 and twelve in FY2025, one in FY2026 to date with vaccinations procured. Note the second-order dynamic: industry HPAI *helped* Vital Farms in FY2024-25 by cutting conventional supply. Flock recovery is the mirror image.

**Category regulatory overhang.** The DOJ Antitrust Division and 17 state attorneys general filed a civil price-fixing action around the industry egg-price benchmark against Cal-Maine and two other producers in late June 2026, settled for approximately $3.3M plus a 53-million-egg donation. Vital Farms does not appear among the reported defendants and no filing connects it to the matter. The relevance is indirect: adverse publicity about egg pricing can affect willingness to pay premiums.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 76.8% against 61.6% one-year realized is rich (15.2-point spread), with the nearest expiry pricing a ±12.1% move on an eight-day tenor. The structure is in contango — 76.8% at one week rising to 82.6% at three months and 88.7% at six months — with the six-month tenor pricing a ±46.3% move. The market is pricing a wider distribution further out, consistent with the FY2026 guidance test in Q3/Q4 and the month-13 coverage covenant, rather than an imminent single-event resolution. The put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.98 to 1.39 since mid-June while the stock rose 11%, and the near-term put/call volume ratio is 2.65 — protection and downside positioning accumulated into strength. Near-term skew is characterized as balanced at -0.7%. We note the longer-dated skew readings (-33.0% at three months, -21.6% at six months) without inferring direction from the sign, since the convention is not defined in the source. Nothing here contradicts a REDUCE; it corroborates that the equity is a wide-distribution instrument, and it makes long premium an expensive way to express either view.

**Short interest cuts against aggressive bearishness.** At 29.3% of float (8.74M shares, 5.7 days to cover), the bear case is crowded. A single in-line quarter or an upside surprise on Q3 volumes could produce a violent squeeze. This is a principal reason we do not extend REDUCE toward AVOID and do not treat the name as shortable.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($18.50, +62%)

The 2026 collapse is a cycle event, not a franchise event, and the evidence supports that: Cal-Maine lost money on the same oversupply; Vital Farms' Q2 revenue decline was volume, not price realization; price/mix stayed positive; store count grew. The supply-reduction program is designed specifically to eliminate the $19.5M/quarter breaker drag, and the CFO stated supply and demand projections are "now in balance as we head into Q3." Non-recurrence of the $7.8M butter charge and $3.3M restructuring charge, plus a ~12% overhead reduction and seasonally stronger Q4 baking demand, bridges much of the required H2 swing. Eleven insiders bought in the open market at $8.00-$8.92. If gross margin exits FY2026 near 28-30% and FY2027 EBITDA reaches $90-100M, the Silver Point loan gets refinanced at conventional pricing, Vital Crossroads restarts rather than impairs, and a 10% household penetration against a 97.3% category leaves the long-term growth story intact.

### Bear Case ($6.00, -48%)

Management does not control the variable that matters. Vital Farms' price is set on a feed-cost formula on six-year contracts; the commodity price it competes against is set daily. If category prices stay 35% below prior year, the retail gap only closes by Vital Farms investing further in price, which caps gross margin in the low 20s. In that world H2 Adjusted EBITDA lands near zero rather than +$21.6M to +$31.6M, and the cash math turns: $32.5-37.5M of remaining FY2026 capex, then $55-80M of Vital Crossroads commitments and $35-45M of farmer payments over the following twelve months, against $14.4M of interest. Pro forma cash of $111M erodes toward $30-40M into the August 2027 coverage test, with an ABL borrowing base shrinking as AR and inventory shrink. At that point the options are an equity raise at a depressed price, a Vital Crossroads impairment against $103.1M of carrying value, or a renegotiation with a secured lender holding all the collateral. FY2027 consensus EPS being cut 58.7% in 90 days is the market's current best estimate of which direction this is trending.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is an upgrade from three consecutive AVOID ratings, and the reason is specific: the acute liquidity binary that drove those calls was resolved by the August 4 financing, which is a completed transaction rather than a forecast. Dimension scores nonetheless declined because Q2 confirmed the operating deterioration was worse than previously assessed (6.6% gross margin, covenant breach) and because the cost of the solution — $14.4M of annual interest, a first lien on all assets, and a 1.10x coverage test — consumes a large share of the equity's recovery value.

**Base case target: $11.50 (approximately flat).** FY2027 revenue ~$820M at a 26% gross margin, SG&A ~$162M, EBITDA ~$70M, valued at 7x less net debt of roughly $14M. Bull $18.50 (+62%); bear $6.00 (-48%). Probability-weighted expected value approximately $11.70, or +2%, against a 48% downside tail.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into strength rather than around the Q3 print (early November 2026), which is the highest-variance single event in the horizon: it is the first quarter that must show the promised inflection, and the six-month option tenor prices a ±46.3% distribution. Given 29.3% short interest and 5.7 days to cover, the reduction should be scaled, not dumped — a squeeze on an in-line print is a real possibility. We would not hold a position into the August 2027 coverage test without an intervening demonstration of positive free cash flow. Prospective investors should wait.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Q3 FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of at least $10M with gross margin at or above 24%, confirming the H2 bridge is real rather than arithmetic.
2. Positive shell-egg **volume** growth year over year (not dollar share), with breaker/wholesale gross-profit drag below $5M in the quarter.
3. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow, or a disclosed reduction of the Vital Crossroads twelve-month capex commitment below $30M.
4. Refinancing or repricing of the Silver Point term loan at a spread below 500bp, which would restore roughly $6-8M of annual pre-tax cash flow.

**Avoid triggers (full thesis impairment):**
1. Q3 revenue below $200M or Adjusted EBITDA below zero, which would put FY2026 guidance out of reach and open the FY2027 coverage test as a live default risk.
2. Any impairment charge against the $103.1M Vital Crossroads construction in progress, or a second covenant waiver.
3. Announcement of an equity or equity-linked issuance — with 18.2M shares of plan authorization headroom and a stock at $11.45, dilution would be expensive.
4. Loss of, or material reduction in, either 10%+ customer relationship (A at 22%, B at 12% of H1 revenue).

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 results, expected early November 2026, with the *Wilkerson* amended complaint (due September 21, 2026) as an interim information event.

## Conclusion

Vital Farms owns a genuine brand with unexhausted penetration runway, in a category where the 2026 downturn hit the largest and most flexible producer as hard as it hit the least flexible one. That argues the franchise is intact and the earnings collapse is cyclical. It does not argue the equity is cheap at $11.45.

Three facts govern the call. The company's contractual structure converts egg deflation into a fixed charge with a six-year tail, and unwinding it costs $35-45M of cash. The financing that saved the balance sheet costs approximately $14.4M a year — more than the entire FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance range — and pledges every asset to a secured lender ahead of a 1.10x coverage test roughly twelve months out. And the analysts closest to the story have cut FY2027 EPS by 58.7% in ninety days, which is the clearest available read on how the recovery is being repriced even as management reaffirms guidance.

Governance compounds rather than offsets: $35.2M of stock repurchased at an average of $16.43 in the six months before the covenant breach, $15.0M of it in Q2 *after* the May guidance cut had made the deterioration public, with the program then terminated as a lender condition and $65M unused; a CEO who is also Executive Chairperson adopting a 10b5-1 plan for approximately 82% of his stake two weeks after eleven other insiders bought at $8. The company also discloses a related-party arrangement with Sandpebble, owned by the CEO's father, which provides project management for facility construction including Vital Crossroads (payments of $553K in H1 FY2026); the amounts are small relative to the capital program and the disclosure is specific. The eleven-insider purchase cluster is a real positive signal, but it was dollar-capped at round numbers and the two officers with the most information did not participate. The PSU expense reversal of $3.3M shows the incentive plan de-accruing as performance deteriorates, which is how alignment should work.

At $11.45 the base case is approximately the current price, the bull case requires a gross margin outcome above anything the company has demonstrated in a balanced market, and the bear case is a 48% decline. **REDUCE.** We would revisit constructively at $7-8 with evidence of positive free cash flow, or at any price once Q3 demonstrates that the second-half inflection is operational rather than arithmetic.
