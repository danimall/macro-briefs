**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coty is one of the largest global beauty companies, with a strong prestige fragrance franchise (Hugo Boss, Calvin Klein, Gucci, Burberry, Kylie) operating alongside a structurally declining mass cosmetics business (CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, Max Factor, Bourjois). The two halves have diverged sharply: 9M FY26 Prestige operating income was $449M at a ~14.8% margin and ~22.8% adjusted EBITDA margin, while Consumer Beauty posted a reported operating loss of $412.7M and swung to an adjusted operating loss. The healthy half is subsidizing a broken half that has now been impaired to zero excess fair value, with management conceding further impairments are "reasonably likely."

The stock has fallen 62% over twelve months to $1.90, and the decline maps to specific, verifiable fundamental damage rather than market noise. Four consecutive earnings misses (the latest two by 20-26%), a withdrawn FY26 guidance in February 2026, a securities-fraud class action alleging that the November 2025 guidance was materially misleading, a $362.8M Q3 impairment, a $201.9M realized loss on the Wella exit, and a forward-repurchase contract structure that bled more than $200M of cash in nine months as the stock kept falling are the reasons sophisticated investors are selling. Short interest sits at 11.5% of float.

We rate COTY AVOID with an overall score of 3.0. The valuation looks superficially distressed and the prestige business has real value, but the equity sits behind $3.2B of debt and $6.7B of intangibles, tangible book is deeply negative, GAAP losses are recurring, governance is compromised, and the forward-contract overhang creates a self-reinforcing cash drain. The modest open-market insider buying by the Consumer Beauty President ($200K) and CFO ($13K) is the lone contrarian signal and is not sufficient to offset the structural and legal overhangs. This is the third consecutive AVOID and the bar for changing that view has not been cleared.

## Company Overview

Coty manufactures and distributes fragrances, color cosmetics, and skin and body care across roughly 123 countries. Fiscal year ends June 30. The business runs two reportable segments:

- **Prestige (~67% of revenue):** Designer and licensed fragrances plus prestige cosmetics, the company's primary earnings engine. Bright spots cited include Gucci Flora, Burberry in Asia, Kylie franchises, and CK Euphoria Elixir.
- **Consumer Beauty (~33% of revenue):** Mass color cosmetics, mass fragrance/body care, plus a distinct Brazil business. This segment generated a 9M FY26 adjusted operating loss of $22.4M (versus +$86.7M prior year).

By category, fragrance is roughly 69% of revenue, color cosmetics ~23%, body care ~5%, skincare ~4%. Coty is predominantly a fragrance company, and global designer fragrance is a structurally attractive, growing category, the company's core asset.

Coty is majority-controlled by JAB Beauty B.V. (~54% of Class A on an as-converted basis, including Peter Harf's HFS holdings and Series B preferred). This is a controlled company, with the governance discount that implies.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is declining and the headline understates the weakness.** Reported FY25 revenue was ~$6.1B. 9M FY26 net revenue was $4,537M, down 2% YoY; Q3 (ended March 2026) was $1,281.6M, down 1%. The declines are volume-driven — unit volume fell 6% for 9M and 8% in Q3 — partly masked by a 4-6% FX tailwind from a weak dollar against the euro and real. Underlying demand is materially softer than the reported numbers suggest. Both segments are now declining; Consumer Beauty fell 5% for 9M.

**Profitability has collapsed into GAAP losses.** 9M FY26 operating loss was $38.8M (versus +$225.6M prior year); Q3 operating loss was $372M. 9M net loss attributable to Coty was $463.8M (versus $299.1M), and FY25 was already a net loss of ~$368M. Three items drive the destruction: the $362.8M Q3 impairment ($237.1M Consumer Beauty goodwill + $125.7M trademark write-downs on CoverGirl, Sally Hansen, Max Factor, Bourjois); the $201.9M realized loss on the Wella stake sale; and gross margin erosion. Q3 gross margin fell ~230 bps to 61.8% on higher manufacturing and material costs, tariff-driven freight (~80 bps), and excess and obsolescence. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 9.9% in Q3 (from 15.7%) and to 16.6% for 9M (from 20.6%).

**The adjusted-versus-GAAP chasm is the central analytical tension.** Coty reports +$198.5M adjusted net income for 9M FY26 against the GAAP loss. The bridge is dominated by impairments that are now recurring every year ($362.8M in FY26, $212.8M in FY25) and accelerating amortization. When "one-time" charges recur annually, they function as an economic cost, not a normalization adjustment. The distressed Altman Z-Score (~1.2) reflects the GAAP reality, and we weight it accordingly.

**The balance sheet is leveraged and deleveraging only slowly.** Total debt is $3,216M (down from $4,008M at June 2025), almost entirely fixed-rate senior secured notes; cash is $257M. Stockholders' equity of $3,091M is supported by $3,810M goodwill + $2,861M intangibles = $6.67B of intangible assets, leaving tangible book deeply negative against a $5.7B accumulated deficit. The Total Net Leverage covenant is ≤4.00x and management states compliance at March 31, 2026, but the EBITDA add-back definitions are generous and falling EBITDA narrows the headroom each quarter.

**Cash flow has a hidden quality problem.** 9M FY26 operating cash flow of $421.8M (roughly flat YoY) looks adequate, but it is being consumed by forward repurchase contract true-ups: the company paid $208.7M in Hedge Valuation Adjustments through 9M as the falling stock price triggered settlements. These contracts were extended again in January 2026 to December 2026/January 2027, deferring rather than resolving the issue. Capex was ~$146M. The Wella sale ($750M cash) was the major liquidity event, used to redeem debt.

**Capital allocation reflects the distress.** The common dividend has been suspended since April 2020 and will not resume until net debt/EBITDA approaches 2x, not a near-term prospect. $796.8M remains authorized under the buyback, but no shares were repurchased in Q3, even as the company keeps paying to carry the forward contracts. Series B preferred accrues 9% cash dividends (~$9.9M/yr).

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth thesis. Revenue is contracting on volume in both segments, and the favorable FX is a tailwind that can reverse. The forward estimate trend is negative:

- Current-year EPS estimate cut to $0.23 from $0.29 ninety days ago (-19.1%).
- Current-quarter estimate down 64.6% over 30 days and -1,555.8% over 90 days as it crossed into loss territory.
- Next-year EPS of $0.37, revised down 4.8% over 90 days.

The earnings surprise record is four straight misses, the last two by 20.2% and 25.5%, with the June 2025 and March 2026 quarters swinging to losses. Falling estimates plus a perfect miss record signal deteriorating visibility, not a trough about to turn. The estimate-revision pattern is the inverse of the positive-momentum signal that would support a turnaround call.

The one credible growth asset is prestige fragrance, a category growing globally where Coty holds genuine franchises. That growth is being diluted by Consumer Beauty's decline. The strategic review of the mass/Brazil assets, if it results in a divestiture at a reasonable price, could improve the consolidated growth profile by subtraction. With Consumer Beauty already impaired to zero excess fair value and the mass color category in structural decline, finding a buyer at an accretive valuation is uncertain. There is no fixed timeline and no confirmed transaction.

The $80M Fixed Cost Reduction Plan (split across FY26/FY27) is real but modest against the margin erosion underway, and cost programs do not address the volume declines driving the top line.

## Valuation Assessment

A trailing/forward P/E of -3.0 is meaningless given negative GAAP earnings. The relevant question is whether the equity offers asymmetric upside behind the capital structure. We conclude it does not at an adequate margin of safety.

On the company's own consensus, $0.23 current-year and $0.37 next-year EPS against a $1.90 price imply ~8.3x current-year and ~5.1x next-year adjusted earnings. That looks cheap until you account for the adjusted-versus-GAAP gap: on GAAP, the company is loss-making, and the "earnings" being capitalized exclude recurring impairments. Enterprise value tells the more honest story. Market cap is ~$1.7B; adding net debt of ~$2.96B (total debt $3,216M less cash $257M) yields an EV of ~$4.6B. Against trailing 9M adjusted EBITDA of $753M (annualizing to ~$1.0B but declining), EV/EBITDA is roughly 4.6x — distressed-cheap, but the EBITDA is shrinking 21% YoY and the equity sits behind the full debt load.

Peer comparison underscores the discount but also explains it. Sector median P/E is 21.7x (PG 21.7, KVUE 21.5), Estée Lauder is unprofitable (-128 P/E), and e.l.f. trades at 137.9x as the structural winner taking share from exactly the heritage brands Coty owns. Coty's discount reflects negative tangible book, recurring impairments, litigation, a controlled-company governance overhang, and the cash-draining forward-contract structure. A low multiple here is the market correctly pricing structural impairment, the value-trap profile rather than the opportunity profile.

Analyst targets span $1.50 to $8.00 with a $2.60 median and ~$3.17 mean, an extremely wide dispersion that reflects the binary nature of the situation (successful prestige-focused turnaround versus continued decline and dilution/distress). The $8.00 high target appears stale relative to the post-Q3 reality. We do not treat the targets as a floor here; this is a sub-$2 distressed equity with deteriorating estimates, the profile where consensus targets lag downward, not upward.

We score valuation 3.5, slightly above the floor because the prestige franchise has real value and the EV multiple is genuinely low, but well below a buy threshold because the cheapness is structurally explained and lacks an independent, dated catalyst to unlock it.

## Competitive Landscape

Coty's competitive position is bifurcated. In prestige fragrance it competes with L'Oréal, Estée Lauder, LVMH, Puig, and Interparfums, and holds a strong designer-fragrance franchise in a structurally attractive category with real momentum behind specific launches. This is the asset worth owning if it could be separated cleanly.

In mass color cosmetics the trajectory is deteriorating. The US and European mass color category is in structural decline, and Coty's heritage brands (CoverGirl, Rimmel, Max Factor, Bourjois) are losing share to indie and value disruptors like e.l.f. Management concedes its share gains "still lag overall growth within the category." Brazil body care/deodorant faces competitive pricing pressure. The Q3 trademark impairments are a financial acknowledgment that the market values these brands materially lower than Coty's prior carrying values.

License dependence is a structural vulnerability on the healthy side: the prestige franchise rests on designer licenses (Hugo Boss, CK, Gucci, Burberry) where non-renewal risk is material. Q3 included accelerated amortization on a brand license, with amortization up 62% YoY. We score competitive position 4.0, holding the prior level: the prestige strength is real but the consolidated trajectory is deteriorating as the mass business erodes and license risk sits on the franchise that matters.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack here is unusually heavy and several items are not yet fully priced:

1. **Further impairment risk.** The Consumer Beauty reporting unit now carries 0% excess fair value. Management quantifies that a 25 bps lower revenue-growth assumption implies ~$35M of additional impairment, and a 25 bps higher discount rate implies ~$43M. Trademark cushions are minimal. More impairments are explicitly "reasonably likely."

2. **Securities and derivative litigation.** A securities class action (*Srinivasan v. Coty*, S.D.N.Y., filed March 23, 2026) alleges the November 2025 FY26 guidance was materially false/misleading (class period Nov 5, 2025 – Feb 4, 2026). Three derivative suits followed, naming current and former officers and directors including former CEO Nabi. The company cannot estimate a loss range. This directly damages governance credibility and creates an open-ended liability.

3. **Talc/asbestos litigation — escalating.** Coty discloses that settlement values and volumes have "increased substantially and are expected to increase," with higher recent demands as cases reach trial. Insurance coverage is partial and uncertain. This is one of the few areas where management concedes a potentially material effect.

4. **Forward repurchase contract overhang.** A depressed stock costs the company cash through hedge true-ups. Repeatedly extending the contracts defers a reckoning rather than resolving it. The more the stock falls, the more cash drains, which pressures the balance sheet, which pressures the stock.

5. **Leverage and refinancing.** Deleveraging is dependent on shrinking EBITDA. The 2027 Euro notes ($573M) are the next maturity. A downgrade below investment grade would reinstate collateral/covenant terms on the notes.

6. **Controlled-company/JAB conflicts.** A JAB executive-services secondment agreement (Oct 2025), a JAB lease guarantee, and the risk of JAB share sales pressuring the stock.

7. **Tariffs and geopolitics.** ~$32M estimated tariff cost through Q1 FY27; Middle East conflict expected to cut Q4 FY26 sales by 2-3%.

We score risk factors 2.0, holding the prior trough level. The combination of recurring impairments, an active securities-fraud class action over the very guidance that was withdrawn, escalating talc exposure, and the cash-draining forward-contract structure represents specific, near-term downside catalysts that are not fully discounted.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.31 to 0.87 between mid-May and mid-June 2026 as the price fell 12.2% to $1.95, indicating accumulating hedging or directional put demand into the decline. Short interest of 11.5% of float (5.1 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The options shift is consistent with our fundamental read; it does not suggest a near-term squeeze setup given the moderate days-to-cover.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The prestige fragrance business is a ~20%+ adjusted-EBITDA-margin franchise in a growing category. If Coty divests the loss-making Consumer Beauty/Brazil assets through its strategic review at any reasonable value, the consolidated business re-rates toward a prestige-pure fragrance peer multiple, and the Wella-driven deleveraging continues. Two insiders bought in the open market at distressed prices — the Consumer Beauty President (83,000 shares at $2.41, ~$200K, March 6, 2026) and the CFO (5,000 shares at $2.65, ~$13K, Feb 10, 2026) — a vote of confidence as the very segment being impaired was written down. Strobel's market-condition options vesting at $5.56/$9.00 align management with a meaningful recovery. At ~4.6x EV/EBITDA the equity is a leveraged call option on a successful prestige-focused turnaround. Bull case ~$4.00 (a partial re-rating toward prestige-peer economics on a successful Consumer Beauty exit), roughly +110% from current.

### Bear Case

Consumer Beauty keeps declining and triggers further impairments; the strategic review fails to find a buyer at an acceptable price; the securities class action and escalating talc liabilities crystallize into material costs; the forward-contract cash drain continues as the stock stays depressed, pressuring covenant headroom on shrinking EBITDA; and a downgrade reinstates harsher debt terms. With negative tangible book and the equity behind the debt load, continued operating losses compress the equity value further. Bear case ~$1.20, roughly -37% from current. Given the leverage, the left tail (a forced restructuring or deeply dilutive recapitalization) is real if EBITDA erosion accelerates.

The asymmetry is not favorable on a probability-weighted basis. The bull case requires a clean divestiture, litigation resolution, and EBITDA stabilization to all occur; the bear case requires only continuation of the current trajectory.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There are several potential downside catalysts but none with a clean, dated trigger. The next quarterly print (Q4 FY26, ~early August 2026) carries miss risk given the four-quarter miss streak, the withdrawn-then-reinstated guidance, and 2-3% Middle East revenue headwind into Q4. Further Consumer Beauty impairment is "reasonably likely" but tied to annual testing, not a fixed date. The securities class action and talc litigation are slow-moving. The forward-contract settlement window (Dec 2026/Jan 2027) is the most concrete dated overhang but is 6+ months out.

**Timing window**: The most actionable catalyst is the August 2026 earnings report. The contract-settlement and litigation catalysts resolve over 6-12+ months, too long to carry comfortably at a sub-$2 distressed name.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: 5.1 days to cover, ~43.2M shares short. This is elevated but not extreme. Days-to-cover of 5.1 creates moderate squeeze risk on any positive surprise (a divestiture announcement, a litigation settlement at a contained level, or a single earnings beat after four misses).

**Borrow and options signal**: With the options market broadly aligned with the bearish thesis, the easy short money has likely been made. Crowding at 11.5% with a sub-$2 price limits incremental downside per dollar of capital at risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$1.20 (-37%) against meaningful squeeze upside on any of several positive surprises (a divestiture at a reasonable price could move the stock +30-50% given low absolute price). The reward:risk is roughly 1:1 to modestly below 2:1, short of the threshold for an active short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and the trajectory is negative, but there is no specific, dated catalyst with favorable mechanics, the short is already moderately crowded, the stock is near multi-year lows (limiting remaining downside per dollar of carry), and the strategic-review optionality creates real squeeze risk. This is a mediocre, distressed business to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID. Timeline for headwinds: 12-18 months.** The structural decline in Consumer Beauty, the recurring impairment cycle, the litigation overhangs, and the forward-contract cash drain (resolving Dec 2026/Jan 2027) are multi-quarter problems. Estimate revisions are still falling; there is no evidence of a near-term turn.

**Re-entry criteria** (any of the following would warrant reassessment toward HOLD or BUY):
- A confirmed divestiture of Consumer Beauty/Brazil at a value that is neutral-to-accretive, separating the prestige fragrance franchise cleanly.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or positive organic (ex-FX) volume trends, with estimate revisions turning positive.
- Resolution or quantification of the securities class action and talc litigation at contained, disclosed amounts, removing the open-ended tail.
- Settlement (not extension) of the forward repurchase contracts, ending the cash drain, combined with net debt/EBITDA on a clear path below 3.0x.

We expect the headwinds to persist through at least FY27, and the prestige value is unlikely to be unlocked for the equity until the capital structure and Consumer Beauty overhang are resolved.

## Conclusion

Coty owns a valuable prestige fragrance business trapped inside a leveraged, governance-impaired holding company with a structurally declining mass segment, recurring impairments, active securities-fraud and talc litigation, and a forward-repurchase structure that drains cash as the stock falls. The 62% twelve-month decline maps directly to verifiable fundamental damage — four consecutive earnings misses, falling estimates, withdrawn guidance, the Q3 impairment, the Wella loss, and a CEO ousted under a class action — rather than to indiscriminate selling. The superficial cheapness (4.6x EV/EBITDA, ~5x next-year adjusted EPS) is explained by negative tangible book, recurring GAAP losses that the adjusted figures exclude, and an equity that sits behind the debt load.

The modest open-market insider buying is the one genuine contrarian signal, but ~$213K of purchases does not offset the structural and legal overhangs. The risk/reward is not favorable enough to own and not asymmetric enough to short. We maintain **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.0, holding the prior trajectory (3.4 → 3.2 → 3.0). We would revisit only on a confirmed, value-accretive Consumer Beauty divestiture, two quarters of volume stabilization with positive estimate revisions, or quantified resolution of the litigation and forward-contract overhangs.
