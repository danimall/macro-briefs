**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegion's July 23 second-quarter report resolved the central question left open by the first quarter. In Q1 2026, reported revenue grew 9.7% while operating income fell $1.1M and diluted GAAP EPS declined 7.0% — double-digit top-line growth producing zero operating leverage, with both segments compressing. Q2 reversed it: revenue $1,151.5M (+12.7%), GAAP operating margin +60bps to 22.1%, adjusted operating margin +50bps to 24.2%, adjusted EPS $2.40 versus a $2.26 consensus (+6.2% surprise), and Americas organic volume of +4.9%, the best volume print in the period reviewed. International, the source of the Q1 damage, improved 440bps sequentially. Management raised FY2026 reported revenue growth to 7.5%-8.5% from 5.0%-7.0% and adjusted EPS to $8.85-$9.00 from $8.70-$8.90.

That is genuine fundamental improvement, and it justifies reversing the declining score trajectory this name has carried since February. The stock has responded: $137.24 on July 20 to $168.94 on August 7, up 23.1% in three weeks and roughly 34% off the mid-May closing low of $125.65. The resulting arithmetic is the whole of the investment case. At $168.94 Allegion trades at 18.9x the $8.925 guidance midpoint and 17.3x FY2027 consensus of $9.75, cheaper than a 24.8x building-products peer median, on a business generating a 16.9% free-cash-flow margin, ~19% ROIC and 27.9%-29.0% Americas segment margins. But the price now sits fractionally below the $170 analyst consensus median and below the $174.64 mean, and estimate revisions, while positive, are running at +2% over 90 days rather than the +30% velocity that signals systematic consensus underestimation.

Two near-term facts temper the momentum read. First, the CFO volunteered that an end-May price increase pulled orders forward into June, that underlying Americas demand was "in the high singles… maybe just not as high as 9%," and that Q3 2025 is a difficult residential comparison because the electronics launch landed in that quarter. Second, H1 operating cash flow fell to $299.7M from $314.2M despite higher net earnings, driven by a $122.0M receivables build consistent with revenue landing late in the quarter. Guidance implies H2 adjusted EPS of roughly $4.65-$4.80 against $4.24 in H2 2025 (+10% to +13%), an acceleration from H1's +7.7%, dependent on the $10M restructuring run-rate arriving in Q4 and International's sequential recovery continuing. HOLD, with the base case at $185 (+9.5%) and a genuine entry at $145-$152.

## Company Overview

Allegion is a pure-play security and access-control manufacturer, incorporated in Ireland in 2013 and spun from Ingersoll Rand in December 2013. FY2025 revenue was $4,067.3M across 40+ brands including Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN, Falcon, CISA, SimonsVoss, ELATEC and AXA, from 37 principal production facilities and ~13,300 employees.

The economics are heavily concentrated. Allegion Americas produced $3,218.8M of FY2025 revenue (79.1%) and $896.5M of segment operating income at a 27.9% margin, which is 92.1% of total segment operating income. Allegion International produced $848.5M of revenue and $76.5M of operating income at a 9.0% margin. In H1 2026 the Americas share of segment operating income rose to 95.4% ($481.9M of $505.0M). This is a North American non-residential door-hardware franchise with a European business that has not covered its cost of capital.

The moat sits in specification rather than product. In North American non-residential construction, life-safety hardware (fire-rated doors, exit devices, closers) must satisfy building codes, and Allegion's field force writes its brands into architectural specifications 12-18 months before revenue recognition. That functions as a quasi-backlog and a project-level switching barrier. The filings describe the process; they do not quantify spec win rates, so the mechanism is credible but not measurable from outside.

Named competitors per the 10-K: Assa Abloy and dormakaba globally, Fortune Brands Innovations in North American residential, plus private-label programs operated by Allegion's own retail customers. One ownership detail: the "Stanley Access Technologies" trademark is the property of Stanley Logistics L.L.C. and used under license from Stanley Black & Decker. Allegion owns the business, not the brand, and license terms are undisclosed.

The four-year trend is one of steady, unspectacular compounding with consistent margin expansion:

FY2025 revenue growth of 7.8% decomposed as pricing +3.1%, volume +1.0%, acquisitions +3.1%, FX +0.6%. One point of organic volume. Gross margin expanded 100bps but SG&A rose to 24.1% of revenue from 23.5%, so operating margin captured only ~40bps. Free cash flow was $685.7M on capex of $98.1M (2.4% of revenue), a 16.9% FCF margin and a 4.8% yield on the current $14.37B market cap. Operating cash flow exceeded net income by $140.0M, consistent with real cash conversion.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total debt $2,031.1M, cash $320.6M, net debt $1,710.5M. Trailing EBITDA of $1,036.0M (TTM EBIT $893.4M plus TTM D&A $142.6M) puts net leverage at ~1.65x, matching management's stated 1.6x adjusted figure, with interest coverage of ~8.8x. Roughly 88% of borrowings are fixed: 3.550% notes due 2027 ($400M), 3.500% due 2029 ($400M), 5.411% due 2032 ($600M), 5.600% due 2034 ($400M), plus $240.6M drawn on a $1.0B revolver at 4.753%. In December 2025 the revolver was upsized from $750M and extended to May 2030, retiring a term loan that would have matured November 2026. There is no near-term maturity wall in a higher-for-longer rate environment, which distinguishes Allegion from the financing-heavy industrials currently repricing on cost of capital. Pension plans are 103.0% funded in aggregate.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 lands in the "grey zone," but the inputs explain it: $1,925.6M of goodwill plus $816.2M of net intangibles equal 51.1% of the $5,360.8M asset base, depressing both working capital/assets (0.122) and revenue/assets (0.779). The formula is calibrated on tangible-asset manufacturers and reads low for acquisition-built branded businesses. What it legitimately flags is intangible concentration and therefore impairment exposure. It does not indicate solvency stress against 8.8x coverage and $685.7M of annual FCF.

**The cash-flow soft spot.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $299.7M was below H1 2025's $314.2M despite net earnings rising from $307.9M to $322.7M. Accounts receivable rose $122.0M to $559.7M. Non-GAAP available cash flow of $260.8M was down 5.3% year over year, against reaffirmed full-year conversion of 85-95% of adjusted net income. Management attributes this to sales weighted to late in the quarter plus acquired working capital. Both explanations are consistent with the disclosed pre-buy ahead of the end-May price increase. Q3 discriminates between benign timing that unwinds and revenue borrowed from the next quarter. I do not annualize H1 cash flow; the company states Q2/Q3 are seasonally stronger for Northern Hemisphere construction while some businesses peak in Q4.

**Tax drift.** The effective rate has moved from 10.9% in FY2022 to 16.2% in FY2025, with H1 2026 at 19.2% versus 18.0% a year earlier, driven by the OECD 15% minimum tax that Ireland has transposed. Applying the 470bp move from FY2024's 14.5% to FY2025 pretax income of $768.4M implies roughly $36M, or about $0.42 per share of cumulative headwind. Quarterly rates are noisy (Q2 2026's 19.0% was below Q2 2025's 20.3%), but the multi-year direction is upward and partly explains why EPS growth has trailed operating income growth.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of growth is changing in a way that matters more than the headline rate.

Through FY2025 this was price plus M&A: +3.1% price, +1.0% volume, +3.1% acquisitions. Q2 2026 decomposed as pricing +3.3%, volume +3.6%, acquisitions +5.1%, FX +0.7%, for enterprise organic growth of +6.9%, with Americas volume at +4.9%. That is the first evidence of the volume leg engaging, and it is corroborated by qualitative demand signals: CEO Stone described specification activity as "as strong as I've seen since I joined the company," broad-based across institutional verticals with cyclical recovery in office and multifamily, and cited AIA data pointing to commercial acceleration into 2027.

Three structural drivers deserve weight. Data centers are "approaching 5% of our non-res business" and growing rapidly, aided by the Krieger Specialty Products acquisition supplying high-technology doors. Mechanical-to-electronic conversion continues: Americas electronics grew low-double-digits in FY2025 and low-teens in Q2 2026, and Schlage Resident Key was first to market with a credential in Apple Wallet and among the first in Google Wallet. Software and services revenue reached $142.9M in H1 2026 (6.5% of total) from $125.5M, with contract liabilities up 37% in six months to $56.4M. That last figure is deferred service and software revenue, not total backlog, and the company does not define it as such.

Against that, the raise was mix-shifted rather than uniform. Americas organic was lifted to the higher end of mid-single digits; International was cut to a low-single-digit organic decline. International H1 organic volume was -4.2% with segment margin falling to 5.1% from 7.1%, and all its growth is acquired (+15.1%) or FX (+6.8%). Germany, the largest International market, has seen sequential GDP downgrades, and the broader European growth backdrop remains weak. Residential is bifurcated: 70% aftermarket, 30% new build, and management states plainly that "new build is still weak." That 30% has no near-term tailwind.

The estimate-revision picture is positive but not extraordinary: current year $8.95 (+1.8% over 30 days, +2.0% over 90), next year $9.75 (+1.9%/+2.1%). This does not meet the threshold at which revision velocity independently signals systematic consensus underestimation. The delivery record is mixed — two beats and two misses in four quarters, with the misses being the Q4 2025 (-4.2%) and Q1 2026 (-6.9%) prints — so I apply moderate rather than high confidence to the guidance range.

The specific bar for the second half: guidance of $8.85-$9.00 against H1 adjusted EPS of roughly $4.20 (Q1 $1.80 plus Q2 $2.40) implies H2 of $4.65-$4.80 versus $4.24 in H2 2025, or +9.7% to +13.2%, accelerating from H1's +7.7%. Q3 consensus of $2.49 requires +8.3% against a quarter that carried the residential electronics launch. Delivery depends on the $10M restructuring benefit reaching full run-rate in Q4, continued Americas volume, and International's sequential margin recovery holding.

EV of ~$16.08B (market cap $14.37B plus $1.71B net debt) against TTM EBITDA of $1,036.0M gives 15.5x. Against the building-products peer set (MAS 18.3x, LII 22.9x, WMS 24.8x, WTS 29.4x trailing, median 24.8x) Allegion's 22.7x trailing and 18.9x forward look reasonable for a business with superior margins (27.9% Americas segment) and lower leverage than most, but the discount is not dramatic and the peers themselves sit 7-22% below their own 52-week highs.

The growth-adjusted read is less flattering. Guided adjusted EPS growth is 9.6% ($8.925 versus FY2025's $8.14), and the 2021-2025 adjusted EPS CAGR was 10.65%. A 18.9x forward multiple on ~10% earnings growth is a PEG near 1.9. That is fair for a high-quality compounder with a 4.8% FCF yield; it is not a mispricing.

The identified positive catalysts are real but bounded: $380.0M of remaining buyback authorization against a $14.37B market cap (2.6%), with management stating intent to remain active and FY2026 guidance assuming an 85.9M share count above the 85.06M actually outstanding, so actual share count likely lands below the guidance assumption. The IEEPA tariff refund claim is court-affirmed in principle and carries no recorded receivable, but management explicitly excluded it from guidance and said it "would not expect any potential IEEPA refund to have a material impact on EPS." Mexican goods qualifying under USMCA were substantially exempt from the invalidated tariffs in the first place, which would independently explain the immateriality. I do not capitalize this.

The decisive valuation fact is positional. The shares closed at $137.24 on July 20 and $168.94 on August 7. The analyst mean target is $174.63 (+3.4%), the median $170 (+0.6%), the range $160-$200. On stocks trading below 30x forward with rising estimates, consensus targets often lag and get revised upward, but the revisions here are +2%, not +30%, and the multiple has already expanded from ~15.4x to 18.9x guided EPS in three weeks. The re-rating that was available at $137 has largely occurred. My base case of $185 applies 19x to FY2027 consensus $9.75, roughly the multiple the market paid at the February high of $183 on then-current estimates, giving +9.5% plus a 1.3% dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory read is split by geography, and the split is informative.

At the enterprise level Allegion outgrew its principal competitor organically in Q2 2026: +6.9% versus Assa Abloy's group organic +4% (reported July 17, up from 3%, headlined "Accelerated sales growth with record margin"). The Americas franchise is widening its position: 29.0% Q2 segment margin, +4.9% volume, electronics growing low-teens, first-mover credentials in Apple and Google Wallet, and a data-center vertical approaching 5% of non-residential revenue. The specification mechanism, code-mandated life-safety requirements and a fragmented supplier base of "thousands of smaller regional and local companies" give the roll-up strategy a durable runway; eight bolt-ons closed 2024-2026.

International is losing. Segment organic was -1.2% in Q2 and -4.2% in H1 while the named competitor accelerated. Management's explanation (exposure weighted to Southern Europe and Germany, German mechanical product hit by sequential GDP downgrades, Italy and Spain stable, European electronics strong with "great margins") is specific and plausible. An alternative reading is that the Q1 ERP disruption cost service levels and share in Europe and that some of the "market weakness" is self-inflicted and therefore reversible. The Assa Abloy 4% is group-wide, not a verified Europe-specific figure, so the direct regional comparison rests on the analyst's characterization rather than confirmed data.

Two structural exposures deserve mention because they are not sector truisms. Allegion's own big-box retail customers run private-label programs, a channel-conflict risk that scales with residential retail exposure. And as mechanical converts to digital, the credential layer increasingly runs through wallet platforms owned by Apple and Google; Allegion's first-mover position there is an asset today and a dependency over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Mexico manufacturing and tariff pass-through.** Both the 10-K and Q2 10-Q disclose that 20-25% of cost of goods sold originates in Mexico, under 5% from China, 5-10% from other non-US countries, with much of the US residential portfolio built in Baja California under the IMMEX program. The company holds no financial derivatives against steel, zinc, brass or electronic components; pricing cadence is the only hedge, and it operates with a lag by construction. If the Q1 Americas margin compression was price/cost lag on tariff inflation rather than a one-off, it can recur whenever input costs step up faster than semi-annual price actions. The 10-Q notes that following the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling, "new tariffs were imposed under other laws and on imports from more countries," so the net trade-cost position may not have improved.

**International impairment tail.** Accumulated goodwill impairment attributable to International is $573.6M against zero for Americas, so roughly 48% of the goodwill ever allocated to that segment has been written off. Remaining International goodwill is $617.1M, and ELATEC, which by residual accounts for the large majority of $631.6M of FY2025 acquisition consideration, sits in that segment. No impairment was recorded in H1 2026 or the FY2025 annual test, and the acquired assets are currently the better part (acquisitions added 80bps to International margin in Q2). But organic deterioration in the same reporting unit that carries the impairment history is a live risk. ELATEC's standalone price, revenue and margin are not disclosed, so no acquisition multiple can be computed.

**Pull-forward and Q3 comp.** Management disclosed the May price increase drove advance ordering, quantified underlying Americas demand as below the reported 9% segment figure, and flagged Q3 2025 as a difficult residential comparison. The $122.0M receivables build and -5.3% available cash flow corroborate late-quarter revenue. A Q3 miss on this mechanism is the single most likely near-term negative catalyst.

**Non-residential cyclicality.** Specification activity and AIA data point to commercial acceleration into 2027, but non-residential construction starts are rate-sensitive, and the policy rate path remains uncertain. The 30% of residential tied to new build faces a weak new-build backdrop that management describes plainly as "still weak."

**Governance and structure.** Irish domicile carries reduced shareholder protections, a 75% threshold to amend articles, and standing shareholder-approved authority for the board to issue up to 20% of ordinary shares including for cash without pre-emption rights, unused in the period reviewed but a latent dilution authority. Offsetting these: a clean 8-K record back to 2022 with no restatements, no Item 4.02 non-reliance events, no auditor changes and no covenant defaults; PwC as auditor; no material legal proceedings; warranty liability of only $26.1M despite life-safety product exposure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 24.4% against 27.3% one-year realized (spread -2.9%, characterized as fair), at the 18th percentile within the current chain, with a flat term structure (24.4% near versus 22.7% at six months). Implied volatility collapsed 12.1 points from 36.5% on July 20 as the earnings event resolved, and put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.24 to 0.09. The 1-month put/call skew of 5.6% is labeled elevated put demand, easing to 2.7% at three months and inverting at six. The market has discharged the earnings uncertainty, prices no near-term dislocation, and the modest near-dated put demand is consistent with hedging a 23% three-week advance rather than a directional bearish position. Short interest of 5.5% of float at 3.8 days to cover is uncrowded. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental read; equally, nothing signals informed positioning ahead of a catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($207, +22.5%).** The Q2 volume inflection is the leading edge of a non-residential upcycle that specification activity (described as the strongest since the CEO's arrival) and AIA data point toward through 2027. Americas holds mid-to-high single-digit organic volume, electronics compounds at low-teens, the data-center vertical scales from ~5% of non-residential, the $10M restructuring benefit reaches full run-rate in Q4 with H1 2027 carryover, and International stabilizes as ERP recovery completes and German comparisons ease. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $9.75-$10.00, buybacks at the remaining $380.0M authorization take share count below the 85.9M guidance assumption, and the multiple re-rates toward the 21-22x the peer set commands. 21.5x on $9.75 gives $210; 21x on $10.00 gives $210.

**Bear case ($142, -15.9%).** Q3 reveals that June's strength was borrowed from the September quarter, compounding a tough residential electronics comparison into a miss against $2.49 consensus. Tariff cost inflation outruns semi-annual pricing, repeating the Q1 pattern in which 9.7% revenue growth produced no operating income growth. International organic decline deepens under a European recession, triggering a goodwill charge against the $617.1M remaining balance in a segment that has already written off 48% of its allocated goodwill. New-residential volumes fall further. FY2027 EPS lands nearer $9.20 and the multiple compresses to 15.5x: $143.

**Base case ($185, +9.5%).** Americas delivers mid-single-digit organic volume with pricing intact, International declines low-single-digits organically without impairment, FY2026 adjusted EPS lands in the $8.90-$9.00 upper half of guidance, and FY2027 reaches consensus $9.75 on ~5% revenue growth plus restructuring benefit and modest share shrink. The multiple holds near 19x, where the market valued the shares at the February high on then-current estimates. $9.75 × 19x = $185, plus 1.3% in dividends.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear gives an expected price return of about 5.7%, or roughly 7% total return including the dividend. That is a positive but market-like expected return with a 16% left tail, which sits below the ACCUMULATE threshold given that the same business was available at 15.4x guided earnings three weeks ago.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and improving; the recommendation reflects entry price, not thesis quality.

- **Base case target: $185** — 19.0x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $9.75, the multiple the market paid at the February 2026 high on then-prevailing estimates. +9.5% from $168.94.
- **Bull case target: $207** — 21.2x $9.75, a peer-median-adjacent multiple on delivered volume acceleration. +22.5%.
- **Bear case target: $142** — 15.5x a reduced $9.20 FY2027 estimate on pull-forward reversal plus an International charge. -15.9%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October 2026), the Q4/FY2026 print and FY2027 guidance (February 2027), and the annual goodwill impairment test.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price of $145-$152 with guidance intact — 16.3x-17.0x FY2026 guidance midpoint and 14.9x-15.6x FY2027 consensus, restoring 20%+ upside to the base case.
2. Q3 2026 Americas organic volume of +3% or better against the residential electronics comparison, confirming the volume inflection was not pull-forward, with H2 available cash flow conversion returning to the 85-95% range and receivables normalizing from $559.7M.
3. International segment margin above 8% for two consecutive quarters with organic decline narrowing to flat, removing the impairment overhang on $617.1M of goodwill.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Q3 adjusted EPS below $2.35 (a >5% miss against $2.49 consensus) with Americas volume turning negative — the pull-forward scenario confirmed.
2. Americas segment margin compression of 100bps or more year over year in any quarter, indicating tariff cost inflation has outrun pricing cadence with no commodity hedges to buffer.
3. Any International goodwill impairment charge, or FY2027 guidance implying adjusted EPS below $9.40.
4. Full-year available cash flow conversion falling below the reaffirmed 85% floor.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which discriminates between benign quarter-end timing and revenue borrowed from Q3, and provides the first read on whether the $10M restructuring benefit is arriving on the stated schedule.

## Conclusion

Allegion is a high-quality asset at a fair price. The Americas franchise earns 27.9%-29.0% segment margins on code-mandated, specification-driven demand, converts 16.9% of revenue to free cash flow, earns ~19% ROIC and carries 1.65x net leverage with 8.8x interest coverage and no near-term maturity, a balance sheet that is an advantage while the long end stays elevated. Management's capital allocation deserves credit: buying back 909,000 shares at a weighted average $132.06 in Q2, now marked 28% higher, with $380.0M of authorization remaining and stated intent to continue. The Q2 print and raised guidance reverse the growth and competitive-position deterioration that drove this name's score lower through the spring, and I have marked those dimensions up accordingly.

What the file does not support is an urgent purchase at $168.94. The multiple has expanded from roughly 15.4x to 18.9x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS in three weeks, the price sits between the $170 consensus median and $174.63 mean, estimate revisions of +2% over 90 days do not indicate systematic consensus underestimation, and PEG near 1.9 on ~10% earnings growth is fair rather than cheap. Set against that, the CFO's own disclosure of order pull-forward ahead of the May price increase, a $122.0M receivables build, available cash flow down 5.3%, an H2 guidance bar requiring EPS acceleration to +10-13%, and a difficult Q3 residential comparison make the next print a genuine two-sided event. Five consecutive years of TSR underperformance against the company's own peer benchmark (cumulative +46.95% versus +119.77%) is largely multiple compression against an adjusted EPS CAGR of 10.65%, but it is a reminder that this business has historically been re-rated downward as often as upward.

Own it if you own it, collect the 1.30% dividend, and let the October print resolve the pull-forward question. New capital gets a materially better risk/reward at $145-$152.
