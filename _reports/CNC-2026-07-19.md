**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Centene is the largest U.S. managed-care company focused on government-sponsored and underserved populations: #1 in Medicaid, #1 in ACA Marketplace (Ambetter), and #1 in standalone Medicare Part D. FY2025 was a disaster: a $6.7B non-cash goodwill impairment plus a Marketplace risk-adjustment miss drove a GAAP net loss of $(6.67)B, or $(13.53)/share, as the consolidated Health Benefits Ratio (HBR) spiked ~360bp to 91.9%. The stock bottomed near $25 in mid-2025. Q1 2026 then delivered a genuine turnaround: adjusted diluted EPS of $3.37 (vs. $2.90 a year prior, beating management's internal bar by ~$0.50), HBR back to 87.3%, and Medicare Advantage swinging toward profitability with no premium deficiency reserve. Shares have since re-rated roughly 128% to $66.44.

The core analytical question is whether the confirmed margin recovery is now adequately priced and whether its durability survives a hostile 2027-2028 policy backdrop. The bull case is a self-help margin-normalization story layered on a *shrinking* membership base: Ambetter Marketplace collapsed from 5.5M members at YE2025 to 3.6M in Q1 2026 as enhanced APTC subsidies expired, and total at-risk membership fell ~6% YoY. Revenue growth (FY2025 +19% to $194.8B) is largely optical, driven by IRA-inflated Part D premiums, not member expansion. Commercial margin quality improved despite the membership loss (gross margin down only 7% on a 6% revenue decline), which supports the view that shedding adverse-selected Marketplace members is margin-accretive even as the top line shrinks.

We rate CNC a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. At $66.44 the stock trades right at the analyst mean of $65.17, below RBC's Sector Perform $70 target, and the negative trailing EPS makes conventional multiples meaningless. On forward earnings the stock is not obviously mispriced in either direction: ~19x current-year consensus EPS of $3.52 and ~15x next-year $4.48. The setup is two-sided: downside protected by $4B+ free cash flow, comfortable statutory capital, and improving leverage; upside capped by phased-in OBBBA Medicaid funding cuts (2027-2028), unproven 2026 Marketplace repricing adequacy, a securities class action, and an insider who sold $5.2M into the rally with no offsetting open-market buying.

## Company Overview

Centene operates a spread-based managed-care model: it collects fixed per-member-per-month premiums from states and CMS and earns the gap between premiums and medical costs, measured by HBR. Thin margins mean small HBR moves swing earnings dramatically, the structural fragility that produced the 2025 blowout. As of Q1 2026 the company served ~26.3M at-risk members across four segments:

- **Medicaid** (~12.4M members): the revenue anchor at $28.9B Q1 revenue (58% of total), run through state-branded subsidiaries (Superior in TX, Sunshine in FL, Meridian, Buckeye).
- **Medicare** (~1.0M MA + ~8.8M PDP): PDP grew sharply on IRA-driven premium inflation; MA membership was deliberately shrunk via bid discipline.
- **Commercial/Marketplace** (~4.1M): Ambetter, the epicenter of the 2025 crisis.
- **Other**: pharmacy, vision/dental, behavioral (Magellan, being divested).

The company is uniquely concentrated in exactly the two programs facing federal cuts: Medicaid (largest insurer) and ACA Marketplace (largest insurer). That concentration magnified the 2025 damage and magnifies exposure to the 2027-2028 policy phase-in, but it also positions Centene for the 2027-2030 integrated D-SNP mandate steering dual-eligibles into aligned plans.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 — a loss year masking underlying cash generation.** Total revenue reached $194.8B (+19% YoY), premium and service revenue $174.6B (+20%), but the growth was low-quality: PDP premium inflation and Marketplace footprint expansion, not durable member economics. The GAAP net loss was driven by the $6.7B goodwill impairment (Q3 2025) plus a $513M Magellan write-down. Adjusted diluted EPS collapsed to $2.08 from $7.17 in 2024. HBR jumped from 88.3%. Commercial gross margin fell $2.6B (−33%) on the Marketplace risk-adjustment miss; Medicaid gross margin fell $556M on behavioral, home-health, and specialty-drug trend.

Operating cash flow was strong at $5.1B (vs. $154M in 2024, which had been depressed by PBM-transition receivable timing), with FCF near $4.3B. The earnings loss was a non-cash accounting event, not a cash-flow event.

**Q1 2026 — the turnaround.** Adjusted diluted EPS beat the prior-year figure and management's internal expectation by ~$0.50. HBR improved from 87.5%, with Medicaid HBR down 50bp on rate/acuity catch-up and MA swinging toward profitability (no 2026 PDR vs. a $270M reserve built Q1 2025). Operating cash flow was $4.4B for the quarter, though partly flattered by a state premium payment received in April 2026 rather than in-quarter.

The four-quarter earnings-surprise record captures the volatility: +56.4% (Q1'26), +3.7% (Q4'25), +454.9% (Q3'25 off a tiny base), and −118.5% (Q2'25, the crisis quarter). Forward estimates cannot be relied on with high confidence; the beat/miss dispersion reflects the genuine unpredictability of medical-cost trend in a spread-based model.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Altman Z of 2.58 (grey zone) and D/E of 2.84 reflect the impairment-driven equity hit, not a solvency problem. Debt-to-capital improved from 46.5% at YE2025 to 43.2% by Q1 2026 after $1.0B of senior-note repurchases, well under the 60% covenant. Regulated subsidiaries held $19.7B statutory capital vs. $11.3B required, with aggregate RBC >350% of Authorized Control Level. Two caveats matter: $11.3B of subsidiary capital is restricted from upstreaming without regulatory approval, and parent dividends for 2026 are guided to only ~$1.2-1.4B. Capital allocation pivoted hard from buybacks ($3.0B in 2024 → $400M in 2025 → zero open-market in Q1 2026) toward debt reduction; $1.8B remains authorized but paused.

**Cash-conversion tell.** A $3.0B CMS Part D risk-sharing receivable is outstanding, and Centene set up a $4.25B master receivable purchase facility in February 2026, selling a $1.0B participating interest in March for $970M net (a $30M discount cost) to fund note redemption. Strong headline cash generation is partly timing-driven and partly monetized at a discount, a reason to discount the FCF optics.

## Growth Analysis

There is no clean growth story here, which drives the lower Growth Prospects score. Membership is *shrinking* — total at-risk down ~6% YoY, with the Marketplace book cut roughly in half in a single quarter as enhanced APTC subsidies expired 12/31/2025 (Congress did not extend). National ACA enrollment is down ~5M, with KFF projecting drops up to 26%. Headline revenue growth is IRA-driven PDP premium inflation, not organic expansion.

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story that argues against a strong growth read. Current-year EPS consensus rose +17.7% over 90 days (to $3.52) and next-year +9.8% (to $4.48) as 2025 fell out of the frame — positive, but this is recovery normalization rather than a fundamental growth acceleration. The next-quarter estimate was cut −76.9% over 90 days (to $0.05), reflecting the seasonal PDP risk pattern and the earn-in cadence of 2026 repricing. The current-year and next-year revisions do not clear the +30% bar that would flag a strong BUY signal, and even where positive, they reflect a business emerging from a trough on a shrinking base rather than accelerating demand.

The one genuine structural growth lever is the 2027-2030 integrated D-SNP mandate, which steers dual-eligibles into aligned D-SNPs where Centene's overlapping Medicaid/MA footprint is an advantage. That opportunity is real but multi-year and collides directly with the OBBBA Medicaid funding cuts phasing in over the same window.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are useless — negative EPS produces a meaningless −5.1x P/E. The relevant lens is forward earnings on the recovering estimate base, at ~19x current-year consensus and ~15x next-year.

Against Managed Health Care peers (median P/E 33.4x — HUM 39.4x, MOH 53.7x, UNH 30.6x, ELV 17.0x), Centene screens cheap on a forward basis, but the discount is warranted rather than a mispricing to exploit. ELV, the most comparable diversified peer, trades at 17.0x trailing; CNC at ~15x next-year is in the same neighborhood once you adjust for its heavier government-program concentration and the 2027-2028 policy overhang that ELV shares only partially. The lower multiple reflects genuine incremental risk: unproven 2026 Marketplace repricing adequacy, OBBBA Medicaid cuts, PDP full-risk exposure with the CMS risk corridors removed for 2026, and a securities-litigation tail.

The cheapness here is a warning flag rather than a buy trigger. Cheap-screening managed-care names have repeatedly been value traps when medical-cost trend or policy shifts against the concentrated exposures — the dynamic that took CNC from the mid-$60s to $25 in 2025. The re-rating from the trough already captured the "2025 in the rearview" repricing; there is no fresh, independent catalyst to argue the market is now materially underpricing the stock. Shares sit near the analyst mean of $65.17, below the median $68.5 and RBC's $70 target. When a recovering-but-not-growing name trades at consensus with a full analyst distribution ($40 low, $80 high), the target reflects a balanced view rather than a floor, and the wide low-end target embeds real downside recognition. We score valuation at 4.5: not stretched, but not offering asymmetric upside, with the low multiple reflecting structural risk rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The entire managed-care sector is absorbing an elevated medical-cost-trend cycle (behavioral health, home health, high-cost specialty and GLP-1-class drugs) plus a hostile policy backdrop (OBBBA Medicaid cuts, APTC expiration, tighter MA rate notices — Centene views the CMS draft 2027 rate as insufficient). This is largely sector-wide and therefore partly priced across the group.

Centene's competitive position is stable-to-mildly-improving on trajectory, which supports a middling score. On the positive side: the local, state-branded subsidiary model is a genuine advantage in Medicaid reprocurements; the overlapping Medicaid/MA footprint is well-positioned for the integrated D-SNP mandate; and shedding 1.9M adverse-selected Marketplace members while aggressively repricing improved margin quality even as revenue fell. On the negative side: the company was not selected in Florida (contract ends 9/2026), is protesting Texas and Georgia losses, saw its Arizona ALTCS award cancelled and rebid to 2027, and New York is terminating Essential Plan-5 by 7/2026. Star ratings are improving (~60% of MA members in ≥3.5-star plans for 2026 vs. 55%) but only ~20% in 4-star, still lagging peers and capping quality-bonus revenue.

Relative to UNH and ELV, Centene is less diversified and more exposed to the two programs being cut. Molina and Oscar share its government concentration. The reprocurement losses and Star-rating lag prevent a stronger read.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Marketplace / APTC cliff (dominant near-term risk).** The remaining risk pool is higher-morbidity (healthy members drop first), pressuring HBR. Centene repriced 2026 in states covering 95% of Marketplace membership, but adequacy is unproven and will not be fully visible until later 2026 quarters.

2. **Medicaid margin/acuity mismatch and funding cuts.** Post-redetermination populations carry higher acuity than lagged rates reflect. OBBBA adds work requirements and more frequent redeterminations (raising morbidity from 2027) and cuts provider taxes/state-directed payments from 2028 — a multi-year federal funding headwind hitting the largest segment.

3. **PDP / IRA risk transfer.** The IRA shifted catastrophic Part D cost onto plans (federal reinsurance cut 80%→20%), and CMS removed the narrowed risk corridors for 2026 just as PDP membership swelled to 8.8M. The outstanding $3.0B CMS receivable is both a liquidity and a collection risk.

4. **Litigation / guidance-credibility overhang.** The *Lunstrum v. Centene* securities class action (SDNY, filed 7/9/2025) plus five derivative suits allege false statements about 2025 guidance tied to the mid-2025 withdrawal. Accruals are "not material" but the outcome is uncertain, and management credibility remains impaired.

5. **Reprocurement losses.** Florida, Texas, Georgia, Arizona, and New York contract actions collectively pressure the Medicaid growth base.

Netting these against what is already priced, the risk profile carries multiple live, partly-unpriced binary and phased catalysts (2026 repricing earn-in, 2027-2028 OBBBA phase-in, litigation resolution, contract rebids), justifying a below-neutral risk score of 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly complacent and does not challenge the thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 45.6% against 1-year historical vol of 50.8% — cheap on a −5.3% spread, at the 3rd percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is essentially balanced (1.6%), and the term structure is in normal contango (near 45.6% < long 50.0%). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±5.1%. Further-dated tenors show meaningfully more put demand and negative skew (6-month P/C OI ratio 5.75, skew −3.8%), consistent with hedging against the 2027-2028 policy phase-in rather than near-term earnings fear. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.33 to 0.53 since mid-June as the stock ticked up, suggesting some incremental downside hedging into strength. Cheap near-term vol offers no directional edge but means protection is inexpensive for holders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80).** 2026 Marketplace repricing proves adequate, HBR continues normalizing toward the mid-80s, MA sustains profitability, and Medicaid rate catch-up holds. Adjusted EPS recovers toward $4.50-5.00 as the base stabilizes, and the D-SNP mandate provides a 2027+ growth kicker. $4B+ FCF resumes buybacks once leverage targets are met. At ~17x mid-cycle EPS power of ~$4.75, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range near $80.

**Bear Case ($40).** OBBBA Medicaid cuts (2027-2028) bite harder than modeled, the higher-morbidity residual Marketplace pool breaks 2026 repricing assumptions, PDP full-risk exposure produces another cost surprise with corridors removed, and the securities litigation resolves adversely. HBR re-elevates toward 90%+, adjusted EPS stalls near $3, and the multiple compresses back to the low-teens on renewed guidance-credibility doubt, taking the stock to the analyst low near $40 — roughly 40% downside from current levels.

**Base Case ($68).** The margin recovery holds through 2026 but growth stays absent on the shrinking base, policy headwinds cap upside into 2027-2028, and the stock trades broadly sideways-to-modestly-higher around the analyst median. ~15-16x next-year EPS supports roughly $68-72.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The margin turnaround is confirmed and the balance sheet is sound, but the stock has already re-rated ~128% off its low to sit at consensus, and the setup is two-sided with unresolved policy and litigation tails. This is the fourth consecutive non-BUY read (HOLD → AVOID → HOLD → HOLD); the trajectory has stabilized around 5.0, and current data confirms rather than reverses that neutral stance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of HBR below 87% with confirmation that 2026 Marketplace repricing is earning in as priced (adequacy proven), alongside a resumption of buybacks.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores forward P/E below ~12x on next-year estimates, widening the margin of safety against the 2027-2028 policy phase-in.
- Clarity that OBBBA Medicaid cuts and CMS 2027 rate notices are less severe than the draft framework implies.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- HBR re-elevating toward 90%+ in any 2026 quarter, signaling repricing inadequacy.
- An adverse development in the *Lunstrum* securities litigation or a material accrual increase.
- A guidance cut or withdrawal reprising the 2025 credibility damage, or a further material Medicaid reprocurement loss.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release, with particular attention to full-year 2026 guidance figures (not captured from a primary filing here and to be verified), the HBR trajectory, and any update on the CMS Part D receivable collection.

## Conclusion

Centene has moved 2025's impairment and guidance crisis into the rearview: Q1 2026 confirmed a real margin turnaround, the balance sheet is de-levering, statutory capital is comfortable, and free cash flow exceeds $4B. But the investment case is a self-help margin-recovery story on a shrinking membership base, not a growth story, and the stock has already captured the recovery repricing in its ~128% run off the low. At $66.44 it trades near the analyst mean with balanced options positioning, a forward multiple that is cheap versus peers only because the government-program concentration warrants a discount, and a live overhang of OBBBA Medicaid cuts (2027-2028), unproven Marketplace repricing adequacy, and securities litigation. A director sold $5.2M into the rally with no offsetting open-market buying, which does not signal deep undervaluation at these levels. The risk/reward has narrowed to roughly symmetric: ~20% upside to the bull case, ~40% downside to the bear case, and a base case near current levels. We maintain HOLD and would want either a lower entry (low-$50s) or fresh evidence that 2026 repricing is proven adequate before turning constructive.
