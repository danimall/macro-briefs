## Executive Summary

Spotify's business has changed character over four years in a way the income statement now shows clearly. Gross margin has risen from 24.95% (FY2022) to 33.4% in Q2 2026, trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is €3,267M on €18,113M of revenue (18.0% FCF margin), capital expenditure runs at 0.35% of revenue, and funded financial debt has been eliminated — the €404M of remaining "total debt" at June 30, 2026 consists entirely of lease liabilities against €9,388M of cash and short-term investments. On the metrics that matter for solvency and reinvestment capacity, this is one of the cleaner balance sheets among large-cap platform companies, and Spotify is not participating in the capex-heavy AI infrastructure cycle that is compressing free cash flow at hyperscale peers. It rents inference capacity as operating expense rather than building it.

The complication is what sits underneath the reported operating leverage. Q2 2026 operating income of €655M was up 61% year over year, and that number anchored the "record profitability" framing. Spotify disclosed that Social Charges — payroll taxes that scale with the intrinsic value of share-based awards, and therefore with the share price — were €1M in the quarter versus €115M a year earlier. Stripping that line from both periods: operating income grew 25.7% rather than 61%, operating margin expanded 127bp rather than 400bp, and operating expenses grew 17.8% rather than the reported 3%. Underlying cost growth (~18%) is running ahead of revenue growth (~14%), with gross margin doing the work. Management's own Q3 guidance corroborates that Q2 was a high-water mark: gross margin guided to 32.9% from 33.4%, operating margin to 13.4% from 13.7%, despite a ~200bp FX tailwind. The mechanism is reflexive: a meaningful re-rating of the shares reintroduces the payroll-tax accrual and compresses reported margins on the way up.

The second complication is growth composition. Ad-supported revenue has gone €1,798M (FY2024) → €1,795M (FY2025) → €1,779M (TTM June 2026) while ad-supported MAUs grew to 494M, with management explicitly citing pricing softness. Monetization is roughly €0.30 per ad-supported user per month against €4.89 Premium ARPU. Management is now treating the free tier as an acquisition funnel — deliberately adding ad-load friction and free-tier limits in India and Indonesia — which is defensible but converts the equity into a near-pure subscription-pricing story. Consensus is adjusting: FY2026 EPS estimates are down 4.1% over 90 days and FY2027 down 2.0% over 30 days. At 23.6x EV/TTM FCF and ~27x FY2027 consensus EPS, with a probability-weighted 12-month return near +10% from the authoritative close (and near +5% from the extended-hours print), the risk/reward is approximately fair. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Spotify is a Luxembourg-domiciled foreign private issuer that files Form 20-F and Form 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K. This has a direct research consequence disclosed up front: the FY2025 20-F narrative — MD&A, Item 3.D risk factors, financial statement notes, legal proceedings — could not be retrieved, and no DEF 14A exists because US proxy rules do not apply. Financial figures below are drawn from SEC-derived structured data cross-checked against the company's Q2 2026 shareholder deck and earnings call as relayed by trade press. Insider Form 4 filings did retrieve successfully and are the only primary-source dataset available. Statements that would normally be filing facts should be treated one confidence tier lower throughout.

Two reported revenue streams:

- **Premium:** €16,334M TTM (90.2% of revenue). 300M Premium Subscribers at June 30, 2026, up 7M sequentially against 6M guided. Monthly Premium ARPU €4.89, +7.4% year over year at constant currency, driven by price increases partially offset by product and market mix.
- **Ad-Supported:** €1,779M TTM (~9.8%). Q2 revenue €446M, +3% constant currency, +1% reported.

Total MAU 777M, +12% year over year, comprising 494M ad-supported users plus Premium users. These series are not additive (Family/Duo members are counted as subscribers; MAU counts unique active users), so the 494M + 300M sum should not be reconciled to 777M.

Premium subscriber geography is unusually diversified for a US-listed platform: Europe 36%, North America 25%, Latin America 24%, Rest of World 15%. That reduces single-market regulatory and macro concentration while weighting the base toward lower-ARPU regions and increasing FX translation sensitivity.

Product direction is toward formats carrying lower content cost than music streams: Audiobooks+ passed $100M annual recurring revenue; owned-and-licensed podcasting drove Q2 sponsorship growth; "Reserved," a ticketing feature launched in the US on June 23, 2026 where **Live Nation/Ticketmaster supply inventory and allocations while Spotify supplies fan identification and distribution**, reserved nearly 100,000 tickets in its first quarter; AI Remix is in pre-launch with UMG and Merlin as **licensors** granting opt-in rights. On AI supply, co-CEO Söderström described Spotify as "vendor agnostic," using models from Anthropic and others — Spotify is a **licensee of third-party foundation models**, which locates the cost as variable purchased compute and the differentiating asset as its own products and behavioral data from 777M users.

Leadership changed in January 2026: founder Daniel Ek moved to Executive Chairman; Alex Norström (business/monetization) and Gustav Söderström (product/technology) became co-CEOs. Q2 2026 was their third reporting quarter.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and the deceleration question

| €M | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 11,727 | 13,247 | 15,673 | 17,186 | 18,113 |
| Growth | +21.3% | +13.0% | +18.3% | +9.7% | +9.0% |
| Premium | 10,251 | 11,566 | 13,875 | 15,391 | 16,334 |
| Ad-Supported | 1,476 | 1,681 | 1,798 | 1,795 | 1,779 |

Reported growth roughly halved between FY2024 and FY2025. Currency explains a material part: Q2 2026 was +14% reported against +15% constant currency, and Q3 carries a ~200bp FX tailwind at quarter-end rates. Underlying growth is therefore mid-teens, not high single digits. It is also not the low-20s of 2021–22, and the composition has narrowed to one lever — subscription price and mix — because the second lever has stopped working. Ad revenue has declined nominally across eighteen months while the ad-supported user base grew, with the company attributing the shortfall to "growth in impressions sold, partially offset by softness in pricing." Whether that is cyclical digital-audio weakness or structural is unresolved; comparative sector ad-spend data was not obtained.

### Margins: real expansion, flattered reporting

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 | Q2'26 | Q3'26 guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 25.64% | 30.14% | 31.98% | 32.80% | 33.4% | 32.9% |
| Operating margin | -3.37% | 8.71% | 12.79% | 14.65% | 13.7% | ~13.4% |

Gross profit grew from €2,926M (FY2022) to €5,941M (TTM) on renegotiated label economics, price increases flowing against fixed-rate royalty structures, and mix shift into audiobooks and owned podcasting. That improvement is the central fundamental achievement and is not in dispute.

The Social Charges adjustment is, in my view, the single most important analytical point in the quarter, and it reproduces cleanly:

- Q2 2026: revenue €4,777M, gross margin 33.4% → gross profit €1,596M; opex €941M → operating income €655M.
- Q2 2025 (back-solved from +61% operating income and +14% revenue): operating income €407M, revenue €4,190M, gross margin 31.5% → gross profit €1,320M, implied opex €913M (consistent with the reported +3% opex growth).
- Excluding Social Charges (€1M vs €115M): opex €940M vs €798M = **+17.8%**; operating income €656M vs €522M = **+25.7%**; operating margin 13.73% vs 12.46% = **+127bp**, against +400bp reported.

Roughly 46% of the €248M year-over-year increase in reported operating income came from the €114M payroll-tax swing. Social Charges are genuine cash payroll taxes, so excluding them is a normalization rather than a claim the costs are fictional, and a defender of the reported number would argue that if the share price stays depressed the low accrual persists. The asymmetry runs the other way: at Q2 2025 levels the charge was 2.7% of quarterly revenue, so a sustained 30%+ recovery in the shares plausibly reintroduces €100M+ per quarter, or roughly 200bp of operating margin. Investors buying the "record operating leverage" framing should expect part of it to reverse if the equity thesis works.

Management is not disguising this. Q3 guidance takes gross margin down 50bp sequentially and operating margin down ~30bp despite the FX tailwind, with 2026 framed as a year to "reinvest to support our long-term potential."

### Earnings quality: use cash, not net income

FY2025: operating income €2,198M, pretax €2,224M, net income €2,212M — implying **€12M of tax expense, a ~0.5% effective rate**, with net income exceeding operating income. TTM: pretax €3,505M against operating income €2,653M implies **€852M of net non-operating income** (plausibly interest on the €9.4B cash pile plus investment and derivative revaluations), and tax of €166M — a ~4.7% rate.

The consequence is that reported EPS and P/E are close to uninformative here. The supplied trailing/forward P/E of 26.85x reconciles to TTM net income per share of roughly $18.9–19.0 in USD, which embeds both the non-operating income and the sub-5% tax rate. Meanwhile the consensus series is denominated in euros (confirmed by the Q4 2025 print of €4.43, which equals the widely reported $5.16 at 1.164), giving FY2026 of €12.36 and FY2027 of €15.57 — below trailing EPS, consistent with consensus not extrapolating the tax benefit. The low rate may persist for some years given Luxembourg domicile and loss carryforwards (retained earnings only turned positive in the TTM period, −€832M to +€421M), but the deferred-tax note could not be read. A normalization to even a 15% effective rate would cut FY2027 EPS by roughly 11%.

The earnings-surprise pattern is consistent with this noise rather than with improving operating visibility: +62.8% (Q3'25), +58.6% (Q4'25), +14.3% (Q1'26), then −6.6% (Q2'26). Two enormous beats followed by a miss is what tax and non-operating items look like when they normalize.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

| €M | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 680 | 2,301 | 2,933 | 3,337 |
| Capex | (6) | (17) | (61) | (70) |
| Free cash flow | 674 | 2,284 | 2,872 | 3,267 |
| FCF margin | 5.1% | 14.6% | 16.7% | 18.0% |

Q2 FCF was €797M, a second-quarter record. Do not annualize it: 4× gives €3.19B, below the actual TTM of €3.27B, because Q4 is seasonally strongest (Wrapped drove record MAU adds of +38M in Q4 2025) and royalty and working-capital timing move quarterly. FCF converts at 1.23x operating income; the €684M gap between operating cash flow and operating income comprises share-based compensation add-back, D&A and favorable subscription working capital (unearned revenue €778M, which is prepaid subscription periods and should not be described as backlog). SBC could not be quantified without the cash-flow notes — a real gap, since it is an economic cost to shareholders that FCF adds back. If SBC runs €350–450M annually, FCF net of it is closer to €2.8–2.9B, which moves EV/FCF from 23.6x to roughly 26–27x.

Balance sheet at June 30, 2026: cash and short-term investments €9,388M; total debt €404M, entirely lease liabilities; net cash €8,984M (≈$10.46B, ~10.4% of market capitalization, ~$51/share on reported shares); current ratio 2.11x. The €1,458M of current-portion debt sitting at December 31, 2025 is absent six months later. How it was retired is inferred, not confirmed: additional paid-in capital rose only €128M, implied share count was roughly flat at ~211M, total liabilities fell €1,323M and long-term investments fell €1,063M — the signature of extinguishing a liability with assets. A partial share settlement masked by €547M of concurrent buybacks cannot be excluded, and the investment decline may partly reflect revaluation given AOCI fell €778M in the same period. Treat "settled in cash" as probable, not established.

Reconstructing a solvency screen from these inputs gives an Altman Z near 12.6, but ~80% of that comes from the market-equity-to-liabilities term, so it largely restates the market's own valuation. The simpler statement is sufficient: €9.0B net cash, €3.3B TTM FCF, €404M of lease-only debt, no maturity wall, no covenant exposure.

## Growth Analysis

Subscriber growth remains healthy in absolute terms: +7M Premium in Q2 against 6M guided, 300M total, with Q3 guided to 305M. ARPU is rising 7.4% constant currency on price. Q3 revenue is guided to €5.0B, ~4.7% sequential growth. Audiobooks+ passing $100M ARR and Reserved's ~100,000 first-quarter tickets are directionally supportive but immaterial against €18B of revenue — they matter as margin-mix evidence, not as revenue drivers.

Three items constrain the growth score.

**First, MAU guidance missed and management is deliberately degrading the funnel in two large markets.** Q2 MAU came in at 777M versus 778M guided (+16M versus +17M), which was the proximate cause of the post-print decline. Norström then disclosed adding "friction in both ad load and some limitations in our free tier" in India and Indonesia, tightening sign-up for "higher quality MAU throughput," and deprecating low-end Android support — stating this will affect Q3 MAUs but should not cost subscriber growth. The bet is that the Latin America conversion playbook transfers. If conversion does not follow, Spotify will have permanently narrowed top-of-funnel in its two largest population markets.

**Second, estimate revisions are negative across every period.** Current quarter −3.0% (30d), next quarter −3.7% (30d), current year −2.9% (30d) and −4.1% (90d), next year −2.0% (30d). The magnitude is modest, but the direction is uniform and it is the opposite of the inflection signature that would justify paying up. No period shows positive revision momentum.

**Third, the margin path to management's 2030 targets requires a change in the cost trajectory.** At the May 21, 2026 Investor Day, Norström targeted gross margin of 35–40% and operating margin above 20%. Against Q2 actuals of 33.4% and 13.7%, that requires roughly 650bp+ of operating margin expansion — approximately a doubling — at a moment when underlying opex is growing ~18% against revenue at ~14% and Q3 guidance points both margin lines down sequentially. The gross margin lever (mix into audiobooks, podcasting, owned formats) is credible and demonstrated. The opex lever is not yet evidenced. Söderström's framing that opex growth is "coming from compute and marketing... both of these are variable and entirely in our control" is the right structural answer; the quarterly evidence has not yet shown the control being exercised.

A reasonable central expectation: constant-currency revenue growth of 11–14% for the next several years, gross margin drifting toward 34–35%, and operating margin expanding 50–150bp per year rather than the pace implied by the 2030 target.

## Competitive Landscape

Spotify's advantage is scale in a category where scale is genuinely scarce: 777M MAU, 300M paying subscribers, and — by its own estimate relayed through media rather than audited — roughly one-third of global recorded-music revenue. Söderström's point that the company has "777 million people and music fans to do reinforcement learning with" is the honest articulation of the moat: proprietary behavioral data plus distribution, applied to models rented from third parties.

The structural weakness is that suppliers are concentrated and also supply competitors. Spotify's entire gross margin is a residual after royalties to counterparties who license Apple Music, Amazon Music and YouTube Music on comparable terms, and those three are features inside vastly larger enterprises that can bundle and subsidize. The Netflix comparison is instructive on this specific mechanism: Netflix runs a ~32% operating margin because it owns much of its content; Spotify runs ~14% because it licenses all of its music. That gap is why the ">20% by 2030" target is ambitious rather than a catch-up exercise. A claim circulating in bearish commentary that 72% of streams are controlled by four entities could not be corroborated against any filing and should be treated as unverified; the structural asymmetry does not depend on that specific number.

The strategic response — owned and lower-cost formats where Spotify captures more of the margin — is working at the gross-margin line. Audiobooks+ at $100M ARR, owned-and-licensed podcasting, and experiences like Reserved carry lower content cost than music streams, and the record 33.4% gross margin was attributed partly to that mix. One discipline point: mix shift establishes margin improvement, it does not establish improved negotiating leverage. Music remains the overwhelming driver of the 300M-subscriber base, and no data on royalty concentration by counterparty, minimum-guarantee commitments, or licence renewal dates was obtainable. Those are the inputs that determine gross-margin durability, and I have none of them.

On the ad side, the trajectory is deteriorating: nominal revenue decline over eighteen months against a growing user base, with pricing softness acknowledged. Whatever share Spotify holds in digital audio advertising, it is not converting user growth into revenue growth there.

## Risk Assessment

**Reflexive Social Charges (underappreciated).** A share-price recovery mechanically raises the payroll-tax accrual and compresses reported operating income by potentially ~200bp of margin. This is a mechanical feature of accounting for stock-linked payroll taxes, not an inference about management conduct, and it partially self-limits the reported-margin narrative in exactly the scenario bulls are underwriting.

**Rights-holder bargaining power with zero visibility.** Gross margin is the whole equity story and it is a residual after royalties to concentrated counterparties who also supply competitors and have their own AI agendas. Spotify's ~$10B of 2024 royalty payments and its scale are leverage, but they also make it the primary target in every renegotiation. Contract durations, minimum guarantees and renewal dates are unknown.

**Earnings-quality normalization.** A move from a ~5% effective tax rate toward the mid-teens would cut EPS by roughly 11%, and non-operating income of €852M TTM is partly interest income that falls if the cash pile is deployed or rates decline. Investors anchoring on the 26.9x FY2027 P/E should recognize that the denominator is a low-tax, non-operating-inflated figure.

**Emerging-market funnel degradation.** Deliberate ad-load friction and free-tier limits in India and Indonesia trade volume for quality. Management expects it to hit Q3 MAUs without costing subscribers; if conversion underdelivers, the addressable funnel in two of the world's largest markets has been permanently narrowed.

**Growth is price-dependent.** ARPU +7.4% cc on price increases against a stalled ad business gives one lever with finite runway. The April Q1 reaction (−14% intraday on soft guidance) shows the market already probing it.

**AI Remix launch execution.** UMG and Merlin have licensed the tool, but Söderström stated Spotify does not need every major label at launch and "we don't expect to have all artists," and analyst questioning on the call focused on why more artists were not consenting. A flagship product launching with an incomplete catalog carries commercial and reputational risk with the counterparties Spotify depends on.

**Disclosure asymmetry (FPI status).** No 10-Q, no DEF 14A, semi-annual narrative disclosure, no US proxy governance detail. Founder voting control via the beneficiary-certificate structure could not be verified — a material gap for a company that just transferred operating control from its founder. Executive compensation, performance metrics and related-party transactions are entirely unknown. Share-based compensation expense is unquantified.

**FX.** With 25% of subscribers in North America and euro reporting, ~200bp swings in reported growth are routine.

**Offsets that are genuinely priced favorably:** no funded debt, no maturity wall, no covenant exposure, 2.11x current ratio, €9.0B net cash, and negligible capital intensity in a cycle where peers are levering up for AI infrastructure. There is no solvency or refinancing tail here.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 47.5% sits 4.5 points above 43.0% one-year realized and in the 85th percentile within the current chain, with a flat term structure (near 47.5% ≈ far 46.0%): the market is pricing elevated but evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a dated binary. Put/call volume 0.95 and open-interest 0.82 are balanced; near-term skew is described as balanced at 2.9%. Longer tenors carry larger implied moves (10.2% at 38 days, 20.7% at 129 days). One unusual print stands out: 1,554 contracts of the August 21 $460 put against 318 open interest (4.9x), 10.1% out of the money, a single dated hedge rather than a pattern. Short interest is 4.2% of float with 3.9 days to cover, so the bearish view is uncrowded and there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

Practical takeaways: rich near-term IV makes long-option expression of a bullish view expensive relative to realized volatility, which argues for equity rather than calls if one is adding. The flat structure gives no evidence of informed positioning around a specific near-term catalyst. Nothing in the options cross-section contradicts the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case (→ $700, +43.4%)

Spotify is a scaled, asset-light subscription business converting 18% of revenue to free cash flow with essentially no capital intensity and 10.4% of market capitalization in net cash. Gross margin has expanded ~840bp in four years through mix shift into audiobooks, owned podcasting and experiences, and management targets 35–40% by 2030 — a path with demonstrated mechanics. Subscriber additions beat guidance (300M, +7M vs 6M guided) with ARPU up 7.4% constant currency, so the pricing power that underwrites the model is intact. The company is renting AI capacity as variable opex rather than building it as capex, so it participates in AI-driven product improvement without the free-cash-flow compression hitting capital-intensive peers, and Söderström's characterization of compute and marketing as controllable variable costs implies the opex line can be dialed back if growth requires defending margins. Funded debt has been eliminated. If constant-currency growth holds at 12–14% and operating margin expands toward 17–18% by FY2028, FY2027 free cash flow near €4.1B at 28x EV/FCF supports roughly $700. The 35% drawdown from the high has already repriced the growth rate; the profitability has not been repriced downward at all.

### Bear case (→ $390, −20.1%)

The reported operating-leverage inflection is substantially an artifact. Excluding Social Charges, Q2 operating income grew 25.7% rather than 61%, margin expanded 127bp rather than 400bp, and operating expenses grew 17.8% against revenue at 14% — underlying cost deleverage offset by gross margin gains that management is now guiding lower (32.9% in Q3 versus 33.4% in Q2, with operating margin also down sequentially despite an FX tailwind). One of the two engines in the standard bull case is gone: ad revenue has declined nominally for eighteen months while ad users grew, monetizing at ~6% of a Premium subscriber, so the equity is a near-pure subscription-pricing story with a finite runway. Consensus is cutting estimates in every period. Reported EPS is inflated by €852M of non-operating income and a sub-5% tax rate that will normalize. Buybacks at $662M over seven months against a $100B capitalization are an anti-dilution program, not per-share accretion — share count rose slightly over the half despite them — leaving $10.5B of net cash under-deployed. The 2030 target of >20% operating margin requires approximately doubling margin over four years while reinvesting, at a moment when the cost base is growing faster than revenue. If revenue growth slips toward high single digits constant currency and the tax rate normalizes, both the numerator and the multiple compress.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners have a positive but unexciting 12-month expectation; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $488.14 and materially less at the $511.82 extended-hours level.

- **Base case target: $565 (+15.7% from $488.14).** ~€3.8B FY2027 FCF ($4.42B at 1.164) × 24x EV/FCF + $10.5B net cash ÷ 205.6M shares. Equivalent to 31.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of €15.57.
- **Bull case target: $700 (+43.4%).** €4.1B FY2027 FCF at 28x plus net cash.
- **Bear case target: $390 (−20.1%).** €3.3B FY2027 FCF at 18x plus net cash, with tax normalization.

## Conclusion

Spotify has done the hard part. It converted a structurally low-margin licensing distributor into a business generating €3.3B of annual free cash flow on €70M of capital expenditure, eliminated funded debt, accumulated €9.0B of net cash, and expanded gross margin by roughly 840 basis points in four years. Those are durable achievements, and the 35% drawdown from the 52-week high has repriced the growth rate without repricing that cash generation.

What keeps this a HOLD is the composition of the most recent improvement and the direction of consensus. Nearly half of the reported operating-income growth in Q2 came from a payroll tax that moves inversely with the share price and will partially reverse if the equity works; underlying operating expenses are growing 18% against 14% revenue growth; the ad-supported segment has been flat-to-down for eighteen months while its user base grew; management has guided both margin lines down sequentially; reported EPS is flattered by a sub-5% tax rate and €852M of non-operating income; and every consensus period has been revised lower over the past 30 and 90 days. At 23.6x EV/trailing free cash flow and ~27x FY2027 consensus earnings, the shares offer roughly 16% upside to a defensible base case against 20% downside, producing a probability-weighted return near 10% from the authoritative close and near 5% from the extended-hours print — approximately fair for a business with 43% realized volatility and twelve unresolved verification gaps, including no primary filing narrative.

Own it if you own it. New capital should wait for either ex-charge operating expenses to grow slower than revenue for two quarters, evidence that the emerging-market funnel trade converts, or a price in the $420–440 range.
