**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DaVita is the dominant U.S. outpatient dialysis provider, holding roughly 36% market share in a duopoly with Fresenius. The investment case is not a conventional growth story. U.S. treatment volume is flat-to-declining (down 1.1% in 2025, guided roughly flat for 2026 as secular end-stage kidney disease growth has stalled), and the bulk of revenue growth comes from rate increases plus acquisition-led international expansion. What drives the equity is financial engineering: stable cash generation (FY25 free cash flow ~$1.0B) combined with an aggressive buyback that reduced the share count ~14.9% in a single year, producing double-digit EPS growth on a flat-volume base. The stock has risen approximately 70% year-to-date in 2026 to all-time highs following a Q1 beat-and-raise.

The Q1 2026 print was strong on the surface (diluted EPS $2.87, +44% YoY, against ~$2.34 consensus), but the magnitude overstates underlying momentum. The comparison quarter was depressed by the April 2025 cyber incident that disrupted billing and collections; revenue per treatment is temporarily inflated by phosphate-binder TDAPA reimbursement that faces a cliff at the end of 2026; and the share-count denominator continues to shrink. Stripping these out, the operating story is stable cash cow plus cost discipline rather than acceleration. Consensus estimates are rising modestly (FY26 EPS revised +4.8% over 90 days to $14.80; FY27 +6.2% over 90 days to $17.18), which supports the EPS-compounding mechanism but falls well short of the +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine fundamental acceleration.

We rate DVA a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The buyback machine remains intact and consensus is drifting higher, which justifies holding through the next several quarters rather than exiting. The reasons not to upgrade: the entire profit pool rests on the ~11-12% of patients on commercial plans, and that pool faces simultaneous, identifiable 2026 headwinds (expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits, OBBBA Medicaid changes, and ongoing legal attacks on charitable premium assistance). Insiders sold heavily into the rally with zero open-market purchases, and the balance sheet runs on engineered negative equity with ~$10.5B of debt. The asymmetry after a 70% run favors patience.

## Company Overview

DaVita operates 2,666 U.S. outpatient dialysis centers serving roughly 200,500 patients as of Q1 2026, contracts with about 740 hospitals for inpatient dialysis, and runs 596 international centers across 14 countries. Business mix in FY2025 was approximately 86% U.S. dialysis and 14% ancillary (Integrated Kidney Care, international, transplant software, clinical research, and venture investments). Within U.S. dialysis, roughly 76% of treatments are outpatient, 18% home-based, and 6% hospital inpatient.

The economic engine is payer mix rather than volume. Approximately 89% of U.S. patients are on government programs (Medicare/Medicare Advantage ~57%, Medicaid ~7%, other government ~3%), but those patients generate only about 68% of revenue. The 11-12% of patients on commercial plans generate roughly 32% of revenue and, by management's own description, "nearly all of the profit." Commercial reimbursement runs roughly 3x Medicare rates. This concentration is the single most important variable in the investment case: small shifts in the commercial share of the patient base are highly leveraged to earnings.

The moat is mature: a scale-based duopoly, dense clinic networks, entrenched relationships with nephrologist medical directors, and regulatory complexity that deters new entrants. The competitive position is stable. The growth question is what management does with the cash, and the answer has been to shrink the equity.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue progressed from $11.61B (2022) to $12.14B (2023) to $12.82B (2024) to $13.64B (2025, +6.5%). That growth splits between roughly 3.5% U.S. dialysis (driven by revenue per treatment, not volume) and +27.3% ancillary, much of it acquisition-driven international expansion (+76 international centers in 2025). U.S. treatments fell to 28.7M in 2025 on elevated mortality and a severe flu season. Revenue per treatment rose to $409.56 (2025) and $417.59 (Q1 2026).

Operating margin sits around 15.7%. GAAP operating income dipped slightly in 2025 ($2,044M vs $2,090M) on the cyber incident and a 2024 one-time ownership gain, while adjusted operating income rose 5.7% to $2,094M. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,887M (down 6.7%) and free cash flow $1,024M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $321M (vs $180M) and FCF $140M (vs negative $45M in Q1 2025, when collections were disrupted by the cyber incident and the company repaid Change Healthcare emergency funding). The Q1 2026 cash-flow recovery is a return to baseline, not a step-change.

The balance sheet is the defining feature. Long-term debt is approximately $10.5B (Q1 2026), with senior notes of $6.25B plus term loans and a partially drawn revolver. Stockholders' equity is negative at -$755M (Q1 2026), worsening from -$651M at YE2025 and +$121M at YE2024. This is engineered, not distressed: DaVita retired all treasury shares and bought back so much stock that book equity went negative. The screening-tool artifacts (an Altman Z-Score of 127 and ROA of 690%) are mathematical noise from the negative-equity denominator and should be disregarded. The real risk profile is a highly levered, equity-funded buyback machine running net debt at 3.0x-3.5x EBITDA per its credit agreement.

Liquidity is adequate: $644M cash, $1.125B undrawn revolver, current ratio ~1.3x. The weighted-average interest rate is ~5.44%, and the company holds $4.75B notional of forward interest-rate caps through 2029 to hedge floating-rate exposure. During 2025 it termed out maturities with new term loans, a $1.5B revolver, and $1.0B of 6.75% senior notes due 2033. Refinancing risk is well-managed; the leverage itself is the structural feature.

Capital allocation is relentless buybacks. FY2025 saw 12.7M shares repurchased for $1,788M, a 14.9% net share-count reduction in one year (basic shares 85M→74M; Q1 2026 basic shares down to 67.4M). The mechanism is the thesis: flat-volume business plus steady FCF plus aggressive share shrinkage equals double-digit EPS growth.

## Growth Analysis

The honest characterization is no organic growth, manufactured EPS growth. Treatment volume is flat. Revenue per treatment is rising modestly but partly on a TDAPA add-on that faces a 2027 reset. International expansion is genuine but acquisition-funded and dilutive to the clean cash-cow narrative. The earnings growth that investors are paying for comes almost entirely from the denominator: fewer shares each year against a stable-to-modestly-growing profit pool.

Rising consensus estimates are the constructive signal for a growth-hold. Current-quarter EPS was revised +5.9% over 30 days to $3.84. The earnings-beat record is solid (three beats in four quarters, including +22.6% in Q1 2026), though the one miss (-37.3% in Q3 2025) reflected the cyber disruption. These revisions are positive but moderate; they do not reach the +30% velocity that signals a fundamental acceleration, so the buyback mechanism, not an operating acceleration, is doing the work.

The forward question is whether the buyback can keep compounding EPS faster than the commercial profit pool erodes. With ~$1B of annual FCF deployed into a falling share count and net debt held at 3.0x-3.5x EBITDA, management can plausibly sustain mid-to-high-single-digit share reduction. If the commercial mix holds, that produces low-double-digit EPS growth. If the commercial mix erodes faster than expected, the same machine compounds a shrinking profit base. The Elara Caring minority stake (~$200M, expected mid-2026 close) and management's explicit interest in "healthcare services not related to kidney disease" signal that they themselves see limited organic growth in dialysis and are hunting adjacencies.

## Valuation Assessment

At the reference price (~$198-201), DVA trades at roughly 13.6x the FY26 consensus EPS of $14.80 and ~11.7x the FY27 estimate of $17.18. On trailing EPS of $9.51, the multiple is ~21x, but the trailing figure was cyber-depressed and is not the right anchor. On forward earnings the stock is not expensive in absolute terms.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use. Health Care Services peers show a median P/E of 38.2, but that median is distorted by high-multiple growth names (BTSG 74.7x, SHC 38.2x) and a loss-maker (HIMS, negative). LH at 23.5x is the closest comparable in business character (stable, capital-intensive, regulated). On that basis DaVita's low-double-digit forward multiple looks reasonable for a flat-volume cash cow, neither a bargain nor stretched. Morningstar's $262 fair value implies meaningful upside, but it embeds continued buyback-driven compounding and assumes the commercial mix holds.

The low forward multiple is partly a fair discount for genuine risks, not pure mispricing, which caps the valuation score. The market is pricing the commercial-mix concentration, the 2026 policy headwinds, and the engineered-leverage balance sheet. A low P/E on a business whose entire profit pool faces simultaneous, identifiable regulatory threats is not the same as a low P/E on a clean compounder. The analyst spread itself reflects this disagreement: targets range from $145 (low) to $235 (high) with a $201 median, an unusually wide band that captures the binary nature of the commercial-payer outcome. We see the stock as roughly fairly valued after the run, with the upside case dependent on the commercial pool holding through 2026 and the downside case on it cracking.

## Competitive Landscape

DaVita operates in an effective duopoly with Fresenius, with rational pricing and high barriers to entry. The competitive position is stable rather than improving. The central industry challenge is that ESKD growth has stalled: historically a reliable demographic grower, the patient population is now pressured by elevated post-COVID mortality near-term and, longer-term, by GLP-1 and SGLT2 inhibitors that slow chronic kidney disease progression, plus the more distant threat of xenotransplantation and artificial kidneys.

Three secular pressures bear watching. First, well-funded value-based-care startups and CMMI model participants can enter pre-dialysis and integrated kidney care with lower regulatory burden and higher risk tolerance, an asymmetry management flags repeatedly. Second, hemodiafiltration and medium-cut-off dialyzers are gaining ground internationally; if they prove clinically superior in the U.S., DaVita faces capital-intensive catch-up. Third, home dialysis is CMS-favored and strategically important but supply-chain fragile (a 2024 supplier plant disruption slowed home growth into early 2025). None of these is an imminent threat to the duopoly structure, but together they explain why management is diversifying into adjacent home-based and value-based care.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is commercial-mix erosion, and it is not a generic sector truism. Because the ~11-12% of commercial patients generate nearly all of DaVita's profit, the earnings are highly leveraged to that pool, and 2026 brings several concrete threats at once. Enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025, which management explicitly cited as a 2026 headwind that reduces commercial affordability and shifts patients toward Medicare or uninsured status. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act introduces Medicaid work requirements, provider-tax limits, and federal Medicaid phase-downs that could shrink coverage for ESRD-disabled patients. Charitable premium assistance via the American Kidney Fund is under regulatory and legal attack (California AB 290-style caps, a DC AG antitrust probe, a CA Department of Insurance inquiry), directly threatening the mechanism that helps patients afford commercial coverage. The Marietta Supreme Court decision created lasting uncertainty about whether employer plans can design around ESRD coverage. A faster-than-expected commercial shift is the asymmetric downside.

Secondary risks include the phosphate-binder TDAPA cliff at the end of 2026 (a 2027 earnings swing factor depending on how binders fold into the ESRD bundle), substantial floating-rate-exposed indebtedness with restrictive covenants, labor intensity and active California unionization petitions, and a cluster of legal matters (the Bayne False Claims Act qui tam with motion to dismiss denied April 2025, the FTC industry investigation into medical-director non-competes opened April 2024, and a no-poach antitrust class action). The 2025 cyber incident's operational impact is largely resolved, but litigation and regulatory tail risk from the data exfiltration remains. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

Berkshire Hathaway, DaVita's largest holder, is a slow-motion seller. Under an April 2024 agreement DaVita systematically repurchases Berkshire shares to keep the stake capped near 45% (Berkshire sold 1.22M shares to DaVita on May 1, 2026 at $149.84). This is an orderly contractual unwind, not a panic sale, but it means a large, price-insensitive seller remains on the tape and a meaningful portion of the buyback volume is absorbing Berkshire's exit rather than purely reducing float.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced and offers no strong directional tell. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 40.8%, exactly in line with one-year historical volatility (spread ~0%), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling an imminent catalyst. The term structure is flat (near 40.8% ≈ far 40.0%), put/call volume ratio is 0.58 and OI ratio 0.28 (call-tilted), and put/call skew is a balanced 1.8% near-term, turning modestly negative (-3.7%) at six months. ATM IV rose 10.2 points since mid-May (30.5%→40.8%) alongside a 2.3% price gain, consistent with uncertainty building around the next print rather than clear bearish positioning. The 14.8% short interest (4.6 days to cover) reflects institutional skepticism about the run and the commercial-mix risk, but at that level it is not a crowded short that would force a squeeze. The combination reads as a market that sees genuine two-sided risk, consistent with the wide analyst target band.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** DaVita is a stable duopoly cash cow with a disciplined, predictable capital return policy. The buyback machine converts ~$1B+ of annual FCF and a 10-15% yearly share-count reduction into low-double-digit EPS growth even with flat treatment volume. Consensus estimates are rising, the company beats regularly, and Q1 2026 cash flow has normalized post-cyber. Cost discipline and labor-productivity gains are real. If the commercial mix holds through the 2026 policy headwinds, FY27 EPS of ~$17 against continued share shrinkage supports a path toward the high-$220s to $235 (the top of the analyst range and near Morningstar's $262 fair value). Bull case ~$235, roughly +18% from the reference price.

**Bear Case.** The entire profit pool rests on ~11-12% of patients, and 2026 stacks ACA tax-credit expiration, OBBBA Medicaid changes, AKF charitable-assistance attacks, and Marietta fallout against that pool simultaneously. If the commercial mix erodes faster than guided, the buyback compounds a shrinking profit base, and engineered negative equity plus ~$10.5B of debt at a 5.44% blended rate leaves no balance-sheet cushion. The TDAPA cliff pressures 2027 revenue per treatment. Insiders sold heavily into the rally with zero open-market buys. A multiple of ~11x FY27 EPS could compress on any commercial-mix disappointment toward the analyst low of $145, roughly -28% from the reference price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), where the key items to monitor are commercial-mix trends, any commentary on ACA tax-credit-driven patient shifts, and 2026 volume tracking against the "roughly flat" guide. The TDAPA reimbursement structure for 2027 is the following major decision point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence over two consecutive quarters that the commercial-payer mix is holding or improving despite the ACA tax-credit expiration, with management raising full-year guidance on commercial strength rather than cost discipline alone.
- A meaningful acceleration in estimate revisions (FY27 EPS revised above ~$18, implying the buyback-plus-mix combination is compounding faster than the current ~6% velocity).
- Clarity on a favorable 2027 phosphate-binder bundle outcome that removes the TDAPA cliff overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guided-down or actual commercial-mix decline that cuts FY26 operating-income growth, signaling the asymmetric downside is materializing.
- An adverse resolution in the Bayne FCA matter, the FTC investigation, or AKF-related regulatory actions that materially threatens the commercial-payment mechanism.
- Net debt/EBITDA drifting above the 3.5x credit-agreement ceiling or a buyback slowdown that removes the EPS-compounding engine.

## Conclusion

DaVita is a well-run, deliberately over-levered buyback compounder bolted onto a stable dialysis duopoly. The growth-hold thesis is specific: ~$1B of annual FCF deployed into a 10-15% yearly share-count reduction produces double-digit EPS growth despite flat treatment volume, and rising consensus estimates confirm the mechanism is intact and the company keeps beating. That is a genuine 12-month appreciation case, sufficient to hold the position.

We stop short of a BUY for three reasons. First, the entire profit pool rests on a thin slice of commercial patients facing simultaneous, identifiable 2026 headwinds, and that risk is asymmetric to the downside. Second, the stock has run ~70% year-to-date and the C-suite sold heavily into the move with no open-market buying, a yellow flag against the momentum narrative. Third, the Q1 2026 EPS surge was flattered by an easy post-cyber comp, TDAPA reimbursement with a 2027 cliff, and the shrinking share count, so the underlying operating story is stable plus cost discipline, not acceleration. At roughly fair value on forward earnings after the run, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD, with the Q2 print and commercial-mix commentary as the next decision point.
