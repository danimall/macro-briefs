## Executive Summary

ProAssurance Corporation is no longer a publicly traded security. On June 26, 2026, The Doctors Company announced completion of its acquisition of ProAssurance for $25.00 per share in cash, approximately $1.3 billion of equity value, and stated that ProAssurance common stock would be deregistered with the SEC and delisted from the NYSE, with public trading having ceased. ProAssurance now operates as a wholly owned subsidiary of The Doctors Company, a physician-owned mutual-structure insurer within TDC Group. Former holders received cash. There is no continuing equity, no stub, and no identified PRA-linked security in which capital can be deployed. The recommendation is therefore AVOID on the basis that no investable instrument exists, and every scenario, target, and margin-of-safety calculation below is bounded by a consideration that has already been paid.

The supplied market data is internally consistent with that conclusion and should not be read as market judgment. The $1.290 billion market capitalization divided by $25.00 gives 51.6 million shares, matching the roughly 51.7 million diluted shares outstanding; the "market cap" is the deal equity value. ## Company Overview

ProAssurance was a Birmingham, Alabama specialty property and casualty insurer (CIK 1127703) with roughly 972 employees, focused on healthcare professional liability. It reported in four segments:

- **Specialty Property & Casualty** — predominantly medical professional liability plus products liability for medical technology and life sciences companies. Net premiums earned were $180.8 million in Q4 2025 and $172.1 million in Q1 2026, roughly 77% of consolidated net premiums earned.
- **Workers' Compensation Insurance** — net premiums earned of $40.3 million (Q4 2025) and $40.7 million (Q1 2026), roughly 18% of the total.
- **Segregated Portfolio Cell Reinsurance** — cell captive structures with gross premiums written of $11.1 million and $11.6 million in those quarters. A meaningful share of this segment's economics accrues to third-party cell participants rather than to ProAssurance shareholders.
- **Corporate** — the investment portfolio and debt. Net investment income of $39.1 million (Q4 2025) and $39.0 million (Q1 2026), against interest expense of roughly $5.1–5.2 million per quarter.

At December 31, 2025 total assets were $5.447 billion, of which $4.4 billion was investments, against shareholders' equity of $1.349 billion. Total liabilities were therefore $4.098 billion; net of $420.4 million of debt, approximately $3.68 billion represented insurance reserves and other operating liabilities, implying non-debt liability leverage of about 2.7x equity. Actual net loss and LAE reserves are lower than that proxy figure, which was not directly observable.

The structural feature that determined the outcome is visible in the segment split. In Q1 2026 the Corporate segment (essentially the investment portfolio) earned $23.9 million while Specialty P&C lost $9.1 million and Workers' Compensation lost $5.4 million. In every quarter for which segment data is available, net investment income of roughly $40 million exceeded the entire consolidated pretax result. Underwriting was a net consumer of the float's earnings.

A sourcing caveat carries through this report: the narrative sections of the FY2025 10-K (Items 1, 1A, 7 and 8) were not accessible. The most recent 10-K filing on the wire is a 10-K/A filed April 8, 2026 containing only Items 10–15, with Part III disclosure substituted for a 2026 proxy, which is routine for an issuer in a pending take-private. Business description, risk factors, MD&A, reserve development tables, reinsurance structure and statutory capital position are therefore unexamined. Statements about competitive position and about the drivers of specific results below are analyst inference from quantitative data, not filing facts.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue contraction was broad-based, not a mix shift.**

| Fiscal year | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $1,106.7M | — |
| 2023 | $1,137.2M | +2.8% |
| 2024 | $1,150.4M | +1.2% |
| 2025 | $1,098.0M | **-4.6%** |

Gross premiums written fell 3.0% year over year in Q4 2025 to $201.5 million and 5.5% in Q1 2026 to $287.0 million. Net premiums earned fell 3.7% and 5.4%. Every underwriting segment shrank: Specialty P&C net premiums earned -2.7% and -6.1%; Workers' Comp -6.1% and -2.1%; Segregated Portfolio Cell gross premiums written -11.1% and -8.7%. The January-1-weighted renewal seasonality means Q1 gross premiums written of $287.0 million far exceed Q4's $201.5 million, so quarterly premium should not be annualized. A uniformly shrinking book across three largely unrelated segments sustained over five-plus quarters is more consistent with underwriting retrenchment or share loss than with one line's cycle; de-risking during merger pendency is an alternative explanation that cannot be separated without the MD&A.

**Six years produced almost no cumulative earnings.** GAAP net income: -$175.7M (2020), +$144.1M (2021), -$0.4M (2022), -$38.6M (2023), +$52.7M (2024), +$50.9M (2025). The six-year sum is approximately +$33.1 million against an equity base ranging from $1.10 billion to $1.35 billion, roughly 0.4% average annual ROE, with three loss years. FY2025 ROE was 3.99% ($50.915M ÷ $1,275.4M average equity) and ROA 0.92%.

**The GAAP versus non-GAAP divergence is the single most important reconciliation.** FY2025 adjusted operating income per share of $1.62 was up 65.3% year over year, while GAAP net income fell 3.5%. On approximately 51.7 million diluted shares, FY2025 adjusted operating income was about $83.7 million versus roughly $50.2 million in FY2024. The implied items excluded from adjusted operating income swung from about +$2.5 million after tax in FY2024 to roughly -$32.8 million in FY2025, a $35 million adverse swing that fully offset the operating improvement. The two most plausible components are merger-related transaction expenses, customarily excluded from non-GAAP operating income, and negative fair-value marks on equity and other non-fixed-maturity investments. The allocation between them could not be established. The method reconciles against disclosed figures: applying it to Q4 2025 reproduces the company's cited 12.8% adjusted operating ROE, and to Q1 2026 yields 3.9% against a cited 3.8%. On that basis FY2025 adjusted operating ROE was approximately 6.6%, a better estimate of normalized earning power than 3.99%, and still well below any plausible 9–10% cost of equity for a long-tail casualty writer.

**Earnings were extraordinarily back-loaded.** Derived quarterly adjusted operating income for FY2025: Q1 ~$6.7M, Q2 ~$26.8M, Q3 ~$7.9M (residual), Q4 ~$42.3M. Q4 alone was roughly 51% of the year. The driver was the Specialty P&C combined ratio falling to 79.9% from 99.9%, a 20-point improvement on a 2.7% decline in net premiums earned, with segment expenses down 23.7% and the segment result swinging from a $6.5 million loss to a $32.4 million profit. Then Q1 2026 reverted: Specialty P&C 105.9%, consolidated 110.4%, segment loss $9.1 million. A large favorable Q4 swing that immediately reverses is the signature of an annual year-end actuarial reserve review releasing prior-accident-year reserves. Earned rate increases finally reaching the earned book, favorable emergence on the 2016–2020 MPL accident years (an industry-wide phenomenon), changes in ceded reinsurance economics, and a non-recurring expense credit are untested alternatives. The driver is not established in accessible evidence, and no inference about management motive is drawn from the timing relative to the merger agreement; reserve reviews are annual and mandatory, and the acquirer conducted its own diligence. Normalized earning power is better approximated by the non-Q4 quarters, roughly $41 million annualized, near 3.2% ROE, than by the full-year figure.

**Underwriting lost money in aggregate.** Consolidated combined ratios: 109.3% (Q4 2024), 115.6% (Q1 2025), 92.3% (Q4 2025), 110.4% (Q1 2026). A full-year FY2025 consolidated combined ratio was not disclosed; working back from pretax income of $72.1 million, interest expense of $20.8 million, and net investment income near $152–155 million against approximately $930 million of net premiums earned implies an underwriting pretax loss on the order of $50–60 million and a consolidated combined ratio near 105–107%. That is a derived estimate with interpolated inputs, sensitive to the treatment of realized investment losses and merger costs, not a disclosed figure.

Workers' Compensation was the clearest structural problem: 117.6% (Q4 2024, derived), 110.2% (Q1 2025, derived), 116.3% (Q4 2025), 114.1% (Q1 2026). Q1 2026 deteriorated 390 basis points year over year and the segment loss widened to $5.4 million from $3.8 million while premium shrank 2.1%. Segregated Portfolio Cell Reinsurance was the only consistently sub-100% segment (67.4% and 84.1%), and is both the smallest and the most economically shared with third parties.

**Cash flow was negative and worsening on a like-for-like basis.** Operating cash flow was -$10.5 million in FY2024 and -$25.6 million in FY2025 (confirmed in XBRL at -$25,620 thousand). Q1 2026 was -$21.3 million against Q1 2025 of -$11.6 million, an 84% worsening on comparable seasonality; Q1 2025 represented 45% of that year's full outflow, so annualizing the quarter is not appropriate. For a P&C insurer, negative operating cash flow means claim payments plus expenses exceeded premium collections plus investment income received, the arithmetic of a long-tail book whose new-business volume is shrinking. Holding-company liquidity thinned accordingly: cash and equivalents fell from $54.9 million (Dec-2024) to $36.5 million (Dec-2025) to $14.1 million (Mar-2026), while total investments held roughly flat at $4.4 billion. The $4.4 billion portfolio provided ample near-term claim coverage; this was a return-on-capital problem manifesting in cash flow, not an imminent solvency problem. Insurance operating cash flow is also lumpy, since reinsurance settlement timing, cash taxes, and individual large claim settlements can dominate a single quarter.

**Book value grew on bond marks, not earnings.** Shareholders' equity rose 12.3% in 2025, from $1,201.7 million to $1,349.1 million, an increase of $147.4 million against net income of only $50.9 million. The $96.5 million residual came from other comprehensive income and equity-compensation activity, overwhelmingly the reversal of unrealized losses on available-for-sale fixed maturities. Book value per share went from roughly $23.5 (Dec-2024) to $26.24 (Dec-2025) to $25.94 (Mar-2026). Retained earnings rose by exactly $50.915 million, equal to net income, establishing that no dividends were charged to retained earnings in 2025, nor in 2024, where the same identity holds. The company confirmed zero share repurchases in 2025 and Q1 2026 with $55.9 million of authorization unused. Merger-agreement interim operating covenants routinely restrict capital returns between signing and closing and are the most likely explanation; capital preservation given negative operating cash flow is an alternative. Neither is verified.

**Capital structure was static.** Debt net of issuance costs was $424.9 million (Dec-2024), $420.4 million (Dec-2025), $419.3 million (Mar-2026), consistent with scheduled amortization only. Debt/equity was 31.2% and debt/total capital 23.8%. Implied average cost of debt was 4.93% ($20.838 million interest ÷ $422.6 million average debt). Instrument-level composition, maturities, covenants, and change-of-control treatment at closing could not be verified and are a genuine gap for anyone assessing residual subsidiary-level claims.

**Goodwill.** The balance fell from $49.610 million (Dec-2022) to $5.500 million (Dec-2023) and held there through Dec-2025, while intangibles amortized smoothly ($66.8M → $48.5M) with no step change. A $44.1 million impairment in FY2023 is the most consistent reading and would largely account for that year's $39.1 million pretax loss; a disposition or deconsolidation carrying goodwill off the balance sheet would produce the same movement. Cause, segment, and mechanism are unverified.

On the absent Altman Z and Piotroski scores: those constructs depend on working capital, inventory, gross margin, and asset turnover, none of which map to an insurance holding company whose 0.20x revenue/assets ratio reflects float leverage rather than efficiency. Their absence is not a negative signal. The applicable lenses (31.2% debt/equity, 2.7x non-debt liability leverage, a $4.4 billion portfolio against $1.35 billion of equity, and negative operating cash flow) describe a solvent company earning below its cost of capital.

## Growth Analysis

There is no forward growth to analyze because there is no continuing security. For completeness, the operating trajectory into the close was negative on every observable axis: revenue -4.6% in FY2025; gross premiums written down 3.0% and 5.5% in the last two reported quarters; all three underwriting segments shrinking simultaneously; and the only sub-100% combined ratio segment (Segregated Portfolio Cell) contracting fastest at -8.7% to -11.1% gross premiums written. Contracting premium at a 105–107% consolidated combined ratio worsens rather than relieves the cash drain, because claim payments on prior accident years run off against a smaller incoming premium base.

The estimate revision data supplied (current year consensus up 13.0% over 30 days to $1.22, next year up 12.3% to $1.25, four and three contributing analysts respectively) is not an earnings-momentum signal here. Revisions of this kind normally justify weight; in this case they were produced on a security whose price was fixed at $25.00 and whose coverage had thinned to a handful of contributors during a fifteen-month merger pendency. The same distortion explains the earnings "surprise" record: adjusted operating income of $0.82 against a $0.26 consensus in Q4 2025 (a 220.9% beat) and $0.52 against $0.21 in Q2 2025 (a 145.2% beat), alongside a -43.9% miss in Q3 2025 and a -3.8% miss in Q1 2026 ($0.25 against $0.26). Consensus of $0.26 against an $0.82 result in a quarter with a 20-point segment combined ratio swing indicates estimates were not being maintained. Post-earnings share moves of -0.4%, +0.9%, and -0.7% confirm the price was severed from fundamental surprise.

## Valuation Assessment

The canonical valuation snapshot is flagged invalid, with errors including missing authoritative price, missing market timestamp, and an official close/market date mismatch. The only price in the record is a $25.00 daily-history close dated July 2, 2026, after the June 26 deal completion, with zero volume, which is a stale-feed artifact rather than a tradable quote. No valid basis for scenario, margin-of-safety, or upside arithmetic exists, and the honest statement is that forward participation is zero because the consideration has been paid.

What can be assessed is whether $25.00 was a good price.

- **Against the unaffected market.** Shares reportedly surged 51.2% to $23.50 in aftermarket trading on the March 19–20, 2025 announcement, implying an unaffected price near $15.54 and a premium of roughly 61%. That derivation rests on a single secondary source's percentage-move statement and inherits its uncertainty.
- **Against book value.** $25.00 equals 1.063x the ~$23.52 December 2024 book value per share, 0.953x the $26.24 December 2025 figure, and 0.964x the $25.94 March 2026 figure. Holders sold below stated net asset value at closing.
- **Against earning power.** $25.00 is 15.4x FY2025 adjusted operating EPS of $1.62 and roughly 25x FY2025 GAAP EPS of $0.985. On the non-Q4 quarters' run rate, closer to $0.80 of annualized adjusted operating EPS, the multiple exceeds 30x.
- **Against cost of capital.** A Gordon-style justified price-to-book of ROE ÷ COE, using 4.0% GAAP to 6.6% adjusted ROE against a 9–10% cost of equity, yields 0.40x–0.73x book. The unaffected market's ~0.66x sat squarely inside that band. Book value is worth 1.0x only when returns equal the cost of capital, so "below book" is the wrong benchmark for this business, and the December 2025 book was inflated by roughly $96.5 million of AOCI recovery on AFS bonds, a mark-to-market item that reverses if rates rise and does not represent distributable value. On economic book, a cash buyer paying 0.95x of a bond-mark-swollen figure arguably paid more.

The supplied peer set (Klarna at -36.9x, Blue Owl at 99.5x, median P/E 99.5) shares nothing with a medical professional liability underwriter and provides no usable relative anchor. The relevant comparison is operational: AMERISAFE, the closest workers' compensation pure-play, also faced margin pressure in Q1 2026 (EPS $0.50 versus $0.52 expected on weaker underwriting margins), indicating that workers' comp softness was partly sector-wide, but AMERISAFE grew premium 7.9% while ProAssurance's workers' comp book shrank 2.1–6.1% at a 114–118% combined ratio. Shrinking volume alongside worsening margin is company-specific.

A note on the fifteen-month pendency: book value per share rose $2.42, or 10.3%, between the December 2024 measurement date and the last reported March 2026 figure, against fixed nominal consideration with no collar, no evident ticking fee, and no dividends. Holding the announcement-date 1.063x multiple constant against $25.94 book implies roughly $27.58, or about $2.58 per share and $133 million in aggregate, of book accretion that accrued to the buyer. Most of that increase was AOCI recovery rather than earned capital, which materially softens the point. For merger-arbitrage holders who bought at $23.50–24.50, the gross return to $25.00 over fifteen months was roughly 2–6%, or 1.6–4.8% annualized, likely below the risk-free rate over that window, before deal-break risk.

The valuation score of 2.0 reflects zero remaining upside from a fully consummated fixed cash price on an instrument that no longer trades. It is not a claim that the business was worth less than $25.00.

## Competitive Landscape

The acquirer, The Doctors Company, describes itself as the nation's largest physician-owned medical malpractice insurer and part of TDC Group, and framed the transaction as building "the most trusted and capable medical professional liability and specialty lines insurer in America." The combined entity was described as serving more than 200,000 healthcare professionals and organizations with direct written premium above $2.5 billion and $12 billion in assets, though the press release's attribution of those figures between combined and TDC-standalone is ambiguous. If $12 billion is the post-close figure, ProAssurance's $5.4 billion of assets was roughly 45% of the combined balance sheet, indicating a merger of comparably sized organizations rather than a bolt-on.

The buyer's mutual, physician-owned structure is analytically important. A mutual acquirer is not disciplined by quarterly EPS or public-market cost of equity in the same way and can rationally pay above what a public buyer would for a sub-cost-of-capital book, because scale, policyholder retention, and expense synergies accrue to member-owners over a long horizon. That is the most plausible explanation for how a franchise the public market valued near 0.66x book cleared at approximately 1.06x announcement-date book. No evidence of a competing bid process was found.

On ProAssurance's own competitive trajectory, the cleanest available evidence is the pay-versus-performance table in the April 11, 2025 proxy. Indexed to $100 at December 31, 2019, ProAssurance total shareholder return read 50, 72, 50, 40, and 46 through 2024 while the peer group read 103, 119, 134, 146, and 193, a cumulative -54% against +93%, roughly 147 percentage points of underperformance across a window (2020–2024) that was strong for P&C insurers generally, featuring a hard market, rising rates, and rising investment yields. ProAssurance did not participate. Combined with premium contraction in all three underwriting segments and a workers' compensation book losing money at 110–118% combined ratios while shrinking, the trajectory was deteriorating, not stable.

## Risk Assessment

For any residual holder, the equity risks are resolved: cash was received at a fixed price. The risks that remain are informational and administrative.

**1. Deal completion is not independently verified against ProAssurance filings.** Completion rests on the acquirer's June 26, 2026 press release, corroborated by a June 29, 2026 local news report. The corresponding Form 8-K, Form 25, and Form 15 were not retrieved; ticker-based EDGAR lookups now fail CIK resolution, which is itself consistent with deregistration, and direct archive fetches returned HTTP 403. The evidence is coherent and multi-sourced but should be confirmed before any administrative action.

**2. Stale data artifacts create false signals.** Beta 0.038, a $25.00 unanimous analyst target from four contributors, 22.1% short interest with 8.1 days to cover, a 1,564% ATM implied volatility print, rising estimate revisions, and a 220.9% Q4 earnings beat would together score attractively on most quantitative screens. Every one is distorted by the fixed-price merger or by a delisted quote feed. The practical instruction is not to score the security on these inputs.

**3. Subsidiary-level obligations are unresolved.** The composition, maturities, covenants, and change-of-control treatment of the roughly $420 million of debt at closing are unknown. Anyone holding ProAssurance-level obligations rather than common equity faces an unexamined situation, compounded by the acquirer's statement that ProAssurance would function as a wholly owned subsidiary "while a review process is conducted to identify the optimal operating structure," meaning operating model, brand disposition, and organizational structure were undetermined at closing.

**4. Historical reserve risk is now the acquirer's.** Non-debt liabilities of approximately $3.68 billion sat against $1.349 billion of equity. Illustratively, if net loss and LAE reserves were near $3.0 billion, 5% adverse development would be $150 million, roughly 11% of former shareholders' equity and about 1.8x FY2025 adjusted operating income. Combined ratios ranging from 92.3% to 115.6% within four quarters indicate low predictability of the reserve estimate. That exposure transferred with the shares.

### Options Market Signal

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (for a former holder, retrospectively).** $25.00 delivered roughly a 61% premium to the unaffected price and about 1.06x announcement-date book value for a franchise generating a 3.99% GAAP and 6.6% adjusted operating ROE against a 9–10% cost of equity, in a business shrinking premium across all segments with negative and worsening operating cash flow. Against a standalone path that the market was capitalizing at 0.66x book, the sale captured approximately 40 points of price-to-book re-rating and transferred the long-tail reserve risk to a mutual buyer with a longer horizon and different capital discipline. Judged against the realistic standalone alternative, the outcome was good, and the board's decision to sell is defensible on the arithmetic.

**Bear case.** Holders sold at 0.96x the $25.94 book value reported at March 31, 2026, having received no dividends and no ticking fee across a fifteen-month pendency during which book value per share rose $2.42. Merger-arb holders earned roughly 1.6–4.8% annualized, plausibly below the risk-free rate before deal-break risk. The five-year relative TSR record of -147 points against the peer group, the roughly 89% goodwill write-down in FY2023, and CEO compensation actually paid peaking at $6.43 million in the final full year before a below-closing-book sale form an uncomfortable sequence, though the compensation structure demonstrably flexed downward when results were poor ($466 thousand in the 2020 loss year, $2.19 million in 2023), and the FY2024 spike is largely explained mechanically by the company-selected measure swinging from -$9.0 million to +$48.6 million and by mark-to-market revaluation of unvested awards, with the December 31, 2024 measurement date preceding the merger announcement.

**Current position.** Neither case is actionable. There is no security to own, short, or hedge, and no residual instrument identified.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID — no investable instrument.** This is a classification of availability, not an assertion of new fundamental deterioration.

**Re-entry criteria.** None exist for the common equity, which was cancelled in exchange for cash. Capital seeking exposure to consolidating medical professional liability underwriting must find it elsewhere, since the acquirer is physician-owned and has no public common stock. The only circumstances that would reopen analysis are: (a) documentary evidence that the transaction did not in fact close as reported, which would revive a $25.00 fixed-consideration merger-arb position with negligible remaining spread; or (b) issuance of a publicly traded ProAssurance-level security, for example registered notes or surplus-note-type instruments, in which case the analysis would center on the unverified $420 million capital structure and reserve adequacy rather than on equity.

**Timeline.** Immediate. The position, if any remains on a statement, should be reconciled against the merger consideration and the record removed from the active coverage universe pending confirmation of deregistration via Form 15.

**Verification tasks before final closure.** Confirm the completion 8-K, Form 25 and Form 15; confirm the treatment of the $420 million of debt at closing; and confirm that any cash consideration was received in full at $25.00 per share.

**Score trajectory note.** The prior sequence was AVOID at 4.6 (May 9) and HOLD at 5.0 (June 19), with growth, valuation and management quality all revised upward. That June upgrade was issued days before completion, when the security was pinned at $25.00 with roughly 1.2% of spread remaining, economically a HOLD only in the sense that a nearly closed merger-arb position has no reason to be sold. The reversal to AVOID at 3.3 is driven entirely by the June 26 completion and the disappearance of the instrument. Growth (4.5 to 2.5), valuation (5.5 to 2.0) and competitive position marks step down because there is no forward participation, no remaining upside from consummated consideration, and no continuing franchise to assess. Financial health at 4.0 and management quality at 5.0 reflect the operating record on its own terms: a solvent balance sheet with negative operating cash flow and thinning holding-company liquidity, and a management team that realized a substantial premium exit after five years of severe relative underperformance. Risk factors at 4.5 reflect resolved equity risk offset by material verification gaps.

## Conclusion

ProAssurance's equity was extinguished on June 26, 2026 at $25.00 per share in cash, and the company now operates as a wholly owned subsidiary of The Doctors Company.
