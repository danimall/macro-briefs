**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest LNG producer in the United States and the second-largest LNG operator globally, with more than 60 mtpa of expected liquefaction capacity across Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi. The business runs on long-term, take-or-pay contracts: roughly 90% of anticipated production is contracted through the mid-2030s under ~30 creditworthy SPAs with a weighted-average remaining life near 15 years, producing a contracted backlog of $290.6B (fixed plus variable) and a structurally stable cash-flow profile. Operating cash flow was ~$5.5B in both 2024 and 2025, free cash flow was $2.46B in 2025, and the share count has fallen ~16% from ~251M (2022) to ~210M (Q1 2026) under a buyback program with $9.7B of remaining authorization through 2030.

The reported financials are noisy and require translation. Q1 2026 showed a $3.5B GAAP net loss (−$16.65 EPS) driven by $4.8B of non-cash, mark-to-market derivative losses on long-term IPM agreements as global-to-Henry-Hub gas spreads widened amid Middle East supply disruption. The same quarter delivered adjusted EPS of $4.77 against a $3.91 consensus, positive operating cash flow of $1.08B, and a raised full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $7.25–7.75B. Consensus current-year adjusted EPS has been revised up to $18.80 (from $14.31 ninety days ago, a +31.4% move), and next-year to $19.27 (+31.5%). On those figures the stock trades at ~12x forward earnings, a reasonable multiple for a contracted infrastructure operator with a long growth pipeline.

The fundamentals support continued ownership, but three specific factors keep this short of a fresh buy. First, a cluster of C-suite open-market sales (CFO Davis ~$8.7M, CCO Feygin ~$11.8M, CLO Markowitz ~$6.5M) occurred within roughly 30 days in late March 2026 at ~$290–300, about 28% above today's $224.86. Second, the guidance raise and the estimate-revision momentum are partly powered by a transient Middle East disruption premium on uncontracted spot/marketing volumes; a Strait of Hormuz reopening would compress the JKM/TTF-to-Henry-Hub spreads that drive those margins, and the 30-day current-quarter estimate has already ticked down 6.9%. Third, valuation is fair rather than asymmetric: EV/EBITDA is ~8.3–8.6x on mid-point guidance with deleveraging still in progress. We rate LNG a HOLD with a constructive 12-month growth bias.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two export terminals. Sabine Pass (Louisiana) has more than 30 mtpa operating, held through publicly traded subsidiary Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), in which Cheniere holds a 48.6% LP interest, the 100% GP interest, and all incentive distribution rights. Corpus Christi (Texas) is 100% owned and includes the Corpus Christi Stage 3 Project (seven midscale trains, >10 mtpa), 96.5% complete at Q1 2026 with five of seven trains substantially complete, plus the FID'd CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (~5 mtpa, ~37% complete, targeting completion in 2H 2028). Approximately 8 mtpa remained under construction at Q1 2026. Since its first cargo in February 2016, Cheniere has shipped more than 4,760 cargoes and over 325 million tonnes to 40-plus countries.

The contract structure is the core of the business. SPAs charge a fixed fee paid regardless of whether the customer lifts the cargo, plus a variable fee indexed to roughly 115% of Henry Hub. The variable fee is designed to cover feedgas, transport, and liquefaction fuel, which insulates Cheniere's economics from US gas-price swings. The cash-flow profile is therefore stable across commodity cycles even though reported revenue moves sharply with Henry Hub pass-through.

## Financial Analysis

Headline metrics are distorted by commodity pass-through and derivative accounting and should not be read at face value.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $33.4B | $20.4B | $15.7B | $20.0B |
| Operating income | $4.6B | $15.5B | $6.1B | $9.1B |
| Net income (to Cheniere) | $1.4B | $9.9B | $3.25B | $5.33B |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $5.39B | $5.54B |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $2.46B |

Revenue swings track Henry Hub because it is embedded in the variable fee. The 2025 increase of $4.3B came from higher Henry Hub pricing (+$2.9B) and incremental volumes from new Corpus Christi Stage 3 trains (+$1.2B), not from improving unit economics on the contracted base.

Derivative mark-to-market is the dominant earnings distortion. 2025 net income included $3.6B of pre-tax non-cash derivative gains from IPM agreements; Q1 2026 then swung to a net loss on the derivative marks as international LNG-to-Henry-Hub spreads widened. These are valuation marks on 15-year contracts, mostly non-cash, and economically hedged. The mechanism: the IPM agreements purchase gas at a global index minus a fixed fee; when spot global-to-domestic spreads widen, the mark-to-market liability rises through the income statement even though the cash economics over the contract life are unchanged. Operating cash flow stayed positive at $1.08B in Q1 2026, and adjusted EPS beat consensus.

Cash generation is strong and durable. Operating cash flow held at ~$5.5B in both 2024 and 2025 while growth capex declined as Stage 3 finished (Stage 3 construction cash fell from $1.5B to $1.3B in 2025). The balance sheet carries $23–24B of total debt against ~$8B of equity, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.41 reads as "distress." That signal is misleading here. The leverage is intentional project finance matched to multi-decade contracted cash flows across four ring-fenced capital structures (SPL, CQP, CCH, and the parent). The credit agencies confirm the strengthening: in 2025–early 2026, S&P upgraded Cheniere/CQP/CCH to BBB+, Fitch to BBB, and Moody's to Baa2/Baa1. The weighted-average senior note rate is 4.65%, liquidity is ~$8.3B, and the deleveraging target is ~4x.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly. The June 2024 plan, expanded in February 2026, authorizes $10B of buybacks (2026–2030) with $9.7B remaining at Q1 2026, equal to roughly 20% of the current market cap. The company repurchased 12.1M shares ($2.7B) in 2025 and 2.7M shares ($537M, ~$199 average) in Q1 2026, and raised the dividend more than 10% to $2.22 annualized. Buyback execution near $199–200 sits well below the ~$290–300 at which insiders sold personal stock in late March.

## Growth Analysis

The growth pipeline is contracted and broad. Stage 3 is nearly complete, with first LNG from Train 6 imminent and Train 7 substantial completion targeted for fall 2026, taking full-year production toward 52–54M tonnes. Beyond that: CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (~5 mtpa, completion 2H 2028), a FERC application filed February 2026 for the CCL Expansion (up to 24 mtpa, FID targeted 2027/2028), an SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa, FID targeted 2026/2027), and a December 2025 FERC filing to add ~5 mtpa to existing Corpus Christi authorizations.

Management raised 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance by roughly $500M, citing about 1 mtpa of incremental production plus tightened global LNG markets. Consensus has followed: current-year adjusted EPS revised up 20.5% over 30 days; next-year up 31.5% over 90 days. The 90-day revision velocity exceeds the +30% threshold that typically signals a fundamental shift, and the combination of rising estimates and a depressed price is constructive.

Two qualifiers temper how much weight that signal carries. First, part of the revision is the raised EBITDA guidance, which management explicitly tied to Middle East supply disruption boosting competition for flexible US cargoes. That benefit accrues to uncontracted spot/marketing margins, not the contracted fixed-fee base, and it reverses if Hormuz reopens and global-to-domestic spreads narrow. Second, the 30-day current-quarter estimate was revised down 6.9% even as the full-year number rose, suggesting near-term margin volatility. Commercial momentum on the durable side is genuine: new long-term SPAs with JERA (~1 mtpa, 2029–2050), CPC Taiwan (up to ~1.2 mtpa, 2026–2050), and a CNR IPM. WoodMac projects global LNG demand growing ~64% to 671 mtpa by 2040 with ~104 mtpa of new supply needed, a structurally favorable long-term backdrop, against a near-term supply wave (~20 mtpa added globally in 2025) that pressures spot prices and raises recontracting competition.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward adjusted earnings the stock is not expensive. Current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $18.80 implies ~12.0x forward earnings at $224.86, and next-year $19.27 implies ~11.7x. The trailing P/E of 33.4x is meaningless here because the $24.13 trailing EPS is inflated by 2025's non-cash derivative gains and distorted by the Q1 2026 GAAP loss.

On enterprise value, the picture is fair rather than cheap. With ~$47.3B equity and ~$23–24B of debt against ~$8.3B liquidity, EV is roughly $62–64B, putting EV/EBITDA at ~8.3–8.6x on the $7.5B mid-point of guidance. FCF yield on 2025's $2.46B is ~5.2%, though free cash flow should expand as Stage 3 capex rolls off. These are reasonable infrastructure multiples that compensate for the contracted backlog, but they do not embed a large margin of safety, and deleveraging toward the ~4x target is still in progress.

Analyst targets sit meaningfully above the current price: mean $303, median $307, low $259, high $340, against $224.86. The breadth of the gap (~37% to the median) reflects partly the estimate-revision tailwind. The reservation is that some of the embedded earnings power reflects the current disruption premium on marketing margins; if that premium fades, the targets compress with it. Cycle-adjusted on the contracted fixed-fee base alone, the floor is solid; the open question is how much of the spot/marketing uplift is recurring. We score valuation 6.5: reasonable forward multiple and supportive target dispersion, offset by contingent earnings power and the absence of clear asymmetry at the entry price.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's moat is the take-or-pay contract book, which insulates the company from gas-price volatility on the bulk of its volumes. No single customer is more than 10% of revenue (one is 18% of receivables), so concentration is moderate. Scale is a durable advantage: largest US producer, second-largest globally, with first-mover infrastructure and established Bechtel EPC relationships that reduce construction-cost risk through fixed-price turnkey contracts.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving. The new long-term SPAs extend the contracted book to 2050, and structural demand drivers are favorable: Europe's LNG demand rose 27% in 2025 to a record ~125 mtpa as it displaces Russian gas, and the EU Parliament voted to ban residual Russian gas/LNG by 2027. The offsetting trajectory risk is recontracting into a market shifting from tightness to a supply wave, where expiring SPAs must be renewed against Qatari expansion, other US projects, and oil-indexed LNG. That is a multi-year, not near-term, concern given the existing book. We score competitive position 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

The most material company-specific risks are not all fully priced:

1. **IPM/derivative GAAP volatility.** Level 3 liquefaction supply derivatives swing billions per quarter on unobservable inputs (international-to-Henry-Hub spreads ranged 60%–537% at Q1 2026). These are non-cash and economically hedged, but the market sometimes conflates the headline loss with deterioration; the stock fell ~5.6% on the Q1 print despite a guidance raise. Headlines on these marks will continue to overstate the cash impact.

2. **Hormuz/disruption-premium reversal.** The marketing-margin uplift currently supporting guidance and estimate revisions depends on wide global-to-domestic gas spreads tied to Middle East disruption. A sustained Strait of Hormuz reopening would compress these spreads and reverse part of the estimate momentum. This is the most actionable near-term downside.

3. **Section 301 US-built-vessel mandate.** Beginning April 2029, 1% of US LNG exports must move on US-built vessels, rising to 15% by 2047, against essentially zero US-built LNG carrier capacity today. This idiosyncratic regulatory risk is slow-developing and largely unquantified.

4. **Property-tax incentive roll-off.** Corpus Christi ad valorem incentives begin expiring in 2026 and Sabine Pass in 2027 (FY2025 ad valorem tax ~$89M); the magnitude of the increase is uncertain.

5. **Recontracting into the supply wave** and ongoing dependence on debt-market access for the growth model.

Offsetting these, the contracted backlog and durable operating cash flow substantially limit downside on the core business. One-time positives in 2026 cushion near-term cash: a confirmed $370M alternative-fuel excise tax credit, a $380M CAMT refund received December 2025, and the new FDDEI 14% export-income regime lowering the effective tax rate. Net, two-sided idiosyncratic risk centered on the spot-margin and IPM-volatility components warrants a risk score of 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing leans cautious. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 38.8% against 27.6% one-year historical, a rich 11.2-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (38.8% near-term versus 31.0% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent event risk rather than a sustained vol regime. The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.09 from 0.95 over the latest snapshot, and put/call skew steepens with tenor (−0.8% at one week to −3.6% at six months), indicating a modest downside tilt building into the longer expiries. The implied one-month move is ~5.8%. This is consistent with the marketing-margin reversal risk and the IPM-headline volatility identified in the fundamentals: the options market is paying up for protection against a near-term shock, not pricing a directional collapse. Short interest is negligible at 1.9% of float (1.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Train 6 first LNG and Train 7 completion lift 2026 production to 52–54M tonnes and validate the EBITDA guidance. Consensus continues catching up to the estimate revisions, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst median of ~$307 as the market recognizes durable adjusted earnings power at ~12x forward. The $9.7B buyback authorization (~20% of market cap) plus the FDDEI tax tailwind compound EPS/DCF per share as capex falls. New long-term SPAs and the CCL/SPL expansion FIDs extend the contracted growth runway into the 2030s. Bull-case target ~$340 (analyst high), roughly +51% from current.

### Bear Case
Hormuz reopens with sustained transit, global-to-domestic gas spreads compress, and the marketing-margin uplift that drove the guidance raise reverses, pulling estimate revisions back down (the 30-day current-quarter cut of 6.9% is an early sign). IPM derivative losses recur and the market again punishes the headline despite stable cash flow. Recontracting competition from the near-term supply wave pressures sentiment, and the Section 301 vessel mandate gains attention. The stock drifts back toward its 52-week low of ~$186, roughly −18% from current, before the contracted backlog and buyback re-establish a floor. Bear-case target ~$186.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) and the status of any sustained Hormuz transit resolution.**

This is a constructive growth_hold rather than a buy. The fundamentals — contracted backlog, durable cash flow, reasonable forward multiple, large buyback — are genuinely attractive, but three concrete, falsifiable concerns prevent an upgrade to BUY at the current price:

- **Insider cluster selling.** Three C-suite officers (CFO Davis ~$8.7M, CCO Feygin ~$11.8M, CLO Markowitz ~$6.5M) sold open-market blocks within ~30 days in late March 2026 at ~$290–300, ~28% above today's price. Clustered selling by three senior officers near a high is a materially cautionary signal, distinct from routine RSU vesting.
- **Contingent earnings power.** The raised 2026 EBITDA guidance and estimate revisions are partly driven by a transient Middle East disruption premium on uncontracted spot/marketing margins, not the contracted base. A Hormuz reopening compresses these spreads. The 30-day current-quarter estimate already declined 6.9%.
- **Fair, not asymmetric, valuation.** EV/EBITDA ~8.3–8.6x and FCF yield ~5.2% with deleveraging still in progress offer limited margin of safety; the bear case (−18% to the 52-week low) is comparable in magnitude to the realistic near-term upside once the disruption premium is discounted.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Sustained Hormuz resolution with the next quarterly marketing margin holding or expanding (demonstrating the EBITDA guidance is durable, not disruption-dependent), confirming the estimate revisions; or
- A pullback toward ~$200 (forward P/E ~10.6x) that restores asymmetric upside to the analyst median; or
- Resumption of open-market C-suite buying, reversing the late-March selling signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Hormuz reopening followed by a guidance cut and falling consensus EBITDA below ~$7.0B; or
- A material adverse development on the Section 301 vessel mandate or a contract termination/credit event among the top SPA counterparties; or
- Evidence the spot/marketing margin collapse is structural rather than cyclical.

## Conclusion

Cheniere is a high-quality LNG infrastructure operator: a contracted backlog, durable operating cash flow, credit upgrades from all three agencies in 2025–26, a share-count reduction since 2022, and remaining buyback authorization. On forward adjusted earnings near 12x with estimate revisions running +31% over 90 days, the long-term case is sound and the headline GAAP loss is a non-cash derivative artifact, not economic deterioration. We continue to recommend holding the position.

We stop short of a buy at $224.86 for specific reasons rather than vague caution: three senior officers sold large open-market blocks near $290–300 in late March, a meaningful portion of the guidance raise and estimate momentum depends on a Middle East disruption premium that a Hormuz reopening would unwind, and the valuation is fair rather than asymmetric with deleveraging still underway. A sustained resolution that proves the marketing margin durable, a pullback toward $200, or a reversal of insider selling would change the call. **Overall score: 6.7. Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias).**
