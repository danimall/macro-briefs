**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allstate has completed one of the sharpest earnings recoveries in the personal-lines P&C sector. Net income moved from a $1.42B loss in 2022 to $10.17B in 2025 as 2022–2024 auto and homeowners rate increases earned through while loss-cost inflation moderated. Q1 2026 delivered a reported Property-Liability combined ratio of 82.0 (versus 97.4 a year earlier), net income to common of $2.43B, and book value per diluted share of $113.52, up 52% YoY. Trailing ROE is roughly 48%. The stock has risen about 34% over the trailing year to a 52-week high near $260.

The central analytical issue is earnings quality. The 82.0 combined ratio embeds roughly 6.8 points of prior-year reserve releases ($1.0B in Q1 2026, of which $840M was auto, $675M of it auto injury, from accident years 2023–2024), plus the benefit of a quiet catastrophe year against a 10-year Q1 cat average of 8.4 points. Normalized for fading reserve tailwinds and a mid-cycle catastrophe load, the underlying combined ratio is closer to the high-80s, still a good result, but the reported figure materially overstates run-rate profitability. This is why the stock trades at ~5.7x trailing earnings: P&C insurers rationally carry low multiples on peak-cycle earnings that reserve releases and low-cat years cannot sustain. The ~9x forward P/E is the honest lens, and it sits roughly in line with the P&C peer median of 9.0.

The trajectory of prior reports (7.7 in February to 6.2 in June, BUY downgraded to HOLD) reflected this recognition that valuation and management-quality scores had been anchored to peak-cycle metrics. The new data confirms rather than reverses that view: the stock has appreciated another ~18% since the June report while the underlying story is unchanged, which compresses forward risk/reward. We maintain HOLD on an income/capital-return basis. The durable bull case is the buyback authorization and dividend; the durable bear case is peak-cycle normalization stacked on catastrophe and regulatory tail risk.

## Company Overview

Allstate is a top-tier U.S. personal-lines P&C insurer operating a multi-channel, multi-product model organized around three reporting areas:

- **Allstate Protection** (~97% of underwriting): personal auto and homeowners sold via exclusive agents, independent agents (National General), and direct. As of Q1 2026, 25.8M auto policies, 7.7M homeowners policies, and 38.6M total policies in force (up 2.3% YoY). Auto premiums earned run ~$9.5B/quarter; homeowners ~$4.2B/quarter.
- **Protection Services**: SquareTrade protection plans, Roadside, Dealer Services, Arity telematics, and Identity Protection — 173M policies in force, higher-growth but a small profit contributor (~$47M adjusted net income/quarter).
- **Run-off Property-Liability and Corporate/Investments**: legacy asbestos/environmental reserves (~$1.4B net) and an ~$85B investment portfolio (92% investment-grade fixed income, duration extended to 5.7 years).

The company has sharpened its focus, exiting most life insurance in 2021 and divesting Employer Voluntary Benefits and Group Health to Nationwide in 2025, leaving a cleaner P&C-plus-protection-services model. Its competitive differentiation rests on scale, brand, a bundled auto+home+protection offer that mono-line auto peers cannot match, and a broad captive-plus-independent distribution footprint.

## Financial Analysis

The recovery is fully matured. Net income progression: –$1.42B (2022) → –$0.32B (2023) → +$4.55B (2024) → +$10.17B (2025). Q1 2026 net income to common of $2.43B ($9.25 diluted) compared to $566M ($2.11) a year earlier, on revenue up 3.0% to $16.94B.

Underwriting detail:
- Property-Liability combined ratio 82.0 (from 97.4), loss ratio 60.7 (from 76.0).
- Homeowners underwriting income swung to +$685M from a –$451M loss (Q1 2025 hit by the California wildfires); homeowners combined ratio 83.5 vs. 112.3.
- Auto combined ratio 81.9 (from 91.3); auto underwriting income $1.73B.

Balance sheet and capital return are strong. Shareholders' equity grew from ~$17.5B (2022) to $31.6B (Q1 2026). Debt-to-capital is 19.2%, debt-to-equity 23.7%, with $27B of same-week liquidity and only a modest $550M maturity in December 2026, conservative for the business model and appropriate. No rating changes. The company completed a $1.5B buyback in February 2026, authorized a new $4.0B program through February 2028 ($3.64B remaining), raised the quarterly dividend to $1.08 (~1.66% yield), and repurchased 3M shares for $620M in Q1.

The earnings-quality caveat governs the entire assessment. Reserve releases are non-recurring by nature; as the over-reserved 2022–2024 accident years run off, this tailwind fades. Current-year auto severity continues to rise on repair costs, medical inflation, and attorney representation. The true underlying combined ratio is meaningfully above the reported 82.0, and forward earnings should be modeled on that normalized figure plus a mid-cycle (not benign) catastrophe load.

The earnings-surprise record shows four consecutive beats (+44.7%, +43.7%, +46.6%, +78.9%), but this is a symptom of the same dynamic: consensus systematically underestimated how favorably reserve development and cat experience would run during the up-leg of the cycle. It is not a signal of durable structural outperformance.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is modest and structurally constrained. Total PIF grew 2.3% YoY; revenue grew 3.0% in Q1 2026. The estimate-revision picture looks dramatic at first glance — current-quarter consensus rose +36.5% over 90 days, current-year +21.1% — but this is a reserve-release-and-cat-driven earnings revision, not a revenue-inflection story. The next-year current estimate of $26.51 sits *below* the current-year figure of $31.30, and next-year revisions are near flat (+2.7% over 90 days). Consensus itself expects EPS to decline meaningfully as the peak-cycle tailwinds normalize. That falling-forward-earnings shape is the opposite of the setup where large 90-day revisions signal continued re-rating; here they mark the top of a cycle.

The strongest growth signal, the +30%+ 90-day revision, applies to stocks recovering from a trough at cheap multiples. Allstate is at the opposite end: recovering earnings are peaking and forward estimates roll over. We therefore treat the revision velocity as confirmation of cyclical peak, not as a BUY trigger.

Structural growth is capped by prudent geographic retreat. Allstate has effectively stopped writing new home/condo business in Florida, is non-renewing there, and is winding down loss-making reciprocal exchanges (Adirondack/Skylands) in NY/NJ. This protects the combined ratio but forfeits growth in the highest-demand catastrophe states. Protection Services is the genuinely higher-growth segment but remains a small profit contributor. Growth prospects score a 5.0: durable but low-single-digit organic growth with forward earnings set to decline off peak.

## Valuation Assessment

Allstate trades at ~5.7x trailing EPS of $38.06 and roughly 8-9x forward EPS. Against the P&C peer set — TRV 9.0x, HIG 9.0x, CINF 9.6x, ACGL 7.0x, WRB 14.3x, median 9.0x — Allstate is not the outlier its trailing multiple implies. On the forward lens it sits about in line with peers; the deep trailing discount reflects peak-cycle earnings that the market correctly declines to capitalize.

Applying the equity-calibration discipline: a low headline multiple is a value-trap warning absent an identifiable catalyst that shows the market is wrong. Here the "cheapness" is well-understood by the market, not a mispricing. Reserve releases and low catastrophe years are unsustainable, and the peer median already reflects the sector's post-hard-market rerating. The 2025 TSR of 214 exactly matched the peer group TSR of 214, so the recovery was a sector rerating rather than idiosyncratic alpha, which argues against expecting Allstate to re-rate above peers from here.

Analyst targets bracket the situation: mean $254.64, median $252.50, high $319, low $176, against a current price of ~$260. The stock trades slightly above the mean and median targets, and the analyst sentiment trend is tagged "degrading." Per the calibration guardrail, this is not an inflection stock at a cheap multiple where targets act as floors; it is a cyclical trading at a full forward multiple where consensus targets are a reasonable ceiling. The book-value support is real (P/B ~2.3x on $113.52 BVPS), but that multiple already embeds a ~48% trailing ROE that will compress as reserve and cat tailwinds fade. Valuation scores a 5.0: fair, not cheap, on normalized earnings, with limited asymmetric upside after a 34% run to a 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

Allstate's moat is scale, brand, and the bundled auto+home+protection proposition that differentiates it from mono-line direct-auto competitors. Its trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. Against Progressive — the direct-auto growth leader still compounding PIF near 9% — Allstate's combined ratio currently beats Progressive's (82.0 vs. a Progressive figure ticking up toward 86.4), a reversal of the historical pattern. But that reversal is heavily aided by reserve releases and benign cats, so it likely narrows as the cycle softens.

Allstate's own PIF growth of 2.3% trails Progressive materially, and the Florida/NY/NJ retreat means it is ceding share in high-growth catastrophe geographies. The competitive position is defensible and not eroding, but neither is it demonstrably widening; per the calibration note that trajectory matters more than level, we score competitive position 5.5: a stable leader without a clear share-gain or moat-widening thesis, in a Financials sector where BUY calls default to negative alpha.

## Risk Assessment

Catastrophe/climate concentration is the defining structural risk. Modeled 1-in-100 annual aggregate PML is ~$3.1B net of reinsurance; the 2025 California wildfires cost ~$1.07B net (gross ~$3.33B). A single active cat year can erase multiple quarters of the reserve-release benefit that currently flatters results. The company mitigates via a large reinsurance tower (per-occurrence up to $11.5B) plus cat bonds/ILS, and by actively shedding Florida homeowners exposure, but the tail is genuine and only partially priced given how benign 2026 cat experience has been.

Additional risks:
- **Reserve-release fade / loss-cost inflation.** The ~$1B/quarter release is the single largest swing factor in reported earnings; tariffs on auto parts and building materials, labor shortages, medical inflation, and rising attorney representation pressure current-year severity. If current-year picks prove optimistic, releases could reverse to strengthening.
- **Regulatory rate suppression.** Growth in CA, FL, and NJ depends on regulators approving adequate rates; management explicitly warns it "may not be able to grow in certain states without regulatory or legislative reforms."
- **Telematics data litigation.** The Texas AG suit and federal class actions over Arity's driving-behavior data collection touch a core piece of usage-based pricing strategy. Aggregate reasonably-possible loss above accruals is estimated at $0–$153M, plus a DOJ/FIRREA suit against National General.
- **CFO transition.** Jess Merten's departure in late 2025 warrants monitoring, though the handoff appears orderly.

Risk factors score 4.5: the catastrophe concentration plus reserve-fade combination represents material downside that is not fully reflected in a stock at a 52-week high after benign cat experience.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 24.4% (spread +5.1%), and the term structure is in backwardation (29.5% near-term vs. 25.0% at six months), which typically signals an anticipated catalyst: the upcoming quarterly print, where a normalizing combined ratio or reserve-release step-down could disappoint. Put/call skew is balanced (1.0%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.40, so positioning is not outright bearish, but the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.60 to 0.98 alongside the price advance, indicating growing hedging demand into strength. The implied one-month move is ±6.4%. This reinforces that the risk/reward is now two-sided at these levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300–319).** Catastrophe experience stays benign, reserve releases continue longer than expected as 2023–2024 accident years prove even more over-reserved, and rate adequacy holds while loss-cost inflation stays contained. The buyback retires shares near ~5–6% of the ~$67B market cap, book value compounds, and the market extends the ~9x forward multiple. TSR pushes toward the high analyst target of $319.

**Bear Case ($176–200).** An active catastrophe year (the $3.1B PML tail materializes) collides with fading reserve releases and rising current-year auto severity, pushing the normalized combined ratio into the low-90s. Forward EPS declines toward the consensus next-year estimate of $26.51 or below, the market compresses the multiple back toward book-value support, and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $176, roughly 33% below the current price.

**Base Case ($250–270).** Reserve releases taper gradually, cat experience normalizes toward the 10-year average, and the underlying combined ratio settles in the high-80s. EPS declines from the peak but capital return supports per-share book value. The stock trades roughly flat-to-modestly-lower from current levels, delivering total return primarily through the dividend and buyback accretion rather than multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (income/capital-return basis).

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a pullback to book-value-supported levels (roughly $200–215, ~1.8–1.9x book) that restores asymmetric upside; (2) evidence that current-year (not prior-year) underwriting margins are structurally improving — an underlying combined ratio holding in the low-80s excluding reserve releases across two consecutive quarters; (3) meaningful open-market insider buying, currently absent.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) reserve development turning from release to strengthening; (2) a normalized combined ratio deteriorating toward the low-to-mid-90s on rising auto severity; (3) an adverse ruling in the Arity telematics litigation that impairs usage-based pricing, or a rate-suppression action in a major state; (4) a cut or suspension of the buyback authorization.
- **Reassessment timeline:** the next quarterly earnings release is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to the reserve-development line, the current-year vs. reported combined-ratio gap, and catastrophe losses relative to the 8.4-point 10-year Q1 average.

## Conclusion

Allstate is a financially healthy, well-capitalized P&C leader delivering peak-cycle earnings and returning capital aggressively. The optical ~5.7x trailing multiple is cheap for identifiable reasons: reported profitability is flattered by non-recurring reserve releases and a benign catastrophe year, and the recovery has been a sector-wide rerating (2025 TSR merely matched the peer group) rather than idiosyncratic alpha. On the forward lens, the stock sits roughly in line with peers, with forward consensus EPS already set to decline off peak.

After a 34% run to a 52-week high, the risk/reward is two-sided. The durable support is capital return — the remaining buyback and dividend, backed by $8B of statutory dividend capacity — which justifies holding for income and per-share book-value accretion. The durable risks are catastrophe/climate concentration and the fade of reserve-release and cat tailwinds. We rate the shares HOLD on an income basis, with a preference to add only on a pullback toward book-value support or on confirmed improvement in underlying (reserve-release-adjusted) underwriting margins.
