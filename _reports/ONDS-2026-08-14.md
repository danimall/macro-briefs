## Executive Summary

Ondas is no longer the industrial wireless company its own annual report describes. Between January 2025 and August 2026 it closed roughly a dozen acquisitions — Sentrycs, Robo-Team, 4M Defense, Apeiro Motion, SPO, Insight, Rotron, World View, Mistral, Omnisys, DZYNE ($879.0M, closed July 6, 2026) and Cyberhawk (closed August 10, 2026) — and on January 16, 2026 deconsolidated Ondas Networks, the founding business, booking a ~$51.5M gain. Revenue went from $7.19M (FY2024) to $50.73M (FY2025) to $133.89M in the first half of 2026 alone, and management has raised FY2026 guidance twice, most recently to $525-550M on August 13.

The operating traction is real and independently anchored in awards rather than narrative: cumulative captured task orders under the U.S. Army's $982M Lethal Unmanned Systems IDIQ exceed $240M following the >$50M August 5 order to Mistral; the NASA IDIQ ceiling was raised from $45M to $395M; Sentrycs' Cyber-over-RF counter-UAS is being integrated into Lockheed Martin's Sanctum platform; backlog is $613M at June 30, $757M pro forma. Liquidity is strong: $1,393.0M of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments at June 30, roughly $1,071M pro forma for the DZYNE and Cyberhawk cash consideration, against $2.95M of short-term debt at the prior year-end. Solvency is not the issue here, and any screen that flags Ondas as a distress candidate is being driven by a $368M historical accumulated deficit and a $1,043.7M non-cash, share-settled warrant liability rather than by any claim on cash.

The issue is what accrues to a share. Shares outstanding went from 380.8M at December 31 to 529.8M at June 30 to roughly 570.4M today, with 44,999,998 more contractually due to DZYNE holders on January 4, 2027 and 196.3M warrants outstanding at a weighted-average $24.91 strike. Q2 stock compensation was $69.1M against $83.8M of revenue, with $404M of unrecognized award expense still to come. Operating cash burn accelerated to $137.4M in 1H26 from $15.1M a year earlier. Revenue grew roughly tenfold in eighteen months and the stock is essentially flat year-to-date. The FY2026 guide implies a Q4 of $251-261M at any point in the range — about 47.6% of the full year and 3.06x Q2 actual. We are moving from AVOID to REDUCE because the price is 21% lower than at our June review while guidance, backlog and awards have all improved, and because $1.07B of net cash removes the solvency tail. But at 7.8x EV to FY2026E revenue with no economic profitability path visible and a dilution pipeline that has repeatedly absorbed operating progress, the risk/reward remains unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Ondas now operates as an acquisition-assembled defense autonomy platform across three arenas. Loitering munitions and lethal unmanned systems, via Mistral's HERO series, is the growth engine and the source of the Army LUS program position. Counter-UAS runs through Sentrycs' Cyber-over-RF technology, differentiated from kinetic and jamming approaches and validated by the Lockheed Sanctum integration. Commercial and industrial autonomy — the Optimus drone-in-a-box, Iron Drone Raider interceptor and now Cyberhawk's AI infrastructure inspection — is the smallest and slowest arena; the FY2025 10-K itself calls the commercial UAS market "nascent" and ties progress to FAA BVLOS rulemaking of "uncertain" timing. DZYNE (multi-domain ISR and precision strike) and World View (stratospheric platforms) sit in a newly formed "Ondas Sentinel" unit.

The intellectual property base is thin relative to a $5.2B market capitalization: 13 issued U.S. patents and 24 issued international patents (plus 10 international pending applications) in the autonomous systems segment. The durable assets are program incumbency and qualification status on fielded systems, plus an Israeli engineering and manufacturing base with combat-proven products. That is a real advantage, but it is a contract position subject to recompete rather than a patent moat.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (audited).** Revenue $50.731M against $7.193M; gross profit $20.156M (39.7% margin vs 4.8%); R&D $20.879M; SG&A $44.474M; operating loss −$58.384M; net loss −$133.381M. The gap between operating and net loss is an $82.2M non-cash warrant fair-value loss. Adjusted EBITDA was −$31.3M. Operating cash flow −$38.746M on $2.034M of capex. The margin swing from 4.8% to 39.7% is predominantly mix change from acquired defense hardware replacing an under-absorbed legacy business, not operating leverage on a common base.

**1H 2026.** Q1 revenue $50.1M, Q2 $83.8M, 1H $133.894M. Q2 GAAP gross margin 43.1%, down from 49.2% in Q1 and 53.1% in Q2 2025, absorbing ~$4.7M of acquired-intangible amortization and ~$1.4M of stock comp in COGS; management's adjusted gross margin was ~50.4% versus ~51.5% in Q1. Operating loss −$205.6M. Adjusted EBITDA −$61.5M, of which −$50.6M in Q2. Operating cash outflow −$137.4M against −$15.1M in 1H25.

Reported 1H26 net income was **+$271.6M**, and this number is worse than useless — it moves inversely to shareholder outcomes. The bridge from a −$205.6M operating loss runs through a ~$404.8M warrant fair-value gain, a ~$51.5M deconsolidation gain and a ~$28.8M non-cash tax benefit, less a ~$46.2M day-one loss on the Indo-Earth transaction. The warrant gain exists because the share price fell from the January 2026 offering price of $16.45 toward $9-10. Any model keyed to GAAP EPS produces its most flattering answer precisely when holders have lost the most. The trailing and forward P/E of 19.14 carried in the market-data feed is internally inconsistent with the reported EPS of −$0.62 and should not be used.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** At June 30: cash $657.9M, restricted $8.5M, short-term investments $726.6M, total $1,393.0M. Goodwill $661.4M, intangibles $583.3M, contingent consideration $132.4M, warrant liability $1,043.7M, equity $1,575.6M. Deducting the ~$322.3M of DZYNE and Cyberhawk cash consideration leaves roughly $1,071M; if Q3 burn approximates Q2's (most of the 1H's $137.4M outflow occurred in Q2), pro forma liquidity is nearer $950M-$1.0B. That is several years of runway at current burn without returning to market, and it is the strongest single fact in the bull case.

**Working capital and revenue quality.** AR rose $22.4M → $72.2M, inventory $22.0M → $52.0M, contract assets $3.2M → $45.2M. AR plus contract assets of $117.4M equals roughly 127 days of Q2 revenue. Over-time revenue recognized in 1H26 was ~$43.7M, essentially equal to the entire contract-asset balance, meaning nearly all over-time revenue is unbilled. Roughly $17.7M of the contract-asset increase arrived via acquisition, so this is not clean same-store deterioration — treat it as a monitoring item rather than evidence of collection stress. Cash taxes paid in 1H26 were approximately zero.

**Stock compensation is the dominant economic cost the adjusted numbers exclude.** Q2 SBC of $69.1M equals 82% of Q2 revenue; 1H total $88.8M. Unrecognized RSU expense is $302.2M over ~2.32 years and unrecognized option expense $101.7M over ~2.55 years, roughly $404M committed. Management's target of adjusted EBITDA profitability for OAS and Sentinel by Q4 2026 and company-wide by Q4 2027 excludes an expense stream running above $200M annualized. Excluding SBC is standard practice; at this ratio to revenue, adjusted breakeven and economic breakeven are years apart.

Financial health scores 5.2: fortress liquidity and negligible funded debt against accelerating cash burn, no demonstrated path to positive free cash flow, ~$2.05B of pro forma goodwill and intangibles exceeding the December equity base, and $132.4M of contingent consideration as a conditional cash claim.

## Growth Analysis

The headline trajectory is extraordinary: $7.2M → $50.7M → guided $525-550M, with FY2027 consensus near $990M. Program evidence is concrete: >$240M captured under the $982M LUS IDIQ, including the >$50M August 5 task order; NASA IDIQ ceiling raised to $395M; the Lockheed Sanctum integration.

Three qualifications materially discount that growth.

First, **the FY2026 guide is concentrated in one unproven quarter.** 1H actual $133.894M plus the Q3 guide of $140-155M leaves an implied Q4 of $251.1M at the low end of the range and $261.1M at the high end — a range of just $10M regardless of where results fall, about 47.6% of the year, 3.06x Q2 actual and 1.74x the Q3 midpoint. Required 2H revenue is $403.6M, or 53.3% of the entire $757M pro forma backlog converted within six months. Part of the step-up is mechanical and benign: DZYNE closed July 6 and Cyberhawk August 10, so Q4 is the first quarter with both fully consolidated, and defense hardware deliveries are genuinely Q4-weighted around government fiscal-year timing. Neither factor obviously explains a 74% sequential jump from an already acquisition-complete Q3 base, and the company has not disclosed a delivery schedule that would resolve it.

Second, **the organic component is unverifiable.** Management's ~85% "pro forma organic" growth claim cannot be reconciled from the filings; the ASC 805 pro forma note ($84.6M Q2 2026 vs $29.7M Q2 2025) retroactively includes only the 2026 acquisitions, not the 2025 cohort, so it is not a same-portfolio comparison. Backlog is a company-defined, unreconciled measure with no disclosed cancellation terms, funding status or conversion period, and cannot be tied to deferred revenue of $29.7M or to any RPO schedule. An IDIQ ceiling is not backlog; only the >$240M of captured task orders represents awards.

Third, **the estimate-revision signal is contaminated.** Current-year consensus EPS moved from −$0.18 to +$0.27 over 90 days, a +247% revision that would normally be a powerful positive. That swing is the ASC 815 warrant mark, not operating improvement. The next-year estimate is −$0.04 and was cut 33% over 30 days. The revision pattern therefore does not support a growth-inflection buy case; it reflects an accounting artifact. The Q2 EPS "beat" (−$0.03 versus −$0.09) sits on the same contaminated base, and the four-quarter record is one beat against three misses.

Growth prospects score 6.5 — high absolute trajectory with real program anchors, discounted for the acquisition-sourced composition, the single-quarter concentration of the guide and the unverifiable organic split.

## Valuation Assessment

Reconciling the capitalization: 529.8M shares at June 30 plus ~40.0M issued at DZYNE closing and ~0.6M at Cyberhawk closing gives roughly 570.4M shares. At $9.22 that is a market capitalization of about $5,259M. Deducting ~$1,071M of pro forma net cash gives an enterprise value near **$4,188M**.

- **7.8x** EV to the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $537.5M
- **4.2x** EV to FY2027 consensus revenue of $990M
- **~24x** EV to approximate trailing-twelve-month revenue

There is no earnings-based anchor. Management's own breakeven target is Q4 2027 on a measure that excludes >$200M annualized SBC. Third-party figures of "EV/Revenue of 40" and "forward P/E of 64" do not reconcile to the filed share count, cash balance or any disclosed earnings base and should be disregarded.

On revenue multiples alone, 4.2x forward sales for a company growing at this rate is not extreme within the defense-autonomy cohort. The multiple compresses only if FY2027 revenue lands, and FY2027 depends on a Q4 2026 print with no historical precedent, while the denominator of value per share keeps rising. Shares grew 50% in eight months. Another 45.0M are contractually due on January 4, 2027, +7.89% of existing shares, or 7.3% ownership dilution post-issuance. The 196,256,760 warrants at a weighted-average $24.91 strike (121.58M struck at $28.00, ~6.44 years remaining) are a different instrument and should not be summed with the DZYNE tranche: at $9.22 the $28.00 block requires a ~204% advance to reach strike, and full exercise would bring in roughly $4.9B of cash while raising the count to ~767M shares (25.6% ownership dilution). That block caps upside participation rather than threatening the balance sheet. Separately, ~116.8M contingently issuable shares plus 33.2M unvested RSUs and 27.9M options were excluded from diluted EPS; those conditions are not itemized and may partially overlap with the DZYNE tranche, so no sum of these buckets constitutes a fully diluted count.

Scenario framework, using EV/forward-sales on a plausible share count:

- **Bear $4.50 (−51%):** Q4 lands near $180M, FY2026 at ~$455M, FY2027 revised toward $750M, multiple compresses to ~2.5x forward sales, plus impairment risk against ~$2.05B of pro forma soft assets. 52-week low is $3.20.
- **Base $8.00 (−13%):** Q4 comes in short of the implied requirement at ~$200-230M, FY2027 lands $850-950M, market applies ~4.0x EV/forward sales on ~$620-630M shares.
- **Bull $14.50 (+57%):** Q4 delivers $251M+, FY2026 hits the guide, FY2027 reaches $1.05B, adjusted EBITDA breakeven is confirmed, and the market pays ~8x forward sales — roughly the January 2026 high of $15.28, still well below the $19 consensus.

At 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear, probability-weighted value is approximately **$8.58, about 7% below spot.** Sell-side positioning is uniformly positive (9 Buy/Strong Buy, zero Holds, mean target $19.06, low $13.00, high $25.00), but consensus targets on a pre-profit roll-up trading above 7x current-year sales are not independent confirmation — they embed the same FY2027 revenue extrapolation the stock does. Valuation scores 4.0: the multiple is not absurd, but there is no margin of safety and the cheap-looking forward multiple is contingent on the least-verified number in the model.

## Competitive Landscape

In lethal unmanned systems, the scale reference is AeroVironment, which reported Q4 FY2026 revenue of $641.6M with adjusted EPS of $1.84 and guided FY2027 to $2.13-2.23B — roughly 4x Ondas's FY2026 target, profitably scaled. Anduril and other well-funded private entrants compete for the same programs. Ondas's advantage is being on contract now; the open question is share retention at recompete against larger, cash-generative incumbents.

Counter-UAS is fragmented and heavily funded, but the Lockheed Sanctum integration is credible third-party validation. Sanctum is Lockheed's platform and Ondas is a capability supplier, not the platform owner. The same precision applies to Palantir: Ondas is the **paying customer**, having deployed Foundry under a June 2026 agreement with an initial term to February 2027. Q2 software costs rose $26.7M year over year in G&A (+$41.6M for 1H), and ~$26.2M of Q2 cash operating expense is attributed to the WarpSpeed and SkyWeaver initiatives and market-development activity with Palantir. That is roughly a third of Q2 revenue spent with one vendor, with no associated revenue separately disclosed, and it is a direct drag on the adjusted EBITDA breakeven targets.

Two mix facts complicate the "U.S. defense platform" framing. Q2 revenue by geography was Israel $37.5M (~45%), Europe $19.4M (~23%), North America $17.3M (~21%) — the LUS program dominates news flow while North America was a fifth of the quarter. And customer concentration worsened: "Customer A" was 40% of Q2 revenue against 19% in Q2 2025, and 37% of 1H26 against 28%. Receivable concentration improved (43% of AR versus 73% in FY2025), which is a collection-timing positive, but the two measures establish different things and should not be conflated into a diversification claim.

Trajectory is nonetheless improving: task orders are being captured, the NASA ceiling was raised, and the portfolio spans counter-UAS, loitering munitions, ISR and stratospheric platforms at a moment when multi-front demand is structurally confirmed. Competitive position scores 5.6.

## Risk Assessment

**Guidance binary (dominant).** The implied Q4 of $251-261M is 3.06x Q2 with no precedent. A miss reprices both FY2026 and the FY2027 consensus that follows from it.

**Dilution mechanics.** Share count +50% in eight months; 45.0M shares due January 4, 2027; roughly twenty separate Item 3.02 unregistered-issuance 8-Ks filed between April 29 and August 10, consistent with continuous warrant exercise and acquisition issuance. In a market where aggregate equity supply is rising and the long end is pressured, a serial issuer faces a less accommodating window than it did in January.

**Cost of the January financing.** ~$1.0B gross was raised at $16.45 per unit with 121.58M warrants struck at $28.00 attached; the warrants were initially fair-valued at ~$1,194.0M, exceeding proceeds allocated and producing a ~$234.9M day-one loss. The accounting is almost certainly correct under ASC 815; the reading it supports is that the effective cost of that capital was well above the headline.

**Purchase-accounting and impairment exposure.** Pro forma goodwill of ~$1.15B and intangibles of ~$0.9B total roughly $2.05B — about 4.5x the $251.8M quantified in the operative risk factors and larger than the December equity base. The Indo-Earth transaction is instructive: ~$117.4M of consideration ($5.7M cash, 2.44M shares, $84.2M contingent) for a single $92.5M customer relationship, treated as an asset acquisition consolidated as a VIE, with a $46.2M day-one loss. That loss is largely mechanical for a non-business asset acquisition, and I do not infer overpayment from the accounting; what the disclosure establishes is that a meaningful piece of the growth story was purchased as a contract right, with an earn-out extending to $140M plus 3.05M milestone shares.

**Disclosure quality.** The Q2 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors, yet Item 1A quantifies goodwill at $251.8M, cites 800M authorized shares against the 1.2B now on the balance sheet, retains a dedicated IEEE 802.16t risk factor and names Ondas Networks executives as key-person dependencies — despite the segment having been deconsolidated on January 16, 2026, more than two months before the 10-K was filed — and contains no treatment of the $1,043.7M warrant liability, the largest item on the balance sheet. The most probable explanation is carry-over under extreme filing load rather than intent to mislead. Either way it raises the base rate for future purchase-accounting revisions or control deficiencies across a dozen integrations, two of them ($879M and ~$125M) closing within five weeks. No material weakness has been disclosed.

**Israel and export control.** ~45% of Q2 revenue, with dependence on the Israeli Defense Export Controls Agency and explicit war-escalation language in the risk factors.

**Governance.** Nevada incorporation, two-thirds vote to remove a director, exclusive Clark County forum, and 5,000,000 blank-check preferred shares issuable without shareholder approval.

Litigation is genuinely clean as of the March 2026 filing — no material proceeding or governmental investigation, which is not a given in this sector. Risk factors score 3.0: the combination of a single-quarter revenue binary, worsening customer concentration, ~$2.05B of impairment-exposed soft assets, a stale primary risk disclosure and an open-ended issuance program is not adequately reflected in a consensus with zero Hold ratings.

### Options Market Signal

The headline 138.4% near-term ATM IV sits on a one-day expiry with a ±3.3% implied move, which reflects expiry mechanics rather than a stress signal. The informative tenors are the one-month at 84.1% (±13.3%) and three-month at 83.0% (±22.7%), both **below** the 116.9% one-year realized volatility, so the market is not pricing an imminent dislocation and near-dated optionality is not expensive relative to what this stock actually delivers. Since the June snapshot, ATM IV rose 21 points while the price fell 20.9%, with the OI ratio essentially unchanged at 0.47.

Combined with 41.5% short interest and just 2.2 days to cover, that configuration is the practical argument against expressing this view through a short. Speculative call demand layered on a heavily shorted float with live award catalysts (further LUS task orders, NASA task awards, additional M&A) is squeeze fuel. The fundamental read here is unfavorable risk/reward for owners, not a tradable short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($14.50, +57%).** Q4 delivers $251M+ as DZYNE and Cyberhawk consolidate fully and Army task orders convert on schedule; FY2026 hits the $525-550M guide; FY2027 reaches $1.05B; OAS and Sentinel confirm adjusted EBITDA profitability in Q4 2026, validating the Q4 2027 company-wide target. Ondas ends up the scaled Western alternative to Anduril in loitering munitions and counter-UAS, with ~$1B of net cash that funds the ramp without further primary issuance. The market pays ~8x forward sales for a 60%+ grower with confirmed program incumbency, and the 45M January share tranche is absorbed. Optionality: strategic interest from a larger prime, which sell-side speculation has floated but no filing supports.

**Bear case ($4.50, −51%).** Q4 lands nearer $180-200M because the ramp was always a backlog-conversion assumption rather than a delivery schedule; FY2027 consensus resets from $990M toward $750M; the forward multiple compresses to ~2.5x. Simultaneously, integration strain across a dozen acquisitions surfaces a material weakness or purchase-accounting revision, and any program slippage triggers impairment testing against ~$2.05B of soft assets. Burn continues above $250M annualized against $200M+ of SBC, so the cash cushion shrinks while the share count keeps rising, the same dynamic that produced a flat stock on tenfold revenue growth. Customer A's 40% share or the 45% Israel exposure delivers a discrete shock.

**Base case ($8.00, −13%).** The company grows fast and misses the Q4 requirement without breaking. FY2026 lands ~$480-510M, FY2027 $850-950M, gross margins hold near 50% adjusted, and adjusted EBITDA breakeven slips a quarter or two. The market keeps paying ~4x forward sales while the share count grinds from 570M toward 620-630M. Enterprise value roughly holds and per-share value drifts lower.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Probability-weighted value of ~$8.58 sits about 7% below the $9.22 basis, with 51% downside to bear against 57% upside to bull on a business whose central forecast has no historical precedent.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength on award headlines rather than into weakness; the November Q3 print (which will show whether Q3 landed inside the $140-155M guide and whether management reaffirms a Q4 of $251M+) is the natural decision point. Investors who wish to retain optionality on the LUS franchise should hold no more than a starter position and expect the January 4, 2027 issuance of 45.0M shares as a known supply event. Do not attempt the short side: 41.5% short interest, 2.2 days to cover and call-heavy options positioning make the mechanics adverse regardless of the fundamental view.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD or better).**
1. Q3 revenue at or above $150M **with** Q4 guidance reaffirmed at $251M+ and a disclosed delivery or funding schedule supporting it.
2. Confirmed adjusted EBITDA profitability for OAS plus Sentinel in Q4 2026, accompanied by quarterly operating cash burn narrowing below $50M.
3. Evidence the dilution treadmill is slowing — a quarter with no Item 3.02 issuance beyond the contractual DZYNE tranche, or SBC falling below 25% of revenue.
4. Price near $6.00-6.50, which would put the shares at roughly 2.8x FY2027 consensus revenue and restore genuine margin of safety against the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (back to AVOID).**
1. Q4 2026 revenue below $200M, or any downward revision to the $525-550M FY2026 target.
2. Disclosure of a material weakness in internal control, a purchase-accounting revision, or any goodwill/intangible impairment against the ~$2.05B soft-asset base.
3. A new primary equity raise while cash exceeds $700M, which would signal burn is outrunning the plan.
4. Loss of a Customer A program, a step-down in Israeli export authorization, or LUS task-order flow stalling for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (early-to-mid November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint; the January 4, 2027 share issuance and the FY2026 10-K, which should finally refresh Item 1A to describe the actual company, are the following two.

## Conclusion

Ondas has done something genuinely difficult: assembled, in eighteen months, a defense autonomy portfolio with fielded products, a U.S. Army program position worth >$240M of captured task orders, a Lockheed integration, and roughly $1.07B of net cash against negligible funded debt. The prior AVOID rating overweighted solvency-flavored concerns that the balance sheet does not support, and the shares are 21% cheaper than at our June review while guidance, backlog and awards have all improved. That combination warrants an upgrade to REDUCE.

It does not warrant ownership. Every dollar of operating progress so far has been offset by issuance: shares up 50% in eight months with 45.0M more contractually due, stock compensation running at 82% of quarterly revenue with $404M still unrecognized, and a $1B financing whose attached warrants the auditors valued above the proceeds. The entire FY2026 outcome, and the FY2027 consensus that flows from it, rests on a Q4 that must triple Q2 with no disclosed delivery schedule to support it. Consensus carries nine Buys, zero Holds and a $19 mean target; 41.5% of the float is short. Both sides of that standoff are levered to one quarterly print, and the equity offers no margin of safety while it resolves. Reduce exposure and revisit on the November report or near $6.00-6.50.
