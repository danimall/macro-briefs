**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reliance is the largest metals service center company in North America, a diversified distributor and first-stage processor rather than a steel producer. It buys metal from domestic mills, holds inventory, performs light value-added processing, and delivers small orders fast to ~125,000 customers across ~311 locations. That model produces structurally more stable margins than a mill would earn, and it has allowed Reliance to gain share every year through a deeply cyclical industry. In FY2025 and Q1 2026, tons sold outperformed the MSCI industry benchmark by roughly 7-8 percentage points even as the broader industry contracted.

Q1 2026 confirmed an earnings inflection that has been building since the 2023-2024 cyclical trough. Revenue rose 15.5% YoY, EPS rose 36% to $5.10, and operating margin expanded 120 bps to 9.1%, driven primarily by Section 232 tariffs (raised to 50% in June 2025) that are propping up domestic metal prices — aluminum average selling price per ton rose 23%, carbon steel 13%. Current-year consensus EPS has risen 9.6% over 90 days, next-year 10.4%. The FY2025 headline EPS decline of 10% was largely a LIFO accounting artifact ($258M swing from LIFO income to LIFO expense); on a FIFO basis, gross margin rose 80 bps and underlying profitability improved ~14.5%.

The core tension is valuation against a policy-dependent driver. The stock trades near its 52-week high at ~26x trailing and ~19.7x forward earnings, having risen ~20% over the trailing year and ~43% off its October 2025 low. The single largest earnings swing factor — Section 232 tariffs — is legally intact for now (the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling explicitly does not touch Section 232) but sits outside management's control. Management warns that any removal or relaxation would materially hurt results. Combined with a full multiple, insider trimming into the rally, and a structurally negative-alpha sector, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate RS a HOLD (growth_hold) on the durable share-gain franchise and rising estimates, and would look to accumulate on a pullback that restores margin of safety against the tariff-reversal tail.

## Company Overview

Reliance operates the largest metals service center network in North America, generating $14.29B in FY2025 revenue across ~311 locations in 41 U.S. states and 10 foreign countries, under roughly 80 decentralized trade names (Earle M. Jorgensen, Metals USA, Precision Strip, Phoenix Metals, AMI Metals, among others). Founded in 1939 and public since 1994, the company sits in a single reportable segment: metals service centers.

The business is a distributor and first-stage processor, not a mill. It buys over 100,000 products (carbon steel, aluminum, stainless, alloy, copper/brass, titanium) primarily from domestic mills, holds inventory, performs light processing (cutting, leveling, sawing, some machining and electropolishing), and delivers to its customers. The defining competitive attributes are extreme diversification and speed: the largest customer is just 0.6% of sales, only 25 customers exceed $30M, over 90% of orders are repeat business, the average order size is ~$3,120, and ~40% of orders are delivered within 24 hours via a ~1,800-truck fleet.

Value-added processing now accounts for ~50% of orders, up from ~40% a decade ago, and management credits this shift for structurally higher gross margins (~28-30% now vs. below 27% historically). Reliance holds roughly 17% of U.S. service-center tons in 2025 (up from ~15% in 2024) against ~8,400 competitor companies — a fragmented industry that management frames as a long runway for both organic share gains and M&A (76 businesses acquired since 1994). Pricing is spot-market with minimal long-term contracts, which lets the company pass through mill cost changes quickly.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a cyclical trough in 2023-2024 followed by a sharp 2026 inflection.

| Revenue | 17,025 | 14,806 | 13,835 | 14,294 | 4,026 (+15.5%) |
| Operating income | 2,507 | 1,740 | 1,160 | 1,013 | 368 (+34%) |
| Net income | ~1,844 | 1,336 | 875 | 739 | 265 (+33%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | $22.64 | $15.56 | $13.98 | $5.10 (+36%) |
| Operating margin | 14.7% | 11.7% | 8.4% | 7.1% | 9.1% |

FY2025 revenue rose 3.3% on record tons sold (+6.2%), which offset a 2.6% decline in price per ton. Reported EPS fell 10%, but the driver was a $258M non-cash swing in LIFO valuation — from $144.4M of LIFO income in 2024 to $113.7M of LIFO expense in 2025 — as metal prices turned up. The standardized "declining earnings" read understates the underlying trajectory, which Q1 2026's EPS confirms.

Q1 2026 showed pricing power returning: average selling price per ton rose 12.6% while tons still grew 2.7% against an industry decline of 5.1%. The mechanism is direct: Section 232 tariffs at 50% → higher domestic metal prices → higher revenue per ton passed through Reliance's spot-pricing model → margin expansion, provided the company can price ahead of its inventory cost. In H2 2025, aluminum-specific demand softness in aerospace and semiconductor pressured that pass-through; Q1 2026 saw it resolve favorably.

Cash flow reflects normal cyclical dynamics. FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $831M (from $1.43B in 2024) as rising prices and volume consumed working capital — a mechanical drag when metal prices rise. Free cash flow was $502M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $151M. This working-capital consumption will continue to dampen FCF conversion if metal prices keep climbing.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Altman Z of 4.4 (safe zone), current ratio 4.88, D/E 0.45. Net debt/total capital was 16.9% at Q1 2026 (up from 10.2% at year-end 2024 as the company levered modestly for buybacks), against a 60% covenant maximum — substantial headroom. Total debt is $1.7B (revolver $550M, term loan $400M, 2.15% notes $500M, 6.85% notes $250M), investment-grade rated, with interest expense of $55.7M annually. Goodwill of $2.17B (~21% of assets) plus $0.96B of intangibles reflect the acquisition history; there has never been a goodwill impairment, only modest indefinite-lived intangible write-downs ($9.9M in 2025).

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly. FY2025 returned $848.8M ($594M buybacks, $255M dividends), with share count down ~4% annually (from 60.6M in 2022 to ~51.0M in Q1 2026). The dividend track record is strong: 67 consecutive years of dividends, 33 increases since 1994, never cut, most recently raised 4.2% to $1.25/quarter in 2026. Roughly $529M of buyback authorization remains.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: the most recent quarter beat by 7.3%, but the prior quarter missed by 17.8%. This confirms that quarterly visibility is limited when the dominant driver (metals prices) swings sharply. The estimate revisions are the stronger forward signal — broadly rising across all periods — but the magnitude (+9.6% current-year, +10.4% next-year over 90 days) sits below the +30% inflection threshold that would flag a systematically underestimated turn.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two components, and separating them is essential to the thesis.

The durable, non-tariff component is share capture. Reliance beat MSCI industry shipments by 7-8 percentage points in both FY2025 and Q1 2026, during an industry contraction. Management attributes this to scale, product and geographic diversity, next-day delivery, and value-added processing, reinforced by a customer shift toward smaller, more frequent orders amid trade-policy uncertainty — a shift that plays directly to Reliance's operating model. This share gain is structural and would persist regardless of tariff policy. The demand tailwind management called out in Q1 2026 — data centers and infrastructure — connects to the broader capex cycle and adds a secular leg that is not tariff-dependent.

The larger, more volatile component is tariff-supported pricing. The 12.6% average selling price increase drove the bulk of the revenue and margin gain, and that increase traces directly to Section 232. This is real earnings, but it is policy-contingent earnings: the same driver that supercharged Q1 2026 could reverse if tariffs are removed or relaxed, which management explicitly identifies as a material risk.

The estimate revision trend is positive across all periods: current quarter +8.3% over 90 days, next quarter +12.2%. The trend is unambiguously positive but not at the velocity that signals a systematically mispriced turn. Applied against the sector caution — Materials is a structurally negative-alpha group — the growth thesis is credible but needs to clear a higher bar of company-specific outperformance, which the share-gain data partially provides. M&A is dry powder: no acquisitions in 2025 (versus four in 2024), and the fortress balance sheet gives ample capacity to resume the accretive roll-up strategy that has compounded for three decades.

RS trades at ~$395.93, ~28x trailing EPS and ~19.7x the current-year consensus of $20.11. The forward multiple looks reasonable and sits at a discount to the steel peer median P/E of 29.9.

The peer comparison is heterogeneous — CLF is loss-making, HCC is a coal name, and the dispersion is wide. Adjusting for quality, Reliance deserves a premium to CMC and WS given its diversification, margin stability, and capital-return record, and the ~19.7x forward multiple against ~+10% forward EPS growth (PEG ~2) is fair rather than cheap.

The reason not to reward the current price is the composition of the earnings that support it. A meaningful portion of the FY2026 earnings power reflects tariff-supported pricing that is legally intact but policy-dependent. If Section 232 were relaxed, imports would rise, domestic prices would fall, and the FY2026 EPS base against which the multiple is applied would compress — meaning the "reasonable" forward P/E is being computed on peak-of-policy earnings. This is the specific reason to resist treating analyst targets as floors here: the estimate base itself carries a reversibility tail that the multiple does not discount.

The analyst target set is tight around the current price — mean $386.25, median $379.50, high $425, low $350 — consistent with a stock that has already re-rated on the inflection and where consensus sees limited further upside. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down, but the combination of a full multiple, a policy-contingent earnings base, and a structurally negative-alpha sector supports a valuation score in the mid-4s. We treat the discount-to-peer-median cheapness signal with skepticism — the discount is largely warranted by the earnings-quality question, not evidence of mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Reliance's competitive position is stable-to-improving, which matters more than absolute market share. The company is the clear #1 in a fragmented industry (~8,400 competitors, ~10,700 locations) and is taking share consistently — a rising trajectory rather than a leader defending eroding ground.

The moat is structural and hard to replicate: scale in purchasing, geographic and product diversity, a delivery fleet enabling next-day service, and value-added processing that now spans half of all orders. Management notes that smaller competitors "do not have the financial ability or risk tolerance" to replicate the small-order, fast-delivery, capital-intensive model. The customer shift toward smaller, more frequent orders under trade-policy uncertainty favors Reliance's operating model over competitors carrying less inventory and less processing capability.

The service-center position also insulates the company from the carbon-intensity and capacity-cycle risks that afflict mills. Reliance passes through cost changes rather than bearing them, turns inventory quickly, and reintroduced ~259,000 tons of recycled scrap in 2025, positioning as a low-emission distributor. Substitution risk (lighter materials, composites, aluminum-for-steel in autos) is a genuine long-term headwind but slow-moving and partially offset by the company's presence in aluminum. Share gains across a contracting industry are the clearest evidence that the competitive position is widening.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff reversibility is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** The 10-K is explicit that removal or relaxation of Section 232 tariffs would raise imports, lower U.S. metal prices, and materially hurt revenue, results, and cash flows. An investor is partly underwriting the durability of U.S. metals tariff policy, which is outside management's control and subject to political and legal challenge.

**Metals price volatility outweighs demand as an earnings driver.** Management states pricing has "a much more significant impact than customer demand." When prices fall, high-cost inventory can't be repriced fast enough (margin squeeze); when prices rise on non-demand factors like tariffs, full pass-through is not guaranteed — exactly the dynamic that pressured aluminum margins in H2 2025. The prior-quarter earnings miss (-17.8%) illustrates how quickly quarterly results can swing.

**End-market cyclicality** across non-residential construction (largest by tons), aerospace, semiconductor, energy, automotive, and heavy equipment. Aluminum demand was soft in commercial aerospace and semiconductor in 2025.

**Governance watch items.** Multiple Item 5.02 (officer/director) 8-Ks in October 2025 (Oct 2 and Oct 22) plus prior transitions — details were not retrievable, but no restatement or adverse event is evident and disclosure controls were deemed effective. Insider activity is routine post-vesting selling (CFO ~$1.25M in April at ~$358, COO ~$7.7M in February at ~$321, GC ~$2M in March), with zero open-market purchases despite the strong quarter — mildly relevant that insiders are trimming into a policy-dependent rally, though not a bearish signal on its own given the record-results context.

**Acquisition and goodwill risk.** 76 deals done, $2.17B goodwill, no impairment ever, but carrying value depends on continued performance. Variable-rate debt of ~$677M is minor given low leverage.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 35.0% sits rich versus 26.5% one-year historical volatility (an 8.5 pp spread), in the 66th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (35.0% near-term vs. 28.7% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst — most likely the Q2 2026 earnings print. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.1% near-term, signaling some downside hedging demand, though the P/C open-interest ratio of 0.20 is low overall. ATM IV rose 8.7 pp since early June on essentially flat price. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±8.1%. This corroborates our view that the risk/reward here is balanced: the market has re-rated the stock on the inflection but is hedging against a disappointing quarter or a tariff-policy headline. Short interest is negligible at 2.6% of float (2.4 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($460-490):** Section 232 tariffs remain in place through 2026-2027, sustaining elevated domestic metal prices and Reliance's pricing power. Share gains continue at 7-8 pts above industry shipments, data-center and infrastructure demand accelerates, and the company resumes accretive M&A with its fortress balance sheet. FY2026 EPS reaches the high end of the ~$20-21 estimate range and FY2027 pushes past $22. On sustained tariff support and continued share capture, a ~22x multiple on $21+ EPS supports a $460+ target. The ~4%/year buyback plus 67-year dividend record provide a compounding floor.

**Bear Case ($300-320, ~19-24% downside):** Section 232 is relaxed or challenged, imports rise, domestic prices fall, and the tariff-supported portion of margins reverses. FY2026 EPS reverts toward the mid-teens as pricing normalizes and the working-capital coil unwinds. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range as the market re-rates the earnings base as peak-of-policy. A ~18x multiple on ~$17 normalized EPS yields ~$305.

**Base Case ($400-420):** Tariffs hold near-term, share gains persist, and FY2026 EPS lands near the ~$20 consensus. The stock grinds modestly higher on estimate revisions and capital return but is capped by the full multiple and the reversibility overhang. A ~20x multiple on ~$20.5 blended forward EPS gives ~$410.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The franchise is worth holding for its compounding share gains, fortress balance sheet, and positive estimate momentum, but the tariff-dependent earnings base and full multiple near record highs cap the near-term appreciation case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $300s that restores margin of safety against the tariff-reversal tail (forward P/E toward ~16x).
- Confirmation that data-center/infrastructure demand is driving a durable, non-tariff acceleration in tons sold, with estimate revision velocity moving toward +20-30% over 90 days.
- Resumption of accretive M&A deploying the balance-sheet capacity, or open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material relaxation, removal, or adverse legal ruling on Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs.
- Two consecutive quarters of tons-sold growth falling below industry shipments (share loss).
- A guidance cut or a return to LIFO-driven margin compression alongside falling estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which the options market is pricing as an imminent catalyst. Beyond that, any Section 232 policy or legal development is a same-day reassessment trigger.

## Conclusion

Reliance is a high-quality business — the dominant, best-diversified metals service center in North America, with a widening moat, a fortress balance sheet, and one of the best capital-return records in the market. Q1 2026 confirmed a real earnings inflection, and the underlying share-gain story is durable and independent of policy. The trajectory of scores has been reasonably stable (financial health strengthening, growth firming), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that improvement.

The reason to hold rather than buy is the interaction of a full multiple with a policy-contingent earnings base. A meaningful slice of the FY2026 earnings power that supports ~19.7x forward rests on Section 232 tariffs that management flags as reversible and outside its control. Modest upside to the base case against a well-defined tariff-reversal downside of ~20%, combined with a structurally negative-alpha sector, insiders trimming into strength, and options markets pricing near-term stress, argues for patience. We would accumulate on a pullback that restores margin of safety, but at ~$396 near the 52-week high, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: trailing P/E corrected from ~26x to ~28x ($395.93 / $13.98) and the off-the-low rally corrected from ~50% to ~43% ($395.93 vs. the $260.31 52-week low / Oct 2025 trough); both make the valuation marginally fuller than stated, reinforcing (not undermining) the HOLD.
