# Investment Research Report: EchoStar Corporation (SATS / now ECHO)

## Executive Summary

EchoStar is no longer a telecom operator in any meaningful analytical sense. Between August 2025 and July 2026 it sold approximately 100 MHz of nationwide spectrum in two transactions — roughly 50 MHz of 600 MHz low-band and 3.45 GHz mid-band to AT&T for about $23 billion (closed July 28, 2026), and AWS-4 plus H-block to SpaceX for approximately $17 billion including roughly $8.5 billion of cash with the balance in SpaceX equity (announced September 8, 2025). What remains is a holding company: a large pool of transaction proceeds, an equity stake in a newly public space company it does not control, and a portfolio of mature-to-declining connectivity businesses (DISH TV, Sling, Hughes satellite broadband and enterprise networking, and Boost Mobile) that now must run on someone else's radio network.

The audited FY2025 financials describe the company that existed before the money arrived, and they are difficult. Revenue fell 5.18% to $15.005 billion after a 6.99% decline the prior year. Reported operating income was -$17.723 billion and pretax income -$18.893 billion; against a $4.386 billion tax benefit, the derived net loss is -$14.507 billion, corroborated independently by the -$14.497 billion change in retained earnings and the -$14.426 billion decline in stockholders' equity. Net PP&E collapsed 75.6% to $2.244 billion, consistent with a write-down of the abandoned 5G build. At December 31, 2025 the current ratio was 0.415x against a $7.230 billion working capital deficit on $1.883 billion of cash, and the business generated -$99.4 million of operating cash flow and -$1.065 billion of free cash flow. That balance sheet was, on its face, dependent on transaction closure for funding.

The AT&T close resolves the largest binary and is the reason the score does not fall despite continued operating deterioration. What it does not resolve is the valuation question, which is now almost entirely a question about one unverified number. At the last reported close of $92.03 (July 17), and after deducting an assumed $25 billion of total debt and $4 billion of cash taxes, the market is implying a SpaceX stake worth roughly $21 billion. Consensus targets of $120–$161 (mean $137.60) imply roughly $35 billion. The only sourced mark I have is $11.1 billion, from a single June 4, 2026 Fortune reference that may predate post-IPO trading. A spread that wide on the dominant asset is not an investable edge in either direction. The August 3, 2026 Q2 report is the first post-transaction disclosure and is three days from the reference date; the appropriate action is to wait for it. HOLD, classified uncertain.

## Company Overview

EchoStar Corporation (Englewood, Colorado; 12,100 employees) reported four segments: Pay-TV (DISH direct broadcast satellite, Sling streaming), Wireless (Boost Mobile, Gen Mobile), Broadband and Satellite Services (Hughes consumer satellite broadband, enterprise/government managed networks, aeronautical connectivity, ground-segment equipment), and Other (the 5G network build). Segment-level revenue, EBITDA and subscriber counts were not obtainable in this research cycle, so all statements about which businesses drove the consolidated declines are inferential rather than filing-confirmed.

Two structural facts define the security today. First, having divested AWS-4, H-block, 600 MHz and 3.45 GHz, EchoStar no longer holds the low- and mid-band portfolio required to operate as a facilities-based national carrier. Boost's forward economics depend on wholesale network terms rather than owned spectrum, and the counterparty on the largest spectrum sale is simultaneously a retail competitor. What spectrum remains (AWS-3, CBRS, 700 MHz E-block, 2 GHz MSS/international rights) could not be confirmed, and that gap matters for residual optionality and for any remaining FCC buildout obligations.

Second, EchoStar received SpaceX equity as consideration for the AWS-4/H-block sale. It holds shares in SpaceX; it has no customer, supplier or control relationship, and no ability to direct SpaceX's capital allocation or monetization timing if lock-ups apply. Following SpaceX's June 11, 2026 IPO, EchoStar's shares posted their best single session of the year and then fell about 7% the next day, demonstrating that the equity now carries SpaceX mark-to-market risk directly. The supplied beta of 0.963 is fitted to a period when the company traded as a levered telecom operator and should be treated as stale.

The ticker changed from SATS to ECHO effective June 24, 2026. The market data supplied here uses the retired symbol.

## Financial Analysis

**A reporting discontinuity to clear first.** The XBRL series shows FY2022 revenue of $1.998 billion and FY2023 revenue of $17.016 billion. That is the DISH combination changing the reporting entity, not growth. Separately, the DEF 14A filed March 21, 2025 reports FY2023 revenue of $1,755.6 million and net income of -$496.1 million on a legacy-standalone basis, inconsistent with the 10-K series' FY2023 comparative of $17,015.6 million. Only the FY2023–FY2025 figures from the 10-K series are usable as a trend, and the 2022→2023 comparison is discarded.

**Revenue: two consecutive years of contraction.**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 17,015.6 | 15,825.5 | 15,005.0 |
| YoY | — | -6.99% | -5.18% |

Cumulative two-year decline: -11.8%.

**Cost structure shows negative operating leverage where management has most control.** Cost of revenue fell 6.8% to $9,445.2 million, improving from 64.0% to 62.9% of revenue. SG&A fell only 1.9% to $2,380.3 million, deteriorating from 15.3% to 15.9% of revenue. Depreciation fell 17.9% to $1,585.5 million, consistent with a shrinking depreciable base.

**The FY2025 charge requires careful handling.** Building operating income from the four available tags — revenue 15,005.0 less cost of revenue 9,445.2 less SG&A 2,380.3 less depreciation 1,585.5 — yields -$406.0 million against reported operating income of -$17,723.1 million, a residual near -$17.3 billion. Running the identical build for FY2024 produces +$1,332.9 million against reported -$304.1 million, a residual of -$1.6 billion, which means the tags are plausibly not cleanly additive. The ~$17.3 billion figure should therefore be read as an order-of-magnitude estimate of impairment and other charges, not a precise number. The balance sheet corroborates the scale independently: net PP&E fell $6.944 billion (-75.6%) to $2.244 billion and total assets fell $17.922 billion (-29.4%) to $43.016 billion.

**The tax line contains a sign-convention trap worth resolving explicitly.** The FY2025 income-tax tag is +$4,386.4 million. Read as an expense it would imply a ~$23.3 billion net loss against an $18.9 billion pretax loss, which is incoherent. Validating against two years with independent checks: FY2022 pretax $233.2M plus tag -$66.7M equals $166.5M, matching the proxy's reported FY2022 net income exactly; FY2024 pretax -$75.6M plus tag -$48.9M equals -$124.5M, matching the proxy exactly. A positive figure is therefore a benefit. Derived FY2025 net loss: -$18,893.3M + $4,386.4M = **-$14,506.9 million**, corroborated by the -$14,497.2 million move in retained earnings (from +$11,618.4M to -$2,878.7M) and the -$14,425.6 million move in equity.

The implied 23.2% effective benefit rate means the loss was substantially tax-benefited rather than fully valuation-allowanced. The most coherent reading is that taxable gains on the spectrum dispositions provided income against which book impairments could be sheltered, and/or that deferred tax liabilities established in the DISH combination reversed as the underlying assets were written down. I did not read the tax footnote, so a carryback, discrete item or jurisdictional effect cannot be excluded. This matters practically: if the write-off of the 5G build shelters a meaningful portion of the spectrum gains, cash tax leakage on ~$40 billion of proceeds could be materially lower than a naive low-basis calculation would suggest.

Current ratio 0.415x (from 1.388x); working capital -$7,230.2 million; long-term debt/equity 3.24x (from 1.27x); derived total liabilities $37,250.6 million, or 6.46x equity.

The $7.002 billion decline in long-term debt should not be described as deleveraging. It sits almost exactly opposite a $6.532 billion increase in current liabilities while cash fell only $2.422 billion. Reclassification of near-term maturities into current is the most parsimonious explanation; genuine repurchases, held-for-sale liability reclassification, or taxes payable on spectrum gains could each account for part of it. Total debt including current maturities at year-end 2025 is unknown, and "$18.7 billion of debt" understates leverage. The conservative working assumption is roughly $25 billion of total debt.

**Cash flow is the weakest part of the story.** FY2025 operating cash flow was -$99.4 million and capex $965.7 million, for -$1.065 billion of free cash flow. The burn is not explained by cash taxes on the spectrum gains, since the tax line was a benefit, which points to operational and interest drivers. FY2023–FY2024 operating cash flow was unavailable, so no multi-year trend is asserted, and a single period is not annualized.

**One under-appreciated mechanical issue.** FY2024 interest expense of $688.7 million against roughly $25.7 billion of long-term debt implies a ~2.7% effective rate, implausibly low for this credit and most consistent with large amounts of interest being capitalized into the 5G construction in progress. With the build halted and the assets written down, P&L interest expense should step up sharply in FY2025–2026 even with unchanged debt outstanding. An alternative reading is that the tag captures only a subset of interest and the low rate is an artifact. Either way, FY2024 interest expense is not a usable proxy for cash interest cost, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment any refinancing of the remaining stack reprices upward.

**Share count.** Basic weighted-average shares rose 4.9% from 274.079 million to 287.589 million. Diluted equals basic in both loss years, so 287.589 million is not a fully diluted capitalization — convertible notes and unvested equity are excluded and could not be sized.

There is a reconcilable discrepancy in the supplied market data: $30.116 billion of market cap divided by 287.589 million shares implies $104.72 per share, while the last price bar (July 17) shows $92.03. Divided by $92.03, the market cap implies 327.2 million shares. Since the market cap is stamped August 1 and the AT&T deal closed July 28, the most internally consistent explanation is that the shares have re-rated roughly 14% since the July 17 close. I use $92.03 as the last verified trade and flag ~$105 as the market-cap-implied current level.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here. Consolidated revenue declined 6.99% then 5.18%, and each retained line faces a structural headwind rather than a cyclical one:

- **Pay-TV**: industry-wide cord-cutting. DISH satellite subscriber attrition is secular; Sling competes in a saturated vMVPD market against far better-capitalized libraries.
- **Hughes consumer broadband**: GEO satellite competes against LEO on latency and speed, dimensions on which GEO cannot win. The primary competitor is Starlink, operated by the company whose equity is now EchoStar's largest single asset.
- **Boost Mobile**: competes against three carriers with 100M+ connections each, plus cable MVNOs, without owned nationwide spectrum. Structural cost position depends entirely on wholesale terms I could not read.
- **Hughes enterprise/government and aeronautical**: the most defensible pockets, but too small to offset the consolidated drag on the evidence available.

The estimate-revision table looks superficially like an inflection signal — current-year EPS revised from -$3.61 to +$1.71 over 90 days, next-year from -$3.97 to +$0.97 over 90 days — and it should not be read that way. Coverage is two analysts on the near-term lines and four on next year; the next-quarter estimate swung from $8.10 ninety days ago to $0.78 today (-90.4%), which is the signature of transaction-gain timing rather than operating momentum. Forward P/E of -911x confirms there is no meaningful earnings base to capitalize. Even taking $1.71 at face value, on ~327 million shares that is roughly $560 million of net income — immaterial against $30 billion of market capitalization and $40 billion of asset transactions. The strong-revision buy signal does not apply: this is accounting noise around asset sales rather than a cyclical trough in operating earnings.

What growth exists is redeployment optionality: after the AT&T close the company holds a large cash balance and a marketable equity position, and could in principle buy assets, retire debt at a discount, or return capital. None of that is disclosed, and Class A holders do not control the decision.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied peer set — Baidu, Omnicom, Liberty Media Formula One, Roku, Live Nation, median P/E 89.7x — has no analytical bearing on a holding company with negative earnings and an unconsolidated equity stake dominating asset value. The correct frame is a sum-of-parts with an explicit sensitivity on the one input that matters.

**Components (reported or externally sourced):**
- AT&T proceeds: ~$23.0 billion gross, closed July 28, 2026 (four independent outlets plus an 8-K dated the same day). Cash form, purchase-price adjustments, escrows and holdbacks unconfirmed.
- SpaceX cash consideration: ~$8.5 billion. Closing status unconfirmed.
- SpaceX equity stake: $11.1 billion per a single June 4, 2026 Fortune reference. Share count, ownership percentage, lock-up terms and current mark unconfirmed.
- Year-end 2025 cash: $1.883 billion (partially consumed since).
- Total debt: assumed $25 billion ($18.659 billion long-term plus ~$6.3 billion assumed reclassified maturities). Unverified.
- Cash taxes on gains: assumed $4 billion. Unquantified, and plausibly lower if impairment losses shelter the gains.

**Conservative branch.** Gross assets $23.0 + $8.5 + $11.1 + $1.9 = $44.5 billion, less $25 billion debt and $4 billion taxes = $15.5 billion of net non-operating value. Against a $30.116 billion market cap, the residual attributed to the operating businesses is $14.6 billion, or roughly 0.97x FY2025 revenue — an expensive multiple for a business declining ~5% annually with negative operating cash flow.

**Flipping the arithmetic.** If the operating businesses are worth ~$5 billion (roughly 0.33x revenue, defensible for a no-FCF declining asset), then at a $30.116 billion market cap the market is implying a SpaceX stake of about **$20.7 billion**: $30.1B = X + $23.0 + $8.5 + $1.9 - $25.0 - $4.0 + $5.0. That is roughly 1.9x the sourced June reference.

**What consensus implies.** The $137.60 mean target on ~327 million shares is a $45.0 billion equity value, which under identical assumptions requires a SpaceX stake of roughly **$35.6 billion** — about 3.2x the June reference. The $120–$161 target range brackets SpaceX marks of roughly $31–$44 billion.

The valuation question reduces to a single unverifiable number, with the market pricing something between the sourced mark and the sell-side assumption. One consideration argues for caution on the aggressive branch: EchoStar is a levered claim on the SpaceX equity (roughly $25 billion of debt against the asset pool), so any SpaceX derating transmits to ECHO with leverage. The 37.5% decline from the $147.25 52-week high to $92.03 is most plausibly explained by that mechanism plus the pre-close funding overhang, rather than by a mispricing the market has failed to notice. At a drawdown of this magnitude the burden of proof sits with the bull case, and I cannot discharge it without the stake's carrying value.

Valuation therefore scores 5.0: genuinely two-sided, wide, and indeterminate on current evidence rather than demonstrably cheap. A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong; here I can only identify why the market is uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

The one competitive advantage the evidence supports is historical and now realized: decades of spectrum accumulation produced roughly $40 billion of announced asset value at a company Fortune described as near bankruptcy a year earlier. That advantage has been monetized and cannot be repeated.

The retained businesses show a deteriorating trajectory in each line. AT&T's disclosure that the acquired 3.45 GHz went live on approximately 23,000 cell sites with materially faster speeds across 400+ markets, while the 600 MHz portion is "not ready," tells us the mid-band was immediately deployable capacity — the asset that left EchoStar was the scarce one. Boost's cost structure is now a wholesale purchase from a retail competitor; the $10+ billion of written-off network assets is evidence that building the alternative was value-destructive, so a favorably priced long-term network services agreement could be economically superior to what was abandoned. Those terms are unread and are the central unresolved question for Boost's durability.

One structural offset deserves explicit mention: EchoStar is financially long the company disrupting one of its core businesses. Starlink share gains that erode Hughes consumer broadband should, all else equal, coincide with appreciation in the SpaceX stake. The hedge is imperfect and asymmetric in timing: Hughes revenue erosion is recurring and near-term, while SpaceX equity appreciation is unrealized, possibly restricted, and driven overwhelmingly by global launch and Starlink consumer economics rather than US GEO share shifts.

A broader industry observation: two entirely different buyer types paid broadly comparable per-MHz economics for roughly 50 MHz each ($23 billion from a terrestrial carrier, ~$17 billion from a satellite operator). Direct-to-device satellite operators have become credible bidders for terrestrial licensed spectrum, which structurally raises clearing prices. That is a positive for any remaining EchoStar spectrum, the extent of which is unknown.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term liquidity, as of the audited balance sheet.** A 0.415x current ratio, -$7.230 billion working capital and $1.883 billion of cash describe a company dependent on transaction closure for funding. The AT&T close plausibly resolves it, but that is seven months after the balance sheet date. Any going-concern or substantial-doubt language in Item 7 could not be read, which is a genuine gap.

**Unverified capital structure.** Total debt including current maturities is unknown; the composition of the $12.363 billion of current liabilities (maturities versus taxes payable versus held-for-sale liabilities) is unknown. The conservative working assumption is roughly $25 billion of total debt at year-end 2025, largely unreduced. This is flagged as a top-priority verification item because any subsidiary-level reorganization would materially change both the debt arithmetic and the structural position of parent-level Class A claims. [unverified — no reorganization or Chapter 11 filing is documented in the provided sources]

**Concentration in a single non-controlled equity holding.** Whatever the mark, the SpaceX position is a large share of enterprise value in one position EchoStar does not control and may not be free to monetize on its own timetable.

**Cash tax leakage.** Unquantified, and a first-order determinant of how much of the ~$31.5 billion of cash proceeds reaches equity holders.

**Governance.** The security is Class A within an apparent multi-class structure under founder control. Class-by-class voting arithmetic was not verified in a filing this cycle, but if confirmed, Class A holders are minority participants in decisions about the SpaceX stake and any monetization, which justifies a holding-company discount to NAV rather than parity.

**Compensation design.** FY2024 plan metrics were free cash flow, revenue, Pay-TV and/or wireless subscribers, and a consumer creation multiple. Three of four were deteriorating (revenue -7.0% in FY2024, FCF -$1.065 billion in FY2025), while the value realized in 2025 came from asset sales that none of the four measures captures. FY2024 compensation actually paid of $16.71 million against $8.93 million granted shows realized outcomes are dominated by share-price mark-to-market. Equity-heavy pay that marks with the stock does align management with an asset-monetization outcome, so this reads as a design mismatch rather than extraction. FY2025 compensation was unavailable — Part III was filed by 10-K/A on April 30, 2026, which itself suggests the FY2025 proxy was delayed relative to the prior year's March 21 filing.

**No insider signal.** Insider transaction data was unobtainable. Draw no inference in either direction; management quality is scored on realized capital allocation and the compensation/TSR record alone (five-year TSR index 52.84 versus peer 111.11 through FY2024, approximately -47% versus +11%).

### Options Market Signal

The available options data is stale and pre-dates both the SpaceX IPO and the AT&T close. Between May 23 and May 26, 2026, ATM implied volatility collapsed from 142.5% to 77.2% on a 0.9% price move, with put/call open interest falling from 0.42 to 0.33 at a price of roughly $123. The IV collapse most likely reflects event-premium unwinding rather than a change in view. The call-tilted open interest is consistent with upside-lottery positioning against 36.5% short interest and 6.6 days to cover — a structure that cuts both ways: it caps how far bearish positioning can push the stock without squeeze risk, and it signals that a large cohort of capital holds a structurally negative view. No current options read is available, and the August 3 print falls outside the observation window. A 0.963 beta materially understates this security's present volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$135, +47% from $92.03; range $135–$155).** The August 3 print discloses a SpaceX stake carried near $35 billion, confirms the AT&T $23 billion arrived in cash with minimal adjustment, shows cash taxes well below $4 billion because the ~$17 billion 5G write-off shelters the spectrum gains, and shows total debt reduced materially with the maturity wall retired. On those facts the equity is a NAV story trading at a discount, consensus targets of $120–$161 are validated, and 36.5% short interest becomes fuel. Boost's network services agreement proves economically superior to the abandoned build, and the SpaceX stake provides a natural hedge against Hughes erosion.

**Bear case (~$58, -37%; range $50–$60).** The SpaceX stake is carried near the sourced $11.1 billion reference or is written toward it as post-IPO trading normalizes, cash taxes approach $5 billion, total debt proves closer to $25 billion than $19 billion, and any subsidiary-level complication weighs on parent-level recovery. Equity value under those inputs is roughly $19.5 billion, or about $60 per share on ~327 million shares. Meanwhile the operating business continues losing 5% of revenue annually with negative free cash flow, P&L interest expense steps up as capitalized interest ends, and a dual-class controlling holder gives Class A holders no mechanism to force monetization or capital return.

**Base case (~$105, +14% from the last verified close; roughly flat against the market-cap-implied Aug 1 level).** SpaceX marked in the low-to-mid $20 billions, taxes $3–4 billion, operating businesses at 0.3–0.4x revenue, and a holding-company discount for control and illiquidity. That produces fair value near the level the market cap already implies: the shares have largely re-rated for the AT&T close, and further appreciation requires the SpaceX mark to be at the aggressive end.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline: August 3, 2026** — the Q2 2026 report and call. This is the first post-transaction disclosure and should provide the SpaceX stake's carrying/fair value and share count, the pro-forma balance sheet with total debt including current maturities, the composition of the current-liability build, cash tax accruals on the gains, and any subsequent-event treatment of the July 28 AT&T close (which falls after quarter-end, so the cash appears only as a subsequent event).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Disclosed SpaceX stake carrying or fair value at or above roughly $28 billion, combined with confirmed total debt at or below $20 billion post-close — which would put NAV per share above $120 and make the current price a verified discount rather than an assumed one.
2. Cash taxes on the AT&T and SpaceX gains disclosed below $2.5 billion (consistent with the impairment sheltering the gains), plus a stated capital-allocation plan — debt retirement, buyback, or a defined distribution.
3. Disclosure of Boost network services agreement economics showing wholesale cost per subscriber that supports positive segment free cash flow, or a return of consolidated operating cash flow to positive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. SpaceX stake carried at or below $15 billion, or disclosure of lock-up terms extending beyond twelve months on the bulk of the position — either would push NAV toward the bear case with no near-term monetization path.
2. Total debt disclosed above $26 billion post-close, or evidence that AT&T proceeds were substantially consumed by taxes and maturities, leaving the parent without net cash.
3. Consolidated revenue decline accelerating beyond -8% year over year, or FY2026 free cash flow burn exceeding $1.5 billion, which would mean the operating business is destroying the proceeds rather than being funded by them.
4. Confirmation that Class A holders face structural subordination in a subsidiary reorganization, or a related-party transaction moving the SpaceX stake outside the public entity.

**Position discipline while holding.** This is an event-driven NAV position with a stale beta, 36.5% short interest and a levered claim on a recently listed equity. Size it as a binary rather than a core holding, and do not add ahead of the August 3 disclosure.

## Conclusion

**HOLD, overall score 4.0.** The trajectory — 3.7, 3.6, 3.0, 4.0, 4.0 — has been improving, and this cycle's evidence sustains rather than extends that. The AT&T close removes the largest binary that had justified the earlier AVOID stance and, on the reported terms, resolves the funding cliff visible in the FY2025 balance sheet. That improvement is offset by continued operating deterioration (revenue -5.2%, operating cash flow -$99.4 million, free cash flow -$1.065 billion, SG&A rising as a share of a shrinking revenue base) and by the recognition that the equity's dominant value driver is a SpaceX mark ranging across the sourced $11.1 billion reference, the market-implied ~$21 billion, and the consensus-implied ~$35 billion. Financial health improves modestly to 4.5 on the confirmed proceeds; growth stays at 3.0 on secular decline in every retained line; competitive position stays at 3.0 given the permanent exit from facilities-based wireless; valuation eases to 5.0 because the price already reflects roughly 14% of re-rating into the close while the key input remains unverified; risk factors hold at 4.0 as one binary closes and several disclosure gaps remain.

The estimate revisions look dramatic and are not a buy signal — two-analyst coverage swinging on transaction-gain timing, with a -90.4% revision to the next-quarter figure sitting beside a +713% revision to the current year. Consensus targets are all above spot, but they embed a SpaceX mark roughly three times the only sourced reference, which makes them a statement about that assumption rather than independent confirmation. A three-day wait for the first post-transaction financials is a low-cost way to replace an assumption with a disclosure. Existing holders should hold and reassess on August 3; new capital should wait for the SpaceX carrying value and post-close debt figure before underwriting anything.
