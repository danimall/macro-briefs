## Executive Summary

Energy Transfer's second-quarter print was the first quarter in a year where the numbers corroborated the operating story rather than complicated it. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA rose 31% to $5,066M, distributable cash flow attributable to partners rose 32% to $2,587M, and management raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $18.8–19.1B from $18.2–18.6B, the second raise from the $15.984B FY2025 base. Growth capital guidance went up with it, to $5.6–5.9B from the $5.0–5.5B disclosed in February. The commercial drivers are specific and verifiable in the filings: Hugh Brinson is in commercial service and expected to flow its full 1.5 Bcf/d Phase I capacity by September 1, 2026; the Nederland ethane expansion (240,000 bpd) was fully subscribed before construction; roughly 300,000 Bbls/d of long-term y-grade transportation and fractionation agreements were signed extending into the 2030s; and NGL exports set a company record, up 25% year over year.

Three things keep this from being a straightforward buy at $20.13. First, the headline growth rate overstates what accrues to ET unitholders. ET consolidates 100% of Sunoco LP, in which it holds 15% of the common and Class D units, and 100% of USA Compression, where it holds 32%. In the first half, ET's own DCF reconciliation deducted $1,376M of subsidiary DCF and added back only $249M of distributions actually received, roughly 18% cash conversion at the parent. Stripping the two investment segments out, core segment Adjusted EBITDA grew 15.2% in the first half rather than 25.6%. Second, the 2.2x distribution coverage is coverage before growth capital. ET's DCF definition deducts maintenance capex ($678M in H1, guided to roughly $1.6–1.67B for the year) but not the $5.6–5.9B of growth spending, against FY2025 D&A of $5.682B. FY2025 required $4.82B of net new debt to fund distributions and capex simultaneously; the first half of 2026 was roughly self-funding, but on front-loaded working capital and before $600M of announced Sunoco LP M&A closes in Q4. Third, the risk stack is heavier than the covenant metrics suggest: up to ~$345M of unaccrued Rover Ohio ad valorem tax exposure, a Williams antitrust trial set for June 21, 2027, Oklahoma Attorney General Uri antitrust actions seeking treble damages, an injunction request against an operating jet-fuel pipeline in Pennsylvania, and a July 2026 successor-issuer conversion whose partnership-agreement and risk-factor changes could not be read.

The result is a HOLD held for income. At $20.13 the $1.36 annualized distribution yields 6.76% and is covered roughly 2.0–2.1x on a full-year partner-DCF sensitivity of $9.4–10.0B ($2.73–2.91 per unit). Consolidated EV/EBITDA is about 8.2x on guidance midpoint. Base case is $22.25 (+10.5%), which with the distribution produces a roughly 17% twelve-month total return; the bear case at $17.00 is −15.5%. That asymmetry is adequate, not compelling, three percent below a 52-week high, and it is not enough to justify new capital at this entry when the sector's recent record of converting good fundamentals into relative outperformance has been poor.

## Company Overview

Energy Transfer operates roughly 140,000 miles of pipeline across 44 states with positions in every major U.S. producing basin. Reporting runs through seven segments: Intrastate Transportation & Storage (~12,200 miles, ~24 Bcf/d, five storage facilities including the 52.5 Bcf Bammel reservoir near the Houston Ship Channel); Interstate Transportation & Storage (~20,090 wholly owned miles at ~20.1 Bcf/d plus ~7,080 JV miles, including Florida Gas Transmission through the 50/50 Citrus JV, which delivers roughly 60% of Florida's gas consumption); Midstream (~13.5 Bcf/d of processing, 5.5 Bcf/d of it Permian across 22 plants); NGL & Refined Products (Mont Belvieu with 1.15 MMBbls/d of fractionation and ~63 MMBbls of salt-dome storage, plus the Nederland and Marcus Hook export terminals); Crude (>18,000 miles, ~73 MMBbls of storage, 36.4% of the Bakken Pipeline system); Investment in Sunoco LP; and Investment in USA Compression.

The structural point governs how every financial line should be read. ET is a holding partnership dependent on distributions from subsidiaries, two of which are separately listed and only partially owned. Because both are fully consolidated, ET's reported revenue, EBITDA and debt include 100% of entities in which its economic interest is a minority. That is not an accounting irregularity, but it makes consolidated growth rates, consolidated leverage, and consolidated EV/EBITDA all imperfect proxies for unitholder economics.

## Financial Analysis

**The 2026 gains concentrate at the level that matters.** Second-quarter revenue was $34,334M against $19,242M, net income $2,530M against $1,458M, diluted EPS $0.59 against $0.32. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $5,066M (+31%) and first-half $10,003M (+25.6%). Decomposed, the five wholly controlled core segments plus All Other produced $3,890M of Q2 Adjusted EBITDA against $3,263M, or +19.2%; first half $7,781M against $6,753M, +15.2%. The Sunoco LP and USAC investment segments supplied the remaining +$573M in the quarter, of which Sunoco LP alone contributed +$928M in the first half, mostly the Parkland acquisition (closed 10/31/2025) and TanQuid (closed 1/16/2026). DCF attributable to partners, as adjusted, grew 32% to $2,587M because it is a smaller post-interest, post-maintenance-capex base and because ET's incentive distribution rights in Sunoco LP scale with SUN's per-unit distribution.

**Earnings quality in 2026 is flattered by items Adjusted EBITDA excludes.** Q2 net income includes $396M of unrealized commodity risk-management gains, approximately $0.114 of the $0.59 diluted EPS on 3,463.1M diluted units, an approximation since the $396M is consolidated and pre-tax. The first half also carried a +$426M favorable Sunoco LP inventory valuation adjustment (lower-of-cost-or-market reserve falling from $472M to $19M) and a $102M Q1 LIFO liquidation benefit (~$0.03/unit). The reported 52.2% "beat" against a ~$0.39 externally reported consensus is therefore substantially narrower on a cash basis, and the basis of that consensus (GAAP versus adjusted) is unconfirmed. Cutting the other way, FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA *included* a $160M non-recurring Winter Storm Uri recognition inside the Midstream segment, which makes 2026 Midstream comparisons harder than they look; first-half Midstream Adjusted EBITDA grew only +$78M despite +1,040 BBtu/d of gathered volume.

**Revenue growth of 78% is not economically meaningful.** Sunoco LP segment revenue of $14.259B against $5.390B explains most of it (Parkland). The Crude segment's revenue near-doubling to $11.051B on cost of products sold of $9.766B, against transportation volumes up only 4% and segment margin up 26%, is characteristic of expanded buy/sell marketing gross-up. The specific driver is not disclosed in the filings reviewed and remains unresolved.

**Cash flow improved materially and cannot simply be annualized.** First-half operating cash flow was $7.649B against $5.679B, cash capex $3.480B, acquisitions $786M, total distributions $3.494B (partners $2.409B, NCI $1.068B). Operating cash flow less cash capex was $4.169B against $3.494B of distributions, a ~$675M surplus before acquisitions. Net debt *fell* $231M in H1 2026 versus a $1.02B increase in H1 2025. But working capital was a +$228M net source, with accounts receivable rising from $11.275B to $16.818B offset by accounts payable rising from $9.469B to $13.720B, a gross-up driven by Parkland marketing and higher throughput that can reverse. Second-half maintenance capex is guided roughly $270M above the first half. A reasonable full-year construction: operating cash flow of $12.5–14.0B less total capex of $7.2–7.6B leaves $4.9–6.8B against roughly $7.0B of total distributions, implying a residual external funding need of perhaps $0.2–2.1B before the $600M Q4 Sunoco acquisition. Better than FY2025's ~$2.57B consolidated gap; not yet self-funding through the cycle.

**A reproducible partner-DCF sensitivity.** The first half converted Adjusted EBITDA to partner-level adjusted DCF at 52.9% ($5,291M / $10,003M). Applying that ratio to implied second-half Adjusted EBITDA of $8.947B (guidance midpoint $18.95B less H1 actual) gives ~$4.73B; deducting ~$270M for higher H2 maintenance capex leaves ~$4.46B, for a full-year partner DCF of roughly $9.7–10.0B, or $2.83–2.91 per unit on 3,443.3M units. If second-half conversion runs 300bp below the first half on mix and no repeat of the H1 one-time items, full-year DCF is nearer $9.4B ($2.73/unit). Against the $1.36 annualized distribution, that is coverage of 2.0–2.1x and a DCF yield of 13.6–14.5% at $20.13. The critical qualifier: this is coverage before the $5.6–5.9B of growth capital that produces the growth being capitalized into the equity.

**Leverage: wide covenant headroom, high absolute debt.** Total debt was $68.405B at 6/30/2026, essentially flat against $68.333B at year-end despite ~$1.6B of closed acquisitions. The credit-agreement Leverage Ratio was 3.01x against a 5.00x maximum, improved from 3.21x. Simple gross debt against guidance-midpoint EBITDA is 3.61x ($68.405B / $18.95B), and 4.27x against the FY2025 actual; the gap most plausibly reflects pro-forma acquisition EBITDA credit in the covenant definition. Revolver availability improved to $3.76B drawn only $1.21B of the $4.84B facility. Floating-rate debt fell to $3.02B (100bp ≈ $30M of annual interest). Interest coverage on guidance is roughly 5.0x ($18.95B / ~$3.76B annualized net interest). In July 2026 ET issued $650M of 6.550% and $1.10B of 6.700% junior subordinated notes due 2057 to redeem the Series H Preferred (redemption August 17, 2026) and repay the revolver. The incremental cost of ET's capital is now mid-6s, which is the relevant hurdle for the growth program.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.54 should be heavily discounted: the model weights retained earnings to total assets, and a partnership required to distribute available cash structurally cannot accumulate retained earnings. Flat total debt, declining net debt, 3.01x covenant leverage, $3.76B of undrawn revolver and a debt fair value ($68.27B) essentially equal to carrying value ($68.41B) are more informative about near-term solvency.

**A data conflict worth naming.** The supplied EPS field of $1.20945 is inconsistent with the supplied 13.7855x P/E; the latter implies trailing EPS of about $1.46, which reconciles with the sum of the last four reported quarters ($0.28 + $0.25 + $0.35 + $0.59 = $1.47). At the $20.13 basis, trailing P/E is therefore roughly 13.7x on $1.47 or 16.6x on the reported field. Given the size of the unrealized-mark and inventory-valuation swings, neither trailing multiple is a useful anchor. Forward consensus of $1.61 (current year) and $1.60 (next year) puts the stock at ~12.5x, but the same GAAP noise makes those estimates unstable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, of which two look durable.

**Texas gas takeaway and power load.** Hugh Brinson is in commercial service with full Phase I capacity expected by September 1, 2026; a 14-mile Abilene lateral was completed in Q2; the Springerville Lateral on Transwestern was announced in May 2026 to support conversion of two coal-fired plants to gas; the Desert Southwest expansion is in development with FERC scoping complete; and two customers added a combined 100 MMcf/d serving power plant or data-center sites in Texas, with management signalling further announcements this year. Intrastate growth capex of $1,475M is budgeted for 2026. The financial evidence is already visible: intrastate margin swung from a $145M decline in FY2025 to a $165M increase in the first half of 2026 on wider basis differentials, plus $31M on storage volatility.

The caution on this leg is that part of the intrastate uplift is transitional. Wide Waha-to-market spreads are a symptom of takeaway scarcity, and the projects ET and its competitors are building narrow those spreads. Demand-charge revenue on new capacity grows; the optimization margin that widened in H1 can compress as the same capacity relieves the constraint it monetizes.

**The NGL export chain.** This is the more defensible franchise and the cleaner growth leg. The June 2026 Nederland expansion (240,000 bpd of ethane and 55,000 bpd of LPG capacity, plus two additional ship docks and expansion of the Mont Belvieu-to-Nederland pipeline) was fully subscribed before construction. Gulf Coast NGL Express upgrades added >90,000 Bbls/d of Permian takeaway. Roughly 300,000 Bbls/d of long-term transportation and fractionation agreements were signed on y-grade assets extending into the 2030s. Mustang Draw I (275 MMcf/d) entered service in the Midland Basin in June. Q2 NGL transportation volumes were up 13% and exports up 25%, both records. ET controls gathering, takeaway, fractionation, storage and water access on the same chain; few competitors control all of it.

**Dakota Access de-risking.** The USACE published the Record of Decision Notice of Availability in the Federal Register on June 5, 2026, with the easement expected in Q3 or Q4 2026, and the D.C. Circuit dismissed Standing Rock's appeal on June 23, 2026 at the Tribe's own request. The commercial relevance is quantified: FY2025 Crude segment Adjusted EBITDA fell $235M, of which $206M was lower Bakken Pipeline joint-venture margin on lower volumes. Legal certainty should support recontracting, which is the mechanism by which some of that margin recovers. It is a hypothesis rather than a disclosed expectation; the ROD carries additional conditions not reviewed, and Bakken volumes ultimately depend on North Dakota drilling economics.

**What limits the per-unit growth rate.** The distribution went from $0.3250 (Q4 2024) to $0.3400 (Q2 2026), roughly 3% annualized despite the nineteenth consecutive increase. Sell-side estimates capture the tension: current-year consensus EPS rose 8.9% over 90 days to $1.61 while next-year consensus is flat at $1.60 (−0.2%). The near-term quarters have been trimmed 1.3% and 5.2% over 30 days after +25% moves over 90 days. The pattern is a 2026 upgrade cycle that has not extended into 2027. On top of that, FERC set the liquids index at PPI-FG minus 0.55% for July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2031, replacing plus 0.78%; at 2.5% PPI-FG inflation that is roughly 130bp less annual escalation on indexed crude and refined-products tariffs. ET does not disclose the revenue base subject to indexing, so the dollar effect cannot be sized.

Capital allocation was decisively redirected away from LNG. Lake Charles liquefaction was suspended in December 2025 with FY2025 impairments of $285M primarily attributable to it, and several offtake agreements terminated for failure to reach FID. Management's stated rationale, pipeline projects offering superior risk/return, is coherent. An equally plausible reading is that acceptable offtake and equity terms could not be assembled after roughly a decade of attempts, and the capital-discipline framing followed. Both can be true; the Q2 strategic highlights do not mention Lake Charles at all.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.13, consolidated enterprise value is approximately $155.2B: equity $69.31B, plus total debt $68.405B, plus preferred carrying value $3.356B, plus noncontrolling interests $15.191B, less cash $1.020B. Against the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $18.95B that is **8.19x EV/EBITDA**. The multiple is internally consistent (100% of subsidiary EBITDA measured against an EV that includes subsidiary debt and NCI) but an ET-standalone multiple cannot be computed from public segment data because debt is not allocated by segment.

That 8.2x sits at the low end of the large-cap midstream range, and the discount has been persistent rather than newly opened. It reflects things that have not changed: complexity of a seven-segment structure with two partially owned public subsidiaries, an MLP governance framework with contractually reduced fiduciary duties and no annual director elections or say-on-pay, and a capital program that consumes more than internal cash generation in ordinary years. Buying the discount requires believing it narrows, and there is no identified catalyst that closes it.

The cash-yield case is stronger than the multiple case. The distribution yields 6.76% at $20.13 with roughly 2.0–2.1x coverage on the sensitivity above, and the partner-level DCF yield is 13.6–14.5%. The gap between those two numbers is the growth capital, so the honest framing is: unitholders receive a well-covered 6.8% cash return while ET reinvests roughly $5.7B annually at returns that will not be observable for two to three years. If those projects earn a spread over a mid-6s cost of capital, the distribution grows faster than 3%; if they do not, the yield is the return.

Analyst positioning is constructive and stable: 5 strong buy, 14 buy, 2 hold with no changes over three months; targets of $22.00 low, $24.00 median, $24.24 mean, $27.00 high. The *low* target sits 9.3% above the current price. On a stock at ~8.2x EV/EBITDA and ~12.5x forward earnings, consensus targets are reasonable evidence that the near-term price is not stretched. But this is a mid-single-digit-growth income vehicle, not a growth stock where estimate momentum drags targets higher, and next-year estimates are flat, which is the more relevant signal for a 2027-anchored valuation.

Two macro inputs matter to the multiple. MLP unit prices compete directly with duration, and a further backup in long rates compresses the multiple on a 6.8% yield vehicle irrespective of operating results. Conversely, elevated U.S. NGL and LPG export demand and product cracks are mildly favorable to Nederland/Marcus Hook volumes and to Sunoco's refined-product economics. [The specific claims about the 30-year Treasury level and Brent crude prices are unverified — no macro figures appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

Scoring valuation at 6.0 reflects moderate positive asymmetry with no specific mispricing to point at. A low multiple on a levered, complex, structurally discounted partnership three percent below its 52-week high is not by itself a reason to pay up.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own competition discussion is unusually blunt: gathering and processing competes on proximity, gas marketing competes against interstate pipeline affiliates and integrated majors, "many of our competitors... have capital resources and control supplies of natural gas substantially greater than ours," NGL fractionation competes "primarily based on the fractionation fee charged," and Sunoco's wholesale fuel market is "highly competitive and fragmented, which results in narrow margins."

Against that, three durable advantages emerge from the asset base. First, physical integration: the Oasis and ET Fuel systems can bypass processing plants when processing margins are unfavorable while still meeting pipeline specification, an operational option most peers lack. Second, control of the NGL chain from Permian gathering through Gulf Coast NGL Express takeaway, Mont Belvieu fractionation and storage, to Nederland and Marcus Hook water access. Third, intrastate Texas positioning at Waha, Katy, Carthage, Agua Dulce and the Houston Ship Channel, precisely where incremental power-generation and data-center load is emerging.

Trajectory is improving. Record NGL transportation, export, crude transportation and gathered volumes in Q2; a fully subscribed export expansion committed before construction; ~300,000 Bbls/d of new long-dated y-grade contracts. Management stated no single segment contributed more than one-third of consolidated Adjusted EBITDA in the quarter. FERC's siting posture has turned more constructive (withdrawal of the draft 2022 Certificate Policy Statement in September 2025, withdrawal of the draft GHG Policy Statement in January 2025, a May 2026 proposal to expand blanket-certificate authority), even as its liquids-rate posture turned less so.

One qualification on diversification. Consolidated revenue concentration is benign (no customer exceeded 10% in 2025) but segment-level concentration is not: two customers are approximately 29% of Intrastate revenues, four customers approximately 27% of Interstate, Trans-Pecos and Comanche Trail each depend on a single primary shipper, Lake Charles LNG derives all revenue from long-term contracts with a single Shell subsidiary, and the Citrus JV's top two customers represent 57% of Citrus revenue. Revenue mix shift from Parkland changes the consolidated arithmetic without reducing pipeline-segment counterparty concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Unaccrued litigation and tax exposure.** Rover's Ohio ad valorem tax exposure runs up to approximately $345M including interest for 2020–2025 if preliminary assessments are upheld, and it is not accrued; the Ohio Supreme Court affirmed the Board of Tax Appeals order on 8/13/2025 and Rover's constitutional challenge survived a motion to dismiss on 3/24/2026. Rover FERC enforcement carries proposed penalties of $20M and $40M. The Williams/Gulf Run antitrust suit over Haynesville-to-Gulf-Coast transportation goes to trial June 21, 2027 with damages unquantified. Oklahoma Attorney General Uri antitrust actions, consolidated 3/19/2026 with motions to dismiss denied twice, seek treble and punitive damages. The Twin Oaks jet-fuel release class action includes a request for a preliminary injunction to enjoin operation of the pipeline. Accrued contingent obligations fell to $133M at 6/30/2026 from $324M (largely the $124M Cline payment in May 2026), with additional reasonably possible losses estimated at up to ~$74M, a figure that plainly does not encompass Rover, Williams or the Oklahoma matters. Partially offsetting, the 10-Q discloses approximately $786M of favorable but unrecorded verdicts (Greenpeace entities and CPS Energy), inclusive of NCI portions and subject to appeal, with collectibility unaddressed.

**Governance and the unverified conversion.** An 8-K12B successor-issuer notification was filed 2026-07-06 alongside a Plan of Conversion dated 2026-07-02, a Certificate of Formation, a new Agreement of Limited Partnership, and restated risk factors as Exhibit 99.1. The entity name and LP form are retained. What is not established: the destination jurisdiction, whether unitholder approval was required or obtained, any change to general-partner powers or fiduciary-duty standards in the new partnership agreement, and any tax consequence. Exhibit 99.1 could not be read, so the specific risk-factor changes are unknown. Separately, the structured proxy query returned only a January 2007 DEF 14A with no compensation, pay-versus-performance or TSR data, consistent with MLP practice, and a reminder that annual director elections and say-on-pay are unavailable here. Two Item 5.02 filings (2025-11-03, 2026-06-03) were not read.

**Structural leverage in a higher-for-longer regime.** New junior subordinated paper priced at 6.55–6.70%. With $5.6–5.9B of growth capex, ~$7.0B of total distributions, and ~$4.40B of principal due on or before 6/30/2027 classified long-term on refinancing intent, ET remains a regular issuer. Covenant headroom is wide; the exposure is to spread and rate levels, not to a breach.

**Spread and volatility sensitivity inside the "fee-based" story.** FY2025 showed the downside: intrastate Adjusted EBITDA fell $145M chiefly on a $214M decline in realized gas sales/optimization, and NGL marketing margin fell $186M. The first half of 2026 showed the upside: intrastate margin +$165M on wider differentials, NGL marketing +$353M. Separately, the All Other segment's power derivative notional grew from 461,896 MW at year-end to 2,115,904 MW at 6/30/2026 with maturities extended to 2033. The most likely reading is commercial support for the power/data-center strategy; a larger hedging or trading position is a non-exclusive alternative, and associated risk limits are not broken out.

**Environmental.** Accruals rose $278M → $416M → $522M. The FY2025 step-up included $140M recorded by Sunoco LP in connection with Parkland, acquired liability rather than legacy deterioration, and ETC Sunoco legacy remediation accruals actually fell from $197M to $186M. The H1 2026 increase of $106M is less benign: roughly $44M is attributed to higher Midstream environmental reserves charged to segment opex. ET is a named PRP at approximately 29 Superfund sites, and approximately 192 engines may require retrofit under the currently stayed EPA Good Neighbor Plan.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $25.50 (+26.7%)

The 2026 guidance raise proves durable and extends into 2027. Hugh Brinson reaches full 1.5 Bcf/d in September and the promised additional gas pipeline announcements materialize, converting Texas power and data-center load into demand-charge revenue that is not spread-dependent. Nederland's fully subscribed ethane expansion and the ~300,000 Bbls/d of new y-grade contracts lift NGL segment margin through 2027–2028 with contracts extending into the 2030s. The Dakota Access easement issues in Q3/Q4, shippers recontract, and a portion of the $206M Bakken JV margin decline reverses. Full-year partner DCF lands near $10.0B ($2.91/unit), coverage holds above 2x, and distribution growth accelerates from 3% toward 5% as the growth program completes and external funding needs fall. The multiple re-rates modestly on a lower risk profile, with the unit yielding 5.6% on a $1.43 run-rate, implying roughly 8.7x EV/EBITDA on $20.0B, still a discount to the large-cap cohort. Elevated NGL and LPG export demand and product cracks add to the export chain and Sunoco.

### Bear Case — $17.00 (−15.5%)

A backup in the long end of the Treasury curve reprices a 6.8%-yield vehicle to an 8% yield without any operating deterioration. That alone gets to $17.00 on the current $1.36 distribution. Fundamentally, the intrastate optimization margin proves transitional as ET's own and competitors' takeaway narrows Waha spreads, so the +$165M first-half tailwind reverses in 2027 exactly as the FERC liquids index at PPI-FG minus 0.55% removes roughly 130bp of annual tariff escalation. Working capital reverses in the second half, the AR/AP gross-up unwinds, and full-year cash generation falls short of the H1-annualized path, returning ET to net debt issuance to fund the distribution alongside $5.6–5.9B of growth capex at a 6.6% marginal cost. An adverse cluster in litigation (Rover upheld at ~$345M, a Williams damages award, the Twin Oaks injunction granted) consumes the $786M of unrecorded contingent gains and more. The July 2026 conversion, once read, turns out to have altered general-partner powers or unitholder protections in ways that widen the governance discount rather than narrowing it.

### Which is more likely

Neither leg dominates. Operating momentum is genuinely positive and verifiable in filings, and the distribution is well covered by cash from existing operations. But the two swing factors, the long end of the Treasury curve and the durability of spread-driven intrastate margin, are both outside management's control, and 2027 consensus is flat while 2026 rose 8.9%. Probability-weighting roughly 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected price near $21.20, or about +5.3%, plus 6.8% of distributions for an expected total return in the low double digits. That is a fair return on a levered income vehicle, not an asymmetric one.

## Conclusion

Energy Transfer is executing better than at any point in the past two years, and the evidence is in the filings rather than the commentary: two upward guidance revisions to $18.8–19.1B of FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, core wholly-owned segment growth of 15–19%, record volumes across NGL transportation, exports, crude transportation and gathering, a fully subscribed export expansion, ~300,000 Bbls/d of new long-dated y-grade contracts, covenant leverage down to 3.01x, and net debt declining in the first half for the first time in years. The controlling owner bought 4.0M units for approximately $68.4M at an average of $17.11 in August and November 2025, and a director added ~$250,000 at $20.23 the day after the Q2 10-Q was filed.

The reasons not to pay up at $20.13 are equally concrete. Consolidated growth of 31% overstates unitholder economics: roughly 18% of subsidiary DCF converted to cash at the parent in the first half. Distribution coverage of 2.2x is coverage before $5.6–5.9B of growth capital, and the marginal cost of that capital is now 6.55–6.70%. The 2026 estimate upgrade cycle has not extended into 2027, where consensus is flat. FERC removed roughly 130bp of annual liquids tariff escalation for the next five years. A stack of unaccrued litigation runs into the hundreds of millions with the Williams trial not until mid-2027. And a partnership conversion completed five weeks ago carries unread changes to the partnership agreement and risk factors, in a structure that already contractually reduces fiduciary duties to unitholders.

At three percent below the 52-week high, the base case offers about 10.5% price upside and roughly 17% total return, against 15.5% downside if long rates keep rising or the spread-driven component of first-half margin proves transitional. That is a fair, income-led holding: own it for the covered 6.76% distribution, do not add here, and revisit as a buyer toward $18.00 or on the specific upgrade triggers above.
