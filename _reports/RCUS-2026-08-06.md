## Executive Summary

Arcus completed a transformation over the last eight months that leaves it a materially different company than the one that entered 2025. Three Phase 3 programs in the anti-TIGIT franchise were discontinued — STAR-221 (December 2025), STAR-121 (April 2026) and PACIFIC-8 (August 2026) — Gilead declined the $100M sixth-anniversary option continuation payment, the collaboration term expired July 14, 2026, and on August 5, 2026 Gilead surrendered all three board seats under an amended Investor Rights Agreement, shrinking the board from eleven to eight while retaining a ~24.7% equity stake. What remains is casdatifan, a wholly-owned HIF-2α inhibitor in clear-cell renal cell carcinoma with two Phase 3 programs, a royalty interest in quemliclustat (licensed to Gilead, Phase 3 PRISM-1 data due 1H 2027), and an early immunology portfolio whose first molecule enters healthy-volunteer dosing in August 2026.

The arithmetic is straightforward. Market capitalization of ~$3.48B less $775M of cash and marketable securities plus $101M of Hercules debt gives an implied enterprise value of roughly $2.8B, of which almost all is casdatifan. Against that, GAAP revenue — which has never included a dollar of product sales — is guided to $65-75M for FY2026 after $247M in FY2025, implying only $7-17M in the second half, and approaches zero from 2027. Gross R&D fell 18% year over year in 1H 2026 but net R&D fell only 10%, because partnership reimbursements dropped 49% as the Gilead cost-sharing engine wound down. The company guides to ~$600M of year-end 2026 cash and runway "until at least the second half of 2028," which is arithmetically achievable at the implied 2H 2026 burn rate but leaves minimal cushion while a second registrational Phase 3 (PEAK-20) starts and five new combination cohorts open.

The decisive external fact is that Merck already won the randomized trial in PEAK-1's setting, against PEAK-1's comparator. LITESPARK-011 (ASCO GU, February 2026) showed belzutifan plus lenvatinib beating cabozantinib monotherapy in IO-experienced ccRCC — median PFS 14.8 versus 10.7 months, HR 0.70, ORR 52.6% versus 39.6%. That validates the mechanism and gives a credible read on PEAK-1's control arm, which raises PEAK-1's probability of success above typical Phase 3 base rates. It also means a positive PEAK-1 could arrive against a standard of care that has moved on, forcing Arcus into cross-trial superiority arguments. Merck is simultaneously pushing belzutifan earlier — adjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan received FDA approval in mid-2026 — which enlarges the HIF-2α-experienced population and is precisely why Arcus opened an ARC-20 cohort with AVEO's tivozanib in belzutifan-pretreated patients. We rate the stock HOLD at 4.55, continuing a declining score trajectory (5.3 → 5.2 → 4.8 → 4.55), with the downgrade driven by the completed revenue cliff, the competitive re-ranking of the mechanism, and the absence of margin of safety at $27.70 after a 190% twelve-month advance.

## Company Overview

Arcus is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company in Hayward, California, reporting as a single operating segment with no approved products and no product revenue. Its self-description in the Q2 2026 10-Q is "a late clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on developing differentiated molecules for patients with cancer and immunology diseases," and it states that "we expect our next wave of clinical-stage molecules to come from our immunology programs." That is a genuine change in center of gravity from the oncology-collaboration model that defined the prior five years.

**Casdatifan** carries the value. Arcus states it "solely own[s] casdatifan and lead[s] its global development and commercialization"; Taiho holds an exclusive license limited to Japan and certain other Asian territories excluding China, and Gilead — following the July 14, 2026 term expiry — can no longer obtain rights. PEAK-1 (casdatifan plus cabozantinib versus cabozantinib alone, IO-experienced metastatic ccRCC) targets enrollment completion by year-end 2026. PEAK-20 (casdatifan plus nivolumab plus ipilimumab, first line) is expected to initiate by year-end 2026. ARC-20, the Phase 1/2 platform study, generates supporting data: the 100mg QD tablet monotherapy cohort in late-line ccRCC showed median PFS 15.1 months and confirmed ORR 45% (February 2026), improved from a 12.2-month pooled figure across 121 patients in October 2025, and the monotherapy dataset was published in *Nature* in July 2026 linking serum EPO suppression to response.

**Quemliclustat** (CD73 inhibitor) is in Phase 3 PRISM-1 in first-line metastatic pancreatic cancer, fully enrolled since September 2025, with results expected 1H 2027. Gilead holds the license; Arcus funds and operationalizes the trial and receives tiered royalties of mid-teens to low-twenties percent plus a U.S. co-promotion/profit-share option. Orphan designations came from FDA (June 2025) and EMA (May 2026).

**Immunology** comprises AB102 (oral MRGPRX2 antagonist, first-in-human August 2026, chronic spontaneous urticaria proof-of-concept mid-2027), an oral TNFR1-selective TNF inhibitor (clinic early 2027), and CCR6, STAT6, CD89 and CD40L programs targeted IND-ready by end-2027. Gilead retains time-limited options on AB801 and AB598 at $150M each, on the TNF program at $45M or $150M depending on stage, and on one oncology research program at $60M or $150M.

### Revenue is an accounting runoff

Two disclosures make the nature of this revenue unusually explicit. Q2 2025 revenue of $160M included a one-time $143M cumulative catch-up when Gilead returned its etrumadenant license, reallocating $256M of remaining deferred revenue. Q2 2026 "other collaboration" revenue of $25M, up from $8M, includes $18M recognized *because* Gilead declined the $100M option continuation payment and the associated material right lapsed. The reported revenue growth in the quarter is the accounting consequence of a partner walking away.

Management's own guidance quantifies the cliff: FY2026 revenue of $65-75M with $58M booked in 1H, implying $7-17M in 2H. Deferred revenue was $56M at June 30, 2026 versus $78M at December 31, 2025, and $14M of that relates to Gilead's rights to certain studies, recognizable only on reinstatement or lapse. This deferred balance is not backlog — it is cash already collected, not future contracted cash. Any screen treating RCUS revenue growth as a fundamental signal will be misled. We note a minor unresolved item: FY2024 reported operating income of −$330M does not tie to revenue less R&D less G&A (−$310M), a $20M gap; the other three years reconcile exactly and net income figures agree across sources.

### Liquidity and the runway arithmetic

Cash plus marketable securities of $775M at June 30, 2026, down from $1,010M at December 31, 2025. Stockholders' equity $464M against an accumulated deficit of $1,704M. Current assets $756M versus current liabilities $198M (3.8x). Debt is $101M carrying value on the Hercules facility ($100M drawn of a $250M commitment), maturity September 2030, interest-only through September 2028, effective rate 12.85%, secured by substantially all assets.

The 1H 2026 decline in cash and securities was $235M against a $257M operating outflow, with $19M of financing inflow. Reaching the guided ~$600M year-end figure requires a 2H decline of roughly $175M — a 25% step-down from 1H, consistent with the domvanalimab wind-down and inclusive of a $15M Taiho PEAK-1 Japan milestone in Q3. From $600M, funding through "at least 2H 2028" at that implied ~$350M annual rate covers roughly 20 months, reaching mid-to-late 2028. Achievable, with no cushion.

Consensus provides a useful cross-check. Next-year (2027) consensus EPS of −$3.10 on ~127-135M shares implies a net loss near $400M, against the ~$300M cash burn the runway claim requires. The gap closes only if non-cash stock compensation runs $60M or more annually — plausible for a company of this size but not verifiable in the data provided. Investors should assume the $200M at-the-market equity facility gets used, and probably a marketed offering, before PEAK-1 reads out.

### Covenant structure is correlated with the risk it should absorb

The December 2025 Hercules amendment imposes a minimum cash covenant beginning July 2027, "conditionally waived if our market capitalization exceeds a specified threshold." The threshold is not disclosed. Undrawn tranches are milestone-gated: $50M on BLA-enabling clinical milestones, $50M on FDA approval, $50M at lender discretion. A casdatifan setback would compress market cap (potentially voiding the waiver), block the milestone tranches, and impair equity access at the same time. Mitigating: the covenant does not bite until July 2027, by which point 2H 2026 ARC-20 data, PEAK-1 enrollment completion and PRISM-1 results will be known.

### Dilution history

Shares outstanding went from 92.2M (December 2024) to 125.3M (December 2025) to 127.0M (June 2026) — a 36% increase in calendar 2025 via two underwritten offerings totaling 29.4M shares for ~$438M gross, including a February 2025 deal priced at $11.00 (Gilead took 1.4M shares for $15M). That is roughly 60% below the current price. The 22.0M antidilutive securities disclosed in the Q2 10-Q (17.4M options, 4.3M RSUs, 0.3M ESPP) are not current fully diluted capitalization — they include unvested and out-of-the-money instruments — but if all were issued they equal 17.3% of shares outstanding, or 14.8% post-issuance ownership dilution. The $200M ATM at $27.70 would add ~7.2M shares, 5.4% post-issuance dilution.

**Financial health score: 5.0.** For a clinical-stage biotech, $775M of liquidity with two-plus years of runway and modest absolute leverage is not distressed, and the current ratio is comfortable. Offsetting: revenue going to zero, a 49% collapse in reimbursements, 12.85% secured debt with an undisclosed market-cap-linked covenant, and a demonstrated willingness to issue equity at trough prices.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here means pipeline value creation, not revenue. On the income statement, the direction is unambiguously down: FY2026 revenue of $65-75M against FY2025's $247M, and near zero thereafter absent new business development.

Consensus loss estimates have narrowed — current-quarter −$0.83 versus −$0.94 ninety days ago (+12.2%), next-quarter −$0.74 from −$0.93 (+20.0%), next-year −$3.10 from −$3.53 (+12.1%) — with no revisions in the trailing 30 days. This is not the estimate-revision inflection signal that precedes re-rating in operating businesses; it is analysts marking down spend as three Phase 3 programs wind down. Reading it as a growth signal would be a category error. The earnings track record (three beats, one miss over four quarters, most recently −$0.72 versus −$0.92) reflects revenue-recognition artifacts and expense timing, not cash-generating improvement. The Q2 beat was largely the $18M non-cash Gilead material-right acceleration plus the Taiho milestone.

The genuine growth case rests on three things. First, casdatifan's clinical profile: 15.1-month median PFS and 45% confirmed ORR in late-line monotherapy, with the *Nature* publication establishing a pharmacodynamic biomarker link. Second, catalyst density in 2H 2026 — initial efficacy for casdatifan plus zimberelimab in first line, initial safety for the triplet with ipilimumab, more mature ORR plus first PFS Kaplan-Meier data for ~45 second-line patients on casdatifan plus cabozantinib with ≥18 months follow-up, and updated late-line monotherapy data including overall survival. Third, capital-efficient expansion: BMS sponsors and runs the ROSETTA arms and Arcus only supplies drug; Summit supplies ivonescimab and AVEO supplies tivozanib into Arcus-sponsored ARC-20 cohorts; a fourth undisclosed anti-PD-x/VEGF bispecific collaboration initiates Q4 2026.

Quemliclustat is a genuinely under-modeled option. PRISM-1 is fully enrolled with data in 1H 2027 in first-line metastatic pancreatic cancer, the trial cost is largely sunk, and Arcus receives mid-teens to low-twenties royalties plus a U.S. co-promote option. Base rates in that indication are poor and CD73 inhibition is unvalidated in Phase 3, so this belongs in the model as a low-probability, moderate-payoff option rather than a second pillar.

**Growth prospects score: 5.2**, down from 5.5. The pipeline breadth and catalyst calendar are real; the revenue cliff is now quantified by management, the reimbursement engine is gone, and the immunology programs are years from a value inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples do not apply. Trailing and forward P/E of −8.9 is meaningless, and the peer set is heterogeneous: median P/E of 18.1 is set by two commercial-stage names (CPRX 18.1, HRMY 14.2, VCEL 91.5), while the two clinical-stage comparables carry negative earnings (CRSP −8.2 at ~$5B market cap, XENE −11.1 at ~$5B). The relevant comparison is enterprise value per unit of de-risked pipeline. On that basis, RCUS at ~$2.8B implied EV for one wholly-owned Phase 3 asset plus a royalty option plus preclinical immunology sits in line with CRSP and XENE, not at a discount.

The scenario arithmetic drives the score:

**Bear ($8, −71%).** PEAK-1 fails, or the immune-mediated adverse-event observations that halted enrollment in eVOLVE-RCC02 propagate into the first-line combination strategy. Year-end 2026 cash of ~$600M less $101M debt is $499M of net cash, or about $3.84 per share on a dilution-adjusted ~130M shares. Add residual value for quemliclustat royalties and an early immunology portfolio in the clinic and $7-9 is the plausible floor. This is below the lowest published analyst target of $22, which is itself worth noting: the sell-side low target embeds no failure scenario.

**Base ($32, +16%).** PEAK-1 enrollment completes on schedule, 2H 2026 ARC-20 readouts are supportive without being definitive, PEAK-20 initiates, and Arcus raises capital via the ATM or a marketed deal. The stock re-rates modestly on catalyst progression and reduced execution uncertainty. $32 is roughly $3.3B EV pro forma for $600M cash, or a ~17% premium to today's pipeline valuation.

**Bull ($47, +70%).** First-line combination data in 2H 2026 show clean tolerability and differentiated activity, positioning casdatifan credibly as the first TKI-sparing HIF-2α regimen; PRISM-1 reads out positive in 1H 2027; the consensus target range migrates toward the current high. This matches the analyst high of $47.

Roughly +16% to base and +70% to bull against −71% to bear. On even a 50/50 weighting between the bull and bear tails — generous given three Phase 3 failures in the last eight months — the expected value does not clear the current price by a margin that justifies new capital. The analyst mean of $37.17 and median of $38.00 imply 34-37% upside, and 10 of 13 ratings are Buy, but those targets appear to average a success case with a partial-success case rather than probability-weight a failure that lands near cash.

Analyst targets function as floors on inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates. That condition does not hold here: there are no earnings to be cheap against, the estimate revisions are loss narrowing, and the targets and the price both reflect the same binary. HC Wainwright's 33% target increase in July 2026 corroborates the first-line strategy's appeal without independently validating it.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** Not a value trap in the classic sense, and not expensive on any metric that would flag it — the issue is that the price already discounts a favorable casdatifan outcome, leaving no margin of safety against a downside that goes to cash.

## Competitive Landscape

The HIF-2α mechanism has been validated by a competitor in the exact line of therapy PEAK-1 addresses, against PEAK-1's exact comparator.

LITESPARK-011 (Motzer et al., ASCO GU Abstract LBA417, February 2026) randomized 747 IO-experienced ccRCC patients to belzutifan 120mg plus lenvatinib 20mg versus cabozantinib 60mg:

| Endpoint | Belzutifan + lenvatinib | Cabozantinib |
|---|---|---|
| Median PFS (BICR) | 14.8 mo | 10.7 mo (HR 0.70; 95% CI 0.59-0.84) |
| 2-year PFS | 35.6% | 19.1% |
| Confirmed ORR | 52.6% | 39.6% (p=0.0002) |
| Median DOR | 23.0 mo | 12.3 mo |
| Median OS | 34.9 mo | 27.6 mo (narrowly missed significance) |

Safety trade-off: anemia 69.2% versus 25.6%, hypoxia 15.4% versus 0%, cardiac dysfunction 7.0% versus 1.1% for the combination.

The constructive reading: adding a HIF-2α inhibitor to a VEGFR-TKI produces a clinically meaningful benefit over TKI alone, and cabozantinib monotherapy performs at ~10.7 months in this population, giving PEAK-1 a credible control-arm expectation. PEAK-1's structural probability of success is therefore higher than a typical Phase 3, which is the single strongest argument for owning the stock through the readout.

The adverse reading: by the time PEAK-1 reports, practice in that line may be belzutifan plus lenvatinib rather than cabozantinib monotherapy, making regulatory success compatible with a compromised commercial position. Arcus would then need cross-trial arguments — casdatifan's ~15-month monotherapy median PFS and a potentially milder tolerability profile than belzutifan's anemia and hypoxia burden. Cross-trial comparison between single-arm ARC-20 data and randomized LITESPARK-011 data is not valid evidence of relative efficacy, and buyers should not treat it as such. Whether Merck has filed for or received approval in this specific second-line indication is unconfirmed in the evidence reviewed, which materially affects how fast the comparator could be superseded.

Merck is also moving belzutifan earlier: FDA approval of adjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan in intermediate-high-or-higher-risk ccRCC (mid-2026, with the Phase 3 published in NEJM in July 2026). Each patient treated in the adjuvant setting is a patient who may be HIF-2α-experienced by the time they need later-line therapy, and it is an open question whether casdatifan retains activity after belzutifan. Arcus is running exactly that experiment — the AVEO/tivozanib ARC-20 cohort in patients with two or more prior lines including a belzutifan-containing regimen, explicitly framed as elucidating "the impact of prior HIF-2α inhibitor treatment on casdatifan's activity." Well-designed, and existential.

Arcus's chosen white space is first-line TKI-free. Management characterizes first-line ccRCC as roughly one-third IO/IO and two-thirds IO/TKI, and targets the IO/IO third with PEAK-20 plus three-to-four bispecific combinations. That is genuine differentiation and also the least de-risked, most capital-intensive part of the plan, with the eVOLVE-RCC02 immune-mediated adverse-event observation standing as a caution on casdatifan/IO combinability.

**Competitive position score: 4.2.** Trajectory dominates level. Arcus improved its economics (casdatifan unencumbered by Gilead; a success accrues almost entirely to Arcus in the U.S. and Europe) while losing its position in the mechanism race, its IO franchise and its funding partner.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-asset binary.** After three Phase 3 discontinuations, casdatifan carries essentially all equity value. The Q2 10-Q now carries the risk language "if we are not able to differentiate our product against other approved products within the same class of drugs our business would be materially harmed."

**Management's own translation warning.** The 10-Q states that EDGE-Gastric showed median OS beyond two years against 13-14 month Phase 3 benchmarks, "Despite" which STAR-221 was discontinued for futility, and then that "data from Phase 2 studies (such as ARC-8 and ARC-20) may not be predictive of the results of any of our Phase 3 studies even if evaluating the same regimen and setting." Management is applying its own recent failure to the casdatifan platform study by name.

**Disclosed safety signal.** Arcus and AstraZeneca agreed not to re-open enrollment in eVOLVE-RCC02 "following observations of potential immune-mediated AEs." The entire first-line strategy is casdatifan combined with immunotherapy. The partner agent is not named in the reviewed text.

**Manufacturing concentration.** WuXi Biologics is the sole manufacturer of zimberelimab, the anti-PD-1 backbone in first-line casdatifan cohorts. The 10-Q flags the U.S. BIOSECURE Act (described as enacted December 2025) and states that a "biotechnology company of concern" designation could materially impact supply or costs, with a reported five-year grandfathering for existing contracts. The 10-Q also names cabozantinib — PEAK-1's comparator — among standard-of-care drugs that are "challenging to source."

**Covenant/market-cap correlation** as described above, plus an IRS notice of examination for FY2023 received June 2026 with no accrual taken.

**Governance.** Gilead retains ~24.7% of the stock with no board representation, a classified board, supermajority charter amendment requirements, and no stockholder action by written consent. A March 23, 2026 8-K under Item 5.02 (departure/election of officers or directors) was not retrievable; its content is unknown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 71.3% against 61.7% one-year realized — rich by 9.6 points — and has collapsed from 103.0% on July 19, a post-earnings vol crush rather than a change in the risk assessment. Term structure is in contango (71.3% at one month, 79.1% at three, 77.3% at six), and implied moves widen from ±13.4% (August 21) to ±22.9% (September 18) to ±37.9% (December 18). The market is pricing the binary out in the six-month window, which spans the 2H 2026 ARC-20 readouts and PEAK-1 enrollment completion.

The put/call skew pattern is the more informative signal: 16.3% at one month, 37.0% at three months, then −3.7% at six months. Elevated put demand concentrated in the September tenor indicates hedging or directional bearishness specifically around the near-term data window, while the six-month tenor shows no such tilt. Combined with 16.4% short interest and 8.2 days to cover, institutional positioning leans bearish into the readouts. That cuts two ways for anyone considering the short side: a clean first-line dataset into 16% short interest and thin daily volume (1.33M average) would produce a violent squeeze. This divergence — bearish options and short positioning against a 10-buy/3-hold sell-side and a mean target 34% above spot — is precisely what a binary looks like when it is fairly priced, and it supports HOLD over either directional call.

**Risk factors score: 3.5.** The magnitude of unpriced binary risk is high: a $2.8B pipeline valuation against a ~$4 per-share cash floor, a disclosed combination safety signal, sole-source geopolitical manufacturing exposure, and a covenant whose waiver depends on the market cap that a failure would destroy.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $47 (+70%)

Casdatifan's monotherapy profile (15.1-month median PFS, 45% confirmed ORR, *Nature*-published EPO biomarker) is best-in-class among HIF-2α inhibitors, and PEAK-1's design is structurally favorable — LITESPARK-011 already demonstrated HR 0.70 for HIF-2α plus TKI over cabozantinib monotherapy, so the mechanism question is largely settled and PEAK-1 is a dosing, tolerability and execution question. Arcus owns the asset outright in the U.S. and Europe with only Taiho carved out for Japan/Asia-ex-China, so success accrues almost entirely to shareholders. 2H 2026 delivers first-line combination efficacy and safety plus mature second-line PFS on ~45 patients with ≥18 months follow-up; clean data establishes the TKI-sparing first-line position where no competitor has a Phase 3 win, backed by four bispecific combinations at low incremental cost (BMS sponsors ROSETTA; Summit and AVEO supply drug). PRISM-1 in 1H 2027 is a free option in a large indication. Gilead's retained 24.7% stake and four remaining program options ($150M each for AB801 and AB598) provide non-dilutive financing paths. Short interest of 16.4% with 8.2 days to cover amplifies any positive surprise.

### Bear Case → $8 (−71%)

Arcus has failed three Phase 3 trials in eight months, and its own filing warns that ARC-20 may not predict Phase 3 outcomes. Revenue goes to near zero from 2027 while gross R&D remains elevated (annualizing 1H 2026 gross R&D of $271M implies ~$540M) and personnel cost is flat, and reimbursements have collapsed 49%, so every incremental dollar of program spend now hits Arcus alone. The guided runway to 2H 2028 requires 2027 burn near $300M against consensus implying a ~$400M loss, so dilution before the PEAK-1 readout is close to certain — with a $200M ATM available and a track record of issuing at $11.00. Merck controls the mechanism narrative: belzutifan plus lenvatinib beat PEAK-1's comparator, adjuvant belzutifan is approved and expanding the HIF-2α-experienced pool, and Arcus is running a defensive experiment to test whether casdatifan even works post-belzutifan. The first-line TKI-free strategy depends on casdatifan/IO combinability, and enrollment in eVOLVE-RCC02 was not reopened after potential immune-mediated adverse events. Insiders have sold consistently and bought nothing; the July 2027 minimum-cash covenant is waived only above an undisclosed market-cap threshold, and the undrawn Hercules tranches are gated on milestones that a failure would prevent. At $8, the stock is net cash plus a royalty stub.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold_rationale: uncertain).** Existing holders should retain positions through the 2H 2026 data window; new capital should wait. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and it is not a continuation of a stale thesis — the composition of the risk has changed materially (single-asset concentration is now complete, the revenue cliff is quantified, the competitive ranking of the mechanism is established), which is why the score falls to 4.55 from 4.8.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. 2H 2026 first-line ARC-20 data showing casdatifan plus zimberelimab (and the ipilimumab triplet) with no immune-mediated safety pattern resembling eVOLVE-RCC02, alongside second-line casdatifan plus cabozantinib median PFS meaningfully beyond the 14.8-month LITESPARK-011 benchmark on ~45 patients with ≥18 months follow-up.
2. A financing event completed on non-dilutive or minimally dilutive terms — a Gilead option exercise on AB801 or AB598 at $150M, or a new ex-U.S. casdatifan partnership — that removes the pre-readout equity-issuance overhang.
3. Price toward $18-20 with the above clinical picture intact, which would restore an asymmetry that does not exist at $27.70.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any 2H 2026 casdatifan combination dataset showing dose-limiting immune-mediated toxicity, or ARC-20 first-line ORR/PFS that fails to differentiate from IO/TKI standards.
2. PEAK-1 enrollment completion slipping past year-end 2026, or PEAK-20 initiation deferred, either of which extends time-to-data against a fixed cash base.
3. Evidence that casdatifan lacks activity in the belzutifan-pretreated AVEO/tivozanib cohort, which would cap the addressable population as belzutifan moves into adjuvant use.
4. An equity raise exceeding 15% of shares outstanding at a price below $22, or disclosure that the Hercules market-cap waiver threshold sits near the current capitalization.

**Reassessment timeline:** the 2H 2026 ARC-20 readouts and the Q3 2026 report (expected early November) are the next natural checkpoints, followed by PEAK-1 enrollment completion and PEAK-20 initiation at year-end 2026, and PRISM-1 results in 1H 2027. The options market prices ±37.9% by the December 18 expiry, which is a reasonable estimate of the distribution over that window.

## Conclusion

Arcus is a fairly priced binary. The pipeline valuation of ~$2.8B rests on a wholly-owned HIF-2α inhibitor with strong single-arm data and a Phase 3 whose success probability is genuinely elevated by a competitor's randomized win against the same comparator.
