## Executive Summary

Wabtec is executing a multi-year transformation from a rail component supplier into a vertically integrated rail technology platform, compounding EPS at 22% annually since 2022 through simultaneous margin expansion (GAAP operating margin from 12.1% to 16.1%, adjusted to 21.9% in Q1 2026), share count reduction (6.4% over three years), and organic plus acquisitive revenue growth. The company's backlog grew to $30.8B as of Q1 2026 (up from $27.4B at year-end 2025) with a book-to-bill of 1.39x in 2025, providing exceptional revenue visibility. Q1 2026 12-month backlog of $9.2B covers approximately 75% of 2026 estimated revenue.

The stock trades at approximately 39.5x trailing GAAP P/E but a more relevant 25-26x on FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $10.25-$10.65. At current prices of ~$270, the stock has appreciated approximately 40% over the past year, driven by fundamental improvement rather than multiple expansion alone. The primary investment debate centers on whether the valuation is justified by superior growth (22% EPS CAGR vs. peer 10-15%) and the structural advantages of a 24,600-unit locomotive installed base generating 60% aftermarket revenue. We believe it is, though acknowledge near-term risks from $3.5B in recent acquisitions requiring integration, elevated post-acquisition leverage ($6.5B total debt), and meaningful Kazakhstan concentration.

## Company Overview

Wabtec operates two segments: Freight (~72% of revenue) encompassing locomotive manufacturing, aftermarket services, digital intelligence solutions, and components; and Transit (~28%) providing brakes, HVAC, doors, signaling, and services for passenger rail vehicles globally. The company's installed base of nearly 24,600 locomotives creates a captive aftermarket ecosystem, analogous to aerospace engine aftermarket economics, where original equipment sales generate decades of recurring service and parts revenue.

Approximately 50% of revenue is international, 60% is aftermarket, and the company employs ~31,000 people across 50+ countries. The 2019 GE Transportation merger transformed the business from a mid-cap components company into the dominant Western freight locomotive manufacturer, competing primarily against Progress Rail (a Caterpillar subsidiary) domestically and CRRC (China) internationally.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue grew from $8.36B (2022) to $11.17B (2025), a 10% CAGR, with Q1 2026 accelerating to +13% YoY ($2.95B). Organic growth contributed $464M (+4.5%) in 2025, supplemented by $355M from acquisitions. The FY2026 guide of $12.19-12.49B implies continued acceleration.

EPS acceleration is the more compelling metric: $4.86 (2022) → $5.92 → $7.56 → $8.97 → guided $10.25-$10.65 (2026). This 22% CAGR reflects operating leverage, mix improvement toward higher-margin aftermarket/digital, and disciplined capital return. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.71 (up 18.9% YoY) beat consensus, and management raised full-year guidance on the result. The FY2026 guidance midpoint of $10.45 implies approximately 16.5% growth over FY2025 adjusted EPS of $8.97.

### Margin Profile

Gross margins expanded 370bps over three years to 34.1% in 2025. Adjusted operating margins reached 21.9% in Q1 2026. The widening GAAP-to-adjusted gap (440bps in Q1) reflects active restructuring — Integration 3.0 targets $115-140M in incremental run-rate synergies by 2028, with $125-155M in associated charges. This is a deliberate margin expansion program with quantified targets and track record (Integration 2.0 delivered on schedule).

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

FCF of $1.50B in 2025 represents a 13.4% FCF margin and approximately 128% conversion of net income, indicating high-quality earnings with limited accrual distortion. Capex runs at only 2.3% of revenue, reflecting an asset-efficient model relative to heavy industrial peers.

Following the 2025 acquisition spree and the Dellner Couplers acquisition that closed in February 2026, total debt increased to $6.54B as of March 31, 2026, with total available liquidity of $2.09B. Moody's upgraded the credit to Baa2 in March 2025. The goodwill balance of $10.2B (46% of assets at year-end 2025) is elevated and warrants monitoring for impairment risk, though it reflects the economic value of acquired installed bases and customer relationships rather than speculative overpayment. With $1.5B+ annual FCF, deleveraging capacity is strong, though current leverage is meaningfully higher than pre-acquisition levels.

### Capital Allocation

Management has demonstrated disciplined allocation: $1.1B in buybacks in 2024, $223M in 2025 (reduced during peak M&A activity), and $242M in Q1 2026 alone, indicating buybacks have already resumed at an accelerated pace. The board refreshed repurchase authorization to $1.2B in February 2026. Growing dividends and acquisition spending directed at adjacent technologies (NDT testing, sensor tech, couplers) expand the addressable market while maintaining the recurring-revenue business model. The portfolio optimization strategy of actively divesting low-margin businesses further supports margin trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**
1. North American locomotive fleet modernization (aging fleet, emissions regulations, fuel efficiency demand)
2. International expansion — Kazakhstan $4.2B deal demonstrates ability to win mega-orders in emerging markets
3. Digital intelligence solutions across the installed base — though recent growth in this area has been primarily acquisition-driven (Inspection Technologies, Frauscher), with organic Digital Intelligence sales declining modestly ($22M) in 2025. Management is investing to accelerate organic adoption, but this remains a watch item.
4. Transit investment globally driven by urbanization and decarbonization mandates

**Acquisitive Growth:**
The $3.5B deployed in 18 months adds adjacent technology capabilities (Inspection Technologies for NDT, Frauscher for train detection, Dellner for couplers) that strengthen the full-stack offering and create cross-selling opportunities into the captive installed base.

**Backlog as Growth Visibility:**
Total backlog reached $30.8B as of Q1 2026. The 12-month backlog of $9.2B means only ~$3.1B needs to be booked and shipped within the year to hit guidance at the midpoint of ~$12.34B. This substantially de-risks the growth outlook.

**Estimate Revisions:**
FY2026 EPS estimates have risen 3.7% over 90 days; FY2027 estimates up 5.1%. While below the 30% threshold that represents the strongest BUY signal, the direction is consistently positive across all forecast periods, indicating growing consensus confidence in the earnings trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$270, WAB trades at:
- ~39.5x trailing GAAP P/E (on $6.83 GAAP EPS which includes restructuring charges)
- ~30.1x FY2025 adjusted EPS ($8.97)
- ~25.8x FY2026E guidance mid-point adjusted EPS ($10.45)
- ~22.3x FY2027E EPS ($12.13)

On a forward P/E basis, WAB at ~25.8x FY2026 EPS trades at a discount to the stated peer median of 31.2x (Cummins at 31.2x, Caterpillar at 43.1x, Paccar at 25.1x). This discount exists despite Wabtec's superior growth rate (22% EPS CAGR vs. peer 10-15%) and its aftermarket-dominant revenue model providing greater earnings stability than pure-cycle equipment makers. The market may be applying a temporary discount for integration risk and elevated leverage following the acquisition spree.

The PEG ratio on forward estimates: 25.8x / 16.5% growth = 1.6x. This is reasonable for an industrial compounder with ~128% FCF conversion and $30.8B backlog visibility.

Analyst consensus targets range from $280 to $318, with a median of $303, implying 12-15% upside from current levels. Given the Q1 beat and guidance raise, these targets are likely to drift higher in the near term.

The valuation constraint preventing a higher score: at ~26x forward, the multiple embeds meaningful execution on Integration 3.0, continued margin expansion, and successful acquisition integration. There is limited margin of safety against a cyclical freight downturn or integration stumbles. This is a "pay fair for quality" situation rather than a mispriced security.

## Competitive Landscape

Wabtec holds structural advantages that widen over time:

**Regulatory moat:** FRA, AAR, UIC, and ERA certifications require years of qualification testing. New locomotive designs take 5-7 years to develop and certify. This creates near-insurmountable barriers for potential entrants in Western markets.

**Installed base economics:** 24,600 locomotives with proprietary digital systems create decades-long aftermarket revenue streams with high switching costs. Railroads cannot easily replace Wabtec digital platforms without replacing entire locomotive fleets.

**Full-stack positioning:** Wabtec is uniquely positioned across OEM + aftermarket + digital + services in both freight and transit. No other Western competitor offers this breadth. Knorr-Bremse comes closest but lacks locomotive manufacturing.

**Patent portfolio:** The company maintains a large active patent portfolio with significant annual new filings, protecting technology positions in braking, propulsion, and digital systems.

The competitive position is improving through acquisitions that add adjacent capabilities (NDT, sensor tech, couplers) and deepen the technology stack offered to the captive installed base.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Acquisition Integration ($3.5B deployed in 18 months):** Integration 3.0 must deliver $115-140M in synergies by 2028. The GAAP-to-adjusted margin gap reflects ongoing restructuring costs. If synergies disappoint or integration distracts from organic execution, the margin expansion thesis breaks.

2. **Kazakhstan Concentration:** The $4.2B KTZ deal represents ~14% of total backlog and introduces meaningful country risk adjacent to Russia. Revenue recognition and collection depend on geopolitical stability in Central Asia.

3. **Tariff and Trade Policy:** 50% international sales with global sourcing creates direct tariff exposure. The 10-K acknowledges tariffs "unfavorably impacted cash from operations" in 2025. Further trade escalation could pressure margins or create supply chain disruption.

4. **Cyclical Freight Exposure:** Despite 60% aftermarket mix, new locomotive orders are cyclical. North American railcar build softness already impacted Components in 2025. A recession scenario could delay or cancel backlog orders.

5. **Digital Intelligence Organic Softness:** Digital Intelligence sales declined organically by $22M in 2025 in North America, with recent growth driven primarily by acquisitions. If the organic digital adoption thesis does not materialize, a key growth pillar is weaker than presented by headline segment growth.

6. **Elevated Leverage:** At $6.54B in total debt post-Dellner with $2.09B liquidity, the balance sheet carries more risk than the pre-acquisition period. While $1.5B+ annual FCF supports deleveraging, the company has less financial flexibility in a downturn scenario.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 28.0% versus 23.2% historical volatility indicates the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty, likely reflecting post-earnings positioning and broader macro concerns. The term structure is in mild backwardation (28.0% near-term vs. 25.3% 6-month), suggesting near-term event risk rather than structural concern. Put/call skew of 4.0% shows modestly elevated put demand, consistent with hedging activity on a stock near 52-week highs. The low OI put/call ratio (0.19) and low short interest (1.9% of float) suggest institutional positioning is constructively long.

## Conclusion

Wabtec merits a BUY recommendation based on: (1) a 22% EPS CAGR with demonstrated operational execution, (2) $30.8B backlog providing exceptional revenue visibility, (3) structural competitive advantages (regulatory certification barriers, installed base lock-in, 60% aftermarket mix), and (4) a clearly defined synergy program (Integration 3.0) providing additional margin upside through 2028.

The valuation at ~26x FY2026 guided earnings represents a discount to the peer median of 31.2x, which is unusual for an industrial compounder demonstrating this growth rate and quality of cash flow conversion. The primary risks — acquisition integration, elevated post-acquisition leverage, Kazakhstan concentration, organic digital softness, and cyclical exposure — are manageable given the scale of the backlog and FCF generation capacity, but warrant monitoring.

Position sizing should reflect the limited margin of safety and the elevated leverage profile. A 2% portfolio allocation is appropriate, with willingness to add toward 3% on any pullback to the $240-250 range.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected balance sheet figures (debt $6.54B, liquidity $2.09B vs. originally stated $5.5B/$3.2B) and organic digital softness qualification moderately increase the near-term risk profile relative to the original thesis, though the corrected Q1 EPS ($2.71 vs. $2.21) and updated backlog ($30.8B) partially offset.
