## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest U.S. LNG exporter and the second-largest globally, operating two major liquefaction facilities with combined operating and under-construction capacity exceeding 60 mtpa (over 53 mtpa currently in operation, with approximately 8 mtpa under construction or commissioning). Approximately 90% of production is contracted under long-term take-or-pay agreements, generating an estimated $290.6 billion in future contracted revenues (comprising $107.7B in fixed fees and $182.9B in variable fees based on forward price and delivery assumptions). This estimated revenue pool represents approximately 5.4x the current market capitalization and provides significant cash flow visibility through the mid-2030s.

The Strait of Hormuz disruption has removed approximately 20% of global LNG supply (primarily Qatari exports), providing Cheniere a significant near-term market tailwind as the dominant non-Middle Eastern supplier. Management raised 2026 EBITDA guidance to $7.25-$7.75B following record Q1 operational performance (187 cargoes loaded). At approximately 17.7x forward earnings, the stock offers moderate upside to the analyst consensus target of approximately $273.

Q1 2026 reported a $3.5B GAAP loss predominantly from IPM mark-to-market despite record adjusted results, which temporarily depressed the stock. This creates an entry opportunity for investors capable of distinguishing accounting volatility from operational fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two Gulf Coast liquefaction terminals: Sabine Pass (Louisiana, 30+ mtpa, 6 operational trains) and Corpus Christi (Texas, 30+ mtpa including Stage 3 under commissioning). Since its first cargo in February 2016, the company has exported over 4,760 cumulative LNG cargoes totaling over 325 million tonnes to 40+ countries.

The pricing structure consists of a fixed liquefaction fee (inflation-adjusted) plus a variable fee generally equal to 115% of Henry Hub natural gas prices. This structure passes through feedstock costs and limits true commodity exposure. Key long-term customers include JERA (through 2050), Shell, TotalEnergies, and major Asian/European utilities, with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue.

Growth pipeline includes: Corpus Christi Trains 8&9 (36.9% complete as of March 31, 2026, expected 2H 2028), SPL Expansion (up to 20 mtpa, FERC application active), and CCL Expansion (up to 24 mtpa, FERC filed February 2026). Combined, these projects could add approximately 59 mtpa of capacity.

## Growth Analysis

**Medium-Term (2028-2030):** Trains 8&9 (36.9% complete, expected 2H 2028) add approximately 3 mtpa. These are under fixed-price Bechtel EPC contract with substantially all capacity already contracted. The Hormuz disruption may accelerate FID decisions on SPL Expansion and CCL Expansion by enabling favorable contract terms with European buyers seeking supply diversification.

**Long-Term Demand Backdrop:** WoodMac forecasts global LNG demand growing 64% from 410 mtpa (2024) to 671 mtpa by 2040, implying approximately 104 mtpa of new production capacity needed. The European Parliament voted to ban all Russian gas (including LNG) by 2027, creating structural demand growth. Asia remains the primary growth driver despite temporary Chinese weakness.

## Valuation Assessment

At current prices near $251, Cheniere trades at approximately 17.7x forward FY2026 consensus EPS of approximately $14.61. The trailing P/E reflects FY2025's $24.13 EPS which included significant derivative gains that inflated reported earnings; forward metrics are the appropriate lens.

The analyst consensus price target is approximately $273, with a range of approximately $232-$301. This implies roughly 8-9% upside to the average target from the current price level.

Regarding the contracted revenue backlog ($290.6B estimated SPA revenues): this figure includes $107.7B in fixed fees and $182.9B in variable fees that are subject to forward price assumptions and require delivery of all contractual volumes. The fixed-fee component alone provides substantial contracted revenue visibility covering debt obligations, but the variable-fee portion should not be treated as guaranteed high-margin backlog. The combined figure represents management's estimate under specific assumptions disclosed in the 10-K.

The valuation reflects a business with 90% contracted cash flows and a visible growth trajectory. The derivative-driven GAAP volatility creates periodic sentiment dislocations that can compress the multiple versus pure-play infrastructure peers. This structural discount is partially justified by the legitimate mark-to-market noise and partially represents an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's competitive position has strengthened in the near term due to the Hormuz disruption:

1. **Qatar Energy** — the world's largest LNG exporter — is operationally impaired, with Baker Hughes indicating the Strait may not fully reopen until 2H 2026. Qatar warned its exports could remain impaired for years.

2. **Henry Hub pricing advantage:** Cheniere's cost-of-production (feedstock at U.S. domestic gas prices) creates a structural margin advantage when international spot prices (JKM, TTF) are elevated. The current JKM-Henry Hub spread exceeds $10/MMBtu.

3. **Operating track record:** 4,760+ cargoes over 10 years versus unproven competitors (Venture Global has experienced commissioning delays; NextDecade's Rio Grande is years from first production).

4. **Brownfield expansion sites:** Cheniere can expand at existing permitted locations, avoiding the 3-5 year greenfield permitting timeline that competitors face.

5. **Customer relationships:** Long-term SPAs with major utilities and national oil companies create switching costs and relationship depth that new entrants cannot replicate.

The primary competitive threat is medium-term supply additions: approximately 20 mtpa of new global supply entered the market in 2025, with more expected through 2028. However, the demand growth forecasts (64% to 2040) and current supply disruption create a favorable window for Cheniere to lock in additional long-term contracts at elevated fixed fees.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Derivative Accounting Volatility (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact on fundamentals):**
IPM agreement mark-to-market creates GAAP earnings swings of billions per quarter with no cash flow impact. Each Hormuz-related price spike creates a corresponding derivative liability that must be marked against income. The market frequently fails to distinguish between accounting and economics, creating selling pressure on headline numbers. The Q1 2026 derivative losses reduced book equity from $7.9B to approximately $3.8B, increasing reported leverage ratios significantly even though underlying business economics are unchanged.

**2. Geopolitical Resolution Risk (MODERATE probability, HIGH impact):**
If the Strait of Hormuz crisis resolves quickly, global LNG prices would normalize rapidly, removing the pricing tailwind. Cheniere's contracted model limits downside: fixed fees are earned regardless of spot prices. The structural advantage from the disruption would be confirmed only if Cheniere secures durable incremental long-term contracts or expansion FIDs during this window; absent that, the benefit remains a near-term market tailwind rather than a permanent positioning shift.

**3. Insider Selling Signal:**
Multiple C-suite executives sold $28.5M in stock at $290-$300 in late March 2026. While following vesting events, the coordinated timing suggests insiders may view peak pricing as transient. CEO Fusco disposed of all net vested shares. The stock has since fallen from those prices, partially validating insider timing.

**4. Section 301 Maritime Requirements:**
Beginning April 2029, increasing percentages of U.S. LNG exports must ship on U.S.-built vessels. Compliance is uncertain given current U.S. shipyard capacity. This creates a cost risk beginning 2029 that could modestly reduce margins.

**5. Construction Execution:**
Stage 3 is 96.5% complete (on track). Trains 8&9 at 36.9% under fixed-price Bechtel contract. Risk is manageable but non-zero for schedule delays.

**6. Medium-Term Oversupply:**
The 10-K acknowledges the LNG market is transitioning into "rapid growth" on the supply side. If global demand disappoints expectations (particularly in China, where imports declined 16% in 2025 due to Russian pipeline gas substitution), pricing could face sustained pressure.

## Conclusion

Cheniere Energy merits a BUY recommendation based on: (1) positive estimate revision trajectory driven by both structural volume growth and Hormuz pricing tailwinds, (2) dominant competitive positioning as the largest non-Middle Eastern LNG supplier during a period of supply disruption, (3) visible growth from Stage 3 commissioning and the 8&9 expansion project, and (4) a contracted cash flow profile that limits fundamental downside even if geopolitical tailwinds fade.

The primary risks preventing a higher-conviction sizing are the coordinated insider selling at $290-$300, the possibility of rapid geopolitical resolution removing the pricing premium, and near-term book-equity deterioration from derivative mark-to-market. The derivative accounting noise creates volatility that investors must accept, but it also creates periodic mispricings when headline GAAP losses drive momentum-oriented selling in a fundamentally sound infrastructure business. The current price, modestly below analyst consensus targets with positive estimate revision momentum, represents an attractive entry for patient investors with a 12-month horizon.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected consensus EPS ($14.61 vs. originally cited $18.80), forward P/E (17.7x vs. 12.9x), and analyst target upside (~8-9% vs. 27%) materially reduce the quantitative margin of safety supporting the BUY recommendation; the thesis now rests more heavily on qualitative factors (contracted cash flows, geopolitical positioning, Stage 3 ramp) than on valuation discount.
