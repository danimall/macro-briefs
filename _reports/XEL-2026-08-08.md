## Executive Summary

Xcel Energy is a four-subsidiary regulated utility holding company running a capital program that is large even by the standards of the current data-center build cycle: $60.0B of base capital expenditure over 2026-2030 against $65.6B of year-end 2025 net property, plant and equipment. The plan explicitly excludes the Colorado Near-Term Procurement, future generation RFPs, MISO/SPP transmission expansion, and the 1,900 MW of renewables tied to the Google data-center agreement in Minnesota. The demand evidence behind it is specific and regulator-endorsed — the CPUC approved a PSCo load forecast reflecting 3% compound sales growth through 2031 with roughly 5,400 MW of capacity need; SPS weather-normalized retail electric sales grew 5.8% in 2025.

The problem is conversion to per-share earnings. In 2025 net income rose 4.2% to $2,018M while GAAP diluted EPS *fell* from $3.44 to $3.42, because weighted-average diluted shares rose 4.6% and shares outstanding rose 8.6% (574.4M to 623.6M) on $3,349M of stock proceeds. Management's own bridge assigns $(0.18) of EPS drag to common equity financing. Simultaneously, utility-subsidiary earned ROE deteriorated to 8.40% ongoing (from 8.69%) and 7.60% GAAP (from 8.55%), with PSCo — the largest 2025 capex recipient at $5,440M — earning 7.55% ongoing against a 9.8% electric request. Free cash flow was negative $6,825M, and operating cash flow has declined 23% over three years ($5,327M to $4,083M) while capex nearly doubled. Financing supplied $6,981M. The 2026-2030 plan calls for a further $7.0B of equity and $22.82B of new debt.

At $78.07 the stock trades at approximately 18.9x the $4.12 2026 consensus and 17.2x the $4.54 2027 consensus, against a multi-utility peer median P/E of 19.7 and a peer median yield of 3.2% versus Xcel's ~3.0%. Two Colorado rate decisions covering $17.7B of combined rate base land in Q3 2026 on a historic 2025 test year — the least favorable convention during a spend surge — and every case decided in Xcel's footprint during 2025-2026 cleared at 9.8% ROE / 52.5% equity against requests of 10.0-10.85% and 55-56%. Against that, the Smokehouse Creek accrual of $430M is disclosed as the low end of a range that excludes penalties, punitive damages and government claims, with the Texas Attorney General having sued SPS in December 2025. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 10% including the dividend, against a -12% bear case and an unquantified liability tail, is approximately fair. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the overall score moves from 5.7 to 5.5, consistent with the -0.16/month trajectory but not accelerating.

## Company Overview

Substantially all earnings come from four rate-regulated utilities: NSP-Minnesota ($1.53 of 2025 GAAP diluted EPS, owns the nuclear fleet), PSCo in Colorado ($1.15), SPS in the Texas Panhandle and eastern New Mexico ($0.67), and NSP-Wisconsin ($0.27), plus $0.03 from the WYCO pipeline JV. Holdco and non-regulated activities were $(0.23). Revenue of $14,669M in 2025 was 82.9% electric and 16.7% gas. Only two reportable segments exist, and the company declines to disclose assets or capex by segment on the grounds that allocation would be arbitrary — which limits outside computation of segment returns on invested capital.

There is no product-market competition; the company is a franchise monopoly in each territory. Its economics are set by three variables: how much rate base it can add, what ROE and equity ratio commissions authorize on that base, and how long rates lag the spending. A dense rider structure mitigates the third — 2026 guidance assumes capital rider revenue rises $535-545M year over year, the largest single item in the guidance bridge and largely outside general rate cases.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Operating income rose to $2,583M in 2025 from $2,386M, on revenue of $14,669M (17.6% operating margin, versus 17.8% in 2024 and 17.5% in 2023). Note that automated screens circulating on this name report a 21.2% operating margin, a 16.6% net margin and 3.45x debt/equity; none reconcile to the as-filed statements (net margin is 2,018/14,669 = 13.8%; debt/equity computes to 1.44x). Margin is a weak tool here regardless — fuel and purchased gas pass through revenue with offsetting expense, inflating the denominator.

The 2025 revenue increase of $1,013M in electric decomposes as non-fuel riders +$250M, fuel and purchased power recovery +$214M, PTCs credited to customers +$172M, Minnesota/North Dakota rate outcomes +$116M, sales and demand +$97M, transmission +$79M, and other items. Only $97M of the $1,013M is volume and demand.

**Cash flow is the defining fact.** Operating cash flow: $5,327M (2023), $4,641M (2024), $4,083M (2025). Capex: $5,854M, $7,364M, $10,908M. Free cash flow: $(527)M, $(2,723)M, $(6,825)M. Adding the $1,282M dividend, roughly $8.1B was funded externally in one year. Management attributes the OCF decline to the Marshall Wildfire settlement payment and deferred fuel timing; normalizing for the ~$640M settlement still leaves a roughly $6.2B gap between normalized OCF and capex. Negative FCF during a rate-base build is the intended model, but the magnitude requires uninterrupted access to both debt and equity markets, and the 10-K names that dependency directly.

Two offsets deserve weight. Xcel *received* $671M of net federal cash in 2025 (and $633M in 2024), including tax-credit transfer proceeds, with $1,474M of federal credit carryforwards remaining. And $32.3B of carrying-value long-term debt carries a fair value of $29.9B — a $2.4B discount that the company has been monetizing.

**Leverage and coverage.** Total debt of $33,883M (LTD $31,832M + current $501M + short-term $1,550M) against $23,609M of equity gives 58.9% debt-to-capitalization and 1.44x debt/equity. Covenant limits are 65%/70%; Xcel Energy Inc. sits at 59.8% and subsidiaries at 44.9-50.0%. Interest charges grew from $925M (2022) to $1,468M (2025), a 59% increase, with 2026 guidance assuming a further $300-310M increase net of AFUDC-debt. Operating income over gross interest is 1.76x; adding AFUDC-equity and other income gives 2.12x. New-issue coupons of 5.05-5.85% against a legacy book containing 1.875-3.20% paper is the mechanical driver.

Liquidity as of February 23, 2026: $4.75B of facilities with $1,640M drawn, $3,110M available, $46M cash, plus $750M of undrawn term-loan capacity — approximately $3.9B. Two funding-strain markers: peak commercial paper usage during 2025 reached $2,965M (versus $1,314M in 2024), and in January 2026 the company added a $1.5B 364-day delayed-draw term loan, drawing $750M.

**Earnings quality.** Two items inflate the $3.80 "ongoing" 2025 base off which management guides 6-8+% growth. First, Xcel Energy Inc. repurchased $787M principal of NSP-Minnesota first mortgage bonds for $607M, booking a $162M pre-tax gain — roughly $0.20-0.22 per share after tax — and this was not excluded from ongoing EPS. Retiring debt at 77 cents on the dollar is genuine value capture, and with a $2.4B remaining discount the opportunity is not exhausted, so calling it purely non-recurring overstates the case. Second, AFUDC-equity, a non-cash construction-period credit, rose from $91M (2023) to $168M (2024) to $281M (2025), contributed +$0.27 to the 2025 EPS bridge, and is guided up a further $140-150M in 2026 — on 589M diluted shares that is roughly $0.19-0.25 of the ~$0.30 midpoint EPS increase. AFUDC is legitimate and enters rate base, but it converts to cash earnings only when assets are placed in service and rates reset. Reported EPS growth is running ahead of cash earnings growth.

**Earnings delivery.** The four-quarter record is one beat, one in-line, two misses: Q2 2026 beat by 17.8% ($0.93 versus $0.79), Q1 2026 was in line, Q4 2025 missed by 2.2%, and Q3 2025 missed by 7.0%. The current-quarter (Q3 2026) estimate was cut 4.4% over 30 days from $1.40 to $1.34, while full-year 2026 consensus held at $4.12 (+0.1% over both 30 and 90 days) and 2027 at $4.54 (+0.1% 30-day, +0.2% 90-day). The headline "estimates broadly falling" characterization reflects an intra-year timing shift, not deterioration in the annual number.

**Financial health: 5.2.** Down modestly from 5.5. The direction of coverage (1.76x EBIT/interest, +$300-310M of 2026 interest, +$26M of 2026 pension cost), three consecutive years of declining OCF, reliance on 364-day paper and near-tripled peak commercial paper, and a payout ratio of 60% on 2025 ongoing EPS versus a 45-55% target all point the same way. Covenant compliance, $3.9B of liquidity, A-range rating objective and large tax-credit cash inflows keep this out of the low bracket.

## Growth Analysis

The capital plan is $60.0B for 2026-2030, shaped $13.79B (2026), $15.13B (2027), $12.65B (2028), $9.02B (2029), $9.41B (2030). By function: transmission $15.44B, renewables $13.92B, distribution $13.71B, generation $9.54B, gas $3.72B, other $3.67B. By subsidiary: NSP-Minnesota $20.13B, SPS $19.03B, PSCo $17.60B, NSP-Wisconsin $4.03B. Financing is disclosed as $30.18B of operating cash flow net of dividends and pension funding, $22.82B of new debt and $7.0B of equity.

Guidance for 2026 is $4.04-4.16 of ongoing EPS (midpoint $4.10, +7.9% on $3.80), with long-term objectives of 6-8+% EPS growth, 4-6% dividend growth and a 45-55% payout. Key assumptions: constructive outcomes in *all* pending rate proceedings, normal weather, weather-normalized retail electric sales +3% (versus 2.2% actual in 2025), rider revenue +$535-545M, O&M +3%, D&A +$350-360M, interest +$300-310M, AFUDC-equity +$140-150M.

Demand evidence is real but geographically lumpy. Colorado C&I growth came from the information and energy sectors, with a CPUC-approved 3% CAGR forecast through 2031 and ~5,400 MW of capacity need. SPS grew 5.8% weather-normalized on oil-and-gas load — the same subsidiary that discloses a planned 100 MW wholesale roll-off in 2026 and is asking New Mexico retail customers to absorb $148M of a $175M request largely through cost reallocation. NSP-Minnesota was roughly flat (+0.3% retail, C&I -0.3%), which is precisely where the Google agreement would change the picture.

**The Google agreement, stated literally.** In Q1 2026 NSP-Minnesota entered an electric service agreement to power a new Google data center in Minnesota, under which Google, as a retail electric customer, pays all costs of its new service for the duration of the agreement per Minnesota large-load requirements. NSP-Minnesota is the service provider and prospective owner of supporting generation. Approval of the ESA and 1,900 MW of proposed renewables was expected to be filed with the MPUC by April 2026. Nothing is approved, so neither the revenue nor the 1,900 MW of incremental rate base should be counted. The cost-allocation structure addresses the classic stranded-cost objection. Note also that the FY2025 10-K carries a risk factor stating that large-load growth *increases* customer concentration, capital requirements and revenue variability — a mix shift toward hyperscale load is not evidence of diversification, and no single-customer revenue percentages are disclosed.

Post-2028 the renewables engine falls off sharply ($3.38B in 2028 to $1.15B in 2029), a shape consistent with a race to begin construction under the OBBB safe harbor. OBBB eliminates wind and solar PTCs/ITCs for facilities placed in service after 2027 where construction begins after July 4, 2026, and adds foreign-entity-of-concern rules for projects commencing after 2025. Management states no impact on the base plan because safe-harbor steps were taken; that is a forward-looking assertion, not an audited fact, and it concentrates execution risk into 2026-2028 — the same years the 10-K describes stretched supply chains for combustion turbines and transformers and strained construction labor.

**Growth prospects: 6.5.** The capex pipeline and load evidence are among the best in the sector, and the disclosed $60B is a floor. But the per-share conversion is where the thesis lives or dies, and 2025 demonstrated dilution fully offsetting net income growth, while a substantial minority of guided 2026 growth is non-cash AFUDC.

## Valuation Assessment

At $78.07 on the $4.12 2026 consensus (essentially at the $4.10 guidance midpoint), the multiple is 18.9x; on the $4.54 2027 consensus, 17.2x. Trailing GAAP EPS of $3.42 gives 22.8x; 2025 ongoing EPS of $3.80 gives 20.5x. The dividend, raised 4.0% in February 2026 to roughly $2.37 annualized, yields ~3.0%.

Peer context (multi-utilities): SRE 31.1x, DTE 24.0x, D 19.7x, ED 17.9x, PCG 13.0x, median 19.7x with a 3.2% median yield. Xcel therefore trades approximately at the peer median forward multiple with a slightly below-median yield. Consensus 2026-2027 EPS growth of 10.2% implies a PEG near 1.7. That is not a mispricing; it is a market-clearing price for a utility with a large pipeline and constrained per-share conversion.

Analyst targets are unusually dispersed: low $74, mean $92.67, median $92.50, high $102, against 3 strong buy / 14 buy / 1 hold / 1 strong sell. The mean implies +18.7% and 20.4x the 2027 number. We do not treat those targets as independent confirmation — they embed the same rate-base extrapolation as the bull case, and on a stock trading at the peer median with stable (not rising) full-year estimates, there is no revision-velocity signal to argue that consensus is stale. The +0.1% 30-day and +0.1-0.2% 90-day revisions to both 2026 and 2027 estimates are the relevant fact: consensus is not moving.

Two structural offsets to the multiple deserve naming. First, the discount rate: a bond-proxy equity funding roughly $8B a year externally faces a direct headwind on both its cost of capital and its terminal multiple from the prevailing rate environment. Second, the equity supply: $7.0B of planned issuance at roughly the current price is about 89M shares, ~14% of the 623.6M year-end count over five years (~2.7% annually); at $65 it would be ~108M shares. Part may be satisfied by the 27.3M shares already under forward contract (~$2.0B minimum proceeds, including 15.1M collared shares settling in December 2026 for which net cash and net share settlement are generally unavailable), and part may come via hybrids receiving rating-agency equity credit — the company already issued $900M of 6.25% junior subordinated notes due 2085 for exactly that purpose. Investors should not treat 650.9M as today's diluted count, nor 589M as the forward count.

**Valuation: 5.2.** No identified mispricing, no absolute yield support relative to peers, PEG near 1.7, and a compressing equity cushion.

## Competitive Landscape

Xcel's advantage inside its regulated construct is the right to self-build rather than contract via PPA — company-owned megawatts become rate base earning an authorized return, PPA megawatts are a pass-through cost with no earnings. The historical record is favorable: the SPS 2023 IRP portfolio was 4,528 MW company-owned of 5,168 MW nameplate (88%); the 2023 Colorado Resource Plan was roughly 3,100 MW of 5,835 MW (53%).

The trajectory is deteriorating. In February 2026 the CPUC approved 3,200 MW from the Colorado Near-Term Procurement, of which the only unconditionally approved company-owned item was a 200 MW gas combustion turbine, with 600-1,500 MW of company-owned wind/solar/storage only conditionally approved pending March 2026 information. The recommended December 2025 portfolio had been 4,900 MW with 2,100 MW company-owned. Colorado's willingness to award capacity to independent producers directly reduces the earnings yield on the same load growth.

The authorized-return trend is the second deteriorating vector. Every decision in Xcel's footprint during 2025-2026 cleared at 9.8% ROE and a 52.5% equity ratio: Wisconsin electric $126M of $151M requested, Wisconsin gas $22M of $24M, Michigan gas $1.6M (at 50% equity), North Dakota electric $24M of $45M requested (a 53% haircut). Minnesota's Department of Commerce re-asserted 9.25% in November 2025 surrebuttal. Against that record, PSCo's pending Colorado cases assume a 55% equity ratio and SPS/New Mexico 56%, with requested ROEs of 9.8-10.75% (Colorado gas 10.75%, Colorado electric 9.8%, New Mexico 10.5%). Those equity ratios have no recent precedent in the company's decided cases. A counter-reading is defensible: Colorado and New Mexico carry higher wildfire hazard, which arguably supports thicker equity layers, and each jurisdiction is decided on its own record.

Regulatory lag is currently binding. PSCo absorbed $5,440M of 2025 capex and earned 7.55% ongoing ROE; consolidated CWIP rose from $4,720M to $8,085M. Mitigants are substantial and quantified — $535-545M of guided 2026 rider revenue, forward or future test years in Minnesota gas, North Dakota, Wisconsin and New Mexico, growing AFUDC, and Colorado's new Grid Modernization Adjustment Clause. But the two largest pending cases use a historic 2025 test year, the least favorable convention in a high-spend period.

One structural note: PSCo is not an RTO member and must self-provide resource adequacy, which is part of why the Colorado capacity need is large — and also means PSCo captures none of the MISO/SPP transmission upside the plan excludes.

**Competitive position: 6.2.** Franchise protection and rider depth remain intact; earned-ROE deterioration and the Colorado self-build outcome move the trajectory down from 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Wildfire (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Marshall Fire (PSCo, 2021) settled in September 2025 for $640M, with $299M of pre-tax charges after expected insurance, $5M of remaining liability and $353M of insurance receivables at year-end; nearly all plaintiffs were documented and paid by February 2026. Smokehouse Creek (SPS, 2024) is the open item: the Texas A&M Forest Service determined both the Smokehouse Creek and 687 Reamer fires were caused by SPS-owned power lines after wooden pole failures across roughly 1,055,000 acres. Recorded estimated losses total $430M before insurance, of which $382M has been settled and $374M paid, leaving $56M accrued and ~$195M of insurance receivables. The 10-K states the $430M represents settlements to date plus the *low end* of the range for remaining estimable losses and excludes governmental penalties or fines, exemplary or punitive damages, government-entity compensation claims, unsettled tree and oil-and-gas equipment damage, and other non-estimable amounts. The company says it cannot estimate the upper end. In December 2025 the Texas Attorney General sued SPS seeking damages and civil penalties — squarely within the excluded categories.

Insurance economics have deteriorated sharply: coverage fell from ~$600M (including $520M wildfire) at a ~$40M premium through Q3 2024 to $450M total ($450M wildfire for the NSP System but only $300M for PSCo and SPS) at ~$130M in October 2024 and ~$135M in October 2025. Cost per dollar of coverage rose roughly 4.5x while retained per-event exposure at the two highest-hazard subsidiaries rose materially. Deferral approvals in Colorado, Wisconsin and New Mexico plus $65M embedded in the pending Colorado electric case mitigate the *premium* earnings drag; nothing in the filing indicates a path to recovering *settlement losses* from customers. Mitigating factors: Texas does not apply strict liability to utilities, and the Smokehouse settlement profile is advanced (both fatalities, subrogated insurers and the three largest claims by acreage resolved).

**Regulatory concentration in Q3 2026.** Colorado electric ($356M request, $13B rate base) and Colorado gas ($190M, $4.7B) both decide in Q3 2026 on a historic 2025 test year; Minnesota electric ($365M, ALJ report April 2026) decides in Q3 2026; New Mexico ($175M) in 2H 2026. Separately, the MPUC found NSP-Minnesota imprudent on the Prairie Island outage — the company argues ≤$6M of refunds, intervenors ~$40M, with an ALJ report in March 2026 and a decision in Q2 2026 — and the Minnesota Court of Appeals remanded executive compensation and prepaid pension treatment for MPUC reconsideration in 2026. PSCo lost its Denver District Court appeal of the 2024 Colorado gas order on all counts in Q1 2026. Individually these are $6-46M items against $2.0B of net income; the cluster in a single year is what matters.

**Financing and rate sensitivity.** A downgrade would raise borrowing costs and, per the 10-K, lower proceeds from equity issuances. A 100bp move in benchmark rates shifts pre-tax interest ~$17M annually on variable-rate debt, up from $7M in 2024. Coal "plant to be retired" stands at $1,595M net, with a risk factor naming possible forced life extensions requiring unplanned capital.

**Accounting item to carry forward.** A Q3 2025 amendment extending certain NSP-Minnesota gas PPAs to 2039 and 2048 reclassified them from operating to finance leases: gross finance-lease ROU assets went from $181M to $1,435M and noncurrent finance-lease liabilities from $60M to $1,262M, with ~$42M of what was previously fuel and purchased-power expense now sitting in interest. The classification follows mechanically from ASC 842 once terms cover substantially all remaining economic life; we draw no inference about motive. Analytically it means year-over-year leverage and interest-coverage comparisons include ~$1.3B of obligation that is not new borrowing, and operating income is flattered while coverage is worsened.

**Governance.** Combined Chairman/CEO. A new severance plan effective March 2026 grants sitting executive vice presidents a 3x change-in-control multiple equal to the CEO's, above the stated framework for their level — plausibly grandfathering legacy agreements, and immaterial in dollar terms. Controls were effective with an unqualified Deloitte opinion and no restatements. Share-based grant dilution is ~0.11% annually; dilution here is a financing story. No Form 4 or proxy data was available, so we make no claim about insider buying or selling.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility of 24.7% sits 5.5 points above 19.2% one-year realized, with an implied move of ±4.4% on the 12-day tenor — a wide distribution for a large-cap utility whose last earnings print was July 30 and whose next falls in late October. The term structure is flat (24.7% near versus 25.3% far), so the uncertainty is distributed rather than event-concentrated at the front. The 6-month tenor (November 20) carries the highest IV of the chain at 25.3% with a put/call volume ratio of 2.29; that window brackets the Q3 2026 Colorado and Minnesota rate decisions and the Q3 print, which is consistent with hedging around the regulatory calendar. Near-term skew is described as balanced (-0.9%); we do not infer direction from the sign. Put/call open interest fell from 1.63 to 0.61 since the June snapshot, indicating protection was reduced. The prior snapshot's 446% ATM IV reading is a data artifact and carries no signal. Short interest at 7.8% of float with 7.4 days to cover is high for a mega-cap regulated utility (typical 1-3%) and is consistent with positioning against the dilution and wildfire-tail arguments, though the underlying motivation is not observable.

**Risk factors: 4.3.** An explicitly open-ended wildfire tail with a state Attorney General as adversary, materially reduced insurance protection, and four consequential regulatory decisions inside twelve months constitute a dense set of specific, dated, asymmetric events that a peer-median multiple does not compensate.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $95 (+21.7%)

Colorado grants a constructive electric and gas outcome in Q3 2026 (ROE at or above 9.8% and an equity ratio near 53%), lifting PSCo's earned ROE from 7.55% toward the mid-9s and closing the largest source of underearning. Minnesota electric settles near the reduced $365M request. The MPUC approves the Google service agreement and the 1,900 MW of supporting renewables, adding rate base excluded from the $60B plan; Colorado's excluded procurement and MISO/SPP transmission add more. Rider revenue of $535-545M plus forward test years in four jurisdictions keep lag contained, and continued debt repurchases at a discount to the remaining $2.4B fair-value gap supplement holdco results. Consensus 2027 EPS of $4.54 proves conservative and the stock re-rates to roughly 21x — near the analyst median of $92.50 and inside the $102 high.

### Bear Case — $69 (-11.6%)

Colorado applies a North Dakota-style haircut (that case granted $24M of $45M) on a historic test year and rejects the 55% equity ratio, leaving PSCo underearning into 2027 while capex peaks at $15.13B. The Texas Attorney General action produces penalties outside the $430M Smokehouse accrual, and insurance receivables of $548M collect slowly or incompletely against only $300M of PSCo/SPS wildfire coverage per policy period. Interest expense rises $300-310M as guided while OCF stays near $4.1B, forcing more equity than $7.0B — or forcing it at a lower price, where the same dollars buy more shares. Prairie Island refunds land nearer the intervenors' ~$40M than the company's $6M. The stock reverts to 16x a $4.30 2027 number, roughly the 52-week low of $69.16.

### What would change our mind

The single most decisive evidence is the Q3 2026 Colorado electric order. A granted ROE at or above 9.8% on an equity ratio at or above 53%, combined with MPUC approval of the Google ESA and the 1,900 MW, would convert the rate-base story into per-share earnings and justify an upgrade despite the dilution. Conversely, an award below 9.5% on a sub-52% equity ratio, or a quantified Texas AG exposure beyond the accrual, would push this to REDUCE.

## Conclusion

Xcel is doing what regulated utilities are supposed to do in a load-growth cycle: spending aggressively into rate base with regulator-endorsed demand forecasts behind it. The disclosed $60B is a floor, the Google agreement carries a favorable cost-allocation structure, and roughly $8B of annual external funding has so far been raised without incident at A-range rating objectives.

What the market is pricing is the conversion problem. In 2025, net income rose 4.2% and GAAP EPS declined, because 49.2M new shares arrived faster than the earnings they funded. Utility-level earned ROE fell to 8.40% ongoing while capex nearly doubled, concentrated at the subsidiary earning 7.55%.
