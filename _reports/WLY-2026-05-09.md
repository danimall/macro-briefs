## Executive Summary

John Wiley & Sons trades at $41.39 (as of report date), representing approximately 9.5-10.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS ($3.90-$4.35) and an estimated 9-9.5% FCF yield on targeted $200M free cash flow (based on approximately 51-53M shares outstanding, implying a market cap of ~$2.1-2.2B). The company sits within a five-firm oligopoly controlling roughly half of the ~$20B academic publishing market, generates 48% recurring revenue and 83% digital revenue, and has undergone a successful portfolio transformation that drove adjusted EBITDA margins from ~24% to a guided 25.5-26.5% in FY2026.

The stock is priced at a significant discount to estimated peer median P/E, which partly reflects justified concerns about NIH funding risk, China concentration (approximately 30% of published articles include China-based authors), and a declining Learning segment. However, the magnitude of the discount appears excessive given: (1) Research Publishing grew 4% constant currency excluding AI comparables, (2) FCF is tracking toward $200M (up 59% YoY), (3) net debt/EBITDA improved from 2.0x to 1.7x, and (4) AI content licensing has reached $42M YTD with a trajectory toward $45-50M annually. The market may be pricing in persistent topline challenges that are contradicted by underlying article submission growth (+26%), open access expansion (+24% YTD FY2026), and a recurring agreement pipeline — though consolidated adjusted revenue remained flat on a constant-currency YTD basis and Learning revenue declined 7% YTD.

The primary analytical concern preventing higher conviction is execution risk on the transformation (significant management turnover, ~$800M debt maturity in November 2027, ERP implementation), combined with genuine binary risk from US federal research funding cuts. At an estimated 9-9.5% FCF yield with improving fundamentals, the risk/reward skews positively.

## Company Overview

John Wiley & Sons, founded in 1807, is one of the world's largest academic and professional publishers. After a major portfolio transformation in FY2024-2025 (divesting University Services, Wiley Edge, and CrossKnowledge), the company now operates two focused segments:

**Research (64% of revenue, 32.1% EBITDA margin):** Publishes 1,800+ peer-reviewed STM journals through subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access article processing charges, AI content licensing, and research solutions via the Atypon platform.

**Learning (35% of revenue, 37.4% FY2025 EBITDA margin):** Academic and professional publishing, digital courseware (WileyPLUS, zyBooks), and workplace assessments (Everything DiSC®, The Five Behaviors®).

The company generates 49% of revenue internationally and serves as a platform for prestigious society publications (American Cancer Society, American Heart Association, American Geophysical Union), creating deep institutional switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

Headline GAAP revenue declined from $2.08B (FY2022) to $1.68B (FY2025), but this was overwhelmingly driven by $238M in divested revenue. Adjusted for divestitures, underlying revenue grew approximately 3% in FY2025 at constant currency. Profitability inflected sharply: adjusted operating income grew 29% and adjusted EPS grew 31% to $3.64 in FY2025.

Q3 FY2026 (ended January 2026) showed: revenue $410M (+1% reported, flat constant currency), adjusted operating income $70M (+22% CC), and adjusted EPS $0.97 (+19% CC). For Q3, total Research revenue grew +1% constant currency, while Research Publishing grew +4% constant currency excluding AI revenue. Management guided to the high end of FY2026 ranges.

**Cash Flow:**

Free cash flow is the strongest fundamental pillar. FY2025 FCF was $126M; FY2026 target is ~$200M, representing 59% growth. YTD operating cash flow of $103M (up $51M vs. prior year) indicates the target is achievable. Based on approximately 51-53M shares outstanding at $41.39, the implied market cap is ~$2.1-2.2B, producing an estimated FCF yield of approximately 9-9.5%.

**Balance Sheet:**

Net debt/EBITDA improved from 2.0x to 1.7x. Total debt stands at approximately $807.5M (as of January 31, 2026) with $501M in unused credit capacity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.35 technically flags distress, but this is structurally misleading: contract liabilities (advance subscription payments) drive negative working capital, which is characteristic of a healthy subscription business, not financial distress. At April 30, 2025, current contract liabilities were $462.7M; at January 31, 2026, they were seasonally lower at $292.8M. The current ratio was 0.59 as of January 31, 2026 (current assets of $411.3M vs. current liabilities of $692.4M). The real concern is the credit facility maturity in November 2027 (with $777.4M in principal maturing in FY2028), which will require refinancing in a potentially elevated rate environment.

**Capital Returns:**

The company pays ~3.5% dividend yield ($1.41/share annualized) and is executing $100M in buybacks for FY2026. Share count is declining meaningfully (52.7M diluted vs. 54.8M year-ago). Total shareholder returns of $126M YTD FY2026 represent 37% growth.

**Earnings Consistency:**

Based on available consensus data, Wiley has demonstrated a pattern of meeting or exceeding guidance, consistent with conservative guidance practices and reliable execution.

## Growth Analysis

**Research Segment (64% of revenue):**
- Article submissions grew 26% YoY
- Article output grew 11% YoY
- Open access article output grew approximately 28% in Q3 FY2026 and 24% YTD FY2026 (following 16% growth in FY2025)
- Research Publishing grew 4% CC excluding AI comparables in Q3
- Total Research revenue grew +4% CC for the nine months YTD
- AI content licensing: $42M YTD, targeting $45-50M for FY2026

The Research segment exhibits genuine organic growth driven by global R&D expansion and the open access transition (which shifts revenue from institution subscriptions to author-funded APCs without reducing total addressable market).

**AI Licensing — Real Revenue on Existing Assets:**

AI content licensing represents monetization of Wiley's proprietary content library at what management suggests carries attractive incremental margins (though specific margin disclosures have not been provided in filings). The trajectory ($23M → $40M → ~$45-50M) is meaningful, and lifetime revenue has crossed $100M. However, AI licensing revenue can be lumpy — Q3 FY2026 AI revenue was $7.3M versus $9M in the prior-year quarter, and Wiley explicitly notes that revenue can fluctuate due to timing and deal nature. The company's first LLM customer outside the U.S. and a recurring revenue agreement with OpenEvidence suggest management is working to build recurring streams, though the extent to which AI licensing will prove structurally recurring versus partly one-time/licensing-timing driven remains to be demonstrated.

**Learning Segment (35% of revenue):**

This is the weak link. YTD FY2026 revenue declined 7%, with both Academic and Professional shrinking. The retail print market is in structural decline. Assessment products (DiSC, Five Behaviors) show resilience but cannot offset book volume declines. Learning's FY2025 EBITDA margin of 37.4% (declining to 34.8% YTD FY2026) makes it a cash contributor, but it is not a growth engine.

**Overall Growth Assessment:**

Low-single-digit topline growth with margin expansion driving mid-teens earnings growth represents the realistic central case. Consolidated adjusted revenue was flat on a constant-currency YTD basis through Q3 FY2026, with Research growth largely offset by Learning declines. At 10x earnings, the stock does not require high growth to deliver adequate returns.

## Valuation Assessment

**Absolute Metrics:**
- Forward P/E: ~9.5-10.6x (on $3.90-$4.35 guided Adj. EPS)
- FCF Yield: ~9-9.5% (on $200M FCF target, ~$2.1-2.2B market cap)
- Dividend Yield: 3.5%
- EV/EBITDA (implied): ~7-8x

**Relative to Peers:**

Based on available data for comparable information-services and publishing companies, WLY at approximately 14.2x trailing adjusted earnings appears to trade at a substantial discount to peer multiples. The precise magnitude of this discount depends on the peer set and metric definition, but directionally, WLY is valued well below specialty publishing and information-services comparables.

**Why the market may be wrong:**

The significant discount reflects the market pricing WLY as a declining legacy publisher. However, underlying organic growth is positive in Research, FCF is growing strongly, margins are expanding, and AI licensing provides optionality. The market may not have fully updated from the FY2023-2024 writedown/divestiture period, during which WLY recorded $200M+ in losses on sold businesses. That period is over. The discount also reflects legitimate concerns: consolidated revenue is flat YTD, Learning is declining, AI revenue is lumpy, and management turnover is ongoing.

**Valuation math for base case:**

At 12x adjusted EPS of $4.13 (midpoint of guided range) = $49.50. At 12x FY2027E EPS of $4.50 (assuming continued margin expansion and buyback accretion) = $54.00. These multiples remain conservative relative to comparable companies and Wiley's FCF characteristics.

## Competitive Landscape

Wiley's position within the academic publishing oligopoly provides structural protection:

1. **Switching costs are extreme.** Libraries cannot cancel Wiley journal subscriptions without losing researcher access to specific, irreplaceable content. Multi-year transformational agreements lock in revenue.

2. **Barriers to entry are high** due to journal prestige, indexing, society relationships, editorial infrastructure, and institutional purchasing behavior. Building a catalog of 1,800+ peer-reviewed journals with decades of prestige and society partnerships is extraordinarily difficult to replicate.

3. **Network effects in societies.** Wiley manages publications for major scientific societies, creating deep institutional relationships that are rarely disrupted.

4. **Scale advantage in AI licensing.** The breadth of Wiley's STM content library (spanning every scientific discipline) makes it highly valuable for LLM training — though other large STM publishers also possess extensive content libraries that could compete for licensing deals.

5. **Platform economics via Atypon.** Serving 2,000+ publishers with technology infrastructure creates data advantages and lock-in.

The competitive position is stable to improving within Research. In Learning, the position is eroding as print declines, though digital courseware (WileyPLUS, zyBooks) and proprietary assessment tools provide defensibility.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NIH/Federal Funding Cuts (Probability: 40-50%, Impact: Moderate)**

The 10-K newly flagged this risk, specifically mentioning potential reductions in National Institute of Health funding and U.S. Department of Education and other university program funding. If enacted, academic library budgets face pressure, which flows directly to subscription pricing power. However, 49% international revenue provides diversification, and transformational agreements are multi-year contracts that buffer near-term impact.

**2. China Concentration (Probability: 25-35% for material disruption, Impact: High if triggered)**

Approximately 30% of articles Wiley published in calendar 2024 included China-based authors. Chinese "early warning lists" for journals with high foreign content, combined with US-China decoupling risk, could divert submission and publication volumes to domestic Chinese publishers. This is a genuine binary risk that is difficult to hedge.

**3. Debt Refinancing (November 2027, ~$777M in FY2028 maturities)**

In a sustained high-rate environment, refinancing costs increase meaningfully. At 1.7x net leverage with $200M+ annual FCF generation, refinancing is achievable — the question is at what rate. Each 100bps increase adds ~$8M in annual interest expense.

**4. AI Disruption (Dual-edged)**

AI simultaneously creates new revenue (content licensing) and threatens existing products (AI-generated research summaries could reduce journal access demand). The 10-K explicitly acknowledges unauthorized content scraping as an enforcement challenge. Near-term, the revenue opportunity dominates; medium-term, the threat could grow.

**5. Learning Segment Decline**

At -7% YTD with margins compressing to 34.8% (from 37.4% in FY2025), Learning's topline erosion is structural. While profitable, continued decline eventually becomes a drag that Research growth cannot offset.

**6. Management Turnover**

Significant executive changes have occurred during the transformation period (multiple Item 5.02 8-K filings over 18 months). Most recently, in May 2026, Jay Flynn (EVP and GM, Research and Learning) departed the company and was replaced by Jessica Kowalski as EVP and GM, Research. Ongoing leadership turnover during a critical execution period represents a meaningful risk to strategic continuity.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($54-66, +30-60%)

- FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $4.50-5.00 on continued margin expansion and buyback accretion
- Market re-rates from 10x to 13-15x as transformation thesis gains credibility
- AI content licensing scales beyond $75M annually as recurring agreements multiply
- Research organic growth accelerates to 4-5% sustained on article submission growth
- FCF generation enables accelerated deleveraging ahead of 2027 maturity

### Bear Case ($28-32, -23-32%)

- NIH funding cuts materialize, pressuring US library budgets and forcing subscription cancellations
- China-based author publications divert to domestic publishers, reducing Research article volumes by 10-15%
- Debt refinancing in 2027 occurs at elevated rates (+200-300bps), compressing FCF
- Learning segment decline accelerates to -10%+ annually, overwhelming Research growth
- AI licensing proves one-time rather than recurring (customers don't renew after initial training)
- Stock reverts to 52-week low (~$28.38)

**Bull case target:** $54-66, based on 13-15x FY2027E EPS of ~$4.40 on multiple re-rating as peer discount narrows. Upside: +30-60%.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**

## Conclusion

Wiley at $41.39 offers a compelling value opportunity in an oligopolistic market undergoing a successful transformation. The estimated 9-9.5% FCF yield, 3.5% dividend, expanding margins, and AI licensing optionality provide multiple paths to value creation from a compressed multiple. The risks (NIH funding, China concentration, debt maturity, management turnover) are real and partially justify the discount — but the discount appears excessive given the fundamental improvement trajectory.

The stock is appropriate for investors with a 12-18 month horizon who can tolerate the binary risks around federal funding policy and accept that Learning segment headwinds will persist. The asymmetry favors buyers at current levels, with meaningful upside if the market begins re-rating the transformation.

**BUY** — with position sizing appropriate for a value/turnaround thesis carrying execution and policy risk.
