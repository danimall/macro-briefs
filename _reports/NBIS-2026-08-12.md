## Executive Summary

Nebius reported Q2 2026 on August 12: revenue of $582.3M, up 454% year over year and 46% sequentially, with adjusted EBITDA of $236.2M at a 40.6% margin — the full-year margin target reached two quarters early, against management's prior indication that Q2 margin would dip on investment timing. FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed across all four axes: revenue $3.0-3.4B, ~40% adjusted EBITDA margin, capex $20-25B, and exit ARR of $7-9B. The operating result is real and the balance sheet is materially better than the neocloud cohort norm: $8.04B of unrestricted cash plus $1.06B restricted against $8.55B of debt, leaving the group roughly net-cash.

The stock rose approximately 16.6% from the prior close on the print. That single session is the reason this report moves from HOLD, held three times, to REDUCE. Fundamentals did not deteriorate — guidance was reaffirmed, not cut, and the margin beat was the genuine surprise. What changed is price against a base case that did not move as much. Working from the filed 271,855,218 share count, enterprise value is approximately $61.7B, or 19.3x the guided FY2026 revenue midpoint, 48x implied FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, and 7.7x the midpoint of a target exit ARR that has not yet been earned. At $193.23 the skew was attractive; at $225.26 it is not.

Three unresolved items carry the downside. First, H1 capex was $8,130.3M against FY guidance of $20-25B, implying roughly $12-17B in H2 against identifiable sources of about $13.8-14.0B that would draw cash to near zero — a plausible $3-5B incremental financing requirement inside two quarters. Second, exit-2026 ARR of $7-9B requires 2.3-3.0x growth from roughly $3B cited on the Q2 call, while the largest reported contract (Meta, reported at ~$12B dedicated plus up to ~$15B optional) is reported to begin in early 2027 and therefore contributes little to the target. Third, depreciation is presented below cost of revenues and equals 44.6% of Q2 revenue — larger than cost of revenues itself — and the useful-life table that would settle the economic-depreciation question was not obtainable. Adjusted EBITDA is computed above that line and does not address it.

## Company Overview

Nebius Group N.V. is a Dutch-domiciled operator of NVIDIA-based GPU compute capacity — a "neocloud" — selling to AI labs, startups and enterprises in competition with hyperscalers and with dedicated GPU clouds including CoreWeave, IREN, Crusoe and Lambda. AI Cloud generated approximately $575M of the $582.3M Q2 revenue. Smaller consolidated pieces are Avride (autonomous vehicles and delivery robots) and TripleTen (tech reskilling). Minority stakes in ClickHouse and Toloka carry $1,606.7M of book value and generate no revenue; Toloka was deconsolidated in Q2 2025.

Two data-integrity points must be stated before any comparison. Nebius files Form 20-F and 6-K as a foreign private issuer — there is no 10-K, no MD&A Item 7, no proxy statement and no 8-K stream, which removes standard channels for compensation, board and control-effectiveness disclosure. And the registrant was Yandex N.V. until August 2024; revenue of $4.79B (2021) through $8.92B (2023) belongs to the divested Russian business. The continuing-operations series begins at FY2024 revenue of $117.5M. The supplied sector tags (Communication Services / Internet Content & Information) and the SIC code are legacy artifacts; the supplied peer set of BIDU, BZ and Z has no analytical bearing on this business, and the supplied 43.05x forward P/E is unusable — it rests on a stale share count and a legacy $0.04 EPS figure.

The stack is moving up from raw GPU rental: AI Cloud 3.5 introduced serverless AI (March 2026), 3.6 added developer and governance tooling (June 2026), and Nebius Token Factory was reinforced by the Eigen AI acquisition (agreed May 1, completed June 16) and a Clarifai transaction that the issuer describes as hiring the core team and licensing inference IP rather than acquiring the company. Physical footprint disclosed by the issuer: 310 MW in Finland, gigawatt-scale groundbreaking in Independence, Missouri, UK expansion, and a Bloom Energy power partnership.

NVIDIA filed a Schedule 13G on Nebius on July 20, 2026, establishing it as a greater-than-5% beneficial owner. The specific 9.3% figure and a reported September 11, 2026 lock-up expiry are press-sourced and unconfirmed. The $2,000.0M of pre-funded warrants issued in Q1 2026 is plausibly the vehicle for that investment; the linkage is inference, not a filing fact.

### Revenue and margin structure

| Net income (cont. ops) | −$641.4M | +$82.5M | +$621.2M | −$190.4M | +$430.8M |

Q1 2026 is derived by subtracting Q2 from H1; the $399.0M revenue derivation matches the independently reported Q1 figure, which validates the dataset.

The margin architecture matters more than the headline. Depreciation and amortization is presented as a separate line, excluded from cost of revenues:

- Cost of revenues Q2: $133.6M, or 22.9% of revenue → 77.1% "gross margin" excluding D&A
- D&A Q2: $259.7M, or 44.6% of revenue — larger than cost of revenues
- Revenue less cost of revenues less D&A: $582.3 − $133.6 − $259.7 = **$189.0M, 32.5% of revenue**

Product development of $191.0M and SG&A of $173.9M take the GAAP operating result to −$175.9M, a −30.2% margin. Adjusted EBITDA of $236.2M is built by adding back $259.7M of D&A, $102.5M of share-based compensation (17.6% of revenue) and $49.9M of transaction costs. For a business whose principal productive asset is depreciating semiconductors, adjusted EBITDA measures pre-asset-cost cash contribution and is not a proxy for earnings power. Management's own reconciliation notes SBC is a significant expense.

### GAAP profitability is a mark-to-model artifact

FY2025 net income of +$82.5M and H1 2026 net income of +$430.8M both sit on non-operating revaluation gains on private stakes: $597.4M in Q2 2025 and $780.6M in H1 2026 (all in Q1; Q2 2026 was zero). Stripping the $780.6M from H1 pre-tax income of $438.7M leaves a pre-tax loss of approximately $341.9M and, after $7.9M of tax, a net loss near $349.8M. Q2, with no such gain, was a −$190.4M GAAP loss and −$0.68 diluted LPS. The adjusted net loss was $33.2M, or −$0.12 per share on 280,381,615 weighted shares. Note that some coverage compared the −$0.68 GAAP figure against a Street adjusted estimate; that is a basis mismatch. The clean beats are revenue (approximately $582.3M against ~$569.9M consensus) and adjusted EBITDA.

Carrying value of non-marketable equity securities rose from $836.6M to $1,606.7M, roughly 15.5% of shareholders' equity. These are real assets that most screens ignore. They are also non-cash, reversible on a down-round, and their timing is not management-controlled. GAAP EPS, P/E, ROE and ROA on this name are uninformative; revenue, adjusted EBITDA, cash-flow and leverage analysis are not affected.

### Cash flow — the structural feature to understand

H1 2026 operating cash flow was +$4,504.1M. Working capital contributed $4,152.6M of that, of which the deferred-revenue increase alone was $4,395.0M and receivable collections $427.9M. Operating cash generation before working capital was therefore approximately $351.5M — roughly 8% of reported OCF. H1 capex was $8,130.3M (Q2 alone $5,657.4M), so OCF less capex was **−$3,626.2M**. Excluding the deferred-revenue inflow, consumption would have been on the order of −$8.0B.

Nebius funded approximately 54% of its H1 capital program with customer prepayments ($4,395.0M ÷ $8,130.3M). Management indicated on the call it expects over $9B of prepayments for 2026. This is genuinely cheaper and less dilutive than secured GPU debt and is evidence that large counterparties will commit cash years ahead of delivery. The offsetting fact: deferred revenue of $5,975.2M ($979.4M current, $4,995.8M non-current) is more than four times trailing revenue and is a delivery obligation, not revenue. Contractual refund and credit terms were not obtainable. A reader who annualizes Q2's $2,246.1M of OCF is extrapolating financing, not earnings.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue trajectory is among the steepest in public markets: $117.5M (FY2024) → $529.8M (FY2025) → $981.3M (H1 2026). ARR moved from approximately $1.25B at year-end 2025 to ~$1.9B at March 31 to ~$3B cited on the Q2 call. Four consecutive quarterly beats, each by 26-83% on the loss line, argue that management is guiding conservatively on the P&L.

The arithmetic of the FY2026 guide is worth spelling out. H1 revenue was $981.3M; the $3.0-3.4B range requires H2 of $2.02-2.42B. Holding 40% sequential growth gives Q3 ≈ $815M and Q4 ≈ $1,141M, or H2 of roughly $1.96B — just short of the low end. The guide therefore embeds sustained sequential growth in the 45-55% range, which is achievable only if capacity energizes on schedule.

Exit-2026 ARR of $7-9B against ~$3B today requires 2.3-3.0x in two quarters. Because ARR is an exit run-rate, contracts energizing in November or December count fully, so the target is primarily a data-center-delivery and power-interconnect question rather than a demand question. The complication is that the reported Meta arrangement (~$12B of dedicated capacity plus an option for up to $15B more) is reported to begin in early 2027 and therefore contributes little to exit-2026 ARR, which must be delivered by the reported Microsoft agreement (~$17B) and other contracts. Contract values, durations and start dates are single-source press reporting and unverified.

Reported backlog figures of $34B and $40B do not reconcile with reported contract values of ~$27B (Meta) plus ~$17B (Microsoft), which suggests the backlog measure excludes optional tranches. Backlog, contracted revenue, ASC 606 remaining performance obligations and deferred revenue are four different measures here, and only deferred revenue ($5,975.2M of cash actually received) has an issuer definition.

Consensus loss estimates are widening: current-year EPS moved to −$2.80 from −$2.14 thirty days ago, and next year to −$5.83 from −$4.87 (thirty days) and −$4.13 (ninety days). For a company scaling capex fivefold, deeper losses are the mechanical consequence of D&A and SBC on a larger asset base rather than a demand signal. But the direction matters for the terminal question: on next-year consensus, GAAP losses widen as revenue triples, which means the equity value rests entirely on the assumption that GPU assets earn returns above their true economic depreciation. That assumption is not testable from current disclosure.

Growth prospects score 8.0, unchanged from the prior report. The Q2 print confirms the trajectory; the widening loss profile and the unverified contract timing keep it below 9.

## Competitive Landscape

Two competitive assets are well-evidenced. NVIDIA's Schedule 13G establishes it as a greater-than-5% holder, which — alongside repeated joint product announcements and the reported first Vera Rubin NVL72 rack validation in Finland — supports privileged allocation in a supply-constrained market. And the prepayment funding structure gives Nebius a cost-of-capital advantage over peers financing with secured GPU debt.

What is not evidenced is a software or switching-cost moat. Token Factory, Eigen AI and the Clarifai team/IP license are recent and unquantified in disclosure. Nebius does not own a legacy generation base — it is buying and partnering for power (Bloom Energy, Missouri, Finland, UK) against IREN's reported hard-to-replicate 5 GW owned position. Scale is one-quarter of CoreWeave's revenue.

A cross-check on the announced 4 GW capacity ambition: at management's cited $40-50M of annual revenue per MW, exit-2026 ARR of $7-9B implies roughly 155-200 MW of revenue-generating capacity. The 4 GW figure is a multi-year secured or planned-power ambition and must not be multiplied by per-MW pricing to derive a forecast.

Capex-to-return math does not yet close. FY2026 capex of $22.5B at the midpoint supporting $8B of exit ARR is a 2.8x ratio; at a 40% adjusted EBITDA margin, $8B of ARR yields $3.2B, implying roughly seven years to recover the capex before interest and tax. Management's stated payback of one year ten months almost certainly counts only directly attributable GPU capex on specific deals, excluding land, shells, power infrastructure and assets not in service, and 2026 capex is building 2027-2028 capacity. Both figures can be true. But ROIC on this buildout is not computable from disclosure, and the payback figure should be treated as an unaudited management metric of undisclosed construction.

Competitive position scores 6.5 — trajectory improving from a small base, moat unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Financing gap, H2 2026 (dominant).** FY capex guidance $20-25B against H1 actual $8,130.3M implies roughly $12-17B in H2, $14.4B at midpoint. Identifiable sources: opening unrestricted cash $8.04B, remaining 2026 prepayments of roughly $4.6B (management's >$9B less the $4.40B H1 deferred-revenue increase), the $775M July facility, and perhaps $0.4-0.6B of pre-working-capital operating cash at higher scale — approximately $13.8-14.0B, which covers the program only by drawing cash to near zero. Holding a prudent buffer plausibly requires $3-5B of incremental financing within two quarters. Timing of capex payments and prepayments is partly management-controlled, so this is a sensitivity rather than a forecast. The debt line, not the revenue line, is the number to watch next quarter.

**Depreciation policy (the explicit short thesis).** Michael Burry disclosed a short on August 6 at approximately $211.77, arguing AI-infrastructure operators depreciate GPUs too slowly. H1 depreciation of PP&E of $462.6M against average net PP&E of $9,299M implies roughly a 10% annualized rate, a crude ~10-year implied life — but that calculation is biased upward by construction-in-progress and assets not in service, by a fleet that grew 135% in six months (overstating the average denominator), and by using net rather than gross book value. The useful-life table that would settle it was not obtainable. Note that a strong adjusted EBITDA print does not refute the argument, because adjusted EBITDA sits above the depreciation line. Unresolved in both directions.

**Exit-ARR delivery.** 2.3-3.0x in two quarters, with the near-term ramp resting on the reported Microsoft arrangement while the reported Meta contract starts in 2027. Falsifiable by the Q3 print in November and definitively by the Q4/FY report.

**Customer concentration — unmeasured.** No disclosed per-customer percentage was obtained. Deferred revenue of $5,975.2M concentrated in a handful of prepaying counterparties implies high concentration, but that is inference. A broadening revenue mix would not by itself establish diversification.

**Contingent dilution.** Convertible note terms for roughly $5.3B+ of issuance are unknown. Maximum dilution cannot be stated.

**Governance and disclosure opacity.** No proxy statement exists for a foreign private issuer; compensation, board composition and the current Class A/Class B voting ratio are unverified against a document. Management lists internal-control effectiveness among its own enumerated risks, and a 20-F/A was filed three weeks after the original FY2025 20-F — the reason is unknown and no conclusion about control effectiveness should be drawn. Fourteen Form 3 filings on a single day (March 18, 2026) and an apparent General Counsel transition are unexplained. "Other income, net" of $81.5M in Q2 ($101.4M H1) is material relative to the quarterly loss and unexplained.

**Vendor circularity.** Nebius pays NVIDIA for GPUs; NVIDIA holds Nebius equity and, by inference, funded $2.0B of warrants. NVIDIA does not guarantee Nebius obligations on any evidence reviewed. A reported September 11 lock-up expiry on NVIDIA's position is unconfirmed as to terms.

**Insider activity.** Across the 25 most recent Form 4 records through July 22, there are zero open-market purchases; every acquisition is a grant or option exercise. Aggregate identified sale proceeds exceed $140M, including a single $101.6M exercise-and-sell by the Chief Infrastructure Officer on May 13. Sellers span CEO, CTO, CFO, CRO, CIO, General Counsel and two directors, with clustering on quarter-start dates. Insider selling is usually low-signal, and there is substantial mitigating evidence here — round-number limit tranches, price-laddered fills, at least eight Form 144 filings, and legitimate diversification needs from concentrated legacy Yandex positions. But the exception that makes selling meaningful is met: clustered disposition by more than three C-suite insiders within short windows, occurring while consensus loss estimates widen rather than while estimates rise. The defensible statement is that insiders have been consistent net sellers and none has bought in the open market.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 137.6% against 108.7% one-year realized is rich by 28.9 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (137.6% at one week versus 98.0% at six months) — the market is pricing imminent event risk rather than a durable change in volatility regime. The nearest expiry is one day out, so its 8.9% implied move is largely same-day mechanics; the more informative reads are ±17.4% over one month and ±24.5% over three, which bracket the Q3 print.

Positioning turned bullish since the prior snapshot: put/call open interest fell from 1.06 to 0.66, put/call volume is 0.61, and skew is call-heavy at −9.0% per the data provider. ATM IV fell 33.6 points from July 21. Against 27.6% short interest and only 2.8 days to cover, that combination suggests a meaningful share of the 16.6% post-earnings move is mechanical short covering plus call-driven flow rather than fundamental re-rating. The options market is not corroborating a bearish fundamental thesis, which is the correct caution against any aggressive short expression — but rich near-term vol into a stretched price argues for reducing exposure rather than adding it.

Risk factors score 3.2 — a dense cluster of binary and unquantified risks, several of them resolvable within two quarters.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($330, +46%)

Capacity energizes on schedule through Q4; exit-2026 ARR lands at or near $9B; the reported Microsoft arrangement and others carry the target without Meta; H2 financing is completed at attractive terms with prepayments covering most of it, limiting equity issuance. The market applies roughly 10x EV to $9B of forward ARR at a 40% adjusted EBITDA margin — $90B EV, approximately $320-340 per share after modest dilution. In this outcome, the prepayment structure is validated as a durable funding advantage, NVIDIA's stake anchors supply allocation, and Nebius compounds into 2027 with the reported Meta contract (~$12B dedicated plus up to ~$15B optional, per press reporting) beginning to convert. The analyst high target of $410 sits above this.

### Bear Case ($120, −47%)

Power interconnect or site delivery slips; exit-2026 ARR lands near $5B; the H2 financing gap forces $4-5B of equity or equity-linked issuance into weakness, taking share count toward 295-300M; and the depreciation debate resolves against the reported margin, either through disclosure or through an accelerated write-down cycle as newer architectures compress the economic life of installed A/H-class fleets. Multiple compresses to roughly 6x forward ARR — 6 × $5B = $30B EV, equity near $34.5B on ~295M shares, approximately $117-120. The analyst low target is $120. This name traded at $148.22 intraday on July 29 and $73.87 in February; a 47% drawdown is well inside its realized range and roughly 0.6 standard deviations on 108% annualized volatility.

### Base Case ($230, +2%)

FY2026 revenue lands near $3.0-3.1B, at or just below the guided low end; exit-2026 ARR reaches roughly $7B rather than the $8B midpoint; approximately $4B of the H2 gap is funded with equity at prices near current levels (17.8M shares, taking the count to ~290M). At 9x EV on $7.0B of forward ARR, EV is $63.0B; adding back net cash and the raise proceeds gives equity of roughly $66.5B, or $230 per share.

Probability-weighting 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear gives $227, approximately 1% above the $225.26 basis. Even at a more favorable 35/40/25 split the expected value is roughly $238, or +5.4% — thin compensation for 108% realized volatility and a −47% tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $230 (+2%), bull $330 (+46%), bear $120 (−47%). Probability-weighted expected value approximately $227.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the post-print strength now, while near-term implied volatility is rich (137.6% versus 108.7% realized) and while short covering is still supporting the bid. For investors who want to retain thematic exposure to AI compute capacity, cut position size by roughly half to a residual thematic allocation and cap it at a size where a 47% drawdown is tolerable. Do not add on the current print. Existing holders who prefer to monetize elevated vol rather than sell outright can do so, but the fundamental instruction is exposure reduction, not repositioning.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Price in the $150-170 range on unchanged guidance — at $160, base-case upside is roughly 44% against a bear case near $120, restoring the asymmetry that existed at the prior close.
2. Q3 print (November) showing ARR at or above $5B, which would put the $7-9B exit target inside one quarter's reach and materially reduce the delivery risk.
3. Disclosure of the H2 financing plan in a form that closes the $3-5B gap without a large equity component — a completed multi-billion prepayment or a secured facility at investment-grade-adjacent terms with disclosed covenants.
4. Disclosure of GPU useful lives and the depreciation schedule in a form that survives scrutiny, retiring the short thesis.

**Avoid triggers.**
1. Exit-2026 ARR guidance withdrawn, or Q3 ARR below $4B.
2. An equity or equity-linked raise exceeding $5B, or one priced at a material discount to market.
3. Disclosure of a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting.
4. Deferred revenue declining sequentially, or evidence of prepayment renegotiation or refund exposure with an anchor counterparty.
5. Verified evidence that the Microsoft or Meta contract values or start dates differ materially from press reporting in an unfavorable direction.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report, expected November, is the decisive checkpoint: it tests the ARR trajectory against the exit target, discloses H2 capex pacing and the debt line, and will reveal whether the H2 financing gap was closed with prepayments or with securities. A secondary near-term marker is the reported September 11 NVIDIA lock-up expiry, though its terms are unconfirmed.

## Conclusion

Nebius has built something real and unusual. Revenue grew 454% year over year, adjusted EBITDA margin reached the full-year 40% target two quarters early, the balance sheet is roughly net-cash while peers carry heavy secured leverage, NVIDIA is a registered greater-than-5% holder, and customer prepayments funded 54% of the H1 capital program at no interest cost and no dilution. Four consecutive earnings beats indicate management guides conservatively. This is not a broken thesis and the company is not distressed.

The problem is price against what is knowable. At $225.26 the enterprise trades at 19.3x guided FY2026 revenue, 48x implied FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, and 7.7x an exit ARR that requires 2.3-3.0x growth in two quarters while the largest reported contract starts in 2027. Reported operating cash flow is 92% customer prepayments; GAAP profitability is a mark-up of private stakes; the depreciation rate on the asset that generates all revenue is undisclosed; a $3-5B financing requirement sits inside two quarters; and eight or more insiders have sold with none buying. Probability-weighted value is approximately flat to the current price against a −47% bear case that is well inside this stock's realized six-month range.

The prior three reports were HOLDs, and the fundamentals since have improved. The entry price improved considerably more against it: the same analysis at the August 11 close of $193.23 produced roughly 19% base-case upside; at $225.26 it produces 2%. Reduce exposure, keep the position small if you keep one at all, and let the November print resolve the ARR and financing questions before paying this multiple again.
