## Executive Summary

Comstock is a single-play, dry-gas producer in the Haynesville and Bossier shales with a proprietary deep extension — the Western Haynesville — that accounts for essentially all of its growth narrative and, by our arithmetic, roughly $2.5 billion of its enterprise value. The June-quarter filing resolved several questions in the wrong direction. Realized gas of $2.54/Mcf unhedged sat below a reproducible full-cost breakeven of $2.74/Mcfe (DD&A $1.48 + cash costs $0.77 + interest $0.49). GAAP operating income was $22.6M on $353.3M of revenue against $55.0M of interest expense, an EBIT/interest ratio of 0.41x. Net debt reached $3,088.9M, lifting net leverage from about 2.69x at year-end to roughly 3.11x on last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDAX of ~$993M, against a 3.5x revolver covenant and a revolver that matures 15 November 2027 with $545M drawn.

The equity has fallen 53% from its 52-week high of $28.10, and it is worth being explicit about what the sellers are responding to rather than asserting that they are wrong. Consensus is being cut hard and continuously: current-quarter EPS -59.1% over 90 days, current-year -48.7%, next-year -31.5% (to $0.96). H1-2026 free cash flow was negative $387.3M on a cash basis, and management guided a further $720–820M of second-half capital, implying FY2026 capex of roughly $1.58–1.68B against hedge-supported operating cash flow that plausibly leaves a $700–800M annual deficit. Production is roughly flat: 527.8 Bcfe (2024) to 450.4 Bcfe (2025) to an implied ~450–465 Bcfe for 2026, on approximately $3.0B of two-year capital. That is the market's case, and the filings support it.

Against that, the bull case is genuine but unquantifiable from disclosure. Proved reserves reached 7,005 Bcfe with 2,561 net undeveloped Western locations excluded from reserves; the five newest Western wells averaged 33 MMcf/d on 9,679-ft laterals, about 3.41 MMcf/d per 1,000 ft versus 2.62 for Legacy — a ~30% per-foot uplift, though per-well drilling costs by play are not disclosed, so the return comparison cannot be closed. Sixth Street paid $600M for 27% of the captive midstream at a ~$2.2B implied enterprise value, an arm's-length underwrite of the gathering and treating leg. And Comstock is the named gas supplier to a NextEra Energy Resources power project sized at 2 GW initially with 8 GW potential for data-center load. No volumes, term, or price appear in any filing. At $13.21 the equity carries ~$8.53/share above proved PV-10 on the SEC $3.07 deck, so the price already assumes the Western Haynesville works.

## Company Overview

Comstock operates 1,074 of its 1,724 gross producing wells and operates properties representing 99% of proved PV-10, holding 1,069,991 gross (802,769 net) prospective acres. It is a pure gas play: Q2-2026 oil sales were $476 thousand against $287.7M of gas sales. Reserve life is roughly 15.6 years (7,005 Bcfe ÷ 450.4 Bcfe of 2025 production), and only 41% of proved reserves are developed. Headcount is 252.

Two structural features matter. First, the company consolidates Pinnacle Gas Services (PGS), which owns the Bethel and Marquez treating plants and 246 miles of high-pressure gathering line. Western Haynesville gas carries CO₂ and H₂S and requires treating, so PGS is the enabling condition for the play rather than an ancillary business. Second, the reported revenue line contains a near-zero-margin third-party gas marketing operation: $500.2M of FY2025 revenue against $516.2M of cost (a negative $16.0M margin), improving to $230.0M/$225.9M in H1-2026. Any revenue-growth or EV/sales screen on Comstock is distorted by this line, which was ~24% of H1-2026 reported revenue and contributed essentially no profit.

Customer concentration is meaningful and LNG-linked: Enterprise Products 18%, Venture Global LNG 11%, Shell Energy North America 10% in FY2025 — 39% across three counterparties. Firm transportation of ~1.7 Bcf/d covers ~1.24 Bcf/d of production, with commitments of $85.4M in 2026 declining to $27.7M by 2030.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 headline overstates operating improvement.** Revenue rose 77% to $2,220.3M, but only $380.6M of the $965.8M increase (39%) came from selling the company's own hydrocarbons. Gas services marketing contributed $294.1M (30%) at negative gross margin, and gains on asset sales contributed $291.9M (30%). Production fell 15% over the same period, partly because of the Shelby Trough divestiture ($417.2M of net proceeds). A screen reading "+77% revenue, net income swinging from -$219M to +$420M" materially misstates what happened, which was a ~60% increase in realized price on 15% less volume plus one-time gains.

**Unit costs are fine; the capital structure is not.** Q2-2026 cash costs of $0.77/Mcfe (gathering and transportation $0.38, LOE $0.25, production tax $0.06, cash G&A $0.08) improved from $0.80 a year earlier. DD&A, however, rose from $1.41 to $1.48/Mcfe, which the 10-Q attributes to higher finding and development costs in 2026. Add $0.49/Mcfe of interest and the full-cycle pre-tax breakeven is $2.74/Mcfe, above the $2.54 unhedged realization and only marginally below the $2.93 hedged realization. Adjusted EBITDAX/interest of 4.45x shows cash interest is comfortably covered; EBIT/interest of 0.41x shows accounting profit is not. The gap is DD&A, which is a historical-cost allocation rather than a cash outflow — a company can under-earn its depletion charge for years without a liquidity event. It cannot do so indefinitely while also growing debt.

**Cash flow quality.** Reported H1-2026 operating cash flow of $442.2M (-15% YoY) includes roughly $62M of receivable release; on the company's own pre-working-capital basis it was $380.4M (-15%). Against $829.5M of cash capex (accrual $863.7M), free cash flow was -$387.3M cash-basis and -$483.3M on the pre-working-capital/accrual pairing. FY2025 was -$444.7M. With H2 operating cash flow plausibly $400–450M (supported by 143.5 Bcf of H2 hedges at a $3.51 swap and $3.50 floor, plus seasonally firmer winter pricing) against $720–820M of guided capex, FY2026 burn plausibly reaches $700–800M and cumulative 2025–2026 burn $1.15–1.25B. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast, and it is sensitive to winter Henry Hub, working-capital timing, and completion pacing.

**Debt and covenant math.** Principal at 30 June 2026: $1,623.9M of 6.75% 2029 notes, $965.0M of 5.875% 2030 notes, and $545.0M drawn on the revolver (from $260.0M at year-end), totaling $3,133.9M against $45.0M of cash. Total liabilities to attributable equity moved from 1.53x to 1.69x. Using company-reported Adjusted EBITDAX, LTM to 30 June is roughly $993M, putting net leverage near 3.11x versus a disclosed 3.5x maintenance covenant, up from ~2.69x six months earlier. Comstock reported compliance at both dates; the headroom estimate is ours, and a Fourth Amendment to the credit agreement dated 15 June 2026 (filed as Exhibit 10.1 to the Q2 10-Q) has not been read, so add-backs, PGS treatment, and averaging conventions could shift the calculation in either direction. Consolidated EBITDAX also includes 100% of PGS while 27% of PGS economics now belong to a third party, so Comstock-attributable leverage is somewhat higher than 3.11x.

Liquidity is $1.2B, of which $150M relates to PGS's own facility and is restricted, alongside $31.7M of PGS cash and $446.8M of PGS net PP&E that are ring-fenced. Comstock-level undrawn capacity of roughly $955M represents perhaps 12–15 months of runway at the estimated burn rate, but the leverage covenant likely binds before the capacity does. The 2029 notes were marked at 98 and the 2030s at 94 of par at quarter-end — not distressed, but the credit market wants six points more than the coupon for 2030 risk.

**The Sixth Street instrument sits outside reported debt.** PGS issued 600,000 Class A-2 units (27%) to a Sixth Street-managed fund for $600M in June, carrying a minimum annualized return and exit provisions, with ownership stepping down to 19.5% once hurdles are met. Proceeds redeemed the Quantum-affiliated preferred for $445M and retired PGS debt. Because control was retained, the $145M excess over carrying value was charged to additional paid-in capital (APIC fell $184.2M) with a $49.2M deferred-tax effect, never touching the income statement — this is the prescribed ASC 810 treatment, not a discretionary choice. The economically relevant point is that a return-guaranteed, structurally senior subsidiary claim now sits in the $584.4M noncontrolling interest line rather than in the $3,133.9M of debt, and the precedent instrument (Quantum's $300M invested, redeemed at $445M, 1.48x) suggests the eventual redemption could exceed $600M. The Class A-2 hurdle rate, put mechanics, and which entity bears the obligation are not disclosed; it is entirely possible the claim is satisfied from PGS's own fee-based cash flows or refinanced at the PGS level. Note also that several secondary write-ups characterized this transaction as debt reduction; consolidated carrying long-term debt rose from $2,809.1M to $3,098.8M over the same half.

**Reported financials versus provided data fields.** The supplied P/E of 7.65 is not reconcilable with the supplied trailing EPS of $1.33 at any recent price (13.21 ÷ 1.33 = 9.9x), and the trailing GAAP figure is in any case inflated by $83.8M of pre-tax unrealized hedge gains in H1 (H1 GAAP EPS $0.40 versus adjusted $0.16) plus prior-year asset-sale gains. We use consensus forward EPS as the valuation anchor.

## Growth Analysis

Production is the cleanest test, and it shows capital buying volume replacement rather than growth. Q2-2026 was 113.1 Bcfe, +16% sequentially off a weak Q1 (97.9 Bcfe) but only +1% year over year; H1 was 211.0 Bcfe, -7% YoY. With FY2026 capital of roughly $1.58–1.68B, a plausible full-year outcome is ~450–465 Bcfe, approximately flat against 2025's 450.4 Bcfe and below 2024's 527.8 Bcfe. Comstock does not publish an explicit annual production range in the materials reviewed, so that estimate is derived from H1 actuals and the Q2 sequential trend. The 2025 comparison is flattered against the company by the Shelby Trough sale, which removed producing volume.

The reserve story is more encouraging as a resource statement and more demanding as a capital claim. Proved reserves rose to 7,005 Bcfe, with PUDs going from 1,030 to 4,163 Bcf (59% of total) on +3,175 Bcf of extensions and discoveries, aided by the SEC 12-month price rising from $1.84 to $3.07/Mcf. Estimated future development cost for PUDs rose from $891.5M to $4,702.3M — about $1.13 per Mcf of development capital before ~$0.80/Mcfe of cash costs, which is why 7.0 Tcfe supports only $4.46B of PV-10. The scheduled conversion capital reaches $1,013.5M in 2028 and $1,458.9M in 2029, the latter approaching the entire current annual budget. Only 13 PUD locations converted to proved developed in 2025, down from 21 in 2024, against a PUD balance that quadrupled — real exposure to negative revisions under the SEC five-year rule. The offsetting explanation is credible: Bethel and Marquez treating capacity and the 246-mile gathering system were only recently completed, so conversions should mechanically accelerate, and the 2026 Western program was raised from 19 to 22 wells.

Estimate momentum is the strongest negative signal in the dataset. Current-quarter consensus fell 35.8% in 30 days and 59.1% in 90; next-year fell 7.5% and 31.5% to $0.96. Revisions of this magnitude in the wrong direction are the mirror image of the inflection pattern that supports buying into a rising multiple. The recent surprise record is also mixed after two beats: -42.0% in Q1 and -12.8% in Q2.

The two demand-side tailwinds are real but not yet contracted to Comstock. Gulf Coast LNG feedgas continues to ramp, which structurally favors the Haynesville as the closest major dry-gas basin. And the NextEra Energy Resources arrangement targets in-basin gas-fired generation for data-center load. Read literally, Comstock is the gas seller; NextEra Energy Resources develops and owns the generation; the hyperscaler offtaker is unnamed. At a ~7,000 Btu/kWh heat rate and 90% capacity factor, 2 GW implies roughly 110 Bcf/yr (~0.30 Bcf/d) and 8 GW roughly 440 Bcf/yr — 24% and 100% of current production respectively. Those are our conversions on stated assumptions; Comstock's contracted share is undisclosed. Notably, the FY2025 risk factors contain no dedicated disclosure for this arrangement and the Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors, which is itself informative about how binding it currently is.

A 1.56x EV/PV-10 multiple is not unusual for an E&P with deep inventory. But it means the equity is priced on the Western Haynesville working, not merely on gas prices recovering. Sell-side targets spanning $9 to $21 around a $13.21 price confirm that this is the disputed variable, and the recommendation distribution (1 buy, 10 holds, 2 sells) has been static for four months. Trailing multiples are unusable here: FY2025 GAAP earnings included $291.9M of asset-sale gains, and H1-2026 GAAP included $83.8M of unrealized hedge gains.

## Competitive Landscape

Comstock's advantages are specific and durable. It operates properties representing 99% of PV-10, giving genuine control over pace, cost, and completion design. It holds a large contiguous position in the basin physically closest to Sabine Pass, Calcasieu Pass, Plaquemines, and Golden Pass, and its disclosed customer list shows it is already plumbed into LNG and pipeline aggregation. Its gas is MiQ-certified for methane intensity, which matters to European and Asian buyers. In the Western Haynesville it captured acreage early and built the treating and gathering capacity that any entrant would have to replicate — Sixth Street's $600M for 27% of PGS at a ~$2.2B implied enterprise value is an independent party underwriting that midstream leg, though it validates a contracted, fee-like, ring-fenced asset rather than the upstream well economics.

The offsetting structural position is equally clear. The Haynesville is deeper, hotter, and costlier than Appalachia, making it the marginal-cost swing supply for US gas — first to curtail when Henry Hub falls, first to benefit when it rises. Comstock's 2024–2026 volume path traces exactly that behavior. There are no liquids to cushion low gas prices, absolute leverage is high, and Western Haynesville wells at 10,500–19,200 ft TVD under HPHT conditions cost materially more than Legacy wells. Rising DD&A per Mcfe ($1.41 to $1.48) is the visible symptom: unit finding and development costs are moving the wrong way. The trajectory on resource capture is improving; the trajectory on unit cost is not, which is why we score competitive position slightly above neutral rather than as a strength.

## Risk Assessment

**Gas price, only partly hedged.** Q2 realization of $2.54/Mcf sat below the $2.74/Mcfe full-cost breakeven. Hedges cover roughly 57–63% of expected H2-2026 volumes (58.9 Bcf of swaps at $3.51, 84.6 Bcf of collars at $3.50/$4.35), but only ~32% of 2027 (146 Bcf of collars at $3.50/$4.44), and the ceilings cap upside on the hedged portion. No hedge requires margin or collateral posting, so a price spike creates no liquidity drain. Two-thirds of 2027 is exposed.

**Leverage and the November 2027 revolver.** Roughly 3.11x against a 3.5x covenant, moving 42 basis points higher in six months while H1 EBITDAX fell 10% YoY, with a $700–800M estimated 2026 deficit and $545M drawn on a facility maturing in about 15 months. This is the mechanism that converts a cyclical downturn into a capital-structure event — a covenant breach or a refinancing at 2026 spreads would likely force capex reduction, asset sales, or equity issuance. Contingent dilution today is immaterial (1.83M PSUs settling into at most 3.66M shares, 1.25% of outstanding), so the equity-issuance risk is discretionary and unused, not embedded.

**Lease clocks forcing spend into a weak deck.** 11% of gross undeveloped acreage expires in 2026, 8% in 2027, 6% in 2028 — roughly 200,000 net acres within three years. That creates pressure to spend regardless of price signals, a partial explanation for a $1.6B program funded through a $2.54/Mcf quarter.

**PUD conversion and reserve revisions.** 59% of proved reserves are undeveloped with $4.7B of required development capital and only 13 conversions in 2025.

**Governance and disclosure.** A five-member board with two insider seats and outside directors aged 80, 83, and one PhD academic oversees a $1.6B program at 3.1x leverage in a novel HPHT play. CEO tenure dates to 1988 and CFO to 1990, with no disclosed succession plan in the materials reviewed. FY2025 CEO compensation was $21.93M (from $12.54M and $9.92M in the prior two years), equal to ~5.2% of consolidated net income, in a year when production fell 15%, free cash flow was -$445M, and ROE was 7%; the metric set (leverage improvement, well-cost efficiency, reserve replacement, relative TSR) is well chosen for an E&P, and strong multi-year relative TSR (559 versus a 243 peer index) plus the large reserve addition plausibly drove the increase. A majority stockholder controls the company; entities it controls bought 12.5M shares at $8.04 in March 2024, and Comstock operates properties for partnerships it owns ($475 thousand of H1 fees, $3.2M receivable). The exact ownership percentage is not verified from the proxy. Risk factors were not updated for controlling-stockholder status, HPHT execution, the NextEra arrangement, or the PGS redemption obligations.

**Insider signal.** One open-market sale (VP Operations, 48,915 shares at $21.205 in March 2026) and no open-market purchases by any officer or director through a ~40% decline. Executives hold substantial existing stakes (CEO 2.80M shares, President/CFO 1.40M) and operate under blackout and pre-clearance policies, which limits practical scope to buy, so we weight the absence of buying as a modest negative rather than a strong signal. It is, however, a negative that sits alongside falling estimates rather than contradicting them.

**Tax attributes.** Of $1.5B federal and $2.0B state NOLs, $720.7M federal and $1.2B state are expected to expire unused under the Section 382 limitation from the 2018 change of control. The IRS is examining 2022 and 2023; Louisiana has an open exam. H1-2026 cash taxes paid: zero.

**Short interest.** 30.7% of float short, 24.07M shares, 8.9 days to cover. This is a two-sided risk. It confirms that a large and informed cohort shares the bearish view, and it means any positive gas or contract catalyst produces a violent upward move. At this level the short side is crowded, which is one reason we recommend trimming rather than treating the name as a short.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating either side aggressively. Near-term ATM IV of 49.8% sits below one-year realized volatility of 55.0% (a -5.3 point spread) and in the 5th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in contango (49.8% at one month, 53.9% at six months). Put/call skew is balanced at -0.5% near-dated and only mildly negative further out, and put/call open interest is 0.13 — among the lowest readings we see, notable given 30.7% short interest. ATM IV fell from 60.2% to 49.8% between 19 July and 7 August as the earnings event passed. The reading is that positioning bearishness is expressed in the stock loan market, not in listed options, and that no near-term dislocation is priced. For holders reducing exposure, cheap near-dated volatility makes protective puts an efficient alternative to outright sales into a thin tape; the $12.12 52-week low is 8% below spot, roughly one implied move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($21.00, +59%).** Winter 2026-27 Henry Hub firms as LNG feedgas demand absorbs incremental supply. Comstock's 2027 collars cap only a third of volumes, so realizations move higher. FY2027 EBITDAX improves, leverage falls, the revolver is refinanced on ordinary terms, and free cash flow turns positive. Concurrently, a definitive gas-sales agreement is disclosed for the NextEra 2 GW project at premium, long-dated pricing — 110 Bcf/yr on our conversion, roughly 24% of production contracted outside the Henry Hub cycle. Western Haynesville PUD conversions accelerate on completed treating capacity, DD&A per Mcfe falls, and the market capitalizes 2,561 net undeveloped locations at something closer to Sixth Street's midstream underwrite. At a higher EV/EBITDAX multiple, equity approaches the sell-side high of $21.

**Bear case ($9.00, -32%).** Henry Hub stays low through 2027 as associated gas and Appalachian supply meet LNG demand. Comstock's 2027 collar floors at $3.50 protect only ~32% of volumes, so realizations sit at or below full-cycle breakeven. FY2026 burn lands near $800M, the revolver approaches $1.0B drawn, and LTM EBITDAX slips toward $900M, pushing net leverage through 3.5x. The company responds with a capex cut that surrenders 2027 volumes and PUD bookings, an asset sale at a distressed multiple, or an equity issuance; the Sixth Street Class A-2 minimum-return obligation comes due into a weak market. PUD revisions reduce reserves and the goodwill balance of $335.9M is tested. The equity converges toward tangible book of $7.64/share and the analyst low of $9.00.

**Base case ($14.50, +9.8%).** Gas averages moderately higher in 2027; realized $3.40–3.60 after basis and collars. FY2027 volumes ~460 Bcfe, cash costs ~$0.80/Mcfe, EBITDAX ~$1.2–1.3B. Net debt peaks near $3.5B after 2026 burn, and leverage settles at 2.7–2.9x — inside the covenant without headroom to spare. At ~6.3x EV/EBITDAX less net debt and the $584M noncontrolling interest, equity is worth roughly $14.50. The NextEra arrangement remains at the development stage with no disclosed contract, so the market continues to carry it as an option rather than an asset.

Probability-weighting bear 35% / base 45% / bull 20% gives an expected value near $14.10, about +6.7% from $13.21. That is a positive expected return, but it is thin compensation for 55% realized volatility, a covenant that could bind within four quarters, and a refinancing event 15 months out. The asymmetry is not the problem in nominal terms; the probability distribution is, because the bear branch involves permanent capital impairment through dilution or forced sales rather than a temporary derating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Trim exposure now rather than around a dated catalyst. The two natural exit windows — the Q3 print in late October and the winter gas curve setting up in November — both arrive before covenant pressure would resolve, and cheap near-dated volatility (IV 49.8% versus 55.0% realized) means holders who want to stage out can buy protection rather than sell into thin liquidity. Appropriate residual exposure for investors who want to keep the Western Haynesville option is roughly one-third of a full position, sized so that a move to the $9.00 bear case is tolerable.

**Scenario summary from the $13.21 basis:** base $14.50 (+9.8%), bull $21.00 (+59.0%), bear $9.00 (-31.9%).

**Upgrade to HOLD requires** any two of: (1) net leverage falling back below 2.9x on company-reported Adjusted EBITDAX in a quarterly filing; (2) quarterly free cash flow (operating cash flow before working capital less accrual capex) reaching breakeven or better; (3) the revolver extended or refinanced beyond November 2028 on terms disclosed in an 8-K; (4) a share price near tangible book, roughly $8–9, at which proved PV-10 alone provides coverage.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY requires** a disclosed, definitive gas-sales agreement tied to the NextEra power project with volumes, term, and pricing quantified in a filing, combined with 2027 hedge coverage extended above 50% at floors of $3.50 or higher. Either one alone is insufficient; together they would convert the company from a levered Henry Hub option into a partly contracted cash generator.

**Downgrade to AVOID on** any of: (1) reported leverage above 3.4x or disclosure of a covenant waiver or amendment to the leverage test; (2) an equity issuance or convertible priced at or below $12; (3) negative PUD revisions exceeding 500 Bcf, or a PUD conversion count below 20 locations for FY2026; (4) disclosure that the Sixth Street Class A-2 redemption or minimum-return obligation is payable by Comstock parent within 24 months at an amount materially above $600M.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3-2026 results in late October 2026 (leverage, H2 capex delivery against the $720–820M guide, PUD conversion progress), then the FY2026 10-K in February 2027 for reserve revisions, the SEC price deck, and the 2027 development schedule. Any 8-K on the credit facility or the NextEra arrangement resets the analysis immediately.

## Conclusion

The scoring trajectory on this name has been persistently negative (4.5 in May to 4.4 in July, now 3.9), and the June-quarter filing confirms rather than reverses the trend. Net leverage rose from roughly 2.69x to 3.11x in six months against a 3.5x covenant, H1 free cash flow was -$387M, revenue fell 24.9% year over year, and consensus current-year EPS has been cut a further 18.4% in the last 30 days while the share price was essentially unchanged. Lower price on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward; unchanged price on deteriorating fundamentals worsens it, and this is the second case.

What keeps this a REDUCE rather than an AVOID is that the assets are real and the financing is not yet impaired. Liquidity is $1.2B, covenant compliance is a stated filing fact, the bonds trade at 94–98 of par, hedge floors at $3.50 sit just above the derived $2.74/Mcfe breakeven and require no collateral, and Sixth Street's $600M underwrite of the midstream at a ~$2.2B implied value is independent evidence that the Western Haynesville infrastructure has value. With 30.7% of the float short and 8.9 days to cover, the bearish view is crowded and any gas or contract catalyst would move the stock hard.

What keeps it from being a HOLD is the arithmetic of the entry point. Enterprise value of $6,967M is 1.56x proved PV-10, so roughly $8.53 of the $13.21 share price rests on unproved inventory and a power-supply arrangement with no disclosed volumes, term, or price. At 13.8x next-year consensus that has fallen 31.5% in 90 days, an investor is paying a full multiple for the option while bearing the covenant and refinancing risk that comes before it can be exercised. There are better ways to own the LNG-driven gas thesis than through the most levered producer in the highest-cost basin two-thirds unhedged into 2027.
