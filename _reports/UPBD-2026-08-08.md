## Executive Summary

Upbound Group presents the arithmetic of a deep value stock: an enterprise value of roughly $2.49B against FY2026 guided Adjusted EBITDA of $500-535M (4.8x), a share price of $19.83 against guided non-GAAP diluted EPS of $4.00-4.35 (4.75x at the midpoint), and a $1.56 annualized dividend yielding 7.87% that is covered better than 2.5x by cash flow before Brigit advance originations. Leverage has fallen from 3.71x total-liabilities-to-equity at 12/31/2025 to roughly 3.2x at 6/30/2026, and management reaffirmed both the EBITDA and EPS ranges at the July 30 print.

The July 30 disclosures also identified why the multiple is where it is. Acima — 52% of revenue and the designated growth engine — originated approximately 10.7% less gross merchandise value in Q2 2026 than a year earlier, and 8.5% less in the first half. Because lease-to-own revenue is recognized over the lease term, GMV leads revenue; Acima revenue was down only 2.5% in the quarter, which understates the trajectory of the book. Management's own Q3 revenue guide of $1.05-1.15B is below the $1.16B just delivered and roughly 6.5% below the consensus revenue estimate of $1,177M. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA guidance is flat against FY2025's $520M even with Brigit growing revenue 37%, which means the two legacy segments are contributing less EBITDA in aggregate than they did a year ago. Consensus next-year EPS has been cut 3.6% over 90 days to $4.58, a figure that still implies 10.6% growth off the FY2026 midpoint — growth the origination data does not support.

Layered on the operating trend is a regulatory exposure whose cash component is bounded and whose business-model component is not. Approximately 43 states plus the District of Columbia are engaged on Acima's lease-to-own practices, with the 10-K stating that any final settlement is expected to include "injunctive relief regarding various aspects of Acima's business." A separate New York Attorney General action carries no accrual because the loss is not reasonably estimable. The company's ABL, term loan, and notes all treat failure to pay a material judgment as an event of default, against $105M of cash. We move to REDUCE from two prior HOLDs: the stock is 8% higher than at our June review while the forward volume indicator, segment margins at Brigit, and consensus estimates have all deteriorated. The dividend pays roughly 8% for the wait, but the base case offers little price appreciation against a bear case near $13.50.

## Company Overview

Despite a "Computer & Electronics Retail" GICS classification, Upbound is a three-brand subprime consumer finance platform serving what its 10-K calls "cash- and credit-constrained consumers."

**Acima** (~$604M of Q2 2026 revenue, ~52%) is virtual lease-to-own placed through the stores and websites of third-party retailers. The consumer leases from Acima, not the retailer; Acima buys the merchandise at retail rather than wholesale prices, a structurally lower-gross-margin model the 10-K explicitly acknowledges. The retailer originates the application and is paid.

**Rent-A-Center** (~$466M, ~40%) is the legacy brick-and-mortar lease-to-own network: roughly 1,651 company-owned and 312 franchised locations at 6/30/2026, down from approximately 2,075 total at 12/31/2025.

**Brigit** (~$71M, ~6%), acquired in 2025 for approximately $395.4M of consideration within a $460M potential envelope, is an earned-wage-access app with a subscription model plus a bank-partnered Credit Builder product originated through Coastal Community Bank. Brigit is a service provider to the bank, not the lender. Per the 10-K, EWA advances are not contractually required to be repaid, so Brigit's loss experience is behavioral rather than legally enforceable.

The economics are levered lending economics: FY2025 revenue of $4.695B produced $73.2M of GAAP net income (1.56% net margin) against $1.44B of face debt at mid-2026.

Revenue compounded at 3.4% over three years, and the FY2025 increase of 8.7% is almost entirely Brigit, which was absent from the FY2024 base. Gross margin was flat (48.39% versus 48.15%). Operating margin contracted 199 basis points to 4.76% as operating income fell $68.3M on $374.5M of incremental revenue.

Roughly 89% of that operating-income decline traces to one line: approximately $60.7M of estimated legal accruals and litigation defense costs within Other Gains and Charges, with the balance-sheet legal accrual rising from $20.7M to $72.0M. This is the strongest single fact in the bull case — FY2025 GAAP earnings were depressed by a legal event rather than by operating deterioration. It is also the fact requiring the most scrutiny, because legal charges have recurred across multiple years in a business where 43 state attorneys general are currently engaged and an additional state action is unaccrued. For this industry, litigation expense is closer to a recurring cost of capital than to a special item.

### Dividend coverage: two different answers

Retained earnings fell $17.511M during FY2025 (from $1,036.169M to $1,018.658M) while net income was +$73.242M, implying approximately $90.8M of common dividends absent other retained-earnings charges. That is a **124% GAAP payout ratio**. On cash flow, FY2025 operating cash flow of $305.6M less $66.9M of capex leaves $238.7M before the company's own deduction for Brigit customer cash advances — roughly 2.6x coverage of the ~$91M annual dividend cost. Against guided non-GAAP EPS of $4.175, the payout ratio is 37%.

The near-term dividend is safe on cash flow. The distinction matters because it locates the fragility precisely: the payout is secure as long as Adjusted EBITDA holds near $500M and no adverse judgment lands above accrual. Both of those conditions are exactly what the Acima volume trend and the unaccrued New York action put in question.

One presentation note: the Q2 2026 10-Q discloses a correction of an immaterial error reclassifying Brigit customer cash advances from operating to investing activities (approximately $28.5M for H1 2025). Net cash and the balance sheet are unaffected, but operating-cash-flow comparisons spanning the acquisition require the restated basis. The 10-K separately flags that Brigit was not subject to Sarbanes-Oxley scoping prior to acquisition.

### Balance sheet and rate exposure

At 6/30/2026: approximately $1.44B of face debt (a ~$988M term loan plus $450M of senior notes), ~$105M of cash, ~$733M of equity, ~$3.10B of total assets. Net debt of ~$1.33B against guided EBITDA of $500-535M is **2.5-2.7x net leverage**. Undrawn ABL commitments were $237.6M at year-end after ~$48.4M of letters of credit.

Approximately $1.1B was variable rate at 12/31/2025 with **no interest rate swaps in place** as of the 10-K date; each 25bp move equals ~$2.8M of annualized pretax expense. A 100bp SOFR decline is worth ~$11.2M pretax, roughly $0.14 per share after tax at 27%. The sign of that sensitivity is not reliably favorable and would become a headwind if rates rise.

The covenant architecture is the material item. Failure to pay a material judgment is an event of default under the ABL, term loan, and notes; substantially all assets are pledged; a third party crossing 40% beneficial ownership triggers ABL/term-loan default and the notes carry a 101% change-of-control put at 50%.

### Earnings quality and one unreconciled bridge

Getting from guided Adjusted EBITDA of ~$517.5M to guided non-GAAP EPS of ~$4.175 requires roughly $244M of non-GAAP net income and therefore ~$334M of pretax income at a 27% tax rate, leaving only about $183M for combined stock compensation, D&A, and net interest. FY2025 interest expense alone was $113.0M. Lower 2026 interest from deleveraging plus a lower guided tax rate could close the gap, but this reconciliation is not verifiable from the filed exhibits, and the company's reconciliation table sits on its IR site rather than in EDGAR. We treat the guided EPS figure as management's number rather than as an independently confirmed one.

A secondary data conflict: the market-data feed reports EPS of $1.25 alongside a trailing P/E of 12.67, which are mutually inconsistent ($19.83 / $1.25 = 15.9x). Neither reconciles with 4.75x on guided non-GAAP EPS. All valuation work below uses guided non-GAAP EPS and guided EBITDA as the primary basis, with FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of roughly $1.27-1.28 noted separately.

## Growth Analysis

The consolidated top line grew 0.5% in Q2 2026 to approximately $1,163M. Excluding Brigit (derivable from the disclosed +37.1% growth on a $71M base), revenue was approximately **-1.2% year over year**.

**Acima is the swing factor and it is shrinking at the origination line.** GMV fell approximately 10.7% in Q2 and 8.5% in H1, against revenue down only 2.5%. That gap is mechanical: lease revenue is recognized over the lease term, so reported revenue reflects originations from prior quarters. A -10.7% GMV quarter tells you Acima revenue declines widen as higher-vintage cohorts run off against a smaller replacement book. Management's Q3 revenue guide of $1.05-1.15B, below the $1.16B just reported, is consistent with the GMV signal rather than the revenue signal.

Two explanations compete, and the distinction determines whether this is a 2027 trough or a structural decline:

1. *Deliberate underwriting tightening.* Management framed Q2 as improved portfolio quality — tighter credit buys better charge-offs at the cost of volume. This is a coherent subprime trade, and the incentive structure supports it: two of four performance measures in the compensation plan are Acima segment revenue and Rent-A-Center segment revenue, so a management team shrinking Acima GMV by 10.7% is acting against its own short-term payout. The 10-K also names the 2021-2022 stimulus-expiration episode as precedent for exactly this sequence.
2. *Retailer attrition.* Acima depends on third-party retailers who can prioritize Progressive Leasing's or Katapult's waterfall, or run both simultaneously. If retailers are de-prioritizing Acima, the volume does not return when underwriting loosens.

We could not resolve this from the filings, and the earnings call Q&A was unavailable. It is the single highest-value open question in the name.

**Rent-A-Center is stabilizing at the company-owned core while the franchise system contracts.** Company-owned same-store sales rose 160bp in Q2, the third consecutive quarter of growth. Total locations nonetheless fell from ~2,075 to ~1,963 in six months, including roughly 69 closures under a Q2 consolidation program and 70 store merges. SSS excludes stores receiving transferred accounts for 30 months, so consolidation mechanically flatters the metric; positive SSS is not evidence that segment revenue is growing. The franchise system is failing: franchisee count fell from ~448 to ~353 during FY2025 with ~96 stores closed or sold, and the receivable allowance rose from ~$12.7M to ~$16.8M in H1 2026 after a ~$9.5M FY2025 increase. Upbound is its franchisees' creditor, so failures cost both revenue and credit losses.

**Brigit grows, at a worsening margin.** Q2 revenue $71M (+37%), paying subscribers ~1.7M (+~30%) on a disciplined definition, ARPU $14.30 (+6.3%). Segment operating margin fell to roughly 10.6% from roughly 20.2%, driven by net advance losses rising to ~3.6% from ~2.6% and roughly $9.1M of additional advertising. Subscriber growth purchased with marketing spend while loss rates rise is the standard pattern of a fintech reaching into a lower-quality cohort; front-loaded acquisition cost against multi-year subscription revenue is also a rational structure. Cohort LTV/CAC is not disclosed. The founders moved to external advisory roles in Q2 with ~$8.3M of accelerated stock compensation — routine at the roughly 18-month mark of an acquisition, but temporally coincident with the margin compression.

Consensus captures the direction only partially. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 9.5% in 30 days to $0.91, current-year 1.3% to $4.14, and next-year 3.6% to $4.58. That next-year figure still embeds 10.6% growth off the FY2026 guidance midpoint, which the origination data does not support. Further downward revision is the more probable path.

## Valuation Assessment

**Inputs:** $19.83 price, ~58.5M diluted shares, ~$1.33B net debt at 6/30/2026.

- Equity value ≈ $1.16B; **enterprise value ≈ $2.49B**
- **EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA ($517.5M midpoint) = 4.8x**
- **P / FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($4.175 midpoint) = 4.75x**
- **Dividend yield 7.87%**, 37% of guided non-GAAP EPS, 124% of FY2025 GAAP net income
- FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS ~$1.27-1.28 → ~15.5x trailing GAAP

The supplied Consumer Discretionary peer set (BLMN, XPEL, THRM, UAA, WINA; median P/E 35.6) is not a usable comparison — none of these is a subprime lender and one carries negative earnings. The relevant comparables are virtual lease-to-own operators (PROG Holdings, Katapult) and EWA fintechs (Dave, MoneyLion), which trade in a materially lower multiple band than the broad discretionary median. Against that cohort, UPBD's 4.8x EV/EBITDA is at the low end but not an outlier, and the discount is explainable: 2.5-2.7x net leverage on a portfolio whose largest segment is originating double-digit-percent less volume, with a multistate regulatory settlement expected to constrain that segment's practices.

The five-year shareholder record frames the multiple. Per the proxy's pay-versus-performance table, $100 invested at the end of 2020 was worth $82 at the end of 2025 against a peer index at $139 — roughly 57 percentage points of cumulative underperformance, with the 2025 index move from 128 to 82 implying an approximately 36% single-year decline. The market has been derating this business for five years, and low multiples on this name have not historically been self-correcting.

**Where does fair value sit?** Adjusted EBITDA of $520M in FY2025 and guidance of $500-535M for FY2026 means the run rate is flat. The Acima book contraction points to FY2027 EBITDA below the FY2026 midpoint absent a GMV recovery — call it $470-500M. At 4.8x on $485M, EV is $2.33B; less $1.25B of net debt after another year of paydown gives $1.08B of equity, or roughly $18.50 per share. A re-rating to 5.5x on the same $485M produces roughly $27. That bracket, $18.50 to $27, is where the argument lives, and the base case sits close to the current price.

On the anchors: the consensus mean target of $28.25 implies 42% upside, but the low target is $20.00 — essentially spot — and analyst sentiment is degrading (one move from buy to hold in the last month against falling estimates). On a name with negative revision momentum, consensus targets lag downward rather than upward; they do not function as a floor here.

Valuation scores 5.7. The absolute multiple and the FCF-covered yield provide real support and prevent a lower score. What prevents a higher one is that the cheapness is fully explained by identifiable forward deterioration rather than by a mispricing we can articulate — the classic profile of a compressed multiple attached to a shrinking earnings base.

## Competitive Landscape

Acima's competitive position is weak by its own filing's account. The 10-K lists among Acima-specific risks: reliance on unaffiliated retailers for advertising, application assistance, and explaining to the consumer that the lease is with Acima rather than the retailer; the risk that retailers substitute or add competitors' waterfalls; "lower barriers to entry and start-up capital costs to launch a competitor" because the model rents third-party store footprints rather than owning locations; "reduced gross margins compared to the Rent-A-Center segment because Acima generally purchases merchandise it leases to customers based on retail, rather than wholesale, prices"; and greater exposure to both consumer and merchant fraud than store-based transactions. The last of those reads differently after Q2's $13.3M of fraudulent contract losses.

The competitive set extends beyond lease-to-own. The 10-K names buy-now-pay-later, installment, payday, and title lenders as substitutes that let the target customer shop at conventional retailers instead. BNPL penetration at the point of sale directly attacks the waterfall's position in the approval sequence.

Rent-A-Center's company-owned base is the better-trending asset on a same-store basis and the one the market has largely written off, but it is shrinking on total locations and its franchise channel is contracting sharply.

Brigit operates in a crowded EWA market (EarnIn, Dave, MoneyLion, Empower, Cleo, plus employer-integrated DailyPay and Payactiv) with a bifurcating regulatory picture. The December 2025 CFPB advisory opinion — withdrawing the prior interpretive rule and concluding that certain structured EWA products are not credit under TILA/Regulation Z, and that optional expedited-funding fees and tips are not finance charges — is a material federal positive. The 10-K correctly notes it lacks force of law and does not preempt state law. California finalized a registration regime without rate caps; Connecticut and Maryland have indicated EWA can be treated as lending subject to licensing and rate limits. Brigit has received inquiries from state regulators about licensing and registration.

Trajectory across all three segments is flat-to-deteriorating. Competitive position scores 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Injunctive relief on 52% of revenue (highest unpriced risk).** Approximately 43 states plus DC are engaged on Acima's lease-to-own practices. The 10-K states any final binding settlement is expected to include injunctive relief regarding "various aspects of Acima's business." The $60.2M accrual at 6/30/2026 bounds the cash. It does not bound a consent framework governing pricing, disclosure, renewal, or account-management practices in the segment that is both the largest and already originating 10.7% less volume. The alternative reading is equally available: AG settlements often codify practices already adopted, in which case settlement removes a multi-year overhang and is a positive catalyst. The direction is unknowable from the filings, which is precisely what makes it a binary the current multiple does not resolve.

**2. Unaccrued New York AG action plus material-judgment cross-default.** The NYAG matter carries no accrual because the loss is not reasonably estimable; a motion to dismiss was pending as of the Q2 10-Q. Failure to pay a material judgment is an event of default under all three debt instruments. With ~$105M of cash and ~$1.44B of secured-and-notes debt, capacity to absorb a judgment materially above accrual without a liquidity event is limited.

**3. Metric-definition sensitivity at Acima.** The headline "lease charge-off rate improved 50 basis points to 8.8%" is measured on a basis that, per the earnings release footnote, excludes fraudulent lease-to-own contract losses. Q2 fraud losses were approximately $13.3M, or 2.20 percentage points of Acima's $604M of revenue, putting all-in losses on the order of 11.0%. If the Q2 2025 fraud base was small, the year-over-year comparison inverts from a 50bp improvement to roughly a 170bp deterioration. Similarly, the reported 117bp segment EBITDA margin expansion to 16.2% would become an approximately 220bp contraction to ~14.0% if the loss sits below the segment EBITDA line — which we could not confirm. Separating discrete cyber-fraud from credit charge-offs is analytically defensible and the exclusion is disclosed; we attribute no motive. The point is that a reader relying on the highlights understates Acima's total Q2 economic loss rate, and the investigation is ongoing.

**4. Unhedged floating-rate debt.** ~$1.1B variable with no swaps at the 10-K date; $2.8M pretax per 25bp. The direction of this exposure is genuinely two-sided depending on the path of rates.

**5. Subprime consumer exposure.** Lease-to-own has a partial countercyclical offset (consumers losing credit access trade down into LTO), and Rent-A-Center's three quarters of positive SSS argue the low-end consumer is still transacting. But rising loss rates at Brigit (3.6% from 2.6%) and franchisee failures are consistent with income stress at the margin. The 10-K's macro risk factor specifically cites the post-2021 stimulus-expiration episode and adds tariff policy as a named driver that raises consumer prices and compresses the target customer's discretionary income.

**6. Earnings quality.** GAAP net income of $73.2M against Adjusted EBITDA of $520M, an unreconciled EBITDA-to-EPS bridge, a cash-flow classification correction, and Brigit's pre-acquisition absence from SOX scoping all argue for discounting the adjusted framing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.7% sits below one-year realized volatility of 45.9% (a -4.2 point spread) at the 13th percentile of the current chain, with a ±6.4% implied move on the August 21 tenor. Options are cheap relative to how this stock has actually traded, which makes protective puts an efficient way to carry existing exposure through the multistate settlement window rather than a reason to add risk. The term structure is in contango (41.7% near versus 52.4% at six months), placing the priced uncertainty in December rather than August — consistent with a legal-resolution or FY2027-guidance event rather than a near-term earnings catalyst. The 1-month put/call skew of 22.5% is described as elevated put demand and the 1-month put/call volume ratio is 11.0 against a 0.48 aggregate ratio, indicating concentrated near-dated put activity. Short interest at 12.8% of float with 5.7 days to cover confirms institutional bearish positioning is already substantial; this limits incremental downside from short selling but also means the bearish view is not contrarian. Note the prior-snapshot ATM IV reading of 564.3% is not a usable comparison and appears to be a data artifact.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $27 (+36%)

The Acima GMV decline is deliberate underwriting tightening, corroborated by the compensation structure paying partly on Acima segment revenue — management is shrinking a metric it is paid on. The 2021-2022 precedent named in the 10-K shows the sequence reverses: tighten, absorb 12-18 months of volume decline, then re-accelerate with a cleaner book. The multistate settlement lands with injunctive relief that codifies practices already in place, removing a multi-year overhang and releasing the legal accrual drag that caused roughly 89% of the FY2025 operating-income decline. Rent-A-Center's company-owned base continues positive SSS, Brigit's advertising spend annualizes into subscription revenue at 1.7M paying subscribers, and deleveraging continues from 2.5-2.7x toward 2.0x while the ~$235M buyback authorization eventually gets used. On $485-500M of FY2027 EBITDA at 5.5x, less ~$1.25B of net debt, equity is roughly $27. Investors collect a 7.9% dividend throughout.

### Bear Case → $13.50 (-32%)

Acima's GMV decline reflects retailer waterfall de-prioritization to Progressive and Katapult rather than voluntary tightening, so volume does not return. Revenue follows GMV down with a two-to-three-quarter lag, and the Q3 guide already below the Q2 actual is the first leg. The multistate settlement imposes economic injunctive relief on pricing or renewal practices in a segment that is 52% of revenue, and the unaccrued New York action produces a judgment above accrual, testing the material-judgment cross-default with $105M of cash. Brigit's loss rates continue rising as the cohort degrades and the founders' departure slows product velocity. FY2027 EBITDA falls to $430-450M, non-GAAP EPS to roughly $3.00, and the dividend — 124% of GAAP earnings and dependent on flat EBITDA — is cut to fund covenant headroom. At 4.5x $3.00 the shares are $13.50, near the $15.82 52-week low with the yield support removed.

The base case sits between: FY2026 guidance delivered, FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of roughly $4.10 as Acima runoff offsets Brigit growth and lower interest expense, a 5.0x multiple, and a $20.50 target. That is +3.4% of price appreciation plus 7.9% of dividend, roughly 11% total return, against a -32% bear outcome. Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 50% / bear 25% gives approximately +2.5% of expected price return, or roughly 10% total including the dividend — adequate compensation for a levered subprime lender only if you underwrite the regulatory binary favorably.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** This is a downgrade from two prior HOLDs. The justification is specific and not price-based: since the June review the shares are 8% higher while Q2 disclosed Acima GMV at -10.7%, a Q3 revenue guide below the quarter just delivered and 6.5% below consensus, Brigit segment operating margin halving to ~10.6%, a $13.3M fraud loss excluded from the headline credit metric, and consensus next-year EPS cut 3.6%. Higher price plus deteriorating forward indicators is worse risk/reward on both axes.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength above $21 rather than selling into weakness; the name has repeatedly traded the $17.50-$22.50 range and the December options tenor prices a ±24.2% move, so patience on exit price is warranted. Reduce to roughly one-third of a normal income-allocation position ahead of the Q3 print (late October) and the multistate settlement window. For holders who want to retain the 7.9% yield, near-dated puts are unusually cheap at 41.7% implied against 45.9% realized volatility and are a lower-cost way to cap the settlement tail than outright liquidation.

**Base case target $20.50** (5.0x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of ~$4.10): +3.4% price, ~11% total return with dividend.
**Bull case target $27** (5.5x on $485-500M FY2027 EBITDA less ~$1.25B net debt): +36%.
**Bear case target $13.50** (4.5x ~$3.00 FY2027 EPS, dividend cut): -32%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the two decision points being the late-October Q3 print (does Acima GMV decline narrow?) and any 8-K announcing the multistate AG settlement terms.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (need two of three):**
1. Acima GMV decline narrows to better than -5% year over year for two consecutive quarters, with lease charge-offs on an all-in basis (fraud included) at or below 10.5%.
2. Multistate AG settlement executed with injunctive terms that do not alter Acima pricing, renewal, or account-management economics, and total cash within the $60.2M accrual.
3. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance at or above $4.35 issued alongside resumption of buybacks, or FY2026 free cash flow confirmed above $250M in an issuer document.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Adverse New York AG outcome, or any judgment above accrual that requires an amendment or waiver under the ABL, term loan, or notes.
2. Dividend reduction or suspension, or Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $480M.
3. Evidence that Acima GMV decline reflects retailer attrition — a named national retailer moving its waterfall to a competitor, or a fourth consecutive quarter of GMV declines worse than -8% while charge-offs stop improving.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026, or immediately upon any 8-K disclosing settlement terms with the state attorneys general.

## Conclusion

Upbound is cheap for reasons that are visible in its own filings. At 4.8x guided EBITDA and 4.75x guided non-GAAP EPS with a 7.87% dividend covered better than 2.5x by cash flow before Brigit advances, the stock offers real absolute-value support, and the balance sheet is genuinely improving — total liabilities to equity fell from 3.71x to roughly 3.2x in six months. FY2025's GAAP earnings decline was approximately 89% attributable to legal accruals rather than operating deterioration, which supports a normalized-earnings argument.

Against that: the segment representing 52% of revenue originated 10.7% less volume in Q2 than a year earlier and reported revenue lags that signal; management's own Q3 revenue guide sits below the quarter just delivered; consolidated Adjusted EBITDA guidance is flat versus FY2025 even with the acquired growth asset up 37%; Brigit's segment margin halved while its loss rate rose; consensus estimates are falling across every horizon; and roughly 43 state attorneys general plus a separate unaccrued New York action sit over a business whose legal characterization as lease rather than credit is contested state by state, with the 10-K itself signaling that any settlement carries injunctive relief on Acima's practices. The material-judgment cross-default converts a litigation question into a financing question.

The base case is a stock that pays you 8% and goes approximately nowhere; the bear case is a 32% decline with the dividend gone. That asymmetry, on a name where five years of low multiples have not proven self-correcting, argues for reducing exposure rather than maintaining it. We would revisit at $15-16 with the multistate settlement resolved on non-economic injunctive terms, or on two consecutive quarters of narrowing Acima GMV declines.
