**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Macy's has stabilized a business that spent most of the last decade in secular decline. Comparable sales (owned + licensed + marketplace) rose +1.5% in FY2025 — the first positive year after -0.9% and -6.0% — and the company has now strung together four consecutive quarters of comp gains through Q1 FY2026. The luxury segment is doing real work: Bloomingdale's posted +10.2% comps in Q1 FY2026 (its best-ever first quarter) and Bluemercury has now comped positive for 20 straight quarters. The balance sheet is healthier, with long-term debt down from $3.0B to $2.4B, operating cash flow of $1,430M, and $1.25B of cash against an undrawn $2.1B ABL facility. Management has resumed buybacks (~$251M in FY25 at ~$14.21/share, below the current price) while raising the dividend to a ~3.3% yield.

The problem is that the reported earnings recovery overstates the underlying improvement. FY25's $2.32 diluted EPS and $1,030M of operating income were flattered by a **one-time $328M interchange-fee litigation settlement** worth roughly $1.19 per share. Stripping that out, adjusted EPS has *declined* three straight years ($3.28 → $2.64 → $2.32) and Adjusted EBITDA has fallen from $2,236M to $1,977M to $1,842M. The turnaround has arrested the top-line bleed faster than it has rebuilt per-dollar profitability. The core Macy's nameplate — the vast majority of revenue and ~98% of goodwill — is still barely comping (+0.4% FY25, +1.6% Q1 FY26) and is being shrunk via ~150 store closures.

The stock has already re-rated to reflect the good news. At ~$23, M is up 87% over the trailing year and sits below its 52-week high of $26.10, and the consensus mean analyst target of $22.77 is *below* the current price. The ~10x earnings multiple looks cheap versus retail peers, but a compressed multiple on a business with declining adjusted earnings and a structurally challenged core is more consistent with a value trap than an asymmetric entry. We rate the stock HOLD. There is enough balance-sheet support, real-estate optionality, and luxury-segment momentum to keep us out of an outright AVOID, but with the discount consumed and adjusted earnings still eroding, not enough forward asymmetry to justify a BUY.

## Company Overview

Macy's is a ~$21.8B-revenue omnichannel retailer operating 665 stores across three nameplates spanning value to luxury:

- **Macy's** — the mainstream mid-to-higher-income department store (including off-price "Backstage" and the "Macy's Marketplace" digital platform). This holds the vast majority of revenue and goodwill and is the structurally challenged, shrinking part of the business.
- **Bloomingdale's** — upscale/luxury department stores plus the smaller "Bloomies" format and licensed international locations. The clear growth engine.
- **Bluemercury** — luxury beauty and spa retail; small but consistently comping positive.

Revenue is diversified by category — Women's Accessories/Shoes/Cosmetics/Fragrances ($9.1B), Women's Apparel ($4.8B), Men's & Kids' ($4.7B), Home/Other ($3.2B) — and the company serves roughly 40M active customers. Two increasingly important non-merchandise streams support margins: the Citibank credit-card revenue-share program ($669M net in FY25, +25% YoY) and Macy's Media Network ($188M in retail advertising, now boosted by an Amazon ad partnership).

The strategy — the multi-year "Bold New Chapter" — has three planks: rationalize the Macy's fleet (close ~150 underproductive stores, reinvest in a "Reimagine 125" go-forward set), accelerate luxury (Bloomingdale's, Bluemercury), and simplify operations (a new automated "China Grove" fulfillment center). The barbell logic is to shrink the declining core to a profitable base while leaning into the segments that are taking share.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is still declining, but the decline is now closure-driven rather than demand-driven.** Net sales moved from $24.4B (FY23) to $22.3B (FY24) to $21.8B (FY25). The FY25 decline of -2.4% was driven almost entirely by ~$700M of lost sales from closed doors; comparable sales rose. The underlying demand base is no longer contracting, a real change from the -6.0% comp environment of two years ago.

**Earnings quality is the central analytical issue.** FY25 operating income of $1,030M (4.6% margin) and net income of $642M look like a strong step up from FY24 ($909M / $582M). But the composition is misleading:

- FY25 operating income included the interchange-fee litigation settlement (net of legal fees), worth ~$1.19/share.
- Partially offsetting: $230M of impairment/restructuring, $67M of pension settlement charges, and $33M of debt-extinguishment losses.
- On an adjusted basis, EPS and Adjusted EBITDA both declined. Gross margin also slipped 40bps to 38.0% on tariff pressure.

The reported recovery is partly optical. The bull case leans on comp inflection; the bear case leans on the fact that profitability per dollar of sales is still eroding.

**Cash flow and the balance sheet are improving.** Operating cash flow rose to $1,430M and free cash flow was roughly $690M–$1,057M depending on which capex figure is used (the MD&A's $740M gross capex is the more complete number; the XBRL $373M understates it). The company de-levered, and net interest expense fell 16%. The current ratio is 1.49 and the ABL facility (reduced from $3.0B and extended to 2030) is undrawn.

**Screening flags are mixed but not alarming.** The Altman Z-score of 2.31 sits in the grey zone, and reported D/E is 3.34x — both largely a function of a heavily lease-laden asset base (~$6.0B of operating-lease obligations) rather than imminent distress. ROE is 13.2%, ROA 4.0%, and the Piotroski score of 5/5 signals solid current-year financial trends. Credit ratings straddle investment grade (Fitch BBB-, Moody's Ba1, S&P BB+, all stable). This is a company with real leverage but manageable near-term maturities and improving coverage.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story bifurcates cleanly: luxury is winning; the core is stabilizing at best. Bloomingdale's comped +7.4% in FY25 and +10.2% in Q1 FY26; Bluemercury has 20 consecutive positive quarters. These are genuine share gains against a strong higher-income consumer. Against that, the core Macy's nameplate comped +0.4% in FY25 and +1.6% in Q1 FY26 (reimagined stores +2.4%) — stabilization, not growth, and it is being actively shrunk by ~150 closures.

Q1 FY2026, reported June 3, 2026, was a clean beat: adjusted EPS $0.13 vs. $0.02 consensus on revenue of $4.89B (+1.8%). Management raised FY2026 guidance to net sales of $21.5–21.75B, comps of +0.5% to +1.2% (up from -0.5% to +0.5%), and adjusted EPS of $2.00–$2.20. The company has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four reported quarters, which lends some credibility to the raised outlook.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest, not the kind of inflection that flags a systematic re-rating. Current-year consensus EPS is up +4.9% over 90 days to $2.21, and next-year is up +6.0% to $2.33. The raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance sits *below* FY25's reported $2.32 — consistent with the erosion in underlying earnings power once the one-time settlement rolls off. The next-quarter revision line (-3003%) is a sign-flip artifact off a near-zero base and carries no signal.

The most important open growth question is whether luxury can grow fast enough to offset core decline and rebuild adjusted EBITDA. So far the top-line answer is trending positive; the profitability answer is still negative. Layered on top is a macro headwind: the mid-market department-store consumer is exactly the cohort under pressure from a weakening housing wealth effect, higher financing costs, and broad discretionary retrenchment. Mid-market discretionary comps across the sector have been decelerating, which raises the risk that the +0.5% to +1.2% comp guide proves optimistic in a softer H2.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$23, Macy's trades at ~10x trailing earnings and roughly 0.27x sales — a clear discount to the Broadline Retail peer set (median P/E ~25x). We do not treat that cheapness as a buy signal, for three reasons.

First, **the trailing earnings that anchor the multiple are inflated by a one-time item.** On the FY26 adjusted EPS guide, the forward multiple is ~11–12x on earnings that are lower than the prior year — a stock that is cheap because its normalized earnings power is declining, not because the market is mispricing durable growth. This is the classic value-trap profile: a low multiple that reflects genuine structural impairment rather than an overlooked catalyst.

Second, **the peer comparison is not clean.** ETSY (28x), EBAY (25x), and AMZN (29x) are marketplace/platform businesses with structurally higher growth and margins; they are not the right comps for a mall-based department-store operator. The more relevant analog is Kohl's at ~7.3x — another challenged department store — which suggests the department-store cohort is being valued in the 7–11x range for good reason. Macy's is roughly in line with that, not below it.

Third, **the "analyst targets as floors" logic does not apply here.** That principle holds for inflection stocks with genuinely rising earnings and cheap forward multiples. Macy's consensus mean target sits *below* the current price, and the rating skews to "Reduce" (per the brief: 2 Sell, 11 Hold, 1 Buy). When Wall Street's target is below where the stock trades on a name that just beat and raised, that is analysts signaling the operational momentum is real but the durability is doubtful. The prior appreciation has consumed the discount that would have made this asymmetric, and it did so on earnings that are flattered.

The one place where a valuation case could exist is real estate. The 10-K devotes a full risk factor to monetizing and redeveloping stores, warehouses, garages, and outparcels — the exact lever activists (Arkhouse/Brigade, later Barington) have pushed. A sum-of-the-parts that separates the real estate from the operating business, or a renewed take-private/breakup approach, could unlock value well above the current equity multiple. That optionality is real but unquantified and event-dependent, and the change-of-control put on ~$2.19B of notes complicates any transaction. We give it credit as downside support, not as a base-case valuation input. Our base case values the equity at ~11–12x the midpoint of the FY26 adjusted EPS guide (~$2.10), or roughly $23–25 — essentially where the stock trades today.

## Competitive Landscape

Macy's competes across an unusually wide front: department stores (Nordstrom, Kohl's), off-price (TJX, Ross), specialty retailers, mass merchants, marketplaces (Amazon), and direct-to-consumer brands. The structural headwind is the secular decline of mall-based department stores — declining foot traffic, share migration to off-price and online, and the resulting cumulative impairments ($160M FY25, $88M FY24, $957M FY23, on top of the $3.0B goodwill write-off in 2020).

The company's competitive trajectory is two-sided. Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury are gaining share in luxury and beauty — improving competitive position. The core Macy's nameplate is losing share to off-price and DTC and is being managed for profitable contraction rather than growth — a deteriorating competitive position that management is addressing by shrinking rather than defending. Supplier concentration is low (no vendor exceeds 4% of purchases), and the credit-card and media-network revenue streams add differentiated, higher-margin economics that a pure merchandise retailer lacks. On balance the portfolio trajectory is mixed: the winning parts are small and the challenged part is large, which is why we hold competitive position below the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff whipsaw (elevated, unresolved).** The 10-K discloses that on February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court struck down the "reciprocal" and "fentanyl" IEEPA tariffs, after which the Administration imposed a 150-day 10% tariff (possibly rising to 15%) on imports. Macy's sources most private-label goods from Asia. Tariffs already cost 40bps of gross margin in FY25, and the FY26 guide assumes a "net-neutral" tariff/fuel impact — a key assumption that could unwind the raised outlook if the regime tightens.

**Earnings-quality erosion.** Adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS have declined three straight years even as comps turned positive, and the FY26 adjusted EPS guide is below FY25's reported figure. This is the risk least reflected in the "cheap on trailing earnings" framing.

**Credit-card program dependence and regulatory risk.** The Citibank revenue-share program is a high-margin stream (~3.1% of sales) that expires in March 2030 and is exposed to consumer credit deterioration, BNPL substitution, and CFPB late-fee rulemaking (the $8 late-fee cap was vacated in April 2025 but remains a live thread).

**Leverage and change-of-control mechanics.** $2.4B of debt plus ~$6.0B of operating-lease obligations sit against an ABL fixed-charge-coverage covenant. A change-of-control combined with a downgrade would trigger a 101% put on ~$2.19B of notes — directly relevant to any renewed take-private/breakup interest.

**Consumer cliff.** Macy's serves precisely the mid-market cohort most exposed to a weakening housing wealth effect and discretionary retrenchment in H2 2026. Sector comp deceleration is a live leading indicator against the raised comp guide.

**Governance watch items.** The company has filed frequent Item 5.02 director/officer changes (Nov 2024, Dec 2025, Mar 2026, May 2026), and CEO "compensation actually paid" spiked to $20.5M despite five-year cumulative TSR (156.83) lagging the peer group (220.77) — a pay-vs-performance misalignment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing meaningful uncertainty. ATM IV is 127.4% on the front week (90th percentile within the chain) against 44.8% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 127.4% vs. 45.3% long-dated), pricing imminent event stress. The extreme 1-day reading (127% IV, -64% put skew) is a thin-options artifact; the more informative 1-month (49.9%) and 3-month (59.7%) tenors still sit well above historical vol. ATM IV has climbed from 52.0% to 88.7% over the past month while the stock fell ~6%, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.52 to 0.89. Longer-dated skew is only mildly negative (-2.4% at six months), and the overall put/call volume ratio (0.95) is close to balanced, so positioning is elevated-uncertainty rather than one-directional. Short interest of 12.4% of float (3.7 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme; the muted market reaction to a strong Q1 beat suggests skepticism is entrenched rather than about to capitulate in either direction. The signal is consistent with a HOLD: rich near-term vol argues against paying up for entry ahead of the next print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The comp inflection is real and durable, luxury (Bloomingdale's/Bluemercury) grows fast enough to offset a stabilizing core, and the Bold New Chapter store rationalization lifts fleet productivity and rebuilds adjusted EBITDA. The de-levered balance sheet and resumed buybacks support the equity, and real-estate monetization or a renewed activist/take-private approach unlocks a sum-of-the-parts value well above the ~10x operating multiple. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. **Bull case: ~$30** (the high analyst target), roughly +29% from current, driven by real-estate optionality or a takeout premium layered on a modest earnings recovery.

### Bear Case
The one-time interchange settlement rolls off, FY26 adjusted EPS comes in at the low end of guidance or below as tariffs bite and the mid-market consumer weakens in H2, and adjusted EBITDA continues its multi-year decline. The core Macy's nameplate resumes negative comps, triggering further impairments, and the multiple compresses back toward the Kohl's-style ~7x on declining earnings. **Bear case: ~$16–17**, roughly -27% to -31% from current, on a de-rating of falling normalized earnings with no offsetting real-estate event.

The defining tension is that operational momentum (four comp gains, four earnings beats) is running directly against still-eroding profitability per dollar and a fully-consumed valuation discount.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The improving-fundamentals story is real but largely priced after an 87% run to near the 52-week high, and the ~10x multiple reflects genuine structural challenges rather than a mispricing. There is enough balance-sheet strength, luxury momentum, and real-estate optionality to avoid an AVOID/short, but not enough forward asymmetry to justify a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EBITDA turns positive year-over-year for two consecutive quarters (the first evidence the recovery is rebuilding profitability, not just stabilizing sales), OR
- A credible, dated real-estate monetization / sum-of-the-parts catalyst emerges (activist agreement, JV, or take-private approach) that values the equity meaningfully above ~12x operating earnings, OR
- The stock pulls back toward the low-to-mid teens (below the ~$14 buyback zone) while comps remain positive, restoring the valuation asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY26 comps turn negative or management cuts the raised comp/EPS guidance (would confirm the H2 consumer-cliff risk and the erosion thesis), OR
- A new material impairment at the core Macy's nameplate, OR
- Gross margin compresses more than ~100bps on a tightening tariff regime while the "net-neutral" assumption is abandoned.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 print (expected late August / early September 2026), which will show whether the raised guide holds against a softer H2 consumer and the rolling-off of the one-time settlement. The tariff regime (the 150-day measure and any escalation to 15%) is a parallel monitor.

## Conclusion

Macy's has executed a credible operational stabilization: the top line is no longer bleeding, luxury is genuinely winning share, the balance sheet is materially healthier, and the team is returning capital with discipline. Those accomplishments justify the improvement in the company's financial-health and growth trajectory versus a year ago. But the investment case is undermined by two facts the headline numbers obscure: adjusted earnings power has declined three straight years, and the stock has already re-rated to reflect the good news, leaving the consensus mean target below the current price. The ~10x multiple is a discount for durable secular pressure, not an overlooked catalyst.

We rate Macy's **HOLD**. The luxury growth engine, de-levered balance sheet, and embedded real-estate optionality provide downside support and keep this out of AVOID territory. But with the valuation discount consumed, core earnings still eroding, tariff and consumer headwinds live, and the stock near its 52-week high, the risk/reward is roughly balanced. We would need either evidence that the recovery is rebuilding profitability (positive adjusted EBITDA growth) or a dated real-estate catalyst — or a pullback toward the low-to-mid teens — before turning constructive.
