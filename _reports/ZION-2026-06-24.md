**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zions Bancorporation is an $88 billion-asset Western commercial bank that, eight months ago, sat at the epicenter of a regional-bank credit scare. In October 2025 the company disclosed a $50M charge-off tied to fraud on two related commercial borrowers within its non-depository financial institution (NDFI) book, and the stock collapsed to a 52-week low of $46.19 alongside a sector-wide selloff that swept up Western Alliance and other regionals. The primary-source data now confirms what the recovery in the share price already implies: the loss was a contained, idiosyncratic event rather than evidence of systemic NDFI deterioration. By Q1 2026, NDFI nonaccruals were $10M, total NDFI exposure was just $2.0B (3.3% of loans), the bank released reserves (a negative $7M provision), and annualized net charge-offs fell to 0.03%.

The fundamental story underneath is healthy and improving. Q1 2026 diluted EPS came in at $1.56, up 38% year over year; net interest margin expanded to 3.27% from 3.10%; the efficiency ratio improved to 65.0%; ROTCE reached 15.5%; and tangible book value per share rose 19% to $41.75 as AOCI losses narrowed and retained earnings accumulated. The franchise has materially de-risked since 2023, carrying available liquidity equal to 134% of uninsured deposits and a CET1 ratio of 11.6%, up roughly 80bps year over year. Estimate revisions are positive across the board (current-quarter EPS revised +11.8% over 90 days), and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters.

The problem for a fresh buyer is timing. The stock has rerated from the mid-$40s to $68.49, a 38% gain over the trailing twelve months and within a percent of its 52-week high and the consensus mean target of $69.24. The recovery trade, buying a well-capitalized bank mispriced by a fraud headline, has largely played out. ZION now trades at roughly 10.4x trailing and forward EPS, a modest discount to the peer median of 12.7x, but that discount partly reflects genuine structural features (no holding company, OCC-required buyback approval, AOCI overhang under Basel III Endgame). We rate the stock a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias, recognizing that the remaining 12-month upside depends on continued NII expansion and the one-time Q2 Visa gain rather than a re-rating of a depressed multiple.

## Company Overview

Zions is a Salt Lake City-headquartered regional bank operating across 11 Western states through seven separately managed, locally branded affiliate banks: Zions Bank (UT/ID/WY), California Bank & Trust, Amegy Bank (TX), National Bank of Arizona, Nevada State Bank, Vectra Bank Colorado, and The Commerce Bank of Washington/Oregon. The affiliate model emphasizes local decision authority, pricing, and community relationships, distinguishing it from typical centralized regional banks.

The corporate structure has two unusual features that matter materially. First, Zions is a standalone national bank (National Association) without a bank holding company, regulated directly by the OCC rather than the Federal Reserve. The 10-K explicitly flags this as a competitive disadvantage: OCC securities and merger frameworks are less developed, shareholder approval is required for all mergers with no minor-transaction exceptions, and the bank cannot pursue holding-company-only activities such as insurance underwriting or merchant banking. Second, share repurchases require OCC approval, which constrains capital-return cadence. These features make Zions structurally harder to use as an acquisition vehicle and reduce takeover optionality.

The business mix is concentrated in commercial banking. Commercial and commercial real estate loans together represent roughly 74% of the $61.3B loan book. The franchise serves small- and medium-sized businesses and is asset-sensitive. Geographic concentration is real: UT, ID, TX, and CA account for 77% of commercial loans. Deposits totaled $76.9B, producing a conservative 80% loan-to-deposit ratio, and noninterest-bearing deposits rose to 35% of the total, a favorable funding mix.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 results were strong on most dimensions. The 38% EPS increase was driven by higher net interest income, higher fee income, and the reserve release. Net interest income reached $662M, up 6% year over year, with NIM expansion driven by funding-cost relief and the runoff of low-yielding securities. The efficiency ratio improvement produced positive operating leverage of 2.7%. ROE was 13.1%.

Capital is the strongest part of the story. Tier 1 leverage stood at 9.1%. The TBV/share growth was propelled by retained earnings and narrowing AOCI losses. The AOCI line still carries a $1.9B net unrealized loss (roughly $1.2–1.5B after tax, tied largely to AFS-to-HTM transferred securities). This is currently excluded from regulatory capital, but the March 2026 Basel III Endgame proposal would include most AOCI in regulatory capital, the single most important forward regulatory watch-item. Management expects to remain well-capitalized either way, and as rates fall and securities pull to par, the drag mechanically shrinks (already visible in the TBV gain).

On liquidity and credit, the post-2023 de-risking is evident. Loans grew 2% and deposits 2%. Uninsured deposits are 46% of the total ($35.0B), against available liquidity of $46.9B. FY2025 net income was $899M, and full-year net charge-offs were only 0.15% of average loans. The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, ranging from 2.8% to 21.8%, with the most recent quarter beating by 7.3%.

One technical note on financial-health screens: the Altman Z-Score of 0.17 ("distress zone") is a model-misapplication artifact and should be ignored here. Altman Z was built for industrial firms; deposit-funded banks are structurally high-leverage, so the elevated debt/equity and near-zero working-capital inputs are normal for the sector. The applicable metrics — positive net income, positive ROA, operating cash flow exceeding net income — are all favorable. We score financial health a 7.0, reflecting strong capital, liquidity, and earnings quality, tempered by the AOCI overhang that could reduce regulatory capital optics under a rule change.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is solid for a regional bank but not exceptional. The primary driver is NII momentum: the asset-sensitive balance sheet (+200bps to rates equals +7.4% to NII) means the bank benefits if rates hold higher for longer, which the current macro setup supports. The flip side is symmetric: a -200bps move would cut NII by 7.2%, so a dovish pivot is a headwind to the core earnings engine.

Estimate revisions are clearly positive. Current-quarter consensus EPS rose +11.8% over 90 days to $1.72, and the current-year estimate climbed +6.2% to $6.60. The forward momentum is real, though the magnitude (~6% on the full year) is well short of the +30% threshold that would flag a structural earnings turn. The current quarter's +7.0% 30-day revision is partly a one-time effect: management expects a ~$215M pre-tax gain in Q2 2026 from selling legacy Visa Class B-1 shares held at zero cost since 2007. Analysts should normalize run-rate EPS for both the Visa gain and the negative provision, which together flatter near-term reported earnings.

Two organic growth levers stand out. The Basis Multifamily Finance acquisition, announced in Q1 2026 and subject to regulatory approval, adds Fannie Mae DUS and Freddie Mac Optigo origination/servicing capabilities, expanding the CRE/capital-markets franchise into permanent multifamily financing, a sensible adjacency that diversifies fee income. And the improving deposit mix (35% noninterest-bearing) provides a durable funding-cost advantage. We score growth prospects a 6.5, acknowledging genuine NII-driven momentum and positive revisions, discounted for the one-time component of near-term earnings and a structural growth ceiling typical of mature regional banks.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the Regional Banks peer median of 12.7x, ZION's ~10.4x is a discount of roughly 18%. The peer set is reasonably tight: ONB at 12.7x, SSB at 10.5x, UMBF at 11.8x, WTFC at 13.1x, and CFR at 14.2x. ZION sits at the low end alongside SSB.

The discount is partly justified by structural features rather than pure mispricing. The no-holding-company status reduces M&A optionality and requires OCC approval for buybacks; the AOCI loss is a latent capital risk under Basel III Endgame; and the franchise carries above-average CRE concentration (22% of loans). The October 2025 fraud event also left a residual sentiment discount that has been closing but is not fully gone. A reasonable case exists that the discount should narrow further given the bank's superior ROTCE (15.5%) and rapid TBV growth (+19%), which support a higher multiple. But the gap is moderate, and closing it is incremental rather than the kind of asymmetric rerating that was available in the mid-$40s.

Against analyst targets, the picture argues for caution rather than aggression. The mean target is $69.24 and the median $68.00, both essentially at the current price; the high is $80.00 and the low $63.00. On a stock with a low forward multiple and a structural earnings turn, we would treat consensus targets as a floor that lags reality. Here, however, the multiple is only modestly below peers, growth is mid-single-digit ex-one-times, and the analyst distribution skews neutral (1 strong buy, 7 buys, 13 holds, 1 strong sell, with hold counts having recently increased). The targets look more like a fair anchor than a stale ceiling. We score valuation a 5.5 — neither a value trap nor a compelling discount, with the recovery-driven cheapness largely arbitraged away. Our calibration leans us to treat a "cheap" multiple skeptically absent a fresh catalyst, and the catalyst here (the fraud-scare mispricing) has already been monetized.

## Competitive Landscape

Zions competes as a relationship-driven Western commercial bank against money-center banks (scale and technology advantages), other regionals, and increasingly fintechs and credit unions. The affiliate model is a genuine differentiator in local commercial relationships, and the deposit franchise has strengthened: noninterest-bearing deposits at 35% of total provide a structural funding-cost edge over deposit-gathering competitors. Liquidity at 134% of uninsured deposits and rising CET1 demonstrate that the franchise emerged from the 2023 stress and the 2025 fraud event materially de-risked rather than impaired.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than share-gaining. The 10-K devotes unusual attention to emerging-technology disruption — stablecoins, tokenized deposits, the GENIUS Act, the proposed CLARITY Act, and AI — reflecting management awareness that deposit franchises face structural change over the medium term. The October 2025 episode also illustrated contagion risk: Zions' own fraud disclosure rippled across the entire regional-bank complex, echoing the 2023 SVB dynamics the 10-K explicitly references. The Basis Multifamily acquisition modestly widens the franchise in agency multifamily lending. We score competitive position a 6.0, marking down from the prior 6.5 not because anything deteriorated but because the level is fundamentally stable rather than improving, and the structural constraints (no holding company, regulatory framework) cap the upside trajectory. Financial sector selection has historically delivered negative alpha, which reinforces a demand for company-specific evidence we do not fully see here.

## Risk Assessment

**NDFI / borrower-fraud credit event (now stabilized).** The defining recent risk was the fraud charge-off on revolving lines of credit extended to two related commercial borrowers financing mortgage origination, which drove a 2.7% annualized NDFI net charge-off rate in 2025. The Q1 2026 data shows sharp stabilization: criticized/classified ratios down to 0.7% and ACL/NDFI coverage raised to 1.40% from 1.03%. The event was contained and idiosyncratic.

**CRE concentration.** CRE is $13.7B (22% of loans). Multifamily is the largest slice ($4.1B, 30% of CRE) with 46% maturing within 12 months, a refinancing-cliff risk if rates stay elevated. Office CRE is $1.6B with a 2.3% nonaccrual ratio and 7.8% classified, but improving (down from 4.0%/9.3% at YE2025), with a weighted-average LTV of 57% and ACL coverage of 2.70%.

**Interest-rate / AOCI risk.** The unrealized securities loss sits outside regulatory capital today. The Basel III Endgame proposal forcing AOCI into capital is the key overhang, though the drag is shrinking as rates fall and securities pull to par.

**Geographic concentration** in UT/ID/TX/CA, including Southern California wildfire exposure named in risk factors.

**Litigation tail.** Two civil cases (Lifescan/J&J 2017; Roche 2019) in NJ federal court allege Zions is liable for a bankrupt borrower's fraud, with trial scheduled April 2027. Management believes accruals are adequate but flags potentially indeterminate damages.

The Q1 2026 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K. We score risk factors a 6.0 — the dominant binary (NDFI contagion) has resolved favorably, but the multifamily maturity wall, the AOCI/Basel overhang, and the 2027 litigation trial remain unpriced tails.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 25.3% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 31.0% (a -5.7% spread), and IV percentile is 7% within the current chain; the options market is pricing low near-term uncertainty after the recovery. ATM IV fell from 30.0% to 25.3% over the past month while the stock rose 8.7%, indicating resolved uncertainty rather than building anticipation. There is mild elevated put demand at the front (5.3% put/call skew, P/C volume 0.72), but the P/C open-interest ratio collapsed to 0.14, consistent with bullish/neutral positioning. The flat term structure (1m 25.3% ≈ 6m 26.3%) suggests no near-dated catalyst is being feared. Cheap IV makes this a low-cost environment for protective puts if a holder wants downside insurance, but the broad signal is complacent rather than confirming a directional thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The bull case rests on the discount-to-peers closing on the strength of demonstrably superior returns. If NIM continues to expand as funding costs ease and low-yield securities run off, NII momentum carries EPS toward $6.76+ next year (consensus, rising), and the Q2 Visa gain plus a benign provision support reported earnings. Continued buyback execution ($77M in Q1, a new $225M authorization in May) and the rising $0.45 quarterly dividend reward holders. A rerating to ~12.5x on $6.76 implies roughly $85, matching the high analyst target. The franchise's strong liquidity and improving credit metrics support a re-rating as the 2025 fraud sentiment fully dissipates.

### Bear Case

The bear case is that the recovery is complete and the remaining risks are not priced. The stock is within a percent of its 52-week high and at the consensus mean target, leaving little margin. A higher-for-longer rate path that turns into refinancing stress on the multifamily book could reignite credit concerns. A dovish Fed pivot would compress NIM (-200bps cuts NII 7.2%), removing the core earnings driver. The Basel III Endgame AOCI inclusion could pressure regulatory capital optics. And the 2025 reported EPS strength is partly inflated by one-time items (Visa gain, reserve releases) that normalize away. In a bear scenario, the multiple compresses back toward the SSB-level ~10x on normalized ~$6.30 EPS, implying roughly $58–63, about 8–15% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: there is a credible 12-month appreciation path, but the asymmetry that justified buying in the mid-$40s has largely been realized.

- **Base case target: $74** over 12 months. This assumes ~11.2x on normalized FY26 EPS of roughly $6.60 (ex-one-times). The discount to peers narrows modestly as fraud sentiment fully clears, but the structural constraints (no holding company, AOCI overhang) keep ZION below the peer median multiple. Upside of ~8% from $68.49.
- **Bull case target: $85** (~12.5x on $6.76 next-year EPS), upside of ~24%, contingent on continued NIM expansion, clean credit, and full sentiment normalization.
- **Bear case target: $63** (~10x on normalized ~$6.30 after one-times wash out and a NIM compression or multifamily credit scare), downside of ~8%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q2 2026 earnings (Visa gain confirmation), the multifamily maturity-wall progression over the next four quarters, and clarity on Basel III Endgame AOCI treatment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A meaningful pullback (toward the low-$60s or below) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring the asymmetry that has been arbitraged away.
2. NIM expansion continuing above 3.35% with positive operating leverage sustained, supporting current-year EPS revisions back above +10%.
3. Resolution of the Basel III Endgame AOCI question in a manner neutral-to-favorable for regulatory capital, removing the structural discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A renewed credit event — NDFI nonaccruals or multifamily classified loans rising materially, or charge-offs returning above 0.50% annualized.
2. NIM compression below 3.10% on a dovish rate path, with current-year EPS estimates turning negative on revision.
3. A failed or value-destructive expansion of the Basis acquisition, or evidence the litigation accruals are inadequate ahead of the April 2027 trial.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (to confirm the Visa gain and underlying NIM trajectory), followed by quarterly credit-quality and multifamily-maturity monitoring.

## Conclusion

Zions has executed the recovery the data predicted. The franchise emerged from the fraud scare well-capitalized, profitable, and de-risked, with disciplined, shareholder-aligned management (a modest 2.5x CEO/NEO pay ratio and credit-quality-weighted incentives).

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is price. The stock has recovered from $46 to $68.49, sits near its 52-week high and the consensus mean target, and trades at a moderate ~18% discount to peers that is partly justified by genuine structural features (no holding company, OCC buyback approval, AOCI overhang). The remaining 12-month upside depends on incremental discount-closing, continued NII momentum, and the one-time Q2 Visa gain rather than the kind of asymmetric mispricing available eight months ago. The trajectory of improving scores (6.1 → 6.7) reflects a real fundamental recovery, and our 6.3 here marks a small consolidation as the valuation cushion thins and competitive position settles at stable rather than improving. We would become buyers again on a pullback to the low-$60s or on a fresh NIM-driven earnings turn; we would exit on renewed credit deterioration or NIM compression. For now, hold for the modest growth and the ~2.7% yield.
