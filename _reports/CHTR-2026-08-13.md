## Executive Summary

Charter is cheap for a legible reason, and the reason got worse in the June quarter. Adjusted EBITDA fell 4.3% year over year in Q2 2026 and 3.2% in the first half — the first back-to-back declines after four consecutive years of growth (FY2021 $20,630M through FY2025 $22,708M). Revenue fell 1.7% in the quarter. Internet customers declined 172,000 in Q2 and 520,000 over twelve months. The change that matters most is the price, not the unit loss: in FY2025, Internet revenue rose $405M as +$785M of rate and mix offset −$380M from fewer customers. In Q2 2026, Internet revenue fell $193M with **−$89M coming from rate**. Residential revenue per relationship dropped to $117.52 from $119.70. Subscriber attrition with pricing power is a manageable annuity in run-off; attrition plus negative rate compounds.

The valuation is extreme. Total economic common equity is roughly 135.3M units (119.3M Class A outstanding plus ~16M A/N Charter Holdings units) — about $20.8B at $153.40, versus the $18.3B "market cap" that captures Class A only. Adding $93.8B of debt principal, the $1.6B EIP facility and the $1.2B deferred payment program, less $0.5B of cash, gives an enterprise value near $116.9B against LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $22.34B: **5.2x**. LTM free cash flow of $4,735M is a **22.8% yield on economic equity**, or roughly $35 per unit against a $153.40 price. Forward P/E is 3.7x current-year consensus ($41.92) versus a cable peer median near 8.2x.

We are not paid for that discount at this price. Charter's equity is about 18% of enterprise value; at 4.18x leverage, the entire investment case reduces to whether EBITDA erosion runs slower than debt reduction. Management has removed the mechanism that historically bridged the gap: the Q2 10-Q replaced the 10-K's language about *increasing* indebtedness to hold 3.5–3.75x leverage with an explicit intent to *decrease* it toward 3.5x, and June open-market program repurchases collapsed to 31,315 shares (the contractually mandated Liberty purchase) from 2.37M in April at $228.65. Layered on top is a pending $34.5B Cox transaction requiring ~$4.2B of cash at closing, assumption of ~$12.4B of net debt, roughly 20% common dilution, and a $6.0B preferred with a 6.875% coupon whose $477.41 conversion price sits 3.1x above the current share price. The preferred represents $412.5M per year of senior claim, equal to 8.7% of LTM FCF, that appears in neither the debt principal nor the 4.18x leverage figure. This is our fifth consecutive negative rating and the first upgrade within that band: the price is 46% below the 52-week high, insiders bought in size, and liability management at 80 cents on the dollar is real value creation. That earns REDUCE rather than AVOID. It does not earn ownership.

## Company Overview

Charter markets broadband, video, mobile and voice under the Spectrum brand across nearly 59 million passings in 41 states, managed as a single reportable segment because the converged network cannot be meaningfully disaggregated. FY2025 revenue of $54,774M split: Internet $23,765M (43%), Video $13,703M (25%), Small business $4,346M, Mobile service $3,762M, Other $3,411M, Mid-market/large business $2,969M, Advertising $1,468M, Voice $1,350M. Roughly 89% is recurring monthly subscription revenue, cancellable at will.

Two structural features frame the analysis. First, mobile — the growth engine, +1.68M lines to 12.54M and +18.9% service revenue in Q2 — is delivered through virtual network operator rights leased from a third party, supplemented by directly held CBRS Priority Access Licenses that the 10-K notes are "subject to revocation and expiration." Charter rents the macro network from the same category of competitor that is attacking its broadband base with fixed wireless. Second, $97.2B of the $155.6B balance sheet is indefinite-lived franchises ($67,471M) and goodwill ($29,710M), against Charter shareholders' equity of $16,952M — the residue of $80.6B of buybacks since 2016.

## Financial Analysis

**The inflection is unambiguous.** Revenue: FY2023 +1.1%, FY2024 +0.9%, FY2025 −0.6%, H1 2026 −1.4%, Q2 −1.7%. Adjusted EBITDA: FY2024 +3.1%, FY2025 +0.6%, H1 2026 −3.2%, Q2 −4.3%. Income from operations fell to $3,063M in Q2 from $3,279M.

Two adjustments are needed before drawing conclusions. Of the $339M Q2 video revenue decline, **$184M is the "seamless entertainment allocation"** — programmer streaming-app costs reclassified out of programming expense and into a revenue offset, with a matching $184M of the $218M programming cost decline. Only $31M came from fewer video customers. The reported 1.7% revenue decline overstates top-line deterioration and the 9.7% programming cost decline overstates cost relief. Separately, Cox transition expenses were $65M in Q2 and $89M in H1 (nil in 2025), plus $34M/$37M capitalized. Adding back the expensed portion leaves roughly $281M of the $370M H1 EBITDA decline as operating.

**Cash flow.** FY2025: operating cash flow $16,077M, capex $11,659M, FCF $5,004M (up $747M). The improvement was mostly non-operating in origin — cash taxes −$669M helped by the July 2025 restoration of 100% bonus depreciation, cash interest −$347M, mobile-device working capital +$398M, versus only +$139M from EBITDA. H1 2026: OCF $8,229M, capex $5,726M, FCF $2,341M versus $2,610M. Derived LTM FCF: $5,004M − $2,610M + $2,341M = **$4,735M**. We do not annualize the $969M Q2 figure; Charter's FCF is back-half weighted and accrued capex swung $209M against the period. Treat ~$3.9B as a downside sensitivity and $4.7B as the observed base; both are flattered by $669M (FY2025) and $536M (H1 2026) of cash-tax reductions that normalize.

Capex mix has turned defensive. H1 upgrade/rebuild spend rose 56% to $1,332M while total line extensions fell to $1,542M from $1,906M and subsidized rural extensions to $816M from $1,010M. Line extensions add addressable homes; upgrades defend the ones Charter already has.

**Leverage and the cost of debt.** Debt principal $93,845M at 6/30/2026 ($12,453M credit facilities, $54,658M investment-grade secured notes, $26,734M high-yield unsecured), plus a $1,596M EIP financing facility at 4.82%, a $1.2B deferred payment program in short-term borrowings (up from $918M at year-end — a financing inflow the 10-Q cites as supporting H1 cash flows), and $725M of supply-chain-finance vendor obligations. Cash $509M; revolver availability $3.7B, down from $4.4B. Company-stated net debt/LTM Adjusted EBITDA 4.18x, up from 4.15x. Interest coverage is adequate: H1 income from operations $6,271M / net interest $2,532M = 2.48x; on EBITDA, 4.38x. CCO Holdings met its indenture leverage test with no default.

Screen outputs (Altman Z of 0.78, current ratio 0.39, D/E 9.61) misread this capital structure. Negative working capital is structural for a subscription business billing monthly in advance with no inventory, and near-zero book equity reflects $80.6B of cumulative buybacks, not asset impairment. The credible risk is not insolvency. It is that **the incremental cost of debt is now rising faster than EBITDA**. September 2025 issuance came at 5.850% and 6.700%; January 2026 at **7.000% and 7.375%**, used to retire 5.500% 2026s and part of the 5.125% 2027s — roughly 175–225bp of incremental coupon on ~$3.0B, about $60M of permanent annual interest from one transaction. Net interest expense fell $187M in FY2025 but **rose $28M in H1 2026**. Only ~13% of borrowings were floating, so repricing arrives tranche by tranche: $3.25B of 5.125% 2027s (partially called), ~$5.5B of 2028s, $1.25B of 2.250% 2029s.

**The offset is real.** In Q2 Charter retired $1.2B of principal in the open market for roughly $957M, booking a $243M gain — an effective ~25% return on capital deployed, and better value than equity at those prices. The debt stack carried a fair value of $83,550M against $93,845M principal, roughly 89 cents; the secured notes trade below the unsecured, which is duration (3.700% 2051s, 3.850% 2061s) rather than credit inversion. That discount is an asset the equity can monetize.

**Earnings quality caveat.** Q2 diluted EPS of $10.66 includes the extinguishment gain: $243M / 121.3M diluted shares = $2.00 pretax, roughly **$1.35 attributable per share** after ~24% tax and ~11% NCI — about 13% of reported EPS, recorded in other income and excluded from Adjusted EBITDA. Income from operations fell 6.5% in the same quarter. The reported 3.0% EPS beat against a $10.35 estimate should not be read as an operating beat without confirming the consensus basis, which we could not verify. The four-quarter surprise record is 2 beats (+3.0%, +3.6%) and 2 large misses (−11.8%, −12.4%): visibility is poor.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis. Total customer relationships fell 1.7% to 31,499K; Internet customers −520K; voice −692K; video −107K. Mobile added 1,684K lines, but the accompanying disclosure is telling: "other costs of revenue" rose $186M in Q2 and $367M in H1 on mobile device sales and direct costs, and the FY2025 sentence that "Adjusted EBITDA grew slightly with mobile revenues growing at a faster rate than mobile direct costs" does not appear in the Q2 10-Q. Mobile at 12.54M lines against 29.39M Internet customers is a 43% attach ratio with runway, but it is functioning as bundle-discounted retention rather than an independent profit pool.

Video is being managed competently toward irrelevance. Residential video losses narrowed to 77,000 year over year and 21,000 in the quarter, against 255,000 for full-year 2025 — the app-bundling strategy demonstrably stabilizes units. It does so by transferring value to programmers through the seamless-entertainment allocation. Video is now a churn-reduction cost centre.

Estimate revisions are mixed in a specific and unhelpful way: current-quarter consensus is down 3.0% over 30 days and 3.7% over 90 days, next quarter down 2.8%/3.9%, yet current-year ($41.92) and next-year ($45.26) are essentially flat. That combination means the annual figures now depend on a materially stronger H2. The reported guidance — standalone 2026 EBITDA declining "around 1%" excluding transition costs, attributed to CFO Fischer through secondary reporting we could not verify against an issuer document — carries the same arithmetic tension: H1 was −3.2%, or roughly −2.5% adding back transition expense. Reaching −1% for the year requires H2 comparisons to improve by several hundred basis points. If they do not, the flat annual estimates fall.

Cox adds scale, not growth: programming leverage, MVNO negotiating scale, fixed-cost absorption and geographic clustering in Southern California, Las Vegas and Atlanta. Scale has not historically solved cable's problem, which is that a wireless incumbent can serve the marginal broadband household at near-zero incremental capex per subscriber.

## Valuation Assessment

Static metrics are extreme. EV/LTM EBITDA 5.2x; forward P/E 3.7x on $41.92 and 3.4x on $45.26; FCF yield 22.8% on economic equity; peer median P/E 8.2x (CMCSA 8.2x, VSNT 7.2x). Comcast trades at a premium on lower leverage and better subscriber trends.

The problem is what that discount buys. Equity is $20.8B of a $116.9B enterprise — 17.8%. The multiple, not the cash flow, sets the outcome:

| Post-Cox scenario | Combined EBITDA | Multiple | Implied EV | Less net debt ~$110B and $6B preferred | Per unit (168.9M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $25.5B | 4.75x | $121B | $5B | ~$30 |
| Conservative | $26.0B | 5.0x | $130B | $14B | ~$83 |
| Base | $26.8B | 5.4x | $145B | $29B | ~$170 |
| Bull | $27.0B | 6.0x | $162B | $46B | ~$272 |

(Combined EBITDA assumes standalone Charter near $22.0–22.3B plus a Cox contribution we cannot verify — Cox Communications standalone financials were not obtained, so the acquisition multiple and accretion are unknown. Treat the table as sensitivity, not forecast.)

At $153.40 the market is applying roughly 5.3x, consistent with the standalone reading, and the range above shows why that is not comfort. A half-turn of multiple is roughly $13B of EV and $77 per unit. This is the arithmetic that makes a 22.8% FCF yield a fair price rather than a bargain.

Three specific reasons not to treat cheapness as the signal here. First, the per-share compounding engine is switched off: weighted average shares fell from 143.1M (FY2024) to 120.1M (Q2 2026), a 16% reduction in eighteen months, and remaining ex-Liberty authorization is $365M against $2.2B of H1 spend. Forward per-unit FCF growth must now come from EBITDA growth (negative) and debt reduction. Second, LTM FCF is not a run rate — it absorbed $1.2B of cash-tax benefit across FY2025 and H1 2026, and post-close it must also absorb the preferred dividend plus interest on ~$4.2B of new borrowing and assumed Cox debt. Third, the analyst distribution is wide and unhelpful: mean $184.41, **median $150.00**, low $101, high $380, with a 1/4/11/3/2 rating split. A $150 median against a $153.40 price is not a floor to lean on.

The genuine mispricing candidate is liability management. Retiring $1.2B at ~80 cents for a $243M gain, and the August 6 pricing of $4.75B of secured notes alongside upsized exchange offers (contents of the 12 August 8-K carrying Items 1.01 and 2.03 unread; use of proceeds unconfirmed), suggests Charter can convert bond-market dislocation into equity value at scale. That is a real channel worth 1–2 points of valuation credit. It is not enough to overcome an 18%-of-EV equity claim on a shrinking EBITDA base at 5.3x.

## Competitive Landscape

Charter's own 10-K names the attackers in order: third-party FTTH, "cell phone home Internet service" (fixed wireless), satellite Internet, and DSL, with competitors continuing "to expand 5G mobile services as they seek to offer converged connectivity services similar to ours" using "intensive marketing efforts with aggressive pricing." Convergence — Charter's stated advantage — is being replicated from the wireless side by operators that own the macro network Charter must lease.

The evidence says the counter-attack is working on volume and failing on value. Connectivity rose from 48.3% of FY2025 revenue to 50.8% in Q2, but that is mix shift within a shrinking household base, not diversification: relationships −1.7%, Internet −520K. Household penetration of a fixed footprint is the concentration measure that matters, and it is deteriorating. Charter's own proxy quantifies the relative damage: $100 invested returns $32 for Charter versus $127 for the peer group over five years.

The SpaceX thread requires care. Bloomberg and Reuters reported executive-level talks on 26 June 2026 about a US consumer mobile offering — talks only, no agreement, no mention in the 10-Q filed four weeks later. If a deal formed, Charter would be a distributor of SpaceX-supplied wireless capacity, analogous to its existing third-party MVNO arrangement, not an owner of Starlink assets. The same capability set is named in Charter's 10-K as a competitive threat to its broadband product. This is not an asset to underwrite.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment monitoring is the most important new disclosure.** The Q2 10-Q adds language absent from the 10-K: "Considering the decline in Charter's stock price during the quarter ending June 30, 2026, we continue to monitor factors that could impact the fair value of our franchises and goodwill... if the lower stock price persists, we may need to evaluate whether this market perception represents a sustained decline in the market value of our business and perform a quantitative impairment assessment." The October 31, 2025 test found franchise fair value exceeded carrying value by "more than 10%" — measured before a further ~40% share-price decline, and Charter had moved that test date forward from November 30 and shifted from qualitative to quantitative testing. A 10% franchise write-down is ~$6.7B, roughly 40% of Charter shareholders' equity. It would be non-cash and would not touch Adjusted EBITDA, FCF, covenants or cash taxes. It would function as management's own concession on terminal value. Management states directly it does not view the decline as fundamental, and controlling-interest fair value legitimately exceeds a float price — we do not model an impairment as expected, but the cushion is thin.

**Cox execution.** ~$4.2B of cash at closing funded with debt; ~$12.4B of assumed net debt; a named contingent trigger under certain Cox supplemental indentures requiring a repurchase offer at 101% of principal if all three of S&P, Moody's and Fitch downgrade below investment grade within 60 days of the change of control, with financing "which may not be available on terms acceptable to us, or at all." The FCC approved in February 2026 and Connecticut PURA in March; a California ALJ recommended CPUC approval with conditions and the CPUC vote was scheduled for the week of 10 August with parties hoping to combine "this month." We could not confirm that the vote occurred or that either the Cox or Liberty Broadband transactions have closed; every post-close figure here is conditional.

**Refinancing.** Structurally unavoidable at 175–225bp of incremental coupon per rolled tranche against $93.8B, partially offset by discounted repurchases and by secured paper pricing tighter than the CCOH unsecured prints.

**Governance and related-party mechanics.** Liberty Broadband held ~29.22% of voting stock and A/N ~13.12%; both designate directors and hold contractual preemptive rights Class A holders lack. Under the November 2024 amendment, Charter must repurchase from Liberty monthly the greater of $100M or Liberty's minimum liquidity threshold — and must *lend* the amount if a repurchase would drop Liberty below 25.25% — with Liberty applying proceeds to its own debt. H1 2026: $595M of purchases plus a **~$359M term loan advanced in May**, a receivable from an entity whose principal asset is Charter stock. The buyback component reduces share count at market-referenced prices; the loan does not. Separately, A/N unilaterally suspended its pro-rata repurchase agreement on 4 August 2025, so Charter bought zero A/N units in H1 2026 versus $232M in Q2 2025. We decline to infer a valuation view from A/N's election.

**Short interest.** 50.4% of float, 22.46M shares short, 7.2 days to cover. Against ~119.3M Class A outstanding of which Liberty holds 38.6M, tradeable float is materially smaller than the outstanding count, which explains both the reported ratio and the violence of the rebound from the 24 July low of $111.55. This makes the name unshortable at any reasonable size and is a genuine reason not to convert a negative view into a short.

**Insider activity, on the other side.** Four open-market purchases (code P) in three weeks: CEO Winfrey ~$1.19M at $172.23 on 28 April, director Wade Davis $995K at $173.72, director Balan Nair $175K at $175.46, and director Mauricio Ramos $1.40M at $140.93 on 15 May — roughly $3.8M, after two bad quarters and before the Q2 print. That is a credible cluster and the strongest single bullish datapoint in the file. It has not yet proven prescient: the stock traded to $123.31 on 24 July. Two Form 4 price fields (Rutledge's April withholding prices near $241.53; Ramos's $140.93 against Charter's own $195.62 May average repurchase price) are inconsistent with contemporaneous market evidence and are treated as feed artifacts; the direction of the activity is well supported, the precise prices are not.

### Options Market Signal

The tape has de-stressed materially. ATM IV collapsed from 179.2% (21 July) to 71.2% while the price rose 18%, and put/call open interest fell from 1.21 to 0.92. The headline 71.2% near-term reading is a one-day expiry and should be discounted as same-day mechanics; the informative tenors are 1m at 50.5% and 3m at 49.5% against 50.6% realized — vol is in line, not rich, which is unusual into a pending closing and argues that near-term event risk is being priced as low. The 6m tenor is the exception: 56.5% IV, a −6.1% skew reading, and a put/call volume ratio of 2.00, the highest across the term structure. Positioning for downside is concentrated beyond the merger close rather than around it, consistent with a terminal-value debate rather than an event trade. The call-heavy near-term skew and 0.92 OI ratio also mean the options market is not corroborating the bearish fundamental read at short horizons — a divergence we note rather than resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($272, +77%).** Broadband unit losses moderate as fixed-wireless operators exhaust spare capacity, and Internet rate returns to positive as promotional intensity normalises. Cox closes on schedule, delivers programming and MVNO leverage, and the combined entity generates $5.5–6.0B of FCF after the preferred dividend. Charter continues retiring debt at 80–90 cents, converting the $10.3B gap between principal and fair value into equity value, and leverage falls toward 3.5x. The multiple recovers to 6.0x — still a discount to Comcast — and the equity roughly doubles because it is a levered residual. Video stabilisation, the 43% mobile attach ratio, and the April–May insider cluster support this reading.

**Bear case ($70, −54%).** Negative Internet rate proves structural rather than promotional. EBITDA declines 4–6% annually; leverage ratchets upward mechanically even with flat debt because the denominator shrinks. Refinancing at 7%+ removes $150–250M of FCF per year. Cox integration consumes management attention and costs more than the H1 run-rate implies, while the preferred takes $412.5M off the top ahead of common. A quantitative impairment test writes down franchises and eliminates book equity, hardening the terminal-value narrative. The multiple compresses to 4.75x and the equity is nearly wiped out at that leverage. Five consecutive years of TSR underperformance says the market has been running this scenario for some time.

**Base case ($170, +11%).** EBITDA roughly flat to −2% as mobile and commercial offset broadband erosion and transition costs roll off; Cox closes and adds scale without meaningful synergy surprise; $3–4B of annual debt reduction roughly offsets EBITDA decline, holding leverage near 4.0x; the multiple stays at 5.2–5.4x. Probability-weighting 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull gives an expected value near $158, about 3% above the current price, with a −54% tail. That is unattractive asymmetry at this entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Upgraded from four consecutive AVOIDs. The change reflects three specific developments, not the price move: discounted debt repurchases as a demonstrated value-creation channel ($243M gain on $1.2B retired), video-unit stabilisation, and a credible insider buying cluster. The rating remains negative because expected return is roughly flat against a 54% downside tail and the per-share compounding mechanism has been formally retired.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into the current strength rather than hold through the Cox close. The stock has recovered 37% from $111.55 and sits 46% below the 52-week high of $285.82 — a level at which residual exposure can be reduced without capitulating at the low. Retain no more than a token position pending post-close disclosure of combined leverage, pro-forma FCF net of the preferred dividend, and the first standalone-plus-Cox subscriber report. New capital should not be committed here.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).** (1) Internet revenue rate contribution returns to positive — reversal of the −$89M Q2 rate line — for two consecutive quarters; (2) Adjusted EBITDA growth back to flat or better on a like-for-like basis excluding transition costs, confirming the reported "around −1%" 2026 guide rather than the H1 −2.5% ex-transition trend; (3) net leverage declining sequentially below 4.0x post-close with the preferred treated as debt; (4) a price near $110–120, which would restore roughly 40%+ upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (back to AVOID).** (1) A quantitative franchise or goodwill impairment charge, which would confirm a lower terminal value; (2) the Cox change-of-control repurchase provision being triggered by three-agency downgrades, requiring incremental financing on unfavourable terms; (3) Q3 Internet losses exceeding the 172,000 Q2 pace alongside a further ARPU decline below $117; (4) 2026 or 2027 consensus EPS falling more than 10% from the current $41.92/$45.26.

**Reassessment timeline.** Immediate on confirmation of the CPUC vote and the Cox and Liberty Broadband closings, then at the Q3 2026 print (late October), which is the first report to test both the H2-loaded guidance and the combined operating base.

## Conclusion

Charter is priced at 5.2x EBITDA and a 22.8% free-cash-flow yield because its EBITDA has begun to shrink, its broadband pricing power has disappeared, and management has replaced the leverage-and-repurchase model that produced a 16% share-count reduction in eighteen months with a debt-reduction target. Each of those is documented in the filings rather than inferred. The offsetting evidence is meaningful — $243M of gains on retiring debt at roughly 80 cents, video losses down from 255,000 annually to 21,000 in the quarter, mobile at 12.54M lines with a 43% attach ratio, a CEO and three directors buying in the open market, and an options market that has stopped pricing near-term stress. None of it changes the arithmetic that equity is 18% of a $116.9B enterprise, so that half a turn of multiple is worth about $77 per unit. Base-case upside of roughly 11% against a bear case 54% lower, from a price that has already recovered 37% off the July low and sits at the $150 analyst median, does not justify capital. Reduce exposure; revisit at $110–120 or on two consecutive quarters of positive Internet rate.
