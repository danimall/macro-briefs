## Executive Summary

Biogen is midway through a substitution: a legacy multiple sclerosis franchise whose patent and regulatory protection has now fully collapsed is being replaced by acquired rare-disease and complement-biology products, a contractual anti-CD20 royalty stream, and a partnered Alzheimer's asset. The July 29, 2026 second-quarter report is the first evidence that the substitution is arithmetically working. Revenue grew 3.4% to $2,736.0M, non-GAAP EPS of $3.60 beat consensus by 19.8% (the fourth consecutive beat of 19%+), management raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to $12–$13, and the full-year revenue guide flipped from an expected decline to growth. For the first time, growth-portfolio revenue exceeded legacy MS revenue.

The GAAP picture looks like the opposite story and should not be read as one. GAAP diluted EPS fell to $0.66 from $4.33 on higher revenue. Reconciling the roughly $612M pretax swing from the 10-Q: $165.4M of restructuring (of which $153.2M is Apellis integration, driven by double-trigger acceleration of converted equity awards), $164.0M of acquired IPR&D and milestone expense (TJ Bio $100M, Ionis $45M plus $15M), $69.7M of Apellis acquired-inventory step-up amortization, and a $37.3M increase in intangible amortization. Stripping step-up charges, Q2 2026 product cost of sales was $417.0M against $415.0M a year earlier — essentially flat while carrying two new commercial products. The reported 5.5-point gross-margin decline is non-cash purchase accounting that runs off by 2028 (Reata) and 2029 (Apellis).

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is that the two largest, highest-margin revenue lines carry step-downs Biogen does not control. The Genentech agreement specifies a **50% reduction to OCREVUS U.S. royalties upon first FDA-approved biosimilar entry** — OCREVUS royalties were $1,414.9M in FY2025 and $381.4M in Q2 2026 alone, earned at essentially zero operating cost. Separately, Hatch-Waxman suits filed in May and June 2026 against Somerset/Odin and Cipla confirm at least two ANDA filers targeting SPINRAZA ($1,546.8M in FY2025). Net debt rose from $2,039.2M to $6,805.2M in a single quarter to fund the $5.41B Apellis acquisition, funded partly by liquidating the entire marketable securities portfolio. At 16.5x the guidance midpoint and 12.4x next-year consensus of $16.59, the multiple is undemanding — and reasonably so given those defined cliffs. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately +6% over twelve months against a 22% downside to a derating scenario.

## Company Overview

Biogen reports as a single operating segment but its revenue is structurally four-legged, and aggregated screens routinely misread it:

1. **Product revenue, net** — $7,119.4M in FY2025 (72% of total). MS (TYSABRI, TECFIDERA, VUMERITY, AVONEX, PLEGRIDY), rare neurology (SPINRAZA, SKYCLARYS, QALSODY), European biosimilars, and since May 14, 2026 SYFOVRE and EMPAVELI.
2. **Anti-CD20 program revenue** — $1,860.6M FY2025, +6.3%. This is a tiered OCREVUS royalty (13.5% rising to 24.0% on U.S. net sales above $900M; 3.0% gross ex-U.S.) plus a U.S. co-promotion profit share on RITUXAN, GAZYVA and LUNSUMIO. Genentech owns development, manufacturing and commercialization. Biogen bears essentially no operating cost against this line.
3. **Alzheimer's collaboration revenue** — $177.7M FY2025 (from $59.9M). Biogen's 50% share of LEQEMBI revenue net of cost of sales, recognized net because Eisai is the principal and holds final decision-making authority.
4. **Contract manufacturing, royalty and other** — $732.9M FY2025, including manufacture of LEQEMBI batches for the collaboration.

The structural problem is that leg 1's legacy component and leg 2 are both under pressure while legs 3 and 4 are partner-controlled. TECFIDERA fell 29.7% in FY2025 and a further 53% year-over-year in Q2 2026 to $90.9M after the European General Court annulled its regulatory protection extension (September 2025) and the EPO revoked patent EP 2 653 873 (November 2025), following which Biogen stopped enforcing it.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of growth matters more than the headline. Q2 2026 revenue +3.4%; MS product revenue $963.3M, **down 13.0%**, with TECFIDERA −53% and PLEGRIDY −20%. Rest-of-world product revenue fell 10.2% in H1. Management states in the 10-Q that it expects TECFIDERA to continue declining and TYSABRI to remain adversely affected by biosimilar competition. MS at roughly $3.84B annualized is still ~37% of revenue, so the drag persists for several years.

Offsetting that: SYFOVRE contributed $97.4M and EMPAVELI $30.4M in roughly six-and-a-half weeks, and management guides both to mid-to-high-teens growth through at least 2028. Biogen now holds exclusive worldwide rights to felzartamab after the $100M TJ Bio payment for greater-China rights (Phase 3 in antibody-mediated rejection, IgA nephropathy and primary membranous nephropathy) — its most advanced immunology asset. LEQEMBI's commercial constraint has been infusion-center capacity, and the FDA's approval of an at-home subcutaneous starting dose (Reuters, July 13, 2026), ahead of an August 24 PDUFA, removes part of it; Eisai forecasts ~$900M of fiscal-2026 LEQEMBI sales.

SPINRAZA deserves care. U.S. Q2 revenue jumped to $204.3M from $149.3M, but H1 total SPINRAZA was **down 5%** ($775.9M versus $816.6M) as rest-of-world fell $45.8M. The U.S. spike is at least partly geographic mix, channel timing, or stocking of the high-dose presentation approved in January 2026, not clean franchise expansion. One quarter does not establish that the lifecycle defense (high-dose plus the Alcyone ThecaFlex DRx intrathecal port) is converting the prescriber base ahead of generic entry.

**On the estimate revision signal.** The feed shows current-quarter consensus cut 46.6% over 30 days ($3.93 → $2.10) and current-year cut 6.9% ($13.37 → $12.45), while next-year is roughly flat (+0.5% to $16.59). Two observations. First, the current-year "cut" is consensus converging *down to* management's raised range — $12.45 sits inside the $12–$13 guidance, whereas the $13.37 estimate 30 days ago sat above the top end. That is a basis correction, not evidence of deterioration. Second, the Q3 cut of $1.83 per share is closely approximated by the RayThera $225M upfront expected to close in Q3, which as an asset acquisition expenses to acquired IPR&D — $225M / 148.7M shares ≈ $1.51 pretax. That is an inference; an alternative explanation is that some contributors model GAAP while others model non-GAAP, producing a mixed-basis consensus. Either way, the explicitly identified strong-buy revision signal (>+30% over 90 days combined with >30% revenue growth) is **not present here**, and I am not treating rising estimates as a re-rating catalyst.

The pipeline gave one clear failure and one ambiguous result in 2026. BIIB122 (LRRK2, Denali collaboration) missed both primary and secondary endpoints in Phase 2b LUMA in May 2026 and was discontinued in idiopathic Parkinson's; Biogen's Denali stake is carried at $184.0M against $227.7M cost. Diranersen (tau ASO, Ionis-partnered) missed its Phase 2 CELIA primary endpoint per Reuters, while showing benefit on five of six cognitive instruments and significant tau reduction at the **lowest** dose, with greater tau reduction but weaker clinical signal at higher doses. An inverse dose-response is a genuine Phase 3 design problem. Management is advancing to Phase 3 while framing it as high-risk/high-reward with a potential payout around 2030 and explicitly "not betting the store" — the capital restraint is the most honest available signal on probability of success. A widely circulated "26% slowing of cognitive decline" figure appears in a single secondary outlet and is not established.

Neither is likely to bite within twelve months (biologic biosimilar timelines and 30-month ANDA stays both push resolution toward 2028–2030), but both compress the terminal multiple an investor should pay today. At 16.6x current-year the stock is not at a premium multiple, so targets are not simply mirroring the price. But the mechanism by which targets become floors — rising estimates — is absent on the current year, which is falling. I treat the $235 mean as corroborating rather than establishing upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The legacy moat is gone, not eroding. TECFIDERA lost both its EU regulatory protection extension and EP '873; a TYSABRI biosimilar has been approved in the U.S. and E.U. since 2023. What remains durable is narrower: rare-neurology infrastructure and specialist relationships behind SPINRAZA and SKYCLARYS; the Genentech anti-CD20 stream, which is contractual and cost-free until the biosimilar clause triggers; and genuine differentiation in intrathecal and CNS antisense delivery, deepened by Alcyone (ThecaFlex DRx) and the Alloy ASO platform license.

The newly acquired complement franchise enters contested markets. SYFOVRE competes with Astellas's IZERVAY in geographic atrophy, where injection burden and reported intraocular inflammation events have constrained category uptake. EMPAVELI's PNH indication faces Alexion/AstraZeneca's SOLIRIS/ULTOMIRIS plus newer oral entrants — which likely explains why Biogen assigned more purchase price to EMPAVELI ($2.4B) than to SYFOVRE ($1.9B), weighting the newer C3G and IC-MPGN kidney indications. That is a reading of the allocation, not a disclosed assumption.

Two structural constraints deserve emphasis. First, the inherited Sobi arrangement: under a July 2025 buy-down, Sobi's royalty obligations were cut by 90% subject to a cap, leaving Biogen with **low-single-digit royalties** on ex-U.S. ASPAVELI sales, with high-teens-to-high-twenties rates reinstating only above the cap. Biogen recognized $13.3M from Sobi in Q2 2026, so the $380M royalty-rights intangible is thin near-term and optionality-heavy. Sobi is simultaneously licensee, royalty payer and supply customer. Second, Eisai holds final decision-making authority across the LEQEMBI collaboration and leads global development and regulatory submissions; Biogen's remedy for a European commercialization plan it considers inequitable is an ICC arbitration filed June 2025 with a hearing set for **May 2027**. Biogen's separate role as drug substance supplier through 2031 is why LEQEMBI batches appear in contract manufacturing revenue.

LEQEMBI collaboration economics remain negative for Biogen: Q2 2026 collaboration revenue $63.7M less $30.8M development share less $77.6M sales-and-marketing share = **−$44.7M**; H1 −$104.5M, improved from −$140.4M. That excludes unquantified contract manufacturing revenue on LEQEMBI batches, so total LEQEMBI economics are better than the collaboration P&L alone.

Policy pressure is broad. The FY2025 10-K quantified the IRA Part D redesign at roughly $90M net unfavorable to 2025 revenue, concentrated in SKYCLARYS and MS. Gross-to-net reserves reached 33.0% of gross product revenue in FY2025, from 32.6% and 32.0% in the prior two years. OBBBA Medicaid changes (2027–2028) and CMS most-favored-nation initiatives add exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Defined revenue step-downs (highest magnitude).** The OCREVUS U.S. royalty halves automatically on first FDA-approved biosimilar entry — the single largest identifiable step-down in the revenue base, entirely outside Biogen's control. The RITUXAN/LUNSUMIO profit share drops from 35% to 30% once LUNSUMIO U.S. gross sales reach $350M in a year. SPINRAZA faces at least two confirmed ANDA filers.

**Tariffs — live and unquantified.** An April 2026 Proclamation imposes a 100% tariff on imports of patented pharmaceuticals, biologics and associated ingredients, with categories effective July 31 and September 29, 2026. Biogen discloses that EMPAVELI, LEQEMBI, TECFIDERA and VUMERITY are manufactured primarily outside the U.S., while roughly three-quarters of 2025 U.S. product revenue came from largely U.S.-made products. Management says it does not expect currently applicable tariffs to be materially adverse **in 2026** — a scoped statement that says nothing about 2027. EMPAVELI, carrying $2.4B of freshly recognized intangible value, is on the ex-U.S. list. Section 301 tariffs of 10.0%–12.5% took effect July 24, 2026; no receivable has been recorded for potential IEEPA refunds following the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling.

**Litigation and tax density.** Genentech won a ~$124.3M judgment over TYSABRI royalties (November 2025, on appeal). A German tax authority issued assessments of ~€209.8M including accruing interest. Federal antitrust and RICO litigation over PBM contracting for TECFIDERA and VUMERITY, including suits by Walgreen and Kroger. Fresenius Kabi holds a German injunction on IMRALDI with a €19.1M French damages claim pending and a validity hearing set for February 2027. Most unusually, having lost both the TECFIDERA EU protection extension and EP '873, Biogen now faces **reverse damages claims from generics for injunctions it obtained before revocation** (Viatris, Glenmark, Sandoz, Neuraxpharm, Polpharma across the Netherlands, Denmark, Finland and Czechia) — a liability tail outliving a product now generating ~$91M per quarter, with no disclosed accrual. Four securities class actions (one preliminarily settled, final approval hearing September 29, 2026), five stayed derivative actions, a Humana suit over copay-foundation contributions, Italian Competition Authority investigations into BYOOVIZ and the STRATIFY JCV assay, and a Louisiana 340B civil investigative demand. One positive: DG-COMP closed its TECFIDERA request in July 2026. A Chief Legal Officer transition (Michael J. Parini succeeding Susan Alexander, reported July 20, 2026) is more consequential at Biogen than at a typical peer given this docket.

**Balance sheet flexibility.** Net debt more than tripled, all marketable securities are gone, the current ratio fell to 1.87x, and up to ~$5.3B of contingent milestone obligations plus the 2026 additions sit off balance sheet. There is no buyback lever being pulled despite $2.05B of authorization.

**Customer concentration, stated precisely.** Three wholesalers were 29.9%, 16.5% and 10.9% of Q2 2026 gross product revenue (57.3% combined) versus two above the 10% threshold in Q2 2025 at 28.2% and 15.8% (44.0%). Roughly ten points of the apparent jump is a third distributor crossing the disclosure threshold; like-for-like, #1 moved 28.2% → 29.9% and #2 15.8% → 16.5%. Concentration is modestly higher, not dramatically higher, and not improving. Revenue *mix* diversified while *channel* dependence did not — different measures.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($275, +33.3%)

Non-GAAP earnings power is being systematically underestimated. Four consecutive beats averaging 22%, a guidance raise to $12–$13, and a revenue guide flipped to growth establish that the growth portfolio can outrun MS erosion sooner than consensus modeled. Underlying product cost of sales was flat year-over-year while absorbing two new commercial products, so operating leverage on SYFOVRE/EMPAVELI growth of mid-to-high teens through 2028 flows through at high incremental margin. The Apellis deal was underwritten on marketed-product DCFs at a 13.25% hurdle with no synergy premium and no equity issued, and the CVRs are recorded at $4.1M against a ~$527M maximum — a favorable asymmetry either way. The anti-CD20 royalty of ~$1.86B annually carries essentially no operating cost and no biosimilar has been approved. LEQEMBI's at-home subcutaneous starting dose removes the infusion-capacity constraint on the single largest addressable indication in neurology, and the collaboration drag is narrowing (−$104.5M in H1 2026 from −$140.4M). FCF of ~$2.05B against a $30.48B market cap is a 6.7% yield that will deleverage the balance sheet at roughly $2B per year if BD spending moderates. At 12.4x next-year consensus versus a 29x peer median, modest multiple recovery to 16.5x on delivered numbers produces $275.

### Bear Case ($160, −22.4%; structurally $130–$150)

## Conclusion

The four-quarter sequence of declining scores in this name (6.0 → 5.5 → 5.1 → 4.8) reflected a real deterioration: patent losses on TECFIDERA, biosimilar pressure on TYSABRI, a Phase 2b failure, and a leveraging acquisition into contested markets. That justifies arresting the decline and marking the overall score up to 5.15, with valuation and growth improving while competitive position, management alignment and the risk profile remain weak.

It does not justify an upgrade in recommendation. At $206.28 the stock trades 16.5x the guidance midpoint and 12.4x next-year consensus — cheap against a 29x peer median, but the discount is doing legitimate work. Roughly $2.9B of annualized, high-margin revenue depends on when a third party launches an OCREVUS biosimilar and how Delaware rules on six SPINRAZA patents. The balance sheet that would normally absorb that risk was spent in May: net debt more than tripled to $6.8B, the securities portfolio is gone, and the $2.05B buyback authorization has sat unused for three years while capital flowed into business development. Five-year TSR of 71.87 against a peer group at 119.92, and a single 3-share open-market insider purchase across a period spanning FY2025 results, the Apellis close, the tau readout and a guidance raise, provide no independent corroboration of the turnaround.

Existing holders should stay. The base case offers roughly 7% appreciation with real optionality on SYFOVRE, felzartamab and — at low probability — diranersen. New capital has no urgency at 6% below the 52-week high; the entry that would make this compelling is $175–$185 with guidance intact, or two consecutive quarters proving the SPINRAZA high-dose conversion works.
