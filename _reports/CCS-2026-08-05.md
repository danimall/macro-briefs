## Executive Summary

Century Communities is a mid-cap, affordability-focused homebuilder: approximately 10,000 annual deliveries across 16 states, 98% of 1H 2026 closings built as move-in-ready spec homes, 96% priced below FHA-insured mortgage limits, with a captive mortgage arm capturing 83% of buyers. The business is not in distress. It is earning a return on equity of roughly 5.7% against a book value that the market discounts by 20%, and the two facts are related.

This report upgrades CCS to HOLD after three consecutive AVOID ratings (5.0 in March, 4.8 in May, 4.6 in June). The upgrade is not driven by price action, though price action is instructive: the stock is up 13.7% since the June 18 review ($63.25 → $71.885) and 52.1% off the May 15 low of $47.28. The upgrade reflects the recognition that the prior thesis was a valuation-compression argument, not a structural-impairment argument, and that the arithmetic no longer supports a negative call. My base-case fair value is approximately $75, above the current price. A probability-weighted scenario set (bull $92 at 25%, base $75 at 50%, bear $56 at 25%) produces an expected value near $74.50, roughly 3.6% above spot. A rating that requires investors to sell cannot rest on an expected value above the market price.

What prevents an upgrade to BUY is equally specific. CCS trades at a 38% premium to the homebuilding peer median P/E (14.8x trailing versus 10.7x for KBH, MHO and GRBK), consensus expects earnings to *decline* next year ($4.59 versus $4.86 current year), adjusted diluted EPS fell 9.7% in Q2 and 22.7% for the half, and 21% of 1H pre-tax income came from a Georgia land sale and a stock-compensation reversal. Operating cash flow was negative $132.4M against negative $47.6M in the same period a year earlier, and the $278.1M revolver draw that plugged the gap also funded $78.1M of buybacks and dividends. The macro channel that matters most for this business, long-end Treasury yields feeding mortgage rates through a builder-funded buydown model, is a key sensitivity.

## Company Overview

CCS operates two brands. Century Communities spans entry-level through lifestyle buyers with limited option and upgrade selection. Century Complete sells exclusively to entry-level buyers through retail studios, centralized locations and the internet, with no option or upgrade selections at all, across 10 states. The Century Complete model is the genuinely differentiated asset: zero configurability plus retail/online distribution structurally lowers SG&A per unit and compresses cycle times, which ran three to four months in 1H 2026.

Seven reportable segments: West (CA, WA), Mountain, Texas, Southeast, Century Complete, Financial Services (Inspire Home Loans, Parkway Title, insurance and escrow), and Century Living (multi-family rental, entirely in Colorado).

The operating model is the defining characteristic and the defining risk. Building 98% spec means capital commits ahead of demand, and the adjustment lever when demand softens is price and financing incentive rather than production restraint. Backlog is thin by design: 1,264 homes / $469.3M at June 30, 2026, under two months of deliveries against a ~10,000-unit annual pace. There is no order book cushion to absorb a demand air pocket.

The 390bp GAAP gross-margin decline in FY2025 (340bp adjusted) drove the 56% earnings collapse. Deliveries fell only 5.6% and ASP 3.3%. Incentive usage, per the 10-K, is the cause.

### Q2 2026: stabilization, not recovery

Headline Q2 looks like a turn: GAAP homebuilding margin rose to 18.1% from 17.6%, net income +3.7%, GAAP diluted EPS +10.5% to $1.26, net new contracts +2.7%. Three corrections from the filing's own reconciliations:

1. **The GAAP margin gain is impairment non-recurrence.** Q2 2025 carried $7.4M of inventory impairment (0.8% of home sales revenue); Q2 2026 carried none. On the adjusted basis excluding impairment, capitalized interest and purchase accounting, Q2 margin was 20.0% versus 20.0%, exactly flat. For the six months, adjusted margin fell 100bp to 19.8%.
2. **EPS growth is share count.** Diluted shares fell 6.6% to 28,653,398 from 30,680,708. GAAP net income rose 3.7% while GAAP EPS rose 10.5%; the gap is essentially the buyback. Adjusted diluted EPS *fell* 9.7% in the quarter.
3. **21% of 1H pre-tax income was non-core.** A Q1 Georgia land sale contributed roughly $13.2M pre-tax (land sales revenue $37.4M less cost $24.2M) versus ~$0.5M a year prior, and a $4.2M stock-compensation reversal (from revised PSU achievement probability) added $0.11 per share. Combined $17.4M against $82.4M of 1H pre-tax income. Excluding the land-sale delta alone, 1H pre-tax income declined roughly 29.6% rather than the reported 17.3%. Land sales are a normal recurring activity for a company that entitles and develops land; the point concerns run-rate quality, not propriety, and 1H 2025 contained a comparable $4.7M stock-comp reversal so that item's year-over-year delta is slightly negative.

Flat adjusted margin after four consecutive quarters of compression is a legitimate stabilization data point, and capitalized interest released into cost of sales rose to 1.8% of home sales revenue from 1.5%, meaning pre-interest unit economics improved marginally. A trough is plausible. A recovery is not yet in the numbers.

### The cash flow and leverage reversal

| | 12/31/2025 | 6/30/2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Revolver balance | $51.5M | $329.6M |
| Total debt | $1,443.1M | $1,683.9M |
| Homebuilding debt/capital | 29.1% | 34.2% |
| Net homebuilding debt/net capital | 25.9% | 31.9% |
| Total liquidity | ~$1.1B | $802.4M |
| Homes under construction | $1,213.8M | $1,451.8M (+19.6%) |
| Land and land development | $2,046.4M | $2,039.3M (flat) |
| Stockholders' equity | $2,591.7M | $2,565.8M |

Negative first-half cash flow is seasonally normal per the company's own disclosure: starts and construction outflows peak in Q2/Q3, receipts concentrate in 2H. The relevant control is the same season prior year, and on that basis inventories absorbed $237.8M versus $85.9M, a 2.8x step-up not explained by seasonality.

The composition matters: homes under construction rose 19.6% while land was flat. CCS increased speculative vertical inventory by nearly a fifth in six months while deliveries fell 7.2% and absorption fell to 2.6/month from 2.8 (and from 3.2 in FY2024). Capital returns of $78.1M ($59.6M buybacks, $18.5M dividends) were funded by the net revolver draw. This pattern began in FY2025, when $178.3M of returns exceeded $153.1M of operating cash flow, cushioned by non-recurring mortgage-servicing-rights and multi-family sale proceeds.

The benign reading is that this is deliberate positioning for the raised delivery guidance and reverses in 2H as spec homes close. The filing does not attribute motive, and both readings fit the disclosed facts. What is not in dispute: roughly 600bp of net leverage and $300M of liquidity headroom were consumed in two quarters.

Solvency is not the question. Two $500M note tranches (3.875% due August 2029, 6.625% due September 2033), revolver maturing November 2028, covenants compliant, $300M of unused accordion capacity, and $232.5M of mortgage repurchase borrowings explicitly not guaranteed by the parent. Two mechanical consequences deserve attention. First, because qualifying assets exceed debt, *all* interest is capitalized ($36.4M in 1H), so a growing revolver arrives later as gross-margin drag rather than as interest expense. Second, refinancing the 3.875% 2029 notes at the 2033 coupon implies roughly $14M of incremental annual interest ($500M × 275bp).

The 2029 notes are carried at $497.6M and fair-valued at $475.0M, roughly 5% below par, while the higher-coupon 2033 notes fair-value slightly above par. That spread is principally a coupon-versus-market-rate effect, not a credit signal.

The 2026 estimated effective tax rate is 26.3% versus 24.1% actual in FY2025. Section 45L credits are disallowed for homes delivered after June 30, 2026 under H.R.1, removing a $2.7M FY2025 offset entirely in 2H.

## Growth Analysis

Every volume metric is contracting. Revenue −9.8%, deliveries −7.2%, ASP −5.4%, absorption 2.6 versus 2.8, selling communities 330 versus 327 (+0.9%) against management's stated low-to-mid-single-digit community growth target. FY2026 delivery guidance was raised to 9,750–10,500 from 9,500–10,500; the raise lifted the floor, not the ceiling, and the ~10,125 midpoint is still 2.5% below FY2025's 10,387. (Guidance rests on secondary reporting of the July 22 call; the transcript was not obtainable from EDGAR.)

Net new contracts +2.7% is the first positive order inflection in the reviewed data and the single most encouraging operating number in the release.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Consensus current-year EPS rose 26.6% over 90 days to $4.86, next-year 11.9% to $4.59, and the last three quarters produced surprises of +114.5%, +68.4% and +61.7%. Rising estimates of that magnitude are a genuine positive and contributed to raising both the growth and valuation scores from the prior review. They do not constitute the inflection setup that would justify a BUY, for three specific reasons:

- **The forward curve declines.** Even after the revisions, next-year consensus ($4.59) sits 5.6% *below* current-year ($4.86). The strongest form of the revision signal requires a cheap price against a rising forward earnings path; here the path bends down, which makes a growth-adjusted multiple undefined rather than favorable.
- **The beats measure model staleness, not accelerating earnings.** The Q2 "beat" was $1.30 against a $0.61 estimate, but $1.30 is the adjusted figure, and it fell 9.7% from $1.44 a year earlier. A 114% beat on a year-over-year decline tells you the model was stale, not that the business inflected.
- **Coverage is one analyst.** Every estimate line in the revision table carries a single contributor. A 26.6% "consensus revision" here is one model update, which is materially lower-information than a broad revision cycle.

The demand backdrop is a binding constraint. CCS manufactures affordability through rate buydowns; its locked pipeline of $134.0M carries a ~4.5% weighted average note rate against prevailing market rates. The cost of each incremental basis point of buydown scales with the gap between market rates and that 4.5%. Rising long-end yields therefore translate directly into either lower volume or lower margin.

## Valuation Assessment

**Book value.** $2,565,751K equity ÷ 28,432,620 shares = **$90.24 per share**. At $71.885, **P/B = 0.797x**.

**Earnings.** Trailing GAAP P/E = $71.885 ÷ $4.86 = **14.8x**; on FY2025 adjusted EPS of $5.99, 12.0x. Forward: doubling 1H GAAP EPS of $2.09 gives $4.18 (17.2x); applying FY2025's 50.3% first-half-to-full-year net income ratio to 1H 2026's $60.557M implies ~$120M, or ~$4.22 per share (17.0x). Correcting for the fact that 2H 2025 absorbed roughly $14.0M of the year's $21.8M impairments (inflating that ratio) raises the implication; losing 45L lowers it. A defensible band is **$4.20–$4.80, or 15–17x**.

**Peer context.** Homebuilding peer median P/E is 10.7x (KBH 10.4, MHO 10.7, GRBK 10.7, DFH 8.7, LGIH 18.2). CCS at 14.8x trailing carries a 38% premium to that median. Peers are not provided on book value, but the structural relationship is inferable: CCS's deeper book discount and higher earnings multiple both reflect the same fact, a lower return on equity. Trailing ROE is $147.6M / ~$2,580M average equity = **5.7%**; annualizing 1H gives roughly 4.7%.

That ROE is the crux. At a 10% cost of equity, a 0.797x book multiple implies a sustainable ROE near 8.0%, equating to about $7.19 of EPS on $90.24 of book. Current earnings power is $4.20–$4.80. The market is therefore already pricing a meaningful margin recovery, adjusted gross margin moving from 19.8% back toward the FY2024 level of 23.3%. Capitalizing current earnings alone at a 9–10% cost of equity produces $45–$56 per share. The gap between that and $71.885 is the recovery option plus the asset floor.

**The asset floor is real but not unlimited.** Berkshire Hathaway acquired Taylor Morrison at $72.50 per share all cash (~$6.8B equity, ~$8.5B enterprise value), a 24% premium, announced May 31/June 1 and closed July 24, 2026, confirmed across AP/AOL, Fast Company, HousingWire, ENR and Motley Fool. That establishes a credible strategic buyer paying a premium for a top-ten builder mid-downturn. Two qualifications: the widely circulated "1.1x book value" characterization originates from Heartland Advisors' Q2 letter and is not independently verified; and HousingWire reported (June 29) that the Taylor Morrison merger proxy disclosed a three-step process in which six potential buyers were approached and **declined** before Berkshire. Six passes is direct evidence against broad builder acquisition appetite. Applying 1.1x to CCS's $90.24 book gives ~$99, a conditional anchor rather than a target, and CCS is smaller, more spec-concentrated, and carries a 13.9% founder block plus a co-principal-executive structure.

**Buybacks are accretive to the floor.** 1H repurchases averaged $61.44 against $90.24 book; buying at 0.68x book adds to book value per share. That mechanism supports a rising floor even in a flat operating environment, and it is a legitimate offset to the earnings-multiple objection.

Consensus targets: mean and median $78 (+8.5%), high $92 (+28%), low $64 (−11%). Analyst distribution is one strong buy, one hold, sentiment stable.

The valuation is genuinely two-sided and I score it neutral at 5.0, an improvement from 4.5 in June. There is no identified mispricing large enough to be asymmetric in either direction: the book discount is defensible given a 5.7% ROE, and the earnings premium to peers is defensible only if margins normalize.

## Competitive Landscape

The segment data validate the affordability strategy and indict the higher-priced end. 1H 2026 pre-tax income: Century Complete $34.4M (+19.3%) at a $259.3K ASP, now the largest homebuilding profit contributor; West $20.1M (−56.7%) at $568.8K ASP with absorption down to 2.8 from 3.6; Mountain −42.4%; Texas −27.9%; Southeast +21.5% (primarily the Georgia land sale); Financial Services +104%.

Two cautions against over-reading this. First, Century Complete's own revenue and deliveries each fell 9.2%, selling communities dropped to 112 from 122, and its Inspire capture rate slipped to 75% from 80%. Its margin improved on lower direct construction costs and the absence of $4.0M of prior-year impairments; volume did not. This is a profit-mix shift, not demand strength. Second, the Financial Services surge is approximately fully explained by non-cash mortgage-servicing-rights capitalization: segment pre-tax income rose $8.957M while MSR originations rose $9.361M, following a strategy reversal from selling to retaining servicing (serviced UPB rebuilt to $1.7B from $769.4M, MSR asset to $25.8M from $11.4M). MSRs on deeply bought-down ~4.5% loans should prepay slowly and are legitimate economic assets, but the valuation discount rate rose to 12.0% from 10.4%, and this line should not be extrapolated as homebuilding recovery. Financial Services now represents 21.3% of consolidated pre-tax income versus 8.6%.

Structurally, industry consolidation is moving scale advantage away from mid-caps. Berkshire is now the #4 builder and folds Taylor Morrison Home Funding into Vanderbilt Mortgage for roughly $8.2B of annual originations. CCS's own forward-looking-statement list newly cites "recent consolidation within the homebuilding industry." Proxy relative TSR shows CCS at 145 versus a peer group at 184, a gap that opened in 2024 and widened in 2025 after outperformance in 2021–2023. Cross-builder margin comparison was not obtainable, so I cannot state whether the 340–390bp compression is better or worse than peers, a genuine evidence gap.

## Risk Assessment

**ARM dependence, and the new risk-factor language.** ARMs were nearly 35% of Q2 originations by principal volume, up from ~30% in Q1 2026 and under 5% in Q1 2025, a seven-fold shift in five quarters. CCS added ARM-specific language to its mortgage-financing risk factor, one of only three risk-factor revisions in the 10-Q: increased reliance on ARMs "may make demand for our homes more sensitive to changes in interest rates, mortgage product availability and lender underwriting standards." Average FICO slipped to 720 from 725 (Century Communities brand 723 from 730). Affordability is being engineered through financing structure rather than price or income growth. The counter-reading, that ARM adoption is rational optimization by buyers expecting to refinance, expanding the qualified pool, is plausible, and there is no evidence of elevated delinquency or cancellation. Monitoring item, not yet a credit event.

**Spec concentration into decelerating absorption.** Homes under construction +19.6% in six months against absorption falling to 2.6/month. If absorption does not recover in 2H, the resolution is deeper incentives or impairment.

**Impairment risk is dormant, not resolved.** Zero impairment in Q2 and 1H 2026 versus $7.8M in 1H 2025 and $21.8M for FY2025. But the testing population widened materially: 34 of 305 communities tested in 2025 versus 15 of 322 in 2024 and 7 of 251 in 2023, at ~12% discount rates. The 10-Q warns that changes in cash flow projections "may change our conclusions on the recoverability of inventory."

**Land strategy whipsaw.** Total lots owned and controlled fell 24.5% in FY2025 to 60,916, with controlled lots down 41.8% and $11.2M of abandonment costs. By June 30 the mix rebalanced to 56.0% owned on 60,128 lots with 1H abandonment costs down to $2.1M. Downside on the controlled portion is capped: 26,447 lots under contract totaling ~$2.0B against only $85.1M of non-refundable deposits.

**Century Living.** Multi-family assets grew to $242.3M from $198.8M with construction borrowings at $119.0M of $140.1M capacity, generating a $48K pre-tax loss, all three properties in Colorado, portions of the loans guaranteed by the parent. Of ~425 completed units, 417 are leased, and the Q4 2025 sale demonstrated a monetization channel.

**Policy.** The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (July 2026) is flagged as a modified risk factor with direction unknown. The January 20, 2026 executive order on institutional homebuying is relevant given 105 previously leased rental homes sold in 2025. HUD energy standards took effect May 2026. Tariffs on cabinets and wood products effective late 2025 produced **no significant cost increases or supply-chain disruption** as of June 30, a positive against a widely feared risk.

**Structural detail for creditors:** Financial Services subsidiaries are non-guarantors of the senior notes. Guarantor-only 1H net income was $44.0M versus $60.6M consolidated, so roughly 27% of earnings sits outside the guarantee perimeter, in the fastest-growing profit line.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.2% against 42.3% one-year historical volatility is fair (1.9pt spread), so the market is not pricing unusual uncertainty in level terms. The informative feature is the shape: term structure is backwardated at 44.2% (15 days) versus 36.6% (43 days) and 39.1% (134 days), which the data source characterizes as pricing imminent stress. With the July 22 earnings print already behind, the August 21 tenor has no company-specific catalyst; the near-dated bid most plausibly reflects macro rate risk (payrolls, the September FOMC), which is the correct risk channel for a builder whose demand is buydown-dependent. Open-interest put/call of 0.79 is call-tilted, so positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis. Near-term skew is balanced (−0.4%); the 3-month and 6-month skew readings (−8.0%, −4.6%) are reported without a directional inference given the undefined sign convention. Short interest of 9.5% of float at 5.4 days to cover is moderate, neither a crowded short nor evidence of informed bearish conviction. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.6%.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($92, +28%)

Adjusted gross margin has stopped compressing (20.0% flat in Q2) and orders inflected +2.7%, which would mark Q1 2026 as the trough. Century Complete, the no-options, retail-studio, $259K-ASP brand, is now the largest homebuilding profit contributor, and 96% of deliveries sit below FHA limits, positioning CCS where the affordability-constrained demand actually is. Book value per share of $90.24 is rising, supported by repurchases at 0.68x book, and Berkshire's confirmed 24% premium for Taylor Morrison establishes that strategic capital will pay above-market for builder assets mid-cycle. Any decline in the 10-year Treasury reduces buydown cost directly, converting to margin. Estimate revisions are positive, coverage is thin, and the consensus mean target sits at $78. If margins recover toward the FY2024 adjusted level of 23.3%, EPS returns toward $6.00+ and 1.0x forward book supports $92–$99.

### Bear Case ($56, −22%)

CCS is priced at a 38% premium to the peer median P/E while earning a 5.7% ROE, and consensus expects lower earnings next year. Adjusted EPS fell 9.7% in the quarter and 22.7% in the half; 21% of 1H pre-tax income came from a land sale and a stock-comp reversal; the fastest-growing segment's improvement is non-cash MSR capitalization. Operating cash flow deteriorated 2.8x on a same-season basis while spec inventory grew 19.6% into falling absorption, and the $278.1M revolver draw funded both that build and $78.1M of shareholder returns, consuming 600bp of net leverage and $300M of liquidity in two quarters. Capitalized interest at 100% means the debt build arrives later as gross-margin drag. If long-end yields rise, buydown costs rise with them. If absorption fails to recover in 2H, the resolution is deeper incentives or a return of impairments against a widened 34-community testing population, and 0.6x book on flat equity is $54–$56.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a balanced-evidence call, not a growth or income thesis. The 1.86% yield is covered by earnings (~29% payout on $4.50) but was not covered by cash flow in 1H, so it does not anchor an income case.

**Scenario set and arithmetic (from $71.885):**
- **Base case $75 (+4.3%).** Book value per share reaching approximately $93 by year-end 2026 (FY2026 net income near $120M, or +$4.22/share, less ~$1.30 of dividends, plus accretion from buybacks below book) held at the current 0.80x multiple. Cross-check: $4.50 FY2026 EPS at 16.5x = $74.
- **Bull case $92 (+28%).** 1.0x forward book, requiring adjusted gross margin recovery toward 22%+ or a strategic bid.
- **Bear case $56 (−22%).** 0.6x book on flat equity, consistent with the May–June 2026 trading range and above the $47.28 low.
- Probability-weighted (25/50/25) expected value ≈ **$74.50, +3.6%**, positive but not asymmetric, which is the arithmetic reason this is neither a BUY nor an AVOID.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. **Adjusted gross margin above 20.5% for two consecutive quarters** with no offsetting incentive increase, the single metric that converts the book-value case into an earnings case.
2. **Operating cash flow positive for 2H 2026 with the revolver balance below $150M at December 31, 2026**, evidence the 1H draw was working-capital timing rather than a structural gap.
3. **Absorption returning to 3.0+/month** alongside net new contracts up more than 10% year over year, confirming the +2.7% inflection.
4. **Price near $58–$62** (0.64–0.69x book), where the earnings-multiple objection resolves against the asset floor.

**Downgrade to AVOID requires:**
1. **Resumption of inventory impairment** at $10M+ in any quarter, which would confirm the widened 34-community testing population is converting to charges.
2. **Net homebuilding debt/net capital above 38%** or liquidity below $600M at the Q3 print, indicating the revolver draw is structural.
3. **Adjusted gross margin below 19.0%** or ARM share above 45% of originations with FICO below 715, evidence that manufactured affordability is being pushed past a prudent limit.
4. A dividend or buyback continued while operating cash flow remains negative through Q3, which would confirm capital returns are being prioritized over balance-sheet repair.

**Next reassessment:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026). The Q3 print is the natural test because it is the first period in which 2H delivery conversion, the 45L credit expiry, revolver trajectory and impairment testing all become visible together. The September FOMC and the intervening payrolls reports are the macro checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Three consecutive AVOID ratings on CCS were wrong. The bear thesis was always a valuation-compression argument (cheap on book, expensive on earnings, ROE too low) rather than an identification of fundamental deterioration that the market had not already absorbed. The market had absorbed it: the stock fell to $47.28 in May, 37.8% below its high, and has since re-rated on a genuine order inflection, an adjusted margin that stopped compressing, and buybacks executed at 0.68x book that raise the asset floor. My own fair value estimate now sits above the current price. Maintaining a negative rating against a positive expected value would be a rating in search of a thesis.

The case for owning CCS here is not made. The stock carries a 38% P/E premium to a peer group earning materially higher returns on equity, consensus expects lower earnings in 2027, adjusted profitability declined in both the quarter and the half, and the two fastest-improving profit lines are a land sale and non-cash MSR capitalization. The company increased speculative inventory 19.6% into falling absorption and funded both that and its shareholder returns with a $278M revolver draw, consuming 600bp of leverage headroom. The macro channel of long-end yields into mortgage rates into buydown cost is a live sensitivity to the margin recovery that the 0.80x book multiple already assumes. That combination does not clear the bar for new capital.

Hold existing positions. New capital should wait for either the $58–$62 zone or two quarters of adjusted margin above 20.5%. Overall score 4.7, up from 4.6.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several macro-rate assertions (mortgage rates "at a one-year high," applications "below year-ago levels," 10-year near "highest since January 2025," 30-year at a "2007 high," a Fed with "three hawkish dissents" and a live September hike, and the options "imminent stress" being tied to a specific macro read) are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and were softened or removed; the bear case's dependence on rising yields is now stated conditionally. The core valuation arithmetic (P/B 0.797x, 14.8x trailing P/E, 5.7% ROE) checks out and is unchanged.
