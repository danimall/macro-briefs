**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Millicom operates fixed and mobile telecom networks across 11 Latin American markets under the TIGO brand, serving more than 46 million customers with a fiber-cable footprint passing over 14 million homes. The investment story has shifted over the past two years from balance-sheet repair to disciplined cash generation. FY2025 delivered record revenue of roughly $5.8 billion, adjusted EBITDA near $2.75 billion at a ~47.2% margin, net profit around $1.3 billion, and equity free cash flow (EFCF) of approximately $916 million, ahead of guidance. Management under CEO Marcelo Benitez has executed an operating turnaround, and the sector-leading EBITDA margin is the single most attractive feature of the business.

The problem is entry point, not quality. TIGO has appreciated roughly 132% over the trailing twelve months, from a 52-week low of $39 to a recent $94.54, within a fraction of its $100.75 high. At the current price the stock trades above the mean analyst target of $87.8 and near the median of $92.5, leaving only about 11% to the high target of $105 against 45% downside to the low target of $52.4. The distribution of analyst views is skewed to the downside, and the options market is pricing near-term stress: near-term implied volatility of 56.6% versus 37.2% realized, a term structure in backwardation, and elevated put skew.

The trailing 12.8x P/E looks cheap against a heterogeneous peer median near 32x, but that comparison is misleading: emerging-market telecoms with USD-denominated debt and Latin American revenue structurally trade at low multiples, and TIGO has already re-rated hard. Forward earnings are more nuanced: current-year consensus of $4.74 implies a temporary earnings dip (integration costs, FX, Coltel consolidation) before recovery to $7.20 next year, or about 13x. The business is a real compounder with Colombia consolidation optionality, but the risk/reward after a triple is balanced-to-unfavorable. We rate TIGO a HOLD (growth_hold), preferring to add on weakness.

## Company Overview

Millicom is a Luxembourg-domiciled operator with principal offices in Doral, Florida, dual-listed on Nasdaq and Stockholm. It files a Form 20-F annual report and Form 6-K interim filings as a foreign private issuer, which means standard U.S. disclosure pipelines (XBRL screens, 10-K sections, Section 16 insider filings, DEF 14A compensation data) return incomplete data. Figures here are drawn from earnings-release-derived reporting and should be re-verified against the FY2025 20-F filed March 24, 2026.

The company runs four core lines: mobile (prepaid and postpaid, the largest segment with meaningful postpaid and ARPU growth cited through 2025–26); home/fixed broadband over fiber-cable HFC networks; Tigo Business (B2B, cloud, security); and adjacencies including TIGO Money (mobile financial services), TIGO Sports, and TIGO ONEtv. Key markets are Guatemala, Colombia, Bolivia, Paraguay, Panama, Honduras (a joint venture), El Salvador, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica. Guatemala historically contributes an outsized share of EBITDA. Millicom is typically the #1 or #2 operator in its markets and is differentiated as a converged fixed-mobile player in underpenetrated, higher-growth emerging markets, a rare structural-growth profile in a sector otherwise fighting for low-single-digit expansion in developed markets.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the case. The ~47.2% adjusted EBITDA margin is extraordinary for a telecom and reflects an operational cost-discipline program under the current management team rather than accounting noise. Net profit divides across 167.1 million shares to approximately $7.78 in reported EPS, consistent with the profile's trailing EPS of $7.83.

Equity free cash flow is management's headline KPI and the basis for capital returns, with a recent quarterly EFCF figure near $225 million cited as a record. Quarterly EBITDA margin around 43.2% was also referenced. The 2026 guidance calls for at least $900 million of EFCF and net leverage of roughly 2.5x, alongside plans to quadruple 5G coverage in Colombia.

Leverage is the balance-sheet watch item. A ~2.5x net-leverage target against ~$2.75 billion of EBITDA implies net debt of roughly $6–7 billion (to be confirmed in the 20-F). By telecom standards, 2.5x is reasonable-to-conservative, and the deleveraging trajectory from the prior "leverage-heavy clean-up" phase is a clear positive. The genuine vulnerability is the currency mismatch: a large portion of debt is USD-denominated while revenue is earned in Latin American currencies. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, refinancing cost and FX translation both work against reported results.

The earnings-delivery record complicates the picture. Over the past four quarters TIGO has posted three misses against consensus, including a -40.2% miss in Q1 2026 ($0.65 actual vs $1.09 estimate) and a -37.1% miss in Q2 2025, offset by one large beat (+55.8%) in Q4 2025. This lumpiness, partly FX-driven and partly the timing of one-time items, limits confidence in point estimates and suggests the current-year consensus of $4.74 embeds real uncertainty. Cash generation has been steadier than reported EPS, which is why management anchors on EFCF.

## Growth Analysis

Millicom is a structural grower relative to developed-market telecoms, driven by subscriber additions, ARPU gains, and homes-passed expansion in underpenetrated markets, layered with inorganic scale via bolt-on and large acquisitions funded by EFCF. Q1 2026 postpaid and home net-add figures were cited as strong (though these may reflect cumulative base rather than quarterly flow, worth verification).

The defining growth catalyst is Colombia. On February 5, 2026, Millicom concluded a tender offer for Telefónica's controlling 67.5% stake in Colombia Telecomunicaciones (Coltel) for USD 214.4 million, and on April 27, 2026 it acquired the remaining government (La Nación) stake, consolidating full control. This is a consolidation play against a retreating Telefónica, positioning Millicom as a stronger, better-capitalized #2 national operator against América Móvil's Claro, with plans to invest in fiber and 5G. The very low headline price for control of a national operator signals that the asset requires substantial capital investment, which explains part of the near-term earnings drag.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and should not be read as a clean trough recovery. Next-year EPS consensus has risen sharply, +44.3% over 90 days and +10.1% over 30 days to $7.20, which is a meaningful positive. But current-year estimates have been cut (-9.2% over 30 days to $4.74, though they are up +11.4% over 90 days) and the current-quarter estimate is falling (-16.3% over 90 days, -4.3% over 30 days to $0.59). The pattern reflects a temporary earnings dip in 2026 on Coltel integration, FX, and capex, followed by recovery, rather than accelerating organic momentum from a depressed base. Because the +44% revision is essentially a recovery to prior earnings power rather than a step-change in the run-rate, and because the stock already trades near consensus, we do not treat it as the strong standalone BUY signal that a genuine trough recovery would warrant.

## Valuation Assessment

At $94.54, TIGO trades at roughly 12.1x trailing EPS ($7.83) and, more meaningfully, about 13.1x next-year consensus ($7.20). The current-year multiple is optically higher at ~20x because 2026 earnings dip before recovering. The peer table shows a Communication Services median P/E near 31.8x, but that group (Charter at 3.8x, Roku at 89.7x, TKO at 79.4x, NYT at 31.8x, FOX at 16.0x) is too heterogeneous to serve as a valuation anchor. The relevant comparison is other emerging-market telecoms, which structurally trade at low double-digit multiples precisely because of the FX, country, and capital-intensity risks TIGO carries. On that basis, 13x forward is fair for the quality, not obviously cheap.

The stronger discipline on valuation comes from the analyst distribution and the price action. The stock now sits about 7% above the mean target. Prior appreciation by itself is not a reason to be bearish, and we do not penalize the stock for having risen on genuine fundamental improvement. But the current price has moved past most analysts' fair-value estimates while the earnings-delivery record has been weak and near-term catalysts are integration-heavy rather than accretive. The asymmetry in the target distribution, modest upside against large downside, argues against paying up at a fresh high.

A low headline multiple on an EM telecom is a well-known value-trap pattern: the "cheapness" reflects structural risk that is permanently priced, not a mispricing the market will correct. Here the cheapness is real but not asymmetric; there is no specific, dated catalyst to force a re-rating from current levels, and the stock has already captured the turnaround narrative. We score valuation at 5.0: fairly valued for the quality, with the burden now on execution to justify the multiple rather than on the market to close a gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Millicom's competitive position is stable-to-improving. It holds #1 or #2 share in most of its 11 markets and is differentiated as a converged fixed-mobile operator, a structurally stronger position than pure-play mobile or fixed competitors. The Colombia consolidation strengthens its #2 position materially as Telefónica exits Latin America; the broader regional trend of consolidation around a few well-capitalized converged operators favors incumbents with EFCF to fund fiber and 5G rollout.

The principal competitor across markets is América Móvil (Claro), a much larger regional player. Millicom's edge is its converged bundle in underpenetrated markets and its cost discipline. The Coltel deal illustrates the consolidator strategy: acquiring control of a national operator cheaply and reinvesting to build a viable second national player. The moat is real but not impregnable; spectrum renewals, price regulation, and competitive intensity constrain pricing power, and the converged advantage requires continued capex to sustain.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are meaningful and not fully priced after the run.

**FX and emerging-market translation risk (primary).** Revenue is earned in Guatemalan quetzal, Colombian peso, Bolivian boliviano, Paraguayan guaraní, and other Latin American currencies while a large share of debt is USD-denominated. Currency depreciation compresses USD-reported results and raises effective leverage. Bolivia specifically has faced dollar-convertibility and dollar-scarcity stress. In the current higher-for-longer rate regime with broad EM currency pressure, this is the fulcrum risk, and the increasing Colombia weighting post-Coltel raises peso sensitivity.

**Concentration.** Guatemala's outsized EBITDA share means a single-country shock (regulatory, political, or macroeconomic) would be material.

**Coltel integration and legacy liabilities.** Consolidating Coltel adds scale cheaply but imports integration risk, regulatory-approval overhang, and potential legacy pension obligations from the asset's state-telecom origins, plus substantial required capex for 5G and fiber. The very low purchase price is a tell that the asset needs investment.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Deleveraging to ~2.5x is a goal, not a locked-in outcome, and debt service in a higher-rate environment with a currency mismatch compounds the FX risk.

**Earnings visibility.** Three misses in four quarters, including the -40% Q1 2026 miss, undermine confidence in forward point estimates.

**Country/regulatory.** Spectrum licensing, price regulation, taxation, and expropriation risk across 11 jurisdictions.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing caution that aligns with the risk assessment. Near-term ATM IV sits well above trailing realized volatility (a rich 19.4-point spread, 71st percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 56.6% > long-term 49.5%), signaling that the market is pricing imminent stress rather than a distant event, and put/call skew is elevated at 3.6% with a ±12.2% implied move on the nearest expiry. Since early June, ATM IV rose 4 points and the put/call open-interest ratio climbed from 0.28 to 0.49 even as the price rose ~10%, indicating hedging demand building into strength. For a stock at a 52-week high above the mean analyst target, this divergence argues against chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105–115).** Coltel integration proceeds smoothly, Colombia becomes a fiber/5G growth engine, and Millicom compounds EFCF at ≥$900M while deleveraging toward and below 2.5x. Next-year EPS recovers to $7.20 and beyond, the ~3% dividend grows, LatAm currencies stabilize, and the market re-rates a de-risked, sector-leading-margin EM telecom toward 15–16x forward. Additional M&A optionality (references to Chile) adds upside. In this scenario the high analyst target of $105 is reached and modestly exceeded.

**Bear Case ($52–65).** LatAm FX weakens materially (Bolivia convertibility stress, Colombian peso depreciation), compressing USD results and raising effective leverage; Coltel integration proves costlier than the headline price implied, with legacy pension and capex drag; earnings misses continue; and the market de-rates the stock back toward the low double-digit multiple on a lower earnings base. The low analyst target of $52.4 reflects roughly this outcome, a ~45% drawdown.

**Base Case (~$92–95).** Earnings dip in 2026 on integration and FX, then recover toward $7.20 next year. The stock consolidates near its median analyst target as the market waits for Colombia execution to prove out. At ~13x forward with a 3% yield, the quality is fairly priced and returns approximate EFCF/dividend accretion plus modest multiple stability, with limited near-term re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 results and any updated Coltel integration/leverage disclosure, followed by the full-year 2026 EFCF delivery against the ≥$900M guide.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (roughly 12–13x recovered forward EPS with the dividend intact), restoring asymmetric upside.
- Two consecutive quarters of earnings meeting or beating consensus, resolving the delivery concern, combined with confirmed net-leverage progress toward 2.5x.
- Evidence that Coltel is accretive to EFCF ahead of plan without a material step-up in required capex or legacy-liability charges.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material LatAm FX deterioration (a sustained Colombian peso or Bolivia convertibility shock) that visibly compresses EFCF or raises effective leverage above ~3.0x.
- A Coltel-related impairment, pension/legacy-liability charge, or capex guidance materially above expectations.
- A dividend cut or EFCF guidance reduction, or a further large earnings miss.

## Conclusion

Millicom is a high-quality, differentiated emerging-market telecom with best-in-class ~47% EBITDA margins, strong and growing equity free cash flow, a disciplined management team that has executed a real turnaround, a reinstated ~3% dividend, and credible consolidation optionality in Colombia. The low headline multiple reflects structural EM risk rather than distress.

The recommendation is HOLD. The stock has tripled off its low to $94.54, now trades above the mean analyst target and near the median with only ~11% to the high target against ~45% downside to the low, the options market is pricing imminent stress, earnings delivery has been inconsistent (three misses in four quarters), and the near-term catalysts are integration-heavy rather than accretive. The forward valuation of ~13x is fair for the quality but not asymmetric, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating from here. Accumulate on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s, or on confirmation that Coltel integration and earnings delivery are tracking to plan.
