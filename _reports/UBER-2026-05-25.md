## Executive Summary

Uber Technologies trades at $71.82, down 29% from its September 2025 high of $102, despite delivering accelerating fundamental performance. Q1 2026 gross bookings grew 25% YoY (21% constant-currency) to $53.7B, adjusted EBITDA grew 33%, and autonomous vehicle trips expanded tenfold year-over-year. The company generated $9.8B in free cash flow in FY2025 on minimal capex ($336M), creating one of the most capital-efficient platforms at scale in the market.

At approximately 18x trailing earnings and ~16x FY2027 estimates ($4.46 consensus), the stock trades at a material discount to industrial peers (median 30x) and at a fraction of its own growth rate. The selloff from $102 to $72 appears to reflect macro concerns and persistent autonomous vehicle disruption fears rather than fundamental deterioration. The $27B buyback authorization is being executed aggressively, and the AV platform strategy is generating operational proof points.

The primary risks are: (1) Waymo or Tesla building competing consumer-facing platforms that bypass Uber entirely, (2) the UK VAT dispute creating material total liability exposure, and (3) macro-driven consumer spending weakness in H2 2026 compressing ride volumes. These are real but adequately compensated by the valuation discount, FCF generation, and structural platform advantages.

## Company Overview

Uber operates one of the world's largest mobility and delivery platforms, connecting 202 million monthly active consumers with drivers and merchants across 15,000+ cities in 70+ countries. The business operates through three segments:

- **Mobility** (57% of revenue): Ride-hailing, including UberX, UberXL, Comfort, Black, and autonomous vehicle trips. $29.7B revenue in FY2025, $7.9B Adjusted EBITDA.
- **Delivery** (33%): Food, grocery, and package delivery. $17.2B revenue, $3.6B Adjusted EBITDA (up 45% YoY).
- **Freight** (10%): Logistics brokerage connecting shippers and carriers. $5.1B revenue, -$33M Adjusted EBITDA (improving from -$74M).

The platform model is agent-based: Uber connects supply (drivers/merchants) with demand (consumers), taking a commission on gross bookings. The take rate was approximately 27% in FY2025. Revenue was $52.0B on $193.5B in gross bookings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
Uber has sustained 18% annual revenue growth for three consecutive years at scale — $37.3B (2023) → $44.0B (2024) → $52.0B (2025). Q1 2026 showed acceleration: gross bookings grew 25% YoY (21% constant-currency) to $53.7B, driven by Uber One membership crossing 50M subscribers (accounting for over half of demand). For Q2 2026, management guided gross bookings to $56.25B–$57.75B, representing 18–22% YoY constant-currency growth.

**Profitability:**
The operating margin trajectory: -6% (2022) → +3% (2023) → +6% (2024) → +10.7% (2025). Operating income more than doubled from $2.8B to $5.6B in one year. Adjusted EBITDA reached $8.7B, growing 35% YoY. Stripping the $5.0B Netherlands DTA release, normalized pre-tax income was approximately $5.8B.

**Free Cash Flow:**
FCF of $9.8B on $336M capex represents an 18.8% FCF margin — strong for any business growing 18-25% annually. The cash-flow-to-capex ratio of 29x demonstrates extreme asset-lightness. However, FY2025 operating cash flow benefited from approximately $2.7B in accrued insurance reserve increases and $967M in other accrued liability growth — working capital items that represent future claims obligations rather than purely recurring discretionary cash generation. Even adjusting for these items, underlying FCF generation remains substantial and supports the buyback program.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of $10.5B against $7.1B cash and $9.8B annual FCF gives net debt of $3.4B, or roughly one-third of annual FCF. The company has actively managed its maturity profile, redeeming $1.15B in convertible notes (Q4 2025) and $1.2B in 2027/2028 notes while issuing 2031 and 2035 senior notes. The $2B commercial paper program established in June 2025 provides additional liquidity.

**Capital Return:**
$6.5B repurchased in FY2025 with $19.2B remaining under the $27B authorization. At the FY2025 repurchase pace, this represents approximately 4.5% of current market cap annually in gross buybacks — partially offset by stock-based compensation dilution. No dividend; all returns via repurchases. The remaining $19.2B authorization represents approximately 13% of market cap if fully executed at current prices, though management has noted repurchases are discretionary and may be suspended.

**Earnings Pattern:**
Q1 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.13 (down from $0.83 in Q1 2025), primarily due to a $1.5B pre-tax headwind from equity-investment revaluations. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.72 beat consensus estimates of approximately $0.70. Revenue of $13.2B grew 14% YoY but was affected by business model changes (including the UK VAT Order 1987 expiration effective January 2026), which negatively impacted revenue growth by approximately 9 percentage points. Adjusted EBITDA of $2.5B beat expectations, and Q2 bookings guidance came in above consensus. The stock rose 8-10% on earnings day, confirming the market looks through the GAAP noise.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Drivers:**
1. Uber One membership flywheel: 50M+ subscribers driving higher frequency and retention
2. Advertising: High-margin revenue growing rapidly ($568M incremental in Delivery alone in FY2025)
3. New verticals: Grocery, package delivery, healthcare transportation expanding TAM
4. Geographic expansion: LatAm, APAC, and Middle East still underpenetrated

**Medium-Term Catalysts:**
1. Autonomous vehicles: Tenfold trip growth in Q1 2026. Commercial launch of autonomous robotaxis targeted for late 2026. Uber's fleet commitments (minimum 20,000 Lucid vehicles with Nuro L4 systems) represent an effort to build direct fleet economics, though the financial terms and comparative advantage versus pure marketplace brokerage remain unproven.
2. Freight cycle recovery: Near-breakeven at cycle trough; meaningful operating leverage when volumes return.
3. Operating leverage: Fixed costs (technology, G&A) growing slower than bookings creates structural margin expansion path.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026 EPS of $2.93 (suppressed by UK VAT headwind), FY2027 EPS of $4.46 (+52% YoY growth). The current-year estimate decline (-13.1% over 90 days) reflects the VAT accounting change, while next-year estimates are rising (+3.3% over 90 days).

The 25% reported bookings growth (21% constant-currency) in Q1 2026 versus the prior 18-19% run rate may suggest Uber One and new verticals are contributing to acceleration, though Uber has not specifically attributed the improvement to these factors. Management's Q2 guidance of 18–22% constant-currency bookings growth suggests the pace may moderate somewhat from Q1's reported levels.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.82 per share:
- **Trailing P/E:** Approximately 18x (TTM EPS of ~$4.03 following Q1 2026's lower GAAP earnings; note that TTM earnings remain influenced by prior-year tax benefits and investment gains)
- **Forward P/E (FY2027):** ~16x on $4.46 consensus EPS (source: consensus aggregator estimates; Yahoo Finance displays a different forward P/E based on next-twelve-month estimates)
- **FCF Yield:** 6.7% ($9.8B FCF / $146B market cap)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~17x on FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $8.7B

Peer comparison: Industrial sector median P/E of 30x (HON 36x, ETN 37x, DE 30x, UNP 22x). Even adjusting for Uber's somewhat different business model, a platform generating 18-25% growth and 18.8% FCF margins trading at 16x forward earnings is mispriced relative to comparables.

More relevant comps: DoorDash (DASH) trades at ~50x forward estimates with inferior FCF conversion, though DoorDash has recently posted higher revenue growth rates (28% in FY2025, 33% in Q1 2026) compared to Uber's 18% and 14% respectively. Lyft trades at ~20x but with dramatically worse competitive positioning and profitability. Uber's valuation discount to its own growth rate (PEG ratio of 0.3x on FY2027 growth) suggests the market is embedding a substantial AV disruption discount.

**What the market may be mispricing:** The ~30% decline from the $102 peak has occurred while AV trips grew tenfold *on Uber's platform* and gross bookings accelerated to 25% reported growth. The stock's current price implies either that Uber's growth will decelerate sharply or that AVs will bypass the platform entirely. Neither is clearly supported by recent operational data.

Analyst consensus target of $104.45 (mean), $106.90 (median) implies 45-49% upside. The low target of $70 essentially matches current price, while the high of $150 represents 109% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Network effects: 202M consumers and millions of drivers create liquidity advantages that new entrants cannot replicate quickly
- Multi-modal platform: Cross-selling rides, delivery, and freight on a single app increases consumer stickiness
- Data moat: Billions of trips generate matching/pricing intelligence unavailable to competitors
- Geographic diversification: Dominant or strong #2 in most markets globally

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Low switching costs: Consumers can download Lyft or DoorDash in seconds; driver multi-homing is common
- AV platform risk: If Waymo, Tesla, or others vertically integrate consumer-facing robotaxi networks, Uber's intermediary position weakens
- Regulatory exposure: Driver classification challenges create jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction risk

**Competitive Position Trajectory:**
Uber One reaching 50M+ members (representing >50% of demand) deepens the defensive moat. Subscription programs increase switching costs — members are incentivized to consolidate all ride/delivery activity on Uber. However, the AV competitive threat is growing rather than shrinking (Waymo expanding, Tesla announcing Robotaxi). Scoring competitive position at 7.0 reflects platform strength partially offset by rising AV displacement risk.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Autonomous Vehicle Disruption (HIGH):**
This is the defining strategic risk. Uber's platform strategy (aggregating AV trips from multiple providers) is logical but requires AV companies to choose Uber over building their own demand platform. Waymo already operates its own app in San Francisco and Phoenix. Tesla has announced competing robotaxi plans. Uber's fleet commitments — including at least 20,000 Lucid vehicles equipped with Nuro L4 systems and a partnership with Rivian for robotaxis targeted for 2028 — represent its hedge against AV companies bypassing the platform. However, the cash requirements for these commitments have not been fully determined, and Uber's filings note there is no assurance the purchases will be completed as contemplated.

Mitigation: Q1 2026 showed AV trips growing tenfold on Uber's platform, suggesting the partnership model is working today. Whether it continues working as AV technology matures and the economics favor vertical integration is uncertain.

**2. UK VAT Liability (Potentially $2-3B+ total exposure, analyst estimate):**
HMRC has issued assessments totaling approximately $1.8B (£1.4B) for March 2022 – September 2024. Uber has paid these amounts to proceed with appeals and records them as receivables. Additional assessments covering 2023-2025 are expected but not yet quantified by the company. If Uber loses the appeal, the amounts already paid would not be recoverable. Separately, the January 2026 expiration of VAT Order 1987 creates a structural ongoing headwind to UK economics — estimated by analysts at approximately $1B annually — which Uber may absorb or pass to consumers through pricing. Total exposure including historical assessments and future structural costs could reach £2-3B+, though this is an analyst estimate rather than a company-disclosed figure.

**3. Consumer Spending Sensitivity:**
In a stagflationary environment with real wages negative, ride-hailing and food delivery are discretionary categories vulnerable to consumer pullback. Macro headwinds and elevated gas prices could compress trip volumes in H2 2026. Uber One membership partially mitigates this by locking in committed spend.

**4. Driver Classification:**
Mexico's reclassification law (December 2024), New Zealand Supreme Court ruling (November 2025), and Massachusetts collective bargaining vote create ongoing regulatory uncertainty. Full reclassification in a major market would add significant costs and potentially reduce driver supply.

**5. Insurance Cost Escalation:**
$851M increase in FY2025 driven by rate-per-mile increases. This is a structural headwind that grows with platform scale and cannot be easily hedged.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-110, +39-53%):**
- Gross bookings growth sustains at 20-25% constant-currency through FY2026-2027 as Uber One drives frequency
- Operating margins continue expanding toward 13-15% as advertising scales and fixed costs leverage
- AV platform strategy proves durable: Uber becomes the demand layer for autonomous mobility
- FY2027 EPS reaches $4.50-5.00; stock re-rates to 22x forward = $99-110
- Buyback provides 3-5% annual net share-count reduction (gross buybacks partially offset by SBC dilution), compounding per-share growth
- Freight cycle recovery adds $500M+ EBITDA

**Bear Case ($55-60, -16-23%):**
- Macro deterioration drives consumer pullback in H2 2026; bookings growth decelerates to 12-15%
- Waymo/Tesla vertically integrate and bypass Uber's platform, causing market to apply permanent disruption discount
- UK VAT appeal fails, creating material liability and permanent margin compression in the UK
- Operating margin expansion stalls as insurance costs and driver incentives rise
- Stock compresses to 12-14x on $4.00 EPS = $48-56

**Base Case ($90-95, +25-32%):**
- Bookings growth moderates to 18-20% (still strong) as macro headwinds partially offset Uber One tailwinds
- Operating margins reach 12% by FY2027 on advertising growth and operating leverage
- AV partnerships continue generating incremental trips without cannibalizing core ride-hailing
- FY2027 EPS of $4.46 at 20-21x forward (discount to peers but narrowing) = $89-94
- Buyback provides 2-3% annual net EPS accretion after SBC offset

## Conclusion

Uber is a best-in-class platform business generating $9.8B in annual free cash flow, growing bookings 25% YoY (21% constant-currency), and trading at approximately 16x FY2027 forward earnings — a valuation that appears to embed excessive pessimism about AV disruption and macro sensitivity. The stock's 29% decline from its September 2025 peak occurred while fundamentals improved (bookings accelerated, margins expanded, AV partnerships advanced). The combination of sub-20x earnings on 18-25% growth, 6.7% FCF yield, active buybacks ($19.2B remaining authorization), and improving AV optionality creates an asymmetric setup.

The primary risk is binary AV disruption: if Waymo or Tesla fully bypass Uber's platform, the intermediary model breaks. Current evidence (tenfold AV trip growth on Uber's platform, multiple partnerships, fleet commitments) suggests this risk is being actively managed, though the economic superiority of Uber's approach remains unproven. At ~16x forward earnings, the market is pricing a high probability of displacement that operational data does not yet support.

BUY at $71.82 with a 12-18 month base case target of $92.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Buyback accretion assumption reduced from 8-10% to ~4.5% gross (lower net of SBC), AV fleet economics reframed as unproven, and FCF quality qualified by working-capital benefits — these corrections moderately reduce the per-share growth trajectory supporting the target price.
