## Executive Summary

AMD delivered its fourth consecutive earnings beat in Q2 FY2026 and the growth is genuine and accelerating: revenue of $11,536M rose 50.1% year over year, Data Center revenue of $6,718M rose 107% to 58% of the company, GAAP gross margin reached 53.8%, and GAAP operating income of $1,990M compared with a $(134)M loss a year earlier. Consensus has followed: FY2027 EPS has been revised from $11.22 to $13.92 over ninety days, a 24.1% increase, and next-quarter estimates are up 11.5%. This is the single strongest estimate-revision profile in the large-cap semiconductor complex, and it is corroborated by hard operating data rather than guidance alone: a record 46.2% revenue share in x86 server CPUs, Microsoft joining Meta, OpenAI and Oracle as a Helios customer, and Q3 revenue guided to roughly $13.0B (+41%).

Two things stop this from being a BUY. First, price. At $518.58 the equity is worth approximately $846.6B on 1,632.5M shares, or 69.5x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $7.46 and 37.3x FY2027 consensus of $13.92. Nvidia, a higher-margin, larger-share competitor with a deeper software moat, trades near 23x its FY2027 consensus. The FY2027 number that makes AMD look reasonable is doing all the work, and reverse-engineering it is uncomfortable: $13.92 on roughly 1,700M diluted shares implies about $23.7B of non-GAAP net income. Against a plausible FY2027 revenue base of $75-78B, that requires a non-GAAP net margin near 30-31% versus 23.9% in Q2 FY2026, roughly 600-700bp of expansion delivered simultaneously with an industry-wide memory shortage, in a product (Helios) that the company itself describes as carrying extreme HBM4 content.

Second, structure. In six months AMD's unconditional purchase commitments went from $12.2B to $30,276M, with $17,386M falling due inside the remainder of FY2026. It has added $15.2B of data-center leases across commenced, signed-not-commenced and post-quarter agreements, up to $5.0B of investment commitments, and $4,100M of maximum potential payments under **guarantees of third parties' data-center lease obligations**, a newly disclosed risk factor. Separately, up to 320M warrant shares issued to OpenAI and Meta at a $0.01 strike sit entirely outside the reported diluted share count, and the filings do not resolve whether vested warrant fair value will be charged as contra-revenue against the very sales that trigger vesting. That is the largest unmodeled item in the FY2027 consensus. HOLD, with a defined add level.

## Company Overview

AMD is a fabless designer of CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs and adaptive SoCs, reporting three segments. FY2025 revenue was $34,639M (+34.3%) with GAAP operating income of $3,694M (10.7% margin). The FY2025 mix was Data Center $16,635M (+32%, 21.7% margin), Client & Gaming $14,550M (+51%, 19.6%) and Embedded $3,454M (−3%, 36.0%).

By Q2 FY2026 the mix had shifted decisively. Data Center at $6,718M (+107%) ran a 31.3% segment margin; Client & Gaming at $3,841M (+6%) saw margin compress to 15.2% from 21.2%, with Gaming down 31% on late-cycle semi-custom console volumes and Client ASPs down 6% on notebook mix even as units rose 34%; Embedded returned to growth at $977M (+19%) with a 39.5% margin. International sales were 70% of Q2 revenue.

One structural detail deserves emphasis because it is routinely modeled incorrectly. The Q2 10-Q states that Helios rack-scale platforms are "system-level architectures … which we develop and license for implementation by customers and their manufacturing partners," and that AMD does "not manufacture or sell the completed Helios rack systems." Helios is an attach-rate and demand-shaping vehicle for AMD silicon, not a rack-revenue line comparable to Nvidia's NVL72 systems business. The March 2025 ZT Systems acquisition ($4,409M total consideration) followed the same logic: AMD kept the rack-integration engineering, classified the factories as held-for-sale on day one, and sold them to Sanmina in October 2025 for roughly $2.4B cash plus 1.15M Sanmina shares, netting about $1.4B of cash. Buying design capability and immediately outsourcing manufacturing was capital-efficient.

Supply is concentrated: TSMC produces all wafers at 7nm and below; GlobalFoundries supplies above-7nm under a wafer supply agreement whose pricing and minimum-volume framework runs only **through 2026**; assembly and test flow largely through joint ventures with Tongfu Microelectronics affiliates ($594M of related-party purchases in Q2, $1.2B in H1).

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet quality is genuinely strong on conventional measures.** At June 27, 2026: cash and short-term investments $13,111M against total debt principal of $3,226M, a net cash position of $9,885M; current ratio 2.61x; debt/equity 4.8%; FY2025 interest coverage roughly 28x on $131M of interest expense. Liquidity was upsized in May 2026: a new five-year $5.0B unsecured revolver replaced a $3.0B facility and the commercial paper program went from $3.0B to $5.5B, with nothing drawn at quarter-end. The debt ladder is trivial for a company this size: $875M due 2026 (now current), then $625M/2028, $750M/2030, $500M/2032, $500M/2052.

The qualifier is asset composition. Goodwill ($25,470M) plus acquired intangibles ($15,635M) total $41,105M, 48.7% of total assets and 61.1% of equity. Tangible book value is approximately $26.1B, or roughly $16 per share. Retained earnings *fell* from $6,699M to $5,909M in six months, which is an intra-equity reclassification, not deterioration: $6,699 + $3,680 of H1 net income − $4,470 charged for retiring 66.9M treasury shares = $5,909, while total equity rose from $62,999M to $67,224M. Any screen reading that decline as losses is wrong.

**Cash generation is strong but the composition warrants discount.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $5,321M against capex of $1,197M, giving derived free cash flow of $4,124M (18.9% margin) versus $1,907M (12.6%) a year earlier. Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow is approximately $9,413M. However, accounts payable rose from $2,929M to $5,359M in six months, a $2,430M source that equals 59% of H1 free cash flow, while receivables (−$966M), inventories (−$548M) and prepaid/other (−$991M, attributed to prepayments under supply agreements) ran the other way. If payables days normalize while receivables, inventory and supply prepayments keep building against 40-50% revenue growth, conversion deteriorates. I am not annualizing H1 free cash flow: construction in progress went from $508M to $1,148M and net PP&E from $2,312M to $3,439M, so capex is stepping up sharply from a low base.

**Two earnings-quality adjustments matter, and one of them cuts the other way from the obvious read.** FY2025 net income of $4,335M exceeded pretax income of $4,140M because AMD booked a net tax *benefit* of $103M, including $853M from releasing uncertain tax positions on dual consolidated losses. Normalizing to a 12% rate gives roughly $3,709M, about 14% below reported, so any FY2025-based P/E or ROE is flattered. Separately, Q2 FY2026 other income of $598M (versus $98M) came from unrealized gains on previously non-marketable equity securities that became publicly listed; marketable equity securities went from $0.2B to $1.2B, with $1.1B under contractual sale restrictions through October 2028. That contributed roughly $0.32 of the $1.38 GAAP diluted EPS.

Critically, it does **not** inflate the non-GAAP number that consensus uses. The equity markup is excluded from non-GAAP, and, with Q2 non-GAAP EPS not directly verifiable in the sources I was given, the reported $1.66 EPS actual against the $1.63 estimate is a clean operating comparison. What non-GAAP does exclude is roughly $500M per quarter of stock-based compensation, 4.4% of revenue and real economic dilution, which is why the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap ran about $2.5B in FY2025 ($6,831M non-GAAP versus $4,335M GAAP).

**The beat magnitude is compressing.** Across the four disclosed quarters (oldest to newest): +2.1%, +14.8%, +4.8%, +1.8%. Four beats in four quarters supports confidence in the direction of consensus, but a 1.8% beat on the most recent print, against a stock that had roughly tripled off its September 2025 low, is a thinner cushion than the streak headline implies.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue trajectory is accelerating, not decelerating: $22,680M (FY2023, −3.9%) → $25,785M (+13.7%) → $34,639M (+34.3%) → $21,789M in H1 FY2026 (+44.1%) → Q3 guided to roughly $13.0B ±$300M (+41% year over year, +13% sequential). Trailing-twelve-month revenue is approximately $41,305M.

Almost all incremental growth is Data Center, and the drivers are identifiable rather than thematic. In server CPUs, third-party data puts AMD's x86 revenue share at a record 46.2% in Q1 2026, with EPYC "Venice" arriving on TSMC's 2nm process ahead of Intel's competing P-core response by a reported margin of roughly twelve months. This franchise is high-margin, sticky, and underappreciated relative to the accelerator narrative. In accelerators, the MI350/MI400/MI450 line plus Helios has attracted OpenAI (intent for up to 6 GW), Meta (up to 6 GW), Oracle and now Microsoft, with initial Helios shipments guided for Q3 FY2026 and the ramp in Q4 into 2027.

Consensus reflects this: current quarter $1.86 (+8.5% over ninety days), next quarter $2.63 (+11.5%), FY2026 $7.46 (+10.5%), FY2027 $13.92 (+24.1%). A +24.1% ninety-day revision on the out-year is the strongest signal in the dataset and normally precedes further re-rating.

The reason I do not treat it as a BUY trigger is the composition of what it requires. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $13.92 on approximately 1,700M diluted shares implies roughly $23.7B of non-GAAP net income. Building revenue bottom-up — Data Center "more than doubling" (a target sourced only to secondary reporting of the earnings call, not verified against company materials) from a FY2026 base near $29B, Client & Gaming near $16B, Embedded near $4B — gets to roughly $75-78B. That implies a non-GAAP net margin of 30-31% against 23.9% in Q2 FY2026. AMD must therefore deliver approximately 50% revenue growth *and* 600-700bp of net margin expansion in the same year that its flagship product carries reportedly 31 TB of HBM4 per rack, into a memory market the 10-Q repeatedly describes as short with rising prices. Q3 guidance of roughly 56% non-GAAP gross margin says the cost pressure is not yet biting; 2027 is the exposure window.

Underneath the headline, the non-AI business is weak. Gaming fell 31%, Client & Gaming segment margin compressed 600bp, and Embedded contracted 3% in FY2025 before recovering. Consolidated +50% growth is a Data Center story layered over a declining console cycle. Semi-custom is contractually cyclical and a next-generation console would reverse it, and the +34% Client unit growth is arguably a better signal than the −6% ASP, but the durable ex-Data-Center growth rate is far below the reported number.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $518.58 basis:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market capitalization (1,632.5M shares) | ~$846.6B |
| Enterprise value (net of $9.9B net cash) | ~$836.7B |
| P/E, FY2026 consensus non-GAAP $7.46 | **69.5x** |
| P/E, FY2027 consensus non-GAAP $13.92 | **37.3x** |
| P/E, TTM GAAP net income ~$6,434M | ~131x |
| EV / TTM revenue $41,305M | ~20.3x |
| EV / annualized Q3 guide (~$52B) | ~16.1x |
| PEG on FY2027 (37.3x ÷ 86.6% growth) | 0.43 |
| Tangible book per share | ~$16 |

At $482.05 these become 64.6x FY2026 and 34.6x FY2027.

The bull framing is the PEG: 37.3x on 87% expected earnings growth is 0.43, and on that basis AMD screens cheap. Two problems with leaning on it. First, PEG collapses when the growth rate normalizes: the same arithmetic at 30% growth and 37x is 1.24, and the multiple would compress toward the growth rate. Second, the peer comparison runs against AMD, not for it. Nvidia trades near 23x its FY2027 consensus with materially higher gross and operating margins, larger scale, and a software ecosystem AMD's own risk factors identify as a competitive moat. An investor who believes the accelerator ramp is real may have cheaper, higher-quality ways to own it. AMD's premium is priced on the *rate* of change, which is the most reversible input in the stack.

Analyst targets sit at a mean of $579.11 and median $600, low $320 and high $1,250, with 41 buy-equivalent ratings, 10 holds and zero sells. Consensus has been stable across four monthly reads. On stocks trading below 30x forward earnings with rising estimates, consensus targets function as floors that get revised upward. At 69.5x current-year earnings that logic inverts: the target and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation, and 41 buys with zero sells means there is no marginal upgrade left to fuel a re-rating. The August 4 reaction demonstrated this: a revenue beat, a raised guide, and the stock fell roughly 6% after hours and further subsequently, with coverage attributing the decline to capex concerns.

Score: 5.0. Not stretched enough to be a short, not discounted enough to underwrite the FY2027 assumption with a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

AMD's position is best described as the credible second source, and its trajectory in the core franchise is clearly improving. The 46.2% x86 server revenue share is a record. EPYC Venice on 2nm arrives roughly a year ahead of Intel's answer. Data Center segment margin expanded to 31.3% from a much lower base. Microsoft's addition as a Helios customer alongside Meta, OpenAI and Oracle broadens the accelerator design-win base beyond the two warrant counterparties. ROCm reportedly reaching 90-95% of H100 throughput on LLM inference is meaningful software progress, though the comparison is against a prior-generation Nvidia part and originates from vendor-adjacent reporting.

Three headwinds are underweighted. The Q2 10-Q newly names the September 2025 Nvidia–Intel partnership, including Nvidia's investment in Intel and collaboration on data-center and client platforms, as a development that "may result in increased competition and pricing pressure." That attacks both AMD franchises simultaneously: the x86 server share gains and the client/notebook momentum. Hyperscalers' internal AI silicon programs are named separately, and the same customers signing 6 GW letters of intent are the ones funding custom accelerators. And Helios being licensed rather than sold caps AMD's revenue capture per deployed rack relative to competitors that sell the system.

The strategic mechanism worth understanding is that AMD's accelerator position is now interlocked with customer *financing*: warrants create purchase incentives (up to 320M shares), $4.1B of lease guarantees let capital-constrained partners sign leases they might not otherwise obtain, $15.2B of direct leases secure capacity, and up to $5.0B of investment commitments seed the ecosystem. The most plausible reading is that this is the market-clearing price of securing scarce power and capacity in 2026 while displacing an entrenched incumbent, financing an ecosystem into existence that AMD's own new risk factor concedes the exposure to.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Warrant accounting is undisclosed and potentially material — the single highest-impact open item.** Two customer warrants, each for up to 160M shares at $0.01: OpenAI (8-K, October 6, 2025) and Meta (8-K, February 24, 2026). Both are liability-classified, no tranches had vested or become exercisable at June 27, 2026, and there was no financial-statement impact. Vesting requires GPU purchase milestones, specified AMD stock-price targets, and for OpenAI additional stock-performance thresholds plus technical and commercial gates. The filings do not state whether vested fair value will be recorded as consideration payable to a customer (contra-revenue) or as expense. If contra-revenue, reported Data Center revenue and gross margin on OpenAI and Meta volume would be materially below gross shipment value, on the exact sales that make the FY2027 consensus work. Neither the $13.92 estimate nor the 41 buy ratings appear to model this. It should not be assumed either way, but its absence from consensus is an asymmetric risk.

**2. Contingent dilution outside the diluted share count.** The 320M warrant shares equal 19.6% of the 1,632.5M shares outstanding and would produce 16.4% post-issuance ownership dilution. Reported Q2 diluted shares of 1,659M exclude them entirely. Dilution is highly conditional and correlated with the value the deals create, which mitigates but does not eliminate the transfer.

**3. Third-party credit exposure at exactly the wrong moment in the AI credit cycle.** The $4,100M of maximum potential payments arises from guarantees of "commercial partner data center lease obligations" with terms up to 15 years, payable on partner default, and "may be issued in exchange for warrants." Publicly reported counterparties — Core Scientific (up to 2.5 GW, 500 MW initial phase by 2027) and Riot Platforms (up to 200 MW) — are companies mid-pivot out of bitcoin mining. Underwriting 15-year lease credit to those balance sheets is a different exercise from selling chips to Microsoft. A guarantee call would be modest in dollars relative to AMD's liquidity but would be read as a demand-quality event.

**4. Commitment stack and excess-capacity risk.** Unconditional purchase commitments of $30,276M with $17,386M due in the remainder of FY2026, plus $1.2B of commenced leases, $4.5B signed-not-commenced, $9.5B entered after quarter-end (terms up to 16 years, commencing 2027-2028), and up to $5.0B of investment commitments — roughly $54.6B in aggregate. Against $13.1B of cash, $9.4B of TTM operating cash flow and $10.5B of undrawn facilities, this is coverable. The 10-Q separately flags the risk of excess capacity on AMD's own cloud-compute commitments if it cannot use or offload them, and notes AI customers may request deferred-payment arrangements from vendors. I could not decompose the $30.3B by category; a large share is probably ordinary wafer and HBM procurement for a high-growth year rather than new balance-sheet risk.

**5. China, unquantified in four separate ways.** MI308 licensing drove $440M of net FY2025 inventory charges. In August 2025 US officials expressed an expectation of receiving 15% of revenue from licensed MI308 China sales, with no regulation published to date. MI325 export licenses were granted in February 2026, but AMD does not know whether China will permit imports, and units shipped to China must first undergo US inspection, incurring a 25% tariff on re-importation into the US for that inspection. China's 2026 State Council Decrees 834 and 835 expand authority to impose countermeasures on companies implementing foreign export controls, creating potential conflicting legal obligations. With 70% of revenue international, this is not a rounding error.

**6. HBM4 cost into the 2027 ramp.** The 10-Q flags an industry-wide memory shortage with rising prices as both a gross-margin risk and a driver of data-center buildout cost. Extreme HBM content per Helios rack makes allocation and pricing gating variables precisely where the FY2027 margin expansion must come from.

**7. Customer concentration probably rising.** The risk factor confirms dependence on a small number of customers; the specific percentages were not retrievable. Data Center at 58% of revenue with named mega-commitments from OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle — several of whom are simultaneously warrant holders, guarantee beneficiaries or capacity partners — points toward increasing rather than decreasing concentration. Revenue mix shift is not diversification.

**8. Governance.** CEO total compensation rose 78% to $55,161,779 in FY2025 while AMD's indexed TSR fell for a second consecutive year (319 → 271 → 234) against a semiconductor peer index that rose from 282 to 547. The FY2026 proxy will look very different because the 2026 rally falls outside that window, and "compensation actually paid" is volatile in both directions (−$160.1M in FY2022). The fair question is grant sizing ex ante. Separately, five Item 5.02 officer/director filings appeared between December 15, 2025 and July 1, 2026 without identified individuals, and a director arrangement adopted May 26, 2026 for 135,000 shares was explicitly flagged as *not* intended to satisfy the Rule 10b5-1(c) affirmative defense, an unusual disclosure choice.

**9. Insider activity is one-directional.** No open-market purchases were observed. Lisa Su sold roughly 334,100 shares for approximately $129M across March, May and June (direct holdings −13%), largely offset by a 362,906-share PRSU grant, and adopted a new 10b5-1 plan on June 8, 2026 covering 450,000 shares through June 2027. Norrod, Grasby, Papermaster and director Denzel all sold; forward plan capacity across named insiders exceeds 730,000 shares. Programmatic diversification into a stock that tripled in four months is ordinary and, set against +24.1% out-year estimate revisions, is not the dominant signal. The complete absence of buying is nonetheless relevant alongside the buyback pause: $221M repurchased in H1 FY2026 versus $1.3B in FY2025, with $9.2B of a $14B authorization remaining, while capital was redirected to leases and ecosystem investment.

**10. Wafer supply agreement renegotiation.** The GlobalFoundries pricing and minimum-volume framework runs only through 2026, with renegotiation landing inside a capacity squeeze. Above-7nm is a shrinking share of mix (I/O dies, legacy, embedded), so the earnings sensitivity is likely small.

### Options Market Signal

Between July 21 and July 22, ATM implied volatility rose from 91.1% to 104.5% (+13.4pp) while the price rose 4.1% and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.73 to 0.34. Rising IV with a rising price and collapsing put demand indicates aggressive call-side accumulation into the Advancing AI event and the Q2 print, not hedging. The print then beat and the stock fell, a positioning unwind rather than a fundamental repricing, which is consistent with the decline from the June 30 high of $584.73. Short interest is 2.5% of float at 1.4 days to cover: no squeeze fuel, and no informed bearish positioning either. IV above 100% against a beta of 2.489 prices substantial two-sided uncertainty; for a holder, that makes covered-call overwriting more attractive than adding delta here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $730 (+40.8%)

AMD executes the MI450/Helios ramp on schedule: initial shipments in Q3 FY2026, volume in Q4, and OpenAI plus Meta each taking approximately 1 GW during 2027. Data Center revenue more than doubles from roughly $29B to $60B+, total revenue reaches $78-82B, and non-GAAP gross margin holds at or above 56% as HBM4 supply agreements (evidenced by the $991M of supply prepayments already on the balance sheet) lock cost. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS comes in above consensus at $16, and the market pays 30x an FY2028 estimate near $24. The x86 server franchise compounds independently toward the >$120B 2030 TAM management now cites, supplying high-margin cash flow that funds the accelerator fight. Warrant fair value is charged as expense rather than contra-revenue, removing the largest unmodeled hit. Analyst targets ratchet toward the $600 median and beyond.

### Bear Case — $300 (−42.1%)

The FY2027 margin assumption breaks. HBM4 cost inflation passes into AMD's gross margin faster than pricing can absorb, holding non-GAAP gross margin near 53-54% instead of expanding toward 60%, and non-GAAP EPS lands near $10 rather than $13.92. Helios shipment timing slips one or two quarters as power, permitting or construction constraints bind — all named risks. Vested warrant fair value is recorded as contra-revenue, reducing reported Data Center revenue and margin on OpenAI and Meta volume. A counterparty in the guarantee book fails to secure project financing, forcing a credit-derivative charge and reframing AMD's growth as vendor-financed. The multiple compresses to 30x on $10, giving $300. This is a derating, not a collapse: the stock traded at $196-210 in March 2026, and the 52-week low is $149.22.

### Base Case — $585 (+12.8%)

FY2027 consensus of $13.92 is roughly delivered, with the memory-cost drag offsetting some upside from volume. By mid-2027 the market looks to an FY2028 estimate near $19.50 and pays 30x, or equivalently 42x FY2027, a premium that persists while growth is above 40% and compresses as it normalizes. $585 sits just under the $600 analyst median and above the $579 mean.

Sensitivity: at the $482.05 daily-history close, base case upside is +21.4%, bull +51.4%, bear −37.8%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $585 (+12.8%).** 30x an FY2028 non-GAAP estimate of approximately $19.50 (FY2027 consensus $13.92 growing 40%), equivalent to 42x FY2027 consensus.

**Bull case target: $730 (+40.8%).** 30x an FY2028 estimate near $24, on FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $16 versus $13.92 consensus.

**Bear case target: $300 (−42.1%).** 30x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $10, reflecting gross margin held near 53-54% by HBM4 costs and/or contra-revenue warrant accounting.

**Timeframe: 12 months, anchored to three dated checkpoints.** Q3 FY2026 results in late October 2026 (Helios initial shipments, Q4 guide, gross-margin trajectory); Q4 FY2026 results in late January/early February 2027 with the FY2027 outlook (the first company-sourced test of the "Data Center more than doubles in 2027" framing, which I have only from secondary reporting and treat as unverified); and the FY2026 10-K, which should resolve warrant accounting treatment and disclose customer-concentration percentages.

**Risk/reward as currently priced:** +12.8% to base against −42.1% to bear is roughly 1:3.3. Bull against bear is approximately 1:1. That asymmetry does not support new capital at $518.58.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Disclosure — in the 10-K, a 10-Q, or an 8-K — that vested customer-warrant fair value will be recognized as expense rather than contra-revenue, or quantification showing the contra-revenue effect is under 3% of Data Center revenue.
2. Q3 FY2026 print with Data Center revenue at or above $8.0B, non-GAAP gross margin at or above 56%, and accounts-payable days flat-to-down (confirming cash conversion is not payables-financed).
3. Price of $380-400, or approximately 27-29x FY2027 consensus $13.92, which restores better than 2:1 asymmetry to the $585 base case.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. Non-GAAP gross margin guided below 53% on memory cost pass-through.
2. Q4 FY2026 revenue guided below approximately $14.0B, which would break the sequential ramp the $2.63 Q4 consensus requires, or FY2027 consensus revisions turning negative on a trailing-30-day basis.
3. A guarantee call or a non-trivial credit-derivative fair-value charge against the $4,100M line, or a financing failure at a named data-center counterparty.
4. Publication of a US regulation imposing the 15% China revenue-share expectation, or a Chinese import denial for MI325.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings, expected late October 2026. Holders should consider overwriting calls while ATM implied volatility exceeds 100%.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** What improved since the last review: Q2 delivered a fourth straight beat with Data Center up 107%, Microsoft joined the Helios customer list, x86 server share hit a record 46.2%, FY2027 consensus rose 24.1% in ninety days, and the stock is roughly 11% below its June 30 high of $584.73. A lower price on improving fundamentals is a better entry, not a worse thesis.

What changed against the thesis is the structure of the growth. In six months AMD added roughly $18B of unconditional purchase commitments, $14B of new data-center leases, up to $5.0B of investment commitments and $4.1B of guarantees on third parties' lease obligations — with a new risk factor disclosing exactly that exposure — while pausing the buyback. The company is converting from asset-light fabless design toward an infrastructure-committed, partly vendor-financing model. That is a defensible strategy for displacing an entrenched incumbent while power and capacity are scarce, and it may well be the market-clearing price of admission. It also means the FY2027 earnings power that justifies today's multiple depends on counterparty credit, HBM4 allocation, and an undisclosed accounting decision about up to 320M warrant shares, in addition to AMD's own execution.

At 69.5x current-year and 37.3x next-year consensus, with the next-year figure requiring both 50% revenue growth and 600-700bp of net margin expansion into a memory shortage, the price already pays for successful execution. Nvidia at roughly 23x forward offers cheaper exposure to the same demand with fewer unresolved structural questions. Own AMD for the Helios ramp and the server-CPU compounding underneath it; add at $380-400, where the base case offers the asymmetry the current price does not.
