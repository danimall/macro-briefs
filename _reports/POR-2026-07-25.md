**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Portland General Electric (POR) is a vertically-integrated, single-state regulated electric utility serving roughly 961,000 retail customers across a 4,000-square-mile Oregon service territory. The investment case rests on a secular tailwind: Oregon's HB 2021 decarbonization mandate combined with strong data-center and industrial load growth (digital-services deliveries up ~10% YoY) supports a $1.3–1.7 billion/year capital program and multi-year rate-base expansion. This is the same data-center demand structure now visible across the regulated-utility complex, and POR is positioned to convert that load into recoverable rate base over the next several years.

Against that runway sit three tightening constraints that have progressively weighed on the story since early 2026. First, the Oregon regulatory climate has turned demonstrably more cost-conscious: Q1 2026 booked $15 million of hard OPUC disallowances, the commission declined to extend the RCE mechanism, and 2025 legislation (HB 3179, HB 3546) constrains recovery timing and reallocates large-load costs. Second, POR announced a $1.9 billion cash acquisition of PacifiCorp's Washington operations in February 2026 — its first move outside Oregon — layering a seven-state approval process, bridge/term-loan financing in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and integration risk on top of an already negative-free-cash-flow capital cycle. Third, the funding of that program relies on structural, ongoing equity dilution (shares up ~6% in fifteen months, with a $500M ATM and a ~$530M equity forward both live), diluting per-share metrics even as absolute earnings grow.

Q1 2026 net income fell 55% to $45 million ($0.38 diluted), part noise (~$17M transformation/deal cost, warm-winter load weakness) but part real signal (the disallowances). At $52.05, POR trades at roughly 23x trailing/forward earnings, a premium to the electric-utility peer median near 19.7x, with a ~4.2% dividend yield that supports the shares but no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion. We rate POR a **HOLD** on an income basis. The dividend and investment-grade balance sheet warrant continued ownership for yield, but the combination of a full valuation, a tougher regulator, and unresolved acquisition/dilution risk removes any asymmetric appreciation case at the current price.

## Company Overview

Portland General Electric is a pure-play Oregon regulated electric utility operating a single reportable segment: generation, transmission, distribution, and retail sale of electricity, supplemented by wholesale-market participation to balance load. Its service territory spans 51 incorporated cities and roughly two million residents, with customer counts growing at approximately 1% annually. The company serves about two-thirds of Oregon's commercial and industrial activity, the source of its most important structural tailwind.

The generation mix as of Q1 2026 is natural gas (34% of system load), wind (8%), hydro (5%), coal via the Colstrip facility (5%), and purchased power (48%). The heavy reliance on purchased power exposes POR to wholesale price volatility and makes the company a net buyer that must actively manage market positions.

The regulatory framework is standard cost-of-service: the Public Utility Commission of Oregon (OPUC) sets retail prices with an authorized ROE (9.5% authorized in 2024; 9.34% in the recent distribution system plan mechanism), while FERC oversees wholesale and transmission activity. The economic engine is conversion of capital investment into rate base at the authorized return, the classic regulated-monopoly model. What differentiates POR from a typical single-state utility is the intensity of Oregon's decarbonization mandate (HB 2021 requires an 80% GHG cut by 2030 and 100% clean energy by 2040) and the scale of data-center-driven load growth, which together create an unusually long runway of required and recoverable capital spending.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue grew from ~$2.64 billion (FY22) to ~$3.55 billion (FY24), roughly +35% over the FY22–FY24 period, driven by rate increases and load growth. Net income was $233M (2022), $228M (2023), $313M (2024), and $306M (2025). Company-selected EPS ran $2.60 → $2.33 → $3.01 → $2.77, with lumpiness reflecting storm costs and regulatory disallowances rather than a smooth compounding trajectory.

**Q1 2026 — the key recent signal.** Net income fell to $45 million versus $100 million ($0.91) in Q1 2025, on revenue of $879 million (-5% YoY). The decline had three components worth separating. Approximately $17 million was "business transformation and optimization" expense (advisory, workforce realignment, holdco/acquisition costs), episodic. An unseasonably warm winter cut high-margin residential load (weather-adjusted residential deliveries -4.6%) and wholesale revenue fell 37%, cyclical/weather-driven. The $15 million of hard regulatory disallowances tied to OPUC orders on the January 2024 storm and RCE deferrals is a structural signal about the recovery climate, and it is the item that matters most.

**Earnings-surprise track record.** The recent print history has deteriorated: Q1 2026 missed by 34.6%, Q4 2025 missed by 26.1%, after in-line Q3 2025 (+2.4%) and Q2 2025 (+2.3%) results. Two beats and two misses across four quarters, with the misses concentrated in the two most recent and largest. This lowers confidence in near-term consensus and is consistent with the current-quarter estimate being revised down 9.5% over 30 days.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets are $13.2 billion, long-term debt $4.66 billion, total equity $4.12 billion. The common equity ratio of 46.9% sits below the company's 50% target, a direct signal of equity-issuance pressure. Debt-to-total-cap on the credit-agreement basis is 53.1% versus a 65% covenant limit, leaving headroom. Credit ratings are Moody's A3 / S&P BBB+, both stable, with capacity to issue up to $972 million of additional first mortgage bonds. For a rate-regulated utility, this leverage profile is normal, not distressed; the low Altman Z-score (~1.1) reflects the business model, not solvency risk.

**Cash flow and capital intensity — the core structural story.** Operating cash flow was positive ($268M in Q1 2026), but the capital program dwarfs it: ~$1.7 billion planned for 2026, within the $1.3–1.7 billion/year range through 2030. POR is a deeply negative-free-cash-flow utility in a capital cycle. The gap is closed through continuous equity dilution. Shares outstanding rose from ~109 million (December 2024) to ~115.7 million (March 2026), roughly +6% in fifteen months. Multiple equity vehicles are live simultaneously: a $500 million ATM program (February 2026) and an equity forward sale agreement for ~10.85 million shares at $50.70 (settleable by February 2028, ~$530 million). The quarterly dividend is $0.525, raised from $0.500.

The financing complexity is unusually high. POR is running, at once, the $500M ATM, the ~$530M equity forward, a ~$1.3B acquisition bridge, a ~$700M delayed-draw term loan, and a $350M term loan, all while the equity ratio sits below target. The higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of financing this program and is a direct headwind to the negative-FCF model. The Manulife JV (up to $600M) on the PacifiCorp deal limits POR's own equity check, but the overall picture is a company that must repeatedly return to capital markets, diluting per-share metrics even as the rate base and absolute earnings grow.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is legitimate and multi-sourced. HB 2021 creates a legally required, long-dated capital program, and data-center/industrial load growth provides the demand backdrop that makes rate-base expansion recoverable. Industrial deliveries rose ~10% YoY on digital-services demand. This is the same regulated-utility data-center dynamic confirmed elsewhere in the sector, where commercial kilowatt-hour growth and contracted large-load pipelines are converting AI-driven demand into rate-base plans.

The procurement pipeline is substantial. The 2023 RFP delivered 615 MW of solar-plus-storage (Biglow $540M, Wheatridge Expansion $490M). The 2025 All-Source RFP shortlist targets ~2,500 MW total, with OPUC acknowledgement expected in May 2026, plus three battery-storage PPAs with ~$1.6 billion aggregate lease consideration signed in early 2026. Transmission ambitions include a 20% stake in the ~$3.2 billion North Plains Connector HVDC line (supported by a $700M DOE GRIP grant) and the Warm Springs Power Pathway ($250M DOE grant, POR as subrecipient).

The PacifiCorp Washington acquisition is the largest single growth lever and the most uncertain. POR agreed to buy select PacifiCorp Washington retail operations for cash: ~140,000 customers, three generating plants (Chehalis 477 MW gas, Goodnoe Hills 94 MW wind, Marengo I&II 234 MW wind), 4,500 miles of transmission and distribution across ~2,700 square miles. The deal would run as a separate WUTC-regulated subsidiary with Manulife/John Hancock as a minority JV partner funding up to $600M. Management explicitly structured the transaction so that Washington and Oregon customers "would not be impacted by acquisition/financing costs," which means accretion must come from operating synergies and rate-base growth rather than cost pass-through, a higher execution bar.

Consensus estimate revisions are flat. Current-year EPS ($3.40) is essentially unchanged over 90 days (-0.5%), and next-year ($3.59) is flat. The current quarter was cut 9.5% over 30 days on the weak print, though next quarter was revised up modestly (+2.6% over 30 days / +4.8% over 90 days). There is no earnings-inflection signal here, no rapidly rising estimates that would justify a growth re-rating. The growth story is a steady mid-single-digit rate-base compounder whose per-share expression is capped by dilution. The rate-base plan implies an EPS trajectory that requires favorable regulatory outcomes and successful, non-dilutive-to-earnings acquisition integration to materialize.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.05, POR trades at approximately 23.4x trailing and forward earnings, with a ~4.2% dividend yield. The peer set of electric utilities (TXNM 42.1x, IDA 23.6x, OTTR 13.3x, MGEE 19.7x, HE 17.9x) carries a median P/E of 19.7x and a median yield of 2.5%. POR trades at roughly a 19% premium to the peer-median multiple while offering a superior yield, suggesting the market is already crediting the growth runway.

The critical point for scoring is that a compressed or premium multiple must be justified by a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock, and here the evidence cuts the other way. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising >30%, so the cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not apply. On flat forward estimates ($3.40 current year, $3.59 next year) at a peer-premium multiple, POR looks fairly-to-fully valued rather than mispriced. The analyst target set (mean $53.09, median $54.00, high $62.00, low $43.00) places the current price essentially at the mean, ~4% below the median, with the low target only 17% below spot. This is a stock priced close to consensus, not one where analyst targets act as a floor pointing to under-appreciated upside.

The valuation dimension is the weakest link in the case. Utilities as a group carry negative selection alpha historically, and a premium multiple with no catalyst for expansion, flat estimates, structural dilution capping per-share growth, and a tougher regulator argues against rewarding the current price. The ~4.2% yield provides absolute support and a floor near the low-$40s (the analyst low target and where the stock traded in mid-2025), but it does not create asymmetric upside. We do not treat the premium multiple as a reason to expect further appreciation, nor as a value trap; it reflects a fairly-valued regulated compounder with the dividend doing most of the work.

## Competitive Landscape

POR's competitive position is the classic regulated-monopoly franchise with no direct retail competition within its territory. The relevant question is trajectory, and here the picture is balanced-to-slightly-tightening. The structural tailwind is real: HB 2021 plus data-center demand give POR a long runway, and it serves roughly two-thirds of Oregon's C&I activity, which concentrates the state's load-growth benefit in its rate base.

The offsetting structural tension is affordability politics. Oregon passed HB 3179 (multi-year rate plans, no residential price increases November 1–March 31) and HB 3546 (data-center cost allocation) in 2025 specifically because of rising bills. The OPUC has become measurably more aggressive on earnings tests and disallowances; the March 2026 orders and the RCE non-renewal are the clearest evidence. A more assertive regulator in a cost-conscious political environment modestly erodes the recovery certainty that underpins a regulated utility's moat. Data-center concentration drives load growth but introduces stranded-asset and cost-allocation risk that OPUC Docket UM 2377 and HB 3546 are actively reshaping.

POR participates in CAISO's Western EIM and is joining the Extended Day-Ahead Market (EDAM, ~2026), having withdrawn from WRAP in October 2025 to pursue an EDAM-aligned resource-adequacy framework. The PacifiCorp acquisition would extend the franchise into Washington for the first time, adding scale but also multi-jurisdiction regulatory exposure. On net, the moat is intact but the recovery-certainty component is under mild pressure, which is why competitive-position scoring has drifted lower over recent reads rather than reflecting a clearly improving position.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory recovery / disallowance risk (dominant).** POR carries ~$795 million of regulatory assets. The Q1 2026 disallowances, the OPUC earnings tests, the RCE non-renewal, and HB 3179 recovery-timing constraints combine here. Multiple pending items (2025/2026 wildfire mitigation O&M, Clearwater RAC, DSP earnings test) carry explicit "could be charged to earnings" language. This is the single most important variable to monitor, and the recent trend is unfavorable.

**Wildfire liability.** Oregon-specific and severe. POR faces a $375 million wrongful-death suit tied to the January 2024 winter storm (three fatalities), with wildfire mitigation spend of ~$47–50M O&M plus $70–84M capital annually. Damages "may not be recoverable through prices or insurance."

**Portland Harbor Superfund.** POR is one of 100+ potentially responsible parties; EPA cleanup is estimated at $1.9–3.5 billion total, with POR's share undetermined but potentially "material to POR's financial position." EPA issued a Special Notice Letter in November 2024; negotiations are expected to conclude by ~fall 2026/March 2027. The PHERA recovery mechanism mitigates but does not eliminate this, and it is subject to earnings-test limits.

**PacifiCorp acquisition execution and financing.** Seven-state approval (WA, OR, ID, UT, CA, WY plus FERC/HSR), Berkshire Hathaway Energy on the other side, a ~12-month close, and permanent financing in a high-rate environment. Meaningful deal risk with a $35M termination fee.

**Colstrip and tax-credit risk.** POR's 20% Colstrip ownership is fully depreciated as of December 2025, but EPA MATS/GHG/ELG rules create regulatory whipsaw (current administration has proposed repeals and granted a MATS exemption to July 2029). The One Big Beautiful Bill Act accelerates wind/solar ITC/PTC phase-outs (placed-in-service deadline 12/31/2027), which could raise the cost of POR's renewable build.

Several of these tail liabilities (Portland Harbor, the wrongful-death suit) sit outside normal rate recovery and are hard to quantify, which is why the risk-factor score sits below neutral. The Q1 2026 10-Q states no material changes to 10-K risk factors but MD&A added the PacifiCorp acquisition as a forward-looking risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is cautionary. Near-term ATM IV of 33.3% is rich against 18.8% one-year historical volatility (a 14.5-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.3% near-term versus 22.6% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress. The one-month put/call skew is elevated at +6.0%, signaling put demand ahead of the next earnings print and the June/August regulatory and holdco-reorganization catalysts. ATM IV jumped 10 points (23.3% → 33.3%) over the prior month on a nearly flat price, consistent with rising uncertainty into a cluster of binary events (holdco order targeted August 25, 2026; PacifiCorp approval process; next earnings). Short interest is moderate at 7.8% of float (5.2 days to cover), not a crowded short. The options tape supports caution near-term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$62, high analyst target).** OPUC approves the pending large-load tariffs and rate cases constructively, the disallowance episode proves transient, the PacifiCorp acquisition closes on schedule with visible synergies, and the rate-base plan compounds EPS at the targeted mid-to-high single digits. In this scenario the yield plus rate-base growth and modest multiple support drive total return toward the low-$60s over 18-24 months. This requires a friendlier regulator than the March 2026 orders suggest and clean acquisition execution.

**Bear case (~$43, low analyst target, ~-17%).** The OPUC continues aggressive earnings tests and disallowances, HB 3179/3546 compress recovery timing further, the PacifiCorp deal encounters approval delays or requires a larger equity check that deepens dilution, and higher-for-longer rates raise financing costs on the negative-FCF program. A tail liability (Portland Harbor share or the wrongful-death suit) surprises to the downside. In this scenario the multiple compresses toward the peer median (~19.7x on ~$3.40) and the stock drifts toward the low-$40s, with the dividend providing a floor.

**Base case (~$53-55).** Most likely, POR continues as a fairly-valued regulated compounder: rate base grows, absolute earnings advance, dilution caps per-share growth, the regulatory climate stays cautious but not hostile, and the acquisition closes with modest accretion. Total return is roughly the dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation, landing near the analyst mean/median over twelve months. This is a yield-driven hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Twelve months, with two near-term checkpoints — the next quarterly earnings print (testing whether Q1's disallowance-driven weakness persists) and the OPUC holdco-reorganization order targeted August 25, 2026, followed by PacifiCorp approval milestones through late 2026/early 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A constructive OPUC rate-case/large-load tariff outcome that reverses the recent disallowance trend, combined with current-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive.
- The PacifiCorp acquisition clearing key state commissions with financing structured to avoid material additional common-equity dilution (i.e., Manulife/debt-weighted), demonstrating credible accretion.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that restores an asymmetric yield-plus-growth entry (yield toward 5%, multiple toward peer median).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continued or escalating OPUC disallowances/earnings-test denials that structurally impair recovery of the capex program.
- A quantified, materially adverse outcome on Portland Harbor (POR share sized higher than expected outside rate recovery) or the $375M wrongful-death suit.
- Acquisition financing that forces a step-change in equity issuance beyond the current ATM/forward, or a deal collapse that strands transformation costs, alongside a dividend-coverage deterioration.

## Conclusion

Portland General is a financially sound, investment-grade Oregon regulated utility with a legitimate secular growth runway from decarbonization mandates and data-center load. That runway is broad-based, but its per-share expression is capped by structural, ongoing equity dilution, and its recovery certainty is under pressure from an increasingly cost-conscious OPUC; the Q1 2026 disallowances and RCE non-renewal are genuine yellow flags, not noise. The PacifiCorp Washington acquisition adds upside optionality but front-loads seven-state approval, financing, and integration risk during a higher-for-longer rate environment.

At $52.05, the stock trades at a premium to peers (~23.4x versus 19.7x median) with flat estimate revisions and no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion. The dividend and A3/BBB+ balance sheet support the shares and justify continued ownership for income, but the risk/reward is symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable: upside to consensus is limited (~4% to median target) while a tougher regulator, acquisition uncertainty, and two large tail liabilities cap the appreciation case. The score trajectory has been declining (5.9 → 5.0 over three months), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift: weaker recent prints, a demanding regulatory climate, and financing complexity offset the intact growth narrative.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Own POR for the dividend and rate-base compounding, not for price appreciation. Reassess on the next earnings print, the August holdco order, and PacifiCorp approval progress. A pullback toward the low-$40s or a confirmed constructive regulatory turn would be required to make the appreciation case compelling.
