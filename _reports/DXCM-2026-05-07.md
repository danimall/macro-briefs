## Executive Summary

Dexcom is a leading company in continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) with $4.66B in 2025 revenue, 60% gross margins, and a strong franchise in a structurally growing market. The stock has declined 32% from its 52-week high following a sequence of product quality concerns: an FDA warning letter citing manufacturing process and quality management system deficiencies, a Hunterbrook investigative report, and a securities class action lawsuit. These events compressed the multiple from mid-30s P/E to approximately 26x trailing and 22x free cash flow.

The Q1 2026 report (April 30) demonstrated that Dexcom's business continues to execute. Revenue grew 15% YoY, adjusted EPS beat by 17%, and management raised operating margin guidance to 23-23.5%. Incoming CEO Jake Leach purchased $1M in open-market stock near the November 2025 lows, a strong insider signal from the executive most directly responsible for operations and quality systems. The combination of mid-teens growth, expanding margins, reasonable valuation relative to the growth profile, and a potential path to quality issue resolution creates an attractive risk/reward at current levels.

The primary risks are real: the FDA warning letter remains unresolved with no assured timeline for resolution, CMS competitive bidding will compress Medicare pricing starting 2028, and GLP-1 therapies create long-term uncertainty about CGM demand in Type 2 populations. These risks justify a discount to historical multiples but appear overpriced at current levels given the demonstrated earnings power.

## Company Overview

DexCom, Inc. designs, manufactures, and commercializes continuous glucose monitoring systems. The company's CGM sensors are small, wearable devices that track interstitial glucose levels and transmit data to smartphones and connected devices.

**Product Portfolio:**
- **Dexcom G7** — Flagship prescription CGM, 10-day sensor wear
- **Dexcom G7 15 Day** — Extended-wear version launched late 2025
- **Stelo** — First FDA-cleared OTC glucose biosensor (launched August 2024), targeting prediabetes and non-insulin Type 2
- **Dexcom ONE/ONE+** — Value-tier CGM for international markets

**Revenue Mix:** U.S. 72% ($3.33B), International 28% ($1.33B). Distribution is primarily through distributors (85%), with certain partners exceeding 10% of total revenue individually.

**Market Context:** The global CGM market is expanding from its historical Type 1 insulin-dependent base into the much larger Type 2 and prediabetic populations (~40M Americans with diabetes, ~100M with prediabetes). Dexcom competes primarily with Abbott's FreeStyle Libre family (approximately $7.6B in CGM sales in 2025) and Medtronic's Guardian/Simplera systems. While Abbott commands larger overall CGM revenue globally, Dexcom maintains leadership in real-time CGM accuracy and integration with automated insulin delivery systems.

## Competitive Landscape

**Competitive Position Assessment:** Dexcom maintains leadership in real-time CGM accuracy and integration with automated insulin delivery systems. Abbott's FreeStyle Libre family commands larger overall CGM revenue globally, but Dexcom holds a strong position in the real-time CGM and AID integration segments. The Abbott patent settlement (December 2024) removed litigation distraction and established a 10-year coexistence framework.

**Moat Components:**
1. **Regulatory moat** — FDA clearance process creates 2-3 year barriers for new entrants
2. **Clinical data** — Largest body of published outcomes data supporting reimbursement decisions
3. **AID ecosystem** — Integration with Tandem, Insulet's Omnipod, and other pump systems creates switching costs
4. **Installed base growth** — Approximately 600-700K net new Dexcom customers (excluding Stelo) added in 2025
5. **Patent portfolio** — Cross-license with Abbott through 2034 creates mutual deterrence

**Competitive Threats:**
- Abbott's Libre 3 at lower price points in developed markets
- Medtronic's Simplera targeting integrated pump users
- Consumer tech entrants (Apple, Samsung interest in non-invasive glucose monitoring — still years from clinical-grade accuracy)
- Chinese competitors in emerging markets

The competitive trajectory is stable. The Abbott settlement removed the most acute IP risk. The FDA warning letter is company-specific and doesn't benefit competitors structurally.

## Risk Assessment

**1. FDA Warning Letter (HIGH — active, uncertain resolution timeline):** The March 2025 warning letter followed inspections at Dexcom's San Diego and Mesa, Arizona facilities and cited observed non-conformities in manufacturing processes and quality management systems. According to Dexcom's 8-K filing, the letter does not currently restrict production, marketing, manufacturing, distribution, or new 510(k) submissions. However, Dexcom has stated it cannot assure that the FDA will be satisfied with its responses or provide an expected resolution date, and additional regulatory action may be taken without further notice. If the situation escalates to a consent decree or production restrictions, it would materially impact the investment thesis.

**2. CMS Competitive Bidding (MEDIUM-HIGH — structural, distant):** Medicare reimbursement compression starting 2028 is the most significant long-term overhang. Pricing at the 75th percentile of winning bids will almost certainly reduce per-unit revenue. Offset partially by: volume growth, cost reduction, pharmacy channel diversification, and the 2+ year runway to prepare.

**3. Class Action Litigation (MEDIUM — uncertain financial impact):** Filed October 27, 2025, covering purchases between July 26, 2024 and September 17, 2025. The lawsuit alleges Dexcom made materially false/misleading statements regarding G7 accuracy, reliability, functionality, enhancements, and manufacturing. Given Dexcom's D&O insurance and cash position, and that the company has not quantified expected damages or settlement probability in its filings, the financial impact remains uncertain but creates headline risk and management distraction.

**4. GLP-1 Competition (MEDIUM — long-term uncertainty):** If GLP-1s reduce the perceived need for glucose monitoring in Type 2 populations, Dexcom's addressable market expansion could be limited. However, management positions CGM as complementary to GLP-1s (monitoring treatment effectiveness), and the Stelo OTC launch specifically targets this population.

**5. Gross Margin Pressure (LOW-MEDIUM):** Manufacturing scale-up in Malaysia and Ireland creates near-term yield challenges. Gross margin declined 40 bps in 2025. The Malaysia tax holiday and production efficiency gains should reverse this trajectory in 2026-2027.

**6. Medicaid/ACA Coverage Erosion:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's projected Medicaid spending reductions and potential increase in uninsured (CBO estimates approximately 7.8M additional uninsured by 2034 from Medicaid provisions) could meaningfully shrink Dexcom's covered-lives base. Partial offset from pharmacy/OTC channel expansion.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: ATM implied volatility appeared elevated relative to historical volatility as of recent observation, with the term structure in backwardation — indicating the market expects near-term event risk that may resolve rather than persist. Put/call open interest ratios confirm elevated protective positioning, though volume ratios suggest recent flow is more balanced. These are time-sensitive market observations and should be interpreted cautiously; elevated near-term volatility could reflect earnings, macro, sector rotation, or FDA-related event risk rather than any single catalyst. The backwardation structure is more consistent with hedging around a known risk than with structural bearishness.

## Conclusion

Dexcom at $61.38 represents a quality medtech franchise trading at a depressed multiple due to identifiable product quality concerns with an uncertain but potentially manageable resolution path. The stock offers 22x FCF for a business growing revenue 12-15% with expanding margins (19.6% → 23-23.5% guided) and a strong competitive position in a structurally growing TAM. The incoming CEO's $1M open-market purchase at $55 provides a direct insider signal that the quality issues are fixable. The 2.3:1 risk/reward ratio supports a BUY rating with standard 2-3% position sizing. The primary risks — FDA resolution, CMS competitive bidding, GLP-1 competition — are real but appear overpriced at current valuations relative to the earnings trajectory.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The FDA warning letter resolution is treated as a base-case assumption but Dexcom's SEC filings explicitly state the company cannot assure FDA satisfaction or provide an expected resolution timeline; if resolution is prolonged or escalates, the base-case target price and risk/reward ratio would be materially impaired.
