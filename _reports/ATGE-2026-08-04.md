## Executive Summary

Covista operates five Title IV–participating institutions across three segments: Chamberlain University (nursing, campus plus online), Walden University (predominantly online adult and graduate), and Medical & Veterinary (AUC, Ross Medical, Ross Veterinary, offshore). Fiscal year ends June 30. Nine-month FY2026 revenue was $1,452.7M, up 9.1%, with adjusted EBITDA of $394.8M (27.2% margin, up 90bp) and adjusted diluted EPS of $6.16 versus $5.01. Management raised FY2026 guidance on May 7, 2026 to revenue of $1.93–1.945B and adjusted EPS of $7.95–8.15, implying Q4 revenue of $477–492M (+4.4% to +7.7%) and Q4 adjusted EPS of $1.79–1.99 (+7% to +19%).

The balance sheet is the strongest element. The March 2, 2026 refinancing replaced the residual old term loan and the entire $405.0M of 5.50% Senior Secured Notes due 2028 with a single $510.0M Term Loan B maturing March 2033, leaving net debt of $363.0M, roughly 0.7x net leverage on TTM adjusted EBITDA of about $519M, an undrawn $500M revolver to August 2030, and no maturity wall for seven years. Against that, the board authorized a **$750.0M** repurchase program in December 2025 — five times the size of the program it replaced — with $661.8M remaining at March 31, 2026, approximately 15% of the equity at the reference price. Weighted diluted shares fell 9.0% year over year in Q3.

What has changed since the prior reviews is the composition of the business and the clarity of the entry case. Walden is now larger than Chamberlain on revenue ($594.1M versus $560.0M for nine months) and far larger on profit (segment adjusted EBITDA $198.3M versus $138.8M), so the "leading healthcare educator" framing carries less of the earnings than it did. Walden's graduate-heavy portfolio in education, counseling, psychology, social work, and public administration is the mix most exposed to the OBBBA "Do No Harm" test, under which a graduate program can lose Title IV eligibility if completers' earnings fail to exceed a bachelor's-degree comparison population for two years in three, with implementing regulations still unwritten. At the same time Chamberlain, the segment that anchors the brand, has decelerated to roughly flat session enrollment (−1.0%, −0.7%, +0.5% in the last three sessions) with nine-month adjusted operating income down 8.8% and margin down 250bp, amid a confirmed CMO departure and an apparent (unconfirmed) president transition. At 16.5x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS (17.8x if the growing "strategic advisory costs" are treated as ordinary opex), the multiple no longer embeds a verifiable margin of safety against a binary regulatory risk stack. **HOLD.**

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the Q3 timing distortion.** Q3 FY2026 revenue was $487.0M, +4.5%. A one-academic-week calendar shift at Walden moved $18.0M of revenue out of Q3 and into Q2; adjusted, Walden Q3 grew 14.7% and consolidated grew 8.4%. The shift flatters Q2 and depresses Q3 symmetrically, so the nine-month figures are the cleaner read and the FY2026 guidance is undistorted.

**Margins.** GAAP operating income was $287.9M (19.8%) versus $264.6M (19.9%), flat on a GAAP basis while adjusted margin expanded 70bp to 21.9%. That gap is the quality-of-earnings question. The $1.17 bridge between nine-month GAAP diluted EPS of $4.99 and adjusted EPS of $6.16 (**19.0% of adjusted EPS**) comprises discontinued-operations loss $0.43, strategic advisory costs $0.47, acquired-intangible amortization $0.23, restructuring $0.15, debt extinguishment and other $0.13, less $(0.24) tax effect.

Two items deserve scrutiny. **Strategic advisory costs** rose from $5.1M (nine-month FY2025, all in Q3) to **$17.0M (nine-month FY2026, including $7.2M in Q3)** and are now in their sixth consecutive quarter, defined only as costs "related to expanding capabilities and bringing new capacities to market." A cost line that recurs and grows across six quarters and is described as building capabilities has the character of growth operating expense. Treated that way, nine-month adjusted EPS would be roughly $5.69 and the FY2026 multiple about a point higher. The disclosure is genuinely ambiguous — these could be transaction fees tied to the strategic-alternatives review disclosed separately in MD&A, which would legitimately be non-recurring on conclusion or abandonment. I do not attribute motive; excluding advisory fees is common practice. But the item is material and unresolved, and it is excluded from two of the four metrics (adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA margin) that determine executive pay.

**Discontinued operations** swung from +$4.6M income to a −$15.4M loss, including −$16.3M in Q3 alone, driven by litigation and settlements on prior divestitures under the 2017 Cogswell Stock Purchase Agreement (DeVry indemnity, $340.0M liability cap, partially consumed). These are add-back-eligible under the company's definition, but the underlying obligations are cash the company owes. Separately, the DeVry earn-out completed at $20.0M with a final $0.5M in Q2 FY2026, so a small recurring income item has permanently ended.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow from continuing operations was $346.4M, up 26.5%, driven by $125.3M more collected from students, $22.9M lower net legal settlement payments, and lower interest and tax payments, partly offset by $82.1M more paid to employees and vendors. Capex was $50.9M versus $31.3M. Derived nine-month free cash flow is **$295.5M**.

That figure should not be annualized, for three reasons. Collections are explicitly seasonal and peak at term starts. Capex is ramping into Q4 on the six-campus build. And a large share of the tax provision was deferred rather than paid: the nine-month provision was $61.5M while the cash-flow statement added back **$65.3M of deferred taxes** (versus $20.0M a year earlier), and the net deferred position swung roughly $59.8M toward liability (from a net ~$1.1M asset at June 30, 2025 to zero asset and a $58.7M liability at March 31, 2026). Mechanically, close to the entire book provision appears to have been deferred, consistent with OBBBA accelerated deductions for qualified property and R&D. Alternative explanations cannot be excluded — valuation-allowance movement or excess tax benefits on the unusually large FY2026 PSU vestings ($20.4M grant-date value versus $2.8M). Charging cash taxes at the ~24% effective rate would reduce nine-month FCF by roughly $60M to approximately **$235M**. Management cited TTM FCF of $336M, up 17%, on the Q3 call — the unadjusted figure.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $2,737.4M; cash $147.0M plus $1.9M restricted; liabilities $1,373.3M; equity $1,364.1M. Goodwill of $961.3M plus intangibles of $757.1M equals $1,718.4M, **62.8% of assets**, making derived tangible book equity **negative $354.3M**. Goodwill concentrates in Walden ($651.1M) and Med/Vet ($305.5M). The $611.1M of indefinite-lived "Title IV eligibility and accreditations" and $141.8M of trade names are purchased regulatory standing, a real asset that impairs if the regulatory standing degrades.

The current ratio of 0.70x is a model artifact. $276.2M of current liabilities is deferred revenue, a performance obligation to deliver instruction already paid for; excluding it, the ratio is 1.33x. Standardized distress screens will read this business as far weaker than 0.7x net leverage and 8.1x interest coverage indicate. On the other side, deferred revenue is **not backlog**: $75.0M of the $305.5M total represents contract liabilities for material rights (future scholarships and discounts the company expects to grant over roughly four years), which is a future price concession, not contracted revenue. No RPO or backlog metric is disclosed.

Off-balance-sheet: $202.6M of surety-backed letters of credit in favor of ED, expiring **January 31, 2027** and requiring renewal, plus $76.9M of state licensure surety bonds and $236.8M of present-value operating lease liabilities.

**Debt.** The March 2, 2026 refinancing is the cleanest management action in the file: repaid $103.3M of old term loan, redeemed the remaining $405.0M of 5.50% notes at par and discharged the indenture, and incurred a $510.0M Term Loan B due March 2033 at SOFR + 2.25% (5.92% at March 31), priced at 99.5, with $1.275M quarterly amortization from September 2026. Total principal fell from $558.3M to $510.0M, the 2028 wall is gone, and the structure is single-tranche with prepayment flexibility. Covenant: Total Net Leverage ≤ 3.25x on the revolver, in compliance.

The trade-off is understated in management's "lower rate" framing. The margin did fall 50bp on the term loan, but 5.92% floating **exceeds** the 5.50% fixed coupon retired, and essentially the entire stack is now floating with no disclosed hedges. That is a live and unhedged exposure of roughly $5.1M of annual pre-tax interest per 100bp on $510M, about 1.5% of nine-month operating income. Manageable, not free.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth engines run at different speeds and with different risk profiles.

**Walden is the engine and the exposure.** Revenue +16.2%, enrollment +12.3% to 54,474, adjusted operating margin up 300bp to 29.8%, adjusted EBITDA margin 33.4%. MD&A attributes it to student-experience and brand investment plus flexibility for working adults through part-time and Tempo Learning competency-based programs, and higher average credit hours per student. New clinical psychology and behavior analysis programs already carry more than 1,400 students, with seven additional program approvals. This is genuine operating momentum. It is also the profit pool most exposed to a graduate-earnings test, and competency-based and subscription modalities are the delivery models regulators scrutinize hardest on outcomes.

**Chamberlain is the problem.** Session growth decelerated from +12.1% (July 2024) and +11.7% (September 2024) to +4.5%, +2.2%, −1.0%, −0.7%, +0.5% across FY2026. MD&A is specific: pre-licensure grew every session while **post-licensure (RN-to-BSN) declined**, and "Management is focused on optimizing marketing and enrollment operations." On the Q3 call management described the post-licensure category as "structurally less robust than several years ago" while noting Chamberlain still leads it, a candid admission of leading a shrinking category. Segment adjusted operating income fell 8.8% to $107.3M on 3.4% revenue growth, with margin down 250bp on labor, marketing, and growth investment. Six new campuses are in development, two with regulatory approval, first teaching September 2026 (Cincinnati confirmed by a May 19, 2026 release). Capacity expansion into a supply-constrained pre-licensure market is the right call strategically; it also means segment margin likely compresses further before the campuses reach scale.

**Med/Vet is quietly the best-executing segment.** Revenue +7.2%, enrollment +4.1%, adjusted operating margin +170bp to 21.2%, aided by 4.5–4.6% tuition increases and Barbados/St. Kitts tax exemptions running to 2039 and 2038. Outcomes support pricing: 97% first-time residency attainment overall, AUC above 98%.

**Guidance and delivery.** Reverse-engineering the guidance raise (revenue $1.93–1.945B described as 8–9% growth; adjusted EPS $7.95–8.15 described as 19–22% growth) implies FY2025 revenue of ~$1.788B and FY2025 adjusted EPS of $6.68. Implied Q4 is revenue $477–492M and adjusted EPS $1.79–1.99, both above prior-year derived comparables, with the Walden week shift not affecting Q4 comparability. Management also flagged an effective tax rate above FY2025 and roughly 100bp of full-year adjusted EBITDA margin expansion.

The composition of that 19–22% adjusted EPS growth matters: revenue growth of 8–9% plus ~100bp margin expansion produces perhaps 12–14% of adjusted EBIT growth, with the remainder supplied by a 9% reduction in diluted shares. Buyback-levered EPS growth is real to shareholders but is not the same as business growth, and it is a metric management influences directly through capital allocation while being paid on it.

Two announced initiatives should be read narrowly. The **Advocate Health** collaboration (June 29, 2026 release) pairs Covista education with Advocate's clinical immersion sites, financial support, and employment pathways; Advocate is a commercial and employer counterparty, no committed enrollment or revenue is quantified, and management explicitly called employer partnerships "a longer-term upside lever rather than a near-term catalyst." The **Google Cloud** relationship is described in a third-party summary of the earnings call as "co-developing an AI-powered classroom" with pilots later in 2026 and 4,000+ learners in new AI credentials; it is unverified in any filing, the scope of "co-developing" is unstated, and Covista is the customer/partner while the educational product is Covista's. Neither belongs in a valuation case yet.

Sixteen and a half times forward adjusted earnings against 19–22% adjusted EPS growth screens as a PEG below 1.0, which on its face argues cheap. Three considerations pull the score down from where that arithmetic alone would put it.

First, the denominator is soft. Roughly $0.60 of the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint is attributable to advisory costs excluded from a metric that also drives incentive compensation; treating them as opex takes the multiple to 17.8x. And the EPS growth is substantially manufactured by the buyback rather than by operations, so the "growth-adjusted" comparison flatters.

Second, no independent valuation anchor is available. There are no analyst price targets in the dataset, no peer benchmark table, and no market quote. The only external valuation opinion located is a June 3, 2026 Seeking Alpha piece rating the stock Hold on the view that it "prices in much of its growth story" and "trades near peer" — a tier-3 source, but the only valuation-skeptical read available, and consistent with my own derivation. For-profit education peers historically clear in the low-to-high teens on forward earnings; 16.5x is inside that band, not below it.

Third, and most important: the discount to the broad market is compensation for a specific, live regulatory option that has no expiry and no quantifiable strike. A multiple that looks reasonable relative to growth may still be the correct multiple when a graduate-earnings eligibility test with unwritten rules sits on top of 41% of revenue and roughly half of segment profit. Cheapness without an identified reason the market is wrong is not an asymmetry.

What does support the price is the capital-return arithmetic. A 6.4–6.9% normalized FCF yield with $661.8M of authorization equal to ~15% of the equity, 0.7x net leverage, and no maturities until 2033 gives management the ability to shrink the share count 5–7% annually without touching the balance sheet. The company demonstrated price discipline in executing it, buying more heavily into the February–March weakness ($112.91 average in January, $98.66 in February, $100.16 in March). That mechanism is the primary reason a HOLD here is a growth hold rather than a wait.

**Valuation score: 5.5.** Reasonable, not mispriced, with a soft earnings denominator and no verifiable current price.

## Competitive Landscape

**Regulation is both the moat and the sword.** The $611.1M of purchased Title IV eligibility and accreditation intangibles is an explicit market price for regulatory standing that a new entrant cannot buy. Multi-year accreditation and state-authorization timelines are not compressible with capital, which is why the sector consolidates through acquisition — Adtalem's own 2021 Walden purchase from Laureate being the case in point. The identical barrier cuts the other way: five institutions on provisional certification, one PPA expired, two institutions four points from a 90/10 cliff, 15,300 unadjudicated borrower-defense claims, and a statutory earnings test with unwritten rules.

**The nursing-education market has bifurcated, and Covista leads the wrong half.** Pre-licensure BSN is supply-constrained by clinical placements and campus capacity, which is why Covista is building six campuses and can cite fifteen consecutive quarters of pre-licensure growth. Post-licensure upskilling is the opposite: the addressable pool of ADN-holding RNs seeking a BSN has been substantially harvested, and employer tuition benefits plus BSN-preferred hiring have shifted the economics. Chamberlain's 250bp nine-month margin decline is the financial signature of leading a decelerating category while spending to build capacity in an adjacent one.

**Profit and regulatory exposure now sit in the same place.** Walden's share of nine-month revenue rose from 38.4% to 40.9%, which makes the portfolio more concentrated in a single institution, not less, and on the measure that governs this business, the consolidated 90/10 rate moved from 77% to 78%. Mix shift toward Walden is not diversification. Walden's field mix (education, counseling, psychology, social work and human services, public administration) is where graduate earnings premia over a bachelor's degree are thinnest, which is the "Do No Harm" comparison. This is inference, not established fact — the final rule's cohort definitions, exclusions, and comparison-population construction could be drawn narrowly enough that few or no programs fail, and the company says it cannot yet assess impact.

**Balance-sheet flexibility is a competitive weapon.** At 0.7x net leverage, no maturities before 2033, an undrawn $500M revolver, and $661.8M of authorization, Covista has more optionality than most of the sector. MD&A states the company "has engaged in and continue[s] to engage in the review and planning of strategic alternatives to refinance or otherwise optimize our capital structure," which "could result in our pursuing one or more significant corporate transactions" — an open-ended disclosure worth monitoring.

**Trajectory verdict:** Walden and Med/Vet improving, Chamberlain deteriorating, and the mix concentrating toward the most regulator-exposed asset. Net trajectory is mixed to slightly negative, a downgrade from the prior reviews' 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Provisional certification across all five institutions, with a $202.6M letter-of-credit requirement expiring January 31, 2027.** ED notified the company in September 2023 that its FY2022 composite score had fallen to **0.2** (against a 1.5 full-responsibility threshold; below 1.0 an institution is not considered financially responsible), an expected consequence of the Walden acquisition. Provisional certification "carries fewer due process protections," and ED "may seek to revoke the institution's certification... without advance notice or opportunity for the institution to challenge the action." The LCs equal 10% of prior-year Title IV receipts and require renewal in under six months.

**2. RUSM has operated on an expired Program Participation Agreement since March 31, 2025.** A timely, materially complete recertification application permits continued Title IV access pending decision, but no decision has come in over a year. Chamberlain (February 2026), Walden, and AUC (March 2026) are recertified through December 2028; RUSVM through March 2027.

**3. 90/10 headroom is thinnest where growth is.** FY2025: Chamberlain 70%, RUSVM 77%, Walden 82%, **AUC 86%, RUSM 86%**, consolidated 78%. Two consecutive years above 90% costs Title IV eligibility outright. AUC and RUSM have four points of cushion, and growth in federally-funded enrollment mechanically pushes the ratio the wrong way.

**4. "Do No Harm" is the largest un-quantified overhang.** Graduate and professional programs can lose eligibility if completers' earnings fail to exceed a bachelor's-degree comparison population for two years in three. Regulations are unwritten; negotiated rulemaking convened in December 2025 and January 2026. This concentrates on Walden's portfolio, which is 41% of revenue and roughly half of segment profit.

**5. Borrower-defense claims: 15,300 outstanding, none adjudicated** (Walden 7,804; Chamberlain 3,144; RUSVM 2,013; RUSM 1,958; AUC 381). No approval notice or recoupment demand received to date. The genuine offset: OBBBA restored the 2019 BDR regulations and delayed the punitive 2022 regulations to **July 1, 2035**, removing the lower misrepresentation threshold, the absence of a claims statute of limitations, and reinstated group discharge — provisions the company itself said "would increase financial liability risk and reputational risk." This is a material and underappreciated tail-risk reduction versus the 2023–2024 posture.

**6. Divestiture indemnities are producing current losses.** The DeVry indemnity carries a $340.0M liability cap, partially consumed. Discontinued operations swung to a −$15.4M nine-month loss including −$16.3M in Q3.

**7. Impairment exposure.** $1,718.4M of goodwill and intangibles against $1,364.1M of book equity means negative tangible book of −$354.3M. Any adverse regulatory determination affecting Walden's Title IV standing would test the $651.1M of Walden goodwill and a large share of the $611.1M Title IV intangible directly.

**8. All-floating, unhedged debt.** $510M at SOFR + 2.25%, no derivatives disclosed. The entire stack is now floating rather than the fixed 5.50% coupon retired, so interest expense moves directly with the SOFR path.

**9. Execution and personnel.** The 10-Q confirms the departure of the **former** CMO (Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted March 9, 2026), against MD&A's acknowledgment of marketing and enrollment-operations problems at Chamberlain. Form 4 data shows Karen Sue Cox filing as Chamberlain president in May 2026 and **Amelia Manning filing as Chamberlain president on July 15, 2026 with a first grant of 4,317 shares and post-transaction holdings of exactly 4,317**; an **8-K filed July 28, 2026 carries Item 5.02**. The most plausible reading is a leadership change at the segment with the enrollment and margin problem. I did not read the 8-K text and no announcement surfaced; treat as unconfirmed. It is the largest open verification gap in this analysis.

No options data was supplied, so no options-market signal is available.

**Risk score: 5.0.** Some of this stack is sector-priced. But the expired RUSM PPA, the 86% 90/10 rates at two named institutions, the composite score of 0.2 with revocation-without-notice authority, and the alignment of the unwritten "Do No Harm" test with the exact institution carrying half the profit are company-specific and not readily quantifiable. The OBBBA borrower-defense relief is the offset that keeps this at 5.0 rather than lower.

## Management Quality

**Capital allocation is the strong suit.** Nine-month FY2026 repurchases of 2,421,920 shares for $238.2M at an average $98.35, executed more heavily into the February–March drawdown. Shares outstanding fell from 35,952k to 34,023k; weighted diluted shares fell 9.0% year over year. Cumulatively the company has retired the equivalent of roughly 60% of shares ever issued. The March refinancing eliminated the 2028 wall, cut principal, and extended maturity seven years. Guidance was raised in May.

**Governance is weaker.** Stephen Beard holds a combined **Chairman and CEO** role. FY2025 CEO summary compensation was $17.24M, with "compensation actually paid" of $120.35M, an SEC pay-versus-performance construct dominated by mark-to-market equity revaluation, driven by cumulative TSR reaching 408 against a peer index of 213, not cash received. Two real shareholder costs follow: the 10-Q attributes the rise in effective tax rate (to 24.0% for nine months from 22.5%) "primarily due to an increase in the limitation of tax benefits on certain executive compensation" — the §162(m) cap biting — and employee withholding taxes on vesting shares rose from $12.5M to **$42.1M**, a real financing cash use. Pay metrics are revenue growth, revenue, adjusted EPS, and adjusted EBITDA margin; two of the four exclude the advisory costs that rose sharply year over year, and adjusted EPS is levered by a buyback management controls. That is a structural feature of most non-GAAP plans, not evidence of intent, but it is worth naming.

**Insider activity is two-sided, and the buy is the higher-information transaction.** Director Michael Malafronte made open-market purchases (code "P") on March 3, 10, and 11, 2026 totaling **37,236 shares for approximately $3.75M** at $97.97–$100.94, into the same post-rebrand weakness during which the company was repurchasing at $98.66 and $100.16. Reported post-transaction holdings across those filings are mutually inconsistent, so the aggregate position is unverified even though purchase quantities are clear. Against that, four executives sold roughly $7.3M between May and July 2026: CEO ~26,456 shares for ~$3.45M (a ~6% trim, retaining 418,742 shares), CFO ~15,314 for ~$1.98M, GC 14,704 for ~$1.91M, and the Chamberlain president 2,000. The cluster began the day after the May 7 guidance raise (the first open window), consistent with window-constrained diversification rather than a directional view, and the CEO retained a very large position. On the standard I apply, this selling is not materially bearish; the director's cash purchase is a genuine positive. But the selling has continued for three months and now includes two July transactions at $135+, which removes any argument that insiders view the current level as they viewed $100.

**Management Quality: 6.5**, down from 7.0. Capital allocation earns a high mark; combined chair/CEO, the non-GAAP-linked incentive structure, and a marketing leadership gap at the underperforming segment cap it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $190 (+43% from the $132.75 reference)

Revenue compounds 7–9% as six new Chamberlain campuses open from September 2026 into supply-constrained pre-licensure demand, Walden sustains low-double-digit enrollment growth on program expansion, and Med/Vet holds 21%+ margins under tax exemptions to 2038–2039. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $9.50 as adjusted EBITDA margin adds another 50–75bp and the share count falls a further 6–7% against $661.8M of authorization. Regulatory overhangs resolve favorably: RUSM is recertified, "Do No Harm" final rules draw comparison populations narrowly enough that Covista programs pass, and the composite score recovers enough to reduce or remove the $202.6M LC requirement. On resolution, the multiple re-rates from 16.5x toward 20x — 20x × $9.50 = **$190**. The Advocate Health pathway and any employer-funded enrollment would sit on top of this and are not in the base case.

### Bear Case — $95 (−28%)

"Do No Harm" draft rules capture a meaningful slice of Walden's education, counseling, psychology, and social-work graduate programs, putting 41% of revenue and roughly half of segment profit under an eligibility question that cannot be hedged. Or AUC/RUSM 90/10 rates drift from 86% toward 88–90%, forcing enrollment or discount decisions that compress Med/Vet margin. Or the January 2027 LC renewal proves costlier. Chamberlain's post-licensure decline continues while six campuses carry fixed costs pre-scale, taking segment margin below 18%. Adjusted EPS stalls near $8.00 and the multiple compresses to ~12x on regulatory uncertainty: **$95**, approximately the level at which the company and a director were buying in March 2026. A more severe scenario, in which an institution's Title IV eligibility is actually impaired, would test the $651.1M of Walden goodwill and take the equity materially below this, given negative tangible book.

### Base Case — $155 (+17%)

Regulatory status quo persists: RUSM continues on its pending application, "Do No Harm" rules remain unwritten or arrive with a long implementation runway, and the LCs renew at similar cost. This target is deliberately close to a status-quo multiple because the case for re-rating requires regulatory resolution, and the case for de-rating requires regulatory deterioration, neither of which is forecastable from the current disclosure.

## Conclusion

Covista is a well-financed, cash-generative operator with a defensible regulatory franchise, a capital structure recently reset to 0.7x net leverage with no maturities until 2033, and a repurchase authorization equal to roughly 15% of its equity that management has executed at sensible prices. Nine-month revenue grew 9.1% with 90bp of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, guidance was raised, and a director committed $3.75M of his own cash at ~$100 in March.

Three things argue against extending the prior BUY. The profit engine has migrated to Walden, the institution whose graduate field mix aligns most closely with an unwritten federal earnings test that could remove Title IV eligibility from specific programs, a binary that cannot be sized from current disclosure and sits against negative tangible book equity. Chamberlain, the segment that carries the healthcare-educator identity, has decelerated to flat with margin down 250bp, a departed CMO, and an apparent president change at a filing I could not read. And at 16.5x guided adjusted EPS (17.8x charging the recurring advisory costs as opex), the multiple sits inside the peer band rather than below it, with no independent analyst anchor and no authoritative price to verify against.

The overall score of 6.1 is down from 6.8, driven by an explicit markdown of risk factors (5.0) and valuation (5.5) rather than by any fundamental miss. Operationally the business delivered. What deteriorated is the clarity of the entry case and the concentration of profit into the most regulator-exposed asset. Existing holders have a reasonable 12-month path to roughly $155 on status-quo assumptions, supported by a 6.4–6.9% normalized FCF yield and share-count reduction. New capital should wait for either regulatory resolution or a price near $105–112.
