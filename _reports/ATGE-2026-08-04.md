## Executive Summary

The security requested as ATGE no longer trades under that symbol. Adtalem Global Education was renamed **Covista Inc.** effective February 5, 2026 (Restated Certificate of Incorporation dated 2/5/2026, disclosed via 8-K Items 5.03/7.01) and began trading as **CVSA** on the NYSE on February 24, 2026. The SEC registrant, CIK 730464, Delaware incorporation, and EIN 36-3150143 are unchanged. The empty market-data return for ATGE is consistent with the symbol change rather than with any corporate event, and any position or watchlist entry still tracked under ATGE requires re-mapping.

On fundamentals, the three-year record is strong and the forward setup is not. Revenue compounded 8.8% annually from FY2022 to FY2025 ($1,387.1M to $1,788.3M) while operating income rose 4.2x and operating margin expanded 1,330 basis points to 19.1%. Diluted share count fell 28.1% from FY2022 to the March 2026 quarter. Net debt is roughly $363M against a 3.25x covenant; on annualized 9M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$526M, leverage is about 0.7x, and on a more conservative ~$460M trailing estimate about 0.8x, with the nearest maturity now 2033. Nine-month FY2026 free cash flow of $295.5M represents a 20.3% FCF margin.

The forward risk concentrates in where the profit now lives and what happens to it on the first day of fiscal 2027. Walden — predominantly online, predominantly graduate — has overtaken Chamberlain as the largest segment ($594.1M vs. $560.0M of nine-month revenue) and now produces $176.9M of $347.5M in segment adjusted operating income, or 50.9%. Multiple outlets report that OBBBA eliminates Grad PLUS loans for new graduate borrowers and imposes new federal borrowing caps effective **July 1, 2026**, which is day one of Covista's FY2027. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q states there were *no material changes* to risk factors and provides no quantification of Walden's Grad PLUS exposure. Chamberlain's adjusted operating income fell 8.8% on 3.4% revenue growth, with adjusted EBITDA margin down 221bps, after eleven consecutive sessions of enrollment deceleration from +12.1% to +0.5%, including two outright declines. At an approximate reference price of $133 — 33% above where a director bought $3.75M of stock in March — the multiple no longer compensates for an unquantified, dated funding change at half the profit pool. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Covista operates five degree-granting institutions in three reportable segments plus an unallocated Home Office:

- **Chamberlain University** — nursing and health professions; campus-based pre-licensure BSN plus online post-licensure programs. 9M FY2026 revenue $560.0M (38.5% of total).
- **Walden University** — predominantly online graduate (master's and doctoral), acquired from Laureate in August 2021. 9M FY2026 revenue $594.1M (40.9%).
- **Medical & Veterinary** — AUC (St. Maarten), Ross University School of Medicine (Barbados), Ross University School of Veterinary Medicine (St. Kitts). 9M FY2026 revenue $298.6M (20.6%). Tax exemptions run to 2039 (Barbados) and 2038 (St. Kitts).

Revenue is essentially all tuition and fees ($1,446.5M of $1,452.7M in 9M FY2026). The structural feature that governs the entire risk profile: all five institutions participate in Title IV, which supplies **77% of consolidated revenue**, and the FY2025 10-K discloses that if any single Title IV institution cannot satisfy its Program Participation Agreement obligations, the others cannot maintain eligibility without assisting in repayment. A bankruptcy filing by Covista or any Title IV subsidiary triggers immediate statutory loss of Title IV eligibility across all institutions notwithstanding the automatic stay. The institutions are regulatorily cross-collateralized, so the distress channel is discontinuous rather than gradual.

Two qualifications. First, within that operating cash flow sits a **$65.3M non-cash deferred-income-tax add-back versus $20.0M** in the prior-year nine months — a $45.3M swing, corroborated by the $33.0M deferred tax asset at 6/30/2025 going to zero while the deferred tax liability rose from $31.9M to $58.7M. Second, **capex rose 63%** on IT and new Chamberlain campus development (Cincinnati announced 5/19/2026, plus one 17-year operating lease with a ~$4.1M asset/liability commencing in Q4 FY2026). ### Balance sheet

Walden carries $651.1M of the goodwill, Medical & Veterinary $305.5M, Chamberlain $4.7M. Indefinite-lived intangibles comprise $611.1M of Title IV eligibility/accreditations and $141.8M of trade name. The annual test date is May 31; the last completed test was 5/31/2025, and the Q3 FY2026 triggering-event assessment found no indicators.

Working capital is structurally negative — current assets $403.8M against current liabilities $580.4M, a current ratio of **0.70**, down from 0.82 and 0.86 in the two prior year-ends. This is normal for a tuition business (current deferred revenue rose to $276.2M from $214.1M), but the two-year trend is one-directional.

Gross accounts receivable rose to $230.1M from $189.9M, **+21.2% against 9.1% revenue growth**. The allowance rose to $56.8M from $46.4M, holding at 24.7% of gross AR, and the bad-debt provision was $48.9M, or 3.36% of revenue, slightly better than the prior year's 3.52%. Financing receivables more than 150 days past due were $8.4M of $35.2M (23.9%, roughly flat). The most likely explanation is enrollment growth and Walden disbursement timing rather than credit deterioration, since provision rate and coverage held. The alternative — student-financed balances pulling revenue recognition ahead of collection — resolves over two to three quarters and should be re-tested at Q4.

An earnings tailwind is embedded: amortizable Walden curriculum intangibles have only **$4.2M of remaining amortization** ($2.8M FY2026, $1.4M FY2027), so the $8.4M of nine-month acquired-intangible amortization largely disappears after FY2027.

### Capital structure

The 3/2/2026 refinancing repaid in full the remaining $405.0M of 5.50% Senior Secured Notes due 2028 and the legacy Term Loan B (amortized to $103.3M), replacing them with a **$510.0M Term Loan B due 3/2/2033** at SOFR + 2.25% (0.75% floor), issued at 99.5%, all-in 5.92% at 3/31/2026. Quarterly $1.275M installments begin 9/30/2026. The revolver was upsized $100M to **$500M** (Amendment No. 4, 8/6/2025) with maturity to 8/6/2030, undrawn with full availability; $337.0M was borrowed and repaid during the nine months. Covenant: total net leverage ≤3.25:1.00, in compliance.

Derived: prior annualized cash coupon ≈ $405.0M × 5.50% + $103.3M × ~6.4% ≈ **$28.9M**; new ≈ $510.0M × 5.92% ≈ **$30.2M**. Cash interest is flat to slightly higher and principal barely moved ($558.3M to $510.0M). What changed is maturity (2028→2033), rate form (fixed to floating), and prepayability. The floating conversion adds rate risk in a regime where the policy path is contested: $510M × 100bps = $5.1M pretax, about **$0.11 per diluted share** after tax.

Net debt = $510.0M − $147.0M = **$363.0M**. Against annualized 9M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$526M, net leverage is roughly **0.7x**; on a conservative trailing estimate near $460M it is ~0.8x with gross leverage ~1.1x. Headroom to the 3.25x covenant is very large. A computed Altman Z-Score of approximately **4.6** places the balance sheet in the safe zone, confirming the balance sheet is not the binding constraint; the binding constraint is regulatory eligibility, where the failure mode is discontinuous.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated growth of 9.1% with adjusted EPS up 23% is respectable, and the composition is where the forward risk sits. Growth is heavily attributable to Walden and Medical & Veterinary; Chamberlain contributed roughly $18M of the $121.5M nine-month revenue increase and *subtracted* $10.4M of adjusted operating income.

**Walden.** Enrollment growth of 12-14% across three consecutive quarters and 300bps of margin expansion make this the engine. Walden revenue per student is modest — $594.1M across an average 53,042 students = ~$11,200 per nine months, or **~$14,900 annualized**. That is well below the annual graduate loan limits reported in the press, which argues the *annual* cap is unlikely to bind for the typical Walden student. The more plausible constraint is the **aggregate/lifetime cap** for doctoral and serial-degree cohorts (DNP, EdD, PhD, DBA), who often arrive with prior undergraduate and master's debt. Because the change applies to *new* borrowers, the effect phases in over years rather than arriving as a cliff, and reported private-lender substitution could mute it further. My per-student figure is a blended average that may mask concentrated exposure in high-cost doctoral programs.

**Chamberlain.** Eleven sessions of monotonic deceleration to roughly flat, with the decline located in the high-margin online post-licensure business, is the most concerning trend in the file. The Cincinnati campus and Fall 2026 applications add pre-licensure capacity, which means more revenue at lower margin and higher capex. Pre-licensure growth is bounded by clinical placement slots rationed across every nursing school in a market, which is the most credible explanation for why 3.4% revenue growth cost 250bps of segment margin.

**Medical & Veterinary.** The most improved segment (+173bps margin, +4.1% enrollment), serving qualified applicants rejected by U.S. MD programs, with tax exemptions running to 2038-39.

The February 2026 "three-year growth strategy" is reported but its quantified targets could not be obtained; treat its content as unverified. FY2027 guidance on **August 6, 2026** is the first opportunity for management to size the Grad PLUS transition.

## Competitive Landscape

The demand backdrop is the strongest element: U.S. healthcare workforce shortages are structural, driven by aging demographics, nurse retirements, and physician/veterinarian supply constrained by residency and accreditation bottlenecks. The company's cited figure of 8.4M open healthcare jobs (CNBC, 2/11/2026) is company-sourced and should be discounted as promotional; the direction is not seriously contested.

Positioning within that demand is uneven. The defensible advantages I can substantiate are scale in nursing education with physical campus infrastructure, and the offshore medical/veterinary franchise, whose moat requires accreditation plus decades-old affiliation agreements with U.S. hospitals for third- and fourth-year clinical rotations. The vulnerability is the declining piece: **online post-licensure nursing**, the high-margin business, competes against non-profit online scale players (Western Governors, Southern New Hampshire, Purdue Global) and traditional universities' online arms, none of which bear the 90/10 rule or proprietary-institution composite-score scrutiny. That asymmetry is the durable structural disadvantage in this industry — it is why Covista holds $202.6M of letters of credit and paid $3.8M of LOC fees in nine months while non-profit competitors hold none.

On the trajectory test that matters more than level: the flagship segment is losing share in its profitable mix; Walden is gaining share into a funding change; Medical & Veterinary is improving with the most durable moat and the highest regulatory exposure. Net trajectory is mixed-to-slightly-negative.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Grad PLUS elimination (highest-consequence, unquantified).** OBBBA reportedly eliminates Grad PLUS for new graduate borrowers and imposes new caps effective 7/1/2026, with Grad PLUS counting toward a reported $257,500 lifetime limit. These reports are consistent across multiple outlets but unverified against statute, ED regulatory text, or any company filing. July 1, 2026 is day one of FY2027; Walden is 40.9% of revenue and ~51% of segment adjusted operating profit. The Q3 10-Q states there were **no material changes** to risk factors and does not quantify exposure. Given a dated statutory funding change at the segment carrying half the profit pool, the absence of quantification is the most consequential informational gap in the file, and it resolves on August 6.

**2. Federal dependence rose as the mix shifted.** FY2025 90/10 ratios: Chamberlain 70%, Walden 82%, AUC 86%, RUSM 86%, RUSVM 77%, **consolidated 78% vs. 77%** in FY2024. Because Walden is more federally dependent than Chamberlain, the mix shift raised the consolidated ratio. Two consecutive years above 90% terminates Title IV eligibility; one year can trigger provisional certification. AUC and RUSM sit four points from the threshold, though both improved a point. The "no customer over 10% of revenue" disclosure is accurate and economically uninformative: the concentration is one funding channel at 77% of revenue.

**3. Composite score 0.2 and provisional certification.** ED notified the company on 9/25/2023 that its FY2022 composite score fell to 0.2 (from above 1.5), making it "not financially responsible" by regulation. All five institutions are provisionally certified. Consequences: **$202.6M of surety-backed letters of credit in favor of ED expiring January 31, 2027** (disclosed as equal to 10% of FY2025 consolidated Title IV receipts), plus $76.9M of state surety bonds and $3.8M of nine-month LOC fees. The credit agreement caps LOCs at $500M, which explains the August 2025 revolver upsize. The January 2027 renewal is a near-dated item.

**4. RUSM's PPA.** Chamberlain (recertified February 2026), Walden, and AUC (March 2026) run provisionally through 12/31/2028; RUSVM through 3/31/2027. **RUSM's last recertification expired 3/31/2025** and it continues accessing Title IV on a timely-filed, materially complete pending application — more than a year past expiration as of the balance-sheet date. Legally permissible, unresolved, and at the institution that also carries an 86% ratio.

**5. Borrower defense claims.** 15,300 claims (Walden 7,804; Chamberlain 3,144; RUSVM 2,013; RUSM 1,958; AUC 381), unquantified in dollars, with **no ED notice of approval or intent to recoup** received. OBBBA is a clear positive here, blocking the 2022/2023 BDR regulations until July 1, 2035 and restoring the more favorable 2019/2020 rules.

**6. Do No Harm earnings tests.** A graduate or professional program whose completers' earnings do not exceed a **bachelor's-degree** benchmark for two years in three can lose Title IV eligibility. Walden's portfolio is heavily master's-level in education, social work, psychology, and business — fields where the graduate earnings premium is thinnest. Implementing regulations are unwritten (negotiated rulemaking announced 7/25/2025; committees met December 2025 and January 2026), so this is a multi-year tail rather than a near-term event. On the related FVT/GE rules, a district judge ruled in ED's favor on 10/2/2025, while ED's own December 2025 rulemaking proposal would eliminate the debt-to-earnings measure — direction genuinely uncertain both ways.

**7. Legacy divestiture liabilities.** Discontinued operations produced a Q3 loss of $16.3M after tax ($21.9M pretax) and −$15.4M for nine months versus +$4.6M. Under the 12/4/2017 stock purchase agreement with Cogswell Education, **Covista is the indemnitor** for DeVry University subject to a **$340.0M Liability Cap**, "a portion of which" has been consumed, with the remainder undisclosed. The offsetting DeVry earn-out of up to $20.0M was fully received as of Q2 FY2026.

**8. Impairment and other.** $1,718.4M of goodwill and intangibles equal 62.8% of assets, with $651.1M of goodwill at Walden — the segment facing the funding change. The last completed test was 5/31/2025, and the Q3 FY2026 triggering-event assessment found no indicators; the annual test date is May 31. Clinical rotation dependency gives certain states authority to limit clinical education programs and force class-size reductions on ~20% of revenue. Geographic concentration includes three Caribbean islands with hurricane exposure. A material change of ownership requires ED recertification, accreditor reevaluation, and state reauthorization, which is a structural impediment to Covista being acquired.

No options data was available this cycle, so no options-implied sentiment check is possible.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$170, +28% from $133)

Grad PLUS proves immaterial because grandfathering, the modest ~$14,900 annualized revenue per Walden student, and private-lender substitution keep new-enrollment growth near 10%; to the extent students shift to private financing, the 90/10 ratio *improves*. Chamberlain post-licensure stabilizes and campus additions season into positive operating leverage. Medical & Veterinary continues margin expansion with RUSM's PPA resolved. FY2027 adjusted operating income grows 8%; the buyback retires 6-7% of shares annually against $661.8M of authorization and 0.7x leverage; adjusted EPS reaches ~$9.20 and the multiple re-rates to 18.5x as the regulatory overhang clears. Amortization of Walden curriculum intangibles ends after FY2027, adding a small GAAP tailwind.

### Bear Case (~$90, −32% from $133)

FY2027 guidance on August 6 quantifies a Walden revenue decline as Grad PLUS removal bites concentrated doctoral and multi-degree cohorts. Chamberlain's post-licensure erosion deepens while campus capex and a 17-year lease commitment are already sunk. The consolidated 90/10 drifts toward 80% as the mix keeps shifting toward Walden. A future goodwill test produces an impairment against $651.1M at Walden. The deferred-tax cash benefit reverses, capex steps up, and FCF conversion falls toward 5-6%. Adjusted operating income declines 8%, adjusted EPS falls to ~$7.50, and the multiple compresses to 12x on renewed regulatory de-rating. The $340M indemnity cap and 15,300 BDR claims remain unquantified overhangs, and the January 2027 LOC renewal arrives with the composite score still below 1.0.

### Base Case (~$140, +5% from $133)

Grad PLUS trims Walden's new-enrollment growth from 12% to mid-single digits, phasing in over FY2027-28; Chamberlain runs flat with margin stabilizing near 19%; Medical & Veterinary continues improving. FY2027 adjusted operating income grows ~2%, offset partly by higher floating-rate interest and capex; the buyback retires ~6% of shares; adjusted EPS reaches ~$8.45. At 16.5x — the current implied multiple — that is roughly $140.

The skew is close to symmetric with thin base-case upside, and the decisive variable resolves in two days without a valid price basis to size entry against.

## Conclusion

Covista has executed a real operational turnaround — 1,330bps of operating margin expansion, $746.7M of debt prepayment, 28.1% share count reduction, and a 20.3% nine-month free cash flow margin — and it is priced at roughly 16-17x FY2026 adjusted earnings with a 7-9% normalized FCF yield and 14.6% of market capitalization in unused buyback authorization. Management's repurchases at a $98.35 average against a $133 July reference price were well-executed, and an independent director committed $3.75M of personal capital at $98-101 in March.

Against that, the profit center has migrated into the segment most exposed to a dated statutory funding change that the company has not quantified anywhere in its filings, while the flagship nursing franchise has run eleven consecutive sessions of enrollment deceleration with adjusted operating income declining in absolute dollars. All five institutions are provisionally certified on a 0.2 composite score, one is operating more than a year past PPA expiration, $202.6M of ED letters of credit expire in January 2027, and 62.8% of assets are goodwill and intangibles concentrated in the segment facing the funding change. The compensation plan measures revenue, adjusted EPS, and adjusted EBITDA margin, with nothing tied to regulatory compliance, student outcomes, or capital efficiency, and $29M of cumulative "strategic advisory costs" are excluded from those same metrics. Three C-suite insiders sold into the rally while a director accumulated at 25% lower prices.

With base-case upside of roughly 5%, bear-case downside near 32%, no valid price basis, no consensus estimate set, and FY2027 guidance landing two days after this report, the correct posture is to hold what is owned and let the August 6 disclosure resolve the Grad PLUS question before committing new capital. Overall score **5.8**, down from 6.1, reflecting a 33% higher reference price on fundamentals that have not improved and a forward funding risk that has moved from distant to immediate.
