**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Matthews International is a 176-year-old diversified manufacturer in the middle of a substantial portfolio transformation. Over the past 18 months the company has deconsolidated its largest brand-solutions business into the Propelis JV (May 2025), sold its warehouse automation unit to Duravant for ~$225M (December 2025), divested European print and Turkish operations, acquired The Dodge Company to strengthen its embalming-supply franchise, redeemed $300M of expensive 8.625% notes, and refreshed most of its board under an agreement with Barington Capital. What remains is a "Memorialization + 40% Propelis stake + contested battery-equipment franchise" story being actively reshaped under shareholder pressure.

The investment debate is whether the streamlined core is worth more than the current ~$827M equity value. The reported return to profitability in the first half of FY26 is an accounting artifact: net income of $21.8M exists only because of a $109.3M net divestiture gain, while operating cash flow was negative $67.4M and total adjusted EBITDA fell to $80.0M from $91.4M a year earlier. That cash bleed is the principal reason this is not a buy. Against it sits a material de-risking event: the February 13, 2026 interim arbitration ruling affirmed Matthews' ownership of the dry-battery-electrode (DBE) technology Tesla had challenged, with a narrow injunction that management states has not materially impeded operations, plus working deleveraging (total debt down from ~$711M to $579M in two quarters, no maturity before January 2029) and a stable Memorialization segment generating $48.8M of adjusted EBITDA at 22-23% margins in the latest half.

I am moving to HOLD from a prior AVOID. The evidence is two-sided rather than structurally impaired: the company is solvent, covenant-compliant, deleveraging, and the strategic logic is defensible. With the stock at ~$25.50 against analyst targets of $36-40 and the largest idiosyncratic overhang (the DBE ownership question) resolved favorably, the reward/risk no longer supports a bearish call. Negative recurring cash flow, an Industrial Technologies EBITDA loss, and falling near-term estimates keep me from a constructive one. This is an uncertain hold pending evidence the core can self-fund.

## Company Overview

Matthews (founded 1850, incorporated in Pennsylvania in 1902, fiscal year ending September 30) now operates three reporting structures:

**Memorialization (~$810M FY25 sales, the stable core).** Bronze and granite memorials, caskets, cremation products, embalming chemicals and supplies, and cremation/incineration equipment sold to cemeteries, funeral homes, and crematories, primarily in North America. Matthews is a leading North American casket maker and the leading supplier of embalming chemicals (strengthened by the May 2025 Dodge acquisition, ~$43M annual sales). Long-run, rising cremation rates pressure bronze/granite/casket volumes, partly offset by cremation-equipment sales and price realization.

**Industrial Technologies (~$342M FY25 sales, shrinking and volatile).** Product identification (marking/coding, inkjet/laser, consumable inks) plus custom energy-storage equipment (calendering/coating/laminating lines for lithium-ion and dry-battery-electrode manufacturing). The warehouse automation business was sold to Duravant in December 2025. This segment houses the Tesla DBE dispute and swung to an adjusted EBITDA loss of roughly $7.8M in 1H FY26 (from +$7.9M a year earlier) on customer project delays.

**Brand Solutions, now an equity-method investment.** On May 1, 2025, Matthews contributed the SGK business into Propelis (SGK merged with SGS & Co.), receiving 40% common equity, $50M preferred equity, and ~$228M net cash. Propelis (~$1B sales, 30+ countries) is deconsolidated and reported on a three-month lag. Matthews' 40% share of Propelis adjusted EBITDA was ~$22.4M in 1H FY26, but the equity-method line was a $11.6M loss after Propelis-level D&A and interest, partly offset by $2.5M of PIK interest on the preferred.

Several points matter for interpretation:

**Revenue decline is largely structural.** FY25's $1.50B reflects SGK deconsolidation from May 2025. In 1H FY26, sales fell to $543M from $829M; roughly $242M of that is the SGK deconsolidation, ~$23M is other divestitures, and the remainder is genuine Industrial Technologies weakness from energy-storage project delays.

**Profitability is distorted by one-time items.** The 1H FY26 net income is entirely attributable to the net pre-tax divestiture gain (a $147.6M gain on the Duravant sale offset by a $38.4M loss on the European roto-gravure sale). Underlying operations are loss-making at the bottom line.

**Cash flow quality is currently poor.** Operating cash flow in 1H FY26 (versus -$18.7M a year earlier) was dragged by working capital, incentive-comp payments, an $8M legal settlement, and the absence of SGK cash generation. Management guides capex down to $20-30M for FY26 and expects a return to positive operating cash, but this is the single most important item to monitor and the reason a BUY is not warranted.

**Deleveraging is working.** Divestiture proceeds funded the January 2026 redemption of the $300M 8.625% 2027 notes (at a 104.313% call premium, a $16.3M extinguishment charge) and an amended credit facility (Feb 2026: $700M revolver + $150M term loan, maturing January 2029). Interest expense dropped to $24.7M in 1H FY26 from $31.5M, a direct, recurring benefit from retiring the most expensive tranche.

**Solvency.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.79 sits in the "distress zone," but this reflects depressed EBIT and heavy goodwill rather than imminent bankruptcy risk. The company was covenant-compliant at 3/31/26, current ratio is ~1.5-1.6, equity rose to $512M, and the ~4% dividend has been maintained and modestly raised. Optionality is finite, though: the most marketable non-core assets have now been sold, so future deleveraging depends increasingly on the core generating cash.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term consolidated growth story. Reported revenue will decline further as the SGK deconsolidation laps. The forward path depends on three components moving in different directions.

Memorialization is a low-single-digit, demographically-anchored business with a slow secular volume headwind from cremation, offset by price realization, the Dodge acquisition, and cremation-equipment sales. It is a stabilizer, not a growth engine.

Industrial Technologies/energy storage is where any growth narrative concentrates, and it is also the source of current losses. DBE/battery equipment demand is tied to EV battery capex and a small number of large customers — chiefly Tesla, whose project timing drives a significant share of Matthews' ~$98.5M of contract assets. Revenue here is lumpy and exposed to a single counterparty's decisions. The favorable February 2026 arbitration ruling on IP ownership matters because it would let Matthews commercialize DBE equipment more broadly beyond Tesla, but that is optionality, not yet revenue.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than uniformly negative. Over the trailing 30 days the current-quarter estimate fell 12.8% and the next-quarter estimate fell 13.2%, signaling near-term softness. But over 90 days the next-quarter estimate is up 30.3% and the current fiscal year is up 7.9% over 90 days. The earnings track record is 3 beats in 4 quarters, including a 119.8% upside surprise in the March 2026 quarter, against one large miss in December 2025. The net read is choppy visibility on a transitional business, not a clean deterioration trend. I do not treat the +30% 90-day forward revision as a BUY signal here because it sits on top of a business with negative operating cash flow and an Industrial Technologies EBITDA loss; the accelerating-revenue signal is meant for stocks recovering from depressed levels with a cash-generative base, which is not yet the case.

The cleaner valuation lens is sum-of-the-parts, because that is the framework the activist-driven restructuring is built around. The market is paying ~$827M of equity value (plus ~$579M of debt) for: a Memorialization segment running at half-year adjusted EBITDA annualizing toward ~$95-100M, which at an SCI-comparable mid-teens multiple supports a meaningful standalone value; a 40% Propelis stake carried at ~$202.5M equity-method plus a $51.6M preferred; and a battery-equipment franchise now de-risked on the ownership question. The opacity of the Propelis stake and the cash-burning state of Industrial Technologies make the SOTP genuinely uncertain, which is why I do not assign a high valuation score: there is a plausible mispricing, but it is not one I can pin down with confidence, and the cheapness signal alone has historically been a weak basis for upside.

Analyst targets of $36-40 (mean $38) imply 41-57% upside, but with only two covering analysts the consensus is thin. I treat these as supportive context rather than a high-confidence anchor. A balanced read is that the stock is fairly priced to modestly cheap on a SOTP basis, with the gap to analyst targets reflecting unproven restructuring optionality that I am not willing to underwrite as a base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Memorialization operates in mature, oligopolistic markets correlated to death rates; Matthews and a small number of rivals dominate North American caskets, embalming supplies, and cremation equipment. Competition is on quality, breadth, delivery, and price, with the structural shift to cremation the key long-run dynamic, a headwind for caskets and memorials but an opportunity in cremation equipment. Chinese casket imports are a persistent competitive pressure. The segment's position is stable rather than improving.

The energy-storage/DBE business is where competitive positioning was most at risk and where it has materially improved. Matthews holds patents on dry-electrode calendering equipment and claims two decades of DBE development. The February 2026 interim arbitration ruling affirming Matthews' ownership of that technology removes the question of whether Matthews even owned what it was selling. The trajectory here is the most improved part of the story, even as near-term EBITDA is negative.

Propelis competes globally on price and scale; Matthews now participates only as a 40% financial owner with reduced control and a reporting lag, so its competitive trajectory is largely outside Matthews' hands.

## Risk Assessment

**Negative recurring cash generation.** The most important risk is operational, not legal. The deleveraging story now depends on the core generating cash after the most marketable assets have been sold. If operating cash flow does not turn positive in 2H FY26 as guided, covenant headroom and the dividend come into question.

**Tesla litigation and customer concentration.** The February 2026 interim ruling was favorable on ownership and liability (Tesla prevailed on "only a limited subset" of claims). But legal costs are heavy (~$11.2M in 1H FY26), damages phases remain, a 9th Circuit appeal is pending, and Tesla is simultaneously the largest litigation adversary and the customer whose project timing drives the bulk of contract assets. This concentration is unusual and remains a live risk even after the ownership question was resolved.

**Industrial Technologies losses and Propelis drag.** The segment swung to an EBITDA loss on Tesla project delays, and the Propelis stake produced an equity-method loss after D&A and interest, reported on a three-month lag that obscures real-time performance.

**Activist/breakup risk, two-directional.** The unusually candid expanded breakup-risk language in the 10-K, the January 2026 Barington settlement, and the wholesale board refresh point to a company being actively reshaped. This is a catalyst (further monetization, potential separation of Industrial Technologies or Propelis) and a risk (separations "pursued under pressure" could destroy synergies and raise costs).

**Secular Memorialization headwind and FX/tariff exposure.** Rising cremation rates pressure the highest-margin volumes over time; ~30% of sales are non-U.S. with a net derivative liability of ~$54.8M and explicit tariff exposure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.5% sits rich to one-year historical volatility of 33.6% (a 7.9% spread), and the put/call skew of 8.6% reflects elevated put demand. The term structure shows the 3-month tenor elevated at 49.2% with implied moves of ±16.8%, consistent with the market pricing event risk around the August earnings window and the ongoing litigation/restructuring. Short interest is elevated at 12.8% of float with 12.1 days to cover. The options and short-interest picture confirms institutional caution rather than contradicting the HOLD; it also means any positive surprise (a clean cash-flow turn or a further accretive divestiture) could force covering given the high days-to-cover.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (path to $36-40)

The streamlined entity is worth materially more than its current equity value once the restructuring completes. Memorialization annualizes toward ~$95-100M of adjusted EBITDA and is worth a mid-teens multiple as a stable, demographically-anchored franchise comparable to SCI. The Propelis stake and preferred (~$254M combined carrying value) are real and could be monetized. The DBE/battery franchise, now confirmed as Matthews' IP, becomes commercializable beyond Tesla once project timing normalizes. Deleveraging continues, interest expense keeps falling, operating cash flow turns positive in 2H FY26, and the activist-driven board pursues further value-unlocking actions. The thin analyst coverage at $36-40 is directionally correct.

### Bear Case (path to ~$18-20)

Operating cash flow stays negative into FY27, Industrial Technologies losses persist on continued Tesla project delays, Propelis remains an equity-method drag, and the company exhausts its sellable assets without the core self-funding. Covenant headroom tightens, the dividend is questioned, and the SOTP optionality the bulls cite proves illusory because the parts are worth less standalone than the activist narrative implies. The distress-zone Z-Score becomes a self-fulfilling concern in a higher-for-longer rate environment. A ~25-30% decline to $18-20 is plausible.

These cases roughly offset. The largest idiosyncratic overhang (DBE ownership) has been removed favorably, deleveraging is real, and the core is stable, but negative recurring cash flow and finite asset-sale optionality are equally real. That balance supports a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY26 earnings release (early August 2026), which will show whether operating cash flow turned positive in the back half as management guides, and the Q4/FY26 report. The Tesla damages phases and the 9th Circuit appeal are additional, less-dated checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating cash flow turns clearly positive in 2H FY26 (the most important single condition), demonstrating the core can self-fund deleveraging.
- Industrial Technologies adjusted EBITDA returns to positive as Tesla/energy-storage project timing normalizes.
- A further accretive monetization (e.g., Propelis stake or Industrial Technologies separation) that crystallizes SOTP value above the current ~$827M cap, with proceeds applied to debt.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating cash flow remains negative through FY26 with no credible path to positive, pressuring covenant headroom.
- An adverse Tesla damages ruling materially exceeding the ~4%-of-revenue DBE exposure, or loss of Tesla as a customer.
- A dividend cut, draw on the unused ATM equity program, or covenant breach — any of which would signal the deleveraging story is failing.

## Conclusion

Matthews is a business in transition, not active collapse. The bearish case I previously held has been undercut by two confirmed developments: the arbitration ruling that affirmed Matthews' ownership of the DBE technology, removing the single largest idiosyncratic overhang, and real deleveraging that retired the expensive notes, cut interest expense, and pushed all maturities to 2029. The Memorialization core is stable and cash-generative, and the company is covenant-compliant with a defensible strategic logic. Against that, negative operating cash flow, contracting adjusted EBITDA, an Industrial Technologies loss, and a Propelis drag are genuine and prevent a constructive call.

At ~$25.50, near peer-level forward multiples with analyst targets of $36-40 and an options market signaling event risk rather than directional collapse, the asymmetry no longer favors the downside. The prior AVOID is not consistent with a business that is solvent, deleveraging, de-risked on its largest legal overhang, and trading where a credible SOTP roughly brackets fair value. This is an uncertain HOLD: the positives (de-risked IP, working deleveraging, stable core, restructuring optionality) and negatives (cash burn, segment losses, finite asset-sale runway) approximately cancel. I would revisit constructively if operating cash flow turns positive in the second half, and turn bearish if it does not.
