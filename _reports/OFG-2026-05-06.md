## Executive Summary

OFG Bancorp is a Puerto Rico-based bank generating 16.4% ROATCE at a 5.36% NIM — metrics that belong to a bank trading at 1.8-2.0x tangible book, yet OFG sits at 1.52x TBV and 9.8x forward earnings. The discount exists because the majority of the bank's operations are concentrated in Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, with natural disaster risk, fiscal uncertainty, and dependence on federal reconstruction funds.

I conclude the discount is excessive given the bank's demonstrated execution, capital return velocity, and the improving Puerto Rico economic backdrop. Current year EPS estimates have risen approximately 10% in the last 30 days (per consensus data providers) following a Q1 2026 beat, the bank has reduced its share count by 11% over three years, and the forward P/E of 9.8x sits at a 33% discount to peer median (estimated at 14.7x per third-party data). The re-rating path: continued 15-16% ROATCE through 2026, 5-6% loan growth, and share count reduction drive EPS to $4.70+ this year, and the market gradually reprices the stock toward 11-12x forward earnings as Puerto Rico risk fades. This represents 12-22% upside from current levels over 12 months.

The primary risk is the Commercial loan portfolio where non-performing loans have increased materially — commercial NPLs rose to $84.0M in Q1 2026 from $53.4M in Q3 2025, with the commercial NPL rate climbing to 2.41% from 1.62%. If credit deterioration accelerates further, the bank's capital return posture would come under scrutiny. While the ACL coverage ratio has been proactively increased and Q1 2026 NCOs moderated to 1.05% from Q4's 1.32%, the trend in nonperforming loans warrants close monitoring.

## Company Overview

OFG Bancorp operates Oriental Bank, a full-service commercial bank with 42 branches in Puerto Rico and 2 in the U.S. Virgin Islands. Total assets reached $12.5B as of FY2025. The bank runs three segments: Banking (lending, deposits, mortgage banking), Wealth Management ($5.2B combined AUM in trust and brokerage), and Treasury (investment portfolio management).

The loan portfolio is diversified across Commercial (41.7%), Auto (32.6%), Mortgage (17.2%), and Consumer (8.5%). A growing Commercial US segment (approximately 10.6% of total loans at Q1 2026) provides geographic diversification beyond Puerto Rico and the USVI. The bank has positioned itself as Puerto Rico's "Digital First" challenger bank, achieving 70% digital retail loan payment rates and deploying AI-driven customer insights for cross-selling.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** OFG's NIM is approximately 175-200 basis points above the U.S. regional bank average. Q1 2026 NIM rebounded to 5.36%, up from 5.12% in Q4 2025 and 5.24% in Q3 2025, reflecting favorable funding-cost and balance-sheet dynamics. The efficiency ratio of 50.97% (Q1 2026) demonstrates strong operating leverage.

**Earnings trajectory:** EPS grew from $3.46 (FY2022) to $4.58 (FY2025), a 32% cumulative increase driven equally by earnings growth and share buybacks. Q4 2025 delivered $1.27 EPS (+16.5% YoY), and Q1 2026 came in at $1.26 beating estimates. Consensus for FY2026 is approximately $4.70 (per third-party estimate aggregators) — implying continued mid-single-digit growth.

**Capital adequacy:** CET1 at 13.75% (Q1 2026, down from 14.13% in Q3 2025) remains well above the 6.50% well-capitalized minimum. This excess capital supports both the buyback program and organic loan growth without constraint, though the declining trend reflects capital deployment through buybacks and loan growth.

**Credit quality:** The credit picture has evolved materially since mid-2025. Net charge-offs were 1.00% in Q3 2025, rose to 1.32% in Q4 2025, and moderated to 1.05% in Q1 2026. The nonperforming loan rate increased from 1.22% in Q3 2025 to 1.59% in Q4 2025 and remained elevated at 1.47% in Q1 2026. Total non-PCD commercial nonperforming loans rose to $84.0M in Q1 2026 from $53.4M in Q3 2025 and $44.2M in Q1 2025, representing a commercial NPL rate of 2.41%. Q1 2026 provision expense was $22.5M, including $3.7M for a previously reserved commercial loan and $1.0M for newly classified small commercial loans. The ACL coverage ratio has been proactively increased to 2.44% of loans.

**Capital return:** For FY2025, OFG repurchased 2.25M shares for $91.6M and declared $53.5M in cash dividends, totaling approximately $145M — representing a combined payout ratio of approximately 71% of net income ($205.1M). As of December 31, 2025, remaining buyback authorization was $38.1M. The combined payout ratio is aggressive relative to the rising credit costs, though supported by excess regulatory capital.

## Growth Analysis

OFG's growth levers are:

1. **Loan growth (5-6% annually):** Commercial lending grew 9.1% YTD through Q3 2025, with US commercial up 18.1%. Auto moderated after a tariff-driven surge but remains positive at 3.8%.

2. **Share buybacks (3-4% annual reduction):** Outstanding shares declined from 48.4M to 43.1M over three years. Remaining buyback authorization at year-end 2025 stood at $38.1M, which at current prices provides limited near-term repurchase capacity absent a new program authorization.

3. **Puerto Rico economic recovery:** Employment at 962,500 jobs (+0.9% YoY per management's disclosures), tourism growth, manufacturing on-shoring, and continued federal reconstruction spending provide a multi-year organic growth runway.

4. **Fee income expansion:** Wealth Management revenue grew alongside AUM growth. The bank's fintech investments through OFG Ventures add optionality.

Growth constraints include potential NIM pressure if rates decline (in a -200 bps instantaneous shock, NII would decline 5.63%), Durbin Amendment headwind (~$5M annually, now fully absorbed), and mortgage portfolio runoff (-4.8% as the bank securitizes production). However, Q1 2026's NIM rebound to 5.36% suggests the compression trend is not linear and depends on balance-sheet dynamics.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current year EPS estimates rose approximately 10% in the last 30 days (per third-party consensus data) and approximately 3% over 90 days. The 30-day revision pace reflects Q1 2026 beat confirmation.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.96, OFG trades at:
- 9.8x forward P/E (FY2026E $4.70)
- 1.52x tangible book value ($30.14 at Q1 2026)
- 10.0x trailing P/E (FY2025 $4.58)
- 2.6% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is estimated at 14.7x (per third-party valuation data), placing OFG at an approximately 33% discount. The peer median dividend yield is approximately 3.0%, slightly above OFG's 2.6%. OFG's ROATCE of 16.4% substantially exceeds the typical community bank return profile (10-13%). A bank generating 16%+ returns on tangible equity should trade closer to 12-14x earnings and 1.7-2.0x TBV.

**Why the discount exists:** Geographic concentration in Puerto Rico and the USVI. The market applies a permanent discount for territory exposure, natural disaster risk, PREPA uncertainty, and federal funding dependence. This discount is partially warranted but has been excessive given that OFG has consistently delivered through multiple Puerto Rico crises (Maria, earthquakes, Fiona, COVID) while growing earnings every year.

**Why the discount should narrow:** (1) Three years of 15-16% ROATCE demonstrate this is not a cyclical anomaly; (2) buybacks at discounted valuations accelerate per-share value creation; (3) the Commercial US portfolio (now ~10.6% of loans) provides gradual geographic diversification; (4) Puerto Rico's economic indicators are trending positively.

A re-rating to 11x forward earnings on $4.70 FY2026E implies $51.70 — 12.5% upside. At 12x (still below peer median), the stock would trade at $56.40 — 22.7% upside. These targets assume no multiple expansion beyond partial convergence toward peers.

## Competitive Landscape

OFG operates in a concentrated Puerto Rico banking market dominated by Popular, Inc. (BPOP) and First BanCorp (FBP). OFG holds low-teens deposit market share and differentiates on digital capabilities and customer experience rather than branch scale.

Competitive advantages:
- Digital penetration (70% digital loan payments) drives lower operating costs and higher customer engagement
- Strong auto lending franchise (32.6% of loans) with established dealer relationships
- Wealth Management platform ($5.2B AUM) provides recurring fee income
- "Libre" and "Elite" account tiers drive customer acquisition in mass and mass-affluent segments

The competitive position is stable-to-improving, with deposit share gains partially offset by the aggressive Commercial US expansion where OFG is a newer and smaller participant competing against mainland banks with deeper expertise.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Puerto Rico Geographic Concentration (HIGH):** A Category 5 hurricane or significant federal funding cuts would directly impair OFG's earnings and asset quality. The PREPA Title III resolution remains pending and could create fiscal uncertainty. The growing but still modest U.S. mainland commercial exposure (~10.6% of loans) provides limited offset. This structural risk justifies a permanent discount, but the magnitude of the current 33% discount to peers overcompensates given demonstrated resilience.

**2. Commercial Credit Deterioration (HIGH):** The bank is participating in middle-market and SMB cash flow loans in the US segment where it lacks local expertise. Management has proactively reserved against this segment (higher recession probability in ACL model) and Q1 2026 provision included specific reserves for commercial credits, but the pace of deterioration warrants elevated monitoring.

**3. NIM Sensitivity (MODERATE):** In a -200 bps rate shock, NII declines 5.63% (~$35M). If the Fed cuts rates materially, NIM would face pressure. However, Q1 2026's rebound demonstrates that NIM dynamics are influenced by funding costs and balance-sheet composition, not just the rate level.

**4. Government Deposit Concentration (MODERATE):** $1.65B in Puerto Rico government deposits (16.5% of total) from a single client relationship represents a funding concentration risk. Withdrawal would require wholesale funding replacement.

**5. Capital Return Sustainability (MODERATE):** The FY2025 combined payout ratio of approximately 71% is aggressive in the context of rising commercial credit costs and declining CET1. If credit losses accelerate, management may need to moderate the pace of capital return.

**6. Regulatory/Policy Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Potential federal spending cuts affecting Puerto Rico reconstruction funds; tariff impacts on island economy; Durbin Amendment already absorbed.

**Options Market Signal:** No options data was available for OFG (per third-party data providers). Short interest at approximately 3.7% of float with approximately 3.1 days to cover (per third-party data) is unremarkable and suggests no material bearish institutional positioning against the stock.

## Conclusion

OFG Bancorp warrants a BUY recommendation at $45.96. The bank generates elite returns (16.4% ROATCE, 5.36% NIM) at a 33% P/E discount to regional bank peers, supported by positive estimate revisions, a consistent earnings beat record, and a capital return program that has reduced share count 11% over three years.

The Puerto Rico concentration risk is real and permanent, but the current discount overcompensates given: (a) three years of uninterrupted earnings growth through multiple crises, (b) proactive credit management (ACL increased to 2.44%), (c) gradual geographic diversification through the Commercial US segment, and (d) improving Puerto Rico economic fundamentals. The stock offers a plausible 12-15% total return (price appreciation + dividend) over 12 months as the market incrementally rewards execution, with further upside if the geographic discount narrows more substantially.

The key monitoring point is commercial credit quality — the pace of deterioration (commercial NPL rate from 1.62% to 2.41% over two quarters) demands vigilance. If nonperformers continue rising while remaining buyback capacity is limited, the capital return component of the thesis weakens. Current profitability metrics and reserve levels provide a buffer, but the margin of safety on credit is narrower than it appeared six months ago.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Commercial credit deterioration is more severe than originally presented (commercial NPL rate 2.41% vs. 1.62% over two quarters), and remaining buyback capacity ($38.1M) is materially lower than originally stated, which narrows the margin of safety supporting the re-rating thesis.
