**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ventas is in the middle of a multi-year senior-housing recovery that is now showing up in the same-store numbers rather than in projections. Q1 2026 same-store SHOP NOI grew +15.4%, driven by occupancy climbing from 87.3% to 90.4% (+310 bps) and revenue per occupied room (RevPOR) up +5.0%. Occupancy gains in senior housing fall through to NOI at high incremental margins because the fixed cost base is already in place, so each point of occupancy is disproportionately valuable. Management raised full-year normalized FFO/share guidance to $3.86 (midpoint), lifted total same-store cash NOI growth guidance to ~10%, and increased the total investment plan to $3B. At $80.66, the stock trades at roughly 20.9x forward FFO.

This report upgrades Ventas to BUY, reversing the prior HOLD. The change reflects a correction of a reasoning error in earlier coverage: the prior caution leaned on falling GAAP EPS estimate revisions (current-year consensus EPS down ~17–20% over 90 days), but GAAP EPS is not the operative metric for a REIT. Depreciation on a large real-estate base mechanically suppresses GAAP earnings (trailing EPS $0.54, hence the meaningless 150x P/E), while the cash-flow metric that matters, FFO per share, was guided up. The earlier coverage simultaneously dismissed the 150x GAAP P/E as noise and elevated GAAP estimate revisions to a thesis-determining caution. Those two positions cannot both hold, and resolving the contradiction removes the central pillar of the HOLD.

What remains is a positively-skewed setup: a genuine organic inflection, a reasonable forward multiple, a ~2.5% yield with an 8.3% dividend increase, and a near-unanimous constructive sell-side (17 of 21 Buy/Strong Buy, zero Sell, lowest target $86 above the current price, mean $97 implying ~20% upside). The case is tempered by two facts: Ventas is the slower-growing #2 to Welltower (15.4% vs ~22% same-store senior-housing NOI growth), and the growth model is heavily equity-funded, so per-share value creation depends on investment spreads exceeding the cost of newly issued equity.

## Company Overview

Ventas owns or holds investments in approximately 1,425 properties across the U.S., Canada, and the UK, organized into three reportable segments:

- **SHOP (Senior Housing Operating Portfolio)** — 783 communities, 57.5% of Q1 2026 NOI. Ventas owns the real estate and contracts third-party operators (Atria, Sunrise, Discovery, Le Groupe Maurice), capturing operating economics directly. This is the growth engine and the source of the current recovery.
- **OM&R (Outpatient Medical & Research)** — 407 properties, 23.1% of NOI. Outpatient medical buildings plus university-affiliated research/life-science centers. Stable, lease-based, contractual escalators.
- **NNN (Triple-Net Leased)** — 200 properties, 18.5% of NOI. Senior housing, SNFs, and post-acute facilities leased to operators (Brookdale, Kindred, Ardent) on fixed rent. Deliberately shrinking as assets convert to SHOP.

Ventas also operates Ventas Investment Management (including the open-ended Ventas Fund) and holds minority stakes in Atria (34%) and Ardent Health (~6.6%). The investable thesis is that an accelerating 80+ population meets a constrained new-supply pipeline (senior-housing construction starts have been depressed for years on tight financing), producing a multi-year runway for occupancy and rate gains in a portfolio where Ventas now captures the operating upside directly.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 results show the operating model working:

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $1,657.0M | $1,358.1M | +22.0% |
| Total NOI | $651.1M | $569.3M | +14.4% |
| SHOP NOI | $374.5M | $264.5M | +41.6% |
| OM&R NOI | $150.6M | $146.0M | +3.1% |
| NNN NOI | $120.2M | $152.6M | −21.2% |
| Normalized FFO | $455.6M | $386.1M | +18.0% |
| Operating cash flow | $394.6M | $321.1M | +22.9% |

The headline SHOP NOI figure (+41.6%) blends acquisitions and NNN-to-SHOP conversions. The cleaner read is same-store SHOP NOI, which strips out portfolio changes and isolates organic occupancy- and rate-driven growth. Same-store OM&R grew +2.3% and same-store NNN +1.7% on contractual escalators.

The −21.2% NNN decline is engineered, not deterioration. It reflects $23.2M of rent forgone on properties converted from NNN to SHOP (Ventas choosing to capture operating upside) plus $9.6M from disposals (10 former-Brookdale properties sold, $15.0M of gains recognized). This is portfolio rotation toward the higher-growth model.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt fell to $12.6B from $13.1B at year-end 2025, ~83% fixed-rate at a 4.58% weighted-average rate. Liquidity is $5.5B (≈$3.5B undrawn revolver, $550M undrawn delayed-draw term loan, $184M cash, $1.4B unsettled equity forwards). The company has been actively terming out maturities: $500M of 4.13% notes repaid in January 2026, the term loan upsized to $700M and extended to January 2031. One watch item is $856M of 3.75% Exchangeable Notes due June 2026, deeply in-the-money at a ~$54.66 exchange price (principal settled in cash, excess in shares, ~5.2M shares of dilutive effect already in the diluted count). This is a manageable near-term cash and dilution event, not a refinancing cliff.

A standardized Altman Z-Score screen will flag VTR as "distressed" (~1.3). That is a false signal: the Z-Score model penalizes asset-heavy, leveraged real-estate balance sheets and undervalues depreciated property carried at historical cost. Investment-grade credit and $5.5B of liquidity are the relevant facts.

**The dilution caveat is real and must be weighted.** Growth is heavily equity-funded. In Q1, Ventas entered $1.2B of ATM equity forwards at an average $84.62/share and settled 10.6M shares for $800M; weighted diluted shares rose from 446M to 487M (+9% YoY). Aggregate NOI/FFO growth of +14–18% overstates the per-share picture. The honest metric is the $3.86 FFO/share guide, which already nets out the dilution and still represents healthy high-single/low-double-digit per-share growth. The model creates value only if acquisition cap rates and SHOP organic growth comfortably exceed the cost of newly issued equity, a discipline test that the current ~10% same-store NOI guide and accretive acquisition pace suggest is being met.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a structural, broad-based demand/supply imbalance rather than a single-company story. Two of the largest healthcare REITs are posting double-digit same-store senior-housing NOI growth on the identical thesis (Welltower ~22%, Ventas +15.4%), which raises the durability of the trend versus an idiosyncratic result.

The mechanism is concrete: the 80+ population is growing while new senior-housing supply remains depressed because construction starts were curtailed for years on tight financing. Limited new inventory plus rising demand pushes occupancy up; occupancy gains at a community with a largely fixed cost base convert to NOI at high incremental margins; rising occupancy also restores pricing power, evidenced by the RevPOR gain. Ventas's same-store occupancy at 90.4% still sits below pre-pandemic stabilized levels for the asset class, leaving multiple years of runway before the organic lever is exhausted.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in delivery: over the trailing four quarters Ventas beat three times (including a +76.9% beat in Q4 2025) and missed once narrowly (Q1 2026, −11.4% on GAAP EPS). Management raised FFO guidance at Q1 rather than reaffirming, a tone indicator that internal results are running ahead of the plan set at the start of the year. The $3B investment plan adds an external-growth layer on top of the organic recovery, though its accretion depends on funding discipline given the equity dependence noted above.

I am explicitly applying the inflection framework here. Revenue is growing +22%, same-store SHOP NOI +15.4%, occupancy +310 bps with runway, and FFO guidance was raised, all against a forward P/FFO of ~20.9x, well below the 30x threshold at which a high multiple already prices the growth. The falling GAAP EPS estimate revisions do not contradict this; they reflect depreciation accounting and a two-analyst GAAP estimate set, not the cash-flow trajectory. The relevant FFO direction is up.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.66 the stock trades at ~20.9x the $3.86 FFO/share midpoint, with a ~2.5% dividend yield. For a REIT delivering high-single/low-double-digit per-share FFO growth plus a multi-year occupancy runway, that multiple is reasonable rather than cheap or stretched.

Peer comparison is complicated by the GAAP-P/E distortion that affects the entire group (DOC 64x, AHR 81x, HR negative, all artifacts of depreciation, not operating quality). The cleaner relative comparison is to Welltower, the direct large-cap analog. Welltower is delivering faster same-store growth and raising its dividend more aggressively (mid-teens vs Ventas's +8.3%), and the market accords it a premium FFO multiple. Ventas is the somewhat-cheaper, slightly-slower-growing #2. Either the growth gap narrows and Ventas catches up (a re-rating opportunity) or it persists and Welltower's premium is justified.

The analyst setup is the strongest external confirmation. Of 21 covering analysts, 17 rate Buy or Strong Buy, four Hold, and none Sell, a distribution that has been stable for three months. The lowest published price target is $86, with a high of $110. When the bearish end of the analyst range sits above the market price, the consensus is signaling limited downside relative to upside. On a recovery name with a reasonable forward multiple, consensus targets tend to lag rising estimates, so I treat $86 as a near-term floor rather than a ceiling.

I do not assign a higher valuation score because there is no clean "the market is wrong" mispricing: consensus already loves the stock, the multiple fairly reflects the growth, and Ventas is the laggard on the thesis. The case rests on continued execution compounding per-share FFO into a still-recovering occupancy cycle, not on a discount the market has overlooked.

## Competitive Landscape

Ventas is a clear #2 to Welltower in scale within U.S. healthcare REITs, levered to the same demand/supply tailwind. Its competitive trajectory is positive on an absolute basis (occupancy recovering, NOI accelerating, portfolio rotating toward the higher-growth operating model), but relative to Welltower it is losing the same-store growth race and raising its dividend more slowly. The market consistently treats Welltower as the premium "growth REIT" of the pair.

The competitive moat is real but not differentiated versus Welltower: both control irreplaceable, supply-constrained senior-housing real estate and both depend on third-party operators (Ventas relies on Atria at 19% of investment mix, Sunrise at 9.1%, Le Groupe Maurice at 6%). Ventas's OM&R and life-science research platform (Wexford, university affiliations) adds a stable, lease-based ballast that diversifies away from pure operating exposure. The trajectory justifies a moderately positive competitive score; the laggard status caps it.

## Risk Assessment

1. **SHOP operating leverage is two-sided.** With 57.5% of NOI in the operating model, Ventas bears direct exposure to occupancy softness, labor inflation, and operator execution with no triple-net lease to cushion it. SHOP property-level expenses rose +30.4% YoY, with labor the largest line ($514M in Q1). An occupancy reversal or wage spike would hit NOI directly. The same leverage that is driving the upside is the principal downside vector.
2. **Equity-market dependence.** The growth model requires continuous access to equity (ATM forwards) and debt. The +9% YoY diluted-share increase is the cost of funding. If equity becomes expensive (a sustained drawdown) or capital markets tighten, the external-growth engine slows and per-share accretion compresses.
3. **Operator/tenant concentration.** Atria (19% of investment mix), Kindred (5.2% of NOI), and Ardent (6.0% of NOI) are the key relationships; Brookdale leases 65 properties expiring in 2035. Operator financial deterioration or reimbursement cuts would impair results.
4. **Exchangeable notes / June 2026 maturity.** The $856M of 3.75% Exchangeable Notes require cash for principal plus share settlement of the excess, a quantified, near-term, but manageable item.
5. **Interest-rate and refinancing risk.** ~$12.6B of debt; a 100 bps rise on variable debt adds ~$11.3M/year. The larger exposure is refinancing fixed-rate maturities into a higher-for-longer rate environment, which would raise the cost of the leveraged portion of the growth model.

No new material risk factors were added in the Q1 2026 10-Q, and disclosure controls were deemed effective. None of these risks is acute or binary; most are priced into a stock that has already moved with the cycle.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 23.7% sits modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 19.3% (a 4.3% spread), with IV in only the 12th percentile of the current chain, so the options market is pricing low near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.3%, and the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 23.7% below longer-dated 26.0%). The put/call open-interest ratio of 1.22 leans modestly defensive but is consistent with routine hedging in an income name, not a bearish positioning signal. (The "598.5% ATM IV" reading in the delta-change data is a thin-options data artifact and is disregarded.) The options market is not flagging an impending catalyst or elevated downside, consistent with a steady compounding thesis rather than a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$96–110, +19% to +36%)
The senior-housing occupancy recovery has a multi-year runway, and Ventas closes the same-store growth gap with Welltower as its portfolio matures. Occupancy continues toward stabilized levels (mid-90s), RevPOR holds at +5%, and same-store SHOP NOI sustains double digits. The $3B investment plan deploys at accretive spreads, FFO/share compounds at low-double-digits through 2027, the multiple re-rates toward the higher end of the healthcare-REIT range as the dividend growth rate accelerates, and the stock reaches consensus high targets near $110.

### Bear Case (~$68, −16%)
Occupancy gains plateau as the easy recovery is harvested, labor inflation re-accelerates and compresses SHOP margins, and the equity-funded model becomes dilutive as the share price falls and the cost of newly issued equity exceeds incremental acquisition yields. A higher-for-longer rate path pressures the multiple and raises refinancing costs. Per-share FFO growth decelerates to mid-single-digits, the market de-rates the laggard #2 toward ~17x forward FFO, and the stock retraces to the high-$60s.

The risk/reward is positively skewed: more upside to base and bull than downside to bear, supported by a ~2.5% yield and double-digit per-share FFO growth. This asymmetry, combined with confirmed same-store delivery and a reasonable forward multiple, is what drives the BUY rather than a neutral call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $88 (+9% from $80.66), 12 months.** ~22.8x the $3.86 FFO/share midpoint, a modest re-rate reflecting continued occupancy-led same-store growth and a raised investment plan, plus the ~2.5% yield for a total return near +11%. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios.
- **Bull case target: $96–110 (+19% to +36%).** Same-store growth sustains double digits, the gap to Welltower narrows, and the multiple re-rates; reaches consensus mean-to-high targets.
- **Bear case target: $68 (−16%).** Occupancy plateau, margin compression, and dilutive equity funding into a falling price.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to quarterly same-store SHOP NOI and occupancy prints and the $3B investment-plan execution. The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings releases.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:** (1) same-store SHOP NOI growth decelerating below ~8% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the occupancy recovery has stalled; (2) management guiding FFO/share down or pausing the dividend-growth cadence; (3) sequential occupancy declines, indicating the supply/demand imbalance is reversing or operator execution is failing; (4) ATM equity issuance at progressively lower prices funding acquisitions at compressing spreads, indicating dilutive rather than accretive growth.

This is a lower-conviction BUY. For investors seeking the highest-quality expression of the senior-housing thesis, Welltower is delivering faster same-store growth and dividend increases; Ventas is the cheaper, slightly-slower #2. A pullback toward the low-$70s would materially improve the entry and is an add opportunity, but the asymmetry already supports establishing a position at current levels.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports a BUY. Ventas is in a genuine, occupancy-led recovery confirmed in the same-store numbers, management raised FFO guidance, and the stock trades at a reasonable forward FFO multiple with a ~2.5% yield. The risk/reward is positively skewed, and a near-unanimous analyst base with its lowest target above the current price corroborates limited downside. The prior HOLD rested on a contradiction (dismissing GAAP P/E as meaningless while leaning on GAAP estimate revisions) that does not survive scrutiny once FFO is correctly identified as the operative metric.

Conviction is moderated by two real qualifiers: Ventas is the slower-growing #2 to Welltower, and growth is heavily equity-funded, so per-share value creation hinges on maintaining accretive investment spreads. These keep the position sizing disciplined and make a pullback an attractive add rather than a reason to wait entirely. On balance, a positively-skewed setup in a confirmed multi-year demographic tailwind, at a fair multiple with yield support, warrants a buy.
