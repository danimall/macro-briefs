**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clearway Energy is a YieldCo — a publicly traded owner of long-term-contracted clean-energy assets that distributes most of its cash flow as a growing dividend. It owns roughly 12.9 GW of gross capacity across 27 states (about 10.1 GW wind/solar/storage plus 2.8 GW of California gas-fired reliability assets), with a renewables offtake book carrying a ~12-year weighted-average remaining contract life and counterparties averaging a Baa1 rating. The investment case rests on a well-covered, rising dividend (~5.6% yield, CAFD coverage ~1.2x) and a growth runway sharpened by the surge in data-center electricity demand. Over the past 12 months the sponsor signed roughly 2 GW of data-center PPAs, including three contracts with Google (~1.1 GW), and reaffirmed a 2030 target of $2.90–3.10 CAFD/share.

The counterweights are structural rather than cyclical. CWEN owns 58.62% of the economics of the underlying LLC but the sponsor, Clearway Energy Group (CEG), holds 54.89% of the voting power, controls the board, provides all employees under a perpetual Master Services Agreement, and sources every drop-down. Growth is funded almost entirely by external capital. A newly disclosed material weakness in the review of HLBV accounting — the judgment-heavy allocation that converts a $231M consolidated GAAP loss into $169M of net income attributable to CWEN — undercuts confidence in reported earnings quality, and it lands alongside a CEG ERP-system migration in Q1 2026 that adds a second execution risk to the remediation.

The stock has drifted from a 52-week high of $41.74 to ~$32.09, largely tracking the higher-for-longer rate path that compresses yield-based valuations rather than any deterioration in cash flow or the dividend. We rate CWEN a HOLD on an income basis. The distribution is covered and growing, and there is a real growth story, but sponsor dependence, the control weakness, external-capital reliance, and rate sensitivity keep the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We move to a buy on evidence of control remediation, confirmed accretive drop-down economics, or a pullback that pushes the yield toward the low-6% area.

## Company Overview

Clearway does not develop projects; it acquires operating, contracted assets — primarily from CEG via drop-downs — and monetizes their contracted cash flows. In 2025, 98% of generation was renewable and roughly 91–97% of revenue was not tied to dispatching emitting power. The portfolio splits into two segments: Renewables & Storage (~10.1 GW wind, solar, and battery storage) and Flexible Generation (~2.8 GW of dispatchable gas, concentrated in California grid-reliability assets such as El Segundo, Marsh Landing, Walnut Creek, and Carlsbad).

The corporate structure is central to the thesis. CWEN, Inc. is a holding company owning 58.62% of the economics of Clearway Energy LLC; CEG owns the remaining 41.38% of economics but 54.89% of voting power through Class B/D shares, controls the board, and operates CWEN as a "controlled company" exempt from certain NYSE governance requirements. As of January 1, 2025, CWEN has no employees of its own; all labor is supplied by CEG under a perpetual Master Services Agreement. CEG itself is backed by TotalEnergies and GIP/BlackRock, which provides deep infrastructure-capital support but also concentrates control outside the public float.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 non-GAAP results were solid within the YieldCo framework: Adjusted EBITDA of $1,217M (+6% from $1,146M), CAFD of $430M (+1% from $425M), and cash from operations of $688M (down from $770M). The GAAP consolidated net loss was $231M, but net income attributable to CWEN was $169M (EPS $1.43). That gap is an artifact of HLBV accounting, which allocated roughly $400M of losses to tax-equity partners.

Revenue rose to $1,429M in 2025 from $1,371M in 2024 and $1,314M in 2023, +4.2% YoY, driven almost entirely by acquisitions and drop-downs reaching commercial operation (Rosamond South I, Luna Valley, Daggett 1, Pine Forest, Catalina, Tuolumne, Dan's Mountain). Organic growth is modest; wind resource ran slightly below normal in 2025. Below the revenue line, operating income fell to $160M from $196M in 2024 and $263M in 2023 as depreciation rose to $682M (accelerated depreciation from Mt. Storm/Goat Mountain repowerings) and O&M rose to $409M, while interest expense climbed to $387M.

For a YieldCo, the relevant read is dividend coverage and the funding model, not GAAP earnings. Dividends and distributions of $358M against CAFD of $430M imply ~1.2x coverage, which is adequate but not a large cushion. Growth is funded overwhelmingly by external capital: 2025 financing sources included $1,124M of net noncontrolling-interest contributions, $701M of revolver draws, $518M of new project debt, and $48M of equity. Total consolidated debt is $8,674M ($6,188M non-recourse at facility level, $2,486M corporate), with D/E ~1.85x and corporate ratings of BB/Ba2 (stable). Year-end liquidity was $1,061M, down from $1,330M as growth capital was deployed. The revolver was fully repaid in January 2026 using proceeds from a new $600M 5.75% senior notes issue due 2034.

An Altman Z-Score of 0.94 would flag "distress" on a naive screen, but it is a false signal for this business model — it reflects consolidated non-recourse project debt and an asset-heavy balance sheet that are standard for YieldCos and utilities. The genuine financial risks are refinancing/rate sensitivity and equity dilution, not near-term solvency. Management launched a $100M ATM plus DRIP/DSPP programs in August 2025 and raised ~$50M of equity at ~$34.60 in Q1 2026, signaling intent to part-fund growth with equity — a mild dilution watch-item at a share price now below that level. The dividend was raised each quarter (Q1'26 declared $0.4602, ~$1.84 annualized), with FY2025 dividend per class share of $1.77, +7.3% YoY.

## Growth Analysis

The growth narrative is more concrete than for most YieldCos. Management reaffirmed 2026 CAFD guidance of $470–510M, up roughly 15% over 2025, and a 2030 target of $2.90–3.10 CAFD/share with 5–8%+ growth beyond. The differentiating catalyst is data-center demand: the signed ~2 GW of contracts to serve data centers includes three Google PPAs (~1.1 GW) — Goat Mountain repower (Texas, 15-year), Swan Solar (Missouri, 650 MW, 20-year, 2028 COD), and Catamount wind (West Virginia, 20-year, 2028 COD). Google here is a customer buying power under offtake, not an owner. Additional offered investments include Royal Slope (520 MW solar+storage, Washington, 2027 COD) and the Deriva Solar third-party acquisition (613 MW, ~$210–230M net, expected 1H26 close). A late-stage CEG pipeline of 11.2 GW underpins the runway into 2028+.

This maps directly onto the confirmed data-center load surge visible across the power complex. The demand for long-tenor contracted renewable capacity is real and broad-based, and CWEN's contracted, operating-asset model captures it with lower merchant-price risk than pure IPPs. Two constraints temper the enthusiasm. First, the July 2025 tax legislation compresses the incentive window — wind/solar beginning construction after July 4, 2026 must be in service by December 31, 2027 to qualify for PTC/ITC, with new "foreign entity of concern" component-sourcing constraints. Management asserts the CEG pipeline through 2030 is unaffected, but the window has narrowed. Second, every leg of growth — development, drop-down pricing, and now the back-office ERP — runs through CEG. The 2028 Google projects remain subject to negotiation with CEG and Independent Director approval, so drop-down economics are not yet locked. The estimate revision picture is mixed and thinly covered (one to two analysts): current-quarter and next-year EPS estimates rose meaningfully (+57.1% and +97.3% over 30 days), but current-year swung to a loss (-212.4%) and next-quarter fell -45.3%, reflecting the volatility of GAAP EPS under HLBV rather than a clean fundamental trend. For a YieldCo, CAFD guidance is the better anchor, and there the trajectory is clearly upward.

## Valuation Assessment

CWEN trades at ~$32.09 with a ~5.6% dividend yield, well above the utility peer-median yield of 2.6% and above its own historical range. The trailing and forward P/E figures (~447x) are meaningless for a YieldCo whose reported EPS is distorted by non-cash HLBV loss allocation; the right lens is CAFD and yield. On 2026 CAFD guidance against ~121M Class C shares plus the broader share base, the stock is priced for the elevated yield the market demands for CEG-dependence, BB/Ba2 leverage, and the control-weakness overhang.

Against the Utilities peer set (OTTR 13.3x P/E, MWH 29.1x, AWR 22.4x, AVA 16.7x, MDU 22.7x; median P/E 22.4x, median yield 2.6%), CWEN's yield premium is the salient comparison — roughly 300bp above the peer median. Part of that gap is justified by structural factors (sponsor control, external-capital dependence, higher leverage, and 5-year TSR of 135.07 that trailed the peer group's 153.04). The remainder embeds the mispricing case: a covered, growing distribution with a concrete 2026 CAFD ramp and a 2030 target should not carry as wide a discount if remediation and drop-down accretion play out. The valuation dimension warrants restraint — cheapness on yield alone has historically been an unreliable buy trigger, and CWEN's discount reflects genuine structural risk rather than a clean mispricing. Analyst targets span $34–60 with a mean of ~$43.67 and median $42.50; Truist initiated in July 2026 at Buy with a $43 target. The wide dispersion signals disagreement over how heavily to weight the sponsor dependence and control overhang. We treat the yield support as a floor rather than the targets as a reliable pull, given the rate-path headwind to yield-based equities.

## Competitive Landscape

CWEN competes for offtake contracts against utilities and IPPs and for asset acquisitions against other YieldCos, IPPs, and infrastructure funds, several far larger. Its advantages are scale and diversification (12.9 GW across 27 states), a long-dated contracted book (~12-year weighted-average duration with Baa1-average counterparties), a low-operating-cost renewable fleet, and a captive development pipeline via CEG. The core competitive vulnerability is the same as the structural risk: growth is not internally sourced, and drop-down pricing is negotiated with the controlling shareholder.

The industry backdrop is favorable — the US is projected to add more than 90 GW of capacity in 2026 as data-center demand strains the grid, giving contracted renewable owners strong pricing and long-tenor offtake opportunities. Counterbalancing this is a hostile federal regulatory shift for wind specifically: EPA repeal of the Endangerment Finding, rollback of eagle/MBTA/NEPA protections, and DOI audits of wind eagle-take permits raise permitting and operational uncertainty for repowerings. Customer concentration is meaningful — SCE (22%) and PG&E (16%) are ~38% of consolidated revenue, top seven ~64% — and the 10-K explicitly recalls the 2019–2020 PG&E bankruptcy blocking distributions from certain assets, a concrete precedent for how counterparty stress can interrupt cash flow. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the demand tailwind but constrained by sponsor dependence and the wind-specific regulatory overhang.

## Risk Assessment

The most consequential company-specific risk is the newly disclosed material weakness in controls over HLBV accounting, identified in Q4 2025. HLBV is the mechanism that allocates income/loss to tax-equity partners and is central to how CWEN reports net income attributable to CWEN. A control weakness on the most judgment-heavy line that converts a consolidated loss into reported profit undercuts confidence in GAAP earnings quality until remediated. Remediation is underway but incomplete, and the concurrent CEG ERP-system rollout in Q1 2026 — on which CWEN relies for financial reporting and internal controls — adds a second execution risk. This does not threaten cash flow or the dividend, but it is a genuine, recently surfaced governance/reporting red flag that is not fully priced.

Structural CEG dependence is the second-order risk: the Master Services Agreement is perpetual and effectively non-terminable except for cause/bankruptcy, and certain PPAs and facility financings contain change-of-control provisions that could be triggered if CEG ceases to hold >50% voting power, potentially allowing counterparties to terminate or accelerate. Policy/tax-credit phase-out under the July 2025 law compresses the development window. Interest-rate and refinancing sensitivity is direct — ~$387M of interest expense and heavy reliance on external financing mean dividend growth is tied to capital-market conditions, and rising rates pressure the yield-based valuation. Tax-benefit dependency (no material cash income tax expected through 2030 on $212M federal NOLs plus PTCs/ITCs) is exposed to IRS challenge — two subsidiary partnership returns are under audit — and CWEN indemnifies tax-equity partners against credit disallowance.

Insider activity offers no signal: recent Form 4s are routine grants, CEO tax-withholding dispositions, and dividend-reinvestment accruals by CEG's backers, with no open-market buying or selling to read into. A May 15, 2026 8-K flags a director/officer change worth monitoring, and a May 1, 2026 8-K shows charter/bylaw modifications approved at the annual meeting.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.0% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 30.4% (a 7.6pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.0% near-term vs. 34.9% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress into the nearest expiry (implied move ±9.9%). Put/call skew is balanced at the front (0.6%) but turns negative at six months (-3.8%), consistent with some downside hedging further out. The near-term put/call volume ratio of 2.84 shows active put trading, though the low put/call OI ratio of 0.17 tempers that read. ATM IV collapsed from 68.3% to 38.0% since mid-June as the stock fell ~12.7%, suggesting a prior event risk has resolved and current pricing reflects more normalized uncertainty. Short interest is moderate at 7.5% of float (4.3 days to cover) — neither a crowded-short setup nor a squeeze catalyst. Net, the options market is consistent with a balanced, income-oriented HOLD rather than confirmation of either a strong upside or a collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$43–48).** Data-center demand converts the signed PPAs and the CEG pipeline into accretive drop-downs at Independent-Director-approved economics. 2026 CAFD lands at the upper end ($510M), the 2030 target is credibly on track, the HLBV material weakness is remediated cleanly, and a Fed pause/cut re-rates the yield-based equity. Truist's $43 target and the $60 high target represent this scenario, roughly 34–87% above the current price plus a ~5.6% yield.

**Bear case (~$26–28).** Higher-for-longer rates keep the yield-based valuation compressed and raise the cost of the external-capital funding model, forcing more dilutive equity issuance. The HLBV control weakness proves harder to remediate or reveals a restatement, and the concurrent ERP migration compounds reporting risk. Drop-down economics tighten as the tax-credit window closes, or a large California utility counterparty (SCE/PG&E, ~38% of revenue) experiences stress that interrupts distributions. This puts the stock 13–19% below current levels, with the ~5.6% yield partially cushioning.

**Base case (~$34–37).** CAFD ramps toward the middle of guidance, the dividend continues its ~7% annual growth and stays covered at ~1.2x, remediation proceeds without a restatement, and the stock holds near current levels as the rate headwind offsets modest CAFD growth. Total return is driven primarily by the ~5.6% yield plus mid-single-digit distribution growth, with limited multiple re-rating until sponsor and control risks de-risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (CAFD tracking vs. guidance and progress on HLBV remediation), the Deriva Solar close (1H26), and any 8-K disclosing completion of the material-weakness remediation or the outcome of the two partnership tax audits.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation that the HLBV material weakness is remediated with no restatement, ideally alongside stable ERP-migration reporting.
- Independent-Director-approved drop-down of the 2028 Google projects (Swan Solar, Catamount) at economics that visibly accrete to CAFD/share toward the 2030 target.
- A pullback that pushes the yield toward the low-6% area (~$29–30) with fundamentals intact, improving the income risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any restatement of net income attributable to CWEN or an expansion of the control weakness beyond HLBV.
- A cut or freeze in the dividend, or CAFD coverage falling below ~1.05x.
- Material stress at SCE or PG&E that blocks distributions from associated assets, or a change-of-control event at CEG triggering PPA/financing acceleration.

## Conclusion

Clearway is a covered-yield vehicle with a genuine, data-center-anchored growth runway and a well-defined 2030 CAFD target, trading at a wide yield premium to utility peers. The score has drifted lower (5.7 in May to 5.5 in June to 5.4 now), and the current read confirms rather than reverses that trend: fundamentals (CAFD coverage, dividend growth, backlog) are intact, but the newly surfaced HLBV control weakness, the concurrent ERP migration, and the structural CEG dependence keep earnings-quality and governance risk elevated, while the higher-for-longer rate path caps the yield-based re-rating. The decline from the 52-week high is predominantly rate-driven and does not reflect cash-flow deterioration, which improves the income risk/reward at the margin without turning it asymmetric.

We rate CWEN a HOLD on an income basis. The ~5.6% yield is covered and growing, and there is upside if remediation and accretive drop-downs materialize, but the risks are specific and not yet priced away. Own it for the distribution, monitor the remediation and drop-down economics closely, and re-rate to a buy on evidence of control cleanup, confirmed accretive growth, or a yield-widening pullback.
