## Executive Summary

MACOM is an operating-leverage story wrapped in a premium multiple, and the two facts have to be weighed against each other rather than resolved. The operating story is quantifiable and improving: revenue of $288.955M in Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 3, 2026) grew 22.5% year over year, GAAP gross margin reached 56.9%, and operating income of $50.831M produced a 17.6% operating margin against 14.8% a year earlier and a 10.1% trough in FY2024. The mechanism is fab utilization. MACOM absorbed three acquisitions between March 2023 and December 2023 (Linearizer, OMMIC, the Wolfspeed RF business) that brought underutilized internal wafer capacity; as volume fills those fabs, incremental gross margin runs approximately 64.6% (Q2 gross profit change of $34.277M on revenue change of $53.068M), roughly eight points above the corporate average. That is why consensus adjusted EPS can compound faster than the ~22% revenue line, and why FY2027 consensus has been revised up 23.5% in 90 days to $6.93 while FY2026 rose 12.4% to $5.03.

The valuation is where the argument gets harder. At $270.87 the equity is worth $20.67B (76.296M shares) and enterprise value is approximately $20.41B after netting $664.9M of cash and short-term investments against $344.3M of 2029 convertible notes, $30.2M of finance leases and a $36.7M financing obligation. That is 19.0x trailing-twelve-month revenue of $1,073.8M, 53.9x FY2026 consensus EPS and 39.1x FY2027 consensus. Both consensus figures are non-GAAP: in Q2 FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $0.60 against company-reported adjusted EPS of $1.09, so 45% of the adjusted number is add-backs, overwhelmingly stock compensation running 8-9% of revenue. On a GAAP basis the trailing multiple is roughly 118x and the FY2027 equivalent is closer to 50-55x.

The stock has fallen 35.3% from its 52-week high of $418.90 while estimates rose. That combination normally argues for upgrading, and the risk/reward is materially better than it was in June. But the peer set tells the honest story: First Solar is 38% off its high, Cerebras 48%, Amkor 32%, SiTime 39%, Lattice 21%. This was a sector-wide multiple event in a complex the market began repricing on cost of capital in late July, and MACOM — at 19x sales, one of the most extended names in it — moved with it. A derating from an extreme starting point to a still-premium one is not value creation. With results for the quarter ended July 3, 2026 due pre-market August 6 and the options market pricing a ±17.2% move, the correct action is to hold what you own for the profit cycle and let the print set the entry for new capital. Overall score 6.25, up from 6.0 in June.

## Company Overview

MACOM designs and manufactures high-performance analog, mixed-signal and photonic semiconductors spanning RF, microwave, millimeter-wave and lightwave frequencies. It reports as a single segment (the CODM is CEO Stephen Daly, who evaluates consolidated gross profit and net income) but discloses three end markets. FY2025: Industrial & Defense $419.8M (43.4% of revenue, +19.4%), Data Center $292.8M (30.3%, +48.0%), Telecom $254.6M (26.3%, +41.4%), total $967.3M, +32.6% — a figure modestly flattered by FY2025 being a 53-week year.

The structure is "fab-lite with strategic internal capacity": owned wafer fabs in Lowell MA, Research Triangle Park NC, Ann Arbor MI and Limeil-Brévannes France, supplemented by external foundries, with assembly and test across six sites including Hsinchu, Taiwan. Two attributes matter competitively. First, the Lowell fab holds Department of Defense Trusted Foundry accreditation, which is required for certain defense programs and which fabless competitors cannot replicate without equivalent qualification. Second, the materials portfolio (GaAs, GaN-on-SiC, InP, SiGe, with GaN-on-silicon in development) is unusually broad for a company at roughly $1.1B of revenue, and it is what allows one manufacturing base to serve defense radar, satellite communications and AI optics simultaneously.

In data center, MACOM's content is the analog and photonic layer around the optical engine: transimpedance amplifiers, modulator drivers, lasers, photodetectors, clock-and-data-recovery devices and optical post-amplifiers, sold as chipsets into 800G, 1.6T and emerging 3.2T pluggables across single-mode, multi-mode and silicon-photonics architectures. This is adjacent to, not competitive with, the DSP/retimer sockets held by Broadcom, Marvell and Credo, a distinction that matters when assessing competitive risk.

Q2 FY2026 end-market detail: I&D $120.652M (+22.4%, 41.8% of revenue), Data Center $98.188M (+36.0%, 34.0%), Telecom $70.115M (+7.6%, 24.2%).

## Financial Analysis

**Margin round-trip, now reversing.** Gross margin ran 60.2% (FY2022), 59.5% (FY2023), 54.0% (FY2024), 54.7% (FY2025), 56.4% (H1 FY2026), 56.9% (Q2 FY2026 GAAP). Operating margin: 19.6%, 16.6%, 10.1%, 13.4%, 16.7%, 17.6%. The FY2024 trough was integration cost and fab underutilization, not structural erosion, and the recovery has been continuous for six quarters without yet reaching the FY2022 peak.

**FY2025 GAAP net income is an artifact.** FY2025 revenue grew 32.6% with operating income of $129.7M and operating cash flow of $235.4M, yet GAAP net income was −$54.2M. The bridge is a $193.1M pre-tax loss on extinguishment of debt recognized in Q1 FY2025 when $288.8M of 2026 convertible notes were exchanged for 2029 notes plus 1,582,958 shares plus $17.6M cash, under early-adopted ASU 2024-04. The charge is overwhelmingly non-cash; only about $23.2M of fees moved through financing cash flow. Any screen anchored on FY2025 net income, ROE or retained earnings materially understates earning power. Screens built on operating income, revenue, gross margin, working capital and cash flow are unaffected. The charge was not costless: shareholders transferred roughly 1.58M shares and $17.6M of cash to extend maturity from 2026 to 2029 and cut the coupon from 0.25% to zero, but it says nothing about ongoing profitability.

**TTM figures (derived).** Revenue for Q3 FY2025 through Q2 FY2026 = $967.258M − $454.009M + $560.567M = $1,073.816M. TTM GAAP net income = −$54.210M − (−$135.864M) + $95.098M = $176.752M, which correctly excludes the Q1 FY2025 extinguishment charge. TTM GAAP diluted EPS ≈ $2.29 on ~77.1M shares.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $235.4M less capex $42.6M gives free cash flow of $192.8M, a 19.9% FCF margin. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $121.589M less $26.126M capex and $7.420M of software/licensed technology purchases, or $88.0M on the stricter definition. I am not annualizing the half-year: inventories rose from $237.844M to $252.195M, DSO sits near 50 days, and the fiscal calendar shifted from 53 to 52 weeks. As a labeled sensitivity, doubling H1 implies $176-191M for FY2026; a normalized case applying FY2025's 19.9% FCF margin to an annualized Q2 revenue run-rate of ~$1.16B implies ~$230M. The gap is working capital, not earnings power. Against $20.41B of enterprise value, even the normalized figure is a ~1.1% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet — the dimension that has genuinely improved.** At April 3, 2026: cash $98.521M plus short-term investments $566.337M = $664.858M liquid; current assets $1,126.050M against current liabilities $149.703M for a 7.52x current ratio; funded obligations approximately $407M; total liabilities/equity 0.42x; net cash roughly $253.0M. The current-ratio jump from 3.70x at year-end is mechanical: the 2026 notes left current liabilities when settled on March 16, 2026, not a working-capital improvement.

The remaining 2029 notes are $344.316M principal at a 0.00% coupon, maturing December 15, 2029, with no financial covenants. Conversion price is approximately $174.03; MACOM must settle principal in cash and may settle excess conversion value in cash or shares at its election. The company may call the notes for cash on or after December 20, 2027 if the stock trades at 130% of conversion (~$226.24) for 20 of 30 trading days, a threshold already cleared, which gives management a lever to retire the obligation on its own timetable.

**Dilution, correctly framed.** Gross shares on full conversion = $344.316M ÷ 1,000 × 5.7463 = 1,978,672, or 2.6% of the 76.296M shares outstanding. But because principal must be settled in cash, realistic net issuance is only the excess: at $270.87, conversion value $535.9M less $344.3M cash equals $191.6M ÷ $270.87 ≈ 707,300 shares, roughly 0.9% of existing shares and ~0.92% post-issuance ownership dilution. Reported Q2 diluted shares of 77.555M already include 1.423M convert-related shares under the treasury-stock method. The larger dilution source over the next two years is ordinary equity compensation: $137.0M of unrecognized RSU/PRSU cost amortizing over ~2.0 years, plus market-based PRSUs that pay 0-200% against the SOX index. These are contingent and belong in scenarios, not in a current diluted count.

**Financial health score: 7.5.** Net cash, covenant-free zero-coupon debt, 7.5x current ratio and a ~20% FCF margin against a business with meaningful fixed manufacturing cost. The offsets — stock compensation at 8-9% of revenue and $51.475M of H1 cash spent on tax withholding versus $26.126M of capex — are real quality deductions but not solvency issues.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, with different durability.

**Data Center (34.0% of Q2 revenue, +36.0%)** is the swing factor and the reason the multiple exists. Management raised full-year data-center growth guidance to over 60% on the May 7 call, though that figure comes from secondary reporting of issuer commentary rather than a filed exhibit. MACOM's content — TIAs, drivers, lasers, photodetectors into 800G/1.6T/3.2T pluggables — scales with port count and with the migration to higher lane rates. The 10-K states that capital spending on data center infrastructure to support AI applications is rapidly expanding and increasing demand for MACOM's products, and warns that a slowdown in that spending may reduce demand.

**Industrial & Defense (41.8%, +22.4%)** is the underappreciated feature. Program-driven, 5-20+ year product lives, low price elasticity, and demand currently supported by satellite communications and defense demand. Having over 40% of revenue in a cyclically decoupled market is genuine ballast against a hyperscaler digestion cycle, and it is the specific attribute external managers have identified.

**Telecom (24.2%, +7.6%)** is decelerating: Q2's +7.6% against a six-month +14.6% is a clear step down. This is the segment to watch on August 6.

**Estimate revisions are strong and broad.** Current quarter $1.35 (+17.6% over 90 days), next quarter $1.57 (+27.1%), FY2026 $5.03 (+12.4%), FY2027 $6.93 (+23.5%, and +1.3% in the last 30 days alone). Fourteen analysts cover the current-year line. Revisions of this magnitude are among the stronger forward indicators available, and I am weighting them positively. I am not treating them as a standalone BUY trigger, because the signal is most reliable when the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates — below roughly 30x forward or a PEG under 1.5. Here FY2026 sits at 53.9x, above the level at which rising estimates alone justify paying up, since continued beats are already the base case embedded in the price.

**Delivery track record argues for restraint.** Over the last four quarters: one beat (+1.6%), one in-line (−0.4%), two small misses (−0.8%, −1.6%). MACOM delivers to consensus rather than through it. At 54x current-year earnings, in-line is not a supportive outcome.

**Visibility is thin by disclosure.** The 10-K states plainly that backlog is not a reliable indicator, sales are purchase-order-based with substantial same-quarter turns business, and contract liabilities were only $4.160M at April 3, 2026, down from $7.676M. That balance is not backlog and should not be treated as such. Investors have essentially no contractual forward-revenue disclosure to underwrite.

**Growth prospects score: 8.0**, marginally below the prior 8.4 — not because growth deteriorated but because Telecom decelerated and because the growth dimension deserves confirmation from the August print before being extrapolated further.

## Competitive Landscape

MACOM sits in the analog/photonic layer of AI optics rather than the DSP/retimer socket, which is the relevant framing for competitive risk. Broadcom, Marvell and Credo — all named competitors in the 10-K — fight over retimers and DSPs; MACOM's direct rivals in TIAs, drivers and lasers are Semtech, MaxLinear, Sumitomo Electric Device Innovations and an expanding set of Chinese suppliers that the 10-K explicitly flags.

Three things support an improving competitive trajectory. The materials breadth (GaAs, GaN-on-SiC, InP, SiGe under one roof, with four internal fabs) is rare at this revenue scale and is what generates ~65% incremental gross margins as utilization rises. The Trusted Foundry accreditation is a real barrier in defense sockets. And the April 27, 2026 IQE plc transaction (subscription for newly issued ordinary shares representing under 12% of IQE, plus convertible loan notes, with board representation and long-term supply agreements) is a capital-light way to secure indium phosphide epiwafer allocation, which is the physical bottleneck for laser and photodetector supply into AI optics. To be precise on roles: MACOM is the investor and the wafer buyer; IQE is the securities issuer and the supplier. MACOM acquires no control. The currency denomination of the "30 million" equity and "15 million" convertible amounts was lost in the filing extraction and is unverified; GBP is likely given IQE's UK listing, but treat the size as unconfirmed. External reporting that IQE won a $14M multi-year Newport order from an unnamed "strategic global technology customer" does not name MACOM, and I do not treat that as connected.

Two competitive risks deserve weight. Migration toward co-packaged optics and more integrated silicon-photonics engines could compress discrete analog driver and TIA content per port over time, though MACOM's disclosed participation in silicon-photonics chipsets partly offsets this. And the 10-K names specific R&D programs that have already slipped: GaN-on-silicon, certain laser products, and an Air Force Research Laboratory process transfer. GaN-on-Si is the cost-structure lever and lasers are the data-center content lever, so these are not generic execution risks.

**Competitive position score: 7.0.** Content share is rising in a growing socket, the supply-chain position is being reinforced, and defense/satcom exposure is differentiated. The score is not higher because the moat is in a layer facing both integration risk and Chinese entry, and because the filings do not let me separate how much of the margin gain is utilization versus mix.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is deteriorating at the top.** Customer A rose to 18% of Q2 FY2026 revenue from 13% a year earlier, and to 19% of accounts receivable from 11% at October 3, 2025. Customer C is 12% of revenue and 12% of AR. The top-10 aggregate *fell* to 57% from 59%, which establishes only that the second tier grew faster than the top tier; it does not establish diversification and should not be netted against the Customer A move. The FY2025 10-K states no *direct* customer reached 10% while two *resellers* individually reached 12.4% and 11.2%, and distributor mix rose to 32.3% of revenue from 29.3% (FY2024) and 24.0% (FY2023). The conservative reading is that A and C are distributors, which means end demand is more fragmented than the number implies but MACOM has less visibility into it and carries channel-inventory risk. If A is instead a direct customer that crossed 10%, single-counterparty dependence is worse than my base reading. The disclosure does not allow me to distinguish these, and the difference matters for revenue durability.

**China exposure is large and growing faster than the company.** 34.3% of Q2 FY2026 revenue versus 27.1% a year earlier; H1 China revenue $184.402M against $126.578M, +45.7%. Geographic revenue is ship-to, so this includes contract manufacturers assembling for customers headquartered elsewhere and does not establish that a third of end demand is Chinese. The export-control and tariff exposure is nonetheless real for an RF/GaN supplier; the 10-K flags BIS export restrictions (October 2022 and 2023 rules) and Chinese indigenous-semiconductor policy as risks.

**No forward-revenue disclosure.** Purchase-order-based, substantial turns business, $4.2M of contract liabilities. Supplier purchase commitments were $157.1M at FY2025 year-end ($145.1M within one year) — MACOM's obligations, not customer orders.

**Multiple compression is the dominant near-term risk to total return.** The 10-K itself discloses a 35.4% cumulative decline between February 5 and April 8, 2025; a second, similar drawdown occurred in July 2026. At 19x sales, a sector-level cost-of-capital move produces losses independent of operating results.

**Governance gaps.** The CEO also chairs the board. Three separate Item 5.02 filings (August 8, 2025; November 25, 2025; January 12, 2026) could not be retrieved, so whether these were routine board refreshment or operating-leadership departures is unresolved. A related-party sale of $0.8M to Empower RF Systems, an affiliate of a director, is immaterial at 0.14% of H1 revenue but worth monitoring.

**Minor items.** Potentially responsible party at the Omega Chemical Superfund site since 1993 with an accrual management describes as immaterial; no material pending legal proceedings as of the Q2 filing; disclosure controls assessed effective; no restatement, non-reliance, auditor-change or impairment filings in the reviewed 8-K index. The $70M CHIPS memorandum of terms signed January 14, 2025 remains non-binding and under discussion eighteen months later and should carry zero value in a base case; the manufacturing investment tax credit, by contrast, is being realized (cumulative $8.0M reduction in asset carrying value at April 3, 2026, with the rate rising from 25% to 35% for assets placed in service after December 31, 2025).

Short interest of 3.0% of float with 1.1 days to cover means there is no squeeze mechanic in either direction; the move will be driven by the fundamental print.

**Risk factors score: 4.3.** Concentration rising, China 34% and growing, no backlog, a premium multiple exposed to sector derating, and a genuine binary in 48 hours.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $400 (58x FY2027 consensus $6.93)

Data-center revenue grows above 60% for the full year as guided and continues into FY2027 on the 1.6T ramp; incremental gross margin holds near 65%, taking adjusted gross margin toward 60% and operating margin above 20%, retracing the FY2022 peak on a much larger revenue base; I&D compounds in the low-20s on satcom and defense; Telecom re-accelerates off easy comparisons. FY2027 adjusted EPS beats $6.93. The IQE relationship secures InP allocation as competitors are supply-constrained. The 2029 converts are called after December 2027 with roughly 0.9-1.4% of dilution and a manageable cash outflow. Consensus targets ($400 median) prove to be the right anchor rather than stale optimism. This is +47.7% from $270.87.

### Bear Case — $175 (25x FY2027 consensus $6.93; equivalently ~35x FY2026 $5.03)

Hyperscaler optics orders digest after two years of pull-forward, and MACOM's absent backlog disclosure means the first visible evidence is a guide-down rather than a slowdown in bookings. Telecom's +7.6% is the leading edge rather than a one-quarter dip. Customer A's 18%/19% revenue and receivable share turns out to reflect channel inventory that has to be worked down. Chinese analog competition and any co-packaged-optics transition compress driver/TIA content pricing, and the 64.6% incremental gross margin proves to be mix and one-time utilization gains — including the non-recurring $10.1M gain on acquired RTP inventory in FY2025 — rather than durable leverage. Export controls constrain the China revenue base. The multiple resets toward the level it held in November 2025. This is −35.4%.

### Base Case — $305 (44x FY2027 consensus $6.93)

Growth decelerates gradually from the current 22.5% toward the high teens as data center normalizes from +36% and Telecom stays soft; operating margin grinds to 18-19%; FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $6.93; and the multiple settles in the low-40s, a premium to the analog group justified by 35%+ EPS growth and a net-cash balance sheet but below the 58x embedded in sell-side targets. This is +12.6% from $270.87.

Base-case upside of +12.6% against bear-case downside of −35.4% is roughly 0.36:1. That asymmetry, not any doubt about the operating story, is what keeps this a HOLD.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD (5.7 → 6.2 → 6.1 → 6.0 → 6.25), and the bar for repeating a call should rise. Two things justify continuing it with a higher score. Financial health improved and stayed improved: the 2026 notes are fully retired, the remaining obligation is covenant-free and zero-coupon, and net cash is approximately $253M. Growth prospects remain strong. Management quality, previously downgraded from 5.5 to 4.5, stays low on evidence that has not changed direction — eight insiders selling across February through June 2026 with zero open-market purchases. The price decline from $391.41 to $270.87 since the June report reflects sector multiple compression, not fundamental deterioration, and it improves risk/reward materially. It does not improve it enough to clear a BUY at 53.9x current-year consensus two days before a ±17% event.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is positive with identifiable catalysts; the entry price is not.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 6, 2026, pre-market — Q3 FY2026 results for the quarter ended July 3, 2026. Secondary checkpoints: the FY2026 fourth-quarter report in November 2026 (first full-year data-center growth confirmation against the >60% framing), and closing of the IQE subscription, expected in fiscal Q3 2026 subject to regulatory approval.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of the following:**
1. Price in the $230-245 range with data-center guidance intact — that would be roughly 33-35x FY2027 consensus $6.93 and a PEG near 0.9, at which point rising estimates and a premium multiple stop working against each other.
2. The August 6 print delivers adjusted gross margin at or above 59% *and* a Q4 revenue guide implying full-year data-center growth of 60% or better, with Telecom re-accelerating above 10% year over year. That combination would validate the 65% incremental margin as utilization rather than mix and support FY2027 estimates above $7.50.
3. Customer A's revenue share stabilizes at or below 18% with accounts-receivable share falling back toward 13-15%, resolving the channel-inventory question.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. Data-center revenue grows below 20% year over year in any quarter, or management withdraws or lowers the full-year data-center growth framing.
2. Adjusted gross margin declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters, indicating the incremental-margin story was mix or one-time utilization.
3. Customer A exceeds 22% of revenue or 25% of accounts receivable, or distributor mix exceeds 38% of revenue — either would signal channel loading ahead of a correction.
4. Any export-control action that restricts shipments into the China revenue base.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold through the print rather than adding. Volatility at 100.3% implied against 56.7% realized makes both hedging and speculating expensive; the fundamental story is intact and the profit cycle is not over.

## Conclusion

MACOM has done the hard operational work. Operating margin has recovered from 10.1% to 17.6% in eight quarters, incremental gross margin runs near 65% as three acquired fabs fill, the balance sheet went from a near-term convertible maturity to $253M of net cash and covenant-free zero-coupon debt, and consensus FY2027 EPS has been revised up 23.5% in 90 days. Roughly 42% of revenue sits in defense and industrial markets that are cyclically decoupled from hyperscaler capex, which is a differentiated attribute in an AI-exposed semiconductor name.

What stops this from being a buy is arithmetic. At $270.87 the enterprise is valued at 19.0x trailing revenue, 53.9x current-year consensus and 39.1x next-year consensus, where consensus is non-GAAP and GAAP earnings run roughly 55% of adjusted. Normalized free cash flow supports about a 1.1% yield. Base-case upside of 12.6% sits against 35.4% bear-case downside, and the company delivers to consensus rather than through it — one beat, one in-line and two small misses in the last four quarters. Results land in two days with the options market pricing ±17.2%. The 35% drawdown from the 52-week high has meaningfully improved the setup, but it compressed an extreme multiple to a high one; it did not create a mispricing. Hold, and let August 6 set the entry.
