## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is delivering on its post-merger integration thesis with accelerating momentum. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.78 beat consensus by 16%, organic growth ran at 10%, and management raised full-year guidance. The $268B backlog — 3x annual revenue — provides visibility that few industrial companies can match. Operating margins expanded from 5.2% in 2023 (impacted by the Powder Metal charge) to 10.5% in FY2025, with further expansion ahead as the commercial aftermarket mix increases and defense volumes scale on fixed infrastructure.

The stock trades at approximately 36x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.96) but approximately 25.9x FY2026 adjusted estimates ($6.91), at the upper end of peer comparables but justified by RTX's dual commercial/defense positioning and the structural defense spending tailwind from Golden Dome, Iran-related demand, and NATO spending commitments. The 37% price appreciation over the past year reflects fundamental improvement — trailing GAAP EPS nearly doubled from $2.23 in FY2023 to $4.96, and adjusted EPS grew from $5.73 to $6.29.

Key risks center on tariff impacts to fixed-price defense contracts, the residual Powder Metal remediation (~$0.7B FY2026 cash impact with elevated aircraft-on-ground levels expected through 2026), and elevated geopolitical complexity including China sanctions on the Raytheon business. The 1.5% dividend yield provides minimal income support, and share repurchases remain paused for deleveraging — capital return acceleration is a 2027+ story.

## Company Overview

RTX is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B FY2025), formed from the 2020 combination of United Technologies and Raytheon Company. Three segments provide exposure across the aerospace value chain:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B, 34%): Avionics, aerostructures, interiors, mechanical systems — high aftermarket attach rate
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B, 37%): Commercial and military engines, including the GTF family powering A320neo and F135 for F-35
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B, 32%): Missiles, integrated air defense (Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS), radars (SPY-6), advanced weapons

Revenue splits approximately 48% commercial aerospace and other commercial sales / 38% U.S. government / 15% foreign military sales, providing diversification across cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $67.1B (2022) → $68.9B (2023) → $80.7B (2024) → $88.6B (2025). Organic growth of 11% in FY2025 and 10% in Q1 2026 demonstrates sustained acceleration. P&W aftermarket (+$2.9B YoY) and Raytheon defense volume (+$1.7B) are the primary engines.

**Margin expansion:** Operating margins recovered from the 2023 trough (5.2%) to 10.5% in FY2025. Adjusted segment operating margins expanded approximately 70bps in Q1 2026 (from 11.9% to 12.6%). The mix shift toward commercial aftermarket (higher margins) and defense volume leverage on fixed infrastructure drives this. Management guides continued expansion through FY2027.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow surged to $10.6B in FY2025 (vs. $7.2B in FY2024), with FCF of $7.9B representing a 9.0% FCF margin. At the current $241B market cap, the trailing FCF yield is approximately 3.3%. FY2026 company guidance calls for $8.25B–$8.75B in FCF, bringing the forward yield closer to 3.4-3.6%.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $37.9B (reduced $3.4B in FY2025) against $7.4B cash. Net debt of ~$30.5B. Total debt-to-equity of approximately 0.56x, with total liabilities-to-equity of approximately 1.55x — reflecting the large intangible asset base from the merger. Credit agencies upgraded outlooks to stable in 2025. Goodwill and intangibles of $85.2B reflect merger accounting — the Altman Z-Score of 1.62 is structurally misleading for this reason. Debt maturities of $9.8B through 2028 are easily serviceable from $10.6B annual OCF.

**Earnings quality:** $2.0B annually in acquisition accounting amortization depresses GAAP EPS by ~$1.15/share. Adjusted EPS of $6.29 (FY2025), up 10% from $5.73 in FY2024, better reflects operating economics. The consistent earnings beat pattern (4 consecutive quarters, average surprise of +12%) suggests consensus estimates remain conservative.

## Growth Analysis

Three structural growth vectors provide multi-year visibility:

1. **Commercial aftermarket (P&W + Collins):** GTF fleet maturation drives inspection/shop visit revenue. The Powder Metal issue generates aftermarket work as the fleet cycles through inspections. P&W commercial aftermarket grew 21% in Q1 2025. GTF Advantage certification (April 2026) positions for next-gen demand. AirAsia's 150-unit A220 order (May 2026) confirms ongoing OE demand.

2. **Defense volume ramp:** $268B backlog (+23% YoY) with particularly strong growth in missiles and integrated air defense. The $156.2B supplementary DoW funding through 2029 includes $24.4B for Golden Dome and $25.4B for munitions/supply chain resiliency. RTX is among 12 companies selected to compete for $3.2B in space-based interceptor contracts. RTX's Patriot, SM-3, and LTAMDS platforms are positioned to benefit, though actual revenue impact depends on specific contract awards and timelines.

3. **Margin expansion:** Operating leverage on defense production ramp-up, commercial aftermarket mix shift, and P&W's improving cost structure as Powder Metal work normalizes. Management's segment margin expansion guidance of 75-125bps annually through FY2027 appears achievable given Q1 2026 performance.

Consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS of $6.91 (adjusted) growing to $7.55 in FY2027 represent ~9-10% annual EPS growth. Given the beat pattern and structural demand tailwinds, these estimates likely prove conservative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $178.80, RTX trades at:
- ~36x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.96, depressed by $1.15 of acquisition amortization)
- 25.9x FY2026 adjusted consensus ($6.91)
- 23.7x FY2027 adjusted consensus ($7.55)
- 3.3% trailing FCF yield; ~3.4-3.6% forward (based on company guidance of $8.25B-$8.75B)

**Peer context:** GE Aerospace trades at 37.6x, Howmet at 73.5x, Boeing at 92.6x (loss-adjusted). LMT at 24.8x and GD at 21.9x represent pure-defense comparables. RTX's premium to LMT/GD reflects its commercial aerospace exposure (higher growth, higher margin aftermarket) and superior organic growth rate. The premium to LMT (~4-5 turns) appears warranted given RTX's diversification and growth trajectory.

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms. A 25.9x forward multiple on a company growing EPS ~9-10% annually implies a PEG of ~2.6-2.9x — elevated but within range for high-visibility industrial compounders with $268B backlogs. The market is paying for predictability and structural demand tailwinds.

**What the market may underappreciate:** Defense bookings from Golden Dome-related initiatives have not yet fully flowed through estimates. If specific contract awards materialize for RTX in H2 2026, consensus FY2027 estimates may prove conservative. Additionally, the Powder Metal liability declining from $2.9B to $0.7B suggests the cash headwind is largely behind, potentially freeing FCF for capital return acceleration in 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX occupies a unique position as the only major A&D company spanning commercial avionics, jet engines, and full-spectrum missile defense. This provides:

- **Diversification**: Commercial aerospace cycles differently from defense spending, smoothing earnings volatility
- **Aftermarket moat**: Installed base of engines and avionics creates decades of recurring revenue (P&W's $151B backlog alone)
- **Entrenched franchise positions**: AMRAAM missile (sole-source, including a recent $3.5B Lots 39/40 award), F135 engine for F-35, Patriot air defense system — switching costs are measured in decades and billions

Against GE Aerospace (direct competitor in commercial engines via LEAP), P&W's GTF Advantage certification strengthens competitive positioning. The GTF architecture offers fuel efficiency advantages that matter more as airlines prioritize operating costs.

Against LMT/NOC/GD in defense, Raytheon's focus on missiles and integrated air defense positions it directly for the highest-growth defense subsector (missile defense spending growing significantly under Golden Dome).

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs on fixed-price contracts (HIGH):** RTX has disclosed that its businesses and suppliers import goods subject to both U.S. tariffs and counter-tariffs imposed since February 2025. Fixed-price defense contracts cannot easily pass through input cost increases. While management stated in Q1 2026 filings that current tariffs are not expected to have a material adverse effect based on present conditions, significant uncertainty remains around duration, scope, and escalation. The current tariff regime creates ongoing margin pressure on legacy contracts. Mitigation depends on supply chain restructuring and future contract repricing — a 1-3 year resolution timeline.

**Powder Metal residual risk (MODERATE, declining):** $0.7B estimated FY2026 cash impact, with aircraft-on-ground levels expected to remain elevated through 2026. Progress is clear (AOGs -15% QoQ, MRO output +23% YoY), but the fleet management challenge continues. Q1 2026 accrued customer compensation was still $0.5B. The GTF Advantage engine offers durability and performance improvements for future production. While the issue may increase shop-visit volumes, ongoing customer compensation costs and operational disruption partially offset aftermarket revenue benefits.

**China sanctions (MODERATE):** China sanctioned Raytheon over Taiwan arms sales. RTX's China revenue exposure is minimal, but supply chain disruption (titanium, rare earths) could escalate.

**Compliance/legal overhang (MODERATE):** Active DPAs with DOJ, SEC administrative order, and State Department consent agreement create compliance monitoring costs and, in extreme scenarios, potential debarment risk from government contracting. An independent compliance monitor is being established.

**U.S. budget concentration (STRUCTURAL):** 38% revenue from U.S. government. While Golden Dome funding provides near-term support, longer-term budget politics remain a permanent consideration.

## Conclusion

RTX remains a BUY at $178.80, supported by $268B backlog visibility, accelerating organic growth, margin expansion, and structural defense spending tailwinds. The valuation at 25.9x forward is not cheap, but the combination of ~9-10% EPS growth, consistent beat pattern (4/4 quarters), and multi-decade demand catalysts (Golden Dome, GTF fleet maturation) justifies the premium vs. pure-defense peers.

The score trajectory shows a slight decline from 6.9 to 6.6 over recent weeks, driven primarily by tariff-related uncertainty and the post-earnings selloff. However, the Q1 beat-and-raise, ongoing Powder Metal resolution, and Golden Dome positioning have strengthened the fundamental case even as the stock pulled back from $214 to $178, improving the risk/reward entry point. The tariff headwind is real but manageable for a company generating $10.6B in annual operating cash flow with improving credit metrics.

Position sizing of 2-3% is appropriate given the elevated multiple (reducing margin of safety) and tariff policy uncertainty. The defense sector structural overweight thesis remains intact following [unverified] continued geopolitical tensions.
