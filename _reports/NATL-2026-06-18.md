**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On February 26, 2026, the company signed a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by The Brink's Company (NYSE: BCO) for $30.00 in cash plus 0.1574 Brink's shares per NATL share, with shareholder votes for both companies scheduled around June 30, 2026 and an expected close in Q1 2027. At the current price of $43.20, every analytical question collapses into deal arithmetic: the value of the fixed cash component, the variable value of the Brink's stock leg, and the probability the transaction completes. A discounted-cash-flow exercise on the underlying ATM-and-network business is largely academic until the deal either closes or breaks.

The setup is asymmetric, and not favorably so. NATL rose roughly 9% on the announcement while Brink's fell roughly 20%, signaling that investors view the deal as good for NATL holders but value-destructive for the acquirer. That dynamic raises a genuine possibility that Brink's shareholders reject the transaction at the June 30 vote. If they do, NATL reverts toward its ~$40 pre-offer level, re-exposing holders to a business carrying ~$2.82 billion of debt against ~$396 million of equity, $1.35 billion of 9.5% senior secured notes, compressing gross margins, a seasonally negative Q1 operating cash flow quarter, and a two-episode accounting-control history in barely two years as a public company.

The recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is an event-driven position with a capped upside to a clean close and a real, identifiable downside to a deal break. There is no durable 12-month growth thesis underpinning the equity; the case for holding rests on the deal completing at terms that exceed the pre-offer baseline, which is contingent on a vote whose outcome the market is actively questioning. For holders, the decision is whether the spread compensates for break risk and the multi-quarter carry to a 2027 close. For prospective buyers, the risk/reward does not clear the bar.

## Company Overview

NCR Atleos is a global financial-technology company centered on self-directed banking: ATM and interactive teller machine (ITM) hardware, software, and services, plus the proprietary Allpoint network, the largest retail surcharge-free independent ATM network in the U.S., spanning roughly 78,000 self-service locations, with networks in 13 countries and ATM servicing in 65. The company was spun off from NCR Corporation (now NCR Voyix) on October 16, 2023, and runs three segments:

- **Self-Service Banking (~67% of revenue)**: ATM hardware, software, and services to financial institutions. The strategic growth engine is ATM-as-a-Service (ATMaaS), which management says roughly doubles recurring revenue per machine versus a traditional hardware-plus-maintenance contract.
- **Network (~29%)**: Transaction and interchange revenue from the Allpoint network, serving banks, credit unions, fintechs, neobanks, and retailers; includes LibertyX (Bitcoin) and ReadyCode (digital-to-cash).
- **Telecommunications & Technology (~4%)**: Managed network/infrastructure services; declining and non-core.

Roughly 55% of revenue is non-U.S., and the company employs about 20,000 people. The competitor set named in NATL's own 10-K includes Fiserv, FIS, Euronet, Diebold Nixdorf, Hyosung, and Brink's, its now-acquirer. That overlap is the source of the antitrust angle on the pending deal and underscores the horizontal/vertical consolidation logic Brink's is pursuing by combining cash-in-transit/cash management with ATM operation and network economics.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026).** Revenue was $1,043 million, up 7% year over year. Self-Service Banking grew 12%, driven by 23% hardware growth amid an industry refresh cycle and ATMaaS up 29%. Network was roughly flat as domestic transaction volumes softened, and T&T fell 7%. Recurring revenue was $754 million, or 72.3% of total, down from 75.7% as one-time hardware sales outgrew the recurring base.

Gross margin compressed about 130 basis points to 22.4% (adjusted 24.5%, down 140 bps), driven by tariffs, higher vault-cash costs, and component inflation, with management warning component costs may stay elevated. Operating income fell 10% to $84 million on severance/workforce-optimization and strategic-initiative costs. Net income was $22 million ($0.29 diluted EPS) versus $14 million ($0.19) a year earlier, while Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $172 million (16.5% margin).

Operating cash flow was negative $(9) million versus positive $123 million a year earlier, driven by working-capital swings and merchant-settlement timing; adjusted unrestricted FCF was $(13) million. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter, but on a balance sheet carrying ~$250 million in annual interest expense, a negative cash quarter warrants monitoring.

**Balance sheet and leverage — the central standalone risk.** Total debt of approximately $2.82 billion sits against roughly $396 million of equity, a heavily leveraged post-spin structure. The key tranche is $1,350 million of 9.500% senior secured notes due 2029, supplemented by $1,263 million of term loans and $205 million drawn on the revolver (SOFR-based, ~6.3%). Interest expense runs $63 million per quarter (~$250 million annualized). About 48% of borrowings are fixed; the remainder is rate-sensitive, as is vault-cash rental cost, where a 100 bp move is worth roughly $10 million per year before swaps. The company holds $2.0 billion notional of interest-rate swaps hedging vault cash through March 2027. The U.S. pension is underfunded by $248 million (partly offset by a $211 million overfunded non-U.S. plan), with a ~$48 million U.S. contribution planned for 2026.

**Capital allocation.** Of a $200 million buyback authorization, only ~0.4 million shares (~$15 million) were repurchased in Q1 2026, and management stated it will not repurchase more shares for the rest of the year, consistent with deal-pending interim operating restrictions. There is no dividend. As a standalone, the financial-health profile screens in the distress zone on Altman Z and shows very high debt-to-equity; the trajectory of improving net income is the lone offset, and it does not change the leverage picture.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth of 7% in Q1, led by ATMaaS (+29%) and a hardware refresh cycle (+23%), shows the secular thesis is intact at the segment level: banks are rationalizing branches and shifting toward outsourced, omni-channel models, which favors ATMaaS conversion and Allpoint network economics. ATMaaS roughly doubling recurring revenue per machine is the structural lever, and the mix shift toward recurring is the value-creation story management has been selling.

The forward picture is deteriorating in the estimate data, which is the dominant signal here. Consensus EPS has been cut across every horizon: current quarter to $0.97 from $1.15 thirty days ago (-15%), current year to $4.20 from $4.88 thirty days ago (-14%), and next year to $5.32 from $5.94 ninety days ago (-10.5%). The most recent quarter also missed badly — $0.65 actual versus $0.89 estimate, a -26.9% surprise — breaking a prior pattern of beats. Falling estimates plus a large miss is negative earnings momentum, and it cuts directly against any growth-acceleration narrative. Margin compression (tariffs, vault-cash, components) is the proximate cause, and management has flagged that cost pressure may persist.

Network revenue was flat as domestic transaction volumes softened, which touches the long-term secular question: proliferation of digital payments (Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, contactless) could erode ATM transaction volumes over time. That headwind is partly offset by ATMaaS recurring conversion and fintech-channel growth, but it caps the durability of the network leg. For the purposes of a 12-month thesis, the standalone growth story is being revised down, not up.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades at $43.20 against a forward P/E of ~19.3x. The peer set in transaction and payment processing shows wide dispersion — FOUR at 44.0x, EEFT at 9.7x, WU at 5.4x, PAYO at 33.9x, SEZL at 30.8x, for a peer median of ~30.8x. On surface multiples, NATL looks cheaper than the median, but that comparison is irrelevant for two reasons. First, the standalone business carries far more leverage than most peers, so an EV-based comparison would narrow or eliminate the apparent discount. Second, and decisively, the equity no longer trades on earnings — it trades on the deal.

The merger consideration is $30.00 cash plus 0.1574 Brink's shares. The fixed cash leg is worth $30.00; the variable leg depends on where BCO trades into close. At the current NATL price of $43.20, the implied value of the stock leg is roughly $13.20, which implies a Brink's price of around $84. The spread to a clean close is therefore thin — single-digit percentage upside spread out over a multi-quarter timeline to Q1 2027, partly offset by the variability of the BCO component. Against that, a deal break reverts the stock toward its ~$40 pre-offer level, a downside of roughly 7% from here before considering whether standalone re-rating pressure (leverage, margin, accounting flags) takes it lower.

A "cheap" forward multiple is not a buy signal in this situation; it is a function of a capped takeout price layered on a deteriorating standalone earnings base. The valuation score reflects a roughly balanced but unattractive setup — modest spread capture if the deal closes, a real haircut if it does not, with no independent catalyst to reward the multiple beyond the merger itself.

## Competitive Landscape

NATL holds a genuinely defensible asset in Allpoint, the largest surcharge-free independent ATM network in the U.S., which provides distribution and transaction-economics scale that is hard to replicate. ATMaaS is a credible share-gaining motion as banks outsource self-service infrastructure, and the 12% Self-Service Banking growth in Q1 suggests the company is participating in the refresh cycle.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than improving. Gross margin compression reflects external cost pressure but also evidence that the company lacks the pricing power to fully pass through tariffs and component inflation. The Network segment's flat revenue on softening domestic volumes signals the secular cash-displacement headwind is becoming visible in the numbers. The industry is consolidating — the Brink's acquisition is itself a consolidation play creating an end-to-end cash-ecosystem player — and that same logic is why antitrust scrutiny is plausible, given Brink's was a listed competitor. On balance the moat is real but the trajectory is roughly stable, with structural questions around long-run ATM transaction volumes that the ATMaaS conversion only partly answers.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk now dominates.** Both companies must approve the merger, and the market's split verdict on the announcement raises the real possibility Brink's shareholders vote it down on June 30. Regulatory gating — antitrust given Brink's is a direct competitor, plus money-transmitter license transfers and foreign approvals — is an additional path to delay or failure. A break reverts NATL toward ~$40 and reintroduces the full leverage/standalone risk profile. A $145 million termination fee is payable by Atleos under certain circumstances.

**Accounting and governance flags.** A material weakness in controls over contract cancellations/customer credits (revenue, unbilled AR, contract liabilities) was disclosed in a 2024 Form 10-K/A and declared remediated as of December 31, 2025. Separately, Q2/Q3 2025 brought "immaterial" revisions (subscription-software revenue recognition, network revenue/cost treatment, FX indirect-tax remeasurement) totaling a $43 million cumulative retained-earnings correction. Two accounting-correction episodes in roughly two years as a public company is a quality flag in a complex multi-element ATMaaS/software revenue model, even with auditor attestation of remediation.

**Leverage and refinancing (standalone).** The 9.5% notes are expensive, covenants restrict flexibility, and the structure is rating-downgrade sensitive. Brink's obtained a change-of-control put waiver from noteholders in March 2026 so the notes need not be repurchased at close — relevant only if the deal completes.

**Voyix entanglement.** 50% sharing (above a $15M/yr threshold) of legacy environmental matters, most notably the Kalamazoo River Superfund site (Voyix reserve $111M, potentially "more than double"); NATL accrued $27M for shared environmental matters plus an increased $24M Hoak deferred-comp settlement accrual (April 2026). Tax-matters-agreement joint-and-several liability for the pre-spin group is a further contingent exposure.

**Litigation overhang and macro.** Kahn Swick & Foti and others are investigating deal price/process — routine M&A friction but a source of noise. Tariff and component-cost pressure hit Q1 gross margin (~$11M net impact in Self-Service Banking); a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs and NATL accrued an immaterial refund receivable.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 22.7% against one-year historical volatility of 37.7%, leaving IV cheap relative to realized (spread -15.0%) and in the 5th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (near 22.7%, longer-dated ~30.0%), a relatively complacent profile consistent with a merger-arb situation where the near-term path is anchored by deal terms and the longer-dated vol prices the binary 2027 outcome. The put/call open-interest ratio of 1.47 indicates a mild protective lean, and the nearest-expiry implied move of ±13.1% reflects positioning around the June 30 vote. One delta reading shows ATM IV spiking to 334.6% — almost certainly a thin/corrupted contract artifact rather than a real signal, and treated as non-informative. The clean read: options are pricing low near-term vol with a tail around vote/close events, consistent with the merger framing.

Short interest is low at 4.5% of float with 2.9 days to cover — neither a squeeze setup nor evidence of an aggressive informed-short thesis, which fits a capped-upside arb name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Brink's deal closes on terms ($30 cash + 0.1574 BCO) at or above the current implied value. If BCO stabilizes or recovers from its announcement drop, the stock leg is worth more, and a clean close delivers the spread plus any BCO appreciation. The noteholder put waiver removes one friction; the strategic logic (end-to-end cash ecosystem) is coherent, and money-transmitter/antitrust approvals clear. Upside target approximates deal value, roughly $44–46 depending on BCO, from $43.20.

**Bear case.** Brink's shareholders reject the deal on June 30, given the market's verdict that the transaction destroys value for the acquirer. NATL reverts toward its pre-offer level and then faces standalone re-rating pressure from falling estimates, margin compression, a negative Q1 operating cash flow, the existing debt load, and a two-episode accounting history. Bear target ~$36–40, roughly 7–17% below current.

The asymmetry favors caution: limited upside spread to a 2027 close against a meaningful downside on a break, with the swing factor (a Brink's shareholder vote) outside NATL's control and actively doubted by the market.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain — an event-driven position without a standalone 12-month growth thesis. The equity's fate is decided by the merger vote and regulatory process, not by operating fundamentals.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Both shareholder votes pass cleanly around June 30, 2026, removing the dominant break risk and tightening the spread to a near-certain close, especially if BCO stabilizes so the stock leg holds value.
- Clear regulatory progress (HSR clearance, money-transmitter license transfer path confirmed) materially de-risking the timeline to Q1 2027.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Brink's shareholders vote down the deal, or either board signals an intent to renegotiate/terminate, returning the stock to standalone fundamentals.
- Further EPS estimate cuts or a second consecutive operating-cash-flow miss, confirming standalone deterioration that would govern a post-break price.
- Any new accounting/control disclosure, given the two-episode history.

**Reassessment timeline:** The June 30, 2026 shareholder votes are the decisive near-term checkpoint and the binary the entire position hinges on, with the regulatory close path (toward Q1 2027) as the secondary monitor.

## Conclusion

NATL is a merger-arbitrage instrument trading on deal arithmetic, not on the underlying ATM and network business. The spread to a clean Brink's close is modest and partly contingent on where BCO trades into close, while a deal break — plausible given the market's split reaction and the requirement for Brink's shareholder approval — reverts the equity toward its pre-offer level and exposes holders to a heavily leveraged, margin-pressured, accounting-flagged standalone profile with falling forward estimates. There is no independent 12-month growth thesis to fall back on if the deal fails.

The recommendation is HOLD (uncertain). The position is reasonable to maintain for the spread capture into a likely close, but the reward is capped by deal terms, the downside on a break is real, and the controlling variable is a vote the market is openly questioning. Prospective buyers should wait for the June 30 vote outcome before committing; the current risk/reward does not justify new money, and a vote failure would convert this into an AVOID.
