## Executive Summary

Acuity Inc. is a structurally sound industrial company generating ~$533M in free cash flow on $4.35B in revenue, with a consolidated gross margin of 48.4% and improving profitability in its technology-oriented AIS segment. The QSC acquisition ($1.2B, closed Jan 2025) is integrating well: AIS operating margin expanded from 10.0% in FY2025 to 14.4% in Q1 FY2026 as purchase accounting distortions roll off. Management has already repaid $300M of the $600M acquisition term loan in under 12 months, demonstrating FCF strength and conservative capital allocation.

The specific, falsifiable risk preventing a BUY recommendation is **Mexico manufacturing concentration**. 55% of Acuity's finished goods are produced in seven Mexican facilities. In the current tariff regime — where the administration has already imposed tariffs on Mexico under IEEPA (though recently struck down by SCOTUS), and Section 232 investigations are underway — this concentration creates a binary downside scenario that the market has not fully resolved. A 25% tariff on Mexican imports would directly impair ~$1.2B of Acuity's $2.2B COGS base, potentially cutting operating income by $150-300M (30-60% of FY2025 operating income of $565M) before any pass-through to customers. The SCOTUS ruling removed IEEPA tariff authority, but the administration is pursuing Section 232 and congressional alternatives that could reimpose tariffs within months. Until this policy uncertainty is resolved — or the company demonstrates it can pass through cost increases without demand destruction — the risk-reward is ambiguous. This risk is concrete, quantifiable, and has a defined resolution timeline (Section 232 investigation outcomes expected H2 2026).

Supporting the cautious stance: estimate revisions are uniformly negative (current quarter EPS down 5.3% over 90 days), the options market is pricing imminent stress (58.8% near-term IV vs. 34.6% HV, steep backwardation), and ABL organic growth remains flat at ~1% against a weakening construction backdrop ($116 oil, consumer contraction, potential recession probability 40-45%).

## Company Overview

Acuity Inc. (renamed from Acuity Brands in March 2025) operates two segments. **Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL)** contributes ~83% of revenue as the largest North American lighting manufacturer, selling through independent agents and electrical distributors into non-residential and residential construction markets. Key brands include Lithonia Lighting, Juno, Holophane, and nLight. **Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS)** at ~18% of revenue (up from 8% pre-QSC) includes Distech Controls (building management), Atrius (spatial analytics), and the recently acquired QSC (audio/video/control platform with Q-SYS software architecture). The company has ~$8.2B market cap, ~30.5M shares outstanding, and trades on NYSE.

The name change signals management's intent to reposition from a lighting manufacturer to a broader industrial technology platform. Market re-rating depends on AIS reaching sufficient scale and demonstrating durable margin expansion.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

FY2025 revenue was $4.35B, up 13.1% YoY — but nearly all growth came from QSC's $429M contribution in eight months. ABL organic growth was 1.1% for FY2025 and 1.0% in Q1 FY2026, confirming the lighting business is a mature, low-single-digit grower. Q1 FY2026 total revenue of $1.14B grew 20.2% YoY, again driven by QSC annualization.

### Margin Structure

Margins have two dimensions:

**Positive — gross margin expansion and AIS profitability improvement.** Consolidated gross margin expanded from 43.3% (FY2023) to 47.8% (FY2025) to 48.4% (Q1 FY2026), driven by AIS mix (59.5% gross margin in Q1) and favorable materials costs. AIS operating margin reached 14.4% in Q1 FY2026, up from 10.0% in FY2025 as QSC acquisition charges ($29.6M inventory step-up, $42.1M intangible amortization) attenuate.

**Negative — ABL gross margin compression.** ABL gross margin fell 110 bps YoY to 44.8% in Q1 FY2026 on flat revenue, attributed to "higher production costs." Given 55% Mexico manufacturing, this is potentially an early signal of tariff/FX/wage pressure. Management offset this through SD&A discipline, maintaining ABL operating margin at 16.6%, but the gross margin deterioration at the core business demands monitoring.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Free cash flow was approximately $533M in FY2025 ($601M operating cash flow minus $68M capex), implying a ~6.5% FCF yield at current market cap — attractive for an industrial with 48% gross margins. Q1 FY2026 produced $141M in operating cash flow.

Net debt stands at ~$421M ($797M total debt minus $376M cash), or roughly 0.7x estimated EBITDA. Total liquidity is $969M. The $300M term loan repayment within 12 months of the $1.2B QSC deal demonstrates rapid deleveraging capacity. The remaining $300M term loan is due June 2027 and will likely be retired well ahead of maturity.

### Earnings Quality

EPS of $12.53 in FY2025 was depressed by $30.9M non-cash pension settlement charges, $29.7M special charges, and $23.8M acquisition costs. Q1 FY2026 EPS of $3.82 (up 14% YoY) represents a cleaner baseline. Annualizing Q1 gives ~$15.28 in EPS, though seasonality makes straight annualization imprecise. Forward consensus is $19.74 for the current fiscal year.

FCF conversion is strong: $533M FCF on $397M net income implies >130% FCF/NI ratio, indicative of high earnings quality.

## Growth Analysis

### ABL: Mature, Replacement-Driven

ABL organic growth of ~1% confirms this is a GDP-linked replacement business. The LED transition is substantially complete. Growth levers include controls/connectivity attach rates, renovation activity, and infrastructure spending (IRA/CHIPS tailwinds). In a stagflationary environment with $116 oil, rising construction costs, and potential rate hikes, ABL revenue faces meaningful headwinds. The 40-45% recession probability described in the macro environment would likely push ABL organic growth negative.

### AIS: The Growth Vector

AIS is the growth thesis. QSC's Q-SYS platform is a leading cloud-first AV/control architecture with a software-defined model that generates recurring revenue. AIS operating margin trajectory (10.0% → 14.4% in one quarter) suggests meaningful earnings leverage as scale increases and acquisition integration costs normalize.

However, AIS competes against well-resourced players (Crestron, Harman, Honeywell, Johnson Controls, Schneider Electric). The $394.6M goodwill and $434M developed technology intangible from QSC represent substantial impairment risk if the AV market softens. Education and hospitality — key QSC end markets — are discretionary spending categories sensitive to economic downturns.

### Estimate Revisions Signal Deterioration

Current quarter EPS estimates fell from $4.29 to $4.06 over 90 days (-5.3%), and the full-year estimate declined from $19.86 to $19.74 (-0.6%). All periods show negative revisions across 30-day and 90-day windows. This is a consistent negative earnings momentum signal. The calibration data shows estimate revisions are among the strongest forward indicators — falling estimates into a deteriorating macro backdrop (stagflation, consumer contraction, oil at $116) increase the probability that forward consensus is still too high.

## Valuation Assessment

At $269, AYI trades at:
- 28.2x trailing EPS ($12.53) — inflated by FY2025 non-recurring charges
- ~17.6x annualized Q1 FY2026 EPS (~$15.28)
- ~13.6x forward FY2026 consensus EPS ($19.74)
- ~12.4x FY2027 consensus EPS ($21.64)
- ~15.4x forward FCF (~$533M)
- ~1.9x trailing sales

**Peer comparison:** Peer median P/E is 42.0x (skewed by ST at 158.5x and MRCY at -141.7x). More meaningful comparables are Powell Industries (20.7x) and EnerSys (18.7x). AYI's forward P/E of ~13.6x on forward consensus looks cheap relative to these, though AYI's flat core growth rate and tariff overhang justify a discount. VICR at 42.0x has higher growth expectations.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean is $383, with a range of $320-$435. At $269, the stock trades at 30% discount to the mean target. However, Baird recently lowered its target to $320 (Neutral), and the sentiment trend is "degrading" with one strongBuy downgraded to hold over the last three months.

The valuation on a forward earnings basis looks reasonable to cheap. The 6.5% FCF yield is attractive. The key variable is whether forward earnings estimates hold or continue declining.

## Competitive Landscape

Acuity is the dominant North American lighting manufacturer by revenue, competing against Signify, Hubbell, Eaton, and Cree. Scale advantages in distribution (independent agent network), brand portfolio breadth (~20+ brands), and installed base create meaningful barriers. However, the lighting market is mature, import competition is persistent, and LED commoditization has compressed pricing power in basic categories.

QSC's Q-SYS platform is differentiated by its software-defined, cloud-first architecture in an AV market that is still transitioning from hardware-centric proprietary systems. If Q-SYS achieves platform dominance comparable to Cisco's in networking, AIS could warrant a materially higher multiple than ABL. Current evidence of margin expansion (59.5% gross margin) supports this thesis directionally.

The competitive moat is moderate. ABL has scale and distribution advantages but faces commoditization pressure. AIS/QSC has technology differentiation but operates in a fragmented market with well-funded competitors.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Mexico Tariff Risk (Severe, Specific, Quantifiable)

55% of finished goods come from Mexico, making this the dominant idiosyncratic risk. A 25% tariff would impact ~$1.2B of COGS, with potential operating income reduction of $150-300M (30-60%) before pass-through. SCOTUS struck IEEPA tariff authority, but Section 232 investigations and congressional legislation could reimpose tariffs within months. The 10-K specifically warns about USMCA uncertainty and Maquiladora status risk. This risk has a defined resolution timeline but binary outcomes.

### 2. Construction Cycle Downturn

ABL revenue correlates with non-residential construction. In a 40-45% recession probability environment with $116 oil, rising rates (35-40% hike probability), and contracting consumer spending, construction activity is likely to decelerate. ABL's 1% organic growth could turn negative.

### 3. QSC Integration and Impairment Risk

$394.6M goodwill and $434M developed technology intangible require sustained revenue growth and margin improvement to avoid impairment. Education and hospitality end markets are cyclically exposed.

### 4. Rising Input Costs

Oil at $116 directly increases petrochemical-derived materials costs (plastics, packaging, freight). ABL's Q1 gross margin compression of 110 bps may be the first manifestation.

### 5. Estimate Revisions Trending Negative

All periods show declining estimates: current quarter down 5.3% over 90 days, full year down 0.6%. In a deteriorating macro environment, consensus may still be optimistic.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 58.8% vs. 34.6% historical volatility — a 24.2pp spread with IV at the 89th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (58.8% near-term → 36.7% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term event or elevated uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio of 1.57 shows more outstanding puts than calls. This options profile is consistent with institutional hedging against a discrete downside risk — most likely tariff policy resolution or a weaker Q2 earnings report. No near-term bullish thesis is supported by options positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($340-435)

- AIS/QSC integration succeeds, margins expand toward 18-20% operating, driving consolidated margin improvement
- Tariff risk does not materialize (USMCA preserved, no Mexico tariffs enacted)
- ABL stabilizes with renovation/infrastructure demand supporting low-single-digit growth
- At 13.6x forward P/E, the stock is pricing in substantial pessimism; any macro improvement triggers re-rating toward consensus $383 mean target
- Rapid debt paydown creates optionality for additional acquisitions or accelerated buybacks
- FCF yield of 6.5% provides downside support

### Bear Case ($190-240)

- Mexico tariffs enacted (Section 232 or congressional legislation) at 15-25% rate, directly impacting $1.2B of COGS
- Construction recession compresses ABL revenue 5-10%, with pricing pressure from import competition
- ABL gross margin compression accelerates from Q1's 110 bps decline
- AIS/QSC growth disappoints as education and hospitality budgets are cut in recession
- Forward estimates revised down further (current trajectory suggests $18-19 FY2026 EPS rather than $19.74)
- Stock re-rates to 12-14x trough earnings of $15-17, implying $190-240

## Conclusion

Acuity Inc. is a quality industrial business with strong cash generation, improving margin mix from the QSC acquisition, and conservative balance sheet management. The stock at $269 offers reasonable valuation on forward earnings (13.6x) with a 6.5% FCF yield. However, the specific risk preventing a BUY rating is the 55% Mexico manufacturing concentration in an active tariff-escalation environment. A 25% tariff on Mexican imports would reduce operating income by an estimated $150-300M, equivalent to a 30-60% earnings impairment that forward consensus does not reflect. This risk is active: Section 232 investigations are underway, and resolution is expected in H2 2026.

Supporting the cautious stance: estimate revisions are uniformly negative, ABL organic growth is flat at 1%, the options market is pricing imminent stress (58.8% IV, steep backwardation, 1.57 P/C OI ratio), and the macro backdrop (stagflation, $116 oil, 40-45% recession probability) is hostile to cyclical industrials. Analyst sentiment is degrading, with one strongBuy downgraded to hold.

**HOLD** with "uncertain" rationale. The tariff risk creates a genuinely asymmetric downside that reasonable valuation alone does not compensate for. The specific catalyst to upgrade to BUY would be: (1) resolution of Mexico tariff risk through USMCA preservation or Congressional action excluding Mexican imports from new tariff frameworks, OR (2) the stock declining to ~$230-240 (11-12x forward earnings), where the tariff scenario is more fully priced, OR (3) management demonstrating ability to pass through 15-25% cost increases without demand destruction over two consecutive quarters.
