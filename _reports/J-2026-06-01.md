**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jacobs Solutions is a structurally different company than it was two years ago. The September 2024 Reverse Morris Trust spin-off of its government-services businesses into Amentum and the March 2026 buy-in of the remaining ~29% of PA Consulting have converted Jacobs into a smaller, asset-light, higher-margin professional-services and infrastructure-delivery firm. The headline data is misleading on first read: the apparent revenue "collapse" from ~$16.4B (FY23) to ~$11.5B (FY24) is the Amentum divestiture (now discontinued operations), and the Q2 FY26 GAAP operating loss of $(81.2)M reflects ~$352M of non-recurring PA deal charges, not operating deterioration. On an underlying basis, segment operating profit grew 13% to $305M, revenue grew 27% YoY, and backlog grew 22% to $26.97B. The dominant driver is the data center / AI infrastructure buildout, which management cited as the principal reason for I&AF's 28% growth and for raised FY26/FY29 targets.

The fundamentals here are improving, but the valuation and balance-sheet picture temper the case. At $123.75 (intraday near $119.86), Jacobs trades at roughly 17x current-year consensus EPS of $7.25 and ~15x next-year $8.27, a discount to the directly comparable engineering peers (EME, FIX, PWR all trade above 27x), but a full multiple for a firm that just nearly doubled its long-term debt to ~$4.08B to acquire PA, posted negative H1 operating cash flow of $(103.4)M, and carries ~$1.51B of floating-rate exposure. The forward multiple already embeds the AI tailwind.

The decisive offsetting signal is insider behavior. CEO Robert Pragada bought 3,601 shares (~$400K) at $111.09 on May 15, 2026, after the post-earnings selloff, and Director Manuel Fernandez bought twice in May at $112-122. Open-market C-suite purchases are the highest-conviction insider signal, and they directly contradict the market's negative reaction to the Q2 print. We rate Jacobs a HOLD with a bullish growth lean. The setup is attractive, but a BUY requires confirmation that H2 FY26 operating cash flow normalizes and the PA-driven leverage is being worked down, neither of which is yet visible in the data.

## Company Overview

Jacobs Solutions is a science-based consulting, advisory, engineering and program-delivery firm with ~47,000 employees. Following its multi-year portfolio reshaping, it reports two segments:

1. **Infrastructure & Advanced Facilities (I&AF)** — ~90% of revenue ($3.34B of $3.69B in Q2 FY26). End-to-end solutions spanning consulting, planning, architecture, design, engineering, program/construction management and facility operations, with growth concentrated in data centers, advanced manufacturing/semiconductors, life sciences, transportation and water/environmental. I&AF operating margin runs ~6.7%.

2. **PA Consulting** — UK-based global innovation and transformation consultancy (~$359M Q2 revenue, ~22% operating margin). Clients include Diageo, Microsoft, Unilever, the UK Ministry of Defence and National Highways.

The two transactions that define the current story: the Amentum spin (creating NYSE: AMTM, which explains the revenue step-down and removed the mark-to-market noise from the retained stake, now fully exited), and the March 20, 2026 PA Consulting buy-in for ~£1.21B (~$1.0B cash + 2.04M shares, with an additional £75M payable in March 2028). PA is now wholly owned, so 100% of its high-margin economics accrue to Jacobs shareholders versus ~71% previously.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and backlog.** Q2 FY26 revenue was $3.69B, +27% YoY; H1 FY26 was $6.99B, +19.6%. I&AF grew 28% in Q2 (data centers, advanced manufacturing, transportation); PA grew 17%. Backlog reached $26.97B at March 2026 versus $22.16B a year prior (+22%), with I&AF backlog at $26.5B. Remaining performance obligations are ~$19.0B, of which ~47% converts within 12 months. Backlog growth is the single most encouraging forward indicator in the file, and it is broad-based across Advanced Manufacturing, Life Sciences and Transportation.

**Margins and earnings — heavily distorted.** The Q2 GAAP operating result was a $(81.2)M loss, but segment operating profit was actually $305M (+13% YoY). The GAAP loss was driven by $352M of restructuring/transaction charges, almost entirely PA deal-related: ~$237.5M from full vesting of PA equity incentive grants and ~$123.9M of employee-benefit-trust distributions classified as compensation. These are non-recurring. H1 FY26 net earnings from continuing operations attributable to Jacobs were +$82.0M ($0.81 diluted) versus a $(6.0)M loss a year ago, when results were depressed by $254.7M of mark-to-market losses on the Amentum stake. Gross margin compressed to 21.5% in Q2 from 25.4%, reflecting higher pass-through costs and softer PA utilization. The effective tax rate is elevated and volatile (34.5% Q2, 37.1% H1) due to non-deductible PA compensation.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — the key concern.** H1 FY26 operating cash flow was $(103.4)M. Management attributes this to a one-time $232.5M PA incentive-grant settlement plus a working-capital build (receivables +$516M). For an asset-light services model, normalized cash generation should be healthy, but the negative print is real and the normalization is unproven. Long-term debt jumped from $2.24B (Sep 2025) to $4.08B (Mar 2026) to fund the PA buy-in: $800M of 4.75% notes due 2031, $500M of 5.375% notes due 2036, $1.2B of term loans, and a new $1.5B revolver. Cash is ~$1.37B with $994.7M of revolver availability. Roughly $1.51B of debt remains floating-rate (only $200M swapped to fixed); a 100bp move shifts annual interest ~$6M. Goodwill of $4.76B is ~40% of $11.9B total assets, with $640M of intangibles, leaving structurally elevated impairment risk given the acquisition-built model.

**Capital return.** The dividend was raised to $0.36/quarter (from $0.32). The company repurchased ~$472M of stock in H1 FY26 at an average ~$139/share, above the current price, with $746.4M remaining on the $1.5B authorization. The buyback execution at $139 against a $120 stock is not a strong allocation signal, though insiders' personal buying at $111-122 is.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q2 FY26) a 5.0% beat ($1.75 vs. $1.67). This is a steady, modestly-conservative-guidance profile that supports reasonable confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a real, broad-based secular driver. Data center and AI infrastructure demand is the cited engine of I&AF's 28% growth, supplemented by advanced manufacturing/semiconductors (CHIPS-linked), life sciences, water/environmental and transportation. Multiple infrastructure and power-delivery names are showing the same demand pattern, which raises the durability of the thesis. Management raised both FY26 and FY29 targets on this momentum plus expected PA synergies.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum, though not at large magnitude. Next-year EPS rose +3.1% over both 30 and 90 days to $8.27; current-year rose +1.7%/+1.3% to $7.25; next-quarter +2.8%. The current quarter was trimmed -3.4% (to $1.83), reflecting margin/utilization caution, but the forward trajectory is upward. The +22% backlog growth and the 47%-within-12-months conversion profile provide visibility that earnings should follow.

The PA buy-in adds a growth and mix-improvement lever: PA grew operating profit 19-23% and runs ~22% margins, three times I&AF's ~7%. Full ownership shifts the company's blended margin upward over time if PA utilization recovers from the currently-soft level flagged in Q2.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$123.75, Jacobs trades at ~17x current-year EPS of $7.25 and ~15x next-year EPS of $8.27. The trailing P/E of ~34.9x is distorted by the PA charges and should be disregarded; forward consensus is the right lens.

Against the construction-and-engineering peer set, Jacobs looks cheap: EME at 27.7x, FIX at 52.8x, PWR at 97.7x (peer median 52.8x). But that comparison overstates the discount — those peers are primarily fixed-asset/installation and electrical/power contractors with higher growth profiles and different margin structures, not consulting-plus-program-management hybrids. The more relevant comparables are AECOM, WSP, Stantec and Arcadis. The PA Consulting component should carry a higher multiple given Big-4-adjacent margins, which supports a modest premium to pure E&C peers.

At ~15-17x forward, the AI/data center tailwind that justifies the growth premium is already in the price. The forward multiple is full and reflects the visible demand rather than a trough-earnings setup trading at a misleadingly high multiple. Analyst targets (low $131, mean $158, median $160, high $181) sit well above the current $120-124 and imply roughly 6-46% upside, but on a stock at a full forward multiple with an open leverage question, those targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the backlog rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric — it offers a 6.0, supported by the peer discount and rising estimates, but capped by the absence of a specific mispricing the market has missed.

## Competitive Landscape

Jacobs operates in a fragmented, competitive global market against AECOM, WSP, Stantec, Arcadis and Fluor (different mix), and against the Big-4 and strategy firms for PA's work. Its differentiation is the integration of PA's high-margin strategy/innovation consulting with Jacobs' large-scale technical program delivery, capturing the full project lifecycle from strategy through implementation. Whether that integration produces genuine cross-sell synergy or merely combines two businesses under one roof is the open question; the thesis is plausible but unproven.

Revenue mix favors stability: ~68% of FY25 revenue is cost-reimbursable (limiting fixed-price inflation risk), with ~32% fixed-price and ~38% international. The competitive trajectory is improving rather than stable: Jacobs is winning data center and advanced-manufacturing work at scale, and TSR of 201 versus the peer group's 146 over the proxy measurement period indicates the reshaped portfolio has outperformed. The post-spin reduction in scale and diversification, which management explicitly flags as making the firm "more vulnerable to changing market and economic conditions," is the offsetting consideration.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Balance-sheet and rate risk (elevated).** With floating-rate exposure at SOFR-based rates near 4.8-4.9%, interest cost is a live variable. The deleveraging pace and resumption of positive FCF in H2 FY26 are the metrics that determine whether the buy-in was well-timed.

2. **Cash conversion (unproven).** H1 operating cash flow was one-time-driven, but normalization is an assumption, not a fact, until H2 prints.

3. **Section 355(e) spin-off tax window (tail risk, dated).** The two-year presumption period on the Amentum separation runs to ~September 2026. A >50% change-of-control or breach of representations within that window could trigger a substantial tax liability. Low probability, but specific and dated.

4. **PA integration and talent retention.** With PA equity now vested and cashed out, retention of key consultants is a swing factor for realizing the margin-mix upside.

5. **Goodwill concentration.** The $4.76B goodwill position creates structurally elevated impairment risk for an acquisition-built model.

6. **Government-funding/policy risk.** ~8% of revenue is US federal; expanded disclosure flags IIJA/CHIPS/IRA timing uncertainty under the new administration plus tariffs.

7. **Consolidated JV arbitration matter** — ongoing, depressed FY25 I&AF results, lower but not resolved in FY26.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-constructive. Near-term ATM IV is 37.3% versus 31.5% one-year realized — modestly rich (5.9pp spread), with IV up 7.1pp since early May, signaling the market is pricing a near-term catalyst. Put/call OI fell from 0.85 to 0.45, a shift away from defensive positioning. The term structure is in contango (37.3% near vs. 39.7% at six months) with the six-month skew at -5.1% (modest put bias further out), and the implied move at the nearest expiry is ±6.3%. There is no acute bearish signal in the options; the structure is complacent-to-normal and does not contradict the constructive insider buying. The richer near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$160-181).** H2 FY26 operating cash flow turns sharply positive as the one-time PA settlement and working-capital build reverse, validating the asset-light model and starting visible deleveraging. The data center / AI buildout sustains 20%+ I&AF growth, backlog continues converting, and PA utilization recovers, lifting blended margins. FY27 prints a clean P&L (no PA charges, no Amentum mark-to-market), and the market re-rates the stock toward the peer cohort and analyst targets. Insider buying at $111-122 proves prescient. EPS reaches the $8.27 next-year consensus and beyond, supporting $160-181 on ~19-22x.

**Bear Case (~$95-105).** Operating cash flow stays weak into H2 as working capital fails to unwind, leverage becomes a drag, and PA integration disappoints with continued utilization softness and consultant attrition. The Section 355(e) window or a fixed-price overrun delivers a negative surprise. The full forward multiple compresses as growth decelerates from the data-center peak. The stock retraces toward ~$95-105, roughly -15% to -21% from current levels.

The base case sits between: a fairly-valued business with a real tailwind, working through a self-inflicted but manageable leverage step-up, where the insider signal and rising estimates argue against the bear case but the cash-flow and balance-sheet questions argue against paying up today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The score sits at 6.1 — below the threshold where a BUY would be warranted. The specific, falsifiable concerns that prevent a BUY: (1) operating cash flow normalization is unproven — a BUY requires seeing H2 cash conversion turn positive; (2) net debt roughly doubled with no deleveraging track record yet; (3) the stock trades at a full forward multiple that already embeds the visible AI tailwind, so there is no identifiable mispricing the market has missed; and (4) the Section 355(e) tax window remains open through ~September 2026. The insider buying and backlog growth are genuinely bullish, which is why this is a HOLD with a clear bullish lean rather than a neutral hold, but the burden of proof for a BUY is not yet met.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- H2 FY26 operating cash flow turns positive (target: trailing-twelve-month FCF returning toward historical asset-light norms, i.e., FCF/net income above 80%), confirming the negative H1 was one-time.
- Net debt declines sequentially in Q3/Q4 FY26, demonstrating deleveraging discipline post-PA.
- Continued backlog growth above +15% YoY with PA utilization recovery lifting blended segment margins above ~8%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating cash flow remains negative or near-zero through Q4 FY26, indicating structural rather than timing-driven weakness.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a Section 355(e) tax event materializes.
- I&AF growth decelerates below 10% YoY, signaling the data-center tailwind is fading faster than expected.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY26, expected early August 2026), with the June FOMC decision relevant for the floating-rate cost question.

## Conclusion

Jacobs is a reshaped, cleaner business with a credible secular tailwind, a fully-owned high-margin consulting arm, and open-market buying from its CEO and a director after the selloff. Those positives are why the stock warrants a constructive lean rather than indifference. The company also nearly doubled its long-term debt, posted negative first-half operating cash flow, and trades at a full forward multiple that already reflects the AI/data-center demand. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not present at $120-124; it would emerge if H2 cash flow normalizes and leverage starts to fall, at which point the stock should re-rate toward analyst targets in the $158-181 range. Until that confirmation arrives, the appropriate posture is to hold with a bullish lean and reassess at the next print.
