## Executive Summary

DNOW closed the acquisition of MRC Global — its closest listed competitor — on November 6, 2025 in an all-stock transaction valued at $1,763M net of cash acquired, issuing 82 million shares and repaying MRC's debt at closing. The strategic logic is straightforward: two Houston-based pipe, valve and fitting (PVF) distributors with overlapping product lines, overlapping branch networks and competing e-commerce platforms combine into a business plausibly generating $4.6–4.9B of revenue (derived, not a filing fact), with purchasing scale, branch rationalization and freight efficiency as the sources of value. Nine months later, the arithmetic is not cooperating. Q1 2026 revenue of $1,183M nearly doubled year over year, but adjusted EBITDA was $39M — a 3.3% margin, against 7.7% in Q1'25 and 7.4% for FY2025. Backing out disclosed quarters from the $202M TTM figure leaves roughly $51M per quarter of adjusted EBITDA for legacy standalone DNOW, corroborated by an externally reported Q2'25 print of $51M at an 8.1% margin. In absolute dollars, the combined company earned less than the acquirer earned alone.

The proximate cause is disclosed and specific. DNOW acquired a company in the middle of a live cloud ERP cutover in MRC's U.S. segment and inherited an unfinished conversion; a newly added FY2025 risk factor states the company has "already experienced operational disruptions attributable to ERP stabilization matters inherited from MRC Global, including challenges affecting our ability to provide financial guidance." Guidance was withdrawn at the February 20, 2026 results call, on which the shares fell roughly 19%. FY2026 capex is guided to ~$55M from $21M, primarily for that ERP implementation. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS has been cut 49.5% over 90 days to $0.32 and next-year 17.1% to $0.85, and Q1 non-GAAP EPS of $0.01 missed by 87.7%.

The unresolved question is whether the margin loss is transitory (ERP disruption, finite, self-correcting) or structural (MRC's mix is genuinely lower-margin, which the 10-Q itself states as the cause of Q1's adjusted gross margin decline to 21.6% from 23.5%). The filings assert both causes and decompose neither. That question is not answerable from one post-merger quarter, and the second quarter's results are imminent — the near-dated options chain has repriced from 41.2% to 86.7% ATM implied volatility in six sessions and prices a ±20.1% move on the August 21 expiry. Buying or shorting a business whose earnings power is genuinely indeterminate, one day before the datapoint that resolves it, at implied volatility 40 points rich to realized, is not a risk-managed decision. HOLD, classified as uncertain rather than as a growth or income thesis.

## Company Overview

DNOW distributes PVF, gas products, pumps and fabricated process and production equipment, plus MRO consumables. It was spun out of National Oilwell Varco in June 2014 and carries a business lineage the company describes as more than 160 years old. Post-merger it operates roughly 300 locations with approximately 5,300 employees at 12/31/25 (about 5,150 per the May 2026 earnings release, a ~3% reduction the company does not attribute to synergies). Segments are geographic: United States (~210 locations), Canada (~35, concentrated in Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba) and International (~55 across the UK, Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and the Middle East).

End markets are disclosed as revenue mix rather than as segments, and the mix moved sharply with the merger. Upstream fell from 70% of Q1'25 revenue to 39% in Q1'26 — but that is denominator arithmetic, since upstream dollars grew 12% year over year ($419M to $468M). Gas Utilities went from $8M (1.3%) to $269M (23%); Downstream & Industrial from $47M to $233M; Midstream from $125M to $213M. Management characterizes the result as "more diversified and less cyclical." The mix data support reduced end-market concentration. They do not establish reduced customer concentration, on which the filings contain no disclosure and no named customer above 10% of revenue, and "less cyclical" is contestable: a newly added risk factor notes gas utility capex depends on regulatory approval and rate cases, and that some utility customers are shifting capital toward electric distribution.

The company's moat claims, in its own framing, are inventory breadth and local availability for products that are "mission critical and represent a fraction of the total cost of a given project"; the Approved Manufacturer's Listing process, in which DNOW staff conduct on-site supplier assessments and become preferred providers of AML products; integrated supply arrangements with employees embedded full-time at customer sites; and purchasing scale ("we are the largest customer for many of our suppliers"). Item 1 also discloses two structural offsets in the company's own words: the industry is "highly fragmented," competing on service, fulfillment, breadth, **price** and total cost; and "most of our suppliers also sell directly to end-users." Disintermediation is the standing condition of the business model, not a hypothetical.

Seasonality matters for reading Q1: U.S. demand is higher in summer and fall, November–December and early-year are weaker, and Canadian activity contracts during spring breakup. Q1 is the seasonally weakest quarter and a working-capital build quarter.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 reported results.** Revenue $2,820M, up 18.8% from $2,373M. GAAP gross margin compressed 543bp to 17.0% while adjusted gross margin was flat at 23.1%. SG&A rose $143M to $559M, which MD&A attributes mainly to merger legal and professional fees. GAAP operating result swung from +$109M to −$93M, and net loss attributable to DNOW was $89M. Adjusted EBITDA was $209M (7.4%), flat on margin. Operating cash flow was $155M against $298M in FY2024, and capex $21M, so free cash flow was $134M — a 4.8% FCF margin.

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap reconciles to merger accounting and execution: a $135M non-cash inventory fair-value step-up in cost of products, $62M of transaction charges, a $27M LIFO increase, $12M of impairment/other (FX translation losses on substantially liquidating certain International subsidiaries), plus stock compensation. The step-up is genuinely non-cash and non-recurring. The LIFO charge is not: DNOW changed U.S. inventory costing from moving-average to LIFO in Q4 2025, retrospective to 2023, and adds the LIFO increase back in both adjusted gross profit and adjusted EBITDA. The LIFO reserve rose from $27M at 12/31/25 to $43M at 3/31/26, so $16M of real Q1 inventory cost inflation sits outside adjusted results. Q1'26 adjusted gross profit of $256M is 33% above GAAP gross profit of $193M.

**Segments.** All merger charges land in the U.S., which swung from +$91M to −$106M of operating profit in FY2025 and posted −$54M in Q1'26. Two quieter facts: Canada is deteriorating independently of the merger — revenue $282M (2023) → $253M (2024) → $214M (2025) → $51M in Q1'26, down 17.7% year over year, with operating profit at $1M versus $4M. International, at $147M of Q1'26 revenue, produced $3M of operating profit, a 2.0% margin.

**The Q1'26 quarter.** Revenue $1,183M, up 97.5% year over year and 23.4% sequentially. Adjusted EBITDA $39M, down 36% sequentially from $61M and down $7M in absolute dollars year over year. Adjusted diluted EPS $0.01 against roughly $0.06 of non-GAAP consensus. Operating cash flow −$95M versus −$16M in Q1'25, with roughly $115M of working capital use attributed principally to cash settlement of severance and legal/professional accruals from the merger (accrued liabilities fell from $300M to $254M) plus receivable and inventory builds. Q1 outflows also included $8M capex, $46M for the Edge Controls acquisition, $50M of buybacks and $5M of tax withholding, funded by $160M of net revolver draws and a $48M cash drawdown.

The Q1 cash figure should not be annualized. Q1 is structurally a build quarter (Q1'25 was also negative), and the bulk of the outflow is settlement of costs already expensed in Q4'25 rather than operating burn. Equally, the offsetting labeled sensitivity is not a forecast: if the combined business ultimately converts at legacy DNOW's ~5% FCF margin on ~$4.7B of revenue, that is ~$235M of FCF, but FY2026 will not resemble that given the $55M capex guide and continuing merger cash costs.

**Balance sheet and covenants.** At 3/31/26: cash $116M, receivables $889M, inventories $1,193M, total assets $3,927M, goodwill $652M, intangibles $563M, long-term debt $571M (from $411M), equity $2,138M with an $880M accumulated deficit. The credit facility is an $850M five-year senior secured ABL revolver maturing November 6, 2030, with a $500M uncommitted accordion, priced at SOFR +1.25–1.75%. The important structural fact is that there is **no ongoing leverage or interest-coverage maintenance covenant** — only a springing fixed charge coverage test of ≥1.00:1.00 that applies when availability falls below stated thresholds. That is materially more forgiving than a term-loan structure and is the main reason solvency risk here is lower than a mechanical distress screen implies. The offsetting weakness is that an ABL borrowing base built on receivables and inventory is pro-cyclical: it contracts exactly when cash is scarcest.

Availability fell from ~$424M to ~$263M in the quarter, and the decline is explained by borrowings, not collateral erosion: $571M drawn + $263M available + $15M of letters of credit = $849M, essentially the full $850M commitment. Total liquidity is $379M. Net debt is $455M, or 2.3x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $202M; on the Q1 annualized run rate of $156M it would be 2.9x, a labeled sensitivity that ignores seasonality. Interest expense is a new and permanent drag: $8M in Q1'26, roughly $32M annualized at the disclosed ~5.0% rate, or about $0.13 per share after tax, against essentially zero pre-merger. A 1% benchmark increase adds ~$1M per quarter.

**Dilution, stated both ways.** DNOW issued 82M shares at an exchange ratio of 0.9489. Share count rose roughly 79% from ~106M pre-merger weighted average basic shares, and legacy DNOW holders' resulting ownership fell from 100% to approximately 56%, with legacy MRC holders receiving approximately 44%. The 79% figure describes new shares relative to existing shares; the 44% figure describes ownership transferred. Q1'26 diluted shares equalled basic (185.76M) because ~5M potentially dilutive shares were antidilutive on the loss. Separately, Q1'26 performance share awards carry payouts from 0 to 823,840 shares contingent on a three-year performance period — not part of current fully diluted capitalization.

**Earnings-quality note.** Operating cash flow of $155M exceeded a net loss of $89M in FY2025, consistent with the loss being driven by non-cash step-up amortization. A standard Altman screen returns 2.63 for DNOW; substituting the classic retained-earnings term (−$836M/$3,924M) gives 1.67. Neither is informative for an ABL-financed distributor whose deficit is a legacy of spin-off accounting and prior-cycle impairments. The relevant solvency read is 2.3x net leverage, no maintenance leverage covenant, a 2030 maturity, $379M of liquidity, and $2.08B of receivables plus inventory as collateral.

**Financial health score: 4.5.** Solvency is not the issue; capital efficiency and cash conversion are. Against the ABL flexibility and collateral coverage sit a GAAP loss, negative Q1 free cash flow, a new $32M annual interest burden on a floating facility, capex rising 2.6x, 54% of book equity in goodwill and intangibles tested only qualitatively, and $50M of buybacks funded by revolver draws in a quarter of negative free cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is real but almost entirely acquired. Legacy DNOW was compounding modestly: reconstructing 2025 quarters from disclosed figures gives Q1 $599M, Q2 $628M, Q3 ~$635M, Q4 $959M, summing to $2,821M against $2,820M reported. Sequential organic progression was positive but low single digit. Canada is shrinking at a 15–18% annual rate. Rig counts fell 6–7% across all regions in FY2025 and U.S. rigs were still down 6.8% year over year in Q1'26 at 548, flat sequentially.

What is improving is price. Q1'26 averages: WTI $71.98 (+20.7% sequentially), natural gas $4.79 (+27.7% sequentially, +15.4% year over year), HRC steel $923.51/ton (+22.6% year over year). For a distributor, rising steel prices support nominal revenue and gross margin dollars on inventory already owned, though under newly adopted LIFO higher replacement cost is expensed immediately — which is precisely why the LIFO charge jumped to $16M. Natural gas strength is arguably the most relevant signal for the enlarged company given its new gas utilities and midstream weighting. The 10-Q attributes part of the oil-price firmness to military conflict involving Iran; the commodity backdrop is more supportive than it was twelve months ago, though tariffs cut both ways: Section 232 steel duties lift prices while the 10-K states tariffs raised customers' infrastructure costs and caused project delays and cancellations among certain downstream clients in H2 2025.

The secular pillars named in the filings are midstream infrastructure supporting data-center power demand, LNG terminals, RNG, CCUS, produced-water handling and municipal water. The CEO cited "early traction from new data center related awards" in Q1'26. No dollar amount, award count or backlog is disclosed, and because revenue recognition uses the practical expedient for contracts predominantly one year or less, remaining performance obligations are not disclosed — the $51M of deferred revenue at 3/31/26 is not a backlog proxy. This is a qualitative pipeline claim, not quantified here.

The growth that matters for equity value is earnings growth, and consensus has moved hard against it: current quarter $0.09 (−44.6% over 90 days), next quarter $0.11 (−44.7%), current year $0.32 (−49.5%), next year $0.85 (−17.1%). One nuance deserves weight: the 30-day revisions are −2.9%, −2.2%, −2.9% and −2.2%. The collapse is contained in the 90-day window that includes the Q1 miss; over the last month estimates have essentially stopped falling. That deceleration is the strongest single piece of evidence that the market has finished repricing the Q1 shock rather than continuing to discover new deterioration. It is not evidence that earnings are inflecting up.

The next-year consensus of $0.85 versus $0.32 this year implies a 166% earnings recovery. That is the crux: it embeds substantial ERP normalization plus synergy capture. Third-party accounts of company statements cite a $23M year-one synergy target and a $70M run-rate synergy / 8% EBITDA margin ambition. Neither figure was verified in an issuer filing, one comes from an investor describing his own long position, and they should not be treated as guidance. Sizing them anyway: $70M on ~$4.7B of revenue is worth about 150bp of margin — meaningful, but on its own insufficient to bridge 3.3% to 8%. And 8% is approximately what legacy DNOW earned standalone (8.1% in Q2'25), so the target requires lifting a blended entity that includes structurally lower-margin MRC volume to the acquirer's pre-merger margin.

**Growth prospects score: 4.5.** Scale and end-market breadth expanded; earnings visibility contracted to the point where management withdrew guidance, and the earnings recovery embedded in consensus is unverified.

On derived pro forma revenue of ~$4.7B (±5–8%, since the ASC 805 supplemental pro forma disclosure was not obtained), the current EV implies 10.8x a 6% adjusted EBITDA margin ($282M) and 8.1x an 8% margin ($376M). Distributors of this quality trade around 9–11x EV/EBITDA through a cycle. So at $14.27 the market is paying a fair multiple for a recovery to roughly 6% — above the 3.3% actually delivered last quarter, below the 7.4% FY2025 rate and below the 8% ambition. That is a balanced price, not a mispricing. Cheapness on trailing revenue and reported book is offset by the fact that 54% of book equity is goodwill and intangibles carried at 2.8x tangible book after only a qualitative impairment assessment, with the purchase price allocation provisional until November 6, 2026.

Analyst coverage is thin — four Buy ratings, no Holds, with targets of $16 low, $17 high, $16.25 mean. One broker cut to $16 from $18 on February 23, 2026 while retaining Buy. A $16.25 mean implies 13.9% upside. Analyst targets are useful floors on inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates; here estimates are falling sharply and the stock is not demonstrably cheap on delivered earnings, so the targets carry no independent weight.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** Not a stretched multiple, not an identified mispricing. The price embeds a specific margin recovery that the most recent quarter contradicts and the filings cannot yet confirm.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger removed DNOW's single most direct listed rival and roughly doubled its scale, creating an entity with an estimated $4.6–4.9B of revenue against ~$2.4–2.5B standalone. That should deliver supplier purchasing leverage, branch and regional distribution center rationalization, freight density and inventory-turn efficiency. Notably, the company's own strategy list emphasizes internal levers — integration, synergies, working capital optimization, technology — rather than pricing, which is the honest read. Characterizations of the combined entity as approaching a monopoly are third-party opinion, not company or filing language: Item 1 describes a highly fragmented industry competing on price and total cost, in which most suppliers also sell directly to end users, and in which pumps and process solutions compete against manufacturers, distributors and fabricators including well-capitalized private-equity-backed players.

The AML process and embedded integrated-supply arrangements are the most defensible elements of the franchise, and both are hard for a regional distributor to replicate. Both also depend on service reliability. That is what makes the ERP situation more than a back-office issue: the moat in this business is availability and fulfillment, and an unstable order-to-cash and inventory system attacks exactly that. Retaining both brands and both e-commerce platforms (DigitalNOW and MRCGO) preserves customer loyalty but defers the systems consolidation that underpins the synergy case.

Adjusted gross margin has stepped down on a consistent path — 23.5% (Q1'25), 22.6% (Q4'25), 21.6% (Q1'26) — and the 10-Q attributes the Q1 decline explicitly to "the impact of MRC Global contributions." That is management stating that acquired mix is lower margin. A new risk factor on customer/product mix and project-based work volatility was added alongside it. Trajectory therefore cuts two ways: structural scale up, near-term service and margin quality down, with genuine risk of share loss to regional competitors and direct-selling suppliers during the stabilization window.

**Competitive position score: 5.5**, a modest step down from the 6.0 assigned previously. Nothing about the strategic scale argument has weakened; the evidence that execution is currently impairing the service proposition has accumulated.

## Risk Assessment

**ERP stabilization — the dominant near-term risk.** DNOW acquired a company mid-cutover on a new cloud ERP for MRC's U.S. segment and must stabilize configuration and data migration while itself running "several ERP systems." Capex rises to ~$55M primarily for this. An admission that a systems problem impairs the ability to provide guidance is a stronger disclosure than generic integration risk — it implies management cannot currently forecast its own order-to-cash and inventory data with confidence. The mitigating read is that ERP stabilizations are common and typically finite within 12–24 months, and that management describes actions as "targeted, decisive" with synergies "ahead of schedule."

**Internal control.** A second new risk factor notes MRC may initially operate outside DNOW's control environment, with explicit reference to a possible material weakness. As of 3/31/26 management concluded disclosure controls were effective with no change in ICFR — a disclosed risk, not a realized one.

**Goodwill and provisional accounting.** $652M goodwill plus $563M intangibles equal 31% of assets and roughly 57% of book equity, assessed qualitatively only for FY2025. The purchase price allocation is provisional until November 6, 2026, and Q1'26 already recorded measurement-period adjustments netting +$8M to goodwill.

**Inherited litigation and audits.** Approximately 446 lawsuits / ~500 claims at 3/31/26 relating to products purportedly distributed by MRC Global (US) Inc., versus ~435 lawsuits / ~713 claims at 12/31/25 — no judgment to date, insurance has substantially covered costs, reserve stated as adequate for 15 years subject to assumptions. Separately, a multi-state unclaimed property audit is newly disclosed and unquantified. A plaintiffs' firm has publicized an investigation regarding disclosure between the November closing and the February ERP admission; no complaint was located and no securities litigation is disclosed in the 10-K or 10-Q, so this remains unconfirmed.

**Gas utilities cyclicality.** The new 23%-of-revenue end market depends on utility capital programs gated by regulatory approval and rate cases, with a disclosed risk that some customers are reallocating capital toward electric distribution — differently cyclical rather than acyclical.

**Interest rate and borrowing base.** Floating ABL with a pro-cyclical collateral base and a disclosed sensitivity of 1% = ~$1M per quarter of incremental expense.

**Capital allocation.** $50M of buybacks at an average $11.92 (90% of shares in March at $11.89) funded by revolver draws during a quarter of negative free cash flow. Constructive reading: the board bought aggressively into a 19% dislocation near reported book value, retiring 1.86% of shares. Cautionary reading: debt-financed repurchase during an integration cash-out period with no guidance outstanding.

**Incentive alignment.** FY2025 GAAP net loss of $89M coincided with CEO total compensation rising 17.1% to $7.12M and compensation actually paid of $7.66M. The company-selected performance measure is EBITDA, which adds back roughly $236M of merger charges. The suite also includes ROCE (which does capture the doubled capital base) and relative TSR (five-year index 184.54 versus peer group 181.72), so alignment is not absent, only weighted toward a metric structurally insensitive to the largest cost event in company history. No motive is inferred; excluding non-recurring M&A costs is standard practice.

**Insider activity.** Every Form 4 in the May 2025 – May 2026 window was a grant or a tax-withholding disposition. No open-market purchases and no open-market sales were found. Despite the stock trading from $16.76 in early February to $11.29 in mid-March — prices established by those very withholding entries — no executive bought personally while the company deployed $50M of corporate cash at $11.92. Blackout during integration and provisional PPA is a plausible explanation, and a 25-transaction window may be incomplete. Net read: neutral to mildly negative, with the corporate buyback the only conviction signal available.

**Unresolved filing items.** The contents of an 8-K filed 2025-05-23 under Item 4.01 (change in certifying accountant) and an 8-K filed 2026-02-27 under Item 5.02 could not be retrieved. Item classifications are known; contents are not, and no inference is drawn from existence or timing.

### Options Market Signal

The near-dated chain has repriced violently: ATM implied volatility moved from 41.2% on July 29 to 86.7% on August 4, and stands at 81.4% for the 16-day tenor against 41.0% one-year realized volatility — a 40-point premium at the 86th percentile of the current chain. Term structure is in backwardation (81.4% at one month, 48.9% at three, 45.5% at six), pricing a discrete near-term event. Given that Q2'25 was reported on August 6, 2025, the Q2'26 print is almost certainly the event, and the August 21 expiry prices a ±20.1% move.

Positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis. Put/call open interest is 0.01 and put/call volume 0.00, with the skew reading of −34.3% characterized as call-heavy; only 28 contracts across three expirations were observed, so the skew reading carries low confidence. That leaves an unusual configuration: 13.1% of float short with 5.6 days to cover, against an options market showing essentially no put demand. A Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin recovery toward 6% could squeeze that short base hard; a second consecutive 3–4% margin quarter has the reverse effect with the shorts already in place. The signal for a fundamental investor is timing, not direction — implied volatility 40 points rich to realized is a poor price at which to take either side, and it argues for observing the print rather than paying for it.

**Risk factors score: 4.0.** The concentration of unresolved binaries — ERP outcome, mix versus transitory decomposition, provisional PPA and impairment risk on 57% of book equity, unquantified audit, inherited asbestos tail, imminent ±20% earnings event — is high relative to what a fairly-priced multiple compensates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.50 (+30%)

ERP disruption is finite and Q1 was the trough. Q2 and Q3 are the seasonally strongest quarters, and the commodity backdrop has firmed materially (WTI +20.7% sequentially, gas +27.7%, HRC steel +22.6% year over year). Synergies land, the $23M year-one target proves conservative against a $70M run-rate ambition, and combined adjusted EBITDA margin recovers to 8% on ~$4.7B of revenue, or ~$376M. At 10.5x EV/EBITDA that is $3.95B of enterprise value; net of ~$430M of net debt, roughly $19.25 per share. Guidance is reinstated, which alone removes a disclosure discount. The board's $50M of buybacks at $11.92 and $73M of remaining authorization keep shrinking the count, and a 13.1% short base amplifies any positive surprise. Legacy DNOW demonstrably earned 8.1% standalone, so the bar is a proven one for at least half the combined revenue.

### Bear Case — $10.00 (−30%)

The margin loss is mix, not systems. The 10-Q already attributes the adjusted gross margin decline to MRC contributions, and the step-down has been monotonic across three quarters (23.5% → 22.6% → 21.6%). ERP instability costs share in a business whose entire value proposition is fulfillment reliability, with fragmented regional competitors and direct-selling suppliers positioned to take it. Combined margin settles at 4.5–5%, or $210–235M on $4.7B; at 8.5x that is $1.79–2.00B of enterprise value, and net of debt roughly $7.30–8.50 per share. Anchoring instead to 0.85x reported book gives ~$9.95. Additional pressure comes from a goodwill impairment on 57% of book equity, an ICFR material weakness disclosure, further Canadian erosion, and a floating ABL whose cost rises with benchmark rates. The stock traded at $10.94 within the last twelve months; that level is not remote.

The bear case is the one with more delivered evidence behind it today — three consecutive quarters of adjusted gross margin decline explicitly attributed by management to acquired mix. The bull case rests on a decomposition the filings do not provide and on synergy figures not verified in any issuer document.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Base case $15.50, +8.6% from $14.27, derived two ways that agree: 18x next-year consensus EPS of $0.85 = $15.30; and a 7% adjusted EBITDA margin on ~$4.7B ($329M) at 10x EV/EBITDA less $455M of net debt = $15.53. Bull $18.50 (+30%), bear $10.00 (−30%). Roughly 1:3.5 reward-to-risk on the base case against the bear case is not an ownership proposition, and the resolution point is days away at implied volatility 40 points rich to realized.

The score moves to 4.6 from 4.8 at the prior review, and the reason is arithmetic rather than new deterioration: the price has risen roughly 11% since early July while consensus drifted down another 2.2–2.9% over 30 days. Same business, higher price, marginally lower estimates — a modestly worse entry. The trajectory across four reviews (4.5 AVOID → 5.6 HOLD → 4.8 HOLD → 4.6 HOLD) reflects a genuine improvement in the solvency read (ABL structure, springing covenant only, 2030 maturity) that has since been partially offset by the margin evidence. Three consecutive HOLDs raise the bar for a fourth, and the honest justification is that the single decisive datapoint has not yet been published.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q2'26 adjusted EBITDA margin ≥6% (roughly ≥$72M on ~$1.2B of revenue) **with** adjusted gross margin stabilized at ≥22%, which would evidence that the Q1 compression was ERP-driven rather than mix-driven.
2. Reinstatement of financial guidance with a quantified synergy run-rate in an issuer document, alongside positive operating cash flow and net debt declining below $400M.
3. A decline toward $11.50–12.00 with the above fundamentals unchanged — that price implies roughly 5.5% steady-state margin at 9.5x, which is defensible against delivered results and would restore asymmetry.
4. Clustered open-market purchases by three or more C-suite insiders.

## Conclusion

DNOW bought its largest listed competitor and, one quarter in, is earning fewer absolute adjusted EBITDA dollars than it earned standalone — $39M in Q1'26 against roughly $51M per quarter for legacy DNOW, on nearly double the revenue. Management withdrew guidance, disclosing that inherited ERP stabilization problems impair its ability to forecast, and consensus current-year EPS has been cut 49.5% in 90 days. Against that, the balance sheet is not the problem: 2.3x net leverage on an $850M ABL with no maintenance leverage covenant, a 2030 maturity, $379M of liquidity and $2.08B of receivables and inventory as collateral. The board bought back $50M of stock at $11.92, the commodity backdrop has firmed, and the 30-day estimate drift of −2.9% suggests the repricing of the Q1 shock is largely complete.

At $14.27 the stock is neither stretched nor cheap: 15.1x TTM adjusted EBITDA, 2.8x tangible book, and an enterprise value that already pays a fair distributor multiple for a recovery to roughly a 6% margin. Whether that recovery arrives depends entirely on a decomposition — transitory ERP disruption versus permanent MRC mix dilution — that the filings assert on both sides and quantify on neither. That question resolves at the Q2 print, which the options market is pricing at ±20% with implied volatility 40 points above realized. Owning the position through that event is defensible for holders with a low cost basis; adding capital into it is paying full price for an unresolved binary. HOLD, with a clear path to BUY at either a 6%-plus margin print with guidance restored or a price near $11.50–12.00 on unchanged fundamentals.
