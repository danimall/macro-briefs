**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

HA Sustainable Infrastructure Capital is a specialty finance company financing the U.S. energy transition across distributed solar, utility-scale renewables, and biofuels. The business is performing well operationally: Q1 2026 delivered record 15.7% adjusted ROE, portfolio yield expanded to 9.2%, and the net interest spread widened to ~3.4%. The company has a credible 2028 target of $3.50-$3.60 adjusted EPS (implying ~30% cumulative growth from 2025's $2.70), supported by a $6.5B pipeline and $2.3B liquidity.

The challenge is valuation. At $41.92, the stock trades at ~95x trailing GAAP EPS (distorted by HLBV timing losses) or approximately 14x forward FY26 adjusted EPS of $3.00. This is expensive relative to financial sector peers (median P/E 11x) and requires the market to accept non-GAAP adjusted earnings as the true economic metric — a reasonable position, but one that introduces analytical uncertainty. The stock has appreciated 63% over the past year, and the current price sits just below its 52-week high of $44.13 with 13.2% short interest suggesting meaningful institutional skepticism.

The company's competitive positioning in energy transition finance is strong and differentiated, but policy risk (IRA dependence), residential solar mezzanine concentration ($1.6B), management turnover, and rate sensitivity create a risk profile that warrants a discount to the premium multiple the stock currently commands.

## Company Overview

HASI deploys capital across three sustainable infrastructure markets: Behind-the-Meter (50% of $7.6B on-balance-sheet portfolio), Grid-Connected (34%), and Fuels, Transport & Nature (16%). Total managed assets reach $16.4B including off-balance-sheet securitization trusts and co-investment vehicles. The company converted from REIT to C-Corp in July 2024, gaining operational flexibility.

The KKR partnership (CCH1) — where both parties committed $1.5B — represents a strategic evolution from pure balance-sheet lender to asset-light fee generator. HASI earns 1% origination fees and 0.5-1.0% ongoing management fees, creating recurring revenue independent of balance-sheet growth.

Investment forms span equity (preferred return/flip structures), mezzanine loans, land leases, receivables, and securitizations across 600+ positions averaging $12M with 16-year weighted average life.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement (Adjusted Basis):**
- Q1 2026 Adjusted EPS: $0.77 (beat $0.70 consensus by 10.6%)
- FY2025 Adjusted EPS: $2.70
- FY2026E Adjusted EPS: $3.00 (consensus)
- FY2027E Adjusted EPS: $3.33 (consensus)
- FY2028 Management Target: $3.50-$3.60

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is enormous and structural. Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(0.57)/share vs. adjusted profit of $0.77/share — a $1.34/share difference driven by HLBV accounting for equity method investments. A $97M timing loss related to an investee's ITC sale agreement (expected to reverse) distorted GAAP results. While the adjusted metric is economically valid, the magnitude of this divergence — and its reliance on management judgment — is a persistent analytical risk.

**Spread Economics:**
- Unlevered Portfolio Yield: 9.2% (up from 8.8% at year-end)
- Average Cost of Debt: 6.1% (up from 5.7%)
- Net Interest Spread: ~3.4% (widening)

The spread expansion is the key fundamental positive. HASI is originating new assets at 9.5-10%+ yields while funding costs rise more slowly. This math supports continued earnings growth even without aggressive balance sheet expansion.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total Debt: $5.4B
- Stockholders' Equity: $2.5B
- Leverage: 1.6:1 (within 1.5-2.0:1 target; board limit 2.5:1)
- Fixed-rate/hedged debt: ~100%
- Liquidity: $2.3B

**Cash Generation:**
- TTM Cash Available for Reinvestment: $939M (17% of market cap)
- Annual dividend: ~$216M ($1.70/share, ~4.1% yield)
- No equity issuance in Q1 2026

**Credit Quality:** 98% of portfolio rated Category 1 (low risk). Ten-year realized loss rate of 0.08% of managed assets. $67M allowance on $3.3B receivables.

## Growth Analysis

HASI's growth trajectory is supported by multiple drivers:

1. **Spread widening**: New originations at higher yields vs. legacy portfolio creates natural margin expansion
2. **Portfolio growth**: $6.5B pipeline (47% grid-connected, 34% BTM, 14% FTN) provides visibility
3. **Fee income scaling**: KKR CCH1 (both at $792M of $1.5B committed) plus securitization management fees
4. **Neogenyx Fuels JV**: $400M investment at $1.8B EV for 30% of biofuels platform

Management's 2028 target of 17% Adjusted ROE and $3.50-$3.60 EPS implies ~11% annualized EPS growth from 2025-2028. Consensus estimates embed 11% growth for FY26 and FY27, broadly consistent.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY26 EPS revised up 2.4% over 90 days, FY27 up 3.0%. These are insufficient to warrant aggressive conviction but confirm a stable-to-improving trajectory.

Structural tailwinds include IRA tax credits, corporate PPA demand, data center electricity needs, and grid modernization requirements. The risk is that these tailwinds are well-understood and largely priced.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.92, HASI trades at:
- ~14.0x FY26E Adjusted EPS ($3.00)
- ~12.6x FY27E Adjusted EPS ($3.33)
- ~11.9x FY28 management target ($3.50-$3.60)
- ~95x trailing GAAP EPS (meaningless given HLBV)
- ~4.1% dividend yield

Relative to financial sector peers (median P/E 11.0x, median yield 3.2%), HASI commands a premium attributable to: (1) higher growth rate, (2) energy transition thematic premium, (3) unique market position. A 14x multiple on a specialty finance company growing at 11% is not unreasonable but leaves limited room for error.

The analyst consensus median target of $50.00 implies 19% upside. The low target of $41.00 is essentially current price, confirming the stock is no longer cheap by any traditional measure.

Key valuation concern: HASI's valuation depends entirely on investors accepting adjusted (non-GAAP) metrics. On a GAAP basis, the company reported a net loss in Q1 2026. If institutional confidence in the adjusted framework erodes — perhaps due to a residential solar credit event affecting underlying receivables — the multiple compression would be severe.

The 63% price appreciation over 12 months has moved the stock from clearly undervalued ($24-25 range) to fully valued. The current price already discounts the 2028 earnings target at a reasonable multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

HASI is the only publicly-traded company exclusively focused on financing the U.S. energy transition at scale. Competitive advantages include:

1. **10+ years of track record** with 0.08% loss rate — demonstrating underwriting discipline
2. **CarbonCount analytics** — proprietary environmental impact measurement
3. **Deep origination relationships** across 600+ investments
4. **Tax credit expertise** — complex ITC/PTC monetization structures others struggle to replicate
5. **KKR partnership validation** — best-in-class PE firm chose HASI as its energy transition finance partner

Competitive threats are real but manageable:
- Brookfield, BlackRock, and other large allocators entering the space with lower cost of capital
- Banks potentially re-entering project finance as rates stabilize
- Political risk to IRA reducing the addressable market

HASI's response — partnering with KKR rather than competing, earning fees while co-investing — demonstrates strategic adaptability.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Residential Solar Mezzanine Concentration**: $1.6B in structurally subordinate loans to residential solar SPEs represents ~21% of on-balance-sheet portfolio. These accrue PIK interest (no cash for years) and are subordinate to tax equity and senior debt. Given Sunnova's recent restructuring, any distress at major residential solar platforms could impair these assets.

2. **HLBV Accounting Creates Headline Volatility**: The $1.34/share GAAP-to-adjusted gap in Q1 is structural. Each quarter's GAAP results depend on tax credit timing, investee distributions, and hypothetical liquidation calculations that are opaque to outside investors.

3. **Interest Rate Sensitivity**: While currently hedged, $600M of senior notes mature June 2026 (imminent) requiring refinancing at current elevated rates. Continued elevated rates create ongoing funding cost pressure.

4. **IRA/Policy Dependency**: The entire investment thesis depends on IRA tax credits continuing. Any political shift weakening ITC/PTC provisions would reduce project economics and pipeline quality.

5. **Management Turnover**: Marc Pangburn (Chief Revenue & Strategy) departing May 2026. Steven Chuslo transitioned to consulting. Founder Jeffrey Eckel sold $5.3M in shares. CEO Lipson is relatively new. The generational transition introduces execution uncertainty.

6. **Short Interest at 13.2%**: With 14.1 days to cover, institutional skepticism is meaningful. This doesn't invalidate the thesis but indicates sophisticated investors identify vulnerabilities.

## Conclusion

HASI is a well-run specialty finance company occupying a genuinely unique niche in energy transition finance. The fundamentals are strong: widening spreads, expanding portfolio, growing fee income, and a credible multi-year earnings trajectory. The ~4.1% dividend provides income while waiting.

At $41.92, the stock has already appreciated 63% in 12 months and trades at 14x forward adjusted EPS — a premium to financial peers justified only by above-average growth and thematic appeal. With 13.2% short interest, management turnover, HLBV accounting complexity, and meaningful IRA policy dependence, the risk/reward is roughly balanced at current levels. The stock is no longer cheap enough to warrant aggressive positioning, and not expensive enough to avoid. Hold with a constructive bias, and revisit on any pullback toward $34-36 or on material catalyst confirmation.
