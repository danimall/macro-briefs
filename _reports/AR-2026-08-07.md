## Executive Summary

Antero Resources has spent 2026 converting balance-sheet capacity into scale. The $2.80B HG Energy acquisition closed February 3, adding 385,000 net acres of core Marcellus in West Virginia; the Utica divestiture closed February 23 for ~$757M of cash, removing 70,000 net acres, 600 Bcfe of proved reserves and 300,000 MMBtu/d of REX firm transport; $315M of bolt-ons followed in July at roughly $2,520 per Mcfe/d of flowing production. Net acreage went from 536,526 at year-end 2025 to approximately 858,000 at June 30, 2026. Q2 2026 production of 377 Bcfe (4,144 MMcfe/d) was up 21% year over year, and gathering, processing and transportation cost per unit fell from $2.25/Mcfe to $1.99/Mcfe — roughly $98M of quarterly benefit at Q2 volumes. Management cut 2026 cash production expense guidance by $0.05 to $2.20–$2.30/Mcfe and signalled exit-2026 production more than 25% above 2025.

The other half of the picture is that the price environment worsened and the reported growth is almost entirely acquisitive. On the pro forma basis in the same 10-Q (HG included in both periods), Q2 2025 revenue was $1,641.1M and diluted EPS $1.00, against Q2 2026 actual revenue of $1,559.8M and EPS of $0.90 — down 5.0% and 10% respectively. Realized price before hedges fell from $3.85 to $3.54/Mcfe; Henry Hub fell 16% to $2.90. On the adjusted basis tracked by consensus, Antero has now missed four consecutive quarters (−45.2%, −16.6%, −0.6%, −10.0%), and FY2026/FY2027 consensus EPS has been cut 4.3% and 8.3% over 90 days to $4.27 and $4.36. Reported GAAP results flatter the picture: 1H 2026 net income of $813.9M includes an estimated ~$227M of unrealized derivative marks and a $60.6M asset-sale gain, so clean 1H net income is closer to $587M.

At $34.90 the stock trades at roughly 8.2x FY2026 and 8.0x FY2027 consensus EPS, with a 9–12% free-cash-flow yield before acquisitions, 1.0–1.13x net debt/EBITDAX (1.13–1.26x including the July bolt-on), and a 29% Antero Midstream stake carried at $259.3M against a disclosed fair value of $3.2B. That combination supports a base case of $40 (+14.6%). Offsetting it: up to $400M of reasonably possible, unaccrued West Virginia royalty exposure; 41% of revenue in NGLs and oil that appear entirely unhedged; $11.09B of fixed contractual commitments with firm transportation peaking at $1,234.4M in 2027; zero balance-sheet cash and commercial-paper reliance; and an insider record of consistent net selling with no open-market purchases. Probability-weighted expected return is modestly positive with wide dispersion, and confidence in the estimate base is low. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Antero is a pure-play Appalachian gas, NGL and oil producer with 632 employees. Every productive well is an Appalachian natural gas well; there are no federal mineral interests. Three reportable segments: exploration and production; a 29% equity-method interest in Antero Midstream Corporation (AM); and marketing of excess firm transportation capacity, which is structurally loss-making by design (−$36.9M operating loss in 1H 2026, −$35.5M in 1H 2025) because it exists to recover demand charges on pipeline capacity Antero cannot fill.

The differentiating asset is the takeaway portfolio: firm transportation on eleven named systems expiring 2027–2058, including TCO westbound ~746,000 MMBtu/d rising to ~800,000 in 2027, TCO eastbound 356,000 MMBtu/d of which 330,000 delivers to Cove Point LNG, ANR Gulf 600,000 MMBtu/d to 2045, Rover 840,000, Mountaineer Xpress 700,000, and Stonewall 900,000 stepping down to a 600,000 minimum in 2027. On liquids, ATEX carries 20,000 Bbl/d of ethane to Mont Belvieu and Mariner East 2 carries 11,500 Bbl/d ethane plus 65,000 Bbl/d propane/butane to Marcus Hook for international LPG loading. Antero is a shipper on all of it, paying demand charges and owing deficiency fees; it owns none of the pipe, none of the 3,100 MMcf/d of contracted cryogenic processing capacity, and none of the gathering, compression or water infrastructure, which Antero Midstream provides under contracts running to 2038.

The stated strategy is a deliberate reversal of the model built over the last decade: "reducing cash costs and expanding margins through incremental dry gas development and lowering commitments on firm transportation over time." Management frames this as a shift from producer-push to demand-pull, betting that regional load — power generation and data centers, including a planned 2-gigawatt combined-cycle plant in Doddridge County, West Virginia — compresses local Appalachian basis enough to make in-basin sales preferable to long-haul transport.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (10-K filed February 11, 2026).** Production 1,256 Bcfe; realized pre-hedge price $3.99/Mcfe; total revenue $5,276M; operating income $883.6M; operating cash flow $1,630.9M; total capex $797M ($658M drilling and completion, $131M leasehold, $8M other); 61 net horizontal wells; total debt net of issuance costs $1,398.0M; buybacks $136M. Leverage is comfortable. An adjusted EBITDAX proxy for 1H 2026 — operating income $1,104.9M plus DD&A $433.5M, impairment $5.4M, equity compensation $25.0M, exploration $1.7M and contract items $13.7M, less ~$226.7M of unrealized derivative marks and the $60.6M asset gain — is $1,296.9M, or ~$2.59B annualized ($2.34B annualizing Q2 alone). Net debt/EBITDAX is 1.02–1.13x, or 1.13–1.26x including the July bolt-on. The single financial covenant is total debt to total capitalization ≤65%; actual is 24.1%. Run-rate cash interest on the June stack is ~$138M annually against $70.0M paid in 1H. Income tax expense of $224.5M in 1H was 98% deferred, supported by ~$960M of federal NOLs and $153M of credits — a real cash advantage today and a real headwind whenever the shelter exhausts, on a date the filings do not disclose.

The item that does not appear in the debt figures is $11.09B of contractual commitments: firm transportation $7,744.7M (remainder-2026 $628.0M, 2027 $1,234.4M peak, 2028 $1,171.1M, thereafter $3,162.0M); processing/gathering/compression/water $1,025.4M; leases $2,001.0M plus $292.9M imputed interest. Cash paid for operating leases in 1H 2026 was $1,016.8M. These are ~4.2x reported net debt and largely fixed regardless of volume or price. A 2027 gas-price shortfall would therefore compress margin faster than headline leverage suggests.

**Financial health score: 6.2.** Low reported leverage, enormous covenant headroom, extended maturities, positive pre-M&A FCF and no material cash tax. Held back by zero cash and commercial-paper reliance, an $11.09B fixed-commitment stack that dwarfs the balance sheet, and the fact that all discretionary cash flow is currently being redirected into acquisitions rather than deleveraging or distributions.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is confirmed and substantial: Q2 production +21% year over year, 26 wells placed to sales including a 24,000-foot lateral, full-year production guidance raised, exit-2026 signalled at more than 25% above 2025, and a 2028 target of cash costs 25% below 2025. There is also a quantified, easily-missed positive: Sixth Street hit its contractual hurdle on May 1, 2026, reverting 85% of overriding royalty distributions to Antero, and Martica Holdings was dissolved June 30 with a $160.6M in-kind liquidating distribution, taking noncontrolling interest from $164.8M to zero. Noncontrolling interest absorbed $20.8M of net income and received $25.0M of cash distributions in 1H 2026; annualizing, roughly $40M of net income and $50–75M of cash that previously leaked out now stays with Antero from July 1 forward. Consensus models built off pre-reversion run rates understate 2H 2026 and 2027 attributable earnings.

Growth per share is not yet visible. Consensus FY2026 EPS has fallen from $4.46 to $4.27 over 90 days and FY2027 from $4.76 to $4.36 — declines of 4.3% and 8.3% — even as production guidance rose. The mechanism is straightforward: volumes up, realizations down. Adjusted EPS has missed consensus in each of the last four quarters, with the most recent miss at 10.0%. The basis conflict matters here: on GAAP, Q2 diluted EPS was $0.90 versus $0.50; on the adjusted basis consensus tracks, $0.76 against $0.84. Different aggregators reported the same $0.90 as a 5.9% beat and a 10.5% beat against different consensus bases, and one described revenue growth as 29.6% against the 10-Q's actual 20.2%. The filed facts are unambiguous; the "beat" is not a fact.

The demand-pull thesis is the growth story that matters beyond 2026, and it is only partially underwritten. Realized Q2 gas of $2.66/Mcf against Henry Hub of $2.90 is a $0.24 discount, against local Appalachian basis being hedged for 2027 at NYMEX minus $0.78 to minus $1.00 — quantifying what the FT portfolio is worth today. The pivot trades that uplift for lower fixed cost, on the bet that regional load compresses local basis. Evidence for the load is real but early: the Doddridge County 2-GW plant, Antero Midstream's Eastside Express (a $200–300M, 1.5–2.0 Bcf/d intrastate line built and funded by AM, underwritten by AR acreage dedication with no minimum volume commitments, construction into 2028–29). No contracted power or data-center gas sales with disclosed terms appear in AR's own filings. Morningstar's thesis that Antero renegotiates legacy long-haul contracts favorably is an analyst expectation; the filings describe scheduled step-downs (Stonewall to 600,000 MMBtu/d in 2027, Tennessee to 200,000 in 2030, REX to 200,000 in 2030) but claim no renegotiations underway, and TCO westbound *increases* in 2027.

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.** Volume and unit-cost trajectory are genuine and management is delivering on the cost line. Per-share earnings growth is not confirmed, all top-line growth is acquisitive, and the annual consensus trend is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 10.00 against EPS of $2.03, which does not reconcile ($34.90/$2.03 = 17.2x). For forward work I use consensus: **8.2x FY2026 ($4.27) and 8.0x FY2027 ($4.36)**. Against peer medians of 13.8x P/E and a 2.7% dividend yield, Antero screens cheap on earnings and pays nothing — the closest comparable, Range Resources, trades at 10.1x with a 1.0% yield; APA at 8.6x with 2.6%; Matador at 13.8x with 2.7%.

Three adjustments matter before treating 8x as cheap. First, trailing and 1H GAAP earnings are inflated by roughly $287M of pre-tax non-operating items (unrealized derivative marks and the Utica gain), so clean 1H EPS is closer to $1.89 than $2.62. Second, the annual consensus that produces the 8x is itself falling and has been missed four quarters running. Third, the company converts none of its cheapness into shareholder return right now: buybacks fell from $85.0M in 1H 2025 to $37.9M in 1H 2026 (1.1M shares at ~$34.23, essentially all in June), $876.6M of a $2.0B authorization remains unused, and there is no dividend. A cheap multiple that is neither returned nor delevered requires a higher demanded yield.

The specific mispricing candidate is the Antero Midstream stake. The 10-Q carries the 29% equity-method interest at **$259.3M against a disclosed fair value of $3.2B** (up from $2.5B at year-end), generating ~$125M of annual dividends and $59.5M of equity earnings in 1H. Enterprise value is $10.73B of equity plus ~$2.95B of pro forma net debt (including the July bolt-on) = **$13.68B**; stripping the AM stake at fair value leaves ~$10.5B of implied E&P enterprise value. The relevant PV-10 comparison ($9,679M at December 31, 2025) is unusable because it excludes HG entirely and still includes divested Utica reserves, and HG's proved volume in Bcfe was not disclosed. A clean reserve bridge waits for the FY2026 10-K.

**Base case $40 (+14.6% from $34.90).** FY2027 adjusted EBITDAX of $2.5B (1H annualized $2.59B, Q2 annualized $2.34B; volume growth offset by flat realizations and the 2027 FT peak), less ~$0.12B of AM equity income, gives $2.38B of E&P EBITDAX. At 5.25x — the middle of the Appalachian gas range — that is $12.5B. Add the AM stake at a 20% discount to disclosed fair value ($2.56B) for tax and liquidity: EV $15.06B. Less $2.95B net debt = $12.11B of equity, or $39.4 per share; round to $40 with the Martica reversion.

**Bull case $51 (+46.1%).** E&P EBITDAX of $2.75B on Henry Hub sustaining $3.75+ and C3+ holding near $45/Bbl, at 5.75x = $15.8B, plus AM at $2.9B, less $2.95B net debt = $15.75B, or $51.2 per share. This is near where consensus sits (mean target $49.10, median $49.00) and where the demand-pull narrative, in-basin basis compression and the 2028 cost target all deliver.

**Bear case $25 (−28.4%).** E&P EBITDAX of $2.0B on Henry Hub near $2.60 and C3+ reverting to $36–38/Bbl, at 4.5x = $9.0B, plus AM at $2.4B, less $3.2B of net debt (higher borrowing plus a partial royalty accrual) = $8.2B, or ~$26.7; a $200M royalty accrual and 2027's $1,234.4M FT peak take it to $25.

Probability-weighting 40% base, 25% bull, 35% bear gives an expected return of roughly **+7%**. That is a positive but unremarkable figure with a wide distribution driven by a commodity price on which no edge exists.

Analyst targets are relevant precisely because even the published low ($37.00) sits above spot, with mean $49.10 and high $60.00, alongside third-party views of $42 (Morningstar fair value), $45 (Barclays, Hold) and $48 (Morgan Stanley, cut). The stock is not at a premium multiple, so these are more credible as reference points than they would be at 50x. But four consecutive misses and a 90-day estimate-cut trend argue the targets are lagging the gas curve rather than leading it, and they are secondary reports of third-party opinions dated June–July 2026, some pre-dating the July 29 print.

**Valuation score: 5.8.** Genuinely inexpensive on FCF yield and on E&P enterprise value net of a marked-to-market midstream stake. Discounted for a falling and repeatedly missed estimate base, no capital return, and the absence of a dated catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Antero's advantage is quantifiable rather than narrative. Realized gas $0.24 below Henry Hub in Q2 2026 while 2027 local Appalachian basis was being hedged $0.78–$1.00 below NYMEX; 19.1 Tcfe of proved reserves against ~1,256 Bcfe of annual production, a ~15-year reserve life before HG; 858,000 net acres post-transaction; 86% of net acreage held by production; largest single purchaser only ~9% of FY2025 product revenue, which is unusually low concentration for an E&P. Realized ethane of $12.54/Bbl against Mont Belvieu's $8.96 is consistent with international LPG netbacks via Mariner East 2 that domestic-hub pricing does not capture.

Two structural qualifications. The marketing segment loses ~$36M per half by design, which is the visible cost of holding more transport than molecules. And the Antero Midstream relationship transfers economic rent: substantially all of AM's revenue comes from AR; AR has dedicated substantially all West Virginia, Ohio and Pennsylvania acreage to AM; fees escalate with CPI; and for newly requested infrastructure AM may elect a cost-of-service fee designed to earn **13% per annum on invested capital over seven years**. AR owns 29% of AM, so ~71% of that return accrues to other AM holders. Eastside Express is the clean illustration: AM invests $200–300M and books the return, AR provides the acreage dedication that underwrites it and pays tariffs on 100% of throughput. The arrangement is disclosed and not abusive — AR avoids the capex, the asset risk and any minimum-volume deficiency exposure, which is precisely why its own capital intensity looks light against peers. But "AR capex" understates the full-cycle capital required to produce an Antero molecule.

Trajectory is mixed. Scale and cost position are improving measurably. Differentiation is being deliberately traded away: the pivot to in-basin dry gas swaps a proven basis uplift for an unproven bet on regional demand compressing local basis, and the FT step-downs that reduce fixed cost also reduce the priority access to Gulf and Cove Point pricing that Morningstar cites as the core asset. **Competitive position score: 6.0.**

## Risk Assessment

**West Virginia royalty litigation — up to $400M unaccrued.** The West Virginia Supreme Court answered certified questions in *Romeo v. Antero* on June 11, 2025, broadening the scope of products on which royalties are owed and limiting post-production cost deductions under leases lacking contrary language. Antero has accrued only an immaterial amount for *Romeo*, but discloses that for other leases not at issue in that case, reasonably possible losses "could be up to $400 million" — 3.7% of market capitalization and 15% of net debt. This is specific to Antero's West Virginia lease base and is not shared uniformly by peers. It is an upper bound on reasonably possible loss, not an accrual or an expected value, and neither timing nor probability is disclosed.

**Unhedged liquids.** Q2 2026 NGL sales were $587.7M plus $59.6M of oil, which is 41% of the top line. Every derivative position disclosed at June 30 is a natural gas instrument; no NGL or crude hedges appear. Gas hedging, meanwhile, rose from 4% of 1H 2025 production to ~54% of expected 2026 volume, with 915,329 MMBtu/d of 2027 Henry Hub swaps at $3.88. Management has protected the commodity that services the acquisition debt and left the higher-beta stream open. That has been a tailwind — Mont Belvieu C3+ went from $38.07 to $45.26/Bbl and WTI from $63.74 to $93.00 year over year — but the crude strength has been a one-sided exposure. A reversion of realized C3+ from $44.33 to $38/Bbl at Q2 volumes costs roughly $70M of quarterly revenue, ~$280M annualized pre-tax, or about $0.72 per share after tax — roughly 17% of FY2026 consensus EPS. The 10-Q's own sensitivity: revenue falls $55M per combined $0.10/MMBtu gas and $1.00/Bbl liquids decline on 1H volumes. A plausible alternative reading is that Appalachian NGL hedging liquidity is thin and international LPG netbacks are worth more unhedged than a Mont Belvieu hedge would capture; no filing explains the choice.

**Fixed commitments concentrated in 2027.** The $11.09B total peaks in firm transportation at $1,234.4M in 2027. Combined with $1,016.8M of cash operating lease payments in 1H 2026, the cost base is largely insensitive to volume and price. **PUD obligations:** 4,671 Bcfe (24% of proved reserves) require $2.3B of development capital over five years, $0.49/Mcfe, and FY2025 already carried a 302 Bcfe downward revision for locations not developed within five years of booking.

**Liquidity mechanics.** Zero balance-sheet cash, book overdrafts in payables, and $182M of commercial paper maturing in July, backstopped by a revolver now extended to 2031. Serviceable, but there is no cushion.

**Verification gaps that constrain confidence.** HG's contribution of $221.5M of net income from February 3 to June 30 includes $128.5M of derivative fair value gains and bears no allocated interest on the ~$2.25B of acquisition debt (~$120M/yr); it must not be annualized as a return on the $2.8B purchase price. HG's proved reserve volume in Bcfe was not disclosed, so no clean PV-10 or reserve-multiple bridge exists. Full quantified FY2026 guidance beyond the cost figure comes via secondary reporting of the July 30 call, not a filed document.

**Governance.** The record from late February through July 2026 shows consistent net insider distribution and no open-market purchases: CEO Michael Kennedy sold 185,826 shares at $39.31–$39.61 on May 4 (~$7.31M), leaving 1,085,192 shares, then terminated his 10b5-1 plan on May 5 after ~93% execution; Yvette Schultz sold 54,490 shares; Sheri Pearce sold over 70% of her post-vest position; Director Hardesty sold 24,000 shares at $36.00 and $44.00. Most sales followed March vesting events and Kennedy's occurred inside a plan adopted November 6, 2025, which is the standard liquidity-driven pattern and no bearish signal on its own. But the absence of any offsetting purchase at $34.71, when the same insiders sold into $36–$44, removes what would otherwise be the cleanest confirmation of the value thesis. PSU calibration is asymmetric: 2022/2023 absolute-TSR awards vested at 82% and 75% of target while 2022/2023/2025 Net Debt/EBITDAX awards vested at 194%, 194% and 200%. New Leverage Ratio PSUs for 2026–2028 are assessed as probable even after $2.25B of acquisition debt, which is defensible since a ratio can improve on acquired EBITDA but worth watching. Reporting identifies Kennedy as CEO of both AR and Antero Midstream; AR's own filings establish only the AR role, and the dual-role claim is unverified against AM's filings. If confirmed, it places one individual on both sides of a related-party relationship in which AM derives substantially all revenue from AR and earns a contractual 13% return on capital AR commits it to build.

**Risk factors score: 5.0.** The royalty contingency and unhedged liquids are specific, quantified and plausibly under-priced. Offsetting positives are real: 9% maximum customer concentration, 24.1% debt-to-capitalization against a 65% covenant, 54% of 2026 gas hedged, no MVC exposure on Eastside Express, and the EPA/DOJ Clean Air Act matter resolved by consent decree for ~$3.8M.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($51, +46%).** HG delivers the cost structure management guided to, exit-2026 production lands as signalled, and the Martica reversion adds annual net income that consensus has not modelled. Regional demand-pull materialises — the Doddridge County 2-GW plant, Eastside Express capacity of 1.5–2.0 Bcf/d, LNG pull through Cove Point — compressing Appalachian basis and validating the in-basin pivot. Henry Hub sustains $3.75+ into 2027, where the swapped volume is already locked at $3.88. The market gives explicit credit for the AM stake at its disclosed $3.2B fair value against a $259.3M carrying value. Free cash flow of $1.2B+ resumes flowing to the remaining $876.6M authorization once the acquisition sprint ends. At 5.75x E&P EBITDAX plus the marked midstream stake, that is $51 — near where consensus already sits.

**Bear case ($25, −28%).** Crude falls toward $70, dragging realized C3+ from $44.33 back to $36–38/Bbl and removing ~$280M of annualized pre-tax revenue from a stream that is entirely unhedged. Henry Hub holds near $2.60 while the 2027 firm transportation commitment peaks at $1,234.4M and Antero remains contractually committed to lease and midstream payments exceeding $2B annually. The West Virginia royalty exposure begins to accrue toward the disclosed $400M ceiling. Adjusted EPS misses for a fifth and sixth consecutive quarter, FY2027 consensus continues its slide from $4.76 through $4.36 toward $3.50, and net debt/EBITDAX drifts to 1.7x+ as the July bolt-on and further deals are debt-funded. At 4.5x depressed E&P EBITDAX the equity is $25–27, near the 52-week low of $29.10 and below it.

## Conclusion

Antero has executed the operational half of its plan. Production is up 21%, unit costs are down 12% per Mcfe, the balance sheet carries 1.0–1.13x leverage against a 65% debt-to-capitalization covenant it uses 24.1% of, maturities are pushed to 2029–2036 with the revolver extended to 2031, and the 29% Antero Midstream stake sits on the books at 8% of its disclosed $3.2B fair value. Pre-acquisition free cash flow of $1.0–1.3B against a $10.73B market capitalization is a 9–12% yield.

The financial half has not followed. Pro forma revenue and EPS both declined year over year in Q2; adjusted EPS has missed four quarters running; FY2027 consensus has been cut 8.3% in 90 days even as production guidance rose; all free cash flow is being redirected into acquisitions rather than distributions; and 41% of revenue sits in liquids that appear entirely unhedged. Add up to $400M of unaccrued West Virginia royalty exposure and $11.09B of fixed commitments peaking in 2027, and the 24% discount to the March high looks like informed selling rather than mispricing.

At $34.90 the base case is $40 and the bear case is $25. That distribution — roughly +7% expected with a 28% left tail and no dividend to bridge the wait — is a fair-value proposition. HOLD, and revisit below $31 or on the first two quarters of estimate stabilization.
