## Executive Summary

Omega Healthcare Investors is the largest pure-play skilled nursing/senior housing REIT by facility count, with 1,027 facilities generating approximately $1.19 billion in revenue and $899 million in Nareit FFO for 2025. The business model—triple-net leasing at ~10.4% average yields funded with 4.2% average cost of debt—generates a 600bps spread that mechanically produces FFO growth as capital is deployed. Q1 2026 results confirmed this trajectory with AFFO of $0.82/share (+9.5% YoY) and guidance raised to $3.19-$3.25/share.

The central tension is between this well-functioning capital recycling machine and the fragility of its operator base. 19% of revenues come from operators on cash basis recognition, the $263.6M Maplewood revolver remains on non-accrual, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's $920 billion in Medicaid cuts over the next decade represents the most significant regulatory headwind to operator margins in years. The stock has appreciated 30% over the trailing year to within 1% of its 52-week high, leaving limited upside to analyst consensus ($50 median) and inadequate margin of safety given the rising-rate environment where 30-year Treasury yields above 5% pressure REIT valuations broadly.

For existing holders, the 5.5% dividend yield with comfortable AFFO coverage (~82% payout) provides adequate compensation for holding through the current uncertainty. For new capital, the risk/reward is unfavorable at current levels.

## Company Overview

OHI is a Maryland-based REIT structured as an UPREIT that provides financing and capital to long-term healthcare facilities through triple-net leases (84% of revenue) and real estate loans (15% of revenue). The portfolio spans 1,027 operating healthcare facilities across 42 U.S. states, Washington D.C., the U.K., and Jersey, operated by 89 third-party operators.

The mix includes 561 skilled nursing facilities (SNFs), 339 assisted living facilities (ALFs), 19 independent living facilities (ILFs), 16 specialty facilities, and 1 continuing care retirement community. Geographic concentration includes the U.K. at 17.7% and Texas at 8.5% of investments. Total gross investment stands at approximately $11.9 billion ($10.5 billion net).

A strategic shift occurred in Q4 2025 with the adoption of RIDEA structures—for the first time, OHI directly owns and operates 4 senior housing facilities through third-party managers, diversifying the income stream while introducing operational exposure. The company also committed to its first Canadian investment in Q4 2025, marking continued international expansion beyond the U.K.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability Trajectory:**

Revenue grew from $878 million (2022) to $1.19 billion (2025), a 35.5% increase driven primarily by acquisitions ($690M deployed in 2025, $740M in 2024), contractual rent escalators, and improved cash collections from previously distressed operators. Q1 2026 revenue of $322.9M (+16.6% YoY) confirms continued momentum.

Nareit FFO reached $899M in 2025 (+22.5% YoY), translating to approximately $3.04/share. Q1 2026 AFFO of $0.82/share implies an annualized run rate of $3.28, consistent with the raised guidance midpoint of $3.22. Net income margin of 51.2% reflects the capital-light nature of triple-net lease structures where OHI bears minimal operating costs.

**Balance Sheet Quality:**

Total debt of $4.3 billion against $5.4 billion in equity produces a debt/total capitalization ratio of 44.1%—moderate for a healthcare REIT. The debt profile is well-managed: 94% fixed-rate (after swaps), weighted average interest rate of 4.2% (down from 4.6% in 2024), and no maturities until April 2027 ($700M of 4.5% notes). The $1.8B revolver availability plus $2.0B ATM program provides substantial liquidity for continued acquisitions.

Credit ratings of BBB-/Baa3 represent the lowest investment-grade tier, providing cost of capital advantage over non-rated competitors but leaving limited cushion before a speculative-grade downgrade.

**Dividend Coverage:**

The $2.68 annualized dividend ($0.67/quarter) is covered at approximately 82% of AFFO and 86% of FAD. Operating cash flow of $879M (2025) versus estimated dividends paid of ~$766M confirms comfortable headroom. The 5.5% forward yield is competitive within the healthcare REIT peer group.

## Growth Analysis

OHI's growth engine operates through three channels:

1. **External acquisitions:** $690M deployed in 2025 at yields averaging low-to-mid 9% to low 10%. With a 600bps spread over cost of debt, each dollar deployed accretively increases FFO. The pipeline remains active with the $480M CommuniCare disposition being recycled into healthier operators.

2. **Contractual rent escalators:** Annual increases embedded in lease structures provide organic growth of approximately 2-3% on existing portfolio revenue.

3. **Operator recovery:** As cash-basis operators stabilize, OHI recognizes deferred straight-line rent adjustments, creating earnings tailwinds. LaVie's emergence from Chapter 11 in May 2025 demonstrated this pathway.

Full-year 2026 guidance of $3.19-$3.25 AFFO/share implies 5-7% growth from 2025's $3.04 FFO/share. Estimate revisions show modest upward trend over 90 days (+3.5-3.6%) but have reversed downward over the most recent 30 days (-1.8%), suggesting some loss of near-term momentum following the Q1 report.

The RIDEA structure adoption opens a new growth avenue but remains de minimis (4 facilities). International expansion into Canada provides additional runway but introduces currency risk.

Growth headwinds include: the OBBBA Medicaid cuts that pressure operator margins and therefore rent-paying capacity; the 30Y Treasury environment above 5% that compresses cap rates and makes acquisitions more expensive on a relative basis; and labor/inflation pressures on operators that may manifest as future rent restructurings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $48.99, OHI trades at:
- **23.6x trailing EPS** ($1.94/share on GAAP basis—though GAAP EPS is less meaningful for REITs)
- **16.1x 2025 FFO/share** ($3.04)
- **15.2x 2026E AFFO/share** (midpoint $3.22)
- **5.5% forward dividend yield**
- **Price/Book:** Approximately 2.7x tangible book

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 60.5x makes OHI appear cheap, but this comparison is misleading—DOC (60.5x) and AHR (88.3x) reflect elevated GAAP P/E ratios driven by large depreciation charges relative to their earnings profiles, while HR is negative. The more relevant comparison is CareTrust REIT (CTRE) at 26.2x and Sabra (SBRA) at 33.3x. OHI's 23.6x represents a discount to both, partially justified by its higher operator risk profile but also reflecting the market's concern about tenant quality.

At 99% of its 52-week high ($49.33), the stock offers only 2.1% upside to the consensus median target ($50.00) and 20.4% to the high target ($59.00). The low target of $43.00 represents 12.2% downside. This asymmetry is unfavorable for new capital—limited upside to consensus with meaningful downside if operator risks materialize or if the rising-rate environment further pressures REIT valuations.

The 5.5% yield provides income support but offers a thin spread over the risk-free rate with 30Y Treasuries above 5%. In prior cycles, healthcare REITs required 200-300bps spreads over 10Y to attract capital; at current levels, that spread is compressed.

## Competitive Landscape

OHI's competitive position benefits from scale advantages that are difficult to replicate:

- **Largest pure-play SNF/ALF REIT** with 1,027 facilities providing superior diversification across 89 operators
- **Investment-grade credit** enables borrowing at 4.2% vs. non-rated competitors paying 200-400bps more
- **Relationship-driven deal flow** through decades of operator relationships
- **Triple-net lease structure** with 86% of leases expiring after 2030, providing long-term revenue visibility

The 600bps positive spread between investment yields (~10.3%) and borrowing costs (~4.2%) is structurally protected as long as OHI maintains investment-grade ratings and operators continue paying rent. This spread is wider than what diversified healthcare REITs like Welltower and Ventas achieve, reflecting OHI's higher-acuity, higher-risk SNF focus.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: OHI is actively recycling capital from stressed operators (CommuniCare, Genesis) into healthier tenants, and the RIDEA adoption expands the addressable investment universe. The Saber JV structure (49% ownership via OP Units) demonstrates creative capital deployment that preserves equity capacity.

The primary competitive risk is structural: SNF-focused REITs face existential regulatory risk if federal or state legislation restricts REIT ownership of healthcare facilities. Massachusetts already enacted such restrictions in January 2025, and similar proposals exist at the federal level.

## Risk Assessment

**Operator Distress (HIGH):** 19% of revenue on cash basis is a structural feature of OHI's portfolio. The Maplewood non-accrual ($263.6M revolver, paid only $58.9M of $69.3M owed in 2025) and Genesis Chapter 11 ($51.2M rent + $17.1M interest) represent concentrated workout situations. If operator health deteriorates further under OBBBA Medicaid pressure, rent restructurings, lease terminations, or additional bankruptcies would directly impair revenue.

**Interest Rate Environment (HIGH):** With 30Y Treasuries above 5% [unverified] and two FOMC members endorsing rate hikes [unverified], the REIT sector faces structural valuation headwinds. OHI's April 2027 debt maturity ($700M at 4.5%) will need refinancing at likely higher rates. The Frankfurt CRE deal collapse [unverified] demonstrates that 5%+ financing costs create deal-killing constraints even for well-capitalized buyers.

**Medicaid/Medicare Reimbursement (MEDIUM-HIGH):** The OBBBA's ~$920B in Medicaid cuts represents a decade-long headwind to operator margins. Combined with Medicare sequestration increasing to 4% and Medicare Advantage utilization management, the reimbursement environment for SNFs is deteriorating. OHI doesn't directly receive government payments, but operator inability to cover rent is the transmission mechanism.

**Legislative Risk (MEDIUM):** Anti-REIT healthcare legislation has moved from theoretical to enacted (Massachusetts). Federal proposals remain active. This is a low-probability, high-severity risk.

**Credit Rating (LOW-MEDIUM):** At BBB-/Baa3, any deterioration in leverage metrics or operator defaults could trigger a downgrade to speculative grade, increasing borrowing costs by 100-200bps and restricting access to investment-grade indices.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($54-59):**
OHI continues deploying capital at 600bps spreads, AFFO grows to $3.40-$3.50 in 2027 as CommuniCare proceeds are redeployed and Maplewood stabilizes. Demographic tailwinds (aging population) support occupancy recovery. Interest rates moderate, compressing cap rates and supporting REIT valuations. Market re-rates OHI to 16-17x AFFO.

**Bear Case ($38-42):**
OBBBA Medicaid cuts begin impacting operator margins in H2 2026, driving additional operators to cash basis or restructuring. Maplewood workout fails, requiring a write-down. Rising rates push 30Y above 5.5%, compressing REIT multiples and reducing acquisition attractiveness. Credit rating agencies put OHI on negative watch. AFFO declines to $2.90-$3.00 as troubled operators renegotiate rent. Stock trades to 13-14x AFFO.

## Conclusion

OHI is a well-managed healthcare REIT executing competently on its capital recycling strategy, generating attractive spreads, and maintaining a comfortable dividend coverage ratio. The 5.5% yield provides adequate compensation for income-oriented holders. However, the stock's 30% appreciation over the past year has priced in much of the operational improvement while leaving insufficient margin of safety against material risks: 19% cash-basis revenue, Maplewood's $263.6M non-accrual workout, OBBBA Medicaid cuts, and a rate environment that structurally pressures REIT valuations. At $48.99, the stock is within 2% of consensus median target. New capital should await a pullback to the low $43-44 range where the yield approaches 6%+ and the risk/reward improves. Current holders should maintain for income with monitoring of operator health metrics at the next quarterly report.
