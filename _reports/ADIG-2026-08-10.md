## Executive Summary

ADI Global Distribution began regular-way trading on August 4, 2026, distributed one-for-two to Resideo Technologies shareholders. It is a $4.8bn-revenue specialty distributor of professionally installed low-voltage products — security, fire/life safety, residential and professional AV, and data communications — operating roughly 200 branches, more than 500,000 SKUs from over 1,000 suppliers, and more than 100,000 installer, dealer and integrator customers. The economics are those of value-added distribution: ~21% gross margin, ~4.4% segment operating margin, working-capital intensity, and competitive differentiation built on inventory availability, branch proximity, technical support and trade credit rather than switching costs.

Three facts define the current setup. First, ADI is the borrower on approximately $1.0bn of new debt — $400M of 7.125% senior unsecured notes due 2034 plus a $600M secured Term B — the proceeds of which were distributed to Resideo, which then repaid $900M of its own Term Loan B with roughly $200M more expected by the end of its third quarter. The equity now bears $63-68M of annual cash interest (our estimate, using the disclosed 7.125% coupon and Resideo's 5.67% Term B weighted average as a proxy for an undisclosed margin) that the segment previously did not carry. Second, growth quality is weaker than the headline: of FY2025's +$587M revenue increase, $446M was Snap One wrap-around from the June 2024 acquisition and $18M was FX, leaving roughly 3.1% organic growth on a $4,197M base, about half of it price/mix rather than volume. Q1 2026's reported +7.6% is flattered by a fiscal quarter ending April 4, 2026 versus March 29, 2025 — approximately 6.8% more elapsed calendar days — and segment operating income was flat at exactly $34M on $85M more revenue. Third, the market has already paid up: the stock closed at $22.00 on its first session and $27.03 four sessions later, a 22.9% move, and now sits 8.1% above the only published price target we can locate ($25.00, one analyst, HOLD).

We rate ADIG **REDUCE**. This is not a judgment that the business is impaired. At roughly 9-11x our estimated standalone EBITDA and an implied 6-8% pre-working-capital free cash flow yield, the valuation is defensible for a scaled distributor. But there is no margin of safety at $27.03 against a base case of approximately $24, the probability-weighted expected return is negative, and several recommendation-critical inputs — the Form 10 pro forma capitalization, opening cash, Term B covenants, and the terms of a Series A Cumulative Convertible Participating Preferred class ranking ahead of the common — are unavailable to us. Distribution recipients should trim into the post-spin strength; new capital should wait for the first standalone 10-Q.

## Company Overview

ADI is a middleman with an inventory position. Over 1,000 manufacturers sell product to ADI; ADI stocks it across roughly 200 branch locations and an e-commerce channel; licensed professional installers and integrators buy from ADI and install for end customers. ADI does not own the end-customer relationship and, for third-party branded product, does not own the intellectual property. Resideo's FY2025 10-K lists the competitive factors for this segment explicitly: product availability, speed and accuracy of delivery, service, price, technical support, and "furnishing of customer credit." ADI is partly a trade-credit provider to a fragmented contractor base.

The Snap One acquisition (Resideo, June 2024, $1,337M cash, partly funded by a $500M Series A preferred issued to CD&R) changed the mix. Snap One brought exclusive brands and software into the ADI segment, contributing +50bp to Resideo's consolidated gross margin in FY2025 and giving a distributor an unusual R&D line — $22M of incremental FY2025 R&D and $12M in Q1 2026 alone. ADI carries $1,063M of goodwill as of April 4, 2026 and the bulk of the amortizable customer-relationship and technology intangibles, which is why GAAP and adjusted earnings will diverge sharply (see Valuation).

Management characterizes ADI as "an industry leader in Security, Residential AV and Fire/Life Safety with growing positions in Data Communications and Professional AV." The distinction is deliberate and useful: leadership is claimed in three mature categories, and the growth categories are ones where ADI is a challenger against broadline electrical and datacom distributors. We have found no independent share study to verify either claim; note also that the language softened from "the market-leading distributor" (June 16 release) to "an industry leader" (August 4 release).

## Financial Analysis

All figures below derive from Resideo's segment disclosures, because no ADIG financial statement exists. Resideo's own definition (Q1 2026 10-Q, Note 3) excludes allocated corporate cost, interest and taxes from segment income, which matters materially.

| ADI segment | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | $4,197M* | $4,784M | $1,121M | $1,206M |
| Gross profit | — | — | $242M* | $256M* |
| Gross margin | — | — | 21.6%* | 21.2%* |
| Segment income from operations | $195M* | $212M | $34M | $34M |
| Segment operating margin | 4.65%* | 4.43%* | 3.03%* | 2.82%* |
| Intangible amortization | — | — | $23M | $24M |
| R&D | — | — | $8M | $12M |
| Capex | — | — | $11M | $13M |

*Derived: FY2024 revenue = $4,784M − $587M disclosed increase; FY2024 segment income = $212M − $17M disclosed increase; gross profit = disclosed revenue less disclosed COGS.

**Revenue growth is not converting into operating profit.** FY2025 revenue rose 14.0% while segment operating margin fell from ~4.65% to ~4.43%, despite Snap One's accretive mix. In Q1 2026 revenue rose 7.6% and segment income was unchanged at $34M. Management's own bridge explains the mechanism: favorable price/mix (+$9M), volume (+$7M) and lower restructuring (+$4M) were fully absorbed by SG&A (+$13M), freight and duties (+$4M) and R&D (+$4M). At a 4.4% operating margin, a 50bp erosion removes more than 10% of operating profit, so the direction of this variable dominates everything else in the model. Two quarters do not establish a trend, and Q1 is seasonally weakest (the 10-K notes a minor revenue increase in Q2/Q3), plus FY2025 absorbed $10M of one-time ERP implementation costs. But the burden of proof now sits with management to demonstrate positive operating leverage in a standalone P&L.

**Capital structure.** Funded debt at separation is approximately $1.0bn: $400M of 7.125% senior unsecured notes due July 15, 2034 (Rule 144A/Reg S, unregistered) plus a $600M senior secured Term B, with a $500M revolver expected undrawn. The notes are structurally junior to the secured facilities. A 7.125% unsecured coupon on a $4.8bn-revenue distributor in mid-2026 is consistent with a single-B/high-BB credit profile — an inference from pricing, not a rating we have verified. Term B rate, maturity, amortization and covenants are undisclosed to us; Resideo's disclosed sensitivity (~$22M per 100bp on ~$2.3bn floating) implies roughly $6M of annual interest sensitivity per 100bp on ADI's $600M term loan, absent hedges we cannot confirm.

**EBITDA and leverage.** Segment EBITDA is approximately $340-350M: $212M of segment operating income plus roughly $98M of ADI intangible amortization (consolidated FY2025 amortization of $122M less the P&S segment's ~$24M, cross-checked against ADI's disclosed $23-24M per quarter) plus $30-40M of property depreciation. The adjustment we cannot make from public filings is standalone corporate cost. Resideo's Corporate segment absorbed $160M in FY2025 — executive, legal, tax, treasury, HR, IR, IT — none charged against ADI's segment income, and ADI was 64% of consolidated revenue. If incremental standalone cost is $40-70M, standalone EBITDA is closer to **$275-310M**, putting net leverage on $1.0bn of debt at **3.2-3.6x** rather than the ~2.9x implied by segment EBITDA. This single unverified number is the swing factor between "reasonably levered" and "fully levered."

**Cash generation.** Working backward: standalone EBITDA of $275-310M, less $65M interest, less ~$50M capex (annualizing the $11-13M quarterly run rate), less cash taxes of $25-30M, gives roughly $130-170M of free cash flow before working capital — a 6.3-8.3% yield on $2,052M of equity. That yield is the strongest element of the bull case and the reason we are not at AVOID. It is also fragile in two directions: a growing distributor consumes cash in receivables and inventory (Resideo consolidated held $1,357M of inventory and $1,114M of receivables at April 4, 2026, the majority of it ADI's), and separation costs are running hot — $18M in FY2025, then $24M in Q1 2026 alone with $15M cash paid.

**One clean positive.** The Honeywell indemnity that dominated Resideo's economics for seven years — $972M of FY2025 expense, a $1,590M one-time cash payment in August 2025 — was terminated pre-spin and, on the evidence available, stayed with Resideo. Any screen showing distress on the ADI complex (Resideo's Altman Z of 2.15, FY2025 FCF of −$1,253M) is an artifact of that settlement and should not be read across. We cannot verify how the Separation and Distribution Agreement allocates residual Honeywell-related exposure or Resideo's $88M Tax Matters Agreement liability.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying demand is low-single-digit. Excluding Snap One's $446M wrap-around and $18M of FX, FY2025 organic growth was approximately $130M on $4,197M, or 3.1% — roughly $66M volume and $64M price/mix. Q1 2026's +7.6% is largely a calendar artifact: 94 elapsed days versus 88, or ~6.8% more. Selling days differ from calendar days and management did not quantify the effect for ADI specifically, so the precise adjustment is uncertain, but the directional conclusion is well supported: like-for-like Q1 growth was flat to low-single-digit. The day-count benefit reverses over the balance of 2026, so full-year comparisons will be clean.

Geographic mix improved — Q1 2026 International $168M (+24.4%) versus Americas $1,038M (+5.3%) — but off a small base and with a $15M FX tailwind. A geographic mix shift establishes geographic diversification; it says nothing about customer concentration, which is not disclosed in any filing we could read.

The forward drivers, in order of weight:

1. **End-market cycle.** ADI keys off residential and commercial construction, repair/remodel, existing home sales and bank lending standards. Resideo's pre-spin 2026 outlook was "slow growth in the U.S. residential housing market and a moderation of growth in the non-residential construction market." A distributor with 4.4% operating margins has very little cushion against a volume decline.
2. **Adjacency expansion into datacom and pro AV.** Extending an existing branch network and installer relationship into adjacent low-voltage categories is a plausible route to above-market revenue growth. It is also where margin dilution is most likely, since challenger share gains in commoditized datacom are typically bought on price.
3. **Exclusive brands.** Snap One's owned portfolio carries higher gross margin than resold product. This is the clearest self-help lever, and it introduces a strategic tension: ADI's private-label push gives its 1,000+ third-party suppliers a reason to diversify channel. The filings do not state that any supplier has reduced ADI allocation for this reason; we flag it as an inference about incentives, not a disclosed conflict.

The single available consensus estimate — one analyst — is $1.47 current-year and $1.56 next-year adjusted EPS, +6.1% growth. There is no revision history (all prior-period fields are $0.00), so the "estimates stable" flag carries no information for a stock that has existed for four sessions. The CEO's qualitative claim of "above market revenue growth" is unquantified and is not evidenced in the segment data.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value.** Equity $2,052M plus funded debt $1,000M equals **$3,052M**, before cash (opening balance unknown, so EV is overstated by whatever cash ADI holds) and before the preferred (terms unknown).

| Metric | Multiple |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2025 revenue $4,784M | 0.64x |
| EV / FY2025 segment operating income $212M | 14.4x |
| EV / segment EBITDA $340-350M | 8.7-9.0x |
| EV / standalone-adjusted EBITDA $275-310M | 9.8-11.1x |
| Price / FY2026E adjusted EPS $1.47 | 18.4x |
| Price / FY2027E adjusted EPS $1.56 | 17.3x |
| Implied FCF yield on equity (pre-working capital) | 6.3-8.3% |

**Peer context, with a caveat.** The supplied Electronic Equipment & Instruments comparables (DBD 24.5x, NSSC 40.1x, CXT 22.0x, MIR 177.7x, OSIS 27.0x; median 27.0x) are manufacturers with materially higher gross margins and are the wrong cohort for a 21%-gross-margin, 4.4%-operating-margin distributor. The economically relevant comparison set is value-added specialty distribution, which historically clears at 12-20x forward adjusted EPS and 8-12x EBITDA. On that basis ADIG at 17.3x FY2027E and 9.8-11.1x standalone EBITDA is inside the range, toward the middle-to-upper half — appropriate for scale and category position, arguably generous given 3% organic growth, negative operating leverage and 3.2-3.6x leverage.

**The GAAP/adjusted gap is unusually wide and needs stating.** Taking $212M of segment EBIT, less $63-68M of interest, less $40-70M of incremental standalone corporate cost, gives pretax income of $74-109M; at 26% tax, net income of $55-81M, or **$0.72-1.07 of GAAP EPS** before any preferred dividend — 25-38x the current price. The bridge to $1.47 adjusted is almost entirely ~$98M of annual Snap One purchase-accounting amortization plus separation costs. Investors will be directed to adjusted EPS; the reconciliation is large and the cash interest inside it is real. Compare GAAP to GAAP and adjusted to adjusted when the first standalone results arrive.

**What the market is pricing.** The four-session move from a $22.00 first close to $27.03 is the opposite of the usual spin-off pattern, in which index-insensitive holders sell indiscriminately and create a discount. Here the discount, if it existed, has been consumed. The single published target of $25.00 sits 7.5% below the current price. We do not treat a single analyst target as authoritative, and on high-momentum inflection names targets do lag; but ADIG is not an inflection name — segment profit dollars are flat, organic growth is 3%, and there are no rising estimates to catch up to.

**Scenarios (from the $27.03 basis):**

- **Bear $14 (−48%):** Standalone dis-synergies at the top of the range ($70M), a housing-led volume decline, gross margin erosion continuing at the Q1 pace, and standalone EBITDA of ~$275M valued at 7.5x — $2,063M EV less $1,000M debt = ~$14/share — with a preferred/covenant resolution that is benign. Anchor $14.
- **Base $24 (−11%):** Standalone EBITDA ~$310M at 9.0x = $2,790M EV, less $1,000M debt = $1,790M equity = $23.60/share. Cross-checks against 15-16x $1.50 of adjusted EPS ($22.50-24.00) and against the single analyst target of $25.
- **Bull $34 (+26%):** Operating margin recovers toward 5.0% on ~$5.0bn of revenue as ERP one-timers roll off and standalone cost discipline exceeds the dis-synergy assumption, standalone EBITDA ~$340M at 10.5x = $3,570M EV less $1,000M debt = $2,570M = $33.85/share.

Probability-weighted at 30/50/20, expected value is approximately **$23.20, or −14.2%** from the official close. That arithmetic, not a view on business quality, drives the rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The disclosed competitive set is broad and the disclosed defenses are thin. Resideo's 10-K names, for ADI specifically: other distributors, manufacturers shifting to their own dealer networks or selling direct, retailers and online commerce platforms, and customers vertically integrating. The listed competitive factors — availability, delivery speed, service, price, support, customer credit — contain no contractual lock-in, no switching costs and no network effects. That is consistent with a mid-single-digit operating margin and low-single-digit organic growth, and it is why the trajectory question matters more than the level.

Two disclosures deserve emphasis because of their construction. On suppliers: manufacturers "have in the past and could in the future decide to shift to or substantially increase their existing distribution with other distributors, their own dealer networks, or directly to resellers or end-users," and terminations or changes in terms from key manufacturers "have in the past and could in the future negatively affect that segment's operating income and margins." The "have in the past" phrasing is an acknowledgment that disintermediation has already occurred. On customers: competitive pressure from "customers taking manufacturing or distribution in house, or purchasing directly from a manufacturer instead of from ADI." ADI faces pressure from both ends of its own value chain. With 1,000+ suppliers no single loss is fatal, but the category leaders in security and AV have the strongest incentive and capability to go direct.

The branch network is the durable asset. Roughly 200 locations near job sites, holding 500,000 SKUs, extending credit to one-truck contractors, is not replicable by a new entrant and not economically attractive for a manufacturer to build. Gross margin trajectory is the metric that tests whether that asset is holding — and it fell ~40bp year over year in Q1 2026.

One framing from the parent is worth carrying: Resideo disclosed that ADI contributed 64% of revenue and 35% of operating income in FY2025, and separated it while retaining the ~18% operating margin Products & Solutions business. The disclosed rationale is focus; the disclosed cash mechanic is Resideo's deleveraging. That should temper any assumption that the separation was primarily about unlocking value inside ADI.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unquantified convertible participating preferred ahead of the common (highest-priority unknown).** ADIG's Form 4 records 150,000 shares of "Series A Cumulative Convertible Participating Preferred" acquired and then disposed of by Resideo, and ADIG's August 4 8-K carries Item 3.02 (unregistered equity sales), Item 3.03 (material modification of securityholders' rights) and Item 5.01 (changes in control of registrant). We could not read the 8-K text or the Form 10 (SEC fetches returned HTTP 403), so the face amount, coupon, conversion price, holder identity and any board or veto rights are unverified. Resideo's analogous instrument — 500,000 shares for $500M, held by CD&R — carried ~19.9% of voting power, up to two board appointments, and blocking consents over charter amendments, senior equity issuance, certain debt-document amendments and any liquidation filing. If ADIG's terms are similar in character, the control and dilution profile differs materially from a share-count-only view. A $1,000-per-share extrapolation implying ~$150M is an inference we explicitly decline to treat as fact, in either direction. No dilution percentage can be responsibly stated until the terms are disclosed. Reported common shares outstanding are ~75.9M; any conversion is additional contingent dilution on unknown terms.

**2. Leverage into a thin-margin, working-capital-intensive, construction-levered business.** $1.0bn of funded debt against $275-350M of EBITDA, with $63-68M of cash interest against 4.4% operating margins. Interest coverage is roughly 4.0-5.6x on EBITDA, adequate but not comfortable if volumes decline. Covenant levels, baskets, Term B amortization and hedging are all unverified.

**3. No audited standalone financials in our hands.** Every EBITDA, EPS and leverage figure above is derived from Resideo's segment disclosures, which by the parent's own definition exclude corporate cost, interest and taxes. Opening cash is unknown. The first standalone reporting period has not been filed.

**4. Unfinished ERP integration inside an IT separation.** Resideo disclosed delays in implementing certain ADI enterprise systems, that the Snap One/ADI application consolidation remains in process, and that FY2025 ADI operating income absorbed $10M of one-time system-implementation impact. ADI must now finish merging two ERPs while disentangling from Resideo's systems. Because inventory availability and delivery accuracy are the core value proposition and both are ERP-dependent, execution failure here hits revenue and margin simultaneously. Transition Services Agreements typically bridge 12-24 months, which limits but does not remove the risk.

**5. China-sourced surveillance regulatory exposure with a disclosed enforcement history.** U.S. federal, state, U.K. and E.U. restrictions apply to surveillance and networking equipment from certain China-based ADI suppliers, and the 10-K states: "We have in the past had inquiries and claims from the U.S. federal government and a U.S. state court regarding sales of certain Chinese made products in the U.S." Video surveillance is a core ADI category. Affected revenue is not quantified and no penalty is disclosed, but supplier substitution raises cost against a 21% gross margin.

**6. Tariff regime in flux.** The 10-K discloses the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court holding that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, the subsequent Section 122 surcharge of not less than 10% effective February 24, 2026 (150-day maximum), and the Q1 10-Q adds 15-50% metals tariffs on certain finished goods from April 6, 2026 plus Court of International Trade litigation. Management's stated position is that a significant portion of ADI product sourced in Mexico is USMCA-exempt and that no material impact is expected — a favorable softening from the 10-K's language. The second-order risk management concedes is more relevant for a distributor: "rising prices and other macroeconomic factors may lead to lower purchase levels by our customers." Exclusive-brand products, the highest-margin portion of the mix, are disproportionately sourced in Asia.

**7. Governance and management-alignment opacity.** No DEF 14A exists. We do not have the CFO name, board roster, charter anti-takeover provisions, or compensation design. CEO Rob Aarnes led ADI as a Resideo segment president, so operating continuity is intact; Resideo simultaneously replaced its own CEO (Geldmacher out, Surran in) at separation. There are no open-market insider transactions to interpret — ADIG's insider record consists of ten Form 3 initial statements filed August 3 plus the mechanical distribution entries. The 75,918,198-share grant and 75,923,198-share disposition are separation mechanics and carry no conviction signal in either direction.

**8. Thin trading history and unreliable technical anchors.** The quoted 52-week range of $19.90-$28.91 largely reflects four regular-way sessions plus the when-issued period. Average volume of ~1.6M shares against 75.9M outstanding, with the extended-hours print 4.6% above the official close, indicates price discovery is still in progress.

There is no options data for ADIG in the supplied inputs, so no options market signal is available.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($34, +26%).** ADI is a scaled, category-leading distributor bought at 0.64x revenue and roughly 9-11x standalone EBITDA, generating $130-170M of free cash flow before working capital — a 6-8% yield on the equity. Standalone incentives improve the operating margin that Resideo's segment structure never optimized: the $10M ERP one-timer rolls off, the SG&A build in Q1 2026 partly reflects separation and extra selling days rather than run-rate cost, and exclusive brands continue to lift gross margin the way Snap One lifted consolidated margin 50bp in FY2025. Adjacency expansion into datacom and pro AV delivers the "above market revenue growth" management claims. Debt amortizes from free cash flow, leverage falls from ~3.4x toward 2.5x within two years, and the equity re-rates as the market gets its first audited standalone financials and a credit rating. Spin-offs with clean self-help stories and no legacy liability — and the Honeywell indemnity did stay with Resideo — have historically been fertile ground.

**Bear case ($14, −48%).** ADI is a 3%-organic-growth, 4.4%-margin distributor that just took on $1.0bn of debt to fund a dividend to its former parent, into a construction cycle that Resideo itself described as slowing. Segment operating profit was flat in dollars on $85M more revenue and gross margin fell 40bp; if that persists, the $63-68M interest bill and $40-70M of standalone corporate cost consume most of the incremental profit the business generates. Standalone EBITDA at the low end of $275M puts leverage at 3.6x with a 7.125% unsecured coupon and floating-rate secured debt, and covenant headroom is unknown to us. Suppliers going direct and online channel encroachment are disclosed as having already occurred. An unfinished ERP consolidation runs concurrently with IT separation, threatening the availability and delivery accuracy that constitute the moat. A convertible participating preferred of unknown size sits ahead of the common with potentially concentrated board and veto rights. And the stock has already risen 22.9% in four sessions past the only published target, meaning the entry point offers no compensation for any of it.

The bull case is coherent but requires the operating-leverage inflection to appear in data we have not yet seen. The bear case requires only the continuation of two observed quarters plus an unfavorable resolution of the standalone-cost unknown.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Base case $24 (−11% from $27.03), bull $34 (+26%), bear $14 (−48%), probability-weighted expected value ~$23.20 (−14.2%).

**De-risking plan.** Distribution recipients should sell into the current post-spin strength rather than around a catalyst — the four-session 22.9% rally has removed the discount that normally compensates for spin-off information asymmetry, and the first standalone 10-Q is the point at which the information gap closes in either direction. A residual position of no more than one-third of the distributed shares is defensible for holders who want optionality on the operating-leverage recovery; new capital should be zero until the Form 10 financials and the preferred terms are in hand.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD.** (1) First standalone 10-Q showing ADI segment-equivalent operating income up year over year in dollars, with gross margin flat or better than 21.2%; (2) Form 10 or 10-Q disclosure of the Series A preferred's face amount, coupon and conversion price that implies fully diluted dilution below 8%, together with Term B covenant headroom above 1.0 turn; (3) price in the $22-23 area, which would restore a modest discount to base case.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY.** Operating margin recovering to 4.8%+ on a standalone basis with confirmed corporate dis-synergy at or below $45M, alongside net leverage disclosed at or below 3.0x and a first-time credit rating at BB− or better; or a price below $20 with fundamentals intact.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Disclosure that the preferred carries a liquidation preference above ~$300M, blocking rights over asset sales or financings, or a conversion price near current levels; (2) any covenant amendment request, revolver draw for working capital, or leverage disclosed above 4.0x; (3) an ERP or system-separation disruption cited as materially affecting fill rates or revenue; (4) a supplier of material size publicly shifting distribution away from ADI.

**Reassessment timeline.** The first standalone quarterly report — the first reported standalone period is Q3 2026, a partial post-spin period — is the decisive event. Between now and then, the Form 10 information statement and the August 4 8-K exhibits are the documents to read; both should resolve the preferred, the opening balance sheet and the standalone cost base.

## Conclusion

ADIG is a real business at a defensible multiple with an indefensible entry point. The distribution franchise — roughly 200 branches, 500,000 SKUs, 100,000 installer customers, $4.8bn of revenue — has genuine scale advantages in inventory availability, proximity and trade credit, and the Honeywell indemnity that dominated its former parent's financials appears to have stayed behind. Free cash flow of $130-170M before working capital against a $2.05bn equity is a respectable starting yield.

Against that, the segment delivered flat operating profit dollars on 7.6% reported revenue growth that was itself flattered by a calendar artifact; FY2025 organic growth was ~3.1% with half from price/mix; gross margin fell 40bp; and the equity now carries $63-68M of annual interest on debt whose proceeds went to Resideo. Leverage is 2.9x on segment EBITDA and plausibly 3.2-3.6x on standalone EBITDA, with the difference resting on a corporate-cost figure we cannot verify. A convertible participating preferred of unknown size and rights sits ahead of the common. The stock has risen 22.9% in four sessions to 8% above the only published target, without a single standalone financial statement having been filed.

The combination — negative expected return to a $24 base case, no margin of safety, and three recommendation-critical unknowns that will be resolved by documents due within a quarter — argues for reducing exposure now and revisiting after the first standalone report. **REDUCE.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bear-case scenario arithmetic ($2,063M EV − $1,000M debt = ~$1,063M equity ≈ $14/share, not $17) and the corrected probability-weighted expected value (~$23.20, −14.2%, versus the report's −11.6%) both push the risk/reward modestly more negative, reinforcing rather than undermining the REDUCE call; several forward-looking statements not grounded in the brief (specific 10-year yield/mortgage-rate/mortgage-application claims and the data-center structured-cabling tailwind) were removed as unverified.
