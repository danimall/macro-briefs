## Executive Summary

EastGroup Properties is a self-administered equity REIT focused on shallow bay industrial distribution properties across U.S. Sunbelt markets. The company has delivered 51 consecutive quarters of FFO growth, maintains the lowest leverage in the industrial REIT sector (~3x debt/EBITDA), and is achieving re-leasing spreads above 40%, an unusually powerful embedded rent growth engine. Q1 2026 results beat estimates by 6.8% ($1.30 vs. $1.22 consensus), and management raised full-year FFO guidance on improving leasing activity and historically low new supply levels.

The fundamental quality is clear: $721M revenue growing 12-13% annually, FFO/share compounding at 7-8%, occupancy above 96%, and a development pipeline yielding ~7% (150bps above market cap rates). The balance sheet carries a Moody's Baa2 rating with positive outlook. The issue is valuation. At ~$198, EGP trades at ~40.6x trailing GAAP P/E and roughly 22x trailing FFO, a significant premium to peer median P/E (26.8x) while offering a 3.1% dividend yield vs. the 4.0% peer median. The quality justifies a premium, but the current price leaves limited margin of safety, particularly given the elevated interest rate environment, tariff-driven construction cost uncertainty, and an 18.8% lease rate on the active development pipeline. At $198, the risk/reward skews toward patience.

## Company Overview

EastGroup Properties develops, acquires, and operates shallow bay industrial distribution buildings averaging ~100,000 sq ft with tenants averaging ~35,000 sq ft. This positions the company in the "last mile" logistics segment: smaller, infill industrial buildings near population centers. The portfolio totals ~61.6 million sq ft in the operating portfolio with an additional 3.47 million sq ft in 17 active development projects.

The geographic footprint spans the fastest-growing U.S. Sunbelt markets: Dallas (10.9% of annualized base rent), Houston (9.5%), and meaningful positions across Florida, Arizona, North Carolina, and Southern California. The company's campus-style approach, clustering buildings near higher-end residential areas, creates tenant retention advantages and pricing power. Top-10 tenants represent less than 7% of total revenue, the lowest concentration in the peer group.

EGP is a member of the S&P MidCap 400 with a market capitalization of approximately $10.8 billion. It has maintained or increased dividends for 32 consecutive years, increasing them for 13 consecutive years, most recently by 10.7% to $1.55/quarter ($6.20/year annualized).

### Same-Store Performance

Same-property NOI growth (excluding lease termination fees) was 7.0% on a straight-line basis and 6.7% on a cash basis in 2025 vs. 2024. Q4 2025 accelerated to 8.5%. Re-leasing spreads averaged 40.1% in 2025, meaning each time a lease turns over, the replacement rent is roughly 40% higher. Only 13.1% of annualized base rent expires in 2026, so this repricing flows through gradually but compounds meaningfully over time.

Average same-property rental rates rose from $8.25/sq ft in 2024 to $8.81/sq ft in 2025 (+6.8%). Portfolio occupancy remained tight at 96.6% as of Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Engine: Re-Leasing Spreads

The 40.1% average re-leasing spread is the most powerful growth driver. With ~15% of the portfolio re-leased annually and rents resetting 30-40% higher, embedded organic revenue growth runs 4-6% annually before any portfolio expansion. Even if spreads moderate from 40% to 25-30% (Q4 2025 was already 34.6%), the multi-year compounding effect is significant. This embedded rent growth provides a high degree of visibility into near-term FFO trajectory.

### Development Pipeline

EGP's 17 active development projects (3.47 million sq ft, $710M total investment) represent a meaningful external growth engine. Stabilized yields are running ~7%, approximately 150bps above market cap rates for comparable properties, implying value creation upon completion. At ~$500M in active construction, the pipeline is on track to add ~$35M in incremental PNOI, roughly 6-7% of current same-property NOI.

The near-term risk is the 18.8% pre-lease rate on the active pipeline. EGP builds speculatively by design (the shallow bay segment historically leases post-completion), and the company demonstrated strong Q4 2025 leasing velocity (662,000 sq ft signed, more than half of all 2024-2025 development leasing). Still, speculative development during a period of tariff uncertainty and slowing economic activity warrants monitoring.

### Supply Dynamics

New industrial starts in EGP's Eastern Region markets have fallen 60% from peak levels. Permitting has become "materially harder" per management, and data centers are competing for the same land and power infrastructure, a novel supply constraint. Management expects industrial supply to be at its lowest level since 2018. For an owner of existing, occupied buildings, this supply picture is highly favorable.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year EPS estimates have been revised upward by +3.1% over the past 30 days and +1.1% over 90 days, positive momentum. Next-year estimates are flatter (+0.2% 30d, -1.7% 90d), reflecting some uncertainty about the durability of current leasing conditions.

## Valuation Assessment

EGP is trading at:

- ~40.6x trailing GAAP P/E ($197.86 / $4.87 EPS)
- ~22x trailing FFO ($197.86 / $8.98 trailing FFO)
- 3.1% dividend yield vs. peer median 4.0%
- Forward P/E of ~36x per Phase 1 data vs. peer median of 26.8x, a 34% premium

The premium is partially justified by the lowest sector leverage, best tenant diversification, 51 consecutive quarters of FFO growth, and 40%+ re-leasing spreads. The development pipeline adds a growth dimension that most peers lack. However, sustaining this premium requires 7-8% FFO growth indefinitely, 96%+ occupancy, and no material compression in leasing spreads. At current levels, the consensus price target median of $210 (range $180-$240) offers only ~6% upside to the midpoint.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation at $198: the stock is trading within 3% of its 52-week high of $203.60, pricing in essentially no risk from tariff-driven construction cost uncertainty, the 18.8% development pipeline lease rate, or broader macroeconomic deceleration. The 3.1% yield provides limited downside protection in a rising-rate environment. At ~36x forward earnings, a modest compression to 32x (still well above peer median) takes the stock to ~$176. The risk/reward at $198 is roughly symmetrical, lacking the asymmetric setup required for a BUY.

At $180-185, where the yield approaches 3.4% and the implied FFO multiple compresses to ~20x, the margin of safety improves materially. That entry point existed as recently as late January 2026.

## Competitive Landscape

EGP operates in a segment of industrial real estate that is structurally advantaged relative to the more widely followed big-box warehouse market. Shallow bay vacancy runs approximately half the overall industrial vacancy rate. The smaller building format serves location-sensitive tenants — distributors, e-commerce fulfillment, light manufacturing — whose demand is driven by proximity to population centers rather than raw square footage.

The company's competitive advantages include:

1. **Development capability:** 7% stabilized yields, 150bps above acquisition cap rates, funded by the lowest-leverage balance sheet in the sector.
2. **Geographic diversification:** Broadest Sunbelt footprint among focused industrial REITs.
3. **Tenant granularity:** Top-10 tenants below 7% of revenue eliminates single-tenant concentration risk.
4. **Campus-style positioning:** Infill sites near residential areas create natural barriers to entry.
5. **Occupancy premium:** EGP achieves occupancy 10-20 percentage points above local market averages in many submarkets (e.g., 99% occupied in Austin where the broader market vacancy is ~20%).

Versus peers: Prologis (PLD) dominates big-box and has minimal overlap. Rexford (REXR) at 41.8x P/E is more expensive with narrower Southern California focus. STAG (26.6x) and First Industrial (33.4x) are more diversified but lack EGP's development platform. Terreno (TRNO at 16.8x) appears cheaper but has a coastal-only focus with limited growth runway.

## Risk Assessment

### Tariff and Trade Policy Uncertainty (Primary Near-Term Risk)
The 10-K expanded tariff-related risk language materially. CEO Loeb explicitly cited "capital decision-making paralysis" following April 2025 tariff announcements. Construction materials costs could increase, compressing development yields below the projected 7%. Tenant expansion plans in tariff-sensitive sectors (import/export distribution) could slow, reducing leasing velocity. This risk is live and not fully reflected in the stock's proximity to 52-week highs.

### Interest Rate Environment
While EGP's existing debt is well-positioned (3.43% average rate, all fixed), the refinancing trajectory is unfavorable. Each maturity replaced at 4.1-4.2% adds incremental interest expense. The macro rate environment also creates competition for income-oriented capital: with a 3.1% REIT yield, EGP requires a compelling growth story to attract incremental buyers.

### Development Execution Risk
The 18.8% pre-lease rate on the active development pipeline ($710M invested, with $162M remaining to be invested) means the vast majority of development is uncommitted. Historical lease-up has been strong, but the combination of tariff uncertainty and potential recession creates a wider-than-normal range of outcomes for development NOI realization.

### Geographic Concentration
Houston and Dallas combined represent ~20% of annualized base rent. Texas energy sector sensitivity is a concentrated risk.

### Supply in Select Markets
While overall supply is declining, Austin (~20% vacancy) and Phoenix (~14-15% vacancy) carry meaningful oversupply of big-box product. EGP's shallow bay niche is more insulated, but not immune to broader market sentiment shifts affecting rental rate expectations.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 21.8% sits 3.7 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility (18.1%), with the term structure in backwardation (21.8% near-term declining to 18.7% at 6 months). This suggests the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, likely connected to the recent earnings release and proximity to 52-week highs. Put/call OI ratio of 0.12 is heavily call-skewed, indicating limited institutional hedging demand. The options market is pricing a ±4.1% move over the next month, consistent with earnings-driven repositioning. The backwardation structure is a mild caution signal: it implies near-term risk that dissipates over time.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($220-240, +11% to +21%)
- Same-store NOI growth sustains at 6-8% as re-leasing spreads remain above 30% and occupancy holds above 96%
- Development pipeline leases up faster than expected, adding ~$35M in incremental PNOI by year-end 2027
- Supply-constrained environment (lowest since 2018) supports continued rent growth
- Moody's upgrade to Baa1 reduces cost of capital
- Nearshoring/reshoring trends drive incremental tenant demand for shallow bay space in Sunbelt markets
- FFO/share reaches $9.80-10.00 in 2027, supporting a 24x multiple = $235-240

### Bear Case ($160-175, -12% to -19%)
- Recession materializes, occupancy drops to 93-94%, re-leasing spreads compress to 15-20%
- Tariff-driven construction cost increases compress development yields below 6%
- Interest rate environment reprices REITs broadly; higher long-term rates push REIT cap rates higher
- Development pipeline encounters extended vacancy; speculative starts prove poorly timed
- FFO/share growth stalls at $9.00-9.20 in 2027, multiple compresses to 18-19x = $162-175

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

2. **Two consecutive quarters of development pipeline lease-up exceeding 200,000 sq ft per quarter**, confirming that tariff/recession fears are not impairing tenant demand
3. **Same-store NOI growth sustaining above 6%** through Q2 2026, combined with re-leasing spreads above 30%

2. **Re-leasing spreads compressing below 15%** — would undermine the embedded rent growth thesis
3. **Development pipeline lease-up stalling below 25%** for two consecutive quarters while new starts continue — would indicate capital allocation discipline breakdown

### Reassessment Timeline
- **Q2 2026 earnings (July 2026):** Primary checkpoint for development leasing velocity and same-store metrics
- **October 2026:** $140M debt maturity at 1.98% — refinancing terms will provide forward cost-of-debt visibility

## Conclusion

EastGroup Properties is a best-in-class industrial REIT with structural competitive advantages, a proven management team executing an orderly succession plan, and a balance sheet that provides strategic flexibility. The shallow bay niche is structurally supply-constrained, and EGP's development capability generates value through a 150bps spread over acquisition cap rates.

The constraint is price. At $198, trading at a significant P/E premium to peers with a 90bps yield discount, the stock prices in continued execution with minimal margin for error. The specific risks not adequately discounted — tariff-driven construction cost inflation, the 18.8% development pipeline lease rate, and macroeconomic uncertainty — create a wider range of outcomes than the current multiple implies. A 10-15% pullback to $180-185 would create a compelling entry point where the quality premium is adequately compensated by a more favorable risk/reward ratio. HOLD with a positive growth bias.
