## Executive Summary

Mirion is a radiation detection, measurement and monitoring franchise with a genuinely difficult-to-replicate qualification moat — products installed in more than 98% of the world's nuclear power plants, radiation-therapy QA presence in more than 80% of cancer centers, and over 80% of nuclear-power end-market revenue derived from the installed base. The end market is improving: the IAEA has raised nuclear projections five consecutive years, roughly 70 reactors are under construction with 115 more planned, and three US units (Palisades, Three Mile Island, Duane Arnold) have entered recommissioning. Management's own 10-K frames AI datacenter electricity demand as a two-sided driver rather than a one-way tailwind, which is a more honest treatment than most companies levered to that theme provide.

The problem is the gap between that setup and current operating delivery. H1 2026 revenue grew 23.4% to $524.4M, but the 10-Q MD&A bridges attribute approximately $82.1M of the Nuclear & Safety increase to acquisitions and $7.7M to currency; consolidated organic growth (price plus volume) works out to roughly +2.1%, and management reported Q2 organic growth of about 1%. Medical revenue declined outright, to $80.6M from $81.2M, with segment operating margin expanding from 13.4% to 18.4% on price and camera growth against a volume decline. Maintained full-year guidance (revenue +22-24%, adjusted EBITDA $292.5M midpoint, adjusted EPS $0.52 midpoint) implies H2 revenue of $604.6M-$623.1M and H2 adjusted EBITDA of $172.9M — 59.1% of the full year, at a 28.2% margin against 22.8% in H1. That is a ~540bp half-over-half step-up, and it must come from volume leverage, price and mix because H1 restructuring spend was $0.6M.

The stock has fallen 48% from its October 29, 2025 high of $30.28 and 30% over twelve months. That derating is information-driven rather than noise: the Q2 print made the organic-growth shortfall and the H2 dependency explicit. I do not think the market is simply wrong here. At 16.1x guided forward EV/adjusted EBITDA, 23.7x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $0.66, and a 2.7%-3.6% free cash flow yield, the shares price a business that must prove the acceleration before the multiple re-rates. Base case fair value is $17.50 (+11.8%), against a $21.50 bull (+37%) and a $10.75 bear (−31%); probability-weighted expected return is roughly +7%. Fourth consecutive HOLD, with the classification honestly labeled uncertain — the H2 organic ramp is not verifiable from the current disclosure set, and it determines whether fair value is $11 or $22.

## Company Overview

Mirion operates two segments. **Nuclear & Safety** ($614.6M of FY2025 revenue, 66.4% of total, 16.4% segment operating margin) supplies radiation monitoring systems, reactor instrumentation detectors, imaging, waste management, laboratory spectroscopy and defense/CBRNE detection across the nuclear fuel cycle, and sells to 23 of 32 NATO militaries. **Medical** ($310.8M, 33.6%, 15.1% margin, up from 7.4% in FY2024) covers radiation therapy QA (SunCHECK, Oncospace), nuclear medicine and radiopharmaceutical handling (ec2 Software), and occupational dosimetry (Instadose, over one million wearers).

The company has integrated eighteen acquisitions since 2016, two of them in 2025 and both material: **Certrec** (July 31, 2025, $82.9M gross; nuclear regulatory compliance) and **Paragon Energy Solutions** (December 1, 2025, $588.6M gross; obsolescence management and safety-related parts qualification, plus ~150 engineers). Together they extend the offering from licensing through operations to parts availability, materially increase US exposure, and move Mirion further onto Curtiss-Wright's and Framatome's ground.

The IP posture is deliberately trade-secret-weighted — 78 issued US utility patents, and the 10-K states management believes public patent disclosure "could harm our competitive position." R&D is 4.2% of revenue ($38.9M FY2025) with 534 engineers out of 3,281 employees. This is a qualification-and-incumbency moat (10 CFR 50 Appendix B, ASME NQA-1, ISO 19443, FDA/ISO 13485; 40-to-100-year plant lifecycles), not a technology-velocity moat, despite the GICS Information Technology label.

Customer concentration is low in absolute terms — no customer above 5% of revenue in FY2023-FY2025, and none above 10% of revenue or receivables in Q2 2026 — but top-five concentration rose from 11% (FY2024) to 13% (FY2025).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability trajectory:**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $717.8M | $800.9M | $860.8M | $925.4M |
| Operating income | $(297.8)M | $(21.9)M | $24.8M | $51.5M |
| Net income | $(276.9)M | $(98.7)M | $(36.6)M | $29.8M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | — | $180.7M | $203.6M | $227.9M |
| Operating cash flow | — | $95.2M | $99.1M | $143.3M |

Revenue compounded at roughly 8.8% annually over FY2022-FY2025, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 22.6% to 24.6%. FY2025 was the first year of positive GAAP net income since the 2021 SPAC merger, and the accumulated deficit was $512.7M.

**GAAP earnings are a low-signal metric at this company, for a specific and disclosed reason.** FY2025 pretax income of $32.7M included a $17.4M foreign exchange gain on intercompany loans; excluding it, pretax would have been roughly $15.3M. The effective tax rate was 8.9%. In H1 2026 net income *fell* to $4.7M from $8.9M despite adjusted EBITDA rising 22.2% ($119.6M vs $97.9M), because the FX line swung $21.0M (from a $16.3M gain to a $4.7M loss) and amortization rose $10.4M. The 10-K is explicit: "We do not currently purchase forward contracts to hedge against the risks associated with fluctuations in exchange rates." The €283.8M of cross-currency swaps outstanding at June 30, 2026 are designated net investment hedges — they hedge euro net-asset translation through OCI, not the intercompany-loan revaluation that runs through the income statement. Adjusted EBITDA, segment operating income and operating cash flow carry the information; trailing GAAP P/E of roughly 125-170x carries almost none.

Amortization of acquired intangibles compounds the distortion: disclosed future amortization is $57.6M for the remainder of 2026, $103.5M in 2027, $86.1M in 2028. With H1 2026's $61.0M, FY2026 tracks toward ~$118.6M versus $102.4M in FY2025. Nuclear & Safety's *GAAP* operating margin fell to 12.6% in H1 2026 from 14.8% because acquisitions carried $16.8M of purchase-accounting amortization, while N&S adjusted EBITDA rose to $51.0M in Q2 from $37.9M. Both are accurate measurements of different things. Amortization add-backs are legitimate for assessing cash generation; they do not cure the genuinely low asset turnover (0.258) or the goodwill-inflated asset base.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt at June 30, 2026 was $1,226.6M — a $450.0M term loan at SOFR + 2.00% (5.70%) maturing June 2032, $400.0M of 0.25% convertibles due 2030, $375.0M of 0.00% convertibles due 2031 — against $418.7M of cash, for net debt of $807.9M. That is 3.54x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA and 2.76x guided FY2026. Two features make this unusually defensive for a levered mid-cap: **no maturity before 2030**, and the only financial covenant (First Lien Net Leverage ≤7.00x) springs only when revolver borrowings exceed 40% of commitments — the $175M revolver was undrawn at June 30 with $162.4M available, so no maintenance covenant is currently in effect. Estimated annual cash interest is roughly $27M (term loan ~$25.7M plus ~$1.0M of convertible coupons), partly offset by $2.6M of quarterly interest income. Mirion has substituted equity optionality for interest expense, and at $15.65 — with the 2030 notes 32% below their $23.11 conversion price and the 2031 notes 46% below $28.82 — it has effectively borrowed $775M at approximately zero cost.

The asset side is where risk concentrates. Goodwill of $1,861.7M is 52.9% of $3,521.0M total assets; with $545.5M of other intangibles, the two are 68.4% of assets against $1,825.8M of stockholders' equity. Tangible book value is approximately negative $581M. Goodwill is carried gross at $2,073.5M against $211.8M of cumulative prior impairment, recorded in the FY2022 period. The Paragon allocation remains provisional through December 1, 2026: goodwill $345.1M, intangibles $253.7M (customer relationships alone $174.4M), negative net tangible assets of $10.2M — essentially the entire $588.6M price is intangible.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $143.3M less $36.4M capex gave $106.9M of free cash flow (11.6% of revenue). H1 2026 operating cash flow was $77.4M (vs $48.0M) less $19.9M capex, or approximately $57.5M. I would not annualize the half: the 10-K states Q4 is the seasonal peak for both sales and cash flow, and H1 benefited from working-capital timing (net contracts-in-progress fell from $69.1M to $63.1M, the doubtful-account allowance from $3.4M to $1.8M, and new-order advance payments can inflate a half without repeating). Treat FY2026 FCF of $130M-$170M as a sensitivity band rather than a forecast. Cash barely moved (from $412.3M to $418.7M) because $45.2M went out in financing, mostly $41.0M of treasury purchases.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.93 (grey zone) rises to approximately 2.66 substituting market for book equity — still grey. The two weakest components, EBIT/assets of 0.014 and asset turnover of 0.258, are largely artifacts of a 68%-intangible balance sheet carrying $100M+ of purchase-accounting amortization, and the score cannot see the maturity schedule or the springing covenant. I read it as a flag on earnings quality and asset composition, not near-term default risk. Financial health scores 5.8: the liability side is genuinely strong, the asset side genuinely weak.

**Earnings surprise record** is constructive on the adjusted line — three beats in four quarters (Q2 2026 adjusted EPS $0.12 vs $0.10, +15.3%; Q2 adjusted EBITDA $65.3M vs $62.78M) against a 1.0% GAAP revenue miss ($266.8M vs $269.6M). The pattern suggests management manages the adjusted print well; it does not resolve the organic question.

## Growth Analysis

The decisive numbers are the H1 2026 organic bridges. Nuclear & Safety's +$96.6M change decomposed into acquisitions +$82.1M, FX +$7.7M, price +$3.7M and organic volume +$3.1M — organic of +$6.8M on a $275.1M base, or +2.5%. Medical's +$2.9M decomposed into price +$7.5M and camera organic +$4.8M against volume −$10.3M and FX +$0.9M — organic of +$2.0M on $149.8M, or +1.3%. Consolidated organic is roughly +2.1%, consistent with the ~1% Q2 figure management cited.

Guidance requires H2 organic growth of 7.5%-11.2%, a three-to-five-fold acceleration. The *reported* full-year 22-24% is likely achievable on the Paragon lap alone — Paragon closed December 1, 2025, so H2 2026 laps essentially zero. The stretch sits in the organic and margin components.

RPO grew 36.0% in 2025 to $1,104.3M and only 3.1% in H1 2026, to $1,138.6M. That is not inconsistent with reported ~40% Q2 order growth — RPO is a net balance already reduced by the $524.4M recognized in the half, while orders is a gross company-defined metric — but the two should not be compared as if 40% orders means 40% backlog. More important, the 10-K explicitly limits RPO's scope: it excludes maintenance activity and agreements that are not firm purchase orders, and the company states outright that "Remaining performance obligations is not a complete measure of our future business." RPO is not total backlog.

Timing coverage shifted. At December 31, 2025, 49% of RPO was expected to convert in 2026 (~$541M). At June 30, 2026, 35% of $1,138.6M was expected in the remainder of 2026 (~$399M). Against implied H2 revenue of ~$614M, roughly a third of H2 must come from outside currently scheduled RPO — short-cycle book-and-ship plus maintenance revenue, which RPO excludes by definition. That exclusion means the gap is overstated by an unquantified amount, but it explains why both Citigroup and Goldman pressed management on Q4 nuclear visibility on the call, and why CFO Schopfer answered with revenue-recognition lag, backlog coverage and easier comparisons while acknowledging "the need for continued order wins and execution." CEO Logan told Melius that "timing remains the biggest risk to translating pipeline into revenue."

The margin ramp deserves the same arithmetic. FY2026 guidance requires a ~540bp H1-to-H2 adjusted EBITDA margin step-up (22.8% to 28.2%). With H1 restructuring of $0.6M and only $2.7M expected over the next twelve months, there is no cost program to deliver it — the step-up depends on the revenue ramp arriving, plus mix from higher-margin Certrec/Paragon content. Gross margin has already improved to 49.9% from 46.0%, which supports the mix argument. The 10-K documents genuine Q4 seasonality tied to fall outages ("our highest volume of sales and cash flows occurs in the fourth quarter"), which is the principal defense of the implied step-up.

Estimate revisions do not confirm acceleration. Current-year consensus is unchanged at $0.54 over 90 days (+0.1%), next year $0.66 (+0.7%), but the current quarter has been cut 0.9% and next quarter 2.3% over 90 days. This is not a strong positive revision trend; it is consensus holding a maintained guide while trimming near-term quarters.

The SMR-dependent portion of the growth story is explicitly flagged by the company. A new FY2025 risk factor states that growth "relies in part on plans to supply our products and services to SMRs and other advanced reactors in the U.S., an emerging market that may grow more slowly than we expect," that many SMR developers "are new entrants… and may fail operationally even if they develop a successful reactor, which may negatively impact the entire market," and that revenue "may be subject to volatility as our SMR partners succeed, delay or cancel their projects, or fail altogether." Logan's framing on the call — SMR growth "will likely outpace overall organic growth for the foreseeable future" — is relative, not absolute, and overall organic growth is currently 1-2%.

A quieter point undercuts the recurring-revenue narrative: service revenue was 25.4% of consolidated revenue in FY2025, 25.3% in FY2024 and 25.4% in FY2023. Despite the Vital Platform launch, SunCHECK, Oncospace, ec2 and Paragon's HIPS, the disclosed mix has not moved. Growth prospects score 5.0 — the end-market runway is real and durable, the current conversion is weak, and the acquired growth carries an equity and goodwill cost.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.65 with 249.03M fully exchanged economic shares (243.27M Class A plus 5.76M Class B exchangeable 1-for-1):

- Equity value $3,897M; EV = $3,897M + $1,226.6M gross debt − $418.7M cash = **$4,705M**
- **EV / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA $227.9M = 20.6x**
- **EV / FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA $292.5M = 16.1x**
- EV / FY2025 revenue = 5.08x
- **Price / FY2026 guided adjusted EPS $0.52 = 30.1x**; on $0.54 consensus = 29.0x
- **Price / FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS $0.66 = 23.7x**
- FY2025 FCF yield = 2.74%; at $140M FY2026 FCF = 3.6%

Peer medians in Electronic Equipment & Instruments are P/E 26.5 with wide dispersion (ITRI 12.9, VNT 10.1, OSIS 26.5, BMI 29.5, PI negative). Every peer trades 30-43% below its 52-week high, so the derating is sector-wide, not Mirion-specific. On adjusted forward earnings Mirion sits near the peer median at 23.7x FY2027 despite delivering 1-2% organic growth — the multiple is carried by the nuclear end-market narrative and by acquisitive top-line optics.

Contrast this with the sell side. All ten covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Hold (2 strong buy, 7 buy, 1 hold) with a mean target of $24.80, median $24.50, and a *low* target of $22.00 — 40.6% above spot. The distribution has not moved in four months. Two readings are possible: targets are stale relative to the Q2 organic disclosure, or the market is discounting something the sell side is not. Given that the price broke to a 52-week low of $14.11 on the print day and that 20.6% of the float is short, I weight the second more heavily. Analyst targets are treated as floors only when a stock looks cheap against rising estimates; here estimates are flat and near-quarter revisions are negative, so the target cluster is corroboration at best.

The genuine valuation question is whether 16.1x forward EV/EBITDA fairly prices an installed-base franchise with 25-27% EBITDA margins, 2.76x net leverage, no maturities before 2030, and a structurally growing end market — or whether it overprices a 2% organic grower whose reported growth requires continuous acquisition funded by equity and goodwill. My answer is that it is close to fair, with the distribution wide enough that the multiple is not the source of edge. Free cash flow supports this: a 3.6% FY2026 FCF yield on the fully exchanged base is unremarkable for mid-single-digit growth and thin for 2%.

Dilution, stated both ways: the convertibles' 30.32M potential shares are 12.2% of the 249.03M existing base, equivalent to 10.9% ownership dilution (existing holders retain 89.1%). GAAP diluted shares of 275.07M in Q2 2026 already include these under the if-converted method regardless of price. Both tranches are deeply out of the money, and $82.6M of purchased capped calls (caps $34.88 and $42.70) absorb economic dilution above the conversion prices. The CEO's 2.5M target performance options at a $20.14 strike (up to 3.75M at maximum, $27.5M expected expense) are 28.6% out of the money and belong in scenario weighting, not current capitalization; at target they are 1.0% of shares.

Valuation scores 5.3 — improved from the prior review because the price is ~10% lower on maintained guidance, but not high, because there is no identifiable mispricing to point to. A compressed multiple on unproven organic growth with a goodwill-heavy balance sheet is exactly the configuration that produces value traps.

## Competitive Landscape

The installed base is the durable asset. Over 80% of nuclear-power end-market revenue comes from it, the US fleet averages over 40 years and the global fleet over 30, and re-licensing, upgrade and replacement cycles are procedurally hard to re-source given qualification requirements. The 10-K also identifies a specific share-gain vector: a large installed base of "orphaned" products at aging plants forcing operators to consider new suppliers.

Mirion's competitors are almost uniformly larger and better capitalized: Thermo Fisher, Ametek (Ortec), Teledyne (FLIR), Curtiss-Wright, Framatome in Nuclear & Safety; PTW, IBA, Standard Imaging, Comecer, LAP in Medical. Fortive competes on both sides — Fluke in industrial instrumentation and Landauer in dosimetry. Mirion competes on qualification, incumbency and breadth rather than scale or R&D budget, which is a defensible position but caps pricing power against well-funded entrants in the fastest-growing niches.

Trajectory is mixed. Nuclear & Safety is strengthening: Paragon and Certrec buy incumbency in compliance and safety-related obsolescence management, adjacencies that fit the installed-base logic, and North American N&S revenue nearly doubled year over year ($134.9M to $216.2M). Medical is weakening at the volume level — revenue down year over year, margin expansion price-led, dosimetry product sales declining, and an Asia-Pacific tariff regime cited for a volume decline. Revenue outside North America fell to 32.9% of Q2 sales from 37.1% for FY2025, which is a concentration increase in North America rather than diversification, occurring alongside top-five customer concentration rising from 11% to 13%.

One structural dependency deserves attention: SunCHECK "requires access to data such as electronic health information from other third-party vendors of our customers, typically original equipment manufacturers." If OEMs engage in information blocking, functionality is impaired — a software growth vector partially dependent on the cooperation of adjacent competitors. Competitive position scores 5.8: a strong level with a mixed trajectory and a genuine scale disadvantage against the largest competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**H2 guidance execution is the dominant near-term risk and it is dated.** The Q3 print (late October) is the first hard test of the 7.5%-11.2% organic guide and the 28.2% implied H2 margin. A miss on either would likely force a FY2027 reset and multiple compression, since roughly a third of implied H2 revenue must come from outside currently scheduled RPO.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1,861.7M of goodwill against $1,825.8M of equity, with the Paragon allocation provisional to December 1, 2026 and $211.8M of prior impairment on the books. A 24% writedown would eliminate roughly a quarter of book equity. It would not touch cash, EBITDA or covenants — the relevant question is whether Paragon's cash flows justify $588.6M, not how the accountants bucketed it.

**Russia/China exposure and a disclosure scope change.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed Russia-only RPO of $103.2M. The Q2 10-Q disclosed Russia *and China combined* RPO of ~$124.0M (10.9% of total) with $18.8M of net contract assets and $10.4M of advance payment guarantees. These figures are not comparable and should not be read as an increase. The aggregation reduces granularity on a live exposure, in the same quarter a China contract was cancelled and the tariff risk factor was expanded to name China localization requirements. The most likely benign explanation is that Chinese exposure crossed a materiality threshold; I have no evidence of intent. The cancellation's dollar amount is not disclosed in the sources reviewed, and management's characterization of it as "unusual but not indicative of broader risk" is a company assertion, not verified fact.

**Unreconciled Asia-Pacific disclosure.** The 10-K states Asia Pacific was ~17% of FY2025 revenue (~$157M); the Q2 10-Q shows Asia-Pacific H1 2026 revenue of $7.3M on $524.4M. These cannot share a measurement basis (likely destination versus selling-entity). Neither figure should be relied upon until reconciled, and this matters precisely because China/APAC is where the tariff and cancellation risk sits.

**Other named, specific risks:** fixed-price nuclear contracts with escalation terms that "may not fully cover all possible cost increases," against $63.1M of net contracts-in-progress at June 30; single-site manufacturing of whole body monitors exclusively in Ontario, Canada, which the 10-K says "would be difficult to remediate elsewhere"; a self-identified control risk from the decentralized model ("certain of our business teams are from time to time staffed leanly") for a company that has integrated eighteen acquisitions since 2016; nuclear public-perception and Price-Anderson reauthorization exposure; and $175M notional of interest rate swaps rolling off (75M settled Q2 2026, 100M in Q4 2026), leaving the $450M term loan unhedged floating, partly offset by $418.7M of cash.

**Governance.** Chairman and CEO roles are combined. No open-market insider purchases were identified. The CAO sold 8,400 shares at $19.64 in May 2026, roughly 27% of his direct position. Logan made two gift transfers (Form 4 code G) totaling 3.41M shares, reducing reported *direct* holdings from ~3.48M to ~52,000; code G is not a sale and indirect holdings were not obtained, so total beneficial ownership cannot be stated to have declined — this is a verification gap, not a signal. CFO Schopfer holds 913,290 shares, which is substantial alignment. The CEO retention grant is well-designed (TSR hurdles over three- and four-year periods, 150% cap, one-year post-vest hold, strike above then-market) but large, and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was revised downward specifically to accommodate it. The DEF 14A endpoint returned only additional-materials cover pages, so compensation levels, peer group, say-on-pay and related-party detail could not be verified from a primary source. An April 13, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K and the July 2, 2026 Loïc Eloy "Letter Agreement and Secondment Addendum" both remain unresolved as to substance.

**Capital allocation record.** Mirion issued 19,906,322 shares at $21.35 in September 2025 (net $409.7M) to help fund Paragon, then repurchased ~$41.0M in H1 2026 at roughly $18 — arithmetically favorable per share for the company, though September buyers are down 26.6% and the H1 buybacks are underwater at $15.65. Cumulative repurchases stand at 3.3M shares for $59.6M (~$18.06 average), with $40.4M of the December 2024 authorization remaining. Management quality scores 4.7: sound liability management and a properly structured incentive grant, offset by no insider buying, buybacks above the current price, verification gaps, and a serial-acquisition model whose returns are unproven while organic growth runs at 1-2%.

Risk factors score 4.5 — the H2 binary, the goodwill overhang, the provisional Paragon allocation and the unresolved geographic disclosures are specific and only partly priced by the 48% drawdown.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $21.50 (+37%)

Q4 seasonality is documented and real ("our highest volume of sales and cash flows occurs in the fourth quarter… increased outages occurring in the fall"), Q2 orders grew ~40% against a 12-to-36-month nuclear sales cycle, and 2025 orders exceeded $1bn for the first time. Paragon and Certrec lap into clean H2 comparisons. If the H2 organic ramp lands within guidance and adjusted EBITDA reaches $292.5M, FY2027 EBITDA of ~$325M is plausible, and a re-rating to ~19x forward EV/EBITDA — still below the 20.6x trailing multiple the shares carry today — gives $21.50. Equivalently, 30x FY2027 adjusted EPS moving toward $0.72. The liability side is genuinely defensive (no maturities before 2030, no live maintenance covenant, ~$27M of cash interest against $228M+ of EBITDA), the 2028 30% EBITDA margin target has been reaffirmed twice, and 20%-plus directional short interest would cover into a validating print. Sell-side targets of $22-$28 become reachable rather than stale.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (−31%)

Q2 organic growth of 1% is the true underlying rate, and Medical volume decline is demand destruction rather than tariff timing. H2 organic lands at 3-5% against a 7.5%-11.2% guide; FY2026 adjusted EBITDA comes in near $270M and FY2027 guidance resets toward $280M. With no cost program available (H1 restructuring $0.6M) and roughly a third of implied H2 revenue outside scheduled RPO, the margin step-up does not materialize. The multiple compresses to 12.5x forward EV/EBITDA — a rate appropriate to a low-single-digit organic grower — giving $10.75. Secondary pressure: a Paragon-related goodwill writedown once the provisional allocation finalizes, further China/tariff attrition on the $124M Russia-plus-China RPO block, and FX-driven GAAP losses that keep the reported P/E uninterpretable. The 48% drawdown from October does not floor the stock; the 52-week low of $14.11 is only 10% below spot.

### Base Case — $17.50 (+11.8%)

H2 organic accelerates but lands below guidance (call it 5-7%), FY2026 adjusted EBITDA arrives near $285M, and FY2027 adjusted EPS approximates the $0.66 consensus. Applying the 26.5x peer-median P/E gives $17.50; the cross-check on EV/EBITDA is ~17x forward, roughly holding the current multiple while EBITDA grows. ## Conclusion

Mirion owns a real qualification moat in a genuinely improving end market, and its liability structure — no maturities before 2030, no live maintenance covenant, $775M of near-zero-coupon convertibles, $418.7M of cash — is better than the grey-zone Altman reading implies.
