## Executive Summary

Hertz Global Holdings is attempting a complex turnaround under CEO Gil West after a catastrophic EV strategy destroyed over $4B in value in 2024 alone. The company currently trades at $6.48 with a market cap of ~$2B against $17.1B in total debt, negative stockholders' equity of -$459M, and an Altman Z-Score of 0.34 (deep distress). Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.5% YoY — the strongest quarter in three years — and depreciation per unit has normalized from $587/month to $310/month, representing genuine operational progress.

The investment case hinges on whether management can achieve its $1B EBITDA target by 2027 (from -$339M in 2025), which would cover the ~$1B annual interest burden and potentially generate positive net income. The Oro Mobility partnership with Uber for autonomous vehicle fleet management provides speculative upside optionality. However, the balance sheet has minimal margin for error: $3.4B in debt matures within 12 months, liquidity has been declining (from $1.84B to $1.49B, further reduced by a $346M litigation payment), and the company operates in a cyclically weak environment for rental pricing with consumer spending under pressure.

Analyst consensus is firmly bearish (0 Buy ratings; 6 Hold, 1 Sell, 2 Strong Sell) with a mean price target of $4.43 — 32% below the current price. Short interest at 44.9% of float and options IV at 120% (vs. 79% HV) confirm the market views this as a distressed, binary situation. The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside given the fragile balance sheet and turnaround uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Hertz operates as the world's second-largest vehicle rental company through its Hertz (premium), Dollar (value), and Thrifty (deep value) brands across ~11,000 locations in approximately 160 countries. Revenue splits approximately 80% Americas RAC and 20% International RAC. The fleet peaked at approximately 527,000 vehicles, with 89% of the Americas fleet being non-program (at-risk) vehicles that expose the company to residual value fluctuations.

The company emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in June 2021, then executed a disastrous EV expansion under former CEO Stephen Scherr (100,000 Tesla order plus other EV brands) that resulted in $3.6B in vehicle depreciation charges in 2024 and pushed the balance sheet back to distress territory. Current CEO Gil West (former Delta Air Lines COO, appointed 2024) is executing a "Back-to-Basics" strategy focused on disciplined fleet management, revenue optimization, and cost control.

In April 2026, Hertz launched Oro Mobility, an affiliated operating company focused on fleet management for ride-sharing and autonomous vehicle operations, signing partnerships with Uber for both AV fleet management and driver-led fleet operations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $6.48 per share and a market cap of ~$2B, traditional valuation metrics fail:
- Forward P/E: -2.7x (negative earnings)
- P/Book: N/A (negative equity)
- EV/Revenue: ~2.2x (EV of ~$19B on ~$8.5B revenue)

**Path-Dependent Valuation:**
The stock's value depends entirely on whether Hertz can survive its debt load and reach profitability:

- If $1B EBITDA by 2027 is achieved: At 6x EBITDA (depressed multiple for highly leveraged cyclical), EV = $6B. Less ~$15B net debt = -$9B equity value... which illustrates the problem. Even at $1B EBITDA, the debt burden consumes virtually all enterprise value for equity holders.
- If $1.5B+ EBITDA (bull case with Oro value): At 6x, EV = $9B. Less $15B debt = still negative.
- For equity to have intrinsic value above zero requires either: (a) sustained EBITDA well above $2B, (b) substantial debt reduction, or (c) strategic value premium for Oro Mobility

## Competitive Landscape

Hertz occupies the #2 position in a mature oligopoly behind privately-held Enterprise Holdings and ahead of Avis Budget Group (CAR).

**Structural Challenges:**
- Enterprise's private ownership allows longer-term fleet decisions without quarterly earnings pressure
- Ride-sharing platforms (Uber, Lyft) have permanently reduced certain short-trip use cases
- Peer-to-peer platforms (Turo) erode pricing power, particularly off-airport
- Airport concession fees and fixed infrastructure costs create high operating leverage (cuts both ways)

**Hertz's Position:**
- Brand recognition remains strong across three tiers (Hertz/Dollar/Thrifty)
- Global footprint with 11,000 locations provides network effects
- Fleet management expertise is genuinely differentiated — this is the Oro Mobility thesis
- Loyalty program provides some pricing power vs. commoditized competition
- 89% non-program fleet = higher margin potential but higher residual risk

**Avis Budget (CAR) Comparison:**
Avis reported weak Q1 2026 (stock -12%), confirming industry-wide pricing pressure. However, Avis maintains positive equity and lower leverage ratios. Hertz's relative competitive weakness is entirely financial (balance sheet), not operational.

**Competitive Position Trajectory:**
Gil West's operational improvements (utilization at 82%, DPU normalization, revenue recovery) suggest the competitive position is stabilizing after the EV-driven deterioration. The Oro Mobility pivot could differentiate Hertz in the AV infrastructure space. However, Enterprise could replicate fleet management capabilities for AV operators without Hertz's balance sheet constraints.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Refinancing Risk (SEVERE):** $3.4B in debt matures within 12 months. With negative equity and sustained losses, any disruption to ABS markets or credit tightening could trigger a liquidity crisis. The minimum liquidity covenant ($400-500M) leaves ~$1B of headroom at current levels — and this is declining.

2. **Consumer Cyclical Exposure:** Rental demand is closely tied to discretionary travel spending. Travel volumes are the first discretionary category cut during economic weakness.

3. **Residual Value Risk:** 89% non-program fleet means Hertz bears used car depreciation directly. While tariffs may support used car values near-term (constraining new supply), any economic downturn causing used car value declines would restart the depreciation spiral.

4. **Dilution:** Multiple equity overhang sources: 82.7M warrants ($13.61 strike), $425M exchangeable notes ($9.24/share = ~46M shares), $250M PIK notes (growing principal), $250M ATM program. Full dilution could exceed 450M shares vs. 315M currently outstanding.

5. **Execution Risk on $1B EBITDA Target:** This requires simultaneous DPU below $300, RPD recovery, volume growth, and cost reduction — in a cyclically weak market with consumer stress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8-10):**
- $1B EBITDA by 2027 is achieved through continued DPU normalization and revenue recovery
- Oro Mobility partnership with Uber scales successfully, generating material revenue by 2027-2028 and creating strategic premium
- Tariffs support used car residual values, keeping depreciation below $300/unit
- Travel demand remains resilient through summer 2026 peak season
- Short squeeze mechanics push stock toward exchangeable note conversion price ($9.24)
- Successful refinancing of near-term maturities at manageable rates

**Bear Case ($2-3):**
- Consumer recession materializes, travel volumes decline 10-15%, RPD falls further
- Used car values decline (tariff relief, economic weakness), DPU reverts toward $400+
- ABS market disruption or credit tightening prevents refinancing of vehicle debt
- Liquidity falls below covenant minimum, triggering default cascade
- Oro Mobility proves immaterial; autonomous mobility scaling timeline extends to 2030+
- ATM dilution at distressed prices destroys per-share value
- Company forced into second bankruptcy or highly dilutive restructuring

**Base Case:**
The most probable outcome is continued muddling: gradual operational improvement insufficient to generate meaningful free cash flow to equity after interest payments, periodic short squeezes creating trading opportunities but no sustained re-rating, and ongoing uncertainty about whether 2027 targets are achievable. The stock oscillates between $4-7 for the next 12 months, with resolution coming only when the refinancing wall forces either successful capital markets access (stock holds/rises) or distressed restructuring (stock collapses).

## Short Candidate Assessment

1. Summer 2026 earnings (likely August) — if peak season RPD disappoints, the $1B EBITDA 2027 narrative collapses
2. Vehicle debt refinancing windows throughout 2026 — $3.1B must be rolled in a tightening credit environment
3. Liquidity approaching covenant minimum as operating losses continue

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The peak summer season (June-August) provides a natural test of the revenue recovery thesis. If summer demand disappoints, the stock likely re-tests the $3.75-4.00 March lows by fall.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 44.9% SI with 5.7 days to cover. This is extremely high and represents the primary risk to any short position. The stock has demonstrated multiple 20-40% squeeze events (Nov 2025, April 2026). Any positive catalyst (Uber expansion announcement, analyst upgrade, short-covering cascade) can drive violent upside.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options pricing at 120% IV makes put strategies expensive. Cost to borrow is likely elevated given 44.9% SI. However, the put/call OI ratio of 2.12 and structural backwardation confirm informed institutional bearish positioning despite the squeeze risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Target $3.50-4.00 on bear catalyst (analyst consensus is $3-5). From $6.48, that's 38-46% downside. Upside squeeze risk to $8-9 (24-39%) in the absence of fundamental change. Risk/reward approximately 1.2:1 to 1.8:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is correct (highly leveraged, loss-making, fragile balance sheet, trading above all analyst targets), but the 44.9% short interest and demonstrated squeeze mechanics make active shorting extremely dangerous. The reward:risk ratio does not clearly exceed 2:1 given the mechanical squeeze risk and periodic positive catalysts (Oro announcements, summer seasonality, retail meme activity). This is a name to avoid owning, not actively short.

## Conclusion

Hertz at $6.48 is a speculative, binary turnaround situation trading above analyst consensus ($4.43 mean target) primarily on retail enthusiasm for the Uber/Oro Mobility partnership and short squeeze mechanics. The operational turnaround under Gil West is real — depreciation normalization, utilization improvement, and Q1 revenue growth confirm progress. But improving from catastrophic does not make it investable.

The fundamental challenge remains: $17.1B in debt generating ~$1B in annual interest expense on a business that produced -$339M in EBITDA in 2025. Even achieving the $1B EBITDA target by 2027 leaves equity holders with minimal residual value after debt service absent a 3-5 year deleveraging trajectory that assumes no further cyclical downturns. The stock currently prices optimism that the balance sheet cannot support through a recession, a used car value decline, or a credit market disruption.

With negative equity, declining liquidity, options pricing extreme uncertainty, and zero analyst Buy ratings, Hertz represents uncompensated risk at current levels. The Oro Mobility optionality is speculative and immaterial for 2-3+ years. Avoid.
