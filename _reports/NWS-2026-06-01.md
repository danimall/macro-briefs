**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corporation's Class B voting shares present a clean version of the long-running conglomerate-discount thesis: a 61.6% stake in publicly listed REA Group whose market value alone represents a large fraction of News Corp's total A+B equity, plus a Dow Jones B2B data franchise (Risk & Compliance growing ~18%, Energy/OPIS ~10-12%) that the market appears to value at little to nothing on a look-through basis. The post-Foxtel portfolio is cleaner and tilted toward recurring, digital, higher-margin revenue. Total Segment EBITDA grew 14% in FY25 and 10% in 9M FY26, with margin expansion the core driver. A near-net-cash parent balance sheet, $2.2B in cash, and a buyback pace that roughly quadrupled to $459M in 9M FY26 (vs. $115M a year earlier) signal board-level conviction the stock is undervalued.

The case stops short of an actionable BUY. Three specific factors keep this at HOLD. First, near-term estimate momentum is negative: next-quarter EPS estimates were cut 10.7% over both the 30-day and 90-day windows, and current-quarter estimates slipped 2.8% over 30 days. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that would convert a discount story into a momentum BUY. Second, there is an active DOJ Antitrust Division grand-jury subpoena (January 2025) plus a state civil investigative demand (April 2025) tied to OPIS/Dow Jones pricing in the PVC-pipe matter; a criminal antitrust probe is an unbounded, binary risk that the current price does not appear to discount. Third, the dual-class Murdoch control structure, reaffirmed by the September 2025 LGC stockholders agreement that retained the 44% Class B voting cap, structurally entrenches the conglomerate discount the bull case requires to close; a controlled company is unlikely to break itself up, which caps the catalyst that would unlock SOTP value.

The trajectory has been positive (5.8 in February to 6.5 in May, with Financial Health and Competitive Position each up a full point), and the current data confirms the operational improvement. But the stock has declined 9.4% since the prior report, and the move down coincides with deteriorating near-quarter revisions rather than pure multiple noise. On unchanged-to-slightly-improving fundamentals, the lower price modestly improves risk/reward, which is why this remains a constructive HOLD with a bullish lean rather than a downgrade. The upside (15-18% to a re-rating target) is real but gated on events outside the company's control, and the downside scenario (-17 to -19%) carries genuine binary tail risk.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates five reportable segments following the April 2025 divestiture of Foxtel to DAZN:

- **Dow Jones** (FY25 revenue $2,331M; Segment EBITDA $588M) — The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, MarketWatch, and the higher-growth B2B data franchises Dow Jones Risk & Compliance and Dow Jones Energy (OPIS/Factiva). Roughly 83-84% digital and recurring; the highest-quality growth engine in the portfolio. Competes with Reuters, RELX/LexisNexis, S&P Global, Argus, and ICIS.
- **Digital Real Estate Services** (FY25 revenue $1,802M; EBITDA $601M, the largest EBITDA contributor) — a 61.6% stake in ASX-listed REA Group (realestate.com.au, Australia's dominant property portal) plus 80% of Move (Realtor.com, the US #2 portal, operated under a NAR license that can be terminated for cause).
- **Book Publishing** (FY25 revenue $2,149M; EBITDA $296M) — HarperCollins, the world's #2 consumer book publisher.
- **News Media** (FY25 revenue $2,170M; EBITDA $153M) — News Corp Australia, News UK (The Times, The Sun), NY Post, talkSPORT. Structurally declining print, managed for cash.
- **Other** — corporate overhead (~-$223M).

A ticker note matters for the valuation lens: NWS is the Class B (voting) stock; NWSA is the Class A (limited-vote) stock. The $5.6B figure cited as "market cap" reflects only the Class B float. News Corp's total equity value across both share classes is materially larger (roughly $15-16B). The SOTP thesis hinges on REA's stake value relative to total A+B equity, not the Class B float in isolation.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Continuing-operations revenue was $8,452M in FY25 (+2%), accelerating to $6,691M in 9M FY26 (+5%), and +9% in Q3 FY26, though the FY26 acceleration is partly an FX tailwind from a weaker dollar against AUD and GBP. The durable story is margin expansion: Total Segment EBITDA rose 14% in FY25 ($1,415M) and 10% in 9M FY26 ($1,204M), driven by Dow Jones data and REA.

**Quality of earnings.** FY25 net income to NWS stockholders of $1,180M was flattered by roughly $800M of non-recurring items: a $692M Foxtel discontinued-ops gain and a $111M PropertyGuru gain at REA. Cleaner continuing-ops net income was $648M. The 9M FY26 continuing net income of $513M is down 9% YoY, but only because the prior-year period contained a $101M PropertyGuru gain; underlying operations improved. The effective tax rate runs structurally high (~30-35%) on foreign-income mix and valuation allowances, which depresses reported EPS relative to peers.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 FCF was $571M (OCF $978M less $407M capex); 9M FY26 FCF was $535M, with management guiding to "strong growth in free cash flow" for the full year. Cash stands at $2.2B (3/31/26) against ~$2.0B of parent-level debt. REA Group's debt is non-recourse to News Corp, so the parent is effectively net-cash. The Altman Z of 1.87 ("grey zone") is misleading here; it penalizes the asset-light, goodwill-heavy media mix (low EBIT/assets) rather than signaling distress. The current ratio of 1.84 and low net leverage confirm a healthy liquidity position. The March 2026 credit agreement refinanced and upsized facilities to $1.5B with maturities extended to 2031, removing any refinancing-cliff concern.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters (12.1%, 14.2%, 17.0%, 0.7% surprises). The beat cadence supports confidence in current-year consensus but is offset by the negative forward revisions discussed below.

These factors support a Financial Health score of 7.0 — strong FCF generation, net-cash parent, no refinancing risk, and improving underlying margins, tempered slightly by a high structural tax rate and goodwill-heavy asset base.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is concentrated and uneven. Dow Jones B2B data is the standout: Risk & Compliance and Energy/OPIS, both recurring with high switching costs and expanding margins. REA Group continues to compound on its near-monopoly Australian portal position. These two franchises carry the structural growth case.

Against them, News Media is in secular print decline, managed for cash via cost cuts and cover-price increases, and HarperCollins is exposed to consumer discretionary spending with the near-term drag of a $120M warehouse equipment finance lease (October 2025) and the California Post launch. Move/Realtor.com is cyclically depressed by mortgage rates and faces NAR commission-rule changes that could pressure agent lead-gen spend.

The forward estimate picture is the binding constraint on the growth score. Current-year estimates are roughly flat (+2.1% over 90 days) and next-year estimates are modestly higher (+3.9% over 90 days), so the longer arc is intact, but the near-term momentum is negative. The combination of a structurally growing data franchise weighed against negative near-quarter revisions and cyclical/secular drags in three of five segments warrants a Growth Prospects score of 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported P/E of 37.8x is not the right lens, because FY25 EPS is depressed by the high tax rate and the reported share base, while the operating businesses carry materially more value than the Class B float implies. The relevant framework is sum-of-the-parts.

The 61.6% REA Group stake is the linchpin. REA's market value implies that News Corp's stake represents a very large fraction of (by some analyses comparable to or exceeding) the company's entire equity value, leaving Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and Move valued at close to zero or negative on a look-through basis. Dow Jones alone, growing double-digits in its data franchises at expanding margins, would command a premium multiple as a standalone B2B information company (peers like S&P Global and RELX trade at premium multiples). On any reasonable parts valuation, the stock is cheap.

Peer comparison reinforces this. The publishing peer set shows a median P/E of 25.7x; NWSA (the Class A line) trades at 33.1x and NYT at 32.3x, both reflecting the digital-recurring-revenue re-rating. NWS Class B at the cited 37.8x trailing looks expensive only because trailing EPS is artificially depressed by one-time tax and mix effects. On a forward look-through basis adjusted for the REA stake, the discount is real.

The accelerated buyback is the corroborating signal: the repurchase program leaves ~$851M remaining on a $1B authorization, funded from a net-cash balance sheet. A board does not quadruple repurchase velocity unless it views the equity as materially undervalued.

The reason the valuation score is 6.5 rather than 8+ is that the discount has persisted for years and the structural catalyst to close it — a break-up or major simplification — is impeded by the Murdoch control structure. Cheap-and-controlled can stay cheap. The Valuation score reflects a genuine but slow-to-realize mispricing, not an imminent re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is improving at the franchise level. Dow Jones Risk & Compliance and Energy/OPIS are entrenched B2B data businesses with high switching costs, gaining share in a structural shift toward recurring digital compliance and pricing data. REA holds a near-monopoly in Australian online property and is extending it via its own A$200M buyback and selective divestitures (PropertyGuru, parts of REA India). HarperCollins holds the #2 global consumer-publishing position.

The offsetting force is generative AI, which the 10-K treats at length as both threat and opportunity. AI scraping, AI search/chatbots reducing referral traffic, and adverse fair-use rulings threaten the news-traffic and subscription model — The Sun has already lost digital ad revenue to platform algorithm changes. The opportunity is content licensing (cited as a growing revenue line within Dow Jones and News UK) plus HarperCollins's participation as a class member in the Anthropic copyright settlement (preliminary approval September 2025, no gain yet recognized). How News Corp monetizes IP in the AI era is the most important long-term swing factor, and the early licensing traction is a positive trajectory signal. Net, a Competitive Position score of 7.0, confirming the prior-period upgrade.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ antitrust probe (the binary tail).** The OPIS/Dow Jones pricing matter also carries a class settlement (preliminary approval July 2025). A criminal antitrust investigation is unbounded and binary; outcomes range from no action to material fines and reputational damage to the OPIS data franchise. This is not visible in trailing financials and does not appear discounted in the price.

**Governance and control.** While the September 2025 LGC stockholders agreement resolves multi-year succession uncertainty (a partial positive), it entrenches concentrated voting control, anti-takeover provisions, and FOX-conflict overlap (Chair Lachlan Murdoch is also FOX Executive Chair/CEO). This structure caps activist optionality and makes the SOTP-unlock catalyst unlikely. A Form 4 RSU grant dated 4/8/2026 in the name "Keith Rupert Murdoch" appears in the data and should be externally verified.

**Cyclical and secular.** Move/Realtor.com is exposed to mortgage rates and NAR commission-rule changes. News Media print is in secular decline. FX translation (large AUD/GBP exposure) inflated reported FY26 growth and could reverse.

**AI disintermediation.** Covered above; a slow-moving structural threat to news traffic and subscriptions.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is thin: the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.09 to effectively 0.00 between early May and June, indicating no meaningful put-protection demand. There is no options-implied warning that contradicts the fundamental view. The more notable positioning signal is the short interest: 17.7% of float short with 8.2 days to cover. That is elevated and reflects bearish institutional positioning — likely a combination of the governance discount, the DOJ overhang, and skepticism that the SOTP gap ever closes. The setup creates modest squeeze potential on any positive catalyst (a clean DOJ resolution or a simplification announcement), but absent a catalyst it reflects the market's structural skepticism.

The Risk Factors score is 5.5 — below neutral because the DOJ criminal probe is a genuine binary tail not reflected in valuation, and the governance structure is an entrenched, non-priced overhang on the catalyst path.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$35, +18%).** The SOTP discount narrows as the accelerated buyback shrinks the share count against a near-net-cash balance sheet and rising FCF; Dow Jones data growth continues to expand margins; the DOJ matter resolves without material penalty; and content-licensing/AI-settlement revenue emerges as a real IP-monetization stream. The September 2025 governance resolution reduces the long-standing control discount at the margin. Continued portfolio pruning (the 10-K flags ongoing evaluation of dispositions) signals further simplification. A re-rate toward the NYT/NWSA digital-recurring multiple on look-through earnings supports ~$35.

**Bear Case (~$24-25, -16 to -19%).** The DOJ criminal antitrust probe produces a material fine or damages the OPIS franchise; near-quarter estimate cuts prove the leading edge of a broader deceleration; AI disintermediation accelerates traffic and subscription erosion at News Media and the WSJ; US real-estate weakness and NAR rule changes pressure Move; and the Murdoch control structure permanently entrenches the conglomerate discount, leaving the stock stuck near 52-week-low territory (low was $25.49 in February).

**Base Case (~$33-34, +11-14%).** Operations continue to improve modestly, buybacks provide a steady bid, but the discount only partially closes and the DOJ matter lingers as an overhang into resolution. The stock re-rates slowly toward the upper end of its recent range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold, bullish lean).** The discount is real and the trajectory is positive, but the upside is gated on a DOJ resolution and a structural-discount unlock that the control structure impedes, while a genuine binary tail sits unpriced. The risk/reward is not asymmetric enough for BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clean resolution of the DOJ/state antitrust matter without material financial or franchise impact.
- Next-quarter and current-quarter estimate revisions turn positive, confirming the operational improvement extends to the near term.
- A concrete simplification or capital-return acceleration (e.g., a tender offer, a Dow Jones value-crystallizing transaction, or buyback velocity sustained above $150M/quarter) that begins to close the SOTP gap.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- DOJ probe escalates to charges or a material penalty affecting OPIS/Dow Jones.
- Two consecutive quarters of Segment EBITDA contraction at Dow Jones, signaling the data-franchise growth is fading.
- A guidance cut on the "strong FCF growth" outlook, or buyback pace reverting toward the prior ~$115M/9M run-rate (which would signal management no longer views the stock as cheap).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026, FY26 Q4/full-year), with the DOJ matter and any 8-K disposition activity monitored continuously. The SOTP/REA-stake valuation should be re-marked each quarter against REA's ASX price.

## Conclusion

News Corp Class B offers a defensible discount: a 61.6% REA stake that anchors the valuation, a Dow Jones B2B data franchise growing double digits at expanding margins, a net-cash parent, and a board signaling conviction through a roughly 4x increase in buyback velocity. The score trajectory (5.8 to 6.5) and the operational data both confirm genuine improvement, and the 9.4% price decline since the prior report has modestly improved entry economics on unchanged fundamentals.

The reason this stops at HOLD rather than BUY is specific and falsifiable. Negative next-quarter revisions remove the earnings-momentum tailwind a BUY would require. An active DOJ criminal antitrust grand-jury subpoena tied to OPIS represents an unbounded binary risk that the price does not discount. And the dual-class Murdoch control structure structurally entrenches the conglomerate discount the thesis depends on closing, making the SOTP-unlock catalyst unlikely without management's own initiative. The upside to a re-rating target (~$35, +18%) is real but contingent on events outside the company's control; the bear case (~$24-25, -16 to -19%) carries genuine tail risk. Accumulate on weakness toward the high-$20s; upgrade to BUY on a clean DOJ resolution or a turn in near-quarter revisions.
