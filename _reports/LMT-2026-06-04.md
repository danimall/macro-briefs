**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest pure-play defense contractor, with $75.0B in FY2025 sales, a $186-194B backlog (~2.6x revenue), and sole-source incumbency on franchise programs that anchor U.S. and allied force structure for decades. The demand backdrop is the strongest in a decade: the FY2026 NDAA authorized $901B, the FY2027 request seeks a "$1.5 trillion" topline, munitions restocking is accelerating, and Missiles & Fire Control margins fully recovered to 13.8% in 2025. The franchise itself is not in question.

The problem is execution and earnings quality. Despite steady single-digit revenue growth, net income fell from $6.9B (2023) to $5.0B (2025) and diluted EPS dropped from $27.55 to $21.49, driven by large fixed-price reach-forward losses on troubled development programs: roughly $1.8B cumulative on a single classified Aeronautics program (with $450M still accrued), ~$1.46B on a classified MFC program, ~$670M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program, and smaller hits on TUHP, F-16 and C-130. Management's repeated language that further losses "could be recognized in any period" is unusually candid hedging. The Q1 2026 print (EPS $6.44 vs $7.28, negative free cash flow of $291M, buybacks paused entirely) confirmed the problem is not behind the company, and the stock fell ~14% in April and sits ~25% below its $692 high.

At ~$516, LMT trades at ~24x depressed trailing EPS and ~17x current-year consensus of $29.89, a discount to the defense peer median P/E of ~38.9 but richer than comparable primes GD (21.2x) and NOC (16.8x) that lack LMT's program overhangs. The 2.7% dividend remains well-covered by $6.9B FY2025 free cash flow, but the Q1 cash dip and paused buyback break from LMT's identity as a cash-return machine. With estimate revisions essentially flat, the underperformance versus peers documented in the company's own proxy (cumulative TSR 156 vs peer group 230), and no confirmed evidence the charge cycle has stopped, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin operates four segments. FY2025 sales were $75.0B (+5.6% YoY), 72% from the U.S. Government (63% DoD) and 28% international:

- **Aeronautics ($30.3B, ~40% of sales):** Home to the F-35 Lightning II, the single largest program at ~27% of total company revenue. Also F-16, F-22, C-130. 1,325 production F-35s delivered to date; backlog of 336 aircraft plus the newly definitized Lot 18/19 award (296 aircraft).
- **Missiles & Fire Control ($14.5B, +14% — fastest grower):** PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM, Javelin, hypersonics. The structural winner from munitions restocking and missile-defense demand.
- **Rotary & Mission Systems ($17.3B):** Sikorsky helicopters (Black Hawk, Seahawk, CH-53K), Aegis, radar, naval combat systems.
- **Space ($13.0B):** Trident II/FBM, Orion, Next-Gen OPIR, GPS III, NGI, hypersonics; equity stake in United Launch Alliance.

The moat rests on sole-source incumbency, a large installed base requiring multi-decade sustainment, and a funded portion of backlog ($120.2B of $193.6B). This is a long-cycle, high-switching-cost business. The thesis is bifurcated by segment: MFC and Space are healthy, while Aeronautics and RMS host the execution problems.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Steady single-digit growth — $66.0B (2022), $67.6B (2023), $71.0B (2024), $75.0B (2025). Q1 2026 sales were flat YoY at $18.0B.

**Margin and earnings deterioration is the headline.** Net income: $6.9B (2023), $5.3B (2024), $5.0B (2025). Diluted EPS: $27.55, $22.31, $21.49. Consolidated operating margin compressed to ~10.3% in 2025. Q1 2026 EPS fell to $6.44 vs $7.28, the trigger for the April drop.

**Root cause — fixed-price reach-forward losses.** A standard screen misses this dynamic:
- **Aeronautics classified program:** $555M loss (2024) plus $950M (Q2 2025); cumulative ~$1.8B, $450M still accrued as of Q1 2026. Drivers: software degradation, design changes, schedule realignment. Management warns of further losses.
- **MFC classified program:** ~$1.4B (2024); cumulative ~$1.46B, $1.12B still accrued; management believes loss-making unexercised options will be exercised.
- **RMS CMHP (Canada):** ~$570M (2025); cumulative ~$670M, $605M of contract assets at risk.
- **TUHP (Turkey):** ~$130M cumulative; further ~$115M possible.
- **Q1 2026 additions:** $125M unfavorable on F-16, $85M on C-130.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 FCF was strong at $6.9B (9.2% margin, up from $5.3B in 2024). But Q1 2026 FCF was negative $291M (vs +$955M Q1 2025), driven by working-capital timing — F-35 contract assets ballooned $2.9B — and higher software capex. Cash fell from $4.1B to $1.9B in the quarter. The cause appears timing-related rather than structural, but it is the freshest red flag.

**Balance sheet — the Z-Score is a false alarm.** An Altman Z-Score of 1.53 ("distress zone") is an artifact of capital structure, not bankruptcy risk. Years of buybacks shrank stockholders' equity to ~$6.7B (equity/assets ~11%), and 2025's charges depressed the EBIT-to-assets term. Debt/equity of 7.9x is distorted by the tiny equity base. Total debt of ~$21.9B against $6.9B+ annual FCF and an investment-grade profile is comfortably serviceable. Piotroski 5/5 (partial) confirms positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF > net income. Goodwill is $11.3B (~19% of assets); impairment test passed Q4 2025. A $479M non-cash pension settlement charge in Dec 2025 (transferring $943M of obligations to insurers) further dented GAAP earnings, with more de-risking signaled.

**Capital allocation:** $3.0B buybacks plus ~$3.2B dividends in 2025; dividend raised to $3.45/quarter (yield ~2.7%). LMT paused buybacks entirely in Q1 2026 (zero vs $750M in Q1 2025) and repaid $1.0B of debt, with ~$8.3B authorization remaining — consistent with conserving cash amid the FCF dip.

## Growth Analysis

The demand environment is the strongest leg of the thesis. Global defense budgets are at records: NDAA FY2026 authorized $901B, the Consolidated Appropriations Act (Feb 2026) funds the DoD through Sept 2026 at $839.2B, and the FY2027 request seeks a "$1.5 trillion" topline with $760B for procurement and modernization, including a Golden Dome missile-defense initiative that would directly benefit MFC and Space. The backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility, and F-35 Lot 18/19 locks in Aeronautics volume.

Growth durability at the earnings level is the open question. Revenue growth is steady single-digit, not accelerating, and consensus EPS estimates are flat: current-year $29.89 (essentially unchanged over 30 and 90 days), next-year $32.11 (+0.5% over 90 days). The earnings algorithm depends on whether the fixed-price charge cycle stops and on whether the DoD's push toward fixed-price structures, progress payments (less favorable than performance-based payments), and aggressive IP acquisition continues to compress margins on new development work. MFC's recovery to ~14% margins shows LMT can convert demand into profit when execution holds; the classified Aeronautics and RMS programs show it sometimes cannot.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$516, LMT trades at ~24x trailing EPS on depressed earnings and ~17x current-year consensus, with a forward P/E around 24x on the reported figure and a 2.7% dividend yield. Against the Aerospace & Defense peer median P/E of 38.9 (skewed by HWM at 58.2, BA at 87.2, TDG at 38.9), LMT looks cheap. The more relevant comparison is to pure defense primes: GD at 21.2x and NOC at 16.8x. On that basis, LMT trades at a modest premium to GD and NOC despite carrying worse near-term margin trajectory and the active program overhangs they lack.

The bull framing is that current earnings are trough-like — depressed by ~$3B+ of cumulative charges and a pension settlement — and that normalized earnings power on the franchise is meaningfully higher. That is plausible, but it requires the charges to actually stop, and management's own language prevents underwriting that with confidence. The estimate-revision data does not support a clean inflection thesis: revisions are flat, not rising, so this does not qualify as the kind of earnings-acceleration setup that justifies looking through a trough multiple.

Analyst targets span $511 (low) to $756 (high), mean $625, median $614, implying ~21% upside to the mean. The wide gap between the current price and consensus reflects analysts anchoring to a normalized-margin recovery the market is not yet paying for. Given the absence of a confirmed catalyst and the flat revisions, the valuation is reasonable and provides downside support via the dividend and franchise, but it is not asymmetric. A valuation score of 6.0 reflects a fair-to-slightly-cheap setup without the mispricing that would warrant a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT's competitive position is strong in level but mixed in trajectory. The franchise programs (F-35, Trident, PAC-3) are near-irreplaceable, and the installed base guarantees decades of sustainment revenue. MFC is gaining clear ground in the structural munitions/missile-defense upcycle, with PAC-3 demand from 17 nations and Golden Dome optionality.

Against this, the company's own proxy documents the problem: cumulative TSR of 156 lagged the peer group's 230, a concrete signal the market currently rewards competitors more highly — likely primes without LMT's classified-program overhangs, plus RTX in missiles. The DoD's procurement shift toward fixed-price development, OTAs that favor non-traditional contractors (Anduril, SpaceX-adjacent players), and aggressive IP-rights acquisition are structural headwinds to the margin model that the broader theme of software-defined and space-domain competition reinforces. Aeronautics and RMS are where share-of-mind and margin are eroding. The 6.5 score reflects a market leader with a widening lead in one segment and execution-driven erosion in others, net stable-to-slightly-positive on the strength of backlog and franchise lock-in.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Fixed-price program execution — the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** The classified Aeronautics and MFC programs, plus CMHP and TUHP, show that LMT's move into fixed-price development carries asymmetric downside, and management explicitly warns of further material losses in any period. This is the single most important item to monitor.

2. **F-35 concentration (~27% of revenue).** Any funding cut, delivery-rate change, or sustainment dispute would be highly material. The FY2027 request increases F-35 purchases (a tailwind), but the concentration cuts both ways and the Q1 contract-asset build of $2.9B sits here.

3. **U.S. Government dependence + budget mechanics.** 72% U.S. Government exposure subjects LMT to continuing resolutions and shutdowns, though near-term funding is in place through Sept 2026.

4. **Executive Order on contractor capital returns (new).** A potential EO/DFARS clause could restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive comp for contractors failing performance requirements — uniquely relevant given LMT's recent issues.

5. **Securities class action (new).** Filed July 2025 (amended Jan 2026) in SDNY plus a derivative suit, alleging misstatements about the classified programs and F-35 during Jan 2024–Jul 2025. LMT deems it immaterial, but it confirms the charges surprised investors.

6. **ULA equity exposure.** Vulcan Centaur challenges could force additional support or impairment of the ~$544M investment.

7. **Tariffs / rare earths.** Tariffs cost ~$485M of cash in 2025 (largely recoverable over time); China rare-earth export controls are an active component-supply concern.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term event stress. Near-term ATM IV is 34.5% vs 26.9% historical volatility (rich by 7.6pp), with the term structure in backwardation (34.5% near vs 28.3% long-dated). Front-end vol elevated above the back end signals the market expects an identifiable catalyst soon rather than a sustained regime of higher volatility. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.8%), and put/call OI of 0.68 is mildly call-tilted, so positioning is not aggressively bearish. Short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float (1.6 days to cover), confirming this is not a crowded short. A standout single print of 491 contracts on the Sep $670 call (+29.8% OTM, 4.6x volume/OI) is a modest upside-recovery bet. The options market expects movement but is not directionally bearish, consistent with a HOLD where the binary is whether the next print shows charges abating.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$650-660, +26%):** Margin recovery takes hold as the troubled classified programs reach their final accrued losses and stop bleeding; MFC sustains ~14% margins and grows on munitions/missile-defense demand and Golden Dome; F-35 Lot 18/19 and the record FY2027 budget drive revenue; FCF normalizes back above $6.9B, buybacks resume, and the dividend continues compounding. On normalized EPS power toward $32-33 at a ~20x multiple, the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of $625 and beyond.

**Bear Case (~$430-450, -13% to -17%):** Fixed-price charges continue — management's own warning materializes — and additional losses on the classified Aeronautics program, CMHP, or new fixed-price development depress earnings further. Negative or volatile FCF persists, pressuring the dividend-growth thesis and forcing extended buyback suspension. The EO on contractor capital returns constrains shareholder returns. The stock de-rates toward GD/NOC multiples on worse margin trajectory.

The two cases are roughly symmetric from $516, with the resolution hinging on whether the Q2-Q3 2026 prints show the charge cycle ending and FCF recovering.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, ~July 2026) is the natural reassessment point — it will show whether Q1's negative FCF was timing-related, whether buybacks resume, and whether new program charges appear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters with no new fixed-price reach-forward losses, plus a reduction in the accrued loss reserves ($450M Aero, $1.12B MFC) without new offsetting charges.
- Free cash flow returns to positive and tracks toward the FY2025 ~$6.9B run-rate, with buybacks resumed.
- EPS estimate revisions inflect positively (consensus current-year moving above $30 on margin recovery, not budget headlines).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new material fixed-price charge (>$300M) on the classified Aeronautics program or a fresh program, signaling the execution problem is structural.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates — sustained negative FCF over two-plus quarters forcing a dividend freeze.
- The contractor capital-returns EO is implemented in a form that materially restricts LMT's dividend or buyback.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin owns a near-irreplaceable defense franchise into the strongest budget environment in a decade, and the stock's ~25% decline from its high reflects genuine, not imagined, problems. The drawdown is justified by fundamental deterioration — falling EPS, ~$3B+ of cumulative fixed-price charges with explicit warnings of more, a Q1 2026 miss, negative quarterly FCF, and paused buybacks — rather than by market noise. The valuation at ~17x current-year consensus is reasonable and the 2.7% dividend is covered, but with flat estimate revisions, no confirmed margin-recovery catalyst, and management still hedging on further losses, the risk/reward is balanced. The franchise and backlog cap the downside; the unresolved execution overhang caps the upside. HOLD, with the Q2 2026 print as the decision point on whether the charge cycle has ended and free cash flow is recovering.
