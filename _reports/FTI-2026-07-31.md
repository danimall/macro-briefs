## Executive Summary

TechnipFMC is a materially different company than it was three years ago, and the numbers support that claim without needing management's help. Consolidated gross margin has expanded 831 basis points, from 13.1% in FY2022 to 21.4% in FY2025, on a revenue base that compounded at 14.1% annually over the same period. Subsea operating margin reached 19.6% in Q2 2026 versus 17.2% a year earlier, and 17.8% versus 15.1% for the half. Total debt has fallen from $1,051.2M (FY2022) to $401.9M, leaving $589.9M of net cash, an entirely undrawn $1.25B revolver, and three ratings upgrades in nine months (Moody's to Baa2, Fitch and S&P to BBB). Net interest expense fell to $9.6M in H1 2026 from $24.3M. This is a company that has fixed its balance sheet and its execution simultaneously.

The quality of the margin improvement is the most persuasive single datapoint in the file. Under percentage-of-completion accounting, contract-estimate revisions are the standard mechanism by which reported margins get flattered. In Q2 2026 the net impact of estimate changes on gross profit was **unfavorable by $17.2M**, versus **favorable by $123.3M** in Q2 2025 — and gross profit still rose to $684.7M from $593.3M. The operating gains are not being manufactured by accounting. That said, the $73.3M of unfavorable cost revisions inside that net figure is the first sizeable negative print in the disclosed periods and deserves monitoring as a possible early cost-inflation or execution-strain signal on specific fixed-price projects.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the order book. H1 2026 total inbound orders were $4,879.0M against $5,920.1M a year earlier, down 17.6%; Subsea inbound fell 17.4%. That produces a book-to-bill of 0.93x consolidated and 0.94x in Subsea, and backlog has stopped growing — $16,440.0M at June 30, 2026 versus $16,571.6M at December 31, 2025. Management has nonetheless reiterated a $10B FY2026 Subsea inbound target, which requires roughly $5.59B in H2 versus $4.41B in H1, a ~27% step-up in quarterly run-rate. Subsea awards are lumpy and FID-timed, so two quarters do not establish a trend. But the shares have roughly doubled in twelve months and now trade at ~23.7x FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.04 and ~20.3x FY2027 consensus of $3.53, with ~12.8x EV to guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.19B. At that price, revenue and margin through 2027 are protected by backlog; the multiple is not. The decisive variable over the next two quarters is order intake, and it is genuinely two-sided.

## Company Overview

TechnipFMC is a UK-incorporated plc operating from Houston, listed on the NYSE, reporting two segments:

- **Subsea** — subsea production systems (SPS), engineering/procurement/construction/installation, and subsea services. This is effectively the whole company: $2,486.9M of $2,763.1M (90.0%) of Q2 2026 revenue and $4,695.3M of $5,255.8M (89.3%) of H1 revenue, against segment assets of $7,487.3M versus $1,174.2M for Surface.
- **Surface Technologies** — wellhead and pressure-control equipment plus completion services. Q2 revenue $276.2M, down 13.3% year over year.

The structural differentiator is **iEPCI**: FTI owns both the SPS product line and the SURF/installation vessel capability, allowing a single integrated award where competitors historically had to assemble separate SPS and SURF tenders. Layered on top is **Subsea 2.0 / Configure-to-Order**, a pre-engineered product architecture for which management claims "up to 25 percent lower product cost" and "as much as a 12-month reduction in delivery time." Those specific figures are company assertions in a filing, not independently audited; the circumstantial corroboration is the 831bp gross-margin expansion since 2022 and the 240–270bp Subsea operating-margin expansion in 2026.

Geographic mix in Q2 Subsea: Latin America $979.1M (39%), Europe & Central Asia $638.1M, Africa $314.9M, North America $220.5M, Asia Pacific $201.4M, Middle East $132.9M. Latin America — predominantly Brazil and Petrobras — is both the largest revenue geography and the largest growth contributor (+$124.3M in Q2, +$376.9M in H1), so mix became *more* concentrated, not less. Named backlog projects include TotalEnergies Mozambique LNG and GranMorgu (Suriname), bp Tiber/Kaskida/Northern Endurance, Petrobras Mero 3 HISEP and Global 24, Shell Orca, Equinor Raia, Eni Coral North. Surface backlog is concentrated in ADNOC and Saudi Aramco.

A useful structural note: US land is less than 5% of total company revenue, and 65% of Surface revenue is international. FTI has almost no North American pressure-pumping beta, which has protected it through the shale slowdown but concentrates Surface in Middle East NOC budget cycles — precisely where the Q2 revenue decline came from.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $991.8M against total debt $401.9M gives net cash of $589.9M (versus $601.9M at year-end). The current ratio of 1.08 looks thin on a screen and is structurally misleading: $2,358.9M of current liabilities is **contract liabilities** — customer advances, not payables. Net contract liabilities stood at **−$1,063.4M** (contract assets $1,295.5M less contract liabilities $2,358.9M). Customers pre-fund over $1B of working capital, which is how a company with $3,271.8M of book equity funded $684.9M of buybacks and cut debt simultaneously. Book value carries no information here (P/B ~8.8x) given the accumulated deficit of $3,652.9M and $1,373.1M of accumulated OCI losses inherited from the 2017 merger and legacy write-downs.

**Cash flow, with the necessary caveat.** H1 operating cash flow was $880.5M against capex of $115.7M, implying $764.8M of H1 free cash flow. That should not be annualized. The half was flattered by working-capital timing — accounts payable +$338.4M and contract liabilities +$73.0M against receivables and contract assets −$436.0M — and, more importantly, capex is running far below depreciation. H1 D&A was $200.7M versus $115.7M of capex, and net PP&E declined from $2,285.3M to $2,257.1M even as revenue grew 10.2%. FY2025 capex was $317.2M, so H2 spending should be materially higher. A naive 2x of H1 FCF is ~$1.53B (5.3% yield); a normalized case assuming no further working-capital benefit and full-year capex at the FY2025 level puts FCF nearer **$1.25–1.40B (4.4–5.0% yield)**. For a vessel-owning contractor, harvesting a prior-cycle asset base for a couple of years is legitimate, but management's own language — "projected capital expenditures do not include any contingent capital that may be needed to respond to contract awards" — flags that a successful order year creates a 2027–2028 capex headwind that current run-rates do not capture.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks are the return vehicle: $684.9M in H1 2026 (versus $500.2M in H1 2025), including $420.1M in Q2 at an average price of $71.55, with $1.5B remaining of a $3.8B authorization. Diluted shares have gone 449.5M (FY2022) → 419.7M (FY2025) → 404.5M (Q2 2026); ordinary shares issued fell from 400.7M to 392.8M in six months. The dividend is a token $0.20 annual ($0.05/quarter, 0.28% yield), with H1 dividends paid of $39.7M against $684.9M of buybacks — a roughly 17:1 buyback-to-dividend ratio. The uncomfortable observation is pace versus price: FTI is spending materially more dollars per quarter on repurchase at roughly double the share price of a year earlier (the 10-K cover discloses a June 30, 2025 close of $34.44). That is a legitimate criticism of timing, not of the underlying cash generation.

## Growth Analysis

**What is locked in.** Remaining unfulfilled performance obligations of $16.4B, with 75.4% recognizable after 2026, give unusually high revenue visibility through 2027. Management's FY2026 framework — Subsea revenue of ~$9.4B at a 21.5% adjusted EBITDA margin midpoint, total adjusted EBITDA raised to ~$2.19B — is a conversion exercise against contracted work rather than a demand forecast. Consensus reflects this: FY2026 EPS of $3.04 rising to $3.53 in FY2027, +16%.

**What is not locked in.** Order intake. H1 Subsea inbound of $4,410.8M against $5,338.6M is a 17.4% decline, and the reiterated $10B target requires ~$2.80B per quarter in H2 versus ~$2.21B in H1. Management's supporting language cites "$3 billion-plus of new subsea opportunities" and a frontier pipeline in Namibia, Suriname and West Africa. The award calendar is genuinely lumpy — a single large iEPCI can be $1–2B — and FY2025 inbound of $11.2B was a record that creates a hard comparison. Both readings are defensible from the data available, which is exactly why H2 inbound is the decisive datapoint rather than a footnote.

**Earnings momentum is confirmed but modest in magnitude.** Four consecutive beats (+12.2%, +37.1%, +11.8%, +10.3%) support confidence in consensus. Estimate revisions are positive but not of the magnitude that signals a re-rating: current quarter +3.6% over 90 days, next quarter +6.4%, current year +2.9%, next year +2.2%. This is a company delivering against expectations, not one where consensus is chasing a fundamental inflection. Note also that consensus has next-quarter EPS at $0.78 versus the current quarter at $0.87 — a sequential step down that implies the sell side already assumes some moderation.

**Structural demand.** Management's outlook argues that offshore has attracted a growing share of capital flows on improved returns, with acceleration concentrated in infrastructure-rich basins (Gulf of America, North Sea) and re-discovered resource regions (West Africa). The genuinely interesting observation is the shift by operators toward "executing multiple projects in parallel rather than waiting for completion of the first project." If real, that favors a standardized configure-to-order supplier with framework agreements over a bespoke bidder — a tailwind specific to Subsea 2.0. Renewables is a candid negative: management describes investment continuing "at a slower pace than previously forecast," which caps the New Energy option value (HISEP, the Northern Endurance CCS iEPCI) at optionality rather than a growth vector.

Deepwater project economics are robust well below current crude, so the oil-price link runs through operator FID confidence rather than project breakevens. A grinding $100–120 environment is supportive of intake; a sharp normalization toward $85 would not break deepwater economics but would slow sanction decisions at the margin. The Middle East conflict is already a *negative* in the reported numbers via Surface, while management frames it as a *positive* for Subsea through a re-rating of Middle East risk pushing capital offshore. That second claim is a hypothesis, not evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

The subsea production market is a tight oligopoly: FTI, SLB's **OneSubsea** joint venture (with Aker Solutions and Subsea7 as announced participants in the venture's formation), Aker Solutions, and Subsea7/Saipem in SURF and installation.

FTI's structural claim — that owning SPS, SURF and vessels enables single-contract iEPCI awards competitors must assemble through JVs — is precisely what OneSubsea was formed to attack. Two recent third-party-reported awards are relevant: bp's Thunder Horse subsea boosting system EPC (reported June 8–9, 2026) and a multi-well EPC award from Eni for Baleine Phase 3 offshore Côte d'Ivoire (reported July 13, 2026). In both, the operator is the paying customer and OneSubsea the contractor. bp is simultaneously an FTI customer (Tiber, Kaskida, Northern Endurance appear in FTI's backlog), so the most supportable reading is that operators are dual-sourcing across integrated providers. The alternative — that these are scope-specific awards where equipment fit and incumbency dominate, saying little about full-field iEPCI win rates — cannot be distinguished from the available evidence. Financial terms were not disclosed and I have no relative win-rate data.

What tightens the concern modestly is the combination: FTI's inbound down 17.4% in Subsea while Aker Solutions reported solid H1 performance and *raised* its 2026 revenue expectation (July 14, 2026). A competitor guiding up is more consistent with a healthy overall market than with a market contraction, which makes FTI's order decline harder to attribute purely to industry timing. That is a trajectory question, and trajectory is what matters for this dimension. I have therefore trimmed competitive position to 6.5 from 7.0 while acknowledging that margin trajectory — the clearest evidence of pricing and execution advantage — continues to improve.

Also worth noting on the strategic side: FTI has filed no acquisition or material-agreement 8-K since April 2023 and no financing 8-K in that period. This is an organic, buyback-funded story with no integration risk and no acquisition accounting to unpick, which is unusual and favorable in oilfield services. The flip side is that a principal competitor has used structural combination to attack FTI's core differentiation while FTI stood pat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. H2 order intake (the dominant risk).** $5.59B of Subsea inbound required in H2 to hit $10B, against $4.41B delivered in H1. A miss does not damage 2026–2027 revenue — that is contracted — but it would remove the growth justification for a ~20–24x forward multiple and would pressure 2028 estimates.

**2. Fixed-price contract and cost-estimate risk, now with a visible negative print.** Q2 2026 saw $73.3M of unfavorable cost revisions on unnamed projects, partly offset by $56.1M of favorable portfolio updates, for a net −$17.2M versus +$123.3M a year earlier. H1 was still net favorable $39.4M versus $137.7M. This is the first sizeable negative print in the disclosed periods and is the mechanism through which cost inflation in a tight vessel and fabrication market would show up.

**3. Concentration.** Latin America is 39% of Q2 Subsea revenue and the largest growth contributor; Petrobras appears twice in named backlog and the Dofcon JV exists to supply pipe-lay vessels offshore Brazil. Surface backlog is concentrated in ADNOC and Saudi Aramco. No 10%+ named customer disclosure was located in the filings reviewed, so customer concentration cannot be quantified — a verification gap, not a clean bill of health. The rising services share of Subsea revenue (67.2% of Q2 Subsea) does not indicate diversification; under FTI's disaggregation, iEPCI project execution is largely captured in "services."

**4. Off-balance-sheet contingent obligations.** Maximum potential undiscounted payments under guarantees of $2,601.7M ($2,412.9M performance, $188.8M financial), equal to 79.5% of $3,271.8M book equity, plus a $228.9M share of Dofcon vessel-debt guarantees. These are contingent and unfunded, normal for an EPC contractor, and should not be added to debt — but they amplify tail risk in a severe project-failure scenario. Investment-grade status directly improves bonding capacity and cost, which is a tangible benefit.

**5. Middle East conflict.** Already in the numbers ($40.1M Q2 / $51.8M H1 Surface revenue reduction attributed partly to regional conflict), not hypothetical. Management's counter-argument that Middle East risk re-rating drives capital offshore is unproven; the alternative is that broad disruption raises costs, delays FIDs and eventually pressures demand.

**6. FX.** Gross derivative assets $413.8M against liabilities $477.6M, net notional positions including €983.9M, £874.0M, NOK 9,290.0M and a −$3,787.5M net USD sold position. The company expects to transfer approximately **$93.2M of loss** from accumulated OCI to earnings over the next twelve months. Q2 corporate items already included a −$19.3M FX loss versus nil a year earlier.

**7. Governance and insider activity.** Douglas Pferdehirt holds the combined Chair/CEO role. The shareholder-approved Value Creation Plan vested ~2.3M PSUs in July 2026 on an ROIC gate ("60% higher than before the VCP was adopted" — baseline undisclosed), of which Pferdehirt received 1,626,240 shares, roughly 73% of the pool and about $117M of market value; Melin and Rounce each received 302,016. Vested dilution is small at ~0.59% of shares outstanding, with ~1.3M PSUs of residual contingent capacity under the 3.6M cap. H1 share-based compensation expense rose to $71.4M from $31.5M. Separately, a same-day cluster on March 9–10, 2026 saw Pferdehirt sell 733,424 shares (~$45.7M), Rounce 172,276, Melin 127,275, Landes 116,194, Duffe 47,951 and Light 6,622, followed by four directors selling between February and May. The most likely explanation is post-vest diversification after a near-doubling in the share price, amplified by the February 24 tax-withholding vest — and the CEO retained 2.41M shares before the July grant, which argues against a bearish read. But it does meet the threshold of clustered selling by three or more C-suite insiders within a short window, alongside estimate revisions of only ~+3%, and there have been no open-market purchases in the last 25 reported transactions. I score this dimension 5.3: capital allocation (debt reduction, meaningful share-count reduction, no value-destroying M&A) is good; alignment optics and buyback price discipline are not.

**8. Unquantified items.** Tariff and trade-policy exposure is named in the risk inventory with no quantified impact disclosed, for a company manufacturing across Europe, Asia and the Americas. UK pension litigation (Virgin Media) is described as largely defused by the Pension Schemes Act 2026 but trustees are still evaluating actions. Supplier-finance balances grew ~17% in six months to $143.7M — modest at 9.2% of trade payables, but worth tracking.

Controls were concluded effective; PwC is the auditor; the two 10-K/A filings in March 2026 were signature-conformity fixes with both restatement cover boxes unchecked, not restatements.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $92 (+28% from ~$71.66)

Subsea remains structurally tight, and the shift toward parallel project execution favors a standardized configure-to-order supplier with framework agreements. H2 2026 inbound delivers the required ~$5.59B on the "$3 billion-plus of new subsea opportunities" pipeline plus Namibia, Suriname follow-ons and West Africa, taking FY2026 Subsea inbound to $10B and returning backlog to growth. Subsea margin holds above 19% as Subsea 2.0 penetration rises, driving FY2027 EPS to the high end of the $3.53 consensus or above. Net cash plus ~$1.25–1.40B of normalized free cash flow funds the remaining $1.5B buyback authorization, shrinking the share count another 4–5% annually. Investment-grade status expands bonding capacity into larger iEPCI awards. At 26x FY2027 EPS of $3.53, the stock is $92; the current analyst high is $87.

### Bear Case — $52 (−27%)

H2 inbound comes in at $4.5–5.0B, FY2026 Subsea inbound lands near $9B, and backlog declines through 2027. The market re-frames FTI as a backlog-conversion story with a visible end date and applies 15x to FY2027 EPS — $53. Compounding factors: the $73.3M unfavorable cost revision proves to be the leading edge of fixed-price cost inflation on a portfolio where vessel and fabrication capacity is tight; contract liabilities stop replenishing (H1 2026 operating inflow was $73.0M versus $261.2M in H1 2025) and the customer-prefunded working-capital advantage reverses into a cash drag; capex normalizes toward and above depreciation as contingent capacity spending arrives, cutting free cash flow toward $1.0B; and $93.2M of accumulated OCI FX losses flow through earnings. OneSubsea continues taking named deepwater scopes at bp and Eni. The analyst low is $45.

### Base Case — $76 (+6%)

FY2027 EPS of $3.53 (consensus, supported by four consecutive beats and a 75.4%-post-2026 backlog) at 21.5x — a modest premium to the peer median that the net-cash balance sheet and margin trajectory justify but that does not assume backlog re-acceleration. H2 inbound lands in the $5.0–5.5B range, slightly short of the $10B target, with backlog roughly flat. Buybacks of ~$1.3–1.4B annually provide ~4–5% of the return. This is a mid-single-digit-return year with a wide distribution around it, which is the definition of a hold rather than an add.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) are the decisive checkpoint. That print contains the first of the two quarters that must average ~$2.80B of Subsea inbound to validate the $10B target, and it will also show whether the Q2 unfavorable cost revision was isolated.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Q3 2026 Subsea inbound ≥ $2.8B**, putting the $10B target within reach and returning consolidated book-to-bill above 1.0x. This is the single cleanest trigger.
2. **Price below ~$60** with backlog and margin guidance intact — that is ~17x FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.53 on a net-cash balance sheet, a level at which the order-intake risk is adequately compensated.
3. **Subsea operating margin sustained above 20%** for two consecutive quarters with no net-unfavorable contract-estimate revision, evidencing that Subsea 2.0 economics are structural rather than mix-driven.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Q3 2026 Subsea inbound below $2.2B**, which would make the $10B target unreachable and put FY2026 inbound below $9B — a second consecutive quarter of sub-1.0x book-to-bill with backlog declining.
2. **A further net-unfavorable contract-estimate revision exceeding $50M**, or any single named project write-down, indicating fixed-price cost inflation is spreading rather than isolated.
3. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $3.20**, or management guiding FY2027 Subsea revenue below FY2026, which would confirm the backlog-conversion-with-an-end-date framing.
4. Contract liabilities declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters alongside sub-1.0x book-to-bill — the working-capital reversal converting from a theoretical risk to a cash drag.

**Position management for existing holders:** hold the position; do not add at ~$72. The stock traded to $77.92 on July 24 and to $68.92 on July 30 within four sessions, so patience costs little. Post-print implied volatility has compressed 3.3pp, which makes protection cheaper than it was — a January 2027 collar or put spread is a reasonable way to carry the position through the two order prints that decide the thesis.

## Conclusion

TechnipFMC has done the hard operational work. Gross margin up 831bp in three years, Subsea operating margin up 240bp year over year in Q2, debt down 62% since FY2022 to a net cash position, three ratings upgrades in nine months, no acquisitions to unpick, no restatements, and a customer-prefunded working-capital structure that lets a $3.3B equity base support a $28B market capitalization without financial strain. The Q2 margin expansion delivered *through* a net-unfavorable contract-estimate revision is the strongest single piece of evidence that the improvement is operational rather than accounting-driven, and it is the reason I am not more negative despite a doubled share price.

What stops this from being a BUY is that the two things the market is paying for — margin and growth — have diverged. Margin and revenue through 2027 are contracted and largely safe. Growth beyond that requires order intake, and order intake fell 17.6% in H1 to a 0.93x book-to-bill with backlog flat, while the company reiterated a target requiring a 27% step-up in H2 run-rate. At ~23.7x FY2026 and ~20.3x FY2027 consensus EPS, ~12.8x guided EV/EBITDA, and ~4.4–5.0% normalized free cash flow yield with capex running $85M below depreciation for the half, the price does not compensate for that unresolved question. The insider record — a six-person C-suite selling cluster in March, a ~$117M single-executive VCP tranche, a combined Chair/CEO, and no open-market purchases in the last 25 transactions — argues for patience rather than urgency.

**HOLD.** Base case $76 (+6%), bull $92 (+28%), bear $52 (−27%). Own it for the backlog conversion and the buyback; do not add until the Q3 order print resolves the only question that matters.
