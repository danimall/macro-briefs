## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is the dominant player in the GLP-1 cardiometabolic market with an estimated 60%+ U.S. share, generating $19.8B in Q1 2026 revenue (+56% YoY) and beating EPS estimates by 27%. Mounjaro surpassed Keytruda in quarterly sales as the world's best-selling drug at $8.66B (+125% YoY), while Zepbound reached $4.16B (+80%). Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $82-85B in revenue and $35.50-$37.00 in adjusted EPS.

The investment case rests on three quantifiable growth vectors: (1) Foundayo, Lilly's oral GLP-1 approved April 1, 2026, with 20,000+ patients in its first weeks and 80% new to the GLP-1 class, (2) Medicare obesity coverage commencing July 2026 at ~$50/month, expanding the eligible population by millions, and (3) international revenue accelerating at +81% YoY with only 30M of an estimated 150M+ eligible patients currently on therapy globally. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $44.44 implies a forward P/E of ~24x on next-year earnings, reasonable for a company growing revenue 28-45% annually with 83% gross margins.

The primary concerns are product concentration risk (tirzepatide = 56% of FY2025 revenue, rising to approximately 65% in Q1 2026), $42.5B in debt funding manufacturing buildout and general corporate purposes, government pricing concessions compressing net realized prices, and the FDA's post-marketing safety monitoring request for Foundayo. These risks are well-identified and partially offset by the visible demand trajectory and pipeline depth (retatrutide, eloralintide in Phase 3).

## Company Overview

Eli Lilly is a $946B market cap pharmaceutical company with two flagship GLP-1 products — Mounjaro (tirzepatide for type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (tirzepatide for obesity) — that together generated $36.5B in 2025 revenue and are growing at 100%+ rates. The company also maintains meaningful positions in oncology (Verzenio, $5.72B), immunology (Taltz), and neuroscience (Kisunla/donanemab for Alzheimer's).

Lilly's strategic position is differentiated by: dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism (superior efficacy to Novo Nordisk's single-agonist semaglutide), a pipeline that includes Foundayo (approved April 2026), the second oral GLP-1 entrant in the obesity market following Novo's oral semaglutide, and a triple-agonist (retatrutide, Phase 3), and manufacturing capacity investments of $7.8B annually to address chronic supply constraints.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** FY2025 revenue of $65.2B represented 45% YoY growth, accelerating to 56% in Q1 2026 ($19.8B). Management guidance of $82-85B for FY2026 implies 26-30% growth at the midpoint, conservative relative to Q1 run-rate ($79.2B annualized before Foundayo ramp and Medicare launch).

**Margins:** Gross margins expanded to 83.0% in 2025 despite -10% net pricing headwinds, driven by favorable product mix (higher-margin Mounjaro/Zepbound growing faster than legacy products). Operating margins appear depressed on a GAAP basis due to $2.9B in acquired IPR&D charges; on an adjusted basis, operating income grew faster than revenue.

**Cash Generation:** Operating cash flow reached $16.8B in FY2025 (up 91% from $8.8B in 2024). Free cash flow of $9.0B reflects heavy capex ($7.8B) for manufacturing expansion. Lilly has indicated that manufacturing investments "will result in meaningfully higher capital expenditures in the near term" (Q1 2026 capex of $2.3B was up from $1.5B in Q1 2025), though FCF should benefit as revenue scales against a more mature manufacturing base.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $42.5B with D/E of approximately 1.60x (based on $26.5B total equity at year-end 2025, improving to ~1.39x at Q1 2026 with $31.2B equity) is manageable for a company of Lilly's cash-generation profile. Lilly is investing aggressively to fund capacity for products growing 50-125% annually. With $16.8B operating cash flow and $10.1B in unused credit facilities, debt service coverage is comfortable.

**Capital Return:** $6.92/share annualized dividend (0.7% yield) with $10.9B remaining buyback authorization. The dividend has been raised consistently; the low yield reflects price appreciation outpacing dividend growth.

## Growth Analysis

Lilly's growth outlook is exceptional and quantifiable across multiple vectors:

**1. Volume-Driven Domestic Growth:** U.S. GLP-1 patients growing from ~20M (end-2025) to ~30M (end-2026). Lilly holds dominant share. Medicare coverage (July 2026) adds millions of eligible patients. LillyDirect and Amazon distribution partnerships reduce access friction.

**2. Foundayo (Oral GLP-1) TAM Expansion:** 80% of initial patients are new to GLP-1 therapy, confirming that the oral formulation reaches patients unwilling to inject. Global GLP-1 market projected at $122.3B by 2030 vs. $45.3B in 2024. The oral format potentially doubles the addressable patient population.

**3. International Acceleration:** +81% international revenue growth in Q1 2026 with +95% volume. Many countries are 2-4 years behind U.S. adoption curves. China and India represent largely untapped markets.

**4. Pipeline Optionality:** Retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3 for obesity/diabetes/MASH) could be a next-generation upgrade. Orforglipron expansion into 8+ additional indications. These are not reflected in consensus estimates.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates rose +5.2% over 90 days and +4.5% over 30 days, with FY2027 at +6.4% over 90 days. These revisions don't meet the +30% threshold for the strongest inflection signal, but they are consistently positive and follow a 27% Q1 earnings beat, suggesting further upward revisions are likely as Foundayo and Medicare contributions become visible.

## Valuation Assessment

At $1,060, LLY trades at:
- 37.0x trailing EPS ($22.95)
- 29.3x FY2026 consensus EPS ($36.19)
- 23.9x FY2027 consensus EPS ($44.44)
- Forward PEG of ~1.05x on FY2026 growth (28% guided) and ~0.55x on FY2027

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E is 19.8x, but no peer is growing close to Lilly's rate. JNJ (26.8x, ~5% growth), MRK (32.4x, declining Keytruda growth), PFE (19.8x, negative growth), BMY (16.7x, flat). The premium is justified by a 28-45% growth rate vs. peer growth of 0-5%.

**Why the valuation score is 5.5, not higher:** At 37x trailing and 29x current-year, the stock prices in substantial execution of the growth plan. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $36.19 at current guidance midpoint ($36.25) leaves minimal room for upside surprise at the full-year level — the Q1 beat has largely been absorbed into estimates. Additionally, the stock has rallied approximately 70% from its August 2025 lows ($624), constraining near-term appreciation potential relative to prior report dates.

**Why BUY despite the 5.5 valuation score:** At 24x FY2027 EPS with visible 20%+ growth beyond that (Foundayo ramp, international penetration, pipeline catalysts), the PEG ratio on a two-year basis is compelling. The base case math: 25-28x $44 FY2027 EPS = $1,100-$1,232, representing 4-16% upside with a multi-year growth runway beyond. Barclays at $1,400 illustrates the bull case if pipeline catalysts materialize. The risk/reward skews positively because the growth drivers are identifiable, scheduled (Medicare July 2026, Foundayo ramp through 2026-2027), and partially de-risked by Q1 results.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market Share Gains:** Zepbound accounts for over 60% of U.S. weight-loss prescriptions and almost 70% of new prescriptions, while Mounjaro has approximately half of type 2 diabetes prescriptions. Novo Nordisk reported Q4 2025 revenue -7.6% YoY, cut 9,000 jobs, and warned of 2026 pricing headwinds. The competitive gap is widening.

**Product Superiority:** Tirzepatide's dual-agonist mechanism delivers superior weight loss relative to semaglutide, translating directly to physician preference and patient outcomes.

**Pipeline Depth:** Retatrutide (triple agonist) in Phase 3 shows 24%+ weight loss in early data. No competitor has a credible triple-agonist in late-stage development.

**Oral Market Position:** Foundayo is the only approved GLP-1 pill for weight loss that can be taken any time of day without food/water restrictions. The 80% new-to-class rate suggests the oral market is largely additive rather than cannibalistic.

**Manufacturing Scale:** $7.8B annual capex creates a supply moat that smaller competitors (Viking Therapeutics, Structure Therapeutics) cannot replicate for years.

## Risk Assessment

**Product Concentration (High Impact, Medium Probability):** Any safety signal, supply disruption, or competitive displacement concentrated in tirzepatide represents an existential risk. The FDA's post-marketing safety data request for Foundayo (liver/cardiac monitoring) warrants close attention, though this appears routine.

**Pricing/Access Compression (Medium Impact, High Probability):** The volume-for-price trade is explicit in management's strategy. U.S. net pricing declined 10% in 2025. If volume growth decelerates before pricing stabilizes, margin compression accelerates. The 10-K reports U.S. rebate/discount reductions of $62.1B in 2025 — rebate liability volatility can swing earnings meaningfully.

**CVS Caremark Formulary Decisions:** Zepbound's removal from certain CVS plans demonstrates PBM negotiating leverage. Quarterly formulary reviews create ongoing uncertainty.

**Manufacturing Execution:** $7.8B capex + $10B contingent purchase obligations represent massive fixed costs. If demand disappoints, these become balance sheet liabilities rather than growth investments.

**Leverage Risk in Rising Rate Environment:** $42.5B debt at 30Y Treasury yields >5% makes future refinancing expensive. Current weighted average cost of debt is manageable, but maturities rolling in 2028-2030 will reprice higher.

### Options Market Signal

Market-implied volatility and publicly available options flow data suggest elevated near-term uncertainty, likely residual from earnings volatility and the FDA Foundayo monitoring request. Available data indicates call-heavy positioning, consistent with the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,300-1,400; +23-32%):** Medicare obesity coverage drives 5M+ new U.S. patients by year-end 2026. Foundayo reaches $3-5B annualized run-rate by late 2026. International growth sustains 50%+ through 2027. Retatrutide Phase 3 data exceeds expectations. Consensus FY2027 EPS reaches $50+ by mid-2027. Stock re-rates to 28x FY2027 = $1,250-1,400.

**Bear Case ($850-900; -15 to -20%):** Tirzepatide safety signal emerges (cardiac/liver). Medicare uptake disappoints on access barriers or patient reluctance. Novo's CagriSema narrows efficacy gap. PBM formulary exclusions broaden. FY2026 EPS comes in at guidance low ($35.50). Stock compresses to 24x = $850.

**Base Case ($1,150-1,250; +10-18%):** Execution continues on the existing trajectory. Medicare adds 2-3M patients in H2 2026. Foundayo contributes $1-2B. FY2026 EPS reaches $37+, FY2027 consensus holds at $44-46. Stock trades at 26-27x FY2027 = $1,155-$1,242.

## Conclusion

Eli Lilly merits a BUY recommendation at $1,060. The specific non-consensus catalyst is that consensus FY2027 EPS of $44.44 does not yet reflect the combined impact of Foundayo ramp (80% of patients are new to class), Medicare coverage (July 2026 launch for millions of eligible patients), and continued international acceleration (+81% in Q1). These three growth vectors are scheduled, partially de-risked by Q1 execution, and likely to drive further estimate revisions through H2 2026.

The 5.5 valuation score reflects that LLY is not cheap in absolute terms — 37x trailing, 29x current year — and the stock has appreciated meaningfully from its August 2025 trough. However, 24x FY2027 EPS with PEG of ~0.55x on visible 40%+ earnings growth through 2027, backed by a dominant market share position and broadening pipeline, represents a favorable setup for long-term investors. The bull-to-bear risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.9:1 supports the recommendation, though base-case upside of +13% versus bear-case downside of -17% underscores the importance of position sizing discipline.

The primary risk justifying sizing discipline rather than maximum conviction is product concentration — 56% of FY2025 revenue from a single molecule, increasing to ~65% in the most recent quarter. Position at 2-3% with explicit stop criteria tied to safety signals or demand deceleration.
