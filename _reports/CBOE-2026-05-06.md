## Executive Summary

Cboe Global Markets reported record Q1 2026 results with net revenue of $728.9M (+29% YoY) and adjusted EPS of $3.70 (+48% YoY), beating consensus estimates. The company benefits from secular growth in options volumes (U.S. market ADV +25% in 2025) and a volatility regime shift driven by geopolitical uncertainty, Fed governance transitions, and persistent inflation. CEO Craig Donohue's strategic realignment — a 20% workforce reduction ($40-50M annualized savings from the May 2026 strategic actions), divestiture of non-core businesses (Australia, Canada for ~$300M), and tightened focus on proprietary derivatives — positions Cboe to compound earnings at 15-20% annually over the next 2-3 years.

The stock has appreciated 48% over 12 months and trades near its all-time high at $344. At 25x FY2026E EPS of $13.70, the valuation is reasonable for an exchange business generating 68% adjusted EBITDA margins with a structural monopoly on SPX/VIX products, though it trades at a discount to peers like Nasdaq (27.5x). Consensus estimates likely still understate the earnings trajectory given the vol regime and restructuring savings. Based on normalized earnings power approaching $15 in FY2027 and structural tailwinds from heightened macro uncertainty, we maintain a BUY.

## Company Overview

Cboe operates the world's largest options exchange network, along with significant equities, futures, FX, and data businesses across North America, Europe, and Asia Pacific. Revenue breaks down as: Derivatives Markets (55% of net revenue), Data Vantage (26%), and Cash & Spot Markets (19%). The company's competitive moat centers on its exclusive license to trade S&P 500 Index options (through December 2032) and its proprietary VIX methodology. The Options and Futures segments together generate approximately 68% of net revenue, with the Options segment operating at 74.3% EBITDA margins. Cboe does not disclose product-level profitability for SPX/VIX specifically, but these proprietary products are the dominant revenue drivers within those segments.

Under CEO Craig Donohue (former CME Group CEO), Cboe has aggressively rationalized its portfolio: exiting Japan, winding down CEDX (European derivatives), selling Australia and Canada operations, shuttering the digital spot exchange, and cutting 20% of the workforce. The strategic refocusing targets: proprietary derivatives expansion, event contracts/prediction markets, tokenization, and clearing growth in the U.S. and Europe.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Dynamics:**

Cboe's financial profile has improved dramatically. Net revenue grew from $1,918M (2023) to $2,429M (2025), a 12.5% CAGR. Operating margins expanded from 53.0% to 60.4% of net revenue in FY2025, and adjusted EBITDA margins reached 67.7%. The Q1 2026 run-rate implies further acceleration: $728.9M quarterly net revenue annualizes to $2.9B+, though some seasonality applies.

Diluted EPS surged from $7.21 (2024) to $10.42 (2025), a 45% increase driven by volume growth, operating leverage, and modest buybacks. Adjusted diluted EPS was $10.67. The Q1 2026 print of $3.70 annualizes to $14.80, though Q1 benefited from elevated volatility.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**

Free cash flow reached approximately $1.68B in FY2025 (~36% FCF margin on total revenue of $4.7B). FY2025 operating cash flow of $1.75B was boosted by approximately $529M in changes in clearing-related margin deposits, clearing funds, and customer bank deposits — items that may not recur at the same magnitude. Excluding these clearing-deposit flows, underlying FCF generation remains strong but somewhat lower than the headline figure. Capex requirements remain minimal ($71M).

As of year-end 2025, the balance sheet carried $1.45B of fixed-rate debt against $2.22B in cash, yielding net cash of approximately $770M. As of March 31, 2026, net cash was approximately $691M. Investment-grade ratings (A-/A2) and approximately $2.5B of total available liquidity (net of ~$191M regulatory capital requirements) provide substantial financial flexibility.

Capital allocation has been conservative: $284M in dividends (17% of FCF), only $67M in buybacks, with approximately $569M remaining on the repurchase authorization as of Q1 2026. The combination of surplus cash, asset sale proceeds (~$300M from Canada/Australia), and restructuring savings creates significant incremental deployment capacity over the next 12-18 months.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Growth Drivers:**

U.S. options market ADV grew 25% in 2025. Cboe's proprietary index options ADV grew 21% and multi-listed options ADV grew 24%. European equities matched notional value grew 31%. These growth rates reflect both structural and volatility-sensitive factors:

1. **0-DTE (zero-days-to-expiration) options proliferation:** Now approximately 50% of SPX options volume. This product barely existed before 2022 and creates recurring daily flow.
2. **Institutional hedging demand:** Pension funds, asset managers, and corporations increasingly using options for risk management as macro uncertainty persists.
3. **Retail options participation:** Continued growth from self-directed platforms.
4. **Potential vol regime shift:** If incoming Fed Chair Warsh reduces or eliminates forward guidance, this could create a structurally higher uncertainty environment that benefits options demand. However, this outcome remains speculative and is not assured.

**Estimate Revisions:**

Sell-side EPS estimates have risen meaningfully over recent months, reflecting Q1 2026's strong beat and raised guidance. This revision velocity indicates consensus may still be catching up to fundamentals, though specific revision magnitudes should be verified against current consensus databases.

**Restructuring Upside:**

The May 2026 strategic realignment actions are expected to generate $40-50M of annualized pre-tax cost savings, with $20-25M expected in 2026 and the full run-rate achieved by 2027. At Cboe's adjusted tax rate and current share count, this translates to approximately $0.28-0.35 of additional EPS once fully realized. Combined with 10-15% organic revenue growth, normalized FY2027 EPS should approach $15.00, supporting a $370-420 price range at 25-28x forward multiples.

## Competitive Landscape

**Proprietary product moat (widening):** The exclusive SPX license (through 2032) and proprietary VIX methodology create an unassailable position in index options. SPX options RPC of $0.924 vs. multi-listed at $0.066 — a 14x differential. No competitor can replicate this without access to the underlying indices.

**0-DTE market leadership:** Cboe pioneered 0-DTE SPX options and now dominates this rapidly growing segment. This product category has minimal substitution risk because it requires the SPX reference price and Cboe's clearing infrastructure.

**Data Vantage monetization (10% organic growth):** Proprietary data from exclusive products creates a high-margin recurring revenue stream that compounds independent of transaction volumes.

**Emerging threats (managed):** New options exchanges (2 expected in H1 2026) primarily compete in multi-listed products where Cboe holds only 24% share and margins are lower. Prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket) represent an emerging competitive dynamic — CEO Donohue has positioned 0-DTE as Cboe's "headstart on prediction contracts," though whether these platforms ultimately serve as complements or substitutes remains an open question.

**Market share dynamics:** Total U.S. options market share slipped slightly (30.8% to 30.3%), with multi-listed options share declining from 24.5% to 24.2%. U.S. equities share fell from 11.4% to 10.0%. These losses are in low-margin commodity segments where Cboe is rationalizing rather than defending. The strategy is correct: compete on proprietary products, not on multi-listed pricing wars.

## Risk Assessment

**SPX License Renewal (Medium-Long Term):** The exclusive S&P 500 options license extends through December 2032 (with the broader license through December 2033). Non-renewal would be catastrophic — roughly equivalent to losing 40-50% of net revenue. However, the renewal is 6+ years away, both parties benefit from the arrangement, and Cboe has successfully renewed historically. Current risk: low probability, high severity.

**Customer Concentration:** Top 10 customers generate 57% of revenue; 3 OCC clearing members handle 71% of options transaction fees. This concentration creates tail risk if a major market maker exits or fails, though clearing obligations partially mitigate this.

**Vol Normalization Risk:** If geopolitical tensions resolve comprehensively and volatility falls to 2017-2019 norms (VIX sub-15 persistently), options volumes could decelerate meaningfully. Current options ADV growth of 20-25% reflects both structural adoption trends and elevated uncertainty. A sustained low-vol regime could reduce growth to 5-10%.

**Execution Risk:** The simultaneous CEO transition, 20% workforce reduction, multiple business exits, and strategic repositioning create organizational strain. Knowledge loss from ~330 departing employees is a real operational risk over the next 12 months.

**Regulatory:** SEC market structure changes (Tick Size/Access Fee Cap, implementation November 2026) could compress equities exchange transaction fees. CAT funding model vacated by court — Cboe may face write-downs on promissory notes. EMIR 3.0 in Europe introduces clearing obligations uncertainty.

**Cboe Clear Europe Credit Facility:** The €1.2B revolving credit facility supporting European clearing terminates June 26, 2026 — imminent renewal risk, though refinancing at investment-grade terms should be straightforward.

## Conclusion

Cboe Global Markets represents a high-quality exchange franchise with a structural monopoly on the world's most valuable options products, operating at 68% adjusted EBITDA margins with accelerating earnings growth. The new CEO is executing a focused strategy that should deliver $40-50M of annualized pre-tax cost savings while redirecting capital toward the highest-growth, highest-margin opportunities.

The stock has moved considerably from $213 (May 2025 low) to $344 today, compressing the near-term risk/reward. Strong estimate revisions confirm fundamental acceleration rather than multiple expansion driving the appreciation. At 25x FY2026E with estimates still rising and tailwinds from an elevated vol environment, the position remains attractive.

The primary risk is valuation sensitivity to a vol normalization scenario — if geopolitical tensions resolve comprehensively and the VIX returns to sub-15, the premium multiple and elevated volumes would both compress simultaneously. Even under that scenario, Cboe's proprietary product moat, restructuring savings, and secular options adoption trends provide a floor at $280-300.

Maintain BUY with the acknowledgment that position sizing should reflect the narrower risk/reward (base case +9%) compared to prior entries at lower prices. The thesis remains structurally intact and may be reinforced by Fed governance transitions in the coming months.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Restructuring savings are $40-50M annualized (not $100-120M), reducing FY2027 EPS uplift from ~$0.87 to ~$0.30; the Fed forward-guidance elimination thesis is speculative rather than factual; these corrections narrow the upside case and may affect the base-case target of $375.
