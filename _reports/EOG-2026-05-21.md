## Executive Summary

EOG Resources is the largest independent U.S. E&P company, generating approximately $8.5B in free cash flow on $6.5B flat capex in 2026, with per-share earnings estimates that have risen approximately 71% over 90 days (from $9.65 to $16.47 for FY2026). The primary driver is sustained $100+/bbl oil prices resulting from the Strait of Hormuz disruption, against which EOG has minimal hedging — creating maximum leverage to the upside scenario.

At ~8.5x forward earnings with a 2.9% dividend yield, 7% share count reduction over three years, and declining per-unit costs ($20.72/BOE, down from $21.12), EOG offers asymmetric exposure to the current oil regime. The Encino acquisition ($5.7B, closed August 2025) adds a third top-tier gas/NGL play (Utica) ramping from 55 to 85 wells in 2026, while the 10-year Brent-linked gas contract commencing 2027 creates a differentiated gas monetization pathway peers lack.

The bear case centers on oil price reversion: if Hormuz normalizes within 6 months, FY2027 estimates ($14.43) reflect 12% earnings decline, and the stock would trade at ~10x normalized earnings — still reasonable but with limited upside. The probability-weighted case favors the long given structural supply constraints (IEA confirming physical depletion), zero US drilling response at $125/bbl, and Russia's aligned financial incentive to prolong the disruption.

## Company Overview

EOG Resources explores, develops, and produces crude oil, NGLs, and natural gas across four core U.S. basins: the Delaware Basin (Permian), South Texas (Eagle Ford + Dorado), Appalachian Basin (Utica, via Encino acquisition), and Rocky Mountain. The company holds 5,514 MMBoe in proved reserves (99% domestic), operates with approximately 3,400 employees (extremely lean for a $75B enterprise), and differentiates through self-sourced sand, proprietary drilling motors, extended laterals (up to 3 miles), and a decentralized operating model.

Total production reached 450 MMBoe in 2025, up 25% over two years, with Q1 2026 running at 1.4M Boed. The company maintains capital discipline: organic capex has remained at $6.3-6.7B despite oil rising from $66/bbl (2025 average) to $100+/bbl (current), with incremental cash flow returned to shareholders rather than plowed into accelerated drilling.

Shares outstanding declined from 583M (2022) to 543M (2025), a 7% reduction that amplifies per-share metrics.

## Competitive Landscape

EOG's competitive advantages are structural and difficult to replicate:

1. **Cost leadership:** Self-sourced sand, proprietary drilling motors, and extended laterals yield per-unit costs below peer averages. Total costs declined to $20.72/BOE despite sector-wide inflation.

2. **Basin diversification post-Encino:** Three top-tier plays (Delaware Basin, Eagle Ford, Utica) provide flexibility to allocate capital toward whichever basin offers the highest returns in any given price environment.

3. **Organizational efficiency:** 3,400 employees generating $22B+ revenue = ~$6.6M revenue per employee. The decentralized model enables faster decision-making at the asset level.

4. **Capital allocation track record:** 7% share count reduction over 3 years + dividend growth + return commitment > 70% of free cash flow. Management has demonstrated they won't chase growth at the expense of returns.

5. **Gas optionality without gas dependency:** The Brent-linked contract (2027) and Dorado's $1.40/Mcf breakeven create asymmetric gas exposure — profitable at any price, but outsized returns if gas strengthens.

Competitive position is improving: the Encino acquisition added a third core play, per-unit costs are declining, and EOG's discipline is increasingly differentiated as peers face pressure to grow into higher prices.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity:** Each $1/bbl change equals ~$174M in net income and ~$223M in OCF. A reversion to $70/bbl oil would reduce FY2026 earnings from $16.47 to approximately $10-11/share, compressing the stock toward $110-120. This is the dominant risk.

**Minimal Hedging Exposure:** EOG is largely unhedged for 2026 crude production — this creates maximum leverage to the upside but equally exposes the downside. Limited natural gas hedges (460-540 MMBtud at ~$3.78/MMBtu) exist but represent a fraction of total gas output.

**Integration Risk:** The Encino acquisition increased debt by $3.7B and added 675,000 Utica acres that require development capital. If gas prices weaken and NGL prices follow, the Utica economics may underperform acquisition assumptions. Mitigant: at current gas strip ($3.84/MMBtu) the play is comfortably profitable, and EOG has flexibility to slow development.

**Tariff/Inflation Pass-Through:** Management explicitly flagged tariff risk on tubulars, steel, and other oilfield supplies. Per-unit costs declining currently, but sustained inflation above management's offset capabilities would compress margins.

**Impairment Pattern:** $843M in 2025 impairments (Barnett/Woodford) signals portfolio high-grading. Future impairments on marginal acreage are possible if prices revert, though they are non-cash events.
