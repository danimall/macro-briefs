**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Morningstar has fallen roughly 50% from its June 2025 high of $316.71 to $156, a decline that has dragged the stock to ~13x forward EPS even as operating fundamentals have accelerated. Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.8% to $644.8M, operating income grew 36.6%, adjusted operating margin hit a record 27.7%, and adjusted EPS rose 42.6% to $3.18. The company beat consensus in all four trailing quarters, the last two by 15-17%. The stock has derated alongside the broader data/software complex on fears that free AI tools will scrape and disintermediate paid-data businesses, while Morningstar's actual results show margin expansion and broad-based revenue growth.

The central catalyst is the CRSP acquisition, closed February 2026 for ~$365M. CRSP indexes underpin roughly $2.9 trillion in assets, including Vanguard's Total Stock Market and Mid-Cap index funds, and the deal lifted Morningstar's index-linked assets from $208.7B to $3,170.1B year-over-year. This converts a small index business into a scaled, recurring, asset-linked franchise. The CME Group licensing deal (June 2026) to launch derivatives on Morningstar equity benchmarks demonstrates early monetization of the expanded index franchise. While passive index licensing carries low basis points (so $2.9T in linked assets does not translate proportionally to revenue), the strategic stickiness and optionality are material and likely under-appreciated at the price paid.

We rate MORN a BUY with disciplined sizing. The bull case rests on a re-rating toward peer multiples as consensus estimates catch up to the operating reality. The risks that keep this from being a maximum-conviction call are genuine: PitchBook growth has decelerated from +8.6% in 2025 to +5.3% in Q1'26, the long-term net impact of AI on the data-licensing model is unresolved, and post-CRSP debt rose to $1.71B with floating-rate exposure in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## Company Overview

Morningstar is a global provider of independent investment data, research, ratings, indexes, and investment-management services. Founded in 1984 by Joe Mansueto (still Executive Chairman, ~37.5% owner), it operates in 32 countries with ~10,973 employees. The business is organized into five reportable segments:

- **Morningstar Direct Platform** ($830.6M revenue in 2025, ~44.5% adjusted operating margin) — the core data/research engine (Morningstar Data, Direct, Advisor Workstation), competing with Bloomberg, FactSet, LSEG, and S&P Global.
- **PitchBook** ($671.8M, ~31% margin) — private-markets data, competing with Preqin (BlackRock), S&P, MSCI, and FactSet.
- **Morningstar Credit / DBRS** ($354.4M, 32.4% margin) — the fourth-largest global credit rating agency on an issuer-pay model, behind Moody's, S&P, and Fitch.
- **Morningstar Wealth** ($251.4M) — model portfolios and the International Wealth Platform; recently sold its US TAMP to AssetMark and is sunsetting Morningstar Office.
- **Morningstar Retirement** ($137.6M, ~49% margin) — managed retirement accounts, the highest-margin segment.

Revenue mix is 70% license/subscription, 14% asset-based, and 16% transaction-based. Renewal rates are strong (Morningstar Data 101%, Direct 104%, PitchBook 103% in 2025), and the largest customer accounts for less than 3% of revenue, eliminating concentration risk. The moat rests on brand and independence, proprietary IP (the Morningstar Rating/Medalist, Style Box, DBRS ratings, PitchBook private data), and breadth of coverage.

## Financial Analysis

The fundamentals are strong and accelerating. A note on the screening data: an Altman Z-Score of 1.46 ("distress zone") and a weak Piotroski reading are mechanical artifacts, not real signals. The Z-Score is distorted because ~$1.6B of goodwill and intangibles inflate the asset base while a temporary revolver draw ($1.07B short-term debt at 12/31/25) used to bridge the CRSP acquisition penalizes the working-capital ratio. Actual leverage is ~1.3x net debt/EBITDA, FCF was $442.6M in 2025, ROE was 30.6%, and covenants are comfortably met.

The structural story is margin expansion. After a 2022-2023 trough caused by heavy investment and soft markets, operating margin doubled to 21.5% (23.8% adjusted), then expanded to 27.7% adjusted in Q1'26. The drivers are Credit operating leverage on a robust issuance market, PitchBook efficiency gains, and Morningstar Wealth swinging to profit (a +$18.9M improvement). Cash generation is strong at ~18% FCF margin; the Q1'26 FCF dip to $53.6M is timing (capex and office refreshes), not deterioration.

Capital allocation has been aggressive and shareholder-friendly: $787M of repurchases in 2025 (reducing diluted shares ~10%, from 43.1M to 39.3M), $300M more in Q1'26, a new $1.0B authorization (October 2025), and a $0.50/share quarterly dividend. The buybacks executed into a falling share price retired ~10% of the float at depressed levels.

The one balance-sheet caution: debt rose from $1.07B (12/31/25) to $1.71B (3/31/26) post-CRSP, with floating-rate exposure. Net interest expense roughly doubled to $13.7M in Q1'26 from $5.4M. At 1.3x leverage this is comfortably serviceable, but in a higher-for-longer rate environment the floating-rate component is a headwind worth monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew +7.5% reported / +8.0% organic in 2025, accelerating to +10.8% reported / +7.6% organic in Q1'26. The growth is broad-based:

- **Credit / DBRS**: +21.7% in 2025, +38.4% in Q1'26, driven by a robust debt-issuance market. This is the cyclical swing factor — strong now, but tied to interest-rate-sensitive issuance volumes.
- **PitchBook**: +8.6% in 2025, decelerating to +5.3% in Q1'26 on persistent corporate-segment churn and now slowing venture-capital expansion. This is the genuine yellow flag and the key metric to monitor — PitchBook was supposed to be the durable double-digit grower.
- **Direct Platform**: +5.4% in 2025, +8.0% in Q1'26.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year EPS consensus moved from $11.36 (90 days ago) to $11.92 (+4.9%), and next-year from $12.98 to $13.45 (+3.6%). These are healthy upward revisions but below the +30% threshold that would constitute a maximum-conviction growth signal. The earnings-surprise record adds confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, the most recent two by 15.5% and 17.2%. A company beating by double digits with rising estimates warrants higher confidence in the $11.92/$13.45 consensus path.

CRSP adds a new growth vector, converting the Indexes business into a scaled, recurring franchise that was immediately accretive to adjusted operating margin in Q1'26. The CME licensing deal monetizes this franchise into derivatives. The realization caveat is real — passive index licensing earns low basis points — but the recurring, sticky nature of the revenue improves the quality of the growth base.

Growth prospects score a 7.5, the highest of the six dimensions. This is the most reliable predictor in our framework, and the evidence here is solid: broad-based revenue acceleration, record margins, a transformational acquisition, and rising estimates backed by a beat streak.

## Valuation Assessment

At $156, MORN trades at ~13x the $11.92 current-year EPS consensus and ~11.6x the $13.45 next-year estimate. This is below the peer median P/E of 15.2 (FactSet 15.2, MarketAxess 13.8, CBOE 25.3, Virtu 9.2). FactSet, the closest comparable as a financial-data subscription business, trades at 15.2x while also sitting ~48% below its 52-week high — the entire data/software cohort has derated on the AI narrative, so peer multiples are themselves depressed.

The valuation case is built on three points. First, the multiple is below peers despite Morningstar delivering faster organic growth (+7.6% Q1'26 organic) and record margin expansion. Second, the AI-scraping fear that drove the derating conflicts with the operating data — operating income grew 30%+ in Q1'26, which argues the market is mispricing the fundamentals, not just the multiple. Third, the analyst price target range of $210-$280 (mean $234, median $212) sits 35-80% above the current price; these targets lag the stock down and, on a name with rising estimates and a sub-15x forward multiple, function as floors that analysts will revise as consensus catches up.

We treat the cheapness with appropriate skepticism — a low multiple alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. The independent catalyst that distinguishes this from a value trap is the combination of (a) the CRSP-driven step-change in index-linked assets and recurring revenue, (b) the four-quarter beat streak with rising estimates, and (c) the sentiment-fundamentals gap that should narrow as quarterly results continue to contradict the AI-disruption thesis. On normalized earnings power — adjusted operating income grew 31.9% in Q1'26 — the trailing multiple understates the business's current run-rate profitability.

Valuation scores 7.0. The asymmetry is favorable, but we are mindful that the AI question is genuinely unresolved and could be the reason sophisticated sellers have taken the stock down 50%, which caps conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

Morningstar sits in the high-quality financial data and analytics oligopoly (Bloomberg, S&P Global, MSCI, FactSet, LSEG, Moody's). It is sub-scale versus these giants but holds defensible niches: #1 in retail/advisor fund data and ratings, a leading private-markets franchise in PitchBook, a top-4 credit rating agency in DBRS, and — post-CRSP — a credible index player. The global ratings market alone is ~$11.8B (TTM Sept 2025).

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Renewal rates remain strong (101-104% across the core products), and CRSP meaningfully improves Morningstar's position in indexes. But two structural pressures are real: vendor consolidation and in-housing (broker-dealers and custodians building their own tools, such as Pershing's Wove, threaten Advisor Workstation), and the AI question. The AI threat is dual-edged. Free AI tools that scrape data — including Morningstar's own content — could erode the paid-data model. Morningstar's response is to become "AI-native," integrating its MCP server into Anthropic Claude, Microsoft Copilot/Foundry, and OpenAI ChatGPT, with PitchBook integrations into Anthropic, OpenAI, Hebbia, and Rogo. The critical attribution point: in these integrations, third-party LLMs consume Morningstar/PitchBook proprietary data, and Morningstar is the data owner and provider, which turns the LLMs into a new distribution channel rather than purely a threat, provided the company can enforce licensing on its IP.

Competitive position scores 6.5. The trajectory is stable-to-improving on the index franchise and renewal strength, but the AI disruption question and in-housing pressure prevent a higher score. Per our framework, this dimension's recent predictive signal runs at roughly half its all-time strength, so we weight it as a secondary confirmation rather than a primary driver.

## Risk Assessment

The risks specific to Morningstar's situation:

1. **AI disruption** — the dominant near-term thesis driver. The 10-K explicitly added language noting that AI-driven sentiment has caused sector-wide stock declines "despite strong operating performance." This is the unresolved binary: if AI scraping erodes the data-licensing model faster than Morningstar can monetize its IP through licensed AI channels, the derating is justified. If Morningstar successfully turns LLMs into a distribution channel, the current price is a gift. This is not yet resolvable from the data.

2. **PitchBook deceleration** — corporate-segment churn and slowing VC expansion. The genuine yellow flag, given PitchBook's role as the durable growth engine.

3. **Cyclicality** — Credit revenue depends on debt-issuance volumes, which are interest-rate sensitive. A slowdown in issuance would reverse a significant share of recent growth. Asset-based revenue tracks markets with a one-quarter lag.

4. **CRSP integration** — newly elevated to the first risk factor in the 10-K. The price looks attractive, but integration and revenue realization (low-basis-point passive licensing) are unproven at scale.

5. **Floating-rate leverage** — the post-CRSP debt load and doubled net interest expense are a headwind in a higher-for-longer environment.

6. **Founder control / governance** — Mansueto's ~37.5% stake gives him effective control over major corporate actions, a concentration risk despite his alignment with shareholders.

Risk factors score 5.5. The AI question and PitchBook deceleration are specific, material, and not yet priced with clear asymmetric recovery upside. We deliberately avoid over-weighting this dimension — in the current regime it has been a weak-to-anti-predictive signal — but the AI binary is genuine enough to keep the score below the midpoint of the favorable range.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 41.8% is rich relative to 35.8% historical volatility (6.0pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (1m IV 41.8% > 6m IV 37.4%), indicating the market expects a near-term catalyst. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.57 to 3.64 since the prior snapshot, a sharp shift toward put positioning that coincided with the 10% price decline. The 6m skew of -3.9% shows modest downside hedging demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.6%. For a BUY thesis, this elevated put positioning is a divergence worth flagging: the options market is not confirming the bullish fundamental view and is pricing meaningful near-term downside risk, likely around the next earnings print. The rich near-term IV makes this a less-than-ideal moment for selling premium but does not invalidate a fundamental accumulation thesis; it argues for patient, scaled entry rather than a single position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($230-280):** The AI-fear derating proves wrong. Quarterly results continue to contradict the disruption thesis — Credit and Direct sustain high-single/double-digit growth, PitchBook stabilizes, and CRSP revenue realization plus the CME index deal demonstrate the index franchise scales. Margins hold at record levels. As consensus estimates rise toward $13.45+ and the multiple re-rates back toward (or above) the 15.2x peer median, the stock returns to $230-280, consistent with the analyst target range. Morningstar successfully positions itself as the data owner monetizing AI distribution channels.

**Bear case ($110-130):** AI scraping erodes the data-licensing model in a way that shows up in renewal rates and net revenue retention over the next 12-18 months. PitchBook deceleration continues, confirming the durable growth engine is impaired. Credit revenue reverses as debt-issuance volumes slow with the rate cycle. The multiple compresses further toward FactSet's range on flat-to-declining earnings, and the stock falls 15-30% from current levels. The 50% drawdown from the high would, in this scenario, have been the market correctly anticipating fundamental deterioration not yet visible in trailing data.

The drawdown magnitude (>30% from the high) requires explaining what the market is missing. Our thesis is that the sellers are extrapolating a sector-wide AI fear onto a business whose Q1'26 results directly contradict the disruption narrative, and whose CRSP-driven index franchise is a confirmed positive that the price does not reflect. The falsifiable counter-thesis is that the AI threat will manifest in deteriorating renewal rates within 12-18 months, which is precisely the metric we would monitor to invalidate the bull case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $200.** This reflects ~15x the $13.45 next-year EPS consensus — re-rating to the peer median multiple on the forward estimate. This sits between the bear ($110-130) and bull ($230-280) scenarios and represents ~28% upside from $156.

**Bull case target: $230-280** (~47-79% upside) — re-rating above peer median as estimates rise and the AI-fear narrative reverses.

**Bear case target: $110-130** (~17-30% downside) — multiple compression on confirmed PitchBook impairment and AI-driven retention erosion.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the next two earnings prints (early August 2026 and late October 2026) as the key checkpoints. The catalysts are quarterly results that either confirm or refute the disruption thesis, continued CRSP/CME index monetization, and estimate-revision velocity.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Renewal rates fall below 100% on the core products (Morningstar Data, Direct, PitchBook), signaling AI-driven retention erosion.
2. PitchBook organic growth decelerates below +3% YoY, confirming the durable growth engine is structurally impaired.
3. Current-year EPS estimates turn negative on a 90-day basis, reversing the current +4.9% revision trend.

## Conclusion

Morningstar is a high-quality financial-data compounder that has been swept up in a sector-wide AI derating, trading at ~13x forward EPS after a ~50% drawdown despite accelerating operating fundamentals. The CRSP acquisition is an under-appreciated positive that lifted index-linked assets to $3.2T and is already margin-accretive. Management is buying back ~10% of the float at depressed prices.

The risks that prevent maximum conviction are genuine: the long-term net impact of AI on the data-licensing model is unresolved, PitchBook growth has decelerated, and post-CRSP floating-rate debt is a modest headwind. The options market is pricing near-term stress (rich IV, backwardation, elevated put OI), and a >30% drawdown means sophisticated sellers hold a real bearish view we should respect. On balance, the disconnect between depressed sentiment and accelerating fundamentals, the favorable valuation versus growth, and the rising-estimate/beat-streak combination tilt the asymmetry toward the upside.

We rate MORN a **BUY** with disciplined sizing. We would accumulate on weakness toward the low-$150s, monitor PitchBook growth and renewal rates as the primary invalidation metrics, and treat the next two earnings prints as the decisive tests of whether the AI fear or the operating reality is the correct read.
