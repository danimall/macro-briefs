**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Catalyst Pharmaceuticals is no longer trading on its fundamentals. On May 6, 2026, the company signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Angelini Pharma S.p.A., a privately-held Italian drugmaker, for $31.50 per share in cash, an all-cash transaction valued at roughly $4.1 billion. The stock closed June 10 at $31.34, pinned within pennies of the deal price, with the trading range over the past month compressed into a few cents of daily movement and volume elevated as merger-arbitrage capital takes positions. Any analysis of CPRX from this point forward is a function of deal completion probability, timing, and the spread, not the standalone biopharmaceutical business.

The price action confirms this. From the April 23 disturbance (deal rumors leaked around April 27, when the stock spiked to an intraday high of $32.56) the shares re-rated from the mid-$20s to the deal price and then stopped moving entirely. Daily ranges since mid-May have been a few cents. The options market corroborates a pinned-to-deal stock: 3-month ATM IV has collapsed to 11.1%, well below the 33.5% one-year historical volatility, and the put/call skew is sharply call-heavy (-29.7%), consistent with downside protection being cheap because the market assigns low probability to a break.

The residual return available is the gross spread of approximately $0.16, or 0.5%, against a Q3 2026 expected close, an annualized low-single-digit return before borrow and transaction costs. The asymmetric risk is a deal break, which would likely return the stock toward the undisturbed April 22 level in the mid-$20s, a decline of roughly 17-20%. This is a merger-arbitrage risk/reward: small, near-certain upside against a larger, low-probability downside. With the spread this thin and the position offering no fundamental optionality, this is a HOLD for existing holders who wish to collect the final spread and an unattractive new entry. We do not see a reason to buy here.

## Company Overview

Catalyst is a commercial-stage, rare-disease biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Coral Gables, Florida, operating as a single reportable segment. Its strategy is to in-license or acquire de-risked, commercial or near-commercial orphan-disease assets rather than pursue internal discovery, with R&D running at roughly 3% of operating expense. The company markets three products in the United States:

- **FIRDAPSE (amifampridine)** — the core asset, treating Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS), an ultra-rare neuromuscular disorder. FIRDAPSE generated approximately $358M in 2025 sales (+18% YoY); Q1 2026 sales were $98.9M (+18.1% YoY). It is roughly 66% of revenue.
- **AGAMREE (vamorolone)** — the growth driver, a novel corticosteroid for Duchenne muscular dystrophy licensed from Santhera for North America. AGAMREE produced approximately $117M in 2025 U.S. sales; Q1 2026 sales were $36.7M (+66.6% YoY) after a March 2024 launch.
- **FYCOMPA (perampanel)** — the declining asset, an epilepsy drug acquired from Eisai that lost patent exclusivity in 2025. Q1 2026 revenue collapsed to $13.8M from $35.6M in Q1 2025 (-61.3%), with marketing ceased at the end of 2025 and multiple generics now on the market.

The acquirer, Angelini Pharma, is a Rome-based family firm founded in 1919, focused on brain health and rare diseases, using Catalyst as its U.S. market entry vehicle. The transaction is partly financed by a minority stake from Italy's sovereign development bank, CDP Equity. The deal fits a broader pattern of European family-owned pharma acquiring U.S. rare-disease specialists; Chiesi, another Italian family firm, bought KalVista for $1.9B the week before.

## Financial Analysis

Catalyst's standalone financials are high quality, which is precisely what made it an attractive target. The relevance of these metrics now is to assess what a buyer is paying for and what the downside floor looks like if the deal breaks.

- **Revenue:** 2025 revenue was approximately $589M (+20% YoY), with 2026 guidance of $615-645M. Q1 2026 total revenue was $149.4M, up only 5.6% YoY because FIRDAPSE and AGAMREE gains were largely offset by the FYCOMPA generic cliff.
- **Profitability:** Q1 2026 operating income was $73.2M, a 49% operating margin; net income was $63.7M (+12.3% YoY), diluted EPS $0.50. Trailing twelve-month EPS is $1.68, putting the trailing P/E at roughly 18x.
- **Balance sheet:** A fortress. $755.9M in cash and equivalents (primarily U.S. Treasuries and money markets), zero debt, $1.01B in stockholders' equity, and working capital of approximately $808M. Total liabilities are only $135M.
- **Cash flow:** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $59.6M, depressed by a $12.5M AGAMREE sales milestone payment to Santhera. Capex is negligible at roughly $27K, reflecting an asset-light model.
- **Margin structure:** Cost of sales fell year-over-year because FIRDAPSE royalty obligations stepped down sharply. As of January 26, 2026, the blended upstream royalty rate on U.S. FIRDAPSE sales dropped to approximately 6%, down from a prior maximum of 18.5%, as the BioMarin/SERB seven-year royalty terms expired (partially offset by the Jacobus royalty rising from 1.5% to 2.5%). This structural margin tailwind is masked in headline numbers by the collapsing FYCOMPA line, and a buyer captures it fully.

The earnings track record is consistent: four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters, with surprises ranging from 4.1% to 24.0%. Estimate revisions are positive, with current-year EPS revised up 10.2% over 90 days and next-year up 19.3%. Under any other circumstance, this revision velocity combined with the royalty step-down would warrant a constructive standalone view. With the stock pinned to a cash bid, these revisions are no longer the operative variable; they are relevant only as evidence the buyer is acquiring an improving asset and as support for a fair-value floor in a break scenario.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth profile is genuinely two-sided and is now frozen by merger covenants. AGAMREE is the engine, having crossed $100M in annual revenue, with Health Canada approval (October 2025) via KYE and expanding EU/UK/Swiss approvals via Santhera. FIRDAPSE remains a steady high-teens grower, supported by an August 2025 NCCN guideline inclusion adding amifampridine to small-cell-lung-cancer guidelines for LEMS, a potential demand driver. Against this, FYCOMPA is a melting ice cube, dragging consolidated growth even as the two core franchises advanced.

Interim operating covenants in the merger agreement restrict Catalyst from M&A, capital-markets access, and other strategic actions pending close. The $200M buyback authorized in October 2025 (1.74M shares repurchased through May 7 at roughly $22.91 average, approximately $39.9M deployed) is effectively halted by the same covenants. For practical purposes, organic growth from the three marketed products continues, but the value-creation lever that defined the equity story is suspended. None of this matters to the realized return if the deal closes at $31.50; it matters only as the standalone earnings power that would re-emerge if the deal broke.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is no longer about intrinsic worth; it is about the deal spread, which the market is pricing as nearly certain to close.

For context on the standalone fundamentals: the trailing P/E of 18x and forward P/E of roughly 18x (on $1.68 EPS) sit above the biotechnology peer median P/E of 14.0x (LGND 31.6x, TGTX 14.0x, HRMY 13.0x, ADMA 11.7x). On forward consensus of $2.86 for the current year, the implied forward multiple compresses to roughly 11x, which would be cheap relative to the franchise quality and growth absent a deal. This is the basis for the deal price being a reasonable strategic valuation rather than an opportunistic lowball, and it supports the floor in a break scenario.

The $31.50 price represented a 21% premium to the undisturbed April 22 price but only about 3% to the May 6 last close, because the stock had already run up on leaks. The headline premium is modest, but the board approved unanimously and insiders are aligned with closing. The relevant valuation question is not whether $31.50 is generous; it is whether the deal closes. We assign a low valuation score because, at the current price, there is essentially no upside to capture and the downside in a break is meaningful and asymmetric.

The trajectory of this scoring reflects exactly this transition: the prior reports scored CPRX as a 7.2 and 7.1 BUY on the standalone growth thesis, then dropped to 6.0 HOLD as the deal repriced the equity into a fixed-value instrument. The valuation dimension fell from 7.5 to 4.5 across that span. We continue that direction. The price decline since the April highs is not a thesis deterioration; the stock simply re-rated to the bid and stopped. There is no fundamental signal in the recent price flatness, only the absence of any reason for the stock to move off the deal price.

## Competitive Landscape

Catalyst's competitive position rests on orphan-drug exclusivity, Orange Book patents, and specialized rare-disease commercial infrastructure (74 field personnel). The most material competitive development is the full resolution of FIRDAPSE patent litigation, announced the same day as the merger (May 6-7, 2026). Catalyst and SERB settled with Hetero, the last remaining ANDA litigant, which agreed to no generic launch before January 2035 (Catalyst paid an $11M litigation-avoidance fee). This followed prior settlements with Teva (February 2035), Lupin (February 2035), and Inventia. With FIRDAPSE's orphan exclusivity having expired November 26, 2025, these settlements now constitute the protective moat, and they extend the FIRDAPSE runway to 2035.

The timing of the Hetero settlement and the merger on the same day is not coincidental. Resolving the last generic overhang on the asset generating roughly 60% of revenue cleared the path to a clean acquisition and almost certainly supported the valuation. The de-risked patent runway is what the strategic buyer was paying for.

In DMD, AGAMREE competes as a better-tolerated corticosteroid with patents to 2029, orphan exclusivity to 2030, and a latest Orange Book patent to 2040. Management positions steroids as foundational therapy even as gene therapies emerge, while flagging the risk that gene-therapy patients may delay or pause AGAMREE. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the strength of the patent settlements, but this is now subsumed into the acquirer's calculus.

## Risk Assessment

Risks bifurcate into deal-completion risk (now dominant) and standalone risk (relevant only in a break).

**Deal-completion risks:** Closing conditions including a shareholder vote and regulatory approvals are not yet satisfied. The acquirer is a foreign (Italian) entity entering the U.S. market with minority sovereign-bank financing, which could draw CFIUS or antitrust scrutiny, though product overlap between Angelini and Catalyst is minimal, reducing antitrust concern. A $155.5M termination fee is payable by Catalyst under specified circumstances. Routine shareholder "fair price" investigations (e.g., Halper Sadeh) have been filed, standard for public-company buyouts. The market's assessment, reflected in the sub-1% spread and collapsed implied volatility, is that close probability is high. We share that assessment but note the residual risk is the dominant determinant of return from here.

**Standalone risks (the break scenario):** Customer concentration is worsening, with Customer A (the exclusive distributor for FIRDAPSE and AGAMREE) rising to 90.1% of net product revenue in Q1 2026 from 74.0% a year earlier, partly mechanical as FYCOMPA declines but heightening fragility. FIRDAPSE single-product dependence (~66% of revenue), the ongoing FYCOMPA cliff with $57.3M of remaining intangible at impairment risk, 100% third-party manufacturing reliance (AGAMREE sole-sourced from Santhera with a second source targeted only for end-2026), and policy exposure (Section 232 pharmaceutical tariff probe, Most-Favored-Nation drug pricing, with Catalyst noting it has not been invited to negotiate MFN relief) are all live. None of these matters if the deal closes; all of them would resurface to define the standalone equity if it breaks.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is consistent with a stock pinned to a cash bid. ATM IV well below trailing historical volatility indicates the market expects little residual price movement. The put/call open interest ratio sits at a low 0.51, signaling little demand for downside protection and a market that assigns low probability to a deal break. The nearest-expiry implied move of ±8.6% is best read as the embedded break-risk premium rather than directional uncertainty. Short interest is modest at 8.3% of float with 2.6 days to cover, providing no squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($31.50, ~+0.5%):** The deal closes on schedule in Q3 2026 at $31.50 cash. Regulatory review (CFIUS, antitrust) clears given minimal product overlap and a well-capitalized, sovereign-backed acquirer. The shareholder vote passes, supported by unanimous board approval and aligned insiders (Chairman McEnany holds approximately 4.17M shares converting to cash). Holders collect the spread. This is the base case and the most likely outcome, but it offers minimal incremental return from the current price.

**Bear Case (~$23-25, roughly -20%):** The deal breaks on a regulatory snag, financing issue, or shareholder rejection on price grounds. The stock returns toward the undisturbed April 22 level in the mid-$20s. The standalone business is high quality (zero debt, $756M cash, 49% operating margins, FIRDAPSE patent runway to 2035) and would find a fundamental floor, but the immediate repricing would be sharp. This is the low-probability, high-magnitude tail.

The reward-to-risk ratio is unfavorable for a new position: roughly 0.5% upside against 17-20% downside, mitigated only by the low probability assigned to the break. This is the standard arithmetic of a late-stage merger arb where the spread has already compressed to near zero.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a HOLD for existing holders content to collect the remaining spread to close, and not a buy for new capital. The classification is "uncertain" because the return is no longer driven by a fundamental view; it is a binary function of deal completion, and the residual spread does not compensate a new entrant for the break risk.

**Upgrade triggers (to a more constructive stance):** (1) A topping bid or revised terms above $31.50, which would be unusual given a privately-held strategic acquirer but would reopen upside; (2) regulatory clearance milestones (CFIUS, antitrust sign-off) that further de-risk the close and could be played by arbitrageurs willing to accept the thin spread; (3) if the deal terminates and the stock falls to the mid-$20s, the standalone business (forward P/E ~11x on $2.86, FIRDAPSE protected to 2035, AGAMREE growing 66%, $756M cash and zero debt) would warrant re-underwriting as a fundamental long.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):** (1) Regulatory pushback from CFIUS or antitrust authorities signaling the deal is in jeopardy; (2) a failed or postponed shareholder vote; (3) any disclosure that financing (CDP Equity / Angelini) is at risk. Any of these would widen the spread sharply and warrant exiting before a confirmed break.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the shareholder vote and regulatory clearance progress through Q3 2026, with the expected close as the resolving event.

## Conclusion

Catalyst Pharmaceuticals has ceased to be an investable growth story and has become a near-completed merger-arbitrage instrument. The standalone business is excellent: zero debt, $756M cash, 49% operating margins, four consecutive earnings beats, a 90-day estimate-revision velocity of +10% on the current year, a FIRDAPSE patent moat freshly extended to 2035, and a rapidly growing AGAMREE franchise. None of that is the operative variable at $31.34. The $31.50 all-cash Angelini bid has fixed the equity's value, compressed the spread to roughly 0.5%, collapsed implied volatility, and frozen the daily range to pennies.

For existing holders, holding to collect the final spread to a likely Q3 2026 close is reasonable; the deal-completion probability appears high based on aligned insiders, unanimous board approval, minimal product overlap reducing antitrust risk, and a well-funded sovereign-backed acquirer. For new capital, the risk/reward is unattractive: a fraction of a percent of upside against a 17-20% downside in the low-probability event the deal breaks. We rate CPRX a HOLD. Whether a signed deal closes is the operative question, and the market has already priced that as near-certain, leaving no margin to underwrite.
