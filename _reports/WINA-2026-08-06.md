## Executive Summary

Winmark is a pure-play retail franchisor that owns zero stores. It licenses five resale brands — Plato's Closet, Once Upon A Child, Play It Again Sports, Style Encore and Music Go Round — to 1,389 independently owned locations and collects a 4-5% royalty on their gross sales, settled weekly. FY2025 system-wide sales were $1,682.0M, on which Winmark realized $76.4M of royalties, a blended 4.54% rate. Total revenue was $86.1M with a 63.4% operating margin, generated on $24.9M of total assets and $192,300 of annual capital expenditure. This is one of the highest-quality royalty structures in small-cap US equities.

The problem is the arithmetic of owning it at 32.9x trailing earnings while reported earnings go sideways. Royalties accelerated from +5.8% (FY2025) to +8.4% (Q1-2026) to +7.8% (Q2-2026), and the franchising segment grew operating income 6.6% in H1-2026. Reported diluted EPS fell from $5.60 to $5.31 in the first half, because FY2025 contained a $2.1M non-recurring leasing-litigation settlement that has now rolled off, SG&A grew 9.6% against 8.1% royalty growth, and the effective tax rate rose from 20.4% to 21.6% as stock-option exercise benefits shrank. Consensus — a single contributor — has been missed four quarters running by 5.1% to 12.3% and has been cut 3.0-4.3% over 90 days.

The stock has de-rated 31% from its October 2025 high of $527.37 and 25% from the March 2026 closing high of $480.67. That drawdown is largely explained rather than mispriced: the loss of the leasing contribution, an unquantified multi-year technology investment cycle, a structurally higher tax rate, franchisee-friction risk from two new fee programs landing on top of the 2026-27 renewal cohorts, and multiple compression on a bond-proxy compounder. Our base case of approximately $370 is essentially spot. We maintain HOLD. Valuation risk has improved; earnings quality and momentum have worsened.

## Company Overview

Winmark sells 10-year franchise licenses with 10-year renewal rights. The initial fee is $25,000 (US) or $36,000 (CAD); additional stores cost $15,000. Franchisees pay 4-5% continuing royalties collected weekly, a $1,500 annual marketing fee, and must spend 5% of gross sales on advertising. Franchisees are contractually required to operate Winmark's proprietary "Data Recycling System" point-of-sale software and to buy POS hardware from the company; this both creates switching costs and gives Winmark the weekly sales data that royalties are computed against. Winmark holds a right of first refusal when a franchisee sells but has no obligation to repurchase, keeping unit-level capital risk entirely with the operator.

Brand mix at FY2025 year-end:

| Brand | Stores | System sales | Royalties + fees | % of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plato's Closet | 526 | $675.5M | $32.2M | 37.4% |
| Once Upon A Child | 441 | $543.4M | $25.9M | 30.1% |
| Play It Again Sports | 309 | $350.0M | $14.9M | 17.3% |
| Style Encore | 67 | $61.7M | $3.2M | 3.7% |
| Music Go Round | 35 | $51.4M | $1.7M | 2.0% |
| **Total** | **1,378** | **$1,682.0M** | **$77.9M** | **90.5%** |

Store count reached 1,389 by June 27, 2026. Approximately 165 locations are Canadian; roughly 9.1% of FY2025 revenue was foreign-denominated, with the 10-K quantifying a 10% currency move at about $782,700 of annual pretax earnings and no hedges in place.

A structural cleanup completed in FY2025: the legacy Winmark Capital equipment-leasing book, in run-off since May 2021, was fully wound down. As of December 27, 2025 there are no leasing customers or leased assets. Winmark is now a single-business franchisor.

The company's core value proposition — paying cash on the spot for used goods and reselling them within the week — is not replicated by consignment or peer-to-peer platforms, which require the seller to list, ship and wait. The 10-K names eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark, thredUP and Amazon as competitors, alongside new-goods retailers (American Eagle, Gap, Old Navy, Target, Walmart against Plato's Closet; Dick's and Academy against Play It Again Sports). Winmark's franchisees also sell through Shopify, eBay, Reverb and Meta, so online resale functions partly as a channel for the system rather than purely as a substitute.

Note 3 of the FY2025 10-K discloses $2,138,200 of leasing income classified as "Other," and MD&A states that $2.2M of the $2.5M of FY2025 leasing income related to settlement of outstanding customer litigation. Removing it: FY2025 operating income was $52,456k versus $52,931k in FY2024, a 0.9% decline. The reported 3.1% growth is an artifact of a one-time legal recovery.

The franchising segment itself grew operating income only 0.9% ($51,593k to $52,057k) on 5.8% royalty growth, because franchising SG&A rose 15.3% to $28,262k. Segment operating margin compressed roughly 250 basis points to 62.4%. The stated drivers were higher compensation and a non-recurring third-party software license expense for franchisees, the first visible P&L cost of the POS modernization program.

Three things follow. First, royalty growth accelerated to +8.1% in H1, the fastest two-quarter pace observable in the disclosure. Second, total revenue grew only 1.1% because the prior-year period contained the leasing settlement; on a franchising-only basis, segment operating income rose 6.6%. Third, the reported EPS decline is a tax-line event: Q2 pretax income rose 3.8% while net income fell 1.9%. Holding the Q2-2025 rate of 16.6% would have produced $2.98 of EPS; normalizing both years to ~21.5% gives $2.80 (2026) versus $2.72 (2025).

That normalization is a derived sensitivity, not a company disclosure, and it does not make the cash taxes unreal. FY2025 recognized a $1,384,500 stock-compensation tax benefit worth 2.6 points of ETR; at a share price 31% below the high, future exercise volumes and spreads shrink. A ~22% run-rate tax rate is the defensible forward assumption, and roughly half of the Q2 consensus shortfall traces to that item, with the other half in the SG&A step-up.

### Cash flow, balance sheet and the payout arithmetic

Operating cash flow was $43,994k (FY2023), $42,158k (FY2024) and $44,897k (FY2025). FY2025 free cash flow of $44,705k after $192k of capex is a 51.9% FCF margin. OCF exceeded net income by $3.2M on stock compensation of $2,283k, D&A of $747k and option tax benefits of $1,619k. Cash conversion quality is excellent.

FY2025 capital returned was $51,532k — $49,113k of dividends plus $2,419k of buybacks at roughly $304 average — equal to 115% of FCF, bridged by $4,958k of option-exercise proceeds and a $1,869k cash drawdown. Each of the last three years included a large special dividend: $9.40 (2023), $7.50 (2024), $10.00 (2025), on top of rising regular quarterlies. The residue is a shareholders' deficit of $(53,682)k at FY2025 year-end, narrowing to $(37,623)k at June 27, 2026. This is the accounting consequence of decades of distributions exceeding earnings, not distress.

H1-2026 changed the pattern: zero share repurchases, dividends of $7,087k ($0.96 then $1.02 per quarter, the latter +6.3% year over year), and a new investing line of $2,210,300 of capitalized POS software not yet placed in service. Cash rose from $10,296k to $25,835k. The absence of a special dividend in H1 is seasonal — reconstructing FY2025, $42.5M of the $49.1M was paid in H2, so the special is a fourth-quarter event.

Debt is $60.0M of principal, unchanged at both period ends: $30.0M of CIBC delayed-draw term loans floating at 4.60-4.75% maturing April 12, 2029, and $30.0M of PGIM notes fixed at 3.18% maturing 2028. The $20.0M revolver is fully undrawn and terminates April 12, 2027. Both facilities are secured, rank pari passu, and carry minimum debt-service coverage and maximum leverage covenants; the company was compliant at both December 27, 2025 and June 27, 2026, and no principal amortizes in 2026 or 2027.

Derived: gross leverage 1.08x FY2025 EBITDA of $55.3M; net leverage 0.62x on $34.2M of net debt; interest coverage 22.3x. Refinancing the 3.18% notes at 6.0% in 2028 would add roughly $0.85M of pretax interest, about $0.18 per diluted share after tax — immaterial.

Two flexibility constraints deserve naming. The Prudential $100M private shelf expired in April 2025 and was not replaced, and only 70,656 shares (1.97% of shares outstanding, ~$25M) remain under the repurchase authorization. The distribution machine has historically been funded by FCF plus option proceeds; with cash at $25.8M mid-year, a special comparable to 2025's $10.00 (~$35.9M) would consume most of H2 cash generation or require revolver support. Do not capitalize a special-dividend yield.

We disregard the reported Altman Z-Score of 6.68, the 167.4% ROA and the undefined ROE. All three are artifacts of a $24.9M asset base and negative book equity, not solvency or return assessments. The informative solvency facts are 0.62x net leverage, 22.3x coverage, an undrawn revolver, covenant compliance, and no maturities before 2028.

## Growth Analysis

The royalty stream is the whole story, and it is accelerating. System sales went $1,589.0M (FY2023) to $1,610.2M (FY2024, +1.3%) to $1,682.0M (FY2025, +4.5%). Decomposing FY2025: system sales per store rose from $1.1927M to $1.2206M (+2.3%) while net units grew 2.07% (55 opened, 27 closed on a 1,350 base) — roughly half productivity, half units. H1-2026 added 11 net units (19 opened, 8 closed) and 82 signed-but-unopened agreements existed at year-end.

Renewals are the durability metric that matters for a stream built on 10-year contracts: 114 of 116 available agreements renewed in FY2025 (98%), 50 of 50 in H1-2026 (100%), with a three-year average of 99% per management. Against that, the 10-K discloses 110 agreements expiring in 2026, 103 in 2027 and 92 in 2028 — about 8%, 7% and 7% of the system annually.

Two new revenue programs begin in H2-2026. Per the Q2-2026 10-Q's newly added risk factor, cross-referencing an Item 8.01 8-K filed March 16, 2026, Winmark is introducing a monthly Software Fee for all locations and a North American Ad Fund for all Plato's Closet locations. Secondary reporting specifies $295 per location per month effective September 1, 2026 (~$400k/month, ~$4.9M annualized) and a 2%-of-sales Plato's Closet ad fund from July 1, 2026, raising franchisees' total marketing obligation from 5% to 6% and equating to roughly $13.5M on FY2025 sales. Both figures reconcile against filing data ($295 × 1,389 = $409,755; 2% × $675.5M = $13.51M), and the 10-K independently confirms Winmark holds a contractual option to take up to 2% of sales into a company ad fund, so this is exercise of a pre-existing right, not renegotiation. The 8-K text itself was not retrieved; treat the specific terms as externally sourced.

The financial consequence is genuinely uncertain in a way that matters for how FY2027 screens. The software fee at ~$4.9M is real incremental revenue against a technology budget management controls, and some portion should reach operating income over time. The ad fund is the wildcard: if recognized gross — consistent with prevailing franchisor practice and with Winmark's existing gross treatment of the $1,500 marketing fee — FY2027 revenue could approach $110M+, a roughly 28% optical increase, while operating income barely moves and the reported operating margin falls from ~63% toward ~50%. If presented net as an agency arrangement, neither line changes materially. No filing yet describes the presentation. The Q3-2026 10-Q resolves it, and anyone modeling off reported revenue and margin without knowing the answer will draw the wrong conclusion.

Our FY2026 build: royalties ~$82.2M (+7.7%), other revenue ~$6.8M, four months of software fee ~$1.6M if gross, giving revenue near $90M; SG&A near $31.5M; operating income ~$56M; interest ~$2.3M; tax 21.7%; net income ~$42.0M on ~3.70M diluted shares, or roughly $11.35. That lands on the $11.37 consensus, but requires H2 royalty growth to hold near 8% and SG&A growth to decelerate from H1's 9.6%. Given four consecutive misses, we carry $11.00-11.35. FY2027 on 6-7% royalty growth, a full year of software fee and modest operating leverage gets to roughly $12.00-12.30, against consensus of $12.49.

Style Encore is the one concept not scaling: 69 units at FY2024 year-end, 67 at FY2025, 66 at June 2026, with zero openings and one closure in H1-2026. System sales still rose to $61.7M from $59.1M, so surviving-store productivity is improving as the footprint prunes. At 3.7% of revenue it is immaterial to valuation, but it is the only evidence available on management's ability to incubate a sixth concept — an option a 30x multiple partly depends on.

## Valuation Assessment

At the authoritative basis of $362.17:

- Market cap on shares outstanding: 3,592,169 × $362.17 = **$1,301M**
- On Q2 weighted diluted shares (3,696,010): **$1,339M**
- TTM diluted EPS = $11.30 − $5.60 + $5.31 = **$11.01** → **32.9x TTM**
- Current-year consensus $11.37 → **31.9x**; next-year $12.49 → **29.0x**
- Enterprise value = $1,339M + $60.0M − $25.8M = **$1,373M**
- EV/FY2025 EBITDA ($55.3M) = **24.8x**; excluding the litigation settlement (EBITDA $53.2M) = **25.8x**
- FCF yield = $44.7M / $1,301M = **3.44%**
- Regular dividend run-rate $4.08 → **1.13% yield**

Peer comparables in the supplied "Other Specialty Retail" set — BBWI at 5.3x, CHWY at 31.3x, TSCO at 15.4x, ULTA at 17.9x, median 17.9x — are of limited use. Those are capital-intensive operators of physical stores with inventory and lease obligations; Winmark is an asset-light royalty collector with a 63% operating margin, 52% FCF margin and $192k of annual capex. The defensible comparison set is high-quality franchisors, which typically clear 25-30x. On that basis 29-32x is inside the range rather than extreme.

The multiple has to survive the growth it is paired with. Reported EPS is roughly flat-to-mid-single-digit growing: $11.04 (FY2023), $10.89 (FY2024), $11.30 (FY2025), ~$11.35 (FY2026E), ~$12.15 (FY2027E). A 29x forward multiple on 6% EPS growth plus a 1.13% dividend produces a ~7% expected annual return with no re-rating. Against a 1.13% dividend yield and an FCF yield below prevailing long-term Treasury yields, that spread is thin, and it is the mechanism that has already compressed the multiple from roughly 41x (at $460 on $11.30) to 32.9x. Higher long-end yields transmit to low-growth quality compounders through the discount rate; nothing in Winmark's operations offsets that.

Scenario framework:

- **Bear $270 (−25%)**: franchisee friction from a 20% increase in mandated marketing spend (5%→6%) plus a $3,540 annual software fee causes renewals to slip from 98-99% toward 92-94% across the 213 agreements expiring in 2026-27, unit growth stalls, royalty growth decelerates to 2-3%, SG&A stays elevated, and the multiple compresses to 23x on $11.50.
- **Base $370 (+2%)**: royalty growth moderates to 6-7%, the software fee contributes modestly net of costs, SG&A growth decelerates as the POS program shifts from expense to capitalized asset, FY2027 EPS $12.15, multiple 30.5x. This is roughly spot.
- **Bull $445 (+23%)**: royalty growth holds near 8%, the software fee largely drops through, FY2027 EPS reaches $13.00, and the multiple re-rates to 34x as the market gains confidence that the FY2025-26 cost step-up was investment rather than permanent inflation. A $10 special dividend adds ~2.8%.

The single sell-side target of $545 implies 50% upside. It comes from one contributor, last acted on before two quarters of reported EPS declines (the brief locates a $510 Buy target as of March 2026 at a $450 share price). Analyst targets serve as floors only when a stock looks cheap relative to rising estimates; this target qualifies as neither, since estimates are falling and the multiple is near 30x forward. We treat it as stale and non-informative.

Valuation score 4.5: no identified mispricing, base case at spot, and a negative FCF-yield spread to Treasuries.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive question is whether online resale structurally erodes physical resale economics. The filing evidence cuts against that for now: FY2025 system sales +4.5% with +2.3% per-store productivity, and H1-2026 royalties +8.1%. The immediate-cash-for-goods model with no listing effort is not what marketplaces offer, and the mandated POS system creates measurable switching costs — franchisees cannot leave the system without abandoning the software their operations run on, plus a two-year non-compete.

Franchisee input dependence is where new-goods inflation and tariffs transmit. Play It Again Sports buys new product from Adidas, Wilson, Champro, Rawlings/Easton, CCM and Bauer; Once Upon A Child sources roughly 30% of new product from Wild Side, Melissa & Doug and Nuby; Music Go Round about 50% from KMC/Musicorp, RapcoHorizon, D'Addario, GHS and Ernie Ball. New product is negligible for Plato's Closet and Style Encore. The two brands representing 67.5% of Winmark's revenue are essentially insulated from import cost inflation, while the sporting-goods and instrument concepts are partially exposed. The hypothesis that new-goods price inflation drives traffic to resale could not be tested against third-party industry data and is not relied upon here.

Concentration is at the brand rather than customer level: Plato's Closet plus Once Upon A Child equal 67.5% of revenue, and the 2% ad fund is being imposed on the largest contributor. There are 1,389 independent payors, so no single-customer risk exists.

Competitive position score 6.5: trajectory is stable-to-improving on royalty and productivity data, offset by 2% unit growth, a shrinking Style Encore, and unproven concept incubation.

## Risk Assessment

**POS modernization (highest-consequence operational risk).** Newly added as a risk factor in the FY2025 10-K. The company cites system instability, workflow interruption, data and reporting inconsistencies, third-party vendor dependence and strain on franchisee relationships. Because franchisees are contractually required to use Winmark's POS and royalties are computed off its data, a botched migration would impair royalty measurement and collection, not merely convenience. The financial footprint is visible: a non-recurring software license expense inside FY2025's 13.7% SG&A increase and $2,210,300 of capitalized software not yet in service at June 27, 2026.

**Fee increases into the renewal cohorts.** The Q2-2026 10-Q states the new initiatives "may increase franchisee operating costs and may not result in immediate or uniform benefits," with potential impact on "franchisee retention, the pace of new franchise development, and the overall performance of the Company's franchise systems." That language is the company pointing at the overlap between a 20% increase in mandated marketing spend plus a new software fee and the 213 agreements expiring in 2026-27. At 98-99% renewal this is benign; the risk is a slip.

**Accounting-presentation uncertainty.** Whether the ad fund is grossed up or presented net will swing FY2027 reported revenue growth between roughly 5% and 28%, and the reported operating margin between ~63% and ~50%, with no change in economics. This is a real risk of investor misinterpretation in both directions.

**Earnings-visibility deterioration.** Four consecutive misses (−8.1%, −5.1%, −9.2%, −12.3%), with the magnitude widening, against a consensus with one contributor and 30d/90d revisions of −3.6% (current quarter), −4.3% (current year) and −3.0% (next year). Winmark provides no guidance and holds no earnings calls, so there is no management-supplied anchor. A widening miss pattern on a thin consensus reduces confidence in the FY2026 and FY2027 numbers.

**Capital-flexibility constraints.** Distributions at 115% of FCF, 1.97% of shares remaining under the repurchase authorization, the expired Prudential shelf, and a revolver terminating April 2027. None of this is solvency risk at 0.62x net leverage and 22.3x coverage, but it does mean the special dividend is discretionary and sizeable buybacks require a board action that has not occurred.

**Supply and regulation.** Franchisee inventory comes from walk-in sellers, which the 10-K notes "is not regular or highly reliable," and is subject to state and local secondhand-dealer statutes. Fourteen US states, the FTC and six Canadian provinces impose pre-sale registration; joint-employer and worker-classification language persists. FX exposure is $782,700 per 10% move, unhedged.

**Legal.** Both the FY2025 10-K and Q2-2026 10-Q state no material litigation. The only recent litigation of consequence produced income.

**Short interest.** 12.9% of float, 328,479 shares, 7.0 days to cover. Elevated for a $1.3bn company with 72,500 average daily volume. That is meaningful bearish institutional positioning and, at 7 days to cover, meaningful squeeze potential on any positive surprise, a two-sided risk that also reflects genuinely low liquidity.

Risk score 5.5: two specific, company-identified operational risks that are not obviously priced, offset by no binary legal or regulatory events, low leverage, no near maturities, and counter-cyclical demand characteristics.

## Management Quality

Brett Heffes serves as both Chair and CEO, a governance negative. FY2025 CEO total compensation was $2,108,124 against an NEO average of $927,261 — a 2.3x ratio that is modest. Equity is delivered almost entirely as options struck at 100% of grant-date fair value, vesting over four years, which pays only on share-price appreciation. Stock compensation expense was $2,283,200 in FY2025 with $8.0M unrecognized at June 27, 2026 over a 4.0-year weighted average. Grant Thornton, auditor since 2006, issued unqualified opinions with no critical audit matters.

The company-selected performance measure is royalty revenues. That is the best available proxy for franchise-system health and is not gameable through buybacks or one-time gains, but it carries a specific misalignment: FY2025 royalty revenue rose 5.8% while operating income excluding the litigation settlement fell 0.9%, because SG&A rose 15.3%. A royalty-only metric does not penalize cost-base inflation.

Insider activity is exercise-and-sell by directors, concentrated post-filing. Director Grassle sold 3,900 shares at $475.80 on March 4, 2026 after exercising 3,900 options at $134.25-$176.20; director Tomlinson sold 1,660 at $460.52 on March 3 after exercises at $197.80-$242.58. Both are ordinary monetization of deep-in-the-money options approaching their economic lives, and we do not infer foresight. Officers exercised without recorded sales: CFO Ishaug exercised 3,293 options at $98.25 and holds 69,776 shares (~$25M at current price); COO Gaudette holds 21,360. That retained ownership is substantial alignment.

No open-market purchase by any insider appears in the window, including during a drawdown that took the stock to $332.52 on July 24. Insiders with strikes at $98-$176 have plenty of paper wealth and no evident inclination to add cash at current levels. Combined with zero share repurchases in H1-2026 while cash built from $10.3M to $25.8M, the revealed preference is neutral on the stock at these prices. Redirecting capital to the POS build is defensible; it is not a vote of confidence in the multiple.

One unexplained item: the June 1, 2026 grant of 10,968 options to CMO Hake at $378.57, more than twice the CEO's 4,620. Retention, role expansion tied to the July ad-fund launch, and a front-loaded multi-year award are all plausible; none is disclosed.

Management quality score 5.5, down from 6.0. Options-only comp and CFO retained ownership are genuine positives; the combined Chair/CEO role, the royalty-only performance metric against 15% SG&A growth, an exhausted buyback authorization not expanded, and the absence of any insider buying into a drawdown net out to below-average forward signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Winmark collects 4.54% of a $1.68bn system's sales weekly, with 98-100% renewal rates, 63% operating margins, 52% FCF margins and $192k of annual capex. Royalty growth accelerated to 8.1% in H1-2026 while the consumer weakened, which is consistent with resale taking share from new-goods retail as households trade down, making this a defensive franchise inside a discretionary sector wrapper. The FY2025-26 SG&A step-up was explicitly labeled non-recurring in part and is now partly capitalized rather than expensed, so FY2027 should show operating leverage returning. The software fee adds ~$4.9M of annual revenue against a controllable cost base. If FY2027 EPS reaches $13.00 and the multiple returns to 34x, the shares are worth $445, plus a special dividend. Franchising-segment operating income already grew 6.6% in H1-2026.

**Bear case.** The market is pricing forward risk correctly. FY2026 loses the ~$2.5M leasing contribution (4.6% of FY2025 operating income). The effective tax rate resets structurally higher as option-exercise benefits shrink at a lower share price. SG&A has grown faster than royalties, and the duration of the technology spend is undisclosed. Two new fee programs raise franchisee costs by roughly 1% of their sales plus $3,540 per year, landing precisely on the 213 agreements expiring in 2026-27, and management itself flagged retention and new-development risk. Unit growth is 2%, Style Encore is shrinking, and no sixth concept has been proven. Four consecutive misses of widening magnitude on a one-analyst consensus argue the forward numbers are still too high. At 29x forward, a 3.4% FCF yield and 1.13% regular dividend, the multiple has further room to compress toward 23x, or $270.

**Our resolution.** Both cases are partly right, which is why the base case sits at spot. The royalty stream is healthy and accelerating; the reported earnings line is not, and will not be until either SG&A growth decelerates below royalty growth or the software fee proves accretive. Neither is verifiable before the October print. The drawdown has removed the premium that made this unattractive at $480 without creating the discount that would make it attractive at $290.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

That print carries three resolutions simultaneously — the first month of software-fee revenue (September), the accounting presentation of the Plato's Closet ad fund (gross versus net), and the first full quarter of the 6% franchisee marketing obligation.
