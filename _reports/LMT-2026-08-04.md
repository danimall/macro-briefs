## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin's July 23 second-quarter report and guidance raise materially changed the near-term evidence set. Q2 sales rose 10.5% to $20,063M, GAAP EPS came in at $7.94 against a $7.18 consensus (+10.5%), free cash flow was approximately $2,917M after a negative $291M first quarter, and management raised all three FY2026 guidance metrics: sales to $79.75–81.75B (from $77.5–80.0B), GAAP diluted EPS to $29.95–30.65 (from $29.35–30.25), and free cash flow to $7.0–7.2B (from $6.5–6.8B). Consensus FY2026 and FY2027 EPS have been revised up 1.7% and 1.6% over 90 days. Separately, the company booked a seven-year THAAD undefinitized contract action of up to $35B in June and, on July 29 — after the quarter closed — an externally reported $53.86B PAC-3 MSE UCA modification bringing that contract to $58.62B. Missiles & Fire Control backlog nearly doubled to $87.9B.

Three prior reports carried an AVOID rating with scores declining from 5.6 to 4.6, built on falling GAAP EPS, spreading reach-forward losses, negative Q1 free cash flow and halted buybacks. Two of those planks have reversed. Free cash flow turned strongly positive, and forward earnings are rising rather than falling. The stock has advanced roughly 15% from the pre-print level near $509, so the improvement is now substantially in the price. That argues for an upgrade to HOLD rather than a reversal to BUY.

What has not changed is the quality of the earnings and the durability of the capital-return story. Stripping the net profit-booking-rate adjustments that percentage-of-completion accounting produces, underlying segment operating margin was 8.92% in both 1H26 and 1H25 — the reported expansion from 7.35% to 10.46% is a $1,155M swing in estimate revisions (–$565M to +$590M), worth $3.92 of first-half EPS by the company's own disclosure. The FAS/CAS pension adjustment supplied $1,518M of FY2025's $7,731M consolidated operating profit. Buybacks were zero in both quarters of 1H26 against $8,324M of remaining authorization, while Senate NDAA §815 would, if enacted as reported, bar both repurchases and dividends for Department of War contractors beginning June 15, 2027 absent a Secretary-granted waiver. At 19.4x guided FY2026 EPS with a base case of roughly $620 and a bear case near $460, the skew is balanced at best.

## Company Overview

Lockheed is the largest defense contractor globally by revenue, with $75,048M of FY2025 sales across four segments: Aeronautics ($30,257M, 6.9% FY2025 margin, 9.2% in 1H26), Rotary & Mission Systems ($17,312M, 7.6%, 10.3%), Missiles & Fire Control ($14,450M, 13.8%, 14.1%) and Space ($13,029M, 10.3%, 9.4%).

Customer concentration is extreme. In FY2025, 72% of sales went to the U.S. Government and 63% to the Department of War; the 1H26 split was 69.4% U.S. Government and 30.2% international. The international mix shift is real but does not represent diversification of demand — 77% of international sales route through U.S. Foreign Military Sales, where the U.S. Government is the contracting party, and the residual buyers are foreign defense ministries. Program concentration is equally acute: the F-35 was 27% of FY2025 sales (~$20.3B) and approximately 28% of Q2 2026 sales, with 1,344 production aircraft delivered through June 28, 2026.

The moat is incumbency rather than technology in the conventional sense: sole-source positions on F-35, C-130, THAAD, PAC-3, Trident II D5, Aegis and Black Hawk/Seahawk; classified facilities and clearances; and installed-base sustainment. Seventeen nations have selected PAC-3 CRI or MSE. Offset and industrial-cooperation obligations of roughly $19.9B notional to 2044, carrying ~$2.2B of potential penalties, cut both ways — they lock in customers and create contingent liability.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating.** Growth ran +2.4% (FY2023), +5.1% (FY2024), +5.6% (FY2025), and the raised guidance midpoint of ~$80.75B implies +7.6% for FY2026. Q2 2026 sales grew 10.5%; 1H26 grew 5.4%. MFC drove the quarter at +19% to $4.1B on PAC-3, THAAD and Precision Strike Missile volume, with Aeronautics +9%, RMS +9% and Space +6%.

**Reported profitability recovered; normalized profitability did not.** Consolidated operating margin went 12.65% (FY2022) → 12.59% (FY2023) → 9.87% (FY2024) → 10.30% (FY2025), and GAAP diluted EPS fell from $27.55 to $22.31 to $21.49 across FY2023–FY2025 while revenue grew 11%. The 1H26 optics are strong — segment profit $3,985M versus $2,656M. But net profit-booking-rate adjustments swung from –$565M to +$590M. Removing them on both sides gives segment profit of $3,395M on $38,084M of sales (8.92%) against $3,221M on $36,118M (8.92%). That normalization is crude — booking-rate adjustments are the mechanism by which percentage-of-completion accounting recognizes both genuine risk retirement and genuine cost deterioration, and both periods contain offsetting items — but it locates the reported improvement in estimate revisions rather than demonstrated cost performance. Supporting that read: 1H26 contained $395M of *new* unfavorable adjustments on four unrelated programs (F-16 –$125M, C-130 –$95M, CH-53K –$95M, Seahawk –$80M).

A second support to reported operating profit deserves attention. The FAS/CAS pension adjustment contributed +$1,518M of FY2025's $7,731M consolidated operating profit (19.6%), and +$843M in 1H26 — CAS pension cost recovered of $867M against FAS service cost of just $24M. Those CAS recoveries are real cash reimbursed by the government, so this is not a purely non-cash benefit; the issue is durability. Salaried defined-benefit plans have been frozen since January 1, 2020 and prepayment credits fell to $1.9B at YE2025 from $2.2B at YE2024. FY2025 also carried a $479M non-cash settlement charge ($1.63/share after tax) from transferring $943M of gross obligations to insurers in December 2025.

**Cash flow: headline strong, working-capital quality weakening.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $8,557M was 1.71x net income of $5,017M, on working-capital timing and lower tax payments following the July 2025 tax legislation. FY2025 free cash flow was $6,908M. 1H26 free cash flow was $2,626M ($3,455M OCF less $829M capex), split between negative $291M in Q1 and roughly $2,917M in Q2. Reaching the low end of the raised guidance requires $4.37–4.57B in 2H26, or about $2.2B per quarter — below the Q2 run rate, and therefore achievable. Neither quarter should be annualized; milestone billings and government collections are lumpy and back-half weighted.

Two qualifications on cash quality. Accrued reach-forward program losses totaled approximately $1,892M at June 28, 2026 ($427M Aeronautics classified, $1.1B MFC classified, $365M CMHP) — already expensed, converting to cash outflows in future periods. And contract assets rose to $16,038M from $13,001M in six months, a $3,037M build against 5.4% sales growth. Expressed in days of annualized sales, that is 76.9 days versus 63.2 days at year-end, roughly 14 days of deterioration in a half-year, attributed to F-35 and tactical & strike missiles. Inventories grew $887M, including pre-contract costs in work-in-process of $1.9B (from $1.5B) — at-risk spending ahead of contract authorization.

**Balance sheet: not distressed, but structurally thin.** A standardized screen flagged an Altman Z-Score of 1.53 with an EBIT/assets input of 0.002, irreconcilable with FY2025 operating income of $7,731M on $59,840M of assets (12.9%). Recomputed from primary data — working capital/assets 0.0339, retained earnings/assets 0.2345, EBIT/assets 0.1292, market equity/total liabilities 2.5584, sales/assets 1.2541 — Z ≈ 3.59, above the 2.99 threshold; approximately 3.55 on a narrower EBIT definition and ~3.70 on the June 2026 balance sheet. The screen's "D/E of 7.9x" is total liabilities divided by equity ($53,119M/$6,721M); actual debt-to-equity at June 28, 2026 is $21.7B/$8,768M = 2.48x. Net debt of $17.9B against an FY2025 EBITDA proxy of $9,418M is roughly 1.9x, and pro forma for the $3.45B Ultra Maritime purchase against an FY2026E EBITDA proxy of ~$11.7B, roughly 1.8x. These EBITDA figures are analyst-constructed proxies, not company measures. Current maturities were $0 at June 28, 2026 after $1,168M of repayments, no revolver or commercial paper was outstanding, and debt fair value ($20.5B) sits below principal ($21.7B).

What is genuinely unusual: stockholders' equity of $8,768M includes accumulated other comprehensive loss of –$7,468M (essentially all retirement-plan actuarial losses), and tangible book equity is negative $4,317M after $11,298M of goodwill and $1,787M of intangibles. Goodwill is 18.1% of assets and 1.29x book equity, tested in Q4 2025 with no impairment. The 10-K itself warns that a stockholders' equity deficit could constrain dividends and repurchases under Maryland law. Reported ROE of 74.6% is an artifact of two decades of buybacks depleting the equity base; ROA of 8.4% is the informative figure.

Note a data conflict worth flagging: the market-data profile shows both trailing and forward P/E at 21.60, which is inconsistent with the same file's $21.49 trailing EPS ($586.29/$21.49 = 27.3x). This report uses $21.49 trailing GAAP diluted EPS as reported in the 10-K and the $30.30 guidance midpoint for forward calculations.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on munitions, and it is specific. THAAD interceptor production is being quadrupled from 96 to 400 units annually under a seven-year UCA of up to $35B awarded June 24, 2026. PAC-3 MSE received a reported $53.86B UCA modification on July 29, taking that seven-year contract to $58.62B — externally reported by Breaking Defense and Barron's citing the company and the Army, with no filing confirmation yet since it post-dates the 10-Q. MFC segment backlog rose to $87.9B from $46.65B. European sales grew 33.9% in 1H26 to $5,427M from $4,054M, the clearest evidence of NATO rearmament reaching the P&L, while Middle East sales fell 19% to $1,178M and Asia-Pacific was flat. The FY2027 request of April 3, 2026 carries a $1.5 trillion defense topline with $760B for procurement, emphasizing munitions frameworks, Golden Dome missile defense and increased F-35 purchases.

Three qualifications materially condition that.

First, **the record backlog contains less near-term revenue than the smaller backlog it replaced.** Backlog rose from $193.6B to $230.4B, but disclosed conversion expectations fell from ~37%/60% (12/24 months) to ~30%/50%. Applying those: $230.4B × 30% = $69.1B convertible within twelve months, versus $193.6B × 37% = $71.6B six months earlier — a decline of about 3.5%. The increase is duration. Since MFC backlog rose ~$41.25B while total backlog rose $36.8B, the other three segments' backlog fell roughly $4.45B in aggregate — an inference from two disclosed aggregates rather than a disclosed figure, since segment-level backlog for Aeronautics, RMS and Space was not obtained. A seven-year production award should convert slowly, so slower conversion is the expected mechanical result of a multi-year framework.

Second, **the munitions funding is not appropriated.** Of the FY2027 request, $10.5B of $11.4B for THAAD procurement and $10.9B of $12.2B for PAC-3 MSE are slated for a potential reconciliation bill rather than regular appropriations; the House Appropriations Committee approved $1.3B for PAC-3 MSE and roughly $174M less than requested for THAAD. The Pentagon's seven-year framework agreements announced July 27 are described as legally non-binding and dependent on congressional funding. This characterization comes from a single think-tank-affiliated analysis in Forbes; the appropriations mechanics are checkable, but were not independently confirmed here.

Third, **F-35 is not replenishing.** Aircraft backlog fell from 368 at year-end to 317 at June 28 while cumulative deliveries rose 51 — implying essentially zero net new aircraft bookings in 1H26. At roughly 100 deliveries per year, that is about three years of coverage. Lot 18/19 (296 aircraft) was definitized in September 2025, so a quiet booking half-year is not by itself a demand signal, but the current backlog is not being refilled. Lot 20 negotiation is the next visible checkpoint.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss across four quarters (+10.5%, –5.2%, +29.0%, +8.2%). Revision direction is mildly positive for the annual periods (+1.8% current year, +1.6% next year over 30 days) with the next two quarters marked down 1.7% and 3.0% — a within-year phasing shift rather than a deterioration.

Valuation score: 5.0. There is no identifiable mispricing to underwrite — the market repriced the guidance raise within one session and the multiple now sits mid-range against the correct peer set.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is diverging by segment, which is the relevant framing.

**Improving:** MFC. Sole-source THAAD and PAC-3 positions with seventeen nations on the PAC-3 family, production capacity quadrupling, 14.1% segment margin, and backlog nearly doubled. Undersea and naval sensing gets scale from the Ultra Maritime acquisition ($3.45B, announced July 6, buying from Cobham Ultra, an Advent International portfolio company; sonar, sonobuoys, torpedo defense, radar and autonomous maritime sensing; closing expected Q4 2026 into RMS; excluded from guidance).

**Deteriorating:** Aeronautics. The 10-Q confirms $66M of charges in Q2 2025 "primarily for the write-off of fixed assets resulting from the U.S. Air Force's Next Generation Air Dominance (NGAD) competition and down-select decision." Lockheed lost the sixth-generation fighter. With F-35 unit backlog contracting and no announced successor manned-fighter program, the long-term growth center of gravity has shifted to MFC and Space. The counter-possibility is that classified work — including the Aeronautics program carrying ~$1.8B of cumulative losses, on which Lockheed continues to incur advance procurement costs — represents next-generation programs not publicly attributable. The filings do not resolve this.

**Structural procurement headwinds cut against incumbency.** An administration directive makes fixed-price the government's default contract type; fixed-price was already 59% of 1H26 sales ($22,481M of $38,084M). For a prime that has absorbed roughly $4.1B of cumulative fixed-price reach-forward losses, that is unambiguously negative. The Department is also expanding use of Other Transaction Authorities exempt from the FAR and of IDIQ/multi-award vehicles, both of which dilute incumbent advantage. Lockheed also discloses that the Government retains the right to unilaterally definitize contracts — directly relevant with THAAD and PAC-3 both sitting as UCAs.

The proxy quantifies how the market has treated the franchise: indexed TSR of 156.36 for LMT in 2025 against 230.49 for the peer group, after outperforming in each of 2021–2024. Lockheed did not participate in the 2025 defense re-rating, which is a company-specific execution verdict rather than a sector one.

Competitive position score: 5.0. A strengthening munitions franchise offsets a weakening combat-aircraft franchise, with procurement-mechanism changes eroding the incumbency premium at the margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Capital-return restriction (the largest under-priced binary).** Executive Order 14372, "Prioritizing the Warfighter in Defense Contracting," was signed January 7, 2026 and appears as a new Item 1A risk factor: it could limit contractors on critical defense contracts from issuing excessive dividends or distributions, making repurchases, and imposes executive-compensation restrictions, implemented via a new DFARS clause and conditioned on a Department of War determination of contract underperformance. Whether the implementing DFARS clause has issued, and whether Lockheed has received any performance determination, is unverified.

Senate FY2027 NDAA §815 is far broader. As reported by outside counsel analysis, beginning June 15, 2027 the Secretary of Defense could not enter any procurement contract unless the contractor agrees in writing not to repurchase its own or its listed parent's equity and not to pay dividends or make other capital distributions — no dollar threshold, no program limitation, no performance condition, no commercial-item exemption. Relief runs through a Secretary-granted waiver tied to an approved "qualifying defense investment" plan (capacity capex, machine tools, non-reimbursable applied R&D, workforce training, critical-material stockpiling, expressly excluding reimbursable costs). Enforcement tools include payment suspension, waiver revocation by business segment, non-responsibility determinations and termination of progress payments. It sunsets January 1, 2031. The provision appears only in the Senate bill; the House-passed version has no counterpart and floor attempts to add one failed, so it goes to conference. Forty-plus trade associations wrote Senate leadership on July 14 urging it be struck, and the underlying standalone legislation is bipartisan (Warren, Hawley, Lee). Outside counsel's assessment is that a narrowed version — with thresholds or program limitations — is the more likely conference outcome than elimination. Even a narrowed version inserts the Pentagon into Lockheed's capital-allocation process.

**Chronic fixed-price losses.** Cumulative reach-forward losses total roughly $4.1B: Aeronautics classified ~$1.8B ($427M still accrued), MFC classified ~$1.5B ($1.1B accrued), CMHP ~$670M ($365M accrued), TUHP ~$130M. The MFC classified program is disclosed with an unusual base case — management expects each remaining fixed-price option to be at a loss while believing the probable options will be exercised. CMHP carries ~$585M of contract assets with restructuring discussions underway that "could result in contract termination," dependent on Canadian priorities and U.S. trade relations. The 1H26 addition of $395M of new unfavorable adjustments across F-16, C-130, CH-53K and Seahawk indicates estimation volatility is broad rather than confined to two classified programs.

**ULA.** The 50/50 joint venture with Boeing carried $617M at June 28, 2026. Vulcan Centaur experienced performance problems in Q1 2026 that negatively affected ULA's financial condition. In Q2 Lockheed agreed to guarantee certain ULA borrowings with maximum potential payments of $500M, recognizing a $64M fair-value obligation, and states that it and Boeing expect to provide additional financial support and could incur impairment and operating losses. Maximum identified exposure of ~$1.1B is 0.8% of market cap — modest in isolation, but a new and pre-announced cash call.

**Litigation.** A putative securities class action was filed July 28, 2025 in S.D.N.Y. (amended January 12, 2026) covering purchasers from January 23, 2024 to July 21, 2025, alleging misstatements about classified programs in Aeronautics and MFC and about the F-35, revealed when the estimated losses were announced. Two derivative complaints followed in D. Maryland. Lockheed does not expect these to be material. The class-period bookends run from the Q4 2023 earnings date to the day before the $1.6B charge disclosure. The disclosed loss drivers — customer-driven test plan changes, a discrete event, safety-critical redesign — read as genuinely emergent, and no view on the merits is offered here; the material point is a live case whose subject matter is the estimation process that determines reported margins. The legacy MTA matter (bench trial concluded December 2014, ~$190M sought, ~$95M counterclaimed) remains undecided after more than eleven years.

**Other:** China has sanctioned Lockheed and certain executives over Taiwan FMS; rare-earth sourcing faces U.S. prohibitions plus Chinese export controls; tariffs cost ~$485M of cash flow in FY2025 with ~$140M of refunds through 1H26; 19% of the workforce is under collective bargaining; classified programs cannot be insured for loss to government property. The content of the Q1 2026 10-Q risk-factor update was not obtained, and that quarter spans EO 14372, the Vulcan problems and negative free cash flow.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($720, +22.8%).** Senate §815 is struck or heavily narrowed in conference, restoring the ~$3B annual repurchase channel against $8.3B of authorization and lifting realized shareholder yield back toward 4.5%. THAAD and PAC-3 definitize on terms that convert the UCA value into funded, appropriated backlog, and reconciliation funding passes. MFC mix (14.1% margin) lifts consolidated segment margin above 10% on genuine volume leverage rather than catch-up recognition, validating the guided +28% segment profit growth. Ultra Maritime closes and proves accretive in RMS. F-35 Lot 20 refills the unit backlog and the FY2027 request's increased F-35 quantity holds. On $32.65 FY2027 consensus, 22x gives $718. The analyst high is $756.

**Bear case ($460, –21.5%).** A new reach-forward charge cycle of $1.0–1.5B lands on the classified programs, F-16/C-130/CH-53K, or CMHP terminates with $585M of contract assets at risk — the 1H26 pattern of $395M in new unfavorable adjustments across four unrelated programs makes this a live rather than theoretical scenario. §815 survives conference near its reported form, forcing Lockheed to choose between contract eligibility and its dividend absent a Pentagon-approved investment waiver. Contract assets continue building at the 1H26 pace, converting the free-cash-flow story into a working-capital financing story while $1.9B of accrued losses convert to cash. Reconciliation funding for THAAD and PAC-3 stalls in a politically contested appropriations cycle. On a haircut EPS near $28.50 at 16x — Northrop's current trailing multiple less a discount for execution — the stock is $456. Note this sits below the $503 analyst low, which reflects a view that the sell-side low case does not incorporate a §815 dividend outcome.

**Base case ($620, +5.8%).** Guidance is met near the midpoint ($30.30 GAAP EPS, $7.1B FCF), §815 emerges from conference narrowed with thresholds or program limitations that permit a partial buyback resumption in 2027, MFC volume growth continues at high-single-digit rates while Aeronautics stagnates, and estimate volatility persists at a level that keeps the multiple capped in the high teens. 19x FY2027 consensus $32.65 = $620, consistent with the $614 analyst median and a modest discount to General Dynamics.

Expected value across those three cases is close to the current price. That, rather than any single bearish finding, is why this is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026) is the primary checkpoint, with the NDAA conference outcome — likely between October and December 2026 — as the parallel policy trigger. Ultra Maritime closing (expected Q4 2026) and the FY2027 appropriations/reconciliation path are secondary.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Segment operating margin ex-net-booking-rate-adjustments rises above 9.5% for a full reported half — the first evidence that cost estimation has stabilized rather than swung favorably. Q3 and Q4 2026 disclosures allow this to be computed directly from the segment tables and disclosed adjustments.
2. §815 is struck or narrowed to a threshold/performance-conditioned form in conference **and** repurchases resume at a $500M+ quarterly pace against the $8,324M authorization, restoring total shareholder yield above 4%.
3. A price near $500–520 without fundamental deterioration, which would put the stock at roughly 15.5–16.0x FY2027 consensus and restore an asymmetry that does not currently exist.

**Downgrade triggers back to AVOID:**
1. Any new reach-forward loss provision exceeding $500M in a single quarter, or cumulative new unfavorable adjustments above $700M in FY2026 (versus $395M in 1H26).
2. FY2026 free cash flow tracking below $6.5B — i.e., 2H26 delivering under $3.9B — or contract assets exceeding $18B at year-end, indicating the working-capital build has become structural rather than timing.
3. §815 enacted in substantially its reported categorical form, or Lockheed receiving an adverse performance determination under EO 14372's DFARS clause.
4. CMHP contract termination, or F-35 Lot 20 slipping past mid-2027 with unit backlog below 280.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** This is an upgrade from three consecutive AVOID ratings, and the justification is specific fundamental change rather than price action. The prior thesis rested on falling GAAP EPS, negative free cash flow, spreading reach-forward losses and halted buybacks. Free cash flow reversed to approximately $2.9B in Q2 and guidance was raised on sales, EPS and free cash flow simultaneously; consensus FY2026 and FY2027 EPS have moved up 1.7% and 1.6% over 90 days; and roughly $88B of THAAD and PAC-3 award value has shifted the growth center of gravity into the highest-margin segment. The correctly computed balance sheet shows an Altman Z near 3.6, debt-to-equity of 2.48x and net debt/EBITDA around 1.9x with zero current maturities — the "distress" screen reading was an input artifact and should not inform the rating.

The reasons this stops short of BUY are equally specific. Underlying segment margin was flat at 8.92% across 1H26 and 1H25; the reported expansion is a $1,155M estimate-revision swing, and $395M of new unfavorable adjustments across four unrelated programs indicates the estimation problem is systemic rather than contained. Roughly 20% of FY2025 operating profit came from a pension accounting spread that depletes as frozen plans wind down.
