**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Agilent is a diversified life-sciences tools and diagnostics business exiting a multi-year customer capital-spending downturn. The Q2 FY26 print (quarter ended April 30, 2026) confirmed the recovery: revenue of $1,835M grew 10% year over year, though roughly 4 points came from FX and the headline 58% EPS jump was flattered by a depressed prior-year base (restructuring and investment/pension losses). Stripping those out, core organic growth was closer to 6%, paired with genuine 2-4 point margin expansion from the Ignite cost-out program. Management raised FY26 adjusted guidance in late May, and the stock rose roughly 10% on the print, recovering from a 52-week low of $108.35 hit eight days earlier.

The structural story is a deliberate shift toward higher-quality earnings. CrossLab, the vendor-neutral services and consumables franchise carrying 32% operating margins and recurring, annuity-like revenue, is the strongest asset. Two capital moves reinforce the mix shift: the ~$950M Biocare Medical pathology acquisition (closed June 25, 2026) and the Frederick, Colorado build-out of Advanced Therapeutics oligonucleotide manufacturing. Both push revenue toward recurring, diagnostics-weighted streams, though Advanced Therapeutics is dilutive to gross margin near-term.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of valuation and momentum. At ~$131.46, Agilent trades at ~22x forward EPS ($6.06 current-year consensus), a discount to the peer median (~29.9x). Estimate revisions are essentially flat over 90 days, so the "+30% revision" buy signal is absent. The company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters (albeit by small margins), the sentiment trend is degrading, and a CEO/CFO transition is underway mid-transformation. Agilent is a solid business at a fair-to-full price without the asymmetric catalyst that would justify a max-conviction position. Prior reports rated the name a HOLD three consecutive times; nothing in the current data warrants a change in direction, though the recovery thesis remains intact.

## Company Overview

Agilent provides an integrated portfolio of instruments, software, services, and consumables spanning the laboratory workflow, organized into three reportable segments (Q2 FY26):

- **Life Sciences & Diagnostics Markets (LSDG)** — ~$732M/quarter (40% of revenue), 22.0% operating margin. Liquid chromatography and LC-mass spec, cell analysis, pathology/companion diagnostics, genomics, and the renamed Agilent Advanced Therapeutics business (synthesized oligonucleotides as active pharmaceutical ingredients).
- **Agilent CrossLab (ACG)** — ~$759M/quarter (41%), 32.0% operating margin. Vendor-neutral repair, maintenance, compliance services, columns, and OpenLab software. The recurring-revenue engine, monetizing an installed base that includes competitors' instruments.
- **Applied Markets** — ~$344M/quarter (19%), 23.3% operating margin. Gas chromatography/GC-MS, spectroscopy, vacuum technologies, and remarketed instruments.

End-market exposure: Pharma & Biotech (36%, growing), Chemicals & Advanced Materials (23%, strong), Diagnostics & Clinical (16%, strong), plus Environmental & Forensics, Food, and Academia & Government (the one soft market, tied to constrained U.S. federal funding). Fiscal year ends October 31. Roughly 53% of revenue is non-USD.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026):**
- Revenue $1,835M, +10% YoY (~4 points FX, ~6% core), with growth across all three segments, all regions, and most end markets.
- Net income $339M vs. $215M (+58%); GAAP diluted EPS $1.20 vs. $0.75. The prior-year base was depressed by ~$56M restructuring and ~$43M of investment/pension losses, so the growth rate overstates the underlying trend.
- Gross margin 54.0% (+2 points); GAAP operating margin 21.7% (+4 points).

**First-half FY2026:**
- Revenue $3,633M (+8%); net income $644M (+21%); operating cash flow $545M, down from $652M. The OCF decline is fully explained by higher incentive/variable-pay payouts (~$150M vs. $98M, itself a reflection of FY25 outperformance), higher cash taxes ($320M vs. $248M), and an $85M inventory build. This is timing and mix, not earnings-quality erosion, though inventory days ticked up to 116 from 111 and warrant monitoring as a demand read.

**Balance sheet (4/30/26):** Total assets $13.1B; cash $1.8B; total debt ~$3.4B; net debt ~$1.6B against ~$1.6B annualized net income. Investment-grade and conservatively levered, appropriate for a business with recurring cash flows. Stockholders' equity $7.1B. Goodwill $4.5B (35% of assets), normal for this industry with no impairment triggers identified.

**Capital allocation:** Balanced. YTD FY26: $217M buybacks (with $1.73B remaining under the 2024 authorization), $144M dividends (quarterly dividend raised to $0.255, ~0.8% yield). Capex guided to ~$450M for FY26, elevated by the Advanced Therapeutics manufacturing build-out.

The financial-health profile is strong: expanding margins, ~1x net-debt-to-earnings, high FCF conversion, and a nearly complete restructuring plan targeting ~$90-95M in annual savings. This dimension is a reliable downside filter, and Agilent clears it comfortably.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a real but measured recovery. MD&A frames it directly: "after an extended period of constrained capital spending, many customers' ability to spend capital budgets has begun to normalize." Core organic growth of ~6% in Q2, layered on 2-4 points of margin expansion, is a healthy exit from the trough. Pharma & Biotech (36% of revenue) is growing, Chemicals & Advanced Materials is strong, and Diagnostics & Clinical is strong.

Two deliberate moves extend the growth mix toward recurring and diagnostics-weighted revenue:
- **Biocare Medical** (~$950M, closed 6/25/26) folds a clinical/research pathology business into LSDG, expected accretive to top-line growth, margin, and non-instrument recurring mix in year one.
- **Advanced Therapeutics** manufacturing expansion in Colorado is a bet on nucleic-acid therapeutics demand, dilutive to gross margin near-term but a structurally growing modality.

The key restraint on the growth score is that estimate revisions are flat: current-year consensus of $6.06 is up only 1.4% over 90 days, next-year $6.61 up 0.5%, and the current quarter has been revised down 0.7%. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimate. Growth is durable but modest. The soft spot, U.S. federal/academic funding pressured by government shutdowns and federal workforce reductions, is the one end market actively declining, and given the fiscal environment described in the broader macro backdrop, it is unlikely to reverse near-term.

Weighting growth heavily as the most reliable winner-selection dimension, the read here is 6.0: durable single-digit core growth with margin support, but no accelerating revision signal to lean on.

## Valuation Assessment

At $131.46, Agilent trades at ~22x forward EPS ($6.06). Against the Life Sciences Tools & Services peer median of 29.9x, it is modestly cheap, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Waters at 52.2x, IQVIA at 22.4x, Illumina at 30.1x, Mettler Toledo at 26.8x, Medpace at 29.9x. Agilent sits toward the lower end of the group, cheaper than Mettler Toledo, and its ~6% core growth does not command a premium to the group.

The PEG math is unhelpful for a BUY: 22x forward on ~6-9% total earnings growth (consensus implies FY27 EPS growth of ~9% to $6.61) is a PEG around 2.5, not a value proposition. Analyst targets — mean $159.3, median $159.0, low $135, high $185 — imply ~21% upside to the mean. The guidance on treating targets as floors applies to cheap inflection stocks trading below 30x with rising estimates; here estimates are flat, so the targets read as genuine consensus optimism rather than a stale lagging floor.

The valuation dimension has been reliably anti-predictive in recent windows: cheap-looking names have underperformed, and "cheap" is often a value-trap warning absent a catalyst. Agilent does have one underappreciated upside option: the IEEPA tariff refund claims, filed but booked at zero receivable, which are not in current estimates. That is a genuine but unquantified call option, not enough to justify a high valuation score. Fair value sits close to current price; the multiple is neither stretched nor compelling. Score: 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Agilent competes in a life-sciences-tools oligopoly against Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Waters, Bruker, Sartorius, and Revvity. Its structural differentiator is CrossLab, whose vendor-neutral services and consumables monetize the installed base including competitors' instruments, delivering a 32% margin and recurring, defensive revenue that most pure instrument makers lack. Deep regulatory entrenchment in pharma QA/QC workflows and diversification across six end markets and three geographies round out the moat.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The Biocare acquisition and Advanced Therapeutics expansion deliberately shift the revenue mix toward higher-recurring, higher-diagnostics streams, the direction management is explicitly optimizing for. Against that, APAC is the weakest region (LSDG APAC +2%, CrossLab APAC -1% in Q2), partly a tough comp from a prior-year Chinese consumables pull-forward ahead of tariffs. There is no evidence of share loss; the moat is intact and modestly widening on mix. Prior reports rated competitive position at 7.0-7.5; the current 6.8 reflects a durable but not accelerating position, consistent with the modest decline in that trajectory. Competitive_position has been a weak recent discriminator, so this score is corroborated by the financial-health and growth reads rather than standing alone.

## Risk Assessment

The most company-specific risks:

1. **U.S. federal/academic funding weakness** — the single genuinely soft end market, exposed to government shutdowns and federal workforce reductions. Identifiable and narrow, but unlikely to reverse near-term.
2. **Tariffs (two-sided).** Agilent pays U.S. import tariffs but has filed IEEPA refund claims after the February 2026 Supreme Court invalidation, booking zero receivable, a potential upside not in estimates. Management says supply-chain optimization and targeted pricing have offset cost pressure.
3. **China/APAC softness**, partly a comp artifact from a prior-year consumables pull-forward.
4. **Advanced Therapeutics margin drag and concentration** — lower gross margin than corporate average, plus exposure to pharma clinical-trial cancellation risk.
5. **FX dependence** — ~53% of revenue non-USD; the 4-point Q2 tailwind reverses if the dollar strengthens.
6. **Earnings track record** — three misses in the last four quarters (all small: -1.6% to -2.6%), suggesting slightly deteriorated forward visibility even as the Q2 print beat by 3.8%.

None of these is a binary catastrophe, and the balance sheet insulates against solvency risk entirely. The main uncertainty is execution during a leadership transition (see Management Quality). Risk factors as a scoring dimension carry little predictive weight recently, so this is scored for magnitude of un-priced idiosyncratic risk: modest, with the tariff refund providing asymmetric recovery upside. Score: 6.0.

This neither strengthens nor weakens the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$155-185):** The customer capex recovery accelerates, core organic growth moves toward high-single digits, Ignite savings and mix shift drive margin expansion, Biocare adds recurring diagnostics revenue accretively in year one, and tariff refunds materialize as a bonus not in estimates. On ~$6.61 FY27 EPS at 28x (a peer-median premium justified by improving mix), the stock reaches the low-$180s, matching the $185 street high.

**Bear case (~$108-115):** The recovery stalls, U.S. federal/academic weakness spreads to other end markets, the FX tailwind reverses, Advanced Therapeutics dilutes margins more than expected, and the CEO/CFO transition disrupts execution mid-transformation. Multiple compresses toward 18-20x on flat-to-down estimates, returning the stock to its 52-week low near $108.

**Base case (~$148):** Core growth holds mid-single digits, margins expand modestly, mix improves, and the stock re-rates toward the low end of analyst targets as the recovery is confirmed over two to three quarters. This implies ~13% upside from $131.46, respectable but not the asymmetry that separates a HOLD from a BUY.

### Management Quality

Management is the dimension that has degraded most in the trajectory (7.0 → 5.5 across prior reports), and the current data supports keeping it at 5.5. The signals are mixed rather than negative. Positives: a blue-chip board (Pascal Soriot, Mikael Dolsten, Dow Wilson); a disciplined capital-allocation record (buybacks with headroom, a raised dividend, a substantially complete restructuring); and a compensation redesign for FY26 that discontinued LTPP-EPS and stock options in favor of TSR-based awards with an FY27 operating-margin gate, a genuine alignment shift toward relative performance and margin discipline.

The offsets: a CEO/CFO transition mid-transformation (new CFO Adam Elinoff signed the Q2 10-Q; predecessor Bret DiMarco departed via a March 2026 separation agreement), and no open-market C-suite buying to signal conviction. Insider activity is otherwise routine (tax-withholding dispositions, director grants), with two modest discretionary director sales by Mikael Dolsten (2,600 shares at ~$149.81 in December near the cycle high, 1,600 at ~$135.42 in May). The sales are small and non-alarming, but the absence of any bullish open-market purchase means management quality provides no forward tailwind. The transition risk is the item to track.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a durable, single-digit core-growth compounder re-rating modestly as the capex recovery confirms, with mix improving toward recurring diagnostics revenue. There is a positive 12-month path to the high-$140s to low-$150s, but not enough asymmetry, given flat revisions, to justify a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (current-year consensus rising >5% over 90 days) alongside confirmed core organic growth accelerating above 8%.
- A pullback toward the low-$120s or below, which would restore ~25%+ upside to the mean target.
- Tariff refund claims recognized as a receivable, or Biocare integration confirmed accretive ahead of plan, adding to estimates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Core organic growth decelerates back below 3% or a fourth consecutive EPS miss with widening magnitude, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- U.S. federal/academic weakness spreads to Pharma & Biotech or Chemicals end markets.
- Execution disruption from the CEO/CFO transition (guidance cut, unexpected additional senior departures, or Ignite savings shortfall).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY26, expected late August 2026), which will provide the first read on Biocare integration and whether the raised guidance holds. The tariff refund resolution is an open-ended second catalyst.

## Conclusion

Agilent is a high-quality, investment-grade life-sciences tools business emerging from a capex downturn, anchored by the defensive CrossLab recurring-revenue engine and steadily improving its earnings mix through the Biocare acquisition and Advanced Therapeutics expansion. Financial health is strong and the recovery is real. The constraints are a full-market multiple that offers limited re-rating room, flat estimate revisions, a soft federal/academic end market, three of four recent quarters missing consensus, and a leadership transition mid-transformation. Agilent is a well-run compounder at a fair price, worth holding for the ~13% base-case path to the high-$140s, but lacking the accelerating-revision, cheap-inflection profile that would warrant a BUY. HOLD, with upgrade contingent on either a positive revision turn or a pullback that restores asymmetry.
