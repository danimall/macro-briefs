**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Burlington is the #3 US off-price retailer, executing a credible multi-year margin-recovery and store-expansion program under CEO Michael O'Sullivan. The core financial evidence is strong: revenue rose from $8.68B (FY2022) to $11.55B (FY2025), net income grew 2.65x to $610M, and net margin expanded from 2.7% to 5.2% over three years on gross-margin gains (40.5%→43.8%) and SG&A leverage. Q1 FY2026 (reported May 28) delivered +6% comparable sales against a 2-4% guide and +26% EPS, prompting management to raise FY2026 EPS guidance to $11.45-$11.80 and reaffirm ~115 net new stores. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, and current-quarter EPS estimates have risen +12.3% over the past 30 days.

The valuation is where the case gets harder. At $313.18, BURL trades at roughly 27x the midpoint of FY2026 EPS guidance and ~23x the consensus FY2027 estimate of $13.79, a meaningful premium to the apparel-retail peer median P/E of 14.9x. Some of that premium is defensible: Burlington's growth runway (targeting ~2,000 stores from 1,212) and margin-catch-up trajectory versus TJX and Ross are real and differentiate it from the discount-multiple names (GAP at 8.7x, ANF at 8.7x) that carry structural decline concerns. The stock sits below its 52-week high of $351.85 for reasons more about a rich starting multiple than any fundamental deterioration, and the consensus price target range ($300 low / $435 high, $367 mean) implies modest base-case upside against a Consumer Discretionary macro where the low-income core customer is the demographic most exposed to spending pressure.

We rate BURL a HOLD (growth_hold). The operational execution is real and the estimate-revision momentum is positive, but the multiple already prices continued beats, leaving asymmetry that favors accumulating on weakness rather than chasing at ~27x forward. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD in this coverage; the fundamentals continue to improve, but nothing in the current data warrants an upgrade to BUY absent either a valuation reset toward the mid-$200s or a step-change in comp durability through H2.

## Company Overview

Burlington operates 1,212 stores across 46 states, D.C., and Puerto Rico (as of January 31, 2026), selling first-quality, current-brand apparel, home, accessories, and coats at up to 60% off other retailers' prices. The business model is opportunistic, in-season "chase" buying: purchasing close to need to preserve flexibility across vendors and categories and to negotiate favorable terms, the same "treasure hunt" model that TJX (TJ Maxx/Marshalls/HomeGoods) and Ross Stores run at larger scale and higher margin.

Two structural features define the equity story. First, Burlington is essentially brick-and-mortar only, with no meaningful e-commerce. This is a cost advantage (no fulfillment infrastructure to support) and a structural vulnerability (no omnichannel offset if store traffic softens). Second, the company is the designated growth/margin-catch-up name in the off-price group. Management targets roughly 2,000 stores long-term, opening ~100+ net new stores annually, and has shifted toward smaller-format (~25,000 sq ft) locations in strip and power centers, downsizing legacy oversized boxes to reduce occupancy cost. Burlington has been acquiring leases opportunistically out of retail bankruptcies, capturing prime real estate in a supply-constrained environment at the cost of near-term pre-opening drag ($35.5M in FY2025 versus $15.7M in FY2024).

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is the crux of the bull case and it holds up under scrutiny.

| Net income ($M) | 230 | 340 | 504 | 610 |

| Op cash flow ($M) | — | — | 863 | 1,231 |

Revenue is up nearly 33% over three years. FY2025 growth was split between new/non-comp stores (+$691M) and comps (+2%, +$241M). The margin expansion is the proof point: gross margin has risen three consecutive years on improved merchandise margin and lower freight, while SG&A improved to 33.1% of sales on supply-chain efficiency and payroll discipline. Adjusted EBIT reached $887M (+19%) and Adjusted EBITDA $1.29B (+18%). Operating margin is structurally up roughly 250bps over three years, narrowing the historical gap to TJX and Ross.

Cash flow quality is solid. Operating cash flow of $1.23B in FY2025 covers heavy capex, but free cash flow is thin during the current investment phase: capex ran $1.06B, driven by distribution-center buildout (a purchased California DC, a Georgia DC under construction, and an Arizona DC starting). Management has guided FY2026 capex down to ~$875M, which should improve FCF conversion as the DC investment cycle normalizes.

The balance sheet is manageable. Total debt is ~$2.0B against $1.8B equity and $1.23B cash. The structure is a $1.72B Term Loan (5.4%, maturing 2031, $1.1B swapped to fixed) plus $297M of 1.25% convertible notes due 2027, with an undrawn $1.0B ABL. Interest coverage is comfortable, with interest expense of $71M against $887M adjusted EBIT. The real leverage sits in operating leases: $5.05B of commitments plus $537M for 96 committed-but-not-yet-occupied stores. Substantially all stores are leased, which is normal for the model, but it means the pressure point in a downturn is occupancy deleverage, not interest expense.

Capital allocation reflects the reinvestment priority: no dividend, and modest buybacks ($251M repurchased in FY2025, with a new $500M authorization through May 2027, $385M remaining). Share count has declined slowly (66.5M in FY2021 to 62.7M in FY2025). The equity story therefore depends entirely on continued execution of the store-growth and margin-expansion plan rather than on capital return.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two engines: unit expansion and margin catch-up. On units, management is opening ~115 net new stores in FY2026 against a ~2,000-store long-term target, a runway that credibly supports high-single-digit revenue growth for several years. The smaller-format shift and opportunistic bankruptcy-lease acquisitions lower occupancy cost per store while accelerating the buildout.

On comps, the picture is the key tension. Full-year comparable sales decelerated (+4% FY2023, +4% FY2024, +2% FY2025) before snapping back to +6% in Q1 FY2026. Bulls read the FY2025 slowdown as weather and macro noise, and the Q1 re-acceleration supports that interpretation. Whether it holds through the heavier H2 (back-to-school and holiday) is the variable to watch, particularly given the low-income consumer's exposure to macro spending pressure.

The estimate-revision picture is constructive and warrants explicit weighting. Current-quarter consensus EPS has risen +12.3% over 30 days (to $2.16), current-year EPS +3.0% (to $11.80), and next-year EPS +2.2% (to $13.79). This is broad-based positive revision momentum, though not the >30% velocity that signals a genuine trough-to-recovery move; Burlington is a steady compounder with improving estimates, not a distressed name re-rating off a bottom. The company has beaten consensus in all four of the last four quarters (surprises of 10.9%, 1.0%, 7.6%, and 18.1%), which warrants above-average confidence in the current guidance range. One caution: next-quarter EPS was revised down slightly (-2.8% over 30 days), consistent with the H2 comp-durability question.

Growth prospects are the strongest dimension here: the combination of a long unit runway, an intact margin-expansion program, and a documented beat streak makes this the most reliable feature of the story.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward multiple is well below the reported trailing P/E of 32.2x because trailing EPS ($9.51) understates forward earnings power; this is a business with accelerating earnings, so forward metrics are the appropriate lens.

Against apparel-retail peers, Burlington carries a clear premium: peer median P/E is 14.9x, with GAP at 8.7x, URBN at 14.9x, BOOT at 23.8x, VSCO at 28.5x, and ANF at 8.7x. Much of this dispersion reflects structural quality differences: the discount names (GAP, ANF) carry secular or fashion-cycle concerns that Burlington's off-price model largely avoids, and off-price as a category has taken share from department stores for over a decade. Adjusting for growth, Burlington's PEG on the ~10% consensus EPS CAGR to FY2027 sits near 2.3x on the forward multiple, which is not cheap.

The honest read is that the multiple already prices continued execution. This is a >30x-trailing / ~27x-forward stock, which sits in the zone where analyst targets are not independent confirmation of upside; the consensus $367 mean and $375 median partly reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. The low target of $300 sits below the current price, and the high of $435 requires the margin-catch-up story to run further and comps to sustain the Q1 pace through H2. Base-case upside to the consensus mean is roughly 17%, respectable but not asymmetric given the Consumer Discretionary macro headwind on the core customer.

We score valuation at 5.2: not a value trap (the growth and quality justify a premium to the sector), but not offering the mispricing that would support a BUY. Cheapness on a low multiple is not the issue; the issue is that the premium is fair rather than opportunistic at the current price. A pullback toward the mid-$200s would restore the asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Off-price retail is a structurally advantaged, defensive-growth niche within apparel. It feeds on excess inventory from full-price channels, appeals to value-conscious shoppers in both good and bad economies, and has taken share from department stores for more than a decade. Burlington's differentiated angle is that it is the margin-catch-up story in the group: historically lower operating margins than TJX and Ross, now closing the gap via leaner inventories, faster chase, smaller stores, and supply-chain investment.

Management frames the macro as a two-sided winner: if inflation eases, the low-income core benefits; if growth slows, higher-income shoppers trade down into off-price. That framing is broadly correct and is a genuine competitive advantage of the model. The structural gaps versus peers are the absence of e-commerce (a rising channel Burlington does not participate in) and reliance on power-center co-tenancy, where anchor closures can reduce traffic.

Competitive position is improving on the margin-catch-up dimension and stable-to-improving on unit share, but the trajectory is not accelerating in a way that would push the score above the low-7s; the comp deceleration in FY2025 and the omnichannel gap temper it. We score competitive position at 6.8, consistent with a solid #3 player narrowing its margin gap but facing a structural channel disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff / IEEPA legal wildcard (timely, two-sided).** The FY2025 10-K discloses that on February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court issued a ruling limiting tariff authority under the IEEPA, creating uncertainty about potential refunds of previously assessed tariffs, with amount and timing "uncertain." Burlington buys mostly from US-based vendors, but the underlying merchandise is produced overseas, so it is indirectly exposed. This is a genuine two-sided factor: potential refunds (upside) versus continued regime uncertainty (risk) that warrants monitoring for quantification in coming quarters.

**Low-income consumer sensitivity.** Management explicitly identifies the low-income shopper as the core customer, vulnerable to inflation, reduced government benefits, and lower tax refunds. This risk is closely aligned with the current macro backdrop. The 10-K also newly emphasizes immigration-enforcement risk "particularly in regions with a relatively high concentration of Hispanic customers," an unusually specific demographic disclosure that flags a real, non-generic exposure.

**No e-commerce.** Brick-and-mortar-only is a structural vulnerability against omnichannel peers if store traffic softens.

**Execution risk on expansion.** The 2,000-store goal, three-DC buildout, and simultaneous supply-chain modernization stress operational resources, and new stores in existing markets can cannibalize comps.

**Lease leverage.** The $5.05B operating-lease obligation is the true fixed cost; occupancy deleverage is the downturn pressure point, not interest expense.

**Convertible dilution.** The 2027 converts (conversion price ~$205.93) are in the money at ~$313. A March 2026 exchange of $111M of notes for $128.6M cash plus 150,831 shares partially de-risks this.

Overall, risk factors score 5.5: several specific, unpriced or partially-priced idiosyncratic risks (IEEPA, immigration-enforcement demographic exposure, macro-consumer exposure on the core customer) sit alongside a fundamentally sound balance sheet. The two-sided IEEPA item and the macro-consumer exposure keep this below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent rather than defensive. Near-term ATM IV is 33.8% versus 38.2% one-year historical volatility, leaving IV cheap on a -4.4% spread and in the 16th percentile of the current chain; options are inexpensive here, which favors entry timing if one were building a position. Put/call skew is -14.1% (call-heavy), and the put/call volume ratio of 2.08 alongside an OI ratio of 36.86 is a mixed signal that, combined with the call-heavy skew, does not indicate elevated downside hedging. The term structure is in contango (near-term below long-term 37.1%), a normal, non-stressed configuration. The most notable shift is IV collapsing from 108.3% (May 26, pre-earnings) to 33.8% post-print; the Q1 beat resolved the near-term catalyst and vol has normalized. There is no options-implied divergence that challenges the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($400-435):** Q1 FY2026's +6% comp re-acceleration holds through H2, FY2026 EPS lands at or above the $11.80 top of guidance, and the margin-catch-up story continues to narrow the gap to TJX/Ross. The ~2,000-store runway sustains high-single-digit revenue growth, capex normalizes as the DC buildout completes (improving FCF), and an IEEPA tariff refund provides an unexpected tailwind. The multiple holds at ~30x forward as consensus estimates revise upward toward $14+ FY2027 EPS. This puts the stock at roughly $400-435, matching the consensus high target.

**Bear case ($235-265):** The macro consumer backdrop hits the low-income core customer, comps decelerate back toward flat or negative, and the FY2025 slowdown (4%→4%→2%) resumes rather than the Q1 re-acceleration persisting. Occupancy deleverage on the $5.05B lease base pressures operating margin, the premium multiple compresses toward the sector median as the growth premium erodes, and tariff uncertainty weighs on merchandise margin. At ~18-20x a flat-to-down FY2026 EPS, the stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $235.

**Base case ($335-360):** Comps run in the +3-5% range for FY2026, EPS lands near the $11.63 guidance midpoint to $11.80, unit growth proceeds at ~115 net new stores, and the multiple holds near ~28-30x forward. Positive estimate revisions continue at a moderate pace. This delivers roughly 7-15% appreciation over 12 months, consistent with the consensus mean of $367 partially discounted for the macro-consumer risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold), 12-month horizon.**

This is a growth-hold: a positive multi-year execution story with continuing margin expansion and unit growth, but the current ~27x forward multiple leaves insufficient asymmetry to justify chasing at $313.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback toward the mid-$200s (below ~22x forward EPS) on macro-driven weakness rather than fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Two consecutive quarters of comps at +5% or better through H2 FY2026, confirming the Q1 re-acceleration is durable rather than a one-quarter print.
- IEEPA tariff refund quantified as a material EPS tailwind, or FY2026 EPS guidance raised above $12.00.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps turn negative for a quarter, or full-year comp guidance is cut below +2%, signaling the low-income consumer environment is deteriorating.
- Gross margin reverses (falls below ~43%), indicating the margin-expansion thesis has stalled or tariffs are compressing merchandise margin.
- A guidance cut on FY2026 EPS below the current $11.45 floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 earnings report (approximately late August 2026), which will test whether the +6% Q1 comp holds into the seasonally heavier back half. The IEEPA refund quantification, if it surfaces in interim filings, is a secondary catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

Burlington is a well-run off-price retailer executing a genuine margin-recovery and store-expansion program, with the financial evidence supporting the growth thesis. The trajectory of scores across prior coverage (6.4 → 6.5 → 6.4) reflects steady, incremental improvement, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend; management quality softened modestly on the pay-versus-TSR gap and routine option-exercise selling, while valuation and growth prospects held firm.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation discipline in an unfavorable sector. At ~27x forward EPS, the multiple prices continued execution, and the Consumer Discretionary macro, where the low-income core customer faces the sharpest spending pressure, argues for a higher bar of company-specific outperformance before adding. The stock offers roughly 7-15% base-case upside over 12 months, respectable but not asymmetric, and the absence of an entry-point discount is the binding constraint. Accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$200s; hold at current levels.
