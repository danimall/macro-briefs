**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BrightSpring Health Services is a home- and community-based healthcare platform with two segments: Pharmacy Solutions (~88% of revenue, dominated by infusion/specialty and long-term-care pharmacy) and higher-margin Provider Services (~12%, home health, hospice, rehab, personal care). The 2026 story is a portfolio-focusing and deleveraging execution that has coincided with a genuine fundamental inflection. Q1 2026 revenue rose 25.6% to $3.61B, Adjusted EBITDA rose 44.8% to $189.8M, and — the most important data point — Pharmacy gross margin expanded to 9.5% from 8.1% as specialty/infusion mix flipped from dilutive to accretive. The March 2026 Community Living divestiture generated $810.9M in cash, lifted liquidity to roughly $1.37B, and cut covenant leverage to 2.27x from 2.99x at year-end and ~4.2x a year earlier.

The market has already priced much of this. The stock has moved from a $19.01 low to ~$71.5, a 252% advance, and now trades at ~93x trailing GAAP EPS. On the more relevant forward basis it sits at roughly 42x current-year consensus EPS ($1.69) and ~34x next-year ($2.13), a meaningful premium to the Health Care Services peer-median P/E of ~23x. Estimate revisions are positive (+8.7% current-year and +8.9% next-year over 90 days), and the beat track record is strong (three beats of four, two of them 20%+). The forward multiple is defensible for a mid-teens EBITDA compounder with real deleveraging, but it does not embed the asymmetric upside a BUY requires at this price.

The offsetting factors keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The Inflation Reduction Act is actively shrinking the legacy Home & Community Pharmacy line (management attributed a 9.2% YoY revenue decline directly to Part D price negotiation and rebates), and ~30% of consolidated revenue sits in Part D. KKR continues to exit via secondaries (two placements in four months, most recently ~14.7M shares at $58.45), leaving ~27M shares of finite overhang. The tangible-equity-unit structure mandatorily converts into 26–31M shares in February 2027, so the fully-diluted base is ~221M, not the ~193M issued. With the stock near the top of the $49–$90 analyst range and options pricing an imminent ±13.6% move into earnings, the risk/reward is roughly balanced.

## Company Overview

BrightSpring serves roughly 475,000 patients daily across all 50 states via ~12,400 clinical providers and pharmacists. It was assembled from KKR and Walgreens' 2017 buyout of PharMerica and 2019 acquisition of BrightSpring, IPO'd in January 2024, and has since seen Walgreens fully exit and KKR reduce its stake aggressively.

**Pharmacy Solutions** is the revenue engine. It splits into Infusion & Specialty Pharmacy (~73% of the segment, the fastest-growing piece on specialty branded drugs, oncology, and home infusion) and Home & Community Pharmacy (the legacy PharMerica long-term-care/assisted-living/hospice book). This is a high-volume, low-margin, pass-through-heavy business: Q1 2026 revenue per script was $295.56 (+27% YoY) while gross profit per script was only $28.03. Reported revenue growth is flattered by branded-drug pass-through — Q1 script volume was actually down 1.4% while revenue per script rose 27%.

**Provider Services** is smaller but structurally higher-margin (segment EBITDA margin ~15% vs ~5% for Pharmacy), spanning home health, hospice, rehab, personal care, and a growing home-based primary care/ACO effort. The 110-branch Amedisys/LHC home-health/hospice acquisition (bought from the UnitedHealth/Amedisys antitrust divestiture) contributed $78.5M of revenue and $7.4M of operating income in Q1 2026.

The strategic thesis is integrated care — co-locating pharmacy and provider services for high-need senior and specialty patients to build referral density and payor value. Cross-sell is still largely aspirational: only ~9,000 patients currently receive multiple integrated services.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was ~$12.9B (+14.6% YoY on a continuing-ops basis restated for the Community Living divestiture), with a swing to net income of ~$190.7M. The Altman Z-Score sat near 3.0 (safe zone), and leverage was the principal watch item.

Q1 2026 marked a clear inflection:
- Revenue +25.6% to $3.61B (Pharmacy +25.2%, Provider +27.9%)
- Gross profit +42.5% to $482.2M; consolidated gross margin expanded to 13.3% from 11.8%
- Operating income +139% to $121.4M
- Adjusted EBITDA +44.8% to $189.8M
- Net income from continuing ops of $74.3M ($0.34 diluted) vs $9.2M prior year; Adjusted EPS $0.39 vs $0.19
- Pharmacy gross margin up to 9.5% from 8.1% — the reversal of the FY2025 compression story, driven by favorable specialty/infusion mix

Balance sheet and cash flow improved materially. The Community Living divestiture closed 3/30/2026 for $810.9M cash (a $103.4M pre-tax gain), lifting cash to $888.8M from $88.4M and total liquidity to ~$1.37B. Debt stands at ~$2.56B (First Lien Term Loan B-5 at SOFR+2.50%, maturity Feb 2031; $475M revolver undrawn), with $1.5B notional interest-rate swaps hedging ~60% of floating exposure. Covenant leverage fell to 2.27x from 2.99x at year-end. Operating cash flow was $122.9M in a seasonally light quarter (dampened by $48.5M of incentive payouts). Working capital is intensive: A/R rose to $1.11B and inventory is a large swing item, so cash conversion should be judged over full years rather than quarters.

Capital allocation has begun to include buybacks — 1.46M shares repurchased for $60M alongside the March 2026 secondary, at a discount to market. The TEU dilution overhang is real: the tangible equity units mandatorily convert on 2/1/2027 into 26.2M–30.8M shares. Diluted share count is already ~221M against ~193M issued, and any per-share valuation should use the higher figure.

The financial-health picture supports a mid-6s score: strong FCF trajectory, sharply reduced leverage appropriate for a services roll-up, hedged floating-rate exposure, and no near-term maturity wall. The offsets are working-capital intensity and a debt load that, while manageable, still exposes ~$10M of annual cash flow to each 100bp of unhedged rate movement.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is broad-based but must be read past the headline. Consolidated revenue +25.6% is heavily inflated by specialty branded-drug pass-through; the durable signal is Adjusted EBITDA +45% and gross-profit-per-script +49.5%. The margin inflection — Pharmacy gross margin moving to 9.5% from 8.1% — is the fundamental change that explains the re-rating, and it directly contradicts the FY2025 fear that scaling low-margin specialty would compress profitability.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-year EPS revised +8.7% and next-year +8.9% over 90 days, current quarter +9.7%. The magnitude here (~9%) is meaningful but below the +30% threshold that would signal a systematic underestimate of an inflection; it reads as consensus catching up to a solid beat-and-raise rather than a step change. The earnings track record (three beats of four, with 41.6% and 26.3% surprises) warrants above-average confidence in the current $1.69/$2.13 EPS path.

Growth drivers are credible and secular: aging demographics, care migration to lower-cost home/community settings, specialty/infusion expansion, tuck-in M&A (the Amedisys/LHC branches being the clearest example), and the higher-margin Provider Services segment growing 27.9% in Q1. The offsetting drag is the IRA, which is quietly shrinking the legacy Home & Community Pharmacy book (down 9.2% YoY, explicitly IRA-attributed). Specialty growth currently masks this erosion; if specialty decelerates, the IRA drag becomes more visible in the reported growth rate. The world model's Health Care M&A theme is supportive here — patent-cliff-driven consolidation and care-setting migration are durable tailwinds, though the sector's BUY alpha has historically clustered near market-neutral, which argues against extrapolating the theme into a valuation premium.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~93x is not the relevant metric — it reflects GAAP earnings depressed by prior-period losses and non-cash items, and the business is inflecting. On forward consensus the stock trades at ~42x current-year EPS ($1.69) and ~34x next-year ($2.13). Against a Health Care Services peer-median P/E of ~23x (DVA 19.6x, CHE 23.0x, SHC 40.1x), BTSG carries a clear premium.

The premium is partly justified: BTSG is growing Adjusted EBITDA ~45% off a deleveraging balance sheet, versus DVA (mature, ~20x, low growth) or CHE (steady but not accelerating). A PEG framed on ~15% durable EBITDA growth and rising EPS estimates lands near 2, which is full rather than cheap. The stock currently trades at $71.5 against a mean analyst target of $76.29 and median of $80, with a range of $49–$90. Proximity to the mean target, combined with a forward multiple above 30x, means the "analyst targets are floors" logic does not apply cleanly — this is not a sub-30x-forward, sub-1.5-PEG inflection name where consensus reliably lags. At ~34–42x forward, the multiple already prices continued beats, so the asymmetry is unfavorable: beats are expected (limited re-rating room), misses would derate the stock hard.

The valuation dimension also has to be weighed against the empirical caution that cheapness has been anti-predictive and, conversely, that a premium multiple is not automatically a sell. Here the issue is not that the multiple is high per se, but that at this price the identified positives (margin inflection, deleveraging) are visible and largely reflected, while the identified negatives (IRA erosion, TEU dilution, KKR supply) are real and unpriced-in-full. That combination supports a valuation score in the mid-4s — full valuation with balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable asymmetry, not a value trap and not a bargain.

Base-case fair value of ~$76 uses ~36x the next-year $2.13 consensus, consistent with the analyst mean. Bull case ~$90 (analyst high) assumes specialty margin gains compound and IRA drag stays masked. Bear case ~$49 (analyst low) reflects an IRA acceleration or specialty deceleration that exposes the legacy erosion, plus TEU dilution.

## Competitive Landscape

BrightSpring competes in fragmented home-based care and specialty/infusion pharmacy against Option Care Health (infusion), Amedisys/Enhabit/Addus (home health and personal care), and various pharmacy players. Its edge is scale and breadth — the largest integrated home/community platform, with the ability to acquire divested assets (the Amedisys/LHC branches) that antitrust actions force onto the market.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The margin inflection in Pharmacy suggests the specialty/infusion pivot is delivering scale economics, and Provider Services momentum (+27.9%) is running ahead of the market. The board addition of Steven Miller, a healthcare/PBM executive, signals a strategic focus on the pharmacy-value chain. The competitive-position score of 6.2 reflects the prior-report decline from 7.0 to 6.3, which I read as appropriate rather than continuing to erode: the integrated-care cross-sell remains more aspiration than realized synergy (~9,000 of 475,000 patients receiving multiple services), and the IRA structurally shrinks the legacy pharmacy franchise. Those two facts cap the moat trajectory even as execution improves elsewhere. The company is a scale leader with a shrinking legacy tail and a growing specialty/provider front — net stable.

## Risk Assessment

**IRA / Medicare Part D — the dominant structural risk.** Part D is ~30% of consolidated revenue. Management explicitly attributed the 9.2% YoY decline in Home & Community Pharmacy to mandatory Medicare drug price negotiation, rebates, and statutory caps. As more drugs enter negotiation and caps deepen, this is a persistent, likely-growing headwind on the legacy pharmacy base. It is currently masked by specialty growth; any specialty deceleration exposes it.

**Reimbursement/government dependency.** Substantially all revenue flows from Medicare/Medicaid/commercial payors; top 10 states account for 52% of revenue. Rate cuts, managed-care shifts, and post-payment audits are recurring threats.

**Leverage and floating rate.** Even post-deleveraging, $2.56B of debt with ~40% unhedged floating exposure means a +100bp move cuts ~$10M of annual cash flow. The 2031 maturity removes near-term refinancing risk.

**KKR overhang.** KKR sold ~19.7M shares at $40.96 in March and ~14.7M at $58.45 in June, leaving ~27M shares. This is a finite, well-understood technical supply event, and the placements have cleared at rising prices — constructive, but expect periodic further secondaries that create near-term pressure.

**TEU dilution.** Mandatory conversion in February 2027 crystallizes 26–31M shares; the ~221M fully-diluted base should anchor per-share math.

**Legal/regulatory.** The 10-Q states no material change to risk factors and no expected material adverse effect, but flags "reasonably possible" period impacts. Historically PharMerica-era False Claims Act and Controlled Substances matters have surfaced; this is a residual tail worth monitoring.

**Integration.** The Amedisys/LHC acquisition remains in purchase accounting (provisional through Dec 2026), with three branches yet to close.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$90):** The margin inflection compounds — specialty/infusion mix continues to lift Pharmacy gross margin, Provider Services grows double-digits, and tuck-in M&A (Amedisys-style divested assets) adds accretive branches. Deleveraging to sub-2x frees capital for buybacks and deals. IRA erosion stays masked by specialty growth. Consensus EPS continues to revise higher toward $2.20+, and the stock holds a ~40x forward multiple as a mid-teens EBITDA compounder. KKR fully exits, removing the overhang, and TEU conversion is absorbed. This delivers ~26% upside.

**Bear Case (~$49):** IRA price negotiation accelerates and Part D erosion widens beyond the 9.2% legacy-pharmacy decline; specialty growth decelerates, exposing the shrinking base. The forward multiple compresses toward the peer median as the growth premium fades. TEU dilution plus continued KKR supply pressures the share count and float. A legal/regulatory matter from the PharMerica era surfaces with a material settlement. This delivers ~31% downside.

The base case (~$76) sits between them at ~36x the $2.13 next-year consensus, roughly the analyst mean, implying ~6% upside from $71.5. The distribution is close to symmetric with a slight downside skew given the multiple already prices the visible good news.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **HOLD** with a **growth_hold** rationale — a positive 12-month growth thesis is intact (margin inflection, deleveraging, secular demand), but the current price offers insufficient asymmetry for a fresh BUY, and the recommendation has now been HOLD across four consecutive reports.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$50s to low-$60s (e.g., on a KKR secondary or general healthcare weakness) that resets forward P/E toward ~28–30x on rising estimates
- Two consecutive quarters of sustained Pharmacy gross-margin expansion (holding above ~9.5%) with Provider Services growth staying double-digit, confirming the inflection is durable rather than a one-quarter mix benefit
- Evidence that specialty growth is more than offsetting IRA erosion at the consolidated level, with EPS revisions re-accelerating above +15% over 90 days

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- IRA-driven Home & Community Pharmacy decline widening materially (beyond ~9.2%) while specialty growth decelerates, exposing base erosion
- A guidance cut, a missed quarter that breaks the beat streak, or Pharmacy gross margin reverting below ~8.5%
- A material adverse legal/regulatory development from the PharMerica-era contingencies
- Forward P/E expanding above ~45x on flattening estimates (multiple outrunning fundamentals)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (implied ±13.6% move priced by options) is the near-term catalyst; the February 2027 TEU conversion is the next structural checkpoint for the diluted-share base.

## Conclusion

BrightSpring has executed one of the cleaner 2026 healthcare-services de-riskings: a well-timed divestiture that cut leverage to 2.27x and added ~$1.37B of liquidity, paired with a genuine Q1 margin and EBITDA inflection that reversed the FY2025 compression fear. The fundamentals have improved, the trajectory of scores confirms it (financial health up +1.3 across recent reports), and the earnings/estimate momentum is real.

The recommendation stays HOLD because the price has done most of the work. At ~$71.5, up 252% off its low and near the top of the $49–$90 analyst range at ~34–42x forward EPS, the market has absorbed the good news while the two live negatives — IRA erosion of ~30% Part D exposure and Feb-2027 TEU dilution atop a still-active KKR overhang — are not fully priced. That combination yields roughly symmetric risk/reward with a slight downside skew and rich near-term implied vol that argues against chasing. The right posture is to hold the growth thesis, monitor the margin durability and IRA trajectory closely, and add on the recurring supply-driven pullbacks that this ownership structure reliably produces.
