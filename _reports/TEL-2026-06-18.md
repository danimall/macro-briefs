**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TE Connectivity is a highly-engineered components company, one of the world's largest makers of connectors, terminals, and sensors, that is transforming from an auto-cyclical supplier into an AI-infrastructure growth story. Despite its "Electronic Manufacturing Services" classification, TEL is a design-in components leader with switching-cost moats rather than a contract assembler. The reporting structure now splits roughly evenly between Transportation Solutions (~51% of sales, anchored by automotive) and a fast-growing Industrial Solutions segment (~49%) whose digital data networks line is compounding at 46-48% organic on AI and cloud demand.

The fundamental trajectory is improving. Q2 FY2026 revenue grew 14.5% YoY to $4,744M (+7.2% organic), first-half revenue rose 18.0%, operating margin expanded to 20.1% from 18.1%, and gross margin reached 36.8%. Operating cash flow for the half was $1,812M. The company raised its buyback authorization by $3.0B, lifted the dividend ~12% to $0.78/quarter, and replaced its $1.5B revolver with a $3.0B facility, signaling continued M&A and return capacity. On adjusted earnings power of roughly $11.24 (current FY) to $12.64 (next FY), the stock at ~$212 trades at 18.9x and 16.8x respectively, below the low end of the analyst target range ($220) and at a discount to its closest quality peer, Amphenol [unverified — Amphenol multiple not in sources].

The setup is a BUY, with disciplined sizing. The combination of a confirmed, broad-based AI infrastructure demand cycle, a quality moat, margin expansion, reasonable valuation, and shareholder-friendly capital allocation outweighs the two genuine concerns: an automotive core in organic decline (-3.8% in Q2) and AI growth that concentrates in a handful of hyperscaler customers TEL itself warns could in-source connectivity. The ~7% post-earnings drop and "fairly valued" sentiment reflect expectations catching up, but the price sits below the consensus floor while estimates stay stable-to-positive, which improves risk/reward.

## Company Overview

TE Connectivity designs and manufactures connectivity and sensor solutions that distribute power, signal, and data across automotive, industrial, data-center, aerospace/defense, energy, and medical end-markets. The company reincorporated in Ireland at the start of FY2025 (previously Switzerland; originally spun from Tyco), with debt issued through subsidiary Tyco Electronics Group S.A.

Two segments drive the business:

- **Transportation Solutions (~51% of sales):** automotive connectors and sensors (73-75% of the segment, ~41% of total company sales), commercial transportation, and sensors. The structural driver here is content per vehicle, more electronics per car, offsetting flat-to-declining global vehicle units.
- **Industrial Solutions (~49% and rising):** digital data networks (AI/data center), automation and connected living, aerospace/defense/marine, energy (grid), and medical. This is the growth engine, expanding 27% in Q2 (16.9% organic).

The moat rests on global manufacturing scale, a broad product catalog, deep design-in relationships with automotive OEMs and hyperscalers, and switching costs once a component is engineered into a customer's design. The business historically absorbs 1-2% annual price erosion, offset by productivity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating, led by Industrial and AI.** FY2025 revenue was ~$17.3B (+~9% YoY). Q2 FY2026 net sales reached $4,744M, up 14.5% YoY (+7.2% organic, +4.4% FX, +2.9% acquisition). First-half revenue was $9,413M, up 18.0% (+11.0% organic). Industrial Solutions grew 27.0% in Q2; within it, digital data networks grew 48% (46% organic) in Q2 and 59% in the first half, attributed primarily to AI and cloud applications. Energy grew 59.5% (largely the Richards Manufacturing acquisition, +11% organic). Transportation grew 4.7%, but automotive organic was -3.8% on weak global vehicle production, partly offset by content gains and FX.

**Margins are expanding meaningfully.** Q2 gross margin rose to 36.8% from 35.2%; first-half gross margin was 37.0% versus 35.3%. Q2 operating margin reached 20.1% from 18.1%, with Industrial Solutions operating margin jumping to 19.4% from 16.6% and Transportation to 20.8%. The improvement reflects volume leverage and manufacturing productivity, aided by lower restructuring charges ($20M in 1H versus $95M prior year).

**GAAP earnings are distorted by taxes — use operating metrics.** The trailing $6.16 GAAP EPS shown in headline data understates true earnings power. FY2024 carried a negative effective tax rate; FY2025's rate jumped to ~42.5% (OECD global minimum tax plus the Ireland move). In Q2 FY2025 a $574M valuation-allowance charge crushed GAAP net income to $13M; Q2 FY2026 rebounded to $855M (EPS $2.90) helped by a $114M tax benefit (9.2% rate). Year-over-year GAAP comparisons are meaningless in both directions. The real story is operating income +28% and ~200bps of margin expansion. Consensus adjusted EPS is $11.24 for the current fiscal year and $12.64 for next year, growing ~12.5%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are sound.** First-half operating cash flow was $1,812M (up from $1,531M) with capex of $528M (~6% of sales, elevated for capacity), implying ~$3B+ annualized FCF. Total debt is $5,655M against $1,110M cash; equity is $13.2B. Net debt of ~$4.5B sits against $3.5B+ annual operating income, roughly 1.3x net leverage, comfortably within the 3.75x covenant (4.25x post-acquisition). Goodwill of $7.4B (up from $5.8B two years ago) reflects the Richards Manufacturing deal, and the company carries ~$4.2B of cumulative historical goodwill impairments; prior acquisitions have been written down.

**Capital allocation is aggressively shareholder-friendly.** First-half buybacks were $818M plus $417M dividends. The board added $3.0B to the buyback authorization in Q2 (~$3.6B remaining) and raised the dividend ~12% to $0.78/quarter ($3.12 annualized). Share count is shrinking (296M versus ~301M a year prior), with 8.3M treasury shares cancelled in Q2.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth driver is AI/data-center capex feeding TEL's high-speed connectivity products. Big Tech AI spending is on track for ~$750B in 2026, roughly double 2025, and data-center demand is confirmed broadly across the infrastructure supply chain. TEL is a clean picks-and-shovels beneficiary: digital data networks at +46-48% organic is the swing factor lifting both the top line and the mix toward higher-margin Industrial revenue.

Secondary drivers include vehicle electrification and content growth (offsetting unit declines), factory automation, grid modernization through the Richards Manufacturing energy platform, and a recovering aerospace/defense cycle. The mix shift is structurally favorable: Industrial Solutions is now ~49% of sales and growing 27%, while the legacy auto core is in organic decline. As Industrial's weight rises, both blended growth and margin should improve.

The growth is real but not in the acceleration band that signals a trough recovery. Consensus EPS revisions are modestly positive (+1.6% current year, +2.2% next year over 90 days) rather than the velocity seen in true cyclical inflections. This is a steady compounder with an AI tailwind rather than a depressed-earnings recovery. Combined with ~12.5% expected EPS growth and buyback accretion, the forward earnings path supports mid-to-high-teens total return potential without requiring multiple expansion.

The principal growth caveat is concentration. AI/cloud is the biggest driver and the most cyclical, tied to hyperscaler capex cycles TEL does not control. Automotive at ~41% of sales is in organic contraction, so the company depends on content-per-vehicle gains and the Industrial ramp to carry consolidated growth.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward P/E of 21.6x cited in headline data reflects the tax-distorted trailing figure; on clean adjusted earnings the multiple is lower, at 18.9x current-year EPS and 16.8x next-year.

The peer table lists Electronic Manufacturing Services names — FLEX (65x), JBL (50x), FN (50x), TTMI (102x), SANM (52x), median 52.3x. These are weaker, lower-margin contract manufacturers and overstate the relevant comparison. The right benchmark is the engineered-connector cohort led by Amphenol; the specific Amphenol multiple is not in the provided sources [unverified — actual: N/A]. On its own merits, TEL at ~17-19x forward looks reasonable for a business with ~20% operating margins and an accelerating AI mix.

The most concrete mispricing signal: the stock trades below the low end of the published analyst target range, with a mean target of $263 and a median of $260 implying ~20-23% upside from current levels. On a stock at <20x forward with stable-to-positive estimate revisions, consensus targets behave as floors that lag rising estimates rather than ceilings; analysts revise upward as the AI mix compounds. The catalyst for re-rating is continued double-digit Industrial growth plus the ~$1.6B/year buyback shrinking the share base.

I temper the valuation score to 6.5 for two reasons. First, the stock fell ~7% after a record Q2, and the market reads it as "fairly valued," indicating the AI premium is at least partially captured rather than a clean dislocation. Second, cheapness alone is not a reliable buy signal; the case rests on the identifiable catalyst (AI-driven Industrial growth, buyback accretion, discount to true quality peer), not on the low multiple in isolation. The asymmetry is favorable but not extreme.

## Competitive Landscape

TEL is a top-tier global connector and sensor maker competing against Amphenol, Molex, Aptiv, and specialized firms. The moat derives from scale, breadth of catalog, and entrenched design-in positions that create switching costs once a component is engineered into a customer's product. In automotive, those relationships are deep but face OEM pricing power; in data center, TEL is winning share in high-speed connectivity as hyperscalers build out AI infrastructure.

The competitive trajectory is improving on mix, with Industrial Solutions operating margin expanding ~280bps to 19.4% as the company converts AI demand into both faster growth and richer margins. The industry tailwind is broad-based and confirmed across multiple companies and hyperscaler capex guidance, giving the data-center growth thesis higher durability than a single-company story.

The structural watch item, flagged in TEL's own 10-K, is customer disintermediation: hyperscalers may develop internal connectivity capabilities, reducing demand, and the segment's customer concentration is increasing. This is the least-priced competitive risk and the reason the position score is strong rather than maximum. The legacy automotive franchise remains a stable but low-growth anchor.

## Risk Assessment

**Automotive concentration and cyclicality (~41% of sales).** Auto organic sales are already declining on weak global vehicle production. The thesis depends on content-per-vehicle gains outrunning unit declines, and large OEM customers exert pricing power.

**China exposure (~25% of sales, 19 manufacturing sites).** Heavy U.S.-China trade and tariff risk on both demand and supply. Roughly 60% of sales are non-USD and the company does not hedge translation exposure; FX added ~4% to recent growth and can reverse.

**AI/data-center demand concentration and disintermediation.** The growth engine is also the most cyclical and customer-concentrated line. The 10-K explicitly warns customers may in-source AI-related connectivity. A normalization of hyperscaler capex would hit the fastest-growing, highest-incremental-margin revenue.

**Structural tax rise.** The OECD 15% global minimum tax took effect for TEL in FY2025 (Ireland implemented), raising the normalized rate and making GAAP earnings volatile. Irish incorporation also adds CFC attribution, dividend withholding, and stamp-duty considerations.

**Goodwill impairment.** $7.4B goodwill with ~$4.2B of cumulative historical impairments; further write-downs are an explicit 10-K risk if acquired businesses underperform.

**Contingent liabilities.** A voluntary U.S. Customs Section 301 duty disclosure ($27M reserved, penalties undetermined) and environmental remediation ($27M reserved, up to $53M). Neither is material today; both are open-ended.

**Raw materials.** Large buyer of copper, gold, silver, palladium, all sharply higher YoY. Commodity swaps mitigate but margins remain exposed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AI/data-center capex continues its run toward ~$750B in 2026 and sustains TEL's digital data networks growth at 40%+ organic. Industrial Solutions keeps expanding margins as it scales, the auto content cycle offsets unit weakness, and the ~$1.6B/year buyback compounds per-share earnings. Estimates revise upward toward and beyond next-year $12.64. The multiple re-rates partway toward the engineered-connector peer cohort. At 24-25x next-year EPS the stock reaches ~$300-320, toward the $338 analyst high.

**Bear case.** Hyperscaler AI capex normalizes or a major customer in-sources connectivity, cutting the highest-incremental-margin revenue. Automotive organic decline deepens, China tariff actions hit demand and supply, and the higher structural tax rate plus a goodwill impairment compress earnings and sentiment. The multiple derates to ~14-15x on flat-to-down EPS, taking the stock to ~$175, roughly -17% from current.

**Base case.** Industrial/AI growth moderates from peak but stays double-digit, auto stabilizes, margins hold near 20%, and buybacks add 2-3% to EPS. The stock re-rates modestly toward consensus on ~20x next-year EPS, reaching ~$255 over 12-18 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $255** (≈20x next-year EPS of $12.64, in line with consensus mean of $263 and median of $260). Upside of ~20% from $212.

**Bull case target: $320** (≈25x next-year EPS, toward the $338 analyst high). Upside of ~51%.

**Bear case target: $175** (≈14x next-year EPS on AI-capex normalization plus persistent auto weakness). Downside of ~17%.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to quarterly digital data networks growth disclosures, Industrial Solutions margin progression, and the FY2026/FY2027 earnings cadence.

**Upside/downside from current ($212):** +20% to base, +51% to bull, -17% to bear, a favorable skew of roughly 1.2-3.0:1 reward to risk.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Digital data networks organic growth decelerates below ~15% YoY, signaling AI capex normalization reaching TEL.
2. Industrial Solutions operating margin reverses below ~17%, or consolidated operating margin falls below ~18%, indicating the mix-driven margin gains are unwinding.
3. Consensus next-year EPS revised down more than ~5% in a single quarter, or a hyperscaler customer materially cuts orders or signals in-sourcing.

## Conclusion

TE Connectivity is a quality engineered-components franchise whose earnings mix is rotating toward AI/data-center connectivity at attractive incremental margins, supported by a broad, confirmed infrastructure demand cycle. At ~17-19x forward earnings, below the low end of the analyst target range, the stock offers favorable asymmetry for a business growing EPS ~12.5% with ~$3B+ FCF, modest leverage, and aggressive capital return. The flat-to-lower price since the prior assessment, against record results and stable-to-positive estimates, improves rather than impairs the risk/reward.

The recommendation is **BUY** with disciplined sizing. The score is held below maximum conviction because two risks are real and partly underpriced: a ~41% automotive base in organic decline, and AI growth concentrated in hyperscaler customers the company itself flags could in-source connectivity. Neither breaks the thesis at current levels, but both warrant monitoring through the quarterly data-center growth and margin disclosures.
