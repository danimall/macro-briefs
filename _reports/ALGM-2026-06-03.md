**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegro MicroSystems is the global market-share leader in magnetic sensor ICs, paired with a fast-growing power-IC franchise, selling roughly 1.5–2.1 billion units annually to more than 10,000 customers. A cyclical recovery off a FY25 trough (revenue ~$725M) is in progress, FY26 revenue rose 22.8% to $890M, and the higher-growth Power IC line (+42.8% YoY in the first nine months of FY26) is shifting mix toward AI data-center power delivery and wide-bandgap (SiC/GaN) content. The company has two independent secular drivers: automotive electrification (~72% of sales) and AI server power conversion.

The stock has already moved a great deal. ALGM is up roughly 95% over the trailing twelve months, from the low-$20s to ~$50, and now trades within striking distance of analyst consensus targets (mean $54.4, median $55, high $62). The FY26 Q4 print (May 7, 2026) showed the central tension: revenue beat at $243M (+26% YoY), but the stock fell from ~$51.4 to ~$47.9 because Adjusted EBITDA margin came in around 20.4%, roughly 10% below consensus. The near-term catalyst for the equity is margin recovery, not top-line growth, and management's named margin levers (operating leverage plus a multi-quarter "gold-to-copper" wirebond conversion program) sit against tariff-driven input inflation.

We rate ALGM a HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 6.1. The franchise quality, mix shift, and improving demand are genuine and well-documented, which justifies holding through the recovery. At ~$50 the risk/reward is balanced: the multiple already embeds the recovery, the ~32% Sanken share overhang is unresolved, and the trailing GAAP earnings remain near breakeven. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s or on confirmed EBITDA-margin re-acceleration.

## Company Overview

Allegro is a fabless designer and manufacturer of magnetic sensor ICs and application-specific power ICs for motion control and energy-efficient systems. It holds the #1 worldwide position in magnetic sensor ICs and offers the broadest magnetic-sensor portfolio available, spanning 1,500+ products. Headquartered in Manchester, New Hampshire, the company operates as one reportable segment but discloses two product lines:

- **Magnetic Sensors (MS):** ~60% of sales ($397.7M of nine-month FY26 revenue, +11% YoY). Measures motion, speed, position, and current, including TMR (tunneling magnetoresistance) sensors from the Crocus acquisition.
- **Power ICs (PIC):** ~40% of sales ($249.3M nine-month, +42.8% YoY). Motor drivers, power management, LED drivers, and isolated gate drivers, the growth engine.

End markets break down as automotive ~72% (ADAS, xEV powertrain, internal combustion, safety/comfort) and Industrial & Other ~28% (the fastest-growing bucket at +33.5% YoY nine-month, encompassing AI data centers, industrial automation/robotics, medical, and clean energy). The competitive moat rests on highly integrated application-specific analog/mixed-signal ICs with 2–4 year design-win cycles, deep OEM relationships, and share leadership in magnetic sensing. Primary competitors are Infineon, Texas Instruments, NXP, Melexis, and onsemi.

The fiscal calendar matters for interpreting recent data: Allegro's fiscal year ends the last Friday of March, so FY2026 closed March 27, 2026, with results reported May 7, 2026. Some third-party feeds mislabel the March-2026 quarter as "Q1 CY2026"; it is fiscal Q4/full-year FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe a cyclical recovery in progress, off the FY25 trough but still below the FY24 peak.

| Metric | FY24 (peak) | FY25 (trough) | FY26 (recovery) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$1,049M | ~$725M | $890.1M (+22.8%) |
| Operating margin | ~18.7% | negative | ~2.1% |
| Net income | $152.9M | –$72.8M | –$14.9M |

The GAAP losses are below-the-line, not operational. The FY25–FY26 net losses were dominated by interest expense, a one-time $34.8M forward-repurchase contract loss in FY25, and Polar Semiconductor (PSL) equity-method losses, not by the core business, which runs gross margins near 46% and generates substantial free cash flow. The reported trailing EPS of –$0.08 and the resulting negative headline P/E are accounting artifacts, not signs of operating distress.

Recent quarterly trajectory confirms the recovery. Q3 FY26 (ended 12/26/25) revenue was $229.2M (+28.9% YoY) with gross margin of 46.7% (up from 45.7%), operating income of $9.6M, and a return to GAAP net income of $8.3M ($0.04 EPS) after a string of losses. Nine-month revenue reached $646.9M (+21.6%). Cash flow quality is strong: nine-month operating cash flow of $127.4M was 3x the prior year's $41.6M, against only $21.2M of capex, producing robust FCF. Cash stood at $155.2M, working capital at $374.3M, current ratio ~3.65.

On the balance sheet, the company is deleveraging. Term debt was reduced from $345M to $285M via $60M of voluntary paydowns in FY26, and a January 2026 refinancing (Amendment No. 4) repriced the $285M term loan to Term SOFR + 1.75% (down from +2.00%) with 0% amortization and a maturity pushed to October 2030. The net-leverage covenant is ≤4.0x and the company is in compliance. Altman Z is ~3.1 (safe zone).

One caveat for normalization: the OBBB Act (July 2025) restored immediate R&D expensing, generating large discrete tax benefits and a wildly swinging effective tax rate (e.g., –1,593% in Q3 FY26). This inflates reported net benefit and should be stripped out when assessing underlying earnings power.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs, both supported by independent confirmation rather than a single-company story. First, automotive electrification raises sensor and power-IC content per vehicle; management emphasizes high dollar-content even within Chinese OEMs, where it claims strong positioning. Second, AI data-center power delivery is a newer but rapidly ramping demand source: fan drivers are the core today, current sensors are ramping quickly, and isolated gate drivers are expected to contribute meaningfully in 18–24 months.

The mix shift is the real story. Power ICs are growing ~43% versus ~11% for magnetic sensors, and the fastest-growing applications (data-center current sensors, SiC gate drivers, robotics) carry higher value content. The product cadence supports this: the ACS37200 ultra-low-loss isolated current sensor (Jan 2026), the Power-Thru self-powered SiC gate driver family for AI data centers and EVs (Jan 2026), and the industry-first production-ready 10 MHz TMR current sensor for wide-bandgap power (Oct 2025).

Estimate revisions corroborate near-term momentum. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $1.05 from $0.94 ninety days ago (+11.0%) and from $0.97 thirty days ago (+8.2%). Current-quarter estimates moved up modestly (+4.3% over 30 days). The one soft spot is next-year EPS, revised down 4.2% over 30 days to $1.47, suggesting analysts are tempering the slope of the margin recovery even as they raise the near-term path. The +11% 90-day current-year revision, combined with 23% FY26 revenue growth, is a positive momentum signal.

Management guidance is constructive: next-quarter revenue guide of ~$250M is above consensus, with the company citing multi-year-high backlog and design-win momentum. The earnings track record shows four consecutive in-line prints (surprises of 2–5%), indicating reasonable forecasting visibility without the upside surprise pattern that would warrant elevated confidence in beats.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here given near-breakeven GAAP earnings distorted by below-the-line items, so the relevant lens is forward and normalized. At $50.41 against the current-year consensus of $1.05, ALGM trades at roughly 48x current-year EPS, and at ~34x the $1.47 next-year estimate. On a cycle-recovery basis these multiples are full but not absurd for a share-leading analog franchise with mid-20s percent revenue growth and a credible margin-recovery path.

Peer context is mixed and the sample is heterogeneous. Among similarly sized semiconductor names, profitable comparables trade at QRVO 28.6x, CRUS 21.6x, SWKS 32.4x, while MXL (-61x) and SLAB (-142x) sit in their own cyclical-trough territory. ALGM's forward multiple sits above the profitable cohort, which is defensible given its share leadership and the AI/EV content mix, but it does not represent a clear discount. There is no dividend to anchor the valuation (yield 0.0%).

The decisive issue is that the recovery is already in the price. While prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark a stock down, the current price implies the market has substantially capitalized both the cyclical rebound and the secular mix shift. The Q4 reaction is instructive: a revenue beat was met with a selloff because EBITDA margin missed the ~22% expected. The marginal buyer is already paying for top-line growth and now requires margin delivery to justify further upside. With next-year EPS estimates drifting lower and the stock at ~34x that number, the asymmetry is modest. We assign a valuation score of 5.0, fairly valued, neither a clear mispricing nor a value trap.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegro's competitive position is improving, which supports the trajectory upgrade reflected in prior reports (competitive position rose from 6.0 to 7.5). It is the worldwide #1 in magnetic sensor ICs with the broadest portfolio, and the design-win cycle (2–4 years) creates genuine stickiness once it is socketed into an OEM platform. The mix shift into higher-content power ICs and data-center applications is expanding the addressable opportunity per system, and the wide-bandgap product launches position it for the next phase of power-density requirements in both EVs and AI servers.

On the FY26 Q4 call, sell-side attention centered on the durability of data-center growth and content gains in Chinese OEM EVs, both areas where management articulated a concrete roadmap. Morgan Stanley turned more positive on improving analog/MCU read-throughs, and the average analyst target rose ~18% to ~$55.5.

The competitive risks are structural rather than acute. Analog/sensor ICs carry inherent ASP declines, requiring continuous design-win replacement to hold revenue. The competitor set (Infineon, TI, NXP, Melexis, onsemi) is well-capitalized, and in power management Allegro is a challenger rather than the incumbent leader. The trajectory of gaining content, leading in TMR sensing, and entering high-growth data-center power favors the company.

## Risk Assessment

**Sanken overhang.** Sanken Electric (Japan) still owns ~32.2% after Allegro repurchased 38.8M shares (~$854M) from it in 2024. A future Sanken sell-down is an explicit risk factor and a structural share overhang that could cap upside or pressure the stock independent of fundamentals.

**Automotive concentration (~72%).** The business is tied to global auto production cycles, EV adoption pace, and Chinese OEM demand; management flagged temporary China-auto seasonal softness.

**Supply-chain and geographic concentration.** The fabless model depends on TSMC, UMC, Tower, and PSL for wafers, with primary assembly/test in the Philippines (AMPI) as a single point of failure. Roughly 90% of sales are outside the US, with Greater China alone ~28%.

**PSL equity-method drag.** Allegro's ~10.2% PSL stake produced –$6.7M of equity losses over nine months of FY26 and is also a related-party wafer supplier (~$30M of purchases), adding both a recurring earnings drag and complexity.

**Margin delivery risk.** The near-term catalyst is EBITDA margin recovery via operating leverage and the gold-to-copper wirebond conversion, set against tariff-driven cost inflation. The Q4 miss showed the market will punish margin shortfalls even amid revenue strength.

**Governance watch.** A May 13, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) indicates a director/officer change concurrent with the annual meeting; detail was not retrievable and warrants follow-up. Pay-vs-performance shows comp resilience despite TSR (140.45) lagging the peer group (187.63) and the FY25 net loss, a modest watch item.

These risks are largely known and partly priced, which is why we score risk factors 5.5 rather than lower. The combination of the Sanken overhang and a margin-dependent catalyst leaves the stock exposed to disappointment at a full multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 83.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 52.4%, rich by a 31-point spread, in the 76th percentile of the current chain, with an implied move of ±13.8% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (83.5% near-term vs. 75.1% long-term), which typically signals the market expects a near-dated catalyst. IV has already compressed sharply since the Q4 print (108.5% → 83.5%), consistent with event premium bleeding off post-earnings. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.2%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.16 and 0.14), so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the 10.7% short interest. The market is pricing event risk, not directional conviction. For a fundamental holder, rich near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting attractive and argues against chasing the stock into the next catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Allegro is a share-leading analog franchise at the intersection of two durable secular drivers. Revenue grew 23% in FY26 with Power ICs up ~43%, gross margins recovering toward 47%, and FCF generation of $127M over nine months. If the data-center ramp accelerates (current sensors now, isolated gate drivers in 18–24 months) and EBITDA margins recover toward the low-to-mid 20s on operating leverage plus wirebond cost-out, FY27–FY28 earnings power could approach $1.80–$2.00. At ~28–30x that, the stock reaches the mid-$50s to low-$60s, matching the analyst high of $62. Continued deleveraging and a resolution of the Sanken overhang (e.g., an orderly, fully-absorbed sell-down) would remove an overhang and could trigger re-rating.

**Bear Case.** The stock already discounts the recovery at ~34x next-year EPS, and next-year estimates are drifting lower. If margin recovery stalls on tariff-driven input inflation, or if auto demand (especially China) softens, the EBITDA-margin miss pattern repeats and the multiple compresses. A Sanken sell-down floods the market with ~32% of the float. Data-center demand proves more cyclical than secular and amplifies the next downturn. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the high-$20s/low-$30s, roughly where it traded for most of 2025, a 30–45% drawdown from current levels.

The balance of these scenarios is roughly symmetric at $50, which underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026), with the multi-quarter wirebond margin program providing intermediate checkpoints. The June 18 options expiry frames near-term volatility.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin re-accelerating toward 23%+ on a reported quarter, confirming the operating-leverage and wirebond cost-out thesis.
- A pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s on unchanged or improving fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Resolution of the Sanken overhang via an orderly, fully-absorbed sell-down, removing the structural share overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive EBITDA-margin miss combined with downward current-year EPS revisions, signaling the margin recovery has stalled.
- Automotive revenue (especially China) declining sequentially for two quarters, indicating cyclical rollover rather than recovery.
- A disorderly Sanken sell-down or a covenant breach on the term loan.

## Conclusion

Allegro is a high-quality, share-leading analog franchise executing a credible cyclical recovery and mix shift into higher-content power and data-center applications. The fundamentals are improving across most metrics: revenue +23%, Power ICs +43%, gross margin ~47%, strong FCF, active deleveraging. The scoring trajectory (4.8 → 5.8 → 6.1) reflects fundamental progress, not just price action.

The constraint is price. After a ~95% twelve-month advance to ~$50, the stock trades at a full forward multiple within ~9% of the consensus mean target, the near-term catalyst (margin recovery) is precisely where the company just disappointed, next-year estimates are softening, and the ~32% Sanken overhang remains unresolved. The risk/reward is balanced. We rate ALGM a HOLD (growth_hold): own it through the recovery, but wait for a margin-driven beat or a meaningful pullback before adding. Our reference points are the analyst target range of $48–$62, with our own base case of fair value near current levels and conviction building below the low-$40s.
