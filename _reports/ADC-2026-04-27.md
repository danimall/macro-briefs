## Executive Summary

Agree Realty is a net lease REIT operating 2,756 retail properties across all 50 U.S. states, with 99.7% occupancy and ~65% investment-grade tenant exposure. The company deployed $404M in acquisitions during Q1 2026 at a 7.1% weighted average cap rate and delivered AFFO of $1.14/share, beating consensus by $0.02. Revenue grew 19% YoY and net income surged 32%. The balance sheet is conservatively leveraged at 3.2x net debt/EBITDA with no meaningful maturities until 2028, and $2.28B in total liquidity provides significant dry powder.

The fundamental quality is strong. The problem is price. ADC trades at ~41x trailing P/E and ~17x 2026 AFFO guidance ($4.54-4.58), a substantial premium to the retail REIT peer median of 23.5x. This premium is partly justified by ADC's superior growth profile (AFFO/share growth of ~5-6% vs. peers at 2-3%), but estimate revisions are trending negatively across all periods (-2.1% to -4.3% over 30 days), and three of the last four quarters have missed EPS consensus. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.34% and mortgage rates approaching 7%, interest-rate-sensitive REITs face a structural headwind that the current valuation does not adequately discount. The 4.1% dividend yield provides income support, but total return upside is constrained until either rates decline or the stock reprices toward the low-$70s.

Insider buying across the C-suite and board totaling >$4.8M in open-market purchases in the $69-76 range provides a meaningful counterweight to the valuation concern. This cluster of purchases by the founder, CEO, CFO, and a director represents genuine conviction from those with the best visibility into forward fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Agree Realty Corporation, founded in 1971 by Richard Agree, is a fully integrated net lease REIT headquartered in Royal Oak, Michigan. The company acquires, develops, and manages single-tenant retail properties under triple-net lease structures where tenants bear the burden of taxes, insurance, and maintenance. This produces a low-operating-cost model: the company manages a $10.2B asset base with just 90 employees.

The portfolio is concentrated in e-commerce-resistant and recession-resistant retail categories: grocery (10.3% of annualized base rent), home improvement (9.0%), convenience stores (7.7%), tire/auto service, auto parts, and dollar stores. No single tenant exceeds 10% of ABR. Geographic diversification spans all 50 states, with Texas (6.9%), Illinois (6.1%), and Ohio (5.3%) as the largest state exposures.

ADC grows through three platforms: (1) acquisitions ($1.45B deployed in 2025, $404M in Q1 2026), (2) build-to-suit development leveraging 50+ years of expertise, and (3) a Developer Funding Platform (DFP) where ADC collaborates with third-party developers on in-process projects. This three-pronged approach gives ADC more levers for accretive growth than pure-acquisition peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** ADC has compounded revenue at 18.6% annually from $430M (2022) to $718M (2025), with Q1 2026 at $201M (+19% YoY). Operating income grew to $340M in 2025 (+12.6% YoY). Net income reached $204M in 2025, and Q1 2026 net income of $62.2M represented 32% YoY growth.

The critical metric for a net lease REIT is AFFO per share growth, which measures whether acquisitions are accretive net of equity issuance. ADC delivered $4.33 AFFO/share in 2025 (up from $4.14 in 2024 and $3.95 in 2023), a ~4.8% CAGR despite diluted shares growing from ~97M to ~111M. Guidance for 2026 is $4.54-4.58, implying 5-6% growth.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt to EBITDA stands at 3.2x as of Q1 2026, down from 3.8x at year-end 2025, among the lowest leverage levels in the net lease sector. Total debt of $3.72B is 87% fixed-rate at a weighted average cost of 4.04%. Interest rate swaps lock the 2029 term loan at 3.57% and the 2031 term loan at 3.22%. The first debt maturity is 2027 ($50M), with the first significant maturity in 2028 ($410M).

Total liquidity of $2.28B includes $1.37B of unsettled forward equity, $780M revolver availability, and $100M undrawn term loan capacity. This liquidity provides acquisition capacity without immediate additional dilution.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow reached $504M in 2025 (+17% YoY), with Q1 2026 at $145M (+14% YoY). The AFFO payout ratio of ~72% ($3.08 dividends / $4.33 AFFO in 2025) is well-covered, leaving meaningful retained cash flow to partially fund growth.

**Equity Dilution:** Shares outstanding have grown 53% since 2022 (79M to ~121M), the unavoidable cost of external growth. The $1.37B in unsettled forward equity (~18M shares at current prices) will settle over coming quarters. While AFFO/share growth has remained positive through this dilution, the growth rate is mechanically constrained by the pace of issuance.

**Interest Expense Trajectory:** Interest expense grew 24% YoY in 2025 to $134.6M and 17% YoY in Q1 2026. The newly issued 2035 notes carry a 5.6% coupon vs. 2.6% on the 2033 notes, illustrating the higher cost of incremental debt in the current rate environment.

## Growth Analysis

ADC's growth profile is among the strongest in the net lease sector. The company acquired 305 properties in 2025 and 85 in Q1 2026, deploying capital at 7.1% weighted average cap rates with 11.3-year average remaining lease terms. The 2026 acquisition pipeline appears robust based on management commentary and Q1 pace.

AFFO/share guidance of $4.54-4.58 for 2026 represents 5-6% growth. However, estimate revisions are moving in the wrong direction: current-quarter estimates fell 4.3% over 30 days, next-quarter fell 4.3%, and next-year fell 2.6%. This negative revision trend, combined with three misses in the last four quarters (Q2 2025 by -5.9%, Q3 2025 by -1.8%, Q4 2025 by -1.4%), suggests consensus may still be too optimistic or that execution is slightly below management's own targets.

The development and DFP platforms provide incremental growth optionality, though acquisitions remain the primary driver (>90% of deployment). Impairment charges of $11.9M in 2025 (up from $7.2M in each of 2023 and 2024) are modest relative to the portfolio's scale but suggest the rapid pace of acquisitions may occasionally include properties that underperform expectations.

Investment-grade tenant concentration declined marginally from 66.8% (year-end 2025) to 65.4% (Q1 2026), indicating that the newest acquisitions are skewing slightly lower in credit quality, a trade-off for maintaining cap rate spreads as the market compresses.

## Valuation Assessment

ADC trades at ~41x trailing P/E ($75.87 / $1.77 EPS) and approximately 16.9x 2026 AFFO guidance midpoint ($75.87 / $4.56). The peer median P/E is 23.5x (BRX 24.2x, NNN 21.6x, FRT 23.5x, KRG 19.0x), placing ADC at a 75% premium to peers on a P/E basis.

The premium is partially justified: ADC grows AFFO/share at 5-6% vs. peers at 2-3%, has lower leverage (3.2x vs. typical 4-5x), and maintains one of the highest investment-grade tenant concentrations. These are real quality differentials. But a 75% premium for a ~300bps growth differential is generous, particularly when:

1. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative across all periods
2. The recent earnings miss pattern (3/4 quarters) undermines confidence in forward estimates
3. Rising rates mechanically compress REIT valuations and increase ADC's future cost of capital
4. The 4.1% dividend yield provides less relative appeal as Treasury yields approach 4.5%

The sell-side consensus target range of $81-92 (median $84.75) implies 7-21% upside, with 12 Buy/Strong Buy and 8 Hold ratings among 20 covering analysts, a constructive consensus that may not adequately price rate risk.

A more appropriate valuation framework: at 15x AFFO ($4.56 midpoint) = $68.40 (bear case), at 17x = $77.52 (fair value), at 19x = $86.64 (bull case requiring rate cuts or accelerated growth). Current price of $75.87 is within 2% of fair value at 17x, leaving limited upside without a rate-driven multiple expansion catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

ADC occupies a distinctive position in the net lease sector: the highest-growth operator at a premium valuation, with meaningfully lower leverage than peers.

**vs. Realty Income (O):** O is 5.5x ADC's market cap with 15,600+ properties and a 5.8% yield, but grows AFFO/share at ~3%. ADC offers nearly double the growth rate at a lower yield.

**vs. NNN REIT (NNN):** NNN deploys $550-650M annually (40% of ADC's pace), carries higher leverage, and faces 2026-2028 refinancing needs at elevated rates. NNN trades at 21.6x P/E with a 5.4% yield, offering more income but less growth.

**vs. Essential Properties (EPRT):** Smaller, focused on mid-market tenants, also growing aggressively but with a lower IG-tenant mix.

ADC's competitive advantages are real: the development platform, commercial paper program, and capital markets sophistication are atypical for a company of its size. The lean 90-employee operating structure produces high margins (47% operating margin).

The competitive position is stable to slightly improving. Portfolio growth from ~2,100 properties (2023) to 2,756 (Q1 2026) is scaling the moat. However, the declining IG-tenant concentration (66.8% → 65.4%) and rising impairments ($7.2M → $11.9M) bear monitoring as early indicators of whether rapid growth is compromising selectivity.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Risk (Primary):** ADC's growth model requires cost of capital below acquisition cap rates (~7.1%). With 10Y Treasuries at 4.34% and new debt pricing at 5.6%, the spread between acquisition yields and funding costs is compressing. If rates rise further, ADC's accretive growth engine could stall. A 100bps increase in funding costs would reduce the investment spread to ~50-100bps before equity dilution, marginal economics.

**Earnings Momentum Risk:** The pattern of three misses in four quarters combined with negative estimate revisions creates downside risk to the premium multiple. If Q2 2026 also misses, the current valuation will be difficult to sustain. Management flagged $0.02-0.04 of tariff-related dilution risk to 2026 guidance without adjusting the range, which may reflect sandbagging or genuine uncertainty.

**Dilution Feedback Loop:** The $1.37B in unsettled forward equity (~18M shares) will settle over coming quarters. If the stock price declines significantly before settlement, future issuance would be less favorable. This risk is mitigated by the forward structure (price locked at time of sale).

**Consumer/Tenant Credit Risk:** Approximately 33% of ABR comes from non-investment-grade tenants. In a recessionary environment with consumer spending under sustained pressure, dollar stores, convenience stores, and smaller retailers face elevated closure risk. ADC's focus on e-commerce-resistant categories provides some insulation, but recession resistance has limits.

**Rate Sensitivity as Duration Proxy:** Net lease REITs function as long-duration bond substitutes for many investors. Duration assets face a structural headwind at current rate levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85-87, ~11-15% upside):** Rate expectations shift dovish; even a 50bps move in 10Y yields would compress REIT discount rates and expand multiples by 1-2 turns. ADC executes 2026 guidance ($4.56 AFFO midpoint), acquisitions remain accretive at 7%+ cap rates, and the development pipeline delivers above-average spreads. At 19x AFFO = $86.64. Insider buying in the $69-76 range proves prescient as it has in prior cycles.

**Bear Case ($65-68, ~10-13% downside):** Rates stay elevated or rise further. Inflation persistence pushes CPI above 4%, making a 2026 rate hike plausible. ADC's cost of capital rises while cap rates don't move proportionally. AFFO growth decelerates to 3-4% as acquisition volumes slow. Multiple compresses to 14-15x AFFO. At 15x $4.56 = $68.40.

**Base Case ($77-80, 0-5% upside + 4.1% yield):** Rates hold steady through year-end. ADC delivers near the low end of guidance ($4.54 AFFO). The stock trades range-bound as growth REIT investors weigh quality against valuation in a high-rate environment. Total return of 4-9% including dividends.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price decline to $68-70 range, creating a 6.5%+ AFFO yield and 15x forward multiple with 15-20% upside to fair value
2. Two consecutive quarters of AFFO beats (Q2 and Q3 2026 both exceed consensus), reversing the current miss pattern
3. 10Y Treasury below 4.0% sustained for 30+ days, which would mechanically expand REIT valuations

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Investment-grade tenant concentration falls below 60%, indicating deteriorating underwriting quality
2. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 4.5x, signaling leverage creep
3. AFFO/share guidance is cut or AFFO growth turns negative, breaking the accretive acquisition model

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). The next earnings report and any developments on the rate front will determine whether the income thesis strengthens or deteriorates.

## Conclusion

Agree Realty is a high-quality net lease REIT with best-in-class growth execution, conservative leverage, and genuine insider conviction. The business is performing well: 19% revenue growth, 32% net income growth, 99.7% occupancy, and improving leverage metrics. The core question is whether $75.87 compensates adequately for rate risk, negative estimate revisions, and a recent pattern of earnings misses.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation is the 75% P/E premium to peers combined with deteriorating earnings momentum. ADC trades at ~17x forward AFFO in an environment where its 4.1% yield is barely competitive with risk-free rates, and where estimate revisions have been negative across every time horizon over the past 90 days. The cluster of insider buying in the $69-76 range is the strongest countervailing signal, suggesting those closest to the business see value here, but insider timing can be early by 2-3 quarters in a rising-rate environment.

HOLD as an income position. The dividend is well-covered at 72% of AFFO, the balance sheet provides downside protection through the cycle, and the business will compound at 5-6% annually if management executes. Total return of 8-10% annually (4% yield + 5-6% AFFO growth) is reasonable but requires patience and is not sufficient to justify a BUY given the risk of multiple compression if rates remain elevated or rise further.
