## Executive Summary

The July 30 second-quarter report is the first genuinely constructive data set HII has produced in four quarters. Revenue rose 10.9% to $3,418M, segment operating income rose 30% to $224M (6.6% margin versus 5.6%), diluted EPS of $5.27 beat the $3.84 consensus by 37%, and management raised shipbuilding revenue guidance by $500M to $10.2-10.4B while lifting the shipbuilding margin floor 50bp to 6.0-6.5%. New awards were $6.7B in the quarter and backlog reached a record $57.3B, 4.34x trailing revenue. Separately — and materially under-discussed — the Ingalls collective bargaining agreements that all expired in March 2026 have been ratified out to March 2031, and Newport News agreements now run to 2030-2031. A strike at Pascagoula against a fixed-price-incentive-heavy backlog was among the most damaging plausible events on the risk list, and that tail is closed for four years.

The offset is cash. First-half operating cash flow was negative $421M and free cash flow negative $611M, an $879M year-over-year deterioration driven by a $985M unfavorable swing in trade working capital: contract assets rose $396M while contract liabilities fell $530M, a $926M net build. Cash fell from $774M to $12M. Management reaffirmed FY2026 FCF of $500-600M, which arithmetically requires H2 free cash flow of $1,111-1,211M against $532M actually delivered in H2 2025 — roughly 2.1-2.3x the prior-year second half, and above anything in the three years of data available. HII's cash generation is genuinely back-half loaded (Q1 was negative $461M and negative $462M in consecutive years; Q2 2025 alone produced $730M), and the July 29 submarine contract modifications should bring advance and progress payments. But the company also missed its own Q2 FCF guide (negative $150M against a negative $100M to positive $100M range), which is the reason to treat the annual reaffirmation as a claim requiring verification rather than a settled fact.

Our score moves to 5.95 from 5.8 in July, the first improvement after four consecutive declines from 6.4 in April. The improvement is located in growth prospects and valuation-adjusted financial health, not in management quality or risk. This is a fifth consecutive HOLD, but on different reasoning than the prior four: in July at $269 the shares offered a real discount to normalized earnings power; the rally since the July 21 low of $268.76 has captured most of it. At 15.5x next-year consensus with a base case near $353 (+9.7%) against a bear case near $240 (-25.4%), the probability-weighted return is positive but thin, and the near-term catalyst — the October Q3 print carrying both the H2 cash conversion test and the first disclosure of HII's share of the submarine award — is genuinely two-sided.

## Company Overview

HII is the largest military shipbuilder in the United States and the only entity capable of designing, building, refueling and inactivating nuclear-powered aircraft carriers. Three segments:

**Newport News Shipbuilding** (FY2025 revenue $6,507M, 52% of total; 5.1% segment margin) holds all nuclear work — Ford-class carrier construction, refueling and complex overhaul (RCOH), and submarine module and delivery work. **Ingalls Shipbuilding** ($3,078M, 7.6% margin) is the sole builder of LHA amphibious assault ships and LPD amphibious transport docks, and one of two builders of DDG-51 destroyers. **Mission Technologies** ($3,044M, 5.0% margin) covers C6ISR, electronic warfare, cyber, training, nuclear and environmental services, and unmanned systems.

The moat is asymmetric by program, and the distinctions matter for how new awards should be modeled. On carriers HII is effectively sole-source, with the only articulated threat in its own risk factors being the possibility that government shipyards become RCOH-capable. On amphibious ships it is sole-source. On destroyers it is a duopolist with General Dynamics Bath Iron Works. On submarines — the largest demand pool in naval shipbuilding — HII is **not** the prime. The FY2025 10-K describes HII as a subcontractor to General Dynamics Electric Boat on Columbia class (six module sections per boat), and the Q2 2026 10-Q now describes HII as "principal subcontractor to Electric Boat" on Virginia class, serving as delivery yard for six of nine Block VI boats. Roughly 81% of FY2025 revenue came from the U.S. Navy (81% in 2023, 80% in 2024 — concentration has not improved), and about 46% of the contract portfolio is fixed-price-incentive with another 50% cost-type. Workforce is ~45,000, of which 45% is unionized across 13 agreements.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and backlog.** Revenue grew 8.2% in FY2025 to $12,484M and accelerated to +12.1% in 1H2026 ($6,517M versus $5,816M), led by carriers and submarines at Newport News (+17.2%) and amphibious assault ships at Ingalls (+15.4%). Mission Technologies revenue declined 1.2% in the half and 3.9% in Q2. Backlog moved $48.7B (12/24) → $53.1B (12/25) → $57.3B (6/30/26) on $16.9B of FY2025 awards and $10.7B in 1H2026.

Two scope points on the backlog. Only $34.6B of the $57.3B (60%) is funded, and Newport News is the least funded segment at $15.8B of $32.5B (49%). Backlog excludes unexercised options and unfunded IDIQ orders — it is a contracted-work measure, not a pipeline. Per Note 6, roughly 35% converts to revenue through 2027 and another 35% through 2029.

**Margins.** The aggregate improvement contains two divergent stories.

| Segment operating margin | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ingalls | 13.2% | 7.6% | 7.6% | 6.8% | 6.9% |
| Newport News | 6.2% | 4.1% | 5.1% | 5.7% | 6.0% |
| Mission Technologies | 3.7% | 3.9% | 5.0% | 6.0% | 7.2% |
| Consolidated segment | 7.35% | 4.97% | 5.74% | 6.08% | 6.55% |

Newport News is recovering off a 4.1% trough, helped by contract adjustments and incentives, though the 10-Q notes the improvement was "partially offset by lower performance in aircraft carriers" — meaning the gain is award-and-incentive driven as much as execution driven. Ingalls, historically the better yard, has deteriorated from 13.2% to 6.8%; the 2023 figure was flattered by $91M of favorable catch-ups, so the true decline is smaller than the headline, but Q2 margin still fell 59bp year over year on 16.7% revenue growth.

Mission Technologies' 269bp margin expansion is substantially non-operating. Segment operating income rose $19M to $55M, and the 10-Q attributes the increase "primarily due to higher equity income from nuclear and environmental joint ventures"; consolidated income from operating investments rose to $21M from $8M, approximately the entire delta, while purchased-intangible amortization fell to $17M from $23M. Some core improvement exists — MT EBITDA margin rose to 9.0% from 8.6% in the half, and the 10-Q cites better Warfare Systems performance — but the reported margin optics substantially overstate it, on declining revenue, in the segment carrying most of the $2,650M of goodwill (20.9% of assets).

**Estimate-at-completion volatility is the defining P&L feature.** Gross cumulative catch-up adjustments were favorable $322M / unfavorable $350M in FY2025 and favorable $234M / unfavorable $223M in 1H2026 alone. Against roughly $700M of annual segment operating income, that churn is the dominant swing factor: net adjustments moved from +$118M (2023) to −$126M (2024) to −$28M (2025) to +$11M (1H2026). Two Q2 items: favorable $28M on the CVN 74 RCOH, and unfavorable $48M on the CVN 73 RCOH — a ship redelivered to the Navy in May 2023. Post-delivery true-ups on multi-year availabilities are routine (rate settlements, claims, warranty), and there is no disclosed link to the welding matter, but a $48M charge three years post-redelivery indicates the closeout risk on completed work is not zero.

**Cash flow is the central issue.**

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H2025 | 1H2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 970 | 393 | 1,196 | 428 | **(421)** |
| Capex, net | (278) | (353) | (396) | (160) | (190) |
| **Free cash flow** | **692** | **40** | **800** | **268** | **(611)** |

The FY26 guide of $500-600M requires H2 FCF of $1,111-1,211M. At FY26 capex of 4-5% of an implied ~$13.2B of revenue ($528-660M), less $190M spent, H2 capex is $338-470M, so H2 operating cash flow must be roughly $1,449-1,681M against $768M in H2 2025 — 1.9x to 2.2x. A sensitivity, not a forecast: if H2 OCF merely repeated last year's $768M with midpoint capex, FY26 FCF would be approximately negative $247M, a $750M+ shortfall. The plausible bridge is reversal of the $926M net contract asset build plus advance payments on the July 29 submarine modifications, and management reaffirmed the number with July collections visibility we do not have. But the required magnitude is unprecedented in the available history, and the company missed its own Q2 guide.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: assets $12,682M, liabilities $7,370M, equity $5,312M. Long-term debt $2,702M against $12M of cash for net debt of $2,690M; true debt/equity is 0.51x (a widely screened "1.51" figure is total liabilities/equity, not debt/equity). TTM EBITDA of approximately $1,020M puts net leverage at 2.64x. Liquidity is a fully undrawn $1.7B revolver to September 2029 and an unused $1.7B commercial paper program — approximately $1.71B available, down from $1.9B at 3/31/26. Cash of $12M is thin in absolute terms and the company briefly drew and repaid $17M on a line of credit during the half. Credit-agreement covenant headroom is not disclosed in the material reviewed and remains a verification gap. A recomputed Altman Z of approximately 2.78 (versus a mis-tagged screened 1.97) places the company well clear of distress thresholds, though the model fits a percentage-of-completion business poorly.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend raised to $1.38/quarter in November 2025; annualized $5.52 is a 1.71% yield with a 32.9% payout on TTM EPS of $16.79 — covered by FY2025 FCF of $800M, not by 1H2026. Buybacks are zero for at least six consecutive quarters despite $1,352.3M authorized through 2028; program-to-date average repurchase price is $167.82. Capex steps from 3.2% of revenue to a guided 4-5%, with 3.0-3.5 points discretionary for capacity expansion. The W International fabrication acquisition closed in 2025 for $133M cash.

**Earnings quality caveat.** FY26 guidance embeds a $213M non-operating, non-cash retirement benefit — roughly a quarter of derived pretax income, with CAS recoveries from customers of only about $46M — and an approximately 17% effective tax rate against a 19.2% first-half actual, implying an H2 rate near 15% and a discrete benefit (most plausibly the IRS R&D credit agreement submitted to the Joint Committee in March 2026). The operational raise is real; the EPS trajectory is flattered relative to it.

## Growth Analysis

Demand is codified rather than forecast. The FY2026 NDAA authorized $900.6B for national security including approximately $26B for shipbuilding, authorized CVN 82 and CVN 83 with incremental funding, and up to five Columbia-class boats. FY2026 appropriations added $1.5B for the Maritime Industrial Base, and the reconciliation act provided more than $29B of *mandatory* — multi-year, not annually appropriated — maritime industrial base funding. The FY2027 request seeks $65.8B for shipbuilding procurement, funding one Columbia, two Virginia, one DDG-51, one LPD-17, one LHA-6 and the first FF(X) frigate.

The constraint is throughput, and here the evidence has improved. Throughput rose approximately 14% in 2025 and 18% year over year in Q1 2026 against a 15% full-year target; outsourced hours doubled in 2025; Kastner attributed the Q2 guidance raise directly to throughput. Supporting actions: the W International acquisition, capex rising to 4-5% of sales, performance-based production agreements worth up to $900M with Path Robotics and GrayMatter Robotics (HII is the paying customer on a pay-for-output structure), and an HD Hyundai Heavy Industries Intelligent Welding System pilot deployed at Ingalls (HD HHI is the technology supplier, HII the host yard). The robotics and welding items are externally reported and appear in HII press releases but not in SEC filings; treat magnitudes as unconfirmed.

Consensus reflects steady rather than explosive growth: current-year EPS $18.57, next year $20.74, an 11.7% increment. Revisions are positive but do not reach the threshold that would flag a fundamental inflection — current year +6.9% over 30 days, next year +3.2% over 30 days and +2.4% over 90, with the current quarter down 3.2%. The Q2 beat pattern (four consecutive beats: +8.5%, +3.1%, +0.9%, +37.2%) supports confidence in the FY26 numbers, though the 37% Q2 surprise was driven substantially by the incentive and JV-income items described above rather than repeatable operating leverage. Management's medium-term framing is a roughly 6% revenue CAGR toward $16B+ by 2030, with FF(X) and BBG(X) as optionality above that.

On the July 29 submarine award, discipline is required. HII's own release specifies a "combined total of approximately $76.6 billion in contract modifications from the U.S. Navy to HII's Newport News Shipbuilding **and** General Dynamics Electric Boat," and discloses HII's role but not its dollar share. Electric Boat separately announced $29.5B for five Columbia boats, with multiple outlets characterizing EB's total as above $70B. Given the prime/subcontractor structure, HII's directly contracted portion is most plausibly a minority of the headline, supplemented by subcontract flow-through. None of it is in the 6/30/26 backlog. Any model crediting HII with $76.6B of backlog is materially wrong. The split should appear in the Q3 10-Q in late October.

Similarly, FF(X) is a lead-yard-support award (long-lead material, design, pre-construction) of undisclosed value with the first ship funded in the FY2027 *request*, and BBG(X) exists only as an advance-procurement budget line with no contract. Both are opportunities, not booked business.

## Valuation Assessment

At $321.76:

| TTM P/E | 19.2x |

| Guidance-derived FY26 EPS ($17.46-19.01) | 16.9x-18.4x |
| Enterprise value | $15.37B |
| EV / TTM EBITDA (~$1,020M) | 15.1x |
| Dividend yield | 1.71% |
| Payout on TTM EPS | 32.9% |

The P/E screens cheap against a peer median of 46.4x (DRS 46.4x, BWXT 51.9x, SARO 29.5x, TXT 17.9x, KTOS 342.5x), but that comparison is close to meaningless: the peer set contains two high-multiple nuclear/electronics names and one company at a cyclical earnings trough. The relevant comparison is Textron at 17.9x, and against that HII's 17.3x current-year multiple is a slight discount for a business with a stronger structural franchise and weaker margins.

The more informative read is EV/EBITDA at 15.1x versus P/E at 19.2x. The gap exists because roughly $213M of annual pension credit and the tax assumption sit below the EBITDA line, flattering net income relative to operating cash generation. A 15x EV/EBITDA multiple on a 6.5%-margin shipbuilder with negative first-half free cash flow is not obviously cheap. That reconciliation is the reason we do not score valuation above 6.0 despite a mid-teens forward P/E.

Scenario framing:

- **Base $353** — 17.0x next-year consensus of $20.74. This assumes FY26 FCF lands within or near guidance, shipbuilding margin holds 6.0-6.5%, and the Q3 backlog disclosure of HII's submarine share is respectable but not transformational. +9.7% from spot.
- **Bull $430** — 20x on approximately $21.50, reflecting delivered H2 cash conversion, shipbuilding margin trending toward 7%+, and a disclosed submarine share plus FF(X) construction award that visibly extends the 2030 revenue path. This approximates the analyst high of $439. +33.6%.
- **Bear $240** — 13x on approximately $18.00, reflecting an FY26 FCF miss of $300M+, a resumption of net unfavorable EAC adjustments, and/or a quantified welding-matter resolution. -25.4%.

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 base/bull/bear gives approximately +7% expected return. Consensus targets ($304 low, $366.67 mean, $367 median, $439 high) bracket the base case closely; sentiment is characterized as improving, with the ratings distribution at 1 strong buy, 5 buy, 6 hold and both Wells Fargo and Deutsche Bank reiterating Hold in early August. On a 15.5x next-year multiple with rising estimates, we would normally treat consensus targets as a lagging floor. Here the mean target sits only 14% above spot and two houses reiterated Hold *after* the beat, which argues the sell side sees the same cash-flow question we do rather than simply lagging price.

On the drawdown: the shares are 30.1% below the March 2 high of $460 and were 41.6% below it at the July 21 low of $268.76. That decline is explained, not mysterious — an 11% single-day fall on February 5 with FY25 results and initial guidance, and a 10% fall on May 5 on Q1 cash flow. The market has been repricing cash conversion, and on the evidence it has been right to demand proof. We are not arguing the sellers missed something; we are arguing the July trough overshot and the August rebound has now removed the margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

There is no credible new entrant in U.S. naval shipbuilding. Nuclear certification, dry-dock capital intensity, security clearance infrastructure and the national-security framework make the carrier and amphibious franchises effectively unassailable, and the destroyer duopoly with Bath Iron Works is stable. Foreign yards are entering as suppliers and productivity partners — the HD HHI welding pilot is technology transfer into HII's yard, not Korean entry into U.S. naval construction.

Two qualifications keep the score at 7.0 rather than higher. First, on submarines — the largest and fastest-growing demand pool — HII is the junior contracting party, which caps its share of the economics and its pricing authority on the very program driving the appropriations cycle. Second, the trajectory within HII's own portfolio is mixed: Ingalls margin has halved from 2023, Mission Technologies is shrinking organically with $2.65B of goodwill sitting against it, and shipbuilding margins guided at 6.0-6.5% remain far below both mid-teens platform-prime norms and HII's own pre-2020 levels. Management's stated expectation that new awards will "establish balanced risk equation and have margins more consistent with historical norms" is the multi-year bull case, and it is not yet visible in reported results outside Newport News. Over the five-year window HII's TSR index of 223 essentially matched its peer group at 220 — the market has awarded neither a franchise premium nor a discount.

## Risk Assessment

**Welding-procedure noncompliance at Newport News (highest unpriced risk).** Identified in 2024, self-disclosed to the government, and as of the July 2026 10-Q HII "continues to work with its U.S. Navy customer to evaluate the full extent of the matter and cannot at this time predict or reasonably estimate the ultimate outcome." No reserve. The same note carries the standard reminder that adverse findings can lead to suspension or debarment, which "would have a material effect on the Company." Two years unresolved on a nuclear quality matter, with no quantification, is the single item most capable of an unmodeled move.

**EAC volatility on roughly half the revenue base.** $223M of gross unfavorable adjustments in 1H2026 alone shows the pre-COVID contract cleanup management describes as nearly complete is still producing charges. A single hull can generate a $48M hit.

**FCF guidance credibility.** The required H2 conversion is unprecedented in available history, and the company missed its own Q2 range. This is the near-term binary.

**Appropriations timing.** A 43-day shutdown ran October 1 to November 12, 2025. The FY2027 request cleared House Appropriations at the President's topline but the Senate committee had not marked up as of the Q2 10-Q.

**Earnings composition.** The $213M pension credit is approximately a quarter of derived pretax income, non-cash, and sensitive to discount rates and asset returns; the guided approximately 17% tax rate implies an H2 rate near 15% with an embedded discrete benefit, and the IRS opened a new audit of 2022-2023 R&D credits in Q1 2026.

**Goodwill against a shrinking segment.** $2,650M, 20.9% of assets, concentrated in Mission Technologies, which declined 1.2% organically in the half. No impairment indicated; worth monitoring.

**Labor cost.** The strike risk is gone through 2030, but the "meaningful wage enhancements" in the new agreements are unquantified and flow into EAC estimates on legacy fixed-price-incentive contracts.

**Resolved or receding.** The no-poach antitrust class action settled in March 2026 at a non-material amount. The COVID business-interruption insurance appeal to the Vermont Supreme Court is an upside option, not a liability.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is complacent into a two-sided catalyst. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.1% against 37.7% trailing realized (cheap by 5.6 points), at the 12th percentile of the current chain, with skew at −4.5% (call-heavy) and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.06 — effectively no standing downside protection. IV collapsed from 46.3% to 32.1% between July 19 and August 7 while the price rose 20.6%, meaning the post-earnings relief has been priced as resolution rather than deferral of the cash-flow question to October. The term structure is flat (32.1% near, 33.9% at six months) with the December tenor implying ±17.4%.

The read: options are inexpensive for anyone who wants either protection through the Q3 print or upside exposure to a favorable submarine-share disclosure, and the near-total absence of puts means an H2 cash shortfall would land on a positioning base with no cushion. Short interest of 3.0% of float and 2.1 days to cover confirms there is no bearish crowd — but also no short base to squeeze on good news.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($430, +33.6%).** Throughput is the whole story and it is working: 18% year-over-year improvement in Q1, a 15% full-year target, doubled outsourced hours, robotic welding deployment, and $900M of pay-for-output supplier commitments. That converts into the shipbuilding revenue raise already delivered and, over 2027-2029, into margins approaching HII's historical norms as pre-COVID fixed-price contracts roll off and new awards price with balanced risk. Demand is legislated, including more than $29B of mandatory multi-year maritime funding that survives continuing resolutions. Backlog at $57.3B and 4.34x revenue does not yet include HII's share of the July submarine modifications or any FF(X) construction award. The CBA extension removes strike risk through 2030. At 15.5x next-year consensus with four consecutive beats and rising estimates, a re-rating toward 20x on $21.50 as H2 cash conversion validates the guide is a reasonable path, and the $1.35B unused buyback authorization becomes a lever once capex peaks.

**Bear case ($240, -25.4%).** The Q2 beat is lower-quality than the headline: Newport News margin improvement is incentive-and-adjustment driven with the 10-Q explicitly citing "lower performance in aircraft carriers," Mission Technologies' entire margin gain traces to JV equity income and falling amortization on declining revenue, and roughly a quarter of pretax income is a non-cash pension credit alongside a tax rate assumption requiring an H2 discrete benefit. Meanwhile the business consumed $611M of cash in six months, holds $12M on the balance sheet, is stepping capex to 4-5% of sales, and needs 2.1-2.3x last year's H2 free cash flow to hit a guide it already missed one quarter of. Ingalls margin has halved since 2023. The welding investigation is two years open with no reserve on a nuclear program. At 15.1x EV/EBITDA the shares are not cheap on operating cash generation, and a $300M+ FCF miss in October with any resumption of net unfavorable EAC adjustments compresses the multiple back toward the July lows.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fifth consecutive), classified as a growth hold.** The 12-month thesis is positive — margin recovery, legislated demand, an under-appreciated labor de-risking, and undisclosed backlog additions still to be booked — but the entry point no longer offers adequate asymmetry after the three-week rally off the July 21 low.

- **Base case $353** — 17.0x next-year consensus EPS of $20.74. +9.7% from $321.76.
- **Bull case $430** — 20x on approximately $21.50. +33.6%.
- **Bear case $240** — 13x on approximately $18.00. -25.4%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 free cash flow that puts the FY26 $500-600M guide within reach — approximately $700M+ of Q3 FCF, with the $926M net contract asset build visibly reversing on the balance sheet.
2. Disclosure in the Q3 10-Q of HII's dollar share of the July 29 submarine modifications at a level that lifts backlog materially above $60B, combined with a quantified FF(X) construction award.
3. A price near $270-285 with FY26 guidance intact, which would restore approximately 25%+ upside to the base case; alternatively, shipbuilding margin printing above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters on non-incentive-driven improvement.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY26 FCF guidance cut, or Q3 FCF below approximately $400M, which would make the annual guide arithmetically unreachable and reopen the February/May derating pattern.
2. A quantified reserve or contract action arising from the Newport News welding investigation, or any suspension/debarment language moving from generic risk-factor to specific disclosure.
3. Net unfavorable cumulative catch-up adjustments exceeding $75M in a quarter, or Ingalls segment margin falling below 6.0%.
4. Mission Technologies revenue declining more than 5% year over year with a goodwill impairment indicator disclosed.

**Reassessment timeline:** late October 2026 (Q3 10-Q and earnings release), with an interim check on Senate Appropriations FY2027 markup progress.

## Conclusion

The trajectory in our scoring — 6.4 in April to 5.8 in July across four straight declines — reflected deteriorating cash conversion, a shrinking technology segment and margin erosion at Ingalls. The Q2 print reverses part of that: revenue accelerated, shipbuilding guidance rose on throughput rather than accounting, backlog hit a record, and the labor tail risk that hung over Pascagoula has been extended to 2031. Our score improves to 5.95, located in growth prospects and valuation. It does not improve in management quality or risk, because the reaffirmed free cash flow guide is unverified, the company already missed its own quarterly cash guide, buybacks have been dormant for six-plus quarters against a $1.35B authorization, the only discretionary insider disposition signal in the record is a 15,000-share 10b5-1 plan with no offsetting open-market purchases, and the welding investigation remains open and unquantified after two years.

At $321.76 the shares are neither expensive nor compelling. Fifteen and a half times next-year earnings for the sole builder of American aircraft carriers is a defensible price, but the 15.1x EV/EBITDA and the negative first-half free cash flow explain why that P/E is not a bargain, and the sell side reiterating Hold after a 37% earnings beat suggests the discount reflects an identified problem rather than neglect. Existing owners should hold through the October print; new capital has a better entry in the $270-285 area or, absent that, after the Q3 report demonstrates that the cash is actually collectible.
