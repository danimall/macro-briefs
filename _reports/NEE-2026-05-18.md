## Executive Summary

NextEra Energy announced its $66.8 billion all-stock acquisition of Dominion Energy on May 18, 2026, creating the world's largest regulated electric utility. The deal thesis centers on combining NextEra's renewables development platform with Dominion's Northern Virginia territory — the largest concentration of data centers globally — where PJM projects 5.4% annual peak demand growth. The strategic logic is sound: AI-driven electricity demand is structural and growing, and the combined entity would control critical infrastructure in the highest-growth corridor.

However, the stock at $87.26 (23.7x forward P/E, 27% premium to peer median 18.6x) already prices significant growth expectations while inadequately discounting the deal's substantial execution risk. Six regulatory approvals are required across FERC, antitrust authorities, and four state utility commissions (FL, VA, NC, SC), creating a multi-year timeline with meaningful probability of adverse conditions or deal failure. Meanwhile, NEE carries $89.6B in long-term debt with interest expense that grew from $1.4B to $3.5B over 2022-2025, and the all-stock deal will add Dominion's $40B+ debt to the combined balance sheet.

The current macro environment — with 30-year Treasury yields above 5.1% and CPI at 3.8% — is particularly hostile to heavily leveraged, duration-sensitive utilities. NEE has underperformed both the S&P 500 and the S&P 500 Utilities Index over five years (18.2% TSR vs. 96.2% and 59.1% respectively). While the AI power demand thesis provides genuine secular support, the combination of deal uncertainty, rate sensitivity, and premium valuation warrants a HOLD until regulatory clarity emerges.

## Company Overview

NextEra Energy operates through two principal segments. **Florida Power & Light (FPL)** is the largest U.S. electric utility with over 6 million customer accounts, 35,963 MW of net generating capacity, and a new rate agreement providing $945M (2026) and $705M (2027) base rate increases through December 2029. **NextEra Energy Resources (NEER)** is one of the largest U.S. energy infrastructure developers with 37,505 MW across 44 states, including 22,404 MW wind, 10,504 MW solar, and 5,177 MW battery storage. Approximately 95% of NEER's capacity is contracted with a weighted-average remaining term of ~14 years.

The company employs ~17,400 people and holds a market capitalization of $182 billion. NEE's competitive identity rests on three pillars: scale in regulated Florida generation/distribution, leadership in U.S. renewables development, and capital markets expertise in monetizing clean energy tax credits.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Operating income recovered to $8.3B in 2025 from $7.5B in 2024, though well below the $10.2B peak in 2023. Operating margin of 30.2% and net margin of 19.5% are solid for a utility. ROE of 9.8% is adequate but below the 11.70% earned regulatory ROE at FPL.

The critical divergence between GAAP and adjusted EPS requires attention: GAAP EPS declined from $3.60 (2023) to $3.30 (2025), while adjusted EPS grew from $3.17 to $3.71 over the same period. The gap is driven by XPLR impairments ($2.5B cumulative over three years), non-qualifying hedge mark-to-market volatility, and interest rate derivative losses. These are real economic costs being excluded from the primary metric management uses for compensation and guidance.

**Capital Structure:**
- Long-term debt: $89.6B (up 23.7% YoY from $72.4B)
- Debt/equity: 2.68
- Interest expense: $3.5B in 2025 (up from $1.4B in 2022, a 150% increase)
- Net available liquidity: $18.7B
- Credit ratings: Baa1/A-/A- (stable outlook)
- Altman Z-Score: 0.79 (structurally low for utilities; not indicative of actual default risk given regulated cash flows)

Annual capital investments of ~$25B require continuous capital market access. NEE relies on approximately 75 banks across its credit facilities. The new ATM equity program of $4B (established December 2025) provides additional funding flexibility but signals ongoing equity dilution.

**Segment Performance (2025):**
- FPL net income: $5.01B (+10.3% YoY), driven by rate base growth and higher authorized ROE
- NEER net income: $2.98B (+29.4% YoY), driven by $967M after-tax new investment contributions
- Corporate: $(1.15)B loss (vs. $104M income prior year), primarily interest rate derivatives

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $12.5B less capex of $24.6B yields a significant funding gap of ~$12B annually, covered by debt issuance, equity issuance, and tax credit monetization (differential membership interests yielded $3.3B in 2025). FCF of $3.8B represents a 2.1% yield on market cap.

## Growth Analysis

NEE's growth thesis rests on four drivers:

**1. FPL Rate Base Growth:** The 2025 rate agreement provides visibility through 2029 with authorized rate increases and ROE of 10.95% (range 9.95%-11.95%). FPL's new large-load tariff (≥50 MW, ≥85% load factor) targets data center customers in Florida. Rate base grew ~$5.5B in 2025.

**2. NEER Renewables Deployment:** 6,262 MW added in 2025 (wind, solar, battery). However, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act phases out wind/solar tax credits for projects not beginning construction before July 5, 2026 or placed in service after December 31, 2027, with complete elimination after 2030. This represents a structural headwind to NEER's business model post-2030.

**3. AI/Data Center Power Demand:** The dominant growth narrative. PJM projects 5.4% annual summer peak demand growth in Dominion's territory. The Dominion acquisition is explicitly positioned to capture this demand.

**4. Nuclear Renaissance:** Duane Arnold restart (targeted 2029 commercial operation) with a 25-year PPA reportedly signed with a major technology company. Point Beach units contracted through 2030-2033. Nuclear receives favorable treatment under revised tax credit legislation.

Consensus estimates reflect modest growth: $4.02 current year, $4.39 next year (9.2% growth). Estimate revisions are flat — no significant upward or downward momentum. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (12.5%, 1.7%, 10.0%, 3.0%) provides some confidence in management's ability to deliver.

Management has guided to 6-8% adjusted EPS CAGR, which historically they have delivered (7.8% CAGR from 2021-2025). Whether this growth rate justifies the current premium multiple depends on deal execution and rate normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.26, NEE trades at:
- Forward P/E: 23.7x (per Phase 1 data)
- Trailing P/E: 23.7x (on GAAP EPS of $3.30, implying ~26.4x)
- Dividend yield: 2.67%
- FCF yield: 2.1%

**Peer comparison:**
- NEE (23.7x) vs. peer median (18.6x) = 27% premium
- Sempra (31.7x), Xcel (23.0x), Dominion (18.6x), ConEd (17.9x), PG&E (13.0x)
- NEE's dividend yield (2.67%) is below peer median (2.9%)

The premium is historically justified by NEE's superior growth rate (6-8% vs. sector 3-5%) and renewables platform optionality. However, several factors challenge the premium's sustainability:

1. **GAAP vs. adjusted divergence** — if valued on GAAP EPS ($3.30), the multiple is effectively ~26.4x
2. **Post-2030 tax credit sunset** — NEER's negative effective tax rate (-18% in 2025) cannot persist beyond 2030 for new wind/solar
3. **Dominion deal dilution** — all-stock transaction with ~$66.8B equity issuance will dilute existing shareholders significantly
4. **Rate environment** — 30Y >5.1% compresses utility multiples through competitive yield dynamics and increases refinancing costs

Analyst consensus target of $99.16 (median $102.50) implies ~13.6% upside, but targets were likely set before the Dominion announcement and the recent rate spike. The wide range ($55-$112) reflects genuine uncertainty about the deal and macro environment.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Largest U.S. electric utility in a high-growth state (Florida population growth, retirement migration)
- Largest U.S. renewables developer with operating scale advantages (6,262 MW deployed in 2025 alone)
- 95% contracted NEER capacity with ~14-year average remaining term
- Strong regulatory relationships (demonstrated by favorable 2025 FPL rate agreement)
- Nuclear operating expertise (unique competency for restart opportunities)

**Challenges:**
- Post-2030 tax credit phase-out removes a primary competitive advantage in renewables economics
- Federal permitting uncertainty for wind (executive order pausing federal land leasing)
- Interconnection queue reforms may slow NEER deployment
- The Dominion acquisition shifts competitive positioning from growth utility toward mega-utility, potentially compressing the growth premium as the regulated rate base becomes a larger share

The new large-load tariff at FPL and the Dominion acquisition together represent a strategic pivot toward data center load as the primary growth vector. This is directionally correct given the AI infrastructure buildout, but execution requires regulatory cooperation across multiple jurisdictions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Dominion Deal Execution (HIGH — Binary):**
Multi-year process with six required approvals. State utility commissions may impose adverse conditions (rate freezes, investment requirements, synergy sharing). Deal failure would leave NEE with elevated costs (legal, advisory) and a stock that has appreciated on deal-driven demand expectations.

**2. Interest Rate Sensitivity (HIGH — Active):**
Every 50bp increase in refinancing rates adds approximately $450M in annual interest on rolling maturities. NEE's cost of debt is structurally rising. The company needs continuous capital market access for its annual investment program — any disruption is existential.

**3. Clean Energy Tax Credit Sunset (MEDIUM — Structural):**
The OBBBA eliminates wind/solar credits post-2030. NEE's current pipeline qualifies, but the long-term business model for NEER requires either (a) unsubsidized renewable economics becoming competitive, (b) nuclear/storage replacing wind/solar as growth vectors, or (c) a future legislative reversal.

**4. Hurricane Exposure (MEDIUM — Recurring):**
FPL's Florida coastal territory faces annual storm risk. 2024 hurricanes cost $1.2B in surcharges. The new rate agreement caps surcharges at $5/1,000 kWh for 12 months, creating potential for under-recovery in severe storm years.

**5. XPLR Impairments (LOW-MEDIUM — Ongoing):**
Three consecutive years of impairments ($963M, $852M, $656M) on the ~52.5% XPLR stake. Additional impairments possible.

## Conclusion

NextEra Energy holds a genuinely advantaged competitive position: the largest U.S. utility, the largest U.S. renewables developer, and now attempting to acquire the territory serving the world's largest data center concentration. The AI power demand thesis is structural and multi-year. However, at 23.7x forward earnings (27% premium to peers) with debt growing at double-digit rates in a hostile rate environment, the risk/reward at current levels is asymmetric to the downside on a 6-12 month horizon.

The Dominion acquisition introduces 12-24 months of regulatory uncertainty that cannot be resolved quickly. The stock has already appreciated 25% over the past year, pricing in much of the AI demand thesis before deal economics are confirmed. With no insider buying, flat estimate revisions, and options markets pricing elevated near-term risk, the correct posture is to wait for either regulatory clarity (approvals) or price clarity (multiple compression toward peer median) before taking a position.

HOLD. Reassess after Q2 earnings and initial regulatory filings.
