## Executive Summary

Micron Technology is in the most powerful earnings upcycle in its 47-year history. Q2 FY2026 delivered $23.9B in revenue (+196% YoY), 75% gross margins, and $12.20 in EPS. Q3 guidance of $33.5B revenue at 81% gross margins implies continued strong growth, though sequential growth is moderating from ~75% (FQ1 to FQ2) to ~40% (FQ2 to FQ3). The fundamental driver is HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) demand from hyperscale AI infrastructure buildouts, where Micron is one of only three global suppliers and where supply remains sold out through calendar 2026 with allocations extending into 2027.

At the current price of ~$577, the stock trades at approximately 10x current fiscal year consensus EPS ($58.08 current year, $101.47 next year). This reflects legitimate cyclical risk but materially underprices the structural nature of AI-driven memory demand if the current demand environment proves durable. Forward estimates have revised upward by 74-134% over the past 90 days — a rate of positive revision rarely seen in large-cap semiconductors.

The key tension is between the structural thesis (AI memory demand is durable, multi-year, and supply-constrained) and cyclical reality (Micron posted a $5.8B net loss as recently as FY2023). If current earnings represent even 60% of normalized run-rate rather than peak-cycle, the stock offers significant upside. If they prove peak-cycle, the downside is severe.

## Company Overview

Micron Technology operates as one of three companies globally capable of manufacturing DRAM at scale (alongside Samsung and SK hynix), forming a tight oligopoly that controls >95% of the world's DRAM supply. The company reorganized into four business units in FY2025:

- **CMBU (Cloud Memory):** 36% of FY2025 revenue; reported $7.75B in Q2 FY2026 (32.5% of quarterly revenue). Houses HBM and hyperscale memory solutions. Revenue grew from $1.87B (FY2023) to $13.52B (FY2025) — a 623% increase over two years.
- **CDBU (Core Data Center):** 19% of FY2025 revenue. Mid-tier enterprise and data center SSDs. Combined, CMBU + CDBU represented 56.3% of Q2 FY2026 revenue ($13.4B), reflecting the company's broad data-center exposure.
- **MCBU (Mobile & Client):** 32% of FY2025 revenue. Memory/storage for smartphones and PCs.
- **AEBU (Automotive & Embedded):** 13%. Automotive, industrial, IoT.

Manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan (majority of DRAM output), with additional facilities in Singapore, Japan, the U.S. (Virginia, Idaho under construction), and Malaysia. The CHIPS Act provides up to $6.4B in grants for new U.S. facilities, with Idaho fabs expected to produce output beginning H2 2027.

## Growth Analysis

### The AI Memory Demand Thesis

Micron's growth story rests on three structural pillars:

1. **HBM demand sold out through 2026, with the FY2027 order book rapidly filling.** CEO Mehrotra stated the company can only deliver a "fraction" (50-66%) of key customer allocation requests. Micron's SEC filings disclose that one customer — primarily in the CMBU segment and likely Nvidia based on public commentary, though not named in filings — accounted for 17% of FY2025 revenue and 13% of first-half FY2026 revenue.

2. **HBM4 ahead of schedule.** Mass production began in March 2026 for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, one quarter ahead of plan. This maintains Micron's competitive position in the highest-margin product.

3. **AI model scaling requires memory scaling.** Each generation of large language models requires exponentially more memory. The Blackwell architecture requires 4x more HBM per GPU than its predecessor.

### Forward Estimates

The estimate revision data is extraordinary:
- Current quarter EPS: $18.97 (up 101% over 90 days)
- Next quarter: $22.54 (up 120% over 90 days)
- FY2028 EPS: $101.47 (up 134% over 90 days)

These represent wholesale repricing of the company's earnings power. The 30-day revisions remain positive (+0.1% to +3.0%), indicating the upward trend has not yet plateaued.

### Growth Risks

The counter-thesis centers on cyclical reversion. Memory has historically been commoditized, with pricing cycles of extreme amplitude. Google's research into memory compression for LLMs represents a medium-term risk to volume growth, though the historical pattern in computing is that efficiency gains increase total demand by enabling new use cases. The more immediate risk is that hyperscaler AI capex could moderate or defer if ROI proof points disappoint.

## Valuation Assessment

### Absolute Valuation

At $577 per share:
- Trailing P/E on FY2025 EPS ($7.59): ~76x (reflecting the stale base year before the current earnings surge)
- Forward P/E (current year, $58.08): 9.9x
- Forward P/E (next year, $101.47): 5.7x
- EV/Revenue (annualized Q2 run-rate): ~6.8x
- Price/FCF (annualized Q2 rate of $6.9B/quarter): ~24x

The trailing P/E on FY2025 EPS is misleading because it uses the depressed earnings base before the AI-driven surge that began in FQ1 2026. The more relevant metrics are the forward multiples, which reflect the company's current earnings power.

The stock has appreciated ~597% over the trailing 12 months. The current price exceeds the analyst consensus median target of $549 and the mean of $551, though the high target is $1,000. The stock is trading near 52-week high levels.

### Relative Valuation

Versus semiconductor peers, Micron trades at a significant discount on forward earnings. Direct P/E comparisons across the semiconductor sector are complicated by the fact that peers sit at different points in their respective earnings cycles. AMD trades at an elevated trailing P/E (~106x) reflecting its own investment cycle, while TSM trades at approximately 31x trailing earnings and TXN at approximately 41x. Micron's low forward multiple (9.9x current year) reflects the market's cyclical discount — effectively pricing these earnings as potentially temporary.

If even 50% of the earnings uplift proves structural (driven by permanently higher memory demand from AI), the fair multiple should be substantially above current levels.

### The Cyclical Valuation Challenge

The core valuation question: is $101.47 in next-year EPS (consensus) sustainable, or is it peak-cycle? Historical memory cycles suggest peaks are followed by 50-70% earnings declines. However, the structural demand argument (AI infrastructure buildout is multi-year, not single-quarter) and oligopolistic supply discipline (only 3 producers, all investing for growth rather than flooding capacity) suggest this cycle may have a longer duration than historical precedent.

A scenario-weighted approach: if next-year EPS is $101.47 and the stock deserves 12x earnings (cyclical premium for structural growth), fair value is ~$1,218. If earnings prove to be peak-cycle and revert 50% over two years to ~$50, a 15x trough multiple on normalized earnings produces ~$750. The weighted average of these scenarios produces upside from current levels, but the range is exceptionally wide.

## Competitive Landscape

### Oligopoly Dynamics

The DRAM market's three-player structure provides structural pricing support. Recent industry data suggests SK hynix may be overtaking Samsung as the largest DRAM producer, with approximate shares of SK hynix ~36%, Samsung ~34%, and Micron ~25% as of early 2025. Unlike the more fragmented NAND market, DRAM has no meaningful fourth player. China's CXMT remains a generation behind on technology and constrained by U.S. equipment export controls.

### HBM Competitive Position

In HBM specifically: SK hynix leads (first to market with each generation), Micron is second (ahead of schedule on HBM4), and Samsung is catching up. Samsung faces labor unrest and technical challenges. Micron's "first to ship 1-gamma DRAM" with EUV lithography demonstrates technology leadership in the underlying node, which feeds into HBM production efficiency.

### Moat Assessment

The DRAM oligopoly represents a genuine economic moat: barriers to entry are $20B+ for a single greenfield fab, technology development cycles are 3-5 years, and yield management expertise takes decades to develop. The moat is widening as HBM requires additional packaging complexity and tighter integration with GPU designers (principally Nvidia).

## Risk Assessment

### Tier 1 — Material Risks

**Cyclical Reversion (High Probability, High Impact):** Memory has been the most cyclical subsector in semiconductors for 40 years. A deceleration in AI spending or shift to lower-memory architectures could compress margins from 81% to 30-40% within 2-3 quarters. The current margin structure requires continued supply tightness and relentless demand growth.

**Taiwan Production Concentration (Low Probability, Catastrophic Impact):** A majority of DRAM output comes from Taiwan. Any disruption — military, natural disaster, or political — would threaten the company's production capacity. U.S. fabs under construction won't produce volume until 2027+.

**Customer Concentration (Moderate Probability, High Impact):** Over 56% of Q2 FY2026 revenue flows from data center segments (CMBU + CDBU), with the single large customer accounting for 13% of first-half FY2026 revenue. Any significant reduction in AI accelerator production plans flows directly to Micron demand.

**Capital Expenditure Burden (Moderate Probability, Medium-High Impact):** With FY2026 capex expected above $25B net of government incentives, Micron is making enormous capital commitments. If demand softens mid-buildout, the company faces a period of excess capacity and compressed returns on invested capital, reminiscent of prior cyclical downturns.

### Tier 2 — Monitoring Risks

**Executive Selling:** $50M+ in insider sales over February-April 2026. While context (stock up 500%+, executives retain substantial holdings) mitigates the signal, the volume warrants attention.

**Valuation Above Consensus:** At $577, the stock exceeds the consensus target of ~$549-551. The market is pricing in continued upward revisions, which requires continued perfect execution.

**Tax Headwinds:** Effective tax rate moving from ~12% to mid-to-high teens starting FY2026-2027 (Singapore Pillar Two, U.S. legislation). At $100+ EPS, every percentage point of tax rate increase reduces EPS by ~$1.20.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($750-850, +30-47%)

- AI memory demand proves structural rather than cyclical; hyperscaler capex is a multi-year commitment
- HBM4 production ramps ahead of schedule, with Micron capturing market share from Samsung
- Q3 FY2026 guidance is met or beaten, forcing further consensus revisions upward
- Next-year EPS reaches $100+ as consensus projects, and the market awards a 7-8x multiple on next-year earnings (historically generous for memory but justified by structural demand)
- Supply discipline holds: no producer floods the market, maintaining 70%+ margins through FY2027
- U.S. CHIPS Act fabs reduce Taiwan concentration risk, earning a geopolitical premium

### Bear Case ($350-400, -30-39%)

- AI infrastructure spending decelerates as hyperscalers demand ROI proof; capex budgets plateau in calendar 2027
- Memory pricing follows historical pattern: as new capacity comes online (Micron Idaho, SK hynix, Samsung), supply exceeds demand, compressing margins from 81% toward 40-50%
- Efficiency breakthroughs in memory compression moderate memory-per-GPU requirements, slowing volume growth
- Geopolitical event disrupts Taiwan production or triggers customer diversification away from Micron
- Insider selling proves prescient rather than routine
- Earnings normalize to $60-70 EPS range with market applying a 5.5-6x multiple, yielding $350-400

## Conclusion

Micron represents the purest play on AI memory demand within a structurally favorable oligopoly. The financial trajectory — revenue growing ~75% sequentially in FQ2 with another ~40% guided for FQ3, margins approaching software-like levels, FCF inflecting to $6.9B/quarter — is supported by hard evidence (supply sold out, allocation requests exceeding capacity, HBM4 ahead of schedule).

The stock has moved enormously (+597% in 12 months) and now trades above consensus targets, which ordinarily would counsel patience. However, the rate of estimate revisions (+101-134% over 90 days) suggests consensus is still catching up to reality. At 9.9x current-year and 5.7x next-year earnings, even accounting for cyclicality, the stock is not expensive if the structural demand thesis holds for 2-3 more years.

The recommendation is BUY with a 2-3% position size, recognizing that: (a) the stock will exhibit 30-40% volatility in either direction given its beta and sector exposure to geopolitical risk, (b) the thesis is contingent on sustained AI infrastructure spending, and (c) the 81% guided gross margins represent the highest in company history and may mark a near-term ceiling even if absolute earnings continue to grow.

The primary risk management consideration is position sizing — memory is inherently cyclical and even if the structural thesis is correct, interim drawdowns of 25-35% are normal and should not trigger exits. The thesis breaks only on fundamental demand deterioration, not price volatility.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** FY2026 capex is above $25B (not ~$18B as originally modeled), and the relative valuation comparison to semiconductor peers was overstated; these corrections reduce confidence in the base/bull price targets and highlight greater capital intensity risk than initially presented.
