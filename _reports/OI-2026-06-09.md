**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

O-I Glass is one of the world's largest glass-container manufacturers, with 64 plants across 18 countries and roughly $6.4B in 2025 revenue. The equity has lost ~42% over twelve months and sits at $7.94-8.24, near a 52-week low of $7.75, with a market cap of ~$1.3B against ~$5.0B of total debt. The central problem is structural: glass packaging faces secular volume erosion to aluminum and PET, the company has posted three consecutive years of GAAP net losses (2023-2025, deepening to -$129M last year), and a 2026 European energy-cost reset collapsed the Europe segment's operating profit to zero in Q1, forcing a full-year guidance cut on adjusted EPS, FCF, and EBITDA.

The bull case is a leveraged turnaround on cost-out: the "Fit to Win" program delivered ~$240M of savings in 2025 (above plan) and targets ≥$750M cumulative through 2027, segment operating profit was up 13% to $846M in 2025, and operating cash flow ran $600M. At ~0.2x price-to-sales, the equity is priced like an option on deleveraging. A cluster of open-market insider buying in May 2026 — CFO, General Counsel, two SVPs, and a director making his first open-market purchase in two-plus years, all near the 52-week low — is the most credible bullish data point in the file and deserves genuine weight.

It is not enough. The balance sheet (D/E 6.4x, Altman Z 0.91, a covenant Secured Leverage Ratio cap of 2.50x, and springing-maturity provisions that accelerate the entire secured facility if the 2027/2028 senior notes aren't refinanced 91 days ahead) leaves thin margin for error as European pricing and volumes deteriorate. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 20-46% over 90 days across every horizon, the strongest forward signal in the dataset and squarely negative. Q1 missed by 72%. We rate OI **AVOID**: this is a distressed turnaround, not a clean short, and the insider buying and operating-cash-flow generation cap the downside enough that active shorting is unattractive.

## Company Overview

O-I Glass supplies glass containers to beer, wine, spirits, non-alcoholic beverage, and food customers under annual and multi-year supply agreements, organized into two reportable segments:

- **Americas** — ~$3.64B net sales in 2025 (the more profitable segment, $549M operating profit), spanning North America and Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Colombia).
- **Europe** — ~$2.69B, concentrated in France and Italy, and highly exposed to natural gas costs. This is the swing factor and is deteriorating.

The business is ~73% non-U.S. by sales, with France, Italy, and Mexico each above 10% of revenue, plus 50/50 joint ventures in Italy and Mexico. The moat rests on manufacturing scale and entrenched, sticky customer relationships, but glass competes against aluminum cans, PET, aseptic cartons, and pouches, and is on the wrong side of long-run consumer preference shifts and lower alcohol consumption.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory is a sustained slide from a 2022 peak:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 6,856 | 7,105 | 6,531 | 6,426 | 1,540 |
| Net income attrib. ($M) | 584 | (103) | (106) | (129) | (73) |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | 1,417 | 1,609 | 1,079 | 1,294 | 1,432 |

Revenue is down ~10% from the 2023 peak, driven by glass-shipment volume declines (−3% in 2025, accelerating to **−9% in Q1 2026**) on destocking in wine/spirits/beer, soft consumer demand, deliberate exits of unprofitable business, and tariff-driven inventory corrections in Mexico/North America beer.

The defining analytical tension is the gap between GAAP and operating results. Reported losses are inflated by restructuring/impairment charges ($443M in 2025, $206M in 2024), and segment operating profit rose 13% to $846M in 2025 on Fit to Win savings. The chain that turns operating profit into a net loss is mechanical: ~$341M of annual interest expense against a thin equity base, plus charges, plus (in 2026) collapsing European pricing. Operating cash flow was $600M in 2025, and management guides $500-600M for 2026 against ~$450M capex. Q1 is seasonally cash-negative (−$294M operating cash flow on working-capital build), and cash fell from $759M to $317M in the quarter.

Distress indicators are unambiguous: Altman Z-Score of 0.91 (deep distress zone), D/E of 6.4x, ~$5.0B total debt, ~30% floating rate. The company also carries a valuation allowance against substantially all U.S. deferred tax assets, citing "lack of sustainable earnings," a formal acknowledgment of cumulative-loss status. Capital allocation is defensive: a $40M anti-dilutive buyback (offsetting stock comp), no dividend, and priority on deleveraging and ~$150M/year of restructuring cash payments.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here. The end-market is in secular volume decline, and the company's own shipments fell 9% year-over-year in Q1. The "growth" that exists is mix-driven — a deliberate shift toward higher-value categories (premium spirits, food, non-alcoholic, ready-to-drink) that have outperformed mainstream beer/wine — and cost-driven, via Fit to Win.

The forward earnings signal is decisively negative. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut across every horizon:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | $0.31 | $0.57 | −46.1% |
| Current Year | $1.19 | $1.94 | −38.4% |
| Next Year | $1.90 | $2.37 | −19.8% |

Revisions of this magnitude and direction are among the strongest leading indicators of continued underperformance. The earnings-surprise record reinforces the deterioration: after beating by 26% and 10% in mid-2025, the company landed in-line in Q4 and then missed by 72% in Q1'26 ($0.05 actual vs. $0.18 estimate). The pattern is a clear loss of forward visibility, driven by the European energy reset. The 2027 recovery embedded in next-year estimates depends entirely on cost-out outrunning price/volume erosion and energy normalizing, neither of which is in hand.

## Valuation Assessment

OI screens cheap on sales (~0.2x P/S) and is not meaningfully valuable on earnings — trailing and forward P/E are both negative on GAAP losses. Against the peer set, the comparison is awkward because the peers are profitable and OI is not:

| Ticker | Name | P/E | Div Yield | 52w Disc. |
|--------|------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| GEF | Greif | 20.4 | 3.9% | −17% |
| SLGN | Silgan | 13.7 | 2.3% | −36% |
| ATR | AptarGroup | 19.4 | 1.7% | −32% |
| **OI** | **O-I Glass** | **neg.** | **0%** | **−53%** |

Peer median P/E is 19.4. OI cannot be valued on the same basis. On EV terms, the ~$5.0B net debt plus ~$1.3B equity gives an EV of roughly $6.3B against guided $500-600M of operating cash flow, a leveraged-equity profile where small changes in EBITDA swing the equity value sharply. On a normalized basis, if Fit to Win delivers and European energy normalizes by 2027, the next-year $1.90 EPS estimate would put the stock at ~4.3x forward earnings, which looks cheap. The problem is that the $1.90 estimate has itself been cut ~20% in 90 days and rests on assumptions (energy normalization, no further European price collapse, no additional impairment) that the Q1 print directly undercut.

This is option-like equity: the upside is real if deleveraging works, but the strike is contingent on outcomes that are deteriorating in the trailing data. We score valuation 4.5 — the discount is genuine, but it reflects structural impairment and distress risk rather than a clean mispricing, and the cheap multiple is not protective when the denominator is falling and the balance sheet is fragile.

## Competitive Landscape

O-I's competitive position is stable-to-eroding. Scale and customer entrenchment are real, but the industry faces secular volume headwinds (shift away from beer/wine, lower alcohol consumption, substitution to aluminum/PET/cartons), and customer consolidation among large brewers and beverage companies intensifies pricing pressure. The company explicitly cited "elevated competitive pressures" driving down European net prices even before the energy reset, which means the European pricing problem is partly structural, not solely an energy pass-through issue that reverses when gas costs fall.

The MAGMA halt is a meaningful competitive signal. O-I spent years positioning its proprietary modular glass-melting technology as a path to lower-carbon, lower-cost production. Abandoning it ($117M charge in Q2 2025) reframes the company as a cost-cutting/deleveraging story rather than a technology-led one, and removes a claimed differentiator on the EU ETS carbon-cost curve (free-allowance phase-out runs 2026-2034). The mix shift toward premium categories is a sensible defensive move, but it is margin defense, not share gain.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are concentrated, partly binary, and not fully priced:

1. **European energy reset — the acute near-term risk.** Favorable European gas contracts expired end-2025. Q1 2026 European segment operating profit collapsed to $0 (from $68M) on ~$76M of negative net price/inflation. Management guides ~$150M of higher European energy costs in 2026, plus a potential additional $75-100M from Middle East conflict-driven energy markets. The company hedged ~75-80% of 2026 European gas, with a price-cap derivative structure — above the cap, O-I bears full cost, which is the wrong exposure in an oil/gas spike scenario.

2. **Leverage and the refinancing cliff.** ~$5.0B debt, a 2.50x Secured Leverage Ratio covenant, and springing-maturity provisions: if the 6.625% notes due 2027 and 6.250% notes due 2028 aren't refinanced 91 days before maturity, the term loans and revolver accelerate. The September 2025 refinancing into a new $2.7B facility (TLA 2030, TLB 2032, $1.25B revolver) and a May 18, 2026 financing 8-K show this is being actively managed, but continued capital-markets access is a precondition for the equity having value. ~30% floating-rate debt adds rate sensitivity.

3. **Goodwill impairment.** $1.49B goodwill (16% of assets). North America goodwill was fully written off ($445M) in 2023. As of October 2025, the Europe reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~21%, a thin cushion ahead of the 2026 European earnings collapse. A further write-down is plausible and would hit the already-thin equity base.

4. **Other:** A French antitrust investigation with unestimable potential fines, legacy CERCLA/environmental liabilities (~$21M accrued), pension obligations, and a U.S. deferred-tax valuation allowance.

The energy-cost backdrop compounds these: a meaningfully priced oil/energy disruption tail is negative for an energy-cost-sensitive, refinancing-dependent issuer with ~30% floating-rate debt and a derivative structure that bears full cost above its price cap.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 69.9% against 1-year historical vol of 46.8% — rich by ~23 points, in the 78th percentile of the current chain, and in backwardation (near-term 69.9% > 6-month 63.3%). The market is pricing imminent stress around the June expiry, with an implied move of ±9.1%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−2.4% near-term) and put/call OI was 0.73 at the latest read, so this is not a one-way bearish bet; it reflects high uncertainty rather than directional conviction. Short interest at 11.5% of float (4.5 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional bearishness but is not at squeeze-risk extremes. The rich, backwardated IV and the high short interest both argue against initiating fresh short exposure at a depressed price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Fit to Win cost-out outruns price/volume erosion; European energy normalizes through 2026-2027; segment operating profit and operating cash flow deleverage the balance sheet ahead of the 2027/2028 maturities; the equity re-rates off ~0.2x P/S as the company returns to GAAP profitability. The May 2026 insider cluster signals management views the stock as a floor. Bull target ~$14-15 (back toward the 52-week high and the analyst high target).

**Bear case.** European pricing pressure proves structural rather than energy-linked, energy costs run above the hedge cap on a Middle East spike, volumes keep falling, a Europe goodwill impairment hits the thin equity, and refinancing the 2027/2028 notes proves costly or constrained, triggering springing-maturity stress. With 6.4x leverage, the equity behaves like a deep out-of-the-money option. Bear target ~$5-6 (−25% to −35%).

The base case sits closer to the bear: estimates are being cut hard, Q1 missed by 72%, and the most acute headwind (Europe) is current and partly unhedged above the cap. The cost-out program is real and the operating business generates cash, which is why this is a distress-discount rather than a zero, but the risk/reward does not favor ownership.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. The relevant question for the reader is what would change the view.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing-to-positive EPS estimate revisions (reversing the current −20% to −46% trend), confirming the European reset has bottomed.
- European segment operating profit recovering off the Q1 zero toward a run-rate above ~$40M/quarter, evidencing energy/pricing normalization rather than a one-quarter trough.
- Concrete refinancing of the 2027/2028 senior notes at manageable rates, removing the springing-maturity overhang and demonstrating durable capital-markets access.
- A return to GAAP profitability, or a Secured Leverage Ratio trajectory clearly tracking below the 2.50x covenant with cushion.

**Timeline:** The European energy headwind is a 2026 event by management's own framing, with the recovery narrative pinned to 2H 2026 and 2027. The refinancing overhang persists until the 2027/2028 notes are addressed. The headwinds should remain live through at least the next two to three quarters; the Q2'26 print (late July/early August) and the next financing update are the key checkpoints. A constructive turn is not confirmable before late 2026 at the earliest.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest negative catalyst is the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected late July/early August), where another European-energy-driven miss or a further guidance cut would pressure the stock, alongside any Europe goodwill impairment disclosure. The 2027/2028 refinancing is a slower-burn risk without a single dated trigger.

**Timing window.** The next earnings print is roughly 6-8 weeks out, within a workable window. The refinancing/maturity risk is 12-24 months out, too distant to carry as a short thesis.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 11.5% of float, 4.5 days to cover. Elevated but below the >20% threshold where squeeze risk dominates. The crowd is already partly positioned bearish, limiting incremental edge.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV is rich and in backwardation, so option-implied carrying cost is high and a directional put position pays a steep premium. Skew is balanced and put/call OI is 0.73 — the options market is pricing uncertainty, not directional downside conviction. That is a warning sign for shorts: the bearish thesis is not being confirmed by one-sided put demand.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$5-6 implies ~25-35% downside from $7.94-8.24. Upside risk is material: a credible cluster of insider buying near the lows, ~$600M of operating cash flow, a cheap ~0.2x P/S floor, and a high-IV setup that punishes timing errors. The reward:risk is roughly 1:1 to at best 1.5:1, below the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and the leverage is genuinely dangerous, but the combination of insider buying near the lows, real operating cash flow, a depressed valuation floor, rich borrow/IV, and a 1:1-ish payoff makes this a name to avoid owning rather than to short. This is a distressed turnaround, not an actively collapsing fraud or a refinancing failure in progress.

## Conclusion

O-I Glass screens as a leveraged-turnaround equity option: a structurally challenged business generating real segment operating profit and operating cash flow, weighed down by ~$5B of debt, three years of GAAP losses, and an acute 2026 European energy shock that drove a 72% Q1 miss and a full-year guidance cut. The score trajectory has improved modestly off a low base (3.4 → 4.1), and the May 2026 insider cluster is a credible signal that management sees value at these prices. The price decline from the mid-teens to ~$8 accompanies genuine fundamental deterioration (European profit to zero, volumes −9%, estimates cut 20-46%), so the lower price does not by itself improve the risk/reward. With Altman Z at 0.91, a 2.50x leverage covenant, springing-maturity acceleration risk on the 2027/2028 notes, and the dominant near-term headwind (Europe) still current and partly unhedged above its cap, the call is **AVOID**. Reassess if estimate revisions turn positive for two quarters, European segment profit recovers off zero, and the note maturities are refinanced cleanly. Overall score: **3.6/10**.
