## Executive Summary

The Hershey Company is navigating the aftermath of an unprecedented cocoa commodity shock that crushed gross margins by 1,380 basis points in FY2025, sending EPS from $10.92 to $4.34. The recovery thesis is straightforward: as record-high cocoa costs roll off inventories and cheaper beans enter the supply chain, margins should inflect in H2 2026 and normalize toward historical levels by 2027. Q1 2026 provided early confirmation — adjusted EPS of $2.35 beat consensus by 14%, net income rose 94% YoY, and management reiterated full-year guidance of $8.20-$8.52 adjusted EPS.

However, the recovery path carries significant risks. Cocoa prices remain volatile (surging ~5% in a single day in May 2026 on West African crop concerns), the compound chocolate substitution controversy damaged Reese's brand equity, and volume declines of 2-4% suggest consumers are resisting price increases. At $184.48 and ~22x FY2026 guided EPS midpoint, the stock prices in meaningful recovery but offers limited margin of safety should cocoa costs re-accelerate or demand weakness intensify. The 12-month thesis centers on gross margin inflection in H2 2026 and the salty snacks segment continuing to grow double-digits, providing a path to $9-10 EPS in FY2027.

## Company Overview

Hershey is the largest chocolate producer in North America with approximately 27% U.S. confectionery market share (by CMG). The business operates through three segments: North America Confectionery (81% of revenue), North America Salty Snacks (11%), and International (8%). The company owns iconic brands including Hershey's, Reese's, Kit Kat (U.S. license), Jolly Rancher, SkinnyPop, and Dot's Homestyle Pretzels.

A dual-class share structure with the Hershey Trust Company (trustee for Milton Hershey School) maintaining voting control via Class B shares makes hostile acquisition effectively impossible — Pennsylvania law requires Attorney General notice for any transaction that would end the Trust's control. Kirk Tanner, former PepsiCo/Frito-Lay executive, became CEO in August 2025 and is implementing a "ONE Hershey" unified commercial model.

GLP-1 drug adoption, SNAP benefit changes, and the "Make America Healthy Again" movement all represent secular demand headwinds for impulse confectionery.

## Competitive Landscape

Hershey's #1 U.S. confectionery position (~27% market share) is a durable competitive advantage. The business benefits from:

1. **Seasonal entrenchment:** Halloween, Valentine's, Easter, Christmas — Hershey's brands are synonymous with these occasions
2. **Convenience channel dominance:** ~27% of sales through McLane distribution to convenience/drug stores where private label penetration is lowest
3. **Scale advantages:** Largest U.S. chocolate manufacturer with 74% domestic manufacturing
4. **Brand portfolio depth:** 85+ brands across price points

However, competitive headwinds are accumulating:
- Lost ~10 bps of CMG market share in FY2025
- Private label growing as consumers seek value during inflation
- Mars (private) and Ferrero investing aggressively
- The compound chocolate controversy specifically undermined brand trust at a vulnerable moment

The salty snacks diversification (gaining 40 bps share in FY2025) partially offsets confectionery pressure, and CEO Tanner's Frito-Lay background suggests competent execution in this space. But at 11% of revenue, salty snacks cannot yet compensate for confectionery weakness.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Cocoa Volatility (HIGH):** Cocoa prices crashed 74% from peaks but surged 4.9% in a single day in May 2026 on weak West African crop outlook. Hershey's hedging program creates P&L timing mismatches ($491M MTM loss in FY2025). If cocoa re-accelerates to $8,000+/ton, the margin recovery thesis delays 2-3 quarters and FY2026 guidance becomes unachievable. Management describes cocoa as "structurally elevated" vs. historical levels.

**Secondary Risk — Brand Damage (MODERATE-HIGH):** The compound chocolate substitution exposed by H.B. Reese's grandson went viral on TikTok. While Hershey committed to returning to classic recipes by 2027, this reversal means accepting higher cocoa butter costs precisely when trying to recover margins — a self-inflicted constraint. The trust damage to Reese's (the #1 confectionery brand in America) is difficult to quantify but could show up in market share data over the next 2-4 quarters.

**Tertiary Risks:**
- Customer concentration: McLane Company represents ~27% of sales (distribution intermediary risk)
- Volume destruction from sustained pricing: 10-12% pricing in Q1 2026 with 2-4% volume declines indicates elasticity limits
- GLP-1 drug adoption as secular headwind to impulse confectionery
- International segment effectively unprofitable ($3.3M income in FY2025, down from $111.5M in FY2024)
- Higher debt costs: $2B refinanced at 4.55-5.10% vs. retired 0.9-3.2% debt

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term IV at 38.5% vs. 26.9% historical volatility (11.6pp rich) in backwardation structure (near-term > long-term) indicates the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, consistent with post-earnings positioning around cocoa volatility. The put/call OI ratio of 1.44 shows modest protective positioning, though the volume ratio (0.23) suggests recent activity skews toward call buying. The backwardation confirms institutional recognition that binary commodity outcomes dominate the near-term.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock falls below $170 (20x FY2026 midpoint), providing meaningful margin of safety for cocoa volatility
2. Q2 2026 earnings confirm gross margin inflection above 38% with stable-to-positive volumes
3. Cocoa futures curve shifts into contango below $6,000/ton for forward 12 months, confirming structural de-risking

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Cocoa futures re-accelerate above $10,000/ton and management revises FY2026 guidance below $7.50 EPS
2. Two consecutive quarters of >4% volume declines (indicating pricing power exhaustion)
3. Market share losses exceed 50 bps in CMG data for 2+ consecutive measurement periods

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected July/August 2026). This is the guided "inflection" quarter for gross margins. If margins fail to inflect despite management's explicit guidance, the recovery thesis weakens materially.

## Conclusion

Hershey presents a textbook commodity-recovery situation: trough earnings in FY2025, confirming Q1 2026 beat, clear path to $8.40+ EPS this year and $9.90 next year. The 12-month growth thesis is the cocoa-driven margin recovery, with a specific catalyst in Q2 2026 when gross margins should begin inflecting higher. This is a quantified recovery story.

However, the stock at $184 is pricing recovery, not distressed recovery. The risk-reward is balanced rather than compelling: ~17% upside to consensus median ($215) vs. 11-16% downside to bear case ($155-165). Cocoa remains genuinely volatile and unpredictable, the brand damage from compound chocolate is an unquantified overhang, and secular headwinds (GLP-1, health consciousness, SNAP changes) create cumulative demand pressure that may prevent volume recovery even as margins normalize.

HOLD is appropriate with a growth thesis: the margin recovery is real and confirmed by Q1 data, but the stock doesn't offer sufficient risk/reward at current levels to justify capital deployment ahead of the Q2 inflection confirmation. Monitor cocoa futures and the Q2 earnings print for upgrade/downgrade signals.
