## Executive Summary

Par Pacific Holdings operates four petroleum refineries in geographically isolated western US markets (Hawaii, Montana, Washington, Wyoming) plus a retail fuel distribution network. The company is a direct beneficiary of the current elevated oil/crack spread environment driven by Hormuz disruptions and structural West Coast refinery closures. Analyst estimates have surged: current quarter EPS revisions are up +525% over 90 days, reflecting the improved margin environment.

However, the stock has already appreciated 209% from its 52-week low of $18.78 to $60.18, and the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) missed estimates by 5.1%. Insider selling has been concentrated and meaningful ($7.4M across multiple executives in March 2026), with zero open-market purchases. The business remains fundamentally a crack-spread derivative with extreme cyclicality: net income swung from +$729M (2023) to -$33M (2024) to +$369M (2025). At 6.7x forward P/E on current estimates, the stock appears optically cheap, but these estimates embed historically elevated crack spreads that may not persist if geopolitical tensions de-escalate.

The renewables JV commissioning (61M gallons/year of renewable diesel and SAF) is a genuine diversifier expected to begin contributing in H1 2026, and the $500M debt refinancing extends maturities to 2034. These structural improvements support a constructive medium-term view, but at current prices the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific Holdings is a Houston-based energy company operating four petroleum refineries with combined throughput capacity of approximately 188 Mbpd:

- **Hawaii (Kapolei):** ~84-90 Mbpd; serves the isolated Hawaiian market with effectively no pipeline-delivered competition
- **Montana (Billings):** ~50-57 Mbpd; serves Rocky Mountain/Pacific Northwest markets
- **Washington (Tacoma):** ~38-39 Mbpd; produces gasoline, diesel, and asphalt
- **Wyoming (Newcastle):** ~13-18 Mbpd; recovered from a 66-day outage in early 2025

The company also operates a retail fuel network (primarily Hawaii), logistics infrastructure (pipelines, terminals, storage), and a 63.5%-owned renewable fuels joint venture with Mitsubishi/ENEOS that is commissioning a 61 million gallon/year renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel facility in Kapolei.

The defining characteristic is extreme cyclicality. FY2025 included a $199.5M non-recurring SRE (Small Refinery Exemption) benefit covering compliance years 2019-2024. Stripping this out, adjusted gross margin per barrel was $11.64 versus the reported $14.60. The underlying refining economics were solid but less exceptional than headline numbers suggest.

### Balance Sheet & Liquidity

Total debt declined from $1.11B (end 2024) to $798M (end 2025), representing material deleveraging. The May 2026 refinancing issued $500M of 7.375% Senior Notes due 2034, repaying and terminating the variable-rate term loan due 2030. This extends maturity and eliminates refinancing risk at the cost of a relatively high fixed rate.

Total liquidity stands at $914.6M ($164M cash + $750.5M ABL availability). Current ratio is 1.61x. D/E ratio is 1.51x — elevated but appropriate for a capital-intensive refiner. Altman Z-Score of 3.11 places the company firmly in the safe zone.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $445M in FY2025 (vs. $84M in FY2024's trough year). Capex was $149M, yielding $296M in FCF. The 2026 capex budget is $190-220M (elevated for Hawaii and Wyoming turnarounds), but with the current crack spread environment, FCF generation should remain robust.

### Share Count Reduction

Shares outstanding declined from ~60M (2023) to ~50.7M (2025) — a 15% reduction driven by $266M in repurchases over 2024-2025. The $250M authorization (February 2025, no end date) provides ongoing buyback capacity.

- **4.7x current-year forward P/E** (on $12.92 FY2026 consensus)
- **6.5x next-year P/E** (on $9.24 FY2027 consensus)

Against peer medians of 11.1x P/E (PBF and DINO), PARR appears cheap on a headline basis. But this comparison is misleading — PARR's earnings are currently at cyclical highs while some peers (CVI at -82.3x, WKC at -2.6x) are at cyclical troughs or have different business mixes.

The more relevant question: **what normalized earnings power does PARR have?** Through-cycle, I estimate $4-6 EPS based on mid-cycle crack spreads ($11-13/bbl combined index) plus renewables contribution. At $60, that's 10-15x normalized earnings — reasonable but not compelling for a highly cyclical business.

The stock has already re-rated from $18.78 to $60.18 — a 220% appreciation driven by the crack spread recovery. The current price embeds the strong earnings environment. At analyst consensus target of $72 (mean) with $80 high, upside is 20-33% — but only if current elevated spreads persist.

Forward P/E at 4.7x looks cheap only if you believe current-level crack spreads are sustainable. History suggests they are not. This is a peak-cycle earnings story, not a structural improvement in earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

Par Pacific's competitive position is among the strongest in independent refining due to geographic moats:

- **Hawaii:** No pipeline access from mainland US. The Kapolei refinery is effectively the sole large-scale domestic refinery serving the state. Competing supply must arrive by tanker, creating natural cost advantages.
- **Montana/Wyoming:** Inland refineries serve markets where logistics constraints (no coastal pipeline access, rail/truck dependency) limit competitive entry.
- **Washington:** While more exposed to competition, the Tacoma refinery benefits from West Coast supply tightening.

The renewables JV adds a competitive dimension — co-locating renewable fuels production at the existing Kapolei site creates shared infrastructure economics and positions PARR in growing low-carbon fuels markets.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Crack Spread Reversion (HIGH PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT).** The current elevated margin environment is geopolitically driven. Any Hormuz de-escalation could compress margins rapidly. Even without de-escalation, seasonal patterns and OPEC+ decisions can shift spreads $5-10/bbl within quarters. This is the dominant risk.

2. **Q1 Miss Pattern (MODERATE).** Two of the last four quarters missed estimates (Q4 2025 by 9.9%, Q1 2026 by 5.1%). The company's capture rate of benchmark crack spreads is volatile and difficult to forecast. Even in a strong macro environment, operational execution can disappoint.

3. **Union Contract Expiration (MODERATE, NEAR-TERM).** CBAs covering 22% of workforce at three of four refineries expired January 31, 2026 and are under rolling 24-hour extensions. A labor disruption could idle significant capacity.

4. **SRE Non-Recurrence.** The $199.5M SRE benefit in FY2025 was a multi-year catch-up that won't repeat. Investors relying on TTM earnings that include this windfall will overestimate sustainable earnings power.

5. **Insider Selling Concentration.** CEO Monteleone sold $5.9M in March 2026 (~$53-55/share). SVP Pitkin, Director Clossey, and SVP Flores also sold. No open-market purchases. While much is tied to option exercises, the scale and clustering at prices 10-15% below current levels warrants attention.

## Conclusion

Par Pacific Holdings is a well-positioned refiner benefiting from both structural tailwinds (West Coast closures, geographic moats) and cyclical tailwinds (Hormuz-driven elevated crack spreads). The estimate revision velocity is extraordinary, and the renewables JV represents genuine diversification.

However, the stock has already repriced from $18.78 to $60.18 (+220%), much of the good news is embedded, and the trailing two quarters have missed estimates. Insider selling at $53-55 (below current price) and unresolved union contracts create near-term headwinds. The business remains a crack-spread derivative at its core, and at current prices, investors are paying for sustained above-mid-cycle earnings that depend on geopolitical conditions persisting.

The prior score trajectory (declining from 6.4 to 5.9) reflected the stock's appreciation without proportional fundamental improvement. The Q1 miss and insider selling reinforce caution. HOLD with a growth orientation — the thesis remains constructive but entry would be significantly better at $45-50 where risk/reward becomes asymmetric. The next 90 days (renewables ramp, Q2 earnings, union resolution) should clarify whether current estimates are achievable or overshoot reality.
