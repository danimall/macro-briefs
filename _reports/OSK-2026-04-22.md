## Executive Summary

Oshkosh Corporation is a $9.3B diversified industrial company operating across three segments: Access Equipment (JLG — global #1 in aerial work platforms), Vocational (Pierce — #1 in fire apparatus, McNeilus in refuse), and Transport (USPS NGDV mail trucks, military tactical vehicles). The stock trades at $149, or ~13.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $11.21, representing a 38% discount to the peer median P/E of 21.4x. The 52-week range of $80.29–$180.49 reflects a stock that has recovered substantially from its April 2025 trough but remains 17% below its February 2026 peak.

The investment case centers on three elements: (1) Vocational segment margin expansion to 16%+ in FY2026 on 13% revenue growth with strong pricing power, (2) the NGDV program providing multi-year Transport revenue visibility with potential margin improvement on follow-on orders, and (3) a valuation that prices in the Access downcycle without crediting the structural improvements in the other two segments. The key risks are tariff escalation ($200M embedded in 2026 guidance, with any increase breaking the forecast), continued Access segment deterioration beyond guidance, and NGDV execution/order timing. The 2-and-2 earnings beat/miss record over the last four quarters and sharply negative current-quarter estimate revisions (–56% over 90 days) warrant caution on near-term expectations, but the valuation discount is wide enough to compensate.

## Company Overview

Oshkosh designs and manufactures purpose-built vehicles and equipment across specialized end markets. The three segments break down as follows:

- **Access (~43% FY2025 revenue, $4.49B):** JLG-branded aerial work platforms and telehandlers sold to equipment rental companies and construction contractors. Most cyclical segment. The Caterpillar telehandler licensing agreement expired in 2024, removing a revenue stream.
- **Vocational (~36%, $3.73B):** Pierce fire trucks, McNeilus refuse collection vehicles, airport ground support, concrete mixers. Replacement-driven municipal demand with high customer loyalty and attractive aftermarket. Currently the highest-margin and best-performing segment.
- **Transport (~20%, $2.10B):** USPS NGDV program (up to 165,000 mail delivery vehicles over 10 years), FHTV military vehicles, international defense. JLTV production completed in 2025; segment is transitioning to NGDV as the primary revenue driver.

Total backlog stood at $14.2B at December 2025, down 3.8% YoY, with Vocational backlog growing 4.7% to $6.6B while Access backlog fell 30.4% to $1.3B. Approximately 53% of backlog is not expected to fill in FY2026, providing multi-year revenue visibility.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

FY2025 revenue was $10.42B, down 2.9% from $10.73B in FY2024. The decline was driven by a $670M decrease in Access sales (lower volumes, Caterpillar contract expiration, higher discounting) and a $59M decline in Transport (JLTV wind-down), partially offset by $417M of Vocational growth.

Operating income fell to $940M (9.0% margin) from $1,011M (9.4% margin). The margin compression was concentrated in Access, where operating margins fell 440 bps to 11.2%. Vocational margins expanded 270 bps to 14.7%, and Transport margins improved 130 bps to 3.7%.

Net income was $647M, or $10.02 diluted EPS, down from $681M in FY2024. Q4 FY2025 was weak — EPS of $2.26 missed the $2.33 consensus by 3.2%. Management guided Q1 FY2026 EPS of approximately $0.85, reflecting tariff front-loading, Access pull-forward effects, and seasonality.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow improved to $783M in FY2025 from $550M in FY2024, partly boosted by ~$90M in tax benefit from the OBBBA legislation. Capital expenditures declined to $165M as South Carolina and Tennessee facility construction completed, yielding free cash flow of $618M (5.9% FCF margin).

The balance sheet is conservatively leveraged: total debt of $1.1B against $4.5B equity, translating to a debt-to-capitalization ratio of 19.5%. Cash on hand was $480M with $1.51B available on the revolver. Investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/Baa3) and a current ratio of 1.94x provide ample liquidity.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: $278M in buybacks in FY2025 (2.3M shares), with 7.9M shares remaining on the authorization. The dividend has increased at double-digit rates for 12 consecutive years, now at $0.57/quarter ($2.28 annualized, ~1.5% yield).

### Segment Economics and 2026 Guidance

Management guides FY2026 revenue of ~$11.0B (up 5.5%), operating income of ~$1.06B (up 12.8%), and EPS of ~$10.90. The segment-level outlook:

| Segment | FY2026E Revenue | YoY | FY2026E Op. Margin | FY2025A Op. Margin |
|---------|----------------|-----|--------------------|--------------------|
| Access | ~$4.2B | –7% | ~9.7% | 11.2% |
| Vocational | ~$4.2B | +13% | ~16.1% | 14.7% |
| Transport | ~$2.5B | +19% | ~4.0% | 3.7% |

Vocational is expected to contribute the majority of earnings growth, with margins expanding 140 bps on continued pricing leverage and throughput improvements. Transport growth depends on NGDV production ramp and a follow-on delivery order, which would trigger a favorable cumulative catch-up adjustment. Access is expected to decline further.

The tariff impact embedded in guidance is $200M, up $165M from FY2025. This is approximately $2.40/share pre-tax. The guidance explicitly assumes tariff rates remain at current levels.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. Vocational and Transport segments are growing at healthy rates (13% and 19% respectively), while Access is contracting. Consolidated revenue growth of 5.5% in FY2026 and consensus next-year EPS of $14.24 (27% growth over FY2026) suggest the downcycle trough is priced into the Access segment already.

However, the estimate revision trend is concerning. Current-quarter EPS consensus has been cut 55.9% over the past 90 days (from $2.36 to $1.04). Full-year FY2026 estimates have declined 9.6% over 90 days. Next-year estimates are more stable, down only 1.7%, suggesting the market views FY2026 as a trough year.

The NGDV program is the primary multi-year growth driver. Only 51,500 of a potential 165,000 units have been ordered. A follow-on order would extend the production runway and improve per-unit economics through the cumulative catch-up mechanism. The 2026 guidance assumes this order is received.

The AUSA acquisition (September 2024) adds European compact equipment exposure — a growing niche within the access market — but is early in integration.

At $149 and $11.21 consensus FY2026 EPS, OSK trades at 13.3x forward earnings. This compares to:

The 38% P/E discount to peers has widened from historical norms. OSK has typically traded at a modest discount given its cyclicality, but a spread this wide suggests the market is pricing substantial risk around the Access downcycle and tariff exposure.

On FY2027 consensus of $14.24, OSK trades at 10.5x — well below any reasonable through-cycle multiple for a company with market-leading franchises and investment-grade credit.

The analyst consensus median price target is $170, with a range of $138–$204. The mean target of $170.91 implies ~14% upside.

A reasonable base case applying 14x to $11.21 FY2026 EPS yields ~$157, and 13x to $14.24 FY2027 EPS yields ~$185. Even discounting FY2027 estimates by 10% for the negative revision trend, a 13x multiple on $12.82 yields $167.

## Competitive Landscape

OSK holds #1 market positions in its three core product categories: JLG in aerial work platforms globally, Pierce in fire apparatus, and a dominant position in the USPS NGDV program (sole-source contract). These are niche markets where performance, reliability, total cost of ownership, and dealer service networks create meaningful switching costs.

Competitive threats are real in Access, where Chinese manufacturers (XCMG, Zoomlion) are entering Western markets at below-market prices. Customer consolidation among large rental companies (United Rentals, Sunbelt) increases buyer bargaining power. However, JLG's installed base, service network, and rental company relationships provide durable advantages that have allowed it to maintain leadership through prior cycles.

Vocational faces less competitive pressure — Pierce's #1 position in fire apparatus is protected by the highly customized nature of the product, long municipal procurement cycles, and deep fleet-wide aftermarket relationships.

## Risk Assessment

### Tariff Escalation (Primary Risk)
Any escalation beyond the $200M assumption in FY2026 guidance would directly impair margins. OSK imports steel, aluminum, and other components. Given elevated global trade tensions, this is a live risk. Each additional $50M in tariffs would reduce EPS by approximately $0.60.

### NGDV Execution and Order Risk
Deferred contract costs exceed future profits on existing NGDV orders by approximately $135M. If USPS does not place additional orders beyond the current 51,500 units, this amount would need to be recognized as a loss. The percentage-of-completion accounting on this program creates significant earnings volatility — cumulative catch-up adjustments reduced Transport income by $37.9M in FY2025 and $46.9M in FY2024.

### Access Cyclical Downturn
Access segment revenue is guided down 7% in FY2026 on top of a 13% decline in FY2025. Backlog is down 30.4%. If non-residential construction weakens further in a recessionary environment, Access could see deeper-than-guided declines. JLG's goodwill/intangibles represent ~78% of the company's total intangibles, creating impairment risk in a prolonged downturn.

### Earnings Visibility
The 2-and-2 beat/miss record, combined with sharply negative current-quarter revisions, indicates limited near-term earnings predictability. Q1 FY2026 is expected to be particularly weak (~$0.85 EPS), and any tariff or order-timing surprises could cause further misses.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 51.4% is significantly rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 36.8% (14.6pp spread, 77th percentile). The term structure is in backwardation (51.4% near-term vs. 38.6% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing near-term stress. However, the put/call OI ratio of 0.32 and volume ratio of 0.27 are both call-heavy, suggesting institutional positioning is tilted bullish despite the elevated uncertainty. Short interest at 4.6% of float with 3.9 days to cover is moderate and does not indicate crowded positioning in either direction.

The backwardated term structure with call-heavy positioning is consistent with high near-term uncertainty (Q1 earnings, tariff developments) alongside a positive directional consensus. This supports a BUY with the caveat that entry timing matters — the implied ±10.1% move over the next month creates a meaningful risk of a pullback to the $135 area.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($185–$200, +24% to +34%)
Vocational margins reach 16%+ as guided, NGDV follow-on order is received triggering a favorable cumulative catch-up, and Access segment stabilizes at the lower guide. Tariffs remain at current levels or are partially reduced through trade negotiations. The market re-rates OSK toward 14–15x as the earnings mix shifts toward less-cyclical Vocational and multi-year Transport revenue. FY2027 consensus of $14.24 at 14x = $199.

### Bear Case ($115–$125, –16% to –23%)
Tariffs escalate materially beyond $200M. NGDV follow-on order is delayed into 2027 or cancelled, triggering $135M+ impairment. Access segment declines more than guided as non-residential construction weakens in recession. OSK trades down to 10x depressed FY2026 EPS (~$9.50 post-tariff haircut) = $95–$115. A mild version of this scenario (tariff escalation + Access weakness without NGDV failure) would put the stock in the $115–$125 range.

### Base Case ($167, +12%)
OSK delivers roughly in line with FY2026 guidance ($10.90–$11.20 EPS). Vocational margins expand as guided. NGDV follow-on order arrives by mid-year. Access weakens per guidance but doesn't fall further. Tariffs remain at current levels. The market applies a 15x multiple to the blended FY2026/FY2027 earnings estimate (~$11.10) = $167. This aligns with the analyst consensus median target of $170.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $167, derived from 15x blended FY2026/2027 consensus EPS of ~$11.10. This sits between the bear case ($115–$125) and bull case ($185–$200).

**Bull case target:** $199, based on 14x FY2027 consensus of $14.24 assuming NGDV follow-on order and Access stabilization.

**Bear case target:** $120, based on 10x tariff-impaired FY2026 EPS of ~$10.00 with delayed NGDV order.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q2 FY2026 earnings (April 2026, already reported — see recent price action), Q3 earnings (July 2026), NGDV follow-on order announcement (expected H1 FY2026), and any tariff policy changes.

**Upside from current:** +12% to base case, +24% to bull case.
**Downside from current:** –20% to bear case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. USPS announces NGDV follow-on order delay beyond December 2026 or program cancellation.
2. FY2026 cumulative tariff impact exceeds $300M (50% above current guidance).
3. Access segment revenue declines exceed 15% in FY2026 (vs. guided –7%), indicating a deeper cyclical downturn than anticipated.

## Conclusion

OSK at 13.3x forward earnings offers a meaningful valuation discount to peers for a company with #1 market positions across its three core product categories, an investment-grade balance sheet, and strong free cash flow generation. The Vocational segment's margin trajectory to 16%+ provides earnings growth even as Access weakens, and the NGDV program offers multi-year Transport revenue visibility.

The risks are concrete: tariff escalation beyond current assumptions, NGDV order-timing uncertainty with $135M impairment exposure, and continued Access weakness. The mixed earnings track record and negative current-quarter revisions mean the next 1–2 quarters could be volatile. The options market is pricing this volatility while maintaining call-heavy positioning, consistent with high near-term uncertainty but a positive 12-month directional view.

The valuation discount, combined with Vocational margin expansion and the NGDV production ramp, provides sufficient margin of safety to issue a BUY recommendation at current levels. The risk/reward profile is approximately 1.6:1 (12% upside to base case vs. 20% downside to bear case). Investors should size the position to account for near-term volatility and the binary nature of the NGDV follow-on order catalyst.
