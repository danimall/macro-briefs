**Research Date: August 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ventas has converted itself from a diversified healthcare landlord into a senior housing operating company that happens to own its real estate. SHOP — the segment where Ventas takes the property-level profit and loss rather than collecting rent — produced $403.5M of Q2 2026 NOI, 58.9% of the total, up from 48.7% twelve months earlier, and 78.8% of consolidated revenue. That transformation is working operationally. Same-store SHOP cash NOI grew 16.3% year over year in Q2 on a 300bp occupancy gain to 90.9% and 5.0% RevPOR growth against 5.4% expense growth, producing 210bp of cash NOI margin expansion (recomputed independently from the segment tables: 31.1% vs. 29.0%). Total same-store cash NOI grew 10.3%. Normalized FFO of $0.97 per share rose 9% and management raised FY2026 guidance to $3.85–$3.90 while lifting the senior housing investment target from roughly $3.0B to $4.5B. Net debt to Further Adjusted EBITDA improved to a company-reported 4.7x from roughly 5.6–5.7x a year earlier.

The valuation is where the case breaks down. At $90.85 the stock trades at 23.4x the FY2026 Normalized FFO midpoint with a 2.29% dividend yield ($2.08 annualized). Since our mid-June review at $81.60, the price is up 11.3% while the FY2026 guidance midpoint moved from $3.855 to $3.875, an increase of 0.5%. The forward multiple therefore expanded from roughly 21.2x to 23.4x on essentially unchanged forward earnings. That re-rating occurred while the September policy-rate outcome remains genuinely two-sided — a demanding macro setting for a 2.3%-yield real asset priced at 23x cash earnings.

The single most decision-relevant fact is an absence. The going-in yield on the $4.5B of senior housing acquisitions is not disclosed in the 10-Q, the 10-K, or the July 29 press release. Management says only that it expects "attractive financial returns." The one anchor available cuts the wrong way: Ventas issued 34.7M forward shares in the first half at an average of $86.02, an implied Normalized FFO yield of about 4.5%, and is simultaneously selling assets and receiving loan repayments at a roughly 7% blended yield. Without the cap rate, the spread over cost of capital on $4.5B of deployment is unverifiable. The guidance revision itself is consistent with either interpretation: $1.5B of incremental investment plus $400M of incremental dispositions added $18.0M to the FY2026 Normalized FFO midpoint, roughly 1.2% of incremental capital — almost certainly a timing artifact that pushes the earnings into 2027, but also arithmetically consistent with thin spreads. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Ventas owns approximately 1,450 properties across the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom. The consolidated count reconciles from the Q2 segment note as 813 SHOP communities, 407 outpatient medical and research (OM&R) properties and 200 triple-net leased (NNN) properties, plus 36 held in unconsolidated vehicles.

Three distinct economic models sit inside one ticker. **SHOP** places the real estate in taxable REIT subsidiaries leased to eligible independent contractors, with third-party managers (Atria, Sunrise, Le Groupe Maurice, Discovery) operating the communities while Ventas receives resident fees and bears operating expenses. The FY2025 10-K discloses that Ventas generally holds the healthcare license and enrolls in government healthcare programs on behalf of these properties, and is "generally responsible for all liabilities of the properties" absent manager gross negligence or fraud. **OM&R** is 407 on- or near-campus medical office and research buildings, with the Lillibridge management platform owned internally; segment NOI was $302.1M for 1H 2026, 22.6% of the total. **NNN** is 200 properties anchored by Ardent, Kindred and a large Brookdale senior housing portfolio; 1H NOI $241.9M, 18.1% of total.

Ventas also runs a small third-party capital business with $552.2M of carrying value and holds roughly 34% of Atria and 6.6% of Ardent.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 revenue was $5,834.0M, up 18.5%, following +9.5% in 2024 and +8.9% in 2023 — a three-year CAGR of 12.2%. First-half 2026 revenue of $3,386.6M rose 21.9%. The acceleration is largely mix. Decomposing 1H 2026 against 1H 2025: resident fees +32.7% to $2,656.3M (now 78.4% of revenue), OM&R rental income +3.7% to $458.7M, NNN rental income **−19.7%** to $247.9M. NNN revenue is shrinking because senior housing assets have been converted out of triple-net leases into SHOP and because ten formerly Brookdale-leased properties were sold. Converting a NNN lease into a SHOP community multiplies gross revenue several-fold while adding only the incremental NOI spread, so headline consolidated revenue growth substantially overstates organic economics. Same-store cash NOI (+10.3%) is the honest growth number.

**The SHOP recovery.** Across 567 same-store communities: cash NOI $304.7M vs. $262.0M (+16.3%), resident fees +8.6%, property operating expenses +5.4%, average occupancy 90.9% vs. 87.9%, RevPOR $5,528 vs. $5,265. The gap between resident-fee growth (+8.6%) and expense growth (+5.4%) is the operating leverage that makes this segment interesting. The U.S. portfolio outperformed the blend materially — U.S. same-store cash NOI +18% with occupancy up 360bp — which most plausibly reflects the Canadian portfolio (Le Groupe Maurice, 5.6% of the investment mix, Quebec 4.7% of Q2 revenue) already operating near stabilized occupancy with less recovery runway. Softer Canadian demand is an alternative explanation the filings do not resolve.

A second, quantifiable piece of embedded value: total SHOP occupancy across all 813 communities averaged 88.7% versus 90.9% for the same-store cohort, a 220bp gap. Non-same-store cash NOI was $100.0M of SHOP's $404.7M, roughly a quarter of the segment. The straightforward read is that recently acquired and transitioned communities sit below the stabilized cohort and represent lease-up upside. The alternative — that these are structurally different assets in secondary markets that stabilize lower — cannot be ruled out from disclosure.

**The other two segments are not contributing.** OM&R same-store occupancy was 90.0% in both Q2 2026 and Q2 2025, literally unchanged, with same-store cash NOI +4.6% driven by escalators and expense control. OM&R also absorbed $25.5M of the $60.3M of 1H 2026 impairments despite being the smaller segment. NNN same-store NOI grew 1.0% (3.1% on a cash basis) — contractual escalators only. SHOP contributed roughly $42.7M of the ~$52.6M consolidated same-store increase.

**Earnings and a definitional change.** Q2 Normalized FFO was $0.97 per share ($483.716M / 497.724M diluted shares = $0.9718), against Nareit FFO of $0.99 and GAAP attributable EPS of $0.14, down from $0.15. Beginning Q1 2026 Ventas excludes non-cash stock-based compensation from Normalized FFO and restated prior periods to conform ($5.3M added back in Q2, $30.2M in 1H). Because prior periods were restated, the guided +8% to +10% FY2026 growth is apples-to-apples; solving backward from $3.85/1.08 and $3.90/1.10 implies a restated FY2025 base near $3.55 versus the $3.48 originally reported and used in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table. The change is disclosed and the convention is common in the sector, but it lifts the base roughly 2%, and anyone comparing $3.85–$3.90 to the historically published $3.48 will overstate growth by about two points. It also means the current 23.4x multiple is roughly 2% lower than it would be on the prior definition.

**GAAP earnings quality, both directions.** First-half attributable net income of $126.5M ($0.26 per diluted share) includes a $41.6M income tax benefit from reversal of valuation allowances on TRS deferred tax assets, roughly $0.084 per share or a third of reported 1H GAAP EPS, against only $5.6M of net cash taxes paid. Management excludes the non-cash portion from Normalized FFO, so the non-GAAP metric is not flattered. Pushing the other way, Ventas acquired $275.2M of intangibles in 1H with a weighted-average amortization period at acquisition of just 2.4 years, down from 3.5 years for FY2025 additions — which is why 1H D&A rose 18.1% while gross real estate rose 8.1%. GAAP EPS will look artificially weak through 2028. For a REIT running this acquisition profile, FFO is the only usable earnings lens.

**Cash flow and the funding gap.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,646.7M (28.2% of revenue). First-half 2026 OCF rose 19.4% to $951.2M. Subtract $324.5M of development and other capex and $483.5M of common dividends and $143.1M was retained. The entire $2,448.8M of net real estate investment plus $301.5M of loan investments was funded externally. That is the defining structural fact of the model rather than a criticism, and it makes the equity analysis below the central issue.

Non-cash rent is low — Q2 straight-line and non-cash rental income totalled roughly 1.8% of $685.2M of NOI, with straight-line rent receivable up only from $250.8M to $257.1M. Earnings quality on the rent side is clean. One flag: the allowance for doubtful accounts rose to $78.0M against net receivables of $108.0M, implying a reserve rate near 42% on gross AR of roughly $186M. The six-month increase was only $6.5M, so this most likely reflects legacy receivables already provided for rather than fresh deterioration; counterparties are not identified.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt principal fell to $12,768.1M from $13,103.6M at year-end and $13,154.9M a year earlier. Cash is $198.96M, down from $741.1M as it was deployed. The book is 84.9% fixed at a 4.59% weighted average effective rate. Liquidity of $4.9B comprises a $3.5B undrawn revolver to April 2028, cash, and $1.5B of unsettled forward equity, less commercial paper and letters of credit. Maturities are $731.1M for the balance of 2026, $917.8M in 2027, $1,538.3M in 2028. Ventas repaid $500M of 4.125% notes in January, extended a $500M 2027 term loan into a $1.25B facility due January 2031 at SOFR+0.85%, and repaid a $200M 2027 term loan.

Our independent leverage cross-check runs modestly above the company's: Q2 NOI $685.208M plus $1.778M interest and other income less $46.986M G&A, annualized, gives $2,560.0M against net debt of $12,488.8M, or 4.88x versus the reported 4.7x. The likely reconciliation is pro-forma annualization of mid-period investments inside "Further Adjusted EBITDA," or netting of unsettled forward equity. Both figures are comfortable. Simple annualization of one quarter ignores senior housing seasonality (winter move-outs, utility costs) and excludes the full-year effect of $3.4B of year-to-date acquisitions, so treat it as a sensitivity. Interest coverage on a Q2 proxy is 4.0x. Debt to total assets fell to 42.8% from 47.2%. Rate sensitivity is modest: 100bp on variable debt adds roughly $19.3M annually, about $0.04 per share.

One data caution for anyone screening mechanically: the XBRL-tagged FY2025 interest expense field reads $0.172M, irreconcilable with Q2 2026 interest expense of $160.0M and FY2026 guidance assuming roughly $646M. Any coverage or distress score built on that field will be wrong, and FY2025 asset turnover of 0.211x with retained earnings at −27.3% of assets mechanically depresses any Altman-style score for structural REIT reasons.

**Dilution.** Shares outstanding went from 474.926M at 12/31/2025 to 512.946M at 7/28/2026, an increase of 38.020M or 8.0% of the year-end base. First-half net proceeds from common stock issuance were $2,548.2M. Ventas entered forward sales for 34.7M shares (roughly $3.0B gross) at an average of $86.02, settling 31.4M. At 7/30/2026, 18.5M forward shares remained unsettled for roughly $1.6B gross, maturing through December 2027 — equal to 3.61% of current shares, or 3.48% ownership dilution on a post-issuance basis. These are contractual forwards at fixed prices, not contingent instruments. The ATM was raised to $3.0B in May with $2.0B remaining. The $862.5M exchangeable notes were settled in June with $856.1M cash plus 5.9M shares for the conversion premium, an event now complete. Guidance assumes a 506M weighted-average diluted count against 497.7M actual in Q2, implying roughly 514M for the second half. Not everything was equity-funded: $448.4M of real estate was acquired non-cash with $289.1M of debt assumed, plus $333.7M of new Q2 mortgage debt.

## Growth Analysis

Guided FY2026 Normalized FFO per share growth of 8–10% is a clear acceleration from the 4.7% annual pace of $2.90 → $2.99 → $2.99 → $3.19 → $3.48 across 2021–2025. Three drivers support the next twelve months.

First, same-store SHOP momentum. Occupancy at 90.9% in the stabilized cohort still sits below pre-2020 senior housing norms, RevPOR is growing 5.0% against 5.4% expense growth, and the 210bp margin expansion has room to continue while occupancy climbs. Second, the 220bp gap between total and same-store SHOP occupancy across roughly 246 un-seasoned communities. Third, and most quantitatively important, the un-annualized acquisition tail: $3.4B closed year-to-date against a $4.5B full-year target, with most of the earnings effect landing in 2027 rather than 2026.

Sizing that tail requires an assumption we cannot verify. If the $4.5B goes in at a 6.0% cash yield against a blended cost of capital near 4.75% (equity issued at an implied 4.5% FFO yield, incremental mortgage debt around 5%), net accretion is roughly $56M, or about $0.11 per share on 506M shares — meaningful at 2.8% of FFO but not transformative, and vulnerable to the going-in yield being 100bp lower. Combining plausible acquisition accretion with continued same-store growth and the second-half share count of roughly 514M, FY2027 Normalized FFO of $4.15–$4.25 per share is a reasonable central expectation.

The offsetting facts deserve equal weight. Two of three segments are contributing essentially nothing: OM&R occupancy is flat year over year and absorbed 42% of first-half impairments, NNN NOI grew 1.0% and NNN revenue is contracting 19.7%. The third-party capital business is static (1H management revenues $8.66M vs. $8.73M). And per-share growth of 8–10% against 8% share count growth implies gross FFO growth near 17–18% — the growth is being purchased, which works only while the cost of equity stays low.

**On the estimate revision data:** the reported consensus EPS revisions are negative (next quarter −18.9%, next year −9.9% over 90 days) on one to three analysts, and these are GAAP EPS estimates. For a REIT with D&A rising 18.1% on 2.4-year intangibles and an 8% higher share count, falling GAAP EPS estimates are the mechanical consequence of the acquisition program, not a signal about cash earnings. The operative metric moved the other way: Normalized FFO guidance was raised on July 29. The reported estimate revision trend should not be read as a growth warning here, and equally should not be read as confirmation of anything.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.85 against a FY2026 Normalized FFO midpoint of $3.875, Ventas trades at **23.4x** forward FFO (24.0x on Nareit FFO of $3.785). Dividend of $2.08 annualized yields **2.29%**, a 53.7% payout on Normalized FFO and roughly 67.5% on an approximate FAD basis using the $400M guided FAD capex. Enterprise value is approximately $59.6B ($46.6B equity + $12.49B net debt + $0.51B redeemable OP and noncontrolling interests). Book equity per share of $28.56 gives a 3.18x price-to-book, but with accumulated depreciation at 32.3% of gross real estate, historical-cost book is not a value anchor.

The GAAP P/E of roughly 166x reported in the data is not a usable metric, and the peer median GAAP P/E of 64.3 is distorted the same way. The comparable that does travel is yield: VTR at 2.29% versus a peer median of 4.7% (OHI 5.8%, DOC 6.0%, CTRE 4.1%, HR 4.7%, AHR 2.1%). Part of that gap is legitimate business-model differentiation — OHI and CTRE are triple-net skilled-nursing yield vehicles with low growth, while VTR and AHR are operating-portfolio growth stories, and AHR's 2.1% yield sits right next to VTR's. The closest true comparable, Welltower, is absent from the supplied peer set, and we were unable to obtain a verified post-Q2 relative multiple. That is a genuine gap in the relative-value work.

What we can assess directly is the change in the multiple against the change in fundamentals. On June 18 the shares traded at $81.60 against then-effective guidance of $3.82–$3.89 (midpoint $3.855), or 21.2x. Today the shares are at $90.85 against $3.85–$3.90 (midpoint $3.875), or 23.4x. Price rose 11.3%; the forward guidance midpoint rose 0.5%. Roughly 95% of the move was multiple expansion. That is a statement about current price versus current forward fundamentals, and it is the reason the valuation score falls from 5.0 to 4.5 rather than any deterioration in the business.

Two further considerations. The implied cap rate on trailing Q2 cash NOI ($667.5M annualized against $59.6B EV) is 4.48% gross of corporate G&A and 4.16% net — a demanding entry yield, though it understates the forward stabilized figure because it excludes the full-year effect of $3.4B of year-to-date acquisitions and ignores seasonality. And the macro setting is adverse: with a live probability of a September policy hike, the discount rate applied to a 2.3%-yield real asset is more likely to rise than fall over the next two quarters. A 23.4x multiple embeds both continued high-single-digit FFO growth and multiple stability; it does not survive a stall in same-store SHOP NOI growth.

Analyst targets: mean $100.73, median $100.50, range $86 to $114. The consensus mix is 4 strong buy / 14 buy / 5 hold with a stable trend. At 23.4x forward FFO with a 2.29% yield, we do not treat these targets as floors — this is not a case where consensus is lagging a compressed multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The FY2025 10-K concedes the structural issue plainly: Ventas competes for acquisitions against parties "some of whom may have advantages compared to us, including greater financial resources and lower costs of capital." In health care REITs the natural reference class for a larger competitor with a lower cost of capital and a bigger senior housing platform is Welltower, though the filing names no one. If a competitor systematically underwrites the same assets at a lower discount rate, that competitor sets the clearing price on the $4.5B Ventas intends to buy — which is precisely why the undisclosed going-in yield matters.

Portfolio positioning is mixed on trajectory. Individual counterparty shares of the investment mix declined between year-end and June: Atria 19.6% → 17.9%, Sunrise 9.3% → 8.6%, Le Groupe Maurice 6.2% → 5.6%, Ardent 4.5% → 4.1%, Kindred NOI share 5.9% → 5.1%. But those declines came largely from an 8% larger denominator, and Discovery rose 5.4% → 7.1% while "all other" went 36.2% → 39.3%. Meanwhile asset-class and business-model concentration increased: senior housing 69.2% → 71.1% of the investment mix, outpatient medical 18.2% → 16.6%, research 5.6% → 5.1%, and SHOP from 72.7% to 78.8% of revenue and 48.7% to 58.9% of NOI. Scaling with a broader operator bench is favorable for deployment capacity; the 10-K flags that "new or smaller third-party managers may have less experience... and may require more oversight."

Management's demand framing — baby boomers turning 80 beginning in 2026 against new supply "at historic lows" — is arithmetically correct on the demographics and plausible on supply, but it is a company characterization we could not corroborate against a primary industry dataset. The 10-K supplies the counterweight in its own risk factors: "Depending on the jurisdiction, there are limited barriers to developing properties in our asset classes, particularly senior housing. As a result, supply and demand dynamics can change quickly." Low current supply reflects a construction-financing environment rather than a structural moat, and the financing environment can change faster than the demographic cohort.

The June 2026 Kindred/Scion restructuring is the most economically interesting piece of competitive positioning in the period. Ventas, as lessor, consolidated all Kindred LTAC leases into a single master lease extended to April 30, 2039 at existing cash rent with a roughly 2.75% escalator; as lender, originated a six-year interest-only senior secured loan to Scion with $300.0M initial principal at a 10.7% effective rate; and as warrant holder, obtained a reduced exercise price and a term extension to June 2036 on warrants for 9.9% of a Kindred parent's equity, described in the 10-Q as partial consideration received. Removing a re-leasing overhang on a reimbursement-dependent asset class while earning a double-digit coupon is a well-compensated trade. The other reading is equally valid: a 10.7% senior secured rate is the market's price for Scion credit risk, and Ventas now holds three correlated claims — lease, loan and warrant — on one operator that would impair together in a reimbursement shock. Kindred is 2.0% of Q2 revenue and 5.1% of NOI, so the sizing is contained.

## Risk Assessment

**Licensing and operating liability scale with SHOP.** Ventas generally holds the healthcare license and enrolls in government healthcare programs on behalf of SHOP properties, exposing it to civil, criminal and administrative penalties including license suspension, non-payment for new admissions, decertification and facility closure, while having limited day-to-day operational control. SHOP moved from 48.7% to 58.9% of NOI in twelve months, so this exposure grew by roughly a fifth in a year. It is the most underappreciated structural consequence of the strategy.

**Unverifiable accretion on $4.5B.** The going-in cap rate is not disclosed. The two available anchors — equity issued at an implied 4.5% FFO yield and dispositions/loan repayments at a ~7% blended yield — bracket a wide range of outcomes. The 2027 guidance introduction, likely February 2027, is when this resolves.

**Rate sensitivity of the multiple, not the interest expense.** Direct floating-rate exposure is small ($19.3M per 100bp, roughly 1% of FFO). The larger exposure is the discount rate applied to a 2.29%-yield asset at 23.4x cash earnings while the September policy decision remains contested. This is the risk least reflected in the current multiple.

**Dilution overhang.** 18.5M unsettled forward shares (3.48% ownership dilution) settle through December 2027, on top of 8.0% share growth already taken in seven months. The 10-K names dilution as a specific risk factor.

**Segment-level deterioration in OM&R.** Occupancy flat at 90.0%, $25.5M of $60.3M in 1H impairments, and a leadership transition — Exhibit 10.2 to the Q2 10-Q is a separation agreement dated May 12, 2026 with the EVP of OM&R, and Form 4 filings show a new segment head receiving grants of 10,694 and 4,314 shares (15,008 total) on August 1 at $93.51 from a zero starting position. A performance-driven change is a reasonable reading; the filings state no reason.

**Government funding.** The FY2025 10-K adds specific language that "in 2025, the U.S. administration adopted substantial policy changes that affect research and government program funding." Research tenants are 5.1% of the investment mix and depend partly on NIH grants; IRF/LTAC is 1.6% and SNF 0.6%. Loss from unconsolidated entities widened to $(15.2)M in 1H from $(4.4)M while investments in those entities declined to $590.6M from $617.6M, with 53%-owned research development JVs at $247.9M — plausibly the source, though ordinary depreciation on newly delivered development assets is an equally consistent explanation.

**Labor and geography.** California SB-525 healthcare wage requirements are named specifically; California is 11.5% of Q2 revenue, Texas 9.1%, New York 7.9%, Florida 5.1%, Quebec 4.7%. Canada is 9.5% of FY2025 revenue, the UK 1.2%.

**2035 lease cliff.** Combined OM&R and NNN annualized base rent expiring in 2035 is $238.4M (22% of total), of which $214.8M comes from 65 Brookdale-leased properties, 47% of NNN ABR. Near-term expirations are manageable at 5% for the balance of 2026 and 9% in 2027.

**Newly added risk factor.** The Q2 10-Q cautionary statement list adds an item on risks related to the UPREIT reorganization that was not in the FY2025 10-K list. The reorganization itself produced no change to consolidated financial statements; redeemable OP and noncontrolling interests rose to $509.1M from $375.2M.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that the fundamentals do not obviously explain, and the earnings event has already passed. Near-term ATM IV of 29.7% sits 8.3 points above 21.4% one-year realized volatility, at the 66th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (29.7% at one month against 25.0% at six months). The one-month put/call skew is 4.4%, which the data characterizes as elevated put demand, and put/call volume is 2.07 — against standing open interest that remains call-tilted at 0.33. The pattern is fresh downside positioning layered onto an existing call-heavy book. The nearest expiry (August 21) spans the payrolls release and the run-up to the September FOMC, which is a coherent explanation for near-dated hedging on a low-yield REIT: the market may be buying insurance against a rate outcome rather than a company event. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.4%; the 6-month tenor implies ±11.8% with skew turning to −1.4%, so the longer-dated market is neutral. Short interest of 5.5% of float with 5.9 days to cover is unremarkable.

Separately worth noting: the stock reached an intraday high of $101.60 on July 28, then closed at $91.92 on July 30 (down roughly 6.1% from the July 29 close of $97.72), the day the 10-Q was filed — one day after a beat-and-raise. Candidate explanations include the filing's fuller disclosure of the equity issuance and forward equity program and the guidance-raise arithmetic showing only $0.02 of per-share accretion on $1.5B of incremental investment. We cannot attribute causation from the available evidence, but the sequence — market rewards the press release, sells the filing — is worth holding onto.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $113 (+24.4%)

Senior housing occupancy is still below pre-2020 norms and the stabilized cohort at 90.9% has room to reach 92–93%. With RevPOR growing 5.0% against 5.4% expense growth, each occupancy point converts to margin at high incremental rates; the 210bp Q2 margin expansion continues into 2027. The 220bp gap between total (88.7%) and same-store (90.9%) SHOP occupancy closes as roughly 246 acquired and transitioned communities season, adding to the $100.0M of quarterly non-same-store cash NOI. The $4.5B deployed in 2026 contributes only a partial year to FY2026, so FY2027 captures a full-year run rate; FY2027 Normalized FFO reaches $4.30–$4.35. Leverage continues down from 4.7x, and if the discount-rate backdrop improves the multiple re-rates to 26x. Management's TSR record supports execution credibility: indexed to $100 at 12/31/2020, Ventas reached 187.19 by year-end 2025 versus 164.51 for the peer group, outperforming in each of 2022–2025. The Kindred restructuring demonstrates a willingness to be paid for taking structured risk.

### Bear Case — $78 (−14.1%)

The multiple, not the operations, does the damage. A 2.29%-yield asset at 23.4x forward FFO requires a stable or falling discount rate; with a genuinely two-sided September policy outcome, a compression to 19x is unexceptional for the sector. Applied to FY2027 FFO of $4.05 — achievable if acquisition spreads are 100bp thinner than assumed and same-store SHOP growth decelerates from 16% toward 8% as the easy occupancy comparisons run out — that is $77. The self-reinforcing risk is that the growth model depends on equity access: 8.0% share growth in seven months at an implied 4.5% cost of equity works only while the stock supports 23x. A de-rating raises the cost of equity, which slows deployment, which slows per-share growth, which validates the lower multiple. Add that two of three segments are contributing nothing (OM&R occupancy flat with 42% of impairments, NNN revenue down 19.7%), that the going-in yield on $4.5B is undisclosed, that 18.5M forward shares still settle through 2027, and that SHOP's rise to 58.9% of NOI has concentrated direct regulatory and operating liability in the segment where Ventas holds the licenses.

### Base Case — $98 (+7.9%)

FY2027 Normalized FFO of $4.18 per share (FY2026 midpoint $3.875 grown 8%, reflecting continued same-store SHOP growth decelerating toward high single digits plus roughly $0.11 of net acquisition accretion, net of the higher share count) at a maintained 23.4x multiple gives $97.8. This sits 2.7% below the analyst median target of $100.50 and assumes no multiple relief from rates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** We expect appreciation to the base case over twelve months, but the risk/reward at 23.4x forward FFO with 7.9% base-case upside against 14.1% bear-case downside — a ratio below 1:1 — does not support new capital at $90.85.

## Conclusion

Ventas is executing well operationally and the balance sheet is materially better than it was a year ago. Same-store SHOP cash NOI growth of 16.3% on a 300bp occupancy gain, 210bp of margin expansion, deleveraging of nearly a full turn to 4.7x, $4.9B of liquidity against $731M of remaining 2026 maturities, and a dividend raised 8.3% to $0.52 quarterly at a 53.7% FFO payout all describe a healthy company. Score movements reflect that: financial health at 6.8 is up sharply from 5.5 in February.

The score decline from 6.6 in June to 5.76 traces almost entirely to valuation and risk, and specifically to the arithmetic that the shares rose 11.3% while the forward guidance midpoint rose 0.5%, taking the forward multiple from 21.2x to 23.4x. That is a compression of risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals, which is a different thing from thesis deterioration. At $81.60 the stock offered upside to the base case with a cushion; at $90.85 it offers 7.9% to base against 14.1% to bear, into a macro setting where a 2.29%-yield real asset is the most discount-rate-sensitive expression available.

Two specific items keep this from being a BUY rather than any general caution about multiples. First, the going-in yield on $4.5B of acquisitions — the largest capital allocation decision in the company's recent history — is not disclosed, and the guidance revision arithmetic ($18.0M of FFO on $1.9B of incremental activity) is consistent with both a timing effect and thin spreads. Second, the compensation plan's five performance measures include no absolute return-on-invested-capital or spread-to-cost-of-capital metric, which is the precise discipline question for a company deploying $4.5B a year funded with 8% annual share growth. Existing holders should own the 2027 annualization tail. New capital should wait for the cap rate disclosure or a price in the low $80s.
