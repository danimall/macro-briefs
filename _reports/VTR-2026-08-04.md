## Executive Summary

Ventas delivered the quarter the bull case required. Q2 2026 total revenue rose 21.7% to $1,729.6M, total NOI rose 16.5% to $685.2M, and the Senior Housing Operating Portfolio (SHOP) produced same-store cash NOI growth of 16.3% on a 300bp occupancy gain to 90.9%, a 5.0% RevPOR increase to $5,528, and 210bps of margin expansion. Getting occupancy, rate and margin simultaneously reflects a genuine supply-demand imbalance rather than a mix artifact. Management raised FY2026 Normalized FFO guidance for a second time, to $3.85–$3.90, and lifted the 2026 investment program from ~$3.0B to $4.5B. Leverage improved to 4.7x net debt/Further Adjusted EBITDA while gross debt *fell* $336M in six months and the $862.5M exchangeable notes overhang was cleared in June.

The problem is the price, not the business. Roughly half the aggregate FFO growth is funded by equity: weighted diluted shares rose 8.7% in the first half (453.0M → 492.3M), so 18.2% Normalized FFO growth became 8.7% per-share growth. At $89.86 the shares carry 23.2x the guided Normalized FFO midpoint of $3.875 and a 2.3% forward dividend yield, against a peer-group median yield of 4.7%. On a forward NOI run-rate of $2.9–3.0B the implied cap rate is approximately 4.9–5.1% versus a 10-year Treasury at [unverified] and a 30-year at [unverified]. That leaves a modest spread on a portfolio now 71.1% weighted to senior housing *operating* assets, which is an operating business rather than a bond substitute.

This is a mechanically reflexive model: Ventas issues equity at a 4.3–4.5% Normalized FFO yield and buys senior housing. It works while the multiple is 23x. If the multiple compresses because long rates rise or sentiment on senior housing cools, the spread narrows and the external growth engine stalls, leaving same-store growth in a legacy portfolio already at 90.9% occupancy. The prior BUY at roughly $81.60 in June has returned about 10%; the multiple has re-rated from ~21x to ~23.2x on essentially unchanged forward fundamentals. **Downgrade to HOLD.** Base case $97 (+7.9%), bull $112 (+24.6%), bear $74 (-17.6%). The low $80s would restore a buy.

## Company Overview

Ventas is an S&P 500 healthcare REIT with approximately 1,456 properties at June 30, 2026 across the U.S., Canada and the U.K., organized in three segments:

**SHOP (813 communities, up from 683 a year earlier).** RIDEA-style structures where Ventas owns the real estate, engages third-party managers, and consolidates resident revenue and property expense. Q2 2026: $1,363.5M of resident fees (78.8% of revenue) and $403.5M of NOI (58.9% of total NOI). The revenue/NOI share gap reflects a ~29.6% operating margin — SHOP overstates its earnings weight on a revenue basis but is where essentially all incremental growth originates.

**Outpatient Medical & Research (407 properties).** $228.6M rental revenue, $151.5M NOI. Same-store cash NOI +4.6%, occupancy flat at 90.0%. A low-growth annuity whose share of the portfolio is shrinking (16.6% of assets from 18.2% in six months).

**Triple-Net Leased (199 properties).** $124.9M rental revenue, $121.7M NOI, down 18.2% year over year — almost entirely conversions into SHOP (~$22.1M) and dispositions ($5.9M) rather than deterioration; same-store NNN cash NOI was +3.1%.

A distinguishing structural feature: Ventas holds equity and credit positions in its own counterparties. It owns 34% of Atria Senior Living (its largest manager, 17.9% of gross investments) with two board appointees; ~6.6% of Ardent Health (5.7% of NOI); and warrants over 9.9% of a Kindred parent alongside a new $300M secured loan to that group. This gives Ventas influence and optionality a pure landlord lacks, and simultaneously means exposure to a struggling operator extends beyond rent.

In July 2026 the company completed an UPREIT reorganization, enabling issuance of OP units as tax-deferred acquisition currency. Redeemable OP/noncontrolling interests already rose from $375.2M to $509.1M in six months.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is real at the aggregate level.** FY2022 revenue of $4.130B grew to $5.834B in FY2025, a 12.2% three-year CAGR, with FY2025 up 18.5%. First-half 2026 revenue was $3,386.6M (+21.9%) and NOI $1,336.3M (+15.5%). Operating cash flow grew 19.4% to $951.2M, tracking NOI and Normalized FFO closely — the reported earnings growth is appearing in cash, which is the single most important quality check on a RIDEA-heavy REIT.

**Per share, the picture is more sober.**

| | 1H 2025 | 1H 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normalized FFO | $794.9M | $939.3M | +18.2% |
| Weighted diluted shares | 453.0M | 492.3M | +8.7% |
| NFFO per share (derived) | $1.7548 | $1.9081 | **+8.7%** |

Shares outstanding rose from 474.9M at December 31, 2025 to 512.9M at June 30, 2026 (+8.0% in six months). Components: 34.7M forward-sale shares entered in 1H at an $86.02 average ($3.0B gross), 31.4M settled for $2.5B net, and 5.9M issued on the exchangeable-note conversion premium. At July 30, 2026, 18.5M forward shares (~$1.6B) remained unsettled with maturities through December 2027. If all settle, share count rises 11.9% from year-end 2025 (56.5M/474.9M); expressed as ownership transferred, a non-participating 1.00% holder would be diluted to 0.894%, roughly 10.6% relative dilution.

The important distinction: this is issuance into an accretive spread, not empire-building for its own sake. At the $86.02 average issuance price, the implied cost of equity is 4.51% of guided NFFO ($3.875/$86.02); at $89.86 it is 4.31%. Guidance assumes ~$700M of dispositions and loan repayments at a ~7% blended yield. Selling at 7% and funding with 4.3–4.5% equity is the inverse of value destruction. Leverage *fell* while this happened, and gross debt declined in absolute terms — the growth was not debt-financed.

**The verification gap that matters most:** the going-in and stabilized yields on the 2026 acquisitions are not disclosed in the filings. Management's accretion claim is an issuer statement. An alternative and entirely consistent explanation for the guidance raise is that more assets multiplied by any positive spread produces more absolute FFO, which does not establish that the spread is wide. The ~7% disposition yield likely reflects triple-net and loan assets with little growth, while the acquisitions carry lease-up upside — so the raw yield comparison flatters the trade somewhat.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt $12.768B (from $13.104B), cash $199.0M, net debt ~$12.569B. Net debt/Further Adjusted EBITDA 4.7x, described as nearly a full turn better year over year; my cross-check implies ~$2.67B of Further Adjusted EBITDA, which bridges plausibly from 1H segment NOI of $1,336.3M less $109.7M G&A annualized plus JV contribution and pro-forma acquisition credit, though I cannot reproduce the company's exact build. Liquidity $4.9B including a fully undrawn $3.5B revolver to April 2028. 84.9% fixed-rate; weighted average rate 4.4% at period end, with the effective rate drifting from 4.51% (1H 2025) to 4.59% (1H 2026). A 100bp move on the $1.93B variable book costs $19.3M, ~$0.04/share. Maturities are laddered: $731.1M remainder of 2026, $917.8M 2027, $1,538.3M 2028, $2,206.4M 2029.

Two refinancing signals point mildly negative: fixed-rate debt trades at 98.5% of par ($10.675B fair value on $10.838B book), implying market yields modestly above existing coupons, and the effective rate is drifting up. Neither is threatening at 4.7x, but it is a persistent low-grade drag on per-share growth. The January 2026 term-loan amendment (from $500M due June 2027 to $1.25B due January 2031) plus repayment of a $200M February 2027 loan cleanly extended the wall.

**Dividend coverage.** $0.52 per quarter in 1H 2026 versus $0.48 in 1H 2025 (+8.3%), tracking per-share NFFO growth of +8.7%. At a $2.08 annualized rate the payout is 53.6% of guided Normalized FFO; on my approximation of FAD (guided NFFO less ~$400M FAD capex, ~$3.08/share on 506M shares) the payout is ~68%. 1H 2026 operating cash flow less all capex covered dividends 1.30x; excluding growth capex, 1.58x. Both adequate. The quoted 2.22% yield reflects trailing 12-month declarations; the forward run-rate yield is 2.31% ($2.08/$89.86).

**Items excluded from Normalized FFO worth tracking.** Impairments of $60.3M in 1H 2026 versus $57.2M in 1H 2025 — ~$120M annualized on a $39.1B gross asset base (~0.3%), immaterial to solvency but persistent across two years and concentrated in SHOP and OM&R. A $48.3M non-cash tax benefit in 1H from TRS valuation-allowance reversals flatters GAAP net income; cash taxes paid were only $5.6M, and the 10-Q warns TRS liabilities could increase significantly as NOLs are exhausted. Transaction and restructuring costs of $20.1M in 1H (from $10.6M) are recurring in substance while a $4.5B annual acquisition program runs.

**Accounting change requiring care.** Beginning Q1 2026 Ventas excludes non-cash stock compensation from Normalized FFO, restating prior periods ($5.3M add-back in Q2, $30.2M in 1H). This raises the *level* — the DEF 14A lists FY2025 Normalized FFO/share of $3.48, while guidance of $3.875 at 8–10% growth implies a restated base near $3.56 — so anyone comparing 2026 guidance to $3.48 overstates growth by roughly 200bps. It does not flatter the *growth rate*: on the old definition Q2 per-share growth would have been +10.0% versus +9.1% reported, because absolute stock comp declined. Prior periods were restated, which is the correct treatment.

## Growth Analysis

The demand thesis is demographically anchored: the 1946 cohort turns 80 in 2026, and the 80+ population is the core senior housing demand pool. Management's claim that new supply "remains at historic lows" is an issuer assertion I have not verified against third-party construction-starts data, though it is consistent with the post-2020 collapse in senior housing starts and elevated construction financing costs. The Q2 data are consistent with both halves holding.

Segment same-store cash NOI, Q2 2026: SHOP +16.3%, OM&R +4.6%, NNN +3.1%, total company +10.3%. U.S.-only SHOP same-store cash NOI +18% with occupancy +360bps, implying the Canada/U.K. portion grew materially slower.

Two qualifications temper the growth score.

**First, the occupancy runway is migrating from recovery to execution.** Same-store SHOP (567 communities) sits at 90.9% occupancy; the *total* SHOP portfolio (813 communities) averages 88.7%. The non-same-store cohort therefore averages materially below 88.7%, consistent with deliberate value-add acquisitions. That is fine, but the growth story has shifted from recovering the legacy portfolio — a largely mechanical process — to leasing up what the company just bought, which carries higher execution risk. The benign alternative is that recently closed deals simply have not been integrated onto the Ventas platform yet, in which case the gap closes with time.

**Second, management attributed the guidance raise to deal volume, not operations:** "primarily because of our increased investment activity." The implied second-half NFFO/share is $3.875 − $1.908 = $1.97 versus $1.908 in 1H — a modest sequential step consistent with mid-period closings rather than an operational acceleration. The Scion loan contributes ~$32.1M annualized ($0.063/share) but only ~$16M in 2026.

The estimate-revision table shows GAAP EPS consensus falling (next-year -9.9% over 90 days on three analysts, current quarter -33.3% on one). This looks alarming and is largely a basis artifact. GAAP EPS is being mechanically suppressed by front-loaded intangible amortization: net intangible weighted average remaining life fell to 6.2 years from 8.1, with the 1H 2026 acquisition vintage amortizing over just 2.4 years versus 3.5 for 2025. D&A rose $60.0M year over year to $407.7M against only $9.7M of incremental interest expense, which is why Q2 diluted EPS *fell* to $0.14 from $0.15 while Normalized FFO/share rose 9%. Guided FY2026 GAAP EPS of $0.58–$0.63 against $3.85–$3.90 Normalized FFO makes the point. The 165x trailing P/E in the data feed is not a meaningful valuation input for this business, and neither is the 64.3x peer median. FFO estimates went the other way — guidance was raised twice. On a one-to-three-analyst GAAP EPS base of $0.11–$0.15, the "3 beats, 1 miss" surprise record likewise carries little information.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $89.86 basis:

- **23.2x** guided Normalized FFO midpoint ($3.875)
- **23.7x** guided Nareit FFO midpoint ($3.785)
- **2.31%** forward dividend yield versus a 4.7% healthcare-REIT peer median
- **PEG on FFO ≈ 2.6** (23.2x / ~9% per-share growth)

Enterprise value: $46.09B equity (512.9M × $89.86) + $12.57B net debt + $0.509B redeemable OP/NCI + $0.053B NCI ≈ **$59.2B**. Annualizing Q2 NOI ($685.2M × 4 = $2.741B) gives a 4.63% nominal cap rate, but that understates run-rate NOI because Q2 does not include a full period of the $2.2B of Q2 closings and senior housing NOI is seasonal. On a forward run-rate of $2.9–3.0B the implied cap rate is **4.9–5.1%**. These are sensitivities I constructed, not company figures.

That is the crux. A 4.9–5.1% cap rate on a portfolio 71.1% weighted to senior housing operating assets leaves a modest spread over prevailing long rates. The market is capitalizing growth, not in-place income. Higher-for-longer at the long end compresses that arithmetic directly, and it also raises the cost of the equity that powers the acquisition program.

Against the prior review, the multiple has expanded from roughly 21x to 23.2x on essentially unchanged forward guidance. That is the entire change in the investment case: fundamentals held, the price moved, and the asymmetry compressed. The peer P/E table is unusable (GAAP distortion across the group; HR is negative), so relative valuation must be done on yield and cap rate — and on both, VTR is the expensive end of the sector, justified by growth that is roughly 9% per share rather than the mid-single digits typical of net-lease peers.

Analyst targets: low $86 (-4.3%), mean $100.73 (+12.1%), median $100.50, high $114 (+26.8%), with 18 buy/strong-buy against 5 holds and a stable sentiment trend. Consensus implies roughly 24x 2027 NFFO. I do not treat these as a floor here: the stock is neither cheap on forward fundamentals nor inflecting off a trough, so the targets embed the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than serving as independent confirmation.

**Base case $97:** 23.2x maintained on FY2027 NFFO/share of $4.18 (8% per-share growth on $3.875). +7.9% price, ~10% total return with the dividend.
**Bull case $112:** 26x on $4.30 — non-same-store SHOP leases up faster than modeled, U.S. same-store growth holds mid-teens, long rates fall.
**Bear case $74:** 19x on $3.90 — a further long-end selloff compresses the multiple, the equity window narrows, and the acquisition program throttles back to same-store growth in a 90.9%-occupied legacy book.

Base-to-bear is roughly 1:1 from here (+7.9% versus -17.6%). That does not support new capital.

## Competitive Landscape

Three elements of the moat are verifiable rather than promotional.

**Cost of capital.** Issuing $3.0B of forward equity in one half-year at an $86.02 average while reducing absolute debt and cutting leverage nearly a full turn is available to very few buyers of senior housing. In a consolidating asset class, this is the binding constraint on who wins deals.

**Operator alignment.** A 34% stake in Atria with two board seats provides influence over the largest manager (17.9% of investments) that a pure landlord lacks. The corollary is that exposure is not limited to rent.

**Structural flexibility.** The July 2026 UPREIT conversion adds OP-unit currency for low-basis private sellers, widening the seller universe at a $4.5B annual deployment pace; early evidence is the $133.9M rise in redeemable OP/NCI. The balance sheet also supports lender-of-choice trades like the Scion Term Loan.

The "Ventas OI" data platform is asserted as a differentiator with no quantified disclosure; I treat it as unverified.

Where the company is weaker: the non-senior-housing 29% of the portfolio is not carrying its weight. OM&R grew same-store cash NOI 4.6% with flat 90.0% occupancy, and its segment head (Peter Bulgarelli, also President/CEO of Lillibridge) departed under a separation agreement dated May 12, 2026. The research/innovation portfolio is generating widening unconsolidated-entity losses — $(15.2)M in 1H versus $(4.4)M — as development assets are placed in service; FFO adds back the depreciation but not the interest. Senior housing concentration *increased* from 69.2% to 71.1% of assets in six months. The right way to underwrite VTR is as a levered, equity-funded bet on U.S. senior housing operating fundamentals, not as a diversified healthcare landlord. Diversifying the operator roster within senior housing (Atria 19.6% → 17.9%, Discovery 5.4% → 7.1%) is not the same as reducing asset-class concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Reflexive dependence on the equity multiple (dominant).** The model requires issuing at a ~4.3–4.5% NFFO yield into assets yielding more. Multiple compression narrows or inverts that spread and stalls external growth. The company's own forward-looking statement names "the risk of potential dilution resulting from future sales or issuances of our equity securities." With 18.5M forward shares still unsettled through December 2027, some of the issuance is already contracted at prices ($86.02 average zone) that would look expensive if the stock traded to $74.

**2. Long-end rate sensitivity.** With an implied cap rate of 4.9–5.1%, VTR's valuation is a direct spread trade on the long bond. A 2.3%-yielding REIT is exposed to a steepening curve regardless of the policy rate, and rising long rates also raise the cost of the equity that funds the program.

**3. Kindred exposure increased in three directions at once.** The June 2026 Scion package extended all LTAC leases to April 30, 2039 at *existing* cash base rent (2.75% escalator), added a $300M six-year interest-only senior secured loan at a 10.7% effective rate, and amended warrants over 9.9% of Kindred's parent to a lower strike with the term extended to June 2036 — with the warrant value increase described as consideration received. Kindred is 5.1% of Q2 NOI, and its IRF/LTAC trailing occupancy is 58.2%. The favorable reading is that Ventas locked 13 years of rent visibility while getting paid 10.7% on senior secured paper. The less favorable reading is that a 58%-occupied tenant needed a capital-structure "streamlining," and Ventas responded by converting rent-only exposure into rent plus secured debt plus equity-linked exposure. Both are consistent with the disclosed facts; the direction of exposure is unambiguous.

**4. Second hospital concentration.** Ardent is 5.7% of NOI with parent guarantees and a ~6.6% Ventas equity stake. Combined with Kindred, hospital operators are ~10.8% of NOI; the declining percentage share is a SHOP-driven denominator effect, not reduced absolute exposure.

**5. Related-party complexity.** Equity in the largest manager, equity plus a nomination right and a former Ventas executive on the board of a hospital tenant, and warrants plus a loan to a third counterparty. All disclosed and commercially rational, but arm's-length pricing of these leases and loans is difficult for an outside investor to assess.

**6. Concentrated lease expiries.** $214.8M of NNN annualized base rent — 47% of NNN ABR — expires in 2035, including 65 Brookdale properties. Deferred nine years and shrunk, not eliminated. Nearer term, OM&R faces $87.8M (14% of ABR) rolling in 2027, the first real test of a flat-occupancy segment.

**7. New credit-underwriting risk.** Secured loans receivable more than tripled from $143.9M to $436.4M in six months. The risk language now names "borrowers" throughout and includes a dedicated foreclosure risk factor. A double-digit coupon is priced for risk, and Ventas has no long track record as a credit underwriter.

**8. Governance/turnover monitoring item.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks between November 2025 and June 2026, plus the confirmed OM&R segment-head departure. Two of those filings could not be retrieved. This is consistent with a senior-team transition and equally consistent with routine annual-meeting director rotation (the May filing also carried the shareholder-vote item). No CEO succession event is established; Debra Cafaro signed the Q2 10-Q on July 30, 2026 in her 27th year as CEO.

**9. Slow-building cash tax cost.** 1H cash taxes were $5.6M against a $48.3M non-cash TRS benefit; the 10-Q warns TRS liabilities "could be significant" as NOLs are exhausted.

The Altman Z-Score of ~1.31 should be read with care rather than dismissed: the retained-earnings term is structurally negative for an equity REIT (Ventas carries a $(7.9)B retained deficit by design) and the working-capital term is negative because REITs run minimal current assets. Direct solvency evidence is better — 4.7x leverage, $4.9B liquidity, undrawn revolver, 84.9% fixed, declining absolute debt, and a derived interest-coverage proxy of ~3.9x (1H segment NOI less G&A over interest expense).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 25.0% sits 3.6pp above 21.4% one-year realized volatility — modestly rich, and it *rose* 4.6pp (from 20.4%) as the price fell 3.7% between August 2 and August 4. Put/call skew of 7.8% at the one-month tenor indicates elevated put demand, and the put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.03 to 0.36, meaning protection is being added to a book that was previously almost entirely call-side. Put/call volume of 1.37 confirms recent flow is defensive. The term structure is flat (25.0% near versus 23.9% at six months), so uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated on a single date, consistent with a rate-driven repricing rather than a company-specific binary. The one-month implied move is ±4.5%.

This corroborates the caution: the market is paying up for near-dated downside protection on a name that fell from its July 28 intraday high of $101.60 into a broader repricing, while the skew flattens to 0.3% by November. Short interest of 5.5% with 5.9 days to cover is uncrowded and neither confirms nor challenges the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($112, +24.6%)

Senior housing is in a multi-year supply-demand window: the 80+ cohort is expanding as the 1946 birth year turns 80, while construction starts collapsed post-2020. Ventas is capturing it in the operating structure that keeps the margin, and Q2 proved all three levers can move at once (occupancy +300bps, RevPOR +5.0%, margin +210bps). The company has the cheapest cost of capital among senior housing buyers, is deleveraging while it grows, cleared the exchangeable-note overhang, extended its maturity wall to 2031, and just added OP-unit currency via the UPREIT. The 246 non-same-store SHOP communities at materially below 88.7% occupancy represent embedded NOI that requires no new capital. The Scion loan adds $0.063/share of annualized interest income at a 10.7% rate on senior secured paper. If per-share NFFO growth holds at 9–10% into 2027 and long rates ease, 26x on $4.30 gets to $112, roughly matching the $114 street high.

### Bear Case ($74, -17.6%)

The valuation is a spread trade on the long bond with modest cushion. If long rates rise, healthcare REIT multiples compress and 19x on $3.90 is $74. The growth engine then stalls: the legacy portfolio is already 90.9% occupied, OM&R compounds at 3–5% with a departed segment head and 14% of ABR rolling in 2027, the research pipeline is generating widening JV losses, and same-store growth alone does not support a 23x multiple. Half of the headline growth is issuance, and the acquisition yields that determine whether the spread is wide or thin are undisclosed. Ventas has increased exposure to a 58%-occupied hospital operator in three simultaneous ways and tripled a loan book it has limited history underwriting. Impairments run ~$120M annually in the two segments management is growing and holding. Guidance was raised on deal volume, and management said so.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — I expect roughly 10% total return to the base case — but base-to-bear asymmetry of approximately 1:1 does not justify new capital at 23.2x.

**Base case target: $97.** 23.2x maintained on FY2027 Normalized FFO/share of $4.18 (8% per-share growth on the $3.875 FY2026 midpoint). +7.9% price appreciation, ~10.2% total return with the $2.08 dividend.

**Bull case target: $112.** 26x on $4.30 per share.

**Bear case target: $74.** 19x on $3.90 per share, -17.6% from $89.86.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 print in late October 2026 and the September FOMC as the two nearest resolution points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price at or below $82 — approximately 21x forward NFFO, restoring the multiple at which the prior BUY was initiated and widening the implied cap-rate spread.
2. Disclosure of going-in or stabilized yields at or above ~6% on the 2026 acquisition vintage, closing the single largest verification gap in the accretion argument.
3. Two consecutive quarters in which total SHOP occupancy (88.7%) converges toward same-store (90.9%), demonstrating the newly acquired cohort is leasing up on plan.
4. A sustained decline in long-term Treasury yields, which restores cap-rate spread and lowers the cost of the equity that funds the program.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store SHOP cash NOI growth decelerating below ~8% for two consecutive quarters — the operational engine that justifies the premium.
2. Forward equity settled below $80, implying a cost of equity above ~4.85% NFFO yield and a materially thinner investment spread, or an announced slowdown in the $4.5B program.
3. A Kindred or Ardent credit event, or Kindred IRF/LTAC occupancy falling below ~55% — Ventas now has rent, $300M of secured debt and warrants on the line.
4. Net debt/Further Adjusted EBITDA rising above 5.5x, or dividend growth decoupling from per-share FFO growth.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October) and the September FOMC.

## Conclusion

Ventas is a well-run healthcare REIT executing a coherent strategy at an opportune moment in the senior housing cycle, with a balance sheet that is genuinely improving — leverage down nearly a full turn, absolute debt lower, the exchangeable-note overhang cleared, and the maturity wall pushed to 2031. Q2 confirmed the operating thesis: occupancy, rate and margin all advanced in the same quarter, and operating cash flow grew 19.4% in line with NOI, so the earnings growth is showing up in cash.

The score trajectory (6.0 → 6.3 → 6.6) and the June BUY were vindicated by a ~10% advance. What has changed is entirely the entry price: the multiple re-rated from roughly 21x to 23.2x forward Normalized FFO on unchanged guidance, compressing the implied forward cap rate to 4.9–5.1%. A model that funds $4.5B of annual acquisitions with equity issued at a 4.3–4.5% FFO yield is reflexive by construction; the thinner the spread to long rates, the less cushion there is if the multiple compresses. Layer on undisclosed acquisition yields, increased exposure to a 58%-occupied hospital tenant across three instruments, a flat-occupancy OM&R segment that lost its head, and persistent $120M annual impairments, and the case for paying up here weakens.

Overall score 6.1, down from 6.6, driven by valuation (6.0 → 5.0) and risk (concentration and rate sensitivity), partly offset by improving financial health (6.5 → 6.8). **HOLD.** Own it for the ~9% per-share compounding and the 2.3% dividend; add in the low $80s, or on disclosure that the acquisition spread is as wide as management implies.
