**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ADT is the leading U.S. residential and small-business monitored-security provider, serving ~6.1 million subscribers through a recurring-revenue subscription model. After a multi-year portfolio cleanup (divesting Commercial in 2023, exiting Solar in 2024, selling Multifamily in October 2025), it is now a pure-play consumer/SMB security company. The investment case has shifted from subscriber growth to cash return. ADT generates ~$1.7B of free cash flow at a 33% FCF margin, trades at roughly 9.3x earnings, yields 3.3%, and is executing a $1.5B three-year buyback that has already shrunk the share count meaningfully (from ~901M basic in 2024 to ~750M common plus 55M Class B). The stock at $6.72 sits near its 52-week low of $6.25, down ~19% over the trailing year.

Two events reframe the setup. First, Apollo Management fully exited its position on May 5, 2026, disposing of ~204M shares (~$1.5B) and reducing its holding to zero. Apollo had repeatedly sold into registered offerings for years, creating a persistent supply overhang that depressed the multiple; that overhang is now gone, and ADT transitions to a normal float. Second, a cluster of five insiders (including CFO Likosar, COO Ahmad, CBO Khan, CMO Miller, and Director Houston at $250K, 36,450 shares) bought open-market stock in May at $6.75–$6.91, right around the current price. Multi-executive purchasing at these levels is a contrarian signal that management sees value.

The offsetting concerns keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Organic growth is muted: recurring monthly revenue (RMR) is roughly flat at $359M, gross attrition has crept up to 13.1% (driven by non-payment disconnects, a consumer-stress signal), and 90-day estimate revisions are negative across every period. The balance sheet carries $7.8B of debt with the near-term maturity wall cleared but 2027+ refinancing and interest-rate-swap roll-off looming, plus a new federal cash-tax drag now that NOLs are exhausted. EPS growth from here is being manufactured as much by the share-count denominator as by operations. The 12-month thesis is a leveraged cash-return vehicle at a cheap multiple with a cleared overhang — credible, but not a high-conviction long.

## Company Overview

ADT operates a single reportable segment after its portfolio simplification. The business is a recurring-revenue engine: it invests upfront to acquire customers (recovered over ~2–2.5 years) and then collects high-margin recurring monthly monitoring revenue over a long subscriber tail. Annualized monitoring revenue runs ~$4.35B against total FY2025 revenue of $5.13B. Competitive advantages are brand (the "blue octagon" is the dominant U.S. security brand), the largest professional monitoring and installation footprint, an authorized-dealer account-generation network, and switching costs from installed hardware and multi-year contracts.

Strategically, ADT is shifting from a Company-owned-equipment model toward outright equipment sales (~30% of new direct subscribers in Q1 2026, up from 25%, with non-ADT+ residential transitioning in early 2026). This aligns with consumer purchasing preferences and reduces subscriber-system-asset capex, but it compresses unit economics versus the legacy model and inflates reported revenue through installation-sale recognition. The technology pivot centers on the proprietary ADT+ platform (launched 2023) and the February 2026 acquisition of Origin AI ($164M), which adds RF-based ambient/presence sensing. Google is a device supplier (exclusive to ADT's DIFM customers through November 2030) and cloud vendor (ADT committed to $200M of Google Cloud purchases through 2030); ADT+ and Origin AI are ADT-owned IP, not Google products.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Continuing-ops revenue grew to $5.13B (2025) from $4.90B (2024) and $4.65B (2023). The growth is modest and increasingly driven by price increases and the outright-sales accounting shift (installation revenue +$170M in 2025) rather than subscriber volume — RMR was roughly flat YoY. Net income rose to $596M (2025) from $501M (2024). Operating margin is healthy at 25.5%, Adjusted EBITDA reached $2.68B, and Adjusted EPS was $0.89 (vs. $0.75 and $0.60 in the prior two years).

**Q1 2026** continued the pattern: revenue $1.279B (+0.9% YoY), net income $168M (+20% YoY), diluted EPS $0.20, Adj EPS $0.23, Adj EBITDA $674M. Operating cash flow was $638M (up from $467M), helped by lower interest payments and timing. The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (by 2.4% to 13.9%), so current estimates carry reasonable confidence even as they drift lower.

**Cash flow is the standout.** FY2025 OCF was $1.88B and FCF $1.71B (33% margin), characteristic of a mature recurring-revenue model with heavy non-cash D&A ($1.37B, much of it subscriber-asset amortization).

**Balance sheet — the central tension.** Total debt is ~$7.8B face value, D/E is 3.19, cash is just $81M (Dec 2025) / $119M (Q1 2026), and the current ratio is 0.93 (negative working capital). Goodwill ($4.9B) plus intangibles ($4.8B) total ~$9.7B, roughly 61% of $15.8B total assets, a legacy of the Apollo-led 2016 acquisition. The Altman Z-Score of 1.11 screens as "distress," but that metric misreads a PE-built, asset-light recurring-revenue rollup: FCF comfortably covers ~$340M of annual cash interest, the company is in compliance with all covenants, and there are no maturities until August 2027 after the 2026 notes were retired. The genuine concern is 2027+ refinancing plus interest-rate swaps (hedging variable-rate debt) that largely mature by 2028, creating re-hedging risk in a higher-rate environment.

**Capital allocation.** Management refinanced extensively in 2025 (new Term Loans B-2/A, $1B 5.875% notes due 2033, redeeming the $1.3B Second Lien 2028 and the 2026 notes), extending the maturity profile. It simultaneously pivoted to shareholder returns: the $1.5B three-year buyback authorized in February 2026, with 18M shares repurchased in Q1 ($116M) and 17M in April ($114M), plus a $0.055 quarterly dividend. A structural FCF headwind to monitor: ADT became a federal cash taxpayer in 2025 as NOLs were exhausted.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the weakest part of the story. RMR is roughly flat at $359M, and gross customer revenue attrition rose to 13.1% (from 12.7% in 2024 and 12.6% in Q1 2025), driven by higher non-payment disconnects. Each point of attrition is worth roughly $40M of annualized RMR, so the trend matters. Revenue growth is being supported by price increases and the installation-revenue shift rather than net subscriber additions.

Estimate revisions confirm muted momentum. Consensus current-quarter EPS has fallen to $0.22 from $0.24 ninety days ago (-8.6%), the current-year figure is down 3.4% on a 90-day basis to $0.91, and next year is down 4.8% to $0.97. There is a modest 30-day stabilization (current year +2.0%), but this does not approach the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection. The growth that does exist is largely a denominator story: a ~17% reduction in share count off 2024 levels mechanically lifts per-share metrics even with flat operating profit.

The forward growth optionality lies in the technology pivot — ADT+ migration and Origin AI's camera-free ambient sensing as a way to differentiate on product rather than compete purely on price against DIY entrants. These are real but small, immaterial to 2026 revenue, with benefits years out. I weight growth_prospects below average, consistent with flat RMR, rising attrition, and negative 90-day revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

ADT trades at ~9.3x trailing and forward earnings and 3.3% dividend yield. The relevant peer set in Specialized Consumer Services shows a median P/E of ~19.5 (FTDR 19.2, SCI 19.5, MCW 21.5), with H&R Block the closest analog at 6.4x. ADT trades at roughly half the peer median. That discount is partly earned — high leverage, flat organic growth, rising attrition, and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet justify a lower multiple than asset-light, growing peers like SCI. But a discount this wide on a business generating $1.7B FCF, with the Apollo overhang now removed, leaves room for modest multiple repair.

The cleaner valuation frame is free cash flow: ~$1.7B FCF against a ~$4.7B market cap is a ~36% FCF-to-equity yield (before the cash-tax drag normalizes it lower). Even accounting for the cash-tax transition and reserving capital for debt service, the equity is being valued as if FCF is in secular decline, which the data do not support. Analyst targets bracket $7.00–$9.00 (mean $8.16, median $8.50), all above the current price — the low end sits ~4% above spot and the median implies ~26% upside. I do not treat these as floors given the modest growth profile, but they reinforce that the stock is priced toward the pessimistic end of the range.

The reason this is a 6 rather than a 7+ on valuation: the cheapness is the thesis, and a low multiple without a clear re-rating catalyst is precisely the value-trap profile that warrants caution. The Apollo exit and buyback provide a partial catalyst, but flat RMR and rising attrition argue the discount could persist. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal here.

## Competitive Landscape

ADT competes in a fragmented, intensely price-competitive monitored-security market across DIFM and DIY segments. Structural pressures are real: DIY/self-monitoring entrants (SimpliSafe, Wyze, Amazon Ring) offer lower price points with no contracts; cable/telecom and Big Tech bundle security into existing services; the Matter interoperability standard lowers switching costs; and insurers could disintermediate if rate-reduction incentives change. ADT's defense is brand, scale, professional monitoring and response, and a differentiated technology stack via ADT+ and Origin AI's camera-free sensing.

The trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-deteriorating. The attrition uptick and the legacy-base obsolescence problem (a majority of subscribers on ADT Pulse / post-3G hardware incompatible with ADT+) point to ongoing churn pressure, while the outright-sales shift to lower-margin DIY-style economics compresses unit profitability. The Origin AI bet signals an intent to differentiate on proprietary technology rather than price, which is the right strategic instinct given commoditization, but execution and integration risk are real and the payoff is distant. Competitive position scores at the midpoint — a dominant incumbent holding share through brand and scale, but facing genuine structural headwinds without a clear share-gain catalyst.

## Risk Assessment

- **Leverage and rate exposure:** The $7.8B debt load is the defining risk. The near-term maturity wall is cleared, but swaps hedging variable-rate debt largely mature by 2028, creating re-hedging/refinancing risk if rates stay elevated.
- **Rising attrition:** Gross attrition is an early read on consumer financial stress and on legacy-base churn as ADT+ migration accelerates. A sustained rise pressures RMR and risks goodwill impairment.
- **Consumer cycle:** As a Consumer Discretionary name with a subscriber base showing non-payment disconnects, ADT is exposed to a weakening-consumer scenario — softening household balance sheets would accelerate involuntary churn.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment:** The $9.7B of intangibles is vulnerable if attrition deteriorates or growth assumptions weaken.
- **Tax-attribute limitation:** A Section 382 ownership change in Q2 2025 (from Apollo's selldown plus buybacks) limits use of disallowed-interest carryforwards; a valuation allowance on the 163(j) DTA is a forward risk.
- **Controls history:** A prior ICFR material weakness (remediated) and a 2022/2023 restatement, with an ongoing CRM/ERP cloud migration flagged as a forward ICFR risk.
- **Regulatory:** FTC click-to-cancel/auto-renewal rules, telemarketing/dealer vicarious liability under a 20-year FTC consent decree (2014), and tariffs on China-sourced components.

Most of these are at least partly priced into a 9.3x multiple and a near-52-week-low stock, which keeps risk_factors at the midpoint rather than lower.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a mildly favorable signal for holders. Near-term ATM IV is 43.3% versus 1-year historical vol of 26.9% (rich by 16.4 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (43.3% near vs. 33.5% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing some near-term event risk. But the skew is call-heavy (-15.8% put/call skew), the put/call volume ratio is just 0.09, and the put/call OI ratio is 0.65 — there is no aggressive downside hedging despite the depressed stock. Short interest is modest at 6.7% of float with 2.1 days to cover. The combination of rich-but-not-panicked IV, call-heavy skew, and light put demand is consistent with the insider-buying signal rather than contradicting the constructive read. (The reported ATM IV jump to 313.9% in the delta snapshot appears to be a thin-chain data artifact and should be disregarded.)

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8.50–$9.00):** With Apollo fully exited, the supply overhang that suppressed the multiple is gone. A leveraged ~$1.7B FCF machine at a ~36% FCF-to-equity yield, aggressively repurchasing shares (~$230M in Q1+April against a $1.5B authorization) and shrinking the count ~17% off 2024 levels, re-rates toward the low end of the peer multiple as the float normalizes and the insider-buying signal proves prescient. Attrition stabilizes, ADT+/Origin AI differentiation slows churn, and EPS compounds on the falling denominator. Multiple repair to ~12–13x on $0.91–$0.97 EPS supports $8.50–$9.00. This matches the analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$5.20, ~-23%):** The consumer cracks, non-payment disconnects accelerate, attrition pushes past 14%, and RMR declines. Estimate revisions continue lower, the 2027+ refinancing arrives into a higher-rate environment with swap roll-off raising interest cost, and the federal cash-tax drag compresses FCF. The market re-rates the equity toward 7–8x distressed-leverage earnings, and goodwill-impairment risk surfaces. Downside to roughly $5.20.

**Base Case (~$7.30):** Flat-to-low-single-digit revenue growth, attrition holds near current levels, buyback supports per-share metrics, and the Apollo overhang removal plus insider signal drive modest multiple repair toward ~10.5–11x on ~$0.91 EPS. Roughly 9% upside plus the 3.3% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a **growth_hold** rationale: the 12-month thesis is total return from a cheap, cash-generative equity with a cleared structural overhang and an active buyback driving per-share appreciation, not merely yield.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for Q2 attrition, RMR, and buyback pace; ongoing monitoring of the 2027 maturity refinancing path.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. Gross attrition stabilizes or declines below 13.0% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the consumer/legacy-churn concern is contained.
  2. RMR returns to positive YoY growth alongside continued buyback execution (share count falling below ~720M), demonstrating EPS growth is not purely denominator-driven.
  3. The 2027 notes are refinanced on favorable terms, removing the leverage/swap-roll-off overhang.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. Attrition rises above 14% on accelerating non-payment disconnects.
  2. FCF falls materially below ~$1.4B as the cash-tax drag and rising interest compress conversion, threatening buyback/dividend sustainability.
  3. Estimate revisions deteriorate beyond -10% on a 90-day basis or management signals a covenant/refinancing concern.

## Conclusion

ADT is a leveraged recurring-revenue cash machine trading at ~9.3x earnings and a 3.3% yield, with two positives that justify holding rather than avoiding: Apollo's complete May 2026 exit removed a years-long supply overhang, and five insiders — including the CFO and a $250K director purchase — bought open-market stock at current levels. The $1.5B buyback against ~$1.7B FCF is meaningfully shrinking the share count and lifting per-share growth.

The conviction ceiling is set by muted fundamentals: flat RMR, attrition creeping to 13.1% on non-payment, negative 90-day estimate revisions, and a $7.8B debt load facing 2027+ refinancing and swap roll-off, with a new federal cash-tax drag on FCF. The cheap multiple is the thesis, and cheapness without a strong re-rating catalyst can persist. The 90-day price decline reflects negative estimate revisions and consumer-cycle concern rather than pure noise, but it has not impaired the cash-generation or capital-return story; at $6.72 near the 52-week low, the risk/reward has improved without becoming compelling. **HOLD**, with the insider-buying cluster and Apollo-exit catalyst as the reasons to own it and the leverage/attrition trajectory as the reasons not to add aggressively.
