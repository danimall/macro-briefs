## Executive Summary

Cummins is undergoing a structural transformation from a cyclical truck-engine manufacturer into a diversified power technology company increasingly driven by data center demand. Power Systems segment EBITDA margins expanded to 22.7% in 2025 (from 14.7% in 2023), and combined power generation revenue across Power Systems and Distribution reached $9.66B — roughly 29% of total revenue, growing approximately 65% over two years. The company beat Q1 2026 estimates by 9% and raised full-year revenue guidance to +8-11% YoY, with data center backlog extending 6-8 quarters.

The fundamental story is strong: investment-grade balance sheet (net leverage 0.22x), 23.9% ROE, $3.6B operating cash flow during a truck market trough, and a clear growth vector in power generation that has multi-year visibility from hyperscaler capex commitments. Management's decisive exit from hydrogen/electrolyzer ($770M in charges absorbed) demonstrates capital discipline.

The challenge is price. At $675, CMI trades at approximately 33x trailing earnings and 25.5x FY2026 consensus EPS ($26.50). This is near the 52-week high of $717 and represents a 123% gain from the May 2025 low. The analyst consensus mean target sits below the current price ($643), and only the high target ($784) offers meaningful upside. The stock prices in both the data center tailwind and a truck market recovery, leaving limited margin of safety against tariff disruption, China geopolitical risk, or slower-than-expected truck recovery.

## Company Overview

Cummins is the world's largest independent engine manufacturer, serving heavy-duty and medium-duty truck, off-highway, and power generation markets across five segments: Engine (26% of sales), Components (25%), Distribution (30%), Power Systems (18%), and Accelera (<1%). The company operates in approximately 190 countries through 640+ owned/JV distributor locations and 13,000+ certified dealer sites.

The critical business mix shift: Power Systems and Distribution (combined 48% of sales) are now the dominant growth and margin drivers, fueled by global data center construction. The traditional Engine/truck business (51% of sales) is in a cyclical trough with early recovery signals in H2 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Total revenue was approximately flat at $33.7B in 2025 (-1% YoY), masking a sharp divergence: Power Systems +16%, Distribution +9%, while Engine -7% and Components -13%. Q1 2026 revenue of $8.4B (+3% YoY) beat consensus, with Power Systems accelerating to +19% and China power generation +84%.

**Profitability:** Gross margins improved to 25.3% in 2025. Power Systems EBITDA margin expansion from 14.7% (2023) to 22.7% (2025) demonstrates exceptional operating leverage as data center volumes scale through existing infrastructure. Adjusted operating income was $4.025B (12.0% margin). FY2026 EBITDA margin guidance of 17.75-18.5% implies continued improvement.

**Cash generation:** Operating cash flow of $3.62B in 2025 (vs. $1.49B in 2024, distorted by $1.9B settlement). Free cash flow of $2.39B represents a 7.1% FCF margin. CapEx guided at $1.35-1.45B for 2026, with 65%+ directed toward North America capacity, indicating confidence in domestic demand trajectory.

**Balance sheet:** Net leverage of 0.22x against a 0.65x covenant maximum. Total debt $7.55B, cash $3.61B. S&P "A" / Moody's "A2" ratings. $4.0B in undrawn revolving facilities. The company has $2.2B in authorized share repurchases unexecuted — meaningful capital return optionality.

**EPS trajectory:** FY2025 reported $20.50. FY2026 consensus $26.50 (+29% YoY). FY2027 consensus $30.81 (+16%). The growth rate reflects truck market recovery layering on top of the sustained data center ramp.

## Growth Analysis

**Data center power demand:** Cummins provides backup and prime power generators (2kW to 3.5MW) that hyperscalers require for data center buildouts. With the AI infrastructure capex cycle validated by committed investment from major tech companies, and 6-8 quarter backlog visibility, this revenue stream has unusually high durability for an industrial company.

**Truck market recovery:** Heavy-duty shipments fell 23% in 2025 to 101,900 units. The cyclical trough appears to be forming, with Q1 2026 Components guidance raised to +5-10% (from flat to +5%). A normalized truck market would add $2-3B in revenue at historical segment margins — this recovery is partially reflected in the FY2026 guidance raise.

**China growth:** China power generation sales surged 84% in Q1 2026. The three major Chinese JVs contributed $223M in equity income in 2025. This is a significant earnings contributor but carries concentrated geopolitical risk.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates rose +1.6% over 90 days and +1.4% over 30 days. Next-quarter estimates rose +5.2% over 90 days. These are positive but modest — the revision velocity does not meet the +30% threshold that would signal a strong BUY.

- 21.9x FY2027E P/E ($30.81)

CMI at 25.5x forward appears relatively cheap versus peers, but CAT's premium reflects a more dominant competitive position, and WAB's 37x reflects a different business mix. Against PCAR (the most comparable truck-related peer), CMI trades at a meaningful premium, justified by the data center growth vector but narrowing the relative value gap.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean $643 is 5% below current price. Median $660 is 2% below. The high target of $784 offers 16% upside but requires sustained execution. The low of $490 implies 27% downside. The current price trades above consensus, meaning the market has already priced in the Q1 beat and guidance raise.

**The specific valuation concern preventing a BUY:** At 25.5x FY2026 earnings, the stock requires: (1) truck market recovery materializing on schedule in H2 2026 (not yet confirmed in order data), (2) data center margins sustaining at 22.7%+ without competitive pricing pressure from Caterpillar, AND (3) no material tariff impact on the global supply chain. If any of these three assumptions fails, the stock re-rates to 20-22x forward (the PCAR multiple), which implies $530-580, representing 12-21% downside. The reward-to-risk ratio from current levels is approximately 1:1 (16% to bull target vs. 15% to bear case), which does not meet the asymmetry threshold for a BUY recommendation.

## Competitive Landscape

Cummins holds a differentiated competitive position as the only independent engine manufacturer with global scale, a captive distribution network (13,000+ locations), and a growing power generation franchise. The 81-year PACCAR relationship (13% of sales) and deep OEM integration create meaningful switching costs.

In power generation, the competitive advantage is operational: Cummins can service installed equipment globally, which hyperscalers with multi-region deployments require. Caterpillar is the primary competitor but lacks Cummins' independent engine OEM relationships and technology breadth in medium-duty applications.

The company is gaining share in power generation while maintaining engine market share during a cyclical trough. The distribution network creates a structural advantage that is not replicable in the medium term.

Key risk to competitive position: regulatory deregulation. The company explicitly flags in its 10-K that relaxed emissions standards could lower barriers to entry — Cummins' technology investments in emissions compliance serve as a moat that weakens under deregulation.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (HIGH):** Cummins' global supply chain (67,400 employees worldwide, manufacturing across multiple countries) is highly exposed to escalating trade friction. While impacts were "immaterial" in 2025, the 2026 tariff environment is materially worse. The company's 10-K explicitly warns of material adverse effects from "continued and increasing tariff costs."

**China concentration (MODERATE-HIGH):** Nearly 50% of $469M in equity investee income comes from Chinese JVs. Export controls, sanctions escalation, or geopolitical deterioration could impair $200M+ in annual earnings with limited mitigation options.

**Cyclical risk (MODERATE):** The truck market is at a cyclical trough. Recovery timing is uncertain, particularly under trade disruption. A delayed recovery could result in H2 2026 guidance disappointment.

**Regulatory paradox (MODERATE):** EPA deregulation could simultaneously create a $127M emission credit write-off and weaken the emissions-technology moat that underpins competitive differentiation.

**Valuation downside (MODERATE):** Trading above analyst consensus with limited upside to high targets creates unfavorable positioning if execution stumbles or macro deteriorates.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($780-800):** Data center power generation grows 25%+ annually through 2027, truck market recovery arrives on schedule in H2 2026 adding $2B+ in incremental revenue, Power Systems margins sustain above 22%, China growth continues at 50%+ rates, and the stock re-rates to 26-27x FY2027 EPS ($30.81). This implies $800+ target (~19% upside).

**Bear Case ($530-580):** Tariffs materially disrupt supply chains, China JV income impaired by export controls or demand weakness, truck recovery delayed into 2027, data center competitive pricing pressure from CAT compresses Power Systems margins to 18-19%. The stock de-rates to 18-20x forward multiple on FY2026 EPS, implying $480-530 (21-29% downside).

**Base Case ($680-720):** Truck recovery arrives modestly in H2 2026, data center growth continues at 15-20%, margins sustain near current levels, tariff impacts partially mitigated. Stock trades at 24-26x FY2026 EPS — roughly where it is today. Limited price appreciation from current levels (0-7% upside).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $580-600 (22-23x FY2026E) without fundamental deterioration — this would create the asymmetric upside missing at current levels.
2. FY2027 estimates revised upward by 10%+ (to $34+) as truck recovery and data center growth compound faster than consensus expects.
3. Tariff clarity — if trade resolution removes the primary margin risk, the current multiple could re-rate further.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 earnings miss with guidance cut (particularly if Power Systems growth decelerates below 10% or margins compress below 20%).
2. Material China JV impairment or forced divestiture announcement.
3. Truck market orders deteriorate further rather than recovering — would signal consensus FY2026 estimates are too high.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected early August 2026) will provide critical data on truck recovery trajectory, tariff impacts, and Power Systems growth sustainability.

## Conclusion

Cummins is a high-quality industrial company executing a successful transformation toward data center power generation, with a fortress balance sheet and best-in-class distribution network. The fundamental thesis is compelling: multi-year demand visibility from AI infrastructure capex, cyclical truck recovery approaching, and expanding margins in the highest-growth segments. However, the stock at $675 prices in nearly all of this good news. Trading above analyst consensus, near 52-week highs, with elevated options-market hedging demand and material tariff/China risks unresolved, the reward-to-risk ratio from current levels does not justify a BUY. The correct entry point is $580-600, where the same thesis offers 2:1 upside-to-downside asymmetry. HOLD with positive conviction on the business, awaiting better price or accelerating fundamentals that push estimates meaningfully higher.
