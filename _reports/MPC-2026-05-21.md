## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is the largest independent refiner in the United States with 3.0 million barrels per day of crude oil processing capacity. The company is experiencing a geopolitically-driven earnings inflection: Q1 2026 EPS of $1.65 crushed consensus of $0.75 by 119%, and full-year 2026 estimates have been revised upward by +113% over 90 days to $28.16/share. At the current price of $248.89, the stock trades at approximately 8.8x FY2026 consensus EPS — cheap relative to both its peer median (18.5x) and the magnitude of its earnings inflection.

The thesis rests on three pillars: (1) Middle East supply disruptions have structurally elevated crack spreads, and MPC's sensitivity to crack spread changes is approximately $1.125B per $1/bbl; (2) aggressive capital returns continue with $5B in fresh buyback authorization on top of an already 40% reduction in shares outstanding since 2022; and (3) MPLX ownership (~$34.6B at market) provides a valuation floor that implies the market is assigning less than 6.5x current-year EBITDA to MPC's standalone refining operations. The primary risk is geopolitical resolution compressing margins back toward 2024 levels, but the probability of diplomatic resolution remains low given structural incentive alignment among conflict participants.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates through three segments: Refining & Marketing (13 refineries across Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, and West Coast), Midstream (via ~64% ownership of MPLX LP), and Renewable Diesel (Dickinson facility + Martinez JV with Neste). The company was spun off from Marathon Oil in 2011, acquired Andeavor in 2018 (adding West Coast/Mid-Continent capacity), sold Speedway in 2021 for $21.4B, and has since returned over $55B to shareholders through buybacks.

MPC's competitive positioning derives from scale, crude flexibility (processing both sweet and sour), geographic diversification across all three major U.S. refining regions, and an integrated pipeline/barge network enabling inter-refinery optimization. The company supplies 7,882 Marathon-branded and 1,162 ARCO-branded retail outlets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**
Revenue has declined from $177.5B (2022) to $132.7B (2025) due to lower commodity prices, but this is misleading — throughput volumes actually increased from approximately 2,903 to 2,989 mbpd, and sales volumes rose from 3,510 to 3,718 mbpd. EPS declined from ~$28.12 (2022) to $10.08 (2024) on normalizing crack spreads, then recovered to $13.22 in 2025.

The Q1 2026 result ($1.65 actual vs. $0.75 consensus) signals a regime change. Geopolitically-elevated refining margins drove adjusted EBITDA of $2.8B, up ~$800M YoY, with near-perfect 99% capture rates on crack spreads. Full-year 2026 consensus has surged to $28.16 — implying a return to 2022-level earnings power.

**Cash Flow and Capital Returns:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $8.3B against $3.5B in capex, generating approximately $4.8B in free cash flow. The company returned $3.4B in buybacks plus $1.1B in dividends. Shares outstanding fell from 512M (2022) to 305M (year-end 2025) — a 40% reduction. At the current pace plus the new $5B authorization, shares could fall below 280M by year-end 2026, amplifying per-share economics on any margin improvement.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total consolidated debt of $33.3B appears large but is misleading: $26.0B resides at MPLX (non-recourse to MPC). MPC-only debt is approximately $7.3B with $6.6B in liquidity. Credit ratings are BBB/Baa2 with stable outlook. The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 (grey zone) reflects cyclically depressed 2025 earnings and mechanically lower equity from buybacks rather than credit impairment.

**Sensitivity Analysis:**
- $1/bbl change in crack spread = ~$1.125B EBITDA impact
- $1/bbl change in sour differential = ~$520M impact
- $1/MMBtu change in natural gas = ~$360M impact

At current geopolitically-elevated crack spreads (estimated $20-25/bbl versus 2025's $14.89 blended average), MPC's refining segment is generating $8-10B+ in annual EBITDA versus the $6.1B achieved in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

**Estimate Revisions — BUY Signal Present:**
Current quarter EPS estimates have risen +129.6% over 90 days (from $4.91 to $11.26), and full-year 2026 estimates have risen +113.1% (from $13.21 to $28.16). This exceeds the +30% threshold by a wide margin. Combined with forward P/E below 10x and PEG well below 1.0, this represents the strongest form of the estimate revision BUY signal.

**Growth Drivers:**
1. **Geopolitical crack spread elevation:** Hormuz Strait disruption has structurally tightened global refined product supply. With confirmed physical shortage and low diplomatic resolution probability [unverified], elevated margins have duration.
2. **MPLX expansion:** $2.4B in Permian-to-Gulf Coast acquisitions in 2025 (Northwind, BANGL, Whiptail) growing fee-based midstream income. MPLX adj. EBITDA grew 3% in 2025 to $6.75B with further growth from new assets ramping.
3. **Share count reduction:** Even at a modest $3-4B annual buyback pace, the company removes 3-5% of float annually at current prices, mechanically growing EPS.
4. **Utilization improvement:** Q2 2026 guided at 94% utilization (versus 89% in Q1), suggesting further earnings uplift from volume alone.
5. **Refinery value-enhancement projects:** Galveston Bay, Robinson, El Paso investments increasing complexity and capture rates.

**Growth Risk:** Next-year estimates ($22.38) are below current year ($28.16), implying the market expects some margin normalization. This is reasonable — the key uncertainty is whether geopolitical premiums persist through 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

MPC is one of two dominant U.S. independent refiners alongside Valero. Key structural advantages:

1. **No new U.S. refining capacity:** Environmental permitting costs and energy transition uncertainty have created an effective moratorium on greenfield refinery construction. This supply constraint benefits all incumbents but especially those with the largest installed base.

2. **Crude flexibility:** MPC processes both light sweet (Permian/Bakken) and heavy sour (WCS, Maya) crude oils. When sour differentials widen — as typically occurs during geopolitical disruptions — MPC captures outsized margins. The sour differential was only -$2.76/bbl in 2025 (down from -$6.31 in 2023), suggesting further upside if differentials normalize higher.

3. **Export capability:** 401 mbpd of Gulf Coast exports position MPC to capture international margin dislocations.

4. **Scale economics:** 3.0M bpd enables optimization across 13 refineries, with MPC's logistics network connecting facilities. Per-barrel operating costs decline with utilization, creating operating leverage as throughput rises.

5. **Midstream integration:** MPLX's fee-based income provides ~$2.56B in annual LP distributions to MPC regardless of refining margins, creating a cash flow floor.

Competitive position is improving: MPC is investing in complexity upgrades (higher capture rates), MPLX is expanding Permian infrastructure (growing fee income), and share buybacks concentrate per-share economics on fewer shares.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Geopolitical Normalization (Moderate Probability, High Impact):**
If diplomatic resolution opens Hormuz to normal transit, crack spreads would likely revert toward 2024 levels ($14/bbl), cutting refining EBITDA by $5-6B annually. Based on current conflict dynamics [unverified — probability estimates not sourced], this risk probability is assessed as moderate within the next 6 months but increases materially over 12-18 months.

**Secondary Risk — Leverage Structure:**
$33.3B consolidated debt with interest expense nearly tripling from $525M to $1,276M (2023→2025). While MPLX debt is non-recourse, MPC depends on MPLX distributions (~$2.56B/year). If midstream volumes decline or MPLX distribution coverage tightens, MPC's cash flow stability weakens.

**Tertiary Risk — California Regulatory:**
SB X1-2 authorizes maximum gross refining margin caps. The CEC has indicated it won't act on margin caps for at least 5 years, but the authority exists. MPC's LA refinery (365 mbpcd) is directly exposed. A margin cap at California would reduce MPC's West Coast contribution by an estimated 15-25% [unverified].

**Cyclicality:**
The $1.125B sensitivity per $1/bbl in crack spread means that a 5-point compression from current levels would reduce annual EBITDA by $5.6B — roughly halving current-run-rate earnings.

## Conclusion

MPC represents a high-quality cyclical beneficiary of geopolitical supply disruption with strong capital allocation execution. The estimate revision signal (+113% over 90 days) combined with a forward P/E below 9x and PEG well below 1.0 constitutes a strong fundamental setup. The MPLX sum-of-parts provides a valuation floor, while aggressive buybacks concentrate future earnings per share on fewer shares.

The stock has appreciated 60% over the past year, but this move is justified by fundamentals: EPS has more than doubled from depressed 2024 levels, and the market is assigning only 8.8x current consensus. The valuation does not yet reflect the duration of geopolitical supply disruption, nor the continued buyback-driven per-share growth.

The primary risk is binary and largely outside management's control: a geopolitical resolution that reopens crude supply channels. Given the current assessment of moderate probability for diplomatic resolution within 6 months, the expected value of the position is positive. Sizing should account for potential 25-30% drawdown on resolution, suggesting 3-4% portfolio weight is appropriate.
