**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Radian Group has moved beyond the pure-play U.S. private mortgage insurer that its sector tag ("Property & Casualty Insurance") implies. On February 2, 2026, the company closed a $1.67B acquisition of London-based Lloyd's specialty insurer Inigo (Syndicate 1301) and simultaneously divested its Mortgage Conduit, Title, and Real Estate Services units. Radian now runs two segments: Mortgage — a ~50-year-old, high-margin, low-volatility credit-protection annuity with a Q1'26 combined ratio of 30.2% — and Specialty, a global commercial/reinsurance book with a combined ratio in the mid-80s and a materially different, cat-exposed, capital-intensive earnings profile. Any framework that treats RDN as a single-business insurer will misprice it.

The near-term financial picture is distorted by purchase accounting. GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations fell to $0.93 in Q1'26 from $1.03 a year earlier, but that decline is almost entirely $22M of deal costs plus ~$23M of non-cash VOBA amortization that substantially rolls off within twelve months. Adjusted operating EPS rose 22% to $1.27, adjusted operating ROE improved to 14.7%, and 90-day consensus estimates for the current year moved up 7.1% to $5.30. The mortgage book remains fortress-quality — risk-to-capital of 10.2:1 versus a 25:1 regulatory ceiling, 66.7% of new insurance written at FICO ≥740, and $35.6M of favorable prior-period reserve release.

Radian paid a full price (roughly 1.0x tangible book, minimal goodwill) to buy growth in specialty just as that market enters a softening rate cycle, most acute in property and property-cat reinsurance, which management expects to persist through 2026. The strategic pivot also introduced a hard constraint: for three-to-five years, every dividend upstreamed from Radian Guaranty requires prior Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval, tightening the primary cash pipe to a holding company that must service a $450M March 2027 note, ~$138M/yr in dividends, and Intercompany Note interest. The CEO-Elect's ~$5.8M open-market purchase near book value offsets some of this, but the interim-CFO gap during a transformative integration and the negative near-term estimate revisions keep the risk/reward balanced. HOLD, with a growth-hold bias tied to the earnings-optics recovery through 2026-2027.

## Company Overview

Radian operates two reportable segments as of Q1 2026:

**Mortgage (Radian Guaranty).** A leading U.S. private mortgage insurer providing credit protection to lenders and the GSEs on low-down-payment (>80% LTV) residential first-lien mortgages. Insurance-in-force stands at $281.7B and risk-in-force at $74.7B, essentially flat versus year-end 2025. Q1'26 new insurance written was $13.5B, up 42% YoY on a refinance uptick as rates dipped. The book is high quality (66.7% of NIW at FICO ≥740, 12-month persistency 82.4%) and capital-strong (risk-to-capital 10.2:1). Competitors include MGIC, Essent, NMI, and Arch, plus the FHA/VA channels and potential GSE-sponsored alternatives.

**Specialty (Inigo / Lloyd's Syndicate 1301).** A global P&C insurance and reinsurance operation serving large commercial and industrial clients across Property (26.9% of net premiums earned), Casualty (22.7%), Financial Lines, Cyber/Aviation/Political Violence, Natural Resources, and assumed reinsurance (25.3%). Roughly 80% U.S. exposure, 20% international. Founded in 2021, Inigo contributed about two months of results in Q1'26.

The discontinued businesses — Mortgage Conduit (no buyer found, now in orderly wind-down as of March 2026), Title, and Real Estate Services — are expected to be fully resolved by the end of Q3 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 consolidated results** (first period with Inigo, two months):
- Total revenue $466.3M, up 58% YoY entirely on Inigo consolidation.
- Net premiums earned $402.5M ($238M Mortgage + $164M Specialty) versus $234M prior-year mortgage-only.
- Net income from continuing operations $129.5M ($0.93 diluted), down from $152M ($1.03), depressed by ~$68M of one-time/non-cash acquisition items.
- Adjusted pretax operating income $231.8M (+15% YoY); adjusted diluted operating EPS $1.27 (+22%); adjusted operating ROE 14.7%.

**Segment profitability.** Mortgage generated $220.8M of adjusted pretax operating income at a 30.2% combined ratio (loss ratio 10.2%), a highly profitable annuity supported by a benign credit environment and home-price-appreciation-driven cure rates. Specialty contributed $40.1M at an 85.3% combined ratio (loss ratio 52.5%), a more volatile and capital-intensive earnings stream. The Corporate segment posted a $29.1M loss driven by acquisition-financing interest drag.

**GAAP drag items** (non-recurring/non-cash): $22M acquisition expenses, $53M VOBA amortization (~$23M net of purchase accounting), $3.9M intangible amortization. The VOBA burden is front-loaded and substantially amortizes within a year, so reported earnings optics should improve through 2026-2027.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets rose from $8.1B to $10.65B post-Inigo. The loss reserve jumped from $400M to $1.82B (Inigo added $1.36B of P&C reserves). Investments total $7.0B. Book value per share is $35.67, putting the stock at roughly 1.08x book at $38.48 (and ~1.09x at the current $38.87). Debt-to-capital rose to 20.2% from 18.3%.

**Capital allocation.** The multi-year buyback reduced share count from ~168M (2022) to 134.8M (Q1'26). The first $900M authorization was exhausted in April 2026; a second $750M authorization runs through December 2027 and remains fully available. The quarterly dividend of $0.255 (~$138M/yr) is unchanged, yielding ~2.6%.

**Liquidity and cash flow.** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $145.8M, down from $206.7M on deal costs. Holding-company liquidity is $391M unrestricted plus $350M revolver availability, totaling $741M. This must service the note, dividend, and Intercompany Note interest against a regulatory-constrained upstream pipe — adequate, but the margin of comfort has thinned.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: RDN has beaten consensus for four consecutive quarters (2.2% to 8.1% surprises). Financial health scores as above-average but not fortress-level, given the newly introduced financing and upstream-dividend constraints.

## Growth Analysis

The 42% YoY jump in NIW and 58% revenue growth overstate underlying momentum — the revenue figure is a consolidation artifact, and the NIW increase reflects a refinance-driven cyclical uptick that doubles as a persistency headwind (82.4% versus 83.7% YoY, with roughly half of IIF carrying note rates ≤5.5% that would run off faster if rates fall further).

The durable growth thesis rests on two pillars. First, the adjusted-earnings recovery: consensus next-year EPS sits at $5.49, and as VOBA amortization rolls off through 2026, the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings should narrow, mechanically improving reported results. Second, the Specialty diversification: Inigo adds a growth vector the legacy mortgage-only franchise lacked, and management's stated posture is margin-focused underwriting through the cycle, non-renewing inadequately priced risk.

The counterweight is timing. The 90-day revision trend is positive, but the 30-day trend has turned modestly negative across every horizon (current quarter -1.0%, current year -0.3%, next year -0.4%). That deceleration coincides with management's warning of "risk-adjusted rate reductions" in specialty, most pronounced in U.S. property and property-cat reinsurance. Radian bought a growth engine at the top of a pricing cycle; the diversification pays off if Inigo's underwriting discipline compounds book value, and disappoints if soft rates plus a bad cat year compress specialty returns before the thesis proves. Growth prospects are real but not yet de-risked.

On book value, the stock sits at ~1.08x versus a business now split between a mortgage annuity that historically supports 1.2x+ and a specialty book that trades closer to 1.0x tangible for disciplined Lloyd's underwriters.

Valuation scores mid-range, tempered by the recognition that cheap multiples in this environment have frequently been value traps absent an independent catalyst; here the catalyst (VOBA roll-off, EPS optics recovery) exists but is gradual.

## Competitive Landscape

**Mortgage.** Radian is a top-tier participant in a disciplined, well-capitalized private MI oligopoly (MGIC, Essent, NMI, Arch). The competitive position is stable-to-favorable: benign credit, strong home-price appreciation driving high cure rates and favorable reserve releases, and a regulatory tailwind from the March 2026 Basel III re-proposal that maintains favorable treatment of mortgage insurance. Persistency erosion on a refi uptick is a mild headwind, and the structural tail risk is a GSE-sponsored alternative to traditional MI. The book itself is not gaining share so much as holding a strong, profitable position.

**Specialty.** Inigo is a four-year-old Lloyd's underwriter entering a softening market after several strong years and a benign 2025 cat season. Increased traditional and alternative capital (cat bonds) is compressing rates, particularly in short-tail property. Inigo's competitive edge — disciplined underwriting, willingness to non-renew underpriced risk, portfolio reshaping — is the right posture for a down-cycle, but it is unproven under Radian's ownership and through a full cycle. The company also flagged adverse reserve development on 2021-2022 general liability underwriting years, a reminder that a young book carries reserve-adequacy risk.

Net, competitive positioning is stable in the cash cow and improving-but-unproven in the growth engine, with the overall trajectory dependent on execution in a segment Radian has operated for two months.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition and strategic-pivot risk (dominant, new).** RDN is entering global specialty, Lloyd's, and international operations with no prior track record, buying a firm founded only in 2021. Management explicitly flags risks of entering new markets and managing international operations.

**Reduced financial flexibility from deal financing (material, new).** The $600M Intercompany Note from Radian Guaranty triggers a requirement that all Radian Guaranty dividends receive prior Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval for three-to-five years, plus a $500M minimum-surplus covenant. This constrains the primary cash pipeline to the holding company. The PMIERs cushion fell to $1.6B/41% from ~$2.2B historically. Radian front-loaded a $140M dividend in Q1'26 before the window fully bit; holdco liquidity must now cover the 2027 note, dividends, buybacks, and Intercompany Note interest.

**Specialty earnings volatility and soft pricing (new).** Softening specialty rates, California wildfire claims already being paid, Middle East geopolitical exposure across political violence/aviation-war/cyber/energy lines, and flagged adverse development on 2021-2022 GL years all sit inside a book with an 85% combined ratio.

**Near-term debt maturity.** $450M Senior Notes due March 2027 (reclassified short-term), plus $625M due 2029 and the $600M Intercompany Note.

**Mortgage cyclicality.** Declining persistency, ~half of IIF at note rates ≤5.5% (runoff risk if rates fall), and a 2.5% default rate (24,625-loan inventory). A housing/credit downturn remains the core exposure.

**Governance.** Interim CFO (Daniel Kobell) serving during a transformative integration, following Sumita Pandit's departure. A permanent CFO would be reassuring.

Idiosyncratic risk is elevated by the pivot and the upstream-dividend handcuff, which are specific and not fully priced. Risk factors score below the midpoint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 34.4% is rich against 23.8% one-year historical vol, with the term structure in backwardation (34.4% near versus 24.4% long-dated) and a 16.3% put/call skew signaling elevated near-term put demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.1%, consistent with an event in the front month. IV collapsed from an anomalous 167.7% snapshot in mid-June to ~35%, and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply to 0.18 (call-heavy on open interest), so the aggregate positioning is not one-sidedly bearish. The signal is elevated near-term uncertainty rather than a durable directional bet against the stock. Short interest is low at 5.2% of float (4.0 days to cover), leaving no crowded-short dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap resolves in shareholders' favor as VOBA amortization rolls off through 2026, lifting reported EPS toward the ~$5.30-5.49 adjusted range and prompting analysts to move targets higher. The CEO-Elect's open-market purchase proves prescient. Inigo's disciplined underwriting compounds book value through the soft cycle, the diversification lowers the earnings beta of a previously mono-line insurer, and the market re-rates the hybrid toward the low end of the specialty-plus-MI multiple range. Continued buybacks against the $750M authorization shrink the share count further. Bull case ~$48 (analyst high), roughly 25% above current, achievable if the integration executes cleanly and specialty pricing stabilizes.

### Bear Case
Soft specialty rates plus an above-average cat year (California wildfire, geopolitical lines) push the Specialty combined ratio above 90%, and adverse development on the 2021-2022 GL years worsens. The upstream-dividend handcuff forces the holdco to lean on the revolver or trim buybacks to service the 2027 note, undermining the capital-return story. The mortgage refi uptick accelerates runoff, pressuring the annuity. The interim-CFO gap persists into a difficult integration. In that scenario the stock derates back toward book value or below, ~$32-34, roughly 12-17% below current.

### Base Case
Adjusted earnings hold in the $5.30+ range, VOBA roll-off improves optics, Inigo neither shines nor stumbles in year one, and capital return continues at a measured pace under the tighter upstream regime. The stock grinds toward the low-$40s as the earnings-optics recovery plays out and consensus targets are affirmed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the 12-month appreciation case is credible (VOBA roll-off lifts reported EPS, sub-9x forward multiple, improving analyst sentiment, insider conviction), but the specialty pricing cycle, upstream-dividend constraint, and interim-CFO gap prevent a confident BUY. The recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → HOLD → HOLD) and declining score velocity argue for a higher bar to upgrade, and the current data — positive 90-day but negative 30-day estimate revisions, soft specialty pricing — does not clear it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing/positive 30-day estimate revisions confirming the adjusted-EPS path toward $5.30+, with GAAP EPS visibly converging toward adjusted as VOBA amortizes.
- A permanent CFO appointment plus early evidence (through Q2-Q3 2026 prints) that Inigo's Specialty combined ratio holds in the mid-80s or better through the soft cycle.
- A pullback toward book value (~$34-35) that widens the risk/reward while fundamentals stay intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Specialty combined ratio deteriorating above ~95% on cat losses or adverse reserve development, indicating the pivot was mistimed.
- Holding-company liquidity pressure forcing a buyback suspension or reliance on the revolver to fund the 2027 note or the dividend.
- A cut or freeze to the $0.255 quarterly dividend, or evidence the PA-approval regime is materially throttling upstream cash.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26, expected early August), which will provide the first three-full-months view of Inigo and an early read on whether the specialty softening is showing up in results.

## Conclusion

Radian is a well-managed hybrid insurer trading at a defensible discount to a heterogeneous peer group, with a near-term catalyst (VOBA roll-off lifting reported earnings) and the incoming CEO's ~$5.8M open-market buy near book. The mortgage franchise remains a fortress annuity, and adjusted operating metrics are improving. Against that, the company bought a growth engine at the top of a specialty pricing cycle, tied its own hands on upstreaming cash for three-to-five years, and is integrating a four-year-old Lloyd's book without a permanent CFO. The 30-day estimate deceleration and rich near-term options positioning reinforce that the market is unsure how the next few prints will land.

The risk/reward is balanced-to-modestly-favorable, not asymmetric. The stock sits near the low end of analyst targets ($39) and about 15% below the mean ($45), which supports patience but not aggression. **HOLD.** The path to a BUY runs through the Q2-Q3 prints demonstrating specialty discipline and earnings-optics recovery; the path to AVOID runs through cat losses, adverse specialty development, or liquidity pressure on the constrained holdco.
