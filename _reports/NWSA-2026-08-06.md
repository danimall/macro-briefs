## Executive Summary

News Corp closed fiscal 2026 (June 30 year-end) with the best continuing-operations year in its post-2013 history: revenue of $9,028M (+7%), Total Segment EBITDA of $1,627M (+15%), net income from continuing operations of $743M (+15%), adjusted diluted EPS of $1.18 versus $0.89, and free cash flow of $811M versus $571M. Management converted that into a fourfold acceleration in repurchases: $641M of News Corp stock bought in FY26 versus $150M in FY25, executed at Q3 average prices of $24.70 (Class A) and $27.97 (Class B), while holding a net cash position of roughly $106M ($2,095M cash against $1,989M of non-current borrowings, no current maturities). The fourth quarter beat consensus adjusted EPS by 62% ($0.35 versus $0.22), the fourth consecutive beat.

Two adjustments matter before extrapolating any of that. First, currency did meaningful work: the AUD/USD average moved from roughly 0.63–0.67 across FY25 to 0.65–0.71 in FY26, adding $189M of revenue (two points of growth) and $43M of Segment EBITDA (three points) for the year, and $71M/$23M in Q4 alone (four and seven points of the Q4 headline). On an adjusted basis revenue grew 4% and Segment EBITDA 12%. Second, essentially the entire year-over-year free-cash-flow improvement landed in the fourth quarter: nine-month FY26 FCF was $535M against $539M a year earlier, so the $240M full-year gain arrived in three months and management attributed part of it to working-capital timing. The durable component is closer to the ~$169M of adjusted EBITDA growth net of cash taxes.

The stock is not cheap on the metrics that matter. Using the full 547.4M shares outstanding across both classes (364.9M Class A + 182.6M Class B, per the Q3 FY26 10-Q cover page), equity value is $16.15B and enterprise value $16.05B, which is 9.9x consolidated Segment EBITDA and 25.0x FY26 adjusted EPS. On consensus, the shares trade at 22.9x FY27 ($1.29) and 20.1x FY28 ($1.47), against expected adjusted EPS growth of roughly 9% and 14%, a PEG of 2.0–2.5. The sum-of-the-parts argument (mark the REA stake, buy the Dow Jones-anchored stub cheaply) is intellectually sound and has been sound, and unrewarded, for years; the dual-class structure and Murdoch control offer no mechanism to close it. I am holding: the balance sheet and buyback support the downside, the Dow Jones professional-information franchise supports the base case, and the Australian tax reform plus the measurable erosion of the free-reach audience funnel cap what I will pay. Score moves to 6.0 from 6.2, with financial health and management quality improving while risk factors deteriorate.

## Company Overview

Following the April 2025 sale of Foxtel to DAZN, News Corp reports five segments. FY2026 (per the August 5, 2026 earnings release):

| Segment | FY26 Revenue | % of total | FY26 Segment EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dow Jones | $2,497M | 27.7% | $663M | 26.6% |
| Digital Real Estate Services | $2,016M | 22.3% | $741M | 36.8% |
| Book Publishing | $2,288M | 25.3% | $287M | 12.5% |
| News Media | $2,227M | 24.7% | $139M | 6.2% |
| Other (corporate) | — | — | ($203M) | — |
| **Total** | **$9,028M** | | **$1,627M** | **18.0%** |

Dow Jones houses the WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch and IBD alongside a Professional Information Business: Risk & Compliance ($392M, +16%), Dow Jones Energy including OPIS ($301M, +8%), Factiva and Newswires. Digital was 84% of segment revenue, up from 82%. Digital Real Estate is a 61.6% interest in ASX-listed REA Group (revenue $1,406M, +12% reported, +7% constant currency) and 80% of Move/Realtor.com ($610M, +11%), with REA holding the other 20% of Move. Book Publishing is HarperCollins, the world's second-largest consumer publisher, with backlist at 62% of consumer revenue and control of the Tolkien, Lewis, Christie and Orwell estates. News Media spans News Corp Australia, News UK, the New York Post, News Broadcasting and Sky News Australia.

The defensible economics are narrower than the revenue mix suggests: REA's two-sided marketplace position in Australian residential listings, Dow Jones's regulatory-mandated compliance data and price benchmarks embedded in enterprise workflows, and HarperCollins's owned backlist. The 10-K's own framing, "reputation, credibility and brands are key assets and competitive advantages," describes the softer half of the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Margins.** Total Segment EBITDA margin expanded 130bp to 18.0% from 16.7%. The expansion came from Dow Jones (EBITDA +$75M/+13% on +7% revenue), DRES (+$140M/+23% on +12% revenue, aided by the absence of $12M of FY25 Rightmove bid costs), and a $20M reduction in corporate cost to $203M. Both remaining segments went backwards: Book Publishing EBITDA fell 3% to $287M despite +6% revenue, though $29M of discrete items (a $16M international inventory write-off and a $13M receivable write-off on a book distributor's closure) explain the entire gap, with ex-items margin roughly flat at ~13.8%. News Media EBITDA fell 9% reported and 14% adjusted on adjusted revenue of -1%, with California Post launch costs and World Cup broadcasting costs cited.

**Cash generation.** Operating cash flow of $1,237M (+26%) against $743M of continuing-operations net income is 1.66x conversion, helped by $485M of D&A. Capex was $426M, leaving $811M of FCF. Two qualifiers. Consolidated FCF includes 100% of REA's cash generation against 61.6% ownership; noncontrolling interests absorbed $170M of FY26 net income, and of the $204M of dividends paid, roughly $94M went to REA minorities (News Corp shareholder dividends approximate $0.20 × ~552M weighted shares ≈ $110M). On that basis attributable FCF is nearer $717M, a 4.4% yield on full equity value rather than 5.0%. And the year-over-year improvement was fourth-quarter and partly working-capital driven; the nine-month figure was flat.

**Balance sheet.** Net cash of ~$106M with no current borrowings. The debt stack is long-dated: $994M of 3.875% notes due May 2029, $495M of 5.125% notes due February 2032, and a $499M term loan A due March 2031 carrying a 4.961% nominal rate but an effective 3.458% after a ~$456M-notional swap hedged to March 2027. The March 27, 2026 Amended and Restated Credit Agreement provides $1.5B of unsecured capacity (revolver raised from $750M to $1B, fully undrawn) with a $250M accordion, and a single maintenance covenant of 3.5x net leverage; against negative net leverage, the headroom is not a constraint. This was an opportunistic refinancing from strength, not a liquidity event.

Three items sit off the debt line. Operating lease commitments total $1,522M, of which $1,055M falls beyond five years. HarperCollins entered an October 2025 finance-lease arrangement for up to $120M of warehouse equipment at SOFR+1.475%, drawable through June 2028, which will add reported obligations on commencement. And $245M of cash sits at REA Group and is explicitly "not readily accessible": REA must declare a dividend for News Corp to reach its share. REA's own A$200M facility is non-recourse to News Corp.

**Capital returns.** FY26 uses: $641M of News Corp repurchases, $141M of REA repurchases of its own ASX shares, $204M of dividends, $426M of capex, $122M of acquisitions. The 2021 $1B authorization was completed in Q3 FY26; $851M remained under the July 2025 authorization at March 31, 2026. Shares outstanding fell from 565.4M (June 30, 2025) to 547.4M (May 1, 2026), a 3.2% reduction in ten months; weighted diluted shares fell 2.0%. Approximately $751M was returned to News Corp shareholders, about 93% of FCF, while simultaneously funding capex, a new masthead and a warehouse. That is only possible on a net-cash balance sheet, and it is the clearest statement management has made about where it thinks the stock is.

The semi-annual dividend was held flat at $0.10 (declared August 5, payable October 7), a 0.68% annual yield. This is not an income holding.

**A data caveat.** The supplied market capitalization of $10.64B covers only the Class A line, and the supplied trailing/forward P/E of 36.9x is computed against $0.84 of EPS, which corresponds to FY2025 continuing-operations diluted EPS rather than the FY2026 result. Neither figure should be paired with consolidated EBITDA or FCF. All multiples below use the full 547.4M share count and FY26 actuals.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is narrow and identifiable. Dow Jones's Professional Information Business grew R&C 16% and Energy 8% for the year, but both decelerated in Q4, to +11% and +4% respectively. That deceleration is the single most important operating datapoint for the base case, because the consumer side is growing more slowly than the subscriber count implies: WSJ digital-only subscriptions rose 8% to 4,465,000 and total digital-only News Products subscriptions rose 9% to 6,258,000, while circulation and subscription revenue grew only 3%. Management attributed part of the subscriber growth to "enterprise news subscriptions," bulk institutional seats at lower revenue per subscriber. Volume growth is outpacing revenue growth in the consumer franchise.

Digital Real Estate's FY26 was strong operationally and flattered optically. REA's Q4 revenue rose 21% reported but 9% constant currency, with $38M of the $68M increase from FX; segment Q4 EBITDA rose 46% reported and 33% adjusted. Australian national residential buy listing volumes were +11% in Q4 with Sydney and Melbourne each +8%. Move's numbers are the most interesting divergence in the release: unique users -6%, lead volume +1%, revenue +13%, driven by a deliberate shift to premium products (RealPRO Select) plus seller, new-homes and rentals. Monetizing fewer, better leads is working now; it also trades future funnel optionality for present ARPU, and it depends on agents continuing to pay more for a narrowing audience.

Book Publishing's +6% (+4% adjusted) with 15% Q4 growth was frontlist-driven: backlist fell from 65% to 60% of Q4 consumer revenue on the strength of specific titles. Frontlist quarters are inherently less repeatable. News Media is in structural decline: adjusted revenue -1%, adjusted EBITDA -14%, advertising -5% constant currency, digital only 41% of Q4 segment revenue, and Australian digital subscribers essentially flat and slightly down (1,162,000 versus 1,166,000).

Consensus reflects this shape: FY27 adjusted EPS of $1.29 (revised up 3.9% over 90 days, 3.1% over 30) and FY28 of $1.47 (up 9.1% over 90 days). That implies 9% then 14% growth, respectable but not an acceleration. The near-term quarter estimate was cut 10.7% to $0.26, but only two analysts contribute to quarterly figures and the annual revisions, from four analysts, are positive. The four-quarter beat record (+17.0%, +14.2%, +12.1%, +62.4%) supports reasonable confidence in the annual numbers.

One externally reported item I cannot verify: a headline dated August 6, 2026 indicating management targets $1B of Dow Jones Segment EBITDA by FY2030. That figure appears nowhere in the earnings release. If accurate, it implies a 10.8% four-year CAGR from $663M, against 13% delivered in FY26 and decelerating PIB growth in Q4. I do not incorporate it into the base case.

## Valuation Assessment

Working from the canonical $29.475 price and 547.4M total shares:

- Equity value: $16,151M (Class B is valued here at the Class A price; the company's own Q3 repurchases were executed at a ~13% Class B premium, so this is conservative)
- Enterprise value: $16,151M + $1,989M − $2,095M = **$16,045M**
- EV / FY26 Total Segment EBITDA ($1,627M) = **9.9x**
- P/E on FY26 adjusted EPS $1.18 = **25.0x**; on reported continuing-ops diluted EPS $1.03 = **28.6x**
- P/E on FY27 consensus $1.29 = **22.9x**; on FY28 $1.47 = **20.1x**
- FCF yield: 5.0% on reported FCF, **4.4%** on minority-adjusted ~$717M
- ROE: $573M / ~$8,650M average equity = **6.6%**

Against publishing peers (median P/E 24.5x; NYT 31.8x, NWS 38.2x, IAC 24.5x, WLY 15.5x, SCHL 18.0x), NWSA at 25.0x adjusted trades at a small premium to the median and a material discount to its own Class B line and to NWS. That is roughly fair. On PEG (22.9x FY27 against ~9% FY27 growth, or ~2.0x against the two-year average of ~11.6%) the shares are not cheap.

The bull case rests on sum-of-the-parts, and the framework is correct: consolidated EV/EBITDA of 9.9x systematically flatters what an NWSA holder owns, because 45.5% of Segment EBITDA sits in a listed subsidiary that News Corp owns 61.6% of and whose cash is partly trapped. The right approach marks the REA stake to its ASX price and values the residual stub (Dow Jones + Move + HarperCollins + News Media, less corporate cost) separately. **I could not verify REA Group's current market capitalization, and that is the single most important missing input here.** What I can say without it is directional: for the stub to be worth zero, the 61.6% REA stake would have to be worth the full $16.0B enterprise value, implying a REA market capitalization above US$26B [unverified — REA market cap not in sources]. Under an assumed $8–10B mark on the stake, the stub implies roughly 6–8x on ~$985M of stub EBITDA (using an illustrative REA EBITDA of ~$640M, which is itself an assumption since REA and Move EBITDA are not disclosed separately). Dow Jones alone at 12–14x would exceed that. So the stub does look cheap.

The problem is that it has looked cheap for years, and there is no mechanism to realize it. The Murdoch Family Trust structure, the Class B voting concentration (the September 8, 2025 New Stockholders Agreement caps LGC entities and family members at 44% of Class B voting power with a forfeiture enforcement mechanism), Class A's near-total absence of voting rights, and the by-law waiver of fiduciary liability for directing corporate opportunities to FOX all argue that the conglomerate discount is a permanent feature of the security rather than a temporary mispricing. This is cheapness without a catalyst, and publishing has produced more value traps than most sectors. The one clause worth watching is that the New Stockholders Agreement terminates on distribution of substantially all Class B shares held by the LGC entities, and those entities hold registration rights, but nothing in the filings indicates timing or intent, and I will not build a target on it.

Analyst coverage is constructive: 2 strong buy, 5 buy, 1 hold, with a mean target of $36.68 and a median of $37.00 against a low of $29.40 and high of $43.00. The current price sits essentially at the low target, 24% below the mean. On a stock at 22.9x forward with positive revisions, consensus targets are a reasonable reference rather than a ceiling, but the dispersion ($29.40 to $43.00) is wide enough that the mean carries limited information.

Valuation score: 5.6. Modest support from the FCF yield, the buyback and the SOTP framework; capped by a 2.0–2.5 PEG, a fair-to-slight-premium peer multiple, and a structural discount with no closing mechanism.

## Competitive Landscape

**Improving.** Dow Jones's PIB sells compliance data whose demand is regulatory-mandated and price benchmarks that the 10-K notes are themselves "subject to regulatory frameworks in the E.U. and other jurisdictions"; regulatory recognition is a barrier to entry. R&C at $392M growing 16% and Energy at $301M growing 8% are the highest-quality revenue lines in the company. REA remains dominant in Australian residential listings, with Q4 revenue growth of 9% constant currency on 11% listing growth attributed to price increases and add-on products, evidence of pricing power intact.

**Deteriorating.** The free-reach properties are losing their referral funnel at a rate I have not seen quantified this starkly before. The Sun's digital offering reached 65 million global monthly uniques in June 2026 versus 87 million a year earlier, -25% (Meta Pixel). The New York Post's digital network reached 77 million versus 90 million, -14% (Google Analytics). Realtor.com's Q4 monthly uniques were down 6%. The FY2025 10-K anticipated exactly this: generative AI chatbots and search overviews "have, and may continue to, reduce traffic to, and subscriber demand for, the Company's digital products," and platform algorithm changes "have adversely affected traffic to some of the Company's digital properties, particularly in the U.K." News Media adjusted EBITDA at -14% is the P&L expression.

Two honest caveats on those numbers. They come from three different measurement systems, and cookie/pixel-based counting methodology changes can produce large apparent declines without equivalent real ones; the company did not attribute the specific declines to AI. And the damage is concentrated in free, ad-supported reach; the paid franchises grew (WSJ digital-only +8%, Times/Sunday Times +6% to 681,000). Still, the direction is consistent across three properties and one full year.

Move's competitive position is the weakest: a Comscore-measured 33% share of visits to U.S. real estate portals, a distant second to Zillow, operating Realtor.com under a **perpetual license from the National Association of Realtors** that NAR may terminate for contractually specified reasons, on which Move would have to hand NAR a copy of the operating software and copies of its advertiser and data-provider agreements. Move generated $610M of FY26 revenue on a licensed brand right, not an owned one.

Supplier relationships are conflicted rather than complementary. The 10-K names reliance on AWS for cloud services and Google for workspace and enterprise services; Google is simultaneously a critical vendor, the operator of the search and AI Overview systems implicated in the traffic decline, and a potential licensing counterparty. HarperCollins depends on Amazon as a dominant retail channel while having been a co-defendant alongside it in e-book antitrust litigation.

On AI licensing: News Corp is the licensor. The CEO stated on August 5 that the company has "trusted content relationships with OpenAI and Meta, and are in advanced discussions with several other companies," under a "woo and sue" approach. Content licensing revenue is cited qualitatively as a driver of Dow Jones and News Media subscription growth, but no dollar amount, contract value, term or renewal date appears in the earnings release, the Q3 10-Q, or the FY2025 10-K, while the 10-K states that "not all of the Company's content license agreements have been renewed." An investor cannot size or model this line today. Non-disclosure most plausibly reflects counterparty confidentiality and immateriality thresholds against a $9.0B revenue base rather than anything about quality, but the practical consequence is the same: the licensing thesis is unquantifiable.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed, with two franchises strengthening and two eroding. Score: 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Australian negative-gearing and CGT reform (highest-consequence).** Reforms limiting negative gearing to new builds and altering capital gains taxation have become law, announced in the May 12, 2026 federal budget, with secondary sourcing indicating a July 1, 2027 effective date. Reuters reported on June 23, 2026 that the overhaul is cooling residential demand. Digital Real Estate produced 45.5% of FY26 Segment EBITDA and the great majority sits in REA, whose economics are levered to Australian listing volumes and yield. The tension in the evidence is unresolved: REA's Q4 listing volumes were *up* 11% nationally. Either investors are pulling listings forward ahead of the effective date, which would make FY26/FY27 artificially strong and FY28 weak, or the transaction impact is modest and the equity reaction overshot. I cannot distinguish these, and the reform appears in no News Corp filing I read; the Q3 FY26 10-Q states there were "no material changes to the risk factors described in the 2025 Form 10-K." A claimed ~25% REA share-price decline and ~18.3x forward EV/EBIT come from a single secondary source and are unverified.

**2. OPIS antitrust and criminal inquiry.** Class actions in the Northern District of Illinois name News Corp subsidiary Oil Price Information Service in PVC pipe antitrust claims seeking treble damages; a May 2025 settlement received preliminary approval in July 2025 but remains subject to final approval, and a parallel action was filed in British Columbia in September 2025 with no loss estimate. Separately, OPIS received a **DOJ Antitrust Division grand jury subpoena in January 2025** and a state AG civil investigative demand in April 2025, both seeking documents on PVC pipe and the PVC and Pipe Weekly Report. Receipt of a subpoena is not an allegation against the company, and the report at issue covers a small niche within a $301M revenue line. But Dow Jones Energy is a benchmark business whose value rests on the regulatory acceptance of how its benchmarks are compiled, so the franchise exposure exceeds the disclosed financial exposure.

**3. AI funnel erosion, already in the numbers.** See above. The 10-K also notes a legal asymmetry: some lower courts have found that unlicensed use of copyrighted material for AI training can constitute fair use, which weakens the negotiating position underpinning the licensing strategy.

**4. Move/NAR license structure.** $610M of revenue operated under a perpetual but terminable agreement whose wind-down provisions transfer the operating software and commercial contracts to the licensor. Separately, the NAR antitrust settlements and elimination of the cooperative compensation rule could, per the 10-K, "reduce the number of leads and other services agents purchase from Move ... or require changes to its business model."

**5. FX reversal.** The AUD tailwind that supplied two points of FY26 revenue growth and three points of EBITDA growth reverses symmetrically. At FY25 average rates, FY26 EBITDA growth would have been 12% rather than 15%.

**6. Receivable and disclosure items.** The $205M News America Marketing deferred consideration was reclassified to non-current assets during the nine months to March 2026 because the payment due date was extended, a collectibility question on 25% of FY26 FCF, though no impairment was recorded and extensions are also negotiated for benign commercial reasons. And the FY2026 10-K was not yet filed at the time of this analysis; the formal risk-factor set has not been updated for the Australian reform, the audience declines, the REA share move, or the Meta arrangement. That filing is the next document to read.

**7. Governance.** The Murdoch Family Trust beneficially owned ~40.6% of Class B as of June 30, 2025. Class A votes only in limited circumstances. The by-laws waive fiduciary liability for directing corporate opportunities to FOX, whose Executive Chair and CEO is News Corp's Chair. Anti-takeover provisions include a 65% supermajority to amend by-laws and blank-check preferred authority. The 10-K notes that buybacks mechanically increase the family's Class B percentage; the 44% cap is what constrains it.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.4% against 26.6% one-year realized is modestly rich (a 5.9pp spread) and sits at only the 25th percentile within the current chain; implied volatility collapsed from 43.3% on July 19 to 32.4% now, a standard post-earnings crush. The term structure is flat (32.4% near, 33.5% at six months), so no dated catalyst is being priced. The dataset flags a 17.6% put/call skew as elevated put demand, but put/call volume of 0.11 and put/call open interest of 0.03 across just 24 contracts in four expirations describe a market with almost no put positioning at all. I treat the skew reading as low-signal noise in a thin chain rather than as a bearish tell, and the flat term structure as consistent with the fundamental view that the consequential risks (the Australian effective date in July 2027, the OPIS inquiry timeline) resolve well beyond the listed tenors. Short interest of 3.4% of float and 1.9 days to cover confirms an uncrowded, unstressed situation with no squeeze mechanics in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $40 (+36%)

Dow Jones compounds: the PIB reaccelerates from Q4's +11% R&C / +4% Energy back toward the full-year 16%/8%, and segment EBITDA advances from $663M toward the (unverified) $1B FY30 aspiration, which would justify a materially higher multiple on 28% of revenue and 41% of segment EBITDA. Australian listing volumes prove resilient through the July 2027 effective date, REA re-rates from a reform-driven trough, and the sum-of-the-parts discount narrows as the stub's Dow Jones anchor is recognized. The AI licensing line is eventually quantified and proves larger than assumed. Management deploys the remaining $851M+ authorization, and buyback plus REA's own repurchases (which raise News Corp's proportionate ownership without News Corp spending cash) compound per-share economics at 3%+ annually. On $1.60 of FY28-29 adjusted EPS at 25x, $40 is reachable.

### Bear Case — $23 (-22%)

Australian listings prove to have been pulled forward, and FY28 REA volumes contract into the reform's effective date while the AUD gives back its FY26 gain, compressing 45% of segment EBITDA and the segment that carries the highest multiple. The tabloid funnel decline (-25% at The Sun) proves to be the leading edge of subscription weakness rather than a free-reach-only phenomenon, and News Media EBITDA falls from $139M toward zero. Move's ARPU-over-audience strategy stalls as agents refuse further price increases on a shrinking funnel, or NAR relations deteriorate. The OPIS inquiry produces a benchmark-integrity finding that damages Dow Jones Energy. On $1.15 of FY28 adjusted EPS at 20x, $23, roughly the February 2026 low of $22.20, is the floor the market has already tested once.

### Base Case — $33 (+12%)

Adjusted revenue growth of 3-5% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 8-10% (below FY26's 12%, reflecting PIB deceleration and no FX tailwind), FY27 adjusted EPS near consensus $1.29 and FY28 near $1.47, roughly 2-3% annual share-count reduction, and no re-rating. 22.5x FY28 $1.47 = $33.1; equivalently 25.5x FY27 $1.29 = $32.9. This sits below the $36.68 analyst mean because I assume no multiple expansion and no quantification of the licensing line.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the risk cluster and a 2.0–2.5 PEG do not justify new capital at $29.48.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
1. **Price at or below $25.50**, approximately 19.8x FY27 consensus $1.29 and 17.3x FY28 $1.47, back inside the range where management itself repurchased $641M of stock ($24.12–$26.41 monthly Class A averages in Q3 FY26). At that price the base case offers ~29% upside against ~10% downside to the bear case.
2. **Australian listing volumes hold through the first two quarters after the July 1, 2027 effective date**, specifically REA quarterly national buy listing volumes flat or better year over year, which would falsify the pull-forward hypothesis and de-risk 45% of segment EBITDA.
3. **Quantified AI licensing disclosure**, a separately disclosed content-licensing revenue line, contract value, or term in the FY2026 10-K or a subsequent filing, sized at or above roughly $150M annually. That would convert an unmodelable asset into a valuation input.
4. **Dow Jones PIB reacceleration**, R&C back above 14% and Energy above 7% growth for two consecutive quarters, reversing the Q4 FY26 deceleration.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. REA Australian residential revenue turning negative in constant currency for two consecutive quarters, or REA guiding to listing-volume contraction.
2. WSJ or Times/Sunday Times digital-only subscriptions turning negative year over year, evidence the funnel decline has crossed from free reach into the paid franchises.
3. Formal charges, a deferred prosecution agreement, or a benchmark-integrity finding arising from the OPIS DOJ inquiry; or a material adverse development in the Move/NAR relationship.
4. Suspension of the repurchase program, or repurchases falling below ~$100M in a fiscal year while leverage rises. The buyback is currently the primary per-share growth mechanism.
5. Failure of the FY26 10-K cash flow statement to substantiate the Q4 FCF step-up as substantially operational rather than working-capital timing.

**Reassessment timeline.** The FY2026 Form 10-K, expected within weeks, is the near-term document; it should update the risk factors for the Australian reform, the audience declines and the Meta arrangement, and resolve the Q4 working-capital question. Q1 FY2027 results in early November 2026 are the next operating checkpoint, with REA's own reporting cycle providing intra-quarter listing data. The July 1, 2027 Australian effective date is the structural event, so FY28 (July 2027–June 2028) is the year the thesis is genuinely tested.

## Conclusion

FY2026 was a good year executed well: 4% adjusted revenue growth translated into 12% adjusted EBITDA growth, 130bp of margin expansion, $811M of free cash flow, and $641M of repurchases at prices 15-20% below today's. The balance sheet is net cash with a covenant that cannot bind and an undrawn $1B revolver, and management demonstrated in FY26 that it will buy stock aggressively when it sees value, which is the most reliable forward-looking evidence available on capital allocation, and the reason management quality and financial health both improved in this review.

What prevents a BUY is the concentration and timing of the risks against a full-ish multiple. Forty-five percent of segment EBITDA sits in a 61.6%-owned subsidiary whose home market just changed its property tax code, with cash partly trapped there and the effective date fourteen months out; two points of FY26's revenue growth and three of EBITDA growth were currency; the free-reach audience is contracting at 14-25% annually across the tabloid properties; and the AI licensing offset that management leads with is nowhere quantified in any filing. At 22.9x FY27 and 20.1x FY28 consensus with 9-14% expected growth, the shares already price competent execution. The sum-of-the-parts discount is real and the stub is cheap, but that has been true through multiple cycles and the dual-class structure supplies no mechanism to close it.

Hold the position for the buyback, the net-cash optionality and the Dow Jones professional-information franchise.
