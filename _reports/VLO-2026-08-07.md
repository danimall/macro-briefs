## Executive Summary

Valero produced the best quarter in its history. Q2 2026 net income attributable to shareholders was $3,720M, or $12.62 per diluted share, versus $714M and $2.28 a year earlier. H1 2026 EPS was $16.78 against $0.37. The mechanism is disclosed in the 10-Q: USGC ultra-low-sulfur diesel less Brent (RVO-adjusted) averaged $43.52/bbl versus $14.79, West Coast CARB diesel less Brent $56.11 versus $20.22, and refining segment margin rose $3,058M year over year with roughly $2,900M of that attributable to distillate alone. This is a supply shock — refining and export infrastructure damage in the Middle East and Russia, plus permanent domestic closures — rather than a demand boom. Peer results confirm it is sector-wide: Marathon Petroleum $5.1B, Chevron $12.1B, PBF likewise a windfall.

The relevant development for an owner is what happened to the multiple. Consensus FY2026 EPS has moved from $27.95 (90 days ago) to $41.66, and FY2027 from $18.65 to $27.72 — revisions of +49% and +49% respectively, with +36% and +29% coming in the last 30 days. Over the same window the stock declined 3.7% from its July 19 level of $309.65. VLO now trades at 7.3x current-year and 10.9x next-year consensus, against a peer median trailing P/E of 17.0 and its own trailing 12.5x (TTM EPS of $24.24 from four consecutive beats). Prior appreciation is not a valuation argument in either direction; what matters is that the price/earnings relationship has become less demanding, not more, since the last review.

That compression is the reason I am nudging the score up from 5.5 rather than continuing the decline, and it is not sufficient to change the rating. Consensus models FY2027 EPS falling 33% from FY2026, so the sell-side agrees these are peak earnings; at 10.9x that already-reduced number the stock is priced near a 2023-type year (FY2023 net income recast to today's 288M share count is roughly $30.68 per share, or ~9.9x). Analyst mean target $309.79 sits 2.2% above spot. My base case of $312 offers +3.0%; the bear case, in which cracks normalize toward something between 2023 and the 2024-25 trough, is $205, or -32%. There is no margin of safety at this price, and the identifiable near-term catalysts — two California bills before the Assembly Appropriations Committee on August 13, existing but dormant California Energy Commission authority to cap refinery margins, and federal windfall-profit bills — cut against the holder rather than for. HOLD, fifth consecutive: the outcome depends on where mid-cycle cracks settle, and the honest range is $205 to $385.

## Company Overview

Valero operates 15 refineries in the U.S., Canada and the U.K. with combined throughput capacity of ~3,190 kbd (~2,700 kbd crude), of which 170 kbd at Benicia was fully idled by April 2026, leaving ~3,020 kbd. Ten refineries have waterborne access; the Gulf Coast cluster (Port Arthur 435 kbd, Corpus Christi 370 kbd, St. Charles 340 kbd, Texas City 260 kbd, Houston 255 kbd, Meraux 135 kbd, Three Rivers 100 kbd) is the export engine into Latin America and global markets. Several plants (Port Arthur, St. Charles, Corpus Christi East, Wilmington) process heavy or sour crude, which monetizes wide differentials; Brent-less-WCS-Houston widened to $13.92/bbl in Q2 2026 from $6.25.

Beyond refining, Valero consolidates Diamond Green Diesel, a 50/50 joint venture with Darling Ingredients (~1.2 billion gal/yr renewable diesel, ~50 million gal/yr renewable naphtha, with Port Arthur able to upgrade ~50% of its 470 million gal to neat SAF), and owns 12 ethanol dry mills at ~1.7 billion gal/yr. Cumulative low-carbon capital through 12/31/2025 was $6.0B. Marketing is wholesale rack and bulk; the ~7,000 branded outlets are independently owned and supplied under contract, so Valero carries no retail operating exposure.

A structural feature to hold in mind: Valero is simultaneously the largest obligated party and one of the larger credit generators under renewable-fuel programs. Refining incurs RFS/LCFS/Canada CFR/U.K. RTFO obligations; Renewable Diesel and Ethanol generate credits. The hedge is partial and lands in different segments, and half of DGD's economics belong to Darling.

Operating income fell 80% from 2022 to 2025 while revenue fell 30%. That ratio describes the business model: a crack-spread manufacturer with roughly fixed conversion costs.

**Segment composition, H1 2026:** Refining $6,276M (83% of segment operating income), Renewable Diesel $856M (11%), Ethanol $408M (5%), with corporate −$552M and intersegment −$61M bridging to $6,927M consolidated. Two qualifiers materially reduce the quality of the non-refining contribution. Renewable Diesel swung from −$220M to +$856M, but $412M of H1 net income was attributed to noncontrolling interests (versus a −$108M loss allocation a year earlier), so roughly half of DGD accrues to Darling. And Ethanol's $408M includes $119M of clean fuel production credits newly available because the One Big Beautiful Bill Act excluded indirect land-use-change emissions from the 45Z calculation, of which $99M landed in Q2 including a Q1 catch-up. That is a policy step-change recognized in one quarter, not a volume or price improvement, and it will not repeat at that magnitude.

**Operating performance was genuinely good.** Q2 throughput was 2,950 kbd versus 2,922 kbd a year earlier — higher, despite Benicia being fully idled and Port Arthur (435 kbd, 13.6% of system capacity) being shut from March 23 following a distillate hydrotreater fire and only reaching normal rates during the quarter. Against post-Benicia capacity of 3,020 kbd, that is ~97.7% utilization. The caveat is that "throughput capacity" per the 10-K includes intermediates and purchased feedstocks, so this is not a crude-only utilization rate comparable to peer disclosures. Directionally, the non-California system ran hard.

**Cash flow, with a correction.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $6,970M against $1,888M. Less total capital investments of $798M, free cash flow was $6,172M. This half-year figure should not be annualized as a forecast. Working capital contributed +$403M net, but the gross swings were $4,037M of receivable build against $4,347M of payable build, both driven by June 2026 prices far above December 2025 levels, and that reverses if prices mean-revert. Deferred turnaround and catalyst spend ex-VIE was $374M in H1 2026 versus $621M in H1 2025, and management guides ~$2.0B of total 2026 capital investments (including ~$250M tied to Port Arthur), implying roughly $1.2B in H2, a ~50% step-up. Cash taxes paid were $830M versus $283M with income taxes payable up $421M, so the catch-up is coming. Q2 also carried a $44M LIFO liquidation benefit and a $51M DGD tariff-refund receivable.

Separately, standard screens showing FY2025 free cash flow near $5,107M (OCF $5,826M less $719M "capex") understate maintenance capital. The $719M tag captures only "capital expenditures excluding VIEs"; Valero reports capital spending across five lines including deferred turnaround and catalyst costs, which are mandatory recurring maintenance at a refiner. Using H1 2025 actual total capital investments of $1,067M and FY2026 guidance of ~$2.0B, FY2025 total capital investment was plausibly $1.7-2.1B, putting true FY2025 FCF near $3.7-4.1B rather than $5.1B. This is a derived estimate; the FY2025 full-year cash flow statement was not read line by line, but the direction is robust.

**Balance sheet (6/30/2026):** cash $7,874M, total liquidity $12,729M. Debt and finance leases $11,349M ($927M current), of which ~$2,248M is finance leases. Net debt including finance leases $3,475M; excluding them, $1,227M. Debt to total capitalization 28.6% (31.2% against Valero-only equity of $25,001M). Weighted average rate 5.0%; H1 interest and debt expense net of capitalized interest only $285M, giving ~24x coverage on H1 operating income. Maturities are benign: $672M remainder of 2026, $564M in 2027, $1,047M in 2028. Creditors of the consolidated VIEs (DGD, Central Mexico Terminals) have no recourse to Valero's other assets, and DGD's $603M of non-current debt on the consolidated balance sheet is not Valero recourse debt.

Two adjustments to headline liquidity: $4.8B of cash sits at foreign subsidiaries, repatriable via dividends without U.S. federal income tax but carrying withholding, state and FX-gain costs; and the LIFO reserve (replacement cost above LIFO carrying value) grew to $7.5B at 6/30/2026 from $2.6B at year-end, an off-balance-sheet economic asset of roughly $26/share pre-tax that is itself a function of high prices and shrinks when they fall.

**Capital returns.** H1 2026 returned $3,550M ($2,836M buybacks, $714M dividends), 51% of OCF. Q2 repurchases were 9,011,171 shares at an average $251.08, 17% below today's price. Shares outstanding fell from 298.94M at 12/31/2025 to 287.93M, a 3.7% reduction in six months; weighted-average diluted shares have fallen 25.8% since FY2022 (396M to 294M). Dilution is negligible: the 10-Q reports zero effect from dilutive securities and no convertibles, warrants or earn-outs. The dividend rose 6.2% to $1.20/quarter (annualized $4.80, 1.59% yield), costing ~$1.4B against $6,970M of H1 OCF.

The Board added $5.0B of authorization on July 16, bringing available capacity to $6,422M. At $302.99 that is ~21.2M shares, 7.4% of outstanding; full execution would take the count to ~266.7M and lift each continuing holder's proportional ownership by ~8.0%. Authorization is capacity, not commitment — the 10-Q says so explicitly.

**Data conflict noted:** the profile field reports EPS of $7.57 alongside a P/E of 12.66, which is internally inconsistent at $302.99. The P/E is consistent with TTM EPS of ~$23.87, which reconciles to the four reported quarters ($3.66 + $3.82 + $4.22 + $12.54 = $24.24). I use the TTM series. The valuation snapshot also flags an official-close/history market-date mismatch; both prices are $302.99, so the arithmetic is unaffected.

## Growth Analysis

Refining is not a volume-growth business, and Valero's capacity is shrinking, not growing: Benicia's 170 kbd is gone and Wilmington's 135 kbd is under unresolved "strategic alternatives" review. There is no meaningful greenfield addition in the plan; 2026 capital investment of ~$2.0B against a $93.8B enterprise value is maintenance-plus.

Consensus reflects this honestly. FY2026 at $41.66 is followed by FY2027 at $27.72, a modeled 33% decline. The estimate revision magnitudes are extraordinary (+49% over 90 days for both years, +36%/+29% over 30 days) and formally satisfy the pattern that usually signals continued outperformance, but that pattern is calibrated to stocks where depressed earnings are recovering on durable demand. Here the revisions are tracking a war premium in product cracks, and consensus is already revising *down* the out-year even as it revises *up* the current year. Rising near-term estimates on a mean-reverting spread are not the same signal as rising estimates on a structural demand shift.

Where growth does exist:

- **Per-share compounding through repurchase.** A 25.8% reduction in share count in under four years converts cyclical cash into durable per-share value. With $6.42B authorized and 28.6% debt-to-cap, this is fundable from operations. The caveat is timing: $612M of buybacks in H1 2025 (trough) versus $2,836M in H1 2026 (peak) is procyclical, the opposite of value-optimal.
- **Renewable Diesel.** Sales volumes rose to 3,432 kgal/day from 2,584, biodiesel RINs averaged $2.12 in Q2 versus $1.09, and EPA's 2026-27 RVO increases support demand. The binding constraint is now the OBBB's requirement that post-2026 production use exclusively U.S., Mexican or Canadian feedstock to qualify for 45Z (capped at $1.00/gal), a hard sourcing limit on a business partly built on imported used cooking oil and tallow.
- **Ethanol carbon intensity.** A standalone CCS agreement for the Linden, Indiana plant was signed in 2025 with more under evaluation; Section 45Q plus lower CI scores are the lever. This is small relative to Refining.
- **Latin American rack marketing** via Mexico and Peru terminals, framed in the 10-K as the growth channel. Not quantified.

Score of 4.5 reflects the absence of volume growth, a consensus that models earnings falling a third next year, and per-share growth that depends on discretionary repurchase pacing.

That average is arguably too low for the current asset configuration, because the 2024-25 trough contained a $1.1B impairment, a DGD loss driven by IEEPA tariffs the Supreme Court invalidated in February 2026, and 45Z transition dislocation; and because global refining capacity is now materially lower (Benicia, Middle East and Russian damage, plus PBF's Martinez outage from February 2025 to May 2026). It is also arguably too high because 2022 was a once-in-a-decade year. Call normalized EPS $24-28 on today's share count, and refiners historically clear 9-12x mid-cycle. Analyst mean target is $309.79 (+2.2%), median $318 (+5.0%), high $365, low $190. On a stock trading at 10.9x next-year consensus with rising estimates, analyst targets would normally be treated as lagging floors, but the condition for that treatment is that the improving estimates be durable. Here consensus is voluntarily marking down the out-year, which is the opposite of a stale-target setup.

Valuation score 5.5: the multiple is not stretched, and the compression since July is real and favorable, but the cheapness is a function of peak earnings that the company's own outlook attributes to geopolitical supply loss with named mitigants. Cheap-on-peak is the pattern that most often disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's Q2 outperformance versus peers is better explained by configuration than by any widening moat: distillate-heavy yield into the largest distillate crack dislocation on record, Gulf Coast waterborne export access, and heavy/sour capability into a $13.92/bbl WCS-Houston differential. That configuration is an asset, and it is also replicable by MPC and PSX to a substantial degree.

The most striking dynamic in the file is California. Valero decided in March 2025 to exit Benicia, took a $1.1B impairment across Benicia and Wilmington, recognized a $337M asset retirement obligation ($170M settled in H1 2026), and fully idled the plant by April 2026. That closure, together with others, helped produce the tightest California product market in years: Argus reported multiyear-high state gasoline and jet prices on surging import dependence, CARB diesel less Brent hit $56.11/bbl versus $20.22, and CARBOB 87 less Brent $46.68 versus $36.98. Valero retained the Northern California customer relationships and will serve them "through imports or other alternative supply arrangements," converting refining exposure into a supply-and-trading position while its remaining 135 kbd at Wilmington earns the scarcity premium. This is a coincidence of timing rather than evidence of foresight, since the decision preceded the 2026 disruption by a year, and it is symmetric in risk, since Wilmington's written-down carrying value means the upside compresses quickly if California legislates.

Working against the competitive position: renewable-fuel compliance cost. The cost of meeting credit obligations across RFS, California LCFS, Canada CFR and U.K. RTFO was $1.4B in H1 2026 versus $740M in H1 2025 (+89%), with market RVO cost per barrel at $11.60 versus $5.45 and $13.78 in Q2 versus $6.14. Reuters reported record credit levels in May on stronger EPA mandates; Bloomberg in July pointed to higher RIN prices into the March 2027 deadline. This is partially self-offsetting — high RINs are revenue in Renewable Diesel and Ethanol — but the offset is structurally smaller than the ~2.9 million BPD obligated volume, and half of DGD's benefit is Darling's. The reported crack spreads in the reference table are RVO-adjusted, so the cost is not purely additive to those margins; the net P&L effect cannot be decomposed from the disclosures.

Trajectory read: shrinking capacity in a structurally tighter market, strong reliability, an unresolved Wilmington question, and a compliance cost line running at double last year's rate. Roughly stable, not improving. Score 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin cyclicality is the dominant risk and is partially priced.** The 10-Q's own outlook attributes the environment to geopolitical developments and unplanned outages at Middle East and Russian refineries and export infrastructure, and lists mitigants explicitly: alternative routes partially bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, coordinated SPR releases, and increased production elsewhere. Management also notes "demand growth has moderated." The demonstrated downside of this asset base is $3.8B and $3.2B of operating income in 2024 and 2025. Consensus at $27.72 for FY2027 already embeds a 33% decline, which is why I treat this as substantially, not fully, reflected.

An asymmetry: refiners are not crude producers. If Hormuz transit normalizes and crude falls while damaged refining capacity stays offline, cracks can widen. The July 8 commentary framing profits as "robust fuel demand and weak crude prices" following the reopening captures this. The durable risk is refining capacity restoration and demand destruction, both slower-moving than a shipping-lane headline.

**Political intervention is the clearest dated downside catalyst.** California Sens. Becker and Allen have introduced a bill adding war to the emergencies triggering the state's price-gouging law (10% cap on price increases) with AG prosecution authority; Sen. Stern has introduced a bill easing sale of conventional non-CARB gasoline with a provider fee. Both faced the Assembly Appropriations Committee on August 13, 2026. A 2022 law already authorizes the California Energy Commission to cap refinery profit margins; the CEC has de-prioritized that authority but "continues to analyze the data." Federal windfall-profit bills have been introduced by Sen. Schiff and Rep. Sherman. Valero's own forward-looking factors name "actions implemented under SBx 1-2" and "windfall, margin, or other taxes or penalties." The Stern bill is the underappreciated one: relaxing the CARB blend requirement would compress the West Coast premium that produced the $56.11/bbl CARB diesel crack. Bounding the exposure: no measure is reported as enacted; the same reporting notes legislators are "skittish about regulating the facilities before electric vehicles have displaced the demand for gas"; and California is under 10% of throughput capacity today, ~4% at Wilmington alone.

**Single-asset operational risk, demonstrated.** The March 23 Port Arthur fire forced a full shutdown of the largest refinery in the system during the best margin quarter in company history. Valero recorded a $78M insurance receivable (no cash received at 6/30), $15M of repair costs, and attributes ~$250M of 2026 capex to the incident. It has received multiple lawsuits including a proposed class action alleging personal injury, property damage and nuisance, several seeking damages above $1M, with no formal regulatory enforcement commenced as of filing and no estimable loss. The undisclosed opportunity cost of the outage could be large.

**Unresolved items an owner should not assume away:** Wilmington's disposition (no decision, buyer or timeline disclosed); further IEEPA tariff refunds beyond DGD's accepted $51M Phase 1 claim (the 10-Q recognizes nothing further); and a single-source report attributing profit growth to Venezuelan crude supply, which the 10-Q does not mention and which should be treated as unconfirmed.

**Insider and governance.** No open-market insider purchases appear in the February-June 2026 Form 4 record. The only repeated selling is by SVP Eric Fisher — roughly 31,200 shares for ~$7.7M gross, including equal 7,500-share tranches on 5/18, 6/18 and 6/29, the signature of a pre-scheduled program, and the 10-Q confirms no 10b5-1 plan was adopted or terminated during Q2. One executive's diversification against record results is weak evidence and I draw no valuation inference from it. CEO Riggs's FY2025 total compensation was $34.2M, up 52.7%, in a year GAAP net income fell from $2,770M to $2,348M. The reconciliation is internally consistent with plan design — adjusted EPS rose from $8.48 to $10.61 (helped by excluding the $1.1B impairment and by a 4% lower share count) and relative TSR beat the peer group 346.01 to 251.49 — and grant-date fair value inflates mechanically in a rising market. Excluding an impairment on a strategic decision management itself made is standard practice and a fair governance question. Also unreconciled: the proxy's pay-versus-performance table reports FY2025 net income of $2,246M against XBRL's $2,348M, a ~$102M gap I cannot explain and do not rely on.

The most informative capital-allocation signal is the company's own: 9.01M shares bought in Q2 at $251.08, and the Board more than tripling remaining authorization on July 16. Set against procyclical historical pacing, that supports a 5.5 on management quality: competent operators with shareholder-friendly returns and imperfect repurchase timing.

Net read: elevated but two-sided uncertainty, no informed directional positioning I can identify, and rich implied vol argues against paying for optionality here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($385, +27%).** Global refining capacity has been structurally reduced — Middle East and Russian facility damage takes quarters to years to repair, and Benicia and other closures are permanent. Product inventories remain low, as the 10-Q's own outlook expects. If crude prices ease while product supply stays short, cracks can hold or widen. In that world FY2027 comes in above the $27.72 consensus, at $30-32, and a 12x multiple on $32 is $384. Valero is configured for exactly this — distillate-weighted, waterborne, heavy-sour-capable — and executes $6.42B of repurchase (7.4% of shares) from operating cash flow while carrying net debt of only $3.5B. The $7.5B LIFO reserve is unrecognized economic value. Estimate revisions of +49% over 90 days, four consecutive beats averaging 22% surprise, and a 12.5x trailing multiple against a 17.0x peer median support the case that consensus still lags.

**Bear case ($205, -32%).** Q2 was a supply-shock quarter with named mitigants already in motion, and H1 results additionally carried a +$403M working-capital tailwind, abnormally light turnaround spend ($374M versus $621M), a $44M LIFO benefit, a $51M DGD tariff refund and a $119M ethanol 45Z catch-up including a Q1 true-up. Cash taxes and H2 turnaround spend both step up. Renewable-fuel compliance cost is running at double last year's rate with RIN prices expected higher into March 2027. If cracks revert toward the midpoint of 2023 and 2024-25 — say $14-16 of EPS — even a generous 14x puts the stock near $205-225. Political intervention is a live, dated risk with the California hearing on August 13, and the Stern bill would compress precisely the West Coast premium that is currently the best margin in the system. Half of the Renewable Diesel upside belongs to Darling. The buyback is authorization, not obligation, and history says pacing slows exactly when the stock is cheapest.

**Base case ($312, +3%).** Cracks normalize partially but not fully as capacity restoration lags demand. FY2027 lands near consensus $27.72; an 11.3x multiple gives $313. Cross-check: 7.5x FY2026 consensus $41.66 gives $312. Analyst mean $309.79 and median $318 bracket it. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear yields roughly +2.6% price return plus a 1.59% dividend — approximately fair, with unattractive dispersion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing owners should maintain exposure; new capital has no entry signal at $302.99.

- **Base case target: $312** (+3.0%). 11.3x FY2027 consensus EPS of $27.72, cross-checked at 7.5x FY2026 consensus $41.66.
- **Bull case target: $385** (+27.1%). 12x an upside FY2027 of $32, assuming structurally reduced refining capacity holds cracks above pre-war levels.
- **Bear case target: $205** (-32.3%). 14x a normalized $14.60, or 9.5x a $21.50 mid-cycle — the range where cracks revert toward the 2023/2024-25 midpoint.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the near-term decision points being the August 13 California Assembly Appropriations hearing, the Q3 print in late October (consensus $14.95, revised +53% in 30 days — a high bar), and the March 2027 RFS compliance deadline.
- **Dividend:** $4.80 annualized, 1.59% yield, covered ~5x by H1 operating cash flow alone.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price below $250 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $27 — that is under 9.3x next-year, at which point the bear case is largely discounted and the buyback becomes accretive at scale.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which distillate cracks hold above $30/bbl USGC ULSD-less-Brent *while* Brent trades below $80, evidencing that the crack premium is capacity-driven rather than war-driven.
3. A disclosed Wilmington resolution (sale or conversion) at terms that remove California regulatory exposure without forfeiting the marketing position, combined with FY2027 consensus stability.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Enactment of a California margin cap or CEC activation of its SBx 1-2 authority, or passage of the Stern non-CARB blend bill — either compresses the West Coast premium that generated $56.11/bbl CARB diesel cracks.
2. FY2027 consensus revised below $22 (a 21% cut from $27.72), which would push the stock above 13.8x a declining out-year.
3. Refining segment margin per barrel declining two consecutive quarters while total capital investment steps up toward the guided H2 run rate of ~$1.2B, with repurchase pacing falling below $500M per quarter — the combination that historically marks the transition from peak to trough.
4. Any material adverse development in the Port Arthur class action or a regulatory enforcement action arising from the March 23 fire.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 print in late October, which will test whether the +53% 30-day revision to $14.95 is achievable and whether H2 turnaround spend and cash-tax catch-up compress free cash flow as the filings imply.

## Conclusion

Valero is a well-run, financially conservative refiner earning extraordinary money from a supply shock it did not create and cannot control. The balance sheet is the strongest element of the story: $3.5B of net debt against $7.0B of half-year operating cash flow, 24x interest coverage, 28.6% debt-to-capitalization, $12.7B of liquidity and a maturity ladder with nothing threatening. The capital-return machinery works: a 25.8% reduction in share count since 2022, a 6.2% dividend increase, and $6.42B of authorization equal to 7.4% of shares.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade is that the cheap-looking multiple is arithmetic on peak earnings. Consensus has raised FY2026 by 49% in 90 days and simultaneously carries FY2027 at 33% below FY2026; the sell-side is telling you these are not run-rate earnings. At 10.9x that already-marked-down out-year, and roughly 10x a 2023-type year recast to today's share count, the price sits close to the middle of a plausible mid-cycle band rather than at its low end. Base case upside of 3% against bear case downside of 32% is not a risk-adjusted proposition worth new capital, particularly with a dated political catalyst on August 13 and renewable-fuel compliance costs running at double last year's rate.

The score moves up marginally from 5.5 to 5.65, reversing a five-month decline, because the forward multiple compressed materially while the stock traded flat-to-down — improving risk/reward on fundamentals that got better, not worse. That improvement is real and insufficient. I would become a buyer below $250 with FY2027 consensus intact, or on evidence that distillate cracks hold above $30/bbl with Brent under $80, which would establish the crack premium as a capacity phenomenon rather than a war premium. Until then, own it if you own it, collect the 1.59% and the share-count reduction, and do not chase.
