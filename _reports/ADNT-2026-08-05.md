## Executive Summary

Adient is executing well on the variable it fully controls — winning content and share — and losing on the variable that determines equity value, which is the price at which it wins that content. Fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended 3/31/2026, the most recent quarter verified against a filing) showed revenue up 7.0% to $3,865M against global light-vehicle production down 0.9%, while adjusted EBITDA fell 4.3% to $223M and consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin compressed 68 basis points to 5.77%. The August 5 fiscal Q3 print repeated the pattern: revenue of $3.93B beat a $3.7B consensus, adjusted EPS of $0.48 missed a $0.53 consensus, and full-year revenue guidance was raised to approximately $15.0 billion. Against the February adjusted EBITDA guidance of roughly $880M, a $15.0B revenue year implies a 5.87% margin versus 6.06% in FY2025.

The mechanism is specific and disclosed rather than inferred. Asia generated $440M of adjusted EBITDA on $2,983M of revenue in FY2025 — 20.5% of sales and 45.6% of pre-corporate segment EBITDA — at a 14.8% margin. In Q2 FY2026 that margin was 12.5%, down from 15.6%, on unfavorable customer pricing, higher launch and overhead costs, and $7M of lower equity income. The FY2025 10-K states directly that management "expects modest margin declines as Adient continues to win new business with local OEMs in China." Meanwhile, revenue mix is shifting toward the Americas at 5.8% margin (48.4% of segment sales versus 46.7%) and away from Asia (18.9% versus 19.4%), so consolidated margin faces both a rate and a mix headwind. If Asia settles at 12.5% for a full year on roughly $3.1B of revenue, the EBITDA impact is about $71M, or 8% of consolidated adjusted EBITDA; the offsetting Americas improvement of 30bp on ~$7.5B recovers under a third of that.

The balance sheet is more resilient than the equity market's pricing implies. Net debt of $1,557M against LTM adjusted EBITDA of $882M is 1.77x; cash was $831M at 3/31/2026 against total liquidity above $1.5B; the maturity ladder shows $6M due in FY2026, $512M in FY2027-28 and $1,891M in FY2031 and beyond. That combination argues against near-term solvency risk. What it does not do is generate shareholder cash: FY2025 free cash flow of $204M was reduced by $77M of noncontrolling-interest dividends in the first half of FY2026 alone, capex is rising ($138M in 1H FY2026 versus $109M), management has guided FY2026 cash flow below FY2025, and the first half's reported $161M of operating cash flow included roughly $90M of commercial and derivative transactions expected to settle in Q3. We rate ADNT HOLD with an overall score of 4.6, a marginal decline from 4.8 in June, reflecting the second EPS miss in four quarters, the July CFO departure notice, and continued Asia margin erosion.

## Company Overview

Adient is a pure-play global automotive seating supplier, spun out of Johnson Controls in 2016 and domiciled in Ireland. The FY2025 10-K describes roughly 200 wholly- and majority-owned manufacturing, assembly or sequencing facilities across 29 countries with more than 65,000 employees, plus partially owned affiliates. Product scope spans complete seat systems and the underlying components: frames, mechanisms, foam, head restraints, armrests and trim covers.

Vertical integration is the stated moat, and it is a genuine one in a specific sense. The 10-K identifies three OEM sourcing patterns: common core structures, component sourcing where the seat is unbundled and each piece awarded separately, and engineering in-sourcing where the OEM designs and the supplier only builds. Adient can bid into any of the three; a component-only or assembly-only competitor cannot. That preserves revenue when OEMs change sourcing behavior. It does not preserve margin, because unbundling removes the systems-integration premium that historically justified the assembler's position.

Reporting is by geography: Americas, EMEA and Asia, with a corporate cost center. The customer list covers essentially every global OEM plus Chinese entrants NIO, XPeng, BYD and Xiaomi Motors. Adient runs 37 manufacturing locations in 22 Chinese cities and holds six joint ventures with OEM partners including GAC, BAIC and FAW.

One structural feature defines the business model and should discipline how forward estimates are used: per the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, customer programs can be cancelled at any time without cause and contain no firm volume or price commitments, and Adient elects the practical expedient that exempts contracts of one year or less from remaining-performance-obligation disclosure. There is no disclosed contractual backlog in the filings. Any externally circulated "booked business" figure is an estimate of expected awards.

Operating cash flow has fallen 33% in two years while adjusted EBITDA fell 6%. Management attributes part of FY2025's $449M to acceleration of commercial settlements into that year and guides FY2026 cash flow lower on reduced profitability, lower volumes, higher growth capex and non-recurring tax settlements. First-half FY2026 operating cash flow of $161M (versus $64M) includes roughly $90M of commercial and derivative transactions expected to be settled or paid in Q3, so reported 1H free cash flow of $23M normalizes to approximately -$67M on that adjustment. Cash generation is structurally back-half weighted, so neither figure annualizes; a normalized full-year FCF in the $100-200M range is consistent with the guided decline.

Three drags compound. Timing is pulling cash forward rather than generating it. Capex is rising into an AI and automation investment cycle. And a meaningful share of consolidated cash flow belongs to joint-venture partners: noncontrolling-interest dividends were $77M in 1H FY2026, and FY2025 net income attributable to noncontrolling interests was approximately $90M. If annual NCI distributions run near $150M, FY2025's headline $204M of FCF corresponds to roughly $50-60M attributable to Adient shareholders, a 3-4% yield on the current $1,654M market capitalization rather than the 12.5% the headline implies. The magnitude here is a sensitivity, not a measured figure: Chinese JV dividends are declared once or twice annually and $77M in one half may not double.

Derived leverage: LTM adjusted EBITDA through 3/31/2026 of $882M ($881M FY2025 less $429M 1H FY2025 plus $430M 1H FY2026) gives net debt/EBITDA of 1.77x and gross of 2.71x. EBITDA/net financing charges of $193M is 4.6x. These are moderate metrics. The ABL was reduced from $1.25B to $1.0B in October 2025 with maturity extended to October 2030 and amended again in January 2026; $150M was drawn and $152M repaid during the first half, consistent with seasonal usage.

The permanent cost of the February 2025 refinancing is real: $795M of 4.875% notes due 2026 were replaced with $795M of 7.50% notes due 2033, an incremental ~$20.9M of annual interest, partially offset by Term Loan B margin step-downs from 3.25% to 2.25%. Net financing charges have run $189-195M annually against $882M of EBITDA.

Goodwill of $1,798M (Americas $608M, EMEA $0, Asia $1,190M) exceeds equity attributable to Adient of $1,713M. Tangible book value is -$390M, or -$4.97 per share. The March 2025 test found Americas and Asia fair values exceeded carrying value by only modestly more than 10%.

### Taxes

FY2025 recorded $103M of tax expense on an $88M pretax loss. First-half FY2026 recorded $74M on $117M of pretax income, a 63% effective rate, with Q2 alone at 42%. The cause is structural: valuation allowances in eight jurisdictions (Germany, Hungary, Luxembourg, Mexico, Poland, Spain, UK, US) mean losses there generate no benefit while profitable Asia pays full rates. Gross unrecognized tax benefits were $335M at 3/31/2026, of which $105M would affect the effective rate. Both the 10-K and 10-Q disclose a reasonable possibility of releasing some valuation allowances within twelve months, with a material benefit. That is genuine, disclosed EPS optionality and the largest single lever on reported earnings, but it is non-cash, requires sustained profitability in the weakest jurisdictions, and such language has recurred without action.

## Growth Analysis

Adient is outgrowing its end market, and this is the strongest element of the bull case. Per S&P Global data cited in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, Q2 global light-vehicle production fell 0.9% year over year, with North America -2.6%, China -5.8%, EMEA flat and Asia ex-China +5.3%. Against that, Adient's revenue rose 7.0%, Americas rose 11%, and China-sourced revenue rose from $621M to $757M (+22%) over the six-month period. Americas outgrowth of roughly 13.6 points versus regional production cannot be explained by currency alone; management framed it as onshoring momentum, and the geographic split (US $1,731M, Mexico $694M) is consistent with production relocating within USMCA.

Fiscal 2026 revenue guidance of approximately $15.0B implies 3.2% growth over FY2025's $14,535M. Derived from reported figures, nine-month revenue is $11,439M, implying Q4 of roughly $3,561M — a 9.4% sequential decline consistent with the 10-K's seasonality disclosure covering July-September model-changeover shutdowns.

Consensus EPS estimates are rising modestly: current year $2.16 (from $2.10 ninety days ago, +2.9%) and next year $3.28 (from $3.16, +3.8%). The current-quarter estimate was cut 6.3% over ninety days before recovering 5.4% in the last thirty. These are not large revisions; the +30% ninety-day threshold that signals a systematic underestimate of an earnings turn is nowhere in evidence. The FY2027 step from $2.16 to $3.28 (+52%) requires a combination of a lower effective tax rate, continued share-count reduction and operating leverage that the flat first-half EBITDA does not yet support.

The growth that matters for equity value is EBITDA, and it has not grown. Adjusted EBITDA was $938M in FY2023 and $881M in FY2025 on a revenue base that fell 5.6%. Holding absolute EBITDA flat through a declining top line is a creditable cost performance; converting 3% revenue growth into flat EBITDA in FY2026 is the same performance producing no shareholder value.

Restructuring returns are also deteriorating in a way that matters for the growth thesis. Disclosed net savings rates: 2023 Plan ~15%, 2024 Plan ~60%, 2025 Plan ~45%. The 2026 Plan recorded $28M of charges in the first half for roughly $15M of expected annual cost reduction, with the 10-Q stating that minimal impact to net earnings is expected. Adient has restructured in each of the last seven fiscal years, the reserve stood at $129M at 3/31/2026, and $31M of cash was used in the half. Because adjusted EBITDA excludes these charges, the gap between adjusted EBITDA and cash earnings is persistent.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $21.10 basis:

- Market capitalization: 78.413M shares × $21.10 = **$1,654M**
- Enterprise value excluding NCI: $1,654M + $1,557M = $3,211M → **3.64x** LTM adjusted EBITDA of $882M
- Enterprise value including NCI ($304M) and redeemable NCI ($71M): $3,586M → **4.07x**
- Price/book: $21.10 ÷ $21.85 = **0.97x**; price/tangible book is not meaningful (negative)
- P/E on current-year consensus of $2.16: **9.8x**; on next-year $3.28: **6.4x**
- Headline FY2025 FCF yield: 12.3%; estimated attributable yield after NCI distributions: **3-4%**

The NCI-inclusive 4.07x is the internally consistent multiple, because the $882M of adjusted EBITDA includes joint-venture-partner economics. The profile field showing a 29.1x forward P/E is inconsistent with the $2.16 consensus and appears to be a data artifact; consensus-based multiples above are the usable figures.

Peer comparables in automotive parts show wide dispersion and limited read-across: Visteon at 20.1x, Standard Motor Products at 10.5x, XPEL at 24.1x, Gentherm at 49.2x, Fox Factory negative. Peer median P/E of 24.1x versus Adient's 9.8x looks like a large discount, but the peer set contains no comparable low-margin seating assembler, and the appropriate anchor is EV/EBITDA against Tier-1 seating economics, where roughly 4x is mid-pack rather than a deep-value outlier.

The discount is explained rather than mispriced, and the explanations are specific: consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin under 6% leaves almost no cushion for a volume shock; roughly $190M of annual interest and a 63% first-half effective tax rate consume most of what EBITDA produces; NCI distributions take a further slice; tangible book is negative; the buyback that drove per-share value creation has paused with $110M of authorization remaining; and management has guided both cash flow and China margin lower.

The one identifiable catalyst that could re-rate the equity without operational improvement is a valuation-allowance release. If the effective tax rate normalized toward 30% and Asia margin stabilized, the $3.28 next-year consensus becomes plausible and 8-9x on that figure supports $26-30. That is the bull path, and it is why the analyst mean of $31.67 sits 50% above spot. But the dispersion in those targets — low $22, high $69, median $29 — indicates that the sell side has no shared view of normalized earnings power.

We score valuation 5.2: cheap on optics, with the cheapness largely accounted for by structural margin repricing, cash leakage and negative tangible book. A low multiple here is not sufficient evidence of mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors are Lear, Toyota Boshoku, Forvia, Magna and Yanfeng. Two structural features work against Adient. First, several competitors are captive or semi-captive — Toyota Boshoku with Toyota, Yanfeng with SAIC — which caps Adient's addressable share at those OEMs. Second, the component-sourcing pattern lets OEMs unbundle the seat and award pieces separately, eroding the integration margin. Adient's vertical integration defends revenue in that scenario better than it defends margin.

The Chinese transition is a volume-for-margin trade being executed in real time. China-sourced revenue rose 22% in the first half while Asia margin fell 160bp. The 10-K describes local Chinese OEMs producing at lower cost and warns of market-share loss for the foreign and luxury OEMs that historically carried Adient's Asia margin, while requiring management to assess "which local OEMs are expected to exist and thrive over the long-term" — an unusual acknowledgment of customer credit risk within a named customer list.

EMEA is being attacked from both directions: Chinese imports competing inside Europe, and declining European exports to China as domestic brands displace European models. EMEA revenue fell from $5,195M in FY2023 to $4,773M in FY2025 with margin halving from 4.5% to 2.6%. Q2 FY2026's 3.5% is an improvement but remains barely above breakeven after corporate allocation, and the goodwill there is already zero.

On trajectory, the assessment is mixed and nets to modestly negative. Volume share is improving (revenue outgrowth of 6-8 points versus production, onshoring wins in the Americas). Margin trajectory is deteriorating (gross margin 7.2% to 6.6%, Asia -310bp, consolidated -68bp). Winning share by conceding price does not widen a moat. Competitive position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Asia margin repricing (highest materiality).** Management-guided, already partially realized, and concentrated in the segment producing 46% of pre-corporate EBITDA on 20% of revenue. A full year at 12.5% costs roughly $71M of EBITDA versus FY2025.

**Concentrated goodwill impairment risk.** Asia goodwill of $1,190M equals 69% of book equity attributable to shareholders, against a fair-value cushion described as modestly above 10% at the March 2025 test date. A second impairment would be non-cash but would take book equity below $1.0B and remove the below-book valuation support that currently anchors the downside. The original trigger was the share price, so an impairment would confirm rather than cause a decline.

**Cash flow quality and NCI leakage.** Guided lower for FY2026; first-half operating cash flow flattered by roughly $90M of timing; capex rising; $77M of NCI dividends in one half.

**Tax.** A 63% first-half effective rate, $335M of gross unrecognized tax benefits, a ~$20M non-recurring foreign audit settlement payment expected in FY2026, and disclosed increasing aggressiveness from tax authorities.

**Tariffs.** FY2025 Americas absorbed $45M of tariff cost against $28M of recoveries, a net $17M hit. The Q2 10-Q discloses that no amounts have been recorded for potential government recoveries related to the pending IEEPA decision and that potential recoveries are not expected to be material, which closes off a hoped-for windfall.

**Governance and continuity.** The CFO notified the company of intent to depart no later than December 31, 2026 (8-K filed 2026-07-10, Item 5.02; text unretrieved), the second CFO turnover in roughly four years, arriving in the same twelve months as a $333M impairment and a guided cash-flow decline. The stated reason is no disagreement with the company, which is the standard formulation.

**Macro.** Global production is contracting, with North America down 2.6% and China down 5.8% in the March quarter, which argues against a near-term recovery in vehicle production volumes. [unverified] Broader interest-rate and consumer-demand conditions are not addressed in the provided sources.

**Verification gaps that constrain confidence.** The fiscal Q3 10-Q (quarter ended 6/30/2026) was not yet indexed at the time of this analysis, so Q3 segment EBITDA, Asia margin, quarter-end cash and year-to-date free cash flow are unknown. The ~$880M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance dates from the February call and may have been revised on August 5; only the ~$15B revenue figure is sourced to the August print. Debt covenants and post-amendment ABL availability were not verified, so the near-term solvency assessment rests on leverage, liquidity and the maturity ladder rather than on confirmed covenant headroom.

**Offsets already priced.** Net leverage of 1.77x, cash of $831M, no maturity before 2028 and 0.97x book do bound the downside in the absence of a recession. The retrieved insider window (roughly November 2025 through June 2026) showed no open-market purchases but also no CEO or CFO sales, with Dorlack holding roughly 630,000 shares unhedged.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $30 (+42%)

Adient is a share gainer trading below book value with a solvent balance sheet. Revenue outgrows production by 6-8 points on onshoring wins in the Americas and content gains with Chinese entrants. Asia's margin decline proves substantially launch-cost timing rather than permanent price, stabilizing at 13.5-14% as programs mature. Americas margin continues improving from 5.5% to 5.8% and beyond as onshoring volume scales operating leverage. A valuation-allowance release drops the effective tax rate toward 30%, delivering the $3.28 FY2027 consensus without operational heroics; 9x that figure is $29.50. Management resumes the buyback that reduced share count 17.3% since FY2022, and the board expands the near-exhausted authorization. The equity's 16.5-21% implied cost of capital in the impairment model is revealed as a mechanical artifact of a depressed share price rather than an economic assessment of a business at 1.8x leverage with $1.5B of liquidity.

### Bear Case — $14 (-34%)

The margin-for-volume trade is structural and accelerating. Asia margin settles at 11-12% as domestic Chinese OEM mix continues to rotate, costing $70-100M of EBITDA, while adverse regional mix adds a second headwind. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA falls toward $800M. A North American production downturn removes the onshoring offset. The Asia goodwill cushion of roughly 10% fails, producing a second impairment that takes book equity below $1.0B and eliminates the below-book support; at 3.5x $800M of EBITDA less net debt and NCI, the equity is worth roughly $11. Restructuring remains a permanent $60M+ annual cash cost with disclosed minimal earnings benefit. Attributable free cash flow after NCI distributions stays under $75M, the buyback does not resume, and the equity remains a 4x-EBITDA business with no mechanism to convert EBITDA into per-share value.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires the Asia decline to be timing. The bear case requires it to be price. The FY2025 10-K states management's own expectation of continued margin decline in China, which favors the bear reading, while the 10-Q's attribution to launch and overhead costs leaves room for the bull. This cannot be resolved without Q3 and Q4 segment data, which is why the recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Two dated checkpoints. First, the fiscal Q3 10-Q (expected within days of this report) will supply Q3 segment adjusted EBITDA, Asia margin, quarter-end cash and nine-month free cash flow — the data that resolves the timing-versus-price question one quarter further. Second, fiscal Q4 and full-year results with the FY2026 10-K, expected mid-November 2026, will confirm whether adjusted EBITDA landed at the guided ~$880M on ~$15.0B of revenue and whether the annual goodwill test produced a second impairment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Asia segment adjusted EBITDA margin recovers to 14% or above for two consecutive quarters with consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 6.0%, which would establish the Q2/Q3 decline as launch-cost timing.
2. Confirmed valuation-allowance release with a guided go-forward effective tax rate below 35%, combined with resumption of share repurchase and board expansion of the authorization beyond the remaining $110M.
3. Full-year free cash flow after noncontrolling-interest distributions of $150M or more, demonstrating that consolidated cash conversion translates to shareholders.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Asia segment margin below 12.0%, or consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin below 5.5%, in any reported quarter.
2. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance reduced below $850M, or FY2027 guidance implying a decline from FY2026.
3. A second goodwill impairment (most plausibly in Asia), any covenant amendment or waiver, or net leverage exceeding 2.5x.
4. Successor CFO not named by the December 31, 2026 departure deadline, or the departure accompanied by any restatement or control-related disclosure.

**Reference scenario values** (consistent with the Investment Thesis): base $23 (+9%), which is 8.8x a normalized $2.60 EPS between the $2.16 current-year and $3.28 next-year consensus, roughly 1.05x book and 4.2x EV/EBITDA including NCI; bull $30 (+42%); bear $14 (-34%). The asymmetry is roughly 1:3.8 against on a base-versus-bear comparison, which is why we are not buying at $21.10 despite the low multiple, and roughly 1.2:1 in favor on bull-versus-bear, which is why we are not shorting a company at 1.8x leverage with no maturity before 2028.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 4.6, a marginal decline from 4.8 in June. The score history — 4.3 (AVOID) in April, 5.0 (HOLD) in May, 4.8 (HOLD) in June — reflected improving recognition of the balance sheet's resilience and the revenue outgrowth. The current adjustment reflects three specific items: the fiscal Q3 adjusted EPS miss ($0.48 versus $0.53), which is the second miss in four quarters and continues the revenue-beats/earnings-misses pattern; the July 10 CFO departure notice, the second CFO turnover in roughly four years; and the absence of any verified evidence that Asia's 310bp margin decline has stabilized, with the Q3 10-Q not yet available.

Financial health at 4.2 recognizes moderate leverage (1.77x net, no maturity before 2028, $1.5B+ liquidity) against poor cash quality (operating cash flow down 33% in two years, first-half cash flow flattered by $90M of timing, rising capex, $77M of half-year NCI leakage, negative tangible book of -$4.97 per share, 63% effective tax rate). Growth prospects at 4.8 credit genuine revenue outgrowth of 6-8 points versus production while discounting it, because three consecutive years of flat adjusted EBITDA on a shrinking then growing revenue base is the outcome that matters. Valuation at 5.2 acknowledges 4.07x EV/EBITDA and 0.97x book while declining to treat a cheap multiple as a mispricing when management itself guides both cash flow and China margin lower. Competitive position at 4.5 nets improving volume share against deteriorating margin and two structurally captive competitors. Management quality at 4.3 weighs a well-executed historical buyback (17.3% share reduction) and genuine realizable-pay alignment (compensation actually paid of -$7.58M in FY2025) against a 38% increase in granted CEO compensation in a $281M loss year, no open-market insider purchases at prices below book, a paused repurchase program and the CFO transition. Risk factors at 4.3 reflect the concentrated Asia goodwill cushion, management-guided margin decline, tax exposure and the specific gap in verified Q3 segment data.
