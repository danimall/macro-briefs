**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UFP Technologies is a contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) that builds single-use and single-patient medical devices and components for the largest medical-device OEMs. Its franchise sits on top of two secular growth vectors, robotic surgery (Intuitive Surgical) and orthopedics/trauma (Stryker), and the business has compounded revenue from $353.8M (2022) to $602.8M (2025), a 19.5% increase in the most recent year. Organic growth, however, was only 1.5% in FY2025 and decelerated to +4.1% total in Q1 2026 as prior acquisitions lapped. Nearly all of the top-line story is inorganic: 2024/2025 deals contributed $168.3M of 2025 sales versus $73.1M in 2024. The equity currently trades near $238, ~27x trailing EPS of $8.75 and ~23x the $10.38 current-year consensus.

This is a good business with a mediocre entry point. The core assets — specialized cross-linked polyurethane and TPU foams, clean-room capability, ISO 13485/FDA compliance across 19/13 facilities, and preferred-supplier status with OEMs — create real switching costs and support a ~28% gross margin, ~15-16% operating margin, and 13% FCF margin with light capex. The balance sheet is sound (Altman Z 3.62, D/E 0.55, current ratio 2.68, LT debt reduced from $189M to $135M in 2025). But the valuation prices continued acquisition-fueled growth at a time when organic growth has stalled, wound-care destocking at two customers is guided to persist through Q3 2026, and Q1 2026 operating cash flow collapsed to $3.2M on a working-capital build.

The score trajectory (6.5 → 6.7 → 5.8, BUY → BUY → HOLD) reflects a deterioration in the fundamentals visible since May, not just price action. We continue that HOLD here at a marginally lower overall score. The stock has round-tripped from a $279 high to $238 without meaningful fundamental improvement, and 24% short interest plus a February cyber incident, a founder-CEO succession, and net insider selling on strength keep the setup balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on a durable organic-growth reacceleration above mid-single-digits or a pullback into the high-$180s/low-$190s.

## Company Overview

UFP Technologies functions as an outsourced medical-device manufacturing partner. Its products span minimally invasive/robotic surgery (surgical drapes for da Vinci systems, endoscopic disposables), infection prevention, advanced wound care, orthopedics, interventional and surgical components, patient handling/surfaces, and biopharma. Roughly 93% of revenue is now Medical (up from ~91% a year ago), with the remainder non-medical (industrial/consumer packaging), and international at ~16-18% (Ireland, Mexico, Dominican Republic, Costa Rica, Puerto Rico).

The moat is relationship-based rather than patent-based; the company holds only 21 active patents. Value derives from specialized materials access, engineering know-how, clean-room manufacturing, regulatory compliance, and preferred-supplier status that embeds UFP into customers' device specifications. Switching costs arise because a qualified, spec-locked supplier is expensive and slow to replace in a regulated device. The company runs as a single reportable segment and has been executing a roll-up of smaller specialty converters (foam, thermoforming, injection molding, fluoropolymers) into a broader MedTech CDMO platform, mirroring peers like Integer Holdings and the private Resonetics.

Two corporate transitions frame the current period. Founder R. Jeffrey Bailly, CEO since 1995, is retiring from the CEO role effective June 4, 2026, becoming Executive Chairman through June 2027; longtime UFP executive Mitchell C. Rock (President since February 2024, at UFP since 1991) succeeds him. Separately, the company disclosed a material cybersecurity incident detected around February 14, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 net income was $68.3M, with a 15.3% operating margin and 28.3% gross margin. The multi-year progression:

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Op Margin | Gross Margin |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| 2022 | $353.8M | $41.8M | 15.7% | 25.5% |
| 2023 | $400.1M | $44.9M | 14.4% | 28.1% |
| 2024 | $504.4M | $59.0M | 16.1% | 29.1% |
| 2025 | $602.8M | $68.3M | 15.3% | 28.3% |

The gross-margin slip from 29.1% to 28.3% in 2025 was driven largely by the AJR labor issue (roughly 100bps of drag; see Risk Assessment). Q1 2026 gross margin recovered to 28.8%, while total revenue grew only 4.1% ($154.2M vs $148.1M) as acquisitions lapped and non-medical sales fell 15%.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $91.9M and FCF was $79.0M (13.1% FCF margin) on light capex of ~$13M, a high-quality conversion profile. The concern is Q1 2026: OCF fell to $3.2M (from $13.8M) as a $15.7M receivables build and $7.2M inventory build consumed nearly all quarterly earnings. One customer represented 32.1% of gross receivables at year-end 2025. Whether the Q1 working-capital drain is timing (billing disruption tied to the cyber incident is a plausible partial explanation) or the start of a trend is the single most important item to monitor in the Q2 print.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $655M, of which goodwill plus intangibles are $338M (51.6%), a material impairment-risk concentration for an acquisition-driven company. Long-term debt was reduced from $189M to $135M during 2025 at a ~5.0% weighted rate; D/E is 0.55 and current ratio 2.68. Altman Z of 3.62 places the company in the safe zone. ROE is 16.1% and ROA 10.4%. The debt load is modest for a business with 13% FCF margins.

**Earnings quality and consensus confidence.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (8.2%, 5.5%, 0.9%, 2.9%), giving reasonable confidence in near-term estimates. Estimate revisions are mixed-to-modestly-positive: current-year EPS estimates rose 2.5% over 90 days to $10.38 and next-year rose 2.0% to $11.69, but the near-quarter estimates drifted down 1.2%/2.1% over 90 days before stabilizing (+1.9%/+1.0% over 30 days). This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags an inflection; it is a business growing EPS in the high single digits with a decelerating top line partly offset by margin recovery and a rising forward estimate.

## Growth Analysis

The distinction between reported and organic growth is the crux of the investment case. Behind FY2025's +19.5% lies only 1.5% organic growth, and Q1 2026 total growth of +4.1% now that acquisitions are annualizing. The underlying secular demand is real — robotic surgery volumes at Intuitive, orthopedic/trauma at Stryker, and minimally invasive procedures broadly are structural growth markets — but UFP's organic participation has been muted, and near-term it is being masked by non-medical revenue down 15% and wound-care destocking at two customers expected to persist through Q3 2026.

The forward growth algorithm therefore rests heavily on continued M&A. The 2025 bolt-ons (AJR ~$2.8M, TPI ~$4.5M, UNIPEC ~$7.5M) are small; the larger 2024 deals (AQF, Welch, Marble) drove the step-up. The roll-up is coherent and the private market for specialty medical converters is deep, but the strategy carries integration risk, contingent-consideration true-ups, and financing risk. The Welch earn-out was written to zero after the target missed EBITDA in both 2024 and 2025, with management deeming 2026 unlikely. That is one recent acquisition demonstrably not working within a portfolio where goodwill/intangibles are 51.6% of assets.

Consensus models roughly high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS growth from FY26's $10.38 to FY27's $11.69 (+12.6%), consistent with margin recovery plus modest organic growth plus additional bolt-ons. That is a respectable but no longer explosive trajectory, and it does not clear the bar for the strong inflection signal that would justify overriding valuation. Growth Prospects score 5.0: the demand backdrop is durable, but organic momentum is weak and the growth story is contingent on continued dealmaking.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$238, UFPT trades at ~27x trailing EPS of $8.75 and ~23x the FY26 consensus of $10.38 (~20x the $11.69 FY27 estimate). Against the Health Care Equipment peer set, the median P/E is 19.4 (CNMD 19.4, LMAT 34.0, INSP 9.5, with ENOV and IART showing negative trailing figures). UFPT sits at a premium to the median, which is defensible on quality grounds (higher margins, cleaner balance sheet, better returns on capital) but is not a discount that signals mispricing.

The cycle-adjustment logic that would rescue a high trailing multiple does not apply cleanly here. EPS is growing steadily, not tripling, and there is no +30% estimate-revision velocity or +40% revenue acceleration to point to. The estimate revisions are modestly positive on the full-year but flat-to-slightly-negative on the near quarters. Applying the discipline that cheapness without a catalyst is a value-trap warning and, conversely, that a premium multiple without an inflection is not a bargain, the valuation offers no obvious asymmetry.

Analyst price targets ($289 low, $360 high, $324.5 mean) imply meaningful upside from spot. On an inflection stock trading below 30x forward with rising estimates, we would treat those targets as lagging floors. Here the stock trades at ~23x forward with only modest estimate revisions and a decelerating top line, so the targets read as sell-side optimism about continued M&A execution rather than as an independent, catalyst-backed floor. We give them weight as a directional signal but do not anchor to them.

Fair value framing: applying a ~22x forward multiple (a modest premium to the 19.4 peer median for the quality differential) to the $10.38 FY26 estimate yields ~$228, essentially spot. Bull case at ~28x forward on $11.69 FY27 reaches ~$327; bear case at ~17x on a stalled FY26 reaches ~$176. That distribution is roughly symmetric, which is why this is a HOLD and not a BUY. Valuation score 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

UFP occupies a defensible niche as a qualified, spec-embedded outsourcing partner to top device OEMs. Its switching costs are real: once a supplier is designed into a Class III device and validated under ISO 13485/FDA, replacing it is slow and costly. The company's rising Medical mix (93%) and preferred-supplier relationships with the leaders in robotic surgery and orthopedics are positives, and the roll-up strategy expands its addressable content per customer.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-pressured rather than improving. Two dynamics temper the moat. First, the industry is fragmented and price-competitive, populated by regional foam/plastics converters. Second, UFP's two largest customers are also its greatest competitive threat: Intuitive Surgical (24.3% of FY25 sales) and Stryker (21.5%) could in-source, a risk the 10-Q explicitly flags. Any share loss at either would be materially damaging given they represent ~46% of revenue. Organic growth of 1.5% suggests UFP is holding, not gaining, share on a like-for-like basis. Combined with the wound-care destocking and the Welch underperformance, the competitive picture does not support an improving-trajectory score. Competitive Position 5.8, down modestly in line with the prior report's trend.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (defining risk).** Intuitive (24.3%) and Stryker (21.5%) were ~46% of FY2025 sales; the top 10 were 68.8%. One customer was 32.1% of gross receivables at year-end. This concentration rides best-in-class secular growth but makes any single-customer decision (in-sourcing, resourcing, pricing) a potential step-change to earnings.

**Cyber incident (new, February 2026).** Disclosed under 8-K Item 1.05, the incident impacted many but not all IT systems, disrupted billing and label-making for customer deliveries, and involved data exfiltration/destruction. Management reports no material financial impact "as of the date hereof," but the investigation is ongoing, and the billing disruption is a plausible contributor to the Q1 working-capital build. Breach-notification, litigation, and remediation costs are open-ended tail risks.

**AJR E-Verify labor issue (new).** A post-acquisition workforce-eligibility review added >$6.3M of incremental labor cost to 2025 COGS (~100bps of gross margin) and forced significant turnover, a cautionary integration data point for an acquisition-led model.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** At $338M (51.6% of assets), with the Welch earn-out already written to zero, impairment risk is concentrated and demonstrably live.

**Cash conversion.** Q1 2026 OCF of $3.2M (vs $13.8M) on working-capital build needs to normalize by Q2; a second quarter of weak conversion would materially change the FCF-quality assessment.

**Geopolitical/input costs.** Dominican Republic manufacturing near Haiti unrest and a new Israel-Iran risk factor tied to petroleum-based polymer input costs. The SCOTUS IEEPA tariff ruling is a modest tailwind (expected refunds/vendor credits).

Given the cluster of concentration, a fresh cyber tail, integration missteps, a leadership transition, and elevated short interest, the idiosyncratic risk profile is above average and not fully priced. Risk Factors score 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.6% is rich versus 44.9% one-year historical vol (a 5.7-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (50.6% near-term vs 40.4% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the upcoming Q2 earnings print and the unresolved cyber investigation. Put/call skew of 5.4% shows elevated put demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.2%. For a BUY thesis this positioning would be a caution; for a HOLD it confirms that the market sees a two-sided event ahead. Short interest of 24.0% of float (6.5 days to cover) adds squeeze potential on any upside surprise, cutting against a bearish lean.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$327, +37%).** Organic growth reaccelerates as wound-care destocking clears after Q3 2026 and non-medical stabilizes; the M&A pipeline delivers accretive bolt-ons without further AJR/Welch-style stumbles; margins hold near 29% with the AJR drag lapping; the cyber incident proves financially immaterial; and 24x-28x forward on the $11.69 FY27 estimate re-rates the stock as consensus targets ($324.5 mean) prove right. High short interest amplifies upside on any clean beat.

**Bear case (~$176, -26%).** Organic growth stays sub-2% and a large customer resources or in-sources; Q1's cash-conversion weakness proves structural rather than timing; a goodwill impairment materializes on an underperforming acquisition; cyber remediation/litigation costs surface; and the multiple compresses toward ~17x on stalled FY26 earnings as the acquisition-fueled narrative loses credibility during a leadership transition.

The distribution is roughly symmetric around a base case near current levels (~$228-238). The business quality is not in question; the entry price is.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — insufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction at the current price).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth reaccelerating above ~5% YoY for two consecutive quarters (i.e., wound-care destocking clearing and non-medical stabilizing), confirmed in the Q2 and Q3 2026 prints.
- A pullback into the high-$180s/low-$190s, where forward P/E compresses toward ~18x and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- Q2 2026 operating cash flow normalizing back above ~$60M annualized run-rate, confirming the Q1 working-capital drain was timing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of sub-2% organic growth combined with weak cash conversion.
- Any indication of share loss or in-sourcing at Intuitive or Stryker.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or cyber-related litigation/remediation costs proving material.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural reassessment point; it will show whether the wound-care destocking is tracking to the Q3 exit, whether cash conversion normalized, and how the CEO transition and cyber investigation are progressing.

## Conclusion

UFP Technologies is a well-run, financially healthy medical-device CDMO with a defensible niche and real exposure to secular growth in robotic and minimally invasive surgery. The price already reflects the quality while the growth story has shifted from explosive to acquisition-dependent, with organic growth at 1.5% and near-term headwinds (wound-care destocking through Q3 2026, weak Q1 cash conversion, a fresh cyber incident, customer concentration, and a founder-CEO handoff) clustering at the wrong moment. At ~23x forward with only modest estimate revisions, there is no valuation cushion and no inflection signal to override it. The declining score trajectory since May reflects genuine fundamental softening, and we continue the HOLD. We would turn constructive on a clear organic reacceleration or a re-entry in the high-$180s/low-$190s; we would move to AVOID on evidence of customer share loss, a second quarter of poor cash conversion, or a goodwill impairment. Overall score 5.25.
