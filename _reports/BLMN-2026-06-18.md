**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bloomin' Brands operates four U.S. casual-dining concepts (Outback Steakhouse, Carrabba's, Bonefish Grill, Fleming's) plus a 33% minority stake and franchise relationship in Brazil following the December 2024 sale of 67% of that business to a Vinci Partners–managed fund. The investment question is narrow: is the early-stage Outback turnaround under new CEO Michael Spanos durable enough to support a leveraged, thinly-capitalized equity, or does the distress-zone balance sheet and a live franchisee-credit overhang dominate?

The data points in both directions, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The bull evidence is real: Q1 FY2026 operating margin recovered to 5.6% from 0.9% in FY2025, restaurant-level margin rose to 14.0% from 11.7%, the company has beaten consensus in three of the last four quarters, Q4 FY2025 marked Outback's first positive quarterly traffic in four years, and current-quarter estimates have been revised up +33% over 90 days. Two directors made open-market purchases near the March lows. The bear evidence is equally concrete: an Altman Z-Score of 1.27 (distress zone), ~8.5x debt/equity, an Outback goodwill cushion of only ~3%, Outback traffic that relapsed to -2.4% in Q1 (growth remains pricing-led), and the Out West franchisee (72 Outback units, ~7% of the system) whose senior lender issued a default notice with forbearance expiring December 27, 2026.

At $7.68, the stock has round-tripped from a $5.19 low (March 2026) through the post-Q1 spike to ~$8.40 and back. A base-case fair value of roughly $7.50 (8.5x the $0.88 current-year EPS estimate) sits essentially at spot, and the analyst mean target of $8.64 and median of $8.50 sit above it. The leverage discount is largely in the price; BLMN already trades at the same ~8.7x as the comparably-levered Wendy's. With the bear case substantially priced, fair value at spot, targets above spot, and no shortable catalyst with favorable asymmetry, the defensible posture is to hold and monitor the two binary items (Out West resolution, sustained Outback traffic) that will determine direction.

## Company Overview

Bloomin' Brands is one of the larger casual-dining operators globally, with 962 company-operated restaurants and 490 franchised units across 46 states and 12 countries as of Q1 FY2026. The portfolio spans the price spectrum:

- **Outback Steakhouse** — casual, ~$29 average check, the flagship at ~58% of U.S. restaurant sales; 666 U.S. units (548 company-owned)
- **Carrabba's Italian Grill** — casual Italian, ~$27 check; 204 units
- **Bonefish Grill** — polished-casual seafood, ~$37 check; 158 units
- **Fleming's Prime Steakhouse & Wine Bar** — fine dining, ~$110 check; 66 units

The primary operating subsidiary is OSI Restaurant Partners, LLC. The business is heavily concentrated in Outback, which is simultaneously the largest revenue driver, the source of most of the company's recent problems, and the center of the turnaround plan.

A structural change distorts the headline financials: on December 30, 2024, Bloomin' sold 67% of its Brazil operations to a Vinci Partners–managed fund, retaining a 33% equity-method stake plus a 20-year franchise agreement at below-historical royalty rates. This is why consolidated revenue dropped from $4.67B (FY2023) to ~$3.95B (FY2024–2025): Brazil shifted from consolidation to equity-method/discontinued treatment, so the ~15% optical revenue decline is not an operating collapse of that magnitude. A put/call mechanism allows monetization of the remaining 33% stake in Q4 2028 at an earnings multiple.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability has been thin and was deteriorating into FY2025 before a Q1 FY2026 inflection.

| Metric (continuing ops) | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.95B | $3.96B | $1.06B (+1.0% YoY) |
| Operating income | $140M | $37.2M | $59.1M |
| Operating margin | 3.5% | 0.9% | 5.6% |
| Net income (attrib.) | $(128M) | $8.2M | $55.7M |
| Diluted EPS | $(1.49) | $0.10 | $0.64 |
| Restaurant-level margin | 13.3% | 11.7% | 14.0% |

FY2025 was a trough. Operating margin compressed to 0.9% on commodity inflation (+1.1%), labor inflation (+1.3%), unfavorable product mix, a $28.2M goodwill impairment that wrote off the entire Bonefish Grill reporting unit, and $45M of impairment/closure charges. Adjusted EPS fell to $1.13 from $1.79.

Q1 FY2026 shows genuine sequential improvement. Operating margin recovered to 5.6%, restaurant-level margin to 14.0%, and EPS jumped to $0.64. Productivity initiatives reduced "other restaurant operating" expense, and G&A fell as the company lapped prior-year severance and FX costs. One caveat: a negative effective tax rate (-22%), driven by FICA tip credits against lower forecasted full-year pretax income, flattered net income. The tip-credit benefit is structural to restaurant economics but should not be extrapolated linearly across a full year of higher pretax income.

Cash flow is the relative strength. FY2025 operating cash flow was $276M against $180M capex, for FCF of roughly $96M (FCF margin 2.4%). In Q1 FY2026, operating cash flow was $75M and capex was cut to $25M from $46M as management slowed new-unit development; cash rose to $71M. The company directed Brazil proceeds (~$228M across 2025) and the suspended dividend toward the revolver, reducing debt from $790M to $755M in the quarter.

The balance sheet is the core problem. The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 sits in the distress zone, debt/equity is ~8.5x, and the current ratio is 0.31. Long-term debt of $755M comprises a $455M revolver at 5.57% and $300M of 2029 Notes at 5.13%, both maturing in 2029. Stockholders' equity is only ~$399M against $3.1B of assets, with a deeply negative accumulated deficit of -$862M. There is a working-capital deficit of ~$591M, though ~$321M of that is unearned gift-card revenue and ~$177M is current lease liabilities with the offsetting right-of-use assets in non-current, a structural feature of restaurant balance sheets rather than an acute liquidity hole. The company carries $1.16B of operating lease commitments (plus $946M of renewal options reasonably certain to be exercised) layered on top of the debt. Interest-rate swaps hedge $300–375M notional, partially insulating against rate moves, relevant given a rate environment that keeps the floating revolver expensive.

Capital allocation has whipsawed. The company repurchased $266M of stock in FY2024, then suspended the dividend (from a $0.96 annual rate, cut to $0.60 run-rate in 2025) in October 2025, and let the $350M buyback expire in August 2025. The pivot to debt paydown is an implicit acknowledgment by the new management team that the prior capital-return posture was unsustainable given leverage. Capex is guided to $185–195M for 2026, focused on remodels rather than new units.

Financial health scores low (3.5). The distress-zone Z-score, 8.5x leverage, thin equity cushion, and 2029 refinancing concentration are not offset by the FCF generation, which at 2.4% margin is too thin to deleverage quickly if margins slip.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is an early, fragile inflection rather than a secular story. The encouraging data: combined U.S. comps were +0.9% in Q1 FY2026, and Bonefish posted +6.1% comps with +3.0% positive traffic. The earnings-revision data supports near-term momentum — the current-quarter estimate rose +33.2% over 30 days and +28.1% over 90 days.

The qualifications are material. Outback Q1 comps were -0.3% with traffic relapsing to -2.4%, so the flagship's growth remains check-/pricing-led rather than traffic-led, the metric that matters most for a turnaround. Full-year estimate revisions are modest (current year +2.8%/90d, next year -7.9%/90d), and the next-quarter estimate was revised sharply lower (-74.6%/90d to -$0.19), reflecting seasonal softness and the non-recurrence of the Q1 tax benefit. The +28% 90-day current-quarter revision earns weight in the growth score, but it does not clear the threshold for treating BLMN as a clean inflection BUY: revenue growth is roughly flat (+1.0% YoY), not the >30% acceleration that pairs with high-conviction earnings-revision signals, and full-year estimates are only modestly higher.

The turnaround plan — four platforms covering dine-in experience, brand relevancy, culture/people, and restaurant investment, plus a goal to remodel nearly all Outback units by end-2028 — is credible under an operations-focused CEO, but it requires sustained capex while simultaneously deleveraging, a competing demand on thin FCF. Management guides to 4.5–5.5% commodity inflation in 2026, a continued margin headwind. Growth prospects score 5.0: the directional improvement and revision momentum are genuine, but flat revenue, negative flagship traffic, and pricing-led comps keep this from being a durable growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.68, BLMN trades at ~31.5x trailing EPS (on depressed FY2025 EPS of $0.10) and roughly 8.7x the $0.88 current-year consensus EPS. The trailing multiple is not meaningful given the FY2025 trough; the forward figure is the relevant anchor.

Against restaurant peers, the comparison is instructive. Peer median P/E is 38.7x, but that median is inflated by higher-multiple, less-levered names (CBRL 39.5x, PZZA 38.7x, BJRI 26.0x). The cleanest comparable for a highly-leveraged casual-dining operator is Wendy's at 8.7x, exactly where BLMN trades. The market is already applying the appropriate leverage discount; BLMN is not cheap relative to its risk profile, it is priced consistently with a peer carrying similar balance-sheet stress.

A base case of roughly 8.5x the $0.88 current-year EPS yields ~$7.50, essentially at the current price. The analyst mean target is $8.64 and median $8.50, both above spot, with a range of $6.00 to $11.00. The bear case is largely in the price: the leverage discount is reflected in the multiple, and prior appreciation from the round-trip is not a valuation input here. There is no specific, identifiable mispricing that argues the market is wrong in BLMN's favor: the forward multiple is reasonable rather than cheap, and the discount to higher-multiple peers is earned by the leverage and weaker traffic profile.

Valuation scores 5.0. The "cheap" optics relative to the peer median are a value-trap signal absent a catalyst, and the relevant levered comparable shows the stock is fairly priced. With fair value at spot and no asymmetric catalyst, neither the bull nor bear case has a valuation edge.

## Competitive Landscape

The casual-dining steak segment is intensely competitive and value-driven, and Bloomin' is losing the value argument at the flagship. External analysis frames the bear case: Texas Roadhouse and Darden's LongHorn offer stronger value propositions and traffic-led growth, while Outback's recent comps are pricing-driven with still-negative traffic. Bloomin's margins sit well below higher-volume peers, and Outback's average unit volume (~$4.0M) has been roughly flat.

The company is responding with the smaller-footprint "Joey" prototype (~5,000 sq ft) to improve new-unit ROI, though new development is deliberately slowed. Off-premises is 25% of Outback sales and 34% at Carrabba's, partially offsetting in-store traffic pressure. Broader structural pressures cited in the 10-K — fast-casual/QSR encroachment, grocery prepared meals, and third-party delivery economics — are sector-wide but bite harder at a thin-margin, highly-levered operator.

The trajectory is the issue. Bonefish and the Q4 Outback traffic turn show the turnaround can work in pockets, but the flagship's Q1 traffic relapse indicates the competitive position has stabilized rather than improved. Competitive position scores 4.0: market leadership in absolute terms, but a deteriorating-to-stabilizing trajectory at the concept that matters most, against share-gaining competitors.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is leverage and liquidity. The distress-zone Z-score, 8.5x debt/equity, and thin equity cushion mean any operating shortfall directly threatens covenant compliance, and the 10-K's debt section is unusually detailed about covenant-acceleration scenarios. Refinancing is concentrated in 2029.

The second binary risk is the Out West franchisee. After Q1, the senior lender to Out West issued a default notice for breaching its own credit covenants. Bloomin' holds forbearance/payment-priority arrangements expiring December 27, 2026; on expiration, deferred amounts owed to Out West's lenders become due and lenders gain priority foreclosure rights. Bloomin' could lose this royalty stream, face contingent lease obligations, or have to support/repurchase the locations. Out West was current with Bloomin' as of March 29, 2026, but this is a live, unresolved overhang with a hard December 2026 resolution date.

Goodwill cushions are wafer-thin. The Outback goodwill cushion fell to ~3% in the Q4 2025 test and the trade name to ~5%; management modeled a ~$36M Outback impairment on a 100bp discount-rate increase. Bonefish is already fully written off. Commodity concentration adds risk: beef, the majority of protein spend, is sourced from only four suppliers, and 2026 commodity inflation is guided to 4.5–5.5%.

Many of these risks are at least partially priced: the 8.7x forward multiple matching the levered Wendy's comp reflects the leverage, and the goodwill cushions are disclosed. The Out West overhang is the least-priced item and the one with a discrete catalyst date. Risk factors score 4.0: specific, quantified downside catalysts (Out West expiration, Outback impairment trigger, covenant sensitivity) exist, but they are not entirely unpriced, which keeps the score from going lower.

### Options Market Signal

The reported ATM IV reading of 687.9% (up from 90.7%) and a put/call OI ratio collapsing to 0.00 are almost certainly thin-options data artifacts on a small-cap name and should be treated as non-informative. The credible signals are the 43.2% price move since early May (the post-Q1 spike) and short interest of 9.9% of float with 2.5 days to cover — moderate, uncrowded, not at squeeze-threatening levels. There is no clean options-implied directional signal to confirm or challenge the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The turnaround is working and the market is underweighting it. Margins recovered sharply in Q1, the beat record is intact, estimate revisions are positive, Outback traffic turned positive in Q4 for the first time in four years, and Bonefish is posting positive traffic. An operations-focused CEO with PepsiCo/Delta/Six Flags pedigree, a CFO from Campbell's, debt being reduced, and two directors buying stock near the lows support an improving-fundamentals read. If full-year EPS holds at ~$0.88–$0.97 and the Out West situation resolves without material loss, a re-rate toward the $8.50 median target and an 8.5x multiple on rising EPS supports a bull case in the $10–11 range (the analyst high), roughly +30% to +43% from spot.

**Bear case.** The inflection is pricing-led and fragile, and the balance sheet leaves no margin for error. Outback traffic relapsed in Q1, the Q1 EPS surge was partly a non-recurring tax benefit, commodity inflation is a 2026 headwind, and the Out West forbearance expiry could force Bloomin' to absorb lease/operational liabilities precisely while deleveraging. A ~$36M Outback goodwill impairment is plausible if 2026 disappoints. In this case, EPS misses the $0.88 figure, the multiple compresses toward the $6.00 analyst low (-22% from spot), and covenant headroom tightens.

The two cases roughly cancel. Fair value sits at the current price, targets are above it, the leverage discount is in the multiple, and the binary risks resolve on a known timeline. This is the profile of a balanced HOLD, not a structurally impaired AVOID. The audit challenge is correct on this point: with the bear case largely priced, fair value at spot, and no shortable asymmetry, AVOID would overstate conviction. The current-quarter revision, the beat record, the Q4 traffic turn, and the two open-market director buys (Lal 10,000 @ $5.69; Kunkel 1,000 @ $6.46, both ~25–30% below spot) are positive signals that offset the distress-balance-sheet and Out West concerns enough to make the call uncertain rather than negative.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 FY2026 earnings release (testing whether the margin recovery holds without the Q1 tax benefit), and the December 27, 2026 Out West forbearance expiration. The full-year deleveraging trajectory and any Outback goodwill re-test at year-end 2026 are the other gates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Outback traffic turns sustainably positive for two consecutive quarters (not just pricing-led comps), confirming the turnaround on the metric that matters.
- Out West resolves without material loss to Bloomin' (no lease/operational absorption), removing the largest binary overhang ahead of the December 2026 expiration.
- Debt reduction continues toward a Z-score out of the distress zone and full-year EPS estimates revise up materially (>+10%/90d), with the forward multiple still below the levered-peer 8.7x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Out West default crystallizes into a material liability or forced repurchase of units, with quantified lease/operational cost to Bloomin'.
- A second-half operating-margin reversal back toward FY2025 levels (operating margin below ~3%), or an Outback goodwill impairment that signals deteriorating flagship economics.
- Covenant pressure surfaces in the 10-Q, or the revolver balance stops declining, indicating FCF is insufficient to both deleverage and fund the remodel program.

## Conclusion

Bloomin' Brands is a balanced setup: a credible-but-early Outback turnaround with real Q1 margin recovery, the beat record, positive current-quarter estimate revisions, and insider buying near the lows, set against a distress-zone balance sheet, pricing-led comps with negative flagship traffic, and a live Out West franchisee-default overhang resolving by December 2026. The valuation work lands fair value (~$7.50) essentially at the current price, with analyst mean ($8.64) and median ($8.50) targets above spot and the leverage discount already embedded in the 8.7x multiple. With the bear case largely priced, no asymmetric short catalyst, and the decisive variables (Out West resolution, sustained Outback traffic) yet to resolve, the appropriate posture is HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the positives and negatives roughly offset. Monitor against the upgrade/downgrade triggers above rather than own with conviction or actively avoid.
