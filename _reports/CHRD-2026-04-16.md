## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the dominant pure-play Williston Basin E&P, producing 276,620 Boepd (56% crude oil) after absorbing Enerplus (2024) and XTO Williston assets (Oct 2025). FY2025 reported net income collapsed 95% to $44.5M, driven entirely by a $539.3M non-cash goodwill impairment and elevated DD&A — operating cash flow held flat at $2.04B, and free cash flow was $693M (~9.1% yield on the current $7.6B market cap). The goodwill is now fully written off, leaving a cleaner balance sheet with no future impairment drag from that line item.

The stock has rallied 47% from its November 2025 trough near $85 to $130, driven by the recovery in oil prices above $100/bbl. Management's 2026 guidance assumes $64 WTI — roughly 60% below current spot — implying the $700M adjusted FCF target is dramatically understated. At consensus FY2026E EPS of $16.03, CHRD trades at ~8.3x forward earnings vs. a peer median of 9.2x. Estimate revisions have surged: current-quarter EPS up 141% in 90 days, full-year up 140%. The combination of beaten-down valuation, massive oil price leverage (only 17% of 2026 production hedged), and structural cost improvements from long-lateral drilling creates an asymmetric setup. Key risks are single-basin concentration and the unresolved DAPL regulatory decision.

## Company Overview

Chord Energy Corporation was formed through the July 2022 merger of Oasis Petroleum and Whiting Petroleum. Since then, the company has executed a consolidation strategy within the Williston Basin, acquiring Enerplus Corporation (May 2024) and XTO Energy Williston Basin assets (October 2025, $542M cash). The result is the premier pure-play Williston Basin operator with estimated net proved reserves of 917.5 MMBoe (PV-10: $9.1B) and substantial contiguous acreage enabling 3-4 mile lateral drilling.

FY2025 production of 276,620 Boepd breaks down as approximately 154,795 Bopd crude oil (56%), 52,600 Bopd NGL (19%), and ~69,200 Boepd natural gas (25%). The company maintains limited non-operated Marcellus Shale interests that realized $3.15/Mcf in FY2025, well above the $1.40/Mcf Williston Basin gas realization.

The capital return program has been extraordinary: $6.7B returned to shareholders since 2021, exceeding the current market capitalization. The FY2025 base dividend of $5.20/share (~3.9% yield at current price) was supplemented by $364.5M in share repurchases at an average price of $104.39 — roughly 20% below the current stock price.

## Financial Analysis

### Cash Flow vs. Earnings Divergence

The central analytical point for CHRD is the massive divergence between reported earnings and underlying cash generation. FY2025 net income of $44.5M was distorted by:

- **$539.3M goodwill impairment** (non-cash; entire Enerplus goodwill eliminated)
- **$1.47B DD&A** (up $362M YoY from acquisition-related purchase price allocation)
- **81.7% effective tax rate** — because the goodwill impairment is non-deductible, pre-tax income of $243.5M generated $199M in tax expense. Normalizing for the impairment, the underlying tax rate is approximately 25%.

Operating cash flow of $2.04B was essentially flat YoY despite a 14% decline in average WTI prices. Free cash flow of $693M — after $1.36B in capex (which came in $60M below plan) — represents a 14.2% FCF margin on revenue. This level of cash generation at depressed oil prices demonstrates operational resilience.

### Revenue and Cost Structure

FY2025 revenue of $4.88B declined 7% from $5.25B despite a 19% increase in production volume, entirely due to lower commodity prices. Average crude oil realization fell to $62.78/Bbl from $73.67. NGL pricing collapsed to $7.22/Bbl (from $9.92) on wider differentials from incremental Enerplus volumes.

On the cost side, the company delivered measurable improvement:
- LOE essentially flat at $9.73/Boe despite adding XTO volumes
- GPT costs improved to $2.88/Boe (down $0.26 YoY)
- G&A dropped $79M as M&A-related costs fell from $89.3M to $9.8M
- Production taxes declined from 8.7% to 7.5% of revenue (partly from a non-recurring ND tax exemption refund)
- F&D costs reduced 22% through long-lateral conversion

### Balance Sheet

Long-term debt nearly doubled from $843M to $1.48B following issuance of $750M 6.000% 2030 notes and $750M 6.750% 2033 notes. The $400M 2026 notes were redeemed. D/E at 0.18x remains low, and the revolver ($2.75B borrowing base, $2.0B elected commitments) had zero borrowings, leaving $2.16B in total liquidity.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.27 falls in the "grey zone," but this is distorted by the goodwill impairment depressing EBIT/Assets to 0.015. On normalized EBIT, the Z-Score would be materially higher. The current ratio of 1.06 is tight on a standalone basis but the $1.97B in unused credit facility capacity provides ample backstop.

### 2026 Guidance and Upside

Management guided 2026 capex of $1.35-$1.45B, targeting $700M adjusted FCF at $64/Bbl WTI. With WTI recently above $100, each $1/Bbl above the $64 assumption adds approximately $40-50M in annualized pretax cash flow (based on ~155K Bopd oil production with 17% hedged). At $100 WTI, the incremental uplift is roughly $1.4-$1.8B pretax, suggesting potential FCF of $1.8-$2.2B or a 24-29% yield on the current market cap.

## Growth Analysis

Organic production growth has been supplemented by accretive M&A. Oil production grew 17% YoY in FY2025, and the XTO acquisition adds incremental volumes. The long-lateral drilling program — now at 80% conversion, ahead of schedule — represents a structural efficiency gain. Four-mile wells are performing at or above expectations with >10% cost savings vs. budget.

The 4-5 operated rig program and 135-165 gross operated well TIL guidance for 2026 suggest production growth in the mid-single digit range organically, with further bolt-on M&A optionality given the $2.16B liquidity position and moderate leverage.

The estimate revision trajectory is strong: current-quarter EPS revised up 141% in 90 days (from $1.33 to $3.20), full-year FY2026E up 140% to $16.03. Next year's estimate at $14.43 implies a modest decline, consistent with consensus expectations for some normalization in oil prices — but at current pricing this estimate appears too conservative by a wide margin.

AI-driven well optimization (99% of rod-lift wells covered, 25% improvement in rod pump run times) is difficult for smaller operators to replicate. The $160M in incremental run-rate FCF from efficiency improvements announced at Q4 2025 earnings provides a concrete runway for further margin expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $133.52, CHRD trades at:
- **~8.3x FY2026E EPS** ($16.03 consensus) — below peer median of 9.2x
- **~9.3x FY2027E EPS** ($14.43 consensus)
- **175.9x trailing P/E** — meaningless due to the goodwill impairment; normalized trailing EPS is substantially higher
- **~9.1% trailing FCF yield** on FY2025 FCF of $693M
- **PV-10 of $9.1B** vs. market cap of $7.6B = 0.83x PV-10 (discount to asset value)

Against peers: SM Energy trades at 3.3x, Matador at 7.0x, CRC at 10.8x, CNX at 9.2x. CHRD's 8.3x forward P/E is reasonable within this range, but the more relevant comparison is on FCF yield at current oil prices. At $100+ WTI with only 17% hedged, CHRD's potential FCF yield of 24-29% is among the highest in the E&P space because (a) the $64 WTI guidance base is so conservative, (b) the hedging book is minimal, providing near-full upside capture, and (c) the cost structure has been structurally improved through long-lateral conversion.

The analyst consensus price target of $161.61 (median $158.50) reflects a 21% upside from current levels. The range of $114-$227 is wide, reflecting commodity price uncertainty. At 9.2x the FY2026E EPS of $16.03, the stock would trade at $147. At 10x — a modest premium for the dominant basin position and capital return track record — the target is $160.

## Competitive Landscape

Chord is the largest pure-play Williston Basin operator. This concentration is both strength and vulnerability. The contiguous acreage position enables long-lateral drilling that smaller competitors cannot replicate without equivalent land positions. The 22% F&D cost reduction and 80% long-lateral conversion rate are evidence of this structural advantage.

Compared to diversified E&Ps (COP, DVN, EOG), Chord lacks geographic diversification but offers higher operational focus and arguably better capital discipline on a cumulative basis. The in-house marketing capabilities with pipeline and rail optionality provide pricing flexibility that single-basin operators often lack.

The XTO acquisition was well-timed — buying assets from ExxonMobil that was rationalizing its portfolio, at a price ($542M) that represents approximately 0.75 years of FCF at current oil prices. The Enerplus integration is complete, with the goodwill fully written off.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH)
With only 17% of 2026 oil production hedged (collars with $55-60 floors), CHRD has nearly full exposure to oil price movements. The collar floors provide catastrophic-scenario protection but limited upside capture. A return to $64 WTI would reduce FCF to the guided $700M — still a 9.2% yield. A sustained decline to $55 would compress FCF further and likely result in capex reductions and dividend cuts.

### Single-Basin Concentration (HIGH)
Substantially all production is in the Williston Basin. Regulatory changes, infrastructure disruptions, or weather events could impact the entire portfolio simultaneously. The XTO acquisition deepened this concentration rather than diversifying it.

### DAPL Pipeline Risk (MODERATE-HIGH)
The Dakota Access Pipeline faces regulatory uncertainty. The Army Corps of Engineers completed its final EIS in December 2025, with a formal decision expected. Three of five alternatives analyzed would require abandonment or reroute of the contested segment. Management has stated that DAPL cessation would cause Williston Basin crude prices to "weaken materially" before rail transport adapts. The duration of this potential basis blowout is uncertain but could last 6-18 months.

### Debt Trajectory (MODERATE)
Debt nearly doubled to $1.48B. Interest expense rose 42% to $80.2M. The 6.750% coupon on the 2033 notes is elevated. While leverage remains low (0.18x D/E), the trajectory warrants monitoring, particularly if oil prices retreat toward the $64 guidance level.

### Fort Berthold Tribal Jurisdiction (LOW-MODERATE)
The pending mineral rights dispute could affect operating costs on tribal lands. Resolution timeline is uncertain.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($175-$200)
Oil prices remain elevated ($100-115/Bbl) through 2026 due to the Hormuz blockade and dual supply compression from Iranian and Russian waiver non-renewals. At these prices, CHRD generates $1.8-2.2B in FCF, enabling accelerated buybacks (at current prices, the $952M remaining authorization would retire ~7M shares, or 12% of the float), potential dividend increases, and rapid deleveraging. DAPL receives favorable regulatory decision. Long-lateral efficiencies continue to improve, pushing F&D costs lower. The stock re-rates to 10-12x elevated earnings: 10x on $20+ EPS = $200. FCF yield compresses from 24%+ toward 12-15% as the market recognizes the earnings power.

### Bear Case ($85-95)
Oil prices retreat toward $65-75 as the Iran conflict de-escalates and OPEC+ increases production. FCF reverts to guided $700M. DAPL receives an adverse regulatory decision, widening the Williston Basin basis differential by $5-8/Bbl and reducing realizations. Variable dividends remain suspended; base dividend comes under pressure. The stock returns to its November 2025 trough range, trading at 7-8x normalized earnings. The 10% short interest exacerbates any selloff.

2. Oil prices fall below $70/Bbl for more than 60 consecutive days, signaling sustained demand destruction or an OPEC+ policy shift

## Conclusion

Chord Energy offers compelling value as a direct oil price exposure with structural cost advantages and a proven capital return track record. The stock trades at a discount to both its peer group (8.3x vs. 9.2x median forward P/E) and its own proved reserves (0.83x PV-10). At current oil prices above $100, the company is generating FCF at a rate that would return the entire market capitalization in approximately 3.5-4 years. The goodwill impairment cleaned the balance sheet, and the long-lateral drilling program provides ongoing margin improvement.

The risks are real — single-basin concentration, DAPL uncertainty, and the inherent cyclicality of E&P — but they are reflected in the discount valuation. Insider selling across multiple executives in March 2026 is a cautious signal, though the sales were modest relative to holdings and coincided with the stock's approach to 52-week highs. The 10% short interest adds short-squeeze potential on any positive catalyst but also reflects legitimate skepticism about commodity price sustainability.

At $133.52, CHRD is a **BUY** for investors seeking direct energy exposure in the current elevated-oil-price environment, with the understanding that position size should reflect single-basin risk and commodity price sensitivity. A 3-5% portfolio allocation is appropriate, with the option to add on any pullback toward $115-120 (the 200-day moving average area).
