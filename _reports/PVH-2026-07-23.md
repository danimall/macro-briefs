**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PVH Corp is a focused two-brand global apparel platform. Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein now generate more than 95% of revenue following the 2023 divestiture of the Heritage Brands intimates business. The company is cash-generative, throwing off $538M of free cash flow in FY2025 on modest $142M capex, and its headline FY2025 net income of $25M is deeply misleading: roughly 95% of the earnings collapse from $599M was a non-cash, discount-rate-driven $480M goodwill/intangible impairment plus $93M of restructuring and a mechanically-inflated 83% effective tax rate. Adjusting these out, pre-tax earnings power remains near $700M. Any screen keying off reported net margin (0.3%), trailing P/E, or the 1.78 Altman Z-score badly misjudges this business, which is investment-grade (BBB−/Baa3) and carrying a functioning balance sheet.

The problem is the demand curve, not the balance sheet. A Middle East conflict that began in March 2026 has hit EMEA, PVH's profit engine (17.5% IBIT margin, $749M of segment income). Q1 FY2026 revenue fell 2% in constant currency with EMEA down 5%, and management cut full-year guidance from "flat to slight increase" to "slight decrease," triggering a roughly 23% after-hours decline. Forward estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: next-quarter EPS was cut 12.3% over 90 days and next-year EPS 9.2%, even as the current quarter benefited from a mechanical +23.3% revision. The DTC/digital reset is working where PVH controls the experience (DTC +3%, digital +11% in Q1), but wholesale declined across every region and the company is a laggard versus peers with more balanced geographic mixes (Ralph Lauren +12% revenue, Burberry's turnaround gaining, Nike's dip beating).

We rate PVH a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The stock at $77.80 is optically inexpensive on adjusted earnings (~6.4x FY2026 consensus EPS of $12.09), but it sits in a structurally negative-alpha consumer-discretionary group at a live demand trough, carries a binary China "Unreliable Entity List" overhang that puts ~20% of profit at asymmetric risk, and offers no identifiable catalyst to force a re-rating before the EMEA drag runs its course. The score has declined from 5.9 in May to 4.7 currently, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend.

## Company Overview

PVH is one of the world's largest apparel companies, 140-plus years old and NYSE-listed for over a century. It has completed a pivot to a two-owned-brand model: Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein, both wholly owned, together produce more than 95% of revenue. More than 70% of revenue is generated outside the U.S., with EMEA the largest and most profitable region. Distribution spans wholesale (department stores, digital pure-plays, franchisees, distributors), roughly 1,350 company-operated freestanding stores, about 1,450 shop-in-shop/concession locations, owned digital commerce, and a high-margin Licensing segment.

Effective FY2025, PVH reorganized into region-focused segments: EMEA, Americas, APAC, and Licensing. The segment economics are highly uneven: EMEA delivered $749M of income before interest and taxes at a 17.5% margin, APAC $292M at 19.3%, and Americas $252M at 9.2%. This concentration matters because the region carrying the highest margins (EMEA) is also the epicenter of the current demand problem.

The strategic framework is the "PVH+ Plan," a multi-year effort to build Calvin Klein and Tommy into the sector's most desirable lifestyle brands. A structural lever is taking back licensed product categories from G-III (its largest licensee for CK/Tommy women's apparel in North America) as licenses expire through 2026, shifting revenue in-house at lower gross margins but with greater control over the brand experience.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has been flat-to-declining across four years once divestiture and FX noise are stripped out. FY2023 $9,024M fell to FY2024 $8,653M (down 4%, including the Heritage divestiture and a 53rd-week effect), then rose to FY2025 $8,950M (up 3%). The entire FY2025 increase was a euro translation tailwind of roughly $251M; underlying volume was flat-to-down.

Reported profitability collapsed in FY2025 for reasons that are almost entirely non-operational. Operating income fell from $772M (8.9% margin) to $231M (2.6%), and net income dropped from $599M to $25M. The driver was a $480M non-cash goodwill/intangible impairment in Q1 FY2025 that fully wrote off Americas ($8M) and APAC ($418M) goodwill plus $54M of Australia license rights. Management attributes the charge to a spike in discount rates (higher WACC), not operational deterioration. Because the impairment was non-deductible, the effective tax rate spiked to 83.3% from 15.2%, further depressing net income. Adding $93M of severance-heavy restructuring, adjusted pre-tax income was roughly $700M+, close to FY2024 levels.

Gross margin compressed 190 bps to 57.5%, split between roughly 80 bps of FY2025 tariffs, 50 bps from the lower-margin in-housing of G-III categories, and 60 bps from promotions, freight, and CK delivery-delay discounts. This margin structure has near-term headwind: management guides to a ~$195M gross tariff hit (~215 bps) in FY2026 assuming a 15% rate effective 2/24/2026, and the G-III take-backs are structurally dilutive to gross margin (~50 bps/year).

Cash flow and the balance sheet remain sound. Operating cash flow was $680M (down from $741M), and FCF was $538M against only $142M of capex — an FCF margin of ~6% and an FCF yield of roughly 15% on the current $3.6B market cap, which is the single most attractive fundamental datapoint here. Cash was $701M against total debt of ~$2.32B (long-term debt rose to $2,291M following a $500M 2030 note issuance to refinance the maturing 2025 notes). Approximately 80% of debt is fixed-rate, and euro-denominated senior notes plus cross-currency swaps provide a natural hedge against the euro goodwill exposure. D/E is 2.44, appropriate for a capital-light apparel model with steady cash generation. The 1.78 Altman Z-score ("distress zone") is a statistical artifact of the impairment-driven EBIT collapse, not a solvency signal: the current ratio is 1.52, operating cash flow exceeds net income, and the company is investment-grade. The €600M notes and €408M term loan maturing in 2027 are a refinancing watch item but manageable given the credit profile and FCF.

Capital allocation strongly favors buybacks: FY2025 saw $561M of repurchases (including a $500M accelerated share repurchase at an average $72.44/share) against only $7M of dividends, leaving $1.212B on the $5B authorization with ≥$300M planned for FY2026. The ASR was struck at ~$72–76, below today's price of $77.80, and the stock subsequently ran to $90–95 before falling back, so buyback timing has been reasonable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the story and the primary reason the score has fallen. Underlying revenue is flat-to-down, the FY2025 reported increase was entirely a euro tailwind, and Q1 FY2026 delivered a 2% constant-currency decline (Tommy −2%, CK −3%). EMEA fell 5% on the Middle East drag, Americas fell 2%, and only APAC grew (+6%, ex-China). Management cut full-year FY2026 guidance to a "slight decrease" and expects the EMEA hit to land fully in Q2 and H2.

The estimate revision data reinforces this. While the current quarter saw a +23.3% 90-day revision (a mechanical artifact of timing/adjustment), the more telling numbers are the forward periods: next-quarter EPS was cut 12.3% over 90 days and next-year EPS 9.2%. Current-year consensus of $12.09 is essentially flat over 90 days (−0.2%). The net read is negative earnings momentum in the periods that matter, the opposite of the accelerating-revision pattern that would justify leaning into growth.

There are two genuine bright spots. DTC grew 3% and digital grew 11% in Q1, evidence the brand-desirability reset is gaining traction where PVH controls the channel. And the company realized more than $200M of cost savings under its Growth Driver 5 program, providing an offset to demand weakness. But the wholesale channel declined across every region, and the earnings trajectory does not currently support a growth thesis. The earnings-surprise track record is strong (four consecutive beats, averaging ~13.7%), which gives some confidence that consensus is not overly optimistic, but beating a lowered bar is different from re-accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted earnings, PVH looks cheap. The stock trades at roughly 6.4x FY2026 consensus EPS of $12.09 and offers a ~15% FCF yield. The peer group in Apparel, Accessories & Luxury Goods trades at a median P/E of 16.7x (Columbia 21.2x, Kontoor 16.2x, Carter's 16.7x, G-III 12.5x, Capri 32.1x), so PVH sits at a substantial discount to the group on a normalized basis. The reported trailing P/E of 23.9x is meaningless because the trailing EPS of $0.52 reflects the impairment and inflated tax rate, not earnings power.

The discipline here is to recognize that cheapness alone is not a buy signal — it has been a reliable value-trap flag across this system's history, particularly in negative-alpha consumer sectors. PVH's discount reflects three real problems the market is pricing: negative underlying growth, an acute EMEA demand shock with no visibility on duration, and the binary China UEL overhang. A compressed multiple that reflects structural or acute impairment with no near-term recovery catalyst is a value trap, not a bargain, until the demand curve turns.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $70, high $128, mean $90.67, median $85.5, against the current $77.80. The mean implies ~17% upside, but the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, and the recommendation mix has shifted (a strong-sell appeared over the last two months). Because estimates are falling in the forward periods, I do not treat these targets as floors that consensus will chase upward. On balance, the valuation offers modest asymmetric upside if EMEA stabilizes, but that "if" is doing all the work, and the sector-alpha and cyclical-trough context argue against rewarding the discount. This warrants a below-neutral valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

PVH occupies a mid-tier "accessible premium" niche, above mass and below true luxury, anchored by two globally recognized brands with premium pricing power, a large international/DTC footprint, and a scaled, low-capital licensing business. Those are durable structural advantages. The competitive concern is trajectory, not level.

PVH is the laggard in a peer set that is doing better: Ralph Lauren beat on +12% revenue, Burberry's turnaround is gaining, and Nike's dip beat expectations, while PVH cited the Middle East/EMEA drag for a guidance cut. Some of this is region-specific bad luck, as PVH's outsized EMEA exposure is a structural headwind that competitors with more balanced mixes do not share as acutely, but the market reads relative underperformance as a competitive signal regardless of cause. The G-III license take-backs shift revenue in-house at lower margins and carry integration risk, wholesale customer concentration is rising (top-5 at 16.6%), and brand value is concentrated and personality-linked (Mr. Hilfiger remains Principal Designer; CK tied to Mr. Klein's legacy).

The DTC/digital momentum is the clearest evidence the brand strategy is working, but it is being outweighed by wholesale weakness and EMEA. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating on trajectory, consistent with the recent decline from 6.0 to 4.5 in this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

**China / MOFCOM "Unreliable Entity List."** This is the standout company-specific risk and is unique to PVH among U.S. apparel peers. In February 2025, China placed PVH on the UEL after finding it "violated normal market trading principles" in connection with supply-chain sourcing scrutiny. No measures have been imposed yet, but potential actions include fines, import/export restrictions, investment bans, and personnel entry denials. China is ~6% of revenue but ~20% of IBIT (ex-impairment), so the exposure is disproportionate to profit. A worst-case forced exit could trigger inventory write-downs, receivable issues, and further impairments. This is a genuine binary overhang: benign if China never acts, materially damaging if it does.

**EMEA/Middle East macro.** The most immediate earnings risk. Because EMEA is the profit engine, the damage flows disproportionately to income. Duration is unknowable.

**Tariffs.** The tariff hit is guided for FY2026, partially offset by sourcing/pricing mitigation. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that many tariffs were unconstitutional, followed by a Section 122 executive order reimposing them for 150 days, creates uncertainty on refunds (which guidance conservatively excludes) and future rates.

**Further impairment risk.** Goodwill ($2.02B) plus other intangibles ($3.10B) equal 44% of total assets. Because FY2025 charges were discount-rate-driven, sustained high rates or a lower stock price could trigger more. Americas/APAC goodwill is already zero, so remaining risk sits in tradenames and EMEA.

**FX translation.** More than 70% of revenue is non-USD, dominated by the euro. A reversal of the euro translation tailwind would pressure reported results.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed rather than acutely bearish. Near-term ATM IV is 46.0% against 47.8% one-year historical volatility, essentially fair (−1.8% spread), and IV percentile within the current chain is low at 11%, suggesting the near-dated market is not pricing an imminent shock. The term structure is in contango (46.0% near, 53.4% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), though the three- and six-month tenors show negative skew (−4.9% and −5.2%), consistent with hedging demand further out. The most notable move is the put/call open-interest ratio jumping from 1.39 to 2.48 over the past month while the stock rose 4.8%, indicating a build in put positioning. Short interest is elevated at 15.3% of float with 3.4 days to cover — institutional bearishness that is not extreme enough to create a strong squeeze setup but confirms the cautious tone. The 1-month implied move of ±10.3% is wide, reflecting the market's uncertainty around the next earnings print and EMEA trajectory.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The market is over-penalizing a temporary, region-specific demand shock. Underlying earnings power is ~$700M+ pre-tax, FCF is $538M (~15% yield), and the balance sheet is investment-grade with the "distress" signal being a pure accounting artifact. If the Middle East conflict de-escalates and EMEA stabilizes, reported EPS reverts toward the $12+ range, the multiple re-rates from ~6.4x toward the peer median of ~16x, and buybacks (≥$300M planned, ~$1.2B remaining) compound per-share value on a shrunken share count. The DTC/digital reset (+11% digital) validates the brand strategy, APAC ex-China is growing, and the new LVMH/Sephora-pedigree CFO adds credibility. Bull case ~$115 (roughly 9.5x on recovering ~$12 EPS, plus buyback accretion — near the high end of the analyst range at $128).

**Bear Case.** The demand shock is not transitory: EMEA weakness persists, wholesale continues to decline across regions, tariff drag (~215 bps) and G-III margin dilution compress profitability, and forward estimates keep falling (next-year already cut 9.2%). China acts on the UEL listing, wiping out a meaningful slice of the ~20% of profit at risk and triggering fresh impairments. The stock is a value trap in a negative-alpha sector at a cyclical trough with no catalyst. Bear case ~$60 (near the 52-week low of $59.60 and just below the analyst floor of $70), roughly 23% downside.

The base case sits in between: EMEA stays soft for two to three more quarters, FY2026 revenue posts the guided slight decline, EPS lands near $12, and the stock trades in a $75–90 range absent a resolution to either the Middle East or China overhangs. This is a roughly balanced risk/reward, which is why the recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY or AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 FY2026 earnings report (which will show the fuller EMEA impact management flagged) and any development on the China UEL listing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Constant-currency revenue returns to flat-or-positive, with EMEA declines narrowing to low single digits or turning positive, confirming the Middle East drag is abating.
- Forward-year (next-year) EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive over a 90-day window, reversing the current −9.2% trend.
- Definitive resolution of the China UEL listing without material measures imposed, removing the binary tail — or the stock compressing to ~$60 (near the low, ~5x adjusted EPS) where the value case becomes asymmetric even with the overhangs intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- China imposes concrete UEL measures (import/export restrictions, investment bans), threatening the ~20% profit contribution and risking further impairments.
- FY2026 guidance is cut again beyond "slight decrease," or wholesale declines accelerate into double digits.
- A fresh goodwill/intangible impairment is announced, or the FY2026 buyback plan is suspended (a signal management sees deeper deterioration).

## Conclusion

PVH is a fundamentally sound, cash-generative apparel platform whose reported financials materially understate its earnings power: the $25M net income and 1.78 Altman Z-score are accounting artifacts of a non-cash impairment, not signs of distress, and $538M of FCF against a $3.6B market cap is attractive. On adjusted earnings the stock is cheap versus a peer median of 16.7x. But cheapness in a negative-alpha consumer-discretionary group at a live demand trough is a warning, not a signal, and the forward evidence is deteriorating: constant-currency revenue is declining, EMEA is under an acute Middle East drag with no visibility on duration, forward-year estimates are being cut, and the China UEL listing is an unresolved binary that puts ~20% of profit at asymmetric risk.

The score has declined from 5.9 in May to 4.7 now, and the new data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it: the valuation, competitive position, and management dimensions have all softened on real fundamental grounds, not merely price action. The risk/reward is balanced: meaningful upside if EMEA stabilizes and China stays benign, meaningful downside if either sours. Absent a catalyst to force the re-rating, we recommend **HOLD** (uncertain rationale). We would turn constructive on evidence of EMEA stabilization plus a turn in forward estimates, or on a pullback toward $60 where the value case overwhelms the overhangs. We would turn negative on concrete China measures or a second guidance cut.
