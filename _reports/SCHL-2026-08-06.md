## Executive Summary

Scholastic spent FY2026 rebuilding its capital structure rather than its income statement. It sold its SoHo headquarters and Jefferson City distribution center for $481.0M gross ($452.4M net), used the proceeds to repay $175.0M of revolver borrowings and repurchase $268.6M of stock at an average $36.61, and cut shares outstanding 25% from 25.0M to 18.7M. Funded debt fell from $256.2M to $80.5M and the company ended the year with a $48.9M net cash position on a funded-debt basis. On July 22, 2026 the board raised the quarterly dividend 25% to $0.25. On the metrics management controls directly, execution was good: adjusted operating income rose 32% to $47.1M and, on a pro forma sale-leaseback basis, 77% to $35.3M.

The operating business did not cooperate. Revenue fell 2.7% to $1,581.9M, with Education down 13.6% to $267.6M (a 23.8% decline over two years), consolidated Trade down 6% for the year and 20% in Q4, and Book Clubs down 11%. Only Book Fairs grew — up 5% to $576.0M with adjusted segment margin expanding to 14.9% from 13.6% — and it now carries the company: Children's Book Publishing & Distribution generated $174.9M of Adjusted EBITDA against $151.5M for the consolidated group, because $77.2M is consumed by unallocated corporate cost. Q4 revenue of $476.1M missed consensus, the third consecutive quarter of soft top-line, and the stock fell from $46.44 to $40.63 over three sessions.

The most important analytical correction is that FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $135-145M is not a decline. The sale-leaseback carries a full-year cost of $33.6M to Adjusted EBITDA, of which only $14.5M landed in FY2026; against the disclosed pro forma FY2026 base of $132.4M, guidance implies +2% to +9.5%. Consensus has already absorbed this: current-year EPS estimates were cut 40.1% in 90 days to $1.60 and next-year 18.1% to $2.42. So the lease drag is priced, and what remains unpriced is whether Education stabilizes and whether Book Fairs can carry 2-4% consolidated revenue growth. Neither the Q4 exit rate nor the estimate trend supports that yet. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Scholastic is a children's book publisher whose durable asset is distribution, not catalog. It is the leading operator of school-based book fairs and book clubs in the United States, serving schools in all 50 states, delivering book fair inventory principally by a fleet of company-owned and leased vehicles, and employing 1,865 people in Book Fairs warehouses alone out of ~6,905 total headcount.

FY2026 segment revenue: Children's Book Publishing & Distribution $964.2M (61.0%), International $277.2M (17.5%), Education $267.6M (16.9%), Entertainment $65.7M (4.2%), and Overhead (headquarters rental income) $7.2M. Within CBPD, Book Fairs was $576.0M (+5%), Book Clubs $57.1M (-11%) and consolidated Trade $331.1M (-6%).

Owned franchises include Harry Potter (U.S. trade rights), The Hunger Games, Dog Man, Captain Underpants, The Baby-Sitters Club, Wings of Fire, I Survived, Goosebumps and Clifford; Peppa Pig and Pokémon are licensed in, not owned. Entertainment consists of 9 Story Media Group (acquired June 2024 for $193.7M) and Scholastic Entertainment, with a new adult/teen animation label, Bad Pencil, launched June 2026.

The competitive disclosure that matters most sits in Item 1: in U.S. school book fairs, Scholastic competes against regional and local operators, other school fundraising activities, bookstores, "as well as one other competitor operating on a national level." The largest and only growing profit pool in the company operates in a national near-duopoly with physical and relational barriers — warehouses, trucks, regional reps, PTO relationships, and a book fair incentive credit program that accounted for $121.9M of the $216.6M contract liability balance at February 28, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2026 reported results

| | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,581.9M | $1,625.5M (-2.7%) |
| Operating income | $15.2M (0.96% margin) | $15.8M |
| Loss on sale of investments | ($17.2M) | — |
| Gain on sale-leaseback | $99.7M | — |
| Net income | $56.7M | ($1.9M) |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $2.34 | ($0.07) |
| Adjusted operating income | $47.1M (+32%) | $35.8M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $151.5M (+4%) | $145.4M |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $1.87 | $0.48 |
| Weighted-average diluted shares | 24.222M | 27.907M |

GAAP EPS of $2.34 is not a measure of earning power; it is dominated by the $99.7M sale-leaseback gain net of a $17.2M loss on disposal of a 26.2% interest in a UK children's publisher. Trailing P/E of 18.2x at $42.48 is therefore not usable. Adjusted EBITDA of $151.5M overstates cash economics because it excludes amortization of capitalized prepublication and film/TV production costs, which are genuine recurring costs of remaining in business. The bridge from pro forma Adjusted EBITDA of $132.4M to pro forma adjusted operating income of $35.3M implies $97.1M of D&A plus content amortization, against $66.3M of FY2026 cash spend ($48.4M PP&E, $17.9M prepublication). That $31M gap is why EV/EBITDA looks cheap while forward P/E does not.

### Segment economics

CBPD produced $143.7M of adjusted operating income (14.9% margin, up from 13.6%) and $174.9M of Adjusted EBITDA. Education swung to a $4.1M reported operating loss and $0.2M adjusted (from $6.9M), with Adjusted EBITDA falling to $25.1M from $31.7M. Entertainment lost $16.1M reported ($9.3M adjusted) including a $5.2M impairment of programs in development, its second consecutive loss year. International earned a 2.6% adjusted operating margin and was flat only because FX contributed $6.3M — roughly -3% in constant currency. Unallocated Overhead consumed $94.6M of adjusted operating loss, 6.0% of revenue, and worsened year over year partly because $4.0M of headquarters rental income disappeared.

One segment representing 61% of revenue earns everything, and half of it is absorbed by corporate cost. That makes corporate cost the largest lever management controls, and it means small revenue declines translate into large percentage swings in consolidated operating income.

### The sale-leaseback and what it actually cost

Closed December 17, 2025: SoHo headquarters for $386.0M (partial leaseback, 15 years, $11.7M initial rent escalating ~4%/yr) and the Jefferson City distribution center for $95.0M (20 years, $6.9M initial rent, CPI-linked 1-4%). Combined lease cost ~$23.7M annually. Net carrying value of assets sold was $334.2M.

Two disclosures deserve weight. First, both leases were discounted at an incremental borrowing rate of **10.4%**, with the company citing "its credit profile, including its higher leverage position at the time." Weighted-average operating lease term rose from 5.3 to 12.3 years and the weighted-average discount rate from 5.6% to 8.6%. Second, the FY2026 contractual obligations table shows $547.3M of undiscounted operating lease payments (year 1 $49.4M; beyond year 5 $335.5M), plus $17.2M finance leases, $37.6M royalty advance commitments, $17.1M film obligations and $23.5M pension/postretirement.

Adjusted leverage on my calculation: $80.5M borrowings + ~$275M lease present value + $14.4M finance leases + $17.1M film obligations ≈ $387M gross, less $134.9M cash ≈ **$252M adjusted net debt**, or 1.9x pro forma Adjusted EBITDA of $132.4M and 1.6x on an EBITDAR basis ($156.1M). Moderate. But the reported "$48.9M net cash position" understates fixed charges materially, and the company itself added a new "Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Capital Structure" section to Item 1A in the year it reported net cash, which is the honest framing.

### Cash flow — the definitional trap

The company reported FY2026 free cash flow of $436.0M against $29.2M prior year. That figure only reconciles by including asset-sale proceeds: $50.9M operating cash flow + $452.4M net proceeds − ~$67M of capex and prepublication ≈ $436M. On a conventional definition FY2026 FCF was **-$15.4M**, or roughly **+$26.0M** normalizing for the $41.4M of higher net tax payments management attributes largely to the gain. Operating cash flow fell from $124.2M to $50.9M.

FY2027 guidance of $35-40M FCF is the relevant run-rate: at the $37.5M midpoint, a **4.7% FCF yield** on $777M of equity value. The $1.00 annualized dividend consumes ~$18.3M, or 49% of that.

### Forward earnings — correcting a widely-circulated estimate

Working from management's own pro forma bridge: FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $140M (midpoint) less ~$97M of D&A and content amortization gives ~$43M of adjusted operating income; less ~$5-8M net interest and taxed at 27% on 18.3M shares yields **roughly $1.30-1.60 of adjusted EPS**. Some published sensitivities double-subtract the ~$14.6M of eliminated building depreciation (it is already excluded from the pro forma bridge) and arrive near $2.15; that figure is too high. My range reconciles closely with current-year consensus of $1.60: FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.87 restated onto the reduced ~18.3M share base is ~$2.48, less roughly $0.76 per share of after-tax incremental lease drag, gives $1.72, and consensus sits slightly below that on modest operating shortfall.

The 40.1% current-year cut is mechanical absorption of a disclosed lease drag plus a soft Q4, not a discovery of new deterioration.

### Screening artifacts to disregard

The Altman Z-Score of 1.91 is driven by EBIT/assets of 0.009 on $15.2M of GAAP operating income that absorbed $31.9M of one-time items (including $16.4M severance) and $10.9M of impairments; on adjusted operating income the term triples. A 25% share retirement also mechanically depresses the Z-Score. A reported 2.3 debt-to-equity ratio is not reproducible: total liabilities of $977.3M ÷ $750.8M equity = 1.30, and interest-bearing debt ÷ equity = 0.11. The screens flag a profitability problem, not a solvency problem.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance: revenue +2% to 4%, Adjusted EBITDA $135-145M, FCF $35-40M. Against the $132.4M pro forma FY2026 base, the EBITDA range is +2% to +9.5%.

The pipeline is unusually concrete: the next Dog Man title in November, publishing tied to the new HBO Harry Potter series (Scholastic holds U.S. trade rights and publishes into the release; it is not a producer), new Baby-Sitters Club, Wings of Fire and I Survived titles, a Clifford animated series on PBS KIDS in 2027, and Bad Pencil's first slate. FY2026 also lapped a hard comparison: *Sunrise on the Reaping* released in Q4 FY2025, driving Q4 FY2026 Trade down 20%. Entertainment inflected in Q4 to +42% revenue and a small positive operating result.

Against that, three of four segments are shrinking and the estimate trend is unambiguous:

| Period | Current | 90d ago | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter (Q1 FY27) | ($3.40) | ($3.29) | -3.2% |
| Next quarter | $3.49 | $3.65 | -4.4% |
| Current year (FY27) | $1.60 | $2.67 | **-40.1%** |
| Next year (FY28) | $2.42 | $2.95 | **-18.1%** |

Only two analysts cover the name, so these figures carry limited information density, but the direction is consistent across all four periods. The Q1 FY2027 estimate of -$3.40 against a prior-year actual of -$2.52 shows the lease drag concentrating in the seasonally weakest quarter (schools closed June-August) alongside the eliminated rental income.

Education is the growth question. Revenue fell from $351.2M (FY2024) to $309.8M to $267.6M, and Q4 was still -13%. The FY2026 10-K added a dedicated risk factor on "restructuring of federal education programs and agencies, including significant changes to or the potential elimination of the U.S. Department of Education," referencing Title I and noting no assurance state or local funding offsets reductions. The "Family and Community" business is explicitly funded by state appropriations. A plausible benign reading is that ESSER pandemic-relief expiration pulled forward sector-wide supplemental purchases and FY2025-26 is an air pocket; that context is not verified in any Scholastic filing and should not be assumed. Management cites "improving trends"; the -13% exit rate does not corroborate it.

The 10-K also added an extensive generative-AI risk factor covering competitive displacement in children's content, LLM-based tutoring diverting district literacy budgets, and copyright exposure running both ways — including the disclosure that Scholastic's portfolio "may have been used, without authorization, in training datasets," framing IP as a contingent asset as well as a risk. Neither prong is quantifiable today, but both sit on the two segments already declining.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.48 with ~18.3M shares:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity value | ~$777M |
| EV (net cash basis, $48.9M) | ~$728M |
| EV including ~$275M capitalized leases | ~$1,003M |
| EV / pro forma FY2026 Adj. EBITDA ($132.4M) | 5.5x |
| EV / guided FY2027 Adj. EBITDA ($140M) | **5.2x** |
| EV-with-leases / EBITDAR ($156.1M) | 6.4x |
| Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus $1.60 | **26.6x** |
| P/E on FY2028 consensus $2.42 | 17.6x |
| FCF yield on guided $37.5M | 4.8% |
| Dividend yield ($1.00 annualized) | 2.35% |
| Remaining buyback authorization ($183.0M) | 23.5% of equity value |

Peer medians in publishing sit at 31.8x P/E (NYT 31.8x, NWSA 33.4x, IAC 24.5x, WLY 15.5x), but the comparison is close to useless — none of these is a physical school-channel distributor with capitalized content amortization of $97M against $66M of cash content spend. Wiley at 15.5x with a 3.2% yield is the only structurally comparable name, and Scholastic trades at a premium to it on forward EPS.

The valuation case reduces to which denominator is honest. On EV/EBITDA, 5.2x is low for a business with a national near-duopoly in its largest profit pool. On forward adjusted EPS, 26.6x is expensive for 2-4% revenue growth. The reconciling metric is free cash flow, which at 4.8% guided yield says the stock is fairly valued — cheaper than its P/E implies, more expensive than its EBITDA multiple implies. My read: fair, not cheap.

Two specific offsets. The $183.0M remaining authorization is 23.5% of market cap and management has demonstrated it will use it, but with the real estate sold, funding it now requires either the $37.5M of guided FCF or re-drawing the revolver at SOFR+1.75% in a higher-for-longer rate environment. And the sole analyst covering the name carries a $42.00 target, below the current price, with a Hold rating; there is no consensus target above spot to re-rate toward.

Scholastic is cheap on EBITDA precisely because 40% of revenue faces demand resets (Education funding, Trade retail concentration and self-publishing, Book Clubs' paper-order-form model) and because the Class A control structure removes takeover optionality: Common holders elect one-fifth of the board and generally have no vote on mergers or asset sales. The $481M asset sale required no Common-holder vote; neither would a sale of the company. A strategic acquirer must negotiate with the Estate, not the market. That is a permanent discount, not a mispricing awaiting correction.

## Competitive Landscape

**Book Fairs — strengthening.** A national near-duopoly with physical barriers: warehouse network, owned/leased truck fleet, regional sales organization, school and PTO relationships, and the incentive credit program that both retains schools and creates a $121.9M deferred obligation. Grew 5% for the year and 5% in Q4 with segment adjusted margin expanding 130bp. This is the asset that justifies owning the equity at all.

**Trade — structurally weak and concentrated.** Top five U.S. trade customers are ~74% of U.S. trade and ~15% of total revenue, with no evidence of improvement. Revenue -6% for the year, -20% in Q4 against the *Sunrise on the Reaping* comparison. Mix shifting toward proprietary channels does not reduce the disclosed concentration.

**Education — demand reset, not share loss.** -23.8% over two years with a new federal-funding risk factor and state-funded direct-to-home business. $213.9M of segment assets at February 28, 2026 against $199.4M of consolidated goodwill across seven reporting units with $39.6M of accumulated historical impairment; continued decline is the most plausible impairment trigger.

**Entertainment — unproven.** 9 Story cost $193.7M including $64.2M of non-deductible goodwill and a $31.9M tax credit receivable. Two years in: reported operating losses of $12.1M and $16.1M. Scholastic holds 100% of economics but only 25% of voting shares with a majority-Canadian board, a structure required to preserve Canadian content credits. The Q4 inflection to +42% revenue and a small profit is the first positive datapoint.

**International — low-margin, FX-flattered.** 2.6% adjusted operating margin; flat revenue only because of a $6.3M FX tailwind.

Vertical integration between owned IP and owned school distribution is real and rare. It has not produced consolidated operating leverage: 61% of revenue generates $174.9M of segment Adjusted EBITDA, of which $77.2M is consumed by unallocated corporate cost.

## Risk Assessment

**Education impairment.** The most likely near-term negative headline. Segment assets $213.9M, revenue -13% at the exit rate, adjusted operating income at breakeven.

**Federal and state education funding.** A new, specific risk factor referencing potential elimination of the Department of Education and Title I changes. Not quantifiable, directly relevant to 17% of revenue.

**Generative AI.** Two prongs on the already-weak segments: cheaper content production amplifying self-publishing pressure on Trade, and LLM tutoring diverting district literacy budgets from Education.

**Consumer discretionary exposure.** Book fair revenue is parent discretionary spending inside schools, making it exposed to household budget compression, though the research materials do not quantify a specific macro linkage. [unverified — no macro data on mortgage rates or a H2 2026 consumer slowdown appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Governance concentration.** The Estate of Richard Robinson beneficially owns a majority of Class A and can elect up to four-fifths of the board; Iole Lucchese controls that vote as special executor while simultaneously serving as Chair, EVP, Chief Strategy Officer and President of Scholastic Entertainment, the segment that lost $16.1M. Minority holders bear those losses with no voting remedy and no realistic activist path. The five-year pay-versus-performance table through FY2025 shows indexed TSR of 66.04 against a peer index of 371.54.

**Succession and unresolved disclosures.** The CEO is 74 and received a large July 21, 2026 grant (46,425 shares plus 21,533 PSUs), consistent with continued tenure. But a "Letter Agreement dated January 29, 2026 between Peter Warwick and the Company" was filed as a compensatory arrangement in the Q3 10-Q and its contents are unknown; a December 22, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K is also unretrieved. Both are material to succession risk and unresolved.

**An unexplained accounting revision.** The nine-month sale-leaseback gain through February 28, 2026 was $118.2M; the full-year figure is $99.7M, implying a **-$18.5M revision in Q4**. Candidate explanations include final proration adjustments, additional transaction costs, or a re-measurement of lease classification. No explanatory note has been located. This is a question mark on disclosure quality that should be resolved before any upgrade, without inferring intent.

**Offsetting upside risk.** Approximately $9.0M of tariff payments are subject to the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling with no receivable recognized. Modest, unrecognized.

**Short interest.** 15.7% of float, 4.6 days to cover, 1.67M shares short. Meaningful bearish institutional positioning that also creates two-sided risk on any Education stabilization print.

### Options Market Signal

The chain carries only 33 contracts across three expirations and the prior snapshot recorded an ATM IV of 1040.7%, so these readings are low-confidence and should not drive positioning. Taking them at face value: near-term ATM IV of 75.9% against 36.3% one-year historical vol, an 85th-percentile IV reading within the chain, backwardation (75.9% at 14 days versus 49.1% at six months), a ±9.5% implied move on the August 21 expiry and a 17.8% put/call skew described as elevated put demand. Put/call open interest of 0.35 is call-tilted, which conflicts with the skew reading.

There is no scheduled catalyst inside 14 days; Q1 FY2027 reports in late September. Rich near-term vol with no identified event, on a chain this thin, is more plausibly a liquidity artifact than informed positioning. The one usable observation is that longer-dated skew turns negative (-4.7% at three months, -2.3% at six months), which does not corroborate a bearish institutional stance despite 15.7% short interest. I am not adjusting the fundamental view on this data.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $55 (+29%)

Book Fairs compounds at 5% with margin expanding past 15%; Education stabilizes as the federal funding air pocket laps, taking segment revenue to flat and Adjusted EBITDA back toward $30M; Entertainment sustains its Q4 inflection on Bad Pencil and the Clifford PBS series; the Dog Man November release and HBO-linked Harry Potter publishing deliver revenue at the +4% top of guidance. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA lands at $145M, and the market re-rates a self-funding, net-cash, duopoly-protected distributor from 5.2x to 6.5x EV/EBITDA while the $183M authorization retires another 8-10% of shares. Equity value ~$992M, ~$55 per share on a further-reduced count.

### Bear Case — $32 (-25%)

Education declines another 10-13%, triggering a goodwill or intangible impairment against $213.9M of segment assets and confirming a permanent step-down rather than a cycle. Trade concentration bites as the top five accounts reduce shelf commitments. A softening consumer compresses book fair per-fair revenue, turning the one growing channel flat. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA comes in at $120M, below the guided floor, and the multiple compresses to 4.5x on a business that has now guided down once. Fixed charges — $49.4M of year-one lease payments plus $18.3M of dividends — leave no repurchase capacity without re-levering. Equity value ~$589M, ~$32 per share.

### Base Case — $45 (+5.9%)

Revenue grows 2%, Adjusted EBITDA lands mid-range at $140M, FCF at $37.5M, and the stock holds 5.5x EV/EBITDA and roughly a 4.5% FCF yield. Buybacks retire 3-4% of shares. Three independent methods converge here: 5.5x on $140M plus $48.9M net cash on 18.3M shares = $44.75; a 4.8% FCF yield on $37.5M = $45.5; 18x FY2028 consensus of $2.42 = $43.6.

Base-case upside of 5.9% against bear-case downside of 25% is unfavorable asymmetry. That is the recommendation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment point:** Q1 FY2027 results, late September 2026. This is the seasonally largest loss quarter (consensus -$3.40 versus -$2.52 prior year) and will be the first clean quarter with the full lease structure in place. The Education revenue line and the Q2 guidance commentary around the November Dog Man release are the two datapoints that matter.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Education segment revenue at or above flat year over year for two consecutive quarters (Q1 and Q2 FY2027), reversing a 23.8% two-year decline and a -13% Q4 exit rate.
2. Price toward $34-36 — at or below the FY2026 average repurchase price of $36.61 — which would put EV at under 4.4x guided FY2027 EBITDA and lift the guided FCF yield above 5.7%.
3. Disclosure resolving the -$18.5M Q4 sale-leaseback gain revision and the January 29, 2026 CEO letter agreement, with a named succession plan or clear multi-year extension.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge in Education, or a third consecutive quarter of Education revenue decline exceeding 10%.
2. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the $135M floor, or revenue guidance cut below +2%.
3. Revolver drawings above ~$200M to fund repurchases, which would push lease-adjusted net debt above roughly 2.7x EBITDAR and convert the dividend from 49% of FCF into a constrained obligation.

**Horizon:** 12 months. The transformation catalysts are behind the stock; the revenue evidence takes two to three quarters to accumulate.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on Scholastic, and the reasoning has changed even as the rating has not. The prior holds were premised on waiting for a balance-sheet transformation to execute. It executed well. Management monetized $481M of real estate, retired 25% of the equity at an average $36.61 (a 21% discount to today's price), cut funded debt to $80.5M, raised the dividend 25%, and delivered 32% adjusted operating income growth on declining revenue. On the pro forma basis management disclosed, FY2027 EBITDA guidance is growth, and screens showing a 4-11% decline against the reported $151.5M are wrong.

What has not changed is the operating business. Three of four segments shrank in FY2026. Education has fallen 23.8% in two years with a new federal-funding risk factor attached and had not stabilized by year-end. Consolidated revenue missed in each of the last two reported quarters. The self-help levers are largely spent: the real estate is sold, $16.4M of severance is taken, and further buybacks at the $183M authorized scale require re-levering the revolver into a higher-for-longer rate path. The overall score declines modestly from 5.0 to 4.85, driven by growth prospects and risk factors rather than by the post-earnings price decline; the specific fundamental deteriorations are the Education exit rate, the 40.1% current-year consensus cut, and the unexplained gain revision.

At $42.48 the stock is 5.2x EV to guided FY2027 EBITDA, 26.6x current-year adjusted EPS, and a 4.8% guided FCF yield. Free cash flow is the honest denominator and it prices the equity as fair. The Class A control structure removes the takeover option that would otherwise close the gap between the EBITDA multiple and the earnings multiple. Existing holders have a reasonable 2.35% yield and a company buying its own stock below the current price. New capital should wait for either an Education inflection or a price in the mid-$30s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the "consumer discretionary" risk was materially overstated with macro figures (mortgage rates at a one-year high, an H2 2026 consumer slowdown) that appear nowhere in the sources and have been demoted to `[unverified]`; the FCF yield was inconsistently stated (4.7% vs. 4.8%) — $37.5M/$777M ≈ 4.8%; and the average-repurchase-to-current-price discount is ~21% ($36.61 vs. $42.48), not 16%.
