## Executive Summary

Talos Energy reported a Q2 2026 that looks, on the surface, like an inflection: revenue of $664.8M (+56.5% versus the 10-Q's $424.7M prior-year base), GAAP diluted EPS of $0.88, Adjusted EBITDA of $402.4M, and a non-GAAP result of $0.57 against a $0.30 consensus — a 92.7% beat. The quarter was produced by a realized oil price of $99.47/Bbl driven by the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption. Sell-side crude forecasts were already being cut through late June and early July on faster-than-expected transit normalization, and WTI traded materially below Q2 levels by late July. The Q2 print describes an environment that no longer exists in the same form. Anyone annualizing H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $695.8M into a 2.3x EV/EBITDA multiple is capitalizing a price spike.

The more durable analytical problem sits in the reserve tables. Proved reserves fell from 194,242 MBoe to 174,693 MBoe during 2025 while the company produced 34,534 MBoe, implying total additions of 14,985 MBoe and a reserve replacement ratio of 43.4% — and that figure *includes* acquisitions. Reserve life is 5.06 years. PV-10 fell 24% to $3,189M. Asset retirement obligations reached $1,394.1M at June 30, 2026, against a $2.40B equity value, and Talos has contractually committed to minimum P&A spending of $90M/yr for 2026-2028 and $45M/yr for 2029-2030 plus $251.5M of surety collateral funding under the November 2025 Cash Flow Support Agreements. Roughly $565M of that lands in 2026-2030, about 24% of market capitalization, and none of it produces a barrel. On enterprise value of $3,247M ($2,399M equity + $1,425M pro-forma debt − $577.6M cash), the stock is 2.71x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $1,197.6M and roughly 1.02x PV-10. PV-10 already deducts abandonment costs; the EBITDA multiple does not. That reconciles most of the apparent cheapness.

The pending acquisition of 50% of Shell's Coulomb field and 25% of the Shell-operated Na Kika platform complex is the near-term swing factor and it has genuinely improved: BP waived its preferential purchase right effective July 20, 2026, removing the scenario in which Talos ended up with Coulomb alone. The assets are high-margin (16 MBoe/d, ~77% oil), and management expects $407.5-457.5M of cash at closing against an $850M unadjusted price. But the acquired reserve life of 3.94 years is shorter than Talos's own, the consideration includes a price-based upside-sharing arrangement paid to Shell whose mechanics are not disclosed, and no figure has been published for the incremental decommissioning liability Talos assumes — arguably the single most important missing number in the transaction. We maintain HOLD. The scores have drifted lower across six prior reviews (5.7 in April to 4.7 in July); this review is essentially flat at 4.6, with the acquisition de-risking and maturity extension offsetting the commodity rollover, +$175M of incremental principal, and new frontier-country exposure.

## Company Overview

Talos is a pure-play offshore E&P with essentially all production and reserves in the U.S. Gulf of America. As of December 31, 2025, Deepwater accounted for 159,697 MBoe (91%) of proved reserves and 31,047 MBoe (90%) of FY2025 production; Shelf and Gulf Coast accounted for the remainder. The portfolio is 75% oil by reserves, 78% proved developed, and 81% operated, with 286 gross / 189.9 net productive wells across 752,424 net U.S. acres and roughly 700 employees.

Asset quality is genuinely bifurcated. Katmai — acquired in the March 2024 QuarterNorth transaction and individually more than 15% of total proved reserves — carried FY2025 lease operating expense of $3.95/Boe against a company-wide $15.83/Boe. At the other end, on July 15, 2026 Talos divested a subsidiary holding non-core, gas-weighted, largely non-operated Shelf and Gulf Coast properties for total consideration of approximately $22.6M "inclusive of liabilities divested and $31.5M cash paid to the buyer." Talos paid to transfer those properties. For the marginal Shelf barrel, the present value of decommissioning exceeds the present value of production — a market-tested data point that is useful for valuing the remaining Shelf ARO, though a negative-price disposal can also simply be an efficient way to settle a recorded liability at a discount.

The Mexico position has been monetized rather than developed. After selling 49.9% of Talos Mexico to a Grupo Carso subsidiary in 2023 and a further 30.1% in March 2026, Talos retains 20.0% of an entity holding 17.35% of the unitized Zama field — a look-through interest of roughly 3.5% gross, with operatorship transferred to Harbour Energy. What remains material is $83.0M of contingent payments receivable on Zama first oil, about 3.5% of market capitalization.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Revenue $1.78B (from $1.97B), net loss $(494.3)M driven by a $454.5M non-cash full-cost ceiling-test impairment plus a $28.8M deferred-tax valuation allowance, Adjusted EBITDA $1,197.6M, operating cash flow $935.8M, cash capex $481.9M. Simple free cash flow of $453.9M; management's proxy-disclosed "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" was $387M on a company-specific definition (2024: $447M).

**H1 2026.** Revenue $1,137.1M (+21.3%), net loss $(106.5)M or $(0.64) per share, Adjusted EBITDA $695.8M, $145.0M of impairment all in Q1. Deriving Q1 by subtraction: revenue $472.3M, net loss $(256.2)M, Adjusted EBITDA $293.4M — the Q1 loss reflecting the $145.0M impairment plus an implied $(151.1)M unrealized derivative mark, both non-cash.

Two things in the cash flow statement matter more than the headline. First, **H1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $474.6M from $619.9M despite revenue rising 21%**. The 10-Q attributes the gap to a $(147.6)M working-capital swing and $96.6M of cash paid on derivative settlements. The working-capital drag is most plausibly a receivables build at a much higher June 30 realized price and should largely reverse as prices normalize, though the disclosure does not break out components and payment timing on accrued liabilities (including a $14.3M litigation settlement) could contribute.

Second, **the hedge book is a real, recurring cost at elevated prices**. Q2 realized $77.95/Boe excluding derivatives versus $69.25/Boe including realized derivatives — a drag of $8.70/Boe, or 11.2% of unit revenue. The H2 2026 book is 3,842 Bbl/d of swaps at $65.03 plus 20,326 Bbl/d of collars at $60.90/$72.83, roughly 35% of oil volumes against Q2 oil production of 68.6 MBopd. At $85 realized, H2 settlement losses compute to roughly $60M; at $75, roughly $15M. The drag decays quickly as prices mean-revert, which partially offsets lower revenue — but the $72.83 collar ceiling means Talos captures little of any renewed spike on that third of its oil.

**Unit costs are inflating.** LOE rose 13.1% year over year to $18.25/Boe in Q2 ($17.23 for H1 versus $15.07), with the workover component of LOE rising to $29.3M in H1 2026 from $5.5M in H1 2025, substantially the Genovesa safety-valve repair at Galapagos. Genovesa returned online late in Q2, so part of this should recede. The DD&A rate fell 15%, which is a mechanical consequence of ceiling-test write-downs shrinking the full-cost pool rather than an efficiency gain.

**Capital structure.** On July 13, 2026 Talos issued $800M of 8.000% second-priority notes due 2034 and redeemed all $625M of the 9.000% 2029 notes at 104.500%. Pro-forma principal is $1.425B (+$175M), pro-forma annual cash coupon $122.6M versus $114.8M — about $7.8M/yr more interest in exchange for a five-year maturity extension and a 100bp lower coupon on the refinanced tranche. Q3 2026 will absorb a $28.1M call premium plus an undisclosed write-off of unamortized issuance costs. Leverage is $1,425M against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $1,197.6M = 1.19x, far inside the revolver's 3.00x Total Debt/EBITDAX covenant; even at a stressed $800M of EBITDA leverage would be 1.78x. **The bank covenant is not the binding constraint.**

**The binding constraint is the decommissioning complex.** ARO of $1,394.1M at June 30 rose $62.0M in six months *despite* $40.6M of cash settlements, because $70.8M of accretion and $25.5M of upward revisions exceeded them. Accretion alone runs roughly $140M annualized and exceeds typical quarterly pre-tax income. Behind that sit $1.5B of performance and surety bonds, $95.7M of letters of credit, $251.5M of CFSA collateral commitments through 2031, and — critically — CFSA events of default that include failure to maintain liquidity of at least $200M or falling below a specified credit rating. Liquidity was $1,181.9M at June 30 ($577.6M cash plus $604.3M of undrawn availability). Pro forma for net notes proceeds, the redemption, and a ~$432.5M closing payment on Coulomb, cash steps down to roughly $277M before H2 operating cash flow. That is manageable but sits uncomfortably close to a $200M contractual floor, and it is why the Q4 2026 borrowing-base redetermination matters more than the leverage ratio.

A distress screen returned an Altman Z of approximately 1.3 with a weak Piotroski. Both are structurally suppressed for a full-cost E&P: Z penalizes the $1,024.9M accumulated deficit (largely legacy ceiling-test write-downs) and thin working capital, while Piotroski penalizes a GAAP loss caused by a non-cash charge. The leverage and liquidity inputs are independently measurable and adequate. Altman captures none of the ARO/surety structure, which is the genuinely solvency-relevant item. **Financial health: 5.0.**

## Growth Analysis

Production went FY2025 94.6 MBoepd → Q1 2026 ~88.9 → Q2 2026 93.7 (68.6 MBopd oil), with H1 averaging 91.3 versus 97.1 in H1 2025. Sunspear added 4.1 MBoepd; Brutus declined 3.5 on an expected gas-recompletion decline; Galapagos/Genovesa cost 1.5. FY2026 guidance issued with the 10-K was 85-90 MBoepd, and H1 actual of 91.3 already exceeds the top of that range with no update pending the acquisition close. Either a materially weaker H2 is expected or the February guidance is stale; the filings do not resolve it, and that is an unsatisfying place for a guidance range to sit in August.

The structural issue is that growth here is maintenance, not expansion. A 43.4% reserve replacement ratio against a 5.06-year reserve life means Talos must drill or buy continuously to hold production flat. The PUD table shows 4,880 MBoe of PUDs *acquired*, so organic replacement was materially below 43%. Converting the existing 37,948 MBoe of PUDs requires $734.0M of future development cost, or $19.34/Boe, $703.5M of it in Deepwater. Exploration results have been mixed — the Daenerys discovery against a non-commercial Manta Ray dry hole in FY2025, following dry holes in 2023 and 2024.

Part of the 2025 reserve decline is price-driven: SEC trailing-average pricing was low, which truncates economic limits. The June 30, 2026 ceiling-test price of $71.93/Bbl already exceeds FY2025's $64.84 realized, so PV-10 and the ceiling-test cushion should mechanically improve into year-end 2026 even with spot falling, which also lowers near-term impairment risk. Reserve *quantity* attrition over three years relative to production is a physical fact price recovery does not fix.

The Coulomb/Na Kika acquisition adds 23 MMBoe proved (10 MMBoe probable) and 16 MBoe/d at ~77% oil — roughly 17% of production. Derived reserve life on the acquired assets is 3.94 years, *shorter* than Talos's own. The transaction shortens consolidated reserve life while adding high-margin cash flow. The ILX inventory and operated development activity that management says will "compete for capital beginning in 2027" is the offset, but it is optionality rather than booked reserves.

Against that, the leasing environment is the most favorable in a decade. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act mandates a minimum of thirty Gulf of America lease sales through the first half of 2040, and Talos won 8 of 11 high bids at the December 2025 sale. For a company whose defining problem is a five-year reserve life, a legislated multi-decade leasing cadence is worth more than it would be to a peer with fifteen years of inventory. The Honduras entry (80% operated WI over >4 million gross acres, 45% closed) and the Mexico Block 29 farm-in ($30M contingent at FID plus up to $20M carry) are cheap, long-dated call options, but they depart from the 10-K's stated Gulf-and-offshore-Mexico mandate and add jurisdictions with no comparable risk disclosure.

Near-term consensus is deteriorating: current-quarter EPS estimates were revised from $0.18 to $(0.02) over 30 days and next-quarter from $0.12 to $(0.04). These are 2-4 analyst estimates on near-zero base numbers, so the percentage changes overstate the information content, but the direction is consistent with the crude rollover. **Growth prospects: 4.5.**

## Valuation Assessment

The peer set makes clear that P/E is not the discriminator here — NOG at −3.2, CRGY at −11.1 and CRC at −10.6 are all loss-making, with only CRK (6.2x) and MGY (15.8x) showing positive earnings. TALO's −3.3x trailing and forward P/E is meaningless; the $0.45 current-year and $0.10 next-year consensus EPS figures (1-2 analysts) simply confirm that after DD&A there is minimal GAAP earnings power. Cash-flow and asset multiples are the only usable anchors.

| Metric | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise value (pre-deal) | $3,247M | $2,399M equity + $1,425M pro-forma debt − $577.6M cash |
| EV / FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA | 2.71x | $1,197.6M earned at $64.84/Bbl realized |
| EV / annualized H1 2026 EBITDA | 2.33x | *Price-spike sensitivity, not a forecast* |
| EV / PV-10 (12/31/2025) | 1.02x | PV-10 $3,189M |
| (EV + ARO) / FY2025 EBITDA | 3.87x | ARO $1,394.1M |

The 2.71x and the 1.02x are the same company, and the difference is decommissioning. PV-10 already deducts future abandonment from future net cash flows; Adjusted EBITDA excludes both accretion and P&A cash spend. Adding ARO to EV and comparing to PV-10 would double-count, but comparing (EV + ARO) to *EBITDA* is legitimate and gives 3.87x. On a PV-10 basis Talos trades at roughly parity, which is not obviously cheap for an E&P. The correct adjustment to any "2.7x looks cheap" screen is the derived ~$565M of contractual P&A plus surety collateral over 2026-2030, about 24% of market capitalization, in cash that generates no new production. PV-10 cuts the other way too: it uses a depressed FY2025 price deck, excludes probable reserves and the 23 MMBoe being acquired, and excludes corporate G&A and interest entirely, which biases it upward as an equity proxy.

Normalizing: at $75-80 realized oil and post-deal production of roughly 105-108 MBoepd, we estimate Adjusted EBITDA of $1.25-1.40B, against roughly $0.95-1.05B at a $62-65 realized price (the HSBC 2027 Brent path). Post-deal enterprise value is approximately $3,547M ($2,399M equity + $1,425M debt − ~$277M pro-forma cash). At 3.0x a $1.30B normalized figure, equity is worth roughly $16.50/share; at 2.7x a stressed $1.00B, roughly $9.50.

Published analyst targets run $17.00 to $22.00 with a $18.89 mean — the entire range sits above spot, and the ratings distribution improved from 5 holds to 3 over the last month with Roth/MKM upgrading to Buy on July 1 following the acquisition. That is a real signal of sell-side conviction, but it is anchored on deal accretion at a higher oil deck than the forward curve now implies, and on a $/flowing-barrel framing that ignores the incremental decommissioning liability Talos assumes. We also note TALO is only 15.6% below its 52-week high while comparable E&Ps sit 20-53% below theirs — relative to the peer group, this is not the discounted name.

Superficially cheap E&Ps with liquidating reserve bases are the archetypal value trap, and we are not willing to treat 2.71x as a mispricing when 1.02x PV-10 and 3.87x on an ARO-inclusive basis explain it. Modest asymmetry exists from the mechanical PV-10 recovery into year-end 2026 and from ~$200M of unused buyback authorization at a program that has averaged $11.00 per share against today's $14.39. **Valuation: 4.8.**

## Competitive Landscape

Talos occupies a defined niche: a mid-cap, majority-operated Deepwater consolidator buying mature assets from majors exiting sub-scale positions. The pattern is consistent — EnVen (2023), QuarterNorth (2024, source of Katmai), incremental Monument working interest (2025), Shell's Coulomb/Na Kika (2026). The model rests on lower overhead than a major, willingness to assume ARO, access to surety capacity, and field-life-extension capability. The vulnerability is that it requires continuous access to capital and surety markets while the ARO stock compounds with each transaction.

Trajectory is mixed. Positives: 81% operated, Katmai's $3.95/Boe LOE, acquiring 25% of Na Kika partially internalizes infrastructure dependence, and shedding the Shelf tail reduces future surety demand. Negatives: reserves and PV-10 both declining, unit LOE inflating 13% year over year, and customer concentration worsening rather than improving. FY2025 revenue concentration was Shell Trading 35%, ExxonMobil 23%, Chevron 12% — 70% of oil, gas and NGL revenue — and the acquisition commits 100% of acquired oil volumes to a Shell affiliate through 2027. Geographic expansion into Honduras and Mexico is not counterparty diversification; only the geographic measure is changing favorably. Mitigating factor: these are investment-grade offtakers on liquid benchmarks, so economic severity is lower than in a contract-manufacturing setting, though operational dependence on Shell-affiliated marketing and Shell-operated infrastructure is real. Genovesa, which produces through non-operated Na Kika, was shut in from Q4 2025 to late Q2 2026 on a safety-valve failure.

Shell's willingness to exit and a private fund's willingness to take the other half of the $1.7B package indicate a deep, competitive market for mature Gulf assets. That validates asset values and means Talos bids against well-capitalized private capital. The $36.96/Boe of proved reserves implied by the $850M unadjusted price is not a distressed entry level. **Competitive position: 4.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Decommissioning and surety** is the risk specific to Talos rather than generic to E&P: $1,394.1M of ARO against $2.40B of equity value, $1.5B of bonds, $251.5M of collateral commitments, $360M of contractual minimum P&A through 2030, a $200M minimum-liquidity event of default and a credit-rating floor. Talos also carries $22.1M of third-party decommissioning obligations inherited from bankrupt counterparties — Gulf decommissioning liability follows predecessors in title.

**Undisclosed acquisition terms.** Two items are material and unverified. The press release describes "a 50% upside sharing agreement effective at closing through year-end 2027 subject to commodity-price-based thresholds if realized price exceeds $60/Bbl," with Talos paying and Shell receiving. Applied literally to ~12.3 MBopd of acquired oil, that would cost roughly $45M/yr at $80 realized and $79M/yr at $95 — 15-26% of the asset's implied annual cash flow, capping accretion precisely in the scenario where the deal otherwise looks best. Thresholds, volume basis and caps are not disclosed, so this is a scenario requiring verification against the PSA exhibit or closing 8-K, not a fact. Separately, **no dollar figure has been published for the decommissioning liability assumed on the acquired interests or the incremental surety requirement**, despite an explicit obligation to provide financial assurance. Also outstanding: a 1.25% ORRI to Shell on future new leases using Na Kika and up to $10M of contingent payments.

**Commodity path.** Q2's $99.47/Bbl is an outlier against every reported bank forecast — Morgan Stanley cut Brent on faster-than-expected Hormuz reopening and flagged a 2027 surplus; HSBC cut 2026 Brent to $80 and 2027 to $65. A 2027 surplus would compress cash flow into exactly the years when contractual P&A calls are largest. Forecasters were wrong in both directions during 2026 (Barclays held $100 Brent on May 22), so the path is genuinely two-sided; with roughly 65% of oil unhedged, both tails are live.

**Impairment sensitivity.** $454.5M in FY2025 and $145.0M in Q1 2026, with the 10-K disclosing that a 10% lower price deck would have produced roughly $807M instead of $454.5M. The rolling trailing-12-month SEC price should rise through 2026, cutting near-term risk.

**Deal completion.** HSR waiting-period expiration remains outstanding, and a $175M special mandatory redemption at par applies if the acquisition is not consummated by December 31, 2026. Shell may retain the $42.5M deposit in specified circumstances. BP's July 20 waiver of its preferential purchase right removed the largest structural obstacle.

**Regulatory — improving.** The March 2026 BOEM proposed rule would restore credit for co-owners and predecessors and lower the qualifying credit-rating threshold from BBB-/Baa3 to BB-/Ba3, which would likely *reduce* Talos's required financial assurance. On March 31, 2026 the Endangered Species Committee unanimously exempted Gulf oil and gas activity from Section 7 consultation. Both remain subject to rulemaking completion and new litigation; NMFS must publish a final Rice's whale critical-habitat rule by July 15, 2027.

**Governance timing.** The cooperation agreement capping Control Empresarial (Slim family) at 25.0% expires December 16, 2026 absent extension. A 24.2% holder that just purchased $150M of the new 8.000% notes would then be unconstrained in accumulating further or pressing for a transaction. These agreements have been routinely extended; the lapse date is a fact, what follows is speculation.

**Other:** a ~$160M, 365-day Deepwater vessel minimum commitment beginning mid-2027; $40.2M of firm transportation minimums; an active suit by former warrant holders of a company acquired in March 2024; and material weaknesses identified in September 2024 and reported remediated, worth monitoring given three CFOs in fourteen months.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 57.0% sits 7.2 points above one-year realized volatility of 49.8% at the 76th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (57.0% at one month versus 54.6% at six). With earnings already reported on August 4-5, the ±9.9% implied move on the 15-day tenor is pricing commodity and deal/HSR path rather than an earnings event. Put/call skew is 4.6% near-term and 9.4% at three months, indicating buyers paying up for downside — which sits against a call-tilted open-interest base (P/C OI 0.22, down from 0.33 in mid-July). The reading we take is hedging of long positions rather than a directional bearish build; IV actually fell 1.2 points while the stock declined 3.7%, so there is no panic bid. Short interest of 8.6% of float and 4.1 days to cover is unremarkable and does not indicate a crowded bear position. Net: the options market corroborates our two-sided view of the commodity path and confirms that near-term protection is not cheap.

**Risk factors: 3.8.**

## Management Quality

Capital allocation has been better than the share price suggests. Cumulative repurchases since March 2023 total 22.7M shares for $249.8M, an average of roughly $11.00 against today's $14.39 — the program has created per-share value. Roughly $200M of authorization remained unused at June 30, with zero repurchased in Q2 2026, which the company attributes to possession of material non-public information during the Shell negotiations. The July refinancing extended maturities five years at a 100bp lower coupon on the refinanced tranche for about $7.8M/yr of incremental interest. Paying $31.5M to exit the Shelf tail is unglamorous and correct. Compensation actually paid to the principal executive officer was negative in both 2023 and 2025, so the equity-linked program has clawed back economic value in line with the share price — genuine alignment.

Offsetting: cumulative TSR indexed at $133.74 against a peer index of $295.12 is a 161-point shortfall over five years. Leadership turnover has been extensive — a CEO transition effective March 2025, a CFO resignation in June 2025, an interim CFO, and a permanent CFO in August 2025, with Item 5.02 8-Ks on six dates across eighteen months. The incentive plan measures Adjusted Free Cash Flow, absolute and relative TSR, and average daily production, with no reserve-replacement or recycle-ratio metric — a notable omission given a 43% replacement ratio. There were no open-market purchases by officers or directors in the Form 4 record; all activity was grants, conversions, and tax withholding at $12.94-$13.47. That is a neutral-to-mildly-negative datapoint rather than evidence of a negative view.

The Slim relationship requires care. Control Empresarial sold roughly 3.09M shares in March and May 2026 at $16.38-$16.80, taking ownership from 25.8% to 24.2%. Pre-sale holdings were 26.09% of the March 31 share count — above the 25.0% cooperation-agreement cap — because Talos's own buybacks shrank the denominator. The sales are largely a cap-compliance mechanic, though they went somewhat below the ~41.74M share line that strict compliance required, so some genuine trimming above today's price is embedded. More decisively, Slim Family Office entities purchased $150M of the new 8.000% notes in July, so total family capital committed to Talos appears to have risen while shifting from equity toward secured debt. Talos also agreed to pay approximately $0.9M in advisory fees to Inbursa, a Slim-controlled bank, on the same issuance. A control-adjacent holder taking 18.75% of a new secured issue while an affiliate collects a fee warrants scrutiny; it is not a vote of no confidence. **Management quality: 4.8.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $20.50 (+42%)

Hormuz normalization stalls or reverses and realized oil holds $85-95 into 2027, against which roughly 65% of oil volumes are unhedged. Post-deal Adjusted EBITDA reaches $1.45-1.55B; the market applies 3.5x post-deal EV/EBITDA. Coulomb closes on schedule with the assumed decommissioning liability disclosed at a modest figure and the upside-sharing arrangement narrower than a literal reading implies. The Q4 2026 redetermination lifts the borrowing base to $850M as amended, the buyback resumes at $50M+/quarter below $15, and YE2026 PV-10 recovers toward $4.0B on a higher SEC deck — cutting impairment risk and re-rating the asset multiple. The 30-mandated-lease-sale cadence plus a finalized BOEM rule lowering assurance requirements structurally reduces the surety burden that has capped the multiple. The December 16 cooperation-agreement lapse introduces a strategic-optionality bid from a 24.2% holder.

### Bear Case — $9.50 (−34%)

HSBC's path plays out: 2027 Brent at $65, realized in the low $60s. Post-deal EBITDA falls toward $1.0B while $90M/yr of contractual minimum P&A runs through 2028, $42.7-43.2M/yr of surety collateral is funded, cash interest is $122.6M, and 2026 capex guidance of $500-550M plus $100-130M of P&A leaves no free cash flow. Pro-forma cash of ~$277M grinds toward the $200M CFSA liquidity floor. The upside-sharing arrangement and the 3.94-year acquired reserve life mean the deal adds a fast-depleting annuity funded with $432.5M of cash and $175M of incremental debt while consolidated reserve life shortens below five years. Another year of sub-100% reserve replacement makes the acquisition treadmill visibly non-accretive per share, and the stock trades toward 2.5-2.7x stressed EBITDA. Multiple compression from a valuation already at 1.0x PV-10 has limited cushion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** No dividend, so this is not an income hold; the appreciation case depends on a commodity path we cannot forecast and on two deal terms that are not disclosed.

- **Base case: $16.50 (+15% from $14.39).** 3.0x post-deal EV/EBITDA of $1.30B (normalized at $75-80 realized oil, ~105-108 MBoepd) = $3,900M EV, less $1,425M debt plus ~$277M pro-forma cash = $2,752M equity ÷ 166.93M shares.
- **Bull case: $20.50 (+42%).** 3.5x on $1.50B EBITDA at $85-95 realized oil.
- **Bear case: $9.50 (−34%).** ~2.7x on $1.00B stressed EBITDA at $62-65 realized.

## Conclusion

The scores have declined from 5.7 in late April to 4.7 in July, and this review lands at 4.6 — essentially flat. That reflects genuine offsetting movement rather than drift. Improving: BP's preferential-purchase-right waiver removed a real binary from the Shell transaction; the July refinancing extended the maturity wall to 2031/2034; Q2 delivered a 92.7% non-GAAP beat; the sell side upgraded and all published targets ($17-22) sit above spot; and the Gulf regulatory environment is the most accommodating in a decade. Underneath both sets of movements, the structural picture is unchanged: a 43.4% reserve replacement ratio, a 5.06-year reserve life, $1,394.1M of ARO against $2.40B of equity, and roughly $565M of contractual decommissioning and collateral cash calls through 2030 that produce nothing. The acquisition adds a high-margin, fast-depleting cash annuity with a shorter reserve life than the base business, on consideration whose full extent is not yet disclosed.

Existing holders should stay. New capital should wait for either the closing 8-K to quantify the assumed decommissioning liability and the upside-sharing mechanics, or a price in the $11-12 area that pays for the reserve-life problem. **HOLD.**
