**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FedEx today does not match the company its historical financials describe. On June 1, 2026 it distributed 80.1% of FedEx Freight (the high-margin LTL business that ran at roughly 17% operating margins before separation costs), leaving a "RemainCo" parcel/express carrier (Federal Express) that operates at approximately 7% margins. The FY2026 10-K still consolidates Freight (~$8.8B of revenue), so the reported $94.7B top line and $18.55 diluted EPS overstate the go-forward earnings base. The ~$75B market cap now prices only the parcel business; shareholders separately received FDXF stock. Any analysis anchored to the historical margin profile flatters the business that actually trades under the FDX ticker.

The near-term signal environment is unusually noisy. FedEx moved its fiscal year-end from May 31 to December 31, creating a seven-month transition period (June–December 2026) that makes year-over-year comparisons non-comparable and has already produced analyst confusion around guidance. The Q4 print on June 23 fit the recent pattern: a clean $6.31 adjusted EPS beat (versus ~$5.97 consensus) followed by a ~5% selloff on soft calendar-year guidance and margin concerns. Consensus estimates are moving the wrong way (current-year EPS revised down 2.4% over 30 days, next-year down 6.8%), which is the more durable signal underneath the beat. The stock has recovered to ~$315, roughly 24% below its 52-week high of $413.87.

The investment case reduces to a single question: can the structural cost programs (DRIVE, Network 2.0, Tricolor, "One FedEx," targeting $1.0B of savings by end of calendar 2026) outrun soft parcel demand and cost inflation from the newly ratified pilot CBA, wages, and purchased transportation. Federal Express operating income rose 21% in FY2026 with margin improving from 6.5% to 7.2%, which is genuine progress. But the demand backdrop is weak, the Amazon insourcing threat is structural, and a $3.3B IEEPA tariff-refund overhang sits unresolved as both a potential windfall and a customer-remittance liability. We rate FDX HOLD. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the negative estimate momentum plus reporting opacity argue against leaning long here.

## Company Overview

FedEx is a holding company operating one of the world's largest integrated transportation networks, serving 220+ countries covering more than 99% of world GDP. Following two structural changes in mid-2026, the entity has been reshaped:

- **FedEx Freight spin-off (June 1, 2026):** Distributed 80.1% of FedEx Freight Holding (one FDXF share per two FDX shares); FedEx retained 19.9%, which it must divest within 24 months. RemainCo is essentially the express/ground parcel business.
- **"One FedEx" consolidation (June 1, 2024):** FedEx Ground and FedEx Corporate Services were merged into Federal Express, creating a single unified air-ground network.
- **New reportable segments (effective June 1, 2026):** Express U.S. Domestic and Express International, with FedEx Logistics folded into Express International. The old segment reporting is obsolete.
- **Fiscal year change:** Year-end moved from May 31 to December 31, with a seven-month transition period reported separately.

The operating scale as of May 31, 2026: roughly 452,000 Federal Express employees, ~84,000 drop-off locations, 700 aircraft (the world's largest all-cargo fleet), 180,000+ motorized vehicles, and ~5,300 independent contracted service providers. Pilots, a small share of the workforce, are unionized under ALPA with a newly ratified CBA effective June 29, 2026, amendable December 2030, which removes a multi-year labor overhang while raising costs.

The core competitive advantage is the integrated global air-ground network the company describes as "extremely difficult, costly, and time-consuming to replicate," supported by a brand ranked top delivery company on Fortune's Most Admired list for 26 consecutive years. Founder Frederick W. Smith is now referenced in filings as "late," marking the end of the founder era.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (fiscal years ended May 31):**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $90.2B | $87.7B | $87.9B | $94.7B (+8%) |
| Operating income | $4.91B | $5.56B | $5.22B | $5.46B (+5%) |
| Operating margin | 5.4% | 6.3% | 5.9% | 5.8% |
| Net income | $3.97B | $4.33B | $4.09B | $4.43B (+8%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $16.81 | $18.55 (+10%) |

FY2026 revenue growth of 8% was real but flattered. The drivers were improved base yields, U.S. domestic volume growth (+5% average daily volume), higher fuel surcharges (indexed to fuel costs), and favorable FX, partially offset by the loss of the USPS contract and trade-policy headwinds. The segment picture matters more than the consolidated numbers: Federal Express operating income rose 21% (margin 6.5%→7.2%), while FedEx Freight collapsed 59% (margin 16.7%→7.0%), though Freight's decline was heavily distorted by $492M of one-time spin-off separation costs. The spun-off Freight unit was the high-margin business, and RemainCo runs at roughly 7% margins on a normalized basis.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow rose to $8.9B (from $7.0B), and with capex of ~$3.8B, free cash flow reached ~$5.1B. Cash ballooned to $13.3B from $5.5B, but this is largely mechanical: $5.3B of debt issuance plus a $4.1B dividend upstreamed from FedEx Freight ahead of the spin. Post-year-end (July 2026), FedEx used that cash to repurchase ~$4.9B of debt, so the cash build should not be read as organic balance-sheet strengthening. Total debt is ~$24B against $31.6B of stockholders' equity, plus $17B of operating lease liabilities (9.2-year weighted term) and $6.4B of self-insurance reserves. Credit ratings are S&P BBB / Moody's Baa2, both stable — solidly investment grade.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.25 flags "distress," but this is a false signal for a capital-intensive, asset-heavy carrier with huge PP&E, large lease obligations, and low equity-to-assets ratios. FedEx generates $8.9B of operating cash flow, holds investment-grade ratings, pays a rising dividend, and repurchases stock. The Z-Score should be disregarded here; the leverage/liquidity concern it flags is not borne out by the cash-generation or ratings data.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.22 in June 2026 (FY2026 dividends $1.4B). Buybacks slowed sharply to $776M in FY2026 (3.3M shares at ~$233 average) from $3.0B in FY2025. A new $5.0B buyback authorization was approved July 20, 2026, with a $1.0B accelerated share repurchase in progress. The dividend yield is modest at ~1.5% — above the peer median of 1.1%; FedEx's payout is well covered by FCF but is not the reason to own this.

FedEx has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters (surprises of 4.8%, 25.7%, 16.3%, 5.5%), which lends confidence to consensus as a floor. But the beats have coincided with negative estimate revisions and post-print selloffs, indicating the market is discounting the near-term prints in favor of the demand and margin trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests almost entirely on self-help, not end-market demand. Structural cost programs — DRIVE, Network 2.0 (deployed at ~410 U.S./Canada locations, targeted complete by end-2027), Tricolor international air redesign, and "One FedEx" — target $1.0B of structural cost reduction by end of calendar 2026. A European workforce reduction (~1,400 employees) is complete for ~$150M of annual savings, and a January 2026 international transformation will cut ~5,000 operational employees at a cost of $225–325M through 2028. Federal Express's operating income growth and margin expansion in FY2026 show the programs are producing results.

The demand backdrop is the counterweight. Management explicitly cites vulnerability to the "highly cyclical, high-fixed-cost" transportation industry, with weak U.S. imports since late 2022, soft industrial production, and tariff-driven de minimis exemption removal pressuring volumes and raising credit losses. The USPS contract loss is a volume headwind partly offset by favorable mix. Nearshoring and shorter shipping distances threaten revenue-per-shipment, and the secular shift from goods to services spending compresses the addressable base.

Estimate revisions confirm the pressure. Current-year consensus fell to $17.92, next-year to $20.50, and next-quarter estimates are down 9.2%. This is negative earnings momentum, not the inflection pattern that would justify treating rising estimates as a buy signal. The fiscal-year change compounds the difficulty: the seven-month transition period and non-comparable guidance create a reporting "dead zone" that will muddy signal quality and likely produce volatility around prints. Growth prospects score low at 4.5, reflecting a company whose earnings trajectory depends on outrunning soft demand and cost inflation with cost cuts, with consensus currently modeling that it won't fully succeed.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$315, FedEx trades at ~17x trailing EPS of $18.55. The critical caveat is that trailing EPS includes the now-departed Freight business, so the go-forward multiple on RemainCo parcel earnings is meaningfully higher than the headline suggests. Next-year consensus of $20.50 on the post-spin entity implies ~15x forward, but that figure is falling and reflects a business at a ~7% margin structure with limited demand tailwind.

Peer comparison offers mixed support. Air Freight & Logistics peers trade at a median P/E of 26.9 (EXPD 26.9, CHRW 38.7, GXO 43.5, HUBG 26.3), well above FedEx's ~17x — but those are asset-light forwarders and 3PLs with different capital intensity and growth profiles. The cleaner comp is UPS at 17.6x, the other half of the parcel duopoly, which carries a 6.4% dividend yield signaling its own distress. FedEx's discount to the forwarders is justified by its capital intensity, margin structure, and cyclical exposure, not evidence of mispricing.

A ~17x multiple on a parcel carrier is not "cheap" in any way that reliably predicts outperformance; the compressed multiple reflects genuine structural questions (Amazon insourcing, secular yield pressure, ~7% margins) rather than a temporary dislocation the market has overlooked. Treating the low headline P/E as a buy signal would be a value-trap error. There is no identified catalyst forcing a re-rating: the cost programs are already known and partially priced, the fiscal-year change obscures near-term earnings power, and estimate revisions are negative. Analyst targets span a wide $160 to $479 (mean $352, median $359), a dispersion that signals genuine uncertainty about the post-spin trajectory rather than confident undervaluation. We score valuation 5.0 — roughly fair, neither offering asymmetric upside nor clearly stretched, with the caveat that the trailing multiple understates the true forward valuation of the parcel-only business.

## Competitive Landscape

FedEx's domestic parcel position is a duopoly with UPS, alongside USPS, regional carriers, and Amazon's rapidly expanding in-house network. The USPS contract loss is a concrete, realized share headwind. The integrated air-ground network remains a durable moat, costly and time-consuming to replicate, but the competitive trajectory is deteriorating rather than stable. The 10-K explicitly names Amazon as building out hubs, aircraft, vehicles, an LTL freight service, and third-party delivery capabilities. Amazon is simultaneously a customer and an emerging competitor, and its insourcing of its own volume plus its move into third-party delivery is the single most important structural threat to FedEx's addressable market.

Internationally, FedEx competes against DHL, UPS, DPD/GeoPost, Royal Mail's GLS, and government-subsidized carriers, many state-owned with structurally lower costs. The LTL industry FedEx exited had "excess capacity" and weak industrial demand, a rational reason to spin Freight at a depressed point in its cycle, though it also means shareholders now hold the cyclical LTL exposure separately in FDXF.

The competitive score of 4.5 reflects trajectory over level. FedEx remains a market leader, but it is losing share (USPS), facing a well-capitalized entrant insourcing volume and encroaching on third-party delivery (Amazon), and contending with secular yield pressure from nearshoring and the goods-to-services spending shift. The prior competitive score of 7.5 (February 2026) has been correctly marked down as the Amazon threat and USPS loss have crystallized in the filings.

## Risk Assessment

**Spin-off execution and tax risk.** The tax-free treatment relied on an IRS private letter ruling; an IRS reversal could trigger significant U.S. tax liabilities. FedEx must divest its retained 19.9% Freight stake within 24 months at uncertain value, and shared brand/IP means Freight reputational issues could bleed into FedEx.

**IEEPA tariff refund litigation ($3.3B exposure).** The Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026. FedEx has submitted $3.3B in refund claims, received ~$800M in cash, and recorded $749M in customer refund obligations. Fourteen class-action suits (13 consolidated in Tennessee) seek tariff refunds from FedEx. Management states the impact "is uncertain… it is reasonably possible that it could be material." This is a genuine two-sided wildcard — a potential windfall from recovering written-off receivables and a liability to remit to customers — with no reliable way to estimate the net outcome.

**MD-11 fleet grounding.** An FAA emergency Airworthiness Directive (November 2025) grounded all MD-11 aircraft, disrupting FY2026 operations. Return to service began May 2026, with full retirement accelerated to 2032.

**Cyclical demand and trade policy.** Management states the company is "particularly vulnerable" to a cyclical, high-fixed-cost industry. Weak imports, soft industrial production, and tariff-driven de minimis removal are pressuring volumes.

**Self-insurance and "nuclear verdicts."** FedEx is self-insured; excess insurance is more expensive and harder to obtain amid trucking-industry jury awards. A 5% claim-severity change equals a ~$320M reserve swing.

**Governance soft spot.** Claude F. Russ serves as Interim CFO and Interim CAO after John Dietrich's departure (he joined American Airlines' board in July 2026). An interim CFO during a spin-off, fiscal-year change, tariff litigation, and international restructuring is a notable near-term uncertainty, compounded by nine Item 5.02 officer/director-change 8-Ks since October 2025. Much of the churn is consistent with the organizational realignment, but the frequency and the permanent-CFO gap warrant monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 40.2% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 29.3% (a rich 10.8-point spread) and in the 63rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (40.2% near-term vs. 32.0% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst — here the fiscal-year-transition reporting cadence and continued guidance uncertainty. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.1% near-term with a put/call OI ratio of 1.21, indicating modest hedging demand. IV has cooled since the prior snapshot (44.7%→40.2%) and the P/C OI ratio fell (1.56→1.21), suggesting some post-earnings de-risking rather than building bearish conviction. The signal is consistent with our HOLD: the market expects volatility around near-term prints without a clear directional lean.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$400+).** The $1.0B structural cost program lands ahead of schedule, Federal Express margins continue expanding beyond 7.2%, and the parcel demand cycle stabilizes. The IEEPA tariff situation resolves as a net windfall (recovered receivables exceeding remittance obligations). The $5.0B buyback and $1.0B ASR shrink the share count at ~$315, and a permanent CFO restores confidence. On stabilized ~$22 EPS and a re-rating toward 18x as the reporting fog clears, the stock approaches the high end of the analyst range. This requires cost execution to visibly outrun demand and cost inflation.

**Bear Case (~$220–240).** Soft parcel demand persists, cost inflation from the new pilot CBA, wages, and purchased transportation erodes the cost-cut benefit, and margins stall near 7%. Amazon's insourcing and third-party delivery push accelerates share loss beyond USPS. The IEEPA situation resolves as a net liability, and next-year estimates continue their downward drift below $20. On ~$18 EPS and a 12–13x multiple reflecting deteriorating competitive trajectory, the stock revisits the low-$220s, near its 52-week low of $216.10. This maps to roughly a 25–30% decline from current levels.

**Base Case (~$320).** Cost programs deliver most of the targeted savings but demand and cost inflation offset much of the benefit, keeping margins near current levels. EPS settles around $20 on the post-spin entity, the tariff situation nets out to a manageable non-event, and buybacks provide modest per-share support. The multiple holds around 15–16x forward. This is essentially the current price, reflecting a business that neither breaks out nor breaks down.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis is genuinely two-sided, and the negative estimate momentum plus reporting opacity prevent a confident lean in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions inflect positive (current-year and next-year consensus rising for two consecutive months) alongside evidence that Federal Express margins are expanding past ~7.5%.
- A permanent CFO appointment plus clarity on the fiscal-transition guidance that lets consensus re-anchor with confidence.
- A favorable IEEPA resolution (net windfall) or clean removal of the $3.3B overhang, combined with a pullback toward the low-$270s where forward valuation would turn asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Federal Express operating margin contracts below 6.5% on soft demand, indicating cost cuts are being fully absorbed by inflation and volume loss.
- Next-year EPS estimates fall below $19 with continued negative revision velocity.
- Evidence of accelerating volume loss to Amazon insourcing beyond the USPS effect, or an adverse IEEPA net-liability resolution materially above the recorded $749M.

**Reassessment timeline.** The seven-month transition period (June–December 2026) reporting and the calendar-2029 target progress are the key windows. The next natural reassessment is the first clean transition-period print and the permanent-CFO decision. Given the reporting dead zone, expect elevated volatility and treat near-term prints skeptically.

## Conclusion

FedEx post-spin is a ~7%-margin parcel/express carrier whose appreciation depends on cost-cut execution outrunning soft demand, cost inflation, and the Amazon insourcing threat. The FY2026 consolidated financials overstate the go-forward earnings base, the fiscal-year change muddies near-term signal quality, and estimate revisions are broadly negative. The ~17x trailing multiple is not the cheap entry it appears; on the parcel-only business it is roughly fair for the growth and quality delivered. Federal Express's FY2026 operating income growth is real progress, but not yet enough to overcome a weak demand backdrop and a deteriorating competitive trajectory.

We rate FDX a HOLD with a $320 base case, ~$400+ bull case, and ~$220–240 bear case. The score trajectory has declined from 6.3 (February) to 5.0 now, driven by justified markdowns to competitive position (Amazon, USPS loss), management quality (interim CFO, executive churn), and growth prospects (negative revisions, weak demand). New data confirms rather than reverses that decline. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met by the genuine balance of a self-help margin story against structural headwinds and reporting opacity. We would need positive estimate inflection, margin expansion past 7.5%, or a pullback toward the low-$270s to turn constructive, and margin contraction below 6.5% or accelerating Amazon share loss to turn negative.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report claimed FedEx's ~1.5% dividend yield was *below* the 1.1% peer median (it is above); this affects a descriptive characterization of the payout, not the valuation score or thesis.
