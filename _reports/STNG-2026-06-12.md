**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is one of the largest pure-play refined-product tanker owner-operators globally, running a fleet of roughly 93 vessels (37 LR2, 42 MR, 14 Handymax, average age ~9.8 years) with heavy spot-market exposure. The company is at the favorable end of a strong product-tanker cycle: LR2 spot voyages have been fixing at $45,000–$50,000+/day in early 2026 and one-year charter rates have exceeded $40,000/day, levels not seen since early 2022. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised up +94.4% in 30 days and +300% in 90 days, and the company has beaten estimates in four consecutive quarters. The balance sheet is in its best shape in a decade after using the 2022–2024 upswing to deleverage aggressively, and management has returned roughly $1.05 billion to shareholders since 2023 against a $3.6 billion market cap.

The central question is whether the freight cycle has peaked, not whether STNG is a good operator. Management's own actions read like top-of-cycle behavior: locking in multi-year time charters above $40,000/day, selling older tonnage at firm prices (four LR2s for $286M, multiple MRs at premium values), and exiting its DHT equity stake. Bank of America downgraded the stock to Underperform with a $53 target in January 2026, arguing that the charter lock-ins signal management believes rates are near a top. That target sits roughly 32% below the current price. Against this, the sell-side as a group is constructive, with a $99 mean target and a $87–$120 range, and the structural bull case (ton-mile demand up ~20% since 2019 on refining-capacity relocation, sanctioned shadow-fleet tightening) is credible.

We rate STNG a HOLD. The stock is statistically cheap at ~7.5x trailing earnings, but trailing earnings sit at or near peak-cycle levels, and the most reliable signal in cyclical shipping — what a disciplined operator does with its capital — points toward a market that management itself is hedging. The roughly 85% twelve-month appreciation has already captured much of the cyclical re-rating. We see the risk/reward as roughly balanced and would require either a meaningful pullback toward asset value or confirmation that the rate environment is durable before turning more constructive.

## Company Overview

STNG owns and operates product tankers that carry refined petroleum products (gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, naphtha) rather than crude oil. The fleet spans three vessel classes: LR2 (Long Range 2), MR (Medium Range), and Handymax. Revenue is driven by time charter equivalent (TCE) rates earned on spot voyages and, increasingly, multi-year time charters. The heavy spot weighting gives STNG strong operating leverage to the product-tanker cycle: outsized upside in tight markets and meaningful downside in slack ones.

The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in Monaco, and commercial/technical operations are tied to the broader Scorpio group (Scorpio pools, Scorpio Ship Management). It is a foreign private issuer that files Forms 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q, which reduces the cadence and granularity of US disclosure. Key executives include Robert Bugbee (Director & President), Lars Dencker Nielsen (CCO), and James Doyle (Head of Corporate Development & IR).

The financial discussion below relies on management presentations, press-release coverage, and analyst commentary, and should be reconciled against the 20-F filed 2026-03-20 and the Q1-2026 6-K filed 2026-05-05.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financial picture is strong, with the caveat that it reflects a cyclical high.

- **Earnings power**: Trailing EPS of $7.03 against a current price of $77.75 puts the stock at ~7.5x trailing. The earnings-surprise record is clean: 16.6% beat in Q1-2026 ($3.02 vs $2.59 est), 0.7% in Q4-2025, 8.8% in Q3-2025, 26.7% in Q2-2025 — four consecutive beats. Q1-2026 delivered $3.02 of EPS, consistent with the >$40k/day rate environment management has described.
- **Balance sheet and liquidity**: Management frames the company as a "financial fortress" after years of debt paydown funded by the freight upswing. Reported revolver availability was $783.9M as of January 2026, a substantial liquidity cushion relative to the company's size.
- **Capital returns**: ~$1.05 billion returned since 2023 (~$850M buybacks, ~$200M dividends). The buyback intensity meaningfully reduced the share count and amplified per-share metrics. The current dividend yield is ~2.35%.
- **Asset monetization**: STNG has been a consistent net seller of older tonnage at firm prices — its remaining DHT stake (~3.55M shares at ~$13.40), 2014-built MRs at ~$32M each, a 2019 LR2 at $61.2M, a 2020 MR at $42M, four LR2s for $286M (May 2026), and two 2015 scrubber-fitted MRs for $70M combined. These dispositions convert peak asset values into cash.

The quality of the franchise's cash generation is high in absolute terms. The unresolved question is normalization: at mid-cycle TCE rates, earnings power is materially lower than the trailing $7.03 implies. Precise FY2025 revenue, net income, EBITDA, and net-debt figures should be confirmed against the 20-F before relying on point estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data is dramatic: current-quarter EPS revised +94.4% over 30 days and +300.2% over 90 days; current-year EPS up +28.4% (30d) and +115.3% (90d); next-year up +33.4% (90d). On the surface this looks like a pattern that warrants aggressive treatment.

The reason we do not read it that way is the nature of the business. These revisions reflect spot freight rates spiking to multi-year highs, not a structural step-change in the company's earning capacity. The revision pattern itself tells the story: next-year consensus EPS of $6.41 sits well below current-year $12.68. Analysts are explicitly modeling earnings to fall by roughly half as the cycle normalizes. Rising near-term estimates that coincide with a forecast halving of forward earnings are a cyclical-peak signature, not a durable-growth signal. The +300% current-quarter revision is the market catching up to a spot-rate spike that the forward curve does not expect to persist.

The structural bull case has merit. Management cites ton-mile demand up ~20% since 2019 as refining capacity relocates — new export refineries in the Middle East offsetting closures in the US, Europe, and Asia, lengthening voyages. Sanctioned Iranian and Russian "shadow fleets" tighten the mainstream insured fleet, and strong simultaneous LR2/Aframax markets limit cross-substitution. These are real ton-mile tailwinds, but they are slow-moving structural forces layered on top of a spot rate that has overshot, and they do not validate sustaining $12.68 of annual EPS.

Fleet renewal is measured rather than expansionary: selling LR2s while adding two newbuild MRs (LOI) downsizes the vessel-class mix and keeps the fleet young, but it is not a growth program. We score growth prospects at 5.0 — the durable component is modest structural ton-mile expansion, while the eye-catching near-term numbers are cyclical and expected to reverse.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing metrics STNG looks cheap: ~7.5x earnings versus a peer median of 12.2x (TDW 12.2x, CRK 6.2x, KNTK 18.4x; BTU and CVI are loss-making). But trailing P/E is the wrong anchor at a cyclical top, and so is forward P/E on current-year estimates that themselves embed peak rates.

The honest valuation lens is normalized earnings power. Consensus next-year EPS of $6.41 implies a forward multiple of ~12.1x — in line with the peer median, not a discount. If the cycle normalizes further toward mid-cycle, earnings could land below $6.41, pushing the effective multiple higher still. The stock is therefore reasonably-to-fully valued on normalized earnings, with the cheap trailing optic being an artifact of peak rates that analysts do not expect to persist.

The sell-side consensus ($99 mean, $100 median, $87–$120 range) sits well above the current $77.75 and would imply meaningful upside. We treat these targets cautiously because they are calibrated to a continuation of the strong rate environment. On a cyclical name, analyst targets calibrated to peak earnings are not independent confirmation of mispricing — they reflect the same favorable rate assumption embedded in the price. BofA's outlier $53 target, derived from a normalization thesis, is directionally more useful as a downside scenario even if its magnitude is aggressive.

A valuation support exists below current levels: net asset value. STNG has been selling vessels at firm prices, and a modern, partly deleveraged fleet provides an asset floor. We would view the stock as more compelling on a pullback toward NAV. At ~$77.75, the asymmetry is not present: the upside requires rates to stay rich, while the downside (normalization) is the base-case forward expectation embedded in consensus. We score valuation 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG operates one of the largest pure-play product-tanker fleets globally. Its competitive advantages are scale, a relatively modern fleet (~9.8-year average age), scrubber fitting on part of the fleet, and access to the Scorpio commercial pools, which improve vessel utilization and rate optimization. The company's competitive position within the product-tanker segment is stable-to-favorable.

The trajectory is not deteriorating, but it is not structurally improving either — product tanker is a commoditized, rate-taking business where no single operator sets prices. STNG's edge is operational and financial discipline rather than a widening moat. The structural ton-mile tailwind benefits the entire mainstream insured fleet, not STNG specifically. We score competitive position 6.0: a high-quality operator in a commodity industry, with the fleet-class rotation reflecting prudent capital cycling rather than share gains.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is cyclical, and it is not fully priced because the trailing multiple makes the stock look statistically cheap while forward fundamentals point lower.

- **Cycle peak / normalization**: This is the central debate. If spot rates revert faster or further than expected, the earnings and dividend trajectory weakens materially. Management's behavior — locking multi-year charters, selling older tonnage, exiting its DHT stake — is what a disciplined operator does when it believes rates and asset values are rich.
- **Russia-Ukraine resolution**: A peace deal could ease sanctions dynamics, release shadow-fleet tonnage, and shorten trade routes, pressuring ton-mile demand and rates. Management counters that aging sanctioned tankers would likely be scrapped rather than re-enter mainstream trade, but this is a genuine bilateral downside catalyst with binary characteristics.
- **Spot exposure**: Heavy reliance on volatile TCE rates means both earnings and the variable dividend can swing sharply.
- **Governance / structure**: Reliance on affiliated Scorpio management entities and pools creates related-party and conflict considerations. Foreign-private-issuer status reduces disclosure cadence and shareholder protections relative to US domestic issuers.
- **Capex re-entry**: Fleet renewal (newbuild MR LOIs) reintroduces capex, delivery, and financing risk, though at a measured pace.

We score risk factors 4.8: the normalization and peace-deal catalysts are specific, the cyclical downside is real and asymmetric against a stock that has already run roughly 85%, and these are not adequately reflected in a price that screens cheap on peak earnings.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive-to-neutral and does not flag elevated downside hedging. Near-term ATM IV is 41.2% versus 1-year realized vol of 38.4% (a fair 2.8% spread), IV percentile is low at 21%, and the put/call OI ratio is 0.69 (call-heavy). The 1-week skew is -3.3% (call-favored). The term structure is in contango (41.2% near vs 45.6% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.3%. Short interest is low at 4.0% of float with 1.6 days to cover, meaning the bearish view (BofA's normalization thesis) is uncrowded and not expressed in positioning. The options market is not pricing imminent stress, consistent with a market that views the cycle as still favorable, and would reprice if a peace deal or rate rollover materializes.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Structural ton-mile demand keeps the product-tanker market tighter for longer as refining capacity relocates and longer voyages persist; sanctioned shadow-fleet dynamics keep the mainstream insured fleet tight; and STNG's multi-year charter lock-ins above $40k/day provide a higher rate floor than the market expects. The fortress balance sheet and the capital-return program sustain shareholder returns through the cycle. On this path, rates stay elevated, FY26 EPS holds near consensus $12.68, the dividend grows, and the stock re-rates toward the $99 mean / $120 high sell-side targets. Bull case: $100–$110.

### Bear Case

The cycle peaks, as management's own hedging behavior implies. A Russia-Ukraine resolution releases tonnage and shortens routes, spot rates revert toward mid-cycle, and earnings fall toward (or below) the $6.41 next-year consensus. The stock de-rates toward NAV/normalized-earnings support. BofA's $53 target captures the aggressive end of this scenario. Bear case: $53–$60, roughly 23%–32% below current.

The base case is normalization without collapse: rates ease, earnings step down toward the $6.41 forward consensus, the stock holds a low-double-digit multiple on normalized EPS, and trades in a $70–$85 range as cyclical optimism and normalization expectations offset. Base case: ~$78–$82.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — the bull and bear cases are roughly balanced and the resolution depends on the freight-rate trajectory, which is genuinely two-sided).

**Reassessment timeline**: Next quarterly 6-K results and TCE-rate disclosure (next earnings ~early August 2026), plus any material development in Russia-Ukraine negotiations.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY)**:
- Stock pulls back toward NAV / mid-$60s while spot LR2 rates hold above ~$40k/day, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Evidence that the elevated rate environment is durable rather than peaking — e.g., next-year consensus EPS revised meaningfully above $6.41 alongside management increasing (not locking/hedging) spot exposure.
- A Russia-Ukraine resolution passes without the feared tonnage release, removing the binary downside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID)**:
- Spot LR2/MR TCE rates roll over decisively (sustained 25%+ below early-2026 levels) confirming the peak.
- A peace-deal-driven release of shadow-fleet tonnage materially loosens the market.
- Dividend cut or buyback suspension signaling management views forward cash flow as deteriorating.

## Conclusion

STNG is a well-run, financially-fortressed operator generating exceptional cash flow at the favorable end of a product-tanker cycle. The statistical cheapness is an artifact of peak earnings; on normalized earnings (~12x forward on $6.41 next-year consensus) the stock is fairly valued, not cheap. The most decision-relevant evidence is management's own capital behavior — locking multi-year charters, selling older vessels at firm prices, exiting equity stakes — which reads as disciplined hedging against a market it believes is rich. That behavior, combined with consensus modeling a roughly 50% earnings decline next year, the twelve-month run, and a live Russia-Ukraine downside catalyst, leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric.

We rate STNG a HOLD. The franchise quality and capital-return discipline justify ownership for existing holders willing to ride cyclical volatility for the ~2.35% yield and buyback support, but we would not add at current levels. A pullback toward NAV / the mid-$60s, or confirmation that the rate environment is structurally durable, would change the call.
