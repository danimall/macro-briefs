## Executive Summary

First Horizon is a $84B-asset southeastern regional bank executing a disciplined post-merger-termination playbook: 15%+ ROTCE, 13% share count reduction over two years, and a 13% dividend increase in Q1 2026. The franchise operates in high-growth southeastern markets (Florida, Tennessee, Texas, North Carolina), and management has demonstrated alignment through aggressive buybacks averaging ~$20.80/share. Q1 2026 results ($0.53 EPS, beating consensus by $0.03) confirmed continued operational momentum.

The challenge for new capital at $24.25 is that the stock already reflects this execution quality at 1.71x TBV and ~11.4x forward earnings. With CRE office/multi-family exposure in a stressed segment, and the 2025 provision release ($85M YoY benefit) creating a non-recurring earnings tailwind, the path to meaningful upside requires either NIM expansion beyond guidance or M&A catalysts that remain speculative. The 2.8% dividend yield and continued buyback support make this a reasonable income-oriented hold, but insufficient asymmetry exists for incremental capital deployment.

## Company Overview

First Horizon operates over 450 business locations across 23 states, with concentration in the southeastern U.S. The company generates revenue through three segments: Commercial, Consumer & Wealth (core banking), Wholesale (FHN Financial fixed income sales/trading and warehouse lending), and Corporate (treasury/ALM). The post-TD-merger-termination strategy centers on organic growth in C&I lending, deliberate CRE runoff, and returning capital in excess of the ~10.5% CET1 target.

Key franchise differentiators include a market-leading warehouse lending operation ($4.7B balances with near-zero historical credit losses), a fixed income trading desk (FHN Financial, $206M revenue in 2025), and geographic positioning in high-growth southeastern metropolitan areas.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** FY2025 NIAC of $982M ($1.87 diluted EPS) represented 27% growth over 2024, driven by NIM expansion (+12 bps to 3.47%), fee income normalization (+$118M, largely from absence of prior-year AFS loss), and a $85M reduction in provision expense. Q1 2026 annualized earnings run rate is $2.12/share; consensus FY2026 is $2.15.

**NIM Dynamics:** Net interest income of $2.62B grew 4% YoY as deposit cost declines (-57 bps on interest-bearing liabilities) outpaced asset yield compression (-26 bps on earning assets). The +200 bps NII sensitivity of +2.6% confirms modest asset sensitivity, meaning the current rate environment is supportive but further cuts would be modestly negative.

**Credit Quality:** Net charge-offs at 19 bps of average loans are benign. ACL/loans declined to 1.31% from 1.43%. Criticized and classified loans declined YoY. The provision drop from $150M to $65M was the single largest EPS driver (+~$0.16/share after-tax). This is unrepeatable absent further material credit improvement, meaning 2026 EPS growth must come from revenue expansion rather than reserve arithmetic.

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 10.63% provides ~63 bps of excess above management's 10.5% operating target. The $997M remaining buyback authorization represents ~8.7% of current market cap. Combined with the $0.68/share dividend (~$323M annually based on ~475M shares), total payout capacity of ~$1.3B over 12 months implies ~11% shareholder yield if fully executed.

**Free Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $628M less $33M capex = $595M FCF. FCF yield on market cap (~$11.5B) = 5.2%.

## Growth Analysis

Management guided 3-7% revenue growth for 2026. Consensus EPS estimates of $2.15 (FY2026) and $2.35 (FY2027) imply 15% and 9% YoY growth respectively. However:

- The 2025-to-2026 growth includes a full year of the elevated run-rate established in H2 2025, not incremental acceleration.
- Estimate revisions are modest: +0.9% for FY2026 and +0.4% for FY2027 over 30 days. This reflects stability, not momentum.
- C&I loan growth of 7% in 2025 was partially offset by intentional CRE runoff (-6%). Net loan growth was 3%.
- The provision release was the single largest contributor to EPS growth in 2025 and is non-recurring.

FHN Financial's fixed income business ($206M in 2025) has upside potential in a steepening yield curve environment, but the current flat-to-inverted curve limits this. Warehouse lending is rate-sensitive and could benefit from mortgage refi activity if rates decline, but elevated mortgage rates suppress volumes.

Organic growth is achievable at mid-single-digit rates. The stock needs either NIM expansion above the 3.47% level, a meaningful acceleration in fee income, or an acquisition catalyst to justify appreciation beyond low-teens EPS growth.

FHN's premium P/TBV is justified by superior ROTCE. On a P/E basis, FHN trades approximately in-line with or slightly below peers at ~11.3-11.9x. The analyst consensus target median is $26.00, representing ~7% upside from current levels. The low target of $25.00 is essentially the current price (+3%), and the high is $30.00 (+24%).

The valuation is fair relative to earnings power but not cheap enough to generate asymmetric upside. At ~11.3x forward earnings with stable estimate revisions, the stock requires a specific catalyst to re-rate. No such catalyst is identifiable with high confidence in the near term.

In an environment where CRE refinancing stress is increasing, regional bank multiples face compression pressure. FHN's valuation already reflects solid execution; it does not yet discount the potential negative scenarios of CRE impairment or regulatory threshold costs.

## Competitive Landscape

FHN holds meaningful competitive advantages in its southeastern footprint. Population growth in Florida, Tennessee, and Texas supports organic deposit/loan growth without aggressive rate competition. The warehouse lending niche is scale-advantaged with extremely low credit losses. FHN Financial provides diversification absent from most peers.

However, FHN's competitive position is stable rather than improving. C&I market share in its core geographies is mature. The intentional CRE runoff means the bank is shrinking its loan book in one major category. No evidence suggests FHN is gaining meaningful share in its primary markets vs. peers like Regions (RF) or Synovus (SNV).

The $100B asset threshold creates strategic uncertainty. Crossing it organically (at 3-5% annual asset growth) implies arriving around 2028-2029, with compliance costs beginning 2-3 years prior. An acquisition could accelerate this timeline but would also dilute the share count reduction narrative.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Exposure ($13.6B, 21% of loans):** Office ($2.7B) and multi-family ($4.5B) represent the two most stressed CRE subsectors nationally. While FHN's CRE NPLs are improving (1.76% from 2.04%), systemic CRE repricing risk remains. A 2% NCO rate on the $7.2B office/multi-family book would represent $144M in losses, material relative to $65M in total 2025 provisions.

**Rate Environment:** NIM expansion from deposit repricing is largely complete. FHN's modest asset sensitivity means limited NIM upside from rate increases. If rates rise further, CRE stress accelerates while NII gains are modest (+2.6% per +200 bps). A -200 bps shock reduces NII by 4.5%.

**Provision Normalization:** ACL/loans at 1.31% is approaching the floor for a bank with 21% CRE concentration. Any credit deterioration in H2 2026 creates earnings headwinds from this direction.

**$100B Threshold:** Compliance costs are already being incurred. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges "significant" costs. While the current administration may reduce requirements, this is speculative and could reverse.

**Geographic Concentration — Florida:** 26% of CRE and 29% of consumer real estate in Florida creates hurricane/insurance concentration. Rising property insurance costs are eroding the cost-of-living advantage driving migration to these markets.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 30.0% trades modestly rich to 26.3% HV (spread 3.8%). The term structure is flat (30.0% 1M vs 28.6% 3M), indicating evenly distributed uncertainty rather than acute event risk. Put/call OI ratio of 0.14 shows minimal put demand, consistent with the near-zero short interest. The options market is not flagging elevated downside risk, but IV percentile at 39% is neutral and does not provide cheap entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($28-30, +16-24%):** NIM expands above 3.50% on further deposit repricing + loan growth acceleration. CRE concerns prove overdone as southeastern markets outperform nationally. M&A announcement to "jump over" $100B threshold at accretive terms. Buyback continues reducing share count 6-8% annually, driving EPS to $2.40+ in FY2026. Multiple re-rates to 12-13x on improved growth visibility.

**Bear Case ($19-21, -12-21%):** CRE losses in office/multi-family materially exceed provisions ($100M+ in additional reserves required). Rate environment compresses NIM below 3.30%. Regional bank sector de-rates on CRE contagion fears (similar to 2023 episode). $100B threshold compliance costs accelerate. EPS falls to $1.70-1.80 and multiple compresses to 10-11x.

**Base Case ($25-27, +3-11%):** FHN delivers on 3-7% revenue growth guidance. Provisions normalize modestly higher ($80-100M). Buyback continues at current pace. EPS comes in at $2.10-2.20. Stock trades at 11.5-12x forward earnings with 2.8% yield support. Total return of 6-14% including dividends.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $21-22 (P/TBV <1.5x, forward P/E <10.5x) on market-wide bank selloff without fundamental deterioration
2. Management announces accretive acquisition with clear $100B-threshold strategy
3. FY2026 EPS estimates revised upward by >10% (to $2.35+) on NIM outperformance or fee income acceleration

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. CRE NPLs rise above 2.5% or NCOs exceed 40 bps for two consecutive quarters
2. Management suspends buyback or cuts dividend
3. CET1 falls below 10.0% requiring capital conservation

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (July 2026). Key datapoint: provision expense trajectory and CRE classified asset trends. If provisions rise meaningfully above $20M/quarter, the provision normalization risk is materializing.

## Conclusion

First Horizon is a well-run regional bank executing effectively on capital return, NIM management, and deliberate CRE derisking. The 15.1% ROTCE, 4-quarter earnings beat streak, and aggressive buyback program ($1B+/year) reflect quality management in a favorable operating environment.

The stock is fairly valued at ~11.3x forward earnings and 1.71x TBV. The specific obstacle to a BUY recommendation is the absence of a non-consensus catalyst with identifiable timing: estimate revisions are stable, the largest 2025 EPS driver (provision release) is non-recurring, and the rate/CRE environment creates identifiable downside risk that the market may be underpricing at current multiples. At current levels, FHN is an income-generating hold (2.8% yield + ~8% buyback yield = ~11% total capital return) rather than a capital appreciation opportunity. A pullback to $21-22 or a demonstrable acceleration in estimates would change this assessment.
