## Executive Summary

Tidewater is the world's largest pure-play offshore support vessel operator, running 208 owned vessels across five global segments. The company sits at the intersection of two favorable forces: a structural supply deficit in OSVs (minimal newbuilds from 2015-2021) and an elevated offshore spending environment. Average day rates have risen 34% over two years to $22,573, and the company generated $353M in free cash flow in 2025 on a base of $1.35B in revenue.

However, the stock has already moved to price much of this thesis. From its April 2025 low of $35.50 to the current $87.85, TDW has appreciated 147% in twelve months. Organic revenue growth flatlined in 2025 (+0.5%), operating income declined 9% to $283M, and utilization fell from 79% to 76% with stacked vessels rising from 1 to 8. Two consecutive quarterly earnings misses (Q3 FY25 by -15.4%, Q4 by -53.1%) indicate the operating trajectory is weaker than headline numbers suggest. The reported $335M net income is inflated by a non-recurring $196M deferred tax benefit; normalized earnings are closer to $138M.

The pending $500M Wilson Companies acquisition adds 22 PSVs in Brazil and will provide a revenue step-up in H2 2026, but also introduces $261M in assumed debt and integration risk in a jurisdiction with meaningful regulatory and currency exposure. At 13.5x trailing earnings (or roughly 32x normalized), and with the consensus median target at $83 below the current price, the stock appears to be pricing in a continued upcycle that the operating data does not fully support. A pullback toward the $65-72 range would offer better risk/reward.

## Company Overview

Tidewater provides marine support services for offshore oil and gas exploration, development, and production globally. The fleet of 208 vessels includes platform supply vessels (PSVs), anchor handling tug supply vessels (AHTS), and crew boats, deployed across five geographic segments: West Africa (66 vessels, $363M revenue), Europe/Mediterranean (50 vessels, $344M), Americas (34 vessels, $270M), Asia Pacific (19 vessels, $190M), and Middle East (43 vessels, $173M).

The company emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2017 and has since pursued aggressive consolidation, acquiring the Swire Pacific Offshore fleet (2022), Solstad's 37 PSVs ($594M, 2023), and now the Wilson Companies' 22 Brazilian PSVs ($500M, pending Q2 2026). This has made Tidewater the dominant player by fleet size in a fragmented industry where the top five operators control less than 30% of the global fleet.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1.353B in 2025, essentially flat with 2024's $1.346B. The 56% growth in 2023 and 33% in 2024 were driven by the Solstad acquisition and a powerful day rate recovery. In 2025, higher average day rates (+6.1% YoY to $22,573) were offset by lower utilization (76.1% vs. 79.0%) and a smaller active fleet (206 vs. 216 average vessels).

**Operating Income:** Declined 9% from $311M to $283M. Operating margin compressed from 23.1% to 20.9%, driven by G&A rising 21% YoY and higher drydock amortization. This margin compression occurred despite a favorable day rate environment and warrants attention as a signal that cost discipline may be loosening.

**Net Income:** Reported $335M includes a one-time $196M deferred tax benefit from the "Vessel Realignment" restructuring (consolidating fleet entities into a single U.S. entity). Normalizing for this, underlying net income was approximately $138M, representing a modest improvement over 2024 on a tax-adjusted basis but a decline on a pre-tax basis.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $379M (up from $282M) benefited from $69M in favorable working capital changes, including collection of $54M in overdue Mexico receivables. Free cash flow of $353M was strong, with maintenance capex of only $26M. However, FCF quality is somewhat lower than headline suggests given the working capital tailwind is non-recurring.

**Balance Sheet:** $579M cash, $649M in 9.125% Senior Notes due 2030, $250M undrawn revolver. Debt/equity at 1.75x. Current ratio of 2.9x provides ample liquidity. The 9.125% coupon is expensive; annual interest expense runs approximately $59M, and a make-whole premium prevents cheap refinancing before July 2027.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** Two consecutive EPS misses — Q3 FY25 missed by -15.4% and Q4 by -53.1%. The Q3 2025 net loss of -$0.8M vs. $46M profit in Q3 2024 showed genuine weakness, impacted by the $27M debt extinguishment charge and higher tax expense alongside lower operating income.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story has clearly decelerated. Day rate growth slowed from +27% in 2024 to +6% in 2025. More concerning, sequential day rate trends in Q3 2025 showed deceleration: Europe/Mediterranean day rates fell 10.8% QoQ, and overall utilization declined QoQ.

Forward estimates reflect this mixed picture. Current-year FY2026 EPS consensus is $4.06, implying roughly $200M in net income — a step-down from reported 2025 levels once the tax benefit is excluded. Next-year estimates of $6.00 incorporate the Wilson acquisition's contribution. 30-day estimate revisions are negative across the board: current quarter up 3.5% over 30 days but down 4.1% over 90 days, next quarter down 7.6% over 30 days, and next year down 2.8% over 30 days. The earnings revision cycle is negative.

The Wilson Companies acquisition, expected to close in Q2 2026 for $500M, adds 22 PSVs operating in Brazil's pre-salt basin. This will provide an inorganic revenue step-up of perhaps $120-150M annually (estimated from the acquired fleet's size relative to Tidewater's Americas segment). Brazil is a structurally attractive deepwater market with Petrobras as the anchor customer, but the transaction brings $261M in assumed debt and introduces integration, regulatory, and currency risk.

The structural tailwind from limited OSV supply and sustained offshore E&P investment supports continued activity globally. However, Tidewater's own 10-K warns that sustained oil prices in the low $60s could delay projects. Oil prices are currently in this range, making this a live risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.85, TDW trades at 13.5x reported trailing EPS of $6.64. Adjusted for the $196M non-recurring tax benefit, normalized trailing EPS is roughly $2.78, putting the adjusted P/E at approximately 31.6x.

On forward estimates, the stock trades at roughly 21.6x current-year consensus ($4.06) and 14.6x next-year ($6.00). The next-year multiple assumes full Wilson contribution and sustained elevated day rates.

Against peers: Oceaneering trades at 10.7x, Cactus at 23.1x, and the peer median is 37.2x (skewed by LBRT and KGS). On an EV/EBITDA basis (rough calculation: $4.4B market cap + $70M net debt = $4.5B EV against ~$380M EBITDA = ~11.8x), TDW is not cheap for an asset-intensive cyclical business.

The consensus analyst median target is $83.00, 5.5% below the current price. The mean target is $83.86. Only 2 of 8 analysts rate the stock Strong Buy; 5 rate it Hold and 1 rates it Sell.

The $500M buyback authorization (approximately 11% of market cap) provides a floor, but execution has been negligible — zero shares were repurchased in Q3 2025, suggesting management is either prioritizing the Wilson acquisition or is selective on price.

On normalized mid-cycle earnings, the stock is fully valued.

## Competitive Landscape

Tidewater's competitive position is strong and improving. Through three major acquisitions since 2022, the company has assembled the largest pure-play OSV fleet globally with the broadest geographic footprint. This scale provides negotiating leverage with customers, more efficient vessel redeployment across basins, and purchasing power on maintenance and drydocking.

The supply side remains favorable: newbuild orders during 2015-2021 were minimal, and the existing global fleet is aging. Tidewater has been actively culling older vessels (33 sold over three years), tightening supply further. The barriers to entry are high — a new-build PSV costs $40-60M and takes 2-3 years to deliver.

However, competitive position is only as durable as the commodity cycle. West Africa, the company's largest segment by revenue, showed a 21% decline in vessel operating profit with utilization dropping from 72.2% to 65.9% and stacked vessels rising from 1 to 5. Regional demand deterioration can quickly erode the pricing power that the supply constraint provides.

The Asia Pacific segment commands the highest day rates ($37K+) but is small (19 vessels). The Middle East segment (43 vessels) faces direct exposure to the Iran conflict, with war-risk insurance costs and vessel seizure risk now explicitly cited in the 10-K.

## Risk Assessment

**Oil Price Dependence (HIGH):** The entire business model depends on sustained elevated oil prices supporting offshore E&P spending. Management identifies the low $60s/bbl as a threshold below which project deferrals begin. With current spot oil prices in this range, this risk is active. A sustained normalization at these levels would compress day rates materially over 12-18 months.

**Wilson Acquisition Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** Brazilian antitrust approval pending. Integration risk in a jurisdiction with complex regulations, currency volatility, and Petrobras-style state-customer concentration.

**Iran Conflict — Middle East Segment (HIGH):** War-risk insurance costs have escalated. The 10-K contains newly specific language about missile attacks, sea lane mining, blockades, and vessel seizure. Direct operational disruption to the Middle East segment is a real risk.

**Earnings Trajectory Deterioration (MODERATE):** Negative estimate revisions across most periods, with operating margins compressing. The operational trajectory is weakening.

**Insider Selling (MODERATE):** CFO sold $1.8M in March 2026 at $80.05; General Counsel sold $2.3M across February-March 2026 at $70-80. While substantial holdings remain, these open-market sales at prices below current levels do not signal management confidence in further upside from here.

**Mexico Counterparty Risk (MODERATE):** PEMEX-related receivables went 90+ days past due in 2025. The $54M was eventually collected, but recurring collection difficulties with state-owned customers in emerging markets are structural.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-117)
Sustained elevated oil prices drive accelerating offshore E&P spending globally. Day rates break above $25K as the aging global fleet cannot be replaced quickly enough. Wilson acquisition closes cleanly and adds $120-150M in high-margin Brazilian revenue. At $6.00+ FY2027E EPS and an 18-19x multiple (justified by supply constraint duration), the stock reaches $108-114. Venezuela PDVSA judgment ($79M potential recovery) provides additional optionality. The $500M buyback program, if executed aggressively, provides structural support.

### Bear Case ($52-62)
Oil prices normalize or remain in the low $60s that management has flagged as problematic. Offshore project deferrals begin within 6 months. Day rates plateau and begin declining from $23K toward $18-20K as customer budgets are cut. West Africa deterioration accelerates. Wilson acquisition integration disappoints or Brazilian regulatory delays extend closing. Normalized mid-cycle earnings of $3-4/share valued at 15-16x = $45-64. The bear case target aligns with the analyst low of $52.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock pulls back to $65-72 range (providing 20%+ upside to base case with limited downside to bear case), OR
2. Q1 FY2026 earnings beat with day rates accelerating above $24K and utilization above 78%, demonstrating organic momentum recovery, OR
3. Wilson acquisition closes with accretion confirmed and management resumes buyback execution at scale

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Oil prices sustain below $65 for 3+ consecutive months, confirming project deferral risk materializing
2. Average day rates decline below $21K for two consecutive quarters, confirming cyclical rollover
3. West Africa utilization drops below 60%, indicating broad offshore demand contraction

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected late May / early June 2026) and Wilson acquisition closing (expected late Q2 2026) are the key catalysts. Reassess positioning around both events.

## Conclusion

Tidewater is a well-positioned operator benefiting from both structural supply tightness in OSVs and an elevated oil price environment. The company's consolidation strategy has been well-executed, creating a dominant competitive position.

The problem is price. At $87.85, the stock has already rallied 147% off its 52-week low and trades above the consensus analyst median target of $83. The underlying operational trajectory — flat revenue, declining operating income, lower utilization, rising stacked vessels, consecutive earnings misses — does not support the current valuation without sustained high oil prices. Insider selling by two senior officers at $70-80, negative estimate revisions, and the options market's elevated near-term pricing all argue for caution.

The appropriate posture is HOLD for existing positions with a BUY target in the $65-72 range, where risk/reward becomes asymmetric. New capital should wait for either a meaningful pullback or concrete evidence that the operational deceleration has reversed.
