## Executive Summary

DaVita reported a strong Q1 2026, beating EPS estimates by 23% ($2.87 vs. $2.34) on improved treatment volumes from Fresenius clinic closures, labor productivity gains, and better-than-feared ACA enrollment headwinds. The stock responded with a 36% single-day move on May 6, 2026 and now trades near its 52-week high of $202.69. Full-year guidance was raised to adjusted OI of $2.15B-$2.25B and EPS of $14.10-$15.20.

The fundamental improvement is real: Fresenius is closing U.S. clinics, handing DaVita patient transfers that improve volume without acquisition cost. The IKC value-based care segment turned profitable in 2025. International is growing 38% YoY. These are durable tailwinds. However, the stock's 95% appreciation from its January low ($101) prices in much of this good news. At 13.3x forward earnings on FY26E of $14.80, DVA is cheap relative to healthcare peers (median P/E 37.6x), but the discount reflects genuine structural risks: $10.2B debt with negative equity, 57% government payor revenue mix, commercial payor mix erosion risk from OBBBA legislation, and secular headwinds from GLP-1/SGLT2 drugs slowing CKD progression. Berkshire Hathaway's systematic position reduction and CFO/COO selling $14M in stock within days of the earnings beat temper enthusiasm at current levels.

The stock warrants holding for its structural competitive improvement but does not offer sufficient margin of safety for new capital at $197.

## Company Overview

DaVita operates approximately 2,657 outpatient dialysis centers in the U.S. serving ~200,500 patients, commanding roughly 36% domestic market share. The company is the second-largest kidney dialysis provider in the U.S. (after Fresenius Medical Care). The competitive gap is shifting as Fresenius actively closes U.S. clinics. Beyond U.S. dialysis, DaVita operates 585 centers across 14 countries ($1.35B international revenue, +38% YoY) and runs an Integrated Kidney Care (IKC) business managing 66,000 patients in value-based arrangements.

Revenue breaks down as approximately 86% U.S. Dialysis and 14% Ancillary Services (IKC + international + other). By payor: 57% Medicare/Medicare Advantage, 7% Medicaid, 3% other government, and 32% commercial. The profitability dynamic is extreme — roughly 11% of patients carry commercial insurance but generate 32% of revenue and nearly all operating profit, because commercial rates substantially exceed government reimbursement.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 months):**
1. **Fresenius clinic closures** — Management raised volume guidance from flat to +25-50 bps specifically on patient transfers. About half transferred by Q1 end, remainder in Q2. This is essentially free organic volume growth.
2. **IKC profitability** — Swung from -$18M (2024) to +$22M (2025). DaVita is the highest aggregate savings generator in CMS's CKCC program with 4.5% gross savings improvement. If kidney care moves toward value-based payment, DaVita has emerging category leadership.
3. **International expansion** — 37.8% revenue growth in 2025 from acquisitions and 17.6% patient growth across 585 centers in 14 countries.

**Medium-Term (12-36 months):**
- Estimate revisions rising: FY26 EPS up +5.9% (30d) and FY27 EPS up +6.2% (90d)
- Management's long-term target: 3-7% OI growth annually
- Continued buyback-fueled EPS growth at current pace ($1.5-2.0B/year) implies 10-15% annual EPS growth from share count reduction alone, assuming flat operations

**Secular Headwinds (3-5 years):**
- GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors may slow CKD progression → fewer patients reaching ESKD → reduced dialysis demand. This is a very long-tail risk (multi-year drug adoption curves, existing patient base still needs treatment), but it caps the terminal growth rate.
- U.S. treatment volumes declined 1.1% in 2025 excluding Fresenius transfers. Normalized non-acquired growth was -0.8%. The domestic patient base is not organically growing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $197, DVA trades at:
- 13.3x FY2026E EPS of $14.80
- 11.5x FY2027E EPS of $17.18
- ~12.5x EV/EBITDA (using ~$3.1B EBITDA estimate)

**Peer Comparison:**
The peer median P/E of 37.6x (skewed by BTSG at 73x and SHC at 37.6x) is not directly comparable — DaVita is more mature with lower growth and higher leverage. The more relevant comparators are CHE (24.1x) and LH (22.6x). DVA's 13.3x forward P/E represents a 40-45% discount to these closer peers.

The discount is partially justified by: (a) negative equity / $10.2B debt, (b) commercial mix concentration risk, (c) government reimbursement dependence, (d) secular volume headwinds. However, the magnitude of the discount seems excessive given accelerating EPS growth (consensus expects 16% growth FY26→FY27) and improving competitive dynamics.

**Fair value range:** Using 14-16x on $14.80 FY26E EPS produces $207-$237. At the current $197, the stock trades at the low end of fair value. The bull case ($235 analyst high target) requires sustained 16x+ on growing EPS. The bear case ($145 analyst low target) implies a return to 10x forward on any operational deterioration.

At $197, after a 95% move from the January low, upside to fair value is limited (~5-20%) while downside to bear case is meaningful (-26%). The risk/reward favors patience over pursuit.

## Competitive Landscape

DaVita operates in a structural duopoly with Fresenius Medical Care. Barriers to entry are substantial: regulatory requirements for dialysis centers, medical director relationships, geographic density advantages, and scale in billing/procurement. New entrants primarily target pre-dialysis/CKD management (Strive Health, Monogram Health) rather than competing directly in dialysis delivery.

Fresenius actively closing U.S. clinics is the most significant competitive development. This structural rationalization is unambiguously positive for DaVita — patient transfers without acquisition cost, potential pricing power improvement, and market share gains.

DaVita's competitive position is strengthening: rising share in a consolidating market, emerging IKC/value-based care leadership, technology investments (ScheduleHub, AI-driven scheduling), and international diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commercial Payor Mix Erosion (High Impact, Medium Probability):** Nearly all profit derives from ~11% of patients on commercial insurance. OBBBA legislation, ACA credit expirations, Supreme Court Marietta decision, and immigration policy changes could each reduce this percentage. Management guided ~$40M headwind in 2026 from ACA credit expiration (trending slightly better than feared in Q1).

**2. Leverage Fragility (High Impact, Low-Medium Probability):** At 3.34x EBITDA and negative equity, DaVita has minimal margin for operational error. Interest expense of $580M consumes 28% of operating income. While maturities are manageable near-term ($82M in 2026), $4.6B comes due in 2029-2030.

**3. Secular Volume Decline (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** U.S. dialysis volumes excluding transfers declined in 2025. GLP-1/SGLT2 adoption may accelerate this over 3-5 years. Fresenius closures provide near-term offset.

**4. Berkshire Exit Overhang (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** Berkshire owns ~28.9M shares (~45% of outstanding). Systematic selling could create persistent supply pressure. DaVita has been absorbing shares via direct repurchase agreement, but at some point the buyback capacity may not match Berkshire's selling pace.

**5. OBBBA Legislative Risk:** Medicaid work requirements, cost-sharing increases, and state funding limitations could reduce patient volumes and shift mix toward uninsured.

## Conclusion

DaVita's Q1 2026 beat was legitimate and driven by structural competitive improvement (Fresenius closures, labor productivity, IKC profitability). The business is in the best competitive position in years, with a consolidating duopoly and emerging value-based care leadership. Management's aggressive buyback strategy has been well-timed and value-accretive.

However, the stock's 95% appreciation from January lows prices in much of this improvement. At $197, DVA trades at 13.3x forward earnings — cheap relative to healthcare peers but appropriate given the debt load, negative equity, government payor exposure, and secular volume headwinds. The CFO/COO selling $14M in stock within days of the beat, combined with Berkshire's systematic reduction of its 45% stake, suggests insiders view current prices as reflecting fair value rather than persistent undervaluation.

The prior score trajectory showed deterioration (6.3 → 5.6) driven by financial health and risk factor concerns. The Q1 2026 beat partially reverses operational concerns, but the move from $111 to $197 has already captured that improvement. The score stabilizes at 5.82, reflecting genuine competitive improvement offset by elevated leverage risk, insider selling, and limited margin of safety at current prices. Rating: HOLD. The competitive dynamics are improving and the buyback math is compelling, but entry should be patient at these levels.
