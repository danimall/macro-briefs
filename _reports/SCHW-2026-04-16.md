## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab delivered record Q1 2026 revenue of $6.5B and adjusted EPS of $1.43, extending a multi-quarter earnings recovery driven by the near-complete unwinding of its 2022-2023 cash sorting crisis. Bank supplemental funding has been reduced by 90% (from $49.9B to $5.1B), net interest margin expanded to 2.88%, and the company returned $2.4B in buybacks in Q1 alone from a $20B authorization. With $11.9T in client assets, 38.5M active brokerage accounts, record 9.9M daily average trades, and consensus EPS estimates rising across all forward periods, the fundamental trajectory is positive.

The stock trades at $100.27, or approximately 16.8x FY2026E EPS of $5.97, a premium to traditional brokerage peers (median P/E 17.4x) but justified by Schwab's superior organic asset growth (5.1% annualized), $2.0B in realized TD Ameritrade cost synergies, and a structural NIM expansion tailwind with years of runway remaining. The primary risk is interest rate sensitivity (49% of revenue from NII), which in the current macro environment of stable-to-rising rates is a favorable setup for continued NIM expansion. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (41.5% near-term IV vs. 21.9% HV), reflecting macro stress rather than company-specific deterioration.

This is the fourth consecutive BUY recommendation. The thesis is being confirmed by fundamentals: earnings estimates are rising, the balance sheet repair is progressing mechanically, and organic client growth is accelerating. The gradual score decline from 7.3 to 7.18 reflects modest valuation compression after the stock ran from $73.76 to $107.50 in the prior year, partially giving back gains in the February selloff.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is the largest U.S. retail brokerage and wealth management platform, custodying $11.9 trillion in client assets across 38.5 million active brokerage accounts. The company operates two segments: Investor Services (~79% of net revenue), serving individual retail clients through brokerage, banking, and managed investing; and Advisor Services (~21%), providing custody and technology to thousands of Registered Investment Advisors managing $6.0 trillion.

Revenue composition: net interest revenue (49%), asset management and administration fees (27%), trading revenue (17%), bank deposit account fees (4%), and other (3%). The completed TD Ameritrade integration added ~$1.9T in assets across 17M accounts and generated $2.0B in annual cost synergies plus $500M in additional restructuring savings.

Recent strategic moves include the April 2026 launch of Schwab Crypto™ (spot BTC/ETH trading for 39M accounts at 75bps), the March 2026 acquisition of Forge Global ($660M, private markets platform), and the complete exit of TD Bank Group from its Schwab ownership position in February 2025, removing a multi-year stock overhang.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Recovery Arc

After the 2022-2023 cash sorting crisis depressed EPS from $3.79 (2022) to $2.54 (2023), earnings have recovered sharply: $2.99 (2024) → $4.65 (2025) → $5.97E (2026). The mechanism is straightforward: as expensive wholesale funding matures and is not replaced, interest expense falls and NIM expands. Interest expense declined from $6.7B (2023) → $6.4B (2024) → $3.8B (2025), a 41% drop over two years. NIM expanded from 1.98% (2023) → 2.12% (2024) → 2.74% (2025) → 2.88% (Q1 2026).

Supplemental funding stands at $5.1B, down from a peak of ~$100B in mid-2023. The remaining $5.1B will continue to mature over the next 12-18 months, providing a mechanical earnings tailwind with high visibility. Each $1B reduction in high-cost funding translates to roughly $30-40M in annual interest expense savings at current rates.

### Profitability Metrics

Pre-tax margin expanded from 39.2% (2024) to 47.9% (2025), with Q1 2026 reaching 49.2% GAAP / 51.4% adjusted. ROE stands at 21% (full year 2025) and 23% (Q1 2026), with ROTCE at 38% and 40% respectively. These compare favorably to GS (14-16% ROE), MS (14-15%), and most other financial services firms.

### Capital Position and Returns

Despite a D/E ratio of 8.93x (standard for banking models), regulatory capital is healthy: Tier 1 Leverage Ratio of 9.3% (well above the 4% minimum), adjusted Tier 1 including AOCI at 7.1% (within the 6.75-7.00% long-term target), and LCR at 131%. The AOCI drag (~$11B in unrealized securities losses) continues to unwind naturally as securities mature.

Capital return has been aggressive: $7.3B in buybacks + $2.0B in dividends + $2.5B in preferred redemptions = $11.8B returned to shareholders in 2025. The $20B buyback authorization (July 2025) has $14.5B remaining. In Q1 2026, the company repurchased $2.4B in shares and raised the quarterly dividend 19% to $0.32.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $9.3B in 2025 with only $548M in capex, yielding $8.8B in free cash flow. FCF/Net Income of approximately 99% indicates high earnings quality with minimal accrual distortions.

### Q1 2026 Specifics

Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $1.43, beating the $1.39 consensus by $0.04 (0.7%). Revenue of $6.5B was a record, up ~16% YoY. Daily average trades hit an all-time record of 9.9M (+34% YoY), driven by elevated market volatility. The one blemish: net interest revenue of $3.14B slightly missed the $3.18B consensus, reflecting modestly lower reinvestment rates on maturing securities. Margin loans surged to $126.7B (+13% from year-end 2025), indicating strong client engagement but also elevated credit exposure.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions

Current quarter EPS estimate: $1.47 (+3.3% over 90 days, +0.9% over 30 days). Full-year 2026: $5.97 (+4.2% over 90 days). FY2027: $6.93 (+3.3% over 90 days). The revision trend is uniformly positive across all periods with no downward revisions. Rising estimates in a volatile macro environment suggest the underlying drivers (NIM expansion, asset growth, trading volumes) are outweighing macro headwinds.

### Organic Growth Metrics

Core net new assets of $519.4B in 2025 (+42% YoY) represent a 5.1% organic growth rate. New brokerage accounts totaled 4.7M in 2025 (+13% YoY). Q1 2026 continued the trend with ~$140-158B in core NNA. Client asset growth compounds fee revenue (27% of revenue comes from asset management fees calculated on AUM).

### Strategic Growth Vectors

**Crypto:** Schwab Crypto™ launched April 2026, offering BTC and ETH trading at 75bps to 39M accounts. The installed base distribution advantage is substantial: Coinbase has ~11M monthly active users, Robinhood ~24M funded accounts. At 75bps, pricing is premium to Coinbase Pro but accessible for mainstream retail investors who value Schwab's brand trust and integrated portfolio view. Revenue contribution depends on sustained crypto trading volumes; regulatory risk (including the CLARITY Act) applies.

**Private markets:** The Forge Global acquisition ($660M, closed March 2026) adds private company stock trading, addressing growing RIA demand for alternative asset access.

**Balance sheet normalization:** The remaining supplemental funding will mature over the next 12-18 months. Additionally, Schwab gained broader discretion post-September 2025 to transfer BDA balances to its own balance sheet from TD Depository Institutions, which can be deployed in higher-yielding assets.

### Growth Risks

Revenue per trade continues to decline: $2.41 (2023) → $2.22 (2024) → $2.06 (2025), a 15% compression over two years. Trading is 17% of revenue, so this erosion is material. Record trading volumes are masking per-unit economics deterioration. If volumes normalize while per-trade revenue continues falling, trading revenue could decline.

Expense growth guidance of 5.5-6.5% for 2026 includes Forge integration costs and crypto platform buildout. Operating leverage depends on revenue growth outpacing this expense trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab's competitive moat rests on three pillars:

**Scale:** $11.9T in client assets generates operating leverage that smaller competitors cannot match. The TD Ameritrade integration delivered $2.0B in cost synergies, equivalent to the entire annual revenue of many mid-tier brokerages. At a 47.9% pre-tax margin, Schwab can compete aggressively on price while maintaining profitability.

**Distribution:** 38.5M active brokerage accounts and 4.7M new accounts in 2025 give Schwab unmatched retail distribution. This installed base is the key advantage for new product launches like Schwab Crypto™: converting even 5% of accounts to crypto trading at 75bps would generate hundreds of millions in revenue.

**Advisor custody:** $6.0T in RIA-custodied assets creates high switching costs. RIAs integrate their technology stacks, compliance workflows, and client reporting around their custodian. Schwab's position as the dominant RIA custodian is durable.

**Competitive threats:** Fidelity remains the primary full-service competitor. Vanguard competes on passive investing. Robinhood and SoFi target younger demographics with lower account balances. The most significant competitive risk is from firms offering higher deposit rates, pulling cash away from Schwab's balance sheet and directly reducing NII. Schwab has historically been willing to sacrifice some deposit gathering to protect margins.

## Risk Assessment

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (HIGH)
Net interest revenue is 49% of total revenue. A 200bp rate decline would reduce NII by approximately 8.8% per the company's MD&A disclosures. Current rate expectations (holding or rising) are favorable for Schwab's NIM, but a severe recession could force rate cuts that compress earnings faster than expected.

### Margin Lending Concentration (ELEVATED)
Margin loans of $126.7B (+51% from year-end 2024) represent a material and growing credit exposure. A sharp equity market correction could trigger margin calls, forced liquidations, and credit losses. Growth in RIA long/short strategies ($21.3B in Q1 2026) adds complexity to the credit risk profile.

### AOCI Drag (MODERATE)
~$11B in unrealized securities losses remain, down from $18B+ peak. These unwind mechanically as securities mature but would re-expand in a rising rate environment. Proposed regulatory rules requiring AOCI inclusion in capital calculations (triggered at $700B in total assets) represent a medium-term constraint; Schwab is at $491B currently.

### Crypto Operational Risk (NEW, MODERATE)
Schwab Crypto™ introduces custody risks (permanent loss of private keys), regulatory uncertainty (CLARITY Act, SEC/CFTC jurisdictional questions), and reputational exposure to crypto market volatility. This is a newly added risk factor in the 2025 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.5% versus 21.9% historical volatility (a 19.6pp spread) indicates the market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (41.5% near-term vs. 26.5% at 6 months), typically signaling imminent catalyst or stress expectations. The OI P/C ratio of 1.65 and unusual put activity at $94-$95 strikes suggest institutional hedging around current levels.

However, call skew of -19.4% and a volume P/C ratio of 0.90 indicate that current flow is slightly call-heavy; the OI accumulation reflects prior positioning while current activity leans bullish. IV dropped 16pp in three days (57.4% → 41.5%) while the stock fell 3.3%, suggesting the acute stress event (likely Q1 earnings volatility) is already resolving.

The options market reflects macro uncertainty rather than company-specific deterioration, consistent with the fundamental picture of uniformly positive SCHW-specific data.

## Conclusion

Charles Schwab remains a BUY at $100.27. The core thesis of mechanical NIM expansion, sustained organic asset growth, and aggressive capital return is being confirmed by every sequential data point. Q1 2026 delivered record revenue and DATs, estimate revisions are uniformly positive, the TD Bank overhang is eliminated, and $14.5B in remaining buyback capacity provides valuation support.

At 16.8x FY2026E and 14.5x FY2027E, the valuation is reasonable relative to 25-30% EPS growth in 2026 (off a depressed 2025 base) followed by 16% growth in 2027. The score decline from 7.3 to 7.18 reflects valuation compression as the stock rallied from $73.76, not fundamental deterioration. The current pullback from $107.50 to $100.27 (-7.3%) creates a more favorable risk/reward entry than recent months.

The primary risk remains interest rate sensitivity, but in the current environment of stable-to-rising rates, this exposure is working in Schwab's favor. Elevated options IV reflects macro uncertainty, not company-specific stress. For investors with a 12-month horizon, SCHW offers a compelling combination of earnings visibility, capital return, and competitive positioning at a reasonable price.
