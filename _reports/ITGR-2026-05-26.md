## Executive Summary

Integer Holdings is the only publicly traded pure-play medical device contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO), generating $1.85B in FY2025 revenue from supplying critical components to OEMs like Abbott, Boston Scientific, and Medtronic. The stock trades at $90.14, down 27% from its 52-week high of $123.78, following a sharp guidance reduction in Q1 2026 that revealed three simultaneous product headwinds at customer OEMs. The company's Board initiated a formal strategic review on April 30, 2026, engaging Goldman Sachs as financial advisor, which creates a credible takeout catalyst.

The investment case hinges on whether the strategic review converts to a transaction. At ~10-11x FY2025 EBITDA, Integer is cheap relative to historical medtech CDMO transaction multiples (12-16x), suggesting 20-50% upside in a take-private. However, three factors prevent a BUY recommendation: (1) consensus EPS estimates are falling materially (-6.3% over 30 days for FY2026), representing active fundamental deterioration; (2) short interest at 14.2% with 8.4 days to cover signals meaningful institutional skepticism about either deal completion or interim earnings trajectory; and (3) the strategic review announcement states the board is "considering" options with no binding offers or set timeline. If no transaction materializes, the stock faces a 2026 revenue air pocket (~flat sales vs. 8% prior-year growth) with no organic catalyst for re-rating.

## Company Overview

Integer designs, develops, and manufactures components, subassemblies, and finished medical devices for multinational OEMs. Revenue splits: Cardio & Vascular (60%, $1.11B), Cardiac Rhythm Management & Neuromodulation (36%, $669M), and Other (4%, declining by design). The company operates from facilities across the US (43% of workforce), Mexico (25%), Ireland (15%), and Dominican Republic (9%), with ~11,000 employees.

Once Integer wins a component contract for an FDA-approved device, it typically remains the supplier for the full product lifecycle (10+ years) due to regulatory validation requirements. This creates visible, sticky revenue streams. The company holds 731 patents and operates at the intersection of advanced materials science and precision manufacturing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** FY2025 revenue of $1.854B grew 8.0% YoY, driven by electrophysiology ramps and three acquisitions (~$179M combined). However, 2026 guidance calls for essentially flat sales ($1.805-1.835B) due to lower-than-expected adoption of three products at OEM customers. Q1 2026 was +0.5% reported (+1.3% organic).

**Profitability:** Gross margin of 27.0% (FY2025) reflects the contract manufacturing nature of the business. Adjusted EBITDA margin runs ~20-22%, with FY2026 guided at $375-399M. Operating margins compressed slightly to 11.9% from 12.1% due to restructuring costs. The current EPS run-rate based on guidance midpoint is ~$6.12 adjusted for FY2026.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $196M (FY2025) with $91M capex yields ~$105M in free cash flow (5.7% FCF margin). The company factors ~$229M of receivables annually, which inflates reported operating cash flow somewhat. FCF yield at current market cap is approximately 3.4%.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.207B (net leverage ~2.7x per credit agreement). The March 2025 refinancing was well-executed — $1.0B in 1.875% convertible notes due 2030 replaced higher-cost variable debt, reducing blended interest cost from 5.25% to 3.42% with ~92% of debt now fixed-rate. Current ratio of 3.32x provides ample liquidity. Goodwill and intangibles at $1.94B (57% of assets) reflect acquisition history.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen from $1.54 to $1.38 over 30 days (-10.3%). Full-year 2026 has dropped from $6.49 to $6.08 (-6.3%). Next-year estimates declined from $7.23 to $6.81 (-5.8%). This is active negative momentum — the market is discovering the 2026 growth pause is more impactful than initially modeled.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (2026):** Growth is impaired. Management attributes the headwind to three products with lower-than-expected market adoption at OEM customers. The CDMO confidentiality model prevents Integer from identifying which products or customers, creating an opacity problem that the market has punished severely. Organic growth for FY2026 is expected at 0-2%.

**Medium-term (2027+):** Management targets 200bp above-market organic growth, which would imply mid-to-high-single-digit rates given the medtech market grows 4-6% annually. Key drivers include: (1) electrophysiology catheter demand from new PFA ablation devices, (2) structural heart expansion, (3) neurovascular growth from complex interventional procedures, and (4) continued coatings platform acquisitions.

**Structural Tailwind:** The medical device outsourcing trend remains intact. OEMs increasingly concentrate R&D resources on product innovation while outsourcing complex manufacturing to specialists like Integer. The shift from in-house to outsourced manufacturing in medtech lags the pharma CDMO trend by roughly a decade.

**Risk to Growth Thesis:** The 2026 headwind is described as "highly unusual" but management cannot provide specifics. If the product adoption issues reflect competitive displacement or clinical failures (rather than timing delays), the 2027 recovery may not materialize as expected.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.14, Integer trades at:
- 22.5x trailing P/E (using reported EPS of ~$4.01 from Yahoo)
- ~14.8x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($6.08 consensus)
- ~13.2x FY2027 adjusted EPS ($6.81 consensus)
- ~10-11x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA
- 3.4% FCF yield

Against peer medians (P/E 36.6x), Integer looks inexpensive. However, the peer set is imperfect — LMAT and LIVN are branded device companies, not CDMOs. The more relevant comparison is to medtech CDMO transaction multiples (12-16x EBITDA), which place the stock's "takeout value" in the $110-$140 range before control premiums.

The valuation is attractive IF one of two conditions holds: (1) the strategic review results in a transaction at fair value, or (2) the 2027 growth recovery materializes and estimates stabilize. Currently, neither is confirmed, and estimates are actively declining. The forward P/E of ~14.8x on declining estimates means the "true" P/E may be higher as estimates have further to fall.

At $90 the stock already discounts meaningful bad news. The decline from peak to trough ($144 to $62) was severe, and the current level implies the market has priced in a structurally lower growth trajectory without giving credit to the strategic review optionality or the moated business model.

## Competitive Landscape

Integer's competitive position is its strongest attribute. As the only publicly traded pure-play medical device CDMO at $1.85B scale, it occupies a unique niche. Key advantages:

1. **Regulatory moat:** FDA device approval requires specific supplier validation. Switching manufacturers mid-lifecycle requires costly re-validation, creating 10+ year lock-in.
2. **Technology breadth:** 731 patents across cardiac rhythm, neurostimulation, vascular access, and interventional devices. The 2025 coatings platform acquisitions (Precision, VSi, Biocoat) add capabilities competitors lack at scale.
3. **Scale economies:** Larger CDMOs can invest in automation, quality systems, and regulatory compliance infrastructure that smaller players cannot match.
4. **Acquisition platform:** The fragmented nature of the industry (hundreds of small specialists) creates a perpetual M&A runway.

The primary competitive threat is OEM in-sourcing: customers deciding to vertically integrate manufacturing. Evidence of this exists in CRM&N (one spinal cord stimulation customer announced a planned decline in 2020). However, the trend favors outsourcing rather than in-sourcing for most complex components.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Strategic Review Outcome Uncertainty (HIGH):** No timeline or binding offers disclosed. If no transaction materializes (25-35% probability), the stock could retrace toward $75-80 as the takeout premium dissipates and investors re-focus on the growth air pocket.

**2. Customer Concentration (HIGH):** Top 3 customers represent 49% of revenue. Loss of a major program or customer in-sourcing decision would be material.

**3. Estimate Deterioration (MODERATE-HIGH):** If Q2 2026 also disappoints, estimates may need further revision, compressing the multiple on lower earnings power.

**4. Tariff Exposure (MODERATE):** 25% of workforce in Mexico, 15% in Ireland. Specific tariff risk called out in 10-K. In the current trade policy environment, this creates margin pressure that may not be fully passed through.

**5. Securities Class Action Litigation:** The company discloses it is "currently experiencing securities class action litigation," likely triggered by the 40%+ stock decline following guidance cuts. While such suits typically settle for modest amounts, they consume management bandwidth and signal governance concerns.

**6. Short Interest:** At 14.2% with 8.4 days to cover, meaningful institutional capital is positioned short. Conversely, if a deal materializes, short covering could accelerate upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-115):** The strategic review yields a take-private transaction at 12-13x EBITDA ($110-125 per share including share count dilution from convertibles). PE firms have demonstrated appetite for medtech CDMOs. Integer's recurring revenue model, growth recovery potential, and margin expansion opportunity (ERP implementation, manufacturing optimization) make it an ideal LBO candidate. Activist pressure from Irenic Capital increases probability of board follow-through.

**Bear Case ($70-75):** The strategic review concludes without a transaction ("no actionable offer at acceptable terms"). Simultaneously, Q2 2026 results show the growth headwind extending beyond three products, estimates get cut further, and the stock re-rates to 11-12x trough adjusted EPS. Tariff costs compress margins by 100-200bp in H2 2026. The stock returns to its November 2025 range.

**Base Case ($90-100):** The strategic review continues through Q3 2026, maintaining a floor under the stock via takeout optionality. Growth stabilizes but doesn't recover meaningfully until 2027. Estimates settle in the $5.80-6.00 range for FY2026. The stock trades in a range bound by deal optionality (upside) and estimate cuts (downside).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Formal announcement of binding acquisition offer or "definitive agreement"
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate revisions (indicating the growth air pocket is resolving)
3. Stock decline to $75 or below with strategic review still active — at that level, risk/reward becomes asymmetrically favorable even without deal certainty

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Strategic review terminated without transaction
2. FY2026 EPS estimates fall below $5.50 (implying growth headwind is structural, not temporary)
3. Major customer loss disclosed or CRM&N organic growth turns negative beyond current expectations

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026). The next checkpoint is whether the growth headwind is stabilizing and whether the strategic review has progressed toward definitive outcomes.

## Conclusion

Integer Holdings presents a genuine variant perception setup: the market is pricing the 2026 growth headwind as partially structural while Irenic Capital views it as temporary and has catalyzed a strategic review that could unlock 20-50% upside. The business quality is high — sole publicly traded pure-play medical device CDMO with regulatory moats, sticky revenue, and broad technology capabilities.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY for specific, falsifiable reasons: (1) EPS estimates are falling at -6% to -10% over the past 30 days across all timeframes, confirmed negative fundamental momentum that could compress the apparent "cheap" multiple further; (2) 14.2% short interest with 8.4 days to cover indicates material informed skepticism; and (3) the strategic review announcement is legally non-committal ("considering a full range of potential opportunities"), with no disclosed timeline or minimum price floor, meaning the deal premium could evaporate without a transaction. A $90 stock with declining earnings and no guaranteed exit mechanism is not a high-conviction long until one of those uncertainties resolves.
