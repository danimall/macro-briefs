## Executive Summary

EVERTEC screens as one of the cheapest names in payment processing: 7.5x the midpoint of raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.94–$4.04), 6.9x FY2027 consensus of $4.36, and 7.1x enterprise value to trailing-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of $397.8M. It is also 20.8% below its 52-week high, carries a 5.7% short interest, and sits below the lowest published analyst target of $30.00. On those inputs alone this would be an accumulation candidate.

Three findings from the Q2 2026 10-Q (filed August 6) argue against acting on the headline multiple. First, the company's adjusted earnings base is materially tax-advantaged: a Puerto Rico Act 73 grant provides a 4% preferential income-tax rate covering more than 90% of Puerto Rico taxable income, and the 10-K states the income-tax benefit expires December 31, 2026 absent renewal or extension. The trailing adjusted effective rate is approximately 8.4%. A move to a 20% blended rate costs roughly $0.50 per share; 25% costs roughly $0.72 — 13% to 19% of guided adjusted EPS. I found no filing or news evidence establishing whether a renewal has been applied for or granted. Second, the growth is not coming from the profitable business. Q2 revenue rose $45.2M year over year, of which $44.8M came from the Latin America segment; the three Puerto Rico–anchored segments grew $3.8M combined, or 2.3%, with segment adjusted EBITDA up 2.9%. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin has fallen from 43.6% in FY2022 to 39.9% on a trailing basis. Third, the balance sheet is being used aggressively on both sides: total debt rose $195.4M in six months to $1,294.3M, secured net leverage moved from 2.08x to 2.55x, and $393.8M of term loan A plus the $200M revolver plus the entire $550M notional interest-rate hedge book all mature between October and December 2027.

The market appears to have read the same documents. The stock opened at $33.99 on August 5 following the August 4 beat-and-raise, then closed at $31.31, $30.94 and $29.85 over the following three sessions — giving back the entire post-print gain as the 10-Q reached investors. Against a base case of $33, a bear case of $23 and a bull case of $40, the probability-weighted return from $29.85 is roughly +8% including the 0.65% dividend. That is approximately fair compensation for a levered small-cap with an unresolved dated tax event, five putative class actions of undetermined scope, and a 2027 refinancing concentration. **HOLD.** Existing owners are adequately paid to wait for the grant question to resolve; new capital should wait for either that resolution or a price in the mid-$20s, where insiders bought and where the company itself repurchased 1.9M shares at a $25.21 average in Q2.

## Company Overview

EVERTEC is a Puerto Rico–domiciled transaction processor and financial-technology vendor operating in 26 countries. It owns and operates the ATH network — one of the leading PIN-debit networks in the Caribbean and Latin America — plus the ATH Móvil peer-to-peer and ATH Business person-to-merchant wallets, and processes over ten billion transactions annually. Four segments, using Q2 2026 total segment revenue and segment adjusted EBITDA:

| Segment | Q2'26 Revenue | Segment Adj. EBITDA | Margin | Q2'25 Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payment Services – PR & Caribbean | $60.9M | $36.9M | 60.6% | 58.5% |
| Latin America Payments & Solutions | $130.9M | $39.7M | 30.3% | 27.1% |
| Merchant Acquiring, net | $52.3M | $21.8M | 41.7% | 42.3% |
| Business Solutions | $58.8M | $22.6M | 38.3% | 40.3% |

Latin America is now the largest segment by revenue, assembled through acquisition (Sinqia, paySmart, BBR, PlacetoPay, Grandata, Lote45, Rosk, Tecnobank, Dimensa, BB Chain, among others) and consisting largely of licensed core-banking, insurance, pension and vehicle-financing software sold to Brazilian financial institutions.

Popular, Inc. is the largest customer at 24% of Q2 2026 revenue and 25% of first-half revenue, down from 31% in the prior-year first half. The relationship has two distinct legs. Under the amended Master Services Agreement (through September 30, 2028) Popular pays EVERTEC for core banking, BPO and related services; a 10% discount on certain MSA services took effect in October 2025. Separately, under the ISO Sponsorship and Services Agreement (through 2035) Banco Popular acts as sponsoring bank for merchant acquiring and receives a share of that revenue — in that leg Popular is a distribution partner and a recipient of revenue share rather than a payer. The ATH Network Participation Agreement runs through 2030. Popular is no longer a shareholder, having exited in an August 2022 secondary offering.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $618.4M | $694.7M | $845.5M | $931.8M | $522.7M |
| Income from operations | $157.4M | $136.2M | $165.7M | $186.4M | $97.8M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $269.5M | $292.0M | $340.2M | $373.4M | $206.4M |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 43.6% | 42.0% | 40.2% | 40.1% | 39.5% |

Revenue compounded at 14.6% from FY2022 to FY2025 and Q2 2026 revenue rose 19.7% to $274.8M, beating the $264M consensus. Adjusted EBITDA margin has compressed roughly 370 basis points over three and a half years, to 39.9% on a trailing basis (trailing adjusted EBITDA $397.8M on trailing revenue of $996.1M). The mechanism is straightforward: revenue mix is shifting from ~47%-margin Puerto Rico segments toward a 30.3%-margin Latin American software business, compounded by the October 2025 Popular price concession.

GAAP operating margin fell to 19.4% in Q2 from 24.4%, as depreciation and amortization rose 41% to $40.0M and SG&A rose 63% to $57.3M. The SG&A line includes cybersecurity incident response and remediation costs that are not separately quantified.

**GAAP earnings and the tax line.** Q2 GAAP diluted EPS was $0.09 versus $0.62; adjusted diluted EPS was $1.05 versus $0.89 and beat the four-analyst non-GAAP consensus of $0.95. Three items explain the GAAP decline: an $8.9M impairment on a Costa Rica–originated joint-venture equity investee that management decided in Q2 to exit; income tax expense of $20.3M on pretax income of $27.2M, a 74.7% effective rate (43.7% for the half) versus 9.0% a year earlier; and interest expense up $3.5M to $20.3M on debt raised for Tecnobank and Dimensa. The tax drivers disclosed are a repatriation tax on a dividend from a foreign subsidiary used to partly fund Dimensa, a valuation allowance against capital losses from the impairment, and geographic mix shifting toward higher-tax jurisdictions. The first two are non-recurring; the third is durable. Cash taxes paid in the half ($29.8M) exceeded book tax expense ($24.5M), so the step-up is a cash event.

**Cash generation.** This is where the multiple has to be tested, because EVERTEC capitalizes substantial internally developed software that appears in investing as additions to software and intangibles rather than in PP&E capex. Using management's own definition of total capital investment:

- FY2025: operating cash flow $227.0M − capital investment $91.5M = **$135.5M FCF**, a 14.5% FCF margin.
- FY2024: $260.1M − $88.4M = **$171.7M**. FY2025 free cash flow therefore *declined 21%* while revenue grew 10%.
- 1H 2026: $90.7M − ($11.3M PP&E + $36.6M software/intangibles + $0.4M non-compete) = **$42.4M**, versus $43.1M a year earlier — flat.

A screen computing FCF as operating cash flow less PP&E capex only would report roughly $203.7M for FY2025 and a 21.9% margin. That excludes approximately $68M of annual capitalized software and overstates cash generation for this model. The first-half figure should not be annualized: the period included $29.8M of cash taxes inflated by the Dimensa repatriation, a $25.1M favorable swing in payables, and EVERTEC discloses seasonal payments strength around holiday shopping. A normalized FY2026 range of $120–150M is more defensible than either extreme.

The gap between adjusted net income and cash is structural. Trailing adjusted net income is $240.1M against FY2025 FCF of $135.5M — roughly 1.7x. The adjustments include $68.4M of trailing acquisition-related intangible amortization (a recurring feature for a serial acquirer, not a one-off) and $39.2M of share-based compensation and severance. Burdening trailing adjusted EPS of approximately $3.81 with after-tax SBC of roughly $0.47 gives about $3.40, moving the multiple from 7.8x to 8.8x. On FY2025 FCF per share, the stock trades near 13–14x.

|---|---|---|
| Cash & equivalents | $306.0M | $260.7M |
| Goodwill | $892.0M | $1,070.5M |
| Other intangibles, net | $553.1M | $610.8M |

Net debt of $1,033.6M puts enterprise value at $2,817M ($2,891M including redeemable NCI), or 7.1x trailing adjusted EBITDA. Trailing interest expense of approximately $72.2M gives 5.5x coverage. Goodwill and intangibles total $1,681.3M, or 67.8% of assets; tangible book equity is roughly negative $1.04B. Covenant maximum is 4.00x against 2.55x reported, and $159.4M of revolver is undrawn.

An Altman Z-Score of 1.48 sits in the distress zone, but reproducing the inputs shows it is driven by asset turnover of 0.42 and an asset base that is two-thirds goodwill and intangibles. Against 5.5x coverage, covenant headroom and positive free cash flow, I do not read it as a solvency signal. It is a legitimate signal about acquisition-funded balance-sheet quality.

The more consequential finding is a maturity concentration no standard leverage screen surfaces. The $393.8M term loan A and the $200M revolver both mature December 1, 2027, and all three interest-rate swaps ($250M at 3.375%, $150M at 4.182%, $150M at 4.172%, each against one-month SOFR) mature in October and December 2027. Roughly $594M of committed facilities and the entire $550M notional hedge book roll off inside the same two-month window, against a 2030-maturity term loan B of $875M. The company's disclosed sensitivity — 100bp adds approximately $7.5M of annual interest — is computed with hedges in place and therefore understates post-2027 exposure. The term loan A applicable margin already stepped from 1.75% to 2.00% between year-end and June 30, indicating a leverage-grid trigger was crossed. Sixteen months of lead time, recurring revenue and demonstrated lender appetite (the term loan B has been upsized and repriced repeatedly) make orderly refinancing the base case, but the repricing will occur at whatever spread then-current leverage commands.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was raised to revenue of $1.09–$1.10B and adjusted EPS of $3.94–$4.04. The midpoint implies 17.5% revenue growth on FY2025's $931.8M. Backing out first-half actuals, guidance implies second-half revenue of $567–577M versus $473.4M (+20% to +22%) but second-half adjusted EPS of $1.99–$2.09 versus $1.86 (+7% to +12%). Revenue is guided to accelerate while adjusted EPS growth decelerates — the arithmetic consequence of lower-margin mix, higher interest expense and a rising tax rate.

The composition matters more than the rate. Of Q2's $45.2M revenue increase, $44.8M came from Latin America (segment revenue $130.9M versus $86.1M), of which $9.1M was favorable FX, primarily Brazilian real. The company does not disaggregate acquired from organic contribution. The three Puerto Rico–anchored segments moved from $168.2M to $172.0M in total segment revenue, up 2.3%, with combined segment adjusted EBITDA up 2.9%.

Two considerations cut against reading the core as structurally stagnant. Business Solutions revenue fell $5.7M in the quarter and $11.7M in the half specifically because of the 10% Popular MSA discount plus a prior-year non-recurring hardware and software sale; the discount is a step-down that annualizes out of comparisons after Q3 2026. Working the other direction, the Payment Services increase itself included an unquantified non-recurring volume-based benefit. Two quarters of clean post-discount comparisons are needed before drawing a conclusion, which places the evidence in Q1 2027.

The secular case for Latin America is credible and the company cites external work projecting a 17.4% five-year CAGR for regional non-cash volumes, with volumes having grown 22.4% from 2024 to 2025. That is the correct reason to own the Brazilian buildout. The reservation is price: Tecnobank cost $148.8M for 75% with $125.5M of goodwill (84% of consideration); Dimensa cost $199.3M for 100% with $151.9M of goodwill (76%); combined 2025–26 Brazilian outlay is approximately $353.7M of which roughly $277M is non-tax-deductible goodwill. EVERTEC is paying for expected synergies and growth rather than acquiring identifiable assets at a discount, in a market where Totvs is the entrenched enterprise-software incumbent.

Consensus estimate revisions are flat: current quarter −0.2% over 30 days, current year +1.7%, next year +0.4%. The earnings-inflection signal that would justify overriding valuation concerns is absent here. The record is three beats and one miss over four quarters, with beats of 1.5%, 2.2% and 8.3% and a 4.3% miss in Q1 2026 — adequate visibility, not exceptional.

Remaining performance obligations of $780.3M at June 30 should not be read as backlog. EVERTEC applies the ASC 606 practical expedients, excluding contracts of one year or less and right-to-invoice arrangements, which management says cover the majority of total revenue. The figure is a partial slice. Contract liabilities declined from $73.6M to $67.8M.

The valuation conclusion depends entirely on which earnings basis is used, and the two bases are far apart.

| Forward P/E on guided adjusted EPS ($3.99) | 7.5x | — |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus ($4.36) | 6.9x | — |
| EV / TTM adjusted EBITDA | 7.1x | — |
| P / FY2025 FCF per share (~$2.10–2.27) | 13–14x | — |
| Dividend yield | 0.65% | 0.0% |

Note that the supplied profile fields conflict internally: trailing and forward P/E are both listed at 19.27 with EPS of $2.20, which cannot both hold at $29.85. The 19.3x figure reconciles to trailing GAAP EPS of roughly $1.55, consistent with trailing GAAP net income attributable of approximately $97.6M on ~61.5M diluted shares. I use the 10-Q and guidance-derived figures throughout.

On GAAP earnings, EVERTEC trades at the payment-processing peer median (19.7x) — not cheap. On adjusted forward earnings it is the cheapest name in the comparable set other than Western Union (5.4x with a 12.8% yield, a business in secular decline) and Euronet (9.7x). The 7.5x forward figure is genuine only if adjusted EPS is a durable proxy for economics, and two adjustments argue it is not: acquisition amortization recurs by design at a serial acquirer, and share-based compensation is a real dilutive cost. Haircutting for after-tax SBC alone moves the multiple to 8.8x. Applying the tax sensitivity on top — a 20% blended rate would take FY2027 adjusted EPS from $4.36 to roughly $3.85 — moves it to about 7.8x on a lower base.

The 13–14x multiple of properly computed free cash flow, a 7.6% equity FCF yield, and a 4.8% yield on enterprise value describe a business priced for low-single-digit growth in its profitable segment and no re-rating of the acquired one. That is defensible rather than mispriced. The five-year total-shareholder-return record disclosed in the proxy — an indexed $73.98 for EVERTEC against $178.50 for the peer group, roughly 105 percentage points of underperformance — while adjusted EBITDA grew 27% from 2021 to 2025, shows the market has consistently declined to pay up for EBITDA growth assembled with debt. Persistent cheapness that does not converge is the profile of a multiple that reflects capital structure and earnings quality rather than a pricing error.

Analyst targets span $30.00 to $40.00 with a mean of $35.60 and median $36.00, against a split rating (two strong buy, one buy, three hold) from six analysts. The stock sits below the lowest target. On a stock at 7.5x adjusted forward earnings that would ordinarily be treated as a floor rather than a ceiling; here I weight it less than usual, because coverage is thin (Zacks polled four analysts on the print), one syndicated note carried a stale $25.00 target as recently as June 10, and the dominant swing variable — grant renewal — is not something the sell side has any better information on than the filings provide.

Scenario framework:

- **Bear $23 (−23%):** grant lapses or is replaced at materially higher rates, taking FY2027 adjusted EPS to roughly $3.70–3.85; cyber litigation produces a quantified liability; 2027 refinancing prices wider. 6.0–6.2x on the reset base.
- **Base $33 (+10.6%):** grant renewed with modest rate drift plus Pillar Two, FY2027 adjusted EPS around $4.20; 8.0x. Cross-check: $145M FY2027 FCF on ~58M shares is $2.50 per share at 13x = $32.50.
- **Bull $40 (+34%):** grant renewed on similar terms, Puerto Rico core reaccelerates once the Popular discount anniversaries after Q3 2026, Latin American margins climb toward the mid-30s on Dimensa integration, buyback sustains 4–5% annual share reduction; FY2027 adjusted EPS $4.50 at 9.0x.

Weighting bear 25% / base 50% / bull 25% gives roughly +8% including the dividend. That is fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

In Puerto Rico the position is entrenched. EVERTEC owns the ATH network and the ATH Móvil and ATH Business wallets, is the incumbent core processor for the island's banks, and is the only non-bank provider of cash-processing services to the U.S. Federal Reserve in the Caribbean. Switching costs on a small Caribbean bank's core system are high, and the 60.6% and 41.7% segment margins reflect that. The market is small, demographically constrained and fiscally stressed, growing at low single digits, and the largest customer has already extracted a 10% price concession with the MSA up for renewal in September 2028. The ISO agreement contains a remedy under which EVERTEC may have to assign up to 50% of its merchant contracts to Banco Popular if that agreement is not renewed or is terminated — an unusually severe contractual term for a processor's largest distribution relationship.

Note also that the reported decline in Popular concentration from 31% to 25% overstates diversification. Applying the disclosed percentages, Popular revenue was approximately $142.1M in 1H 2025 and $130.7M in 1H 2026, an absolute decline of roughly $11.4M closely matching the $11.7M Business Solutions decline. Roughly 3.8 points of the 6-point ratio decline came from denominator growth via acquisitions and FX; about 2.2 points came from Popular revenue contracting. Dependence has not fallen as much as the ratio implies, and the economically deepest ties (ISO through 2035, ATH through 2030) extend beyond the 2028 MSA expiry.

In Brazil, EVERTEC is a consolidator entering against Totvs and Stefanini plus global core-banking vendors, at 30.3% segment margins, with goodwill at 76–84% of purchase prices. Tecnobank warrants specific attention: it operates as a trusted third party between lending institutions and Brazilian state traffic departments, recording liens on vehicle documentation. That is a regulated-intermediary franchise dependent on the continuation of a specific regulatory regime and on Brazilian auto-finance origination volumes.

The competitive factor that does not appear as a named competitor is Pix, Brazil's central-bank-operated instant payment rail, free at the point of use. EVERTEC monetizes connectivity and processing into Pix rather than an interchange-bearing card rail. The August 2025 incident, in which approximately R$710M of unauthorized transactions affected two Sinqia customers, illustrates that participating in the rail carries operational liability without the economics of owning it.

## Risk Assessment

**Puerto Rico Act 73 grant expiry (highest-magnitude, dated, unresolved).** The 4% preferential rate covers more than 90% of Puerto Rico taxable income; income-tax benefits expire December 31, 2026 absent renewal, with property-tax benefits expiring December 31, 2027 and municipal license benefits June 30, 2028. Reported effective rates were 4.1% in FY2024, 6.4% in FY2025 and 43.7% in 1H 2026 (distorted by one-off items). The trailing adjusted effective rate is approximately 8.4%. Sensitivities: roughly $0.50 per share at a 20% blended rate, $0.72 at 25%. Grant commitments (≥700 Puerto Rico employees, 80% resident workforce, ≥$200M cumulative investment plus best-efforts $75M by December 31, 2026) appear to be things EVERTEC already satisfies, and such grants have historically been renewable for qualifying employers, so a routine renewal that makes this a non-event is the more likely single outcome. But nothing in the filings or available news establishes renewal status either way, and Pillar Two took effect for EVERTEC on January 1, 2026 with the Brazilian qualified domestic minimum tax still under evaluation. The grant and Pillar Two are correlated exposures.

**Cybersecurity, now in litigation.** Two incidents in ten months at a company selling trust to regulated banks. The August 2025 Sinqia/Pix event involved approximately R$710M of unauthorized transactions across two customers, who recovered significant portions; FY2025 cost of revenues includes unquantified expenses for contractual claims related to client losses. The Puerto Rico incident, discovered May 13, 2026 and disclosed in early June, involved potential unauthorized access via a third-party support platform exposing transaction records, some payment card numbers and some contact details. Five putative class actions have been filed since June 12, 2026, and the issuer stated it had not determined full scope, potential liabilities, or the extent of insurance coverage. Both incidents occurred in environments adjacent to core switches rather than in them, and neither produced disclosed operational disruption; the 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors. Recurrence nonetheless bears on renewal pricing and on the 2028 Popular negotiation.

**2027 refinancing and hedge rolloff.** Described above. Base case orderly; the exposure is spread, not access.

**Redeemable non-controlling interest puts.** $74.2M sits in mezzanine equity outside reported debt. Lote45 (43.68%) becomes puttable from January 1, 2027 at a formula tied to net sales, EBITDA margin and net debt; Tecnobank (25%) from April 30, 2029. The company already paid $23.1M in the first half to acquire NCI positions.

**Goodwill.** The annual test date is August 31. The August 31, 2025 test cleared, but $151.9M of Dimensa and $125.5M of Tecnobank goodwill entered the Latin America reporting unit afterward at prices struck when the real traded differently. Impairment risk is concentrated in Latin America ($731.4M of $1,070.5M), not in the Popular-exposed units.

**Brazil concentration.** 45% of the ~5,327 employees, approximately 2,330 unionized; FX added $9.1M to Q2 and $15.8M to first-half revenue and can reverse. The AOCI translation loss improved from −$63.4M to −$4.6M in six months, a $58.8M translation flow into comprehensive income that should not be confused with operating performance.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 23 contracts across three expirations — so these readings carry low reliability and should not be weighted heavily. As supplied: near-term ATM implied volatility of 106.3% against 39.4% one-year realized, term structure in backwardation (106.3% at 11 days, 63.8% at 39 days, 36.5% at 102 days), elevated put demand with a 5.5% one-month and 8.0% three-month skew, and a ±17.1% implied move on the nearest expiry. Implied volatility has fallen from 121.6% on June 1 to 106.3% while the stock rose 20.5%, so the direction of change is toward less stress. The residual structure prices meaningful event risk in the September window even though earnings have passed; candidates include the August 31 goodwill test, the class-action consolidation docket, and any grant-related disclosure. Short interest at 5.7% of float with 3.8 days to cover is uncrowded and provides no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The elevated put demand is directionally consistent with the caution embedded in this HOLD rather than a challenge to it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($40, +34%).** Puerto Rico's Act 73 grant is renewed on comparable terms — the most likely single outcome given EVERTEC's compliance with employment and investment commitments and the commonwealth's interest in retaining 700+ data-processing jobs — removing the largest overhang and validating an 8–9x multiple on $4.50 of FY2027 adjusted EPS. The Popular discount anniversaries after Q3 2026, restoring reported Puerto Rico growth to the underlying volume trend. Dimensa integration lifts Latin American segment margin from 30.3% toward the mid-30s, arresting consolidated margin dilution. Buybacks continue at the recent cadence — 6.1% share-count reduction in 19 months, 1.9M shares in Q2 alone at a $25.21 average, authorization refreshed to $150M on July 31 — compounding per-share value at a 7.5x multiple. The May–June insider cluster (five insiders, 94,202 shares, ~$2.25M, no offsetting open-market sales) proves to have been informed.

**Bear case ($23, −23%).** The grant is not renewed or is replaced at a materially higher rate, resetting FY2027 adjusted EPS to $3.70–3.85 and exposing the fact that roughly a third of reported adjusted earnings was a tax artifact. The Puerto Rico core, once clean comparisons arrive in Q1 2027, proves to be growing at 2–3% structurally rather than temporarily, and the market marks the consolidated business toward the multiple appropriate to a 30%-margin Brazilian software roll-up rather than a 60%-margin network. Cyber litigation produces a quantified liability with insufficient insurance recovery. The December 2027 refinancing prices wider on higher leverage, and Lote45's January 2027 put exercise consumes cash. Debt-funded buybacks executed against an earnings base that reprices downward compound the error rather than the value.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The bull case requires an outcome I cannot underwrite from the evidence available, on a clock that runs out in under five months. The bear case is not remote — three of its four legs are already partly visible in the filings (2.3% core growth, five filed lawsuits, leverage at 2.55x and rising). At $24.77 in early June the price compensated for that ambiguity; at $29.85, after a 20.5% advance and a post-print reversal that gave back the entire beat-and-raise gain, it approximately does not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 6–9 months, anchored to two dated events — the December 31, 2026 grant expiry and the Q4 2026 report (late February 2027), which will carry the first clean post-Popular-discount comparison and the FY2027 tax-rate guide.

**Scenario summary from $29.85:** base $33 (+10.6%); bull $40 (+34%, matching the high analyst target); bear $23 (−23%). Probability-weighted return approximately +8% including the 0.65% dividend.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Filed or announced confirmation that the Act 73 grant (or an Act 60 successor) has been renewed at a rate below 10%, or FY2027 guidance embedding an effective tax rate under 12%. This alone would justify an 8–9x multiple and re-rate the stock toward the base-to-bull range.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Puerto Rico–anchored segment revenue growth above 5% once the Popular discount anniversaries after Q3 2026, with combined segment adjusted EBITDA growing at least in line.
3. A price of $25.50 or below on unchanged fundamentals — the level where five insiders bought in May–June and where the company repurchased 1.9M shares at a $25.21 average. At that price the base case offers roughly 30% upside against the bear case, restoring the asymmetry.
4. Term loan A and revolver extended beyond 2027 with the applicable margin at or below 2.00% and a replacement hedge book put in place.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2027 guidance embedding an effective tax rate above 18%, or explicit disclosure that the grant will lapse without replacement. That takes adjusted EPS below $3.90 and the fair-value range toward the bear case.
2. Secured net leverage above 3.25x, or a further debt-funded acquisition before the 2027 maturities are addressed.
3. A quantified cyber liability, adverse consolidation or class-certification ruling, or disclosure that insurance recovery is materially unavailable.
4. Any goodwill impairment in the Latin America reporting unit at the August 31 test date, which would mark the Tecnobank/Dimensa prices to market.
5. Buyback suspension, which would remove the primary per-share value mechanism and signal balance-sheet strain.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report (early November 2026), which should carry management commentary on grant-renewal status ahead of the December 31 expiry and the August 31 goodwill-test outcome. An interim reassessment is warranted on any 8-K addressing the tax grant or the class actions.

## Conclusion

EVERTEC is a genuinely franchise-quality business in Puerto Rico attached to a debt-funded, lower-margin Brazilian software roll-up, priced at a multiple that reflects both facts. At 7.5x guided adjusted EPS, 7.1x EV/EBITDA and 13–14x properly computed free cash flow, the valuation is undemanding — but the adjusted earnings base rests on an 8.4% effective tax rate whose statutory support expires in under five months with no confirmed renewal, and the growth that supports the multiple is arriving at 30% margins with 76–84% goodwill content while the 47%-margin core grows 2.3%.

The positives are real and specific: a five-insider open-market buying cluster with no offsetting sales, buybacks executed below the current price that have retired 6.1% of shares in 19 months, a raised guide, 5.5x interest coverage against a 4.00x covenant, and a mark-to-market compensation structure that has demonstrably paid executives less than granted through the derating. Those support holding. They do not overcome a dated binary that could reset 13–19% of earnings, five lawsuits of undetermined scope, and a December 2027 window in which $594M of facilities and $550M of hedges roll off simultaneously — not at a price 20.5% above where insiders and the company itself were buying.

**HOLD.** Own it through the grant resolution; add on confirmation of renewal or on a decline toward $25.50.
