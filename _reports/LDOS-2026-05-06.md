## Executive Summary

Leidos Holdings has declined 34% from its November 2025 high of $205.77 to $135.40, driven by two distinct factors: (1) the May 5, 2026 Q1 FY2026 earnings release revealed lower-than-expected GAAP EPS ($2.56 vs. $3.13 adjusted) due to Entrust-related costs, triggering an approximately 7% selloff, and (2) broader DOGE/government efficiency narrative has compressed multiples across the defense IT services sector (CACI -27%, SAIC -24%, KBR -36% from 52-week highs). Despite this, Q1 FY2026 adjusted EPS beat consensus by approximately 8.7% ($3.13 vs. $2.88), full-year revenue guidance was raised to $18.0-$18.4B, operating margins expanded to 12.3% in FY2025, and FCF reached $1.63B in FY2025.

The stock now trades at approximately 11.0x forward earnings (based on FY2026 guidance midpoint EPS of ~$12.30). Consensus estimates are rising across all periods (next-year EPS up 3.0% over 90 days). The $48.4B backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility, though only $9.6B is funded. The market is pricing a scenario where government budget cuts materially impair Leidos — but with 87% of revenue from the U.S. government, 53% of employees holding security clearances, and defense/intelligence spending explicitly prioritized by the current administration, the discount appears excessive relative to the probability-weighted downside.

## Company Overview

Leidos is a $17B+ defense technology and services company with approximately 50,000 employees (post-Entrust acquisition). Beginning in fiscal 2026, it operates across four realigned segments: Intelligence & Digital, Health, Homeland, and Defense. The company serves primarily U.S. government customers with a contract base spanning DoD, intelligence community, VA, FAA, DOE, and DHS.

The March 2026 completion of the $2.4B Entrust acquisition doubles its energy infrastructure design capabilities and adds 3,100 employees. This diversifies revenue away from pure government dependence and positions Leidos within the grid modernization investment cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability trajectory is exceptional.** Operating margins expanded from 7.6% (FY2022) to 12.3% (FY2025). The Health segment (formerly part of Health & Civil) operates at exceptionally high margins — FY2025 Health & Civil achieved 23.7% — an anomalous margin level for government services, driven by managed health (VA disability exams) with limited competition and technology-enabled scale efficiencies. However, Q1 FY2026 showed GAAP margin deterioration in certain segments: Homeland operating margin fell to 4.0% from 7.9% YoY (impacted by Entrust/JV costs and write-downs), and Defense margin declined to 7.0% from 8.4% (due to contract completions and schedule delays on a fixed-price development program).

**Cash flow quality is high.** FY2025 OCF was $1.75B, exceeding net income ($1.45B) by 21%. FCF of $1.63B on just $125M capex produces a 9.5% FCF margin. At the current $17.1B market cap, FCF yield is approximately 9.5% — significantly above both the risk-free rate and the sector average.

**Balance sheet leverage has increased materially post-Entrust.** Following the March 2026 Entrust close, the Q1 FY2026 balance sheet (April 3, 2026) shows total debt of approximately $6.3B with cash of $457M. The acquisition was funded through $600M of 4.10% notes due 2029 and $800M of 5.00% notes due 2036 (the $1.4B bridge facility was undrawn and terminated after the note issuance). The company also had $300M of commercial paper outstanding at quarter-end. The $1.5B revolver (expanded from $1.0B in February 2026) had no borrowings outstanding. Post-Entrust debt/equity is approximately 1.25x. Altman Z-Score of 2.82 (pre-Entrust) sits near the safe boundary.

**Capital returns have been aggressive.** FY2025 saw $900M in buybacks ($400M open market + $500M ASR) plus $211M in dividends against $1.63B FCF. The current 1.2% dividend yield is modest but well-covered. The Warfighter Executive Order introduces novel constraints on future buyback/dividend activity if the Secretary of War determines "underperformance."

**Key metrics:**
- Revenue: $17.2B (FY2025), guidance $18.0-$18.4B (FY2026)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 14% (Q1 FY2026)
- ROE: 29.7% (FY2025)
- FCF yield: ~9.5% on current market cap
- Goodwill: $8.1B post-Entrust (approximately 53% of Q1 FY2026 total assets of $15.4B)

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has decelerated from 7.9% (FY2024) to 3.1% (FY2025) on an organic basis. However, with Entrust contributing for approximately nine months of FY2026, reported growth should reach 5-7% ($18.0-$18.4B guidance range). Organic growth of 3-4% is realistic given backlog conversion rates.

The $48.4B total backlog (funded + unfunded) provides multi-year revenue coverage. However, only $9.6B is funded backlog, and remaining performance obligations of $19B (with ~62% expected to convert over 12 months) provide a more conservative measure of near-term visibility. The FY2025 backlog policy change added $4.8B from sole-source IDIQ task order expectations. While defensible, this reduces the informational content of backlog comparisons.

**Growth vectors:**
1. Energy infrastructure (Entrust doubles addressable market to ~$1.2B base)
2. Air defense recapitalization ($617M IFPC contract win in April 2026)
3. AI/digital modernization (R&D spending up 46% over two years to $187M)
4. Grid modernization demand driven by data center buildout

**Consensus estimates** show gradual improvement: FY2026 EPS guidance midpoint ~$12.30, FY2027 EPS $13.16 (7% growth). The 90-day revision trend is modestly positive (+3.0% for next year), though not at the velocity associated with a strong re-rating signal. This is steady-state growth with modest upward momentum rather than a dramatic earnings acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $135.40 and FY2026 guidance midpoint EPS of approximately $12.30, LDOS trades at roughly 11.0x forward earnings. Among named government-services peers (CACI, SAIC, KBR, Amentum), available forward P/E multiples suggest LDOS trades approximately in line with the group median, with CACI as the premium-valued outlier at approximately 20x.

**What the current valuation reflects:**
- DOGE/government efficiency narrative creating uncertainty about future contract awards
- Post-Entrust integration risk and near-term EPS dilution (accretive to non-GAAP EPS only in FY2027)
- Broader de-rating of government IT services sector (CACI, SAIC, KBR all deeply discounted from highs)
- Warfighter Executive Order creating novel capital allocation constraints
- Materially higher leverage post-Entrust ($6.3B debt vs. $457M cash)

**Why the stock may be undervalued:**
- 9.5% FCF yield on a business with 87% government revenue, 53% cleared workforce, and $48.4B backlog
- At 15x forward earnings ($12.30 midpoint), the stock would trade at approximately $185 — consistent with the analyst consensus median of $201.50
- The adjusted EPS beat streak demonstrates that underlying business performance is tracking above expectations
- Health segment margins remain exceptionally high, providing earnings stability

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $199.21, median $201.50, low $165, high $232. Current price represents 47% upside to mean target.

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos occupies a defensible position in government IT services. Three structural advantages sustain its competitive moat:

1. **53% security-cleared workforce** — this is the single most important barrier to entry in defense IT. Clearance processing takes 12-24 months and requires existing contracts to sponsor candidates. New entrants cannot replicate this at scale.

2. **Health segment incumbency** — the VA managed health services business operates at exceptionally high margins with limited competitive overlap from traditional defense primes. This functions as an annuity stream.

3. **Post-Entrust positioning** — the combination of defense IT + energy infrastructure design creates a differentiated capability set. Grid modernization is a secular growth theme with multi-year investment visibility driven by electrification, data center demand, and resilience requirements.

**Competitive threats:** FAR overhaul under Executive Order 14275 could expand competition from commercial firms. OTA agreements bypassing traditional procurement favor agile competitors. AI integration is becoming a competitive differentiator, though Leidos' 46% R&D spending increase suggests management awareness.

The competitive position trajectory is stable-to-improving. Organic growth has decelerated but market positioning has strengthened through Entrust.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Government budget concentration (HIGH).** 87% of revenue from U.S. government with 49% from DoD/IC. Continuing resolutions delay new program starts. The DHS appropriations failure in February 2026 demonstrated vulnerability. A genuine DOGE-driven spending reduction in IT services would be highly impactful.

**2. Warfighter Executive Order (MEDIUM-HIGH).** This is a novel and poorly understood risk. Potential restrictions on buybacks/dividends during "underperformance" periods, plus mandated executive comp tied to delivery metrics, could constrain shareholder returns and talent retention. Referenced 5+ times in the 10-K risk factors — management is clearly concerned.

**3. Entrust integration and elevated leverage (MEDIUM-HIGH).** $2.4B acquisition funded through $1.4B in new senior notes plus commercial paper, pushing total debt to ~$6.3B while cash declined to $457M. Not accretive to non-GAAP EPS until FY2027. Integration of 3,100 employees and 40+ locations carries execution risk. Post-Entrust goodwill of $8.1B represents approximately 53% of total assets, increasing impairment sensitivity.

**4. Q1 FY2026 GAAP margin deterioration (MEDIUM).** While adjusted metrics beat expectations, GAAP operating income declined 4.2% YoY, GAAP EPS fell to $2.56 from $2.77, Homeland margins compressed to 4.0%, and Defense margins declined to 7.0%. Fixed-price development program schedule delays represent ongoing execution risk.

**5. Health segment concentration.** This segment generates a disproportionate share of operating income relative to its revenue contribution. Any contract loss or VA re-competition in managed health services would be disproportionately impactful to earnings.

**6. FAR overhaul.** First comprehensive Federal Acquisition Regulation reform could reshape competitive dynamics over 2-3 years. Timeline and magnitude of impact are uncertain.

## Conclusion

Leidos represents a high-quality defense IT services business with demonstrated margin expansion, strong FCF generation, and a $48.4B backlog, trading at approximately 11x forward earnings. The valuation reflects legitimate concerns about government budget uncertainty, post-acquisition leverage, integration execution, and GAAP margin deterioration in Homeland and Defense segments. However, these risks appear overpriced at a 9.5% FCF yield given the company's cleared workforce moat, health segment profitability, and administration prioritization of defense/intelligence spending.

The stock is appropriate for investors with a 12-18 month horizon who can tolerate near-term volatility related to geopolitical events and government spending narrative shifts. Position sizing should reflect the elevated post-Entrust leverage, integration risk, and binary nature of the defense spending outlook. A 2-3% portfolio allocation is appropriate given the asymmetric risk/reward profile.
