**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Archer-Daniels-Midland is a global agricultural commodity originator, processor, and merchandiser, one of the "ABCD" oligopolists (with Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus) that intermediate the world's grain and oilseed trade. Despite its Consumer Staples classification, the business is sharply cyclical: net income fell 75% from $4.34B in 2022 to $1.08B in 2025 as crush spreads compressed, U.S. biofuel policy was deferred, and U.S.-China soybean friction disrupted trade flows. 2025 was the trough year, with GAAP EPS of $2.23 carrying roughly $580M of pre-tax specified items.

The investment debate is whether the 2026 recovery is durable. Q1 2026 confirmed a recovery: Carbohydrate Solutions operating profit rose 48% and Nutrition 42%, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $4.15-$4.70 (versus $3.43 in 2025), citing finalized 2026/2027 Renewable Volume Obligations and proposed Section 45Z clean-fuel regulations that lift crush and ethanol margins. Consensus EPS estimates have risen +15.4% for the current year and +11.3% for next year over the past 90 days, with the current quarter revised +50.2%. The company has beaten on EPS for four consecutive quarters by an average of ~8%. On forward earnings of roughly $4.43-$4.72, the stock trades at ~16-17x, below the 22.1x staples peer median.

The offset is a stack of company-specific risks that the rally has had to digest: a securities-fraud class action over improper Nutrition-segment intersegment accounting that survived a motion to dismiss in March 2025 with an un-estimable loss range; a 22.5% Wilmar stake carried ~$0.6B above market value with recurring impairment risk; a negative credit outlook across all three rating agencies; paused buybacks; and a core Ag Services & Oilseeds segment that still declined in Q1. These are real and material, and they have been public since November 2024. With the stock having rallied 41% off its trough and consensus price targets clustered at spot (mean $74.6, median $75.5), the upside and the risk are now roughly balanced. We rate ADM a HOLD.

## Company Overview

ADM operates in approximately 75 countries through three reportable segments:

- **Ag Services & Oilseeds (AS&O)**: ~$61.6B of 2025 revenue (~77% of total). Origination, transportation, storage, and trading of grains, plus oilseed crushing into vegetable oils, protein meals, and biodiesel feedstock. Includes the strategic 22.5% equity stake in Singapore-listed Wilmar International (~$4.0-4.1B carrying value).
- **Carbohydrate Solutions**: ~$10.7B. Corn and wheat wet/dry milling: sweeteners, starches, and ethanol (Vantage Corn Processors), plus carbon capture and storage operations.
- **Nutrition**: ~$7.5B. Higher-margin specialty ingredients: flavors, plant proteins, probiotics, texturants, and animal/pet feed. Intended as the structurally higher-margin growth engine, and the segment at the center of the 2024 accounting scandal.

ADM's moat is scale and an integrated origination-to-processing-to-transportation network with proximity to North and South American crop supply. AS&O and Carbohydrate Solutions are commodity-margin businesses with limited pricing power: earnings are driven by crush spreads, basis, trade flows, and biofuel policy rather than brand differentiation. The "Consumer Staples" label materially understates how cyclical the underlying business is.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant feature of ADM's recent history is a severe multi-year contraction off the 2021-22 commodity super-cycle:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $101.6B | $93.9B | $85.5B | $80.3B |
| Net income | $4.34B | $3.48B | $1.80B | $1.08B |
| Net margin | 4.3% | 3.7% | 2.1% | 1.3% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | — | — | $4.74 | $3.43 |
| GAAP diluted EPS | — | — | $3.65 | $2.23 |

**2025 trough mechanics.** GAAP pretax income fell 44% to $1.26B. Crushing operating profit collapsed from $844M to $159M as feedstock demand softened on deferred biofuel policy. The year also absorbed ~$372M of alt-protein/startup revaluation losses, a $179M capitalized-software impairment, $283M of restructuring, and a $163M Wilmar penalty charge. Adjusted ROIC declined steadily: 13.6% (2022) → 12.2% (2023) → 8.3% (2024) → 6.3% (2025), now below the cost of capital.

**Cash flow is a genuine support.** Operating cash flow was $5.45B in 2025 (up from $2.8B in 2024), aided by a ~$1.5B working-capital release on lower commodity prices, with FCF of ~$4.2B (5.2% margin). One caveat: OCF is partly inflated by ~$1.1B of brokerage-customer payables timing at ADM Investor Services, which is not core ag cash generation.

**Balance sheet is sturdier than the headline suggests.** Equity is $22.7B against total debt of ~$7.4B. The reported 2.3x D/E and the 2.57 Altman Z ("grey zone") are mechanically distorted by ~$9-10B of brokerage-customer payables (offset by segregated investments) and by trough-year EBIT. True financial leverage is moderate and investment-grade, with ~$9-10B of liquidity and a 1.37 current ratio. The real flag is the **negative outlook held by all three rating agencies**, which matters given ADM's reliance on cheap commercial-paper access for working capital.

**Capital allocation signals defense.** The dividend was prioritized ($987M paid in 2025, raised to $0.52/quarter in 2026; ADM is a 50+ year Dividend King), but **share buybacks were halted in 2025** ($0 versus $2.3B in 2024). That pause is a tacit acknowledgment that trough FCF and earnings quality did not support the prior repurchase pace.

**Q1 2026 recovery.** Revenue rose 1.6% to $20.5B; adjusted EPS was $0.71 (vs. $0.67 estimate). The Carbohydrate Solutions and Nutrition gains offset a 34% AS&O decline driven by negative mark-to-market timing.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data describes a clear earnings recovery. Current-year consensus EPS reached $4.72 and next-year $5.30. The combination of sharply rising estimates and a consistent beat cadence is among the more reliable forward indicators available, and at a forward multiple of ~16-17x the signal is not pre-priced into a premium valuation.

The qualifier is the *source* of the growth. The recovery is policy-driven, not self-help-driven. The largest single driver is external: the RVOs and Section 45Z regulations lifting crush and ethanol margins. The policy uncertainty that suppressed 2025 has substantially resolved, making the 2026 estimates a bet on realized regulation rather than a speculative binary. ADM generated no significant 45Z credits in 2025, so the credit regime represents incremental upside as it ramps.

Self-help is progressing but is not yet the earnings driver. The February 2025 portfolio-simplification plan targets >$500M of cumulative cost savings over 3-5 years, supported by an active JV/divest strategy (Two Rivers cottonseed JV, Akralos animal-feed JV with Alltech, Plainsman cottonseed JV). 2026 capex is guided to ~$1.4B. Nutrition, the intended higher-margin growth leg, generated only $417M of 2025 operating profit, small relative to AS&O, and its credibility was damaged by the accounting issue. The durable, structural growth story is therefore unproven; the cyclical recovery is real but rests on regulatory tailwinds that can reverse.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 34x P/E is not the right lens. It uses GAAP EPS of $2.23, trough earnings burdened by one-time specified items, as the denominator for a cyclical business inflecting off its low. For a commodity processor coming off a down-cycle, the appropriate metric is forward or normalized earnings.

On management's raised FY2026 guidance midpoint (~$4.43) and consensus CY EPS of $4.72, ADM trades at ~16-17x forward, below the 22.1x Consumer Staples peer median:

A forward multiple at a discount to the peer median, with estimates rising, does not support a low valuation score. But the appropriate peer set complicates the read: ADM is not a defensive staples name, and a 16-17x multiple on *peak-cycle-recovery* earnings carries the risk that those earnings prove temporary. The market is pricing ADM closer to a fairly-valued cyclical at the start of a recovery than a cheap mispricing. Analyst price targets reinforce this: mean $74.6 and median $75.5 cluster within 1% of the spot reference, with a $58 low and $95 high, a wide, two-sided distribution that reflects genuine uncertainty about earnings durability rather than consensus optimism.

The conclusion is a valuation that is reasonable, not asymmetric. The forward multiple at peer parity removes the case for AVOID-on-valuation; the policy-dependence and un-estimable litigation tail remove the case for a strong BUY. Fair value sits modestly above current levels in a base case, with a wide error band on either side. Our re-entry/accumulation level is the mid-$60s, roughly 10-15% below spot, which is a valuation preference, not a structural-impairment gap.

## Competitive Landscape

ADM operates in a consolidating, capital-intensive, low-margin global oligopoly. Peers are scaling: Bunge merged with Viterra, and state-owned competitors such as COFCO add price competition with non-commercial return objectives. ADM's competitive position is stable rather than improving: its scale, integrated network, and crop-supply proximity are durable, but AS&O (the ~77% core) is structurally commodity-margin and declined again in Q1 2026 on mark-to-market timing.

The Nutrition pivot is the intended differentiation lever, a higher-margin, less-cyclical growth leg, but at $417M of 2025 operating profit it remains small, and the 2024 accounting scandal directly damaged the credibility of the segment ADM markets as its premium story. The 2026 recovery is industry-wide (driven by biofuel policy that benefits all ABCD processors) rather than ADM-specific share gains. The competitive trajectory is flat: a defensible scale franchise that is neither widening nor visibly compressing its moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Accounting/governance overhang (most important company-specific risk).** The November 2024 Item 4.02 "Non-Reliance" filing flagged improper intersegment-sales accounting in the Nutrition segment. This triggered a securities-fraud class action whose motions to dismiss were **denied in March 2025** (the case proceeds), plus multiple shareholder derivative suits. ADM cannot estimate a loss range. This un-estimable financial and reputational tail risk raises the bar for trusting management's heavy reliance on adjusted (non-GAAP) metrics: adjusted EPS ($3.43) was 54% above GAAP EPS ($2.23) in 2025.

**Commodity-margin cyclicality (structural).** Crush spreads, basis, and trade flows are largely outside management control. 2025 showed how fast margins compress when supply is abundant and trade is disrupted.

**Biofuel/regulatory dependence.** The 2026 recovery thesis is fundamentally a bet on favorable implementation of the RVO and Section 45Z. The 2025 deferral directly compressed margins; a policy reversal would unwind much of the recovery.

**Wilmar carrying-value risk.** The stake traded at a market value of $3.4B at YE2025, already drove a $461M impairment in 2024 and a $163M penalty in 2025, and is a recurring source of non-operating earnings volatility with live other-than-temporary-impairment risk reassessed each quarter.

**Credit outlook.** All three agencies hold ADM investment-grade with a negative outlook, a flag for a business dependent on cheap commercial paper.

**Geopolitical/trade.** Russia-Ukraine Black Sea grain flows, U.S.-China soybean trade, and the IEEPA tariff/refund mechanics all affect ADM's trade flows and freight costs.

Most of these risks have been public since November 2024, and the stock rallied 41% through them, suggesting they are substantially priced. They do not constitute structural impairment, but they remove any asymmetric skew to the upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.0% is rich versus 26.9% one-year historical volatility (an 84th-percentile reading within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (50.0% near-term vs. 36.0% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk. The implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±1.0%, so the backwardation likely reflects a near-dated catalyst rather than a directional bet. Put/call skew is balanced (0.1%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.31, so positioning is not bearish: there is no elevated downside hedging despite the known overhangs. One print stands out: 1,708 contracts of the August $80 call (7.1x open interest), a bullish positioning above spot. IV rose from 41.8% to 50.0% as the price fell 6.8% since mid-May, consistent with uncertainty rather than a directional verdict. Net read: the options market is neutral-to-mildly-constructive, which aligns with a HOLD rather than confirming a bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90-95)
The biofuel-policy recovery proves durable, the 45Z credit regime ramps through 2026/2027, and crush/ethanol margins normalize toward mid-cycle. FY2026 EPS lands at the top of the guide and 2027 estimates hold, supporting continued upward revisions. The $500M cost program delivers, Nutrition recovers credibility, and the securities litigation resolves without a material judgment. At ~18-19x normalized $5.00+ earnings, the stock reaches the analyst high of $95.

### Bear Case ($55-60)
Biofuel policy implementation disappoints or partially reverses, crush spreads stay compressed, and the cyclical recovery proves shallow. The securities-fraud case advances toward a material settlement, Wilmar takes a further impairment, and the negative credit outlook converts to a downgrade, raising working-capital funding costs. Earnings revert toward the mid-$3 range and the stock derates to the analyst low near $58.

### Base Case ($75-82)
FY2026 EPS lands near the guidance midpoint, 2027 sees modest further growth, and the forward multiple holds at ~16-17x. The known overhangs persist without resolving sharply in either direction. The stock grinds modestly higher from spot, supported by the ~2.7% dividend yield and Dividend King status, with the analyst median of $75.5 as the anchor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will test whether the AS&O core stabilizes and whether the 45Z credit ramp begins to contribute. The biofuel-policy implementation cadence and any movement in the securities litigation are the ongoing monitorables.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward multiple compresses to <14x on stable-or-rising estimates (a pullback to the mid-$60s on unchanged fundamentals), restoring a margin of safety.
- AS&O operating profit turns to year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the recovery has reached the core rather than just Carbohydrate Solutions and Nutrition.
- Securities-fraud litigation resolves with a quantified, immaterial loss, removing the un-estimable tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse development in the securities-fraud case (a large quantified loss estimate or unfavorable summary-judgment ruling).
- A credit-rating downgrade from any of the three agencies, raising working-capital funding costs.
- FY2026 guidance cut below the $4.15 floor, or a biofuel-policy reversal that compresses crush margins.
- A further Wilmar other-than-temporary impairment of material size.

## Conclusion

ADM is a genuine cyclical recovery that has rallied 41% off its 2025 trough on estimate-confirmed earnings, the profile where reflexively avoiding a rallying depressed name is a documented error. The forward multiple of ~16-17x sits below the staples peer median, estimates are rising sharply, the company has beaten for four straight quarters, and the key policy catalyst (RVO finalization, 45Z) has largely resolved favorably rather than remaining an open binary. That evidence rules out an AVOID.

What it does not support is a BUY. The recovery is policy-dependent rather than self-help-driven, the ~77% AS&O core still declined in Q1, and a stack of un-estimable risks (securities-fraud litigation past the motion-to-dismiss stage, recurring Wilmar impairment, a negative credit outlook, and paused buybacks) offsets the recovery and removes any asymmetric upside. Consensus targets cluster within 1% of spot, and our preferred accumulation level (mid-$60s) is only ~10-15% below current price, a valuation preference rather than a structural-impairment gap. The risk and reward are balanced, the durability of the earnings recovery is genuinely uncertain, and the appropriate posture is to hold for the ~2.7% dividend while monitoring the Q2 print and the litigation docket. **HOLD.**
