## Executive Summary

NCR Atleos is the world's largest independent ATM services provider, operating across 65+ countries with 600,000+ serviced machines. The company was spun off from NCR Corporation in October 2023 and has since demonstrated meaningful operational improvement: GAAP net income swung from -$134M in FY2023 to +$162M in FY2025, ATMaaS revenue grew 33% to $258M, and adjusted EBITDA margins expanded to 19.1%.

The investment thesis is now dominated by the February 26, 2026 announcement that The Brink's Company will acquire NATL for approximately $50.40/share ($30 cash + 0.1574 Brink's shares). At the current price of $44.19, this represents a 14% gross spread to deal completion, expected Q1 2027. The stock has appreciated 53% over the past year on a combination of improving fundamentals and the deal premium.

The merger arbitrage spread must compensate for: regulatory approval across 65+ jurisdictions, NATL shareholder vote, Brink's financing execution ($4.5B bridge facility), and approximately 10 months of time value. With options markets pricing elevated near-term volatility (48.3% IV vs 39.5% HV) in steep backwardation, and the most recent quarter showing an EPS miss (-26.9% vs consensus), the situation demands a neutral posture pending deal clarity.

## Company Overview

NCR Atleos operates three segments: Self-Service Banking (66% of revenue, ATM hardware/software/managed services), Network (29%, the Allpoint surcharge-free ATM network with ~78,000 locations), and Telecommunications & Technology (4%, a legacy managed network business declining ~13% annually). The company employs approximately 20,000 people, generates $4.35B in annual revenue, and maintains manufacturing operations in Chennai, India.

The strategic transformation centers on converting traditional hardware-plus-maintenance relationships into comprehensive "ATM as a Service" (ATMaaS) contracts, which roughly double recurring revenue per customer and achieve ~40% gross margins. ATMaaS units reached 33,200 as of Q3 2025, with 40+ new customers added in the trailing twelve months.

Major retail partners include Circle K, Costco, CVS, Kroger, Target, and Walgreens. International markets contribute 55% of revenue, providing geographic diversification away from U.S. cashless payment trends.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $4.19B (FY2023) → $4.31B (FY2024) → $4.35B (FY2025). Headline growth of 1% masks stronger core performance: Self-Service Banking grew 7% in FY2025, diluted by wind-down of Voyix commercial agreements (-72% in "Other" revenue). Q1 2026 showed 7% revenue growth.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating income improved from $271M to $478M over FY2023-2025. Adjusted EBITDA reached $830M (19.1% margin). Gross margin expanded steadily: 22.2% → 23.7% → 24.4% (GAAP). The business is demonstrating operating leverage as ATMaaS scales.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $356M in FY2025; free cash flow of $239M (5.5% margin). Adjusted free cash flow (unrestricted) improved 35% to $326M. Capital expenditures are rising ($117M vs $87M prior year) to support ATMaaS deployment.

**Balance Sheet:** This is the central vulnerability. Total debt of $2,798M against stockholders' equity of only $403M produces a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 6.9x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.18 places the company in the distress zone. However, net leverage of 2.99x Adjusted EBITDA is within the 4.25x covenant requirement and has been declining. Available liquidity is ~$900M. Interest expense of $270M in FY2025 consumed 56% of GAAP operating income. The $1,350M of 9.5% senior secured notes due 2029 is callable at 104.75 in October 2026.

**FCF Yield:** At the current $3.26B market cap, the $239M GAAP FCF represents a 7.3% yield. On an adjusted basis ($326M), it's 10.0%. These are attractive yields for a business growing mid-single digits with improving margins.

## Growth Analysis

The ATMaaS conversion represents genuine structural growth within a mature industry. Key metrics:
- ATMaaS revenue: $153M → $194M → $258M (FY2023-2025), with Q3 2025 tracking 37% YoY growth
- ATMaaS ARR run-rate: $268M as of Q3 2025
- 40+ new ATMaaS customers added in trailing twelve months
- Gross margins expanding approximately 700bps on converted units

The Network segment faces headwinds from U.S. immigration policy changes affecting prepaid payroll card transactions and structural competition from digital payments. Network Managed Units declined from 83K to 78.4K since 2023, though ARPU reached a record $16.2K, suggesting effective monetization of a smaller but higher-value base.

Long-term growth ceiling exists: global cash usage faces secular decline from digital payments. Atleos mitigates this through international expansion, new transaction types (crypto, deposits, bill pay), and the essential-service nature of cash access for unbanked populations. R&D spending of $70M (1.6% of revenue) is low, reflecting the company's operational rather than technological competitive advantage.

Consensus estimates project EPS of $4.88 (current year) and $5.66 (next year), though recent revisions are negative: next year EPS cut 4.7% over the past 90 days. The Q1 2026 EPS miss of -26.9% vs consensus creates uncertainty around near-term trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $44.19, NATL trades at approximately 20.9x trailing earnings ($2.14 EPS) and roughly 9.0x forward EPS on the $4.88 consensus. Against the peer group (median P/E of 12.8x), the stock appears slightly premium on trailing metrics but reasonable on forward estimates.

However, the Brink's acquisition at ~$50.40/share makes traditional valuation analysis secondary. The relevant metrics are:
- Gross spread to deal: 14% ($50.40 vs $44.19)
- Time to close: ~10 months (Q1 2027)
- Annualized spread: ~17%

Typical merger arbitrage spreads on announced deals with committed financing run 5-8% annualized for high-probability transactions. The 17% annualized spread here suggests the market assigns meaningful probability to deal disruption.

On a standalone basis (if the deal breaks), NATL would likely trade at $35-40 based on: 8-9x forward EPS of $4.88 = $39-44, discounted for the leverage premium and Q1 miss. The EV/EBITDA of approximately 7.3x ($830M EBITDA, ~$6.1B EV) is reasonable for the industry but doesn't offer deep value protection if the deal collapses.

## Competitive Landscape

NCR Atleos holds genuine competitive advantages: the largest global ATM installed base, the only end-to-end offering combining hardware, software, services, and network operations, and the Allpoint network's scale (78,000 locations with major retail partners). No competitor offers the same breadth.

The Brink's combination would create a vertically integrated cash lifecycle company covering vault storage, armored transport, ATM operations, and network management. Strategic logic: $200M in identified annual cost synergies, elimination of third-party handoffs, and a comprehensive "cash management as a service" offering.

Competition from Diebold Nixdorf, Euronet, Hyosung, and others remains, but Atleos's scale (600,000+ machines) and established customer relationships create switching costs that protect the installed base. The ATMaaS model further deepens lock-in by converting discrete transactions into multi-year managed services contracts.

Market share in ATMaaS is expanding, ARPU is at records, and the Brink's deal would widen the moat further. The competitive position score reflects this strengthening dynamic, tempered by the secular headwind of declining cash usage.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Deal Completion Risk:** Required approvals include NATL shareholder vote, regulatory clearances across multiple jurisdictions (given 65+ country operations), and Brink's permanent financing. The $4.5B bridge facility from Morgan Stanley needs replacement with permanent capital markets execution in a volatile rate environment.

2. **Extreme Leverage:** $2.8B debt on $403M equity. While manageable at current EBITDA levels, any revenue disruption would quickly strain covenants. The 9.5% coupon on the senior secured notes creates a $128M annual interest burden on those instruments alone.

3. **Cash Obsolescence:** The existential long-term risk. Digital payments (Venmo, Zelle, Apple Pay, CBDCs) are structural headwinds. International markets provide a buffer, but the trajectory is directionally negative for physical cash in developed markets.

4. **Q1 2026 EPS Miss:** The -26.9% miss versus consensus raises questions about earnings quality and visibility. Management has not provided detailed explanation in available data. If this reflects structural deterioration rather than one-time items, standalone value deteriorates materially.

5. **Voyix Environmental Indemnification:** Costs tripled from $14M to $51M in FY2025 (Kalamazoo River site). The 10-K notes these obligations "may not be resolved for many years." This is an unpredictable, open-ended liability.

6. **Immigration Policy Sensitivity:** Reduced immigrant workforce has directly impacted prepaid payroll card transactions. Network segment EBITDA declined 11% in 2025 partly from this factor. Politically-driven and unpredictable.

7. **Pension Underfunding:** U.S. plan underfunded by $248M. Expected $48M contribution in 2026 is material relative to $239M FCF.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. Near-term IV of 48.3% sits 8.8 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility (39.5%), placing IV at the 73rd percentile. The term structure is in steep backwardation (48.3% near-term vs 21.2% at 6 months), indicating the market expects an imminent resolution of uncertainty followed by lower volatility — consistent with a merger arbitrage situation approaching a catalyst (potentially shareholder vote or regulatory ruling). The put/call OI ratio of 2.68 shows elevated put demand, suggesting institutional hedging of deal-break risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50-51)
The Brink's acquisition closes as planned in Q1 2027 at ~$50.40/share. NATL shareholders receive $30 cash + 0.1574 BCO shares. The spread closes over 10 months, delivering approximately 17% annualized return. Regulatory approval proceeds smoothly given the complementary (vertical) rather than horizontal nature of the combination. Brink's financing executes without difficulty.

### Bear Case ($32-38)
The deal breaks through regulatory rejection, shareholder opposition, financing failure, or Brink's exercising a material adverse change clause (potentially triggered by the Q1 2026 earnings miss or environmental liability escalation). NATL reverts to standalone trading at 7-8x forward EPS ($4.88), suggesting a range of $34-39. The leverage overhang, Q1 miss, and negative estimate revisions pressure the standalone multiple toward the lower end. Downside from current: 12-27%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Spread widens to >20% (price drops to ~$42 or below) while deal progress indicators remain positive (regulatory filings proceeding, no MAE triggers)
- Regulatory clearance announced, reducing remaining risk to shareholder vote and financing only
- Q2 2026 earnings demonstrate Q1 miss was non-recurring

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Brink's files to terminate or renegotiate at lower price
- Regulatory objection or extended review timeline announced
- NATL discloses material adverse developments (covenant breach, environmental liability escalation >$100M)
- Brink's credit deteriorates materially (downgrade, credit spread blowout)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/August 2026); any regulatory filing milestones; Brink's Q2 2026 earnings for financing health assessment.

## Conclusion

NCR Atleos at $44.19 presents a merger arbitrage situation with a 14% gross spread to the $50.40 Brink's deal value, expected to close Q1 2027. The underlying business is performing reasonably — ATMaaS is growing 33%, margins are expanding, and the competitive position is strong. However, the Q1 2026 EPS miss (-26.9%), negative estimate revisions, extreme balance sheet leverage, elevated options-implied volatility, and 10-month timeline to close introduce sufficient uncertainty to prevent a high-conviction BUY.

The spread is attractive on an annualized basis (~17%) compared to typical merger arb (5-8%), but this premium exists for valid reasons: complex multi-jurisdictional regulatory approval, material financing execution required by Brink's, and the Q1 earnings deterioration that could provide a path to material adverse change claims. For fundamental investors rather than deal arbitrage specialists, the risk/reward profile warrants monitoring rather than active positioning. HOLD until deal certainty improves or the spread widens sufficiently to compensate for break risk.
