## Executive Summary

HealthEquity is the largest US health savings account provider by account count (10.635 million HSAs at April 30, 2026, +7.6% year over year) and the second largest by assets, with roughly 20% share of HSA assets. The account metrics understate that this is primarily a deposit-spread business: custodial revenue — interest paid to HQY by insurance-company and depository partners on member HSA cash, of which members received only 0.24% in FY2026 — was 48.5% of FY2026 revenue, and the 10-K discloses partner fees rising from 39% of revenue in FY2024 to 48% in FY2026. The near-term earnings driver is mechanical and disclosed: $3.2 billion of the $16.5 billion HSA cash ladder currently earning 1.8% reprices during the remainder of FY2027, worth roughly $70 million of annualized custodial revenue at close to 100% incremental margin.

The problem is what happens after that. Recomputing the blended ladder yield with the FY2027 tranche replaced at 4.0% gives 3.97% against 3.6% today, a 37 basis point lift. The FY2028 tranche already earns 3.9%, FY2029 3.5%, and the FY2030 tranche 4.3%, above the ~3.9% five-year rate management says it has locked via $3.5 billion of Treasury forwards. Once the 1.8% bucket is gone, custodial revenue growth converges on HSA cash balance growth, which is running +3% year over year (+2% on an average daily basis). HSA investments grew 38% to $19.613 billion and now exceed HSA cash of $17.494 billion for the first time; members are migrating balances from the asset HQY monetizes at ~3.84% gross into an asset carrying an undisclosed and evidently much smaller percentage-of-assets fee. Service revenue grew 1.4% in FY2026 and 2.6% in Q1 FY2027 despite the investment book compounding at 26–38%.

Since the last review at $97.67 there has been no new earnings data; the stock is 7.5% higher, at a 52-week high of $106.42 intraday, and at $104.97 sits within $0.03 of the lowest published analyst target ($105) against a mean of $118.53. The February 2026 low of $72.76 was the market pricing repricing exhaustion; the subsequent 44% recovery has largely un-priced it. We stay at HOLD with a growth bias: the September print carries genuine raise potential, the buyback authorization is 12% of market capitalization and was executed at $80.90 average in Q1, and higher-for-longer long-end rates are a tailwind for the unhedged ~79% of the ladder. But 22.4x guided FY2027 non-GAAP EPS for a business whose volume engine grows 2–4% does not offer the asymmetry required to add here.

## Company Overview

## Financial Analysis

**Margins are genuinely expanding, and the unit-cost improvement is real.** Q1 FY2027 gross margin was 72.3% against 67.8%; adjusted EBITDA margin 46.4% against 42.4%; non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.24 against $0.97. Service cost per average total account fell from $5.14 to $4.39 in the quarter (−14.5%) and from $21.57 to $19.08 for the full fiscal year (−11.5%) while accounts grew 6%. Management credits AI-driven servicing efficiency (manual handling of service emails down 25%, manual effort in card servicing and claims inquiries down more than 90%, 50,000 fewer card-related contact-center calls).

Two qualifications. First, the efficiency is partly self-funded: technology and development expense rose from $239.5M to $262.5M in FY2026 (flat at 20.0% of revenue) and from $61.4M to $67.8M in Q1, plus $46–64M per year of capitalized software. AI spend lands in technology cost while the benefit shows in service cost, so the gross-margin optics overstate net operating leverage. Second, roughly $2.9 million of the $9.7 million quarterly service-cost reduction is fraud normalization — reimbursements of ~$0.3 million versus ~$3.2 million a year earlier, against a FY2025 base the 10-K describes as absorbing "a significant loss" from a step-up in fraud sophistication. That is a one-time level shift; the year-over-year comparison cannot repeat from Q2 FY2027 onward. Whether the lower *level* persists depends on whether the prevention capability is durable, which management asserts and we cannot verify.

Headline GAAP comparisons flatter the trend further. FY2025 operating income of $162.3M included a non-recurring $30.0M lease-termination settlement and $96.4M of stock-based compensation versus $73.1M in FY2026 (the decline driven by forfeitures on the former CEO's retirement). The clean read is adjusted EBITDA +20% and non-GAAP EPS +28.2% ($3.12 → $4.00), not the +123% GAAP net income figure.

**Cash flow.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $457.1M on $215.2M of net income. A naive screen subtracting only $1.969M of PP&E purchases produces free cash flow of $455.1M; HQY's own disclosed capital expenditure is $48.5M, because the majority is purchased and capitalized software excluded from the PP&E tag. Corrected FY2026 FCF is $408.6M, a 31.1% margin, a 4.68% yield on the ~$8.73bn market capitalization and 4.3% on enterprise value. Q1 FY2027 FCF of $81.2M should not be annualized — the quarter absorbs a $31.2M annual bonus outflow and the 10-K describes cost concentration in Q3/Q4 as Network Partners onboard January plan-year accounts. Growing FY2026's corrected figure with guided EBITDA growth of 10–12% gives roughly $450–460M for FY2027 as a normalized case.

**Balance sheet.** $265.4M cash against $600.0M of 4.50% senior notes due October 2029 and $346.9M drawn on a $1.0bn revolver maturing August 2029. Net debt $681.5M, or 1.08x guided FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $629M midpoint. Covenants allow 5.00x total net leverage and require 3.00x interest coverage; actual coverage is 5.6x on FY2026 operating income and 8.2x on Q1. Current ratio 3.44x. Undrawn revolver ~$653M plus a $450M accordion. No maturities before 2029. Debt was reduced by $100.0M in FY2026 and a further $15.0M in Q1.

The genuine balance-sheet caution is not leverage but composition: $1.648bn of goodwill plus $1.073bn of intangibles equal 82.3% of assets, so tangible book value is −$674M. Goodwill is tested at a single reporting unit against whole-enterprise fair value, which makes near-term impairment unlikely but any impairment event large. The Altman Z-Score of 2.70 is measuring this asset composition, not distress. A nine-signal Piotroski computation from FY2026 versus FY2025 data returns 9 of 9 (ROA 6.30% vs 2.93%, long-term debt/assets 28.3% vs 30.6%, current ratio 3.27x vs 3.06x, gross margin 69.5% vs 64.8%, asset turnover 0.385 vs 0.363, declining share count), though several signals benefit from the depressed FY2025 base.

**Earnings delivery.** Four consecutive beats: +16.2%, +7.7%, +4.5%, +10.1%. That record argues consensus of $4.72 for FY2027 — a hair above the $4.695 guidance midpoint — is achievable and probably conservative. It also frames the guidance tension: Q1 revenue was 25.1% of the guidance midpoint, essentially identical to Q1 FY2026's 25.2% of FY2026 actual, so guidance embeds last year's seasonality and no sequential acceleration despite management stating the $3.2bn of maturities is back-half weighted. Implied EPS for the remaining three quarters averages $1.15 against Q1's $1.24. Either guidance is deliberately conservative, or the repricing benefit is being absorbed by the headwinds management flagged — lower average service fees per account, a lower average rate on payment card transactions, and continued cash-to-investments migration. We cannot adjudicate from the filings, but the beat record tilts toward the former.

## Growth Analysis

Decompose guided FY2027 growth. Management guided average HSA cash yield to ~3.85% against 3.53% actual in FY2026. On the Q1 average daily HSA cash balance of $17.706bn, +32bp contributes ~$57M; roughly 2% balance growth on $636.8M of FY2026 custodial revenue adds ~$12M; total ~$69M of custodial growth against ~$102M of implied total revenue growth at the midpoint. **Approximately two-thirds of guided FY2027 revenue growth is HSA cash repricing rather than account, engagement, or product growth.**

The volume engine underneath is weak. Total accounts were 17.785M at April 30 against 17.791M at January 31 — flat sequentially. HSAs grew 7.6%; CDB accounts fell 0.3%. Gross new HSAs from sales were 1,040 thousand in FY2026, exactly flat against FY2025, before re-accelerating +17% in Q4 and +15% in Q1. Service revenue grew 1.4% then 2.6%. The 10-K states in the present tense that HQY "has seen an increase in Network Partners that have decided to offer HSAs or other CDBs directly to their customers," which is the most plausible filing-grounded explanation for the CDB decline, though COBRA and commuter accounts are also employment- and return-to-office sensitive.

Beyond FY2028 the arithmetic is unforgiving on the current rate path: custodial growth converges toward 2–3% cash balance growth, service revenue is growing 1–3%, interchange ~8%. That blends to mid-single-digit revenue growth. Against that, three offsets carry real weight:

1. **Buyback.** Authorization stands at ~$1.056bn after the May 2026 $1.0bn increase — 12.1% of market capitalization. Diluted weighted-average shares fell from 88.828M (FY2025) to 87.473M (FY2026) to 85.006M (Q1 FY2027); shares outstanding fell 1.27% in the single quarter. At ~$450M of normalized FCF with M&A capacity reserved, a $350–450M annual pace retires 3.3–4.3M shares at current prices, 4–5% of the count, adding roughly $0.20–0.24 to annual EPS.
2. **Rate environment.** Roughly $13bn of the $16.5bn ladder is unhedged. The Q1 pre-tax hedge loss of $34.3M is consistent with benchmark long rates having risen during February–April, which is economically constructive for future placements even though it depresses AOCI; if prevailing long-end rates remain elevated, the forward placement yields HQY strikes on later tranches plausibly land above the schedule's implied path.
3. **Marketplace optionality.** More than 10,000 members accessing programs at ~$90–100 per participating member per month for metabolic health and "north of $50" for men's health. At 10,000 participants that is ~$11M annualized, 0.8% of revenue — immaterial, consistent with management declining to break it out. At 100,000 it would be ~8% of revenue at high margin. Genuine option value, unquantifiable today.

Estimate revisions are close to flat: current-year consensus $4.72 (+2.0% over 90 days, −0.3% over 30) and next-year $5.45 (+2.0%, −0.8%). The revision magnitudes are under one percentage point in either direction, so the earnings-momentum signal neither supports nor contradicts the position. The Zacks upgrade to Rank #2 on August 3 rests on the same modest 90-day upward drift and is a third-tier input.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory matters more than level, and the trajectory is mixed with a negative tilt on the economics that matter. HQY is #1 by accounts and grew HSAs 8% against Devenir-reported market growth of 6% for calendar 2025, so it is gaining account share. But it is #2 by assets, its ~20% asset share was unchanged between the June 2025 and December 2025 Devenir readings, and the industry is migrating precisely toward the asset class where it is not the leader.

The 10-K names Fidelity Investments, Optum Bank, and Webster Bank, and states that "well-known retail mutual fund companies, such as Fidelity Investments, have entered the HSA and CDB business and gained significant market share." The strategic asymmetry, which is inference rather than disclosure: Fidelity monetizes HSA investments through its own fund and recordkeeping economics; HQY monetizes HSA cash through a custodial spread. Industry-wide migration from cash to investments strengthens the former and dilutes the latter per dollar of assets. The counter-argument is legitimate — HQY's bundled HSA-plus-CDB single-source offering and 200+ Network Partner integrations serve an employer-administration need that direct-to-consumer strength does not address, so the two may compete for partly non-overlapping demand.

The July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act expanded HSA eligibility to Bronze and Catastrophic ACA enrollees and broadened qualifying services. This is a genuine TAM expansion, but the newly eligible cohort is a direct-to-consumer population reached outside HQY's B2B2C machinery, likely with lower balances and higher churn. The company's own filing supplies the two-sided reading: "An increased focus on HSA-favorable healthcare regulatory reforms may create renewed interest and investment by our competitors in their HSA offerings and lead to greater competition." That would simultaneously raise competitive intensity and reduce the supply of divestable portfolios that underpins the acquisition thesis.

On acquisitions: BenefitWallet was bought at $690 per acquired HSA against HQY's own enterprise value of ~$886 per HSA today ($9.42bn ÷ 10.635M), so portfolio deals have been accretive on a crude per-account basis. HQY completed no acquisitions in FY2026 or Q1 FY2027; capital has rotated to buybacks.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate dependency, now 48% of revenue and rising** (from 39% two fiscal years earlier). Roughly half the revenue base is a rate call on a ladder largely repriced after FY2028. Revenue shifting toward custodial is concentration, not diversification.

**Mix shift.** Investments $19.613bn (+38%) now exceed cash $17.494bn (+3%). Investment fees are asset-value-based, so the growing investment book imports equity-market beta into revenue that the cash book did not carry.

**Unreserved cybersecurity litigation.** A consolidated putative class action in the District of Utah plus "several regulatory inquiries" arising from a FY2025 breach of a business partner's user account containing PII. The court dismissed HQY's motions to dismiss and to compel arbitration without prejudice on May 5, 2025; HQY refiled its renewed arbitration motion on May 15, 2026. **No loss accrual has been recorded.** Unquantified and unpriced.

**Single points of operational failure.** A single bank identification number sponsor for the Visa-linked cards, with no long-term contract, supporting 15% of FY2026 revenue; a single card processor after recent consolidation.

**Non-bank custodian net worth requirement** under Treasury Regulations §1.408-2(e). Failure triggers revocation procedures for custodial authorization — remote, but existential, and invisible to any leverage screen. Worth monitoring if HQY continues levering for buybacks.

**Compounded GLP-1 exposure.** A marketplace partner offers access to compounded GLP-1 medications in a "volatile" regulatory environment; metabolic health is the highest-economics Marketplace program.

**Goodwill/intangibles at 82.3% of assets** with negative tangible book of −$674M and single-reporting-unit testing.

**Governance verification gap.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks in six weeks (March 30, April 6, May 8, 2026), unread; Form 4 evidence is circumstantial for an EVP/CTO departure (last transaction April 6) and a Chief Customer Officer arrival by July 9. Amendment No. 1 to the employment agreements of all five named executive officers, each dated May 5, 2026, appears in the 10-Q exhibit index and is unread. Simultaneous NEO amendments are equally consistent with routine post-CEO-transition harmonization; we record this as unresolved, not as a finding. It matters because the CTO oversees the platform modernization on which the AI cost-efficiency thesis rests.

**Insider activity.** Discretionary open-market sales only: Fiore 7,966 shares at $95.00 across May 29/July 2/July 10 (~$757K, retaining 52,244 shares); Ladd 7,500 at $90.00 (~$675K, retaining 91,141); Wellborn an option exercise and sale. No open-market purchases by any insider in the period covered. The amounts are small relative to retained holdings, priced $10–15 below spot, and the repeated identical prices and regular cadence resemble plan-based selling. Mild negative, low signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 40.4% is rich against 35.5% one-year realized, and the term structure is backwardated (40.4% at 14 days versus 34.7% at 105 days), with the 42-day September 18 tenor implying an 11.4% move — that tenor spans the Q2 FY2027 report expected in early September. IV rose 4.8 points while the stock gained 6.9% since the prior snapshot; rising vol alongside rising price is event positioning rather than complacency.

The positioning composition is the more informative reading. Put/call open interest is 0.10 and put/call volume 0.06 — among the most call-dominant structures we track. Almost no downside protection is being bought into a print carrying an 11.4% implied move on a stock at a 52-week high. That cuts against, not for, a buy here: an unhedged holder base into a two-sided catalyst amplifies the price response to a disappointment. Short interest of 8.0% of float at 4.9 days to cover is moderate and does not create squeeze mechanics in either direction. We use the supplied qualitative skew characterization ("balanced" at the near tenor) and do not infer direction from the raw longer-dated skew value.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Guidance embeds no sequential acceleration despite management stating the $3.2bn of 1.8% maturities is back-half weighted; four consecutive beats averaging 9.6% suggest another raise at the September print, taking FY2027 non-GAAP EPS to $4.85–4.95 and FY2028 consensus above $5.45. Unit service costs are down 11.5% year over year on AI-driven servicing while accounts grew 6%, and gross margin has moved 64.8% → 69.5% → 72.3%. The $1.056bn authorization is 12.1% of market cap; full execution at $104.97 would take shares from ~83.6M to ~73.3M, a 12% reduction, with proportionate ownership rising accordingly, and Q1 execution at $80.90 average demonstrates price discipline. Balance sheet is unencumbered — 1.08x net leverage, no maturities before 2029, $653M undrawn. If long-end rates stay elevated, the unhedged ~79% of the ladder reprices above the schedule's implied path. On a re-rating to 24x FY2028 EPS of $5.45, the stock is worth $131.

**Bear case.** The custodial tailwind is arithmetically self-extinguishing: after the FY2027 tranche reprices at 4.0%, the blended ladder yield is 3.97% against 3.6% today, and the FY2030 tranche at 4.3% could reprice down. Underneath, HSA cash grows 3%, average daily cash 2%, service revenue 2.6%, CDB accounts −0.3%, and total accounts were flat sequentially. The 10-K acknowledges in the present tense that Network Partners are increasingly going direct. Investments now exceed cash, meaning the asset base is compounding at 19% into the line HQY monetizes least richly, while Fidelity — already #1 by assets — captures the economics of that migration. Roughly $2.9M of the $9.7M quarterly service-cost improvement is non-repeatable fraud normalization, so the gross-margin comparison deteriorates from Q2. Add an unreserved cyber class action, negative tangible book against 82% intangibles, and an unresolved executive-change cluster. If the market re-applies the February multiple of ~17x to $4.90, the stock is worth $83.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $114** — 21x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $5.45. Rationale: by mid-2027 the market prices FY2028 as the current year; a 21x multiple reflects a modest de-rating from today's 22.4x forward to account for the post-ladder growth step-down, offset by 4–5% annual share-count reduction. Upside from $104.97: **+8.6%**.

**Bull case target: $132** — 24x $5.45, achieved if the September and December prints deliver guidance raises toward $4.90+ for FY2027, HSA cash balance growth re-accelerates above 5%, and the buyback runs at the upper end of capacity. Upside: **+25.8%**. This sits between the analyst mean of $118.53 and high of $135.

**Bear case target: $84** — 17x $4.90, the multiple the market applied in February 2026 when repricing exhaustion was the dominant narrative. Downside: **−20.0%**.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q2 FY2027 report in early September 2026 (the 42-day option tenor prices an 11.4% move around it) and confirmation at the Q3 print in early December, by which point the back-half repricing should be visible in the realized HSA cash yield.

Reward-to-risk from spot is roughly 0.43:1 to base and 1.29:1 to bear-adjusted bull — insufficient asymmetry for new capital.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. HSA cash balances growing above 5% year over year for two consecutive quarters, or the cash-to-investments mix stabilizing (investments growth decelerating below 20% while cash grows), which would restore the durability of the custodial line beyond FY2028.
2. FY2027 revenue guidance raised to $1.44bn or above with realized average HSA cash yield above 3.90% excluding one-time items (the reported Q1 3.84% yield was 3.78% ex the $2.4M Depository Partner termination benefit).
3. A price of $88–92, equating to 17–18x FY2028 consensus, which would re-open the February risk/reward.
4. Resumption of portfolio acquisitions at below $700 per acquired HSA, restoring the inorganic growth lever.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. HSA cash declining in absolute year-over-year terms, which would make custodial revenue growth dependent entirely on rate and cap the post-FY2028 trajectory at zero.
2. Total accounts declining year over year — CDB attrition accelerating past HSA additions would confirm Network Partner disintermediation.
3. A loss accrual recorded on the cybersecurity class action, or a quantified regulatory action from the pending inquiries.
4. FY2027 guidance cut, or a rate path that pulls the guided average HSA cash yield below 3.70%.
5. Any goodwill impairment charge, given single-reporting-unit testing implies a large event.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** The score moves from 6.0 to 5.85: since the July review at $97.67 there has been no new fundamental information, while the price has risen 7.5% to a 52-week high that sits at the bottom of the published analyst target range. The trajectory divergence flagged in prior work — financial health improving to 7.0 while growth and risk deteriorated — is confirmed rather than reversed by this review. Financial health is the genuine strength: 1.08x net leverage, 5.6–8.2x interest coverage, $409M of corrected FY2026 free cash flow at a 31% margin, no maturities before 2029, a nine-of-nine Piotroski profile, and a buyback authorization equal to 12% of market capitalization executed at $80.90 in the most recent quarter. Management's capital allocation is the clearest evidence of shareholder alignment in the file.

What does not support adding is the composition of growth. Two-thirds of guided FY2027 revenue growth is the repricing of $3.2 billion of HSA cash from 1.8%, and after that tranche clears, the blended ladder yield is 3.97% against 3.6% — a 37 basis point residual on a business whose underlying volumes grow 2–4% and whose largest asset pool is migrating from the product it monetizes at 3.84% into one it monetizes at an undisclosed and evidently much smaller rate. Service revenue growing 2.6% while the investment book grows 38% is the single most decision-relevant datapoint in the filings.

That combination — an excellent balance sheet, real margin expansion partly built on non-repeatable fraud normalization, a mechanical but finite rate tailwind, and a weak volume engine — is worth about what it currently trades for at 22.4x guided FY2027 non-GAAP earnings and 15.0x forward EV/EBITDA. Existing holders should stay through the September print, where the beat record and the back-half-weighted repricing schedule give the raise scenario better-than-even odds; the near-term option market prices an 11.4% move with essentially no protection outstanding, so position sizing matters more than direction. New capital should wait for either $88–92 or evidence that HSA cash balances, not HSA cash rates, are driving custodial growth.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $104.97
- Fair value: $114.00
- Margin of safety: +8.6%
- Initiate/add below: $92.00
- Reduce/exit above: $132.00
- Rationale: Base target is 21x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $5.45, a modest de-rating from 22.4x forward to reflect the post-ladder growth step-down, offset by 4-5% annual share-count reduction.

**Thesis:** HealthEquity is the largest HSA provider by accounts but economically a deposit-spread business: custodial revenue is 48.5% of sales and roughly two-thirds of guided FY2027 growth is the repricing of $3.2bn of HSA cash from 1.8%. After that tranche clears the blended ladder yield rises only to ~3.97% from 3.6%, so growth converges on 2-3% cash balance growth. A 12% buyback authorization and 31% FCF margin support the price rather than create upside.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats the repricing step as a durable growth inflection. We read it as self-extinguishing: HSA investments now exceed cash and are compounding 38% into the line HQY monetizes least, while service revenue grows 2.6%. The bull re-rating case therefore rests on a shrinking earnings driver.

### Catalysts

- **High — Early September 2026:** Q2 FY2027 print in early September: back-half-weighted $3.2bn repricing plus four consecutive beats give better-than-even odds of a guidance raise toward $4.85-4.95 FY2027 EPS.
- **Medium — Next 12 months:** Buyback authorization of ~$1.056bn equals 12.1% of market cap; Q1 execution averaged $80.90, and 3.3-4.3M shares retired annually adds roughly $0.20-0.24 to EPS.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **Quarterly prints:** HSA cash balances decline year over year, making custodial growth wholly rate-dependent and capping post-FY2028 trajectory at zero.
- **Quarterly prints:** Total accounts decline year over year as CDB attrition outpaces HSA additions, confirming Network Partner disintermediation.
- **Two consecutive quarters:** HSA cash growth exceeds 5% for two consecutive quarters or investments growth decelerates below 20%, restoring custodial durability and supporting an upgrade.
