## Executive Summary

Macerich is a mall-focused REIT in the middle innings of a turnaround under CEO Jackson Hsieh, who joined in March 2024 with a "Path Forward Plan" to deleverage, consolidate joint ventures, and reposition the portfolio. The strategy is showing tangible results: FFO grew 8.7% in FY2025, leasing spreads have been positive for 17 consecutive quarters, the $116M signed-not-opened pipeline provides embedded NOI growth through 2028, and net debt/EBITDA has declined from ~8.8x to 7.76x. Q1 2026 FFO of $0.34/share beat consensus of $0.31 by $0.03, and the recent $272M Annapolis acquisition demonstrates confidence in the offensive phase.

However, the stock at $22.23 (near its 52-week high of $22.55, up 50% from May 2025 lows) is now pricing in much of this recovery. Total debt of $6.59B on a $5.6B market cap leaves leverage elevated, with $1.36B maturing within 12 months in an environment where CRE refinancing rates are 5.5-7.0% versus origination rates of 3.7-5.0%. The company has already strategically defaulted on multiple properties (Santa Monica Place $300M, Country Club Plaza, Towne Mall), demonstrating both the optionality of non-recourse structures and the fragility of the capital structure. With GAAP EPS still negative, 3 of 4 recent quarters missing consensus, and analyst sentiment degrading (two strong buys dropped, two holds added), the path from here requires continued execution against structural headwinds including e-commerce penetration, department store anchor weakness (19 vacant anchor locations), and consumer stress.

The investment case hinges on whether Hsieh can achieve the target leverage of low-to-mid 6x while growing NOI 3%+ annually — a dual mandate that creates tension given simultaneous acquisitions ($562M in Crabtree + Annapolis). I rate MAC a HOLD with a positive bias contingent on the company continuing to reduce leverage while maintaining occupancy above 93%.

## Company Overview

Macerich owns or has interests in 37 regional shopping centers and one community/power center, totaling approximately 39 million square feet of gross leasable area across the United States. The portfolio is concentrated in California, New York, and Arizona — dense, affluent markets that support above-average sales productivity ($881/sq ft for spaces under 10,000 sq ft, up from $837 in 2024).

The company has defined a 32-property "Go-Forward Portfolio" representing its core assets, with the remaining properties subject to disposition or strategic default. Under the Path Forward Plan, MAC has shed weaker assets, consolidated joint ventures to gain operational control, and repositioned anchor spaces with higher-rent tenants in categories like dining, fitness, medical, and experiential retail.

Key tenants include Dick's Sporting Goods (4.0% of rent), Victoria's Secret (2.2%), Signet Jewelers (1.9%), and Abercrombie & Fitch (1.8%). No single tenant exceeds 4% of total rent, providing reasonable diversification. The anchor roster includes 127 stores dominated by Macy's/Bloomingdale's (31 stores, 5.77M sq ft), JCPenney (18), Dillard's (10), and Nordstrom (6).

## Financial Analysis

**FFO (the appropriate REIT earnings metric):**
- FY2025: $397.0M diluted, or ~$1.50/share on 265M weighted average shares
- Growth: +8.7% vs. FY2024's $365.3M
- Q1 2026: $92.4M ($0.34/share), beating consensus of $0.31

**GAAP earnings remain negative** at -$197.1M net loss in FY2025, driven by $357M in depreciation and $147.4M in impairment charges. This is structurally typical for mall REITs with aging assets being written down.

**Operating cash flow:** $321.6M in FY2025, up 13.5% from $283.4M in FY2024, demonstrating genuine cash generation improvement.

**Balance sheet — the central concern:**
- Total debt (pro rata): $6.59B
- Market cap: $5.6B
- Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDA: 7.76x (down from ~8.8x at Path Forward inception, target: low-to-mid 6x)
- Cash: $280.2M + $649.4M revolver availability = ~$930M total liquidity
- Near-term maturities: $1.36B within 12 months

The near-term maturity wall is substantial. While most debt is non-recourse and secured by individual properties (providing "walk away" optionality), refinancing at current rates compresses net cash flow versus prior origination rates. The company has demonstrated willingness to strategically default (Santa Monica Place $300M, Country Club Plaza $148M at MAC's share, Towne Mall), which preserves cash but carries reputational cost with lenders.

**Dividend:** $0.68/share annually (3.1% yield), representing only ~45% of FFO — well below the 70-90% REIT norm. This is deliberately conservative, preserving cash for deleveraging. A potential dividend increase could serve as a catalyst once leverage approaches target levels.

**Dilution:** Share count grew from 215M (2022-2023) to 254M (2025), an 18% increase that dampens per-share value creation. An additional $374M remains available under the 2024 ATM program.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth drivers:**
1. **SNO pipeline ($116M):** Represents leases signed but not yet generating revenue — this flows into NOI over the next 12-24 months with no additional capital required
2. **Rent uplift from repositioning:** $107M in total rent increase from new stores opening through 2028 vs. prior uses in same spaces
3. **Releasing spreads:** +6.7% on 2025 signings (17th consecutive positive quarter)
4. **Sales productivity gains:** $881/sq ft in 2025, up from $837 in 2024 (+5.3%)

**Leasing velocity accelerating:** 1,199 leases signed in 2025 for 7.1M sq ft — a 46% increase in lease count and 85% increase in square footage vs. 2024 on a comparable basis. Management is targeting 88-89% physical permanent occupancy and an 85% "leasing speedometer" (currently 83%).

**Acquisitive growth:**
- Crabtree Mall (Raleigh, NC): $290M, June 2025
- Annapolis Mall (Annapolis, MD): $272M, May 2026
- Combined $562M in acquisitions represents confidence in the offensive phase but creates tension with deleveraging goals

**FY2026 Guidance:** At least 3% Go-Forward NOI growth expected. All 30 anchor repositionings are committed.

The growth profile is credible but moderate: 3% NOI growth plus embedded SNO pipeline converts to perhaps 4-5% FFO growth before dilution impacts. This is reasonable for a stabilizing mall REIT but insufficient to justify significant multiple expansion from current levels (~14.8x FFO).

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.23, MAC trades at approximately:
- 14.8x trailing FFO ($1.50/share)
- ~14x forward FFO (assuming 5% growth to ~$1.58)
- 0.35x price-to-GLA value (approximate)
- 3.1% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** Retail REIT peers trade at median P/E of 44x on a GAAP basis, but this comparison is misleading since many peers have positive GAAP earnings while MAC does not. On an FFO multiple basis, Simon Property Group trades at ~14x forward FFO, making MAC's 14-15x roughly in line with the industry leader despite significantly higher leverage and lower asset quality.

The stock has appreciated 50% from its May 2025 low of $14.62 to $22.23, largely reflecting the turnaround execution. At the current price, MAC is trading above the analyst median target of $21.50 and the mean of $21.63, with a high target of $25 and a low of $17. The consensus is essentially "fairly valued here."

**Valuation math:** If MAC achieves mid-6x leverage (requires ~$800M+ in debt paydown or EBITDA growth), FFO per share could reach $1.70-$1.80 by FY2027-2028. At 14x FFO, that implies $24-25. At a re-rated 16x FFO (reflecting lower leverage and improved credit quality), that implies $27-29. Against this, bear case at current leverage with refinancing stress and occupancy headwinds could compress FFO to $1.30 and justify a 12x multiple = $15-16.

## Competitive Landscape

Macerich operates in the challenged but stabilizing regional mall sector. Key dynamics:

**Versus Simon Property Group (SPG):** Simon has lower leverage (~5.5x), higher portfolio quality (average sales productivity >$700/sq ft across all categories), better credit ratings (A-), and larger scale. MAC is the smaller, more leveraged competitor attempting to close the quality gap through repositioning. The valuation gap between MAC and SPG has narrowed as MAC's turnaround gains credibility.

**Structural position:** MAC's concentration in dense, affluent California/Arizona/New York markets provides a moat that lower-quality mall REITs in secondary markets lack. Scottsdale Fashion Square ($1,500+ sales/sq ft) and Queens Center compete effectively against e-commerce because of their irreplaceable locations and demographics.

**Tenant demand trajectory:** New-to-mall categories (dining, fitness, medical, experiential) are filling former department store spaces. Luxury brands (LVMH), fast fashion (Zara, Primark), and international retailers are actively seeking quality mall locations, supporting MAC's repositioning thesis.

**Anchor vulnerability:** 19 vacant anchor locations represent both risk and opportunity. Each repositioning costs $15-30M but can generate materially higher rent than traditional anchors. Execution timeline and capital availability are the constraints.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Refinancing risk in elevated-rate environment (HIGH):** $1.36B maturing within 12 months at a time when CRE lending conditions are tight and rates are 150-300bps above origination rates. MAC's track record of strategic defaults may limit lender appetite, though non-recourse structures provide protection.

**2. Consumer stress and tenant bankruptcy exposure (MODERATE-HIGH):** The current macro environment features potential tariff impacts on import-dependent retailers. MAC saw 8 tenant bankruptcies in 2025 affecting $12.9M in annual rent, with 3 additional filings in early 2026. Further consumer deterioration would accelerate tenant distress.

**3. Dilution risk (MODERATE):** $374M still available under the ATM program. Each incremental share issuance dilutes existing holders. If another equity raise is needed to bridge refinancing gaps, per-share FFO growth stalls.

**4. Execution risk on dual mandate (MODERATE):** Simultaneously deleveraging while acquiring and redeveloping ($459-505M in pipeline) creates competing demands on limited capital. FlatIron Crossing's $245-265M mixed-use redevelopment includes residential — outside MAC's core competency.

**5. Department store secular decline (STRUCTURAL):** Macy's (31 stores, 5.77M sq ft) is MAC's dominant anchor exposure. Further Macy's retrenchment would accelerate the vacant-anchor problem.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($25-27, +12-22%):**
- Path Forward Plan achieves low-to-mid 6x leverage by late 2027
- SNO pipeline converts fully, driving 4-5% annual NOI growth
- Annapolis and Crabtree acquisitions perform above underwriting
- Dividend increased to $1.00-$1.20/share (4.5-5.4% yield at current price), attracting income investors
- Occupancy stabilizes at 95%+ as repositioned anchor spaces lease up
- Rate environment moderates, reducing refinancing pressure and enabling multiple expansion to 16x FFO

**Bear Case ($15-17, -24-33%):**
- Refinancing stress forces additional strategic defaults or dilutive equity raises
- Consumer recession drives tenant bankruptcies beyond 2025 pace
- Occupancy declines below 92% as tariff impacts hit import-dependent retailers
- Further Macy's closures create anchor voids requiring $200M+ in repositioning capital MAC cannot fund
- CRE lending tightens further; MAC loses access to unsecured markets
- Stock re-rates to 10-12x FFO reflecting permanent discount for leverage and structural risk

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Net debt/EBITDA declines below 7.0x (from current 7.76x), demonstrating accelerated deleveraging
2. Occupancy recovers above 94.5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming leasing pipeline conversion
3. Stock pulls back below $19 (12-13x trailing FFO), creating sufficient margin of safety against refinancing risk

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 8.0x (reversal of deleveraging progress)
2. Occupancy drops below 92% for any reported quarter
3. Additional equity raise exceeding $200M announced at current share price levels (accelerated dilution)
4. Another strategic default on a Go-Forward Portfolio property (vs. only non-core assets to date)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will show full-quarter contribution from Annapolis acquisition, updated occupancy trends reflecting summer leasing season, and refinancing progress on near-term maturities.

## Conclusion

Macerich under Jackson Hsieh is executing a credible turnaround with measurable progress: FFO growth, positive releasing spreads, declining leverage, and meaningful insider buying from the CEO. The $116M SNO pipeline and $107M in cumulative rent uplift through 2028 provide organic growth visibility that most mall REITs lack.

At $22.23 (essentially at the 52-week high and analyst consensus target), the stock is pricing in continued execution without meaningful margin of safety for the substantial risks: elevated debt with near-term maturities, a rising-rate environment hostile to CRE refinancing, vacant anchor locations requiring capital, and consumer headwinds that could accelerate tenant distress. The growth thesis is real but requires another 2-3 years of execution to fully materialize, during which refinancing risk and macro headwinds could derail progress.

HOLD. The turnaround is investable below $19; at $22, the risk/reward is balanced and requires patience for the next catalyst (either a pullback for entry or continued execution proving the thesis at higher prices).
