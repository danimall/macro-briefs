## Executive Summary

Liquidity Services has executed the most important strategic change in its recent history, and the market has largely paid for it. Through the first nine months of FY2026, GMV rose 6.4% to $1,240.8M while reported revenue rose only 3.6% to $371.5M and cost of goods sold *fell* 5.5% to $194.8M. That combination is the entire story: management is shifting gross merchandise volume out of inventory-taking "purchase" programs and into commission-based consignment and sell-in-place formats. Segment direct profit rose 16.0% to $176.7M, income from operations rose 37.4% to $33.1M, and Adjusted EBITDA rose 34.3% to $56.8M. Direct profit per dollar of GMV — the cleanest unit-economics measure available for this business — improved from 13.07% to 14.24%, a 9.0% gain, while take rate (revenue/GMV) *declined* from 30.7% to 29.9%. Anyone reading revenue or take rate in isolation would draw the opposite conclusion from the correct one.

The balance sheet supports the operating story. At 6/30/2026 the company held $219.8M of cash plus $11.3M of short-term investments against zero funded debt, and it runs on roughly $127M of negative operating working capital — operating current assets of $40.4M versus $167.3M of operating current liabilities, including $72.2M of payables to consignment sellers. Buyers pay before sellers are remitted, so GMV growth generates cash ahead of earnings. Cash rose $45.1M in nine months on $25.4M of net income.

Where the case weakens is price and the durability of the growth rate. The FY2026 profit surge is a one-time mix reset, not a repeatable growth rate. Q4 guidance calls for GMV of $415-455M and revenue of roughly $104-114M against $118.1M in Q4 FY2025 — a year-over-year revenue decline — and on a per-GMV basis guided Q4 direct profit lands near 13.8% versus 14.09% actually achieved in Q3, so the headline jump to a "mid-fifties" direct-profit margin is largely a shrinking denominator. Consensus (two analysts) has FY2027 EPS at $1.76 versus $1.58 for FY2026, or +11.4%, and the U.S. federal NOL carryforward was fully utilized in FY2025, pushing the effective tax rate from 25.4% (9M FY2025) to a guided 30-34%. At $42.66 the stock is ~26-27x FY2026E adjusted EPS, ~24x FY2027E, ~36-39x FY2026E GAAP EPS, and 13.5-14.0x FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA on a gross-cash EV of $1,105M — closer to 18-19x if one deducts stock compensation running at $15.8M for nine months (+65.9% year over year). Base case fair value is ~$46, or +8%; the bear case is ~$31, or -27%.

## Company Overview

Liquidity Services operates B2B e-commerce marketplaces and related software for the disposition of surplus, returned, and end-of-life assets. Incorporated in Delaware in 1999 as Liquidation.com and headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland, it reports five operating segments consolidated into four reporting lines (9M FY2026 figures):

- **GovDeals** — state, local, and federal government surplus plus adjacent commercial sellers (GovDeals, Bid4Assets, Sierra Auction). GMV $714.6M (58% of consolidated), revenue $69.1M, direct profit $65.2M — a **94.4% direct-profit margin**, effectively a pure agency model.
- **RSCG** — retail excess, returns, and overstock (Liquidation.com, AllSurplus Deals, Secondipity, Retail Rush). GMV $348.1M, revenue $253.0M (68% of consolidated revenue), direct profit $68.0M (26.9% margin, recovered from 21.6%).
- **CAG** — commercial capital assets globally via AllSurplus. GMV $178.1M (**-2.9% y/y**), revenue $33.1M, direct profit $28.6M (86.5% margin, down from 90.6%).
- **Machinio & Software Solutions** — equipment-listing search plus private-label auction SaaS. Revenue $16.4M (+13.7%), direct profit $14.9M (91.4% margin). Remaining performance obligation is only $4.8M and covers subscription contracts, not marketplace activity.

The accounting distinction that governs interpretation: in consignment the company books only its commission; in purchase it takes title and books gross resale proceeds with offsetting COGS. In 9M FY2026 consignment was 81.7% of GMV but 30.1% of revenue; purchase was 18.3% of GMV and 63.8% of revenue. Reported revenue is therefore a poor growth metric — a dollar of GMV moving from purchase to consignment can cut revenue by 70-90 cents while raising profit. Management says this explicitly in its Q4 guidance assumptions.

## Growth Analysis

The distinction that determines the recommendation is between the FY2026 profit inflection and the sustainable growth rate beneath it.

**What is durable.** GMV growth of 6.4% (9M) and 9.7% (Q3) reflects genuine volume expansion: registered buyers 6.4M (+9%), completed transactions ~334,000 in Q3 (+17%), and GovDeals GMV +7%. The industry tailwinds — e-commerce returns volume, landfill-diversion mandates, and government digitization of surplus disposal — are real and reflected in the numbers rather than asserted. GovDeals' lobbying for state and local legislation permitting online auctions of foreclosed and tax-foreclosed real estate (via Bid4Assets) is a genuine regulatory-expansion option, though real estate carries lower take rates and higher event-cancellation variability.

**What is a one-time reset.** The 34.3% Adjusted EBITDA growth comes primarily from moving GMV out of purchase and into consignment. Consignment was 81.7% of GMV in 9M FY2026 and is guided to "mid-eighties" in Q4. That runway is nearly exhausted — mix can go from 83% to 88%, not from 83% to 130%. Once complete, EBITDA growth converges toward GMV growth plus modest cash operating leverage, or roughly low-double-digits. Consensus agrees: FY2027 EPS of $1.76 versus $1.58 is +11.4%.

**Guidance decomposition.** Q4 FY2026: GMV $415-455M, GAAP net income $10.0-13.0M, Adjusted EBITDA $22.0-25.0M, GAAP diluted EPS $0.30-0.39, adjusted diluted EPS $0.41-0.50, ETR 30-34%. Added to nine-month actuals: FY2026E GMV $1,656-1,696M (+5% to +8%), Adjusted EBITDA $78.8-81.8M (+30% to +35%), GAAP EPS ~$1.08-1.17, adjusted EPS ~$1.58-1.67. Implied Q4 revenue of $104-114M is *below* the $118.1M derived for Q4 FY2025. Full-year FY2026 revenue lands near $475-486M against $476.7M — roughly flat.

The guided "mid-fifties" direct-profit margin looks like a six-point jump from Q3's 49.3%, but on a per-GMV basis it computes to ~13.8% against 14.09% achieved in Q3. Reading 26%/56% instead of 25%/55% flips that to ~14.6%. Q4 per-GMV economics are indeterminate within guidance rather than clearly improving.

**Estimate revisions and delivery.** Current-year consensus rose 6.8% over 90 days to $1.58 and next year 5.7% to $1.76; the current quarter rose 6.2%. Four consecutive beats (+27.9%, +7.2%, +15.9%, +25.1%). This is positive earnings momentum but not the >30% revision velocity that signals a systematic mispricing of an inflection. Coverage is two analysts, which limits both the informational content of the consensus and the breadth of any future re-rating.

**Headwinds to EPS conversion.** The NOL exhaustion is mechanical and permanent: pre-tax income grew 34.9% in 9M FY2026 while net income grew 25.5%, and the ~9-point gap is tax. Rising diluted share count (32.85M in Q3, guided ~33.0M in Q4) is a further modest drag.

## Competitive Landscape

The company discloses no patents and no registered copyrights. Its defensible assets are a 6.4-million-buyer marketplace network, a portfolio of category-defining domains, and — most durably — embedded seller relationships in government.

**GovDeals is the franchise.** Government surplus disposal involves thousands of municipalities, counties, school districts, and transit authorities operating under procurement, transparency, and record-keeping requirements. Switching costs are procedural rather than technical, and once a marketplace sits inside a municipal workflow it is rarely displaced casually. A 94.4% direct-profit margin, +7% GMV, and 58% of consolidated GMV make this the asset that justifies a premium multiple.

**RSCG is the exposure.** The 10-K discloses that a "significant portion" of purchased inventory comes from vendor contracts with Amazon, that alternative supply likely could not be procured on acceptable terms, and that Amazon's share of revenue, GMV, or profit is not quantified. The only disclosed data point is Amazon-sourced inventory: $10.1M (9/30/2025), $12.2M (9/30/2024), $11.1M (6/30/2026). The consignment shift mechanically reduces revenue-line exposure to purchased inventory, but purchase revenue was still $237.0M and flat in absolute dollars, and Amazon-related inventory is *higher* than at fiscal year-end. There is no evidence that concentration itself has improved. Separately, RSCG supply agreements obligate the company to take delivery regardless of demand, limiting inventory flexibility.

**CAG is deteriorating at the margin.** GMV -2.9% for nine months, direct-profit margin down from 90.6% to 86.5% (take-rate gains came with more cost-bearing service content), project lumpiness, personnel in China, and named exposure to the Iran conflict and Venezuela instability.

**Machinio is the best model and the smallest.** 91.4% direct-profit margin, subscription revenue, +13.7% growth — on $16.4M of nine-month revenue, 4.4% of the total. It is also the most exposed to the 10-K's AI-search risk factor: an equipment-listing search engine monetizing referral traffic faces direct pressure if AI answer layers satisfy buyer intent without a click-through.

One operating metric deserves monitoring. Q3 auction participants fell 5% to ~1,046,000 while completed transactions rose 17% to ~334,000, so bidders per completed transaction fell from 3.84 to 3.13 (-18%) and GMV per transaction fell 6.1% to $1,356. The most likely explanation is compositional — small-lot, sell-in-place, and D2C listings create many more auctions, each with fewer bidders. Rising direct profit per GMV dollar supports management's reading that recovery rates are improving. But thinning bidder depth per auction is the mechanism by which a marketplace's core value proposition to sellers erodes, and it is the metric I would watch most closely.

## Risk Assessment

**Amazon concentration (unquantified).** Disclosed as material with no sizing. A change in Amazon's terms, mix, or quantity would hit RSCG's purchase business directly, and the filings give no basis for estimating magnitude.

**Adverse selection in retail returns.** The 10-K names the specific risk that retail clients investing in their own returns capacity send reduced volume *or* a mix stripped of high-value returns. This is embedded in the business model rather than generic competition, and it degrades economics gradually and invisibly in aggregate GMV.

**Tax normalization.** NOL fully utilized in FY2025; ETR from 25.4% to a guided 30-34%. Roughly 5-7 points of permanent conversion drag on pre-tax growth.

**Float reversal.** The ~$127M negative working capital position is a structural asset while GMV grows and a cash drain if GMV declines. The $12.9M nine-month increase in payables to sellers was the second-largest contributor to operating cash flow and is explicitly timing-driven.

**Goodwill.** $102.8M, 24.5% of assets. Annual testing occurs at the start of Q4 FY2026 — the quarter currently in progress — with the Software Solutions unit's $5.1M flagged as more sensitive. No interim indicators at 6/30/2026.

**Geopolitical.** A new risk factor was added in the Q3 10-Q covering the U.S./Israel-Iran conflict following February 2026 strikes and Iranian retaliation, with Venezuela instability referenced in MD&A. CAG is the exposed segment. Project-based international asset sales in oil, gas, and industrial categories are precisely the transactions that get postponed.

**Governance and disclosure.** CEO Angrick is named a key-person risk with no key-person insurance; he holds 16.7% of shares. Anti-takeover provisions include a staggered board and undesignated preferred. An Item 5.02 8-K dated 2/9/2026 (officer/director departure or election) is unread and unaccounted for.

**Mitigants.** No funded debt, revolver compliance at 6/30/2026, a favorable unanimous jury verdict in July 2026 in the former-CMO discrimination suit (post-trial motion and appeal deadlines had not expired at filing), and no other material litigation disclosed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The purchase-to-consignment shift is a structural upgrade to return on capital, not an accounting artifact — direct profit per GMV dollar rose 9.0% year over year, and sell-in-place eliminates warehousing, freight, and markdown risk entirely. GovDeals compounds at 94% direct-profit margins inside compliance-bound municipal workflows that rarely change vendors, with Bid4Assets real-estate legislation as free optionality. Machinio's SaaS revenue is 91% direct-profit and growing double digits. The company has $7.38 per share of cash, no debt, negative working capital, and four consecutive beats averaging 19% upside. If GMV growth accelerates toward the $2B target and margins hold, FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $1.85-1.90 and the multiple holds at 29-30x, giving $55.

**Bear case.** FY2026's profit surge is a one-time mix reset with limited remaining runway, and beneath it GMV grows 6-8% while CAG shrinks, revenue declines year over year in Q4, and the ETR permanently absorbs 5-7 points. RSCG depends on an unquantified Amazon relationship and on retailers not cherry-picking their high-value returns. Bidders per auction fell 18%, which is the leading indicator of recovery-rate compression. Stock compensation grew 66% and is excluded from the metric that determines those grants; the Board has left a $15M repurchase authorization essentially untouched while spending $8.0M on employee tax withholding, and $231M of cash earns money-market yields. At 24x forward earnings for an 11% grower, a single quarter of GMV disappointment compresses the multiple to 19x and the stock to ~$31.

**Where I come out.** Both sides are supported by the filings, and the deciding factor is price. The operating improvement is genuine and verified in the cost-of-goods line, which is hard to manufacture. But the multiple now embeds the improvement while consensus embeds only 11% forward growth, and the two primary quality signals point in opposite directions: financial health is strong, while capital allocation and insider behavior are not. That combination supports owning the business and declining to pay up for it.

**Management quality detail.** The prior report cut this dimension from 7.0 to 5.5, and current evidence broadly sustains that level. Negative: zero shares repurchased in Q3 FY2026 with the full $15.0M authorization outstanding, $1.5M of nine-month buybacks against $8.0M of net-share-settlement tax withholding, SBC +65.9%, CEO compensation up 84% over four years ($2.78M FY2021 → $5.12M FY2025) against Adjusted EBITDA +42% and net income -45%, and an incentive metric that excludes the cost of equity grants. Broad one-directional insider selling April-August 2026 with no open-market purchases: the General Counsel sold 45,246 shares outright at $34.00 (a ~61% position reduction), the CTO 10,663 at $34.23, co-founder Mateus-Tique ~85,000 shares for ~$3.2M, plus extensive exercise-and-sell by the CFO and CCO. Most of that volume is mechanical (strikes as low as $9.46 with finite lives) and clustered in the first open window after Q2 earnings; the CHRO's activity is now explained by a confirmed retirement after 16 years, with Karen Fascenda appointed effective July 6, 2026. Positive and important: the CEO, the largest holder at 5,228,943 shares (16.7%), sold nothing; Consolidated Direct Profit is the correct incentive metric for this business and its selection is consistent with the FY2026 pivot away from low-margin revenue; and five-year TSR of +268% against a peer index of +3% is real outperformance. Declining to repurchase at 36-39x forward GAAP earnings is defensible discipline. I cannot distinguish that reading from simple inertia on the available evidence, and no filing discloses an acquisition pipeline or capital-return policy change.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Base case $46 (+7.8%), bull $55 (+29%), bear $31 (-27%) from $42.66 over 12 months. Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at 24x forward earnings, ~2.6% below the 52-week high.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE/BUY on any of:**
1. **Price near $35-36** (roughly 20x FY2027 consensus of $1.76) with GMV growth intact — the same business at a materially better entry.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of direct profit per GMV dollar above 14.5%** with GMV growth of 8%+, evidencing that sell-in-place economics compound rather than plateau. Q3 FY2026 was 14.09%; Q4 is guided to roughly 13.8-14.6%.
3. **Capital deployment**: a repurchase program materially larger than $15M executed at reasonable prices, or an acquisition at a defensible multiple that puts the $231M cash balance to work. Either would resolve the central management-quality question.
4. **FY2027 initial guidance (November 2026) implying adjusted EPS above $1.90**, which would break the 11% consensus growth ceiling.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. **GMV growth below 3% year over year in any quarter**, or a second consecutive quarter of CAG GMV decline steeper than 5% — the float would begin reversing and consuming cash.
2. **Bidders per completed transaction falling below 3.0** alongside direct profit per GMV dollar below 13.5%, which together would indicate the small-lot/D2C pivot is diluting marketplace liquidity and compressing recovery rates.
3. **Any quantified disclosure of Amazon supply loss or materially changed terms**, or RSCG direct-profit margin retreating below 24%.
4. **Goodwill impairment** in the Q4 FY2026 annual test, particularly at Software Solutions.

**Reassessment timeline.** The FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year report, expected in November 2026, is the next substantive checkpoint: it delivers the Q4 mix outcome against guidance, the annual goodwill test, initial FY2027 guidance, and whatever the Board decides about the untouched repurchase authorization. Interim monitoring items: the unread Item 5.02 8-K dated 2/9/2026, any 8-K disclosing capital deployment, and Middle East disruption affecting CAG project timing.

## Conclusion

Liquidity Services has done the right thing operationally. Nine-month cost of goods sold fell 5.5% on 6.4% higher GMV, direct profit per dollar of GMV improved 9.0%, operating income rose 37.4%, and the company achieved this while holding $231.1M of cash against zero funded debt and funding operations with roughly $127M of negative working capital. GovDeals — 58% of GMV at a 94.4% direct-profit margin inside sticky municipal procurement workflows — is a genuinely good business. Four consecutive earnings beats and rising estimates confirm the operating momentum.

The problem is that essentially all of this is in the price. At $42.66 the stock trades at ~26-27x FY2026E adjusted EPS, ~24x the FY2027 consensus of $1.76, ~18-19x EBITDA net of stock compensation, and 42x trailing GAAP earnings, against forward consensus growth of 11%, a permanent 5-7 point tax headwind, guided Q4 revenue below the prior year, and a consignment mix runway that is nearly exhausted.
