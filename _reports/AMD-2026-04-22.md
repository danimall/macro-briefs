## Executive Summary

AMD has undergone a structural transformation over the past 18 months, evolving from a competitive CPU/GPU supplier into a validated AI infrastructure platform company. The OpenAI (6GW GPU, October 2025) and Meta (~$60B, February 2026) partnerships represent the two largest semiconductor procurement agreements outside of NVIDIA's customer base and provide multi-year revenue visibility that did not exist at prior analysis dates. FY2025 revenue grew 34% to $34.6B with operating cash flow more than doubling to $6.5B, and the balance sheet carries $7.4B in net cash.

The critical change since prior HOLD recommendations is the combination of rising consensus estimates (+6.7% next-year EPS revision over 90 days, now at $10.98), confirmed hyperscaler customer diversification away from NVIDIA, and ASML's April 2026 guidance raise to €40B validating upstream AI capex durability. At $284 (approximately 43x CY2027E consensus EPS of $6.70 for FY2026 and 26x FY2027E of $10.98), AMD trades at a premium to the semiconductor peer median P/E of 41x but well below NVIDIA's multiple, while offering higher revenue growth visibility than any peer except NVIDIA itself. The primary risk is execution: MI450 must ship competitively in 2H 2026, and 320 million performance warrants create meaningful dilution if both mega-deals execute fully.

## Company Overview

AMD is a fabless semiconductor company designing CPUs, GPUs, and AI accelerators manufactured primarily at TSMC. Following a December 2024 segment restructure, the company reports three segments: Data Center ($16.6B, 48% of FY2025 revenue), Client & Gaming ($14.6B, 42%), and Embedded ($3.5B, 10%). CEO Lisa Su has led the company since 2014, overseeing a transformation from single-digit server CPU market share to approximately 41%.

The company's strategic pivot toward full-stack AI infrastructure — combining EPYC CPUs, Instinct GPUs, Pensando networking, and the Helios rack-scale platform (enabled by the ZT Systems design acquisition) — differentiates AMD from pure-play chip vendors and positions it as the primary alternative to NVIDIA's end-to-end data center offering.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $34.6B grew 34% year-over-year, driven by Data Center (+32%) and Client & Gaming (+51%). Embedded declined 3%, reflecting continued digestion of elevated post-COVID channel inventory. Gross margin expanded to 50% from 49% despite absorbing approximately $440M in net inventory charges related to China export controls on MI308 GPUs.

Operating income nearly doubled to $3.7B, though GAAP margins (10.7%) remain depressed by $2.3B in Xilinx-related intangible amortization and $1.6B in stock-based compensation. Non-GAAP net income was $6.8B vs. GAAP of $4.3B — a $2.5B gap that investors must track. The FY2025 effective tax rate of -2.5% (vs. 19% in FY2024) reflects an $853M one-time IRS benefit that will not recur.

R&D spending increased 25% to $8.1B (23.4% of revenue), and SG&A surged 52% to $4.1B. These are the costs of competing on an annual AI GPU cadence against NVIDIA; if the MI450/MI500 roadmap delivers, this investment pays off in operating leverage. If it doesn't, these costs become a drag on margins.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the strongest in AMD's history: $10.6B in cash and short-term investments against $3.2B in debt, yielding $7.4B net cash. The current ratio stands at 2.9x with an undrawn $3.0B revolver and $3.0B commercial paper program providing additional liquidity. However, the intangible-heavy nature of the balance sheet ($25.1B goodwill + $16.7B other intangibles = 54% of total assets) reflects the $49B Xilinx acquisition premium.

Inventory grew 38% to $7.9B, a deliberate build to support advanced-node product ramps. This is a bet on sustained demand and a meaningful risk if the AI capex cycle decelerates.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow from continuing operations rose 113% to $6.5B. With only $974M in capex (the fabless advantage), free cash flow reached approximately $5.5B, implying a 16% FCF margin. The company repurchased $1.3B in shares with $9.4B remaining under its buyback authorization.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions

Current-quarter EPS estimates have risen 5.1% over 90 days (from $1.21 to $1.28). Next-year estimates have risen 6.7% (from $10.29 to $10.98). This is broad-based, with 41-43 analysts contributing, and the trajectory is accelerating: 30-day revisions are positive across every period. Rising estimates into a stock that has already rallied 232% from its April 2025 lows typically signal fundamental support, though the magnitude of the price move exceeds the magnitude of the estimate revisions.

### Revenue Growth Visibility

The OpenAI and Meta agreements provide a floor under Data Center revenue growth that AMD has never had. Combined, these two deals represent up to $120B+ in potential procurement over approximately five years, dwarfing AMD's current annual revenue. Even partial execution (50-60% of maximum) would sustain 25-30%+ annual revenue growth through FY2028.

The MI450 (2nm-class TSMC process, shipping 2H 2026) is the critical execution milestone. It must demonstrate competitive training and inference performance against NVIDIA's Blackwell/Rubin architectures. ASML's April 2026 guidance raise to €40B with a €12B buyback provides upstream validation that foundry demand for leading-edge nodes remains robust through the MI450 volume production window.

### Server CPU Renaissance

AMD's EPYC franchise benefits from a structural trend: as AI inference workloads scale, CPU-bound pre/post-processing becomes a bottleneck. AMD's 41% server CPU market share represents a durable franchise with high switching costs, and growing CPU demand from inference scaling adds a second growth vector alongside AI GPUs.

### France Sovereign AI and European Expansion

The Alice Recoque exascale supercomputer partnership with France opens a new customer category (sovereign AI) that is politically motivated and less price-sensitive than commercial hyperscaler procurement.

## Valuation Assessment

AMD trades at approximately 109x trailing earnings (depressed by the FY2025 tax benefit dynamics and GAAP intangible amortization), 43x forward consensus FY2026E EPS of $6.70, and 26x FY2027E consensus EPS of $10.98. The peer median P/E is 40.9x.

At 43x FY2026E, AMD trades at a modest premium to the peer median, justifiable given AMD's revenue growth rate (34% FY2025) vs. Texas Instruments (~10% growth) or Analog Devices (~15% growth). The meaningful comparison is NVIDIA, which trades at higher absolute multiples but with greater near-term revenue scale in AI. Micron at 21.5x forward is not directly comparable due to its memory cyclicality.

On a FY2027E basis, 26x P/E for a company growing revenue 30%+ annually with $7.4B net cash is reasonable. Achieving FY2027 estimates requires MI450 to ship on time and in volume, and for OpenAI/Meta procurement to begin ramping meaningfully.

Analyst consensus median target is $300 (1% upside from $297 reference price), with a low of $220 and high of $365. The consensus target appears stale relative to the recent price surge; 36 of 49 analysts rate AMD Buy or Strong Buy with zero Sells.

At the current $284 price (last trade), the stock is approximately 5% below the 52-week high of $298. The valuation is not cheap on an absolute basis, but it reflects a business whose revenue base is structurally inflecting.

## Competitive Landscape

AMD occupies a unique competitive position as the only company simultaneously challenging NVIDIA in AI GPUs and Intel in server CPUs, while offering an integrated full-stack solution (CPU + GPU + networking + rack-scale platform).

**vs. NVIDIA:** NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem remains the primary competitive moat. AMD's ROCm stack has improved, and the OpenAI/Meta deals prove hyperscalers are willing to adopt it at scale, but the software ecosystem gap persists for the broader developer community. NVIDIA's Blackwell architecture sets the performance benchmark that MI450 must match or approach. Hyperscalers want a second source to reduce supplier concentration risk and pricing power. This structural demand for diversification is AMD's most durable competitive advantage.

**vs. Intel:** Intel's Foundry struggles and technology delays continue to cede server CPU share to AMD. The gap has widened; Intel's trailing P/E of -1142x reflects a company in fundamental transition. AMD's EPYC franchise faces emerging ARM-based competition (NVIDIA Grace, custom ARM designs at Amazon and others), but x86 compatibility and the installed software base provide meaningful switching costs.

**vs. Custom Silicon (Broadcom, Marvell, in-house designs):** Google's TPU, Amazon's Trainium, and Meta's potential custom chips represent the long-term competitive risk to both AMD and NVIDIA. However, custom silicon development takes 2-3 years per generation and requires massive engineering investment. AMD's product-as-a-service model with annual cadence updates offers hyperscalers faster time-to-deployment.

## Risk Assessment

### Warrant Dilution (HIGH)
The OpenAI and Meta deals each include warrants for 160 million AMD shares, totaling 320 million potential new shares (approximately 20% of current shares outstanding). These vest based on purchase milestones and stock price targets. Full execution of both deals would reduce EPS by approximately 17-18% from current share count projections.

### Export Controls (HIGH)
The ~$800M MI308 inventory charge demonstrated that export policy can destroy value rapidly. The U.S. government's stated expectation of 15% revenue from licensed China MI308 sales creates ongoing regulatory uncertainty. Additional restrictions (discussed in March 2026 reporting) could further limit AMD's addressable market.

### Execution Risk on MI450 (HIGH)
MI450 on TSMC's 2nm-class process must enter production in 2H 2026 to meet OpenAI/Meta deployment timelines. Any TSMC yield issues, design problems, or production delays would directly impact revenue recognition from those contracts.

### Customer Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH)
If OpenAI and Meta procurement ramps as contracted, these two customers could represent 30-40% of AMD's total revenue by FY2028. Loss or significant reduction of either relationship would be material.

### Inventory Risk (MODERATE)
$7.9B in inventory (+38% YoY) converts to write-downs in a downturn.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 73% is 15.8pp above 1-year historical volatility of 57.2%, placing IV in the 74th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (73% near-term vs. 57.4% at 6-month), pricing imminent event risk around the April 24 weekly expiry. Unusual put activity at $280-$295 strikes (31x volume/OI ratios) suggests near-term hedging rather than directional bets, consistent with protection around a stock that has rallied 35% since early April. The P/C OI ratio declined from 1.04 to 0.50 over two weeks, indicating institutional rotation toward bullish positioning. At 73% IV, option premiums are expensive; entry via options is unfavorable at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($340-365)
MI450 ships on schedule in 2H 2026 with performance competitive to NVIDIA's Blackwell. OpenAI and Meta procurement ramps ahead of schedule. Server CPU share continues expanding toward 45%+. FY2027 EPS reaches $11-12 (above current consensus of $10.98). At 30x FY2027E EPS of $11.50, the stock reaches $345.

### Bear Case ($190-220)
MI450 delays or underperforms vs. NVIDIA Blackwell. Hyperscaler AI capex decelerates (macro-driven or ROI-skepticism). Export controls tighten further. Warrant dilution materializes. Stock reverts to 20x FY2027E EPS of $10 = $200.

### Base Case ($320-340)
MI450 ships on time with acceptable performance. OpenAI/Meta procurement begins but ramps gradually. Estimates continue rising modestly. At 30x FY2027E EPS of $10.98 = $329.

## Conclusion

AMD has been rated HOLD four consecutive times with scores in the 5.9-6.1 range. The fundamental picture has materially changed since those assessments. The OpenAI and Meta deals, confirmed via 8-K filings with definitive warrant agreements, provide multi-year revenue visibility that did not exist previously. Estimate revisions are accelerating upward across all timeframes. ASML's upstream guidance raise validates sustained AI capex through AMD's critical MI450 production window.

The specific risks that previously justified HOLD — valuation uncertainty and unclear AI GPU competitive positioning — have been partially resolved by hyperscaler customer validation. The remaining risks (warrant dilution, export controls, MI450 execution) are real but quantifiable and priced into a 43x FY2026E / 26x FY2027E multiple that is not extreme for the growth profile.

The recommendation is upgraded to **BUY** at $284, with a 12-18 month base case target of $329. The primary re-evaluation checkpoint is the Q1 FY2026 earnings report (expected late April/early May 2026) for MI450 production timeline confirmation and initial Meta/OpenAI contract revenue recognition.
