## Executive Summary

Campbell's is in the third year of a portfolio transition that has not worked. The Sovos acquisition ($2.899B, closed March 2024) lifted reported revenue through FY2025, but with that benefit fully lapped, the underlying base is now visibly shrinking: nine-month FY2026 net sales of $7,607M were down 4.1%, composed of volume/mix −4% and net price realization +1%. Both segments fell 4% in the third quarter and for the nine months. U.S. soup fell 8% in Q3. A branded incumbent taking one point of price while surrendering four to five points of volume is ceding share on price-sensitive occasions, and management's own filings describe the mechanism: channel shift away from traditional grocery, retailer-demanded promotional funding, and private-label expansion.

The earnings translation is worse than reported EPS suggests. GAAP EPS rose in Q3 ($0.41 vs $0.22) purely because the prior year carried a $150M Snyder's of Hanover trademark write-down. On the company's own comparability tables, adjusted EPS fell 32% in Q3 to $0.50 and 24% year-to-date to $1.78; segment operating earnings, which require no non-GAAP judgment, fell 18% over nine months, with Snacks operating margin down roughly 300bp to 9.9%. Nine-month gross margin of 28.4% was 200bp lower, of which the gross tariff impact was 240bp. Tariffs cost approximately $0.02 per share in all of FY2025 and $0.17 in nine months of FY2026. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.15–$2.25 implies a ~26% decline from the $2.98 FY2025 adjusted base, and the CFO steered to "$2.20 or below."

At $23.17 the stock is 11.9x FY2027 consensus of $1.94 and 10.7x the $2.17 FY2026 figure, against a 14.5x packaged-food peer median. That discount is a reasonable price for a business with declining volumes, ~4.4x net leverage, negative tangible equity of roughly −$5.3B, and a dividend management has explicitly said will not be raised. FY2027 consensus has been cut 11.2% in 90 days; sell-side mean and median targets of $21.59 and $20.00 are both below the current price, and six of twenty ratings are sell or strong sell. Our base case fair value of $21.50 sits 7% below spot; adding the $1.56 dividend produces roughly flat total return, with a −31% bear case against a +21% bull. That asymmetry argues for trimming. What keeps this out of AVOID territory is genuine, if thinning, cash generation ($542M of nine-month simple free cash flow against $354M of dividends), investment-grade standing with an undrawn $1.85B revolver to April 2030, roughly 4.7x of derived headroom against a 3.25x interest-coverage covenant, and short interest at 31.2% of float with 7.8 days to cover, which makes the bearish view crowded rather than proprietary.

## Company Overview

Campbell's operates two reportable segments. **Meals & Beverages** (FY2025 net sales $6,050M, segment operating earnings $1,076M) covers condensed and ready-to-serve soup, Swanson, Pacific Foods, Prego, Pace, SpaghettiOs, V8, and the Sovos brands, Rao's and Michael Angelo's. **Snacks** (FY2025 $4,203M, $560M) covers Pepperidge Farm, Goldfish, Snyder's of Hanover, Lance, Cape Cod, Kettle Brand, Late July and Pretzel Crisps. Latin America retail moved from Snacks into Meals & Beverages beginning FY2026 with prior periods restated, a reporting change rather than a business change.

By product category for the nine months ended May 3, 2026: Snacks $3,134M (41%), Soup $2,234M (29%), other simple meals $1,720M, beverages $519M. Four brands now exceed $1B in annual sales: Campbell's, Rao's, Goldfish and Pepperidge Farm.

The economically distinctive asset is Rao's, carrying a $1,470M indefinite-lived trademark, roughly 40% of the $3,678M indefinite-lived trademark pool. It is also the only genuine growth engine, and until May 2026 its tomato-based sauces were produced entirely by a single third-party manufacturer at one facility in Italy, the vulnerability the La Regina transaction addresses.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $8,562M → FY2023 $9,357M (+9.3%) → FY2024 $9,636M (+3.0%) → FY2025 $10,253M (+6.4%, of which Sovos contributed 8 points and the 53rd week 2 points, against −2 divestitures, −1 volume/mix, −1 price). Nine-month FY2026: $7,607M, −4.1%. Stripping acquisition and calendar effects, the legacy base was already flat-to-down in FY2025; FY2026 makes the organic decline visible in reported numbers.

**Margins.** Gross margin: 31.2% (FY2023) → 30.8% → 30.4% → 28.4% (9M FY2026). The Q3 bridge management provides is instructive: cost inflation/supply chain/other −530bp, of which gross tariffs alone were −310bp; volume/mix −60bp; cost-savings program costs −20bp; offset by productivity +310bp and price +110bp. Roughly 60% of the inflation drag is tariff, which is a policy variable rather than a permanent structural cost, the reason we do not treat the FY2026 margin as terminal.

**Earnings.** Adjusted EPS: Q3 $0.50 vs $0.73; 9M $1.78 vs $2.35; FY2025 full year $2.98. Total segment operating earnings 9M $1,047M vs $1,277M. Snacks operating margin 9.9% vs 12.9% nine months, 10.1% vs 14.3% in Q3. The CFO called Snacks margin performance "not acceptable" on the record and disclosed that the sequential improvement from roughly 7% to 10% EBITDA margin came partly from lower trade spending, less marketing, and cancelling most fresh-bakery promotions, spending withdrawal that may cost volume later rather than a structural cost fix.

Note two comparability items. Nine-month adjusted EPS includes an $0.08 per-share net pension actuarial and curtailment gain that is non-recurring, so underlying decline excluding it is worse. Conversely, adjusted figures exclude $107M of nine-month cost-savings charges that are largely cash and recurring in nature; a stricter treatment would lower adjusted earnings further.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow $839M (vs $872M), plant capex $297M, simple free cash flow $542M against $354M of dividends paid, 65% of that measure. Management attributes the OCF decline to "lower cash earnings, partially offset by changes in working capital," a less benign explanation than the prior year's. FY2026 capex guidance was cut mid-year from ~$420M to ~$370M, itself a cash-preservation signal, and roughly $55M of that budget is wastewater remediation (Napoleon, Ohio ~$35M plus ~$20M elsewhere) rather than growth or productivity.

Soup is seasonal, with the May–August fourth quarter the weakest, so annualizing nine-month cash flow overstates the year. Two labelled sensitivities: if Q4 OCF resembles the FY2025 implied Q4 ($259M) and Q4 capex is the guided remainder (~$73M), FY2026 FCF lands near $728M; if Q4 cash earnings track the −24% adjusted EPS trend, $620–660M is plausible. Against an indicated dividend of roughly $465–472M, coverage is real but leaves only $150–260M for debt reduction before working-capital swings.

**Balance sheet.** At May 3, 2026: assets $15,142M, liabilities $11,112M, equity $4,030M, cash $402M (up from $132M at year-end). Short-term borrowings $864M including $340M commercial paper; long-term debt $6,146M; total debt carrying value $7.01B against $6.479B fair value. Goodwill $4,993M plus other intangibles $4,325M means tangible book equity of roughly −$5.3B, normal for a serially acquisitive branded-food company, but it removes asset protection. Working capital is −$395M (improved from −$674M) with a 0.77 current ratio, supported in part by 0–120 day supplier terms and $253M of supplier-finance obligations sitting inside payables (stable versus $240M at year-end, so not a growing source of cash-flow support).

Derived net leverage: annualizing nine-month EBITDA (EBIT $848M + D&A $306M = $1,154M) gives roughly $1.5B against $6.6B of net debt, or ~4.4x. Management targets the "low-three-times range over the next couple of years." The arithmetic is demanding: at $700M of FCF less $470M of dividends, roughly $230M per year goes to debt, so two years of paydown takes net debt to ~$6.15B, which requires EBITDA near $1.76B, 17% above the current run rate, to reach 3.5x. **The deleveraging plan is EBITDA-recovery dependent, not debt-paydown dependent.** If EBITDA does not recover, the dividend becomes the flex item. The CFO raised the possibility of hybrid instruments at 150–200bp above straight debt in exchange for partial rating-agency equity credit, which signals a preference for expensive capital over a dividend cut.

Covenant position is not the near-term problem. All facilities carry a minimum 3.25:1.00 adjusted EBITDA to interest coverage test; derived coverage is roughly 4.7x ($1.54B annualized EBITDA against ~$330M annualized interest), higher if credit-agreement add-backs for the ~$107M of nine-month program charges are permitted. The Altman Z-Score of 1.46 flags balance-sheet fragility and should be read that way rather than as a bankruptcy signal; the metric penalizes precisely the negative-working-capital, thin-book-equity profile a leveraged staple runs by design.

**Refinancing ladder.** $400M of 5.30% notes matured and were repaid in March 2026, funded partly by $550M of 4.55% notes issued December 15, 2025. Next maturity is $500M of 5.20% notes due March 2027, then $600M in 2029. Interest expense is finally falling ($246M for nine months versus $260M) on lower debt. Debt trading below par means each refinancing is modestly dilutive to interest expense relative to the 4.57% weighted-average fixed coupon. A $600M notional fixed-to-floating swap on part of the 2035 notes adds near-term rate variability, a defensible carry trade, though an odd posture for a company describing investment grade as "an imperative." [unverified — the level of the 30-year Treasury is not in the sources.]

**Capital allocation.** Dividend held at $0.39/quarter with no intention to increase "anytime soon." Zero shares repurchased in the entire third quarter, with $473M of authorization unused. M&A "off the table." This is a defensive allocation stance, appropriate to the circumstances, and it removes the buyback and dividend-growth levers from the total-return case.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth case for the consolidated company over the next 12–18 months. FY2026 organic sales are guided to −1% to −2% (CFO: −2% "probably a more realistic assumption"), and Q4 shape per management is net sales "flattish to slightly up" helped by a $30M favorable Meals & Beverages comparison from last year's Sovos ERP conversion, with Snacks "similar to the third quarter or a little bit worse."

Consensus reflects this: FY2027 EPS of $1.94 is down 11.2% over 90 days, and next-quarter estimates are down 14.2%. The 30-day pace has decelerated to −1.5% for FY2027 and −0.1% for FY2026, which suggests the bulk of the post-Q3 downgrade cycle is complete rather than accelerating. That is stabilization of the revision *rate*, not a turn in direction.

The FY2027 bridge contains identifiable headwinds beyond volume. Management sized the incentive-compensation reset at roughly $40M, a mechanical consequence of FY2026 underperformance (performance RSUs for the period ended August 2025 vested at 48% of target and TSR units at 50%), worth about $0.10 per share after tax. Base FY2027 inflation was planned near 3%; at oil around $100 the CFO framed total inflation at 5–6%. The company is "nearly fully hedged for fiscal 2026," which places the unhedged exposure squarely in the first half of FY2027, the live risk, compounded by diesel and driver-shortage freight pressure the 10-Q newly cites.

Offsets exist. The cost-savings program was raised to ~$375M of annual savings by FY2028, with $200M realized through May 2026, implying roughly $175M remaining, or $70–90M ($0.18–0.23 per share) in FY2027 if execution holds. A Q4 tariff refund of $0.03–0.04 per share is expected, with some recoveries potentially extending into FY2027, and legal challenges to the tariffs themselves are pending. Rao's continues to grow; premium ready-to-serve soup (~20% of that portfolio) and condensed soup used as a cooking ingredient (>50% of the condensed portfolio) are growing sub-segments; Goldfish multipacks were cited at +6% over 13 weeks.

The strategic plan — SKU rationalization, fewer and larger innovation launches, brand-by-brand advertising review, shifting trade spend from temporary price reductions to feature-and-display — is conventional and appropriate for a share-losing snacking portfolio. It is also subtractive to revenue before it is additive. The nine-month volume/mix of −4% and the guided Q4 Snacks deterioration are consistent with the plan being in its subtractive phase, which will persist through at least the first half of FY2027.

## Valuation Assessment

On FY2027 consensus of $1.94, the stock trades at 11.9x. On FY2026 consensus of $2.17 — consistent with the company's non-GAAP $2.15–$2.25 guidance, so the bases match — 10.7x. Peer medians in packaged foods sit at 14.5x (LW 25.1x, POST 14.5x, CALM 12.8x, PPC 12.0x; CAG's negative P/E reflects impairments and is not usable). Indicated dividend yield is 6.73% ($1.56 / $23.165).

The discount to peers is not evidence of mispricing. It compensates for: organic revenue declining 4% with volume down 5%; ~4.4x net leverage against a peer set generally running lower; negative tangible equity; a frozen dividend at an 80% payout of FY2027 consensus EPS; and estimate revisions that are the worst in the comparable group. On a growth-adjusted basis there is no PEG argument to make — forward earnings growth is negative.

Analyst targets do not provide a floor here. Mean $21.59 and median $20.00 are both *below* spot, and the distribution (1 buy, 13 hold, 2 sell, 4 strong sell) is unusually bearish for a consumer staple. The $56 high target is a stale outlier inconsistent with any current earnings path; the $14 low is a plausible bear anchor.

Scenario values, on FY2027 adjusted earnings power:

| Case | FY2027 EPS | Multiple | Value | Price return | Total return incl. $1.56 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | $2.07 | 13.5x | **$28.00** | +20.9% | +27.6% |
| **Base** | $1.94 | 11.1x | **$21.50** | −7.2% | −0.5% |
| **Bear** | $1.68 | 9.5x | **$16.00** | −30.9% | −27.5% (dividend halved) |

Bull requires tariff recoveries to recur, FY2027 inflation to land near the 3% base plan rather than 5–6%, Snacks margin to recover 200bp+ toward mid-teens, and cost savings to flow through at the top of the range. Bear requires unhedged first-half FY2027 inflation at 5–6%, Snacks to deteriorate further off already-reduced promotional support, and a dividend reset to fund the deleveraging commitment.

At 50/20/30 base/bull/bear weights, expected price is $21.15, roughly 8.7% below spot; total expected return including a probability-weighted dividend is roughly −4%. That is a below-fair expected risk-adjusted return with unfavorable asymmetry (−31% bear against +21% bull), which is the REDUCE case. The 32% drawdown from the $34.18 fifty-two-week high is explained by fundamentals — adjusted EPS down 24%, FY2027 consensus down 11% — so there is no contrarian mispricing to exploit. Valuation scores 4.2: cheap enough that the multiple is not the primary risk, not cheap enough to compensate for the earnings trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K discloses no category market-share figures, which for a category incumbent is itself informative. The evidence available points to erosion. Company-wide net price realization was −1% in FY2025 and +1% in FY2026 against −4% to −5% volume/mix. The company's own risk language states that consumers "may be less willing to pay a price differential for our branded products," that retailers are using buying power to demand supplier-funded promotions and customized products while expanding private label, and, most structurally, that its businesses are "largely concentrated in the traditional retail grocery trade, which has experienced slower growth than other retail channels," a trend it expects to continue and which "may also create consumer price deflation."

Customer concentration has not improved despite a fully reshaped portfolio: top five customers at 47% of consolidated sales in FY2025, FY2024 and FY2023, with Walmart at 21%, 22% and 22%. Revenue mix changed materially; negotiating position did not.

Within the portfolio the bifurcation is clean. Growing or stable: Rao's, Pacific, broth, foodservice, Prego, Pepperidge Farm cookies, premium RTS, condensed soup as an ingredient. Declining: U.S. soup broadly (−8% in Q3), mainstream ready-to-serve, SpaghettiOs, V8, Pace, Snyder's of Hanover, Lance, chips, crackers, fresh bakery, and third-party contract manufacturing. Contract-manufacturing customer losses drove accelerated intangible amortization of $7M/$27M/$20M in FY2023–FY2025; the absence of such charges in FY2026 indicates the write-down is complete, which also means the revenue is gone.

The 10-K concedes relative disadvantage explicitly: competitors "are larger than us, may be less exposed to tariff impacts, and have substantial financial, marketing and other resources." We do not have peer margin and volume data to establish whether the 200bp gross margin contraction is company-specific or sector-wide; the proxy's peer TSR index (107 versus CPB's 77 at FY2025, a 30-point five-year shortfall) establishes share-price underperformance only. Competitive position scores 3.2 on deteriorating trajectory with one genuinely advantaged asset (Rao's) inside a portfolio losing volume.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff and energy cost pass-through (highest near-term severity).** Tinplate steel for cans is a named input, structurally exposing the condensed-soup and broth franchise. FY2026 is nearly fully hedged; FY2027 first half is not, into a 3% base inflation plan that becomes 5–6% at $100 oil. This is the single variable most likely to determine whether FY2027 lands near $1.94 or near $1.68.

**Pending trademark impairment test (dated, September 2026).** At the FY2025 annual test, indefinite-lived trademarks with 10% or less excess of fair value over carrying value totalled $2.587B — Rao's, Snyder's of Hanover, Pace, Pacific Foods, Late July, Allied. Published sensitivities: a 1% WACC increase alone triggers roughly $95M at Rao's and $65M at Snyder's; a 1% royalty-rate reduction triggers $45M at Snyder's and $30M at Pacific Foods. Actual impairments were $129M in FY2024 and $176M in FY2025. With EBIT falling and market-required yields on the company's own debt above its coupon, the FY2026 test carries real probability of another charge. These are non-cash, do not affect covenants that run off adjusted EBITDA, and thin coverage on recently fair-valued Rao's is partly a mechanical artifact of acquisition accounting. The impact is to book equity, reported net income and sentiment, not to cash generation.

**Dividend sustainability.** Covered by FY2026 free cash flow at a 65–73% payout, and 80% of FY2027 consensus EPS. Not at imminent risk, but not durable if EBITDA does not recover, given the stated low-3x leverage target. Management's willingness to discuss hybrid capital rather than reset the dividend is supportive near-term and value-dilutive at the margin. A 6.7% yield on a business with negative earnings growth is thin compensation for the risk profile. [unverified — the level of the 10-year Treasury and the implied spread are not in the sources.]

**Off-balance-sheet distributor guarantees.** Approximately 4,500 bank loans to independent contractor distributors are guaranteed by the company, maximum potential future payments $570M — about 8% of market capitalization — indirectly secured by the distribution routes. The exposure is correlated with the operating problem: route economics weaken precisely when snack volumes fall. Mitigant: the company actively buys and resells routes ($56M purchased, $51M sold over nine months), giving it a mechanism to absorb distressed routes.

**La Regina structure.** 49% acquired May 4, 2026 for $286M total — $146M cash at closing plus $140M due around May 2027 at the company's sole discretion in cash or unregistered shares. At $23.17 the stock alternative is roughly 6.0M shares, about 2.0 percentage points of ownership dilution taking existing holders to ~98.0%; the 19.9% figure in the agreement is a contractual ceiling that would only bind near a $2.36 share price. The call option on the remaining 51% prices off ~$584M implied total equity value plus up to a 20% control premium, so full consolidation would cost roughly $584–644M cumulatively. The analytically significant leg is the counterparty's put, exercisable from roughly mid-2030, a contingent obligation the seller controls, potentially colliding with the deleveraging plan.

**Other:** independent-contractor classification litigation in the DSD model; Napoleon, Ohio Clean Water Act suits with DOJ and private plaintiffs in settlement discussions; MAHA/FDA synthetic-dye reformulation risk across Goldfish, soup and salty snacks, unquantified; credit-rating downgrade risk with $340M of commercial paper as a live funding channel (the 10-Q newly added rating outlook to its forward-looking factors); four Item 5.02 officer/director filings in twelve months, the contents of which are unverified.

**Short interest.** 31.2% of float, 48.2M shares, 7.8 days to cover. Informed bears are heavily positioned and have been right, but the trade is crowded, and a Q4 print that clears a low bar or a favorable tariff ruling could force a sharp mechanical rally. Float is compressed by family ownership — a single Dorrance trust holds roughly 17% of shares outstanding — which amplifies squeeze mechanics and reduces the probability of activist or takeover intervention.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.5% is rich against 31.5% one-year realized, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.5% near versus 32.6% at six months), with the 42-day September 25 tenor at 39.1% IV pricing a ±10.3% move. That tenor captures both the Q4 FY2026 release (early September) and the 10-K with the annual impairment test, so the market is paying up for a dated, two-sided event rather than a directional view. Skew is balanced (−0.0% overall), put/call volume is 0.58 and put/call open interest 0.62 — call-tilted, and down from 0.81 at the prior snapshot. Options positioning does not corroborate the bear case despite 31% short interest, which is consistent with shorts hedging via calls and with income overwriting on the yield. For holders trimming into the print, rich near-term premium makes covered-call overwriting a more efficient de-risking route than outright sales; for prospective buyers, elevated IV argues against paying for upside optionality ahead of September.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28, +21%)

FY2026 is a tariff-and-timing trough rather than a permanent margin reset. The gross tariff impact was 240bp of the 200bp nine-month gross margin decline; the company already expects $0.03–0.04 per share of Q4 refunds with more potentially extending into FY2027, and legal challenges to the tariffs are pending. If FY2027 inflation lands near the 3% base plan rather than 5–6%, and the remaining ~$175M of the $375M savings program flows through, FY2027 adjusted EPS can hold near $2.05–2.10 rather than the $1.94 consensus. Snacks margin recovering 200–300bp from a 9.9% base — the CFO has staked credibility on it being "not acceptable" — adds $0.15–0.20. La Regina secures the supply of the one growing $1B brand and captures manufacturing margin. At 13.5x recovering earnings the stock is worth $28, and holders collect a 6.7% dividend while waiting. With 31.2% of float short, any positive surprise is amplified mechanically.

### Bear Case ($16, −31%)

Volume decline is structural rather than cyclical. Traditional grocery is losing share to channels that create price deflation; retailers are expanding private label and demanding supplier-funded promotions; Campbell's can take one point of price only by losing four to five points of volume. The Snacks fix so far has come from withdrawing promotions and marketing, which borrows volume from FY2027. Unhedged first-half FY2027 inflation at 5–6%, a $40M incentive-comp reset, and continued volume erosion take FY2027 EPS toward $1.65–1.70. At ~4.4x net leverage with $230M of annual post-dividend free cash flow, the low-3x target is unreachable without EBITDA recovery, so either the dividend resets or the company issues expensive hybrid capital — both negative for the equity. A September trademark impairment on the $2.587B thin-coverage pool reinforces the narrative even though it is non-cash. At 9.5x $1.68 the stock is $16, near the $14 low analyst target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $21.50 (11.1x FY2027 consensus $1.94, essentially the $21.59 sell-side mean), −7.2% price from $23.165 and roughly −0.5% total return including the $1.56 dividend. Bull $28.00 (+20.9%). Bear $16.00 (−30.9%). Probability-weighted expected price of $21.15 at 50/20/30 weights implies ~−9% price and ~−4% total return. Horizon: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint in the next 4–6 weeks.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength ahead of the early-September Q4 FY2026 release and the September 10-K. The stock has rallied 5.9% since late July on no fundamental improvement, and 39.1% implied volatility on the September 25 tenor means the option market is paying well for upside; covered-call overwriting at strikes near $25 is a reasonable alternative to outright sales for taxable holders. Target residual exposure at no more than one-third of a full position, held for the yield, ahead of the FY2027 inflation and hedge-roll-off period. Do not add on weakness before the impairment test and FY2027 guidance are on the table.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $2.10 with FY2027 inflation framed at or below 4%, indicating tariff and energy pass-through is contained.
2. Snacks segment operating margin above 12% for two consecutive quarters achieved with trade and marketing spend flat or higher year-over-year — margin recovery from cost structure rather than spending withdrawal.
3. Organic net sales decline improving to −1% or better with volume/mix no worse than −2%, confirming the subtractive phase of the turnaround has ended.
4. Net leverage below 3.75x on a trailing basis with the dividend maintained and no hybrid issuance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. Any dividend reduction, or the issuance of hybrid capital at +150–200bp coupons to preserve the rating.
2. A negative rating action or outlook change that impairs commercial paper access (currently $340M outstanding).
3. FY2027 guidance below $1.75 adjusted EPS, or Snacks segment operating margin below 9% in any quarter.
4. Free cash flow falling below the annual dividend requirement (~$470M) on a trailing twelve-month basis.

**Reassessment timeline.** Early September 2026 Q4 FY2026 release and FY2027 guidance; the 10-K roughly two weeks later carrying the annual impairment test on the $2.587B thin-coverage trademark pool; then the Q1 FY2027 print in December 2026, which is the first quarter with hedges rolled off.

## Conclusion

Campbell's is a levered, volume-losing branded-food business trading at a defensible discount to peers for defensible reasons. The bull case rests on tariffs being a timing problem, a claim with real support in the disclosure, given the $0.02-to-$0.17 per-share step-up in a single year, expected Q4 refunds, and pending legal challenges. The bear case rests on the volume and channel evidence, which is unambiguous: both segments down 4%, U.S. soup down 8%, one point of price bought at the cost of five points of volume, customer concentration unchanged at 47% for three years, and a Snacks margin fix so far achieved by withdrawing promotional support.

At $23.17 the market is paying 11.9x for FY2027 earnings that consensus has cut 11% in 90 days, with sell-side mean and median targets below spot and six of twenty ratings at sell or worse. Our base case sits 7% below the current price, and total return to that base is roughly flat after the dividend, against a −31% bear. The dividend does not compensate for that asymmetry, and the two dated catalysts in September are more likely to confirm the deterioration than to resolve it favorably.

We stop short of AVOID. Free cash flow of $620–730M covers the $470M dividend; covenant coverage is roughly 4.7x against a 3.25x minimum; the revolver is undrawn to 2030; and 31.2% short interest with 7.8 days to cover makes the bearish view crowded rather than differentiated, with meaningful squeeze risk on any positive surprise in a name whose float is compressed by 17%+ family ownership. Existing holders should reduce into the September events and retain only what they are content to hold for the yield through an unhedged FY2027 first half. Prospective buyers should wait for FY2027 guidance and evidence that Snacks margin recovery is coming from cost structure rather than spending cuts.
