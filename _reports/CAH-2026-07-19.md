**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cardinal Health has transformed from a low-growth, defensive drug distributor into a specialty-care roll-up, and the market has repriced it accordingly. The stock is up roughly 43% over the past twelve months to $228.52, sits below its 52-week high of $243.21, and now trades at ~35x trailing earnings and ~21x forward EPS on consensus, a premium of roughly 60% to the trailing multiple of its two closest peers, McKesson (20.5x) and Cencora (21.6x). That re-rating is grounded in results. Non-GAAP EPS inflected from $8.24 in FY25 to $8.35 in the first nine months of FY26 (+36% YoY), the OptumRx contract loss (17% of FY24 revenue) has been fully backfilled and then some (9-month revenue +17%), and the higher-margin "Other" segment (nuclear, home solutions, freight logistics) grew profit 47%.

The valuation now embeds continued flawless execution of a debt-funded specialty pivot that has already shown its first crack. In Q3 FY26 the company took a $184M goodwill impairment on the Navista/ION oncology MSO, roughly fifteen months after acquiring ION for $1.1B, because long-term plan assumptions were reduced and the discount rate rose. The remaining carrying value ($1.1B, of which $909M is goodwill) carries a thin cushion: another 0.5% of discount rate erases $70M. Cardinal has deployed more than $7B on acquisitions across FY25-26 (GI Alliance, ION, Specialty Networks, ADS, Solaris) financed largely with new notes, driving interest expense up 91% year-over-year. The strategy is working on the adjusted-EPS line, but a growing share of the "adjustments" are real economic costs (physician equity share-comp with $490M still to vest) being excluded from non-GAAP.

We maintain a HOLD. The 12-month growth path is credible and analyst estimates are drifting higher (current-year consensus +4.5% over 90 days), which supports a growth-hold rather than a value trap. But at $228 the stock offers no margin of safety against a slate of live risks: CVS at 30% of revenue, an opioid liability that just saw a favorable verdict vacated, a DOJ investigation directly targeting the MSO structure the company is doubling down on, and a widening GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap. Our base case is $250, roughly 9% upside, insufficient reward-to-risk to justify a fresh BUY at these levels. This continues a three-report HOLD sequence, and with the scores declining (6.5 → 6.3 → 6.1), the current data does not reverse the trend.

## Company Overview

Cardinal Health is one of the U.S. "Big Three" pharmaceutical distributors alongside McKesson and Cencora. FY25 (ended June 30, 2025) revenue was $222.6B on gross profit of $8.2B: a 3.7% gross margin, 1.0% operating margin, and 0.7% net margin. These are the economics of high-volume drug logistics: margin is thin, asset turnover is high (4.19x), and the working-capital model runs deliberately negative (payables of $37.8B exceed inventory plus receivables), generating float.

The business splits into two reportable segments plus "Other":

- **Pharmaceutical & Specialty Solutions ("Pharma")** — ~92% of revenue ($204.6B FY25, $2.26B segment profit). Distributes branded, generic, and specialty drugs. Profit is driven by generic sourcing through the Red Oak Sourcing JV with CVS (extended to June 2029), branded distribution-service fees, and increasingly by newly acquired physician-practice management-services organizations (MSOs).
- **Global Medical Products & Distribution (GMPD)** — $12.6B revenue but only $135M profit (1.1% margin). Manufactures and sources Cardinal-branded medical/surgical products and distributes national brands. Chronically low-margin, tariff-exposed, and already fully goodwill-impaired ($675M FY24, $1.2B FY23 write-offs; zero goodwill remaining). A plausible future divestiture candidate.
- **"Other"** — Nuclear & Precision Health, at-Home Solutions (Edgepark/ADS), and OptiFreight Logistics. Small (~$5.4B revenue) but the fastest-growing and highest-margin unit ($516M profit, +47% in 9-mo FY26).

The defining strategic move, catalyzed by Elliott Management's 2023 involvement and a subsequent Board Business Review Committee, is a pivot up the value chain into specialty care and physician-practice management — oncology (Navista), GI and urology (The Specialty Alliance, now ~76% owned), and home diabetes (ADS). This mirrors peers' specialty pushes but is capital-intensive and execution-dependent.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and profitability are genuinely inflecting.** FY25 revenue fell 2% on the OptumRx expiration, but the company more than replaced that volume: 9-month FY26 revenue rose 17% to $190.6B, with Q3 up 11% to $60.9B. Non-GAAP EPS climbed from $5.85 (FY23) to $7.53 (FY24) to $8.24 (FY25), and 9-month FY26 already stands at $8.35, up 36% year-over-year. Segment profit momentum is strong: 9-month Pharma +24%, Other +47%, while GMPD lags (Q3 profit −36% on tariffs). The earnings surprise record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats, the last three by 9-16%.

**Cash flow is solid.** FY25 operating cash flow was $2.4B and FCF $1.85B; 9-month FY26 OCF reached $3.5B, aided by payables timing. On the current market cap of ~$53.4B, FY25 FCF represents a ~3.5% yield, respectable but not cheap for a distributor.

**Capital deployment is aggressive and M&A-first.** FY25 saw $5.3B on acquisitions, $765M of buybacks, $494M of dividends, and $400M of debt repayment; 9-month FY26 added $1.9B (Solaris) plus $1.0B of buybacks. Share count has fallen from ~279M (FY22) to ~234M (April 2026), and $1.8B of buyback authorization remains (expiring December 2027). The dividend is deprioritized: $2.04/share annualized, raised roughly 1%, yielding under 1%.

**The balance sheet requires interpretation.** Stockholders' equity is negative, −$2.83B at March 2026, but this is an artifact of cumulative buybacks (treasury stock of −$7.2B) exceeding paid-in capital, not accumulated losses. Retained earnings are positive at $1.7B, and the Altman Z-score of 4.27 places the company in the safe zone. A naive screen would flag this as distress; it is aggressive shareholder return. Total debt is ~$8.9B, up on acquisition financing, and net leverage is within the ≤3.75x covenant. The material trend is interest expense: 9-month FY26 expense of $269M is up 91% year-over-year, a direct consequence of the debt-funded M&A flywheel in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**The quality-of-earnings caveat.** The GAAP-to-non-GAAP divergence is widening. Cardinal excludes not only amortization but large and recurring acquisition-related cash and share-based comp: Specialty Alliance physician equity was $243M in 9-month FY26, with $490M still to vest over roughly four years. These are genuine economic costs of the roll-up strategy. GAAP Q3 operating earnings fell 30% (hit by the $184M impairment and higher share-comp), even as non-GAAP EPS rose 35%. Investors should weight GAAP more heavily here than for a typical distributor.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine has three components. First, core Pharma distribution is growing again after the OptumRx unwind, helped by branded volume and the generics program. Second, the specialty/MSO layer — oncology, GI, urology — adds higher-margin physician-services revenue on top of drug distribution. Third, the "Other" segment (nuclear imaging, home solutions, freight logistics) is compounding at 47% profit growth and carries structurally better margins than distribution.

Estimate revisions are supportive but not spectacular: current-year consensus EPS rose 4.5% over 90 days to $10.78, and next-year to $12.03 (+3.9%). These are the estimates that anchor the forward multiple; at $228 the stock trades at ~21x current-year and ~19x next-year consensus. The 90-day revision magnitude (+3.9% to +4.5%) is well below the +30% threshold that would flag a systematically underestimated inflection — the market has largely caught up to the improving numbers. The 30-day revisions are essentially flat, indicating consensus has stabilized rather than accelerated.

The durability question centers on the MSO roll-up. The model assumes Cardinal can acquire physician practices, retain the physicians (whose equity vests over years), grow the underlying specialty-drug and services revenue, and earn a return above the debt cost used to finance the deals. The Navista/ION impairment is direct evidence that at least one of these acquisitions is tracking below the deal model. State-level moves to restrict corporate ownership of physician practices (Oregon has already acted) add a regulatory headwind to the entire strategy. Growth is real, but its highest-margin, highest-multiple component is the least proven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $228.52, Cardinal trades at ~35x trailing GAAP EPS ($6.45) and ~21x current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS ($10.78). Against peers, this is a clear premium: McKesson at 20.5x, Cencora at 21.6x, Henry Schein at 24.3x, with a peer median of 21.6x on a trailing basis. Cardinal's forward multiple sits roughly in line with peers on consensus non-GAAP figures, but the trailing GAAP premium is stark and reflects the widening adjustment gap discussed above.

The bull argument is that Cardinal deserves a growth premium because non-GAAP EPS is inflecting (+36%) while peers grow low-double-digits, and because the specialty mix should command a higher multiple than pure distribution. There is merit here: the "Other" and specialty segments do warrant a premium to core distribution economics. But two considerations cap the valuation score. First, the premium is being paid on non-GAAP earnings that increasingly exclude real costs; on GAAP, the premium is far larger and less defensible. Second, the stock has already re-rated to capture much of the good news: it sits within 6% of its 52-week high, and the analyst price-target mean of $249.60 (median $255, range $215-275) implies only ~9-12% base-case upside from here.

A "cheap" screen is not a buy signal in reverse; this stock is not cheap, and the multiple already prices in continued execution. Baird's May 2026 PT cut to $248 from $257, even while reiterating Outperform, captures the setup: analysts like the direction but see limited near-term room. The removal from Russell 1000 Defensive indices in July 2026 signals the market no longer views Cardinal as a defensive holding, which removes a structural buyer base. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is stretched relative to the GAAP fundamentals and the quality of the earnings being capitalized, with the upside case requiring the roll-up to keep working flawlessly.

## Competitive Landscape

Cardinal operates in a stable Big-Three distribution oligopoly with enormous scale barriers, but structurally limited pricing power against very large customers (CVS, and the Vizient/Premier GPOs at 27% of revenue combined). Core distribution is a low-growth, low-margin, scale game. The Red Oak Sourcing JV with CVS (extended to 2029) is a genuine generic-sourcing moat that lowers Cardinal's cost of goods.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the specialty/MSO and "Other" pivot moves Cardinal into higher-margin, stickier physician relationships where it can differentiate from pure distribution, a share-and-margin gain if execution holds. On the negative side, GMPD is a structural weak point: fully impaired, tariff-exposed, and competing against Medline and Owens & Minor from a position of chronic sub-scale margins. And the specialty strategy is not proprietary; McKesson and Cencora are pushing into the same specialty and oncology adjacencies, so the roll-up premium is contested. The Navista impairment suggests Cardinal may be paying full price for assets that do not yet clear its cost of capital.

We score competitive position 6.3, consistent with the declining trajectory (7.5 → 6.8 → 6.3 across prior reports). The core moat is intact, but the strategic pivot's competitive payoff is unproven and the impairment is a data point against it.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** CVS Health represents 30% of FY25 revenue and 26% of trade receivables; the top five customers are 43%. The precedent is not theoretical: OptumRx, at 17% of FY24 revenue, walked in June 2024. Loss or repricing of the CVS relationship would be materially destabilizing, and this risk is not fully priced at a premium multiple.

**Specialty/MSO execution risk is newly elevated.** The Navista/ION impairment, with a thin remaining cushion, is concrete evidence the oncology roll-up is underperforming. State restrictions on corporate ownership of physician practices add regulatory risk to the whole strategy.

**Opioid litigation overhang persists.** $4.3B accrued at March 2026, with up to $4.1B more in payments through 2038 and a compliance monitor until 2027. The previously-won Cabell County/Huntington case was vacated by the 4th Circuit in October 2025 and remanded, reopening tail risk.

**Regulatory and tax:** The DOJ Civil Investigative Demand (November 2023) on Anti-Kickback/False Claims relating to a 2022 rheumatology MSO/GPO purchase is directly relevant given the MSO strategy Cardinal is scaling. An IRS NOPA (February 2026) asserts ~$160M plus interest on a 2017 restructuring. A Most-Favored-Nation drug-pricing EO could pressure branded economics.

**Tariff optionality (potential positive):** ~$200M of IEEPA tariffs was paid on charges later ruled unlawful (Supreme Court, February 2026); refunds, if they arrive, would flow disproportionately to the weak GMPD segment. Timing is highly uncertain and the government has substituted Section 122 tariffs, so cost pressure continues.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed but leans complacent-to-cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 26.0% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 29.5% (a −3.6% spread), placing IV in the 1st percentile of its own chain: the options market is pricing minimal near-term risk, which can be favorable entry timing but also signals complacency. The term structure is in contango (near-term 26.0% below 6-month 28.7%), normal. The one caution: near-term put/call skew of 8.6% shows elevated put demand at the front, and the 1-week put/call volume ratio of 3.43 indicates active near-term downside hedging. ATM IV has collapsed from 43.5% to 26.0% since mid-June as the last earnings event passed. For a HOLD, the read is that the market is not pricing an imminent shock, but the front-end put skew is worth respecting given the concentration and litigation tail.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($275):** The specialty/MSO pivot continues to compound non-GAAP EPS at 20%+, the "Other" segment keeps growing 40%+, GMPD stabilizes as tariff refunds arrive, and the market awards a full growth premium. Consensus estimates keep drifting higher, buybacks reduce the share count further, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $275, roughly 20% above current. This requires the Navista impairment to prove an isolated event and the DOJ/state MSO regulatory risks to remain contained.

**Bear Case ($185):** The Navista impairment is the first of several as the debt-funded roll-up runs into higher discount rates and physician-retention friction. The widening GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap draws scrutiny and the multiple compresses toward peer levels (~20x forward). A CVS repricing or the DOJ investigation escalating adds a fundamental shock. Rising interest expense squeezes the leverage flywheel. The stock reverts toward $185, roughly 19% below current, near the low end of the analyst range and the FY25 pre-inflection zone.

**Base Case ($250):** The growth engine continues but at a decelerating pace, GAAP scrutiny caps multiple expansion, and the stock tracks toward the analyst mean of $249.60 over 12 months as estimates rise modestly. This is roughly 9% upside, a real but unexciting return for the risk carried.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4/FY26 earnings report (expected August 2026), followed by FY27 guidance. The DOJ CID and Cabell County remand are open-ended but material.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$190s (≤~18x forward EPS) that restores a margin of safety while estimates hold or rise — this would flip the reward-to-risk to 2:1 or better.
- Clean FY27 guidance with no further specialty/MSO impairments AND evidence the DOJ investigation is resolving favorably.
- GMPD stabilization plus confirmed tariff refunds, removing a structural drag and adding a cash tailwind.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any additional goodwill impairment on the Specialty Alliance or Navista assets, signaling the roll-up model is broadly failing.
- A CVS contract repricing/loss disclosure, or a materially adverse DOJ resolution.
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions turning negative alongside a GAAP operating-earnings decline that cannot be attributed to one-time items.

**Base case target:** $250, derived from ~21x the next-twelve-months consensus EPS midpoint (~$11.90 blending FY26 $10.78 and FY27 $12.03), consistent with the analyst mean of $249.60. This sits between the $185 bear and $275 bull.

**Upside/downside from current ($228.52):** +9% to base ($250), +20% to bull ($275), −19% to bear ($185).

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to FY26 results, FY27 guidance, and progression of the DOJ/opioid overhangs.

## Conclusion

Cardinal Health has executed a credible turnaround: it backfilled a 17%-of-revenue customer loss, reaccelerated top-line growth to 17%, and delivered a 36% non-GAAP EPS inflection driven by a deliberate move into higher-margin specialty care. That story is real, and the growth thesis is intact enough to warrant holding rather than selling; analyst estimates are rising and the earnings surprise record is clean.

The reward-to-risk at $228 does not support a new buy. The stock has re-rated to a ~60% trailing premium to distributor peers on non-GAAP earnings that increasingly exclude real costs, the Navista/ION impairment is a concrete crack in the roll-up model fifteen months after purchase, interest expense is up 91% as the leverage flywheel spins, and the concentration (CVS 30%), litigation (opioid, vacated verdict), and regulatory (DOJ CID targeting the MSO structure) risks are live and only partially priced. Our base case offers ~9% upside against a bear case of ~19% downside.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** We would become buyers on a pullback to the low-$190s or on confirmation that the specialty pivot is de-risking without further impairments. We would move to AVOID on a second impairment, a CVS disclosure, or an adverse DOJ development. The scores continue their gradual decline (6.1 → 5.7), driven by valuation catching up to the growth and risk factors remaining elevated; the current data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory.
