## Executive Summary

Leidos Holdings is a $18.4B government technology and mission services contractor trading at $146.06, a 29% discount to its 52-week high of $205.77. The stock offers a forward P/E of ~13.1x against consensus FY2026 EPS of $12.34, well below the peer median of 22.7x and direct competitor CACI at 22.7x. This discount reflects three compounding concerns: elevated post-acquisition leverage following the $2.4B Entrust deal, DOGE-related federal procurement uncertainty, and a selloff from recent highs. Each of these concerns is legitimate. But the underlying business is generating $1.6B in annual FCF, carries a $49B backlog, and is seeing upward estimate revisions (+2.5% for FY2027 over 30 days). The market is pricing the risks without adequately pricing the cash flow.

The Entrust acquisition, which closed March 30, 2026, carries both strategic upside and integration risk. It doubles Leidos's energy infrastructure presence, adds a commercial revenue stream that reduces government concentration from 87% toward ~80%, and taps into secular grid modernization demand driven by data center load growth. It also adds ~$2.4B in incremental debt and won't be EPS-accretive until 2027. The Health & Civil segment's strength (23.7% operating margins, 57% of operating income) is the financial backstop that makes this acquisition feasible without threatening the dividend or core operations.

## Company Overview

Leidos operates four segments serving U.S. government and commercial customers: National Security & Digital (44% of revenue), Health & Civil (29%), Commercial & International (13%), and Defense Systems (13%). The company employs approximately 47,000 people (50,000+ post-Entrust), with the vast majority holding security clearances. Revenue split is approximately 87% U.S. government, with the Department of Defense (recently renamed Department of War) and Intelligence Community representing ~49% of total revenue.

The contract mix — 44% cost-reimbursement/FPIF, 43% firm-fixed-price, 13% time-and-materials/FPLOE — provides both revenue predictability and margin expansion opportunity on well-executed fixed-price work. The company's NorthStar 2030 strategy targets five growth pillars: space & maritime, energy infrastructure, digital modernization & cyber, mission software, and managed health services.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** FY2025 revenue reached $17.2B (+3% YoY), while operating income surged 15% to $2.11B as operating margins expanded 130bps to 12.3%. Net income of $1.45B represents an 8.5% net margin, up from 7.5% in FY2024. The margin expansion is concentrated in Health & Civil (23.7%, up from 21.8%) and recovery in Commercial & International and Defense Systems, both of which posted 60%+ operating income growth after prior-year write-downs.

**Free Cash Flow:** FCF of $1.625B (9.5% FCF margin) is the strongest signal in the financial profile. Operating cash flow grew 22% YoY to $1.75B, aided by OBBBA tax benefits (~$91M expected reduction in FY2026 tax payments) and favorable working capital. FCF conversion relative to net income exceeds 100%, indicating high-quality earnings.

**Balance Sheet — Post-Acquisition Leverage is the Key Concern:** Pre-Entrust, total debt was $4.65B against equity of ~$2.67B (D/E of 1.74x). The Entrust deal was funded with $1.4B in bridge loans and $1.39B in new senior notes at 5.40-5.50%. Post-close, total debt is approximately $7B+, pushing D/E north of 2.5x. The revolving credit facility was preemptively expanded from $1.0B to $1.5B in February 2026.

This leverage level is manageable given $1.6B+ annual FCF generation, implying net debt/EBITDA of roughly 2.5-3.0x post-close, but it constrains capital allocation flexibility. Goodwill will increase significantly from the current $6.34B (47% of assets), adding impairment risk if energy infrastructure market conditions deteriorate.

**Capital Returns:** FY2025 saw $900M in buybacks ($400M open market + $500M ASR) plus $211M in dividends, totaling ~$1.1B or 68% of FCF. Post-Entrust, expect buyback activity to moderate substantially as the company prioritizes deleveraging. The Executive Order on "Prioritizing the Warfighter" could further restrict buybacks during periods of perceived contract underperformance.

**Backlog:** $49.0B total ($9.7B funded), up from $48.4B. However, a methodology change adding $4.8B in estimated sole-source IDIQ revenue to unfunded backlog inflates comparability. Underlying net bookings declined from $23.2B to $17.5B (book-to-bill of 1.02x vs. 1.39x prior year). The bookings deceleration warrants monitoring — a sub-1.0x book-to-bill for two consecutive quarters would signal organic demand erosion.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth drivers are identifiable but moderate in magnitude:

1. **Entrust integration and energy infrastructure expansion:** The acquisition doubles Leidos's energy presence and accesses the grid modernization market ($100B+ opportunity). AI data center buildout, electrification, and resilience requirements provide secular demand. Revenue contribution is immediate; EPS accretion expected in 2027.

2. **Defense spending on air defense, cyber, and space:** The $617M IFPC Increment 2 Army contract (April 2026) validates ongoing demand. Sustained global security spending supports continued procurement.

3. **Managed health services margin expansion:** Health & Civil's margin represents operational excellence in a high-barrier, high-switching-cost niche. Continued write-ups and efficiency gains could push margins further, though the ceiling for services margins is real.

4. **OBBBA tax benefits:** Restored R&D deduction treatment improves after-tax economics through FY2026 and beyond.

Analyst estimates reflect this trajectory: FY2026E EPS of $12.34 (rising from $12.22 thirty days ago) and FY2027E of $13.18 (rising from $12.86). The +2.5% upward revision in next-year estimates over 30 days is the most constructive revision signal among defense contractors of this size.

Growth Prospects score of 6.5 reflects moderate organic revenue growth (~3-5% base rate) augmented by acquisition contribution, offset by bookings deceleration and the 1+ year EPS drag from Entrust integration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $146.06, LDOS trades at:
- **13.1x forward P/E** (FY2026E EPS of $12.34) vs. peer median of 22.7x
- **~11.1x FY2027E EPS** of $13.18
- **~9% FCF yield** on pre-acquisition FCF of $1.625B (post-acquisition FCF will be moderately lower due to integration costs)
- **29% below 52-week high** of $205.77

The discount to peers is substantial. CACI trades at 22.7x, KBR at 10.5x, and RBA at 50.6x. The LDOS discount relative to CACI is partly justified by higher leverage post-Entrust and greater government concentration, but 13.1x for a business generating 9.5% FCF margins with $49B backlog materially underprices the cash flow stream. Applying a conservative 15x multiple to FY2026E EPS yields $185, representing 27% upside.

The sell-side consensus target is $199 (mean $199.21, median $201.50), with a range of $165-$232. The low-end target of $165 provides only 13% upside, suggesting limited downside in the analyst community's worst-case scenario. The wide dispersion ($165-$232) reflects genuine uncertainty about Entrust accretion timing and government spending trajectories.

The 7.5 valuation score reflects a clear market mispricing: the stock is being valued as if organic growth has stalled and Entrust will fail, while estimate revisions are moving in the opposite direction. The specific catalyst that could close this gap is demonstrated Entrust revenue contribution in Q2-Q3 FY2026 earnings reports and clarity on deleveraging pace.

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos occupies a strong position among the top-tier government IT and services contractors. Its 47,000+ cleared employees, deep incumbent relationships across DoD (now Department of War), IC, DHS, and civilian agencies create meaningful barriers to entry. The company's adoption of "Department of War" nomenclature and alignment with warfighter-centric procurement suggests political positioning that may support contract wins.

The Entrust acquisition shifts competitive dynamics by creating a dual-market presence. In energy infrastructure, Leidos will compete with Quanta Services, MasTec, and Burns & McDonnell — larger, more experienced players. The integration risk is that Leidos's government contracting culture may not translate seamlessly to commercial utility work. However, the combination of IT/software capabilities with physical infrastructure engineering is differentiated and difficult to replicate.

Within defense IT, the competitive set (CACI, SAIC, BAH, ManTech) is stable but faces identical procurement reform risks. Leidos's scale advantage and diversification across all four segments provides resilience that smaller competitors lack. The risk from EO 14275 (federal procurement reform opening pathways for commercial firms) is real but unlikely to meaningfully erode market share for at least 2-3 years given the complexity of cleared government work.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Post-Acquisition Leverage (High):** Net debt/EBITDA likely exceeds 2.5x post-Entrust. With rates elevated and buyback restrictions possible, the company has limited margin for error. A miss on integration targets or a downturn in energy infrastructure spending could strain the balance sheet. The $311M SES goodwill is an additional impairment watch item.

**2. Government Spending Concentration (High):** At 87% (declining toward ~80% post-Entrust), any continuing resolution, government shutdown (DHS is already experiencing one), or sequestration-type event directly impacts revenue. The Executive Order on warfighter prioritization introduces a new mechanism for government intervention in contractor capital allocation.

**3. Health & Civil Margin Sustainability (Moderate):** This segment generates 57% of operating income on 29% of revenue. The 23.7% margin relies on write-ups and operational efficiencies on managed health programs. Re-competition losses, pricing pressure, or performance issues here would have outsized profitability impact.

**4. Bookings Deceleration (Moderate):** Net bookings declined from $23.2B to $17.5B. The backlog methodology change masks this deterioration. Two consecutive quarters of sub-1.0x book-to-bill would signal a structural demand problem.

**5. Fixed-Price Contract Inflation Risk (Moderate):** With 43% of revenue on FFP contracts and energy/labor cost inflation persistent, margin compression on new bids is possible.

### Options Market Signal

LDOS near-term IV of 40.6% is substantially above 1-year HV of 28.8%, a premium of 11.8 percentage points. The term structure is in backwardation (40.6% near-term vs. 31.7% at 6 months), indicating the market prices elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 2.06 and volume ratio of 1.66 show elevated put demand, consistent with institutional hedging around post-earnings uncertainty and Entrust integration concerns. The options market is pricing approximately ±8% movement in the near term, implying a range of roughly $134-$158.

This elevated put demand suggests informed participants are hedging downside, which diverges modestly from the fundamental BUY thesis. The divergence warrants position sizing discipline (2-3% rather than a full-conviction 4-5% position) and argues for patience on entry timing.

## Conclusion

Leidos at $146 offers a well-run defense and government IT franchise with $1.6B FCF, rising estimate revisions, trading at 13x forward earnings — a 42% discount to the peer median. The discount exists for identifiable reasons (post-Entrust leverage, DOGE uncertainty, government spending risk), and each risk is real. But the market appears to be pricing a permanent impairment rather than a temporary absorption period for a strategically sound acquisition. At these levels, the stock is valued as if Entrust will fail, Health & Civil margins will compress, and defense spending will contract — all simultaneously. That combined scenario has a probability well below what the current multiple implies.

The prior three analyses rated LDOS as BUY with scores trending upward (6.8 → 6.9 → 7.0). The current analysis acknowledges the growth deceleration (bookings decline, integration drag) that moves the score modestly lower at 6.68, but the valuation compression since the last analysis (from ~$155 to $146) has widened the margin of safety. The options market's elevated put demand and backwardated term structure confirm near-term uncertainty pricing, which creates the entry opportunity for a 12-month holding period.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% position sizing given elevated leverage and options-implied volatility. Target $180, stop-loss review at $125.
