## Executive Summary

Boyd Gaming is a well-capitalized regional casino operator whose consolidated operating earnings have not grown in five years. Adjusted EBITDAR — the company's own selected performance measure, disclosed in the March 2026 proxy — ran $1,366M (2021), $1,391M (2022), $1,395M (2023), $1,391M (2024) and $1,354M (2025), and trailing-twelve-month EBITDAR through June 30, 2026 is approximately $1,326M. Over the same period weighted-average diluted shares fell from 108.9M (FY2022) to 74.8M (Q2 2026), a 31.3% reduction. First-half 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.53 versus $3.49 was delivered on EBITDAR that declined 4.0%. The buyback did the work, and it is now partly financed with the revolver: principal debt rose $580.4M in the first half, to $2,641.1M, against $311.1M of repurchases, $297.1M of capex and a $341.0M one-time tax settlement.

The offsetting facts are substantial. Leverage is 1.75x net debt to TTM Adjusted EBITDAR against a 4.50x covenant; the January 2026 credit agreement pre-funds the $1.0B 4.750% 2027 notes through an $800M undrawn delayed-draw term loan plus $1,094.7M of revolver availability; and Midwest & South, at roughly 56% of segment EBITDAR, grew revenue 3.1% and EBITDAR 3.6% in Q2 with margin expansion, absorbing the November 2025 closure of Sam's Town Tunica. The two large drags are identifiable and, importantly, dated: the Online segment's EBITDAR collapse is contractual (market access fee revenue down $28.9M in the half following the FanDuel transaction) and largely complete by year-end 2026, and the Las Vegas Locals weakness is concentrated in destination business at The Orleans plus roughly $3M per quarter of Suncoast construction disruption that management expects to end in Q3.

At $83.42 the stock is 11.8x the $7.08 FY2026 consensus and 10.75x the $7.76 FY2027 consensus, versus Red Rock Resorts at 19.9x and Churchill Downs at 15.9x. That discount is real but not unearned: consensus models adjusted EPS declining ~5% in 2026 despite the share count falling ~9%, which means pre-share-count earnings power is contracting roughly in the low double digits as the depreciation from Cadence Crossing, the Norfolk transitional casino and the renovation program arrives before the returns. Estimates are falling in every window (current quarter -3.0% over 30 days, current year -2.2% over 90 days). Base case is $92, roughly 10% above spot, against a $70 bear case. That combination — modest positive expected return, a strong balance sheet, a dated catalyst path, and no margin of safety — is a HOLD for the fifth consecutive review, with an explicitly named upgrade path rather than an open-ended one.

## Company Overview

Boyd owns and operates 27 gaming properties across 11 states, manages one tribal casino (Sky River, for the Wilton Rancheria tribe in northern California), and owns Boyd Interactive, a B2B/B2C online gaming business. Five reporting segments, with Q2 2026 detail:

| Segment | Q2 2026 revenue | Q2 2026 Adj. EBITDAR | YoY EBITDAR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midwest & South | $556.9M | $208.7M | +3.6% |
| Las Vegas Locals | $225.9M | $106.4M | −5.6% |
| Downtown Las Vegas | $52.1M | $16.9M | −12.9% |
| Online | $158.2M | $10.6M | −52.3% |
| Managed & Other | $41.3M | $30.7M | +18.2% |
| Corporate (net of SBC) | — | ($22.9M) | — |
| **Total** | **$1,034.4M** | **$350.5M** | **−2.1%** |

The most consequential structural fact is that Boyd is not primarily a Las Vegas company. Midwest & South generates roughly 56% of segment-level EBITDAR before corporate expense; the two Nevada segments together generate about 33%. Boyd owns most of its real estate outright, unlike MGM, Caesars or Penn; only four properties (Ameristar Kansas City, Ameristar St. Charles, Belterra Resort, Belterra Park) sit under a Gaming and Leisure Properties master lease at roughly $114M of annual rent, renewed through April 30, 2031 with five further five-year options. Downtown Las Vegas is a genuinely differentiated niche: Hawaiian customers were 55% of occupied rooms in Q2 2026 (up from 53%), with Hawaiian room nights up 3.8%, fed by a company-owned Hawaii travel agency.

One presentation caveat governs all trend work. Beginning in Q3 2025, Boyd grosses up "online reimbursements" — gaming taxes for which it is primary obligor under certain market access agreements — in both revenue and expense at exactly zero margin ($126.4M in Q2 2026). Reported revenue growth is therefore contaminated by third-party operators' tax obligations. Adjusted EBITDAR is the only clean consolidated trend metric, which is why this report anchors on it.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 and first-half results.** Q2 revenue was $1,034.4M versus $1,034.0M (flat). Adjusted EBITDAR fell 2.1% to $350.5M; Adjusted EBITDA after master lease rent fell 2.4% to $321.6M. GAAP diluted EPS was $1.75 versus $1.84; adjusted EPS was $1.93 versus $1.87. Against the structured consensus of $1.90, the adjusted print was a 1.4% beat — the third beat in four quarters, following an 8.5% miss in Q1 2026 ($1.60 versus $1.75). Note that several secondary outlets characterized the quarter as a "double miss" by comparing GAAP $1.75 against a non-GAAP consensus; on matching bases the quarter was in line on revenue and modestly ahead on earnings.

**The gap between EBITDAR and operating income is depreciation, and it matters.** Q2 operating income fell 17.2% to $200.7M while EBITDAR fell 2.1%. The reconciling items are D&A of $91.1M versus $70.0M (+30.2%) and project development/preopening/writedowns of $15.4M versus $2.8M. For the half, D&A was $186.1M versus $138.2M (+34.7%), and project charges $35.6M versus $1.2M (including roughly $28.0M of demolition and writedowns tied to the Eastside Cannery implosion and the Jokers Wild replacement, plus $7.6M of preopening).

Adjusted EPS excludes the writedowns and preopening costs but not depreciation. That is the mechanical explanation for why consensus expects FY2026 adjusted EPS of $7.08 despite a ~9-10% reduction in share count: Cadence Crossing (opened March 25, 2026), the Norfolk transitional casino (November 7, 2025) and roughly $75M of hotel renovations have entered service and begun depreciating before contributing full-run-rate EBITDAR. An alternative explanation I cannot exclude from the filings read is that part of the D&A increase reflects shortened useful lives on assets slated for the Par-A-Dice transformation and Orleans refresh, which would make some of the step-up a forward signal of further asset churn rather than purely new-asset placement.

**Cash flow looks alarming and is not.** Reported first-half operating cash flow was $110.8M versus $461.4M. The difference is almost entirely a $341.0M cash payment for purchased 2025 transferable federal energy tax credits, used to settle federal tax on the 2025 FanDuel gain (accrued at $371.3M on December 31, 2025; $23.4M remaining at June 30, 2026). Adding that back gives normalized first-half OCF of roughly $452M versus $461M, down about 2%, tracking EBITDAR. Against $297.1M of first-half capex, normalized free cash flow was roughly $155M; against maintenance-only capex (~$125M, half of the $250M full-year maintenance guide), maintenance FCF was roughly $327M. Cash conversion quality is intact.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: $315.0M revolver + $400.0M Term A + $26.1M swing line, $1,000.0M 4.750% notes due 2027, $900.0M 4.750% notes due 2031; cash $322.7M; net debt $2,318.4M. Derived leverage is 1.75x net debt/TTM Adjusted EBITDAR and 1.91x on Adjusted EBITDA after master lease rent, against covenants of 4.50x maximum net leverage and 2.50x minimum interest coverage. (Credit-agreement-defined EBITDA will differ from Adjusted EBITDAR; the cushion is directional, not precise, but the direction is unambiguous.) First-half net interest expense fell 41.5% to $59.9M on average debt of $2,339.2M versus $3,474.9M.

Refinancing risk on the 2027 notes is effectively eliminated. The January 21, 2026 facility provides a $1,450M revolver plus a $1,200M delayed-draw Term A maturing January 2031, drawable until July 1, 2027, with a springing September 1, 2027 maturity if the 2027 notes are not termed out. Remaining capacity of $800M Term A plus $1,094.7M revolver equals $1,894.7M against $1.0B of 2027 maturities. The cost is a modest interest headwind: retiring 4.750% fixed paper into floating bank debt at prevailing rates, with 28.1% of debt already floating and a 100bp move worth roughly $7.4M annually.

**A screening artifact worth correcting.** A widely used financial-health screen returns an Altman Z of 1.33 (distress) for Boyd on an operating margin of −0.4% and EBIT/assets of −0.002. Those inputs are irreconcilable with FY2025 operating income of $748.4M on total assets of $6,574.7M (11.4% EBIT/assets). Recomputing the five-factor Z with FY2025 data and current market equity gives approximately 2.40 — the grey zone, which is the normal location for an asset-heavy, deliberately negative-working-capital cash business. The current ratio of 0.54 is by design; the working capital deficit narrowed from $448.5M to $141.0M in the half precisely because the tax credit liability was settled. Direct evidence dominates: 1.75x leverage, $1.9B of committed liquidity, interest expense down 41.5%.

## Growth Analysis

**What is growing.** Midwest & South delivered first-half EBITDAR of $401.4M versus $384.6M (+4.4%) with margin expansion, driven by increased core and retail play plus recent capital investments, and it did so while losing Sam's Town Tunica in November 2025 — so same-store growth exceeds the reported 3.1%. Managed & Other grew EBITDAR 18.2% in Q2 on Sky River's completed expansion, at roughly 74% segment margin. These two segments together are roughly 64% of segment EBITDAR.

**What is shrinking, and why.** Online EBITDAR fell 52.3% in Q2 and 58.5% in the half, to $18.9M from $45.6M. This is contractual, not cyclical: market access agreements were terminated in connection with the FanDuel equity sale and replacements carry lower rates, with market access fee revenue down $13.9M in the quarter and $28.9M in the half. Ohio market access ended June 30, 2025. The 10-Q states the lower margins will continue. Secondary reporting indicates management raised FY2026 online EBITDAR guidance to $35–40M, which is arithmetically consistent with the first-half actual; I could not verify that figure in a filing. The relevant point for forward modeling is that the step-down completes by end-2026, giving 2027 a clean comparison.

Las Vegas Locals EBITDAR fell 5.9% in the half with margin compressing from 49.2% to 47.1%. Management attributes it to destination softness "primarily at the Orleans" plus roughly $3M per quarter of Suncoast construction disruption, and states that excluding those two properties the segment grew with property margins above 50%. Downtown fell 12.9% in Q2 on reduced pedestrian traffic across the downtown area, with the Hawaiian channel intact (+3.8% room nights).

**Is Nevada weakness market-wide or Boyd-specific?** Red Rock Resorts reported its second-highest second-quarter gaming revenue and profitability in company history while absorbing a $385M Durango expansion and simultaneous renovations at two other properties. That comparison argues Boyd's locals weakness is at least partly asset- and positioning-specific: The Orleans is Boyd's most destination-exposed locals asset (large convention space, Strip proximity, an arena), while Red Rock's portfolio skews to newer, higher-income residential submarkets. The counter-reading is that Durango's ramp is a company-specific tailwind masking a soft market. Both are probably partly true; the asset-specific component is non-trivial and is being answered with capital.

**The capital answer.** FY2026 capex guidance is $650–700M: $250M maintenance, $75M hotel renovations principally at Orleans, $50M growth (Cadence Crossing completion, Par-A-Dice design), and $300M for Norfolk. Management's stated milestones: Suncoast casino floor, sportsbook, bingo, high-limit and meeting space complete by end of Q3 with improved performance from Q4; Orleans casino floor refresh starting in 1H 2027; more than 70% of Las Vegas room inventory renovated and 17 new F&B concepts by early 2027. Cadence Crossing is expected to begin contributing later in Q3 and ramp into Q4. Norfolk's full $750M resort opens late 2027.

**Forward EBITDAR path.** FY2026 EBITDAR should land near $1,310–1,330M. FY2027 has four identifiable additions — a clean online comparison, completed Suncoast/Orleans room renovations, a full year of Cadence Crossing, and continued 3–4% Midwest & South growth — plausibly supporting $1,370–1,400M, or 4–6% growth, which is what the $7.76 FY2027 consensus (+9.6% off the 2026 base, with buyback contribution) implicitly requires. It is achievable, not assured, and the Norfolk preopening drag runs against it.

**The 2029 item consensus is not discussing.** The Q2 10-Q states Boyd manages Sky River for a seven-year period from the August 15, 2022 opening. Management fee revenue was $54.7M in the half, annualizing near $110M at close to 100% incremental margin — roughly 8% of TTM Adjusted EBITDAR — from a contract that on its face runs out around August 2029. Under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, management contracts are generally capped at five years, extendable to seven with National Indian Gaming Commission approval, so seven years appears to be the statutory maximum, which argues against a simple renewal. A successor arrangement (consulting, development, branding, or renegotiated structure) is plausible given Boyd's relationship with the tribe dating to 2012, and I did not locate disclosure of renewal mechanics. This is an inference from the disclosed term, not a filing statement of expiry, but it is the largest unpriced item in the forward EBITDAR bridge.

## Valuation Assessment

**Reported multiples are unusable.** The trailing and forward P/E fields of ~3.6x on EPS of $22.56 reflect FY2025 GAAP net income of $1,843.3M, which includes the FanDuel gain. Ignore them.

**On consensus adjusted EPS:** $83.42 / $7.08 = 11.8x FY2026; $83.42 / $7.76 = 10.75x FY2027.

**On enterprise value:** market cap $6,060.9M + net debt $2,318.4M = EV $8,379.3M. Against TTM Adjusted EBITDAR of $1,326.3M that is 6.32x. Capitalizing the $644.5M on-balance-sheet operating lease liability as debt gives 6.8x. If the GLPI master lease sits outside that balance and requires separate capitalization (roughly $914M at 8x rent), the multiple rises to about 7.0x. I did not verify the lease classification, so treat 6.3–7.0x as the range.

**Peer context.** Peer medians are distorted: Caesars (−12.5x) and Penn (−3.1x) carry negative earnings. The usable comparisons are Red Rock Resorts at 19.9x (the direct Las Vegas locals competitor, growing, with a newer asset base), Churchill Downs at 15.9x (31% below its 52-week high) and Wynn at 30.9x (destination/Macau/UAE). Boyd at 11.8x forward is the cheapest identifiable comparable on earnings. The question is whether the discount is a mispricing or a fair reflection of flat operating earnings.

I think it is mostly fair, and this is why I do not score valuation above 6. Boyd's growth is being purchased rather than generated: EBITDAR down 2.6% in 2025 and roughly 2% TTM, adjusted EPS expected to fall ~5% in 2026, and the entire five-year per-share record attributable to a 31% share-count reduction that now requires incremental borrowing. Against normalized annual operating cash generation of roughly $850–900M, a $650–700M capex program plus ~$58M of dividends plus a ~$600M annualized buyback pace does not close — the first half proved it, with debt up $580.4M. Norfolk keeps capex elevated through 2027 before it drops sharply in 2028. So the buyback either moderates, or leverage drifts from 1.75x toward 2.3–2.5x over two years. Either path reduces the per-share tailwind that has been the whole story.

**What does support the multiple.** The absolute level is low for an asset-owning operator with 1.75x leverage and refinancing pre-solved. The remaining $551.1M authorization equals roughly 6.6M shares, or 9.1% of outstanding, at current prices. And the price is 4.9% lower than at the prior review on a quarter that was in line to modestly ahead on adjusted earnings — which improves risk/reward at the margin, even though estimates drifted down 1–3% over the same window.

**Analyst targets.** Mean $95.81 (+14.9%), median $93.00 (+11.5%), low $87.00 (+4.3%), high $111.00 (+33%). Every published target sits above spot, yet the rating distribution is 6 buy / 12 hold / 0 sell, with one strong-buy downgraded to buy in the last month. That combination — targets above price, ratings at hold — is the sell-side's way of saying the valuation is undemanding but the earnings trajectory does not justify conviction. I would not treat these targets as independent confirmation of upside: the condition under which analyst targets lag reality is rising estimates, and Boyd's estimates are falling in every window.

**Base case $92.** 11.8x the $7.76 FY2027 consensus, i.e. holding today's multiple on the consensus recovery year. Cross-check on EV: 6.75x on FY2027 EBITDAR of $1,380M = $9,315M EV, less roughly $2,500M of year-end 2026 net debt = $6,815M across ~70M shares (after further repurchase) = $97. Averaging the two anchors and haircutting for the falling-estimate trend gives $92, about 10% above spot.

**Bull case $105.** 13.5x $7.76 or 7.5x on $1,400M of EBITDAR with the buyback sustained: requires demonstrated Q4 2026 inflection in Las Vegas Locals, Cadence Crossing ramping to plan, and a clean online comparison in 2027.

**Bear case $70 (−16%).** 10x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $7.00 if Las Vegas destination softness persists past the renovation window, the H2 consumer slowdown reaches regional gaming volumes, and the buyback moderates to preserve leverage. A goodwill or gaming-license impairment in Midwest & South, where the cushion appears thin, would reinforce the derating even though it is non-cash.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives roughly +4.6% price return plus a 0.96% dividend — approximately fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

Boyd's moats are real but narrow. The Boyd Rewards nationwide loyalty program and a locals/repeat-visitation customer base give it lower volatility than destination-dependent operators. Downtown's Hawaiian franchise, fed by a proprietary travel agency, is a defensible niche in a small pond — and the Hawaiian data (55% of occupied rooms, room nights +3.8%) confirms the channel is holding even as the segment declines on walk-in traffic. Owning nearly all of its real estate, with only four properties under the GLPI master lease, is a structural advantage over MGM, Caesars and Penn in a higher-for-longer rate environment, because rent escalators are a fixed cost that compounds against flat EBITDAR.

The trajectory, however, is mixed rather than improving. In Midwest & South, Boyd is gaining: 3–4% revenue growth with margin expansion in a segment representing 56% of segment EBITDAR is the best part of the business, and it is under-appreciated by investors who file Boyd as a Las Vegas name. In Las Vegas Locals, Boyd is losing relative ground to Red Rock, and the response is a multi-year capital program rather than a pricing or product advantage. In Online, Boyd's position was structurally weakened by the FanDuel transaction: the market access economics that produced $100M+ of near-costless EBITDAR have been replaced at materially lower rates, and the Design Works Studios acquisition (April 1, 2026, $53.3M total consideration, $32.1M to goodwill) is a sensible but small vertical-integration step that does not restore the lost rate structure.

Industry capital continues to flow toward destination assets — Penn's CEO said in August 2026 that Penn would consider the right Strip acquisition, and Wynn's $5.8B UAE resort opens in 2027. Boyd is going the other way, deploying into regional markets and locals renovation. Given where its growth actually is, that is the right allocation.

## Risk Assessment

**Sky River contract expiry (~August 2029).** Roughly $110M of annualized management fee revenue at near-100% incremental margin, about 8% of TTM Adjusted EBITDAR, from a seven-year term that appears to be the IGRA statutory maximum. This would be a step-function loss, not a fade. Renewal or a successor structure is plausible; nothing is disclosed. Unquantified in the risk factors I reviewed.

**Capital-intensity squeeze on the buyback.** The per-share engine requires roughly $600M annually against post-capex, post-dividend free cash flow of roughly $100–200M in 2026. Norfolk keeps capex elevated through 2027. The gap has been closed with the revolver, and continuing that raises leverage toward 2.3–2.5x. Covenant risk is nil; thesis risk is not.

**Impairment cushion.** Boyd recorded $128.4M of impairments in FY2025 ($10.5M FY2024, $107.8M FY2023), against carrying values of $958.0M goodwill and $1,475.0M intangibles at year-end 2025 — roughly 37% of the balance sheet. My recollection of the FY2025 10-K is that Midwest & South reporting-unit fair values did not significantly exceed carrying values; that recall is moderate-confidence and should be verified against the next 10-K. An impairment in the growing segment would be a genuine negative surprise.

**Norfolk development and structure.** A $750M resort opening late 2027, with Boyd consolidating Golden Eagle Consulting II LLC as a variable interest entity, funding its losses and receiving significant economic benefits, while explicitly not directing the activities of the Pamunkey Indian Tribe or its gaming authority. Boyd's precise economic and ownership percentage, and the terms of the June 1, 2026 membership-interest option exercise, are not disclosed in what I read. Concentrated single-project execution risk with an unquantified economic split.

**Consumer exposure.** Regional casino volumes track discretionary income for middle-income households. Midwest & South's continued growth is the counter-evidence and is the single most important number to watch each quarter. [unverified — macro claims about mortgage rates at a one-year high, July payrolls contracting, and a two-phase housing pattern are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Nevada persistence.** Both Nevada segments are declining. If destination softness at The Orleans is structural rather than cyclical, the 1H 2027 Orleans casino refresh extends the disruption window rather than closing it.

**Unverified governance transition.** Marianne Boyd Johnson's Form 4 title field moved from "Executive Chairman" (February 2026) to "Chairman" (May) to "Director" (June), and a February 24, 2026 8-K contained an Item 5.02. Form 4 title fields are frequently maintained inconsistently, and the underlying 8-Ks were not retrievable. Keith Smith was still identified as President and CEO on the July 23 release. Treat as unverified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 33.2% against 26.4% one-year realized — rich by 6.8 points, at the 67th percentile within the current chain — with a backwardated term structure (33.2% at 12 days versus 24.4% at 40 days and 26.8% at 131 days) and a ±5.5% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Backwardation two weeks after an earnings release is unusual and would normally suggest a discrete near-term event; the only identifiable candidate is the pending Sam's Town Shreveport sale closing in Q3. Two cautions temper the read: the chain is thin (128 contracts across four expirations) and the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.01 is almost certainly a data artifact rather than a positioning signal. The put/call skew is reported as call-heavy at every tenor, so there is no evidence of concentrated downside demand. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV fell from 36.1% to 33.2% while the price declined 4.9% — falling volatility on a falling price, consistent with orderly drift rather than stress. Short interest of 7.4% of float at 4.8 days to cover is moderate and not a squeeze setup in either direction. Net read: the options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis, near-term protection is expensive, and there is no signal that changes the fundamental assessment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($105, +25.9%)

Boyd trades at 6.3x EV/EBITDAR and 10.75x FY2027 earnings with 1.75x leverage and refinancing pre-solved — a valuation that assumes flat-to-down operating earnings continue indefinitely. Four dated items argue otherwise. Suncoast's renovation completes at the end of Q3, removing a ~$3M-per-quarter drag and, on management's account, delivering improved performance from Q4. Cadence Crossing begins contributing in Q3 and ramps into Q4. The online EBITDAR step-down completes by year-end 2026, so 2027 gets a clean comparison against a base of $35–40M rather than the FY2025 level. Midwest & South continues at 3–4% with margin expansion. Together that supports FY2027 EBITDAR of $1,370–1,400M, or 4–6% growth after two down years, on top of which the remaining $551.1M authorization retires up to 9.1% of shares. Re-rating to 7.5x EV/EBITDAR — still below where Red Rock trades — plus the share count reduction produces roughly $105. The mean sell-side target of $95.81 and a low target of $87 both sit above spot.

### Bear Case ($70, −16%)

The five-year record is the base rate: EBITDAR has not grown since 2021 and has fallen for two consecutive years, and every dollar of per-share improvement has come from a share count now shrinking on borrowed money. The D&A step-up (+34.7% in the half) shows the capital program is consuming earnings before generating them, which is exactly why consensus expects adjusted EPS down ~5% in 2026 despite 9% fewer shares. Estimates are falling in every window. Two of five segments are in EBITDAR decline against a competitor posting record results in the same market, which points to asset-quality gaps that renovation capex may narrow but will not close. Capex stays elevated through 2027, forcing either a moderated buyback or leverage drifting to 2.5x. Sky River removes roughly 8% of EBITDAR at near-100% margin around August 2029 with no disclosed renewal path. If the H2 consumer slowdown reaches regional gaming volumes, Midwest & South — the sole growth engine — stalls, and a 10x multiple on $7.00 of earnings puts the stock at $70.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has declined from 6.3 (April) to 5.0 (July) before ticking to 5.15 here. The uptick is deliberate and narrow: the Q2 print was in line on revenue and 1.4% ahead on adjusted EPS, the price is 4.9% lower than at the prior review, and the online drag now has a defined end date — so risk/reward improved marginally on unchanged-to-slightly-better fundamentals. Nothing in this quarter reverses the trend that drove the earlier downgrades: EBITDAR is still eroding, estimates are still falling, and the buyback is still debt-funded.

- **Base case: $92 (+10.3%).** 11.8x the $7.76 FY2027 consensus — today's multiple on the consensus recovery year. Cross-checked at 6.75x EV/EBITDAR on $1,380M FY2027 EBITDAR less ~$2,500M net debt across ~70M shares, which yields $97; the $92 anchor haircuts for the negative revision trend.
- **Bull case: $105 (+25.9%).** 7.5x on $1,400M EBITDAR with the buyback sustained and Las Vegas inflecting from Q4.
- **Bear case: $70 (−16.1%).** 10x on $7.00 FY2027 adjusted EPS with persistent Nevada softness and a moderated buyback.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 2026 print (late October — Cadence contribution, Suncoast completion) and Q4 2026 (February 2027 — the first quarter in which management's "improved performance" claim is testable).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDAR growing year over year for two consecutive quarters, with Las Vegas Locals EBITDAR flat or positive — the specific test of whether the renovation capex earns its depreciation.
2. FY2027 consensus stabilizing or rising (currently $7.76, revised −1.3% over 90 days) alongside a buyback pace maintained without further increase in net debt beyond roughly $2.6B.
3. A price near $74–76 (approximately 9.5x FY2027 consensus and the 52-week low of $76.33) with fundamentals unchanged, which would restore a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. Midwest & South EBITDAR growth turns negative for a quarter — the sole growth engine failing removes the entire recovery bridge.
2. Net debt exceeds roughly $2.9B (approximately 2.2x) with the buyback maintained, or the buyback is materially curtailed to defend leverage. Either outcome breaks the per-share mechanism.
3. FY2027 consensus is cut below $7.30, or a goodwill/intangible impairment is recorded in Midwest & South.

## Conclusion

Boyd Gaming is a soundly financed operator whose operating earnings have gone sideways for five years and modestly down for two, with per-share results carried by a 31% reduction in diluted shares that now requires incremental borrowing to sustain. The balance sheet is genuinely strong — 1.75x leverage, $1.9B of committed liquidity, the 2027 maturity pre-funded — and the growth engine, Midwest & South at 56% of segment EBITDAR, is compounding at 3–4% with margin expansion. Against that, the two Nevada segments are declining while the closest comparable posts record results, the Online step-down has permanently removed roughly 5% of company EBITDAR, capex stays elevated through 2027, and a management contract worth roughly 8% of EBITDAR at near-100% margin appears to run out in 2029 with no disclosed successor.

At $83.42 the stock is 11.8x a declining 2026 and 10.75x a recovering 2027, at 6.3x EV/EBITDAR. That is inexpensive in absolute terms and cheap against Red Rock at 19.9x, but the discount reflects a real earnings trajectory rather than an oversight, and the falling estimate revisions in every window argue against treating the sell-side's above-spot targets as confirmation. Base case $92 (+10%) against a $70 bear (−16%), with a 0.96% dividend and no margin of safety, is approximately fair compensation for the risk. Existing holders should stay, monitor the Q3 and Q4 prints for the Las Vegas inflection management has promised, and watch net debt as the tell on whether the buyback engine keeps running. New capital has no urgent reason to enter here; $74–76 with fundamentals intact would.
