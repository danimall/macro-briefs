**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vishay Intertechnology is one of the world's largest manufacturers of discrete semiconductors (MOSFETs, diodes, optoelectronics) and passive components (resistors, inductors, capacitors). After a two-year cyclical downturn that took operating margin from 17.6% in 2022 to 0.2% in 2024 and pushed the company to a small net loss in both 2024 and 2025, the business is inflecting. Q1 2026 delivered $839M revenue (+17.3% YoY, above the $800–830M guide), gross margin recovered to 21.0%, GAAP EPS turned positive at $0.05, and book-to-bill jumped to 1.34 with backlog at 5.7 months. Consensus estimates have risen sharply — current-year EPS revised +46.8% and next-year +53.1% over 90 days. The cyclical turn is genuine and broad-based across the components complex.

The problem is price. VSH has risen roughly 300% year-to-date, from the mid-teens in early January to the low-$60s, sweeping into the broad semiconductor rally. The income statement has produced about $0.05 of quarterly GAAP EPS against a stock that now trades near $60. On next-year consensus of $1.54, that is roughly 39x forward; on this year's $0.75 it is about 80x. The market has priced a full multi-year recovery — margins reverting toward the 2022 peak of 30% gross / 17% operating — that the company is only beginning to deliver and that depends heavily on the MOSFETs segment (–4.8% operating margin in 2025) and the Newport/Itzehoe fab ramps turning profitable. Those are the highest-execution-risk parts of the portfolio.

We rate VSH AVOID. The fundamental thesis is improving but the risk/reward at the current price is unfavorable: the company is running negative free cash flow through a large capacity build, U.S.-parent liquidity is structurally tight, governance is shareholder-unfriendly (family super-voting control, pay rising in a loss year), and two governance flags surfaced in early 2026 — an auditor change of unconfirmed cause and two executive departures. The score trajectory (4.9 → 4.1, AVOID reiterated three times) reflects a widening gap between price and fundamentals; the Q1 beat confirms the operational turn but does nothing to close the valuation gap. We rate this AVOID on valuation and risk rather than as a short; the bookings momentum and 11.3% short interest make active shorting unattractive.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1962 and headquartered in Malvern, PA, Vishay operates six reporting segments across three semiconductor lines (MOSFETs, Diodes, Optoelectronics) and three passive lines (Resistors, Inductors, Capacitors), employing roughly 22,600 people. About 76% of revenue comes from outside the U.S. End markets span automotive, industrial, computing, telecom, military/aerospace, healthcare, and consumer, sold through OEMs, EMS providers, and distributors. The top 30 customers represent about 74% of revenue with no single customer above 10%, though individual product lines carry higher concentrations.

The product mix in 2025 was roughly 26% commodity, 48% certified, and 26% custom, with only about 25% of revenue from products developed in the last five years, a modest innovation cadence consistent with a legacy components franchise. Vishay holds genuine leadership in select niches: wirewound power shunts, thin-film SMD resistors, IHLP power inductors, wet/polymer tantalum capacitors, power rectifiers, and low-voltage Siliconix MOSFETs. Its strategic stance is to avoid commodity volume leadership and instead pursue higher-margin certified/custom products and niche acquisitions, positioning as the "independent second source" amid industry consolidation.

The "Vishay 3.0" program is a multi-year capacity-investment effort layered on this base: the acquired Newport (UK) 200mm wafer fab for SiC MOSFETs/diodes, a new 12-inch fab in Itzehoe, Germany, and expansions in Mexico, Taiwan, and Italy. The bet is on higher-growth power semiconductors tied to EV, factory automation, AI/data-center power, 5G, and smart-grid demand.

## Financial Analysis

The annual trajectory shows the depth of the trough and the early turn:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.50B | $3.40B | $2.94B | $3.07B (+4.5%) |
| Gross margin | 30.3% | 28.6% | 21.3% | 19.4% |
| Operating margin | 17.6% | 14.3% | 0.2% | 1.9% |
| Net income | $429M | $324M | –$31M | –$9M |
| Adj. dil. EPS | — | $2.44 | $0.48 | –$0.05 |

The quarterly cadence is more encouraging. Operating margin moved from –7.9% in Q4'24 to positive territory across 2025. Q2 2026 guidance of $875–905M revenue at ~22% gross margin implies sequential acceleration. With incremental operating income of roughly $450–470K per $1M of revenue change, the operating leverage in a sustained upturn is substantial.

The balance-sheet picture warrants caution. Operating cash flow was $184M in 2025, but capex of $273M produced free cash of –$88M, following –$143M in 2024 — three consecutive years of negative-to-marginal FCF spent through the trough. 2026 capex is guided to $400–440M (≥70% on capacity), implying continued burn; Q1'26 FCF was already –$47M. Net debt deteriorated to $436M at YE2025 from $299M a year earlier, with total debt of $951M including $750M of 2.25% convertible notes due 2030 plus ~$219M of revolver draws. No principal is due until 2028, and the company is covenant-compliant (net leverage 2.42x against a 3.25x limit, interest coverage 9.2x, with ~$254M of revolver capacity).

A structural concern sits under the headline ~$515M cash balance: only about $13.4M sits at the U.S. parent, with nearly all cash offshore. Dividends, buybacks, and debt service must be funded at the parent level, which is in a net-borrowing position expected to persist through 2026. The new $150M receivables-sale facility and reliance on the revolver are workarounds rather than strengths. Altman Z of 2.37 sits in the grey zone and Piotroski is 3/5, neither distressed nor robust. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses over the last four quarters), which tempers confidence in the precision of forward consensus even as the direction is clearly up.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is the strongest part of the story. Estimate revisions are running well above the threshold that typically precedes continued upward consensus migration, with current-quarter and next-quarter estimates up +15.9% and +28.2%. Revenue growth in Q1, the book-to-bill of 1.34 (1.47 semis, 1.23 passives), and rising backlog all point to a sector-wide restocking cycle turning up after the 2024–25 inventory correction. This is a sector recovery rather than a company-specific one: peers across the components and discrete-semi complex (TI, onsemi, STMicro, Microchip) saw similar pressure and are recovering in parallel, which raises the durability of the demand signal relative to a single-company story.

Two things temper how much weight to put on this. First, the revision velocity is operating off a trough base: next-year consensus of $1.54 is still below the $2.44 adjusted EPS Vishay earned in 2023 and far below the 2022 peak. The estimates are recovering, not breaking out to new highs. Second, the growth thesis is concentrated in the weakest, highest-risk part of the portfolio. The passive segments (Inductors 23–26%, Capacitors ~16%, Resistors ~16% operating margins) are healthy and stable. The entire margin-recovery narrative rests on MOSFETs reverting from a –4.8% segment operating margin (gross margin collapsed from 33.3% in 2023 to 9.8% in 2025 on price erosion plus Newport startup costs) and on the Newport SiC fab and Itzehoe 12-inch fab ramping profitably. These carry construction, yield/qualification, and staffing risk, with roughly $239M still to spend on the MOSFETs fab.

The secular framing — EV power, factory automation, AI/data-center power, smart grid — is real and supports a structurally higher-margin mix over time. But only ~25% of revenue comes from products developed in the last five years, so the secular re-rating is gradual rather than abrupt. The growth prospects are genuinely good for a legacy franchise emerging from a trough, which justifies a score above the midpoint, but the inflection is cyclical-plus-execution rather than a durable secular acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the dimension that drives the recommendation. At roughly $60 on ~136M shares, VSH carries an equity value near $8.0B and an enterprise value north of $8.4B with net debt. Even crediting the cycle-adjusted approach fully — valuing the stock on next-year recovering estimates rather than trough trailing earnings — the multiple is rich. A ~39x forward P/E for a business whose next-year EPS is still below its 2023 level, and well below the 2022 peak, prices in not just a full recovery to mid-cycle but a sustained run beyond it.

The peer set is heterogeneous and not especially clarifying: Belden at 18.9x, Knowles at 55.4x, Corning at 85.0x, with Littelfuse and Rogers showing negative trailing P/E on their own trough earnings (median 55.4x). These multiples are themselves distorted by where each peer sits in the cycle. The more useful anchor is Vishay's own normalized earnings power. If margins revert toward the 2023 level (~$2.44 adjusted EPS) over a multi-year horizon, the stock at $60 trades around 25x peak-ish earnings — still full for a company that has demonstrated it cannot hold those margins through a downturn and is adding fixed costs into the recovery. To reach the 2022 peak requires MOSFET and Newport execution that is far from guaranteed.

The sell-side data underscores the disconnect: analyst targets cluster at a low of $28, high of $40, mean and median of $34 — roughly 35–45% below the current price. On a high-momentum inflection stock one would normally treat consensus targets as lagging floors. That principle does not apply here. These targets sit far below the price, not at it, which means the market has run well past where analysts who model the fundamentals see fair value. With the stock at ~39x forward, the asymmetry has inverted: a beat is largely priced, while any sign the upcycle is stalling, a MOSFET margin disappointment, or a Newport ramp delay would derate the multiple sharply. We score valuation 3.0 — a stretched multiple on recovering-but-not-exceptional earnings, with the burden of proof firmly on the bull case given the ~300% YTD move.

## Competitive Landscape

Vishay's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving but not a source of pricing power that would justify the current multiple. The breadth of its portfolio — components that can populate more than 80% of a typical board — and its leadership in niche passives (specialty resistors, power inductors, tantalum capacitors) constitute a real moat in the passive franchise, where it competes against Murata, TDK-EPCOS, Yageo, Panasonic, Bourns, and KOA. The passives segments are the durable profit engine, with specialty resistors and capacitors showing more price resilience than commodity lines.

On the semiconductor side the competitive picture is weaker. Vishay competes against larger, better-capitalized players — Infineon, onsemi, STMicro, Nexperia, Renesas, Toshiba — in markets (low-voltage MOSFETs, power rectifiers) prone to ASP erosion. The MOSFETs gross-margin collapse to 9.8% in 2025 reflects this pricing pressure plus startup drag. The Newport SiC bet is an attempt to move up the value chain into higher-growth power semiconductors, but it pits Vishay against incumbents with deeper SiC roadmaps. The "independent second source" positioning has merit in a consolidating industry but is a defensive rationale, not a share-gain story.

Net, this is a market leader in some niches holding share, with a turnaround bet in a competitively disadvantaged segment. Trajectory is roughly stable, justifying a score near the midpoint rather than above it.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the second driver of the AVOID, and several items are not reflected in a stock that has tripled.

**Cyclicality and capacity timing.** The defining risk is a heavy fixed-cost base with capacity being added in the upturn. If the recovery stalls, that capacity becomes excess in the next downturn — the company flags this explicitly. The run-up leaves the equity acutely exposed to any disappointment in the demand or margin trajectory.

**Execution.** The thesis rests on MOSFETs recovering from negative margins and the Newport and Itzehoe fabs ramping profitably, with ~$239M still to spend and disclosed risks around construction delays, cost overruns, and yield/qualification.

**Liquidity structure.** The U.S. parent holds only ~$13.4M of cash and is in a net-borrowing position through 2026, funding the dividend and debt service via the revolver and a new receivables-sale facility. The $750M convertible notes due 2030 would, on conversion, require cash settlement of principal — a potential liquidity strain. Capped calls partially offset dilution.

**Governance.** Family super-voting control via Class B shares (Ruta Zandman controls ~89.7% of Class B / ~44.4% of total voting power), a classified board, and blank-check preferred form a set of takeover defenses that leave minority holders bearing cyclical risk without takeover-premium optionality. The pay-vs-performance package is a yellow flag: CEO total comp rose to $10.3M in 2025 from $5.5M in 2024 in a loss-making year, while five-year TSR of 77.3 lagged the peer-group's 268.2.

**Unresolved flags.** A January 2026 auditor change (8-K Item 4.01) of unconfirmed cause, and executive-departure 8-Ks in February and May 2026, both warrant confirmation. An auditor change concurrent with leadership turnover during a sharp run-up is the kind of cluster that merits caution until clarified.

**Goodwill.** $180M remains after a $66.5M impairment in Q4'24; a stalled recovery or share decline could trigger more.

These are largely company-specific and binary or asymmetric, not generic sector risk, which supports a low risk score of 3.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 126.7% is rich against 1-year historical volatility of 56.1% (a 70.6-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-month 126.7% > 6-month 119.8%), indicating the market expects imminent volatility. Put/call skew is elevated at 127% with put-heavy near-term volume (1-month P/C volume ratio 1.53) and a steepening 6-month put skew of –11.7%, consistent with demand for downside protection. The implied 1-month move is sizable (±28.2%). ATM IV rose from 200% to 218% even as the price climbed 88% since the prior snapshot, which typically signals the market pricing a catalyst or building two-sided uncertainty rather than complacency. Short interest of 11.3% of float with only 2.0 days to cover means bearish positioning exists but squeeze fuel is limited. The options configuration corroborates the fundamental read that risk is skewed to the downside from here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The components inventory correction is over and a multi-year upcycle is underway. Book-to-bill at 1.34, backlog at 5.7 months, and broad-based peer recovery confirm durable demand. Vishay's ~46% contributive margin means revenue growth flows powerfully to the bottom line; as MOSFETs recover and Newport/Itzehoe ramp, gross margin reverts from 21% toward the high-20s, and EPS rebuilds toward the 2023 level ($2.44) and beyond. Secular tailwinds in EV power, AI/data-center power, and automation lift the long-term margin mix. On normalized peak earnings the stock is reasonable, and rapidly rising estimates (+53% next-year over 90 days) pull consensus and the price higher. Bull case ~$75 (roughly 48x recovering current-year, or ~30x a $2.50 normalized EPS).

### Bear Case
The recovery is a cyclical restock that decelerates by late 2026, leaving Vishay with added depreciation and fixed costs into a softer market and more net debt. MOSFET margins fail to recover meaningfully, and Newport's ramp slips or comes in below yield targets. U.S.-parent liquidity tightens, forcing harder choices on the dividend or further revolver reliance. The multiple compresses from ~39x forward toward something more defensible for a cyclical components maker. Analyst fair value clusters at $28–$40; a derate toward the high-$30s would be a 35–40% decline. Bear case ~$32, roughly the mean analyst target and ~21x next-year EPS.

The asymmetry favors the bear: the bull requires both sustained demand and successful execution of the hardest part of the portfolio, while the bear requires only that one of those falter.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no clean, dated negative catalyst. Q2 2026 guidance implies sequential improvement, and bookings momentum is positive. The next earnings report (likely August 2026) is more likely to confirm the upcycle than break it in the near term. Stretched valuation alone is not a timed catalyst. The unresolved auditor change and executive departures are potential negative catalysts only if they reveal a disagreement or material weakness, which is currently unconfirmed and speculative.

**Timing window.** No catalyst resolves on a definable near-term schedule. A multiple derate would more likely come from a demand-deceleration signal one or two quarters out, beyond a comfortable carry window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 11.3% of float with only 2.0 days to cover — elevated but not extreme, with limited squeeze fuel. The thesis is moderately crowded, reducing remaining downside available to new shorts.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms a bearish near-term lean (127% skew, backwardation, put-heavy volume), but near-term ATM IV of 126.7% makes put-based downside expression expensive, and rich IV raises the carrying cost of any short. The market is already pricing significant downside.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A derate toward the mid-$30s analyst zone is ~40% downside, against meaningful upside risk from continued bookings beats and the broad semiconductor rally (the stock has shown the capacity for violent up-moves, +88% since mid-May). With positive operational momentum, no dated catalyst, and rich IV raising carry, the reward:risk does not clear the 2:1 bar with confidence.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The stock is overvalued and risk-skewed to the downside, but the fundamental momentum is positive, there is no timed negative catalyst, and the cost of carry (rich IV) plus continued upcycle risk make active shorting unattractive. We rate this AVOID — do not own at this price — rather than a short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
- A valuation reset to roughly 20–25x next-year EPS — approximately the high-$30s to mid-$40s on the current $1.54 estimate — would bring the price into line with a cyclical components recovery and create a reasonable entry. That is roughly 30–40% below the current price.
- Confirmation that MOSFET segment operating margin has turned durably positive (two consecutive quarters above ~5%) and that Newport is profitable, de-risking the execution leg of the thesis.
- Clarification that the January 2026 auditor change was a routine rotation with no reportable disagreements or material weaknesses, and that the February/May executive departures do not disrupt the core Vishay 3.0 leadership team.
- Evidence that U.S.-parent liquidity is no longer reliant on the revolver and receivables facility — i.e., the parent moves out of a net-borrowing position.

**Timeline.** The capacity build runs through 2026 with continued FCF burn, so the structural concerns persist for at least 12–18 months. The valuation gap could close either through a price correction or through several quarters of margin delivery that the fundamentals grow into. A re-rating opportunity is more plausible on a pullback than on continued fundamental improvement, given how far the price already leads the income statement.

**What would change the view to constructive.** A pullback of 30%+ on unchanged or improving fundamentals, combined with MOSFET margin confirmation, would shift this toward a HOLD or BUY. We would not chase the stock at current levels regardless of bookings strength.

## Conclusion

Vishay is a real cyclical recovery story attached to a stock price that has run far ahead of it. The operational turn is confirmed — Q1 2026 revenue +17.3%, return to GAAP profitability, book-to-bill 1.34, and estimate revisions up ~50% over 90 days are all genuinely positive, and the broad-based components restock raises the durability of the demand signal. Were the stock in the $35–$45 range, the inflection plus rising estimates would make a constructive case.

At ~$60, roughly 300% above where it started the year and ~39x next-year consensus that is still below 2023 earnings, the market has priced a full multi-year recovery plus successful execution of the riskiest part of the portfolio (MOSFETs, Newport, Itzehoe). Layered on top are three consecutive years of negative FCF through an aggressive capex build, structurally tight U.S.-parent liquidity, family super-voting control, a pay package that rose in a loss year, and unresolved auditor and executive-departure flags. The options market is pricing imminent stress (126.7% IV in backwardation, 127% put skew), and analyst targets sit 35–45% below the current price. The score trajectory (4.9 → 4.1, AVOID reiterated) captures a widening gap between price and value; the Q1 beat confirms the operations but does not close that gap. We maintain AVOID. We do not view it as a short — bookings momentum and moderate short interest make active shorting unattractive — but it is not a stock to own at this price.
