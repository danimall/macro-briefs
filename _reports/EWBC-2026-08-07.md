## Executive Summary

East West Bancorp is among the most operationally efficient banks in the US regional universe, and the market knows it. A 36.7% efficiency ratio, operating noninterest expense of 1.29% of average assets, 1.79% ROA and roughly 17% return on tangible common equity are top-decile or top-quartile metrics on every axis, delivered alongside a 15.4% CET1 ratio and 10.4% tangible common equity ratio that most peers cannot match. The franchise — a California-anchored commercial bank whose deposit base and customer relationships are rooted in Chinese-American and pan-Asian business communities — is genuinely defensible, with 700,000+ customer accounts, 26% demand deposits and interest-bearing deposit costs that management has held below aggressive competitors without visible outflow.

The question is price, not quality. At $132.21 the stock trades at 12.4x the $10.64 2026 consensus and 11.7x the $11.35 2027 consensus, against a regional-bank peer median of 12.6x. On book value the premium is explicit: ~2.12x the March 31 tangible book of $62.27 versus peers generally at a lower multiple. Solved through a Gordon-growth frame at 17% ROTCE and 6% growth, the observed multiple discounts EWBC at roughly an 11.2% cost of equity — a real but modest risk premium for the California, CRE, geopolitical and non-depository-financial-institution concentrations. That is a coherent price, not a wrong one. The stock has advanced 33.9% over twelve months while EPS compounds at roughly 12%; multiple expansion has consumed the discount that supported the April 2026 BUY.

Three specific items make the next two quarters two-sided rather than clearly favorable. First, $13 billion of CDs — over half the $25.4B time-deposit book — reprices in Q3 2026 at a spread that has flattened to roughly neutral. Second, roughly a quarter of Q1's EPS growth came from a 400bp decline in the effective tax rate, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act is narrowing the renewable-credit pipeline behind it. Third, classified loans rose 14.7% sequentially in Q1 and net charge-offs doubled to 19bp in Q2 while the allowance ratio drifted down a basis point. None of these is a thesis-breaker; together they justify waiting rather than adding. **HOLD**, with a base case of $145 and an accumulation zone in the mid-$110s.

## Company Overview

East West Bank is an $82.9 billion-asset (3/31/26) commercial bank holding company headquartered in Pasadena, with over 110 locations in the US and Asia and roughly 3,400 employees. It reports three segments: Consumer & Business Banking, Commercial Banking, and Treasury & Other.

The "cross-border" label invites a misreading worth correcting. Direct Asia-domiciled exposure is small: the Hong Kong branch held $2.735B of assets (3.3% of consolidated) generating $21.2M of revenue, and East West Bank (China) Limited held $2.063B (2.5%) generating $6.1M — down from roughly $7.75M a year earlier. Total international branch deposits were $3.906B. Direct Asian assets are approximately 6% of the balance sheet and shrinking on the mainland side. Geopolitical risk therefore transmits through *customer* economics — tariffs on clients' import/export businesses, immigration policy, depositor sentiment — rather than through marks on Chinese assets. This matters for scenario construction because it means a US-China escalation shows up in credit costs and deposit behavior with a lag, not in an immediate securities or FX writedown.

Loan mix at 3/31/26 on $58.10B of held-for-investment loans: C&I $19.55B (34%); CRE-related $21.43B (37%, comprising CRE $15.49B, multifamily $5.13B, construction $0.81B); residential $17.07B (29%, comprising single-family $15.12B and HELOC $1.95B). Management's stated target is "a third, a third, a third," to be reached by growing C&I and residential faster rather than shrinking CRE — the CFO was explicit that there is "no intent for us to shrink those portfolios."

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** Net income progressed $1,128M (2022) → $1,161M (2023) → $1,166M (2024) → $1,325M (2025), with FY2025 diluted EPS of $9.52. Q1 2026 delivered net income of $357.8M (+23.2% YoY) and diluted EPS of $2.57 (+23.6%) on net interest income of $671.2M (+11.8%), NIM of 3.49% (+14bp), noninterest income of $102.6M (+11%), noninterest expense of $280.3M (+11.2%) and provision of $36.0M versus $49.0M. Q2 2026, reported at the July 21 release and earnings call but not yet verifiable against a filed 10-Q, showed net income of $363.7M, EPS of $2.63, record NII of $685M, NIM of 3.43%, fee income of $96M and provision of $33M.

**The tax line deserves top billing.** The effective rate fell from 25.8% in Q1 2025 to 21.8% in Q1 2026 on tax credits and benefits of $65.5M versus $49.3M. Reproducing the arithmetic: Q1 2026 pretax income of $457.4M taxed at the prior-year 25.8% rate yields $339.4M of net income, or about $2.44 per diluted share on ~139.2M shares, versus the $2.57 reported. Offsetting this within pretax expense, amortization of tax-credit and CRA investments rose from $15.7M to $22.0M (~$4.9M after tax). Net contribution: roughly $13M of net income, or $0.09-0.10 per share — approximately a fifth to a quarter of the $0.49 year-over-year EPS gain. The FY2025 10-K added a risk factor stating that OBBBA's accelerated phase-out of renewable energy credits could require a shift in the company's tax-credit investment strategy. A reversion toward a 24-26% effective rate would cost roughly $0.50-0.70 of annual EPS at current pretax run rates (3-4pp on ~$1.8B annualized pretax income ÷ 138M shares). The company has not guided to a specific rate, and substitution into affordable-housing, CRA or historic-rehabilitation credits could preserve it. Treat this as a live scenario, not a forecast.

**Net interest margin and the Q3 funding event.** NIM ran 3.35% → 3.49% → 3.43% across the last five quarters, with the Q2 sequential dip attributed to day count. The engine has been deposit repricing: interest-bearing deposit costs fell from 3.34% (Q1 2025) to 2.84% (Q1 2026) to a 2.76% spot rate at 6/30/26 — roughly 49bp of relief against 75bp of Fed cuts, an implied realized downward beta near 65%, better than the ~50% assumption in the 10-Q's sensitivity modeling.

That tailwind is spent. The CFO disclosed $13 billion of CD roll-off in Q3 2026, consistent with a $25.4B time-deposit book (37% of deposits) structured predominantly at six-month maturities. Prior 3.40% specials have already rolled into 3.60-3.68%; current offers are 3.60% (6-month) and 3.75% (12-month), and management is "priced for retention, not necessarily for CD balance expansion" while conceding that with no Fed hike, "our need to be competitive on deposit pricing might step up a tad." As a labeled sensitivity: each 15bp of upward repricing on $13B costs ~$19.5M pretax annualized, roughly $0.11 per share after tax; 30bp costs ~$0.22. Against a raised NII growth guide of 7-9%, the offset management is relying on is continued demand-deposit mix improvement, which they have delivered for four consecutive quarters.

Rate sensitivity reconciles across two disclosures that look contradictory. The Q1 10-Q shows a +200bp *ramp* worth +3.3% of NII and economic value of equity at -13.8%; the CFO described a 25bp move as "about $2 million a month with about a 45-day lag" (~$24M annually per 25bp, ~7% of annualized NII for 200bp fully phased). The ramp captures roughly half the fully-phased effect over twelve months. Net read: modestly asset-sensitive, with a flat-funds base case embedded in guidance.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Assets grew $76.0B → $80.4B → $82.9B; loans HFI +2.2% and deposits +2.7% sequentially in Q1. Loan-to-deposit ratio 84%. Total available liquidity of $42.1B covers $30.75B of uninsured deposits (excluding collateralized and affiliate) at 137%. Uninsured share of domestic deposits rose from 45% to 47% during Q1 — worth monitoring, not alarming at that coverage.

**Securities marks.** At 3/31/26, AFS carried $507.2M of gross unrealized loss already in AOCI; HTM carried $406M of *unrecognized* loss that appears nowhere in equity. Against tangible common equity of roughly $8.53B (137.0M × $62.27), the HTM gap is ~4.8% of TCE, and the combined AFS+HTM mark would be ~10.7%. Against 15.4% CET1 this is manageable, but a screen reading reported equity understates the economic haircut.

**Expenses and operating leverage.** Management headlined "$268 million" of total operating noninterest expense for Q2. Q1 GAAP expense was $280.3M and reconciles exactly to the reported 36.23% efficiency ratio over computed revenue of $773.8M. Applying the same basis to Q2's 36.7% on ~$781M of revenue implies GAAP expense nearer $287M — up sequentially, not down. The gap is presumably the tax-credit/CRA amortization that also lowers the tax rate; the two effects should be assessed together. This is a disclosure-comparability issue rather than a red flag, and the Q2 reconciliation tables were not obtainable. The substantive point: expense growth guidance of 8-9% sits at or above blended revenue growth of 7.5-9.5%, so operating leverage is roughly neutral. The record-earnings cadence comes from balance-sheet growth and the tax rate, not widening margins.

**Tangible book.** TBVPS went $61.27 (12/31/25) → $62.27 (3/31/26), +$1.00. Reconciling: $2.57 of EPS less $0.80 of dividend is $1.77 of retention; AOCI deteriorated $42.6M (~$0.31/share); expected build ~$1.46 against $1.00 actual, leaving ~$0.46 of residual. The most plausible explanation is repurchase above tangible book, which is mechanically TBVPS-dilutive at 2.1x. Share issuance from the March 1 RSU vest and intangibles/DTA movements are not excluded, and the Q1 repurchase dollar amount was not obtained — so the attribution is inference. The decision-relevant conclusion holds regardless: buying stock at 2.1x tangible book destroys tangible book per share, and management's decision to rank buybacks below M&A is defensible on that arithmetic. In Q2, $117M of authorization remained unchanged from 3/31, implying essentially no repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance was raised twice in 2026: full-year loan growth to 6-8% (from 5-7%), NII growth to 7-9% (from 6-8%), with expense growth narrowed to 8-9%. Q2 growth was led by residential (+$300M+) and C&I (+$300M+), the latter up 11% year over year.

Consensus translates this into $10.64 of 2026 EPS (+11.8% over FY2025's $9.52) and $11.35 in 2027 (+6.7%). Fifteen analysts have moved estimates essentially nowhere: current quarter +0.1% over 30 days and -0.3% over 90 days; current year +0.1% / -0.1%; next year +0.5% / +0.4%. There is no revision-velocity signal here in either direction — this is not an inflection stock, and the estimate-momentum case for a re-rating is absent.

Two growth qualifiers matter. First, the headline 19% year-over-year growth in noninterest-bearing deposits is flattered by $200-250M of transitory tariff-refund inflows that the CFO conceded "most of it has already gone back," and he agreed that average balances (+15%) are the right modeling base. The underlying driver — a door-to-door small-business checking acquisition campaign rather than a pricing action — appears durable and has produced four consecutive quarters of mix improvement.

Second, the fee-diversification story is real in growth rate and small in contribution. Wealth management fees rose 63% year over year in Q1 to $22.3M and 71% for the first half. Total fees of $198.6M against H1 revenue of roughly $1,554.8M is 12.8%; wealth is 2.5-3% of revenue. EWBC remains ~87% spread-dependent, and the CFO was candid that he is not "calling for a sustained 70% year over year growth." A shifting revenue mix at this scale does not reduce sensitivity to rates, credit, or the California/Asian-American customer concentration.

The honest growth profile is high-single to low-double-digit earnings growth in 2026, decelerating toward mid-single digits in 2027 as the tax and deposit-repricing levers exhaust, with balance-sheet growth doing most of the work.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is a deposit franchise, not a lending advantage. Cost of interest-bearing deposits at 2.76% spot with 26% demand deposits, held below the most aggressive competitors, is the source of the 36.7% efficiency ratio and the 17% ROTCE. Management's framing of the niche cuts both ways: "it's a relatively small universe and we know all the players and all the players know us" is simultaneously a statement of limited competition for the Chinese-American commercial banking relationship and an acknowledgment of a bounded addressable market with few acquisition targets.

The strategic answer to that constraint is wealth management, where the bank made a 2023 investment in an outside asset manager (reconciling to 349,000 performance-based RSUs carried as a $13.0M liability award) and where stated M&A appetite is explicitly for "a wealth platform or a wealth-oriented banking organization."

Two competitive pressures are worth tracking. Deposit competition is intensifying and management is choosing not to match, betting on relationship stickiness — the $13B Q3 CD roll is the natural test of whether that is pricing power or unrealized outflow risk. And the FY2025 10-K newly framed stablecoins as competition, noting the GENIUS Act's potential to accelerate adoption; for a franchise built partly on cross-border payments and FX for Asian trade corridors, dollar-stablecoin rails are a more pointed threat than for a generic domestic regional. That inference follows from the franchise description rather than from a company statement.

On the asset side, management notes peers are now imitating the residential-mortgage tilt for risk-weight reasons — evidence the strategy was correct and that the differentiation is eroding.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit: levels excellent, direction one-directional.** Management called nonperforming assets, criticized loans and charge-offs "broadly stable," which is defensible on absolute levels and uneasy against the Q1 10-Q's direction of travel. Nonaccruals rose from $165.8M (0.29%) to $180.6M (0.31%); criticized from $1,141.1M to $1,229.6M; classified from $796.3M (1.40%) to $913.4M (1.57%), a 14.7% increase. Special mention *fell* 8.2% while classified rose — credits migrating through the watch category rather than accumulating in it, a pattern more consistent with genuine downgrades than conservative early flagging. CRE accruing past due rose 223% to $45.2M; multifamily accruing past due 308% to $5.3M; HELOC nonaccrual 68.6% to $29.0M. Newly modified loans of $149.1M versus $63.8M a year earlier represent a 134% increase in the flow, and $51.7M of the trailing-twelve-month modified population is 90+ days past due (11.6% re-default). In Q2, charge-offs doubled sequentially to $27M (19bp) while the allowance ratio slipped from 1.44% to 1.43%.

Full-year charge-off guidance of 15-25bp was *reaffirmed* despite the first half running near 14bp annualized, implying management expects second-half charge-offs of roughly 16-36bp. That is a quiet caution inside an upbeat call. The CRO's explanations were specific and testable: C&I downgrades came from identified cash-flow reductions in scheduled financial-statement reviews, partly offset by upgrades out of substandard; four CRE credits moved to nonaccrual with "some resolved in the quarter or subsequent" and limited expected loss content. At 0.31% nonaccruals this is normalization off an extraordinarily benign base, not stress — but it wants two more quarters of monitoring before it can be dismissed.

One embedded segment detail: the single-family "Bridge to Home Ownership" sub-product carries a 2.70% allowance rate against 0.14% on traditional SFR — roughly 19x — on an implied ~$1.39B balance. A riskier residential sleeve sits inside the "conservative 52% LTV" narrative.

**NDFI concentration is the largest under-disclosed exposure.** The FY2025 10-K added a standalone risk factor for lending to non-depository financial institutions to fund their lending activities; banks do not write dedicated risk factors for immaterial exposures. Management's Q2 commentary establishes materiality and composition without a verifiable balance: NDFI moved just $24M in Q2 "reflecting expected paydowns in our private equity loan book and consumer credit portfolios," Q1 saw "a very significant uptick in private equity capital call line activity," and NDFI "in some cases can be yieldier" than core C&I. The dollar figure is a single-source, uncorroborated reading of the 10-K MD&A of roughly $7.6B (prior year ~$6.0B), which could not be confirmed across three retrieval attempts. If the magnitude is in that range it would be roughly 13% of loans and 39% of the C&I book. **Treat the existence, materiality, above-core yield and two-sleeve composition as established; treat the dollar amount and derived percentages as unverified.**

The composition is the crux. Private equity capital-call and subscription lines secured by institutional LP commitments are historically very low-loss. "Consumer credit portfolios" implies warehouse lending to non-bank consumer lenders — materially more cyclical, and characteristically showing zero deterioration until the underlying lender's book breaks, at which point losses arrive quickly and correlated. A portfolio yielding above core C&I with near-zero realized losses is either well-structured or not yet tested; available disclosure does not distinguish these.

**Regulatory.** The Q1 10-Q discloses that March 2026 proposed revisions to the US capital framework modify risk-based treatment of exposure categories "material to the Company" — the company's own word, unquantified, and two-sided given heavy residential and CRE weightings. At $82.9B growing 6-8%, EWBC crosses $100B and into Category IV / Large Financial Institution requirements in roughly three years; asked directly about LFI expense, the CFO provided no quantification for a known, dated cost step-up. A Deutsche Bank analyst restated prior guidance of a 160-180bp Basel-related capital uptick versus ~100bp for peers; the CFO neither confirmed nor denied, so treat that figure as an analyst recollection.

**Other.** California concentration with explicit wildfire and earthquake risk factors; $2.2B of media and entertainment C&I exposure in a structurally pressured sector; $503M of art finance; oil and gas customer derivative notionals of 19M barrels and 280M MMBTUs; a two-thirds supermajority requirement for business combinations that entrenches management and reduces takeover-premium probability; and key-man risk in a founder-CEO who has led the bank since the early 1990s and holds the Chairman, President and CEO titles simultaneously with no independent chair identified.

Two named verification gaps carry forward: the contents of March 3, 2026 amendments to both Ng's and Vice Chairman Krause's employment agreements (the succession-planning reading is an inference from timing, not a reading of the documents), and $99.0M of Q1 premises and equipment purchases versus $2.5M a year earlier — roughly $0.72 per share of tangible book into fixed assets, unexplained in any filing reviewed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 20.4% sits below 25.3% trailing realized volatility, in the 4th percentile of the current chain, having collapsed from 30.2% pre-earnings. The term structure is in contango (20.4% at 13 days, 20.6% at 41 days, 24.2% at 104 days) with put/call open interest at 0.31 and put volume ratio at 0.08 — call-tilted, complacent positioning. The 13-day tenor shows elevated put demand on skew (21.5%) but on negligible volume, while the 41-day and 104-day tenors show flat skew.

The takeaway is a divergence rather than confirmation: the $13B CD roll-off is a Q3 event, and the option market is pricing 20.4% vol into the nearest expiry with essentially no put activity. For a holder concerned about funding-cost transmission, the six-month tenor at 24.2% with flat skew is the least expensive way to own that uncertainty. Options positioning provides no independent support for either the bull or bear case and should not drive the rating. Short interest of 3.6% of float and 4.3 days to cover confirms no crowded bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($165, +24.8%).** The deposit franchise absorbs the $13B Q3 repricing with interest-bearing costs holding at or below 2.80%, because 15% average demand-deposit growth from the small-business checking campaign offsets CD cost pressure. NIM holds at or above 3.45%, NII lands at the top of the 7-9% guide, and charge-offs stay at or below 20bp with classified loans flattening. Management deploys part of roughly $2.9B of excess CET1 (15.4% against a hypothetical 10.5% operating target on $58.6B of RWA — the target is an assumption, not company guidance) into an accretive wealth platform, converting idle capital into fee revenue and lifting the durable growth rate. Estimates move above $11.35 for 2027, and the multiple re-rates toward 14.5x as the cost of equity compresses toward 10.5%.

**Bear case ($105, -20.6%).** The tax rate reverts toward 25% as OBBBA narrows the renewable-credit pipeline (-$0.55), CD repricing steps up 30bp (-$0.22), and charge-offs normalize toward 40bp as the classified-loan migration converts, with the NDFI consumer-credit sleeve contributing correlated losses (-$0.71). EPS resets to $9.20-9.50 and the multiple compresses to 11.4x on impaired earnings visibility. California CRE and the media/entertainment book add idiosyncratic loss content. The Basel capital uptick lands at the higher end of the 160-180bp range, absorbing the excess capital that supported the option-value argument.

**Base case ($145, +9.7% price / ~12.1% total return).** Loan growth of 6-8% and NII growth of 7-9% deliver 2026 EPS near consensus $10.64, with 2027 around $11.35 as the deposit-repricing and tax levers exhaust and expense growth of 8-9% keeps operating leverage neutral. Credit normalizes gradually within the 15-25bp guide. The multiple holds near 12.8x forward and 2.2x forward tangible book. Capital continues to compound without deployment, which is a mild drag on ROTCE and a real but unpriced option.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Fifth report, fourth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score declining from 7.0 (April) to 6.1. The decline is driven by valuation compression of the opportunity — the stock is up 33.9% in twelve months against ~12% earnings growth — and now by two newly specific risks from the Q2 disclosures (the $13B Q3 CD roll and the doubling of sequential charge-offs alongside a declining allowance ratio). It is not driven by business deterioration: guidance was raised twice, capital built to 15.4% CET1, and estimates are stable. This is a quality franchise at a fair price, not a deteriorating one.

**Base case target $145** — 12.8x 2027 consensus EPS of $11.35, equivalent to roughly 2.2x forward tangible book against the current 2.12x. +9.7% from $132.21; ~12.1% including the 2.42% dividend.

**Bull case target $165** — 14.5x 2027E, requires CD repricing absorbed with NIM ≥3.45% and accretive capital deployment. +24.8%.

**Bear case target $105** — 11.4x a reset $9.20-9.50 of EPS. -20.6%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the mid-October Q3 2026 print (the first full quarter reflecting the $13B CD roll) and the January 2027 Q4 print with initial FY2027 guidance.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. Q3 interest-bearing deposit cost at or below the 2.76% spot rate after the $13B roll, with NIM at or above 3.40% and NII tracking the upper half of the 7-9% guide.
2. Classified loans flat or lower sequentially with the allowance ratio stable at or above 1.43% and charge-offs at or below 20bp.
3. Price below approximately $115 (≈10.1x 2027E, ≈1.8x forward tangible book) absent fundamental deterioration.
4. Disclosure quantifying the NDFI book with the split between private-equity capital-call lines and non-bank consumer warehouse lending, plus realized loss history.

**Downgrade to REDUCE requires any of:**
1. Net charge-offs above 35bp annualized in any quarter, or classified loans above 2.0% of loans.
2. Effective tax rate above 25% for two consecutive quarters without offsetting operating improvement.
3. NII growth guidance cut below 7%, or NIM below 3.30%.
4. An acquisition at a premium to book after twelve years without M&A, funded from excess capital, without a clear accretion path — the risk of a value-destructive first deal after a long dry spell is real and management's capital-priority ordering places M&A above buybacks.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in mid-October 2026.

## Conclusion

East West Bancorp earns its premium book multiple. A 36.7% efficiency ratio, 1.79% ROA, ~17% ROTCE and 15.4% CET1 are the combination the regional-bank sector aspires to, and the deposit franchise underlying them — 700,000+ accounts, 26% demand deposits, interest-bearing costs held below market without visible outflow — is genuinely difficult to replicate. Compensation is structured on return metrics rather than size, granted CEO pay has compounded at 2.8% while net income compounded at 11.0%, and the refusal to repurchase stock at 2.1x tangible book is the arithmetically correct decision even though it leaves ~$2.9B of capital idle.

At $132.21 the shares discount roughly an 11.2% cost of equity against a 17% return — a coherent price. The base case of $145 offers about 12% total return over twelve months, which approximates the cost of equity. That is a reason to own the stock, not a reason to add to it. The specific near-term tests are dated and quantifiable: $13B of CDs reprice in Q3 at a spread that has flattened to neutral, the tax lever that supplied roughly a quarter of Q1's EPS growth is being legislatively narrowed, classified loans rose 14.7% in a quarter while the allowance ratio drifted down, and a materially sized but unquantified non-bank financial institution book includes warehouse exposure to non-bank consumer lenders. Existing holders should stay. New capital has a better entry in the mid-$110s, or after the October print resolves the funding-cost question.
