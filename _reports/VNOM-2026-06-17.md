**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is a Permian Basin mineral and royalty company that collects a percentage of every barrel produced on its acreage without bearing any drilling, completion, or lease operating cost. This structure produces high incremental margins and converts production growth directly into cash flow. Operating cash flow rose 70% to $1.05B in FY25 and Fitch upgraded the company to investment grade, so the franchise is sound; the open question is whether the current price reflects the earnings inflection now underway.

The data argues it does not. Consensus current-year EPS has risen 101% over the past 90 days, with current-quarter estimates up 161%, and these revisions are still positive over the trailing 30 days. The company has beaten estimates in three of the last four quarters, including a 28% beat in Q1 2026, confirming the revisions are tracking real results rather than analyst optimism. At $43.46 the stock trades at roughly 16x current-year and 17.5x next-year EPS, below the peer median of 19.3x. The reported GAAP net loss of -$206M is entirely a $768M non-cash full-cost ceiling-test impairment driven by a common-control accounting quirk; it does not touch cash, the dividend, or the credit rating.

The prior neutral stance on this name leaned heavily on a directional view that oil was being repriced lower. That view is not supported by the financial data in front of us, and it conflicts with the measurable evidence: rising estimates, confirmed beats, an investment-grade balance sheet, dividend coverage near 3x on operating cash flow, and a buyback authorization (~$1.2B remaining) providing a price floor. All 18 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, and the lowest analyst price target ($49) sits 13% above spot. The read is an inflection BUY, with sizing tempered by the commodity leverage embedded in any royalty model and by the structural Diamondback overhang.

## Company Overview

VNOM owns subsurface mineral and royalty interests beneath approximately 96,003 net royalty acres (4.46M gross acres) in the Permian Basin. As a royalty holder it receives a fixed percentage of revenue from wells drilled by third-party and affiliated operators, with no exposure to capital or operating expenditure. At year-end 2025 there were 43,355 gross productive wells on its acreage. FY25 net production averaged 95,126 BOE/d with a Q4 exit rate near 134,000 BOE/d, and 2026 guidance is 120-132 MBOE/d. Proved reserves stand at 406,035 MBOE (48% oil, 78% proved-developed-producing).

The defining structural feature is the relationship with Diamondback Energy (FANG). VNOM has no employees; Diamondback provides all management and administrative services under a secondment agreement, owns roughly 42.1% of the voting power, operates about 35% of the net royalty acreage, and accounts for 55% of royalty income. VNOM functions as the minerals affiliate and financing vehicle within the Diamondback ecosystem. This delivers a low-cost deal pipeline, land and operating intelligence, and access to drop-down assets, while creating concentration and governance dependencies addressed in the risk section.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and production.** Royalty income grew from $717M (2023) to $854M (2024) to $1,346M (2025), a 58% YoY increase. Of the 91% production growth in 2025, roughly 46% came from the Diamondback Drop Down and 32% from the Sitio acquisition. Realized prices fell over the same period, from $46.85/BOE in 2024 to $38.77/BOE in 2025, with oil from $75.48 to $63.27/bbl, so volume growth more than offset price weakness on the top line.

**The net loss is non-operational.** FY25 reported a net loss of -$206M (EPS -$0.48) against +$604M in 2024. The entire swing is the non-cash ceiling-test impairment. The root cause is unusual: properties acquired from Diamondback in the Drop Down were booked at Diamondback's historical carrying value under common-control accounting, which exceeded the SEC-priced full-cost ceiling as oil prices fell. Management has flagged that a further impairment is "reasonably likely" in Q1 2026. This is cosmetically negative for headline EPS but carries no cash implication. A standardized screen reading the GAAP loss would flag a distress-zone Altman Z-Score and weak Piotroski; both are materially misleading for a royalty company carrying a paper write-down.

**Cash flow is the operative metric.** Operating cash flow reached $1,053M, up 70% YoY, and fully covers the dividend roughly 3x at the base level. Impairments do not touch this figure. The disconnect between the reported loss and operating cash is the central analytical point: cash flow, the dividend, and the credit rating all move opposite the GAAP loss.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $12.7B against long-term debt of $2.19B ($500M of 4.90% notes due 2030, $1.1B of 5.70% notes due 2035, with the term loan and revolver since repaid). Following the non-Permian divestiture the company is approaching its $1.5B net-debt target, with liquidity near $1.4B. Fitch upgraded VNOM to investment grade (BBB-) in May 2025, joining S&P at BBB-; Moody's holds at Ba1.

**Capital allocation.** The buyback authorization was raised from $750M to $1.75B in February 2026, with roughly $1.2B remaining. The base dividend was lifted to $1.52/yr, and the company returned 90% of cash available for distribution in Q4 2025, targeting up to 100% going forward. This is a disciplined return-of-capital posture, with the buyback providing a structural floor under the equity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture meets the threshold for a genuine earnings inflection rather than incremental improvement. Consensus current-year EPS has risen from $1.33 to $2.68 over 90 days (+101.2%), and current-quarter estimates from $0.30 to $0.78 (+161.3%). Next-year EPS is up 47.8%. The 30-day trend is still positive (+2.1% to +2.5%), so the revision momentum is intact, not rolling over. Revisions of this magnitude, well above the level that historically precedes continued outperformance, combined with revenue growth of 58%, mark a fundamental inflection that the market has only partially digested.

The earnings track record confirms the inflection is operational. VNOM beat in three of the last four quarters: +12.6% (Q2'25), +8.8% (Q3'25), in-line (Q4'25), and +28.4% (Q1'26). The Q1'26 beat against a backdrop of rising estimates raises confidence that current consensus is, if anything, still catching up.

The forward-volume story supports durability. 2026 production guidance of 120-132 MBOE/d versus a 95 MBOE/d FY25 average implies 26-39% volume growth at the midpoint, driven by the integration of the Sitio acreage and continued Drop Down inventory. The development runway is deep: 1,388 wells in active development, 1,370 line-of-sight, and 1,653 PUD locations. The capital-light model means this volume converts to cash at very high incremental margins. The principal sensitivity is oil price, which amplifies both the upside and downside of the variable dividend.

## Valuation Assessment

This is a textbook case for cycle-adjusted valuation. The trailing P/E is negative because of the non-cash impairment and is meaningless. On forward earnings the stock trades at ~16.3x current-year ($2.68) and ~17.5x next-year ($2.49) EPS. Both sit below the peer-group median of 19.3x for Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation comparables (AM 24.9x, KNTK 19.3x, DTM 31.1x; the shipping names INSW 7.5x and LPG 9.6x are structurally different businesses and skew the low end). For a capital-light, investment-grade royalty franchise growing production 26-39% with EPS estimates rising 100%+, a below-median forward multiple is the mispricing.

The dividend yield of ~6.2% provides absolute valuation support and exceeds the peer median of 4.2%. The remaining buyback authorization adds a second source of demand.

On the analyst-target framing: with rising estimates and a forward multiple below peers, consensus targets here function as floors rather than ceilings. The analyst targets ($49 low, $58.5 median, $65 high) all sit above the $43.46 spot, and the low target alone implies 13% upside. As estimates continue to rise, these targets are likely to be revised upward. The valuation score is held below an 8 only because the entire model is levered to a single volatile input, which caps the asymmetry rather than eliminating it.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. minerals and royalty market is fragmented and consolidating, and VNOM is a leading consolidator — it absorbed Sitio, a major public peer, in a $4B all-equity deal in 2025. Its differentiated advantages are the Diamondback land, data, and drop-down pipeline; an investment-grade balance sheet that lowers the cost of capital for accretive deals; and a core Midland/Delaware position with deep inventory.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. The same Diamondback relationship that provides advantage also caps independence: FANG can compete for the same minerals and has no obligation to offer acquisitions to VNOM. The cyclical headwind is capital discipline — operators including Diamondback directed 2025 free cash to debt paydown and buybacks rather than rig expansion, which moderates organic production growth in soft-price environments. On balance the franchise is confirmed and constructive; the score reflects a solid but not dominant position with a clear structural sponsor.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity leverage.** As a royalty on production with a variable dividend, VNOM's cash flow and payout swing directly with oil. This is the single most important value driver in both directions. The 2025 price decline compressed the dividend; a sustained higher-price environment lifts it. Near-term oil-price uncertainty is genuine, but it is a two-sided sensitivity, not a one-directional headwind, and the forward financial data does not support a confident directional call either way.

**Near-term impairment overhang.** The likely additional Q1 2026 ceiling-test write-down is non-cash and cosmetic but could weigh on headline sentiment.

**Diamondback control and conflicts.** FANG owns ~42%, has no fiduciary obligation to favor VNOM, can compete for the same assets, and the certificate of incorporation waives corporate-opportunity duties. Drop-down deals are related-party transactions vetted by independent committees.

**Concentration.** Diamondback at 55% and ExxonMobil at 14% of royalty income; ~100% Permian geography post-divestiture; total dependence on Diamondback personnel and systems given zero employees.

**Integration risk** from the $4B Sitio deal, which was excluded from the FY25 internal-control assessment and represented 32% of consolidated assets.

Most of these are either non-cash, structural and long-disclosed, or two-sided. None constitutes fresh fundamental deterioration. The risk score is held at 5.5 to reflect the genuine commodity sensitivity and the near-term impairment headline, which are real but largely understood.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Estimate revisions, quarterly beats, and 26-39% production growth converge on an earnings inflection that the market has under-rated at ~16x forward EPS, below the peer median. Operating cash flow covers the base dividend ~3x, the balance sheet is investment grade, and the buyback plus the dividend yield provide downside support. A stable-to-firmer oil environment lifts the variable dividend and drives a re-rating toward the analyst median of $58.5, with the high case at $65 on a stronger price deck.

**Bear case.** A sustained oil-price decline compresses both cash flow and the variable dividend, the flagged Q1 2026 impairment weighs on headlines, and continued Diamondback share sales (~$589M in March 2026) create persistent supply overhang. In this scenario the stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $35 (-19% from spot), with the forward multiple offering limited additional protection because the earnings denominator falls with the price of oil.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $54.** Applying ~20x (a modest premium to the 19.3x peer median, justified by the higher growth rate and IG quality) to the current-year EPS of $2.68 yields ~$54, roughly the midpoint of the $49-58.5 analyst range. This implies ~24% upside from $43.46, plus a ~6.2% yield.

**Bull case target: $65.** Consistent with the high analyst target, achieved on a firmer oil deck that lifts realized prices and the variable dividend while estimates continue to rise.

**Bear case target: $35.** A retest of the 52-week low on sustained oil weakness, implying ~-19% from current price.

**Timeframe: 12 months,** with key checkpoints at the Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings releases (to confirm the production ramp toward 120-132 MBOE/d and continued estimate momentum) and any further analyst target revisions following beats.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Current-quarter or current-year EPS estimates turn negative on a 30-day basis (revision momentum reversing rather than merely decelerating).
2. 2026 production tracks below the low end of the 120-132 MBOE/d guidance, signaling the volume inflection is not materializing.
3. The base dividend is cut, or operating-cash-flow coverage of the base dividend falls below ~2x, indicating commodity weakness is impairing the core cash engine.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports a BUY. Every element of an inflection thesis is present and measurable: estimate revisions that are still rising, three beats in four quarters, a forward P/E below peers, an investment-grade balance sheet, dividend coverage near 3x on cash flow, and a buyback providing a floor. The GAAP loss is a non-cash artifact; operating cash flow grew 70%. The principal genuine risk is commodity leverage, which is two-sided and inherent to the royalty model rather than a sign of deterioration, and the near-term impairment is cosmetic. The score is held at 6.6 rather than higher because the model's entire upside is levered to oil price, and because the broader Energy sector has been a difficult place to generate index-beating returns, so position sizing should be measured rather than aggressive. On the company-specific evidence, this is a cheap, growing, cash-generative franchise where the market is anchored to a misleading trailing loss, and the recommendation is BUY.
