## Executive Summary

Cathay General Bancorp is a $24.05bn-asset Chinese-American commercial banking franchise whose 2026 earnings recovery is, mechanically, a funding-cost event. In Q1 2026 the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 47bp year over year while earning-asset yields fell only 19bp, producing 28bp of spread expansion, an 18bp NIM improvement to 3.43%, and a 25% increase in net income to $86.9M. The company's own volume/rate attribution confirms the source: of the $14.0M decline in interest expense, $18.3M was rate. This reverses 2022-2024, when the same structure — 47.0% of deposits in time deposits, 99.7% maturing within twelve months, only 16.5% non-interest-bearing — compressed earnings as rates rose. Management's disclosed sensitivity makes the dependency explicit: −100bp lifts net interest income 3.4% and −200bp lifts it 6.4%, while +200bp reduces NII 3.7%.

That dependency is the problem with the current entry point. The rate path is genuinely two-sided [unverified — no macro rate-path data appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data], and CATY's earnings model is not. The stock has meanwhile advanced: $63.56 is 2.1% below the 52-week high of $64.89 and 41% above the price a year ago, leaving 11.5x FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.53 and 10.7x FY2027 of $5.93, but 1.63x the $38.95 tangible book value per share reported at 3/31/26 against a 13.3% annualized return on tangible common equity. On a Gordon-style framework — P/TBV = (ROTCE − g)/(COE − g) — 13.3% ROTCE with 4% growth and a 10% cost of equity supports roughly 1.55x. The stock is at or slightly through fair value on book, cheap on earnings, and the earnings are the part that is rate-contingent.

The credit picture is the second reason to withhold new capital. Non-accrual loans fell 42.4% year over year to $89.0M, which reads as sharp improvement until it is set against accruing loans modified for borrowers experiencing financial difficulty, which rose from $8.2M to $84.3M over the same twelve months and are excluded from the NPA table. Combined identified problem credit of roughly $173M at 3/31/26 compares with roughly $167M at year-end and $163M a year earlier — flat. Criticized assets moved the wrong way in the quarter: CRE substandard $181.8M → $207.4M (+14%), construction substandard $32.7M → $43.4M (+33%). Set against 13.47% CET1, a 49% weighted-average CREC loan-to-value with 86% of the book at ≤60% LTV, and a modelled downside CECL gap of only $70.7M (2.7% of tangible common equity), this is an earnings risk rather than a capital risk. It is nonetheless not the credit trajectory that justifies paying a peak multiple on book. Fourth consecutive HOLD, with the honest classification being uncertainty rather than a growth or income thesis.

## Company Overview

Cathay General Bancorp is the Delaware holding company for Cathay Bank, founded 1962, headquartered in Los Angeles, with $24.05bn of total assets at 3/31/26 (down from $24.23bn at year-end). The branch network is 41 California locations, 9 in New York, 4 in Washington, and single-digit representation in Illinois, Texas, Maryland, Massachusetts, Nevada and New Jersey, plus one Hong Kong branch and representative offices in Taipei, Beijing and Shanghai limited to liaison work. Hong Kong branch deposits are not FDIC-insured.

The strategy is stated plainly in the 10-K: operate primarily in California markets with concentrations of Chinese-American individuals and businesses, and expand into other domestic markets with the same demographic. The claimed advantage is underwriting knowledge and relationship depth inside that niche, and the filings concede the moat is inseparable from concentration.

The balance sheet is a real-estate lender. Gross loans of $20.17bn at 3/31/26 break down as commercial real estate $10.59bn (52.5%), residential mortgage and equity lines $6.01bn (29.8%), C&I $3.28bn (16.3%), construction $289M (1.4%). CRE plus construction totals $10.88bn, or 278% of the Bank's total risk-based capital, down from 287% at year-end but inside the 300% supervisory attention level.

Revenue is almost entirely spread income: Q1 2026 net interest income of $194.2M against $20.7M of total non-interest income, of which only $14.5M was ASC 606 contract revenue. Wealth management at ~$7.1M per quarter (+15.1% y/y) is the only visibly growing fee line. There is no mortgage banking, card, or capital-markets engine, and no trust business — the 10-K names trust services as an offering CATY lacks relative to competitors. Roughly 90% of gross revenue is net interest income, so earnings durability is a function of NIM and credit, not fee diversification.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year earnings shape.** Net income was $360.6M (FY2022), $354.1M (FY2023), $286.0M (FY2024) and $315.1M (FY2025). Diluted EPS $4.83 / $4.86 / $3.95 / $4.54. FY2025 net income remains 12.6% below the FY2022 peak; EPS is 6.6% below FY2023. EPS recovered faster than net income because diluted shares fell from 74.7M to 69.5M. This is a partial rebound off a trough, not a new earnings high.

**Q1 2026 detail.** Net interest income $194.2M (+10.0%), NIM 3.43% (+18bp), earning-asset yield 5.70% (−19bp), cost of interest-bearing liabilities 2.99% (−47bp), spread 2.71% (+28bp), net income $86.9M (+25.0%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.29, ROA 1.47%, ROE 11.88%. Time-deposit expense alone fell $11.2M, of which −$12.3M was rate.

Two large offsetting non-recurring items sit inside that quarter: a +$17.3M unrealized gain on equity securities and a −$15.7M AFS impairment taken on securities designated for sale (sold in April at $210.5M book value). Net charge-offs were unusually low at $2.1M (0.04% annualized), helped by $7.2M of recoveries versus $0.37M a year earlier, and FDIC assessments fell ~$1.0M as the final SVB/Signature special-assessment instalment cleared (remaining accrual zero, a ~$4M annualized expense tailwind).

**A basis conflict worth naming.** The 10-Q reports Q1 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.29. The consensus-surprise series carries $1.52 actual against a $1.24 estimate for the March quarter and $1.52 against $1.37 for the June quarter — that is an adjusted basis (the $15.7M pre-tax impairment is roughly $0.17 per diluted share after tax), not GAAP, and the two should not be compared. Within the consensus provider's own basis, the record is three beats in four quarters (+22.7%, +11.1%, +7.1%, −4.1%). The FY2026 consensus of $5.53 reconciles closely to GAAP: $1.29 actual Q1, plus roughly the balance across the remaining quarters. Delivering the second-half GAAP EPS the consensus implies requires further NIM expansion, which returns to the rate path.

**The efficiency-ratio illusion.** Reported FY2025 efficiency was 43.41% versus 51.35% in FY2024, an apparent 794bp improvement. Reproduce it: FY2025 revenue base $817.9M (NII $742.5M + fee $75.4M), non-interest expense $355.1M. Non-interest expense fell $19.6M, but amortization of affordable-housing and alternative-energy partnership investments fell $29.0M. Add that back and the adjusted ratio is $384.1M / $817.9M = 46.96%. Roughly half the improvement is the geography of a tax-credit structure, not cost control, and the offset appeared in the tax line: the effective rate rose from 9.94% to 19.24% and tax expense rose ~$43.5M, so pre-tax income rose $72.7M while net income rose $29.1M. Q1 2026 continues the pattern (amortization $6.7M vs $9.1M; effective rate 21.0% vs 19.8%; the 2026 rate reconciliation cites only low-income-housing credits, with alternative-energy credits absent). The classification follows standard accounting for these vehicles; the modelling error to avoid is pairing a 43% efficiency ratio with a low tax rate. A plausible alternative reading is that project vintages matured with replacements not yet ramped — $287.3M of partnership investments and $85.5M of unfunded commitments remain, so both the amortization and part of the benefit could return.

**Balance sheet, liquidity, capital.** Loans +0.1% sequentially; deposits −1.0% to $20.68bn, with non-interest-bearing down $106.1M. FY2025 deposit growth of ~$1.21bn was accompanied by brokered deposits rising from ~$1.06bn (5.4%) to ~$1.59bn (7.6%), so roughly 44% of that year's growth was wholesale. Uninsured-and-uncollateralized deposits were $9.21bn, 44.6% of total, against FHLB unused capacity $7.95bn, unpledged securities $1.66bn and short-term cash ~$1.21bn — coverage above 100%. Zero FHLB advances outstanding at period end, though $4.33bn was borrowed and repaid intraquarter. AFS securities are just $1.68bn (7.0% of assets) with 1.9-year effective duration, $63.2M of gross unrealized losses (2.4% of tangible common equity) and no HTM bucket concealing additional marks. Bancorp CET1 13.47%, total capital 15.20%, leverage 11.15%, all improved sequentially. Long-term debt is $119.1M of trust preferred at a 6.23% weighted-average rate, repricing downward, carried above a fair value of $82.8M.

Derived book values at 3/31/26: equity $2,986.6M / 66,972,039 shares = $44.59 book; tangible common equity $2,608.5M (after $375.7M goodwill and $2.4M other intangibles) / 66.97M = $38.95 tangible book. Annualized ROTCE $347M / $2,608.5M = 13.3%.

**Financial health score: 6.3.** Capital and liquidity are genuinely strong, the securities book is small and short, the dividend is covered at ~29-31% of run-rate earnings, and there is no HTM mark hidden from equity. Against that, the funding structure is the weakest part of the franchise (47% time deposits, 16.5% non-interest-bearing, 44.6% uninsured-uncollateralized, brokered rising, deposits contracting), the allowance at 1.03% of loans is thin relative to a 52.5% CRE concentration even with low LTVs, goodwill is 14.4% of tangible equity, and reported earnings now carry $17-21M quarterly swings from a ~$42.6M private-fund equity position at a bank earning ~$87M a quarter.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is absent. Loans grew 0.1% sequentially, with +$98.0M commercial and +$24.0M CRE offset by −$53.6M residential and −$48.5M (−14.4%) construction. Deposits shrank 1.0%. There is no M&A — the 8-K index shows no Item 1.01 material agreements or Item 2.01 acquisitions, despite acquisition being a stated strategy. The fee franchise is ~10% of revenue with one growing line (wealth management, +15% off a ~$28M annualized base).

What has grown is earnings per share, from three sources: spread expansion (rate-driven), a 5.5% reduction in share count over five quarters, and a lower expense base helped by the tax-credit amortization roll-off and the FDIC special-assessment expiry. Consensus captures the arithmetic honestly — FY2026 $5.53 (+21.8% on FY2025's $4.54) then FY2027 $5.93 (+7.2%). The sharp deceleration is the market correctly identifying that 2026 is a cycle rebound, not a new growth trajectory.

Estimate revisions are positive but small: current quarter +2.7% over 30 days and +2.9% over 90 days; current year +0.9%/+1.0%; next year +0.4%/+0.6%, across five analysts. This is nowhere near the >+30% ninety-day revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection, and I am not treating it as such. It confirms that the current quarter is tracking slightly ahead, no more.

The bridge to consensus 2026 requires the remaining quarters to build on the $1.29 GAAP Q1, which requires further funding-cost relief. The normalized run-rate work — stripping the offsetting equity gain and impairment and normalizing charge-offs toward the FY2025 rate of 0.18% of loans — produces ~$4.90-5.16 annualized off Q1, below the $5.53 consensus. That gap is closable by continued CD repricing, and Q2's adjusted beat suggests it is closing. It is not closable if the Fed holds through year-end.

**Growth prospects score: 5.0.** Flat balance sheet, no fee engine, no deal pipeline, and consensus itself sees 7% earnings growth in 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

CATY is one of the two dominant Chinese-American commercial banking franchises in the U.S., competing directly with East West Bancorp (larger, higher-return), Preferred Bank and RBB Bancorp, adjacent Korean-American franchises, and the money-center and foreign banks in its metros. The niche is a real differentiator in origination and relationship retention. It is also the source of the structural funding disadvantage: this depositor base competes hard on CD rates, which is why non-interest-bearing balances are 16.5% rather than 25-30% and why the 10-K concedes competition "may cause our cost of funds to exceed that of our competitors." A 13.3% ROTCE at 49% CRE LTVs is respectable but below what a low-cost-deposit franchise generates at comparable credit quality.

The 10-K is candid about scale disadvantages: competitors have greater lending limits, offer services CATY does not (trust services named explicitly), and can absorb technology and compliance costs that "have a disproportionate impact on our ability to compete with larger institutions."

Trajectory matters more than level: loans flat, deposits contracting, construction deliberately shrinking, no share gains identifiable, funding-cost disadvantage structural, and one growing fee line off a small base. Portfolio quality within CRE is a genuine competitive asset — average CREC loan size of $2.0M, 49% weighted-average LTV, 86% of the book at ≤60% LTV, 25% owner-occupied, and only 21% floating with another 40% in hybrid fixed periods. That granularity is the reason a CRE concentration of this size is not a solvency question.

**Competitive position score: 6.0**, down from 6.7. Stable niche, no evidence of share gain, structural funding cost gap, and a client base with specific tariff/trade transmission exposure — the 10-K's unusually detailed tariff language points directly at import/export and wholesale/retail borrowers, which are already the second-largest non-accrual business category at $23.7M combined.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-path asymmetry (primary).** Disclosed 3/31/26 sensitivity: NII +3.4% at −100bp, +6.4% at −200bp, −3.7% at +200bp, with market value of equity −27.6% at +200bp. Management attributes the asymmetry to CD repricing lags. The MVE figure is a model output under an instantaneous parallel shock, not a capital loss — the actual AFS mark is $63.2M against $2.61bn of tangible common equity with 1.9-year duration and no HTM bucket. The NII sensitivity is real, and the FY2025 10-K notes only that the Fed's December 2025 projections implied lower rates through 2026-2027 while stating "there can be no such assurance that any such decreases in the federal funds rate will occur," so the earnings recovery is contingent on a rate path the filings themselves decline to guarantee. Deposits already shrank 1.0% in Q1 and management expects "some outflow of these deposits upon maturity due to the keen competition."

**Credit — modification migration.** Non-accruals fell from $154.6M to $89.0M year over year while accruing modified loans rose from $8.2M to $84.3M; modified accruing loans are excluded from the NPA table. Combined identified problem credit is flat at ~$173M. Q1 modifications included $36.6M of construction loans — 12.68% of that entire portfolio — at a weighted-average 0.6-year extension, $25.95M of it combining rate reduction, term extension and payment delay. $16.33M of $19.03M of modified commercial loans were 30-89 days past due at 3/31/26, and $2.43M of trailing-twelve-month modifications defaulted in Q1 versus zero a year earlier. The construction allowance jumped from $6.48M to $15.40M in one quarter, 5.3% of a $289M book.

Two counterweights deserve equal billing. Genuine resolution occurred: commercial non-accruals fell from $53.4M to $7.7M year over year, UCC-secured non-accruals from $17.5M to $2.4M, and recoveries were $7.23M in the quarter. And the 10-Q attributes the ACL increase primarily to quantitative model recalibration — "updates to certain model assumptions intended to better capture the portfolio's historical loss experience across economic cycles" — which is methodology, not observed deterioration. Separately, the FY2025 10-K five-year table shows $0 for accruing modifications in 2023 and 2024, inconsistent with ASU 2022-02's 1/1/2023 effective date and with the $8.2M comparative in the 10-Q, so part of the apparent ten-fold increase may be presentation. That reconciliation is unresolved and I treat the modification finding as a material caution rather than an established deterioration.

**New York multifamily.** New York is 46% of the $2.87bn multifamily book, or $1,333.6M — 6.6% of gross loans and 51.1% of the derived $2,608.5M tangible common equity, in the asset class that has produced the most acute regional-bank stress since New York's 2019 rent law. The filings disclose no rent-regulated share, no DSCR distribution and no maturity ladder. This is the largest single information deficit in the credit book. Portfolio-level mitigants (49% LTV, 86% ≤60% LTV, $2.0M average loan) mean a very large price decline is absorbable, and multifamily is not currently where criticized assets are migrating.

**Earnings quality.** Equity securities rose to $69.2M, of which only $26.6M is Level 1 and roughly $42.6M is private investment funds; the +$17.3M unrealized gain in Q1 2026 versus a $4.2M loss a year earlier accounted for the entire 84.8% increase in non-interest income. The FY2025 10-K warns a liquidity event or third-party valuation adjustment on concentrated private holdings "could have a significant impact." At 1.4% of tangible equity this is volatility, not solvency.

**Other.** CRE at 278% of risk-based capital with regulators applying heightened scrutiny; $375.7M goodwill (14.4% of tangible equity) tested against market-based fair value at 1.43x book; $287.3M of tax-credit partnerships with $85.5M of unfunded commitments subject to recapture; explicit Asia/Hong Kong transfer risk and a named exposure to deterioration "in China or Taiwan in particular"; California catastrophe and property-insurance-availability risk against CRE collateral; a classified board with super-majority combination provisions and blank-check preferred, which materially reduces the probability of a value-realizing sale in a consolidating $20-30bn asset tier. Modelled CECL downside adds $70.7M (2.7% of tangible equity), down from $97.4M at year-end — a manageable adverse case.

**Q2 2026 disclosure gap.** An earnings 8-K was filed 7/22/26 (Items 2.02/7.01) and its contents are not in evidence here. I have the adjusted EPS datapoint ($1.52 versus $1.37) and the price reaction (close $61.36 on 7/22, $62.53 on 7/23, drifting to $64.35 by 8/4), but not the 6/30/26 NIM, non-accrual balance, modified-loan balance, allowance ratio, or deposit trend. Every credit and funding figure cited above is as of 3/31/26.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin rather than informative: 27 contracts across three expirations, put/call open-interest ratio 0.03 (down from 0.21 on 6/25), near-term ATM implied volatility 60.7% against 24.8% one-year realized, term structure in backwardation (60.7% at 13 days versus 26.3% at six months), implied move ±7.9-9.3% on the 8/21 expiry. A 60.7% near-term print on a $4bn regional bank thirteen days out, with essentially zero put open interest and no scheduled catalyst (Q2 was reported 7/22), is more consistent with an illiquid-chain artifact than with the market pricing imminent stress; a genuine stress signal would show put open interest accumulating, not collapsing to 0.03. Near-term skew is reported as balanced at 0.3%; the six-month figure of 7.0% is reported without a directional inference since the sign convention is not defined. Short interest is 6.0% of float at 4.5 days to cover — elevated for a regional bank, uncrowded in absolute terms, and consistent with the one strong-sell rating in the analyst distribution. There is no options-derived confirmation or contradiction of the fundamental view, and I am not weighting it.

**Risk factors score: 5.3**, down from 5.8. The mark-down rests on three items, not on price level: the rate skew now runs against a disclosed liability-sensitive posture; CRE and construction criticized balances rose 14% and 33% sequentially; and the largest exposure relative to capital remains undisclosed in its loss-relevant detail.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $78 (+22.7%)

The Fed's easing path resumes. With 99.7% of $9.72bn of time deposits repricing within twelve months and only 21% of CRE floating, 100bp of cuts flows almost entirely to the liability side, and the disclosed +3.4% NII benefit understates the effect if deposit competition eases alongside. NIM moves toward 3.60-3.70%, FY2027 EPS reaches $6.30, the ~$4M annualized FDIC tailwind and continued C&I credit healing hold, the $150M authorization retires another 2.5% of shares, and the market pays 12.4x — or 1.85x a ~$42 tangible book — for a 14%+ ROTCE bank with 13.5% CET1. That is $77-80, consistent with the high analyst target of $80. The modification cohort cures rather than re-defaults, criticized CRE stabilizes, and the New York multifamily book proves to be the low-LTV, small-ticket, largely unregulated product the portfolio statistics imply.

### Bear Case — $51 (−19.8%)

Rates hold or move higher. CD costs stop falling and begin rising as 47% of the deposit book reprices, NIM retraces toward 3.20-3.30%, and the disclosed −3.7% NII sensitivity to +200bp is partially realized. Simultaneously the modification cohort behaves the way its 30-89 day delinquency already suggests: the $84.3M of accruing modified loans migrates back to non-accrual, construction losses land against a $289M book (a 20% loss is ~$58M pre-tax, roughly two months of pre-tax income — painful but not structural), and provisions normalize toward 40-50bp of loans, or $85-100M annually versus $72.6M in FY2025. FY2027 EPS falls to $4.60. The multiple compresses to 1.25x tangible book on concentration concerns as the New York multifamily disclosure gap becomes a market question. $50-52.

### Base Case — $67 (+5.4%)

11.3x FY2027 consensus of $5.93 = $67.0, cross-checked against 1.60x a pro-forma year-end-2026 tangible book of roughly $41.75 = $66.8. Consensus mean sits at $65.80. Add the $1.52 dividend and total return is ~7.8%.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives $65.20 plus $1.52 of dividends = $66.72, an expected total return of +5.0% over twelve months. Against an equity cost of capital near 10% for a bank with this concentration profile, that is approximately fair — the definition of HOLD, not of ACCUMULATE. It is also not negative enough for REDUCE: capital is strong at 13.47% CET1, the dividend is covered at ~29-31% of run-rate earnings, the CECL downside gap is only 2.7% of tangible equity, and management has demonstrated it will retire shares below 1.4x tangible book. The distinguishing feature versus REDUCE is that the downside case is an earnings event, not a capital event, and the dividend plus buyback provide a genuine floor to total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders maintain exposure and collect the 2.39% yield; prospective buyers have no entry signal at 1.6x tangible book with the rate path unresolved.

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the Q3 2026 earnings release (mid-to-late October) as the decisive checkpoint.

**Targets:** Base $67 (+5.4%; +7.8% total return with dividends). Bull $78 (+22.7%). Bear $51 (−19.8%).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of the following:**
1. Price at or below $55-57 (≈1.38-1.43x pro-forma tangible book) with FY2027 consensus holding at $5.85 or above — restoring roughly 18% upside to base against a ~10% bear gap.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which combined identified problem credit (non-accrual plus accruing modified) declines, with construction substandard falling from $43.4M and CRE substandard from $207.4M.
3. Fed funds cut 50bp or more with CATY confirming NIM at or above 3.55% and deposits returning to growth without brokered balances rising above 8% of total.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. The Fed hikes, or explicitly guides to a hike, while CATY reports NIM contraction of 10bp or more sequentially — that removes the entire 2026 earnings mechanism at a peak multiple on book.
2. Accruing modified loans exceed $110M, or trailing-twelve-month modification re-defaults exceed $10M, or quarterly net charge-offs exceed 30bp annualized.
3. Deposits decline more than 2% in a quarter, or brokered deposits exceed 10% of total funding.
4. Any disclosure indicating that more than half of the $1.33bn New York multifamily book is rent-regulated without offsetting DSCR detail.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:** provisions exceed $40M in a single quarter with non-accruals returning above $150M, or CRE plus construction rises back above 300% of Bank total risk-based capital, or the dividend is reduced.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 release in October, which will provide the first clean read on 6/30 and 9/30 modification balances, criticized-asset migration, and whether the $150M authorization was deployed above 1.6x tangible book. The 7/22/26 Q2 8-K should be read in full before any position change — it is the single largest unresolved input.

## Conclusion

Cathay General is a well-capitalized, competently run niche franchise that has delivered a real earnings recovery — 25% net income growth and 18bp of NIM expansion in Q1 2026, three adjusted beats in four quarters, a 5.5% share-count reduction over five quarters executed at roughly 1.32x tangible book, and an 11.8% dividend increase covered at a modest payout. None of that is in dispute.

The problem is what the market is now paying for it and what the earnings depend on. At $63.56 the stock is 2.1% from its 52-week high after a 41% twelve-month advance, at 1.63x reported tangible book against a 13.3% ROTCE — a multiple that a Gordon framework supports at roughly 1.55x. The forward P/E of 11.5x looks cheap only if the earnings are durable, and they are explicitly rate-contingent: 47% of deposits reprice within twelve months, management's own model shows NII +3.4% at −100bp and −3.7% at +200bp, and the FY2025 10-K itself declines to guarantee that the projected rate decreases will occur. Estimate revisions of +0.9% on the current year do not constitute an inflection signal. The sell side is at 0 strong buy / 1 buy / 3 hold / 1 strong sell with a mean target 3.5% above spot.

Underneath, the headline 42% decline in non-accruals is offset almost entirely by a rise in accruing modified loans from $8.2M to $84.3M, criticized CRE and construction balances rose 14% and 33% sequentially, and $1.33bn of New York multifamily — 51% of tangible common equity — sits in the filings without rent-regulation, DSCR or maturity detail. Low LTVs (49% weighted average, 86% at ≤60%) and 13.47% CET1 mean this is an earnings risk rather than a capital risk, and the modelled adverse-case reserve addition of $70.7M is only 2.7% of tangible equity.

Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 5% against a 20% bear-case decline is approximately fair, not compelling. Fourth consecutive HOLD, with the rating now resting on genuine two-sided uncertainty — the rate path and the modification cohort — rather than on a positive appreciation thesis. The place to add is the mid-$50s, or on confirmation that the modification cohort is curing rather than deferring.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's rate-path bear case relied on specific unverifiable macro claims (five FOMC holds, three hawkish dissents, 30-year yield highs, −23,000 July payrolls) that are not in the sources and have been removed/demoted to the filings' own hedged rate language; and the earnings-surprise magnitudes/estimates for the March and June quarters were transposed. Neither correction alters the capital, credit, or valuation math underpinning HOLD, but the two-sided-rate-risk framing is now grounded only in the filings' non-guarantee, not in external macro data.
