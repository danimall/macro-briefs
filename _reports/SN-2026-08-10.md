## Executive Summary

SharkNinja delivered its strongest quarter since 2024 and raised full-year guidance for the second time in six months. Q2 2026 net sales rose 22.2% to $1,765.5M, adjusted EPS rose 29.9% to $1.26 against a $1.14 consensus, and FY2026 guidance moved to +16-17% sales growth and $6.45-$6.55 adjusted EPS from the +10-11% and $5.90-$6.00 initially guided in February. That is a 550-600 basis point upgrade to the top-line guide inside two quarters, and management attributes only about $0.15 of the $0.45 EPS raise to the tariff refund. The underlying business is doing the work.

The problem is what the market now pays for it. At $184.70 the stock sits at an all-time high, 28.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.57, 24.6x FY2027 $7.52, and roughly 19.0x guided FY2026 EV/adjusted EBITDA — a multiple that adds back share-based compensation which just tripled to a run rate equal to about 22% of GAAP operating income. Consensus mean and median targets of $203.41 and $205.00 imply 10-11% upside. My base case of $198 implies 7%. The bear case, which requires only a return to low-double-digit growth and a mid-teens-to-19x multiple, lands near the low analyst target of $137.51, or -27%.

Between those two, a dense and unusually specific risk stack sits at the highest price the stock has ever traded. Material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting — spanning IT general controls, revenue and accounts receivable, inventory and cost of goods sold, and the financial close process — were unremediated at both December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026, with disclosure controls concluded not effective at both dates. Top-three customer concentration is 45-48% of sales and Customer A's share rose year over year. The $247.1M tariff refund lands in Q3 as a receivable, not cash, with aspects of the underlying court order under government appeal. And the CEO sold 996,511 shares for approximately $162.0M across six transactions from February to August — roughly 38% of his pre-sale holding, with the two largest tranches executed at $170 and $185 on and immediately after the guidance raise — while the Chairman sold $401.2M in a single July block. There were no open-market insider purchases in the reviewed record. The growth is real; the entry price and the governance/control overlay are not compensated. HOLD.

## Company Overview

SharkNinja designs and markets household appliances and personal-care devices under two brands: Shark (cleaning, beauty) and Ninja (cooking, beverage, food preparation). It separated from JS Global Lifestyle Company Limited on July 31, 2023, ceased foreign-private-issuer status effective January 1, 2026, and filed its first Form 10-K on March 2, 2026 for fiscal 2025.

Products are designed in-house and manufactured by third parties in China, Vietnam and other non-U.S. regions. Distribution runs through more than 180 retailers and distributors — Amazon, Costco, Walmart, Target and Best Buy among them — plus a growing direct-to-consumer channel including TikTok Shop. The company reports two operating segments (Domestic and International) aggregated into one reportable segment, with gross profit as the measure reviewed by the CEO as chief operating decision maker.

Q2 2026 composition: Cleaning Appliances $522.0M (+4.1%), Cooking & Beverage $499.0M (+36.5%), Food Preparation $458.6M (+13.3%), Beauty & Home Environment $285.8M (+65.3%). Brand split was Ninja $957.6M (54.2%) and Shark $807.8M (45.8%). Domestic was $1,141.9M (+15.5%) and International $623.6M (+36.6%); the U.S. share of sales fell from 63.7% to 61.5%.

The stated innovation cadence is roughly 25 new product introductions plus 20 launches within existing categories annually, supported by R&D of $368.1M in FY2025 (5.8% of sales) rising to $208.2M in H1 2026 (6.6% of sales) and advertising of $632.9M in FY2025 (9.9%). Sub-category count went from 38 at FY2025 year-end to 39 at June 30, 2026, with a 40th entered in July.

The SBC step-up is mechanical: H1 2026 grants of 2,637,219 RSUs carry a $111.24 weighted-average grant-date fair value against a prior unvested pool at $43.90. Unrecognized SBC is $221.7M over 2.5 years. H1 SBC of $77.5M equals 22.5% of H1 GAAP operating income ($343.9M). The counter-argument has force: FY2025 SBC of $43.9M (0.69% of sales) was likely abnormally low, since G&A SBC fell from $60.5M in FY2024 to $17.5M in FY2025 as separation-era awards vested out. H1 2026 at roughly 2.4% of sales is unremarkable for the growth profile. Either way, the practical consequence is that the adjusted EBITDA figure supporting a 19x multiple now excludes an expense equal to about a fifth of operating profit.

The 70bp adjusted-gross-margin decline was attributed to U.S. tariff cost, unfavorable FX, and increased retailer activations, partially offset by cost optimization, mix and the sourcing-fee roll-off. Two of the three headwinds are exogenous and reversing. The third — retailer activations — speaks to competitive intensity and is corroborated by accrued customer incentives, which rose from 4.88% of sales in FY2023 to 5.27% in FY2024 to 6.40% in FY2025, growing 40.6% against 15.7% sales growth.

Revisions of +6-7% on the annual periods are healthy earnings momentum. They are not the +30% velocity that marks a genuine cyclical inflection, and this is not a trough-earnings situation — FY2025 GAAP net income was up 4.2x from FY2023. The stock is being valued on continuation of an established high-teens compounding trajectory, not on a recovery from depressed levels. That distinction matters for how much the revision signal should override the multiple.

Note also that consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.57 sits 1.1% above the guidance midpoint of $6.50, so analysts already embed another modest beat. The four-for-four beat record (+10.7%, +2.7%, +3.9%, +8.2%) supports that assumption.

**Implied second-half math.** Guidance of +16-17% on FY2025's $6,399.2M implies FY2026 revenue of $7,423-7,487M. Less H1's $3,178.3M leaves H2 of $4,245-4,309M against H2 2025's $3,731.7M — growth of +13.7% to +15.5%, decelerating from H1's +19.1%. On earnings, $6.45-6.55 less H1's $2.34 implies H2 adjusted EPS of $4.11-4.21 versus $3.44, or +19-22%; stripping the ~$0.15 refund contribution leaves +15-18%. So the guide requires back-half margin expansion after H1 delivered 70bp of adjusted gross-margin contraction and 120bp of GAAP operating-margin contraction. Q4 2025 produced 18.8% adjusted EBITDA margin (+250bp), so holiday seasonality makes it achievable, but this is not a conservative setup.

**Where the growth comes from.** International at 35.3% of Q2 sales growing 36.6% is the highest-conviction lever, supported by conversion of distributor relationships to direct operating models — Nordics, Poland and Benelux in Q4 2025, Italy and Spain in H1 2026 — which captures distributor margin and improves consumer data. Category adjacency (Beauty & Home Environment +65.3% off a $172.9M base) is the second. Channel shift to DTC and social commerce is the third, though it carries visible cost: Q2 included $8.8M of incremental credit-card and merchant fees and $2.6M of product-sample costs.

**Where it doesn't.** Cleaning Appliances, the largest category at $2,205.8M in FY2025, grew 6.9% in FY2025 and 4.1% in Q2 2026. Food Preparation's H1 growth of 6.3% included a decline in frozen drinks — the Ninja Slushi adjacency that drove FY2025's +31.6% is now lapping a hard comparison. The growth algorithm depends on continuously landing new $200M+ categories because the mature core grows in the low-to-mid single digits. That is the central durability question for the multiple, and it cannot be resolved from current data.

The physical commitments argue management believes the runway. The Needham headquarters lease was renewed in June 2026 extending the term 11 years and adding $41.9M each to ROU asset and liability; a seven-year Southaven, Mississippi warehouse added $19.9M in May. Total ROU assets rose from $142.5M to $193.2M in six months.

## Competitive Landscape

The most decision-relevant external datapoint, per Circana data relayed through secondary reporting rather than verified in a filing: the total U.S. market in SharkNinja's categories declined low single digits in FY2025 and worsened to mid-single-digit declines in Q4 2025, excluding SharkNinja's own results. Against that, SharkNinja grew net sales 15.7% in FY2025 with eleven consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. Essentially all domestic growth is share capture from incumbents in a contracting category.

That framing cuts both ways and the direction matters for the score. Share gain against a declining backdrop is powerful evidence of product and go-to-market superiority, and it is the reason the trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. It is also structurally more fragile than growth riding a rising category: zero-sum share capture invites competitive response, and the mechanism SharkNinja used to displace incumbents — e-commerce lowering barriers to entry and shortening the distance from design to consumer — is the same mechanism by which a design-led, outsourced-manufacturing model can itself be displaced. The filings flag counterfeiting and imitation as a distinct risk, which is the characteristic vulnerability of this model.

Three items cut against the moat narrative. First, rising retailer activation spend and accrued customer incentives climbing from 4.88% to 6.40% of sales in two years indicate the shelf is being bought, at least in part. Second, customer concentration is flat to worse, not improving: Customer A moved from 25.4% to 26.5% of Q2 sales, and top-three customers are 45-48% of sales and about 45% of receivables. Geographic diversification (U.S. share falling from 63.7% to 61.5%) is a different measure and does not establish customer diversification — a single global e-commerce or club retailer can grow alongside international expansion. Third, under the Brand License Agreement SharkNinja granted JS Global exclusive rights to distribute and sell Shark- and Ninja-branded products in certain APAC markets for a 20-year term from July 2023 in exchange for a 3% royalty. Royalty income to SharkNinja fell 24% year over year in H1 2026 to $6.52M, implying licensed sales in those markets are contracting. The amounts are immaterial; the structural point is that a large region is closed to direct entry until roughly 2043 and appears to be underexploited by the licensee.

Competitive position score of 6.5: trajectory clearly improving on share and international conversion, moat quality moderate and partly purchased.

## Risk Assessment

**Unremediated material weaknesses in internal control (the most underappreciated item).** Material weaknesses in ICFR were unremediated at December 31, 2025 and remained unremediated at June 30, 2026, with disclosure controls and procedures concluded not effective at both dates. The identified areas: COSO framework components, IT general controls, accounting for revenue and accounts receivable, inventory and cost of goods sold, and the financial statement close process. The Q2 10-Q states no changes during the quarter materially affected ICFR. No material misstatement has been identified and FY2025 was audited.

The weaknesses overlap precisely with the accounts where reported figures have behaved unusually and where the next quarter's most consequential entry will be booked: receivables (FY2025 DSO extended roughly eight days while consuming $350.5M of cash) and cost of goods sold (where a $247.1M tariff refund will be recognized in Q3). The benign reading is real — headcount grew from ~2,619 in 2021 to ~4,143 in 2025, and companies newly subject to full SOX 404 after transitioning from 20-F reporting on January 1, 2026 routinely report exactly this cluster as a scaling problem. But two reporting periods without disclosed remediation progress means the usual independent assurance layer is thinner than normal at a moment when the numbers carry a 28x multiple.

**Tariff position is not resolved.** The Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 decision in *Learning Resources v. Trump* struck down IEEPA tariff authority; the Court of International Trade ordered refunds on March 4, 2026, with aspects under government appeal. CBP accepted $247.1M of SharkNinja refund claims in July. Section 301 and 232 tariffs remain in force, the Section 122 replacement surcharge expired July 24, 2026 without extension, and new Section 301 investigations are open. FY2026 guidance explicitly assumes minimum rates of 10% for Indonesia/Malaysia/Cambodia and 12.5% for China/Vietnam/Thailand persist. Reinstatement under different statutory authority is a live scenario the guide does not contemplate.

**The Q3 GAAP print will be badly misleading in both directions.** The full $247.1M pre-tax refund lands in Q3 cost of sales. At the guided 22-23% tax rate and ~142.5M shares, that is roughly $1.33-$1.35 of non-recurring GAAP EPS. Anyone extrapolating Q3 GAAP margin or comparing to a GAAP estimate will overstate the run rate. Simultaneously, Q3 recognizes a receivable, not cash; collection follows CBP's timetable, which management does not control. Of the roughly $123.5M of 2026-attributable refund, only about $30M reaches guided adjusted EBITDA and $0.15 reaches guided EPS — the remaining $90-95M is stated as a "current intention" to reinvest in retail activation, media, technology/AI and tariff mitigation. That intention is not a commitment.

**Insider selling and controlling-shareholder supply.** The CEO sold 996,511 shares for approximately $162.0M in six transactions between February 26 and August 6, 2026, on an ascending price ladder from $129 to $185, retaining 1,617,959 shares plus 300,000 unvested RSUs. That is roughly 38% of his pre-sale holding. The two largest tranches (250,000 at $170 on August 5, 250,000 at $185 on August 6) bracket the Q2 release and guidance raise. The Chief Commercial Officer sold 29,667 shares on August 7. Chairman Xuning Wang sold 2,668,200 shares for $401.2M on July 10, taking his stake from roughly 37.8% to 35.9% — still well above the 30% threshold governing his board-appointment right, so no governance inflection is imminent, but each block is a technical supply event. No open-market purchases appear in the reviewed February-August Form 4 record. The CFO held 1,750 shares and the Chief Legal Officer 73 shares after their (small) sales.

The mitigating reading is genuine: the CEO's sales closely follow two large vesting events (467,909 units February 28, 200,000 August 7), post-vest diversification is the standard pattern, selling into an earnings window is more consistent with a pre-set plan than with opportunism, and Barrocas retains roughly $300M of stock. Critically, whether the sales were made under a Rule 10b5-1 plan could not be confirmed — the Q2 10-Q disclosed plans for the CFO and CLO only, and a plan adopted outside that window would not appear. I decline to characterize the sales as either planned or opportunistic. What is not ambiguous: the aggregate reduction in economic exposure by the two people with the most information, at prices near the top of the range, with nothing on the buy side.

**Buyback is dilution absorption, not accretion.** Against the $750M authorization from February 11, 2026, H1 repurchases were 1,008,368 shares for $119.7M (average $118.71). H1 issuance on net RSU vesting plus ESPP was 767,732 shares. Net reduction: approximately 240,636 shares, or 0.17% of shares outstanding. Monthly average purchase prices rose through the period ($110.47 April, $114.11 May, $132.00 June) — more shares bought at higher prices. An evergreen provision adds 0.6% of prior-year-end shares to the plan each January 1 through 2033 (846,948 added on January 1, 2026), so absorption demand is structural.

**Product safety.** FY2025 included a voluntary recall with a $4.6M gross-profit and $11.2M operating-income impact, plus $1.1M more in H1 2026. Absolute amounts are immaterial against $6.4B of sales; the recurrence across periods is the signal. Warranty liability was $35.5M at June 30, 2026. No material pending legal proceedings are disclosed. The specific product recalled was not identified in the filings — a gap.

**Macro overlay.** SharkNinja sells discretionary durable goods into the U.S. consumer. Higher-ticket appliance purchases correlate with household formation and home transactions, so a discretionary spending break is a conditional risk. Guidance does not appear to contemplate such a break, and none is yet visible in the company's own data — Q2 accelerated. This risk would show up first in Domestic Cleaning and Cooking volumes. [The specific claims about mortgage rates at a one-year high, below-year-ago application demand, and a negative housing wealth effect building into 2027 are not supported by the provided sources and have been removed.]

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $240 (+30%)

The innovation engine keeps landing categories. Beauty & Home Environment compounds off $285.8M quarterly at 40%+ as skincare devices scale; the 40th sub-category entered in July and the two planned 2026 launches each reach $150-200M within eighteen months. International, at 35.3% of sales growing 36.6%, sustains 30%+ as Italy, Spain, Benelux, the Nordics and Poland convert from distributor to direct — capturing distributor margin and lifting mix. The reinvested $90-95M of tariff refund produces measurable retail activation and DTC conversion returns rather than merely offsetting cost. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $8.00 and the market pays 30x for a proven high-teens compounder with a net cash balance sheet, taking the stock to $240 — the current high analyst target. Supporting evidence: four consecutive beats averaging 6.4%, a guidance path raised 550-600bp on the top line in six months, DSO improving from 95.1 to 83.6 days, and operating cash flow swinging from -$63.9M to +$275.5M year over year in H1.

### Bear Case → $135 (-27%)

The mature core determines the outcome. Cleaning stays at 4% growth, Food Prep laps the Slushi comparison into flat, and Cooking & Beverage decelerates once Luxe Café and Crispi anniversary. One of the two 2026 launches disappoints, and the pattern that made FY2025 work — a new $200M category every year — breaks. Retailer activation spend keeps rising (accrued incentives already 6.40% of sales from 4.88% two years ago), so share gain increasingly costs gross margin, and H2 fails to deliver the required back-half margin expansion after H1 compressed 70bp. Tariffs return under a different statutory authority, or the CIT refund order is reversed on appeal and the $247.1M receivable is written back. A material weakness produces a restatement in revenue, receivables or cost of sales. Growth normalizes to low double digits, FY2027 EPS lands at $7.10, and the multiple compresses to 19x — a level still well above Whirlpool and Spectrum Brands. That is $135, near the $137.51 low target. Insider selling of approximately $563M across the CEO and Chairman, plus 13.3% short interest, indicates who is already positioned for some version of this.

HOLD. Own it for the compounding if you already do, hedge or trim into strength above $200, and put new capital to work at $150-160 or on disclosed remediation of the control weaknesses alongside restored gross-margin expansion.
